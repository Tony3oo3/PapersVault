## Problem

Existing weakly supervised video anomaly detection methods are evaluated primarily by AUROC, which the paper shows is deceptively optimistic under the severe normal/anomalous segment imbalance, masking very low recall at practically meaningful false positive rates. The root cause is that standard top-k segment selection during MIL training is rigid, biased toward simple high-motion contexts, and fails to capture diverse or subtle abnormal events, leading to high false negatives in safety-critical deployments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video segments whose features are extracted by a frozen I3D network pretrained on Kinetics, along with video-level binary labels (abnormal/normal) during training. At inference, segment-level anomaly scores from the dual-exploration model are fused with training-free predictions from the InternVL3-14B vision-language model (queried with a structured prompt and averaged over multiple runs) via a weighted sum to produce the final per-segment anomaly score. The VLM operates in a completely training-free manner, requiring no fine-tuning or gradient updates.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual exploration strategy that replaces rigid top-k segment selection during MIL training. Temporal Clustering-Based Selection (TCBS) clusters segments by adaptive cosine-similarity thresholds (set per-video via a quantile of adjacent-segment similarities) and selects at most one highest-scoring segment per cluster, ensuring coverage of temporally and semantically distinct events. Uncertainty-Based Selection (UBS) computes per-segment uncertainty as the standard deviation of scores across an ensemble of M differently-initialized models, then selects segments that are both uncertain (above threshold τ_u) and semantically similar to the abnormal memory unit, applying a BCE loss targeting score 1. The total training objective combines the standard MIL loss, the uncertainty loss (weighted by γ), and the UR-DMU baseline loss. At inference, the dual-exploration model's scores are fused with InternVL3-14B predictions via a tunable weighted sum (λ), leveraging the VLM's strength on generalized anomalies (e.g., explosions, accidents) to complement the exploration model's strength on subtle, human-centric events (e.g., abuse, shoplifting).

## Results

On UCF-Crime, the proposed Dual Exploration + InternVL fusion achieves the highest AP of 38.33% among all weakly supervised and training-free baselines, with Recall@FPR=2% of 0.263 and Recall@FPR=3% of 0.336, substantially outperforming UR-DMU (AP 35.48%, Recall@FPR=2% 0.170) and VadCLIP (AP 33.55%, Recall@FPR=2% 0.155). On XD-Violence, the method reaches AP 84.58% and Recall@FPR=5% of 0.715, edging out VadCLIP (AP 84.50%, Recall@FPR=5% 0.672) and surpassing UR-DMU (AP 79.14%, Recall@FPR=5% 0.638). Notably, the training-free InternVL3-14B achieves higher recall at very low FPR (0.301 at 1% FPR on UCF-Crime) but lower overall AP (29.50%), motivating the fusion strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On UCF-Crime, the proposed method wins on AP against all weakly supervised baselines (38.33% vs. UR-DMU 35.48%, VadCLIP 33.55%, PEL4VAD 33.99%) and also surpasses the training-free InternVL3-14B (29.50%). At practical FPR thresholds, it outperforms UR-DMU by 0.093 at 2% FPR (0.263 vs. 0.170) and by 0.124 at 3% FPR (0.336 vs. 0.212), and beats VadCLIP by 0.108 at 2% FPR (0.263 vs. 0.155). However, InternVL3-14B alone achieves higher recall at 1% FPR (0.301 vs. 0.173), which the authors attribute to the VLM's strong confident predictions on obvious anomalies, though its lower AP reflects poor ranking of subtler anomalies. On XD-Violence, the method achieves the best AP (84.58%) and highest recall at 4% and 5% FPR (0.657 and 0.715), narrowly beating VadCLIP (84.50% AP, 0.635/0.672 recall) and substantially outperforming UR-DMU (79.14% AP, 0.590/0.638 recall). The ablation confirms that TCBS contributes most at very low FPR while UBS adds gains at 2–3% FPR, and VLM fusion provides the largest single improvement in AP (+1.91 points over dual exploration alone).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UR-DMU memory-augmented MIL framework [32] by replacing its top-k segment selection with a dual exploration strategy combining temporal clustering-based selection (TCBS) and uncertainty-based selection (UBS). It departs from the broader WSVAD lineage (Sultani et al. [17], RTFM [18], BNSVP [14], DRO [15], Unbiased MIL [10]) that all rely on highest-scoring segment selection, arguing this biases toward simple, motion-heavy anomalies. The VLM fusion with InternVL is positioned as a training-free alternative to fine-tuning-based CLIP approaches like VadCLIP [24], leveraging pretrained semantic knowledge without additional training cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most impactful contribution is demonstrating that AUROC, the standard metric in WSVAD, masks a 'low practical recall' phenomenon where high-AUROC models miss thousands of true anomalies at operationally feasible FPR thresholds — a critical concern for safety-critical surveillance deployment. The dual exploration strategy addresses a genuine limitation of top-k selection under weak supervision, though the AP gains over UR-DMU are modest (35.48→38.33%), with much of the improvement attributable to VLM fusion rather than the exploration mechanism alone. Open questions include whether the exploration strategy generalizes to truly novel anomaly categories absent from both the memory unit and VLM training data, and whether extending knowledge sharing into the training phase (as the authors note) could yield larger gains.
