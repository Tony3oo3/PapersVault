## Problem

Existing infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) methods rely on large quantities of spatiotemporally aligned image pairs, which are costly and labor-intensive to collect due to registration challenges. Moreover, rigid pairing constrains the diversity of cross-modal relationships a model can learn, limiting generalisation. This paper asks whether the strictly-paired training paradigm (SPTP) is truly necessary, and whether unpaired or arbitrarily paired training can achieve comparable fusion performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an infrared image and a visible image as input and produces a single fused image combining thermal signatures and visible textures. Crucially, during training the two inputs need not be spatiotemporally aligned or even from the same scene—they can be drawn from different locations, times, or entirely different datasets. The three lightweight baselines (CNN, Transformer, GAN) each have only 0.71–0.88 MB of parameters, and the fused output is evaluated with both no-reference (EN) and full-reference (MI, VIF, Qabf, SSIM) metrics.

## Method

The core insight is that pixel-level self-supervised loss functions in IVIF do not require content consistency between modalities, only pixel-level relationships, which makes arbitrary cross-modal pairing viable. The authors formalise Arbitrarily Paired Training (APTP) as the union of SPTP (i=j) and UPTP (i≠j), showing via a decoupled joint probability that N base images expand to N² trainable pairs. To enable this, they design adaptive per-pixel weighted losses (intensity L1, gradient, and SSIM) using a universal linear weighting function W(a;a,b)=a/(a+b) that automatically allocates attention to whichever source is more informative at each pixel and information level. Three lightweight baselines—CNN for local context, Transformer for long-range modelling, and GAN for adversarial refinement—are trained under SPTP, UPTP, and APTP to validate the paradigm, demonstrating that 150 arbitrarily paired images match the performance of 15,000 strictly paired images.

## Results

On the four evaluation datasets (LLVIP, MSRS, RoadScene, TNO), the proposed CNN baseline (0.81 MB) achieves the best or near-best scores across all five metrics. For example, on MSRS the CNN baseline reaches MI=2.69, VIF=0.93, Qabf=0.65, SSIM=1.00, surpassing the strongest SOTA (CrossFuse: MI=2.17, SSIM=0.88) by a wide margin. On LLVIP, the Transformer baseline attains MI=2.56 and SSIM=0.92, outperforming the best SOTA (DeFusion: MI=2.33, SSIM=0.83). All three baselines are trained with only 150 strictly-paired images expanded to 15,000 via APTP, yet they outperform methods trained on substantially larger paired datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three proposed baselines (CNN, Transformer, GAN) outperform every SOTA method across all four datasets on the majority of metrics. On MSRS, the CNN baseline achieves MI=2.69 and SSIM=1.00, exceeding the closest competitor CrossFuse (MI=2.17, SSIM=0.88) by 0.52 in MI and 0.12 in SSIM; on LLVIP, the Transformer baseline reaches MI=2.56 and SSIM=0.92 versus DeFusion's MI=2.33 and SSIM=0.83. On RoadScene, the GAN baseline leads with EN=7.46 and MI=2.36, surpassing CrossFuse (EN=7.25, MI=2.13) and SAGE (EN=6.76, MI=2.17). On TNO, the GAN baseline attains SSIM=0.99 and VIF=0.76, edging out SAGE (SSIM=0.97, VIF=0.63) and CrossFuse (SSIM=0.88, VIF=0.74). Notably, the proposed methods are trained on only 150 paired images (expanded to 15,000 via APTP) with model sizes of 0.71–0.81 MB, whereas SOTA methods like DDF (2108 MB) and CrossFuse (79.40 MB) are orders of magnitude larger and rely on substantially larger strictly-paired training sets. The consistent superiority across unseen datasets (RoadScene, TNO) particularly highlights the generalisation advantage of the arbitrarily-paired training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in infrared-visible image fusion (IVIF) that mandates spatiotemporally aligned training pairs, drawing conceptual inspiration from unpaired image-to-image translation (CycleGAN) but repurposing it for a setting where no ground-truth fused output exists. Rather than adopting adversarial or cycle-consistency losses, the authors exploit the pixel-level self-supervised structure of fusion to design adaptive weighted losses (intensity, gradient, SSIM) that remain valid under arbitrary cross-modal pairings. The theoretical contribution frames arbitrarily-paired training (APTP) as the formal union of strictly-paired (SPTP) and unpaired (UPTP) paradigms via a probability decomposition, positioning the work as a generalization rather than a replacement of existing methods. Three lightweight baselines (CNN, Transformer, GAN) are constructed solely to validate the paradigm, keeping architectural novelty out of scope.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is substantial: a 150-pair base dataset can be expanded to 15,000 trainable pairs through re-pairing, matching the performance of a 100× larger strictly-paired collection and dramatically reducing the cost of data acquisition under challenging conditions (adverse weather, nighttime, smoke). However, the baselines are very small (~0.8 MB), and the SOTA comparison relies on cross-dataset unpaired training, which introduces a confound between the pairing paradigm and domain diversity. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to fusion tasks lacking pixel-level self-supervision (e.g., multi-spectral or medical fusion), whether it interacts favorably with larger or more complex architectures, and whether the adaptive weighting mechanism generalizes beyond the specific loss decomposition used here.
