## Problem

Conventional domain generalization in semantic segmentation (DG-SS) is restricted to a fixed set of known categories, while open-vocabulary segmentation (OV-SS) models, though capable of recognizing unseen classes, suffer sharp performance drops under domain shifts. No existing paradigm jointly addresses both unseen domains and unseen categories, a gap that is particularly critical in autonomous driving where models must handle novel objects (barriers, cones, railways) across diverse weather, lighting, and geographic conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB urban scene images at 512×512 (ViT-B/16) or 448×448 (ViT-L/14) resolution, producing a 32×32 token grid, along with an open text vocabulary of class names (19 to 58+ categories) encoded by a frozen EVA-CLIP text encoder. The output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask over the full open vocabulary, including classes never seen during training. Training uses only 7 driving categories (road, sidewalk, building, vegetation, sky, person, car) from Cityscapes or GTA, while evaluation spans 19 or 58 categories across seven unseen target domains.

## Method

S²-Corr replaces CAT-Seg's cross-attention correlation aggregation with a selective state-space model (SSM) that processes text-image correlation embeddings sequentially via input-dependent recurrent state updates, where a decay gate A_t controls how much past state is preserved and an input gate B_t modulates new information injection. Three key innovations are added: (i) image- and text-conditioned modulation (FiLM-style scaling and shifting) that injects domain-relevant cues before aggregation, (ii) a learnable geometric decay prior that blends the data-driven gate with a head-specific exponential attenuation rate to suppress long-range noise propagation, and (iii) a chunk-wise snake scanning strategy that divides the spatial grid into 16 chunks and alternates scanning direction between consecutive rows, passing end states between chunks to maintain spatial continuity. The class-wise aggregation also uses the same SSM formulation, avoiding the O(N_C²) complexity of attention and scaling efficiently to large open vocabularies. Only 26M (ViT-B) or 76.8M (ViT-L) parameters are updated, trained for 20k iterations in ~2–4 hours on a single GPU.

## Results

Under the Real-to-Real OVDG-SS setting (CS-7 → Dv-19/Dv-58), S²-Corr with ViT-B/16 achieves 50.3% mIoU on Dv-19 and 47.9% on Dv-58, surpassing the previous best (CLIPSelf at 45.7%/45.0%) by 4.6 and 2.9 points respectively. With the larger ViT-L/14 backbone, S²-Corr reaches 55.8% on Dv-19 and 53.2% on Dv-58, outperforming the strongest baseline (CLIPSelf at 53.3%/51.5%) by 2.5 and 1.7 points. Under the more challenging Synthetic-to-Real setting (GTA-7 → Dv-19/Dv-58), S²-Corr attains 48.2%/46.7% (ViT-B/16) and 49.9%/49.4% (ViT-L/14), consistently leading all baselines. The method also achieves superior efficiency: 26.1 FPS at 19-class vocabulary and 18.3 FPS at 150-class vocabulary with only 9.2 GB GPU memory, versus 5.7–5.1 FPS and 13.8–15.7 GB for CAT-Seg and ESC-Net.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²-Corr outperforms all 14 baselines across both Real-to-Real and Synthetic-to-Real settings. Among training-free methods (CLIP-DINOiser, ClearCLIP, ProxyCLIP), the gap is largest: S²-Corr exceeds ProxyCLIP (the strongest training-free method) by 13.9 points on Dv-19 and 10.0 points on Dv-58 with ViT-B/16. Among training-based OV-SS methods trained on COCO-Stuff (CAT-Seg, DeCLIP), the advantage is even more pronounced, with S²-Corr leading by over 17 points on Dv-19. The closest competitors are CLIPSelf and RSC-CLIPSelf (which use additional COCO training data): S²-Corr surpasses CLIPSelf by 4.6/2.9 points (ViT-B/16) and 2.5/1.7 points (ViT-L/14) on Dv-19/Dv-58. Domain-generalization-augmented baselines (CAT-Seg+AdvStyle, CAT-Seg+DGInStyle) also trail S²-Corr by 5.7–8.6 points on Dv-19, confirming that explicit style augmentation alone is insufficient for open-vocabulary cross-domain transfer. On the Synthetic-to-Real setting, S²-Corr maintains its lead over all baselines, with the largest margin over CAT-Seg (4.3 points on Dv-19, ViT-B/16) and a consistent 1.1–2.0 point advantage over the strongest prior methods (CLIPSelf, RSC-CLIPSelf).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: domain-generalized semantic segmentation (DG-SS), which handles unseen environments but is restricted to a fixed class set, and open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OV-SS), which handles unseen categories but is sensitive to domain shift. It builds directly on CAT-Seg's correlation-based OV-SS pipeline, replacing its Swin-style cross-attention aggregation with a selective state-space model (Mamba-style SSM) for sequential correlation refinement. The key conceptual departure is treating domain shift as a first-class problem that specifically corrupts the text-image correlation maps produced by VLMs, rather than addressing it through data augmentation or style transfer as prior DG-SS methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of OVDG-SS as a unified task and the accompanying driving benchmark (spanning synthetic-to-real and real-to-real settings with 19 and 58 categories) fills a clear gap: prior OV-SS evaluations assumed a fixed domain, and prior DG-SS evaluations assumed a fixed vocabulary. The efficiency advantage of SSM-based aggregation over attention (3× faster at 150-class vocabulary, lower GPU memory) is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment. Open questions include whether the specific SSM design choices (chunk size, snake scanning, geometric decay) are optimal, whether the approach transfers to non-driving domains, and whether the Stable Diffusion–augmented test sets (ACDC-41, BDD-41) adequately represent truly novel categories versus synthetic perturbations of known ones.
