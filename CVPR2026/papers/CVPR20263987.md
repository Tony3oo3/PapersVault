## Problem

Existing spatial-temporal video super-resolution (STVSR) methods are designed for visible-spectrum videos and ignore the thermophysical structure inherent in infrared imagery, where pixel intensities directly encode surface temperature fields governed by heat conduction dynamics. This leads to outputs that may appear perceptually plausible but violate underlying thermal consistency. Additionally, the field lacks a high-resolution infrared video benchmark for fair evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263987/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts k+1 low-resolution, low-frame-rate infrared frames of size H×W×C (odd-timestamped frames only) and produces 2k+1 high-resolution frames of size 4H×4W×C, performing simultaneous ×4 spatial and ×2 temporal upscaling. The input is a bicubically downsampled thermal video sequence, and the output includes both the original odd-timestamped frames (upscaled) and newly synthesized even-timestamped intermediate frames. Evaluation is conducted on the newly introduced IRVAL dataset (512×512 resolution, 108,512 frames) as well as LLVIP and SGMP benchmarks.

## Method

THERIS embeds heat conduction physics into three coupled components. The Thermal Diffusion Interpolation Module (TDIM) reinterprets the temporal axis as a 1D spatial heat field, applies DCT to obtain spectral coefficients, and synthesizes intermediate frames by multiplying with a learnable exponential decay kernel exp(−κ(n;eᵢ)ωₙ²Δt) that mimics frequency-dependent thermal diffusion, with κ produced by a small MLP from time embeddings. The Thermo-Aware State Space Module (TSSM) then refines features using a Mamba-style selective state-space recurrence whose output matrix C is modulated by a thermal prompt derived from TDIM's time embeddings, combined with a learnable FFT-based spectral mask to recover attenuated high frequencies and three scan orders (space-first, time-first, Hilbert curve) for diverse spatiotemporal dependencies. A Temperature Field Modeling Loss enforces discrete 2D heat-equation consistency (∂u/∂t = D∇²u) via central-difference and 5-point Laplacian approximations, weighted by an edge-aware exponential term that suppresses regularization in high-gradient regions, and is combined with standard L1 reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the IRVAL test set, THERIS achieves 21.37 PSNR, 0.7872 SSIM, 55.59 MUSIQ, and 0.2990 DOVER, outperforming the strongest unified STVSR baseline BF-STVSR (20.18 PSNR, 0.7634 SSIM, 45.41 MUSIQ, 0.1636 DOVER) by 1.19 dB in PSNR and 10.18 points in MUSIQ. On LLVIP, THERIS reaches 28.05 PSNR / 0.9177 SSIM, surpassing the best baseline ZoomingSlowMo (27.56 / 0.9142). On SGMP, THERIS attains 37.83 PSNR / 0.9517 SSIM, exceeding TMNet (37.54 / 0.9474). The method also achieves the highest downstream object detection mAP of 50.7 on LLVIP using a YOLO detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

THERIS outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On IRVAL, the largest gap is against the unified STVSR methods: THERIS exceeds BF-STVSR (the visible-domain SOTA) by 1.19 dB PSNR and 10.18 MUSIQ points, and surpasses the best VFI+VSR pipeline (VFIMamba + RealBasicVSR, 20.50 PSNR) by 0.87 dB. The paper explicitly notes that BF-STVSR, despite being SOTA for visible videos, performs suboptimally on infrared data, underscoring the domain-specific challenge. On LLVIP, THERIS leads ZoomingSlowMo (27.56 PSNR) by 0.49 dB and TMNet (27.51) by 0.54 dB; on SGMP, it beats TMNet (37.54) by 0.29 dB. In the downstream object detection experiment, THERIS achieves the highest mAP of 50.7, with qualitative results showing fewer missed detections (e.g., traffic lights, pedestrians near trucks) compared to competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the STVSR line of work (ZoomingSlowMo, TMNet, VideoINR, MoTIF, BF-STVSR) from the visible spectrum to infrared video, arguing that visible-domain methods fail because TIR encodes temperature fields rather than reflected light. Architecturally, it builds on Mamba's selective state-space formulation (Eq. 9) and augments it with a learnable spectral mask and Hilbert-curve scanning, while the temporal interpolation module (TDIM) repurposes the classical 1D heat equation's DCT-domain solution as a physically grounded frame synthesis operator. The Temperature Field Modeling Loss is a novel physics-informed regularizer that has no direct analogue in prior visible-spectrum STVSR or infrared image SR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of IRVAL (108,512 frames at 512×512) addresses a genuine data bottleneck that has limited infrared video research, and the heat-conduction prior offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for enforcing temporal coherence that is specific to thermal imaging. Open questions include whether the physics prior generalizes to mid-wave infrared or non-thermal IR modalities, how the TFM loss behaves under large thermal transients (e.g., rapid object entry/exit), and whether the Mamba-based backbone's linear-complexity advantage persists at higher spatial scales. The approach also raises the broader question of how tightly physics constraints should be coupled to learned representations in other physics-grounded imaging tasks.
