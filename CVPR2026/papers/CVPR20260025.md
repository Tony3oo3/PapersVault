## Problem

State-of-the-art open-world counting models cannot specify what NOT to count, require manual annotation of visual exemplars for every input image, and only accept exemplars from the input image itself. These restrictions make visually specifying objects extremely tedious and prevent the model from distinguishing visually similar objects (e.g., different blood cell types or fruit varieties) in real-world applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260025/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an input image together with a positive text prompt, any number of positive and negative visual exemplars (bounding boxes), and corresponding negative text prompts. Crucially, exemplar images can be external to the input image—natural or synthetic—so a single annotated exemplar image can be reused across an entire dataset without per-image annotation. At inference, the model outputs bounding boxes with confidence scores that are enumerated to produce the final count; a two-condition filter (confidence threshold and positive-over-negative similarity) rejects queries matching negative prompts.

## Method

COUNTGD++ extends the CountGD-Box/Grounding DINO transformer architecture by processing input and exemplar images in separate streams through a shared Swin Transformer encoder, extracting exemplar tokens via RoIAlign, and fusing corresponding positive/negative text and visual tokens with self-attention in a Feature Enhancer (6 blocks) before cross-attending to input image tokens. Training uses a Focal Loss objective that pushes object queries closer to positive prompt features and away from negative ones, with Hungarian matching allowing different queries within an image to correspond to different classes. At inference, a two-condition filter (sigmoid similarity above threshold AND positive similarity exceeding all negative similarities) rejects false positives from visually similar objects. The model also introduces pseudo-exemplars—top-scoring predicted boxes fed back as visual exemplars for a second forward pass—eliminating manual annotation, and supports an LLM agent loop that iteratively refines counts or generates synthetic exemplars via diffusion models.

## Results

On FSCD-147 (text-only setting), COUNTGD++ with pseudo- and synthetic exemplars (Ours_t+p+s) achieves MAE 8.39, RMSE 27.03, AP 38.93, and AP50 71.35, the best counting and detection results among all text-only methods. On the multi-class Blood Cell Detection dataset, adding negative prompts reduces MAE from 11.56 (positive-only) to 1.52 (with external negative exemplars), an order-of-magnitude improvement. On ShanghaiTech crowd counting, COUNTGD++ with pseudo-exemplars achieves MAE 116.0 (Part A) and 28.0 (Part B), outperforming all baselines. On VideoCount (Crystals), pseudo-exemplars reduce MAE from 69.1 (text-only CountVid) to 10, a ~7× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COUNTGD++ outperforms all box-outputting baselines on FSCD-147: it beats CGD-B (MAE 15.01, RMSE 118.16) by 44% in MAE and 77% in RMSE, and DAVEprm (MAE 15.52, RMSE 114.10) by 46% in MAE. Against text-only methods without box output, CountSE achieves a slightly lower MAE (7.84 vs. 8.39) but has a 3× higher RMSE (82.99 vs. 27.03) and cannot produce bounding boxes; GrREC (MAE 10.12) and CAD-GD (MAE 10.35) are both outperformed on both counting metrics. On the multi-class Blood Cell Detection and OmniCount (Fruits) benchmarks, COUNTGD++ with negative prompts reduces MAE by ~7× (11.56→1.52) and ~4.7× (1.97→0.56) respectively compared to its own positive-only setting, and by ~7× over CountGD (MAE 10.99) and CGD-B (MAE 11.34). On ShanghaiTech, COUNTGD++ (MAE 116.0/28.0) beats CGD-B (132.2/32.2), CountSE (129.7/–), CLIP-Count (192.6/45.7), OWLv2 (420.2/81.5), and Grounding DINO (394.9/58.3). On VideoCount (Crystals), pseudo-exemplars reduce MAE from 69.1 (text-only CountVid) to 10, also surpassing CountVid with manual exemplars (MAE 12). On PrACo, COUNTGD++ with negative text achieves the best NMN (0.07), PCCN (97.99), CntP (0.90), and CntR (0.96), outperforming TFPOC and DAVE.

## Relation to Prior Work

COUNTGD++ extends the CountGD/CountGD-Box lineage (itself built on Grounding DINO) by adding three orthogonal prompt capabilities: negative text and visual exemplars that act as filters, pseudo-exemplars that automate visual example selection via a two-pass inference loop, and external exemplars drawn from separate natural or synthetic images. The contrastive training objective (Focal Loss pushing queries toward positive and away from negative prompt features) is the key architectural departure from CountGD-Box, which only handled positive prompts. The paper also positions itself within the broader LLM-as-controller trend (ViperGPT, HuggingGPT), showing how the counting model can be wrapped as an expert agent callable by a language model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative-prompting capability addresses a real practical gap: distinguishing visually similar objects (red vs. white blood cells, ripe vs. unripe fruit) without manual per-image annotation. Pseudo-exemplars and external exemplars together dramatically reduce the annotation burden from per-image to one-time, which is critical for deploying counting in production settings like medical imaging or industrial inspection. Open questions include how the approach scales to many negative classes simultaneously, whether the mosaic-based multi-class training data is sufficient for truly open-world generalization, and whether the LLM agent pipeline adds meaningful value over the simpler two-pass pseudo-exemplar loop it largely replicates.
