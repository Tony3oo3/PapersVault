## Problem

Conventional knowledge distillation for semantic segmentation preserves in-domain accuracy but systematically degrades out-of-domain generalization, a problem that is amplified when distilling from vision foundation models (VFMs) like DINOv2. The paper identifies an optimization conflict: jointly optimizing task loss and feature distillation in a single stage causes the student to inherit teacher biases tied to visible domains rather than their domain-invariant representations. The central question is whether VFMs' strong cross-domain robustness can be transferred to compact models without sacrificing it.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB driving-scene images (GTAV at 2048×1024, Cityscapes at 2048×1024, BDD100K at 1280×720, Mapillary at 1920×1080) or aerial remote-sensing images (ISPRS Potsdam at 6000×6000, Vaihingen at 2000×2000), cropped to 512×512 during training. The student ViT encoder produces patch-level token features that are distilled against the teacher VFM's features at matching spatial resolutions, and the final output is a per-pixel segmentation mask over 19 driving classes or 6 remote-sensing classes, generated by a Mask2Former decoder head.

## Method

GKD decouples representation learning from task learning into two sequential stages. In the domain-general distillation stage, the student first learns task-agnostic features from the VFM teacher on ImageNet (a proxy dataset free of task bias), then continues distilling domain-agnostic features on the source domains, both via a proposed Query-based Soft Distillation (QSD) mechanism. QSD uses student features as queries to attend over all teacher spatial tokens (W = φ(v_s)·v_t^T), reconstructs student features by reweighting them with the softmax attention (v_s' = σ(W)·φ(v_s)), and aligns the result to teacher features via MSE; it additionally includes masked-patch distillation and CLS-token distillation to capture both local spatial structure and global semantics. In the task learning stage, the student encoder is frozen and only the Mask2Former decoder is trained with standard segmentation loss on source annotations, preventing the domain-general representations from being biased toward source-specific decision boundaries. This multi-stage schedule eliminates the optimization conflict observed in single-stage KD, where task gradients and distillation gradients interfere, yielding smoother convergence and substantially better cross-domain mIoU.

## Results

In the F2L setting (DINOv2-L → ViT-B), GKD achieves 57.9% average mIoU on GTAV→Citys+BDD+Map, 64.6% on Citys→Night+Snow+Fog+Rain, and 65.1% on P-R→P-I+V-I, outperforming the best baseline (G2SD) by 6.8%, 10.8%, and 5.6% respectively. In the F2F setting (DINOv2-L → ViT-B), GKD reaches 59.8% on GTAV→Citys+BDD+Map and 68.0% on ACDC, surpassing the strongest baseline (Proteus) by 1.1% and 1.3%. Across all five domain generalization benchmarks, GKD yields average gains of +10.6% in F2L and +1.9% in F2F over existing KD methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GKD consistently outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark in both F2L and F2F settings. In the F2L setting (DINOv2-L → ViT-B), GKD achieves 57.9% avg mIoU on GTAV→Citys+BDD+Map, beating the strongest baseline G2SD (51.1%) by 6.8% and Vanilla KD (49.9%) by 8.0%; on the harder Citys→ACDC target, GKD reaches 64.6% vs. G2SD's 53.8% (+10.8%). In the F2F setting (DINOv2-L → ViT-B), gains are more modest but still consistent: GKD scores 59.8% on GTAV→Citys+BDD+Map vs. Proteus (58.7%) and Vanilla KD (58.2%), and 68.0% on ACDC vs. Proteus (66.7%). Notably, conventional KD methods (Vanilla KD, CWD, Af-DCD, Vitkd) often degrade the student below the teacher's generalization, whereas GKD narrows or even closes the gap to the teacher (e.g., DeiT-B+GKD reaches 57.9% vs. DINOv2-B's 58.8%). The advantage is most pronounced in the label-scarce F2L regime, where GKD with only 1/16 labels (51.4% on Citys+BDD+Map) surpasses Af-DCD with full labels (47.1%) by 4.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from conventional single-stage KD (FitNets, CWD, Af-DCD, ViT-KD, Proteus) by arguing that jointly optimizing task loss and feature distillation creates an optimization conflict that degrades out-of-domain generalization. It builds on the multi-stage distillation idea from G2SD and [49], but explicitly freezes the student encoder during task learning to prevent domain overfitting—a stricter decoupling than prior generic-to-specific schedules. The Query-based Soft Distillation mechanism is a novel departure from point-wise feature matching, using student features as attention queries over teacher tokens to selectively retrieve spatial relational structure rather than imitate local activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that conventional KD actively harms generalization (students worse than teachers on unseen domains) challenges the default assumption that distillation is always beneficial and provides a concrete diagnostic for practitioners deploying compressed models under distribution shift. The +10.6% F2L gain and the label-efficiency results (51.4% mIoU with 1/16 labels vs. 35.7% baseline) make GKD practically relevant for edge deployment of VFM-quality segmentation. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder constraint is too restrictive for tasks requiring representation adaptation, whether QSD generalizes to non-ViT architectures or other dense prediction tasks (detection, depth), and whether the ImageNet proxy stage can be replaced by cheaper alternatives.
