## Problem

Feed-forward 3D/4D reconstruction models depend on dense geometry and camera pose labels that are expensive to obtain and especially scarce for dynamic in-the-wild scenes, preventing truly large-scale visual geometry learning. The paper asks how dense 2D correspondences (optical flow) from unlabeled videos can serve as an effective supervisory signal for 3D geometry prediction, and specifically how the flow prediction module should be structured to directly guide learning of both scene geometry and camera motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of N multi-view RGB images (static or dynamic) as input, encoded by a DINOv2-initialized patch encoder into per-image patch tokens. It outputs per-view camera parameters (rotation, translation, intrinsics), per-pixel depth maps, global 3D pointmaps in a shared coordinate frame, and dense flow fields between image pairs. For unlabeled training data, pseudo-ground-truth flow is extracted by a pre-trained UFM teacher model, and the flow loss is applied only to the unlabeled sequences while 3D supervision is applied to labeled ones.

## Method

Flow3r introduces a factored flow prediction head that computes flow between a source and target image by conditioning the source view's geometry latents (patch tokens) on the target view's camera latents (global token), then decoding the fused representation through a DPT head. This asymmetric factorization mirrors the projective-geometry decomposition (source geometry + target pose) but operates entirely in latent space, avoiding the instability of explicit 3D projection and naturally extending to dynamic scenes where flow reflects both camera and scene motion. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the flow head is trained with a frozen backbone on labeled data; second, the full model is unfrozen and fine-tuned end-to-end on ~34K labeled sequences plus ~800K unlabeled video sequences (Kinetics-700, SpatialVID, EPIC-Kitchens) using a robust Charbonnier flow loss. The paper demonstrates that this factored formulation outperforms both a VGGT-style tracking head (which only encourages discriminative features) and a projection-based flow head (which is unstable), and that performance scales consistently with the amount of unlabeled video data.

## Results

Flow3r achieves state-of-the-art results across eight benchmarks spanning static and dynamic scenes. On dynamic in-the-wild videos (Kinetics-700), Flow3r reduces RPE rotation to 0.830° (vs. 1.006° for π³ and 1.392° for VGGT) and RPE translation to 0.018 (vs. 0.023 for π³), while also improving geometry (MSE 0.256, f-score 0.599). On static 7-Scenes, Flow3r reaches RTA@30 of 91.66 and f-score@5 of 0.807, surpassing π³ (87.69 / 0.737) and VGGT (86.51 / 0.665). The largest relative gains appear on in-the-wild dynamic videos where labeled 3D data is most scarce.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow3r outperforms all four baselines (DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT, π³) on every metric across all eight evaluation datasets. Against the strongest baseline π³, Flow3r improves RPE rotation on Kinetics-700 from 1.006° to 0.830° (17% reduction) and RPE translation from 0.023 to 0.018, while also improving geometry (MSE 0.267→0.256, f-score 0.585→0.599). On static 7-Scenes, Flow3r gains 3.97 points in RTA@30 over π³ (91.66 vs. 87.69) and 7.0 points in f-score@5 (0.807 vs. 0.737). Against VGGT, the margins are larger on dynamic scenes (e.g., RPE rot 0.830 vs. 1.392 on Kinetics-700) and on static 7-Scenes (RTA 91.66 vs. 86.51). DUSt3R and CUT3R are substantially outperformed, with DUSt3R showing particularly poor rotation accuracy (9.343° vs. 0.830° on Kinetics-700). The gains are most pronounced on in-the-wild dynamic videos (Kinetics-700, EPIC-Kitchens, Sintel), consistent with the paper's claim that unlabeled flow supervision is most beneficial where labeled 3D data is scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow3r extends the feed-forward visual geometry learning paradigm established by DUSt3R, VGGT, and π³, which predict camera poses and 3D geometry from multi-view images via a shared multi-view transformer. Its key departure is replacing (or augmenting) expensive dense 3D/pose supervision with a factored flow prediction objective: predicting flow between two views using geometry latents from the source and pose latents from the target, rather than the symmetric pairwise matching ('tracking') head in VGGT or brittle explicit projection. This asymmetric factorization is motivated by projective geometry but implemented as a learned latent-space decoder (DPT head), making it robust to geometric errors and naturally extensible to dynamic scenes. The paper also builds on recent dense correspondence models (UFM, RoMa, DKM) as pseudo-label providers for the flow supervision signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Flow3r provides a practical recipe for scaling visual geometry learning to in-the-wild data without 3D annotations, addressing a fundamental bottleneck that has limited the field's progress relative to self-supervised scaling in other vision tasks. The clean ablation showing that flow-tracking (VGGT-style) yields almost no geometry/pose improvement despite accurate flow prediction, while factored flow does, is a useful diagnostic for the community. Limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf flow models for pseudo-labels (which can fail in novel domains), potential struggles with scenes containing multiple independently moving objects, and the current moderate scale (~800K videos) leaving open whether the scaling trend holds at 10–100M videos.
