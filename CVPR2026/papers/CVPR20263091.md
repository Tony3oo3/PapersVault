## Problem

Existing Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods for LiDAR relocalization treat all predicted point correspondences equally, making them vulnerable to noise and degenerate scene regions. Additionally, their network architectures lack inherent robustness to yaw variations, producing inconsistent predictions when the sensor orientation changes during driving. These two weaknesses limit relocalization accuracy in real-world autonomous driving scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single raw LiDAR scan as input, which is first rectified to the horizontal plane via Patchwork++ ground segmentation, then mapped to cylindrical coordinates and voxelized at 0.2 m resolution with a circumferential resolution of 1024 cells. The network outputs per-point 3D scene coordinates in the world frame along with a scalar reliability score per point; at inference, only the top-k highest-reliability points (minimum 50) are retained to drive a RANSAC-based 6-DoF pose solver, after which the ground-plane rectification is inverted to recover the final pose in the original sensor frame.

## Method

LEADER introduces two coupled components. First, a Robust Projection-based Geometric Encoder (RPGE) applies cylindrical projection followed by a U-Net-style hierarchy of cyclic sparse convolutions (channel widths 32→384, then upsampled and fused to 512 dims), where initial per-point features are restricted to [radial distance, height, intensity] to enforce yaw invariance, and symmetric cyclic padding ensures seamless feature continuity across the angular boundary. Second, a Truncated Relative Reliability (TRR) loss assigns each point a learned reliability score, applies arc-tangent scaling with clamping to bound magnitudes, and computes normalized exponential weights that suppress gradients from ambiguous points while amplifying those from discriminative regions. A Multihead Max Regressor (4 parallel heads, 5 stacked layers with LayerNorm and LeakyReLU) maps the 512-dim features to the 4-dim output (3D coordinates + reliability), and Cartesian Recovery inverts the cylindrical projection to produce world-frame coordinates. At inference, adaptive reliability thresholding selects high-confidence correspondences for RANSAC pose estimation, decoupling learned correspondence prediction from geometric verification.

## Results

On the Quality-enhanced Oxford RobotCar dataset, LEADER achieves a mean position error of 0.63 m and mean orientation error of 1.11°, the best position error among all compared methods. On the NCLT dataset, LEADER achieves 0.31 m mean position error and 1.81° mean orientation error, representing a 73.9% reduction over the APR baseline DiffLoc (1.19 m) and a 79.5% reduction over the SCR baseline LiSA (1.51 m). Notably, 90.0% of NCLT test frames are localized within 0.5 m error and 98.3% within 1 m, compared to 34.3% and 72.0% for the next-best method (LiSA). LEADER runs at 20 Hz on Oxford and 10 Hz on NCLT, meeting real-time requirements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEADER outperforms all eight baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest APR method DiffLoc, LEADER reduces position error by 66.1% on Oxford (0.63 m vs. 1.86 m) and by 73.9% on NCLT (0.31 m vs. 1.19 m). Among SCR methods, LEADER beats LightLoc (the best SCR on Oxford) by 24.1% in position error (0.63 m vs. 0.83 m) and LiSA (the best SCR on NCLT) by 79.5% (0.31 m vs. 1.51 m). On NCLT, LEADER also achieves the lowest orientation error (1.81°) compared to all baselines, whereas on Oxford it is second only to DiffLoc in orientation error (1.11° vs. 0.87°). The gap is most dramatic on the challenging NCLT trajectory 2012-05-26, where LEADER maintains 0.32 m accuracy while DiffLoc degrades to 1.88 m and LiSA to 3.30 m, reflecting the benefit of yaw-invariant encoding and reliability-based filtering in non-overlapping regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEADER extends the Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) paradigm for LiDAR relocalization established by SGLoc, RALoc, LiSA, and LightLoc, which decouple per-point coordinate prediction from RANSAC-based pose recovery. Its two key departures are architectural and loss-based: the Robust Projection-based Geometric Encoder repurposes cylindrical projection (inspired by Scan Context) combined with cyclic sparse convolution in a U-Net to achieve yaw-invariant representations, while the Truncated Relative Reliability loss introduces point-wise reliability weighting that has no direct analogue in prior SCR methods. Unlike APR methods (PointLoc, DiffLoc) that regress poses end-to-end, LEADER remains in the SCR family but addresses the specific failure modes of yaw sensitivity and degenerate-region correspondences that prior SCR work has not systematically tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEADER is the first implicit LiDAR relocalization method to achieve sub-0.5 m positional accuracy on NCLT, and its 73.9% error reduction over the prior best SCR method on that benchmark is substantial. The reliability-aware inference strategy (top-k filtering before RANSAC) offers a practical mechanism for handling dynamic objects and textureless regions that is broadly applicable to other correspondence-based localization tasks. However, the method still depends on ground-plane detection (Patchwork++) to correct pitch and roll, limiting its generality; the authors explicitly flag full SE(3) relocalization without ground-plane assumptions as future work. The reliance on only two datasets (one of which is small-scale) and the modest orientation-error gains leave open questions about whether the cylindrical-projection invariance generalizes to more extreme viewpoint changes or indoor environments.
