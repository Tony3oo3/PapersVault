## Problem

Long-video VLM inference is bottlenecked by the O(N²) prefill cost, where a single hour-long video can generate over 2 million visual tokens. Existing remedies force an accuracy-latency trade-off: naive frame sparsification loses critical temporal-spatial context, while naive parallelism (e.g., tensor parallelism) incurs prohibitive cross-GPU communication and memory overhead. The paper argues this trade-off is unbreakable unless sparsification (what to compute) and parallelism (how to compute it) are co-designed as a unified optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a long video (≥4 minutes, up to 140 minutes in LVBench) and a free-form text query, and produces a text answer. The video is first decomposed into semantically cohesive scenes via PySceneDetect; a CLIP model scores each scene's relevance to the query, and a pixel-difference metric measures intra-scene temporal redundancy. The total sampled frame budget M (32, 64, or 128 frames) is allocated non-uniformly across scenes, with at least one frame guaranteed per scene. Inference runs on 2 H100 GPUs connected via NVLink, with the CPU-side CAS module pipelined against GPU-side prefill to hide preprocessing latency.

## Method

SegMo co-designs two modules driven by the empirical observation of Local Cohesion in VLM attention (dense intra-scene, sparse inter-scene). Content-Aware Sparsification (CAS) computes a per-scene Information Value V = w·RL + (1−w)·RD, where RL is a CLIP-based query-relevance score and RD is a grayscale pixel-difference redundancy measure; the frame budget is then allocated proportionally to V, concentrating frames on relevant, dynamic scenes. Locally-Cohesive Segment Parallelism (LSP) partitions the video at scene boundaries and co-locates each scene's frames on a single GPU, eliminating all cross-GPU communication during prefill. A lightweight Global Context Injection mechanism prepends head-frames from the top log₂M most relevant scenes to each GPU shard to recover lost global context at near-zero cost. A hardware-aware greedy partitioner assigns indivisible scene units to GPUs to balance workload against per-GPU capacity, minimizing the makespan. The full pipeline is further optimized with asynchronous CPU-GPU pipelining that overlaps scene detection and frame sampling for the next request with prefill/decode of the current request.

## Results

SegMo achieves a peak accuracy improvement of 12.00% over the uniform-sampling baseline on LVBench (Qwen2-VL-7B, 32 frames, CAS-only: 44.44 vs. 32.44) and a peak prefill speedup of 3.55× on LongVideoBench (32 frames: 0.701 s vs. 2.490 s TTFT). With both CAS and LSP enabled, SegMo still maintains a significant accuracy gain of up to 8.31% (LVBench, Qwen2-VL, 32 frames) while delivering 2.83–3.55× prefill acceleration across all tested settings. Gains are most pronounced on ultra-long videos (LVBench, 30–140 min), where uniform sampling struggles to capture query-relevant content.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is a straightforward combination of uniform frame sampling and 2-GPU data parallelism (no content-aware sparsification, no segment-level parallelism). SegMo (CAS-only) wins on every benchmark and model configuration, with the largest margin on LVBench (+12.00% for Qwen2-VL-7B at 32 frames; +10.92% for MiniCPM-o 2.6 at 32 frames), where ultra-long 30–140 min videos expose the weakness of uniform sampling. When LSP is added (CAS+LSP), accuracy drops slightly relative to CAS-only (e.g., 40.75 vs. 44.44 on LVBench) due to the loss of cross-segment attention, but still exceeds the baseline by 8.31% (Qwen2-VL) and 7.10% (MiniCPM-o 2.6) on LVBench. On latency, SegMo (CAS+LSP) achieves 2.83–3.55× TTFT speedup over the baseline across all four benchmark/frame-count settings, with the peak 3.55× on LongVideoBench (32 frames). The paper does not compare against other named sparsification or parallelism methods (e.g., AKS, FastVID, PEVLM, MERV) in the main results table; those are discussed qualitatively in Related Work.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously orthogonal research threads—sparse key-frame sampling for long-video VLMs (e.g., AKS, Q-Frame, FastVID) and distributed prefill acceleration (e.g., TP, SP, PEVLM, MERV)—into a single co-design optimization problem. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes lightweight off-the-shelf tools (CLIP for relevance scoring, PySceneDetect for scene boundaries, pixel-difference for redundancy) in a novel hierarchical combination, and introduces the Locally-Cohesive Segment Parallelism paradigm that exploits an empirically observed attention locality property to eliminate cross-GPU communication during prefill. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the explicit coupling of the sparsification policy (what to compute) with the partitioning strategy (how to compute it), rather than treating them as independent knobs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing accuracy-latency trade-off in long-video VLM inference can be broken simultaneously (12% accuracy gain and 3.55× prefill speedup), which is practically significant for deploying VLMs on hour-scale videos. However, the evaluation is limited to 2 H100 GPUs and two 7-8B models, leaving open whether the local-cohesion assumption and the greedy partitioning strategy scale to larger clusters or more heterogeneous hardware. The GCI mechanism's fixed log₂M budget and reliance on a single CLIP model for relevance scoring are simplifications that may not generalize to all video domains or query types, and the approach has not been tested in streaming or real-time settings.
