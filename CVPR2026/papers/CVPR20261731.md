## Problem

Most VLA models assume a Markov property, predicting actions solely from the current observation and thus suffering from temporal myopia that fragments long-horizon trajectories. Existing remedies—stacking raw past frames or generating pixel-level future subgoals—introduce substantial computational overhead and pixel-level redundancy that obscures task-relevant dynamics. The paper asks whether a compact, structured representation of inter-state change (motion) can replace both historical frames and future pixel predictions to enable efficient bidirectional temporal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261731/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes the current RGB observation (encoded by both DINOv2 and SigLIP), a text instruction, and a historical window of MPEG-4 motion vectors (16×16 macroblock displacements, tensor shape h×(H//16)×(W//16)×2, default h=8) as the hindsight prior. It jointly outputs an n-step action chunk (n=8) and a corresponding n-step foresight motion sequence; during deployment the motion head is optional and can be dropped. The motion vectors are extracted offline via an MPEG-4 GOP structure where the current frame serves as the keyframe, making the historical input orders of magnitude smaller than stacked RGB frames.

## Method

HiF-VLA builds on the OpenVLA-OFT / Prismatic-7B backbone and adds three modules. (1) A lightweight ViT + 3D-convolution hindsight encoder compresses the historical motion-vector tensor into K_h compact tokens. (2) K_f learnable foresight queries and K_a empty action tokens are appended to the VLM input so the model reasons in parallel about future motion and actions, producing foresight motion tokens M_f and action latent tokens A_f. (3) A Hindsight-Modulated Joint Expert fuses M_f and A_f via non-causal cross-stream attention with separate FFNs, while the hindsight tokens are injected as an AdaLN conditioning vector (not into the VLM, to preserve vision-language alignment), modulating both streams at every layer. Training uses a combined L1 loss on actions and motion (λ=0.01) over 150k steps on LIBERO and 80k on CALVIN, with all new modules randomly initialized on top of the pretrained OpenVLA weights.

## Results

On LIBERO-Long, HiF-VLA achieves 94.4% average success rate (third-view) and 96.4% (multi-view), surpassing the main baseline OpenVLA-OFT by 3.4 and 2.4 percentage points respectively. On CALVIN ABC-D, HiF-VLA reaches an average task length of 4.08 (third-view) and 4.35 (multi-view), outperforming the strongest baselines (VPP at 4.33 multi-view, Seer at 4.28). In terms of efficiency, HiF-VLA adds only 1.05× peak GPU memory and 1.67× inference latency over the baseline, compared to 2.06× memory and 3.15× latency for frame-stacking approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiF-VLA outperforms all 14 compared baselines on both benchmarks. On LIBERO-Long (third-view), it beats the closest competitor MemoryVLA [29] by 1.0 pp (94.4% vs 93.4%) and the main baseline OpenVLA-OFT [18] by 3.4 pp (94.4% vs 91.0%), while older methods like OpenVLA [17] (54.0%) and UniVLA [22] (63.0%) trail by 30+ pp. On LIBERO-Long (multi-view), HiF-VLA leads OpenVLA-OFT [18] by 2.4 pp (96.4% vs 94.0%) and Seer [33] by 8.7 pp (96.4% vs 87.7%). On CALVIN ABC-D (multi-view), HiF-VLA achieves 4.35 average task length, narrowly edging out VPP [12] (4.33), Seer [33] (4.28), RoboVLMs [24] (4.25), and OpenVLA-OFT [18] (4.10), while substantially surpassing π_0 [3] (3.92), UP-VLA [42] (4.08), VidMan [37] (3.42), and GR-1 [39] (3.06). On CALVIN third-view, HiF-VLA (4.08) leads UniVLA [6] (3.80), π_0 [3] (3.65), VPP [12] (3.58), CLOVER [5] (3.53), OpenVLA [17] (3.27), and SuSIE [4] (2.69). Notably, HiF-VLA's third-view variant (94.4%) matches or exceeds multi-view baselines, demonstrating that motion-based temporal reasoning compensates for the absence of a wrist camera.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiF-VLA builds directly on OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) as its base VLA architecture, inheriting the Prismatic-7B backbone and DINOv2/SigLIP dual visual encoders, and adopts the motion vector representation from Video-LaVIT (Jin et al., ICML 2024) as its core temporal primitive. It departs from prior temporal modeling in robotics—frame stacking (Octo, GR-2, RoboVLMs) and pixel-level subgoal prediction (CoT-VLA, Seer, UP-VLA)—by replacing both with compact, structured motion vectors that serve bidirectionally as hindsight priors and foresight targets. The Hindsight-Modulated Joint Expert, which conditions the action decoder via AdaLN rather than injecting motion tokens into the VLM, is a deliberate architectural departure motivated by the finding that direct VLM injection disrupts pretrained visual-language alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical and efficient solution to temporal myopia in VLAs: motion vectors expand the temporal receptive field at negligible inference cost (1.67× latency vs. 3.15× for frame stacking) while improving long-horizon success rates, making bidirectional temporal reasoning viable for real-time robotic control. The real-world validation on the AgileX Piper arm, particularly the Press-Buttons-Order task where subtle state transitions defeat frame-based baselines, underscores the practical relevance. Key limitations include sensitivity of motion estimation to noise in highly dynamic scenes and the absence of large-scale pretraining on internet video for motion understanding, which the authors flag as future work. An open question is whether the MPEG-4 16×16 macroblock granularity is optimal across all manipulation scales or whether learned, task-adaptive motion tokenization could further improve fidelity.
