## Problem

Existing visual grounding benchmarks (RefCOCO/+/g) target objects occupying ~19% of the image area, leaving small objects (~1–2% area) unaddressed despite their practical importance in cluttered scenes. Two gaps remain: no large-scale benchmark exists for small-object visual grounding, and standard VG architectures fail on small targets because their weak visual features are diluted during deep network propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image resized to 518×518 and a natural-language referring expression (average 8.67 words, roughly 2.5× longer than classic VG expressions due to the need for disambiguating small, similar objects). It jointly outputs a 2D bounding box (REC head) and a pixel-level binary segmentation mask (RES head) for the referred small object, whose average area is only 1.60% of the image—about 1/50 of the image versus ~1/5 in classic VG datasets.

## Method

SoVG-Net is a dual-encoder transformer (BERT for text, DINOv2 for vision) whose key innovation is a Hierarchical Textual Infusion (HTI) module inserted at four deep layers (15, 18, 21, 24) of the visual encoder. At each insertion point, a unidirectional cross-attention lets visual tokens query the projected text features, and the result is merged back via a residual connection and layer normalization, progressively re-injecting textual semantics to counteract feature dilution of small objects. A cross-modal decoder with a learnable [SOG] token then fuses the enhanced visual features with the original text, feeding two heads: a two-layer MLP for box regression (L1 + GIoU loss) and a convolutional-sigmoid path for mask prediction (Focal + Dice loss). The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW under a multi-task objective, and the RefCOCOs benchmark (323K expressions over 72K small-object instances) is constructed via an MLLM-based pipeline (GPT-4o + Qwen2.5-VL verification) on COCO images with area < 100×100 px.

## Results

On the proposed RefCOCOs benchmark, SoVG-Net achieves 72.69% Acc@0.5 and 60.85% mIoU on the validation split, and 72.33% Acc@0.5 / 60.40% mIoU on testB, outperforming the strongest baseline EEVG by ~5.6% in Acc@0.5 and ~6.1% in mIoU on testB. Without training on RefCOCOs, all baselines (TransVG, HiVG, EEVG) drop to below 14% Acc@0.5, confirming the dataset's necessity. On classic datasets, SoVG-Net achieves 89.46% Acc on RefCOCO val (REC) and 79.54% cIoU on RefCOCO val (RES), matching or exceeding prior SOTA methods like EEVG and HiVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the novel RefCOCOs benchmark, SoVG-Net decisively outperforms all reproduced baselines: it beats EEVG (the strongest multi-task competitor) by 5.57% in Acc@0.5 and 6.10% in mIoU on testB, and surpasses HiVG by 6.99% in Acc@0.5 on testB. The gap is even more dramatic without RefCOCOs training, where all methods collapse to ~11–14% Acc@0.5, highlighting that existing VG models are essentially blind to small-object grounding. On classic RefCOCO/+/g datasets (Table 3), SoVG-Net achieves SOTA or near-SOTA: it leads in REC on RefCOCO val (89.46% vs. EEVG's 88.08%) and RefCOCOg val (81.54% vs. EEVG's 79.60%), and in RES on RefCOCO val (79.54% vs. EEVG's 78.23%) and RefCOCOg val (73.12% vs. EEVG's 69.15%). On RefCOCO+ REC testB, SoVG-Net (69.77%) slightly trails EEVG (69.15%) by a narrow margin, and on RefCOCO RES testB it scores 74.22% vs. EEVG's 76.58%, indicating a small trade-off on the hardest classic split. Overall, the paper demonstrates that training on small-object data does not degrade general VG capability and in several cases improves it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within visual grounding by targeting objects at roughly 1/50 image area, where classic RefCOCO-series datasets (averaging ~19% area) provide essentially no coverage. It departs from Small Object Detection, which is a category-level recognition task, by requiring instance-level disambiguation via natural language. The dataset construction repurposes MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL) as automatic annotators on COCO, a pipeline not previously applied to VG benchmarking. Architecturally, SoVG-Net follows the standard dual-encoder + cross-modal decoder paradigm (DINOv2 + BERT + transformer decoder) but introduces the HTI module, which injects text-conditioned cross-attention at multiple layers of the visual backbone rather than only at the fusion stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark fills a clear and previously unaddressed gap: existing VG methods drop from ~65–90% Acc@0.5 on classic datasets to ~13% on RefCOCOs, revealing that current models have virtually no small-object grounding capability. The automatic MLLM-based pipeline makes large-scale small-object VG annotation practical without expensive human labeling, and the 323K-expression dataset is the largest in the VG family. Open questions include whether human-annotated expressions (rather than MLLM-generated) would shift the difficulty profile, and whether the findings extend to video grounding. The paper also notes that the MLLM-generated expressions may not fully capture the diversity of natural human language, a limitation the authors flag for future work.
