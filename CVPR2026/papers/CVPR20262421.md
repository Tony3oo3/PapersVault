## Problem

Personalized video generation from a single reference image faces a persistent trade-off: methods that achieve high identity resemblance (e.g., SkyReels-A2) rely on copy-paste mechanisms that degrade prompt following and introduce visual artifacts, while methods with strong semantic alignment (e.g., Phantom) sacrifice facial fidelity. Achieving temporally coherent identity preservation from limited references under varying viewpoints and lighting remains an open problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262421/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image of a person and a natural-language text prompt as input, and produces a temporally coherent video in which the subject's identity is preserved while the scene, motion, and lighting follow the prompt. The reference image is consumed in two parallel pathways: a 512-d ArcFace embedding for identity conditioning and dense VAE latent features for spatial detail. Training data spans heterogeneous aspect ratios and temporal lengths, handled via a frame-packing mechanism rather than fixed-resolution bucketing.

## Method

Lynx builds on the Wan2.1-14B DiT video foundation model and introduces two lightweight adapters injected via cross-attention at every transformer layer. The ID-adapter converts an ArcFace facial embedding into 16 identity tokens (dim 5120) through a Perceiver Resampler, concatenated with 16 register tokens, providing a compact identity representation. The Ref-adapter processes the reference image through a frozen copy of the base model (noise level 0, text prompt 'image of a face') to extract intermediate activations from all DiT blocks, injecting dense spatial details that complement the compact identity tokens. Training follows a progressive curriculum—image pretraining (40k iterations, with the Resampler initialized from InstantID) followed by video training (60k iterations)—using NaViT-style spatio-temporal frame packing with 3D-RoPE to batch heterogeneous inputs. A data pipeline of 50.2M person-text-video pairs is constructed with expression augmentation (X-Nemo) and portrait relighting (LBM) to improve robustness, followed by identity-verification filtering.

## Results

On a benchmark of 800 test cases (40 subjects × 20 prompts), Lynx achieves the highest face resemblance scores across all three evaluators: 0.779 (facexlib), 0.699 (insightface), and 0.781 (in-house), outperforming the second-best SkyReels-A2 (0.715/0.678/0.725) by 6–9 points. On the Gemini-2.5-Pro quality pipeline, Lynx leads in prompt following (0.722), aesthetic quality (0.871), and overall video quality (0.956), while VACE edges it out on motion naturalness (0.851 vs. 0.837). The ablation confirms both adapters are necessary: removing the Ref-adapter drops prompt following from 0.722 to 0.624, and removing the ID-adapter collapses identity resemblance from 0.699 to 0.523.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lynx outperforms all five baselines on face resemblance, with the largest gap over SkyReels-A2 (0.779 vs. 0.715 on facexlib) and MAGREF (0.779 vs. 0.575). On prompt following, Lynx (0.722) beats VACE (0.691) and Phantom (0.690), while SkyReels-A2 trails badly at 0.471 due to its copy-paste generation mechanism. VACE is the only method that surpasses Lynx on motion naturalness (0.851 vs. 0.837), reflecting its stronger temporal modeling, but Lynx compensates with the highest overall video quality (0.956 vs. 0.935). Phantom achieves competitive prompt alignment (0.690) but sacrifices identity fidelity (0.664/0.659/0.689), illustrating the resemblance–controllability trade-off that Lynx resolves. Stand-In and MAGREF lag behind on both identity and quality metrics, with MAGREF scoring lowest on face resemblance across all three evaluators.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lynx extends the image-domain identity-preservation paradigm (IP-Adapter, InstantID) to the video setting by transplanting the Perceiver Resampler + ArcFace embedding pipeline into a DiT-based video foundation model (Wan2.1). It departs from concurrent personalized video methods (SkyReels-A2, VACE, Phantom) that concatenate reference latents with noisy tokens or use copy-paste mechanisms, instead introducing two complementary cross-attention adapters: a compact ID-adapter for global identity and a Ref-adapter that extracts dense VAE features through a frozen copy of the backbone (inspired by ReferenceNet/Animate Anyone). The training strategy borrows NaViT-style spatio-temporal frame packing to handle heterogeneous video/image batches, and the progressive image-then-video curriculum is a practical adaptation rather than a conceptual departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lynx demonstrates that a frozen DiT backbone plus two lightweight adapters can achieve state-of-the-art identity fidelity without per-subject fine-tuning, making personalized video generation more scalable and practical. However, the paper acknowledges that identity injection can produce physically unreasonable dynamics (e.g., a train reversing before moving forward, a violin bow not touching strings), suggesting that regularization or physics-aware losses remain an open challenge. Future directions include multi-subject personalization, cross-modal (audio-visual) generation, and controllable motion editing, all of which are currently out of scope. The reliance on Gemini-2.5-Pro as an automated judge for prompt following and video quality, while scalable, raises questions about evaluation robustness compared to human studies.
