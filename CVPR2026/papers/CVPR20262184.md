## Problem

Most existing LVLM methods refine visual representations in the spatial domain, where fine-grained local details and high-level global context are implicitly entangled. This lack of explicit frequency-band separation hinders fine-grained control of visual signals and complicates hierarchical alignment with the natural structure of language, which ranges from global semantics to local refinements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262184/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (typically 336×336 or 384×384) and a text prompt. The image is encoded by a frozen or lightly-tuned ViT-based vision encoder, and the text by a CLIP or model-specific text encoder. The output is a free-form text response generated by the LLM; the only trainable component is a two-layer MLP projector that maps the modulated visual tokens into the LLM's embedding space, adding no parameters to the frequency-domain operations themselves.

## Method

LFM partitions the L layers of the vision encoder into K groups (default K=3), aggregates each group's intermediate features, and applies FFT to obtain frequency-domain representations. A language-guided emphasis map is computed via cosine similarity between each group's frequency spectrum and the linguistic feature vector, then applied as an element-wise multiplicative filter (with a group-specific coefficient α_k following a decreasing schedule) to selectively enhance or attenuate frequency bands. The modulated spectra are inverse-FFT'd back to the spatial domain, combined with the original layer features, concatenated across groups, and projected through a trainable MLP into the LLM. By the Convolution-Multiplication Theorem, this frequency-domain modulation is equivalent to a language-conditioned dynamic convolution in the spatial domain, providing an interpretable mechanism for balancing global and local visual signals without introducing any additional trainable parameters beyond the projector.

## Results

On the LLaVA framework with Vicuna-13B (0.5M+0.6M data, 384px), LFM achieves 76.2 on SQA, 74.8 on MMB, 1631 on MME, 43.2 on MM-Vet, 37.6 on MMMU, and 66.8 on GQA, consistently outperforming LLaVA-v1.5 (71.6/67.7/1531/36.1/36.4/63.3) across all six benchmarks. With the larger Mini-Gemini data (1.2M+1.5M), LFM (Vicuna-13B) reaches 80.7 SQA, 79.9 MMB, 1648 MME, 50.2 MM-Vet, 41.6 MMMU, and 89.8 LLaVA-W, surpassing the Dense Connector baseline (77.1/74.4/1579/47.8/37.2/88.9) on every metric. When plugged into Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LFM lifts MME from 2347.0 to 2385.3, MM-Vet from 67.1 to 69.8, MMStar from 63.9 to 66.1, and MathVista from 68.2 to 69.8, demonstrating consistent gains across SoTA LVLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LFM (Vicuna-13B, 0.5M+0.6M) outperforms LLaVA-v1.5 by +4.6 on SQA, +7.1 on MMB, +100 on MME, +7.1 on MM-Vet, +1.2 on MMMU, and +3.5 on GQA, confirming that frequency-domain modulation adds substantial value over the standard MLP projector. Against the strongest same-data baseline Dense Connector (Vicuna-13B, 1.2M+1.5M), LFM with the same data scale wins on all seven reported metrics (e.g., +3.6 SQA, +5.5 MMB, +69 MME, +2.4 MM-Vet, +4.4 MMMU, +1.1 LLaVA-W). With Llama3-8B, LFM surpasses LLAVA-LLAMA3 by +5.3 SQA, +8.1 MMB, +124 MME, and +8.0 MMMU, though it trails slightly on LLaVA-W (71.9 vs 73.6). When integrated into Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LFM improves over the base model on every benchmark (e.g., +38.3 MME, +2.7 MM-Vet, +2.2 MMStar, +1.6 MathVista), and similarly boosts InternVL2.5-8B by +32.5 MME and +1.5 MM-Vet. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that cosine modulation in the frequency domain (+2.18 MMStar, +3.18 MMB over no-interaction) substantially outperforms spatial-domain cross-attention (+1.18/+1.53) and spatial cosine modulation (+0.40/+1.50), validating the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual-refinement sub-problem in LVLMs (how to process vision-encoder features before the LLM) by moving the modulation from the spatial domain to the frequency domain. Prior approaches—linear projection (LLaVA), Q-former (BLIP-2), Dense Connector, and visual prompting (API, CPT)—all operate implicitly in pixel/patch space, where global context and local details are entangled. LFM repurposes the Fourier transform as a parameter-free decomposition mechanism, pairing it with a cosine-similarity emphasis map derived from linguistic features, and applies it to grouped intermediate ViT layers. The grouped-layer strategy echoes Dense Connector's use of multi-depth features, but the frequency-domain modulation and its explicit hierarchical alignment with linguistic structure (low-frequency ↔ global semantics, high-frequency ↔ local modifiers) is a new conceptual framing not present in prior LVLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LFM offers a genuinely plug-and-play, parameter-free modulation that can be inserted into any existing LVLM pipeline, which lowers the barrier for practitioners to adopt frequency-aware visual processing. The consistent 1–3 point gains across 2B–34B LLMs and multiple vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, InternViT) suggest the frequency-domain perspective is a broadly applicable inductive bias rather than an architecture-specific trick. Open questions include whether richer frequency-domain operations (e.g., learned spectral filters, multi-scale band selection) could yield larger gains, whether the simple cosine-similarity emphasis map is near-optimal, and how the approach extends to video where temporal frequency components add another axis. A limitation is that the modulation remains a scalar per-patch gain, so it cannot reshape spatial structure—only re-weight existing signals.
