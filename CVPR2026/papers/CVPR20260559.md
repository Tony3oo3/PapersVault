## Problem

Existing aerial VLN methods exhibit poor generalization to unseen urban scenes, accumulate errors over long-range trajectories that cause UAVs to lose the goal, and inadequately model spatial continuity in open city environments. These limitations manifest as low success rates and high navigation errors, particularly when generalizing from seen to unseen scenes or navigating long distances with complex landmark-based instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260559/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent consumes a natural language target description, top-down RGB and depth images as real-time visual feedback, a dynamically constructed incremental navigation map (a C×H×W top-down grid encoding landmarks and trajectory history), and the UAV's 3D position plus yaw angle. The system produces a two-level output: the MacroPlanner emits the next waypoint sub-goal, while the MicroActor selects from a six-action discrete space (go_up, go_down, forward, turn_left, turn_right, stop). An episode is successful when the final position falls within 20 m of the ground-truth target on the CityNav benchmark (5,850 targets, 32,326 instruction-trajectory pairs after data correction).

## Method

HTNav uses a two-stage hybrid IL-RL paradigm: Stage 1 trains a multi-task goal predictor via imitation learning on expert trajectories while simultaneously learning a state-value function; Stage 2 fine-tunes the policy with PPO, initializing the critic from the Stage 1 value network to convert unguided exploration into guided exploration. The tiered decision mechanism decomposes navigation into a MacroPlanner that fuses map features, UAV pose, and the target description to generate sequential waypoints (avoiding local optima in long-range tasks) and a MicroActor that selects fine-grained discrete actions conditioned on real-time RGB observations and the current sub-goal. A dedicated map representation module encodes the navigation map through residual convolutional blocks that preserve fine-grained spatial details, followed by an SCConv module that adaptively suppresses redundant features across spatial and channel dimensions. The reward function combines distance reduction, heading alignment, goal proximity, and step penalty terms, with the RL loss weighted by λ_RL (optimal at 0.20) to balance exploration against the imitation baseline.

## Results

On the revised CityNav benchmark (Test Unseen split), HTNav achieves NE = 40.3 m, SR = 25.49%, OSR = 48.82%, and SPL = 21.56, setting new state-of-the-art across all four metrics. Compared to the MGP baseline, the success rate more than doubles (9.70% → 25.49%) and navigation error is nearly halved (82.6 m → 40.3 m). On Validation Seen, HTNav reaches SR = 31.05% and NE = 37.5 m, and on Validation Unseen it achieves SR = 17.69% and NE = 49.8 m, consistently outperforming all baselines at every scene level and difficulty tier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HTNav outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three evaluation splits. Against the closest competitor FlightGPT* (a VLM-based method), HTNav reduces NE from 61.4 m to 40.3 m (−34%) on Test Unseen while achieving a higher SR (25.49% vs. 24.47%) and OSR (48.82% vs. 42.17%). Against the MGP baseline (the direct architectural predecessor), HTNav more than doubles SR (9.70% → 25.49%) and nearly halves NE (82.6 → 40.3 m) on Test Unseen. The earlier Seq2Seq+GSM and AerialVLN+GSM baselines are substantially outperformed, with SR gaps of over 21 percentage points on Test Unseen. Notably, even HTNav* trails human performance by a wide margin (SR 25.49% vs. 87.86%), indicating that spatial reasoning and dynamic decision-making remain open challenges.

## Relation to Prior Work

HTNav extends the aerial VLN line initiated by AerialVLN and formalized at city scale by CityNav/MGP, which rely on imitation learning over expert trajectories and a Geographic Semantic Map for spatial grounding. It departs from this purely imitative paradigm by introducing a staged IL-RL training pipeline (PPO fine-tuning initialized from an IL value function) and a two-level MacroPlanner/MicroActor architecture that decomposes long-range path planning from reactive local control. The map representation module, combining residual convolutions with SCConv for spatial-channel redundancy suppression, is a targeted architectural addition not present in prior aerial VLN systems like FlightGPT or GeoNav, which instead lean on vision-language models for subgoal decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework narrows the gap between learned aerial navigation and human performance on CityNav (SR 25.49% vs. 87.86% on Test Unseen), but the remaining ~60-point gap signals that spatial reasoning and long-horizon planning in open urban environments remain far from solved. The dataset revision (~800 landmark corrections, 311 non-navigable trajectories removed) is a practical contribution that benefits all future work on CityNav, and the ablation confirming that the landmark map is indispensable while target/surroundings maps are dispensable simplifies the pipeline for subsequent researchers. Open questions include whether the hybrid IL-RL recipe and tiered decomposition transfer to 3D simulation benchmarks (OpenUAV, OpenFLY) or to multi-agent and dynamic-traffic settings, and whether the PPO weight sensitivity (optimal at λ_RL = 0.20) reflects a general property of aerial VLN or is dataset-specific.
