## Problem

Medical VQA faces a dual bottleneck: high-quality annotated data is scarce for cross-modal clinical tasks, and existing large vision-language models are too computationally expensive for resource-constrained clinical deployment. Lightweight models that could fill this gap have not been designed with mechanisms to capture the global structural and local spatial cues that medical images demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260740/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single medical image (across 9 modalities including MRI, CT, X-ray, ultrasound, OCT, endoscopy, etc.) and a natural-language question as input, producing a free-text answer. Questions span four clinical aspects—imaging features, anatomical structures, pathological manifestations, and clinical implications—and come in both open-ended and multiple-choice formats. The visual backbone (SigLIP2-so400m) is frozen and its token count is reduced via modality projection before feeding into the 360M-parameter SmolLM2 language model.

## Method

MedFG-VQA introduces two lightweight modules between a frozen vision encoder and a small LLM. FreqMemoryFusion (FMF) applies DCT to decompose visual features into low- and high-frequency components, uses the low-frequency part as a query to retrieve top-k entries from a learnable memory bank (regularized by a diversity loss), fuses them via a weighted residual, and reconstructs the spatial feature through IDCT followed by a gated residual connection. Graph-Aware Cross-Attention (GACA) first performs multi-head cross-attention with image patches as queries and text tokens as keys/values for global semantic alignment, then builds a dynamic KNN graph over the resulting patch features and applies a single GCN layer to aggregate local spatial context, finally combining the global and local representations through a learned sigmoid gate. The full model (795M parameters) is trained for 2 epochs on the newly constructed SynMedVQA dataset (2.059M GPT-4o-generated Q&A pairs across 11 source datasets) with a combined cross-entropy and memory-diversity loss.

## Results

On the SynMedVQA benchmark (2.059M Q&A pairs across 11 medical imaging datasets), MedFG-VQA (795M params) achieves an average accuracy of 0.6441, surpassing the next-best model Qwen3-VL (4B) by approximately 9.5 percentage points. On public medical VQA benchmarks, the model attains the highest open-ended accuracy on SLAKE (0.9595) and PathVQA (0.8062), while remaining competitive on closed-ended tasks despite having 5–10× fewer parameters than baselines. The model's strongest per-dataset gains appear on OCTMNIST (0.7056), DermaMNIST (0.7225), and LungColon (0.6493), where complex anatomical or histopathological reasoning is required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedFG-VQA (795M) decisively outperforms all five baselines on the SynMedVQA benchmark, achieving 0.6441 average accuracy versus Qwen3-VL's 0.5492 (4B), MiniCPM-V 4.0's 0.4964 (4B), InternVL3.5's 0.4846 (1B), LLaVA-Med's 0.4834 (7B), and Gemma3's 0.4590 (4B) — a 9.5-point margin over the strongest competitor despite having 5–10× fewer parameters. On public benchmarks, the model wins all open-ended evaluations: SLAKE open (0.9595 vs. Qwen3-VL 0.9006), VQA-RAD open (0.7865 vs. MiniCPM-V 0.7978, a near-tie), and PathVQA open (0.8062 vs. Qwen3-VL 0.5249, a 28-point margin). On closed-ended tasks, larger models retain an edge — Qwen3-VL leads on VQA-RAD closed (0.7868 vs. 0.6324) and Gemma3 on PathVQA closed (0.8410 vs. 0.6694) — which the authors attribute to the baselines' substantially larger pretraining corpora. LLaVA-Med performs poorly on open-ended questions (e.g., 0.1232 on SLAKE) because it does not follow the multiple-choice instruction format, making it an unreliable open-ended baseline. Overall, the paper demonstrates that a sub-1B model with frequency-domain memory and graph-structured cross-attention can match or exceed 4–7B VLMs on medical VQA, particularly for open-ended reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of lightweight vision-language models (SmolVLM, MobileVLM) and medical VQA (LLaVA-Med, BiomedCoOp), but departs from both lines by introducing frequency-domain feature processing (DCT decomposition with a learnable memory bank) and KNN graph convolution as explicit architectural components for cross-modal alignment. Unlike prior medical VQA work that relies on large pretrained VLMs fine-tuned on limited data, MedFG-VQA targets the sub-1B parameter regime and compensates with structured inductive biases (global low-frequency priors, local topological aggregation) rather than raw model scale. The synthetic data generation pipeline via GPT-4o follows the LLaVA-Med recipe but scales it to 2M+ pairs across 11 datasets, addressing the data-scarcity bottleneck that has constrained small-model medical VQA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 795M-parameter model can match or exceed 4B+ VLMs on medical VQA benchmarks, which is directly relevant to resource-constrained clinical deployment where large models are infeasible. The SynMedVQA dataset (2.059M Q&A pairs, 9 modalities, 10 organs) is a substantial open resource that could benefit the broader medical VQA community. Key limitations include the reliance on single-view images (clinical practice often requires multi-view reasoning), the inherent quality ceiling imposed by GPT-4o generation, and the fact that the main comparison uses zero-shot baselines while the proposed model is trained on SynMedVQA, making the 9.5% gap partly attributable to data exposure rather than architecture alone. Open questions include whether the frequency-memory and graph-attention components transfer to 3D volumetric imaging or multi-modal fusion settings.
