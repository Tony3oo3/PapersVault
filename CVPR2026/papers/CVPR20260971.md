## Problem

Stereo video inpainting suffers from two compounding issues: (1) existing data construction pipelines rely on forward warping, which produces scattered pixels and misaligned artifacts at object edges, degrading both training data quality and inpainting performance; and (2) all prior diffusion-based methods process every pixel uniformly despite occluded regions occupying only a small fraction of each frame, making real-time HD video processing infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260971/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video (e.g., 81 frames at 768×1280) along with a depth/disparity estimate and produces the corresponding warped stereo view with occluded regions filled in, plus a geometrically consistent occlusion mask. The occluded regions are scattered along object boundaries and typically cover less than 30% of pixels per frame. At inference, the model runs single-step (NFE=1) diffusion at ~25 FPS on a single A100 GPU, and an optional blended variant fuses the inpainted result with the warped input using the GAPW-derived mask for further perceptual gains.

## Method

The approach comprises three tightly coupled components. First, Gradient-Aware Parallax Warping (GAPW) replaces forward warping with backward warping and computes the Jacobian of the coordinate mapping to derive smooth, continuous occlusion masks, eliminating the scattered-pixel artifacts of prior methods. Second, Parallax-Based Dual Projection (PBDP) warps a monocular video to a virtual second view via GAPW, then warps back to recover the occlusion mask in the original view, yielding high-quality stereo inpainting training pairs from monocular video alone without any stereo ground truth. Third, Sparsity-Aware Stereo Inpainting (SASI) builds on Wan2.1-1.3B (a DiT with flow-matching objective and a 3D VAE) and, at inference, dilates the occlusion mask to select only ~25–35% of visual tokens for DiT computation, reducing attention cost from O(N²) and achieving a 10.7× speedup with negligible quality loss; training remains fully dense, and the sparsity is applied purely as an inference-time acceleration.

## Results

On HD-100 (768×1280), DreamStereo achieves a PSNR of 30.48 dB (32.65 dB with blending) at a single NFE with a latency of 40.1 ms/frame (~25 FPS) on a single A100 GPU, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. On the Dynamic Replica and SVD AVP datasets for 2D-to-3D conversion, the method reaches PSNR 29.12/24.88 (blended: 29.17/25.30), surpassing the best prior methods by 5–6 dB. The sparsity-aware token pruning retains only ~25–35% of visual tokens, yielding a 10.7× DiT inference speedup with negligible quality loss (PSNR drop < 0.5 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamStereo decisively outperforms all five baselines on the primary HD-100 stereo inpainting benchmark. ProPainter (ICCV 2023) achieves the second-best PSNR (28.30 dB) but is 16.7× slower (668 ms vs. 40 ms) and produces noticeably blurry outputs; VACE-1.3B (arXiv 2025) lags further at 25.38 dB with a 69× latency penalty. ZeroStereo (arXiv 2025) exhibits strong NFE sensitivity, dropping from 24.73 dB at 50 steps to 19.12 dB at 8 steps, making it impractical for real-time use. StereoCrafter (arXiv 2024) reaches 23.99 dB at 8 NFEs but suffers from scattered edge artifacts in its forward-warping masks, degrading geometric consistency. On the 2D-to-3D conversion task (Table 2), Deep3D (ECCV 2016) scores only 17.11/19.32 dB on Dynamic Replica/SVD, while DreamStereo achieves 29.12/24.88 dB, a 12+ dB improvement, confirming that the GAPW-based warping and sparsity-aware inpainting generalize well beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the warp-and-inpaint paradigm for stereo video generation (StereoCrafter, TrajectoryCrafter, ImmersePro) by replacing forward warping with a backward-warping approach that uses the Jacobian of the coordinate mapping to produce cleaner occlusion masks (GAPW). It departs from prior data-construction pipelines that rely on expensive stereo video collections by generating pseudo-stereo inpainting pairs from monocular videos via dual projection (PBDP). The sparsity-aware token pruning (SASI) is a novel efficiency contribution not present in any prior diffusion-based stereo inpainting method, building on the DiT architecture of Wan2.1-1.3B with LoRA fine-tuning and a flow-matching training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving real-time (25 FPS) HD stereo inpainting on a single A100 GPU removes a major practical bottleneck for AR/VR content creation, where monocular-to-stereo conversion is needed at scale. The monocular-video-based data construction eliminates the cost and inconsistency of stereo video collection, making the pipeline more accessible. Limitations include dependence on external depth estimation (DepthCrafter), the fact that sparsity is applied only at inference (training remains dense), and the need for a blended variant to reach best quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to very long videos, larger camera baselines beyond the tested range, or multi-view (beyond binocular) settings.
