key_contribution: A training-free token merging method (SAD-TM) for Vision Transformers that fuses offline saliency outlier scores (Pearson correlation of gradient-based saliency maps) with online class attention to identify cross-layer-consistent redundant tokens, achieving near-baseline ImageNet accuracy with 23–45% FLOPs reduction across DeiT, MAE, and LV-ViT architectures without any retraining.
limitations: The method is evaluated exclusively on ImageNet image classification with standard ViT architectures; generalization to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) or non-classification ViT variants is not explored. ## Problem

Vision Transformers suffer from quadratic computational complexity in the number of tokens, and existing token merging methods select tokens to merge based solely on attention parameters (e.g., key matrices, class attention) that vary substantially across layers. This layer-local criterion lacks a global, cross-layer consistent signal for identifying truly redundant tokens, leading to suboptimal merging decisions especially in early blocks where attention patterns are unstable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263203/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (e.g., 224×224 for ImageNet) partitioned into N patches and produces a class label through the ViT's MLP head. Unusually, a saliency map for each input patch is precomputed offline via backward propagation of the classification loss, yielding a per-patch outlier score that is independent of any particular layer. During inference, the model dynamically computes class attention per block, and the two signals are fused to produce a merged (shorter) token sequence that feeds into subsequent FFN layers, ultimately reducing FLOPs while preserving the full token information through merging rather than discarding.

## Method

SAD-TM establishes a cross-layer consistent token importance criterion by combining two complementary signals: (1) an offline saliency outlier score computed from pairwise Pearson correlations between per-patch saliency vectors (derived from ∂L/∂x_i), which captures tokens that deviate from the global gradient direction and are thus safe to merge; and (2) an online class attention score averaged across all heads at each layer, which captures local semantic relevance. The final importance score is a weighted sum m_{l,i} = α·a'_{l,i} + β·o_i (α+β=1), and tokens with the lowest scores are merged into their most similar candidate via importance-weighted averaging. The deferred-merging variant (SAD-TM-DM) suspends merging in the first τ = ⌈ρL⌉ blocks (ρ∈[0.25,0.35]), motivated by empirical MSE analysis showing that class attention matrices change by orders of magnitude more between adjacent early blocks than later blocks, making early-layer merging unreliable. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only pre-trained ViT weights and an offline saliency computation pass.

## Results

On ImageNet with DeiT-Small, SAD-TM-DM achieves 79.78% Top-1 accuracy with only 28.26% FLOPs reduction (3.3G vs. 4.6G baseline), losing just 0.09% over the pretrained baseline. At a more aggressive 45.65% FLOPs reduction (2.5G), SAD-TM retains 79.15% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming all training-free baselines at that compute level. On DeiT-Tiny, SAD-TM achieves 72.02% Top-1 with 23.08% FLOPs reduction, a mere 0.15% drop from the 72.17% baseline. On the much larger MAE-Huge model, SAD-TM-DM reaches 86.42% Top-1 at 44.68% FLOPs reduction, surpassing ToMe (86.01%) and DiffRate (86.40%) at comparable compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAD-TM-DM consistently outperforms all training-free baselines at matched FLOPs levels: at 36.96% reduction it reaches 79.65% vs. DiffRate (79.58%) and ToMe (79.49%); at 43.48% reduction it hits 79.27% vs. EViT (78.90%) and SCOP (77.50%). At the most aggressive 45.65% FLOPs reduction, SAD-TM (79.15%) surpasses ToMe_MC (78.32%) and ATS (77.60%) by a substantial margin of 0.8–1.6%. On DeiT-Tiny, SAD-TM at 23.08% FLOPs reduction (72.02%) dramatically outperforms SViTE (70.10%) by nearly 2 points, and at 46.15% reduction SAD-TM-DM (71.15%) beats DTEM (70.70%) and HVT (69.70%). On MAE-Huge, SAD-TM-DM at 44.68% FLOPs reduction (86.42%) edges out ToMe (86.01%) and DiffRate (86.40%), while at 39.01% reduction it achieves 86.58%, the best accuracy among all compared methods. On LV-ViT-S, SAD-TM-DM (82.60% at 40.91% reduction) matches or exceeds EViT* (82.50%) and DiffRate (82.56%), and SAD-TM maintains 81.95–82.45% across 42.42–46.97% FLOPs reductions, outperforming ToMe (79.80%) and ATS (80.40%) at the same compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAD-TM builds directly on the token merging framework introduced by ToMe (Bolya et al., ICLR 2023), reusing its candidate-set/merging-set partitioning and importance-weighted averaging mechanism. However, it departs from the entire line of attention-only merging methods (ToMe, DTMFormer, KFF, PS-ViT) by introducing a cross-layer, input-output saliency criterion (Pearson correlation of gradient-based saliency maps) that is computed offline and is invariant to layer depth. The deferred merging strategy (SAD-TM-DM) is a novel scheduling mechanism motivated by the empirical observation that class attention MSE between adjacent blocks is orders of magnitude larger in early layers, a phenomenon not previously exploited for merging-rate allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of SAD-TM makes it immediately deployable on any pretrained ViT without retraining, which is practically valuable for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning large models. The cross-model transferability of saliency outlier scores (≤0.12% accuracy difference when swapping scores between DeiT-Tiny/Small/Base) suggests a reusable, architecture-agnostic prior that could simplify deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dense prediction tasks (segmentation, detection) where per-pixel saliency semantics differ, whether the offline saliency computation cost is acceptable for very large models, and whether the deferred merging threshold τ can be made fully adaptive rather than a fixed fraction of depth.
