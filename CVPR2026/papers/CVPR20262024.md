## Problem

Radiance field representations face a persistent tradeoff: fast-to-render methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting introduce popping artifacts from approximate sorting and are still slower than conventional mesh rendering, while high-quality neural fields are too slow for real-time use. Radiant Foam's Voronoi-based approach avoids popping but relies on ray-tracing complex convex polyhedra (averaging 15.5 faces per cell), making GPU rasterization prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether a tetrahedral mesh representation can achieve exact volume rendering at hardware-rasterizer speeds while remaining differentiable and editable.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed RGB images and a sparse COLMAP SfM point cloud as input, and produces a set of Delaunay tetrahedra each carrying a constant density, a base color encoded as spherical harmonics, and a linear color gradient. The tetrahedra are re-triangulated every 10 optimization iterations as the underlying 3D points move. Beyond novel-view rendering, the representation directly supports surface mesh extraction (by thresholding peak color contribution), physics-based simulation via xPBD on vertices, and Boolean editing, all without retraining.

## Method

The scene is represented as optimizable 3D points whose Delaunay tetrahedralization defines the volumetric cells; each tetrahedron's density and appearance are queried from an Instant-NGP hash grid using a Zip-NeRF-style downweighting function keyed to the tetrahedron's circumradius, which handles the topological discontinuities (edge flips) that arise when points move. Volume rendering is exact: each tetrahedron contributes a closed-form integral of its linearly-varying color under constant density, and tetrahedra are sorted front-to-back by the power of their circumspheres relative to the camera origin, a sort valid for any projection sharing a common ray origin (including fisheye). A novel hardware rasterization pipeline emits each tetrahedron as four individually-wound triangles and uses mesh shaders with warp-shuffle to load tetrahedron attributes only once per cell, eliminating the redundant per-vertex loads of instanced rendering. Densification uses two complementary criteria—SSIM-based error back-projection and total pixel-variance scoring—to select which tetrahedra to split, with new points placed at the approximate intersection of the two highest-error rays.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360 outdoor scenes, Radiance Meshes achieves 24.64 PSNR / 0.735 SSIM / 0.272 LPIPS at 240 FPS, matching 3DGS quality (24.63 PSNR) while rendering 66% faster (240 vs. 145 FPS) and 6.7× faster than EVER (240 vs. 36 FPS). On indoor scenes it reaches 30.31 PSNR at 384 FPS, and on Tanks&Temples+DeepBlending it achieves 26.53 PSNR at 475 FPS, where RadFoam fails catastrophically (19.20 PSNR). The method supports ~15M tetrahedra on an RTX 4090 versus ~3.3M for RadFoam, and achieves a 17% ray-tracing speedup over RadFoam on indoor scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS, Radiance Meshes matches outdoor quality (24.64 vs. 24.63 PSNR) and is slightly lower on indoor (30.31 vs. 31.05 PSNR) and T&T+DB (26.53 vs. 26.65 PSNR), but renders 66% faster on outdoor (240 vs. 145 FPS) and 53% faster on indoor (384 vs. 251 FPS) without the temporal popping artifacts inherent to splatting. Against EVER, quality is comparable (within 0.1 PSNR on outdoor) but rendering is 6.7× faster on outdoor (240 vs. 36 FPS), 5.8× faster on indoor (384 vs. 66 FPS), and 11.6× faster on T&T+DB (475 vs. 41 FPS). Against SVRaster, the method is faster across all splits (240 vs. 193, 384 vs. 137, 475 vs. 327 FPS) with comparable quality. Against RadFoam, the method wins decisively on quality—especially on T&T+DB where RadFoam collapses to 19.20 PSNR versus 26.53—and is faster on indoor (384 vs. 162 FPS) and T&T+DB (475 vs. 353 FPS), while also supporting ~4.5× more primitives in VRAM (15M vs. 3.3M tetrahedra on RTX 4090). The main trade-off is training time: 4.5 GPU-hours on outdoor scenes versus 0.61 for 3DGS, though this is offset by the method's exact volume rendering and hardware rasterization compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the classical Delaunay tetrahedralization—a structure long used in computational geometry and volume visualization—as the spatial backbone for a radiance field, in contrast to the Voronoi-based approach of Radiant Foam. It inherits the Instant-NGP hash grid and Zip-NeRF downweighting to parameterize per-tetrahedron attributes, and uses power sorting (Karasick et al.) to enable front-to-back rasterization. The key conceptual departure from 3DGS and other splatting methods is that each tetrahedron is rendered as a set of triangles with a closed-form volume integral, making the representation natively compatible with hardware triangle rasterizers and mesh shaders rather than requiring per-primitive sorting or approximate alpha compositing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving exact volume rendering at 32% higher frame rates than 3DGS on consumer hardware, this work narrows the gap between radiance field quality and the speed of traditional mesh rendering, while eliminating the temporal popping artifacts that plague splatting-based methods. The hardware compatibility (triangle rasterizer, mesh shaders, WebGPU) opens the door to integration with existing real-time graphics engines and enables downstream applications like physics simulation and mesh extraction that are awkward with Gaussian or ellipsoid primitives. A notable limitation is the significantly higher training cost (~4.5 GPU-hours vs. ~0.6 for 3DGS), and the approach's scalability to very large or unbounded scenes beyond the tested datasets remains to be demonstrated.
