## Problem

Multi-teacher distillation for agglomerative vision foundation models has shown promise in unifying complementary capabilities (e.g., image-text alignment and dense visual understanding) within a single backbone, but prior approaches like RADIOv2.5 require very large training budgets (~1.1T image tokens) and the learning dynamics—data efficiency, multi-resolution stability, and relational geometry preservation—remain underexplored. The paper asks whether a standardized, more efficient recipe can match or exceed prior agglomerative models with substantially less data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260206/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student ViT (dense or MoE) consumes RGB images at native resolution (256×256 to 768×768), packed into fixed-token-budget sequences via FlexAttention to prevent inter-image attention. It outputs a CLS global token, four register tokens, and patch-level tokens, each projected through teacher-specific MLP heads into SigLIP2 and DINOv3 embedding spaces. For the downstream grounding VLM, the input is an RGB image paired with a text expression, and the model produces bounding boxes (detection) or pixel-level segmentation masks (segmentation) via lightweight task heads over text-conditioned visual features.

## Method

SigLino distills a single MoE student (18-layer, 0.3B active / 0.6B total, 28 experts with 6 active) simultaneously from SigLIP2 (image-text alignment) and DINOv3 (dense features) using three key mechanisms: (1) Asymmetric Relational Knowledge Distillation (ARKD), which matches pairwise geometry within a batch but only penalizes one-sided errors—shrinking distances for pairs close in teacher space and expanding for pairs far, split at the intra-batch median—to accelerate alignment without degrading kNN clustering; (2) token-balanced batching that packs variable-resolution images into uniform token budgets with per-image loss normalization, stabilizing multi-resolution training and preventing low-resolution forgetting; and (3) a 200M-image OpenLVD200M corpus curated via 4-level hierarchical clustering and balanced sampling from a 2.3B LAION+DFN pool, yielding +4.15 average accuracy over random sampling. Training uses PHI-S normalization to equalize teacher feature scales, proceeds in two stages (256→768 resolution) over ~230B tokens (4.7× fewer than RADIOv2.5), and the distilled MoE initializes an early-fusion grounding VLM where Gram anchoring against frozen distillation weights preserves dense patch geometry during fine-tuning.

## Results

SigLino-0.6B (dense) achieves 84.40% macro-averaged image-text classification and 90.70% kNN classification at 512×512, outperforming RADIOv2.5-H (82.26% / 85.12%) while using only ~230B image tokens versus RADIO's 1.1T (4.7× fewer). The MoE variant (0.3B active / 0.6B total) reaches 84.13% / 88.06% at half the active parameters, and the ultra-sparse MoE (0.15B active) still attains 83.10% / 89.80%. On linear-probing segmentation, SigLino-0.6B leads with 65.38 mIoU on Cityscapes and 87.71 on PASCAL-VOC, surpassing RADIOv2.5-H (64.11 / 85.70). For retrieval, SigLino-0.6B achieves 55.60 Recall@1 on MSCOCO5k T2I and 81.90 on Flickr30k T2I, exceeding RADIOv2.5-H (53.24 / 80.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RADIOv2.5-H (0.6B, 1.1T tokens), SigLino-0.6B wins on every macro-averaged metric: +2.14 on image-text classification (84.40 vs. 82.26), +5.58 on kNN (90.70 vs. 85.12), +1.27 mIoU on Cityscapes (65.38 vs. 64.11), and +2.36 on MSCOCO5k T2I retrieval (55.60 vs. 53.24), all while consuming 4.7× fewer training tokens. The per-head ensembling gap is particularly large on fine-grained FGVC-Aircraft (82.40 vs. 70.26 for RADIO), where RADIO's heads show severe imbalance. Against TIPS (ViT-g, 1.1B, 0.46T tokens), SigLino-0.6B is substantially stronger on image-text classification (84.40 vs. 68.11) and kNN (90.70 vs. 84.50), despite TIPS having nearly double the parameters. The MoE variants trade a small amount of accuracy for efficiency: the 0.3B-active MoE retains 84.13/88.06 (within 0.27/2.64 of dense), and the ultra-sparse 0.15B-active MoE still exceeds RADIOv2.5-H on both averages (83.10/89.80 vs. 82.26/85.12). On grounding, SigLino initialization with Gram-anchoring more than doubles scratch-training performance on RefCOCO detection (61.06 vs. 29.15) and segmentation (63.38 vs. 23.64), and multi-teacher initialization outperforms single-teacher by +9.66 on RefCOCO RES (54.72 vs. 45.06 for DINOv3-only).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the multi-teacher distillation recipe established by AM-RADIO and RADIOv2.5, which distill complementary vision foundation models (e.g., CLIP + DINO) into a single student backbone. The key departures are: (1) replacing the dense student with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture to naturally accommodate modality-specific teacher signals, (2) introducing Asymmetric Relational Knowledge Distillation (ARKD) as a targeted fix for the kNN degradation observed with symmetric RKD in the DINOv3 head, and (3) transplanting hierarchical clustering-based data curation—previously used only in self-supervised learning (DINOv3's LVD-1.7B)—into the multi-teacher distillation setting to produce OpenLVD200M. The early-fusion grounding VLM initialization extends the agglomerative model's utility beyond standalone representation benchmarks into a decoder-only multimodal architecture, building on MoMa's modality-specific expert design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.7× reduction in training tokens (230B vs. 1.1T) over RADIOv2.5 at comparable or better accuracy makes agglomerative vision foundation models substantially more accessible, and the released OpenLVD200M dataset plus five checkpoints lower the barrier for the community. The finding that SSL-style hierarchical clustering transfers effectively to supervised multi-teacher distillation suggests a broader principle: concept-uniform data sampling may be a general efficiency lever across training paradigms, not just self-supervision. Open questions include whether the early-fusion grounding VLM approach scales to larger models and more complex tasks, whether the MoE architecture's advantage over dense students persists at higher parameter counts, and whether ARKD's asymmetric gating generalizes beyond the two-teacher setting to larger teacher ensembles.
