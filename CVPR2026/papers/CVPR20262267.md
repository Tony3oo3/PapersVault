## Problem

Existing OOD object detection methods rely on instance-level feature regularization (e.g., outlier synthesis) that struggles to truly learn the concept of 'unknown' without real OOD data, especially when ID and OOD objects coexist in the same scene context. These approaches lack the global abstraction and relational reasoning that humans use to perceive novelty, leading to poor localization of OOD objects and distorted ID/OOD decision boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a frozen, pre-trained object detector (Deformable DETR with ResNet-50 backbone); the UCD module is trained separately on ID data only (PASCAL VOC or BDD-100K) and requires no retraining of the detector. At inference, it produces a per-object OOD score that combines image-level reconstruction similarity with a quantile-based logit threshold, yielding a binary ID/OOD label for each detected bounding box. The slot representations are computed independently of the detector, making the adapter plug-and-play across different detection backbones.

## Method

UNO-Adapter injects the concept of unknown into a frozen detector via three components. First, Unsupervised Concept Discovery (UCD) extracts sparse, object-centric slots using a DINOSAUR-based slot attention framework, then applies relational regularization (a lightweight network predicts per-slot importance scores, and a learned relational matrix captures inter-slot interactions) and slot refinement via a variational information bottleneck with importance-weighted KL divergence to compress redundant information. Second, the Neural Concept Binder (NCB) adaptively allocates the detector's target queries to slots proportional to their normalized importance scores and fuses them via weighted addition, requiring no training and preserving each component's original feature space. Third, an image-guided OOD score multiplies a calibration factor (max sigmoid of instance logits), a cosine similarity between slot-decoder-reconstructed features and DINO backbone features, and a quantile-based logit threshold to produce the final per-object ID/OOD confidence.

## Results

On the primary OOD-OD benchmark (Table 1), UNO-Adapter achieves AUROC/FPR95 of 91.68/32.61 (PASCAL-VOC→MS-COCO), 95.40/19.90 (PASCAL-VOC→OpenImages), 97.61/9.88 (BDD-100K→MS-COCO), and 99.04/3.80 (BDD-100K→OpenImages). The headline improvement is an 11.96% FPR95 reduction over the previous best (SAFE+WFS) on BDD-100K→MS-COCO (9.88 vs. 21.84). On open-world object detection (Table 2), UNO-Adapter achieves the highest U-Recall (17.0%) and best mAP across all task splits. On ImageNet classification OOD detection (Table 4), UNO-Adapter reaches 87.90 AUROC / 50.42 FPR95 (near OOD) and 97.84 / 16.92 (far OOD) on ImageNet-200, outperforming the prior best OODD by 2.16 AUROC and 3.47 FPR95 on near OOD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNO-Adapter outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every OOD-OD evaluation setting. On BDD-100K→MS-COCO, it reduces FPR95 by 11.96% over SAFE+WFS (9.88 vs. 21.84) and by 12.79% over SyncOOD (9.88 vs. 22.67), while also achieving the highest AUROC (97.61). On PASCAL-VOC→MS-COCO, UNO-Adapter leads TIB (90.36 AUROC) and DFDD (90.79) by 1.32 and 0.89 AUROC points respectively, and cuts FPR95 from 41.34 (DFDD) to 32.61. For open-world object detection (Table 2), UNO-Adapter surpasses the strongest baseline ASGS by 0.5 U-Recall (17.0 vs. 16.5) and 1.1 mAP on 'Both' known classes (37.1 vs. 36.0) in Task 2. On ImageNet-200 classification OOD detection, UNO-Adapter beats OODD by 2.16 AUROC on near OOD (87.90 vs. 85.74) and by 3.47 FPR95 on far OOD (16.92 vs. 20.89). Notably, DFDD exhibits performance degeneration on ID data (e.g., 90.74 AUROC on BDD→COCO vs. 97.61 for UNO-Adapter), attributed to its training-time regularization pushing the decision boundary too aggressively toward ID samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on object-centric learning via slot attention [Locatello et al., NeurIPS 2020] and its real-world adaptation DINOSAUR [Seitzer et al., ICLR 2023], which reconstructs DINO features rather than raw pixels. It departs from the dominant OOD-OD paradigm—where methods like VOS, SIREN, and SAFE integrate instance-level regularization or feature synthesis directly into the detector's training pipeline—by proposing an external adapter (UNO-Adapter) that injects a 'concept of unknown' without modifying the detector's weights or architecture. The slot refinement step draws on the variational information bottleneck [Alemi et al., ICLR 2017], extended here with importance-weighted KL regularization and relational slot interactions, which prior slot-attention work did not address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adapter formulation is practically attractive: a pre-trained detector can be augmented for OOD awareness with only 0.1 hours of fine-tuning on the UCD module, compared to hours of retraining required by SIREN or SAFE. The 11.96% FPR95 reduction on BDD-100K/MS-COCO is a meaningful margin, and the demonstrated transfer to OWOD and image classification suggests broad applicability. However, the UCD module is trained exclusively on PASCAL-VOC and kept frozen across datasets, raising open questions about domain transferability and whether the slot codebook generalizes to substantially different visual domains. The reliance on DINO features as the backbone for both the detector and the slot module also couples the method's performance to the quality of that specific self-supervised representation.
