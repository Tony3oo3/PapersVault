## Problem

Existing unified image generation models are predominantly architected for single-input, single-output tasks, while video-based many-to-many approaches inherit motion priors from contiguous clips that rarely contain hard cuts, abrupt transitions, or large camera/subject motions. This limits their ability to generate highly dynamic multi-image outputs while preserving temporal and semantic consistency across the set.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a variable number of reference images (up to 4) and a single text instruction, producing a variable number of output images (up to 4) in one forward pass with 50 diffusion steps. Reference images are encoded independently by a 3D VAE and concatenated as clean tokens with noisy target tokens; the text interface uses a purely instruction-based format with interleaved <image_n> placeholders and a system-style preamble specifying the output count, requiring no masks or auxiliary visual embeddings. Input images may have different resolutions while output resolution is fixed per task.

## Method

iMontage repurposes the HunyuanVideo MMDiT backbone (initialized from its I2V checkpoint) into a many-to-many image generator by casting all input and output images as pseudo-frames along the temporal axis. The key architectural innovation is Marginal RoPE: a separable 3D rotary positional embedding that assigns input images to early temporal indices {0,…,7} and output images to late indices {24,…,31} out of 32 total, creating a wide head-tail gap that reduces positional interference and promotes diverse outputs while preserving the pretrained spatial RoPE and temporal coherence. Training follows a three-stage regimen—large-scale pretraining on 5M image-edit pairs and 15M motion-filtered video frame pairs, multi-task SFT using a difficulty-ordered CocktailMix schedule, and a brief high-quality annealing pass—all under flow matching with a constant 1e-5 learning rate on 64 H800 GPUs. A dedicated data curation pipeline applies optical-flow-based motion filtering and cross-segment re-clipping to inject high-dynamics supervision, while task-specific subsets (Multi CRef, SRef, Multi-View, Storyboard, Multi-Turn Editing) are assembled via VLM-assisted generation and scoring.

## Results

On the GEdit benchmark (one-to-one editing), iMontage achieves an overall score (G_O) of 6.94, the best among all open-source models, and a Motion Change G_O of 5.53, surpassing all open-source baselines. On ImgEdit, iMontage scores 4.11 average (4.48 on Action), outperforming the previous best open-source model Step1X-Edit (3.90) and approaching closed-source GPT-4o (4.30). On the OmniContext many-to-one benchmark, iMontage achieves 7.41 average, the best open-source result, edging out OmniGen2 (7.18) while trailing GPT-4o (8.80). On storyboard generation, iMontage leads all baselines in both identity preservation (DINO 0.585, CLIP 0.690) and temporal consistency (DINO 0.615, CLIP 0.745), with a VLM preference score of 9.556 for temporal consistency versus 7.889 for the next-best OmniGen2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iMontage outperforms all open-source baselines on ImgEdit average (4.11 vs. 3.97 for HiDream-E1.1 and Flux-Kontext-dev, 3.90 for Step1X-Edit) and on GEdit Motion Change overall (5.53 vs. 5.64 for HiDream-E1.1 which is slightly higher, but iMontage leads on GEdit Overall at 6.94 vs. 6.97 for Step1X-Edit which is marginally higher). On the OmniContext many-to-one benchmark, iMontage (7.41) surpasses OmniGen2 (7.18), BAGEL (5.73), UNO (4.71), and OmniGen (4.34), but remains below closed-source GPT-4o (8.80) and Gemini 2.5 (7.84). On storyboard generation, iMontage dominates all baselines: it beats UNO (DINO 0.519→0.585 identity, 0.479→0.615 temporal), OmniGen2 (0.486→0.585, 0.460→0.615), and StoryDiffusion (0.367→0.585, 0.440→0.615) on both DINO and CLIP feature similarity, with VLM preference scores of 7.909 (identity) and 9.556 (temporal) versus the next-best 6.857 and 7.889. In the user study, iMontage leads on all four axes (IF 4.46, IP 3.91, TC 4.31, OQ 4.16) with the largest margin over OmniGen2 (3.97, 3.07, 3.04, 3.23). Against closed-source models, iMontage is competitive on GEdit (within ~1 point of GPT-4o's 8.01) and ImgEdit (4.11 vs. 4.30), but trails on OmniContext where GPT-4o's 8.80 reflects its stronger multi-reference fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

iMontage follows the video-centric paradigm introduced by UniReal, which reframed multi-image generation as discontinuous video generation to exploit pre-trained motion priors. It departs from token-centric unified models (OmniGen, BAGEL) that autoregressively generate multimodal token streams, instead repurposing HunyuanVideo's MMDiT backbone with a minimally invasive Marginal RoPE strategy that assigns input and output pseudo-frames to head and tail temporal indices, preserving the model's original spatial geometry while disambiguating discrete image sets from continuous video. The CocktailMix difficulty-ordered training schedule represents a practical departure from flat or curriculum-based multi-task mixing, motivated by observed variance in per-task training difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iMontage is among the first open-source models to unify many-to-many image generation with highly dynamic content (hard cuts, large camera/subject motions) in a single forward pass, bridging the gap between video generation's temporal coherence and image generation's content diversity. The practical CocktailMix training recipe and Marginal RoPE design offer transferable insights for anyone repurposing video diffusion models for image tasks. Key limitations remain: the model is capped at four inputs and four outputs, and several editing capabilities are still weaker than dedicated single-task models. Open questions include whether long-context many-to-many generation is feasible with current architectures and whether the video-prior approach generalizes to even more heterogeneous task combinations.
