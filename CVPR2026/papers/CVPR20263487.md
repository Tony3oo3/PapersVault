## Problem

Real-time human interaction-to-reaction generation requires an agent to instantly respond to dynamic cues from other people and the scene, but existing frameworks face two fundamental gaps: (1) interaction data is scarce and fragmented across heterogeneous single-person, human-human, and human-scene domains, making unified training infeasible, and (2) diffusion-based models achieve high fidelity but are too slow for interactive use, while autoregressive designs accumulate drift over long horizons. No prior method simultaneously addresses data heterogeneity, real-time latency, and scene-aware multi-agent reactivity.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model consumes a text description (conditioning the universal prior only), a short history of surrounding agents' poses (encoded by a TCN), a voxelized 3D occupancy grid of the environment (encoded by a ViT), and the ego's own recent motion window (H=2 frames). It autoregressively outputs future SMPL-X pose sequences (body pose, hand pose, root motion, and dynamic attributes) in segments of F=8 frames at 10 FPS, with per-frame refinement applied during rollout to incorporate newly observed cues without re-running the full diffusion process.

## Method

ReMoGen decouples universal motion dynamics from interaction-specific adaptation through a three-layer modular design. A frozen latent-diffusion VAE prior (following DART) pretrained on HumanML3D provides the backbone generator; independently trained Meta-Interaction Modules (one per domain: HHI on Inter-X, HSI on LINGO) inject interaction cues into the denoiser via cross-attention and FiLM-style affine modulation, keeping the prior frozen to preserve kinematic quality. At inference, multiple modules can be composed with user-defined weights and L2-norm clamping for mixed-modality settings. A lightweight Frame-wise Segment Refinement (FWSR) adapter then performs per-frame corrective updates on the segment-level latent using the latest dynamic observations, achieving 0.042–0.047 s/frame latency while maintaining FID comparable to the unrefined segment rollout.

## Results

On Inter-X (Human-Human Interaction), ReMoGen achieves FID 0.181, R-Precision (Top-3) 0.464, and MM-Dist 4.076, outperforming all baselines while maintaining a per-frame latency of 0.042 s (well under the 0.1 s real-time threshold). On LINGO (Human-Scene Interaction), ReMoGen reaches FID 1.201, R-Precision 0.530, and MM-Dist 3.408 with the same 0.042 s latency, surpassing both TRUMANS and LINGO by large margins. Adding the Frame-wise Segment Refinement module further reduces FID to 0.166 and Peak Jerk to 0.218 with only a 0.005 s latency increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMoGen outperforms all five baselines on the primary HHI benchmark (Inter-X). Against ReGenNet [42], it reduces FID from 11.622 to 0.181 and Peak Jerk from 2.321 to 0.222, while also cutting latency from 0.210 s to 0.042 s per frame. Compared to FreeMotion [8] (online), ReMoGen improves FID from 3.383 to 0.181 and R-Precision from 0.284 to 0.464; even the offline FreeMotion variant (FID 0.492) is surpassed. SymBridge [2] is the closest real-time competitor, achieving slightly lower latency (0.040 s vs. 0.042 s) and marginally lower Peak Jerk (0.212 vs. 0.222), but ReMoGen dominates on FID (0.181 vs. 2.569), R-Precision (0.464 vs. 0.355), and MM-Dist (4.076 vs. 4.955). On the HSI benchmark (LINGO), ReMoGen beats TRUMANS [17] by a wide margin (FID 1.201 vs. 4.731; MM-Dist 3.408 vs. 10.822) and LINGO [16] (FID 1.201 vs. 3.633; R-Precision 0.530 vs. 0.218), while also being the only method meeting the 0.1 s real-time threshold (0.042 s vs. 0.074 s and 0.189 s respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMoGen builds directly on DART [49] as a frozen latent autoregressive diffusion prior, extending the prior-guided adaptation philosophy (LoRA, ControlNet, T2I-Adapter in 2D vision) to 3D human motion generation. It departs from prior interaction-motion work (ReGenNet, FreeMotion, LINGO, TRUMANS) by decoupling universal motion learning from domain-specific interaction adaptation via independently trained FiLM-based Meta-Interaction Modules, rather than end-to-end joint training. The Frame-wise Segment Refinement mechanism is a novel architectural contribution that bridges the fidelity-latency gap between segment-level diffusion (high quality, high latency) and per-frame autoregression (low latency, error accumulation), a trade-off that prior real-time motion systems have not resolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular frozen-prior-plus-adapters design offers a practical recipe for handling the data scarcity and heterogeneity that plagues interaction-motion research, with the strong data-efficiency result (10k fine-tuning steps from prior surpassing 500k from-scratch steps) suggesting broad applicability to other data-limited motion tasks. The composable inference (weighted sum of HHI/HSI modulations) enables zero-shot generalization to mixed-modality settings, a capability absent in prior single-domain systems. Limitations include reliance on SMPL-X representation, evaluation on relatively small datasets (Inter-X, LINGO, EgoBody), and a modest 10 FPS real-time threshold; open questions include scalability to larger multi-agent scenes, longer-horizon coherence, and whether the modular approach extends to interaction types beyond HHI/HSI (e.g., human-object, multi-robot).
