## Problem

MoE-based continual instruction tuning for LVLMs still suffers from catastrophic forgetting despite expert isolation, because newly trained router parameters cause routing drift: old-task tokens become mis-routed to newly added experts. The paper identifies the root cause at the token level—the 'token's dilemma'—where ambiguous and old tokens in new-task data offer minimal learning benefit yet directly corrupt the router's policy for old tasks when left unguided.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multimodal instruction-response triplets (an RGB image, a text instruction, and a text answer) and produces a text answer via autoregressive generation. The backbone is LLaVA-v1.5-7B (CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder + Vicuna-7B language model), where only newly added LoRA experts and their associated router parameters are trainable at each incremental step while all previously learned experts and router weights remain frozen. The method is evaluated on the CoIN benchmark's eight VQA tasks (ScienceQA, TextVQA, ImageNet, GQA, VizWiz, RefCOCO, VQAv2, OCR-VQA) with 569k training and 261k test samples.

## Method

LLaVA-DyMoE incrementally expands a dynamic MoE layer (LoRA experts + router) for each new task, freezing all old parameters. Its core contribution is a two-fold drift-aware regularization applied during training: (1) Token Assignment Guidance (TAG) computes a relative ambiguity score D_rel between old and new expert-group confidence logits, then applies a binary mask that routes ambiguous (D_rel ≤ τ) and old-dominant tokens exclusively to frozen old experts, suppressing their residual affinity for new experts; (2) Routing Score Regularization (RSR) adds an exclusivity loss (product of collective gate outputs for old and new groups) to enforce clean group separation, plus a specialization loss (BCE between new-group routing weight and a soft target) to promote new-expert utilization on genuinely novel tokens. The total objective combines the standard NTP loss, auxiliary load-balancing loss, and the two RSR terms weighted by a single hyperparameter α, with no additional constraints at inference time.

## Results

On the CoIN benchmark (8 VQA tasks), LLaVA-DyMoE achieves a Mean Final Accuracy (MFN) of 57.03%, Mean Average Accuracy (MAA) of 57.70%, and Backward Transfer (BWT) of −4.67%, outperforming the strongest MoE baseline IncMoELoRA (MFN 49.68%, MAA 49.50%, BWT −16.67%) by 7.35%, 8.20%, and 12.00 percentage points respectively. The method also surpasses regularization-based baselines (EWC, LWF) and parameter-isolation baselines (LoRA, O-LoRA) by 7–16 points on MFN. When combined with data-based strategies (ASD or replay), LLaVA-DyMoE further reduces BWT to −4.75% and −1.55% respectively, outperforming SEFE (−10.45%) and ProgLoRA (−6.59%) on forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVA-DyMoE outperforms all seven baselines on MFN and BWT. Against the simplest baseline LoRA (MFN 41.79, BWT −23.12), the gain is 15.24 points on MFN and 18.45 on BWT. The MoE-based MoELoRA/CoIN (MFN 43.93, BWT −22.18) and regularization methods EWC (MFN 40.86) and LWF (MFN 43.98) are all surpassed by 13–16 points on MFN. The strongest parameter-isolation baseline O-LoRA (MFN 49.53, BWT −17.54) is beaten by 7.5 points on MFN and 12.9 on BWT. The closest MoE baseline IncMoELoRA (MFN 49.68, BWT −16.67) is outperformed by 7.35 on MFN and 12.0 on BWT, confirming that the drift-aware token assignment is the key differentiator. When combined with data-based strategies, LLaVA-DyMoE+ASD (BWT −4.75) beats SEFE (BWT −10.45) by 5.7 points on forgetting, and LLaVA-DyMoE+Replay (BWT −1.55) beats ProgLoRA (BWT −6.59) by 5.0 points, though it trails ProgLoRA slightly on MAA (61.93 vs. 62.38).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MoE-with-LoRA paradigm for multimodal continual instruction tuning (established by MoELoRA/CoIN [7], SEFE [8], ProgLoRA [73], and LLaVA-CMoE [81]) by shifting the focus from task-level routing or architectural isolation to the token-level routing dynamics within the MoE layer. Prior dynamic-expansion methods (IncMoELoRA, LLaVA-CMoE) freeze old experts and add new ones but leave the router to learn freely, which the authors show causes routing-drift. The key departure is a diagnostic analysis that categorizes new-task tokens into 'new,' 'old,' and 'ambiguous' types based on routing score distributions, followed by targeted regularization (TAG + RSR) that operates on individual token assignments rather than on task identity or parameter subspaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The token-level decomposition of routing-drift provides a mechanistic explanation for why MoE-based continual learners forget even with frozen experts, offering a principled lever (token assignment guidance) that is orthogonal to and composable with data-replay, task-specific routing, and other MCIT strategies. The practical impact is significant: a 12% reduction in forgetting and 7% MFN gain on CoIN without additional inference-time overhead. Open questions include whether the ambiguity threshold τ and the TAG/RSR interaction generalize beyond the 8-task VQA sequence on a 7B model, whether the approach scales to larger LVLMs or more heterogeneous task streams, and whether the token-type taxonomy holds when tasks share substantial visual or linguistic overlap.
