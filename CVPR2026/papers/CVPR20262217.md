## Problem

High-dimensional raw radar tensors from modern MIMO FMCW sensors (e.g., ~100 MB per frame) saturate the low-bandwidth sensor-to-compute link (e.g., CAN bus at a few Mbps), yet no generalized, task-aware codec exists for radar data. Existing approaches either apply fixed compression ratios that cannot adapt to varying scene complexity, or reduce radar to sparse point clouds via CFAR, causing irreversible information loss. The paper asks whether an online, adaptive compression scheme can dynamically modulate the compression ratio at test time to maintain downstream perception accuracy under a strict bandwidth budget.

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes a complex-valued radar feature map X ∈ C^{C×H×W} (e.g., 32 channels × 512 range × 256 Doppler at 32-bit float for RADIal), converts it to a real-valued 2C×H×W tensor, and splits it into M×M non-overlapping blocks (M=64). The output transmitted over the link is a set of pruned, s-bit quantized DCT coefficients plus one per-block floating-point scale factor, achieving 0.27–1.07 bpp (101×–117× compression). On the compute side, the decoder reconstructs the radar cube via IDCT and block merging, which feeds a standard detection or segmentation network producing bounding boxes with confidence scores or pixel-wise class maps; the adaptive pruning ratio r_t is also updated each frame as a scalar control signal.

## Method

AdaRadar compresses radar in the spectral domain: each M×M block undergoes a Type-II DCT, and a rank-based pruning rule retains only the k = ⌊M²/r⌋ largest-magnitude coefficients, where the pruning ratio r is the single adaptive parameter. A per-block scaled quantizer (scale = peak magnitude / max code) preserves the high dynamic range of radar echoes at negligible overhead. The key novelty is a zeroth-order proxy-gradient controller that adjusts r online without backpropagation: it perturbs r by a small ε, performs two forward passes through the frozen downstream network, and uses the difference in max bounding-box confidence (or segmentation entropy) as a gradient estimate to update r via a simple gradient-descent step. This bypasses the non-differentiability of pruning and quantization and avoids transmitting gradient tensors that would be as large as the original radar data. The scheme is task-agnostic, requiring only post-hoc confidence outputs, and the sensor-side implementation needs only DCT, sorting, and rounding kernels available on embedded DSPs.

## Results

On the RADIal dataset, AdaRadar achieves a 101× compression ratio (0.32 bpp with 4-bit quantization and 12.57× spectral pruning) while maintaining F1 of 95.13% (vs. 96.58% uncompressed, a ~1.5%p drop) and actually improving segmentation mIoU from 75.97% to 79.34%. On CARRADA, the method reaches 117× compression with mIoU of 54.03% (vs. 55.25% baseline, ~1.2%p drop). On Radatron, 30× compression yields mAP of 48.46% and AP75 of 49.07%, both exceeding the uncompressed baseline (46.07% and 44.16% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the uncompressed FFTRadNet baseline [3], AdaRadar achieves 101× compression on RADIal with only a 1.45%p F1 drop (95.13% vs. 96.58%), and on CARRADA [45] it reaches 117× compression with a 1.22%p mIoU drop (54.03% vs. 55.25%). Against the index-value compression of RadarOcc [9], the proposed method dominates across all three datasets: on RADIal, F1 is 95.13% vs. 75.91% and mIoU is 79.34% vs. 49.86%; on CARRADA, mIoU is 54.03% vs. 38.96%; on Radatron [11], mAP is 48.46% vs. 45.72% and AP75 is 49.07% vs. 47.54%. Notably, on Radatron the compressed method actually outperforms the uncompressed baseline [11] in mAP (48.46% vs. 46.07%) and AP75 (49.07% vs. 44.16%), suggesting spectral pruning acts as a beneficial noise filter. Compared to Pixor [46] in the rate-accuracy tradeoff analysis, AdaRadar achieves higher precision and markedly higher recall at equivalent bit rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior radar compression approaches—CFAR-based point-cloud extraction, autoencoder-based coding, and RadarOcc's spatial-domain index-value pairs—by shifting compression to the spectral domain via DCT and, crucially, by introducing an online adaptive rate controller. The adaptive control mechanism borrows the zeroth-order gradient estimation idea from black-box optimization but applies it to a novel setting: adjusting a compression ratio in real time using only forward-pass confidence scores from a downstream detector, thereby avoiding backpropagation through non-differentiable pruning and quantization. Unlike open-loop image codecs (JPEG, learned image compression) that optimize rate-distortion offline at a fixed ratio, AdaRadar closes the loop at test time, making it the first radar codec to dynamically modulate compression based on task feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >100× compression with ~1% performance drop directly addresses a real engineering bottleneck in autonomous driving (the low-bandwidth sensor-to-compute link, e.g., CAN bus), and the method's reliance on lightweight DSP-friendly operations (DCT, sorting, rounding) makes it deployable on existing radar front-ends without hardware changes. The finding that spectral pruning can act as a denoising filter—improving AP75 by up to 5% on Radatron and mIoU by 4% on RADIal segmentation over the uncompressed baseline—is practically useful but also raises open questions about whether this effect generalizes across radar hardware and operating conditions. Limitations noted by the authors include the absence of temporal modeling across frames and no consideration of camera-image compression, both of which would be needed for a full multi-sensor fusion pipeline.
