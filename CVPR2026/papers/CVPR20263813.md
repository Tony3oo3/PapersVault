## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation approaches treat motion as a holistic sequence, learning one-to-one mappings from text to motion without capturing the structured composition of underlying motion concepts. This lack of decompositional understanding limits the ability to infer, disentangle, and recombine motion primitives the way humans naturally do, preventing flexible synthesis of novel motions from known components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263813/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single or multiple text prompts (each decomposed into K=2 sub-concept descriptions) and a holistic 3D motion sequence encoded into a skeleton-aware VAE latent space. It produces a full 3D motion sequence, K independently extractable decomposed motion concepts, and recombined novel motions by mixing concepts from different source motions. Notably, the self-supervised variants (OSS, SC) require no decomposed text at inference, discovering concepts purely from the holistic text embedding partitioned along its dimensional axis.

## Method

DEMOGEN reformulates text-to-motion generation as a compositional energy-based diffusion problem where each of K motion concepts is represented as a separate energy score, and the composite denoising target is the average of K individual energy gradients. The architecture follows a skeleton-aware latent diffusion backbone (SALAD), with the denoising network serving as a latent-aware EBM and a proposed Decompositional Cross-Attention (DCA) module—splitting cross-attention into K parallel branches with distinct key-value pairs—serving as a semantic-aware EBM. Three training variants provide varying levels of compositional supervision: DEMOGEN-EXP trains on explicitly decomposed text prompts with a text-mixing strategy; DEMOGEN-OSS partitions the text embedding dimensionally and enforces an orthogonality loss on the resulting sub-embeddings; DEMOGEN-SC adds a smooth L1 semantic consistency loss aligning partitioned embeddings with ground-truth decomposed text. Crucially, no decompositional 3D motion ground truth is required during training—the model adaptively discovers spatial-temporal concept boundaries through the energy aggregation objective alone.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion generation, DEMOGEN-OSS (latent-aware) achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.588 and Top-3 of 0.861, while DEMOGEN-EXP (semantic-aware) attains the highest Top-3 of 0.863. On the MTT benchmark, DEMOGEN-OSS reaches R@1 of 14.9 for multi-concept generation and DEMOGEN-EXP reaches R@1 of 16.2 for compositional generation, both surpassing the prior SOTA (EMG+AGD at 14.0 and EMG+SEF at 15.9). On the DeCompML motion composition task, DEMOGEN (semantic) achieves Top-1 of 0.567 and FID of 0.102, substantially outperforming SALAD (Top-1 0.342, FID 1.267) and EnergyMoGen (Top-1 0.326, FID 2.502).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SALAD [24], the strongest text-to-motion baseline, DEMOGEN-OSS (latent) edges out on R-Precision Top-1 (0.588 vs. 0.581) and MM-Dist (2.625 vs. 2.649), while SALAD retains a lower FID (0.076 vs. 0.092), indicating slightly smoother motions. Against EnergyMoGen [71], the direct EBM predecessor, DEMOGEN achieves consistent gains across all metrics: R-Precision Top-1 improves from 0.523 to 0.588 (latent-OSS) and FID drops from 0.188 to 0.092. On MTT multi-concept generation, DEMOGEN-OSS (R@1 14.9, TMR 0.584) surpasses EMG+AGD (R@1 14.0, TMR 0.570), and on compositional generation DEMOGEN-EXP (R@1 16.2, TMR 0.597) beats EMG+SEF (R@1 15.9, TMR 0.591). The most dramatic margin appears on DeCompML motion composition, where DEMOGEN (semantic) achieves Top-1 of 0.567 and FID of 0.102, compared to SALAD's 0.342/1.267 and EnergyMoGen's 0.326/2.502 — a roughly 65% relative improvement in Top-1 over the best prior method. MDM [61], MLD [66], ReMoDiffusion [73], and FineMoGen [74] are all clearly outperformed on R-Precision, with gaps of 0.06–0.17 in Top-1, confirming that the decompositional training paradigm yields meaningful gains over standard holistic text-to-motion diffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on EnergyMoGen's formulation of denoising networks and cross-attention as energy functions, but inverts the generative direction: rather than composing multiple text-conditioned energy distributions at inference to produce a motion, DEMOGEN trains a single holistic motion against K decomposed text embeddings so that the model learns to factor the motion into interpretable concepts. The energy-based diffusion connection (Du et al., Song et al.) and the compositional image decomposition line (Liu et al., Su et al.) provide the theoretical and methodological scaffolding, while the VAE backbone follows SALAD. The key conceptual departure from prior compositional motion work (SINC, TEACH, EnergyMoGen) is the shift from forward composition (given concepts → produce motion) to inverse decomposition (given motion → recover concepts), which the authors argue is the more human-like and practically useful direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decompositional framing opens a path toward interpretable and editable motion generation: if a model can reliably extract reusable motion primitives, downstream tasks like motion editing, long-horizon composition, and zero-shot recombination become more tractable. The DeCompML dataset (87K decomposed text groups) is a practical resource that can serve as a benchmark for compositional motion generation and as an augmentation source for text-to-motion training. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond K=2 concepts, whether the energy-based formulation is strictly necessary versus simpler factorization objectives, and whether the decomposed concepts remain semantically stable across diverse motion categories and longer sequences.
