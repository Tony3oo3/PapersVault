## Problem

Existing Process Reward Models (PRMs) for robotic reinforcement learning suffer from two fundamental flaws: their reward estimators lack step-aware, fine-grained understanding and rely on single-view perception, making them unreliable under occlusion in manipulation scenes; and their reward shaping procedures are theoretically unsound, creating a 'semantic trap' where the agent learns to stagnate in high-progress states rather than complete the task. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously with a general-purpose, multi-view reward model and a policy-invariant shaping scheme.

## Input / Output

The GRM is prompted with a natural-language task description and conditioned on four sets of synchronized multi-view images (wrist and third-person cameras): the initial state s_0, the goal state s_M, a 'BEFORE' state s_p, and an 'AFTER' state s_q. It outputs a single hop value H(s_p, s_q) ∈ [-1, 1] representing relative progress normalized by the remaining (or already-covered) task span. At deployment, the fused progress Φ*(s_t) is converted into a dense per-step reward r_GRM = r_gold + γΦ*(s_{t+1}) − Φ*(s_t), which drives the RL policy; the policy itself outputs standard robot actions (end-effector poses or joint torques) depending on the downstream RL algorithm (PPO, Cal-QL, ReinFlow, etc.).

## Method

Dopamine-Reward trains a General Reward Model (GRM), a vision-language model, on a 35M-sample, 3,400-hour multi-view dataset using a hop-based relative progress formulation: instead of regressing absolute progress, each training pair predicts a normalized hop H(s_p,s_q) that scales the progress delta by the remaining distance to goal (forward) or distance from start (backward), guaranteeing reconstructed progress stays in [0,1]. At inference, Multi-Perspective Progress Fusion averages three complementary estimates—incremental (step-by-step accumulation), forward-anchored (from initial state), and backward-anchored (from goal state)—to cancel drift and improve robustness. Dopamine-RL then adapts the pre-trained GRM to a new task via one-shot fine-tuning on a single expert trajectory (MSE on hop labels), and applies a Policy-Invariant Reward Shaping term F(s_t,s_{t+1}) = γΦ*(s_{t+1}) − Φ*(s_t) derived from the continuous discounted potential e^{−λt}Φ*(s_t). This shaping term telescopes to the constant −Φ*(s_0) under discounted summation, so the shaped Q-function is a state-wise shift of the original, preserving the optimal policy while providing dense gradient signal for fast convergence.

## Results

The GRM (8B, Multi-View) achieves an average VOC of 0.96 (Sparse), 0.96 (Medium), and 0.94 (Dense) across seven datasets, substantially outperforming GVL (0.20/0.12/0.13) and VLAC-2B (0.24/0.29/0.33). On task completion classification, the Multi-View GRM reaches 92.8% accuracy, surpassing GPT-5 (83.9%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (81.1%). For end-to-end policy learning, Dopamine-RL achieves 95.2% real-world success rate within 150 rollouts (~1 hour of robot interaction), compared to 68.0% for ConRFT (183 rollouts) and 9.8% for BC (50 demos).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two prior process reward models, GVL [38] and VLAC-2B [61], the GRM dominates by a wide margin: average VOC jumps from 0.12–0.33 (baselines) to 0.94–0.96 (Ours-8B Multi-View), with the gap most pronounced on long-horizon tasks like LIBERO and RoboBrain-X. On task completion judgment, the Multi-View GRM (92.8%) beats the strongest generalist VLMs—GPT-5 (83.9%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (81.1%)—by 9 and 12 percentage points respectively, while single-view PRMs (GVL 37.2%, VLAC 33.9%) fail catastrophically due to occlusion. For policy learning, Dopamine-RL outperforms BC (50 demos) by 85.4 points in real-world success rate (95.2% vs 9.8%) and requires only 150 rollouts versus 183 for ConRFT [12] (68.0% SR) and 560 for sparse-reward PPO (79.9% SR in simulation). In simulation, Dopamine-RL matches or slightly exceeds sparse-reward PPO (81.0% vs 79.9%) while using 30% fewer rollouts (395 vs 560). The ablation confirms that removing policy-invariant shaping causes a 43.7-point collapse, validating that the reward shaping design—not just the reward model—is critical for practical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of learned Process Reward Models for robotics (GVL, VLAC, SARM, LIV) by addressing two specific deficiencies: single-view, task-specific reward estimation and theoretically unsound reward shaping. The reward shaping component is grounded in Ng et al.'s (1999) Potential-Based Reward Shaping framework, but the paper identifies and resolves a 'semantic trap' where naive dense progress rewards incentivize stagnation rather than task completion. The hop-based relative progress formulation and multi-perspective fusion (incremental, forward-anchored, backward-anchored) are novel architectural choices that depart from prior PRMs' uniform or single-perspective progress regression, enabled by a 3,400-hour multi-view dataset spanning real robots, simulation, and egocentric human video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles the central bottleneck of reward design in real-world robotic RL with a practically deployable pipeline: one-shot GRM adaptation from a single expert demo, followed by policy-invariant dense reward shaping that converges in ~150 rollouts. The 43.7% performance collapse when removing policy-invariant shaping provides strong empirical validation that the 'semantic trap' is not merely theoretical but a dominant failure mode in practice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially longer-horizon or multi-task settings, the computational cost of running a multi-view VLM as a real-time reward oracle, and whether the one-shot adaptation generalizes beyond manipulation to locomotion or navigation tasks.
