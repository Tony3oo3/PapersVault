## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary HOI detection methods built on CLIP suffer from two intertwined limitations: (1) the one-stage query-based paradigm overfits to seen interaction categories, creating a significant seen-unseen performance gap, and (2) CLIP's image-level visual encoder provides only coarse global semantics, causing attention to disperse over irrelevant regions rather than focusing on fine-grained interaction cues. No prior OV-HOI method explicitly addresses both semantic overfitting and spatial under-localization simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces, for each detected interaction, a human bounding box, an object bounding box, and an interaction label (action verb). It requires no pre-trained object detector; instead, it uses CLIP ViT-B/16 as the visual backbone with 8 prefix tokens and 4 conjunctive tokens in the text encoder to compose action-object phrases. At inference, the final interaction score is the product of the interaction classifier score and the detection confidence raised to a power ξ>1, which suppresses overconfident detections.

## Method

SD-IF augments a CLIP-based DETR-style one-stage HOI detector with two reinforcement learning modules. The Semantic Diversification (SD) module treats each learnable query as an RL agent that samples Gaussian perturbations from a conditional policy network; a dual-level exploration mechanism combines local visual consistency (perturbations conditioned on the global visual context) with global semantic expansion (a log-barrier loss pushing queries away from seen-category text-embedding centroids), while a proxy reward based on KL divergence between text-space and visual-space distributions balances semantic novelty against visual coherence. The Interaction Focusing (IF) module formulates an actor-critic scheme in which the actor predicts an attention distribution over interaction embeddings conditioned on diversified queries, detection predictions, and interaction features, and the critic scores this attention via a hybrid reward combining spatial overlap with the human-object region mask and cosine similarity between the refined representation and the query. Training jointly optimizes the standard Hungarian-matched detection loss with the SD and IF reinforcement objectives, and at inference both modules operate deterministically using the policy mean and direct attention output.

## Results

On HICO-DET, SD-IF achieves 28.01 mAP on unseen, 30.09 mAP on seen, and 29.63 mAP on full categories, outperforming the previous best open-vocabulary method SGC-Net by 4.74%, 1.75%, and 2.41% respectively. On SWIG-HOI, SD-IF reaches 25.01 (non-rare), 18.86 (rare), 15.57 (unseen), and 19.48 (full) mAP, surpassing SGC-Net by 1.34%, 2.31%, 3.11%, and 2.28% respectively. The seen-unseen mAP gap is narrowed from 5.07% (SGC-Net) to 2.08% (SD-IF) on HICO-DET, demonstrating substantially improved generalization to novel interaction categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-IF outperforms the strongest open-vocabulary baseline SGC-Net [22] on all four SWIG-HOI metrics (non-rare, rare, unseen, full) and all three HICO-DET metrics (unseen, seen, full), with the largest relative gains on rare (+14.0%) and unseen (+25.0%) categories on SWIG-HOI. Against CMD-SE [16], the direct architectural baseline, SD-IF improves full mAP from 15.26 to 19.48 on SWIG-HOI and from 22.35 to 29.63 on HICO-DET. SD-IF also surpasses INP-CC [17] and THID [37] by substantial margins across all open-vocabulary metrics. Notably, SD-IF exceeds zero-shot methods that rely on pre-trained object detectors (HOICLIP [30], OpenCat [45], GEN-VLKT [21]) on the unseen category (28.01 vs. 23.48 mAP on HICO-DET) despite not using any pre-trained detector, while those methods retain an advantage on seen and full categories due to their detector priors. On SWIG-HOI, methods depending on pre-trained detectors (GEN-VLKT, MP-HOI) perform poorly on rare and unseen categories, whereas SD-IF leads across all four metrics without any external detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CMD-SE (CVPR 2024) architecture for CLIP-based one-stage open-vocabulary HOI detection, which itself inherits the DETR-style learnable query and Hungarian matching formulation. It departs from the entire line of OV-HOI methods (THID, CMD-SE, INP-CC, SGC-Net) by introducing reinforcement learning as the optimization mechanism: a stochastic policy for semantic perturbation of queries (SD module) and an actor-critic scheme for adaptive spatial attention (IF module). Prior OV-HOI work has addressed interaction understanding through prompt engineering, multi-granularity features, or LLM-based descriptions, but none has tackled the seen-unseen overfitting bias or the coarse spatial awareness of CLIP through learned exploration policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two concrete failure modes of one-stage OV-HOI detection—semantic overfitting to seen categories and diffuse spatial attention—that prior methods have acknowledged but not systematically resolved. The narrowing of the seen-unseen mAP gap from ~5% to ~2% and the strong relative gains on rare/unseen classes (25% relative on SWIG-HOI unseen) suggest RL-based exploration is a viable direction for open-set generalization in detection. However, absolute mAP values remain modest (15.57 on SWIG-HOI unseen), the evaluation is limited to two small-scale benchmarks, and it remains open whether the RL formulation scales to larger, more diverse datasets or whether simpler regularization strategies could achieve comparable gains at lower training cost.
