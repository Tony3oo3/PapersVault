## Problem

In-the-wild facial expression recognition faces two co-occurring challenges—visual perturbations (occlusions, pose variations) and label noise from subjective or erroneous annotations—that existing methods address in isolation. When both challenges compound, models degrade significantly because techniques tuned for one perturbation type do not transfer to the other. This paper asks whether a single unified framework can simultaneously mitigate both effects without sacrificing clean-label accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262706/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB facial expression images, which are detected and aligned via MTCNN, cropped to the facial region, and resized to 3×224×224. During training, each image is transformed into two views (weak and strong augmentations) that pass through a dual-branch encoder; at inference, a single image is fed to the momentum-updated Key branch, which outputs a confidence vector over 7 or 8 expression classes (depending on the dataset), with the argmax yielding the predicted label.

## Method

D³FER combines a dual-channel augmentation strategy (weak/strong views) with a momentum-updated Query-Key dual-branch architecture inspired by MoCo. A dynamic queue of length L=1024 stores historical classification confidence scores from both augmentation channels and Key-branch features from the most recent T batches, enabling adaptive per-class noise thresholds computed from confidence bias (max posterior minus target-class score). Samples are partitioned into clean, uncertain, and noisy sets; noisy samples are relabeled to their fused dual-channel argmax, while uncertain samples are trained with a noise-tolerant GCE loss instead of standard cross-entropy. Contrastive learning (NCE loss) is performed between current-batch Query features (weak aug) and queued Key features (strong aug), using expression labels to define positive/negative pairs, which enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class separability under visual corruption. At inference, the stabilized Key branch—whose parameters are a moving average of the Query branch—produces smoother, more consistent predictions.

## Results

On clean in-the-wild benchmarks, D³FER achieves state-of-the-art accuracy of 90.71% on RAF-DB, 89.86% on FERPlus, 66.00% on AffectNet-7, and 62.38% on AffectNet-8, surpassing the previous best (NHG at 90.09% on RAF-DB, EAC at 89.64% on FERPlus, MEK at 65.73% on AffectNet-7). Under 10% symmetric label noise, D³FER reaches 89.37% (RAF-DB), 89.19% (FERPlus), and 64.89% (AffectNet), and retains 86.41%, 87.57%, and 63.34% even at 30% noise. On occlusion and pose-variation subsets, D³FER leads with 87.48% on Occlusion-RAF-DB and 63.10% on Occlusion-AffectNet, outperforming the prior best by 1.36% and 0.12% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D³FER outperforms all 22 compared baselines on every clean-dataset metric in Table 4: it leads NHG [11] by 0.62% on RAF-DB (90.71 vs 90.09), EAC [52] by 0.22% on FERPlus (89.86 vs 89.64), MEK [53] by 0.27% on AffectNet-7 (66.00 vs 65.73), and MEK by 0.22% on AffectNet-8 (62.38 vs 62.16). Under label noise (Table 3), D³FER wins at 10% noise on all three datasets (e.g., 89.37% vs ReSup's 88.43% on RAF-DB), but at 20% noise it ties ReSup on RAF-DB (87.29%) and trails ReSup on AffectNet (63.60% vs 63.97%); at 30% noise it still loses to ReSup on RAF-DB (86.41% vs 86.86%) while winning on FERPlus (87.57% vs 86.97% NLA) and AffectNet (63.34% vs 62.89% ReSup). On visual-perturbation subsets (Table 2), D³FER dominates occlusion benchmarks (87.48% on Occlusion-RAF-DB vs CC-KD's 86.12%; 63.10% on Occlusion-AffectNet vs VTFF's 62.98%) but is slightly behind DSAN on large-pose FERPlus (89.23% vs 89.83% at Pose>30°). Overall, D³FER's advantage is most pronounced on clean and occlusion-heavy settings, while ReSup [50] remains marginally stronger under extreme (30%) label noise on RAF-DB.

## Relation to Prior Work

D³FER directly extends MoCo's momentum-updated dual-branch architecture and memory queue mechanism, repurposing the queue to store not only Key-branch features for contrastive learning but also dual-channel classification confidence scores for adaptive noise threshold estimation. It departs from the prevailing FER literature where visual perturbation robustness (occlusion, pose) and label noise tolerance are addressed in isolation by prior methods like VTFF, DSAN, ReSup, and NLA, instead unifying both challenges within a single training pipeline. The contrastive objective is adapted from MoCo's unsupervised InfoNCE to a supervised, label-aware formulation that enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class separability specific to expression semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in addressing the co-occurrence of visual perturbations and label noise that real-world FER deployments inevitably face, a gap the paper identifies as underexplored. The dynamic queue-based noise threshold estimation offers a training-free-tuning mechanism for label correction that avoids the instability of single-batch thresholding. However, the evaluation is confined to a ResNet-18 backbone and three standard benchmarks, leaving open whether the framework's gains transfer to larger architectures, video-based FER, or more severe real-world degradations. The modest margins over the strongest baselines (ReSup, NHG) suggest the approach is a solid engineering integration rather than a fundamental methodological breakthrough.
