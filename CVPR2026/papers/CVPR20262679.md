## Problem

Video diffusion transformers suffer from quadratic attention complexity that makes high-resolution, long-video generation prohibitively slow (e.g., 1691s for 81 frames at 720p on a single A40). Existing sparse attention methods apply a uniform Euclidean clustering strategy to both queries and keys with a fixed number of clusters, ignoring the fact that query and key distributions differ substantially in structure and vary across layers, leading to missed critical tokens and degraded output quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262679/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AdaCluster is a training-free inference-time acceleration applied to existing video DiTs (CogVideoX-2B, HunyuanVideo, Wan-2.1). It takes a text prompt and generates a video at resolutions from 480p to 1120p with 81 frames, corresponding to input sequences of 34K–176.4K tokens. The method modifies only the attention computation within each DiT layer during the iterative denoising loop, leaving the model weights, text encoder, and VAE untouched, and produces a video with PSNR ≥ 29 dB relative to full-attention output.

## Method

AdaCluster redesigns the cluster-then-select pipeline with role-aware strategies. For queries, it exploits the observation that relative query-key score magnitudes are independent of query vector length, so it normalizes queries onto a unit sphere and applies angle-preserving K-means clustering, achieving a much higher compression ratio than Euclidean clustering. For keys, it uses a multi-stage K-means procedure that adaptively assigns cluster counts per layer based on a compactness score (inverse MSE), iteratively re-clustering outlier tokens beyond a distance threshold until all tokens fall into compact clusters or a max-cluster limit is reached (triggering a fallback to full attention for that layer). Cluster centers from the first denoising step are reused as initialization in subsequent steps to avoid redundant clustering. Finally, TensorQuest reformulates the Quest upper-bound scoring as a matmul of positive and negative parts of queries and keys, shifting the computation from CUDA cores to tensor cores and yielding up to 5× speedup in critical cluster selection.

## Results

AdaCluster achieves up to 1.67×–4.31× end-to-end speedup over full attention on CogVideoX-2B, Wan-2.1-1.3B, and HunyuanVideo, with the largest gains at longer sequences (4.31× at 176.4K tokens on HunyuanVideo). In terms of fidelity, AdaCluster consistently attains the highest PSNR (30.99 on CogVideoX, 29.08 on Wan-2.1, 30.58 on HunyuanVideo) and SSIM, while achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.231, 0.393, 0.203 respectively) among all sparse-attention baselines. VBench quality scores (imaging quality, background/subject consistency, temporal flicker) remain within 1–3% of the full-attention reference across all three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpargeAttn (block-level sparsity), AdaCluster wins on every similarity metric across all three models: e.g., on HunyuanVideo PSNR improves from 28.16 to 30.58 (+2.4 dB) and LPIPS drops from 0.596 to 0.203, while also delivering a higher speedup (1.68× vs 1.33×). Against SVG2 (fixed 100-query / 500-key clustering), AdaCluster again leads on PSNR (30.58 vs 29.32 on HunyuanVideo; 29.08 vs 28.23 on Wan-2.1) and LPIPS, with a modest speedup advantage (1.68× vs 1.57× on HunyuanVideo; 1.85× vs 1.61× on Wan-2.1). The gap widens substantially at longer sequences: at 176.4K tokens AdaCluster reaches 4.31× speedup versus only 1.78× for SpargeAttn, and SVG2 becomes infeasible beyond 101.1K tokens due to memory overhead. On VBench quality scores, all methods stay within ~1–3% of full attention, though CogVideoX shows a notable drop in subject consistency for both SpargeAttn and AdaCluster, which the authors attribute to the base model's weaker quality. Overall, AdaCluster dominates on the primary fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) while matching or exceeding baselines on speedup, with the advantage growing with sequence length.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaCluster extends the clustering-based sparse attention line initiated by SVG2, which first introduced semantic-aware token clustering for video DiTs but applied a uniform Euclidean K-means strategy with fixed cluster counts to both queries and keys. The key departure is a role-aware redesign: queries are normalized and clustered by angle (exploiting the scale-invariance of relative attention scores), while keys undergo a multi-stage Euclidean K-means with per-layer adaptive cluster counts driven by measured intra-cluster compactness. For critical token selection, the paper reformulates Quest's per-dimension max/min scoring into a tensor-core-friendly matmul (TensorQuest), borrowing the conceptual framework from LLM KV-cache sparsity but adapting it to the compute-heavy diffusion denoising setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With attention consuming 67–84% of DiT inference time and sequence lengths reaching 176K tokens at high resolution, a training-free 1.67×–4.31× speedup with PSNR above 30 dB is practically significant for deploying video generation on consumer-grade hardware. The per-layer adaptive cluster assignment and the observation that token distributions shift substantially across layers (Figure 4) suggest that one-size-fits-all sparsity patterns are fundamentally mismatched to DiT internals, a finding that could inform future training-based sparse attention designs. Open questions include how the method behaves at even longer sequences or with different denoising schedules, whether the denoising-step stability assumption (reusing cluster centers) degrades under aggressive step-count reduction, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to audio-visual or 3D generation DiTs.
