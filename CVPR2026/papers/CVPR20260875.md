## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for diffusion model acceleration degrade substantially in the practical 20–30 step regime, where larger timestep intervals cause polynomial extrapolators (e.g., TaylorSeer) to suffer error accumulation and trajectory drift, while reuse-based methods violate their near-identical-feature assumption. Additionally, current approaches apply a uniform prediction strategy across all denoising phases, ignoring the distinct dynamical behavior of early structural formation versus late detail refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260875/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TC-Padé is a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper around existing diffusion transformers (FLUX.1-dev, Wan2.1-1.3B, DiT-XL/2) that accepts the same text prompts or class labels as the base model and produces the same generated images (256×256 for ImageNet, up to 1024×1024 for FLUX) or videos. The method operates at a fixed 20 denoising steps and partitions the sampling trajectory into cache intervals of N timesteps, within which it adaptively skips full network evaluations to achieve 1.46×–2.88× latency reduction without modifying model weights or architecture.

## Method

TC-Padé predicts inter-layer residuals (differences between consecutive DiT block outputs) using a [2/1] Padé-like rational approximation over three cached historical residuals, rather than extrapolating raw features with Taylor polynomials. A Trajectory Stableness Indicator (TSI) computed from normalized residual differences adaptively gates whether each timestep requires full computation or can be predicted, concentrating compute on unstable trajectory segments. Coefficients of the rational predictor are modulated by an exponential stability factor that dampens prediction confidence when recent residuals change rapidly. A step-aware strategy further tailors the prediction: a weighted residual combination in the early phase (t > 0.7T), the full Padé approximant in the middle phase, and a Padé prediction augmented with a first-order difference term in the late refinement phase (t < 0.2T).

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (20 steps, COCO 2017), TC-Padé (fast) achieves a 2.88× latency speedup (3.20s vs 9.22s) with FID 24.14 (vs 23.38 baseline), CLIP 31.82, PSNR 21.96, SSIM 0.78, and LPIPS 0.29. On Wan2.1-1.3B (text-to-video), TC-Padé (fast) yields 1.72× speedup with VBench-2.0 score of 60.38% (vs 64.16% baseline) and PSNR 21.70. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet 256×256), TC-Padé (fast) achieves 1.46× speedup with FID-50k of 6.93 (vs 3.56 baseline) and IS 185.12.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TC-Padé (fast) achieves the highest speedup (2.88×) among all compared methods, surpassing TaylorSeer (2.59×), TeaCache (2.15×), ToCa (1.81×), and Δ-DiT (1.80×). On perceptual quality, TC-Padé (fast) substantially outperforms all baselines: PSNR 21.96 vs. TaylorSeer's 17.46/16.57, ToCa's 17.29, and Δ-DiT's 16.23; SSIM 0.78 vs. 0.52–0.61 for all baselines; LPIPS 0.29 vs. 0.42–0.62. TaylorSeer, despite achieving 2.31–2.59× speedup, suffers severe FID degradation (marked †), indicating broken generation quality, while TC-Padé (fast) maintains FID 24.14 (only 3.2% above the 23.38 baseline). TC-Padé (slow) is nearly lossless with FID 23.85 and PSNR 24.67 at 2.20× speedup. On video generation (Wan2.1), TC-Padé (fast) retains VBench-2.0 at 60.38% vs. TaylorSeer's 54.74% and TeaCache's 58.40%, while on ImageNet (DiT-XL/2) it achieves the best FID-50k of 6.93 vs. TaylorSeer's 7.84–7.86 and ToCa's 10.72.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prediction-based feature caching paradigm introduced by TaylorSeer, which uses truncated Taylor expansion to extrapolate features to future timesteps. The key conceptual departures are threefold: (1) replacing polynomial Taylor extrapolation with Padé (rational function) approximation to better capture asymptotic and transitional dynamics, (2) shifting the prediction target from raw features to inter-layer residuals, which exhibit higher temporal similarity, and (3) introducing an adaptive Trajectory Stableness Indicator (TSI) that selectively skips computation only on smooth trajectory segments. The broader lineage includes reuse-based methods (DeepCache, ToCa, Δ-DiT, TeaCache) that the paper positions as fundamentally limited under large timestep intervals due to their near-identical-feature assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing caching methods degrade substantially in the 20–30 step regime that industry applications actually use, while the Padé-based approach maintains quality with 2.88× speedup on FLUX.1-dev and 1.72× on Wan2.1. The method is training-free and plug-and-play, and its orthogonality to quantization (yielding ~6× total latency reduction) makes it immediately deployable. Open questions include whether the rational-function advantage persists at even lower step counts (≤10), whether the TSI threshold and phase boundaries require per-model tuning, and whether the approach generalizes to architectures without clear residual structure (e.g., MMDiT's dual-stream design).
