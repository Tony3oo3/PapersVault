## Problem

Existing monocular 3D human-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction methods rely exclusively on single-view features, which fundamentally limits their ability to recover occluded body parts and accurately estimate contact regions when the human and object mutually occlude each other. This paper addresses the gap by introducing cross-view feature priors—synthesizing novel-view image features from a single input—to supplement spatial geometric information at the feature level without requiring additional camera inputs at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260696/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image of a human-object interaction scene along with human and object segmentation masks, which are concatenated into a 5-channel input (RGB + two masks) and cropped to the interaction region. It produces a 3D human mesh (431 vertices, downsampled from SMPL+H's 6890), a 3D object mesh (64 vertices), and a per-vertex binary contact map of dimension 431+64 indicating which human and object vertices are in contact. The cross-view generator is pre-trained offline on multi-view image pairs with camera intrinsics, but at inference only the single-view image and masks are required.

## Method

CrossHOI introduces a pre-trained cross-view generator that uses a lightweight cross-attention module with learnable key-value tokens (initialized from the target view's features and camera parameters) to infer novel-view image features from a single-view input, trained with MSE and cosine losses on maximally different viewpoint pairs. During reconstruction, a spatial cross-view fusion module performs cross-attention (view A as query, view B as key/value with residual) to aggregate complementary cues before predicting initial SMPL+H and 6DoF object parameters via the Hand4Whole framework. For contact estimation, vertices of the initial meshes are projected onto both view feature maps to construct two sets of 3D vertex features, which are fused through a bidirectional cross-view Transformer (mutual cross-attention in both directions) to predict the contact map. A final refinement stage multiplies the contact map with vertex features, applies another round of bidirectional cross-attention, and regresses per-vertex offsets to produce the final meshes, focusing adjustments on interaction-critical regions while preserving global geometric consistency.

## Results

On BEHAVE, CrossHOI achieves CD_human of 4.27 cm and CD_object of 7.68 cm, with contact precision of 0.687 and recall of 0.576, outperforming all baselines. On InterCap, it reaches CD_human of 5.17 cm, CD_object of 8.38 cm, contact precision of 0.724, and recall of 0.491. Compared to the strongest baseline HOI-TG, CrossHOI improves CD_human by 4.7% and CD_object by 3.5% on InterCap, while on BEHAVE it improves CD_human by 14.4% and CD_object by 8.7% over CONTHO. Under severe occlusion (500-sample subset), CrossHOI gains 5.6 pp in contact precision and 6.1 pp in recall over the CONTHO baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossHOI consistently outperforms all six baselines on both datasets across all four metrics. Against the closest competitor HOI-TG (graph-based Transformer, CVPR 2025), CrossHOI reduces CD_human from 4.59 to 4.27 cm (−7.0%) and CD_object from 8.00 to 7.68 cm (−4.0%) on BEHAVE, and from 5.43 to 5.17 cm (−4.7%) and 8.68 to 8.38 cm (−3.5%) on InterCap, while also improving contact precision by 2.5 pp and 2.4 pp respectively. Against CONTHO (CVPR 2024), the gains are larger on BEHAVE: 14.4% reduction in CD_human, 8.7% in CD_object, and 5.9 pp / 8.0 pp in contact precision / recall. The predefined-contact-region method PHOSA (ECCV 2020) and the neural-field method CHORE (ECCV 2022) are substantially outperformed, with PHOSA showing CD_human over 11 cm on both datasets. The two general-purpose human mesh recovery methods (METRO, Graphormer) are included only as weak baselines and are far behind all HOI-specific methods, confirming that task-specific interaction modeling is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

CrossHOI builds directly on the two-stage HOI reconstruction paradigm established by CONTHO and HOI-TG, adopting Hand4Whole for initial human mesh estimation and following the same initial-reconstruction → contact-estimation → refinement pipeline. Its key departure is the introduction of a pre-trained cross-view generator that infers novel-view image features from a single-view input, a mechanism absent from all prior monocular HOI methods (PHOSA, CHORE, CONTHO, HOI-TG) that rely exclusively on single-view features. The bidirectional cross-view Transformer for vertex-level fusion extends standard cross-attention into a symmetric, residual formulation tailored to contact-sensitive geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that feature-level novel-view synthesis can meaningfully improve monocular 3D HOI reconstruction—especially under severe occlusion—this work opens a path toward richer geometric reasoning without requiring multi-view inputs at inference time. However, the cross-view generator still requires multi-view training data (BEHAVE, InterCap) for pre-training, and the generated features carry residual noise (evidenced by the A→B > B→A asymmetry), limiting immediate deployment in in-the-wild settings with arbitrary viewpoints. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes, diverse object categories beyond the 20 in BEHAVE, and whether the pre-training cost can be reduced or replaced with self-supervised view synthesis.
