## Problem

Existing scene text editing methods either require large-scale paired training data and substantial compute (training-based) or rely on generic attention manipulation that fails to preserve typographic structure and character accuracy in complex scenes (training-free). A key unresolved issue is phase-dependent controllability: early denoising steps lack structural/style preservation, while later steps lack the fine-grained semantic and spatial guidance needed for correct text rendering, leading to character duplication, distortion, and style drift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261647/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image containing text (padded to 384×256 with background-similar color), a source caption formatted as 'A picture with word T_src', and a target prompt formatted as 'Please replace the word T_src with T_tar'. It produces a single edited image where the original text is replaced by the target text while preserving font style, color, size, and background. Text embeddings are extracted via T5 and CLIP, and the image is encoded through a VAE; inference runs 50 denoising steps with an Overshoot scheduler on FLUX-Kontext, requiring no fine-tuning or paired data.

## Method

TextFlow is a two-phase, training-free editing framework built on FLUX-Kontext (a DiT with flow matching). In the first phase, the Flow Manifold Steering (FMS) module constructs a noise-injected source latent via linear interpolation, computes a differential geometric correction to the target latent, concatenates source and target states, and derives a velocity-field differential ΔV between the two trajectories; this differential is applied as a controlled trajectory shift to preserve global style and structure early in denoising. In the second phase, the Attention Boost (AttnBoost) mechanism extracts cross-attention maps from the double-stream transformer block, applies targeted amplification to text-token query indices, aggregates the resulting text-to-image attention through summation over text tokens, spatial pooling, and min-max normalization, and feeds the refined guidance signal into a modified scheduler to sharpen character-level rendering. The two phases are decoupled by timestep, so FMS governs early steps (style/structure) while AttnBoost governs later steps (text accuracy), enabling a single forward pass with no task-specific training.

## Results

On the ScenePair dataset, TextFlow achieves the best image quality across all four metrics: SSIM 89.03, PSNR 22.47, MSE 0.91, and FID 13.53. For text rendering accuracy, it reaches 79.98% character accuracy and 0.914 NED, slightly below TextFlux's 80.40% ACC but with a substantially lower FID (13.53 vs. 54.64), indicating far superior visual realism. The method outperforms the closest training-free baseline FlowEdit by 1.43 SSIM, 1.58 PSNR, and 11.88 FID, while also surpassing the powerful FLUX-Kontext foundation model by 1.95 PSNR and 1.88 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextFlow outperforms all training-based STE methods (DiffSTE, TextDiffuser, AnyText) by a wide margin on image quality metrics, with SSIM exceeding 89 vs. their 22–31 range, and FID of 13.53 vs. 51–180. Against the closest training-free competitor FlowEdit, TextFlow improves FID from 25.41 to 13.53 and ACC from 45.51% to 79.98%, while also gaining 1.58 PSNR. Compared to the FLUX-Kontext foundation model it builds upon, TextFlow achieves +1.94 PSNR, +1.95 SSIM, and −1.88 FID, with comparable text accuracy (79.98% vs. 78.72% ACC). The only metric where a competitor edges ahead is NED: FLUX-Kontext scores 0.920 vs. TextFlow's 0.914, and TextFlux achieves 80.40% ACC vs. 79.98%, though TextFlux's FID is 4× worse (54.64 vs. 13.53). Overall, TextFlow delivers the best balance of visual fidelity and text correctness without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextFlow is built directly on FLUX-Kontext's flow-matching DiT backbone and inherits the inversion-free editing paradigm popularized by FlowEdit, but departs from prior training-free editing work by introducing a phase-aware decomposition: FMS handles structural/style preservation in early denoising steps via differential geometric trajectory correction, while AttnBoost targets fine-grained text rendering in later steps through attention-region amplification. This two-phase strategy contrasts with prior training-free methods (Stable Flow, ICEdit, KV-Edit) that apply a single uniform guidance mechanism across all timesteps, and with training-based STE methods (DiffSTE, AnyText, TextFlux) that require paired data and task-specific fine-tuning. The AttnBoost mechanism is conceptually related to the AMO/Overshoot sampler but repurposes it specifically for text-token attention regions rather than global denoising trajectory extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a training-free pipeline can match or exceed training-based STE methods on both visual quality (FID 13.53 vs. 54.64 for TextFlux) and text accuracy (79.98% vs. 80.40%), this work lowers the practical barrier to scene text editing—no paired datasets, no fine-tuning, no OCR encoders. The phase-aware decomposition (style first, detail second) offers a clean conceptual template that could transfer to other structured-content editing tasks. However, the framework remains tied to the FLUX-Kontext backbone, struggles with multiline text and complex layouts, and the 50-step inference on 4×A6000 GPUs limits real-time deployment, leaving open questions about scalability to high-resolution and multi-line editing scenarios.
