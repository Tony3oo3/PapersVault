## Problem

Existing monocular pose estimation methods face a fundamental trade-off: dynamic-scene-aware approaches (semantic segmentation, per-sequence 3DGS-based detectors, or limited synthetic-trained networks) are brittle, fail on short sequences, or degrade on static-only inputs, while static-optimized methods like DROID-SLAM lose accuracy when moving objects violate the static-world assumption. No prior framework achieves state-of-the-art performance simultaneously across dynamic, static, and low-ego-motion benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a calibrated monocular RGB video sequence (known intrinsics fx, fy, cx, cy) at 384×512 resolution during training and similar resolution preserving aspect ratio at inference. It produces per-frame SE(3) camera poses, per-edge motion masks at H/8 × W/8 resolution, and downsampled disparity maps. A MoGe2 metric depth prior is used to resolve monocular scale ambiguity, and the system operates as online visual odometry with a sliding-window BA, supplemented by loop closure and periodic global BA for long sequences.

## Method

WildPose fuses the rich 3D-aware features of the frozen MASt3R ViT encoder with the end-to-end differentiable bundle adjustment pipeline of DROID-SLAM. Two key architectural changes are introduced: (1) a new update operator that replaces DROID-SLAM's scratch-trained CNN with MASt3R's ViT features passed through a lightweight two-layer convolutional adapter into the ConvGRU recurrent network, and (2) a high-capacity motion mask detector that aggregates multi-level correspondence tokens from MASt3R's transformer decoder via a DPT-style fusion layer and a CNN head to predict per-edge (pairwise) motion masks, avoiding the temporal ambiguity of per-frame segmentation. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: the update operator is first trained end-to-end on static data, then finetuned on mixed static/dynamic data with ground-truth motion masks injected into the BA weighting matrix, and finally the motion detector is trained with the frozen operator using a combined pose loss and binary cross-entropy mask loss. At inference, predicted motion masks down-weight dynamic residuals in the BA objective, and a depth regularization term from MoGe2 anchors metric scale in the local window but is removed during final global BA to avoid noise from the depth prior.

## Results

On the Wild-SLAM MoCap dynamic benchmark, WildPose achieves 0.39 cm average ATE RMSE, outperforming the next-best method WildGS-SLAM (0.46 cm) by 15% and MegaSaM (2.40 cm) by over 6×. On static benchmarks, WildPose achieves 0.027 m ATE on TUM static and 0.049 m on 7-Scenes, matching or beating DROID-SLAM (0.038 m and 0.049 m respectively) while dynamic-only methods like MegaSaM degrade to 0.066 m on TUM. On the low-motion Sintel dataset, WildPose achieves 0.017 m (scale-normalized), the best among all full-trajectory methods. For downstream depth estimation on Bonn, WildPose attains 96.3% δ₁.₂₅ and 0.12 Abs.Rel., surpassing all baselines including Video-Depth-Anything (95.7%, 0.14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildPose outperforms all full-trajectory baselines on Wild-SLAM MoCap (0.39 vs. 0.46 cm for WildGS-SLAM, 2.40 cm for MegaSaM, 2.59 cm for ViPE, and 16.17 cm for DROID-SLAM), with the largest margins on Table1/Table2 sequences where semantic-based ViPE fails (14.8 cm) due to tables being misclassified as static. On Bonn RGB-D Dynamic, WildPose (2.36 cm) is competitive with WildGS-SLAM (2.31 cm) but clearly beats MegaSaM (3.51 cm) and ViPE (2.93 cm); WildGS-SLAM's slight edge is attributed to its explicit illumination-change handling. On TUM Dynamic, WildPose achieves the best result (1.57 cm), narrowly beating ViPE (1.58 cm) and WildGS-SLAM/MegaSaM (1.63 cm). On static benchmarks, WildPose matches DROID-SLAM on 7-Scenes (0.049 m) and beats it on TUM static (0.027 vs. 0.038 m), while dynamic-specialized methods like MegaSaM (0.066 m) and ViPE (0.065 m) degrade substantially—validating the paper's unified-design claim. Feed-forward methods (VGGT, π³, MASt3R-SLAM) are evaluated only on keyframes due to memory constraints, yet still underperform WildPose's full-trajectory estimates on dynamic sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildPose fuses two previously separate paradigms in deep visual pose estimation: the rich 3D-aware perceptual frontend of feed-forward models (specifically MASt3R's ViT encoder) and the end-to-end differentiable bundle adjustment backend of DROID-SLAM. It departs from DROID-SLAM by replacing its scratch-trained CNN encoder with a frozen, pre-trained transformer backbone and by training the update operator on a mixed static-and-dynamic curriculum rather than static data alone. It also departs from prior dynamic-scene methods (WildGS-SLAM, MegaSaM, ViPE) by using a dedicated high-capacity motion mask detector that reads multi-level 3D-aware features and produces edge-dependent (pairwise) masks, rather than relying on semantic priors, per-sequence 3DGS rendering inconsistency, or a low-capacity recurrent hidden state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a single unified system that maintains state-of-the-art accuracy on static benchmarks (TUM, 7-Scenes, Sintel) while also leading on dynamic benchmarks (Wild-SLAM, Bonn, TUM dynamic), addressing a gap where prior dynamic-focused methods (MegaSaM, ViPE) degrade significantly on static inputs. The edge-dependent mask design resolves a subtle but important temporal ambiguity in dynamic object detection that per-frame approaches cannot handle. Open questions include scalability to very long sequences, real-time deployment (the pipeline involves a ViT encoder plus BA per edge), and whether the MoGe2 metric-depth dependency can be removed for truly monocular operation. The training cost (~2 weeks on 8 A100s) also limits accessibility.
