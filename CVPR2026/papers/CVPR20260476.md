## Problem

Scalable robot learning is bottlenecked by the high cost of collecting diverse, high-quality embodied demonstrations, as existing data generation methods depend on hard-to-access hardware (robotic platforms, teleoperation devices, depth cameras) or simulators, require substantial manual effort, and generalize poorly to diverse scene configurations. The paper asks whether diverse, photorealistic, and kinematically plausible robot data can be synthesized from a single monocular human video without any of these dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB human demonstration video (e.g., recorded with a smartphone or Azure Kinect) as its sole input, requiring no depth sensors, robot hardware, or teleoperation devices. It outputs synthetic robot demonstration episodes structured as paired RGB images (captured from front and side camera views) and absolute joint positions of a Franka arm, along with end-effector pose trajectories. Each generated episode incorporates randomized object poses, tabletop textures, scene backgrounds, lighting conditions, and camera parameters to maximize diversity.

## Method

Video2Robo is a three-stage pipeline built on 3D Gaussian Splatting. First, Scene and Skill Parsing extracts temporally consistent 6D object trajectories from the monocular video by combining Entityv2 instance segmentation, CoTracker3 pixel tracking, TRELLIS-generated 3DGS object models, and VGGT depth estimation; a novel differentiable 6D pose optimization (initialized via Differential Evolution, refined through 3DGS rendering losses on RGB/depth/mask plus a CoTracker3-based Track loss) ensures temporal consistency, and skills are segmented from relative inter-object motions. Second, Scalable Data Generation rearranges objects into novel configurations and executes a robot-object coupled motion strategy (Transit → Grasp → Transfer → Task Execution) to produce kinematically consistent dynamic 3DGS fields, while applying multi-dimensional augmentations (texture, background, lighting via Gaussian ellipsoid color manipulation, and virtual camera perturbation) to every frame. Third, a Diffusion Policy is trained on the synthesized image-action pairs and deployed zero-shot to a physical Franka arm without calibration or fine-tuning.

## Results

On 6D pose estimation (BOP Challenge Average Recall), Video2Robo achieves a mean of 78.41%, outperforming MegaPose (68.13%) and FoundationPose-O (55.36%). For data generation, Video2Robo produces each sample in 5.23 s on average with a 100% success rate, compared to 17.23 s / 97.40% for MimicGen and 8.88 s / 96.98% for SkillGen. In real-world deployment, policies trained on Video2Robo data achieve 62.50% mean success rate under pose-only variation and 58.33% under multi-variation settings, substantially exceeding all baselines (best baseline: SkillGen-O at 32.50% and 5.83% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 6D pose estimation, Video2Robo (78.41% mean AR) beats MegaPose (68.13%) by ~10 points and FoundationPose-O (55.36%) by ~23 points, with the largest gains on Place (+28.1 over F.P.-O) and Sweep (+18.4 over MegaPose). For data generation efficiency, Video2Robo is 3.3× faster than MimicGen (5.23 s vs 17.23 s) and 1.7× faster than SkillGen (5.23 s vs 8.88 s), while achieving a perfect 100% generation success rate versus 97.40% and 96.98% respectively. In simulation under multi-variation settings, Video2Robo (63.83%) outperforms SkillGen-O (44.67%) by ~19 points and MimicGen-O (29.67%) by ~34 points. In the real world under multi-variation settings, the gap widens dramatically: Video2Robo (58.33%) surpasses SkillGen-O (5.83%) by over 52 points and even exceeds a 20-real-demonstration baseline (27.50%) by ~31 points, demonstrating that synthetic data from a single video can outperform additional real teleoperation data in terms of generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

Video2Robo builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) as its scene representation and on RoboSplat (Yang et al., 2025) for the Franka arm 3DGS model and Gaussian-ellipsoid lighting manipulation. It departs from the dominant robot data generation paradigm—where methods like MimicGen, SkillGen, CyberDemo, and Phantom all require robot hardware, simulators, teleoperation devices, or depth sensors—by reducing the input to a single monocular RGB video. The key conceptual departure is repurposing 3DGS not merely as a renderer but as a differentiable tracking framework: by making object 6D poses learnable parameters within the 3DGS rendering pipeline and coupling them with CoTracker3 pixel tracking, the authors achieve temporally consistent pose estimation without depth sensors. This positions the work at the intersection of the Real2Sim2Real 3DGS literature (SplatSim, RoboGSim) and the human-video-to-robot imitation line (Phantom, YOTO, R2R2R), but removes the simulator and hardware dependencies that all prior methods retain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a smartphone video becomes a complete data generation pipeline, dramatically lowering the barrier for non-expert users to teach robots new tasks and for researchers to scale training data without expensive teleoperation rigs. The finding that 3DGS-rendered synthetic data (with multi-dimensional augmentation) outperforms 20 real teleoperation demonstrations in real-world transfer (62.5% vs. 46.7% mean success) suggests that visual diversity and photorealism may matter more than physical fidelity for visuomotor policy training. However, clear limitations remain—only rigid objects are supported, grasp poses require manual annotation, and absolute success rates (58–62%) are still moderate. Open questions include whether the approach extends to articulated and deformable objects, dual-arm manipulation, and whether the kinematic (non-physics) generation strategy holds for tasks requiring force or contact dynamics.
