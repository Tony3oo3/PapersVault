## Problem

Existing DiT-based Video-to-Audio (V2A) models treat text captions as coarse global semantic cues, making them unable to control the precise timing of individual sound events, especially when visual cues are insufficient (small regions, off-screen sounds, occluded objects). No prior method provides a mechanism for users to act as 'Foley directors' who specify when and how sounds should emerge, nor can existing models handle complex multi-event scenarios with both in-frame and out-of-frame sounds under explicit temporal guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261514/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video clip, a global text description, and Structured Temporal Scripts (STS)—a sequence of 1-second segment-level captions (e.g., 'dog barking, loud, sharp timbre') that can be auto-generated by Qwen-Omni or hand-written by users. It produces a temporally controlled audio waveform where each sound event is placed at user-specified segments. A key design property is that disabling the SG-TFM adapter reverts the model to standard V2A generation with no quality degradation, enabling seamless switching between free generation and directed synthesis.

## Method

FoleyDirector extends the pretrained MMAudio DiT generator with three components. First, STS features are extracted by encoding each 1-second segment caption with a CLIP text encoder and pooling to a single vector, then replicating across video tokens to form a temporally structured conditioning sequence. Second, the Script-Guided Temporal Fusion Module (SG-TFM) injects these features via an additional Temporal Script Attention (TSA) layer in each DiT block, leaving MMAudio's original joint audio-video-text attention untouched; Interleaved RoPE is applied by upsampling STS tokens, interleaving them with audio tokens, applying rotary positional encoding, and un-interleaving to enforce temporal alignment. Third, the Bi-Frame Sound Synthesis Framework duplicates the audio latent into two parallel streams—one conditioned on video+text+STS for in-frame sounds and one on a learnable null visual embedding+text+STS for out-of-frame sounds—then fuses them temporally per segment. Training fine-tunes MMAudio-medium for 1.2M iterations with STS randomly dropped at p=0.1 for classifier-free guidance, on 8×A800 GPUs.

## Results

On DirectorBench, FoleyDirector achieves an overall F1 of 0.4819, more than doubling the best baseline (Hunyuan-Foley at 0.2451). On the counterfactual control subset, F1 rises from 0.2331 (Hunyuan-Foley) to 0.5284, and on the temporal control subset from 0.2572 to 0.4354. On VGGSound-Director, FoleyDirector also achieves the best distribution matching (FD_VGG = 1.17 vs. 1.45 for MMAudio), lowest KL divergence (KL_PANN = 1.42, KL_PaSST = 1.23), and highest Inception Score (14.84), confirming that temporal control does not degrade audio quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoleyDirector outperforms all four baselines on every controllability metric in DirectorBench. Against MMAudio (its base model), overall F1 improves from 0.2378 to 0.4819 (+103%), with the largest gain on counterfactual control (0.1825 → 0.5284). Against Hunyuan-Foley, the strongest baseline, overall F1 doubles from 0.2451 to 0.4819, and FD_VGG improves from 7.51 to 6.19, showing better audio quality alongside superior control. ThinkSound and Video-Foley trail further behind (overall F1 of 0.1707 and 0.2163 respectively), with ThinkSound also showing the weakest semantic alignment (IB = 0.22) on VGGSound-Director. On VGGSound-Director, FoleyDirector matches or exceeds MMAudio on all quality metrics (FD_VGG 1.17 vs 1.45, ISC 14.84 vs 14.38, DeSync 0.432 vs 0.439), confirming that the added temporal control does not compromise the base generator's audio fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoleyDirector extends the DiT-based V2A paradigm established by MMAudio (CVPR 2025) by adding a lightweight adapter (SG-TFM) that injects segment-level temporal scripts, rather than modifying the base generator. It borrows the interleaved RoPE mechanism from HunyuanVideo-Foley for temporal alignment but repurposes it to fuse a new conditioning signal (Structured Temporal Scripts) rather than aligning pre-existing modalities. The key conceptual departure from prior V2A work (FoleyCrafter, Diff-Foley, Frieren, ThinkSound, Kling-Foley) is the shift from a single global caption to a sequence of per-segment scripts, treating audio generation as a series of short, independently controllable segments. The Bi-Frame framework further departs from prior single-stream V2A by explicitly separating in-frame and out-of-frame audio generation paths, a design not present in earlier multimodal audio synthesis systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to give users explicit, segment-level temporal control over V2A generation, moving the task from passive audio restoration toward active 'Foley directing'—a practically important capability for content creators who need to specify when off-screen or visually subtle sounds should occur. The seamless switchability (disabling SG-TFM reverts to standard V2A) makes the approach low-risk for practitioners already using MMAudio. Open questions include whether the 1-second STS granularity generalizes to longer-form video, how annotation errors from the MLLM-based pipeline propagate at scale, and whether the Bi-Frame separation degrades when in-frame and out-of-frame events overlap temporally. The self-constructed DirectorBench, while well-motivated, limits external validation of the reported controllability gains.
