## Problem

Diffusion-based dataset distillation methods typically require additional fine-tuning of the diffusion model on the target dataset or auxiliary classifier training, limiting their efficiency. Moreover, existing guidance mechanisms (e.g., mode-point guidance in MGD³) treat synthetic samples in isolation, neglecting the underlying distribution structure and inter-sample diversity. This paper asks whether effective, training-free guidance can be designed during the diffusion sampling process to simultaneously achieve semantic alignment and distributional structure matching.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained latent diffusion model (DiT) and a target dataset of 256×256 RGB images with class labels. During sampling, it consumes the target dataset's latent representations (projected onto a hypersphere) to compute optimal transport costs, and class labels for classifier-free guidance. The output is a small synthetic surrogate dataset (N_S ≪ N_T) of generated images with hard or soft labels, structured to preserve both per-class semantic content and the overall distributional geometry of the target. All guidance is applied at inference time during the denoising steps, requiring no gradient updates to the diffusion model.

## Method

DMGD decouples dataset distillation into two training-free guidance objectives applied during diffusion sampling. For Semantic Matching, it leverages classifier-free guidance to approximate conditional log-likelihood (eliminating auxiliary classifiers) and introduces a dynamic soft-label mechanism that modulates label conditioning across three temporal stages—stochastic exploration, dynamic soft-label guidance, and semantic refinement—to prevent over-sampling of high-density regions and enhance diversity. For Distribution Matching, it formulates an entropy-regularized optimal transport loss between the surrogate and target distributions in latent space, guided via the FREEDOM-style energy gradient. Two efficiency strategies make OT tractable: Distribution Approximate Matching replaces the full target distribution with a class-wise K-means quantization (K=10 support points), and Greedy Progressive Matching optimizes samples sequentially while freezing previously generated ones to prevent mode collapse. Theoretically, the paper proves that under semantic alignment, the OT distance between surrogate and target distributions upper-bounds the risk discrepancy (Theorem 1), and that the K-means approximation preserves this bound up to an additive ε term (Corollary 1).

## Results

On ImageNet-Woof (hard-label, ResNet10-AP), DMGD achieves 42.4% at IPC-10 and 60.8% at IPC-50 when deployed on MiniMax, outperforming the prior SOTA MGD³ (40.4% and 56.5%) by 2.0% and 4.3% respectively. On ImageNet-Nette, it reaches 68.7% (IPC-10) and 80.7% (IPC-50), surpassing MGD³ by 2.3% and 1.2%. On ImageNet-1K (soft-label, ResNet-18), DMGD attains 46.3% at IPC-10 and 61.4% at IPC-50, exceeding RDED by 4.3% and MGD³ by 1.2% at IPC-50. All gains are achieved without any additional fine-tuning of the diffusion model, whereas competing methods like MiniMax require ~0.7 hours of fine-tuning per 10-class subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMGD outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor MGD³ [8], it gains 0.4–3.6% on ImageNet-Woof and 1.1–2.3% on ImageNet-Nette, with the largest margin (3.6%) at Woof IPC-50 where distribution matching matters most. Compared to MiniMax [22], which requires ~0.7 h of fine-tuning per subset, DMGD (training-free) improves by 3.2–4.5% on Woof and 4.1–6.7% on Nette, demonstrating that the guidance mechanism alone closes the gap that fine-tuning was previously needed to bridge. On ImageNet-1K, DMGD surpasses RDED [61] by 4.3% at IPC-10 (46.3% vs 42.0%) and MGD³ [8] by 1.2% at IPC-50 (61.4% vs 60.2%), while also leading on the larger ResNet-101 backbone (50.6% vs 48.3% at IPC-10). The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by consistent gains when layered onto both DiT and MiniMax backbones, with average improvements of 8.1% over raw DiT and 5.0% over MiniMax on Nette. The only baseline it does not strictly dominate is MGD³ at Nette IPC-20 (71.1% vs 71.2%), a near-tie within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) to reframe diffusion model sampling as conditional likelihood optimization for semantic matching, and on training-free energy-guided diffusion (FREEDOM, Yu et al., 2023) to embed an optimal transport loss directly into the denoising steps. It departs from prior diffusion-based distillation methods (Minimax, MGD³, D⁴M) that either require fine-tuning the diffusion model or rely on isolated mode-center guidance, by decoupling the problem into two complementary objectives—semantic alignment and distributional alignment—both applied training-free during sampling. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) connects the OT distance between surrogate and target distributions to an upper bound on risk discrepancy, providing a principled justification for the distribution matching objective that prior heuristic approaches lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of DMGD is practically significant: it can be layered on top of existing diffusion-based distillation pipelines (e.g., Minimax) to yield further gains without any additional training, and the full surrogate dataset generation for ImageNet-Woof at IPC-50 takes only 0.26 hours. Open questions include whether the heuristic temporal window (t∈[30,45]) for distribution matching generalizes across architectures and noise schedules, whether the K-means-based distribution approximation remains effective for highly multimodal or long-tail class distributions, and whether the framework extends to video or 3D distillation where OT computation becomes even more prohibitive. The method still depends on a strong pre-trained diffusion model, so its ceiling is bounded by the base model's generative quality.
