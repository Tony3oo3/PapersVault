## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based methods for inverse problems assume measurements are corrupted only by Gaussian noise and fail when observations contain arbitrary outliers (e.g., sensor faults, transmission errors). This paper addresses the gap by formulating outlier-robust diffusion solvers that can handle a fraction ρ of arbitrarily corrupted measurement entries while still leveraging the generative prior of a pretrained diffusion model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263912/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy measurement vector y ∈ ℝ^m (with a fraction ρ of entries replaced by arbitrary outlier values), a forward operator A(·) (linear matrix or nonlinear learned blur), and a pretrained score/data-prediction network. It produces a reconstructed 256×256 RGB image after N diffusion sampling steps, each involving J inner optimization iterations. The method is task-agnostic and plug-and-play: no retraining or paired data is required, and it handles both linear operators (inpainting, super-resolution, Gaussian/motion deblurring) and nonlinear operators (learned deblurring kernels).

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage robust objective solved at each diffusion timestep: (1) explicit noise estimation refines the measurement y to ȳ by analytically estimating the additive Gaussian noise component, and (2) the L2 data-fidelity term is replaced with an element-wise Huber loss expressed in quadratic form via an iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) scheme, where the diagonal weight matrix W_δ down-weights large residuals. Two solvers are proposed: Robust-GD uses standard gradient descent with a fixed learning rate, while Robust-CG replaces GD with the conjugate gradient method using the Fletcher-Reeves update rule and a closed-form step size (exact for linear operators, finite-difference Jacobian approximation for nonlinear ones), eliminating the need for delicate learning-rate tuning. The entire procedure is embedded within a DDIM-style sampling loop, where at each timestep the robust optimization subproblem is solved to obtain x̄_0, and the next latent is sampled from N(α_{t_{i-1}} x̄_0, σ_{t_{i-1}}² I).

## Results

On the super-resolution (4×) task with ρ=0.10 contamination, Robust-CG achieves 28.96 dB PSNR and 0.129 LPIPS on CelebA, outperforming the best baseline (DPS at 20.95 dB PSNR) by over 8 dB. On inpainting (random 70%) under the same conditions, Robust-CG reaches 29.74 dB PSNR and 0.093 LPIPS on CelebA, compared to the best baseline DCPS at 24.26 dB PSNR. Under high Gaussian noise (σ=0.5), Robust-CG still leads with 23.04 dB PSNR on super-resolution versus 20.55 dB for DPS. The method's advantage is most pronounced at the higher contamination level (ρ=0.10), where non-robust baselines degrade severely (e.g., DiffPIR drops to 14.76 dB PSNR on SR-CelebA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust-CG outperforms all five baselines (DPS, DiffPIR, DCPS, RED-diff, DAPS) across nearly every metric and task setting. The gap is most dramatic at ρ=0.10: on super-resolution (CelebA), Robust-CG achieves 28.96 dB PSNR versus 20.95 for DPS, 16.63 for DAPS, and 14.76 for DiffPIR — a margin of 8–14 dB over the strongest baselines. On inpainting (CelebA), Robust-CG reaches 29.74 dB PSNR, beating the best baseline DCPS (24.26 dB) by 5.5 dB. Robust-GD also consistently outperforms all baselines but by a smaller margin (e.g., 26.52 vs. 20.95 dB on SR-CelebA), confirming that the conjugate gradient solver provides a meaningful improvement over plain gradient descent. Notably, DiffPIR and DCPS suffer the most severe degradation under high contamination (ρ=0.10), with PSNR dropping by 5–7 dB relative to their ρ=0.02 performance, while Robust-CG degrades by less than 1 dB, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Huber-loss-based robust objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: DM-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DiffPIR, DCPS, RED-diff, DAPS) and robust estimation with Huber loss. It adopts DAPS's multi-step reverse process for signal estimation and its time-dependent guidance schedule (γ_t = 1/σ_t), then introduces a Huber-loss-based robust fidelity term following Song & Huang (IJCV 2024), expressed in iteratively reweighted least squares form per Kümmerle et al. (NeurIPS 2021). The key departure from all prior DM-based IP methods is the explicit treatment of arbitrary outliers in the measurement, which none of the compared baselines address. The conjugate gradient solver with a g^T g step-size formula and finite-difference Jacobian-vector products is a practical optimization contribution layered on top of the robust objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real-world measurements (sensor faults, transmission errors) routinely contain outliers that destroy standard L2-based DM solvers, yet no prior DM-IP method handles them. The plug-and-play nature (no retraining, works with any pretrained DM) makes the approach broadly applicable. Limitations include testing only on 256×256 images, a single outlier model (constant −1 values), and no comparison against other robust strategies (trimmed loss, median-of-means) applied within the DM framework. Open questions include whether the Huber threshold δ can be learned or adapted per-timestep, how performance scales to higher resolutions, and whether the method extends to structured or correlated outliers beyond the i.i.d. corruption model.
