## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for VLMs operate late (after ViT completes full computation), missing the dominant computational cost within the ViT itself, which accounts for over 50% of prefill latency. Early pruning inside ViT is underexplored because it lacks cross-modal signals: without textual guidance, pruning based solely on visual saliency discards query-critical but visually inconspicuous tokens (e.g., small text), causing irrecoverable information loss.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by ViT into a sequence of N visual tokens) and a text query (embedded into the LLM's text space). Pruning is applied at three fixed ViT depths (1/4, 1/2, 3/4 of total blocks) with a configurable rate R (25% during training, adjustable at inference without retraining). The output is a standard auto-regressive text answer, with the pruned visual token sequence (plus one aggregated token) passed to the LLM. The 2×2 spatial grouping ensures compatibility with the pixel-shuffle or MLP-based token merging used by InternVL and Qwen-VL before LLM input.

## Method

QuietPrune introduces a lightweight text-to-vision adapter that converts query embeddings into the visual domain by applying an inverse transformation of the pretrained vision-to-text projector (using Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse for non-invertible linear layers, approximate scale/bias inversion for LayerNorm, and the same GELU activation). The adapter's output is pooled into a single [Q-CLS] token prepended to the visual sequence; attention scores between [Q-CLS] and each visual token—naturally computed during ViT forward pass with zero extra latency—serve as the visual-textual relevance metric. Pruning is semi-structured: spatially adjacent 2×2 token groups are scored by their mean relevance, and low-relevance groups are removed as indivisible units to preserve positional continuity for downstream token merging. Pruned tokens are aggregated into a single weighted-sum token to retain residual context. Training uses self-distillation (KL divergence between pruned and un-pruned logits plus cross-entropy) updating only the adapter while keeping the entire VLM frozen, requiring ~40 minutes on a single A100 with 10K samples.

## Results

At a 50% average pruning rate in the LLM, QuietPrune achieves 98.5% relative accuracy (RA) and 42.1% latency reduction (LR) on InternVL3-1B, 95.7% RA and 33.1% LR on Qwen3-VL-4B, and 97.4% RA and 49.3% LR on InternVL3-8B. The method outperforms the best late-pruning baseline by up to 4.2% in RA while delivering up to 19% greater latency reduction. On OCRBench specifically, QuietPrune shows the largest accuracy advantage (e.g., 77.3 vs. 66.9 for FastV on InternVL3-1B), confirming the benefit of query-guided retention for fine-grained text regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuietPrune outperforms all six baselines on relative accuracy across every model size. Against the strongest late-pruning method PACT (94.0% RA on InternVL3-1B), QuietPrune leads by 4.5% RA while also achieving 22% greater latency reduction (42.1% vs. 20.0%). DivPrune and AIM suffer from expensive pruning-decision overhead: DivPrune actually increases prefill latency by 24.8% on InternVL3-1B (negative LR), and AIM drops to 86.6% RA with only 3.2% LR. SAINT-Early achieves comparable latency reduction (39.0% on InternVL3-1B) but loses 15.7% RA due to unstructured, visual-saliency-only pruning that discards query-critical tokens. VisPruner's unstructured pre-projector pruning is incompatible with 2×2 token merging, yielding the worst accuracy (76.1% RA on InternVL3-1B). FastV, the earliest baseline, shows moderate accuracy (94.3% RA) but limited latency gains (16.8% LR) because it only prunes within the LLM, leaving the ViT computation untouched.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant late-pruning paradigm (FastV, DivPrune, PACT, AIM, VisPruner) that removes visual tokens only after the ViT has fully processed them, arguing that the ViT itself is the dominant latency bottleneck (>50% of prefill, >75% for small models). It also extends the only prior early-pruning method (SAINT) by adding query guidance via a text-to-vision adapter initialized through inverse transformation of the pretrained projector, and by introducing semi-structured 2×2 group pruning to remain compatible with the token-merging operations in modern VLMs like InternVL3 and Qwen3-VL. The inverse-transformer initialization trick repurposes an existing architectural component (the vision-to-text projector) in reverse, avoiding the need for a separately trained cross-modal encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ViT computation dominates prefill latency—especially for smaller, more deployable VLMs—shifts the efficiency focus from the LLM side to the vision encoder, a region largely ignored by prior pruning work. The method's ability to simultaneously reduce latency (up to 19%) and improve accuracy (up to 4.2%) over late pruning is practically significant for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed three-layer pruning schedule generalizes to video or ultra-high-resolution inputs, whether the adapter can be made fully training-free, and whether the semi-structured constraint remains optimal as token-merging strategies evolve beyond 2×2 blocks.
