## Problem

Real-time uncalibrated monocular SLAM remains challenging because existing feed-forward geometry-based pipelines (MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM) require per-frame dense depth inference and operate below 30 FPS, while classical sparse geometric SLAM systems lack modularity and cannot handle unknown camera intrinsics. No prior framework combines the efficiency of sparse keypoint tracking with the geometric priors of feed-forward estimators to achieve real-time uncalibrated monocular SLAM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262287/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a stream of uncalibrated monocular RGB frames with timestamps, requiring no camera intrinsics (focal length is estimated online via P4Pf and then fixed to a median). Unlike dense baselines that drop two of every three frames, SLAM-MER processes every frame of a 30 FPS stream in real-time (80+ FPS). Outputs include per-frame camera poses, a sparse 3D map of keypoints, a semi-dense point cloud anchored to those map points, and a continuously updated covisibility graph maintained by an incremental ISAM2 solver.

## Method

SLAM-MER introduces a spatio-temporal querying mechanism for 3D map points: a temporal buffer of N recent frames preserves short-term continuity by tracking keypoints across frames, while a 3D cell-based spatial index retrieves relevant points from previously reconstructed regions via rasterization of visible cells. Localization uses only 3D-2D correspondences (not per-frame 3D-3D matching), with feed-forward depth inference (MASt3R) invoked exclusively at keyframe creation to generate local 3D points that are scaled and added to the map. Pose estimation employs the P4Pf minimal solver within RANSAC for the uncalibrated case, transitioning to P3P once the focal length stabilizes. The adjustment module runs in a parallel thread using ISAM2/GTSAM for incremental factor-graph optimization, unifying local and global adjustments without separate branches, and loop closure adds constraints to the graph without triggering explicit re-optimization.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes dataset (uncalibrated setting), SLAM-MER achieves an average ATE of 0.059 m at 103.2 FPS, compared to VGGT-SLAM (SL(4)) at 0.056 m but <5 FPS, and MASt3R-SLAM at 0.058 m at 15.0 FPS. On TUM RGB-D (uncalibrated), SLAM-MER reports 0.056 m average ATE at 86.6 FPS, versus VGGT-SLAM's 0.053 m at <5 FPS. For dense 3D reconstruction on 7-Scenes, SLAM-MER achieves the best accuracy (0.034 m) among all methods, a 35% improvement over the best baseline, though it trails on completion (0.135 m) due to its anchor-point-based semi-dense representation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among uncalibrated methods on 7-Scenes, SLAM-MER (0.059 m avg ATE) is within 0.003 m of VGGT-SLAM SL(4) (0.056 m) and MASt3R-SLAM (0.058 m), while running at 103.2 FPS versus <5 and 15.0 FPS respectively — a 20×+ speedup over VGGT-SLAM. On TUM RGB-D (uncalibrated), SLAM-MER (0.056 m) is marginally behind VGGT-SLAM SL(4) (0.053 m) but 17× faster (86.6 vs <5 FPS). Compared to the calibrated sparse-loc baseline DPV-SLAM++ (~30 FPS, 0.054 m avg on TUM), SLAM-MER is 3× faster while producing a semi-dense map. Against DROID-SLAM (~20 FPS), SLAM-MER is at least 5× faster. For dense 3D reconstruction, SLAM-MER achieves the best accuracy (0.034 m) but the worst completion (0.135 m) and Chamfer distance (0.084 m) among uncalibrated methods, as its anchor-point densification leaves regions without supporting map-points uncovered.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLAM-MER carves a hybrid position between two established lines of work: traditional geometric SLAM (MonoSLAM → PTAM → ORB-SLAM) that relies on sparse keypoint tracking and 3D-2D pose estimation, and the recent wave of feed-forward geometry estimators (MASt3R, VGGT, DUSt3R) that produce dense point clouds from uncalibrated images. Rather than committing to either extreme, the paper repurposes the sparse keypoint machinery of classical SLAM for per-frame localization while invoking feed-forward depth inference only at keyframe creation, and introduces a spatio-temporal querying mechanism (temporal frame buffer + 3D cell-based spatial index) that has no direct analogue in prior SLAM pipelines. The factor-graph back-end follows the ISAM2/GTSAM lineage, and the uncalibrated pose estimation leverages the P4Pf minimal solver, but the overall pipeline architecture and the dual-query retrieval strategy are new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SLAM-MER is the first uncalibrated monocular SLAM system to exceed 80 FPS while maintaining competitive ATE, a 5–10× efficiency gap over the closest dense-map competitors (MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM). The modular C++ framework, to be publicly released, lowers the barrier for integrating future feed-forward estimators or VPR models into real-time pipelines. Key limitations remain: the anchor-based densification sacrifices completion relative to fully dense methods, the spatial cell query degrades under high drift (requiring explicit loop closure), and the system has not been validated on outdoor or large-scale sequences. The authors note that incorporating SL(4) pose representation is a promising next step, and the open question of whether the sparse-localization + occasional-depth paradigm can match fully dense 3D-3D matching on more challenging, texture-poor scenes remains unresolved.
