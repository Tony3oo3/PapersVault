## Problem

Existing head-swapping methods rely on face-centered cropped datasets with limited view angles, restricting their ability to handle diverse head orientations, expressions, and hairstyles in full upper-body images. Because ground-truth head-swapped pairs do not exist, models are forced into self-supervised training, which severely limits generalization across facial expressions and head poses. This paper tackles the gap by enabling zero-shot head swapping on full upper-body images that faithfully preserves source identity, hairstyle, and accessories while reenacting the target's pose and expression.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source head image and a target upper-body image as input, along with derived conditioning signals: a densepose map of the target, a head normal map rendered from EMOCA-extracted FLAME parameters (with source shape substituted into target pose/expression), and an adaptive inpainting mask. The output is a full upper-body image where the source person's head (identity, hairstyle, skin tone, accessories) seamlessly replaces the target's head while matching the target's head orientation, pose, and facial expression. Unlike face-swap methods that operate on cropped face regions, AHS handles the entire head-and-hair region within the context of the full upper body.

## Method

AHS builds on an SDXL Inpainting U-Net (S-Net) augmented with a dual cross-attention mechanism: a face encoder (inspired by Photomaker) and a head encoder (inspired by IP-Adapter) inject high-level identity semantics, while a self-attention branch (following virtual try-on literature) pulls low-level features from a frozen reference H-Net. Pose and expression control is achieved by merging a densepose map with a normal map rendered from FLAME geometry, providing explicit geometric conditioning without requiring a separate ControlNet. The key training innovation is a synthetic data augmentation strategy that uses GAGAvatar to generate head-reenacted pairs with varied orientations and expressions, overcoming the self-supervised training bottleneck where source and target are the same person. Additionally, adaptive mask augmentation (dilation, widened bounding boxes, random mask merging) prevents the model from overfitting to the target's hair silhouette and handles heterogeneous head sizes. At inference, FLAME shape parameters from the source are substituted into the target's pose and expression parameters before rendering the normal map, and a two-stage inpainting procedure (wide bounding box followed by precise head mask) ensures natural blending for large head-size or hairstyle differences.

## Results

On the SHHQ dataset, AHS achieves the best scores in identity similarity (0.6230), cropped FID (5.02), CLIP-I head quality (0.9132), and expression preservation (6.204) among all compared methods. AHS trails REFace on head orientation error (8.01 vs. 5.57) and HID on full-image FID (5.78 vs. 5.63), though the paper attributes REFace's FID advantage to its crop-and-paste strategy rather than holistic generation. A user study with 19 participants across 20 image pairs confirms AHS is consistently preferred over all baselines on overall quality, reenactment fidelity, hairstyle similarity, and accessories preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AHS outperforms all three baselines on identity similarity (0.6230 vs. 0.5555 for HID, 0.5239 for REFace, 0.2829 for InstantID), cropped FID (5.02 vs. 6.83, 24.31, 11.43), CLIP-I head quality (0.9132 vs. 0.8577, 0.7859, 0.8223), and expression preservation (6.204 vs. 7.308, 7.591, 8.474). Against HID, the closest zero-shot head-swapping baseline, AHS improves ID similarity by 12% and expression error by 27%, but HID retains a slight edge on full-image FID (5.63 vs. 5.78) and head orientation (11.51 vs. 8.01 — actually AHS is better here; HID is worse). REFace achieves the best head orientation (5.57) by directly inputting cropped faces and landmarks, but at the cost of the worst cropped FID (24.31) and significantly lower identity and expression scores. InstantID, adapted via IP-Adapter and ControlNet, shows the weakest identity preservation (0.2829) and is outperformed by AHS on every metric except full-image FID where they are nearly tied (5.79 vs. 5.79). Overall, AHS wins on 4 of 6 metrics and is competitive on the remaining two, with the paper arguing that its holistic generation approach is more practically useful than REFace's crop-and-paste strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AHS extends the zero-shot head swapping line initiated by the authors' own HID [25] and the few-shot approach of Shu et al. [50], moving from face-centered crops or video-based preprocessing to full upper-body images with a single unified model. Architecturally, it composes identity encoders from Photomaker [35] and IP-Adapter [57] with a self-attention mechanism borrowed from virtual try-on [9], while replacing the ControlNet-OpenPose conditioning of HID with a simpler densepose + normal-map input derived from EMOCA [14]. The key conceptual departure is the use of GAGAvatar [11] as a synthetic data augmentation engine to generate diverse pose/expression training pairs, directly addressing the self-supervised training bottleneck that plagues all prior head-swapping methods lacking paired ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Head swapping remains substantially underexplored relative to face swapping, and AHS provides the first zero-shot, single-model solution that handles full upper-body images with diverse hairstyles, accessories, and extreme pose variations without video data or multi-stage pipelines. The synthetic augmentation strategy is a practical recipe that could transfer to other self-supervised editing tasks where paired data is unavailable. However, the paper acknowledges that lighting invariance is not yet solved (requiring additional relighting augmentation à la IC-Light), and edge cases with severe occlusions or extreme facial-structure mismatches still produce artifacts, leaving robustness in the wild as an open question.
