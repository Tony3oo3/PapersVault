## Problem

Current affective multimodal language models are 'level specialists' that excel within a single cognitive stratum (perception, understanding, or interaction) but fail to integrate knowledge across the full perception-to-empathy continuum. This fragmentation, combined with the lack of task-aware feature fusion and the heavy resource demands of 7–9B parameter models, prevents the development of a unified, deployable affective agent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260999/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Nano-EmoX consumes video frames (3×H×W), 1.6 kHz Mel-spectrogram audio, and text (dialogues/subtitles) simultaneously, producing six distinct outputs: discrete emotion labels, continuous sentiment scores, open-vocabulary multi-label emotion descriptions, intention labels, causal emotion-reasoning explanations, and empathetic text responses. For the empathetic response generation task, inputs are structured as turn-based dialogue history (Q₁,R₁),…,(Qₙᵗ,Qₙᵛ,Qₙᵃ) with the model's chain-of-thought reasoning wrapped in think tags before the final response. The entire 2.2B model operates with compact token budgets (32 visual, 32 speech, 4 facial, 1 fusion tokens) to maintain efficiency.

## Method

Nano-EmoX integrates four modality-specific branches into a Qwen2.5-1.5B language backbone: a CLIP-Large visual encoder with Q-Former resampling, a HuBERT-Large speech encoder with Q-Former, an enhanced FaceXFormer encoder shifted from image-level to frame-sequence processing with cross-attention temporal modeling, and a hierarchical expert fusion encoder where three independently-weighted experts process multi-layer features from the visual and speech encoders (layers 12/16/22 and 16/18/22 respectively) via speech-guided cross-attention, with a gating network dynamically routing expert contributions per task. Training follows the P2E (Perception-to-Empathy) three-phase curriculum: Phase 1 aligns modality adapters to the LM embedding space on unimodal emotion datasets; Phase 2 activates the fusion encoder through multimodal intention recognition as a bridge task; Phase 3 performs multitask instruction tuning with LoRA (r=32, α=16) on a curated data mixture (MER:OV-MER:MIR:ERI:ERG = 18:28:5:31:18). The entire pipeline trains on a single RTX 4090 in 32 hours, and the shallow-to-deep curriculum ordering is validated by a reverse-order ablation showing 33% degradation on ERG.

## Results

Nano-EmoX (2.2B) achieves an average score of 74.01 across MSA, MER, and OV-MER benchmarks, approaching the 74.77 of the much larger AffectGPT (6.1B) with 73% fewer parameters. It sets new SOTA on MER2023 (79.09 Hit Rate), MELD (56.55 Hit Rate), and OV-MERD (64.75 WAF). On the ERG task, Nano-EmoX reaches a Hit Rate of 91.13 (a 22.54% improvement over the previous SOTA) and an Acc of 54.82 for emotion recognition prior to empathetic response generation. On MIntRec 2.0 (MIR), it surpasses GPT-4 (3-shot) by 12.4% in accuracy and 7.6% in WF1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest large-scale baseline AffectGPT (6.1B), Nano-EmoX (2.2B) trails by only 0.76 in average score (74.01 vs. 74.77) while using 73% fewer parameters, and actually surpasses it on MER2023 (79.09 vs. 78.54), MELD (56.55 vs. 55.65), and OV-MERD (64.75 vs. 62.52). Among small-scale models, Nano-EmoX dominates: it outperforms R1-Omni (0.1B) by 15.55 in average (74.01 vs. 58.46), MobileVLM V2-3B (0.8B) by 24.06, and MobileVLM V2-1.7B (0.5B) by 29.11. On the ERI task, Nano-EmoX (Clue Overlap 7.83, Label Overlap 5.78) matches Emotion-LLaMA (7.83/6.25) and approaches Omni-Emotion (8.22/6.78) and Emotion-Qwen (8.25/8.16) despite being 3–4× smaller. On MIR (MIntRec 2.0), Nano-EmoX beats GPT-4 3-shot by 5.22 Acc (47.32 vs. 42.10) and 3.35 WF1 (47.27 vs. 43.92), but remains well below Qwen-VL-72B (66.99 Acc) and LLaVA-Video-72B (64.98 Acc). On ERG, Nano-EmoX achieves the best Hit Rate (91.13) and Acc (54.82) among all small-scale models, surpassing AffectGPT(s) (90.88/47.36) and MobileVLM V2-3B (61.42/25.18), though it lags behind the 7B+ models enhanced with E3RG (e.g., Ola-Omni-7B at 75.6 Hit Rate). The P2E curriculum itself is validated by the 3.6% average gain over AffectGPT(s) trained with its original method, and the 17.93% Hit Rate improvement over joint training on ERG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the affective MLM line (Emotion-LLaMA, AffectGPT, Omni-Emotion) by introducing a three-level cognitive hierarchy that organizes previously fragmented tasks—perception, understanding, and interaction—into a unified progression. Architecturally, it builds on FaceXFormer (enhanced with temporal cross-attention for frame-sequence processing) and borrows the gated mixture-of-experts pattern from MoE literature, but departs from prior work by combining a dedicated facial branch, a hierarchical audio-visual fusion encoder, and a 1.5B LM into a single 2.2B model. The P2E curriculum framework is the key conceptual departure: rather than joint or single-stage training used by AffectGPT or EmoVerse, it explicitly sequences learning from modality alignment → cross-modal fusion → multitask empathy, mirroring a cognitive development model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 2.2B model can match or exceed 7–8B affective MLMs across six tasks, which is practically significant for deploying emotion-aware systems on edge or resource-constrained hardware. The P2E reverse-order ablation (33% ERG Hit Rate drop) provides strong evidence that cognitive progression in training order is not merely a heuristic but a structural requirement for empathy-level capabilities. Open questions remain: MIR performance still lags substantially larger models (suggesting a resolution/token-count bottleneck), the framework has not been validated beyond the specific task set, and whether the hierarchy generalizes to new affective tasks or modalities (e.g., physiological signals) is untested.
