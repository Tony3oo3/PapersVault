## Problem

Existing dynamic SLAM methods either rely on predefined object-detection priors or couple uncertainty estimation to high-quality NeRF/Gaussian Splatting scene representations, causing both to fail in cluttered, unconstrained real-world environments where geometric mapping is unreliable. This paper addresses the gap by decoupling dynamic uncertainty estimation from scene representation quality, enabling robust tracking without requiring accurate 3D reconstruction or known object classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261472/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a casually captured RGB video sequence (e.g., 1200×1600 frames from the DROID-W dataset or YouTube clips up to 30 minutes long) and internally operates at 1/8 resolution. It produces three outputs: an SE(3) camera trajectory, a dynamic 3D point cloud, and a per-pixel dynamic uncertainty map that delineates moving regions. The system runs in real time at approximately 10 FPS on a single RTX 3090 GPU, with sequences longer than 5 minutes partitioned into non-overlapping segments.

## Method

DROID-W adapts DROID-SLAM by introducing a differentiable Uncertainty-aware Bundle Adjustment (UBA) that weights reprojection residuals with a per-pixel dynamic uncertainty term in the Mahalanobis distance. The key insight is measuring multi-view inconsistency via cosine similarity of DINOv2 (FiT3D) features across image pairs rather than reprojection errors or photometric losses tied to a scene representation, making uncertainty estimation robust even when geometric mapping degrades. Optimization is interleaved: pose and depth are refined via Gauss-Newton on the uncertainty-weighted energy, while uncertainty is updated via gradient descent with weight decay on a similarity loss plus a logarithmic prior, with bidirectional uncertainties per image pair to decouple inter-frame dynamics. Uncertainty is parameterized through a local affine mapping followed by Softplus applied to DINOv2 features, providing spatial regularization within a sliding window. Metric3D monocular depth predictions serve as an additional regularization term in the BA cost to stabilize tracking during initialization in highly dynamic scenes.

## Results

On the Bonn RGB-D Dynamic dataset, DROID-W achieves 2.30 cm average ATE RMSE, outperforming the closest baseline WildGS-SLAM (2.52 cm) and the static DROID-SLAM (4.91 cm). On the novel large-scale outdoor DROID-W dataset, it achieves 0.230 m ATE RMSE, substantially beating WildGS-SLAM (0.637 m) and DROID-SLAM (1.460 m). The system runs at approximately 10 FPS on an RTX 3090, achieving a 40× speedup over WildGS-SLAM while maintaining real-time performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DROID-W achieves the best average ATE RMSE across all four evaluation datasets, outperforming every baseline. On Bonn RGB-D Dynamic, it beats the closest competitor WildGS-SLAM by 0.22 cm (2.30 vs 2.52) and the static DROID-SLAM by 2.61 cm (2.30 vs 4.91); on TUM RGB-D it leads WildGS-SLAM by 0.15 cm (1.36 vs 1.51). On the DyCheck dataset, DROID-W (0.034) surpasses DROID-SLAM (0.044) and WildGS-SLAM (0.056), with the gap widening on sequences with heavy dynamic motion. On the novel DROID-W outdoor dataset, the advantage is most dramatic: 0.230 m vs 0.637 m for WildGS-SLAM and 1.460 m for DROID-SLAM, a roughly 3× improvement over the best prior method. Feed-forward approaches (MonST3R, TTT3R) consistently trail optimization-based SLAM by large margins (e.g., 22.55 cm vs 2.30 cm on Bonn). In terms of speed, DROID-W runs at ~10 FPS, 40× faster than WildGS-SLAM (0.22 FPS) and only slightly slower than DROID-SLAM (19.89 FPS), making it the fastest method that handles dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DROID-W directly extends DROID-SLAM's differentiable bundle adjustment framework by inserting a per-pixel dynamic uncertainty term into the Mahalanobis weighting, enabling the system to downweight correspondences corrupted by motion. It departs conceptually from recent uncertainty-aware SLAM methods (WildGS-SLAM, UP-SLAM, NeRF-on-the-go) that optimize uncertainty under photometric/depth supervision from a rendered NeRF or 3DGS map, instead estimating uncertainty purely from multi-view DINOv2 feature similarity—decoupling it from scene-representation quality. It also contrasts with feed-forward reconstruction approaches (MonST3R, TTT3R) that handle dynamics in short clips but lack the metric consistency and long-sequence stability of optimization-based SLAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The system delivers real-time (~10 FPS) dynamic SLAM on large-scale outdoor scenes where prior methods either fail or run orders of magnitude slower, and the introduced DROID-W dataset fills a gap in unconstrained outdoor dynamic benchmarks. The key open limitation is that uncertainty estimation depends on frame-to-frame alignment, making it unreliable during SLAM initialization when poses are still poor; incorporating reconstruction priors into the initialization stage is identified as a natural next step. More broadly, the finding that feature-similarity-based uncertainty suffices without a high-quality 3D map challenges the assumption in prior work that uncertainty optimization is tightly coupled to scene-representation fidelity.
