## Problem

Existing SNN robustness research evaluates adversarial vulnerability using time-averaged gradients, treating the temporal dimension as a monolithic whole. This overlooks the fact that SNN gradients and learning dynamics vary significantly across individual time steps, potentially masking weak points that targeted attacks could exploit and limiting the effectiveness of defenses built on averaged-gradient assumptions.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) or event streams (DVS-CIFAR10), which are encoded into spike sequences of length T (typically 4–10 timesteps) via direct replication or temporal encoding. Beyond standard class labels, the framework produces per-timestep Temporal Sensitivity Values (TSV) as a closed-form robustness metric, and TG-attack generates adversarial examples as perturbed spike sequences where each timestep is independently perturbed along its own gradient direction before being recombined into a single adversarial input.

## Method

The paper introduces three linked components. First, TG-attack replaces the conventional time-averaged gradient (1/T)Σ∂L/∂s_t with per-timestep gradients ∂L[t]/∂s_t, constructing perturbations independently at each step and combining them into the final adversarial example, which yields higher attack success rates than FGSM/PGD/BIM baselines. Second, a theoretical derivation (Theorem 1) shows that the KL-divergence-based robustness at time t is governed by the Hessian of the log-softmax output with respect to s_t, defined as the Temporal Sensitivity Matrix (TSM), whose element-wise sum gives the scalar TSV. Third, TG-Reg is a training-time regularization that minimizes the sum of TSV across all timesteps; to avoid expensive second-order Hessian computation, TSV is approximated by the Frobenius norm of the outer product of first-order input-output gradients at each step, making it a lightweight addition to the standard cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (VGG11, T=4) trained with adversarial examples, TG-Reg achieves 48.64% accuracy under FGSM and 31.12% under PGD, outperforming AT (37.88%/20.01%), RAT (40.78%/22.35%), and SR (36.87%/13.11%). On CIFAR-100 (VGG11, T=8), TG-Reg reaches 20.67% under FGSM and 11.83% under PGD, surpassing AT (17.14%/9.76%), RAT (19.07%/10.42%), and SR (11.48%/3.62%). Under the proposed TG-attacks, the gap widens further: TG-Reg achieves 27.54% under TG-PGD on CIFAR-10 versus 17.11% (AT) and 12.14% (SR). TG-Reg also improves over FEEL-SNN by up to 3% (FGSM), 2% (PGD), and 3% (BIM) on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TG-Reg consistently outperforms all three adversarial-training baselines (AT, RAT, SR) across both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 under every evaluated attack. On CIFAR-10 (VGG11, T=4), TG-Reg gains +10.76 pp over AT and +11.77 pp over SR under FGSM, and +11.11 pp over AT and +18.01 pp over SR under PGD. On CIFAR-100 (VGG11, T=8), the advantage over SR is particularly large: +17.21 pp under PGD (11.83% vs 3.62%) and +7.81 pp under FGSM (20.67% vs 11.48%). Against the temporal-dynamics baselines (StoG, DLIF, FEEL-SNN) trained with original samples, TG-Reg also leads: on CIFAR-100 it exceeds FEEL-SNN by +3.39 pp (FGSM), +2.55 pp (PGD), and +2.28 pp (BIM). The proposed TG-attack itself is more effective than standard attacks, e.g., reducing VGG11 CIFAR-10 accuracy from 12.71% (FGSM) to 9.72% (TG-FGSM) and from 0.38% (PGD) to 0.21% (TG-PGD) for vanilla-trained models, confirming that per-time-step gradients expose vulnerabilities invisible to averaged-gradient attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends standard gradient-based adversarial attacks (FGSM, PGD, BIM) to the SNN setting by replacing the conventional time-averaged gradient with per-time-step gradients, arguing that prior SNN robustness work (StoG, DLIF, FEEL-SNN, RAT, SR) implicitly suppresses time-specific vulnerability directions. The theoretical contribution—linking per-time-step robustness to the Hessian of the log-softmax (Temporal Sensitivity Matrix)—is a new analytical tool not present in prior SNN robustness literature, which has focused on parameter-level (leak factor, threshold) or coding-level (frequency, smoothing) interventions. The proposed TG-Reg regularization departs from adversarial training (AT, RAT) and gradient-sparsity regularization (SR) by directly constraining the first-order gradient magnitude at each time step, offering a lighter-weight alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that per-time-step attacks cause substantially larger accuracy drops than averaged-gradient attacks (e.g., 12.71% → 9.72% under FGSM vs. TG-FGSM on VGG11/CIFAR-10) suggests that prior SNN robustness evaluations may have been overly optimistic, which is practically important for anyone deploying SNNs in safety-critical settings. TSV provides a gradient-only diagnostic for identifying vulnerable time steps without generating adversarial examples, which could streamline robustness auditing. Open questions include whether the TSV approximation (Eq. 21, dropping the Hessian term) remains valid for deeper networks or longer time horizons, and whether the ~2× training overhead of TG-Reg scales acceptably to larger architectures and datasets beyond CIFAR/Tiny-ImageNet.
