## Problem

Existing RAW-to-RGB pipelines for visual perception face a fundamental trade-off: large learned ISP networks (e.g., MW-ISPNet, InvISP) achieve strong accuracy but impose prohibitive computational and memory costs for on-device deployment, while lightweight parameter-tuning approaches (e.g., DIAP, RAW-Adapter) remain constrained by the limited representational capacity of conventional ISP designs that can only adjust global or per-channel parameters. Neither extreme adequately captures the complex, spatially varying transformations that downstream perception tasks require across diverse scenes and lighting conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes packed RAW sensor tensors (12-bit PASCAL RAW, 24-bit ROD, or synthetic RAW from ADE20K) at resolutions up to 3840×2160 and produces a standard RGB image that is directly fed to a pretrained downstream model (RetinaNet, YOLOX, or SegFormer). The ISP is jointly optimized end-to-end with the downstream model, so the RGB output is not a perceptually faithful reconstruction but a task-oriented representation shaped by the detection or segmentation loss. The entire ISP module contains only ~3K parameters and processes a full-resolution 3840×2160 frame in under 27 ms.

## Method

TA-ISP factorizes RAW-to-RGB adaptation into three sequential, multi-granularity pixel-modulation modules that operate at global, regional, and pixel scales. First, Global Luminance Calibration computes per-channel mean and variance of the RAW tensor, feeds them through fully-connected layers with a Softplus activation to predict per-channel multiplicative gains (shifted to be >1), and applies them to correct exposure and color imbalance. Second, Hierarchical Spatial Attention builds channel-aggregated spatial descriptors via average and max pooling, passes them through multiple convolutional branches with different kernel sizes to produce multi-scale attention maps, and adaptively fuses them using softmax weights derived from a global descriptor, then applies the fused map via element-wise multiplication. Third, Region-Guided Feature Conditioning predicts K spatial masks via Gumbel-Softmax (τ=0.1) from a convolutional head, assigns each mask a learned scalar weight through a small MLP, and combines regionally enhanced features using a weighted power transformation (X_s^(1/w_k)). This hierarchical decomposition expands the representational space far beyond parameter-tuning ISPs while keeping computation centered on compact modulation operations rather than dense convolutions.

## Results

On PASCAL RAW with ResNet-50, TA-ISP achieves 90.2 AP, surpassing RAW-Adapter (89.7) and DIAP (89.7) while using only 0.003M parameters and 26.43 ms latency at 3840×2160 resolution—roughly 6× faster than RAW-Adapter and 93× faster than MW-ISPNet. On the low-light LOD dataset, TA-ISP reaches 63.9 AP versus 62.1 for RAW-Adapter and 59.5 for DIAP. On the high-dynamic-range ROD dataset, TA-ISP leads in both daytime (38.0 AP) and nighttime (59.7 AP) settings, and on ADE20K segmentation it attains 36.29 mIOU (normal) and 26.77 mIOU (dark), outperforming all baselines by 1–4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TA-ISP outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric reported. Against MW-ISPNet [14], the closest network-based ISP, TA-ISP gains +0.3 to +1.8 AP on PASCAL RAW/LOD while reducing parameters from 9.14M to 0.003M and latency from 2426 ms to 26 ms. Against InvISP [31], TA-ISP improves AP by 4.5 points on PASCAL RAW (R50) and 7.0 points on LOD, with a 60× latency reduction. Against DIAP [32], the most comparable lightweight joint-training method (0.08M params), TA-ISP adds +0.5 to +4.4 AP and is roughly 3× faster (26 ms vs. 80 ms). Against RAW-Adapter [4], TA-ISP leads by +0.5 AP on PASCAL RAW (R50) and +1.8 AP on LOD while using 253× fewer parameters (0.003M vs. 0.76M) and running 6× faster. Against the classical Karaimer et al. [17] pipeline, TA-ISP gains +1.8 AP on PASCAL RAW (R50) and +9.5 AP on LOD, confirming that learned task-aware modulation substantially exceeds hand-tuned ISP parameters under low-light conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TA-ISP positions itself between two established extremes in RAW-to-RGB processing: heavy end-to-end learned ISP networks (MW-ISPNet, InvISP, Dirty Pixels) that achieve strong quality at high computational cost, and lightweight parameter-tuning of traditional ISP pipelines (DIAP, RAW-Adapter, ReconfigISP) that are cheap but fundamentally limited in representational capacity. Rather than building directly on either lineage, the paper introduces a factorized multi-granularity modulation design—global luminance calibration, hierarchical spatial attention, and Gumbel-Softmax region-guided conditioning—that borrows the task-aware joint-training philosophy from DIAP/RAW-Adapter while replacing their single-scale parameter adjustments with a hierarchical, spatially-varying operator family. The conceptual departure is that expressiveness is achieved through a small set of learned modulation operators at multiple spatial scales rather than through dense convolutions or a fixed ISP parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme efficiency: 3K parameters and ~26 ms latency on 4K RAW inputs make task-aware ISP genuinely deployable on resource-constrained edge devices, a regime where prior learned-ISP methods (hundreds of millions of FLOPs) are infeasible. The data-efficiency finding—that 25% of training data with TA-ISP outperforms full-data baselines—suggests the factorized design acts as a strong inductive bias, though this result is reported on a single dataset and backbone. Open questions include whether the Gumbel-Softmax region partitioning generalizes to tasks requiring more than a handful of spatial regions, whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame RAW processing, and whether the gains persist when the downstream model is frozen rather than jointly trained.
