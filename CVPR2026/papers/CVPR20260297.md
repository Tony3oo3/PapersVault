## Problem

Prospective 3D MRI reconstruction from a single ultra-sparse k-space measurement (one spoke, ~170 ms) is highly ill-posed and must run within a strict latency window for clinical applications like MRI-guided radiotherapy. Existing retrospective methods cannot meet this latency, while current prospective approaches rely on linear motion bases or hand-crafted discrete priors that fail to capture complex, nonlinear respiratory deformation, limiting accuracy and generalizability to unseen motion states.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single instantaneous radial spoke (448 readout samples across k_z=96 partitions, acquired in ~170 ms) and a patient-specific static template image pre-constructed from a prior scan. It outputs a full 3D MRI frame at the current timepoint along with the corresponding deformation vector field (DVF) that warps the template to the current motion state. The DVF is the key intermediate: rather than reconstructing the image from scratch, the method estimates only the displacement field and applies it to the known template, reducing the problem dimensionality dramatically.

## Method

PDMR retrospectively learns a compact 12-dimensional latent manifold of deformation vector fields using an auto-decoder formulation that jointly optimizes latent codes and a geometry-aware mapping network against k-space measurement consistency and temporal smoothness regularization. The mapping network uses a tri-plane representation: the latent vector is projected into three orthogonal 2D feature planes (xy, xz, yz), whose features are concatenated at each spatial coordinate and decoded by a lightweight MLP into a 3D displacement vector, enabling high-resolution and structurally coherent DVF generation. During online prospective reconstruction, the mapping network is frozen and only the low-dimensional latent vector is optimized against the single incoming spoke, requiring just a few gradient iterations to recover the current motion state. This manifold constraint reduces the search space from the full DVF (m×3 parameters) to 12 scalars, making fast adaptation to unseen motion states both feasible and stable.

## Results

PDMR achieves the best reconstruction quality across all four evaluation settings (XCAT and in-house, Immediate and After-2min). On the in-house abdominal MRI data, PDMR reaches 46.32 ± 4.06 dB PSNR / 0.994 SSIM (Immediate) and 43.39 ± 4.58 dB / 0.978 SSIM (After 2 min), yielding a ~2 dB PSNR gain over the strongest baseline MR-MOTUS (41.04 dB / 0.981). On the XCAT phantom, PDMR scores 26.28 dB / 0.958 SSIM (Immediate), outperforming MR-MOTUS (24.39 dB / 0.931) by ~1.9 dB. Analytical methods (NUFFT, GRASP) and retrospective methods (TDDIP, SPINER) all fall well below the prospective baselines, confirming the difficulty of ultra-sparse single-spoke reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDMR outperforms all six baselines in every setting. Against the analytical methods, NUFFT (7.80 dB) and GRASP (8.47 dB) on XCAT produce highly blurred images with near-zero SSIM, confirming that traditional reconstruction cannot handle ultra-sparse single-spoke measurements. Among retrospective methods, SPINER (20.25 dB on XCAT; 35.43 dB in-house) fails to generalize to unseen time points, producing nearly static outputs, while TDDIP (17.73 dB on XCAT) lags further behind. Among prospective methods, Prior-INR (15.05 dB on XCAT; 26.72 dB in-house) suffers from its hand-crafted discrete motion manifold, yielding discontinuous z-t trajectories and poor deformation accuracy. MR-MOTUS (24.39 dB on XCAT; 41.04 dB in-house) is the strongest baseline but is limited by its linear subspace assumption, struggling to capture small motions and nonlinear organ deformations. PDMR surpasses MR-MOTUS by ~1.9 dB on XCAT and ~2 dB on in-house data, with the gap widening in the After-2min setting where motion drifts further from the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDMR sits at the intersection of motion-compensated MRI reconstruction, manifold learning, and 3D generative modeling. It repurposes the tri-plane representation from 3D GANs (Chan et al., CVPR 2022) as a geometry-aware mapping from a low-dimensional latent code to full 3D deformation vector fields, a use case not previously explored in medical imaging. Conceptually, it departs from prior prospective methods—MR-MOTUS's linear subspace model and Prior-INR's discrete hand-crafted manifold—by learning a continuous, nonlinear motion manifold offline and then performing fast online adaptation by optimizing only a 12-dimensional latent vector. The auto-decoder training formulation and the MoCo decomposition (template + DVF) follow established retrospective reconstruction practice, but the combination with a tri-plane generator for DVF estimation is the paper's core architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Prospective 3D MRI reconstruction from a single spoke is a critical but underexplored problem for MRI-guided radiotherapy, where sub-second latency is required for real-time motion tracking. PDMR's ~2 dB PSNR gain over the strongest baseline (MR-MOTUS) and its demonstrated robustness to unseen motion offsets (up to 3 mm) suggest that nonlinear manifold-based DVF modeling is a viable path toward clinically deployable prospective reconstruction. However, the evaluation is limited to six subjects, a single respiratory motion type, and a specific golden-angle radial protocol; generalization to cardiac motion, multi-organ deformation, or different acquisition geometries remains open. The 12-dimensional latent code and 50-iteration offline training are encouraging for efficiency, but whether the approach scales to more complex or pathological motion patterns is untested.
