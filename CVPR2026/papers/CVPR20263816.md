## Problem

Existing ultra-low bitrate image compression methods (below 0.05 bpp) rely on heavy pretrained encoders such as VAEs or tokenizers combined with secondary latent-space encoders, creating multi-encoder pipelines that are impractical for bandwidth- and computation-constrained edge devices. This paper asks whether shallow encoders can suffice for extreme compression, motivated by the observation that ultra-low bitrates inherently reduce the representational complexity of the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263816/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 768×512, 1920×1088, and 2K resolution) and produces a compressed bitstream at ultra-low bitrates (0.005–0.035 bpp) along with a reconstructed image. The encoder operates directly in pixel space with a 32× spatial compression ratio, producing a compact latent that is quantized and entropy-coded via a quadtree-partition context model. The decoder reconstructs the image through a one-step diffusion denoiser followed by a lightweight VAE decoder, with no textual prompt or timestep conditioning required at inference.

## Method

AEIC employs an asymmetric architecture pairing a shallow StarNet-based analysis transform (0.94M params for AEIC-SE, 3.09M for AEIC-ME) with a generative one-step diffusion decoder built from a pruned, unconditional SD-Turbo denoiser and a 50%-channel-pruned VAE decoder. A dual-branch decoding structure splits the synthesis transform output into a texture latent (decoded by the one-step denoiser) and a structural residual (added element-wise before the VAE decoder). A key theoretical contribution shows that as bitrate decreases, latent variance diminishes sharply, justifying shallow encoders since the information to encode is inherently compact. To compensate for the shallow encoder's limited capacity, a dual-side feature distillation scheme transfers knowledge from the moderate-encoder teacher (AEIC-ME) to the student (AEIC-SE), aligning intermediate encoder latents and decoder UNet features via learnable projection layers. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule: relaxed-bitrate end-to-end optimization, aggressive ultra-low-bitrate finetuning with GAN loss (DINOv3-based discriminator), and a short high-resolution finetuning stage specific to AEIC-SE.

## Results

AEIC-SE achieves the fastest encoding speed among all compared ultra-low bitrate codecs, reaching 35.8 FPS on 1080P images (27.9 ms on RTX 4090D), a 19.3× speedup over StableCodec (538.4 ms) and 18.2× over DLF (508.1 ms). In terms of model complexity, AEIC-SE requires only 16.10M parameters and 46.02K MACs/pixel for encoding, compared to 102.23M and 2537.51K for StableCodec and 437.35M and 1915.35K for DLF. On perceptual quality, AEIC-SE attains the best LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and KID scores across the 0.005–0.035 bpp range on CLIC 2020 Test and DIV2K Val, while maintaining competitive PSNR and MS-SSIM. The moderate-encoder variant AEIC-ME (55.91M params, 204.26K MACs) also outperforms StableCodec across the full ultra-low bitrate range in both rate-perception and rate-distortion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against StableCodec [70] (the closest prior method using one-step diffusion), AEIC-SE reduces encoding parameters by 84% (16.10M vs. 102.23M) and encoding MACs by 98% (46.02K vs. 2537.51K), while achieving superior perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, FID, KID) and ~20% bitrate savings at matched quality. Compared to DLF [62], AEIC-SE is 27× lighter in encoding parameters and 42× lower in encoding MACs, with 18.2× faster encoding latency. Against multi-step diffusion methods PerCo [10] (20 steps) and DiffEIC [38] (50 steps), AEIC-SE avoids the prohibitive decoding cost (both exceed 1B decoder parameters and >10⁴ MACs) while delivering better perceptual quality. Versus the normal-bitrate real-time codec EVC-Small [18], AEIC-SE has comparable encoding complexity (16.10M vs. 11.64M params) but operates at ultra-low bitrates (<0.05 bpp) where EVC-Small is not designed. On distortion metrics (PSNR, MS-SSIM), AEIC-SE is slightly behind AEIC-ME but remains competitive with StableCodec, while clearly leading on all four perceptual metrics. The shallow encoder variant also matches AEIC-ME's performance at 2K resolution after a short 5K-iteration high-resolution finetuning stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in ultra-low bitrate image compression, where methods like StableCodec, DLF, and GLC rely on complex pretrained encoders (VAEs or tokenizers) followed by a secondary latent-space encoder for entropy modeling. AEIC instead places a shallow StarNet-based encoder directly in pixel space and shifts all generative complexity to the decoder side (one-step diffusion + lite VAE decoder), building on StableCodec's dual-branch one-step diffusion decoding and SD-Turbo's adversarial distillation. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical argument that ultra-low bitrates inherently constrain latent variance, making deep encoders unnecessary, combined with a dual-side feature distillation strategy to compensate for the shallow encoder's limited capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AEIC-SE is the first ultra-low bitrate codec to achieve real-time 1080P encoding (35.8 FPS) while maintaining SOTA perceptual quality, making extreme compression practical for bandwidth- and compute-constrained edge devices and IoT terminals. The theoretical bitrate-latent variance relationship provides a principled justification for encoder simplification that could inform future codec design beyond this specific architecture. However, the decoder remains heavy (~914M parameters, ~2762K MACs/pixel), so the asymmetry only benefits scenarios where the sender is the bottleneck; extending efficiency to the decoder side or to video compression remains open.
