## Problem

Unified multimodal models that combine autoregressive VLMs with diffusion generators face a fundamental tension: using only the VLM's final layer as conditioning provides insufficient fine-grained detail, while tightly integrating both into a single transformer increases training complexity and reduces flexibility. Existing multi-layer approaches also fail to exploit the distinct hierarchical properties of individual VLM layers, missing an opportunity to guide reinforcement learning more effectively.

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces 1024×1024 RGB images via a SANA-1.5-1.6B diffusion backbone. The VLM (InternVL3-2B) processes the prompt through 28 transformer layers using 256 learnable queries; the key distinction is that queries are extracted from every layer simultaneously rather than only the final layer. For understanding, the model accepts image + text and produces text responses using the frozen VLM's autoregressive head, preserving the base model's comprehension ability unchanged.

## Method

ParaUni introduces a Layer Integration Module (LIM) that processes learnable queries from all 28 VLM layers in parallel: each layer's query passes through a shared Transformer encoder followed by LayerNorm, and the results are averaged to form a single fused conditioning vector fed into the diffusion model's cross-attention. The core empirical finding is that VLM layers encode a hierarchy from low-level texture (shallow) to high-level semantics (deep), and that different layer regions respond preferentially to different reward signals—middle layers (12–23) drive aesthetic and preference scores while deep layers (24–28) drive CLIP alignment. Building on this, the Layer-wise Dynamic Adjustment Mechanism (LDAM) injects calibrated Gaussian noise perturbations into reward-specific layers during Flow-GRPO training when a reward shows sustained decline or GradNorm spikes, with a cooling-off period to stabilize optimization. Training proceeds in three stages: aligning the frozen VLM with the diffusion model, fine-tuning on high-quality data (BLIP3-o-60k), and sequential RL on aesthetic, preference, and CLIP rewards while preserving layer-specific perturbations across reward switches.

## Results

On GenEval, ParaUni achieves an overall score of 0.87, surpassing the strongest unified baseline OpenUni (0.86) and all generation-only models (best: SD3-Medium at 0.74). On DPG-Bench, ParaUni scores 83.45 overall, outperforming OpenUni (83.08) and Janus-Pro-1B (82.63). The method also demonstrates plug-and-play utility: when applied to Janus-Pro-1B, GenEval improves from 0.73 to 0.80, and for BLIP3-o-4B, from 0.81 to 0.84.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaUni (0.87 GenEval, 83.45 DPG-Bench) outperforms the closest unified baseline OpenUni (0.86, 83.08) by a narrow margin on both benchmarks, and substantially exceeds other unified models such as BAGEL (0.82), BLIP3-o-4B (0.81), and MetaQuery (0.74) on GenEval. Against generation-only models, ParaUni surpasses the strongest, SD3-Medium (0.74 GenEval), by 0.13, confirming that unified understanding-and-generation architectures raise the performance ceiling. On DPG-Bench, ParaUni leads all unified baselines, beating Janus-Pro-1B (82.63) by 0.82 and MetaQuery (80.04) by 3.41. The method also demonstrates plug-and-play transferability: attaching ParaUni to Janus-Pro-1B lifts GenEval from 0.73 to 0.80 (+0.07) and DPG-Bench from 82.63 to 83.65 (+1.02), while for BLIP3-o-4B it improves GenEval from 0.81 to 0.84 (+0.03) and DPG-Bench from 79.36 to 81.97 (+2.61). Notably, ParaUni's advantage over OpenUni is most visible in the Position sub-metric (0.83 vs. 0.82) and Two Obj. (0.94 vs. 0.92), suggesting that multi-layer conditioning particularly benefits spatial and compositional reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParaUni builds directly on the MetaQuery architecture (learnable queries bridging a frozen VLM to a diffusion model) and the Flow-GRPO RL framework, adopting the OpenUni training recipe (InternVL3-2B + SANA-1.5). Its key departure from prior unified models is the parallel multi-layer conditioning strategy: rather than using only the final VLM layer (as in Show-o, OpenUni, or DALL-E-style pipelines) or tightly integrating VLM and diffusion into a single transformer (as in Transfusion, Show-o), ParaUni extracts learnable queries from every VLM layer and fuses them via a lightweight Layer Integration Module, preserving architectural modularity. The Layer-wise Dynamic Adjustment Mechanism further distinguishes the work by exploiting an empirical finding—that middle VLM layers govern aesthetic/preference rewards while deep layers govern semantic alignment—to apply targeted Gaussian perturbations during RL, a strategy absent from prior multi-reward RL for diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-reward alignment finding (middle layers → aesthetics/Pickscore, deep layers → CLIP) offers a practical, interpretable handle for multi-objective RL in unified models, and the plug-and-play nature demonstrated on Janus-Pro and BLIP3-o suggests broad applicability. However, the performance gains over the strongest unified baseline (OpenUni: 0.87 vs. 0.86 GenEval) are modest, the layer-to-reward mapping is determined empirically on a single 2B-scale model and may not transfer to larger or architecturally different VLMs, and the LDAM's cooling-off schedule and perturbation scale (γ) are tuned heuristically. Open questions include whether the hierarchical layer-reward correspondence holds at scale, whether the mapping can be learned end-to-end, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where temporal/spatial hierarchies add further complexity.
