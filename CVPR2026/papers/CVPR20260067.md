## Problem

Existing spatial representations—linguistic prepositions and object-level scene graphs—treat objects as indivisible units, leaving them unable to specify which regions actually support, contain, or contact one another. This coarseness makes the translation from relational descriptions to concrete 3D configurations fundamentally ambiguous, producing physically inconsistent layouts. The paper argues that part-level relations are needed to bridge high-level language and low-level geometric constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260067/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a Part-centric Assembly Graph (PAG), a directed acyclic graph whose nodes are two-level (object nodes with semantic category queries and child part nodes with labeled canonical surfaces) and whose edges encode both coarse object-level spatial relations (left-of, behind) and fine part-level geometric relations (on, in, against) tied to specific labeled surfaces. It produces a collision-free 3D scene with per-object poses (rotation, translation, scale), a part-level contact graph identifying all part pairs within 1 mm, and a final physically stable configuration after Sapien simulation. For the VLM evaluation track, the input is a rendered RGB image with marked objects, and the output is a scene graph of 2D bounding boxes plus relation triplets.

## Method

The core representation is the PAG, a DAG organized hierarchically so that each object has a unique physical supporter, decomposing the global constraint-satisfaction problem into a sequence of localized subproblems solved in topological order. The Part-Aware Spatial Configuration Solver instantiates each object via a coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) coarse localization prunes occupied regions from the 2D support surface and contracts the feasible area using object-level spatial edges; (2) part-level alignment resolves geometric constraints (e.g., coplanarity, contact) between specific labeled surfaces of connected part nodes, either explicitly specified or inferred by geometric reasoning (e.g., finding the lowest bottom surface for an 'on' relation); (3) final pose sampling draws from the minimally contracted subspace and validates for 3D collision and semantic plausibility via multi-directional raycasting. A brief Sapien dynamic simulation refines the assembled scene for physical stability. For downstream tasks, the authors fine-tune Qwen3-VL on rendered PARSE-10K scenes with part-level relation annotations, and build a graph-transformer diffusion network (inspired by InstructScene) that encodes object geometry via Michelangelo, encodes the PAG via CLIP into a relation embedding matrix, and injects relational constraints through FiLM-style modulation at each denoising layer.

## Results

On the VLM spatial reasoning benchmark, the PARSE-10K fine-tuned Qwen3-VL achieves 97.4% on Visual Relation MCQ and 86.2% on Part-level Contact MCQ, substantially outperforming all generalist VLM baselines. In Scene Graph Generation, the fine-tuned model reaches an F1 of 76.6 (with bbox matching) and 78.2 (grounding-agnostic), compared to the best baseline Gemini-2.5-Pro at 44.2 and 47.3 respectively. In the scene generation user study, the PAG-conditioned model trained on PARSE-10K receives 47.5% preference votes for complexity, 38.8% for realism, and 45.0% for contact fidelity, versus 7.5%/33.8%/28.8% for InstructScene trained on 3D-FRONT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned model dominates all five VLM baselines on every metric. On Visual Relation MCQ, it leads Qwen3-VL (86.2%) by 11.2 points and Gemini-2.5-Pro (85.0%) by 12.4 points; on Part-level Contact MCQ it beats Gemini-2.5-Pro (75.6%) by 10.6 points and Qwen3-VL (60.4%) by 25.8 points. The largest gap appears in Scene Graph Generation: the fine-tuned model's F1 of 76.6 (with bbox) is 32.4 points above Gemini-2.5-Pro (44.2) and 43.4 points above GPT-5 (13.8), indicating that generalist VLMs struggle significantly with dense, part-level relation enumeration. For scene generation, the PAG-conditioned model trained on PARSE-10K outperforms InstructScene (trained on 3D-FRONT) by 40 points on complexity and 16.2 points on contact fidelity in the user study, while the unconditioned PARSE-10K model already surpasses InstructScene on complexity (45.0% vs 7.5%) but trails on realism, confirming that PAG conditioning is critical for physical plausibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends object-level scene graphs to a part-level formulation, arguing that linguistic prepositions and whole-object relations are too coarse to specify which surfaces actually support, contain, or contact one another. The Part-centric Assembly Graph (PAG) draws conceptually from scene graph representations and procedural generation systems (ProcTHOR, Infinigen) but departs from both by encoding explicit part-part geometric constraints within a DAG structure, enabling a deterministic constraint-solver rather than learned or rule-based placement. For the generative component, the paper repurposes the InstructScene graph-transformer diffusion architecture and injects PAG structure via FiLM modulation, while the spatial-reasoning experiments fine-tune Qwen3-VL on self-constructed part-level MCQ and scene-graph tasks. The dataset construction consolidates part-annotated assets from ABO, Objaverse, PartNeXt, and CLAY into a unified retrieval library, filling a gap no prior indoor scene dataset (HSSD-200, 3D-FRONT, FurniScene, METASCENES) addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PARSE-10K is the first large-scale indoor scene dataset with consistent part-segmentation and explicit part-level contact annotations, providing supervision that is simply absent from existing resources and directly enabling part-level spatial reasoning benchmarks. The PAG representation offers a clean, interpretable intermediate between natural-language spatial descriptions and raw 3D geometry, potentially useful for embodied planning, manipulation, and controllable generation beyond the paper's scope. Key limitations temper the impact: PAG construction remains partially manual and sensitive to canonical poses, the constraint solver is deterministic and procedural (not learned), and the spatial-reasoning evaluation is largely on self-constructed tasks rather than established external benchmarks. Open questions include whether part-part relations can be learned directly from geometry, whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, and how to integrate PAGs into closed-loop embodied manipulation.
