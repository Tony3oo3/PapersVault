## Problem

VGGT's global attention layers process all-to-all attention over tokens from all views, creating sequences exceeding 20k tokens even for small scenes and becoming the dominant latency bottleneck for medium-to-large scene reconstruction. Existing token merging methods either apply uniform merging across all attention heads (causing feature collapse after concatenation) or use global matching strategies whose cost grows quadratically with sequence length, making them impractical for the long inputs VGGT requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

HTTM operates as a training-free acceleration layer inside VGGT's global attention layers, consuming the Q/K/V token sequences (up to 1000 frames × ~1374 tokens/frame) and producing the same camera poses, depth maps, and dense 3D geometry as the unmodified model. The method reduces query sequences to 20% and key/value sequences to 30% of their original length before the attention kernel, then restores full-length outputs via head-wise unmerging so downstream heads see the original token count. All inference runs in BFloat16 with FlashAttention on an A100 GPU.

## Method

HTTM introduces four coupled mechanisms: (1) head-wise merging, where each attention head independently partitions its Q/K tokens into source and destination sets and merges the top-r most similar pairs, preserving head-specific representational diversity after concatenation; (2) temporal reordering, which stacks spatially corresponding blocks of size n_s across n_t consecutive frames to form temporal merging blocks of size n_b = n_s × n_t, aligning the spatio-temporal similarity structure induced by RoPE within each block; (3) block-wise matching, which restricts the O(N²) similarity computation to within-block O(n_b²) lookups, reducing total merging cost from quadratic to linear in N; and (4) adaptive outlier filtering, which identifies the top d% tokens with largest L₂ deviation from their merged counterparts across all heads under a global budget and unmerges them, allocating more protection to heads with higher outlier density. The combination achieves up to 7× end-to-end speedup over VGGT at 1000 frames with negligible Chamfer distance degradation.

## Results

On NRGBD (stride 10), VGGT+HTTM achieves a Chamfer distance accuracy of 0.012 and completeness of 0.010, matching the full VGGT baseline (0.010/0.010) while reducing query tokens to 20% and key/value tokens to 30% of their original length. On 7Scenes, HTTM records 0.020 accuracy and 0.023 completeness, comparable to FastVGGT (0.018/0.020) at a higher merging ratio. In terms of latency, HTTM achieves up to 7× speedup over the VGGT baseline at 1000 input frames (102.8 s vs. 724.6 s) and reduces the token-matching cost by 4.58× compared to FastVGGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full VGGT baseline, HTTM maintains near-identical reconstruction quality on NRGBD (0.012 vs. 0.010 accuracy) and 7Scenes (0.020 vs. 0.019 accuracy) while cutting inference time roughly in half at 100 frames and by 7× at 1000 frames. Compared to FastVGGT, HTTM wins on NRGBD (0.012 vs. 0.016 accuracy, 0.010 vs. 0.013 completeness) and ETH3D (0.091 vs. 0.120 accuracy, 0.054 vs. 0.076 completeness) despite using a more aggressive merging ratio (20% Q tokens vs. 34%), and is faster on every dataset (e.g., 6.8 s vs. 7.0 s on NRGBD, 102.8 s vs. 175.2 s at 1000 frames). On 7Scenes the two methods are essentially tied in quality (0.020 vs. 0.018 accuracy), with HTTM slightly slower in completeness (0.023 vs. 0.020) but still faster in wall-clock time (4.3 s vs. 4.5 s). The CUT3R online baseline is substantially worse in reconstruction quality on both 7Scenes and NRGBD, confirming that offline global-attention methods remain superior in accuracy even after aggressive token compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

HTTM extends the token merging paradigm established by ToMe and ToMeSD (bipartite soft matching with src/dst partitioning and unmerging) to the specific context of VGGT's global attention layers. It departs from FastVGGT, which applied ToMeSD uniformly across all attention heads, by introducing head-wise independent merging that preserves head-specific representational diversity after concatenation. The temporal reordering strategy draws on spatio-temporal token merging ideas from video transformer acceleration, but is specifically motivated by VGGT's RoPE-induced periodic similarity patterns that create strong temporal correspondence between spatially aligned tokens across consecutive frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7× speedup on 1000-frame sequences with negligible reconstruction quality loss makes VGGT practically viable for large-scale 3D scene reconstruction that was previously limited by quadratic attention costs. The training-free nature means it can be dropped into existing VGGT deployments without retraining. However, the method is tightly coupled to VGGT's specific architecture (per-layer RoPE, alternating frame/global attention), and it remains open whether the head-wise merging principle generalizes to other multi-head transformer architectures or whether the aggressive merging ratios (20% Q, 30% K/V) hold up on more challenging or diverse scenes beyond the evaluated datasets.
