## Problem

Existing event-based gait recognition methods aggregate asynchronous event streams into grid-based event images over long time windows, collapsing the microsecond temporal resolution that makes event cameras valuable and producing sparse spatial representations that standard CNNs struggle to interpret. This temporal voxelization discards the fine-grained high-frequency motion dynamics critical for gait discrimination, causing prior event-based approaches to underperform both camera-based and LiDAR-based gait recognition. Additionally, the field lacks large-scale event-based gait benchmarks, with prior work validated only on datasets of roughly 100 identities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes asynchronous DVS event streams, which are voxelized into two-scale temporal slices: short-term slices (small ΔT) with 2×K×H×W tensors encoding positive and negative polarities separately for the dynamic stream, and long-term slices (full exposure window T) for the static stream. During training, synchronized RGB frames are additionally required as teacher input for CroSA, converted to grayscale to avoid color interference. All spatial inputs are processed via Pad-and-Resize to 64×64 to preserve body proportions, and the output is a single fused gait descriptor embedding used for identity classification via cross-entropy and triplet losses.

## Method

EventGait is a dual-stream framework that separately models motion and shape from event data. The Dynamic Motion Stream employs a Mixture of Spiking Experts (MoSE), where N parallel Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) spiking experts—each initialized with a distinct membrane time constant τ—process short-term event slices; a lightweight spiking gating network computes adaptive mixture coefficients to combine expert outputs, enabling robust perception across varying illumination and motion speeds (fast neurons capture high-frequency bursts in bright scenes, slow neurons integrate sparse spikes in low-light). The Static Shape Stream uses a CNN encoder trained via Cross-modal Structure Alignment (CroSA), which distills dense structural priors from a frozen DINOv2 teacher by minimizing L2 distance between event-based and grayscale-RGB-based features, compensating for the spatial sparsity of long-term event integration. The two streams are fused via concatenation and a fusion module, and the overall training objective combines cross-entropy, triplet, and alignment losses. The paper also introduces a v2e-based synthetic event pipeline that converts RGB gait videos into realistic event streams, yielding two new large-scale benchmarks (SUSTech1K-E with 1,050 identities and CCGR-Mini-E with 970 subjects).

## Results

On SUSTech1K-E, EventGait achieves 92.8% overall Rank-1 accuracy, surpassing the best LiDAR-based method LidarGait++ (92.7%) and the best silhouette-based method Origin-S (86.9%) by +5.9% and +0.1% respectively. Under low-light conditions, EventGait maintains 83.2% overall accuracy (only a 9.6% drop from normal lighting), while silhouette-based GaitBase drops from 76.1% to 41.5% (a 34.6% drop). On CCGR-Mini-E, EventGait reaches 40.3% Rank-1 / 38.7% mAP / 25.5% mINP, outperforming the best silhouette baseline DeepGaitV2 (39.4% / 36.0% / 16.8%). On the real-world DVS128-Gait dataset, EventGait achieves 87.4% Rank-1, exceeding EV-Gait (81.8%) and GaitBasee (74.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventGait (92.8% overall) outperforms all 10 silhouette/multi-modal camera-based baselines on SUSTech1K-E, with the largest margins over GaitSet (+27.8%), GaitBase (+16.7%), and DeepGaitV2 (+10.5%); it also beats the strongest multi-modal baselines MultiGait++ (87.4%) and DepthGait (87.6%) by +5.4% and +5.2% respectively. Against the LiDAR-based SOTA LidarGait++ (92.7%), EventGait achieves a marginal +0.1% overall gain while using a sensor roughly 50× cheaper (USD 1.5K vs. USD 75K). Compared to prior event-based methods, EventGait improves over EVGait (65.4%) by +27.4% and over GaitBasee (63.1%) by +29.7%, closing the gap that previously made event-based gait recognition inferior to camera-based approaches. On CCGR-Mini-E, EventGait (40.3% Rank-1) edges out DeepGaitV2 (39.4%) and GaitBase (27.0%), and on the real-world DVS128-Gait dataset it leads EV-Gait by +5.6% (87.4% vs. 81.8%). In cross-illumination evaluation, EventGait's 9.6% accuracy drop under low light is dramatically smaller than GaitBase's 34.6% drop, confirming the illumination robustness advantage of the event modality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based gait recognition line initiated by EV-Gait [65] and [66], which aggregated events into long-window voxelized images and processed them with standard CNNs or GNNs. EventGait departs from this paradigm by preserving fine-grained temporal structure through a two-scale event representation and introducing spiking neural network dynamics (LIF neurons) as the native processing unit for high-frequency motion, a repurposing of SNN concepts from the event-vision literature [17] into biometric recognition. The static stream's Cross-modal Structure Alignment borrows the knowledge-distillation-from-VFM trend established in recent gait work (BigGait [75], BiggerGait [76]) but applies it cross-modally from RGB to sparse event inputs. The event synthesis pipeline builds on the v2e toolbox [21] and prior synthetic-event augmentation strategies [2, 29, 30, 38], adapted here to gait-specific covariates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of two large-scale event-based gait benchmarks (SUSTech1K-E with 1,050 identities, CCGR-Mini-E with 970 subjects) fills a critical gap that previously limited event-based gait research to ~100-identity datasets, enabling meaningful comparison with camera- and LiDAR-based methods. The demonstrated cost advantage (≈$1.5K per event camera vs. ≈$75K per LiDAR sensor) combined with competitive or superior accuracy—especially the +37.3% night-time gain over silhouettes—makes event-based gait recognition a practically viable alternative for large-scale surveillance deployment. Key open questions remain: the primary benchmarks rely on synthetic events generated from RGB videos, so the sim-to-real gap is unquantified; the authors explicitly call for large-scale real-event gait datasets and multimodal (RGB/LiDAR/event) fusion as future directions.
