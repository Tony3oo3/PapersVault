## Problem

Existing driving world models can generate future scenes but lack the ability to interpret, describe, or query the 3D driving environment, and their reliance on BEV or point-cloud representations only achieves feature-level (not spatially explicit) alignment between text and 3D geometry. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a unified framework that simultaneously performs 3D scene understanding and multi-modal scene generation using a 3D Gaussian scene representation with direct language-to-geometry alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260465/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes six surrounding RGB camera images, a pre-built 3D Gaussian ellipsoid scene (hundreds of thousands of primitives per scene), and a free-form text query as input. For understanding, it outputs textual answers, 2D/3D bounding boxes, and a high-level language feature vector; for generation, it produces RGB images and single-channel depth maps at spatial shifts of 1–4 m or temporal horizons of 1–2 s, at resolutions up to 424×800. The dense Gaussian set is reduced to 4096 tokens via task-aware sampling before being fed to the LLM, and depth maps are encoded as pseudo-RGB (channel-replicated) to satisfy the shared VAE interface.

## Method

Each 3D Gaussian primitive is augmented with a CLIP language embedding (inherited from SAM segmentation), compressed by a scene-wise autoencoder (512→3 dims), and projected into a shared 4096-dim space via Fourier-embedded coordinates, sigmoid-constrained opacity, and per-attribute MLPs fused with learnable softmax weights. A task-aware hybrid sampling strategy then selects the most relevant Gaussians: uniform + top-k sampling for holistic understanding tasks, and text-to-Gaussian similarity scoring for 2D/3D visual grounding, injecting compact spatial tokens into a Qwen3-8B LLM. The LLM produces both a textual answer and a high-level language feature that, together with low-level projected point-cloud conditions (for RGB and depth), jointly condition a denoising UNet with a frozen VAE under a v-prediction objective. Training proceeds in three stages—Gaussian tokenizer/projector pre-training, LLM alignment (frozen VLM + full-parameter aligner for 5k steps, then LoRA for 30k steps), and multi-modal generation (low-res RGB → low-res RGB-D → high-res RGB-D with rectified flow)—followed by end-to-end joint optimization to ensure consistency between understanding and generation.

## Results

On the NuInteract benchmark, GaussianDWM achieves a state-of-the-art average score of 59.23 across four scene understanding subtasks (2D RD&Pre, 2D VG, 3D VG, Planning), outperforming the previous best DriveMonkey (52.12) by 7.11 points and InternVL2-8B (45.42) by 13.81 points. In 2D visual grounding, it reaches 34.95 mAP (vs. 19.47 for DriveMonkey), and in 3D visual grounding it achieves 52.78 mAP, surpassing specialized 3D detectors like CAPE (32.94) and PETR (31.34). For spatial novel view synthesis on nuScenes, GaussianDWM attains the best FID of 8.36 at ±1 m shift and 11.27 at ±2 m shift, outperforming DiST-4D (10.12 and 12.97 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianDWM decisively outperforms all general-purpose VLM baselines (LLaVA1.5, InternVL series, Qwen2VL) on the NuInteract benchmark, with the largest gains in 2D visual grounding (34.95 mAP vs. 20.61 for InternVL2-8B) and 3D visual grounding (52.78 mAP vs. 24.67). Against the closest driving-specific competitor DriveMonkey, GaussianDWM improves the overall average by 7.11 points (59.23 vs. 52.12), driven primarily by a 15.48-point mAP gain in 2D VG and an 18.25-point mAP gain in 3D VG, though DriveMonkey retains a slight edge in Planning accuracy (82.64 vs. 80.95). In 3D VG, GaussianDWM also surpasses dedicated 3D detectors CAPE (32.94 mAP) and PETR (31.34 mAP) by roughly 20 points, demonstrating that the 3D Gaussian representation enables competitive grounding without task-specific detection heads. For spatial novel view synthesis on nuScenes, GaussianDWM achieves the lowest FID at all tested shifts (8.36 at ±1 m, 11.27 at ±2 m), beating DiST-4D by 1.76 and 1.70 FID points respectively; however, DiST-4D retains a marginal FVD advantage at the extreme ±4 m shift (105.29 vs. 116.40), suggesting the dual-condition generation is most effective at moderate viewpoint changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified driving world model line initiated by Hermes and UniFuture, which couple scene understanding with generation via BEV or depth features. The key departure is replacing BEV/point-cloud representations with 3D Gaussian Splatting (building on LangSplat for language-augmented Gaussians), enabling explicit per-primitive spatial-textual alignment rather than the feature-level alignment that BEV provides. On the generation side, it departs from prior NVS methods (PVG, StreetGaussians, DiST-4D) by introducing a dual-condition diffusion model that fuses high-level LLM-extracted world knowledge with low-level geometric cues, supporting both spatial and temporal multi-modal (RGB + depth) synthesis within a single architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single 3D Gaussian representation can simultaneously serve as the input to an LLM for scene understanding and as the geometric backbone for generative scene synthesis, this work offers a concrete architectural template for unified driving world models that prior BEV-based approaches could not achieve with comparable spatial fidelity. The task-aware language-guided sampling strategy addresses a practical bottleneck (hundreds of thousands of Gaussian tokens vs. LLM context limits) that will be relevant to any future 3D-representation-based VLM. Open questions include whether the 3D Gaussian advantage persists at larger scene scales or in real-time inference settings, whether the dual-condition generation generalizes beyond driving to other embodied domains, and how the framework handles dynamic scene changes over longer temporal horizons.
