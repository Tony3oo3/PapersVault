## Problem

Existing object-centric datasets for inverse rendering and relighting are constrained in at least one of three key aspects: realism (synthetic datasets like Objaverse and ABO exhibit a domain gap), scale (real-world OLAT datasets like OpenIllumination contain only 64 objects and OpenSubstance only 187), or precise lighting control (large-scale real datasets like DTC and OmniObject3D lack controlled illumination). No prior dataset simultaneously provides large-scale coverage of diverse real objects and high-fidelity appearance under precisely controlled, individually addressable light sources.

## Input / Output

Each of the 765 objects is captured by 35 RED Komodo 6K cameras under 355 distinct lighting conditions (1 uniform, 12 polarized gradient, 10 environment, 331 OLAT), yielding ~12K images per object and ~9M total. The dataset provides calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics (0.86 px mean reprojection error), per-view object masks, polarized and non-polarized surface normals, and diffuse albedo. For the validation benchmark, 42 objects are downsampled to 750×1.4k with every 5th camera and every 3rd lighting held out as inference views, and meshes are reconstructed via MetaShape for all baseline methods.

## Method

OLATverse is acquired on a spherical lightstage with 331 individually controllable RGBAW LEDs and 35 synchronized cameras at 30 FPS. Camera calibration is performed every 20–30 capture sessions using fixed Lambertian reference objects and feature-based algorithms in MetaShape, reusing parameters across sessions to maintain consistency. A semi-automatic mask pipeline fuses bgMatting (coarse object-stand separation), SAM (clean contour via bounding-box prompts), and RMBG-2.0 (detailed edges) through a compositing formula, achieving 95% success across all views. Surface normals and diffuse albedo are recovered via polarized photometric stereo under spherical gradient illumination, following Ma et al., with linear polarization filters on five cameras to suppress view-dependent specular reflection. Relighting exploits OLAT linearity: a target environment map is decomposed into per-LED masks, and the relit image is a weighted sum of OLAT captures. The paper further establishes a 42-object benchmark evaluating inverse rendering (Mitsuba, GS³, RNG, BiGS) and diffusion-based normal estimation (StableNormal, GeoWizard, RGBX, DiffusionRender) under four environmental illuminations per object.

## Results

On the inverse rendering benchmark (42 objects, 14 material categories), GS3 achieves the best PSNR of 38.54 and SSIM of 0.982, outperforming Mitsuba+Mshape (35.91 PSNR, 0.976 SSIM), BiGS (32.98 PSNR, 0.940 SSIM), and RNG (32.07 PSNR, 0.962 SSIM). For normal estimation, StableNormal (SN) achieves the lowest mean angular error of 31.85° and median of 30.25°, while GeoWizard (GW) achieves the highest percentage of pixels below 11.25° (10.98%). RGBX performs worst on normal estimation with a mean angular error of 51.95°, and none of the methods recover accurate normals for objects with complex geometric structures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For inverse rendering, GS3 clearly leads with 38.54 PSNR and 0.982 SSIM, beating Mitsuba+Mshape by ~2.6 dB in PSNR and RNG/BiGS by ~5–6 dB, while tying Mitsuba on LPIPS (0.026). RNG and BiGS, both 3DGS-based methods, trail significantly, suggesting that current Gaussian-splatting inverse rendering approaches still struggle with the diverse material categories in OLATverse. For normal estimation, SN and GW (purpose-built for normals) outperform RGBX and DR (designed for relighting), with SN achieving the lowest mean angular error (31.85° vs. 34.42° for GW, 34.88° for DR, and 51.95° for RGBX). However, GW edges out SN on the 11.25° threshold (10.98% vs. 8.93%), indicating GW captures sharper high-frequency details. The paper notes that RGBX and DR produce visually finer high-frequency details despite worse angular metrics, and that no method reliably handles complex geometric structures—highlighting OLATverse's value as a challenging real-world benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

OLATverse extends the lightstage-based OLAT capture paradigm established by Debevec et al. and refined in datasets like OpenIllumination and OpenSubstance, but scales it from tens to hundreds of objects (765) while adding polarized gradient illumination for normal/albedo recovery (following Ma et al.) and a semi-automatic segmentation pipeline combining SAM, bgMatting, and RMBG-2.0. It departs from prior OLAT datasets primarily in scale and multi-modal annotation richness, and from large-scale synthetic datasets (Objaverse, ABO) by providing photorealistic captures under precisely controlled lighting. The benchmark evaluation draws on the current 3DGS-based inverse rendering lineage (GS3, BiGS, RNG) and diffusion-based normal estimation methods (StableNormal, GeoWizard, RGBX, DiffusionRender), positioning OLATverse as the missing real-world evaluation ground for these methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OLATverse addresses a critical bottleneck: the domain gap between synthetic training data and real-world evaluation that limits the reliability of inverse rendering and relighting research. By providing 9M images of 765 objects with calibrated cameras, masks, normals, and albedo, it enables the first comprehensive real-world benchmark across multiple tasks simultaneously. The benchmark results reveal that even state-of-the-art methods (GS3 at 38.5 dB PSNR; StableNormal at 31.9° mean angular error) still struggle substantially on real objects with complex geometry and materials, highlighting that the field has not yet solved realistic inverse rendering. Open questions include whether the dataset can train generative priors that close the remaining gap, and whether the normal extraction artifacts on glossy/low-reflectance surfaces limit its utility for those material classes.
