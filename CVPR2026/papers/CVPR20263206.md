## Problem

VLM-based active learning in medical imaging suffers from overconfidence: temperature-scaled softmax treats text-image cosine similarities as deterministic probabilities, causing the model to confidently select samples it already believes it understands rather than truly informative ones. Existing calibration methods provide only global adjustments and reduce uncertainty to a single scalar, failing to distinguish whether uncertainty stems from missing knowledge (unseen phenotypes) or from conflicting hypotheses (ambiguous boundaries), which limits clinically interpretable sample selection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes medical images across diverse modalities (X-ray, MRI, OCT, ultrasound, histopathology) and PubMed-retrieved class descriptions as text prompts, with the image encoder (BiomedCLIP ViT-B/16) kept frozen. Beyond a standard class label, the SEH outputs Dirichlet concentration parameters α_k that are decomposed into a vacuity score (K/S, measuring total evidence deficit) and a dissonance score (measuring pairwise belief conflicts), which are combined via a round-dependent linear schedule into a single acquisition score for ranking unlabeled samples.

## Method

SaE introduces a Similarity Evidence Head (SEH), a dual-branch MLP that takes the frozen VLM image embedding and the raw similarity vector as inputs and predicts a scalar evidence strength λ via a softplus-activated fusion layer. The Dirichlet concentration parameters are then computed as α_k = λ·p_k + 1, where p_k is the temperature-scaled softmax of similarities, effectively scaling the total evidence budget by a sample-specific confidence signal. The SEH is trained with a dual-objective loss that aligns 1/λ with cross-entropy difficulty and with the inverse of VLM softmax entropy, jointly capturing empirical hardness and latent VLM certainty. For acquisition, a linear schedule shifts the weighted combination from vacuity-dominant in early rounds (prioritizing rare/unseen phenotypes for coverage) to dissonance-dominant in later rounds (prioritizing ambiguous boundary cases for refinement), yielding clinically interpretable rationales for each annotation request.

## Results

Under a 20% labeling budget across ten medical imaging datasets, SaE achieves a macro-averaged Top-1 accuracy of 82.57%, outperforming the strongest baseline MedCoOp+BADGE (77.75%) by 4.82 points. The largest per-dataset gains are on RETINA (+8.34% over MedCoOp+BADGE), Kvasir (+6.66%), and BUSI (+6.27%). On BTMRI, SaE also demonstrates superior calibration with ECE = 0.021 and NLL = 0.425, compared to PCB's ECE = 0.116 and NLL = 0.757, confirming that the Dirichlet evidence formulation effectively mitigates VLM overconfidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaE outperforms all five baselines on every one of the ten datasets. Against the strongest baseline MedCoOp+BADGE (macro 77.75%), SaE gains +4.82 points on average, with the largest margins on RETINA (+8.34%), Kvasir (+6.66%), and BUSI (+6.27%). MedCoOp+Entropy (77.39%) and MedCoOp+Coreset (73.84%) trail SaE by 5.18 and 8.73 points respectively, indicating that scalar uncertainty or diversity heuristics alone are insufficient. PCB (Bang et al., CVPR 2024) achieves only 71.41% macro accuracy and exhibits severe overconfidence (ECE = 0.116, NLL = 0.757 on BTMRI), with over 60% of predictions in the highest-confidence bin yet only ~85% accuracy there. BiomedCoOp (Koleilat et al., CVPR 2025), used as a few-shot reference without active learning, scores 71.35% macro, confirming that the active selection strategy itself contributes substantially beyond the VLM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SaE sits at the intersection of three established threads: prompt-learning adaptation of biomedical VLMs (CoOp, BiomedCoOp), evidential deep learning with Dirichlet parameterization (Sensoy et al., Jøsang's Subjective Logic), and pool-based active learning for medical imaging. Its key departure is the Similarity Evidence Head, which reframes raw VLM text-image cosine similarities as a total-evidence budget to be allocated across classes, rather than forcing them through a rigid temperature-scaled softmax. This contrasts with prior VLM-based AL methods (PCB, MedCoOp+BADGE) that reduce uncertainty to a single scalar from softmax probabilities, and with standard EDL that derives evidence from classifier logits rather than from the VLM's geometric similarity space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-factor acquisition schedule (vacuity-prioritized early, dissonance-prioritized later) gives clinicians an interpretable rationale for why a specific scan is selected, which is a practical requirement in annotation workflows that scalar-uncertainty methods cannot satisfy. The near-perfect calibration (ECE 0.021, NLL 0.425 on BTMRI) suggests the Dirichlet evidence can be trusted as a genuine confidence measure during iterative AL, reducing wasted annotation budget on confidently-wrong selections. Open questions include whether the fixed linear schedule generalizes to different budget sizes or round counts, whether the SEH transfers across VLM backbones beyond BiomedCLIP, and whether the PubMed-augmented prompt retrieval scales to open-ended or multi-label diagnostic settings.
