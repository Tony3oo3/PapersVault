## Problem

Existing 4D animal reconstruction methods require joint per-video optimization of motion and appearance, making them computationally expensive (e.g., GART needs 15 minutes per video) and dependent on clean 360-degree turntable sequences that are rare in real-world recordings. No prior feed-forward approach combines a state-of-the-art equine parametric model (VAREN) with high-fidelity appearance reconstruction from sparse viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261967/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a monocular video clip (processed in 16-frame windows via sliding window for arbitrary length) for motion recovery and a single 448×448 representative image (the first frame of the video) for appearance reconstruction. It outputs per-frame VAREN pose (38 joint rotations), shape (39 coefficients), and translation parameters, plus a canonical animatable 3D Gaussian avatar of 55,486 primitives bound to VAREN mesh faces, which is deformed to each frame via linear blend skinning to produce full 4D geometry-and-appearance renderings at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

4DEquine disentangles 4D reconstruction into two feed-forward sub-problems bridged by the VAREN parametric model. For motion, AniMoFormer uses a spatio-temporal transformer (per-frame spatial features → cross-frame temporal self-attention over a 16-frame window → VAREN parameter decoder) followed by a Post-Optimization stage that refines alignment via differentiable rendering against pseudo-GT keypoints (ViTPose++) and masks (Samurai). For appearance, EquineGS extracts multi-scale image features with a DINOv3 ViT-Large backbone and 3D point features from a subdivided VAREN mesh (55,486 vertices), then fuses them through a novel Dual-Stream Transformer Gaussian (DSTG) decoder—a modification of the MMDiT block from Qwen-Image—to predict per-Gaussian position offsets, rotations, scales, colors, and opacities in a single forward pass. Both components are trained exclusively on two new synthetic datasets: VarenPoser (1,171 video clips with diverse camera trajectories and MV-Adapter textures) for motion, and VarenTex (150K multi-view images generated via UniTex diffusion conditioned on normal maps, canonical coordinate maps, and ControlNet reference images) for appearance.

## Results

On motion reconstruction (Table 1), AniMoFormer achieves PCK@0.05 of 61.8% on APT-36K, 84.2% on AiM, and 90.5% on VarenPoser, with the lowest acceleration error (21.8 on AiM) and Chamfer Distance (3.4 on VarenPoser) among all methods. On full 4D appearance reconstruction (Table 2), 4DEquine achieves SSIM of 0.8364 and LPIPS of 0.1720 on the horse subset, and SSIM of 0.7828 and LPIPS of 0.2000 on the zebra subset (zero-shot), outperforming all baselines on perceptual and structural metrics. The method runs at 11 seconds per frame on a single A100 GPU, compared to GART's fixed 15 minutes per video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AniMoFormer decisively outperforms all five motion baselines across every metric and dataset. The closest competitor, AniMer [19], achieves PCK@0.05 of 44.5% on APT-36K and 55.5% on AiM, trailing AniMoFormer by 17.3 and 28.7 points respectively; model-free methods 3D-Fauna [16] and 4D-Fauna [49] are further behind (PCK@0.05 of 20.1–25.5% on APT-36K). For appearance reconstruction, 4DEquine surpasses GVFDiffusion [46] and both Fauna variants on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) for both horse and zebra. Against GART [12], 4DEquine trades a small PSNR deficit on horse (15.66 vs. 16.19) for substantially better SSIM (0.8364 vs. 0.7819) and LPIPS (0.1720 vs. 0.2308), and wins all three metrics on the zero-shot zebra subset (15.54/0.7828/0.2000 vs. 15.21/0.6752/0.2287). The few-shot GART* variant is outperformed by 4DEquine across all metrics on both subsets, confirming the advantage of the feed-forward single-image approach over per-video optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VAREN parametric horse model [57] and the AniMer spatial transformer [19], extending the latter with a temporal attention module to handle video sequences. It departs from the dominant optimization-based paradigm (GART, 4D-Fauna) that jointly refines motion and appearance per video, instead disentangling 4D reconstruction into a feed-forward motion regressor (AniMoFormer) and a single-image Gaussian avatar generator (EquineGS). The DSTG decoder in EquineGS is explicitly derived from the MMDiT block in Qwen-Image [31], repurposed for 3D Gaussian attribute prediction rather than image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11-second-per-frame inference (vs. ~15 minutes for GART) makes 4D equine reconstruction practical for real-time or near-real-time applications in animal welfare and veterinary medicine, and the zero-shot transfer to zebras and donkeys—despite training exclusively on horse-specific VAREN—suggests the learned image-to-Gaussian mapping captures species-general appearance cues. Key limitations remain: the VAREN model inadequately represents tail and mane dynamics, and the framework cannot handle dynamic lighting or environmental variation. Open questions include whether fusing multiple keyframes could capture unique markings more faithfully, and whether physics-based representations for appendages can be integrated without breaking the feed-forward pipeline.
