## Problem

Quantization of diffusion models introduces noise that disrupts the directional estimation at each sampling step, and this disruption is particularly severe for high-order samplers that rely on multiple intermediate evaluations to jointly estimate a sampling direction. Prior work treated fast samplers and model quantization as independent acceleration strategies, overlooking that quantization error can transform a stable probability-flow ODE into a variance-exploding SDE, causing trajectory diffusion and generation failure under aggressive bit-widths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263612/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantized model accepts the same conditioning as the original diffusion model—class labels for ImageNet 256×256, no conditioning for LSUN-Bedroom/Churches 256×256, or text prompts for MS-COCO 512×512—along with Gaussian noise, and outputs a synthetic image. The quantization calibration itself consumes dual-order trajectories: first-order sampler inputs at each timestep and second-order sampler intermediate points within each interval, which are used to supervise the quantization parameters rather than standard activation statistics.

## Method

The core contribution is Mixed-Order Trajectory Alignment (MOTA), which reformulates the quantization objective to align the quantized first-order direction trajectory with the full-precision higher-order direction trajectory at intermediate nodes, thereby imposing a stricter per-step error bound and fostering a more linear probability flow. For 8-bit quantization (SA-PTQ), MOTA is realized via dual-order trajectory sampling followed by module-level reconstruction using Adaround, where each module's rounding parameter is optimized to minimize the mismatch between its output on first-order inputs and the full-precision output on second-order intermediate inputs. For low-bit settings (SA-QLoRA), MOTA is combined with a cosine direction-alignment loss (L_COS) to jointly fine-tune LoRA weights and quantization scale/zero-point parameters, preserving directional fidelity under W4A8 and W4A4 configurations where prior methods fail.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with LDM-4 and DPM-Solver-2 (20 steps), SA-PTQ (W8A8) achieves FID 10.16 and sFID 9.89, outperforming all prior 8-bit quantization methods. Under W4A8, SA-QLoRA reaches FID 8.55 and sFID 8.51—surpassing even the full-precision model (FID 9.45). In the extreme W4A4 setting where prior methods fail entirely, SA-QLoRA still produces valid samples with FID 12.45 and sFID 13.73, compared to EfficientDM's FID 17.28. On MS-COCO 512×512 text-to-image (SD-v1.4), SA-PTQ (W8A8) achieves the best FID of 13.10 and sFID of 19.52.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four prior diffusion quantization methods (PTQ4DM, Q-diffusion, PTQD, EfficientDM) and the general-purpose BRECQ baseline. SA-PTQ (W8A8) wins on FID and sFID across all three evaluated datasets: on ImageNet it improves FID by 0.60 over PTQD (10.16 vs. 10.76) and by 1.27 over PTQ4DM (10.16 vs. 11.43); on LSUN-Bedroom it reduces FID by 0.48 over PTQD (9.83 vs. 10.31); on MS-COCO it achieves the best FID (13.10) and sFID (19.52) among all 8-bit methods. SA-QLoRA (W4A8) dominates the 4-bit weight setting, achieving FID 8.55 on ImageNet—0.9 lower than the full-precision model—and outperforming EfficientDM by 1.36 FID points. In the W4A4 regime, PTQ4DM, Q-diffusion, and PTQD all fail to produce valid samples, while SA-QLoRA achieves FID 12.45 versus EfficientDM's 17.28, a 4.83-point improvement. The ablation confirms that the Mixed-Order Trajectory Alignment calibration (MOTAC) contributes 4.1 FID reduction over plain BRECQ, and the additional cosine direction constraint (L_COS) adds a further 0.95 FID improvement over QLoRA alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously decoupled research threads: fast ODE/SDE solvers for diffusion sampling (DPM-Solver, DDIM, PNDM/PLMS) and post-training quantization of diffusion models (PTQ4DM, Q-diffusion, PTQD, EfficientDM). Prior quantization work treats the noise estimation network as a static function to be approximated, while sampler work assumes a full-precision network; this paper identifies the interaction between the two—specifically that quantization noise disrupts the multi-step directional estimation inherent to high-order solvers—as the critical failure mode. Methodologically, it builds on BRECQ/Adaround for the PTQ variant and extends QLoRA for the low-bit variant, but the core contribution is the Mixed-Order Trajectory Alignment objective, which aligns the quantized first-order direction with the full-precision higher-order direction at intermediate nodes, a constraint absent from all prior diffusion quantization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying diffusion models on edge or low-memory hardware: the paper demonstrates that W4A4 quantization, which causes outright generation failure in prior methods (transforming the deterministic ODE into a variance-exploding SDE), can be made to produce usable samples with only moderate quality loss. However, limitations remain: the W4A4 FID (12.45) is still substantially above full-precision (9.45), the method requires calibration data and (for SA-QLoRA) additional fine-tuning, and evaluation is confined to LDM-4/8 and SD-v1.4 on image tasks. Open questions include whether the mixed-order alignment strategy generalizes to video diffusion, DiT-based architectures, or even lower bit-widths (W2A2), and whether the calibration overhead is acceptable in production pipelines.
