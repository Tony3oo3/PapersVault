## Problem

NeRF-based CBCT reconstruction methods uniformly fuse all multi-resolution hash-grid levels, entangling low- and high-frequency components into a single representation. Under real CBCT acquisition conditions—where projections are corrupted by scatter, beam hardening, and detector glare—this uniform fusion propagates projection-induced bias into the volume, producing spurious textures in homogeneous tissue and blurred structural boundaries. No prior method adaptively selects which frequency levels to emphasize based on local spatial context while simultaneously modeling projection discrepancies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse CBCT projection sets (100–200 views on real chest phantom data; 15–50 views on synthetic volumes) and requires no ground-truth volume, training entirely self-supervised by matching rendered projections to measured ones. The output is a continuous neural attenuation field parameterized by an MLP that, at any 3D query point, returns the linear attenuation coefficient μ, a discrepancy density σ, and a heteroscedastic variance β². Rendering follows Beer–Lambert physics with a differentiable line-integral forward model, so the field is evaluated by ray sampling rather than voxel grids.

## Method

GH-NAF augments a multi-resolution hash encoder with a lightweight attention MLP that predicts per-point softmax weights over L hash levels, adaptively fusing coarse and fine features according to local complexity. The attenuation network outputs (μ, σ, β²), and a discrepancy-aware renderer applies a sinh-based correction term to the primary line integral to account for projection-model errors. Training uses a heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL loss where the learned variance β² modulates per-ray confidence, creating a curriculum that prioritizes low-uncertainty regions early. A key self-supervised signal for the attention module is derived from centered finite-difference attenuation gradients along rays: these gradients are mapped to soft Gaussian target distributions over hash levels, and the predicted attention is aligned via an uncertainty-weighted KL divergence—requiring no external annotations. Log-sum sparsity and depth-decay occlusion regularizers further stabilize the ill-posed sparse-view problem.

## Results

On the pronounced-discrepancy chest phantom (MANIQA, no-reference), GH-NAF achieves 0.338 / 0.414 / 0.392 / 0.393 at 100 / 125 / 150 / 200 views, outperforming NAF (0.252–0.296) and SAX (0.268–0.353) by 0.04–0.12 across all view counts. On the low-discrepancy FIPS dataset (25 views), GH-NAF reaches PSNR 39.91 / 46.54 / 43.84 and SSIM 0.972 / 0.991 / 0.989 on Seashell / Walnut / Pine, surpassing NAF and SAX in SSIM while trailing R²-Gaussian and Vol3DGS in PSNR by 1.6–2.4 dB. On the discrepancy-free synthetic benchmark (15 views), GH-NAF attains the best SSIM (0.934) among all methods, exceeding SAX (0.925) and NAF (0.916), though R²-Gaussian leads in PSNR (34.34 vs. 33.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NAF [45], GH-NAF consistently wins on every metric and dataset: MANIQA improves by 0.04–0.12 on the chest phantom, and SSIM gains 0.01–0.03 on FIPS and synthetic data. Against SAX [2], GH-NAF dominates on MANIQA (e.g., 0.414 vs. 0.268 at 125 views on the chest phantom) and on SSIM across FIPS and synthetic settings, though SAX edges out GH-NAF in PSNR on FIPS Seashell at 25 views (39.50 vs. 39.91 is actually GH-NAF winning; SAX wins at 50 views: 42.08 vs. 42.66 — correction: GH-NAF wins here too). Against the Gaussian-based baselines R²-Gaussian [46] and Vol3DGS [30], the trade-off is clear: these methods achieve 1.5–3.5 dB higher PSNR on FIPS and synthetic data (e.g., R²-Gaussian 45.35 vs. GH-NAF 42.66 on Seashell at 50 views), but their SSIM is consistently 0.01–0.03 lower, indicating weaker structural coherence. On the chest phantom, where projection discrepancy is the dominant challenge, GH-NAF's discrepancy-aware rendering gives it a decisive MANIQA advantage over all baselines, while the Gaussian methods exhibit visible beam-hardening shading and residual artifacts. FDK [5] and iterative methods (SART, ASD-POCS) serve as classical references on the synthetic dataset and are substantially outperformed by all neural methods (e.g., FDK PSNR 21.53 vs. GH-NAF 33.56 at 15 views).

## Relation to Prior Work

GH-NAF extends the NAF paradigm (Fang et al., 2022) for sparse-view CBCT reconstruction by introducing a gradient-guided, uncertainty-modulated attention mechanism over the multi-resolution hash grid levels of Instant-NGP (Müller et al., 2022). The discrepancy-aware rendering term (Eq. 6) is directly adopted from Park et al. (2025), and the heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL supervision follows Kendall & Gal (2017); the density and occlusion regularizers are borrowed from InfoNeRF (Kim et al., 2022) and Yang et al., respectively. The key conceptual departure from prior NeRF/NAF methods is the rejection of uniform hash-level fusion in favor of spatially adaptive, frequency-aware level selection, which the authors argue is necessary to prevent cross-level entanglement under sparse views and projection discrepancies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real CBCT projections contain irreducible discrepancies (scatter, beam hardening, detector glare) that uniformly-fused hash encodings propagate as low-frequency bias and spurious textures. The hash-level attention mechanism is lightweight and could be grafted onto other NeRF-based reconstruction pipelines, making it a potentially reusable component. However, the evaluation is limited to three FIPS cases and a single chest phantom, the FIPS ground truth is FDK-based (itself discrepancy-affected), and Gaussian baselines still outperform GH-NAF on PSNR in several settings, leaving open whether the structural-fidelity gains (SSIM, MANIQA) translate to clinical utility at scale. Future work should validate on larger, multi-site datasets and explore whether the discrepancy model generalizes across scanner geometries.
