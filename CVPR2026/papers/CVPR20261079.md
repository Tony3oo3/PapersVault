## Problem

Generating diverse high-resolution images for seed/prompt search in diffusion models is computationally expensive, and naively producing low-resolution candidates does not guarantee perceptual consistency with their full-resolution counterparts. The paper identifies that the downsampling operation and the flow matching velocity field do not commute, causing LR trajectories to diverge from downsampled HR trajectories, and asks how to enforce this consistency in a training-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261079/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pretrained rectified flow model (FLUX.1-dev or SD3.5-L) and produces a low-resolution preview image (e.g., 512×512) that faithfully mirrors the composition, color, and structure of the corresponding 1024×1024 HR generation. Sampling runs at full resolution up to a designated timestep t_D (≈30% of total NFE), after which the latent is downsampled by a scale factor s and continued at LR resolution for the remaining steps, yielding the preview without any additional training or super-resolution stage.

## Method

The core insight is to enforce a commutator-zero condition [D, v_θ](x_t, t) = 0 between the downsampling matrix D and the flow matching velocity field, ensuring the LR trajectory equals the downsampled HR trajectory. Two training-free mechanisms achieve this: (1) Downsample matrix selection generates s² mutually exclusive binary block-diagonal matrices and picks the one minimizing the commutator L2-norm via a single forward pass; (2) Commutator-zero guidance applies a fixed-point-inspired update over m subsequent timesteps, reusing the stored HR velocity at t_D (justified by rectified flow's velocity consistency property) to correct the LR latent without backpropagation. The approach is fully plug-and-play, requires no gradient computation, and generalizes to other spatial operations such as warping and translation.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, the proposed method achieves the best perceptual similarity (DreamSim 6.83, DiffSim 0.8721) and low-level similarity (PSNR 21.18 dB, FSIM 0.7953) among all baselines while delivering a 1.53× speedup (9.76 s vs. 14.78 s). On SD3.5-L, it attains the best DreamSim (13.47), PSNR (14.46 dB), DiffSim (0.8117), and FSIM (0.7408) with a 1.50× speedup. When combined with TaylorSeer temporal-axis acceleration, the method reaches a 3.05× overall speedup while maintaining superior HR consistency (DreamSim 7.79, PSNR 19.95 dB) compared to both TaylorSeer alone and NFE reduction to 10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three spatial/temporal baselines: NFE reduction to 20, direct 512×512 generation, and naive nearest-neighbor downsampling at t_D. On FLUX.1-dev, the proposed method outperforms all three in DreamSim (6.83 vs. 7.25/21.02/9.20) and PSNR (21.18 vs. 20.63/11.94/18.22 dB), though the NFE-20 baseline edges it out on FSIM (0.8268 vs. 0.7953) and PIQE (34.78 vs. 28.55). On SD3.5-L, the method wins on all perceptual and low-level similarity metrics (DreamSim 13.47, PSNR 14.46 dB, FSIM 0.7408) while ranking second only in PIQE. In the TaylorSeer integration (Table 3), Ours+Taylor achieves 3.05× speedup with DreamSim 7.79 and PSNR 19.95 dB, beating TaylorSeer alone (DreamSim 9.17, PSNR 18.67 dB) and NFE=10 (DreamSim 16.97, PSNR 16.43 dB) on all consistency metrics, confirming that spatial and temporal acceleration are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is grounded in the flow matching and rectified flow frameworks (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023; Liu et al., ICLR 2023), leveraging the linear-trajectory property of rectified flow to justify reusing velocity predictions across nearby timesteps. It departs from existing spatial-axis acceleration methods (Bottleneck Sampling, RALU) that also downsample latents mid-sampling but do not enforce perceptual consistency with the HR counterpart; the key conceptual contribution is the commutator-zero condition, which formalizes when downsampling and the velocity field can be interchanged. The fixed-point-iteration-inspired guidance draws on a separate line of work on gradient-free corrections in diffusion models, but applies it to a new objective (minimizing the commutator norm) rather than to inversion or editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Preview Generation workflow addresses a real practical bottleneck: users must generate many expensive HR candidates to find a satisfactory seed, and this paper offers a principled, training-free way to cheaply preview candidates with guaranteed perceptual alignment. The commutator-zero condition is a generalizable algebraic principle that extends beyond downsampling to warping and translation, suggesting a unified view of resolution- and transformation-consistent generation. Limitations include the assumption of trajectory compliance (not strictly satisfied in learned models), evaluation on only two base models, and a modest standalone speedup (~1.5×) that becomes more compelling only when combined with orthogonal temporal acceleration. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video generation, whether more aggressive scale factors remain stable, and whether the commutator-zero condition can be enforced during training rather than post-hoc.
