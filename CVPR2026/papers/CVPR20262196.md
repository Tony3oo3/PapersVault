## Problem

Existing zero-shot anomaly detection methods built on CLIP rely on coarse prompt fusion or implicit attention that loosely couples visual and textual representations, yielding unstable alignment and imprecise anomaly localization under domain shifts. This paper addresses the absence of a principled mechanism to organize and stabilize cross-modal interaction by reframing multimodal alignment as an adaptive potential field governed by attraction and equilibriumilibrium forces.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image (resized to E18×E18) and a pair of natural-language text prompts describing normal and abnormal states, both encodedoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14-336 backbone. It produces two outputs in one forward pass: an image-level anomaly score (a learnable class token attending over the graviton set, passed through a linear-sigmoid layer) and a pixel-wise anomaly probability map (averaging channel-wise scoring vectors from N=20 individual graviton classifiers, followed by sigmoid). Training follows a cross-dataset fine-tuning protocol—the model is fine-tuned on one dataset's test split and Ealuated zero-shot on all remaining benchmarks to prevent data overlap.

## Method

The core architecture introduces N=20 learnable semantic gravitons—dynamic mediator tokens that bridge visual and textual modalities through a Graviton Interaction Network of K=4 stages. At each stage, gravitons alternately absorb linguistic priors from text embeddings via a gated cross-attention module and visual patterns from the evolvingolving feature map via bidirectional attention, progressively forming localized semantic Equilibria. An adaptive Graviton Potential Regularization stabilizes this interaction with two physics-inspired forces: an attraction term computed as the 2-Wasserstein distance between modality-specific Eji distributions around each graviton, and an Eilibrium term measuring the free-Eji discrepancy between modalities, both weighted by attention-derived association coefficients. In the prediction head, each final-stage graviton acts as an individual classifier projecting a channel-wise scoring vector over the hierarchically decoded feature map, and the pixel-wise anomaly mask is obtained by averaging all graviton responses followed by a sigmoid activation.

## Results

On image-level zero-shot anomaly detection (AUROC), SGNet achieves 93.5 on MVTec-AD, 85.9 on VisA, 80.8 on MPDD, 92.9 on BTAD, 99.2 on DAGM, 98.7 on DTD-Synthetic, 96.4 on BrainMRI, and 97.8 on Br35H, setting new state-of-the-art on all eight benchmarks. At the pixel-level, SGNet reaches 92.8 (MVTec-AD), 95.9 (VisA), 97.5 (MPDD), 97.2 (BTAD), 99.5 (DAGM), 99.3 (DTD-Synthetic), 88.5 (CVC-ClinicDB), and 87.6 (Kvasir), outperforming the previous best (FE-CLIP) by up to 7.8 points on medical datasets. The gains are most pronounced on fine-grained industrial textures (MPDD: +2.8 over FE-CLIP at image-level) and medical imaging (CVC-ClinicDB: +4.0, Kvasir: +7.8 at pixel-level).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGNet outperforms the most recent SOTA, FE-CLIP [14], on all eight image-level benchmarks by margins of 1.6–2.8 AUROC points (e.g., 93.5 vs. 91.9 on MVTec-AD, 80.8 vs. 78.0 on MPDD) and on 7 of 8 pixel-level benchmarks, with the largest pixel-level gains on medical datasets (CVC-ClinicDB: 88.5 vs. 84.5, Kvasir: 87.6 vs. 79.8). Against AA-CLIP [30], SGNet wins on 7 of 8 image-level datasets but trails on BTAD (92.9 vs. 94.8) and on CVC-ClinicDB pixel-level (88.5 vs. 89.9). SGNet also surpasses AdaCLIP [6] on 7 of 8 image-level datasets (losing only on VisA by 0.1) and AnomalyCLIP [46] on all eight by 2.0–11.5 points. Compared to earlier methods—WinCLIP [17], APRIL-GAN [7], CoOp [44], and raw CLIP [36]—SGNet leads by 1.7–59.3 AUROC points depending on dataset and metric, with the most dramatic improvements on BTAD (92.9 vs. 34.5 for CLIP) and MPDD (80.8 vs. 63.6 for WinCLIP). VCP-CLIP [35] is competitive on DAGM pixel-level (99.4 vs. 99.5) but does not report medical results, limiting direct comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection line (AnomalyCLIP, WinCLIP, VCP-CLIP, FE-CLIP, AA-CLIP) by replacing their heuristic prompt fusion and implicit attention coupling with a structured, physics-inspired interaction mechanism. Rather than treating cross-modal alignment as a one-shot similarity computation, SGNet introduces learnable 'semantic gravitons' that iteratively mediate between text and vision through a Graviton Interaction Network, and regularizes this process with a Wasserstein-distance-based potential field loss. The conceptual departure is from ad-hoc prompt engineering to an energy-equilibrium formulation, though the underlying operations (cross-attention, gating, softmax weighting) remain standard transformer components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The physics-inspired potential field framing offers a more principled inductive bias for cross-modal alignment than the heuristic fusion strategies dominant in current CLIP-based ZSAD, and the Wasserstein-based regularization provides a concrete mechanism for enforcing isomorphic semantic basins across modalities. However, the performance gains over strong 2024–2025 baselines (FE-CLIP, AA-CLIP) are modest (≈1–2 AUROC points) and inconsistent across medical benchmarks, raising questions about whether the gravitational metaphor yields a genuine inductive advantage or primarily reorganizes existing attention operations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the cross-dataset fine-tuning protocol used here, whether the number of gravitons and interaction stages scale meaningfully to more complex or higher-resolution anomaly scenarios, and whether the physics framing translates to interpretability benefits in practice.
