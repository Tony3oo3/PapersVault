## Problem

Existing adversarial patches for evading AI surveillance are conspicuous, high-contrast textures that are always-on and typically single-modal (RGB or infrared only), making them impractical for everyday deployment where the wearer must remain visually unremarkable. This paper addresses the dual challenge of concealing the adversarial pattern in a default state and enabling on-demand, simultaneous deception across both visible and infrared detection modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The optimization consumes aligned visible-infrared image pairs from FLIR, INRIA, PennFudan, and LLVIP datasets, with the patch constrained to cover no more than 30% of the person's bounding box area and matched in area to prior square patches for fair comparison. Physically, the system takes a temperature control signal (20–70 °C range via a digital controller) as its activation input, and produces a polygonal adversarial patch (up to 50×50 cm on an XL T-shirt) whose RGB texture and thermal signature jointly degrade detection confidence below 0.5 within 50 seconds of heating.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage adversarial patch optimization coupled with a thermally activated garment architecture. In the shape-update stage, a polygonal template is iteratively evolved by adjusting vertex count, radius, and angle parameters to maximize attack success rate against an infrared detector; in the texture-update stage, the fixed shape is assigned an RGB texture optimized under EOT transformations (rotation, brightness, blur, scaling) with a combined total-variation and average-precision adversarial loss to fool a visible-spectrum detector. The physical garment stacks four layers—thermochromic microcapsule coating, adversarial patch fabric, flexible silicone heating pads (nickel-alloy wire, ~1 mm thick, 0.4 W/cm²), and a thermal-insulation substrate—so that heating above ~30 °C simultaneously renders the thermochromic layer transparent (revealing the RGB pattern) and generates a controlled thermal signature that perturbs infrared detectors, while cooling restores the plain black appearance.

## Results

On the INRIA visible-spectrum dataset, the proposed dual-modal patch achieves ASR of 54.4% (YOLOv3), 49.9% (YOLOv5), 46.8% (Faster R-CNN), and 38.4% (DETR), substantially outperforming the strongest visible baseline AdvYOLO (34.6%, 31.4%, 29.7%, 25.2%). On the LLVIP infrared dataset, the patch reaches 85.4%, 88.6%, 83.3%, and 80.2% ASR across the four detectors, exceeding the best infrared baseline AdvIB (72.7%, 75.1%, 70.3%, 65.8%) by 10–15 percentage points. Physical experiments confirm >80% adversarial success rate across indoor, shopping-mall, and outdoor street scenarios, with full pattern activation within 50 seconds and effective evasion up to 30 m distance and ±35° viewing angle.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed dual-modal patch outperforms all 11 baselines across both visible and infrared detection settings. On INRIA (visible), it surpasses AdvYOLO by ~20 pp (54.4% vs 34.6% on YOLOv3) and also beats T-SEA, CAP, FDA, AdvTexture, and AdvCloak, which are all single-modal visible patches. On LLVIP (infrared), it exceeds AdvIB by 12.7 pp on YOLOv3 (85.4% vs 72.7%) and by 14.4 pp on DETR (80.2% vs 65.8%), and similarly outperforms AdvIC, AIP, HIC-IR, and Bulb. The key advantage stems from the algorithmically optimized polygonal shape (vs. fixed square/rectangle in prior work) combined with the two-stage shape-then-texture optimization under EOT, which simultaneously degrades both visible and infrared detection confidence. No baseline matches the proposed method on both modalities simultaneously, as all prior patches are either visible-only or infrared-only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial patch literature by combining two previously separate threads—visible-spectrum patch optimization (Thys et al., AdvTexture, CAP) and infrared-spectrum patch optimization (AdvIB, AIP, HIC-IR)—into a single dual-modal patch, building on the cross-modal formulations of CDUPatch [46] and Wei et al. [60]. The key departure is physical: rather than deploying an always-on, high-contrast patch, the authors repurpose thermochromic microcapsule chemistry and flexible silicone heating elements to make the adversarial pattern on-demand and visually concealed as a plain black T-shirt at ambient temperature. The two-stage optimization (polygonal shape search for IR evasion, then EOT-regularized texture learning for visible evasion) follows established adversarial patch training recipes, so the algorithmic contribution is incremental; the novelty resides in the co-design of the thermal activation hardware with the patch geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates a practical, user-controllable countermeasure against the rapid global expansion of dual-modal AI surveillance, addressing a real gap where existing patches are too conspicuous for everyday wear. However, several practical limitations temper its impact: the 50-second activation delay, the 30 m effective range, the 3 °C thermal hysteresis that complicates rapid re-cycling, and the dependence on a portable power bank all constrain real-world deployment. Open questions include long-term durability of the thermochromic coating under repeated thermal cycling, whether defenders can detect the heating pad's own thermal signature as a tell, and whether the approach generalizes to more sophisticated detectors (e.g., video-based trackers or foundation-model-based systems) beyond the four static-frame detectors evaluated here.
