## Problem

Existing instance-level machine unlearning methods remove designated data but ignore the semantic relations among retained instances, causing progressive structural collapse in the representation space. This collapse degrades the deletion-retention balance: as unlearning proceeds, embeddings of retained instances drift away from their original semantic organization, undermining both retention accuracy and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260907/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained CNN (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50), a forget set of k images, and class-level attribute descriptions generated by GPT-4o, which are encoded into L2-normalized anchor vectors via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder. Crucially, the retention set is inaccessible; instead, adversarial variants of forget inputs pushed toward target-class features serve as surrogate probes. The output is an unlearned model that misclassifies forget instances while preserving retention and test accuracy, with a learnable two-layer projector bridging the feature extractor and semantic encoder spaces.

## Method

STRUCTGUARD introduces semantic anchors ('stakes') constructed from LLM-generated class attribute descriptions encoded by a frozen semantic encoder, and defines structure as the affinity matrix between instance embeddings and these anchors. Two complementary constraints preserve this structure: (1) structure-aware alignment minimizes the negative cosine similarity between the original and unlearned affinity matrices, maintaining relative anchor-instance relations; (2) structure-aware regularization penalizes parameter updates weighted by their structural importance (measured via gradients of the alignment loss with respect to each parameter), suppressing changes to structure-critical weights. A learnable projector calibrates unlearned features into the semantic encoder space for retention, while the deletion objective bypasses it for effective erasure. The total loss jointly optimizes alignment, regularization, retention cross-entropy (through the projector), and deletion cross-entropy (bypassing the projector).

## Results

On image classification at k=256 (the most challenging setting), STRUCTGUARD achieves 56.32% test accuracy and 61.67% retention accuracy on CIFAR-10, 56.91% and 83.30% on CIFAR-100, and 41.15% and 44.91% on ImageNet-1K, while maintaining 100% forget-set accuracy across all datasets. On face recognition (Lacuna-10, k=64), it reaches 27.71% test and 29.97% retention accuracy. The method achieves average gains of 32.9%, 22.5%, and 19.3% over the strongest baseline (L2UL) in test accuracy on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-1K respectively, with all methods achieving 100% deletion accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRUCTGUARD decisively outperforms all baselines across every dataset and deletion size. Against the strongest baseline L2UL [Cha et al., AAAI 2024], it gains 6.39% on CIFAR-10 test accuracy (56.32 vs. 45.44 at k=256) and 7.93% on retention accuracy (61.67 vs. 48.95), with the gap widening to 15.70% on CIFAR-100 retention (83.30 vs. 67.60). Against ADV [Cha et al.], gains are even larger: 13.76% on CIFAR-10 test and 16.05% on retention. The older methods from Golatkar et al. [CVPR 2020] (FISHER, NEGGRAD) and Wu et al. [NeurIPS 2020] (RAWP) collapse to near-chance accuracy (1–15%) at k=256 on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K, failing to balance deletion and retention. Notably, STRUCTGUARD surpasses the ORACLE (retrained-from-scratch reference) by 17.73% on CIFAR-10 test accuracy at k=256, demonstrating that structure preservation can exceed what full retraining achieves without access to the retention set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instance-level machine unlearning line initiated by Golatkar et al. (FISHER, NEGGRAD) and Cha et al. (ADV, L2UL), which focus on either reproducing a retrained model or enforcing misclassification on forget samples. It departs from these methods by introducing an explicit structural-preservation objective: semantic anchors generated from LLM-derived class attribute descriptions (following Menon & Vondrick) and encoded via CLIP serve as fixed reference points, and two constraints (structure-aware alignment and structure-aware regularization) are added to maintain anchor-instance affinities during unlearning. The surrogate probe set is constructed using adversarial variants of forget inputs (Ilyas et al.), consistent with the no-retention-data regime adopted by Cha et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central diagnostic—that progressive structural collapse in the representation space is the mechanism behind the deletion-retention trade-off—gives the field a concrete, measurable quantity to track during unlearning, and the anchor-based remedy is practical because it requires only the pretrained model and forget set. Limitations include reliance on GPT-4o for attribute generation and a single CLIP encoder (ViT-B/32), and the evaluation is confined to k ≤ 256 forget instances on classification, face recognition, and retrieval; whether the approach scales to very large forget sets or to generative and multi-modal models remains open. The moderate gains over the strongest baseline (L2UL: ~4–8% on CIFAR-10/100) suggest the structural-preservation framing is helpful but not yet a dominant factor in unlearning performance.
