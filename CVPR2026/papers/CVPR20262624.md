## Problem

Occluded person Re-ID suffers from two intertwined failures: similar occlusion patterns (e.g., the same vehicle blocking multiple pedestrians) create misleading feature similarity that causes erroneous matches, and severe occlusion removes discriminative information. Existing augmentation methods emphasize occlusion diversity but ignore the misleading similarity from shared occlusion patterns, while feature reconstruction methods recover missing information at the cost of high inference latency that limits real-time deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262624/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB pedestrian images resized to 256×128 as query and gallery inputs, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT backbone with 16×16 patches. A frozen CLIP text encoder with learnable prompt tokens (conditioned on image features via CoCoOp) generates foreground heatmaps used for both augmentation and scoring. At inference, the output is a refined retrieval ranking: standard Euclidean-distance similarities are modulated by per-gallery prompt scores, and a two-stage re-ranking (top-K₁=200 candidates scored against top-K₂=5 high-confidence intermediate references) produces the final similarity vector converted back to distances for standard CMC/mAP evaluation.

## Method

COPE builds on a CLIP ViT backbone with prototype contrastive learning and introduces three modules. The Cross-Identity Consistent Occlusion (CICO) module applies identical Gaussian-shaped occlusion groups to different identities within a batch and enforces an occlusion consistency loss (mean pairwise MSE of occlusion-region features via global weighted average pooling) so the model learns to suppress attention on shared occlusion tokens. The Prompt Background Filling (PBF) module uses a CoCoOp-style conditional prompt (learnable tokens + 'person') through a frozen text encoder to produce foreground heatmaps supervised by human parsing labels, then randomly fills the background and applies a foreground alignment loss to preserve identity-relevant features under environmental variation. The Prompt Similarity Scoring (PSS) module converts the foreground heatmap into a scalar prompt score via an MLP, supervised by cosine similarity to the class center in a memory bank; at inference, it selects top-K₂ high-prompt-score gallery samples as intermediate references and adds their weighted similarity to the top-K₁ candidate scores, achieving re-ranking-quality gains at roughly 30% of the inference time of standard re-ranking.

## Results

COPE achieves state-of-the-art performance on the challenging Occluded-Duke dataset with 82.4% Rank-1 and 76.4% mAP (with PSS), surpassing the previous best (SGFNet) by +5.9% Rank-1 and +9.2% mAP. On P-Duke-REID, it reaches 94.3% Rank-1 and 89.5% mAP, and on Market-1501 it attains 96.3% Rank-1 and 93.3% mAP. On MSMT17, COPE achieves the best mAP of 77.2% among all compared methods. The PSS module contributes an additional +5.3% Rank-1 and +6.5% mAP over the training-only COPE on Occluded-Duke, while adding only ~9 seconds of inference time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Occluded-Duke, COPE (82.4/76.4) outperforms the strongest ViT-based FPC (76.7/72.8) by +5.7% Rank-1 and +3.6% mAP, and the recent CLIP-based SGFNet (76.5/67.2) by +5.9% Rank-1 and +9.2% mAP. On P-Duke-REID, COPE (94.3/89.5) leads SGFNet (94.1/86.3) and PCL-CLIP (91.9/84.8) by a clear margin in both metrics. On Occluded-REID (cross-domain), COPE achieves the best mAP (90.9) but trails SGFNet (93.0) and ProFD (92.3) in Rank-1, indicating slightly weaker top-1 retrieval under domain shift. On Market-1501, COPE (96.3/93.3) surpasses SGFNet (96.2/91.1) and PCL-CLIP (95.7/91.4), confirming gains extend to holistic settings. On MSMT17, COPE achieves the best mAP (77.2) but is slightly behind SGFNet (90.0) in Rank-1 (89.5 vs. 90.0). Against the feature-reconstruction method FPC, COPE is faster (51.4s vs. 95.7s inference) while delivering +5.4% Rank-1 and +8.8% mAP on Occluded-Duke.

## Relation to Prior Work

COPE builds directly on PCL-CLIP's prototype-based contrastive learning framework for CLIP fine-tuning in Re-ID and adapts CoCoOp's conditional prompt mechanism to generate foreground heatmaps for occluded pedestrians. It departs from the dominant data-augmentation paradigm (SPT, ADM, OAMN) that emphasizes occlusion *diversity*, instead arguing that *consistent* occlusions shared across identities, combined with a feature-alignment loss, more effectively suppresses misleading similarity from recurring occlusion patterns. It also contrasts with feature-reconstruction methods (FPC, FRT, RFCnet) that recover missing features via k-nearest gallery neighbors at high inference cost, replacing that expensive step with a lightweight prompt-score-weighted similarity refinement at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COPE demonstrates that occlusion-aware Re-ID can be substantially improved through training-time augmentation design (consistent occlusions + background filling) and a cheap inference-time re-ranking, without the computational overhead of feature reconstruction. The PSS module's ability to outperform both standard k-reciprocal re-ranking and the SOTA FPC reconstruction while running in roughly half the time makes it a practical drop-in for deployment. Open questions include whether the prompt-scoring mechanism generalizes to other occlusion-heavy domains (e.g., vehicle Re-ID, video tracking), whether the reliance on human-parsing labels for PBF pre-training limits applicability to unlabeled settings, and whether the consistent-occlusion insight transfers to other transformer-based retrieval tasks beyond Re-ID.
