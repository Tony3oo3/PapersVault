## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D shape tokenizers for autoregressive generation rely on geometric level-of-detail hierarchies (octrees, progressive meshes) originally designed for rendering and compression. These spatial hierarchies are token-inefficient, requiring thousands of tokens even for coarse shapes, and their early prefixes decode into geometrically and semantically implausible scaffolds that are unusable in AR workflows. The paper asks whether tokens can instead be ordered by semantic salience so that short prefixes already yield complete, category-plausible shapes.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 3D shape's point cloud, encodes it into a Direct3D triplane latent (32×32×3 planes, 16-dim features), and produces a 1D sequence of up to 512 continuous 32-dim register tokens. Any prefix of this sequence can be decoded into a complete 3D shape via a generative DiT decoder. For autoregressive generation, LoST-GPT takes a text prompt (via OpenCLIP) or a conditioning image and autoregressively predicts 128 continuous tokens, which are then decoded into a 3D mesh. Tokens remain in continuous space without vector quantization.

## Method

LoST uses a ViT encoder with learnable register tokens (not tied to spatial patches) to restructure triplane geometry into a 1D semantic hierarchy, enforced by causal masking and nested dropout over power-of-2 prefix lengths. A DiT-based generative decoder reconstructs the full triplane from any prefix, transitioning from plausible generation at short lengths to high-fidelity reconstruction at longer ones. The key training innovation is the RIDA loss, which pre-trains a 3D semantic extractor to align relational distances in triplane latent space with DINOv2 feature distances via three components: global relational contrast (multi-positive InfoNCE), inter-instance rank distillation, and spatial structure distillation. This semantically structured latent space then guides the LoST decoder through a perceptual alignment loss. For AR generation, LoST-GPT is a LlamaGen-style transformer with a small MLP diffusion head (following MAR) that predicts continuous tokens, conditioned on OpenCLIP embeddings for text- or image-to-3D synthesis.

## Results

On autoregressive 3D generation (image-to-3D), LoST-GPT achieves a FID of 34.251 and DINO similarity of 0.758 using only 128 tokens, outperforming ShapeLLM-Omni (FID 48.702, DINO 0.680 with 1024 tokens) and OctGPT (FID 66.926 with ~50,000 tokens). In tokenizer reconstruction, LoST with 512 tokens achieves FID 13.591 and DINO 0.921, surpassing OctGPT (~239k tokens: FID 88.483, DINO 0.695) and VertexRegen (~7.5k tokens: FID 86.098, DINO 0.791) on semantic metrics while using 0.1%–10% of the tokens. Even a single LoST token yields DINO similarity of 0.731, already exceeding OctGPT's best (0.695) at 239k tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoST-GPT decisively outperforms all four baselines on autoregressive generation. Against ShapeLLM-Omni (the closest competitor, also image-to-3D), LoST-GPT reduces FID from 48.702 to 34.251 (−30%) and improves DINO from 0.680 to 0.758, while using 8× fewer tokens (128 vs 1024). Against OctGPT, LoST-GPT achieves a 2× lower FID (34.251 vs 66.926) with roughly 400× fewer tokens. Llama-Mesh, a text-to-3D method, shows the weakest FID (118.576) and is not directly comparable on DINO. In the tokenizer reconstruction comparison (Table 1), LoST dominates on semantic metrics (FID, DINO) across all token budgets; VertexRegen achieves a lower Chamfer Distance (0.034 vs 0.234) at its maximum ~7,530 tokens, but LoST matches or exceeds it on CD at 64 tokens (0.382) while using 15× fewer tokens, and far surpasses it on FID (21.133 vs 86.098) and DINO (0.880 vs 0.791) at the same budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoST directly extends the Level-of-Semantics tokenization paradigm from 2D images (Flextok, Semanticist) to 3D shapes, adapting their nested dropout, register tokens, and generative decoder strategies to the triplane latent space of Direct3D. It departs from the dominant geometric LoD tokenization approach (OctGPT, VertexRegen) by arguing that spatial coarse-to-fine hierarchies—designed for rendering and compression—produce token-bloated, semantically incoherent prefixes unsuitable for AR modeling. Where prior image tokenizers rely on REPA to align internal features with DINO, LoST introduces RIDA to bridge the modality gap between 3D triplane latents and 2D DINO features via relational distance alignment rather than direct feature regression.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 128-token budget achieving SOTA AR 3D generation (vs. ~50,000 for OctGPT) represents a dramatic efficiency gain that could make AR 3D generation far more practical, and the any-prefix property enables early stopping and interactive generation. However, the method is tied to the Direct3D triplane representation, relies on a diffusion decoder that adds inference cost, and the few-token regime still exhibits artifacts. Open questions include whether the semantic ordering principle generalizes to other 3D representations (Gaussian splats, meshes), whether variable-length generation with complexity-aware stopping closes the remaining gap, and whether the self-generated 300k-shape training set limits robustness compared to Objaverse-scale data.
