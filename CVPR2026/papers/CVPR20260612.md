## Problem

Rare disease facial phenotype analysis is severely constrained by the absence of standardized benchmarks reflecting real-world ultra-low-data conditions (1–7 images per condition) and by the lack of systematic evaluation for phenotype-aware synthetic augmentation. Prior work either focuses on a small number of well-represented syndromes with hundreds of samples per class or generates synthetic faces without assessing their downstream diagnostic utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260612/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 224×224 frontal pediatric facial images (resized and ImageNet-normalized) paired with one of 103 disease class labels; for synthetic generation, real images are first upscaled to 512×512 via Real-ESRGAN and colorized with DDColor before serving as conditioning exemplars. Outputs include 103-way Top-k classification scores, 512×512 synthetic facial images, structured VLM phenotype reports (region-wise descriptions plus a predicted diagnosis), and 5×5 landmark distance matrices used for cosine-similarity ranking. The dataset itself comprises 456 images across 103 Orphanet-verified conditions with an average of 4.4 samples per class.

## Method

RDFace introduces a unified evaluation protocol that benchmarks six pretrained backbones (ResNet-152, DenseNet-169, FaceNet, VGG16, Swin Transformer, CLIP) under both standard supervised classification (75/25 split, 5-fold CV) and Prototypical Network few-shot learning (5/10/15-way 1-shot episodic training). The key technical contribution is a phenotype-aware synthetic augmentation pipeline: DreamBooth is fine-tuned per disease class with the prompt 'a child with [disease_abbr] disease' to generate 100 class-conditioned images, while FastGAN is trained unconditionally from scratch for 8,000 iterations; generated samples are then ranked by 5-landmark cosine similarity to real class prototypes and injected in Top-n subsets (1000–6000) to study scaling behavior. Fidelity is assessed through a three-part protocol combining landmark-based structural similarity, dual-expert clinical plausibility review (Cohen's κ), and VLM-generated phenotype description comparison (Qwen2.5-VL / LLaVA-NeXT reports embedded via BioBERT for cosine similarity). The central finding is that class-conditioned DreamBooth augmentation improves Top-1 accuracy by up to 13.7% in ultra-low-shot regimes, whereas unconditioned FastGAN synthesis degrades performance, demonstrating that phenotype fidelity—not sample volume—drives diagnostic gains.

## Results

On the RDFace benchmark (456 images, 103 rare disease classes, avg. 4.4 samples/class), DenseNet-169 achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy of 15.93% and Top-30 accuracy of 64.42% under standard supervised classification. In the 5-way 1-shot few-shot setting, DenseNet reaches 26.20% accuracy. Phenotype-aligned DreamBooth augmentation improves DenseNet's Top-1 from 15.93% to 17.52% (Top-1000) and up to 21.06% (Top-6000), while unconditional FastGAN augmentation degrades performance (13.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external baseline is DeepGestalt (reproduced as 'Gestalt'), the representative prior diagnostic system for rare disease facial recognition. DenseNet-169 outperforms Gestalt by 9.74 percentage points on Top-1 accuracy (15.93% vs. 6.19%) and by 15.22 points on Top-30 (64.42% vs. 49.20%), indicating that standard transfer-learning backbones with a 103-way classifier substantially exceed the retrieval-based DeepGestalt architecture under the RDFace ultra-low-shot protocol. All six benchmarked backbones surpass Gestalt on Top-1, with the gap ranging from 0.71 pp (ResNet, 6.90% vs. 6.19%) to 12.92 pp (CLIP, 3.01% vs. 6.19% — here CLIP actually underperforms Gestalt). In the few-shot setting, no direct comparison to Gestalt is reported, but DenseNet's 26.20% (5-way 1-shot) demonstrates that metric-based learning with pretrained features is competitive in the constrained episodic regime. The paper also shows that generic augmentations (MixUp: 15.75%, CutMix: 16.11% for DenseNet) provide negligible gains over real-only baselines, whereas phenotype-aligned DreamBooth augmentation yields consistent improvements across backbones (e.g., VGG: 11.68% → 16.64%), confirming that class-conditioned synthesis is the key differentiator over both the external baseline and standard augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rare disease facial phenotype recognition line (DeepGestalt, GestaltMatcher) by shifting from well-represented syndromes with hundreds of images per class to an ultra-low-shot regime of 1–7 samples per condition, a setting the authors argue prior benchmarks have not addressed. It repurposes DreamBooth (class-conditioned diffusion) and FastGAN (unconditioned GAN) for phenotype-aware augmentation, departing from GestaltGAN which generated synthetic faces for privacy but never evaluated them in downstream diagnostic tasks. The VLM-based phenotype description evaluation (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-NeXT) adapts multimodal report-generation techniques from radiology to the RD craniofacial domain, where no prior systematic fidelity assessment existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RDFace provides the first standardized, ethically curated benchmark for ultra-low-shot pediatric rare disease facial classification, enabling reproducible comparison of backbones, few-shot strategies, and synthetic augmentation under realistic data scarcity. The finding that phenotype-aligned (DreamBooth) augmentation consistently helps while unconditioned (FastGAN) synthesis degrades performance offers a clear design principle for future synthetic medical data pipelines. Key limitations include the small scale (456 images, 103 classes), heterogeneous sourcing with incomplete demographic metadata, and the still-modest absolute accuracy (~21% Top-1 best case), leaving open whether these gains transfer to larger, more diverse clinical cohorts or to the full 10,000+ rare disease space.
