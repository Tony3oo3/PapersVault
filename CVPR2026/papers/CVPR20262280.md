## Problem

Contrastive vision-language models like CLIP and SigLIP learn bag-of-words representations that fail at compositional tasks such as attribute-object binding. Prior fixes rely on synthetically generated hard negatives that are benchmark-specific, fail to generalize, and substantially degrade zero-shot and retrieval performance. The paper identifies two root causes: long training captions make BoW representations sufficient, and the final global pooling in both encoders destroys the spatial and structural information needed to learn binding.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes RGB images paired with captions from CC3M (DreamLIP variant), from which noun-phrases are extracted offline via spaCy dependency parsing. The cross-modal attention pooling that produces concept-specific visual embeddings is used exclusively during training; at inference, the pipeline is identical to vanilla SigLIP, producing a single image embedding and a single text embedding whose cosine similarity yields the image-text score. No additional forward passes or architectural changes are required at test time.

## Method

C²LIP fine-tunes SigLIP with two auxiliary losses that require no new parameters. First, a noun-phrase concept loss (L_npc) extends the SigLIP sigmoid loss to a multi-positive variant, aligning the global image embedding simultaneously with all concept (noun-phrase) embeddings from the caption, forcing the model to represent every concept in the global representation. Second, a cross-attended concept-aware loss (L_xac) applies a parameter-free cross-modal attention pooling—using concept embeddings as queries over the projected visual tokens—to extract concept-specific visual embeddings, which are then contrastively aligned with their corresponding text concept embeddings. Because this attention pooling has no learnable parameters, the learning signal propagates directly to the penultimate visual representation, encouraging binding before the global pooling operation. The total loss is a weighted sum of the standard SigLIP contrastive loss, L_npc, and L_xac, and the entire procedure requires only a few fine-tuning epochs on regular captions without any hard-negative generation pipeline.

## Results

C²LIP (ViT-B/16) achieves an average score of 75.0 across all benchmarks, the highest among all ViT-B-based models, compared to 70.0 for the original SigLIP and 71.5 for SigLIP fine-tuned on CC3M alone. On compositionality, C²LIP reaches 94.2/88.3/73.1 on SugarCrepe (Add/Replace/Swap) and 79.7/75.3/55.2/44.2 on SugarCrepe++ (Replace-I2T/Replace-TOT/Swap-I2T/Swap-TOT), substantially outperforming hard-negative-based methods like NegCLIP (69.1/70.9/53.4/39.1 on SugarCrepe++) and DAC-LLM (53.7/59.6/32.2/18.1). On retrieval, C²LIP improves over SigLIP on Flickr30K (97.0 vs 95.2), MSCOCO (82.7 vs 78.9), and IIW (76.4 vs 75.3), with only a modest 2.6-point drop on ImageNet (73.5 vs 76.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among ViT-B composition-aware baselines, C²LIP achieves the best average (75.0) and dominates on SugarCrepe++: it outperforms NegCLIP by +10.6 on Replace-I2T (79.7 vs 69.1) and +25.1 on Swap-TOT (44.2 vs 18.1), and beats DAC-LLM by +26.0 on Replace-I2T (79.7 vs 53.7) and +26.1 on Swap-TOT (44.2 vs 18.1), while also surpassing them on all retrieval benchmarks (e.g., Flickr30K 97.0 vs 92.4 for NegCLIP, 83.7 for DAC-LLM). CLIC is the closest competitor on SugarCrepe++ Swap-I2T (59.0 vs 55.2), but C²LIP recovers on Swap-TOT (44.2 vs 27.0) and all retrieval tasks. Against the larger CLIPS model (ViT-L/14, ~5× more parameters), C²LIP is within 0.2 points on average (75.0 vs 75.2), winning on SugarCrepe Add (+7.6) and Replace (+2.2) but losing on Swap (−4.7) and ImageNet (−3.3). Compared to SigLIP2 (same ViT-B/16 backbone, multi-task training), C²LIP leads on SugarCrepe Add (+5.2), Replace (+2.3), and SugarCrepe++ Replace-I2T (+2.0), while SigLIP2 retains a 5.0-point edge on ImageNet. The only consistent loss for C²LIP is ImageNet (73.5 vs 76.1 for SigLIP), which the authors attribute to the shift toward scene-centric representations that conflicts with ImageNet's object-centric classification objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SigLIP's architecture (learnable query token attention pooling and sigmoid contrastive loss) and the broader CLIP contrastive V&L framework, but departs fundamentally from the dominant hard-negative paradigm (NegCLIP, CE-CLIP, DAC, SLVC, CoN-CLIP, TripletCLIP) that has driven prior compositionality improvements. Rather than synthesizing adversarial captions or images, the authors identify two structural root causes—long captions being solvable by bag-of-words and global pooling destroying binding information—and address them with noun-phrase extraction via spaCy and a parameter-free cross-modal attention pooling mechanism. The approach is conceptually related to object-centric binding work (Assouel et al.) but is substantially simpler, requiring no LLM decomposition, no additional forward passes, and no learnable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that compositionality can be improved to SOTA levels on SugarCrepe/SugarCrepe++ without the generalization failures and zero-shot degradation that plague hard-negative methods, and without any inference-time overhead. This makes C²LIP a drop-in fine-tuning recipe for existing SigLIP/CLIP checkpoints. Open questions remain: the modest ImageNet drop (scene-centric vs. object-centric representations) is not fully resolved, the final global pooling layer is acknowledged as suboptimal, and the method is validated primarily on object-attribute binding rather than broader compositional reasoning (spatial relations, multi-object interactions). Whether the approach extends to other CLIP-like architectures beyond SigLIP is left as future work.
