## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods (CAT-Seg, DeCLIP, PartCATSeg) refine cost volumes through a serial pipeline of spatial aggregation followed by class aggregation. This cascading structure causes knowledge interference: biases introduced by one aggregation propagate into the other, distorting class semantics and degrading image-text alignment. The paper asks how to decouple these two aggregation types while still effectively integrating their complementary information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image and a set of text category descriptions, which CLIP's visual and text encoders convert into a cost volume of shape H×W×N (N = number of categories). It outputs a per-pixel segmentation mask (semantic or part-level) via a decoder. For part segmentation evaluation, two protocols are used: Pred-All (relying solely on model predictions) and Oracle-Obj (providing ground-truth object masks to guide part-level segmentation). Training uses COCO-Stuff (171 categories, 118k images) with ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14 backbones at 80k iterations.

## Method

PCA-Seg replaces the serial spatial→class aggregation with a parallel architecture where both operations process the cost volume embedding simultaneously, producing independent spatial context and class-level semantic features. An Expert-Driven Perceptual Learning (EPL) module integrates these two streams: a multi-expert parser (4 identical conv-BN-GeLU blocks) extracts complementary knowledge from the concatenated features, and a lightweight coefficient mapper (two conv layers + softmax) learns pixel-specific weights to adaptively fuse expert outputs. A Feature Orthogonalization Decoupling (FOD) loss (squared cosine similarity between the two streams, weighted by λ=0.01) enforces orthogonality, ensuring the experts learn diverse rather than redundant representations. Each parallel block adds only 0.35M parameters and 0.96G GPU memory over the serial counterpart, yet yields consistent mIoU gains across eight benchmarks.

## Results

On OVSS with ViT-B/16, PCA-Seg achieves 16.1 mIoU on A-847, 22.3 on PC-459, 38.1 on A-150, 62.2 on PC-59, 97.3 on PAS-20, and 82.7 on PAS-20b, setting new state-of-the-art across all six benchmarks. With ViT-L/14, it reaches 18.3 on A-847 and 84.4 on PAS-20b. On OVPS, PCA-Seg attains 49.3 h-IoU (Pred-All) and 52.9 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj) on Pascal-Part-116, and 26.1 h-IoU (Pred-All) and 51.8 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj) on ADE20K-Part-234, outperforming the previous best PartCATSeg by 3.5% and 2.5% h-IoU respectively on Pascal-Part-116.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCA-Seg outperforms all 18 compared baselines across both OVSS and OVPS. Against the closest serial-aggregation competitor DeCLIP [50], it gains 0.8–1.8% mIoU on OVSS (e.g., 38.1 vs 36.3 on A-150, 82.7 vs 81.3 on PAS-20b with ViT-B/16) and 3.5% h-IoU on Pascal-Part-116 (Pred-All). Versus H-CLIP [36], the margin is larger: 3.6–5.7% mIoU on OVSS and 4.5% on PAS-20b. Against PartCATSeg [13] (the strongest OVPS baseline), PCA-Seg improves h-IoU by 3.5% (Pred-All) and 2.5% (Oracle-Obj) on Pascal-Part-116, and by 1.9% and 1.8% on ADE20K-Part-234. Earlier methods (CAT-Seg [11], EBSeg [43], SED [54], Mask-Adapter [27]) trail by 2–5% mIoU on A-150, while two-stage approaches (LSeg+ [17], ZegFormer [15], ZSseg [57], OVSeg [28], GKC [19]) are surpassed by 10–14% mIoU on A-150. On the ViT-L/14 backbone, PCA-Seg also beats LLMFormer [45] (18.3 vs 16.5 on A-847) and ODISE [56] (18.3 vs 11.1 on A-847), confirming consistent gains across backbone scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the cost aggregation paradigm established by CAT-Seg (CVPR 2024) and refined by DeCLIP (CVPR 2025) and PartCATSeg (CVPR 2025), which process the CLIP-derived cost volume through serial spatial-then-class aggregation blocks. The key architectural departure is replacing this serial pipeline with a parallel one, motivated by the observation that cascading aggregations cause knowledge interference between class-level semantics and spatial context. The integration mechanism draws on Mixture-of-Experts-style multi-expert parsing with learned pixel-specific gating (coefficient mapper), while the feature orthogonalization decoupling loss is a novel regularization not present in prior cost aggregation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-motivated fix for a specific architectural limitation in the dominant CLIP-based OSPS pipeline, achieving SOTA on eight benchmarks with only 0.35M additional parameters per block. The diagnostic showing >88% feature coupling in serial vs. <3% in parallel aggregation gives practitioners a concrete tool for understanding when and why their cost volume refinement degrades. Open questions include whether the parallel architecture generalizes to non-CLIP backbones or other dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, optical flow), whether the orthogonalization constraint remains beneficial at larger scales, and whether the EPL module's multi-expert design can be further simplified without performance loss.
