## Problem

Stereo camera calibration in autonomous systems degrades over time due to mechanical vibrations, thermal fluctuations, and material wear, yet recalibration requires returning to controlled laboratory settings. Existing online tracking methods either rely on data-driven training, complex robust estimators (RANSAC), or learned matchers, making them difficult to deploy in resource-constrained real-time perception pipelines. The paper asks whether a simple, training-free optimization of a robust epipolar error can achieve competitive calibration tracking precision without explicit outlier rejection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stereo image pair (undistorted via OpenCV polynomial model), their respective camera intrinsics K₀ and K₁, and a reference essential matrix from offline calibration. Keypoints are detected with SIFT (128-d descriptors), normalized into camera coordinates via K⁻¹, and tentative correspondences are formed by k=5 nearest neighbors in descriptor space using Faiss. The output is an updated essential matrix on its 5-DoF manifold, decomposed into rotation (3 angles) and translation (direction + rescaled magnitude) for comparison against ground truth or LiDAR-derived depth.

## Method

TESO minimizes a kernelized epipolar error — a sum of Gaussian kernels exp(−(yᵀE(θ)x)²/2σ²) over bidirectional kNN correspondences — directly on the essential matrix manifold parameterized as E(θ) = U exp[Ω₁(θ)] Σ₀ exp[−Ω₂(θ)] Vᵀ with five parameters θ ∈ ℝ⁵. The kernel correlation inherently down-weights outlier matches, eliminating the need for RANSAC or learned robust estimators. Optimization proceeds frame-by-frame using Schaul et al.'s adaptive stochastic second-order method: per-parameter exponential moving averages of gradient, Hessian diagonal, and gradient variance drive both an adaptive memory size (stabilizing under abrupt changes) and a quasi-Newton step scaled by the signal-to-noise ratio of the gradient. Only two hyperparameters are required (kernel width σ ≈ pixel angular resolution, and ε = 10⁻⁷ for numerical stability), and no data-driven training is involved, making the method directly deployable on any stereo system.

## Results

On the CARLA-FlowGuided dataset, TESO's kernelized epipolar error optimized via differential evolution achieves rotation precision of 0.007° (Rx), 0.027° (Ry), and 0.012° (Rz), with a translation direction error of 1.34°. On the CARLA-Drift dataset with ±0.01°/frame drift, TESO tracks rotations with 0.011° (Rx), 0.039° (Ry), and 0.015° (Rz) mean absolute error, while reducing depth consistency error from 2.06 m to 0.52 m. On MAN TruckScenes, TESO achieves 0.12° precision on the Y-axis rotation (critical for stereo depth) under calibrated conditions, and after correcting KITTI intrinsic decalibration, improves Y-axis precision 20× to 0.025° and depth accuracy 50×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TESO (DE) clearly outperforms Kumar et al. [21] on every reported metric: Ry is 8.5× lower (0.027° vs 0.23°), Rz is 9× lower (0.012° vs 0.11°), and depth consistency is 1.2 m better (4.6 vs 5.6). Against Rockwell et al. [27], TESO wins decisively on Ry (0.027° vs 0.153°, a 5.7× improvement) and Rz (0.012° vs 0.017°), matches on Rx (both 0.007°), but loses on translation direction error (1.34° vs 2.73° in TESO's favor—actually TESO wins here too at 1.34° vs 2.73°). The closest competitor is Gong et al. [15], the best end-to-end learning method: Gong achieves slightly better Rx (0.003° vs 0.007°), Rz (0.006° vs 0.012°), translation (0.86° vs 1.34°), and depth consistency (3.2 m vs 4.6 m), but TESO substantially outperforms on Ry (0.027° vs 0.077°, a 2.9× advantage) and matches on keypoint offset (both 0.51 px). Overall, TESO is competitive with the strongest learning-based baseline without any training data, while being the only method that requires no data-driven training and operates with only two hyperparameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

TESO combines three established components into a new pipeline: the adaptive-learning-rate stochastic optimizer of Schaul et al. [29], the kernel correlation robustification of Tsin & Kanade [34], and the 5-parameter essential-matrix manifold parameterization of Helmke et al. [18]. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in online stereo calibration (Dang et al., Cvišić et al., Kumar et al., Gong et al.), which handles outliers through robust estimators (RANSAC), learned matchers, or Kalman-filter state tracking. TESO instead robustifies the loss function itself via kernelization, making the choice of keypoint detector non-critical and eliminating the need for explicit outlier rejection or data-driven training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying stereo systems on resource-constrained platforms (autonomous trucks, robotics), TESO offers a calibration tracker with only two hyperparameters, no training data, and low CPU/memory overhead—making it straightforward to integrate. The empirical finding that the kernelized loss alone (optimized by differential evolution) matches end-to-end learning methods on CARLA-FlowGuided suggests that loss-function design, rather than learned feature matching, may be the critical ingredient for robust calibration. Open questions include extending tracking to intrinsic parameters (focal length), handling larger multi-sensor rigs, and validating on real-world decalibration events rather than simulated drift.
