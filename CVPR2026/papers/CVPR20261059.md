## Problem

Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) for vision-language models improves accuracy but produces poorly calibrated predictions, and existing calibration fixes (C-TPT, O-TPT) that add geometric regularization terms create a trade-off between accuracy and calibration. The paper reveals that the underlying mechanism is convergence to flat versus sharp minima in the loss landscape, but existing methods are limited to single-step updates that cannot adequately explore flat regions, and explicitly enforcing flatness via output-feature constraints degrades performance.

## Input / Output

The pretraining stage takes only a predefined text prompt (e.g., the hard template 'a photo of a' or a learned prompt from CoOp/MaPLe) and a set of class names as input, requiring no images or labeled data. It produces a new prompt vector that serves as a drop-in initialization for any existing TPT pipeline, after which the standard entropy-minimization adaptation proceeds unchanged. The final output is a class label with a calibrated confidence score, and the pretraining incurs no additional cost during test-time inference.

## Method

FPP (Flatness-aware Prompt Pretraining) optimizes a new prompt vector using two complementary losses: an alignment loss (L2 distance between the learned prompt's text features and the original prompt's text features) to preserve zero-shot semantics, and a flatness loss (cosine distance between text features computed under small random perturbations of both the class names and the prompt) to reduce output sensitivity. The paper proves that existing C-TPT/O-TPT regularization terms are mathematically equivalent to a fixed-perturbation SAM step, showing that the perturbed point yields higher expected EM loss (Theorem 1), which is why they implicitly guide prompts toward flat minima. By pretraining the prompt directly into a flatter region, FPP enables the subsequent single-step TPT update to land in a flat minimum without any perturbation or geometric constraint on outputs. The weighting λ = γ₁ + γ₂/K downweights the flatness term for larger class sets, and the entire pretraining is data-free, requiring only 1K iterations with AdamW and a cosine schedule.

## Results

On the fine-grained classification benchmark (10 datasets, CLIP-ViT-B/16, TPT framework), FPP achieves an average accuracy of 65.37%, ECE of 4.13, and SCE of 0.96, setting new state-of-the-art on all three metrics simultaneously. This is the first method to break the accuracy–calibration trade-off observed in prior work: TPT achieves 64.62% accuracy but suffers 11.67 ECE, while O-TPT reduces ECE to 4.46 at the cost of dropping accuracy to 64.12%. FPP outperforms TPT by +0.75% accuracy and −7.54 ECE, and outperforms O-TPT by +1.25% accuracy and −0.33 ECE. Under natural distribution shifts (ImageNet variants), FPP maintains only a 0.42% accuracy drop relative to TPT while reducing OOD ECE from 11.99 to 4.63.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPP outperforms all four baselines (CLIP, TPT, C-TPT, O-TPT) on average accuracy, ECE, and SCE simultaneously, which no prior method achieves. Against TPT (the primary adaptation baseline), FPP gains +0.75% accuracy while reducing ECE by 64% (11.67→4.13) and SCE by 17% (1.15→0.96). Against O-TPT, the strongest prior calibration method, FPP improves accuracy by +1.25% (64.12→65.37) while further reducing ECE by 0.33 (4.46→4.13) and SCE by 0.19 (1.15→0.96), demonstrating that the accuracy–calibration trade-off is not inherent. Against C-TPT, FPP gains +0.89% accuracy and reduces ECE by 1.19 (5.32→4.13). The advantage is consistent across frameworks: with CoOp/MaPLe prompts (Table 2), FPP achieves 67.01% accuracy and 4.07 ECE vs. MaPLe+O-TPT's 65.05% and 4.11 ECE; with DynaPrompt (Table 3), FPP reaches 65.26% accuracy and 4.56 ECE vs. DynaPrompt+O-TPT's 63.35% and 6.48 ECE. On OOD ImageNet variants, FPP reduces ECE from 11.99 (TPT) to 4.63 with only 0.42% accuracy loss, whereas O-TPT drops accuracy by >2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TPT (Shu et al., NeurIPS 2022) and its calibration-aware extensions C-TPT (Yoon et al., ICLR 2024) and O-TPT (Sharifdeen et al., CVPR 2025), which add geometric regularization to the entropy minimization objective. The key conceptual departure is a theoretical analysis showing that these regularization terms are mathematically equivalent to a fixed-perturbation SAM-like update, reframing calibration improvement as convergence to flat minima rather than as a feature-dispersion constraint. Rather than modifying the TTA objective, FPP decouples the flatness objective into a data-free pretraining stage that produces better initial prompts, leaving the downstream TPT pipeline entirely unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'replace only the initialization' simplicity makes FPP immediately deployable alongside any existing TPT pipeline without architectural changes or additional inference cost, which is practically significant for safety-critical VLM applications (healthcare, autonomous systems) where calibration reliability is non-negotiable. The empirical demonstration that flat minima inherently improve calibration even without explicit regularization (Table 8, Fig. 3) opens a broader question about the intrinsic role of loss-landscape geometry in probabilistic reliability beyond the TPT setting. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-B/16 and the O(D^{-3/2}) approximation in Theorem 1, leaving open whether the flatness–calibration link holds for larger backbones or in fully supervised regimes.
