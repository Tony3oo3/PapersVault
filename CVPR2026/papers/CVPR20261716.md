## Problem

Existing video-text datasets lack a clear specification of what to describe and effective oversight for quality control, producing inconsistent captions with imprecise terminology, missing information, subjective language, and visual hallucinations. This limits the ability of video-language models to capture fine-grained aspects like subject motion and camera dynamics, and prevents reliable post-training with preference-based methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261716/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes video clips (films, commercials, games, user-generated content) as input and produces structured captions of 200–400 words organized across five aspects (subject, scene, motion, spatial, camera), each grounded in hundreds of defined visual and motion primitives. For reward modeling and critique generation, the input is a (video, caption) pair, yielding a binary preference score or a correctional critique respectively. Downstream, re-captioned ~150K professional videos are used to fine-tune Wan2.2 for text-to-video generation with prompts up to 400 words specifying camera motion, angle, lens, focus, and framing.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-part recipe: (1) a structured specification co-developed over a year with 100+ professional video creators, defining 200+ primitives with decision rules across camera motion, setup, and cinematography; (2) a Critique-based Human-AI (CHAI) oversight framework where a VLM drafts a high-recall pre-caption from human-labeled primitives, a trained human expert writes a correctional critique identifying errors and omissions, and the model produces a refined post-caption, iterated until accurate; (3) post-training on the resulting (pre-caption, critique, post-caption) triplets using SFT, DPO, and RLHF-V on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, with explicit auxiliary training on critique generation and preference-label classification (Yes/No probability as reward). The key finding is that critique quality—precision, recall, and constructiveness—directly governs downstream performance, and that this modest expert supervision enables the open-source model to outperform closed-source Gemini-3.1-Pro across captioning, reward modeling, and critique generation.

## Results

On the unified benchmark (~5k held-out triplets spanning films, games, commercials, and UGC clips), the best full-data post-training method (SFT + RLHF-V on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) achieves 17.8 average BLEU-4 for caption generation, 87.1% binary accuracy for reward modeling, and 27.5 average BLEU-4 for critique generation. This substantially outperforms the strongest closed-source baseline Gemini-2.5-Pro (6.0 / 62.0 / 3.0) and Gemini-3.1-Pro (5.1 / N/A / 3.3) across all three tasks. Adding explicit preference and critique supervision (full-data vs. caption-only) yields large gains, e.g., reward modeling jumps from 53.2% to 86.8% under SFT, and critique BLEU-4 from 3.8 to 26.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's SFT + RLHF-V (All) model on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct outperforms all 19 baselines across all three tasks. In caption generation, it achieves 17.8 average BLEU-4, more than 2× the best closed-source model Gemini-2.5-Pro (6.0) and ~3× Gemini-3.1-Pro (5.1); the gap is especially large on Motion (11.0 vs. 4.2 for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and Spatial (25.2 vs. 7.2). In reward modeling, the 87.1% binary accuracy far exceeds Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.0%) and GPT-5 (59.5%), with the largest margin on Subject (90.2% vs. 58.2%). In critique generation, the 27.5 BLEU-4 is roughly 8× the best baseline Gemini-3.1-Pro (3.3), indicating that no prior model can produce constructive correctional feedback at scale. Among open-source baselines, Qwen3-VL-72B-Instruct is the strongest (5.3 / 60.0 / 2.8), yet the 8B fine-tuned model still surpasses it by 3.4× on captioning and 1.45× on reward modeling. The comparison also reveals that current models (both open and closed) perform well on Subject and Scene aspects but struggle with Motion and Camera, suggesting these dimensions are underrepresented in existing training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scalable oversight paradigm from NLP (Saunders et al., Bowman et al.) to video captioning, where humans critique model-generated pre-captions rather than writing from scratch, and builds on standard post-training methods (SFT, DPO, RLHF-V) by adding explicit critique and preference-label supervision on top of the usual contrastive objectives. It departs from prior video-text datasets (MSR-VTT, ShareGPT4Video, TUNA-Bench, etc.) by introducing a structured specification of hundreds of visual and motion primitives developed collaboratively with professional filmmakers and game designers, formalized in the concurrent CameraBench-Pro work. The reward modeling approach follows VQAScore's probability-of-'Yes' formulation, and the downstream video generation pipeline builds on Wan2.2 with re-captioned professional footage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified benchmark jointly evaluating caption generation, reward modeling, and critique generation for video, and demonstrates that a modestly supervised open-source 8B model can outperform closed-source Gemini-3.1-Pro on this benchmark, suggesting that data quality and specification matter more than raw model scale for precise video understanding. The CHAI oversight framework and the professional specification are practical contributions likely to be adopted by teams curating video-language data at scale. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes beyond the five-aspect taxonomy, whether stronger critique models can eventually replace human annotators, and whether the re-captioning pipeline transfers to other video generation architectures beyond Wan2.2.
