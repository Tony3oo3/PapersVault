## Problem

Standard image priors (PnP, RED, diffusion models) regularize the full image but leave the null-space component of the reconstruction unconstrained, allowing the solver to hallucinate arbitrary invisible details. While NPN introduced learnable null-space projections, it selects arbitrary directions without exploiting the geometric structure of natural images, wasting capacity and introducing bias. The paper asks: can we impose a principled, graph-smooth structure specifically on the invisible component to reduce hallucination while improving convergence and predictability?

## Input / Output

The method requires the full sensing matrix H (Hadamard for compressed sensing, structured Toeplitz for deblurring/SR, Bayer pattern for demosaicing) and the noisy measurement vector y ∈ R^m. A U-Net predictor G is trained offline to map y to a p-dimensional GSNR vector, and the null-restricted Laplacian T = P_n L P_n must be eigendecomposed once per (H, L, n, p) configuration. The output is a full-resolution reconstructed image x̂ ∈ R^n, with the GSNR term and an optional null-only graph penalty added to the standard data-fidelity and denoiser-based prior in the optimization objective.

## Method

GSNR constructs a null-restricted graph Laplacian T = P_n L P_n, where L is a 4- or 8-nearest-neighbor grid Laplacian encoding local pixel similarity, and P_n is the null-space projector of H. The p-smoothest eigenmodes of T (lowest graph frequencies) form an orthonormal projection matrix S ∈ R^{p×n} that captures the most structured, predictable null-space directions. A U-Net predictor G is trained via supervised regression to estimate Sx* from y, and at reconstruction time two terms are added to the solver objective: a learned GSNR penalty ‖G*(y) − Sx̃‖² and a null-only graph regularizer x̃ᵀT x̃ weighted by γ_g. Theoretically, the p-smoothest T-modes are proven minimax-optimal over a graph-energy ellipsoid in Null(H) (Theorem 2), achieve higher null-space coverage than geometry-free bases under a GMRF prior (Theorem 1), and enjoy stronger per-mode statistical coupling to the measurements (Proposition 1), while the graph regularizer improves the condition number of the PnP-PGD fixed-point iteration for faster convergence.

## Results

On the CelebA test set, GSNR-PnP with L4nn achieves 29.42 dB PSNR on super-resolution (SRF=4) and 39.89 dB on demosaicing, outperforming vanilla PnP (27.37 / 39.35 dB) by ~2 dB and the closest baseline NPN (29.21 / 39.77 dB) by 0.2 dB. On deblurring (σk=1, n=64²·3), GSNR with L8nn reaches 38.18 dB on CelebA and 33.60 dB on Places365 with Lip-DnCNN, improving over the PGD-PnP baseline by ~2 dB and over NPN by ~0.3 dB. The paper reports up to 4.3 dB gains over baseline formulations across all four tasks and up to 1 dB over end-to-end learned models (DDN, FISTA-Net).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSNR-PnP consistently outperforms all seven baselines on both SR and demosaicing. Against vanilla PGD-PnP, GSNR gains ~2 dB on SR (29.42 vs 27.37) and ~0.5 dB on demosaicing (39.89 vs 39.35), confirming that null-space structure injection is the primary source of improvement. Compared to NPN (which uses a geometry-free L=I null-space basis), GSNR with graph Laplacians wins by 0.2 dB on SR and 0.12 dB on demosaicing, a small but consistent margin attributable to the graph-smooth eigenmode ordering. Against end-to-end learned baselines, GSNR-PnP surpasses DDN-Cascade (28.92) and FISTA-Net (28.97) by ~0.5 dB on SR, and beats DDN-Independent (38.92) and FISTA-Net (38.82) by ~1 dB on demosaicing. On deblurring, GSNR with L8nn improves over NPN by 0.38 dB (CelebA, Lip-DnCNN) and 0.38 dB (Places365), while the graph regularizer (γg) has negligible effect on final PSNR but accelerates convergence in the transient regime. For diffusion solvers (DPS, DiffPIR), GSNR provides modest but systematic gains (e.g., 30.19→30.48 dB SSIM/PSNR), indicating the null-space constraint helps the generative prior resolve ambiguities without hallucinating.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends NPN (Jacome et al., NeurIPS 2025), which introduced a learnable low-dimensional projection matrix S into Null(H) with a supervised predictor G(y), by replacing the arbitrary/learnable null-space basis with the p-smoothest spectral modes of a null-restricted graph Laplacian T = P_n L P_n. It departs from prior null-space methods (DNSN, DDN) that treat all invisible directions as equally relevant, and from classical graph-based regularization (graph-wavelet filterbanks, nonlocal graph regularization) that operates on the full image rather than isolating the null component. The solver infrastructure (PnP-PGD, DIP, diffusion solvers like DPS and DiffPIR) is inherited from the broader plug-and-play and generative restoration literature, with GSNR inserted as an additional null-only regularizer and predictor term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSNR provides the first principled, theoretically grounded selection criterion for null-space directions (coverage via GMRF spectral analysis, minimax optimality over graph-energy ellipsoids, and per-mode predictability bounds), turning what was previously an ad-hoc or fully learned subspace choice into an interpretable eigenvalue problem. The plug-compatible design means practitioners can drop GSNR into existing PnP, DIP, or diffusion pipelines without architectural changes, and the coverage/predictability curves offer objective diagnostics for choosing the projection dimension p. Key limitations include the requirement for exact knowledge of H (problematic for real-world deblurring), the offline EVD cost at large scales, and restriction to linear forward operators; open directions include learning the graph structure L, extending to nonlinear sensing, and integrating GSNR into latent-space diffusion models.
