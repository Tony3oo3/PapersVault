## Problem

Continual learning in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) suffers from catastrophic forgetting, and existing solutions each carry critical drawbacks: architecture-based methods add inference overhead and generalize poorly, rehearsal-based methods require storing historical data (raising privacy and storage concerns), and parameter-orthogonality regularization is theoretically insufficient to prevent inter-task interference. The paper identifies that gradient orthogonality is the principled condition for lossless task disentanglement, yet prior gradient-based methods fundamentally depend on access to past task data, which is often unavailable in practice.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the LLaVA-v1.5-7b backbone with LoRA adapters on all linear layers of the language model, consuming standard image-and-text instruction pairs and producing free-form text responses. Evaluation spans six diverse multimodal tasks (ImageNet-R, ArXivQA, VizWiz, IconQA, CLEVR-Math, Flickr30k) covering visual question answering, caption generation, and mathematical reasoning, with VQA tasks scored by accuracy and captioning tasks by a composite of BLEU-1/4, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr. The framework is rehearsal-free: only the current task's data and previously merged LoRA weights are needed at each training stage.

## Method

Octopus introduces History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization (HiFGO), which enforces orthogonality between the current task's LoRA parameters and the Gradients of Previous parameters Within Current data distribution (GPWC), computed by backpropagating the current-task loss through the merged weights of the most recent prior task—requiring no historical data. A Taylor-expansion analysis shows this gradient-space condition, rather than parameter-space orthogonality, is the necessary and sufficient first-order guarantee that new-task updates do not degrade prior-task loss. To avoid the plasticity-stability conflict that arises when regularization competes with the task objective, Octopus employs a two-stage finetuning strategy: Stage 1 performs unconstrained LoRA training to reach a local optimum on the current task, and Stage 2 refines within a small data subset under the combined orthogonality loss and L2 norm regularization, keeping parameters near the unconstrained optimum while enforcing disentanglement. A historical-task proxy approximation further reduces the orthogonalization cost from O(number of tasks) to O(1) by using only the most recent merged weights as a surrogate for the full task history.

## Results

On the UCIT benchmark (6 multimodal tasks: ImageNet-R, ArXivQA, VizWiz, IconQA, CLEVR-Math, Flickr30k), Octopus achieves 71.08% Avg and 71.01% Last accuracy, establishing new SOTA. This surpasses the previous best method HiDe-LLaVA by 2.14% in Avg and 6.82% in Last. Notably, Octopus also exceeds the vanilla rehearsal baseline (69.90% Avg, 68.44% Last) without storing any historical data, and its ArXivQA Last score (93.03%) even surpasses the multi-task joint training upper bound (92.20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Octopus outperforms all six compared baselines on both Avg and Last metrics. Against the prior SOTA HiDe-LLaVA [17] (MoE-based, Avg 68.94 / Last 64.19), Octopus gains +2.14% Avg and +6.82% Last, with particularly large improvements on VizWiz (+7.55 Avg, +9.68 Last) and CLEVR-Math (+7.20 Avg, +21.03 Last). Over the closest regularization-based competitor O-LoRA [48] (Avg 64.54 / Last 58.36), Octopus improves by +6.54% Avg and +12.65% Last, demonstrating that gradient-space orthogonalization (HiFGO) is substantially more effective than parameter-space orthogonalization. Octopus also surpasses the architecture-based MoELoRA [7] by +9.75% Avg and +18.95% Last, and the prompt-based L2P [50] by +17.09% Avg and +22.50% Last. Classical regularization methods LwF [27] and EWC [24] trail by over 11% in Avg and 21% in Last. Notably, Octopus even exceeds the vanilla rehearsal baseline (which stores historical data) by +1.18% Avg and +2.57% Last, confirming that history-free gradient orthogonalization can match or beat data-replay strategies without any replay buffer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-orthogonalization line of work (OGD, Farajtabar et al. 2020) by making it history-free: instead of requiring stored gradients from past tasks, it computes Gradients of Previous parameters Within Current data distribution (GPWC), using only the merged LoRA weights of the most recent task as a proxy. It departs from parameter-orthogonality methods like OLoRA by providing a Taylor-expansion argument that gradient orthogonality is the theoretically correct constraint for lossless task disentanglement, since the LoRA update direction need not align with the instantaneous gradient at the converged solution. The two-stage finetuning strategy (unconstrained exploration followed by constrained refinement) is inspired by annealing schedules and addresses the well-known plasticity-stability trade-off that plagues single-stage regularization approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Octopus demonstrates that a rehearsal-free, architecture-free method can match or exceed methods that store replay buffers or maintain task-specific LoRA modules, which is practically significant for privacy-sensitive and resource-constrained deployment of MLLMs. The positive backward transfer (+0.41 BWT) suggests that well-constrained sequential learning can yield beneficial cross-task transfer rather than merely preventing degradation. However, all experiments are conducted on a single benchmark (UCIT) with a single base model (LLaVA-v1.5-7B), and the authors acknowledge limitations regarding the upper bound on task count and performance on highly analogous tasks, leaving open whether the GPWC proxy remains effective as task diversity grows or when tasks share substantial representational overlap.
