## Problem

Conventional UAV geo-localization relies on decoupled pipelines fusing GNSS with visual-inertial odometry for ego-pose and active sensors (e.g., laser rangefinders) for target localization, making them fragile in GNSS-denied environments and hardware-intensive. This paper reformulates both tasks as a unified pixel-to-3D registration problem against a geo-referenced 3D map, but must simultaneously satisfy the 'impossible triangle' of drift-free accuracy, robustness to aggressive 6-DoF motion and day-to-night/seasonal appearance changes, and real-time performance on resource-constrained onboard hardware.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream (512 px inputs), a geo-referenced 3D map (e.g., Google Earth tiles), known camera intrinsics, and a single coarse pose prior for the first frame (up to 10 m / 10° noise from GNSS/IMU or ground truth). It outputs the UAV's 6-DoF pose per frame and, for any queried pixel, its real-world geographic coordinates (longitude, latitude, altitude) via ray casting into the 3D map using the estimated pose. No GNSS or IMU signals are required after initialization, and the 3D map is rendered on-the-fly by a dedicated thread rather than pre-computed.

## Method

PiLoT employs a dual-thread architecture where a Render Thread generates a single geo-referenced reference view and back-projects N depth-valid pixels into 3D geo-anchors, while a concurrent Localization Thread registers the incoming query frame against this shared reference. A lightweight MobileOne-S0 encoder with a U-Net decoder extracts a three-level feature pyramid (1/4, 1/2, 1 resolution, C=32) trained end-to-end via a geometric reprojection loss on a million-scale synthetic dataset generated through an AirSim-Cesium-Unreal pipeline, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The core optimizer, JNGO, first generates 144 pose hypotheses via rotation-aware anisotropic sampling (wider pitch/yaw ranges) with Gaussian translational noise, then refines all hypotheses in parallel using a coarse-to-fine Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer over 500 geo-anchors in CUDA, and finally selects the best hypothesis by combining feature-alignment cost with a motion regularization term against the Kalman-filter predicted pose. This one-to-many strategy (many hypotheses vs. one rendered reference) achieves robust convergence under inter-frame displacements up to 10 m and 10° yaw while sustaining 25+ FPS on NVIDIA Jetson Orin.

## Results

On the synthetic SynthCity-6 benchmark, PiLoT achieves a median translation error of 0.46 m and rotation error of 0.03° at 28 FPS, outperforming the best baseline Render2Loc(RoMaV2) (0.47 m / 0.04°) while running 35× faster. On the real-world UAVScenes dataset (zero-shot, no fine-tuning), PiLoT reaches 1.27 m / 0.47° median error, beating Render2Loc(RoMaV2) (1.42 m / 0.58°) by ~11%. On the challenging UAVD4L-2yr dataset with two-year seasonal gaps, PiLoT attains 0.92 m / 0.89° median error with 100% completeness, surpassing all baselines including Render2Loc(RoMaV2) at 1.05 m / 0.97°. For target geo-localization, PiLoT achieves 90.81/94.32/96.85% Recall@1/3/5 m on real single-target tracking, the best among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PiLoT outperforms all seven baselines across all three datasets in both median translation and rotation error. The closest competitor is Render2Loc(RoMaV2), which achieves 0.47 m on SynthCity-6 and 1.05 m on UAVD4L-2yr, but PiLoT edges ahead (0.46 m and 0.92 m respectively) while running 35× faster (28 FPS vs 0.8 FPS). The hybrid methods Render2ORB and Render2RAFT suffer severe drift, with median errors of 4.37–5.21 m on synthetic data and only 38–92% completeness, confirming the limitations of VO-based tracking without global correction. Among absolute localizers, PixLoc (5 FPS) and Render2Loc variants (0.4–20 FPS) all trail PiLoT by 0.1–1.6 m in median translation error on real data. Notably, PiLoT achieves 100% completeness on all three datasets, whereas Render2ORB drops to 38.4% on SynthCity-6 and PixLoc to 86.5% on UAVD4L-2yr, highlighting PiLoT's superior robustness under aggressive motion and appearance variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PiLoT departs from the dominant decoupled paradigm (VIO+GNSS for ego-pose, active sensors for targeting) by reformulating both as a single pixel-to-3D registration problem against a geo-referenced 3D map. Conceptually, it synthesizes ideas from map-based visual localization (PixLoc, Render-and-Compare, MeshLoc), feature-matching pipelines (LoFTR, RoMaV2, Aerial-MAST3R), and direct-alignment optimizers, but reorganizes them into a dual-thread architecture with a joint stochastic-gradient optimizer (JNGO) that has no direct predecessor. The large-scale AirSim-Cesium-Unreal synthetic dataset is positioned as filling a gap left by existing UAV benchmarks (UAVScenes, MatrixCity, UAVD4L) that lack sufficient scale, geometric ground truth, and viewpoint diversity for sequential training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating GNSS and IMU dependencies while achieving 25+ FPS on Jetson Orin, PiLoT addresses a critical practical gap for autonomous UAVs operating in GNSS-denied environments (urban canyons, indoor, contested airspace). The zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with a lightweight MobileOne-S0 network outperforming heavy matching-based approaches (0.4–2 FPS) at 28 FPS is a notable efficiency-accuracy tradeoff that could influence how future UAV localization systems are designed. Key limitations remain: the system requires pre-existing high-fidelity 3D mesh maps, restricting deployment to areas with such data, and performance degrades under extreme conditions (dense fog, significant calibration bias). The authors identify extending to universal representations (DOM, DEM) as the natural next step to broaden geographical coverage.
