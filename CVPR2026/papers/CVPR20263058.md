## Problem

Most Earth Observation foundation models are pretrained with masked image modeling, which emphasizes local pixel reconstruction and imposes weak constraints on global semantic structure, while modern optical vision foundation models (e.g., DINOv3) rely on contrastive self-distillation to produce semantically rich feature spaces. This objective mismatch makes it difficult to effectively distill optical VFM knowledge into multispectral EO models, and no prior work has aligned the student's pretraining paradigm with the contrastive training of the VFM teacher to enable coherent cross-modal transfer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 10-channel Sentinel-2 multispectral imagery (with the first three channels forming the RGB subset) at 60 m resolution, augmented with high-resolution aerial counterparts from fMoW-RGB where available. During pretraining, random crops produce global views (224×224) and local views (96×96) that are fed to separate teacher and student branches. The pretrained Swin-Tiny backbone outputs frozen feature representations (class tokens and patch tokens) that are then attached to a UPerNet decoder for segmentation/change detection or a linear probe for classification.

## Method

DEO employs a dual-teacher contrastive distillation framework with a Swin transformer student. The multispectral branch follows a DINO-style self-distillation setup where an EMA-updated teacher sees only global views while the student sees both global and local views, with a coding rate regularizer preventing representation collapse. The optical branch distills class tokens and patch tokens (from both final and intermediate layers) from a frozen DINOv3 teacher into corresponding student features via separate projection heads, decoupling the optical and MS learning paths. The key design insight is that because the student's MS pretraining objective is itself contrastive self-distillation—matching DINOv3's training paradigm—the student's latent space aligns more closely with the VFM's feature geometry than MIM-based alternatives, yielding more effective cross-modal knowledge transfer. The two losses are combined additively, training both modalities in unison without compromising either feature space.

## Results

DEO achieves an overall average macro mIoU of 69.72 across all optical and multispectral segmentation benchmarks, outperforming the next-best method (SatDiFuser, 65.99) by 3.73 points. On optical datasets, DEO reaches 81.98 average mIoU (vs. 81.76 for SatDiFuser and 79.17 for DINOv2-B), while on multispectral datasets it achieves 57.45 (vs. 53.25 for CROMA and 51.11 for Copernicus-FM). In change detection, DEO scores 75.9 average F1 (1.2 points above Copernicus-FM's 74.7), and in classification it reaches 69.22 average (1.31 points above DINOv3-B's 67.91). The model uses only 87M parameters and 0.5M pretraining images, yet outperforms DINOv3-LS (303M params, 493M images) and SatDiFuser (949M params).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEO outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset in both optical and multispectral settings. The closest optical competitor is SatDiFuser (81.76 vs. 81.98 avg mIoU), but SatDiFuser uses a 949M-parameter model and trails DEO by 7.24 points on MS datasets (50.21 vs. 57.45). Among MS-capable baselines, CROMA (53.25 MS avg) and Copernicus-FM (51.11 MS avg) are the strongest, yet DEO surpasses them by 4.20 and 6.34 points respectively, with particularly large gains on crop and flood segmentation (GB-SA-c.: 37.96 vs. 34.67; PASTIS: 28.96 vs. 24.66). DINOv3-LS, the largest optical VFM tested (303M params, 493M pretraining images), achieves 76.40 optical and 50.40 MS, both well below DEO's 81.98 and 57.45 despite using ~1000× more pretraining data. In change detection (Table 2), DEO leads by 1.2 points over Copernicus-FM (75.9 vs. 74.7 avg F1), and in classification (Table 3) it exceeds DINOv3-B by 1.31 points (69.22 vs. 67.91). Notably, DEO achieves these results with only 87M parameters and 0.5M pretraining images, yielding the best overall average rank of 1.3 versus 3.3 for the next-best method (SatDiFuser).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO-style contrastive self-distillation framework (Caron et al., 2021) with a coding-rate regularizer (Wu et al., 2025) to the multispectral EO domain, and introduces a second, frozen optical VFM teacher (DINOv3) for cross-modal knowledge transfer. It departs from the dominant MIM-based EO pretraining paradigm (Scale-MAE, SatMAE, Copernicus-FM) by arguing that objective-level alignment between student and teacher—both using contrastive self-distillation—yields more coherent feature-space transfer than coupling MIM reconstruction with VFM distillation, as attempted in Copernicus-FM. The dual-teacher architecture (EMA-updated MS teacher + frozen optical VFM teacher) is a novel combination in EO, and the use of a Swin backbone with patch size 4 to capture fine-grained features while distilling from a ViT-based VFM is a practical architectural choice not explored in prior EO distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a model pretrained on only 0.5M images can outperform DINOv3 (1.689B images) and all prior EOFMs across optical and multispectral benchmarks, suggesting that distillation-centric pretraining is a far more data-efficient path to strong EO representations than scaling MIM pretraining. The objective-alignment insight (contrastive student + contrastive teacher > MIM student + contrastive teacher) is broadly applicable to any domain where a strong general-purpose VFM exists but the target modality requires additional spectral or sensor-specific information. Limitations include dependence on the optical VFM's quality, an assumption of spatial alignment between modalities, and no extension to non-optical sensors like SAR; the authors note that sensor and temporal alignment remain open challenges for a truly unified EO foundation model.
