## Problem

LVLMs systematically fail on counterfactual inputs where visual evidence contradicts commonsense world knowledge (e.g., a five-legged llama), defaulting to their learned priors rather than the actual image content. Existing hallucination mitigation methods target general object hallucination but do not specifically localize or override the counterfactual elements that cause the model to ignore visual evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261677/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a standard RGB image and a text question as input and produces a free-form text response, requiring no additional training or model modification. Internally, it consumes the image twice through the LVLM's vision encoder (once as-is, once with counterfactual tokens replaced by pad tokens) and additionally invokes a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 model to synthesize a prior-consistent visual impression and a pixel-wise uncertainty map. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, operating as a decoding-time wrapper around any LVLM.

## Method

EnAR operates in three stages. In Envision, the input image is inverted into the latent space of a diffusion model via DDIM, then perturbed using annealed Langevin dynamics guided by the Tweedie gradient estimator to produce a 'visual impression' that corrects implausible regions toward the learned visual prior; K stochastic perturbations yield a pixel-wise uncertainty map. In Attend, both the original and impression images pass through the LVLM's vision encoder, and the element-wise absolute difference of their 6th-layer attention maps localizes counterfactual tokens, which are unioned with the top-5% uncertainty positions and replaced by pad tokens to form a second visual input stream. In Respond, contrastive decoding (following VCD's formulation) is applied between the original and padded inputs, amplifying tokens supported by the true visual content while suppressing those driven by the masked counterfactual regions.

## Results

On the counterfactual hallucination benchmark VLMBias, EnAR achieves 31.36% overall accuracy on InternVL3.5-8B (vs. 19.83% for the regular baseline), 28.02% on Qwen2.5VL-7B (vs. 22.63%), and 22.20% on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (vs. 16.92%), representing a 10.82% average gain. On the general hallucination benchmark POPE, EnAR achieves the best F1 score across all three difficulty levels and all three backbones, e.g., 88.9% F1 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (Random) vs. 80.8% for the regular baseline. On WHOOPS, EnAR elevates overall accuracy from 62.45% to 74.15% on InternVL3.5-8B and from 49.34% to 62.30% on Qwen2.5VL-7B. On HallusionBench, EnAR improves the average score by 1.08% (InternVL), 1.16% (Qwen2.5VL), and 3.59% (LLaVA) over the regular baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EnAR consistently outperforms all five decoding-level baselines (VCD, M3ID, RITUAL, DeGF, AGLA) across every benchmark and all three LVLM backbones. On VLMBias (InternVL3.5-8B), EnAR reaches 31.36% overall accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline VCD (23.76%) by 7.6 points and M3ID (20.11%) by 11.3 points; the gap is even larger on the Logos category (34.78% vs. 7.86% for VCD). On POPE (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Random), EnAR achieves 88.9% F1, beating VCD (86.6%), DeGF (87.5%), and AGLA (87.7%) by 2.3–2.4 points. On WHOOPS (InternVL3.5-8B), EnAR's 74.15% average exceeds AGLA (72.09%) and VCD (69.45%) by 2.1 and 4.7 points respectively, with the largest margin on the Symbolic subcategory (79.78% vs. 75.84% for AGLA). Notably, M3ID and RITUAL show near-zero or negative gains on VLMBias for InternVL3.5-8B (20.11% and 18.82% vs. 19.83% regular), indicating they fail to address counterfactual hallucination, whereas EnAR delivers substantial improvements in all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

EnAR builds directly on VCD's contrastive decoding formulation for its Respond stage, but departs from the broader hallucination-mitigation literature (VCD, M3ID, RITUAL, DeGF, AGLA) by targeting the specific sub-problem of counterfactual hallucination, where visual evidence contradicts the LLM's accumulated world knowledge. The key conceptual departure is the Envision stage: rather than perturbing the input with noise (VCD) or random transformations (RITUAL), EnAR uses a pre-trained diffusion model with DDIM inversion and annealed Langevin dynamics to generate a 'visual impression'—a prior-consistent counterpart of the input—along with a pixel-wise uncertainty map. The Attend stage then localizes counterfactual elements via attention contrast between the original and impression images, a mechanism inspired by human predictive coding and visual expectation literature, which no prior decoding-level method explicitly exploits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Counterfactual hallucination is a critical reliability failure in safety-critical LVLM deployments (autonomous driving, medical imaging), yet existing decoding-level methods are shown to be largely ineffective in this setting. EnAR's training-free, model-agnostic design (validated across InternVL3.5, Qwen2.5VL, and LLaVA-v1.5) makes it immediately deployable without retraining, and the 10.82% gain on VLMBias demonstrates that explicitly grounding attention in visual priors can overcome the LLM's prior dominance. Open questions include the computational overhead of running a diffusion model with K perturbations per inference, whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or spatially distributed counterfactuals, and how the method interacts with increasingly strong LVLMs that may already partially resist prior bias.
