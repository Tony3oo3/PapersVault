## Problem

Existing animal video datasets rely on passive observation of spontaneous behavior, capturing only coarse actions (walking, standing) while missing fine-grained welfare-relevant behaviors and the structured context needed for behavioral profiling. They lack controlled experimental protocols that systematically probe specific behavioral dimensions such as exploratory motivation, neophobia, and social competence, making it impossible to disentangle individual differences from situational factors or to study consistent behavioral patterns across repeated testing contexts.

## Input / Output

The primary input is synchronized multi-camera video from a 60 m² standardized arena, with individual test sessions spanning 1200–1500 seconds and each subject completing 5–7 scenarios for a total of 20–25 minutes of footage. Pose-based benchmarks consume time-series of 39 anatomical keypoint coordinates per frame rather than raw pixels, isolating kinematic information from appearance. Outputs are structured at three levels: dense per-frame labels over 23 behavior classes for temporal segmentation, discrete behavior class predictions from skeleton trajectories, and a 3-class rearing group label (full/half/none maternal contact) inferred from behavioral patterns recorded 9 months post-treatment.

## Method

MooCap is built around ethologically validated behavioral assays in which each of 43 Holstein heifers undergoes a standardized sequence of seven controlled stimuli (novel environment, novel object, human approach, unfamiliar conspecific restricted/unrestricted, and dam reunion restricted/unrestricted), with all subjects tested in the same fixed order and duration to ensure comparability. Annotation uses BORIS software with inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa > 0.8) to produce 23 fine-grained behavior labels, 39 anatomical keypoints per frame, and 4-quadrant spatial occupancy, synchronized across multiple elevated camera views. The longitudinal design assigns calves to three rearing treatments (full-time, half-time, or no maternal contact) from 48 hours to 10 weeks of age, with behavioral recordings collected 9 months post-weaning, enabling inference of latent phenotypic traits from observable behavior. Three benchmarks are established: temporal action segmentation over long sequences (evaluating FACT, LTContext, DiffAct, and unsupervised TSA-ActionSeg), skeleton-based behavior recognition using GCN architectures (MS-G3D, AMGCN, 2S-AGCN), and longitudinal phenotyping with video Transformers (UniFormer, TimeSformer, ViViT, Video Swin).

## Results

On the primary temporal action segmentation benchmark (23 behavior classes, 1200–1500 s sequences), the best supervised model FACT achieves 66.39% frame accuracy (MoF) and 30.57% F1@0.50, while the best unsupervised method (TSA-ActionSeg with FINCH clustering) reaches only 14.73% MoF. For skeleton-based behavior recognition, MS-G3D attains the highest mean F1 of 0.39 across seven action classes. On the longitudinal phenotyping task, UniFormer achieves 84.23% mean accuracy in classifying early-life rearing groups from video, substantially outperforming TimeSformer (45.50%), ViViT (38.74%), and Video Swin Transformer (29.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACT [25] dominates all other supervised segmentation models, outperforming LTContext [2] by 17.5 points in MoF (66.39 vs. 48.87) and nearly doubling its F1@0.50 (30.57 vs. 17.81); DiffAct [22], ASFormer [43], SSTDA [11], MS-TCN++ [20], and UVAST [4] all fall below 36% MoF, indicating that diffusion-based and earlier Transformer/TCN architectures struggle with the extreme temporal extent and class imbalance of MooCap. The unsupervised TSA-ActionSeg [7] baseline (FINCH clustering, 14.73% MoF) trails the best supervised method by over 51 points, underscoring the difficulty of discovering behavioral structure without labels. In skeleton-based recognition, MS-G3D [23] (mean F1 0.39) edges out AMGCN [42] (0.36) and clearly surpasses 2S-AGCN [35] (0.26), with MS-G3D strongest on repetitive actions (grooming 0.51, playful 0.52, push 0.53) while AMGCN excels on threat detection (0.50). For longitudinal phenotyping, UniFormer [19] (84.23% mean accuracy) far outperforms TimeSformer [6] (45.50%), ViViT [1] (38.74%), and Video Swin Transformer [24] (29.72%), though TimeSformer anomalously reaches 96.67% on dam-reunion scenarios, suggesting scenario-specific rather than generalizable phenotypic learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MooCap carves new ground by bridging two extremes in animal behavior datasets: large-scale passive 'in-the-wild' benchmarks (MammalNet, Animal Kingdom) that lack controlled experimental structure, and small-scale laboratory pose-tracking studies (DeepLabCut-based) that lack scalability. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes ethological assay design—standardized stimuli probing neophobia, social competence, and human-animal relationship—within a multi-view video annotation framework. The annotation pipeline draws on BORIS for behavioral coding and DeepLabCut-style keypoint protocols, but the combination of dense temporal action labels, 39-keypoint pose, spatial occupancy, and longitudinal rearing-history labels into a single benchmark is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a genuine gap: passive footage cannot reveal how individuals respond to specific stimuli, while lab studies don't scale to video-level benchmarks. The longitudinal phenotyping task (predicting early-life rearing group from adult behavior nine months later) is a new formulation with direct implications for welfare diagnostics and behavioral genomics. Key limitations include single breed (Holstein), single facility, N=43 subjects, and fixed camera positions, which constrain generalizability. Open questions include whether the 84% UniFormer accuracy on longitudinal classification reflects a real phenotypic signal or scenario-specific shortcuts, and whether the 66% frame-accuracy ceiling on temporal segmentation reflects fundamental difficulty or insufficient model capacity for 20-minute non-rigid animal sequences.
