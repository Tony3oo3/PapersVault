## Problem

Open-set object detection struggles when text representations fail to capture complex or domain-specific visual concepts, and when image-text pairs for rare categories are scarce. Existing visual-prompted detectors (e.g., T-Rex2, CP-DETR) require tightly coupled multi-modal architectures and multi-stage training, prolonging development cycles, while effective training strategies for data-driven open-set detectors remain largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260635/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector accepts an RGB image plus either a text prompt (concatenated category names) or a visual prompt (bounding box coordinates of exemplar instances). Visual prompts come in two forms: instance-level boxes for interactive detection (Exemplar-Guided Route) or aggregated embeddings across multiple images for generic category recognition (Global-Concept Route). The model outputs bounding boxes with category labels, supporting both prompt types simultaneously within a single architecture.

## Method

PET-DINO extends Grounding DINO with an Alignment-Friendly Visual Prompt Generation (AFVPG) module that extracts visual prompt embeddings from enhanced image features (post deformable self-attention and FFN) rather than raw backbone outputs, and shares parameters with the text prompt branch to reduce development cost. Two prompt-enriched training strategies simulate real-world prompting scenarios in parallel: Intra-Batch Parallel Prompting (IBP) uses visual prompts from other images in the same batch as cross-image and class-level global prompts for the current image, while Dynamic Memory-Driven Prompting (DMD) maintains a FIFO Visual Cues Bank that dynamically accumulates and aggregates historical prompt embeddings across training iterations. The inheritance-based design initializes from a pre-trained text-prompted detector (MM-Grounding-DINO), freezing the backbone and only updating visual-prompt-related modules, which the ablations show yields a higher performance ceiling than training from scratch.

## Results

Under the Visual-I (interactive) protocol, PET-DINO with Swin-T and only 0.6M training samples (Object365) achieves 64.0 AP on COCO, surpassing CP-DETR-T (61.8 AP) by 2.2 AP while using 5.5× less data. With the full 2.5M training set, PET-DINO (Swin-T) reaches 48.3 AP on ODinW35, a +7.3 AP improvement over CP-DETR-T. The Swin-L variant with 2.5M data attains 65.8 AP on LVIS minival and 49.7 AP on ODinW35, establishing state-of-the-art zero-shot interactive detection. Under the Visual-G (generic) protocol, PET-DINO (Swin-L, 0.6M) achieves 46.7 AP on COCO, outperforming T-Rex2 (46.5 AP) with substantially less training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CP-DETR-T (Swin-T, 3.3M data), PET-DINO (Swin-T, 0.6M) wins on COCO by +2.2 AP (64.0 vs 61.8) with 5.5× less training data, and with 2.5M data wins on ODinW35 by +7.3 AP (48.3 vs 41.0). Against T-Rex2 (Swin-T, 3.1M), PET-DINO achieves large margins: +7.4 AP on COCO (64.0 vs 56.6) and +10.6 AP on ODinW35 (48.3 vs 37.7) with the 2.5M configuration. For the Swin-L backbone, PET-DINO (2.5M) trails CP-DETR-L* on COCO (66.5 vs 68.4) and LVIS (65.8 vs 71.6), but CP-DETR-L* is not truly zero-shot as it was pre-trained on the evaluation dataset with text prompts. In the Visual-G (generic) protocol, PET-DINO (Swin-T, 0.6M) edges out T-Rex2 on COCO (40.3 vs 38.8) but falls short on LVIS (29.6 vs 37.4), which the authors attribute to T-Rex2's use of CLIP and the SA-1B dataset for broader long-tail coverage. In in-domain COCO evaluation (Table 4), PET-DINO (Swin-L, Visual-G) achieves 56.3 AP, surpassing DINOv (54.2 AP) by +2.1 AP and approaching OpenSeed (58.2 AP) within 1.9 AP.

## Relation to Prior Work

PET-DINO extends the Grounding DINO / MM-Grounding-DINO text-prompted open-set detection line by adding a visual prompt route through the Alignment-Friendly Visual Prompt Generation (AFVPG) module, which shares parameters with the text branch rather than introducing a separate visual encoder as in T-Rex2 or CP-DETR. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing design philosophy of tightly coupled multi-modal architectures with multi-stage training (T-Rex2, CP-DETR, YOLOE) in favor of an inheritance-based strategy: start from a strong text-prompted detector and bolt on visual prompting with targeted training. The two proposed training strategies—Intra-Batch Parallel Prompting and Dynamic Memory-Driven Prompting—are positioned as the first large-scale training schemes specifically designed for dual-modality (text + visual) prompted detectors, addressing the limited prompt diversity inherent to single-image visual prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the development cycle and data cost for building universal detectors: PET-DINO matches or exceeds T-Rex2 and CP-DETR with roughly 5× less training data (0.6M vs. 3.1–3.3M samples) and without requiring a VLM (CLIP) for supervision. The inheritance-based philosophy suggests a reusable recipe—any strong text-prompted detector can be upgraded with visual prompting via AFVPG plus the proposed training strategies—lowering the barrier for practitioners. However, the Visual-G (global-concept) performance on LVIS still lags behind T-Rex2, likely due to the inherited model's weaker long-tail coverage and the absence of CLIP's open-vocabulary prior, leaving open the question of whether purely visual-prompt-based global recognition can fully close the gap with VLM-augmented methods at scale.
