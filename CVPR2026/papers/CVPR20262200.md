## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces millions of unordered Gaussians, making level-of-detail construction and model compression highly challenging. Existing LoD methods either modify the 3DGS formulation (breaking compatibility with the standard pipeline) or rely on manually predefined hierarchical partitions with multi-stage training, which limits scalability, degrades rendering quality, and roughly doubles training time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard multi-view RGB images with camera poses and produces a 3DGS model augmented with a per-Gaussian hierarchy feature (a 2D vector mapped to a continuous scalar H^exp in [0, 10]). At inference, a user-specified splat percentage selects the top-k Gaussians by ascending H^exp (smaller = more important), yielding a rendered novel-view image from that subset. The hierarchy is continuous and differentiable, so any arbitrary Gaussian count can be selected without retraining.

## Method

Each Gaussian receives a lightweight 2D unconstrained hierarchy feature (H^feat) mapped to a bounded continuous value H^exp via softmax-weighted expectation, jointly optimized with all other Gaussian attributes. During training, a Differentiable Decreasing Step Function (sigmoid with β=10) modulates each Gaussian's opacity, partitioning them into saturated (m≈1), active (0<m<1), and cutoff (m≈0) regions; the narrow active region amplifies gradients on H^exp in proportion to each Gaussian's rendering contribution, naturally ordering them by importance. A PDF-Guided Active-Region Sampling strategy shifts the activation center μ across iterations by sampling from the empirical 50-bin histogram of H^exp, ensuring comprehensive coverage and encouraging the hierarchy distribution to approach uniformity. A hierarchy balance loss (weighted squared distance from H_max) counteracts the natural drift of all H^exp values toward H_min. DDSF and full rendering share the same CUDA rasterization pipeline in parallel, adding only ~10% training overhead over standard 3DGS-MCMC.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360 at 25% splats, the method achieves 28.35 PSNR / 0.86 SSIM / 0.13 LPIPS, outperforming CLOD (28.26/0.87/0.14) and LapisGS (28.01/0.86/0.15), while 3DGS-MCMC drops to 18.18 PSNR. At 100% splats, it reaches 29.74 PSNR / 0.90 SSIM / 0.10 LPIPS, marginally surpassing 3DGS-MCMC (29.73/0.89/0.11) and clearly beating CLOD (29.59/0.89/0.12). Training time is 26:41 min on Mip-NeRF360, only ~10% more than 3DGS-MCMC (23:53) but roughly half of CLOD (59:34) and one-third of LapisGS (78:20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-MCMC (the base model without hierarchy), the proposed method dramatically outperforms at low splat ratios—e.g., 28.35 vs. 18.18 PSNR at 25% on Mip-NeRF360—while at 100% it matches or slightly exceeds the baseline (29.74 vs. 29.73 PSNR), confirming that the hierarchy feature does not degrade full-model quality. Against PRoGS (post-hoc reordering), the method wins at every splat percentage across all three datasets, with the largest gap at 25% (28.35 vs. 25.28 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360). Against LapisGS (bottom-up multi-stage), the method is superior at 25% and 50% splats on most metrics and trains in roughly one-third the time (26:41 vs. 78:20 min). The closest competitor is CLOD (top-down continuous LoD): the two methods are within 0.1–0.5 dB PSNR at most settings, but the proposed method wins at 25% on Mip-NeRF360 (28.35 vs. 28.26), at 50% on Tanks & Temples (24.20 vs. 24.01), and at 100% on Mip-NeRF360 (29.74 vs. 29.59), while also training in less than half the time (26:41 vs. 59:34 min). CLOD's 100% performance drop is attributed to catastrophic forgetting from its second-stage subset training, whereas the single-stage DDSF approach preserves full-model fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-MCMC framework by adding a learned, continuous hierarchy feature to each Gaussian, departing from prior LoD approaches that rely on multi-stage training (LapisGS, CLOD), manual partitioning (FLoD, LODGE), or post-hoc reordering (PRoGS). The key conceptual departure is the Differentiable Decreasing Step Function (DDSF) rendering, which modulates Gaussian opacity during training to simultaneously approximate subset rendering and produce hierarchy-proportional gradients — a mechanism absent from prior single-stage or multi-stage methods. The PDF-Guided Active-Region Sampling further distinguishes the work by adaptively shifting the modulation window to cover high-density regions of the hierarchy distribution, addressing a practical training imbalance that fixed-threshold or insertion-order methods (e.g., CLOD) cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive LoD and pruning quality with only ~10% additional training time over vanilla 3DGS-MCMC, compared to 2×+ for multi-stage baselines, making hierarchical 3DGS feasible in resource-constrained pipelines. An open question is whether the learned hierarchy generalizes well to unseen viewpoints or dynamic scenes, since the ranking is optimized against a fixed training set of views. The paper also leaves open the question of how the approach scales to very large scenes (millions of Gaussians) where the PDF histogram computation and parallel rasterization overhead may become non-trivial.
