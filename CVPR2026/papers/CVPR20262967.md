## Problem

Adapting SAM's 2D masks to online 3D instance segmentation suffers from two compounding failures: part-level masks shatter into spatially disconnected fragments under occlusion, and conventional IoU-based tracking cannot maintain identity when new detections have sparse geometry while accumulated instances have rich 3D structure. Existing methods patch these issues with hand-crafted geometric heuristics and uniform matching rules, causing persistent identity drift. The paper reframes online segmentation as a learnable composition problem where both spatial grouping and temporal association are optimized end-to-end.

## Input / Output

At each timestamp the method consumes a streaming RGB-D observation (RGB image, depth map, camera pose) together with a persistent two-tier scene memory (a fixed-budget FIFO short-term buffer of 50 instances plus an unbounded long-term store). It outputs per-frame 3D instance masks, bounding boxes, semantic class labels, and objectness scores, and updates the scene memory via exponential moving average for features/boxes and spatial union for masks. The SAM-derived 2D masks are treated as intermediate 'mosaic tiles' rather than final instance units, and the entire pipeline runs at 11.2 FPS with FastSAM.

## Method

SAMosaic3D is a dual-level, query-based differentiable framework. Spatially, Fragment-to-Instance Adaptive Assembly predicts the instance count and a center bank from a global scene descriptor, computes a soft assignment matrix via softmax over squared fragment-center distances, derives a co-instance affinity matrix W, and refines fragment features through an L-layer decoder with soft-gated attention that biases attention logits by β·log(W+ε) to suppress inter-instance interference; layer-wise predictions are supervised with assignment-weighted bipartite matching losses. Temporally, Instance-to-Scene Online Merging partitions memory into short-term (learnable spatial-weighted cross-attention for semantic association) and long-term (IoU-based geometric verification) tiers, cascading the two stages so that semantic context from recent observations informs re-identification of long-term instances after occlusion. Training is progressive (spatial module first, then temporal), with an explicit BCE merge-association loss that directly supervises the soft merging matrix toward ground-truth instance correspondences, preventing the attention from learning spurious spatial-proximity shortcuts.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (class-agnostic, 200+ categories), SAMosaic3D with SAM achieves 46.1 AP / 68.5 AP₅₀ / 84.2 AP₂₅, and with FastSAM reaches 48.7 AP / 69.3 AP₅₀ / 85.4 AP₂₅ at 11.2 FPS. On ScanNet (20 categories), it attains 45.3 AP (SAM) and 46.5 AP (FastSAM), surpassing the strongest online baseline AutoSeg3D by +1.9 AP. Zero-shot cross-dataset transfer from ScanNet200 to SceneNN yields 31.7 AP (SAM) / 32.4 AP (FastSAM), and to 3RScan (fast-moving cameras) yields 16.3 AP / 17.0 AP, establishing state-of-the-art among online methods in all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMosaic3D outperforms all online baselines on ScanNet200: it beats AutoSeg3D (SAM) by +0.6 AP and AutoSeg3D-E (FastSAM) by +2.5 AP, with the larger FastSAM gap reflecting the framework's VFM-agnostic design. Against ESAM/ESAM-E, gains are more substantial (+3.9 AP with SAM, +5.3 AP with FastSAM), indicating that learned fragment assembly and cascaded merging substantially exceed the Hungarian-matching + superpoint-clustering pipeline. On ScanNet (20-class), SAMosaic3D leads AutoSeg3D by +1.9 AP and ESAM-E+FF by +2.7 AP. In zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to SceneNN, it surpasses AutoSeg3D-E by +1.5 AP and ESAM by +2.9 AP; on the motion-heavy 3RScan benchmark it is within 0.5 AP of AutoSeg3D-E (16.3 vs 16.8) while exceeding ESAM by +2.2 AP. Offline methods (SAMPro3D, SAI3D) remain far behind, confirming that the online streaming paradigm with SAM-based lifting is now competitive with or superior to full-reconstruction approaches for instance-level accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAMosaic3D builds directly on SAM/FastSAM as a 2D mask provider and on the 3D sparse U-Net backbone for point-cloud feature encoding, but departs conceptually from prior online SAM-based methods (SAM3D, ESAM, AutoSeg3D) that treat lifted 2D masks as final instance units and rely on geometric clustering or IoU-based tracking for temporal association. The key reframing is from 'correction' (patching over-segmentation with hand-crafted heuristics) to 'composition' (end-to-end differentiable assembly of fragments via soft-gated attention and cascaded semantic-geometric merging). The temporal merging stage borrows spatial-weighted cross-attention from La-MoTR [37] for learned association, while the two-tier FIFO memory architecture is a novel practical design not seen in prior online 3D segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck for embodied perception: SAM's part-level over-segmentation combined with fragile IoU-based tracking causes persistent identity drift in streaming 3D scenes, and the proposed differentiable assembly pipeline offers a principled, learnable alternative. The 11.2 FPS real-time performance with FastSAM makes the approach practically deployable for AR/robotics. However, the framework remains limited to static indoor scenes (no independently moving objects), lacks open-vocabulary semantics, and the performance gains over the strongest baseline (AutoSeg3D) are modest (+1.5 to +2.5 AP), leaving open whether the composition paradigm scales to outdoor or dynamic-object settings.
