## Problem

3D self-supervised learning for indoor scene understanding is fundamentally bottlenecked by the scarcity of real 3D scans — the largest widely used indoor dataset contains only ~5k scenes, far below the billions of unlabeled images available in 2D vision. This paper asks whether unlabeled room-walkthrough videos, which are abundant on the web, can be converted into a scalable pre-training signal for 3D representation learning without any real 3D sensors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263330/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 9D point cloud features (3D coordinates, RGB colors, and surface normals) from video-generated point clouds (VGPC) reconstructed by the feed-forward model π³ from room-tour video sequences. Each scene is processed as a set of points with a kNN graph built on top; during pre-training, the student sees local crops or masked global views while the teacher sees full global views of the same scene. Downstream, the learned point-wise embeddings are frozen (linear probing) or fine-tuned to produce per-point semantic or instance segmentation masks on ScanNet, ScanNet200, ScanNet++, and S3DIS.

## Method

LAM3C extends Sonata's Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering objective with a teacher-student Point Transformer V3 backbone and two noise-regularized losses tailored to imperfect VGPC. The Laplacian smoothing loss builds a kNN graph per scene with distance-based edge weights (w_ij = exp(−‖p_i−p_j‖²/σ²), σ set to the median kNN distance) and applies a Huber penalty to encourage spatially adjacent points to share similar embeddings, while down-weighting noisy outliers. The noise consistency loss enforces that corresponding points in two differently augmented views of the same scene produce identical embeddings under the EMA teacher and student, stabilizing global representations despite reconstruction noise. The RoomTours dataset (49,219 scenes) is constructed by collecting room-tour videos from YouTube and existing datasets, segmenting them into indoor scene sequences via CLIP zero-shot classification, reconstructing VGPC with π³, and aligning each scene to a Z-up coordinate frame with scale normalized to the ScanNet distribution.

## Results

On ScanNet semantic segmentation (Full-FT, mIoU), LAM3C-49k with PTv3-Base achieves 77.7%, and LAM3C*-49k with PTv3-Large reaches 79.5%, matching or exceeding Sonata trained on 15k real 3D scans (78.5%) without using any real 3D data. On S3DIS, LAM3C* achieves 75.5% Full-FT mIoU vs. 75.2% for Sonata (all real). For instance segmentation, LAM3C* attains 47.2% mAP on S3DIS, surpassing Sonata (all real) at 41.5%, while the full Sonata setup (18k real + 121k synthetic) reaches 45.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Sonata [53], the state-of-the-art 3D-SSL method extending DINOv2 to point clouds. LAM3C-49k (PTv3-Base, 49k VGPC) is competitive with Sonata trained on 15k real scans: it trails by 2–4 mIoU points on linear probing (e.g., 66.0 vs. 69.4 on ScanNet LP) but is within 1 point on full fine-tuning (77.7 vs. 78.5). With the larger PTv3-Large backbone (LAM3C*), the gap closes or reverses: LAM3C* matches Sonata (all real) on ScanNet LP (69.5 vs. 69.4) and slightly exceeds it on ScanNet Full-FT (79.5 vs. 78.5) and S3DIS Full-FT (75.5 vs. 75.2). Against the full Sonata setup (18k real + 121k synthetic), LAM3C* remains within 1–3 points on most benchmarks, a notable result given it uses zero real 3D scans. On instance segmentation, LAM3C* clearly outperforms Sonata (all real) on S3DIS (47.2 vs. 41.5 mAP) but is slightly behind Sonata (full) on ScanNet (41.7 vs. 42.4 mAP). MSC [50] and PPT [52] are substantially weaker baselines, with MSC achieving only 78.2% ScanNet Full-FT mIoU and PPT (supervised) reaching 78.6%, both below LAM3C*.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Sonata/DINOv2 line of Sinkhorn-Knopp clustering for 3D point clouds by introducing two noise-regularized losses (Laplacian smoothing and noise consistency) to handle the imperfections inherent in video-generated point clouds. Its key conceptual departure from prior 3D-SSL work (PointContrast, MSC, Sonata) is the data source: instead of relying on expensive real 3D scans or synthetic renders, it reconstructs point clouds from unlabeled web videos using feed-forward models like π³. The RoomTours dataset (49k scenes) is positioned as a scalable alternative to the ~5k-scene ceiling of existing indoor 3D datasets, while the method itself remains a teacher-student clustering framework with PTv3 backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in 3D-SSL by demonstrating that unlabeled videos—a virtually unlimited resource—can substitute for real 3D scans in pre-training, potentially unscaling the field. However, the best results (matching Sonata) require PTv3-Large with 437k steps, substantially more compute than the base-model comparisons, and the base-model results remain slightly below Sonata in most settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes, whether the noise-regularized losses transfer to other reconstruction models or data regimes, and whether the modest global-structure degradation observed in PCA visualizations limits downstream tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning.
