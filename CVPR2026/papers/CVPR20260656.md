## Problem

Existing medical video diagnosis methods map visual progressions directly to diagnostic outputs without modeling the two hallmarks of expert clinical reasoning: clinically grounded diagnostic principles and hypothesis-driven counterfactual thinking. This causes models to conflate causal pathological cues with non-pathological variations (illumination shifts, reagent color changes, camera motion), severely limiting reliability in data-scarce clinical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260656/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes medical video sequences (colposcopy: four-stage reagent videos; colonoscopy: endoscopic videos) resized to 256×256, processed as overlapping clips of length L with temporal stride. For colposcopy, output is a multi-label probability vector over fixed clockface biopsy positions; for colonoscopy, snippet-level scores (32 snippets of 16 frames per video) are propagated to frame-level polyp detection. During inference, the counterfactual generator synthesizes a benign-variant of the current frame from the preceding frame, enabling differential reasoning between factual and hypothetical tissue appearances.

## Method

MEDVCR comprises three components: (1) a Counterfactual Generator (CG), a conditional diffusion model (U-Net, K=1000 steps) pretrained to synthesize next-stage tissue frames conditioned on a specified health state (benign/malignant) and a reference frame; (2) a Counterfactual Representation Learning (CRL) module using an I3D visual encoder and temporal Transformer, regularized by three clinical rules—temporal consistency (contrastive loss across stages), pathological separability (soft separation of benign/malignant features), and counterfactual alignment (triplet loss pulling factual features toward pathology-consistent counterfactuals and away from incompatible ones); (3) a Dual Diagnostic Prediction (DDP) strategy that fuses video-level temporal logits with frame-level counterfactual contrast (factual logit minus opposite-hypothesis logit) through a sigmoid head. The generator is pretrained first, then the learner and diagnostic head are jointly optimized with a hybrid loss combining diffusion reconstruction, the three clinical-rule losses, and binary cross-entropy.

## Results

On the in-house colposcopy dataset (fully supervised biopsy-site localization), MEDVCR achieves 93.0% Recall@1, 80.3% Recall, 74.4% Precision, and 55.0% Accuracy, surpassing the best prior method (SurgFormer, 82.8% Recall@1) by 10.2%. On the combined HyperKvasir + LD-PolypVideo colonoscopy benchmark (weakly supervised polyp-frame detection), MEDVCR attains 94.8% AP and 99.6% AUC, exceeding the strongest baseline (TEmory, 92.2% AP) by 2.6%. All results are averaged over five-fold cross-validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEDVCR outperforms all 17 baselines across both tasks. On colposcopy, general video models (TimeSformer 70.4%, VideoSwin 75.2%, VideoMAEv2 77.6% Recall@1) reveal a substantial domain gap, while medical-domain models (SurgFormer 82.8%, STDDNet 82.3%, SurgVLP 82.0%) narrow but do not close the gap; MEDVCR's 93.0% Recall@1 exceeds the best medical baseline by 10.2%. On colonoscopy, weakly supervised anomaly-detection baselines from natural video (GCN-Ano 75.4%, CLAWS 80.4%, RTFM 78.0% AP) are clearly inferior to medical-specific methods (CTMIL 86.6%, Endo-FM 89.2%, TEmory 92.2% AP), and MEDVCR's 94.8% AP surpasses the strongest prior (TEmory) by 2.6%. Notably, the ultrasound foundation model EchoPrime [64] underperforms in both settings (80.2% Recall@1 on colposcopy, 75.7% AP on colonoscopy), suggesting that cross-modality transfer from ultrasound to endoscopic/gynecological video is limited. The consistent margin over both general and medical baselines supports the paper's claim that counterfactual reasoning and clinical-rule regularization provide gains beyond what stronger backbones or larger pretraining alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of medical video understanding and counterfactual reasoning, two literatures that have developed largely independently. On the medical video side, it builds on spatiotemporal encoders (I3D, SlowFast, ViViT) and weakly supervised detection frameworks (CTMIL, TEmory) but departs from them by arguing that pixel-level temporal modeling conflates causal pathological cues with non-pathological variations. On the counterfactual reasoning side, it repurposes DDPM-style diffusion models—previously applied to image attribute editing or causal inference in natural scenes—for synthesizing pathology-conditioned tissue transitions across examination stages, and introduces clinical rules (temporal consistency, pathological separability, counterfactual alignment) as explicit representation constraints that have no direct analogue in prior counterfactual generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in medical video diagnosis: existing methods lack the hypothesis-driven, differential-reasoning process that clinicians actually employ, which is especially critical in data-scarce clinical settings. The +10.2% Recall@1 gain on colposcopy is substantial, though it is measured on a small in-house dataset (623 cases) against cross-domain baselines rather than colposcopy-specific methods, tempering the strength of the comparison. Open questions include whether the binary benign/malignant counterfactual formulation generalizes to multi-class or multi-stage pathological taxonomies, whether the pretrained diffusion generator can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for real-time clinical deployment, and whether the hand-crafted clinical rules can be learned or discovered automatically from data.
