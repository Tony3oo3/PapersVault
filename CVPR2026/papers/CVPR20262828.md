## Problem

Existing RGB-Event object detectors perform undifferentiated feature-level fusion of both modalities, failing to explicitly disentangle shared semantic features from modality-private features (e.g., event motion cues in low-light, RGB texture for static objects). This uniform treatment dilutes the distinctive strengths each modality provides under challenging conditions, limiting detection accuracy in scenarios where one modality is degraded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262828/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a synchronized RGB frame (1440×1080 on DSEC-Det, 346×260 on PKU-DAVIS-SOD) and a corresponding event image (640×480 on DSEC-Det, 346×260 on PKU-DAVIS-SOD) as input. Event streams are converted to three-channel frames representing positive polarity, negative polarity, and their sum. The network outputs K bounding boxes (x, y, w, h) with category labels via a DETR-style bipartite matching mechanism, evaluated under the standard COCO protocol (mAP, mAP₅₀, mAP₇₅).

## Method

SPFD follows a DETR-style architecture (ResNet-50 backbone, 100k iterations) with three key components. The FCFS module applies 2D FFT to both modality features, then uses spectral coherence γ² and a strength-balance term η to compute a shared mask and two private masks in the frequency domain, which are applied element-wise before inverse FFT to recover spatial-domain shared (z_s), RGB-private (z_r), and Event-private (z_e) features. The TriAdapt Encoder uses multi-scale deformable attention with shared features as queries and private features as values, combined with a sigmoid gating map that spatially and channel-wise controls the RGB-vs-Event fusion ratio per encoder layer. The TriInject Decoder injects projected private features into each cross-attention layer via learnable per-layer scaling coefficients γ_r and γ_e, enabling hierarchical specialization where different decoder depths prioritize spatial texture or temporal motion cues.

## Results

SPFD achieves 34.6% mAP and 56.7% mAP50 on DSEC-Det, and 32.0% mAP and 62.4% mAP50 on PKU-DAVIS-SOD, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (SFNet) by 4.2% and 0.1% in mAP, and by 5.3% and 2.8% in mAP50 on the two datasets respectively. The model uses 78.6M parameters with a ResNet-50 backbone. Ablation studies confirm that each component contributes: FCFS adds +0.6% mAP, TriAdapt Encoder adds +1.1%, and TriInject Decoder adds a further +0.2% mAP and +0.75% mAP75 over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPFD outperforms all 14 baselines on both datasets. On DSEC-Det, the closest competitor is SFNet (30.4% mAP, 51.4% mAP50); SPFD improves by 4.2% mAP and 5.3% mAP50, a substantial margin. On PKU-DAVIS-SOD, SPFD edges out SFNet by 0.1% mAP (32.0 vs. 31.9) and 2.8% mAP50 (62.4 vs. 59.6). Among RGB-Event methods, SPFD also surpasses CAFR (12.5% mAP on DSEC-Det) and SODFormer (20.7% mAP on PKU-DAVIS-SOD) by large margins, indicating that explicit shared/private feature decoupling is far more effective than uniform cross-modal fusion. Compared to single-modal baselines, SPFD exceeds the best RGB-only method (YOLOv11: 30.3% mAP on DSEC-Det) by 4.3% and the best Event-only method (SMamba: 14.8% mAP) by 19.8%, confirming the value of the multimodal design. Notably, SPFD achieves these gains with 78.6M parameters, comparable to SODFormer (82.0M) and CAFR (82.4M), while using a lighter backbone (ResNet-50 vs. CSPDarknet53 for SFNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DETR-style detection paradigm (DETR, Deformable DETR) and uses MI-DETR as its single-modal baseline, extending it to the RGB-Event multimodal setting. Its key departure from prior RGB-Event fusion methods (SODFormer, SFNet, CAFR, FPN-Fusion) is the explicit frequency-domain disentanglement of shared versus modality-private features via spectral coherence, rather than the undifferentiated feature-level fusion or cross-attention that those methods employ. The frequency-domain separation idea draws loosely on DeCUR's contrastive disentanglement and HiLo Transformer's frequency-band modeling, but no prior work has applied spectral coherence to decompose RGB-Event features into shared and private streams for downstream detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shared/private decomposition addresses a practical gap: in low-light or high-dynamic scenarios one modality dominates, and undifferentiated fusion dilutes the informative signal. The frequency-domain coherence criterion provides a physically interpretable basis for the separation, and the layer-wise asymmetric injection in the decoder is a clean mechanism for modality-specific refinement. However, the paper acknowledges that when both modalities are severely degraded, coherence estimation becomes unreliable, and the gains are modest (4.2% mAP on DSEC-Det, 0.1% on PKU-DAVIS-SOD), leaving open whether this decomposition generalizes to other multimodal pairs (RGB-infrared, RGB-LiDAR) or to streaming/online detection settings.
