## Problem

Existing self-supervised ECG methods are either uni-modal (limited representational capacity) or multi-modal but rely on coarse-grained global alignment, neglecting fine-grained correspondences between localized waveform anomalies and diagnostic phrases. No prior work jointly models time-domain signals, frequency-domain spectrograms, and clinical reports within a unified framework with dual-level (global and local) semantic alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263986/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, each sample is a triplet: a 12-lead ECG signal (500 Hz, 10 s), its STFT spectrogram (Hann window, 125-point window, 62-point overlap), and the associated clinical report. At zero-shot inference, the model takes a 12-lead ECG and produces a single ECG embedding, which is scored by cosine similarity against class-level text prompts from the CKEPE dictionary to yield multi-label disease predictions without any fine-tuning.

## Method

TAMER comprises three modules operating on tri-modal features. The TFEP module uses a 1D ResNet-34 for ECG, a 2D CNN for spectrograms, and a frozen Med-CPT encoder for reports, extracting both global (pooled) and local (hidden-layer) features projected into modality-specific latent spaces. The GLTSA module enforces global temporal-spectral consistency via rhythm-level contrastive alignment (RLCA), then fuses local temporal and spectral features through a two-stage residual attention mechanism (WLAI) with attention-based pooling to produce a unified ECG embedding, regularized by dropout-based view consistency (UECR). The RAAR module performs diagnostic-level contrastive alignment between the unified ECG embedding and the global report embedding (RADA), plus a report-guided wave-level refinement (RGWR) that applies dual cross-attention between local ECG wave tokens and report tokens, weighted by the report encoder's [CLS] attention weights to emphasize diagnostically critical phrases. Training is fully self-supervised on 771K ECG-spectrogram-report triplets from MIMIC-IV-ECG, optimizing the sum of all contrastive losses without any labels.

## Results

TAMER achieves zero-shot macro-AUC of 76.5% on PTBXL-Super, 88.3% on CPSC2018, and 78.7% on CSN, with an average of 81.2% across the three datasets. Under cross-domain (domain-shift) evaluation without fine-tuning, TAMER reaches an average AUC of 83.1%, with individual scores of 80.95% (PTBXL-Super→CSN), 86.73% (CPSC2018→CSN), and 87.62% (CSN→CPSC2018). All uni-modal SSL baselines (fine-tuned on 100% labeled data) score between 56–76% AUC, while the closest multi-modal competitors MERL and C-MET achieve 74.2–82.8% and 76.2–80.1% respectively in the zero-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMER outperforms all ten uni-modal SSL baselines by a wide margin; the strongest uni-modal method, ST-MEM, reaches only 62.27–75.19% AUC on domain-shift tasks versus TAMER's 82.00–86.73%, a gap of roughly 15–20 points. Against the closest multi-modal competitor MERL, TAMER gains +5.5 AUC on CPSC2018 zero-shot (88.3 vs 82.8), +4.3 on CSN zero-shot (78.7 vs 74.4), and +2.3 on PTBXL-Super zero-shot (76.5 vs 74.2). TAMER also surpasses C-MET on every zero-shot benchmark (by +0.3 to +8.2 AUC) and on five of six domain-shift transfers. The sole exception is the PTBXL-Super→CPSC2018 domain-shift setting, where MERL scores 88.21 vs TAMER's 84.71, and the PTBXL-Super→CSN transfer where ST-MEM (84.50) edges out TAMER (80.95); the authors attribute these to the robustness of masked modeling under specific distribution shifts. Overall, TAMER's tri-modal design (ECG + spectrogram + report) yields consistent gains over both uni-modal and prior bi-modal (ECG + report) approaches across all three public datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAMER extends the CLIP-style contrastive alignment paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) from image-text pairs to the ECG domain, building most directly on MERL (Liu et al., 2024), which aligned ECG signals with clinical reports for zero-shot diagnosis. The key conceptual departure is introducing 2D STFT spectrograms as an explicit visual modality alongside the 1D ECG waveform and text, reformulating ECG analysis as a tri-modal (signal–image–language) representation learning problem. Unlike prior time-frequency SSL methods (CRT, MassMIB) that only exploit temporal-spectral complementarity, or report-alignment methods (MERL, C-MET) that perform only global coarse matching, TAMER enforces dual-level (rhythm-level and wave-level) alignment across all three modalities, addressing the semantic misalignment and local-correspondence gaps identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot ECG diagnosis addresses a critical clinical bottleneck: the scarcity of labeled data for rare arrhythmias and ischemic patterns. TAMER's consistent gains across three datasets and both zero-shot and domain-shift settings (average AUC 81.2%/83.1%) suggest that tri-modal semantic alignment is a viable path toward generalizable ECG models without task-specific fine-tuning. However, the 713% increase in ECG encoder FLOPs (14.40G vs. 1.77G) raises practical deployment concerns, and the reliance on a frozen Med-CPT encoder limits adaptability to new clinical vocabularies. Open questions include whether the spectrogram modality's contribution (modality ablation: 81.19→77.53 without it) would persist with more powerful time-frequency encoders, and whether the framework scales to longer recordings or multi-lead subset settings common in wearable ECG.
