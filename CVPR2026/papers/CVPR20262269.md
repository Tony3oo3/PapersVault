## Problem

Existing brain imaging VQA benchmarks suffer from two critical limitations: they cover only a narrow set of imaging modalities (typically 1–2, e.g., sMRI alone) and evaluate only a subset of the clinical workflow (e.g., tumor type identification without prognosis or treatment planning). This prevents a comprehensive assessment of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) across the full clinical continuum from anatomical recognition through therapeutic cycle management.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs one or more 2D brain imaging slices (extracted from 3D volumes along axial, sagittal, or coronal planes to disrupt pixel-level retrieval) with a natural-language clinical question. Closed-ended items (6,823 pairs) require selecting one of five lettered options; open-ended items (2,704 pairs) demand free-form clinical reasoning descriptions scored by ROUGE, BLEU, BERTScore, and an LLM-as-a-judge protocol. The benchmark spans 15 modalities (CT, T1W, T2W, FLAIR, DWI, SWI, T1CE, MRA, PD, fMRI, PET, SPECT, anatomical diagrams, histopathology, and generic MRI) and 31,706 images total.

## Method

The benchmark is constructed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) data collection from 30 public brain imaging datasets (BraTS, ISLES, fastMRI, UK Biobank, ADNI, Radiopaedia, etc.), (2) question augmentation via rule-based templates for disease/modality-specific cases and GPT-5 API for generating plausible five-option distractors from multi-granular clinical text, yielding 259,628 VQA pairs (OmniBrainVQA), and (3) systematic filtering that removes non-brain content, reformulates questions with GPT-5 to prevent memorization, deduplicates via Sentence Transformers and DINO-V2 embeddings, and applies K-center clustering to select representative samples, producing the final 9,527 clinically verified pairs. The 15 tasks are organized into five sequential clinical phases (Anatomical & Imaging Assessment → Lesion Identification & Localization → Diagnostic Synthesis & Causal Reasoning → Prognostic Judgment & Risk Forecasting → Therapeutic Cycle Management), each validated by a chief radiologist with 13+ years of experience through a tiered verification workflow that eliminates language-only biases.

## Results

On the closed-ended VQA of OmniBrainBench (6,823 multiple-choice pairs across 15 subtasks), physicians achieve 91.35% overall accuracy, while the best MLLM, Gemini-2.5-Pro, reaches only 66.58%, leaving a 24.77-point gap. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-30B leads general-purpose models at 56.40%, and HuatuoGPT-V-34B tops medical MLLMs at 63.56%. On open-ended VQA (2,704 free-form pairs), Lingshu-32B achieves the highest ROUGE-1 (26.45) and BERTScore (8.35) among open-source models, while GPT-5-mini leads proprietary models on BERTScore (9.13). The hardest subtasks for all MLLMs are Risk Stratification (best: 40.84%) and Preoperative Assessment (best: 46.98%), where even the strongest models score below 50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All 24 MLLMs fall substantially short of the physician baseline (91.35%): the best model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves 66.58%, a 24.77-point deficit that underscores the visual-to-clinical reasoning gap. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (66.58%) > GPT-5-mini (65.00%) > GPT-5 (63.37%) > GPT-4o (61.64%) > Claude-4.5-Sonnet (59.78%) > Grok-4 (56.65%) > Deepseek-V3.1 (40.14%), with Deepseek-V3.1 anomalously weak on visual tasks (AIA 41.01%, LIL 30.83%). Medical MLLMs show the widest variance: HuatuoGPT-V-34B (63.56%) nearly matches top proprietary models and leads in Risk Stratification (40.84%) and Modality Identification (69.55%), while Llava-Med-7B (38.84%) trails all other models. Open-source general-purpose models generally trail both proprietary and medical MLLMs, with Qwen3-VL-30B (56.40%) as the best, though InternVL3-8B (53.25%) and Qwen3-VL-4B (50.45%) show competitive performance on specific subtasks like Abnormal Screening and Clinical Sign Prediction. The most striking finding is that no MLLM exceeds 47% on Preoperative Assessment or 41% on Risk Stratification, revealing a critical failure in complex multi-step clinical reasoning that even the strongest proprietary models cannot overcome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying the fragmented landscape of brain imaging benchmarks—Brain Tumor VQA (sMRI only, 3 tasks), NOVA (single-modality MRI, 4 tasks), and VQA-RAD (2 modalities, 6 tasks)—into a single framework covering 15 imaging modalities and 15 multi-stage clinical tasks aligned with the full diagnostic-to-therapeutic workflow. Conceptually, it extends the medical VQA paradigm from isolated perception or single-stage diagnosis to end-to-end clinical reasoning spanning anatomical identification through postoperative assessment. The data curation pipeline repurposes standard techniques (K-center clustering, DINO-V2/Sentence-Transformer embeddings for deduplication, GPT-based question reformulation) but applies them at unprecedented scale (259,628 pairs from 30 sources) specifically for neuroimaging, a domain where prior benchmarks have been modality- and task-limited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniBrainBench establishes the first comprehensive evaluation standard for brain imaging MLLMs, and its public release (9,527 verified VQA pairs, 31,706 images) will likely become a default benchmark for the neuroimaging-AI community. The ~25% accuracy gap between the best MLLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 66.58%) and physicians (91.35%), concentrated in complex reasoning tasks like risk stratification and preoperative assessment, highlights a critical visual-to-clinical reasoning gap that current architectures cannot bridge. Key open questions include whether native 3D volumetric evaluation (beyond 2D slice approximation) would close part of this gap, how to fully mitigate pre-training data contamination in a domain with limited public corpora, and whether the long-tail therapeutic-cycle-management tasks (currently underrepresented) will reveal additional failure modes at scale.
