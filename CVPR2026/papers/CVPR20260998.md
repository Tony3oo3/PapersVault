## Problem

Existing expert skipping methods (NAEE, MC-MoE, DiEP) were designed for unimodal LLMs and rely solely on intra-layer routing probabilities to decide which experts to deactivate. When applied to MoE MLLMs, these methods cause over 10% accuracy drops at high skipping ratios because they ignore two critical factors: the imbalanced global contribution of experts across layers (shallow layers matter far more than deep ones) and the distinct modality-specific behaviors of text versus vision tokens in FFN layers.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on standard MoE MLLMs (Kimi-VL, Qwen3-VL-MoE, InternVL-3.5) that accept image/video frames plus a text prompt and produce a text response. During a one-time offline calibration phase, 1024 samples from GQA are used to compute per-layer importance factors α^(l) and to search for optimal modality-specific thresholds (τ_t, τ_v) under a user-specified target skipping ratio ρ. At inference, the model produces the same text output as the unmodified model, but each token at each MoE layer is routed to a dynamically reduced subset of experts determined by comparing its importance score against the modality-appropriate threshold.

## Method

MoDES is a training-free framework with two core mechanisms. First, Globally-Modulated Local Gating (GMLG) computes per-expert importance scores s_i^(l) = α^(l) · π_i^(l), where π_i^(l) is the standard local routing probability and α^(l) is a globally-calibrated layer importance factor obtained by measuring the average KL divergence between the original model's output distribution and that of a model with all experts in layer l skipped. Second, Dual-Modality Thresholding (DMT) applies separate thresholds τ_t and τ_v for text and vision tokens respectively, skipping any routed expert whose importance score falls below the threshold corresponding to the token's modality. To find the optimal (τ_t, τ_v) pair under a target skipping ratio constraint, the paper introduces a frontier search algorithm that exploits the monotonicity of both the performance loss and the skipping ratio with respect to thresholds, reducing the search complexity from O(ND²) to O(ND) and cutting calibration time from days to under 4 hours for 20–30B parameter models.

## Results

On Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct, MoDES retains 96.25% of the full model's average accuracy while skipping 83% of experts, compared to 82.81% (NAEE), 88.32% (MC-MoE), and 87.58% (DiEP). On Qwen3-VL-MoE-30B-A3B-Instruct at an aggressive 88% skipping ratio, MoDES achieves 97.33% average accuracy versus 80.60% (NAEE), 86.66% (MC-MoE), and 85.30% (DiEP). Inference speedup reaches 2.16× in prefilling and 1.26× in decoding for Qwen3-VL-MoE-30B-A3B-Instruct. The frontier search algorithm reduces threshold optimization time from ~2 days (naive O(ND²) search) to under 2 hours (O(ND) search), a ~45× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoDES consistently outperforms all three baselines (NAEE, MC-MoE, DiEP) across every skipping ratio and all three model families. At the most aggressive 83% skipping on Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct, MoDES retains 96.25% average accuracy, beating DiEP (87.58%) by 8.67 points, MC-MoE (88.32%) by 7.93 points, and NAEE (82.81%) by 13.44 points. The gap widens further on Qwen3-VL-MoE-30B at 88% skipping, where MoDES achieves 97.33% versus 86.66% for MC-MoE (the strongest baseline), a 10.67-point margin. Even at a moderate 50% skipping ratio, MoDES (99.91%) surpasses DiEP (98.17%) and MC-MoE (97.69%) by 1.74 and 2.22 points respectively. The baselines rely solely on intra-layer routing probabilities and were designed for unimodal LLMs, which explains their significant degradation when applied to multimodal settings with top-k > 2 routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the expert-skipping line of work for MoE LLMs (NAEE, MC-MoE, DiEP) to the multimodal setting, identifying that these methods—designed around intra-layer routing probabilities for text-only models—fail when transferred to MoE MLLMs. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) incorporating a globally-calibrated layer-wise importance factor (GMLG) to account for the fact that shallow-layer experts contribute far more to final outputs than deep-layer ones, and (2) introducing modality-specific thresholds (DMT) that exploit the empirically observed geometric difference between text and vision tokens in FFN layers. The frontier search algorithm for threshold optimization is a new efficiency contribution that exploits monotonicity to reduce search from O(ND²) to O(ND).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE MLLMs (Kimi-VL, Qwen3-VL-MoE, InternVL-3.5) become the dominant architecture for vision-language tasks, a training-free, plug-and-play method that achieves ~2× prefilling speedup while retaining >95% accuracy is practically significant for deployment. The finding that skipping certain experts can actually improve accuracy on some benchmarks (RealWorldQA, VideoMME) suggests that not all activated experts are beneficial, opening questions about whether expert selection during training could be further refined. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to additional modalities (audio), how it interacts with other compression techniques (pruning, distillation) at scale, and whether the calibration set size (1024 samples) remains sufficient for much larger models.
