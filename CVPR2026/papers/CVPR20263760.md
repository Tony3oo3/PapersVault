## Problem

Diffusion-based super-resolution methods apply a uniform restoration intensity across the entire image, ignoring the pronounced spatial heterogeneity in remote sensing images where texture-rich regions (roads, built-up areas) and texture-sparse regions (water, snow) are globally stochastic yet locally clustered. This spatially-invariant approach causes hallucinated artifacts in simple areas and blurry, detail-poor reconstructions in complex ones, and existing methods lack mechanisms to recognize regional texture disparities and adaptively allocate representational capacity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution remote sensing image (degraded via the Real-ESRGAN pipeline) and a text prompt as input, and produces a 4× super-resolved high-resolution remote sensing image. An intermediate Relative Texture Density Map (RTDM) is either directly estimated from the LR-HR pair during training or predicted by a dedicated U-Net-style network at inference, then binarized and downsampled to latent-space resolution to serve as a spatial guidance signal throughout the diffusion process.

## Method

TexADiff builds on SDXL and introduces a binarized Relative Texture Density Map (RTDM) that quantifies pixel-wise texture density discrepancies, leveraging it in three synergistic ways: as an explicit spatial condition fed through a lightweight MiniControlNet (adapted from ControlNext, ~20M params) that fuses the LR image, RTDM, and noisy latent in a single branch; as a loss modulator (Texture-Aware Diffusion Loss) that upweights noise-prediction errors in texture-rich regions; and as a dynamic sampling schedule adapter that skips late denoising steps in texture-sparse regions via an alternating update strategy. To bridge the domain gap between natural-image priors and remote sensing data, a subset of the U-Net (first downsampling block and all upsampling blocks) is selectively unfrozen during training, while the MiniControlNet keeps total trainable parameters at 1.3B versus 2.6B for fully fine-tuned FaithDiff.

## Results

On the four synthetic benchmark datasets (AID, DOTA-Test, LoveDA-Val, RSC11), TexADiff (thr=0.35) achieves the best DISTS on AID (0.1939), DOTA (0.1934), and RSC11 (0.2374), and the best LPIPS on DOTA (0.3358), LoveDA (0.3264), and RSC11 (0.4708), consistently outperforming all diffusion-based baselines on perceptual reference metrics. On the real-world SIRI-WHU dataset, TexADiff (thr=0.40) achieves the best user-study preference (46.4% vs. PASD's 36.7%) and competitive no-reference scores (BRISQUE 24.77, CLIP-IQA+ 0.5628). For the downstream semantic segmentation task on LoveDA, TexADiff attains the highest Overall Accuracy (64.57%), mIoU (44.06%), and mF1 (60.27%), surpassing both PASD and FaithDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based Real-ESRGAN, TexADiff consistently achieves lower PSNR/SSIM (e.g., 22.87 vs. 23.22 on AID) but substantially better perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.3788 vs. 0.4270, DISTS 0.1939 vs. 0.2409 on AID), reflecting the expected trade-off between pixel fidelity and perceptual realism. Against ResShiftL, TexADiff dominates on every metric across all four datasets, with the largest gaps in DISTS (e.g., 0.1934 vs. 0.2822 on DOTA). Against PASD, TexADiff wins on LPIPS across all datasets (e.g., 0.3264 vs. 0.3548 on LoveDA) and on DISTS for AID, DOTA, and RSC11, though PASD retains a DISTS advantage on LoveDA (0.1594 vs. 0.1766). Against the closest competitor FaithDiff, TexADiff achieves better DISTS on all four datasets (e.g., 0.1934 vs. 0.2130 on DOTA) and better LPIPS on DOTA and RSC11, while FaithDiff edges out slightly on LPIPS for AID (0.3752 vs. 0.3788) and CLIP-IQA+ on DOTA (0.5815 vs. 0.5677). On the real-world SIRI-WHU set, TexADiff (thr=0.40) wins the user study decisively (46.4% vs. PASD 36.7%, FaithDiff 16.9%) and achieves the best BRISQUE (24.77) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SDXL latent diffusion backbone and adapts the lightweight ControlNext architecture into a MiniControlNet to efficiently fuse multiple spatial conditions (LR image + RTDM) without the parameter overhead of multi-ControlNet stacks. It departs from prior diffusion-based RSISR methods (FaithDiff, PASD) that apply uniform restoration intensity across the entire image, by introducing a Relative Texture Density Map (RTDM) that explicitly encodes the spatially imbalanced, locally clustered texture distribution characteristic of remote sensing imagery. The RTDM is then leveraged in three synergistic roles—spatial conditioning, loss modulation (TADL), and dynamic sampling schedule—that collectively constitute a texture-aware generation paradigm absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode in diffusion-based RSISR (hallucinated textures in sparse regions, blurring in rich regions) that prior methods overlooked, and demonstrates measurable gains in both perceptual quality and downstream segmentation accuracy. A key limitation is that the RTDM prediction network achieves only 71–79% pixel accuracy against the HR-based oracle, and the gap between predicted and ground-truth RTDM performance (Table 7) suggests substantial room for improvement in the prediction module. Open questions include whether the texture-aware strategy generalizes to other domains with spatially heterogeneous content (e.g., medical imaging, satellite time series) and whether the binarization threshold sensitivity can be made adaptive per scene.
