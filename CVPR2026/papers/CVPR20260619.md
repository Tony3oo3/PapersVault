## Problem

Current VLA models perceive the world through 2D visual inputs but must execute actions in 3D physical space, leaving a substantial gap between visual perception and embodied action grounding. Existing approaches either rely on robot data for pretraining or treat 3D spatial understanding as an afterthought, rather than explicitly teaching the model to map 2D visual cues to 3D geometry and physical motion before policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260619/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes 1–4 consecutive RGB video frames (sampled at 1 fps) paired with text instructions to produce VQA-style spatial reasoning answers or sequences of discretized 3D motion tokens. During post-training and deployment, it takes single or dual RGB views, a language instruction, and the robot proprioceptive state, and outputs a continuous action chunk via a flow-matching DiT head. The 3D encoder (Cut3R) internally produces per-pixel point cloud estimates that are scale-calibrated against MANO hand joint positions to ensure metric consistency with real-world action scales.

## Method

VIPA-VLA employs a dual-encoder architecture where a semantic vision encoder (InternVL3.5-2B) and a 3D geometric encoder (Cut3R) produce complementary embeddings fused via a cross-attention layer (semantic features as queries, 3D features as keys/values). Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 freezes all pretrained parameters and trains only the fusion layer on ~300K VQA pairs from Hand3D-visual (3D spatial relations, task completion, hand/camera movement); Stage 2 extends the LLM vocabulary with motion tokens and trains it to predict discretized 3D wrist trajectories from Hand3D-action (~1M video-instruction-motion pairs). The Hand3D dataset is constructed from nine heterogeneous human manipulation video sources, with 3D annotations derived from Cut3R point clouds, GroundingDINO object boxes, and MANO hand poses, unified via a median-based scale calibration between relative point cloud depths and absolute hand joint depths. Post-training attaches a DiT action head trained with flow matching, conditioned on the fused visual-language hidden states and robot state, updating only the LLM backbone and action head.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (single-view), VIPA-VLA achieves 92.4% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial 92.6%, Object 97.2%, Goal 94.2%, Long 85.6%), surpassing all baselines that do not use robot pretraining and slightly exceeding GR00T N1.5 (92.1%). Under two-view input, VIPA-VLA reaches 96.8% average, nearly matching π0.5 (96.9%) despite using no robot data during pretraining. On real-robot tasks, VIPA-VLA achieves 83%/60% (sub-task/whole-task) on Wipe-Board and 57%/50% on Water-Plant, substantially outperforming GR00T N1.5 (57%/30% and 53%/30%) and the InternVL3.5 backbone (43%/10% and 37%/20%). In unseen environments, VIPA-VLA retains 83%/50% on Wipe-Board-Unseen while all baselines drop to ≤43%/10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LIBERO single-view, VIPA-VLA (92.4% avg) outperforms all spatial-reasoning baselines including 4D-VLA (88.6%), TriVLA (87.0%), and SpatialVLA (78.1%), and slightly edges out GR00T N1.5 (92.1%)—notably without any robot pretraining data. On LIBERO two-view, VIPA-VLA (96.8%) surpasses π0 (94.4%), UniVLA (95.5%), and GR00T N1 (93.9%), trailing only π0.5 (96.9%) by 0.1%. On real-robot tasks, VIPA-VLA dominates Wipe-Board (83%/60% vs. GR00T N1.5's 57%/30%) and Water-Plant (57%/50% vs. 53%/30%), while on Put-Three-Object it achieves a higher sub-task rate (52%) but lower whole-task rate (10%) than GR00T N1.5 (48%/40%), attributed to more consistent partial progress. In unseen environments, VIPA-VLA's advantage is most pronounced: 83%/50% on Wipe-Board-Unseen versus 43%/10% for GR00T N1.5 and 40%/10% for InternVL3.5, demonstrating superior generalization from human-video pretraining. The ablation backbone InternVL3.5 (without dual-encoder or pretraining) scores 34–43% sub-task on real tasks, confirming that both the architecture and Spatial-Aware Pretraining contribute meaningfully.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLA pretraining line (RT-2, OpenVLA, GR00T, π-series) by introducing a spatial-aware pretraining stage that uses 3D-annotated human manipulation videos rather than robot data, addressing the 2D-perception-to-3D-action gap that prior VLA models leave unaddressed. Architecturally, it builds on Cut3R for explicit 3D geometric encoding and flow matching for action generation, but departs from prior 3D VLMs (which focus on static perception) and prior human-video approaches (Being-H0, EgoMimic) by explicitly constructing 3D visual-physical alignment supervision rather than learning implicit representations or directly mapping human to robot action spaces. The dual-encoder design with cross-attention fusion and the two-stage pretraining (spatial alignment → motion token prediction) represent a new methodological contribution within the established robot-manipulation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that human-video-only pretraining can match or exceed models pretrained on large-scale robot datasets (e.g., 92.4% vs 92.1% on LIBERO single-view) suggests a more scalable and accessible path to spatially grounded VLA models, since human demonstrations are far easier to collect across diverse environments. The strong unseen-environment generalization on real robot tasks (83%/50% on Wipe-Board-Unseen vs 43%/10% for GR00T N1.5) indicates the learned 3D grounding is genuinely transferable rather than overfit to training scenes. Key limitations include the small post-training data (50 trajectories per task), evaluation on a single robot platform (Franka), and reliance on accurate MANO hand pose annotations for scale calibration; the paper itself notes that combining this paradigm with robot-data pretraining is a natural next step for broader coverage.
