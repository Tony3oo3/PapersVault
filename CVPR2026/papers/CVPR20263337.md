## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models suffer severe performance degradation on long document VQA due to two compounding issues: a low signal-to-noise ratio where critical evidence is buried among many irrelevant pages, and supervision scarcity where existing datasets provide only final short answers without intermediate reasoning or evidence-localization signals. This forces models to learn fragile memorization shortcuts rather than genuine localization-and-reasoning capabilities, resulting in poor out-of-distribution generalization and uninterpretable outputs.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question followed by N document page images, each prefixed with a textual page identifier (e.g., 'Page i') that is interleaved with the page's visual tokens in the input sequence. During evaluation, pages are rendered at 1024×784 resolution. The output is a structured ALR chain: a Question Analysis segment, an Evidence Localization segment explicitly citing relevant page IDs, a Reasoning Process segment synthesizing cross-page information, followed by a list of evidence page numbers and a final concise answer.

## Method

DocSeeker builds on Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and introduces the ALR (Analysis-Localization-Reasoning) paradigm, which enforces a structured, page-grounded reasoning path rather than free-form chain-of-thought. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I performs SFT on ALR CoT data distilled from Gemini-2.5-Flash using an efficient strategy that feeds the teacher only ground-truth evidence pages plus a few distractors (rather than the full document), followed by a two-stage verification pipeline (EM check + GPT-4o semantic rescue). Stage II applies EviGRPO, a modified GRPO with a weighted multi-dimensional reward combining format compliance (λ₁=0.1), evidence-page F1 with β=2.0 prioritizing recall (λ₂=0.3), and answer ANLS (λ₃=0.6). To handle GPU memory constraints on long documents, the EGRA strategy differentially allocates resolution during training: evidence pages stay at 1024px while 70% of non-evidence pages are randomly downsampled to 256px, preserving high-resolution distractors for robustness while reducing total token count.

## Results

DocSeeker (7B, no RAG) achieves 57.4 ANLS on DUDE, 86.2 ANLS on MPDocVQA, 40.1 Acc on MMLongBench-doc, 51.7 Acc on LongDocURL, and 77.1 F1 on SlideVQA. Compared to the same-architecture Baseline (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct), DocSeeker yields a 23–63% relative improvement across all five benchmarks. On the OOD MMLongBench-doc (up to 468 pages), DocSeeker (40.1 Acc) surpasses all open-source MLLMs and RAG-based methods, approaching GPT-4o (42.8 Acc) while using only 7B parameters and no retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocSeeker outperforms all open-source end-to-end MLLMs (InternVL3, mPLUG-DocOwl2) and all RAG-based methods (M3DocRAG, Vis-RAG, SV-RAG, VDocRAG) on every benchmark where both report results, with the largest margins on MMLongBench-doc (40.1 vs. 24.1 for InternVL3, 21.0 for M3DocRAG) and SlideVQA (77.1 vs. 55.7 for M3DocRAG). Against closed-source models, DocSeeker beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on DUDE (57.4 vs. 46.0), MMLongBench-doc (40.1 vs. 28.2), and LongDocURL (51.7 vs. 50.9), and surpasses GPT-4V on MMLongBench-doc (40.1 vs. 32.4). However, DocSeeker trails GPT-4o on MMLongBench-doc (40.1 vs. 42.8) and LongDocURL (51.7 vs. 64.5), indicating that the strongest proprietary model still holds an edge on the hardest OOD benchmarks. Notably, DocSeeker achieves these results without any retrieval module, whereas all RAG-based competitors require an external visual retriever (e.g., ColPali) to pre-filter pages. The paper further shows that when DocSeeker is integrated with ColPali retrieval, its performance improves further, demonstrating synergy rather than redundancy with RAG pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DocSeeker builds on the Qwen-2.5-VL-7B backbone and extends Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a bespoke multi-dimensional reward that jointly scores format compliance, evidence-page localization, and answer accuracy. Conceptually, it departs from both OCR-parsing pipelines (which suffer cascading errors) and prior pure-visual end-to-end MLLMs (which treat long documents as undifferentiated token streams) by introducing a structured Analysis-Localization-Reasoning (ALR) output format that forces explicit page-level evidence grounding. The ALR paradigm draws on recent large-reasoning-model conventions (separating thought from answer) and the human find-then-reason cognitive strategy, while the EGRA resolution-allocation trick is a novel training-time efficiency mechanism not seen in prior document VQA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated synergy between DocSeeker's localization capability and visual RAG retrievers (performance improves rather than degrades as retrieved page count grows) offers a concrete path toward robust, interpretable RAG-based document systems, addressing the well-known top-k dilemma. The finding that a model trained exclusively on ≤20-page documents generalizes to 468-page inputs with minimal degradation, while a same-architecture baseline collapses, suggests that structured evidence-grounding supervision is a more effective inductive bias for long-context robustness than raw sequence length. Open questions include whether the ALR overhead scales favorably to multi-document or cross-document reasoning, whether the approach transfers to other backbone architectures, and whether the structured reasoning cost is justified for high-throughput deployment.
