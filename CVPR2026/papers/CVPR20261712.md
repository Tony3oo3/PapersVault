## Problem

Video matting models struggle to generalize to real-world footage because high-quality alpha matte annotations are extremely scarce (limited to controlled chroma-key or specialized camera setups, predominantly human portraits) and existing training data relies on synthetic compositing that introduces unrealistic artifacts in lighting, motion blur, and temporal coherence. No large-scale video matting dataset exists where foreground and background naturally co-occur in real captured footage, creating a fundamental domain gap that hinders robustness on in-the-wild videos.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB video frames and a corresponding binary segmentation mask track (e.g., from SAM2) as input, with all modalities encoded into a shared VAE latent space and concatenated along the channel dimension alongside Gaussian noise. It produces a continuous alpha matte video in [0,1] per pixel via a single forward pass (no iterative denoising), decoded back to pixel space through the VAE decoder. Training uses 1024×1024 single frames for spatial detail and 704×704 three-frame clips for temporal consistency, while inference supports variable frame counts from 1 to 24 frames.

## Method

VideoMaMa adapts Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) for mask-guided matting by replacing the original image conditioning with a channel-wise concatenation of video latents, mask latents, and noise, and formulating generation as a single-step v-parameterized prediction from noise to clean alpha matte latents. A two-stage training strategy decomposes learning: stage 1 freezes temporal layers and trains spatial layers at high resolution on single frames to capture pixel-level detail, then stage 2 freezes spatial layers and fine-tunes temporal layers on short video clips for temporal coherence. To prevent the model from trivially copying the input mask, mask augmentation (polygonization and downsample degradation) creates a gap between coarse input and fine-grained target. Semantic knowledge injection aligns intermediate SVD features with frozen DINOv3 features via a learnable MLP and patch-wise cosine similarity loss, improving boundary understanding for complex objects. The pixel-level training loss combines L1 and Laplacian terms on decoded alpha mattes, and the resulting model is used to pseudo-label 50K+ real videos from SA-V masks, producing the MA-V dataset for downstream matting model training.

## Results

On the first-frame mask-guided matting task, SAM2-Matte (SAM2 fine-tuned on MA-V) achieves MAD of 2.61 on V-HIM60 Hard and 1.27 on YouTubeMatte (1920×1080), substantially outperforming MatAnyone (5.72 and 1.99 respectively) and the raw SAM2 baseline (7.85 and 3.85). In the all-frame mask-guided setting, VideoMaMa refines SAM2-generated masks to MAD 2.44 on V-HIM60 Hard and 1.74 on YouTubeMatte, beating the best prior method MaGGIe-FT (3.64 and 2.20). The MA-V dataset alone (without existing matting data) already surpasses MatAnyone on V-HIM60 Hard (MAD 3.18 vs 4.67), and combining MA-V with existing datasets yields the best overall matting quality (MAD 2.61).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2-Matte decisively outperforms MatAnyone [49] on the hardest benchmarks: on V-HIM60 Hard, MAD drops from 5.72 to 2.61 (54% reduction) and Gradient error from 9.82 to 5.09; on YouTubeMatte, MAD improves from 1.99 to 1.27 (36% reduction). Against the raw SAM2 [31] baseline (which only produces binary masks), SAM2-Matte reduces MAD by 67% on V-HIM60 Hard (7.85→2.61) and by 67% on YouTubeMatte (3.85→1.27), demonstrating that the MA-V pseudo-labels add substantial matting detail. In the all-frame mask-guided setting (Table 2), VideoMaMa beats MaGGIe [14] and its fine-tuned variant MaGGIe-FT on both benchmarks across all mask types (e.g., SAM2 masks on V-HIM60 Hard: MAD 2.44 vs 3.64), and also outperforms the image-based MGM [50] and MGM-IW [28] methods. Notably, SAM2-Matte trained on MA-V alone already surpasses MatAnyone on V-HIM60 Hard (MAD 3.18 vs 4.67), confirming that the pseudo-labeled real-world data is more effective than the synthetic composition-based data used by prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoMaMa extends the diffusion-for-perception paradigm pioneered by Marigold (depth estimation) and Diception (general dense prediction) to the video matting domain, repurposing Stable Video Diffusion's generative priors to convert binary segmentation masks into continuous alpha mattes. It builds on the SAM2/SAM lineage for mask generation and the iterative self-training loop concept, while departing from prior mask-guided matting methods (MaGGIe, MatAnyone, GVM) that are either restricted to human portraits or trained exclusively on synthetic compositions. The image-level mask-to-matte conversion of ZIM is extended to the video setting with temporal consistency, and the two-stage spatial-then-temporal training strategy with DINOv3 semantic injection represents a novel architectural adaptation not seen in prior diffusion-based matting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MA-V dataset (50K+ real-world videos) is the first large-scale video matting benchmark where foreground and background naturally co-occur, addressing a fundamental data scarcity bottleneck that has limited the field to hundreds of synthetic compositions. The pseudo-labeling pipeline is simple and reproducible, making it a practical tool for the community to generate matting annotations at scale. Open questions include whether pseudo-labels from a single diffusion model introduce systematic biases that limit downstream model performance, how the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions, and whether the synthetic-to-real generalization holds for extremely rare object categories or adversarial conditions not covered by the diffusion prior's training distribution.
