## Problem

Image-to-point-cloud registration suffers from severe performance degradation in unseen scenes because appearance-driven 2D features and geometry-driven 3D features follow fundamentally different distributions, making even correct correspondences exhibit low feature similarity. Existing approaches either refine cross-modal features or prune incorrect correspondences in isolation, but lack a unified structure that jointly reasons over both 2D and 3D relationships, leaving the generalization gap largely unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260030/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 640×480) and a colored 3D point cloud (voxel-downsampled at 1.5 cm, up to 3×10⁵ points with RGB attributes) as input. Both modalities are first segmented into non-overlapping regions using 2D SAM (FastSAM at inference) and 3D SAM, which form the vertices of a heterogeneous graph. The output is a set of 2D-3D pixel-to-point correspondences from which a camera pose in SE(3) is estimated via RANSAC-based PnP, with outliers pruned by graph-based projection consistency criteria.

## Method

Hg-I2P constructs a heterogeneous weighted graph G_H whose vertices are SAM-segmented 2D and 3D regions and whose edges include intra-modal (I2I, P2P) and cross-modal (I2P) connections. Three modules operate on this graph: (1) MP-mining predicts the I2P edge matrix by mining three multi-path adjacency relations (I2I→I2P, I2P→P2P, I2I→I2P→P2P) and applying self-attention over the concatenated path features; (2) HE-adapting refines region-level features via a two-step message generation–interaction scheme, where cross-modal messages are computed by weighted aggregation of neighboring region features along learned I2P edges and then injected into each region's feature map through self-attention with a residual mixing coefficient β; (3) HC-pruning is a non-learning module that removes outlier correspondences using two graph-derived projection consistency criteria (reprojection distance and relative-coordinate cosine similarity) given an initial RANSAC-PnP pose. Training jointly optimizes a circle-loss on patch-guided correspondences and an L2 supervision on predicted I2P edges against GT edges computed via IoU of projected regions under the ground-truth pose.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes cross-scene benchmark (RR threshold 2.5 cm), Hg-I2P achieves average IR of 0.581/0.558/0.688 and RR of 0.667/0.690/0.853 for training scenes C/O/K respectively, outperforming the strongest baselines (MinCD: 0.445/0.568/0.568 IR; Top-I2P: 0.433/0.469/0.596 IR). On KITTI (outdoor, LiDAR), Hg-I2P attains 0.172 m RTE and 0.502° RRE, the best among all compared methods. On ScanNet, Hg-I2P reaches 0.586 IR and 0.922 RR, surpassing Top-I2P (0.493/0.901) and MinCD (0.464/0.898). On TUM, Hg-I2P maintains RTE below 5 cm across all three sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hg-I2P consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On 7-Scenes cross-scene evaluation, it surpasses the strongest prior methods MinCD (ICCV 2025) and Top-I2P (IJCAI 2025) by 10–13% in IR and 5–7% in RR (e.g., 0.581 vs. 0.445 IR for C→X). On KITTI, Hg-I2P achieves 0.172 m RTE and 0.502° RRE, beating the next-best CMR-Agent (0.195 m / 0.589°) and ImCorr (0.200 m / 1.240°), with a notable 2.5× reduction in RRE over GraphI2P (1.650°). On ScanNet, Hg-I2P leads in both IR (0.586 vs. 0.568 for FreeReg) and RR (0.922 vs. 0.901 for Top-I2P). On TUM, it reduces RTE to 0.035–0.050 m and RRE to 1.147–1.455°, outperforming MATR and Top-I2P on all three sequences. On RGBD-V2, Hg-I2P achieves 0.428 IR and 0.690 RR, exceeding FlowI2P (0.401/0.684) and DiffReg (0.378/0.874 RR but lower IR). The ablation variant Hg-I2P† (without HC-pruning) already beats most baselines, confirming that the heterogeneous graph feature adaptation is the primary driver of generalization, while HC-pruning provides an additional 3–7% IR boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Top-I2P line of work [2], which first leveraged 2D/3D SAM segmentation and topological relationships for I2P registration, by formalizing the inter-region mapping as a heterogeneous graph with three edge types (I2I, P2P, I2P). Unlike prior methods that address either cross-modal feature refinement or correspondence pruning in isolation, Hg-I2P unifies both within a single graph structure: multi-path edge mining learns the heterogeneous I2P edges, graph-guided message passing adapts features, and graph-based projection consistency prunes outliers. The backbone and matching pipeline are inherited from MATR [23], while the graph learning and pruning components represent the primary architectural departure from existing I2P registration architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified heterogeneous graph framework offers a principled, single-architecture solution to the long-standing generalization gap in I2P registration, avoiding the fragmented treatment (separate feature refinement + separate pruning) that has characterized prior work. Consistent gains across six indoor and outdoor datasets under cross-domain evaluation strengthen the case that graph-structured cross-modal reasoning is a viable generalization strategy. Open questions include the sensitivity to SAM segmentation quality (poor segmentation would degrade the graph structure), the reliance on GT poses for edge supervision during training, and whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes where the number of segments M×N could become prohibitive. The non-learning HC-pruning module's effectiveness also raises the question of whether learned pruning could further improve robustness.
