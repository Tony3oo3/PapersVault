## Problem

State-of-the-art hyperparameter optimization methods, including the leading ifBO, are difficulty-agnostic: they apply uniform surrogate architectures, identical exploration horizons, and hand-crafted parametric priors regardless of how rugged or smooth the local optimization landscape is. This homogeneity wastes budget on easy configurations while under-exploring complex, multi-phase landscapes, causing up to 43% higher regret on high-difficulty tasks.

## Input / Output

At each freeze-thaw iteration, the method consumes a context set of observed (hyperparameter, timestep, performance) tuples from partially trained configurations plus a query point, and computes a 192-dimensional difficulty descriptor from three hierarchical levels (task spread, k-NN local variance, and per-curve statistics like improvement rate and saturation). The DA-PFN outputs a discretized predictive distribution over 1000 bins in [0,1] in a single forward pass, from which 50 samples are drawn to evaluate the DC-MFPI acquisition function and select the next configuration to continue training.

## Method

DABO integrates difficulty as a first-class conditioning variable across the entire pipeline. A conditional 1D U-Net diffusion model, trained on ~120K real learning curves from HPO-Bench and JAHS-Bench-201, synthesizes 1M difficulty-annotated curves that replace ifBO's four hand-crafted basis functions, achieving 2.3× higher fidelity (FCD 1.24 vs. 2.83). The DA-PFN surrogate extends the standard PFN Transformer with two difficulty-injection mechanisms: a global additive bias from task-level difficulty before the first layer, and a similarity-modulated attention multiplier S_ij = 1 + γ·exp(−‖D_i − D_j‖₂) that up-weights context points with similar difficulty profiles. The DC-MFPI acquisition function adapts its look-ahead horizon exponentially with configuration-level difficulty (shorter for rugged regions) and its improvement threshold with task-level difficulty (more relaxed for complex tasks), enabling difficulty-stratified resource allocation within the freeze-thaw loop.

## Results

On the three HPO benchmarks (75 tasks total), DABO achieves the lowest final normalized regret at B=1000: 0.014 on LCBench, 0.028 on PD1, and 0.039 on Taskset, outperforming the state-of-the-art ifBO by 12.5%, 17.6%, and 11.4% respectively. In surrogate quality (Table 1), DA-PFN improves log-likelihood over ifBO's FT-PFN by 10–30% (e.g., 2.50 vs. 1.13 on PD1) while maintaining comparable MSE. DABO also shows stronger anytime performance, reaching 90% of ifBO's final regret with ~15% less budget on PD1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DABO outperforms all seven baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the primary state-of-the-art ifBO (FT-PFN), DABO reduces regret by 12.5% (LCBench), 17.6% (PD1), and 11.4% (Taskset), with statistically significant gains (p<0.05, Wilcoxon) on PD1 and Taskset. The gap over ifBO is largest on high-difficulty tasks (e.g., PD1's ImageNet-ResNet, Taskset's 8-hyperparameter transformers), where difficulty-adaptive allocation provides >20% per-task improvement, while on simple tasks both methods perform similarly. Against online-training methods DyHPO and DPL, DABO achieves 33–43% lower regret while being 20–30× faster per inference (2.05 s vs. 42–60 s per 1000 predictions). Against bandit-based methods (Hyperband, ASHA) and the original Freeze-Thaw GP, DABO achieves 55–65% lower regret, confirming the advantage of learned surrogates over fixed schedules. The ablation further shows that difficulty conditioning in the surrogate (DA) contributes the largest single-component gain (7.5%), followed by diffusion-based data generation (5%) and adaptive acquisition (3.75%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DABO directly extends ifBO (Rakotoarison et al., ICML 2024), the state-of-the-art freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization method that uses Prior-data Fitted Networks for in-context learning. The key departure is twofold: replacing ifBO's four hand-crafted parametric basis functions with a conditional diffusion model trained on 120K real learning curves, and injecting a hierarchical difficulty signal (task complexity, configuration sensitivity, curve characteristics) into both the surrogate's attention mechanism and the acquisition function. Architecturally, the DA-PFN builds on the PFN Transformer paradigm (Müller et al., ICLR 2022) with difficulty-modulated attention, while the generative prior draws on DDPM/DDIM (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) applied here for the first time to HPO learning-curve synthesis. The most direct conceptual precursor is LC-PFN (Adriaensen et al., NeurIPS 2023), which introduced difficulty for single-curve extrapolation; DABO generalizes this to full multi-configuration freeze-thaw optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a genuine and pervasive limitation—difficulty-blindness—in all prior freeze-thaw and multi-fidelity HPO methods, and demonstrates that explicitly modeling landscape complexity yields substantially larger gains on hard tasks (43% regret reduction) than on easy ones (12%), validating the core thesis. However, the 1056 GPU-hours of offline training (diffusion model + DA-PFN) is a non-trivial upfront investment that limits immediate adoption, and the 130ms per-iteration overhead, while small relative to training time, adds complexity. Open questions include whether the difficulty hierarchy generalizes to much larger search spaces (e.g., 50+ hyperparameters), whether the diffusion prior's advantage over parametric priors persists as real-curve training data grows, and whether the approach transfers to settings where learning curves are noisy or non-monotonic (e.g., reinforcement learning).
