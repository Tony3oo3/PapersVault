## Problem

Multi-view inpainting requires 3D consistency across views, but existing two-stage pipelines (independent 2D inpainting followed by 3D optimization) produce artifacts and require lengthy optimization with specially crafted losses. The single-stage alternative, MVInpainter, relies on implicit optical-flow motion cues that are brittle for non-video inputs. The paper asks whether explicit geometric correspondences from a 3D foundation model can be injected directly into a diffusion inpainter to achieve 3D consistency from the outset, eliminating the need for post-hoc 3D optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked target image (VAE latent), its binary inpainting mask, and an unmasked reference view from a different camera position. A 3D foundation model (VGGT) is run once per view pair to produce a per-pixel 3D point map of the reference, shared camera intrinsics, and the relative pose [R; t]. The output is a set of inpainted images that are individually plausible and 3D-consistent across views, directly suitable for training a NeRF with only a standard image reconstruction loss (no perceptual or adversarial terms). The inpainting mask is constrained to 30–50% of the projected 3D bounding box area to preserve sufficient unmasked context for the 3D foundation model.

## Method

LaRP augments a pretrained Stable Diffusion inpainting UNet by cloning its encoder as a trainable copy while locking the original. The cloned encoder processes the reference image at timestep t=0 (clean latent, zero mask) to produce multi-scale appearance feature maps in the UNet's latent space. These features are lifted to a 3D feature point cloud using the VGGT-estimated reference point map, reprojected onto the target view via the relative pose, and splatted into target-space conditioning tensors (holes set to zero). The reprojected conditioning is injected into the original UNet's decoder through zero-initialized convolution layers. The critical design choice is performing reprojection only after the cloned encoder has produced its activations, so the encoder always sees a clean, in-domain reference image rather than a geometrically warped image with holes. Training uses a scalable data pipeline that repurposes the Objectron video dataset: FPS-based view selection enforces moderate camera baselines (10th–30th percentile of pairwise distances), and 3D-aware mask generation slices the projected bounding box with a random line to produce plausible partial-object masks. The model trains for 20k steps on a single RTX 4090, achieving sudden convergence at ~2,000 steps versus ~7,000 for a ControlNet baseline.

## Results

On the SPIn-NeRF dataset, LaRP achieves the best multi-view consistency (MEt3R_M = 0.1109, MEt3R_R = 0.1293) and the best NVS quality in FID (34.84) and m-FID (226.9) among all compared methods, while completing NeRF training in just 20 minutes versus 960 minutes for the prior best-performing MALD-NeRF (~50× speedup). Remarkably, even a 4-minute NeRF training run with LaRP inpaintings surpasses the FID of all prior methods (39.64 vs. 37.97 for MVInpainter-F). On the wider-baseline 360-USID dataset, LaRP achieves superior FID (98.20 vs. 103.8) and m-FID (182.5 vs. 185.7) compared to AuraFusion360 while being 3× faster (27 min vs. 85 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against single-view inpainting baselines LaMa and LDM, LaRP dominates on all metrics: MEt3R_M improves from 0.1374 (LaMa) and 0.1790 (LDM) to 0.1109, and FID drops from 60.07/72.49 to 34.84. Against the prior reference-based SOTA MVInpainter-F, LaRP achieves marginally better multi-view consistency (MEt3R_M 0.1109 vs. 0.1113) and substantially better FID (34.84 vs. 37.97) and m-FID (226.9 vs. 235.0) at the same 20-minute NeRF training budget, while also training ~15× faster (14 h on 1× 4090 vs. 3 days on 8× A100). Against the two-stage NeRF-based methods SPIn-NeRF and 3DGIC, LaRP wins decisively on all quality metrics (FID 34.84 vs. 60.07 and 72.49) and is 4–7× faster. Against MALD-NeRF, LaRP trades a small LPIPS gap (0.2458 vs. 0.2288) for a large FID advantage (34.84 vs. 44.97) and a ~48× speedup (20 min vs. 960 min), since LaRP's inherent consistency eliminates the need for adversarial fine-tuning. On 360-USID, LaRP beats AuraFusion360 on FID (98.20 vs. 103.8) and m-FID (182.5 vs. 185.7) while being 3× faster, though it trails slightly on LPIPS (0.3626 vs. 0.2942).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaRP departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (independent 2D inpainting followed by 3D optimization with crafted losses) and from MVInpainter's single-stage approach that relies on implicit optical-flow motion cues. Instead, it injects explicit geometric correspondences from a 3D foundation model (VGGT) directly into a pretrained diffusion inpainting model via a latent-space reprojection mechanism, deliberately contrasting with ControlNet's pixel-space conditioning. The architecture reuses the pretrained UNet's input convolution and encoder weights to encode reference-view appearance, then reprojects the resulting multi-scale features geometrically before injection into the decoder—a design that preserves the generative prior while providing 3D-aligned guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by front-loading 3D consistency into the inpainting step, LaRP eliminates the need for specialized perceptual or adversarial losses during NeRF training, yielding a ~50× speedup over MALD-NeRF while matching or exceeding its NVS quality. The finding that a single-object-category training set (chairs) performs nearly as well as multi-category data suggests geometric rigor in data curation matters more than semantic diversity, a useful insight for future 3D-aware generative training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to unbounded scenes with very large baselines, how it interacts with more advanced base diffusion models, and whether the VAE's known limitations on fine patterns (text, dense textures) can be mitigated without sacrificing the latent-space reprojection design.
