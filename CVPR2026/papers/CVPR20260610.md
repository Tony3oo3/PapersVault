## Problem

Existing RL-based multimodal reasoning models are typically trained for a single task and operate exclusively on either images or videos, limiting practical versatility and hindering cross-task and cross-modal knowledge transfer. The paper asks whether a single model can be trained to simultaneously handle both image and video understanding across diverse fundamental visual tasks—QA, captioning, grounding, tracking, and segmentation—while maintaining strong performance on each.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image or a video clip (capped at 128 frames during training) paired with a text query, and produces a two-part response: a chain-of-thought reasoning block enclosed in think tags followed by a task-specific answer block. For perception-oriented tasks (grounding, tracking, segmentation), the answer is a structured JSON containing bounding boxes, time spans, or box-plus-point sets (3 positive and 3 negative points for segmentation), enabling automatic parsing and verifiable reward computation. For open-ended QA and captioning, an external reward model (POLAR-7B) scores answer similarity rather than exact match.

## Method

OneThinker is built on Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B and trained in two stages: SFT cold-start on OneThinker-SFT-340k (CoT-annotated by Seed1.5-VL from the 600k corpus) followed by multi-task RL on the full OneThinker-600k dataset spanning 8 task types across both modalities. The key algorithmic contribution is EMA-GRPO, which addresses two complementary imbalances in multi-task RL: standard GRPO's sample-wise standard-deviation normalization biases updates toward low-variance rollouts (intra-task imbalance), while removing it (as in Dr.GRPO) lets sparse-reward tasks like math dominate over dense-reward tasks like grounding (inter-task imbalance). EMA-GRPO resolves both by maintaining per-task exponential moving averages (β=0.99) of reward first and second moments, then normalizing each rollout's advantage by its task's EMA standard deviation, giving every task a stable yet adaptive normalization scale. For segmentation, the model predicts a bounding box plus positive/negative points that are fed into SAM2 to produce the final mask, with a Gaussian-kernel reward over point distances and (for video) keyframe time.

## Results

OneThinker-8B achieves 70.6% on MMMU, 77.6% on MathVista, and 64.3% on MathVerse for image QA, surpassing GPT-4o (70.7/63.8/41.2) and all open-source R1-style models. On video QA, it reaches 66.2% on VideoMMMU and 35.0% on VideoMathQA, outperforming Video-R1-7B (52.4/21.4) and VideoChat-R1-7B (46.4/27.6). For perception tasks, OneThinker attains 84.4 R@0.5 on GOT-10k tracking, 93.7/88.9/92.0 on RefCOCO spatial grounding, and 54.9 J&F on ReasonVOS video segmentation, setting new state-of-the-art across all 31 benchmarks spanning 10 task types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneThinker-8B outperforms GPT-4o on 7 of 8 image QA benchmarks (e.g., MathVista 77.6 vs 63.8, MathVerse 64.3 vs 41.2, ScienceQA 96.5 vs 90.1), while remaining competitive on MMMU (70.6 vs 70.7). Against the proprietary Gemini 2.5 Pro and Seed1.5-VL, OneThinker trails on MMMU (70.6 vs 81.7/77.9) and MMBench (86.6 vs 90.1/89.9), but surpasses them on MathVerse (64.3 vs –/–) and ScienceQA (96.5 vs –/–). Among open-source R1-style models, OneThinker leads all competitors by a wide margin: +10.0 over Vision-R1 on MathVerse, +13.3 over SophiaVL-R1 on MMMU, and +12.2 over VL-Rethinker on MathVerse. On video QA, OneThinker beats Video-R1-7B by +13.8 on VideoMMMU and +13.6 on VideoMathQA, and outperforms VideoChat-R1-7B by +19.8 on VideoMMMU. For tracking on GOT-10k, OneThinker achieves 84.4 R@0.5 versus 76.6 for R1-Track and 30.6 for VideoChat-R1, a substantial margin. The base model Qwen3-VL-Instruct-8B is improved by +10.4 on MMMU, +6.2 on MathVerse, and +4.5 on ScienceQA after unified multi-task RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm popularized by DeepSeek-R1 to the multimodal setting, building directly on the training objective and clip-based policy update. It departs from the prevailing single-task, single-modality approach (Vision-R1 for image QA, Video-R1 for video QA, Seg-R1 for segmentation, etc.) by jointly training one model across ten visual tasks spanning both images and videos. The key methodological departure is EMA-GRPO, which addresses reward-scale heterogeneity across tasks by maintaining per-task exponential moving averages of reward statistics, resolving both the intra-task variance bias of standard GRPO and the inter-task dominance problem of Dr.GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OneThinker is the first model to demonstrate that a single 8B MLLM can simultaneously handle QA, captioning, grounding, tracking, and segmentation across both image and video modalities with competitive performance on 31 benchmarks, and the ablations provide concrete evidence of cross-task and cross-modal knowledge transfer (e.g., removing temporal grounding data degrades video QA and tracking). The EMA-GRPO formulation is a practical, drop-in improvement for any multi-task RL pipeline with heterogeneous reward scales. Open questions include whether these benefits persist at larger model scales, whether the omitted video-segmentation mask reward (due to SAM2 latency) limits the full potential of that task, and how far zero-shot generalization to truly novel task formulations can be pushed.
