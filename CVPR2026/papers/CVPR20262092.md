## Problem

Real-world object detectors must simultaneously handle emerging object classes, shifting visual domains (e.g., day→night→fog), and identification of truly novel objects as 'unknown'—yet no existing paradigm addresses all three under exemplar-free constraints. Open-world detection methods assume a static domain and rely on exemplar replay, while domain-incremental methods operate under closed-world label spaces, leaving a critical gap for detectors deployed in continuously evolving environments where prior data cannot be stored or revisited.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images from sequentially arriving domains (Pascal Series: VOC, Clipart, Watercolor, Comic; Diverse Weather: BDD-100k, FoggyCityscapes, Adverse-Weather) and produces bounding boxes with class labels drawn from a growing set of known classes plus a dedicated 'unknown' class. During training on task t, annotations cover only the current task's known classes; instances of previously learned classes and truly novel objects present in the images remain unlabeled, mirroring real deployment where supervision is sparse and delayed.

## Method

EW-DETR augments DETR-based detectors (RF-DETR, Deformable DETR) with three synergistic modules. First, Incremental LoRA Adapters maintain a frozen aggregate adapter (accumulating compressed knowledge via truncated SVD) and a trainable task-specific adapter, merged with a data-aware coefficient β_t inversely proportional to the current task's sample ratio, enabling stable knowledge consolidation without storing any exemplars. Second, the Query-Norm Objectness Adapter applies LayerNorm followed by L2 normalization to decoder queries, decoupling class semantics (feature direction) from a class-agnostic objectness cue (feature norm) without any auxiliary supervision. Third, Entropy-Aware Unknown Mixing blends a classifier-driven unknown probability (from a learned unknown logit) with an objectness-driven unknown probability (high objectness + high classification entropy), calibrating the final unknown score and softly suppressing known-class logits for high-uncertainty, high-objectness queries.

## Results

On the primary Pascal Series benchmark (VOC [1:10] → Clipart [11:18]), EW-DETR with RF-DETR backbone achieves a FOGS score of 61.08, outperforming the next-best method ORTH (29.78) by 105%. The method attains near-perfect forgetting resistance (FSS: 96.19), the best open-set performance (OSS: 78.62), and competitive generalisation (GSS: 8.42), all with only 1.8M trainable parameters versus 24–106M for baselines. Across the Diverse Weather multi-phase benchmark, EW-DETR (RF-DETR) achieves FOGS scores of 47.27–55.56, again leading all competitors by a wide margin (next best: ORTH at 33.4–38.12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EW-DETR (RF-DETR) dominates all seven baselines on the composite FOGS metric across every benchmark, achieving 61.08 on Pascal Series versus the next-best ORTH at 29.78 (a 105% relative gain). On forgetting (FSS), EW-DETR's 96.19 is nearly 2.4× higher than DuET (41.05), the only other method with non-trivial retention, while replay-based OWOD methods (ORE, OW-DETR, PROB, OWOBJ) all score 0, confirming catastrophic forgetting. On open-set detection (OSS), EW-DETR (78.62) surpasses PROB (67.58) and OWOBJ (60.73), which were previously the strongest open-world detectors. On generalisation (GSS), ORTH leads on the two-phase Pascal benchmark (32.44 vs. 8.42), but EW-DETR recovers and leads on the multi-phase Diverse Weather benchmark (GSS: 15.42–22.18 vs. ORTH's 22.81–52.89 in individual transitions, with EW-DETR winning the overall FOGS). Notably, EW-DETR achieves all of this with only 1.8M trainable parameters, compared to 24–106M for baselines, thanks to the frozen backbone plus low-rank LoRA adapters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—open-world object detection (OWOD: ORE, OW-DETR, CAT, PROB, ORTH, OWOBJ), domain-incremental object detection (DIOD: LDB), and dual incremental object detection (DuIOD: DuET, the authors' own prior work)—into a single paradigm (EWOD) that requires all three capabilities simultaneously under exemplar-free constraints. Methodologically, it repurposes LoRA (originally from NLP parameter-efficient fine-tuning) as a dual-adapter mechanism for DETR encoder-decoder layers, and introduces a Query-Norm Objectness Adapter that exploits the natural norm structure of DETR decoder queries as a free objectness signal, avoiding the auxiliary supervision losses used by prior OWOD methods. The Entropy-Aware Unknown Mixing module departs from energy-based or proposal-memory approaches in OWOD by operating as a lightweight logit-calibration layer trained purely through the standard detection loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EWOD formalises a practically critical deployment scenario (autonomous vehicles, warehouse robots) where detectors must handle new classes, shifting weather/lighting, and truly novel objects without storing past data—a constraint driven by privacy regulations and storage limits. The FOGS metric provides a single-number holistic evaluation that prior fragmented metrics (F-map, RAI, U-Recall, WI) could not capture, enabling fairer cross-method comparison. However, the low GSS scores (14–22 across all methods) reveal that cross-domain generalisation remains largely unsolved, and the benchmarks (Pascal series with 20 classes, 7-class weather) are small relative to real-world complexity. Open questions include scalability to ImageNet-scale class spaces, more severe domain shifts, and whether the LoRA-based approach generalises to non-DETR architectures.
