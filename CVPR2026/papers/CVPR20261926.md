## Problem

Existing adversarial patches for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models overfit to a single surrogate and degrade sharply on unseen architectures, finetuned variants, or sim-to-real shifts, leaving black-box transferability largely unexplored. This paper addresses the gap by studying universal, transferable patch attacks that remain effective across diverse VLA families (OpenVLA, OpenVLA-oft, π₀) under unknown architectures, varying camera poses, and domain shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB frames from a robot's visual stream, a text instruction, and gradient access to a single surrogate VLA (e.g., OpenVLA-7B) as input, and produces a single universal physical patch δ ∈ [0,1]^{h_p × w_p × 3} subject to an area budget ρ. At deployment, the patch is rendered onto each frame via a randomly sampled area-preserving geometric transformation (position, skew, rotation), making it location-agnostic. No target-model weights, architecture details, or fine-tuning recipes are used during optimization, enforcing a strict black-box transfer protocol.

## Method

UPA-RFAS learns the patch in a shared feature space (vision encoder + projector) rather than end-to-end through the policy, guided by a theoretical lower bound (Proposition 1) showing that surrogate-side ℓ₁ feature deviation provably induces target-side displacement under a linear-alignment assumption validated via CCA (R² ≈ 0.654). The training objective combines an ℓ₁ deviation term with a repulsive InfoNCE contrastive loss to push patched features away from clean anchors along batch-consistent, high-CCA directions. A robustness-augmented bi-level procedure (RAUP) emulates adversarial training without retraining the VLA: an inner PGD loop learns an invisible sample-wise perturbation that hardens the surrogate, and an outer AdamW loop optimizes the universal patch against this hardened neighborhood under heavy geometric randomization. Two VLA-specific losses are added: Patch Attention Dominance (PAD) increases patch-routed text→vision attention increments on action-relevant queries while suppressing non-patch increments with a one-sided margin, and Patch Semantic Misalignment (PSM) pulls the pooled patch representation toward cross-model-stable probe-phrase anchors (e.g., 'put', 'left') while repelling it from the current instruction embedding, inducing a persistent label-free image-text mismatch.

## Results

On LIBERO, UPA-RFAS reduces the average task success rate of the OpenVLA-oft-w victim from 98.25% (benign) to 5.75% in simulation and 40.25% in the physical setting, representing drops of over 92 and 58 percentage points respectively. Against the OpenVLA-off victim (fine-tuned on a single suite), success rates fall to 43.50% (simulated) and 61.50% (physical). All six RoboticAttack baselines (UMA, UADA, TMA variants) retain average success rates between 41.25% and 91.25%, substantially higher than UPA-RFAS across every task category and setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline family is RoboticAttack [57] (ICCV 2025), whose six white-box objectives (UMA1, UMA1-3, UADA1, UADA1-3, TMA1, TMA7) are re-evaluated under the same black-box transfer protocol. UPA-RFAS dominates every baseline on every metric: in the simulated OpenVLA-oft-w setting it achieves a 5.75% average success rate versus 41.25%–69.25% for the baselines, and in the physical setting it reaches 40.25% versus 65.00%–91.25%. The gap is most dramatic on the Long-horizon suite, where UPA-RFAS drops success to 6% (sim) and 28% (phys) while the strongest baseline (TMA7) still retains 34% and 90% respectively. Even the weakest baseline (UADA1-3 at 41.25% sim) is roughly 7× less destructive than UPA-RFAS. The paper also reports transfer to the architecturally distinct π₀ model (Appendix D), where UPA-RFAS again outperforms all RoboticAttack objectives, confirming that the advantage is not limited to same-family OpenVLA variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the white-box VLA patch attack formulation of Wang et al. (RoboticAttack, ICCV 2025) to a black-box, transferable setting where the attacker only has gradient access to a surrogate model. It repurposes feature-space transferability techniques from the image-classification adversarial literature (ℓ₁ deviation, repulsive InfoNCE contrastive alignment, CCA-based shared-subspace analysis) and adapts them to the multi-modal VLA pipeline. The key methodological departure is the introduction of two VLA-specific losses—Patch Attention Dominance and Patch Semantic Misalignment—that exploit the cross-modal attention bottleneck and instruction-conditioned decoding unique to vision-language-action policies, combined with a bi-level robustness-augmented optimization that emulates adversarial training without retraining the VLA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLA models move toward real-world robotic deployment, the demonstration that a single physical patch can reduce task success by over 92 percentage points in simulation and still degrade performance across fundamentally different architectures (autoregressive OpenVLA → flow-based π₀) exposes a concrete, practical attack surface that current safety evaluations miss. The paper establishes a strong transferable baseline that future defenses must account for, but open questions remain: whether the shared low-dimensional feature subspace (R²≈0.654) holds for more heterogeneous VLA families, how defenses (e.g., attention masking, patch detection) scale to multi-step long-horizon tasks, and whether the results generalize beyond the relatively simple LIBERO and BridgeData manipulation suites. The physical-setting results, while still strong, show a smaller gap than simulation, suggesting sim-to-real robustness of the attack is not yet fully characterized.
