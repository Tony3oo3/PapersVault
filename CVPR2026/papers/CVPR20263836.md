## Problem

Existing norm rescaling methods for long-tailed recognition rely on parameter regularization (e.g., weight decay, label smoothing) that introduces continuous hyperparameters to which LTR performance is notoriously sensitive, requiring case-specific tuning. Additionally, the literature offers conflicting explanations for why rare classes underperform—some attribute it to underfitting, others to overfitting—leaving the theoretical foundation of norm rescaling unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263836/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, standard resolution for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist2018) and outputs class labels via a standard softmax classifier. SAMN specifically consumes the per-class weight vectors and biases from the first-stage (representation learning) classifier and produces rescaled weights whose norms are monotonically ordered by an inverse frequency metric, applied during a 20-epoch retraining stage.

## Method

SAMN decouples each classifier weight into a unit direction and a learnable scalar magnitude, then applies the Pool Adjacent Violators Algorithm (PAVA) to project these scalars onto a monotonic sequence ordered by an inverse class-frequency or inverse first-stage-norm metric. The exponential of the PAVA-projected scalars yields the final positive scaling factors for both weights and biases, ensuring rare classes receive larger norms to compensate for representation underfitting. This eliminates all continuous regularization hyperparameters, replacing them with a single categorical choice of order metric. The paper further provides a class-conditional distribution analysis showing that reducing a class's weight norm performs a power-law contraction on its implicit distribution, proving that small norms for rare classes reflect underfitting rather than overfitting.

## Results

On CIFAR100-LT (IF=100), GLMC+SAMN achieves 57.7% accuracy, outperforming the previous best (GLMC alone at 56.8%) and all other baselines. On CIFAR10-LT (IF=100), GLMC+SAMN reaches 88.5% accuracy. On the large-scale ImageNet-LT benchmark, GLMC+SAMN attains 57.7% overall accuracy (vs. 56.3% for GLMC alone), and on iNaturalist2018 it achieves 72.7% overall accuracy (vs. 72.2% for GLMC). The largest single improvement from adding SAMN is 6.6% (CE baseline on CIFAR100-LT, IF=100), demonstrating that the benefit is most pronounced under high imbalance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLMC+SAMN achieves the best accuracy across all six CIFAR-LT settings in Table 1, beating the strongest prior method (GLMC alone) by 1.1–1.8% on CIFAR100-LT and 0.1–0.5% on CIFAR10-LT. When plugged into the simpler CE baseline, SAMN yields the largest absolute gains (up to 6.6% on CIFAR100-LT IF=100), surpassing all 22 baselines including WD+MaxNorm (53.4%), IWB (53.3%), and SLAS (52.7%). On ImageNet-LT, GLMC+SAMN reaches 57.7% overall accuracy, exceeding IWB (55.2%), WD+MaxNorm (53.9%), and GLMC alone (56.3%); on iNaturalist2018 it achieves 72.7% overall, edging out GLMC (72.2%) and IWB (71.5%). The paper also demonstrates that SAMN is hyperparameter-friendly: while Weight Decay and SLAS show 4.1% and 5.6% accuracy swings across their hyperparameter ranges, SAMN maintains stable performance without any continuous hyperparameter tuning. SAMN consistently improves the base method it is applied to (CE, SLAS, or GLMC), confirming its role as a universal plug-and-play strategy rather than a standalone method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage decoupling paradigm for long-tailed recognition, specifically targeting the classifier retraining stage where per-class weight norm rescaling has become a central technique. It builds directly on the norm-rescaling lineage from cRT (τ-normalization), weight balancing (WD+MaxNorm), Class-Balanced Regularization, SLAS, and GLMC, all of which introduce sensitive hyperparameters via parameter regularization. The key departure is twofold: a class-conditional distribution analysis that resolves the underfitting-vs-overfitting debate in favor of underfitting, and the repurposing of the Pool Adjacent Violators Algorithm (a classical isotonic regression tool from statistics) to enforce monotonicity on weight norms without any regularization hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMN offers a practical, drop-in strategy for LTR practitioners who struggle with the hyperparameter sensitivity of existing norm rescaling methods, and its universality (plugging into CE, SLAS, GLMC) makes it broadly applicable. The theoretical resolution of the underfitting debate strengthens the conceptual foundation for why norm rescaling works. Open questions include whether the monotonicity assumption (rare classes should always have larger norms) holds in all regimes, whether the slight 'Many'-class accuracy drop can be eliminated, and whether the approach transfers to non-CNN backbones or non-vision modalities. The order metric, while easier to set than continuous hyperparameters, remains a categorical choice that could be further automated.
