## Problem

Existing Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) for vision models implicitly assume honest query inputs, leaving their adversarial robustness entirely unexplored. No prior work has examined whether an adversary can imperceptibly perturb a non-member image to fabricate membership and thereby subvert privacy audits, copyright verification, or right-to-be-forgotten compliance checks. This paper formalizes this previously overlooked threat surface as adversarial membership manipulation and provides the first unified analysis of its mechanism and mitigation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-member image (x, y) with its true label and a white-box trained classifier f, operating within an ℓ∞ perturbation ball of radius ε (typically 2/255 to 8/255, i.e., imperceptible to the human eye). It produces three outputs: (1) a perturbed image x′ = x + δ that maximizes model confidence p_y to fabricate membership, (2) a binary detection decision distinguishing fabricated from true members via input-gradient-norm thresholding, and (3) a robust membership prediction that jointly rejects non-members and fabricated members. White-box access is required to compute input gradients ∇_x ℓ(f(x), y) for both the attack and the detection/robustness components.

## Method

The framework unifies three components around a single geometric insight: adversarial membership fabrication drives non-members into high-confidence, low-gradient basins, producing a characteristic gradient-norm collapse trajectory that separates fabricated from true members even when their semantic embeddings are nearly identical (verified via t-SNE). The Member Fabrication Attack (MFA) inverts PGD by performing confidence ascent—maximizing p_y within the ε-ball—using a momentum-based cosine-annealed step-size schedule for stable optimization. The Member Fabrication Detection (MFD) leverages the input gradient norm ‖∇_x ℓ(f(x),y)‖ as a detection statistic, supported by a local theorem showing gradient-norm decrease under small-step fabrication. The Adversarially Robust MIA (AR-MIA) embeds robustness directly into inference by weighting the existing MIA statistic S(x,y) with w(x,y) = tanh(λ·‖∇_x ℓ(f(x),y)‖), where the tanh prevents non-members with naturally large gradient norms from skewing the weighted decision, yielding the rule 1[w(x,y)·S(x,y) > τ].

## Results

The proposed MFA (cosine-annealed momentum ascent) achieves the highest Error Area and EER across all four datasets, indicating the strongest ability to fabricate membership and deceive MIAs. On CIFAR-10, MFA reaches 0.9451 Error Area and 87.60% EER, outperforming inverted-PGD (0.9399 / 86.75%) and inverted-AutoAttack (0.9369 / 86.30%). On the harder CIFAR-100, the gap widens: MFA achieves 0.6136 Error Area and 59.80% EER versus 0.5917 / 58.05% for inverted-PGD. For detection, the gradient-norm statistic (MFD) provides substantially better separation between fabricated and true members than Mahalanobis distance or LID, and the AR-MIA framework (gradient-norm-weighted LiRA, Attack R, RMIA) consistently improves AUC over the unweighted baselines across CIFAR-10, SVHN, and CINIC-10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against inverted-PGD [39], the proposed MFA wins on all four datasets for both Error Area and EER, with the largest margin on CIFAR-100 (+0.0219 EA, +1.75 pp EER) where the fixed-step PGD inversion is most unstable. Against inverted-AutoAttack [13], MFA also dominates across all datasets, though the gap is smaller on SVHN (0.9618 vs. 0.9560 EA) where AutoAttack's halving heuristic happens to be more competitive. For detection, the gradient-norm statistic clearly outperforms both Mahalanobis distance [31] and LID [36] (Figure 6): the distributions of fabricated and true members overlap heavily under Mahalanobis and LID, while the gradient-norm histogram shows a pronounced separation with fabricated members concentrated at lower norms. The AR-MIA framework further demonstrates that incorporating the tanh-weighted gradient norm into LiRA, Attack R, and RMIA consistently shifts the ROC curve toward the upper-left corner compared to the unweighted versions, confirming that the geometric signal adds discriminative power beyond what semantic confidence alone provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the optimization direction of PGD (Madry et al., ICLR 2018) to perform confidence ascent rather than descent, repurposing the adversarial attack machinery for a fundamentally different objective: fabricating membership rather than causing misclassification. It extends three strong MIAs—LiRA (Carlini et al., IEEE S&P 2022), Attack R (Ye et al., ACM CCS 2022), and RMIA (Zarifzadeh et al., ICML 2024)—by multiplying their test statistics with a gradient-norm weight, creating the first adversarially robust MIA variants. Conceptually, the paper departs from the adversarial detection literature (Mahalanobis distance, LID) by showing that semantic-feature-based detectors fail for fabricated members, and instead identifies a gradient-geometry signal (gradient-norm collapse) as the distinguishing fingerprint, supported by a local Taylor-expansion theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reframes membership inference auditing as an adversarially vulnerable process with direct implications for copyright verification, dataset provenance tracking, and right-to-be-forgotten compliance—domains where a manipulated MIA verdict could have legal consequences. The gradient-norm collapse discovery provides a principled, architecture-agnostic detection signal that is resilient to adaptive attacks, opening a new research direction at the intersection of adversarial robustness and privacy auditing. Open questions include whether the gradient-norm signature persists for transformer-based vision models, in black-box or federated settings, and whether the tanh-weighted AR-MIA formulation generalizes beyond the four MIA families tested. The paper is limited to white-box access for detection and robust inference, which constrains immediate deployability in API-only auditing scenarios.
