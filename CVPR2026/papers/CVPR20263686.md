## Problem

Existing spatial gene expression prediction methods crop fixed-size spots (typically >100 μm) from histopathology images and map each crop to a single expression vector, inherently losing sub-spot spatial resolution and tying the model to one spatial scale. This makes them unable to generalize to the much finer 2 μm spots provided by emerging technologies like Visium HD, where a spot corresponds to only ~20 pixels in image space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263686/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full histopathology slide image (H×W×3 RGB) as input and produces a dense gene expression map G ∈ R^{H×W×M} with one expression value per pixel per gene. Spot-level predictions are derived post-hoc by summing G over a circular region of arbitrary radius centered at the spot location, meaning the same trained model can predict expression for spots of any size or position at test time without retraining. During training, supervision is applied only at the sparse set of spots with measured expression, not pixel-wise.

## Method

PixNet encodes the slide image with the UNI2-h pathology foundation model to extract L-level pyramidal feature maps, then progressively decodes them into a high-resolution gene expression map using a U-Net-style decoder with Separable Attention Fusion Blocks (SAFBs) that concatenate refined encoder features with upsampled decoder features and apply attention-based fusion. Deep decoder stages use depth-to-space upsampling to preserve spatial information without learned interpolation, while shallow stages use bilinear interpolation to recover fine spatial detail. Training employs a sparse loss combining MSE and batch-wise Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) computed only on aggregated spot values, accommodating the inherent sparsity of spatial transcriptomics data. The key architectural insight is that generating a continuous expression map and aggregating within spots decouples the prediction resolution from the training spot size, enabling zero-shot generalization across spatial scales.

## Results

On the Breast Cancer Visium HD dataset, PixNet achieves MSE 0.153, MAE 0.274, and PCC@M 0.325, outperforming the previous best method SGN (MSE 0.230, PCC@M 0.226) by 43.8% in PCC@M. On the Brain Cancer Visium HD dataset, PixNet reaches PCC@M 0.304 versus SGN's 0.195. On the STNet dataset, PixNet attains PCC@M 0.409 (vs. TRIPLEX 0.352), and on Her2ST it achieves PCC@M 0.453 (vs. TRIPLEX 0.404), representing a 12.1% improvement. PixNet also generalizes to unseen spot sizes (2 μm, 8 μm, 16 μm) when trained only on 100 μm spots, achieving PCC@M 0.198–0.226 versus 0.118–0.139 for the next-best method SGN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixNet outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and every reported metric (MSE, MAE, PCC@F, PCC@S, PCC@M). The closest competitor on the Visium HD datasets is SGN [36], which PixNet surpasses by 0.099 in MSE and 0.099 in PCC@M on Breast Cancer (0.325 vs. 0.226) and by 0.084 in MSE and 0.109 in PCC@M on Brain Cancer (0.304 vs. 0.195). On the 100 μm-spot datasets (STNet and Her2ST), the strongest baseline is TRIPLEX [8] (PCC@M 0.352 and 0.404 respectively), which PixNet exceeds by 0.057 and 0.049 in PCC@M. Notably, PixNet also wins the cross-scale generalization test (Table 5): when all methods are trained on 100 μm spots and tested at 2 μm, 8 μm, and 16 μm, PixNet achieves PCC@M of 0.198–0.226, while the next-best SGN reaches only 0.118–0.136, confirming that the dense-prediction formulation transfers to unseen spot geometries. Methods relying on fixed-size spot crops (STNet, HistoGene, EGN, EGGN, BLEEP, BG-TRIPLEX) show the largest degradation at 2 μm, with PCC@M dropping to 0.087–0.115, whereas PixNet's advantage widens at finer scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixNet departs from the dominant spot-level regression paradigm in spatial gene expression prediction (STNet, HistoGene, EGN, TRIPLEX, MERGE, SGN, etc.), where fixed-size crops are mapped to single expression profiles, by reformulating the problem as dense pixel-level prediction followed by spatial aggregation. Conceptually, it repurposes the U-Net-style encoder-decoder architecture and pyramidal feature extraction well established in medical image segmentation (H-DenseFormer, U-Netmer) and super-resolution (Deform-Mamba), adapting them to the sparse-supervision setting of spatial transcriptomics. The closest prior work, iStar, also produces pixel-wise gene expression maps but frames the problem as super-resolution from coarse to fine data (an ill-posed task); PixNet instead predicts directly from histology at native resolution, avoiding that ill-posedness while retaining multi-scale flexibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to predict gene expression at arbitrary spot sizes (2 μm, 8 μm, 16 μm, 100 μm) from a single model trained on one scale is practically significant for emerging platforms like Visium HD, where prior methods trained on 100 μm spots simply cannot operate. The sparse supervision strategy—applying loss only at measured spots while producing a full dense map—offers a template for other partially-observed spatial prediction tasks. However, absolute PCC values remain modest (0.2–0.45), and the method still requires measured gene expression for training; the authors acknowledge that self-supervised or unsupervised pretraining on large-scale histopathology data is an important open direction. Whether the dense-prediction formulation scales to whole-slide (gigapixel) images without tiling artifacts also remains unaddressed.
