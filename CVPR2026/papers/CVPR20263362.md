## Problem

Existing methods for learning latent motion from internet videos rely on discrete VQ-VAE representations with small codebooks (e.g., 8–16 codes) to prevent shortcut learning, but this causes significant information loss and creates an inherent distribution mismatch with continuous robot actions, hindering unified policy co-training. The paper asks whether continuous latent motion can be learned purely self-supervised from action-less videos while still avoiding the model collapse into background memorization that plagues naive continuous approaches.

## Input / Output

The IDM consumes a video frame pair (current O_t and future O_{t+n}) but critically does NOT feed the future frame features directly to the encoder; instead it receives only the current frame ViT tokens concatenated with the element-wise temporal difference D_t = F_{t+n} − F_t. The FDM takes the pooled latent motion embedding plus a linear patch embedding of O_t to reconstruct O_{t+n} at pixel level. For unified policy training, the continuous latent motion z_t (dimension scalable up to 512) is paired with robot observations to form pseudo trajectories, which are co-trained with real (observation, action) pairs through separate lightweight heads in either a diffusion or autoregressive policy.

## Method

CoMo introduces two synergistic mechanisms to enable VQ-free continuous latent motion learning. First, an early temporal difference (Td) strategy replaces direct future-frame encoding with token-level feature subtraction (D_t = F_{t+n} − F_t) computed before the Motion Qformer, and explicitly removes F_{t+n} from the encoder input, so the model sees only [F_t, D_t]; this amplifies motion signals while raising the difficulty of shortcut learning. Second, a temporal contrastive learning (Tcl) scheme constructs positive pairs from slightly offset future frames (Z_{t,t+n+δ}, Z_{t,t+n}, δ ∈ [−n/5, n/5]) and negative pairs from temporally reversed directions (Z_{t,t+n+δ}, Z_{t+n,t}), optimized via InfoNCE to enforce structured, foreground-focused representations. The two mechanisms are complementary: Td alone leaves sparse, noisy representations that degrade at higher dimensions, while Tcl alone risks capturing only object identity rather than motion dynamics. The FDM decoder reconstructs future frames from the current frame plus pooled motion embedding, and the entire system is trained with a weighted combination of InfoNCE, reconstruction, and perceptual losses. Because both the latent motion and robot actions are continuous, the unified policy simply allocates separate lightweight prediction heads, eliminating multi-stage pretraining or two-stage motion-before-action pipelines.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (10 robot demos per task), CoMo achieves an average success rate of 80.1%, outperforming the strongest baseline GR00T (75.9%) by 4.2 points and the no-video baseline DP (70.4%) by 9.7 points. On CALVIN (ABC→D), CoMo's unified auto-regressive policy reaches an average task completion of 3.070, surpassing the discrete latent motion baseline Moto (2.255) by 0.815. In real-world Franka experiments, CoMo achieves the highest success rates on 4 out of 5 tasks, with the largest gains on fine-grained manipulation (e.g., 60% on Open vs. 40% for discrete, 35% on Insert vs. 10% for discrete).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMo outperforms all four compared baselines on the LIBERO average success rate. Against GR00T [7] (discrete pre-quantized latent motion, the strongest baseline at 75.9%), CoMo gains 4.2 points, with the largest per-suite margin on LIBERO-Object (+13.7) and LIBERO-Long (+2.3). Against GR2-like [13] (future frame visual features as predictive signal, 73.8%), CoMo leads by 6.3 points, showing that continuous latent motion is a more informative predictive signal than raw future visual features. Against Dynamo [19] (covariance-regularized latent motion, 73.7%), CoMo wins by 6.4 points, indicating that the Td+Tcl mechanism is more effective than covariance regularization at suppressing shortcut learning. Against ATM [66] (2D point trajectory prediction, 65.7%), CoMo leads by 14.4 points, though ATM's weakness is concentrated on LIBERO-Long (44.0 vs. 62.0), suggesting point trajectories struggle with long-horizon tasks. On CALVIN, CoMo (3.070) also surpasses the discrete Moto baseline (2.255) by 0.815 in average task completion, confirming the advantage of continuous latent motion in auto-regressive policy architectures as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoMo builds directly on the inverse dynamics encoder–forward dynamics decoder paradigm established by Moto-GPT [17] and LAPA [73], which extract discrete latent motion via VQ-VAE to suppress shortcut learning. The key departure is removing vector quantization entirely and replacing it with two complementary mechanisms: an early temporal difference operation (inspired by temporal difference networks [63] in video understanding) that amplifies motion cues by feeding feature differences rather than raw future-frame features into the encoder, and a temporal contrastive learning objective (InfoNCE) that enforces structured, foreground-focused representations. This positions CoMo as a continuous-representation alternative to the discrete latent motion line (LAPA, GR00T, Moto-GPT) while also distinguishing itself from supervised continuous latent action models (CLAM [41], [54]) that require robot action labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that continuous latent motion can be learned purely self-supervised without VQ-VAE, CoMo removes a representational bottleneck that limited prior methods to coarse, quantized motion tokens, and the consistent continuous distribution between latent motion and robot action eliminates the need for multi-stage pretraining or two-stage motion-before-action pipelines. The practical impact is a more scalable, data-efficient paradigm for co-training unified policies on large-scale action-less video. However, the authors note that policy gains remain modest in some tasks, and the approach is most advantageous for fine-grained manipulation; extending to multi-frame, multi-view settings to handle occlusions and pushing to larger, more complex real-world benchmarks remain open challenges.
