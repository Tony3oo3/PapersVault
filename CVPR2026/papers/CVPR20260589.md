## Problem

Existing video diffusion models lack an explicit intrinsic representation space, meaning they cannot independently control physical properties like albedo, irradiance, material, and normal without entangling them in pixel-space conditioning. Prior controllable video editing methods (e.g., GenProp, VACE, AnyV2V) inject auxiliary cues directly into pixel space without intrinsic disentanglement, causing property drift and inconsistent edits across frames. No prior work provides a closed-loop framework that jointly performs video inverse rendering, intrinsic-conditioned video synthesis, and keyframe-based intrinsic editing in a single end-to-end system.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB video (processed at 832×480) and one or more user-edited keyframes (modified via Photoshop or a text-to-image generator) as input. It first decomposes the video into four intrinsic channels—albedo, normal, material (roughness, metallic, ambient occlusion), and irradiance—then produces an edited video that propagates the keyframe modifications across the full sequence while preserving untouched intrinsic properties. The inverse rendering stage outputs per-frame intrinsic maps, and the forward rendering stage outputs a temporally coherent RGB video conditioned on the interleaved intrinsic sequence and edited keyframe references.

## Method

V-RGBX is built on the WAN 2.1 T2V-1.3B DiT backbone and comprises three stages: an Inverse Renderer (RGB→X) that predicts intrinsic channels via a DiT conditioned on the VAE-encoded video with velocity-prediction loss, an Intrinsic Conditioning Sampler that constructs a single interleaved conditioning sequence by randomly selecting one non-conflicted modality per frame (or an edited modality at keyframes), and a Forward Renderer (X→RGB) that synthesizes the output video from this interleaved sequence plus edited keyframe references. The key architectural contribution is the interleaved conditioning mechanism, which avoids the memory overhead of jointly modeling all intrinsic channels simultaneously while still enabling cross-modality propagation. A Temporal-aware Intrinsic Embedding (TIE) packs per-frame modality one-hot embeddings within each 4-frame latent chunk to preserve both temporal order and modality identity in the compressed latent space. Edited keyframes are injected as additional channel-concatenated reference latents, and classifier-free guidance is applied at inference to balance fidelity and edit consistency.

## Results

On the full RGB→X→RGB cycle consistency task, V-RGBX achieves PSNR 22.57 / SSIM 0.7985 / FVD 367.61 on synthetic Evermotion data and PSNR 17.88 / SSIM 0.7533 / FVD 633.76 on real RealEstate10K data, substantially outperforming both baselines. On the X→RGB generation task, V-RGBX reaches PSNR 22.42, LPIPS 0.1930, and FVD 367.89, compared to RGBX (PSNR 16.53, FVD 1037.15) and DiffusionRenderer (PSNR 12.66, FVD 1015.09). For inverse rendering (RGB→X), V-RGBX attains the best PSNR across all three intrinsic channels (albedo 17.73, normal 21.59, irradiance 19.94) and the lowest LPIPS (0.2406, 0.1407, 0.2187 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-RGBX consistently outperforms both quantitative baselines across all tasks. Against RGBX [52] (frame-wise image decomposition), V-RGBX gains +7.28 dB PSNR and reduces FVD from 1099 to 368 on Evermotion cycle consistency, and +3.48 dB PSNR with FVD dropping from 2083 to 634 on RealEstate10K. Against DiffusionRenderer [29] (video-level inverse/forward rendering), V-RGBX improves PSNR by +10.15 dB (Evermotion) and +5.35 dB (RealEstate10K) while cutting FVD by roughly 65–70%; DiffusionRenderer's slightly higher smoothness score (0.9803 vs 0.9808) is attributed to faded, low-contrast outputs rather than genuine temporal coherence. AnyV2V [25] and VACE [23] are compared only qualitatively in Figures 3–4, where both exhibit property drifting over time and fail to disentangle intrinsic channels (e.g., VACE introduces unintended geometry changes during relighting, AnyV2V shows appearance evolution), whereas V-RGBX maintains stable, physically plausible propagation of single-channel edits.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-RGBX builds on the WAN (HunyuanVideo) diffusion transformer backbone and extends the intrinsic image decomposition line (RGB↔X, IntrinsicEdit, DiffusionRenderer) from the image domain to video. It departs from prior video editing approaches (GenProp, VACE, DaS, AnyV2V) that inject appearance-level or implicit latent cues directly into pixel space without explicit intrinsic disentanglement, by introducing a structured intrinsic editing space (albedo, normal, material, irradiance) with an interleaved conditioning mechanism and temporal-aware intrinsic embedding to propagate keyframe edits across time. The key conceptual departure is treating intrinsic channels as first-class conditioning signals in a unified RGB→X→RGB pipeline rather than as post-hoc decomposition artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end framework that unifies video inverse rendering, intrinsic-conditioned video synthesis, and keyframe-based intrinsic editing, enabling physically grounded operations like relighting and material swapping that prior appearance-level methods cannot achieve without unintended cross-channel artifacts. However, the framework is currently limited to indoor synthetic training data, samples exactly one intrinsic modality per frame (restricting multi-attribute edits), and depends on a fixed-length pretrained backbone, leaving open questions about outdoor generalization, longer video sequences, and real-time applicability. The interleaved conditioning strategy is a lightweight, extensible design choice that could inform future controllable video generation beyond intrinsic properties.
