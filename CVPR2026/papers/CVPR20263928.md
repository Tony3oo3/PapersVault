## Problem

Existing guided depth map super-resolution (GDSR) methods face a fundamental trade-off: deep learning approaches achieve high accuracy but are black-box and uninterpretable, while graph-optimization-based methods offer theoretical clarity but are computationally prohibitive (O(H³W³) for fully connected graphs) or limited to short-range structures (sparse local graphs). Additionally, many graph-based methods flatten 2D depth maps into 1D vectors, disrupting the natural row-column spatial topology and causing geometric detail loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263928/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution depth map (e.g., 200×200 from PMD CamCube or 512×424 from Kinect v2) and a spatially aligned high-resolution RGB color image as input. The LR depth is first upsampled via bicubic interpolation to match the HR resolution before entering the network. The output is a single-channel HR depth map at the same spatial resolution as the guidance image, supporting arbitrary upscaling factors (4×, 8×, 16×) and input sizes without architectural modification.

## Method

LapNet formulates a dual graph Laplacian prior that independently constructs row-wise (S_r ∈ R^{H×H}) and column-wise (S_c ∈ R^{W×W}) affinity graphs from the current depth estimate, reducing computational complexity from O(H³W³) to O(H³+W³) while explicitly enforcing piecewise smoothness along both spatial axes. A lightweight U-shaped proximal network serves as a deep implicit prior, extracting high-frequency structural cues from the guidance image to complement the handcrafted graph regularization. These components are unified in a variational objective solved via ADMM alternating minimization over three variables (X, H, J), with closed-form updates for X and H and a learned proximal operator for J. The iterative optimization is unfolded into a 3-stage deep network with non-shared parameters per stage, dynamic L2-based graph reconstruction at each iteration, and a fine-grained feature propagation strategy that feeds previous-stage decoder features into the current encoder to reduce information loss across iterations.

## Results

On the NYU v2 dataset, LapNet achieves RMSE of 1.05 (4×), 2.33 (8×), and 4.55 (16×), outperforming the second-best method SGNet (1.10, 2.44, 4.77) across all upscaling factors. Averaged over six benchmark datasets (NYU v2, Sintel, DIDOE, SUN RGB-D, RGB-D-D, DIML), LapNet records 1.64 / 2.82 / 4.39 RMSE for 4×/8×/16×, the best in every column. Notably, LapNet achieves this with only 3.84M parameters, roughly 6.6× fewer than SGNet (25.33M), while also reporting lower inference latency on an RTX 5090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LapNet achieves the lowest RMSE across all six datasets and all three upscaling factors (4×, 8×, 16×), outperforming every one of the 16 baselines. The closest competitor is SGNet (AAAI 2024), which trails by 0.06 / 0.10 / 0.13 RMSE on the average column (1.70 vs 1.64 at 4×, 2.92 vs 2.82 at 8×, 4.52 vs 4.39 at 16×); on NYU v2 16× the gap widens to 4.77 vs 4.55. Among graph-based methods, LGR (CVPR 2022) and DADA (CVPR 2023) are the most relevant prior work but lag substantially (e.g., 5.20 vs 4.39 average at 16×). On the real-world RGB-D-D dataset, LapNet also leads all methods, with the largest margin over DORNet (2.45 vs 3.35 at 16×). A key practical advantage is model size: LapNet uses only 3.84M parameters versus 25.33M for SGNet, a 6.6× reduction, while also reporting lower inference latency on an RTX 5090 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm for guided depth super-resolution by replacing the conventional global pixel-wise affinity graph (as in de Lutio et al., CVPR 2022) with a dual row/column graph Laplacian that decomposes the HW×HW similarity structure into two compact H×H and W×W matrices. It also borrows the fine-grained feature propagation strategy from Zhou et al. (IJCV 2023) to mitigate information loss across unfolding stages. The key conceptual departure is that prior graph-based unfolding methods either incur O(H³W³) cost for fully connected graphs or sacrifice long-range structure with local sparse graphs; LapNet's factorization along the two spatial axes preserves 2D topology while achieving O(H³+W³) complexity, and it couples this explicit prior with a learnable proximal network as a complementary implicit prior within a single ADMM-solved variational objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the parameter efficiency: LapNet achieves SOTA RMSE with only 3.84M parameters versus 25.33M for the prior best (SGNet), making it attractive for deployment on resource-constrained RGB-D pipelines. The interpretable, stage-wise structure (each unfolding step corresponds to a named optimization subproblem) offers a template for other inverse problems where graph regularization is desired but global graph construction is prohibitive. Open questions include whether the row/column factorization is the optimal decomposition for arbitrary 2D structure (e.g., diagonal or anisotropic textures), how the method scales to very high resolutions where even H×H or W×W matrices become large, and whether the fixed three-iteration budget generalizes to extreme upscaling factors beyond 16×.
