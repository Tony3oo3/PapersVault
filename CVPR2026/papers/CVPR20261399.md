## Problem

In-context segmentation frameworks (e.g., PerSAM-F, Matcher) auto-generate prompts via support-query similarity, but visual inconsistencies between support and query images produce false-positive, ambiguous, or insufficient prompts that degrade SAM's mask quality. No existing mechanism refines these sub-optimal prompts at test time without additional training, architectural changes, or curated data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261399/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a support image with a rough object mask and a query image, both encoded by a shared image encoder (SAM ViT-H or DINOv2 ViT-L/14 depending on the baseline). It produces a final segmentation mask selected from T=5 candidate masks, along with refined point prompts at each iteration. The refined query embedding is used only for prompt resampling, while mask decoding always uses the original stable embedding z_0^q to avoid instability from direct feature modification.

## Method

PR-MaGIC formulates prompt refinement as an entropy-regularized KL-divergence gradient flow in the query embedding space, where SAM's mask decoder serves as a lightweight discriminator D_φ whose logit d_φ approximates the log-density ratio between the candidate and target embedding distributions. At each iteration, the query embedding is updated via an Euler-Maruyama step (Eq. 12) using gradients from the mask decoder, the similarity map is recomputed, and new prompts are resampled; the mask is then decoded from the original embedding with the new prompts. A top-1 support-query similarity selection over all T candidate masks provides robustness against step-size sensitivity and convergence failures when the proximity assumption (initial embedding near decoder-optimal) is violated. The paper proves exponential convergence under the proximity assumption and validates the approach as a plug-and-play module on six benchmarks for both semantic and part segmentation.

## Results

PR-MaGIC is evaluated as a training-free, plug-and-play refinement module on six datasets across semantic and part segmentation, reporting mIoU with Top-1 selection (T) and Oracle selection (O*) over T=5 iterations. For PerSAM-F, the largest gain is +8.8%p on FSS-1000 (58.41→67.19) and +3.2%p on DIS5K (46.82→49.99). For Matcher (1-shot), gains reach +8.4%p on DIS5K (46.65→55.08), +3.8%p on PACO-Part (50.27→54.08), and +3.5%p on Pascal-Part (54.76→58.28). Oracle selection further lifts results by 2–5%p over Top-1, indicating substantial headroom in the candidate mask set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PR-MaGIC is compared against two auto-prompting baselines: PerSAM-F (SAM-based, fine-tuned) and Matcher (DINOv2 + SAM, training-free). Against PerSAM-F, PR-MaGIC achieves consistent mIoU gains on all six datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on FSS-1000 (+8.8%p, 58.41→67.19) and substantial gains on part segmentation (DIS5K +3.2%p, Pascal +1.2%p, PACO +1.1%p). Against Matcher (1-shot), PR-MaGIC wins on 5 of 6 datasets, with the largest gains on part segmentation (DIS5K +8.4%p, PACO +3.8%p, Pascal +3.5%p) and moderate gains on semantic tasks (COCO +1.7%p, LVIS +2.1%p); the sole exception is FSS-1000 where Matcher's baseline is already near-saturated (92.08→92.06, a negligible −0.02%p). Against Matcher (5-shot), PR-MaGIC improves on all five available datasets (COCO +3.05%p, LVIS +3.65%p, PACO +4.49%p, Pascal +4.73%p), confirming that the refinement benefit persists even with more support examples. Overall, PR-MaGIC never degrades performance beyond a trivial margin and delivers the largest relative gains when the baseline prompt quality is weakest (PerSAM-F, part segmentation).

## Relation to Prior Work

PR-MaGIC repurposes the entropy-regularized KL gradient flow from discriminator-based generative model refinement (Ansari et al., ICLR 2021) by substituting SAM's mask decoder as a lightweight proxy for the density-ratio discriminator, enabling a training-free update rule in the query embedding space. It positions itself as a plug-and-play refinement layer atop existing in-context segmentation frameworks (PerSAM-F, Matcher), departing from both fine-tuning variants of SAM (HQSAM, SAM-Adapter, MobileSAM) that require extra data and architectural changes, and from heuristic prompt-refinement strategies (cascaded refinement, robust sampling) that operate in prompt space rather than embedding space. The key conceptual departure is treating the mask decoder's logit as a sufficient statistic for the gradient flow, avoiding any dedicated discriminator training while retaining the Fokker-Planck / Euler-Maruyama simulation structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic nature of PR-MaGIC makes it immediately applicable to any SAM-based auto-prompting pipeline without retraining, which is practically valuable given the rapid proliferation of VFM-based segmentation tools. However, the sensitivity analysis honestly reveals that the proximity assumption underlying the convergence guarantee is fragile in practice—mIoU trajectories are non-monotonic and step-size-sensitive—meaning the top-1 similarity selection is not merely a convenience but a necessary safeguard. Open questions include whether the gradient flow can be made more robust (e.g., adaptive step sizes, better density-ratio approximations) to reduce reliance on the selection heuristic, and whether the method generalizes beyond SAM's specific decoder architecture to other promptable segmenters.
