## Problem

Existing transferable adversarial attacks on vision-language pre-training (VLP) models rely on static cross-modal interactions (one or two fixed-direction updates) and optimize only against positive image-text pairs, neglecting negative samples that shape semantic decision boundaries. This produces adversarial examples that drift along a single direction in embedding space, yielding limited cross-modal disruption and poor transferability across models and tasks. Additionally, input transformation strategies proven effective in traditional image attacks remain largely unexploited in the VLP setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a benign image-text pair (v, t) along with M paired text captions for the image and K randomly sampled negative image-text pairs from the dataset. It produces an adversarial image v' constrained to an l∞ ball of radius εv=8/255 and an adversarial text t' generated via BERT-Attack with a perturbation budget of εt=1 word substitution drawn from a 10-word candidate vocabulary. The adversarial image is optimized with momentum-based iterative updates (I=5 outer dynamic-interaction rounds × J=10 inner PGD steps), while the adversarial text is updated once per outer round.

## Method

SADCA introduces a dynamic contrastive interaction mechanism that iteratively alternates between updating the adversarial image and adversarial text, so that gradient directions are recalibrated at each step based on the current semantic states of both modalities. A contrastive loss simultaneously minimizes cosine similarity between the adversarial sample and positive pairs (a semantically centered image v_p aligned to M captions, plus the original text set) while maximizing similarity to K randomly selected negative pairs weighted by λ=0.2, creating both repulsive and attractive forces that push adversarial examples across semantic boundaries. A semantic augmentation module further diversifies the optimization landscape: local semantic image augmentation generates S=10 views via random cropping (ratio r~U(0.4,0.8)), resizing, and stochastic operations (rotation, brightness, flip), while mixed semantic text augmentation concatenates random pairs of adversarial captions to broaden the textual semantic space. The image update uses momentum-accumulated sign gradients (μ=1.0, α=2/255) over the augmented views, and the text update minimizes a combined contrastive loss against both the current adversarial image and the centered positive image.

## Results

On the Flickr30K image-text retrieval task, SADCA achieves the highest average ASR@R1 across all four source VLP models. When generated from CLIPViT, SADCA reaches 90.68% (TR) and 91.55% (IR) average ASR, surpassing the strongest baseline SA-AET(LI)+SIA by 6.30% and 4.70% respectively. From CLIPCNN, the gap widens to 9.66% (TR) and 6.83% (IR) over SA-AET(LI)+SIA. In cross-task transfer (ITR→VG and ITR→IC), SADCA causes the largest performance drops, e.g., reducing CIDEr from 133.3 (clean) to 50.3 on MSCOCO captioning, compared to 65.7 for SA-AET.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADCA consistently outperforms all six baselines across every source-to-target transfer setting. Against the strongest baseline SA-AET [7] (and its enhanced variant SA-AET(LI)+SIA), SADCA gains 4.5–9.7% in average ASR depending on the source model, with the largest margin observed when transferring from CLIPCNN (e.g., 60.71% vs. 51.05% TR). DRA [2] and SGA [18] are mid-tier baselines; SADCA surpasses them by roughly 20–30% in average ASR. The unimodal baselines PGD [19] and BERT-Attack [12] achieve the lowest transferability (single-digit to low-20% ASR), confirming that joint multimodal perturbation is essential. Co-Attack [40], the earliest cooperative multimodal attack, remains well below the set-level and trajectory-based methods, with SADCA exceeding it by 25–75% in average ASR. In cross-task transfer (Table 2), SADCA also leads: it drops VG TestB from 46.25 (clean) to 37.10 and CIDEr from 133.3 to 50.3, outperforming SA-AET (38.58 and 65.7) and SGA (40.66 and 91.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transferable vision-language attacks initiated by SGA (Lu et al., ICCV 2023), DRA (Gao et al., ECCV 2024), and SA-AET (Jia et al., TPAMI 2025), which all generate adversarial examples through static cross-modal interactions limited to positive image-text pairs. SADCA departs from this lineage in two key ways: it replaces one-shot or two-shot interactions with an iterative dynamic contrastive mechanism that alternately updates adversarial images and texts, and it introduces negative-sample guidance via a contrastive learning objective that simultaneously repels from positive and attracts toward negative semantic regions. Additionally, it repurposes input transformation strategies (SIA) from traditional image adversarial attacks and extends them with a semantic augmentation module (local crop-resize for images, text concatenation for captions) that was previously absent from VLP attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent transferability gains across four VLP architectures and three downstream tasks (ITR, VG, IC), combined with the demonstration that even frontier LVLMs (GPT-5, Claude-4.5, Gemini-2.0) remain highly vulnerable, underscores that current multimodal models lack robustness against semantically structured adversarial perturbations. The finding that random negative sample selection outperforms similarity-based strategies is a useful practical insight for designing contrastive objectives in adversarial settings. Open questions include whether the dynamic interaction mechanism can be adapted for adversarial training or defense, whether the semantic augmentation generalizes to video-language or audio-language models, and whether the observed LVLM vulnerabilities will persist as these systems incorporate stronger alignment or safety training.
