## Problem

Existing document image restoration methods are designed for single-type degradations, requiring either separate specialized models per degradation type or explicit prior knowledge of which degradation is present. Real-world documents frequently exhibit complex mixed degradations (blur, shadow, text watermark, and seal co-occurring), yet no prior benchmark or unified framework addresses this scenario, and no method can simultaneously identify which degradations are present and restore the image in a single pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261554/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a degraded RGB document image at 1024×1024 resolution and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'determine whether the image contains noise or interference and identify the specific type; then remove blur, seal') as joint input. It produces two outputs: a restored clean document image and a step-by-step textual response that identifies which degradation types are present or absent. During training, two instruction variants are used per sample (no specified types, or randomly specifying 1–4 types to remove), and the model must correctly detect unmentioned degradations as well.

## Method

MMDIR couples a ViTDet vision encoder (producing 256 visual tokens via 16×16 patches and a two-layer convolutional projector) with a frozen Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM that jointly processes concatenated visual, question, and answer token sequences to learn cross-modal alignment. During inference, the LLM autoregressively decodes a degradation-identification answer, and the averaged hidden states of the decoded tokens are multiplied by a learnable scalar W_t and added to the visual feature map as a semantic prompt (GuidedLayer). A NAF-based vision decoder then performs multi-scale reconstruction via iterative PixelShuffle upsampling (×2 per stage) with channel-concatenated feature fusion, yielding the restored image. Training is supervised multi-task: a cross-entropy loss on the degradation-identification text is combined with L1 pixel loss, a masked local L1 loss over degradation regions, and SSIM loss, all optimized jointly over 110k synthetically mixed-degradation images for 80 epochs on two A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the single-degradation benchmarks, MMDIR achieves PSNR 29.03 / SSIM 0.977 / LPIPS 0.0150 / DISTS 0.0233 on BMVC deblurring and PSNR 19.60 / SSIM 0.928 / LPIPS 0.0547 / DISTS 0.0816 on OSR deshadowing, attaining state-of-the-art on LPIPS and DISTS for both tasks. On the newly proposed MixedDoc benchmark (1–4 co-occurring degradations per image), MMDIR reaches PSNR 24.43 / SSIM 0.908 / LPIPS 0.1217 / DISTS 0.1323, outperforming the strongest baseline (DiffUIR at 1024 px) by +2.34 dB PSNR and −0.079 LPIPS. The model also achieves the best OCR character error rate (CER 0.17) and FID (9.07) on MixedDoc while running at 1.51 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BMVC deblurring, MMDIR surpasses the strongest prior method LGA-Doc [34] by +0.08 dB PSNR and substantially on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.0150 vs 0.0169, DISTS 0.0233 vs 0.0470), while trailing by only 0.001 in SSIM. Against the unified DocRes [41] and diffusion-based NAF-DPM [5], MMDIR leads by 1.68 and 1.42 dB PSNR respectively, with large perceptual gains. On OSR deshadowing, MMDIR achieves the best LPIPS (0.0547) and DISTS (0.0816), beating BGSNet [43] and DiffUIR [45] on perceptual quality, though BGSNet retains a PSNR advantage (21.25 vs 19.60) at the cost of over-smoothed outputs. On the MixedDoc benchmark, MMDIR dominates all baselines across every metric: it outperforms DiffUIR*₁₀₂₄ [45] by +2.34 dB PSNR, +0.055 SSIM, −0.079 LPIPS, and −0.074 DISTS, and beats DocDiff*₁₀₂₄ [40] by +3.29 dB PSNR. The OCR and FID results (Table 5) further confirm the advantage, with MMDIR achieving CER 0.17 vs 0.24 for DiffUIR and FID 9.07 vs 21.45, while remaining faster (1.51 s vs 1.80 s) despite a larger parameter count (735M vs 36M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end image-to-image paradigm in document restoration (DocDiff, NAF-DPM, LGA-Doc) and from DocRes's visual-prompt approach, which still requires rigid preprocessing pipelines and prior knowledge of degradation categories. Instead, it repurposes the multimodal LLM architecture popularized in document understanding (DocPedia, DocLayLLM) for restoration, coupling a frozen Qwen2.5 LLM with a ViTDet encoder and NAF-based decoder so that textual instructions drive both degradation identification and semantic guidance for pixel reconstruction. The method also draws on natural-scene unified restoration (DiffUIR, UniRestore) as baselines, transferring them to the document domain for fair comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MixedDoc, the first benchmark with randomized multi-degradation combinations (blur, shadow, watermark, seal), fills a clear data gap and gives the community a realistic evaluation setting that prior single-type datasets cannot capture. The instruction-driven design adds interpretability—users receive an explicit degradation diagnosis alongside the restored image—which is valuable for human-in-the-loop document processing pipelines. However, the 735M-parameter model (dominated by the LLM) is roughly 90× heavier than DocDiff's 8M, raising practical deployment concerns, and the reliance on synthetic mixed degradations limits confidence in real-world generalization. Open questions include whether the LLM component can be replaced by a lighter classifier without losing the mixed-degradation advantage, and whether the framework scales beyond four degradation types.
