## Problem

Most neighbourhood-preserving 3D shape matching methods assume clean meshes with consistent topology, making them inapplicable when source and target shapes differ in genus (e.g., touching hands create topological handles). This paper addresses the gap by proposing a formalism that preserves local neighbourhood structure while remaining robust to topological noise, without sacrificing computational efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262394/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two 3D surface meshes (source and target) that may differ in genus and discretisation, cleaned and downsampled to at most 1k triangles. Each source triangle is matched to a surface element on the target (a single vertex, an edge, or a triangle with vertices at most k=2 edges apart), and the final output is a vertex-to-vertex correspondence extracted by merging per-triangle matchings. The matching is not required to be bijective, and per-vertex features from ULRSSM serve as unary costs.

## Method

The paper casts non-rigid 3D shape matching as a multi-label MRF where each source triangle is assigned a label encoding its correspondence to a surface element on the target. The key pairwise cost measures the geodesic distance (on the target shape only) between the projected shared vertices of two neighbouring triangles, forming a pseudometric that is zero when adjacency is preserved. Because each label belongs to exactly one triangle's disjoint label space, the authors derive a custom α-expansion variant that replaces per-move graph cuts with a linear-time best-label search per triangle, yielding significant speed-ups while retaining approximation guarantees. A concave saturation function is applied to the pairwise cost to limit the influence of topologically-induced geodesic shortcuts, and the overall energy is minimised with λ=100 weighting between ULRSSM-based unaries and pairwise terms.

## Results

The paper evaluates using geodesic errors (normalised by shape diameter) and Dirichlet energies across six datasets. On clean datasets (FAUST, SMAL, DT4D), the proposed MRF method consistently lies on the Pareto front for runtime versus matching accuracy, producing notably smooth correspondences (low Dirichlet energy) attributable to the pairwise cost term. On topologically noisy datasets (SCAPET, TOPKIDS, TOPFAUST), all methods degrade in performance, but the proposed method achieves the highest geodesic accuracy overall. The custom α-expansion variant is faster than standard α-expansion, with only KernelMatch being faster in runtime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On clean datasets (FAUST, SMAL, DT4D), the proposed method is competitive with neighbourhood-preserving methods (SpiderMatch, GeCo, SuPaMatch) in accuracy while being significantly faster, and outperforms ULRSSM in smoothness (Dirichlet energy) due to its explicit pairwise regularisation. On topologically noisy datasets (SCAPET, TOPKIDS, TOPFAUST), SpiderMatch, GeCo, and SuPaMatch are excluded as they become inapplicable when genus changes; among the remaining methods (ULRSSM, SmoothShells, DiscrOpt, Hope, KernelMatch), the proposed MRF method achieves the lowest geodesic error, with the gap being most pronounced on TOPFAUST where all methods degrade substantially. KernelMatch is the fastest baseline but yields less accurate matchings, while Hope handles topological noise but is less accurate. The method's advantage is most clear in the topologically noisy regime, where its non-symmetric pairwise cost (computing geodesic distances only on the target shape) provides robustness that symmetric formulations lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the α-expansion algorithm for multi-label MRFs (Boykov et al., 2002) and repurposes it for 3D shape matching by introducing a triangle-based labelling scheme where each source triangle is assigned a label encoding its correspondence to a surface element on the target. It departs from neighbourhood-preserving methods (SpiderMatch, GeCo, SuPaMatch) that require clean, genus-consistent meshes, and from functional-map-based approaches that rely on globally consistent intrinsic geometry. The closest prior work is Kernel Matching, which also targets topological robustness but uses symmetric heat-kernel distances; this paper's non-symmetric formulation (computing geodesic distances only on the target) and its metric pairwise costs enable both topological robustness and the theoretical approximation guarantees of α-expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topological noise (e.g., touching hands changing genus) is a pervasive real-world artefact that renders many state-of-the-art neighbourhood-preserving matchers inapplicable, yet this paper shows a purely intrinsic MRF formulation can handle it while remaining competitive on clean data. The custom α-expansion variant that exploits the disjoint label-space structure to avoid per-move graph cuts is a practical efficiency win. Open questions include whether the approximation factor (bounded by the target's maximum geodesic distance) can be tightened, whether GPU-accelerated variants (à la FastDOG) can further reduce runtime, and whether the approach extends to partial or multi-shape matching under topological noise.
