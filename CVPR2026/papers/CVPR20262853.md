## Problem

Conventional diffusion transformers use identical modules to both encode low-frequency semantics and decode high-frequency details from noisy inputs, creating an inherent optimization dilemma that slows training convergence. This paper investigates whether explicitly decoupling semantic encoding from detail decoding into separate encoder and decoder components can resolve this tension and accelerate convergence without sacrificing generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262853/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of an off-shelf SD-VAE-f8d4-ft-EMA (downsample factor 8), taking a class label and a noisy latent tensor as input and regressing the velocity field to produce a 256×256 or 512×512 synthetic image. The noisy latent is patchified into continuous tokens, while the timestep and class label are injected as external conditions via AdaLN-Zero. At inference, the model uses a 250-step Euler solver with classifier-free guidance applied over the interval [0.3, 1.0].

## Method

DDT splits the standard decoder-only diffusion transformer into a dedicated condition encoder (extracting semantic self-condition z_t from x_t, t, y) and a velocity decoder (regressing v_t from x_t, t, z_t), both built from interleaved Attention+FFN blocks. The encoder is supervised by a REPA-style DINOv2 representation alignment loss to enforce local consistency of z_t across adjacent timesteps, while the decoder is trained with the standard linear flow-matching loss. A key empirical finding is that an aggressively larger encoder relative to the decoder (e.g., 22 encoder + 6 decoder layers for XL) yields the best performance, with the encoder-to-decoder ratio becoming more skewed as model size grows. As a by-product of the encoder's local consistency, the paper proposes a statistical dynamic programming algorithm to find the optimal set of timesteps at which to recompute z_t, enabling up to 3× inference speedup with negligible FID degradation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance, DDT-XL/2 (22En6De) achieves a state-of-the-art 1.31 FID at 256 epochs and 1.26 FID at 400 epochs, using the off-shelf SD-VAE-f8d4. This represents approximately 4× faster training convergence compared to REPA (1.42 FID at 800 epochs). On ImageNet 512×512, DDT-XL/2 fine-tuned for 500K steps achieves 1.28 FID, outperforming REPA-XL/2 (2.08 FID) by a 0.80 FID margin. The DDT-L/2 model reaches 1.64 FID in just 80 epochs, surpassing MAR-L (1.78 FID at 800 epochs) with 10× fewer training epochs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDT-XL/2 decisively outperforms REPA-XL/2, the strongest prior diffusion transformer baseline, achieving 1.31 FID at 256 epochs versus REPA's 1.42 FID at 800 epochs — a 0.11 FID improvement with 4× fewer training epochs. Against the original DiT-XL (2.27 FID, 1400 epochs) and SiT-XL (2.06 FID, 1400 epochs), DDT-XL/2 improves FID by 0.96 and 0.75 respectively while requiring far fewer epochs. At the L-scale, DDT-L/2 (1.64 FID, 80 epochs) beats MAR-L (1.78 FID, 800 epochs) by 0.14 FID with 10× less training. On ImageNet 512×512, DDT-XL/2 achieves 1.28 FID, surpassing REPA-XL/2 (2.08 FID) by 0.80 FID and SiT-XL/2 (2.62 FID) by 1.34 FID. At the B-scale (400K steps, no CFG), DDT-B/2 (16.32 FID) outperforms Improved-REPA-B/2 (19.1 FID) by 2.78 FID, and DDT-XL/2 (6.62 FID) beats Improved-REPA-XL/2 (8.14 FID) by 1.52 FID, confirming consistent gains across model sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

DDT extends the decoder-only diffusion transformer lineage (DiT, SiT) by introducing an explicit encoder-decoder split, motivated by the observation that these models face an optimization dilemma between low-frequency semantic encoding and high-frequency detail decoding. It incorporates REPA's DINOv2 representation alignment loss into the condition encoder and operates within the linear flow-matching framework of SiT, while drawing on classic CV design principles of prioritizing large encoders over lightweight decoders. The paper departs from prior work by showing that the bottleneck in diffusion transformers is fundamentally in semantic encoding capacity (demonstrated via time-shift experiments), rather than in decoding, and by proposing a statistical dynamic programming approach to optimize encoder sharing for inference, going beyond the uniform sharing strategy of DeepCache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~4× training acceleration (256 epochs vs. 800 for REPA to reach comparable or better FID) directly addresses one of the most significant practical bottlenecks in diffusion transformer research, making iterative model development substantially cheaper. The finding that increasingly aggressive encoder-decoder ratios (up to 22:6) are beneficial as model scale grows provides a concrete scaling guideline that could influence future architecture design beyond ImageNet. However, the evaluation is limited to class-conditional ImageNet generation with an off-shelf VAE, leaving open whether the decoupled design and its optimal ratios transfer to text-to-image, video generation, or other conditioning paradigms. The reliance on DINOv2 alignment as a proxy for semantic supervision also raises questions about how the approach generalizes to domains where strong pretrained visual encoders are unavailable.
