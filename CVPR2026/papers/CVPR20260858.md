## Problem

Existing PEFT-based CZSL methods suffer from two fundamental flaws: composition is constructed only implicitly via token-level prompt concatenation rather than an explicit operation in the embedding space, and imperfect visual disentanglement leaves attribute, object, and composition features mutually contaminated. Together, these issues cause the model to overfit seen compositions and fail to generalize to unseen attribute-object pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder into a visual feature vector. It produces an attribute-object composition label by computing calibrated scores over the candidate composition space (closed-world or open-world protocol). The framework operates entirely within the shared CLIP feature space and is designed as a plug-and-play module that attaches to existing CZSL pipelines (CSP, Troika) without modifying their backbone encoders or prompt structures.

## Method

FlowComposer learns two rectified flow models (attribute and object) that transport visual features toward their corresponding text embeddings via time-conditioned velocity fields, trained with a combined MSE velocity-regression loss and a contrastive endpoint classification loss. A learnable Composer then explicitly fuses the two primitive velocities into a composition velocity by predicting combination coefficients (a, b) supervised by least-squares targets derived from the ground-truth composition velocity, replacing implicit token concatenation with an operational composition rule in the embedding space. To exploit inevitable residual entanglement, a leakage-guided augmentation scheme reuses cross-branch visual features (e.g., object-stream features fed to the attribute flow) as additional transport sources, converting imperfect disentanglement into constructive supervision. At inference, a single-step transport (evaluating velocity at t=0) maps image features to the text space without numerical integration, keeping overhead negligible.

## Results

In the closed-world setting, Troika+FlowComposer achieves state-of-the-art AUC on all three benchmarks: 23.5 on MIT-States, 46.8 on UT-Zappos, and 15.9 on C-GQA, surpassing LLM-augmented methods like LOGICZSL (23.4, 45.8, 15.3). The corresponding HM values are 40.2, 58.6, and 34.0, representing gains of +1.0, +3.2, and +4.3 over the Troika baseline respectively. In the open-world setting, Troika+FlowComposer improves HM by +1.3 (MIT-States), +4.4 (UT-Zappos), and +0.9 (C-GQA) over Troika, while also achieving the best AUC on UT-Zappos (35.5) and C-GQA (3.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowComposer consistently outperforms all 11 compared baselines on HM and AUC across all three datasets in the closed-world setting. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline IMAX [18], Troika+FlowComposer gains +1.1 AUC on MIT-States, +6.2 on UT-Zappos, and +3.1 on C-GQA. Against LLM-augmented methods, it surpasses LOGICZSL [49] on AUC in closed-world (23.5 vs 23.4 on MIT-States, 46.8 vs 45.8 on UT-Zappos, 15.9 vs 15.3 on C-GQA) without requiring external textual knowledge, though it trails LOGICZSL slightly on open-world AUC (7.5 vs 8.7 on MIT-States). Compared to the direct base pipelines, plugging FlowComposer into Troika [15] yields +1.0 to +4.3 HM and +1.4 to +5.0 AUC improvements, while plugging into CSP [40] gives +1.0 to +3.6 HM and +1.1 to +5.4 AUC gains. The method also beats earlier prompt-based approaches (DFSP [35], CoOP [54]) and the raw CLIP [44] baseline by large margins (e.g., +33.0 AUC over CLIP on UT-Zappos), confirming that the flow-matching composition mechanism provides substantial gains over token-level prompt concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the rectified flow formulation (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) from generative modeling into a classification/composition setting, where velocity fields transport visual features toward text embeddings rather than noise toward images. It departs from the dominant PEFT-based CZSL paradigm (CSP, Troika, DFSP) that encodes composition implicitly via token-level prompt concatenation, instead introducing an explicit, learnable composition operation in the embedding space via a Composer that combines primitive velocity fields. The leakage-guided augmentation further reframes the well-known limitation of imperfect visual disentanglement in multi-path CZSL architectures as a source of auxiliary supervision rather than a failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that flow matching is a natural and effective paradigm for CZSL, this work opens a path toward more principled composition mechanisms that go beyond prompt engineering, potentially applicable to other compositional recognition tasks. The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for adoption, and the consistent gains across three benchmarks and two baselines suggest robustness. Open questions include whether the linear combination in the Composer generalizes to more than two primitives or hierarchical compositions, whether the one-step transport approximation remains valid at scale, and whether the leakage-guided augmentation transfers to settings without explicit disentanglers. The paper does not explore computational cost beyond noting 'negligible inference overhead,' leaving the training-time overhead of multiple flow branches unquantified.
