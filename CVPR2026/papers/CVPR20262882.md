## Problem

Image inversion for text-guided editing requires recovering the exact initial noise that a diffusion model would denoise into a given image, but this is theoretically intractable. Existing approaches (DDIM inversion, optimization-based methods, rectified-flow solvers) require 30–50+ steps, accumulate errors along the trajectory, and produce artifacts, making real-time interactive editing impractical. No prior method achieves both high inversion accuracy and single-step speed with modern few-step flow models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262882/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB image, its original text prompt, and an edited text prompt as input. The image is encoded into the latent space of Flux-Schnell, and the inversion network F maps this latent (concatenated with the previous-step reconstruction in multi-step mode) plus the prompt embedding and timestep to a Gaussian noise vector. The output is the inverted noise, which is then fed into the few-step generator with the edited prompt to produce the final edited image; the full inverse-forward pipeline completes in approximately 1 second on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

FlashIn trains a neural network F (initialized from MMDiT blocks of Flux-Schnell) to directly map a clean latent to its seed noise in a single forward pass. The key training insight is to create explicit noise-image pairs by generating latents from random noise with the few-step model, providing ground-truth targets that avoid the weak KL-divergence and implicit reconstruction losses used by prior work. A cycle-consistent loss simultaneously penalizes noise mismatch (‖ε − ε̂‖²) and latent mismatch (‖z₀ − ẑ₀‖²), while an adversarial discriminator trained on real image latents pushes the reconstructed output to preserve fine-grained texture and detail. For multi-step refinement, F's input channels are doubled to accept both the true latent and the previous step's reconstruction, allowing iterative noise correction across timesteps {1.0, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25}. The entire framework is trained on LAION (recaptioned with Qwen-VL) for 100K steps at lr 1e-5, with the generator G kept frozen.

## Results

On PIE-Bench (700 images, 10 editing types), FlashIn achieves the best background preservation on 3 of 4 metrics: PSNR 31.91 (8% above the second-best TurboEdit-Wu at 29.52), LPIPS×10³ 32.11, and MSE×10⁴ 15.51, while ranking second on SSIM×10² (88.76 vs. TurboEdit-Wu's 91.59). For editing fidelity, FlashIn attains the highest CLIP similarity on both whole-image (25.67) and edited-region (23.94) measures. The full inverse–forward pipeline completes in ~1.05 s (0.666 s inverse + 0.382 s forward) on a single A100 GPU, making it the fastest method in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashIn outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR, LPIPS, MSE, and both CLIP similarity metrics, with the largest margins over MasaCtrl (PSNR +9.74) and ExactDPM (PSNR +7.37). The closest competitor is TurboEdit-Wu [32], which FlashIn surpasses by 2.39 dB in PSNR, 12.63 in LPIPS×10³, and 1.60 in edited-region CLIP, while TurboEdit-Wu retains a slight SSIM edge (91.59 vs. 88.76). Compared to the other fast method TurboEdit-Deutch [7], FlashIn improves PSNR by 4.27 dB and LPIPS by 20.20×10³. Against multi-step inversion baselines (DirectInv, Renoise, ExactDPM), FlashIn is 8–23× faster in the inverse step (0.666 s vs. 5.41–15.11 s) while simultaneously achieving better reconstruction quality. In the forward (generation) step, FlashIn is the fastest at 0.382 s, beating even the few-step methods Renoise (0.445 s) and TurboEdit-Wu (0.508 s). Qualitatively, FlashIn preserves backgrounds more faithfully and aligns edits more closely with target prompts than Edit-Friendly [14], Renoise [9], TurboEdit [32], and RF-solver-edit [31].

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashIn extends the line of trainable inversion networks (TurboEdit, SwiftEdit) that map images directly to seed noise, but departs from their reliance on weak constraints (KL divergence and implicit reconstruction loss) by introducing explicit noise-image pairs generated from the same few-step model, enabling cycle-consistent supervision. It also repurposes adversarial training from few-step diffusion distillation (ADD, LADD, SDXL-Lightning) to the inversion setting, a connection not previously explored. The method is built on rectified flow models (Flux-Schnell) rather than traditional DDIM-based schedulers, positioning it alongside recent rectified-flow inversion work (RF-solver-edit, FireFlow) while achieving single-step inversion where those methods still require multi-step trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing inversion to a single forward pass of a learned network, FlashIn makes high-quality text-guided image editing feasible in real time (~1 second on one GPU), a practical threshold that prior methods (30–50 step DDIM inversion, optimization-based approaches) could not meet. The plug-in demonstration with instruction-based editors (Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) suggests the inverted noise can serve as a general anchor for diverse editing pipelines, broadening the method's utility beyond its native few-step generator. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes beyond 512×512 resolution, whether the synthetic-to-real distribution gap is fully resolved by adversarial training at scale, and whether the framework extends cleanly to video as the authors note as future work.
