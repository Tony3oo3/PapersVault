## Problem

Existing event-based motion deblurring methods rely on large-scale, pixel-aligned blur-sharp pairs that require elaborate hardware (beam splitters, precise time synchronization, ND filters, and color calibration) to collect, making them impractical for real-world deployment. This paper addresses the gap by formulating motion deblurring in a fully unpaired setting where no one-to-one correspondence between blurry and sharp images exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single blurry image and its corresponding event stream (represented as a voxel grid with bin size 10) and outputs a deblurred sharp image. During training, it operates on two disjoint unpaired sets: a blur domain (blurry images + events) and a sharp domain (sharp images + events), with no alignment between them. The framework is model-agnostic and has been validated by plugging into three existing backbones (GEM, EFNet, REDNet), all trained from scratch on 256×256 patches with batch size 1.

## Method

EMP bridges the disjoint blur and sharp domains through two complementary event-driven mechanisms. First, an event-based physical prior computes the EDI inverse double-integral factor, which is injected as an auxiliary cue into the deblurring backbone; a per-pixel confidence map derived from event-count and polarity consistency then masks a self-supervision L1 loss so only reliable regions contribute to training. Second, a frequency-domain blur synthesis module extracts low-frequency spectral cues from unpaired blur-event pairs via centered Fourier transforms, predicts a spatial mask and per-channel strength, and modulates the magnitude (while preserving phase) of the sharp image's Fourier representation to generate realistic pseudo-blur. A four-part training pipeline alternates between deblurring (supervised by EDI self-loss or reconstruction loss) and blurring (supervised by adversarial losses against real blur), with cycle-consistency reblurring to stabilize the synthesis model.

## Results

On the unpaired setting, EMP (GEM backbone) achieves PSNR 31.16 / SSIM 0.947 on HighREV, PSNR 35.49 / SSIM 0.957 on REBlur, and PSNR 32.99 / LPIPS 0.139 on EventAid, outperforming all unpaired baselines by a wide margin. Notably, EMP (GEM) surpasses the paired frame-based method FFTformer (30.83 PSNR on HighREV, 35.44 on REBlur, 30.22 on EventAid) across all three datasets, and remains within 0.25 dB of its own paired counterpart GEM (33.24 PSNR on EventAid). The ablation confirms that the self-supervision loss L_self contributes the largest single gain (+5.16 dB over the no-loss baseline on REBlur), while the EDI cue and frequency module each add further 0.3–0.5 dB improvements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unpaired baselines, EMP (GEM) dominates across all three datasets: it beats the strongest unpaired competitor EVDI by 3.80 dB on HighREV (31.16 vs. 27.36), 1.73 dB on REBlur (35.49 vs. 33.76), and 1.94 dB on EventAid (32.99 vs. 31.05), while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.139 vs. 0.293). The frame-based unpaired methods CycleGAN and CUT lag far behind (e.g., 21.34 and 19.90 PSNR on HighREV), and even the stronger Blur2Blur* (27.77 on HighREV, 21.37 on EventAid) is outperformed by 3.4–11.6 dB. Against paired methods, EMP (GEM) surpasses FFTformer on all datasets (31.16 vs. 30.83 on HighREV; 35.49 vs. 35.44 on REBlur; 32.99 vs. 30.22 on EventAid) and also outperforms the paired event-based eSL-Net (27.43 on HighREV, 35.24 on REBlur, 29.89 on EventAid). Compared to its own paired counterpart GEM (32.61 / 37.51 / 33.24), the unpaired EMP (GEM) closes most of the gap, losing only 0.25 dB on EventAid PSNR while gaining 0.35 dB on HighREV. The top paired event-based methods MAT and MAENet still lead on REBlur (38.69 and 38.47 PSNR) and HighREV (33.42 and 33.98), indicating that paired supervision retains an advantage in the most challenging real-blur regime, but EMP narrows this gap to under 3 dB without requiring any aligned blur-sharp pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: event-based motion deblurring (which has relied on paired blur-sharp-event data from beam-splitter rigs) and unpaired image-to-image translation (CycleGAN-style cycle consistency). It repurposes the EDI physical model (Pan et al., CVPR 2019) not as a standalone deblurring tool but as a self-supervisory signal and auxiliary input cue within a learned, adversarially-trained framework, and borrows CycleGAN's cycle-consistency principle to enforce content preservation during blur synthesis. The frequency-domain blur transfer mechanism is a novel component that has no direct predecessor in either the event-deblurring or unpaired-translation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical bottleneck this addresses is real: collecting paired blur-sharp-event data requires beam splitters, precise time synchronization, ND filters, and expert calibration, making it infeasible for most real-world deployments. Demonstrating near-parity with paired training (32.99 vs. 33.24 PSNR on EventAid) using only unpaired data is a meaningful step toward making event-assisted deblurring deployable on consumer devices. Open questions include how the method behaves when event streams are extremely sparse or noisy (beyond what the tested datasets cover), whether the approach extends naturally to video deblurring with temporal consistency, and whether the 1:1 train-split heuristic for constructing unpaired domains is optimal or sensitive to domain gap.
