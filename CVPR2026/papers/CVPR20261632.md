## Problem

Existing multi-agent motion generation methods are restricted to fixed agent counts or pairwise relationships, requiring retraining for new interaction configurations, and they largely neglect precise temporal synchronisation across agents. This leads to timing misalignments in coordinated actions such as handing or receiving objects, even when individual motions appear realistic. The paper tackles the open challenge of scalable, temporally aligned multi-agent interaction without task-specific model redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261632/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural-language instruction (which may reference top-view grid coordinates for spatial grounding, e.g. 'at grid (10,18)') and a 3D scene with object segmentation and spatial relations as input. It produces per-agent 3D motion sequences with aligned interaction timing, along with a structured event dependency graph G=(E,R) that encodes both sequential (causal) and parallel (synchronised) temporal relations among single-actor events. The motion sequences are generated autoregressively in fixed-length segments and refined to enforce global temporal consistency.

## Method

SyncMos is a two-stage, model-agnostic framework that extends a single-agent diffusion model (LINGO) to multi-agent scenarios without retraining. The first stage is an LLM-based dependency-aware story planner that uses chain-of-thought prompting to convert text instructions into a structured event graph with explicit sequential and parallel dependencies, grounded to spatial positions via a top-view grid. The second stage is a temporal synchronisation module with two sub-steps: (1) auto-regressive preliminary estimation, where each agent's motion is generated with partial denoising (stopping at t=30) and intermediate diffusion states are stored in a buffer; and (2) temporally guided refinement, where spline-based motion warping produces a target trajectory, and Diffusion Posterior Sampling with an L2 timing constraint (Eq. 4) steers the reconstruction toward the warped timing while preserving the diffusion prior's motion quality. This decouples planning (what/when) from precise timing alignment (how to synchronise), enabling scalability to arbitrary agent counts.

## Results

On the Dependency test set, the proposed planner improves Dependency Accuracy (DA) from 11.8–20.5% (baseline) to 80.4–96.9% across LLM backbones, with Scenario Pass Rate (SPR) rising from 0% to as high as 80% (GPT-4o). On the Synchronisation test set, DA improves by up to 21.5 percentage points (68.4% → 89.9%, Qwen-3-235B). For temporal synchronisation, the model achieves 75–88% success rate for ±0.5–1.0 s grasp-timing offsets, compared to 0% for the unmodified LINGO backbone, with success dropping to ~37% at ±1.5 s offsets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Event-Driven Storytelling [13], which autoregressively generates events without explicit global dependency modelling. On the Dependency subset (long causal chains), the proposed planner dramatically outperforms the baseline: DA jumps from 11.8–20.5% to 80.4–96.9%, and SPR goes from 0–6.7% to 46.7–80.0% across all four LLM backbones. On the Synchronisation subset (parallel multi-agent actions), the gain is more moderate but still consistent, with DA improving by 2–21.5 p.p. depending on backbone (largest gain: Qwen-3-235B, 68.4% → 89.9%). Token usage analysis further shows the baseline's cost grows rapidly beyond eight events due to redundant regeneration, while the proposed planner remains nearly constant. For temporal synchronisation, the unmodified LINGO backbone achieves 0% success on all timing-offset conditions, whereas the proposed refinement module reaches 75–88% for offsets within ±1.0 s, confirming that the DPS-based constraint-guided denoising is essential for cross-agent timing alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SyncMos builds directly on LINGO [9] as its single-agent diffusion motion backbone and on Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) [6] as the mechanism for constraint-guided refinement, combining them with classical spline-based motion warping [20] to achieve post-hoc temporal alignment. It departs from prior multi-agent motion work—InterGen [12] (fixed two-person joint modelling) and Event-Driven Storytelling [13] (autoregressive event generation without explicit dependency graphs)—by decoupling planning from generation: an LLM-based event planner produces a structured dependency graph, and a separate synchronisation module enforces cross-agent timing without any retraining of the underlying motion model. The key conceptual departure is treating temporal synchronisation as a post-generation refinement problem rather than a joint multi-agent modelling problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for scaling single-agent motion generators to arbitrary group sizes, which is relevant to embodied AI, virtual production, and interactive simulation where retraining for each new agent configuration is infeasible. However, several limitations temper the impact: large timing adjustments (>1.5 s) destabilise the diffusion refinement, the 2D top-view spatial grounding precludes fine-grained 3D contact reasoning, and the entire pipeline inherits the quality ceiling of the LINGO backbone. Open questions include whether the DPS-based synchronisation generalises to non-autoregressive motion generators, whether physics- or contact-aware constraints can be folded into the refinement loop, and whether the LLM planner's dependency accuracy degrades on genuinely ambiguous or long-horizon narratives beyond the 30-scenario benchmark.
