## Problem

Multi-frame Transformer-based trackers leverage historical template frames to capture richer spatio-temporal cues, but this introduces a massive number of visual tokens that imposes quadratic computational cost and, more critically, degrades tracking performance due to visual redundancy. Existing token reduction strategies in related domains (e.g., MLLMs) rely on handcrafted, non-learnable rules that may not align with the tracking objective, leaving a gap for an adaptive, end-to-end learnable compression mechanism tailored to visual tracking.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes 5 historical template frames and a current search region image, both downsampled by a stride-4 convolution and split into 16×16 patches by the Fast-iTPN backbone. The ATC module compresses the template tokens by a configurable keep ratio (default 0.9, retaining ~40% of tokens), and the final prediction head outputs a classification score map, a 2-channel offset map for sub-pixel correction, and normalized bounding box dimensions, all at resolution H_x/P × W_x/P.

## Method

ETCTrack is a compress-then-interact framework built on two synergistic modules. The Adaptive Token Compressor (ATC) first applies a Token Correlation Module (stacked self-attention layers) to model global spatio-temporal relationships across all template tokens, then performs Mask-Guided Token Pruning and Merging: a fixed random projection generates importance scores, tokens are partitioned into a preserved set (high score) and a redundant set (low score), and each redundant token is absorbed into its most cosine-similar preserved token via greedy matching, yielding a compact yet discriminative template representation. The Hierarchical Interaction Block (HIBlock) then performs a three-stage asymmetric interaction: (1) cross-attention with template as query and search as key/value to make the template context-aware, (2) joint processing through stacked backbone blocks for deep feature fusion, and (3) a second cross-attention with search as query and the deeply encoded template as key/value to guide search refinement, followed by a ConvFFN. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with weighted focal loss for classification and L1 + generalized IoU loss for box regression, using a dynamic memory bank at inference to selectively update template frames based on a confidence threshold.

## Results

ETCTrack-B224 achieves 79.2% AO on GOT-10k, 74.9% AUC on LaSOT, 54.6% AUC on LaSOT_ext, 86.0% AUC on TrackingNet, and 61.3% AUC on TNL2K, setting new state-of-the-art on all five benchmarks. The larger ETCTrack-B384 variant further improves to 80.1% AO (GOT-10k), 75.9% AUC (LaSOT), 55.1% AUC (LaSOT_ext), 87.3% AUC (TrackingNet), and 63.0% AUC (TNL2K). On NfS and OTB100, ETCTrack-B224 scores 71.3% and 73.3% AUC respectively, outperforming all compared trackers including those with substantially larger model sizes. The ATC module reduces template tokens by 60%, yielding a 21.4% reduction in MACs with only a 0.4% accuracy drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETCTrack-B224 outperforms the closest same-resolution competitor MCITrack-B224 by 1.3% AO on GOT-10k (79.2 vs. 77.9) and by 0.9% AUC on LaSOT (74.9 vs. 74.0), while also surpassing it on LaSOT_ext (54.6 vs. 52.0) and TNL2K (61.3 vs. 60.3). Among higher-resolution trackers, ETCTrack-B384 leads DreamTrack-B384 by 0.9% AUC on LaSOT (75.9 vs. 75.0), 0.6% on LaSOT_ext (55.1 vs. 54.5), 0.8% on TrackingNet (87.3 vs. 86.5), and 1.8% on TNL2K (63.0 vs. 61.2). On NfS, ETCTrack-B224 (71.3% AUC) beats the strongest baseline ARTrackV2-L384 (68.4%) by a substantial 2.9% margin despite using a much smaller model. On OTB100, ETCTrack-B384 (73.6%) surpasses ODTrack-B384 (72.3%) by 1.3%. Across all seven benchmarks, ETCTrack achieves the top score in every comparison, with the largest margins observed on LaSOT_ext and TNL2K where multi-frame redundancy is most detrimental to competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ETCTrack sits at the intersection of two research threads: multi-frame Transformer trackers (OSTrack, ODTrack, TemTrack, SPMTrack) that increasingly rely on historical template frames for richer spatio-temporal context, and visual token compression techniques developed for Multimodal Large Language Models (LLaVA-Prumerge, FocusLLaVA, InternVL-X). The key departure is that prior multi-frame trackers naively concatenate all template tokens, incurring quadratic cost and suffering performance degradation from visual redundancy, while prior token compression methods in MLLMs rely on handcrafted, non-learnable selection rules. ETCTrack repurposes the MLLM compression principle into a fully learnable, end-to-end module (ATC) guided by the tracking objective, and pairs it with a structured multi-stage interaction encoder (HIBlock) that goes beyond the joint self-attention used in one-stream trackers like OSTrack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete bottleneck in multi-frame tracking—visual redundancy that degrades performance beyond ~5 template frames—offering a principled solution that reduces template tokens by 60% with only a 0.4% accuracy drop. This efficiency gain is practically significant for real-time deployment and suggests that explicit information condensation is a promising design axis for future trackers. The authors note as a limitation that the compression ratio is fixed at inference; a fully dynamic mechanism that adapts compression level to scene complexity or target state changes remains an open and impactful direction.
