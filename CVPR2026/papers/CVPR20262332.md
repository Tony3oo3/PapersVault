## Problem

Existing e-commerce poster generation systems rely on multi-stage pipelines that compose scenes and render text separately, leading to style inconsistency, subject fidelity loss, and inaccurate text rendering. Single-stage approaches that do incorporate text control still struggle with complex scripts and small glyphs, and none offer efficient simultaneous control over background style, subject appearance, and glyph text within a unified model. The paper tackles the joint problem of tri-conditional poster synthesis while avoiding the quadratic attention overhead that naive multi-condition token concatenation introduces.

## Input / Output

The method accepts four heterogeneous inputs: a free-form text prompt, a background-style image (or equivalently a style prompt), a subject image where non-product regions are filled with black to form an explicit mask, and a glyph image containing the target text (supporting both Chinese and English). These are mapped into a shared token space and jointly processed by an MM-DiT backbone in latent space, producing a single e-commerce product poster image that simultaneously honors the specified style, product appearance, and textual content. The framework operates end-to-end without intermediate rendering or compositing stages.

## Method

InnoAds-Composer builds on an MM-DiT backbone and introduces three key mechanisms. First, a dual-branch Text Feature Enhancement Module (TFEM) encodes the entire glyph image via VAE and processes individual glyph crops through an OCR backbone augmented with absolute position, font-size, and local-position encodings, then fuses both via a lightweight character encoder to improve glyph sharpness and readability. Second, an importance-aware condition injection strategy analyzes attention preference weights across all layers and diffusion timesteps, revealing that style dominates early layers/steps, subject persists in mid-to-deep layers, and glyph matters most in later timesteps; each condition is then routed only to its most responsive positions (retaining ~40% style, 50% subject, 20% glyph tokens by default), shortening the active sequence. Third, a decoupled attention design removes the condition-query-to-noisy-latent-key pathway while preserving the reverse, eliminating redundant cross-attention and enabling condition activations to be cached across timesteps during inference. Training proceeds in two stages: full-token training followed by fine-tuning with pruned tokens and importance-proportional timestep sampling to recover any quality loss from pruning.

## Results

On InnoComposer-Bench, InnoAds-Composer (Stage I) achieves the best scores in nearly all metrics: Sen. Acc 0.857, NED 0.976, DINO 0.923, IoU 0.972, CSD 0.729, IR-Score 1.036, and FID 54.39, outperforming all open-source and closed-source baselines. After importance-aware token pruning (Stage II), the method retains competitive quality (Sen. Acc 0.847, DINO 0.914, FID 55.24) while reducing inference latency by 37.8% and FLOPs by 38.1% relative to Flux-Kontext. In terms of efficiency, Stage I runs at 55.87 s with 165.56 T FLOPs and 39.71 GB memory, versus 76.02 s / 218.45 T / 55.29 GB for Flux-Kontext.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InnoAds-Composer (Stage I) outperforms the closest multi-condition competitor, Qwen-Image-Edit, on Sen. Acc (0.857 vs 0.831), NED (0.976 vs 0.960), IoU (0.972 vs 0.903), CSD (0.729 vs 0.722), IR-Score (1.036 vs 0.994), and FID (54.39 vs 69.86), though Qwen-Image-Edit leads on CLIP-I (0.660 vs 0.582). Against the closed-source Seedream 4.0, the proposed method wins on DINO (0.923 vs 0.864), IoU (0.972 vs 0.837), CSD (0.729 vs 0.700), IR-Score (1.036 vs 1.012), and FID (54.39 vs 64.21), but trails slightly on Sen. Acc (0.857 vs 0.865) and CLIP-I (0.582 vs 0.639). Compared to the poster-specific baseline PosterMaker, InnoAds-Composer improves Sen. Acc by 9.2 points (0.857 vs 0.765), NED by 12.8 points (0.976 vs 0.848), and FID by 6.16 (54.39 vs 60.55), while also providing style-consistency metrics that PosterMaker cannot report. Against general-purpose conditional generation models (Flux-Kontext, OmniControl2, OmniGen2, USO), the gap is even larger: InnoAds-Composer leads on DINO by 9–11 points, IoU by 15–29 points, and FID by 20–27 points. The text-rendering baselines (Glyph-ByT5-v2, AnyText2) are far behind on both text accuracy (Sen. Acc 0.685/0.721 vs 0.857) and overall quality (FID 82.63/86.35 vs 54.39), confirming that single-purpose text renderers cannot match a unified multi-condition poster generator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MM-DiT (Flux) backbone for multi-condition image generation by introducing a unified tokenization scheme that maps heterogeneous controls—background style, subject, and glyph text—into a shared token space, departing from prior multi-stage pipelines (e.g., PosterMaker, Product2Img) that compose scenes and render text sequentially. The Text Feature Enhancement Module (TFEM) draws on both VAE-based visual encoding and OCR backbones with positional cues, going beyond prior visual-text rendering methods (Glyph-ByT5, AnyText2) that handle text in isolation. The efficiency contributions—importance-aware condition injection and decoupled attention—are novel architectural modifications to MM-DiT that reduce quadratic attention cost without a separate pruning framework, building conceptually on prior work on efficient conditioning for diffusion transformers (OminiControl2, FullDiT2).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of InnoComposer-80K and InnoComposer-Bench fills a notable gap: no prior dataset jointly annotates subject, glyph, and style conditions for e-commerce posters, making fair multi-condition comparison previously infeasible. The practical efficiency gains (≈38% latency and FLOPs reduction in Stage II) make tri-conditional poster generation viable at scale for industrial deployment. Open questions include whether the observed layer/timestep importance patterns generalize across different MM-DiT architectures or condition types, and whether the single-stage formulation can extend to additional constraints (e.g., layout grids, multi-product compositions) without further inflating sequence length.
