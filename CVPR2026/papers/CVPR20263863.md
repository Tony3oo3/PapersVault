## Problem

Existing multimodal sarcasm detection datasets and methods are limited to single-image settings, failing to capture sarcasm that arises from semantic or affective contrasts across multiple images in a post. This gap means current benchmarks do not reflect the complexity of real-world social media content, where a substantial fraction of tweets contain two to four images whose inter-image relations drive the sarcastic intent.

## Input / Output

CIRM takes a text sequence (averaging ~31 words, with emojis preserved), 2–4 RGB images (padded to a fixed maximum of 4 with blank placeholders and a binary mask), OCR token sequences extracted per image via PP-OCRv5, and an optional star-rating embedding. It outputs a binary sarcasm label (1 = sarcastic, 0 = non-sarcastic) via a linear head over a fused multimodal representation. For single-image baselines evaluated on MMSD3.0, all images are tiled into one composite canvas to keep visual evidence identical without altering model architectures.

## Method

CIRM encodes images with a ViT (CLS tokens), text and OCR separately with RoBERTa-Emoji, then applies positional embeddings and a padding mask to make the model order-aware over the image sequence. The Dual-Stage Bridge Module (DSBM) performs cross-modal multi-head attention with gated residuals before a Mamba-inspired sequential block (depthwise Conv1D + selective state-space updates) that models intra-modal long-range dependencies, followed by a second cross-modal attention stage after sequence modeling. The Relevance-Guided Fusion Module (RGFM) aligns both modalities to OCR cues via cross-attention, computes per-image relevance scores blending cosine similarity and a learned MLP scorer (mixed with α=0.3), and produces a weighted cross-modal feature that suppresses irrelevant or padded images. Final classification concatenates pooled post-bridge text/vision features with the RGFM vector and an optional rating embedding, trained end-to-end with weighted cross-entropy (AdamW, lr 2e-5, 20 epochs, batch 8 on a single H100).

## Results

On the new MMSD3.0 multi-image benchmark, CIRM achieves 85.16% accuracy and 84.42% macro-F1, outperforming all baselines including MLLMs (GPT-4o: 72.62% Acc, Qwen2.5-VL-32B: 71.94% Acc) and prior multimodal methods (Tang et al.: 82.20% Acc, Multi-view CLIP: 81.96% Acc). On the single-image benchmarks, CIRM sets new state-of-the-art results: 94.02% Acc / 93.76% F1 on MMSD and 92.12% Acc / 91.69% F1 on MMSD2.0, surpassing the previous best (RCLMuFN) by ~1.5 points on MMSD2.0. Ablations confirm that removing the Dual-Stage Bridge Module or Relevance-Guided Fusion Module causes the largest drops (F1 falls to 81.41% and 81.36% respectively), and shuffling image order reduces F1 from 84.42% to 83.51%, indicating meaningful reliance on image sequence information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MMSD3.0, CIRM (85.16% Acc / 84.42% F1) outperforms the strongest prior multimodal baseline Tang et al. [38] by ~3.0 Acc and ~3.5 F1 points, and beats Multi-view CLIP [34] (81.96/81.96) and DIP [42] (81.83/76.11) by similar margins. Notably, all three MLLMs (GPT-4o, LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) score 12–24 F1 points below CIRM, indicating that general-purpose vision-language models struggle with the fine-grained cross-image incongruity required for sarcasm. On MMSD2.0, CIRM (92.12/91.69) surpasses the previous SOTA RCLMuFN [40] (91.57/90.25) by ~0.6 Acc and ~1.4 F1, and leads Tang et al. [38] (86.43/86.34) by over 5 points. On MMSD, CIRM (94.02/93.76) edges out RCLMuFN (93.09/91.52) and G2SAM [41] (90.48/89.65). The shuffled-order variant of CIRM retains 83.51% F1 (only ~1 point drop), showing robustness to image ordering while confirming that positional information contributes meaningfully to performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal sarcasm detection line initiated by MMSD (Cai et al., ACL 2019) and refined by MMSD2.0 (Qin et al., 2023) by shifting the setting from single-image to multi-image posts, a gap the authors argue has been overlooked despite multi-image tweets being common in practice. The proposed CIRM architecture repurposes several established components—ViT image encoding, RoBERTa-Emoji text encoding, Mamba-inspired state-space sequence modeling, and cross-modal attention—into a dual-bridge pipeline with OCR-guided relevance weighting, rather than introducing a fundamentally new modeling primitive. Conceptually, the paper positions multi-image sarcasm as a distinct reasoning challenge (inter-image contrast and sequential narrative) that single-image incongruity models cannot capture, drawing an analogy to how document-level sarcasm (Du et al., AAAI 2024) extends sentence-level detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMSD3.0 provides the first dedicated multi-image sarcasm benchmark, and the finding that prior single-image methods and even powerful MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) degrade substantially on it underscores that inter-image reasoning remains an open problem. The relatively small dataset (~10K samples, English-only, Twitter/Amazon) and the modest absolute performance (84.42 F1) leave room for significant improvement, and it is unclear whether the multi-image gap persists at larger scale or in other languages and platforms. A key open question is whether the relevance-guided fusion and positional encoding mechanisms generalize to longer image sequences or to settings where image order is semantically irrelevant (e.g., collage-style posts).
