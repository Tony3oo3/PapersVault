## Problem

Standard similarity-based retrieval for visual in-context learning (e.g., RICES, MUIER) selects demonstrations that are visually or textually close to the query but often share confounding attributes, causing VLMs to mimic surface correlations rather than identify the attributes that truly determine the answer. This limitation is especially severe under information scarcity or distribution shift, where correlated neighbors provide little guidance on how changing specific attributes affects the outcome. No prior method explicitly incorporates counterfactual-style interventions into the example selection process for visual ICL.

## Input / Output

Given a query image and a natural language question, CIRCLES retrieves 32 in-context examples (16 counterfactual + 16 correlational) from a training corpus of (image, question, answer) triples, then feeds them to a frozen VLM (Gemma3-4B/12B or Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) for answer generation. A frozen CLIP ViT-g/14 provides all image and text embeddings used for retrieval scoring. The method is entirely training-free: the VLM is used at inference time for attribute identification, counterfactual caption generation, and final prediction, while the CIR component (OSrCIR) is also training-free.

## Method

CIRCLES constructs demonstration sets through two complementary retrieval branches. The causal branch first prompts the VLM to identify key attribute-value pairs decisive for answering the query, then for each attribute generates a counterfactual caption describing the query image under an atomic intervention do(a_i = v_i') (one attribute changed, all others fixed). Candidate training images are scored by the sum of image-to-counterfactual-caption similarity and query-question similarity, and the top-k are selected as counterfactual demonstrations. The correlational branch performs standard image-image nearest-neighbor retrieval (RICES-style) to provide broader visual context. The two sets are unioned into a 32-example context that is prepended to the query for VLM inference. The key insight is that composed image retrieval operationalizes Pearl-style do-interventions on visual attributes, exposing the model to explicit class transitions across attribute changes and enabling disentangled, causal reasoning beyond surface similarity.

## Results

CIRCLES achieves the highest average EM across all four VLM backbones: 63.54% (Gemma3-4B), 71.71% (Gemma3-12B), 71.44% (Qwen2.5-VL-3B), and 75.58% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Gains are most pronounced on fine-grained classification: on CUB with Gemma3-4B, CIRCLES reaches 71.97% accuracy versus 65.40% for RICES and 65.21% for MUIER (+6.6 pp), and on Flowers it reaches 93.32% versus 86.70% for RICES. On VQA tasks, CIRCLES leads with 31.27 EM on OK-VQA and 57.61 EM on VizWiz (Gemma3-4B), extending the advantage to knowledge-intensive and noisy real-world settings. Under information scarcity (75% of training samples removed), CIRCLES's relative advantage over RICES widens from 10.05% to 16.28% on Gemma3-4B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIRCLES outperforms all three retrieval-based baselines (RICES, MUIER, MMICES) on every dataset and backbone configuration. Against RICES, the closest competitor, CIRCLES gains +6.57 pp accuracy on CUB and +6.62 pp on Flowers with Gemma3-4B, and +4.62 pp EM on OK-VQA; with larger models (Gemma3-12B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) the margin narrows to 0.5–3.8 pp, indicating diminishing returns as internal model knowledge grows. MUIER tracks RICES almost exactly (differences < 0.3 pp on most cells), confirming that adding text-image similarity to retrieval provides negligible benefit over pure image retrieval. MMICES, a two-stage re-ranking approach, performs near-random or worse on classification tasks (e.g., 13.95% on CUB vs. 65.40% for RICES with Gemma3-4B), suggesting its re-ranking stage is counterproductive for fine-grained visual discrimination. The largest absolute gains from CIRCLES appear on small models and fine-grained datasets, where counterfactual attribute interventions most effectively compensate for limited internal knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIRCLES sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: retrieval-augmented in-context learning (RICES, MUIER, MMICES) and composed image retrieval (CIR). It repurposes CIR—which prior literature treats as an end task (FashionIQ, CIRR)—as a tool for constructing counterfactual demonstrations, rather than retrieving a single modified image. The correlational retrieval component is standard RICES-style nearest-neighbor search, while the causal component leverages OSrCIR to generate attribute-intervened captions and retrieve examples that isolate the effect of individual visual attributes on the answer. The key conceptual departure from MUIER and MMICES is the explicit introduction of counterfactual-style examples into the demonstration pool, moving beyond pure similarity-based selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, training-free mechanism to inject causal-reasoning signals into VLM in-context learning without formal causal identification, which is appealing for practitioners working with small or frozen models. The finding that performance gaps widen under information scarcity suggests counterfactual retrieval is a robustness tool, not just an accuracy booster. However, the approach depends on the VLM correctly identifying decisive attributes and on CIR quality (OSrCIR vs. CIReVL matters), and the 'causal' framing is explicitly approximate—no do-calculus or interventional guarantees are provided. Open questions include whether the attribute-identification step generalizes to open-ended VQA with many interacting factors, and whether the benefits persist as backbone models grow large enough to internalize fine-grained attribute reasoning on their own.
