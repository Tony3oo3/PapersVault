## Problem

AdamW, the dominant optimizer for large-scale Transformer training, loses its typical advantages when naively applied to Differentially Private Federated Learning (DPFL): non-IID data and DP noise jointly inflate second-moment variance, gradient clipping and noise injection introduce systematic bias in the second-moment estimator, and DP amplifies AdamW's sensitivity to local overfitting, worsening client drift. No prior work has systematically addressed adaptive optimization for large-model DPFL, leaving a gap between the efficiency gains AdamW provides in centralized settings and the stability requirements of private federated training.

## Input / Output

Each client receives the global model parameters, the previous round's block-wise second-moment statistics (one scalar per parameter block rather than per-parameter), and an estimated global descent direction. Clients compute per-sample clipped gradients with Gaussian noise, maintain local AdamW moments, and upload only their local parameter delta plus a single block-mean second-moment vector of size O(B) where B is the number of architecture-aligned blocks. The server outputs updated global parameters, updated block-wise second-moment statistics, and a refreshed global descent direction estimate broadcast to all clients.

## Method

DP-FedAdamW restores AdamW's effectiveness under DPFL through three complementary mechanisms. First, it replaces per-parameter second-moment communication with block-wise aggregation: parameters are partitioned into B blocks aligned with model architecture (per-attention-head for Q/K/V, per-layer for projections, MLPs, embeddings, and output layers), and only the mean second-moment per block is transmitted, stabilizing variance while keeping communication at O(B). Second, it applies an explicit bias correction by subtracting the known DP noise variance term (σC/sR)² from the bias-corrected second-moment estimate, yielding an unbiased preconditioner with respect to the clipped-gradient second moment. Third, it augments each local AdamW step with a local-global alignment term γΔ_G^t, where Δ_G^t is an empirical estimate of the global descent direction computed from the previous round's aggregated client updates, softly steering local trajectories back toward the global path to suppress client drift. The paper proves a linearly accelerated convergence rate O(√(LΔσ_l²/(SKTτ²)) + LΔ/T + σ²G_g²/(s²R²)) without any bounded-heterogeneity assumption, and provides tighter (ε,δ)-DP guarantees via Rényi DP composition over K local steps and T rounds.

## Results

DP-FedAdamW achieves the best accuracy across all seven datasets and three architecture families (ResNet-18, ViT/Swin Transformers, RoBERTa). On Tiny-ImageNet with Swin-Base at ε=1 (strongest privacy), it reaches 34.23% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the best baseline (DP-LocalAdamW, 28.40%) by 5.83%. On CIFAR-100 with Swin-Base under severe non-IID (α=0.1), it achieves 67.45%, outperforming DP-FedSAM (66.28%) and DP-FedAvg-LS (66.08%). On GLUE MNLI with RoBERTa-Base, it attains 78.68% accuracy, exceeding DP-LocalAdamW (75.20%) by 3.48%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-FedAdamW outperforms all six baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. Against the strongest same-budget baselines (DP-FedAvg-LS and DP-LocalAdamW), it gains 3.81% on CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, α=0.1), 4.85% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-18, α=0.1), and 5.83% on Tiny-ImageNet (Swin-Base, ε=1). Against DP-FedSAM—which consumes approximately 2× the privacy budget due to two gradient evaluations per step—DP-FedAdamW still leads by 2.58% on CIFAR-100 (Swin-Tiny, α=0.6) and 2.68% on Tiny-ImageNet (Swin-Base, α=0.1), making the comparison even more favorable in a privacy-normalized sense. On language tasks (RoBERTa-Base, GLUE), DP-FedAdamW beats DP-LocalAdamW by 0.13–3.48% across SST-2, QQP, QNLI, and MNLI, with the largest gap on MNLI. In the dedicated comparison with DP variants of federated adaptive optimizers (Table 9), DP-FedAdamW surpasses DP-FedLADA (the best prior adaptive method) by 1.42% on CIFAR-100 and 2.91% on Tiny-ImageNet (Swin-Base, α=0.1, σ=1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPFL framework, which has been dominated by SGD-based methods (DP-FedAvg, DP-SCAFFOLD, DP-FedAvg-LS, DP-FedSAM), by introducing the first AdamW-based adaptive optimizer for differentially private federated learning. It builds directly on AdamW's decoupled weight decay formulation and connects to the federated adaptive optimization line (FedOpt, FAFED, FedLADA), but departs from those works by explicitly accounting for the three distortions that DP noise and non-IID data impose on moment estimators—variance amplification, systematic bias, and client drift—which prior federated adaptive methods do not address. The local-global alignment mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from SCAFFOLD-style control variates but is integrated into the adaptive update rule rather than applied to raw gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As large Transformer models become the default for both vision and language tasks, the inability of standard DPFL optimizers to efficiently train them is a practical bottleneck; DP-FedAdamW provides a principled, communication-efficient path to close this gap with provable convergence and privacy guarantees. The finding that AdamW can underperform SGD in DPFL challenges the assumption that adaptive optimization is universally beneficial and highlights the need for DP-aware optimizer design. Limitations include evaluation on models up to ~125M parameters (Swin-Base, RoBERTa-Base) rather than truly large-scale models, and the block-wise aggregation, while communication-efficient, sacrifices per-parameter adaptivity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other adaptive families (e.g., LAMB, Lion) and how it behaves under even stricter privacy budgets or with truly heterogeneous client compute.
