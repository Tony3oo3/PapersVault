## Problem

State-of-the-art ViT-based deepfake detectors rely on the pre-trained [CLS] token for detection, but this token suffers from Pre-trained Information Bias (PIB): it predominantly aggregates global semantics due to constraints imposed by pre-trained multi-layer parameters, while failing to highlight the subtle, localized forgery cues that are critical for generalizable detection. This global-semantic bias causes the detector to overemphasize background regions and real-like patches, degrading performance on unseen manipulation sources.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262827/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes face video frames (8 frames sampled for training, 32 for inference) at 224×224 resolution with standard augmentations (Gaussian Blur, JPEG Compression). Rather than using the backbone's [CLS] token, detection is performed on a fused global token produced by averaging K learnable query tokens that attend to the final-layer patch representations. The output is a standard real/fake binary classification score, with the framework designed as a plug-and-play module compatible with any ViT backbone and fine-tuning strategy (Effort, LoRA, FFT).

## Method

QTFP introduces K randomly initialized learnable query tokens that operate independently of the pre-trained backbone and interact with final patch tokens via a lightweight multi-head attention layer, producing a detection-specific global token free from [CLS] semantic constraints. A fake-likelihood contrastive learning (FLCL) loss weights each patch token by its signed cosine-similarity difference to batch-level real and fake prototypes, so that truly forged patches dominate optimization while real-like patches are suppressed; the paper provides an SNR analysis showing this weighting achieves reliable separation (SNR ≈ 2.5–3.0). A real-attention alignment (RAAL) loss minimizes KL divergence between query-token attention and [CLS] attention exclusively on real images, preserving useful pre-trained priors as a regularization term. The total objective combines classification loss with λ₁·FLCL and λ₂·RAAL, and the framework is validated as a plug-and-play enhancement across CLIP, BEiTv2, DINOv3, SigLIP2, and PE backbones under Effort, LoRA, and full fine-tuning.

## Results

On the cross-dataset evaluation (trained on FF++ c23, tested on 10 unseen datasets), QTFP achieves a video-level AUC of 0.947 on average, outperforming the strongest baseline Effort (0.923) by 2.4 points and Forensics Adapter (0.914) by 3.3 points. On the cross-method evaluation (DF40 benchmark), the method reaches 0.983 average AUC, surpassing both Effort and Forensics Adapter (0.940) by 4.3 points. The method also demonstrates consistent gains across all fine-tuning strategies (FFT, LoRA, Effort) and all tested vision foundation models (CLIP, BEiTv2, DINOv3, SigLIP2, PE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QTFP outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both cross-dataset and cross-method evaluations. On the cross-dataset benchmark, it leads Effort (the primary baseline it builds upon) by 2.4 AUC points (0.947 vs. 0.923) and Forensics Adapter by 3.3 points (0.947 vs. 0.914), with the largest margins on FaceShifter (+4.5 over Effort) and FFIW (+2.9 over Effort). On the cross-method DF40 benchmark, QTFP achieves 0.983 average AUC, beating both Effort and Forensics Adapter (tied at 0.940) by 4.3 points, with particularly strong gains on UniFace (0.988 vs. 0.962) and MobSwap (0.990 vs. 0.953). Among older CNN-based methods, the gap is even larger: QTFP surpasses F3Net by 22.4 points and SRM by 19.2 points on average AUC. The method also maintains a clear advantage under perturbation robustness tests (Figure 6), retaining higher AUC than CLIP, Forensics Adapter, SBI, and LSDA across all four perturbation types and five severity levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Effort [48], a SVD-based fine-tuning strategy for VFM-based deepfake detectors, and positions QTFP as a plug-and-play module that can be layered on top of Effort, LoRA, or full fine-tuning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant practice of relying on the [CLS] token for detection by introducing independent learnable query tokens that aggregate patch-level evidence through a lightweight attention layer, drawing inspiration from the fine-grained detection line (ForAda, UDD, FIA-USA) but reframing the problem as token-level purification rather than patch-level contrastive learning or shuffling. The fake-likelihood contrastive loss extends standard patch-level contrastive objectives with a theoretically grounded weighting mechanism, while the real-attention alignment loss is a novel regularization that explicitly preserves pre-trained semantic priors on real images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Pre-trained Information Bias as a concrete, measurable limitation in VFM-based detectors provides a useful diagnostic for the community, and the plug-and-play design (validated across five backbones and three fine-tuning paradigms) makes the technique broadly adoptable without architectural changes. The SNR-based theoretical justification for the weighting strategy adds rigor beyond empirical tuning. However, the method still operates within the pre-trained feature space, which the authors acknowledge may limit effectiveness on low-quality or previously unseen manipulation types; incorporating generative priors or multi-scale reasoning remains an open direction for further generalization.
