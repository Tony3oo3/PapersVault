## Problem

Conventional diffusion loss provides only implicit supervision for modeling fine-grained text-image correspondence, and this alignment degrades progressively at larger timesteps where more noise is present. Existing inference-time optimization methods (e.g., Attend-and-Excite) can explore cross-attention maps during generation but suffer from limited generalizability and scalability, leaving a gap in how to explicitly supervise text-image alignment during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260769/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image as output, operating within standard text-to-image diffusion pipelines (SD 2.1 at 512×512, SD 3 at 1024×1024). During training, the method additionally consumes cross-attention maps extracted from the denoising network's forward pass at two sampled timesteps as intermediate supervision signals, and uses part-of-speech tagging (via Stanza) to select only noun-token attention maps for the calibration loss. Fine-tuning is performed with LoRA on both the text encoder's self-attention and the denoising network's attention layers.

## Method

CTCAL is a model-agnostic fine-tuning paradigm that samples two timesteps (t_stu > t_tea) per training step and uses the cross-attention maps from the lower-noise timestep t_tea as explicit supervision to calibrate the higher-noise timestep t_stu, with gradients truncated on the teacher side. Three key design choices refine this core mechanism: (1) a part-of-speech-based selection strategy restricts the calibration loss to noun tokens that carry spatial semantics, (2) a pixel-semantic joint optimization combines direct MSE on attention maps with a semantic-level loss through a lightweight autoencoder (with a reconstruction proxy to prevent mode collapse), and (3) a subject response alignment regularization equalizes attention response magnitudes across multiple subjects. A timestep-aware linear weighting (λ_t = t_stu / T_train) scales the CTCAL loss so it contributes minimally at small timesteps and dominantly at large timesteps, harmonizing with the standard diffusion loss. The approach is validated on both diffusion-based (SD 2.1) and flow-based (SD 3) architectures.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, SD 2.1+CTCAL achieves 0.7233 on ColorB-VQA, 0.5149 on ShapeB-VQA, 0.6754 on TextureB-VQA, 0.2142 on 2D-SpatialUniDet, 0.3862 on 3D-SpatialUniDet, 0.5084 on NumeracyUniDet, 0.7723 on Non-SpatialShare-CoT, and 0.3403 on Complex3-in-1, outperforming all prior SD 2.1-based methods. On SD 3(2B)+CTCAL, scores further improve to 0.8443 (ColorB-VQA), 0.5968 (ShapeB-VQA), 0.7581 (TextureB-VQA), 0.3476 (2D-Spatial), 0.4117 (3D-Spatial), 0.6292 (Numeracy), 0.7867 (Non-Spatial), and 0.3814 (Complex). On GenEval, SD 3(2B)+CTCAL raises the overall score from 0.62 to 0.69, with gains across all sub-categories (e.g., Two-object: 0.74→0.85, Counting: 0.63→0.70, Color attribution: 0.36→0.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CTCAL consistently outperforms all SD 2.1-based baselines: it beats the strongest prior method (GORS1, a supervised fine-tuning approach) by +6.3% on ColorB-VQA (0.7233 vs. 0.6603) and +17.5% on 2D-Spatial (0.2142 vs. 0.1815). Against inference-time optimization methods, CTCAL surpasses Attend-and-Excite [5] by +13.1% on ColorB-VQA and +47.3% on 2D-Spatial, and outperforms structured diffusion guidance [10] and composable diffusion [23] across all metrics. On the SD 3(2B) backbone, CTCAL improves over the base model [9] by +3.1% on ColorB-VQA (0.8443 vs. 0.8132) and +8.6% on 2D-Spatial (0.3476 vs. 0.3200), and also edges out the GORS2 fine-tuning baseline. Against stronger proprietary models, SD 2.1+CTCAL approaches but does not fully match DALL-E 3 [3] (0.7233 vs. 0.7785 on ColorB-VQA) or FLUX-schnell [19] (0.7233 vs. 0.7407), while SD 3(2B)+CTCAL surpasses both on most spatial and compositional metrics. On GenEval, SD 3(2B)+CTCAL achieves an overall score of 0.69 versus 0.62 for the base SD 3, with the largest relative gains in Two-object (0.74→0.85) and Color attribution (0.36→0.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inference-time optimization paradigm for text-image alignment (e.g., Attend-and-Excite [5], Prompt-to-Prompt [15]) by shifting the intervention to the training phase, where it introduces an explicit cross-timestep self-calibration loss on cross-attention maps. The core empirical observation—that cross-attention alignment quality degrades monotonically with increasing diffusion timestep—provides the motivation and is validated through direct visualization. Conceptually, CTCAL repurposes the model's own reliable small-timestep attention as a self-supervision signal for its unreliable large-timestep attention, a design that is model-agnostic and compatible with both classical diffusion (SD 2.1) and rectified-flow (SD 3) architectures. The part-of-speech-based token selection and pixel-semantic joint optimization are practical refinements that distinguish it from a naive attention-matching loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CTCAL addresses a persistent and practically important gap: compositional text-to-image models still struggle with fine-grained spatial-semantic binding, and prior fixes have been either inference-time (limited scalability) or architecture-specific. By providing a training-time, model-agnostic fine-tuning recipe that requires only LoRA and a reward-curated dataset, it offers a low-friction path for practitioners to improve alignment without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the self-calibration principle extends to video diffusion or multi-modal generation, whether the reward-driven dataset construction introduces subtle biases that the cross-benchmark GenEval results only partially rule out, and whether the approach remains effective as base models grow substantially larger.
