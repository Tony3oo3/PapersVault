## Problem

Existing 3D VAEs face a fundamental trade-off: SDF-based methods require costly watertight preprocessing and cannot represent open or non-manifold topologies, while point-cloud methods produce surfaces with holes and discontinuities. Moreover, the global-bottleneck compression-decompression design in prior sparse-voxel VAEs inherently discards high-frequency geometric details, making ultra-high-resolution reconstruction (e.g., 2048³) unattainable. No prior framework simultaneously handles arbitrary topologies, preserves fine geometric detail, and scales to extreme resolutions without architectural changes.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the method takes a 3D triangle mesh, converts it to an unsigned distance field, and sparsifies it by retaining only voxels within threshold τ=4/N of the surface; the sparse volume is then partitioned into fixed-size UBlocks (D=8) with padding α=2, so the number of active blocks L scales with input resolution while each block's internal size stays constant. For image-to-3D generation, an RGB image is first mapped to a sparse 128³ structure by a flow model, then decoded through the LoG-VAE decoder. The output is a high-resolution 3D mesh extracted via Marching Cubes at isosurface threshold 1/N, alongside a set of sparse latent codes (16 channels per active UBlock) that can be sampled for generation.

## Method

LoG-VAE replaces SDFs with unsigned distance fields to eliminate the watertight constraint, then partitions the sparse UDF volume into a grid of uniform subvolumes called UBlocks, decoupling model complexity from input resolution. Within each UBlock, a local 3D CNN with max-pooling progressively extracts fine-grained spatial features, while a global sparse transformer with shifted-window attention models long-range dependencies across active UBlocks to enforce structural coherence. A Pad-Average strategy pads each UBlock by α voxels before encoding and averages overlapping regions during reassembly, eliminating boundary artifacts without additional network parameters. The encoder-decoder is symmetric and trained end-to-end with a Huber UDF reconstruction loss plus KL-divergence regularization on the latent codes. Because only the number of sparse tokens (L) grows with resolution while per-block parameters remain fixed, the architecture scales seamlessly from 1024³ to 2048³ without architectural modification.

## Results

On Toys4K, Ours-2048 achieves NMSE 0.29, SNE 0.85, CD 0.06×10⁻⁵, and F1(0.001) 42.98×10², outperforming the strongest baseline TripoSF (NMSE 1.27, SNE 6.38, CD 0.07, F1(0.001) 36.16) by 4.3× on NMSE and 7.5× on SNE. On the out-of-distribution iHome dataset, Ours-2048 further improves to NMSE 0.17, SNE 0.94, and F1(0.001) 39.37, again dominating all baselines. The 2048³ model also achieves a two-fold higher compression rate than TripoSF while matching or exceeding its reconstruction quality, and the 1024³ variant already surpasses every baseline at the same compression ratio as Direct3D-S2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoG3D is compared against five VAE baselines: Hunyuan3D-2.1, TRELLIS, and Dora (all at 256³ resolution), and Direct3D-S2 and TripoSF (both at 1024³). LoG3D wins decisively on all metrics across both datasets. Against the closest competitor TripoSF (1024³), Ours-2048 reduces NMSE from 1.27 to 0.29 (4.4×) and SNE from 6.38 to 0.85 (7.5×) on Toys4K, while improving F1(0.001) from 36.16 to 42.98; on iHome the SNE gap widens to 9.2× (8.63→0.94). Against Direct3D-S2 (1024³), the advantage is even larger: NMSE drops from 3.17 to 0.29 and CD from 0.23 to 0.06 on Toys4K. The 256³ baselines (Hunyuan3D-2.1, TRELLIS, Dora) are substantially outperformed, with F1(0.001) gaps of 35+ points on Toys4K. For image-to-3D generation, LoG3D also outperforms Direct3D-S2 and Sparc3D at 1024³, producing sharper edges and smoother surfaces (Fig. 5). Notably, LoG3D achieves these gains at a two-fold higher compression rate than TripoSF, and its architecture scales to 2048³ without parameter changes—only the number of sparse tokens increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SDF-based VAE lineage (Direct3D-S2, Sparc3D) by adopting Unsigned Distance Fields, which eliminate the costly watertight preprocessing step and naturally handle open and non-manifold topologies. It builds on the sparse voxel VAE tradition (XCube, TRELLIS, TripoSF) but introduces UBlock partitioning to decouple model complexity from input resolution, and a Pad-Average reassembly strategy to resolve boundary artifacts. The local-to-global architecture—3D convolutions for intra-block detail plus shifted-window sparse transformers for inter-block coherence—draws on Swin-style attention and TRELLIS's structured latent design, but applies them to a fundamentally different (UDF, block-partitioned) input representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a VAE-based 3D model can scale to 2048³ resolution while handling arbitrary topologies, LoG3D opens a practical path toward production-quality geometry reconstruction without the SDF watertight constraint that has limited prior methods. The UBlock design means resolution scaling requires only more sparse tokens rather than larger model parameters, a property with broad implications for future 3D generative pipelines. Key open questions include integrating texture generation (currently geometry-only), reducing the heavy Marching Cubes cost at 2048³, and whether the UDF advantage over SDFs persists when both are given equivalent architectural treatment at matched resolutions.
