## Problem

Existing slide-level pathology foundation models partition WSIs into rigid fixed-size patches or regular grid regions that ignore tissue morphology boundaries, lacking an explicit ROI concept and creating long-range interactions across thousands of patches. These models also rely on natural-image backbones not tailored for histopathology, limiting their ability to capture coherent tissue architecture and produce clinically interpretable representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263644/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CARE takes H&E-stained WSIs at 20× magnification, tiles them into patches, and extracts frozen patch features via CONCH v1.5; WSIs are further split into sub-WSIs of ≤360 patches via DBSCAN to enable practical batch training. For cross-modal pretraining, it additionally consumes RNA expression profiles (3,999 genes selected from 50 Hallmark gene sets) and protein expression profiles (top-10 proteins per sample). The model outputs a slide-level embedding (aggregated via SPF), a single ROI feature (the highest-weighted adaptive region descriptor), and the full adaptive region partition of the WSI.

## Method

CARE introduces an Adaptive Region Generator (ARG) that replaces fixed-grid subregions with irregular, morphology-coherent regions by combining anchor-distance soft inclusion (spatial proximity) with cosine-similarity-based semantic affinity between patch and subregion descriptors, assigning each patch to its best-scoring candidate subregion. Within each adaptive region, Adaptive Region Self-Attention (ARSA) produces a region-level [CLS] feature and context-enriched patch features, while a Region Structuring Loss (RSL) prevents partition collapse by pulling the mean expected candidate rank toward a target value. A lightweight Semantic and Prior Fusion (SPF) module aggregates region features into a slide-level embedding by blending coverage prior (patch proportion) with gated attention weights. Pretraining follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 applies iBOT self-supervised learning on 34,277 WSIs (285,710 sub-WSIs) with frozen-feature augmentation, and Stage 2 performs CLIP-style contrastive alignment first with RNA embeddings (initialized from scGPT) then with protein embeddings (initialized from ESM-2), using symmetric InfoNCE loss to refine region boundaries and representations.

## Results

Across 33 downstream benchmarks, CARE achieves the best average balanced accuracy under linear probing on morphological classification (85.7%), molecular classification (69.5%), molecular classification without validation (64.9%), and survival analysis (C-index 58.0), using only ~1/10 of the pretraining data of mainstream foundation models. Under fine-tuning, CARE remains highly competitive (87.0% morphological, 74.7% molecular), trailing TITAN by 0.7 points on morphological FT and FEATHER by 0.9 points on molecular FT. On the combined RCC subtype-classification task with logistic regression, CARE improves balanced accuracy by 3.8 points and F1 by 0.7 points over the next-best model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE outperforms all six slide-level foundation model baselines (CHIEF, PRISM, GigaPath, TANGLE, FEATHER, TITAN) on the majority of linear-probing and kNN evaluations. On morphological classification with LR, CARE leads TITAN (85.4) by 0.3 points and FEATHER (81.1) by 4.6 points; on molecular classification with LR, CARE (69.5) edges out TANGLE and PRISM (both 69.1) by 0.4 points. The largest margin appears in survival analysis, where CARE's C-index of 58.0 surpasses GigaPath (55.7) by 2.3 points and PRISM (42.1) by 15.9 points. Under fine-tuning, TITAN narrowly leads CARE on morphological classification (87.7 vs 87.0) and FEATHER leads on molecular classification (75.6 vs 74.7), but CARE remains within 1 point of the best in both cases. Notably, CARE achieves these results using only ~34,277 WSIs for pretraining, roughly one-tenth the data used by the compared foundation models, highlighting a substantial data-efficiency advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE extends the hierarchical patch→region→WSI paradigm introduced by HIPT by replacing its fixed, rule-based region partitioning with an adaptive region generator that discovers morphologically coherent, irregular regions. For pretraining, it adapts iBOT's self-supervised masked-patch objective to the WSI setting and then layers a CLIP-style cross-modal alignment stage on top, extending TANGLE's transcriptomics-guided approach by adding a protein-level curriculum and using scGPT/ESM-2-initialized molecular encoders. The patch-level backbone (CONCH) is frozen, positioning CARE as a slide-level aggregator that restructures input granularity rather than retraining the feature extractor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The data-efficiency result—competitive or superior performance across 33 benchmarks using roughly one-tenth of the pretraining WSIs typical of mainstream pathology foundation models—suggests that structural inductive bias (adaptive regions) and molecular supervision can substitute for raw scale. The ROI-centric output aligns with how pathologists actually read slides, offering an interpretability angle that global-attention slide models lack. Open questions include whether the adaptive region generator generalizes to non-H&E staining or other organ systems, whether the molecular guidance can be extended to spatial transcriptomics or single-cell data, and whether the DBSCAN-based sub-WSI partitioning introduces artifacts at tissue boundaries.
