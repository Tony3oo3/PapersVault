## Problem

Existing unsupervised camouflaged object detection (UCOD) methods suffer from two critical limitations: they over-rely on the self-supervised DINO backbone due to an inability to extract effective supervision from unlabeled data, and they treat all pseudo-labels uniformly during distillation, wasting the signal from high-quality labels while being corrupted by low-quality ones. This results in a significant performance gap compared to supervised and even weakly-supervised COD methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512) with no annotations as input. The teacher pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL-3B + SAM ViT-H) produces bounding boxes and three candidate segmentation masks per image, which are then graded into quality tiers (0/1/2) by the Graded Mask Evaluator. The student model (PVT V2 or ResNet50) outputs a pixel-level binary segmentation mask. In zero-shot mode, the same teacher pipeline directly produces the final mask without any student training.

## Method

UCOD-MKD is a teacher-student framework where a frozen MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) guided by a 5-step Camouflaged-Aware Chain-of-Thought (CA-CoT) prompt generates localization bounding boxes, which are fed to SAM to produce three candidate masks. A Graded Mask Evaluator (GME) scores each mask using inter-candidate similarity (IoU + SSIM), edge truncation count, and connected-component fragmentation, assigning a quality grade (0, 1, or 2) and filtering out low-quality masks. The student is trained via Graded Knowledge Distillation (GKD), which applies differentiated losses per grade at the image level (self-distillation for grade 0, cross-entropy for grade 1, CE+L1+MSE for grade 2) and modulates pixel-level supervision with an entropy-based confidence weight derived from the candidate masks plus a background prior from the bounding box. The key insight is that COD distillation follows a quality-over-quantity principle: a small number of high-quality pseudo-labels outperforms large quantities of noisy ones, so selective, graded distillation is essential.

## Results

Under the unsupervised setting with PVT V2 backbone, UCOD-MKD achieves MAE 0.071 / Em 0.875 / Fwβ 0.753 on CAMO, MAE 0.031 / Em 0.908 / Fwβ 0.740 on COD10K, and MAE 0.040 / Em 0.918 / Fwβ 0.803 on NC4K, outperforming the previous best UCOD method (UCOD-DPL) by average gains of 42.6% (MAE), 14.0% (Sm), 9.2% (Em), and 22.5% (Fwβ). In the zero-shot setting (Qwen2.5-VL 3B + SAM), the method achieves MAE 0.036 / Em 0.882 / Fwβ 0.755 on COD10K, surpassing all prior zero-shot COD methods. The student model requires only 60.3M parameters and 50.7 MACs, delivering 34.8 FPS—faster than the weakly-supervised SAM-COD (31.2 FPS) while achieving better accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior unsupervised method UCOD-DPL [45], UCOD-MKD (PVT V2) reduces MAE from 0.108 to 0.071 on CAMO (−34%) and from 0.059 to 0.031 on COD10K (−47%), while improving Fwβ from 0.621 to 0.753 and 0.577 to 0.740 respectively; it also eliminates the dependency on the DINO backbone. Among zero-shot methods, UCOD-MKD (Qwen2.5-VL 3B + SAM) achieves the best COD10K results (MAE 0.036, Em 0.882, Fwβ 0.755), outperforming ProMaC [24] (MAE 0.045, Fwβ 0.624) and CVP [40] (MAE 0.065, Fwβ 0.592) by large margins, while using fewer parameters (~3B vs. 7–13B) and only two foundation models. In the unsupervised setting, UCOD-MKD surpasses the weakly-supervised SAM-COD [6] (box supervision) on COD10K (MAE 0.031 vs. 0.028, Em 0.908 vs. 0.914) and NC4K (MAE 0.040 vs. 0.037, Em 0.918 vs. 0.923), remaining competitive despite requiring no manual annotations. Compared to fully-supervised RUN [20], the gap narrows substantially (e.g., COD10K Em 0.908 vs. 0.941), whereas prior UCOD methods lagged by over 10 points on Em. The method also runs faster (34.8 FPS, 50.7 MACs) than SAM-COD (31.2 FPS, 52.6 MACs) and PCOD (31.2 FPS, 52.6 MACs) with fewer parameters (60.3M vs. 62.6M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised COD (UCOD) line initiated by UCOS-DA and UCOD-DPL, which relied heavily on the DINO self-supervised backbone to extract supervision from unlabeled data. It departs from prior zero-shot COD methods (CVP, EASE, ProMaC, GenSAM) that use MLLMs and SAM purely at inference time, by instead leveraging them as a frozen teacher to generate graded pseudo-labels for training a lightweight student. The knowledge distillation paradigm from Hinton et al. is repurposed here with a key twist: rather than treating all pseudo-labels uniformly, the proposed GKD differentiates supervision strength at both image and pixel levels based on a quality grade assigned by the Graded Mask Evaluator, which itself builds on SAM's multi-candidate mask generation and the quality-over-quantity principle observed empirically in COD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a fully unsupervised pipeline (zero manual annotations) can match or exceed weakly-supervised COD methods that require per-image scribbles, boxes, or points, which is practically significant for deployment in search-and-rescue or ecological monitoring where labeling is infeasible. The quality-over-quantity finding (Fig. 2) — that even ~2% extremely low-quality pseudo-labels degrade COD distillation and >15% causes a sharp collapse — provides a useful design principle for any pseudo-label-based training in fine-grained segmentation. Open questions include whether the GME thresholds (60%/90%) generalize across domains, whether the approach scales to other visually non-salient tasks (shadow, medical), and whether the MLLM teacher's localization errors remain the bottleneck as model sizes grow.
