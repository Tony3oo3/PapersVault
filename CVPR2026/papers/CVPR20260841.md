## Problem

Existing SNN online training methods detach temporal gradients to keep GPU memory constant, but this introduces a forward-backward discrepancy because surrogate gradients depend on membrane potential values, making temporal gradient contributions non-constant and inseparable. Moreover, these methods only optimize training memory without providing any inference-stage advantages (accuracy, parameter memory, power, or NOPs) over STBP-trained models, limiting their practical deployment value.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts static RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event streams (DVS-CIFAR10) and outputs a single class label. Inputs are processed over T discrete time-steps (4 for static datasets, 10 for event data) through spiking neuron layers. A distinctive aspect is that synaptic weights are compressed to 1-bit ({−1,+1}) or 1.5-bit ({0,±1}) representations, and firing spikes can be further quantized to 4-bit integers, reducing parameter memory by ~15× compared to full-precision LIF baselines.

## Method

The HD-LIF neuron introduces a Precise-Positioning Reset (P2-Reset) mechanism: above the firing threshold, the membrane potential is reset to exactly θ and the transmitted spike equals the excess (m_t − θ_t) rather than a fixed value, making the surrogate gradient a piecewise constant independent of membrane potential. This achieves two theoretical properties proven in Theorem 4.2: (i) temporal gradient separability (ε[i,t] becomes a constant from a finite set), so online training gradients are equivalent to STBP gradients up to a known scaling factor, and (ii) spatial gradient alignment (no non-differentiable step in the firing process). The model family extends this with Parallel HD-LIF (eliminating leakage/charging to reduce NOPs to T ADDs), Mem-BN HD-LIF (temporal-dimension batch normalization on membrane potential that re-parameterizes into vanilla HD-LIF at inference via Eq. 8), and SECA (a 1D-convolution channel attention with O(K) parameters shared across time-steps). Training proceeds online by performing back-propagation at only one randomly selected time-step per batch, keeping GPU memory O(1) in T.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 4 time-steps), HD-LIF achieves 95.59% top-1 accuracy with only 2.82 MB parameters, outperforming GLIF (94.88% at 6 steps, 44.66 MB) and SLTT (94.44% at 6 steps, 44.66 MB). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 78.45% (4 steps, 3.00 MB) versus GLIF's 77.28% (6 steps, 44.84 MB) and SLTT's 74.38% (6 steps, 44.84 MB). On ImageNet-1k (ResNet-34, 4 steps), HD-LIF attains 69.77% with 10.06 MB parameters, surpassing SLTT (66.19% at 6 steps, 87.12 MB) by 3.58% while saving ~88% parameter memory. On DVS-CIFAR10 (VGG-SNN, 10 steps), it achieves 83.00%, exceeding NDOT (77.50%) by 5.50%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HD-LIF consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every dataset. On CIFAR-10, it beats the strongest STBP baseline GLIF [48] by 0.71% (95.59% vs. 94.88%) while using fewer time-steps (4 vs. 6) and ~16× less parameter memory (2.82 MB vs. 44.66 MB); against the online baseline SLTT [30], the margin is 1.15%. On CIFAR-100, the advantage over GLIF is 1.17% (78.45% vs. 77.28%) and over SLTT is 4.07% (78.45% vs. 74.38%). On ImageNet-200, HD-LIF surpasses ASGL [41] by 3.87% (60.68% vs. 56.81%) with only 4 time-steps versus 8, and exceeds DCT [8] by 3.78% while using 1/31 the time-steps (4 vs. 125). On ImageNet-1k, it leads SLTT [30] by 3.58% (69.77% vs. 66.19%) and OTTT [44] by 4.62% (69.77% vs. 65.15%), all with fewer steps and ~88% less parameter memory. On DVS-CIFAR10, HD-LIF (VGG-SNN) exceeds NDOT [20] by 5.50% (83.00% vs. 77.50%) and Dspike [27] by 7.60% (83.00% vs. 75.40%), again with dramatically reduced parameter memory (2.49 MB vs. 37.05/44.66 MB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online learning paradigm for SNNs introduced by Xiao et al. (OTTT, NeurIPS 2022) and Meng et al. (SLTT, ICCV 2023), which detach temporal gradients to achieve constant GPU memory but suffer from a forward-backward discrepancy. The key departure is architectural rather than algorithmic: by redesigning the LIF neuron with a Precise-Positioning Reset (P2-Reset) mechanism, the authors make the surrogate gradient independent of membrane potential values, thereby guaranteeing (via Theorem 4.2) that online-training gradients are provably separable from and equivalent to STBP gradients. The framework also repurposes ECA-Net's 1D convolution into a Spiking Efficient Channel Attention (SECA) module and incorporates 1-bit/1.5-bit synaptic weight compression, combining ideas from binary neural network literature with SNN-specific dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical bottleneck: STBP's linear GPU memory growth with time-steps makes deep SNNs with many temporal steps infeasible, and prior online methods sacrificed accuracy to fix this. By providing a theoretical guarantee of gradient separability rather than merely an empirical heuristic, the work gives practitioners a principled path to memory-efficient SNN training without the typical accuracy penalty. However, the training speed is notably slower than vanilla LIF (37.93 vs. 20.52 s/epoch in Table 2), and the framework is currently validated only on LIF-type neurons with CNN backbones; open questions include whether the P2-Reset mechanism generalizes to more complex neuron models (e.g., multi-compartment or dendritic neurons) and transformer-based SNN architectures, and whether the training-speed overhead can be further reduced.
