## Problem

Existing training-free caching methods for accelerating diffusion model inference (e.g., TeaCache, MagCache) rely on empirical heuristics—such as time-embedding differences or residual magnitudes—to decide when to reuse cached denoiser outputs. These heuristics lack theoretical justification, require extensive per-model hyperparameter tuning, and produce static caching schedules that cannot adapt to the varying difficulty of individual samples, leading to over-caching on challenging samples or under-caching on easy ones.

## Input / Output

SenCache is a training-free, architecture-agnostic wrapper around any DiT-based video diffusion model (Wan 2.1, CogVideoX, LTX-Video) that takes a text prompt and iteratively denoises a noisy latent through a standard ODE/SDE sampler, producing a video clip (e.g., 81 frames at 832×480 for Wan 2.1). The only additional input is a small calibration set of 8 diverse videos (from MixKit) used once to precompute sensitivity profiles, which are then cached and looked up during inference. The method is sampler-agnostic and modality-agnostic in principle, though experiments focus on text-to-video generation.

## Method

SenCache derives a first-order Taylor expansion bound showing that the denoiser output change between consecutive steps is bounded by ‖J_x‖‖Δx_t‖ + ‖J_t‖|Δt|, where J_x and J_t are the Jacobians of the denoiser with respect to the noisy latent and timestep. It defines a sensitivity score S_t combining both terms and reuses the cached output only when S_t falls below a user-specified tolerance ε, making the cache/reuse decision dynamic and sample-specific. The Jacobian norms are estimated via directional finite-difference (secant) approximations computed once on a small calibration set, avoiding expensive exact Jacobian computation at inference time. A maximum consecutive cache length n prevents error accumulation from the first-order approximation drifting as the trajectory evolves, and the framework provides a theoretical explanation for why prior heuristics (which capture only one of the two sensitivity terms) succeed in some regimes and fail in others.

## Results

On Wan 2.1 (T=50, 81 frames, 832×480), SenCache-fast achieves LPIPS 0.0540, PSNR 29.14 dB, and SSIM 0.9219 at 21 NFE (58% cache ratio), outperforming MagCache-fast (LPIPS 0.0603, PSNR 28.37) and TeaCache-fast (LPIPS 0.0966, PSNR 25.07) at the same compute budget. On CogVideoX, SenCache reaches LPIPS 0.1901 and SSIM 0.7786 at 22 NFE, beating MagCache (LPIPS 0.1952, SSIM 0.7332) and TeaCache (LPIPS 0.5855, SSIM 0.5702). On LTX-Video, SenCache attains LPIPS 0.1625, PSNR 23.67 dB, and SSIM 0.8293 at 27 NFE, surpassing MagCache (LPIPS 0.1795, SSIM 0.8224) and TeaCache (LPIPS 0.2763, SSIM 0.7270).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SenCache is compared against two full-forward caching baselines: TeaCache (CVPR 2025), which uses time-embedding difference as a skip trigger, and MagCache (arXiv 2025), which uses residual magnitude as a skip trigger. In the conservative (slow) regime on Wan 2.1, all three methods achieve nearly identical quality (LPIPS ≈ 0.039), but SenCache matches MagCache's 25 NFE while TeaCache requires 33 NFE. In the aggressive (fast) regime, SenCache clearly wins: it achieves LPIPS 0.0540 vs. MagCache's 0.0603 and TeaCache's 0.0966 at the same 21 NFE, a 10% LPIPS improvement over MagCache and a 44% improvement over TeaCache. On CogVideoX, SenCache (LPIPS 0.1901, SSIM 0.7786) beats MagCache (0.1952, 0.7332) at a lower NFE (22 vs. 23), while TeaCache degrades severely (LPIPS 0.5855). On LTX-Video, SenCache (LPIPS 0.1625, SSIM 0.8293) outperforms MagCache (0.1795, 0.8224) at fewer NFE (27 vs. 28), and TeaCache lags substantially (LPIPS 0.2763). Overall, SenCache wins or ties on every model and every metric, with the largest margins appearing in the fast regime and on CogVideoX where TeaCache's heuristic breaks down.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the caching-based acceleration line for diffusion/flow-matching inference, which includes TeaCache (time-embedding-difference heuristics), MagCache (residual-magnitude heuristics), and earlier feature-level methods like DeepCache and Δ-DiT. Its key departure is replacing these ad-hoc triggers with a first-order sensitivity criterion derived from the denoiser's Jacobian norms with respect to both the noisy latent and the timestep, thereby providing a theoretical justification for when reuse is safe. The authors show that TeaCache and MagCache each implicitly approximate one term of the sensitivity bound (timestep vs. latent) while ignoring the other, which explains their failure modes. The sensitivity concept itself is borrowed from the network-sensitivity/robustness literature and repurposed here as a dynamic, per-sample caching decision rule rather than a fixed schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding cache/reuse decisions in a local Lipschitz-style bound, SenCache offers a principled speed-quality knob (ε) and a sample-adaptive policy that prior static heuristics cannot provide, which is practically important for deploying large video DiTs where inference cost is the bottleneck. The finding that only 8 diverse calibration videos yield stable sensitivity profiles makes the approach nearly free to adopt. Open questions include whether higher-order or learned sensitivity estimators can tighten the bound in highly nonlinear regimes, whether dynamically scheduling ε across timesteps (exploiting the known criticality of early steps) can further improve the trade-off, and whether the principle extends cleanly to audio, text, or multimodal diffusion settings.
