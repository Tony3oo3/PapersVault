## Problem

Holistic text-to-motion methods treat the body as a monolithic entity, failing to capture fine-grained part-specific actions described in text (e.g., 'left leg lunge'). Part-wise methods improve part expressiveness by generating motions for individual body parts independently, but this introduces two critical failures: insufficient alignment between textual semantics and specific body parts, and incoherent full-body motions resulting from naively combining independently generated parts. ParTY targets this fundamental trade-off between part-specific expressiveness and full-body coherence.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text description of human motion as input, encoded via CLIP into a sentence embedding. It produces a 3D full-body human motion sequence represented as joint positions, generated autoregressively as discrete codebook tokens and decoded by a pre-trained VQ-VAE. The body is decomposed into arms and legs for part-level generation, while the holistic transformer produces the complete skeleton. Evaluation is conducted on HumanML3D (14,616 motions) and KIT-ML (3,911 motions) using standard pose representations.

## Method

ParTY operates in two stages: a Temporal-aware VQ-VAE quantizes motion sequences into discrete codebooks while preserving temporal structure via Local Temporal Enhancement (learned weighted summation within frame windows) and Global Temporal Enhancement (a GCN over group-level features), and a Part-Guided Network generates motion tokens autoregressively. The Part-aware Text Grounding module transforms a single CLIP embedding into K diverse embeddings through distinct MLPs trained with contrastive loss, then a gating network dynamically selects part-relevant embeddings for arms and legs, supervised during training by LLM-generated part-specific descriptions. During generation, part transformers first produce T-step part motion tokens that form Part Guidance, which conditions the holistic transformer; a Holistic-Part Fusion module continuously integrates part tokens into the holistic sequence via self-attention over concatenated tokens followed by cross-attention, ensuring part information is incorporated throughout the entire generation process rather than only at a final combination step.

## Results

On HumanML3D, ParTY achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.550, FID of 0.035, and MM-Dist of 2.779, outperforming the strongest holistic baseline MoMask (0.521, 0.045, 2.958) and the part-wise baseline ParCo (0.515, 0.109, 2.927). On KIT-ML, ParTY similarly leads with R-Precision Top-1 of 0.449, FID of 0.155, and MM-Dist of 2.694. In coherence-level evaluation, ParTY scores 0.88 TC and 0.92 SC, surpassing both MoMask (0.84/0.90) and ParCo (0.49/0.59), confirming that part-specific expressiveness and full-body coherence are simultaneously achieved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParTY outperforms all six baselines on R-Precision, FID, and MM-Dist across both HumanML3D and KIT-ML. Against the strongest holistic method MoMask [12], ParTY improves R-Precision Top-1 by 0.029 (0.550 vs 0.521) and reduces FID by 22% (0.035 vs 0.045) on HumanML3D, while also achieving higher coherence scores (TC 0.88 vs 0.84, SC 0.92 vs 0.90). Against the part-wise method ParCo [40], ParTY narrows the FID gap dramatically (0.035 vs 0.109 on HumanML3D) and more than doubles the temporal coherence score (0.88 vs 0.49), directly addressing ParCo's coherence deficiency. Against BAMM [25] and MMM [26], ParTY leads on all alignment and quality metrics but trails on KIT-ML MultiModality (1.166 vs 1.609 and 1.232 respectively), suggesting slightly less per-text diversity on the smaller dataset. On HumanML3D MultiModality, ParTY achieves the highest score (2.155), exceeding even MDM (2.799 is higher but MDM is far worse on all other metrics) and T2M-GPT (1.856). Overall, ParTY is the only method that simultaneously leads on text-motion alignment, motion quality, part-level expressiveness, and full-body coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParTY carves new ground at the intersection of two established but conflicting paradigms in text-to-motion generation: holistic methods (MoMask, BAMM, MMM) that treat the body as a monolithic entity, and part-wise methods (ParCo, LGTM, SCA) that decompose the body into independently generated segments. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes the VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer pipeline common to both camps and introduces a dual-generation Part-Guided Network that uses part motions as conditioning for holistic generation, alongside a Temporal-aware VQ-VAE that addresses a known limitation of fixed-window quantization. The use of LLM-generated part descriptions as auxiliary training signal echoes LGTM's text decomposition, but differs in retaining the full sentence embedding and using LLM output only for supervision rather than as the sole input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of part-level and coherence-level evaluation metrics (TC, SC) fills a genuine gap in the field, where prior metrics operated exclusively at the holistic level and could not diagnose the coherence failures of part-wise methods. The Temporal-aware VQ-VAE, which achieves 85–88% reconstruction FID improvement with minimal parameter overhead, is a practical component likely to be adopted by other VQ-VAE-based motion generation pipelines. Open questions include whether the part-guided framework generalizes beyond the binary arms/legs split to finer-grained part decompositions, whether the LLM auxiliary signal can be replaced by learned part-specific text encoders to remove the training-time dependency, and whether the added inference cost of separate part transformers becomes prohibitive for real-time applications.
