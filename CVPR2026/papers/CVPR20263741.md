## Problem

Existing distributed multi-view image compression (DMIC) methods, such as LDMIC, fuse side-view features via simple average pooling, assigning equal importance to all views regardless of their semantic relevance to the target view. This ignores occlusions and varying degrees of inter-view correlation, introducing noise and degrading reconstruction quality. The paper addresses this by proposing a mechanism to explicitly model and exploit the semantic relevance between arbitrary pairs of information sources during joint decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts an arbitrary number K of RGB multi-view images (tested with 2–6 views at resolutions from 1080×860 to over 4K) and produces independently encoded bitstreams per view plus jointly reconstructed images. A key constraint is that each view is encoded in isolation with no access to other views at the encoder side, while the decoder jointly reconstructs all views using cross-view information. The entropy model operates on quantized latent representations partitioned into 8 channel slices of 24 channels each, decoded sequentially with a checkerboard anchor/non-anchor scheme.

## Method

The core contribution is the OmniParallax Attention Mechanism (OPAM), which applies horizontal and vertical parallax attention sequentially to capture full 2D spatial context at O(N³) complexity (vs. O(N⁴) for full 2D self-attention), producing both an aligned feature map and a cycle-consistency map that quantifies semantic relevance between two sources. Building on OPAM, the Parallax Multi Information Fusion Module (PMIFM) adaptively fuses multiple side sources into a main source by weighting each source's aligned feature by its normalized consistency score. PMIFM is integrated into two components: the Parallax Joint Decoder (Para-JD), which refines each view's latent using all other views in two stages, and the Parallax Entropy Model (Para-EM), which replaces MLIC's channel-wise and global context modules with parallax-based versions (PCCM and PGCM) that aggregate context from all previously decoded channel slices. The entire ParaHydra framework is trained end-to-end with a standard rate-distortion loss using MSE or MS-SSIM distortion.

## Results

ParaHydra achieves BDBR savings of 19.72% (PSNR) and 24.18% (PSNR) over LDMIC on WildTrack with 3 and 6 views respectively, and 18.20% on Mip-NeRF 360 (3 views). On the 4K-resolution Mip-NeRF 360 dataset with 4 views, ParaHydra surpasses the SOTA MIC codec LMVIC by 34.11% in bitrate. In terms of computational efficiency, ParaHydra is up to 65× faster in decoding (0.33s vs. 21.43s) and 34× faster in encoding (0.27s vs. 9.27s) than LDMIC, while using fewer parameters (105.25M vs. 214.98M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParaHydra outperforms all seven baseline families across every dataset where they are comparable. Against traditional codecs VVC and MV-HEVC, ParaHydra achieves 25–106% BDBR savings in PSNR, confirming the superiority of learned DMIC over hand-crafted pipelines. Among stereo image compression methods (ECSIC, BiSIC, BiSIC-fast, CAMSIC), ParaHydra is the only method that achieves negative BDBR on both InStereo2K and Cityscapes, saving 6.92% and 1.42% in PSNR respectively, while all stereo baselines either match or underperform LDMIC. The most notable comparison is against LMVIC (SOTA MIC codec using 3D Gaussian priors at encoding): ParaHydra, which requires no inter-view information at the encoder, beats LMVIC by 3.9% (3 views) and 34.11% (4 views) on Mip-NeRF 360, making it the first DMIC method to surpass a joint-encoding MIC codec. Against the closest DMIC baseline LDMIC, ParaHydra's advantage grows with the number of views: from 6.92% (2 views) to 19.72% (3 views) to 24.18% (6 views) in PSNR BDBR, demonstrating that OPAM's multi-source correlation modeling scales effectively. LDMIC-fast, a speed-optimized variant, is also consistently outperformed by ParaHydra on all multi-view datasets while ParaHydra itself is 34× faster in encoding than full LDMIC.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParaHydra extends the distributed multi-view image compression (DMIC) paradigm introduced by LDMIC, which established that independent encoding with joint decoding can match joint encoding-decoding performance. The key departure is replacing LDMIC's naive average pooling with the OmniParallax Attention Mechanism (OPAM), a two-stage extension of parallax attention from stereo matching that captures full 2D spatial context at cubic complexity. The entropy model (Para-EM) is built on MLIC++'s checkerboard architecture, with its channel-wise and global context modules replaced by parallax-based variants (PCCM, PGCM) that exploit the same OPAM correlation signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first DMIC method to surpass a SOTA MIC codec (LMVIC, which uses 3D Gaussian geometric priors at encoding), suggesting that explicit inter-view correlation modeling at decode time can compensate for the absence of encoder-side geometric knowledge. The scalability finding—that bitrate savings grow from ~7% (2 views) to ~24% (6 views)—indicates the approach becomes more advantageous in dense multi-camera deployments, which is practically relevant for autonomous driving and VR. Open questions include whether the cubic-complexity OPAM remains tractable for very large view counts (e.g., 10+), whether the framework extends naturally to video (temporal dimension), and how sensitive performance is to camera calibration quality in uncontrolled settings.
