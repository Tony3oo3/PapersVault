## Problem

Existing 3D point tracking methods depend on dense depth estimation or scene reconstruction to recover geometric structure, incurring substantial computational overhead (from milliseconds to seconds per frame) and coupling tracking accuracy to the quality of a separately trained depth model. This makes real-time spatial tracking difficult and limits scalability to large-scale real-world data. The paper asks whether a purely feed-forward architecture can directly predict 3D trajectories from monocular video without any depth or pose prior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260975/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames and N query points (2D pixel coordinates in a reference frame) as input, and produces per-frame 2D coordinates, 3D coordinates in each frame's camera coordinate system (using a log-depth parameterization), and a visibility probability for every query point. The 2D head operates at a fixed 518×518 classification resolution scaled to match input at test time, while the 3D head regresses normalized coordinates with scale-invariant log-space supervision. No external depth map, camera pose, or scene reconstruction is required at inference.

## Method

The tracker is built on a pretrained π³ visual geometry transformer backbone (DINOv2 encoder + alternating global/frame-level self-attention) that extracts multi-scale geometry-grounded patch features. A query initialization mechanism samples these features at each query location across selected layers to form a global track embedding, which is then broadcast to all frames as initial frame-level embeddings. The core innovation is a dual-branch embedding decoder: a global branch applies multi-layer cross-attention (MLCA) over all frames followed by self-attention to maintain sequence-wide track coherence, while a frame-level branch applies the same structure restricted to each frame's features for fine-grained per-frame refinement. A cross-branch interaction module enables bidirectional information exchange (global-to-frame via concatenation+FFN, frame-to-global via cross-attention) after each decoding stage. The tracking head predicts 2D coordinates via a classification-based decoder with truncated soft argmax, 3D coordinates via log-depth regression, and visibility via a sigmoid FFN, all trained jointly with a multi-task loss.

## Results

On the TAPVid-3D benchmark, the proposed method achieves SOTA among Type II (depth-free) trackers with an average AJ of 19.0 and ADP of 28.9, surpassing DELTA (AJ 17.8, ADP 26.3) and SpaTrackerV2-Type-II (AJ 18.7, ADP 27.9). It also outperforms TAPIP3D (Type III) on AJ (19.0 vs 18.8) and ADP (28.9 vs 27.4), though it trails SpaTrackerV2-Type-III (AJ 21.4, ADP 31.0) by roughly 2%. In terms of speed, the method runs at 28 ms/frame end-to-end with no depth prior, compared to 35–309 ms/frame plus ~1.2 s of MegaSaM depth estimation for all competing methods. On 2D TAP-Vid, it achieves AJ 61.4, δvis 74.6, and OA 89.8, the best δvis and OA among 3D trackers but trailing the best 2D tracker AllTracker (AJ 67.8, δvis 79.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among Type II (depth-free) methods, the proposed tracker achieves the best average AJ (19.0) and ADP (28.9), beating DELTA with MegaSaM by +1.2 AJ and +2.6 ADP, and SpaTrackerV2-Type-II by +0.3 AJ and +1.0 ADP, though it trails both on OA (85.5 vs 86.4/90.5). Against Type III methods that use explicit camera pose optimization, it surpasses TAPIP3D on AJ (+0.2) and ADP (+1.5) but remains ~2.4 ADP behind SpaTrackerV2-Type-III (28.9 vs 31.0). The most striking advantage is inference speed: at 28 ms/frame with no external depth model, it is 1.2–11× faster than competitors that require 1.2 s of MegaSaM depth estimation plus 35–309 ms of tracking (e.g., 43× faster than SpaTrackerV2's 309 ms + 1.2 s pipeline). On 2D TAP-Vid, it achieves the best δvis (74.6) and OA (89.8) among 3D trackers but lags the dedicated 2D SOTA AllTracker by 6.4 AJ and 4.9 δvis, which the authors attribute to the coarser patch resolution of the geometry transformer backbone. Overall, the method trades a small OA gap for substantially higher ADP and a large speed advantage, positioning it as the fastest competitive 3D point tracker.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the visual geometry transformer lineage (VGGT, π³) for feature extraction and on TAPNext's classification-based tracking head and loss formulation, but departs fundamentally from the dominant 3D tracking paradigm that relies on explicit dense depth estimation (SpatialTracker, DELTA) or joint depth-pose reconstruction (SpatialTrackerV2, TAPIP3D). The key conceptual shift is treating the geometry transformer's implicit multi-view 3D understanding as sufficient for spatial tracking, eliminating the separate depth model entirely. The dual-branch (global + frame-level) decoder with cross-branch interaction is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior 3D trackers, which typically use iterative refinement or correlational features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic 40×+ speedup (28 ms vs >1.5 s per frame) while maintaining SOTA Type-II accuracy makes real-time 3D point tracking practically viable for applications like AR and robotics that were previously bottlenecked by depth estimation. The finding that explicit depth is unnecessary challenges the field's working assumption that geometric priors must be computed separately. Open questions include whether the quadratic-attention backbone can be adapted to streaming/long-sequence settings, whether metric scale can be recovered without a dedicated head, and whether incorporating optional camera poses would close the remaining ~2% gap to Type-III methods.
