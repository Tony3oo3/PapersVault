## Problem

Transformer-based 3D lifting models achieve multi-category scalability through permutation equivariance, but this property destroys token identity, making 2D-only supervision impossible and forcing reliance on expensive 3D labels. MLP-based lifters (PAUL, C3DPO) learn from 2D supervision alone but require manually designed per-category architectures that cannot generalize. The paper asks whether a single foundation model can perform accurate 3D lifting from 2D landmarks across 45+ diverse object categories without any 3D supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260433/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N 2D keypoints (typically N ∈ [10, 25]) from a single image as input, with no RGB appearance, temporal cues, or multi-view geometry. It outputs N corresponding 3D keypoints in millimeters, recovered up to an unknown global scale and rotation via differentiable Procrustes alignment. For multi-category training, all categories are padded to a shared N_max with binary masks, and the 2D reprojection loss is applied only to valid (unmasked) keypoints.

## Method

The core contribution is injecting correspondence positional encoding (CPE) into every attention layer's Q, K, and V projections, rather than adding PE once at the input as in standard ViTs. This per-layer injection continuously reinforces spatial token identity, breaking the permutation equivariance that Proposition 1 shows makes 2D-only lifting non-identifiable. The PE itself uses an analytical Random Fourier Feature schedule (deterministic frequencies via inverse Gaussian CDF, σ=2.5) or graph Laplacian eigenvectors, applied to 2D keypoint coordinates rather than token indices. The architecture is a standard transformer (6–24 layers, 8–16 heads, D=256–1024) with a learnable category embedding, trained end-to-end with only the 2D reprojection loss (minimizing ‖X − sPRŶᵀ‖² over scale s and rotation R). A single ~25M-parameter model handles 45+ categories, with per-layer PE adding <2% FLOPs over a vanilla transformer.

## Results

On Pascal3D+, 2D-LFM achieves 8.1 mm MPJPE using only 2D supervision, compared to 5.2 mm for the 3D-supervised 3D-LFM and 9.4 mm for PAUL. On Human3.6M, 2D-LFM reaches 30.9 mm MPJPE, substantially outperforming 3D-LFM (46.3 mm), PAUL (88.3 mm), and C3DPO (95.6 mm). Scene-level depth back-projection via VGGT yields 89.4 mm (Pascal3D+) and 107.8 mm (Human3.6M), while naive transformers with input-only positional encoding fail catastrophically at 92.3 mm and 52.4 mm respectively. The per-layer PE injection strategy is the critical factor: switching from input-only to per-layer injection reduces Pascal3D+ error from >90 mm to 8.1 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 3D-supervised baseline, 3D-LFM, 2D-LFM trades a small Pascal3D+ gap (8.1 vs 5.2 mm) for a large Human3.6M win (30.9 vs 46.3 mm), all without any 3D labels. Against MLP-based 2D-only methods, 2D-LFM dominates: it beats PAUL by 1.3 mm on Pascal3D+ and by a massive 57.4 mm on Human3.6M (30.9 vs 88.3), and outperforms C3DPO by 6.9 mm and 64.7 mm respectively. Against scene-level reconstruction (VGGT), 2D-LFM is superior by over 80 mm on both benchmarks, confirming that dense depth back-projection is fundamentally unsuitable for object-specific landmark geometry. Against SimpleBaseline3D (3D-supervised), 2D-LFM wins on both datasets (8.1 vs 8.7 mm; 30.9 vs 52.1 mm). The ablation rows in Table 1 further show that the per-layer injection strategy is what separates success from failure: input-only PE variants remain in the 35–92 mm range, while per-layer injection drops to 8.1 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical non-rigid SfM (Tomasi-Kanade factorization, PAUL, C3DPO) and modern transformer-based lifting (3D-LFM, NRSfM-Transformer). It departs from the MLP-based line by adopting a transformer backbone for cross-category scalability, and from the transformer line by eliminating the 3D supervision requirement that prior work considered fundamental. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting positional encoding not as spatial context but as a correspondence-restoring mechanism: the authors show that standard input-only PE is insufficient because attention mixing re-establishes permutation symmetry, and that per-layer injection of Fourier-feature-based PE is the minimal architectural change needed to break this symmetry and enable 2D-only learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical and empirical argument that correspondence is a necessary (not merely helpful) condition for single-frame non-rigid 3D lifting, formalized in Proposition 1 and validated by the dramatic gap between input-only PE (>100 mm) and per-layer PE (8.1 mm). Practically, it removes the 3D-label bottleneck for multi-category lifting, enabling foundation-model-scale training from 2D keypoints alone with strong low-data transfer (up to 92% improvement for rare categories). Limitations include residual monocular ambiguities (depth flips, foreshortening), the ~3 mm gap to 3D-supervised 3D-LFM on Pascal3D+, and the fact that the method still requires known landmark definitions per category. Open questions include whether per-layer PE injection generalizes to other geometric tasks (e.g., dense reconstruction, multi-view) and whether the approach can be combined with motion or physical priors to resolve single-frame ambiguities.
