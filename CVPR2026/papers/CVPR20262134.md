## Problem

Existing visual odometry methods are trained and evaluated at fixed observation frequencies (e.g., 10 Hz or 12 Hz) and typically require calibrated cameras with known intrinsics, causing severe performance degradation when deployed under unseen frame rates or applied to uncalibrated dashcam footage. This limits their generalizability for real-world trajectory reconstruction from Internet-sourced videos where frame rates and camera parameters vary widely across devices.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of consecutive RGB dashcam frames at arbitrary spatial resolution along with the video's frame rate f as an explicit conditioning signal. Camera intrinsics are inferred on-the-fly via WildCamera and metric depth via Metric3Dv2, so no pre-calibration is needed. The output is a relative camera pose in SE(3) at real-world metric scale: rotation R ∈ SO(3) parameterized probabilistically via a Fisher matrix, and translation t ∈ R³ predicted directly in world coordinates, with no post-hoc SE(3) or Sim(3) alignment applied.

## Method

OpenVO combines three key components: (1) a Time-Aware Flow Encoder that extracts optical flow via MaskFlowNet, injects frame-rate information through sinusoidal positional encoding of Δt = 1/f to modulate flow features via lightweight alpha/beta conditioning layers, and constructs a differentiable 2D-guided 3D flow field by back-projecting pixels with metric depth and warping via optical flow; (2) a Geometry-Aware Context Encoder that builds a normalized intrinsic ray field from WildCamera-predicted intrinsics and a depth-modulated 3D point distribution from Metric3Dv2, processed by eight self-attention layers; and (3) a World-Coordinate Egomotion Decoder that fuses both representations to predict rotation via Fisher matrix distribution and translation via a metric-scale regression MLP. Training employs multi-time-scale augmentation (sub-sampling at 4, 6, and 12 Hz) to expose the model to diverse temporal dynamics, with the time-conditioned encoder adapting its embeddings to each observation frequency.

## Results

OpenVO achieves state-of-the-art visual odometry across three benchmarks: on KITTI 00-10 (10 Hz), it attains an ATE of 93.23 m (24% lower than ZeroVO‡ at 123.42 m); on nuScenes (12 Hz), an ATE of 5.91 m (29.6% lower than ZeroVO‡ at 8.40 m); and on Argoverse 2 (10 Hz), an ATE of 2.39 m (58% lower than ZeroVO‡ at 5.71 m). Under varying inference observation rates (2.5–20 Hz), OpenVO reduces trajectory errors by 46%–92% compared to ZeroVO‡, demonstrating substantially greater temporal robustness. The method also outperforms ZeroVO+ (which uses additional text guidance and YouTube self-training data) on all three datasets without any auxiliary supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most directly comparable baseline ZeroVO‡ (same Metric3Dv2 depth + WildCamera intrinsics, no text or YouTube data), OpenVO√ reduces ATE by 24% on KITTI (93.23 vs 123.42), 29.6% on nuScenes (5.91 vs 8.40), and 58% on Argoverse 2 (2.39 vs 5.71), while also improving t_err and s_err on all three datasets. OpenVO also surpasses ZeroVO+ (which adds text guidance and large-scale YouTube self-training) on KITTI ATE (93.23 vs 104.69) and Argoverse 2 ATE (2.39 vs 3.05), and matches it on nuScenes (5.91 vs 6.03), all without any auxiliary supervision. Compared to XVO, OpenVO achieves substantially lower ATE across all benchmarks (e.g., 93.23 vs 168.43 on KITTI), and similarly outperforms the Metric3Dv2+DroidSLAM pipeline (M+DS) by large margins. The non-differentiable variant OpenVO× already achieves competitive results (e.g., 109.01 ATE on KITTI), confirming that the geometric context encoder and time-aware flow conditioning are the primary drivers of generalization, with the differentiable 3D flow providing additional gains. On the varying-rate ablation (Table 4), OpenVO consistently reduces errors by 46%–92% over ZeroVO‡ across all tested frequencies (2.5–20 Hz), whereas ZeroVO‡ degrades severely (e.g., ATE jumps from 553.52 at 2.5 Hz to 36.14 at 20 Hz on Argoverse 2).

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenVO extends the generalized visual odometry lineage (XVO → ZeroVO) by addressing two gaps those methods leave open: fixed observation frequency and reliance on known camera intrinsics. It reuses the two-frame pose regression setup and Fisher-matrix rotation formulation from ZeroVO/XVO, the MaskFlowNet flow backbone, and the metric-scale regression module from TartanVO, but introduces a time-conditioned flow encoder (sinusoidal positional encoding of frame rate modulating correlation features) and a differentiable 2D-guided 3D flow module that were absent in all prior VO frameworks. The geometry-aware context encoder, combining WildCamera intrinsics and Metric3Dv2 depth as transformer tokens, departs from ZeroVO's language-prior approach by grounding geometric awareness purely in inferred camera parameters and metric depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for extracting metric-scale trajectories from uncalibrated, variable-frame-rate dashcam videos (e.g., YouTube crash footage), a task with clear downstream value for dataset construction, simulation, and driving VQA. The 46–92% error reduction under unseen observation rates, validated across three benchmarks with ablations isolating the temporal-encoding contribution, provides strong evidence that temporal overfitting is a real and previously unaddressed failure mode in VO. Open questions remain: the multi-time-scale training frequencies are set empirically, depth and intrinsic estimation errors propagate independently, and an adaptive, on-the-fly sampling strategy could further improve robustness. A more principled joint optimization of calibration, depth, and pose estimation would likely reduce error compounding.
