## Problem

SAM's segmentation quality depends heavily on prompt quality, yet existing automatic prompt generation methods (e.g., PPO) treat prompts as static inputs optimized in isolation from SAM's segmentation feedback, making them highly sensitive to initial prompt quality and poor at generalizing across domains. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether prompt optimization can be made adaptive and robust through direct coordination with SAM's segmentation output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes an RGB target image and a set of initial point prompts (typically generated via feature matching from a single reference image) and outputs a refined subset of those prompts along with the resulting SAM segmentation mask. The defense agent operates on a dual-space graph built from DINOv3 patch features of the target image, so no task-specific labels or reference masks are needed at test time. Only the defense agent is deployed, making the system plug-and-play across arbitrary prompt configurations and downstream tasks.

## Method

PPD constructs a task-agnostic dual-space graph where image patches are nodes and edges encode both DINOv3 feature distances and physical (geometric) distances, providing both semantic and spatial structure for prompt reasoning. Two adversarial agents trained with DQN operate in this environment: an attacker that activates inactive prompts to maximize segmentation degradation (reward = negative Dice change) and a defender that deactivates active prompts to restore accuracy (reward = positive Dice change). Training alternates between the two agents over episodes initialized from ideal prompts sampled from ground-truth masks, so the defender learns to counteract diverse adversarial perturbations. At inference, only the defender is deployed to filter arbitrary noisy prompt sets, leveraging the implicit structural cues in the dual-space graph to identify and suppress harmful prompts without any retraining or task-specific supervision.

## Results

On the one-shot SAM-based segmentation benchmark, FM-PPD achieves 73.0% mDSC / 63.3% mIoU on PASCAL VOC, 76.3% mDSC / 64.2% mIoU on ISIC, and 64.1% mDSC / 54.9% mIoU on Kvasir, outperforming all five baselines on every dataset and metric. The gains are most pronounced on the medical datasets: on Kvasir, FM-PPD surpasses the next-best method (FM-PPO) by 21.2 mDSC and 23.4 mIoU, and on ISIC by 6.9 mDSC and 3.8 mIoU. On PASCAL VOC, FM-PPD leads Matcher (the strongest natural-image baseline) by 3.8 mDSC and 1.8 mIoU. In the ablation, the defense agent recovers segmentation from 34.2% to 83.5% mDSC on PASCAL VOC after adversarial attack, and improves feature-matching prompts by +31.7 mDSC on PASCAL VOC and +32.7 mDSC on Kvasir.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM-PPD wins against all five baselines on every dataset and metric. Against Matcher (the strongest natural-image baseline at 69.2/61.5 on PASCAL VOC), FM-PPD gains +3.8 mDSC and +1.8 mIoU, while the gap widens dramatically on medical data: +10.9 mDSC on ISIC and +30.9 mDSC on Kvasir. Against FM-PPO (the closest RL-based competitor), FM-PPD leads by +11.3 mDSC on PASCAL VOC, +6.9 mDSC on ISIC, and +21.2 mDSC on Kvasir, confirming that the adversarial dual-agent training provides substantially better generalization than single-agent PPO. VRP-SAM, which relies on a learned visual reference prompt encoder, collapses on medical datasets (5.9 mDSC on ISIC, 0.0 on Kvasir), highlighting the fragility of task-specific prompt encoders under domain shift. PerSAM and PerSAM-F, which use only one or a few prompts, remain the weakest baselines across all settings. GBMSeg, a heuristic geometric prompt method, is outperformed by FM-PPD by +16.3 mDSC on PASCAL VOC and +26.0 mDSC on Kvasir.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of SAM prompt optimization work, building directly on the RL-based Point Prompt Optimizer (PPO) [21] and geometric prompt engineering methods like Matcher [22] and GBMSeg [19]. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of an adversarial two-agent (attack/defense) DQN framework within a dual-space graph environment using DINOv3 features, rather than the single-agent optimization in PPO or the heuristic prompt generation in Matcher/GBMSeg. By coupling prompt actions directly to SAM's segmentation feedback during training and deploying only the defense agent at inference, PPD shifts from static or supervised prompt strategies to a dynamic, feedback-driven adversarial paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PPD offers a practical, training-free, plug-and-play mechanism to refine noisy or suboptimal point prompts for SAM across diverse domains without task-specific fine-tuning, which is valuable for automated segmentation pipelines where manual prompting is impractical. The strong cross-domain generalization (trained on FSS-1000, evaluated on medical datasets) suggests the adversarial training effectively captures domain-agnostic prompt utility. However, the method still depends on the quality of initial prompts from feature matching, and the paper acknowledges that integrating more adaptive prompt generation (e.g., text-guided) could further improve cold-start robustness. An open question is whether the adversarial framework generalizes beyond point prompts to box or mask prompts, or to other foundation models beyond SAM.
