## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods require dense multi-view captures from camera arrays of dozens to hundreds of synchronized devices, confining 4D reconstruction to research labs. Under extremely sparse-view settings (e.g., four cameras), 4DGS suffers from a biased optimization that overfits to easy appearance cues while failing to recover clean geometry due to insufficient spatial supervision, producing noisy and temporally inconsistent novel views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes synchronized RGB video from as few as four portable cameras (GoPro-style, 1920×1080 at 60 FPS, 300 frames per scene) along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces an explicit 4D Gaussian representation with time-varying position, rotation, opacity, scale, and 4D spherical harmonics coefficients. The output supports real-time novel-view rendering at arbitrary viewpoints and timesteps via rasterization, with the entire capture setup costing under 1,500 USD.

## Method

4C4D introduces a Neural Decaying Function f_θ, a lightweight neural network that takes each 4D Gaussian's center, opacity, and rotation as input and predicts a decay factor τ that neurally modulates the Gaussian's temporal opacity (Eq. 6), thereby redirecting optimization gradients toward geometric learning and mitigating the appearance-geometry imbalance inherent in sparse-view 4DGS. A visibility detection strategy operating in both spatial (view intersection) and temporal (time intersection) domains identifies which Gaussians contribute to rendering at each view and timestep. A separate decay strategy is then applied: visible Gaussians receive the learned neural decay factor, while invisible Gaussians receive a small constant decay (β=0.999) to stabilize training. The Neural Decaying Function and the 4D Gaussian primitives are jointly optimized via gradient backpropagation under a standard photometric rendering loss, with no additional supervision required.

## Results

On the Neural3DV dataset (4-camera sparse-view setting), 4C4D achieves 22.29 PSNR, 0.098 DSSIM₁, 0.062 DSSIM₂, and 0.146 LPIPS, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. On ENeRF-Outdoor, 4C4D reaches 24.32 PSNR and 0.121 LPIPS, surpassing the closest competitor 4DGS (23.52 PSNR, 0.151 LPIPS). On Mobile-Stage, 4C4D attains 22.36 PSNR and 0.121 LPIPS, again edging out 4DGS (22.15 PSNR, 0.180 LPIPS). The method consistently wins on all four metrics across all three quantitative benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4C4D outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three quantitative datasets. The largest gains are over ST-GS, which lags by 4.59 dB in PSNR on Neural3DV (17.70 vs. 22.29) and by 0.179 in LPIPS (0.325 vs. 0.146), indicating severe geometric and perceptual degradation under sparse views. Ex4DGS (19.33 PSNR) and 4DGaussians (20.82 PSNR) also trail 4C4D by 2.96 and 1.47 dB respectively on Neural3DV. The closest competitor is 4DGS, which achieves 23.52 PSNR on ENeRF-Outdoor and 22.15 PSNR on Mobile-Stage, but 4C4D still leads by 0.80 and 0.21 dB respectively, with a more pronounced LPIPS advantage (0.121 vs. 0.151 and 0.180). On the self-captured Dyn4Cam dataset (no held-out test views), 4C4D produces visibly more temporally stable and geometrically faithful renderings than all baselines, though no quantitative metrics are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS), which extends 3DGS to dynamic scenes by embedding time-aware attributes (temporal center, duration, 4D spherical harmonics) into Gaussian primitives. It departs from prior 4DGS and deformation-based approaches (4DGaussians, Ex4DGS, ST-GS) by reframing the core challenge not as a representation problem but as an optimization imbalance: under sparse views, appearance fitting is easy while geometry recovery is starved of spatial supervision. The Neural Decaying Function—a small network predicting a per-Gaussian opacity decay factor—and the spatial-temporal visibility detection strategy are the paper's two architectural departures from the standard 4DGS training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: reconstructing temporally consistent 4D scenes from four consumer GoPro cameras (under $1,500 total) rather than 18–24 synchronized research-grade cameras opens 4D content creation to everyday users and small studios. The introduced Dyn4Cam dataset provides the first benchmark for truly portable-camera 4D reconstruction. Open questions include whether the neural decaying mechanism transfers to other Gaussian-based dynamic representations (e.g., deformation-based 3DGS), how performance degrades below four cameras, and whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences or unbounded scenes without the current per-scene optimization.
