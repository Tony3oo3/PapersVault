## Problem

Prior class-incremental surgical instrument segmentation methods treat each instrument's prompt independently, overlooking the potential of positive forward knowledge transfer (how shared structural knowledge from past instruments aids new-class learning) and positive backward knowledge transfer (how discovering new instrument features can refine and improve previously learned representations). This paper addresses how to unlock both directions of positive transfer while avoiding catastrophic forgetting in a sequential, privacy-constrained training setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264032/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of surgical video datasets (e.g., EndoVis 2017→2018, CholecSeg8k→M2CAI-Seg), where each episode provides N_v RGB frames of H×W resolution with per-instrument segmentation masks, and only the current episode's data is accessible due to medical privacy constraints. At each episode, new, regular, and old instrument classes coexist in the frames. The model outputs a per-instrument segmentation mask for every class seen so far, evaluated by per-class IoU, Backward Transfer (BWT), and Forward Transfer (FWT) metrics across all episodes.

## Method

The framework builds on a frozen pre-trained backbone (SAM ViT-H or DeepLabv3+) with instrument-aware prompts inserted at multiple decoder depths via cross-attention. For positive forward transfer, it organizes prompts into a three-layer hierarchical prompt parsing tree (instrument-shared root → n-part-shared intermediate → instrument-distinct leaf), so that learning a new instrument only requires training its leaf-node partition while inheriting shared knowledge from the root and matching intermediate node. For positive backward transfer, it constructs a directed-weighted graph rooted at the new leaf node with edge weights decaying by γ^distance, then applies directed graph convolution (with a PageRank-style teleportation term) to propagate self-reflection updates that refine existing prompt partitions proportionally to their topological proximity, improving representativeness without destabilizing distant or old-class knowledge.

## Results

On the porcine robot-assisted nephrectomy benchmark (EndoVis 2017+2018), the proposed method with DeepLabv3+ achieves 40.92% average IoU across all classes, outperforming the best SOTA (CAT-SD, 35.09%) by 5.83 points, while with SAM backbone it reaches 59.44% All IoU, approaching the jointly-trained upper bound (Joint-T, 63.16%). On the laparoscopic cholecystectomy benchmark (CholecSeg8k+M2CAI-Seg), the method achieves 56.12% All IoU (DeepLabv3+) and 62.58% (SAM), surpassing the best SOTA (CAT-SD, 51.74%) by 4.38 points. Critically, the method is the only one to achieve positive Backward Transfer (BWT=6.22 with DeepLabv3+ on EndoVis; BWT=3.24 on Cholec), whereas all baselines show negative BWT, indicating that learning new instruments actually degrades old-class performance in prior work.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 11 class-incremental segmentation baselines (SI, LWF, ILT, PLOP, MiB, RASD, RCIL, EWF, LISM, CoinSeg, CAT-SD) plus a sequential fine-tuning baseline (Seq-F) and a jointly-trained upper bound (Joint-T). With the DeepLabv3+ backbone on EndoVis, the proposed method outperforms the best SOTA (CAT-SD) by 5.83 points in All IoU (40.92 vs. 35.09), 2.70 points on old classes (22.46 vs. 19.76), and 8.87 points on regular classes (59.79 vs. 50.92). The most striking difference is in BWT: the method achieves +6.22 while every baseline is negative (ranging from -0.88 for CAT-SD to -9.92 for LWF), confirming that prior methods suffer catastrophic forgetting when learning new instruments. On the cholecystectomy benchmark, the method leads CAT-SD by 4.38 points in All IoU (56.12 vs. 51.74) and 3.37 points in BWT (3.24 vs. -0.13). With the SAM backbone, the method reaches 59.44% All IoU on EndoVis, within 3.72 points of the Joint-T upper bound (63.16%), and 62.58% on Cholec, within 1.09 points of Joint-T (63.67%), demonstrating that the hierarchical prompt framework nearly closes the gap to the oracle setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior prompt-based continual learning methods (L2P, DualPrompt) that treat each class's prompt as an independent, frozen unit after training. Instead, it organizes instrument prompts into a three-level hierarchical tree (instrument-shared → n-part-shared → instrument-distinct), enabling new classes to inherit reusable knowledge from the past (positive forward transfer) and existing knowledge to be refined via directed graph propagation (positive backward transfer). The self-reflection mechanism repurposes directed graph convolution (DiGIn) from its original graph-classification context to refine prompt partitions across a knowledge hierarchy. It is also the first work to apply SAM as a frozen backbone for class-incremental surgical instrument segmentation, extending the CIL literature beyond CNN-based models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The positive backward transfer result (BWT > 0 while all baselines are negative) is a meaningful conceptual shift: learning new instruments can genuinely improve old-instrument segmentation rather than merely failing to degrade it. However, the experimental scope is limited to only two incremental episodes and two dataset pairs, leaving open whether the hierarchical structure and self-reflection mechanism scale to longer sequences (e.g., 5–10 episodes) where catastrophic forgetting compounds. The assumption that part-wise compositional information (number of instrument parts) is available at inference time is a practical constraint that may not always hold in clinical settings, and the paper acknowledges this only briefly in supplementary material.
