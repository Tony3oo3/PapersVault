## Problem

Human action recognition under low-light conditions remains largely unexplored: existing HAR datasets are collected in well-lit settings, and no large-scale paired RGB-thermal dataset exists for dark-environment action recognition. Moreover, benchmarking reveals that both unimodal models and multimodal fusion frameworks degrade substantially in low illumination, and even state-of-the-art vision-language foundation models achieve below 20% Top-1 accuracy in zero-shot low-light settings, exposing a critical gap in illumination-robust multimodal perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262680/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes precisely synchronized RGB and thermal video clips (30 fps, 4.5–19.1 s duration) captured by a binocular Dahua DH-TPC-BF2241 camera across 27 action categories, multiple viewpoints (upward, eye-level, downward), and varied distances. Each clip is processed through separate modality-specific embedding branches before fusion. The output is a single action class label predicted by a linear classifier over flattened, concatenated final-stage features from both modalities, trained with standard cross-entropy loss.

## Method

DarkAct-Net builds on an MViTv2-B Transformer backbone with two novel modules. The Motion-Aware Attention (MAA) module computes temporal motion saliency via sine-mapped frame differences and combines it with a spatial-tolerant query (concatenated max-pool and avg-pool projections) to perform channel-dimension attention that highlights human-centric dynamic regions while suppressing noisy backgrounds and tolerating RGB-thermal pixel misalignment. The Light-Adaptive Fusion (LAF) module is inserted after every Transformer stage and adaptively integrates cross-modal information through a dynamic attention mechanism: the query fuses motion-enhanced and stage features from both modalities, modality-specific keys are extracted via dilated convolutions with sigmoid gating, and the value is constructed from bidirectional cross-attention between RGB and thermal features followed by hierarchical pooling. This design allows the network to weight each modality according to scene illumination and modality reliability, achieving 74.4% Top-1 accuracy—substantially outperforming the best unimodal (69.1% thermal) and prior multimodal (52.7%) baselines.

## Results

On the DarkAct dataset, DarkAct-Net achieves 74.4% Top-1 and 92.9% Top-5 accuracy in the multimodal RGB-thermal setting, outperforming the best unimodal thermal model (MViTv2-B, 69.1% Top-1) by 5.3 points and the best unimodal RGB model (Conv2Former, 62.1% Top-1) by 12.3 points. Among existing multimodal fusion baselines, the strongest is CMX at 52.6% Top-1, which DarkAct-Net surpasses by 21.8 points. In zero-shot evaluation, all tested VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen3-VL-7B, etc.) score below 20% Top-1 accuracy, with the best (Qwen3-VL-7B) reaching only 18.1% on combined RGB-thermal input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DarkAct-Net (74.4% Top-1) decisively outperforms all 18 unimodal baselines: it beats the best RGB model (Conv2Former, 62.1%) by 12.3 points and the best thermal model (MViTv2-B, 69.1%) by 5.3 points. Against the 8 existing multimodal fusion baselines, DarkAct-Net leads the strongest competitor CMX (52.6%) by a substantial 21.8 points, with the gap over the second-best MRFS (52.7%) being 21.7 points. Notably, most multimodal fusion methods (CMX, EGFNet, CDDFuse, SegMiF, FTransUNet, MRFS, CFFormer, DFformerv2) actually underperform the best single-modality thermal model, indicating that standard fusion architectures are ill-suited for low-light RGB-thermal action recognition. In the zero-shot VLM evaluation, all 10 tested foundation models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen3-VL-7B, Claude 4.0 Sonnet, etc.) score below 20% Top-1, with Qwen3-VL-7B achieving the highest at 18.1% on combined input—far below DarkAct-Net's supervised 74.4%. The ablation confirms both MAA and LAF contribute: removing MAA drops Top-1 by 3.2 points and removing LAF by 2.1 points, with removing both causing a 3.5-point drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of low-light perception and multimodal action recognition. While HAR has been extensively studied on RGB datasets under normal illumination (UCF101, Kinetics-400) and low-light RGB-only settings (ARID), no prior work provides a large-scale paired RGB-thermal benchmark for dark-environment action recognition. The proposed DarkAct-Net repurposes temporal-difference-based motion saliency (common in video understanding) into a channel-attention mechanism (MAA) and introduces a dynamic cross-modal fusion module (LAF) that adapts to illumination reliability, departing from fixed-weight or early-fusion strategies used in prior RGB-thermal segmentation and detection work. The MViTv2 backbone is adopted as a standard Transformer encoder, so the architectural novelty lies in the fusion and attention modules rather than the backbone itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DarkAct fills a clear and previously unaddressed gap: a large-scale, multi-view, multi-scene RGB-thermal dataset for low-light HAR, enabling systematic study of how illumination degrades recognition and how thermal sensing compensates. The benchmarking reveals that even state-of-the-art VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude 4.0) drop below 20% Top-1 accuracy, and that most existing multimodal fusion architectures underperform single-modality thermal baselines—findings that challenge the assumption that general-purpose fusion or foundation models transfer readily to adverse-illumination settings. Open questions include whether the MAA/LAF design generalizes to other low-light tasks (detection, tracking), whether the 27-class taxonomy captures sufficient behavioral diversity, and whether the geographic and demographic scope (11 volunteers, central China) limits external validity.
