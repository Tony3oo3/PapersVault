## Problem

Conventional CBCT reconstruction assumes monochromatic X-ray attenuation, which breaks down in the presence of high-attenuation metals and produces severe beam-hardening streaks and shading. Existing neural-field-based polychromatic methods (e.g., Polyner) address this physics but suffer from oversmoothing, noise amplification, and prohibitive runtime in full 3D cone-beam geometry, while faster splat-based methods remain limited to monochromatic models and cannot explain metal-induced nonlinearity.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D CBCT projection sinograms acquired over a single rotation (e.g., 90 kVp with 1.0 mm Al filtration) together with a simulated X-ray spectrum response η(E) over 10–90 keV (N=15 uniformly spaced energy bins from SPEKTR). It outputs a 3D attenuation volume evaluated at the effective energy and the per-Gaussian parameters (position, covariance, density, and a scalar material parameter u_i). Notably, no metal mask or paired supervision is required; the material parameter is inferred jointly during optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a compact, physically grounded material parameterization that approximates each Gaussian's mass attenuation coefficient (MAC) curve with a single scalar u_i interpolating along a quadratic Bézier curve defined by three NIST-derived control vectors (minimum, intermediate, maximum MAC across biological tissues and clinically relevant metals). This reduces the high-dimensional energy-dependent attenuation to one continuous variable per primitive, stabilizing joint optimization of geometry and material without spatially inconsistent estimates. The polychromatic forward projection integrates the Beer–Lambert law over the full energy spectrum within the Gaussian splatting framework, and the reconstruction pipeline is made fully differentiable so that image-domain priors (TV regularization) can be backpropagated through the projection model. Optimization minimizes a combined L1 + SSIM + TV loss over the Gaussian parameters {p_i, Σ_i, δ_i, u_i}, yielding an order-of-magnitude speedup over NeRF-based polychromatic baselines while preserving high-frequency structural detail near metal boundaries.

## Results

On the three synthetic CBCT phantoms (Lung, Teeth, Broccoli), the proposed method achieves 3D PSNR of 28.96, 27.40, and 27.76 dB with SSIM of 0.994, 0.993, and 0.997, respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline Polyner by 7.3–8.3 dB in PSNR. On real CBCT scans (walnut, garlic, avocado, chicken, enoki mushroom, blueberry), the method consistently suppresses metal-induced streaking while preserving high-frequency structural detail, whereas all baselines exhibit either residual artifacts or excessive blurring. In terms of efficiency, the method completes reconstruction in 19–42 minutes per scene, roughly 3–4× faster than Polyner and Park et al. (1–2 hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all eight baselines on every synthetic scene. Against the strongest physics-based baseline Polyner [35] (polychromatic NeRF with metal masks), the gain is 7.3–8.3 dB in 3D PSNR (e.g., 28.96 vs. 20.63 on Lung), attributed to the compact Bezier material parameterization and splat-based efficiency that avoid Polyner's oversmoothing. Park et al. [22] (linear energy-dependent model) degrades catastrophically on Broccoli (0.07 dB PSNR) and produces heavily blurred reconstructions on all scenes, confirming that a simplified attenuation model cannot capture metal-induced nonlinearity. Projection-domain interpolation methods (LIMAR [15], NMAR [19]) improve over FDK [8] by 2–3 dB but still leave visible streaks and cannot model the underlying polychromatic physics. Supervised learning-based MAR methods (ACDNet [32], DICDNet [31], OSCNet [33]) fail to generalize beyond their training distribution, scoring 13–14 dB PSNR—worse than the uncorrected FDK baseline—highlighting the domain-gap limitation of data-driven post-processing. In efficiency, the method is 3–4× faster than both Polyner and Park et al. across all nine scenes (e.g., 20 min vs. 1 h 30 min on garlic), demonstrating the computational advantage of Gaussian splatting over volumetric neural rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends R²-Gaussian [39], which introduced Gaussian splatting with monochromatic Beer-Lambert modeling for CBCT reconstruction, by replacing the single-energy assumption with a full polychromatic forward projection that integrates over the X-ray spectrum. The key conceptual departure is a compact quadratic Bézier material parameterization (a single scalar u_i per Gaussian) that approximates the mass attenuation coefficient curve, making the nonlinear polychromatic optimization tractable without metal masks. It also draws on Polyner [35] for the physics of polychromatic modeling but substitutes the NeRF backbone with Gaussian splatting and adds a differentiable reconstruction pipeline with TV regularization, eliminating the need for supervised training or metal segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Metal-induced beam hardening remains a persistent clinical problem in CBCT (dental fillings, orthopedic implants), and this work demonstrates that explicit polychromatic physics within an efficient splatting representation can outperform both heuristic correction and NeRF-based methods by a wide margin while being roughly 4–8× faster. The compact material parameterization is a reusable idea: if a single scalar suffices to span the MAC manifold of clinically relevant tissues and metals, it could simplify other spectral CT or multi-energy reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or limited-angle acquisitions, how sensitive it is to spectrum simulation errors (since real spectra are not directly measurable), and whether the Bezier manifold assumption holds for exotic alloys or contrast agents.
