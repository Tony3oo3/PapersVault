## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models suffer from quadratic computational scaling with the number of views and image resolution due to full attention over all multi-view image tokens, and they generate pixel-aligned Gaussians whose count is locked to input resolution, producing massive redundancy. Additionally, the one-shot generation paradigm lacks the iterative refinement mechanism that optimization-based methods (e.g., 3D-GS) exploit to progressively capture fine geometric and appearance details.

## Input / Output

The model takes N multi-view RGB images (e.g., 256×256 up to 540×960) with corresponding camera poses (intrinsics, rotation, translation) and outputs 3D Gaussian parameters (mean, opacity, covariance, color) organized per viewpoint at a resolution H^v×W^v that is decoupled from the input image resolution. This decoupling allows the number of Gaussians to be controlled independently of image resolution—for example, 8 half-resolution viewpoints at 256×256 input yield only 131,072 Gaussians versus 200 million for pixel-aligned methods at 1K/200 views. Training uses held-out target views with MSE and perceptual loss on rendered images.

## Method

iLRM initializes compact viewpoint tokens from Plücker ray embeddings at a lower resolution than the input images, then iteratively refines them through 12 stacked update layers, each consisting of per-view cross-attention (viewpoint tokens attend to their corresponding high-resolution image tokens) followed by global self-attention across all viewpoint tokens. A token uplifting mechanism expands each low-resolution viewpoint token into k=2 finer-grained query tokens before cross-attention to bridge the resolution gap, and a mini-batch cross-attention scheme (inspired by mini-batch gradient descent) selectively samples subsets of image and viewpoint tokens per layer to further reduce cost. The two-stage attention design—efficient one-to-one cross-attention plus self-attention over the compact low-resolution viewpoint space—reduces relative computational cost to 0.08× that of full multi-view attention (e.g., 16 views at 256×256, patch size 8). The final viewpoint tokens are decoded into Gaussian parameters via a single linear layer, and the entire network is trained end-to-end with rendering supervision, making each layer analogous to an optimization step where image tokens provide gradient-like feedback signals.

## Results

On RE10K, iLRM with 8 half-resolution viewpoints (8,H,F) achieves PSNR 31.57, SSIM 0.935, and LPIPS 0.082, outperforming all feed-forward baselines by a substantial margin (e.g., +4.1 dB over DepthSplat, +3.5 dB over GS-LRM) while running in 0.028 s on an RTX 4090. Even the 2-view full-resolution setting (2,F,F) reaches PSNR 28.65 in 0.025 s, surpassing GS-LRM (28.10) and DepthSplat (27.47). On the wide-coverage undistorted DL3DV setting (540×960, 32 views), iLRM completes inference in 0.53 s with PSNR 24.30, matching the optimization-based 3D-GS (24.43) that requires ~8 minutes of per-scene optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iLRM outperforms all five feed-forward baselines on RE10K across every metric. Against PixelSplat (PSNR 25.89) and MVSplat (26.39), iLRM(8,H,F) gains +5.7 and +5.2 dB respectively, while also being 3–4× faster. Against the stronger baselines GS-LRM (28.10) and DepthSplat (27.47), iLRM still leads by +3.5 and +4.1 dB in PSNR, and uses fewer parameters (185M vs 300M/354M). Gen-Den, despite using 347K Gaussians (2.7× more), trails iLRM by 4.5 dB. On the wide-coverage undistorted DL3DV (540×960, 32 views), iLRM (PSNR 24.30, 0.53 s) slightly underperforms per-scene 3D-GS (24.43, 8 min) but is ~900× faster, and beats Long-LRM (23.97, 0.84 s) by +0.33 dB while also being faster. In the 10-epoch fine-tuning setting, iLRM further narrows the gap to 3D-GS (25.67 vs 25.56 PSNR) in only 4.5 s versus 11 s for Long-LRM₁₀.

## Relation to Prior Work

iLRM builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its output representation and positions itself within the feed-forward 3D reconstruction model family (PixelSplat, MVSplat, GS-LRM, DepthSplat, Long-LRM). It departs from the dominant pixel-aligned paradigm—where Gaussians are generated one-per-pixel from input images—by decoupling the 3D representation into compact viewpoint embeddings initialized from Plücker ray coordinates. Conceptually, it bridges optimization-based 3D-GS (iterative render-measure-update loops) and feed-forward architectures by stacking cross-attention (per-view image conditioning) and self-attention (global view aggregation) layers that mimic successive optimization steps, a design inspired by but distinct from gradient-based iterative methods like G3R and Gen-Den.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses the central scalability bottleneck of feed-forward 3D reconstruction—quadratic attention cost in both view count and image resolution—enabling 32-view, 540×960 inference in 0.5 s versus ~8 min for per-scene 3D-GS optimization, with comparable or better quality. The iterative-refinement-within-feed-forward framing is a practical recipe likely to be adopted by subsequent work, and the mini-batch cross-attention trick (75% FLOP reduction for ~0.3 dB PSNR loss) offers a general efficiency lever. Open questions include whether the self-attention bottleneck across viewpoint tokens limits further view scaling, whether the approach can be extended to pose-free or dynamic settings, and whether explicit gradient signals (as in G3R) could complement the learned refinement.
