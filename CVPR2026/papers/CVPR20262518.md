## Problem

Existing dense perception methods built on text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models suffer from a representation mismatch: T2I generators are trained for stochastic semantic composition from unstructured text prompts, making them poorly aligned with the deterministic, geometry-aware nature of pixel-level prediction tasks. The paper argues that image-to-image (I2I) editing models, pre-trained to produce structurally coherent modifications conditioned on an existing image, provide a fundamentally better inductive bias for dense perception.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and a text prompt as conditioning, with interactive positive/negative points added for the matting task. All inputs are encoded into VAE latent space and concatenated as token sequences. Outputs are dense maps at input resolution: single-channel depth (with a theoretically derived square-root normalization to mitigate BF16 quantization error on long-tailed depth distributions), 3-channel unit-length surface normals, or single-channel alpha mattes in [0,1]. Inference is single-step and deterministic via a fixed random seed, producing a unique input-to-output mapping without stochastic sampling.

## Method

Edit2Perceive fine-tunes the FLUX.1 Kontext flow-matching DiT (all DiT parameters trainable, VAE and text encoder frozen) to predict the velocity along a Rectified Flow straight-line trajectory from Gaussian noise to the target latent, conditioned on the input image and text. Two key innovations enhance geometric fidelity: (1) a pixel-space consistency loss (L_Cons) computed between the VAE-decoded prediction and ground truth, with task-specific formulations (scale-shift-invariant L1 for depth, gradient-stable atan2 angular error for normals, region-decomposed L1 for matting), combined with the latent-space flow matching loss via an adaptive curriculum weight λ that starts at zero and linearly increases; and (2) a theoretically derived square-root depth mapping g(y)=√y, proven optimal via Cauchy-Schwarz for minimizing relative quantization error under BF16 precision. At inference, a single Euler step with a fixed seed yields the target latent, reducing FLOPs compared to multi-step generative baselines while maintaining SOTA accuracy.

## Results

On zero-shot monocular depth estimation, Edit2Perceive achieves an average rank of 1.5 across five benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE), with AbsRel of 4.4% on NYUv2, 7.9% on KITTI, and 4.3% on ETH3D, while using only 74K training images. For surface normal estimation, it ranks first on all four benchmarks with an average rank of 1.4 (e.g., 15.7° mean angular error on NYUv2). For interactive matting, it achieves the lowest errors on all three datasets with an average rank of 1.2 (e.g., MSE 0.006 on AIM-500). All results are obtained with single-step deterministic inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edit2Perceive outperforms all 13 baselines across all three tasks and all benchmarks. In depth estimation, it beats the strongest diffusion-based competitor Lotus-D (59K data) by 0.7 AbsRel on NYUv2 (4.4 vs 5.1) and by 27% on ETH3D (4.3 vs 5.5), while also surpassing the heavily supervised DepthAnything V2 (62.6M images) on NYUv2 (4.4 vs 4.5) with ~850× less training data. For normal estimation, it leads Marigold v1.1 (the prior best) by 0.4° mean angular error on NYUv2 (15.7 vs 16.1) and 1.2° on ScanNet (14.1 vs 14.5), and outperforms the task-specific DSINE by 0.5° on NYUv2. In interactive matting, it reduces MSE by 45% over SDMatte on AIM-500 (0.006 vs 0.011) and by 87% over MAM (0.006 vs 0.075), while also achieving the best Gradient and Connectivity scores on all three matting benchmarks. Notably, the I2I-vs-T2I ablation (Table 4) shows that even with identical fine-tuning pipelines, the FLUX.1 Kontext (I2I) backbone consistently outperforms FLUX.1 (T2I) by 25–27% AbsRel, confirming the representation-mismatch hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established paradigm (Marigold, GeoWizard, GenPercept, Lotus) of fine-tuning text-to-image diffusion models for dense perception, arguing instead that image-to-image editing models like FLUX.1 Kontext provide a more natural inductive bias due to their pre-training on structured scene understanding. It builds directly on FLUX.1 Kontext's flow-matching DiT architecture and adopts Marigold's annealed multi-resolution noise strategy, while introducing a pixel-space consistency loss and a theoretically derived square-root depth normalization absent from prior diffusion-perception work. The positioning is explicitly contrasted with concurrent work FE2E, which also uses editing models but focuses on zero-noise denoising and logarithmic depth mapping rather than geometric fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core claim—that I2I editing models outperform T2I generators as perception backbones—challenges the default assumption in the diffusion-perception literature and, if it generalizes beyond the FLUX.1 family, could redirect how the community selects base models for dense prediction. Single-step deterministic inference at competitive accuracy is practically significant for deployment, and the theoretically grounded depth mapping offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc normalization. Open questions include whether the I2I advantage persists across other editing architectures and scales, whether the single-step quality gap versus multi-step matters for downstream 3D reconstruction pipelines, and how the framework extends to tasks beyond the three evaluated here.
