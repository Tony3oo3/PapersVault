## Problem

Optimization-based meta-learning methods like MAML degrade significantly in cross-domain few-shot settings because diverse task distributions produce conflicting task-specific gradients during inner-level optimization, causing the aggregated meta-gradient to cancel out and preventing the acquisition of generalizable prior knowledge. The paper identifies this gradient discrepancy as the fundamental bottleneck and asks how to align task input distributions with shared prior knowledge to produce consistent meta-gradient updates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N-way K-shot RGB image tasks (support and query sets) processed by a 4-CONV or ResNet-12 backbone, with a learnable visual prompt element-wise added to pixel-space inputs before feature extraction. At meta-test time, only the lightweight prompt (e.g., a padding template with p=5, yielding ~0.2M parameters) and the classifier are updated per task while the backbone remains frozen, producing a class label for each query image. The prompt is shared across all tasks during training but instantiated per-task during inner-level adaptation.

## Method

DCML introduces a meta-learnable visual prompt that participates in both inner- and outer-level optimization, unlike prior prompt-based methods that restrict prompts to test-time adaptation. During inner-level optimization, the backbone is frozen and the prompt plus classifier are updated task-specifically to transform each task's support inputs toward the shared prior knowledge, producing more consistent gradient directions. During outer-level optimization, the task-specific prompt is applied to query images before computing the meta-gradient, reducing gradient discrepancies so that the aggregated update to the prior knowledge is coherent rather than self-cancelling. The prompt and prior knowledge are jointly optimized at the outer level, allowing the prompt to learn how to align diverse task distributions with the shared representation. At test time, rapid adaptation is achieved by updating only the prompt and classifier (parameter-efficient, ~0.2M vs. 4.6M for MAML) while the backbone stays frozen.

## Results

On miniImageNet (5-way), DCML achieves 55.52% (1-shot) and 73.31% (5-shot) accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline GAP (54.86% / 71.55%) by 0.66 and 1.76 points respectively. On tieredImageNet, DCML reaches 58.01% (1-shot) and 75.96% (5-shot), again surpassing GAP (57.60% / 74.90%). In the more challenging cross-domain setting (miniImageNet → CUB, 5-way 5-shot), DCML achieves 66.14%, exceeding the best baseline GAP (64.88%) by 1.26 points, and on miniImageNet → CIFAR-FS it reaches 67.33% vs. GAP's 65.27%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCML outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and setting reported. Against the strongest competitor GAP [16], DCML gains +0.66% on miniImageNet 1-shot, +1.76% on miniImageNet 5-shot, +0.41% on tieredImageNet 1-shot, and +1.06% on tieredImageNet 5-shot. The advantage widens in cross-domain transfer: on miniImageNet → CUB, DCML leads GAP by +1.26% (66.14 vs. 64.88), and on miniImageNet → CIFAR-FS by +2.06% (67.33 vs. 65.27). Compared to the original MAML [9], DCML improves miniImageNet 5-shot accuracy by 10.2 points (73.31 vs. 63.11) and cross-domain CUB accuracy by 13.4 points (66.14 vs. 52.70). Parameter-efficient baselines ANIL [28] and BOIL [25] trail DCML by 7.6 and 6.9 points respectively on miniImageNet 5-shot, while DCML uses only 0.2075 × 10⁵ learnable parameters at test time—comparable to ANIL's 0.1601 × 10⁵ and far fewer than MAML's 4.6234 × 10⁵.

## Relation to Prior Work

DCML extends the MAML bi-level optimization framework by adopting ANIL's strategy of freezing the backbone and updating only the classifier, then introducing a meta-learnable visual prompt (following the template designs of Bahng et al.) as a data-space alignment mechanism. The key conceptual departure is from prior prompt-based methods, which restrict prompt usage to test-time adaptation with a frozen model, and from prior meta-learning methods (GAP, L2F, MMAML) that address cross-task conflicts in parameter space. DCML instead operates in input/data space, arguing that aligning task-specific input distributions with shared prior knowledge is a more direct route to reducing gradient discrepancies than modulating parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The gradient discrepancy diagnosis (low cosine similarity, high ℓ₂ distance between task gradients and the meta-gradient) provides a concrete mechanistic explanation for why MAML degrades under distribution shift, and the MMD analysis showing progressive inter-task alignment without collapsing task diversity is a useful diagnostic for meta-training. The parameter-efficiency advantage (0.2M vs. 4.6M learnable parameters at test time) makes DCML practically attractive for deployment. However, the shared (non-task-adaptive) prompt design is a clear limitation acknowledged by the authors, and the in-domain gains over strong baselines like GAP are modest (~1–2 points), with the larger benefits appearing primarily in cross-domain transfer. Open questions include whether task-conditioned prompts or multi-prompt strategies could further close the cross-domain gap, and whether the data-centric alignment principle generalizes beyond classification to other few-shot tasks.
