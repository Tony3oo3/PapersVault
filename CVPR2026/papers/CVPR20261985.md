## Problem

Pixel diffusion models must jointly model high-frequency signals and low-frequency semantics within a single DiT operating on a single-scale input, which makes training slow and degrades image quality because high-frequency noise distracts the transformer from learning semantic structure. Existing multi-scale cascade approaches (e.g., PixelFlow, Relay Diffusion) partially address this but still require a single architecture to handle all frequencies per timestep and rely on complex denoising schedules that slow inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet) or a text prompt (text-to-image) as conditioning and generates 256×256 or 512×512 RGB images. Internally, the DiT consumes a downsampled trajectory (patch size 16, yielding 16×16 or 32×32 tokens) while the pixel decoder consumes the full-resolution noised image (256×256 or 512×512 pixels) as dense queries. Inference uses 100 Euler steps (or 50 with Heun sampler) without classifier-free guidance for class-to-image, and 50 steps for text-to-image.

## Method

DeCo decouples frequency modeling into two specialized modules: a DiT operating on downsampled inputs predicts low-frequency semantic features, and a lightweight attention-free pixel decoder (3 linear blocks, hidden dim 32, patch size 1) takes the full-resolution noised image as dense queries and uses AdaLN-Zero modulation from the upsampled DiT output to predict the final pixel velocity, thereby generating high-frequency details. A frequency-aware flow-matching loss complements the standard FM objective by transforming predicted and ground-truth velocities into the frequency domain via block-wise 8×8 DCT (in YCbCr space) and weighting each frequency band by the normalized reciprocal of JPEG quantization tables (quality 85), which emphasizes perceptually salient frequencies and suppresses insignificant high-frequency noise. The total training objective combines the standard FM loss, the frequency-aware FM loss, and a REPA alignment loss that matches DiT intermediate features to DINOv2 representations. The pixel decoder replaces the final two DiT blocks, reducing total parameters from 459M to 426M while improving FID from 61.10 to 31.35 at 200k iterations.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DeCo achieves a FID of 1.62 (250 steps) and 1.90 (100 steps), outperforming all end-to-end pixel diffusion methods and approaching two-stage latent diffusion (REPA-XL/2: 1.42). On ImageNet 512×512, DeCo reaches FID 2.22, substantially beating PixNerd (2.84). For text-to-image generation, DeCo attains a GenEval overall score of 0.86 and DPG-Bench average of 81.4, surpassing SD3 (0.68), FLUX.1-dev (0.67), and OmniGen2 (0.80) on GenEval. Inference latency is 1.05s per image (100 steps), far faster than RDM (38.4s) and PixelFlow (9.78s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct pixel-diffusion baseline (same DiT-XL/16 architecture without the pixel decoder), DeCo reduces FID from 2.79 to 1.90 at 320 epochs and 1.62 at 800 epochs, while also improving IS from 296 to 303. Among other pixel diffusion methods, DeCo outperforms PixelFlow (FID 1.98, 9.78s latency) and PixNerd (FID 1.95, 1.18s) at 256×256, and beats PixNerd at 512×512 (2.22 vs. 2.84 FID). Compared to the concurrent JiT method, DeCo achieves a lower FID of 1.69 vs. 1.86 with the Heun sampler at 600 epochs, using fewer parameters (682M vs. 953M). On text-to-image benchmarks, DeCo's GenEval score of 0.86 surpasses all two-stage latent diffusion models listed (SD3: 0.68, FLUX.1-dev: 0.67, DALL-E 3: 0.67) as well as unified models BLIP3o (0.81) and OmniGen2 (0.80), while its DPG-Bench score of 81.4 is competitive with FLUX.1-dev (84.0) and DALL-E 3 (83.5). In training efficiency, DeCo reaches FID 2.57 in 400k iterations, exceeding the baseline's FID at 800 epochs—a 10× speedup—and its per-iteration training time (0.24s) is far below PixelFlow's 1.61s.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeCo builds on the DiT architecture and conditional flow matching framework, incorporating REPA alignment for semantic learning. It departs from the standard pixel diffusion paradigm—where a single DiT jointly models all frequency components—by introducing an explicit architectural split: a lightweight attention-free pixel decoder handles high-frequency reconstruction from full-resolution input while the DiT operates on downsampled tokens for low-frequency semantics. This is conceptually related to multi-scale cascaded methods (Relay Diffusion, PixelFlow) that perform temporal frequency separation, and to the concurrent JiT which addresses the same high-frequency-noise interference problem by predicting the clean image; DeCo instead provides a high-frequency shortcut via the decoder. The frequency-aware FM loss, which repurposes JPEG quantization tables as perceptual priors for loss weighting, is a novel contribution not seen in prior pixel or latent diffusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeCo demonstrates that end-to-end pixel diffusion can close the quality gap with two-stage latent diffusion (FID 1.62 at 256×256, 2.22 at 512×512) while avoiding VAE-dependent artifacts and achieving 10× faster training convergence, making VAE-free generation a more practical option. The text-to-image results (GenEval 0.86) outperform large latent diffusion models like SD3 and FLUX.1-dev, suggesting pixel diffusion is no longer a secondary paradigm. Open questions include whether the JPEG-derived frequency prior generalizes beyond natural images (e.g., medical, satellite), whether the approach extends to video generation, and whether the lightweight decoder's capacity limits become a bottleneck at higher resolutions or more complex scenes.
