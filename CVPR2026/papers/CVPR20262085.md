## Problem

Existing image abstraction methods expose only a few entangled controls, so the amount of smoothing and the set of preserved semantic structures cannot be adjusted independently. Pushing smoothing to suppress texture inevitably erodes important contours, leaving users without a clear way to specify what should be preserved versus how far simplification should proceed. The paper addresses this by formalizing a two-dimensional abstraction space that decouples these two axes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262085/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a family of progressively abstracted base images parameterized by a continuous strength (number of donor-gated update iterations) and a discrete semantic granularity (MuGE alpha values). The boundary maps used to gate smoothing are computed once from the original input image and held fixed within each granularity stage, so the output trajectory is fully determined by the input and the chosen stopping point along the schedule. The resulting simplified base images are intended as drop-in inputs for downstream NPR operators such as XDoG, cartoonization, or sketching.

## Method

The core contribution is the Semantic Scale Space (SSS), a 2D framework where strength t controls smoothing progress and granularity g controls which boundaries act as stopping sets, externalized via a MuGE boundary detector. The concrete traversal policy, AGSS, derives a one-sided donor-gated averaging operator by breaking the symmetry of the standard anisotropic diffusion discretization: only the donor pixel's boundary likelihood A_g(y)=1−S_g(y) gates its contribution, preventing cross-boundary mixing while remaining non-expansive. AGSS follows a fine-to-coarse granularity schedule G=[3,2,1,0], advancing stages adaptively when the change in Mean Absolute Difference between successive iterates falls below a decaying threshold, so texture is removed first at fine granularity before regions merge at coarser levels. This design ensures that fine-scale textures are suppressed before major semantic structures are affected, yielding the P3 selectivity property that prior methods lack.

## Results

On the SBD test set under effect-matched RHI levels, AGSS achieves the best Boundary Preservation F1-score (BPF) and lowest geometric Drift at medium and strong abstraction levels. At the strong level (σ=3.0), AGSS reaches BPF 0.594 and Drift 82.98 px, a +10.5% relative BPF improvement over the average of the three canonical baselines and a 4.0% Drift reduction. Notably, AGSS is the only method whose BPF increases monotonically with abstraction strength (0.535 → 0.575 → 0.594), while baselines stagnate or degrade. In the DIV2K user study, AGSS-based NPR outputs are preferred in 72.9% of 2AFC trials (95% CI [68.5, 76.9], p < .001).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGSS outperforms all four baselines at medium and strong abstraction levels on both BPF and Drift. Against WLS (Farbman et al., SIGGRAPH 2008), AGSS gains +0.034 BPF and −2.67 px Drift at the strong level; WLS's texture suppression relies partly on global contrast compression that weakens selective abstraction. Against Perona-Malik diffusion (Perona & Malik, TPAMI 1990), AGSS achieves +0.068 BPF and −2.87 px Drift at strong level; PM's large conductance parameter needed to reach target RHI makes diffusion nearly isotropic, blurring structures and textures together. Against iterative Guided Filter (He et al., TPAMI 2013), AGSS leads by +0.052 BPF and −4.91 px Drift at strong level; GF-it leaves fine residual details while softening important contours. The most informative comparison is DT+MuGE (Semantic Filtering, Yang, CVPR 2016), which uses the same MuGE boundary cues as AGSS: the remaining gap (+0.039 BPF, −5.99 px Drift at strong level) isolates the contribution of the donor-gated smoothing dynamics rather than boundary quality alone. At the weak level, all methods are broadly comparable, confirming that the selectivity advantage of AGSS emerges as abstraction strength increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads—PDE-based edge-preserving filters (Perona-Malik, bilateral, guided filter), global optimization smoothers (WLS, L0, RTV), and semantic boundary detection (MuGE)—into a single two-dimensional abstraction space (Semantic Scale Space) where smoothing strength and semantic granularity are explicitly decoupled. The key conceptual departure from prior work is the externalization of the stopping set: rather than letting a single scalar weight implicitly decide which gradients are 'structure' versus 'texture' (as in WLS or L0), the paper uses a controllable boundary detector to define which boundaries inhibit smoothing, independent of how far diffusion proceeds. The donor-gated (one-sided) averaging operator breaks the symmetry of standard anisotropic diffusion to reduce cross-boundary mixing, and the fine-to-coarse granularity schedule provides a concrete traversal policy that no prior method explicitly formalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SSS framework offers a clean conceptual separation—'what to preserve' versus 'how much to smooth'—that could reshape how practitioners design controllable abstraction pipelines for NPR and creative applications. The RHI-based effect-matched evaluation protocol is a useful methodological contribution that enables fair selectivity comparisons across heterogeneous methods. A key limitation is that achievable selectivity is bounded by the quality of the underlying boundary detector (MuGE), and the method's gains, while consistent, are moderate (≈10% relative BPF improvement, ≈4% Drift reduction). Open questions include whether content-adaptive traversal policies could further improve selectivity, and whether tighter coupling between detection and smoothing (rather than the current fixed-per-stage boundary maps) would yield additional gains.
