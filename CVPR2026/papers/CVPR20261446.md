## Problem

Current video-to-audio (V2A) evaluation relies on distributional and semantic metrics (FAD, CLAP) that measure surface-level plausibility rather than whether models capture the underlying physical processes that produce sound. No existing benchmark tests causal responsiveness: whether generated audio correctly changes when a single physical factor (material, geometry, environment) is systematically manipulated while all else is held fixed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 5–10 second indoor video clips featuring discrete impact-style events (tapping, clapping, plucking) with manually annotated onset timestamps, plus an optional text caption. Counterfactual pairs are constructed by time-warping the counterfactual video so that annotated impact peaks align exactly with the factual video, isolating a single physical variable. Evaluation produces two structured outputs: temporal alignment scores (Hit Coverage %, Timing Error in ms, Perfect Align %) and per-metric physical correctness Confidence scores across nine physics-grounded descriptors (Attack Time, Decay Rate, F0, Spectral Centroid, Spectral Flux, Spectral Rolloff, Temporal Modulation, RT60, DRR).

## Method

FlatSounds introduces a two-mode evaluation framework: (1) time-warped factual-counterfactual pairs that isolate one physical factor while aligning impact timings, and (2) single-video pattern tests probing internal consistency (repeated identical hits) and directional trends (e.g., ascending piano notes). Physical correctness is measured via a 'soft gate' that weights each of 10 generation seeds by both temporal alignment and semantic plausibility before checking whether the metric delta has the expected sign, yielding a Confidence score per metric. The benchmark is validated against a pairwise human ELO study, showing that FlatSounds metrics (Confidence, Hit Coverage, Perfect Align) correlate more strongly with human preference (Spearman ρ = 0.9) than standard metrics like FAD-PASST (ρ = 0.7) or CLAP (ρ = 0.2). Evaluation of five state-of-the-art models with and without captions reveals a consistent trade-off: text captions improve semantic and physical accuracy but degrade temporal synchronization, exposing a fundamental weakness in video encoders' ability to extract physical properties from pixels.

## Results

On FlatSounds-Physics, all evaluated V2A models show low physical Confidence scores (0.205–0.306), indicating that no current model reliably captures causal physical reasoning from pixels. MMAudio-Phys (w/ Caption) achieves the highest average Confidence (0.306), while Hunyuan-V2A (w/o Caption) achieves the best temporal alignment with 91.50% Hit Coverage and 70.50% Perfect Align. A consistent trade-off emerges: adding text captions improves physical/semantic Confidence by ~5–10% but degrades Hit Coverage by 1–8% and increases Timing Error by 1–4 ms across all models. On VGGSound, ThinkSound (w/ Caption) attains the best FAD-PASST (52.44) and MMAudio-Phys (w/ Caption) the best FAD-PANN (3.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four state-of-the-art V2A models (FoleyCrafter, Hunyuan-V2A, MMAudio, ThinkSound) plus a physics-caption-finetuned variant (MMAudio-Phys) on the new FlatSounds benchmark. On physical Confidence, MMAudio-Phys (w/ Caption) leads at 0.306, narrowly ahead of Hunyuan-V2A (w/ Caption) at 0.305, while FoleyCrafter trails at 0.205–0.217. On temporal alignment, Hunyuan-V2A dominates: without captions it reaches 91.50% Hit Coverage and 70.50% Perfect Align, substantially outperforming ThinkSound (33.74%/51.52%) and MMAudio (31.12%/52.03%) with captions. A key finding is that captions consistently hurt temporal alignment—every model shows lower Hit Coverage and higher Timing Error with captions—while simultaneously improving physical Confidence (except FoleyCrafter, where captions slightly reduce Confidence from 0.217 to 0.205). On standard VGGSound metrics, ThinkSound (w/ Caption) achieves the best FAD-PASST (52.44) and MMAudio-Phys (w/ Caption) the best FAD-PANN (3.97), but these semantic gains come at the cost of temporal synchronization (DeSync worsens from 0.326 to 0.340 for Hunyuan-V2A when captions are added). Human ELO ratings (Table 7) broadly agree with the FlatSounds-Physics ranking, with Hunyuan-V2A (w/ Caption) at 1556 and FoleyCrafter (w/ Caption) at 1438, validating the benchmark's discriminative power.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant V2A evaluation paradigm (FAD, CLAP, DeSync) that measures distributional plausibility and semantic correlation, arguing these metrics cannot distinguish a model that truly simulates physics from one that merely pattern-matches. It draws on the counterfactual intervention framework from causal inference and extends physical-reasoning benchmarks previously applied to video generation (VideoPhy, VBench, Morpheus) into the audio domain. The time-warping technique for constructing temporally aligned factual-counterfactual pairs is a novel methodological contribution that enables single-factor causal attribution in V2A, a capability absent from prior evaluation suites like AVBenchmark or KlingAudio-Eval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that all current SOTA V2A models achieve very low physical confidence scores (~0.2–0.3), exposing that video encoders are not extracting physical properties from pixels but instead 'reading' them from text captions when available. The consistent finding that captions improve semantic/physical accuracy while degrading temporal alignment across every evaluated model points to a fundamental architectural bottleneck in visual representation learning for physical processes. Key limitations include the single-factor, indoor-only scope (185 clips) and the inability to test compound interactions; scaling the causal framework to multi-factor, in-the-wild scenarios remains an open challenge. The work reframes the central V2A problem from audio synthesis quality to building video encoders that internalize physics from observation.
