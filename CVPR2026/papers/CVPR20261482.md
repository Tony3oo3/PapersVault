## Problem

Subject-driven text-to-image generation suffers from a similarity-controllability paradox: improving textual adherence degrades subject fidelity and vice versa. The paper identifies the root cause as the entanglement of roles in overloaded text prompts, where descriptive entity words (e.g., 'a duck toy') activate the model's generic priors and conflict with the high-fidelity visual information in the reference image, making simultaneous optimization of both objectives impossible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261482/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image containing the target subject and a text prompt describing the desired scene modification. At inference, the prompt is automatically simplified by a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 72B) that replaces the subject's entity word with a generic pronoun, while GroundingDINO localizes the subject in the reference image using the original entity word. The output is a single generated image that preserves the subject's fine-grained visual identity (color, texture, shape) while placing it in the textually specified context, evaluated on DreamBench (30 subjects × 25 prompts = 750 cases) using CLIP-I, DINO-I, CLIP-T, and ImageReward metrics.

## Method

DisCo operates in two stages on a FLUX DiT backbone with a unified token sequence [X; C_T; C_I] and 3D RoPE. First, a textual-visual decoupling module isolates subject identity from textual control: a VLM identifies and strips the entity word from the prompt (replacing it with 'this item'), while GroundingDINO uses that entity word to produce a spatial mask localizing the subject in the reference image, ensuring the model sources identity exclusively from visual features. Second, a GRPO re-coupling stage addresses the compositional gap left by strict decoupling; a dedicated reward model (Qwen3-VL-30B) is trained on 25k preference pairs where negative examples are synthetically created by a VLM generating editing instructions that corrupt subject identity or subject-context interaction. During GRPO, groups of 16 images are sampled per prompt, scored by the reward model, and the policy is updated using group-relative advantage normalization with a KL penalty to the reference policy, enabling natural integration of the visually-defined subject into the textually-generated context.

## Results

On DreamBench (750 evaluation cases), DisCo achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six metrics simultaneously. For subject similarity, it scores 0.928 (CLIP-B-I), 0.937 (CLIP-L-I), and 0.903 (DINO-I), surpassing the next-best FLUX Kontext by 1.8–7.6 points. For text controllability, it reaches 0.329 (CLIP-B-T) and 0.273 (CLIP-L-T), and for overall image quality, it attains an ImageReward score of 1.339, outperforming all baselines including FLUX Kontext (1.276).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DisCo outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric, resolving the similarity–controllability trade-off that plagues prior work. Against the strongest baseline, FLUX Kontext [dev], DisCo gains +0.018 on CLIP-B-I, +0.026 on CLIP-L-I, +0.064 on DINO-I, +0.008 on CLIP-B-T, +0.005 on CLIP-L-T, and +0.063 on ImageReward. Among FLUX-based methods, DreamO and UNO achieve competitive subject similarity (DINO-I 0.813/0.827) but sacrifice text controllability (CLIP-B-T 0.322/0.311) and image quality (IR 1.186/0.854), illustrating the paradox. OminiControl and ACE++ sit in the middle, with moderate subject fidelity (DINO-I 0.713/0.734) and text scores (CLIP-B-T 0.320/0.321). SDXL-based methods (IP-Adapter, Emu2, SSR-Encoder, MS-Diffusion, RealCustom++) are consistently 5–20 points behind on subject similarity and 1–10 points behind on text controllability, with Emu2 even producing negative ImageReward scores (−0.148). In the human study, DisCo wins 80% against UNO, 82% against DreamO, 71% against OminiControl, 66% against ACE++, and 51% against FLUX Kontext.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the unified-sequence DiT formulation from OminiControl, which concatenates reference image tokens with noisy latents and text tokens under 3D RoPE, and on Flow-GRPO's adaptation of Group Relative Policy Optimization to flow-matching models. Its key conceptual departure is diagnosing the long-standing similarity-controllability trade-off not as an architectural limitation but as a signal-entanglement problem: text prompts that describe both the subject and the desired edit inject conflicting priors. The solution repurposes off-the-shelf tools (a VLM for prompt simplification, GroundingDINO for subject localization) into a decoupling pipeline, then applies GRPO with a purpose-built reward model to re-couple subject and context—rather than proposing a new attention mechanism or encoder as prior encoder-based methods (IP-Adapter, SSR-Encoder) or unified-sequence methods (OminiControl, DreamO) do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a simple prompt-level intervention (replacing the entity word with a pronoun) plus RL-based re-coupling can simultaneously maximize subject fidelity and text controllability, the paper offers a practical recipe that could be grafted onto existing DiT-based editing pipelines without architectural changes. However, the approach adds substantial inference-time dependencies (a 72B VLM call, GroundingDINO localization, and multi-sample GRPO scoring), raising questions about latency and scalability to multi-subject or video settings. An open question is whether the decoupling principle generalizes beyond single-subject editing to more complex compositional instructions where the subject's role in the scene is inherently descriptive.
