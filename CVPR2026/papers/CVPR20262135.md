## Problem

Language-conditioned robot manipulation requires bridging high-level motion intent with low-level control, but existing approaches either map observations directly to actions (VLA models) or generate future RGB frames and extract motion indirectly via tracking or optical flow. This paper argues that explicitly predicting dense pixel motion as a structured intermediate representation—rather than deriving it from generated videos or sparse trajectories—yields a more interpretable and data-efficient interface between perception and control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262135/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes two RGB views (a static third-person camera and a gripper-mounted camera), a natural language instruction, and low-dimensional robot joint states. The Motion Director outputs a 3-channel pixel motion image [u, v, (u+v)/2] of the same spatial resolution as the input frame, encoding per-pixel horizontal and vertical displacement over a temporal offset k; this motion is grounded to the static camera view for interpretability. The Action Expert then produces a chunk of h executable robot actions (joint-space trajectories) by iteratively denoising a Gaussian-prior action sequence.

## Method

DAWN is a two-stage diffusion pipeline. The Motion Director repurposes a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion U-Net + VAE) to generate dense pixel motion: at each denoising step, the U-Net denoises a noisy latent concatenated with the uncorrupted VAE encoding of the current frame, conditioned via cross-attention on CLIP embeddings of the gripper view and language instruction plus a temporal offset k; ground-truth motion is computed offline with RAFT optical flow and only the U-Net is trained. The Action Expert is a transformer-based Enhanced Diffusion Policy that cross-attends over token embeddings of the predicted pixel motion (encoded by a frozen DINOv3/ConvNeXt-S), language (T5-small), and robot state (2-layer MLP) to iteratively denoise a noisy action chunk into a coherent trajectory. Both modules are trained with MSE noise-estimation loss and can be trained in parallel since the optical flow ground truth is independent of the policy, enabling modular upgrades. The key architectural insight is that predicting motion directly in latent image space via a pretrained diffusion backbone is simpler and more data-efficient than generating full RGB video and post-processing it for motion cues.

## Results

On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark (no external data), DAWN achieves an average task completion length of 4.00, setting a new state-of-the-art that surpasses VPP (3.93), Seer-Large (3.83), and RoboUniview (3.65). On MetaWorld, DAWN achieves 65.4% overall success rate, outperforming the previous best LTM (57.7%) by 7.7 points. In real-world single-arm manipulation, DAWN achieves the highest lift-and-place success rates across nearly all six object categories (e.g., 19/20 for Apple vs. 16/20 for VPP), while in bimanual manipulation it reduces action prediction MSE from 0.128 to 0.117 over the Enhanced Diffusion Policy baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CALVIN without external data, DAWN (Avg. Len 4.00) outperforms all baselines, including VPP (3.93), Seer-Large (3.83), and RoboUniview (3.65), with the largest gap over Diffusion Policy (0.56) and Robo-Flamingo (2.47). When external DROID data is used, DAWN (4.10) remains competitive but trails DreamVLA (4.44) and VPP (4.33), which benefit from substantially more diverse pretraining data (193k human + 179k robot trajectories for VPP). On MetaWorld, DAWN (65.4%) clearly surpasses LTM (57.7%), ATM (52.0%), and AVDC (43.1%), with particularly large gains on semantically similar task pairs like door-open (94.7% vs. 77.3% for LTM). In real-world single-arm manipulation, DAWN achieves 19/20 success on Apple and 20/19 on Avocado, outperforming VPP (16/14 and 15/15) and π0 (10/9 and 6/6), while also showing fewer wrong-object grasps (0 vs. 2 for VPP on Apple). For bimanual manipulation, DAWN reduces action prediction MSE to 0.117 versus 0.128 for Enhanced Diffusion Policy, confirming that pixel motion benefits extend to multi-arm coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAWN repurposes a pretrained latent diffusion model (originally for RGB image synthesis) to generate dense pixel motion fields, departing from prior generative-robotics approaches (Gen2Act, VPP, UniPi) that first synthesize future RGB frames and then extract motion signals. It also extends Diffusion Policy into a multimodal 'Enhanced Diffusion Policy' (Action Expert) conditioned on the predicted motion, visual observations, language, and robot state. Unlike sparse pixel-trajectory methods (Track2Act, Im2Flow2Act, General Flow) that rely on object detectors or point selection, DAWN predicts full-resolution dense motion directly, and unlike end-to-end VLA models (RT-2, OpenVLA, π0), it introduces an explicit, interpretable intermediate representation between perception and control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAWN demonstrates that a structured pixel-motion intermediate can match or exceed much larger VLA models (e.g., π0, VPP) with substantially less data and smaller model capacity, suggesting that the gap between hierarchical and end-to-end robot policies may stem from underpowered components rather than architectural choice. The sim-to-real transfer with only 1k episodes and minimal finetuning is practically encouraging. Open questions include whether the two-stage design's inference overhead (~320 ms vs. ~113 ms for the single-stage baseline) becomes prohibitive at higher control frequencies, whether the approach scales to more complex multi-object or deformable-object manipulation, and whether the motion representation generalizes beyond the specific camera geometries tested.
