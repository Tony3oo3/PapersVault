## Problem

Existing primate behavior recognition methods rely on human-centric pretrained video models and are specialized to individual datasets, limiting generalization across species, settings, and tasks. While domain-level pretraining (pretraining on diverse data from the same domain without including the target dataset) has proven effective for language models, it remains unexplored for video models, and no large-scale, diverse primate-centric pretraining dataset existed to enable it.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution, spatially cropped around primate bounding boxes (ground truth or predicted by SAM3) to focus computation on the animal. The frozen attentive classifier produces per-class action probabilities via C learned CLS tokens (one per class), using softmax for single-label datasets (PanAf500, BaboonLand, ChimpBehave) and sigmoid for the multi-label ChimpACT protocol. At test time, three views per sample are aggregated for the final prediction.

## Method

The approach continues self-supervised pretraining of V-JEPA (ViT-L encoder, 304M params; 12-layer predictor, 22M params) on the 424-hour PriVi dataset using masked latent-space prediction, optionally followed by 10k steps of dataset-level pretraining (DaLP) on the target dataset. PriVi is assembled via a scalable curation pipeline that filters YouTube videos with a CLIP-embedding-based relevance classifier (82.8% recall, 90.3% precision) and removes empty frames using GroundingDINO zero-shot primate detection, combining 250h of filtered web footage with 174h of research/observational recordings from 11 field sites. For evaluation, a lightweight frozen attentive classifier (220k trainable parameters) downprojects encoder tokens from 1024 to 64 dimensions, prepends C learned CLS tokens (one per class), and applies three self-attention layers before per-class scoring. The key finding is that domain-level pretraining on diverse primate video consistently outperforms both human-centric V-JEPA initialization and dataset-level pretraining alone, and the combination of PriVi + DaLP + frozen evaluation surpasses fully finetuned specialist models across all four benchmark datasets.

## Results

On the four primate behavior benchmarks, the full pipeline (PriVi pretraining + dataset-level pretraining + frozen attentive classifier) achieves 40.00 mAP / 59.29 mAPw on ChimpACT, 85.01% Acc / 62.96% B-Acc on PanAf500, 76.42% Acc / 38.57% B-Acc on BaboonLand, and 96.02% Acc / 75.14% B-Acc on ChimpBehave. The lightweight classifier uses only 220k trainable parameters while the encoder is frozen. On ChimpACT without ground-truth bounding boxes (using SAM3 detections), the method reaches 30.76 mAP / 41.28 mAPw, surpassing the fully finetuned AlphaChimp (25.35 / 40.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's method (PriVi + DaLP) outperforms every listed baseline on all four datasets. On ChimpACT, it achieves 40.00 mAP versus 31.5 for VideoPrism-g (frozen foundation model), 27.05 for X3D (fully finetuned), and 25.7 for InternVideo-L — a 5–14 point margin. On PanAf500, it reaches 85.01% Acc / 62.96% B-Acc, edging out ChimpVLM (84.91 / 61.94) and substantially beating X3D (80.00 / 50.35) and InternVideo-L (78.57 / 54.01). On BaboonLand, it achieves 76.42% Acc / 38.57% B-Acc versus 64.89 / 31.41 for X3D and 63.45 / 28.67 for UniformerV2-B. On ChimpBehave, it reaches 96.02% Acc / 75.14% B-Acc, surpassing VideoMAEv2 (92.3 / 74.8) and X3D (89.3 / 62.8). Notably, the 220k-parameter frozen classifier outperforms ChimpVLM's 167M-parameter fully finetuned model on PanAf500, and the zero-shot SAM3 + PriVi pipeline beats the fully finetuned AlphaChimp on ChimpACT without ground-truth detections (30.76 vs. 25.35 mAP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on V-JEPA (Bardes et al., TMLR 2024), continuing its self-supervised masked-prediction-in-latent-space pretraining on a new domain-specific dataset. It departs from prior primate behavior recognition work (ChimpVLM, AlphaChimp, PanAf methods) which are dataset-specific and rely on human-centric pretrained backbones like Kinetics, by introducing a domain-level pretraining strategy on a diverse primate video corpus that excludes the target datasets. The data curation pipeline also distinguishes itself from prior large-scale curation efforts (DINOv2, DataComp) by requiring neither seed datasets nor text captions, instead using CLIP-based relevance scoring and zero-shot primate detection. The lightweight 220k-parameter attentive classifier is a deliberate departure from the heavier classifiers in V-JEPA (12M) and V-JEPA2 (49M), motivated by overfitting concerns on small animal behavior datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that domain-level pretraining—pretraining on diverse in-domain videos without including the target dataset—improves video model performance, a result previously established only for language models. The PriVi dataset (424 hours across 11 settings) and the three-stage framework (domain-level pretraining → optional dataset-level pretraining → frozen evaluation) provide a practical, scalable recipe for low-label primate behavior analysis that the community can adopt. Key limitations include coverage of only 11 research setups and evaluation restricted to chimpanzees and baboons, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other primate species or broader animal domains. The finding that even unfiltered YouTube primate videos outperform human-centric pretraining suggests the approach may be robust to data quality, but this has not been stress-tested at larger scale.
