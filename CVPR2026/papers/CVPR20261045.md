## Problem

Standard 2D echocardiography produces disconnected image planes with no explicit spatial information, yet accurate 3D cardiac quantification requires knowing the 6D pose (position and orientation) of each slice. External tracking hardware (IMUs, EM sensors) is impractical due to drift, calibration errors, and workflow integration challenges, and no public dataset pairs 2D LV scans with 6D pose annotations. This paper asks whether 6D slice poses can be estimated purely from 2D image content, enabling 3D LV reconstruction from routine sparse 2D acquisitions without additional hardware.

## Input / Output

The network takes five 224×224 binary LV segmentation masks (A4C, A3C, A2C, SAXA, SAXB) rather than raw ultrasound images, along with initial affine pose assumptions encoding standard clinical slice positioning. The A4C view anchors a local coordinate system to reduce learning variability. The output is a 6D residual correction (Δx, Δy, Δz, Δα, Δβ, Δγ in axis-angle form) per slice, which is applied to the initial affines via matrix multiplication to yield final poses; these positioned masks then drive GHD mesh morphing to produce a 3D LV surface mesh from which ejection fraction and cavity volumes are computed.

## Method

EchoPOSE fuses local and global context through a two-stage transformer pipeline: a CNN backbone extracts spatial features from each segmentation map, an MLP encodes the initial 6D affine, and a Local Transformer (self-attention) refines the concatenated representation before cross-attention with a learnable patch codemap yields per-view descriptors. All five descriptors are then processed by a Global Transformer whose cross-token attention captures inter-slice geometric relationships. A final FC layer regresses the 6D pose update. Training uses a composite loss combining MSE on 6D parameters with a novel intersection loss that samples 100 points along each predicted slice-pair intersection line and penalizes mask-intensity mismatches, enforcing spatial coherence across views. The model is trained on 42,800 synthetic samples generated by slicing MITEA 3D volumes with random perturbations (±20 mm, ±15°), and the resulting poses feed a Graph Harmonic Deformation reconstruction that is end-to-end differentiable via Differentiable Voxelization and Slicing.

## Results

On the MITEA dataset (52 scans), EchoPOSE achieves a mean 6D pose error of 3.78 mm (translation) and 8.65° (rotation), yielding 87.52% 3D Dice, 3.03% LV volume error, and 1.44% ejection fraction (EF) error via GHD reconstruction. On the MITEA+AI dataset (AI-segmented inputs), performance degrades modestly to 4.53 mm / 9.68° pose error, 83.82% Dice, 5.44% volume error, and 3.29% EF error. On real routine TTE scans (18 volunteers), EchoPOSE+GHD achieves 78.04% 3D Dice, 4.22% volume error, and 6.46% EF error, substantially outperforming the assumed-affine GHD baseline (57.99% Dice, 16.68% volume error).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the prior pose-estimation method of Freitas et al. [7] (heatmap-based, no global context), EchoPOSE reduces translation error from 10.83 mm to 3.78 mm (−65%) and rotation error from 15.04° to 8.65° (−42%) on MITEA, demonstrating the benefit of the transformer-based global multi-view aggregation. Compared to the clinical standard Simpson's biplane [11], EchoPOSE+GHD cuts LV volume error from 10.45% to 3.03% (MITEA) and from 17.15% to 4.22% (real TTE), while reducing EF error from 2.98% to 1.44% and from 9.35% to 6.46% respectively — a clinically meaningful improvement given that EF category boundaries sit at 40% and 50%. Against the OReX + marching-cubes reconstruction strategy [21] (ablation, Table 2), GHD achieves 87.52% vs. 69.27% 3D Dice and 3.03% vs. 20.05% volume error, confirming that graph-harmonic deformation is far more suitable for sparse 2D echo inputs than SDF-based pipelines. The assumed-affine GHD baseline (no pose correction) performs poorly across all settings (64.31% Dice on MITEA, 57.99% on TTE), validating that explicit 6D pose estimation is essential rather than relying on standard view geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Graph Harmonic Deformation (GHD) mesh morphing algorithm of Luo et al. [15] for 3D shape reconstruction, extending it by solving the previously unaddressed problem of how to obtain the 6D slice poses that GHD requires. It departs from prior slice-pose estimation work in other modalities (X-ray C-arm guidance, fetal ultrasound, cardiac CT/MRI, and FoCUS) by introducing a transformer-based architecture that jointly models local CNN features and global multi-view context, rather than relying on predefined view sets, dense volumetric data, or local frame processing. The geometry-aware intersection loss, inspired by intensity-based registration [24], is a novel addition that enforces spatial coherence across intersecting planes without requiring explicit 3D supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external tracking hardware (IMUs, EM sensors) and reducing sensitivity to operator skill in probe positioning, this framework could make 3D cardiac quantification accessible in settings where 3D transducers are impractical, potentially narrowing the gap between 2D and 3D echo in routine clinical practice. Key limitations include a small clinical validation set (18 scans from healthy volunteers), reliance on synthetic training data derived from 3D echo volumes, and evaluation restricted to five standard views. Open questions include generalization to diseased populations at scale, real-time inference feasibility, handling of non-standard or additional views, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap widens with more pathological variability.
