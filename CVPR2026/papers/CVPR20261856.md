## Problem

Existing VLM-based Concept Bottleneck Models suffer from two critical inconsistencies: semantic inconsistency, where VLMs fail to precisely localize fine-grained part-attribute concepts (e.g., distinguishing a fish head from a fish body), and object inconsistency, where object-agnostic concepts (e.g., 'head: streamlined front profile') ground on irrelevant objects in the scene, injecting spurious evidence into the bottleneck. No prior method jointly enforces both semantic and object-level consistency in concept grounding.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a list of part-attribute text concepts (e.g., 'head: streamlined front profile') as input. The text encoder is Llama3 (replacing CLIP's 77-token-limited encoder) to handle complex compositional descriptions, while the image encoder remains CLIP's ViT producing H×W×d patch embeddings. Outputs include a spatial concept cost volume (H×W×N_T), a class-agnostic object mask (H×W), pooled concept scores, and a final class label via a linear head.

## Method

OA-CBM introduces two complementary modules. For object consistency, a Hierarchical Clustering (HC) module uses N stages of learnable cluster tokens interacting with object text tokens and image patch embeddings via cross-attention, slot attention, and token mixing, ultimately producing a class-agnostic foreground mask through cosine similarity with the final clustered embedding. For semantic consistency, a Cost Aggregation (CA) module refines the initial cosine-similarity cost volume using a Spatial Aggregation Transformer (Swin blocks for local/semi-global coherence) and a Concept Aggregation Transformer (permutation-invariant, no positional embeddings) to model inter-concept dependencies. The final concept scores are computed as Pool(Ĉ ⊙ M), gating the aggregated cost volume with the object mask before a linear classifier. The authors also curate two part-attribute segmentation datasets (PartAttrCUB, PartAttrImageNet) by prompting GPT-5 for attribute descriptions, deduplicating via SemHash, and enforcing ontology constraints through LLM-guided part-wise clustering.

## Results

On the proposed Open Concept Grounding (OCG) task, OA-CBM achieves h-IoU of 35.7 on PartAttrImageNet and 20.8 on PartAttrCUB under the fully self-predicted Pred-All protocol, surpassing the best baseline (SC-CLIP at 9.8 and CLIP at 4.4 respectively) by over 25 and 16 points. Under oracle settings, OA-CBM reaches 51.4 h-IoU (Oracle-Obj) on PartAttrImageNet, nearly doubling the best baseline (DINO at 34.7). For classification, OA-CBM attains 80.7% top-1 on CUB-200, 83.1% on ImageNet, 97.2% on CIFAR-100, 94.5% on FOOD-101, and 98.4% on Oxford 102 Flowers using the CLS-token setting, outperforming all baselines on CUB-200 and ImageNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OA-CBM outperforms all four baselines across every grounding protocol and both datasets. The gap is most dramatic under Pred-All (no oracle masks or labels): on PartAttrImageNet, OA-CBM's 35.7 h-IoU exceeds SC-CLIP (9.8), OCLIP/SALF-CBM (7.1), DINO/DOT-CBM (5.1), and CLIP/Labo (1.9) by 25.9, 28.6, 30.6, and 33.8 points respectively. Under Oracle-Obj, DINO (DOT-CBM) is the strongest baseline at 34.7 h-IoU on PartAttrImageNet, but OA-CBM still leads by 16.7 points; on PartAttrCUB, DINO leads baselines at 28.8 but OA-CBM reaches 33.9. For classification (CLS-token), OA-CBM wins on CUB-200 (80.7 vs. 78.3 for SC-CBM) and ImageNet (83.1 vs. 78.7 for DOT-CBM), while DOT-CBM edges ahead on CIFAR-100 (97.3 vs. 97.2) and FLOWER (99.4 vs. 98.4). The ablation confirms that the Hierarchical Clustering module is responsible for the large Pred-All gains (e.g., +22.8 h-IoU on PartAttrImageNet), while the Cost Aggregation module drives the overall grounding quality improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) lineage by addressing two specific failure modes in VLM-based CBMs—semantic inconsistency (noisy fine-grained part grounding) and object inconsistency (cross-object spurious activations)—that prior methods (Labo, SALF-CBM, DOT-CBM, SC-CBM) implicitly assume away. Methodologically, it borrows the cost-aggregation transformer from CAT-Seg for semantic consistency and introduces a hierarchical slot-attention clustering module for object-level consistency, while replacing CLIP's text encoder with Llama3 to handle longer part-attribute concept descriptions. The paper also departs from prior CBM work by redefining concepts from object-level labels to part-attribute pairs (e.g., 'head: streamlined front profile'), a conceptual shift that enables finer-grained spatial grounding and is supported by two newly curated segmentation datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of semantic and object inconsistency as distinct, measurable failure modes in VLM-based CBMs provides a clearer diagnostic framework for the interpretability community, and the proposed Open Concept Grounding (OCG) benchmark with its three evaluation protocols (Oracle-Obj, Oracle-All, Pred-All) fills a gap in how concept-level spatial grounding is assessed. The part-attribute datasets (PartAttrCUB, PartAttrImageNet) are a practical resource for evaluating fine-grained concept localization. However, the classification gains (~2.9%) are modest relative to the grounding gains, and the approach's reliance on GPT-5-assisted dataset curation and Llama3 as a text encoder raises questions about scalability to open-world scenes with multiple overlapping objects or to domains where part-attribute taxonomies are less well-defined.
