## Problem

Existing music style transfer systems rely on textual prompts or reference audio, leaving the visual modality—color, lighting, composition—as an untapped source of stylistic guidance that encodes affective attributes parallel to timbre and harmony. Additionally, even inversion-free flow editing, which avoids stochastic noise accumulation, can still distort melodic patterns under large stylistic shifts, creating a tension between expressive style change and structural fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261417/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source instrumental audio clip (vocals removed via Demucs stem separation), a free-form text prompt describing the target style, and a style reference image. Audio is converted to Mel-spectrograms and encoded into latent tokens by a lightweight VAE; the image is encoded by both CLIP and ViT; the text is encoded by Flan-T5. The output is a full-length stylized audio waveform decoded from the edited latent, preserving the source's melodic and rhythmic structure while adopting the target style's timbral and harmonic character.

## Method

The framework builds on the MAA3 DiT backbone and performs inversion-free flow editing (à la FlowEdit) in latent space, constructing a deterministic source-to-target velocity difference without noise reconstruction. A cross-adapter module injects dual visual embeddings (CLIP for semantic alignment, ViT for global structure) and text features into each transformer block via cross-attention, where the main and control streams share query projections but maintain distinct key/value projections with separate RoPE time grids. To preserve melodic identity, a differentiable normalized chroma constraint extracts 12-bin pitch-class energy from the decoded waveform at each flow step and applies gradient-based proximal updates (with cosine-decayed weighting) to pull the latent trajectory back toward the source's pitch-class distribution, balancing structural fidelity against stylistic flexibility.

## Results

On the unified MeLBench–MusicCaps multimodal benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best score on every reported metric: FAD 2.43, FD 24.06, IMSM 0.828, F₀-PCC 0.416, CCS 0.878, OVL 89.27, REL 88.13, and MOS_con 89.20. Compared to the strongest text-only generation baseline (MAA3), it reduces FAD by 42% (2.43 vs. 4.23) and improves overall quality by 2.3 points. Against the closest inversion-based style-transfer baselines (MusicTI, ZETA), it raises F₀-PCC from 0.367/0.322 to 0.416 and CCS from 0.820/0.764 to 0.878, confirming the benefit of the chroma-guided constraint.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all ten baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against text-only generation systems (MusicLM, MusicGen, TANGO, MAA3), it achieves substantially lower FAD (2.43 vs. 4.23–5.26) and higher subjective scores (OVL 89.27 vs. 80.73–86.93), while also reporting structure-preserving metrics (F₀-PCC, CCS) that those systems cannot provide. Among multimodal approaches, it surpasses MeLFusion [3] on FAD (2.43 vs. 3.24), FD (24.06 vs. 25.73), and IMSM (0.828 vs. 0.783), indicating stronger visual–musical alignment. Versus inversion-based style-transfer methods, it leads MusicTI [27] by 0.049 in F₀-PCC (0.416 vs. 0.367) and 0.058 in CCS (0.878 vs. 0.820), and ZETA [34] by 0.094 in F₀-PCC and 0.114 in CCS, attributing the gap to the normalized chroma constraint. In the inversion-strategy ablation (Table 3), it edges out FlowEdit [23] on F₀-PCC (0.416 vs. 0.368) and CCS (0.878 vs. 0.852) at a negligible FAD cost (2.43 vs. 2.41), while both inversion-based flow methods (RF Inversion, RF Edit) trail by 0.05–0.09 in F₀-PCC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inversion-free flow editing paradigm (FlowEdit [23]) from the image domain to music style transfer, replacing the stochastic diffusion inversion used by MusicTI [27] and ZETA [34] with a deterministic source-to-target velocity mapping. It builds on the Make-An-Audio 3 (MAA3) DiT backbone and introduces a cross-adapter mechanism that fuses CLIP and ViT visual embeddings with Flan-T5 text features, departing from prior multimodal music generation work (MeLFusion [3], MusFlow [53]) that focused on from-scratch synthesis rather than structure-preserving editing. The normalized chroma constraint is a novel loss term that has no direct analogue in prior MST literature, which typically relies on reference audio or inversion fidelity for melodic preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly condition music style transfer on both visual and textual modalities, and the reorganized MeLBench–MusicCaps multimodal benchmark (16-genre annotations, aligned triplets) provides a reusable evaluation resource for the community. The information-theoretic argument that direct visual embeddings strictly dominate caption-mediated conditioning (via the data-processing inequality) is a clean justification for the design choice, though the empirical margin over text-only conditioning is modest. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form or multi-track music, whether the chroma constraint generalizes to tasks requiring rhythmic (not just pitch-class) preservation, and whether the visual modality's contribution remains significant at larger model scales or with stronger text encoders.
