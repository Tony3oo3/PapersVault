## Problem

Federated learning under Non-IID data suffers from local model drift that slows convergence and degrades global accuracy, while the same distributional heterogeneity makes client gradients more distinctive and amplifies privacy leakage risks. Existing approaches either mitigate data skew without formal privacy guarantees or apply differential privacy without addressing the underlying distributional divergence, leaving a gap for a unified framework that simultaneously reduces inter-client statistical disparity and enforces rigorous DP constraints.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local image dataset (CIFAR-10 at 32×32 RGB or MNIST at 28×28 grayscale) that is partitioned into a sampled subset for local training and an unsampled subset for computing distributional statistics. The client uploads only DP-perturbed scalar moments (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) and a DP-perturbed gradient vector; the server never accesses raw images or unperturbed gradients. The final output is a globally trained image classifier whose parameters are updated via weighted aggregation of noisy client gradients.

## Method

FedAlign aligns local data distributions to a global reference by matching the first four statistical moments under client-level differential privacy. In each round, every client computes mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis on its unsampled data, clips each statistic, injects Gaussian noise via the Gaussian mechanism, and uploads the perturbed values; the server performs sample-size-weighted aggregation and broadcasts the global moments back. Each client then transforms its sampled training data so that its empirical moments match the received global statistics before performing local SGD for τ epochs. The resulting local gradient is clipped to bound sensitivity and further perturbed with Gaussian noise before being sent to the server for weighted averaging. Theoretically, the paper decomposes the expected squared gradient disparity into expected bias, local variance, cross-client covariance, and DP noise terms, and derives a convergence bound in which a statistical-disparity term Γ_stat is explicitly controlled by the four moment deviations, showing that mean and variance dominate gradient bias while skewness and kurtosis modulate tail-driven variance.

## Results

Under Dirichlet α=0.5 with Gaussian noise β=0.05, FedAlign achieves 54.9% test accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 99.26% on MNIST, outperforming all five baselines. Under the more challenging β=0.1 setting, FedAlign still leads with 54.3% on CIFAR-10 and 99.12% on MNIST. The paper also reports that ResNet50 on CIFAR-10 shows consistent gains from distribution alignment, and ablation studies confirm that jointly aligning all four statistical moments (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) yields the best convergence and accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAlign consistently outperforms all five baselines on CIFAR-10 under both Non-IID settings. At β=0.05, it achieves 54.9% versus FedAvg (49.1%), FedProx (51.1%), Scaffold (49.6%), FedNova (52.4%), and FedRDN (52.8%), corresponding to relative improvements of 11.9%, 7.5%, 10.7%, 4.8%, and 3.9% respectively. At the harder β=0.1 setting, the gap widens: FedAlign reaches 54.3% while the strongest baseline (FedAvg) only attains 50.7%, a 3.6-point margin. On MNIST, FedAlign leads at β=0.05 (99.26% vs. Scaffold's 99.22%) and ties with Scaffold at β=0.1 (both 99.12%), while clearly surpassing FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova, and FedRDN. The results indicate that statistical moment alignment is particularly effective on the more challenging CIFAR-10 dataset where feature heterogeneity is more pronounced, whereas on the simpler MNIST task the baselines already perform near ceiling and the gains are marginal.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedAlign sits at the intersection of two well-studied FL sub-areas: Non-IID mitigation (FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedNova, FedRDN) and differentially private FL (Fed-SMP, FLAME, McMahan et al.). It departs from prior Non-IID methods by operating at the data-distribution level via statistical moment alignment rather than at the model-update level (proximal terms, control variates, or update normalization), and from prior DP-FL work by arguing that reducing inter-client distributional divergence before training inherently lowers gradient sensitivity, thereby softening the privacy–utility trade-off. The theoretical contribution—decomposing gradient disparity into mean, variance, skewness, and kurtosis components under DP noise—extends the convergence analysis of Li et al. [17] and Reddi et al. [25] by explicitly parameterizing the Non-IID term through client-level moments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical bridge between client-level statistical moments and the global convergence bound under DP, which is useful for practitioners tuning privacy budgets in heterogeneous settings. However, experiments are limited to CIFAR-10 and MNIST with CNN/ResNet-50, leaving open whether moment alignment remains effective at ImageNet scale, with thousands of clients, or on non-Gaussian feature spaces. The ablation confirming that mean and variance dominate (higher-order moments contribute marginally) suggests the practical mechanism is essentially standardization, raising the question of whether the full four-moment framework is necessary or whether a simpler two-moment alignment suffices. Communication overhead from uploading four noisy statistics per client per round also warrants further study in large-scale deployments.
