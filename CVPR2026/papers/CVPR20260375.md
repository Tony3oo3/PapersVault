## Problem

Current MLLMs fail to resolve deictic questions (e.g., 'What is this?') in egocentric video when the referent is indicated only by a pointing gesture, because training data lacks natural gesture–deictic co-occurrence and architectures provide no explicit mechanism to encode hand pose or pointing direction. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a dedicated dataset and an architectural component that makes gesture information available to the language model.

## Input / Output

The model receives 32 uniformly sampled frames from a 3–8 second egocentric clip (448×448 synthetic or 1536×2048 real), a multiple-choice deictic question in first-person text, and per-frame 21-point 3D hand keypoints in camera space extracted by WiLoR. The keypoint adapter emits at most one continuous token per frame (suppressed when detection confidence falls below τ = 0.5), so the gesture stream adds less than 1% of total LLM tokens. The output is a single multiple-choice letter (A–D) selected from 4–5 options generated per question.

## Method

HINT (Hand Intent Tokens) adds a lightweight Keypoint Adapter that flattens 21 3D hand keypoints (63-dim vector), applies LayerNorm → linear → GeLU → linear to produce a single frame-aligned token in the LLM embedding space, and interleaves these tokens with the standard visual tokens in the input sequence. This gives the LLM an explicit, temporally ordered gesture stream alongside video and text, enabling it to ground deictic pronouns to the pointed object without requiring the model to infer hand pose from raw pixels. Training uses LoRA on the vision encoder and LLM with the adapter trained from scratch, on a mixed synthetic (AI2-THOR) and real (Meta Ray-Ban) dataset of 18,745 QA pairs spanning six deictic task categories. Ablations show the learned adapter outperforms alternatives such as overlaying visual keypoints/arrows on frames or feeding keypoint coordinates as text, and that uniform 32-frame sampling beats oracle keyframe selection, indicating temporal context is essential for gesture resolution.

## Results

On the EGOPOINTVQA test set (300 real-world egocentric videos, 672 QA pairs), HINT-InternVL3-14B achieves 68.1% average multiple-choice accuracy across six deictic task categories, surpassing the best open-source baseline InternVL3-14B (62.7%) by 5.4 pp and the best proprietary model GPT-5 (62.6%) by 5.5 pp. The largest per-task gain is on Reference (object identification), where HINT-14B reaches 73.8% vs. 63.1% for the backbone (+10.7 pp). HINT tokens add less than 1% of total input tokens and only 0.26 s of inference overhead (2.58 s → 2.84 s on 32-frame inputs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HINT-14B (68.1% avg) outperforms every baseline, including the strongest proprietary model GPT-5 (62.6%) and the largest open-source model Qwen3-VL-32B (67.5%), by 5.5 pp and 0.6 pp respectively. Against its own backbone, HINT-14B improves over InternVL3-14B by 5.4 pp on average, with the largest single-task gain on Reference (+10.7 pp: 63.1% → 73.8%). HINT-8B (63.7%) similarly beats InternVL3-8B (58.0%) by 5.7 pp and surpasses all ≤8B baselines including EgoGPT-7B (55.9%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (55.9%). Specialized models offer limited benefit: EgoGPT-7B leads small models on Reference (67.3%) but drops sharply on Temporal (46.4%), while VGLLM-QA-8B and ViSpeak-7B trail general MLLMs on most tasks. Scaling within a family yields only modest gains (e.g., InternVL3: 58.0% → 62.7% → 65.8% → 66.6% from 8B to 78B), confirming that the bottleneck is the absence of explicit gesture encoding rather than model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric video QA, region-specific VQA, and visual prompting. Prior egocentric VQA work (EgoThink, EgoGPT, Ego-R1) targets general scene understanding and long-term memory, while region-specific VQA methods (Ferret, Osprey, Omni-RGPT) assume the target region is explicitly provided via bounding boxes or masks. The paper departs from both by treating the user's natural pointing gesture as the sole referential signal, and from visual prompting work (Set-of-Mark, VIP-LLaVA) by using a learned 3D keypoint-to-token adapter rather than artificial 2D overlays. The HINT architecture repurposes off-the-shelf hand reconstruction (WiLoR) as a frozen front-end, with only a lightweight adapter and LoRA fine-tuning of the MLLM backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EGOPOINTVQA benchmark is the first to systematically evaluate deictic reference resolution from pointing gestures in egocentric video, filling a gap that is critical for next-generation wearable AI assistants (Apple Vision Pro, Meta Ray-Ban). The HINT approach is practically attractive—adding less than 1% token overhead and ~0.26s latency—making it deployable in real-time settings. Open questions include generalization to multi-person scenes, more complex or ambiguous gestures, and whether the 3D keypoint representation is truly necessary versus simpler 2D signals. The finding that even oracle keyframes underperform uniform sampling suggests the field still lacks a principled understanding of how much temporal context gesture interpretation requires.
