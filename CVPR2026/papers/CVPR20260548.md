## Problem

Existing conformal prediction and risk-control methods (CRC, RCPS, BQC) control only mean expected loss, implicitly treating all errors as equally costly. In safety-critical vision deployments—medical imaging, autonomous driving, multilabel detection—rare but high-loss failures matter far more than average accuracy, yet no distribution-free framework existed to explicitly control tail-focused risks such as CVaR while maintaining finite-sample guarantees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260548/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is entirely post-hoc: it takes a frozen pre-trained predictor (CLIP, PSPNet, SegFormer, or a regression model), a size-n i.i.d. calibration set, and a bounded loss function ℓ∈[0,1] parameterized by a threshold λ (or a (τ,k) pair for multilabel tasks). It outputs a calibrated deployment parameter that certifies the spectral risk is below a target α with confidence 1−δ, yielding prediction sets, prediction intervals, or per-pixel label sets whose tail risk is provably controlled without any model retraining.

## Method

BQ-SRC replaces the uniform integrator in Bayesian-quadrature conformal prediction with a monotone spectral density φ, so the calibration-conditional upper envelope L_φ⁺(λ) becomes a weighted sum of sorted calibration losses where weights are determined by φ evaluated at uniform order statistics—this envelope upper-bounds the spectral risk ρ_φ(λ) (Theorem 3.1) and recovers CRC when φ≡1. For quantile estimation, the method swaps the conservative DKW inflation for an exact one-sided Clopper-Pearson binomial lower confidence bound on the non-exceedance probability, empirically reducing Monte Carlo conservatism by roughly 3× (from ≈0.0276 to ≈0.0096 at M=5000) while preserving familywise validity over a finite grid via Bonferroni. For multilabel classification, a two-parameter (τ,k) control jointly tunes a score threshold and a top-k cutoff, weakly dominating single-parameter methods in efficiency. The entire procedure is black-box, distribution-free under exchangeability, and requires only the same calibration data as standard conformal methods.

## Results

On MS-COCO multilabel classification (α=0.1, 1−δ=0.95, M=5000), BQ-SRC-LCB (mix 95/5) achieves a mean prediction-set size of 3.39 with 1.80% violation rate, outperforming RCPS (3.57 set size, 0% violations) in efficiency while staying well below the δ=5% target. The two-parameter BQ-SRC-2D variant further reduces violations to 0.60% at a modest efficiency cost (set size 3.47). On heteroskedastic regression, CRC fails catastrophically with 46.18% violations, whereas BQ-SRC (mix 95/5) achieves only 0.90% violations with an interval length of 15.62 (vs. RCPS's 14.32). On ADE20K semantic segmentation, the mix 95/5 spectrum reduces empirical miscoverage risk from 0.099 (CRC) to 0.082 while increasing coverage from 0.901 to 0.918, at a cost of only ~0.25 extra predicted classes per pixel.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CRC, BQ-SRC is a clear winner on validity: CRC violates the spectral risk target in 44.50% of COCO runs and 46.18% of regression runs, while BQ-SRC stays at 1.80% and 0.90% respectively; however, CRC produces smaller prediction sets (2.93 vs. 3.39 on COCO) and shorter intervals (8.00 vs. 15.62 on regression) because it ignores tail risk entirely. Against RCPS, BQ-SRC-LCB wins on efficiency (mean set size 3.39 vs. 3.57 on COCO) while maintaining validity (1.80% vs. 0% violations, both below the 5% target), and the two-parameter variant further tightens violations to 0.60% at a small efficiency cost (3.47). Against BQC, BQ-SRC improves both validity (1.80% vs. 5.00% violations on COCO; 0.90% vs. 2.80% on regression) and spectral risk (0.087 vs. 0.090 on COCO), though BQC achieves a smaller set size (3.04) at the cost of exceeding the δ=5% violation threshold. The binomial LCB tightening is the key differentiator: it reduces Monte Carlo conservatism by roughly 3× compared to DKW inflation (≈0.0096 vs. ≈0.0276 margin at M=5000), directly translating into tighter prediction sets without sacrificing the finite-sample guarantee.

## Relation to Prior Work

BQ-SRC sits at the intersection of three threads: (i) conformal risk control (CRC) and risk-controlling prediction sets (RCPS), which provide distribution-free guarantees for mean loss; (ii) Bayesian-quadrature conformal prediction (BQC), which reframes the conformal calibration step as integration against a uniform integrator over the loss quantile function; and (iii) the spectral/coherent risk-measure literature (Kusuoka, Acerbi, Rockafellar–Uryasev), which encodes tail aversion via monotone spectral densities. The paper's core move is to replace BQC's uniform integrator with a general monotone spectral density φ, thereby recovering CRC as the φ≡1 special case and enabling CVaR or CVaR-mixture control, while swapping the conservative DKW inflation for an exact Clopper–Pearson binomial lower confidence bound to tighten the Monte Carlo certification step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical vision deployments (medical imaging, autonomous driving), controlling the tail of the loss distribution rather than its mean is arguably the more relevant guarantee, and BQ-SRC provides the first distribution-free, finite-sample framework that does so via a flexible spectral family. The ~3× reduction in Monte Carlo conservatism from the binomial LCB is a practical win that makes the method more efficient without weakening validity. Key limitations remain: guarantees are calibration-conditional (not fully distribution-free under arbitrary shift), the finite-grid Bonferroni correction does not extend to continuous or adaptive parameter selection, and the pure-CVaR case in segmentation degenerates to predicting all labels. Open directions include coupling with anytime-valid e-processes, handling distribution shift, and exploring application-specific spectral densities.
