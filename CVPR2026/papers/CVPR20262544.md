## Problem

Pre-trained visual representations for robotic control are task-agnostic because they remain frozen during policy learning, forcing practitioners to manually select the best representation per task. While text conditions successfully adapt diffusion models to other vision tasks, the paper shows they yield minimal or negative gains in control environments due to a domain gap between web-collected training images and simulated robotic scenes, where the model fails to ground words to relevant image regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB observation frames from MuJoCo-based control environments (DMC, MetaWorld, Adroit) and produces robot actions via a policy network. The frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net is queried at timestep t=0 with a composite condition built from 4 learnable task tokens and 16 visual tokens (dense DINOv2 features projected through a small convolutional layer). Visual representations are extracted from the downsampling blocks (down_1 through down_3) and the bottleneck (mid) of the U-Net, concatenated, and passed through a compression layer before feeding the policy network.

## Method

ORCA conditions a frozen Stable Diffusion model with two types of learnable prompts learned end-to-end under a behavior cloning objective: (1) task prompts—4 shared learnable tokens that implicitly encode task-relevant regions without explicit text, avoiding the grounding failures of natural-language captions; and (2) visual prompts—dense per-frame DINOv2 features projected to 16 tokens that capture fine-grained, frame-specific dynamics. The composite condition C* = τ_θ(p_t; p_v) is injected via cross-attention in the U-Net, and intermediate feature maps from the early downsampling and bottleneck blocks serve as the task-adaptive visual representation. Only 10.6M parameters (the prompts and policy) are trained, while the entire diffusion backbone remains frozen, enabling prompt swapping across tasks without retraining. The key design choice is using dense rather than global visual features and avoiding separate optimization steps (unlike TADP's Textual Inversion), so that frame-wise conditioning emerges naturally during policy learning.

## Results

ORCA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 12 tasks in three benchmarks. On DeepMind Control, ORCA reaches a mean normalized score of 74.3, surpassing the best task-adaptive baseline TADP (70.7) and the best task-agnostic diffusion baseline SCR (68.3). On MetaWorld, ORCA attains a mean success rate of 95.2% (vs. TADP 93.1%, SCR 90.4%), and on Adroit it achieves 65.3% mean success rate (vs. SCR 58.0%, CoOp 58.0%). ORCA uses only 10.6M learnable parameters (task + visual prompts) while fine-tuning SCR requires 346.7M parameters yet collapses to 9.3% mean success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORCA outperforms all five baselines on every one of the 12 tasks. Against the task-agnostic frozen representations, ORCA improves DMC mean by +14.8 over CLIP (74.3 vs. 59.5) and +21.2 over VC-1 (74.3 vs. 53.1), confirming that frozen encoders cannot adapt to task-specific visual requirements. Among diffusion-model-based baselines, ORCA surpasses SCR (frozen SD1.5 features) by +6.0 on DMC and +4.8 on MetaWorld, showing that conditioning is critical even when the backbone is already a diffusion model. Against the task-adaptive prompting baselines, ORCA beats CoOp (which fixes the task name and adds learnable prefix tokens) by +3.7 on DMC and +2.7 on MetaWorld, and beats TADP (which appends a single Textual Inversion style token to Gemini-generated captions) by +3.6 on DMC and +2.1 on MetaWorld. The largest individual-task gains appear on DMC Cheetah-run (+9.0 over TADP, +6.6 over SCR) and DMC Walker-walk (+7.0 over TADP), where frame-level visual detail is most critical. On Adroit, ORCA leads SCR by +7.3 mean success rate, with the gap widening on Relocate (44.0% vs. 32.0%), a dexterous manipulation task requiring fine-grained hand-object interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORCA bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) frozen pre-trained visual representations for robotic control (VC-1, SCR, R3M, CLIP), which are task-agnostic, and (2) diffusion-model conditioning for visual perception (VPD, TADP, CoOp), which uses text or learned tokens to make representations task-adaptive. The paper departs from the perception line by showing that text conditions—successful in segmentation and depth estimation—fail in control environments due to a domain gap between web-scale training data and simulated robot scenes. It also departs from the control line by avoiding both frozen encoders and full fine-tuning, instead adopting the learnable-prompt paradigm from Textual Inversion [8] and CoOp [52] to condition a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net with task-specific and frame-specific information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why the widely-used text-conditioning recipe does not transfer to robotic control, and offers a lightweight (10.6M learnable parameters) alternative to fine-tuning that avoids the catastrophic overfitting observed when SCR is fine-tuned (58% → 9.3% success rate). This positions prompt-based conditioning as a practical path to task-adaptive representations without per-task model storage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world (non-simulated) control, multi-task or zero-shot settings, and whether the specific choice of DINOv2 as the visual prompt encoder is critical or interchangeable.
