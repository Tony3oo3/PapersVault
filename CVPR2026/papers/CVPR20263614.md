## Problem

LUT-based image restoration methods face a fundamental trade-off: expanding the receptive field to improve quality requires cascading more LUTs or larger kernels, which inflates storage and inference latency and hinders edge deployment. Additionally, the symmetric dual-branch architecture inherited from SPLUT applies identical computational depth to both MSB and LSB streams, wasting computation on the inherently sparse LSB branch where activations approach 100% zero at depth.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8-bit input images (bicubically downsampled for ×4 SR, Gaussian-noised grayscale for denoising, JPEG-compressed at QF=10 for deblocking) and splits them into 6-bit MSB and 2-bit LSB components before LUT-based processing. Outputs are full-resolution restored images: 4× upscaled RGB for SR, same-size denoised grayscale, and same-size deblocked images. All models are extremely compact (24–104 KB) and evaluated on-device at 320×180 resolution on a Snapdragon 888 CPU, with runtimes of 22–84 ms.

## Method

ShiftLUT introduces three key mechanisms. First, a Learnable Spatial Shift (LSS) module predicts per-channel spatial offsets via a lightweight offset network and applies them to feature maps, expanding the effective receptive field (up to 65×65) without enlarging any LUT; a two-stage training strategy first learns continuous offsets then replaces them with fixed integer shifts, eliminating interpolation at inference. Second, an asymmetric dual-branch architecture assigns deep stacked Shift-Blocks (LSS + PwBlock + DwConv) only to the information-dense MSB branch while reducing the sparse LSB branch to a single 3×3 convolution, cutting inference latency nearly in half. Third, Error-bounded Adaptive Sampling (EAS) selects a per-LUT optimal power-of-two stride under a predefined interpolation error bound and caches interpolated outputs in a small reusable buffer, reducing LUT storage by over 50% while restoring runtime close to the uncompressed baseline. All convolutions are converted to 1D LUTs via the separable mapping strategy, and a rotation ensemble trick further expands coverage during inference.

## Results

On the primary ×4 super-resolution task, ShiftLUT-L achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 28.19/0.8014 across five standard benchmarks (Set5, Set14, BSDS100, Urban100, Manga109), surpassing the previous best LUT-based method TinyLUT-F (28.01/0.7956) by 0.18 dB while reducing storage from 171 KB to 104 KB and runtime from 146 ms to 84 ms. The smallest variant, ShiftLUT-S, uses only 24 KB and runs in 22 ms while still outperforming TinyLUT-S (27.39 vs. 27.13 avg PSNR). For denoising (noise level 15), ShiftLUT-L reaches 32.43 dB on Set12 and 31.32 dB on BSD68; for deblocking (QF=10), it reaches 29.12 dB on Classic5 and 28.96 dB on LIVE1, both with 60 KB storage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShiftLUT-L outperforms all ten compared baselines on average PSNR for ×4 SR. Against the closest LUT-based competitor TinyLUT-F, it gains +0.18 dB average PSNR (28.19 vs. 28.01) while halving storage (104 vs. 171 KB) and cutting runtime by 42% (84 vs. 146 ms). It also surpasses the DNN-based VDSR (28.07) and FSRCNN (27.56) in average PSNR despite using orders of magnitude less storage (104 KB vs. 2660 KB and 48 KB respectively) and running 160× faster than VDSR. Among other LUT methods, ShiftLUT-L leads SPFLUT+DFC by +0.31 dB, RCLUT by +0.60 dB, MuLUT by +0.71 dB, and ECLUT by +1.27 dB. On denoising, ShiftLUT-L beats TinyLUT-F by +0.21 dB (Set12) and +0.12 dB (BSD68) with 3× less storage; on deblocking, it gains +0.38 dB (Classic5) and +0.29 dB (LIVE1) over TinyLUT-F while also outperforming the CNN-based ARCNN.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShiftLUT sits at the intersection of two research threads: LUT-based image restoration (SR-LUT, MuLUT, SPLUT, TinyLUT) and feature-shifting operations (ShiftNet, PCS, Group ShiftNet). It inherits the MSB/LSB dual-branch decomposition from SPLUT and the 1D separable LUT conversion from TinyLUT, but departs from both by introducing learnable (rather than fixed) channel-wise spatial shifts to expand receptive field, and by replacing the symmetric dual-branch design with a radically asymmetric one that reduces the LSB path to a single convolution. The Error-bounded Adaptive Sampling strategy also departs from prior fixed-stride or uniform sampling approaches by per-LUT adaptive stride selection under an explicit error budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners targeting edge deployment, ShiftLUT establishes a new Pareto frontier for LUT-based restoration: 104 KB storage and 84 ms on-device runtime with a 65×65 receptive field, outperforming the previous best (TinyLUT-F) by 0.21 dB average PSNR while using less memory and less time. The empirical finding that LSB-branch activations become nearly 100% zero at depth provides a concrete justification for asymmetric designs that prior work had only partially explored. Open questions include whether the two-stage 'learn-then-freeze' offset strategy generalizes to video or multi-frame restoration, and whether the fixed integer shifts discovered during training remain optimal under distribution shift (e.g., different sensor noise profiles).
