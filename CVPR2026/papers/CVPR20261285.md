## Problem

Existing training-free token pruning methods for VLMs face a fundamental trade-off: attention-based approaches retain redundant tokens due to unstable attention scores, while diversity-based methods may discard semantically critical regions by treating all tokens uniformly. No prior method explicitly quantifies each token's actual influence on the model output (sensitivity) in a training-free, backpropagation-free manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261285/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes visual tokens produced by a pre-trained vision encoder (576 tokens at 336×336 for LLaVA-1.5, up to 2880 at 672×672 for LLaVA-NeXT) and a text prompt. It outputs a pruned subset of k visual tokens (e.g., 64–640) that are concatenated with the text embedding and fed to the LLM for generation. Sensitivity estimation is performed at the lightweight projection layer using m=64 Gaussian perturbation directions with step size h=0.01, requiring only forward passes through a few projection layers rather than the full vision encoder.

## Method

ZOO-Prune estimates per-token sensitivity via zeroth-order gradient approximation at the projection layer: for each token, m random unit-norm Gaussian perturbations are injected, and the average L2 norm of the symmetric finite-difference responses through the projection function M yields a scalar sensitivity score S(i). A Proposition shows this score approximates E_u[‖J(x)u‖₂], capturing the magnitude of local output response rather than gradient direction. The paper empirically validates (via Spearman rank correlation) that sensitivity rankings computed at the projection layer align with those from the full vision encoder, justifying the lightweight proxy. Token selection then proceeds greedily using a multiplicative fusion score Score(i) = Ŝ(i)·Div(i, P), where Div(i, P) = 1 − max_{j∈P} cos(Z_i, Z_j) enforces feature diversity among already-selected tokens. This multiplicative design requires no additional weighting hyperparameters and ensures the first selected token is the most sensitive (unlike DivPrune's farthest-point initialization), while subsequent selections balance informativeness against redundancy.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2880 tokens), ZOO-Prune retains 98.3% of baseline performance at 77.8% pruning (640 tokens) and 95.4% at 94.4% pruning (160 tokens), outperforming VisionZip (90.4%) and DivPrune (92.4%) at the most aggressive setting. On LLaVA-1.5-7B (576 tokens), it achieves 95.20% average with only 64 tokens (88.9% pruning), surpassing the closest competitor DivPrune (94.42%) and attention-based methods like FastV (71.10%) by a wide margin. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ZOO-Prune reaches 96.2% at 20% token budget and 90.8% at 10%, consistently leading VisionZip and DivPrune. End-to-end inference is accelerated by up to 2.30× and prefilling by 2.59× at the 160-token setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZOO-Prune outperforms all seven baselines across every model and pruning ratio tested. The closest competitor is DivPrune (diversity-based), which ZOO-Prune beats by 0.78% on LLaVA-1.5-7B at 88.9% pruning (95.20% vs. 94.42%) and by 3.0% on LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 94.4% pruning (95.4% vs. 92.4%), with the gap widening as pruning becomes more aggressive. VisionZip (attention-based) trails ZOO-Prune by 2.5% at 94.4% pruning on LLaVA-NeXT-7B (90.4% vs. 95.4%) and by 2.46% at 88.9% on LLaVA-1.5-7B (92.74% vs. 95.20%). Attention-based methods FastV and ToMe degrade severely under aggressive pruning, dropping to 71.10% and 70.12% respectively at 88.9% on LLaVA-1.5-7B, far below ZOO-Prune's 95.20%. HiRED and PyramidDrop, which use attention-guided or pyramid strategies, achieve 87.95% and 72.66% at 88.9% pruning on LLaVA-1.5-7B, respectively, confirming that attention-centric signals are unreliable at high compression. SparseVLM (question-driven cross-attention) is competitive at moderate pruning but lacks full-benchmark reporting on LLaVA-NeXT-7B, making direct comparison incomplete.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZOO-Prune repurposes the Randomized Gradient Estimator (RGE) from zeroth-order optimization [18, 28]—originally designed for black-box convex minimization—as a token-importance signal in VLMs, a novel application that avoids backpropagation entirely. It builds directly on DivPrune's [1] max-min diversity selection but augments it with a sensitivity term, addressing DivPrune's tendency to discard task-critical regions in favor of feature-diverse but semantically irrelevant tokens. The key conceptual departure from the broader token-pruning literature (FastV, VisionZip, ToMe, SparseVLM, PyramidDrop) is the shift from attention-based or purely geometric scoring to an output-influence metric computed at the lightweight projection layer, which the authors validate as a reliable proxy for full-encoder sensitivity via Spearman rank correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZOO-Prune offers a practical, plug-and-play solution for VLM deployment on resource-constrained hardware: 94.4% token reduction with only ~5% average performance drop and 2.3× end-to-end speedup, all without calibration data or fine-tuning. Its generalizability across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and Qwen2.5-VL (with different vision backbones and token counts) suggests the projection-layer sensitivity signal is architecture-agnostic. Open questions include whether the m=64 perturbation budget remains sufficient for even larger models (e.g., 2880+ tokens at higher resolutions), whether the projection-layer proxy degrades for non-linear or multi-layer projectors, and whether the sensitivity signal can be further distilled into a single forward pass to eliminate the remaining overhead.
