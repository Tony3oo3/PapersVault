key_contribution: WPT (World-to-Policy Transfer) is a training paradigm that distills world-model predictive knowledge into a lightweight driving policy through a trainable reward model and dual distillation (policy + world reward), achieving state-of-the-art open-loop performance (0.61m L2, 0.11% collision) and strong closed-loop scores (79.23 driving score) while enabling 4.9× faster inference at deployment by discarding the world model after training.
limitations: The training pipeline is multi-stage and computationally heavy, requiring a pretrained world model (Drive-OccWorld), a teacher policy, a reward model, and a student policy, which adds overhead and dependency on the quality of the world model's future-state predictions. The distilled student model, while 4.9× faster at inference, accepts a measurable accuracy loss (0.66m vs. 0.61m L2; 0.24% vs. 0.11% collision), and the closed-loop driving score improvement (79.23 vs. 67.92 for next-best DiffAD) is less pronounced than open-loop gains, suggesting that real-world closed-loop constraints are tighter and that the 4D occupancy modality may limit generalization to other world-model representations such as video or latent features without further adaptation.

surprise_factor: The closed-loop driving score gap (79.23 vs. 67.92 for the next-best DiffAD) is substantial, and the ablation showing WM-generated occupancy outperforms ground-truth occupancy when simulation rewards are active is mildly counterintuitive. However, the overall approach—distilling a WM-augmented teacher into a lightweight student—is a natural application of well-known distillation principles, and the open-loop gains, while SOTA, are incremental over strong baselines like SSR and GaussianAD.

## Problem

World models for autonomous driving either require tight runtime coupling with the policy (causing substantial inference overhead from autoregressive rollouts) or rely on offline reward signals that hinder end-to-end optimization. The paper asks how an AD policy can internalize world-model predictive knowledge during training while deploying as a lightweight, real-time planner without any world-model dependency at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261151/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a temporal sequence of multi-view camera images (h historical frames) and encodes them into a BEV world representation. The teacher policy generates N multi-modal candidate trajectories evaluated against the world model's predicted future 4D occupancy, while the distilled student policy produces a single trajectory in one forward pass. At deployment, the student requires only the camera input and outputs a future driving trajectory with no world-model or occupancy-prediction overhead.

## Method

WPT is a training-time paradigm with four components: (1) a frozen pretrained world model (Drive-OccWorld) that predicts future 4D occupancy conditioned on candidate trajectories; (2) a trainable reward model that scores each candidate by jointly encoding the predicted future world state and the trajectory, producing both an imitation reward (softmax-normalized L2 distance to expert) and a simulation reward (five NAVISIM-style metrics: no-collision, drivable-area compliance, time-to-collision, ego progress, comfort); (3) a multi-modal teacher policy that generates N candidates and selects the best via the combined reward; and (4) dual distillation into a single-modal student — policy distillation aligns the student's planning queries to the teacher's world-conditioned queries via L2 loss, while world reward distillation minimizes the gap between the student's trajectory reward score and the teacher's optimal trajectory reward score. After training, the world model and teacher are discarded, leaving a compact policy that achieves 4.9× faster inference while retaining most of the teacher's planning gains.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop evaluation, WPT-Teacher achieves 0.61 m average L2 error and 0.11% average collision rate, while the distilled WPT-Student retains most gains at 0.66 m / 0.24% without any test-time world-model overhead. On Bench2Drive closed-loop evaluation, WPT-Teacher attains a 79.23 driving score and 54.54% success rate, substantially outperforming the strongest prior methods (DriveTransformer: 63.46 DS / 35.01% SR; DriveDPO: 62.02 DS / 30.62% SR). The student model matches the baseline's 64 ms inference latency while being 4.9× faster than the teacher (312 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WPT-Teacher outperforms all 20+ baselines on nuScenes in both L2 error and collision rate. Against the closest world-model-based competitors, WPT-Teacher reduces L2 from 0.75 m (SSR) and 0.85 m (Drive-OccWorld) to 0.61 m, and cuts collision from 0.15% (SSR) and 0.29% (Drive-OccWorld) to 0.11%. Compared to strong imitation-learning methods like UniAD (1.03 m / 0.31%) and VAD-Base (1.22 m / 0.53%), WPT-Teacher improves L2 by 40–50% and collision by 2–5×. On Bench2Drive closed-loop, WPT-Teacher's 79.23 driving score exceeds DriveTransformer (63.46) by +15.77 points and DriveDPO (62.02) by +17.21 points, while also achieving the highest success rate (54.54% vs. 35.01% and 30.62%). The distilled WPT-Student retains most of these gains (72.61 DS, 45.45% SR) at 4.9× lower inference latency, confirming that world-model knowledge transfers effectively to a lightweight policy without runtime world-model calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

WPT builds directly on Drive-OccWorld (Yang et al., AAAI 2025) as its frozen world-model backbone, but departs from the two dominant paradigms for integrating world models into AD policies: (1) runtime-coupled methods like WoTE and DriveDPO that require autoregressive WM rollouts at inference, and (2) simulator-based RL approaches that depend on synthetic environment fidelity. The key conceptual departure is treating the world model as a purely training-time teacher, using a trainable reward model to score candidate trajectories against predicted future states, then distilling both the planning queries and the reward-based selection into a lightweight student. This positions WPT as a bridge between world-model reasoning and efficient deployment, rather than a new world model or a new planning architecture per se.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm of training with a world model and deploying without one addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited real-world adoption of WM-based planners; the 4.9× inference speedup with minimal quality loss makes this a viable path for production systems. The paper notes an efficiency–comfort trade-off in closed-loop results and leaves reward-shaping refinements for future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other WM modalities (video, latent) beyond 4D occupancy, how sensitive the gains are to WM prediction quality, and whether the reward model can be further simplified or replaced by learned preference signals without explicit simulation metrics.
