## Problem

Task arithmetic enables training-free model editing by algebraically combining task vectors, yet the fundamental reason for its success remains unexplained. The existing concept of weight disentanglement describes the desired non-interfering outcome but does not identify what intrinsic properties of the pre-trained model or task vectors actually cause it, leaving practitioners without a principled way to select suitable foundation models or construct well-composing task vectors.

## Input / Output

The method operates on CLIP-pretrained Vision Transformers (ViT-B-32, ViT-B-16, ViT-L-14) with frozen text encoders, fine-tuning the image encoder on task-specific RGB image datasets (8 classification benchmarks). During fine-tuning, the orthogonal regularizer is computed on the weight update matrices (ΔW) of all linear layers (FC and attention projections), penalizing deviation of each layer's Gram matrix from the identity. The output is a set of orthogonalized task vectors that, when summed with a single uniform scaling coefficient α, yield a merged multi-task model; the same vectors also support task negation (subtraction) for unlearning.

## Method

The paper identifies Task-Feature Specialization (TFS)—the model's allocation of disjoint internal feature subsets to different tasks—as the sufficient condition for weight disentanglement (Theorem 1) and shows it geometrically manifests as block/column-wise orthogonality of weight matrices (Corollary 1). Since TFS rarely holds in practice due to feature overlap, the authors propose OrthoReg, a plug-and-play regularizer L_ortho = Σ_l ‖(ΔW^(l))ᵀΔW^(l) − I‖²_F added to the standard fine-tuning loss, which enforces column-wise orthogonality and unit norm on each linear layer's weight update. Theorem 2 proves that even under feature overlap, this constraint promotes disentanglement through a dual mechanism: bounding the task vector norm and driving the inter-task vector angle toward 90° (Angle Control). The paper further establishes that OrthoReg and Tangent Task Arithmetic both succeed via the same inter-task orthogonality principle, but OrthoReg achieves it explicitly at a fraction of TTA's computational cost (no Jacobian computation required).

## Results

On the 8-task addition benchmark with CLIP ViT-L-14, OrthoReg consistently improves all baselines: Non-lin. FT gains +4.16 Abs.Acc. (84.07→88.23%) and +10.89 Norm.Acc. (89.19→100.08%), while ATT-FT reaches the best absolute accuracy of 90.41% (+2.60) with 100.05% normalized accuracy. The normalized accuracy exceeding 100% on ViT-L-14 indicates the merged model matches or surpasses individually fine-tuned single-task models, confirming near-perfect weight disentanglement. On task negation (ViT-L-14), OrthoReg applied to ATT-FT reduces target accuracy by an additional 10.18 points (24.85→14.67%) while preserving ImageNet control accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrthoReg wins against all four baselines across all three model scales (ViT-B-32, ViT-B-16, ViT-L-14) on both absolute and normalized accuracy. The largest absolute gains come from Non-lin. FT [10] (+4.16 on ViT-L-14) and ATT-FT [12] (+2.60 on ViT-L-14), while TTA [24] sees a smaller but consistent improvement (+1.33 on ViT-L-14), suggesting TTA's tangent-space fine-tuning already partially enforces orthogonality. LoRA-ATT [9] benefits most on ViT-B-16 (+4.16 Abs.Acc.), indicating OrthoReg is particularly effective when the parameter budget is constrained. The ATT-FT+OrthoReg combination achieves the best absolute accuracy (90.41%) on ViT-L-14, establishing a new state-of-the-art on this benchmark. On task negation, OrthoReg also outperforms all baselines in forgetting effectiveness, with the most dramatic gain on ATT-FT (−10.18 target accuracy on ViT-L-14).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the task arithmetic framework [Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023] and the weight disentanglement concept from Tangent Task Arithmetic [Ortiz-Jimenez et al., NeurIPS 2023] by identifying Task-Feature Specialization (TFS) as the underlying causal mechanism that simultaneously produces both the functional property (disentanglement) and the geometric property (weight vector orthogonality). Rather than treating orthogonality as a heuristic or disentanglement as a phenomenological description, the paper unifies them under a single sufficient condition and then proposes OrthoReg—a simple Frobenius-norm regularizer on weight updates—as a practical way to enforce the geometric consequence when the ideal TFS condition is violated. The method is positioned as a pre-merging technique in the same family as TTA and Attention-Only Fine-tuning [Jin et al., ICLR 2025], but with a direct theoretical link showing all three converge on inter-task vector orthogonality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TFS framework provides the first principled explanation for why task arithmetic works, moving the field from empirical observation to a testable theoretical condition, and the ≥100% normalized accuracy results on ViT-L-14 offer concrete evidence that near-perfect disentanglement is achievable with a simple regularizer. The plug-and-play nature of OrthoReg (a single added loss term) makes it immediately applicable to any fine-tuning pipeline for model merging, with consistent gains across four baselines and three model scales. Limitations include reliance on the NTK linearization hypothesis, focus on linear layers only, and the fact that TFS itself remains an idealization rarely met in practice; the authors note that exploring more diverse orthogonality constraints is a natural next step. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to language models, deeper non-linear interactions, and whether the NTK assumption holds for the parameter shifts involved in modern fine-tuning.
