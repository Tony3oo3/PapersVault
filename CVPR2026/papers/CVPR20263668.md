## Problem

Reconstructing a physiologically plausible full cardiac cycle (ED→ES→ED) from a single arbitrary echocardiography frame remains unsolved. Existing image-to-video methods synthesize motion only forward in time from a fixed starting point, failing to capture the intrinsic periodicity and asymmetric systole/diastole dynamics of cardiac motion, which limits temporal coherence and prevents reconstruction from mid-cycle prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263668/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single 112×112 (or 224×224) apical four-chamber echocardiography frame from any arbitrary phase of the cardiac cycle—not restricted to the first frame—and produces a 13-frame complete cardiac cycle video spanning one full ED→ES→ED transition. The phase of the input frame is estimated internally by the model, enabling bidirectional generation (backward to ED, forward through ES, and back to ED) without requiring the user to specify temporal direction.

## Method

EchoVDiff is a three-stage pipeline centered on an explicit, learnable cardiac phase axis. Stage 1 (PCDM) trains a ViT-based video encoder via multi-task learning—joint LV segmentation (MedSAM2 pseudo-labels), differentiable area regression, and circular phase regression—regularized by physiological self-consistency losses (monotonicity of area within systole/diastole, ED/ES peak alignment, and a learnable area-phase curve). Stage 2 (Echo-VRDM) is a phase-conditioned Transformer diffusion model that learns the distribution of compact per-frame representation vectors P(R|Φ) in the encoder's latent space, with phase injected via cross-attention and classifier-free guidance. Stage 3 (Echo-LVDM) fine-tunes a pretrained VideoLDM U-Net by injecting the generated representations R into spatial cross-attention layers (anatomical content) and phase embeddings Φ into newly added temporal cross-attention layers (motion dynamics), achieving disentangled control over structure and cardiac rhythm in pixel space.

## Results

On EchoNet-Dynamic, EchoVDiff achieves the best FVD of 535.61, FID of 71.34, tLPIPS of 0.218, and tSSIM of 0.821, outperforming the strongest baseline VTG (FVD 630.78) by 15.1% on FVD and 9.6% on FID. On EchoNet-Pediatric, it similarly leads with FVD 643.57 and FID 82.45, beating VTG (FVD 718.64) by 10.4%. Notably, performance remains stable when prompted from arbitrary cardiac phases (ES or late diastole), with FVD varying by less than 1.5% compared to the ED starting frame, confirming robustness to input phase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoVDiff outperforms all six baselines on every metric (FVD, FID, tLPIPS, tSSIM) on both EchoNet-Dynamic and EchoNet-Pediatric. Against the closest competitor VTG (ICCV'25), it reduces FVD by 15.1% (630.78→535.61) and FID by 9.6% (78.92→71.34) on EchoNet-Dynamic, with similar margins on the pediatric set. The medical-specific baseline Ultrasound-I2V (MICCAI'24) trails by 21.6% in FVD, while general-purpose diffusion generators VideoCrafter and DynamiCrafter lag by 37–38% in FVD. Motion-transfer methods FOMM and TPS are the weakest, with FVD values exceeding 1000, indicating poor temporal coherence without explicit phase modeling. The consistent advantage across both adult and pediatric datasets suggests the phase-aware conditioning generalizes across patient populations.

## Relation to Prior Work

EchoVDiff builds on the VideoLDM latent diffusion architecture (Blattmann et al., CVPR 2023) as its pixel-space generation backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior I2V and ultrasound video generation methods by introducing an explicit, learnable cardiac phase axis rather than treating temporal indices as generic motion signals. Where prior approaches (FOMM, TPS, VideoCrafter, Ultrasound-I2V, VTG) either transfer motion from a reference video or condition on fixed start/end frames, EchoVDiff learns a circular phase manifold through multi-task supervision (LV segmentation, area regression, phase regression) with physiological consistency constraints, then injects this phase signal into both a representation-level diffusion model and the latent video denoiser. The use of a medical foundation model (EchoFM) as a frozen feature extractor and MedSAM2 for pseudo-label generation positions the work at the intersection of foundation-model-based medical imaging and generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a complete, physiologically consistent cardiac cycle (ED→ES→ED) can be reconstructed from a single frame at an arbitrary phase, which has direct clinical relevance since real-world acquisitions rarely start at end-diastole. The phase-aware formulation provides an interpretable, disentangled control mechanism (anatomy via representation, motion via phase) that could generalize to other periodic medical imaging tasks (e.g., respiratory cycles, fetal heart motion). Open questions include generalization beyond the apical four-chamber view, scalability to longer or irregular cycles (arrhythmias), and whether the 13-frame cycle length is sufficient for downstream clinical tasks like ejection fraction estimation. The reliance on MedSAM2 pseudo-labels and a fixed systolic/diastolic ratio (α=1/3) may limit robustness to pathological cases with altered timing.
