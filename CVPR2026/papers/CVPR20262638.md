## Problem

Existing training-free visual token pruning methods for MLLMs discard tokens in early Transformer layers before the model has fully absorbed their visual content, causing inevitable performance degradation that worsens at high pruning ratios (80–90%). The fundamental gap is that these methods make pruning decisions purely from token features without any mechanism to transfer information from the tokens being removed to those being kept.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query, which are processed by a vision encoder and projector to produce N visual tokens and L text tokens. It outputs a reduced visual token set of size K = (1−r)·N (where r is the pruning ratio, up to 90%) that replaces the full set for all subsequent LLM layers, ultimately producing a text response. The method is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no fine-tuning or architectural modification, and supports FlashAttention integration for the fusion computation in early layers.

## Method

Hi-Lo Prune operates in three stages guided by the principle of 'look at what you will lose before you remove it.' First, hierarchical token selection uses a coarse-to-fine greedy farthest-point sampling (with cosine distance) over pixel-level texture features to identify αK candidates, then refines to K retained tokens using semantic-level embeddings; tokens selected coarsely but discarded finely form a fusion set F (~30% of all tokens). Second, Prune-Aware Fusion (PA-Fusion) modifies the attention logits in early LLM layers by additively boosting attention between retained tokens K and fusion tokens F, scaled by the original attention value and gated by an adaptive softmax threshold and top-k condition, so that high-affinity pruned tokens transfer their visual context to retained tokens before removal. Third, at a designated prune layer, all tokens outside K are removed, reducing computation for all subsequent layers. Unlike SparseVLM which fuses pruned tokens after removal, PA-Fusion lets to-be-pruned tokens contribute information before they are discarded, and unlike multi-layer progressive pruning, it achieves this in a single pruning event with minimal overhead.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B with 90% token pruning (retaining only 10%), Hi-Lo Prune achieves 93.51% average score across 8 benchmarks, outperforming the next-best method DivPrune (92.42%) and CDPruner (83.87%). At 80% pruning, the average reaches 95.04%, with only a 4.96-point gap from the unpruned baseline (100%). In terms of efficiency, Hi-Lo Prune reduces total inference time by 43.75% and prefill time by 49.79% on Qwen3-VL-8B, while cutting FLOPs by 85.35% (from 2.576T to 0.377T), with an MME score of 2042.58 versus 2390.83 for the vanilla model (14.6% degradation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hi-Lo Prune consistently outperforms all four single-layer pruning baselines (FastV, DART, DivPrune, CDPruner) across all three Qwen models at both 80% and 90% pruning ratios. The closest competitor is DivPrune (diversity-based), which trails by 1.1–4.3% in average score; CDPruner is second-closest on Qwen3-VL-8B (85.38% vs. 87.17% at 90% pruning) but falls further behind on smaller models. Importance-based methods FastV and DART degrade most severely at 90% pruning, with DART dropping to 69.58% on Qwen3-VL-8B (a 30.4-point loss from vanilla) versus Hi-Lo Prune's 12.8-point loss. Against multi-layer pruning baselines, Hi-Lo Prune also wins: on Qwen2-VL-2B at 90% pruning it scores 85.02% average versus 83.41% (SparseVLM) and 82.10% (PyramidDrop), while on LLaVA-1.5-7B it achieves 93.88% versus 92.20% (DivPrune) and 91.38% (CDPruner). The ablation in Table 6 further shows that adding PA-Fusion to CDPrune and DivPrune yields consistent gains (e.g., +67.96 MME points for DivPrune at 90% pruning on Qwen3-VL-8B), confirming that the fusion mechanism is the key differentiator rather than the selection strategy alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free visual token pruning line (FastV, DART, DivPrune, CDPruner) by introducing a 'look at what you'll lose' principle: rather than simply selecting tokens to retain via importance or diversity scores, it explicitly transfers information from to-be-pruned tokens to retained ones before removal. The key conceptual departure from SparseVLM, which fuses pruned tokens *after* removal, is that Hi-Lo Prune lets candidates contribute to retained tokens *before* they are discarded, via a lightweight attention-boosting mechanism (PA-Fusion) in early LLM layers. The hierarchical coarse-to-fine selection (pixel-level texture features → semantic embeddings) repurposes standard greedy farthest-point sampling at two granularities, but the fusion step is the genuinely new architectural element.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature and consistent gains across Qwen2/2.5/3-VL and LLaVA make this a practical drop-in for anyone deploying MLLMs with high-resolution inputs, and the ablation showing PA-Fusion improves *other* pruning methods (CDPruner, DivPrune) suggests the fusion mechanism is a broadly useful component. Open questions include whether the fixed 30% fusion-set ratio and two-layer fusion window are optimal, how the approach behaves on video token sequences (where N is orders of magnitude larger), and whether the attention-boosting interacts adversely with FlashAttention's tiling in very long sequences. The paper does not explore adaptive per-sample fusion strength or layer selection, which could further close the gap to the vanilla model at 90% pruning.
