## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D Gaussian Splatting hair reconstruction methods (e.g., GaussianHair) require millions of Gaussian primitives with per-primitive appearance parameters, resulting in prohibitive storage and rendering costs. While hair geometry and appearance exhibit strong structural regularity across a hairstyle, prior 3DGS-based approaches fail to exploit this redundancy, leaving a gap between visual quality and practical deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261935/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence (approximately 100 uniformly sampled frames) as input, from which camera poses, hair segmentation masks, and Gabor-filter orientation maps are precomputed. The output is a compact CGHair representation consisting of ~25,000 strands organized into 800 hair cards, further grouped into 64 card clusters each sharing a 10-entry, 64-dimensional appearance codebook, plus a globally shared MLP decoder—totaling under 1 MB of appearance parameters versus 163.7 MB for the uncompressed baseline, while supporting real-time novel-view rendering via the standard 3DGS pipeline.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, strand geometry is generated by optimizing PERM's pretrained StyleGAN2 guide decoder and VAE style decoder (parameterized by codes α and β on the scalp mesh) jointly with cylindrical Gaussians under photometric and orientation losses, achieving 4× speedup over prior methods. Second, strands are clustered via k-means into hair cards; each card's geometry is a B-spline-centered strip mesh with normals solved by constrained optimization, and 2D strand textures are produced by inverse UV mapping with learnable normal displacements. Third, cards are clustered by texture features into N_T groups, each assigned a shared appearance codebook of K compact D-dimensional entries; per-strand Gumbel-Softmax logits route to codebook entries, and a globally shared MLP decoder expands the 64-dim latent feature into per-Gaussian spherical harmonic coefficients, achieving over 200× compression while preserving rendering quality.

## Results

CGHair achieves a 230.6× compression ratio (0.71 MB vs. 163.7 MB for the per-strand unique SH baseline analogous to GaussianHair) while maintaining competitive rendering quality: PSNR 32.40 vs. 33.99 (−1.6 dB), SSIM 0.970 vs. 0.982, and LPIPS 0.026 vs. 0.019. The strand generation pipeline is 4× faster than GaussianHaircut and 3× faster than GaussianHair. The method uses N_T=64 card clusters, K=10 codebook entries, and D=64 latent dimensionality as the default configuration, yielding the best quality–size trade-off in ablations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaussianHair [36], CGHair achieves comparable novel-view rendering quality (PSNR 32.40 vs. 33.99, a ~1.6 dB gap) while reducing the appearance model size by over 200×. GaussianHair preserves fine flyaway hairs near boundaries and offers the most detailed strand-level renderings, but can over-sharpen (e.g., side hair in the curly case) and requires tedious manual setup; CGHair is fully automatic. Against GaussianHaircut [76], CGHair is 4× faster in strand reconstruction and recovers higher-frequency geometric and appearance details, whereas GaussianHaircut produces the smoothest results but loses strand-level detail (especially in curly and long-straight cases) and exhibits forehead artifacts from strand-geometry misalignment. The paper also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility by integrating CGHair on top of GaussianHair's reconstructed strands (Ours-GH), yielding superior hair details without modifying the upstream pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key threads: the parametric hair model PERM (He et al., ICLR 2025), whose pretrained guide/style decoders and PCA-based strand decoder provide the geometric prior for strand generation, and GaussianHaircut (Zakharov et al., ECCV 2024), whose cylindrical-Gaussian strand representation and 3DGS rendering pipeline form the rendering backbone. The conceptual departure is the introduction of a hierarchical hair-card clustering structure—borrowed from traditional graphics asset pipelines—combined with vector-quantization-style shared appearance codebooks and a globally shared MLP decoder, creating a compact 3DGS hair representation that no prior Gaussian-based hair method has attempted. This bridges the gap between the millions-of-primitives paradigm of GaussianHair/GaussianHaircut and the compactness of classical hair cards, while remaining fully differentiable and image-driven.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 200× appearance compression with comparable rendering quality addresses a critical practical bottleneck for real-time hair rendering and storage in virtual production, avatars, and AR/VR. However, the evaluation is limited to three real hairstyles and synthetic HairSalon samples, leaving open whether the card-clustering structure generalizes to highly diverse or dynamic hairstyles, and whether the fixed geometry (13.64 MB, ~7.7% of baseline) becomes a bottleneck at scale. The plug-and-play integration demonstrated with GaussianHair suggests broader applicability, but the method's reliance on a fitted FLAME head and PERM prior constrains it to human hair. Future work could explore extending the codebook framework to dynamic hair, multi-person scenes, or combining it with relighting capabilities.
