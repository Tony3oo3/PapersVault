## Problem

Memory-based test-time adaptation methods like TDA perform inference via a two-step modality mapping chain (vision-text then vision-vision), but the asymmetry between these mappings causes non-transitivity: vision-text alignment is semantic-guided while vision-vision alignment is appearance-dominated. This incoherence leads to misclassification when videos from different action categories share similar backgrounds or visually irrelevant factors, a problem particularly acute in video action recognition due to temporal redundancy and scene complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled test video (32 frames sampled at 224×224 spatial resolution) and text prompts constructed from action class names via templates (e.g., 'an action of [class y]'). It produces top-1 and top-5 action class predictions. The visual cache is progressively built during inference from previously seen test samples and their pseudo-labels, with the cache storing condensed video tubes (k=10 semantic patches expanded into spatial-temporal regions) rather than global video features. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates with batch size 1 on a single GPU.

## Method

CONDA condenses video features to make vision-vision alignment semantically guided, rectifying the non-transitive mapping chain. The first module, Probability-based Semantic Patch Selection (PSPS), computes a per-patch semantic activation map by propagating gradients and MHSA attention maps through the visual encoder layers (Eq. 4), then applies softmax to obtain activation probabilities and uses inverse transform sampling to select k diverse semantic patches, avoiding the concentration bias of top-k selection. The second module, Adaptive Tube Construction (ATC), expands each selected patch spatially via random uniform cropping (scale sampled from U(0.3, 0.7)) and temporally by using the mean-pooled region feature as a query to retrieve the most similar patch in each frame (cosine similarity threshold τ=0.5), forming spatial-temporal tubes that capture both structure and motion. These condensed tubes replace global features in the TDA cache, so that vision-vision matching operates on semantically relevant regions rather than appearance-dominated global descriptors. The approach is training-free, plug-and-play, and compatible with arbitrary VLM backbones (CLIP, ViCLIP, ViFi-CLIP, OST, EgoVLP) at both ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 scales.

## Results

On the primary zero-shot action recognition benchmarks with ViCLIP-ViT-B/16, CONDA achieves 48.4% top-1 on HMDB-51, 77.6% on UCF-101, and 72.1% top-1 / 91.8% top-5 on Kinetics-600, outperforming the training-free TDA baseline by 1.0%, 0.9%, and 1.2% respectively. On long-term video recognition, CONDA reaches 67.4% on COIN and 75.2% on ActivityNet-200, surpassing TDA by 1.8% and 2.0%. On egocentric benchmarks, CONDA achieves 12.3% on EK-100 and 22.7% on EGTEA, improving over TDA by 1.3% and 3.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONDA consistently outperforms all training-free TTA baselines: over TDA by an average of 1.3% on K600 (72.1% vs. 70.9%) and 1.0% on HMDB-51 (48.4% vs. 47.4%), and over DPE by 1.3% on K600 and 0.8% on HMDB-51, despite DPE requiring unsupervised fine-tuning during inference. Against the training-required TPT, CONDA gains 1.3% on K600 and 1.3% on HMDB-51 while eliminating all training overhead. On long-term video (Table 2), CONDA beats TDA by 1.8% on COIN (67.4% vs. 65.6%) and 2.0% on ActivityNet-200 (75.2% vs. 73.2%), and surpasses DPE by 0.9% and 1.6% respectively. On egocentric video (Table 3), CONDA achieves 12.3% on EK-100 (vs. 11.0% TDA, 11.3% DPE) and 22.7% on EGTEA (vs. 19.2% TDA, 19.7% DPE), a 3.5% gain over TDA on EGTEA. CONDA also plugs into stronger backbones: on top of OST it improves UCF-101 from 78.2% to 81.5% (+3.3%) and K600 from 74.6% to 75.1%, and on ViFi-CLIP it lifts HMDB-51 from 52.3% to 53.6%. Uni-modal zero-shot methods (E2E, ER-ZSAR, JigsawNet) are outperformed by large margins (e.g., 48.4% vs. 38.7% on HMDB-51), confirming the advantage of VLM-based adaptation over hand-crafted video representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TDA (Karmanov et al., CVPR 2024), the pioneering training-free dynamic adapter that performs inference via a two-step vision-text → vision-vision mapping chain. The key conceptual departure is diagnosing the 'non-transitivity' of this chain: the vision-text step is semantic-guided while the vision-vision step is appearance-dominated, causing incoherence when visually similar but semantically distinct videos are cached together. CONDA addresses this by condensing cached features to semantic patches (via gradient-based activation inspired by Grad-CAM and Transformer interpretability) before vision-vision matching, and it draws on probabilistic sampling (inverse transform) rather than hard top-k selection to ensure patch diversity. The method is positioned as a plug-and-drop module compatible with arbitrary VLMs, sitting alongside but distinct from prompt-tuning TTA methods (TPT, DTS-TPT) and other memory-based adapters (DPE, Point-Cache, BoostAdapter).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-transitivity diagnosis provides a clear, actionable explanation for why naive cache-based VLM adaptation fails on visually confusable action pairs, and the training-free, plug-and-play design makes it immediately deployable across VLMs without retraining. The consistent 1–3% gains over TDA across seven benchmarks and four VLM backbones (CLIP, ViCLIP, ViFi-CLIP, OST, EgoVLP) demonstrate broad applicability, particularly in long-term and egocentric settings where background confusability is most acute. Open questions include whether the gradient-based semantic activation scales efficiently to very long videos or high-resolution inputs, whether the non-transitivity framing generalizes to other VLM adaptation tasks beyond action recognition (e.g., video retrieval, dense prediction), and whether the modest per-dataset gains compound meaningfully in safety-critical or long-tail deployment scenarios.
