## Problem

The quadratic complexity of self-attention in Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) becomes the dominant computational bottleneck for long videos, with attention accounting for up to 95% of total FLOPs at one-million-token sequences. Existing sparse attention methods rely on fixed or unstructured dynamic patterns that are either inflexible or hardware-inefficient, while linear attention suffers from low-rank expressiveness limits. The paper identifies that video DiT attention maps are high-rank and sparse with strong block-diagonal spatiotemporal structure, making them naturally representable by Monarch matrices, but directly applying MonarchAttention to video DiTs introduces block-structure mismatch, first-frame attention-sink artifacts, and prohibitive memory overhead for long sequences.

## Input / Output

The method operates within existing Video DiT backbones (Wan2.1-1.3B, Wan2.1-14B, Wan2.2-5B) on video latent tokens of shape N=THW (e.g., 61×448×832 ≈ 22.8K tokens or 93×704×1280 ≈ 84K tokens), where T is the number of latent frames and H×W is the spatial token grid per frame. Text prompts condition the diffusion process as in standard text-to-video generation, and the output is a decoded video clip. The Monarch block size b is set to HW (one frame's spatial tokens) and m to T, so the factorization explicitly separates spatial and temporal dependencies; the method is applied across all 50 denoising timesteps without any full-attention warmup steps.

## Method

VMonarch factorizes the N×N attention matrix into two Monarch factors L∈R^{m²×b} and R∈R^{b²×m} via t=2 iterations of alternating minimization, with m=T and b=HW so that L captures inter-frame (temporal) correlations and R captures intra-frame (spatial) correlations, reducing complexity from O(N²d) to O(tN(T+HW)d) ≈ O(tTd). To fix the attention-sink phenomenon that causes excessive softmax temperature on the first frame during Monarch optimization, a first-frame recomputation strategy replaces the Monarch output for frame 0 with a full-attention computation at modest O(bNd) cost. A novel online-entropy algorithm, analogous to FlashAttention's online softmax, is fused into a custom GPU kernel to compute the R-factor update and its entropy term c_L in a single pass over K and V blocks, reducing HBM traffic and achieving 8× speedup over a naive implementation. After minimal fine-tuning (1500 steps at 1.3B scale, 800 steps at 14B/5B scale with lr=1e-6), VMonarch matches or exceeds full-attention VBench quality while reducing attention FLOPs by 17.5× and achieving >5× kernel speedup at 62K tokens.

## Results

After minimal fine-tuning (1500 steps for 1.3B, 800 steps for 14B/5B), VMonarch achieves VBench scores comparable to or exceeding full attention across all model scales. On Wan2.1-1.3B at 61×448×832, VMonarch reaches 65.58% AQ, 95.84% BC, 64.49% IQ, and 93.23% SC versus 66.07%/95.75%/64.57%/94.15% for full attention, while reducing attention TFLOPs by 53% (75.4 vs 159.7) and inference time by 25% (47.7s vs 63.4s). At the larger Wan2.1-14B scale (93×704×1280), VMonarch achieves 95.68% SC (surpassing full attention's 95.23%) and 97.32% BC while cutting inference time from 2222s to 969s (2.3× speedup). Kernel-level benchmarks show >5× speedup over FlashAttention-2 at 62k tokens, with an estimated 17.5× attention FLOPs reduction for long videos.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Full Attention (FA2), VMonarch trades a small Dynamic Degree drop (62.50% vs 68.06% at 1.3B) for a 53% TFLOPs reduction and 25% wall-clock speedup, while matching or exceeding full attention on BC, IQ, and SC at the 14B and 5B scales. Against VSA (90% sparsity, top-k), VMonarch consistently outperforms on Aesthetic Quality, Background Consistency, Imaging Quality, and Subject Consistency across all model sizes, and is faster in end-to-end inference (47.7s vs 49.9s at 1.3B; 969.2s vs 970.9s at 14B) despite having slightly lower sparsity (87.5–92% vs 90%). Against VMoBA (90% sparsity, top-p), VMonarch achieves comparable or better quality on most metrics while being substantially faster (47.7s vs 71.7s at 1.3B, a 1.5× speedup), owing to its structured Monarch matrix computation versus VMoBA's unstructured dynamic block selection. At the kernel level, VMonarch achieves >2× speedup over FA2 at 34k tokens and >5× at 62k tokens, surpassing both VSA and VMoBA at 90% sparsity. The only metric where VMonarch trails is Dynamic Degree in the training-free setting (54.17% vs 69.44% for FA2), which is fully recovered after 1500 steps of fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMonarch extends MonarchAttention (Yaras et al., 2025), which applies structured Monarch matrices with alternating minimization to approximate attention in general Transformers, to the specific domain of Video Diffusion Transformers. It departs from prior sparse attention methods for video (VSA, VMoBA) that rely on unstructured dynamic block patterns with irregular memory access, instead exploiting the inherent spatiotemporal locality of video tokens through a structured factorization where Monarch factors L and R capture temporal and spatial dependencies respectively. The online-entropy GPU kernel is directly inspired by FlashAttention-2's online softmax, adapted to jointly compute attention output and entropy in a single pass for efficient Monarch factor updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VMonarch provides a practical path to scaling video DiTs to longer sequences by reducing attention FLOPs by 17.5× with over 5× kernel speedup while maintaining generation quality comparable to full attention after only 1500 steps of fine-tuning. The structured approach avoids the hardware inefficiency of unstructured sparse patterns, making it more amenable to GPU optimization. Open questions include whether the spatiotemporal Monarch structure generalizes to other modalities with similar locality (e.g., 3D point clouds, multi-view images), whether the 2-iteration default is optimal across all video lengths, and whether the first-frame recomputation overhead becomes non-negligible at extreme temporal resolutions. The method's reliance on fine-tuning (even if minimal) limits its applicability to frozen or proprietary models.
