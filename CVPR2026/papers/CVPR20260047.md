## Problem

When super-resolution is applied independently to each low-resolution training view for 3D Gaussian Splatting, the resulting view-inconsistent hallucinated textures introduce conflicting gradients that degrade 3D reconstruction quality. Prior SR-for-3DGS methods (SRGS, S2Gaussian, SuperGaussian) apply SR uniformly across every pixel, injecting generative detail even in regions already well-constrained by closer LR views, which unnecessarily amplifies multi-view inconsistencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes low-resolution multi-view RGB images (e.g., 240×135 for Tanks & Temples, ~500×375 for Mip-NeRF 360) along with camera poses from COLMAP/SfM, and produces a high-resolution 3DGS model renderable at 4× (or 8×) the training resolution. A frozen single-image SR model (StableSR or SwinIR) generates the SR supervision, and the method additionally requires an initial LR 3DGS fit to extract scene geometry for computing per-Gaussian sampling statistics. The per-view weight maps are rendered from the LR model's Gaussian attributes and modulate the SR loss pixel-wise during HR training.

## Method

SplatSuRe's core mechanism is a geometry-aware selective SR strategy: it first trains a standard LR 3DGS model, then computes a per-Gaussian fidelity score from the ratio of each Gaussian's maximum to minimum screen-space radius across all training views (ρ = r_max/r_min), where a high ratio indicates the Gaussian is well-sampled at high frequency in at least one view. These scores are rendered into per-view spatial weight maps that are high in undersampled regions (low ρ, or where the current view provides the closest observation) and low where other views already supply reliable high-frequency supervision. During HR 3DGS training, the total loss combines a standard LR loss (downsampled HR render vs. LR ground truth) with a spatially-weighted SR loss (HR render vs. frozen SISR output, scaled by the weight map), so generative detail is injected only where needed. No additional neural components are introduced; the method modifies only the supervision losses on top of the standard 3DGS rendering and densification pipeline.

## Results

On Tanks & Temples at 4× super-resolution, SplatSuRe achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics: PSNR 23.81 dB, SSIM 0.784, LPIPS 0.272, FID 37.72, and DreamSim 0.0413, outperforming the strongest baseline SRGS (PSNR 23.32, FID 49.11). On Deep Blending, SplatSuRe leads all methods with PSNR 29.01 dB, SSIM 0.872, and CMMD 0.496. On Mip-NeRF 360, SplatSuRe (PSNR 26.34, SSIM 0.740) outperforms SRGS but is slightly behind Mip-Splatting (PSNR 26.48, SSIM 0.759), which benefits from the dataset's dense, smooth camera trajectories that minimize undersampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatSuRe outperforms all three baselines on Tanks & Temples across every metric except NIQE, where the aliased low-resolution 3DGS renderings coincidentally score better (3.412 vs. 3.928) due to no-reference metrics favoring noisy statistics. Against SRGS (the closest SR-based competitor), SplatSuRe gains +0.49 dB PSNR, reduces FID from 49.11 to 37.72, and lowers DreamSim from 0.0535 to 0.0413, confirming that selective SR injection reduces multi-view inconsistencies. Compared to Mip-Splatting, SplatSuRe improves PSNR by 0.71 dB and FID by 14.74 points, showing that super-resolution supervision adds detail that anti-aliasing filters alone cannot recover. On Deep Blending, SplatSuRe leads all methods on every metric (PSNR 29.01 vs. 28.43 for Mip-Splatting). On Mip-NeRF 360, Mip-Splatting edges out SplatSuRe on most metrics (PSNR 26.48 vs. 26.34, LPIPS 0.292 vs. 0.323), which the authors attribute to the dataset's dense circular camera paths providing sufficient multi-view coverage that makes uniform anti-aliasing competitive with selective SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatSuRe builds directly on the 3DGS pipeline, modifying only the supervision losses while retaining the standard rendering and densification machinery. It departs from prior SR-for-3DGS methods (SRGS, S2Gaussian, SuperGaussian) that apply super-resolution uniformly across all pixels, instead introducing a geometry-aware Gaussian fidelity score that determines where SR is actually needed based on multi-view sampling disparity. The approach is conceptually distinct from Mip-Splatting, which addresses aliasing through scale-adaptive filtering but does not inject new high-frequency content, and from diffusion-based 3D SR methods that rely on large pretrained generative models rather than explicit camera-geometry reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective SR strategy offers a lightweight, plug-and-play improvement to 3DGS training that requires no additional neural components or architectural changes, making it broadly applicable to existing 3DGS pipelines. The finding that Mip-Splatting outperforms SR-based methods on well-sampled scenes (Mip-NeRF 360) is a useful reminder that generative detail is not always beneficial and that anti-aliasing alone may suffice in dense multi-view settings. Open questions include extending the framework to multi-scale upsampling, handling high-contrast boundaries where conservative SR suppression may miss useful detail, and whether the approach generalizes to sparse-view or dynamic-scene settings where the Gaussian fidelity score may be less reliable.
