## Problem

Existing VLM-based image vectorization methods are trained and evaluated on clean, high-resolution SVG renderings, causing them to generalize poorly to real-world inputs such as text-to-image model outputs, images rendered with different backends, or low-resolution graphics. Additionally, prior approaches use raw SVG path commands as the generative representation, which is neither compact nor canonical, amplifying coordinate errors in long autoregressive sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raster image (potentially from text-to-image models) as input, which is first decolorized into a black-and-white stroke-only outline via a classical vectorizer and rendered at 448×448 for the ViT encoder. The model autoregressively outputs a sequence of rounded-polygon tokens—triples of (x, y, d) coordinates quantized to two decimal places in a 128×128 viewBox—where d encodes per-vertex corner roundness. Colors and z-order are recovered in a post-processing step by sampling the original input image along the predicted paths.

## Method

VectorArk fine-tunes InternVL2-1B end-to-end to autoregressively generate a novel rounded-polygon representation: each SVG path is decomposed into line and arc primitives via Cornocopia fitting, then converted to polygon vertices with a distance-based roundness parameter d_i per vertex (arc radius computed as r_i = d_i·tan(α_i/2)), reducing degrees of freedom compared to cubic Béziers. The input is a decolorized outline raster rather than a colored image, acting as a normalization step that eliminates appearance-related train-test mismatch. During training, a degradation model renders ground-truth SVGs at randomly reduced resolutions (224–336), applies Gaussian blur, and re-vectorizes with a classical tool to simulate real-world imperfections, teaching the model to produce clean geometry from noisy inputs. At inference, test-time scaling generates N stochastic candidates and selects the best via DINO-ViT-B/16 cosine similarity between rendered output and input, improving reliability without architectural changes.

## Results

On the SArena benchmark, VectorArk achieves SSIM of 0.937 (Easy), 0.895 (Medium), and 0.857 (Hard), consistently outperforming the strongest baseline StarVector (0.876, 0.75, 0.626) across all difficulty tiers. On SVGenius, the gains are similarly pronounced: SSIM of 0.944, 0.868, and 0.83 versus StarVector's 0.89, 0.71, and 0.672. DINO similarity reaches 0.992/0.981/0.975 on SArena and 0.995/0.977/0.958 on SVGenius, while LPIPS drops to as low as 0.031 (SArena Easy) and 0.028 (SVGenius Easy). The advantage is most dramatic on Hard samples, where VectorArk's SSIM exceeds the next-best method by 0.23 (SArena) and 0.16 (SVGenius).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VectorArk outperforms all four baselines—GPT-4o, Gemini, OmniSVG, and StarVector—on every metric (SSIM, LPIPS, MSE, DINO) across both SArena and SVGenius at all three difficulty levels. StarVector is the strongest open-source baseline, but VectorArk still leads by a substantial margin: on SArena Hard, SSIM is 0.857 vs. 0.626 (+0.231) and LPIPS is 0.093 vs. 0.252 (−0.159). OmniSVG, which uses a raw SVG-command tokenization, trails VectorArk by 0.339 SSIM on SArena Hard (0.518 vs. 0.857) and 0.192 on SVGenius Hard (0.638 vs. 0.83). The proprietary models GPT-4o and Gemini perform worst overall, with SSIM below 0.57 on Hard splits, confirming that general-purpose VLMs without specialized vectorization training struggle with geometric fidelity. Notably, even in the single-sample setting without test-time scaling (N=1) on SVGenius, VectorArk still surpasses the strongest baseline, indicating the gains are not solely attributable to the ranking strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-based SVG generation line (StarVector, OmniSVG, LLM4SVG) by replacing the SVG command vocabulary with a canonical rounded-polygon representation built on the Cornocopia line-arc fitting algorithm, reducing per-curve parameters from four (cubic Bézier) to three and enforcing piecewise-constant curvature by construction. It departs from prior work in two further ways: conditioning on decolorized outline rasters rather than full-color inputs to eliminate appearance mismatch, and introducing a vectorization-based degradation model during training to simulate the imperfect inputs (text-to-image outputs, low-resolution rasters) that prior methods were never exposed to. The test-time scaling strategy with DINO-based candidate selection follows the broader LLM test-time compute literature but is applied here to vectorization for the first time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that prior VLM vectorizers are brittle to even minor rasterization pipeline changes and fail on text-to-image model outputs, a gap that matters as generated imagery becomes a primary input for design workflows. The rounded-polygon representation's 28–47% token reduction while maintaining near-lossless quality is a practical efficiency win for autoregressive vector generation. However, the method targets medium-complexity graphics; highly detailed illustrations, text, and gradient effects are simplified, and dense local path structure remains challenging. Open questions include whether the representation can be extended to richer primitives under controlled constraints and whether a learned appearance module can replace the current post-hoc color extraction without sacrificing geometric fidelity.
