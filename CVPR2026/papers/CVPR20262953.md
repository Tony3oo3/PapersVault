## Problem

Vehicle trajectory prediction models rely on real-time online priors (HD maps or agent interactions) that are statically embedded in model parameters, causing sharp performance degradation in out-of-distribution and cross-domain scenarios. Both map-based and map-free paradigms are fundamentally limited by this dependence on uncertain online perception, making robust zero-shot generalization infeasible without exhaustive real-world training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262953/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a historical agent trajectory of T_hist 2D position steps and a conditionally available online HD map as input, and outputs K future trajectory modes each parameterized as a bivariate Laplace distribution (mean and scale per timestep) with an associated probability. The framework operates in two configurations: map-based (online HD map + RAG) and map-free (RAG database alone supplies geometric knowledge). The offline knowledge base is built from heterogeneous datasets standardized to a unified protocol, with the FAISS index constructed strictly from training splits to prevent leakage.

## Method

RAG-TP reframes trajectory prediction as retrieval from a structured offline knowledge base, decoupling inference from uncertain online priors. The knowledge base is constructed via a self-supervised multi-branch autoencoder (separate encoders for history, scene, and future modalities), followed by K-Means clustering on concatenated history-scene embeddings to extract representative driving patterns, yielding key-value pairs (key = history embedding, value = scene+future embeddings) indexed with FAISS. At inference, a frozen query encoder performs initial retrieval, and a learnable reranker selects the top-N most relevant units. A Retrieval-Driven Mixture-of-Experts module then treats these retrieved units as dynamic 'experts,' using cross-attention gating with the query to compute routing weights and a shared non-linear projection for permutation equivariance, producing an augmented context embedding. This context feeds a Proposal-Refinement decoder (adapted from MTR) that generates K multimodal trajectories via a Laplace Mixture Model, trained with winner-takes-all NLL regression and cross-entropy classification losses, deliberately omitting the load-balancing loss to preserve the semantic rank ordering of retrieved priors.

## Results

On the map-based Argoverse 2 benchmark (5s→6s), RAG-TP achieves the best minADE₆ of 0.60, minFDE₆ of 1.14, and MR₆ of 0.13, outperforming or matching all map-based baselines including QCNet (0.67/1.27/0.16), DeMo++ (0.62/1.16/0.13), and MTR (0.82/1.55/0.22). On the map-free Argoverse 1 benchmark (2s→3s), RAG-TP achieves 0.72 minADE₆ and 0.13 MR₆ (both best), though it trails MLB-Traj (1.25) and STAM-P (1.30) on minFDE₆ (1.31). In cross-domain zero-shot settings, a model trained on Argoverse 2 but retrieving from WOMD's knowledge base achieves 1.05 minADE₆ on WOMD, substantially closing the gap versus MTR's 1.58 under the same transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the map-based setting, RAG-TP (0.60 minADE₆) outperforms QCNet by 0.07, DeMo++ by 0.02, Polaris by 0.03, and MTR by 0.22, while tying FutureNet-LOF and DeMo++ on MR₆ (0.13) and slightly beating them on minFDE₆ (1.14 vs. 1.16). In the map-free setting, RAG-TP achieves the best minADE₆ (0.72 vs. 0.77 for MLB-Traj and 0.82 for STAM-P) and best MR₆ (0.13 vs. 0.14–0.17), but concedes minFDE₆ to MLB-Traj (1.25) and STAM-P (1.30) by 0.06 and 0.01 respectively. The plug-and-play experiments confirm the RAG module's transferability: bolting it onto MTR reduces minADE₆ from 0.82 to 0.76 and MR₆ from 0.22 to 0.18, while on Forecast-MAE it improves minADE₆ from 0.80 to 0.73. In cross-domain zero-shot evaluation, RAG-TP trained on Argoverse 2 but retrieving from WOMD's knowledge base reaches 1.05 minADE₆ on WOMD, compared to MTR's 1.58 and AutoBot's 1.52 under the same source-to-target transfer, demonstrating that retrieval-based adaptation substantially narrows the domain gap without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAG-TP adapts the Retrieval-Augmented Generation paradigm from NLP to the non-language, spatio-temporal domain of vehicle trajectory prediction, building on the Proposal-Refinement decoding backbone of MTR [35] and the retrieve-then-rerank strategy from retrospective-memory prediction [42]. It departs from prior retrieval-based trajectory work (memory networks, associative memory) by constructing a structured, clustered knowledge base from heterogeneous datasets and introducing a Retrieval-Driven MoE that treats retrieved knowledge units as dynamic experts rather than static parametric branches. The paper explicitly positions itself against Driving-RAG [4] as a specialized framework for spatio-temporal prediction, and against both map-based (QCNet, Polaris) and map-free (STAM-P, MLB-Traj) methods by decoupling online inference from a scalable offline knowledge base.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a concrete pathway to address the OOD generalization bottleneck that plagues current trajectory prediction models, with the zero-shot cross-domain results (e.g., Argoverse-2-trained model retrieving from WOMD's knowledge base achieving 1.05 minADE6 vs. 1.42 for a WOMD-trained MTR) providing strong evidence that external knowledge retrieval can substitute for retraining. The plug-and-play nature—consistently improving MTR and Forecast-MAE when the RAG module is added—suggests broad practical applicability. Open questions remain around online knowledge updates for continual learning, robustness when the query falls outside the knowledge base's coverage, and whether the computationally intensive offline construction scales to truly massive, multi-city deployments.
