## Problem

Current 3D human motion analysis relies heavily on intrusive multi-camera vision systems, yet no existing dataset unifies the data-centric needs of computer vision (multi-view video, MoCap, semantic annotations) with the individual-centric precision demands of biomechanics (plantar pressure, sEMG, anthropometrics) under a single standardized protocol. This gap prevents rigorous benchmarking of privacy-preserving, wearable-sensor-based motion inference methods.

## Input / Output

For the activity recognition benchmark, the model consumes a 3-second sliding window of plantar pressure (16 sensors/foot at 100 Hz) and optionally MoCap (120 Hz) or sEMG (1259 Hz) signals during training, producing one of 30 discrete action labels per window. For the 3D pose estimation benchmark, input is the full insole signal vector (32 pressure channels + 18 IMU-derived values: acceleration, total force, center of pressure per foot) at 100 Hz, and output is the 3D Cartesian coordinates of 16 lower-body joints (arms excluded) in a pelvis-centered, anthropometrically scaled coordinate frame. All signals are normalized per-subject (pressure to body weight or peak, EMG to reference contraction, joints to pelvis origin with body-dimension scaling) to enable cross-participant generalization.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is the HUMAPS-4D dataset (32 subjects × 30 actions × 10 sessions = 320 instances, 14 hours, 5.76M frames) with synchronized Qualisys MoCap (42 joints, 120 Hz), 3 RGB cameras (720p, 120 Hz), 16-channel sEMG (1259 Hz), Moticon OpenGo insoles (16 pressure sensors + IMU per foot, 100 Hz), and three layers of semantic annotation (clinician narratives, atomic action descriptions, frame-level temporal segmentation). Two baseline benchmarks are established: (1) a CNN-BiLSTM with attention for insole-based action recognition using a joint multimodal loss with equal per-modality weighting under a leave-one-subject-out protocol, and (2) a two-stream transformer (inspired by SolePoser) for 3D pose from foot pressure, augmented with a pose-data encoder during training and a novel 'Inconsistency' metric measuring temporal variance of joint-position errors. The pose model is validated via 4-fold cross-validation (8 held-out subjects per fold) and trained with MPJPE, MPJAE, reconstruction MSE on insole signals, and a cross-modal Feature Loss enforcing shared latent representations between the pressure and pose encoders.

## Results

For insole-based activity recognition under a leave-one-subject-out protocol, the fusion model (insoles + MoCap) achieves 90.33%±4.3 overall accuracy, outperforming insoles alone (82.71%±3.9) and MoCap alone (89.45%±5.9). Postural actions are best recognized (96.89%±3.3 with fusion), while dynamic actions remain the hardest (87.57%±8.9 with fusion, 74.36%±9.4 with insoles alone). For 3D pose estimation from plantar pressure, the baseline model achieves a total MPJPE of 31.1±0.8 cm, an Inconsistency of 7.5±0.1 cm, and an MPJAE of 13.3±1.5° across all action categories, with interaction actions being the most challenging (34.0±0.8 cm MPJPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a dataset paper, HUMAPS-4D does not benchmark against external methods but instead establishes internal modality comparisons as baselines. For activity recognition, the insole-only model lags MoCap by 6.7 percentage points overall (82.71% vs. 89.45%), with the largest gap on dynamic actions (74.36% vs. 92.05%) where plantar pressure is sparse during airborne phases. The multimodal fusion model (insoles + MoCap) achieves the best overall accuracy at 90.33%, surpassing both individual modalities, and shows the strongest gains on locomotion (+5.36 pp over insoles) and postural actions (+6.36 pp over insoles). For 3D pose estimation from foot pressure, the baseline (inspired by SolePoser [26]) yields a total MPJPE of 31.1 cm, indicating that full-body reconstruction from plantar signals alone remains far from ground-truth MoCap accuracy. The authors note that no prior dataset or method provides a direct apples-to-apples comparison under the same LOSO protocol and 30-action protocol, making these baselines the first standardized reference points for the community.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research communities—computer-vision-driven 3D pose estimation and biomechanical motion assessment—by constructing a unified multimodal dataset where no prior resource had combined all modalities under one protocol. The baseline pose-estimation architecture is directly inspired by SolePoser [26] (two-stream transformer with double-cycle consistency), extended with an additional pose-data encoder for training-time supervision. Prior datasets are either vision-centric (SP-Sport, P2P-Insole, MMVP) or biomechanics-centric (MovePort, SIAT-LLMD, Smart-insole), each covering only a subset of the sensors, semantic annotations, and anthropometric metadata that HUMAPS-4D integrates simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUMAPS-4D is the first large-scale resource pairing low-level physiological signals (plantar pressure, sEMG, IMU) with high-level semantic and visual data, enabling privacy-preserving motion analysis that reduces reliance on cameras. The baseline results underscore that 3D pose reconstruction from plantar pressure alone remains highly challenging (~31 cm MPJPE), and that insole-only action recognition (82.7%) still lags behind MoCap (89.5%), highlighting a significant information gap that future methods must close. Open questions include whether language-conditioned generative models can produce clinically meaningful motion assessments from biomechanical signals, and whether the 2026 public challenges will catalyze practical, deployable insole-based pose systems.
