## Problem

Long-form video understanding with MLLMs is bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of self-attention over long RGB frame sequences and the redundancy of repeatedly encoding static backgrounds. Uniform frame sampling forces a tradeoff between missing brief events (sparse sampling) and prohibitive compute (dense sampling), while existing token-compression strategies blur fine-grained motion cues. The paper asks whether operating directly on compressed video representations—where temporal redundancy is already factored out by the codec—can enable scalable, motion-faithful long-video reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video re-encoded with H.264 using scene-adaptive keyframe detection (384×384 spatial, 16 fps, max GOP length 32, 4×4 macroblocks), decomposed into GOP tuples of one I-frame plus block-level motion vectors from P/B-frames, alongside a tokenized text instruction. No pixel-level decoding of inter-frames is performed; only the I-frame is fed through the vision encoder while motion vectors are processed by the RMR module. Output is an autoregressive text sequence generated by the LLM, evaluated as multiple-choice or open-ended video QA answers.

## Method

ReMoRa replaces redundant RGB frames with a codec-native appearance-motion decomposition: I-frames provide spatial patch embeddings via a frozen SigLIP ViT-SO encoder, while P/B-frame motion vectors are converted into dense, fine-grained motion representations by the RMR module, which is pretrained against CoTracker3 optical flow to denoise and densify the inherently coarse, block-based codec vectors. Temporal modeling is handled by the HMSS module, a two-stage bidirectional Mamba (SSM) architecture that mirrors the GOP hierarchy: a local scan fuses motion and appearance tokens within each GOP, and a global selective-scan layer compresses the per-GOP summaries into a sequence L_g/N_p times shorter than the naive flattened sequence, achieving linear-time long-range temporal reasoning. The resulting video features are projected into the Qwen2-7B LLM's embedding space (adapted via LoRA) and concatenated with instruction tokens for autoregressive answer generation, trained on 200K instruction-tuning samples with standard cross-entropy loss.

## Results

ReMoRa achieves the best scores on LongVideoBench (60.8), NExT-QA (84.2), and MLVU (72.1), outperforming the second-best models (BIMBA and LLaVA-Video) by 1.3, 1.0, and 1.3 points respectively. On the overall average across six benchmarks, ReMoRa scores 69.8, surpassing BIMBA (68.9) and LLaVA-Video (68.7) by 0.9 and 1.1 points. On open-ended VideoQA, ReMoRa attains the highest ActivityNet-QA accuracy (60.5) and score (3.7), and a competitive second-best MSVD-QA score (4.0). Inference efficiency matches BIMBA at 10.59 GB peak memory, halving LLaVA-Video's 23.21 GB requirement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor BIMBA (which also uses SSM-based temporal modeling), ReMoRa wins on LongVideoBench (+1.3), NExT-QA (+1.0), MLVU (+1.5), and VideoMME (+1.3), but trails slightly on Perception Test (67.7 vs. 68.1, −0.4). Compared to LLaVA-Video, ReMoRa leads on all five overlapping benchmarks by 1.0–2.6 points and halves peak GPU memory (10.59 vs. 23.21 GB). Against the codec-aware baselines EMA and Video-LaVIT, ReMoRa gains substantial margins (e.g., +13.8 on LongVideoBench over EMA, +14.9 on MLVU), demonstrating that the RMR module's optical-flow pretraining and HMSS's hierarchical SSM aggregation are critical over raw motion-vector usage. On open-ended ActivityNet-QA, ReMoRa's 60.5 accuracy surpasses the next-best EMA (52.1) by 8.4 points, while on MSVD-QA it is marginally behind EMA (4.0 vs. 4.1 score). Overall, ReMoRa sets new state-of-the-art on three of six long-video benchmarks and achieves the highest average score among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMoRa sits at the intersection of two recent threads: codec-aware video MLLMs (Video-LaVIT, EMA) that exploit H.264 GOP structure to avoid redundant RGB decoding, and state-space-model-based video understanding (BIMBA, Mamba) that replaces quadratic self-attention with linear-time scans. The key departure is the RMR module, which uses CoTracker3-generated dense optical flow as a pretraining target to denoise and densify the inherently coarse, block-level motion vectors that prior codec-aware methods fed directly into the language model. The HMSS module then mirrors the GOP hierarchy with a two-stage bidirectional Mamba scan (intra-GOP fusion followed by inter-GOP long-range modeling), adapting BIMBA's selective-scan compression to the compressed-domain token layout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compressed-domain framing offers a practical path to long-video understanding without the quadratic cost of dense RGB processing, and the demonstrated robustness to varied H.264 encoding parameters (Table 7) suggests the approach could generalize across real-world video sources without retraining. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (BIMBA, LLaVA-Video) are modest (1–1.3 points on most benchmarks), and the method's dependence on a specific codec structure (H.264 GOPs with 4×4 macroblocks) raises open questions about portability to newer codecs (AV1, VVC) or to videos with irregular GOP structures. A key limitation is that the RMR module is pretrained on CoTracker3 optical flow, introducing a dependency on an external tracker and a potential domain gap if the codec's motion estimation diverges significantly from true optical flow in complex scenes.
