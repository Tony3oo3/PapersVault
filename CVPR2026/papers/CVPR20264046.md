## Problem

CLIP's global contrastive objective and 77-token context limit prevent it from associating specific visual regions with fine-grained text, leaving a significant gap on dense and long-text retrieval tasks. While prior work has explored region-level or patch-level alignment, none addresses the challenge of simultaneously aligning multiple textual granularities (full captions, sentences, phrases) with their corresponding visual regions while handling the inherent semantic overlap across these hierarchical levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264046/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a long text caption (averaging ~107 words from ShareGPT4V) as input. The caption is decomposed into K hierarchical text queries (1 full caption + K_sent sentences + K_phrase noun/verb phrases extracted via spaCy dependency parsing, up to K=36), each independently encoded by the CLIP text encoder using its EOS token. During training, the output is a BK×BK similarity matrix between K text-conditioned visual embeddings and K text embeddings per image; at inference, the model reduces to standard CLIP (CLS token + text EOS), preserving the original caching efficiency without requiring text-conditioned pooling.

## Method

β-CLIP introduces a shallow modified Transformer block for cross-attention pooling, where each text query dynamically attends over ViT patch tokens (with Q-K mixing skipped in the final attention layer to preserve locality) to produce query-specific visual embeddings. The key training objective is the β-Contextualized Contrastive Alignment Loss (β-CAL), which treats all intra-image feature pairs as positives with tunable strength controlled by β∈[0,1]: at β=0 only exact self-matches are full positives, while at β=1 all intra-image pairs compete uniformly. This is instantiated in two forms—a soft Cross-Entropy variant that row-normalizes target weights into probability distributions, and a hard Binary Cross-Entropy variant that applies β as a gradient weight on off-diagonal intra-image positives while keeping all intra-image pairs as binary targets. The total loss combines β-CAL with standard CLIP global loss on CLS-caption pairs, and the entire framework is trained by fine-tuning CLIP ViT-B/16 on WIT-400M + ShareGPT4V-1M without hard negatives.

## Results

On Urban-1k long-text retrieval, β-CLIP (BCE, K=36, β=0.5) achieves 91.8% T2I and 92.3% I2T at R@1, setting a new state-of-the-art among methods trained without hard negatives. On fine-grained FG-OVD (Hard split), the CE variant reaches 30.9% R@1, outperforming FG-CLIP trained without hard negatives (24.5%) and recovering 55% of the gap to FG-CLIP with 10M hard negatives (46.1%). On DCI, the BCE variant achieves 65.1% T2I R@1, surpassing Long-CLIP by +7.7 points. On coarse-grained MSCOCO and Flickr30k, the BCE variant maintains absolute gains over the CLIP baseline (+6.7/+1.5 and +7.7/+1.5 R@1 respectively), avoiding the short-text degradation typical of long-caption fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On fine-grained FG-OVD (Hard), β-CLIP CE (30.9%) outperforms FG-CLIP without hard negatives (24.5%), FLAIR (13.3%), Smart-CLIP (18.9%), and Long-CLIP (9.2%), but trails FG-CLIP with 10M hard negatives (46.1%) by 15.2 points. On Urban-1k long-text retrieval, β-CLIP BCE (91.8% T2I) surpasses Smart-CLIP (87.4%), TULIP (86.6%), FLAIR (87.7%), and Long-CLIP (79.5%), establishing a clear SOTA margin. On DCI, β-CLIP BCE (65.1% T2I) edges out Smart-CLIP (64.5%) and Long-CLIP (57.4%) but is slightly below FLAIR (66.2%). On coarse-grained MSCOCO/Flickr30k, β-CLIP BCE preserves CLIP-level performance with small gains (+6.7/+1.5 R@1 on MSCOCO), while the CE variant degrades below CLIP (34.0 vs 33.1 T2I), confirming that the BCE formulation better preserves global alignment when fine-tuning on long captions. Among methods trained on the same WIT-400M + ShareGPT4V-1M data, β-CLIP consistently outperforms Long-CLIP, TULIP, FineLIP, and Smart-CLIP on at least one of the fine-grained or long-text benchmarks, demonstrating that the multi-granular text-conditioned objective—not merely the training data—drives the improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

β-CLIP extends the text-conditioned cross-attention pooling introduced by FLAIR to a hierarchy of textual queries (captions, sentences, phrases) and departs from FLAIR by using this conditioning only during training, reducing to standard CLIP at inference. The multi-positive contrastive framing draws on DreamLIP's decomposition of long captions into sub-captions, while the β-CAL loss is a novel parameterized objective that interpolates between strict self-matching and relaxed intra-image contextualization under both CE and BCE formulations. The paper positions itself against region-supervised methods (FG-CLIP, FineCLIP) by showing that text-conditioned patch pooling without explicit bounding boxes can recover a substantial fraction of their fine-grained gains, and against long-context extensions (Long-CLIP, TULIP) by addressing the semantic-overlap challenge that arises when multiple granularities share the same image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The β-CAL loss provides a principled, tunable mechanism for handling the semantic overlap inherent in hierarchical text-to-image alignment, and the finding that CE and BCE variants specialize to different retrieval granularities (CE for fine-grained, BCE for long-text and coarse-grained) offers practitioners a practical recipe for choosing objectives. The result that β-CLIP recovers ~55% of the CLIP-to-FG-CLIP gap on FG-OVD without hard negatives or region-level supervision suggests that text-conditioned pooling is a viable alternative to expensive region mining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer or more structured texts (e.g., multi-paragraph descriptions), whether the β trade-off generalizes across architectures beyond ViT-B/16, and whether the BCE variant's weaker fine-grained separation can be addressed without sacrificing its long-text advantages.
