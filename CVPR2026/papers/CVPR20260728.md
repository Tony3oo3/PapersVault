## Problem

Existing uncertainty estimation methods for Gaussian Splatting either modify the training pipeline with stochastic sampling (increasing cost and degrading quality) or estimate per-primitive parameter uncertainty via Fisher information, which does not directly predict rendering or depth error. There is no post-hoc, interpretable framework that captures how well each Gaussian primitive is actually covered by training views and how much it contributes to reconstruction error, enabling reliable predictive uncertainty for novel views without altering the GS model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully trained GS model (Gaussian primitives with position, covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color) together with the posed training views used during GS optimization, and a novel camera pose at which uncertainty is desired. It produces dense pixel-wise uncertainty maps for both RGB rendering error and depth error, computed by rendering 13 handcrafted primitive-level feature maps and passing them through a gradient-boosting regressor trained on a single holdout view. For active view selection, the direction-dependent coverage feature map alone (no regression) directly scores candidate viewpoints to select the next training view.

## Method

PRIMU constructs three types of Gaussian primitive representations in a post-hoc step: a field-of-view count recording how many training views observe each primitive, a coverage representation aggregating each primitive's volume-rendering contribution factors (α_k·T_k) across training views, and a rendering-error representation weighting those contributions by per-pixel L1 reconstruction error. Direction-dependent variants encode viewing-angle sensitivity using von Mises-Fisher distributions around each primitive's observed training-view directions, represented via spherical harmonics for efficient rendering. To obtain pixel-wise uncertainty at a novel pose, the primitive representations replace the color term in the standard front-to-back compositing equation, yielding 13 uncertainty feature maps that feed a lightweight gradient-boosting regressor trained on one holdout view to predict per-pixel error. For active view selection, the direction-dependent coverage map with max aggregation and dropped opacity is rendered for all candidate views, and the view with the lowest mean coverage value is selected, requiring no regression model or additional holdout data.

## Results

For rendering error UE, PRIMU* achieves AUSE of 0.286 (LF), 0.378 (MipNeRF360), and 0.281 (LLFF) with Pearson correlations of 0.351, 0.236, and 0.368 respectively, substantially outperforming all baselines. For depth UE on LF, PRIMU* reaches AUSE 0.118 and Pearson 0.728, nearly doubling the correlation of the next-best method (BayesRays at 0.243). In active view selection on MipNeRF360, PRIMU-AVS achieves PSNR 20.721, SSIM 0.625, and LPIPS 0.34, surpassing FisherRF (20.392/0.586/0.363) and manifold (19.620/0.592/0.376). In the object-centric scene separation study, PRIMU* achieves AUSE below 0.1 and Pearson above 0.8 with only one hold-out view for training the regressor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIMU* decisively outperforms FisherRF [11] across all three rendering UE datasets, reducing AUSE by 40–72% (e.g., 0.286 vs. 0.753 on LF) and flipping Pearson from negative (−0.13) to positive (0.351), indicating FisherRF's uncertainty maps are anti-correlated with true error. Against the stochastic GS methods manifold [19] and var3DGS [17], PRIMU* achieves lower AUSE and higher Pearson on every dataset (e.g., 0.286/0.351 vs. 0.435/0.099 for manifold on LF), while also preserving rendering quality since it is purely post-hoc. For depth UE on LF, PRIMU* (AUSE 0.118, Pearson 0.728) surpasses the NeRF-based BayesRays [5] (AUSE 0.173, Pearson 0.243) by a wide margin in correlation, though BayesRays benefits from a lower AUSE partly due to its inherently lower rendering quality. In active view selection, PRIMU-AVS outperforms both FisherRF and manifold in final PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on MipNeRF360, demonstrating that the coverage-based feature map alone (without a trained regressor) is sufficient for effective view selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIMU builds on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023) but departs fundamentally from prior GS uncertainty estimation methods (FisherRF, var3DGS, manifold) that quantify parameter uncertainty per primitive and render it to pixel level. Instead of learning stochastic distributions over Gaussian parameters or computing Fisher information, PRIMU constructs handcrafted primitive-level representations of training-view coverage and projected rendering error, optionally made direction-dependent via von Mises-Fisher distributions encoded in spherical harmonics. A lightweight gradient-boosting regressor then maps rendered feature maps to pixel-wise error predictions, making the entire framework post-hoc and decoupled from GS training. This positions PRIMU as a predictive-uncertainty approach rather than a parameter-uncertainty one, aligning more directly with downstream tasks like error estimation and active view selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that reliable, interpretable UE for GS can be obtained without modifying the training pipeline or incurring the computational overhead of stochastic sampling, which is critical for safety-critical deployments in robotics and medical imaging. The finding that object-centric UE dramatically outperforms whole-scene UE (AUSE < 0.1, Pearson > 0.8 with a single holdout view) suggests that foreground/background separation should become standard practice in GS UE pipelines. Open questions include whether the gradient-boosting meta-regressor generalizes to dynamic or multi-object scenes, how the method scales to very large scenes with millions of primitives, and whether the direction-dependent representations offer meaningful gains beyond the small consistent improvement observed for rendering UE.
