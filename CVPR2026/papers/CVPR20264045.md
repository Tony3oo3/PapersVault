## Problem

Existing lip-sync systems rely on GANs (unstable training, visual artifacts) or iterative diffusion models (multi-step denoising, high latency), making faster-than-real-time deployment difficult. The paper asks whether a highly conditioned task like lip-sync—where the output is largely determined by a reference identity, a target frame, and precise lip-pose cues—can be solved with a single deterministic reconstruction step, eliminating adversarial objectives and diffusion schedules entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264045/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a reference image of the target identity, a target video frame to be edited, and driving audio as input. At inference, no explicit mouth mask is required; the model localizes edits internally. The audio is converted to wav2vec 2.0 features plus an emotion embedding, and the visual inputs are encoded by a frozen SDXL VAE into latents. The output is a single lip-synced frame in which only the mouth region is modified while identity, head pose, background, and all other facial details are preserved from the target frame.

## Method

FlashLips is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 is a one-step latent-space editor (U-Net 250M or ViT 300M) that receives the channel-wise concatenation of the masked target latent, a projected reference latent, and a spatially replicated 12D lips-pose vector, then predicts a latent residual toward the ground-truth edit using only reconstruction losses (latent L1, pixel L1 on lower-face and lips regions, VGG-19 perceptual, and VGGFace2 features)—no GAN or diffusion. A mask-free self-refinement step fine-tunes the editor on symmetric source↔changed pseudo-pairs it generates itself, teaching it to localize edits to the lips without external segmentation. Stage 2 is a 150M-parameter transformer conditioned on wav2vec 2.0 features, a pre-trained audio emotion encoder, and K=4 reference lip latents, trained with a conditional flow-matching objective to predict the 12D lips-pose vector (8D from a frozen expression encoder + 4D mouth-crop residual, distilled into a single ResNet-50 at inference). The key design principle is disentangling the control space so the lips vector carries only pose configuration, while appearance (teeth, lip color, skin tone) is sourced from the reference and target latents in Stage 1.

## Results

FlashLips achieves the best FID (4.43) and FVD (12.31) in reconstruction, and best FID (5.89) and FVD (29.40) in cross-audio, outperforming all six baselines on both protocols. It also attains the highest LipScore (0.71 recon / 0.38 cross-audio) and top VBench total (0.687), confirming superior lip-audio alignment and perceptual quality. The U-Net variant runs at 109.4 FPS on a single H100 GPU—up to 30.4× faster than the next-best method (KeySync at 3.6 FPS)—while the Transformer variant reaches 66.8 FPS with marginally better visual metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashLips dominates all six baselines on FID and FVD in both reconstruction and cross-audio settings, with the Transformer variant achieving FID 4.43 / FVD 12.31 (recon) and 5.89 / 29.40 (cross-audio), versus the next-best KeySync at 5.48 / 24.80 and 6.81 / 37.55 respectively. On LipScore, FlashLips leads by a wide margin (0.71 vs. 0.56 for KeySync in reconstruction; 0.38 vs. 0.36 for KeySync in cross-audio), confirming stronger audio-visual alignment. LatentSync is the closest competitor on pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 33.61 vs. 32.88, SSIM 0.97 vs. 0.94, LPIPS 0.015 vs. 0.021) and retains a slight edge in cross-audio identity preservation (ID 0.84 vs. 0.81), but FlashLips is 19.2× faster (109 vs. 5.7 FPS). KeySync marginally outperforms FlashLips on HyperIQA (74.18 vs. 74.06 recon; 74.13 vs. 73.84 cross-audio), a no-reference perceptual quality proxy, but trails substantially on all other metrics and is 30.4× slower. DiffDub and TalkLip are clearly outperformed across the board, with FlashLips achieving roughly 3–4× lower FID and 5–6× lower FVD while running 59× and 2× faster respectively. IP-LAP and Diff2Lip are intermediate: FlashLips improves FVD by ~3× over both and LipScore by 0.21–0.31 points, while being 5.5–25.8× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashLips departs from the dominant GAN-based (Wav2Lip, StyleSync) and diffusion-based (DiffDub, Diff2Lip, LatentSync, KeySync) lip-sync paradigms by replacing adversarial or iterative generative objectives with pure reconstruction losses in SDXL VAE latent space. It inherits the latent-space editing formulation from Rombach et al. [36] and the two-stage control-then-render design from DiffDub [28], but replaces the diffusion auto-encoder with a single feed-forward U-Net/Transformer and the SyncNet-based supervision with a flow-matching audio-to-pose transformer conditioned on wav2vec 2.0 features. The mask-free self-refinement step (synthesizing pseudo-pairs with the model itself) extends prior mask-free feasibility results [33] by generating supervision on-the-fly rather than relying on dataset-constructed pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: >100 FPS on a single H100 makes high-quality lip-sync viable for real-time dubbing, localization, and interactive avatar applications where diffusion-based methods (1.9–51 FPS) are prohibitive. The conceptual finding that a deterministic, one-step reconstruction suffices for a highly conditioned editing task challenges the field's assumption that iterative generative models are necessary for photorealistic output. Open questions remain around robustness under occlusion and extreme motion, the limited control space (12D lips vector may not capture prosody or emotion nuances), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the mouth region to broader facial editing tasks.
