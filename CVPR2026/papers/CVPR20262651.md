## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics predictors from H&E images treat spots as conditionally independent or model only pairwise neighbor relations, missing synergistic and antagonistic effects among multiple neighboring cells. Naively extending to high-order (many-body) interactions causes combinatorial explosion in attention tokens, making global many-body attention infeasible on high-resolution whole-slide images. The paper addresses how to explicitly capture high-order, many-to-many cross-cell dependencies while remaining computationally tractable at WSI scale.

## Input / Output

The model consumes H&E-stained WSI patches (RGB images at standard spot resolution) paired with 2D spatial coordinates for each spot, organized into a sparsified spatial graph using distance priors. At inference, it starts from a zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) noise sample and iteratively transports it toward the target gene-expression distribution via a learned flow-matching velocity field, producing per-spot gene expression vectors over the top highly variable genes. Spatial encodings are E(n)-invariant to handle rotations, translations, and reflections across slides.

## Method

MCToGene introduces a many-body attention module that lifts pairwise features into triplet-level interactions via inner and outer update pathways, modeling forward and reverse relational dependencies among node pairs (i,j) and a third node k. To control the combinatorial cost, a hierarchical interaction module couples a pairwise attention pathway (local neighbor screening) with the many-body pathway (high-order aggregation), with a hierarchical decoder that fuses both representations through learned reweighting rather than simple concatenation. The entire prediction is framed within a flow-matching generative objective, where a time-conditioned vector field transports ZINB noise to gene-expression targets, and a pretrained UNI pathology foundation model provides the visual encoder. E(n)-invariant spatial attention and sinusoidal time embeddings ensure geometry-robust, time-adaptive conditioning throughout the generation trajectory.

## Results

On HEST-1k, MCToGene achieves a mean Pearson correlation of 0.435 across 10 tumor types, outperforming the strongest baseline STFlow (0.415) by 4.8% relative and TRIPLEX (0.395) by 10.1% relative. On STImage-1K4M, MCToGene reaches 0.316 average PCC, a 7.85% relative improvement over STFlow (0.293). Gains are most pronounced in high-density cohorts: COAD improves from 0.326 (best baseline) to 0.410 (+25.8%), and SKCM from 0.704 to 0.711. For biomarker prediction, MCToGene attains 0.806 average PCC across four markers (GATA3, ERBB2, UBE2C, VWF), surpassing STFlow (0.786) by 2.5% relative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCToGene outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets. Against the strongest slide-based competitor STFlow, MCToGene gains +0.020 PCC on HEST (0.435 vs 0.415) and +0.023 on STImage (0.316 vs 0.293), with the largest margin on COAD (+0.084, i.e., 25.8% relative). Versus TRIPLEX, the gap widens to +0.040 on HEST and +0.064 on STImage, particularly on Prostate (0.283 vs 0.148, nearly 2×). Spot-based methods (UNI, BLEEP, Gigapath) trail by 0.067–0.140 PCC on HEST averages, confirming that local patch-level regression without spatial context is insufficient. Gigapath-slide, which uses global all-to-all attention, fails with OOM errors on large slides (IDC, COAD, Stomach), whereas MCToGene completes all runs with only ~1.41× the memory of STFlow (8,720 MB vs 6,164 MB) and ~1.5× the inference time (2.01 s/epoch vs 1.35 s/epoch). On biomarker prediction, MCToGene leads STFlow by +0.020 average PCC and TRIPLEX by +0.044, with the largest single-marker gain on VWF (0.692 vs 0.666 for STFlow).

## Relation to Prior Work

MCToGene builds directly on STFlow's flow-matching generative framework and E(n)-invariant spatial encoding, while adopting pretrained pathology foundation models (UNI) as the visual backbone. Its key departure is from the dominant pairwise-attention and GNN-based neighborhood aggregation used by prior slide-level methods (TRIPLEX, STFlow, HisToGene) toward explicit triplet-level many-body attention, a concept borrowed from molecular property prediction and hypergraph cell-communication models (scTensor, scHyper). The hierarchical coupling design—using pairwise attention as a screening layer before many-body aggregation—is the paper's central architectural novelty, addressing the combinatorial explosion that makes naive high-order attention infeasible at WSI scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit high-order, many-to-many cell interactions provide consistent and biologically interpretable gains over pairwise-only modeling, with the largest improvements appearing in high-density, spatially complex cohorts (COAD, IDC). The hierarchical coupling strategy offers a practical recipe for incorporating many-body expressiveness without prohibitive memory overhead, making it a reusable design pattern for other spatial or graph-structured prediction tasks. Open questions remain around scaling to even larger neighbor sets (adaptive/sparser sampling), extending beyond triplet-level to higher-order interactions, and validating the learned multi-cell interaction patterns in independent biological experiments.
