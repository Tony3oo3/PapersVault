## Problem

Pre-trained Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) such as FLUX and SD3 suffer from spatial layout collapse and degraded texture fidelity when generating images beyond their training resolution. Existing training-free methods address this with two-stage pipelines that first denoise at base resolution and then guide high-resolution synthesis, adding complexity and fundamentally treating the task as super-resolution rather than unlocking the model's intrinsic generative capability at higher resolutions.

## Input / Output

ResDiT takes a text prompt (and optionally depth or HED edge maps for spatially controlled generation) and directly produces high-resolution images up to 4096×4096 from models trained at 1024×1024, without requiring any base-resolution reference image. It supports arbitrary aspect ratios (e.g., 2048×4096, 4096×2048) and operates entirely in the high-resolution noise space, making it a single-stage generation process. The method is fully training-free and integrates seamlessly with downstream control modules like ControlNet.

## Method

ResDiT identifies that positional embeddings (PEs) govern spatial layout while attention receptive-field scale controls detail fidelity in DiTs, and restructures the vanilla self-attention into two complementary branches. The global branch applies scaled PE (interpolating HR position indices back to the training resolution range) with full global attention to preserve coherent spatial arrangement, while the local branch assigns each patch an independent base-resolution PE and restricts attention to patch-level windows to recover fine-grained details. To avoid grid artifacts from patch partitioning, the method uses minimum-overlap partitioning (patches slightly overlap to share boundary context) combined with Gaussian-weighted splicing that smoothly blends overlapping regions. A patch-wise spectral fusion module then merges the two branches in the frequency domain via FFT, retaining low-frequency structural components from the global branch and high-frequency detail components from the local branch. Temporally, the global branch handles early denoising timesteps, the local branch handles later timesteps, and spectral fusion bridges the remaining steps, following the coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion denoising.

## Results

At 3072×3072, ResDiT achieves the best KID (0.0189), patch IS (10.87), CLIP score (32.85), and user study rating (4.8/5) among all compared methods, while also attaining the best overall IS (12.91, tied with I-Max at 12.96). At 4096×4096, ResDiT shows a moderate drop in KID (0.0217) and IS (11.46) relative to the two-stage baselines, which the authors attribute to the distributional shift inherent in direct high-resolution sampling without base-resolution guidance. All evaluations use 500 prompts with KID computed against LAION-Aesthetics v2 6.5plus 2K reference images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 3072×3072, ResDiT outperforms all four baselines on KID (0.0189 vs. 0.0190–0.0211), patch IS (10.87 vs. 10.13–10.67), CLIP score (32.85 vs. 31.92–32.76), and user study (4.8 vs. 3.1–4.6), with the largest margins over Demofusion (+0.0022 KID, +0.66 ISp, +0.93 CLIP, +1.7 user score). HiFlow is the closest competitor, leading only on patch KID (0.0194 vs. 0.0199) and overall IS (12.87 vs. 12.91, where I-Max actually edges ahead at 12.96). At 4096×4096, ResDiT trails HiFlow on KID (0.0217 vs. 0.0203), patch KID (0.0252 vs. 0.0245), IS (11.46 vs. 11.65), and patch IS (9.97 vs. 10.12), and also falls behind I-Max on IS (11.46 vs. 11.79); however, it ties HiFlow on user study (4.3) and remains competitive on CLIP (32.71 vs. 32.74). The authors note that the 4096×4096 gap stems from ResDiT's single-stage design lacking base-resolution guidance, which causes a distributional shift that penalizes KID/IS but not perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResDiT departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm (I-Max, HiFlow, Demofusion, DiffuseHigh) that first generates a base-resolution image and then uses it to guide high-resolution sampling. Instead, it draws on two orthogonal ideas: positional interpolation from LLM context-window extension (Chen et al., 2023) to rectify spatial layout, and windowed local attention from Swin Transformers (Liu et al., 2021) to recover fine details. The key conceptual departure is treating high-resolution DiT generation as an intrinsic capability unlocked by restructuring attention, rather than a super-resolution problem requiring external guidance. This positions ResDiT alongside U-Net-specific training-free methods (ScaleCrafter, PBC) but explicitly generalizes to the DiT architecture family (FLUX, SD3).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic decomposition—positional embeddings govern spatial arrangement while attention receptive-field scale controls detail fidelity—provides a clear diagnostic framework for understanding and fixing resolution extrapolation failures in DiTs, which is valuable beyond this specific method. The training-free, single-stage design avoids the computational overhead and distributional anchoring of two-stage pipelines, making it attractive for practical deployment. However, the observed performance drop at 4096×4096 relative to two-stage baselines raises an open question about how far single-stage intrinsic scaling can push before the model's training distribution becomes too constraining. Future work could explore whether progressive resolution scheduling or learned PE schedules close this gap without reintroducing base-resolution dependencies.
