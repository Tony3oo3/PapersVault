## Problem

Existing monocular 3D lane detectors treat 3D coordinates as auxiliary supervision while prioritizing 2D image features, leaving the 2D-to-3D lifting ill-posed and prone to structural collapse (concavities, bulges, twists). The core gap is the absence of an invariant geometric-topological coupling between lanes and the underlying road surface that would preserve metric and topological structure during lifting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260906/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 360×480 and outputs K lane instances, each consisting of Q fixed-length 3D point sequences (x, y, z) sampled along a shared longitudinal reference set Y_ref (Q=20 on OpenLane, Q=10 on ApolloSim) plus a per-lane category label. The y-coordinates are not predicted but drawn from a predefined set, so the network only regresses lateral (x) and vertical (z) offsets at each sampled longitudinal position.

## Method

ReManNet bootstraps initial lane predictions via an Anchor3DLane-style backbone and detection heads, then encodes geometry on the SPD manifold: a position-weighted convolution extracts local spatial features, k-means clustering groups them into S clusters, and each cluster's Gaussian is mapped to a unit-determinant SPD matrix via a Schur-complement construction. Riemannian statistics (AIRM mean, tangent-space covariance) are computed with parallel transport to a learnable SPD reference, then mapped to the Lie algebra via matrix logarithm, passed through a learnable lower-triangular transform, and projected to a compact descriptor. A gated residual module adaptively fuses this geometric descriptor with anchor-level visual features before classification and regression heads. Training uses a novel 3D-TLIoU loss that computes slice-wise overlap of equal-radius tubular neighborhoods along each lane plus a tangent-direction cosine penalty, providing shape-level supervision beyond point-wise distance.

## Results

On OpenLane, ReManNet (ResNet-50) achieves a state-of-the-art F1 of 65.7%, improving by +8.2% over the Anchor3DLane (R50) baseline (57.5%) and by +1.8% over the previous best (GLane3D R50, 63.9%). It also attains the highest category accuracy (94.7%) and the lowest localization errors in both near and far ranges (Ex/N=0.189 m, Ex/F=0.205 m, Ez/N=0.060 m, Ez/F=0.072 m). On ApolloSim, ReManNet (R50) delivers the best far-range x-axis error across all three subsets and the best F1 on the Visual Variations subset (96.9%, +1.6% over the previous best).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReManNet (R50) outperforms all 13 compared baselines on OpenLane overall F1, beating the strongest prior method GLane3D (R50) [48] by +1.8% (65.7% vs. 63.9%) and the Anchor3DLane (R50) baseline [20] by +8.2%. It also surpasses PVALane (R50) [75] by +3.0% and Anchor3DLane++ (R50) [21] by +3.3% in F1, while achieving the lowest near- and far-range x/z localization errors across all competitors. In challenging scenarios, ReManNet (R50) leads in Extreme Weather (+6.6% over PVALane), Night (+5.1% over GLane3D), and Intersection (+5.2% over GLane3D), though it trails GLane3D (R50) on Curve (67.9% vs. 71.1%) and Merge & Split (59.9% vs. 64.8%), suggesting topological transitions remain a weakness. On ApolloSim, ReManNet (R50) achieves the best far-range x-axis error on all three subsets and the best F1 on Visual Variations (96.9%), but is slightly behind GLane3D on Balanced F1 (96.9% vs. 98.1%) and Rare F1 (97.2% vs. 98.4%), indicating the manifold encoding is most beneficial under appearance and illumination stress rather than in clean, balanced conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReManNet builds directly on the Anchor3DLane anchor-regression pipeline (CVPR 2023) as its initialization backbone, and adapts the Gaussian-to-SPD embedding and semidirect-product parameterization from GeomNet (ICCV 2021) to the lane-detection setting. Conceptually, it departs from the three dominant families in monocular 3D lane detection—depth-guided, BEV-centric, and line-modeling methods—by arguing that all of them underutilize 3D coordinates as carriers of intrinsic metric and topological structure, instead relegating them to auxiliary supervision. The paper's key positioning move is the Road-Manifold Assumption, which formalizes the road as a smooth 2D Riemannian manifold with lanes as 1D submanifolds, providing a principled geometric foundation that prior methods only approximate through ad-hoc priors (parallelism, smoothness, nominal width).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled geometric framework that could generalize beyond lane detection to other tasks requiring coherent 3D reconstruction from 2D observations (e.g., road surface reconstruction, curb detection, or vectorized HD map building). A notable limitation is the degraded performance on Merge & Split scenarios (59.9% F1 vs. 64.8% for GLane3D R50), where topological changes locally violate the manifold-consistency assumption, suggesting the framework needs extensions for non-smooth or branching geometries. Open questions include whether the SPD-manifold encoding is strictly necessary compared to simpler geodesic regularizers, how the method scales to longer sequences or higher point densities, and whether the k-means-based clustering step introduces unnecessary discretization that could be replaced by differentiable alternatives.
