## Problem

Diffusion-based human avatar generators are non-causal and computationally expensive, making them fundamentally incompatible with real-time streaming. Existing interactive avatar methods further suffer from identity drift over long sequences and model only one-way talking behavior, ignoring the avatar's listening state and restricting motion to the head-and-shoulder region. StreamAvatar tackles all three gaps simultaneously: real-time streaming, long-duration stability, and full-body talking-listening interaction.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single reference image (one-shot) and a continuous audio stream as input. The audio is processed by Wav2Vec 2.0, then multiplicatively masked by a TalkNet-derived binary mask (1 = speaking, 0 = listening) to produce separate talking and listening feature sequences that are injected via two dedicated audio attention modules. The output is a 720p full-body streaming video at 1.2-second latency on two H800 GPUs, with the text prompt fixed to 'a person is speaking and listening' so that all motion is driven purely by the audio signal.

## Method

StreamAvatar uses a two-stage autoregressive adaptation framework built on the Wan2.2 TI2V-5B backbone (30 DiT blocks). Stage 1 re-architects the bidirectional DiT into a block-causal model (chunk size C=3) and distills it into a 3-step causal generator via Score Identity Distillation with a student-forcing scheme, reducing inference to 1/40 of the original DiT runtime. Two mechanisms ensure long-video stability: a Reference Sink that permanently retains the reference frame's KV pairs in a rolling cache, and Reference-Anchored Positional Re-encoding (RAPR) that caps RoPE positional indices at a maximum distance D to prevent attention decay and train-test mismatch. Stage 2 applies adversarial refinement with a dual-branch Consistency-Aware Discriminator (local per-frame realism + global cross-attention consistency against the reference) trained with relativistic GAN loss and R1/R2 penalties. For interaction, a TalkNet audio mask separates talking and listening phases, and two audio attention modules (Talk and Listen) inject phase-specific Wav2Vec features to drive expressive co-speech gestures and natural listening reactions with smooth transitions.

## Results

On the short talking-avatar dataset (50 images, 5 s audio), StreamAvatar achieves the best FID (74.21), IQA (4.68), ASE (3.03), and Human Anomaly score (0.974) among all compared methods, while using only 3 denoising steps at 720p resolution. On the long dataset (25 images, 20 s audio), it maintains the best HA (0.993) and IQA (4.94). Generation of a 5-second clip takes 20 seconds on a single H20 GPU, compared to 7–74 minutes for the baselines. For interactive (talking + listening) generation, StreamAvatar substantially outperforms the talking-only baseline on all listening-motion metrics (LBKV 15.88 vs 6.05, LHKV 16.24 vs 4.53, LFKV 7.11 vs 2.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against **StableAvatar** (40 steps, 480p, 12 min), StreamAvatar wins on FID (74.21 vs 75.20), IQA, ASE, Sync-C, and HA, but trails on FVD (707 vs 604) and Sync-D, while being ~36× faster. Against **OmniAvatar** (25 steps, 480p, 36 min), StreamAvatar dominates on FID (74 vs 87), FVD (707 vs 852), IQA, ASE, and HA, though OmniAvatar edges ahead on short-clip Sync-C (7.60 vs 7.06) and Sync-D (7.99 vs 8.21); StreamAvatar is ~108× faster. Against **HY-Avatar** (50 steps, 720p, 74 min), StreamAvatar wins on FID, IQA, ASE, Sync-C, Sync-D, and HA, but HY-Avatar retains advantages in FVD (557 vs 707) and motion dynamics HKV (54.31 vs 48.35 on short; 80.46 vs 65.77 on long); StreamAvatar is ~222× faster. Against **Hallo3** (51 steps, 480p, 32 min) and **EchoMimicV3** (25 steps, 480p, 7 min), StreamAvatar outperforms on essentially every metric (FID, FVD, IQA, ASE, Sync-C, Sync-D, HKV, HA) while running 96× and 21× faster respectively. The principal trade-off is that the aggressive 3-step distillation slightly reduces FVD and peak motion amplitude relative to the 50-step HY-Avatar, but the gains in speed, resolution, and anomaly-free generation are substantial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) distilling bidirectional video diffusion models into few-step causal autoregressive generators (CausVid, Self-Forcing, Seaweed-APT2) and (2) audio-driven interactive avatar generation (INFP, ARIG, MIDAS, X-Streamer). It departs from prior autoregressive distillation work by identifying Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) as a specific, previously underappreciated source of long-video instability and proposing the RAPR re-encoding mechanism to fix it without requiring long-video training data. On the interactive side, it extends head-and-shoulder talking-listening methods to full-body generation and replaces audio track separation with a TalkNet-derived audio mask applied post-Wav2Vec extraction, which the ablation shows preserves feature fidelity better.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time, full-body, interactive (talking + listening) avatar generation at 720p with ~1.2 s latency is a practically significant milestone for applications in virtual assistants, education, and entertainment, and the two-stage distillation-plus-adversarial-refinement pipeline is a reusable recipe for adapting other bidirectional video models to streaming. The RAPR insight—that capping RoPE positional indices relative to a reference frame resolves both train-test mismatch and attention decay—generalizes beyond avatars to any long-sequence autoregressive diffusion setting. Open questions remain: the limited temporal context causes inconsistencies in persistently occluded regions, distillation inherently constrains motion diversity, and VAE decoding still dominates latency, all of which the authors flag as future work.
