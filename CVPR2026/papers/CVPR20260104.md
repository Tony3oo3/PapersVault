## Problem

Tagged MRI analysis has long treated motion tracking, tag-free cine synthesis, and super-resolution as separate, sub-optimally coupled tasks. Tag fading violates brightness constancy for optical-flow methods, spectral overlap breaks Fourier-based approaches like HARP, and the entanglement of anatomy, tags, and motion means each task in isolation degrades the others. No prior framework jointly recovers high-resolution anatomy, cine images, and motion from raw tagged MRI in a single nonlinear blind inverse formulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-orientation (i, j, k), multi-time (T frames) sequence of low-resolution 3D tagged MRI volumes as sole input—no paired cine, no external training data, and no known tag frequency or PSF. It jointly outputs four quantities: a super-resolved reference anatomy, a tag-free cine sequence at the deformed anatomy's resolution, a sequence of 3D diffeomorphic motion fields linking all frames to the reference, and the anisotropic Gaussian point-spread functions of the imaging system. Volumes are processed at 192×224×192 voxels, and the diffusion prior is pretrained on 80,000+ 1 mm isotropic T1w brain volumes, providing the resolution target for the super-resolved output.

## Method

InvTag formulates the tagged MRI reconstruction as a nonlinear blind inverse problem where the forward model couples an anisotropic Gaussian PSF, sinusoidal tag patterns with affine fading, and diffeomorphic warping (via a physics-informed neural network using exponential mapping). To solve the ill-posedness, it introduces a Coordinate Descent with Diffusion Prior (CDDP) scheme that alternates between (A) sampling the anatomy from a pretrained 3D U-Net diffusion model using a DPS-style data-consistency gradient (Tweedie's formula through the full forward model) and (B) maximum-likelihood updates of all forward-model parameters—differential evolution for low-dimensional PSF/tag/fading parameters and Adam for the high-dimensional PINN motion weights. At the first time frame, CDDP jointly estimates anatomy, PSF, and tag parameters over L iterations; for subsequent frames, anatomy, PSF, and tags are frozen while only fading and motion are updated, enforcing temporal anatomical consistency. The key insight is that MR physics provides hard structural constraints (how blur, tags, and fading are formed) while the diffusion prior supplies soft data-driven anatomical plausibility, and their synergy within coordinate descent yields stable convergence where joint optimization fails.

## Results

On motion estimation (primary task), InvTag achieves a mean EPE of 0.60±0.11 voxels and EPE@95 of 1.31±0.22 voxels, outperforming the strongest baseline DRIMET (0.79±0.14 / 1.61±0.53) by ~24% and ~19% respectively. For tag-to-cine synthesis, InvTag reaches PSNR 28.41 and SSIM 0.84 at t=6, surpassing HARP Demodulation (PSNR 23.93, SSIM 0.54) by over 4 dB and 0.30 SSIM. All optimization-based methods including InvTag produce nearly diffeomorphic fields (NegDet < 0.001%), while learning-based baselines show small but nonzero foldings (0.04–0.06%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InvTag outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the two learning-based motion trackers, LKUnet [24] (EPE 1.35) and DeepTag [61] (EPE 1.27), InvTag reduces mean EPE by 55% and 53% respectively, with the gap widening at the 95th percentile (1.31 vs. 2.94/2.97), indicating far fewer large local errors. Among optimization-based methods, SyN [4] (EPE 1.06) improves over learning-based baselines but still trails InvTag by 43% in mean EPE; DRIMET [11] (EPE 0.79), which leverages sHARP phase representations, is the closest competitor yet remains 24% worse in EPE and 19% worse in EPE@95. For tag-to-cine synthesis, HARP Demodulation [41] (PSNR 23.93, SSIM 0.54 at t=6) is substantially outperformed by InvTag (PSNR 28.41, SSIM 0.84), a gap that grows with tag fading over time, while the simpler LowpassFuse baseline (PSNR 26.68, SSIM 0.66) also falls well short. The consistent advantage across both synthesis and motion tasks underscores the benefit of jointly estimating PSF, tag parameters, fading, and anatomy within a single physics-constrained inverse framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework [16]—which handles linear or known-operator inverse problems—into a setting where the forward model itself is unknown (blind) and nonlinear (diffeomorphic deformation). It departs from prior tagged-MRI analysis, which treats motion tracking (HARP, DRIMET), cine synthesis (Liu et al.), and super-resolution as separate sub-problems, by formulating them as a single joint inversion. The CDDP scheme, which alternates diffusion-posterior anatomy sampling with maximum-likelihood updates of PSF, tag, fading, and motion parameters, is a new optimization strategy not present in prior diffusion-based inversion work, which typically assumes a fixed forward operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying anatomy recovery, cine synthesis, and motion estimation into one blind inverse problem, InvTag eliminates the need for separate cine acquisitions and avoids the spectral-overlap failures of Fourier-based tag tracking. The requirement of no paired training data makes it applicable in domains where tagged/cine pairs are scarce. However, the 1.2-hour-per-frame runtime, reliance on sinusoidal tags, and validation primarily on simulated data (with a single real gel phantom) limit near-term clinical adoption. Open questions include extension to cardiac and grid-tagged sequences, acceleration of the diffusion sampling loop, and incorporation of uncertainty quantification.
