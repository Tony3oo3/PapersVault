## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation methods distill knowledge from 2D open-vocabulary models, which restricts intrinsic 3D geometric learning and inherits errors from 2D occlusions and viewpoint ambiguities. Directly incorporating pretrained 3D geometric features fails to improve performance because heterogeneous supervision signals between modalities introduce training instability. The core gap is how to preserve and leverage intrinsic 3D geometric priors during 2D-to-3D knowledge distillation without degrading the pretrained geometric structure.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a 3D point cloud (N×3), multi-view RGB images with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and depth maps, and pixel-level features from frozen 2D open-vocabulary segmentation models (LSeg, OpenSeg, or SAS). During inference, only the 3D point cloud and free-form text category prompts are required; the three geometry-guided modules are dropped and the 3D-2D adapter output is matched against CLIP text embeddings. Voxelization uses 2 cm resolution for indoor scenes and 5 cm for outdoor scenes, following OpenScene conventions.

## Method

GeoGuide freezes a pretrained 3D backbone (Sonata) for geometric features and a 2D open-vocabulary segmentation model for semantic features, bridging them via a lightweight two-layer MLP adapter that maps 3D geometric features into the vision-language space. Three hierarchical modules enforce geometry-semantic consistency from local to global: (1) Uncertainty-based Superpoint Distillation computes per-point reliability weights from the discrepancy between superpoint-level and point-level geometric/semantic features, then performs uncertainty-weighted pooling within superpoints to suppress noisy 2D projections; (2) Instance-level Mask Reconstruction randomly occludes portions of class-agnostic instance masks and trains the model to reconstruct complete masks via cosine-similarity scoring, enforcing intra-instance semantic coherence; (3) Inter-Instance Relation Consistency aligns pairwise geometric similarity matrices with semantic similarity matrices at both superpoint and instance levels via MSE loss, preventing viewpoint-induced semantic drift among same-category objects. The total training loss is a weighted sum of the superpoint cosine distillation loss, mask reconstruction BCE loss, and similarity alignment MSE loss, with all three modules removed at inference time.

## Results

GeoGuide achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation with 64.8 mIoU / 77.3 mAcc on ScanNet v2, 50.3 mIoU / 74.2 mAcc on nuScenes, and 51.9 mIoU / 66.3 mAcc on Matterport3D (using SAS* 2D features). These represent improvements of +5.6, +4.9, and +5.6 mIoU over the strongest prior zero-shot baseline SAS (stage1) on the three datasets, respectively. With LSeg features alone, GeoGuide reaches 59.8 mIoU on ScanNet v2, surpassing the OpenScene (LSeg) 2D-3D ensemble by +5.6 mIoU without requiring the time-consuming self-distillation strategies used by SAS or GGSD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoGuide (SAS*) outperforms the strongest prior zero-shot method SAS (stage2) by +2.9 mIoU on ScanNet v2 (64.8 vs. 61.9), +2.8 mIoU on nuScenes (50.3 vs. 47.5), and +3.3 mIoU on Matterport3D (51.9 vs. 48.6). Against the OpenScene 2D-3D ensemble baselines, GeoGuide gains +5.6 mIoU with LSeg (59.8 vs. 54.2 on ScanNet v2) and +5.9 mIoU with OpenSeg (53.4 vs. 47.5), demonstrating that the gains are not tied to a specific 2D feature extractor. GeoGuide also surpasses GGSD by +3.3 mIoU on ScanNet v2 (59.8 vs. 56.5) and OV3D by +2.5 mIoU (59.8 vs. 57.3), while matching or exceeding PGOV3D (59.8 vs. 59.5). Notably, GeoGuide narrows the gap to fully-supervised methods: its 64.8 mIoU on ScanNet v2 exceeds early supervised approaches like TangentConv (40.9) and TextureNet (54.8) by large margins, though it remains below the strongest supervised baselines (PTv3 at 77.5, Mix3D at 73.6). On nuScenes, GeoGuide (OS) achieves 47.5 mIoU, surpassing OpenScene (OS) 2D-3D by +5.4 mIoU and DMA (OS) by +2.4 mIoU, while also improving mAcc by +5.5 over OpenScene (OS).

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoGuide builds directly on the OpenScene [46] 2D-to-3D distillation pipeline, inheriting its geometric correspondence establishment and feature projection strategy, but departs fundamentally from the assumption that 2D features should be treated as uncorrupted supervision. While prior methods (SAS, GGSD, CUA-O3D) focus on improving the quality or diversity of 2D features being distilled, GeoGuide instead introduces a frozen pretrained 3D backbone (Sonata) as a geometric anchor, arguing that the core failure mode is the destruction of intrinsic 3D geometric structure during distillation rather than insufficient 2D supervision. The three proposed modules (uncertainty-weighted superpoint distillation, instance mask reconstruction, inter-instance relation consistency) form a hierarchical local-to-global constraint hierarchy that has no direct analogue in prior open-vocabulary 3D segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates a non-trivial failure mode: naive 2D-to-3D distillation can actively degrade pretrained 3D geometric priors, with the severity depending on the specific 2D model used. This reframes the open-vocabulary 3D segmentation problem from 'how to get better 2D features' to 'how to preserve 3D geometry while absorbing 2D semantics,' a distinction that could influence future work beyond this specific task. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other 3D understanding tasks (detection, instance segmentation), whether the specific Sonata backbone is essential or any strong 3D pretraining suffices, and how the method scales to very large outdoor scenes where multi-view coverage is sparse. The method still requires multi-view RGB during training, limiting its applicability to purely LiDAR-only settings.
