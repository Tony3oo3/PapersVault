## Problem

Existing vision-language pre-training paradigms align entire video clips with holistic captions, leaving models unable to ground specific textual mentions to individual objects or spatial-temporal trajectories. This global-only supervision provides no incentive for the encoder to attend to instance-level details, limiting performance on fine-grained retrieval, spatial-temporal grounding, and object-centric tasks. The paper addresses this by embedding instance-awareness directly into the pre-training objective rather than grafting it on post-hoc via detectors or auxiliary heads.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 (or single-frame images treated as 1-frame videos) alongside text queries that can be either a holistic scene caption or a fine-grained instance description. For instance-level evaluation, visual inputs are cropped to bounding boxes (images) or temporal trajectories of boxes (videos) before encoding. Outputs include bidirectional retrieval relevance scores (T2V and V2T), and when a box-regression head is attached, the model predicts bounding boxes or spatial-temporal trajectories for visual grounding.

## Method

InstAP uses a two-stage training procedure. Stage 1 is self-supervised masked video modeling: a ViT-L student reconstructs a frozen CLIP-ViT-L teacher's semantic features on unmasked tokens, with an attention-guided binary mask (80% ratio) that removes the least informative tokens. Stage 2 introduces the core novelty—an instance-aware global-local alignment objective where bounding-box/trajectory crops are encoded by the same video encoder, then cross-attended with full-scene tokens to inject global context into each instance embedding. These instance-aware embeddings are contrasted against instance-level text embeddings using a modified VTC loss that masks false negatives from the same video, alongside instance-level VTM and MLM losses. The total loss combines masked-video reconstruction, three global alignment objectives (VTC, VTM, MLM), and three instance-level counterparts, each with independent weighting, trained jointly on 2M images and 50K videos from the newly introduced InstVL dataset with dual-granularity annotations.

## Results

On the InstVL-1K (video) instance retrieval split, InstAP achieves 60.63 T2V R@1, substantially exceeding the strongest prior method (VideoPrism at 40.86) and the same-data UMT-L (g+i) baseline (40.38). On zero-shot text-to-video retrieval, InstAP sets new state-of-the-art R@1 of 41.1 on MSR-VTT and 54.0 on DiDeMo, surpassing UMT-L (39.7 and 47.0 respectively). On the grounding task, InstAP improves IoU@90 on the video split from 14.44 (UMT-L) to 25.13, confirming precise spatial-temporal localization. Critically, InstAP outperforms UMT-L (InstVL; g+i) trained on the identical corpus by 9.22 points on InstVL-10K (img) instance T2V R@1 (44.05 vs. 34.83), isolating the benefit of the instance-aware objective over data alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstAP outperforms all 20 compared baselines on every InstVL instance-retrieval split, with the largest margin over UMT-L (InstVL; g+i) on the video split (60.63 vs. 40.38 T2V R@1, +20.25). On zero-shot retrieval, InstAP beats UMT-L on MSR-VTT (41.1 vs. 39.7 R@1), DiDeMo (54.0 vs. 47.0), MSVD (49.2 vs. 47.0), and ActivityNet (50.7 vs. 44.3), but trails UMT-L on LSMDC (23.5 vs. 26.0 R@1). Notably, naively fine-tuning UMT-L on the InstVL corpus (g or g+i variants) degrades zero-shot performance relative to the original UMT-L (e.g., MSR-VTT drops from 39.7 to 35.4/34.0), whereas InstAP simultaneously improves both instance and global metrics. On the grounding task (Table 3), InstAP surpasses UMT-L across all three splits and IoU thresholds, with the most dramatic gain on video IoU@90 (25.13 vs. 14.44, +10.69). Among image-domain baselines, SigLIP and OpenCLIP are the strongest on instance retrieval (38.17 and 37.88 T2V R@1 on InstVL-1K img), but both fall well short of InstAP's 50.25, highlighting the advantage of the dedicated instance-aware training objective over general-purpose contrastive pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

InstAP builds directly on the UMT (Unmasked Teacher) framework for its self-supervised masked video modeling stage, adopting the teacher-student semantic regression paradigm and the class-token-removed joint space-time attention design. It departs from the dominant CLIP-style global-only video-text alignment by introducing an instance-aware contrastive objective that grounds specific textual mentions to spatial-temporal trajectories via a Global-Local Cross Attention mechanism. This contrasts with prior approaches that inject instance-level signals post-hoc through external detectors (OSCAR, VinVL) or auxiliary segmentation heads, which the authors argue inherit detector errors and fail to achieve true instance-level alignment in the core representation. The InstVL dataset itself fills a gap identified in the related work: existing grounded datasets (Visual Genome, VidOR, ActivityNet-Entities) provide either static region annotations, closed-vocabulary predicates, or single-frame groundings, but none offer free-form, trajectory-grounded captions at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is the InstVL dataset (2M images, 50K videos with dual-granularity annotations), which provides the first large-scale, general-domain resource with free-form trajectory-grounded captions, enabling systematic benchmarking of instance-level video understanding. The finding that instance-aware pre-training simultaneously improves global retrieval (e.g., +4.0 R@1 on DiDeMo over the same-corpus baseline) challenges the assumption that fine-grained and holistic objectives are in tension, suggesting a more unified representation is achievable. However, the annotation pipeline relies on GroundingDINO and SAM2, meaning the dataset inherits their detection/tracking errors, and the 50K video count remains modest relative to the 2M images. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-form video, whether the annotation quality ceiling limits further gains, and whether the instance-aware objective transfers to generative or reasoning tasks beyond retrieval and grounding.
