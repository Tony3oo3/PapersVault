## Problem

Existing rendering-based synthetic datasets for Scene Text Recognition (STR) suffer from a significant domain gap with real data due to insufficient diversity in corpus (only single semantic words), fonts (conventional typefaces), and layout (horizontal, uniform-size text). This limits their ability to simulate challenging scenarios such as curved text, multi-oriented characters, artistic fonts, contextless strings, and multi-word phrases, causing models trained on them to underperform on real-world benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The UnionST engine takes a sampled text string, a compatible font file, and a background image, then renders each character as an independent layer with individually computed position, orientation, and size before compositing onto the background with effects (elastic deformation, perspective, shadows, embossing). The resulting synthetic image is paired with its exact ground-truth text label. The downstream STR model (SVTRv2-AR) takes cropped scene text images and outputs a recognized text string via an autoregressive attention decoder, replacing the standard CTC decoder to handle non-monotonic character alignments in curved and multi-oriented text.

## Method

UnionST is a rendering-based synthesis engine that addresses three gaps: (1) corpus enrichment with contextless random strings, incomplete words (randomly removed characters), and multi-word phrases; (2) a 113.8K-font collection filtered for clear upper/lowercase distinction; and (3) per-character placement modeled via a parametric quadratic curve (curvature parameter a ∈ [20,200]) with global rotation φ ∈ [0,2π), enabling curved, multi-oriented, and multi-sized text. The STR backbone is SVTRv2 with its CTC decoder replaced by an attention-based autoregressive decoder for flexible input-output alignment. The Self-Evolution Learning (SEL) framework then bridges the remaining real-synthetic gap: a model trained on UnionST-S pseudo-labels a large unlabeled real dataset, the predicted texts serve as a new corpus to synthesize UnionST-P (5M samples), and iterative self-refinement (ISR) fine-tunes on high-confidence (≥0.9) pseudo-labeled samples over multiple rounds, reserving manual annotation only for the ~9% remaining low-confidence hard cases.

## Results

Training SVTRv2-AR on UnionST-S (5M samples) achieves 95.32% average accuracy on six common benchmarks and 83.00% on the challenging Union14M-Benchmark, outperforming the much larger ST-2D (36M) by 9.64% on U14M-Bench. When combined with real data (UnionST-SP+R, 10M+3.22M), the model reaches 91.39% on U14M-Bench—the first time STR performance exceeds 90% on this benchmark. Notably, fine-tuning the UnionST-S pre-trained model with only 1% (32K) of real annotations matches the full real-data performance (87.26% vs. 87.22%), and the SEL framework with iterative self-refinement achieves 89.81% using only ~9% manual annotation, a 91% reduction in labeling cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnionST-S (5M) dramatically outperforms all existing synthetic datasets on the challenging U14M-Benchmark: it surpasses ST-2D (36M, 73.36%) by 9.64%, SynthTIGER (10M, 57.87%) by 25.13%, TextSSR (3.55M, 48.46%) by 34.54%, and MJ (8.92M, 31.52%) by 51.48%. On common benchmarks, UnionST-S also leads with 95.32% vs. ST-2D's 94.90%, despite using 7× fewer samples. When combined with real data, UnionST-SP+R (91.39%) beats ST-2D+R (89.71%) by 1.68% and the real-only baseline (87.22%) by 4.17%. In the low-resource setting, UnionST-S fine-tuned on just 1% of real labels (87.26%) matches the full real-data model (87.22%), while the SEL framework with iterative self-refinement reaches 89.81% using only ~9% manual annotation—narrowing the gap to the full-supervision SOTA (91.39%) to just 0.16%. Among generative methods evaluated on ScenePair, the best (TextCtrl) achieves only 84.67% word accuracy, confirming that rendering-based synthesis remains superior for training data generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the rendering-based synthetic data paradigm established by MJ (Jaderberg et al., 2014) and refined by ST, SynthAdd, CurvedST, and SynthTIGER, arguing that prior engines fail not in visual realism but in the diversity and combination of challenging conditions (curved, multi-oriented, contextless, multi-word text). It builds on SVTRv2 (Du et al., ICCV 2025) as the base recognition model, modifying its CTC decoder to an autoregressive attention decoder to better handle non-monotonic text layouts. Conceptually, the paper departs from the growing trend toward diffusion-based text generation (TextSSR, TextCtrl) by demonstrating that rendering-based synthesis, when properly diversified, remains superior for STR training due to guaranteed label correctness, and introduces a self-evolution learning loop that bridges the remaining synthetic-to-real distribution gap without full annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first STR result exceeding 90% on Union14M-Benchmark and demonstrates that a 5M-sample synthetic dataset can outperform 36M-sample alternatives by nearly 10 points on challenging subsets, reframing the synthetic data problem as one of component diversity rather than scale or visual fidelity. The SEL framework's 91% annotation reduction (3.22M → 290K labels) while staying within 0.16% of full-supervision SOTA offers a practical pathway for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multilingual and low-resource settings, document OCR, and handwriting recognition, and whether the pseudo-corpus strategy (UnionST-P) can be further refined to close the remaining gap on the Artistic subset, which still lags real-data performance by ~4 points.
