## Problem

Split conformal prediction provides finite-sample coverage guarantees for zero-shot medical VLMs, but prediction sets are often overly large and exhibit high class-conditioned coverage gap (CCV), especially under few-shot, imbalanced, or domain-shifted conditions. Naively adapting the VLM on calibration labels before conformalizing breaks the exchangeability assumption and voids coverage guarantees. The paper asks whether one can sharpen zero-shot predictions to improve set efficiency and class-wise balance while strictly preserving split-conformal validity, without gradient updates or reusing calibration labels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a medical image (histology patch, fundus photograph, or chest X-ray) and a small labeled calibration split (K=16 shots per class, sampled to match label marginals). It produces a set-valued prediction C(x) containing all class labels whose failure-aware nonconformity score falls below a calibrated threshold, along with refined per-class probability vectors. The VLM encoders remain frozen throughout; LATA operates in a windowed transductive setting (W=256 samples) over the joint calibration-and-test pool, requiring no backpropagation and adding only ~0.05–0.06 s/image of compute.

## Method

LATA refines zero-shot class probabilities by solving a KL-regularized graph-smoothness objective over a sparse symmetric union kNN graph built on ℓ2-normalized image embeddings of the joint calibration-and-test pool. The non-convex quadratic is decomposed via the Concave-Convex Procedure (CCCP), yielding a simple multiplicative mean-field update (Eq. 6) that is run for 5–10 iterations without gradients. Because the same deterministic transform is applied identically to calibration and test samples, exchangeability is preserved and SCP validity is maintained. A failure-aware conformal score (Eq. 8) then reweights the base nonconformity (LAC/APS/RAPS) using a frozen ViLU module: the instance-level failure probability u(x) inflates scores on hard inputs to protect coverage, while the image-conditioned label attention α(x) discounts plausible labels to shrink sets. An optional prior knob (β) injects calibration label marginals as a fixed logit bias applied symmetrically, enabling a label-informed variant (LATA-LI) that tightens coverage with minimal efficiency cost.

## Results

Across three medical VLMs and nine tasks (averaged), LATA-LF (β=0) at α=0.10 reduces average prediction set size from 3.99–4.16 (SCP) to 2.95–3.29 and CCV from 9.55–9.96 to 6.32–6.85, while maintaining or exceeding nominal coverage (0.900–0.903). At α=0.05, set size drops from 4.88–5.12 to 3.76–4.02 and CCV from 5.54–5.68 to 3.35–4.25. The label-informed variant LATA-LI (β=0.2) further tightens coverage (e.g., 0.910 under APS at α=0.10) with only a marginal size increase (2.95→3.03). LATA achieves these gains at ~0.05–0.06 s/img and 0.8 GB peak memory, roughly 17–20× faster than SCA-T or TIM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SCP baseline, LATA-LF reduces set size by 23–28% (e.g., APS: 4.05→2.95) and CCV by 34–36% (e.g., LAC: 9.96→6.40) while matching or exceeding nominal coverage. Versus the strongest label-free transductive baseline SCA-T [31], LATA-LF shrinks sets by 7–12% (APS: 3.35→2.95) and cuts CCV by 10–15% (LAC: 7.47→6.40) at α=0.10. Against Conf-OT [30], LATA achieves comparable or smaller sets but substantially lower CCV (APS: 8.64→6.32), indicating more balanced per-class coverage. The label-using oracle FCA [33] achieves similar set sizes and CCV (APS: 3.06/6.12) but requires per-label adapters on calibration data; LATA-LI (β=0.2) closes this gap (3.03/6.25) without any target-domain labels at transfer time. Adapt+SCP, which reuses calibration labels for both adaptation and conformalization, systematically under-covers (0.842–0.858 at α=0.10), violating exchangeability, whereas LATA maintains nominal coverage. TIM [4] and TransCLIP [40] also under-cover (0.887–0.888 and 0.726–0.733 respectively), with TransCLIP exhibiting severely imbalanced CCV (21.8–22.3), while LATA runs 17–20× faster than TIM/SCA-T (0.05 s vs. 1.04–1.18 s per image).

## Relation to Prior Work

LATA sits at the intersection of graph-based semi-supervised learning (Laplacian regularization à la Belkin et al.) and split conformal prediction (Vovk et al.), but departs from both in a key way: it applies a deterministic, symmetric mean-field transform (via CCCP) over a joint calibration-test kNN graph, which preserves the exchangeability that inductive adapters and linear probes violate. The failure-aware scoring builds on the ViLU module (Lafon et al., ICCV 2025) to inject multimodal difficulty and label-plausibility signals into standard nonconformity scores (LAC/APS/RAPS). Unlike full conformal prediction, which requires per-query label-wise refits, LATA is a single fixed-point pass with no gradients, no VLM updates, and no target-domain labels at transfer time—positioning it between vanilla SCP and the label-using FCA oracle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical medical deployment, the ability to tighten prediction sets and balance class-wise coverage without retraining or consuming additional labeled data is practically significant; LATA's ~0.05 s/img and 0.8 GB footprint make it deployable alongside existing frozen VLM pipelines. The ablation showing that the graph-smoothing component alone (no ViLU) delivers most of the gain suggests the core insight—deterministic symmetric transforms preserve conformal validity while sharpening posteriors—is broadly applicable beyond medical imaging. Open questions include extension to dense/segmentation outputs, robustness under severe domain shift where embedding quality degrades, and whether the kNN graph construction can be made adaptive per-dataset without re-tuning γ.
