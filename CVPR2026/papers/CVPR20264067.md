## Problem

Multimodal large language models are trained to represent vision and language in a shared embedding space, yet it remains unclear whether this joint representation actually enables consistent reasoning when the same information is delivered in different modalities. Prior benchmarks either evaluated a single model, did not control for OCR failures (conflating recognition errors with reasoning inconsistencies), or lacked a guaranteed-unseen task to rule out memorization. This paper asks whether frontier MLLMs produce the same answer regardless of whether the input is text, a rendered image, or a mixed prompt, and what internal factors drive any observed inconsistency.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample presents semantically identical content in three input formats: a text-only prompt, an image-only prompt (text rendered as a high-resolution image at 200 DPI, white background, black DejaVu Sans, ≤800 characters, no LaTeX), and a mixed prompt (context as image, question as text, or vice versa for multiple-choice). REST+ extends this with 10 visual permutations per image varying font family (DejaVu Sans, Courier New, Cursive), resolution (50/100/200 DPI), and text color. Outputs are structured as per-question correctness across modalities, aggregated into three consistency metrics (RER, CFR, MMC), per-modality accuracy, and OCR character error rate; consistency scores are computed only on the subset where the model perfectly transcribed the rendered text.

## Method

The paper introduces REST and REST+ as controlled stress tests for cross-modal consistency. REST draws from MMLU, AI2-ARC, and GSM-Symbolic (rendered as images) plus a newly generated SOEBENCH of linear-equation systems with a restricted symbol set (digits 0–9, letters A–E) to guarantee zero prior exposure and minimal OCR burden. For each question, the model is prompted four times (OCR transcription, text answer, image answer, mixed answer) with chain-of-thought and temperature 0, and answers are parsed via regex. Three metrics quantify inconsistency: RER (fraction of questions answered identically across all three modalities), CFR (fraction solvable in at least one but not all modalities, excluding universally-failed questions), and MMC (fraction solvable in at least one modality). REST+ permutes each image across 10 visual variants to isolate the effects of resolution, font, and color. For RQ4, the authors compute mean-pooled hidden-state embeddings for matched image/text/written-label pairs on 1,000 ImageNet samples and show that the cross-modal cosine similarity (retrieval accuracy) at the best layer correlates with each model's RER, linking the modality gap in embedding space to behavioral inconsistency.

## Results

On the REST benchmark (OCR-correct subset), RER (Render-Equivalence Rate) ranges from 6.6% (DeepSeek-Tiny) to 90.7% (GPT-5-mini) across 15 MLLMs, with CFR (Cross-Modality Failure Rate) ranging from 8.7% to 98.0%. No model achieves full cross-modal consistency; even the best model (GPT-5-mini) fails to produce the same answer across text, image, and mixed modalities on 9.3% of solvable questions. On the harder REST+ benchmark (varying font, resolution, colour), consistency drops substantially, with the best model (InternVL3-14B) reaching only 72.1% RER. Almost all models prefer the text modality, with statistical significance (t=17.7, p<0.05 for text > image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, GPT-5-mini (90.7% RER) and Claude Haiku 4.5 (90.3%) lead, outperforming GPT-4o-mini (71.3%) and Gemini-2.5 Flash Lite (54.3%) by 19–36 percentage points. Among open-source models, Qwen-2.5-32B (84.7%) is the most consistent, followed by InternVL3-14B (78.4%) and Mistral-Small (73.6%), while DeepSeek-Tiny (6.6%) and Phi-4 (14.9%) show extreme inconsistency. On the harder REST+ benchmark, InternVL3-14B (72.1% RER) surpasses GPT-5-mini (67.6%) and Claude Haiku 4.5 (65.7%), suggesting its visual encoder handles varied rendering conditions more robustly. Notably, the 'OCR-first-then-solve' strategy does not consistently help: it improves GPT-5-mini by only +0.7 to +1.2 points but hurts DeepSeek-Small by up to −13.1 points on MMLU. The paper's central finding is that no MLLM, regardless of scale or architecture, achieves full cross-modal consistency, and the degree of inconsistency is not explained by OCR failures alone (as shown on the novel SOEBENCH where OCR is near-perfect yet text modality still dominates).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal consistency evaluation line initiated by Zhang et al. [44] and Omni-R [8], which documented inconsistent answers across modalities but either evaluated a single model or conflated OCR failures with reasoning errors. It also builds on the modality gap literature (Liang et al. [18], Shukor and Cord [32]) by using implicit alignment scores as a mechanistic probe for inconsistency. The key departure is the introduction of controlled OCR (via SOEBENCH, a novel system-of-equations dataset with restricted symbols), systematic visual characteristic manipulation (REST+), and the explicit separation of recognition from reasoning, which prior benchmarks lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that no evaluated MLLM (including GPT-5-mini and Claude Haiku 4.5) achieves full cross-modal consistency has direct practical implications for deployment: answers can change based solely on input format, undermining reliability. The correlation between embedding-space similarity and consistency score suggests a concrete mechanistic target (closing the modality gap) for future training, though the paper explicitly notes causality remains untested. Open questions include whether the persistent text-modality preference (even at perfect OCR) reflects data contamination or an architectural bias, and whether the surprising benefit of colored text over black text generalizes beyond rendered text to natural images.
