## Problem

Existing neural decoding research reconstructs only visual content from visual-only stimuli using a single neural modality, overlooking both the brain's natural audiovisual integration (e.g., auditory signals modulating visual perception) and the complementary strengths of fMRI (fine spatial resolution) and EEG (millisecond temporal resolution). No prior dataset synchronizes fMRI and EEG under naturalistic, temporally dynamic audiovisual conditions, and no systematic exploration of fMRI-EEG fusion for video reconstruction has been conducted.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4-second clips of synchronized brain signals: fMRI at 1.25 Hz yielding 5 frames × 8,405 visual voxels (expandable to 18,946 voxels with auditory ROIs) and EEG at 1000 Hz across 64 channels × 4,000 samples. The output is a reconstructed 4-second video clip at 480×720 resolution and 8 fps (33 frames), generated by a diffusion model conditioned on the fused brain representation rather than text. Stimuli are 720p episodes of The Big Bang Theory, downsampled to 480×720 for decoding.

## Method

CineSync comprises a Multi-Modal Fusion Encoder (MFE) and a Neural Latent Decoder (NLD). The MFE uses independent 12-layer ViT backbones (2048 hidden dim, 227 tokens) for fMRI and EEG, fused via a learned MLP, with a five-way contrastive alignment objective (CLIP loss) anchoring both modality class tokens to SigLIP video and text embeddings plus a cross-modal fMRI-EEG term. The NLD is a LoRA-tuned CogVideoX-5B diffusion model whose text conditioning is replaced by the fused brain vector z_b, trained with standard noise-prediction loss on 3D-VAE-encoded video latents. A key empirical finding is that separate dual-transformer encoding substantially outperforms joint or cross-attention fusion, revealing large representational gaps between the two modalities that make early shared attention counterproductive.

## Results

On CineBrain, CineSync achieves 0.909 video-level 2-way accuracy, 0.319 50-way accuracy, and FVD of 52.78, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best variant, CineSync* (incorporating auditory ROIs), further improves to 0.926 2-way, 0.336 50-way, and FVD 44.77. At the perceptual level, CineSync reaches SSIM 0.262 and PSNR 11.99, roughly doubling the SSIM of the strongest baseline (GLFA at 0.123). Temporal consistency metrics (DTC 0.915, CTC 0.967) also exceed all baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CineSync decisively outperforms all three baselines across every metric. Against EEG2Video [28] (EEG-only), CineSync improves video-level 50-way accuracy from 0.162 to 0.319 and reduces FVD from 146.23 to 52.78, while SSIM more than doubles from 0.109 to 0.262. Against GLFA [23] (fMRI-only), CineSync gains 0.152 in 50-way accuracy (0.319 vs 0.167) and cuts FVD by 59% (52.78 vs 128.76). Against NeuroClips [16] (fMRI-only), the advantage is similar: 50-way accuracy rises from 0.183 to 0.319 and SSIM jumps from 0.087 to 0.262. Notably, even the single-modality ablations (CineSync-fMRI and CineSync-EEG) already surpass all baselines, indicating that the architectural design (dual-transformer encoder + diffusion decoder) is itself a major contributor, with the fMRI-EEG fusion providing an additional ~5% gain in 50-way accuracy and ~10% FVD reduction over the best single-modality variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neuro-decoding line of work (fMRI-to-video via GLFA, NeuroClips, Cinematic Mindscapes; EEG-to-video via EEG2Video) in two key directions: it moves from visual-only to audiovisual stimuli and from single-modality to simultaneous fMRI+EEG brain signals. The architectural design draws on contrastive learning strategies established in Mind's Eye and Mind-3D, and the decoder builds on CogVideoX-5B with LoRA fine-tuning, following the broader trend of adapting pretrained diffusion models for brain-conditioned generation. The systematic comparison of five fusion architectures (joint transformer, cross-attention, spatial concatenation, dual transformer) is a novel empirical contribution not present in prior single-modality decoding work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CineBrain is the first large-scale dataset synchronizing fMRI and EEG under naturalistic audiovisual viewing, filling a critical gap that has limited cross-modal and cross-modality decoding research. The demonstration that auditory cortical activations improve visual decoding provides empirical grounding for the well-known cross-modal facilitation in perception, now quantified in a generative decoding setting. However, the dataset is limited to six participants and a single TV series, raising questions about generalizability across content types, populations, and scanner sites. Open questions include whether the fMRI-EEG complementarity advantage persists at larger scale, whether the dual-transformer design generalizes to other modality pairs, and whether the audiovisual decoding task can be extended to full audio reconstruction.
