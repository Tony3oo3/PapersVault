## Problem

Controllable driving scene generation requires both photorealism and precise multi-agent control, yet existing approaches fail to achieve both simultaneously: reconstruction-based methods replay scenes faithfully but cannot synthesize novel trajectories or agents, while generation-based methods hallucinate content but lose structural and geometric consistency. No simple, unified framework with a systematic study of controllable driving editing existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262900/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source driving video (from Waymo), a user-specified joint trajectory set T that prescribes the motion of the ego vehicle and all surrounding agents simultaneously, and optionally a natural-language text prompt describing a vehicle to insert. The output is a temporally coherent, photorealistic driving video in which all agents follow the prescribed trajectories and any text-described vehicle appears as a geometry-aligned 3D mesh. Notably, once 3D assets are harvested from the source video, each edited variation is produced in a single feed-forward pass without per-trajectory optimization.

## Method

HorizonForge is a two-stage pipeline: (1) 3D Assets Harvesting reconstructs the input video into editable 3D Gaussian Splats (via OmniRe) and 3D vehicle meshes (via SAM segmentation, Pix2Gestalt amodal inpainting, and Hunyuan3D mesh generation, with GPT-based pose alignment), and (2) Video Rendering repositions all assets per target trajectories, rasterizes the edited scene, and refines it with a fine-tuned video diffusion model (TrajectoryCrafter on the CogVideoX backbone) conditioned on the rasterized frames. Training pairs are constructed via a cycle-reconstruction strategy—perturbing trajectories, re-reconstructing Gaussians, and rendering back to form clean-noisy pairs—plus mesh-Gaussian mixed rendering to close the photorealism gap. The key empirical findings are that Gaussian Splats encode substantially richer appearance cues than colored point clouds or bounding boxes, and that video diffusion's temporal priors are essential for cross-frame coherence, outperforming image-diffusion baselines by a wide margin.

## Results

On the overall assessment of HorizonSuite, HorizonForge achieves FID 33.19 and FVD 536.49, outperforming the second-best method OmniRe (FID 44.37, FVD 546.00) by 25.2% in FID. On the most challenging insertion task, HorizonForge reaches FID 117.46 and OSR 5.86, substantially ahead of StreetCrafter (FID 267.76, OSR 4.62) and Difix3D (FID 267.53, OSR 4.71). In the user study, HorizonForge wins 91.02% of pairwise comparisons (456/501), an 83.4% preference gain over the next-best method OmniRe (7.78%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HorizonForge is compared against three baselines spanning reconstruction (OmniRe), reconstruction+denoising (Difix3D), and generation (StreetCrafter) paradigms. Against StreetCrafter and Difix3D, HorizonForge dominates across all task categories and metrics, with the largest gaps on insertion (FID 117.46 vs. 267.76/267.53) and overall FID (33.19 vs. 91.16/80.84). Against the strongest baseline OmniRe, HorizonForge wins on overall FID (33.19 vs. 44.37), insertion FID (117.46 vs. 182.29), removal FID (29.01 vs. 33.97), and most ego/agent manipulation tasks; however, OmniRe retains an edge on ego-vehicle Direction Change FID (80.60 vs. 144.83), suggesting HorizonForge's video diffusion backbone introduces slightly more spatial distortion under large ego heading changes. In the user study, HorizonForge's 91.02% win rate dwarfs OmniRe's 7.78%, confirming that the quantitative FID gap translates into clearly superior perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HorizonForge sits at the intersection of reconstruction-based simulation (3D Gaussian Splatting via OmniRe) and generation-based simulation (video diffusion via CogVideoX/TrajectoryCrafter), departing from prior hybrid methods like ReconDreamer and DriveDreamer4D that require costly per-trajectory optimization. It repurposes standard video diffusion as a feed-forward rendering module conditioned on editable 3D assets (Gaussian splats + meshes), rather than training a bespoke architecture. The mesh harvesting and text-to-vehicle insertion pipeline borrows from Hunyuan3D and SAM, extending the controllable editing scope beyond trajectory manipulation to language-guided object insertion—a capability absent in StreetCrafter and Difix3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HorizonForge provides a practical, scalable paradigm for multi-agent driving scene editing that avoids per-trajectory optimization, making it more deployable for large-scale simulation data generation. The HorizonSuite benchmark fills a gap by standardizing evaluation across ego- and agent-level manipulation tasks with fine-grained metrics (VIMS, BAS, OSR) that prior benchmarks lacked. Key open questions include scalability to longer video sequences, generalization beyond Waymo's driving environments, and whether the mesh harvesting pipeline can be fully automated. The finding that a simple video diffusion backbone with 3D conditioning outperforms more complex architectures suggests the field may be over-engineering the generation stage.
