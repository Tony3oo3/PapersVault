## Problem

Existing 3D generators rely on a single conditioning modality: image-conditioned models achieve high visual fidelity but are highly sensitive to viewpoint informativeness, forcing hallucination of unobserved regions, while text-conditioned models provide broad semantic guidance but lack pixel-aligned visual detail. This paper asks whether combining both modalities can yield more flexible and faithful 3D generation, and empirically demonstrates that even naive late fusion of the two already outperforms either alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260179/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (one of front, top, or bottom views) and a free-form text prompt as joint conditions, and produces a 3D mesh or 3D Gaussian Splatting. Thanks to condition dropout during training, TIGON also supports text-only or image-only inference, making the conditioning regime flexible at test time. The 3D output is generated in the UniLat3D/TRELLIS latent space (a 16×16×16 structured latent) and decoded via the corresponding VAE decoder.

## Method

TIGON employs a dual-branch architecture with separate image-conditioned and text-conditioned DiT backbones, motivated by the granularity mismatch between dense view-grounded image tokens and sparse abstract text tokens that would degrade a single unified backbone. Early fusion is achieved via zero-initialized cross-modal linear bridges (inspired by ControlNet) inserted between every pair of corresponding DiT blocks, enabling bidirectional feature exchange while preserving each branch's pretrained single-modality capability. Late fusion simply averages the two branches' predicted velocity fields at each denoising step. Training is two-stage: branches are pretrained separately, then jointly fine-tuned with 0.5 condition dropout on each modality, producing a uniform mixture over unconditional, text-only, image-only, and joint regimes for classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On Toys4K with joint text-image conditioning (3DGS output), TIGON achieves 92.33 CLIP and 61.59 FD_DINOv2, substantially outperforming the best image-only baseline (UniLat3D: 91.20 CLIP, 85.30 FD) and the best text-only baseline (UniLat3D: 86.14 CLIP, 154.88 FD). On the harder UniLat1K benchmark, TIGON (I+T, GS) reaches 92.42 CLIP and 130.08 FD_DINOv2, again beating both single-modality counterparts by wide margins. For mesh outputs on Toys4K, TIGON (I+T) scores 41.36 ULIP and 36.68 Uni3D, exceeding the best image-only mesh baseline (UniLat3D: 40.32 ULIP, 35.75 Uni3D).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under joint text-image conditioning, TIGON (GS) decisively outperforms all single-modality baselines: on Toys4K it reduces FD_DINOv2 from 85.30 (UniLat3D image-only) and 154.88 (UniLat3D text-only) to 61.59, and on UniLat1K from 155.99 and 282.36 to 130.08. Against the strongest image-only mesh baselines (UniLat3D, Hunyuan3D-2.1, TRELLIS), TIGON's mesh output (I+T) improves ULIP from 40.32/40.22/39.97 to 41.36 and Uni3D from 35.75/35.70/35.61 to 36.68 on Toys4K. In single-modality settings, TIGON is competitive but not always best: its image-only GS result (84.62 FD) slightly beats UniLat3D (85.30), while its text-only GS (152.34 FD) marginally improves over UniLat3D (154.88) but trails TRELLIS (148.21). The simpler SimFusion baseline (naive velocity averaging without cross-modal bridges) already achieves 66.78 FD on Toys4K, confirming complementarity, but TIGON's full architecture with zero-initialized bridges and joint fine-tuning closes the gap to 61.59. Weaker baselines like TripoSR (596.44 FD) and Step1X-3D (361.44 FD) are far behind, highlighting the advantage of native 3D diffusion over SDS-based or reconstruction-based pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIGON builds directly on UniLat3D's single-stage rectified-flow architecture (itself derived from TRELLIS's structured 3D latents), repurposing its DiT backbone as the image-conditioned branch and training a parallel text-conditioned branch from scratch. It departs from the single-modality paradigm that dominates native 3D generation (TRELLIS, UniLat3D, Hunyuan3D, Step1X-3D) by introducing joint text-image conditioning, and distinguishes itself from the closest prior work—TICD (SDS-based) and FlexGen (2D multi-view)—by operating on native 3D representations. The cross-modal bridge design borrows the zero-initialized linear projection idea from ControlNet, adapted here for bidirectional feature exchange between two full DiT backbones rather than a single trunk and a control adapter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing text-image conditioned 3D generation and showing that even naive velocity averaging outperforms both single-modality baselines by a wide margin, the paper opens a clear research direction: the complementarity between pixel-grounded visual cues and abstract semantic text is underexploited in 3D generation. The minimalist TIGON baseline (zero-init bridges + simple averaging) is practical and easy to extend, but the evaluation is limited to single-object generation on ~4K and ~1K benchmarks, leaving open whether the complementarity and the sufficiency of simple fusion persist for multi-object scenes, articulated objects, or higher-fidelity PBR materials. A key open question is whether the observed gains come primarily from the early-fusion bridges or would also emerge with stronger single-modality backbones, and whether more principled fusion (e.g., learned per-region weighting) could further close the gap to oracle multi-view conditioning.
