## Problem

Inverse design of 3D metamaterial microstructures typically relies on domain expertise, iterative simulations, and hand-crafted design spaces, limiting accessibility and design diversity. While data-driven methods have accelerated exploration, most still depend on expert-defined numerical conditions rather than natural language, and existing text-to-structure approaches lack end-to-end 3D generation with explicit physical property control. This paper tackles the gap of generating physically plausible, semantically consistent microstructures directly from free-form textual descriptions that encode both structural semantics and quantitative material properties.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language prompt describing the desired microstructure, optionally augmented with quantitative physical properties (Young's modulus, isotropy index, volume fraction) rendered as descriptive text. During training, each physical property is independently masked with probability r_p, so the model learns to handle fully specified, partially specified, and property-free prompts. The output is a 3D voxel grid representing material occupancy, generated by a U-Net-like 3D denoising network; no mesh or surface extraction is performed.

## Method

PropDiff-TMG builds on a self-conditional DDPM framework where a 3D U-Net denoises a noisy voxel grid, recursively incorporating its own previous prediction as auxiliary input (replaced by zero with 50% probability to limit error accumulation). Physical properties are encoded as augmented text and injected into intermediate denoising features via FiLM affine modulation, enabling fine-grained, property-aware generation without separate conditioning branches. A dual alignment strategy enforces consistency: during pre-training, a symmetric contrastive loss aligns a text encoder and a 3D visual encoder by matching cross-modal similarity logits to averaged intra-modal similarity distributions; at test time, a reward-guided sampling loop iteratively edits local voxel regions using a composite reward combining CLIP text-structure similarity and a 3D discriminator authenticity score, with batch-level normalization to balance the two terms.

## Results

On Geometries 2000, PropDiff-TMG achieves 91.0% classification accuracy, a CLIP score of 0.6936, and a Chamfer Distance of 0.0395, outperforming Txt2Microstruct-Net (86.95% accuracy, CLIP 0.5599, CD 0.0932) and the diffusion baseline (89.59% accuracy, CLIP 0.5856, CD 0.0694). The R²-squared for property prediction reaches 0.961/0.928/0.956 (Young's modulus / anisotropy / volume fraction), substantially exceeding Txt2Microstruct-Net's 0.773/0.795/0.771. On the larger GenText-Microstruct dataset (14K+ samples), PropDiff-TMG reduces FID from 84.46 to 47.74 and CLIP from 0.3281 to 0.6463 relative to the property-prompt diffusion baseline [53].

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Txt2Microstruct-Net [61] (a VAE-based text-to-voxel method), PropDiff-TMG wins on every metric: accuracy improves from 86.95% to 91.0%, CLIP score rises from 0.5599 to 0.6936, Chamfer Distance drops from 0.0932 to 0.0395, and R²-squared for all three physical properties increases by roughly 0.15–0.19. Against the property-prompt diffusion baseline [53] on GenText-Microstruct, PropDiff-TMG nearly halves FID (47.74 vs. 84.46), more than doubles the CLIP score (0.6463 vs. 0.3281), and reduces CD by 34% (0.0442 vs. 0.0666), indicating that the dual alignment strategy and FiLM-based property conditioning provide substantial gains over simply appending property values to the text prompt. The internal diffusion baseline (without contrastive pre-alignment) shows a much higher FID of 186.54 on Geometries 2000, confirming that the contrastive text-structure pre-training is critical for distributional fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based generative modeling for materials and text-conditioned 3D generation. It builds on self-conditional DDPM [7] and FiLM conditioning [34] from the broader diffusion literature, and extends property-conditioned diffusion for metamaterials [53] by replacing numerical property vectors with natural-language descriptions augmented by quantitative physical properties. The dual alignment strategy combines CLIP-style contrastive pre-training (in the lineage of [37, 5, 33]) with a test-time reward-guided local-editing loop, a combination not previously applied to microstructure generation. The closest prior work, Txt2Microstruct-Net [61], uses a VAE with a CLIP alignment module but requires multi-stage training and lacks explicit physical property conditioning or reward-guided refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making natural language the primary design interface for metamaterial microstructures, this work lowers the barrier for non-experts to explore the microstructural design space and could accelerate iterative design workflows in aerospace, biomedical, and energy applications. However, several limitations temper the impact: the GenText-Microstruct dataset (14K samples) is still small, the voxel representation limits geometric fidelity, and the FEM validation is qualitative with acknowledged distortions in the generated auxetic structure. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-phase or functionally graded materials, whether the reward-guided editing loop generalizes beyond the tested property set (E, I, V_f), and how well generated structures perform under complex loading beyond uniaxial compression.
