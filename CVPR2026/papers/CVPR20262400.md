## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion-based dataset distillation methods either perform unguided denoising or rely on simple Euclidean mode guidance toward IPC centroids, which can cause generated samples to drift off the latent data manifold. No prior method explicitly constrains the denoising trajectory to remain manifold-faithful while preserving semantic consistency, leading to degraded fidelity and representativeness in the distilled dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a real dataset (e.g., ImageNette, ImageWoof, ImageNet-100) encoded into VAE latent space, discrete class labels, and a pre-trained DiT diffusion model. It produces a synthetic distilled dataset of 256×256 RGB images with a configurable IPC budget (10, 20, or 50 images per class), where the number of synthetic samples per class is determined by the hierarchical IPC coreset. The distilled dataset is then used to train classifiers (ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, ResNet-18) from scratch under a hard-label protocol, with no teacher information leakage.

## Method

ManifoldGD decomposes the conditional diffusion score into a marginal denoising term and a mode guidance term, then corrects the mode guidance by projecting it onto the local tangent space of an estimated diffusion manifold. IPC centroids are selected via divisive (bisecting k-means) hierarchical clustering on class-wise VAE latents, with a level-wise sweep from a chosen start depth to the root to capture both coarse semantic modes and fine intra-class variability. At each denoising timestep t, the neighborhood of each IPC centroid is forward-diffused with Gaussian noise to construct a noisy local manifold patch, from which tangent and normal subspaces are estimated via PCA on K nearest neighbors. The mode guidance vector is then corrected by subtracting its normal-space projection, yielding a manifold-aligned update that preserves semantic attraction while preventing off-manifold drift. The full denoising step combines the pretrained score function with this manifold-corrected guidance, and an exponential annealing schedule on the neighborhood radius progressively tightens the local geometric constraint as diffusion proceeds toward clean samples.

## Results

On ImageNette (ResNetAP-10, hard-label protocol), ManifoldGD achieves 64.1% accuracy at IPC=10, 69.7% at IPC=20, and 78.4% at IPC=50, outperforming the closest training-free baseline MGD by +2.2, +3.2, and +1.4 points respectively. On ImageNet-100, ManifoldGD reaches 27.6% (IPC=10) and 35.3% (IPC=20), surpassing MGD by +1.5 and +2.1 points. ManifoldGD also attains the lowest FID, ℓ₂ distance, and MMD among all compared methods, confirming superior distributional fidelity. On ImageWoof, gains over MGD range from +0.5 to +2.0 points across IPCs 10–100 and all three classifiers (ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, ResNet-18).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManifoldGD consistently outperforms all training-free baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. Against MGD (the closest training-free competitor), gains are +2.2 to +3.2 points on ImageNette, +1.5 to +2.1 on ImageNet-100, and +0.5 to +2.0 on ImageWoof. Against DiT (unguided diffusion), improvements are larger: +5.0 to +10.6 points on ImageNette and +4.4 to +6.9 on ImageNet-100. Notably, ManifoldGD also surpasses or matches training-based methods: it exceeds MinMaxDiff by +2.1 to +2.9 points on ImageNette and by +2.8 to +3.0 on ImageNet-100, and outperforms D4M and DM at all IPCs. In distributional metrics, ManifoldGD achieves the lowest FID (e.g., ~15% lower than MGD at IPC=10 on ImageNette), the smallest ℓ₂ and MMD distances, and the highest representativeness and diversity scores, confirming that manifold-constrained guidance yields both better semantic coverage and tighter alignment with the real data distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

ManifoldGD extends Mode-Guided Diffusion (MGD) [37], which introduced Euclidean attraction toward IPC centroids as a training-free guidance mechanism for diffusion-based dataset distillation. The key departure is the addition of a manifold-consistent correction: the mode-alignment vector is projected onto the local tangent space of an estimated diffusion manifold, preventing the off-manifold drift that unconstrained Euclidean guidance induces. The IPC selection itself is upgraded from flat k-means to a hierarchical divisive (bisecting k-means) clustering that yields a multi-scale coreset spanning coarse semantic modes to fine intra-class variability. Conceptually, the tangent-space projection draws on manifold-constrained guidance ideas from CFG++ [10] and Manifold Preserving Guided Diffusion [17], but applies them specifically to the dataset distillation setting with time-dependent local manifold patches constructed from forward-diffused IPC neighborhoods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ManifoldGD establishes that geometry-aware guidance (tangent-space projection) can close much of the gap between training-free and training-based distillation without any fine-tuning, which is practically significant for settings where retraining the generator is infeasible. The consistent gains across three datasets, three classifier architectures, multiple IPC budgets, and three kernel functions suggest the manifold correction is a robust, general-purpose component rather than a dataset-specific hack. However, the paper acknowledges that at high noise levels the tangent estimates become unreliable, and low-rank approximations risk over-smoothing on highly curved manifolds; a formal analysis of projection error and curvature sensitivity remains open. The modest absolute gains (+1.4 to +3.2 accuracy over MGD) also suggest that the remaining gap to full-data performance is not primarily a guidance-geometry problem.
