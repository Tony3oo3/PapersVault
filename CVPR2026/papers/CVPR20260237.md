## Problem

Open model repositories enable free-riders to download fine-tuned checkpoints and merge them via parameter-level composition (e.g., Task Arithmetic, TIES) to inherit specialized capabilities without authorization or training cost. Existing proactive defenses like PaRaMS rely solely on deterministic parameter rearrangement, which is insufficient to fully disrupt merging compatibility across diverse architectures and merging strategies, leaving a gap in robust, geometry-aware protection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fine-tuned model's full parameter tensor θ_def, the corresponding pretrained base θ_pre, and (for Stage 1 only) the original task training data to perform L2-regularized finetuning. Stage 2 is entirely training-free, requiring only the two weight tensors and two hyperparameters (k′=10% critical layers, k=0.1 perturbation fraction). The output is a single protected weight tensor θ̂_def that retains near-original standalone accuracy (<1.5% drop) but causes any merged model to collapse, with specific exclusions for embedding/norm/lm_head layers in LLMs to prevent instability.

## Method

MERGEGUARD is a dual-stage defense that reshapes the parameter geometry of a fine-tuned model to break merging compatibility. Stage 1 (Density-Aware Finetuning) retrains the model with an added layer-wise L2 penalty, dispersing task-critical information from a few high-magnitude weights into a broad, isotropic distribution across all layers, which flattens the local loss basin curvature. Stage 2 (Adversarial Weight Negation) is training-free: it identifies the top k′% most critical layers via masking ablation, constructs a binary mask M that preserves those layers and the least-significant parameters while targeting the intermediate k(1−k′)% range, then subtracts β·M⊙(θ′_def − θ_pre) from the weights. This operation rotates the task vector away from the shared linear subspace assumed by merging algorithms, increasing the destructive interference term ΔL_merge ≈ λ₁λ₂‖τ′_def‖‖τ_fr‖(1−cos φ) so that merged parameters land outside the compatible basin, while the standalone model remains functional because the perturbation is distributed across many small weights.

## Results

On image classification (ViT-L-14), MERGEGUARD preserves standalone accuracy with less than 1.5% drop (e.g., GTSRB: 99.24% → 98.25%, MNIST: 99.69% → 99.27%) while reducing merged-model accuracy by up to 90% (GTSRB: 86.78% → 12.91%, MNIST: 98.94% → 11.35% under Task Arithmetic). On LLMs, post-merge accuracy collapses to single digits: Gemma2 drops from 69.6% to 1.52% on GSM8K and from 64.02% to 21.34% on HumanEval; Mistral with TA drops from 75.7% to 0% on GSM8K. The average merged-model accuracy drop across 8 image classification datasets is 52.11 percentage points for MERGEGUARD versus 30.76 for the PaRaMS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaRaMS [18] is the sole proactive defense baseline, employing parameter rearrangement and random multi-head scaling. MERGEGUARD substantially outperforms PaRaMS in merged-model degradation: the average accuracy drop is 52.11 vs. 30.76 percentage points across 8 image classification datasets. The gap is most dramatic on GTSRB (12.91% vs. 52.25%) and MNIST (11.35% vs. 96.02%), where PaRaMS barely impairs the merged model on MNIST. On the standalone model, PaRaMS preserves accuracy perfectly (no drop) while MERGEGUARD incurs a small loss (e.g., Cars: 92.39% → 88.29%, EuroSAT: 99.81% → 95.46%), though all drops remain under 1.5% on most datasets. The paper also evaluates two adaptive attacks (UNMASK and GRADERASE) that partially recover performance to 64.64% and 51.77% average accuracy respectively, but both remain well below the unprotected merged model's 84.47%, confirming MERGEGUARD's robustness under adversarial settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the second proactive defense against unauthorized model merging, following PaRaMS [18], which relies on deterministic parameter rearrangement and random multi-head scaling. Conceptually, MERGEGUARD inverts the sparsification principle that merging techniques (TIES, DARE, AdaMerging) exploit to reduce cross-task interference: rather than concentrating task information in sparse, high-magnitude weights, it deliberately disperses it via L2-regularized training and then injects structured perturbations to misalign task subspaces. The theoretical framing draws on loss-landscape geometry (Hessian curvature, shared basin assumptions) and task-vector theory, but the method itself is a repurposing of standard regularization and masked subtraction in a defensive context rather than a departure from a specific prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open model repositories (Hugging Face, ModelScope) make fine-tuned checkpoints freely downloadable, unauthorized merging poses a concrete intellectual-property threat that existing licensing mechanisms cannot enforce at the parameter level. MERGEGUARD's practical appeal lies in its simplicity (fixed hyperparameters across architectures, no task-specific tuning) and its robustness to two adaptive attacks, though the UNMASK attack recovers average accuracy to ~65% (vs. 84% unprotected), indicating the defense is not airtight. Key limitations include the exclusion of PEFT-based merging (LoRA, shallow tuning), the requirement for retraining in Stage 1, and the fact that the defense is evaluated only against merging with a single free-rider model from the same pretrained source. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to iterative multi-model merging, whether more sophisticated adaptive attacks (e.g., learned un-merging) can fully neutralize it, and how it interacts with emerging PEFT-compatible merging paradigms.
