## Problem

Unified multimodal models that jointly handle understanding and generation still operate in a single pass, producing one output without mechanisms for self-evaluation or iterative refinement. While test-time scaling via extended chain-of-thought reasoning has proven effective for text-only LLMs, extending this paradigm to unified multimodal models—where generation, verification, and editing capabilities are scattered across specialized architectures—remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260329/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts (compositional generation instructions or sequential editing commands) and optionally an input image for editing tasks. At inference, a computational budget C (1–10 image generation rounds, C=4 for multi-turn editing) controls how many iterative refinement cycles the model performs. Each round produces interleaved textual chain-of-thought reasoning (verification, subgoal planning, editing instructions) followed by a generated or edited image, with the final image as the output. For multi-turn editing, C refinement rounds are applied independently to each of three sequential user turns.

## Method

UniT integrates three components: (1) an agentic data synthesis pipeline where Flux Pro generates initial images, Qwen3-VL performs verification and subgoal decomposition via explicit thinking tokens, and Flux Kontext/Qwen-Image-Edit applies refinements iteratively, producing ~12K multi-round trajectories exhibiting verification, subgoal decomposition, and content memory; (2) supervised fine-tuning of the Bagel unified multimodal model on these trajectories for 700 H100 hours, with 10% of intermediate editing instructions masked to simulate user prompts; (3) budget forcing at inference that suppresses EOS to extend reasoning or truncates to enforce a fixed number of image generation rounds. A key finding is that sequential chain-of-thought scaling (iterative refinement building on prior outputs) outperforms parallel best-of-N sampling by 2.5× in compute efficiency, and models trained on 3.6-round trajectories generalize to 4.7-round inference chains. Inference uses nested classifier-free guidance—text CFG (scale 4.0) followed by image CFG (scale 2.0)—to independently control prompt adherence and visual consistency across multi-turn sequences.

## Results

On OneIG-Bench compositional generation, UniT achieves 0.843 overall alignment, outperforming Bagel (0.764) by 10.34% and Bagel+CoT (0.790) by 6.7%. On CompBench multi-object editing, UniT reaches 0.988 overall score, surpassing the strongest dedicated editing model FLUX.1 Kontext (0.965) and Bagel (0.936). On ImgEdit multi-turn editing, UniT scores 4.26/10 in human evaluation versus 1.31 for Bagel (225% relative improvement from C=1 to C=4). On MIRA visual reasoning, UniT achieves 11.5% accuracy, a 53.33% relative gain over its C=1 baseline, though still below GPT-5 (16.5%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (13.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniT outperforms all unified-model baselines across every benchmark: on OneIG-Bench it beats Janus-Pro (0.552) by 29.1%, BLIP3-o (0.706) by 19.4%, and its own base Bagel (0.764) by 10.3%. On CompBench multi-object editing, UniT (0.988) surpasses all dedicated editing systems including FLUX.1 Kontext (0.965), Step1X-Edit (0.844), GoT (0.668), AnyEdit (0.622), UltraEdit (0.609), SEED-X (0.595), Qwen-Image-Edit (0.782), and HQ-Edit (0.007). On ImgEdit multi-turn editing, UniT (4.26) more than doubles Bagel+CoT (1.92) and triples Bagel (1.31), with the largest gap in content memory (4.29 vs. 2.24) and version backtracking (3.48 vs. 0.84). On MIRA visual reasoning, UniT (11.5) improves over Bagel (7.5) and Bagel+CoT (9.2) but remains below the frontier VLMs GPT-5 (16.5) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (13.1), which the authors attribute to base-model scale differences rather than methodological limitations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only test-time scaling paradigm (budget forcing from s1 [45], iterative refinement from self-refine [43]) to unified multimodal models that interleave text and image generation. It builds directly on Bagel [15] as the base unified architecture, fine-tuning it on ~12K agentic multi-round trajectories synthesized by a multi-model pipeline (Flux Pro + Qwen3-VL + Flux Kontext). The key departure from prior multimodal chain-of-thought work (GoT [18], Uni-CoT [49], interleaved reasoning [26]) is that those methods operate in single-pass or single-round settings, whereas UniT explicitly studies compute scaling across multiple iterative refinement rounds and demonstrates beyond-training generalization in the multimodal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that multimodal test-time scaling is a viable paradigm benefiting both generation and understanding simultaneously, with the notable finding that training on generation/editing trajectories improves out-of-distribution visual reasoning (MIRA +53.33%). The beyond-training generalization (3.6 → 4.7 rounds) and the 2.5× compute efficiency of sequential over parallel scaling provide practical guidance for deploying unified models under variable latency budgets. However, absolute performance remains bounded by the base model (Bagel scores 11.5 on MIRA vs. GPT-5's 16.5), and the framework's scalability to video, audio, or larger unified architectures remains untested. Open questions include whether the agentic data synthesis pipeline can be replaced by self-play or RL, and whether the nested CFG strategy generalizes beyond the specific diffusion-based unified architecture used here.
