## Problem

Existing unsupervised concept extraction methods either extract only object-level concepts or extract intrinsic (object + attribute) concepts without ensuring composability, because no constraints are imposed on the concept embedding space. This lack of composability makes the extracted concepts poorly interpretable and limits the ability to reconstruct or explain the original image content through concept combination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262520/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single RGB image containing N objects each with M attributes, the method produces (M+1)·N concept tokens and their corresponding hyperbolic embedding vectors, partitioned into N object-level tokens and N·M attribute-level tokens. The embeddings are constrained to lie on a horosphere submanifold within the Poincaré ball, ensuring that vector addition of individual concept embeddings reconstructs the composite concept (satisfying Proposition 1). Object masks and anchor text descriptions are obtained via a preliminary segmentation stage before concept learning begins.

## Method

HyperExpress operates in hyperbolic space and consists of two stages. In concept learning, a Hyperbolic Contrastive Learning (HCL) module uses triplet loss in the Poincaré ball to hierarchically separate object-level from attribute-level concepts, while a Hyperbolic Entailment Learning (HEL) module enforces associative relationships by constraining attribute embeddings to fall within the entailment cone of their parent object (computed in the Lorentz model for tractable angle/radius formulas). In concept-wise optimization, a Horosphere Projection (HP) module finds n geodesic directions that maximize post-projection variance, then projects all concept embeddings onto the resulting zero-curvature horosphere submanifold; the isometry of this projection (Proposition 2) preserves inter-concept distances and thus the learned hierarchy, while the Euclidean-like structure of the horosphere enables native vector addition for compositional reconstruction.

## Results

On UCEBench, HyperExpress achieves the highest concept disentanglement accuracy, reaching 50.4% ACC¹ and 73.6% ACC³, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: AutoConcept at 35.0% ACC¹ and 52.0% ACC³). However, it trails ICE on reconstruction fidelity, scoring 0.699 SIM^I and 0.786 SIM^C versus ICE's 0.738 and 0.822. On ICBench, HyperExpress outperforms ICE across all six intrinsic-concept metrics (e.g., 0.280 vs. 0.249 on SIM^T-T object; 0.305 vs. 0.264 on SIM^V-V object), confirming better alignment of extracted concepts with human-interpretable descriptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperExpress decisively wins on disentanglement accuracy: it surpasses AutoConcept (the strongest prior method on ACC) by 15.4 points on ACC¹ (50.4% vs. 35.0%) and 21.6 points on ACC³ (73.6% vs. 52.0%), and beats ICE by 17.9 and 21.8 points respectively. On reconstruction similarity (SIM^I and SIM^C), ICE remains the best method (0.738 / 0.822), with HyperExpress trailing by 3.9 and 3.6 points; the authors attribute this gap to the compositional constraints imposed on the embedding space. Against Break-A-Scene and ConceptExpress, HyperExpress leads on all four UCEBench metrics. On ICBench, HyperExpress outperforms ICE on every intrinsic-concept metric (object, material, color × text-text and vision-text), with the largest margin on object-level SIM^V-V (0.305 vs. 0.264, +4.1 points), indicating that the hyperbolic entailment structure yields concepts more faithful to the actual image content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on ICE [5] for the initial single-image concept extraction pipeline (object localization, mask generation, and anchor text descriptions) and on CCE [41] for the principle that composable concepts should satisfy linear superposition in embedding space. The key departure is the move from Euclidean to hyperbolic geometry: the authors adapt HoroPCA's horospherical projection [6] to enforce composability while using hyperbolic contrastive and entailment losses to capture the hierarchical and associative structure between object-level and attribute-level concepts—something Euclidean methods cannot express. Unlike CCE, which requires multiple images per concept, HyperExpress operates on a single image, and unlike ICE, it explicitly constrains the embedding space for composability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing unsupervised concept extraction methods produce concepts that cannot be reliably recombined to reconstruct the original image, undermining interpretability and controllability of diffusion models. The hyperbolic formulation is a natural fit for the object-attribute hierarchy, and the isometry property of horospherical projection (Proposition 2) elegantly guarantees that composability constraints do not disrupt learned hierarchical relationships. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (D1) with only one direct intrinsic-concept baseline (ICE), and the method trades some reconstruction fidelity (SIM^I: 0.699 vs. ICE's 0.738) for substantially better composability (ACC^1: 0.504 vs. 0.325). Open questions include scalability to scenes with many objects and diverse attribute types, and whether the hyperbolic approach generalizes beyond the specific diffusion backbone tested.
