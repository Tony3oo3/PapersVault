## Problem

Existing fine-grained cross-modal alignment methods rely on text-guided cross-attention to aggregate visual patches into semantic regions before matching with words. This paradigm introduces two critical issues: redundant patch-word correlations that dilute critical semantic information with noise from irrelevant patches, and high computational cost from computing alignment weights between every text query and all visual patches, which becomes prohibitive for large-scale retrieval with millions of images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261776/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (224×224 or 384×384) and a text caption as input. The image is encoded by a ViT or Swin Transformer into a grid of patch tokens (14×14, 24×24, 7×7, or 12×12 depending on backbone and resolution), while the text is encoded by BERT or CLIP text encoder into word-level features. The output is a sparse region-word similarity matrix of size N_q×L (8 regions × text length) and a scalar image-text alignment score computed via max-sum pooling, used for bidirectional retrieval.

## Method

CoV-Align performs fine-grained alignment through a text-free region aggregation pipeline. First, a Coarse Visual Semantic Feature Extractor (CVSFE) uses Deformable Attention with 8 learnable region queries to attend to sparse semantically relevant spatial locations, followed by a Consistent Assign Attention that applies a shared projection matrix to enforce semantic consistency across attention distributions, combined with a dynamic threshold mask (σ=0.7) that prunes patches with low similarity scores. Second, a Cohesive and Discriminative Feature Optimization (CDFO) refines region representations via two complementary losses: a Spatial Concentration Loss that penalizes attention weights deviating from each region's computed centroid, and a Visual Contrastive Loss that pushes different region features apart in a shared embedding space. Finally, a Sparse Region-Word Alignment module computes cosine similarity between the 8 optimized region features and word features, scored with max-sum pooling. The overall training objective combines bidirectional contrastive retrieval loss with the spatial concentration and visual contrastive losses, achieving 3–5× speedup over text-guided methods like LAPS while improving RSum by up to 11.7 points.

## Results

On Flickr30K with Swin-Base-384, CoV-Align achieves an RSUM of 557.0, surpassing the strongest fine-grained baseline LAPS (545.3) by 11.7 points and AVSE (548.2) by 8.8 points. On MS-COCO 5K with the same backbone, CoV-Align reaches 481.9 RSUM versus LAPS's 470.1 and AVSE's 474.0. When integrated into CLIP-ViT-L/14, CoV-Align attains 491.9 RSUM on MS-COCO 5K, outperforming LG-MGC (465.3) by 26.6 points. In terms of efficiency, CoV-Align achieves a 3× speedup over LAPS (114.0s vs. 442.2s on COCO) and uses 49% less GPU memory (25G vs. 49G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoV-Align consistently outperforms all fine-grained Transformer-based baselines across every backbone configuration. Against LAPS [10] (the strongest text-guided fine-grained method), CoV-Align gains 8.7 RSUM points on Flickr30K with ViT-224, 12.8 with ViT-384, 5.0 with Swin-224, and 11.7 with Swin-384, with the margin widening at higher resolutions. Against AVSE [22], the closest competitor, CoV-Align leads by 3.7 (ViT-224), 9.0 (ViT-384), 2.6 (Swin-224), and 8.8 (Swin-384) RSUM points on Flickr30K. Detector-based methods (TGDT [21], CHAN [23], HREM [9], NUIF [33]) are all surpassed by a wide margin (e.g., 557.0 vs. 535.9 for NUIF on Flickr30K). Among CLIP fine-tuning variants, CoV-Align (491.9 RSUM on COCO 5K with ViT-L/14) exceeds LG-MGC [32] by 26.6 points and fine-tuned CLIP [24] by 25.9 points, while remaining competitive with VLP models like BLIP [18] (512.9) and ALBEF [17] (504.6) under the same evaluation protocol. In efficiency, CoV-Align achieves the lowest FLOPs (2289.22G) and GPU memory (25G) among all compared fine-grained methods, versus 2577.61G and 49G for LAPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoV-Align departs from the dominant text-guided aggregation paradigm (LAPS, SPARC) that uses text embeddings to direct which visual patches to aggregate before alignment. Instead, it repurposes deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) and introduces a consistent assign attention with dynamic masking to perform text-free region grouping, then applies a sparse region-word alignment. The dual-encoder backbone follows the CLIP/ViT lineage, and the max-sum pooling alignment strategy inherits from CHAN, but the core architectural shift—decoupling region generation from text—distinguishes it from all prior fine-grained methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3–5× inference speedup with equal or better retrieval accuracy makes fine-grained alignment practically viable for large-scale retrieval systems where text-guided cross-attention is a bottleneck. The text-free aggregation paradigm could transfer to other multimodal tasks (VQA, captioning) where per-query text conditioning is expensive. Open questions include whether the fixed region count (Nq=8) and manual threshold (σ=0.7) can be made adaptive, and whether the approach generalizes beyond retrieval to generative or reasoning tasks. The paper does not explore multi-image or video settings, limiting the scope of the efficiency claims.
