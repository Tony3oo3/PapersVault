## Problem

Mobile thermal sensors are inherently low-resolution due to miniaturization constraints, producing blurry images that lack structural detail. Existing RGB-guided thermal super-resolution methods rely on pixel-level camera calibration, which is laborious, fragile to parallax and temporal misalignment, and impractical for real-world multi-camera deployment on smartphones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 64×64 low-resolution thermal image and an uncalibrated 512×512 RGB reference image as input, producing a 512×512 high-resolution thermal image (8× upscaling). Text prompts are automatically generated from the RGB image via the RAM tagging model to provide semantic guidance to the diffusion process. The RGB input is intentionally unaligned with the thermal image, simulating real-world multi-camera parallax and capture desynchronization.

## Method

3M-TI builds on SD-Turbo (one-step latent-space diffusion) and introduces a Cross-Modal Self-Attention (CSM) module that replaces the original self-attention layers in the diffusion UNet. The key mechanism concatenates RGB and thermal VAE latents into a single token sequence of length M×H×W, so that standard self-attention computes pairwise dependencies across both modalities simultaneously, enabling content-adaptive cross-modal alignment without explicit registration. A misalignment augmentation strategy applies translation, scaling, rotation, and perspective warping to RGB inputs during training, forcing the CSM to learn robust correspondences under geometric and temporal offsets. Zero-initialized skip connections from encoder to decoder preserve structural consistency, and LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16 for UNet, rank 4 for VAE decoder) keeps the parameter budget small while adapting the pretrained generative prior to thermal reconstruction.

## Results

On the combined public test set (IRVI, LLVIP, M³FD, PBVS 2025), 3M-TI with misalignment augmentation achieves the best perceptual scores: LPIPS 0.1787, MANIQA 0.4443, and MUSIQ 36.66, while maintaining competitive fidelity (PSNR 30.09, SSIM 0.8610). On the real-world smartphone dataset (300 uncalibrated pairs), 3M-TI attains the highest no-reference scores (MUSIQ 30.62, MANIQA 0.3589), outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. For downstream detection (Grounded-SAM, zero-shot), 3M-TI-enhanced thermal images reach F1-score 0.4724 and IoU 0.3427, surpassing the RGB reference (F1 0.4643, IoU 0.3359) and approaching GT thermal performance (F1 0.4887, IoU 0.3494).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3M-TI decisively outperforms all non-diffusion RGB-guided baselines (CoReFusion, CoRPLE, SwinFuSR, SwinPaste) on perceptual metrics: LPIPS improves from 0.3075–0.3214 to 0.1787, and MANIQA jumps from 0.2719–0.2833 to 0.4443, while retaining competitive PSNR (30.09 vs. CoRPLE's 30.47). Against diffusion-based methods without RGB guidance (SeeSR, OSEDiff), 3M-TI wins on both fidelity (PSNR 30.09 vs. 29.41/28.05) and perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.1787 vs. 0.1828/0.2113), demonstrating that cross-modal RGB guidance complements the diffusion prior. Compared to the thermal-specific diffusion method DifIISR, 3M-TI achieves substantially better PSNR (30.09 vs. 27.48), SSIM (0.8610 vs. 0.7905), and LPIPS (0.1787 vs. 0.3484), though DifIISR edges out by 0.08 on MUSIQ (36.74 vs. 36.66). The misalignment augmentation provides the largest relative gain for 3M-TI (LPIPS 0.1917→0.1787, MANIQA 0.4265→0.4443) while yielding only marginal improvements for baselines, confirming that the cross-modal self-attention module specifically benefits from learning robust correspondences under geometric perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SD-Turbo (one-step adversarial diffusion distillation) as its generative backbone and repurposes the multi-view/video diffusion paradigm—where multiple views are jointly processed through shared self-attention—for cross-modal RGB-thermal fusion in the VAE latent space. It departs from prior RGB-guided thermal SR methods (CoReFusion, SwinFuSR, SwinPaste) that assume pixel-aligned, calibrated camera pairs by introducing a calibration-free cross-modal self-attention module (CSM) and a misalignment augmentation strategy that trains the model to handle uncalibrated, unsynchronized captures. The use of LoRA for efficient fine-tuning of both the UNet and VAE decoder, combined with RAM-generated text prompts for semantic guidance, positions the work at the intersection of efficient diffusion adaptation and multi-modal image restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling high-quality thermal super-resolution on commodity mobile hardware (a sub-$100 thermal module + smartphone) without the laborious and fragile cross-camera calibration that has hindered real-world deployment of RGB-guided thermal SR. The downstream task gains (detection F1 approaching GT thermal, segmentation outperforming RGB in low-light) demonstrate that the enhanced thermal images carry actionable semantic information, not just perceptual quality. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution thermal sensors, whether the heavy reliance on a large pretrained diffusion model (SD-Turbo) limits edge deployment, and how performance degrades under more extreme misalignment or domain shifts (e.g., different thermal sensor types).
