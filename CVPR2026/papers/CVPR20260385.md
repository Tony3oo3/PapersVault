## Problem

Splat-based CT reconstruction (R²-Gaussian) produces distinctive streak and strip artifacts on real cone-beam acquisitions that are far more severe than in conventional FDK or iterative methods, even under sparse-view conditions. The paper identifies that these artifacts originate primarily from pose inaccuracies in the acquisition geometry rather than from view sparsity itself, and that the anisotropic Gaussian weighting in the splatting formulation makes the reconstruction inherently more sensitive to geometric misalignment than voxel-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260385/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse-view cone-beam X-ray projections (typically 75 views, evaluated down to 25) along with initial camera pose parameters (rotation as 4D quaternion, translation as 3D vector) and produces both a 3D attenuation volume represented as M anisotropic 3D Gaussians and calibrated pose corrections (small increments Δq_k, Δt_k per view). The volume is queried on a voxel grid after optimization, and the calibrated poses can be reused in other tomographic reconstruction pipelines. Projections are rendered via EWA splatting with a 3D ray-space covariance to simulate X-ray attenuation along the ray path, distinguishing it from the 2D projection used in RGB Gaussian splatting.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint optimization framework that simultaneously refines volumetric Gaussian parameters (centers, covariances, densities) and per-view pose corrections using a differentiable EWA splatting projection. Pose perturbations are parameterized as small increments (Δq_k, Δt_k) relative to the initial camera configuration, ensuring stable gradient flow under small-angle approximation and preventing gradient explosion. The loss combines L1 and SSIM terms between given and estimated projections, deliberately omitting total variation regularization, which the authors show suppresses essential geometric gradients needed for pose recovery. A key technical detail is the explicit backward gradient computation through both the perspective projection matrix P_k and the rotation matrix W_k, revealing how cross-derivative dependencies between pose and volume parameters were neglected in prior splat-based CT methods. The camera learning rate (2e-4, decaying to 2e-5 over 30k steps) is tuned separately from Gaussian parameters, and the entire pipeline runs faster than the R²-Gaussian baseline due to the absence of TV regularization.

## Results

On the noisy synthetic dataset (σ_rot=0.03, σ_trans=1.0), the method achieves mean PSNR of ~32.5 dB across 15 scenes, outperforming the R²-Gaussian baseline by 4–7 dB (e.g., 30.44 vs 26.69 on Chest, 40.48 vs 33.15 on Beetle). In terms of geometric calibration, the mean translation error is 0.726 AU and mean orientation error is 0.627°, substantially lower than NeAT (1.437 AU, 2.881°) and Thies et al. (2.463 AU, 4.076°). The method also achieves the lowest reconstruction time at 20.89 min, faster than the R²-Gaussian baseline (23.19 min) and significantly faster than NeAT (48.35 min) and Thies et al. (31.15 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the R²-Gaussian baseline [48], the proposed method matches performance on noise-free data (within ~0.5 dB) but gains 4–7 dB PSNR under geometric perturbations, confirming that the improvement comes from self-calibration rather than a better volumetric model. Compared to NeAT [30], the method reduces mean translation error from 1.437 to 0.726 AU and orientation error from 2.881° to 0.627°, while also achieving higher PSNR and faster reconstruction (20.89 vs 48.35 min). Against Thies et al. [39], the method exceeds PSNR by approximately 10 dB and reduces calibration errors by roughly 3× in translation and 6× in orientation. FDK [8] and SART [1] produce severe radial and structured artifacts under 75-view sparse sampling and are not competitive. SAX-NeRF [4] suppresses radial streaks but over-smooths fine details due to uncorrected calibration errors, while R²-Gaussian's TV regularization trades artifact suppression for loss of surface detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the splat-based CT reconstruction line initiated by R²-Gaussian [48] by incorporating joint geometric calibration, drawing on the quaternion-based pose increment formulation from self-calibrating Gaussian splatting [7]. It departs from prior joint calibration methods (NeAT [30], Thies et al. [39]) that rely on implicit neural representations or FDK-based pipelines, instead operating entirely within the differentiable EWA splatting framework. The key conceptual departure is a systematic analysis showing that pose sensitivity, not view sparsity, is the dominant artifact source in splat-based CT—a distinction that prior splat-based CT work had not isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that geometric misalignment (not sparsity) drives streak artifacts in splat-based CT reframes how practitioners should approach real-world sparse-view reconstruction, shifting emphasis from data augmentation or regularization to geometric self-calibration. The method is practically attractive: it is faster than the R²-Gaussian baseline (20.9 min vs. 23.2 min) due to omitting TV regularization, requires no pre-calibrated hardware, and produces reusable calibration parameters. However, the approach still exhibits needle-like artifacts under extreme sparsity (25 views), and the real-world validation is limited to a single object (walnut), leaving open whether the findings generalize to more complex clinical or industrial scanning scenarios with larger pose errors.
