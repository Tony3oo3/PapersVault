## Problem

Federated diffusion model training either lacks formal privacy guarantees (standard FL protocols remain vulnerable to gradient reconstruction and memorization attacks) or relies on DP-SGD, which degrades sample quality severely in high-dimensional image generation. Moreover, existing federated approaches train a single global generator with no client-specific control, leaving all clients exposed to extraction attacks and unable to tailor synthetic data to their own distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard Gaussian noise (x_T ~ N(0,I)) and a class label as conditioning input, and produces a synthetic RGB image through a two-stage denoising pipeline. Unusually, the global model never receives clean client images—only images that have been clipped and corrupted by t_0 steps of forward diffusion—so the server-side model operates exclusively on a privatized, coarsened representation. The final sample is assembled by first running the global denoiser for T steps and then applying the client's private denoiser for an additional t_0 steps to recover fine-grained, client-specific details.

## Method

PFDM splits the reverse diffusion process into a shared component and a personalized component. In Stage 1, each client trains a local denoiser on its private data, then applies t_0 steps of forward diffusion (with ℓ₂-clipping) to produce a noisy dataset that it uploads to the server; the diffusion depth t_0 acts as a tunable privacy knob, with larger t_0 injecting more noise and yielding stronger (ε,δ)-LDP guarantees. In Stage 2, the server trains a single global denoiser on the pooled noisy datasets, which captures cross-client structural patterns while being unable to reconstruct any individual client's data. At sampling time, the global denoiser generates a coarse intermediate image from pure noise, and the client's local denoiser refines it over t_0 additional steps to produce a final image reflecting both collaborative structure and client-specific detail. The paper proves that the per-sample privacy loss is bounded by a function of the effective noise variance σ² = (1−ᾱ_{t₀})/ᾱ_{t₀} and the clipping parameter C, and establishes a 2-Wasserstein utility bound on Gaussian mixtures that smoothly interpolates between the non-collaborative rate O(d²/n_k^m) and the centralized rate O(d²/N_k) as σ² varies.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, PFDM achieves an average FID of 27.82 (majority 19.85, minority 35.78), closely matching the non-collaborative baseline (28.16) while substantially outperforming it on minority classes. On CelebA, the improvement is more pronounced: PFDM reaches 23.10 average FID versus 32.40 for non-collaborative training, a 28% reduction driven by minority-class FID dropping from 41.38 to 28.09. On MNIST under ε=10, PFDM achieves an average FID of 6.96 compared to 33.73 for the DPDM federated baseline, a nearly 5× improvement. Privacy evaluations confirm near-random membership inference (AUC ≈ 50%), zero memorized samples, and reconstruction scores indicating no recognizable recovery of client data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-private FedDM baseline [95], PFDM trades a modest FID increase (e.g., 27.82 vs 18.60 average on CIFAR-10) for formal (ε,δ)-LDP guarantees, but the gap narrows substantially on minority classes where PFDM's personalization helps. Against the DPDM federated baseline [26] on MNIST (ε=10), PFDM dramatically outperforms with average FID 6.96 versus 33.73, showing that direct DP-SGD training of diffusion models in a federated setting is far less effective than the proposed split-denoising approach. Compared to the non-collaborative (private extreme) baseline, PFDM consistently improves FID across all three datasets, with the largest gains on minority classes (e.g., CelebA minority FID drops from 41.38 to 28.09, a 32% reduction). The method remains below the non-private centralized baseline in FID, as expected, but the gap is most pronounced on CIFAR-10 (27.82 vs 16.95) and smallest on Colorized MNIST (3.26 vs 1.66), reflecting the lower dimensionality and simpler structure of the latter dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DDPMs (Ho et al.) as the generative backbone and departs from prior federated diffusion work (FedDM, de Goede et al.) that trains a single global denoiser, by splitting the reverse diffusion process into a shared global component (trained on partially-diffused, clipped client data) and a personalized local component (trained on clean client data). The privacy mechanism is conceptually distinct from DP-SGD-based approaches (Sattarov et al., Dockhorn et al.): rather than adding calibrated noise to gradients, the forward diffusion process itself serves as the privacy channel, with the diffusion depth t₀ acting as the privacy knob. The personalization architecture echoes prior personalized FL frameworks (Mishchenko et al., Jothiraj and Mashhadi) but is applied specifically to the denoising trajectory, leveraging the coarse-to-fine structure of diffusion to separate generalizable cross-client features from client-specific details.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck: institutions needing a generative model for multiple downstream tasks without repeatedly consuming privacy budget or sharing raw data. The formal LDP guarantee (stronger than client-level central DP) and the clean utility interpolation between private and centralized extremes provide a principled privacy-utility trade-off that practitioners can tune via t₀. Key limitations include the utility analysis being restricted to Gaussian mixture models, the relatively large ε=10 (justified as LDP but still substantial), and the requirement that local models be sufficiently strong for the split to be effective. Open directions include extending the theory to general distributions, scaling to high-resolution generation, and handling strategic client behavior.
