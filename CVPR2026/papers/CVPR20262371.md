## Problem

Diffusion models alter their output relative to the input even when given explicit identity-preserving instructions (e.g., 'make no change'), yet no prior work has systematically quantified this no-op drift or revealed its structural dependence on noise strength. The paper asks whether this drift is a prompt-induced artifact or a fundamental property of the decoder architecture, and whether it can be captured by a single model-agnostic functional form.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262371/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each experiment takes an RGB image (512×512 for SD15/SD21/InstructPix2Pix, 1024×1024 for SDXL), a null or copy text prompt, and a scalar noise strength σ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}, and produces a drifted output image plus the squared L2 deviation ||I′−I₀||². The primary analytical output is the drift kernel K_M(σ) = E[||I′−I₀||² | σ], a function of σ fitted per model, alongside per-sample MSE, MAE, PSNR, and SSIM. All runs use 15 DDIM steps, guidance scale 5.0, and a fixed seed, so the only varying factor across the strength sweep is σ.

## Method

The paper introduces the Drift Kernel K_M(σ) and derives its quadratic form K_M(σ) ≈ k_M σ² + c_M via a first-order Taylor expansion of the decoder D around the mean latent: the noise ε ~ N(0, σ²I) is filtered through the decoder Jacobian J_D, so the expected squared deviation equals Tr(J_D J_D^T) σ² plus a strength-independent constant. This shows drift is governed by the decoder's geometry (its Jacobian trace) rather than the conditioning prompt, explaining why null and copy prompts produce nearly identical kernels (differences < 17%). Empirically, variance-driven models (SD15, SD21, SDXL) confirm the σ² scaling with aggregate R² = 0.97 across 120,000 baseline samples, while the instruction-tuned InstructPix2Pix exhibits a distinct flat, high-variance edit-driven regime where cross-attention conditioning introduces stochasticity decoupled from σ. Synthetic decoders with analytically controlled Jacobians (linear, curved, edit-biased) validate that the quadratic structure is mechanistic rather than dataset-specific, with the linear case recovering the ideal form at R² = 0.9999.

## Results

Across 120,000 baseline image pairs (10,000 images × 3 null prompts × 4 models), all four diffusion architectures exhibit measurable no-op drift: mean MSE ranges from 0.0052 (InstructPix2Pix) to 0.0085 (SDXL). Variance-driven models (SD15, SD21, SDXL) follow the quadratic drift kernel K_M(σ) ≈ k_M σ² + c_M with aggregate R² = 0.97, where k_M values are 0.0345, 0.0685, and 0.0710 respectively. Prompt wording has negligible effect: null and copy prompts produce drift kernel coefficients differing by less than 17% (SD15: 0.4%, SDXL: 8.5%, SD21: 17.0%). InstructPix2Pix exhibits a distinct edit-driven regime with flat mean drift but high variance (±0.0071), breaking the σ² scaling pattern.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four diffusion architectures under identical null-prompt conditions. Among variance-driven models, SDXL (2.6B params, 1024×1024) exhibits the highest drift (MSE=0.0085, k_M=0.0710), followed by SD21 (MSE=0.0069, k_M=0.0685), and SD15 (MSE=0.0059, k_M=0.0345), suggesting larger models amplify latent variance more. InstructPix2Pix achieves the lowest mean drift (MSE=0.0052) and highest SSIM (0.7802), but with the highest variance (±0.0071), indicating unpredictable, strength-independent editing behavior rather than the predictable σ² scaling seen in the other three models. All three variance-driven models show strong quadratic fits (per-model R² > 0.96 for kernel coefficients), while InstructPix2Pix's drift is flat across σ values (R²=0.059 for copy prompts), confirming two fundamentally different drift mechanisms. The prompt-independence analysis shows that switching from null prompts to detailed copy instructions changes k_M by only 0.4% (SD15), 8.5% (SDXL), and 17.0% (SD21), proving drift is structural rather than prompt-induced.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing 'no-op drift' as a measurable structural quantity (the Drift Kernel) rather than building on a specific prior method. It departs from identity-preservation work (Prompt-to-Prompt, Null-text inversion, SDEdit) by showing that these techniques reduce but do not eliminate drift, and from editing-faithfulness methods (FGS, CorrFill, Odo) by isolating the null-operation case where no edit is requested. The theoretical contribution repurposes the decoder Jacobian from general neural network sensitivity analysis, applying a first-order Taylor expansion to derive the σ² scaling law, while the empirical contribution is a large-scale measurement (120K pairs) that no prior work has attempted for this specific diagnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Drift Kernel provides a practical stability diagnostic (k_M) that could be used to predict identity drift before running a diffusion pipeline, which is relevant for safety-critical applications like medical imaging and forensic analysis. However, the evidence is somewhat limited: only four models, four strength values, and per-model R² of 0.10–0.26 (the aggregate R²=0.97 relies on pooling across models), and pixel-wise MSE misses severe visual collapse in low-texture domains. Open questions include whether the quadratic law holds at higher resolutions, in video/3D generation, and whether the kernel can be actively minimized during training or inference to enforce true identity preservation.
