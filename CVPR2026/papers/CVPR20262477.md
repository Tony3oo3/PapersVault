## Problem

Existing GUI trajectory synthesis pipelines, particularly reverse-task-synthesis methods like OS-GENESIS, over-sample trivial semantic-intuitive actions (over 70% of traces collapse into 'open menu' or 'tap back') while severely under-representing semantic-ambiguous actions whose meaning depends on context, sequence, or visual cues. Moreover, their one-shot instruction generation produces underspecified or misaligned descriptions that introduce noisy supervision, causing trained agents to plateau at superficial behaviors and fail to generalize to unseen workflows.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes GUI screenshots and optional accessibility (a11y) trees as state observations, with a VLM backbone (InternVL2-4B/8B or Qwen2-VL-7B) performing all exploration and refinement steps, and GPT-4o handling instruction synthesis and reward modeling. It outputs verified (instruction, execution-sequence) pairs that must achieve an action-level reconstruction recall of at least 0.7 through iterative replay, forming a 1K-trajectory SFT corpus. Only trajectories passing alignment verification are admitted; rejected trajectories still contribute their misalignment score as a hardness signal back into the search tree.

## Method

HATS unifies exploration and data verification in a single Hardness-Driven Monte Carlo Tree Search (HD-MCTS) loop with four stages: UCB-based selection, tree expansion, bounded-depth rollout, and backpropagation of an alignment-derived hardness reward. The key innovation is replacing the conventional task-return signal with a hardness reward r(A,B) = (R(A,B)+ε)^(-α), where R is the action-level reconstruction recall between a reference sub-trajectory and its replayed execution; this reward is backpropagated up the tree to bias future UCB selection toward semantically challenging, under-represented regions. The Alignment-Guided Refinement module performs a multi-round replay-refine loop (select sub-trajectory → synthesize instruction → execute → verify similarity → inject missing context and re-execute) until recall meets a threshold or a refinement budget is exhausted, replacing OS-GENESIS's one-shot instruction generation. All refinement sub-steps are implemented as prompt-driven VLM calls, and the final verified trajectories are used for two-stage SFT (planning-level and action-level) following the OS-GENESIS training paradigm.

## Results

On AndroidWorld, HATS achieves 22.60% overall success rate with InternVL2-4B, 24.35% with InternVL2-8B, and 24.35% with Qwen2-VL-7B, consistently outperforming all baselines across every task category (DLS, S&C, S&U, P&W). On WebArena, HATS reaches 20.60% (InternVL2-4B), 24.87% (InternVL2-8B), and 23.28% (Qwen2-VL-7B) overall success rate. The headline improvement over the closest baseline OS-GENESIS is 100% on AndroidWorld (22.60 vs. 11.30) and 215% on WebArena (20.60 vs. 6.53) for the 4B backbone. Gains are most pronounced in semantically complex categories such as Social & Communication (38.46% vs. 11.11% on S&C) and Productivity & Work (9.88% vs. 1.85% on P&W).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HATS outperforms all four baselines across every backbone and both benchmarks. Against OS-GENESIS (the closest competitor and the method HATS directly builds upon), HATS more than doubles the overall success rate on AndroidWorld (22.60% vs. 11.30% for InternVL2-4B; 24.35% vs. 14.81% for InternVL2-8B) and triples it on WebArena (20.60% vs. 6.53% for InternVL2-4B; 24.87% vs. 8.16% for InternVL2-8B). The largest relative gains appear in semantically ambiguous categories: on AndroidWorld S&C, HATS reaches 38.46% where OS-GENESIS achieves only 11.11% (InternVL2-4B) and 0.00% (Qwen2-VL-7B); on P&W, HATS scores 9.88% versus 1.85% for OS-GENESIS. Task-Driven and Task-Driven + Self-Instruct baselines are consistently weaker, with overall rates below 11% on AndroidWorld and below 13% on WebArena, confirming that manual goal specification and one-shot self-instruction produce less diverse and less aligned trajectories. The GPT-4o Zero-Shot reference (45.22% on AndroidWorld, 22.82% on WebArena) remains the strongest single-model baseline but relies on a much larger proprietary model, whereas HATS-trained 4B/7B open-source models close much of this gap through superior data quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HATS builds directly on OS-GENESIS [59], which established the reverse task synthesis paradigm for GUI agents (explore the UI first, then synthesize instructions from observed outcomes). The key departure is replacing OS-GENESIS's uniform random exploration with a Hardness-Driven MCTS that uses alignment-derived rewards as the exploration signal, and replacing its one-shot instruction generation with a multi-round replay-and-repair refinement loop. The closed-loop integration—where misalignment detected during refinement is backpropagated as a hardness reward to steer future exploration—distinguishes HATS from both task-driven pipelines (AutoWebGLM, Self-Instruct) and prior exploration-driven methods that treat data collection and quality control as separate offline stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies semantic ambiguity (context-dependent, sequentially-dependent, or visually-ambiguous UI actions) as the primary bottleneck preventing GUI agents from generalizing beyond trivial interactions, and provides a principled mechanism to target it during data synthesis. However, absolute success rates remain low (22.60% on AndroidWorld, 20.60% on WebArena), suggesting that data quality alone is insufficient and that model capacity, planning depth, or multi-app coordination remain open challenges. The computational overhead of multi-round VLM-based replay and refinement, while bounded by F_max, raises practical questions about scaling the pipeline to larger corpora or more complex multi-application workflows. Whether the hardness signal generalizes beyond the two tested environments and whether the closed-loop design can be extended to online or continual learning settings are important open questions.
