## Problem

Existing PTQ-based mixed-precision quantization for SR models relies on static statistics (e.g., activation standard deviation) to estimate layer-wise quantization sensitivity, which fails to capture actual reconstruction loss variations and inter-layer dependencies. Additionally, SR models typically remove batch normalization to preserve high-frequency details, causing activation ranges to fluctuate with input data and making fixed quantization ranges inadequate for accurate representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261251/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The quantization framework takes a pretrained FP32 SR model (EDSR, RDN, or SwinIR) and a small calibration set of 100 LR images sampled from DIV2K, requiring no ground-truth supervision. It outputs a quantized model with per-layer mixed bit-widths (base ± 1) for both weights and activations, which then maps LR input images to HR reconstructions at ×4 scaling. The entire quantization pipeline completes in 20–87 seconds depending on model size, compared to hours for QAT-based alternatives.

## Method

The proposed framework operates in three stages: (1) bound initialization estimates clipping ranges from calibration data; (2) Gradient-guided Bit Allocation (GBA) introduces a learnable continuous bit offset s_k per layer, computes the batch-averaged gradient of the reconstruction-plus-feature-alignment loss with respect to s_k using a straight-through estimator (round in forward, tanh in backward), ranks layers by gradient magnitude, and maps ranks to discrete offsets {−1, 0, 1} added to a base bit-width; (3) bit-aware fine-tuning optimizes quantization ranges with the now-fixed bit-widths. A Dynamic Activation Range Normalization (DAN) module performs per-channel min-max normalization to [−1, 1] before quantization and restores the original scale afterward, compensating for the absence of BN without introducing learnable parameters or the distributional distortions that BN causes in SR models.

## Results

On 4-bit quantization of EDSR×4, the proposed method (full MP on weights and activations) achieves 31.67 dB PSNR on Set5 and 25.61 dB on Urban100, outperforming the best PTQ baseline AdaBM by 0.48 dB and 0.25 dB respectively, while completing quantization in 26 s (vs. 50 s for AdaBM). On RDN×4 at 4-bit, the gains are larger: 25.87 dB on Urban100 (+2.43 dB over AdaBM) and 27.46 dB on BSD100 (+1.37 dB), with quantization time halved (87 s vs. 167 s). On the Transformer-based SwinIR×4 at 4-bit, the method reaches 32.15 dB on Set5 (+0.38 dB over 2DQuant) in 73 s versus 2 hours for 2DQuant. Against QAT-based methods (CADyQ, CABM) on EDSR, the PTQ-based approach matches or exceeds their quality (e.g., 25.70 vs. 25.38 dB on Urban100 at 4-bit) while being thousands of times faster (20 s vs. 16–30 hrs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two uniform-precision PTQ baselines, MinMax+FT (Jacob et al., CVPR 2018) and Percentile+FT (Li et al., CVPR 2019), the proposed method wins by large margins (e.g., +1.79 dB on Urban100 for EDSR 4-bit over MinMax+FT; Percentile+FT suffers catastrophic degradation, dropping to 21.96 dB on Urban100). PTQ4SR (Tu et al., CVPR 2023), which uses distribution-aware range adjustment plus fine-tuning, is outperformed by 0.49 dB on Urban100 (EDSR 4-bit) and 2.57 dB (RDN 4-bit). The closest PTQ competitor, AdaBM (Hong & Lee, CVPR 2024), which allocates bits by activation standard deviation, is beaten by 0.25 dB on Urban100 for EDSR 4-bit but by a much larger 2.43 dB for RDN 4-bit, where the absence of BN makes AdaBM's static range estimation particularly fragile. On the Transformer-based SwinIR, 2DQuant (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2024) is surpassed by 0.38 dB on Set5 and 0.02 dB on Urban100, while the proposed method runs in 73 s versus 2 hours. Against QAT-based MPQ methods CADyQ (Hong et al., ECCV 2022) and CABM (Tian et al., CVPR 2023), the PTQ-based approach achieves higher PSNR on all three test sets (e.g., +0.32 dB over CABM on Urban100 at 4-bit, +0.37 dB at 3-bit) while requiring only 20 s of calibration versus 16–30 hours of full retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTQ-based mixed-precision quantization (MPQ) line for super-resolution, building directly on the fine-tuning loss formulation from 2DQuant (NeurIPS 2024) and the calibration-based PTQ pipeline of PTQ4SR (CVPR 2023). Its key departure is from AdaBM (CVPR 2024), which estimates layer-wise quantization sensitivity via activation standard deviation; here, sensitivity is instead captured through gradients of the reconstruction loss with respect to learnable continuous bit-width parameters (via the straight-through estimator), enabling the method to account for inter-layer dependencies that static statistics miss. The Dynamic Activation Range Normalization (DAN) component addresses a problem specific to SR architectures that omit batch normalization, a gap not addressed by prior MPQ methods designed for classification or detection networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deploying SR models on edge and mobile hardware: the method achieves higher PSNR than both PTQ and QAT baselines while completing quantization in under a minute (vs. hours for QAT), making it a viable drop-in compression step. The gradient-guided bit allocation is a general principle that could transfer to other low-level vision tasks (denoising, deblurring) where BN is similarly absent and activation distributions are input-dependent. Open questions include whether the restricted ±1 bit offset around a base width is sufficient for extreme low-bit regimes (2-bit or below), and whether the approach extends to autoregressive or diffusion-based SR models where the quantization error propagation differs fundamentally.
