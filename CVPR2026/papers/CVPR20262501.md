## Problem

Foundation models for medical image segmentation (e.g., BiomedParse) produce fragmented false positives and near-zero specificity on out-of-distribution tumors due to non-separable visual embeddings under scanner, protocol, and modality shifts. Fine-tuning to correct this causes catastrophic forgetting on in-distribution organ segmentation, and existing test-time adaptation methods still generate numerous small false positives. The paper asks whether a frozen foundation segmentor can be made robust to OOD tumors without any parameter updates.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D medical image slice (CT or MR, axial or sagittal) and a free-form text prompt naming the tumor type (e.g., "bladder tumor"). The text prompt is consumed twice: by an LLM planner that produces a structured AnatomyPlan (anchor organs, ROI geometric rules, reasoning trace) and by the frozen BiomedParse segmentor as a segmentation prompt. The output is a binary tumor mask at native resolution; a three-level gating policy can override the output to an all-zero mask when no candidate passes statistical and geometric thresholds, explicitly handling tumor-free slices.

## Method

R²-Seg is a training-free, two-stage Reason-and-Reject pipeline wrapping a frozen BiomedParse segmentor. In the Reason stage, an LLM planner decomposes the tumor prompt into anchor organs and ROI instructions (padding δ, scale jitter set Γ, square enforcement); BiomedParse segments each anchor organ, and multi-scale square ROIs are constructed by dilating the union bounding box. In the Reject stage, the frozen segmentor performs multi-view TTA (identity, left-right, top-bottom flips) within each ROI with max-fusion, and connected components are extracted as tumor candidates. Each candidate's pixel-level intensity features are compared against the normal-organ mask features via an unbiased squared MMD two-sample test with a Gaussian kernel; permutation p-values are computed over B random shuffles, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control at level α retains only statistically significant candidates. A three-level false-positive gating policy (global existence gate using KS test and positive ratio, candidate-level area/probability/overlap constraints, and a case-level score threshold) further suppresses spurious detections in tumor-free slices, all without updating any model parameters.

## Results

On five OOD tumor types (bladder, uterus, prostate, breast, cervix), R²-Seg achieves the highest class-average accuracy (CA) across all datasets, ranging from 0.762 (bladder, breast) to 0.890 (prostate), compared to 0.546–0.686 for BiomedParse-LoRA and 0.546–0.632 for zero-shot BiomedParse. R²-Seg substantially improves specificity over both baselines (e.g., 0.587 vs. 0.434 for prostate; 0.728 vs. 0.520 for breast) while sacrificing some sensitivity, reflecting a deliberate calibration trade-off. On in-distribution CT tumors (liver, pancreas), R²-Seg yields 10–30% relative gains in Dice and CA over the frozen BiomedParse. FROC analysis shows R²-Seg retains >80% scan-level sensitivity at 10 false positives per scan under aggressive rejection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares R²-Seg against two baselines: zero-shot BiomedParse (lower bound) and BiomedParse-LoRA (low-rank fine-tuning on tumor slices). Zero-shot BiomedParse fails catastrophically on OOD tumors, achieving 100% sensitivity but 0% specificity on bladder, uterus, prostate, and cervix (it segments the entire organ rather than the tumor), yielding CA values of only 0.546–0.632. BiomedParse-LoRA improves Dice substantially (e.g., 0.578 on bladder vs. 0.069 for BiomedParse) but still lags R²-Seg on CA by 8–34 percentage points across all five OOD tumor types. R²-Seg wins on CA and specificity in every OOD comparison, with the largest margin on prostate (CA 0.890 vs. 0.555 for LoRA, a 60% relative improvement). However, R²-Seg trades sensitivity: it drops to 0.299–0.645 sensitivity versus 0.852–0.960 for LoRA, which the authors frame as a deliberate calibration choice to suppress harmful over-diagnosis. On in-distribution CT tumors (liver, pancreas), R²-Seg achieves 10–30% relative Dice and CA gains over frozen BiomedParse without any parameter updates, while fine-tuned models exhibit catastrophic forgetting on normal organ segmentation (AMOS22, M&Ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BiomedParse, a text-prompted foundation model for biomedical segmentation, as its frozen backbone, and departs from the dominant fine-tuning and LoRA-based adaptation strategies by proposing a fully training-free test-time pipeline. The Reason stage draws on the LLM planning literature (ReAct, Plan-and-Solve, chain-of-thought) to generate anatomically grounded ROIs, while the Reject stage repurposes classical nonparametric two-sample testing (MMD with permutation p-values and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control) as a model-agnostic confidence filter. Unlike prior TTA methods (TENT, CoTTA, TTT) that update normalization layers or self-supervised losses, R²-Seg keeps all parameters frozen and instead reshapes the decision boundary through external statistical evidence, positioning itself at the intersection of vision-language prompting, LLM-guided planning, and hypothesis testing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design offers a practically deployable solution for OOD tumor segmentation that avoids the catastrophic forgetting demonstrated in fine-tuned baselines on in-distribution organ tasks, making it attractive for clinical settings where retraining is infeasible. However, the substantial sensitivity drop (e.g., bladder 1.00→0.34, cervix 1.00→0.30) reveals a significant calibration trade-off that limits immediate clinical utility for early detection, and the authors themselves flag the simultaneous improvement of sensitivity and specificity as an open challenge. The statistical rejection framework (MMD + BH) is general and could transfer to other OOD anomaly-detection problems beyond medical imaging, but its dependence on a reliable normal-organ reference mask and the sensitivity of results to the FDR level α leave room for further investigation.
