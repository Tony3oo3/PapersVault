## Problem

First-Frame Propagation (FFP) video editing methods are hampered by reliance on cumbersome run-time guidance such as per-video LoRA fine-tuning or auxiliary inputs like depth maps, which limits generalization and incurs high computational cost. The paper identifies the root cause as inadequate training data: existing datasets are too short, low-resolution, and lack the task diversity needed to teach models robust temporal priors, forcing them to depend on external guidance as a crutch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260973/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (720p, up to 81 frames) and a single edited first frame (RGB image, spatially aligned with the source) as input, and produces a temporally coherent edited target video at the same resolution and length. Notably, the pipeline is fully guidance-free at inference time—no depth maps, optical flow, masks, or per-video fine-tuning are required; the model conditions solely on the source video latent and the edited first-frame latent. The edited first frame is assumed to be produced by an external image editing tool (Qwen-Edit in their evaluation setup), decoupling semantic interpretation from temporal propagation.

## Method

Built on Fun-Control (a conditional video DiT derived from Wan 2.1), the framework introduces two core mechanisms to resolve the tension between first-frame appearance anchoring and source motion preservation. First, Adaptive Spatio-Temporal RoPE (AST-RoPE) uses a lightweight transformer-MLP module to predict content-aware spatial (α_S) and temporal (α_T) scaling factors from the source latent; spatial heads use α_S to compress the perceived positional distance to the first frame (strengthening appearance propagation), while temporal heads use α_T to rescale the temporal axis (matching the source video's motion intensity). Second, a self-distillation strategy introduces a parallel identity-propagation task (reconstructing the target video from itself) as a teacher, with two distillation losses: an inter-frame Gram-matrix alignment for global motion structure and an MMD-based first-frame consistency loss that constrains the evolution of token-wise relational distributions over time. Training uses LoRA (rank 128, 2 epochs) with a combined flow-matching and distillation objective on the FFP-300K dataset (290K pairs at 720p/81 frames, generated via a two-track pipeline covering local object edits and global stylization).

## Results

On EditVerseBench (125 filtered videos), both variants of FreeProp achieve state-of-the-art results across all six metrics. The 33-frame variant (Ours-33f) attains the highest PickScore (20.419) and VLM Score (7.631), while the 81-frame variant (Ours-81f) leads in DINO temporal consistency (0.991), Frame alignment (28.316), and Video alignment (25.925). Both variants tie for the best CLIP temporal consistency (0.991). In the user study, FreeProp scores 4.250/5 on Editing Accuracy, 4.333/5 on Motion Accuracy, and 4.146/5 on Video Quality, outperforming all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeProp outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on EditVerseBench. Against the strongest commercial competitor Aleph, Ours-33f gains +0.13 PickScore (20.419 vs 20.291) and +0.48 VLM Score (7.631 vs 7.154), while also leading in temporal consistency (CLIP 0.991 vs 0.989, DINO 0.990 vs 0.984). Against the strongest academic instruction-based method EditVerse, the gains are larger: +0.29 PickScore and +0.53 VLM Score. Among FFP-based methods, FreeProp surpasses Señorita* (the best FFP baseline using Qwen-Edit for first-frame editing) by +0.51 PickScore and +0.29 VLM Score, and VACE by +0.32 PickScore and +1.56 VLM Score. The training-free baselines (TokenFlow, STDF) and instruction-based methods (InsV2V, LucyEdit) trail by a wider margin, with VLM Scores of 4.9–5.7 versus FreeProp's 7.6. In the user study, FreeProp leads all three criteria (EA, MA, VQ) over EditVerse, Señorita-2M, and Aleph, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the First-Frame Propagation (FFP) paradigm established by I2VEdit, AnyV2V, and StableV2V, but departs fundamentally from their reliance on runtime guidance (per-video LoRA, depth maps, optical flow) by attributing that need to a data gap rather than an architectural deficiency. Architecturally, it builds on Fun-Control (a Wan 2.1 derivative) and adapts RoPE positional encodings via the novel AST-RoPE mechanism, drawing on head-specialization observations in DiTs. The self-distillation objective borrows the relational-alignment idea from VideoREPA but repurposes it as a self-referential identity-propagation regularizer rather than distilling from an external foundation model. The data pipeline leverages VACE for synthesis, Qwen2.5-VL for captioning/verification, and Grounded-SAM2 for segmentation, integrating existing tools into a two-track (local/global) construction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FFP-300K (290K pairs at 720p/81 frames) is the first dataset purpose-built for guidance-free FFP training, filling a concrete gap that prior datasets (Señorita-2M, InsViE, VPData) left open with short, low-res, or task-narrow clips. The practical implication is that FFP editing can be deployed without auxiliary inputs like depth or flow, simplifying production pipelines. Open questions include whether the 81-frame limit generalizes to longer sequences, whether the self-distillation overhead is prohibitive at scale, and whether the approach transfers to instruction-based editing or other conditional generation tasks. The ablation revealing that the dataset alone (baseline Fun-Control fine-tune) captures most of the gain suggests future work should focus on data quality over architectural complexity.
