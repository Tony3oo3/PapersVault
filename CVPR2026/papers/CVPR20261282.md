## Problem

CLIP's zero-shot recognition is highly vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, and the recent test-time counterattack (TTC) defense remains fragile under strong attacks because it relies on a single corrupted original view for guidance and employs a noise-driven hard-gating scheme that cannot adapt to varying corruption severity. This paper addresses both limitations to achieve robust defense without any tuning or retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (potentially adversarially perturbed) as input and produces a class label as output. Internally, it generates N=2 augmented views and applies counterattack perturbations with an internal ℓ∞ budget of ε_ca=8 over K=4 iterations, but these perturbations operate entirely within the defense pipeline so the external image quality is preserved. Because no gradients or optimizer states are stored, the method supports batch-level inference (batch size 128) with only 0.27 GB additional memory and 0.14 s/image latency.

## Method

MAC is a tuning-free test-time defense with three components. First, it constructs N=2 augmented views via stochastic augmentations (random affine, color jitter, Gaussian blur, additive noise) to obtain diverse CLIP embeddings. Second, it performs a multi-view guided counterattack that jointly optimizes view-wise perturbations to steer all views away from their corrupted states in embedding space (Frobenius-norm objective with per-view ℓ∞ constraints), replacing TTC's reliance on a single corrupted original view. Third, it introduces a corruption degree measure—defined as the ℓ₂ deviation of a view's normalized embedding under a fresh stochastic augmentation—and maps it through a sigmoid (threshold τ_thres=0.7, temperature τ_temp=0.01) to produce per-view soft weights that scale the counterattack intensity, amplifying it on highly corrupted views while suppressing it on clean or weakly perturbed ones. The final prediction is obtained by averaging per-view CLIP similarity scores across the adaptively counterattacked views.

## Results

On 10 fine-grained recognition datasets under PGD-100 (ε=4.0), MAC achieves 45.2% average adversarial robustness, surpassing the strongest tuning-based baseline R-TPT (37.5%) by +7.7 points and the best tuning-free method MTA (25.8%) by +19.4 points, while maintaining competitive clean accuracy (58.7% vs. 58.9% for CLIP). On ImageNet and four OOD benchmarks, MAC reaches 38.3% average robustness, outperforming R-TPT (30.5%) by +7.8 points. MAC also achieves the highest robustness under diverse attacks (DI²-FGSM: 21.3%, CW: 47.5%, AutoAttack: 47.4%) and operates at 0.14 s/img with only 0.27 GB memory overhead, compared to 6.73 s/img and 1.89 GB for R-TPT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAC outperforms all four competing test-time defenses on the primary robustness metric. Against the closest tuning-free competitor MTA, MAC gains +19.4 points average robustness (45.2% vs. 25.8%) on fine-grained datasets and +20.2 points on ImageNet+OOD (38.3% vs. 18.1%). Against TTC, the direct predecessor, MAC improves robustness by +38.4 points on fine-grained datasets (45.2% vs. 6.8%), demonstrating that multi-view guidance and soft weighting are critical under strong attacks. MAC also surpasses the strongest tuning-based method R-TPT by +7.7 points on fine-grained datasets while being ~48× faster (0.14 vs. 6.73 s/img) and using ~7× less memory (0.27 vs. 1.89 GB). The one scenario where MAC underperforms is the MAC-adaptive white-box attack (17.5% vs. R-TPT's 36.2%), where the adversary differentiates through the MAC pipeline itself; however, MAC still leads all tuning-free methods in this setting (vs. TTC's 2.4% and MTA's 29.8%).

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC directly extends the test-time counterattack (TTC) framework of Xing et al. (CVPR 2025), which was the first tuning-free defense for CLIP that perturbs inputs at inference to steer embeddings away from corrupted states. The paper identifies two specific limitations of TTC—reliance on a single corrupted original view for guidance and a noise-driven hard-gating mechanism—and addresses them through multi-view guided counterattacks and corruption-aware soft weighting, respectively. It also positions itself against tuning-based test-time prompt tuning methods (R-TPT, TAPT) and the MeanShift-based augmentation (MTA), arguing that tuning-free counterattack designs can match or exceed their robustness while being orders of magnitude faster and more memory-efficient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the combination of strong robustness (45.2% avg under PGD-100, beating the best tuning-based method by +7.7 points) with near-baseline inference speed (0.14 s/img) and minimal memory overhead (0.27 GB), making it deployable in real-world CLIP pipelines without retraining or per-instance tuning. A key open question is whether the corruption-degree measure and multi-view counterattack paradigm generalize beyond CLIP ViT-B/32 to other VLM architectures or to multi-modal (text-side) adversarial attacks. The slight clean-accuracy drop on some datasets (e.g., ImageNet: 61.7 vs. 62.2 baseline) and the dependence on hand-crafted augmentation choices also suggest room for further refinement.
