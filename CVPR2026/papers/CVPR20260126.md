## Problem

RAFT-based optical flow methods rely on dense all-pairs correlation volume sampling, whose default implementation has quadratic memory complexity in the number of pixels, making it infeasible at high resolutions. The memory-efficient on-demand alternative avoids storing the volume but is significantly slower due to hardware-unfriendly operations and lack of result reuse. This trade-off forces prior methods to downsample inputs, losing fine-grained detail, or to abandon cost volumes entirely at the expense of accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a drop-in replacement for the correlation sampling step within existing RAFT-based architectures, taking D-dimensional feature maps extracted from a pair of RGB video frames and producing the bilinearly sampled matching costs at each flow update iteration. It is designed for very high-resolution inputs (up to 8192×3432 px) where the default implementation would require hundreds of gigabytes of memory, and it achieves linear O(n) time and space complexity in the number of input pixels. The paper also introduces a cascaded test-time extension for SEA-RAFT that recursively initializes flow from lower-resolution estimates without additional training.

## Method

The key insight is that empirical analysis of RAFT's sampling patterns reveals only ~1.6% of the full 4D correlation volume is ever accessed, and this sparsity is further amplified by reshaping inputs into a patch-major layout that groups 2D neighborhoods contiguously in memory. The algorithm computes only the necessary blocks of the correlation volume via tiled block-sparse matrix-matrix multiplications, guided by a computation mask derived from the known sampling grid. A fused CUDA kernel (implemented in CUTLASS CuTe DSL with bfloat16 tensor cores) eliminates all intermediate global memory writes: each thread block serially computes and samples one row of blocks using shared memory and registers, with an implicit computation mask built via inter-thread atomic-minimum voting. This achieves up to 92% speedup over on-demand sampling at equally low memory, and up to 99% memory reduction over the default implementation at comparable speed, while producing bit-exact results matching the original RAFT operator.

## Results

On the 8K CHARGE dataset, SEA-RAFT with cascaded inference at full 8192×3432 resolution achieves the best 1px error of 13.3% and EPE of 2.70 px, outperforming all prior methods. At 4096×1716 (1/2 scale), the same method achieves 15.8% 1px error and 1.90 EPE with a runtime of only 0.45 s. The efficient correlation sampler reduces end-to-end runtime by up to 63% for RAFT (2.62 s → 0.96 s) and 33% for SEA-RAFT (0.62 s → 0.42 s), while maintaining up to 99% lower peak memory compared to the default dense volume implementation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed SEA-RAFT (Cascaded) at full 8K resolution achieves the best 1px error (13.3%) and EPE (2.70 px) among all methods, beating WAFT (15.3%, 2.74 px) and MS-RAFT+ (14.5%, 1.92 px) on accuracy while running 4.4× faster than WAFT (1.91 s vs 8.47 s) and 2.2× faster than MS-RAFT+ (1.91 s vs 4.15 s). At 4K, SEA-RAFT (Cascaded) achieves the best EPE (1.90 px) and competitive 1px error (15.8%), matching MS-RAFT+ (1.92 px EPE) while being 9× faster (0.45 s vs 4.15 s). The efficient correlation sampler provides the largest relative speedup for RAFT (63% reduction, 2.62 s → 0.96 s) and SEA-RAFT (33%, 0.62 s → 0.42 s), with smaller gains for DPFlow (9%) and CCMR (16%) due to their different architectures. Methods that avoid cost volumes entirely (ReCoVEr-CX, WAFT) achieve competitive 1px error but suffer from much higher large-motion EPE (58.71 and 65.14 px respectively) compared to the proposed method (21.53 px at 8K). Non-RAFT methods like GMFlow and PWC-Net are significantly worse in accuracy (43.9% and 24.4% 1px error) and cannot match the quality of RAFT-based approaches at high resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RAFT's all-pairs correlation volume formulation, which offered two implementations: a dense 4D volume (quadratic memory) and an on-demand sampler (memory-efficient but slow). While several subsequent works (SCV, HCVFlow, ReCoVEr, WAFT) attempted to avoid or approximate the full volume at the cost of accuracy, this paper instead exploits the structured sparsity of actual sampling access patterns to compute only the necessary blocks, matching the exact RAFT operator. The approach is conceptually analogous to FlashAttention's IO-aware tiling strategy applied to correlation volumes, and the cascaded inference extends SEA-RAFT with a training-free multi-resolution warm-start.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: RAFT-based methods become feasible at 8K resolution (previously OOM at 4K for dense, prohibitively slow for on-demand), and the 8K CHARGE dataset provides the first realistic ultra-high-resolution benchmark for optical flow. Limitations include forward-pass-only optimization (no backward pass for training), no architecture-specific tuning beyond CUTLASS tensor-core kernels, and the block size being fixed at 8. Open questions include whether the same block-sparse strategy transfers to other correlation-based tasks (e.g., stereo, scene flow) and whether training-time memory can be similarly reduced to enable higher-resolution training.
