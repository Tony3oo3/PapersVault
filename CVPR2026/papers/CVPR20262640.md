## Problem

Existing pipeline-based Visual Active Tracking methods suffer from two critical gaps in real-world deployment: they operate at the category level and cannot distinguish a specific target instance from visually similar distractors, and they rely on simple centering controllers (e.g., PID) that fail completely when the target is occluded by obstacles, since they lack any mechanism to plan trajectories around occluders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262640/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each time step the agent receives a 160×120 RGB image plus a single reference image of the target instance provided at episode start; the occlusion-recovery planner additionally conditions on a predicted target bounding box. The system outputs a 4-dimensional continuous action vector (forward, lateral, vertical linear velocities and yaw rotation), an updated target bounding box, and—when tracking is lost—a multi-step recovery trajectory generated by the diffusion-based planner.

## Method

OA-VAT is a three-module pipeline. First, a training-free offline module constructs a discriminative instance prototype by extracting DINOv3 features from the reference crop and its multi-view augmented variants (horizontal/vertical flips, diffusion-generated views for humans), then averaging and normalizing them; a theoretical proposition shows this aggregation increases inter-instance separation. Second, an online tracker matches the prototype to segmentation candidates via cosine similarity, refines it with an exponential moving average of newly observed target features, and predicts motion through a confidence-aware Kalman filter whose measurement noise is modulated by a sigmoid of the tracker's confidence score, suppressing updates when confidence is low. Third, an occlusion-aware trajectory planner—trained via conditional diffusion (DDPM-style denoising) on the newly introduced Planning-20k dataset of 20k A*-generated expert trajectories in UnrealCV—takes the current image and predicted bounding box as conditions to produce collision-free recovery paths, making the policy target-agnostic and generalizable to unseen objects.

## Results

On UnrealCV distractor maps, OA-VAT achieves 0.93 average Success Rate (SR), outperforming the SOTA TrackVLA (0.91) by 2.2% while using 584M parameters vs. TrackVLA's >7B. On the DAT benchmark, OA-VAT improves average Cumulative Reward by 32.6% (242→321) and Tracking Success Rate by 19.4% (0.72→0.86) over GC-VAT. On real-world image datasets (VOT, DTB70, UAVDT), OA-VAT achieves 90.8% average Correct Action Rate, surpassing GC-VAT by 12.1%. On a DJI Tello drone, OA-VAT reaches 81.6% TSR (vs. 18.9% for the best baseline) at 35 FPS on an RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OA-VAT outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest RL-based tracker EVT (748M params), OA-VAT improves average SR from 0.81 to 0.93 on UnrealCV distractor maps and achieves 81.6% vs. 18.9% TSR on the real drone. Against the SOTA pipeline method TrackVLA (>7B params, 10 FPS on RTX 4090), OA-VAT gains +2.2% average SR (0.93 vs. 0.91) while running 3.5× faster (35 FPS on RTX 3090) with 12× fewer parameters. On the DAT benchmark, OA-VAT surpasses GC-VAT (NeurIPS 2025) by 32.6% in CR and 19.4% in TSR, with notably lower variance (±0.01–0.02 vs. ±0.29–0.32). On real-world image evaluation, OA-VAT achieves 90.8% average CAR, beating GC-VAT (81.0%) by 12.1% and ORTrack (77.4%, which requires ground-truth bbox initialization) by 13.4%. Older RL baselines (SARL, AD-VAT, D-VAT) and pipeline baselines (DiMP, FAn) are substantially outperformed, with SR gaps exceeding 50% on UnrealCV.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two VAT lineages: RL-based end-to-end trackers (SARL, AD-VAT, D-VAT, GC-VAT, EVT) and pipeline methods that decouple perception from control (DiMP, RSPT, FAn, TrackVLA). It departs from the pipeline tradition by introducing instance-level discrimination via DINOv3 multi-view prototypes (borrowing from the passive tracking and re-identification literature) and by replacing the standard PID controller with a conditional diffusion-based trajectory planner inspired by Diffusion Policy. The Planning-20k dataset, collected in UnrealCV with A*-generated expert trajectories, is a new contribution that enables target-agnostic occlusion recovery without requiring per-target demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OA-VAT addresses two practical deployment bottlenecks—distractor confusion and occlusion failure—that have limited real-world VAT systems, and does so with a 584M-parameter model running at 35 FPS, making it far more deployable than VLA-based alternatives like TrackVLA (>7B params, 10 FPS). The zero-shot generalization to unseen target categories (vehicles in DAT) and the large drone TSR gap (81.6% vs. 18.9% best baseline) suggest the approach transfers well beyond training conditions. Open questions include whether the Planning-20k planner generalizes to more complex 3D occlusion geometries beyond the SimpleRoom setting, how the method scales to multi-target or cooperative tracking, and whether the theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) holds under the distribution shifts encountered in truly unstructured environments.
