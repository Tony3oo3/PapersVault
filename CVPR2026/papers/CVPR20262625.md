## Problem

Aligning diffusion models with human preferences faces a dual bottleneck: SFT requires scarce, expensive high-quality image datasets, while DPO-style preference optimization methods depend on large-scale preference pairs (hundreds of thousands of samples) and incur heavy computational overhead from online sampling. No existing approach achieves strong preference alignment with both minimal data and low training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CRAFT takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion model (SD1.5 or SDXL). During training, the method self-generates its own (prompt, image) pairs using the base model, refining 10,000 prompts with Qwen-Plus and generating N=4 images per prompt with random seeds. The final fine-tuning dataset consists of only 100 highest-scoring (text, image) pairs selected via composite reward filtering, and the model is trained for just 120–200 steps with full-parameter UNet updates.

## Method

CRAFT operates in three stages: (1) data construction, where prompts are refined and images are self-generated by the base diffusion model; (2) Composite Reward Filtering (CRF), which uses three reward models (HPSv2.1, PickScore, AES) to retain only prompt groups where refined-prompt images outperform original-prompt images across all three rewards; (3) weighted SFT, where the standard denoising loss is multiplied by a group-based advantage (reward normalized by within-group mean and std) and an indicator restricting gradients to filtered samples. The key theoretical result proves that this weighted SFT objective is a lower bound of group-based reinforcement learning (GRPO-style), establishing a principled equivalence between selective supervised fine-tuning and RL without requiring online policy sampling. This eliminates the need for large preference datasets or iterative online generation, reducing training to ~4 GPU-hours on SDXL with only 100 samples.

## Results

On SDXL, CRAFT achieves the best HPSv2.1 score of 32.67 on HPDv2, 31.10 on Parti-Prompt, and 32.18 on Pick-a-Pic, while also leading on ImageReward (1.312/1.252/1.308) and MPS (15.62/12.41/12.53) across all three benchmarks. On SD1.5, CRAFT similarly dominates with HPSv2.1 of 29.17 (HPDv2), 28.30 (Parti-Prompt), and 28.44 (Pick-a-Pic). Critically, CRAFT achieves these results using only 100 training samples and ~4.0 GPU hours (SDXL) / ~1.9 GPU hours (SD1.5), compared to 851k+ samples and 63–638 GPU hours for baselines, yielding 11–220× speedups. On GenEval (SD1.5), CRAFT attains the best overall score of 45.82, leading in counting (38.12), colors (77.66), position (6.75), and attribute binding (8.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric on both SDXL and SD1.5. Against the strongest baseline SPO (CVPR 2025), CRAFT gains +0.35 HPSv2.1 and +0.209 ImageReward on HPDv2 (SDXL), while using 40× fewer samples (100 vs. 4,000) and 15.8× less GPU time. Against SmPO (arXiv 2025), CRAFT improves HPSv2.1 by +1.27 and ImageReward by +0.231 on HPDv2 (SDXL), with a 30× reduction in GPU hours. Against Diff-DPO (CVPR 2024), the gap is even larger: +2.91 HPSv2.1 and +0.275 ImageReward on HPDv2 (SDXL), at 159.5× lower compute cost. On SD1.5, CRAFT beats Diff-KTO (NeurIPS 2024) by +0.61 HPSv2.1 and +0.013 ImageReward on HPDv2, while requiring 218.7× fewer GPU hours. Notably, CRAFT also leads on ImageReward and MPS—metrics not used in its training reward—demonstrating generalization beyond the composite reward used for data filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT sits at the intersection of two lines of work on diffusion model alignment: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on curated data and DPO-style preference optimization (Diffusion-DPO, SmPO, SPO). It departs from DPO-style methods by eliminating the need for preference pairs entirely, and from online RL methods (SPO, GRPO-style) by operating fully offline with a single forward pass per sample. The key conceptual departure is the theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) showing that advantage-weighted SFT on filtered data constitutes a lower bound of group-based RL, thereby reframing a simple supervised procedure as a principled RL surrogate. The composite reward filtering strategy for data curation is novel but builds on the well-established idea of reward-model-guided selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving competitive or superior alignment with 100 samples and ~4 GPU hours (vs. 851k samples and ~638 GPU hours for Diff-DPO) dramatically lowers the barrier to diffusion model fine-tuning for resource-constrained labs. The theoretical bridge between weighted SFT and group-based RL provides a clean justification for why data quality matters more than data quantity, a finding supported by the ablation showing Top-100 outperforming All-2312. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger architectures (e.g., FLUX, SD3), whether the Qwen-Plus prompt-refinement step is a hidden dependency that limits reproducibility, and whether the composite reward weights (0.4/0.4/0.2) transfer across domains or require re-tuning.
