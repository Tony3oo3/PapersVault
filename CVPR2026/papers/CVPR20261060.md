## Problem

Vision-language models perform well on egocentric spatial reasoning but degrade sharply on allocentric tasks, where spatial relations must be inferred from the perspective of an object within the scene. Existing remedies—training on allocentric data, fine-tuning, chain-of-thought prompting, or converting allocentric queries to egocentric ones—are either impractical at scale, generalize poorly, or underuse the VLM's intrinsic reasoning capacity. The paper asks whether allocentric reasoning can be reformulated into a form that VLMs already handle well, without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a single RGB image and a text prompt specifying an allocentric spatial question (e.g., 'From the dog's perspective, is the ball on the left or right?'). Internally it invokes GroundingDINO for bounding boxes, DepthPro for a monocular depth map, and OrientAnything for the reference viewer's facing direction. The output is a reformulated symbolic-layout question—a 2D image containing colored circles on a bipartitioned background plus a modified prompt that asks which color region a given symbol occupies—which is then fed to a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL by default) to yield the final spatial-relation answer.

## Method

SymPL is a zero-shot, two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Spatial Information Extraction) classifies the reference viewer and target objects from the prompt, estimates each object's 3D position by unprojecting depth-map pixels within its bounding box, and retrieves the reference viewer's facing vector via OrientAnything. Stage 2 (Question Reformulation) applies four factors sequentially: (i) Projection—selecting an orthogonal top or front view centered on the reference viewer so that allocentric relations map to intuitive 2D directions; (ii) Abstraction—replacing each object with a featureless colored circle to eliminate shape-based distractions; (iii) Bipartition—splitting the 2D plane into exactly two regions (linear for directional categories, circular for distance categories) whose boundary encodes the spatial relation to be judged; (iv) Localization—filling the two regions with distinct colors and rewriting the question as 'which color region contains the target symbol?', reducing a 3D relational judgment to a simple 2D containment check. The resulting symbolic-layout question is passed to a VLM, which answers the localization query, and the answer is mapped back to the original spatial relation.

## Results

On the allocentric COMFORT# benchmark, SymPL achieves 69.00% (left/right), 97.33% (closer), 91.41% (visibility), and 91.50% (facing), substantially outperforming all baselines. On the real-world 3DSRBench, SymPL reaches 79.94% (left/right), 75.00% (visibility), and 70.95% (facing), again leading or near-leading across categories. Notably, most general-purpose VLMs (including GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash) perform at or below the random baseline (~50%) on COMFORT# allocentric tasks, highlighting the severity of the allocentric reasoning gap that SymPL addresses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymPL outperforms all 16 baselines on COMFORT# across every category, with the largest margins on closer (97.33% vs. GPT-5's 84.25%) and visibility (91.41% vs. SpatialRGPT's 53.75%). Among general-purpose VLMs, GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash are the strongest but still fall 13–40 points below SymPL on COMFORT#; on 3DSRBench, Gemini-2.5-Flash edges out SymPL on facing (72.25% vs. 70.95%) while SymPL leads on left/right (79.94% vs. 38.40%) and visibility (75.00% vs. 64.10%). The allocentric-specific baselines APC-Num and APC-Vis show the closest competition on 3DSRBench left/right (77.94% and 61.75% respectively vs. SymPL's 79.94%) but collapse on COMFORT# (47.83% and 43.75% on left/right), revealing poor cross-dataset generalization. Egocentric spatial reasoning methods (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, SpatialBot, SD-VLM) all perform near or below random on allocentric COMFORT# tasks, confirming their egocentric bias. General reasoning aids (CoT, SoM, SCAFFOLD) provide only marginal gains over the base Qwen2.5-VL and remain 17–46 points below SymPL, indicating that prompting strategies alone cannot resolve the viewpoint-transformation challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SymPL carves new ground by departing from the two dominant strategies for allocentric spatial reasoning: training-based approaches (SAT, SpatialVLM) that require costly allocentric data, and perspective-conversion methods (APC) that reduce allocentric queries to egocentric ones. Instead, SymPL reformulates the problem into a 2D symbolic-layout question using four factors (projection, abstraction, bipartition, localization), leveraging the observation that VLMs handle color-coded 2D localization far better than 3D viewpoint transformation. The pipeline repurposes existing foundation models (GroundingDINO, DepthPro, OrientAnything) for 3D estimation but applies a novel question-reformulation layer that has no direct precedent in the spatial-reasoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stark finding that most general-purpose VLMs (including GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash) perform at or below random on allocentric tasks highlights a critical blind spot for embodied AI applications like autonomous driving and robotic manipulation. SymPL's training-free reformulation offers a practical path forward, but its heavy reliance on monocular depth estimation and orientation estimation from foundation models introduces a fragile dependency that may not transfer to real-world scenes with occlusion, textureless surfaces, or dynamic objects. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object relational reasoning beyond pairwise comparisons, whether the 3D estimation errors compound in cluttered real-world scenes, and whether the symbolic-layout paradigm can be extended to temporal or continuous spatial queries.
