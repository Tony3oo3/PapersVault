## Problem

Existing human mesh recovery methods rely on weak-perspective camera assumptions or fully coupled feature representations, making it difficult to recover metric scale, global 3D position, and disentangle local pose from translation in a single forward pass. Multi-stage pipelines that bolt on separate trajectory or depth modules introduce accumulated errors and are inherently limited by the performance of their external components.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped RGB image of a person along with a constructed bounding camera ray map (derived from camera intrinsics and bounding box geometry; intrinsics are estimated via AnyCalib when unavailable) as dual-channel input to the network. It outputs SMPL pose (72-D), shape (10-D), and metric 3D translation (3-D) for the human, plus a per-pixel refined metric depth map that aligns the human mesh with the surrounding 3D scene geometry.

## Method

MetricHMSR encodes camera intrinsics, bounding box position, and cropping/scaling effects into a pixel-aligned bounding ray map, which is jointly fed with the cropped image through separate ViT encoders (ViTPose for the image, ViT-L/16 for the ray map). The concatenated tokens are processed by HumanMoE, a soft Mixture-of-Experts decoder comprising a Patch MoE (dynamically routes image patches to 4 routed experts + 1 shared expert based on semantic content, enabling feature-level disentanglement of local pose vs. global position) and a Global MoE (aggregates full-image context), plus a dedicated ray expert for camera-ray tokens. A load-balancing auxiliary loss prevents expert collapse. The recovered metric human mesh then serves as a geometric anchor for a human-guided depth refinement module: a hybrid UNet-ViT backbone predicts spatially varying affine fields (s(x), b(x)) that correct MapAnything's monocular depth, regularized by total-variation and spatial-variance terms and supervised by an anchor consistency loss at visible human surface pixels.

## Results

On 3DPW, MetricHMSR achieves the best local pose metrics across all three measures: PA-MPJPE 33.6 mm, MPJPE 53.0 mm, and PVE 62.7 mm. On EMDB-1, it remains competitive (PA-MPJPE 43.2, MPJPE 70.7, PVE 81.6), ranking second or third behind PromptHMR. For global trajectory on EMDB-2 with predicted camera extrinsics, MetricHMSR (online) achieves WA-MPJPE 72.1 mm and RTE 1.4%, matching or exceeding the best offline method (PromptHMR-vid: 71.0 mm, 1.3%). For metric depth on PROX, the human-guided refinement yields AbsRel 0.13 and δ₁ 0.91, outperforming MapAnything (0.18, 0.83).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3DPW local pose, MetricHMSR outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric, with the closest competitors being CameraHMR (PA-MPJPE 35.1, PVE 65.9) and PromptHMR (PA-MPJPE 35.5, PVE 67.3), yielding a 1.5–2.4 mm margin. On EMDB-1, PromptHMR retains a lead in PA-MPJPE (40.1 vs. 43.2) and MPJPE (68.1 vs. 70.7), while MetricHMSR matches CameraHMR in PVE (81.6 vs. 81.7). For global trajectory on EMDB-2 with predicted extrinsics, MetricHMSR as an online method (WA-MPJPE 72.1, W-MPJPE 199.5) surpasses all other online baselines (next best: Human3R at 112.2/267.9) and is within 1.1 mm of the best offline method PromptHMR-vid (71.0/216.5). On the static-camera RICH dataset, MetricHMSR achieves the best RTE (1.9%) among all online and offline methods and the best W-MPJPE (165.5) among online methods, though offline methods like GVHMR (WA-MPJPE 78.8) still lead in WA-MPJPE. For metric depth on PROX, the human-guided refinement improves over MapAnything by 0.05 in AbsRel and 0.08 in δ₁, and substantially over UniDepth (0.24/0.56) and Metric3D (0.76/0.01).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the human mesh recovery lineage from HMR through camera-modeling improvements (CLIFF, CameraHMR, PersPose) and global-trajectory methods (WHAM, GVHMR, TRAM) by unifying local pose, metric shape, and metric 3D position in a single network without external depth or SLAM modules. Architecturally, it departs from the standard dense-MLP or transformer decoder by introducing HumanMoE (a soft Mixture-of-Experts with Patch and Global variants) to disentangle local-pose and global-position features, and from weak-perspective or focal-length-estimation camera models by encoding intrinsics and bounding-box geometry as a pixel-aligned bounding ray map. For scene reconstruction, it builds directly on MapAnything's feed-forward metric depth prediction, adding a human-mesh-anchored per-pixel affine refinement rather than treating depth estimation as an independent upstream module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By recovering a metric human mesh and refining scene depth in one pass, MetricHMSR eliminates the accumulated errors of multi-stage pipelines (HMR → external depth → alignment) and produces physically consistent human-scene compositions from a single image, which is directly relevant to embodied AI and physical simulation. The bounding ray map offers a clean, differentiable way to inject camera and crop information that prior methods handled ad hoc, and the MoE-based disentanglement provides an interpretable mechanism for separating pose from position. Key limitations remain: no multi-person support, sensitivity to severe occlusion, and reliance on MapAnything for the base depth signal. The authors note that pseudo-GT annotation of in-the-wild datasets (COCO, MPII, AIC) is a promising path toward scaling, but the method has not yet been validated on truly unconstrained internet-scale data.
