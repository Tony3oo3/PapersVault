## Problem

Existing video QA benchmarks rarely provide the fine-grained, spatio-temporal evidence needed to rigorously evaluate causal reasoning in VLMs. Most focus on descriptive questions or offer only temporal grounding, leaving the ability to actively discover and localize chained cause-effect evidences unmeasured. This paper asks whether current VLMs can accurately ground a multi-step causal chain in both space and time to answer causal questions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip (avg. 13.68 s) and a causal question, and must first output a variable-length causal chain of K evidences—each with explicit start/end timestamps, a textual rationale, and a 1-FPS map of bounding boxes (avg. 5.65 s per evidence, 8.3% spatial coverage)—before producing a final answer. The benchmark provides both 6-option multiple-choice and open-ended questions, with distractors deliberately designed from text-only confounders and causally irrelevant visual instances to probe spurious-correlation reliance.

## Method

CaST-Bench is constructed via a multi-stage human-AI collaborative pipeline: (1) naturalistic videos are selected from SegmentAnything-Video and filtered by a VLM for multi-object complexity; (2) dense instance-level descriptions are generated by a VLM using combined full-frame and cropped-video prompts, then validated by human annotators (yielding 10,728 descriptions); (3) a VLM acting as a causal-thinking agent generates QA pairs with explicit ST causal chains across four question types (causal explanation, counterfactual, predictive, inferential); (4) a novel mask-based filter removes QA pairs where a VLM can still answer after all causal-evidence regions are masked, ensuring chain completeness. The evaluation suite introduces Instance-Matched tIoU and vIoU for chain grounding, Faithful/Spurious Answer Rates to decouple correct answers from well-grounded evidence, and an LLM-as-a-judge protocol for open-ended justification quality.

## Results

On CaST-Bench, the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 50.34% MCQ accuracy, while the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct reaches 45.30%, both far below the 91.89% human baseline. Evidence grounding is the weakest dimension: IM-tIoU scores range from 2–28% (proprietary) and 3–12% (open-source), while IM-vIoU is even lower (0.09–4.31%), indicating that precise spatio-temporal localization of causal evidence is largely absent. Faithful answer rates (correct answer supported by well-grounded evidence) remain below 13% for all models, whereas spurious rates (correct answer despite poor grounding) stay at 30–45%, revealing that many correct answers rely on spurious correlations rather than genuine causal evidence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, CaST-Bench evaluates 15 VLMs rather than proposing a new method. Proprietary models (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 50.34%, GPT-5 at 46.32%, Gemini-2.5-Flash at 45.60%) consistently outperform all open-source models (best: Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct at 45.30%) on MCQ accuracy, and the gap widens dramatically on grounding metrics: GPT-5 achieves IM-vIoU of 4.31% versus 0.93% for Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct and 0.25% for InternVL-3.5-30B-A3B. All models fall far short of the 91.89% human baseline, with a 40+ point gap. An ablation (Table 4) shows that providing ground-truth causal chains boosts accuracy by 20–25 points (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro from 50.34% to 75.61%), confirming that the inability to self-generate accurate causal chains is the primary bottleneck. Notably, 'thinking' variants do not consistently beat their 'instruct' counterparts (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct at 43.13% vs. Thinking at 39.16%), and larger models within families (e.g., InternVL-3.5-30B vs. 8B) show higher spurious rates, suggesting scaling alone does not improve causal grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate benchmarking threads: causal-reasoning video QA (Causal-VidQA, Video-Holmes, VR-Bench) and spatio-temporal grounding (TVQA+, V-STaR, NExT-GQA). Prior causal benchmarks either provide only narrative textual clues or lack any spatio-temporal annotations, while grounding benchmarks focus on direct single-target localization rather than discovering a chain of causally linked evidences. CaST-Bench is the first to require models to output a variable-length sequence of ST-grounded evidences (timestamps + tracked bounding boxes) forming a causal chain before answering, and it introduces a mask-based filtering procedure to validate causal-chain completeness—a methodological contribution absent from prior video QA construction pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 41-point gap between human (91.89%) and best-model (50.34%) accuracy, combined with the ablation showing that providing ground-truth causal chains yields a further +20–25 points, pinpoints causal-chain construction as the primary bottleneck in multimodal causal reasoning rather than final-answer generation. The persistently high spurious answer rate (30–45%) reveals that many correct answers are produced via linguistic or visual shortcuts rather than genuine evidence-grounded reasoning, a concern for deployment in safety-critical video analysis. Open questions include whether scaling alone can close the causal-chain gap (InternVL-30B shows no grounding improvement over 8B), how to train models to produce faithful ST causal chains, and whether the task generalizes beyond the 13.7-second average clip length used here.
