## Problem

Generative VER models like AutoVER achieve strong accuracy but suffer from high inference latency (~1569 ms), limited generalization to unseen entities, and heavy computational demands (13B parameters, 47M training pairs). Contrastive approaches are efficient but have historically underperformed, leaving a gap between accuracy and practical deployability in open-domain VER with millions of candidate entities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single 224×224 RGB query image and returns the highest-scoring Wikipedia entity from a label space of up to ~6M entities. Entity-side inputs are Wikipedia documents (text truncated to 256 tokens + associated images) whose representations are precomputed offline; at query time only a single forward pass through the frozen CLIP encoder and a FAISS nearest-neighbor lookup are required, yielding ~14.5 ms latency.

## Method

WikiCLIP is a dual-encoder framework with a frozen EVA-CLIP-8B visual encoder for queries and a lightweight Vision-Guided Knowledge Adaptor (VGKA) for entities. The VGKA uses CLIP patch-level features as cross-attention queries against LLaMA3 token embeddings (projected to the CLIP dimension) as keys/values, selectively retaining text tokens that are visually grounded, then mean-pools them into a single entity vector in the CLIP embedding space. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss with a Hard Negative Synthesis strategy: visually similar queries are clustered into batches, and their entity text descriptions are swapped to create synthetic negatives that share the same image but carry mismatched semantics, forcing the model to learn fine-grained textual distinctions. Only the 0.08B-parameter cross-attention module is trained (19–23 h on 8 A100s), while all encoder weights remain frozen.

## Results

On the OVEN Entity benchmark, WikiCLIP-L achieves 28.5% unseen accuracy and 31.6% harmonic mean (HM), surpassing the previous contrastive SOTA CLIP2CLIP (11.5% HM) by over 20 points and outperforming the generative SOTA AutoVER-13B on unseen accuracy (28.5% vs. 24.5%). On INFOSEEK, WikiCLIP-L reaches 62.7% overall accuracy without any task-specific fine-tuning, exceeding Echosight (53.2%) which was explicitly trained on E-VQA data. Inference latency is 14.49 ms per query, roughly 108× faster than AutoVER-13B (1569 ms), while requiring only 0.08B tunable parameters and 1.9M training samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generative baseline AutoVER-13B, WikiCLIP-L wins on unseen accuracy (28.5% vs. 24.5%) and HM is competitive (31.6% vs. 35.6%), while reducing inference latency from 1569 ms to 14.49 ms (~108× speedup) and using only 0.08B tunable parameters versus 13B. Compared to the previous contrastive SOTA CLIP2CLIP, WikiCLIP-L improves HM by 20.1 points (31.6% vs. 11.5%) and unseen accuracy by 18 points. Against GiT-Large trained on the massive REW-47M dataset, WikiCLIP-L achieves higher unseen accuracy (28.5% vs. 25.1%) and HM (31.6% vs. 29.6%) using only 1.9M OVEN samples and no external data. On zero-shot MLLMs, WikiCLIP-L outperforms GPT-4V (28.5% vs. 19.3% unseen) and GPT-5-nano (28.5% vs. 13.0% unseen) on OVEN. On INFOSEEK, WikiCLIP-L (62.7% overall) surpasses Echosight (53.2%) which was fine-tuned on E-VQA; on E-VQA, WikiCLIP-L (31.9% overall) trails Echosight (36.5%) but the latter was explicitly fine-tuned on that dataset. WikiCLIP-L does not match AutoVER-13B on seen accuracy (35.5% vs. 63.6%), reflecting the generative model's advantage on in-distribution entities.

## Relation to Prior Work

WikiCLIP revisits the contrastive dual-encoder paradigm for open-domain Visual Entity Recognition after generative methods (AutoVER, GER-ALD, REW) had established dominance, arguing that contrastive learning remains a powerful but underexplored alternative. It builds directly on CLIP's embedding space as the matching substrate, but departs from prior contrastive VER methods (CLIPFusion, CLIP2CLIP) by replacing the CLIP text encoder with LLM embeddings and introducing a Vision-Guided Knowledge Adaptor that uses patch-level visual features to selectively filter encyclopedic text via cross-attention. The Hard Negative Synthesis strategy is conceptually distinct from AutoVER's visually grounded hard negative mining, instead synthesizing negatives through textual perturbation of visually similar entities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× inference speedup (14.49 ms vs. 1569 ms) and 0.08B tunable parameters make contrastive VER practically deployable in latency-sensitive pipelines where generative models are infeasible, while the superior unseen-entity generalization (28.5% vs. 24.5%) challenges the assumption that generative paradigms inherently generalize better. A key open question highlighted by the authors is how to better exploit LLM knowledge: performance saturates at 256 tokens and scaling from 3B to 8B LLMs yields only marginal gains, suggesting the bottleneck lies in representation extraction rather than model capacity. Future work on dedicated text-embedding models optimized for encyclopedic knowledge extraction could unlock further improvements.
