## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer severe performance degradation under extreme illumination (overexposure or near-darkness), where RGB images lose irrevocable structural and semantic information, leading to hallucinations and factual errors. Existing mitigation strategies—image restoration pipelines, tool-calling agents, or specialized low-light encoders—are reactive and do not prevent information loss within the MLLM's own reasoning process. Event cameras, which capture brightness changes with >120 dB dynamic range, retain rich structural cues when RGB sensors saturate, but have never been integrated into MLLMs for instruction-following or reasoning tasks.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a degraded (extreme-light) RGB frame and its spatially/temporally matched event frame; no normal-light reference is needed. During training, a triplet of normal-light RGB, extreme-light RGB, and event frame is used, with the normal-light frame serving only as a supervision signal for feature alignment. The model outputs free-form text answers for multi-choice scene-understanding questions (with comma-separated option letters) and structured numeric sequences for object-counting tasks (e.g., '4,1,1,0,0,0' for cars, people, bicycles, trains, trucks, buses). Evaluation spans 17 brightness rates from 0.05× to 20×, with metrics averaged across all levels.

## Method

Event-MLLM fuses event and extreme-RGB features through an illumination-guided mechanism built on two components. First, an Illumination Indicator is derived by passing the extreme-light frame through a frozen DINOv2 encoder followed by a lightweight learnable MLP; this signal is concatenated with event features (extracted via the same frozen vision encoder plus a separate MLP) before being jointly fused with extreme-RGB features through a final fusion MLP, enabling adaptive, illumination-conditioned weighting of event information. Second, an Illumination Correction Loss (L2 distance between fused features and frozen-encoder features of the normal-light frame) provides a feature-space distillation objective that forces the fusion module to reconstruct normal-light semantics from degraded inputs. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) self-adaptive feature fusion, where only the fusion MLPs are trained to minimize the correction loss, and (2) LoRA fine-tuning of the full Qwen-3B/7B MLLM for instruction following. At inference, the model operates solely on the extreme-light RGB and event frame, with the illumination indicator dynamically modulating how much event information compensates for RGB degradation.

## Results

On the proposed Multi-Illumination Benchmark (averaged over 17 brightness levels from 0.05× to 20×), Event-MLLM-7B achieves 53.13% accuracy and 85.43% F1 on the Multi-Choice task, and 74.66% accuracy with 0.4557 MAE on the Object-Counting task. The 3B variant reaches 34.48% / 80.35% (Multi-Choice) and 71.08% / 0.6127 MAE (Object-Counting). Scaling from 3B to 7B yields substantial gains (+18.65% multi-choice accuracy, +3.58% object-counting accuracy), indicating the approach benefits from larger language-model capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Event-MLLM-7B outperforms all five external baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest general MLLM (InternVL3-8B), it gains +21.63% multi-choice accuracy (53.13 vs. 31.50) and +2.10% object-counting accuracy (74.66 vs. 72.56), while reducing MAE from 0.8641 to 0.4557. The event-only model EventGPT performs worst on multi-choice accuracy (3.16%), confirming that event streams alone cannot substitute for RGB semantics in instruction-following. The illumination-adaptive Q-Instruct (15.86% MC accuracy) and LLaVA-7B (18.43%) are both far below Event-MLLM, showing that neither generic MLLM robustness nor dedicated low-light instruction tuning closes the gap. Compared to its own Qwen-7B baseline (44.71% MC accuracy), the full Event-MLLM pipeline adds +8.42% MC accuracy and +3.48% F1, with object-counting MAE dropping from 0.5303 to 0.4557, validating the contribution of the Illumination Indicator and Illumination Correction Loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) event-based vision-language models (EventGPT, EventVL, LLaFEA) that process event streams without RGB fusion; (2) RGB-event fusion for low-level vision tasks (denoising, deblurring, HDR) that never reached the reasoning/instruction-following level; and (3) illumination-robust MLLMs (Q-Instruct, restoration pipelines) that address degraded inputs without leveraging complementary sensors. Event-MLLM departs from all three by introducing the first MLLM architecture that adaptively fuses event and extreme-light RGB features in a shared latent space, guided by a DINOv2-derived Illumination Indicator and supervised by an Illumination Correction Loss that distills normal-light semantics into the fused representation. The two-stage training recipe (feature alignment before LoRA fine-tuning) is a practical departure from end-to-end joint training used in prior event-MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored failure mode of MLLMs—catastrophic degradation under extreme illumination—and demonstrates that event-camera signals, long confined to low-level vision, can meaningfully rescue high-level reasoning when fused in feature space. The introduced multi-illumination event-instruction dataset (2,241 samples, 17 brightness levels) and benchmark provide the first systematic evaluation protocol for this setting, which the community lacked. Key limitations temper the impact: the method requires synchronized event-camera hardware, the dataset is relatively small, and absolute accuracy remains modest (~53% on multi-choice), suggesting substantial headroom. Open questions include generalization to sparse or intermittent event signals, extension beyond Qwen backbones, and whether the illumination-correction paradigm transfers to other degradation types (motion blur, fog, sensor noise).
