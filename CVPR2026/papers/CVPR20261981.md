## Problem

SAM's prompt-to-mask generalization, learned on in-air imagery, degrades sharply underwater due to wavelength-dependent absorption, scattering, and turbidity that shift the data distribution. Existing underwater SAM adaptations (e.g., USIS-SAM) freeze the dense prompt or jointly train prompts and parameters without an explicit mechanism to capture their mutual dependency, leaving the severe cross-domain gap unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single underwater RGB image resized to 1024×1024 and produces per-instance pixel-level segmentation masks with class labels. Internally, the image is processed by both a ViT-Huge image encoder and a frozen ResNet-50 backbone to generate complementary global and local multi-scale features, which are fused before conditioning the SAM mask decoder on a 256-dimensional dense prompt and a sparse prompt from a generator.

## Method

BiPA formulates dense prompt learning as a bilevel optimization: the upper level treats the 256-dim dense prompt as a hyperparameter optimized via Bayesian optimization (Optuna) over 16 group-level scalar weights, while the lower level updates model parameters via gradient descent on the full Mask R-CNN loss. A two-stage training strategy makes this tractable at scale (7,442 training images): the adaptation stage alternates Ka=30 inner parameter updates with Kb=21 BO trials over T=3 outer iterations, then the fine-tuning stage freezes the optimized prompt and refines model parameters for Kc=7 steps. A Foreground-Attentive Injection (FAI) block complements the ViT backbone by extracting multi-scale local features from a frozen ResNet-50, refining them with Efficient Multi-scale Attention, and fusing them into ViT features via a learned global-guided weighting that conditions on both streams. Only the dual prompts, the adapter, and the FAI block are trained, keeping the ViT-Huge and ResNet-50 backbones frozen.

## Results

On the UIIS dataset, BiPA achieves 32.1 mAP, 48.7 AP50, and 35.2 AP75, surpassing the previous best underwater-specific method USIS-SAM (29.5 mAP) by 2.6 absolute mAP (~8.8% relative) and the best generic method Mask2Former (28.1 mAP) by 4.0 mAP. On USIS10K, BiPA reaches 45.2 mAP (multi-class) and 64.2 mAP (class-agnostic), exceeding USIS-SAM by 2.1 and 4.5 mAP respectively, and ConvNeXt-V2 (best generic in class-agnostic) by 1.9 mAP. The method adds only ~5.6% parameters and 0.4% FLOPs over USIS-SAM while delivering a 6.95% average mAP gain across both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPA outperforms all 16 compared baselines on every reported metric across both UIIS and USIS10K. Against the closest underwater-specific competitor USIS-SAM [18], BiPA gains +2.6 mAP on UIIS (32.1 vs 29.5) and +2.1/+4.5 mAP on USIS10K multi-class/class-agnostic (45.2 vs 43.1; 64.2 vs 59.7), with the largest gap appearing in AP75 (52.5 vs 48.5) indicating better high-IoU mask quality. Versus the strongest generic methods, BiPA beats Mask2Former [4] by 4.0 mAP on UIIS and ConvNeXt-V2 [28] by 1.9 mAP in the USIS10K class-agnostic setting, confirming that domain-specific bilevel prompt adaptation is necessary rather than relying on generic segmentation architectures. Against SAM-based baselines (SAM+Mask R-CNN at 38.5 mAP on USIS10K), BiPA improves by 6.7 mAP, demonstrating that simply bolting SAM onto a detector is insufficient without explicit cross-domain prompt learning. The earlier underwater-specific WaterMask [17] trails by 5.7 mAP on UIIS, and all generic methods (Mask R-CNN, QueryInst, etc.) fall 4–12 mAP below BiPA, underscoring the severity of the underwater domain gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the USIS-SAM line of work, which first adapted SAM's ViT-Huge backbone and mask decoder to underwater salient instance segmentation but kept the dense prompt frozen. BiPA departs from this by jointly learning a dual prompt (dense + sparse) and formulating dense prompt learning as a bilevel optimization problem, where the prompt acts as a hyperparameter at the upper level and model parameters at the lower level. Architecturally, it introduces a Foreground-Attentive Injection (FAI) block that fuses frozen ResNet-50 multi-scale features into the ViT stream via global-guided attention, complementing SAM's global self-attention with local CNN cues. The practical solver replaces hypergradient-based bilevel methods (infeasible at the 7,442-image scale) with Bayesian Optimization via Optuna for the upper-level prompt search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiPA is the first work to apply bilevel optimization to underwater instance segmentation, providing a principled framework for prompt-to-model co-adaptation that avoids the overfitting pitfalls of naive joint training. The preservation of SAM's zero-shot generalization to unseen underwater categories (Artiodactyla, Mollusk, Garbage) after domain adaptation is practically important for ecological monitoring where new species are routinely encountered. However, the method remains tied to the SAM backbone and a specific underwater benchmark suite, and the authors acknowledge real-time performance as an open limitation, pointing toward future knowledge-distillation-based lightweight designs.
