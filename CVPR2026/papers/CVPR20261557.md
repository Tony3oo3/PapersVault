## Problem

Existing medical RAG systems rely on a single similarity signal for retrieval, ignoring complementary intra-modal and cross-modal cues in clinical data, and treat all retrieved documents uniformly during generation without accounting for their varying relevance. This leads to imprecise retrieval and dilution of critical evidence by irrelevant content, producing factually unreliable answers in high-stakes medical settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261557/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multimodal medical query consisting of a radiographic or fundus image and an associated question text, and produces a free-form answer text. A dual-encoder retriever (ResNet-50 for images, BioClinicalBERT for text) selects Top-15 candidate documents from a corpus of image-report pairs, which are concatenated with the query and fed into LLaVA-Med-1.5. The only trainable component is a lightweight adapter with three scalar weights (α, β, γ); the backbone LVLM and encoders remain frozen.

## Method

MR-RAG introduces two coupled modules. First, Multimodal Cooperative Retrieval (MCR) computes a fused relevance score by combining image-to-text, image-to-image, and text-to-text cosine similarities with learnable weights optimized via InfoNCE contrastive loss on a small dataset of positive/negative retrieval candidates. Second, Importance-Aware Information Flow Augmentation (IFA) injects these fused relevance scores into the LVLM's attention mechanism: in layers 8–15, a Min-Max-normalized relevance bias is added to the attention logits along the Reports→Question path, amplifying information flow from highly relevant documents while suppressing irrelevant ones. This soft modulation was identified through a layer-wise attention blocking analysis showing that the Reports→Question pathway in mid-depth layers is the primary conduit for evidence integration, making it the optimal target for relevance-aware steering.

## Results

MR-RAG achieves the best performance across all four metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1) on all three medical VQA datasets. Headline accuracy gains over the strongest baseline are +4.9% on HarvardFairVLMed (84.47% vs. 79.59%), +1.3% on IU-Xray (88.13% vs. 86.85%), and +6.4% on MIMIC-CXR (79.35% vs. 72.93%). On F1, MR-RAG reaches 66.78 (HarvardFairVLMed), 85.19 (IU-Xray), and 78.61 (MIMIC-CXR), consistently leading all decoding-based and RAG-based competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR-RAG outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. Among decoding-based methods, Greedy decoding is the closest competitor (72.93% Acc on MIMIC-CXR), yet MR-RAG still leads by 6.4 points; OPERA and DoLa perform well on IU-Xray (86.49% and 85.92% Acc) but fall 1.6–2.2 points behind MR-RAG. Among RAG-based methods, FactMM-RAG is the strongest baseline (79.59% Acc on HarvardFairVLMed, 86.85% on IU-Xray), but MR-RAG surpasses it by 4.9 and 1.3 points respectively, with a particularly large F1 gap on HarvardFairVLMed (66.78 vs. 60.29). MMed-RAG underperforms even the base LLaVA-Med-1.5 on HarvardFairVLMed (70.57% vs. 77.35% Acc), suggesting its retrieval strategy is poorly suited to fundus images. Against open-source Med-LVLMs, MR-RAG leads Med-Flamingo by 6.1 points on HarvardFairVLMed accuracy and MiniGPT-Med by 18.7 points, confirming that retrieval augmentation with relevance-aware attention substantially exceeds what domain-pretrained models achieve without external knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: medical RAG systems (RAMM, RULE, MMed-RAG) that improve retrieval quality but treat all retrieved documents uniformly during generation, and attention-steering techniques (ActAdd, ITI, PASTA, PAI) that modulate internal attention but are designed for unimodal settings or require heavy supervision. MR-RAG bridges these by repurposing retrieval-stage relevance scores as lightweight attention biases during generation, eliminating the need for additional training or supervision beyond a small contrastive adapter. The MCR module extends single-similarity retrieval to a learnable multi-similarity fusion, while IFA introduces a novel soft-modulation mechanism that injects document-level importance into specific attention paths (Reports→Question in layers 8–15), identified through systematic attention-blocking experiments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-stage design addresses a practical failure mode in medical AI where irrelevant retrieved documents dilute or override correct evidence, a risk that is especially dangerous in clinical decision-making. The demonstrated robustness to retrieval size K (2.6% fluctuation vs. 8.6% for beam search) suggests the method could reduce the sensitivity of deployed systems to imperfect retrieval, a common real-world concern. However, the current IFA mechanism only modulates text-to-text attention paths, leaving the role of image tokens in information flow unexplored; extending this to cross-modal attention pathways is a natural next step. The authors also note that the cooperative-retrieval-plus-importance-modulation paradigm is domain-agnostic and could transfer to other knowledge-intensive settings like legal or scientific QA.
