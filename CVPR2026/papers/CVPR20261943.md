## Problem

Existing training-free watermarking methods for diffusion models (e.g., Tree-Ring, WIND, RingID) embed watermarks in the initial noise latent and require computationally expensive DDIM inversion (~15–22 s per image) for extraction, creating a severe latency bottleneck that hinders large-scale, real-time deployment. The paper asks whether watermarking can be reframed so that extraction becomes direct and inversion-free while retaining the semantic fusion and robustness that make in-generation watermarking attractive.

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes a text prompt, a secret message W (converted to a 16-bit binary sequence), and a secret key K (which deterministically generates a binary mask matrix M selecting mid-frequency DCT positions in the first channel of the VAE latent). The output is a watermarked RGB image produced by the standard diffusion pipeline. For extraction, the watermarked image is VAE-encoded back to its latent, DCT-transformed, and the signs of coefficients at mask-specified locations are read out with a majority vote across redundant bit copies—no inversion or iterative denoising is needed, yielding extraction in ~0.24 s.

## Method

GROW replaces one-shot initial-noise embedding with progressive gradient guidance applied during the denoising loop. Starting at step t > T·r_start, the method predicts the clean latent ẑ₀ from the current noisy latent, computes the MSE between its DCT coefficients and a target signal S (constructed from the message bits at mask-selected mid-frequency positions), and takes a single gradient step to nudge ẑ₀ toward the watermark target. The modified ẑ₀ is back-projected into a guided noise prediction, which is then fed into the standard classifier-free guidance and scheduler to produce the next latent. Because each perturbation is small and applied along the U-Net's learned data manifold, the watermark is woven into natural textures (e.g., wood grain, water ripples) rather than appearing as an artifact. Extraction is simply VAE-encode → DCT → sign-read at masked locations with majority voting, making it ~100× faster than inversion-based methods.

## Results

On MS-COCO, GROW achieves an average Message Accuracy (M-ACC) of 97.8% across all attacks (Rotation, JPEG, Crop, Blur, Noise, Brightness, Adversarial), surpassing the best training-free baseline WIND (92.5%) by over 5 points. On Stable-Diffusion-Prompts, GROW reaches 97.6% average M-ACC. In terms of imperceptibility, GROW attains PSNR of 27.54 dB and FID of 12.32 on MS-COCO, substantially outperforming all initial-noise-based methods (e.g., WIND: PSNR 7.44, FID 21.89). Extraction time is 0.24 s per image, nearly 100× faster than inversion-based methods (12.5–21.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROW outperforms all six baselines on both imperceptibility and robustness simultaneously. Against the strongest training-free competitor WIND [2], GROW improves average M-ACC from 92.5% to 97.8% on MS-COCO while boosting PSNR from 7.44 dB to 27.54 dB (a ~20 dB gain), indicating WIND's initial-noise strategy severely degrades image quality. RingID [9] achieves comparable robustness (92.1% vs 97.8%) but at the cost of much lower PSNR (15.73 vs 27.54) and higher FID (23.63 vs 12.32). Gaussian Shading [40] is the closest on imperceptibility (PSNR 26.21) yet trails GROW by 6.5 points in average M-ACC (91.3% vs 97.8%), particularly under Rotation (59% vs 100%) and Adversarial attacks (93% vs 95%). Post-processing methods DwtDctSvd [35] and Hidden [44] achieve high PSNR but suffer catastrophic robustness (48.7% and 76.5% M-ACC respectively), confirming that in-generation embedding is essential for attack resilience. The decisive practical advantage is extraction speed: GROW extracts in 0.24 s versus 12.5–21.7 s for all inversion-based baselines, a ~50–90× speedup that enables real-time verification.

## Relation to Prior Work

GROW departs from the dominant training-free paradigm (Tree-Ring, WIND, Gaussian Shading, RingID) that embeds watermarks in the initial noise latent and requires costly DDIM inversion for extraction. Instead, it repurposes the gradient-steering mechanism from classifier-free guidance in diffusion models, applying frequency-domain (DCT) gradient guidance progressively across denoising steps to weave the watermark into the latent. Conceptually, this shifts the mechanism from 'passive scattering' (the denoiser implicitly filters a watermarked noise) to 'active guidance' (explicitly steering the trajectory toward a watermark target), while retaining the endogeneity advantage of in-generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× extraction speedup (0.24 s vs. 12–22 s) removes the primary practical barrier to deploying training-free diffusion watermarks at scale, making real-time verification feasible. GROW simultaneously improves both robustness (97.6–97.8% M-ACC) and imperceptibility (FID 12.32 on MS-COCO) over prior methods, which typically trade one for the other. Open questions include capacity limits inherent to the DCT mid-frequency band, vulnerability to geometric attacks like random-ratio stretching, and whether a learnable attack-resilient representation space (as the authors suggest) could be integrated into the progressive guidance loop without sacrificing the training-free property.
