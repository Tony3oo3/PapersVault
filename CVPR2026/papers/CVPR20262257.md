## Problem

Chain-of-thought prompting in multimodal LLMs paradoxically degrades VQA accuracy, yet prior work attributed this to weakened perception without explaining the underlying mechanism. This paper identifies the root cause as spatial attention dispersion: during multi-step reasoning, visual attention scatters away from question-relevant regions, causing the model to 'lose focus' even when perceptual descriptions remain largely correct. No training-free intervention existed to restore this focus at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image and a text question, producing a text answer as the final output. Internally, it computes per-head attention maps from the final question token to all visual tokens, derives an Entropy-Focus Ratio per head, and constructs a refined attention map over the image token grid. The only modification to the model's forward pass is a multiplicative reweighting (factor 1+γ) of attention weights for a subset of tokens in a subset of heads during autoregressive generation; no additional tokens are injected and no parameters are updated.

## Method

VRGA is a training-free, two-stage inference-time framework. First, it localizes question-relevant visual regions by selecting vision-focused heads via an Entropy-Focus Criterion (low EFR = H_img/R_img combined with high R_img), then subtracting a background-head attention map to suppress attention-sink bias, and thresholding the resulting refined map to obtain relevant token indices. Second, during response generation, it multiplies the attention weights of those tokens by (1+γ) exclusively in the selected vision-focused heads, then renormalizes to preserve total attention mass. The key enabling insight is a strong cross-model linear correlation (Pearson r > 0.9) between image attention ratio and spatial entropy, which provides a supervision-free criterion for identifying which heads genuinely process visual information. Head-masking ablations confirm that the selected heads are causally critical for visual reasoning, with accuracy dropping from ~87% to ~24–42% when masked, versus negligible drops for random or low-visual baselines.

## Results

VRGA consistently improves the comprehensive score (S) by 1–6 points across all evaluated model scales and benchmarks. On HaloQuest, Qwen2.5-VL-7B+VRGA achieves 73.96% accuracy and S=0.549 (vs. 66.67% and S=0.502 for the baseline), while Qwen2.5-VL-3B+VRGA reduces irrelevance from I=0.601 to I=0.405. On HallusionBench, VRGA lifts Qwen2-VL-7B accuracy from 55.10% to 57.10% and Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 53.40% to 55.40%. The method is training-free and requires no architectural modification, operating purely through inference-time attention reweighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRGA outperforms both CCoT (Mitra et al., CVPR 2024) and ICoT (Gao et al., CVPR 2025) across all benchmarks and model scales. CCoT, which injects detected objects and spatial relationships as structured reasoning components, actually degrades accuracy substantially—e.g., Qwen2-VL-7B on HaloQuest drops from 53.81% to 29.30%, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops from 66.67% to 51.08%. ICoT, which interleaves image patches at each reasoning step, also underperforms the baseline (Qwen2-VL-7B HaloQuest: 53.81%→47.85%; MMStar: 52.37%→42.27%). In contrast, VRGA maintains or improves accuracy while significantly reducing the irrelevance degree (I), e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B on HaloQuest sees I drop from 0.601 to 0.405. The key distinction is that CCoT and ICoT modify the input globally (injecting extra tokens or structured descriptions), which can introduce irrelevant visual details, whereas VRGA performs selective, context-aware attention reweighting only on identified visual heads, preserving reasoning fluency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-analysis line of work (Zhang et al. [30] at layer level; Liu et al. [13] on overall image attention) to a finer head-level granularity, revealing that visual processing requires both high image attention and low spatial entropy. It departs from prior mitigation strategies (ICoT, DeepEyes, CCoT, DDoT) that address *what* visual information to inject during reasoning, by instead addressing *where* the model attends—framing CoT degradation as an attention-allocation problem rather than a perceptual-availability problem. The training-free, inference-time reweighting approach contrasts with the supervised-fine-tuning or reinforcement-learning paradigms used by competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanistic finding that CoT prompts cause spatial attention dispersion (not perceptual failure) reframes how the community should think about reasoning-perception trade-offs in MLLMs, and the R_img–H_img correlation provides a principled, architecture-agnostic criterion for identifying visual heads. However, the practical gains are modest (1–6 points on comprehensive scores), the method requires white-box attention access, and experiments are limited to the Qwen model family. The authors themselves flag that post-hoc reweighting is a stopgap; the open question is whether reinforcement learning can train models to maintain focused visual attention natively during extended reasoning, which would be a more durable solution.
