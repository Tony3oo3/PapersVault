## Problem

Existing semantic grasp generation methods struggle with the large modality gap between 3D object geometry and natural language instructions, and lack explicit spatial or semantic constraints during generation. This leads to physically invalid grasps (e.g., interpenetration) or semantically inconsistent poses that fail to match the user's intended interaction, such as confusing 'grasp the handle' with 'hold the rim' of a teacup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263061/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud P_g and a free-form textual instruction I (e.g., 'Grip the handle of the mug') as input. It produces a 61-dimensional MANO parameter vector h_p and a corresponding 778-vertex hand mesh h_m. The point cloud is encoded via two independent PointNet encoders (one for geometry, one for the predicted affordance map), while the instruction is encoded by RoBERTa; the hand pose is operated on in a compact latent space learned by a pre-trained autoencoder rather than in raw vertex space.

## Method

AffordGrasp comprises three integrated components: (1) an Affordance Generator (based on LASO architecture) that predicts point-wise affordance probabilities from the object point cloud and instruction, trained with Focal+Dice loss and expanded via a self-training pseudo-labeling loop; (2) a cross-modal latent diffusion model that synthesizes hand poses in a VAE latent space conditioned on a fused triplet of RoBERTa text features, PointNet geometry features, and PointNet affordance features, sampled via DDIM; and (3) a Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM) that performs a single-pass post-sampling refinement by fusing the denoised latent with spatial and instruction features through multi-head attention with a dual residual mechanism, trained with the diffusion model frozen and supervised by reconstruction plus physical contact losses. The key architectural insight is using the predicted affordance map as an explicit intermediate representation that bridges linguistic semantics and 3D geometry, reducing the cross-modal alignment burden on the diffusion process itself.

## Results

On the in-domain OakInk dataset, AffordGrasp achieves 80.08% semantic accuracy (ACC), outperforming FastGrasp (78.05%), D-VQVAE (76.4%), and TTA (60.83%), while also attaining the lowest simulation displacement (1.43) and highest contact ratio (98%). On GRAB, it reaches 62.50% ACC with the lowest penetration volume (3.06) among all methods. In out-of-domain evaluation, AffordGrasp achieves 72.00% ACC on HO-3D and 69.71% on AffordPose, substantially surpassing the next-best baselines (66% and 63.99% respectively) and reducing penetration volume by roughly 40–55% compared to prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffordGrasp outperforms all three baselines on semantic accuracy (ACC) across all four datasets: on OakInk it leads FastGrasp by 2.03 points (80.08% vs 78.05%) and TTA by 19.25 points; on GRAB it edges out FastGrasp by 1.0 point (62.50% vs 61.50%); on HO-3D it surpasses TTA by 6 points (72% vs 66%); and on AffordPose it beats D-VQVAE by 5.72 points (69.71% vs 63.99%). On penetration volume, AffordGrasp achieves the lowest values on GRAB (3.06 vs 4.61 for FastGrasp), HO-3D (7.38 vs 12.55 for TTA), and AffordPose (10.36 vs 19.41 for TTA), though on OakInk it is nearly tied with D-VQVAE (7.31 vs 7.33). The one metric where AffordGrasp does not lead is simulation displacement on GRAB, where FastGrasp achieves 1.20 vs AffordGrasp's 1.66, though the gap is modest. The ControlNet ablation variant (replacing DAM with ControlNet) consistently underperforms the full pipeline, confirming that the Distribution Adjustment Module is critical for the observed gains in physical plausibility and semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-conditioned grasp synthesis line (Text2Grasp, SemGrasp) by inserting an explicit affordance prediction stage (borrowing the LASO architecture) as an intermediate geometric-semantic bridge, rather than fusing raw point clouds and text directly. The generative backbone follows the latent diffusion paradigm (LDM) with a VAE-encoded hand latent, and the post-sampling Distribution Adjustment Module departs from training-free guidance methods (e.g., SGC, AffordDP) by learning a lightweight single-pass refinement that enforces contact and semantic constraints without iterative gradient steps. The automated self-training annotation pipeline also distinguishes it from VLM-based multi-turn labeling approaches that suffer from error propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, test-time-adaptation-free pipeline for instruction-aligned grasp generation with strong out-of-domain generalization (HO-3D, AffordPose), which is critical for embodied AI and AR/VR applications where objects are unseen. The DAM module provides a reusable pattern for post-sampling constraint enforcement in diffusion-based generation beyond grasping. Key limitations remain: the method is purely data-driven without explicit physics priors (gravity, friction), and the affordance generator's reliance on pseudo-labels from a self-training loop may propagate systematic errors. Open questions include whether integrating physics-based simulation during training or inference could close the remaining gap to fully stable real-world grasps.
