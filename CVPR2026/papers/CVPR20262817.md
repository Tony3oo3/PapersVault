## Problem

Existing radiotherapy dose prediction models are trained from scratch on limited RT data and struggle to generalize across institutions, scanners, and planning protocols, while pretrained diffusion priors from distant domains (natural video, CT) have not been explored for generative transfer to dose prediction. Additionally, standard diffusion training optimizes voxel-level reconstruction losses rather than clinically meaningful plan-quality criteria, and RL-based preference alignment has not been applied to RT dose planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates on seven 3D volumetric modalities (planning CT, PTV mask, OAR mask, body mask, beam plate, angle plate, dose) all rasterized onto a shared voxel grid and encoded into a common VAE latent space. In the primary dose-prediction setting, CT plus anatomical masks and beam geometry serve as conditions while dose is the generation target, but the Any2Any formulation allows any modality to be the prediction target from the remaining six. All inputs are min-max scaled to [-1,1] before modality-specific 3D patch embedding, and the VAE is frozen throughout training.

## Method

DiffKT3D transfers pretrained 3D diffusion backbones (Wan 2.1 1.3B DiT or MAISI) to dose prediction via lightweight fine-tuning of only the DiT blocks while keeping the VAE frozen. The Any2Any conditioning scheme uses modality-specific patch embedding heads, a binary role embedding (target vs. condition) added per-token, and slot-aware 4D RoPE that extends standard 3D rotary positional encoding with a dedicated slot axis to distinguish modality origins, all processed through full self-attention without cross-attention. Training uses a v-parameterized diffusion objective with uniform target-modality sampling and curriculum masking over conditioning subsets. A ScardNFT RL post-training stage, adapted from DiffusionNFT, converts institutional Scorecard metrics (DVH points, mean dose, ring constraints) into a normalized reward and optimizes a dual implicit-policy loss that increases likelihood for clinically preferred dose configurations while preserving voxel-level fidelity via a combined diffusion loss.

## Results

On the GDP-HMM test set, DiffKT3D (Any2Any + ScardNFT) achieves a voxel-wise MAE of 1.93 Gy, reducing the top challenge solution's 2.07 Gy by 0.14 Gy, while also improving the clinical Scorecard from 134.81 to 137.55, PSNR from 32.06 to 32.73 dB, SSIM from 0.974 to 0.980, and LPIPS from 0.033 to 0.020. On the REQUITE prostate test set, the Any2Any model reaches MAE 1.01 Gy (0.97 Gy with best-of-10 sampling), outperforming the best regression baseline (tyxiong123, MAE 1.37 Gy) by 0.36 Gy and improving PSNR from 34.74 to 36.80 dB. The ScardNFT post-training stage adds a further +2.89 Scorecard points (135.36 → 138.17 on validation) with negligible change in MAE (1.90 → 1.91), confirming that RL reshapes clinical preference without degrading voxel-level fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffKT3D outperforms all six regression-based challenge entries (Yasin, tyxiong123, rcgao, PVmed, SKLSDE-BH, MedVision) on every metric in both validation and test splits. Against the strongest regression baseline (Yasin, MAE 2.07 Gy test), the full Any2Any + ScardNFT model reduces MAE to 1.93 Gy (−0.14 Gy) and gains +2.74 Scorecard points (134.81 → 137.55). Compared to the MAISI-based diffusion baseline (MAE 1.95 Gy test), DiffKT3D achieves a further 0.02 Gy MAE reduction and a +2.32 Scorecard improvement, while the conditional DiT baseline (MAE 2.12 Gy) is surpassed by 0.19 Gy, confirming that the Any2Any formulation is critical. On REQUITE prostate, the Any2Any model (MAE 1.01 Gy) beats the best regression baseline tyxiong123 (MAE 1.37 Gy) by 0.36 Gy and improves PSNR by 2.06 dB, with best-of-10 sampling pushing MAE to 0.97 Gy. The ScardNFT post-training stage specifically improves the clinical Scorecard by +2.89 points (validation) over the Any2Any-only model while keeping MAE essentially unchanged (1.90 → 1.91), demonstrating that RL alignment targets plan-quality preferences rather than reconstruction accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan 2.1 video diffusion backbone (a 1.3B-parameter DiT) and the MAISI medical CT diffusion model, repurposing their pretrained generative priors for radiotherapy dose prediction—a domain where prior diffusion-based methods (e.g., DiffDP) were trained from scratch in slice-wise formulations. The Any2Any conditioning scheme draws conceptually from UniDiffuser, Versatile Diffusion, and OmniGen, but adapts them to heterogeneous clinical modalities (CT, PTV/OAR masks, beam geometry) via modality-specific patch embeddings, role-aware token tagging, and slot-aware 4D RoPE rather than cross-attention. The RL post-training stage (ScardNFT) is a direct adaptation of DiffusionNFT's implicit policy-target formulation, with the reward replaced by a clinically interpretable Scorecard metric, distinguishing it from general-purpose preference frameworks like ImageReward or HPS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that large pretrained video diffusion models can transfer effectively to 3D clinical dose prediction with only light fine-tuning, and that RL-based post-training with explicit clinical Scorecards can improve plan-quality alignment without degrading voxel-level fidelity—two findings that together suggest a scalable recipe for RT planning where data are scarce and institutional preferences vary. The Any2Any formulation also enables cross-modal imputation (e.g., predicting CT or beam plates from dose), broadening the framework beyond single-target dose prediction. Key limitations include the high computational cost of the 1.3B Wan backbone with full 3D attention, evaluation on only two-to-three cancer sites, and the reliance on deterministic ODE sampling for RL candidates; open questions include whether lighter backbones or sparse attention can preserve the transfer benefit, and whether the Scorecard reward generalizes to sites without established templates.
