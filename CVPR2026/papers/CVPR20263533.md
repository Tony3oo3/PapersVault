## Problem

Existing 3D shape generative models produce visually plausible but physically invalid geometries—car wheels intersecting the body, chairs with broken legs, aerodynamic shapes with wide turbulent wakes—because they are trained solely on static shape datasets without the engineering physics (e.g., aerodynamic efficiency) that governs real-world design. No prior method integrates physical knowledge directly into the generative process in latent space, leaving a gap between visual fidelity and functional correctness for industrial design applications.

## Input / Output

The method optionally accepts a 2D image (sketch or real single-view photo) as a conditioning signal, along with a target drag coefficient and optionally a target surface pressure field. The output is a 3D mesh reconstructed via marching cubes from a learned SDF, together with a continuous surface pressure field P: ℝ³ → ℝ⁺ and a scalar drag coefficient C_d, all decoded from a single unified latent code. The latent space jointly encodes geometry and physics, so physical properties are directly recoverable from the same code that defines the shape.

## Method

PhysGen introduces a Shape-and-Physics VAE (SP-VAE) built on Dora's dual cross-attention encoder, extended with a pressure decoder (fusing self-attention, squeeze-excitation channel, and MLP branches) and a drag decoder, all trained in two stages (independent pretraining then joint fine-tuning) to produce a latent space where geometry and aerodynamics are co-represented. On top of this, a rectified-flow matching model generates shapes via an alternating update strategy: Phase 1 performs velocity-based denoising with a physics-aware regularization term that backpropagates the drag decoder's deviation from the target coefficient (analogous to classifier guidance), while Phase 2 applies M steps of physical refinement that compute directional forces (drag, lateral, lift) from the predicted surface pressure, face normals, and areas, and backpropagate a composite force loss to the latent code. The refined latent is re-noised to a mid-timestep and the cycle repeats for K iterations, ensuring the trajectory stays on the shape manifold while progressively satisfying physical objectives—avoiding the geometric distortion that pure post-optimization methods suffer once shapes drift out of distribution.

## Results

On shape reconstruction (DrivAerNet++), PhysGen achieves 96.73% overall accuracy and 91.89% overall IoU, outperforming the strongest baseline Dora† (95.31% / 88.61%) and Hi3DGen (91.47% / 81.52%). For drag coefficient estimation, the method attains MSE 4.0×10⁻⁵ and MAE 4.83×10⁻³, roughly 2.3× lower MSE than the next-best TripNet (9.1×10⁻⁵). In the unified physics-guided generation setting, PhysGen reaches F-score(0.01) of 89.65 and CD of 20.99×10⁻³, improving over the post-optimization baseline (SP-VAE+TripOptimizer) by 21.1% in F-score and 22.7% in CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysGen outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated task. In unified physics-guided generation (Table 1), it beats the post-optimization pipeline SP-VAE+TripOptimizer [38] by 15.7 points in F-score (89.65 vs. 73.93) and 6.14 in CD (20.99 vs. 27.13×10⁻³), while the stronger TripOptimizer† setting actually degrades to 67.70 F-score due to shape distortion. For shape reconstruction (Table 4), PhysGen surpasses Dora† [5] (the closest competitor) by 1.42 points in O-Acc and 3.28 in O-IoU, and leads Hi3DGen [44] by 5.26 in O-Acc and 10.37 in O-IoU; 3DSet2VecSet [46] and Hunyuan3D 2.1 [16] trail by 23.15 and 7.30 O-Acc points respectively. In drag coefficient estimation (Table 5), PhysGen achieves MSE 4.0×10⁻⁵, halving the error of TripNet [3] (9.1×10⁻⁵) and reducing GCNN [18] (17.1×10⁻⁵) and PointNet [33] (14.9×10⁻⁵) by over 4×. For pressure field prediction (Table 6), it edges out FigConvNet [8] (MSE 4.55 vs. 4.99×10⁻²) and substantially outperforms Transolver [40] (7.15×10⁻²) and RegDGCNN [12] (8.29×10⁻²). In structural optimization (Fig. 8), PhysGen produces stiffer, less-deformed shapes than PhysiOpt [45], which introduces geometric artifacts due to lack of shape-manifold awareness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Dora's VAE architecture for shape encoding/decoding and rectified flow for generative modeling, but departs from both by jointly encoding physics (drag coefficient, surface pressure) alongside geometry in a unified latent space (SP-VAE). It contrasts with post-optimization approaches like TripOptimizer and PhysiOpt, which apply physics gradients after generation without shape-manifold awareness, and with recent diffusion-based physics injection methods that suffer from unreliable physics estimates on noisy intermediate samples. The key conceptual departure is integrating physics guidance directly into the flow-matching trajectory via an alternating velocity-update/physics-refinement loop, rather than treating physics as a separate post-processing stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in industrial design where visually plausible 3D shapes can be physically infeasible (e.g., intersecting car wheels, unstable chair legs), and demonstrates that physics-aware generation can simultaneously improve geometric accuracy and aerodynamic efficiency. The finding that joint shape-physics encoding actually improves shape reconstruction over geometry-only VAEs suggests that physical constraints act as useful inductive biases for geometry learning. Open questions include generalization beyond automotive aerodynamics to other physics domains (thermal, structural at scale), the computational cost of the alternating loop for real-time design, and whether the approach extends to multi-physics or coupled systems.
