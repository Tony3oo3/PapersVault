## Problem

Existing video trajectory editing methods struggle with precise camera control and long-range temporal consistency: embedding-based approaches lack fine-grained pose control, while warp-and-repaint methods construct each coarse frame from a single source frame, causing source misalignment and temporal flicker when inpainted regions drift across chunked segments in long videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (e.g., 720×960 or 1280×720, 5–15 s) and a target camera trajectory as input, and produces a new video following the target path while preserving scene content. Internally, Pi3 estimates per-frame point clouds and camera poses, VGGT provides depth for training data, and the model operates at 480×832 resolution. The autoregressive generator emits 20-frame segments conditioned on 21 overlapping history frames, enabling arbitrarily long outputs.

## Method

The framework has two core components. First, a hybrid warping scheme decouples each source frame into dynamic regions (warped one-to-one to the target view) and static regions (progressively fused into a lightweight world cache by iteratively sampling L frames and appending only newly visible points); the cache is rendered to target poses and depth-fused with the dynamic warp to produce globally consistent coarse frames. Second, a Coarse-Video-Controlled Diffusion Model (CCDM) built on Wan-T2V-1.3B uses ControlNet to condition on the coarse video and masks, Plücker embeddings for camera poses, and LoRA-augmented source-frame token concatenation for motion/appearance priors. For long videos, a history-guided autoregressive scheme processes T=20 current frames jointly with T*=21 history frames (held Δt noise steps ahead), and after each segment the world cache is incrementally updated with newly inpainted static regions via SAM2 segmentation and Pi3 point-cloud estimation, reinforcing already-generated content in subsequent segments.

## Results

On the iPhone-PTZ benchmark (full video), CamDirector achieves PSNR 13.99, LPIPS 0.4752, and FID 72.33, outperforming Gen3C (13.27 / 0.5497 / 86.21) by a clear margin while using only 2.0B parameters versus Gen3C's 6.7B. On the original iPhone benchmark (full video), it reaches PSNR 14.12, LPIPS 0.5103, and FID 107.44, again the best across all three metrics. VBench evaluation on full-length videos further confirms superiority in subject consistency (0.8574 vs. 0.8228 on iPhone-PTZ), background consistency (0.8816 vs. 0.8597), and imaging quality (0.7557 vs. 0.7304).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CamDirector is compared against three prior VTE methods: RecamMaster (1.3B), TrajectoryCrafter (5.3B), and Gen3C (6.7B). RecamMaster and TrajectoryCrafter can only produce short clips (41 frames), so full-video metrics are unavailable; on short clips, CamDirector still leads by 1.0–3.6 dB in PSNR and 0.10–0.29 in LPIPS. Gen3C is the only baseline that supports full-length generation and is the closest competitor, yet CamDirector surpasses it on every metric on both benchmarks — e.g., on iPhone-PTZ full video, PSNR improves from 13.27 to 13.99 (+0.72 dB), LPIPS drops from 0.5497 to 0.4752 (−0.074), and FID drops from 86.21 to 72.33 (−13.9). Notably, CamDirector achieves these gains with only 2.0B parameters, roughly one-third of Gen3C's 6.7B. On VBench full-video metrics, CamDirector also wins on subject consistency, background consistency, temporal flickering, and imaging quality on both datasets, with the largest gap in subject consistency on iPhone (0.9400 vs. 0.8510).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: video trajectory editing (VTE) and long-video generation. On the VTE side, it departs from the two dominant paradigms—embedding-based camera control (RecamMaster, GCD) and single-frame warp-and-repaint (TrajectoryCrafter, Gen3C)—by introducing a hybrid warping scheme that explicitly aggregates the entire source video into a world cache for static regions while directly warping dynamic regions, thereby avoiding the limited representational capacity of embeddings and the information loss of per-frame warping. On the long-video side, it adapts history-guided autoregressive diffusion (inspired by Diffusion Forcing and related work) to the VTE setting, where the additional constraint of strict spatial alignment with the source video makes naive autoregressive extension insufficient. The base architecture builds on Wan-T2V-1.3B with ControlNet conditioning and RecamMaster-style source-token concatenation, augmented with LoRA for adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The iPhone-PTZ benchmark is a practically important contribution: it addresses clear limitations of the widely used iPhone dataset (only 5 scenes, simple handheld-to-static motions, narrow FOVs) by introducing 10 scenes with dolly, pan, orbiting motions and substantially larger trajectory variations, giving the community a more realistic evaluation target. The hybrid warping + progressive world-cache update provides a concrete, memory-efficient mechanism for long-range source alignment that prior warp-and-repaint methods lacked, and the 2B-parameter model outperforming 5–7B competitors suggests the architectural choices (explicit 3D aggregation over implicit attention) are more effective than brute-force scaling. The acknowledged limitation—over-smoothed textures due to training on a synthetic dataset with coarse rendered textures—points to an open question about whether real multi-view data or higher-fidelity synthetic pipelines can close the photorealism gap without sacrificing the geometric consistency that the world cache provides.
