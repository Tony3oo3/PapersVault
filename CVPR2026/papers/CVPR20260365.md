## Problem

Flow Matching models produce curved transport trajectories when trained with random or batch-wise couplings, increasing discretization error and degrading sample quality especially at low step counts. Existing remedies—iterative rectification, shortcut/average-field methods—either cause model collapse or reduce step count without actually straightening the underlying vector field. The paper asks whether the optimal-transport coupling problem can be made tractable and effective by decomposing it into smaller, cluster-local subproblems.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method first samples a cluster index k (proportional to cluster size), then draws a noise vector from the cluster-specific Gaussian N(μ_{0,k}, Σ_{0,k}) rather than a single global N(0,I). For conditional image generation the class label is appended as conditioning; for robotic manipulation the input is a text instruction paired with a visual observation. Outputs are 2D point clouds, 32×32 or 256×256 RGB images, or robot action sequences, generated by integrating the learned ODE in T steps (as few as 1).

## Method

COT-FM alternates two stages. In Stage 1, a pretrained FM model is run in reverse (ODE integration from t=1 to t=0) to trace each data sample back to its source; per-cluster Gaussian statistics (mean and covariance) are then estimated from these reversed samples. In Stage 2, batch-wise optimal transport is computed within each cluster between the drawn source samples and the cluster's data samples, producing a set of cluster-wise OT maps that jointly define a straighter target vector field; the FM model is fine-tuned to regress this field via the standard conditional flow-matching loss. Because each cluster contains far fewer samples than the full dataset, the batch OT approximation is more accurate and the resulting paths are significantly straighter. The framework is plug-and-play—it modifies only the target probability path, leaving the FM architecture and I/O interface unchanged—and converges within one or two alternating cycles.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, COT-FM reduces FID from 12.6 to 8.23 at 10 steps and from 4.45 to 3.97 at 50 steps compared to Rectified Flow, and dramatically improves 1-step FID from 378.0 to 205.0 and 2-step FID from 173 to 59.1. On 2D transport benchmarks, COT-FM achieves the lowest Wasserstein distance (0.1995 on 5-Gaussian, 0.0266 on Two Moons, 0.2550 on Checkerboard) and lowest curvature across all methods. On LIBERO robotic manipulation, COT-FM reaches 96.1% (Spatial) and 94.5% (Long) success rate with just 1 NFE, surpassing FLOWER's 4-step performance of 97.1% and 93.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COT-FM outperforms Rectified Flow [26] across all sampling budgets on CIFAR-10, with the largest gains at low step counts (1-step FID drops from 378.0 to 205.0; 2-step from 173 to 59.1) and consistent improvements at 10 and 50 steps. Against OT-CFM [42], which already uses batch-wise optimal transport, COT-FM still achieves lower FID at every step (e.g., 8.23 vs. 10.6 at 10 steps), demonstrating that cluster-wise OT is more effective than global batch OT. COT-FM also improves MeanFlow [15], reducing 1-step FID from 2.92 to 2.60 and 2-step from 2.88 to 2.53, confirming that straightening the underlying vector field benefits even shortcut-based methods. On LIBERO, COT-FM with 1 NFE (96.1% Spatial, 94.5% Long) surpasses FLOWER [32] with 4 NFEs (97.1%, 93.5%) on the Long suite and matches it on Spatial, while also beating 2-Rectified Flow (95.7%, 91.5%) at the same 1-NFE budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

COT-FM builds directly on the Flow Matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the Rectified Flow paradigm (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), which established that straighter transport paths reduce discretization error and sampling steps. It departs from prior coupling strategies—random pairing, global OT (computationally infeasible), and batch-wise OT (suffers from locality issues, as in OT-CFM)—by partitioning the transport problem into cluster-level subproblems, each with its own source distribution obtained by reversing a pretrained FM model's ODE. This is conceptually distinct from k-Rectified Flow's iterative self-refinement (which risks model collapse) and from shortcut/distillation methods like MeanFlow that reduce step count without modifying the underlying vector field. The framework is explicitly positioned as architecture-agnostic and plug-and-play, requiring no changes to the FM model's input-output interface.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that COT-FM offers a drop-in training modification that simultaneously improves generation quality and reduces required sampling steps across 2D transport, image generation (CIFAR-10, ImageNet), and robotic manipulation, without retraining from scratch. Key open questions remain: the reverse ODE step for estimating cluster-wise source distributions does not scale well to very large datasets, the method's performance depends on clustering quality (K-Means on DINO features here), and the theoretical understanding of why alternating refinement converges within one or two cycles is lacking. The paper also highlights that shortcut methods like MeanFlow reduce step count but leave the underlying flow curved, suggesting that flow straightness and step reduction are orthogonal axes of improvement that future work should address jointly.
