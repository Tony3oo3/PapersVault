## Problem

Diffusion-based face swapping methods formulate the task as conditional inpainting, masking the target face to prevent identity leakage. However, this masking removes critical low-frequency attribute cues—lighting, skin tone, makeup, and subtle expression dynamics—causing swapped outputs to have plausible yet misaligned attributes. The paper addresses how to generate high-quality pseudo-labels that faithfully preserve these target attributes so a student model can learn under a direct editing objective rather than a degraded inpainting proxy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source face image (identity to transfer) and a target face image (attributes to preserve) at 512×512 resolution. The source image is masked before being fed into the PulID identity encoder, while the target is processed through OminiControl for attribute conditioning (3DMM landmarks, eye landmarks from a gaze estimator, and glass segmentation masks). The student model uniquely consumes clean, unmasked pseudo-label images as its attribute conditioning input at inference, eliminating the need for complex preprocessing pipelines or auxiliary networks that inpainting-based methods require.

## Method

APPLE is a diffusion-based teacher-student framework built on FLUX.1-Krea (rectified flow) with PulID identity encoding and OminiControl attribute conditioning (LoRA rank 64). The teacher replaces the conventional conditional inpainting objective with a conditional deblurring formulation: the target face is downsampled to 8×8 and upsampled back, with blurring applied only to the facial region via a face-parsing mask, preserving low-frequency cues (skin tone, illumination) while removing high-frequency identity details. At inference, an attribute-aware inversion scheme inverts the target image using attribute-only conditioning (no identity), producing noise enriched with target attributes but free from identity bias, which anchors fine-grained details like makeup during pseudo-label generation. The student is then trained on pseudo-triplets (source, teacher-generated pseudo-label, original target) under a direct image-editing flow-matching objective, conditioning on the clean pseudo-label rather than a degraded masked input, enabling it to outperform the teacher in attribute fidelity while maintaining competitive identity similarity.

## Results

On FFHQ, APPLE (Student) achieves the best FID of 2.18, the lowest pose error of 1.85, and the lowest expression error of 0.64 among all 11 baselines, while maintaining a competitive identity similarity of 0.54 and ID Retrieval Top-1 of 90.50%. The teacher model alone (FID 3.68, Pose 2.07, Expr. 0.70) already outperforms all prior diffusion-based methods on attribute metrics. The student's improvement over the teacher confirms that training on high-fidelity pseudo-labels with clean attribute conditioning yields further gains in fine-grained attribute preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APPLE (Student) achieves the best FID (2.18), pose error (1.85), and expression error (0.64) across all 11 baselines, substantially outperforming the closest diffusion-based competitor FaceDancer (FID 3.80, Pose 2.23, Expr. 0.74) and GAN-based methods like SimSwap (FID 18.54, Expr. 1.73). On identity metrics, CSCS (ID Sim. 0.65, Top-1 99.00%) and REFace (ID Sim. 0.60, Top-1 97.60%) report higher identity similarity, but at the cost of much worse attribute preservation (Pose 3.64/3.67, Expr. 1.44/1.08), which the authors attribute to copy-and-paste-like artifacts. APPLE's identity similarity of 0.54 and Top-1 of 90.50% are competitive with DiffFace (0.54, 90.70%) and InfoSwap (0.54, 91.40%) while delivering far superior attribute fidelity. Among diffusion-based baselines, DiffSwap shows the weakest identity transfer (ID Sim. 0.34, Top-1 41.92%), and FaceAdapter has the worst pose error (5.12), highlighting the trade-off issues in prior inpainting-based formulations. Overall, APPLE achieves the best balance between identity transfer and attribute preservation, with no single baseline dominating both dimensions simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based face swapping by replacing the standard conditional inpainting formulation (used by FaceAdapter, DiffSwap, ReFace) with a conditional deblurring objective, arguing that blurring preserves low-frequency attribute cues (lighting, skin tone, pose) that masking destroys. It builds on the rectified flow / flow-matching framework and adapts DDIM-style inversion into an attribute-aware inversion scheme that exploits residual semantic information in inverted noise rather than suppressing it. The teacher-student pseudo-labeling design departs from DreamID's approach of using an off-the-shelf GAN (FaceDancer) to generate pseudo-triplets, instead training a dedicated diffusion teacher whose outputs are more attribute-faithful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a simple blur is a strictly better conditioning signal than a binary mask for preserving target attributes—is practical and potentially transferable to other conditional generation tasks where the conditioning image must suppress identity while retaining style. The student model's design (conditioning on clean pseudo-label images rather than degraded inputs) removes the need for auxiliary attribute-extraction networks at inference, improving deployment simplicity. However, evaluation is limited to FFHQ at 512×512 with a single identity encoder (PulID), and the pseudo-label pipeline adds training complexity; whether the deblurring advantage persists at higher resolutions or with different base models remains open.
