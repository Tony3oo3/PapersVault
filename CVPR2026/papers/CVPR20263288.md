## Problem

While spiking neural networks (SNNs) offer unique temporal dynamics, it remains unclear how these temporal characteristics influence model robustness against adversarial perturbations. Existing robustness methods either adapt spatial ANN techniques (adversarial training, weight regularization) that ignore temporal structure, or exploit temporal dynamics without explaining the underlying mechanism. This paper asks: can we establish a principled, information-theoretic link between SNN temporal characteristics and robustness, and use it to design a targeted regularizer?

## Input / Output

The method accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) or event streams (DVS-CIFAR10), which are encoded into binary spike trains over T time steps for SNN processing, and produces a class label. For the TMI regularizer specifically, the input and last-layer spike trains are reduced to 1D firing-rate sequences by averaging over channel, height, and width dimensions at each time step, yielding T-length vectors in [0,1] that are then passed through a kernel density estimator with 256 uniform bins and Gaussian kernel bandwidth σ=0.4.

## Method

The paper first proves via the information bottleneck principle that the robustness error of a network is upper-bounded by the mutual information I(X;Z) between input and latent representation. It then shows that SNN temporal characteristics—specifically the cumulative firing mechanism (many membrane potential values map to the same spike) and spike-to-spike temporal dependency (each spike depends on all prior inputs)—create a many-to-one mapping that guarantees H(X|Z) > 0, yielding a strictly tighter MI bound than the spatial one-to-one mapping in ANNs where H(X|Z) = 0. Building on this, the TMI regularizer computes I(X;Z) between the first-layer input spike train and the last spike neuron layer's spike train using KDE-based density estimation on the 1D firing-rate sequences, and adds λ·I(X;Z) (with λ=0.05) to the standard cross-entropy loss. This encourages the network to compress temporal information, tightening the robustness error bound without requiring adversarial examples during training.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with VGG11 (T=8), SNN-TMI achieves 49.53% FGSM and 25.86% PGD robust accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline SNN-RAT (45.23% FGSM, 21.16% PGD) by ~4.3% and ~4.7% respectively. On CIFAR-100 with VGG11 (T=8), SNN-TMI reaches 27.32% FGSM and 12.46% PGD, surpassing SNN-RAT (25.86% FGSM, 10.38% PGD) and FEEL-SNN (9.60% FGSM, 2.04% PGD). The method also generalizes to event-based data (DVS-CIFAR10: 30.12% FGSM) and ImageNet (RN19: 9.12% FGSM, 8.56% PGD), while maintaining a training cost of only 160s per 300 epochs—comparable to non-adversarial methods and roughly half the cost of adversarial training (300s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SNN-TMI consistently outperforms all SOTA robust SNN baselines. Against SNN-RAT [12] (the strongest adversarial-training-based method), SNN-TMI gains +4.3% FGSM and +4.7% PGD on CIFAR-10 (VGG11, T=8), and +1.5% FGSM and +2.1% PGD on CIFAR-100. Against FEEL-SNN [44] and FEEL-AT [44], the gains are even larger: +17.7% FGSM and +10.4% PGD on CIFAR-100, confirming that temporal MI regularization is more effective than frequency-based encoding. Against StoG [15] and DLIF [14], SNN-TMI achieves roughly 3× higher FGSM accuracy on CIFAR-10 (49.53% vs. 16.22% and 13.24%). In the MI-estimation comparison (Table 1), the KDE-based temporal MI outperforms both the histogram-based AIMIE [17] (which actually degrades robustness by −0.97% FGSM on CIFAR-100) and the spatial MINE [4] (+0.43% vs. +1.69% FGSM improvement). In terms of training efficiency (Table 6), SNN-TMI (160s) is comparable to DLIF (170s) and StoG (150s), and roughly half the cost of full adversarial training AT [38] (300s) or SNN-RAT (294s), while delivering substantially higher robust accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) information-bottleneck-based robustness bounds for neural networks (Amjad & Geiger; Shamir et al.), which link generalization/robustness error to mutual information between input and latent representation, and (2) SNN-specific robustness methods that either adapt ANN defenses (adversarial training, SNN-RAT, DLIF) or exploit temporal dynamics heuristically (FEEL-SNN, spike-timing coding). The key departure is providing the first theoretical argument that the LIF neuron's cumulative firing mechanism and inter-spike temporal dependency create a many-to-one input-to-latent mapping, inherently compressing mutual information relative to the one-to-one spatial mapping in ANNs. The KDE-based TMI regularizer then operationalizes this insight as a lightweight training-time penalty, complementing rather than replacing existing spatial or temporal robustness techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, information-theoretic explanation for why SNNs may possess inherent robustness advantages over ANNs, moving beyond the empirical observation that discrete spike encoding helps. The TMI regularizer is composable (it can be added to SNN-RAT, FEEL-SNN, etc.) and computationally cheap (160s vs. 300s for adversarial training), making it practically attractive. Open questions include whether the MI bound is tight enough to be predictive in practice, whether the KDE-based estimation is optimal for high-dimensional spike trains, and whether the temporal-compression argument generalizes to other recurrent or event-driven architectures beyond LIF-based SNNs. A limitation is that the theoretical bound remains quite loose, so the regularizer is best understood as a heuristic instantiation of the theoretical insight rather than a direct optimization of the bound.
