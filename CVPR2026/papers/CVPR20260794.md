## Problem

Existing 3D foundation models excel at global tasks like retrieval and classification but transfer poorly to fine-grained part-level reasoning. Current dense 3D approaches rely on multi-view rendering pipelines that are computationally expensive, lack geometric grounding (predictions depend on 2D appearance rather than 3D structure), and degrade significantly without careful LLM prompt engineering. No prior method achieves open-vocabulary 3D part segmentation in a single feed-forward pass directly from point clouds.

## Input / Output

The model consumes only XYZ coordinates of a 2048-point cloud (no RGB, normals, or other modalities) and a free-form text query naming the target part. It outputs 128 patch-level language-aligned feature vectors (each patch covering 32 points), which at inference are matched against text embeddings via cosine similarity; the argmax label per patch is then propagated to individual points through nearest-centroid assignment, yielding a dense point-level segmentation map.

## Method

PatchAlign3D is a 12-layer PointBERT-style transformer encoder operating on 128 patches of 32 points each, pre-trained in two stages on 32K Objaverse shapes with SAM+Gemini pseudo-annotations. Stage 1 distills dense DINOv2 features (back-projected from 10 rendered views and aggregated to patch level) into the 3D encoder via a cosine-similarity regression loss, transferring fine-grained visual priors into geometric tokens. Stage 2 freezes all but the final transformer block and a linear projector, then aligns patch embeddings with OpenCLIP ViT-bigG-14 text embeddings using a SigLIP-style sigmoid contrastive loss with fractional multi-positive labels (y_{i,j} ∈ [0,1] proportional to the fraction of patch points belonging to part j) and within-sample negatives only. The key design choice is operating at the patch level rather than the point level: local aggregation averages out the noise and inconsistency inherent in SAM-derived annotations, and the multi-positive fractional-label objective handles ambiguous boundaries without requiring clean per-point supervision.

## Results

On ShapeNetPart, PatchAlign3D achieves 56.9% mIoU and 53.1% cIoU, surpassing the strongest rendering-based baseline COPS (25.6% mIoU, 32.2% cIoU) by +31.3 and +20.9 points respectively, and the strongest feed-forward baseline Find3D (23.3% mIoU, 23.9% cIoU) by +33.6 and +29.2 points. On FAUST (non-rigid human shapes), it reaches 67.8% mIoU vs. Find3D's 63.2%. On PartNetE and ScanObjectNN, PatchAlign3D scores 41.4% and 22.7% mIoU respectively, outperforming all baselines by 14+ points. Inference time is 0.4 s per shape, matching feed-forward baselines and far faster than rendering-based methods (1.2–1.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchAlign3D decisively outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest rendering-based method COPS (DINOv2 multi-view), it gains +31.3 mIoU on ShapeNetPart and +14.4 mIoU on PartNetE, while also being ~3× faster at inference (0.4 s vs. 1.38 s). Against the strongest feed-forward baseline Find3D (trained on the same data), it improves by +33.6 mIoU on ShapeNetPart, +4.6 mIoU on FAUST, +25.0 mIoU on PartNetE, and +3.9 mIoU on ScanObjectNN, demonstrating that the two-stage patch-level training yields substantially stronger local representations. On Objaverse-General, PatchAlign3D exceeds Find3D on both seen (+8.6 mIoU) and unseen (+1.0 mIoU) categories. The mesh-based baselines SATR and 3DH are included for reference but are not directly comparable due to their reliance on high-resolution mesh inputs; SATR achieves 32.8 mIoU on ShapeNetPart but requires 111 s per shape. PointCLIPv2 and PartSLIP are consistently the weakest point-cloud baselines, confirming that CLIP-based rendering pipelines struggle with fine-grained part-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PatchAlign3D positions itself at the intersection of 3D foundation models (OpenShape, ULIP, Uni3D) and multi-view rendering pipelines (COPS, PointCLIPv2, SATR), arguing that neither paradigm adequately handles local part-level reasoning. It builds directly on Find3D's data engine (SAM + Gemini pseudo-annotations on Objaverse) and COPS's multi-view back-projection strategy for Stage 1 distillation, while adopting SigLIP's sigmoid contrastive formulation for Stage 2 text alignment. The key departure is operating at the patch level with a two-stage decoupled training (2D feature distillation, then language alignment) rather than Find3D's point-level encoder-decoder, and eliminating all rendering at inference in favor of a single feed-forward point cloud transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving zero-shot 3D part segmentation in a single 0.4s forward pass without multi-view rendering or LLM prompt engineering, PatchAlign3D removes the primary practical barriers (compute cost, prompt sensitivity, geometric ungrounding) that have limited deployment of open-vocabulary 3D segmentation. The finding that the same training data yields 56.9 vs. 23.3 mIoU depending on whether supervision is patch-level or point-level highlights that annotation granularity and training structure matter more than data scale for noisy pseudo-labels. Open questions include whether the approach scales to the full 800K+ Objaverse corpus, whether adaptive patching can handle variable point cloud sizes, and whether the encoder can be augmented with the global shape understanding that current 3D foundation models provide.
