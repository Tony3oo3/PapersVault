## Problem

Converting a single language instruction into a long-horizon, contact-rich dexterous manipulation execution remains challenging because text-to-motion synthesis produces noisy references, human-to-robot retargeting introduces embodiment bias, and fixed-reference tracking lets small deviations snowball into failure. Prior trackers treat the reference as immutable and simply chase it, so the controller becomes aggressive, contacts destabilize, and drift accelerates over long horizons. The paper asks whether placing a feedback modulator in the loop—adjusting the reference, object representation, and positional targets online—can make language-conditioned tracking robust and generalizable.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a natural-language instruction (synthesized into a time-indexed HOI reference via DiffH2O with GPT-5 paraphrase augmentation), an object point cloud (encoded to a 64-dim latent by a pre-trained point-cloud encoder), and the retargeted reference trajectory (22-D joint positions plus 5×7 rigid-body poses for the hand, 7-D pose for the object, with future references at offsets k∈{1,2,4,12}). The tracker outputs 22-D relative joint-position actions executed by a PD controller; the modulator additionally produces a residual reference adjustment, a modulated 64-dim object latent, and a small bounded positional-target correction that is added to the tracker's action before execution.

## Method

AdaDexTrack is a two-component hierarchical policy. The lower-level generalist tracker is obtained by large-scale teacher-student distillation: DiffH2O synthesizes text-guided HOI trajectories (augmented via GPT-5 paraphrases to 2,765 valid sequences over 50 objects), each is tracked by a PPO-trained specialist, and a single generalist is learned via behavior cloning on the union of successful specialist rollouts. The upper-level modulator, trained with RL under the same task reward as the tracker, performs three coupled modulations: (i) reference modulation adds small residuals to near-future hand and object references to continually re-align the tracking target and cancel accumulated drift; (ii) object-latent modulation treats the 64-dim object latent as a continuous control variable that interpolates across the tracker's implicit skill library, recruiting phase-appropriate behaviors (e.g., open hand for reach, closed hand for grasp); and (iii) positional-target modulation applies a small, bounded residual to the tracker's 22-D action to absorb high-frequency execution-level discrepancies. The key design choice is that the modulator shares the tracker's objective and operates in the same state space, ensuring tight alignment rather than a loosely coupled planner-over-controller stack.

## Results

On the unseen-trajectory split, AdaDexTrack (full modulator: R+O+T) achieves 88.99% mean-error success and 77.92% completion, with object position error of 4.49 cm and wrist position error of 8.77 cm. On the harder unseen-object split, it reaches 46.12% mean-error success and 53.78% completion (T_o = 15.45 cm, T_h = 9.21 cm). In zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, adding the modulator doubles the completion rate on unseen trajectories (52.36% vs. 26.63%) and improves unseen-object completion from 22.27% to 36.71%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaDexTrack (full modulator) outperforms DexTrack on both splits: on unseen trajectories, it improves mean-error success from 76.13% to 88.99% (+12.9 pp) and completion from 73.89% to 77.92% (+4.0 pp), while also reducing object position error from 6.93 cm to 4.49 cm. On unseen objects, the gap is 46.12% vs. 39.59% mean-error success (+6.5 pp) and 53.78% vs. 47.37% completion (+6.4 pp). Against ObjDex, the gains are larger: 88.99% vs. 51.72% mean-error success on unseen trajectories (+37.3 pp) and 46.12% vs. 28.16% on unseen objects (+18.0 pp), reflecting that ObjDex's loosely coupled planner-over-controller architecture cannot correct reference-level or representation-level drift. The tracker-only variant (no modulator) underperforms DexTrack on unseen objects (24.08% vs. 39.59% mean-error success), showing that the distilled generalist alone is insufficient without in-loop modulation; the modulator recovers and surpasses DexTrack. Vanilla RL (PPO) trained from scratch on the same corpus is consistently the weakest baseline, confirming that the distillation pipeline is essential for coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DexTrack's generalist tracker (distilled from multiple RL-trained specialists) by inserting an RL-trained modulator in the feedback loop that adjusts the reference, object latent, and positional target at every timestep. It departs from the monolithic fixed-reference tracking paradigm shared by DexTrack and from the loosely coupled planner-over-controller hierarchy of ObjDex, where the reference and object encoding remain outside the feedback loop. The modulation mechanism is conceptually related to classical trajectory adaptation methods (DMP, RMP), but replaces hand-designed dynamical representations with a learned, objective-aligned policy trained under the same task reward as the tracker.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modulator-in-the-loop design offers a practical recipe for making language-conditioned dexterous manipulation robust to the noise inherent in text-to-motion synthesis and human-to-robot retargeting, with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer demonstrating real-world viability. A key open question is whether the three modulation channels generalize beyond the specific hand-object tracking formulation, and whether the object-latent-as-continuous-knob interpretation holds for more diverse skill libraries. The reliance on explicit 6-DoF pose estimation limits applicability to symmetric objects, and the authors suggest symmetry-aware equivariant embeddings as a future direction.
