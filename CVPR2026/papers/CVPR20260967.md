## Problem

Current multimodal moving instance segmentation methods follow a simplified paradigm that extracts appearance from images and motion from events, leaving complementary cues in each modality underexploited. Event cameras, despite their high temporal resolution, produce sparse features at limited spatial resolution, making small instance segmentation particularly difficult. Additionally, event features inherently entangle appearance and motion cues, which degrades the quality of cross-modal fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260967/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized grayscale image frames and event streams, where events are discretized into B=10 temporal bins and accumulated spatially into a voxel representation. Input resolutions are downscaled to 320×180 (MouseSIS), 512×384 (SEVD-Fixed), or 346×260 (EVIMO) to match the lower spatial resolution of DVS sensors. At inference, the model produces per-instance segmentation masks filtered by a motion confidence threshold (θ=0.1), while during training, Hungarian matching assigns supervision to predicted masks and binary motion labels without thresholding.

## Method

DIMOS employs a dual-branch encoder per modality (image and event) that simultaneously extracts appearance and motion features, enforced by intra-modal contrastive learning (InfoNCE) that pulls same-type features from consecutive frames together while pushing different-type features apart. A multi-granularity cross-modal alignment then fuses the four feature streams: at the distributional level, adversarial domain adaptation with an asymmetric reference (images anchor appearance, events anchor motion) learns modality-invariant representations; at the semantic level, bidirectional convolutional translation modules enforce mutual reconstructability between cross-modal features of the same type. Extra task-specific losses—unsupervised optical flow estimation for the motion branch and bounding box regression for the appearance branch—provide additional supervision that constrains each disentangled branch to its intended semantics. The overall pipeline builds on a Mask2Former-style architecture with Hungarian matching, and all alignment losses are training-only, introducing no inference overhead.

## Results

DIMOS achieves state-of-the-art mIoU_ins on all three benchmarks: 70.25% on MouseSIS, 62.05% on SEVD-Fixed, and 72.08% on EVIMO. On MouseSIS, it also leads in mIoU_01 (77.30%) and mAP (45.18%), with a particularly large mAP gain of +14.24 over the next-best method (EvInsMOS). On SEVD-Fixed, DIMOS outperforms EvInsMOS by 5.55% in mIoU_ins under challenging outdoor conditions with small instances. On EVIMO, DIMOS achieves the best mIoU_ins and mAP but trails ModelMixSort on mIoU_01 (75.74% vs. 78.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIMOS outperforms all three baselines on the primary instance-level metric (mIoU_ins) across all three datasets. Against the image-only baseline IDOL [46], DIMOS gains +9.59% on MouseSIS, +16.56% on SEVD-Fixed, and +2.73% on EVIMO, confirming the benefit of event data and explicit disentanglement. Against the event-assisted EvInsMOS [41], DIMOS improves mIoU_ins by +7.71% (MouseSIS), +5.55% (SEVD-Fixed), and +0.82% (EVIMO), with the largest gains on the small-instance-heavy datasets. Against ModelMixSort [15], which uses a YOLO-SAM pipeline with 5.5T FLOPs, DIMOS achieves higher mIoU_ins on all three datasets (70.25 vs. 63.72, 62.05 vs. 49.56, 72.08 vs. 71.67) at a fraction of the computational cost (60–201G vs. 5.5T FLOPs). The sole metric where DIMOS trails a baseline is mIoU_01 on EVIMO (75.74% vs. ModelMixSort's 78.33%), suggesting the YOLO-SAM pipeline retains a slight edge on coarse binary foreground recall in that specific setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIMOS builds on the Mask2Former/SeqFormer video instance segmentation architecture and adopts the mask fusion and optical flow loss from EvInsMOS, but departs from the dominant multimodal MIS paradigm that assigns appearance to images and motion to events. Instead, it introduces a dual-disentangling encoder that extracts both appearance and motion features from each modality independently, motivated by the observation that event features are inherently entangled (as shown by cosine similarity analysis across training checkpoints). The multi-granularity cross-modal alignment (adversarial domain adaptation plus bidirectional modality translation) is a novel fusion strategy that goes beyond the concatenation-and-convolution approach used by prior multimodal MIS methods like ModelMixSort and EvInsMOS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal moving instance segmentation: small objects under low-light or fast-motion conditions where event sparsity and limited resolution degrade feature density. The dual-disentangling framework offers a reusable design pattern for any multimodal dense prediction task where one modality's features are entangled. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the strict dependence on synchronized paired inputs; single-modality robustness remains an open problem. The approach also carries a computational cost (60–201 G FLOPs depending on dataset) that is roughly 2× the single-encoder EvInsMOS baseline, which may limit deployment on edge devices despite the MobileNetV2 ablation showing graceful degradation.
