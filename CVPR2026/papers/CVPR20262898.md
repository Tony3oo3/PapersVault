## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation methods struggle to simultaneously achieve fine-grained semantic alignment (matching audio events to visual content) and precise temporal synchronization (aligning audio dynamics with visual motion). Prior approaches either rely on noisy paired datasets with weak audio-visual correspondence, or convert video features into text embeddings that lose temporal detail. Additionally, preference-based alignment for cross-modal video-to-audio synchronization had not been explored.

## Input / Output

The model takes an 8-second silent video clip (frames extracted at 8 fps for semantic encoding and 24 fps for temporal encoding, resized to 256×256) and a descriptive text caption as input, and produces a 44.1 kHz audio waveform. Audio is generated in a 40-dimensional VAE latent space (128-bin mel-spectrogram tokens) and decoded back to waveform via a HiFi-GAN vocoder. Both video and text conditions can be independently omitted at inference by substituting empty tokens, enabling text-only or video-only generation.

## Method

VisioSonic is a Rectified Flow Matching diffusion transformer that concatenates temporally aligned audio-video latents (via a token aligner that interpolates video embeddings to match audio token count and projects both to half-channel before channel-concatenation) and fuses them with text through a co-attention block: self-attention operates on the aligned audio-video stream while cross-attention injects text via key-value projections with a zero-initialized gate. A multimodal conditioner provides hierarchical guidance—global video-text embeddings from CLIP (8 fps) for semantics and high-frame-rate Synchformer features (24 fps) for temporal cues—applied as scaling and gating modulations. STAR-DPO extends the framework with automatic preference optimization: ImageBind-AV and Synchformer serve as reward models to rank N generated audio candidates per prompt, selecting best/worst pairs to fine-tune the model with a DPO objective augmented by the RFM loss, eliminating the need for human-annotated preference labels.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, VisioSonic w/STAR-DPO (151M params) achieves the best distribution-matching scores: FD_PaSST of 55.48, FD_PANNs of 4.36, KL_PANNs of 1.44, and KL_PaSST of 1.29, while also attaining the highest Inception Score (18.41) and the lowest DeSync (0.41 s) for temporal alignment. The base model (without STAR-DPO) already leads on FD_PaSST (58.27) and IS (18.12) with the fewest trainable parameters (151M) among all compared methods. On out-of-domain benchmarks (MovieGen-Audio and Kling-Audio), VisioSonic achieves the best DeSync scores (0.72 and 0.50 s respectively), confirming strong zero-shot temporal synchronization. In the user study, VisioSonic w/STAR-DPO receives the highest ratings across all criteria (Overall 4.60/5), surpassing MMAudio (4.45) and Frieren (3.78).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisioSonic w/STAR-DPO outperforms all baselines on distribution matching (FD_PaSST, FD_PANNs, KL_PANNs, KL_PaSST) and perceptual quality (IS), while using the fewest parameters (151M vs. 157M–1.22B for competitors). Against the closest competitor MMAudio-S (157M), VisioSonic improves FD_PaSST by 9.77 (55.48 vs. 65.25), FD_PANNs by 1.19 (4.36 vs. 5.55), and DeSync by 0.034 s (0.41 vs. 0.444). The only metric where VisioSonic trails is IB-score: HunyuanVideoFoley reports 36.0 (benefiting from IB-filtered training data) versus VisioSonic's 33.1, and V2A-Mapper achieves a lower FD_VGG (0.84 vs. 0.99). On out-of-domain benchmarks, VisioSonic achieves the best DeSync on both MovieGen-Audio (0.72 vs. 0.74 for HunyuanVideoFoley) and Kling-Audio (0.50 vs. 0.54), though HunyuanVideoFoley leads on IB-score (0.35 vs. 0.27 and 0.38 vs. 0.32). In the subjective user study, VisioSonic w/STAR-DPO wins on all four criteria, with the largest margin over MMAudio in temporal synchronization (4.75 vs. 4.65) and overall quality (4.60 vs. 4.45).

## Relation to Prior Work

VisioSonic builds on the rectified flow matching paradigm and the Next-DiT co-attention architecture, but departs from MMAudio's MMDiT-style shared self-attention by explicitly concatenating audio-video latents along the temporal axis and using a separate cross-attention path for text conditioning. The multimodal conditioner repurposes CLIP (semantic) and Synchformer (temporal) as frozen feature extractors, following the broader trend of leveraging pre-trained encoders for cross-modal guidance. STAR-DPO extends the DPO-for-diffusion line (DPO-Diffusion, VideoDPO, TangoFlux) from single-modality generation to cross-modal V2A alignment, replacing human-annotated preferences with automatic ranking via ImageBind and Synchformer as proxy reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 151M-parameter model matching or exceeding 1B+ parameter competitors (MMAudio-L, FoleyCrafter) on VGGSound suggests that architectural efficiency in cross-modal attention design can substitute for raw model scale in V2A generation, a practical finding for resource-constrained deployment. STAR-DPO's automatic preference synthesis removes the need for expensive human annotation in audio-video alignment, though the observed performance degradation after two DPO iterations hints at a limited optimization horizon that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the co-attention design and preference framework generalize to longer-form video, more diverse sound categories beyond VGGSound's 310 classes, and whether the reliance on frozen ImageBind/Synchformer reward models introduces a ceiling on achievable alignment quality.
