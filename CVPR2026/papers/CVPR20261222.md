## Problem

Discriminative world models collapse to conditional-mean predictions under uncertainty, failing to capture the diverse futures needed for downstream decision-making. Existing generative world models (e.g., Cosmos) address this but remain computationally expensive: they operate in pixel-reconstruction latent spaces, require multiple sequential forward passes per sample, and represent each frame as a dense spatial token grid that ignores the strong temporal redundancy between consecutive frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261222/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a short sequence of context video frames (4 frames at evaluation) encoded by a frozen DINOv3 ViT-B into patch token grids, plus K Gaussian noise queries. It outputs K diverse future delta tokens — a 1D temporal sequence where each token encodes only the change from the previous frame — which are decoded back into full spatial VFM feature maps for downstream segmentation or depth heads. At 512×512 resolution, this reduces each frame from 1,024 spatial tokens to a single token, a 1,024× compression.

## Method

DeltaTok is a ViT-based autoencoder that takes two consecutive VFM feature maps (x_{t-1}, x_t) and compresses their difference into a single continuous delta token z_t; the decoder reconstructs x_t by transforming x_{t-1} using z_t, trained with MSE reconstruction loss. DeltaWorld replaces the spatial feature map predictor with a ViT-B cross-attention predictor operating on the 1D sequence of delta tokens, conditioned on K noise queries. Training uses a Best-of-Many objective: all K hypotheses are generated in one forward pass, but only the one closest to ground truth receives gradient, enabling diverse sampling without iterative denoising. The key insight is that inter-frame change is low-dimensional, so a single token suffices, collapsing video from a 3D spatio-temporal grid to a 1D temporal sequence and reducing the predictor to 0.5% of inference FLOPs when generating 20 samples.

## Results

On the dense forecasting benchmark, DeltaWorld achieves best-of-20 mIoU of 55.4 (short) and 50.1 (mid) on VSPW, 65.8 and 55.4 on Cityscapes, and RMSE of 3.00 and 3.88 on KITTI, while using only 3.1×10⁴ GFLOPs to generate all 20 samples. This represents over 35× fewer parameters and ~2,000× fewer FLOPs than the Cosmos-4B/12B generative baselines (6.0–6.4×10⁷ GFLOPs). DeltaWorld's mean predictions (e.g., 51.3 mIoU mid-horizon on Cityscapes) remain competitive with the discriminative DINO-world baseline (49.8), confirming that the sampled futures are realistic rather than noisy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Cosmos-4B and Cosmos-12B generative world models, DeltaWorld's best-of-20 predictions surpass both across all six metrics (e.g., 55.4 vs. 51.1/51.7 mIoU on VSPW short, 65.8 vs. 55.1/55.3 on Cityscapes short, 3.00 vs. 3.82/3.72 RMSE on KITTI short), while using roughly 2,000× fewer FLOPs. DeltaWorld's mean scores also exceed Cosmos on most metrics, and the best-to-mean gap is consistently larger for DeltaWorld, indicating more meaningful sample diversity. Against the discriminative DINO-world baseline, DeltaWorld's best predictions substantially outperform the single deterministic output (e.g., +3.8 mIoU on Cityscapes short, +2.2 on VSPW mid), while its mean scores are competitive: slightly better on Cityscapes mid (51.3 vs. 49.8) but modestly worse on VSPW mid (46.7 vs. 47.9) and KITTI mid (4.17 vs. 4.07). Overall, DeltaWorld demonstrates that a compact generative model operating on delta tokens in VFM feature space can match or exceed both discriminative and large generative baselines at a fraction of the compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discriminative DINO-world architecture [3], which predicts future frames in the feature space of a frozen vision foundation model (DINOv3), by making it generative through a Best-of-Many (BoM) training objective [5] that maps different noise queries to different future hypotheses in a single forward pass. The key architectural departure is DeltaTok, a tokenizer that compresses the feature difference between consecutive frames into a single continuous token, drawing on the principle of interframe (delta) compression from video coding [67] but operating non-spatially in VFM feature space rather than per-pixel motion vectors. This collapses video from a 3D spatio-temporal grid to a 1D temporal sequence, fundamentally different from prior generative world models (Cosmos, GAIA-1, Genie) that operate in pixel-reconstruction latent spaces and require iterative denoising or autoregressive spatial prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2,000× FLOP reduction and 35× parameter reduction relative to Cosmos while matching or exceeding its dense-forecasting accuracy makes generative world modeling practically deployable for downstream tasks like planning and decision-making, where many diverse futures must be sampled cheaply. The conceptual insight that a single delta token per frame suffices in VFM feature space challenges the assumption that world models require dense spatial representations, and the 'no change preserves the previous frame' prior offers a natural inductive bias that stabilizes mean predictions. Open questions include whether this approach scales to longer rollouts, larger predictor backbones, and tasks requiring pixel-level fidelity (e.g., full video generation), and whether the single-token bottleneck limits expressiveness for highly dynamic scenes.
