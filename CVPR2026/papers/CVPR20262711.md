## Problem

Current VPR methods face a fundamental efficiency bottleneck: they either scale up backbone capacity or rely on computationally intensive reranking stages to handle extreme environmental variations, making them impractical for real-time deployment. One-stage methods that avoid reranking still struggle to adapt feature extraction to sample-specific discriminative regions and produce high-dimensional descriptors that inflate memory footprints, particularly when using compact backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262711/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB query image (resized to 322×322 at inference, trained at 266×266) and produces a compact 3456-dimensional global descriptor for direct nearest-neighbor retrieval against a prebuilt database. Unlike two-stage methods, no local feature storage or geometric verification (e.g., RANSAC) is required at query time, and the descriptor dimension is roughly 28% of BoQ's and 0.7% of FoL's, drastically reducing database memory for large-scale deployment.

## Method

EfficientVPR builds on a frozen DINOv2-small backbone and introduces two core mechanisms. First, SceneVPT inserts N_p learnable prompts at every encoder layer and dynamically re-weights them per sample using the CLS token's attention scores to those prompts (passed through a sigmoid with a learnable threshold), so that sample-relevant prompts from the previous layer are preserved while irrelevant ones are replaced by new layer-specific prompts—requiring no extra modules beyond the backbone's existing attention. Second, an instance-dependent key local feature enhancement module fuses multi-scale global features (MsIA), orientation-aware local features extracted via asymmetric 1×3 and 3×1 convolutions with orthogonal fusion (OLE), and a semantic-guided cross-attention selector (SGFS) that uses the backbone-processed prompts as adaptive queries to emphasize task-critical local regions. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on GSV-Cities with MS Loss, adding only 7.7M trainable parameters (8% of an adapter-based approach) while achieving SOTA accuracy among DINOv2-small-scale methods across seven benchmarks.

## Results

EfficientVPR (DINOv2-S, 3456-D descriptor) achieves an average R@1 of 90.3% across seven benchmarks, establishing a new SOTA for DINOv2-small models. It outperforms the one-stage SOTA BoQ (DINOv2-S, 16k-D) by 1.0% average R@1 while using only 28% of the feature dimensions, and surpasses the two-stage SOTA FoL (DINOv2-S, 469k-D) by 1.4% average R@1 with just 0.6% of its descriptor size. Inference latency is 3.1 ms/query (73× faster than FoL-DINOv2-L at 227.1 ms), with peak GPU memory of only 0.2 GB versus 33 GB for FoL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-scale one-stage competitors, EfficientVPR (DINOv2-S) outperforms BoQ (DINOv2-S) by 1.0% average R@1 (90.3% vs 89.3%) and SALAD (DINOv2-S) by 2.4%, while using 28% and 41% of their respective feature dimensions. On the most challenging domain-shift dataset AmsterTime, the margin over BoQ widens to 5.0% (60.2% vs 55.2%). Against the two-stage SOTA FoL (DINOv2-S, 469k-D), EfficientVPR achieves 1.4% higher average R@1 with only 0.6% of the descriptor size and 34× lower latency (3.1 ms vs 106.0 ms). Compared to the larger DINOv2-L-based SelaVPR, EfficientVPR trails by only 2.5% average R@1 while being 73× faster and 10.4× smaller in model size. Against DINOv2-B-based methods (CricaVPR-16, BoQ-B, SALAD-B), EfficientVPR matches or exceeds their accuracy with less than 34% of the model parameters and 26% of the training parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Visual Prompt Tuning (VPT) from Jia et al. by replacing static, scene-agnostic prompts with a dynamic selection mechanism driven by CLS-token attention weights, enabling per-sample adaptation without additional modules. It departs from the adapter-based PEFT approaches used in CricaVPR and SelaVPR, arguing that direct modification of entity feature tokens disrupts pretrained knowledge, and instead preserves the backbone's feature space through prompt-only intervention. The instance-dependent local feature enhancement module (SGFS) draws conceptual inspiration from BoQ's learnable-query aggregation but substitutes backbone-processed prompts as adaptive queries, bridging the gap between one-stage global descriptors and the discriminative local cues typically exploited by two-stage reranking methods like FoL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a new SOTA for DINOv2-S models and delivering a 73× speedup with 60% lower-dimensional features relative to the leading two-stage method, this work provides a practical pathway for deploying VPR in resource-constrained settings (autonomous driving, robotics) where memory and latency are critical. The ablation revealing that full fine-tuning causes catastrophic forgetting on domain-shift datasets (AmsterTime) while SceneVPT preserves pretrained generalizability offers useful guidance for adapting compact foundation models. Open questions include whether the prompt-selection mechanism generalizes to backbones beyond DINOv2, whether the local enhancement module can fully replace geometric reranking in all extreme-viewpoint scenarios, and how the approach scales to even larger databases where the 3456-D descriptor's compactness becomes a decisive advantage.
