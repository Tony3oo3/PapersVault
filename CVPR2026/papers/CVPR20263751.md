## Problem

Large VLA models with diffusion-transformer action heads are too memory- and compute-intensive for deployment on robotic platforms, yet existing efficiency methods target the vision encoder or redesign architectures while leaving the DiT action head in full precision. No prior post-training quantization approach has successfully quantized a DiT action head within a VLA pipeline, because quantization-induced scale drift distorts attention-logit temperature and residual-stream energy in ways that unimodal PTQ methods do not address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263751/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The underlying VLA policy consumes a short history of RGB frames (embedded via SigLIP2 or DINOv2), a natural-language instruction, and robot proprioception, and produces a continuous action sequence through iterative flow-matching denoising in the DiT head. The QuantVLA framework itself requires only a small unlabeled calibration buffer (no labels, no gradients) to estimate per-head and per-layer scalar corrections, which are then folded into existing dequantization scales so that the inference-time operator schedule and integer GEMMs remain unchanged.

## Method

QuantVLA is a training-free PTQ framework built on DuQuant-style reparameterization (per-channel smoothing, block-orthogonal rotations, zigzag permutation) applied selectively: all linear layers in the LLM and all MLP blocks in the DiT are integerized (W4A8), while the Q, K, V, O attention projections remain in floating point to avoid amplifying the two identified drifts. Two lightweight calibrations correct the residual cross-module scale mismatch: Attention Temperature Matching (ATM) estimates a per-head scalar α from the ratio of teacher-to-student logits standard deviation and divides the quantized logits by α to restore the softmax temperature; Output Head Balancing (OHB) estimates a per-layer scalar β from the ratio of teacher-to-student RMS of the post-projection output and rescales the residual injection to restore the layer-normalization operating point. Both scalars are clipped to a safe range, passed through a neutrality band to suppress calibration noise, and folded into dequantization scales once at calibration time, introducing zero additional operators or GEMM computation at inference.

## Results

On LIBERO, QuantVLA (W4A8) achieves 97.6% average success rate on π0.5, exceeding the FP16 baseline of 97.1%, while reducing memory from 4.27 GB to 1.28 GB (70% savings). On GR00T N1.5, QuantVLA reaches 88.0% average success rate, surpassing the FP16 baseline of 86.5%, with memory reduced from 2.02 GB to 0.91 GB (55% savings). Under more aggressive W4A4 quantization, QuantVLA still maintains 95.3% average on π0.5, and across denoising steps (8 vs. 16) on GR00T N1.5 it consistently matches or exceeds the full-precision baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DuQuant (W4A8, applied to both LLM and DiT), a rotation-based PTQ method designed for unimodal LLMs. QuantVLA decisively outperforms DuQuant on both models: on π0.5, QuantVLA achieves 97.6% average success versus DuQuant's 76.3% (a 21.3-point gap), and on GR00T N1.5 it reaches 88.0% versus DuQuant's 70.0% (an 18-point gap). The degradation is most severe for DuQuant on long-horizon tasks (50.0% on π0.5 Long, 76.0% on GR00T N1.5 Long), confirming that unimodal PTQ assumptions break down under the tight language-to-diffusion coupling in VLA pipelines. Notably, QuantVLA not only recovers the FP16 baseline but exceeds it on both models (97.6% vs. 97.1% on π0.5; 88.0% vs. 86.5% on GR00T N1.5), while simultaneously reducing memory by 70% and 55% respectively. The paper also reports additional comparisons with SmoothQuant under different precision settings in the appendix, but DuQuant is the primary in-text baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

QuantVLA extends rotation-based PTQ (specifically DuQuant's dual-transformation reparameterization) from unimodal LLMs to the tightly coupled VLA setting, where a language backbone conditions a diffusion-transformer action head. It departs from existing VLA efficiency frameworks (TinyVLA, EfficientVLA, VLA-Cache, MoLe-VLA) that redesign architectures or add caching/routing around an unchanged policy, instead targeting numerical precision while preserving the original operator schedule. The key conceptual departure from prior PTQ work (SmoothQuant, DuQuant, SVDQuant) is the identification of cross-modal scale drift—where quantization of the language backbone shifts the effective attention-logits temperature and residual-stream energy in the DiT head—requiring the two lightweight calibration mechanisms (ATM and OHB) that have no direct analogue in unimodal PTQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a DiT action head can be quantized without degrading (and in some cases slightly improving) task success, opening a practical path to ~70% memory reduction for VLA deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. The selective layout (integerize LLM + DiT MLP, keep Q/K/V/O in FP) plus scalar calibrations folded into dequantization scales is architecturally non-invasive and could be adopted by other VLA systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even lower bit widths (W4A4 shows degradation), whether the small FP16-exceeding gains persist on real robots with sensor noise, and whether the calibration buffer requirements scale to longer-horizon or multi-task settings beyond LIBERO.
