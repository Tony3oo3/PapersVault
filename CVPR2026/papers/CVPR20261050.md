## Problem

Existing generative climate emulators rely on weather-scale autoregression rolled out over decades, which is computationally prohibitive for long horizons, accumulates drift in long-term statistics, and has not demonstrated stable rollouts under nonstationary forcings like greenhouse gas emissions. Additionally, high-quality video generative models depend on pretrained VAEs unavailable for climate data or on autoregressive temporal modeling that scales poorly, and no sufficiently large multi-model climate dataset existed to train robust emulators.

## Input / Output

The model takes five forcing inputs on a 96×144 grid—cumulative CO₂ and CH₄ emissions, spatial maps of SO₂ and black carbon emissions, and stratospheric aerosol optical depth (AOD)—and produces probabilistic samples of surface air temperature and precipitation anomalies on a 192×288 latitude-longitude grid. A key design feature is that the model can directly output clean samples at any of three timescales (decadal, yearly, monthly) without first generating finer temporal resolution, and all generation occurs in pixel space without a VAE. Each sample is a full spatiotemporal field (e.g., 10 yearly frames or 12 monthly frames) generated in parallel rather than autoregressively.

## Method

SPF partitions the flow matching trajectory into a three-stage spatiotemporal pyramid (decadal → yearly → monthly) where each stage denoises at progressively finer spatial resolution (×2 per stage) and temporal resolution (×10 then ×12), using a single MM-DiT backbone with spatial scale and timescale embeddings to distinguish stages. The core training objective is conditional flow matching with linear interpolation paths, augmented with a multi-timescale extension that randomly samples whether each stage performs spatiotemporal or spatial-only refinement, enabling direct clean output at any timescale. A generalized rescaling-renoising correction (Eq. 5) ensures distributional continuity at stage transitions for arbitrary heterogeneous resampling factors across space and time. A temporal funneling mechanism slices the denoised latent to a single frame before upsampling, yielding 10× and 12× memory/FLOP reductions per stage. The model is trained on ClimateSuite (33,739 simulation-years from 10 ESMs including 39 SAI experiments) and fine-tuned on ClimateBench, using sequence packing to handle variable-length token sequences from different resolutions within a single batch.

## Results

On ClimateBench (held-out SSP2-4.5), SPF achieves the best CRPS at the yearly timescale for both model sizes: 0.238 (100M) and 0.222 (200M), outperforming PixelFlow (0.247/0.224) and PyramidalFlow (0.368/0.327). At the monthly timescale, the 100M SPF model achieves the best CRPS (0.462), RMSE (1.100), and bias (−0.043) among all models. Sampling a 10-year trajectory takes only 3 s (yearly) and 6 s (monthly) with the 100M model, representing a 28× speedup over autoregressive PyramidalFlow (84 s) at the monthly scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PyramidalFlow [24], SPF wins decisively on both accuracy and speed: at the monthly timescale, SPF (100M) achieves CRPS 0.462 vs. 0.501 and runs in 6 s vs. 84 s (a 14× speedup); at 200M, the gap widens to 11 s vs. 105 s (>9× faster) with CRPS 0.453 vs. 0.473. Against PixelFlow [8], SPF achieves lower CRPS at both yearly (0.238 vs. 0.247 at 100M; 0.222 vs. 0.224 at 200M) and is faster (3 s vs. 6 s at 100M), though PixelFlow retains a slight RMSE edge at yearly (0.549 vs. 0.565 at 100M). Against ClimaX [38], a pre-trained foundation model, SPF substantially outperforms on CRPS (0.238 vs. 0.347 at 100M) and bias (0.004 vs. −0.073) while being trained only on the smaller ClimateBench set. Against UNet [45], SPF dominates on all metrics at both parameter scales (e.g., CRPS 0.238 vs. 0.396 at 100M yearly). The 200M Multi-Monthly Flow variant (0.231 CRPS yearly, 0.443 monthly) slightly outperforms full SPF on monthly CRPS, suggesting the multi-timescale training objective trades a small amount of monthly sharpness for the ability to sample at any timescale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPF extends the pyramid flow matching lineage from Jin et al. (PyramidalFlow, 2024) and Chen et al. (PixelFlow, 2025) by generalizing spatial-only cascades with homogeneous resampling to spatiotemporal cascades with heterogeneous per-stage resampling factors (e.g., ×10 decadal→yearly, ×12 yearly→monthly). It departs from PyramidalFlow's autoregressive-over-time design in favor of parallel generation with temporal funneling, and from PixelFlow by adding a temporal dimension and multi-timescale direct sampling. The conditioning on physical forcings (CO₂, CH₄, SO₂, black carbon, stratospheric AOD) follows the regression-based emulator paradigm of Watson-Parris et al. (ClimateBench) but embeds it within a generative flow matching framework, bridging the gap between deterministic surrogate models and probabilistic generative emulators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPF addresses a core bottleneck in climate science: the computational cost of long-horizon, multi-ensemble ESM rollouts that limits scenario exploration for policy. The 28× speedup over autoregressive baselines while matching or exceeding accuracy, combined with the ability to directly sample at coarser timescales without generating finer ones, makes probabilistic climate emulation practical for integrated assessment and sectoral impact studies. ClimateSuite (33,000+ sim-years, 10 ESMs, first to include SAI interventions) substantially expands the data available for training multi-model superemulators. Key open questions remain: SPF does not enforce physical conservation laws, generalization to unseen parameterizations or extreme forcings is untested, and inter-model transfer has not been evaluated.
