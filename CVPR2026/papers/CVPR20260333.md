## Problem

Existing inner-LLM token compression methods for large vision-language models rely on attention weights to identify important visual tokens, which introduces two critical flaws: an information-agnostic positional bias that systematically retains later-positioned tokens regardless of semantic content, and incompatibility with efficient operators like FlashAttention that causes peak memory to exceed even uncompressed models. The paper asks whether token importance can instead be assessed through intrinsic behavioral patterns within the model, eliminating both issues simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method operates on visual tokens (M tokens of dimension D′) produced by a ViT encoder and projector, paired with text prompt tokens, and produces autoregressive text responses. It is training-free and plug-and-play: no architectural changes or retraining are required. The token count is progressively reduced from M to a predefined schedule (e.g., 576 → 384 → 256 → 192 for LLaVA-1.5-7B), with the final retained tokens feeding into the remaining LLM layers for generation. It supports both fixed-resolution images and variable-resolution/long-video inputs (e.g., Qwen2-VL's native resolution mode).

## Method

V²Drop identifies 'lazy tokens'—visual tokens whose representations change minimally between consecutive LLM transformer layers—as safe to drop, based on the insight that tokens actively participating in reasoning exhibit significant inter-layer variation while irrelevant tokens remain static. At three strategically selected layers (shallow, middle, deep), the method computes L2 distance between each token's representation at layer l and l−1, ranks tokens by variation score in descending order, and retains only the top-K tokens. This progressive dropping schedule (M → K_a → K_b → K_c) avoids the information loss of one-time pruning while the content-dependent variation metric naturally eliminates positional bias. A first-order Taylor expansion theorem formally links token variation magnitude to output influence, and the approach requires no attention weight computation, preserving full compatibility with FlashAttention and incurring negligible overhead (~0.002% of a full forward pass).

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B image understanding, V²Drop retains 97.6% of full-model average performance with only 192 tokens (66.7% reduction) and 94.0% with 128 tokens (77.8% reduction), outperforming all baselines at every compression level. On LLaVA-OV-7B video understanding, V²Drop achieves 98.6% of original performance at 25% token retention and 93.9% at 15% retention. In terms of efficiency, V²Drop reduces LLM generation latency by 31.5% (image) and 74.2% (video) while maintaining peak GPU memory comparable to random dropping, unlike attention-based methods that increase memory by up to 54.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V²Drop outperforms all seven baselines across every retention ratio on LLaVA-1.5-7B: at 192 tokens it leads PDrop (96.0%) and SparseVLM (95.9%) by 1.6–1.7% in average performance, and at 128 tokens it surpasses PDrop (93.6%) by 0.4% while HiRED (91.6%) and SparseVLM (93.2%) trail further behind. On Qwen2-VL-7B, V²Drop beats DART by 0.5% (96.0% vs 95.5%) at 66.7% reduction and by 0.5% (92.3% vs 91.8%) at 77.8% reduction, while FastV lags by 3.1–2.8%. For video understanding on LLaVA-OV-7B, V²Drop achieves 98.6% at 25% retention, edging out SparseVLM (98.4%) and substantially outperforming DyCoke (87.1%) and PDrop (96.0%); at aggressive 15% compression, V²Drop (93.9%) leads SparseVLM (92.1%) by 1.8%. On Qwen2-VL video (20% retention), V²Drop reaches 93.3% versus DART's 90.5% and FastV's 81.3%. Critically, V²Drop matches random dropping in peak GPU memory (15,046 MB vs 15,045 MB on LLaVA-1.5) while attention-based methods like SparseVLM inflate memory by 23.5% (image) or 54.8% (video), confirming its compatibility with FlashAttention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant inner-LLM token compression paradigm (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop) that relies on attention weights as the importance signal, identifying two fundamental flaws: information-agnostic positional bias and incompatibility with FlashAttention. Rather than building on any single prior method, it repurposes the general progressive-dropping framework but replaces the selection criterion with a simple inter-layer L2-norm variation metric, positioning itself as the first study to frame token compression from a dynamic token-variation perspective. The theoretical grounding via first-order Taylor expansion connects the variation signal to output sensitivity, providing a principled justification absent in prior heuristic attention-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in FlashAttention compatibility: attention-guided methods like SparseVLM actually increase peak GPU memory by up to 54.8% over uncompressed models, making them counterproductive in deployment, whereas V2Drop achieves comparable or better performance with negligible overhead. The positional bias finding explains why attention-guided methods disproportionately retain irrelevant late-position tokens (worsening hallucinations) and provides a diagnostic for practitioners choosing compression strategies. Open questions include whether variation-based signals generalize to audio or other modalities, whether the first-order approximation remains valid in very deep or MoE architectures, and whether the fixed three-layer dropping schedule can be made adaptive per input.
