## Problem

Existing 3D head avatar methods either require studio-level multi-view capture with per-identity optimization, are restricted to fixed input configurations (a single image or exactly four calibrated views), or lack explicit cross-view correspondence, making multi-view information aggregation unreliable. There is no feed-forward approach that accepts an arbitrary number of pose-free images without camera or expression annotations while maintaining high-fidelity, real-time animatable output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263131/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary number (1 to 16) of pose-free RGB portrait images at 512×512 resolution with no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or expression parameters. It produces a canonical 3D Gaussian head avatar of approximately 130K Gaussians initialized on the FLAME 2023 (jaw) template mesh, which is animatable via standard linear blend skinning and renders at 220 FPS. The internal representation uses 9216 learnable UV tokens (reshaped to a 96×96 grid) decoded into a 384×384×256 UV-space feature map.

## Method

A facial correspondence estimator (frozen DINOv3 encoder + trainable DPT head, inspired by Pixel3DMM) predicts pixel-wise UV coordinates for each input image, enabling color reprojection from screen space into a shared, pose- and expression-invariant UV space where observations are aggregated with a confidence map. A 12-block MM-Transformer (16 heads, D=1024) processes learnable UV tokens through a novel dual-attention mechanism: standard screen-space attention captures local details, while a UV-space attention branch provides structured global context from the reprojected features. A DPT-based UV decoder outputs per-Gaussian attributes (color, fuse weight, opacity, position offset, scaling, rotation), and a self-adaptive fusion strategy blends the predicted global color with the reprojected local color via learned per-Gaussian weights. The model is trained end-to-end on VFHQ, HDTF, NeRSemble-v2, and a newly curated synthetic multi-view dataset (7,500+ identities × 9 views × 13,000+ frames) generated by combining SphereHead 3D synthesis with LivePortrait 2D animation, using L1 + LPIPS + SSIM photometric losses plus a geometry offset regularizer.

## Results

In the monocular setting (VFHQ + NeRSemble-v2), UIKA achieves PSNR 21.69, SSIM 0.867, and LPIPS 0.105 for self-reenactment, outperforming Portrait4D-v2 (21.03/0.859/0.134), GAGAvatar (20.34/0.850/0.160), and LAM (18.29/0.810/0.206). In the multi-view setting (NeRSemble-v2), UIKA achieves PSNR 22.50 and LPIPS 0.120, substantially surpassing DiffusionRig (16.97/0.395), GPAvatar (17.11/0.313), and InvertAvatar (16.35/0.394). The method also achieves real-time rendering at 220 FPS via standard 3D Gaussian splatting without an additional neural renderer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the monocular setting, UIKA outperforms all three baselines on every self-reenactment metric: it gains +0.66 dB PSNR over Portrait4D-v2 (21.69 vs 21.03), +1.35 dB over GAGAvatar (21.69 vs 20.34), and +3.40 dB over LAM (21.69 vs 18.29), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.105 vs 0.134/0.160/0.206) and identity similarity (CSIM 0.738 vs 0.688/0.693/0.602). For cross-reenactment, UIKA leads on AED (0.114) and APD (0.123) but trails GAGAvatar slightly on CSIM (0.649 vs 0.678). In the multi-view setting, the margins are dramatically larger: UIKA achieves PSNR 22.50 versus 16.97 (DiffusionRig), 17.11 (GPAvatar), and 16.35 (InvertAvatar) — a gap of over 5 dB — and LPIPS 0.120 versus 0.395/0.313/0.394. The paper attributes the multi-view advantage to explicit UV-space correspondence, noting that GPAvatar and InvertAvatar actually degrade in quality as more input views are added due to the lack of structured cross-view alignment. UIKA also supports real-time animation at 220 FPS, whereas DiffusionRig requires ~30 minutes of iterative denoising per identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

UIKA sits at the intersection of feed-forward 3D Gaussian head avatar reconstruction (LAM, GAGAvatar, GPAvatar, Avat3r) and UV-space facial modeling (FlashAvatar, GaussianAvatars), but departs from prior work by introducing explicit pixel-wise facial correspondence (inspired by Pixel3DMM) to establish a shared canonical UV space across arbitrary pose-free inputs. Where previous feed-forward methods either lack cross-view correspondence (GPAvatar, PF-LHM) or are restricted to fixed input configurations (Avat3r's four calibrated views), UIKA generalizes to an arbitrary number of uncalibrated images via a UV attention branch in the Transformer and a self-adaptive fusion strategy that blends predicted global appearance with reprojected local pixel colors. The synthetic multi-view dataset curation pipeline (SphereHead + LivePortrait) also extends prior data strategies that relied on identity-scarce studio captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for camera calibration and expression annotations while supporting arbitrary input counts, UIKA lowers the practical barrier to high-fidelity head avatar creation from casual smartphone photos, with real-time 220 FPS rendering via standard LBS rather than a neural renderer. The 7,500+ identity synthetic dataset addresses a persistent bottleneck in head avatar training (identity diversity and expression range), and the consistent quality improvement with more input views—while baselines like GPAvatar and InvertAvatar degrade—suggests that explicit correspondence is a key missing ingredient in multi-view aggregation. Open questions include generalization to full-body avatars, handling of dynamic hair and accessories, and whether the UV-guided formulation extends to non-rigid body parts beyond the FLAME head model.
