## Problem

Adapting generative MLLMs into single-embedding discriminative retrievers for Composed Image Retrieval introduces a paradigm conflict that degrades the model's native fine-grained compositional reasoning—a phenomenon the authors term Capability Degradation. Prior MLLM-based CIR methods perform static contrastive fine-tuning without diagnosing or repairing this loss, leaving the retriever unable to distinguish subtle visual-semantic differences that the foundation model could handle in its native VQA mode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263436/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language modification text as a composed query, encodes them jointly into a single embedding via a LoRA-adapted MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, rank 16), and retrieves the target image by cosine similarity against a gallery of image embeddings. The framework is model-agnostic and has been validated on both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B backbones. Evaluation uses Recall@K on CIRR (general-domain, with an additional Recall_subset@K over 6-candidate subsets) and FashionIQ (fashion-domain, averaged over Dress/Shirt/Top&Tee categories).

## Method

ReCALL follows a diagnose-generate-refine pipeline. In the diagnose stage, the baseline retriever performs self-guided informative instance mining: it retrieves on the training set, isolates failure cases, and extracts the top-K hard negatives erroneously ranked above the ground truth as cognitive blind spots. In the generate stage, the foundation MLLM is prompted with a two-step Chain-of-Thought procedure (intent decomposition & verification, then minimal edit synthesis) to produce corrected modification texts for each hard negative, forming new triplets; a VQA-based consistency check filters unreliable outputs. In the refine stage, a Grouped Contrastive Refinement strategy batches each original triplet with its corrective counterpart in the same micro-group, optimizing a hybrid objective of standard InfoNCE (preserving global structure) plus an in-group triplet margin loss (enforcing explicit separation between the target and its specific hard negative), thereby realigning the discriminative embedding space with the MLLM's intrinsic compositional reasoning.

## Results

On CIRR, ReCALL achieves a state-of-the-art R@1 of 55.52% and R_subset@1 of 81.49%, surpassing the previous best (TME at 53.42% R@1) by over 2 points and its own baseline R_base (51.23%) by 8.38% relative. On FashionIQ, ReCALL attains the highest average R@10 of 57.04% and R@50 of 76.42%, improving over R_base by 7.16% relative on R@10 (with up to 10.71% gain in the Dress category). The Avg. metric on CIRR (computed as (R@5 + R_subset@1)/2) reaches 82.81%, outperforming all prior methods including TME (82.01%) and CIR-LVLM (81.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReCALL outperforms all 16 compared baselines on both CIRR and FashionIQ. On CIRR, it leads TME (53.42% R@1) by 2.10 points, CIR-LVLM (53.64%) by 1.88 points, and CCIN (53.41%) by 2.11 points on R@1, while also securing the best R_subset@1 (81.49% vs. TME's 81.04%). On FashionIQ, ReCALL's average R@10 of 57.04% exceeds CIR-LVLM (56.21%), TME (55.15%), and SPRC (54.72%), with the largest margin over older dual-tower methods like CoVR-2 (49.96%) and FAME-ViL (46.84%). The most notable gain is over its own baseline R_base: +8.38% relative on CIRR R@1 and +7.16% on FashionIQ Avg R@10, confirming that the diagnose-generate-refine pipeline specifically recovers the fine-grained reasoning lost during initial retrieval fine-tuning. ReCALL also generalizes across backbones, delivering consistent gains when applied to the stronger Qwen3-VL-8B (55.93%→57.09% R@1 on CIRR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: MLLM-based composed image retrieval (CIR-LVLM, VLM2Vec, MM-Embed) and self-improvement for language models (STaR, Reflexion, Self-Refine). It departs from the prevailing single-stage, static fine-tuning paradigm used by recent MLLM-CIR methods by introducing an iterative diagnose-generate-refine loop that explicitly targets the model's own failure cases. Conceptually, it repurposes the self-improvement idea of using a model's native reasoning to produce corrective supervision, but transplants it into the discriminative retrieval setting where the 'reasoning' must be compressed into a single embedding vector. The paper does not build on a specific prior architecture but rather identifies a gap (paradigm conflict between generative and discriminative objectives) that prior MLLM-CIR work has not addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of 'Capability Degradation' as a systematic consequence of compressing a generative MLLM into a single-embedding retriever reframes how the community should think about MLLM-to-retrieval adaptation, suggesting that standard contrastive fine-tuning may silently destroy fine-grained compositional reasoning. The model-agnostic diagnose-generate-refine pipeline offers a practical recipe that could be applied to any MLLM-based retriever, and the validation across two backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B) strengthens the claim. Open questions include whether the degradation is specific to LoRA-based adaptation or also manifests with full fine-tuning, whether the pipeline scales to larger galleries or more diverse domains beyond fashion and general objects, and whether the VQA-based quality control step remains reliable as the foundation model's capabilities evolve.
