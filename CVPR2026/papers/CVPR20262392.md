## Problem

Pretrained monocular depth estimation (MDE) models produce scaleless depth that existing methods correct via a global affine (scale-and-shift) transformation. However, when three or more objects or local regions are placed at incorrect relative depths by the MDE model, no single scale-and-shift can reconcile the misalignment, leaving a fundamental gap in metric depth recovery from cheap radar-guided pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262392/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (H×W×3) and a synchronized mmWave radar point cloud of roughly 100 3D points (N_C×3) as input. A frozen pretrained MDE model (e.g., UniDepth) first converts the image into a scaleless depth map, which is then transformed into a metric depth map (H×W) by evaluating a learned polynomial of degree N (default 8) at each pixel. The radar point cloud is extremely sparse relative to the image (~10² points vs. ~10⁶ pixels), making the polynomial degree a critical regularizer on degrees of freedom.

## Method

POLAR predicts N+1 polynomial coefficients from a fused radar–MDE representation: radar points are encoded with sinusoidal 3D positional embeddings and aggregated via learnable prototypes (soft clustering) to capture recurring spatial patterns, while the scaleless depth map is encoded by a depth encoder and fused with radar features through a learnable soft-correspondence (attention) module. The fused scene representation passes through a shallow CNN and global average pooling before an MLP outputs the coefficient vector, which is applied as d̂(x,y) = Σ ĉᵢ·z(x,y)ⁱ to produce the metric depth map. A novel first-derivative regularization term (‖1 − d d̂/dz‖₁) enforces approximate monotonicity of the polynomial, preventing harmful oscillations while still permitting the inflection points needed to correct cross-region misalignments. This formulation keeps the number of free parameters (N+1) close to the radar point cardinality, yielding a well-posed fitting problem that is both more expressive than affine and far more constrained than per-pixel regression.

## Results

On the standard 50 m evaluation, POLAR achieves MAE 1014.4 / RMSE 2475.7 on nuScenes, MAE 578.0 / RMSE 1108.6 on ZJU-4DRadarCam, and MAE 1293.7 / RMSE 2420.6 on View-of-Delft, setting new state-of-the-art on all three benchmarks. The paper reports average improvements of 24.9% in MAE and 33.2% in RMSE over the best prior method. POLAR also achieves the lowest inference latency (24.81 ms, ≈40 fps) and computational cost (89.70 GFLOPs) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POLAR outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is TacoDepth on nuScenes (MAE 1046.8 vs. 1014.4, a 3.1% gap), but POLAR's advantage widens dramatically on ZJU (MAE 578.0 vs. 930.2, a 37.9% reduction) and View-of-Delft (MAE 1293.7 vs. 1895.1 for RadarCam-Depth, a 31.7% reduction). Against the earlier baselines RadarNet, SparseBeatsDense, and GET-UP, POLAR reduces MAE by 41–64% depending on dataset. In addition to accuracy, POLAR is the fastest method in both training (33.16 min/epoch vs. 86–250 min for others) and inference (24.81 ms vs. 29.30 ms for TacoDepth and 315.64 ms for RadarCam-Depth), while also requiring the fewest FLOPs (89.70 vs. 139.87 for TacoDepth and 619.02 for RadarCam-Depth).

## Relation to Prior Work

POLAR builds on frozen pretrained MDE foundation models (e.g., UniDepth) as a geometric prior for scaleless depth, but departs fundamentally from prior radar-camera depth estimation methods (RadarNet, RadarCam-Depth, TacoDepth, GET-UP) that decode dense metric depth directly from fused multi-modal features. Instead of learning per-pixel depth corrections or explicit radar-pixel correspondences, POLAR formulates the problem as a low-dimensional scene-fitting task: predicting N+1 polynomial coefficients from a compact fused representation. This contrasts with the depth-completion paradigm inherited from lidar-camera work, where the goal is to densify sparse range measurements; here, radar sparsity is exploited as a well-posed constraint on a parametric transformation rather than a signal to be interpolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: mmWave radar is ubiquitous on modern vehicles and orders of magnitude cheaper than lidar, yet prior methods for radar-camera depth estimation have been computationally heavy. POLAR's 85.5% reduction in GFLOPs relative to RadarCam-Depth while achieving better accuracy makes real-time deployment on embedded automotive platforms far more feasible. The core insight—that MDE models preserve local structure but misalign regions, and that a few polynomial inflection points can correct this—offers a generalizable diagnostic for any foundation model whose outputs are locally accurate but globally inconsistent. Open questions include whether the polynomial degree can be learned adaptively per scene, whether the monotonicity constraint suffices for very high degrees, and whether this parametric-fitting paradigm extends to correcting other foundation model outputs (e.g., segmentation, pose).
