## Problem

Watermarking for dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) models remains unexplored. Existing 3DGS watermarking methods, when naively extended to 4D, either fine-tune all Gaussian parameters (causing temporal geometric inconsistency) or restrict updates to a limited subset (lacking embedding capacity). Moreover, the highly variable motion magnitudes across time in real-world 4D scenes make uniform watermark supervision suboptimal, as static intervals suffer overfitting while dynamic intervals weaken encoded signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 4DGS model (deformation field plus initial Gaussian set) and an L-bit binary message (L ∈ {32, 48, 64}) as input, and produces a watermarked 4DGS model with learnable offsets applied only to Gaussian positions and SH coefficients (opacity and covariance are left untouched to avoid geometric distortion). Decoding operates on rendered video clips of T=8 consecutive frames from a held-out camera, which are passed through a differentiable distortion module, encoded by a frozen X-CLIP video encoder, and decoded by a frozen 3-layer MLP to recover the binary message.

## Method

Mark4D introduces three complementary mechanisms for temporally consistent 4DGS watermarking. First, an X-CLIP guided decoder operates in the video-text latent space rather than pixel space, mapping a bit-to-token sequence through a frozen X-CLIP text encoder and a trainable MLP to reconstruct the binary message, making the watermark robust to pixel-level distortions. Second, trajectory-aligned offsets ε_i(t) are parameterized as a 3-term Fourier series and constrained via a cosine-distance alignment loss to follow each Gaussian's local motion tangent, ensuring smooth temporal evolution without disrupting geometry. Third, a motion-adaptive loss weighting scheme normalizes per-window motion magnitude to [0,1] and linearly interpolates the message-loss weight between λ_min=0.5 and λ_max=1, so static intervals receive weaker supervision (preserving fidelity) while dynamic intervals receive stronger supervision (ensuring robust embedding). Training is two-stage: the decoder is pre-trained on X-CLIP text embeddings, then the 4DGS model is fine-tuned for 4000 iterations with the frozen decoder, a reconstruction loss (L1 + LPIPS), the alignment loss, and the motion-adaptively weighted message loss.

## Results

On the combined D-NeRF and DyNeRF benchmarks, Mark4D achieves 96.34% bit accuracy at 32 bits with 42.32 dB PSNR and 0.0018 LPIPS, and maintains 92.71% bit accuracy at 64 bits with 41.27 dB PSNR. At 64 bits, it surpasses the strongest 3DGS baseline (3D-GSW) by 9.35 percentage points in bit accuracy and 10.40 dB in PSNR, and outperforms the best video-level baseline (VideoSeal) by 14.79%p in bit accuracy and 5.11 dB in PSNR. As message length increases from 32 to 64 bits, Mark4D degrades by only 3.63%p in bit accuracy, compared to 7.10%p for 3D-GSW and 10.23%p for VideoSeal, demonstrating superior capacity scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mark4D outperforms all nine baselines on every metric (bit accuracy, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across all three message lengths. Among 3DGS-adapted baselines, 3D-GSW is the strongest in bit accuracy (90.46% at 32 bits) but suffers the lowest PSNR (31.98 dB) due to aggressive parameter fine-tuning; GuardSplat achieves the best PSNR among baselines (38.69 dB) but trails Mark4D by 8.01%p in bit accuracy. Among 2D frame-level methods, StegaStamp and SSL reach ~85% bit accuracy at 32 bits but degrade sharply to ~76–78% at 64 bits, while CIN is the weakest overall (67.66% at 32 bits). Among video-level baselines, VideoSeal achieves 88.21% at 32 bits but drops to 77.98% at 64 bits (a 10.23%p decline), whereas Mark4D's video-level evaluation reaches 96.96% at 32 bits and 92.77% at 64 bits. RivaGAN, which only supports 32-bit messages, achieves 80.51% bit accuracy and 30.41 dB PSNR, well below Mark4D. The consistent margin (5–28%p in bit accuracy, 3–11 dB in PSNR) across all baselines indicates that the combination of latent-space decoding, trajectory-aligned offsets, and motion-adaptive weighting provides a substantial advantage over both static 3DGS watermarking and generic 2D/video watermarking approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS watermarking line (GuardSplat, GaussianMarker, 3D-GSW) to the 4D dynamic setting, where direct parameter fine-tuning causes temporal inconsistency. It departs from pixel-space decoding (used by HiDDeN, StegaStamp, and most 3DGS watermarkers) by moving to the X-CLIP video-text latent space, building on GuardSplat's CLIP-guided decoder but generalizing from image-text to video-text. The trajectory-aligned Fourier-series offset and motion-adaptive loss weighting are novel mechanisms absent from all prior 3DGS or video watermarking work, motivated by the observation that 4DGS motion magnitude varies non-uniformly over time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 4DGS becomes a standard representation for digital humans, dynamic content generation, and surgical reconstruction, protecting ownership of these assets is an unmet practical need that this paper addresses for the first time. The motion-adaptive weighting insight—that uniform supervision overfits static intervals and under-supervises dynamic ones—generalizes to any temporal watermarking or fine-tuning problem with heterogeneous motion. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with modest scene complexity, message lengths capped at 64 bits, and baselines that are 3D methods naively adapted rather than purpose-built 4D watermarkers, leaving open whether the large performance margins would narrow against a dedicated 4D baseline.
