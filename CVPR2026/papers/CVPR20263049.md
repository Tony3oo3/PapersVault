## Problem

Existing federated learning methods identify noisy labels via the small-loss heuristic, but under Non-IID data heterogeneity (especially label skew), tail-class samples inherently exhibit high losses regardless of label correctness, rendering loss-based noise detection unreliable. This paper asks whether the intrinsic representation geometry—decoupled from annotated labels—can provide a more robust signal for distinguishing clean from noisy samples in distributed settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) paired with potentially corrupted labels from K=10 clients partitioned under a Dirichlet(α=0.1) Non-IID split. Beyond the final class prediction, each client locally produces a clean/noisy sample partition (via GMM on geometric consistency scores) and a personalized C×C noise absorption matrix that is retained on-device rather than aggregated globally. The vMF mixture parameters and class-to-geometry mapping are also kept per-client across communication rounds.

## Method

FedRG operates in two stages. Stage I performs federated SimCLR pretraining to produce label-agnostic unit-norm representations on the hypersphere S^{d-1}, establishing a stable geometric substrate independent of noisy supervision. Stage II fits a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) mixture model (G semantic components plus a uniform background) to these spherical features, extracting label-free posterior responsibilities as geometric evidence; it then constructs a complementary label-dependent evidence vector by aggregating responsibilities per observed class with Dirichlet smoothing. Noisy samples are identified by measuring the inner-product divergence between a sample's geometric responsibilities and its label's class-to-geometry template, with a two-component GMM on the resulting scores partitioning local data into clean and noisy subsets. Robust optimization combines Symmetric Cross-Entropy over all samples with a forward-correction likelihood loss that routes classifier outputs through a client-specific noise absorption matrix (a learnable linear layer), explicitly modeling the label transition kernel without requiring true labels.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, FedRG achieves the best accuracy across all four noise settings: 59.99% (localized symmetric), 63.29% (globalized symmetric), 61.52% (localized pairflip), and 64.88% (globalized pairflip), with F-scores ranging from 55.31% to 60.98%. On SVHN, FedRG reaches 62.70% accuracy under symmetric noise and a striking 80.62% under pairflip noise (F-score 68.83%). On CIFAR-100, FedRG obtains 51.95% (symmetric) and 60.80% (pairflip) accuracy, though SymmetricCE edges it out on CIFAR-100 symmetric accuracy (56.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRG dominates all 14 baselines on CIFAR-10 across every noise setting, with the largest margins over loss-based methods: it outperforms FedCorr (35.83–40.39%) by 20–26 points in accuracy and FedClean (39.87–47.82%) by 12–25 points. Among noise-robust FL methods, SymmetricCE is the closest competitor on CIFAR-10 globalized symmetric (59.23% vs. 63.29%) and on CIFAR-100 symmetric (56.36% vs. 51.95%), where it actually beats FedRG by ~4.4 points in accuracy. On SVHN pairflip, FedRG achieves 80.62% accuracy, far exceeding the next-best FedSAM (75.25%) which suffers severe precision collapse (15.41%). FedLSR degrades catastrophically under pairflip noise (35.12% on CIFAR-10, 8.17% on CIFAR-100), while FedNoRo and FedCorr remain near random-level performance across most settings. Overall, FedRG is the only method that maintains balanced accuracy, precision, and F-score simultaneously across all three datasets and four noise configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedRG departs from the dominant small-loss heuristic used by prior FL noise-robust methods (FedCorr, FedNoRo, FedClean) by arguing that scalar loss values become unreliable under heterogeneous, long-tailed client distributions where tail-class samples naturally exhibit high loss regardless of label correctness. Instead, it repurposes SimCLR-style contrastive pretraining to produce label-agnostic spherical representations, then applies a von Mises-Fisher mixture model to extract geometric cluster evidence that is compared against label-conditioned geometry via a class-to-geometry mapping. The robust optimization stage combines Symmetric Cross-Entropy with a personalized noise absorption matrix (borrowed from the learning-from-crowds literature), making the method a synthesis of self-supervised representation learning, directional statistics, and forward-correction noise modeling within a federated protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'representation geometry priority' principle offers a principled alternative to loss-based filtering that is particularly relevant as FL deployments face increasingly heterogeneous and imperfectly labeled data. The personalized (non-aggregated) noise absorption matrix is a practical design choice that preserves client-specific noise characteristics, which is important for real-world federated systems where noise patterns vary across sites. However, the method is currently limited to image classification with a fixed number of vMF components, and the paper acknowledges that extension to graph and NLP domains remains open. A key open question is whether the geometric consistency signal remains discriminative at higher noise rates or with more extreme class imbalance than the ε=0.4, α=0.1 settings tested here.
