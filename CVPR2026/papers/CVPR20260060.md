## Problem

Current causal video diffusion models apply causal attention densely across all denoising steps, all layers, and the full temporal context, tightly entangling temporal reasoning with iterative visual denoising. This design incurs substantial redundant computation, since it is unclear whether multi-step refinement is truly necessary for the temporal reasoning component. The paper asks: can causal reasoning be separated from the denoising process to reduce latency without sacrificing quality?

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (T2V) or a reference image (I2V) plus previously generated clean frames as conditioning, and produces video frames autoregressively one at a time in latent space (e.g., 832×480 resolution for the fine-tuned Wan-based model, 128×128 for TECO-Minecraft, 64×64 for UCF-101). The causal encoder consumes the historical frame tokens (or a high-noise version of the current frame during fine-tuning) and emits a fixed-length context token sequence of the same spatial dimensions as one frame. The diffusion decoder then takes the noisy current-frame tokens concatenated with the context tokens along the sequence dimension and outputs a velocity prediction for iterative denoising.

## Method

SCD decouples temporal reasoning from iterative denoising into two modules: a causal transformer encoder that runs once per frame using causal attention over KV-cached history to produce a compact context latent c_i, and a lightweight frame-wise diffusion decoder that iteratively denoises the current frame conditioned on c_i using only bidirectional intra-frame attention (no cross-frame computation). This design is motivated by two empirical findings from probing Wan-2.1: middle-layer features exhibit >0.95 cosine similarity across denoising steps (redundant temporal computation), and deep layers allocate negligible attention mass to past frames (temporal independence). Training is joint end-to-end with a flow-matching velocity loss under teacher forcing, augmented by Gaussian corruption of the context tokens to reduce exposure bias. For fine-tuning from a pretrained bidirectional T2V model, the encoder input is reparameterized to accept high-noise frames (matching the teacher's distribution), and a leave-one-out layer analysis guides the split of the 30-layer teacher into a 25-layer encoder plus a 10-layer decoder (35 layers total), followed by self-forcing distillation for few-step generation.

## Results

On VBench (832×480, fine-tuned from Wan 2.1), SCD (1.6B) achieves 11.1 FPS throughput and 0.29 s per-frame latency, outpacing the closest AR baseline Self Forcing (8.9 FPS, 0.45 s) by ~1.3× while matching its VBench Total score (84.03 vs. 84.26). SCD is >10× faster than the non-causal Wan 2.1 (0.78 FPS, 103 s) with a comparable overall score. On training-from-scratch benchmarks, SCD-M on TECO-Minecraft attains the best LPIPS (0.179), SSIM (0.524), PSNR (19.3), and FVD (37.6) at 0.52 s/frame—over 4× faster than Causal DiT-M (2.4 s/frame)—and SCD-B on UCF-101 achieves the best FVD (174.7) at 1.1 s/frame, roughly 3.5× faster than Causal DiT-B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest AR baseline Self Forcing [30], SCD wins on throughput (11.1 vs. 8.9 FPS) and latency (0.29 vs. 0.45 s) but trails by 0.23 on VBench Total (84.03 vs. 84.26) and 0.70 on Semantic (79.60 vs. 80.30), a gap the authors attribute to the architectural mismatch of decoupling encoder and decoder. Against Pyramid Flow [32], SCD dominates on every axis: higher throughput (11.1 vs. 6.7 FPS), lower latency (0.29 vs. 2.5 s), and better Total (84.03 vs. 81.72) and Semantic (79.60 vs. 69.62) scores. Against the non-causal Wan 2.1 [65], SCD is >10× faster (11.1 vs. 0.78 FPS) with a near-identical Total score (84.03 vs. 84.26). Against LTX-Video [23] and NOVA [14], SCD leads on all three VBench sub-scores and throughput. On training-from-scratch benchmarks, SCD-M beats Causal DiT-M [65] on all four metrics (LPIPS 0.179 vs. 0.196, FVD 37.6 vs. 38.7) at 4.6× lower latency, and SCD-B beats Causal DiT-B on FVD (174.7 vs. 187.6) at 3.5× lower latency on UCF-101.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AR-diffusion hybrid paradigm (MarDini, VideoMAR, Fluid) by identifying a specific functional split within causal video diffusion models: temporal reasoning concentrates in early layers and is largely invariant across denoising steps, while deep layers perform predominantly intra-frame rendering. It departs from the standard causal diffusion design (as in Self Forcing and FAR) where causal attention is applied densely at every layer and every denoising step, instead proposing a clean encoder-decoder decomposition. The fine-tuning experiments build directly on Wan 2.1's pretrained 30-layer DiT and adopt Self Forcing's distillation framework, but restructure the architecture into a once-per-frame causal encoder plus a lightweight frame-wise diffusion decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The separability finding provides a principled justification for decoupling temporal reasoning from iterative denoising, yielding 2–4× latency reductions while matching or exceeding baseline quality across multiple benchmarks. This is particularly impactful for the growing demand for real-time, streaming, and interactive video generation, where per-frame latency is the binding constraint. Key limitations remain: step-wise invariance weakens near the end of the denoising trajectory (cosine similarity drops to ~0.8), and deep layers retain a small but non-zero cross-frame attention mass, which likely accounts for the slight quality gap at high resolution. Open questions include whether the separability pattern holds at much larger scales, how to integrate pretrained reasoners and denoisers operating in different latent spaces, and whether the architecture's scaling law mirrors that of language models.
