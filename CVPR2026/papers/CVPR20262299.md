## Problem

Existing medical vision-language models are constrained to single-phase, single-organ evaluations and produce only qualitative textual descriptions, fundamentally ignoring the longitudinal history of sequential prenatal examinations and the continuous biometric values (HC, AC, BPD, FL) that carry critical diagnostic weight. No prior framework jointly models multi-phase fetal growth trajectories and generates clinically coherent next-phase reports with numerically precise biometric predictions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a variable-length sequence of T historical phases, where each phase provides three canonical ultrasound planes (transventricular brain, transverse abdomen, longitudinal femur) resized to 448×448 pixels along with a 4-dimensional biometric vector, all at irregular gestational-age intervals (11–40 weeks). The output is dual: a 4-element continuous biometric prediction for the next phase (produced by a dedicated regression head with Smooth-L1 loss to avoid cascading errors from text-based number extraction) and a structured free-text ultrasound report generated autoregressively by the LLM.

## Method

F²-Assist integrates three tightly coupled modules within a unified MLLM pipeline. First, Cross-Phase Organ Alignment (CPOA) uses learnable semantic prototype queries to perform soft attention over ViT patch tokens, disentangling organ-specific features and fusing them with anatomical identity embeddings before concatenating in a fixed clinical plane order to enforce cross-phase semantic consistency. Second, History-Aware Temporal Encoding (HATE) applies a 4-layer, 8-head causal Transformer with gestational-age-aware positional embeddings over the aligned phase tokens, enabling the model to capture nonlinear, patient-specific growth dynamics under irregular examination intervals. Third, the Growth Parameter Adapter z-normalizes the biometric vector, projects it through an MLP into a differentiable soft token, and anchors it with a learnable numeric query so the LLM can attend to precise quantitative information alongside visual and textual inputs. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage I freezes the 7B Qwen decoder and trains all visual/temporal/adapter modules; Stage II fine-tunes LLM attention layers via LoRA (rank 16) end-to-end with a joint objective combining cross-entropy text loss and Smooth-L1 biometric regression loss.

## Results

On the GrowthFetus dataset, F²-Assist achieves the best performance across all metrics: BLEU-4 of 0.53, CIDEr of 3.66, and ROUGE-L of 0.69 for report generation, and MAE of 0.80 cm, Acc@±10% of 77.3%, and R² of 0.78 for next-phase biometric forecasting. These results substantially outperform the strongest baseline (Lingshu), which reaches only BLEU-4 0.44, CIDEr 3.34, MAE 1.13 cm, and R² 0.59. The R² improvement from 0.59 to 0.78 represents a 32% relative gain in explained variance for growth parameter prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F²-Assist outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest medical-domain baseline Lingshu (7B), it improves BLEU-4 by 0.09 (0.53 vs 0.44), CIDEr by 0.32 (3.66 vs 3.34), reduces MAE by 0.33 cm (0.80 vs 1.13), and raises R² from 0.59 to 0.78. Against the strongest general-domain baseline InternVL-3 (7B), gains are even larger: BLEU-4 +0.16, CIDEr +1.24, MAE −0.58 cm, and R² +0.19. The gap is most pronounced on numeric prediction, where all baselines cluster around R² 0.41–0.59 while F²-Assist reaches 0.78, confirming that the Growth Parameter Adapter and History-Aware Temporal Encoding provide capabilities absent in standard MLLM architectures. Even the best general-purpose model (Qwen2.5-VL, 8B) trails by 0.20 in R² and 0.44 cm in MAE, underscoring that domain-specific longitudinal modeling is essential for fetal growth forecasting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from three distinct but related lines of work: (1) medical MLLMs for report generation (LLaVA-Med, LLM-CXR, HuatuoGPT, Lingshu), which are confined to single-phase, single-organ evaluations and qualitative text; (2) longitudinal/spatio-temporal reporting methods (RECAP, HERGen, STREAM), which model temporal progression but target chest X-ray and do not encode continuous biometric values; and (3) ultrasound-specific models (TransVFS), which handle cine dynamics or deformation but stop at single-phase classification. F²-Assist carves new ground by unifying cross-organ feature alignment, irregular temporal encoding, and differentiable numeric tokenization within a single MLLM, a combination absent from all three threads. The Growth Parameter Adapter repurposes the soft-prompt/learnable-query paradigm from vision-language alignment to encode continuous biometric vectors, bridging a gap that prior MLLMs simply ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GrowthFetus is the first large-scale multi-phase, multi-organ fetal ultrasound dataset, and the unified forecasting-plus-reporting task it enables is clinically under-served; a working framework here could directly support personalized prenatal monitoring at scale. The Growth Parameter Adapter (encoding continuous values as differentiable tokens) is a general-purpose technique applicable to any MLLM task requiring precise numeric reasoning, not just fetal biometry. Key limitations include single-site data (one hospital, 2020–2023), no external validation, and notable accuracy degradation for AC/FL beyond 36 weeks due to data sparsity. Open questions include generalizability across ethnicities and gestational complications, handling of abnormal growth trajectories, and prospective clinical validation before deployment.
