## Problem

Existing uncertainty quantification methods for 3DGS (e.g., FisherRF, VIMC) do not model visibility, causing them to assign falsely low uncertainty to regions never observed by training views and thus failing to drive effective exploration. The prior visibility-based approach NVF addresses this but requires retraining a neural network at every planning step (minutes to hours) and models visibility as isotropic (position-only), ignoring the inherently view-dependent nature of visibility in 3DGS where a particle can self-occlude.

## Input / Output

Given a trained 3DGS (from observed RGB images and camera poses) and a set of collision-free candidate camera poses, GAVIS first constructs an anisotropic visibility field stored as 9 spherical-harmonic coefficients (L=2) per Gaussian particle, plus a small set of zero-opacity virtual particles (5–10% of total) to flag underexplored regions. It then queries this field in constant time per particle per ray to produce a per-pixel uncertainty map (GMM entropy) for each candidate view, from which the maximum-entropy pose is selected as the next observation. The entire visibility-field construction completes in ~0.17 s and uncertainty quantification runs at ~200 FPS on a single A40 GPU.

## Method

GAVIS defines an anisotropic visibility field V^(i)(d) for each 3DGS particle as a function of rendering direction d, where per-view visibility combines a binary FOV indicator, a transmittance term, and a directional visibility function ν(d; d_p) = ζ·exp(κ·d·d_p) proportional to a von Mises-Fisher distribution that decays as the novel-view direction deviates from the training-view direction. To avoid the O(|P|⁴L³) cost of directly multiplying SH expansions across views, the paper applies the AM-GM inequality to derive a lower bound on multi-view visibility that depends only on the sum of per-view visibilities, yielding SH coefficients γ_ℓm computed analytically in O(|P|) time with no gradient-based training. The visibility field is then integrated into a Bayesian-network-based uncertainty-aware 3DGS rasterizer that models each pixel's color as a Gaussian Mixture Model with a visibility-corrected prior, and the entropy of this GMM serves as the information-gain objective for next-best-view selection. A virtual-particle density-control module distinguishes truly empty (pruned) space from underexplored regions by assigning zero visibility to virtual particles that fall outside all training-view frustums.

## Results

GAVIS achieves the best PSNR across all four datasets: 24.26 dB (NeRF Synthetic), 26.14 dB (Space), 24.42 dB (Gibson), and 23.97 dB (HM3D), along with the lowest LPIPS and highest completion ratio (CR) in every setting. In terms of efficiency, GAVIS constructs the visibility field in 0.17 s (vs. 149.13 s for NVF, a ~500× speedup) and quantifies uncertainty at 251.5 FPS (vs. 11.9 FPS for NVF, ~30× faster). On scene visual coverage (VIS), GAVIS is competitive with NVF, achieving 0.890 on Gibson and 0.876 on HM3D compared to NVF's 0.915 and 0.873 respectively, with the small gap attributed to VIS being an inherently isotropic metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAVIS outperforms all three baselines on every image-quality metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and completion ratio across all four datasets. Against FisherRF (ECCV 2024), GAVIS gains +1.9 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic (24.26 vs 22.34) and +6.3 dB on Gibson (24.42 vs 18.11), while also being ~4× faster in UQ FPS (251.5 vs 39.8 on Gibson). Against VIMC (SIGGRAPH Asia 2024), GAVIS leads by +8.7 dB PSNR on Gibson (24.42 vs 15.70) and is ~500× faster in preparation time (0.42 s vs 90.47 s). Against NVF (CVPR 2024), the closest competitor, GAVIS wins on all image metrics (e.g., +1.67 dB PSNR on NeRF Synthetic, +1.13 dB on HM3D) and is ~500× faster in visibility-field construction (0.17 s vs 149.13 s) and ~30× faster in per-view uncertainty quantification (251.5 vs 11.9 FPS). The only metric where NVF edges out GAVIS is VIS on Gibson (0.915 vs 0.890), which the authors attribute to VIS being an isotropic, direction-agnostic metric that does not capture the benefit of anisotropic visibility modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAVIS extends the Neural Visibility Field (NVF) framework from NeRF to 3D Gaussian Splatting, replacing NVF's neural-network-learned isotropic visibility field with an analytically constructed anisotropic visibility field represented in spherical harmonics. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a direction-dependent visibility function (inspired by the von Mises-Fisher distribution) that accounts for self-occlusion of Gaussian particles, combined with an AM-GM inequality trick to avoid the prohibitively expensive multiplication of spherical harmonics across training views. The Bayesian-network GMM formulation for uncertainty-aware rendering is inherited from NVF but adapted to the 3DGS rasterizer, and the virtual-particle density control mechanism is a novel addition to handle the ambiguity between pruned free space and underexplored regions in 3DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500× speedup in visibility field construction (from minutes to ~1 second) makes visibility-driven active mapping practically viable for real-time robotic deployment, which was a major bottleneck with NVF. The ability to apply GAVIS post-hoc to existing 3DGS uncertainty methods (FisherRF, VIMC) broadens its utility beyond a standalone pipeline. Open questions include whether the AM-GM lower bound remains tight as the number of training views grows very large, whether the fixed SH degree L=2 suffices for highly anisotropic scenes, and how the approach scales to multi-robot or long-horizon exploration where the trajectory length becomes very large.
