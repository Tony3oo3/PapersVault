## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (VGGT, π³) achieve excellent quality but cannot process streaming video due to quadratic memory complexity, while existing streaming variants require costly retraining and may not fully leverage the geometric priors of these offline models. A key unresolved challenge is that simple Sim(3) alignment between temporal windows fails because monocular scale ambiguity causes relative depth scales of different scene layers to vary inconsistently between windows, producing anisotropic distortions that accumulate over long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video stream processed in overlapping temporal windows of L=20 frames (with O-frame overlap), requiring no camera calibration or per-scene optimization. It incrementally produces a globally consistent 4D reconstruction: dense per-frame 3D point maps, camera poses (rotation + translation), and pseudo-depth maps, all expressed in a single world coordinate frame. The system operates at ~14 FPS with 6 GB peak memory on a single RTX A6000 GPU, scaling to kilometer-long sequences that exceed the memory capacity of offline methods.

## Method

LASER is a training-free framework that wraps a frozen offline reconstructor (VGGT or π³) in a sliding-window pipeline. Each window's local submap is registered to the growing global map via a Sim(3) transform estimated with IRLS for scale and the Kabsch algorithm for rotation/translation. The core novelty is Layer-wise Scale Alignment (LSA): after global Sim(3) registration, each depth map is segmented into discrete depth layers using Felzenszwalb graph-based segmentation, and a directed layer graph is constructed with inter-window edges (matching layers at overlapping timestamps via IoU > 0.3) and intra-window edges (temporal continuity between adjacent frames). Per-layer scale factors are estimated via IRLS on point-wise depth correspondences across inter-window edges, then propagated and aggregated along all graph edges using IoU-weighted averaging, correcting the anisotropic depth scaling that a single global scale cannot resolve.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, LASER (π³) achieves 0.061 ATE on Sintel, 0.031 ATE on ScanNet, and 0.016 ATE on TUM, surpassing its offline backbone π³ (0.073/0.030/0.014) on Sintel and TUM while matching it on ScanNet. On video depth estimation, π³+Ours achieves 0.247 Abs Rel on Sintel and 0.048 on Bonn, closely matching the offline π³ backbone (0.245/0.050) while operating in streaming mode. On large-scale KITTI odometry, LASER (π³) achieves 24.17 m mean ATE, outperforming the training-free concurrent work VGGT-Long (27.64 m) and π³-Long (30.72 m) by 12–21%. The method runs at ~14.2 FPS with only 6 GB peak memory on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASER (π³) outperforms all streaming baselines across every dataset and metric in Table 2: on Sintel it reduces ATE from 0.201 (TTT3R, best streaming) to 0.061 (−69.7%), and on ScanNet from 0.052 (STream3Rβ) to 0.031 (−40.4%). On video depth (Table 1), π³+Ours achieves 0.247 Abs Rel on Sintel and 0.048 on Bonn, matching its offline backbone π³ (0.245/0.050) while beating the best streaming methods (STream3Rβ: 0.264/0.069; CUT3R: 0.421/0.078). On large-scale KITTI (Table 3), LASER (π³) achieves 24.17 m mean ATE, beating the training-free concurrent work VGGT-Long (27.64 m) and its π³-backbone re-implementation π³-Long (30.72 m) by 12–21%, while offline models VGGT and π³ simply run out of memory on all sequences. On 7-Scenes point map estimation (Table 4), π³+Ours achieves 0.013 m mean Acc, improving over the best streaming baseline STream3Rβ (0.042 m) by 69% and approaching the offline π³ backbone (0.011 m). Classical SLAM systems (ORB-SLAM2, DROID-SLAM) remain competitive on KITTI but require camera calibration and produce only sparse reconstructions, whereas LASER is calibration-free and dense.

## Relation to Prior Work

LASER builds directly on frozen feed-forward offline reconstructors (VGGT, π³) as its processing backbone, positioning itself in the lineage from DUSt3R through VGGT to π³. It departs from the dominant streaming paradigm—learned causal attention, persistent memory, or test-time training (CUT3R, StreamVGGT, Spann3R, TTT3R)—by requiring no retraining at all, instead using a sliding-window strategy with geometric alignment. The closest concurrent work, VGGT-Long, also uses chunk-and-align but relies on global Sim(3), which LASER shows is insufficient due to monocular scale ambiguity causing layer-wise depth misalignment. The key conceptual departure is importing classical layered scene decomposition (Baker et al., Shade et al.) into the modern transformer-reconstruction setting to correct per-layer scale inconsistencies that global similarity transforms cannot resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LASER provides a practical, drop-in recipe for converting any future offline reconstruction model into a streaming system at 14 FPS with 6 GB peak memory, scaling to kilometer-scale sequences that exhaust offline methods. This is significant because the field has been investing heavily in learned streaming architectures that require expensive retraining and may not fully exploit the geometric priors of state-of-the-art offline models; LASER shows that a lightweight geometric refinement can close most of the quality gap without any of that cost. Open questions include how the layer-wise alignment behaves under highly dynamic scenes with large non-rigid motion, whether the depth-layer graph approach generalizes to multi-camera or RGB-D inputs, and whether the IRLS-based scale estimation remains robust under severe textureless or repetitive-structure conditions.
