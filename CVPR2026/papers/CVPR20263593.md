## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) mapping systems assume static scenes or handle only short-term dynamics (objects moving within the camera's view), leaving long-term dynamics—structural changes occurring outside the camera view during data capture—largely unaddressed. When the environment evolves during mapping, stale observations and outdated map geometry corrupt the 3DGS optimization, producing erroneous reconstructions that cannot be reliably re-rendered from novel viewpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263593/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

GaME consumes a single-camera posed RGB-D image stream (monocular RGB-D sensor) processed online, with ground-truth or estimated camera poses. It produces a consistent, up-to-date 3D Gaussian map that supports real-time novel view synthesis of both color and depth from previously unseen viewpoints. The output reflects only the most recent state of the scene, with all prior structural changes (additions, removals, relocations) already integrated into the Gaussian representation.

## Method

GaME introduces two complementary mechanisms for online 3DGS mapping in evolving scenes. First, a Dynamic Scene Adaptation (DSA) module performs two operations: Add detects regions where observed depth is closer than rendered depth and seeds new Gaussians in unexplored areas, while Remove identifies stale Gaussians where rendered geometry penetrates the observed scene (inverted depth criterion) and color disagrees, then uses dense SAM masks and FlashSplat to extract and remove complete objects even under partial visibility. Second, a keyframe management system selectively masks only the stale regions within covisible keyframes rather than discarding entire frames, preserving useful background constraints while preventing outdated observations from corrupting the photometric and geometric loss optimization. The system operates incrementally, triggering DSA and keyframe updates at each new keyframe selected by standard translation/rotation thresholds.

## Results

On the Flat dataset (synthetic evolving scenes), GaME achieves 24.55 dB PSNR and 6.9 cm L1 depth error on input views, outperforming the strongest baseline MonoGS (21.24 dB, 30.95 cm) by 3.3 dB and 4.5× in depth. On the real-world Aria dataset, GaME reaches 31.39 dB PSNR and 1.22 cm depth error, a 29.7% relative PSNR improvement and ~4× depth reduction over MonoGS (24.20 dB, 5.07 cm). GaME also matches or exceeds state-of-the-art on static TUM-RGBD scenes (20.63 dB average PSNR vs. SplaTAM's 21.19 dB), confirming no degradation in non-dynamic settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaME decisively outperforms all three baselines on both evolving-scene datasets. Against the most competitive baseline MonoGS [18], GaME gains +3.3 dB PSNR and reduces depth L1 error by 4.5× on Flat (24.55 vs. 21.24 dB; 6.9 vs. 30.95 cm), and +7.2 dB PSNR with 4× depth reduction on Aria (31.39 vs. 24.20 dB; 1.22 vs. 5.07 cm). Against SplaTAM [11], the gap is even larger: +8.7 dB PSNR on Flat and +9.0 dB on Aria, with SplaTAM also showing severe novel-view degradation (12.69 dB on Flat novel views vs. GaME's 24.26 dB). DG-SLAM [16], a dynamic 3DGS change-detection method, performs worst across all metrics (13.72 dB on Flat, 14.20 dB on Aria), confirming that offline session-based change detection is ill-suited for continuous evolving-scene mapping. On static TUM-RGBD scenes, GaME (20.63 dB avg.) is within 0.56 dB of SplaTAM (21.19 dB) and outperforms MonoGS (17.48 dB), showing the DSA and keyframe management mechanisms do not harm static reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaME extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) mapping lineage (Gaussian-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS) by introducing a Dynamic Scene Adaptation mechanism that performs Add and Remove operations on the Gaussian set, and a keyframe management system that selectively masks stale regions rather than discarding entire frames. It departs from prior evolving-scene mapping work (Panoptic Multi-TSDFs, Khronos, POCD) which handles long-term dynamics but relies on non-renderable representations, and from dynamic 3DGS methods (4DGS, Dynamic 3DGS) that address only short-term in-view motion. The use of FlashSplat for optimal mask-to-Gaussian assignment in the Remove step is a notable integration that provides multiview consistency for object-level removal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaME fills a clear gap: no prior system could both handle long-term scene evolution and produce photorealistic novel-view renders, which is essential for AR/VR, digital twins, and autonomous driving in changing environments. The keyframe management finding—that naively discarding stale keyframes is far worse than selective region masking—provides a practical design principle for any incremental 3DGS system operating in non-static settings. Open questions include unifying short- and long-term dynamics, integrating change-aware pose tracking, and extending to a full 4D history representation, all of which the authors identify as future directions.
