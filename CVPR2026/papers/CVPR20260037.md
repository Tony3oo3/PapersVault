## Problem

Existing fragile-watermark-based manipulation localization methods (e.g., EditGuard, OmniGuard) assume the adversary is unaware of the embedded watermark, making them vulnerable to watermark removal attacks such as low-pass filtering and generative regeneration. Furthermore, dual-watermark strategies that pair a fragile watermark with a robust copyright watermark suffer from mutual interference and limited embedding capacity, and most prior methods neglect the ability to recover original content in tampered regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260037/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 256×256 RGB image, segments it into a facial saliency region and background using tools like MTCNN, YOLOSeg, or SAM2, and compresses the face into a latent representation via an encoder to fit within the background's limited capacity. At inference, given a suspicious image and the original face, it produces a binary manipulation localization mask (via pixel-wise difference between extracted and current face), a recovered face image, and a binary ownership decision based on whether the NCC between the extracted and original face exceeds 0.95.

## Method

RecoverMark's core insight is to treat the face content itself as the watermark and embed it robustly into the surrounding background, exploiting the practical constraint that adversaries must preserve background semantic consistency. The architecture uses a UNet hiding network (HNet) that takes channel-concatenated [encoded face, background] as input, with CEILNet-based encoder, decoder, and extraction networks. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 jointly trains all four networks with fidelity, watermark, and clean losses; Stage 2 freezes the encoder/decoder and inserts a distortion layer between HNet and ENet that simulates saliency noise, global post-processing (JPEG, Gaussian noise, low-pass filter), and a state-of-the-art regeneration attack. A progressive training strategy in Stage 2 introduces the regeneration attack first (occupying 50% of epochs) before sequentially adding simpler distortions, which the ablation shows is critical for achieving robustness against the strongest attack rather than converging to a local minimum effective only against weaker perturbations.

## Results

On the ID dataset (CelebA) under Structpix2pix manipulation, RecoverMark achieves localization F1 scores of 0.83–0.88 across all six attack types (Regeneration, Patch Remove, Noise, JPEG, LP Filter, Lattice), while the strongest baselines (OmniGuard, EditGuard) drop to 0.09–0.55 F1 under most attacks. On the OOD dataset (FFHQ), RecoverMark maintains F1 of 0.73–0.90, again far exceeding all baselines. For content recovery, RecoverMark achieves PSNR of 20.6–23.7 dB and MS-SSIM of 0.47–0.68, substantially outperforming Imuge+ (7.3–22.7 dB PSNR). Ownership verification reaches a 99.9% success rate (NCC > 0.95) across all test settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecoverMark decisively outperforms all five baselines on localization F1 across nearly every attack condition. The gap is most dramatic under the Regeneration attack, where passive methods (MVSS-Net, HiFiNet) collapse to 0.04–0.05 F1 and proactive methods (Imuge+, EditGuard, OmniGuard) fall to 0.09–0.11, while RecoverMark retains 0.72–0.88 F1. Under the unseen Lattice attack, RecoverMark still achieves 0.75–0.88 F1, whereas EditGuard (0.44–0.71) and OmniGuard (0.11–0.28) degrade substantially, confirming generalization to attacks not seen during training. The closest competitive scenario is Patch Remove, where OmniGuard reaches 0.54–0.91 F1 and EditGuard 0.24–0.46 F1, but RecoverMark still leads at 0.56–0.60 F1. For recovery quality, RecoverMark achieves 20.6–23.7 dB PSNR versus Imuge+'s 7.3–22.7 dB, a gap of roughly 10–15 dB under most attacks. No baseline matches RecoverMark on any metric under any attack type; the method wins across the board on both ID and OOD data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant proactive manipulation-localization paradigm (EditGuard, OmniGuard, Imuge+) that embeds a separate fragile watermark and relies on its corruption to localize tampering. Rather than introducing a new watermark signal, RecoverMark repurposes the protected content itself (the face) as the watermark, embedding it robustly into the surrounding background via a two-stage progressive training pipeline with a distortion layer. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between fragile-watermark localization and robust-watermark copyright protection by eliminating the dual-watermark architecture entirely, while the progressive distortion training draws on adversarial-training ideas applied to watermarking for the first time in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By exploiting the practical constraint that adversaries must preserve background semantic consistency, RecoverMark addresses a fundamental vulnerability in all prior proactive localization methods: their fragility to watermark removal attacks. The simultaneous achievement of localization, content recovery, and ownership verification in a single embedding eliminates the capacity-conflict problem of dual-watermark systems. Key open questions include the inherent capacity trade-off (large faces in small backgrounds degrade fidelity), generalization beyond facial regions to arbitrary objects, and whether higher-capacity robust embedding techniques can close the remaining gap for extreme aspect ratios.
