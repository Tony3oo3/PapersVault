## Problem

Existing cross-modal transfer methods follow a task-oriented paradigm where modality adaptation and task learning are coupled in a single supervised training process, requiring full retraining and separate model storage whenever the downstream task or target modality changes. This leads to substantial redundancy in labeled data, computation, and storage, since the modality gap that dominates cross-modal discrepancies is largely shared across tasks yet is re-learned from scratch each time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261784/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image paired with one or more non-RGB modalities (thermal, depth, event, or polarization) at matching spatial resolution and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask over a task-specific class set. A key operational detail is that Stage 1 consumes only unlabeled target-modality images collected from diverse sources, while Stage 2 requires labeled RGB-X pairs for the specific downstream task; the number of non-RGB modalities is flexible (2 or 3), and the same modality LoRA is reused across all tasks of that modality.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage decoupled transfer paradigm. In Stage 1, a Progressive Self-Supervised Tuning (PSST) strategy first warms up a modality-specific LoRA via MAE-based masked reconstruction to capture low-level structural priors, then jointly trains the MAE branch with a DINOv2-style self-teaching branch (image-level and MAE-guided patch-level consistency) on a filtered high-complexity subset, progressively learning from structure to semantics using only unlabeled data; the resulting LoRA is frozen and stored for reuse. In Stage 2, a Task-Prompted Mixture-of-Modality Experts (TP-MoME) module injects lightweight learnable visual prompts into every transformer block for task-specific knowledge, and inserts MoME fusion layers at selected blocks where one generalizable expert sees the full multimodal input while N modality-specific experts receive randomly masked subsets to encourage complementary specialization; a learned gating network dynamically weights all experts, and the hierarchical fused embeddings feed a task decoder. This design separates the one-time, task-agnostic modality gap bridging from the flexible, lightweight task adaptation, yielding reusable LoRAs and minimal per-task parameter overhead.

## Results

On SUN-RGBD, the proposed method with Hiera-L backbone achieves 56.3% mIoU, surpassing the best full fine-tuning method GeminiFusion (54.6%) by 1.7% and the best PEFT method SPEFT with Swin-L (55.0%) by 1.3%. On NYU Depth v2, it reaches 61.4% mIoU, outperforming GeminiFusion (60.2%) by 1.2% and SPEFT Swin-L (59.9%) by 1.5%. Across six cross-modal transfer scenarios (RGB-Depth, RGB-Thermal, RGB-Polarization, RGB-Event, RGB-Depth-Event), the method consistently achieves the best mIoU, with the largest margin of 2.4% over MLE-SAM on DELIVER RGB-Depth-Event segmentation. The method also demonstrates superior efficiency: with only 20% labeled data it surpasses CWSAM trained with 100% labels, and reduces trainable parameters by 44.3% compared to MemorySAM for the same set of downstream tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 baselines across all six evaluation scenarios. Against full fine-tuning methods, it surpasses GeminiFusion [17] by 1.7% mIoU on SUN-RGBD and 1.2% on NYU Depth v2, and beats CMNeXt [57] by 1.3% on DELIVER RGB-Depth. Among PEFT methods, it leads SPEFT [3] by 1.3% (SUN-RGBD) and 1.5% (NYU Depth v2) with Hiera-L, and by 0.6% and 1.1% with Hiera-B+ on MFNet and PST900 respectively. On MCubeS, it outperforms MemorySAM [26] by 1.6% (RGB-A) and 1.5% (RGB-A-D), and MLE-SAM [63] by 2.2% and 3.5% on the same settings. On DELIVER, it surpasses MLE-SAM [63] by 1.3% (RGB-Depth) and 2.4% (RGB-Depth-Event), and SPEFT [3] by 1.1% on RGB-Event. Notably, the method achieves these gains while using only 7.6M trainable parameters per task versus 116.6M for CMNeXt [57] and 12–17M for MemorySAM [26], and requires only 20% labeled data to match or exceed baselines trained with 80–100% labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAM 2 / Hiera backbone and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, prompt tuning) to address cross-modal semantic segmentation, but departs fundamentally from the task-oriented transfer paradigm used by prior methods like SPEFT, DSAM, and MLE-SAM, which couple modality and task adaptation in a single supervised training pass. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage decoupling: Stage 1 uses a Progressive Self-Supervised Tuning strategy that repurposes MAE (He et al., 2022) for modality-aware structural reconstruction and DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) for semantic alignment on unlabeled data, producing a reusable modality LoRA; Stage 2 introduces a Task-Prompted Mixture-of-Modality Experts module that goes beyond the simple LoRA-expert gating in MLE-SAM by incorporating random modality masking during training and a generalizable expert for balanced multimodal fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling paradigm offers a practically significant efficiency story: a one-time 1.3M-parameter modality LoRA is shared across all downstream tasks of that modality, yielding a 44.3% reduction in trainable parameters versus MemorySAM and a 77% reduction versus CMNeXt for five tasks, while also reducing labeled-data and training-epoch requirements. This is particularly relevant for deployment scenarios where multiple modalities and tasks must coexist on edge or resource-constrained hardware. Open questions include whether the self-supervised modality transfer generalizes to truly novel or extremely low-data modalities, whether the MoME design scales gracefully beyond three modalities, and whether the progressive MAE→DINOv2 curriculum is optimal or could be replaced by other SSL objectives tailored to specific sensor physics.
