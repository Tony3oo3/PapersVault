## Problem

State-of-the-art VLA models struggle to ground high-level language instructions into concrete action sequences for complex, multi-step manipulation tasks, because the semantic gap between an abstract instruction and the required visuo-motor execution is too large. Existing planning approaches either decompose tasks into textual subtasks (still leaving the VLA to resolve visual grounding) or rely on video generation (too slow and open-loop for closed-loop control). The paper asks whether providing the VLA with imagined future observations—visual instructions rather than purely linguistic ones—can substantially reduce this grounding burden.

## Input / Output

The foresight image generator takes a single 640×480 head-camera frame and a short subtask instruction (e.g., 'pick up the corn') and produces a 640×480 predicted future observation in 0.33 s on an H100. The VLA then receives the current multi-camera views (head + two wrist cameras) concatenated with the generated foresight image as its visual input, while the subtask description replaces the original high-level instruction as the language condition. The final output is a chunk of H robot actions; the VLM additionally emits a textual subtask description consumed by both the generator and the VLA.

## Method

ForeAct decomposes the VLA's conditional policy into a high-level planner π_h and a low-level VLA π_l. The planner consists of (1) a VLM (Qwen-3-VL-8B) that reasons over the task to produce an immediate subtask description, and (2) a foresight image generator built on SANA's 32× deep-compression autoencoder + linear DiT architecture, which performs conditional denoising (current observation concatenated with noise) to predict the future head-camera frame in 8 flow-matching steps. The generator is pretrained on ~10 M frame pairs spanning 1.16 M subtasks from four cross-embodiment datasets (AgiBot-World, RoboMind, Galaxea, Bridge) using flow matching, then lightly fine-tuned on 370 real-world episodes. Integration with any VLA requires no architectural change: the foresight image is simply appended to the visual input and the subtask text replaces the instruction. A cloud-edge deployment runs the VLM and generator on an H100 while the VLA executes on an RTX 5090, enabling closed-loop control at real-time rates.

## Results

On 11 diverse real-world manipulation tasks (Galaxea R1 Lite), ForeAct achieves an average success rate of 87.4%, a +40.9% absolute improvement over the vanilla π0 baseline (46.5%) and +30.3% over VLM+π0 (57.1%). On the LIBERO simulation benchmark with the π0.5 backbone, ForeAct raises the average success rate from 96.8% to 97.5%, surpassing CogVLA (97.4%) and all other baselines. The foresight image generator produces a 640×480 future observation in 0.33 s on an H100 GPU, enabling real-time closed-loop control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForeAct consistently outperforms all five compared baselines. Against the vanilla π0 [7] (3.3B VLA), ForeAct achieves a +40.9% absolute gain on real-world tasks (87.4% vs 46.5%) and a +3.3% gain on LIBERO (97.5% vs 94.2%). Against the stronger π0.5 [23] backbone, ForeAct still improves real-world success from 70.3% to 88.2% (+17.9%) and LIBERO from 96.8% to 97.5%. On LIBERO, ForeAct surpasses OpenVLA [27] by a wide margin (97.5% vs 76.5%) and CoT-VLA [51] by 13.6 points (97.5% vs 83.9%), confirming that visual foresight planning is far more effective than goal-image generation within a unified model. The closest competitor is CogVLA [31] at 97.4% on LIBERO, which ForeAct edges out by 0.1 points; however, on real-world tasks the gap is substantially larger, as CogVLA is not evaluated in that setting. The VLM+π0 ablation (textual subtask guidance only, 57.1%) further isolates the contribution of the visual foresight component, showing that language-only subtask decomposition is insufficient compared to providing imagined future observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SANA's efficient image generation architecture (32× deep compression autoencoder + linear DiT) and adapts it from text-to-image synthesis to conditional image-to-image foresight prediction by concatenating the current observation with noise input. It departs from prior visual-prediction-for-control work (SuSIE, COT-VLA, video-generation-based methods) by generating a single high-resolution frame in 0.33s rather than a video sequence, and from hierarchical VLM planning approaches by providing concrete visual targets instead of abstract textual subtask descriptions. The key conceptual departure is treating the foresight image as a drop-in visual augmentation to the VLA's input rather than requiring architectural changes or unified planning-control models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play integration (no VLA architectural modification, just augmented visual input) and 0.33s generation latency make this practically deployable for closed-loop robot control, addressing a key bottleneck that has limited prior visual-prediction approaches. The 30-point gap between textual subtask guidance (57.1%) and visual foresight (87.4%) suggests that concrete visual targets are fundamentally more informative than language for grounding VLA actions, a finding with implications for how future VLA systems should be conditioned. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon tasks beyond the 11-task benchmark, whether the cloud-edge deployment latency is acceptable for all control frequencies, and whether the foresight generator's cross-embodiment pretraining transfers to entirely new robot morphologies.
