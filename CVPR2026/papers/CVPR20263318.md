## Problem

Existing foundation model-based VLN methods suffer from two coupled limitations: passive perception, where agents uniformly process token-heavy panoramic or multi-view inputs that dilute attention across irrelevant features and obscure instruction-critical fine-grained cues, and unfocused reasoning, where all historical waypoints and observations are treated indiscriminately, preventing the model from isolating high-value navigation contexts in long trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263318/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a panoramic RGB image at each timestep, a natural language instruction, the online-discovered navigation graph, and accumulated multimodal memory from prior steps. The panoramic image is split into K overlapping directional views for object detection; the system then generates targeted visual queries paired with 2D focus regions (sub-crops of the panorama) that the VLM perception agent processes in isolation. The final output is a next-waypoint selection from the navigable candidates, mediated by top-k high-value waypoint candidates extracted from the search tree and per-waypoint semantic value scores in [0,1].

## Method

ProFocus is a training-free multi-agent framework with two core mechanisms. First, Reasoning-Guided Proactive Perception converts panoramic observations into ego-centric semantic maps (object bounding boxes, monocular depth via Depth Pro, and heading angles), then an LLM orchestration agent iteratively generates targeted visual queries with focus regions; a VLM perception agent performs fine-grained sensing only within those cropped regions, and the loop continues until the orchestration agent judges information sufficient, after which semantic values are assigned to newly discovered waypoints. Second, Branch-Diverse MCTS (BD-MCTS) adapts standard MCTS to cyclic navigation graphs by replacing rollouts with semantic value initialization, applying asymmetric dynamic backpropagation along the selected root-to-current path, and enforcing a branch-diversity constraint (at most 2 children per parent in the top-k set) combined with a normalized shortest-path distance penalty. The LLM decision agent then performs focused reasoning exclusively over the filtered top-k candidates and their associated historical contexts retrieved from the memory bank, rather than diffusing attention across all accumulated waypoints.

## Results

On R2R validation unseen, ProFocus (Qwen3-Max + Qwen3-VL-Max) achieves 52.5% SR, 65.0% OSR, 39.8% SPL, and 4.92m NE, while the DeepSeek-V3 + GLM-4.5V configuration reaches 50.0% SR, 63.0% OSR, and 41.2% SPL. On REVERIE validation unseen, ProFocus (Q3+Q3VL) attains 40.0% SR and 24.8% SPL, and ProFocus (DS3+GLM) reaches 36.9% SR and 25.9% SPL. Both configurations set new state-of-the-art zero-shot results on both benchmarks, with the largest gains appearing on REVERIE where the Q3+Q3VL configuration more than doubles the SR of NavGPT (40.0% vs. 18.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under fair re-implementation with matched backbones, ProFocus (Q3+Q3VL) outperforms NavGPT†(Q3) by +5.5% SR (52.5 vs. 47.0) and +1.4% SPL (39.8 vs. 38.4) on R2R, and by a dramatic +22.0% SR (40.0 vs. 18.0) and +11.1% SPL (24.8 vs. 13.7) on REVERIE. ProFocus (DS3+GLM) beats NavGPT†(DS3) by +14.0% SR (50.0 vs. 36.0) and +13.1% SPL (41.2 vs. 28.1) on R2R, and surpasses MapGPT†(GLM) by +8.6% SR (50.0 vs. 41.4) and +10.4% SPL (41.2 vs. 30.8). On REVERIE, ProFocus (DS3+GLM) slightly trails MapGPT†(GLM) on SR (36.9 vs. 37.1) but leads on OSR (57.1 vs. 50.5) and SPL (25.9 vs. 24.7), indicating better exploration coverage. Against the strongest original zero-shot baselines (MSNav GPT-4o at 46.0% SR, DiscussNav GPT-4 at 43.0% SR on R2R), ProFocus (Q3+Q3VL) exceeds both by 6.5 and 9.5 percentage points in SR respectively. The consistent advantage across both model configurations and both datasets confirms that the gains stem from the proactive perception and BD-MCTS mechanisms rather than from a particular foundation model's capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProFocus builds on the foundation model-based VLN paradigm (NavGPT, MapGPT, NaviLLM) but departs from its passive perception and uniform historical context handling by introducing a multi-agent architecture where an LLM orchestration agent actively directs a VLM perception agent to query specific regions, and a modified MCTS (BD-MCTS) filters historical waypoints before a decision LLM reasons over them. Conceptually, it extends standard MCTS [4] by replacing random rollouts with LLM-based semantic value estimation, adding branch-diversity constraints to ensure spatial coverage, and using path-aggregated value with distance penalties for top-k selection. The ego-centric semantic map construction (combining object detection, SAM2 segmentation, and monocular depth) repurposes existing perception tools into a structured intermediate representation that enables LLM spatial reasoning without end-to-end training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design lowers the barrier to deploying VLN agents in new environments without domain-specific fine-tuning, and the closed perception-reasoning loop offers a principled alternative to the token-heavy panoramic processing that dominates current VLM-based navigation. However, the framework's reliance on iterative LLM/VLM calls introduces significant inference latency, and evaluation is limited to discrete waypoint graphs on two benchmarks (R2R, REVERIE) without continuous-environment or multi-goal settings. Open questions include whether the BD-MCTS filtering generalizes to much longer horizons (beyond the 30-episode long-trajectory analysis), how the approach scales to 3D continuous navigation, and whether the multi-agent overhead can be reduced without sacrificing the focused-reasoning benefits.
