## Problem

Traditional machine unlearning methods rely on reverse-training to degrade model performance on undesired data, but in Vision-Language Models this approach disrupts cross-modal alignment rather than genuinely erasing knowledge. The paper demonstrates through probing experiments that apparent forgetting in VLMs is largely superficial misalignment, leaving residual knowledge in the internal language model that poses a leakage risk.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263012/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained VLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B or Qwen2-VL-7B), a forget set of image-text pairs (synthetic personal profiles with portraits and 7 Visual-QA questions each), and retain set images used to construct the vacuum-space projector via SVD on mean-pooled patch-level image representations. LoRA adapters are applied exclusively to the FFN linear layers of each transformer block. The output is a modified VLM whose FFN weights have the projected LoRA parameters subtracted, producing text responses that no longer encode the forgotten knowledge while preserving utility on retain and real-celebrity sets.

## Method

VL-Eraser reformulates VLM unlearning as a two-stage distill-then-delete process. In the distillation stage, frozen FFN weights serve as a teacher while LoRA adapters act as students, trained with a combined FFN-wise cosine-similarity distillation loss and cross-entropy loss on the forget set. Crucially, the distillation is constrained within a 'vacuum space'—the left null space of retain-set image representations computed via SVD—so that LoRA parameters are projected orthogonal to retain-specific knowledge, ensuring only forget-specific knowledge is captured. In the deletion stage, the trained LoRA parameters (projected into the vacuum space) are simply subtracted from the FFN weights, zeroing out forget-set representations while leaving retain-set representations unchanged due to the orthogonality constraint. This avoids the cross-modal alignment degradation inherent in reverse-training, enabling more complete and targeted knowledge removal.

## Results

On MLLMU-Bench (5% Forget), VL-Eraser achieves the lowest Forget-set classification accuracy across both VLM backbones: 26.2% on LLaVA-1.5-7B Visual-QA (vs. 51.2% vanilla) and 33.6% on Qwen-2-7B Visual-QA (vs. 52.5% vanilla), representing average degradations of 24.1% (LLaVA) and 19.6% (Qwen) in classification accuracy. On the Retain set, VL-Eraser preserves 43.4% accuracy (LLaVA) and 55.8% (Qwen), remaining closest to vanilla performance (47.9% and 56.2% respectively). For generation (ROUGE-L), VL-Eraser reduces Forget-set scores to 0.256 (LLaVA) and 0.265 (Qwen) while maintaining Retain scores of 0.473 and 0.568.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VL-Eraser outperforms all five baselines on Forget-set accuracy across both VLM backbones and all three task types (classification, generation, cloze), achieving the lowest scores (best unlearning). The closest competitor, MMUnlearner, reaches 32.6% Forget accuracy on LLaVA Visual-QA versus VL-Eraser's 26.2%, a 6.4-point gap. Traditional reverse-training methods (GA, GA_Diff, KL_Min, NPO) show substantially weaker unlearning (37–43% Forget accuracy) while simultaneously degrading Retain-set performance more severely (e.g., GA drops Retain to 39.7% vs. VL-Eraser's 43.4%). On utility preservation, VL-Eraser maintains the highest Retain and Real scores among all unlearning methods, with the vacuum-space mechanism limiting collateral damage to non-target knowledge. The ablation confirms that removing the vacuum constraint (w/o Vacuum) further lowers Forget accuracy to 25.4% but causes a 7-point drop in Retain accuracy (36.6%), validating the design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reverse-training paradigm for machine unlearning (gradient ascent, KL-based negative optimization, NPO) by reframing unlearning as a two-stage distill-then-delete process rather than iterative performance degradation. It repurposes LoRA not as a fine-tuning tool but as a dedicated knowledge container, and introduces a 'vacuum space' constraint via SVD on retain-set image representations to ensure the distilled knowledge is orthogonal to retained knowledge. The parameter-arithmetic deletion step (subtracting LoRA from the base FFN) connects conceptually to task-vector-based unlearning strategies, but the vacuum-space projection provides a principled mechanism for selective knowledge isolation that prior task-vector methods lack. The paper also builds on the established understanding that FFN layers serve as primary knowledge repositories in transformers, targeting distillation specifically to those layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that traditional unlearning in VLMs primarily disrupts cross-modal alignment rather than erasing knowledge is a useful reframing that explains why prior methods show apparent forgetting on visual tasks but leak knowledge through text-only probing. The distill-then-delete paradigm offers a practical, computationally efficient alternative (comparable to NPO in training time) that avoids the alignment degradation problem by construction. However, the evaluation is limited to a single synthetic benchmark (MLLMU-Bench) with 7B models, and the vacuum space construction via SVD on image representations may not generalize to more complex or larger-scale unlearning scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unlearning from the vision encoder itself, whether it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the orthogonality assumption holds when forget and retain knowledge are highly entangled.
