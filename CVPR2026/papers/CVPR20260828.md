## Problem

Existing self-supervised 3D hand pose estimation methods (e.g., S²Hand, HaMuCo) rely on deterministic 2D pseudo-labels from off-the-shelf detectors, making them highly susceptible to pseudo-label noise and prone to training instability. These methods also fail to adequately exploit fine-grained spatial correlations and temporal dynamics, resulting in insufficient capture of complex 3D hand structure and continuous motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260828/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized, calibrated multi-view RGB video sequences (V views × T frames) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics, processing a sliding temporal window of 5 frames centered on each target frame. Supervision comes exclusively from 2D pseudo-labels (21 joint positions per view) generated by an offline detector such as Wilor or OpenPose. The output is a set of 21 3D hand joints and a 778-vertex MANO hand mesh in world coordinates, parameterized through the MANO model from the refined joint positions.

## Method

UST-Hand operates in two synergistic stages. In stage 1, a ResNet34 backbone extracts multi-scale features; a heatmap head produces joint locations and per-joint confidence scores; a confidence-aware self-attention (CASA) mechanism with adaptive-GCN layers fuses cross-view features while suppressing low-confidence pseudo-labels; and a conditional RealNVP normalizing flow models the full 2D joint position distribution, sampling M+1 diverse hypotheses per view to explicitly preserve uncertainty. In stage 2, these multi-view hypotheses are lifted via confidence-weighted DLT triangulation into a unified probabilistic 3D point cloud space, where anchor points (from random samples) preserve distributional uncertainty and query points (from the mode) serve as refinement targets. A Spatiotemporal Point Transformer (STPT) then iteratively refines query points over K steps through a dual-stage attention mechanism: neighborhood-aware spatial self-attention captures local geometric structure, temporal self-attention models hand motion across the 5-frame window, and cross-attention with the anchor cloud injects distributional uncertainty, progressively improving pose estimation under noisy supervision.

## Results

On HanCo (8 views), UST-Hand achieves 5.82 mm MPVPE and 5.19 mm MPJPE, reducing MPVPE by 37.8% over HaMuCo (9.35 mm) and 54.1% over Wilor (12.70 mm). On DexYCB-MV (8 views), it reaches 8.16 mm MPVPE and 7.84 mm MPJPE (14.5% and 15.2% improvements over HaMuCo). On OakInk-MV (4 views), UST-Hand attains 10.02 mm MPVPE and 8.70 mm MPJPE, outperforming HaMuCo by 23.2% and 27.7% respectively. AUC-V gains of 16.2%, 3.3%, and 6.5% over HaMuCo are observed on the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UST-Hand consistently outperforms HaMuCo [68], the prior SOTA self-supervised multi-view method, on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest gap on HanCo (37.8% MPVPE reduction, 9.35→5.82 mm) and OakInk-MV (23.2% MPVPE reduction, 13.04→10.02 mm). Against Wilor [42], a SOTA monocular 3D hand detector used as a reference for 3D reconstruction quality, UST-Hand achieves substantially lower errors (e.g., 5.82 vs. 12.70 mm MPVPE on HanCo), demonstrating that multi-view self-supervised learning with uncertainty modeling surpasses even strong supervised single-view baselines. In the pseudo-label quality ablation (Table 3), UST-Hand also dominates EpipolarTrans [28] and Learnable Triangulation [30] under all three supervision settings (OpenPose, Wilor, and ground-truth 2D labels), achieving 7.5 mm MPJPE with noisy OpenPose labels versus 11.2 mm for EpipolarTrans and 10.3 mm for LT-Algebraic. Under ground-truth 2D supervision (upper bound), UST-Hand reaches 3.7 mm MPJPE and 2.4 mm PA-J, surpassing HaMuCo by 38.3% and LT-Algebraic by 32.7%, confirming that the uncertainty-aware spatiotemporal framework effectively exploits high-quality cues as well as denoises low-quality ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

UST-Hand extends the self-supervised 3D hand pose estimation paradigm initiated by S²Hand (CVPR 2021), which supervised reconstruction via noisy 2D pseudo-labels, and HaMuCo (ICCV 2023), which added multi-view collaborative learning in 2D feature space. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing deterministic pseudo-label supervision with conditional normalizing flows (RealNVP) that model the full uncertainty distribution of hand joints and sample diverse hypotheses, and (2) lifting these hypotheses into a unified probabilistic 3D point cloud space where a Spatiotemporal Point Transformer performs iterative neighborhood-based refinement. This positions the work at the intersection of multi-hypothesis pose estimation (normalizing flows, VAEs) and point cloud processing (Point Transformer, PointNet), but uniquely combines them in a probabilistic, uncertainty-aware framework for articulated object reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uncertainty-aware probabilistic formulation addresses a fundamental practical bottleneck: self-supervised hand pose methods are highly sensitive to pseudo-label noise, and deterministic approaches tend to overfit to that noise rather than learn robust geometric priors. The consistent outperformance across three datasets, three pseudo-label quality levels (OpenPose → Wilor → ground-truth), and sparse-view settings (2–8 cameras) suggests the probabilistic point cloud space captures genuine spatiotemporal structure beyond what 2D-space multi-view methods can exploit. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to single-view or uncalibrated settings, whether the 5-frame temporal window is sufficient for longer motion sequences, and whether the probabilistic point cloud paradigm extends to other articulated objects (e.g., full-body pose, facial landmarks) under similarly noisy supervision.
