## Problem

Most feature attribution methods for image classifiers capture only marginal (per-feature) effects, overlooking higher-order feature interactions where groups of pixels jointly drive predictions. Existing interaction-based approaches for images are either restricted to coarse superpixel-level granularity, computationally intractable at pixel resolution, or fail to satisfy core interpretability axioms such as completeness and sensitivity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262055/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained CNN classifier (VGG16, ResNet101, DenseNet121, or MobileNetV3), an RGB input image, and a target class, and produces up to 5 semantically coherent interaction sets (each containing up to 2000–3000 pixels) with scalar importance scores, which are aggregated into a pixel-level saliency map. SAM segmentation masks serve as a replaceable spatial prior to constrain merges within coherent regions, and a baseline image (e.g., zeros) is required for the path-integral attribution step.

## Method

H-Sets is a two-stage framework. In the detection stage, it computes the input Hessian of the target class logit (using a smoothed ReLU approximation for stable second-order derivatives) to identify pairwise interactions exceeding a threshold μ, then recursively expands these pairs into higher-order sets via a breadth-first merge strategy bounded by a size limit ν and spatial coherence from SAM masks. In the attribution stage, IDG-Vis constructs a unit direction vector over the pixels in each interaction set, integrates the directional gradient along a linear path from a baseline to the input (discretized as a Riemann sum), and aggregates over Monte Carlo-sampled subsets using Harsanyi dividends from cooperative game theory. This design restricts Hessians to detection only (avoiding gradient-saturation issues in attribution) and yields a set-level score that satisfies nine axioms spanning game theory and attribution literature, including approximate completeness, sensitivity, and implementation invariance.

## Results

On ImageNet, H-Sets achieves the highest ROAD_AOPC faithfulness scores across all four architectures: 0.34 (VGG), 0.26 (ResNet), 0.38 (DenseNet), and 0.37 (MobileNet), outperforming the strongest baseline MOXI by 0.02–0.04. On the fine-grained CUB dataset, H-Sets reaches 0.65 (VGG), 0.63 (ResNet), 0.65 (DenseNet), and 0.60 (MobileNet), leading all baselines by 0.01–0.07. For sparsity (Gini index), H-Sets attains 0.94–0.98 on ImageNet and 0.89–0.93 on CUB, consistently matching or exceeding the best baseline. Ablations confirm that faithfulness is robust to the Hessian threshold μ (stable across 0.1–0.8) and that 5 interaction sets provide the best sparsity–faithfulness trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-Sets outperforms all four baselines on faithfulness (ROAD_AOPC) across every architecture–dataset combination. Against MOXI, the strongest interaction-based competitor, H-Sets leads by 0.02 on ImageNet-VGG (0.34 vs 0.32), 0.03 on ImageNet-DenseNet (0.38 vs 0.35), and 0.07 on CUB-VGG (0.65 vs 0.58). Against Archipelago, H-Sets gains 0.07 on ImageNet-VGG (0.34 vs 0.27) and a dramatic 0.59 on CUB-VGG (0.65 vs 0.06), where Archipelago's superpixel-level granularity severely degrades faithfulness on fine-grained classes. CAFO and CASO trail H-Sets by 0.12–0.24 on ImageNet and 0.16–0.25 on CUB, confirming that context-aware first/second-order perturbations without explicit non-additivity isolation are less faithful. On sparsity (Gini), H-Sets is competitive with MOXI (which slightly edges H-Sets on ImageNet-MobileNet at 0.96 vs 0.95) but clearly surpasses Archipelago, CAFO, CASO, and IG across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

H-Sets extends the IDG framework from NLP [Sikdar et al., ACL 2021] to vision by constructing directional vectors over pixel sets rather than tokens, and grounds set-level attribution in Harsanyi dividends from cooperative game theory. It departs from Archipelago [Tsang et al., NeurIPS 2020] by replacing mask-search-based detection with curvature-driven Hessian detection, and from Integrated Hessians [Janizek et al., JMLR 2021] by restricting Hessians to the detection stage while using gradient-path integration for attribution. The spatial prior from SAM is modular and replaceable (QuickShift, MedSAM), distinguishing it from methods that hard-code superpixel or patch granularity like MOXI [Sumiyasu et al., CVPR 2024].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most widely used attribution methods (IG, Grad-CAM, SmoothGrad) capture only marginal per-pixel effects, while existing interaction-based methods are either too coarse (superpixel-level) or fail to satisfy core axioms. H-Sets provides a principled two-stage pipeline that satisfies nine interpretability axioms and produces consistently sparser, more faithful saliency maps across four architectures and two datasets. Key open questions include adaptation to Vision Transformers (where token-based representations make pixel-level Hessians non-trivial), the computational cost of second-order derivatives at scale, and whether the SAM spatial prior is truly necessary or merely helpful for fine-grained classes.
