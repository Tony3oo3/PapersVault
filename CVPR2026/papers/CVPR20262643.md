## Problem

Diffusion Transformers incur quadratic self-attention cost over all spatial tokens at every denoising step, yet existing acceleration methods (fewer steps, feature caching, quantization) treat all spatial regions uniformly and ignore the well-known coarse-to-fine structure of the generative process. This paper asks how to dynamically allocate computation only to spatially salient regions during early stages while deferring detail-heavy regions, without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a single RGB image via the FLUX.1-dev pipeline. Internally, the VAE-encoded latent is spatially patched into N tokens of dimension d; at each ODE step only a dynamically selected subset of m_k anchor tokens (m_k < N) is fed to the Transformer, while the full N-token state is maintained and evolved via extrapolation. The final full-dimensional token sequence is de-tokenized and decoded by the VAE into the output image.

## Method

JiT formulates a Spatially Approximated Generative ODE (SAG-ODE) in which the full N-token velocity field is reconstructed from a sparse m_k-token Transformer output using an augmented lifter operator: exact velocities are embedded at anchor positions and smooth spatial interpolation fills the inactive subspace, guaranteeing zero approximation error on the anchor tokens themselves. Stage transitions that expand the active token set are handled by a Deterministic Micro-Flow (DMF), a short finite-time hitting ODE that drives newly activated tokens to a target state built from a Tweedie's-formula clean prediction fused with correctly-scaled Gaussian noise, preventing spatial discontinuities and distribution shifts. Token selection at each transition is governed by Importance-Guided Token Activation (ITA), which ranks inactive tokens by the local variance of the predicted velocity field (computed via 3×3 average pooling) so that computation is directed toward high-frequency, structurally active regions. The entire framework is training-free and requires only a predefined schedule of transition times and token counts.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, JiT achieves a 4.24× speedup (6.02 s, 706 TFLOPs) with near-lossless quality: CLIP-IQA 0.6166, ImageReward 1.017, HPSv2.1 29.77, GenEval 0.6540, and T2I-CompBench 0.4991, all matching or exceeding the 50-NFE reference (CLIP-IQA 0.6139, ImageReward 1.004, GenEval 0.6565). At the more aggressive 7.07× speedup (3.67 s, 423 TFLOPs), JiT still attains CLIP-IQA 0.5397, ImageReward 0.9746, HPSv2.1 29.02, GenEval 0.6457, and T2I-CompBench 0.4961, substantially outperforming all competing methods at the same speed tier. In a user study with 1000 blind pairwise votes, JiT was preferred over every baseline at both acceleration levels, with win rates ranging from 69.1% to 93.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the ~4× tier, JiT (18 NFE) outperforms all spatial and caching baselines on every quality metric: it beats Bottleneck by +0.211 on CLIP-IQA (0.6166 vs 0.4056) and +0.021 on T2I-CompBench (0.4991 vs 0.4779), surpasses RALU by +0.067 on CLIP-IQA and +0.037 on T2I-CompBench, and edges out TeaCache by +0.016 on CLIP-IQA and +0.014 on T2I-CompBench while also being the fastest (6.02 s vs 6.98 s). At the ~7× tier, the gap widens dramatically: JiT (11 NFE) leads RALU by +0.053 on CLIP-IQA (0.5397 vs 0.4865) and +0.023 on T2I-CompBench (0.4961 vs 0.4731), outperforms TaylorSeer by +0.123 on CLIP-IQA and +0.038 on T2I-CompBench, and beats TeaCache by +0.021 on CLIP-IQA and +0.034 on T2I-CompBench. JiT also achieves the lowest latency (3.67 s) and FLOPs (423.26) at 7×, making it both the fastest and highest-quality method. The vanilla FLUX.1-dev at 7 NFE suffers severe degradation (CLIP-IQA drops to 0.4134), whereas JiT retains 0.5397, demonstrating that spatial acceleration preserves far more fidelity than simply reducing NFEs. In the user study, JiT is preferred over every baseline at both tiers, with the largest margins against FLUX.1-dev(7) at 93.1% and TaylorSeer at 89.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

JiT departs from the dominant temporal-acceleration paradigm (fewer NFEs via high-order solvers, distillation, or feature caching) by targeting spatial redundancy within each sampling step. It is conceptually related to hierarchical/pyramidal spatial methods (RALU, Bottleneck Sampling) that generate at low resolution and upscale, but JiT eliminates explicit upsampling operators entirely, instead operating on nested token subsets with a projector-based ODE formulation inspired by subspace diffusion. The deterministic micro-flow for seamless stage transitions and the importance-guided activation based on local velocity-field variance are novel mechanisms not present in prior spatial or temporal acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free 7× speedup on FLUX.1-dev with near-lossless quality is a practically significant efficiency win that can be immediately applied to any flow-matching DiT without retraining, lowering the barrier for real-time and consumer-grade deployment. The spatial-redundancy framing (global structure before fine detail) is well-motivated and suggests the approach could extend to video DiTs where token counts are far larger, though this remains untested. Open questions include whether the interpolation-based extrapolation degrades at higher resolutions or with more aggressive sparsity schedules, and whether JiT composes cleanly with temporal acceleration (caching, distillation) for multiplicative gains.
