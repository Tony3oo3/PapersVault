## Problem

Diffusion models require dozens of sampling steps for inference, and existing one-step distillation methods (full fine-tuning or LoRA) jointly optimize both weight norm and direction, increasing optimization difficulty due to their strong coupling. The paper identifies that during distillation, weight direction changes (mean 2.2%) vastly exceed norm changes (mean 0.1%), yet no prior method explicitly targets directional updates in isolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled one-step generator takes a text prompt and produces a single 256×256 RGB image in one forward pass (NFE=1), operating in the latent space of U-Net (SD 1.5/2.1) or DiT (PixArt-α) backbones. Only ~10% of the backbone parameters (83.8M–94.4M out of 610M–865M) are trainable, with the student initialized from the frozen teacher weights. The framework is image-free, requiring only 1.4M text prompts from JourneyDB for training.

## Method

WaDi introduces LoRaD (Low-rank Rotation of weight Direction), which reparameterizes weight updates as learnable block-diagonal 2×2 rotations applied to odd-even row pairs of pre-trained weight matrices, inspired by RoPE. The rotation angle matrix Θ is factorized into two low-rank matrices (Θ = AB, rank r) to keep trainable parameters minimal. This is integrated into Variational Score Distillation (VSD), where the one-step student uses a high-rank rotation (rank 256) to fit the teacher distribution while a fake model uses a low-rank rotation (rank 32) for adaptive guidance, with alternating optimization. By operating purely in direction space and preserving weight norms, LoRaD decouples the optimization, reducing the effective search space and enabling stable convergence with far fewer parameters than FT or standard LoRA.

## Results

On COCO 2014, WaDi achieves FID 10.79 (SD 1.5), 12.34 (SD 2.1), and 18.99 (PixArt-α) with only 1 NFE, outperforming all one-step baselines on FID and Recall across all three backbones. The method uses only ~10% trainable parameters (83.8M/860M for SD 1.5, 94.4M/865M for SD 2.1, 81.2M/610.9M for PixArt-α) compared to full fine-tuning methods that update all 860M+ parameters. WaDi also achieves competitive CLIP scores (0.31 on SD 1.5 and SD 2.1) and the highest Precision (0.62 on SD 1.5) among one-step methods, while being image-free (no real-image supervision during training).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the SD 1.5 backbone, WaDi (FID 10.79) outperforms DMD2 (12.96), DMD (11.49), SiD-LSG (14.27), InstaFlow (13.10), and UFOGen (12.78) by 1.2–3.5 FID points, while using only 83.8M trainable parameters versus 860M for full fine-tuning methods. On SD 2.1, WaDi (FID 12.34) beats TiUE (13.49), SiD-LSG (15.17), SwiftBrushv2 (15.98), and SwiftBrush (16.67), with the closest competitor being TiUE at a 1.15 FID gap. On the DiT-based PixArt-α backbone, WaDi (FID 18.99) substantially outperforms PG-SB (25.58) and SwiftBrush (29.89) by 6.6 and 10.9 FID points respectively. In terms of CLIP score, WaDi ties or leads most baselines (0.31 on SD 1.5/2.1, 0.30 on PixArt-α), though SD-Turbo (0.33) and SwiftBrushv2 (0.33) marginally exceed it on SD 2.1. Notably, WaDi achieves the best Recall (0.48) on both SD backbones and the best Precision (0.62) on SD 1.5, indicating superior diversity and perceptual quality. All WaDi results are image-free, matching or exceeding methods that require real-image supervision (e.g., DMD, InstaFlow, LCM-LoRA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Variational Score Distillation (VSD) from ProlificDreamer as its distillation objective and borrows the low-rank factorization idea from LoRA, but departs from both by introducing a rotation-based parameterization (LoRaD) that operates exclusively in the weight-direction subspace. Unlike LoRA and DoRA, which apply additive low-rank updates to weights (or to normalized weights followed by re-normalization), LoRaD parameterizes the update as a low-rank orthogonal rotation, preserving weight norms by construction. The key conceptual departure is the empirical finding that directional changes dominate over norm changes in one-step distillation, which motivates restricting the adapter to direction space and is inspired by the weight reparameterization literature and the block-diagonal rotation structure of RoPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direction-dominance finding provides a useful mechanistic lens for understanding what distillation actually changes in a diffusion model, potentially guiding future adapter design beyond VSD-based methods. WaDi's ~10% trainable parameter budget with SOTA FID on COCO is practically attractive for practitioners who need one-step generation without full fine-tuning, and the demonstrated transfer to ControlNet, Reversion, and Dreambooth suggests broad applicability. However, the FID gains over DMD2 are modest (≈2 points on SD 1.5), the evaluation is limited to 256×256 resolution for the DiT backbone, and it remains open whether the direction-dominance phenomenon generalizes to other distillation paradigms (consistency, progressive) or to video generation.
