## Problem

Existing motion generation methods are designed for single-character human-object interaction or multi-person social scenarios without object manipulation, leaving a gap for generating physically plausible, coordinated dual-human co-manipulation motions. The challenge is that co-manipulation requires tight triadic coupling between two humans and a shared object, demanding synchronized motion, natural inter-person coordination, and stable grasp dynamics that no single prior framework addresses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263295/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object mesh, its per-frame rigid 6D trajectory (rotation and translation over T frames), and per-frame BPS shape descriptors (1024-dim vectors computed from fixed point sampling of the mesh) as conditioning. It outputs a 128-frame dual-human SMPL-X motion sequence (6D joint rotations, 10-dim shape coefficients, and 3D global translations for each of two agents) along with a contact strategy specifying per-hand world-space anchor positions, surface normals, local mesh offsets, and binary validity flags. The object trajectory is consumed as a hard constraint that the generated hand positions must track, while the BPS features provide shape-aware context to the flow-matching transformer.

## Method

The core framework is a transformer-based flow-matching model that regresses a continuous velocity field transporting Gaussian noise to the data manifold of dual-human SMPL-X sequences, conditioned on object 6D poses and BPS features. Three modules are integrated into the flow: (1) an affordance-informed manipulation strategy where a diffusion model, conditioned on predicted surface affordance scores and BPS features, generates diverse contact anchors that are injected as differentiable gradient cues (Eq. 11) to pull wrists toward graspable regions during each Euler step; (2) an adversarial interaction prior consisting of two discriminators (individual pose and paired interaction) trained with non-saturating BCE, whose log-probability gradients (Eq. 14) steer the flow toward natural and coordinated poses both during training and inference; (3) a stability-driven simulation executed at the penultimate Euler step, where CMA-ES samples corrective pose offsets, a PD controller drives SMPL-X humanoids in PyBullet, and the lowest-cost simulated result (minimizing pose similarity and object stability losses) replaces the flow state for the final integration step. The components are trained separately (flow matching, contact diffusion, discriminators) but executed jointly at inference with K=10 Euler steps.

## Results

On Core4D-S1, the proposed method achieves IDF 0.22, Contact Accuracy 0.44, FID 25.5, and Penetration 0.05, outperforming all baselines on every metric except Diversity (1.15 vs. InterGen's 1.21). On Core4D-S2, the method further improves to IDF 0.20, Contact Accuracy 0.46, FID 21.6, and Penetration 0.06, while matching InterGen on Diversity (1.18). The largest gains are in Contact Accuracy (roughly 2× over the next-best OMOMO) and Penetration (3–4× lower than all baselines), reflecting the contribution of the stability-driven simulation module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three adapted baselines: ComMDM (text-conditioned motion diffusion), OMOMO (object-trajectory-guided single-character HOI extended to dual-human), and InterGen (diffusion-based multi-human interaction). The proposed method dominates all three on every metric except Diversity. Against ComMDM, the gains are the most dramatic: Contact Accuracy improves from 0.11 to 0.44 (S1) and FID drops from 52.5 to 25.5, reflecting ComMDM's lack of explicit object-trajectory conditioning. Against OMOMO, the method roughly doubles Contact Accuracy (0.21→0.44) and reduces Penetration by 3× (0.15→0.05), showing that the stability-driven simulation and affordance-guided contact strategy address OMOMO's heuristic single-to-dual extension. Against InterGen, which already achieves the lowest Penetration among baselines (0.11), the method still halves it (0.05) and nearly doubles Contact Accuracy (0.13→0.44), while InterGen retains a slight edge in Diversity on S1 (1.21 vs. 1.15). Overall, the method wins 4 out of 5 metrics on both splits, with the sole trade-off being a marginally lower Diversity score on S1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) as its generative backbone, extending it from single-character HOI synthesis (OMOMO, NIFTY, NAP) to the dual-human co-manipulation setting. It departs from prior multi-person interaction methods (InterGen, ComMDM) that model social or dance-like coordination without object manipulation, and from RL-based cooperative HOI (CooHOI) that fails to generalize across objects. The key architectural departure is the integration of three orthogonal refinement signals—affordance-guided contact anchors, adversarial interaction priors, and a sampling-based physics simulation (CMA-ES + PD controller in PyBullet)—all injected as gradient corrections into a single deterministic flow, rather than relying on stochastic denoising or learned policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a concrete gap: no prior open-source method generates physically plausible dual-human motions conditioned on an object trajectory, and the paper demonstrates that combining flow matching with in-the-loop physics simulation can reduce penetration by ~3× and nearly double contact accuracy over adapted baselines. However, the 3-minute inference cost for the physics simulation and the restriction to exactly two humans and one rigid object limit immediate practical deployment. Open questions include whether the sampling-based refinement scales to more agents or articulated objects, whether the adversarial priors transfer to unseen interaction types, and whether the flow-matching + simulation pipeline can be made real-time for VR or robotics applications.
