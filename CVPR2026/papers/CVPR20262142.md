## Problem

Lightweight VLA models built on compact VLMs (e.g., SmolVLM-0.5B) suffer from weak spatiotemporal reasoning, leading to poor localization and imprecise manipulation trajectories. Existing approaches to inject 3D/4D information either require heavyweight VLMs to handle cross-modal fusion or introduce dedicated spatial branches that add substantial parameter overhead, defeating the purpose of a lightweight design. The paper addresses how to endow a compact VLA with 4D spatiotemporal understanding at minimal computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262142/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB images (left, right, front), a natural-language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state. Notably, 4D spatiotemporal features are derived entirely from these standard 2D images via a frozen pretrained 4D visual geometry transformer—no depth cameras or LiDAR are required. At inference, only the 2D image branch is retained; the 4D extractor, reconstruction heads, and trajectory head are all dropped, so the deployed model takes the same inputs as a standard lightweight VLA and outputs a continuous action chunk via a conditional diffusion decoder.

## Method

SwiftVLA pairs a ~0.5B SmolVLM backbone with a ~100M-parameter diffusion action expert. A frozen pretrained 4D visual geometry transformer with a FIFO temporal cache incrementally converts streaming multi-view frames into 4D features, providing temporal context without extra sensors. Learnable Fusion Tokens are injected into the VLM to interact with 2D features, 4D features, language, and state embeddings via cross-attention; their outputs are supervised by the robot end-effector's future trajectory to enforce task-relevant cross-modal alignment. A mask-and-reconstruct training strategy randomly drops either the 2D or 4D modality and requires the action expert to reconstruct the masked features alongside predicting diffusion noise, distilling 4D geometric knowledge into the VLM's internal representations. At inference, the 4D branch and all auxiliary heads are removed, leaving only the VLM and action expert (~450M total parameters) while preserving comparable performance to the full 4D-input configuration.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, SwiftVLA (0.45B parameters) achieves an average success rate of 94.7%, surpassing π0 (3B, 94.1%) and GR00T-N1 (3B, 93.9%) while using only ~15% of their parameters. On RoboTwin 2.0, SwiftVLA reaches 0.53 average SR across short/medium/long-horizon tasks, outperforming π0 (0.47) and SmolVLA (0.29). On the NVIDIA Jetson Orin edge device, SwiftVLA runs at 0.167 s inference latency (18× faster than π0's 2.966 s) with 1,398 MB memory (12× less than π0's 16,236 MB), while achieving a higher real-world SR of 0.76 versus π0's 0.48.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SwiftVLA (0.45B) outperforms all same-size and smaller baselines: it beats SmolVLA (87.3%) by 7.4 points, SmolVLA† (92.2%) by 2.5 points, UniAct (77.8%) by 16.9 points, and VLA-OS (85.6%) by 9.1 points on LIBERO average. Against large 3B models, SwiftVLA edges out π0 (94.1%) by 0.6 points and GR00T-N1 (93.9%) by 0.8 points, while also surpassing π0+FAST (85.5%) by 9.2 points. Among spatio-temporal enhanced 4B methods, SwiftVLA exceeds SpatialVLA (78.1%) by 16.6 points and 4D-VLA (88.6%) by 6.1 points, but trails QDepth-VLA (94.9%) by a narrow 0.2 points. Against the strongest 7B–9B models, SwiftVLA falls slightly behind OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), DD-VLA (96.3%), and UniVLA (95.4%), yet remains within 2.4 points of the best while using 15–20× fewer parameters. On RoboTwin 2.0, SwiftVLA (0.53 avg SR) dominates π0 (0.47), GO-1 (0.46), SmolVLA (0.29), and TinyVLA (0.07), and on the Jetson Orin it achieves 18× lower latency and 12× less memory than π0 while delivering a higher real-world SR (0.76 vs. 0.48).

## Relation to Prior Work

SwiftVLA sits at the intersection of two active research threads: lightweight VLA models (SmolVLA, TinyVLA, MiniVLA) that shrink VLM backbones to enable edge deployment, and 3D/4D-enhanced VLAs (3D-VLA, SpatialVLA, Evo-0, PointVLA, 4D-VLA) that inject geometric or spatiotemporal features to improve spatial reasoning. The paper departs from both lines by using a frozen pretrained Streaming 4D Visual Geometry Transformer [95] as a plug-and-play feature extractor (requiring no extra sensors) and by introducing a mask-and-reconstruct training objective that distills 4D knowledge into the VLM so the 4D branch can be entirely removed at inference. This contrasts with prior fusion approaches that either require large VLMs to handle cross-modal alignment or add a dedicated spatial branch with substantial parameter overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 0.45B-parameter model matching or exceeding 3B-parameter baselines (π0, GO-1) on both simulation and real-robot benchmarks while running 18× faster and using 12× less memory on edge hardware addresses a critical bottleneck for real-time robotic deployment. The mask-and-reconstruct strategy raises an interesting open question about how much explicit geometric structure can be internalized into learned representations versus how much must remain as an explicit input at test time. Limitations include reliance on multi-view cameras, a frozen 4D transformer that cannot adapt to novel embodiments, and evaluation limited to pick-and-place-style manipulation tasks; whether the distilled 4D knowledge generalizes to highly dynamic or out-of-distribution scenes remains untested.
