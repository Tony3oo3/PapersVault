## Problem

Existing diffusion acceleration methods (caching, Taylor forecasting) apply a single global predictor uniformly to all tokens and timesteps, ignoring the empirical fact that tokens exhibit heterogeneous temporal dynamics—some evolve slowly while others undergo rapid displacement. This one-size-fits-all approach causes error accumulation and severe quality degradation at aggressive acceleration ratios, and prior adaptive schemes rely on hand-tuned thresholds that limit scalability. The paper addresses the open challenge of selecting the optimal predictor per token at each step without additional training or manual calibration.

## Input / Output

TAP operates as a training-free wrapper around a pre-trained diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, or HunyuanVideo) conditioned on a text prompt, producing generated images (evaluated on DrawBench) or 480p videos of 2 seconds (17 frames at 8 FPS, evaluated on VBench with 950 prompts × 5 seeds). The only additional computation beyond the base model is a single first-layer evaluation per N-step window to obtain the modulated input probe and the input-output residual; no extra parameters, finetuning, or auxiliary networks are introduced. The method is compatible with both vanilla and few-step distilled samplers.

## Method

TAP constructs a compact family of 15 Taylor predictors by varying expansion order (0, 1, 2) and prediction horizon (discretized range [k−4, k] with step δ=1), covering diverse token dynamics from abrupt to smooth. At each N-step window boundary, TAP performs one full model evaluation and caches the modulated first-layer input and the global residual; for subsequent N−1 steps, all candidate predictors generate predictions in parallel from these cached tensors. A probe-then-select mechanism scores each predictor per token by computing cosine distance between the predictor's predicted modulated input and the actual modulated input, then assigns each token the predictor with the smallest proxy error—requiring no hand-tuned thresholds since selection is purely relative. The selected per-token residuals are assembled into the full predicted output, and the entire procedure adds only ~0.1 GB memory and ~0.015% FLOPs over a single global-predictor baseline while achieving up to 6.24× speedup with no perceptual quality loss.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), TAP achieves 6.24× acceleration (N=8) with ImageReward 0.99, CLIP Score 31.19, PSNR 16.11, SSIM 0.64, and LPIPS 0.44 — matching or exceeding the unaccelerated 50-step baseline (ImageReward 0.95, CLIP 30.63) while reducing latency from 42.18 s to 8.40 s. On Qwen-Image at 3.57× speedup, TAP attains ImageReward 1.23 and PSNR 20.13, surpassing the 50-step baseline (1.23, 33.74 CLIP). On HunyuanVideo (VBench), TAP at N=6 achieves a 4.98× speedup with a VBench score of 65.46%, only 1.7% below the unaccelerated 66.61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAP consistently outperforms all four baselines on every quality metric at matched or comparable acceleration ratios. Against FORA (direct-reuse caching), TAP gains +0.19 ImageReward and +2.68 dB PSNR at ~6.24× speedup (0.99 vs. 0.80; 16.11 vs. 13.43), showing that simple feature reuse degrades severely at long horizons. Against TeaCache (threshold-based caching), the gap is even larger: TAP scores 0.99 vs. 0.66 ImageReward at ~6× speedup, indicating TeaCache's hand-tuned threshold fails to protect quality under aggressive acceleration. Against TaylorSeer (global Taylor forecast), TAP improves ImageReward by +0.08 (0.99 vs. 0.91) and PSNR by +1.39 dB (16.11 vs. 14.72) at 6.24×, confirming that per-token adaptive selection of Taylor order and horizon beats a single global predictor. Against SpeCa (speculative draft-then-verify), TAP leads by +0.01 ImageReward and +1.34 dB PSNR at ~4.2× speedup. On Qwen-Image, TAP further extends its lead over TaylorSeer by +0.05 ImageReward and +2.11 dB PSNR at 3.57×, and on HunyuanVideo it achieves the highest VBench score (65.46%) among all accelerated methods at 4.98×.

## Relation to Prior Work

TAP extends the forecasting-based feature caching line (TaylorSeer, FreqCa, SpeCa) by replacing a single global predictor with a per-token adaptive selection mechanism. It departs from prior caching methods (TeaCache, FORA, DeepCache) that either reuse features directly or apply a fixed prediction policy uniformly across all tokens, and from speculative methods (SpeCa) that rely on hand-tuned thresholds, by introducing a probe-driven scoring stage that uses a single first-layer evaluation to rank a compact family of Taylor predictors per token. The conceptual link to model ensembles is explicit: TAP treats predictor selection as a lightweight ensemble decision made at inference time, but grounded in a physically motivated proxy (first-layer activation error) rather than learned gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAP offers a training-free, drop-in acceleration path for large diffusion transformers that achieves 6×+ speedup with negligible quality loss, which is practically significant for deployment of models like FLUX and HunyuanVideo where inference cost is the primary bottleneck. The threshold-free, probe-driven design avoids the manual calibration burden of prior adaptive methods and generalizes to heterogeneous predictor families, making it a flexible framework rather than a single fixed recipe. Open questions include whether the first-layer probe remains informative for non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net-based models), how the approach behaves under extreme acceleration (N > 10) where all predictors may fail, and whether the predictor family can be further diversified (e.g., with learned or frequency-aware predictors) to push the accuracy-efficiency frontier further.
