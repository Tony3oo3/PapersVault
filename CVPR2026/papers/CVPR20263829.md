## Problem

In dynamic urban driving scenes, 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from incomplete geometry initialization due to sparse multi-camera overlap (<15%), LiDAR blind spots, and moving objects, causing photometric gradients to be erroneously attributed to occluded or invisible Gaussians. Existing densification strategies (splitting/cloning) can only refine already-initialized primitives and cannot reconstruct geometry in regions with no initial points, leading to distortions and floater artifacts that degrade generalization to unseen viewpoints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-camera RGB images, fused LiDAR point clouds, SfM point clouds, calibrated camera poses, and instance segmentation masks (from SAM) as input. It outputs a densified set of 3D Gaussian primitives that support real-time novel view rendering, along with per-pixel depth and surface normal maps. A key operational detail is that MVS reconstruction draws on cross-camera, cross-timestamp views (not just adjacent frames from one camera), and the voxel visibility reasoning requires z-buffered rasterization of voxel distances to produce dense depth supervision that constrains errors within voxel resolution.

## Method

VAD-GS introduces a three-stage visibility-aware densification pipeline. First, it voxelizes the initialized point cloud and rasterizes visible voxel surfaces via z-buffering to produce dense depth maps and pixel-to-voxel index mappings; by comparing Gaussian-rendered depth against voxel-derived depth per instance, it flags regions with missing or distorted geometry. Second, a diversity-aware scoring function (balancing voxel proximity, lateral translation, longitudinal displacement, and orientation difference) selects top-k supporting views per reference view via maximum-weight k-clique optimization, ensuring strong stereo constraints across different cameras and timestamps. Third, a patch-matching MVS algorithm with plane hypotheses reconstructs reliable 3D points from the selected views, extended to dynamic objects by transforming observations into the object's local frame and using rasterized visible voxels as occlusion-aware prompts for instance segmentation. Newly reconstructed points initialize additional Gaussian primitives, and the full model is optimized with a composite loss combining photometric, normal, and depth (hard/soft) terms.

## Results

On the Waymo Open dataset, VAD-GS achieves PSNR 35.59, PSNR* (dynamic objects only) 31.31, SSIM 0.950, and LPIPS 0.047, outperforming the strongest baseline StreetGaussians (PSNR 34.61, PSNR* 30.23, SSIM 0.938, LPIPS 0.079) by approximately 1.0 dB in PSNR, 1.08 dB in PSNR*, 0.012 in SSIM, and 0.032 in LPIPS. On the sparser nuScenes dataset, VAD-GS consistently improves SSIM by over 0.06 and PSNR by more than 0.78 dB across most scenes compared to StreetGaussians, with the exception of Scene 05 where the ego vehicle's linear trajectory limits cross-camera overlap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VAD-GS outperforms all seven baselines on every metric (PSNR, PSNR*, SSIM, LPIPS) on the Waymo Open dataset. The closest competitor, StreetGaussians [7], is surpassed by ~1.0 dB PSNR and ~1.08 dB PSNR* (dynamic objects), while older methods like 3D GS [18] and NSG [40] trail by 5–7 dB in PSNR. On nuScenes, VAD-GS achieves the best SSIM in all six evaluated scenes (e.g., 0.81 vs. 0.65 for StreetGS on Scene 00) and the highest PSNR in five of six scenes, with Scene 05 being the sole exception where the linear ego trajectory limits multi-view overlap. The paper notes that LPIPS is not always the lowest on nuScenes because MVS-based reconstruction assumes object rigidity, which degrades for non-rigid objects like pedestrians. The authors also note that Waymo comparisons are somewhat conservative since baselines were originally reported using only the forward-facing camera, restricting VAD-GS's cross-camera advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

VAD-GS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for dynamic urban scenes (StreetGaussians, DrivingGaussian, OmniRe) by replacing passive, photometric-error-driven densification (splitting/cloning existing Gaussians) with active, MVS-guided geometry recovery. It builds on GaussianPro's patch-matching-based geometry completion but generalizes it from static, single-camera, adjacent-frame settings to dynamic, multi-camera, cross-timestamp driving scenarios. The voxel-based visibility reasoning with z-buffer rasterization is a novel mechanism for occlusion-aware geometry identification that departs from prior depth-normalization approaches (DNGaussian, GeoTexDensifier) which are confined to regions with existing primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3DGS-based autonomous driving simulation: sparse LiDAR/SfM initialization leaves blind spots that corrupt gradient-based optimization, degrading generalization to unseen viewpoints. The visibility-aware densification strategy is a reusable component that could benefit other 3DGS pipelines beyond driving. However, the rigidity constraint imposed to relax static MVS assumptions means non-rigid objects (pedestrians, cyclists) fall back to standard gradient-based densification, leaving a clear gap for future work on deformable geometry recovery in dynamic scenes.
