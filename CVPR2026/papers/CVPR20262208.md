## Problem

Latent Diffusion Models denoise all latent components synchronously at the same noise level, forcing the diffusion backbone to simultaneously capture high-level semantics and low-level textures. This conflicts with the inherent coarse-to-fine nature of diffusion, where structural information naturally forms before fine-grained details, and leads to slow convergence. Recent semantic-enhanced methods (REPA, ReDi, REG) embed discriminative features into the latent space but still denoise them in lockstep with textures, neglecting the natural temporal ordering that could accelerate and improve generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and random Gaussian noise as input and generates a 256×256 RGB image. Internally, the diffusion operates on a 48-channel composite latent (16 semantic channels from SemVAE + 32 texture channels from SD-VAE) with 256 spatial tokens per image. At inference, the fully denoised semantic latent is discarded and the final image is decoded solely from the texture latent via the SD-VAE decoder, so the semantic pathway acts purely as a generation guide rather than a reconstruction target.

## Method

SFD introduces a dedicated Semantic VAE (SemVAE, 29M params) that compresses frozen DINOv2-B patch features into 16-channel compact latents via a Transformer encoder-decoder with KL regularization, preserving spatial layout while reducing dimensionality. These semantic latents are channel-concatenated with SD-VAE texture latents to form a 48-channel composite latent. The key mechanism is asynchronous denoising with a fixed temporal offset Δt=0.3: during training, the semantic timestep t_s is sampled from U(0, 1+Δt) and the texture timestep t_z = max(0, t_s − Δt), ensuring semantics always experience less noise than textures. A dual-timestep DiT backbone receives both noisy latents and their respective timesteps, jointly predicting velocity fields for each component. Inference follows a three-phase schedule—semantic-only initialization, joint asynchronous generation, then texture-only refinement—controlled by binary masks, with the total number of diffusion steps unchanged. Training combines velocity prediction losses for both latents (weighted by β=2.0) with a REPA alignment loss that reconstructs clean VFM features from the diffusion hidden states.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with guidance, SFD achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.04 (LightningDiT-XXL, 1.0B params) and 1.06 (LightningDiT-XL, 675M params) at 800 epochs, with sFID of 3.75 and 3.89 respectively. Remarkably, at only 80 epochs SFD already reaches FID 1.19 (XXL) and 1.30 (XL), surpassing most competing methods trained for 800 epochs. Without guidance, SFD achieves FID 3.53 at 400K iterations, outperforming DiT-XL/2 at 7M iterations (FID 9.62) and LightningDiT-XL/1 at 4M iterations (FID 6.50), corresponding to 100× and 33.3× faster convergence respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFD(XXL) at 800 epochs achieves the best FID (1.04) and sFID (3.75) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest competitors REPA-E (FID 1.12, sFID 4.09) and RAE DiTDH-XL (FID 1.13) by 0.08 and 0.09 FID points respectively. SFD also surpasses all autoregressive baselines (VAR FID 1.80, MAR 1.55, xAR 1.24) and standard latent diffusion models (DiT-XL 2.27, SiT-XL 2.06, DDT 1.26) by substantial margins. In terms of training efficiency, SFD at just 80 epochs (FID 1.19–1.30) already beats REPA-E (1.12), DDT (1.26), and xAR (1.24) trained for 800 epochs, and approaches the 800-epoch performance of most baselines. The main trade-off is Inception Score: SFD reports IS 264–267, lower than MDTv2 (314.7), DDT (310.6), and REPA (305.7), though its Precision (0.78) and Recall (0.66–0.67) remain competitive. The paper also demonstrates that the semantic-first mechanism generalizes to ReDi, improving its FID from 5.33 to 4.41 without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic-enhanced latent diffusion line (REPA, ReDi, REG, VA-VAE) by introducing a temporal dimension to the denoising process: rather than denoising semantic and texture latents synchronously at the same noise level, SFD assigns them distinct timesteps with a fixed offset Δt so that semantics are always cleaner than textures. It builds directly on LightningDiT's SD-VAE as the texture encoder and incorporates REPA's alignment loss, while drawing conceptual inspiration from asynchronous denoising methods like Diffusion Forcing and AsynDM, which apply per-token or per-pixel noise schedules. The key departure is moving from 'what to denoise' (adding semantic features to the latent) to 'when to denoise' (ordering the formation of semantics before textures), formalized through a dedicated Semantic VAE and a three-phase inference schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100× and 33.3× convergence speedups over DiT and LightningDiT respectively are practically transformative for training budgets, and the technique is simple to implement (a timestep offset plus a small SemVAE). The finding that a moderate offset (Δt=0.3) outperforms both synchronous (Δt=0) and fully sequential (Δt=1.0) generation validates the asynchronous design and rules out the simpler teacher-forcing alternative. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet to text-to-image, video, or multi-modal generation, and whether the optimal Δt is dataset- or architecture-dependent. The preservation of SD-VAE reconstruction fidelity (unlike RAE or VA-VAE) also positions SFD as more suitable for downstream editing and consistency-sensitive tasks.
