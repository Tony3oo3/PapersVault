## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary scene graph generation methods rely on off-the-shelf VLMs to map visual concepts to labels, lacking discriminative attribute extraction for novel objects and relationships. This leads to misclassification of novel categories as similar base classes, and the independent assignment of object and relationship labels without semantic interaction limits image-text alignment accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261563/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a class prompt (a concatenated list of candidate object and relationship category labels, with negative words randomly sampled during training to cap vocabulary size at N) as input. It outputs a scene graph consisting of K object nodes (each with a bounding box and category label) and pairwise relationship edges (each with a category label), where K=100 object queries are used per image. Open-vocabulary classification is performed by matching node/edge features against CLIP word embeddings in a shared semantic space, with object assignment solved via bipartite graph matching.

## Method

MoE-FD introduces a Mixture-of-Experts feature decoupling module where 8 object experts and 6 relationship experts (each a 3-layer MLP) specialize in distinct visual-semantic subspaces (e.g., shape, texture, spatial layout). A routing network computes expert weights via softmax over concatenated node/edge features, ConceptNet semantic priors (2-hop subgraph encoded to 1024-d vectors), and a relation-aware query, enabling adaptive selection of the most relevant experts per input. A second component, Iterative Feature Refinement, performs C=4 rounds of bidirectional cross-attention: edges are updated by aggregating pairwise node interactions, then nodes are refined by attending over adjacent edge features, progressively strengthening triple-level semantic associations. The full pipeline uses a frozen Grounding DINO backbone (Swin-T/B + BERT) for feature extraction, a DETR-style context extractor with object and relation queries, and Focal Loss for open-vocabulary classification, with weakly supervised pre-training on COCO captions to improve novel category perception.

## Results

On the most challenging OvD+R-SGG setting (both novel objects and relations) with Swin-B backbone, MoE-FD achieves R@20 of 17.35 (Joint Base+Novel), 16.99 (Novel Obj.), and 16.61 (Novel Rel.), outperforming the strongest baseline ACC by 3.85%, 3.53%, and 4.24% respectively on R@20. With Swin-T, the method reaches R@20 of 14.88 (Joint), 14.88 (Novel Obj.), and 14.49 (Novel Rel.), surpassing ACC by 2.27%, 2.40%, and 3.11%. In the OvD-SGG setting (Swin-B), MoE-FD achieves R@50 of 26.64 (Base+Novel) and 20.94 (Novel), improving over OvSGTR by 5.29% and 0.98% on R@50 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoE-FD consistently outperforms all OVSGG baselines across all three evaluation settings (OvD, OvR, OvD+R) and both backbone sizes. Against the closest competitor ACC [16], MoE-FD gains 3.85–4.24% on R@20 in the OvD+R-SGG setting (Swin-B), with the largest margin on Novel Rel. R@20 (16.61 vs. 12.37). Compared to OvSGTR [3], the improvement is even more pronounced: 4.98% on Joint R@20 and 6.05% on Novel Rel. R@20 (Swin-B, OvD+R). In the OvD-SGG setting (Swin-B), MoE-FD improves R@50 by 5.29% over OvSGTR on Base+Novel objects (26.64 vs. 21.35). Closed-set baselines (IMP, MOTIFS, VCTREE, TDE) score near zero on novel categories, confirming that open-vocabulary methods are necessary for generalization. The pre-training comparison (Table 4) further shows MoE-FD outperforming ACC by 0.88% on R@20 (Swin-B) when used as a pre-trained model for downstream OVSGG.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on OvSGTR (Chen et al., ECCV 2024), which established the fully open-vocabulary SGG formulation using VLM-based visual-semantic alignment and weakly supervised pre-training. The key departure is the introduction of MoE-based feature decoupling: rather than treating VLM-extracted features as monolithic representations (as in VS3, ACC, and RAHP), MoE-FD routes object and relationship features through specialized expert subnetworks guided by ConceptNet semantic priors, enabling adaptive extraction of discriminative attributes without explicit attribute annotations. The iterative cross-attention module further distinguishes this work by modeling bidirectional object-relationship interactions, addressing the limitation in prior OVSGG methods that independently assign labels to objects and relations in the semantic space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode in OVSGG—confusion between visually similar novel categories (e.g., 'cup' vs. 'bowl')—by showing that adaptive feature decoupling via MoE experts can recover discriminative sub-features without requiring attribute-level supervision. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (VG150) and a fixed number of experts/queries, and the authors themselves note that the fixed architecture may struggle with broader novel category ranges. Open questions include whether the ConceptNet-guided routing generalizes to domains with sparse knowledge-graph coverage, whether the approach scales to MLLM-based OVSGG pipelines, and whether learnable expert selection (e.g., via RL or LLM guidance) can overcome the current fixed-expert limitation.
