## Problem

Existing NVS-based pose estimation methods (e.g., NOPE) treat the viewpoint transformation as an ill-posed problem because the starting orientation of the reference view is undefined, relying solely on pixel-level supervision that provides no explicit geometric constraints. This leads to geometry-distorted novel views and unreliable template matching for unseen objects. The paper identifies that intrinsic object orientation is a degradation-robust geometric cue that, when explicitly leveraged, can define the transformation and resolve this ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image and a query RGB image as input. From the reference image, it estimates probabilistic orientation distributions over three discrete angles (azimuth and in-plane rotation at 360° resolution, elevation at 180° resolution) defined in a canonical coordinate system. It generates 342 novel view templates in latent feature space from a twice-subdivided icosahedron of viewpoints, then selects the template whose orientation-aware similarity score (combining L2 latent distance and KL divergence of orientation distributions) best matches the query image, yielding the 3D rotation pose.

## Method

OrienPose follows a generate-and-compare paradigm with two key innovations. First, an Orientation-Aware Guidance (OAG) module injects orientation distributions (estimated by a retrained Orient-Anything model) into the reference image's latent embedding via cross-attention, providing an explicit geometric initialization for the NVS transformation. Second, an Orientation Consistency Loss (OCL) supervises the NVS network using KL divergence between predicted and ground-truth orientation distributions for each angle, complementing standard L2 pixel loss to enforce geometry-aligned synthesis. The NVS network is a U-Net-like backbone with pose conditioning that formulates view synthesis as probabilistic distribution learning. At inference, the orientation-aware similarity metric combines both appearance (L2 in latent space) and geometric (KL divergence of orientations) cues for template matching, enabling more discriminative pose selection especially for isotropic objects.

## Results

On the ShapeNet test set (10 unseen categories), OrienPose achieves 59.6% ACC@30 and 20.4° median pose error, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art NOPE (52.3% ACC@30, 27.7° median) by 7.3 percentage points and 7.3° respectively. On the real-world NAVI dataset (5 unseen instances), OrienPose attains 50.9% ACC@30 and 35.7° median error, again surpassing NOPE (36.8%, 49.1°) and also beating regression-based PIZZA (46.3%, 37.0°) and RelPose (45.0%, 31.3°). Under heavy image degradation (40% Gaussian blur or occlusion), OrienPose retains 56.0% ACC@30 and 24.9° median (blur) and 53.3% ACC@30 and 29.0° median (occlusion), demonstrating strong robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrienPose decisively outperforms NOPE, the closest G&C competitor, by +7.3% ACC@30 and −7.3° median on ShapeNet, and by +14.1% ACC@30 and −13.4° median on NAVI, confirming that explicit orientation injection and consistency loss substantially improve viewpoint control over pixel-level supervision alone. Against the diffusion-based NVS baseline Free3D, OrienPose gains +19.2% ACC@30 and −36.9° median on ShapeNet, indicating that orientation cues are more effective than multi-view 3D priors for single-image pose. Among regression/matching/probability-based methods (PIZZA, MicKey, RelPose, RelPose++), OrienPose leads by 13–15% ACC@30 and 11–14° median on ShapeNet, though on NAVI RelPose achieves a slightly lower median (31.3° vs. 35.7°) at the cost of 6% lower ACC@30. Multi-view methods OnePose++ and BoxDreamer, even given 48 and 5 views respectively, trail OrienPose by 14% and 31% ACC@30 on ShapeNet, highlighting the advantage of the single-image G&C paradigm with orientation guidance. The only method that edges out OrienPose is GigaPose (61.6% vs. 59.6% ACC@30 on ShapeNet), but GigaPose requires a known CAD model, which OrienPose explicitly avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

OrienPose extends the 'generate-and-compare' paradigm introduced by NOPE (CVPR 2024), which first applied NVS to single-image unseen object pose estimation without CAD models. The key departure is addressing the ill-posed nature of NVS viewpoint transformation: while NOPE and other NVS-based methods rely solely on pixel-level supervision, OrienPose explicitly injects orientation priors (via the Orient-Anything model) into the reference latent embedding through cross-attention and adds a KL-divergence-based orientation consistency loss to provide geometric-level supervision. This positions the work as a principled fix to a recognized limitation in the G&C line, rather than a new paradigm, while also building on the broader model-free pose estimation literature (OnePose++, Gen6D) that eliminated CAD dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit orientation cues serve as a degradation-robust geometric constraint that meaningfully improves NVS quality and downstream pose accuracy, offering a practical recipe for making generative view synthesis more geometry-consistent. However, performance still degrades notably under large viewpoint changes (>45°) and heavy occlusion, leaving open the question of how to incorporate richer 3D geometric priors or multi-view information to handle these challenging regimes. The sim-to-real results on NAVI also reveal that simpler regression-based methods (RelPose, PIZZA) retain surprising robustness to the domain gap, suggesting that the generative approach's advantage may narrow in real-world deployment.
