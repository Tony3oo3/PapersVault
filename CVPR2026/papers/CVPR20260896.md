## Problem

Volumetric super-resolution research overwhelmingly relies on synthetically downsampled LR-HR pairs, creating an over-idealistic training scenario that misrepresents the discrepancies present in physically acquired low-resolution scans. No large-scale dataset with paired, coregistered multi-resolution CT scans existed to study this domain shift, leaving it unclear whether SR models genuinely reconstruct lost microstructure or merely invert a known downsampling operator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260896/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The SR task takes a low-resolution 3D CT volume (scanned at 4× lower resolution than its HR counterpart, with LR patches of 32³ voxels) and produces a high-resolution 3D CT volume. All volumes are stored in OME-Zarr format with multi-resolution pyramids (up to 8× downsampled levels), enabling out-of-core 3D patch sampling. The dataset spans 16 samples (~194 gigavoxels total) across wood, composite, and bone microstructures, with both physically scanned LR volumes and synthetically downsampled versions for each HR scan.

## Method

VoDaSuRe is constructed by scanning 16 biological and non-biological samples at two resolutions (4× gap) using two lab-CT scanners, followed by ITK-Elastix registration, per-slice CDF intensity matching, and OME-Zarr conversion with empirically optimized chunk sizes. The paper benchmarks eight SOTA SR methods (six volumetric: EDDSR, SuperFormer, MFER, mDCSRN, MTVNet, RRDBNet3D; two 2D: RCAN, HAT) trained with L1 loss on both downsampled and registered LR data across CTSpine1K, LiTS, LIDC-IDRI, and VoDaSuRe. The key empirical finding is a domain shift: models trained on downsampled data recover fine structures (PSNR ~25.5 dB at 2×), while those trained on real LR scans produce spatially smoothed predictions (PSNR ~18.25 dB at 2×), confirmed via total variation analysis. Cross-domain experiments show that models trained on downsampled data degrade sharply on real LR inputs, and ablations rule out misregistration as the cause, attributing the gap to intrinsic acquisition differences.

## Results

On medical CT datasets (CTSpine1K, LiTS, LIDC-IDRI) with synthetically downsampled LR inputs, the best method (RRDBNet3D) achieves PSNR of 35.57 dB at 4× and 39.88 dB at 2×, with SSIM ≥ 0.93. On VoDaSuRe (downsampled), performance drops sharply to 19.08 dB PSNR at 4×, and on VoDaSuRe (registered, real LR scans) it drops further to 16.24 dB PSNR at 4× and 18.25 dB at 2×. Total variation analysis confirms that SR predictions from real LR data lose substantially more high-frequency detail than those from downsampled inputs, revealing a domain shift that is not reproducible via misregistration alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRDBNet3D consistently achieves the best PSNR across all five dataset/scale combinations at 4×, leading by 0.4–1.2 dB over the next-best methods (mDCSRN, MTVNet) on medical datasets and by ~0.3–0.5 dB on VoDaSuRe. On the medical CT datasets (CTSpine1K, LiTS, LIDC-IDRI), all eight methods perform comparably well (PSNR 29.5–35.6 dB at 4×), with 3D volumetric methods (RRDBNet3D, mDCSRN, MTVNet) outperforming 2D methods (HAT, RCAN) by 3–5 dB. The critical finding is not which method wins but the dramatic domain shift: on VoDaSuRe (registered), every method's PSNR drops by 2.5–4 dB relative to VoDaSuRe (downsampled), and SSIM collapses from ~0.65–0.68 to ~0.35–0.44, indicating that no current SR architecture can faithfully reconstruct microstructure from real LR scans. Cross-domain experiments (Table 3) confirm that models trained on downsampled data lose 1.5–2 dB PSNR when tested on registered data, while the reverse (training on registered, testing on downsampled) is not explored, underscoring that the domain gap is asymmetric and intrinsic to the LR acquisition process.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in volumetric SR research, where low-resolution inputs are generated by downsampling high-resolution scans (Gaussian smoothing + interpolation, k-space truncation, etc.), and instead introduces physically acquired paired LR-HR scans to expose a domain shift that synthetic degradation cannot capture. It builds on the OME-Zarr storage format and ITK-Elastix registration pipeline for data curation, and benchmarks eight established SR architectures (EDDSR, SuperFormer, MFER, mDCSRN, MTVNet, RRDBNet3D, HAT, RCAN) that were previously evaluated almost exclusively on downsampled medical datasets (Kirby 21, IXI, BraTS, HCP). Unlike prior paired multi-resolution datasets (Kodama et al., Klos et al., Karamov et al., FACTS), which are small-scale, narrow in domain, or restricted in access, VoDaSuRe spans wood, composites, and bone at ~194 gigavoxels, positioning itself as the first large-scale, publicly available benchmark for realistic volumetric SR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that all eight state-of-the-art SR models produce spatially averaged, structurally smoothed predictions when trained on real LR data, in stark contrast to the sharp reconstructions obtained with downsampled data—challenges the field's implicit assumption that SR models are genuinely recovering lost microstructure rather than inverting a known degradation. This has direct implications for how SR methods are evaluated and for their applicability to real acquisition pipelines (photon-counting CT, cone-beam CT, micro-CT). The paper does not propose a solution to the domain gap, leaving open the question of whether architectural changes, more sophisticated degradation-aware losses, or larger paired datasets can close it. A limitation is that the dataset is confined to X-ray CT of biological and composite materials, so direct transferability to MRI or clinical CT remains untested.
