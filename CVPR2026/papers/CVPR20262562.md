## Problem

Temporal action detection in long untrimmed videos (up to ~40 min) requires simultaneously capturing fine-grained short actions, long sustained activities, and densely overlapping concurrent actions. Existing CNN and Transformer approaches either lack sufficient receptive fields or incur quadratic complexity, while prior SSM/Mamba methods operate at a single temporal scale and have not been applied to densely labeled TAD in untrimmed ADL videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262562/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an untrimmed video segmented into T contiguous non-overlapping windows of 8 or 16 frames, each encoded by a frozen visual backbone (I3D or CLIP-L/14) into a feature vector, yielding an input tensor of shape B×T×D (T=2500 for TSU, T=256 for Charades). It produces per-segment multi-label binary action predictions (one logit per action class per temporal segment) for TAD, or per-frame importance scores via a linear regression head for video summarization.

## Method

MS-Temba introduces dilated SSMs (Temba blocks) that partition the temporal sequence into η disjoint subsequences sampled at stride η, process each subsequence with an independently parameterized SSM branch, and reassemble outputs via an invertible mapping. Three stacked blocks with progressively increasing dilation (η=1,2,3) and feature expansion (γ=1.5) build a hierarchy from fine-grained to long-range temporal representations. A pairwise cosine-similarity consistency loss aligns the output projection matrices across dilated SSM branches within each block, while scale-aware auxiliary BCE losses supervise each block's intermediate predictions. A lightweight Multi-scale Mamba Fuser (MS-Fuser) aggregates the K block outputs via element-wise summation after linear projection to a common dimension, followed by an additional SSM for cross-scale temporal integration, yielding the final fused representation for the classification head. The entire model uses only 17M parameters.

## Results

On the TSU dataset (avg. 21-min untrimmed ADL videos), MS-Temba with a CLIP backbone achieves 44.0 mAP, outperforming the previous best AAN (41.3 mAP) and MS-TCT (40.6 mAP) while using only 17M parameters versus 87M for MS-TCT. On Charades, MS-Temba (CLIP) reaches 33.6 mAP, surpassing AAN (32.0) and MS-TCT (31.9). With an I3D backbone, MS-Temba (17M params) achieves 36.1 mAP on TSU, beating DualDETR (34.8, 21M params) and matching MS-TCT (33.7, 87M params) with 80% fewer parameters. On video summarization, MS-Temba sets new state-of-the-art on both SumMe (τ=0.254, ρ=0.284) and TVSum (τ=0.221, ρ=0.289), exceeding LLMVS (τ=0.253/0.211, ρ=0.282/0.275).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against I3D-based baselines, MS-Temba (17M params) achieves 36.1 mAP on TSU, surpassing DualDETR (34.8, 21M) by +1.3 and MS-TCT (33.7, 87M) by +2.4, while matching MS-TCT's Charades score (25.4) with 80% fewer parameters. Against CLIP-based baselines, MS-Temba (17M) leads AAN (41.3/32.0) by +2.7/+1.6 mAP on TSU/Charades and MS-TCT (40.6/31.9, 87M) by +3.4/+1.7 mAP, demonstrating that the dilated SSM architecture extracts more discriminative temporal structure from the same foundation-model features. On video summarization, MS-Temba edges out LLMVS (the strongest prior method) on both SumMe (τ 0.254 vs 0.253, ρ 0.284 vs 0.282) and TVSum (τ 0.221 vs 0.211, ρ 0.289 vs 0.275), confirming generalizability beyond TAD. The Action-Conditioned metrics (Table 2) further show MS-Temba outperforming MS-TCT at τ=0 (F1_AC 25.90 vs 23.64 with CLIP), validating superior handling of densely overlapping actions. Overall, MS-Temba wins or ties on every reported benchmark while consistently using the fewest parameters among competitive methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS-Temba extends the Mamba/Vision Mamba line of work (selective SSMs with bidirectional scanning) into the temporal action detection setting, where prior SSM-based video methods (VideoMamba, Video Mamba Suite) were limited to short clips with a single global label. The key architectural departure is the introduction of dilated SSM branches within each Temba block, analogous to dilated convolutions in CNNs but operating in the state-space domain, enabling multi-scale temporal receptive fields without the quadratic cost of transformer attention. The MS-Fuser module and the pairwise consistency loss on SSM output projections are novel design choices not present in prior Mamba-for-video work, and the scale-aware auxiliary supervision explicitly encourages each dilation level to specialize, a strategy borrowed from multi-scale CNN/Transformer TAD architectures (MS-TCT, PDAN) but transplanted into the SSM framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× parameter reduction (17M vs. 87M for MS-TCT) while matching or exceeding SOTA on densely labeled ADL benchmarks makes long-video action detection substantially more accessible in resource-constrained deployment settings (smart homes, patient monitoring). The demonstrated generalization to video summarization suggests the dilated-SSM multi-scale representation is a broadly useful temporal encoding, not merely a TAD-specific trick. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer videos (hours) without the fixed temporal dimension, whether the consistency loss's modest standalone contribution indicates it is primarily a regularizer rather than a core mechanism, and whether the dilated SSM design transfers to other sequential video tasks beyond detection and summarization.
