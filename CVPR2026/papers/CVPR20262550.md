## Problem

VGGT's frame-global self-attention over concatenated tokens from all frames incurs quadratic complexity, causing out-of-memory failures at ~500 images and ~20-minute inference for 1000 images on an H20 GPU. Existing acceleration strategies—sequential streaming, cross-scene quantization calibration, and generic token merging designed for LLMs or diffusion models—either break single-pass end-to-end inference or ignore the tight geometric coupling between VGGT tokens and their corresponding image patches and 3D points, leading to loss of critical reconstruction detail.

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary-length sequence of RGB images (evaluated up to 1000 frames at 392×518 resolution) and produces camera parameters, per-pixel depth maps, dense 3D point clouds, and tracking features in a single forward pass. A key design constraint is that token merging reduces sequence length only during the global attention block; before the task-specific prediction heads, tokens are unmerged (replicated) back to the original per-patch count so that dense outputs retain full spatial resolution without architectural changes to the heads.

## Method

LiteVGGT inserts a geometry-aware cached token merging module on both sides of VGGT's global attention. A geometry-aware feature map fuses Sobel pixel gradients (edge/texture cues) with locally pooled token variance (semantic variability) to score each token's geometric importance; the top 10% are retained as GA tokens, spatially balanced low-score tokens become dst anchors, and the remainder are src tokens merged into the most cosine-similar dst token via feature averaging. To eliminate redundant computation, merge indices are computed once every 6 layers and cached for intervening layers, exploiting the observed stability of inter-layer token similarity. After global attention, merged tokens are replicated back to original length before frame attention and prediction heads. The strategy is complemented by fine-tuning the aggregator and heads on a multi-dataset mix (CO3Dv2, BlendMVS, DL3DV) and FP8 quantization via NVIDIA Transformer Engine, yielding up to 10× speedup and ~22% memory reduction while matching or exceeding VGGT's reconstruction accuracy.

## Results

On ScanNet-50 with 1000 input images, LiteVGGT achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.428 in 127.2 s, delivering a ~10× speedup over VGGT* (CD 0.485, 1275.1 s) while also improving reconstruction accuracy. At 96 images, LiteVGGT reaches CD 0.329 in 3.5 s versus VGGT*'s 0.418 in 16.7 s. On 7Scenes, LiteVGGT matches VGGT's accuracy (Acc 0.020 vs 0.021) while running ~7× faster (19.2 s vs 133.1 s). On Tanks & Temples, LiteVGGT outperforms FastVGGT in average F1 (0.57 vs 0.54 at ×10 threshold) and is ~7.5× faster (29.5 s vs 66.2 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT and its optimized variant VGGT*, LiteVGGT achieves a ~10× speedup (127.2 s vs 1275.1 s on 1000 images) while simultaneously improving CD from 0.485 to 0.428, and it handles 1000 images where vanilla VGGT OOMs. Compared to FastVGGT (the closest competitor using generic token merging), LiteVGGT is ~2× faster (127.2 s vs 258.3 s) and achieves lower CD (0.428 vs 0.436) at 1000 images, with the gap widening at shorter sequences (0.329 vs 0.409 at 96 images). Against Fast3R, LiteVGGT is 4.4× faster (127.2 s vs 563.7 s) with substantially better CD (0.428 vs 0.692). CUT3R is marginally faster (112.2 s) but has far worse CD (0.780 vs 0.428). On camera pose estimation (CO3Dv2), LiteVGGT (AUC@30 = 83.2) is slightly below VGGT (86.3) and on par with FastVGGT (83.4); on Tanks & Temples pose (AUC@30), LiteVGGT (88.0) is close to VGGT* (89.2) and clearly beats FastVGGT (87.9). On DTU object-level reconstruction, LiteVGGT (Overall 0.716) sits between VGGT (0.534) and FastVGGT (0.739), indicating a small accuracy trade-off for the speed gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteVGGT extends VGGT, a 1.2B-parameter feedforward 3D foundation model that jointly predicts camera parameters, depth, and point clouds in a single forward pass. It departs from generic token merging strategies (ToMe, FastVGGT) designed for LLMs, diffusion models, or VLMs by introducing a geometry-aware partitioning that exploits the one-to-one correspondence between VGGT tokens and image patches/3D points. The approach also builds on the broader feedforward 3D reconstruction lineage (DUST3R, MASt3R, Fast3R, CUT3R) but specifically targets the quadratic-complexity bottleneck of VGGT's frame-global attention, complementing token merging with cross-layer index caching and FP8 quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling 1000-image scene reconstruction in ~2 minutes (vs. OOM or 20+ minutes for vanilla VGGT), LiteVGGT removes a major practical barrier to deploying 3D foundation models on large-scale scenes for robotics, AR, and autonomous navigation. The observation that edge-rich regions (high pixel gradients) are the backbone of 3D geometric reasoning—validated by successful reconstruction from pure edge maps—offers a transferable principle for designing efficient 3D vision pipelines. Limitations include the need for a 3-day fine-tuning stage on 8 GPUs to recover accuracy, and the method is currently tailored to VGGT's specific architecture; whether the geometry-aware merging generalizes to other 3D foundation models or video inputs remains open.
