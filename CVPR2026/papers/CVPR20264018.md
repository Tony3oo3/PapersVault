## Problem

Current spatial transcriptomics clustering methods learn spatial-view and expression-view embeddings independently, causing the same spot to exhibit divergent representations across views and weakening discriminative power. Additionally, existing approaches rely on GCN encoders that capture only local neighborhood patterns, lacking explicit semantic-level supervision to enforce coherent global cluster structures and prototype-level consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264018/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes spot spatial coordinates (used to build a spatial graph with Euclidean distance threshold r=550) and a normalized gene expression matrix (3,000 highly variable genes retained after SCANPY preprocessing) as input. It produces spatial domain cluster assignments via S learnable prototypes, node-level embeddings in a shared latent space, and a full gene expression reconstruction through a ZINB decoder. The feature graph is constructed via KNN (k=14 for DLPFC, k=15 for HBC and MBA) with cosine similarity edge weights.

## Method

MHAL performs hierarchical alignment at two levels. At the sample level, a positive sample alignment loss (MSE between spatial and expression view embeddings) enforces cross-view consistency for each spot. At the semantic level, S learnable prototypes serve as semantic anchors: node-to-prototype assignments are computed via entropic optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp), a swapped-prediction mechanism enforces cross-view agreement, and Gromov-Wasserstein OT topologically aligns the prototype graph with the data graph (α=0.7). The architecture uses a shared GCN encoder followed by view-specific GCNs, a self-adaptive fusion module (multi-head attention to dynamically weight spatial vs. feature graphs), and a ZINB decoder for gene expression reconstruction. A momentum update mechanism (β₁=0.9, β₂=0.99) stabilizes the prototype graph and marginal distribution during training.

## Results

MHAL is evaluated on 10 spatial transcriptomics datasets (8 DLPFC slices, 1 Human Breast Cancer, 1 Mouse Brain Anterior) using Adjusted Rand Index (ARI). MHAL achieves the best ARI on 9 out of 10 datasets, with headline values of 86.04 on DLPFC slice 151672, 82.45 on 151671, 68.96 on HBC, and 49.30 on MBA. The largest margins are on DLPFC slice 151508 (73.60 vs. next-best 54.73) and 151670 (69.00 vs. next-best 53.95), while the closest competition is on HBC where MHAL edges out StGCL (68.96 vs. 68.00) by 1.42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MHAL outperforms all 11 baselines on 9 of 10 datasets. The strongest competitor is StGCL (heterogeneous graph contrastive learning), which MHAL surpasses by 4.94 ARI on 151507 (78.94 vs. 74.00), 5.52 on 151510 (78.52 vs. 73.00), and 4.04 on 151672 (86.04 vs. 82.00); on HBC the margin narrows to 0.96 ARI (68.96 vs. 68.00). MAFN is the second-strongest baseline on several DLPFC slices (e.g., 82.00 on 151671, 76.00 on 151672) but is consistently outperformed by MHAL by 0.45–17.6 ARI points. On the Mouse Brain Anterior dataset, MHAL achieves 49.30 ARI, beating StGCL (45.00) by 4.30 and Tsctc (44.82) by 4.48. Traditional methods such as SCANPY, SpaGCN, and SCGDL lag substantially, with ARI values typically below 55 across all datasets, highlighting the advantage of the hierarchical contrastive learning framework over simpler GCN or autoencoder-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

MHAL adapts the prototype-based contrastive learning framework from THESAURUS (Deng et al., AAAI 2025)—which uses swapped cross-view prediction with semantic prototypes as clustering anchors—to the spatial transcriptomics domain, and integrates Fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport (Vayer et al., ICML 2019) to topologically align a prototype graph with the data graph. It departs from prior spatial transcriptomics GNN methods (SpaGCN, DeepST, GraphST, StGCL, MAFN) by introducing an explicit sample-level MSE alignment loss between spatial and expression embeddings, addressing a cross-view consistency gap those methods leave unaddressed. The self-adaptive fusion module and ZINB decoder draw on MAFN's multi-view fusion paradigm and standard single-cell generative modeling, respectively, but the hierarchical two-level alignment structure (sample + semantic) is the paper's distinguishing architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and underexplored failure mode in multi-view spatial transcriptomics clustering—divergent embeddings for the same spot across views—and proposes a principled two-level fix, which could inform future multi-modal tissue analysis. However, the ablation revealing that removing the ZINB decoder actually improves average ARI by over 9.9% raises questions about whether domain-specific generative reconstruction is beneficial or merely adds optimization complexity in this setting. Open questions include whether the hierarchical alignment generalizes to larger tissue sections, additional modalities (e.g., histology images), and whether the simple MSE alignment can be replaced by more expressive cross-view consistency objectives without sacrificing the reported gains.
