## Problem

Existing infrared-visible video fusion methods treat frames independently, producing temporal flickering and ghosting artifacts, while diffusion models—despite their strong generative priors for detail restoration—are prohibitively slow for video due to iterative multi-step denoising. The paper tackles the fundamental dilemma of achieving both high per-frame fidelity and cross-frame temporal coherence at a speed suitable for real-time deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261872/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an aligned infrared-visible video pair {I_t, V_t} of T frames at resolution H×W and produces a fused video {Ŷ_t} at the same resolution. Crucially, the streaming design requires only the current frame, the previous frame, and a compact multi-scale memory state at inference time, rather than the entire sequence, enabling frame-by-frame processing with constant memory. A DCEvo-style task-adaptive encoder-decoder replaces the standard visible-domain VAE to avoid distribution shift on fused latents.

## Method

SDMFusion adopts a two-stage design: Stage I trains a cross-modal encoder-decoder on static image pairs, and Stage II freezes this backbone and inserts a streaming diffusion model (SDM) with one-step sampling. The SDM performs a single residual correction in latent space at a fixed diffusion time, replacing the full iterative denoising chain while preserving the frozen U-Net's generative priors. A temporal aggregation adapter, placed after each U-Net decoder layer, uses content-aware cross-attention with a learned sigmoid gate to fuse flow-warped memory features into the current decoder features, selectively absorbing complementary temporal cues. An optical-flow estimator (SpyNet) warps the previous memory state to the current time, and a temporal consistency loss penalizes discrepancies between the current fused frame and the motion-compensated previous frame in valid regions, jointly suppressing flicker and drift.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets (HDO, M3SVD, VTMOT, NOT-156), SDMFusion achieves the best or near-best scores across all 12 metric-dataset combinations (SCD, VIF, mSSIM). Headline numbers include SCD 1.776 / VIF 0.926 / mSSIM 0.955 on M3SVD (best on all three), SCD 1.583 / mSSIM 1.059 on VTMOT (best on both), and VIF 0.937 / mSSIM 1.094 on NOT-156 (best on both). On the downstream object-tracking task (NOT-156, ByteTrack + YOLOv11n), SDMFusion attains the highest AUC (0.3799), SR@0.5 (0.4350), and DP@20 (0.3900), trailing only Mask-Dif on SR@0.75 (0.1700 vs 0.1785). Inference is more than 1.42× faster than the second-best competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest image-level baselines, SDMFusion trades a small SCD deficit for clear gains in VIF and mSSIM: on HDO it trails SAGE (1.655) and DCEvo (1.604) on SCD but leads both on VIF (0.794 vs 0.599/0.765) and mSSIM (1.135 vs 1.065/1.127); on NOT-156 it similarly concedes SCD to CDDFuse (1.614) and SAGE (1.611) while winning VIF (0.937 vs 0.890/0.658) and mSSIM (1.094 vs 1.091/1.010). On M3SVD and VTMOT, SDMFusion achieves the best score on all three metrics, surpassing DCEvo by 0.029 SCD / 0.055 VIF / 0.022 mSSIM on M3SVD and UniVF by 0.025 SCD / 0.022 mSSIM on VTMOT. Against the two dedicated video-fusion baselines, the margin is decisive: UniVF is outperformed on SCD and mSSIM on three of four datasets, and TemCoCo is beaten by a wide margin on every metric (e.g., SCD 1.572 vs 0.618 on HDO, VIF 0.937 vs 0.455 on NOT-156). On the downstream tracking task, SDMFusion leads all nine baselines on AUC, SR@0.5, and DP@20, with only Mask-Dif edging ahead on the stricter SR@0.75 threshold (0.1785 vs 0.1700). Overall, the method dominates on structural and fidelity metrics (VIF, mSSIM) while remaining competitive on spatial contrast (SCD), and it substantially outperforms prior video-specific approaches across the board.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DCEvo image-fusion backbone (Stage I) and repurposes a frozen Stable Diffusion U-Net as a generative prior for video fusion, departing from prior diffusion-based fusion methods (DDFM, Mask-DiFuser) that require expensive multi-step denoising. It extends the streaming/memory-augmented paradigm from video super-resolution (e.g., MIA-VSR, TMP) into the infrared-visible fusion setting, replacing the multi-frame input schemes of UniVF and TemCoCo with a compact optical-flow-warped memory that only requires the current frame plus a prior state. The temporal aggregation adapter with gated cross-attention is a new architectural insertion point that preserves the frozen U-Net's generative capability while injecting cross-frame coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing diffusion-based video fusion to a single forward pass with a lightweight memory update, SDMFusion makes generative-prior fusion practically deployable in latency-sensitive scenarios such as 24/7 surveillance and autonomous driving, where multi-step or multi-frame methods are infeasible. The consistent (if modest) gains over strong baselines like DCEvo and UniVF across four diverse benchmarks, combined with downstream tracking improvements, suggest the streaming-memory design is a reusable pattern for other video restoration tasks. Open questions include how the fixed diffusion timestep and frozen U-Net limit expressiveness on highly dynamic scenes, whether the memory degrades over very long sequences, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two-modality infrared-visible setting.
