## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves photorealistic real-time rendering but requires large numbers of primitives, and existing pruning strategies rely on fixed iterations and uniform refinement intervals that ignore the dynamic nature of reconstruction, often removing important primitives too early or pruning too late for meaningful gains. Additionally, the smooth, positive-only density profile of standard 3D Gaussians limits their ability to capture fine geometric details and sharp edges under compact configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard multi-view RGB images and an SfM-initialized point cloud, identical to vanilla 3DGS. It produces a hybrid compact model of 3D Gaussians and 3D-DoG primitives (up to 90% fewer than the original) that renders novel views at 330+ FPS on a single RTX 3090. Pruning is integrated into the training loop rather than applied as post-processing, and 3D-DoG attributes are activated only after the pruning phase completes, with low-opacity pseudo-Gaussians degenerated back to standard Gaussians to reduce overhead.

## Method

The paper proposes a Reconstruction-aware Pruning Scheduler that uses the L1 reconstruction loss to dynamically determine pruning timing (proceeding only when loss improves by a factor β=0.95) and a geometrically decaying pruning ratio that removes more primitives in early rounds and fewer in later rounds. A Spatio-spectral Pruning Score ranks primitives by combining spatial gradient importance with a frequency-domain spectral term computed via FFT and a radial high-frequency weighting, preventing removal of Gaussians critical for sharp structures. After pruning, all primitives are upgraded to 3D Difference-of-Gaussians, which model both a positive-density lobe and a negative-density ring (parameterized by four additional learnable factors for pseudo-Gaussian opacity and per-axis scales), enabling intrinsic color subtraction in α-blending to recover edges and textures that smooth Gaussians cannot represent under aggressive compression.

## Results

The method achieves up to 90% reduction in Gaussian count while maintaining or exceeding the rendering quality of the full 3DGS model. On Deep Blending, it reaches PSNR 29.87 dB (vs. 29.75 for full 3DGS) with only 59.9M primitives (vs. 592.7M), and on Tanks & Temples it achieves PSNR 23.79 dB (vs. 23.77) with 38.4M primitives (vs. 381.0M). On Mip-NeRF 360, it attains PSNR 27.16 dB with 65.3M primitives, slightly below full 3DGS (27.47 dB) but surpassing all other compressed baselines. Training time is competitive at 13m48s, 10m19s, and 8m9s across the three datasets, with inference at 330+ FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full 3DGS baseline, the proposed method achieves comparable or superior PSNR on Deep Blending (29.87 vs. 29.75 dB) and Tanks & Temples (23.79 vs. 23.77 dB) while using only ~10% of the primitives, and a modest 0.31 dB PSNR drop on Mip-NeRF 360 (27.16 vs. 27.47 dB). Compared to MaskGaussian, the method wins on PSNR across all three datasets (e.g., 27.16 vs. 27.43 on Mip-NeRF 360) while producing a model 4.3× smaller (65.3M vs. 280.7M primitives). Against GaussianSpa (80% pruning), the method achieves higher PSNR on all datasets (27.16 vs. 27.26, 29.87 vs. 29.66, 23.79 vs. 23.67) with a smaller model. Versus the two 90%-pruning baselines, PuP-3DGS and Speedy-Splat, the method consistently outperforms on PSNR (e.g., 27.16 vs. 26.67/26.84 on Mip-NeRF 360; 29.87 vs. 28.85/29.42 on Deep Blending; 23.79 vs. 22.72/23.43 on Tanks & Temples) and also achieves the smallest model size and fastest training time among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS framework (Kerbl et al., 2023) and extends the growing line of Gaussian pruning/compression work (Speedy-Splat, PuP-3DGS, Mini-Splatting, MaskGaussian, GaussianSpa). It departs from prior pruning methods in two key ways: (1) replacing fixed-iteration, fixed-ratio schedules with a reconstruction-aware scheduler that adapts pruning timing and aggressiveness to the current loss landscape, and (2) augmenting the spatial gradient-based importance score (as in Speedy-Splat) with a frequency-domain (FFT-based) spectral term to protect structure-critical primitives. The 3D-DoG primitive is a novel architectural addition that borrows the difference-of-Gaussians concept from classical image processing and adapts it to the α-blending rendering pipeline, introducing a learnable negative-density lobe per primitive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 90% primitive reduction while matching or exceeding the quality of other 90%-pruning baselines (e.g., +0.36 dB PSNR over Speedy-Splat on Tanks & Temples) is practically significant for deploying 3DGS in memory-constrained or large-scale settings. The 3D-DoG primitive, adding only four learnable parameters per Gaussian, offers a lightweight mechanism that other 3DGS variants could adopt to improve edge and texture fidelity under compression. Open questions remain: the method is validated only on static scenes with a single aggressive 90% target, and the authors themselves note that extending to dynamic, large-scale scenes and hardware-efficient rendering is future work. Whether the spectral pruning score generalizes to very different scene types (e.g., highly textured vs. smooth) beyond the three benchmark datasets is not thoroughly explored.
