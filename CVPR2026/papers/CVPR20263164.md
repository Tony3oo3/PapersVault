## Problem

Imitation-learned grasping policies suffer from imprecise grasp execution, limited spatial and object generalization, and poor robustness under visual disturbances. Prior methods that integrate grasp detection priors treat grasp poses merely as weakly-correlated conditional inputs, failing to effectively steer the action generation process, while data-centric approaches like GraspVLA incur prohibitive computational costs and high inference latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB images from wrist and agent cameras, depth maps, and robot proprioceptive states, augmented by two grasp priors from a pre-trained detector: a 6-DoF grasp pose and a graspness map back-projected from point-cloud to pixel space (thresholded at τ to limit noise) and superimposed on the wrist image as a masked visual cue. The output is a fixed-horizon action chunk (default 8 steps) decoded from a compact VAE latent by an asymmetric decoder that concatenates the latent with the selected grasp pose, producing end-effector trajectories executed by the manipulator.

## Method

GraspLDP is a two-stage latent diffusion policy. In the Action Latent Learning stage, a lightweight VAE encoder compresses action chunks into compact latents, and an asymmetric decoder reconstructs actions from the latent concatenated with the target grasp pose, enabling precise pose guidance in a low-dimensional space rather than as a dilute conditional input. In the Diffusion on Latent Action Space stage, a denoising network operates on these compact latents, conditioned on observations that include the graspness-cued wrist image; an auxiliary self-supervised reconstruction loss forces intermediate denoising representations to recover the graspness-cued image, strengthening attention to grasp-relevant regions. At inference, a Heuristic Pose Selector (HPS) filters candidates via collision checking and NMS, then selects the pose minimizing a weighted SE(3) geodesic distance to the current end-effector state, balancing grasp quality with kinematic feasibility for smoother trajectories.

## Results

On the LIBERO simulation benchmark, GraspLDP achieves 80.3% success rate in-domain, 71.1% spatial generalization, 58.2% object generalization, and 64.6% visual generalization, yielding a 68.6% average across all splits. In real-world evaluation on a Franka Research 3, GraspLDP reaches 84.0% on in-domain/spatial generalization and 78.7% average, comparable to the open-loop AnyGrasp detector (79.7%). The method also attains 92.3% scene completion rate in cluttered multi-object scenarios and demonstrates dynamic grasping capability where Diffusion Policy and GraspVLA largely fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraspLDP substantially outperforms vanilla Diffusion Policy across all simulation splits, with the largest gains in object generalization (58.2% vs 11.4%) and visual generalization (64.6% vs 16.3%), confirming that grasp priors are critical for OOD robustness. Against GraspVLA, which relies on 1 billion simulated frames, GraspLDP wins on every simulation metric (e.g., 80.3% vs 50.8% in-domain) while running ~154 ms faster per inference step. In the real-world setting, GraspLDP edges out the open-loop AnyGrasp detector on in-domain/spatial grasping (84.0% vs 82.0%) but trails slightly on object (75.0% vs 78.0%) and visual (77.0% vs 79.0%) generalization, yielding a near-tie in average (78.7% vs 79.7%). OpenVLA, even when fine-tuned on the same 500 demonstrations, remains far behind (57.5% in-domain), highlighting the difficulty of grasping for general-purpose VLA models without task-specific design. In cluttered scenes, GraspLDP matches AnyGrasp's 92.3% scene completion rate while achieving a 12.7% higher per-grasp success rate (82.8% vs 70.1%), despite being trained only on single-object demonstrations.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraspLDP extends the Diffusion Policy framework by adopting the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) to action generation, compressing action chunks into a VAE-encoded latent space where grasp pose guidance is injected. This departs from prior grasp-integrated policies (RoboGrasp, Spatial RoboGrasp, GPA-RAM) that treat grasp poses as simple conditioning inputs, which the authors argue dilutes guidance strength and creates a semantic mismatch with visual observations. The approach also contrasts with the data-centric GraspVLA, which relies on 1B synthetic frames and a large VLA backbone, by instead leveraging a lightweight pre-trained grasp detector for both pose and graspness priors at negligible additional inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured prior knowledge from a grasp detector can be effectively injected into a closed-loop diffusion policy without the prohibitive data or compute costs of billion-scale synthetic training, achieving near-parity with open-loop grasp detectors (AnyGrasp) while retaining the adaptability of closed-loop control. The ablation showing that latent-space guidance substantially outperforms naive conditioning on the same grasp pose (6.8% vs. 19.7% in-domain drop) validates the architectural choice and offers a general recipe for integrating low-semantic priors into generative policies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to highly deformable or fragile objects (acknowledged limitation), how it scales to bimanual or multi-step manipulation, and whether tactile/force signals can be incorporated into the latent diffusion loop.
