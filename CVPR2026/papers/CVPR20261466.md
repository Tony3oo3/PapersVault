## Problem

Reinforcement learning methods for text-to-image generation, particularly GRPO-based approaches, rely on uniform prompt sampling that ignores the match between prompt difficulty and the model's current capability. This results in training batches dominated by prompts with low marginal learning utility—either already mastered or too difficult—reducing sample efficiency and slowing convergence. The paper addresses this by asking how to dynamically identify and prioritize prompts that remain actively learnable as the model's capability evolves during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261466/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes natural language text prompts as input and produces RGB images via a flow-matching generative model (SD3.5-Medium fine-tuned with LoRA, α=64, r=32). During training, each prompt generates a group of G=24 images scored by a reward model, with 10 denoising timesteps used for training and 40 for inference. The sampling mechanism operates at the prompt level using independent Bernoulli trials (Poisson-based paradigm) rather than fixed-size batch selection, with a rejection-sampling loop to fill batches of 48 prompts.

## Method

CGPO integrates an adaptive curriculum into the GRPO training loop by using the variance of group rewards as an online proxy for prompt inconsistency, grounded in the Zone of Proximal Development principle (optimal learning at ~50% success probability). Prompts with high reward variance—indicating partial but unstable mastery—are assigned higher sampling probabilities via linear rescaling within each batch. The probability list is updated each iteration with two safeguards: a self-increment mechanism (adding 1/N) for unsampled prompts to prevent permanent neglect, and a 3-step moving-average smoothing for selected prompts to avoid catastrophic forgetting. Additionally, a category calibration module solves a proportional fairness optimization in closed form (q_i = (1+λv_i)/(c+λ)) to rebalance sampling across reward categories, prioritizing underperforming categories with lower average rewards.

## Results

On GenEval, CGPO achieves an overall score of 0.96, surpassing Flow-GRPO (0.94) and SD3.5-Medium (0.63), with the largest gains in Attribute Binding (0.89 vs. 0.82 for Flow-GRPO) and Position (0.99 vs. 0.98). On T2I-CompBench++, CGPO leads in 6 of 7 sub-tasks, notably improving Texture (0.7521) where Flow-GRPO actually regresses below SD3.5-M. On DPG Bench, CGPO achieves 85.5 overall, marginally ahead of Flow-GRPO (85.4). In terms of training efficiency, CGPO reaches Flow-GRPO's peak performance (0.944) in only 160 GPU hours, roughly 2× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow-GRPO (the direct RL baseline), CGPO wins on all six GenEval sub-tasks, with the most notable margin in Attribute Binding (+0.07) and Counting (+0.01); on T2I-CompBench++ it leads in 6 of 7 sub-tasks (losing only Shape by 0.0065) and on DPG Bench it edges out Flow-GRPO on overall score (85.5 vs. 85.4) while trailing slightly on Global (84.2 vs. 84.8) and Relation (92.9 vs. 93.1). Compared to the base SD3.5-Medium model, CGPO improves the GenEval overall score by 0.33, with dramatic gains in Position (0.99 vs. 0.24) and Attribute (0.89 vs. 0.52). Against closed-source and other open models listed in Table 1 (GPT-4o at 0.84, SANA-1.5 at 0.81, Janus-Pro-7B at 0.80), CGPO's 0.96 overall is the highest reported. The training-efficiency comparison (Figure 3) shows CGPO reaching Flow-GRPO's peak in half the GPU hours, confirming that the adaptive sampling strategy yields both higher final quality and faster convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

CGPO extends the GRPO/Flow-GRPO pipeline for RL-based text-to-image fine-tuning by replacing uniform prompt sampling with an adaptive, variance-driven curriculum. Conceptually, it operationalizes the Zone of Proximal Development principle from Prompt Curriculum Learning (PCL) by using group reward variance as a fully online, label-free proxy for prompt difficulty, avoiding the static difficulty rankings that plague prior curriculum methods. The category calibration component repurposes Kelly's proportional fairness framework from network rate control to rebalance multi-reward training, a cross-domain application that is novel in the T2I context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× training speedup over Flow-GRPO at matched peak performance is a practically significant efficiency gain for RL-based T2I fine-tuning, where GPU-hour costs are substantial. The fully online, annotation-free curriculum design makes the approach broadly applicable to any GRPO-style T2I pipeline without requiring pre-computed difficulty labels. However, the absolute performance gains over Flow-GRPO remain modest (e.g., 0.96 vs. 0.94 on GenEval overall), and the evaluation is limited to a single base model (SD3.5-Medium) trained on GenEval prompts, leaving open whether the variance-based proxy generalizes to noisier reward models, larger datasets, or different T2I architectures such as autoregressive or latent diffusion systems.
