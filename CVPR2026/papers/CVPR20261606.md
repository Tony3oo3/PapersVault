## Problem

Existing IQA transfer methods focus on aligning marginal distributions P(X) or P(Y) between source and target datasets, but often fail because they ignore the conditional distribution P(Y|X), which encodes dataset-specific perceptual preference structures. This paper identifies perceptual preference structure mismatch as a fundamental and underexplored cause of transfer failure, showing that models trained on different datasets rely on systematically different perceptual cues for quality judgment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 random crops during training, 5-patch averaging during testing) and produces a scalar quality score via a regression head on top of a pretrained backbone (ResNet-50 or Swin-B). Unusually, the core selection mechanism operates not on the images themselves but on per-sample feature vectors (globally pooled from all backbone stages) paired with their quality scores, computing a dataset-level correlation profile (PPR) that is then used to guide which source samples to retain.

## Method

PreSTA introduces the Perceptual Preference Representation (PPR), a d-dimensional vector of Pearson correlations between each pretrained feature dimension and quality scores, which quantifies how a dataset weights different perceptual cues. Perceptual Preference Consistency (PPC) is then defined as the cosine similarity between source and target PPRs, serving as a training-free compatibility metric. The selection algorithm greedily eliminates source samples whose removal most increases PPC with the target, using incremental O(d) statistics updates (mean, variance, covariance) to avoid full recomputation at each step. Three stopping criteria—minimum retention ratio (20%), a similarity threshold, and a minimum improvement threshold—balance alignment quality against data diversity. For targeted joint transfer, the same procedure is applied independently to each auxiliary source, and only preference-aligned subsets are combined with the target data for training.

## Results

PreSTA-S achieves substantial SRCC gains in cross-domain transfer using only 20% of source data: KADID-10k→LIVEC improves from 0.589 to 0.744, and KADID-10k→BID from 0.739 to 0.833. In the reverse direction (KonIQ-10k→synthetic), TID2013 SRCC rises from 0.452 to 0.516 and LIVE from 0.812 to 0.849. For targeted joint transfer (PreSTA-J), the method reaches 0.905/0.919 SRCC/PLCC on LIVEC with only 2,793 additional samples, outperforming full same-domain joint training (0.899/0.915) that uses over 21,000 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other named transfer methods; instead, it compares PreSTA against two natural baselines: (1) direct transfer using 100% of source data, and (2) full multi-dataset joint training. In cross-domain transfer, PreSTA-S with only 20% of source data outperforms the full-data baseline by 0.155 SRCC on LIVEC (0.744 vs. 0.589) and 0.094 on BID (0.833 vs. 0.739), demonstrating that preference-aligned subset selection is far more effective than simply using more data. In within-domain transfer, gains are smaller but consistent (e.g., CSIQ: 0.840 vs. 0.773 SRCC at 27.9% data). For targeted joint transfer, PreSTA-J surpasses full same-domain joint training on LIVEC (0.905 vs. 0.899 SRCC) while using only 13% of the additional samples (2,793 vs. 21,198), and also outperforms cross-domain joint training (0.876 SRCC) that actually degrades below the target-only baseline (0.883). The one case where PreSTA-S does not improve SRCC is KonIQ-10k→CSIQ (0.720 vs. 0.723), though PLCC still improves (0.784 vs. 0.750).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant IQA transfer paradigms—domain adaptation methods that align marginal feature distributions P(X) (e.g., FreqAlign, StyleAM, DGQA) and joint training strategies that harmonize label spaces P(Y) (e.g., UNIQUE, StairIQA, Q-Align)—by arguing that the fundamental transfer barrier lies in the conditional distribution P(Y|X), which encodes dataset-specific perceptual preference structures. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture or loss, it repurposes standard pretrained backbones (ResNet-50, Swin-B) as fixed feature extractors and introduces a correlation-based diagnostic (PPR/PPC) to quantify preference alignment, then applies a greedy elimination strategy to select preference-consistent source samples. The conceptual lineage is closest to domain adaptation, but the mechanism (sample-level preference matching via statistical correlation profiles) is distinct from feature-space alignment or label calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20% of preference-aligned source data can outperform 100% of unaligned data in cross-domain IQA transfer (e.g., KADID-10k→LIVEC SRCC 0.589→0.744) offers a practical, data-efficient alternative to the prevailing 'more data is better' assumption in multi-dataset IQA training. The PPR/PPC framework provides a training-free, interpretable diagnostic for source selection that could generalize beyond IQA to other tasks where conditional distribution shift matters. Open questions include whether the linear correlation-based PPR captures higher-order or nonlinear preference interactions, whether the greedy selection scales efficiently to very large source pools, and whether the approach extends to video quality assessment or other perceptual tasks with richer preference structures.
