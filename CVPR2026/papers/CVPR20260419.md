## Problem

Existing deep unfolding networks for compressive sensing perform inference in a single solution space, ignoring the inherent ill-posedness of the underdetermined CS system that mathematically admits multiple valid reconstructions. This single-hypothesis paradigm forces the network to regress one optimal result from an ambiguous solution manifold, degrading reconstruction quality and overlooking inter-solution correlations that could be exploited for better recovery.

## Input / Output

The method takes compressed measurements y = Ax obtained via block-based sampling (32×32 blocks) with a sampling matrix A, and produces a single reconstructed grayscale image. Internally, the network maintains T=16 parallel hypotheses throughout K=10 unfolding stages, which are fused into the final output via a Merg operation. For the CS-MRI extension, the sampling operator becomes Φ = SF (sub-sampling mask times DFT), and the method is evaluated on brain MRI at sampling rates 0.10 and 0.20.

## Method

MHC-DUN unfolds proximal gradient descent over a set of T=16 hypotheses, with each stage comprising a Multi-Hypothesis Collaborative Gradient Descent Module (MHC-GDM) and a Multi-Hypothesis Collaborative Proximal Mapping Module (MHC-PMM). For gradient descent, AlphaNet jointly predicts spatially varying step-size maps for all hypotheses by fusing image-domain and feature-domain inputs through a residual spatial attention mechanism, enabling coordinated dynamic updates. For proximal mapping, Feature Extraction Blocks (FEBs) with dual CNN + Swin Transformer branches enhance features, which are then passed through T independent reconstruction heads to generate diverse hypotheses; a Multi-Hypothesis Collaborative Block (MHCB) with channel and spatial attention fuses these hypotheses by modeling both intra- and inter-hypothesis priors. Training uses a composite loss balancing per-hypothesis measurement fidelity, pairwise cosine-similarity-based diversity regularization, and final reconstruction accuracy, with hyperparameters λ₁=0.50 and λ₂=0.01.

## Results

On Set11, MHC-DUN achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 33.91/0.8951 across sampling ratios 0.01–0.40, surpassing the strongest DUN baseline USB-Net (33.46/0.8887) by 0.45 dB and the best DBN NL-CSNet (31.97/0.8774) by 1.94 dB. On Urban100, the average PSNR/SSIM is 31.90/0.8760, outperforming USB-Net by 1.04 dB and CPP-Net by 1.20 dB. On the Brain CS-MRI dataset, MHC-DUN reaches 38.03/0.9417 average PSNR/SSIM, edging out USB-Net (37.90/0.9412). The paper reports overall gains of 0.4–1.4 dB over state-of-the-art methods depending on dataset and sampling ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MHC-DUN consistently outperforms all 22 baselines across every dataset and sampling ratio tested. Among DUNs, the closest competitors are USB-Net [18] and CPP-Net [17]; on Set11, MHC-DUN exceeds USB-Net by 0.45 dB PSNR (33.91 vs. 33.46) and CPP-Net by 0.53 dB, while on Urban100 the margins widen to 1.04 dB and 1.20 dB respectively. Against the best DBN baselines (NL-CSNet [9] and CSformer [44]), the gains are substantially larger: 1.94 dB and 2.62 dB on Set11, and 1.57 dB and 2.13 dB on Urban100. On the Brain CS-MRI task, MHC-DUN edges out USB-Net by 0.13 dB average PSNR (38.03 vs. 37.90) and surpasses all other MRI baselines including HiTDUN [50] by 0.54 dB. The advantage is most pronounced at low sampling ratios (0.01, 0.10), where the multi-hypothesis strategy provides the greatest benefit from exploring diverse solution candidates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding network (DUN) lineage for compressive sensing—rooted in ISTA-Net, AMP-Net, and more recent feature-passing architectures like OCTUF, CPP-Net, and USB-Net—by introducing a multi-hypothesis optimization paradigm. Rather than unfolding a single-solution PGD solver, MHC-DUN jointly optimizes T=16 candidate reconstructions, motivated by the null-space non-uniqueness of underdetermined CS systems. The AlphaNet module for spatially adaptive step sizes builds on the content-adaptive gradient updating idea in DPC-DUN and DUN-CSNet, while the MHCB's dual-branch CNN + Swin Transformer design follows the local-global fusion trend in recent DUNs. The key conceptual departure is treating the ill-posedness not as a nuisance to be regularized away but as a resource to be exploited through explicit hypothesis diversity and inter-hypothesis correlation modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-hypothesis framing offers a principled way to address CS ill-posedness that goes beyond stronger regularizers, and the 0.4–1.4 dB gains over strong 2024–2025 baselines (USB-Net, CPP-Net) across multiple datasets and sampling ratios validate the approach. However, the method remains block-based (32×32 patches), is evaluated only on grayscale images, and the diversity loss contributes only ~0.03 dB, raising questions about whether the multi-hypothesis mechanism is truly necessary or whether a sufficiently expressive single-solution network could match it. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video CS, whether the optimal number of hypotheses is task-dependent, and whether the block-based constraint limits gains at very low sampling ratios (0.01) where the gap over SOTA narrows.
