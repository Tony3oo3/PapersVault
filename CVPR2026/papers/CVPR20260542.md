## Problem

Offline feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods such as VGGT store scene geometry in a variable-length Key-Value space that must be queried via global softmax attention, resulting in O(n²) compute and memory that quickly becomes infeasible for large image collections (thousands of views). Existing mitigations like sparse attention or token merging reduce constant factors but retain quadratic asymptotic complexity, leaving a fundamental scalability gap for large-scale scene reconstruction from in-the-wild photo collections.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered collection of RGB images (no camera poses or intrinsics required) and produces per-image camera extrinsics (rotation and translation), pinhole intrinsics, dense depth maps, and a global point map. A key property is that after test-time optimization, the scene is encoded in a fixed-size MLP that can be queried with a single novel image to predict its pose relative to the reconstructed scene, enabling feed-forward visual localization without explicit feature matching. The mini-batch formulation of the TTT objective allows processing arbitrarily large collections on a single GPU by offloading batches to host memory, or scaling linearly across multiple GPUs via distributed data parallelism.

## Method

VGG-T³ replaces the quadratic global softmax attention in VGGT with a test-time training (TTT) layer that compresses the variable-length KV mapping into the weights of a fixed-size SwiGLU MLP (the 'fast weights'), reducing per-layer complexity from O(n²) to O(n). At test time, the MLP is optimized for 2 steps per global layer using a dot-product loss on K→V' pairs, where V' is produced by applying a 3×3 ShortConv2D to the Value tokens to inject non-linear spatial context and prevent the trivial linear K→V solution. The model is initialized from pre-trained VGGT weights (with LayerNorm replaced by L2 normalization for fast convergence) and fine-tuned for 100k steps on only the global attention layers (~12% of VGGT's training cost). Because the TTT loss decomposes as a sum over tokens, gradients can be computed on mini-batches independently, enabling single-GPU inference on arbitrarily large collections via CPU offloading and multi-GPU distributed inference via all-to-all gradient synchronization of the small MLP weights.

## Results

On pointmap estimation, VGG-T³ achieves Chamfer Distance of 0.030 on 7scenes-D, 1.654 on DTU, and 0.480 on ETH3D, outperforming the O(n) baseline TTT3R by 2–2.5× on DTU, ETH3D, and NRGBD-D while remaining competitive with O(n²) methods (VGGT: 0.024, 1.537, 0.279). On video depth, VGG-T³ reaches 0.967 δ<1.25 on KITTI (vs. TTT3R's 0.818) and 0.581 on Sintel (vs. TTT3R's 0.510), matching O(n²) baselines. For large-scale reconstruction, VGG-T³ processes 1k images in 58 s (11.6× faster than VGGT's 11 min) and 2k images in 48.5 s with 4 GPUs (33× faster than VGGT). On feed-forward visual localization, VGG-T³ achieves 32.04° rotation error on Wayspots vs. TTT3R's 74.45°, a 2.3× improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT (O(n²)), VGG-T³ trades a small accuracy gap (e.g., CD 0.030 vs. 0.024 on 7scenes-D; 0.480 vs. 0.279 on ETH3D) for an 11.6× speedup at 1k images and 33× at 2k images with 4 GPUs; on DTU the gap narrows to 1.654 vs. 1.537. Against SparseVGGT and FastVGGT (also O(n²)), VGG-T³ is consistently faster while matching or exceeding their accuracy on DTU (1.654 vs. 1.541/1.683) and ETH3D (0.480 vs. 0.327/0.594). Against TTT3R (O(n), autoregressive), VGG-T³ wins decisively on pointmap (2–2.5× lower CD on DTU, ETH3D, NRGBD-D), video depth (0.967 vs. 0.818 δ<1.25 on KITTI; 0.581 vs. 0.510 on Sintel), and visual localization (32.04° vs. 74.45° rotation error on Wayspots). The one area where VGG-T³ underperforms is camera pose estimation (ATE 0.070 on ScanNet vs. VGGT's 0.035), which the authors attribute to VGGT's heterogeneous camera-token design being difficult for the MLP to memorize; however, VGG-T³ still beats TTT3R on ordered inputs and uniquely supports unordered image collections where TTT3R degrades severely (RPE_r 3.942 vs. 0.878 on ScanNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT's multi-view feed-forward architecture and the test-time training (TTT) framework of Sun et al., repurposing TTT's fast-weight optimization to compress VGGT's variable-length KV scene representation into a fixed-size MLP, thereby converting quadratic global attention into a linear-time operation. The linearization strategy (converting a pre-trained softmax-attention model to a linear one) follows the LLM post-training linearization literature, while the ShortConv2D spatial mixing in the Value space draws from sequence-mixing layers in linear language models (Hyena, Gated Delta Networks). Conceptually, the approach parallels DeepSDF's fixed-size decoder with a small instance-specific latent code, but applied to multi-view 3D geometry. Unlike the concurrent TTT3R, which is autoregressive and requires ordered input, VGG-T³ is global and offline, handling unordered image collections with full bidirectional context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reconstructing 1k–2k image collections in under a minute on a single GPU (vs. 11+ minutes or OOM for softmax-attention baselines) makes feed-forward 3D reconstruction viable for large-scale in-the-wild photo collections, a regime previously dominated by classical SfM pipelines. The emergent feed-forward visual localization capability—querying the frozen MLP with a novel image—unifies mapping and localization in a single model, a direction the authors flag as promising but still far from explicit-mapping baselines. Key open questions include closing the remaining accuracy gap versus softmax attention (especially in wide-baseline settings), improving camera pose estimation where the TTT MLP struggles with heterogeneous camera tokens, and whether the fixed-size MLP can be made more expressive without sacrificing linearity.
