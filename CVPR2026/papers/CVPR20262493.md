## Problem

Diffusion Transformer inference is expensive due to dozens of sequential forward passes, and existing forecasting-based feature caching methods (TaylorSeer, FoCa) rely on hand-crafted formulas whose coefficients are fixed a priori by the expansion order and step size. The paper shows these formulas all reduce to the same fixed linear combination of historical features, making them suboptimal and fragile under aggressive skipping, and asks whether data-driven learnable coefficients can close the gap to the theoretical optimum of linear prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262493/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play predictor that operates on the final-layer hidden features of a pre-trained DiT. During training, it consumes 50 images' full 50-step feature trajectories (each a sequence of 50 feature vectors) to fit a 49×49 weight matrix. During inference, given a text prompt, it runs the DiT only at anchor timesteps and uses the learned linear predictor to approximate features at skipped timesteps, ultimately producing a generated image at the same resolution as the base model (e.g., 1024×1024 for FLUX.1-dev).

## Method

L2P replaces the fixed, formula-derived coefficients of TaylorSeer/FoCa with a learnable 49×49 matrix W, where row t holds the linear weights for predicting the feature at step t from all preceding features. The key theoretical contribution is a unified analysis showing that Taylor expansion, BDF2, and related forecasting schemes all collapse to the same fixed linear combination form (Eq. 5–6), and an empirical projection-fidelity study confirming that >90% of timesteps have <5% relative residual when projecting onto the span of past features. Training minimizes MSE between predicted and true final-layer features over 50 cached trajectories, converging in ~20 seconds on a single A100 with an initialization equivalent to naive caching (W_{t,t-1}=1). At inference, the predictor is applied at every skipped step to approximate the DiT output, bypassing the expensive transformer forward pass while preserving generation quality up to 7× FLOPs reduction.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, L²P achieves a 4.55× FLOPs reduction and 4.15× latency speedup at N=5 with a PSNR of 31.459, SSIM of 0.8028, and LPIPS of 0.2147, substantially outperforming TaylorSeer (PSNR 29.328) and FoCa (PSNR 29.413) at the same acceleration level. Under extreme acceleration at N=10 (7.14× FLOPs reduction, 6.03× latency speedup), L²P maintains a PSNR of 30.031 while all baselines fall below 29.1. On Qwen-Image, L²P achieves a 7.18× FLOPs reduction at N=10 with PSNR 29.60, and when plugged into the 8-step Qwen-Image-Lightning model at N=3, it yields an additional 1.63× speedup with a near-lossless PSNR of 32.068.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L²P consistently outperforms all three baselines across every acceleration level and both evaluated models. Against TaylorSeer (Liu et al., ICCV 2025), L²P gains approximately 2.1 dB PSNR at N=5 on FLUX.1-dev (31.459 vs. 29.328) and 1.9 dB at N=7 (30.627 vs. 28.671), with the gap widening to 1.65 dB at N=10 (30.031 vs. 28.381). Against FoCa (Zheng et al., AAAI 2026), L²P leads by 2.05 dB PSNR at N=5 (31.459 vs. 29.413) and 1.43 dB at N=7 (30.627 vs. 29.193), while also achieving lower FLOPs (818.28T vs. 893.54T at N=5). Against FORA (Selvaraju et al., 2024), L²P surpasses it by 3.03 dB PSNR at comparable FLOPs reduction (31.459 vs. 28.432 at ~4.16× FLOPs speedup). On Qwen-Image, the advantage persists: at N=10, L²P achieves PSNR 29.60 while TaylorSeer (N=9) drops to 28.25 and ToCa (N=12) to 28.69. The key differentiator is that L²P's learnable coefficients adapt to each model's feature dynamics, whereas all baselines use fixed formula-derived coefficients that degrade sharply under aggressive skipping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 'cache-then-forecast' paradigm introduced by TaylorSeer (Liu et al., ICCV 2025) and extended by FoCa (Zheng et al., AAAI 2026), which model feature evolution across denoising steps using Taylor expansion and BDF2-based prediction, respectively. The key departure is a unifying theoretical observation: all such forecasting methods, despite their different mathematical appearances, reduce to a fixed linear combination of historical features with coefficients determined entirely by the formula's order and step size. Rather than designing yet another hand-crafted forecasting formula, L2P retains the linear framework but replaces the a priori coefficients with a small learnable matrix (49×49) trained via MSE on 50 cached trajectories, effectively treating the optimal projection (demonstrated to have >0.95 fidelity for most timesteps) as a learnable target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 20-second, single-GPU training procedure on 50 images yields a plug-and-play predictor that consistently outperforms all existing caching baselines across FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, and HunyuanVideo, especially at high acceleration ratios (7×+ FLOPs reduction) where fixed-coefficient methods degrade sharply. The robustness finding—that even gibberish training prompts produce near-identical performance—suggests the method captures content-agnostic feature dynamics rather than semantic content, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the linear framework remains sufficient for longer video generation trajectories, whether learned coefficients transfer across model architectures without retraining, and whether non-linear predictors (e.g., small MLPs) could close the remaining gap to the optimal projection at extreme skip intervals.
