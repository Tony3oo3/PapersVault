## Problem

Existing image relighting methods either rely on imprecise controls (text prompts, environment maps), demand explicit 3D/2.5D scene reconstruction that fails on occlusions and complex materials, or offer only coarse scribble-based edits. What is missing is a representation that enables precise, continuous, and spatially localized illumination control directly in the image domain—bridging the flexibility of 3D lighting tools with the accessibility of 2D editing—without requiring geometry or material estimation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 960-px RGB image plus a set of lighting attribute tokens: scalar parameters (ambient scale, per-light intensity, diffuse level, transition flag) encoded via Gaussian Fourier features, vector parameters (3D light position, RGB color) flattened into one token per component, and an optional binary light mask for in-scene fixtures encoded through the VAE into latent-space tokens. All 3D coordinates are defined in a scene-agnostic canonical cube relative to the camera, so the user specifies light positions without any scene geometry. The output is a single relit image at the same resolution, produced by a 50-step DDIM sampling process with classifier-free guidance (scale 2) applied only to the light-token conditioning.

## Method

TokenLight formulates relighting as conditional image generation: a latent diffusion transformer (initialized from the Wan text-to-video checkpoint) processes a concatenated sequence of image latent tokens and lighting attribute tokens with full self-attention, trained end-to-end with a flow-matching objective. The key architectural insight is a scene-agnostic parameterization where all scale-dependent lighting parameters (position, radius, intensity) are defined relative to a canonical cube, and Sim(3) transformations of that cube let the camera capture any scene region while the model sees only canonical-space tokens—eliminating the need for 3D reconstruction. Training uses a large-scale synthetic dataset rendered in Blender/Cycles with per-light decomposition and on-the-fly linear combination in linear RGB to synthesize paired supervision, supplemented by ~600 real indoor captures with toggled fixtures. The model learns physically plausible light-scene interaction (occlusion, specular highlights, translucency) purely from this synthetic supervision, without any explicit inverse-rendering loss.

## Results

On the synthetic Objaverse benchmark, TokenLight achieves 21.24 PSNR / 0.9724 SSIM / 0.0228 LPIPS on PanoGT targets and 21.98 PSNR / 0.9759 SSIM / 0.0210 LPIPS on PointGT targets, substantially outperforming Neural Gaffer (16.72/16.76 PSNR) and DiffusionRenderer (13.51/13.53 PSNR) across all metrics. On the real-world VisibleFixture-60 dataset, TokenLight reaches 20.08 PSNR / 0.8476 SSIM / 0.2784 LPIPS, nearly doubling the PSNR and SSIM of ScribbleLight (14.64 / 0.5186 / 0.6114). In a user study, TokenLight is preferred over both GenLit and Careaga et al. for spatial lighting control, and precision analysis shows lower diagonal error (A) and higher off-diagonal sensitivity (B/A) than Neural Gaffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two environment-map-based baselines on the synthetic Objaverse benchmark, TokenLight wins decisively: it gains ~4.5 dB PSNR over Neural Gaffer and ~8.4 dB over DiffusionRenderer on PanoGT, with similar margins on PointGT, while also achieving the best SSIM and lowest LPIPS. On the real-world VisibleFixture-60 dataset, TokenLight outperforms ScribbleLight by 5.4 dB PSNR and 0.33 SSIM, indicating far more accurate reproduction of on/off fixture effects including reflections and cast shadows. In the user study, TokenLight is preferred over both GenLit and Careaga et al. for spatial lighting quality and target matching. Qualitatively, TokenLight handles centered light placement, backlit rim effects, and low-angle views more consistently than GenLit (whose spatial behavior drifts with camera configuration), and produces convincing results on semi-transparent and occluded surfaces where Careaga et al.'s two-stage 2.5D reconstruction pipeline struggles. Compared to LightLab, TokenLight achieves similar quality for toggling visible fixtures but additionally supports 3D light insertion and continuous attribute control that LightLab does not offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

TokenLight builds on the latent diffusion transformer architecture (Peebles & Xie; Rombach et al.) and the flow-matching training objective (Lipman et al.), initializing from a pretrained Wan video model checkpoint. Its key departure from prior relighting work is the tokenized lighting-attribute representation: rather than conditioning on environment maps (Neural Gaffer, DiffusionRenderer), text prompts, scribbles, or requiring explicit 2.5D/3D scene reconstruction (Careaga et al.), it encodes physically meaningful parameters—3D light position, color, intensity, diffuse level, ambient scale—directly as tokens in the transformer's sequence. This unifies spatially localized light placement, ambient editing, and in-scene fixture control under a single scene-agnostic formulation that skips inverse rendering entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that a model trained purely on synthetic path-traced renders with ground-truth lighting annotations develops an implicit, geometry-aware understanding of light-scene interaction—handling occlusion, transparency, and specular highlights—without any explicit inverse-rendering supervision. This suggests that sufficiently rich synthetic supervision can substitute for the fragile 3D reconstruction pipelines that have bottlenecked prior methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporally consistent video relighting, how it scales to scenes with many simultaneous light sources, and whether the synthetic-to-real gap (addressed here with only ~600 real captures) fully closes for outdoor or highly complex indoor environments.
