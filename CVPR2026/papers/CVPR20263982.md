## Problem

Existing reference-to-video (R2V) generation methods depend on explicit reference image–video–text triplet datasets (e.g., OpenS2V-5M, Phantom-Data) whose construction involves costly multi-stage pipelines and yields limited subject diversity, making large-scale training infeasible. This paper asks whether R2V capability can be acquired without any dedicated R2V data, using only the abundant video-text pairs already available for text-to-video training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a variable number K of reference images at arbitrary resolutions (H_k × W_k × 3) together with a free-form text prompt, and produces a video of F frames at a fixed target resolution (H × W × 3). At inference, a pre-trained segmenter (BiRefNet) extracts foreground subject masks from each reference; if a reference is intended as a background scene, segmentation is skipped and the full image is used with an all-ones mask. The number of references is not fixed at training time—Saber generalizes zero-shot to any K, including multi-view inputs of the same subject.

## Method

Saber is finetuned from Wan2.1-14B (a DiT with VAE and umt5-xxl text encoder under a Flow Matching objective) using only video-text pairs. During training, reference images are synthesized on the fly by randomly sampling frames from the target video and applying a binary mask from a diverse shape library (ellipse, Fourier blob, convex/concave polygon) with a stochastically sampled foreground area ratio r; random affine augmentations (rotation, scaling, shear, translation, flip) are applied jointly to the frame and mask to disrupt spatial correspondence and suppress copy-paste artifacts. In the transformer, reference latents are concatenated temporally after the noisy video latents, with additional mask and zero-latent channels, and a tailored attention mask restricts self-attention so that video tokens attend bidirectionally while reference tokens attend only to valid (non-zero) regions. Cross-attention then injects text-prompt guidance, enabling the model to align reference identity with the textual description. This masked-training strategy, combined with the attention design, allows Saber to learn identity-consistent, reference-aware representations without any explicit R2V triplets.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Eval benchmark, Saber achieves the highest overall Total Score of 57.91% among all compared methods, surpassing the best explicit R2V-data-trained method (BindWeave, 57.61%) and the strongest closed-source commercial method (Kling1.6, 56.23%). Saber also attains the best NexusScore (47.22%) for subject consistency, exceeding Phantom by 9.79% and VACE by 3.14%. On NaturalScore (72.55%), Saber is competitive with Kling1.6 (74.59%) but slightly trails it, while on GmeScore (67.50%) it is marginally below MAGREF (70.47%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Saber outperforms all eight baselines on Total Score, with the largest margins over closed-source methods: +6.03% over Pika2.1, +5.96% over Vidu2.0, and +1.68% over Kling1.6. Among explicit R2V-data-trained methods, Saber beats BindWeave by 0.30%, VACE-14B by 0.36%, Phantom-14B by 1.14%, MAGREF by 5.40%, and SkyReels-A2 by 5.66%. On the key subject-consistency metric (NexusScore), Saber leads all methods, exceeding Phantom by 9.79% and VACE by 3.14%. However, Saber trails Kling1.6 on NaturalScore (72.55% vs. 74.59%) and MAGREF on GmeScore (67.50% vs. 70.47%), indicating slightly weaker text-video alignment and naturalness compared to those specific baselines. Notably, Saber achieves these results in a zero-shot setting without any explicit R2V triplet data, whereas all R2V-trained baselines rely on costly datasets like OpenS2V-5M or Phantom-Data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Saber builds directly on the Wan2.1-14B diffusion transformer (Flow Matching objective, VAE, umt5-xxl text encoder) but departs fundamentally from all prior R2V methods (Phantom, VACE, SkyReels-A2, MAGREF, PolyVivid, BindWeave, HunyuanCustom) that require explicitly constructed reference image–video–text triplet datasets. Instead of collecting such triplets, Saber simulates the R2V task during training by randomly masking frames from video-text pairs and using them as pseudo-references, paired with a tailored attention mask that restricts cross-token interaction to valid reference regions. The mask augmentation pipeline (affine transforms applied jointly to image and mask) is a practical addition not seen in prior R2V architectures, specifically targeting the copy-paste artifact problem identified in Phantom and others.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expensive, hard-to-scale R2V triplet construction, Saber opens a path to training R2V models on the same abundant video-text corpora already used for T2V/I2V, potentially democratizing the task. The emergent abilities (multi-view same-subject integration, cross-modal prompt alignment) suggest the masked training strategy learns richer reference representations than explicit triplet supervision. However, the paper acknowledges that generation quality degrades with many references (e.g., 12), and fine-grained motion control under complex prompts remains unsolved—open questions for scaling R2V to truly diverse, multi-subject scenarios.
