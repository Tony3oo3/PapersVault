## Problem

Existing video generation and latent-dynamics models fail to learn transferable, task-relevant knowledge from raw real-world videos for complex, long-horizon tasks. The core issue is the entanglement of action dynamics with visual appearance: models overfit to irrelevant details (backgrounds, lighting, textures) rather than extracting the compact, generalizable action representations needed to execute multi-step tasks in novel environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single input frame from an unseen environment plus a task instruction and autoregressively predicts a sequence of discrete latent dynamics codes, which are then decoded into a coherent long-horizon execution video (93 frames at 480px, ~5s at 16fps per clip, generated iteratively). The latent code vocabulary is compact (FSQ with 1000 levels, N=4 query embeddings per temporal step), making the autoregressive prediction tractable over minute-long task sequences.

## Method

VideoWorld 2 introduces a dynamics-enhanced Latent Dynamics Model (dLDM) that decouples action dynamics from visual appearance by pairing a causal VQ-VAE encoder with a pretrained Video Diffusion Model (VDM). The VQ-VAE (MAGVITv2-style) uses learnable query embeddings with cross-attention to compress inter-frame visual changes into discrete latent codes, while the VDM handles all appearance modeling, freeing the codes to encode only task-relevant motion. A ControlNet-like branch feeds gradient-stopped VQ-VAE decoder reconstructions into the VDM to provide coarse motion cues, stabilizing training without requiring the VDM to infer dynamics from scratch. An autoregressive transformer (Cosmos AR 4B) is then trained to predict these latent codes conditioned on the initial frame and task instruction, learning long-horizon policies entirely from unlabeled video. This decoupling yields latent codes that remain consistent across environments, enabling robust transfer to novel settings without explicit action supervision.

## Results

On Video-CraftBench (7-step sequential paper folding), VideoWorld 2 achieves 68.8% final-step success with Craft-only training and 72.3% with OpenX pretraining, compared to 0% for all four state-of-the-art VDMs (Cosmos AR 4B, Cosmos DiT 2B, Hunyuan-13B, Wan 2.2 14B) and 0% for VideoWorld. On block stacking, VideoWorld 2 reaches 81.5% (Craft) / 85.8% (OpenX) versus 42.6% for the best VDM (Wan 2.2 14B). On CALVIN, OpenX-pretrained VideoWorld 2 achieves 30.9% five-task sequential success and 2.88 average task length, outperforming LAPA (27.0%, 2.51) and a video next-token prediction baseline (23.0%, 2.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four pre-trained VDMs (Cosmos AR 4B, Cosmos DiT 2B, Hunyuan-13B, Wan 2.2 14B), VideoWorld 2 dominates on long-horizon success: while the best VDM (Wan 2.2 14B) reaches only 10.6% at step 4 and 0% at steps 5–7, VideoWorld 2 maintains 83.3% at step 4 and 68.8% at step 7 (Craft-only), a gap of roughly 70 percentage points. Among latent action/dynamics baselines, VideoWorld 2 outperforms VideoWorld (its direct predecessor) by 68.8% vs. 0% at step 7 and 77.5% vs. 28.5% on block stacking (Craft-only); with OpenX pretraining, the gap widens to 72.3% vs. 31.9% at step 7. The concurrent CoLA method, which also uses a VDM but is limited to 2-frame transitions, achieves 40.2% at step 7 versus VideoWorld 2's 72.3%. On CALVIN, VideoWorld 2 with OpenX latent pretraining (30.9% five-task success, 2.88 avg length) surpasses LAPA (27.0%, 2.51) and a video next-token prediction baseline (23.0%, 2.46), confirming that the dLDM's decoupled latent codes are a more effective transfer paradigm than raw video tokens or LAPA-style latent actions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends VideoWorld [51], which demonstrated knowledge acquisition from synthetic videos (Go games, simulated robotics) using an autoregressive latent dynamics model. The key architectural departure is the dynamics-enhanced LDM (dLDM), which replaces VideoWorld's VQ-VAE decoder with a pretrained Video Diffusion Model (Cosmos DiT 2B) to offload appearance modeling, allowing the VQ-VAE encoder and learnable queries to focus exclusively on task-relevant action dynamics. Conceptually, it also positions against two other lines: SOTA video generation models (Wan 2.2, Hunyuan, Cosmos) that produce high-fidelity frames but fail at long-horizon task execution, and latent action models (LAPA, Moto, AdaWorld, CoLA) that compress inter-frame changes but overfit to environment-specific appearance. The ControlNet-like conditioning from the VQ-VAE decoder to the VDM is a practical training trick borrowed from image generation to stabilize the joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that transferable, long-horizon task knowledge can be extracted from raw real-world videos without language supervision, with the critical insight that decoupling appearance from dynamics is necessary for generalization. The cross-domain transfer from Open-X robotics data to CALVIN suggests a practical pathway for scaling manipulation knowledge from the growing pool of unlabeled robot videos. However, the evaluation scope remains narrow (handicrafts + one robotics benchmark), the VDM component adds significant computational overhead, and the paper explicitly defers scaling to future work. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to tasks with continuous action spaces, whether the VDM can be replaced by lighter appearance priors, and how performance scales with video diversity and duration.
