## Problem

Federated network pruning faces a fundamental dilemma: server-side pruning enforces uniform compression without personalization, while client-side pruning is computationally prohibitive for resource-constrained edge devices. Additionally, the pruning process itself induces parametric divergence among heterogeneous submodels, destabilizing local training and hindering global convergence during aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261149/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The server consumes historical client model parameters (as distributional proxies in lieu of raw data), the current global model, and per-client pruning rates from a discrete set {0, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4} to generate structurally heterogeneous submodels. Each client then trains its assigned submodel locally on its private dataset, and the server aggregates the updated submodels into a new global model. The OT transport plans are computed layer-wise with complexity O(L·M²), keeping per-client server-side latency under 1.2 seconds even for MobileNetV2.

## Method

SubFLOT introduces three synergistic modules. The OTP module formulates server-side personalized pruning as a progressive layer-wise Wasserstein distance minimization between the global model and each client's historical model, computing transport plans that align functionally equivalent neurons before fusing aligned global weights with client-specific parameters via a convex combination (α=0.5). The SAR module adds a local regularization term ρ_i·‖W_i − W̃_i‖² that adaptively penalizes deviation from the server-provided anchor, with penalty strength proportional to the client's pruning rate to prevent heavily pruned models from diverging. The OTA module repurposes the same OT machinery in reverse during aggregation, aligning each updated client submodel back to the global parameter space before weighted averaging, thereby suppressing destructive interference from structurally disparate submodels. A convergence theorem establishes linear convergence to a neighborhood whose size depends on stochastic noise, heterogeneity, OT perturbation, and personalization deviation.

## Results

SubFLOT achieves the highest mean accuracy across all eight evaluation settings in Table 1. Under pathological label skew, it reaches 86.89% on CIFAR-10, 58.37% on CIFAR-100, and 29.30% on Tiny-ImageNet, surpassing the best baseline (FlexFL) by 1.76, 9.16, and 7.07 percentage points respectively. Under practical label skew, SubFLOT attains 44.88% on CIFAR-100 and 25.15% on Tiny-ImageNet, outperforming FlexFL by 9.61 and 2.96 points. On the real-world HAR sensor dataset, SubFLOT scores 79.72%, exceeding the next-best method (FedMP at 75.55%) by over 4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubFLOT outperforms all ten baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet under pathological skew, where SubFLOT beats the strongest baseline FlexFL by 9.16 and 7.07 points respectively, and under practical skew by 9.61 and 2.96 points. On CIFAR-10, the gap is smaller (1.76 points over FlexFL under pathological skew) because all methods perform well on this simpler task. On the real-world HAR sensor dataset, SubFLOT leads FedMP (the best baseline at 75.55%) by 4.17 points, confirming its advantage in practical deployment. On AG News (NLP), SubFLOT achieves 87.88% versus FlexFL's 86.02%, demonstrating cross-modality generalization. The consistent superiority across CV, NLP, and IoT modalities, as well as across both pathological and practical non-IID partitions, indicates that the OT-based personalization and SAR regularization provide robust gains regardless of data domain or heterogeneity type.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new middle ground in federated network pruning by introducing server-side personalized pruning, departing from both uniform server-side strategies (FedMP, FedDSE) that ignore local data and computationally expensive client-side approaches (HeteroFL, FedRolex) that burden edge devices. It repurposes Optimal Transport from its prior use in feature-space alignment (FedOTP, FedAli) to a parameter-space alignment problem for pruning, using historical client models as distributional proxies. The SAR module contrasts with HeteroFL's post-hoc weight magnitude corrections by proactively regularizing training dynamics, and the same OT mechanism is elegantly dual-used for both submodel generation (OTP) and aggregation (OTA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SubFLOT addresses a genuine deployment bottleneck: enabling personalized, resource-adaptive models on heterogeneous edge devices without the privacy or compute costs of client-side pruning. The convergence analysis with explicit OT perturbation and personalization error bounds provides theoretical grounding that prior federated pruning work largely lacks. However, the strong convexity assumption in the analysis limits direct applicability to deep networks, and the reliance on historical models as distributional proxies raises open questions about cold-start scenarios and rapidly shifting data distributions. The practical impact is significant for IoT and mobile FL deployments where per-client model size must vary.
