## Problem

Autoregressive models achieve scaling success in language but struggle over continuous pixel data due to extremely long, high-dimensional sequences, while normalizing flows that map images to a fixed standard Gaussian introduce out-of-distribution latents that degrade sample quality. The paper addresses how to unify NFs and AR models for tractable, exact likelihood estimation and high-quality image synthesis directly from raw pixels without VAE compression or discrete tokenization.

## Input / Output

FARMER takes a 256×256×3 RGB image and a class label as input. The image is dequantized via annealed Gaussian noise (σ from 0.1 to 0.005) and patchified with a downsampling factor of 8 or 16 into N continuous-valued tokens of dimension d=768 (no compression). The model outputs a generated 256×256 image and provides an exact per-pixel likelihood computed from the AR log-likelihood plus the AF Jacobian log-determinant, enabling principled density evaluation.

## Method

FARMER jointly optimizes an Autoregressive Flow (AF) and a causal AR Transformer end-to-end: the AF invertibly maps patchified image tokens to latent sequences, and the AR Transformer models the latent distribution with K-component Gaussian Mixture Models conditioned on a replicated class embedding. A self-supervised dimension reduction splits each latent token into informative channels (d^I=128, modeled autoregressively token-by-token) and redundant channels (d^R=640, modeled jointly by a single shared GMM conditioned on the full informative sequence), effectively converting N high-dimensional tokens into N+1 lower-dimensional ones and encouraging disentanglement of structure from color/detail. A one-step distillation scheme trains a bidirectional student AF to replicate the teacher's reverse path in a single forward pass, accelerating NF inference by 22×. A resampling-based classifier-free guidance algorithm approximates the intractable guided GMM by proposing candidates from conditional and unconditional GMMs, weighting them by the guidance ratio, and resampling, yielding substantially better generation quality than naive CFG.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, FARMER-1.9B (patch 8) achieves FID 3.60, IS 269.21, Precision 0.81, and Recall 0.51 with 1.9B parameters trained for 320 epochs. The smaller FARMER-1.1B (patch 8) reaches FID 5.02 and IS 237.00. FARMER significantly outperforms JetFormer (2.8B, 500 epochs) by 3.04 FID points (3.60 vs. 6.64) while using fewer parameters and fewer training epochs. With one-step distillation, total inference time drops from 0.2189 s to 0.0567 s per image (~4× speedup) with negligible quality loss (FID 5.63 vs. 5.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FARMER-1.9B (patch 8, FID 3.60) substantially outperforms the closest comparable baseline JetFormer (2.8B, FID 6.64) by 3.04 FID points, while using fewer parameters (1.9B vs. 2.8B) and fewer training epochs (320 vs. 500). Among pixel-space NF models, FARMER beats TARFlow (FID 5.56) and STARFlow patch 8 (FID 4.69) by 1.96 and 1.09 FID points respectively. FARMER also surpasses pixel-space diffusion baselines ADM (FID 4.59) and CDM (FID 4.88), as well as the GAN baseline BigGAN (FID 6.95) and the AR baseline FractalMAR-H (FID 6.15). However, FARMER trails the strongest pixel-space diffusion methods—PixelFlow-XL/4 (FID 1.98), PixNerd-XL/16 (FID 1.93), and SiD2 (FID 1.38)—as well as latent-space models like MAR-L (FID 1.78) and GIVT (FID 2.59), which benefit from VAE-compressed latent spaces or multi-stage pipelines. The paper positions FARMER as competitive within the pixel-based NF+AR family and as a single-stage, end-to-end alternative that provides exact likelihoods, rather than as a state-of-the-art FID competitor to the best multi-stage pixel diffusion models.

## Relation to Prior Work

FARMER directly extends JetFormer's NF+AR framework for raw pixel generation, but makes two key architectural departures: it replaces JetFormer's Jet-based NF with an Autoregressive Flow (AF) to maintain a consistent causal formulation throughout, and it abandons JetFormer's assumption that redundant latent channels follow an independent standard Gaussian prior, instead conditioning them on the full informative sequence and class context. The GMM prediction head follows GIVT, and the one-step distillation scheme addresses the sequential reverse-inference bottleneck identified in TARFlow and STARFlow. The self-supervised dimension reduction generalizes the channel-splitting idea from RealNVP and JetFormer by removing the independence assumption and allowing the redundant group to be informed by both the informative tokens and the conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FARMER provides a principled route to exact pixel-space likelihood estimation with competitive generation quality (FID 3.60 at 256×256), bridging the gap between the expressivity of AR models and the tractable densities of NFs. The 22× acceleration of the NF reverse process via one-step distillation and the resampling-based CFG are practical techniques likely to be adopted in other AF-based generative systems. However, FARMER still trails the best latent-space diffusion models (FID 1.12 for REPA-E) and the authors acknowledge two unresolved issues: reliance on noise injection for dequantization and the computational complications of the log-determinant loss term, both of which limit scalability to higher resolutions.
