## Problem

Visual reinforcement learning agents face a fundamental plasticity-stability dilemma: they must continuously adapt their representations and value estimates to non-stationary observations while avoiding catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills. Existing approaches—data augmentation, self-supervised representation learning, and dynamics modeling—operate under a single monolithic learning process that forces a global trade-off between rapid adaptation and knowledge consolidation, without explicitly decoupling these competing objectives. The paper demonstrates empirically that vanilla critics suffer progressive plasticity degradation (increasing dormant neurons) and representation drift (measured via CKA and value-consistency metrics), and that no single critic can simultaneously satisfy both requirements.

## Input / Output

The agent receives 84×84 RGB image frames stacked over three timesteps to capture temporal dependencies, along with a continuous action, and outputs a continuous control action for the environment. Internally, the shared visual encoder produces three feature projections: a shared embedding z that serves as the policy query, and two critic-specific projections z1 and z2 that act as keys in the cross-attention fusion module. The two critics each produce a scalar Q-value, which are then adaptively weighted by the cross-attention mechanism to yield a single fused Q-value that drives both policy optimization and exploration.

## Method

CD-CCA builds on SAC and introduces two functionally heterogeneous critics under an in-task continual learning paradigm: a plastic critic enhanced with Continual Backpropagation (CBP), which tracks per-neuron contribution utility via an exponential moving average of activation-weighted outgoing magnitudes and reinitializes persistently low-utility units to maintain representational expressivity, and a stable critic regularized by Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC), which uses a Fisher Information Matrix estimated from replay-buffer mini-batches to penalize deviation of previously critical parameters. A cross-attention fusion module then dynamically integrates the two Q-values: the shared encoder embedding z serves as the query, the critic-specific projections z1 and z2 serve as keys, and the scalar Q-values Q1 and Q2 serve as values, producing observation-conditioned weights δ1 and δ2 that adaptively balance plastic and stable estimates. The total training loss combines the fused Q-value Bellman error, individual critic Bellman errors (weighted by β), and the EWC regularization term, while the policy is updated via the standard SAC reparameterization objective using the fused Q-value.

## Results

On the four hard DMControl tasks at 1M environment steps, CD-CCA achieves an average reward of 857.0, outperforming DrQ-v2 (813.5), ResAct (630.2), TACO (584.8), MaDi (566.0), and Flare (546.2). At 500K steps, CD-CCA reaches 683.0 average reward versus 636.8 for DrQ-v2, demonstrating superior sample efficiency. On the CARLA Highway scenario, CD-CCA attains the highest episode reward (343±63) among all methods, while on Jaywalk it achieves the best distance (183±58 m). Notably, CD-CCA exhibits substantially lower standard deviations across seeds (e.g., ±8 on Walker Run at 500K vs. ±79 for DrQ-v2), indicating more stable convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-CCA (built on DrQ-v2) outperforms DrQ-v2 on 3 of 4 DMControl tasks at both 500K and 1M steps, with the largest gains on Finger Turn hard (+198 at 500K, +120 at 1M) and Walker Run (+58 at 500K, +13 at 1M), while trailing DrQ-v2 on Pendulum Swingup at 500K (756 vs. 842) but matching it at 1M (817 vs. 812). Against the next-best baselines, CD-CCA leads ResAct by 226.2 points in average reward at 500K and 226.8 at 1M, and surpasses TACO, MaDi, and Flare by even wider margins. On CARLA, CD-CCA (as DeepMDP+CD-CCA) achieves the highest Highway episode reward (343 vs. 283 for ResAct, 256 for MLR) but slightly trails ResAct on Highway distance (287 vs. 299 m); on Jaywalk it leads all methods in both reward (204 vs. 194 for MLR) and distance (183 vs. 177 for MLR). The paper also notes that CD-CCA's standard deviations are consistently lower than baselines (e.g., ±8 on Walker Run at 500K vs. ±79 for DrQ-v2), suggesting more reliable and reproducible training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAC as the base actor-critic algorithm and repurposes two continual learning mechanisms—Continual Backpropagation (CBP, Dohare et al., Nature 2024) and Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al., PNAS 2017)—which were originally designed for sequential task learning, into a within-task dual-critic architecture for visual RL. It departs from prior visual RL methods (DrQ, CURL, DBC, Flare, TACO) that address representation quality through data augmentation, self-supervised objectives, or dynamics modeling, by instead targeting the critic's learning dynamics directly. It also extends the multi-critic literature (Double Q, TD3, Bayesian ensembles) by introducing functional heterogeneity rooted in distinct continual learning rules rather than homogeneous critics combined via min or averaging, and adds a cross-attention fusion module for observation-conditioned value integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic framework (dormant neuron ratio, CKA drift, value consistency) that empirically characterizes the stability-plasticity trade-off in visual RL critics, and demonstrates that architecturally separating plastic and stable learning into two critics with adaptive fusion outperforms homogeneous ensembles. However, the approach is currently validated only on SAC-based architectures across four DMControl tasks and two CARLA driving scenarios, and the CARLA results show more modest gains (e.g., DeepMDP+OURs does not consistently beat ResAct on Jaywalk). Open questions include whether the CBP+EWC pairing generalizes to other plasticity mechanisms, whether the cross-attention fusion scales to more than two critics, and whether the framework transfers to discrete-action or model-based settings.
