## Problem

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) inherently lack cross-view 3D geometric modeling capability, which is critical for safe navigation in complex autonomous driving environments. Existing mitigations—constructing large-scale Q&A datasets to teach spatial concepts or attaching independent action decoders—fail to fundamentally equip VLMs with solid geometric priors and disconnect scene understanding from decision-making. The paper asks how to effectively infuse VLMs with the cross-view geometric grounding of mature 3D foundation models like VGGT to close this capability gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view surround images (6-camera for nuScenes-based benchmarks, 3 front-facing cameras for NAVSIM) alongside a language instruction; for trajectory planning tasks, ego-vehicle state (velocity, acceleration) and navigation commands (Go Straight, Turn Left, Turn Right) are appended to the textual prompt. Outputs are generated autoregressively as text tokens, covering diverse task formats: trajectory waypoints, risk object detection with state prediction, motion forecasting, scene captions, and action labels. The base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) processes all modalities through a unified transformer decoder, with the CVGE operating internally on visual embeddings without altering the input/output interface.

## Method

VGGDrive introduces a plug-and-play Cross-view 3D Geometric Enabler (CVGE) that bridges frozen VGGT 3D features with the VLM's 2D visual representations via a hierarchical adaptive injection mechanism. The base LLM's decoder layers are decoupled: at each layer, 2D visual embeddings are extracted via positional masking, passed through a per-layer CVGE module (where 2D features act as queries and VGGT 3D features as keys/values in multi-head cross-attention), and the geometry-enhanced embeddings are injected back through a residual connection. Camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters are explicitly encoded as tokens and added to the key/value vectors to ground the 3D features in the LiDAR coordinate frame. Training proceeds in two stages—first training only CVGE parameters with the VLM frozen, then jointly fine-tuning both—allowing the 3D features to be absorbed before the full model adapts to driving tasks.

## Results

On NAVSIM closed-loop evaluation, VGGDrive achieves a PDMS of 88.76, surpassing the best E2E method (WoTE, 88.30) and all VLA baselines (best: ReCogDrive, 86.80), while improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by +2.72 PDMS. On NuInstruct, VGGDrive achieves a MAP of 37.49 for cross-view risk object perception, a 31.34-point improvement over the baseline (6.15) and 7.37 points above the previous SOTA (VGGT-Add, 30.12). On DriveLM, VGGDrive reaches 77.50% accuracy and 49.77% Match, outperforming the prior best (I-VL4Drive: 73.39% / 47.65%). On nuScenes open-loop planning, VGGDrive reduces average L2 error to 0.31 m and collision rate to 0.22%, both best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, VGGDrive (PDMS 88.76) outperforms all VLA baselines by at least +1.96 over ReCogDrive (86.80) and +2.56 over AdaThinkDrive (86.20), and also surpasses the strongest E2E methods WoTE (88.30) and DiffusionDrive (88.10). The two simpler VGGT integration schemes—VGGT-Dist (86.68) and VGGT-Add (86.10)—yield only marginal gains over the baseline (86.04), confirming that naive feature distillation or addition is insufficient for driving-scale geometric grounding. On NuInstruct, VGGDrive's MAP of 37.49 dominates VGGT-Add (30.12), VGGT-Dist (28.51), and InMLLM (21.93) by 7.37, 8.98, and 15.56 points respectively, while also improving BLEU from 75.75 to 81.13. On DriveLM, VGGDrive leads I-VL4Drive (60.02 avg) by +1.24 and FSDrive (57.12) by +4.14 in average score, with the largest gap in the Match metric (49.77 vs. 47.65). On OmniDrive captioning, VGGDrive (52.85 avg) marginally improves over the fine-tuned baseline (52.64) while substantially exceeding the original OmniDrive model (46.40), showing that geometric injection does not degrade language generation. On nuScenes open-loop planning, VGGDrive achieves the lowest L2 (0.31 m) and collision rate (0.22%) among all methods, beating BEV-Planner (0.35 m / 0.34%) and the VLA baseline (0.36 m / 0.41%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLA paradigm for autonomous driving (Qwen2.5-VL as backbone) and the emerging line of work integrating visual 3D foundation models (VGGT) with VLMs. It departs from prior VGGT+VLM integration schemes—distillation-based alignment (3DRS) and simple feature addition (Zheng et al.)—by proposing a hierarchical adaptive injection mechanism (CVGE) that applies multi-head cross-attention at every decoder layer rather than at a single point. The paper also positions itself against two common workarounds: constructing large-scale spatial Q&A datasets to teach VLMs geometry, and attaching independent action decoders for trajectory prediction, arguing both fail to establish genuine geometric grounding within the VLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VGGDrive demonstrates that a frozen 3D foundation model can substantially close the cross-view geometric gap in VLM-based driving systems, with the plug-and-play CVGE offering a reusable integration recipe for other VLM+3D-model combinations. However, the approach assumes known camera intrinsics/extrinsics as prior, limits evaluation to two specific model pairs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + VGGT), and incurs a ~28% inference-time increase (0.81s → 1.04s) that may constrain real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the hierarchical injection generalizes to other VLM architectures and 3D backbones, whether it scales to more than 6 views or longer temporal sequences, and whether the gains persist in truly closed-loop, reactive driving scenarios beyond the non-reactive NAVSIM protocol.
