## Problem

Current generative and vision-language models treat floor plan synthesis as a purely visual process without explicit room-instance-level reasoning, producing layouts that appear locally plausible but lack global spatial coherence. Existing frameworks also fail to unify understanding, generation, and editing within a single architecture, and most remain computationally heavy and difficult to deploy locally in practical design workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262338/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt or instruction, a binary floor plan outline mask, and (for understanding/editing) an existing floor plan image, all at 256×256 resolution. The floor plan is decomposed into an outline plus N room instances, each quantized into a variable-length discrete token sequence; the full layout is serialized as an interleaved sequence of outline tokens, semantic label tokens, and room tokens. Outputs include a rendered floor plan layout, a textual description, a bubble diagram, and structured JSON encoding room type, area, centroid, and pairwise spatial relations.

## Method

HouseMind discretizes continuous floor plan geometry into a unified token vocabulary via two hierarchical VQ-VAE branches: one encodes the global outline into a sequence of outline tokens, and a second (conditioned on the outline) encodes each room instance into room tokens, so that the entire plan becomes an interleaved sequence [z_o, ℓ_{r1}, z_{r1}, …, ℓ_{rN}, z_{rN}]. These spatial tokens are injected into a Qwen3-0.6B LLM vocabulary as trainable embeddings, and the model is trained through a three-stage pipeline: (S1) embedding initialization to ground spatial codes in the language model's token space, (S2) multimodal pre-training with autoregressive next-token prediction over mixed text-and-spatial sequences to align geometry and semantics, and (S3) supervised instruction tuning on curated data covering understanding, generation, and editing tasks. This formulation reduces all three tasks to a single autoregressive sequence-modeling problem, enabling the LLM to perform room-by-room spatial reasoning, conditional layout synthesis, and localized token-level editing within one coherent architecture.

## Results

On the RPLAN benchmark (2,308 test samples), HouseMind-G achieves Micro/Macro IoU of 0.709/0.653, FID of 1.91, and GED of 1.01 for generation, outperforming the strongest prior method (ChatHouseDiffusion: IoU 0.589/0.521, FID 11.3, GED 2.36) by over 10% in IoU and reducing FID by ~6×. For understanding, HouseMind-U reaches 0.998 room match rate, 0.969 location accuracy, and 0.808 spatial relation accuracy, surpassing the best VLM baseline (MiniCPM-V4.5: RMR 0.904, LocAcc 0.492, RelAcc 0.208) by 40+ absolute points on localization and adjacency. For editing, HouseMind-E achieves ΔIoU of 0.608 and GED of 0.467, compared to FLUX.1-Kontext-dev (ΔIoU 0.053, GED 8.91), while running in ~3 s vs. ~240 s per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HouseMind dominates all nine baselines across every task and metric. In generation, it outperforms ChatHouseDiffusion (the strongest prior floor-plan-specific method) by +12% Micro IoU (0.709 vs. 0.589) and reduces FID from 11.3 to 1.91; FloorPlanLLaMA is close in IoU (0.607) but lags in FID (49.3) and edge overlap (0.574 vs. 0.880); Tell2Design and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 are far behind on all metrics. In understanding, HouseMind-U surpasses the four VLM baselines (LLaVA, Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, MiniCPM-V) by 40+ points on location accuracy and adjacency accuracy, and reduces mean area error from 3.6–13.8 m² to 0.55 m². In editing, HouseMind-E achieves ΔIoU of 0.608 versus 0.053 for FLUX.1-Kontext-dev and 0.088 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, while preserving structural integrity (Node F1 0.998 vs. 0.765/0.915). Notably, HouseMind runs in ~2–3 s per sample on a single RTX 3090, compared to ~240 s for the diffusion-based image editors and ~30 s for ChatHouseDiffusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

HouseMind builds on the VQ-VAE framework for discrete representation learning [36] but repurposes it hierarchically—separately quantizing building outlines and individual room instances—so that a small autoregressive LLM (Qwen3-0.6B) can reason over spatial structure as a token sequence. It departs from prior diffusion-based floor plan generators (ChatHouseDiffusion, FloorPlanLLaMA) that treat layout synthesis as a purely visual process, and from modular LLM-based pipelines (Tell2Design, ChatDesign) that handle understanding, generation, and editing in separate architectures. The closest conceptual precursor is MaskPLAN [57], which also uses VQ-VAE to encode geometric attributes into discrete tokens, but HouseMind extends this to a full multimodal instruction-tuning pipeline unifying all three tasks in one model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 0.6B-parameter model with discrete spatial tokens can match or exceed 8B+ vision-language models and diffusion-based generators on floor plan tasks while running in ~2–3 seconds on a single GPU, making locally deployable architectural co-design feasible. However, the editing module is limited to simple room addition/deletion, functional elements (doors, windows, furniture) are unmodeled, and alignment with human aesthetic preferences remains open. Future directions—complex topological transformations, architecture-structure co-design, and performance-aware generation—suggest the current framework is a foundation rather than a complete solution for professional design workflows.
