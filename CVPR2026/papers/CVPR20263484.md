## Problem

Existing test-time training methods for point cloud models rely on decoder-based auxiliary objectives (e.g., reconstruction or skeleton prediction) that are weakly coupled with the primary classification task and require updating large portions of model parameters, leading to high adaptation cost and catastrophic forgetting. The paper identifies that the true source of OOD robustness in masking-based pretrained models is feature-level invariance under masked perturbations, and asks whether this invariance can be directly exploited as a lightweight, decoder-free test-time adaptation signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud tokenized via Farthest Point Sampling and K-Nearest Neighbors, processed by a 12-block PointMAE Transformer encoder. During test-time adaptation, a single randomly masked view (masking ratio 0.1) and the complete view of the same point cloud are fed through the shared encoder; the classifier is trained on masked-view features but inference uses the complete view, justified by the alignment objective enforcing feature equivalence. Only the low-rank modulation units (rank 16, scaling factor 64) embedded in Q, K, V, and FFN projections are updated per test sample with a single AdamW step.

## Method

LoTT-PC introduces two core components: (1) Low-rank modulation units that inject trainable rank-16 LoRA matrices into the attention and FFN layers of a frozen PointMAE encoder, restricting all adaptation to a small parameter subset; and (2) a Perturbation-Invariant Alignment Mechanism (MFA) that computes a decoder-free cosine-similarity loss between the global features (CLS token concatenated with max-pooled non-CLS tokens) of a masked view and a complete view, directly aligning the representations used by the classifier. Training proceeds in two stages: a joint training phase that optimizes the classifier and LoRA units with a combined cross-entropy and MFA loss (masking ratio 0.9), followed by an online test-time training phase where only the LoRA units are updated via MFA on a single masked view per sample (masking ratio 0.1), after which the frozen classifier predicts on the complete view. This design eliminates auxiliary decoders, ensures tight coupling between the auxiliary alignment objective and the primary classification task, and reduces per-sample adaptation to one gradient step.

## Results

On the three corrupted 3D benchmarks, LoTT-PC-Online achieves 75.5% on ModelNet40-C, 54.1% on ScanObjectNN-C, and 67.4% on ShapeNet-C, yielding a mean accuracy of 65.7%—the highest across all methods. Even without test-time adaptation, LoTT-PC-SO reaches 64.3% mean accuracy, surpassing the fully adapted MATE-Online (63.0%) and SMART-PC-Online (62.5%). The method requires only a single gradient step per test sample with one masked view, compared to MATE's 48 augmentation views, making it substantially more efficient.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoTT-PC-Online outperforms the two strongest TTT baselines—MATE-Online and SMART-PC-Online—on ModelNet40-C (75.5% vs. 71.3% and 72.9%) and ScanObjectNN-C (54.1% vs. 48.5% and 47.4%), with margins of +4.2%/+2.6% and +5.6%/+6.7% respectively. On ShapeNet-C, MATE-Online retains a slight edge (69.1% vs. 67.4%), likely because its reconstruction objective aligns well with clean CAD geometries, but LoTT-PC still beats SMART-PC-Online (67.1%) and achieves the best overall mean (65.7% vs. 63.0% and 62.5%). The source-only variant (LoTT-PC-SO, 64.3% mean) already exceeds the fully adapted MATE-Online and SMART-PC-Online, indicating that the joint MFA training itself confers strong intrinsic robustness. General TTA baselines (SHOT, TENT, T3A, BFTT3D) perform substantially worse, with mean accuracies between 30.5% and 51.4%, confirming that decoder-free feature-level alignment is far more effective than entropy minimization or classifier-only adjustment for 3D point clouds.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoTT-PC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) test-time training for point clouds (MATE, SMART-PC, TTT-KD), which adapts models at inference via auxiliary self-supervised objectives; (2) masked autoencoder pretraining for 3D (Point-MAE, Point-BERT), whose implicit feature invariance under masking the authors identify as the true source of OOD robustness; and (3) parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA), repurposed here not for downstream transfer but as the sole updatable substrate during test-time adaptation. The key departure from prior TTT work is twofold: replacing decoder-based reconstruction or skeleton-prediction heads with a decoder-free masked feature alignment loss that directly operates on the encoder's global features, and restricting all test-time updates to lightweight low-rank matrices rather than fine-tuning the full encoder and auxiliary heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single gradient step with one masked view replaces MATE's 48-view augmentation pipeline, making online TTT substantially cheaper in latency and memory. The finding that the source-only model (no TTT) already outperforms fully adapted baselines suggests that the joint MFA training objective, not the test-time update itself, is the primary driver of robustness—an insight that could redirect effort toward better pretraining/training objectives rather than more complex adaptation procedures. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (PointMAE) and a single task (classification); whether the decoder-free alignment principle transfers to segmentation, detection, or other 3D tasks remains open. The modest gap on ShapeNet-C (synthetic CAD data) versus MATE also hints that reconstruction-based objectives may retain an advantage when the corruption is mild and geometry is regular.
