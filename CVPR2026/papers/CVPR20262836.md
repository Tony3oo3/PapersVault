## Problem

Group-relative RLVR methods like GRPO treat all rollouts symmetrically, so when a group is all-wrong the gradient vanishes, and when one rollout happens to be correct the update ignores why the other near-miss failures are close-but-wrong. This uniform credit assignment produces high gradient variance, training instability, and reinforcement of spurious reasoning chains that arrive at the right answer by chance.

## Input / Output

The model receives one or more RGB images paired with a text query and emits a structured response: a step-by-step rationale enclosed in <think>... tags followed by a final answer in <answer>...</answer> tags. A programmatic verifier parses only the answer span for exact-match scoring, while the rationale span is used for embedding-based hard-negative selection and receives a heavily down-weighted gradient (γ⁺=0.005) during training. Inference uses a single decode with no test-time reflection.

## Method

CARE replaces GRPO's full-group normalization with an anchored-contrastive subgroup: it selects the verified-correct rollout with the shortest rationale as an anchor, mines hard negatives by cosine proximity of rationale embeddings (mean-pooled final-layer hidden states over the think span), and z-scores rewards within this compact subgroup while applying a negative-only penalty scaling (s=0.5) to prevent over-sharpening. A second component, Reflection-Guided Resampling, activates only when a positive exists: it inserts a brief repair cue into one hard negative's rationale, resamples exactly once, and either replaces the failure with the corrected rollout or retains it with a halved penalty. An all-negative rescue injects a small zero-sum pseudo-contrast (δ=0.1) on a pseudo-anchor to prevent gradient stalling when no rollout succeeds. The token-weighted policy objective assigns weight 1 to answer tokens, γ⁺=0.005 to positive rationale tokens, and 0 to negative rationale tokens, concentrating gradient signal on the answer while preserving a small rationale signal only for correct chains.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CARE achieves a macro-averaged accuracy of 50.57 across six verifiable visual-reasoning benchmarks (MathVista-mini, MathVerse-mini, MATH-Vision-full, MMMU-val, MMMU-Pro-standard, MMMU-Pro-vision), improving +4.62 points over GRPO (45.95), +2.27 over DAPO (48.30), and +0.70 over GSPO (49.87). On the larger Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct backbone, CARE reaches 82.1 on MathVista-mini and 46.7 on MMMU-Pro-standard, setting new state-of-the-art among open models in the paper's reproducible setting. The anchored-contrastive objective alone accounts for 84.4% of the total gain on the 7B backbone, with Reflection-Guided Resampling contributing the remaining 15.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE is compared against three RLVR baselines—GRPO, DAPO, and GSPO—all trained on the same 49.3K-prompt mixture with identical rollout budget (G=8) and decoding settings on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. CARE wins on every benchmark against all three baselines: over GRPO the gains range from +5.8 (MATH-Vision) to +1.4 (MMMU-val), yielding a +4.62 macro-average improvement; over DAPO the gains are +2.1 to +3.2 per benchmark (+2.27 macro); and over GSPO the margins are smaller but consistent at +0.6 to +1.1 per benchmark (+0.70 macro). The largest absolute gains appear on MATH-Vision-full (+6.9 over GRPO, +3.2 over GSPO) and MMMU-Pro-vision (+4.3 over GRPO, +0.7 over GSPO), suggesting the anchored-contrastive mechanism is most beneficial on harder, more compositional visual reasoning. On the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, CARE extends the Instruct model by +4.9 on MathVista-mini and +7.6 on MathVerse-mini, and achieves the best reported MMMU-Pro-standard score (46.7) among open models in the table, surpassing MiMo-VL-7B-RL (46.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE is a direct extension of GRPO's group-relative policy optimization, replacing full-group z-score normalization with an anchored-contrastive subgroup that isolates the best rollout against semantically proximate hard negatives, and adding negative-penalty scaling to suppress over-sharpening. It departs from the broader RLVR line (DAPO, GSPO, Dr.GRPO) by reframing the training objective as failure-centric: rather than treating all rollouts symmetrically, it explicitly mines near-miss errors via cosine-embedded rationale proximity and converts them into structured contrast signal. The Reflection-Guided Resampling step introduces a training-time, one-shot self-repair mechanism that has no direct analogue in prior RLVR or multimodal RL work (Vision-R1, MMR1, VL-Rethinker), which rely on outcome-only rewards or separate reward models rather than in-loop corrective resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The failure-centric framing offers a principled way to extract learning signal from the most common training scenario in RLVR—groups where most rollouts fail—without inflating test-time compute. The mechanistic signature (√K′/1/√K′ advantage scaling) gives practitioners a diagnostic tool for verifying that the contrastive objective is behaving as intended. Open questions include whether the cosine-based negative selector generalizes beyond math/geometry reasoning to open-ended visual tasks, whether RGR's benefit persists at larger model scales (only tested up to 8B), and whether the 49.3K-prompt training mixture is sufficient for broader generalization. The modest +0.70 gain over GSPO suggests diminishing returns as RLVR baselines mature.
