## Problem

Reconstructing hidden people from diffuse wall reflections (NLOS imaging) is severely underdetermined: the indirect light signal is extremely weak, entangled with sensor non-linearities, and the 2D-to-hidden-space mapping lacks sufficient constraints. Existing diffusion-based generative priors introduce stochasticity that conflicts with the reproducibility required for a reliable sensing system, and prior methods treat wall measurements as deterministic feature maps without modeling pixel-wise uncertainty, preventing principled data-consistency enforcement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263919/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired multi-exposure wall sequences (K calibrated illumination levels each for empty and occupied scenes) captured in RAW format with a fixed Sony A7SII camera at an L-junction corner. After radiometric linearization and per-pixel exposure-weighted differencing via a lightweight ELA adapter, the input becomes a single 3×H×W differential state response tensor. The output is a deterministic hidden-scene RGB image (posterior mean μ) together with a pixel-wise precision map Λ, where the precision encodes spatial confidence in the reconstruction.

## Method

SLD-Net operates in two coupled stages. First, DeLi-Inversion jointly regresses an initial reconstruction μ₀ and a pixel-wise precision field Λ₀ from the differential response, trained with a heteroscedastic Gaussian negative log-likelihood that couples accuracy and confidence (inaccurate pixels are pushed toward low precision). Second, SiCo-Diffusion reframes DDIM sampling (η=0) as a deterministic posterior solver by injecting a precision-weighted ascent step x̃₀ = x̂₀ + γₜΛ₀(μ₀ − x̂₀) at each denoising step, so high-precision wall regions act as hard constraints while low-precision regions are shaped by the generative prior. A final annealed Bayesian fusion combines the DeLi likelihood (μ₀, Λ₀) and the diffusion prior estimate (μ_P, Λ_P) via precision-weighted averaging with a temperature τ that anneals from near-zero to 1, yielding the posterior mean and precision. The entire pipeline is deterministic for a fixed x_T initialization, ensuring identical wall observations always produce identical reconstructions (similarity-consistency).

## Results

On Reflect-Corridor, SLD-Net achieves PSNR 15.58 dB, SSIM 0.67, LPIPS 0.30, and FID 73.54, outperforming the best generic baseline (Restormer: PSNR 13.62, FID 264.91) and the best NLOS-specific baseline (Phasor Field: PSNR 14.01, FID 387.46). On Reflect-Room, SLD-Net reaches PSNR 12.49 dB, SSIM 0.71, LPIPS 0.25, and FID 26.89, again surpassing all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., FID 26.89 vs. 177.05 for Restormer and 372.93 for Phasor Field). The most dramatic gains are in FID, where SLD-Net reduces the score by 3.6× on R-C and 6.6× on R-R relative to the strongest non-diffusion baseline, indicating substantially better perceptual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLD-Net outperforms all 11 baselines on PSNR, LPIPS, and FID across both datasets. Against the strongest generic baseline (Restormer), SLD-Net gains +1.96 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 264.91 to 73.54 on R-C, and +1.84 dB PSNR with FID dropping from 177.05 to 26.89 on R-R. Against the best NLOS-specific method (Phasor Field), SLD-Net improves PSNR by +1.57 dB (R-C) and +0.47 dB (R-R) while cutting FID by over 5× on both datasets; however, Phasor Field retains a slight SSIM edge on R-C (0.71 vs. 0.67). The diffusion-based baselines (vanilla DDIM and DPS) are substantially weaker, with DPS achieving only 8.32 dB PSNR and 96.37 FID on R-C, confirming that naive diffusion guidance without calibrated likelihood is insufficient for this low-SNR inverse problem. Physics-inspired methods (DGUNet, DeepSN-Net) and other NLOS approaches (NLOST, NOD-LTM, NLOS-OT) all fall 1–4 dB below SLD-Net in PSNR and exhibit FID scores 2–7× higher, indicating that neither unfolded optimization nor task-specific NLOS architectures can match the perceptual quality of the likelihood-guided diffusion framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of steady-state NLOS imaging and diffusion-based inverse problem solving. It builds directly on DDIM's deterministic sampling trajectory, but departs from prior diffusion-for-inverse-problems work (DPS, DDIM+CFG) by replacing global heuristic guidance with a learned heteroscedastic pseudo-likelihood: the DeLi-Inversion module regresses both a mean reconstruction and a pixel-wise precision map from multi-exposure wall differentials, which is then injected as a precision-weighted energy term into the DDIM update. The annealed Bayesian precision fusion—treating the DeLi estimate and the diffusion prior as two diagonal-covariance Gaussian factors whose product yields the posterior—differs from standard CFG or projection-based data-consistency steps by making the measurement constraint spatially adaptive and probabilistically calibrated rather than a single global scalar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that faint, multi-exposure wall reflections around an L-junction are computationally invertible into recognizable human reconstructions using only a standard camera, which is practically significant for surveillance and safety applications where specialized time-resolved hardware is infeasible. The deterministic (similarity-consistent) property—identical wall observations always yield identical reconstructions—addresses a real reproducibility concern for sensing systems that stochastic samplers cannot guarantee. Key limitations include the requirement for paired empty/occupied sequences under calibrated illumination, a fixed corner geometry, and the modest absolute PSNR (~12–15 dB), suggesting the method is best suited for detection/localization rather than high-fidelity imaging. Open questions include robustness to unknown photometric nonlinearities, extension to arbitrary NLOS layouts, and whether the precision-weighted guidance principle transfers to other ill-posed imaging tasks beyond NLOS.
