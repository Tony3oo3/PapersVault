## Problem

Existing visual-guided VLA models face a fundamental modality gap between high-dimensional pixel observations and low-level robot actions, making it difficult to extract task-relevant motion signals from predicted future frames. Additionally, jointly optimizing visual prediction and action generation introduces competing objectives that destabilize training, while the 'predict-then-act' paradigm incurs prohibitive inference latency for high-frequency manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262008/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction, a single RGB observation frame, and the robot's proprioceptive state (position, rotation, gripper force). The VLM backbone autoregressively emits a sequence of discrete tokens indexing a shared codebook; these tokens are simultaneously decoded into a future-frame video (horizon T) and a continuous action trajectory (horizon H) parameterized as DCT coefficients of Δx, Δθ, and ΔF. At deployment, the visual decoder is discarded and only the VLM backbone plus action decoder remain, yielding 60 Hz action-level throughput on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

VITA constructs a cross-modal vector-quantization framework in which a DINOv2+M-Former visual encoder and a DCT+MLP action encoder both project into a single shared codebook, establishing a unified discrete latent space without requiring paired video-action supervision during warmup. A Gemma-2B VLM backbone (with SigLIP visual tokenizer) then autoregressively generates codebook-index tokens under a progressive attention scheme (input → textual subtask CoT → cross-modal tokens), and a single token stream is routed to two parallel decoders for future-frame reconstruction and action trajectory recovery. Training follows a three-stage regimen: (1) self-supervised warmup of modality-specific autoencoders and the shared codebook on independent video and action data, (2) co-training of the VLM backbone with both decoders on a mixed stream of unlabeled human videos and synchronized robot trajectories, and (3) fine-tuning of only the action decoder on target datasets. The key design insight is that visual prediction serves as an implicit inductive bias regularizing action generation rather than an explicit decoding target, filtering motion-irrelevant pixel details while preserving dynamics-relevant structure.

## Results

On CALVIN ABC-D, VITA achieves an average of 4.73 consecutive tasks completed (out of 5), surpassing the previous best UniVLA (4.41) by 7.3% and UP-VLA (4.08) by 15.9%. On LIBERO, VITA reaches 96.7% average success across four suites, outperforming UniVLA (95.5%) and Pi0-FAST (85.5%). On SimplerEnv, VITA attains 57.4% (Google Robot) and 71.5% (WidowX) average success rates. In real-world evaluation on UR-5e, VITA achieves 80.5% average success across six tasks, substantially outperforming Pi0 (53.5%) and GR00T N1.5 (44.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITA outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark. On CALVIN, VITA (4.73) leads UniVLA (4.41) by 7.3%, DeFI (4.51) by 4.9%, and Pi0 (3.92) by 20.7%, with the largest gap over GR-1 (3.06) and OpenVLA (3.27). On LIBERO, VITA (96.7%) surpasses UniVLA (95.5%) by 1.2%, Pi0-FAST (85.5%) by 13.1%, and CoT-VLA (81.1%) by 19.2%, with the most dramatic gain on LIBERO-Long (96.8% vs. 69.0% for CoT-VLA). On SimplerEnv, VITA beats DeFI (57.4% vs. 51.2%) on Google Robot and UniVLA (71.5% vs. 69.8%) on WidowX, with a particularly large jump on 'Pick Carrot on Plate' (68.8% vs. OpenVLA's 20.8%). In real-world evaluation, VITA (80.5%) dominates Pi0 (53.5%), GR00T N1.5 (44.6%), OpenVLA (43.4%), and Octo (29.8%), maintaining 66.9–71.3% on OOD tasks where baselines drop to 17–48%. VITA also achieves lower inference latency than CoT-VLA (60.6 ms vs. 168.2 ms at Len=12), confirming that the implicit visual CoT avoids the 'predict-then-act' computational overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

VITA builds directly on the Pi0 architecture (SigLIP + Gemma backbone) and adopts FAST's DCT-based action tokenization, but departs fundamentally from the 'predict-then-act' paradigm used by CoT-VLA, UniVLA, and DeFI, where visual prediction and action generation are separate sequential or parallel streams. Instead, VITA introduces a shared vector-quantization codebook that projects both visual motion features and action trajectories into a unified discrete latent space, so that a single autoregressive token sequence from the VLM backbone is simultaneously decoded into future frames and robot actions. This positions VITA as a unification of the forward-dynamics (video prediction) and inverse-dynamics (action reconstruction) literatures that prior visual-guided VLAs kept architecturally separate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shared-codebook design offers a clean solution to two persistent problems in visual-guided VLAs: the modality gap between pixel-level predictions and low-level motor commands, and training instability from competing reconstruction objectives. The 60 Hz inference throughput (comparable to non-CoT baselines) while retaining visual dynamics as an inductive bias makes the approach practically deployable for high-frequency manipulation. Open questions include whether the shared discrete latent space scales to multi-embodiment or dexterous settings, how sensitive performance is to codebook size and the warmup data mix, and whether the implicit CoT benefit persists when the VLM backbone is scaled up or down significantly.
