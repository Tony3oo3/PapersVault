## Problem

Existing VLM-LLM model merging methods enforce strict one-to-one positional layer alignment and apply uniform merging weights across all layers, ignoring substantial functional disparities between VLM and LLM layers at the same position. This rigid paradigm risks misaligning incompatible functionalities and introducing detrimental parameter combinations that degrade performance, while uniform weights fail to reflect varying compatibility across layer pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263636/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input. The VLM first generates a descriptive caption of the image, which is then fed to the LLM alongside the question; both models independently process their respective inputs to extract per-layer hidden representations. The output is a text answer produced by the dynamically merged VLM, where the merging plan (which layers to pair and at what weights) is computed fresh for each image-question pair rather than being pre-computed, making the approach input-adaptive.

## Method

Chain-of-Merging (CoM) is a training-free, three-stage framework that adaptively merges a specialized LLM into a VLM's language model per input. In the Adaptive Layer Matching stage, all VLM-LLM layer pairs are scored using a combination of Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA with linear and RBF kernels) for structural consistency and cosine similarity on pooled representations for semantic coherence, with a normalized positional distance penalty to discourage implausible cross-layer pairings; pairs below a threshold τ_match are discarded. In the Dynamic Weight Merging stage, each surviving pair receives a layer-specific weight linearly mapped from its normalized matching score into [w_min, w_max], and parameters are fused via Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP) on the unit hypersphere, which preserves geometric structure without requiring a base model. This SLERP-based approach reduces peak GPU memory from six 7B models (task-vector paradigm) to three, a 50% reduction, while maintaining comparable merging quality.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B merged with Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CoM achieves 71.0% on MathVista, 25.69% on MathVision, 50.25% on Mathverse, and 58.22% on DynaMath, yielding a 4.4% average improvement over the baseline (68.2/18.09/47.54/53.85) across the four mathematical reasoning benchmarks. CoM also improves on multi-domain reasoning (M3CoT: 66.82 vs. 60.74 baseline) while preserving visual perception (MMStar: 64.6 vs. 63.9; Charxiv: 42.6 vs. 42.5). For Llava-NeXT-8B merged with R1-Distill-Llama, CoM reaches 39.7% on MathVista and 45.56% on M3CoT, outperforming the baseline (37.4/43.18) and all prior merging methods. On larger models (32B, 72B), CoM continues to lead, e.g., 40.95% on MathVision for Qwen2.5-VL-72B vs. 37.83% for Linear Merging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoM consistently outperforms both layer-wise merging baselines—Linear Merging (ICML'25) and IP-Merging (NeurIPS'25)—across all seven benchmarks for both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Llava-NeXT-8B. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B + Qwen2.5-Math, CoM gains +1.5 to +2.5 points over IP-Merging on math benchmarks (e.g., 71.0 vs. 69.5 on MathVista, 25.69 vs. 23.19 on MathVision) and +1.5 points on M3CoT (66.82 vs. 65.35). A critical distinction is that Linear Merging degrades visual perception (MMStar drops from 63.9 to 63.5; Charxiv from 42.5 to 41.2), whereas CoM maintains or improves these scores (64.6 and 42.6), confirming that adaptive layer matching avoids introducing incompatible parameter combinations. Against inference-time methods (MCTS, Predictive Decoding, ICoT, DyFo) evaluated on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CoM achieves the best accuracy on all three benchmarks (71.0/25.69/66.82) at 2.71e+13 FLOPs, which is 15× cheaper than MCTS (4.05e+14) and 6.8× cheaper than Predictive Decoding (1.85e+14), while ICoT and DyFo trail by 1.5–23.5 points. On the 72B scale, CoM still leads Linear Merging by +3.12 points on MathVision (40.95 vs. 37.83) and +1.29 on M3CoT (73.55 vs. 72.26), though the margin narrows relative to 7B, suggesting diminishing returns as base model capacity grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model merging literature (task arithmetic, TIES-merging, model soups) from LLMs to vision-language models, specifically targeting the integration of specialized reasoning LLMs into VLM language backbones. It departs from prior VLM-LLM merging work (IP-Merging, linear merging for visual reasoning) by rejecting the strict positional layer correspondence and uniform weight assumptions, instead introducing CKA-based structural matching with semantic cosine similarity for adaptive cross-positional pairing, and SLERP for memory-efficient interpolation. The use of Centered Kernel Alignment as a layer-compatibility metric is borrowed from representation similarity analysis (Kornblith et al.) and repurposed here as a merging-planning signal rather than a diagnostic tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoM offers a practical, training-free route to enhance VLM reasoning without retraining, and the 50% peak-memory reduction from SLERP (84 GB → 42 GB for 7B models) makes the approach more deployable on commodity hardware. However, the method requires identical architectures between VLM and LLM, limits its applicability to same-family model pairs, and incurs roughly 3.2× the inference FLOPs of the baseline due to loading both models and computing hidden representations per query. Open questions include whether adaptive matching generalizes to architecturally dissimilar VLM-LLM pairs, whether the per-query merging plan can be cached or amortized, and whether the diminishing returns observed at 72B scale indicate a fundamental ceiling for parameter-merging-based reasoning transfer.
