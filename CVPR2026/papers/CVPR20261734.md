## Problem

Agentic photo editing models suffer from two compounding failures: (1) text-only chain-of-thought reasoning creates an information bottleneck where the model cannot observe or verify intermediate edited images during planning, leading to instruction hallucination; and (2) reinforcement learning against a static reward model enables reward hacking, where the policy exploits reward function flaws rather than genuinely improving editing quality. No existing framework addresses both the perception gap during reasoning and the reward instability during self-improvement simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a source RGB image and a bilingual (English/Chinese) text editing instruction, then produces a preservatively edited image by sequentially invoking 200+ Adobe Lightroom tools (global adjustments, local masks, color grading, etc.) via the A2L protocol. Unlike generative editors that synthesize new pixels, JarvisEvo modifies the original image in-place, so outputs maintain pixel-level content fidelity. The full output also includes an interleaved multimodal reasoning trajectory (text hypotheses, tool calls, intermediate edited images) and a structured self-evaluation with aesthetic quality and instruction-following scores on a 1–5 scale.

## Method

JarvisEvo (built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, interleaved multimodal CoT (iMCoT) replaces text-only reasoning with a closed perception–action loop: the model generates a text hypothesis, executes a Lightroom tool call to produce an actual intermediate image, observes that image, and then reflects before the next step. Second, Synergistic Editor-Evaluator Policy Optimization (SEPO) runs two co-adaptive GRPO loops: the editor loop uses the model's own evaluation scores as intrinsic pairwise-preference rewards (with selective loss masking to exclude self-evaluation tokens from the loss, preventing information leakage), while the evaluator loop is trained on human-annotated scores with a Gaussian score-alignment reward (RLVR-style), grounding the editor's self-rewards against human judgment and curbing self-deception. Third, an on-policy reflection pipeline automatically converts superior-vs-inferior trajectory pairs into supervised reflection samples for a final fine-tuning stage, teaching the model to detect and correct its own editing errors. Training proceeds in three stages: 200K-sample SFT, SEPO on 10K+10K samples, and 5K-sample reflection fine-tuning, all on 32 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On ArtEdit-Bench-Lr (English set), JarvisEvo achieves the best scores across all five metrics: L1×10² = 7.82, L2×10³ = 12.45, SC = 8.53, PQ = 9.03, and overall O = 8.77, outperforming Nano-Banana by an average of 18.95% and by 44.96% on pixel-level content fidelity (L1/L2). On the Chinese set, JarvisEvo similarly leads with L1×10² = 7.63, L2×10³ = 11.54, SC = 8.54, PQ = 9.04, O = 8.76. For self-evaluation on ArtEdit-Bench-Eval, JarvisEvo attains SRCC = 0.7243 and PLCC = 0.7116, surpassing the strongest baseline (Gemini-2.5-Flash at SRCC = 0.6188) by over 10 points. In pairwise human evaluation, JarvisEvo achieves a 49% win rate versus Nano-Banana's 28%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JarvisEvo dominates all 14 baselines on every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest commercial competitor Nano-Banana, JarvisEvo reduces L1 by 32% (7.82 vs 11.54) and L2 by 56% (12.45 vs 28.34) while also leading on SC (8.53 vs 8.33), PQ (9.03 vs 8.92), and O (8.77 vs 8.59). Against GPT-Image-1, the pixel-fidelity gap is even larger (L1: 7.82 vs 21.20; L2: 12.45 vs 82.77), reflecting the advantage of Lightroom-based preservative editing over generative approaches. Among open-source agent-based editors, JarvisEvo outperforms its predecessor JarvisArt (L1: 7.82 vs 13.21; O: 8.77 vs 7.89) and MonetGPT (L1: 7.82 vs 9.46; O: 8.77 vs 7.62). On the evaluation task (Table 2), JarvisEvo's SRCC of 0.7243 exceeds Gemini-2.5-Flash (0.6188), Qwen3-VL-256B (0.5706), and task-specific IQA models like VisualQuality-R1 (0.5645) and Q-Insight (0.5369), indicating its self-assessment aligns more closely with human judgment than dedicated assessment models. In the human pairwise study, JarvisEvo wins 49% of comparisons against Nano-Banana (28%), a 21-point margin, confirming the quantitative gains are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

JarvisEvo extends the agentic photo-editing line initiated by JarvisArt (reusing its A2L protocol for 200+ Lightroom tools) and MonetGPT, but departs fundamentally from text-only CoT reasoning by introducing interleaved multimodal CoT (iMCoT), where the model observes intermediate edited images during its reasoning loop rather than relying on a single initial encoding. For optimization, it builds on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) but introduces a dual-loop SEPO framework that hybridizes RLIF (self-rewarding) and RLVR (verifiable reward) paradigms, addressing the static-reward-model limitation of RLHF and the self-deception risk of pure RLIF. The base model is Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, fine-tuned through a three-stage SFT → SEPO → reflection fine-tuning pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SEPO framework offers a principled recipe for self-improving agents without external reward models, and the ablation evidence (editor scores inflating while actual performance degrades without the evaluator loop) concretely demonstrates the reward-hacking failure mode that plagues RLHF-based editing. However, the evaluation is conducted on a self-constructed benchmark (ArtEdit-Bench), the reflection pipeline depends on Gemini-2.5-Pro as an oracle, and the Lightroom tool integration limits generalizability to preservative editing tasks. Open questions include whether SEPO scales to generative editing or video, whether the dual-loop optimization remains stable at larger model scales, and whether the iMCoT advantage persists when the editing toolset is less deterministic than Lightroom sliders.
