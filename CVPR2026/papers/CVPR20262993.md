## Problem

Existing agent-based long-video QA frameworks are fundamentally correlation-driven: they iteratively search for clips that appear semantically related to the question without first articulating what must be true in the video for each candidate answer to hold. This reactive retrieval leads to semantic drift, cascading reasoning errors, and expensive trial-and-error cycles that never explicitly check whether gathered evidence supports or refutes a candidate answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a long video (frames sampled at 1 fps) and a multiple-choice question as input. Frames are first converted to textual captions via a clip-based captioner (LaViLa for EgoSchema, CogAgent for NextQA/IntentQA), then condensed into a query-conditioned summary; frame-level captions are reserved exclusively for temporal grounding during verification, while the summary drives all global reasoning. The output is a multiple-choice answer accompanied by a transparent reasoning chain that documents which hypotheses were tested, what visual evidence was observed, and how the final decision was reached.

## Method

VideoHV-Agent reformulates long-video QA as a structured hypothesis-verification process executed by four cooperating LLM agents (all GPT-4o). In Stage 1, a Thinker agent rewrites each answer option into an explicit testable hypothesis naming key entities, actions, and temporal-causal constraints, while a Judge agent distills a single discriminative clue capturing the minimal visual observation needed to distinguish among competing hypotheses. In Stage 2, a Verifier agent uses the clue to localize a small temporal window via frame captions, then invokes fine-grained GPT-4o captioning on at most five frames to produce a structured verification status (VERIFIED, PARTIAL, NOT_VERIFIED) with a rationale. A self-refinement loop regenerates sharper hypotheses and clues when verification is inconclusive, and a final Answer agent integrates all validated evidence with the summary to produce the answer. The key design principle is 'thinking before finding': the system commits to what must be observed before searching, replacing open-ended correlation-based retrieval with targeted, logically grounded evidence collection.

## Results

VideoHV-Agent achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot accuracy on all three long-video benchmarks: 81.0% on EgoSchema (500-question validation), 80.7% on NextQA validation (71.2% on the harder ATP-hard subset), and 75.6% on IntentQA. On the NextQA ATP-hard subset, it outperforms the strongest supervised baseline (LinVT-Qwen2-VL, 69.1%) by 2.1 points while remaining zero-shot. The method also achieves the lowest per-question latency (123.66 s) among compared agent-based systems, versus 129.46 s (VideoAgent) and 160.21 s (VideoTree).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EgoSchema, VideoHV-Agent (81.0%) edges out the closest competitor VideoAgent2 (80.6%) by 0.4 points and surpasses VideoMultiAgents (75.4%) by 5.6 points. On NextQA, it leads all zero-shot baselines on both the full validation set (80.7% vs. VideoAgent2's 80.5%) and the ATP-hard subset (71.2% vs. 68.2% for VideoAgent2 and 69.1% for the supervised LinVT-Qwen2-VL), indicating stronger causal/temporal reasoning. On IntentQA, VideoHV-Agent (75.6%) beats VideoAgent2 (73.9%) by 1.7 points and VideoINSTA (72.8%) by 2.8 points. Across all three benchmarks, the gap over retrieval-based or CoT-only agents (VideoAgent, VideoTree, LLoVi, SeViLA) is consistently 7–18 points, supporting the claim that hypothesis-verification outperforms correlation-driven retrieval. The method also matches or exceeds supervised methods (e.g., LLaVA-OV 80.2%, VideoChat2 79.5% on NextQA) without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant correlation-driven retrieval paradigm in agent-based long-video QA (VideoAgent, VideoAgent2, TraveLER, VideoMultiAgents), where agents iteratively search for semantically related clips and re-plan based on what is found. Instead, it reframes the reasoning process as a structured hypothesis-verification pipeline: a Thinker formulates testable hypotheses from answer options, a Judge distills a discriminative clue, and a Verifier grounds that clue in targeted video evidence before an Answer agent integrates the results. The multi-agent architecture and frame-level captioning infrastructure follow prior work (VideoAgent, VideoAgent2, LLoVi), but the key conceptual shift is from reactive, correlation-based search to deliberate, hypothesis-guided verification with explicit verification states (VERIFIED/PARTIAL/NOT_VERIFIED) that drive self-refinement loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hypothesis-verification framing offers a principled alternative to the trial-and-error retrieval loops that plague existing video agents, and the ablation (7% drop without verification status) supports that the mechanism is functionally necessary rather than cosmetic. The practical appeal is the simultaneous gain in accuracy and efficiency (123.66s vs. 129–160s for competitors), suggesting that focused, clue-guided evidence collection can outperform broader iterative search. However, the method's heavy reliance on GPT-4o as the backbone for all four agents limits reproducibility and raises open questions about whether the paradigm transfers to open-weight or smaller LLMs, and whether the gains hold on open-ended (non-multiple-choice) video QA or substantially longer videos beyond the 3-minute EgoSchema clips.
