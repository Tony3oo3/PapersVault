## Problem

Existing video pre-training methods for reinforcement learning model motion at a global level, treating the agent as an indivisible entity. This global modeling is tightly coupled with the agent's morphology, so learned representations fail to transfer across domains with different body structures. The paper asks whether a local modeling paradigm—decomposing global motion into morphology-agnostic local components—can yield transferable motion representations that improve cross-domain RL.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pre-training, the method consumes unlabeled video sequences (Something-Something-V2) and internally computes dense optical flow via Sea-RAFT, foreground masks via Grounded SAM, and tracked keypoint trajectories via Co-tracker, from which it crops P×P×2 local optical flow patches (Atomic Actions). During fine-tuning, it additionally receives RGB observations and the agent's real action signals to train the Action-Specific Dynamics Model. The output is a policy for continuous robotic control, with the intermediate representation being a per-frame aggregated action vector produced by the [MAT] token and a latent state z_t from the dynamics model.

## Method

The Deconstruct-Recompose Paradigm (DRP) first deconstructs global motion into Atomic Actions by tracking motion-salient keypoints and cropping local optical flow patches around them. A Dual-Attention Encoder (DAE), a Transformer trained with a Masked Autoencoder objective, learns local motion representations by applying Intra-Frame self-attention (spatial relationships among parts within a frame) and Inter-Frame causal self-attention (temporal trajectories of each part) over tokenized Atomic Actions augmented with coordinate and temporal embeddings. In the Recompose phase, a learnable Motion Aggregation Token [MAT] prepended to each frame's token sequence aggregates local representations into a single action vector, which conditions a Dreamer-style latent dynamics model (posterior, prior, image decoder) trained with reconstruction and KL objectives to imbue the representations with dynamic semantics. At fine-tuning, the DAE and dynamics model are progressively adapted, and a lightweight adapter plus a new Action-Specific Dynamics Model conditioned on the agent's real actions bridges the pre-trained local motion knowledge to the downstream action space.

## Results

On DMControl Remastered (DMCR), DRP achieves 681±39 on Walker Run and 796±114 on Hopper Stand, substantially outperforming IPV (595±67 and 634±128) and all other baselines in both sample efficiency and asymptotic return. On Meta-World, DRP achieves state-of-the-art success rates across all six manipulation tasks, notably mastering the challenging 'Dial Turn' task where baselines struggle, and significantly improving sample efficiency on 'Drawer Open', 'Button Press Topdown wall', 'Reach', and 'Door Lock'. The ablation in Table 1 further shows that removing the pre-trained dynamics model degrades DRP by ~10–11%, whereas removing IPV's dynamics model has only a ~1–2% impact, confirming that DRP's gains come from learned transferable motion dynamics rather than static representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRP outperforms all six baselines (DreamerV2, DreamerV3, APV, IPV, PreLAR, DisWM) on both DMCR and Meta-World in both sample efficiency and asymptotic performance. On DMCR, DRP's Walker Run score of 681±39 exceeds IPV's 595±67 by ~14% and DreamerV3's by a wider margin, while on Hopper Stand DRP reaches 796±114 versus IPV's 634±128 (~25% improvement). On Meta-World, DRP is the only method to successfully master 'Dial Turn', a task where all baselines plateau at low success rates, and it shows the fastest convergence on four of six tasks. The 'Global Flow' ablation (replacing local Atomic Actions with a single global flow embedding) fails to beat IPV, confirming that the advantage comes specifically from the local deconstruction strategy rather than the overall framework. PreLAR and APV, which also model motion at a global level, show consistently weaker transfer than DRP across both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two established lines of work: Dreamer-style latent dynamics models (RSSM) for model-based RL, and unsupervised video pre-training for RL (APV, IPV, PreLAR, DisWM). All prior pre-training methods model motion at a global level, treating the agent as an indivisible entity, which the authors argue couples learned representations to morphology and hinders cross-domain transfer. DRP departs from this by introducing a local motion modeling paradigm: it deconstructs global optical flow into morphology-agnostic 'Atomic Actions' (local flow patches around tracked keypoints) and learns representations via a Dual-Attention Encoder, then recomposes them through a Motion Aggregation Token within a Dreamer-style latent dynamics model. The pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (Sea-RAFT, Grounded SAM, Co-tracker) for the deconstruction step, while the MAE-based DAE training and the adapter-based fine-tuning schedule are novel architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that local motion components (e.g., arm swing, body rotation) are more transferable across agents than global motion—provides a principled rationale for why prior global-modeling pre-training methods struggle with morphology mismatch, a long-standing practical limitation in video pre-training for RL. The clean ablation showing that global optical flow modeling fails to beat global image modeling (IPV), while local flow modeling significantly outperforms both, validates the deconstruction hypothesis. Limitations include reliance on a multi-tool extraction pipeline (optical flow + segmentation + tracking) that adds engineering complexity, evaluation limited to simulated robotic benchmarks with relatively simple agents, and unclear scalability to agents with highly variable numbers of local components or to real-world deployment where keypoint tracking may be unreliable.
