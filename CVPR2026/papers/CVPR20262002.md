## Problem

Diffusion models offer strong restoration priors but face three barriers in infrared imaging: RGB-learned priors introduce physically implausible textures inconsistent with thermal radiation, iterative denoising incurs prohibitive latency for real-time sensing, and task-specific adaptation demands costly full-model fine-tuning. No existing method simultaneously addresses physical fidelity, single-step efficiency, and flexible multi-task transfer for infrared perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded infrared image at 512×512 resolution, where degradation is an arbitrary mixture of thermal noise, sensor non-uniformity, and atmospheric effects. It outputs a restored infrared image in one forward pass; the same restored image can then be fed to frozen downstream evaluators (YOLOv8 for detection, SegFormer for segmentation) or a small-target detector, with task selection controlled entirely by a 1×512 learnable prompt vector rather than architectural changes. The dynamically estimated timestep (a scalar in [0, 1000]) is the only per-sample adaptive quantity, computed from VAE encoder features.

## Method

The framework builds on SD-Turbo as a frozen generative backbone and reformulates infrared restoration as a single-step diffusion inversion: a lightweight MLP head over multi-scale VAE features regresses the log-SNR of the input, mapping each degraded image to its optimal position on the diffusion trajectory so that one denoising step suffices. A wavelet-domain spectral regularization term enforces consistency in inter-band energy distribution across scales, preserving intrinsic thermal signatures without matching absolute radiometric values. For task adaptation, a task-aware LoRA decomposes weight updates into shared rank-r bases A, B and a task-specific bottleneck C generated by a two-layer hypernetwork from a learnable prompt p_τ; adapting to a new task freezes all shared parameters and optimizes only the 512-dimensional prompt. Training combines a base reconstruction loss (MSE + LPIPS + GAN), the spectral constraint, and a task-specific semantic alignment loss, with weights adjustable to prioritize visual fidelity or downstream accuracy.

## Results

On the HM-TIR benchmark under composited degradations, the proposed method achieves the best PSNR of 27.92 dB (Normal) and 25.68 dB (Hard), the lowest LPIPS (0.137 / 0.186), and the lowest DISTS (0.098 / 0.123), surpassing the strongest baseline PPFN by 2.68 dB in PSNR on the Normal subset. For downstream perception, it attains an mAP of 0.718 on M³FD (detection) and an mIoU of 0.447 on FMB (segmentation), outperforming PPFN by 10.7 mAP points and ResShift by 11.8 mIoU points. In terms of efficiency, the single-step design reduces FLOPs to 1.191T, a 27× reduction over SUPIR (32.577T) and a 10× reduction over DiffBIR (11.796T).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine restoration baselines on PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS, and AHIQ across both Normal and Hard degradation levels; the closest competitor PPFN leads only on SSIM (0.8160 vs. 0.8021 Normal; 0.7644 vs. 0.7572 Hard). Among generative-prior methods, SUPIR and DEAL perform worst (PSNR < 18 dB), confirming that multi-step diffusion without infrared-specific constraints introduces severe artifacts. DiffBIR and ResShift, the strongest diffusion-based baselines, trail the proposed method by 5.77 dB and 5.86 dB in PSNR (Normal), while UniRestore, despite its task-oriented design, lags by 5.14 dB. On downstream detection (M³FD), the method achieves 0.718 mAP versus PPFN's 0.611 and MoCEIR's 0.604; on segmentation (FMB), it reaches 0.447 mIoU versus ResShift's 0.329 and MoCEIR's 0.321. For small-target detection (IRSTD-1K, NUAA-SIRST), prompt-only adaptation of the frozen backbone surpasses the dedicated SCT baseline on both P_d and mIoU, demonstrating that the task-aware LoRA mechanism generalizes to unseen tasks without retraining the shared parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SD-Turbo's adversarial diffusion distillation to enable single-step inference, departing from the iterative denoising paradigm used by DiffBIR, ResShift, and SUPIR. It extends the infrared restoration line (PPFN, DEAL, MoCEIR) by introducing a physically-motivated spectral regularization in the wavelet domain and a dynamic timestep estimator that maps real-world degradation to a position on the diffusion trajectory. The task-aware LoRA with a hypernetwork-generated modulation operator draws on the broader parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature but repurposes it for cross-task infrared perception, where prior methods required full-model retraining per task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in infrared sensing: leveraging powerful generative priors without the latency of iterative sampling or the physical inconsistency of RGB-trained models. The 10–27× FLOPs reduction and prompt-only task adaptation make the approach deployable in resource-constrained perception pipelines. Open questions include whether the single-step formulation generalizes to video sequences, whether the wavelet-domain spectral constraint transfers across different infrared bands (MWIR vs. LWIR), and whether the dynamic timestep estimator remains calibrated under out-of-distribution sensor degradations not seen during training.
