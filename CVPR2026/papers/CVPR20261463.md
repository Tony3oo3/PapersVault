## Problem

Video Diffusion Transformers rely on uniform spatiotemporal patchification that assigns the same token granularity to all regions regardless of content complexity, producing substantial redundant computation in static or visually simple areas. Prior adaptive patchification methods operate at too coarse a level (per-timestep or per-prompt) or depend on heuristic entropy signals that are not jointly optimized with the generative objective, limiting their ability to make routing decisions that actually benefit denoising quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a video at 360p resolution (higher resolutions also supported) using a 2B-parameter DiT with 50 sampling timesteps. Internally, the router consumes the noisy 3D VAE latent representation (not raw pixels or explicit timestep embeddings) and outputs a discrete patch-size map over fixed (2,4,4) spatiotemporal regions. The unpatchify layers restore the token sequence to the original latent resolution so the 3D VAE decoder can reconstruct the video without architectural changes.

## Method

DynaPatch introduces a lightweight 3-layer MLP router (hidden dim 1024) that predicts one of three patch sizes—(1,2,2), (2,2,2), or (1,4,4)—per spatiotemporal region from the noisy VAE latent, with the noise level implicitly encoding the denoising timestep. The router is trained end-to-end with the DiT backbone via three jointly optimized losses: (i) the standard diffusion denoising loss propagated through a Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of the discrete routing decision, (ii) an attention-guided alignment loss that matches the router's soft probability for the finest patch to a region-level attention map extracted from data-selected DiT layer-head pairs, and (iii) a differentiable token-budget regularizer that steers the expected token count toward a target reduction ratio. Learnable patchify/unpatchify linear layers handle embedding and reconstruction at each granularity, while positional encodings for coarser patches are obtained by averaging the encodings of their constituent finest-grained patches, preserving spatiotemporal consistency across mixed patch sizes.

## Results

On VBench, DynaPatch achieves a Total Score of 83.42 at 30% token reduction (vs. 83.61 for the no-reduction baseline) while delivering a 1.5× speedup, and 82.19 at 40% reduction with 1.8× speedup. The Quality Score remains within 0.2–1.7 points of the baseline across all reduction levels, and the Semantic Score degrades by at most 3.3 points at 40% reduction. DynaPatch consistently outperforms all three compared methods (FlexiDiT, D²iT, SPViT) by 1.2–4.2 points in Total Score at every token reduction rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaPatch outperforms all three baselines across every token reduction rate on VBench Total Score. At 30% reduction, DynaPatch (83.42) beats FlexiDiT (80.25) by 3.17 points, D²iT (81.08) by 2.34 points, and SPViT (79.20) by 4.22 points. The advantage grows with higher reduction: at 40%, DynaPatch leads FlexiDiT by 2.52, D²iT by 3.81, and SPViT by 3.85 points. FlexiDiT, which only varies patch size across timesteps, cannot adapt to spatial complexity within a frame and shows the steepest quality drop. D²iT relies on static entropy-based routing that does not reflect generative importance during denoising, while SPViT's per-frame token pruning introduces temporal inconsistency. DynaPatch's content-aware, jointly-optimized routing preserves fine details in salient regions while compressing static areas, yielding the best quality–speed tradeoff among all compared methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaPatch extends the line of dynamic patchification work in diffusion transformers (Lumina-Video, FlexiDiT, CAT, APT, D²iT) by moving from coarse timestep-level or prompt-level scheduling, and from heuristic entropy-based region routing, to fine-grained spatiotemporal region-wise patch size selection. The key conceptual departure is that the lightweight MLP router is jointly optimized end-to-end with the diffusion denoising objective via Straight-Through Gumbel-Softmax, rather than being supervised by static entropy labels or prompt complexity scores. An attention-guided alignment loss further grounds routing decisions in the DiT's own learned saliency patterns, and a differentiable token-budget regularizer replaces hard token-count constraints with a soft, content-adaptive formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video DiT models remain computationally prohibitive in practice (e.g., >300 s for a 5-second 720p clip on 8×A100), so any method that reduces token count while preserving fidelity has direct practical value. DynaPatch's model-agnostic design (learnable patchify/unpatchify layers plug into any DiT backbone) makes it broadly applicable, and the 1.3–1.8× speedup at near-baseline VBench scores demonstrates that content-aware tokenization is a viable axis for scaling video diffusion. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to substantially longer videos or higher resolutions where temporal redundancy is more pronounced, whether the three-patch-size decision space is sufficient for all content types, and how the method interacts with other efficiency techniques (e.g., sparse attention, distillation) that are increasingly being combined in production pipelines.
