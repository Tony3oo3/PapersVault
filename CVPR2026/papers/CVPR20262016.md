## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval benchmarks (CIRR, FashionIQ, CIRCO) rely on single ground-truth answers and recall-based metrics, making them blind to false-positive retrieval, sensitivity to instruction phrasing, and multi-image compositional reasoning. PinPoint addresses these gaps by introducing explicit hard negatives, multiple ground truths (avg. 9.1 per query), six paraphrase variants, and multi-image queries at a scale of 7,635 queries and 329K relevance judgments across 23 domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query pairs one or two reference images (13.4% are multi-image) with a natural-language modification instruction, evaluated against a 109,601-image retrieval corpus. Unlike prior benchmarks with a single target, every query carries an average of 9.1 annotated positives and 32.8 explicit hard negatives, plus six paraphrase variants that share identical annotations. Evaluation reports mAP@10, a ΔmAP@10 measuring degradation when hard negatives are added, Negative Recall@10 for false-positive frequency, and a linguistic sensitivity range computed as the max–min mAP across the six paraphrases.

## Method

The primary contribution is the PinPoint benchmark, constructed via a multi-LLM pipeline (GPT-5, Claude 4 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Pro) that generates diverse modification instructions, paraphrases, and candidate relevance labels, with all final annotations requiring unanimous LLM agreement and human verification. For the empirical study, 20+ models spanning four paradigms (CLIP baselines, CIR-specific dual-encoders, proxy-based text generation, and MLLM-based retrievers) are evaluated zero-shot. The paper further proposes a training-free pointwise reranker built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: for each candidate image, the MLLM receives the query image, instruction, and candidate, and produces yes/no logits whose difference is passed through a sigmoid to yield a relevance probability that re-sorts the first-stage results. This drop-in reranker consistently improves mAP@10 and reduces Negative Recall@10 across all tested methods, with the best configuration (MMRet-MLLM-S1 + reranking) reaching 0.290 mAP@10 and 0.056 Negative Recall@10.

## Results

On PinPoint (7,635 queries, 109,601-image corpus), the best zero-shot method without reranking is GPT-5-Text-Premerge at 0.266 mAP@10, followed by MMRet-MLLM-S1 at 0.224. With the proposed training-free MLLM reranker (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), MMRet-MLLM-S1+Reranking achieves the best overall mAP@10 of 0.290 and the lowest Negative Recall@10 of 0.056, while also showing the smallest ΔmAP (2.01%) indicating minimal sensitivity to explicit hard negatives. CLIP baselines (Meta CLIP2 variants) achieve only 0.017–0.044 mAP@10, and all models suffer 48–72% performance drops on multi-image queries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMRet (Megapairs) is the strongest dedicated CIR method, with MMRet-MLLM-S1 achieving 0.224 mAP@10, outperforming MagicLens-CLIP-L (0.155), LinCIR (0.110), and Pic2Word (0.046) by 44%, 104%, and 386% respectively. GPT-5 text-proxy retrieval (0.266 mAP@10 for Premerge) surpasses all CIR-specific methods without reranking, suggesting that frontier LLM reasoning compensates for the lack of learned composition. The proposed MLLM reranker consistently improves every method: MMRet-MLLM-S1 jumps from 0.224 to 0.290 (+29%), MagicLens-CLIP-L from 0.155 to 0.231 (+49%), and Meta CLIP2-Combined from 0.044 to 0.087 (+98%), while also reducing Negative Recall@10 by 20–50% across the board. However, reranking worsens linguistic sensitivity by 10–30% (e.g., MagicLens-L from 0.182 to 0.201), and multi-image performance remains severely degraded (48–72% drop) regardless of reranking. CLIP baselines (Meta CLIP2) are far behind all CIR methods on mAP@10 (0.044 vs. 0.224 for MMRet-S1), but their text-only variant shows the lowest Negative Recall@10 (0.018) without reranking, highlighting a precision-safety tradeoff that CIR specialization worsens.

## Relation to Prior Work

PinPoint positions itself as a direct response to the evaluation gaps in established ZS-CIR benchmarks (CIRR, CIRCO, FashionIQ, MERIT), which rely on single ground-truth answers, lack explicit negatives, and ignore multi-image composition and linguistic variation. Rather than proposing a new retrieval architecture, the paper repurposes the two-stage retrieval-plus-reranking paradigm (previously applied in text-only IR and multimodal retrieval) by plugging an off-the-shelf MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as a training-free pointwise reranker on top of any first-stage CIR encoder. The evaluation spans the full spectrum of existing CIR method families—dual-encoder CLIP baselines, instruction-tuned models (MagicLens, MMRet), concept-mapping approaches (Pic2Word, LinCIR), and LLM-generated text proxies—making it a comprehensive empirical study rather than a methodological contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes three failure modes invisible to prior evaluations: a persistent precision-safety tradeoff where stronger CIR models retrieve more false positives, a 25.1% performance swing across paraphrases suggesting overfitting to specific phrasings, and a 48–72% collapse on multi-image queries that no current architecture handles well. The practical takeaway is that a simple training-free MLLM reranker closes much of the false-positive gap across all tested methods, offering an immediately deployable intervention. Open questions remain foundational: how to architecturally support multi-image composition, how to train for linguistic robustness without sacrificing peak accuracy, and whether supervised fine-tuning on PinPoint-style data (multiple positives, explicit negatives, interleaved images) would resolve these gaps. Limitations include zero-shot-only evaluation, English-only queries, and multi-image queries fixed to two images.
