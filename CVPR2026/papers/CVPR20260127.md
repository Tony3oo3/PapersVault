## Problem

Existing few-shot acoustic synthesis methods treat RIR prediction as a deterministic mapping, producing a single output even though sparse scene context (a handful of recordings plus geometry) leaves the true RIR fundamentally ambiguous. These methods also require 8–20 reference recordings, limiting their applicability in data-scarce settings. The paper asks whether a probabilistic generative model can capture the distribution of plausible RIRs under minimal context and outperform deterministic baselines with far fewer shots.

## Input / Output

FLAC takes K monaural RIR recordings (converted to magnitude spectrograms), their corresponding source positions expressed in the target receiver's local coordinate frame, a single equirectangular panoramic depth map captured at the target receiver pose, and the target source-receiver pair. The depth map is converted to 3D coordinates via equirectangular projection, and reflection maps are computed by subtracting each source position from these coordinates before encoding. The output is a monaural, omnidirectional RIR at 22,050 Hz for the target source-receiver configuration in a potentially unseen room.

## Method

FLAC is a conditional latent generative model trained with rectified flow matching: a VAE (4 strided-conv blocks with Snake activations, 32-dim latent) compresses RIRs, and a 12-block DiT (8 heads, width 256) predicts the velocity field transporting noisy latents toward the data distribution. Multimodal conditioning is injected through two channels—AdaLN modulates self-attention and feedforward layers with the noise timestep and target pose, while cross-attention attends to tokens from three encoders: a jointly trained ResNet-18 for acoustic spectrograms, sinusoidal positional embeddings for 3D source coordinates, and a fine-tuned DINOv3 ViT-S/16 for geometric reflection maps. Classifier-free guidance (ω=1) is used at inference, and generation requires only a single ODE step for best perceptual metrics. The companion AGREE module is a CLIP-style dual-encoder (fine-tuned VAE encoder + DINOv3 ViT-S/16) trained with a contrastive objective to align RIR and geometry embeddings in a shared space, enabling zero-shot cross-modal retrieval and geometry-consistency evaluation via recall and Fréchet distance metrics.

## Results

On unseen AcousticRooms scenes, FLAC with a single reference RIR (K=1) achieves 9.95% T60 error, 1.046 dB C50 error, and 40.04 ms EDT error, outperforming the 8-shot xRIR baseline (9.98%, 1.354 dB, 49.40 ms) on all perceptual metrics. FLAC also dramatically surpasses xRIR on geometry-consistency retrieval: R@5 of 18.92% vs. 2.00% and R@10 of 26.87% vs. 3.38%. On the real-world Hearing-Anything-Anywhere dataset, FLAC (K=8) achieves 3.10% T60 and 84.52 ms EDT, beating the per-scene-trained Diff-RIR (3.74%, 88.09 ms) while using no room-specific training. A listening study with 46 participants confirms FLAC (1-shot) is preferred over xRIR (8-shot) in 93.01% of cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against xRIR (the strongest few-shot baseline), FLAC with K=1 matches or beats xRIR with K=8 on all perceptual metrics (T60: 9.95% vs. 9.98%; C50: 1.046 vs. 1.354 dB; EDT: 40.04 vs. 49.40 ms) and dominates on geometry-consistency retrieval (R@5: 18.92% vs. 2.00%), using 8× fewer reference RIRs. Fast-RIR is clearly outperformed by FLAC on every metric at both K=1 and K=8, with T60 errors of 18.97%/17.71% versus FLAC's 9.95%/8.60%. On the real-world HAA dataset, FLAC (K=8) beats the per-scene-trained INRAS on all metrics (T60: 3.10% vs. 6.61%; EDT: 84.52 vs. 158.07 ms) and edges out Diff-RIR on T60 (3.10% vs. 3.74%) and EDT (84.52 vs. 88.09 ms), though Diff-RIR retains an advantage on R@5 (26.97% vs. 17.41%) and FD_G (0.263 vs. 0.585), likely because it is trained per-room with 12 references. Overall, FLAC is the only method that simultaneously achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality and geometry-consistent retrieval, and it does so with a single reference RIR.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLAC sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) few-shot RIR synthesis, where prior methods (xRIR, Few-ShotRIR, MAGIC) learn deterministic mappings from sparse audio-geometry context to a single RIR; (2) generative flow matching and latent diffusion, which FLAC adapts from image/audio generation (rectified flow, DiT, VAE-based latent spaces) to the explicit RIR domain for the first time; and (3) CLIP-style contrastive embeddings, repurposed here as AGREE to align RIRs with panoramic geometry in a shared space. The key conceptual departure from prior few-shot work is treating RIR synthesis as a conditional generative problem that models the full distribution of plausible RIRs rather than collapsing to a point estimate, thereby capturing the inherent ambiguity when scene context is sparse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that one RIR recording plus geometry can outperform eight recordings from prior deterministic SOTA, FLAC establishes a new data-efficiency benchmark for cross-room acoustic synthesis and validates that probabilistic modeling is essential when context is underconstrained. The uncertainty analysis—showing that low-frequency variance persists longer and aligns with the Schroeder frequency transition from sparse to dense modes—provides a physically grounded interpretation of what the generative model is learning. Open questions include the sim-to-real gap (the VAE was not fine-tuned on real recordings, and HAA's simplified geometry annotations limit transfer), the single 22.05 kHz sample rate, and whether AGREE's embedding generalizes beyond the AcousticRooms/HAA domains to broader audio-visual tasks.
