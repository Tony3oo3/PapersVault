## Problem

Transformer-based multi-view 3D reconstruction faces a fundamental scalability bottleneck: the quadratic cost of self-attention grows rapidly with the number of input views, making it impractical to process the tens to hundreds of images needed for large-scale scenes. Existing remedies—replacing attention with linear-complexity Mamba blocks, relying on full global attention, or restricting attention to local view neighborhoods—each sacrifice either expressiveness, scalability, or global 3D consistency. Additionally, full global attention suffers from attention dilution in long-context settings, causing performance to plateau or degrade as view count increases.

## Input / Output

The model takes N posed RGB images (16–256 views at 960×540) as input, where each image is concatenated with its 9D Plücker ray map to form a 12-channel posed-image tensor, then patchified into non-overlapping patches with four register tokens appended per view. The output is a dense per-pixel set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity, color) parameterized with spherical harmonic coefficients for view-dependent appearance, which can be rendered to synthesize novel views. The entire pipeline runs in a single forward pass, achieving sub-second inference on a single H100 GPU for up to 128 views.

## Method

MVP introduces a Dual Attention Hierarchy that structures attention along two complementary axes across three stages. The inter-view hierarchy progressively expands the attention scope from frame-wise (Stage 1) through group-wise (Stage 2, with a fixed group size of 4 views) to full global attention (Stage 3), while the intra-view hierarchy simultaneously downsamples spatial tokens by 2× per stage (patch sizes 8→16→32) and doubles the embedding dimension (256→512→1024), forming a feature pyramid. A Pyramidal Feature Aggregation module fuses the multi-scale outputs from all three stages via top-down residual convolutional fusion before a single linear head predicts the 3D Gaussian attributes. The key insight is that this dual hierarchy prevents the number of tokens participating in any single attention operation from growing excessively, thereby avoiding attention dilution and maintaining stable multi-view reasoning as input view count scales well beyond the training range. Training uses a three-stage curriculum (low-res/fixed views → high-res/fixed views → high-res/mixed views) with MSE + perceptual loss and an opacity regularization term, and the model is trained with PRoPE camera encoding and FlashAttention3.

## Results

On the DL3DV benchmark, MVP achieves 25.96 PSNR / 0.847 SSIM / 0.187 LPIPS at 32 input views in 0.17 s, outperforming iLRM (24.30 / 0.803 / 0.256, 0.53 s) and Long-LRM (23.97 / 0.778 / 0.267, 0.84 s) by a wide margin. At 128 views, MVP reaches 29.02 PSNR in 0.77 s while iLRM drops to 22.98 PSNR (5.61 s) and Long-LRM to 21.24 PSNR (6.39 s), demonstrating strong scalability. At 256 views, MVP attains 29.67 PSNR in 1.84 s—within 0.7 dB of the optimization-based 3D-GS baseline (30.39 PSNR, 8 min)—while Long-LRM runs out of memory and iLRM degrades to 20.63 PSNR in 20.92 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP consistently outperforms both feed-forward baselines (iLRM and Long-LRM) across all view counts and all metrics on DL3DV, with the gap widening as the number of input views increases: at 128 views, MVP leads iLRM by 6.04 dB PSNR and Long-LRM by 7.78 dB PSNR while being 7–8× faster. Against the optimization-based 3D-GS baseline (30K steps, 8 min), MVP is slightly behind in PSNR (29.02 vs. 29.43 at 128 views; 29.67 vs. 30.39 at 256 views) but is over 250× faster. On zero-shot Tanks&Temples and Mip-NeRF360, MVP leads both baselines by 0.9–3.3 dB PSNR at 32 views and by 2.1–3.8 dB at 128 views, confirming that the advantage grows with context length. On the low-resolution RE10K dataset (4 and 8 views), MVP-fine achieves 32.12 and 33.40 PSNR respectively, surpassing CLiFT (30.13 / 29.68) and iLRM (30.37 / 28.90). Long-LRM fails entirely at 192 and 256 views due to GPU memory exhaustion (80 GB limit), whereas MVP handles 256 views in 1.84 s.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVP generalizes VGGT's Alternating-Attention module into a unified inter-view hierarchy that spans frame-wise, group-wise, and global attention, while simultaneously introducing an intra-view spatial hierarchy inspired by Swin Transformer's progressive token merging. It departs from prior scalable multi-view reconstruction methods (Long-LRM, iLRM, LVT) that each address the quadratic attention bottleneck through a single mechanism (Mamba blocks, compact scene tokens, or local-view windows) by instead combining both view-level and spatial-level hierarchies in a single architecture. The Pyramidal Feature Aggregation module extends the Dense Prediction Transformer's top-down fusion to the three-stage hierarchical setting, and the use of PRoPE for camera encoding and Plücker ray maps for pose conditioning follows the DUSt3R/VGGT lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that full global attention across many views actually degrades reconstruction quality due to attention dilution, and that a hierarchical design not only scales efficiently but continues to improve as views increase (32→256) while baselines saturate or degrade — a useful diagnostic for the field. Practically, processing 128 views at 960×540 in under 1 second on a single H100, and 256 views in ~2 seconds within 0.7 dB of optimization-based 3D-GS, makes large-scale feed-forward 3D reconstruction viable for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the fixed group size of 4 and the reliance on COLMAP-estimated poses limit applicability to pose-free or dynamic settings, and whether the hierarchical design transfers to other multi-view tasks beyond 3DGS reconstruction.
