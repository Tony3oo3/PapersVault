## Problem

Context-dependent segmentation tasks (camouflaged object detection, medical lesion segmentation, etc.) require pixel-level annotations that are extremely costly (~10 min/image) and contain many redundant, easily distinguishable samples that contribute little to model generalization. No prior work has addressed coreset selection specifically for CD tasks, where the defining challenge is the subtle foreground-background relationship rather than class identity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263632/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images annotated only with a small number of foreground points (≈2 s/image vs. ≈10 min for pixel masks) and produces a pruned coreset of images plus a per-sample transport cost score. The points are consumed by the Attenuation Diffusion Process to construct a spatially decaying attenuation matrix that localizes noise to the foreground region. The final coreset is then used to train a standard UNet segmentation model with cross-entropy + IoU loss, with no data augmentation applied.

## Method

The pipeline decomposes coreset selection into sample evaluation and selection. For evaluation, an Attenuation Diffusion Process (ADP) selectively perturbs the foreground by adding Gaussian noise modulated by a distance-based attenuation matrix centered on the annotated points, with an adaptive stopping criterion based on Wasserstein divergence between successive noisy foreground states. A UNet reconstruction network then transports the background distribution back toward the foreground, and the average projected 1-D Wasserstein distance between the reconstructed and original images yields a per-sample transport cost (importance score). For selection, the Maximum Distance Entropy (MDE) strategy first drops the lowest-cost (easiest) samples, splits the remainder into k groups by cost rank, and within each group greedily selects samples that maximize Wasserstein distance from a running centroid vector, ensuring both coverage of the full cost spectrum and spatial diversity within each group.

## Results

Across six context-dependent segmentation tasks, the proposed method (using only point annotations) achieves IoU of 73.4 (SOD), 52.0 (COD), 53.8 (MIS), 57.7 (RSIS), 82.0 (TOD), and 71.8 (SD) at a 40% pruning rate, incurring only ~1% IoU loss relative to full training on most tasks. At the more aggressive 80% pruning rate, the method still leads all baselines by 2–5% IoU (e.g., 66.2 vs. 62.1 on SOD, 48.4 vs. 45.1 on RSIS). Notably, all five baselines require full pixel-level annotations, whereas the proposed method needs only sparse point labels (~2 s/image vs. ~10 min/image for pixel masks).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines (Entropy, Forgetting, EL2N, CCS, TFDP) on every task at every pruning rate, and it does so using only point annotations while all baselines require full pixel-level labels. The closest competitor is TFDP, which is the only method designed for segmentation-style pruning; at 40% pruning the gap ranges from 1.5% (MIS: 53.8 vs. 52.3) to 3.3% (TOD: 82.0 vs. 78.7) IoU. At the more aggressive 80% pruning rate the advantage widens to 2.5–5.5% IoU (e.g., SOD 66.2 vs. 62.1, COD 39.3 vs. 33.8, RSIS 48.4 vs. 45.1). The classification-derived baselines (Entropy, Forgetting, EL2N, CCS) are consistently the weakest, trailing the proposed method by 2.5–6.5% IoU at 40% pruning, with CCS and Forgetting performing worst on COD and MIS. The ablation in Table 3 further confirms that the MDE selection strategy contributes up to 3.1% IoU over a simple TailK selection at 80% pruning, and that the ADP-based transport cost closely approximates the GT-based upper bound (66.2–74.8% vs. 66.9–75.0% on SOD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by applying coreset selection specifically to context-dependent (CD) segmentation, a domain where prior pruning methods (Entropy, Forgetting, EL2N, CCS, TFDP) were designed for classification or generic segmentation without exploiting foreground-background relationships. Conceptually, it repurposes optimal transport—previously used in CV for image reconstruction and domain adaptation—as a sample-scoring mechanism, and adapts diffusion-model perturbation ideas into a targeted Attenuation Diffusion Process (ADP) that selectively destroys foreground rather than the full image. The Maximum Distance Entropy selection strategy draws on k-center and coverage-centric coreset selection literature but introduces a Wasserstein-distance-based greedy diversity criterion within cost-ranked groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for CD segmentation practitioners: point-based annotation (~2 s/image) replaces pixel-level labeling (~10 min/image) while retaining within 1% IoU at 40% pruning, making large-scale CD dataset curation feasible. The finding that GT+MDE at 10% pruning slightly exceeds full-data training (75.0% vs 74.8% on SOD) reinforces that easy, redundant samples can actively harm generalization—a useful principle for dataset curation beyond CD tasks. Open questions include whether the ADP+OT pipeline scales to video or 3D medical volumes, how sensitive the method is to the number and placement of foreground points in highly cluttered scenes (TOD, RSIS), and whether the approach transfers to other weakly-supervised settings such as scribble- or box-annotated CD tasks without architectural changes.
