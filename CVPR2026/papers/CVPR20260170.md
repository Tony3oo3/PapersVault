## Problem

Pre-trained open-vocabulary object detection models achieve strong zero-shot performance on general benchmarks like OV-COCO but suffer severe degradation on vertical-domain datasets (e.g., ArTaxOr, DIOR, UODD) due to scarce, semantically limited category labels that produce weak textual representations. Existing fine-tuning approaches that improve downstream performance simultaneously cause catastrophic forgetting of open-vocabulary capability, and prior prompt-based methods lack multi-aspect textual descriptions of visual semantics, resulting in poor vision-language alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of category label strings as input. The image is processed by a LoRA-augmented Swin Transformer backbone to produce multi-scale feature maps (S=3 scales), from which global average-pooled vectors are extracted for prompt selection. The output consists of predicted bounding boxes with class labels, where the classification score is computed via Kronecker product between query embeddings and text embeddings. During inference, the Semantic-Aware Router makes a binary routing decision (reconstruction error vs. threshold τ=0.039) to determine whether to use domain-specific augmented semantics or the original pre-trained representations.

## Method

HSA-DINO builds on OV-DINO and introduces two core components for parameter-efficient adaptation. The Multi-Scale Prompt Bank (MSPB) maintains N=10 learnable (key, prompt) pairs; at each of S=3 feature pyramid scales, cosine similarity selects the most relevant key, and the corresponding M=12 learnable prompt vectors are concatenated with the category label embedding before text encoding, enabling hierarchical coarse-to-fine semantic augmentation. The Semantic-Aware Router (SAR) explicitly decomposes input features into domain statistics (mean, std) and a normalized content embedding, trains an autoencoder to reconstruct only the content, and uses the reconstruction error against a fixed threshold to route between augmented and original pre-trained semantics at inference. Training uses LoRA on the image encoder with auxiliary matching loss (pulling selected keys toward image features) and orthogonal loss (diversifying prompt embeddings), while the SAR autoencoder is trained separately with MSE reconstruction loss.

## Results

Under the parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) setting, HSA-DINO achieves the highest harmonic mean H across all three downstream tasks: 60.5 on ArTaxOr, 53.0 on DIOR, and 49.6 on UODD, surpassing the second-best method (ZiRa) by 3.3, 2.8, and 2.0 points respectively. A key advantage is that HSA-DINO preserves open-vocabulary generalization: on ArTaxOr, mAP_coco remains at 49.9 (vs. 50.6 zero-shot OV-DINO) while mAP_tgt jumps from 1.4 to 76.8. On the expanded OV-COCO+ benchmark, HSA-DINO achieves 52.3, 50.1, and 50.5 mAP when jointly evaluated with ArTaxOr, DIOR, and UODD respectively, outperforming the runner-up by up to 9.2 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among PEFT baselines, ZiRa [3] is the closest competitor, achieving H values of 57.2/49.9/46.5 on ArTaxOr/DIOR/UODD, but HSA-DINO outperforms it by 3.3/2.8/2.0 H points while maintaining mAP_coco above 49 (vs. ZiRa's 42.8–46.3). OV-DINO with LoRA [29] suffers severe catastrophic forgetting (mAP_coco drops to 24.0/13.5/46.3), yielding H values of only 36.8/22.1/47.6. MR-GDINO [5] shows extreme domain-specificity, with mAP_coco collapsing to 0.1 on UODD (H=0.2). Under full fine-tuning, YOLO-World-X [1] and GLIP-L [14] lose nearly all open-vocabulary capability (mAP_coco ≈ 0–7.6), while GroundingDINO-T [17] retains moderate generalization (H=51.0/46.5/36.7) but still trails HSA-DINO. For textual augmentation ablations, CoOp [39]+SAR and AttriCLIP [30]+SAR achieve H_mean of 52.1 and 53.0 respectively, both below MSPB+SAR's 54.4. For routing, DDAS [33] (which does not explicitly model content vs. domain) yields H_mean of 46.2, substantially below SAR's 54.4, confirming that explicit domain-content decomposition is critical for accurate routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSA-DINO builds directly on OV-DINO as its detection backbone and extends the prompt-learning line of work (CoOp, CoCoOp, L2P, AttriCLIP) by replacing single-scale global feature-based prompt selection with a multi-scale prompt bank that draws hierarchical semantics from the Swin Transformer's feature pyramid. It also departs from the DDAS/MoEAdapter4CL routing mechanism, which relies on raw reconstruction error to identify domain, by explicitly decomposing features into content and domain statistics to reduce cross-domain overlap in reconstruction errors. The parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy (LoRA on the image encoder only) positions the work within the broader PEFT-for-OVOD trend initiated by ZiRa and MR-GDINO, but distinguishes itself by targeting the textual side of the vision-language alignment rather than the detection branch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: pre-trained OVOD models collapse on vertical-domain datasets (arthropod taxonomy, remote sensing, underwater scenes) while fine-tuning destroys their open-vocabulary capability on general domains. The semantic-aware router offers a clean, inference-time mechanism to preserve generalization without sacrificing downstream accuracy, and the multi-scale prompt bank provides a reusable recipe for enriching textual representations with image-derived hierarchical semantics. Limitations include evaluation on only three downstream datasets, a fixed routing threshold, and dependence on downstream training data for both LoRA and the autoencoder. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dozens of heterogeneous domains, whether the router can be made fully training-free, and whether the multi-scale prompt selection generalizes to other vision-language tasks beyond detection.
