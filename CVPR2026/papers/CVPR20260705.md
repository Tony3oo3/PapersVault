## Problem

Existing video LLM reasoning methods either rely on complex multi-stage inference pipelines with auxiliary models for key-frame selection (making them computationally expensive and task-specific) or produce single-stage reasoning traces that lack explicit temporal grounding, leading to temporal inconsistencies. No prior approach integrates frame-level references directly into a single-shot, text-only chain-of-thought structure while also providing an efficient, scalable data generation pipeline for training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260705/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a uniformly sampled sequence of 30 video frames interleaved with textual identifiers (e.g., 'Frame-1', 'Frame-2') in the InternVL encoding format, along with a natural-language question. It produces a single-stage output consisting of a natural-language reasoning trace that explicitly cites specific frame IDs (e.g., 'In Frame 3, the red cube moves left...') followed by the final answer. The frame-position identifier is agnostic to video duration and sampling frequency, and the model can process videos of arbitrary length despite the fixed 30-frame sampling at inference.

## Method

Chain-of-Frames (CoF) fine-tunes video LLMs to generate reasoning traces with explicit frame references in a single inference pass, eliminating auxiliary frame-selection networks. Training data (COF-DATA, 164k samples) is generated from two sources: real videos from VideoEspresso, where Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct converts frame-aligned captions into question/reasoning/answer triplets, and synthetic CLEVRER videos, where manual templates produce quantitative questions (object count, appearance order, relative distance) with exact frame-grounded answers at near-zero cost. The approach leverages the InternVL format that already interleaves frames with text identifiers, making frame references a natural extension of the input encoding. Fine-tuning is applied via full parameter updates (InternVL2.5-4B) or LoRA (InternVL3-8B) with the vision encoder frozen, and the model learns to selectively reference frames only when temporally relevant rather than uniformly across all questions.

## Results

CoF-InternVL3-8B achieves an average accuracy of 72.1% across five video understanding benchmarks, improving 5.1% over the base InternVL3-8B (67.0%) and outperforming all open-source baselines including Qwen2-VL-72B (62.7%) and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (58.0%). It attains the best scores on VSI-BENCH (51.3) and MVBENCH (77.1), surpassing closed-source GPT-4o (34.0 on VSI-BENCH) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (48.8 on VSI-BENCH). CoF-InternVL2.5-4B improves 3.8% over its base model (64.6% vs 60.8% average) and surpasses several larger 7B open-source models. On NEXTQA, CoF-InternVL3-8B reaches 87.3% accuracy (+4.9% over base), substantially outperforming M-LLM's +0.8% improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoF-InternVL3-8B (72.1% avg) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines: Qwen2-VL-72B (62.7%), LLaVA-OneVision-72B (58.0%), and all 7B models, despite having fewer parameters than the 72B competitors. Against closed-source models, it beats GPT-4o on VSI-BENCH (51.3 vs 34.0) and Gemini-1.5-Pro on VSI-BENCH (51.3 vs 48.8) and MVBENCH (77.1 vs not reported), while remaining slightly behind Gemini on VIDEO-MME (73.7 vs 75.0) and GPT-4o on EVENTHALLUSION (78.7 vs 91.9). CoF-InternVL2.5-4B (64.6% avg) surpasses Qwen2-VL-7B (58.0%), LLaVA-OneVision-7B (53.2%), and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (50.2%), and in some cases even closed-source GPT-4V/4T (43.7 on MVBENCH). Against prior video CoT methods, CoF-InternVL3-8B achieves +7.8% on VIDEO-MME and +4.9% on NEXTQA over its base, compared to M-LLM's +0.6% and +0.8% respectively, and Video-of-Thought's +9.7% on NEXTQA (but from a weaker 7B base). The ablation (Table 3) confirms that explicit frame references in CoF traces are critical: removing them (SFT with CoT) drops EVENTHALLUSION from 71.2% to 53.1%, while QA-only fine-tuning degrades performance on most benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the chain-of-thought reasoning paradigm from text-only LLMs (Kojima et al., 2022) to video understanding by introducing explicit frame references within a single-stage reasoning trace. It departs from prior video CoT methods in two key ways: unlike multi-stage approaches (Video-of-Thought, VideoEspresso, M-LLM) that rely on auxiliary models for key-frame selection and scene-graph construction, CoF embeds temporal grounding directly in natural-language reasoning without any additional inference modules. It also improves upon single-stage video CoT (VideoCoT) by ensuring frame-level temporal alignment in training data, avoiding the temporal inconsistencies that arise from non-aligned video descriptions. The method is specifically designed to exploit the frame-identifier format (Frame-1, Frame-2, …) introduced by InternVL models, making it a natural extension of that architecture's design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoF offers a practical, low-overhead path to improving video LLM reasoning without the computational and architectural complexity of multi-stage pipelines, making it attractive for practitioners who want to fine-tune existing open-source video models. The finding that synthetic CLEVRER data alone—despite a large distributional gap—transfers effectively to real-world benchmarks suggests a scalable, low-cost data generation strategy for video reasoning. However, the approach is limited to video LLMs operating at fixed frame rates (e.g., InternVL), leaving open the question of adaptation to dynamic-frame-rate models like Qwen2.5-VL. Scaling the training data and model size, and validating the synthetic-data transfer finding across more architectures, are natural next steps.
