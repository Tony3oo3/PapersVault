## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models suffer from exposure bias: they train on clean ground-truth context frames but must condition on their own imperfect generated frames at inference, causing errors to compound and quality to drift over long horizons. Prior mitigations rely on bidirectional teacher models, distribution-matching losses that suppress diversity, or back-propagation through time, each introducing significant practical or quality trade-offs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (static or dynamically updated per chunk for multi-prompt generation) and optionally an input video clip for extension, producing long-horizon videos at 832×480 resolution and 16 FPS (up to 50 seconds in evaluation). Generation operates in the latent space of a 3D VAE (81 pixel frames mapped to 21 latent frames) with a block-causal attention mask of 3 latent frames per chunk, and long-video inference uses a sliding window with full KV-cache recomputation per window rather than a rolling cache.

## Method

BAgger's key insight is that reversing the model's own drifted autoregressive rollouts yields valid corrective trajectories, since time-reversed videos remain in-distribution for text-conditioned video models (with a corresponding prompt modification, e.g., 'a person walking' → 'a reversed video of a person walking'). Following a DAgger-style iterative loop, each round samples drifted rollouts from the current model, reverses them, aggregates them with the seed dataset, and fine-tunes using the standard Diffusion Forcing objective—no teacher, no distribution-matching loss, no BPTT. For corrective samples, the first 4 chunks are kept clean as a 'drifted' prefix (no loss computed on them), and the DF loss is applied only to the remaining 3 chunks, teaching the model to recover from its own error states. The architecture is a causal diffusion transformer (Wan2.1 1.3B) with block-causal attention, trained for 16K steps per round on 27K corrective trajectories aggregated with 55K seed clips from Pexels.

## Results

On 50-second text-to-video generation evaluated with VBench, BAgger Round 3 achieves the best subject consistency (84.05), background consistency (89.58), per-frame aesthetic score (55.35), and imaging quality (63.41) among all compared methods. Crucially, it also exhibits the lowest drift: ΔAesthetic of 3.29 and ΔImaging of 3.57, compared to 5.12/7.34 for Diffusion Forcing and 8.83/14.56 for History Guidance. Against open-source AR models (Table 2), BAgger attains a motion quality score of 87.59, substantially exceeding Self Forcing (73.93), MAGI-1 (73.11), and SkyReels-V2 (71.87), while its frame quality (59.38) trails only Self Forcing (64.81), which benefits from distillation from a 14B teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAgger Round 3 outperforms Diffusion Forcing (σ=0) on subject consistency (+3.35), background consistency (+1.84), aesthetic (+2.30), imaging (+3.44), and both drift metrics (ΔAesthetic 3.29 vs 5.12; ΔImaging 3.57 vs 7.34), though it trails slightly on dynamic degree (76.56 vs 81.25). Against History Guidance, BAgger wins decisively on every metric except smoothness, with the largest gaps in dynamic degree (+17.18), aesthetic (+6.11), and ΔImaging (3.57 vs 14.56), indicating History Guidance's anchoring to drifted context actually worsens error accumulation. Compared to the open-source Self Forcing model (distilled from a 14B teacher), BAgger achieves substantially higher motion quality (87.59 vs 73.93) but lower frame quality (59.38 vs 64.81), reflecting Self Forcing's trade-off of static, high-fidelity frames. BAgger also surpasses MAGI-1 and SkyReels-V2 on both motion and frame quality without requiring a teacher model or distillation, confirming that the DAgger-style aggregation loop with standard diffusion objectives is sufficient to close the train-test gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

BAgger repurposes the DAgger iterative loop from imitation learning—train, roll out, collect corrective data, retrain—for autoregressive video diffusion, with the key conceptual substitution that time-reversed model rollouts serve as oracle-free 'expert corrections.' It builds directly on Diffusion Forcing [12] as its training objective and departs from the Self Forcing / distribution-matching line of work [30, 76] by eliminating the need for a bidirectional teacher and mode-seeking losses, instead relying on the standard score/flow-matching objective applied to reversed corrective trajectories. The assumption that the video data manifold is closed under time reversal (with prompt modification) is what makes this oracle-free correction valid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on a large teacher and distribution-matching objectives, BAgger offers a simpler, more scalable recipe for long-horizon autoregressive video generation that preserves motion diversity—a known weakness of DMD-style distillation. Open questions include the optimal seed-to-corrective data ratio, whether continuing training from the previous round (rather than retraining from the bidirectional base) further improves stability, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the single 1.3B Wan2.1 architecture tested. The paper also notes that a few BAgger rounds may not fully cover the drifted-state space over very long horizons, and that layering few-step distillation on top could improve runtime without inheriting mode-seeking constraints.
