## Problem

Existing black-box adversarial attacks on video models rely on iterative querying, consuming hundreds to thousands of model inferences per attack, which is impractical in real-world settings and infeasible when scaling to Video-LLMs. No prior work in the video domain directly leverages DNN-extracted feature maps to shift the clean-video feature space in a zero-query manner, leaving a gap between the image-domain zero-query attack (ZQBA) and the video modality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean victim video (resized to 16 frames of 112×112, or 32 for Kinetics-400/I3D), an arbitrary attack video from any source, and a pretrained 3D-CNN (C3D or I3D) as the feature extractor. It produces a single adversarial video in [0,1]^{C×T×H×W} where a one-frame feature-map perturbation is replicated across all temporal frames. The attack video is consumed only for optical-flow computation and gradient extraction—it is never the target—while the clean video is the sole object that receives the perturbation.

## Method

FeatureFool couples Maximum Optical-Flow frame selection with Guided Back-propagation (GB) to extract a single, motion-rich feature map that serves as a universal perturbation. Dense optical flow (Farneback) is computed between consecutive frames of the attack video; the frame with the highest average flow magnitude is selected as the most motion-informative instant. GB is then applied at that frame through a pretrained 3D-CNN, where standard ReLU is replaced by Guided-ReLU during backpropagation to suppress negative gradients and yield a sharper, semantically discriminative gradient map. This single-frame gradient is scaled by α, clipped to the ℓ∞ budget ε, and broadcast (replicated) along the temporal axis to every frame of the victim video, producing a globally coherent perturbation that is robust to the varied frame-sampling strategies of Video-LLMs. The entire pipeline requires zero queries to the victim model and no iterative optimization, operating entirely offline with only a pretrained 3D-CNN.

## Results

FeatureFool achieves an attack success rate (ASR) of 70–74% against C3D and I3D video classifiers on UCF-101, HMDB-51, and Kinetics-400, using zero queries to the victim model. Adversarial videos maintain high perceptual quality with SSIM > 0.87 and PSNR > 28 dB, and low temporal inconsistency (TI ≈ 3.2–4.3). On Video-LLMs (VideoLLaMA2 and ShareGPT4Video), FeatureFool bypasses harmful-content detection with 71–76% ASR on violence/crime and pornography clips, far exceeding all query-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four query-based baselines, FeatureFool achieves comparable or superior ASR (70–74%) while requiring zero queries, whereas BSC needs ~3,000–5,100 queries, PatchAttack ~4,600–6,500, and Adv-watermark ~350–770. On ASR alone, FeatureFool slightly trails BSC on Kinetics-400/C3D (70% vs 76%) and I3D/HMDB-51 (73% vs 74%), but matches or exceeds it on all other dataset–model pairs. In perceptual quality, FeatureFool dominates: its PSNR (~29–30 dB) is 2–3× higher than Adv-watermark (~10 dB) and ~4× higher than PatchAttack (~6–7 dB), and its SSIM (>0.87) exceeds all baselines. On Video-LLMs, the gap is dramatic: FeatureFool reaches 71–76% ASR, while the strongest baseline (BSC) manages only 34–35%, and Sparse-RS/Adv-watermark stay below 17%. Cross-architecture and cross-dataset transfer experiments confirm that even when the source and victim models differ, ASR remains above 64%, validating the generality of the feature-map perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FeatureFool extends the zero-query black-box attack paradigm from the image domain (ZQBA [14]) to video, but departs fundamentally from prior video attacks by eliminating iterative model queries entirely. Its core mechanism repurposes Guided Back-propagation [44]—originally a visualization tool for CNNs—as a one-shot perturbation generator, coupled with a novel Maximum-Optical-Flow frame selection criterion that has no direct analogue in prior adversarial video work. Unlike query-based baselines (Sparse-RS, Adv-watermark, PatchAttack, BSC) that rely on hundreds to thousands of model inferences, FeatureFool extracts a single semantically rich feature map offline and broadcasts it globally, positioning itself as the first feature-map-driven, zero-query attack in the video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the zero-query constraint: as Video-LLMs are increasingly deployed behind APIs with rate limits or no gradient access, iterative attacks become infeasible, making FeatureFool's approach a more realistic threat model. The >70% bypass rate on VideoLLaMA2 and ShareGPT4Video highlights a critical safety gap—simple 3D-CNN feature maps can undermine the content-moderation capabilities of much more complex multimodal systems. However, the method's reliance on pretrained C3D/I3D as feature extractors raises open questions about whether the approach transfers to newer video backbones (e.g., SlowFast, X3D) or transformer-based Video-LLM encoders, and whether the global-broadcast perturbation strategy remains effective as Video-LLMs adopt more diverse frame-sampling and temporal-attention mechanisms.
