## Problem

Adversarial training remains the most effective defense against adversarial attacks but is computationally expensive and tied to specific attack types seen during training. Simpler defenses like Gaussian noise addition or image filtering each provide only modest robustness gains and have been studied in isolation, with no systematic analysis of whether their combination could yield supralinear improvements. This paper asks whether a theoretically grounded pairing of noise and filtering can close much of the robustness gap at a fraction of the training cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262596/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 CIFAR-10, 64×64 ImageNet10) and outputs a class label. Before the CNN sees the image, a preprocessor applies pixel-wise zero-mean Gaussian noise (variance 0.032) followed by multiple iterations of bilateral filtering (e.g., 20 iterations during training, 100 during inference for the best model). This preprocessor is applied identically during both training and inference, and the noise-then-filter ordering is the default configuration.

## Method

The paper provides a geometric analysis showing that Gaussian noise and denoising filters defeat fundamentally different shapes of adversarial attacks: noise disrupts thin filamentary perturbations distributed around the image, while filters attenuate clustered perturbations near the boundary of the allowed perturbation radius. Their combination therefore covers a wider attack space than either alone, yielding supralinear robustness gains. Practically, the preprocessor (Gaussian noise + repeated bilateral filtering) is integrated into the standard adversarial training pipeline (TRADES protocol with EDM-generated synthetic data) for Wide Residual Networks. This combination achieves +0.6% higher robust accuracy than the previous state-of-the-art on AutoAttack while using only ~35% of the training FLOPs, ~50% fewer parameters, ~33% fewer epochs, and ~15% of the synthetic data, and it scales efficiently across model sizes with 2–8× less total compute to match competing accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the best model (WRN-82-12 + Prepro.20/100) achieves 74.32% robust accuracy under AutoAttack and 73.00% under EoT AutoAttack, with 90.12% clean accuracy. This surpasses the previous state-of-the-art (WRN-94-16, 73.71% AutoAttack) by 0.61% while using only ~35% of the training FLOPs, a model with 50% fewer parameters, ~33% of the training epochs, and ~15% of the synthetic data. Against the EoT variant of AutoAttack, the preprocessor yields a 2–6% improvement over the same WRN models without it. The method also scales efficiently, matching competing models' accuracy with 2–8× less total compute across ~3 orders of magnitude of training FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the current state-of-the-art (Bartoldson et al. [4], WRN-94-16), the proposed WRN-82-12 + preprocessor achieves 74.32% AutoAttack accuracy vs. 73.71%, a +0.61% gain, while using roughly half the model size, 6× less synthetic data (50M vs. 150M), and ~33% of the training epochs. Compared to the WRN-82-12 without preprocessor from the same paper (71.41%), the preprocessor adds +2.91% AutoAttack accuracy. In the FLOPs-vs-accuracy scaling analysis (Figure 2), the proposed models consistently outperform prior work from Cui et al. [14] and Wang et al. [45] at equivalent training budgets, achieving the same target robust accuracy with only 15–50% of their training FLOPs. In the ablation comparisons, JPEG compression defenses (Das et al. [15], Dziugaite et al. [16], Guo et al. [22]) are less effective than bilateral filtering alone for adversarial perturbations and significantly weaker against Gaussian noise. Fast Adversarial Training (Wong et al. [46]) achieves 20.9% robust accuracy against the C&W attack, whereas the proposed preprocessor reaches 30.8%, though FAT is stronger against the EoT attack where the preprocessor is weakest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three previously studied defense categories—adversarial training, additive noise/randomization, and image filtering—and is the first to systematically combine bilateral filtering with Gaussian noise as a unified preprocessor applied during both training and inference. The theoretical contribution departs from prior work by providing a geometric analysis showing that noise and filters target fundamentally different shapes of adversarial perturbations (thin filaments vs. clustered spheres in image space), offering a principled justification for their combination. Empirically, it builds on the TRADES adversarial training protocol and diffusion-augmented data generation from recent SotA work [4, 45], but the core novelty is the lightweight preprocessor layered on top rather than changes to the training procedure itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is the negligible computational overhead: a simple preprocessor that can be dropped into any existing adversarial training pipeline and yields a 0.6% robust accuracy gain over SotA while using only ~35% of the training FLOPs. However, the paper honestly acknowledges diminishing returns at scale, the possibility that some gains stem from gradient masking in the bilateral filter rather than genuine robustness, and an unexplained gap between reproduced and reported baselines. Open questions include whether the geometric framework can be made more rigorous, whether the approach transfers to vision transformers, and whether the supralinear effect persists on larger, more diverse datasets beyond CIFAR-10 and ImageNet10.
