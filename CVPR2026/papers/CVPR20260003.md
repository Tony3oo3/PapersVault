## Problem

Current pathology foundation models aggregate patch-level features using strategies that lose the global spatial organization of tissue architecture, which pathologists critically assess for diagnosis and prognosis. Even large-scale vision-language models trained on hundreds of thousands of slides fail to explicitly capture higher-order topological structure such as connected components and loops that characterize tumor distribution, necrosis, and immune exclusion patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260003/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes histopathology WSIs scanned at 20× magnification, divided into 512×512 pixel patches whose embeddings are extracted by a frozen CONCHv1.5 encoder (768-dim). The output is a 768-dim slide-level embedding produced by attention pooling over ViT-processed patch tokens, alongside predicted topological descriptors (persistence histograms of 0D connected components and 1D loops, plus cluster proportions) for each of 7 K-means-identified histologic clusters. The conditional prediction mechanism requires sample patch embeddings as auxiliary input at inference time to query cluster-specific topology from the slide embedding.

## Method

TopoSlide decomposes each WSI into 7 histologically meaningful clusters via K-means with PCA initialization over CONCH patch embeddings, then characterizes each cluster's spatial arrangement using persistent homology with a distance-to-measure (DTM) filtration, yielding persistence histograms of connected components and loops. An 8-layer ViT with 12 multi-head attention blocks is trained self-supervisedly via a three-component objective: (1) a conditional topological loss that uses Linear Element-wise Attention (LEA) modules to predict cluster topology and proportions from the slide embedding conditioned on sampled patch embeddings, with Huber loss plus cumulative-sum (earth-mover) matching over persistence histograms; (2) a local topological loss that classifies which patches are death critical points and regresses their persistence values; and (3) a SimCLR-style contrastive loss with hard negative mining that pulls same-cluster patches from different slides together. This multi-task design forces the slide embedding to encode both local histologic identity and global spatial organization, achieving competitive or superior performance on retrieval, survival, and mutation tasks while training on only hundreds of slides.

## Results

On the DHMC-LUAD histologic pattern retrieval task (5 classes, majority-vote KNN), TopoSlide trained on TCGA-LUAD achieves 51.4% macro F1 at K=1 and 49.6% at K=5, outperforming the best vision-language model TITAN (44.7% and 42.9%) by up to 15% in macro F1. On survival prediction, TopoSlide (TCGA-trained) reaches 0.643 C-index on TCGA-LUAD DSS (matching GigaPath's 0.646) and 0.634 on CPTAC-LUAD PFI (comparable to TITAN's 0.636). For gene mutation and biomarker linear probing, TopoSlide achieves the best AUROC in its category (0.636 on CPTAC-LUAD, 0.641 on TCGA-BRCA), competitive with or exceeding all foundation models trained on 10–100× more data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoSlide (TCGA-trained) achieves the best macro F1 across all K values on DHMC-LUAD retrieval, beating the strongest VL baseline TITAN by 6.7 points at K=1 (51.4 vs. 44.7) and 6.7 points at K=5 (49.6 vs. 42.9), and outperforming same-cohort vision baselines TITAN-V and GigaPath by 25.9 and 33.9 points respectively. On survival prediction, TopoSlide matches GigaPath (0.646) on TCGA-LUAD DSS (0.643) and is within 0.002 of TITAN on CPTAC-LUAD PFI (0.634 vs. 0.636), while substantially exceeding other VL models (PRISM 0.523, CHIEF 0.538). For gene mutation/biomarker AUROC, TopoSlide leads its vision-only category on CPTAC-LUAD (0.636) and TCGA-BRCA (0.641), though it trails TITAN (0.645) and CTransPath (0.640) on CPTAC-BRCA. On BRCA histologic subtype retrieval, TopoSlide does not surpass the best VL models, which the authors attribute to report-based alignment providing implicit task supervision during VL pretraining. Overall, TopoSlide trained on only hundreds of slides matches or exceeds foundation models trained on 60K–340K slides across all three task categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in pathology foundation models (GigaPath, TITAN, PRISM, CHIEF), which aggregate patch-level features via attention or language alignment but treat spatial organization implicitly. While persistent homology has been used in pathology for patch-level tasks (segmentation, instance localization, generative models), TopoSlide is the first to apply topological descriptors as a global inductive bias for whole-slide representation learning. The method repurposes TDA tools (distance-to-measure filtration, persistence diagrams) and standard self-supervised objectives (contrastive learning, regression) into a conditional multi-task framework that forces the slide encoder to encode cluster-level spatial topology, bridging a gap between how pathologists interpret tissue architecture and how computational methods process WSIs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is data efficiency: TopoSlide trains on hundreds of slides per cohort yet matches or exceeds foundation models trained on hundreds of thousands, suggesting topological inductive bias can partially substitute for scale in pathology representation learning. The topology-based conditional retrieval (querying by histologic pattern to retrieve slides with matching spatial organization) is a genuinely new capability not offered by existing foundation models. However, performance is inherently bounded by the frozen CONCH patch encoder, and the paper acknowledges that joint optimization of patch embeddings and topological learning remains open. Integrating topological supervision into large-scale multi-cancer pretraining pipelines and extending to multimodal settings are natural next steps.
