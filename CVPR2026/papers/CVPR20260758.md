## Problem

Existing consistency-model (CM) based solvers for inverse problems either require task-specific retraining of an external encoder (e.g., CoSIGN's ControlNet) or rely on pseudo-inverse guidance that becomes unstable for ill-conditioned or nonlinear forward operators (e.g., CM4IR). No prior work has embedded CMs into a plug-and-play optimization framework that provides a unified, general-purpose solver with convergence guarantees across both linear and nonlinear inverse problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260758/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded measurement vector y and a forward operator A (linear matrix or nonlinear function) as input, and outputs a reconstructed image. Natural image experiments operate at 256×256×3 RGB resolution, while MRI reconstruction uses 320×320×2 complex-valued multi-coil k-space data. The forward operator can be any linear or nonlinear mapping (e.g., Gaussian blur, 4× downsampling, 70% inpainting mask, JPEG compression, phase retrieval with coded mask, or multi-coil MRI undersampling), and the solver requires only the operator and its adjoint/gradient—no task-specific retraining of the generative prior.

## Method

PnP-CM reinterprets a pre-trained consistency model as the proximal operator of an image prior within a PnP-ADMM scheme, alternating between a data-fidelity update (a Tikhonov-regularized quadratic minimization solved in closed form for linear operators or via first-order methods for nonlinear ones) and a single CM evaluation that maps a noisy state directly to a clean estimate. Two acceleration mechanisms target the low-NFE regime: (1) controlled Gaussian noise injection into the CM input, which allows the model to operate at its preferred higher noise levels while a convergence theorem guarantees that diminishing noise sequences preserve ADMM fixed-point convergence; and (2) Nesterov-style momentum on both primal and dual variables to reduce the number of outer iterations needed. The quadratic penalty in the z-update provides implicit Tikhonov regularization, making the data-fidelity subproblem less sensitive to the conditioning of A compared to PGD-based alternatives like CM4IR. The full algorithm requires only 4 NFEs (or 2 for meaningful results) and is evaluated across linear and nonlinear natural-image tasks as well as multi-coil MRI reconstruction, where a CM is trained from scratch on fastMRI for the first time.

## Results

On linear inverse problems (CelebA-HQ, σ_y=0.05), PnP-CM achieves 27.27 PSNR / 0.285 LPIPS on 4× super-resolution, 28.94 / 0.249 on Gaussian deblurring, and 29.23 / 0.201 on 70% inpainting, all with only 4 NFEs. On fastMRI reconstruction (R=8), PnP-CM reaches 33.24 PSNR / 0.884 SSIM (Coronal PD) and 31.42 / 0.804 (Coronal PD-FS), outperforming DPS (1000 NFE) by ~1.4 dB PSNR and DDS (100 NFE) by ~1.0 dB. The method produces meaningful reconstructions in as few as 2 NFEs and consistently outperforms all CM-based baselines (CoSIGN, CM4IR) at the same 4-NFE budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PnP-CM (4 NFEs) outperforms all CM-based baselines at the same compute budget: on CelebA-HQ Gaussian deblurring it beats CM4IR (27.30 PSNR) by 1.64 dB and CoSIGN (22.87) by 6.07 dB; on LSUN Bedroom inpainting it surpasses CM4IR (26.78) by 0.34 dB and CoSIGN (25.68) by 1.44 dB. Against DM-based methods requiring 100–2923 NFEs, PnP-CM matches or exceeds quality: it beats DPS (1000 NFE) by 2.89 dB on CelebA-HQ deblurring and DiffPIR (100 NFE) by 1.62 dB on CelebA-HQ deblurring, while using 25–700× fewer NFEs. On nonlinear tasks (JPEG, nonlinear deblur, phase retrieval), PnP-CM with 4–8 NFEs outperforms DPS (1000 NFE) and DPnP (2923 NFE) on PSNR for JPEG restoration (26.14 vs. 25.55 and 21.31 on CelebA-HQ) and nonlinear deblurring (24.55 vs. 23.55 and 22.29). For MRI reconstruction at R=8, PnP-CM (4 NFEs + 10 CG iters) achieves 33.24 PSNR / 0.884 SSIM on Coronal PD, surpassing DPS (31.88 / 0.863, 1000 NFEs), DDS (32.27 / 0.868, 100 NFEs), and CM4IR (29.60 / 0.755, 4 NFEs) by 1.36, 0.97, and 3.64 dB respectively. The only case where a baseline edges ahead is CelebA-HQ inpainting LPIPS, where DiffPIR (0.207) slightly beats PnP-CM (0.201), though PnP-CM still leads on PSNR (29.23 vs. 30.71 is actually DiffPIR winning on PSNR too at 30.71 vs. 29.23 — correction: DiffPIR wins on CelebA-HQ inpainting PSNR by 1.48 dB but PnP-CM wins on LPIPS 0.201 vs. 0.207).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets consistency models (CMs) as proximal operators within the plug-and-play ADMM framework, bridging two previously separate lines of work: CM-based few-step samplers (CoSIGN, CM4IR) and PnP optimization (PnP-DM, DPnP). It departs from CoSIGN, which requires task-specific ControlNet retraining for each forward operator, and from CM4IR, whose pseudo-inverse data fidelity is tied to the conditioning of the forward operator and does not extend cleanly to nonlinear or ill-conditioned problems. The ADMM formulation with its quadratic penalty provides Tikhonov-like regularization that makes the data-fidelity subproblem well-conditioned regardless of the operator, and the paper adds noise injection and momentum updates (with a convergence theorem for the former) to improve performance in the 2–4 NFE regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PnP-CM offers a unified, task-agnostic framework that handles both linear and nonlinear inverse problems with only 4 NFEs, a ~250× reduction over typical DM-based solvers, while matching or exceeding their quality. The first demonstration of CMs trained and applied to MRI reconstruction opens a practical path toward near-real-time clinical imaging. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D volumetric or video inverse problems, whether the fixed momentum and noise schedules can be adapted per-problem, and whether the convergence guarantees extend to the nonlinear data-fidelity subproblem solved with first-order methods.
