## Problem

Existing change detection models are trained on single-type annotated data and cannot jointly leverage diverse binary (BCD) and semantic (SCD) change detection datasets, because semantic labels conflict across sources (e.g., 'building' is a positive class in one dataset but negative background in another). Additionally, BCD and SCD require fundamentally different architectures, producing a proliferation of specialised models with poor generalisation and limited practical versatility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of dual-temporal RGB remote sensing images (1024×1024 for WHU-CD, S2Looking, LEVIR-CD+; 512×512 for SECOND) together with a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'segment all changed areas' or 'segment semantic masks of changed areas'). It produces pixel-level masks whose type is determined by the instruction: a binary change mask, per-temporal semantic segmentation masks, or semantic 'from-to' transition masks. The MLLM autoregressively generates a text response that embeds special tokens at positions corresponding to the requested mask types, and the decoder produces the actual pixel masks from those token positions.

## Method

UniChange builds on LLaVA-7B as the MLLM backbone and RSBuilding-ViT-L as the vision encoder, introducing three special tokens ([T1], [T2], [CHANGE]) into the vocabulary whose last-layer embeddings are projected via an MLP to serve as dynamic, instruction-conditioned queries for a four-level Token Driven Decoder. The decoder (inspired by SAM/RSBuilding) hierarchically refines these queries through self-attention and cross-attention against flattened multi-scale dual-temporal feature tokens, then generates masks by filtering upsampled feature maps with the refined query embeddings via Einstein summation. Only the language decoder receives LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8); all other components are fully fine-tuned. Training uses a combined loss of autoregressive cross-entropy on the text output plus a mask loss comprising BCE, Dice, semantic segmentation cross-entropy, and a cosine-embedding semantic change loss, with the latter two activated only on SCD data. This 'embedding as change' paradigm eliminates predefined classification heads, allowing the model to learn from multi-source datasets with conflicting class definitions in a single end-to-end framework.

## Results

On the three BCD benchmarks, UniChange achieves the best F1 and IoU across all datasets: 94.96/90.41 on WHU-CD, 69.32/53.04 on S2Looking, and 88.19/78.87 on LEVIR-CD+. On the SCD benchmark (SECOND), UniChange attains the highest scores on all five metrics: IoU 57.62, mIoU 72.85, F_scd 63.50, F_bcd 73.12, and SeK 23.02. These results represent state-of-the-art performance, surpassing the previous best methods (ChangeCLIP, LSKNet, SFCD-Net for BCD; MambaSCD, HGINet for SCD) by 0.3–2.7 points in IoU depending on the dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniChange outperforms all 29 baselines on every evaluated dataset. On WHU-CD, it leads the closest competitor ChangeCLIP (IoU 90.08) by 0.33 points and SA-CDNet (89.52) by 0.89 points. On S2Looking, the gap is larger: UniChange (IoU 53.04) surpasses LSKNet (50.96) by 2.08 points and TTP (50.80) by 2.24 points. On LEVIR-CD+, UniChange (IoU 78.87) exceeds SFCD-Net (76.12) by 2.75 points and ChangeCLIP (75.63) by 3.24 points. On the SCD benchmark SECOND, UniChange (IoU 57.62, mIoU 72.85, F_scd 63.50, SeK 23.02) beats the strongest prior methods MambaSCD (57.24/72.73/62.83/21.31) and HGINet (56.13/71.73/62.88/21.83) across all five metrics, with the largest margin on SeK (+1.71 over MambaSCD, +1.19 over HGINet). Notably, no single prior method dominates all SCD metrics simultaneously—MambaSCD leads on F_bcd but trails on SeK, while HGINet leads on F_scd but trails on mIoU—whereas UniChange achieves the best score on every metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniChange builds on two distinct lineages: the MLLM paradigm (specifically LLaVA-7B) for generative visual-language understanding, and the RSBuilding token-driven decoder for pixel-level segmentation from foundation model features. It departs fundamentally from the dominant siamese/transformer CD architecture (FC-Siam-diff, BiT, Changer, SAM-CD) by reframing change detection as an autoregressive language generation task where special token embeddings ([T1], [T2], [CHANGE]) serve as dynamic queries into a vision decoder, rather than relying on fixed classification heads or dedicated change-fusion modules. This 'embedding as change' paradigm is the key conceptual departure, enabling a single model to handle both BCD and SCD and to absorb knowledge from datasets with conflicting semantic labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of BCD and SCD under one MLLM-based framework addresses a long-standing fragmentation problem: practitioners previously needed separate, task-specific models for each detection type and could not jointly train on multi-source datasets with contradictory class definitions. The consistent SOTA across four benchmarks validates the approach, but practical concerns remain — a 7B-parameter MLLM is substantially heavier than typical CD models, raising questions about inference cost and deployment in real-time monitoring. Open questions include whether the MLLM's language priors provide genuine benefit over a well-tuned non-linguistic backbone for purely pixel-level tasks, how the framework scales to more semantic categories or higher-resolution imagery, and whether the joint-training gains persist when datasets are more heterogeneous in scale and domain.
