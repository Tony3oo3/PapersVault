## Problem

Prompt-based continual learning (PCL) methods achieve competitive accuracy on edge devices but largely ignore training-time memory and computational costs, which are critical for on-device adaptation under strict resource constraints. Existing token-reduction techniques (ToMe, PatchDropout) are task-agnostic and degrade PCL accuracy when applied naively, while high-accuracy PCL methods like C-Prompt incur prohibitive memory overhead. The paper addresses this gap by asking how to reduce peak training memory and backpropagation cost in PCL without sacrificing accuracy on memory-constrained hardware.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R, 224×224 for CUB-200) and outputs a class label over the cumulative set of classes seen so far. A key operational detail is that during training, only a sparse subset of patch tokens (controlled by reduction ratio r, default 0.4) is forwarded through the prompt-injected backbone, while the frozen query encoder always processes all tokens to compute patch importance scores. At inference, the full patch set is used, and the decoupled classifier training phase explicitly aligns the classifier with this full-input regime.

## Method

CPS-Prompt integrates two modules into the standard two-stage PCL pipeline (frozen query encoder + prompt-injected ViT-Tiny backbone). Critical Patch Sampling (CPS) computes a per-patch critical score as the product of class-to-patch attention weight and value-vector L2 norm from the final transformer block of the frozen query encoder, converts scores to a sampling distribution via temperature-scaled softmax, and draws k patches by multinomial sampling without replacement, reducing stored activations and peak training memory. Decoupled Prompt and Classifier Training (DPCT) then splits the training budget into two phases: a prompt-training phase that jointly optimizes prompt and classifier parameters on sparse-patch inputs, followed by a classifier-only phase that freezes the prompt and fine-tunes the classifier on full-patch inputs, thereby mitigating the train-inference representation mismatch and eliminating gradient flow through the prompt in the second phase. Together, these modules yield ~1.6× reductions in peak memory, training time, and energy over CODA-Prompt while maintaining accuracy within 2% of C-Prompt.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, ImageNet-R, and CUB-200 (10 tasks each), CPS-Prompt achieves average accuracies of 66.89%, 49.96%, and 52.85% respectively, with forgetting of 13.15%, 11.06%, and 11.28%. Compared to the balanced CODA-Prompt baseline, CPS-Prompt uses ~1.6× less peak memory, ~1.5× less training time, and ~1.6× less energy on the Jetson Orin Nano while maintaining statistically comparable accuracy on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-R. Relative to the accuracy SOTA C-Prompt, CPS-Prompt incurs only a ~2% average accuracy drop but saves ~4.3× memory, ~3.1× training time, and ~3.3× energy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPS-Prompt is statistically on par with CODA-Prompt on CIFAR-100 (66.89 vs. 67.06) and ImageNet-R (49.96 vs. 50.24) while achieving lower forgetting (13.15 vs. 14.73 on CIFAR-100; 11.06 vs. 13.50 on ImageNet-R), and it uses ~1.6× less peak memory and ~1.5× less training time. Against the accuracy SOTA C-Prompt, CPS-Prompt trails by 1.45% on CIFAR-100, 3.36% on ImageNet-R, and leads by 0.21% on CUB-200, yet saves ~4.3× memory and ~3.1× training time. Compared to OS-Prompt (which collapses the two-stage pipeline), CPS-Prompt matches or slightly exceeds accuracy on all three datasets while using ~1.6× less memory and ~1.1× less training time. Against earlier prompt methods L2P and DualPrompt, CPS-Prompt gains 3.9–4.9% accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 4.4–5.1% on ImageNet-R. The non-prompt baselines (SGD, LwF, ER) are far behind in both accuracy and forgetting, confirming the advantage of the prompt-based paradigm for rehearsal-free CL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt-based continual learning (PCL) and ViT token reduction. On the PCL side, it builds directly on the two-stage query-then-prompt architecture established by L2P, DualPrompt, and CODA-Prompt, and acknowledges C-Prompt's accuracy gains while identifying its prohibitive memory overhead. On the token-reduction side, it departs from task-agnostic methods like ToMe and PatchDropout by leveraging the frozen query encoder's attention and value activations to perform task-aware patch selection, and from OS-Prompt's single-stage collapse by preserving the two-stage structure while reducing backpropagation cost through decoupled prompt/classifier training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but underexplored gap: training-time memory and compute efficiency for on-device continual learning, validated on real edge hardware (Jetson Orin Nano). The 1.6× efficiency gain over CODA-Prompt with <2% accuracy loss relative to C-Prompt makes PCL more deployable on memory-constrained devices. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-Tiny/16 with 10 tasks in a class-incremental setting, and the fixed reduction ratio of 0.4 in main comparisons; open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones, dynamic resource budgets, and other CL scenarios (domain- or task-incremental).
