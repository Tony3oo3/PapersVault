## Problem

In near-domain image restoration tasks (deraining, deblurring, deshading), the pixel-wise residual between degraded and clean images is inherently low-rank, yet existing diffusion-based restoration methods perform the generative process in the full-rank space. This forces the model to explore a largely empty semantic space, making sampling inefficient and hindering stable reconstruction. The paper asks whether explicitly exploiting the low-rank structure of residuals can yield both tighter variational bounds and more efficient sampling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single degraded RGB image (rainy, motion-blurred, or shadowed) at 512×512 resolution and produces the corresponding clean restored image. Rather than generating the full image, the network predicts the residual (pixel-wise difference between the degraded input and the clean target) within a low-rank subspace, which is then added to the input to recover the output. The degraded input image is also provided as a conditioning signal throughout the diffusion process.

## Method

LRDM introduces an Asymmetric Residual Diffusion Process where the forward noise-injection step is constrained to a rank-k subspace S_k (via orthogonal projection Q_k on the residual), while the reverse denoising network operates in the full ambient space to preserve expressivity for the full-rank posterior. This asymmetry is theoretically justified by the Low-Rank Residual Hypothesis, which shows that confining the residual to S_k yields a strictly tighter Variational Lower Bound since the complementary subspace contributes only non-negative KL loss. To handle the evolving structural complexity across timesteps, an Adaptive Rank Selection mechanism schedules the rank k(t) dynamically—using linear or polynomial increasing/decreasing envelopes—so that early diffusion steps use lower rank (coarse structure) and later steps allow higher rank (fine detail). The model is trained with standard supervised diffusion losses on residual prediction and noise prediction, and experiments show that a polynomial-decreasing schedule (p=3) with maximum rank 256 achieves the best quality-efficiency tradeoff, reaching 34.65 dB PSNR on Rain1400 with only 2 sampling steps.

## Results

On Raindrop, LRDM achieves 33.09 dB PSNR and 0.967 SSIM, ranking first among all diffusion-based methods and achieving the best SSIM overall. On Rain1400, LRDM delivers the best overall performance with 34.39 dB PSNR and 0.954 SSIM, surpassing all transformer/CNN and diffusion baselines. On real-world deblurring, LRDM attains 30.21 dB / 0.933 SSIM on RealBlur-J and 37.92 dB / 0.976 SSIM on RealBlur-R, both state-of-the-art. Notably, the adaptive polynomial-decreasing (PD) rank schedule with p=3 achieves 34.65 dB PSNR on Rain1400 with only 2 sampling steps, outperforming full-rank configurations that require more steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRDM outperforms the closest diffusion-based baseline RDDM by +0.58 dB PSNR and +0.011 SSIM on Raindrop, and by a substantial +2.18 dB PSNR and +0.002 SSIM on Rain1400, demonstrating that the low-rank constraint significantly improves residual modeling. Against strong non-diffusion baselines, LRDM surpasses Restormer by +1.42 dB on Raindrop and +0.71 dB on Rain1400, and beats DRSformer by +0.73 dB on Rain1400. On real-world deblurring (RealBlur-J/R), LRDM leads the strongest non-diffusion baseline AdaRevD by +0.09 dB / +0.039 SSIM and +1.39 dB / +0.019 SSIM respectively, while outperforming all diffusion-based deblurring methods (HI-Diff, DiffBIR, OSEDiff, Diff-Plugin, FideDiff) by 1–4 dB. The only method with a marginally higher PSNR on Raindrop is MPerceiver (33.21 vs 33.09), but LRDM's SSIM advantage (+0.038) indicates substantially better structural fidelity. Overall, LRDM achieves the best or near-best results across all seven evaluated datasets, with the largest gains observed on Rain1400 and RealBlur-R where the low-rank inductive bias most effectively suppresses irrelevant high-frequency noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Residual Denoising Diffusion Model (RDDM, Liu et al., CVPR 2024) by observing that in near-domain restoration tasks (deraining, deblurring, deshading), the pixel-wise residual between degraded and clean images exhibits a rapidly decaying singular-value spectrum, and then constraining the forward diffusion process to the corresponding low-rank subspace. All prior diffusion-based restoration methods—DDRM, DPS, Palette, Whang et al., RDDM, DiffUIR, DeblurDiff—operate the generative process in the full ambient space, treating the residual as a full-dimensional signal; LRDM departs from this by introducing an asymmetric design where the forward noise-injection is low-rank while the reverse denoising remains full-rank, and by adding an adaptive rank schedule that modulates subspace dimensionality across timesteps. The theoretical contribution connects to the classical low-rank literature in vision (PCA, RPCA, matrix completion) but applies it specifically to the variational bound of a diffusion model, showing that the VLB is strictly tighter under the low-rank residual assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides both a principled theoretical justification (tighter VLB) and a practical efficiency gain—achieving competitive or superior PSNR with as few as 2 sampling steps—making low-rank residual diffusion a viable inductive bias for near-domain restoration pipelines where inference cost matters. The adaptive rank selection mechanism and the ablation showing that the low-rank module transfers to other diffusion baselines (RDDM, ResShift) suggest the idea is broadly applicable beyond the specific architectures tested. However, the authors explicitly acknowledge that the low-rank hypothesis relies on the near-domain assumption, so applicability to far-domain tasks with extreme structural deformations (e.g., severe inpainting, style transfer) remains an open question. A key open direction is whether the rank schedule can be learned end-to-end rather than following a fixed parametric family, and whether the approach extends to latent-space diffusion models at scale.
