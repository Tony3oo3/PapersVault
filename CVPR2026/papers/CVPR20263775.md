## Problem

Text-only LLMs for semantic route planning frequently hallucinate geographically implausible transitions (e.g., jumping across distant districts or backtracking between non-adjacent POIs) because they lack explicit spatial grounding. Existing datasets further constrain the field to coarse-grained, citywide itineraries, leaving fine-grained, localized, theme-specific route construction—where spatial continuity and local POI density are critical—largely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three complementary inputs: a natural-language user query, up to 20 retrieved POIs with structured metadata (category, suitability tags), and a north-up map tile rendered at 980×980 pixels showing only those candidate POIs as labeled markers. Output is a two-part structured response: a chain-of-thought rationale (within  tags) explaining spatial and thematic reasoning, followed by an ordered list of POI indices (within <answer> tags) constituting the final route. POI retrieval is hybrid (lexical + dense vector) over ~500K POIs, with MAD-based outlier pruning to ensure spatial compactness.

## Method

SMAP conditions a multimodal LLM (Qwen2.5-VL) on the joint input of query, POI metadata, and map tile to generate spatially grounded itineraries in a single step. Its core anti-hallucination mechanism is a two-stage pipeline: first, a map-grounded self-editing step where a generator MLLM drafts a route and a verifier MLLM refines it against geographic evidence, producing draft–refined pairs without human annotation; second, Hallucination-Penalized DPO (HDPO) trains the generator on these pairs, treating the refined route as accepted and the draft as rejected. The key insight is that because the verifier makes only minimal edits correcting hallucinations, the accepted and rejected responses share high token overlap with nearly identical preceding contexts, causing the DPO learning signal to concentrate almost entirely on the corrected (hallucinated) segments rather than being diluted across stylistic differences. Training proceeds in two phases—SFT on Gemini-2.5-Pro-generated routes for cold start, followed by HDPO—on the newly introduced MM-Route dataset of 3,000 multimodal queries spanning citywide trips to fine-grained thematic walks.

## Results

On MM-Route, the best SMAP model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B-HDPO) achieves a Route Distance Ratio (RDR) of 0.831, Time Schedule Pass Rate (TSPR) of 77.5%, Overall Route Score (ORS) of 3.89/5, and Spatial Hallucination Rate (SHR) of 14.0%, while maintaining a 100% Planning Success Rate. Compared to its pretrained base (Qwen2.5-VL-32B), post-training improves RDR from 0.667 to 0.831, TSPR from 34.5% to 77.5%, and halves SHR from 31.9% to 14.0%. In head-to-head comparison, the 32B-HDPO model achieves a 51.5% Comparison Preference Rate (CPR) against GPT-5 routes, indicating it is preferred over the larger proprietary model in pairwise judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL-32B-HDPO wins on route efficiency (RDR 0.831 vs. 0.825), temporal plausibility (TSPR 77.5% vs. 68.8%), and overall quality (ORS 3.89 vs. 3.76), while GPT-5 retains a slight edge on spatial faithfulness (SHR 13.3 vs. 14.0) and theme adherence (RTPR 94.0% vs. 91.0%). Against GPT-4o, SMAP-32B-HDPO dominates across nearly all metrics: RDR 0.831 vs. 0.688, TSPR 77.5% vs. 36.5%, ORS 3.89 vs. 2.12, and CPR 51.5% vs. 10.5%. Against OpenAI-o1, SMAP-32B-HDPO improves RDR (0.831 vs. 0.780), TSPR (77.5% vs. 37.0%), ORS (3.89 vs. 3.10), and CPR (51.5% vs. 15.0%), with comparable RTPR (91.0% vs. 90.9%) and slightly higher SHR (14.0% vs. 21.2%). Against the Qwen2.5-VL-32B pretrained base, post-training yields the largest absolute gains: RDR +0.164, TSPR +43.0 pp, SHR −17.9 pp, and ORS +2.20, demonstrating that task-specific multimodal alignment is the primary driver of performance rather than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LLM-based semantic route planning (ITINERA, ChinaTravel, TravelPlanner) from text-only generation to a multimodal setting by conditioning on map tiles, and departs from agent-based multi-step planning (ReAct, Reflexion) in favor of one-step RAG-augmented generation. The training pipeline builds directly on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) but introduces a key modification: preference pairs are constructed via minimal-edit self-correction rather than contrasting substantially different responses, concentrating the learning signal on hallucinated segments. In the MLLM-geospatial literature (GeoReasoner, TraveLLaMA), prior work focuses on QA or retrieval validation; SMAP is positioned as the first to perform end-to-end route synthesis with visual map input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MM-Route dataset (3,000 multimodal queries spanning citywide to neighborhood-scale routes) fills a gap in evaluation resources for spatially grounded planning, and the HDPO insight—that minimal-edit preference pairs avoid diluting the DPO signal across stylistic differences—offers a transferable recipe for any domain where hallucination correction is the primary training signal. However, the evaluation relies on Gemini-2.5-Pro both for data generation and as judge, creating a circularity concern that limits confidence in the absolute numbers. Open questions include whether the map-tile approach scales to multi-city or continental itineraries, whether the self-editing verifier can be replaced by cheaper rule-based checks, and whether the HDPO construction generalizes beyond route planning to other spatial-reasoning tasks.
