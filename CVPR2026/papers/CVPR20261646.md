## Problem

Existing MLLM unlearning methods target only final answers, leaving sensitive information embedded in intermediate chain-of-thought steps (reasoning leakage), while LRM-style unlearning that suppresses such leakage collapses coherent reasoning into incoherent, repetitive outputs. No prior benchmark or method jointly evaluates and addresses both reasoning-level leakage suppression and general reasoning competence preservation in multimodal reasoning models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261646/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on structured multimodal profiles (an RGB image paired with textual attributes) and a designed query, producing a two-field output: a sanitized Answer field and a sanitized Reasoning (CoT) field. At inference time, the model is prompted with a refusal-style prefix so that the hidden states over both the answer span and the reasoning span are available for steering; the output must contain no literal or semantically implied reference to the forgotten key-value pairs in either field.

## Method

R-MUSE is a training-free, inference-time activation steering framework with three coupled components. First, a span-hybrid unlearning subspace is built by contrasting recall-guided versus refusal-guided generations, pooling hidden states separately over the answer token span and the CoT token span, z-scoring both differentials, and extracting a compact SVD basis that encodes both factual recall and reasoning traces. Second, a Reasoning Retain Subspace (RRS) is constructed from retain-set examples contrasting stepwise solutions against direct answers; it serves dual roles as a binary gate (steer only when the query's hidden state is weakly aligned with RRS) and as an orthogonal projector that removes the reasoning-supporting component from the steering direction. Third, Adaptive Calibration Steering (ACS) formulates the steering magnitude as an optimal transport problem on the unit sphere: the squared geodesic distance between the current hidden-state direction and a sanitized target direction determines the rotation angle, which is applied via norm-preserving spherical interpolation (slerp), yielding a per-token, per-layer adaptive strength with no hand-tuned global coefficient.

## Results

On RMLLMU-Bench (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, 5% forget rate), R-MUSE reduces forget-set classification accuracy to 32.50% (vs. 60.70% vanilla) and forget-set ROUGE-L to 0.385 (vs. 0.710 vanilla), achieving the strongest forgetting across all baselines. Critically, R-MUSE attains the lowest Reasoning Information Leakage (RIL) on the forget set at 48.20%, compared to 66.20% for the best baseline (R²MU) and 75.90% for MMUnlearner, while retaining retain-set accuracy (54.00% vs. 54.10% vanilla) and celebrity-set accuracy (60.40% vs. 60.80% vanilla) nearly intact. The same pattern holds on LLaVA-1.5-7B and at 10% forget rate, where R-MUSE consistently achieves the lowest Fgt RIL (38.50% and 41.30% respectively) while preserving utility metrics within 1–2 points of the vanilla model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-MUSE decisively outperforms all seven baselines on both forgetting efficacy and reasoning leakage. On forget-set classification accuracy, R-MUSE (32.50%) reduces performance far below the next-best R²MU (57.00%) and MLLM-specific methods MMUnlearner (56.80%) and MANU (55.20%), indicating substantially stronger answer-level forgetting. On the key RIL metric, R-MUSE achieves 48.20% on the forget set, a 18-point improvement over R²MU (66.20%) and a 27.7-point improvement over MMUnlearner (75.90%), demonstrating that only R-MUSE effectively suppresses sensitive information in intermediate reasoning steps. Among training-based baselines, gradient-ascent methods (GA, GA_Diff) and KL_Min achieve moderate answer forgetting but leave RIL in the 72–77% range, confirming that parameter updates alone cannot erase reasoning traces. The LRM-specific method R²MU performs best among baselines on RIL (66.20%) but still lags R-MUSE by 18 points, and it degrades retain-set reasoning capability (Ret RIL 69.30% vs. R-MUSE's 78.50%). On utility preservation, R-MUSE matches or slightly exceeds vanilla on retain and celebrity splits (Ret Acc 54.00% vs. 54.10%; Cele Acc 60.40% vs. 60.80%), whereas training-based methods like GA and KL_Min drop retain accuracy by 5–6 points, showing that R-MUSE's training-free steering avoids the collateral damage of weight updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) MLLM unlearning methods (MMUnlearner, MANU) that adapt gradient-ascent or neuron-pruning strategies to erase image-text associations but operate only on final answers; (2) LRM unlearning (R²MU) that targets reasoning traces but in text-only settings; and (3) inference-time activation steering and representation engineering (Li et al. 2023; Rimsky et al. 2024; Zou et al. 2023) that modulate model behavior via linear subspace projections. R-MUSE departs from prior work by unifying the span-hybrid differential (capturing both answer and CoT spans) with a Reasoning Retain Subspace that orthogonally protects general reasoning, and by replacing fixed-strength steering with an optimal-transport-calibrated adaptive update. The benchmark RMLLMU-Bench extends MLLMU-Bench with structured reasoning chains and two new metrics (RIL, RCR) that no prior unlearning evaluation provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical blind spot in current MLLM deployment: answer-level unlearning is insufficient because sensitive facts persist in intermediate reasoning steps, a leakage channel that standard benchmarks never measure. The finding that even soft activation steering without RRS protection collapses reasoning retention (Ret accuracy 54.1%→34.0%) underscores that 'just steer a bit' is not a safe default. Limitations include reliance on explicit CoT (implicit reasoning remains unaddressed) and potential collateral damage when non-target knowledge shares activation geometry with target knowledge. Open questions include whether the subspace approach generalizes to larger or differently-architected RMLLMs, how it interacts with iterative unlearning requests, and whether the optimal-transport calibration offers advantages over simpler adaptive schemes at scale.
