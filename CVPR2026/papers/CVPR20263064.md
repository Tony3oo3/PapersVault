## Problem

RL-based fine-tuning (e.g., GRPO) dramatically improves reasoning in large MLLMs but surprisingly degrades it in lightweight 3B models on video tasks. The paper identifies the root cause as 'perceptual shortcuts' in training data—74% of CLEVRER counterfactual questions are merely observational, allowing models to answer via superficial visual matching rather than genuine causal reasoning. Existing causal debiasing methods target intermodal bias (text vs. vision) but leave this intramodal shortcut problem unaddressed, and no prior work provides a mechanism to actively repel a model from learned shortcut behaviors during RL fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct) receives a video sampled as up to 16 frames at 128×28×28 resolution during training (32 frames at 256×28×28 during evaluation), a text question, and a set of candidate answer choices, and outputs a single selected answer. Training uses a soft accuracy reward combined with a format reward, with group size G=8 and learning rate 1e-6. The framework requires no Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage—only 1K RL training samples from CLEVRER are used for the main model, plus a separately curated bias dataset for the bias model.

## Method

VideoThinker is a two-stage causal debiasing framework grounded in a structural causal model where the query Q acts as a confounder opening a spurious backdoor path Q→O→A (observation-to-answer) alongside the genuine reasoning path Q→T→A. Stage 1 (Bias Aware Training) trains a dedicated bias model π_bias on a curated subset of CLEVRER containing only observational/shortcut questions, with the KL-divergence constraint entirely removed (inspired by DAPO) to accelerate convergence to superficial pattern-matching behavior. Stage 2 (CDPO) fine-tunes the main policy using a modified GRPO objective in which the KL-divergence term carries a positive sign (+β·D_KL(π_θ‖π_bias)) instead of the standard negative sign, transforming the regularizer from an attractive constraint into a repulsive force that actively pushes the policy's distribution away from the bias model's shortcut behaviors. This repulsive objective, combined with the standard reward-driven advantage term, forces the model to discover alternative, more complex reasoning pathways that the bias model cannot access, serving as a practical gradient-based approximation of the intractable backdoor adjustment integral.

## Results

VideoThinker-R1 (3B, trained with only 1K RL samples) achieves 79.1% on CLEVRER counterfactual, 56.8% on MMVU-mc, 34.3% on Video-Holmes, 60.9% on MVBench, 63.5% on TempCompass, and 52.4% on VideoMME (no subtitles). The headline gain is a 14.2% absolute improvement over the GRPO baseline on CLEVRER under identical 1K-RL training conditions, and a 22.1% gain on TempCompass. Against the much larger Video-UTR-7B, VideoThinker-R1 leads by 2.1% on MVBench and 3.8% on TempCompass while using only 1% of the training data and no SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct GRPO baseline (same 1K RL budget), VideoThinker-R1 gains +14.2% on CLEVRER_cf, +22.1% on TempCompass, +6.0% on MVBench, and +4.8% on MMVU_mc, confirming that the improvement stems from the debiasing mechanism rather than additional data. Versus VideoRFT-3B (which uses 110K SFT+RL samples, 110× more data), VideoThinker-R1 still leads on every reported benchmark, most notably by +7.0% on VideoMME and +19.8% on CLEVRER_cf. In cross-scale comparison, the 3B VideoThinker-R1 outperforms the 7B Video-UTR-7B by +2.1% on MVBench and +3.8% on TempCompass, though it trails by a marginal 0.2% on VideoMME (52.4 vs. 52.6). Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-3B (CoT) without any RL, gains range from +10.1% (MVBench) to +33.5% (TempCompass). At the 7B scale (Table 3), VideoThinker-R1-7B achieves 65.0 on MVBench and 73.7 on TempCompass, matching or exceeding Video-R1-7B and VideoRFT-7B, though the relative advantage over GRPO is smaller than at 3B, consistent with the paper's argument that perceptual shortcuts are primarily a limitation of smaller models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO-based RL fine-tuning for MLLMs (DeepSeekMath, Video-R1, VideoRFT) and departs from prior causal debiasing work in multimodal models, which has focused almost exclusively on intermodal bias (text vs. vision). The key conceptual departure is identifying an intramodal failure mode—'perceptual shortcuts'—where models exploit superficial observational evidence within the video itself to bypass genuine counterfactual reasoning. The CDPO algorithm repurposes the KL-divergence regularizer from a constraining (attractive) force into a repulsive one, drawing on Pearl's backdoor adjustment as theoretical motivation but replacing the intractable marginalization with an adversarial approximation via a dedicated bias model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 74% of CLEVRER's counterfactual training samples are answerable via observational shortcuts, and that RL fine-tuning actively degrades inferential reasoning in 3B models (73.9%→63.1%), reframes a practical problem (small models underperforming under RL) as a data-bias problem with a principled causal explanation. The practical recipe—no SFT, only 1K RL samples, a repulsive KL term—is appealing for edge deployment. However, the bias model construction relies on ground-truth collision annotations specific to CLEVRER, and the 7B scaling shows diminished gains, leaving open whether the framework generalizes to domains without such structured annotations or to non-counterfactual reasoning tasks.
