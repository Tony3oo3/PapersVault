## Problem

Existing text-guided medical segmentation models behave like closed-set multitask networks: they rely on fixed label sets, are brittle to phrasing variations and synonyms, and perform only a single late-stage fusion between text and image features. This limits their ability to handle free-form clinical descriptions, generalize to unseen concepts or modalities, and segment instance-specific structures from spatially grounded radiology report language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D medical image volume (CT, MRI, or PET, ~4 TB across 62K+ scans) and an arbitrary free-text prompt as input, ranging from single anatomical terms ("liver") to full clinical sentences with spatial grounding ("spiculated carcinoma in the left upper lobe with pleural contact"). It outputs a corresponding 3D volumetric segmentation mask at the native resolution of the input volume. The text encoder is frozen (Qwen3-Embedding-4B), so the model must handle synonyms, rephrasings, and minor spelling errors purely through its learned visual-textual alignment.

## Method

VoxTell replaces the single late-stage text-image dot product used by MaskFormer/SAT with repeated cross-modal fusion at every UNet decoder stage. A ResEncL encoder produces multi-scale feature maps; a frozen Qwen3-Embedding-4B embeds the text prompt, which a 6-layer transformer prompt decoder expands into scale-specific guidance tensors (G=32 channels per stage). At each decoder resolution, a channel-wise dot product between the upsampled image features and the corresponding text guidance tensor is concatenated back into the feature stream, followed by a segmentation head with deep supervision. This forces the network to incorporate prompt conditioning early in the hierarchy rather than only at the final output, enabling spatially grounded, location-aware representations. Training uses 62K+ volumes across 1,087 concepts with a 9,682-label vocabulary, SGD with polynomial decay, and both positive and negative prompt sampling on 64 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On zero-shot 3D segmentation across 11 unseen CT, MRI, and PET datasets, VoxTell achieves a mean Dice of 70.85, substantially outperforming the next-best text-promptable method SAT (51.23) and all other baselines. On individual structures, VoxTell reaches 89.65 Dice on lung & airway, 73.24 on liver lesions, and 97.59 on bones with fractures. On the ReXGroundingCT instance-level benchmark (fine-tuned), VoxTell achieves 28.2 Dice and 67.8 HIT₅%, versus SAT's 13.1 Dice and 49.8 HIT₅%. On a held-out in-house SBRT radiotherapy cohort with sentence-level clinical prompts, VoxTell scores 50.2 Dice while SAT scores 0.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VoxTell outperforms all seven baselines across the board. Against the strongest prior text-promptable method SAT, VoxTell gains +19.6 mean Dice (70.85 vs. 51.23), with particularly large margins on MS lesions (+59.0), brain metastases (+26.0), and adrenal tumors (+77.1, where SAT scores near zero). Against BioMedParseV2 and SegVol (both ~30–31 mean Dice), VoxTell leads by roughly 40 Dice points. TotalSegmentator, a non-text-promptable classical tool, is only evaluated on two CT datasets where it scores 67.64 and 85.23; VoxTell exceeds it on both (72.94 and 89.65) while additionally handling 9 more datasets. On the ReXGroundingCT instance-level benchmark, VoxTell (28.2 Dice, 67.8 HIT₅%) more than doubles SAT's performance (13.1 Dice, 49.8 HIT₅%). On cross-modality transfer (e.g., breast cancer in PET), VoxTell achieves 72.27 Dice versus SAT's 58.26, and on unseen concepts (esophageal tumor in CT) VoxTell scores 69.07 while SAT fails entirely (0.00).

## Relation to Prior Work

VoxTell extends the MaskFormer/Mask2Former paradigm for text-promptable segmentation by replacing the single late-stage dot-product fusion with repeated cross-modal interactions at every decoder stage, while retaining a UNet-style (ResEncL) backbone rather than a transformer encoder. It builds directly on prior text-guided medical segmentation models (BioMedParse, SegVol, Text3DSAM, SAT) but departs from them in three ways: a substantially larger and more diverse training corpus (62K+ volumes across CT/MRI/PET, 1K+ classes), a strong frozen pretrained text encoder (Qwen3-Embedding-4B) for robust linguistic interpretation, and deep supervision that forces prompt conditioning into intermediate representations. The multi-stage fusion design is the core architectural departure, motivated by the observation that spatially grounded prompts (e.g., 'lesion in right lung') cannot be captured by a single shared segmentation head receiving text only at the final output step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VoxTell demonstrates that text-promptable 3D medical segmentation can move beyond closed-set label selection toward genuine open-set generalization: it handles sentence-level clinical language from radiology reports, transfers known structures to unseen modalities, and segments semantically related but untrained concepts. The complete failure of prior methods (SAT at 0.0 Dice) on spatially-grounded sentence prompts versus VoxTell's 50.2 Dice on the same cohort highlights a qualitative architectural gap, not merely an incremental one. Key open questions remain: performance on truly out-of-distribution anatomical regions (e.g., knee) is still poor, and the model has not yet been tested in prospective clinical deployment. Future directions include few-shot adaptation for unseen body regions and leveraging large-scale radiology report–image pairs for broader concept coverage.
