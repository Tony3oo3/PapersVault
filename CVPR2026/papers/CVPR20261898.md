## Problem

Learning-based low-light RAW denoisers require large paired clean-noisy datasets that are expensive and cumbersome to acquire. Existing noise synthesis methods either rely on simplified parametric models (e.g., Poisson-Gaussian) that fail to capture the full complexity of real sensor noise, or depend on large paired datasets for learning-based noise modeling. This paper asks whether realistic sensor noise can be synthesized from just a single noisy image and a single dark frame per ISO setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261898/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes exactly one noisy RAW image and one dark frame (captured in a lightless environment) per ISO level. From these two inputs, it generates an unlimited number of synthetic dark frames that, when combined with Poisson noise applied to clean images, produce clean-noisy training pairs for denoiser training. The synthetic dark frames preserve both the Fourier magnitude spectrum and the full pixel-wise marginal distribution (including non-Gaussian tails) of the reference sensor noise, and maintain inter-channel correlations critical for suppressing banding artifacts.

## Method

The core contribution is a Fourier-domain spectral sampling algorithm for signal-independent noise, inspired by the random phase noise (RPN) texture synthesis procedure. Given a single dark frame, the pipeline first removes fixed-pattern structure via large-kernel Gaussian blur, then computes the 2D DFT of the stochastic residual. New noise realizations are generated by preserving the magnitude spectrum while randomizing the phase, with the critical design choice of replicating the same random phase offset across all color channels to preserve inter-channel correlation (e.g., banding patterns). An iterative refinement loop (K=10) alternates between histogram matching (to align the marginal distribution) and spectral correction (to restore frequency content), ensuring both statistical and spatial fidelity. Signal-dependent photon noise is modeled separately with a Poisson distribution whose gain parameter is estimated via linear regression from the single noisy image.

## Results

On the SID test set, the proposed method achieves 43.57/0.961 (PSNR/SSIM) at ×100, 41.24/0.945 at ×250, and 37.77/0.929 at ×300, using only one noisy image and one dark frame per ISO. On the ELD test set, it reaches 47.13/0.986 at ×100 (best among all methods) and 44.89/0.969 at ×200. Among physics-based (non-learning) methods, the approach consistently achieves the best or near-best performance across all exposure ratios on both datasets, while requiring orders of magnitude less data than competing methods (1 dark frame vs. 400 for PMN/NoiseDiff/LRD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the simplest non-learning baseline (Poisson-Gaussian), the proposed method gains 2.5–4.4 dB PSNR across all settings, confirming that the simplified PG model is insufficient for real sensor noise. Compared to ELD (which requires flat-field and dark-frame calibration), the method improves by 1.6–4.4 dB, and against SFRN (which samples from 10 dark frames per ISO), it gains 0.7–1.4 dB. The most competitive comparison is with PMN, which uses 1865 real clean-noisy pairs plus 400 dark frames per ISO; the proposed method matches or slightly exceeds PMN on ELD (47.13 vs. 46.99 at ×100) and is within 0.1–0.3 dB on SID, while requiring only 1 noisy image and 1 dark frame. Against the learning-based NoiseDiff (which also needs 1865 pairs and 400 dark frames), the method is within 0.35 dB on SID but outperforms it on ELD ×100 (47.13 vs. 46.95 PSNR). LRD, the other learning-based baseline, is consistently outperformed by the proposed method on both datasets. The key practical advantage is that the proposed method achieves near-state-of-the-art denoising quality with a data requirement that is 1865× smaller in paired images and 400× smaller in dark frames compared to PMN, NoiseDiff, and LRD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Random Phase Noise (RPN) texture synthesis algorithm [16]—which preserves Fourier magnitude while randomizing phase—to model signal-independent sensor noise, a conceptual bridge from texture synthesis to imaging. It departs from parametric models (Poisson-Gaussian, ELD) that require multi-parameter calibration, and from learning-based approaches (NoiseDiff, LRD, PMN) that demand large clean-noisy paired datasets. The closest prior work, SFRN [45], also samples from real dark frames but crops patches from a small database, limiting diversity; this paper's spectral sampling generates unbounded diverse realizations from a single exemplar, and adds iterative histogram matching to capture non-Gaussian marginal statistics that the magnitude spectrum alone cannot encode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing noise synthesis data requirements from ~1865 paired images and 400 dark frames to a single noisy image and one dark frame per ISO dramatically lowers the barrier to training high-quality denoisers for new sensors. The finding that inter-channel correlation (ICC) must be preserved—enforced here by replicating the random phase across channels—is a useful diagnostic for practitioners whose synthetic noise pipelines may silently break this property. Open questions include extending the method to account for temperature-dependent dark current, exposure-time effects, and whether the single-dark-frame assumption holds for sensors with significant fixed-pattern drift over time.
