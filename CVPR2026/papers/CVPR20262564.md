## Problem

Differential privacy provides formal guarantees against data leakage from trained models, but directly applying DP-SGD to 2D human pose estimation causes severe utility degradation because keypoint prediction is inherently fine-grained and spatially precise. Prior privacy approaches for HPE (blurring, pixelation, GAN-based anonymization) lack formal guarantees and destroy clinically relevant visual details. This paper presents the first systematic framework for differentially private 2D-HPE that achieves strong privacy-utility trade-offs through complementary noise mitigation mechanisms.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image as input and produces 2D keypoint coordinates (16 joints for MPII, 17 for COCO) via a coordinate classification head that quantizes continuous positions into discrete bins using a splitting factor k. During training, each private image is paired with a public variant generated by Gaussian blur (ψ), which suppresses facial and body-structure details while preserving coarse pose cues. The model is pretrained on the public COCO dataset (~118k images) and then differentially private fine-tuned on the private MPII or HumanART datasets.

## Method

The architecture uses a TinyViT backbone with a SimCC-style coordinate classification head. The core contribution is Feature-Projective DP, which combines two noise-reduction mechanisms: (1) subspace projection, where a k-dimensional gradient subspace is estimated from a public dataset via eigendecomposition of the gradient covariance, and noisy private gradients are projected onto this subspace to discard noise in irrelevant directions; and (2) Feature Differential Privacy (FDP), which decomposes the total loss into a public component (computed on the blurred image ψ(x), yielding a noise-free gradient) and a private component (computed on the raw image, where gradients are clipped to norm C and Gaussian noise is added). The final update sums the clean public gradient with the projected, denoised private gradient. A convergence analysis shows the combined privacy error scales as Õ(k·C²) rather than Õ(p·G²), yielding a multiplicative utility gain under the same (ε,δ)-FDP guarantee.

## Results

On MPII (PCKh@0.5, finetuning, C=0.01), Feature-Projective DP achieves 82.61% at ε=0.8, recovering 73% of the privacy-induced gap relative to the non-private upper bound of 89.36%. Under the tightest privacy budget (ε=0.2, C=0.01), subspace projection alone lifts DP-SGD from 63.85% to 78.48%, while FDP lifts it to 75.46%. On HumanART, the method attains 51.6 mAP at ε=0.8 (finetuning, C=0.01) versus a non-private finetuning upper bound of 63.3 mAP. In the most extreme setting (training from scratch, C=0.1, ε=0.8), Feature-Projective DP reaches 33.48% PCKh@0.5 compared to only 6.85% for vanilla DP-SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is vanilla DP-SGD [Abadi et al., CCS 2016], evaluated under identical privacy budgets (ε ∈ {0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8}), clipping thresholds (C ∈ {0.01, 0.1, 1.0}), and training strategies. Feature-Projective DP wins in every reported configuration. The largest absolute gains appear under tight privacy and tight clipping: at ε=0.2, C=0.01 (finetuning), the combined method recovers roughly 14–19 percentage points over DP-SGD (82.61% vs 63.85% at ε=0.8; 78.48% vs 63.85% for projection alone at ε=0.2). Under the most adversarial setting (training from scratch, C=0.1, ε=0.8), the gap widens to nearly 5× (33.48% vs 6.85%). At C=1.0, where DP-SGD collapses to 12.53%, Feature-Projective DP still achieves 71.66%, a six-fold relative improvement. On HumanART, the same pattern holds: Feature-Projective DP (51.6 mAP at ε=0.8) substantially outperforms DP-SGD, and the finetuning-with-frozen-backbone strategy becomes preferable to full finetuning under DP, reversing the non-private ordering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously independent privacy mechanisms—Feature Differential Privacy (FDP, Mahloujifar et al. 2025), which decomposes gradients into public and private components, and gradient subspace projection (Zhou et al. 2020/2021), which restricts noisy updates to a low-dimensional subspace—into a single 'Feature-Projective DP' framework. Both mechanisms are applied on top of standard DP-SGD (Abadi et al. 2016) but had not been jointly deployed for a fine-grained vision task. The paper departs from prior DP-for-vision work by treating the entire raw image as the private component (requiring no manual feature curation) and by using a simple Gaussian-blur public feature map, positioning itself as the first systematic DP benchmark specifically for 2D human pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first rigorous DP benchmark for 2D-HPE, providing a practical blueprint for deploying pose estimation in privacy-sensitive settings such as hospitals and homes where formal guarantees are required. The theoretical result showing multiplicative (rather than additive) utility gains from combining FDP and projection gives practitioners a principled reason to adopt the hybrid approach. Key limitations include reliance on a public dataset from a similar distribution for subspace estimation, a single architecture (TinyViT), and only two evaluation datasets; open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D pose, video, or larger transformer backbones where the DP noise-to-signal ratio worsens with parameter count.
