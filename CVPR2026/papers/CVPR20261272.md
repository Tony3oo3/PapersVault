## Problem

Under sparse-view conditions, COLMAP produces severely sparse point clouds that fail to constrain 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization, leading to overfitting, floating artifacts, and degraded geometry. Existing monocular-depth-based fixes apply only a single global scale-offset or learned correlation, which cannot correct the spatially varying, non-rigid misalignment between backprojected depth and true scene geometry. The paper addresses how to produce a dense, geometrically calibrated initialization for 3DGS when SfM is unreliable and monocular depth is scale-ambiguous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3–12 sparse RGB views (downsampled ×8 for DTU/LLFF, ×4 for Mip-NeRF360), their camera intrinsics/extrinsics, the (often extremely sparse) COLMAP point cloud, monocular depth maps from Depth Anything V2, and dense pixel correspondences from the MAST3R matcher. It outputs a dense set of calibrated backprojected points (CBP) sampled within a radius r = S/8 of triangulated control points, which is concatenated with the COLMAP PCL to initialize 3DGS; the full TWINGS-Init pipeline completes in ~12.45 s. The final output is a trained 3DGS model producing real-time novel-view renderings.

## Method

TWINGS has three components. First, it establishes globally consistent multi-view correspondences across all training views using MAST3R, then triangulates each correspondence track via DLT plus nonlinear reprojection-error minimization to obtain 3D desired control points. Second, it backprojects the monocular depth of a query image into a dense pointmap and defines a Thin Plate Spline (TPS) deformation—comprising a global affine term and a local non-affine radial-basis term with U(r)=r—that exactly interpolates the initial (backprojected) control points to the desired (triangulated) control points while minimizing bending energy, yielding calibrated backprojected points (CBP) for every pixel. Third, Calibrated Backprojected Points Sampling (CBPS) selects only CBP within a scene-scale-fraction radius of the triangulated control points, ensuring the initialization is both dense and geometrically anchored to reliable geometry. The resulting point set is a plug-and-play module (TWINGS-Init) that can replace or augment the COLMAP PCL in any 3DGS pipeline; during training the paper additionally applies DropGaussian's structural regularization and a depth-consistency loss against the monocular estimate.

## Results

On DTU (the most challenging sparse-view benchmark), TWINGS achieves the best PSNR of 21.52 dB (3-view), 25.81 dB (6-view), and 28.22 dB (9-view), along with the lowest LPIPS (0.107/0.063/0.040) and AVGE (0.069/0.039/0.024), outperforming the strongest 3DGS baseline DropGaussian by +3.11 dB PSNR in the 3-view setting. On Mip-NeRF360 (12-view), TWINGS reaches 20.35 dB PSNR and 0.618 SSIM, surpassing DropGaussian (19.74 dB, 0.577 SSIM). On LLFF (3-view), TWINGS attains 21.49 dB PSNR and 0.167 LPIPS, beating CoMapGS (21.11 dB, 0.182 LPIPS), though CoMapGS edges ahead in the 6- and 9-view settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DTU, TWINGS dominates all 10 baselines across every metric and every view setting (3/6/9 views), with the largest margin over DropGaussian (+3.11 dB PSNR in 3-view) and CoR-GS (+2.31 dB PSNR in 3-view). On Mip-NeRF360 (12-view), TWINGS leads in PSNR (+0.61 dB over DropGaussian) and SSIM (+0.041 over CoMapGS), but in the 24-view setting it ties DropGaussian on SSIM (0.762) and trails it on LPIPS (0.252 vs. 0.225). On LLFF, TWINGS wins the critical 3-view setting across all metrics (21.49 dB PSNR, 0.754 SSIM, 0.167 LPIPS vs. CoMapGS's 21.11/0.747/0.182), but CoMapGS surpasses TWINGS in the 6-view (25.20 vs. 24.97 dB PSNR) and 9-view (26.73 vs. 26.23 dB PSNR) settings, suggesting the initialization advantage diminishes as more views provide stronger geometric constraints. NeRF-based methods (Mip-NeRF, DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) are consistently outperformed by all 3DGS-based methods, with TWINGS extending that gap further. The plug-and-play ablation (Table 5) confirms that adding TWINGS-Init to vanilla 3DGS alone yields +2.56 dB PSNR, making it competitive with prior SOTA without any architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

TWINGS positions itself within the sparse-view 3DGS literature by targeting the initialization bottleneck that prior methods (FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian, DropGaussian, CoMapGS) address through training-time modifications such as densification strategies, co-regularization, depth regularization, or dropout. Rather than altering the optimization loop, TWINGS introduces a plug-and-play initialization module that uses Thin Plate Splines to perform non-rigid warp alignment between monocular depth backprojections and multi-view triangulated control points, departing from earlier depth-alignment approaches (linear scaling, FFD) that are limited to rigid or lattice-based corrections. The method leverages MAST3R for dense multi-view correspondences and Depth Anything V2 for monocular depth, combining these with classical multi-view triangulation to produce geometrically calibrated initialization points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a well-calibrated, dense initialization alone—without any training-loop modifications—can make vanilla 3DGS competitive with state-of-the-art sparse-view methods, suggesting that initialization quality is a more fundamental bottleneck than previously acknowledged. The plug-and-play design (12-second overhead) makes it broadly applicable across 3DGS variants. However, the authors note the benefit tapers as view count increases or in texture-rich scenes where SfM already recovers reliable geometry, limiting the method's scope to genuinely sparse settings. Open questions include whether the TPS-based alignment generalizes to unbounded or dynamic scenes, and whether the approach can be extended to real-time 3-view surface reconstruction for AR/VR applications.
