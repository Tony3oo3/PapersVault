## Problem

Existing image retrieval methods rank by visual similarity and are trained to be invariant to capture time, while geo-localization models encode image, location, and time independently and align only final embeddings via contrastive loss. No prior approach can retrieve an image captured at the same physical location as a query but at a specified target time, despite large seasonal, lighting, or structural appearance changes, because none explicitly models how visual appearance at a fixed location evolves over time through cross-modal interaction.

## Input / Output

TIGeR accepts any subset of three modalities — an RGB image (encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14), 2D GPS coordinates, and a timestamp — each projected into token sequences via modality-specific encoders (patch embeddings for images, random Fourier features for GPS and time). At inference the most common configuration is a query image plus a target timestamp, which are fused into a single geo-temporal embedding and matched by cosine similarity against precomputed gallery image embeddings. The same shared embedding space also supports image-only geo-localization and image (or image+location) time-of-capture prediction, with an entropy-adaptive reranking step that blends continuous retrieval scores with discrete HEALPix or time-bin classifier priors.

## Method

TIGeR's architecture has three stages: modality-specific encoders produce token sequences for image, location, and time; a shared multi-modal transformer then processes all six possible modality combinations (V, L, T, V∥L, V∥T, L∥T) via self-attention, allowing each modality to attend to the others and learn fine-grained geo-temporal correlations rather than aligning independently learned embeddings post hoc; pooled tokens are projected and L2-normalized into a shared d-dimensional geo-temporal space. Training combines five InfoNCE contrastive losses across all meaningful modality pairs with two auxiliary classification losses using soft metric targets — Haversine distance on HEALPix cells for location and geodesic distance on a flat torus for time — that enforce smooth, structured organization of the embedding space. At test time, an entropy-adaptive reranking scheme weights the discrete classifier prior by (1 − H(ŷ)/log B), so the prior strongly influences retrieval only when the classifier is confident, mitigating boundary effects and domain shift.

## Results

On the primary geo-time aware image retrieval task, TIGeR achieves R@1=3.51%, R@5=23.30%, R@10=37.51% on TIGeR-Test-86K and R@1=14.55%, R@5=24.46%, R@10=29.98% on CVT, outperforming the strongest baselines by up to 14% in recall. For time-of-capture prediction, TIGeR reduces time-of-year error to 51.49° (TIGeR-Test-86K) and 62.88° (CVT) from image alone, and further to 48.86° and 47.00° when GPS is provided as auxiliary input, a 16% improvement over the best prior method. On geo-localization, TIGeR reaches 48.63% within 200 km and 65.61% within 750 km on TIGeR-Test-86K, and 51.90% / 66.53% on CVT, surpassing GeoCLIP and GT-Loc on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TIGeR-Test-86K, TIGeR dominates all baselines across every recall rank, achieving 3.51% R@1 versus 2.60% for Zhai et al. w/ CLIP (the strongest baseline) and 0.34% for GT-Loc, a roughly 3.5× improvement. On CVT, the picture is more nuanced: GT-Loc edges out TIGeR on R@1 (16.45% vs. 14.55%), but TIGeR recovers and surpasses it on R@5 (24.46% vs. 20.44%) and R@10 (29.98% vs. 22.59%), indicating better ranking of the top-10 candidates. For time-of-capture prediction, TIGeR (I→t) reduces time-of-year error to 51.49° on TIGeR-Test-86K, beating Zhai et al. w/ CLIP (57.51°) by 6° and GT-Loc (74.58°) by 23°; with GPS input (Il→t), the gap widens to 48.86° vs. 55.15° for the best baseline. On geo-localization, TIGeR substantially outperforms all baselines on TIGeR-Test-86K (48.63% at 200 km vs. 28.58% for Zhai et al. w/ CLIP) and also leads on CVT (51.90% vs. 43.62% for GeoCLIP at 200 km), confirming that the multi-modal transformer's cross-attention provides a consistent advantage over independent-encoder + contrastive-alignment approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIGeR directly extends GT-Loc [29] from the same group, which learned a shared embedding space for image, location, and time but processed each modality independently and aligned them only through contrastive loss at the embedding level. The key architectural departure is replacing this post-hoc alignment with a multi-modal transformer that enables cross-modal self-attention, allowing the model to learn how visual appearance at a fixed location evolves over time rather than merely matching independently learned representations. The contrastive training paradigm follows the CLIP-style lineage (GeoCLIP, SatCLIP), and the dataset curation builds on the AMOS webcam corpus and CVT, but the entropy-adaptive reranking and soft-target classification with HEALPix/torus geometry are novel additions to the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a practically relevant task (retrieving an image of the same place at a different time) that is central to digital forensics, environmental monitoring, and cultural heritage, yet had no dedicated benchmark or method. The 4.5M-image training set and 86k-image test set with balanced global coverage and no camera overlap between splits fill a significant gap for geo-temporal research. However, absolute retrieval recall remains low (3.5% R@1), and the method's reliance on a frozen CLIP ViT backbone and webcam imagery limits generalizability to arbitrary user-captured photos or indoor scenes. Open questions include whether the cross-modal attention mechanism scales to video or multi-view settings, and whether the unified representation degrades gracefully when one modality is entirely missing at inference.
