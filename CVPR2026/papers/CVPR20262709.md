## Problem

Learned world models excel at interpolative generalization but fail at extrapolation to novel physical properties (e.g., unseen mass ratios, friction, gravity) because they capture pixel-level statistical correlations rather than the environment's underlying generative rules—specifically, the conservation laws derived from physical symmetries. The paper argues that explicitly learning these invariances is the key to building world models grounded in physical reality, and that passive data collection is insufficient: the agent must actively probe the system to discover its symmetries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2D RGB pixel observations from 3D physics environments (DMCS, GymFetch) along with discrete/continuous agent actions. The SAVi-based encoder maps each observation to N object-centric slots, each structured as a pair of generalized coordinates q and canonical momenta p, yielding a viewpoint-invariant latent state Z_t. The Hamiltonian H_φ(Z_t) outputs a scalar energy value, the symplectic integrator produces the next-state mean, and a GRU maintains a deterministic recurrent state h_t for the decoder and policy. During inference, the model predicts the next observation image and the policy outputs actions entirely within imagination.

## Method

DreamSAC integrates two core components. First, a Hamiltonian World Model replaces the standard RSSM dynamics predictor with a G-invariant internal Hamiltonian H_φ (implemented as a Lie Transformer enforcing SE(3) invariance by construction) and a learned input matrix g(q), whose vector field is integrated via a symplectic integrator to produce the next-state mean under a factorized Gaussian prior. A self-supervised contrastive viewpoint-robustness loss (L_vr) is added to the ELBO, applying opposing pressure to the reconstruction loss to force the encoder to factor out camera viewpoint and isolate a physically invariant latent state. Second, Symmetry Exploration provides an intrinsic reward r_sym ≈ |ΔH_φ| that motivates the agent to perform interactions causing the largest predicted energy change, thereby collecting the most physically informative data; this reward is annealed from a stable RND novelty bonus to the physics-based signal as H_φ matures. Downstream adaptation uses differentiated fine-tuning: the encoder is frozen, the Hamiltonian is fine-tuned at a small learning rate (its invariant architecture acting as a regularizer), and a new actor-critic is trained in imagination on extrinsic rewards.

## Results

On OOD generalization tasks (Table 2), DreamSAC achieves 22%–163% higher performance than DreamerV3 baselines. The most dramatic gains appear on parametric OOD: on Walker-walk with 1.5× unseen gravity, DreamSAC scores 499.91 vs. 189.76 (DreamerV3+Policy) and 167.52 (DreamerV3+RND), a ~163% improvement. On structural OOD, DreamSAC reaches 0.91 success rate on FetchReach (Unseen Goal) vs. 0.76 for DreamerV3+Policy. On world model prediction MSE (Table 1), DreamSAC achieves 0.2064 on Acrobot (H=16) vs. 0.7723 for DreamerV3+Policy, and 0.302 on FetchPush (H=8) vs. 1.048 for DreamerV3+Random.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamSAC is compared against two families of baselines built on DreamerV3: (1) DreamerV3+Policy (trained on its own policy replay) and (2) DreamerV3+RND (with Random Network Distillation curiosity). DreamSAC wins on all eight OOD sub-tasks in Table 2. The largest margin is on parametric OOD: on Walker-walk with unseen gravity, DreamSAC (499.91) outperforms DreamerV3+Policy (189.76) by 163% and DreamerV3+RND (167.52) by 198%. On structural OOD, DreamSAC leads on FetchReach Unseen Goal (0.91 vs. 0.76/0.72) and Reacher-hard Unseen View (321.90 vs. 265.33/313.97). On in-distribution tasks (Table 3), DreamSAC is competitive but not uniformly superior—e.g., it trails DreamerV3+RND on Hopper Hop (366.1 vs. 389.1) but leads on Walker Walk (996.5 vs. 979.3). The paper also includes a DreamSAC+Random ablation (random exploration instead of Symmetry Exploration), which consistently underperforms the full method on prediction MSE, confirming that the Hamiltonian-based curiosity signal is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamSAC builds on the DreamerV3 RSSM architecture but replaces its black-box dynamics predictor with a G-invariant Hamiltonian prior (Lie Transformer) and a symplectic integrator, drawing on the Hamiltonian Neural Network and Symplectic ODE-Net line of work. It departs from prior physics-structured models (HNNs, LNNs) that were limited to low-dimensional state inputs or offline settings by integrating the Hamiltonian prior into an end-to-end, pixel-based online MBRL agent. The object-centric encoder (SAVi) and the self-supervised contrastive viewpoint-robustness loss are borrowed from representation learning, but their specific role—resolving the reconstruction-vs-invariance conflict to feed a G-invariant Hamiltonian—is novel. For exploration, the paper extends RND by annealing into a physics-based curiosity signal (change in Hamiltonian) rather than relying on statistical novelty alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a well-recognized failure mode of world models—poor extrapolation to unseen physical parameters—by grounding dynamics in conservation laws rather than pixel statistics, and the 22–163% gains on OOD tasks (especially parametric shifts like 1.5× gravity) are substantial. However, all experiments are confined to relatively simple single-body DMCS and GymFetch environments; whether a single learned Hamiltonian can capture the richer, multi-body, contact-heavy physics of real-world robotics remains an open question. The contrastive viewpoint-invariance loss and the symmetry-aware curiosity reward are modular ideas that could transfer to other structured world-model settings, but their interaction with more complex encoders or higher-dimensional action spaces is untested.
