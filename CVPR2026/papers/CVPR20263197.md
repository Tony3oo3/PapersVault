## Problem

High-resolution (1024×1024) image generation via discrete tokenizers requires 4096 tokens under standard VQGAN-style 16× spatial downsampling, creating prohibitive computational costs and complicating integration with LLMs. Existing token compression methods (residual codebooks, 1D tokenization) struggle to preserve high-frequency details at this resolution. The paper asks whether discrete tokens can be aligned with the compact latent space of pretrained latent diffusion models to achieve faithful 1024-pixel reconstruction with only 256 tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, a 1024×1024 RGB image is first downsampled to 224–288 resolution before encoding into 256 discrete tokens; the decoder then reconstructs the full 1024×1024 image by operating in the LDM's 128×128 latent space. For text-to-image generation, a text prompt (≤20 words describing objects, attributes, and spatial relations) is encoded by a text encoder and fed to an autoregressive transformer that sequentially predicts the 256 tokens, which are then decoded into a 1024×1024 image. The unusual aspect is the resolution asymmetry: encoding operates on a heavily downsampled image while decoding reconstructs at 4× higher resolution via the pretrained LDM decoder.

## Method

LacTok comprises a transformer encoder, a quantized codebook, and a Latent Diffusion Decoder (LADD) built by freezing SDXL and cloning selected blocks with zero-convolution connections (ControlNet-style). Training proceeds in two phases: first, LADD is trained with diffusion loss to predict the LDM latent code conditioned on the quantized tokens; second, a consistency model (HyperSD for 1-step or TLCM for 2-step) is merged into LADD to collapse multi-step sampling into 1–2 steps, enabling direct pixel-level supervision via LPIPS reconstruction loss that corrects color/brightness discrepancies. For text-to-image generation, LacTokGen adds an autoregressive transformer (initialized from LlamaGen's 77M-parameter GPT-XL with RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and 2D RoPE) trained with cross-entropy loss and classifier-free guidance to predict the 256-token sequence from text. The key insight is that consistency models bridge the gap between the LDM's multi-step denoising and the need for direct pixel gradients, making the LDM usable as a differentiable decoder for discrete token supervision.

## Results

For 1024×1024 image reconstruction with only 256 tokens, LacTok-H achieves 10.80 rFID on MSCOCO-2017 5K, 2.78 rFID on ImageNet, and 11.34 rFID on MJHQ-5K, outperforming all compared tokenizers on complex-scene datasets. For text-to-image generation, LacTokGen-L* scores 0.73 on GenEval and 0.304 HPSv2 on MSCOCO-2017, surpassing SDXL (0.55 GenEval), SD3 (0.62), and FLUX.1-dev (0.68) on GenEval while matching or exceeding their HPSv2 scores. Inference time is 2.4 s per image on A800, substantially faster than FLUX.1-dev (50.2 s), Show-o (21.2 s), and LlamaGen (7.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LacTokGen-L* outperforms all autoregressive baselines by a wide margin: it beats LlamaGen by 0.41 on GenEval (0.73 vs 0.32) and 0.031 on HPSv2 (0.304 vs 0.273), HART by 0.17 on GenEval (0.73 vs 0.56), and Show-o by 0.20 on GenEval (0.73 vs 0.53). Against diffusion models, LacTokGen-L* surpasses SDXL by 0.18 on GenEval and SD3 by 0.11, while matching FLUX.1-dev on HPSv2 (0.304 vs 0.306) and beating it on GenEval (0.73 vs 0.68) at roughly 20× lower inference cost (2.4 s vs 50.2 s). For the tokenizer itself, LacTok-H achieves 10.80 rFID on MSCOCO-2017 5K, beating LlamaGen (11.23), FlexTok (13.08), and TiTok-S-128 (12.31), though TiTok and FlexTok achieve slightly lower rFID on object-centric ImageNet (2.32 and 2.00 vs 2.78); however, LacTok-H leads on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on ImageNet. SeedTok performs poorly across all datasets (23.28 rFID on MSCOCO), confirming the difficulty of using a raw LDM decoder without the proposed pixel reconstruction loss. The paper notes that lower rFID on ImageNet does not necessarily translate to better generative quality, as LacTok-H* (trained on curated data) achieves higher ImageNet rFID but superior T2I scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discrete image tokenization paradigm (VQGAN, LlamaGen) by replacing the conventional pixel-space decoder with a pretrained latent diffusion model (SDXL), inspired by ControlNet's zero-convolution + cloned-blocks architecture. It further departs from prior tokenizers (TiTok, FlexTok, SeedTok) that compress tokens for 256×256 images by targeting 1024×1024 reconstruction with the same 256-token budget, and introduces a two-stage training objective—diffusion loss followed by pixel reconstruction loss via consistency models (HyperSD/TLCM)—to resolve color/brightness drift inherent in LDM-based decoding. The autoregressive generation component (LacTokGen) reuses LlamaGen's GPT-XL backbone and flan-t5-xl text encoder, positioning the contribution squarely as a tokenizer innovation rather than a new generation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× token compression for 1024×1024 images (256 vs. 4096) makes discrete-token autoregressive T2I generation and LLM integration substantially more tractable, and the recipe—freeze an LDM, attach a ControlNet-style trainable copy, then add consistency-model acceleration for pixel-level supervision—is a practical template likely to be adopted by other groups. A notable empirical finding is that lower rFID on ImageNet does not predict better generative quality, suggesting reconstruction benchmarks may mislead tokenizer selection for generation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other LDM backbones (e.g., FLUX, SD3), whether the 256-token ceiling can be pushed further without quality loss, and whether the method extends to video or 3D generation where token budgets are even tighter.
