## Problem

Extracting high-fidelity PBR materials (albedo, normal, roughness, height) from a single in-the-wild image is an ill-posed inverse problem because surface appearance entangles intrinsic material properties with unknown geometry and illumination. Existing single-image methods either bake perspective distortions into recovered materials via instance-specific LoRA fine-tuning (Material Palette) or impose an incorrect sequential/temporal dependency on a fundamentally static estimation task (MaterialPicker's video DiT), leading to error propagation and degraded fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image under unconstrained real-world conditions plus a user-provided segmentation mask (obtainable via SAM or manual annotation) and produces four tileable PBR material maps: albedo, normal, roughness, and height. A pre-trained monocular depth estimation model is additionally invoked at inference to supply the geometric prior for coarse rectification. Thanks to the resolution-agnostic KV-injection conditioning, the model can generate material maps at arbitrary output resolutions from a reference image of any resolution, decoupling input and output spatial dimensions.

## Method

MatE employs a coarse-to-fine pipeline that decomposes the difficult end-to-end inverse mapping. First, a geometric rectification module uses a pre-trained depth map to unproject the masked region into 3D and resample it into a canonical 2D view via a splatting operation, with neighborhood-averaging hole-filling to produce a dense, coarsely rectified texture. Second, a dual-branch diffusion model (Reference U-Net + Main U-Net) operates in VAE latent space: the Reference U-Net encodes the masked input image to extract multi-scale KV features, which are injected into the Main U-Net's self-attention layers to guide parallel denoising of all four material maps (channel-concatenated into a single 16-channel latent). Training is supervised on a synthetic dataset where planar meshes are deformed via thin-plate spline transformations, textured with MatSynth PBR materials, and rendered under randomized HDRI illumination and camera poses; camera azimuth is used to rotation-align ground-truth material maps, enforcing view-dependent consistency without requiring the model to learn a canonical orientation prior.

## Results

On the real-world Polyhaven dataset (226 pairs), MatE achieves the best LPIPS across all four material attributes, ranging from 0.342 (roughness) to 0.489 (height), and the best CLIP-Score for albedo (0.731) and roughness (0.766). On the synthetic Blender-rendered dataset (717 pairs), MatE again leads on LPIPS for every attribute (0.345–0.508) and on CLIP-Score for albedo (0.744) and roughness (0.753). MatE is the only method that produces all four PBR maps (albedo, normal, roughness, height); Material Palette omits height entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatE outperforms Material Palette [24] on every reported metric (LPIPS, SSIM, CLIP) for all three shared attributes on both datasets, with the largest LPIPS gaps on albedo (0.445 vs. 0.701 real; 0.423 vs. 0.529 synthetic). Material Palette also fails to produce height maps, whereas MatE recovers all four PBR channels. Against MaterialPicker [25], MatE achieves lower LPIPS on all four attributes on both datasets (e.g., 0.395 vs. 0.552 for normal on real-world; 0.345 vs. 0.460 for roughness on synthetic), and higher CLIP-Score for albedo and roughness. The one consistent weakness is normal-map SSIM, where MaterialPicker scores higher (0.317 vs. 0.248 real; 0.406 vs. 0.303 synthetic), suggesting MatE's normal maps differ structurally from ground truth even when perceptually closer. The paper also demonstrates that MaterialPicker's sequential video-DiT design causes cumulative error propagation (a wrong normal map degrades the subsequent roughness prediction), whereas MatE's parallel architecture isolates per-attribute errors.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatE builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the KV-injection conditioning mechanism from cross-image attention work (Zhou et al. 2024, Tumanyan et al. 2023), repurposing them for the specific challenge of material map generation from a single in-the-wild image. It departs conceptually from Material Palette (Lopes et al. 2024), which relies on LoRA/DreamBooth fine-tuning and thus bakes perspective distortion into recovered materials, and from MaterialPicker (Ma et al. 2025), which imposes a sequential video-DiT structure on an inherently parallel, static estimation problem. The key architectural departure is the coarse-to-fine decomposition: an explicit depth-based geometric rectification stage precedes the diffusion-based material extraction, rather than expecting a single end-to-end model to implicitly invert severe non-linear perspective distortion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MatE offers a practical path toward democratizing PBR material creation from casual smartphone photos, removing the need for specialized capture rigs or expert post-processing. The geometric rectification module's demonstrated plug-and-play utility (improving Material Palette without retraining) suggests it could be adopted as a preprocessing step in other material or texture pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges limitations with non-static textures possessing strong regular internal structure, highly complex geometry beyond what coarse depth-based unprojection can resolve, and extreme specular highlights that defeat illumination invariance. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-planar or curved surfaces and whether the depth-estimation dependency introduces a hard failure mode in textureless or reflective regions.
