## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning systems rely on text-only chain-of-thought, rigid schema-based pipelines (e.g., fixed bounding-box coordinate prediction), or single-step workflows, making them inflexible, task-specific, and unable to dynamically compose tools or validate intermediate steps against visual evidence. Moreover, prior methods do not address when a model should invoke tools, leading to degenerate tool underuse on hard tasks or overuse on easy ones. The proprietary o3 system demonstrates 'thinking with images' but remains a black box, leaving the field without an open, verifiable, and generalizable medium for executable visual reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text prompt, then generates multi-turn rollouts that interleave natural-language reasoning with executable Python code snippets (e.g., PIL cropping, OpenCV drawing, matplotlib plotting). Each code snippet is executed in a sandbox, and the resulting visual artifact (cropped region, annotated image, plot) is concatenated back into the context as new visual evidence for the next reasoning turn. The process iterates up to 6 turns during training and 10 during evaluation, terminating when the model emits a final text answer or the turn budget is exhausted.

## Method

CodeDance uses executable code as a unified reasoning medium, allowing the MLLM to define, compose, and execute diverse visual-symbolic operations (cropping, drawing, plotting, counting) that produce intermediate artifacts for transparent, self-checkable reasoning. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) SFT on a 34K curated dataset built via weak-to-strong filtering and multi-turn atomic supervision, teaching fundamental code-based skills; (2) RL using GRPO with a novel difficulty-adaptive reward R_BAT that decomposes into a sequence-level component R_seq (a sigmoid-scaled factor conditioned on group-level accuracy, suppressing tool incentives on easy problems and amplifying them on hard ones) and a turn-level component R_turn (a discounted penalty for failed code executions that provides dense correction signals). This two-level reward design balances exploration with efficiency, mitigates tool spamming and reward hacking, and prevents entropy collapse during training. Built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the framework empirically exhibits emergent behaviors during RL—including novel tool compositions, cross-task transfer of visual operations, and generation of unseen code routines—without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

CodeDance-7B achieves 91.2 on CountBench, 77.1 on PixmoCount, 84.8 on V* Bench, 75.2 on HR-Bench 4K, 72.3 on HR-Bench 8K, and 87.5 on ChartQA, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by 19.2%, 53.0%, 11.0%, 9.0%, 9.5%, and 1.4% respectively. On math reasoning, it reaches 29.6 on MathVision, 70.3 on MathVista, 46.8 on MathVerse, and 39.6 on WeMath, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 4.6, 2.2, 1.7, and 4.2 points. Notably, CodeDance-7B exceeds the 4× larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B on CountBench (91.2 vs. 87.8), PixmoCount (77.1 vs. 56.0), and HR-Bench 8K (72.3 vs. 71.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, CodeDance-7B wins decisively on visual search (V* Bench 84.8 vs. 67.5; HR-Bench 8K 72.3 vs. 59.6) and counting (CountBench 91.2 vs. 87.9), but trails on CharXiv (44.1 vs. 47.1), MathVision (29.6 vs. 36.5), and WeMath (39.6 vs. 44.2). Versus the same-size Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CodeDance improves every benchmark, with the largest gains on PixmoCount (+26.7 pts) and CountBench (+14.7 pts). Against the 4× larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B, CodeDance-7B surpasses it on CountBench (91.2 vs. 87.8) and PixmoCount (77.1 vs. 56.0) but falls short on V* Bench (84.8 vs. 85.9) and CharXiv (44.1 vs. 47.6). Among tool-integrated baselines, CodeDance outperforms DeepEyes-7B on CountBench (91.2 vs. 80.4), PixmoCount (77.1 vs. 57.2), and ChartQA (87.5 vs. 78.2), yet loses on V* Bench (84.8 vs. 90.4); it beats Thyme-VL-7B on CountBench (91.2 vs. 84.8) and HR-Bench 8K (72.3 vs. 72.0) but trails on HR-Bench 4K (75.2 vs. 77.0). Overall, CodeDance-7B achieves the best or near-best results across all seven visual reasoning benchmarks while remaining competitive on math, demonstrating that code-based tool integration at 7B scale can rival or exceed much larger models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CodeDance extends the GRPO-based RL framework from DeepSeekMath by introducing a two-level (sequence-level + turn-level) reward structure tailored to multi-turn tool reasoning, and departs from prior tool-integrated MLLMs (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Thyme, OpenThinkImg) by replacing fixed-schema operations (e.g., predicting bounding-box coordinates) with executable code as a unified, composable medium. Conceptually, it operationalizes the 'thinking with images' paradigm demonstrated by OpenAI's o3 in an open, verifiable form, while addressing a gap those systems leave open: when and how often tools should be invoked. The difficulty-adaptive reward (R_BAT) is the key methodological departure, conditioning tool incentives on group-level accuracy to balance exploration against efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the reasoning medium executable code rather than a rigid schema, CodeDance offers a more general and transferable substrate for multimodal tool use, and the observed emergent behaviors (spontaneous tool composition, cross-domain transfer, generation of unseen OpenCV code) suggest that code-based reasoning may scale in ways schema-based pipelines cannot. However, the evidence for emergent capabilities is primarily qualitative (a handful of Figure 3 examples), and the quantitative gains, while consistent, are moderate relative to the 7B baseline. Open questions include whether these emergent behaviors persist and strengthen at larger scales (the 3B→7B scaling study is encouraging but limited), whether the code medium truly outperforms well-engineered schema-based approaches on novel tool suites, and how the difficulty-adaptive reward generalizes to tasks with highly variable complexity distributions.
