## Problem

Current multimodal intent recognition methods treat semantic cues as flat representations, overlooking the hierarchical structure (localized tokens → mid-level concepts → inter-concept relations) that underlies complex human intents. Additionally, existing approaches rely on static, suboptimal reasoning processes and lack the capacity for self-evolving refinement during inference, limiting their adaptability to diverse and nuanced multimodal scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263949/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips and accompanying text, which are encoded by Qwen2-VL into a unified token sequence in a shared semantic space. The hierarchical pipeline progressively compresses these tokens into a configurable number of mid-level concept vectors (e.g., 40–50) and a smaller set of inter-concept relation vectors (e.g., 20–25), which are injected as special tokens into a structured CoT prompt. The final output is a single intent class label drawn from a task-specific taxonomy (20, 30, or 12 classes depending on the benchmark).

## Method

HIER builds on Qwen2-VL (fine-tuned via LoRA, rank 8) and introduces a three-level hierarchical semantic representation. First, a label-guided Spherical K-Means++ clustering groups modality-specific tokens into mid-level concepts, where each centroid is refined by a convex combination with intent label embeddings weighted by cosine similarity. Second, an information bottleneck network encodes pairwise concept interactions, and Jensen-Shannon divergence between relation classification logits and individual concept logits scores each relation's semantic novelty; only top-ranked relations are retained. Third, a structured three-stage CoT prompt (context understanding → concept analysis → relational reasoning) guides Qwen2-VL through progressive inference, while a self-evolution module projects concept and relation hidden states through the model's generation head, extracts normalized Yes/No logits as confidence scores, and multiplicatively modulates the features to refine reasoning adaptively. The overall training objective combines autoregressive language modeling loss with a relation-guided cross-entropy auxiliary loss (β = 0.01).

## Results

On MIntRec, HIER achieves 80.00% ACC and 76.91% F1, surpassing the strongest MLLM baseline Qwen2-VL (76.56% ACC, 74.59% F1) by 3.44% and 2.32% respectively. On the more challenging MIntRec2.0, HIER reaches 64.15% ACC and 60.31% F1, outperforming Qwen2-VL by 4.33% ACC and 12.58% F1. On MELD-DA, HIER attains 61.95% ACC and 54.80% F1, exceeding the best baseline VideoLLaMA2 (60.81% ACC, 51.24% F1) by 1.14% and 3.56%. Across all three datasets, HIER delivers the highest F1 scores with margins of 7.67%, 5.57%, and 3.34% over the second-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIER outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Against the strongest MLLM baseline Qwen2-VL, HIER gains 3.44% ACC and 2.32% F1 on MIntRec, and a substantial 4.33% ACC and 12.58% F1 on MIntRec2.0, where Qwen2-VL's F1 drops to 47.73% due to the larger 30-class taxonomy. On MELD-DA, VideoLLaMA2 is the closest competitor (60.81% ACC, 51.24% F1), but HIER still leads by 1.14% ACC and 3.56% F1. Among dedicated intent recognition methods, MVCL-DAF and MIntOOD are the strongest baselines on MIntRec (73.63% and 73.48% ACC), yet HIER surpasses them by over 6% ACC. The gap widens considerably on MIntRec2.0, where HIER's 64.15% ACC exceeds the best SOTA method (MAG-BERT at 60.38%) by nearly 4 points, and its 60.31% F1 leads by over 5 points, confirming that hierarchical semantic modeling and self-evolving reasoning provide the largest gains on fine-grained, multi-class intent tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HIER builds directly on Qwen2-VL as its MLLM backbone, repurposing its encoders and generation head for hierarchical semantic extraction and self-evaluation. Conceptually, it departs from prior multimodal intent recognition methods (TCL-MAP, SDIF-DA, MIntOOD, MVCL-DAF) that focus on cross-modal fusion or contrastive alignment without explicitly modeling the layered structure of semantic information (tokens → concepts → relations). It also extends the self-evolution literature for MLLMs (RLAIF-V, SIMA, SENA), which relies on preference-based alignment, by introducing an internal feedback loop that refines intermediate representations during inference rather than during training. The closest prior work, LGSRR, uses LLM reasoning for intent understanding but is noted by the authors as having a 'relatively shallow' reasoning process dependent on specific semantic concepts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize a multi-level progressive reasoning paradigm for multimodal intent recognition, and the self-evolution mechanism that dynamically refines semantic features via MLLM feedback during inference is a practical contribution that could transfer to other structured reasoning tasks. However, the method remains tied to specific MLLM backbones and the gains, while consistent (1–3%), are moderate; the paper acknowledges persistent gaps on semantically inconsistent categories (Taunt, Flaunt, Leave) where multimodal cues conflict. Open questions include whether the hierarchical clustering-and-selection pipeline scales to open-vocabulary or out-of-distribution intent settings, and whether the self-evolution feedback loop can be extended beyond binary affirmative/negative scoring to richer self-critique.
