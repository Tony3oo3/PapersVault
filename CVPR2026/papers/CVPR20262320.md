## Problem

Medical vision-language models inherit and compound biases from both visual and textual sources, yet existing fairness mitigation approaches suffer from intersectional blindness—debiasing one attribute (e.g., race) inadvertently amplifies disparities along another (e.g., gender). Conventional prompt learning processes samples in isolation without inter-sample relational context, making it fundamentally incapable of capturing the fine-grained, attribute-conditioned dependencies required for equitable performance across intersectional demographic subgroups.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (SLO fundus photograph or OCT scan) and an optional clinical text note as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, along with a sensitive attribute vector encoding race and gender. It produces a diagnostic class label (e.g., glaucoma present/absent) and an adaptive context prompt structured as [T_prefix; C_final; T_suffix], where C_final fuses local instance features with global relational fairness context propagated through the graph. Notably, during inference the relational graph is constructed from visual features alone without requiring sensitive attributes, while the fairness modulator that uses explicit demographic labels operates only during training.

## Method

FRP reformulates prompt generation as a dynamic, fairness-aware reasoning process by representing each mini-batch as a relational graph with two complementary adjacency components: a hyperbolic structural prior that maps concatenated CLIP image-text features into the Poincaré ball to capture latent demographic hierarchy, and a fairness-conditioned modulator that uses an MLP over paired sensitive attributes to produce attribute-aware edge weights. A unified adjacency matrix (learnable convex combination of both) drives a Hyperbolic Graph Layer that performs message passing natively in hyperbolic space via tangent-space linear transformations and Möbius addition, preserving hierarchical geometry without Euclidean distortion. The HGL outputs are then fed into a dual-path prompt synthesis module: a local path pools instance-level image features, while a global path fuses the HGL's image and text representations through cross-attention and mean pooling, and the two paths are averaged and added to static base prompts. Training jointly optimizes a CLIP-style contrastive task loss and a fairness loss defined as the variance of group-wise average losses across intersectional demographic groups, with a theoretical information-gain theorem (Theorem 3.1) formally justifying that relational and intersectional modeling strictly increases fairness-conditioned mutual information over independent sample processing.

## Results

On the FairVLMed benchmark, FRP achieves the highest AUC of 77.50% while simultaneously attaining the lowest racial disparity (DPD = 4.14, DEOdds = 6.37) and gender disparity (DPD = 0.38, DEOdds = 2.16) among all compared methods. On the vision-only Harvard-GF benchmark, FRP reaches 85.94% AUC (exceeding BiomedCLIP by 2.82 points) with the best ES-AUC of 81.32 and the lowest DEOdds of 8.67 under race. FRP also yields the highest group-wise AUC for Asian (81.25%) and Black (78.19%) patients on FairVLMed, demonstrating that the accuracy-fairness trade-off is resolved rather than merely shifted.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRP outperforms all nine baselines on both AUC and fairness metrics across both datasets. On FairVLMed (Race), FRP achieves 77.50% AUC, surpassing the strongest prompt-tuning baselines MaPLe (75.19%) and VPT (74.98%) by 2.3 and 2.5 points respectively, while reducing DPD from 8.51/7.82 to 4.14 and DEOdds from 10.82/15.73 to 6.37. The dedicated fairness method FairCLIP achieves lower AUC (70.24%) and higher disparity (DPD 6.07, DEOdds 10.50) than FRP, confirming that distribution-alignment alone is insufficient. Medical-domain CLIP variants (BiomedCLIP, MedCLIP, PubMedCLIP) all trail FRP by 5–9 AUC points and exhibit substantially worse fairness (DEOdds 7.99–18.05 vs. 6.37). On Harvard-GF, FRP reaches 85.94% AUC, exceeding BiomedCLIP (83.12%) by 2.82 points and achieving the lowest DEOdds (8.67) and best ES-AUC (81.32), demonstrating that the relational-hyperbolic design generalizes from vision-language to vision-only settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads: hyperbolic graph neural networks (Chami et al., 2019) for hierarchical structure, relational/graph-based fairness modeling, and parameter-efficient prompt learning for CLIP-style VLMs (CoOp, MaPLe). It departs from FairCLIP's single-attribute distribution-alignment approach by explicitly modeling intersectional identities through a hyperbolic graph layer that operates on a dynamically constructed sample-level relational graph, and from standard prompt learning by embedding fairness reasoning directly into prompt generation rather than treating prompts as static conditioners. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) provides an information-theoretic justification for why relational + intersectional modeling yields quantifiable gains over independent sample processing, a formalization absent from prior fairness-in-VLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that single-attribute fairness optimization in medical VLMs can inadvertently worsen disparities along other demographic axes (intersectional blindness), a practical concern for clinical deployment that prior distribution-alignment methods do not address. However, several limitations temper the impact: the framework assumes binary gender labels, constructs graphs only within mini-batches (limiting scalability and cross-batch relational reasoning), and the hierarchical structure of intersectional identities is simplified. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic relational approach generalizes beyond glaucoma to broader medical imaging tasks, how it scales to more than two sensitive attributes, and whether the mini-batch graph construction can be replaced with a global or amortized mechanism without losing the fine-grained fairness signal.
