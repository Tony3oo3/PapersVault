## Problem

Existing dynamic novel view synthesis datasets suffer from limited dynamic content (typically under 13% of pixels), few testing cameras (often just 1), imperfect or estimated camera poses, and a lack of diverse camera configurations within the same scenes. Additionally, emerging feed-forward 3D foundational models are evaluated almost exclusively on unimodal benchmarks, leaving the interplay between geometry and appearance outputs unassessed. This paper addresses these gaps by introducing a unified benchmark that covers static and dynamic reconstruction across dense, sparse, and monocular setups with ground-truth poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262121/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides posed RGB images at 2048×858 resolution and 96 fps across 8 scenes, accompanied by metric depth, surface normals, object segmentation, optical flow, UV maps, and dynamic content masks. Three camera configurations are offered per scene: Dense (25 train + 16 test cameras), Sparse (3/6/9 train + 10 test cameras from a non-overlapping perspective), and Mono (4 trajectory types × 4 static test cameras + 4 relative test cameras). For NVS evaluation of foundational models, two protocols are used: one aligns target cameras to predictions via Umeyama fitting, and the other re-runs the model on source+target views to render from predicted target poses.

## Method

The dataset is rendered from the Blender Studio animated short film 'Charge', with the original postprocessing pipeline removed to ensure 3D consistency and replaced with direct camera rendering plus additional modality passes. Camera rigs are placed on a spherical section (forward-facing) with per-scene manual adjustment of rig position and radius. The monocular setup introduces four trajectory types—Spline Fast/Slow and Random Walk Fast/Slow—designed from empirical phone-camera velocity measurements (15–50 cm/s) to avoid the teleporting artifacts common in prior monocular benchmarks. A novel FOV overlap metric (Eq. 1) quantifies task difficulty by measuring the weighted covisibility between test and training views. The benchmark evaluates 11 methods spanning dynamic Gaussian Splatting (4DGS, STG, Ex4DGS, D-3DGS, SC-GS, MoSca) and static foundational models (VGGT, π³, AnySplat, WorldMirror, Off The Grid), reporting PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS with dynamic-only and static-only variants, camera pose AUC, and depth AbsRel/δ<1.25.

## Results

On the Dense setup, Ex4DGS achieves the best PSNR of 29.75 (SSIM 0.893, LPIPS 0.187), followed by STG (29.29) and 4DGS (28.94), indicating that even with 25 training cameras, dynamic reconstruction remains challenging. In the Mono-Spline Fast configuration, D-3DGS edges out 4DGS (24.88 vs. 24.85 PSNR) while achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.201 vs. 0.264), and MoSca—despite being best on DyCheck—drops to 23.39 PSNR. In the Sparse setup, performance degrades sharply with fewer views: 4DGS drops from 26.67 PSNR (9 views) to 19.71 (3 views), and the FOV overlap metric confirms the increasing difficulty (0.64 → 0.54). For the static foundational-model benchmark, Off The Grid leads camera pose estimation (AUC@5 = 0.6318 on Dense), π³ leads depth estimation (AbsRel = 0.0896 on Sparse-6), and WorldMirror/Off The Grid outperform AnySplat on NVS (PSNR 24.44/25.16 vs. 18.74 on Dense).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the Dense multi-view setup, Ex4DGS [16] outperforms both 4DGS [43] and STG [22] on all metrics (PSNR 29.75 vs. 28.94/29.29), with the gap widening on dynamic-only regions (PSNR-D 28.57 vs. 26.84/28.15). In the Sparse setup, 4DGS consistently leads across all view counts (3/6/9), while Ex4DGS narrows the gap at 9 views (PSNR 24.50 vs. 26.67) and STG trails most noticeably at 3 views (18.80 vs. 19.71). Among monocular methods, D-3DGS [44] and 4DGS [43] are closely matched on Spline trajectories, but MoSca [17]—the top performer on DyCheck—underperforms on PSNR for Spline Fast (23.39) yet recovers to lead all methods on Random Walk Slow (24.29 PSNR, 0.871 SSIM), suggesting its trajectory-estimation preprocessing struggles with large, structured motions. SC-GS [11] is consistently the weakest monocular method on PSNR but competitive on SSIM-D. For the static foundational-model benchmark, Off The Grid [27] dominates camera pose estimation (AUC@5: 0.6318 Dense vs. 0.5149 for VGGT [39] and 0.3359 for AnySplat [12]), while π³ [41] leads depth estimation (AbsRel 0.0896–0.1014 vs. 0.0959–0.1085 for VGGT). On NVS, WorldMirror [24] and Off The Grid [27] both surpass AnySplat by 5–7 dB PSNR on Dense (24.44/25.16 vs. 18.74), with Off The Grid also achieving the best LPIPS (0.4728), indicating that coupling NVS with geometry prediction in AnySplat introduces pose-shape ambiguity that degrades rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in dynamic NVS evaluation by drawing inspiration from MPI Sintel's synthetic-data philosophy but applying it to 3D reconstruction rather than optical flow. It departs from prior dynamic datasets (Neural 3D Video, Technicolor, DyCheck, Nerfies) by being rendered from a professional Blender animation, providing perfect camera poses, 96 fps capture, and—critically—unifying dense, sparse, and monocular setups within the same scenes with rich multi-modal annotations (depth, normals, segmentation, optical flow, UV maps). On the method side, it benchmarks the 3DGS-based dynamic reconstruction lineage (4DGS, STG, Ex4DGS, D-3DGS, SC-GS, MoSca) alongside the emerging feed-forward foundational model paradigm (VGGT, π³, AnySplat, WorldMirror, Off The Grid), bridging two communities that have previously been evaluated in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Charge addresses a critical evaluation gap: existing dynamic NVS datasets suffer from limited dynamic content, imperfect or estimated camera poses, and very few test views, making it difficult to fairly compare methods or diagnose failure modes. The introduction of the FOV overlap metric provides a principled, dataset-agnostic measure of task difficulty that enables meaningful cross-setup comparisons. Open questions remain about whether synthetic renderings fully capture real-world challenges (sensor noise, uncontrolled lighting, compression artifacts), and whether the findings on foundational models' pose-shape ambiguity will persist as those architectures mature. The dataset's scale (185,600 frames, 8 scenes, 6 modalities) and the unified static+dynamic benchmarking protocol position it as a likely standard reference for the next generation of 4D reconstruction and feed-forward 3D models.
