## Problem

Existing diffusion-based driving scene generators suffer from implicit inter-condition dependency between HD maps and 3D bounding boxes, causing generation failures when one condition is modified independently of the other. Additionally, brief view-invariant captions provide insufficient semantic context for high-fidelity background modeling, and uniform spatial weighting in the denoising loss neglects foreground structural details, leading to distortions and blurriness in generated objects and roads.

## Input / Output

The method consumes color-coded HD map layouts (driving area, lane, pedestrian crossing) and 3D bounding boxes (10 object categories) as image inputs fed through separate T2I-Adapter branches, alongside multi-view hierarchical text descriptions generated by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-72B model covering six semantic aspects (time, weather, road type, surroundings, objects, spatial relationships). Camera parameters ensure multi-view geometric consistency. The output is a set of six synchronized driving scene images at 224×480 resolution, generated via DDIM sampling with 25 steps and CFG scale 3.

## Method

DrivePTS builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1 with T2I-Adapter-based geometric conditioning and introduces a two-stage progressive learning strategy: Stage 1 alternates between road-only and object-only training with region-specific loss masking to decouple the two geometric conditions, while Stage 2 jointly adapts both adapters under a mutual information constraint (modified InfoNCE loss) that explicitly minimizes feature similarity between map and box representations. A VLM fine-tuned with LoRA and DPO on driving-domain data generates per-view hierarchical captions across six semantic dimensions, replacing the dataset's default brief descriptions. A frequency-guided structure loss applies a Fourier-based high-pass filter (cutoff τ=0.5) to the predicted and target images, weighting the denoising objective toward high-frequency structural components such as road edges and object contours. The overall training objective combines the standard diffusion loss, the frequency-guided loss (λ_freq=0.5), and the MI constraint (λ_MI=0.05) with region-specific masking that varies across training stages.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, DrivePTS achieves an FID of 11.45, outperforming the previous best (PerLDiff, 13.36) by 16.7% and MagicDrive (16.20) by 29.3%. For geometric controllability, it attains a road mIoU of 63.95 (+2.69 over PerLDiff), vehicle mIoU of 27.82 (+0.69), and 3D detection NDS of 25.49 (+1.44 over PerLDiff, +2.17 over MagicDrive). In a data-augmentation experiment, synthetic validation data from DrivePTS boosts BEV road segmentation to 67.49 mIoU, nearly matching the upper bound of real validation data (67.53) while all other generators lag by 0.93–1.79 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DrivePTS outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor PerLDiff, it reduces FID from 13.36 to 11.45, improves road mIoU by 2.69 points (61.26→63.95), vehicle mIoU by 0.69 (27.13→27.82), and NDS by 1.44 (24.05→25.49). Compared to MagicDrive, the gains are larger: FID drops by 4.75, road mIoU rises by 2.90, and NDS increases by 2.17. Panacea* trails DrivePTS on all metrics, with the largest gap in vehicle mIoU (22.77 vs. 27.82) and road mIoU (57.11 vs. 63.95). BEVControl and BEVGen report only partial metrics (no mAP/NDS/mAOE) and are substantially weaker on FID (24.85 and 25.54 respectively) and road mIoU. In the data-augmentation setting (Table 2), DrivePTS synthetic data yields a road mIoU of 67.49, only 0.04 below the real-data upper bound, whereas MagicDrive, Panacea, and PerLDiff fall short by 1.41, 0.93, and 1.79 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based driving scene generation line (MagicDrive, BEVControl, PerLDiff) by replacing the heavier ControlNet conditioning with the lighter T2I-Adapter, enabling two separate geometric condition branches (HD map and 3D bounding boxes) without prohibitive compute cost. It departs from prior joint-conditioning approaches by introducing a progressive learning schedule—learning road layout before object placement—combined with a mutual information constraint to explicitly decouple the two geometric modalities. The VLM-driven multi-view hierarchical captioning builds on recent findings that longer, more descriptive prompts improve diffusion quality, but applies it specifically to driving scenes with structured semantic axes (time, weather, road type, surroundings, objects, spatial relationships) and fine-tunes the VLM with LoRA and DPO for domain alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inter-condition dependency problem (e.g., a model refusing to generate a road where parked cars previously appeared) is a practical bottleneck for simulation-based validation of autonomous driving systems, and the progressive learning strategy offers a clean, interpretable solution. The near-parity of synthetic-data augmentation with real-data augmentation (67.49 vs. 67.53 road mIoU) suggests that high-fidelity scene generators can meaningfully reduce the cost of collecting rare driving scenarios. Open questions include whether the progressive strategy generalizes to more than two geometric conditions, whether the VLM captioning pipeline scales to video or higher-resolution generation, and whether the frequency-guided loss transfers to other structured-image domains beyond driving.
