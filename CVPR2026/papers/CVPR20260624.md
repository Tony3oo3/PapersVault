## Problem

Dynamic editing of static 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes with scene-wide weather effects faces a fundamental conflict: physics simulation provides plausible motion but cannot synthesize photorealistic appearance for new elements, while video-based Score Distillation Sampling alone cannot generate coherent motion for complex multi-particle scenarios requiring continuous particle emission. Existing static editing methods cannot model temporal evolution, and data-driven 4D generation methods fail to produce physically plausible dynamics across an entire scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260624/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images of a static scene (reconstructed into a 3DGS representation) and a text prompt describing the desired weather effect (e.g., 'snow falling', 'purple snow falling'). It produces a temporally evolving 3D Gaussian Splatting scene where dynamic particles are continuously emitted, interact with static geometry via mesh-based collision handling, and accumulate over time. The output supports arbitrary camera trajectories for rendering, and the text prompt can specify creative appearance variations beyond standard weather (e.g., glittering yellow sand, magical dust particles) while preserving the physically plausible motion of the corresponding effect.

## Method

The framework uses Material Point Method (MPM) simulation to generate reference particle trajectories that serve as a guidance prior rather than fixed motion. A recurrent neural dynamics model (state-specific MLPs with Fourier and sinusoidal encodings) is conditioned on these reference trajectories and predicts per-Gaussian velocity corrections, angular velocity, and appearance deltas at each timestep. The model is optimized end-to-end via a combined loss: Video-SDS for photorealistic motion and appearance refinement, plus physics regularization losses (position, velocity, rotation L2/angular distances to simulation) and appearance delta penalties to prevent recurrent drift. A key mechanism is SDS-adaptive physics guidance, where all regularization weights are dynamically scaled by the instantaneous Video-SDS loss magnitude, amplifying physics constraints when the diffusion model is uncertain and relaxing them when confident. Appearance parameters are initialized by an LLM from the text prompt, and the recurrent model evolves them additively over time.

## Results

Across five real-world scenes (Garden, Bicycle, Stump from Mip-NeRF 360; Playground, Truck from Tanks and Temples) and four weather effects (snowfall, rainfall, fog, sandstorm), the method achieves the highest scores on all five evaluation metrics. On the Fog+Snow subset, it reaches CLIPSim 0.29, CLIPDir 0.12, VQAScore 0.92, ViCLIP-T 0.20, and VE-Bench 0.45. Across all weather effects, it scores CLIPSim 0.28, CLIPDir 0.11, VQAScore 0.89, ViCLIP-T 0.19, and VE-Bench 0.41, with the largest margin on the video-specific VE-Bench metric (0.41 vs. 0.21 for GaussCtrl and 0.19 for Instruct-4DGS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against ClimateNeRF (static weather on NeRFs), it gains +0.06 CLIPSim, +0.05 CLIPDir, +0.05 VQAScore, +0.05 ViCLIP-T, and a substantial +0.17 VE-Bench on the Fog+Snow subset, reflecting that static methods cannot capture continuous particle emission and temporal accumulation. Against GaussCtrl (text-guided 3DGS editing), the advantage is consistent across all metrics, with the largest gap on VE-Bench (0.41 vs. 0.21 across all effects), confirming that static appearance edits lack the temporal coherence of physics-guided dynamics. Against Instruct-4DGS (4D editing via score distillation without physics), the method leads by +0.07 CLIPSim, +0.04 CLIPDir, +0.32 VQAScore, +0.06 ViCLIP-T, and +0.22 VE-Bench, demonstrating that without a physics-based motion prior, pure Video-SDS optimization produces incoherent, physically implausible multi-particle motion. The paper also notes that RainyGS, a rain-specific physics-based method, is not directly comparable because it does not generalize beyond rain and its code is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation, MPM-based physics simulation for motion, and Video-SDS for appearance optimization. Prior work splits into two camps—physics-based methods (PhysGaussian, PAC-NeRF, RainyGS) that produce plausible motion but cannot synthesize photorealistic appearance for newly introduced elements, and data-driven 4D editing methods (Instruct4DGS, Animate3D) that generate photorealistic appearance but fail at physically coherent multi-particle motion. The key departure is treating physics simulation not as a fixed motion constraint but as a *guidance prior* that conditions a recurrent neural dynamics model while Video-SDS jointly refines both motion and appearance, resolving what the authors frame as a fundamental conflict between the two paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical recipe for adding scene-wide dynamic weather effects (snow, rain, fog, sandstorm) to existing 3DGS reconstructions, a task that was previously either manual or limited to static/single-effect approaches. The ablation showing that fixing physics motion (w/o PG) prevents effective Video-SDS appearance optimization is a useful diagnostic: it reveals that the diffusion model needs freedom to adjust motion to satisfy the text prompt, not just to render appearance. Open questions include bidirectional interaction (dynamic effects deforming static geometry), updating static Gaussian appearance to reflect environmental changes (shadows, wetness), and whether the assumption that learned trajectories stay close to the physics prior holds for more extreme or long-horizon effects.
