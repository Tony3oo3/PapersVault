## Problem

Existing coreset selection methods either couple to a proxy DNN (introducing architectural bias) or rely on heuristics without theoretical guarantees, and neither explicitly enforces full distributional equivalence of the selected subset. Prevalent distribution metrics (MSE, KL, CE, MMD) are further limited in capturing higher-order moments and multivariate correlations. The paper tackles the core obstacle that continuous distribution matching was considered inapplicable to discrete dataset sampling, and that standard Characteristic Function Distance suffers from a vanishing phase gradient in medium-to-high frequency regions.

## Input / Output

FAST takes a full image dataset (ranging from 32×32 CIFAR/SVHN to 256×256 RESISC45, or text instruction data like Alpaca) and a target keep ratio (10%, 20%, or 30%) as input, and outputs a discrete subset of the original samples—no synthesis or augmentation is performed. The entire selection pipeline runs on CPU with as little as 1.7 GB memory (or on an edge device like RK3588 with 4 GB), requiring no GPU and no proxy DNN, which distinguishes it from DNN-based baselines that need forward/backward passes through a network during selection.

## Method

FAST formulates coreset selection as a graph-constrained continuous optimization problem in a spectral manifold feature space. It first builds a multi-scale fuzzy-topology graph (UMAP-style) and extracts Laplacian eigenvectors as manifold features; a continuous coreset representation is then optimized to match the reference distribution via a novel Attenuated Phase-Decoupled CFD (PD-CFD) loss that decouples phase from magnitude with an adaptive penalty λ_φ(ω)=λ_p/(1+α‖ω‖²) to counteract the vanishing phase gradient at medium/high frequencies. To bridge the continuous-to-discrete gap, three complementary constraints are imposed: a DPP-based diversity loss, a Hungarian-algorithm-driven positional alignment to real data points, and a Laplacian graph regularizer preserving local topology. A Progressive Discrepancy-Aware Sampling (PDAS) curriculum progressively expands the frequency band from low to high, with anisotropic frequency initialization and discrepancy-weighted importance sampling, ensuring stable convergence with a minimal set of discriminative frequencies.

## Results

FAST achieves an average accuracy improvement of 9.12% over the best DNN-free baseline (NMS) and 17.63% over DNN-based methods across six image benchmarks at 10/20/30% keep rates. On texture-rich datasets DTD and RESISC45, the gain reaches 21.93% on average, directly attributable to PD-CFD's ability to capture high-frequency structural details. FAST also reduces energy consumption by 96.57% and achieves a 2.2× average speedup over baselines while running entirely on CPU with 1.7 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAST outperforms all nine baseline families across every dataset and keep rate. The closest competitor is NMS (the only other DNN-free method), which FAST surpasses by 3.75% on CIFAR-10 (90.32 vs. 86.57) and by a wider margin on texture-rich DTD (45.77 vs. 38.63, +7.14%). Among DNN-based methods, the strongest submodular baselines (DQ, GC) trail FAST by 5.11% and 5.30% on CIFAR-10 respectively, while gradient-matching methods (Craig, GradM) and bilevel optimization (Glister) lag by 29–33% on CIFAR-10. On the high-resolution RESISC-45 dataset, FAST leads NMS by 10.74% (85.00 vs. 74.26) and the best DNN-based method (DQ) by 12.88%, confirming the advantage of frequency-domain distribution matching on detail-rich data. Cross-architecture transfer further differentiates FAST: when the coreset is retrained on a different backbone, FAST degrades by only 0.53% on average versus 8.68% for DNN-based methods, validating its architecture-agnostic design.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAST extends the frequency-domain distribution matching framework of NCFM [41]—which was confined to synthesis-based dataset distillation and relied on DNN feature extraction—into the discrete coreset selection domain by introducing topology-aware graph constraints (derived from UMAP's fuzzy topological theory [27]) that bridge the continuous-to-discrete gap. It departs from both DNN-based coreset methods (Craig, GradM, Glister), which introduce architectural bias, and existing DNN-free methods (NMS, Herding, kCenter), which rely on heuristics without distributional guarantees, by formulating selection as a graph-constrained optimization grounded in spectral graph theory. The novel PD-CFD loss and PDAS curriculum strategy are specific to this paper and address identified failure modes (vanishing phase gradient, frequency selection instability) not present in prior CFD applications to GANs or domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a DNN-free method can outperform DNN-based coreset selection by 17.63% on average, while reducing energy consumption by 96.57% and running on a 1.7 GB CPU, validates the hypothesis that architectural bias in proxy-model-based selection is a fundamental limitation rather than a minor trade-off. The 'write once, run anywhere' cross-architecture property (0.53% degradation vs. 8.68% for DNN-based) makes the selected coreset a reusable asset across downstream tasks. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets (beyond ~120k samples) where graph construction and spectral decomposition costs may dominate, whether the anisotropic frequency initialization generalizes to non-image modalities, and whether the PD-CFD phase penalty can be made fully adaptive rather than relying on a fixed decay schedule.
