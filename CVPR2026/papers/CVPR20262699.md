## Problem

Pre-trained foundation models (LLMs, VLMs, embedding models) struggle to recognize implicit and long-tail patterns in text and visual data, yet finetuning them is often infeasible due to limited training samples, high computational cost, and inaccessibility of closed-source model parameters. Existing data augmentation strategies rely on random generation, which is wasteful given the cost of advanced generative models and introduces high gradient variance that destabilizes few-shot convergence. The paper asks how to adaptively select which synthetic samples to generate at each training step to minimize gradient shifting and guarantee fast convergence with minimal data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query (a text passage or an RGB image) and a set of K class descriptions, and outputs a single class label via softmax over calibrated embedding inner products. Critically, the query and class descriptions are first passed through a frozen pre-trained embedder (e.g., CLIP-ViT-Large for images, OpenAI-text-embedding-3-small for text), and only the resulting fixed-dimensional vectors (768–1536 dims) are consumed by the trainable calibrators. The calibrators are three-layer feedforward networks with residual connections sized at (d_e/4, d_e/4, d_e), adding only ~0.7–2.7M parameters on top of the frozen embedder.

## Method

ADAMAB combines two components: (1) a lightweight embedding calibrator consisting of two small residual feedforward networks (one for query embeddings, one for label embeddings) that act as a simplified single-head cross-attention with identity values, trained with cross-entropy loss on the inner product of calibrated embeddings; and (2) a modified UCB-based adaptive data augmentation loop where, at each epoch, a class is selected to augment by maximizing an acquisition function that trades off estimated gradient-shifting reduction (exploitation) against a confidence bound scaled by 1/√(n_{t-1}+Δn)·1/√n_{C,t-1} (exploration). Synthetic samples for the selected class are generated by a frozen generative model (GPT-4o-mini for text, GPT-Image-1-mini for images) conditioned on the class label. The paper proves that under β-smoothness and bounded-gradient assumptions, this procedure converges to a stationary point with an O(1/T) + O(√(log T/T)) rate, and shows the relaxed confidence bound is necessary for the regret to vanish as training progresses.

## Results

ADAMAB achieves up to ~40% accuracy improvement over the underlying embedding models in few-shot pattern recognition. On text datasets (zero-shot), ADAMAB with OpenAI-text-embedding-3-small reaches 58.15% on MultiWD, 89.54% on Forbidden Question Set, and 61.63% on TREC, improving by +18.94%, +16.62%, and +26.60% respectively over the uncalibrated embedder. On image datasets, ADAMAB with CLIP-VIT-Large achieves 93.20% on OxfordPets, 93.17% on Flowers102, and 68.60% on CUB200, with the largest gain being +35.42% on CUB200. The calibrator adds only 0.664M–2.654M trainable parameters on top of frozen embedding models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADAMAB substantially outperforms all reranker and embedding baselines across every dataset. Against BGE-reranker-v2-m3, ADAMAB (OpenAI embedder) wins by 55.07 points on MultiWD zero-shot (58.15% vs. 3.08%), 43.39 points on FQS (89.54% vs. 46.15%), and 37.50 points on TREC (61.63% vs. 24.13%). Against Cohere-v3.5, the margins are similarly large: 55.07 points on MultiWD, 35.39 on FQS, and 28.93 on TREC. On image tasks, ADAMAB with CLIP-VIT-Large beats Jina-rerank-m0 by 19.56 points on OxfordPets (93.20% vs. 73.64%), 36.57 on Flowers102 (93.17% vs. 56.60%), and 33.38 on CUB200 (68.60% vs. 35.22%). ADAMAB also improves over the uncalibrated CLIP-VIT-Large by 10.32, 32.18, and 35.42 points on OxfordPets, Flowers102, and CUB200 respectively, confirming that the lightweight calibrator plus adaptive augmentation is the primary source of the gain rather than the choice of base embedder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) lightweight calibration of frozen embedding models (extending the residual-adapter idea from PEFT methods like LoRA to a simpler feedforward structure that works without parameter access), (2) data augmentation with generative models (GPT, diffusion), and (3) multi-armed bandit exploration-exploitation strategies (specifically UCB, from Carpentier et al. 2011). The key departure from prior active learning is replacing the assumption of a large unlabeled pool with on-the-fly synthesis via a generative model, and the key departure from random augmentation is the bandit-based acquisition function that explicitly targets gradient-shifting reduction. The theoretical contribution—a convergence guarantee for the adaptive augmentation loop under biased gradients—has no direct precedent in the data-augmentation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADAMAB offers a practical recipe for calibrating closed-source or API-only embedding models with as few as 2–5 initial samples per class, which is a common constraint in domain-specific deployments. The convergence guarantee (Theorem 2) is the first of its kind for adaptive synthetic-data augmentation, giving practitioners a principled stopping criterion. However, the ablation revealing that excessive synthetic data degrades accuracy due to generator homogeneity highlights a fundamental limitation: the method's ceiling is bounded by the diversity of the underlying generative model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-set or hierarchical classification, whether more diverse or iterative generators (e.g., self-play) can push past the homogeneity wall, and whether the UCB relaxation generalizes beyond the balanced-class assumption in the gradient estimate.
