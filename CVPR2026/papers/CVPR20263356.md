## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods handle either continual domain shifts or open-set OOD detection, but not their coupled interaction. When both domain and semantic shifts occur simultaneously, the feature space collapses, mixing ID and OOD embeddings into an inseparable cluster that degrades both classification accuracy and outlier detection. This paper formalizes the Open-set Continual Test-Time Adaptation (OCTTA) setting and shows that prior methods suffer severe performance degradation under this coupled regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB image batches (size 64) arriving as a nonstationary stream across M target domains, along with pre-computed source feature statistics (mean and standard deviation from 300 unlabeled source samples) and frozen ViT-B/16 classifier weights. Each batch is processed in a single forward-backward pass with one-step prompt updates. The output is a dual signal: a class label (argmax over source classes) for ID samples and an energy-based OOD score for detection, with the OOD ratio κ=0.5 stochastically varying per batch to simulate real-world streams.

## Method

DOCO learns L=8 visual prompt tokens on a frozen ViT-B/16 via a three-component closed loop. First, Adaptation-Conditioned Sample Splitting applies K-Means (K=2) on prototypical distances (1 minus max cosine similarity to frozen class prototypes) of prompted features to partition each batch into likely ID and OOD subsets. Second, Back-to-Source Prompt Learning optimizes the prompt on the ID subset by minimizing a combined loss: L2 distance between batch and source feature statistics (statistical alignment) plus a Frobenius-norm structural regularizer that preserves pairwise cosine-similarity geometry to prevent semantic overfitting. Third, Intra-Batch Prompt Propagation immediately applies the updated prompt to OOD samples in the same batch, neutralizing the shared domain factor δ_t and isolating semantic novelty for reliable detection. This creates a virtuous cycle: better splitting yields purer ID sets for prompt learning, which in turn improves future splitting and detection.

## Results

On the primary ImageNet-C benchmark (severity 5, κ=0.5, averaged over 15 corruptions and 6 OOD datasets), DOCO achieves 61.5% ID accuracy, 82.7% OOD detection AUC, and a harmonic H-score of 70.1%, surpassing the next-best method UniEnt (65.4% H-score) by 4.7%. On the more extreme LAION-C benchmark (severity 3), DOCO attains 32.7% H-score, leading the closest competitor DPCore (30.3%) by 2.4%. Under the closed-set CTTA setting across five domain-shift datasets, DOCO achieves the best average accuracy of 43.1%, edging out DPCore (42.2%) and EATA (41.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOCO outperforms all 10 baselines on the primary ImageNet-C OCTTA benchmark, with the largest margin over UniEnt (65.4% → 70.1% H-score, +4.7%) and E-COME (65.2% → 70.1%, +4.9%). Among continual TTA methods, DOCO surpasses DPCore by 7.5% H-score and EATA by 12.3%, while normalization-based methods like CoTTA and SAR trail by 15+ points. On the harder LAION-C benchmark, DOCO leads DPCore (30.3%) and UniEnt (29.3%) by 2.4% and 3.4% H-score respectively, with most baselines collapsing to near-chance accuracy (e.g., Tent at 0.6%, S-COME at 0.3%). In the closed-set CTTA setting (Table 3), DOCO achieves the best average accuracy (43.1%) across ImageNet-C, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, and LAION-C, confirming that its prompt-based design does not sacrifice performance when no OOD classes are present. Notably, entropy-minimization baselines (Tent, CoTTA) suffer catastrophic accuracy drops under coupled domain-semantic shifts, while DOCO's structural regularizer and sample-splitting mechanism maintain stable performance across all domain orderings and OOD ratios tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

DOCO sits at the intersection of two established TTA research axes—continual TTA (CoTTA, EATA, SAR, ViDA, DPCore) and open-set TTA (UniEnt, STAMP, COME, OSTTA)—but departs from both by treating their coupling as the core challenge rather than addressing them in isolation. Its adaptation mechanism builds directly on Visual Prompt Tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022), repurposing prompt tokens not as a generic adapter but as a domain-compensation signal learned via statistical alignment to pre-cached source feature moments. The intra-batch propagation step (learning a prompt from ID samples and immediately applying it to OOD samples) and the K-Means-based prototypical-distance splitting are novel mechanisms not present in prior TTA or OOD-detection literature, though each draws on standard tools (cosine similarity, energy scoring).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OCTTA formulation is a meaningful step toward realistic deployment, where domain shifts and novel objects co-occur and interact; prior methods that handle only one axis degrade sharply when the other is present. The closed-loop design (splitting → prompt learning → propagation → improved splitting) offers a practical, parameter-efficient recipe that could be adopted in safety-critical perception pipelines. Open questions remain: the batch-wise domain factor assumption (δ_t) may break under heterogeneous batches, the K-Means splitting is sensitive to batch composition and the OOD ratio κ, and the method has only been validated on ViT-B/16 with ImageNet-scale data. Whether the back-to-source statistical alignment generalizes to non-ViT backbones or to shifts that alter feature geometry rather than just moments is untested.
