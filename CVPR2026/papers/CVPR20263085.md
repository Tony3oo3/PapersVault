## Problem

Face verification accuracy has saturated on standard test sets (LFW, CPLFW, AgeDB-30, etc.), prompting the creation of harder benchmarks that artificially degrade image quality via synthetic masks, resolution reduction, or adversarial artifacts. However, no existing test set isolates the natural variation of specific facial attributes (facial hair, exposure) or the difficulty of discriminating identical twins under the standard LFW protocol, leaving a gap in understanding where FR models genuinely struggle without artificial degradation.

## Input / Output

Each test set consists of 6,000 image pairs (3,000 genuine, 3,000 impostor) organized into 10 folds of 300 genuine and 300 impostor pairs each, following the LFW cross-validation protocol. Images are high-quality, controlled-environment mugshots (MORPH5) or festival photos (Twins Challenge), cropped and aligned via img2pose. The output is a single verification accuracy per model, computed as the mean over 10 folds where the best threshold is selected on 9 training folds and applied to the held-out test fold.

## Method

The paper introduces a curation pipeline with three 'Goldilocks' constraints—limiting each image to at most 6 pair occurrences, balancing demographic groups equally, and enforcing identity-disjointness across validation folds—to produce test sets that are challenging but not trivially unsolvable. For Hadrian, facial hair attributes are predicted with a 0.9 confidence threshold, and genuine pairs are formed from {no-facial-hair, full-facial-hair} while impostor pairs use {full-facial-hair, full-facial-hair}; for Eclipse, exposure is binned into five percentile groups and pairs span extreme tails (strongly under- vs. strongly over-exposed); for ND-Twins, pairs are selected by head pose (0° vs. ±45°) or indoor/outdoor environment variation. After automated selection, a manual review removes identity label noise and misclassified attribute pairs. The resulting benchmarks are evaluated with 15 model configurations (ArcFace, CurricularFace, MagFace, AdaFace, UniFace each trained on MS1MV2, WebFace4M, or Glint360K with ResNet100), showing that Hadrian, Eclipse, and ND-Twins are 1.42%, 11.23%, and 22.48% harder than CPLFW respectively, and harder than artifact-based sets like MLFW and XQLFW.

## Results

Across 15 model configurations (5 algorithms × 3 training datasets), the average accuracy on the proposed test sets is 92.62% (Hadrian), 82.81% (Eclipse), and 71.56% (ND-Twins), compared to 94.04% on CPLFW, the most challenging prior LFW-protocol test set. This means Hadrian is 1.42% harder, Eclipse is 11.23% harder, and ND-Twins is 22.48% harder than CPLFW. Notably, ND-Twins (71.56%) is dramatically harder than existing similar-looking benchmarks SLLFW and DoppelVer, where models achieve over 97% accuracy. An ablation on a randomly sampled MORPH5 test set yields >99% accuracy, confirming the difficulty stems from the designed attribute emphasis rather than domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions its three proposed test sets against existing LFW-protocol benchmarks in terms of difficulty (lower accuracy = harder). Hadrian (92.62% avg) is 1.42% harder than CPLFW (94.04%), the previously most challenging attribute-focused test set, and Eclipse (82.81%) is 11.23% harder. ND-Twins (71.56%) is 22.48% harder than CPLFW and dramatically harder than SLLFW and DoppelVer, where all 15 models exceed 97% accuracy, revealing that doppelganger-based benchmarks fail to capture the true difficulty of similar-looking persons. Eclipse and ND-Twins also outperform MLFW (mask-based) and XQLFW (low-quality) in difficulty, showing that natural attribute variation is as challenging as artificially degraded images. Among the five evaluated algorithms, MagFace and AdaFace consistently achieve the highest accuracy on Hadrian (92.80% and 92.35% with MS1MV2), while on ND-Twins the spread is wider (65.73%–68.58%), indicating greater sensitivity to model choice on the hardest test set. Training on Glint360K (largest dataset) yields the best results across all test sets, with ArcFace reaching 95.68% on Hadrian and 84.67% on ND-Twins, yet still far from saturation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing trend of creating challenging face verification benchmarks by degrading image quality or injecting synthetic artifacts (MLFW, XQLFW, TALFW), instead arguing that natural facial attribute variation—facial hair, exposure, and identical-twin similarity—is sufficient to stress-test state-of-the-art models. It repurposes the LFW 10-fold cross-validation protocol and the MORPH5/Twins Challenge image pools, but introduces three 'Goldilocks' curation rules (capped image occurrence, demographic balance, identity-disjoint folds) that prior LFW-protocol test sets (LFW, CPLFW, CALFW, CFP-FP, AgeDB-30) do not enforce. For similar-looking persons, it moves beyond the doppelganger-based SLLFW and DoppelVer benchmarks, which the authors show are too easy (>97% accuracy), toward a dedicated identical-twin test set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that high-quality, controlled-environment images with natural attribute variation are as hard or harder than artificially degraded ones, redirecting attention toward intra-class attribute modeling rather than robustness to noise. The finding that accuracy gaps between demographic groups exceed 30% on Eclipse underscores that unbalanced test sets mask significant fairness issues in deployed FR systems. Limitations include the 6,000-pair scale (smaller than IJB-family benchmarks), the demographic imbalance in ND-Twins (85/15 White/Black split), and the reliance on a single commercial source (MORPH5) for two of three sets. Open questions include whether these attribute-specific weaknesses can be addressed by synthetic data generation and whether the Goldilocks curation rules generalize to other biometric or recognition tasks.
