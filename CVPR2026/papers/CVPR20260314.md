## Problem

Existing robust cross-view geo-localization methods are trained at a single fixed field-of-view (FoV) and collapse when tested at unseen FoVs or unknown orientations, requiring deployment of multiple specialized models. Prior attempts at dynamic FoV training simply randomize FoVs uniformly, implicitly assuming all FoVs are equally difficult, which fails to achieve true robustness. The paper asks whether a single model can inherently maintain consistent, high performance across all orientations and FoVs without explicit view transformations or additional modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260314/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes north-aligned panoramic ground images (360°) paired with satellite images, generating limited-FoV crops (70°–360°) with random horizontal shifts and satellite rotations as augmented positives. At test time, it accepts ground-view queries with arbitrary orientation and FoV (as narrow as 70°) and retrieves the best-matching satellite image from a database via cosine similarity in the learned embedding space, producing a ranked list of candidates. On University-1652, where no panoramas exist, two distinct ground-view images at the same location substitute for the panorama–crop pair.

## Method

SinGeo combines two module-free strategies: (1) a dual discriminative learning (DDL) architecture that creates self-supervised positive pairs in both branches—cropping/rotating the ground panorama to I_g* and rotating the satellite image to I_s*—so that intra-view contrastive losses (InfoNCE) enforce discriminability within each branch while cross-view losses align the four embeddings (g, g*, s, s*) in a shared space; and (2) a curriculum learning schedule that progressively narrows the ground FoV from 360° to 70° and increases the satellite rotation probability from 25% to 100% over training epochs via a monotonic scheduling function, with Dynamic Similarity Sampling re-mining hard negatives after each epoch. This easy-to-hard progression lets the model first learn stable global correspondences before specializing to extreme FoVs, yielding a single backbone (ConvNeXt-B, ViT, or CNN+attention) that outperforms FoV-specific models even at 70° without any explicit geometric transformation.

## Results

On CVUSA, SinGeo achieves 79.1% average R@1 across all FoV settings, with 96.8% R@1 at FoV=360°, 91.8% at FoV=180°, 70.1% at FoV=90°, and 58.0% at FoV=70°—the first single model to surpass 70% and 50% R@1 at FoV=90° and 70° respectively. On CVACT, SinGeo reaches 55.8% average R@1, outperforming all single-model baselines. On VIGOR (cross-area), SinGeo achieves 24.7% R@1 at FoV=360° and 4.9% at FoV=90°, substantially exceeding ConGeo (16.2% and 3.9%). On University-1652, SinGeo matches ConGeo's R@1 (5.9%) while reporting R@10 of 23.73% (St2S) and 22.8% (S2St).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SinGeo (single model) outperforms Sample4Geo [3], its direct baseline, by 10.6 points in average R@1 on CVUSA (79.1% vs 68.5%) and 8.8 points on CVACT (55.8% vs 47.0%), with the largest gains at extreme FoVs: +15.0 R@1 at 90° and +17.1 at 70° on CVUSA. Against ConGeo [13], which requires separate models per FoV, SinGeo wins decisively at FoV=90° (70.1% vs 55.5%) and FoV=70° (58.0% vs 49.1%) on CVUSA, though it trails slightly at FoV=180° (91.8% vs 92.3%). On VIGOR cross-area, SinGeo improves R@1 from 16.2% (ConGeo) to 24.7% at FoV=360° and from 3.9% to 4.9% at FoV=90%. On University-1652, SinGeo ties ConGeo on R@1 (5.9% St2S, 7.1% S2St) while additionally reporting R@10 (23.73% and 22.8%). In the cross-architecture transfer experiment, SinGeo boosts the ViT variant of Sample4Geo from 16.7% to 76.0% R@1 at FoV=360°, far exceeding ConGeo's 19.7%, and elevates GeoDTR by over 47 points, confirming that the curriculum-guided training paradigm generalizes across CNN, ViT, and hybrid backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

SinGeo builds directly on Sample4Geo's hard negative sampling framework as its baseline, repurposing its Dynamic Similarity Sampling block within a curriculum learning loop. It departs from the dominant FoV-specific training paradigm (ConGeo, ArcGeo, GAL, DSM) that trains at a fixed field-of-view and collapses on unseen FoVs, and from explicit transformation methods (polar, BEV) that introduce distortions. The key conceptual departure is treating both ground and satellite branches symmetrically via dual discriminative learning, and applying curriculum learning—previously unexplored in robust CVGL—to schedule FoV difficulty progressively rather than assuming all FoVs are equally hard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: a single model replaces the need to deploy multiple FoV-specialized models, simplifying real-world deployment for robotic navigation and AR applications. The proposed consistency metrics (OC, FC) offer a new, interpretable lens for diagnosing why models fail under view variation, potentially guiding future robustness research beyond CVGL. A key limitation is the dependence on panoramas during training, leaving datasets like University-1652 (no aligned panoramas) as an open challenge. Whether the curriculum scheduling principle generalizes to other domain-shift scenarios beyond FoV/orientation remains to be explored.
