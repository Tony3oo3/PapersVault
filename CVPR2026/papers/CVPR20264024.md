## Problem

State space models achieve efficient video semantic segmentation through linear-complexity state compression, but their fixed-size state space inevitably forgets pixel-level spatiotemporal specifics such as boundaries, textures, and local variations. This 'specifics forgetting' limits the model to coarse object-location segmentation and undermines temporal consistency at the pixel level, a gap that existing SSM-based VSS methods like TV3S have not addressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264024/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of video frames encoded by a pretrained MiT or Swin-Transformer backbone into feature maps of shape C×H×W. During training on VSPW, only the current frame T plus frames T−9, T−6, and T−3 are fed in (following TV3S), with input crops of 480×480 (VSPW, CamVid), 480×640 (NYUv2), or 512×1024 (Cityscapes). The output is a per-frame pixel-wise segmentation mask produced by a linear decoder applied to the final dual-path SSM feature maps.

## Method

RS-SSM stacks L dual-path SSM layers, each containing two SSM modules (θ₁ and θ₂) that share a weight-projected input. A Channel-wise Amplitude Perceptron (CwAP) applies 2D FFT to the projected features, partitions the frequency spectrum into K radial bands, and computes per-channel spectrum features as the cumulative energy ratio of the top-k_h high-frequency bands, quantifying how much specific information each channel carries. A channel information loss (1 − mean pairwise cosine similarity of normalized spectrum features across the batch) encourages consistent allocation of specifics to the same channels across samples. A Forgetting Gate Information Refiner (FGIR) then inverts the forgetting gate of θ₂ (A^I = A_max + A_min − A), computes per-channel importance β from the original gate, and forms an adaptive inverting weight α = F̃ ⊙ (1−β) that is high for channels rich in specifics yet heavily compressed by θ₂. The refined gate A^R = (1−α)⊙A + α⊙A^I is fed to θ₁, so θ₁ complementarily preserves the specifics that θ₂ discarded, while θ₂ continues to model common semantic structure. Training uses standard cross-entropy plus the channel information loss with an AdamW optimizer and poly LR schedule.

## Results

On the large-scale VSPW dataset, RS-SSM achieves 51.6 mIoU with MiT-B5 and 52.2 mIoU with Swin-S, surpassing the previous SSM-based state-of-the-art TV3S by 1.8 and 1.6 mIoU respectively. On NYUv2 and CamVid, RS-SSM reaches 49.1 and 64.4 mIoU (MiT-B5), outperforming TV3S by 2.2 mIoU and CFFM++ by 2.1 mIoU. On Cityscapes (512×1024), RS-SSM with MiT-B1 achieves 78.3 mIoU, exceeding TV3S by 2.6 mIoU while reducing GFLOPs from 83.6 to 59.5 and improving FPS from 25.1 to 27.2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RS-SSM consistently outperforms all baselines across every backbone scale and dataset. Against the strongest SSM-based competitor TV3S, RS-SSM gains 1.7 mIoU on VSPW (MiT-B1), 1.8 mIoU (MiT-B5), 1.2 mIoU (Swin-T), and 1.6 mIoU (Swin-S), while also improving FPS by 12.6 (MiT-B1) and 6.2 (MiT-B5). Against Transformer-based methods, RS-SSM surpasses CFFM++ by 1.8 mIoU on VSPW (MiT-B1) and 1.5 mIoU (MiT-B5), and beats MRCFA by 2.8 mIoU (MiT-B1) and 1.7 mIoU (MiT-B5). On CamVid, RS-SSM with MiT-B5 (64.4 mIoU) exceeds the best Transformer baseline CFFM++ (62.3) by 2.1 mIoU. On Cityscapes, RS-SSM (MiT-B1) achieves 78.3 mIoU, beating TV3S (75.6) by 2.6 mIoU and CFFM++ (75.7) by 2.6 mIoU, while using 24.1 fewer GFLOPs than TV3S. The efficiency advantage is notable: RS-SSM with MiT-B5 uses 128.8 GFLOPs versus 444.3 for CFFM++ and 373.0 for MRCFA, while delivering higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TV3S [22], the first SSM-based video semantic segmentation method, by identifying a fundamental limitation: the fixed-size state space in SSMs inevitably compresses away pixel-level spatiotemporal specifics (boundaries, textures) while preserving common semantic structure. The proposed RS-SSM introduces a dual-path architecture where one SSM path (θ2) performs standard temporal compression and a second path (θ1) uses an adaptively inverted forgetting gate derived from θ2 to complementarily recover the forgotten specifics. The CwAP module's use of 2D FFT to quantify per-channel specific information content draws on frequency-domain analysis techniques from dense prediction [3,4], while the overall selective state space formulation builds on Mamba/Mamba2 [9,13].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic for why SSM-based VSS methods underperform at pixel-level boundaries compared to Transformer-based approaches, and offers a principled mechanism (forgetting gate inversion guided by frequency-domain channel analysis) to close that gap while retaining linear complexity. This positions SSM-based VSS as a more competitive alternative to quadratic-complexity Transformers for long video sequences. Open questions include whether the channel-alignment loss (L_ci) remains beneficial at larger batch sizes or with different SSM variants, whether the frequency-band partitioning (k_h) is sensitive to input resolution, and whether the dual-path inversion strategy generalizes to other dense prediction tasks beyond segmentation.
