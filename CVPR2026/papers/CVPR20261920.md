## Problem

While recent VLMs show strong text-only reasoning, they remain inadequate at selecting, invoking, and coordinating visual tools (grounding, chart parsing, math solvers) for multi-step visual reasoning. Naively exposing tools to base models actually degrades accuracy, and existing tool-use training efforts are confined to single specialized tools or narrow task domains, leaving a gap for a scalable, unified training environment for visual agentic reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a question text paired with one or more RGB images and produces a multi-turn trajectory of interleaved reasoning segments (think blocks) and structured tool-call invocations (typed tuples specifying tool identifier and arguments), terminated by a final answer extracted from an answer tag. The 26 available tools span perception (GroundingDINO, SAM, EasyOCR), chart understanding (ChartMoE), diagram formalization (CDL, Inter-GPS), and math solvers (G-LLaVA, MultiMath), each returning structured observations or error messages that feed back into the reasoning loop.

## Method

VISTA-Gym provides a Gymnasium-style executable environment unifying 13 datasets across 7 reasoning task types with a standardized 26-tool API, verifiable feedback, and Ray-based asynchronous multi-turn rollout infrastructure. VISTA-R1 is trained in two stages: (1) behavioral cloning on GPT-5-generated expert trajectories (filtered for correctness and densified with Qwen3-VL-235B rationales) to instill tool-call syntax and selection priors, then (2) online multi-turn RL using GRPO with group-normalized advantages over G rollouts. The reward is sparse and format-aware, combining a high-priority repetition penalty, a structural format reward, and a binary correctness reward, so positive signal is allocated only to repetition-free, well-formed, and correct trajectories. A tail-patch curriculum further refines performance by concentrating RL budget on hard-but-learnable instances identified via rollout pass rates.

## Results

VISTA-R1-8B (InternVL3-8B backbone) achieves 71.14% average accuracy across 11 reasoning-intensive VQA benchmarks, outperforming same-size open-source baselines by 9.51%–18.72% with tools and by 2.03%–11.24% even without tools. The 2B variant (63.27%) matches or exceeds 8B baselines, while VISTA-R1-14B (76.58%) surpasses InternVL3-78B (74.85%) and GPT-5 (75.84%) on average. Ablations confirm that removing RL (w/o Reasoning) drops 8B accuracy to 48.40%, and removing tools (w/o Tools) drops it to 63.66%, demonstrating that the synergy of interleaved reasoning and tool execution is essential.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA-R1-8B (71.14% avg) decisively outperforms all 7–13B open-source baselines: it beats R1-VL-7B (61.63%) by 9.51 points, Perception-R1-7B (55.29%) by 15.85 points, VToolR1-7B (53.20%) by 17.94 points, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (52.42%) by 18.72 points. Against larger open-source models, VISTA-R1-8B surpasses InternVL3-14B (68.82%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (70.82%), and is within 0.75 points of InternVL3-38B (71.89%). The 14B variant (76.58%) exceeds InternVL3-78B (74.85%) and GPT-5 (75.84%) on average, though it trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.33%) and Claude-4.5-Sonnet (78.76%) by roughly 2 points. Notably, VISTA-R1-2B (63.27%) already matches or exceeds the 8B baselines (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B at 52.80%), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. The w/o Reasoning ablation (48.40%) confirms that simply exposing tools without RL-trained reasoning is counterproductive, while w/o Tools (63.66%) shows that RL-learned reasoning alone still provides substantial gains over the untrained base model (57.49%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tool-integrated reasoning (TIR) paradigm for VLMs by providing a unified, scalable training environment (VISTA-Gym) rather than the single-tool, narrow-task setups in prior work (e.g., MMSearch-R1 for image search, GRIT/Chain-of-Focus for zoom-in). Methodologically, it builds on the ReAct think-then-act loop for structuring agent trajectories and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL algorithm, but departs from prior agentic RL environments (GEM, AgentGym-RL, RAGEN) by being the first to support multimodal, tool-integrated visual reasoning with executable feedback. A key conceptual departure is the demonstration that naively exposing tools to VLMs degrades performance, positioning RL not as an optional refinement but as a prerequisite for effective tool use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VISTA-Gym provides an open-source, extensible training ground that lowers the barrier for developing tool-integrated visual reasoning in VLMs, and the clear ablation showing RL is essential (48.40% without reasoning vs. 71.14% full) gives practitioners a concrete recipe: SFT for syntax, then online RL for reasoning-guided tool orchestration. Open questions remain around stepwise (rather than terminal-only) reward signals, broader tool ecosystems beyond the 26 provided, and whether the gains transfer to truly open-domain visual reasoning beyond the curated VQA tasks. The parameter-efficiency finding (2B model competitive with 8B baselines) suggests the approach could democratize agentic visual reasoning to resource-constrained settings.
