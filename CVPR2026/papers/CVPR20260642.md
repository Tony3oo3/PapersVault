## Problem

Existing point cloud registration methods degrade significantly under low-overlap conditions (below 30% shared geometry) because non-overlapping regions exhibit geometric or semantic similarities that produce outlier matches, obscuring true inlier correspondences. Prior approaches either predict point-wise overlap weights (relying on consistent feature representations) or strengthen global features via attention, but both strategies fail as the overlap ratio drops. The paper asks whether low-overlap registration can be reframed as identifying a subset of high-overlap sub-cloud pairs rather than solving the full alignment directly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of color-enhanced 3D point clouds (source X ∈ R^{m×3}, target Y ∈ R^{n×3}) with partial overlap, processed through a 4-level KPConv-style backbone that produces multi-scale downsampled features. It outputs a rigid transformation T = {R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³} estimated via RANSAC or the RANSAC-free LGR estimator, along with refined point correspondences. The pipeline operates on coarsest-level points for sub-cloud subdivision (k=6 sub-clouds per cloud) and uses densest-level points for final correspondence refinement.

## Method

SuP reformulates low-overlap registration as a high-overlap anchor-pair mining problem via the Dual-phase Sub-cloud Anchor Mining (DSAM) module. First, each cloud is subdivided into k sub-clouds around FPS-selected centroids; an overlap-guided prior-weighting scheme (OPS) scores all k² sub-cloud pairs using mutual top-k entries of a Gaussian feature-correlation matrix to select preliminary candidates, for which weighted-SVD transformations are estimated. A multi-scale post-weighting network (MPN) then retrieves cross-scale overlapped points under each preliminary transform, computes Hadamard-product affinity features across two scales, applies a light-weight self-attention layer, and predicts a feature consensus weight per candidate via max-pooling and an MLP. The top-weighted candidates become anchor pairs whose inlier correspondences are merged and refined in a coarse-to-fine merge-to-match stage. Training uses an alignment-aware weighting loss that supervises MPN with on-the-fly RMSE errors from ground-truth transforms, applying a soft penalty (λ_a) to near-threshold failures to encourage higher consensus weights for marginally misaligned pairs.

## Results

On Color3DMatch (C3DM), SuP achieves 98.1% Registration Recall (RR) and 99.7% Feature Matching Recall (FMR) with 5k sampled correspondences, and 91.1% Inlier Ratio (IR) with 250 samples. On the more challenging Color3DLoMatch (C3DLM, 10–30% overlap), SuP reaches 90.4% RR, 97.0% FMR, and 76.0% IR. Under the RANSAC-free LGR estimator, SuP attains 97.8% RR on C3DM and 90.2% RR on C3DLM, with the lowest geometric errors of 1.374°/0.046 m (C3DM) and 2.493°/0.074 m (C3DLM).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuP outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across both C3DM and C3DLM. The closest competitor is ColorPCR [13], which SuP surpasses by 1.4% RR (98.1 vs 96.7) and 7.1% IR (82.3 vs 75.0) on C3DM, and by 1.5% RR (90.4 vs 88.9) and 15.0% IR (66.2 vs 51.2) on C3DLM. Among the transformer-based methods, PEAL [29] is the strongest baseline (94.6% RR on C3DM, 81.7% on C3DLM), still trailing SuP by 3.5% and 8.7% RR respectively. Older hand-crafted and early deep methods (FCGF, D3Feat, SpinNet, Predator) are significantly outperformed, with RR gaps exceeding 8–17 points on C3DLM. Under the LGR estimator, SuP also beats REGTR [26] (weighted-SVD) on geometric accuracy, achieving 1.374° RRE vs 1.567° on C3DM and 2.493° vs 2.827° on C3DLM. As a plugin module, SuP further boosts GeoTransformer, PEAL, and ColorPCR by 1.3–2.2% RR, confirming its generality as a post-processing enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuP builds directly on ColorPCR's color-enhanced KPConv backbone and color-guided geometric self-attention for feature extraction, and on GeoTransformer's dual-normalization strategy, point matching loss, and LGR estimator. Conceptually, it departs from the two dominant prior strategies for low-overlap registration—explicit overlap-weight prediction (Predator) and global feature strengthening via attention (GeoTransformer, PEAL, ColorPCR)—by reframing the problem as mining high-overlap sub-cloud anchor pairs through a dual-phase weighting pipeline (OPS + MPN). The alignment-aware weighting loss, which uses on-the-fly alignment RMSE as a soft supervision signal for the post-weighting network, is a new training mechanism not present in prior registration methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-cloud decomposition strategy offers a practical and interpretable way to bypass the geometric/semantic ambiguity in non-overlapping regions, and the demonstration that SuP functions as a drop-in plugin improving GeoTransformer, PEAL, and ColorPCR suggests broad utility beyond a single pipeline. However, evaluation is limited to indoor color-enhanced datasets (C3DM/C3DLM), and the authors acknowledge that extreme cross-modal or appearance-distortion scenarios could degrade OPS and MPN performance. Open questions include whether the k=6 sub-cloud granularity and the FPS-based subdivision generalize to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether the approach extends to non-rigid or partial deformable registration.
