## Problem

Estimating dense 3D hand poses from sparse IMU signals (2–7 sensors) is inherently ambiguous due to the large semantic gap and information density difference between local inertial motion and global hand morphology. Existing cross-modal distillation methods (e.g., VIFNet) transfer only coarse-grained, entangled global features, which fails to exploit complex hand kinematics and leads to temporal incoherence and morphological distortion, especially for fast-changing gestures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student model consumes only a sequence of T=32 frames of sparse IMU data (7, 3, or 2 sensors, each providing linear acceleration and angular velocity channels). The teacher additionally ingests masked MANO pose (45 DoF) and shape (10-dim) parameters during training. Outputs are MANO pose θ and shape β per frame, decoded through the MANO layer into 3D joint coordinates (T×J×3) and mesh vertices (T×N_v×3), evaluated via MPJPE and MPVPE in millimeters.

## Method

MGDHand pre-trains a MANO-IMU fusion teacher (dual-stream DSTFormer with cross-attention fusion) that learns a unified latent representation Z encoding hand morphology and motion dynamics. The key novelty is the Multi-Granularity Decoupled Distillation (MGDistill) scheme: Z-guided cross-attention refines the morphology and kinematic branches, then three enhancement modules (MSEM, CEM, ISEM) decompose the entangled features into static shape, dynamic pose, and temporal motion priors via element-wise difference, product, and addition operations. On the student side, spatial attention (joint-axis MHSA) and temporal attention (time-axis MHSA) decouple IMU latents into matching granularities. Three L2 distillation losses—SSD on globally pooled shape descriptors, DPD on frame-wise pose features, and TMD on motion-trend features—are combined with reconstruction loss in a two-stage training protocol (train teacher → freeze → distill to student), achieving 40.7% MPJPE improvement over the undistilled student.

## Results

On the VIHand benchmark with 7 sparse IMUs, MGDHand with multi-granularity distillation achieves 9.13 mm MPJPE and 10.46 mm MPVPE, outperforming all IMU-only baselines by a large margin. Compared to the strongest IMU-based distillation baseline VIFNet-S (13.69 mm MPJPE), MGDHand reduces error by 33.3% in MPJPE and 36.7% in MPVPE. Notably, MGDHand also surpasses vision-based methods HaMeR (11.98 mm) and WiLoR (10.49 mm) in MPJPE despite using only 7 IMUs and no visual input. The distillation strategy yields a 40.7% MPJPE improvement over the same student architecture without distillation (15.40 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MGDHand decisively outperforms all IMU-only baselines: it reduces MPJPE by 58.2% over TransPose (21.84→9.13 mm), 50.8% over FusePose (18.56→9.13 mm), 46.1% over VIFNet (16.93→9.13 mm), and 33.3% over VIFNet-S (13.69→9.13 mm). Against vision-based methods, MGDHand beats HaMeR (11.98 mm) by 23.8% and WiLoR (10.49 mm) by 12.9% in MPJPE, despite using no RGB input. The only method that outperforms MGDHand is VIFNet-T (7.86 mm), which uses both RGB and IMU at inference, confirming that the remaining gap is attributable to the absence of visual input rather than distillation quality. Among alternative distillation strategies (Table 4), MGDistill outperforms SCJD (11.24 mm) by 2.11 mm, DKD (11.92 mm) by 2.79 mm, DWPose (13.61 mm) by 4.48 mm, and SimKD (13.78 mm) by 4.65 mm in MPJPE, demonstrating that multi-granularity decoupling is substantially more effective than global-feature or two-stage distillation for this cross-modal setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-modal knowledge distillation line initiated by VIFNet [34], which first distilled RGB-IMU fused features into an IMU-only student for hand pose estimation. It departs from VIFNet in two key ways: (1) replacing the RGB-IMU teacher with a MANO-IMU fusion teacher that explicitly encodes parametric hand morphology, and (2) replacing coarse-grained global feature distillation with a multi-granularity decoupled scheme that separates shape, pose, and motion priors before transfer. The DSTFormer backbone is inherited from MotionBERT [46], and the overall distillation philosophy draws on the broader 3D HPE KD literature (SCJD, DWPose, AITEPose), but the semantic decoupling along shape-pose-motion axes is novel to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that an IMU-only student with multi-granularity distillation (9.13 mm MPJPE) approaches or matches vision-based methods (10.49–11.98 mm) without any visual input at inference is practically significant for occlusion-robust, privacy-preserving, and power-constrained applications (smart rings, data gloves, EAI). The graceful degradation from 7 to 2 IMUs suggests the framework could support ultra-minimal sensor deployments. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (VIHand), the teacher requires MANO parameter annotations during training, and it remains open whether the shape-pose-motion decomposition generalizes to other articulated-body tasks or to teachers without explicit parametric structure.
