## Problem

Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods replace slow per-scene optimization with direct prediction of Gaussian attributes, but they struggle with primitive redundancy and rendering quality. Pixel-level approaches generate an excessive number of Gaussians (e.g., over 500K for two 512×512 views), while voxel-based alternatives introduce quantization errors and waste computation on empty cells. The paper addresses how to efficiently aggregate and compress Gaussian primitives while preserving spatial structure for high-quality novel view synthesis in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–12 RGB views (360×640 on RealEstate10K, 256×448 on DL3DV, 256×256 on ACID) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and outputs multi-level 3D Gaussian primitives (position, anisotropic scale, opacity, rotation quaternion, 2nd-order SH color) that are rendered into novel views. A DINOv2-Small backbone produces intermediate depth maps via a DPT head, which are back-projected into 3D point maps to initialize Gaussian centers. Z-order pooling progressively compresses the primitive count (e.g., from ~500K pixel-level Gaussians down to ~285K at L1 or ~142K at L2 for two views), and a Z-order-based Maximum Coverage Viewpoint Selection algorithm can further reduce the number of input views during inference.

## Method

The core contribution is a Z-order transformer (ZFormer) that serializes unstructured 3D Gaussian points into a spatially coherent 1D sequence using the Morton curve, enabling efficient neighborhood-aware attention without a dense voxel grid. Each ZFormer block applies a sparse attention mechanism combining group attention (block-level average pooling over sequential Z-ordered segments) and top-k attention (selecting the most relevant blocks via differentiable importance scores), fused through a learned gate network. Z-order pooling then right-shifts the Morton codes to cluster spatially adjacent points, reducing the primitive count while preserving structural details. Two stacked ZFormer blocks produce two levels of Gaussians (L1, L2) fed to a two-layer MLP Gaussian head that predicts residual offsets and SH parameters initialized from pixel color. Training uses multi-task supervision with depth distillation from a frozen Depth-Anything-V2 model plus MSE and LPIPS rendering losses, and the DINOv2-Small encoder is initialized from pre-trained weights.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (360×640) with 12 input views, Ours#L1 achieves PSNR 28.56, SSIM 0.901, and LPIPS 0.110, outperforming the best feed-forward baseline AnySplat (PSNR 26.94) by 1.62 dB. On DL3DV (256×448) with 12 views, Ours#L1 reaches PSNR 27.09, SSIM 0.892, LPIPS 0.124, surpassing AnySplat (PSNR 24.90) by 2.19 dB. In terms of efficiency, Ours#L2 compresses Gaussian primitives to 1.42×10⁵ for 2 views (vs. 4.61×10⁵ for DepthSplat) while maintaining PSNR 26.42, and inference runs in 0.135 s—approximately 1000× faster than per-scene optimization methods like 3DGS (2 m 15 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two per-scene optimization methods (3DGS, MipSplatting) and two feed-forward methods (DepthSplat, AnySplat). Against the optimization baselines, Ours#L1 is dramatically superior in both speed (~1000× faster) and quality: with only 2 views on RealEstate10K, it achieves PSNR 26.43 versus 16.80 (3DGS) and 18.09 (MipSplatting). Among feed-forward methods, DepthSplat is the closest competitor at 2 views (PSNR 26.03 vs. 26.43), but Ours#L1 pulls ahead substantially as more views are added, reaching 28.56 vs. 26.33 at 12 views—a 2.23 dB gap. AnySplat, which uses voxel-based aggregation, trails Ours#L1 by 1.62 dB at 12 views on RealEstate10K and by 2.19 dB on DL3DV. The two-layer variant (Ours#L2) achieves the fewest Gaussian primitives (1.42×10⁵ for 2 views, vs. 4.61×10⁵ for DepthSplat and 3.53×10⁵ for AnySplat) while retaining PSNR 26.42, demonstrating that the Z-order compression preserves quality with a 2–3× reduction in primitives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting line (PixelSplat, DepthSplat, AnySplat) by replacing both pixel-level Gaussian assignment and voxel-grid aggregation with a Z-order (Morton curve) serialization of 3D points into a spatially coherent 1D sequence. The Z-order strategy is borrowed from spatial databases and point-cloud processing (notably Point Transformer V3), and is here repurposed to enable efficient neighborhood aggregation and adaptive Gaussian compression without the cubic memory blowup of voxel grids. The sparse attention design (group + top-k) draws on recent efficient-attention literature, while the backbone and depth initialization follow DINOv2 and Depth-Anything-V2, positioning the work as an architectural contribution within the established feed-forward GS paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical path toward real-time, generalizable 3D scene reconstruction by reducing Gaussian primitive counts by 2–3× over prior feed-forward baselines while improving PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS, and by eliminating the per-scene optimization bottleneck entirely. The Z-order serialization is a lightweight, hardware-friendly alternative to voxelization that could be adopted in other 3D representation pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether other space-filling curves (Hilbert, etc.) might offer better locality preservation, and how the method behaves under extreme view sparsity (e.g., 1–2 views) where depth estimation errors compound.
