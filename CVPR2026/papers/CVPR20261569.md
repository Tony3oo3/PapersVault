## Problem

Existing transferable adversarial attacks against MLLMs rely on surrogate models from a single learning paradigm (e.g., CLIP alone) and optimise perturbations independently in each model's feature space. This single-paradigm constraint limits feature diversity and causes perturbations to overfit one representational bias, while independent optimisation without cross-paradigm coordination leads to redundant gradient directions and local optima, collectively weakening transferability across heterogeneous MLLM architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image x_s and a target image x_t (drawn from ImageNet, Flickr30K, or MME) and produces a single adversarial perturbed image x_adv = x_s + δ under an ℓ∞ budget of ε = 16/255. During generation, the source and target images are simultaneously fed into three frozen surrogate encoders (CLIP, InternVL3-1B, DINOv2), and the MLLM's text generator produces natural-language descriptions of both images that are re-encoded by CLIP's text encoder to supply a semantic text channel. At inference, the adversarial image is passed to a black-box MLLM (open-source or closed-source), and an LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4o-mini) scores semantic similarity against a 0.5 threshold to determine attack success.

## Method

MPCAttack constructs a unified multi-paradigm feature representation by ℓ2-normalising and concatenating features from three complementary encoders: a cross-modal alignment branch (CLIP image features fused with CLIP text features of MLLM-generated captions via a λ-weighted combination, λ=0.6), a multi-modal understanding branch (InternVL3-1B), and a visual self-supervised branch (DINOv2). The core optimisation, Multi-Paradigm Collaborative Optimisation (MPCO), applies a contrastive matching loss over the concatenated features that simultaneously pulls z_adv toward z_t and pushes it away from z_s, using temperature-scaled cosine similarity (τ=0.2) and a balancing factor ω=2 to regulate the positive/negative attraction trade-off. This joint contrastive objective forces the perturbation to satisfy all three paradigm constraints simultaneously rather than optimising each independently, expanding the effective search space and mitigating single-paradigm representational bias. The perturbation is optimised for 300 iterations with a step size of 1/255 on a single RTX 3090, and the resulting adversarial example is evaluated against both open-source (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA, GLM-4.1V) and closed-source (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude-3.5, Gemini-2.0) MLLMs in targeted and untargeted settings.

## Results

On ImageNet (open-source MLLMs), MPCAttack achieves 63.33% average ASR in the targeted setting and 92.10% in the untargeted setting, outperforming the strongest baseline FOA-Attack (48.60% and 79.80% respectively). On closed-source models (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude-3.5, Gemini-2.0), MPCAttack reaches 63.38% targeted ASR and 90.55% untargeted ASR, again leading all baselines. On Flickr30K, the targeted ASR drops to 29.54% (open-source average) due to multi-caption ambiguity, but MPCAttack still nearly doubles FOA-Attack's 15.41%. The method uses ε=16/255 perturbation budget with 300 optimization steps on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPCAttack consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest competitor FOA-Attack, MPCAttack gains +14.73% targeted ASR (63.33% vs 48.60%) and +12.30% untargeted ASR (92.10% vs 79.80%) on open-source ImageNet models, with similar margins on closed-source models (+15.65% targeted, +7.92% untargeted). M-Attack, the second-strongest baseline, trails MPCAttack by 19.25% in targeted and 16.80% in untargeted open-source ASR. X-Transfer, which only appears in untargeted settings, achieves 55.98% average ASR—36.12% below MPCAttack. AnyAttack and CoA are essentially ineffective (ASR < 2% targeted, < 24% untargeted), confirming that single-paradigm or self-supervised-only approaches fail to produce transferable perturbations. The gap is most pronounced on InternVL3-8B (88.7% vs 72.3% targeted) and LLaVA-1.5-7B untargeted (99.3% vs 96.3%), suggesting multi-paradigm collaboration is especially beneficial for models with diverse internal representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transferable adversarial attacks on MLLMs (CoA, FOA-Attack, M-Attack, AnyAttack, X-Transfer) by departing from the single-paradigm surrogate model assumption that underpins all prior methods. Rather than optimizing perturbations within one feature space (e.g., CLIP's cross-modal alignment space) and fusing results post-hoc, MPCAttack jointly optimizes across three complementary paradigms—cross-modal alignment (CLIP), multi-modal understanding (InternVL3), and visual self-supervised learning (DINOv2)—using a contrastive matching objective (MPCO) that adaptively weights paradigm contributions. The conceptual departure is from independent per-paradigm optimization with simple aggregation to a unified collaborative optimization over concatenated, ℓ₂-normalized multi-paradigm features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results expose a persistent and substantial vulnerability in both open-source and closed-source MLLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude-3.5, Gemini-2.0), with untargeted ASR reaching 92–99% on several models, underscoring that current MLLM defenses are far from adequate. The finding that multi-paradigm collaboration yields consistently larger transferability gains than any single paradigm suggests that future attack and defense research should account for the heterogeneity of representation spaces rather than assuming a single surrogate suffices. Open questions include whether the gains saturate with additional paradigms, whether the approach extends to video or audio-visual MLLMs, and whether the identified vulnerabilities can be mitigated through training-time robustness or inference-time filtering.
