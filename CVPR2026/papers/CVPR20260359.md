## Problem

Existing methods for character motion synthesis and virtual cinematography operate in isolation, each solving only one of the two generation tasks required for storytelling 3D animation. The joint generation of coordinated multi-character motions and camera motions within a single framework has not been previously explored, despite the tight coupling between how characters move and how the camera frames them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is unconditional, taking only Gaussian noise as input and jointly producing a 120-frame (6-second at 20 fps) two-character motion sequence (150-dim per frame, encoding SMPL-22 joint rotations in 6D representation plus a 12-dim global placement offset per character) and a camera motion sequence in Toric space (6-dim per frame: normalized 2D on-screen head positions for both characters plus yaw and pitch angles). The Toric representation naturally encodes the spatial relationship between camera and characters, making it well-suited for modeling their coordination.

## Method

The architecture extends MDM's diffusion framework to a three-instance motion space with three parallel Transformer encoder backbones (one per entity: character A, character B, camera) coupled through three pairwise interaction modules. Each interaction module takes the feature embeddings of two entities and predicts bidirectional residual corrections that are summed into the original embeddings, enabling explicit modeling of character-character and camera-character dependencies without merging entities into a single sequence. The model is pre-trained on HumanML3D for the single-character backbone, then trained end-to-end on a curated 7,228-clip dataset mixing real film footage (extracted via MeTRAbs) and synthetic data from the Cine Tracer virtual production software, for 180,000 steps with 1,000 diffusion steps per sample.

## Results

On the character motion task, the proposed method achieves the best FID (0.113), InterFID (0.651), Coverage (0.264), and Density (0.990) among all baselines, outperforming the closest competitor ComMDM (FID 0.156, InterFID 0.746). On camera motion, it attains the lowest SeqFID (0.256) and FrameFID (0.268), roughly halving the error of CDM (0.629/0.638) and DC3D (0.417/0.605), while also achieving the highest Density (1.937). For character-camera coordination, the method records the lowest CLIP-based alignment loss (5.885) versus M2C-T (6.136) and AutoVisNarr (6.019).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-character motion baselines, the proposed method dominates on all quality metrics: it reduces FID by 28% over ComMDM (0.113 vs 0.156) and by 68% over InterGen (0.113 vs 0.354), while also achieving substantially better Coverage (0.264 vs 0.160 and 0.018) and Density (0.990 vs 0.627 and 0.226). The only metric where a baseline edges ahead is Diversity, where RIG (0.609) and InterGen (0.577) slightly exceed the proposed method (0.565), though the gap is small. On camera motion, the method halves the SeqFID of CDM (0.256 vs 0.629) and DC3D (0.256 vs 0.417) and improves FrameFID by 58% and 56% respectively, while DC3D retains a higher Diversity score (1.036 vs 0.771). For character-camera coordination, the joint diffusion model achieves the lowest alignment loss (5.885), beating the regression-based M2C-T (6.136) and the prior AutoVisNarr method (6.019), confirming that explicit pairwise interaction modeling yields tighter semantic coupling between character actions and cinematography.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDM (Human Motion Diffusion Model) architecture from single-character to a three-entity joint generation space (two characters + one camera), introducing pairwise interaction modules that explicitly model character-character and camera-character dependencies. It departs from the prior landscape where character motion synthesis (InterGen, ComMDM, RIG) and camera motion planning (CDM, DanceCamera3D, AutoVisNarr) are treated as separate problems, with camera methods typically requiring pre-existing character motions as input. The camera representation borrows Toric space coordinates from Christie and Lino, and the character representation follows the SMPL + 6D rotation convention established in recent motion generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly generate multi-character and camera motions in a single model, addressing a natural gap in automated storytelling animation pipelines where character and camera planning have been handled in isolation. The 7,228-clip dataset combining real film footage and synthetic Cine Tracer recordings is a useful resource for the community. However, the current scope is limited to exactly two characters and unconditional generation; the authors note that extending to multi-character scenes and text-conditional generation are key open directions. The modest margin on character-camera alignment (Table 3) suggests that capturing true narrative-level coordination remains an open challenge.
