## Problem

Existing shape deformation and interpolation methods either rely on fixed templates and parametric models (e.g., SMPL) that require manual rigging, or extract deformations from a single global latent vector that makes vertex-level local control infeasible. Computing natural, physically plausible deformations of only selected parts of a source mesh to match a target—without correspondence maps, texture, or skeletal joints—remains largely unaddressed in a purely data-driven framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260094/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source mesh in neutral pose and a target deformed mesh (which may have different vertex count and topology) as input, plus an optional per-face binary mask specifying which regions to deform. At test time, the mask is applied via a Hadamard product on the global latent encoding, so the model is trained exclusively on full deformations and generalizes to arbitrary masks. The output is a continuous deformation trajectory γ(t) over t∈[0,1] producing a sequence of near-isometric meshes, along with a per-vertex displacement field recovered from a predicted Jacobian field.

## Method

PaNDaS learns a per-face local feature field on the source mesh using DiffusionNet and a global pose encoding of the target via a novel spectral aggregation that decomposes local features into the cotangent Laplacian eigenbasis and concatenates the first s eigenprojections. These are concatenated into a joint feature field ω_j = (u_j, z), which feeds a Neural Jacobian Fields generator that predicts per-face Jacobian matrices; a differentiable Poisson solve then recovers a smooth vertex displacement. Partial deformations are achieved at test time by element-wise masking the global encoding z with a binary face mask, requiring no retraining. Training is supervised with MSE reconstruction loss plus a Jacobian normal-map regularization term, and the entire pipeline is remeshing-invariant by construction since both feature extractors use DiffusionNet.

## Results

On full DFAUST interpolation, PaNDaS achieves a mean MSE of 4.1 and mean Cosine distance of 0.10, outperforming ARAPReg (MSE 6.2, Cosd 0.17) and SMS (MSE 7.2, Cosd 0.14). On COMA face expressions, PaNDaS reaches MSE 0.06 and Cosd 1.3×10⁻², beating ARAPReg (MSE 0.08, Cosd 1.4×10⁻²) and NJF (MSE 0.27, Cosd 2.5×10⁻²). For partial deformations on DFAUST, PaNDaS attains MSE 3.4 and Cosd 0.09, and on unregistered DFAUST scans it achieves CD 4.80×10⁻³ and HD 0.65, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaNDaS consistently outperforms ARAPReg [23] on both MSE and Cosd across DFAUST (4.1 vs 6.2 MSE; 0.10 vs 0.17 Cosd) and COMA (0.06 vs 0.08 MSE; 1.3 vs 1.4 Cosd×10⁻²), with the largest gap on the 'jumping' sequence (MSE 5.8 vs 10.6). Against SMS [12], PaNDaS wins on both metrics for DFAUST full interpolation (MSE 4.1 vs 7.2; Cosd 0.10 vs 0.14). For partial deformations, PaNDaS beats VCMC [60] on MSE (3.4 vs 6.0) and Cosd (0.09 vs 0.15), and surpasses localized ARAP [47] on MSE (3.4 vs 4.4) though ARAP edges out slightly on Cosd (0.08 vs 0.09). On unregistered DFAUST scans, PaNDaS clearly dominates both NJF [1] (CD 4.80 vs 6.67×10⁻³; HD 0.65 vs 0.79) and ARAP (CD 4.80 vs 6.03×10⁻³; HD 0.65 vs 0.71), confirming that the learned local feature representation captures geometry better than concatenating a global code to vertex positions.

## Relation to Prior Work

PaNDaS builds directly on Neural Jacobian Fields (NJF) for its deformation generator (predicting per-face Jacobians and recovering displacement via a Poisson solve) and on DiffusionNet for remeshing-invariant local feature extraction. It departs from prior learning-based deformation methods (ARAPReg, SMS, LIMP, NeuroMorph) that encode shapes as a single global latent vector, by introducing a per-face local feature field concatenated with a global pose encoding. The key conceptual departure is that partial deformations are achieved at test time by simply masking the global component of the feature field, while training uses only full deformations—no local supervision or additional priors (texture, skeleton, user prompts) are required, unlike recent diffusion-prior or handle-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PaNDaS fills a practical gap: it provides vertex-level partial shape deformations and interpolations without committing to a parametric template, rig, or skinning map, making it applicable to arbitrary shape categories beyond humans. The simplicity of the masking strategy (zeroing the global latent for unmasked faces) is appealing for adoption, and the remeshing invariance inherited from DiffusionNet extends applicability to unregistered scans. Limitations include training on registered meshes only, boundary artifacts from binary masking, and the lack of a guarantee that deformation is strictly confined to the masked region. Open questions include whether weighted or learned masking can eliminate boundary artifacts, and whether the framework can be trained end-to-end on unregistered data.
