## Problem

Current open-world promptable 3D segmentation methods (e.g., Find3D) infer part semantics by matching geometry to text in the raw input pose space, which breaks down when geometrically similar parts serve different functions (chair arms vs. legs) or when geometrically distinct parts share semantics (airplane vs. bird wings). Humans resolve such ambiguities by mentally rotating objects into canonical frames, but no existing 3D segmentation model internalizes this canonical-space reasoning, leading to inconsistent predictions under pose variation, symmetry, and cross-category transfer.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (5,000 uniformly sampled surface points with RGB color and normal vectors, normalized to a unit bounding box) and a free-form text prompt (either a single word like 'leg' or a compositional phrase like 'leg of a chair') as input, and produces a per-point part-level segmentation mask in a single feedforward pass. During training, the canonical branch additionally outputs an RGB-encoded 3D canonical map (three continuous scalar fields per part) and a 6D semantic bounding box per part, both used only as supervisory signals and discarded at inference.

## Method

CoSMo3D introduces a dual-branch architecture: a feature-extraction branch (PointTransformerV3 backbone + SigLIP text encoder + 3-layer MLP projection) handles geometry-language alignment at both training and inference, while a training-only canonical embedding branch predicts per-part canonical maps and semantic bounding boxes to induce a latent canonical reference frame from data. The training objective combines three losses: a hard-negative-sampling contrastive alignment loss for soft point-text affinities, a canonical map anchoring loss that matches predicted and ground-truth per-part canonical distributions via bidirectional Chamfer distance (making it inherently symmetry-robust and order-invariant without manual axis annotations), and a canonical box calibration loss (L1 on 6D box parameters) that tightens spatial extents. The training data is a unified cross-category canonical dataset built by LLM-guided hierarchical alignment: GPT clusters 200 categories into 19 functional groups, then intra-cluster alignment uses discrete rotations and cross-cluster alignment verifies high-level semantic consistency (e.g., shared forward direction). Training proceeds in two stages—first the contrastive alignment loss alone, then the two canonical-space regularizers are added—to stabilize convergence.

## Results

On the primary open-world benchmark 3Dcompat200, CoSMo3D achieves 47.51–54.55% mIoU on the coarse-grained test set and 24.46–31.29% mIoU on the fine-grained test set, outperforming the strongest 3D feedforward baseline (Find3D retrained on their data) by 8–11% and 4–7% absolute mIoU respectively. On ShapeNet-Part, it reaches 32.84–36.16% mIoU (29.89% average gain over the best baseline), and on PartNet-E, 17.19–18.48% mIoU (5.01% gain). Inference runs at 0.9 seconds per shape, compared to ~2.5 minutes for 2D rendering-based methods like PartSLIP++.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoSMo3D outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and test setting. Against Find3D (the closest 3D feedforward competitor), it gains 8–11% absolute mIoU on 3Dcompat-Coarse and 4–7% on 3Dcompat-Fine; even when Find3D is retrained on the authors' curated canonical dataset (Find3D*), CoSMo3D still leads by 10.35% (Coarse, {Part} of {Obj.} canonical) and 5.41% (Fine, {Part} of {Obj.} canonical). The 2D rendering-based baselines (PartSLIP++, PointCLIPV2) are far behind—e.g., PointCLIPV2 scores only 14.09% vs. 47.51% on 3Dcompat-Coarse canonical {Part} of {Obj.}—and are also ~165× slower (2.5 min vs. 0.9 s per shape). On ShapeNet-Part, CoSMo3D leads Find3D* by 5.54% (canonical {Part} of {Obj.}) and 3.26% (rotated {Part}), while on PartNet-E the margin is smaller (1.11–2.11%) due to the dataset's emphasis on material-level annotations rarely seen in other 3D benchmarks. Notably, PartSLIP++* achieves an anomalously high 32.71% on PartNet-E canonical {Part} prompts, likely benefiting from its GLIP backbone pretrained on ~27M image-text pairs, but it collapses to 3.87% under rotated poses, underscoring the pose-fragility of 2D rendering pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Find3D, which introduced open-world promptable 3D segmentation via geometry-text contrastive alignment, and retains its Pt3 + SigLIP feature extraction backbone. The key departure is the argument that geometry-text matching alone is insufficient—similar shapes can carry different semantics (chair arms vs. legs) while dissimilar shapes can share semantics (airplane vs. bird wings)—and that a canonical spatial reference frame is the missing ingredient. The paper operationalizes this through two novel contributions: an LLM-guided cross-category canonicalization pipeline that extends prior intra-category canonicalization to a unified 200-category space, and a dual-branch training architecture with canonical map anchoring (distribution-level Chamfer distance) and canonical box calibration losses that were absent from Find3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The canonical-space framing provides a principled explanation for why pose-robust, cross-category 3D part segmentation has been hard, and the 25.55% average mIoU gain over Find3D (with consistent improvements under rotation and compositional prompts) validates the approach. The LLM-guided cross-category canonicalization pipeline is a reusable data-construction recipe that could benefit other 3D understanding tasks. Open questions include whether the induced canonical frame generalizes to truly unseen object families beyond the 200 training categories, whether the two-stage training and dual-branch overhead are justified at scale, and whether the canonical-space representation can be transferred to downstream tasks like 3D agents or compositional query answering as the authors speculate.
