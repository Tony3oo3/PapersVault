## Problem

Maritime multimodal vision requires accurate cross-modal registration and fusion of visible and infrared imagery, yet no large-scale dataset provides precisely aligned visible-infrared pairs under realistic adverse maritime weather. Existing urban-focused datasets (KAIST, LLVIP, RoadScene) do not transfer to sea scenes, while maritime datasets (Tri-band, VAIS, RGBT-Tiny) either lack pixel-level alignment or fail to capture complex weather conditions such as fog, rain, and typhoon.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263509/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 128,100 spatially and temporally aligned visible-infrared image pairs at uniform resolutions of 960×770 or 640×512, plus 50 annotated video sequences at 15 fps, spanning seven weather conditions (Sunny, Cloudy, Night, Rainy, Foggy, Low-light, Typhoon) and three viewpoints (UAV, coastal fixed, shipborne). Unregistered raw data are also included to support registration algorithm evaluation. Downstream tasks consume these pairs to produce aligned image pairs, fused images, bounding boxes for ships and buoys, or infrared images generated from visible inputs.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-platform data acquisition and calibration pipeline using custom long-range/mid-range telephoto pods and a DJI Matrice 4 airborne pod, each with rigidly fixed visible (1920×1080) and infrared (8–14 µm, 1280×1024 or 640×512) cameras. A multi-scene adaptive homography calibration procedure estimates per-scene transformation matrices by extracting cross-modal correspondences with MINIMA, fitting homographies via RANSAC with least-squares refinement, and validating alignment through reprojection error minimization and manual consistency checks on sea horizons and vessel contours. The benchmark then systematically evaluates nine SOTA registration methods (sparse, semi-dense, dense), ten fusion methods, YOLO11 fine-tuned for ship/buoy detection, and five visible-to-infrared translation methods, establishing baselines across all seven weather conditions.

## Results

On the MMVIP registration benchmark, the fine-tuned dense matcher MINIMA_RoMa achieves the best performance across all six metrics: MSE 0.0039, RMSE 0.0464, SSIM 0.8822, NCC 0.8537, MI 1.6406, with 0% failure rate and 49.64% inaccurate rate, substantially outperforming the next-best RoMa (MSE 0.0191, SSIM 0.8244) and sparse method MINIMA_LG (MSE 0.0198, SSIM 0.8297). For object detection with YOLO11, visible images yield higher mAP@0.5 for Ship (0.907 vs. 0.854) and Buoy (0.795 vs. 0.599) compared to infrared, though infrared achieves slightly higher precision on Buoy (0.889 vs. 0.874). Dense matching methods dominate registration accuracy but incur 4–5× higher runtime (RoMa: 5042.6 ms vs. MINIMA_LG: 480.8 ms per 960×770 pair).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine registration methods benchmarked, the fine-tuned dense matcher MINIMA_RoMa [28] dominates all six accuracy metrics, reducing MSE by ~5× over RoMa [6] (0.0039 vs. 0.0191) and by ~23× over the worst performer JamMa [25] (0.0888), while also cutting runtime nearly in half versus RoMa (2078.8 ms vs. 5042.6 ms). Among semi-dense methods, CasP [4] and XoFTR [36] are the strongest (SSIM 0.7256 and 0.7558 respectively), approaching RoMa's level but still trailing MINIMA_RoMa by 0.13–0.16 SSIM; JamMa [25] and RDD/RDD* [3] perform poorly with 80–96% inaccurate rates. In the sparse category, MINIMA_LG [28] (built on LightGlue [19]) offers the best speed–accuracy trade-off at 480.8 ms per pair, whereas RDD [3] is both slower (904.6 ms) and less accurate (MSE 0.0562). For object detection, YOLO11 [17] on visible images achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.907 (Ship) and 0.795 (Buoy), outperforming infrared by 5.3 and 19.6 points respectively, confirming that visible texture remains critical for localization even in adverse weather. In image fusion and translation, no single method wins uniformly: DCEvo [23] and LUT-Fuse [46] favor infrared saliency at the cost of hull texture, while RFfusion [41] and GIFNet [5] preserve visible detail but under-highlight distant targets; for visible-to-infrared translation, DR-AVIT [13] produces the most thermally consistent outputs under typhoon and night conditions, whereas sRGB-TIR [18] and ThermalGen [43] exhibit notable artifacts and blurred thermal hierarchies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new algorithmic method but rather fills a critical data gap in the visible-infrared multimodal vision literature. Prior visible-infrared paired datasets (KAIST, LLVIP, RoadScene, FMB) target urban or terrestrial scenes, while existing maritime collections (SMD, VAIS, Tri-band, RGBT-Tiny) either lack precise spatial alignment or fail to capture adverse weather conditions. MMVIP carves new ground by combining rigorous electro-optical pod calibration (intrinsic/extrinsic + multi-scene homography via RANSAC) with large-scale acquisition across seven weather states, repurposing standard calibration and registration pipelines in a domain where sparse sea-surface features make them particularly challenging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a bottleneck that has constrained maritime multimodal perception research: the absence of precisely aligned visible-infrared pairs under realistic adverse weather. The benchmark results reveal that even the best registration method (MINIMA_RoMa) still yields a ~50% inaccuracy rate, and all fusion and translation methods show clear degradation under maritime-specific challenges (salt haze, wave reflections, high dynamic range), indicating substantial headroom for future work. Open questions include handling platform-motion blur, detecting minuscule targets, and suppressing sea-surface clutter in cross-modal generation—directions the authors explicitly flag for future expansion.
