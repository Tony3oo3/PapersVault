## Problem

Existing one-shot 3D Gaussian head avatar methods (GAGAvatar, LAM, Avat3r) suffer from redundant Gaussian utilization, excessive computational cost that scales poorly with mesh resolution, and an inability to dynamically adjust detail level for diverse hardware. They also produce incomplete reconstructions in non-head regions such as shoulders, and none support runtime level-of-detail control to balance quality against inference speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262385/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB source image (cropped to the head region) and optionally a driving image for reenactment. It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian head-and-shoulder avatar with three selectable LOD levels (Sub#0 ≈5K, Sub#1 ≈29K, Sub#2 ≈80K Gaussians), rendered via 3D Gaussian Splatting at up to 126 FPS on an RTX 4090. For reenactment, only the positional component of the Gaussian set is updated from driving FLAME parameters, while all other attributes (color, opacity, scale, rotation) remain fixed from the source identity.

## Method

OMG-Avatar extracts global features via cross-attention using FLAME positional embeddings as queries at only the initial 5K-vertex resolution, then progressively refines both geometry and features through two levels of mesh subdivision (reaching ~80K vertices) with per-level MLPs. Local features are obtained by projecting subdivided vertices into the image plane and bilinearly sampling DINOv2 feature maps. An occlusion-aware fusion module uses a depth-buffer-derived binary visibility mask to selectively add local features only for visible vertices, while occluded regions rely on global features alone. The head (FLAME-based Gaussians) and shoulders (CNN-predicted Gaussians from a masked feature plane) are modeled independently and concatenated into a unified Gaussian set. Training is self-supervised on VFHQ video frame pairs with a coarse-to-fine curriculum that gradually increases subdivision depth, and a UNet neural refiner upsamples multi-channel splatted feature maps into the final RGB output.

## Results

On VFHQ, OMG-Avatar (Sub#2) achieves PSNR 22.72, SSIM 0.831, LPIPS 0.091, and CSIM 0.869 for self-reenactment, outperforming the closest competitor LAM (PSNR 22.65, LPIPS 0.109, CSIM 0.822). On HDTF, the gains are more pronounced: PSNR 24.14, LPIPS 0.061, and CSIM 0.943 versus LAM's 23.43, 0.097, and 0.865. Notably, even the low-resolution Sub#1 variant (~29K Gaussians) surpasses LAM (80K Gaussians) and GAGAvatar (180K Gaussians) on both datasets. Inference speed reaches 85 FPS on A100 and 126 FPS on RTX 4090, the fastest among neural-rendering-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMG-Avatar (Sub#2) outperforms all 12 baselines on both VFHQ and HDTF across every reported metric. Against the strongest Gaussian-based competitor LAM (80K Gaussians), it gains +0.07 PSNR and −0.018 LPIPS on VFHQ, and a more substantial +0.71 PSNR and −0.036 LPIPS on HDTF, while also improving identity consistency (CSIM 0.869 vs 0.822 on VFHQ; 0.943 vs 0.865 on HDTF). Against GAGAvatar (180K Gaussians), the advantage is even larger: +0.89 PSNR and −0.031 LPIPS on VFHQ, with GAGAvatar also exhibiting visible neck blurring. Among NeRF-based methods, Portrait4D-v2 is the closest (PSNR 21.34 on VFHQ), but OMG-Avatar leads by +1.38 PSNR and −0.053 LPIPS; older NeRF methods like HideNeRF, OTAvatar, and ROME trail by 2–5 dB in PSNR. The low-resolution Sub#1 variant (~29K Gaussians) still beats both LAM and GAGAvatar on all metrics, demonstrating that the hierarchical feature extraction and occlusion-aware fusion strategy is effective even at reduced Gaussian budgets. In terms of speed, OMG-Avatar achieves 85 FPS (A100) / 126 FPS (RTX 4090), surpassing GAGAvatar (67 FPS) and all NeRF-based methods (4.5–18 FPS), though LAM reports 280 FPS without neural rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

OMG-Avatar builds directly on the GAGAvatar pipeline (DINOv2 feature extraction, FLAME-based 3DMM parameterization, multi-channel feature map rendering) and the FLAME morphable model as its geometric prior. Its key departure from LAM and GAGAvatar is a coarse-to-fine mesh subdivision strategy: cross-attention is performed only at the initial 5K-vertex level, and high-resolution geometry is obtained through progressive subdivision and projection-based sampling, avoiding the exponential cost of attending over 80K+ vertices. The occlusion-aware depth-buffer fusion and the separate shoulder-region modeling via image segmentation are novel architectural choices not present in prior one-shot Gaussian head methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.2-second reconstruction time and 85 FPS rendering (126 FPS on RTX 4090) make animatable Gaussian head avatars practical for real-time applications like virtual meetings and live streaming, where prior methods were too slow. The multi-LOD capability is a practical differentiator: a single model can serve devices with vastly different compute budgets by switching subdivision levels at inference. However, reliance on the FLAME prior limits expressiveness (no tongue, hair, or fine skin dynamics), and monocular training restricts robustness beyond ±60° head rotation—open questions that multi-view training and more expressive priors could address.
