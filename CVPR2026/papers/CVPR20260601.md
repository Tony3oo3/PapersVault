## Problem

Existing Continual Category Discovery (CCD) methods assume that streaming unlabeled data originates from the same domain as the labeled training set, an assumption that fails in real-world deployments where data arrives from heterogeneous, shifting sources (e.g., different hospitals, weather conditions). This paper formalizes the more challenging Open Continual Category Discovery (OCCD) setting, where a model must simultaneously discover novel categories and maintain known-class recognition under concurrent domain and category shifts—a combination that causes severe negative transfer in prior approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ViT-B/16 backbone (DINO-pretrained, fine-tuned on labeled known-category images from a source domain) and sequentially processes unlabeled RGB image batches drawn from one or more unseen target domains, each batch mixing known and novel class samples. At each stage it outputs soft class probabilities for known categories and Affinity Propagation cluster assignments for novel ones, dynamically expanding the classifier head. The separation of known vs. unknown samples is performed internally via PUOT marginal probabilities without requiring any prior knowledge of the unknown-class proportion.

## Method

The framework comprises three modules operating on a shared ViT feature extractor. First, a Weight-Aware Separation Module (WSM) formulates instance-to-prototype assignment as a Partial Unbalanced Optimal Transport problem with a KL-relaxed target marginal, then computes each sample's marginal transport probability across seven temperature values to build a binary response-spectrum matrix, on which K-means yields a known/unknown partition. Second, a Cross-Domain Semantic Alignment module estimates confidence-weighted prototypes for known classes in the target stream and trains a CNN discriminator (with a Gradient Reversal Layer) to make source and target prototypes indistinguishable, yielding domain-invariant known-class representations. Third, a Category Topology Consistency Constraint applies FFT-based AdaIN-style frequency augmentation to unknown samples and minimizes a Gaussian-potential loss that enforces invariance of the sample-to-prototype distance ranking across the original and stylized views, preserving semantic relational structure under domain shift.

## Results

On the SSB-C benchmark, the proposed method achieves 52.9% All accuracy on CUB-C (Original) and 47.2% on CUB-C (Corrupted), outperforming the strongest baseline PromptCCD by 22.8% and 19.8% respectively. On DomainNet (Real→Painting), the method reaches 60.2% All accuracy on the source domain and 38.9% on the target domain, surpassing PromptCCD (56.5% / 31.5%) and HiLo (56.1% / 31.0%). Across all four DomainNet domain pairs and three SSB-C datasets, the method consistently leads on both known (Old) and novel (New) class accuracy, with the largest margins on the corrupted/target domains where distribution shift is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and domain setting. Against the strongest CCD baseline PromptCCD, gains range from +12.2% (FGVC-Aircraft-C Original All: 43.3 vs 29.9) to +22.8% (CUB-C Original All: 52.9 vs 30.1), with the largest margins on corrupted domains where visual drift is most pronounced. HiLo, the only GCD method explicitly designed for domain shifts, is surpassed by 20.6% on CUB-C Original and 13.4% on CUB-C Corrupted, confirming that its patch-mixing augmentation introduces semantic noise under streaming conditions. Among CCD-specific methods, DEAN and PA-CGCD trail PromptCCD by 1–2 points, while G&M underperforms all others (16.4% on CUB-C Original), suggesting its grow-and-merge strategy is particularly fragile under distribution shift. On DomainNet, the method leads PromptCCD by 3.7–12.2% on source-domain All accuracy and by 7.4–13.7% on target-domain All accuracy across the four domain pairs, with the largest target-domain gain on Real→Quickdraw (23.5 vs 19.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Continual Category Discovery (CCD) methods such as DEAN, PromptCCD, and PA-CGCD by introducing the 'Open' dimension—simultaneous domain shift alongside novel category emergence—which prior CCD work explicitly assumed away. The Weight-Aware Separation Module repurposes Partial Unbalanced Optimal Transport (originally from domain adaptation) for known/unknown sample partitioning, while the Cross-Domain Semantic Alignment borrows adversarial prototype matching from source-free domain adaptation. The Category Topology Consistency Constraint draws on the GCD insight that inter-class relational structure aids discovery, but enforces it via FFT-based frequency augmentation rather than spatial mixing. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of CCD, unsupervised domain adaptation, and open-set recognition, unifying them under a single streaming framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing OCCD, the paper highlights a critical gap: real-world data streams (medical imaging across hospitals, street-view under varying conditions) inherently combine category novelty with domain drift, yet prior CCD methods silently assume distributional stationarity. The substantial margins over CCD baselines (e.g., +22.8% on CUB-C) underscore how fragile existing approaches are under even moderate visual corruption. Open questions include whether the PUOT separation generalizes to extreme domain gaps (e.g., X-ray to MRI), whether the three-stage pipeline scales beyond T=3 stages without compounding drift, and whether the frequency-domain augmentation transfers to non-natural-image modalities. The framework also raises the question of whether adversarial prototype alignment remains stable when the number of known classes grows over many stages.
