## Problem

Real-world opera video super-resolution faces two intertwined challenges: existing degradation modeling approaches either produce out-of-distribution synthetic noise or introduce style-mismatch artifacts when borrowing real noise from external datasets, and image-only feature representations lack the high-level semantic guidance needed to reconstruct realistic textures in complex scenes such as opera performances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260198/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the network consumes 7-frame degraded clips (64×64, generated via a Real-ESRGAN high-order pipeline) paired with two types of text: degradation-descriptive text (binning-based severity labels like 'light blur, medium noise') fed to the negative branch, and content-descriptive text (frame-level captions from LLaVA) fed to the positive branch. At inference, only the positive branch operates, taking the degraded video and its per-frame content text as input and producing 256×256 super-resolved frames; the degradation text and negative branch are discarded.

## Method

TextOVSR is a dual-branch network built on BasicVSR's bidirectional propagation, where the positive branch fuses CLIP-encoded content text early (before deep feature extraction) and the negative branch fuses degradation text late (after feature extraction), both via a Degradation-Robust Feature Fusion (DRF) module that applies self-attention to image features and cross-attention (image as Query, text as Key/Value) to suppress degradation-induced contamination. A Text-Enhanced Discriminator (TED) extends a standard UNet discriminator by concatenating filtered text features with image features for more semantically informed adversarial guidance. Training proceeds in two stages: first optimizing reconstruction and negative losses alone, then fine-tuning with perceptual, CLIPIQA, and adversarial losses under the GAN framework. Optical flow from frozen SPyNet handles temporal alignment, and the entire system is trained on the COVC dataset with pre-generated degradations to ensure text-degradation alignment across epochs.

## Results

On the OperaLQ benchmark, TextOVSR achieves the best scores on 8 out of 9 reference-free quality metrics, including CLIPIQA+ (0.5667 vs. 0.3990 for NegVSR), TOPIQ (0.4636 vs. 0.4354), NIQE (3.5139 vs. 4.0756), and the video-level DOVER score (45.0415 vs. 40.6763). The only metric where it trails is MUSIQ (58.30 vs. 58.64 for NegVSR), a marginal 0.33-point gap. In terms of efficiency, TextOVSR uses 5.7M parameters and 309.6 GFLOPs, comparable to NegVSR (3.4M, 292.0 GFLOPs) and far lighter than Self-BlindVSR (139.5M, 2518.0 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TextOVSR is compared against seven real-world VSR baselines on the OperaLQ benchmark. It decisively outperforms NegVSR [31] (its direct architectural predecessor) on 8 of 9 metrics, with the largest gains on CLIPIQA+ (+0.168), DOVER (+4.37), and NIQE (−0.56), while trailing NegVSR by only 0.33 on MUSIQ. Against RealViformer [49], the second-strongest baseline, TextOVSR leads on every metric, with particularly large margins on CLIPIQA+ (0.5667 vs. 0.3774) and DOVER (45.04 vs. 39.43). The remaining baselines—DBVSR [25], RealBasicVSR [7], FTVSR [26], Self-BlindVSR [1], and BVSR-IK [51]—are all substantially outperformed across all quality metrics, with Self-BlindVSR [1] being the heaviest model (139.5M params, 2518 GFLOPs) yet scoring far below TextOVSR on every metric. Notably, TextOVSR achieves these gains with only 5.7M parameters and 309.6 GFLOPs, making it competitive in efficiency with the lightweight NegVSR (3.4M, 292 GFLOPs) while delivering markedly better perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TextOVSR is built directly on NegVSR's dual-branch architecture with negative noise constraints and the BasicVSR bidirectional propagation backbone, adopting Real-ESRGAN's high-order degradation pipeline for training data synthesis. Its key departure from prior RWVSR work is the injection of two types of textual prompts—degradation-descriptive text in the negative branch and content-descriptive text in the positive branch—via a novel cross-attention-based DRF module, rather than relying on the computationally expensive diffusion models that recent text-guided SR methods (e.g., STAR, DiffVSR) employ. The two-stage GAN training strategy follows RealBasicVSR, but the Text-Enhanced Discriminator (TED) is a new contribution that selectively filters textual features before adversarial discrimination, distinguishing it from prior CLIP-based discriminators like GALIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that lightweight text-guided priors can substantially improve real-world VSR without the multi-step denoising overhead of diffusion models, offering a practical path for deployment-constrained settings. However, the evaluation is confined to a single domain-specific benchmark (OperaLQ, 50 videos), and the method depends on an external MLLM (LLaVA) for content text generation, adding a non-trivial inference dependency. Open questions include whether the DRF module and TED generalize to other video domains (surveillance, medical, sports), whether degradation-descriptive text can be inferred at test time to eliminate the training-only constraint, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher resolution factors.
