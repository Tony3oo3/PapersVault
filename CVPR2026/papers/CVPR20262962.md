## Problem

All-in-one image restoration with a shared backbone suffers from cross-task interference: parameters specialized for one degradation conflict with those needed by others, destabilizing joint training as the number of tasks grows. Existing remedies—prompt injection, MoE routing, or diffusion-based conditioning—enlarge the parameter space, add routing hyperparameters, or increase inference latency, making them impractical for efficient deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262962/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single degraded RGB image (hazy, rainy, noisy, blurred, or low-light) at 224×224 resolution and outputs a restored clean RGB image of the same size. No explicit degradation label is required at inference; the degradation matching module automatically identifies the corruption type from the input features. The degradation amalgamator, which constructs the degradation bank from paired (LQ, HQ) data, is used only during training and is entirely removed at test time.

## Method

R2R adopts a lightweight NAFNet-based U-shaped encoder-decoder as a task-agnostic backbone and externalizes degradation knowledge into a compact degradation bank of task-level clean priors. During training, a degradation amalgamator encodes residuals and ground-truth images through a NAF-style encoder, then applies 3D convolutions along the batch axis (grouped by degradation label) to aggregate intra-class features into unified key-value prototypes. At inference, a degradation matching module flattens the encoded query features, computes similarity against all bank keys, applies local-mean scoring over task windows to select the correct degradation, masks irrelevant entries, and fuses the retrieved value prior with the query via channel-interleaved concatenation and a gated grouped convolution. Training uses a composite loss combining pixel L1, FFT-domain L1, and two cross-entropy classification losses (one on the amalgamator backbone, one on the matching stage) to enforce degradation separability and correct prior retrieval.

## Results

On the five-degradation all-in-one benchmark (SOTS, Rain100L, BSD68 σ=25, GoPro, LOL-v1), R2R achieves an average PSNR of 30.48 dB and SSIM of 0.921 using only 12G MACs, outperforming PromptIR (29.15 dB, 132G), Gridformer (29.33 dB, 251G), and AirNet (25.49 dB, 238G) by 1.33, 1.15, and 4.99 dB respectively. On the three-degradation setting, R2R reaches 32.53 dB average PSNR with 12G MACs, surpassing PromptIR (32.06 dB, 132G) by 0.47 dB while using 91% fewer MACs. The model also demonstrates the smallest performance drop when scaling from single-task to multi-task settings, confirming robustness to cross-task interference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2R outperforms all all-in-one baselines on average PSNR and SSIM across both the three- and five-degradation settings while using 88–95% fewer MACs. Against PromptIR (132G MACs), R2R gains +0.47 dB (3-deg) and +1.33 dB (5-deg) in average PSNR; against Gridformer (251G), the gains are +0.34 dB and +1.15 dB respectively. Compared to AirNet (238G), R2R improves by +1.33 dB (3-deg) and +4.99 dB (5-deg), with the larger gap on five tasks reflecting AirNet's severe cross-task interference. Against the strongest recent all-in-one method Perceive-IR, R2R leads by +0.37 dB average PSNR (5-deg) and +0.42 dB SSIM. On individual tasks, R2R achieves the best GoPro deblurring score (30.93 dB vs. 29.46 for Perceive-IR) and the best SOTS dehazing score (30.64 dB vs. 28.19 for Perceive-IR), while matching or slightly trailing on Rain100L deraining (36.61 vs. 37.25 for Perceive-IR). Against general-purpose restorers (Restormer, MambaIR, NAFNet), R2R gains 1.5–2.9 dB on average, confirming that task-specific degradation priors provide substantial benefit over generic architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

R2R builds on the NAFNet lightweight CNN backbone (Chen et al., ECCV 2022) and borrows the degradation classification head idea from Hu et al. (2025) to supervise feature separability. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant all-in-one restoration paradigm—where methods like PromptIR, AirNet, and MoCE-IR inject prompts, contrastive embeddings, or MoE routing *inside* a shared backbone to handle multiple degradations—by externalizing degradation knowledge into a compact, retrieval-based degradation bank. The key positioning is that intra-class similarity and inter-class separability of common degradations (streaks in rain, veiling in haze, high-frequency noise) make a task-level prior sufficient, eliminating the need for heavy internal modulation or expert ensembles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× reduction in MACs (12G vs. 132G for PromptIR) while matching or exceeding SOTA PSNR across three and five degradation benchmarks makes R2R a practically compelling option for latency- and memory-constrained deployment of all-in-one restorers. The retrieval-based design is inherently extensible: adding a new degradation type requires only a new bank entry rather than retraining or re-routing the backbone, which addresses a real scalability concern in the field. Open questions include how well the approach generalizes to truly unseen or mixed degradations not represented in the bank, and whether the assumption of strong intra-class similarity holds for more subtle or compound corruptions. The paper does not explore these regimes, leaving the boundary of the method's applicability somewhat unclear.
