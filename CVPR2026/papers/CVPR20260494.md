## Problem

LogitNorm, a popular training-time method for OOD detection, inadvertently induces feature collapse—both dimensional collapse (features compress into a few dominant directions) and origin collapse (OOD features cluster near the origin)—which degrades ID classification accuracy and restricts compatibility with post-hoc OOD scoring functions. No existing hyperparameter-free training formulation simultaneously improves OOD detection across diverse scoring methods while preserving strong in-distribution performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260494/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for ImageNet-200/1K) processed by a CNN backbone (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50). At inference, the trained model produces standard class logits from which any post-hoc OOD score (MSP, KNN, GEN, ReAct, SCALE, fDBD) can be computed; the training modification does not alter the architecture or inference pipeline, only the loss function used during optimization.

## Method

ELogitNorm replaces the LogitNorm scaling factor τ‖f‖ (proportional to the feature norm ‖z‖, i.e., distance to the origin) with D(z), the average point-to-plane distance from the feature vector z to all competing class decision boundaries in the final linear layer. This is a hyperparameter-free modification to the cross-entropy loss: the logit for each class is divided by D(z) before the softmax, making the effective temperature sample-dependent and boundary-aware. Theoretically, while LogitNorm's minimum scaling factor is attained at a single point (the origin), ELogitNorm's minimum is attained on an (m−c+1)-dimensional affine subspace, preventing collapse to a singular point. Empirically, this yields a more evenly distributed singular value spectrum, better-calibrated predictions (lower ECE), and consistent OOD detection gains across all tested post-hoc scoring methods without sacrificing ID accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ID) with ResNet-18, ELogitNorm-enhanced post-hoc methods achieve substantial far-OOD gains: SCALE* reaches 96.94% AUROC / 13.18% FPR95 (vs. 86.46% / 67.49% baseline), a +10.48 AUROC and −54.31 FPR95 improvement. On ImageNet-1K (ID) with ResNet-50 and MSP scoring, ELogitNorm attains 93.19% AUROC / 27.74% FPR95 on far-OOD, outperforming both Cross-Entropy (85.23% / 51.45%) and LogitNorm (91.54% / 31.32%). The method is hyperparameter-free and preserves ID classification accuracy (95.11% on CIFAR-10, 87.12% on ImageNet-200 at 200 epochs), matching or exceeding the Cross-Entropy baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELogitNorm consistently outperforms LogitNorm on far-OOD detection (93.19 vs. 91.54 AUROC on ImageNet-1K) while remaining stable on near-OOD where LogitNorm degrades (76.88 vs. 74.62 AUROC). Against other training-based methods, ELogitNorm with KNN scoring achieves 96.08 AUROC on ImageNet-200 far-OOD, surpassing CIDER (90.66) and NPOS (94.83), despite CIDER and NPOS being specifically designed for KNN. The method enhances all six evaluated post-hoc scorers (MSP, ReAct, KNN, GEN, fDBD, SCALE) with the largest gains on far-OOD (e.g., SCALE far-OOD FPR95 drops from 67.49% to 13.18%), whereas LogitNorm can actually degrade performance under certain scorers like ReAct. On ID classification accuracy, ELogitNorm matches or slightly exceeds Cross-Entropy (95.11% vs. 95.10% on CIFAR-10 at 200 epochs) while LogitNorm lags behind (94.83%). For calibration, ELogitNorm with boundary-aware scaling f/D(z) achieves 1.8% ECE, far below LogitNorm's 52.3% under the same scaling, demonstrating superior confidence calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LogitNorm (Wei et al., ICML 2022), which normalizes logits by their L2 norm to reduce overconfidence, by diagnosing that its scaling factor τ‖f‖ implicitly constrains features by distance to the origin, inducing both dimensional and origin collapse. The proposed ELogitNorm replaces this origin-based scaling with an average point-to-plane distance to all competing decision boundaries D(z), a geometrically motivated generalization that is hyperparameter-free. The work also positions itself against post-hoc OOD scoring methods (MSP, GEN, KNN, ReAct, SCALE, fDBD) and training-based approaches (CIDER, NPOS, VOS), showing that ELogitNorm acts as a drop-in training objective that broadly enhances existing scorers rather than replacing them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of feature collapse as the mechanism behind LogitNorm's limited compatibility with certain post-hoc scorers provides a concrete diagnostic that practitioners can use to understand when and why normalization-based training degrades OOD performance. ELogitNorm's hyperparameter-free design and broad compatibility with existing scoring functions make it a practical drop-in replacement, though near-OOD improvements remain modest compared to far-OOD gains. Open questions include whether the boundary-distance formulation transfers to transformer-based classifiers or non-linear decision boundaries, and whether the adaptive scaling perspective can be further exploited for uncertainty-aware representations.
