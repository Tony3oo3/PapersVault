## Problem

Kinematic-based HOI reconstruction methods produce visually plausible but physically implausible results, exhibiting interpenetration, floating objects, and jittering because they do not model contact forces, gravity, or collisions. Existing physics-based HOI imitation frameworks assume clean motion-capture data and fail when applied to the extremely noisy monocular reconstructions that arise from occlusions and fast motion. The paper tackles the challenge of recovering physically consistent HOI sequences directly from noisy monocular video reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of a person interacting with a single object as input. A first stage (VisTracker) produces noisy kinematic estimates parameterized as SMPL-H human poses (global orientation, 51 joint rotations, 10-dim shape, root translation) and 6DoF object poses at 30 fps. The second stage refines these into physically plausible 3D human and object pose sequences that satisfy contact, support, and collision constraints, evaluated on the BEHAVE (35 clips) and InterCap (38 clips) test sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline that first reconstructs HOI kinematics with VisTracker, then trains an RL tracking policy (initialized from InterMimic) inside a physics simulator to reproduce the motion under physical laws. Because monocular reconstructions are extremely noisy, the paper introduces Traverse RSI to uniformly visit all frames early in training, followed by an adaptive sampling strategy that progressively increases the probability of initializing rollouts from frames whose rollout lengths indicate reliable contact configurations. A dual propagation mechanism with kinematics update then trains forward and backward policies simultaneously: successful rollout states overwrite the noisy kinematic references, and these refined states serve as both new initialization points and new tracking targets, propagating physically plausible motion in both temporal directions. The policy observes proprioceptive state plus future (or past, for the backward policy) kinematic references and outputs 51 joint targets in exponential-map form for PD control, optimized with a product reward over human tracking, object tracking, contact alignment, proximity, and energy terms.

## Results

On BEHAVE, the method achieves a contact rate of 0.89 (vs. 0.52 for VisTracker), reduces penetration from 6.64 cm to 3.91 cm, cuts object floating from 0.30 to 0.10, and lowers object jerk from 524.9 to 188.5, at the cost of a ~1.4 cm increase in Chamfer Distance. On InterCap, similar gains are observed: contact rate rises from 0.48 to 0.81, object floating drops from 0.49 to 0.06, and object jerk falls from 508.2 to 151.2. Against the physics-based InterMimic baseline (finetuned), the method more than doubles the binary success rate (51.4% vs. 17.1% on BEHAVE; 52.6% vs. 21.1% on InterCap) and frame-level success rate (60.0% vs. 26.7%; 57.1% vs. 29.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the kinematics-based VisTracker [44], the method trades a small Chamfer Distance increase (~1.4 cm on both datasets) for dramatic improvements on all physics-aware metrics: contact rate nearly doubles (0.89 vs. 0.52 on BEHAVE, 0.81 vs. 0.48 on InterCap), penetration is reduced by 41–43%, object floating drops by 67–88%, and object jerk is cut by roughly 64–70%. Against the physics-based InterMimic [48] (finetuned on a single sequence), the method wins on nearly every metric: binary success rate is ~3× higher (51.4% vs. 17.1% on BEHAVE, 52.6% vs. 21.1% on InterCap), frame-level success rate is more than double, and contact distance is consistently lower (e.g., 5.99 vs. 6.40 cm hand-level on BEHAVE). InterMimic's direct inference (without finetuning) is essentially infeasible on noisy VisTracker reconstructions, achieving 0% binary success rate on both datasets. The only metric where the method does not clearly win is CD-H on InterCap (7.04 vs. 6.39 for VisTracker), reflecting the inherent trade-off between physical plausibility and raw 3D pose accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: kinematic HOI reconstruction from video (VisTracker, InterTrack) and physics-based HOI imitation from clean MoCap (InterMimic, PhysHOI, SkillMimic). It departs from prior physics-based HOI trackers by explicitly targeting the noisy, incomplete reconstructions produced by monocular vision methods rather than assuming clean motion capture input. The core mechanism—Traverse RSI for identifying reliable frames, combined with dual forward/backward propagation and iterative kinematic updates—adapts the Reference State Initialization idea from DeepMimic into a self-correcting loop that progressively cleans the reference signal during training, a strategy not present in prior HOI imitation frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work bridges a practical gap between vision-based HOI reconstruction and simulation-ready character animation, with potential downstream applications in robotics learning from internet-scale human video. However, the two-stage pipeline remains a bottleneck: overall success rate is still constrained by the quality of the initial 4D reconstruction, and the method handles only a single object with relatively static contacts. Open questions include whether an end-to-end video-to-dynamics formulation could eliminate the kinematic intermediate, and whether the adaptive sampling and dual propagation ideas generalize to multi-object, multi-human, or scene-aware interactions.
