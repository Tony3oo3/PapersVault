## Problem

Existing continual VQA benchmarks suffer from two structural flaws that undermine fair evaluation: shared answer vocabularies across tasks allow models to exploit answer memorization rather than genuine visual-semantic reasoning, inflating apparent retention, and static answer priors within each task make train and test distributions nearly identical, masking robustness failures under distribution shift. This paper identifies these biases and proposes both a corrected benchmark (UCo-VQA) and a method (MaDQ) that addresses the resulting challenges.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a free-form natural language question, and generates a text answer in a generative (autoregressive) framework rather than selecting from a fixed candidate set. Answers are drawn from task-specific token-level disjoint vocabularies: binary responses are encoded as three-digit octal codes (e.g., 000/001 for task 1, 002/003 for task 2), and open-ended answers undergo dynamic prefix transformations (e.g., 'it's [answer]' → 'that's [answer]') to eliminate lexical overlap across tasks. Under the Proposed Splits (PS) setting, the answer distribution in the test set deliberately differs from the training set within each task, creating controlled intra-task distribution shifts.

## Method

MaDQ (Matching and Distillation with Question replay) builds on a BLIP backbone with a Dual-LoRA architecture: a trainable working adapter (LoRA-w) adapts to the current task, while a momentum adapter (LoRA-m) updated via exponential moving average serves as a stable teacher for distillation. The training objective combines four terms: standard cross-entropy for the current task, KL-based answer prediction distillation on pseudo-samples formed by pairing current-task images with replayed questions from a small buffer (400 questions per task, no images stored), a binary image-question matching loss that explicitly enforces cross-modal grounding, and a matching-consistency distillation term that preserves both positive associations and negative rejection boundaries across tasks. The key design choice is question-only replay, which avoids storing full image-question-answer triplets, reducing per-task memory from ~67 MB to 0.01 MB while still providing sufficient cues for knowledge retention through the dual-level distillation mechanism.

## Results

On the proposed UCo-VQA benchmark, MaDQ achieves the best performance across all four settings. Under the more challenging Proposed Splits (PS) with intra-task distribution shifts, MaDQ attains FAA=36.15, CAA=37.82, FFM=4.54 on VQA v3 and FAA=40.72, CAA=45.18, FFM=9.37 on GQA v2, surpassing the strongest replay-based baselines (ER, CLS-ER) by 4.18% and 2.21% in CAA respectively. Under Standard Splits (SS), MaDQ reaches FAA=53.21 (VQA v3) and FAA=43.51 (GQA v2) with the lowest forgetting (FFM=16.79 and 6.96). Notably, MaDQ stores only 0.01 MB per task (questions only) versus 67.53 MB for full-triplet replay methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaDQ outperforms all nine baselines across every setting. Against the strongest replay-based competitors, MaDQ beats ER by 4.18% CAA (37.82 vs. 33.64) and 4.73% FFM (4.54 vs. 9.27) on VQA v3 (PS), and by 2.21% CAA (45.18 vs. 42.97) and 2.50% FFM (9.37 vs. 11.87) on GQA v2 (PS) over CLS-ER. Parameter-efficient methods without replay (SFT/LoRA, Layered-LoRA, MoE-Adapters) suffer severe forgetting (FFM > 35 on VQA v3 PS) and achieve near-random accuracy, while regularization-based EWC and LwF also underperform substantially (FAA < 12 on VQA v3 PS). ZAF and GAB, which incorporate external data or VLM-generated samples, improve over SFT but remain 20+ points below MaDQ in CAA under PS, with GAB particularly brittle to distributional drift. A key finding is that MaDQ achieves these gains while storing only question text (0.01 MB/task) rather than full image-question-answer triplets (67.53 MB for ER), making it both more accurate and 6,700× more memory-efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a methodological correction to VQACL [61], the first continual VQA benchmark, by identifying that shared answer vocabularies across tasks create spurious anti-forgetting signals. It repurposes the distribution-shift evaluation ideas from VQA-CP and GQA-OOD (designed for static robustness testing) into a continual learning context, where such shifts compound across tasks. On the method side, MaDQ combines question-only replay (echoing Marouf et al. [42]), LwF-style distillation, and BLIP-style image-question matching into a unified Dual-LoRA framework, but the key departure is treating robustness and forgetting as coupled objectives rather than separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a large fraction of reported anti-forgetting performance in prior continual VQA work is attributable to answer vocabulary overlap (Spearman r = 0.73) is a significant methodological correction that should prompt re-evaluation of existing results. The UCo-VQA benchmark, with its token-level disjoint answer spaces and intra-task distribution shifts, provides a more honest testbed that the field can adopt. Open questions include whether the robustness-forgetting coupling generalizes beyond VQA to other multimodal CL tasks, whether the octal-code and prefix-transformation tricks for enforcing disjointness are sufficiently natural, and whether question-only replay can be further reduced or eliminated for privacy-sensitive deployments.
