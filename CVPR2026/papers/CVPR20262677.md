## Problem

Multi-view 3D geometry networks like π³ produce globally consistent reconstructions but scale quadratically with the number of input frames due to all-to-all global self-attention, making them prohibitively slow for real-time streaming applications. Existing streaming alternatives (CUT3R, TTT3R, Point3R) maintain implicit memory that drifts over long sequences and cannot close loops, while offline methods cannot operate online. The paper asks: can we exploit the full multi-view attention of a pretrained model in an online setting without retraining?

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB frames at 308×308 or 518×518 resolution; for object tracking, SAM 2 segmentation masks are applied to zero out background pixels. A keyframing policy (angular threshold on azimuth/elevation) selects which frames enter the mapping buffer. Outputs include 6-DoF camera pose in a world frame, per-pixel local point maps, and per-point confidence scores; the KV-cache itself serves as the persistent scene representation, growing linearly with the number of keyframes and patch count.

## Method

KV-Tracker decomposes operation into two interleaved, parallelisable processes: mapping runs the full π³ model with bidirectional global self-attention over a set of keyframes and caches the key-value projections from every global attention layer; tracking encodes only the new query frame and performs cross-attention against the frozen cached KV pairs, reducing per-frame complexity from O((NM)²) to O(M²(N+1)). The KV-cache is never mutated during tracking—new keyframes trigger a full recomputation—so the representation is immune to drift and catastrophic forgetting. Keyframe selection uses an angular-threshold criterion comparing the current frame's azimuth and elevation against all existing keyframes, with a rejection policy that prunes low-confidence keyframes and reverts to the prior cache. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only that the backbone contain global self-attention blocks, and achieves ~27 FPS tracking on an RTX 4090.

## Results

On 7-Scenes, KV-Tracker achieves 0.059 m average ATE, outperforming the strongest baseline TTT3R (0.143 m) by 58% and CUT3R (0.205 m) by 71%. On TUM RGB-D, it reaches 0.098 m ATE, nearly matching the sparse-patch odometry system DPVO (0.095 m) while providing dense geometry, and runs at 27 FPS versus 17 FPS for CUT3R/TTT3R and 5 FPS for Point3R. For object tracking on OnePose Low Texture, the high-resolution variant with 2D bounding boxes achieves 94.4% recall at the 5 cm/5° threshold, surpassing OnePose++ (72.1%) while operating at 16 FPS versus 11 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KV-Tracker outperforms all three streaming reconstruction baselines (Point3R, CUT3R, TTT3R) on every camera-tracking and object-tracking benchmark. On 7-Scenes it beats TTT3R by 58% (0.059 m vs 0.143 m ATE) and CUT3R by 71%; on TUM RGB-D the margin over TTT3R narrows to 25% (0.098 m vs 0.132 m), where DPVO (0.095 m) is marginally ahead but lacks dense geometry output. On the Arctic object-tracking dataset, KV-Tracker achieves 0.228 m ATE versus 0.305 m (CUT3R) and 0.303 m (TTT3R), a ~25% improvement. Against the offline object-pose methods, KV-Tracker wins at the 5 cm/5° recall threshold on both OnePose (92.9% vs OnePose++ 87.7%) and OnePose Low Texture (94.4% vs 72.1%), but falls behind OnePose++ at the strictest 1 cm/1° threshold (12.1% vs 16.8%), reflecting the advantage of a complete offline 3D model over online reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lineages: the multi-view 3D reconstruction transformer family (DUSt3R → MASt3R → VGGT → π³) and real-time SLAM/tracking systems (PTAM, ORB-SLAM). It departs from recent streaming approaches (CUT3R, TTT3R, Spann3R) that maintain a recurrent or sliding-window implicit memory updated per frame, instead repurposing the KV-cache mechanism familiar from LLM inference as a static, keyframe-anchored scene representation. The key conceptual move is recognizing that π³'s per-frame independent decoding heads and global self-attention structure allow a single query frame to attend to cached keys/values without retraining, converting the O(N²) global attention cost into O(N) during tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling real-time (27 FPS) tracking with powerful multi-view geometry models that were previously too slow for online use, this work opens a path to deploying learned 3D priors in AR/VR, robotics, and autonomous driving without depth sensors or CAD models. The training-free, model-agnostic nature means the technique could transfer to other architectures with global self-attention (VGGT, MapAnything) without retraining. However, memory grows linearly with keyframe count, currently limiting the system to small workspaces or single objects; the authors identify cache pruning, compression, and incremental KV computation as necessary next steps toward full-scale SLAM. An open question is how well the KV-cache representation degrades as keyframe count grows beyond ~110 frames, where the quadratic residual cost of the query frame's own attention becomes non-negligible.
