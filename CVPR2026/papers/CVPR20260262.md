## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) video generation methods rely on 2D conditioning signals (optical flow, masks, bounding boxes, point trajectories) that lack true spatial expressiveness, leading to unrealistic deformations and physically implausible interactions. This 2D-only paradigm also prevents the use of synthetic 3D data from simulators, forcing expensive real-world data collection and annotation. The paper asks whether explicit 3D representations can be directly incorporated into video diffusion to achieve both geometric fidelity and cross-domain (real/simulated) flexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260262/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real RGB image at 720×480 as the appearance anchor, a sequence of T=49 per-frame 3D point clouds (N×3) encoding hand-object geometry, and an optional 3D tracking sequence. The 3D conditions can be sourced from real egocentric video (via VGGT reconstruction) or from simulation (Blender mesh sequences, ARCTIC, HOT3D), enabling domain-bridged generation. The output is a 49-frame, 720×480 hand-object interaction video that is temporally coherent and spatially consistent with the 3D constraints.

## Method

HVG-3D builds on a frozen CogVideoX-5B-I2V diffusion transformer backbone and attaches a dedicated 3D ControlNet that encodes point clouds (via a point cloud encoder producing T×L×768 latent tokens) and 3D tracking sequences, projecting them to a common latent dimensionality before concatenation. The ControlNet replicates all pretrained DiT blocks and injects its outputs into the corresponding backbone layers through zero-initialized convolutions, so 3D structural and motion cues are available at every denoising step. Training fine-tunes only the ControlNet's copied DiT blocks (AdamW, lr=1e-4, 20 epochs, effective batch 4 on 8×H20 GPUs) while keeping the backbone frozen, and employs a mask-weighted diffusion loss that up-weights reconstruction error in hand-object regions to suppress background distractions. A hybrid data pipeline recovers masks (SAMURAI), bounding boxes (YOLOv8-X), 3D point clouds (VGGT), and tracking (SpatialTracker) from monocular egocentric video, while at inference the same interface accepts 3D conditions from simulators or pre-captured HOI datasets without retraining.

## Results

On the TASTE-Rob benchmark, HVG-3D achieves the best FID (58.2) and GMSD-T (0.40) among all methods on full-frame evaluation, while matching DaS on C-FID (14.6) and CLIP score (0.96). Within the hand-object masked region—the primary interaction area—HVG-3D attains the best score on every metric, including FID (88.5 vs. 128.0 for DaS), C-FID (13.1 vs. 16.0), ST-SSIM (0.93 vs. 0.88), and GMSD-T (0.15 vs. 0.16). The ablation study confirms that removing the 3D point cloud condition drops PSNR from 24.15 to 18.44 and SSIM from 0.81 to 0.37, underscoring the critical role of explicit 3D conditioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor DaS [17], HVG-3D wins decisively on the hand-object masked region across all nine metrics (e.g., FID 88.5 vs. 128.0, C-FID 13.1 vs. 16.0, ST-SSIM 0.93 vs. 0.88), while on full-frame evaluation DaS retains a slight edge on low-level pixel metrics (L1 7.77 vs. 9.50, PSNR 24.83 vs. 24.15) but HVG-3D surpasses it on FID (58.2 vs. 75.5) and GMSD-T (0.40 vs. 0.44). Compared to the specialized HOI method InterDyn [1], HVG-3D improves FID from 73.3 to 58.2 (full frame) and from 104.5 to 88.5 (masked), with consistent gains in ST-SSIM and C-FID. The general-purpose video generators CogVideoX [76], Wan 2.2 [62], and Kling [32] are substantially outperformed on every metric in both evaluation settings, with FID gaps exceeding 100 points on the masked region. Qualitatively, only HVG-3D reliably executes complex manipulations (folding, vertical motion) without geometric deformation of the hand or object, whereas DaS and InterDyn introduce noticeable shape distortions under such conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HVG-3D builds directly on CogVideoX-5B-I2V as its frozen diffusion backbone and extends the ControlNet paradigm—previously applied to 2D signals like depth maps, normals, and pose visualizations—by feeding raw 3D point cloud sequences and tracking data through a dedicated 3D ControlNet with zero-initialized injection layers. It departs from prior HOI video generation methods (InterDyn, DaS) that condition on 2D masks or projected tracking videos, arguing that such projections lose intrinsic spatial structure and depth relations. The hybrid pipeline, which pairs a single real RGB image with 3D conditions sourced from either real reconstruction (VGGT) or simulation (Blender, ARCTIC, HOT3D), is a practical departure from prior work that typically requires co-registered real video for both appearance and motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to condition on simulator-generated 3D sequences substantially lowers the data-collection barrier for training HOI video generators, which is directly relevant to the growing use of synthetic video for robotic manipulation policy learning. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (TASTE-Rob) at 720×480 resolution and 49-frame clips, and full-frame reconstruction metrics (L1, PSNR) still trail DaS slightly, suggesting the 3D conditioning primarily benefits the interaction region rather than global scene fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more complex multi-object scenes, and closed-loop integration with real-time robotic control, all of which the authors flag as future work.
