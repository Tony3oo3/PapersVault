## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models excel at reproducing known visual concepts but struggle to synthesize truly novel, out-of-distribution ideas. Existing creative generation methods are limited to discrete pairwise concept fusion (BASS, CreTok) or unconditional token optimization without human guidance (ConceptLab), leaving a gap in fine-grained, controllable composition over multiple concepts with arbitrary class proportions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260603/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a class distribution vector over K known concepts (e.g., 0.25 pig, 0.25 dog, 0.5 cat) or a randomly sampled latent vector from a zero-mean distribution, and produces a 768-dimensional creative concept token. This token is embedded into a natural language prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a ⟨t_crt⟩') and fed to a pre-trained diffusion model (Kandinsky 2.1) to synthesize the final image. The generated tokens are style-agnostic and reusable across diverse prompts without retraining, and the 20-dimensional latent space supports sampling from any zero-mean, unit-variance distribution (Gaussian, Laplace, Cauchy) for open-ended exploration.

## Method

DisTok is an encoder-decoder framework where a Distribution Encoder (two-layer MLP) projects class distributions into a 20-dim latent space and a Creative Decoder (two-layer MLP) maps any latent vector—encoded or randomly sampled—into a creative concept token. Training jointly optimizes three objectives: (1) Continuous Concept Fusion, which iteratively samples concept pairs from a growing Concept Pool and fuses them via cosine-similarity losses between adaptive and restrictive prompts with thresholding to prevent dominant-concept overfitting; (2) Class Distribution Estimation, which periodically decodes random latents into tokens, generates images, and queries a VLM (BLIP) to predict class distributions, adding novel concepts to the pool; and (3) Distribution Consistency Enforcement, which aligns the text embedding of a decoded token with the VLM-predicted distribution of its generated image via cosine distance. The Concept Pool grows iteratively, enabling progressively complex multi-concept compositions, and the framework unifies conditional (distribution-guided) and unconditional (random-sampling) exploration within a single architecture, achieving 13× and 40× speedups over BASS and ConceptLab respectively.

## Results

On the class-pair (TP2O) task, DisTok achieves VQAScore 0.840, PickScore 22.33, and ImageReward 1.168, substantially outperforming BASS (0.667 / 21.67 / 0.387) and CreTok (0.695 / 21.97 / 1.018). On the novel class-distribution task, DisTok reaches VQAScore 0.734, demonstrating fine-grained control over multi-concept proportions. GPT-4o creativity evaluation scores DisTok at 9.2–9.9 across all dimensions (integration, alignment, originality, aesthetics, comprehension) on the distribution task, versus 8.0–8.8 for the strongest baseline FLUX. In a 100-participant user study, DisTok wins the majority of votes in every head-to-head comparison, with the largest margin against BASS (396:104).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BASS [17], DisTok wins decisively on all three standard metrics (VQAScore 0.840 vs 0.667, ImageReward 1.168 vs 0.387) and is approximately 13× faster (~3 s vs ~40 s per concept), since BASS requires iterative sampling and candidate filtering while DisTok encodes and decodes in a single pass. Against CreTok [9], DisTok improves VQAScore by 0.145 and ImageReward by 0.15, and produces visually distinct hybrids for different concept pairs where CreTok generates near-identical outputs (e.g., Lion+Snake vs Lion+Shrimp). Against SOTA diffusion models (SD3, SD3.5, Kandinsky, FLUX) on the distribution-conditional task, DisTok leads on every GPT-4o creativity dimension, with originality at 9.8 vs FLUX's 8.0 and aesthetics at 9.9 vs 8.8. Against ConceptLab [30] on unconditional exploration, DisTok wins the user study 308:192 and is ~40× faster (~3 s vs ~120 s), while also offering structured latent-space sampling that avoids ConceptLab's sparse, repetitive token clusters. Overall, DisTok is the only method evaluated that handles both class-pair and class-distribution conditions within a single framework, and it outperforms all baselines in every head-to-head user-study comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic exploration line for creative T2I generation (BASS, CreTok, ConceptLab) by replacing discrete pairwise fusion or unconditional token optimization with conditioning on continuous class probability distributions, inspired by the soft outputs classifiers produce on out-of-distribution inputs. It builds most directly on the authors' prior CreTok work for the prompt-level fusion strategy, but departs fundamentally by encoding arbitrary multi-class distributions into a shared latent space via a Distribution Encoder, thereby unifying the conditional (pair/distribution-guided) and unconditional (random sampling) regimes under a single encoder-decoder framework. The approach also draws on VLM-based supervision (BLIP) to ground generated tokens in visual semantics, going beyond the purely text-embedding-based alignment used in prior token-level methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Distribution-Conditional Generation paradigm offers a principled, interpretable interface for creative synthesis that scales beyond the pairwise combinations dominating prior work, and the 13×/40× inference speedups over BASS/ConceptLab make fine-grained concept composition practically deployable. However, all experiments are confined to 60 animal concepts from CangJie with a single backbone (Kandinsky 2.1), leaving open whether the distribution-conditional formulation generalizes to larger, more diverse concept spaces or to non-animal domains. The reliance on a VLM for distribution estimation also introduces a dependency on the VLM's own class coverage and calibration, which could limit fidelity for truly novel or cross-domain compositions.
