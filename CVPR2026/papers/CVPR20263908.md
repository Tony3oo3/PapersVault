## Problem

Single-image relighting is fundamentally ill-posed because geometry and materials are unobserved, yet existing diffusion-based methods either depend on dense G-buffer/intrinsic supervision (making them fragile and expensive to scale) or operate purely in latent space without physical grounding (yielding unreliable fine-grained control over direction, intensity, and color). The paper asks whether a minimal set of physically meaningful cues—rather than full intrinsic reconstruction—can provide sufficient structure for precise, controllable relighting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source RGB image and a relative lighting specification Δℓ encoding continuous changes in light direction (yaw/pitch), intensity (lux scaling), and correlated color temperature (K shift), and produces a relit RGB image of the same object or scene under the target illumination. No multi-view input, 3D model, or explicit material map is required at inference time; the lighting parameters can be specified as arbitrary continuous values (e.g., '60° down', 'dimmed by 200 lux', 'shifted to 1800K'), enabling smooth and precise control rather than discrete presets.

## Method

LightCtrl fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion backbone on the newly constructed ScaLight dataset (300K+ objects, 1M+ renders with full camera-light metadata) and injects three lightweight physical cues into the denoiser. A few-shot latent proxy encoder predicts compact PBR maps (albedo, normals, roughness, metallicity) from only a small subset of PBR-supervised images, then spatially pools them into a single 768-dim conditioning token that constrains the denoising trajectory with material-geometry priors. A lighting-aware mask predictor, conditioned on the source image and Δℓ, produces a spatial weight map that modulates the diffusion loss to concentrate edits on illumination-sensitive pixels while preserving appearance-invariant regions. Finally, a DPO-style post-training stage refines the proxy encoder using a physics-based reward (albedo/roughness L1, normal angular deviation, metallic BCE) to stabilize intrinsic cues under sparse supervision without modifying the diffusion backbone itself.

## Results

On the ScaLight held-out set (1.5K unseen objects), LightCtrl achieves PSNR of 30.2 / 25.6 / 27.1 dB and RMSE of 0.053 / 0.074 / 0.083 for temperature, position, and energy shifts respectively, outperforming the closest competitor LBM (27.8 / 23.1 / 25.3 dB PSNR) by up to +2.4 dB. Against intrinsic-based baselines (x2rgb, IC-Light) the margin is far larger, exceeding +20 dB PSNR. In a 35-participant user study, LightCtrl earns the highest preference rate at 81.45% (object-level) and 55.73% (scene-level), versus 11.45% and 22.07% for the next-best methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightCtrl outperforms all five baselines on RMSE and PSNR across all three lighting variations (temperature, position, energy). The closest competitor, LBM [5], achieves 27.8 dB PSNR on temperature shifts versus LightCtrl's 30.2 dB (+2.4 dB), but LightCtrl's SSIM on position shifts (0.929) is marginally below LBM's (0.936)—the authors attribute this to a perception-distortion trade-off where LightCtrl synthesizes sharper, physically correct shadows that reduce structural similarity. Against intrinsic/G-buffer baselines (x2rgb [40], IC-Light [41]), the gap is overwhelming: LightCtrl exceeds them by 20+ dB PSNR, indicating that dense G-buffer supervision does not translate to better photometric accuracy. LumiNet [35], the strongest image-conditioned baseline, trails LightCtrl by roughly 14 dB PSNR on temperature and 10 dB on position. DreamLight [19] sits between the text-prompt and image-conditioned groups but remains far behind LightCtrl. In the user preference study, LightCtrl dominates with 81.45% (object) and 55.73% (scene) preference, compared to 11.45% for IC-Light and 22.07% for NanoBanana at the scene level.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightCtrl positions itself as a middle ground between two dominant paradigms in relighting: physics/intrinsic-guided pipelines (Neural LightRig, RGB-X, Neural Gaffer) that require dense G-buffer supervision, and purely latent diffusion approaches (IC-Light, LBM, LumiNet) that lack physical grounding. It builds on Stable Diffusion as the generative backbone and repurposes DPO (originally for LLM alignment) to refine a lightweight PBR proxy encoder under sparse supervision. The key conceptual departure is the claim that full intrinsic decomposition is redundant—sparse, spatially targeted material-geometry cues plus a lighting-aware mask suffice to constrain a diffusion model for precise illumination control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ScaLight dataset (300K+ objects, 1M+ rendered images with complete camera-light metadata) is a substantial resource that addresses the data bottleneck in controllable relighting research, and the 'sparse physical cues suffice' framing offers a practical recipe for adding physical grounding to diffusion models without expensive G-buffer annotation. However, the paper acknowledges that long-range cast-shadow recasting and high-frequency detail preservation under strong illumination contrast remain open challenges, and generalization to cluttered real scenes is still limited. Whether the DPO-refined proxy approach transfers to other physically-grounded generation tasks (e.g., material editing, shadow synthesis) is an interesting open question.
