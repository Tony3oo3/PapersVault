## Problem

Recent RGB-T trackers achieve high accuracy through deep dual-branch transformers with heavy cross-modal fusion, but the resulting FLOPs and parameter counts make real-time inference infeasible on edge devices such as UAVs and mobile robots. The paper identifies two specific inefficiencies: network depth is treated as a fixed hyperparameter even though score maps saturate at intermediate layers, and cross-modal interaction relies on expensive global cross-attention or adapter stacks that add substantial overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes paired RGB and thermal infrared images: two 128×128 template frames (one stable, one adaptively updated) and one 256×256 search frame per modality, all patchified into token sequences. The output is a target bounding box regressed from a score map plus a confidence score. Unusually, the effective network depth is not fixed: the AEE mechanism may terminate inference at any of several intermediate layers depending on a per-frame confidence threshold, so the computational cost varies dynamically across frames of differing difficulty.

## Method

ADTrack combines two mechanisms on a frozen ViT-T dual-stream backbone. First, Adaptive Early-Exit (AEE) attaches lightweight anytime prediction heads at multiple depths and trains them with a self-calibration loss that aligns each head's score map to the final output, enforces monotonic confidence growth, and penalizes misalignment around a halting threshold τ; at inference, the tracker stops at the first layer whose max/sum confidence exceeds τ. Second, Holistic-Token-Guided Interaction (HTGI) extracts K compact holistic tokens from one modality's template via a Token Router (a small MLP producing per-patch weights applied element-wise then globally summed), concatenates them with the other modality's search tokens, and processes the combined sequence through a single Transformer block before discarding the injected tokens; this bidirectional exchange adds only 37.3K×3 parameters and is inserted at layers 3, 6, and 9 to fuse low-level spatial cues with high-level semantics without dedicated cross-attention layers.

## Results

On LasHeR, ADTrack achieves 70.2% Precision, 66.3% Normalized Precision, and 56.3% Success Rate, matching BAT and TATrack on PR while trailing GMMT by only 0.5%. On RGBT234, it reaches 88.6% MPR and 66.1% MSR, surpassing CAFormer by 0.3% in MPR. The key advantage is speed: ADTrack runs at 148.3 FPS on GPU, 50.2 FPS on CPU, and 28.7 FPS on a Jetson Orin NX edge device, making it the only RGB-T tracker achieving real-time inference across all three platforms. On GTOT and RGBT210, ADTrack leads all baselines by 1.2% PR and 0.7% SR (GTOT) and 2.2% MPR and 2.0% MSR (RGBT210).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-lightweight ViT-B trackers, ADTrack (ViT-T) matches BAT and TATrack at 70.2% PR on LasHeR and trails GMMT by only 0.5%, while running 2.3× faster than CAFormer (148.3 vs 63.5 FPS GPU) and 19× faster than XTrack (148.3 vs 7.8 FPS). On RGBT234, ADTrack edges out CAFormer by 0.3% MPR (88.6 vs 88.3) and surpasses BAT by 1.8% MPR. Among lightweight trackers, ADTrack dominates: it beats SUTrack-Tiny by 3.5% PR and 2.4% SR on LasHeR, and by 2.7% MPR and 2.3% MSR on RGBT234, while also running 1.4× faster on GPU. On GTOT, ADTrack leads CAFormer by 1.2% PR and 0.7% SR; on RGBT210, it leads by 2.2% MPR and 2.0% MSR. On VTUAV-ST, ADTrack surpasses AINet by 0.5% MPR and 0.9% MSR, and on VTUAV-LT it beats USTrack by 1.2% MPR and 0.7% MSR.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADTrack carves new ground at the intersection of two threads in RGB-T tracking: lightweight dual-stream ViT architectures (following the width-narrowing trend of SUTrack-Tiny, TBSI-Tiny, LightFC-T) and adaptive computation. It departs from the dominant cross-modal fusion paradigm of heavy cross-attention or adapter stacks (BAT, XTrack, CAFormer) by replacing them with a 37.3K-parameter holistic-token routing module, and from the fixed-depth inference assumed by all prior trackers by introducing confidence-calibrated anytime heads that treat network depth as a per-frame compute budget. The early-exit idea itself is not new in deep learning, but its application here is self-supervised (no teacher distillation, unlike CMD) and grounded in the empirical observation that RGB-T score maps saturate well before the final layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: ADTrack is the only RGB-T tracker demonstrated to run in real time simultaneously on GPU (148 FPS), CPU (50 FPS), and an edge device (29 FPS), opening deployment on UAVs and portable surveillance systems that were previously infeasible. The AEE mechanism is a general principle—treating depth as an allocatable budget with learned convergence detection—that could transfer to other tracking or detection tasks. Open questions include whether the early-exit strategy degrades gracefully when both modalities are simultaneously degraded (e.g., heavy fog affecting TIR), whether holistic-token routing scales beyond two modalities, and whether the confidence threshold requires per-scene recalibration in truly open-world deployment.
