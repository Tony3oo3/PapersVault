## Problem

Mask-based segmentation models such as Mask2Former and InternImage generate high-quality masks but exhibit a substantial gap between their actual classification accuracy and the upper bound achievable with perfect mask labeling. This classification bottleneck, particularly under ambiguous boundaries and imbalanced class distributions, limits overall segmentation performance across semantic, instance, and panoptic tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262460/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

ViT-P receives an RGB image (cropped to 518×518 for COCO/ADE20K or 518×1036 for Cityscapes) along with N point coordinates extracted as the highest-value pixel from each mask proposal generated by a frozen proposal network. The N points are encoded as [x, y, 0, 0] tuples to maintain a unified input format with the box-annotation pre-training stage. The model outputs per-point class probabilities that are geometrically ensembled with the mask generator's own class scores (α=0.4) to produce final mask labels, with a 'no object' token re-appended for instance and panoptic tasks.

## Method

ViT-P is a two-stage framework that decouples mask generation from classification: a frozen proposal generator (OneFormer or InternImage) produces class-agnostic binary masks, and ViT-P classifies each mask by attending to its highest-value point. Architecturally, ViT-P extends a vanilla Vision Transformer by concatenating N point embeddings (produced by a linear layer from normalized coordinates) with standard patch embeddings before the transformer encoder, reusing the pre-trained CLS positional embedding for all point tokens. Training follows a two-phase strategy: pre-training on bounding-box annotations (Objects365) where box coordinates serve as the point input, followed by fine-tuning on point-based inputs derived from fine or coarse segmentation annotations. At inference, the point classifier's output is fused with the mask generator's class predictions via a geometric ensemble (C_fuse = C_m^(1−α) · C_p^α), and the entire ViT-P module acts as a pre-training-free adapter that can be plugged onto any pre-trained vision transformer backbone without modifying its architecture.

## Results

On ADE20K, ViT-P achieves 54.0 PQ, 40.7 AP, and 63.6 mIoU (multi-scale) with Mask2Former†+ViT-P† on InternImage-H, setting new state-of-the-art across all three segmentation tasks. On Cityscapes, the method reaches 87.4 mIoU (multi-scale) for semantic segmentation, surpassing the previous best of 87.0. On COCO-Stuff-164K, ViT-P attains 53.7 mIoU (multi-scale), outperforming Mask2Former with InternImage-H (52.8 mIoU). The geometric ensemble of point and mask classifier scores (α=0.4) is used at inference to combine predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ADE20K, ViT-P with OneFormer† (DiNAT-L) improves over OneFormer† by +0.6 PQ, +0.5 AP, and +1.3 mIoU, and surpasses OpenSeeD†† by +0.3 PQ while matching its AP. With the larger InternImage-H backbone, Mask2Former†+ViT-P† achieves 63.6 mIoU, beating Mask2Former† alone (62.9) by +0.7 and Mask2Former†+ViT-Adapter (BEiT-3, 62.8) by +0.8. On Cityscapes, ViT-P with InternImage-H reaches 87.4 mIoU, outperforming Mask2Former† (InternImage-H) at 87.0 by +0.4, and OneFormer† (ConvNeXt-L) at 85.2 by +2.2. On COCO-Stuff-164K, ViT-P with InternImage-H achieves 53.7 mIoU, exceeding Mask2Former (InternImage-H) at 52.8 by +0.9. The method consistently wins or ties across all three tasks (panoptic, instance, semantic) on all four benchmarks, with the largest gains appearing in semantic segmentation where classification errors are most impactful.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViT-P extends the mask-based segmentation paradigm established by MaskFormer and Mask2Former, where segmentation is formulated as mask classification, by explicitly decoupling mask generation from mask classification into two independent stages. The point-based classification mechanism draws on the point-supervision literature (Bearman et al.) and the observation that the highest-value point in a generated mask sits near its center, making it a reliable classification anchor. The geometric ensemble for fusing mask and point classifier scores follows prior open-vocabulary segmentation work, while the use of coarse and bounding-box annotations for training is inspired by MaskDINO and OpenSeeD. Architecturally, ViT-P is a lightweight adapter on top of vanilla ViT that reuses pre-trained weights (e.g., DINOv2) without modifying the backbone, positioning it as a drop-in classifier compatible with any existing mask proposal generator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously under-appreciated bottleneck in state-of-the-art mask-based segmentation: classification accuracy lags far behind mask quality, as evidenced by the large gap between actual performance and the upper bound with perfect classification. The modular two-stage design is practically valuable because any frozen mask generator can be paired with ViT-P, and any pre-trained ViT backbone can serve as the classifier, reducing the need for task-specific retraining. The demonstration that coarse and box annotations nearly match fine annotations for the classification stage offers a meaningful reduction in annotation cost. However, the actual performance gains are modest (0.4–1.3% mIoU), the approach adds inference latency and model complexity, and the gap to the upper bound remains substantial, leaving room for further improvement in both classification and mask quality.
