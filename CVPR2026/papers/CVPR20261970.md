## Problem

Existing dehazing methods suffer from two intertwined limitations: (1) they treat perceptual domains (spatial, frequency, non-local, etc.) as independent statistical spaces, failing to establish a physics-grounded cross-domain alignment that distinguishes true scattering features from domain-specific noise, and (2) they do not explicitly address haze-induced chromatic entanglement in HSV space, where haze destroys the native channel independence and causes persistent color casts, especially under colored haze conditions like sandstorms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hazy RGB image and outputs a dehazed RGB image. Training uses unpaired hazy and clean images (no pixel-wise correspondence), with 2,500 hazy samples from RTTS/URHI and 1,800 clean samples from SOTS. Internally, the network converts HSV channels into a stable Cartesian representation (D_x, D_y, D_z) to handle the circular nature of hue, and the final model is lightweight at 2.38M parameters with 0.062s inference time on an RTX 3080Ti.

## Method

CIM-D integrates two synergistic components. First, a Cross-Domain Invariant Manifold (CIM) maps features from five domain-specific encoders (spatial, frequency, non-local, diffusion, compressed-sensing) into a unified latent space via a domain translation network, where a consensus density (geometric mean of per-domain Gaussian densities) identifies cross-domain physical agreement; contrastive losses (scattering semantics alignment and manifold traversal dehazing) optimize this manifold so that dehazing corresponds to traversal toward high-density clear prototypes. Second, a Physics-Guided HSV Decomposition Network (U-Net with multi-scale convolution and residual decoupling blocks) explicitly reverses haze-induced channel coupling by computing Sobel-gradient cosine similarities between HSV channels and applying adaptive residual corrections weighted by gradient magnitudes. The total loss further enforces channel-decoupling regularization (Gaussian-constrained mutual information), spectrally-balanced scattering constraints derived from the atmospheric scattering model, and a hue-consistency penalty, creating mutual reinforcement between manifold consensus and chromatic separation.

## Results

On the synthetic Raw2ah dataset, CIM-D achieves the best PSNR (17.89 dB) and SSIM (0.585), outperforming the next-best C2P (17.26 dB / 0.553). On SOTS, CIM-D ranks second behind C2P with 25.51 dB PSNR and 0.935 SSIM. On the real-world RTTS dataset, CIM-D attains the best FADE (0.795) and NIQE (3.844) scores, with the second-best BRISQUE (16.32). The method also achieves the fastest inference time (0.062 s) and a compact parameter count of 2.38 M.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIM-D achieves the best full-reference scores on Raw2ah (PSNR 17.89, SSIM 0.585), beating the strongest baseline C2P by 0.63 dB and 0.032 SSIM. On SOTS, C2P retains the lead (27.22 dB / 0.955 SSIM) while CIM-D is a clear second (25.51 dB / 0.935), ahead of FSDGN (25.09 / 0.929) and UCL (23.33 / 0.910). On the real-world RTTS benchmark, CIM-D wins on FADE (0.795 vs. UCL's 0.824) and NIQE (3.844 vs. PTTD's 3.887), but trails PTTD on BRISQUE (16.32 vs. 15.25). In terms of efficiency, CIM-D is the fastest method (0.062 s per image) with only 2.38 M parameters, substantially lighter than KA-Net (55.25 M), IPC (18.78 M), and UCL (11.38 M), making it well-suited for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from multi-domain dehazing methods (FSDGN, frequency-compensated diffusion) that rely on empirically designed feature transfers or domain-specific heads, instead positing a physics-grounded cross-domain invariant manifold where five perceptual domains (spatial, frequency, non-local, diffusion, compressed-sensing) converge through consensus-density-driven contrastive learning. It also extends perceptual color-space dehazing (HSV/YCbCr priors as supplementary guidance) by making HSV the primary representation space and explicitly reversing haze-induced channel coupling via gradient-based decoupling blocks. The manifold-learning concept is borrowed from multilingual NLP (the semantic hub hypothesis), repurposed here to argue that scattering semantics are domain-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CIM-D addresses two intertwined failure modes—feature ambiguity and chromatic distortion—within a single physically consistent framework, and its 0.062 s inference with 2.38 M parameters makes it practical for real-time deployment. The ablation reveals that the spectrally-balanced scattering constraint (L_asm) is the single most critical loss term (PSNR drops from 25.51 to 20.57 without it), suggesting that explicit physics constraints may matter more than learned manifold geometry for photometric fidelity. Open questions include whether the cross-domain manifold generalizes beyond dehazing to other atmospheric degradations (rain, blur), whether the HSV disentanglement transfers to other color spaces, and whether the noted halo artifacts near depth edges can be resolved without sacrificing the physical constraints.
