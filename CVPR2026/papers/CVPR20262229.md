## Problem

Existing language-based tracking methods still require a visual template or initial bounding box, limiting their use in open-world and interactive scenarios where the user may not provide one. Even when language is used, correlation-alone localization is brittle under large search regions due to spatial uncertainty and ambiguous vision-language saliency in the presence of distractors. This paper addresses the gap by formulating a fully template-free tracking paradigm that relies solely on natural language and introduces a principled mechanism to adapt the search region online.

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a natural-language description (e.g., 'a woman wearing blue clothes with long hair') and an RGB video frame as input, with no bounding box or visual exemplar required. The text is tokenized into 77 tokens by a frozen CLIP encoder, and the image is patchified into 196 tokens (14×14 grid) by a HiViT encoder. At each grid location the network outputs a matched triple—classification score, spatial offset, and box size—yielding 196 candidate boxes from which the final bounding box is selected via the MVLM confidence gating rule.

## Method

The architecture concatenates visual and language tokens into a unified sequence processed by a stack of transformer encoder layers, followed by three prediction heads (classification, offset, size) on a shared spatial grid. The key training signal is a Correlation Margin (CM) loss that maximizes the gap between the target region's vision-language alignment and the hardest background negatives, directly suppressing mis-localization. At inference, the proposed MVLM confidence fuses a standardized correlation margin (top-1 vs. top-2 correlation score), a classification margin, and an exponentially weighted moving average of past confidences into a single scalar in [-1,1]. This scalar gates the search strategy: high confidence restricts the next frame's search to a compact ROI around the current estimate, while low confidence triggers global re-localization over the full image. Theoretically, the paper derives exponential bounds showing that the mis-localization probability decays with the squared correlation margin, and decomposes total tracking failure into ROI-exclusion and within-ROI mis-localization terms, both controlled by the MVLM threshold.

## Results

Under the template-free (tracking-by-language) setting, MVLM achieves PRE/AUC of 60.9/57.8 on TNL2K, 65.5/60.7 on LaSOT, 84.3/60.7 on OTB99, and 55.5/63.5 on MGIT, setting new state-of-the-art PRE on all four benchmarks. In the template-based (tracking-by-bbox-and-language) setting, MVLM reaches 73.0/66.9 on TNL2K and 66.3/71.7 on MGIT, again achieving the best PRE. The paper reports PRE improvements of +2.0%, +4.5%, +3.3%, and +5.2% over the second-best tracker on TNL2K, LaSOT, OTB99, and MGIT respectively in the language-only setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the template-free (language-only) setting, MVLM outperforms all baselines on PRE across all four benchmarks: it beats MambaVLT by +2.0 PRE on TNL2K (60.9 vs 58.9), UVLTrack-B by +4.5 PRE on LaSOT (65.5 vs 61.0), QueryNLT by +3.3 PRE on OTB99 (84.3 vs 81.0), and MambaVLT by +5.2 PRE on MGIT (55.5 vs 50.3). On AUC, MVLM leads on LaSOT (60.7 vs 57.2 for UVLTrack-B) but trails MambaVLT on TNL2K (57.8 vs 58.4), QueryNLT on OTB99 (60.7 vs 61.2), and MambaVLT on MGIT (63.5 vs 64.6). In the template-based (bbox+language) setting, MVLM achieves the best PRE on TNL2K (73.0 vs DUTrack's 70.6) and MGIT (66.3 vs DUTrack's 65.4), but is slightly behind SUTrack-B224 on LaSOT AUC (72.7 vs 73.2) and MambaVLT on OTB99 (94.0 vs 94.4 PRE; 70.6 vs 72.2 AUC). Overall, the paper's core claim—that language-only guidance without any visual template is sufficient for competitive tracking—is strongly supported, with the largest margins appearing on MGIT and LaSOT where distractors and appearance changes are most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant template-based tracking paradigm (Siamese networks, MixFormer, OSTrack) by eliminating visual exemplars entirely, relying solely on vision-language correlation as the semantic anchor. It builds on SUTrack's unified prediction heads and training loss structure, and extends prior language-guided trackers (JointNLT, QueryNLT, UVLTrack) that still require an initial bounding box or visual template. The key conceptual departure is the MVLM confidence mechanism, which fuses correlation margins, encoder statistics, and temporal memory to dynamically gate between local ROI search and global re-localization—a design absent from prior language-based trackers that treat the search region as fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing template-free tracking with theoretical bounds (exponential decay of mis-localization with correlation margin, decomposed failure into ROI-exclusion and within-ROI terms), the paper provides a principled framework for understanding when and why language-only tracking succeeds or fails. The target-switching capability—changing the tracked object mid-sequence by simply updating the text description—opens a path toward truly interactive, open-world tracking without manual re-initialization. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences with cumulative drift, handling of multi-object or ambiguous language queries, and whether the sub-Gaussian noise assumption holds under severe occlusion or domain shift.
