## Problem

Existing LiDAR-camera BEV fusion methods suffer from spatial misalignment between modalities caused by calibration errors and rolling shutter, which corrupts depth supervision and degrades cross-modal feature aggregation. Prior approaches either avoid projection entirely (sacrificing context), fuse already-misplaced features with robust modules, or apply global smoothing that incorrectly modifies already-correct depth in stable regions. The paper identifies that misalignment is not random but concentrated at object-background boundaries with sharp depth discontinuities, and no prior method selectively corrects these critical regions while preserving aligned areas.

## Input / Output

The method consumes N surround-view camera images (resized to 448×800), a 360-degree LiDAR point cloud, and 2D bounding boxes from a YOLOv9 detector that serve as geometric priors for alignment. It produces 3D bounding boxes (10 classes on nuScenes, 23 on Argoverse 2) and, as an intermediate representation, a discrete probability distribution over predefined depth bins (1–60 m, 0.5 m resolution) for each pixel, which drives the Lift-Splat-Shoot projection into a unified BEV feature map fused with LiDAR BEV features.

## Method

The framework introduces three synergistic modules built on the BEVFusion baseline. (1) PGDC uses 2D detection boxes to isolate object regions, then within each box applies a KDTree-based neighbor selection (2 nearest + 2 farthest of 10 neighbors) to construct a 5-channel depth feature processed by a lightweight conv to produce aligned sparse depth, while simultaneously amplifying image features in-box via class-specific scaling and an SE block. (2) DAGF computes a discrepancy map between raw and aligned depth, masks unreliable points exceeding a 10% threshold, then performs 20×20 block-based densification extracting per-block average depth and maximum local gradient to form a 2-channel dense structural representation. (3) SGDM fuses the enhanced image features and DAGF's dense geometric map through parallel convolutions, a gated attention mechanism producing a spatial confidence map, and a residual connection preserving the original camera stream, ultimately predicting a per-pixel depth distribution. Training uses a composite loss combining Focal Loss on the dense depth map, a gradient-weighted Edge-Critical Loss that amplifies penalties at depth discontinuities, plus standard classification and box regression losses.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, the proposed method achieves a state-of-the-art mAP of 71.5% and NDS of 73.6%, surpassing the previous best (GraphBEV) by 1.4 mAP and 0.7 NDS. On the Argoverse 2 validation set, it reaches a competitive mAP of 41.7%, outperforming all compared methods. The full pipeline adds only 15 ms of latency over the BEVFusion-PKU baseline while delivering these gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both nuScenes and Argoverse 2. On nuScenes, it leads the closest competitor GraphBEV [31] by 1.4 mAP (71.5 vs. 70.1) and 0.7 NDS (73.6 vs. 72.9), and surpasses the BEVFusion-PKU [15] baseline (its architectural foundation) by 3.6 mAP and 2.6 NDS. Gains are particularly pronounced on smaller and more challenging classes: Truck improves from 64.2 (GraphBEV) to 68.5, and Pedestrian from 88.6 to 90.1. On Argoverse 2, the method achieves 41.7 mAP, edging out GraphBEV (41.1) and M3Net (40.9), with consistent per-class improvements across all 22 categories. Notably, the method also surpasses the LiDAR-only VoxelNeXt [6] (30.7 mAP) by over 11 points on Argoverse 2, confirming the value of the camera-LiDAR fusion strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BEVFusion framework and the Lift-Splat-Shoot view transformation paradigm, extending them with a targeted pre-fusion alignment strategy. It departs from prior approaches that either avoid projection errors entirely (TransFusion's attention-based querying), design robust fusion modules that tolerate misalignment (MetaBEV, RobBEV), or apply global geometric corrections that over-smooth stable regions (GraphBEV). The key conceptual departure is the observation that projection errors are spatially predictable—concentrated at object-background boundaries—and can be addressed with 2D detection priors before fusion, rather than corrected reactively after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in multi-sensor autonomous driving: calibration drift and rolling-shutter distortion that degrade LiDAR-camera fusion. The insight that misalignment is boundary-concentrated and 2D-prior-addressable offers a principled, low-cost correction (only +15 ms latency) that could be integrated into existing BEV pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to sensor configurations with larger calibration errors (e.g., after a collision), whether learned 2D priors can replace the explicit YOLOv9 dependency, and whether the block-based densification in DAGF scales gracefully to higher-resolution inputs.
