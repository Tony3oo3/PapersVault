## Problem

Existing micro-expression recognition methods rely on onset/apex frame pairs to capture discriminative motion, but apex annotation is labor-intensive and the resulting limited number of pairs increases overfitting risk on small ME datasets. This paper asks whether effective MER can be achieved without any apex annotations by exploiting the spatial consistency and intensity variation of motion across multiple intra-sequence frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260152/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a micro-expression video sequence and samples N=16 consecutive frames from the middle portion of the sequence. For each sampled frame, TV-L1 optical flow is computed relative to the onset frame, producing 3-channel optical flow maps (horizontal flow, vertical flow, and optical strain) that form an instance set. At inference, only the middle instance from this set is fed to the online network to produce a single emotion class label.

## Method

Ra-ICL represents each ME sequence as a set of multiple onset/near-median optical flow instances (MIR) instead of a single onset/apex pair, exploiting spatial stability and intensity variation across frames. The Instance Region Consistency (IRC) module uses a BYOL-style Siamese network where random instance pairs are processed by online and target branches; flip semantic consistency is enforced between their Class Activation Mapping heatmaps to identify invariant activation regions. The Multi-Region Discovery (MRD) module employs a Transformer decoder with N learnable facial queries to discover subtle local regions in a self-supervised manner, using cross-entropy pixel-assignment alignment and cosine similarity consistency between the two branches. The overall objective jointly optimizes classification loss, attention consistency loss, and MRD alignment/consistency losses with equal weighting (λ=0.5 each).

## Results

Under the standard CDE protocol (442 samples, 68 subjects, LOSO), Ra-ICL achieves 88.05% UF1 and 89.11% UAR on the composite dataset, surpassing the second-best method (HTNet, 86.03% UF1 / 84.75% UAR) by 2.02% and 2.23% respectively. The margin is largest on SAMM, where Ra-ICL reaches 86.68% UF1 / 88.85% UAR versus HTNet's 81.31% / 81.24% (a 5.37% UF1 gap). On CAS(ME)³, Ra-ICL leads all settings: 75.85% UF1 (3-class), 61.03% UF1 (4-class), and 44.32% UF1 (7-class), outperforming the next-best method (Lite-Point-GCN) by 7.66%, 13.39%, and 8.68% UF1 respectively. Notably, Ra-ICL achieves these results without any apex frame annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ra-ICL outperforms all 12 baselines on the composite CDE dataset in both UF1 and UAR. The closest competitor is HTNet (86.03% UF1), which itself relies on apex-annotated onset/apex pairs; Ra-ICL gains 2.02% UF1 without any apex annotation. On SAMM, the gap widens to 5.37% UF1 over HTNet, suggesting the multi-instance representation is particularly beneficial for datasets with more diverse motion patterns. On CAS(ME)³, Ra-ICL beats the strongest baseline Lite-Point-GCN by 7.66% UF1 (3-class), 13.39% (4-class), and 8.68% (7-class), with the largest margin in the harder 4-class setting. Traditional hand-crafted methods (LBP-TOP, Bi-WOOF) and early deep methods (STSTNet, RCN) trail by 10–30% UF1, confirming the advantage of the learned multi-instance paradigm. The paper also notes that MFDAN, while competitive on CASME II (91.34% UF1), drops to 68.15% on SMIC-HS, whereas Ra-ICL maintains more balanced performance across all four sub-datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the BYOL Siamese architecture and flip-semantic attention consistency (from image classification) into a motion-instance consistency framework for micro-expression recognition, while drawing on self-supervised facial query mechanisms from recent facial representation learning work. Its key conceptual departure from the dominant MER paradigm is replacing the single onset/apex optical-flow pair with a set of multiple onset/near-median frame pairs (Multiple Instances Representation), thereby eliminating the need for labor-intensive apex annotations. The IRC and MRD modules jointly exploit spatial stability and intensity variation across these instances, bridging supervised classification with self-supervised region discovery in a way not previously attempted in MER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the apex annotation requirement, this work addresses a major practical bottleneck in MER data collection and could substantially lower the barrier to building larger, more diverse micro-expression datasets. The finding that weaker, non-apex motion instances carry sufficient discriminative information—validated across four datasets and multiple sampling strategies—challenges the field's long-standing assumption that the apex frame is the essential motion carrier. Open questions include whether the middle-sampling heuristic generalizes to very short or very long sequences, whether the approach transfers to other subtle-motion tasks (e.g., micro-gestures), and whether the ResNet-18 backbone limits scalability to higher-resolution or longer-duration inputs.
