## Problem

State-of-the-art fake image detectors overfit to generator-specific fingerprints in their training data and catastrophically fail on out-of-distribution fakes, effectively treating the real category as a sink class that absorbs all unseen samples. The paper argues that the real image distribution is far more stable than the rapidly evolving fake distribution, making it more principled to learn a tight decision boundary around real images and treat fakes as the sink class. Additionally, existing evaluation protocols relying solely on average accuracy mask severe per-generator variance, obscuring a detector's true reliability under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images and produces a binary real/fake classification. During training, real images are not used directly: their DINOv3 embeddings are linearly blended with randomly sampled fake image embeddings before being fed to the classifier, so the model never sees a 'pure' fake during training. Embeddings are precomputed offline (taking ~22–50 minutes on an H100), after which the 2-layer MLP trains in under 3 minutes, making the pipeline orders of magnitude faster than end-to-end approaches like AIDE.

## Method

SimLBR introduces Latent Blending Regularization (LBR), which reformulates fake detection as learning a tight boundary around the real image distribution. During training, each real image is randomly assigned a label; when labeled 'fake,' its DINOv3 embedding is linearly interpolated with a randomly sampled fake embedding using α ~ Uniform(0.5, 0.8), ensuring the blended sample retains at least half its real-image information while injecting enough fake signal to make classification non-trivial. Operating in DINOv3's semantic latent space (rather than pixel space) prevents the model from exploiting low-level artifacts and forces it to learn generator-agnostic semantic distinctions. A lightweight 2-layer MLP with ReLU activations is trained with binary cross-entropy on these precomputed embeddings, achieving up to +24.85% accuracy and +69.62% recall on the Chameleon hard testset while maintaining the lowest cross-generator variance among all compared methods.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (trained on SD v1.4, tested on 7 generators), SimLBR achieves 94.54% mean accuracy, outperforming the prior SoTA AIDE (86.88%) by 7.66 percentage points, with a standard deviation of only 3.74 (vs. 12.33 for AIDE) and a reliability score of 11.91 (vs. 2.99). On the AIGC benchmark (trained on ProGAN, tested on 15 generators), SimLBR achieves 88.40% mean accuracy (3rd highest) but the lowest standard deviation (6.23) and highest reliability (6.16), and is the only method to exceed 75% on every generator. On the curated hard testset Chameleon, SimLBR surpasses AIDE by up to +25% accuracy and +70% recall (e.g., 84.33% vs. 58.37% under ProGAN training). Worst-case accuracy (minimum across all generators) is 88.00% on GenImage and 75.54% on AIGC, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimLBR outperforms AIDE (the prior SoTA) by 7.66% mean accuracy on GenImage (94.54% vs. 86.88%) and by a large margin on the hardest OOD generator BigGAN (88.00% vs. 66.89%), while also reducing cross-generator variance from 12.33 to 3.74. On AIGC, SimLBR trails AIDE in mean accuracy (88.40% vs. 92.77%) and PatchCraft (89.31%), but achieves the lowest standard deviation (6.23 vs. 7.92 and 8.89) and highest reliability score (6.16 vs. 5.40 and 4.42), and is the only method above 75% on all 15 generators. Against UnivFD, SimLBR wins decisively on both benchmarks (94.54% vs. 73.29% on GenImage; 88.40% vs. 78.43% on AIGC) with far lower variance. On the Chameleon hard testset, SimLBR (84.33% / 85.57%) dramatically outperforms AIDE (58.37% / 62.60%), PatchCraft (53.76% / 56.32%), and DIRE (58.19% / 59.71%), confirming that the gap widens under perceptual difficulty. Worst-case accuracy (minimum across generators) is 88.00% for SimLBR on GenImage and 75.54% on AIGC, versus 66.89% and 73.25% for AIDE, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in fake image detection—learning a decision boundary around training fakes—by inverting the objective to learn a tight boundary around the (relatively stable) real image distribution and treating fake as a sink class. The core mechanism, Latent Blending Regularization (LBR), repurposes the blending idea from pixel-space deepfake detection (e.g., self-blended images [36]) into the semantically rich DINOv3 latent space, where simple vector arithmetic creates controlled perturbations that are harder for the model to shortcut via low-level artifacts. Conceptually, it extends the latent-space detection insight of UnivFD [27] and the DCT+CLIP approach of AIDE [49] by showing that a lightweight MLP on precomputed DINOv3 embeddings, regularized by LBR, can match or exceed far more complex pipelines. The paper also introduces a Sharpe-ratio-based reliability metric and worst-case performance estimates, filling a gap in evaluation methodology that prior works largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: SimLBR trains in under 3 minutes on a single H100 GPU versus roughly 2 hours on 8 A100s for AIDE, making robust fake-image detection accessible at a fraction of the compute cost. The +24.85% accuracy and +69.62% recall gains on the Chameleon hard testset address a critical failure mode where existing detectors catastrophically misclassify perceptually convincing fakes as real. However, the method's reliance on DINOv3's manifold structure (DINOv2 fails to deliver the same gains) raises open questions about which embedding-space properties are necessary for LBR to work, and the assumption that the real image distribution remains stable across domains limits applicability to rapidly shifting visual domains. The proposed reliability and worst-case metrics are likely to influence how the community evaluates future detectors beyond average accuracy.
