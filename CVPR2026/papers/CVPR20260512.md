## Problem

Speculative decoding (SD) accelerates autoregressive models by drafting multiple tokens and verifying them in a single target-model forward pass, but its rigid 'draft one step, verify one step' paradigm never reduces the number of target model forward passes—the dominant source of inference latency. Existing visual AR adaptations (e.g., LANTERN's relaxed acceptance) improve token acceptance rates yet still conservatively verify every draft proposal, leaving a substantial speedup on the table. No prior work has explored bypassing verification steps entirely for visual AR generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260512/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on the token-sequence level of a visual AR model (LlamaGen-XL): a text prompt conditions the autoregressive generation of a sequence of discrete visual tokens, which are then decoded by a pre-trained VQ-VAE/VQ-GAN decoder into the final RGB image. During inference, the draft model proposes candidate token trees at each step, and the target model either verifies them (standard SD) or the framework skips verification and directly accepts a selected path, deferring its check to a subsequent 'post-verification' forward pass that processes both the unverified tokens and new candidates together.

## Method

VVS introduces partial verification skipping into the SD pipeline for visual AR generation, built on two empirical observations: (i) candidate token paths in the draft tree exhibit high mutual similarity (cosine > 0.7 in 75% of iterations), making exhaustive verification redundant, and (ii) token-level hidden-state features remain sufficiently similar across consecutive steps (similarity ≈ 0.68) to support drafting without fresh target-model states. Three modules implement this: a verification-free token selector that uniformly samples a candidate path (avoiding greedy bias) and dynamically truncates it to the average tree path length; a token-level feature caching and reuse mechanism that retrieves the most recent cached features (with mixed staleness) to guide drafting when the prior step was skipped; and a fine-grained scheduler that skips verification only when the exponentially decayed weighted cosine similarity among candidate paths exceeds a threshold, with a hard constraint forbidding two consecutive verification-free steps to bound error accumulation. This yields up to 2.86× fewer target model forward passes over vanilla AR decoding while preserving or improving FID, CLIP score, and Precision.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017, VVS reduces target model forward passes by up to 2.86× (TPF) and achieves up to 1.76× wall-clock inference speedup over vanilla AR decoding on LlamaGen-XL Stage II, while maintaining a FID of 47.19 (vs. 48.23 for vanilla AR). On LlamaGen-XL Stage I, VVS achieves 2.24× TPF and 1.63× speedup with a FID of 24.96, matching vanilla AR quality (24.88). The method also improves Precision over vanilla AR on both stages (0.5416 vs. 0.5084 on Stage I; 0.4856 vs. 0.4028 on Stage II), though Recall and HPSv2 show modest trade-offs at higher acceleration settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VVS outperforms both baselines on speed across both model stages. On LlamaGen-XL Stage I, VVS achieves 1.63× wall-clock speedup vs. LANTERN's 1.45× and EAGLE-2's 0.87× (which is actually slower than vanilla AR), while matching LANTERN's FID (24.96 vs. 24.97). On Stage II, the gap widens: VVS reaches 1.76× speedup and 2.86× TPF versus LANTERN's 1.26× and 1.83×, and also improves FID (47.19 vs. 50.23), showing that LANTERN's relaxed acceptance at δ=0.2 degrades quality more than VVS's verification skipping at δ=0.1. EAGLE-2, which uses strict verification without relaxation, fails to accelerate visual AR models at all (0.87× and 0.92× speedup), confirming that standard SD is ineffective for this domain. VVS trades a small amount of Recall and HPSv2 for substantially higher speed, but the FID-TPF Pareto front (Fig. 7) shows VVS dominates both baselines across the quality-speed spectrum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding lineage from Leviathan et al. (ICML 2022) and the feature-based drafting paradigm of EAGLE-2 (EMNLP 2024) into the visual AR domain, while incorporating LANTERN's (ICLR 2024) relaxed acceptance mechanism to handle the low-confidence, interchangeable nature of visual tokens. The key departure is that all prior visual-AR SD work (LANTERN, GSD, SJD) preserves the 'draft one step, verify one step' structure and merely loosens the acceptance criterion, whereas VVS explicitly skips verification for selected steps, reducing target-model forward passes rather than just increasing tokens-per-pass. The paper frames this as the first exploration of partial verification skipping, motivated by two empirical observations—verification redundancy across similar token paths and stale feature reusability—that are specific to the visual token distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By cutting target-model forward passes by up to 2.8× while maintaining competitive FID and CLIP scores, VVS offers a practical acceleration route for interactive visual AR generation that is orthogonal to and complementary with relaxation-based speedups. The finding that visual token paths in the draft tree are highly similar (cosine > 0.7 in 75% of iterations) and that substituting verification results with alternative paths barely affects fidelity challenges the assumption that every drafted token must be verified. Limitations include evaluation on only LlamaGen-XL (two stages) and MS-COCO, a noticeable recall drop at the highest speedup setting (Stage II: 0.5784 → 0.4588), and the hard constraint against consecutive skips that caps the achievable skip ratio. Open questions include whether specialized draft-model training co-designed with the skipping mechanism could push the speed-quality frontier further, and whether the approach generalizes to other visual AR architectures or higher-resolution generation.
