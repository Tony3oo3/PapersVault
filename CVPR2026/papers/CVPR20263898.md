## Problem

Conventional self-supervised trackers lack explicit context modeling, making it difficult to learn context-aware cues from unlabeled videos. Existing context association methods rely on randomly initialized, non-semantic queries that fail to extract reliable contextual information in unlabeled settings, limiting generalization in long-term and complex tracking scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263898/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a reference frame with an initial bounding box annotation and a sequence of unlabeled video frames, processed through a ViT-Base backbone at 256×256 or 384×384 resolution. It outputs a predicted bounding box and a classification score per frame. The dual-mode contextual association mechanism is applied exclusively during training and is omitted at inference, preserving an efficient 59 FPS runtime on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

PNTrack introduces a Dual-mode Contextual Association (DCA) mechanism that progressively modulates learning difficulty across two training stages. In the early stage, semantically meaningful instance tokens are sampled from search features by cross-correlating multi-head attention maps with classification response maps, then propagated as contextual prompts to both forward and backward tracking branches to accelerate knowledge acquisition. In the later stage, randomly sampled background tokens (contextual noise) are injected via cross-attention to perturb the feature embedding space, forcing the tracker to learn robust representations under a more challenging distribution. The overall framework follows a siamese cycle-consistency architecture with a ViT-Base backbone initialized from DropMAE weights, optimized with focal loss for classification and GIoU + L1 loss for regression.

## Results

On the four primary benchmarks, PNTrack-384 achieves AUC scores of 72.7 (GOT10K AO), 67.1 (LaSOT), 81.8 (TrackingNet), and 49.1 (LaSOT_ext), consistently outperforming the strongest self-supervised baseline SSTrack-384 by 1.2–1.4 AUC points. On VOT2020, PNTrack-384 reaches an EAO of 0.522, surpassing SSTrack-256 (0.458) by 0.064 and approaching the best fully supervised tracker LMTrack (0.586). The self-supervised gap to the best fully supervised method (MCITrack) narrows to 8.2% on LaSOT and 5.5% on LaSOT_ext, demonstrating that the dual-mode contextual association mechanism effectively bridges the supervision gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest self-supervised baseline SSTrack-384, PNTrack-384 wins on all four primary benchmarks: +1.2 AUC on LaSOT (67.1 vs 65.9), +1.4 AUC on TrackingNet (81.8 vs 80.4), +0.6 AUC on LaSOT_ext (49.1 vs 48.5), and +3.7 SR0.75 on GOT10K (69.9 vs 66.2). On VOT2020, PNTrack-384 achieves EAO 0.522 vs SSTrack-256's 0.458, a 0.064 improvement, while remaining 0.064 below the best fully supervised LMTrack (0.586). On OTB100 and UAV123, PNTrack-384 reaches AUC 71.2 and 66.4, surpassing SSTrack-256 by 0.7 and 0.3 points respectively, and matching or exceeding several fully supervised trackers (e.g., TransT at 69.4/69.1, Mixformer at 70.0/70.4). The gap to the top fully supervised MCITrack narrows to 8.2% on LaSOT and 5.5% on LaSOT_ext, compared to SSTrack-384's gaps of 9.4% and 6.1%. The ablation confirms that both contextual prompts (−1.8 AUC if removed) and contextual noise (−0.8 AUC if removed) contribute meaningfully, and that semantic patch tokens outperform randomly initialized non-semantic queries by 1.3 AUC.

## Relation to Prior Work

PNTrack builds directly on SSTrack's decoupled spatio-temporal consistency framework for self-supervised tracking, which separates forward and backward tracking branches to handle unlabeled and labeled frames. It departs from both vanilla self-supervised trackers (which lack explicit context modeling) and fully supervised contextual trackers (which rely on randomly initialized non-semantic queries) by introducing a dual-mode contextual association mechanism that injects semantically meaningful instance tokens as prompts during early training and background noise tokens during later training. The use of attention maps to guide token sampling draws on insights from DETR-style detection, repurposing cross-attention weights as a spatial localization signal for self-supervised settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PNTrack narrows the performance gap between self-supervised and fully supervised tracking to roughly 8% AUC on LaSOT, making self-supervised approaches more practical for scenarios where annotation is expensive. The training-only DCA mechanism preserves inference efficiency, which is important for real-time deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that the generalizability of DCA beyond tracking (e.g., to semi-supervised video segmentation) remains unexplored, and the modest 1–2% gains over SSTrack suggest the approach may hit diminishing returns without further architectural changes. An open question is whether the easy-to-hard curriculum (prompts → noise) generalizes to other self-supervised paradigms or is specific to the cycle-consistency tracking formulation.
