## Problem

Vision-language models excel at semantic understanding of instructions and 2D images but remain geometrically naive: they cannot reliably infer global 3D spatial layout across viewpoints or translate high-level plans into physically executable, collision-free action sequences. This semantic-geometric gap makes VLM-based agents unreliable navigators in continuous 3D environments, especially in zero-shot settings where no in-domain training is available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261450/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction and multi-view (front, left, right) RGB-D images with camera pose at each timestep. The VLM's visual input is a composite of the egocentric RGB image with numbered waypoint markers projected onto it, plus a multi-channel 2D bird's-eye-view map rasterized from the 3D scene (geometric channel for obstacles/navigable areas, semantic channel for object instances, and decision channel for waypoints, historical trajectory, and subtask completion markers). The VLM outputs a discrete high-level action (a turn command or a selected waypoint index), which is then converted by A* into a sequence of low-level ROTATE and FORWARD primitives executed in the simulator.

## Method

HSGM constructs a dynamically updated 3D scene representation in three layers: a Geometric Map (backprojected RGB-D point cloud partitioned into navigable regions, obstacles, and detected stairs), a Semantic Map (YOLO-E instance segmentation backprojected into 3D and merged across frames), and a Decision Map (a Global Waypoint Graph for A* path planning plus a Local Waypoint Set sampled from the agent's current view with cylindrical occupancy and reachability checks). The 3D HSGM is rasterized into a 2D BEV map that serves as the VLM's spatial input, converting a continuous 3D planning problem into a discrete waypoint-selection task aligned with the VLM's native 2D reasoning. Navigation is fully decoupled: the VLM (GPT-5) performs structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning to select the next waypoint or turn action, while A* over the pre-built global graph computes the collision-free path between waypoints. A subtask management mechanism decomposes long instructions into ordered, verifiable subtasks with a finite-state progress tracker, double-confirmation for final termination, and automatic backtracking on step-limit exceedance, all operating training-free.

## Results

On R2R-CE (Val-Unseen), HSGM achieves a Success Rate of 47.9%, SPL of 32.8%, Navigation Error of 5.42 m, and Oracle Success Rate of 58.7%, setting a new state-of-the-art among zero-shot methods. On the more challenging RxR-CE (Val-Unseen), it reaches 41.8% SR, 25.1% SPL, 7.43 m NE, and 54.9% nDTW. These results surpass all existing zero-shot baselines by a wide margin and even exceed several fully supervised approaches (e.g., MapNav, NaVid), though they remain below the strongest supervised methods ETPNav (57.0% SR) and Dynam3D (52.9% SR) on R2R-CE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSGM decisively outperforms all zero-shot baselines on both benchmarks. On R2R-CE, it improves SR by +15.1 points over the strongest zero-shot competitor DreamNav (32.8%) and by +22.4 points over AO-Planner (25.5%); on RxR-CE, its 41.8% SR nearly doubles AO-Planner's 22.4% and its nDTW of 54.9% far exceeds AO-Planner's 33.1%. Against supervised methods, HSGM surpasses MapNav (39.7% → 47.9% SR on R2R-CE; 32.6% → 41.8% on RxR-CE) and NaVid (37.4% → 47.9%; 23.8% → 41.8%), confirming that the training-free paradigm can rival in-domain-trained systems. However, HSGM still trails the two strongest supervised methods on R2R-CE: ETPNav achieves 57.0% SR and Dynam3D reaches 52.9%, both exceeding HSGM's 47.9%. The gap to ETPNav is particularly notable in SPL (49.0 vs. 32.8) and NE (4.71 vs. 5.42 m), suggesting that supervised end-to-end training still yields more geometrically efficient trajectories despite HSGM's superior semantic grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot VLN-CE line of work (InstructNav, AO-Planner, SmartWay, DreamNav) by introducing a hierarchical 3D scene representation (HSGM) that is rasterized into a 2D BEV map for VLM consumption, rather than relying on learned waypoint predictors or forcing the VLM to reason directly over 3D geometry. The geometric map construction follows InstructNav's RGB-D backprojection pipeline, while the decoupled planning paradigm builds on the waypoint-based and value-map-based approaches but replaces learned low-level controllers with a classical A* search over a sampled global waypoint graph. The key conceptual departure is treating the VLM strictly as a discrete waypoint selector over a geometrically validated candidate set, fully separating semantic reasoning from physical execution without any in-domain training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully training-free framework (GPT-5 + YOLO-E + A*) outperforms several supervised VLN-CE methods by 8–18 points in success rate challenges the prevailing assumption that VLN requires domain-specific training data and fine-tuning. The hierarchical map design offers a reusable architectural template for grounding VLMs in 3D environments beyond navigation. Open questions include scalability to larger outdoor or multi-agent settings, the sensitivity to VLM quality (results rely on GPT-5), the computational cost of maintaining and rasterizing 3D point clouds in real time, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation rather than coarse waypoint-level decisions.
