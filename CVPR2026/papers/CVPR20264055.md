## Problem

VQA model development and dataset curation operate in disconnected loops: models iterate on fixed benchmarks while new subjective studies collect labels without systematically targeting the failure modes of current top-performing VQA models. This 'easy dataset problem' means most available data is trivially handled by existing models, so additional annotations yield diminishing returns and fail to improve cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264055/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large unlabeled video pool (e.g., 42,000+ clips across gaming, live-streaming, short-form, and AI-generated domains), a frozen base VQA model (VisualQuality-R1 on Qwen2.5-VL), and CLIP frame-level semantic features for each video. It produces a budgeted 5% subset selected for human labeling, per-video difficulty scores in [1,5] from the LoRA-based failure predictor, and a fine-tuned VQA model. Newly annotated data is represented in scale-free pairwise form so it merges seamlessly with existing pairwise VQA datasets without cross-dataset perceptual scale alignment.

## Method

MDS-VQA augments a frozen base VQA model with a LoRA-based failure predictor g(·) (rank 64, α=128) trained under a Thurstone-model ranking objective with fidelity loss, so that g(·) learns a relative difficulty ordering rather than absolute error magnitudes—making it robust to cross-domain MOS scale shifts. At inference, a greedy subset-selection procedure iteratively picks the video that maximizes a weighted sum of its predicted difficulty and its mean Chamfer distance (computed over CLIP frame features) to already-selected samples, balancing informativeness against content redundancy under a fixed labeling budget (λ=0.25). The selected subset is then human-labeled and used for active fine-tuning via LoRA on the base model, following the reinforcement-learning-to-rank recipe of VisualQuality-R1. The entire pipeline is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated to work with both VLM-based and CNN/Transformer-based VQA backbones.

## Results

With only a 5% labeled subset per target domain, MDS-VQA improves mean SRCC from 0.651 (base VisualQuality-R1) to 0.722 and mean PLCC from 0.683 to 0.749 across six VQA datasets. On failure identification (Table 2), MDS-VQA achieves the lowest mean SRCC of 0.241 (vs. 0.365 for the best competitor NoiseStability), indicating it selects the most model-challenging samples. Under gMAD worst-case evaluation on LSVQ-1080p, MDS-VQA attains rank 1 in both SRCC and gMAD with zero rank discrepancy, confirming consistent gains under both average-case and worst-case criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDS-VQA outperforms all eight baselines on mean SRCC and PLCC after active fine-tuning (Table 3). The closest competitor is FreeSel [45] (mean SRCC 0.713 vs. 0.722), followed by ALCS [46] and NoiseStability [13] (both 0.708). On failure identification (Table 2), MDS-VQA's mean SRCC of 0.241 is substantially lower than NoiseStability (0.365) and FreeSel (0.371), confirming superior targeting of model blind spots. Diversity-only methods (Core-set [23], Sampling by RD [42], Greedy sampling [2]) and uncertainty-only methods (MC dropout [21]) all trail MDS-VQA by 0.015–0.030 in mean SRCC, validating the joint difficulty-plus-diversity formulation. Notably, MDS-VQA achieves the best per-dataset SRCC on CGVDS (0.874) and YouTube-UGC (0.819), while its margin narrows on AIGVQA-DB (0.769 vs. 0.790 for NoiseStability), suggesting AI-generated video distortions remain a harder regime. Under gMAD worst-case evaluation, MDS-VQA ranks 1st (tied with FreeSel at 2nd), whereas methods like Core-set and NoiseStability drop 3 ranks between SRCC and gMAD, indicating MDS-VQA's gains are more robust to rare failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDS-VQA builds directly on VisualQuality-R1, a VLM-based VQA model that uses reinforcement-learning-to-rank for quality scoring, as its frozen base model. It departs from prior active learning strategies (core-set, MC dropout, FreeSel, NoiseStability, etc.) by introducing an explicit model-informed difficulty signal: a LoRA-augmented failure predictor trained with a Thurstone-based ranking objective, which prior VQA data-selection methods lack. The diversity component repurposes CLIP frame-level features with Chamfer distance, a standard tool from active learning, but the key conceptual departure is closing the loop between model failure diagnosis and targeted data acquisition rather than treating selection as a purely representativeness- or uncertainty-driven problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a structural inefficiency in VQA research where new datasets are collected without targeting the specific failure modes of current models, leading to diminishing returns on labeling effort. Demonstrating that a 5% budgeted subset can lift mean SRCC from 0.651 to 0.722 and achieve top gMAD rank (worst-case differentiation) suggests a practical path to more efficient dataset growth, especially for emerging regimes like AI-generated video. Open questions include calibrating the difficulty-diversity trade-off without manual tuning, extending difficulty modeling to fine-grained failure taxonomies (spatial vs. temporal vs. semantic), and operationalizing multi-round active learning with principled stopping criteria and cost-aware annotation protocols.
