## Problem

Existing I2V benchmarks evaluate visual fidelity, temporal smoothness, and physical plausibility but fail to assess higher-order reasoning capabilities. Despite Veo 3's 'chain-of-frames' concept hinting at step-by-step visual reasoning in video generation, no systematic benchmark exists to rigorously measure whether video generative models can perform structured, rule-based, or symbolic reasoning comparable to LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample provides an initial-state image (adapted to 720p for horizontal video) paired with a narrative-style text prompt that specifies start/end states and implicit constraints without prescribing the solution path. The model produces a generated video, which is scored via two metric types: process-and-goal consistency (e.g., trajectory tracking in maze solving) or final-state validation (e.g., grid comparison for Sudoku, feature matching via DINO for sequence completion). Prompts are deliberately descriptive rather than instructional, leaving intermediate reasoning steps for the model to infer.

## Method

TiViBench organizes 595 image-prompt samples across 4 reasoning dimensions (Structural Reasoning & Search, Spatial & Visual Pattern Reasoning, Symbolic & Logical Reasoning, Action Planning & Task Execution), 24 task scenarios, and 3 difficulty levels, with prompts generated by Gemini-2.5-Pro and validated by three human annotators. The second contribution, VideoTPO, is a test-time prompt optimization loop that generates two candidate videos per round, uses GPT-4o as a VLM to produce qualitative textual critiques comparing their strengths and weaknesses (textual loss), derives actionable prompt modification suggestions (textual gradient), and iteratively refines the prompt without any weight updates, external reward models, or additional training data. This differs from prior single-pass rewriting by leveraging multi-candidate preference alignment for finer-grained optimization, and from TPO [26] by replacing external reward models with VLM self-analysis and using only 2 samples per round for efficiency.

## Results

On TiViBench (Pass@1), Sora 2 achieves the highest overall reasoning accuracy of 27.90%, followed by Veo 3.1 at 26.05% and Kling 2.1 at 11.60%. Among open-source models, Wan2.2 leads at 9.41%, Wan2.1 at 8.40%, HunyuanVideo at 4.03%, and CogVideoX1.5 at 2.02%. Under Pass@5 evaluation, open-source models show notable gains (e.g., Wan2.2 improves from 9.41% to 16.47%), revealing latent but inconsistent reasoning capability. Applying VideoTPO further boosts HunyuanVideo from 4.03% to 10.25% and Wan2.1 from 8.40% to 18.15% overall, outperforming both pre-rewriter and post-rewriter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Commercial models dominate: Sora 2 (27.90%) and Veo 3.1 (26.05%) substantially outperform all open-source models, with Sora 2 leading in Structural & Search (18.71%) and Spatial & Visual Pattern (31.76%), while Veo 3.1 excels in Planning & Execution (51.59%). Among open-source baselines, Wan2.2 (9.41%) and Wan2.1 (8.40%) are the strongest, but both trail Kling 2.1 (11.60%) by a wide margin; HunyuanVideo (4.03%) and CogVideoX1.5 (2.02%) are far behind. Under Pass@5, Wan2.2 improves to 16.47% and Wan2.1 to 15.29%, indicating latent reasoning ability that is inconsistently realized. For prompt optimization, VideoTPO outperforms both the pre-rewriter baseline (Veo prompt guide) and the post-rewriter baseline (Self-Refine), achieving 18.15% on Wan2.1 versus lower gains from either single-pass strategy. Cross-model prompt transfer (applying HunyuanVideo-optimized prompts to Wan2.1) yields limited or negative gains, confirming that model-specific preference alignment is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Veo 3's 'chain-of-frames' reasoning demonstration (Wiedemer et al., 2025) from a few illustrative tasks (graph traversal, maze solving) into a systematic hierarchical benchmark spanning 24 task scenarios across four reasoning dimensions. It departs from existing I2V evaluation suites (VBench, VBench++, WorldScore, TC-Bench) that focus on visual fidelity, temporal smoothness, and physical plausibility, by introducing correctness-based reasoning metrics with explicit ground-truth states. VideoTPO adapts the test-time preference optimization (TPO) framework from LLMs to video generation, replacing external reward models with VLM self-analysis of paired candidate videos, and positions itself against pre-inference prompt enrichment and single-pass post-inference rewriting (Self-Refine).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TiViBench fills a clear gap by providing the first hierarchical, reasoning-specific evaluation for I2V models, and the finding that open-source models show a substantial Pass@5-over-Pass@1 gap (e.g., Wan2.2: 9.4%→16.5%) suggests latent reasoning capability that current training scale has not yet stabilized. The VideoTPO result—nearly doubling Wan2.1's accuracy (8.4%→18.2%) without any weight updates—offers a practical, low-cost path for practitioners to improve reasoning performance. Key open questions remain: whether explicit rule encoding or process-level RL is needed to close the gap on symbolic tasks (sudoku, maze), whether VAE compression fundamentally limits fine-grained visual feature extraction for reasoning, and whether the cross-model prompt transfer failure indicates that reasoning prompts are inherently model-specific.
