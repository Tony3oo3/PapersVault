## Problem

Current diffusion-based generative models fail at studio-level multi-object compositing because they simultaneously struggle with preserving each object's identity, maintaining precise background and color fidelity, providing layout control, and ensuring all specified objects appear without omission or duplication. No existing model accepts the natural input combination of multiple object images, a background, and a descriptive caption while meeting all four requirements at once.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262739/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts N unsegmented object photographs, an optional background image, and a free-form text caption describing the desired composition (layout, lighting, props, colors). At inference, objects are randomly scattered on the background (or a white canvas) to form a naive first frame F1, and the model generates a K-frame video (9–33 frames) whose final frame is the polished composite image. The intermediate video frames can also be used directly as animated output, and the model handles variable numbers of objects (1–7) with or without a background.

## Method

PLACID fine-tunes a pretrained image-to-video diffusion transformer (Wan 2.1 I2V-14B) via a lightweight LoRA adapter using Flow Matching, exploiting the video model's temporal priors on object identity and interaction. Two architectural modifications enable multi-object conditioning: (i) full-resolution object images and the background are individually CLIP-encoded and fed through cross-attention alongside the naive composite F1, preventing detail loss and identity entanglement; (ii) special tokens (<OBJ>, </OBJ>, <BG>, </BG>) in the caption unambiguously bind text descriptions to specific visual inputs. The key data-generation insight is synthesizing training videos where objects follow smooth linear trajectories from random initial positions to their final composite layout, rather than naively interpolating between first and last frames—this preserves temporal coherence, prevents object erasure or duplication, and aligns with the video model's motion priors. Training data (~50K tuples) combines professional multi-object photography, Subject-200k subject-driven pairs, and synthetic side-by-side 3D renders, with augmentations for objects, backgrounds, and captions.

## Results

PLACID achieves the best background preservation (MSE-BG = 0.019) and color fidelity (Chamfer = 4.641) among all compared methods, substantially outperforming the next-best NanoBanana (MSE-BG = 0.029, Chamfer = 13.146) and Wan 2.1 (MSE-BG = 0.047, Chamfer = 7.746). It also produces the fewest missing objects (4.4% vs. 4.8% for Wan 2.1 and 13.8% for NanoBanana) and the second-highest VQAScore (0.912, behind only NanoBanana's 0.929). Object identity scores (CLIP-I = 0.705, DINO = 0.440) are competitive but slightly below OmniGen (0.724/0.478) and Wan 2.1 (0.711/0.446), which the authors attribute to PLACID's deliberate trade-off toward natural composition and reposing over strict pixel-level identity matching. User studies confirm PLACID is preferred over all open-source baselines in both identity preservation and overall visual quality, with a slight edge over the closed-source NanoBanana.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLACID decisively wins on background and color fidelity: its MSE-BG of 0.019 is 34% lower than NanoBanana (0.029) and 60% lower than Wan 2.1 (0.047), while its Chamfer distance of 4.641 is 40% lower than Wan 2.1 (7.746) and nearly 3× lower than VACE (9.948). On object completeness, PLACID's 4.4% missing rate is the best, edging out Wan 2.1 (4.8%) and dramatically outperforming NanoBanana (13.8%) and OmniGen (12.8%). On text alignment, PLACID's VQAScore of 0.912 trails only NanoBanana (0.929) and surpasses all other baselines, while its CLIP-T (0.336) is comparable to DSD (0.347) and NanoBanana (0.344). On raw identity metrics (CLIP-I, DINO), PLACID is slightly behind OmniGen (0.724/0.478) and Wan 2.1 (0.711/0.446), but the authors argue this reflects a design choice favoring natural reposing and composition over pixel-locked identity, a trade-off validated by user studies where PLACID is preferred over all open-source methods and slightly ahead of NanoBanana in both identity and overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLACID builds on the Wan 2.1 I2V diffusion transformer as its base architecture, adapting it from video generation to static multi-object compositing via LoRA fine-tuning with flow matching. It departs from prior object compositing methods (AnyDoor, IMPRINT, ObjectStitch, MultiTwin) that rely on image-to-image diffusion and typically handle single objects or require bounding boxes, by instead exploiting video models' temporal priors on identity consistency and object interaction. The key conceptual departure is the synthetic trajectory data pipeline: rather than interpolating between initial and final frames (which produces incoherent intermediate states), objects follow smooth linear paths, preserving the video model's learned motion dynamics. This positions PLACID at the intersection of subject-driven generation (UNO, MS-Diffusion, OmniGen) and video editing (VACE), but uniquely targets the studio-quality multi-object compositing setting with simultaneous identity, background, and color fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap where current generative models fail at studio-level multi-object compositing—altering colors, omitting or duplicating objects, and producing incoherent layouts—making it directly relevant to e-commerce, marketing, and design workflows. The synthetic trajectory data curation strategy is a reusable technique that could benefit other tasks requiring controlled object motion in video models. Key limitations include the need for novel view synthesis during significant reposing (only one image per object is provided) and the computational cost of video-based inference (~26–80 s per composite). Open questions include whether multi-view object inputs could resolve the reposing limitation, how the approach scales beyond 7 objects, and whether the video-prior advantage persists as image models improve in identity preservation.
