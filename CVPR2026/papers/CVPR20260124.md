## Problem

Latent inpainting in diffusion models universally composites VAE latents by linearly interpolating under a downsampled, channel-broadcast mask, an operation that is mathematically unsound because modern VAE decoders are nonlinear and spatially entangled. This produces boundary halos, global color drift, and off-manifold seams that denoising steps amplify, and the 8× mask downsampling discards fine geometric structure. The paper formalizes pixel-equivalence (PE) as a necessary criterion for latent operators and shows empirically that no convex latent interpolation can satisfy it in modern VAEs.

## Input / Output

DecFormer consumes two VAE latents at latent resolution (h×w×C, where h=H/8, w=W/8) and a full-resolution pixel mask M∈[0,1]^{H×W×1}; the mask is processed once by a 0.7M-parameter CNN prior into a content-agnostic blend seed α₀ and cross-attention tokens. The model outputs per-channel blend weights α∈[0,1]^{C×h×w} and a residual correction s∈R^{C×h×w}, which combine as (1−α)⊙z_A + α⊙z_B + s to yield a single composited latent. Unlike heuristic masking, the blend is channel-adaptive and the mask geometry is preserved at full pixel resolution rather than being downsampled and broadcast.

## Method

DecFormer is a 7.7M-parameter transformer with multi-scale blocks (patch sizes [4,2,1,1]) that predicts per-channel blend weights and a nonlinear residual correction, trained with the PELC objective: latent-space MSE against the encoded pixel composite plus pixel-space LPIPS and a halo-weighted L1 loss that emphasizes mask-boundary regions. Conditioning uses FiLM from a softly decaying halo band around mask edges and cross-attention to mask tokens restricted to the finest (patch=1) blocks, while a lightweight CNN prior seeds the blend. Training is staged: the α head converges first, then the residual s head is warmed up with ramped halo losses. For diffusion inpainting, DecFormer is inserted via a z₀ re-targeting scheduler step—decode the predicted z₀, apply the learned composite, then re-target the velocity field—making it a drop-in replacement for heuristic blending with only 3.5% FLOP overhead on FLUX.1-Dev.

## Results

On COCO-2017 validation (masked area >15%), DecFormer with a lightweight LoRA achieves FID 19.280 and LPIPS 0.303, matching or exceeding the fully finetuned Flux.1-Fill (FID 19.343, LPIPS 0.313) while using only 7.7M additional parameters and 3.5% FLOP overhead. In the compositing-fidelity evaluation (Table 2), DecFormer reduces Halo-L1 error by up to 53% and LPIPS by ~55% over heuristic latent blending across all mask types (soft, binary, thin). The frozen-backbone DecFormer alone already improves SSIM from 0.643 to 0.682 and FID from 23.514 to 20.556 over the heuristic baseline, with no backbone finetuning required.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flux.1-Fill (a fully finetuned 12B-parameter inpainting model), DecFormer + LoRA achieves a lower FID (19.280 vs. 19.343) and lower LPIPS (0.303 vs. 0.313), indicating comparable or slightly better perceptual quality, while Flux.1-Fill retains a clear advantage in pixel-level PSNR (16.750 vs. 14.231 dB). SSIM is essentially tied (0.680 vs. 0.681). Compared to the heuristic latent-blending baseline, DecFormer alone (no LoRA, no backbone finetuning) improves SSIM by +0.039, LPIPS by −0.040, and FID by −2.96, confirming that correcting the compositing operation itself yields substantial gains. The LoRA-only variant (without DecFormer) improves PSNR slightly over the heuristic but does not match DecFormer on SSIM or FID, showing that the compositor and the semantic adapter address complementary failure modes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the near-universal practice in latent diffusion inpainting of downsampled, channel-broadcast mask blending by formalizing pixel-equivalence (PELC) as a correctness criterion for latent operators. It builds on the LDM/VAE framework (Rombach et al., FLUX VAE) and the rectified-flow sampling schedule, but introduces a new lightweight compositor (DecFormer) rather than extending any prior inpainting architecture. Conceptually, it repurposes alpha compositing from pixel-space graphics into latent space and connects to Oring et al.'s observation that convex latent blends leave the data manifold, while distinguishing itself from trajectory-level editors (SDEdit, DiffEdit, Blended Diffusion, Differential Diffusion) that still rely on the same flawed broadcast-mask operation at each denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard heuristic latent masking is mathematically unsound for modern VAEs with large receptive fields and mid-layer attention challenges a foundational assumption in virtually every production inpainting pipeline. DecFormer's 7.7M-parameter footprint and 3.5% FLOP overhead make it a practical drop-in, and its ability to approach fully finetuned 12B inpainting models (Flux.1-Fill) with only a small LoRA suggests a path to reducing the maintenance cost of separate inpainting checkpoints. Key limitations include validation on a single VAE family (FLUX), the inherent residual error from lossy VAE compression, and the fact that PELC corrects fusion geometry but not semantic reconstruction. Open questions include extension to video, other VAE architectures, and whether training inpainting models or ControlNets with PELC in the loop reduces task difficulty or convergence time.
