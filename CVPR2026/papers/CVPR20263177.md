## Problem

Generating pictorial charts that blend visual imagery with data structures requires balancing data faithfulness with visual expressiveness, a tension that existing spatial-control methods (Canny edges, depth maps) cannot resolve because they assume pixel-perfect correspondence and are designed for natural images with far greater structural complexity than charts. No prior method provides a control representation that is simultaneously precise enough to encode chart data and flexible enough to allow stylistic deformation of visual elements.

## Input / Output

The method accepts three optional inputs: a chart skeleton (a sparse, chart-type-specific abstraction—vertical lines for bars, polylines for line charts, colored radial lines for pie slices), a reference image for subject-driven visual consistency, and a text prompt for semantic context. The skeleton and reference image can be used independently or jointly. Output is a 512×512 RGB pictorial chart image where visual elements conform to the data-encoding geometry of the skeleton while preserving the appearance of the reference subject.

## Method

ChArtist fine-tunes FLUX.1-DEV (a pretrained Diffusion Transformer) with two task-specific LoRA adapters: LoRA_S trained on skeleton–pictorial pairs for spatial control and LoRA_R trained on reference–pictorial pairs for subject control. A RoPE-based positional encoding strategy aligns the skeleton with latent tokens while offsetting the reference image, enabling both controls to coexist in a unified [T, X, C] token sequence. The key architectural contribution is Spatially-Gated Attention, a training-free inference mechanism that computes a spatial mask from skeleton-to-latent attention and uses it to dynamically gate subject attention weights (W′ = M ⊙ W + β(1−M) ⊙ W), establishing an explicit spatial→subject dependency that prevents cross-condition interference such as structure misalignment and style leakage. Training uses LoRA rank 16 at 512×512 resolution on the CHARTIST-30K dataset (30,000 skeleton–reference–pictorial triplets) for 25,000 iterations per task.

## Results

In the dual-control setting (Table 3), ChArtist achieves the highest data accuracy across all three chart types: 0.931 (bar), 0.905 (line), and 0.753 (pie), with the line chart margin being the largest (0.905 vs. 0.728 for the second-best). It also leads on visual consistency (DINO: 0.837/0.728/0.689; CLIP-I: 0.892/0.815/0.747) across all chart types. In the spatial-only task (Table 1), ChArtist achieves the best average score on bar (0.599) and line (0.584) charts, and is competitive on pie (0.515 vs. 0.513 for SDEdit). In the human study (300 participants), ChArtist ranks 2nd on both spatial and subject questions, indicating the most balanced performance across data accuracy and semantic alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChArtist outperforms all eight baselines on data accuracy in the dual-control setting, with the most dramatic margin on line charts (0.905 vs. 0.728 for ControlNet-Canny+IP-Adapter, a 17.7-point gap). Paint-by-Example is the closest competitor on bar chart data accuracy (0.912 vs. 0.931) but fails to preserve reference visual style, scoring 0.586 on DINO versus ChArtist's 0.837. ControlNet (Canny/Depth) combined with IP-Adapter consistently underperforms, as the rigid edge/depth conditioning conflicts with the flexible deformation needed for pictorial charts. Among the advanced image-editing models, GPT-Image-1 achieves the highest CLIP-IQA (0.712) and competitive DINO scores, but its data accuracy drops sharply on pie charts (0.422), indicating poor structural adherence. Qwen-Image-Edit and Nano Banana show moderate data accuracy but trail ChArtist by 0.15–0.30 on DINO, confirming weaker subject preservation. In the spatial-only task, Inpainting narrowly beats ChArtist on bar chart data accuracy (0.923 vs. 0.894) but at the cost of the lowest CLIP-T score (0.231 vs. 0.304), while SDEdit ranks 1st in human spatial preference (2.897) yet still lags ChArtist on CLIP-T across all chart types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the OmniControl conditional-LoRA architecture and the FLUX.1-DEV Diffusion Transformer backbone, extending unified multi-condition generation to the specific domain of pictorial charts. It departs from the standard dense control representations (Canny edges, depth maps) that dominate controllable diffusion by proposing a minimal skeleton-based representation that encodes only the data-encoding dimension of a chart, occupying a middle ground between overly rigid pixel-level conditions and overly weak sparse conditions like bounding boxes. The Spatially-Gated Attention mechanism is a novel training-free inference-time solution to the cross-condition interference problem that arises when composing multiple LoRA adapters in parallel, a challenge identified in the broader multi-control literature but not previously addressed with an explicit sequential dependency between conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CHARTIST-30K dataset and the unified data-accuracy metric provide the first systematic infrastructure for evaluating data faithfulness in generated pictorial charts, a gap the authors identify in prior authoring tools. The skeleton-based control representation and Spatially-Gated Attention mechanism are potentially transferable to other structured-visual generation tasks where a sparse structural signal must coexist with a dense appearance signal. However, the evaluation is limited to three chart types (bar, line, pie) on a fully synthetic dataset, and the generalization to more complex topologies (scatter, radar, treemap) or to real-world design workflows with user-specified data remains an open question. The reliance on a synthetic training pipeline also raises questions about robustness to distribution shift when applied to genuinely novel reference images or chart configurations.
