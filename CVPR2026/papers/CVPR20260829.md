## Problem

Current visual representation learning relies on aggregating diverse, web-scale data, but it remains unknown whether a single person's egocentric video experience contains sufficient geometric signal to train a generalizable vision model. The paper asks whether independently trained models on distinct individual lives converge to a shared geometric understanding (the Shared World Hypothesis) and whether single-life data can match diverse-data baselines at equivalent scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of egocentric video frames from a single individual, sampled either by temporal proximity or by 3D point-cloud overlap (Jaccard index). Each frame is split into non-overlapping patches; a high ratio of target patches is randomly masked. The encoder outputs per-patch visual feature embeddings, the decoder reconstructs masked target pixels, and the cross-attention maps between source and target patches are extracted to compute the Correspondence Alignment Score (CAS) between independently trained models. Downstream, a lightweight attention head on the frozen encoder produces depth maps or zero-shot correspondence maps.

## Method

The paper adopts the CroCo Siamese encoder-decoder transformer (ViT encoder + transformer decoder with cross-attention) and trains it via cross-view completion on pairs of frames from a single person's egocentric video. Two pairing strategies are introduced: temporal pairing (consecutive frames) and spatial pairing (frames with high 3D point-cloud overlap), with their union yielding the strongest representations. A key technical contribution is the Correspondence Alignment Score (CAS), a training-free, patch-level metric that extends the mutual k-NN score from the Platonic Representation Hypothesis by comparing top-K cross-attention correspondences between two independently trained models on held-out image pairs. The paradigm trains 20 distinct models (one per 'life') from random initialization, enabling direct comparison of alignment and generalization across indoor kitchens, outdoor walking tours, and week-long recordings of up to 38 hours.

## Results

At the 30-hour mark, single-life models (especially from the ALD dataset) match or surpass a size-matched Kinetics-400 baseline on NYU-Depth-V2 depth estimation (δ₁) and HPatches zero-shot correspondence (AEPE), though K400 retains an advantage on the more diverse ScanNet benchmark. Alignment with the pretrained CroCo checkpoint (measured by CAS) emerges after a critical duration of 30 minutes (Walking Tours), 1 hour (ALD), or 2 hours (HD-Epic). For architecture comparison on Walking Tours, DINOv2 achieves δ₁ = 0.562 ± 0.026 on NYU-Depth-V2 versus CroCo's 0.538 ± 0.001, but CroCo achieves lower AEPE (19.4 ± 0.38 vs. 21.2 ± 4.16) on HPatches correspondence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the pretrained CroCo checkpoint [2] (trained on diverse synthetic + real image pairs), single-life models approach but do not fully match the CAS alignment score, though the gap narrows substantially at 30 hours; non-life control videos achieve near-zero CAS, confirming that egocentric motion is essential. Against DINOv2 [30] trained on the same single-life data, CroCo wins on HPatches zero-shot correspondence (AEPE 19.4 vs. 21.2, lower is better) while DINOv2 wins on NYU-Depth-V2 depth estimation (δ₁ 0.562 vs. 0.538), indicating that the cross-view completion objective encodes richer geometric correspondence while the contrastive objective yields slightly better depth features. The most striking comparison is against the size-matched K400 diverse-data baseline: at 30 hours, ALD single-life models match or exceed K400 on NYU-Depth-V2 and HPatches, demonstrating that a single person's week of egocentric video is as effective as 30 hours of unrelated web clips for learning geometric priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CroCo cross-view completion architecture and the Siamese MAE training objective, repurposing them from their original setting (diverse, curated image pairs) to a highly constrained data regime: egocentric video from a single individual. Conceptually, it extends the Platonic Representation Hypothesis—which posits convergence of independently trained models on web-scale data—into a 'Shared World Hypothesis' where convergence is expected even when each model sees only one person's experience. It departs from prior video-stream learning work (DoRA, Carreira et al.) by not focusing on the online/continuous nature of learning, and from prior representation alignment studies by introducing a patch-level cross-attention metric (CAS) rather than image-level similarity scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 30 hours of one person's egocentric video yields geometric representations competitive with 30 hours of diverse web data (K400) challenges the field's core assumption that data diversity is a prerequisite for generalizable representations, with implications for embodied AI, personalization, and low-data-regime learning. The CAS metric provides a new, training-free tool for comparing independently trained models at patch granularity. Key limitations include the restriction to geometric (not semantic) downstream tasks, the modest maximum duration (38 hours), and the use of only two architectures (CroCo, DINOv2). Open questions include whether the paradigm scales to longer recordings, whether semantic representations also emerge from single-life data, and which self-supervised objective is optimal for this setting.
