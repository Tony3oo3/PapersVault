## Problem

Monolithic scaling of VLMs (increasing parameter count uniformly) yields diminishing returns for visual document understanding because documents demand multi-step procedural reasoning, diverse specialized skills (layout parsing, text extraction, numerical calculation), and are highly sensitive to small procedural errors that cascade without correction. The paper argues that a shift from monolithic scaling to procedural scaling—decomposing the task and applying targeted test-time compute to each functional entity—is needed.

## Input / Output

The system takes one or more visual document images (multi-page reports, slides, charts, tables, webpages, infographics) and a natural-language question as input, and produces a single text answer. Visual inputs are consumed by the planning and execution agents (VLMs), while the judgment and answer agents (LLMs) operate on the textual execution traces. The answer agent receives both correct and incorrect execution segments plus mistake descriptions, using the error-correction pairs as a logical closed loop to synthesize the final response.

## Method

MACT decomposes document VQA into four role-specialized agents—planning, execution, judgment, and answer—connected by a self-correction loop where the judgment agent flags errors and routes them back to the responsible agent (up to 3 corrections). The key differentiator is agent-wise adaptive test-time scaling: the planning agent generates N_p parallel reasoning paths via analogical prompting, the execution agent produces N_e candidate executions per step scored by a process reward model, and the judgment agent uses budget-forcing to enforce minimum thinking tokens. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: (1) SFT on document and non-document datasets for the VLM agents and on GPT-4o-generated judgment labels for the LLM agents, then (2) RL via GRPO with a mixed reward combining agent-specific process/outcome rewards (VisualPRM, Skywork-VL-Reward) and a global path-level reward to mitigate agent self-interest.

## Results

MACT-MiMo-VL-Series-28B achieves the best average score of 77.2% across 15 benchmarks, followed by MACT-InternVL3-Series-28B (75.3%) and MACT-Qwen2.5-VL-Series-24B (74.8%). All three variants outperform every open-source model under 100B parameters and all closed-source models (GPT-4o-latest 67.2%, Claude-3.7-Sonnet 69.1%, Gemini-2.0-Pro 71.3%), with the MiMo variant leading by 5.6% over the best open-source model (InternVL3-78B, 71.6%) and 5.9% over the best closed-source model. The three MACT variants collectively top 13 of 15 individual benchmarks, with the largest margins on long-context (MMLongBench-Doc: +7.1%) and mathematical reasoning (MathVision: +10.6%) tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MACT-MiMo-VL-Series-28B (77.2% avg) outperforms the strongest closed-source model Gemini-2.0-Pro (71.3%) by 5.9% and the strongest open-source model InternVL3-78B (71.6%) by 5.6%, while using fewer than 30B parameters versus 78B. MACT-InternVL3-Series-28B (75.3%) beats its own family's 78B monolithic model (71.6%) by 3.7% and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (70.5%) by 4.8%. MACT-Qwen2.5-VL-Series-24B (74.8%) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-72B (70.5%) by 4.3% and InternVL3-78B (71.6%) by 3.2%. Against document-specialist baselines, MACT dominates: MDocAgent-39B (59.9%), M3DocRAG-10B (47.7%), and mPLUG-DocOwl2-8B (no avg reported) are all substantially outperformed. The largest individual-benchmark margins appear on MMLongBench-Doc (+7.1% over second-best) and MathVision (+10.6%), indicating the procedural multi-agent design is most beneficial for long-context and reasoning-intensive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MACT extends the multi-agent document understanding line (MDocAgent, M3DocRAG) by introducing a fixed four-agent decomposition—planning, execution, judgment, answer—with an explicit self-correction loop that separates judgment from correction, a design choice motivated by the failure modes of internal correction and combined judge-corrector agents. Its test-time scaling strategy repurposes existing techniques (analogical prompting for parallel plan generation, budget forcing for the judgment agent, reward-model-guided candidate selection for execution) but applies them agent-wise rather than uniformly, which the authors argue is critical for multi-agent architectures. The RL training stage builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) and leverages pretrained process and outcome reward models (VisualPRM, Skywork-VL-Reward) to optimize the collaborative pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides empirical evidence that a ~24–28B multi-agent system can outperform 72–78B monolithic VLMs on document VQA by 3.7–6.6% on average, challenging the assumption that parameter scaling is the primary lever for document understanding. The ablation showing that a single agent with concatenated multi-agent prompts underperforms even the monolithic baseline is a useful negative result, suggesting that true architectural separation (not just prompt engineering) is necessary. Open questions include the total inference-time compute cost of multiple sequential agent calls, whether the framework generalizes to non-document multimodal tasks, and whether the gains persist as base models continue to improve in capability.
