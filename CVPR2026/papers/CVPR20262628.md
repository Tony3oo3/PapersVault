## Problem

Prior work adapts GRPO-based reinforcement learning to visual perception tasks by directly transferring training strategies designed for reasoning-oriented domains (e.g., math), assuming the two settings are interchangeable. This paper shows that assumption is invalid: perception tasks require breadth-first exploration over a much wider output space (multiple visual attributes can support the same answer) and demand fine-grained, scale-calibrated rewards, whereas reasoning tasks naturally induce depth-oriented exploration and tolerate binary rewards. Ignoring these differences leads to suboptimal policy optimization and degraded generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262628/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLLM receives an RGB image paired with a natural-language referring expression or instruction and produces a structured reasoning trace that must contain <look>…</look> tags enclosing visually-grounded observations, a <think>… block, and a final <answer> block in restricted JSON format listing bounding boxes and point prompts for each target object. At inference, these predicted boxes and points are fed as prompts to a frozen SAM2-Large model to generate pixel-level segmentation masks; the VLLM itself never outputs mask pixels. For multi-object scenes (e.g., the COCONut benchmark averaging 5.14 objects per image), the JSON answer contains multiple box–point pairs, and the reward is computed per-object and aggregated.

## Method

Dr. Seg is a plug-and-play GRPO post-training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SAM2-Large that introduces two synergistic components. First, the Look-to-Confirm strategy prompts the model to explicitly tag salient visual evidence with <look> markers during its reasoning trace, forcing breadth-oriented exploration across diverse visual dimensions (shape, material, spatial relations) and preventing the policy from collapsing into a narrow output region; this is reinforced by a dedicated format reward r_look. Second, the Distribution-Ranked Reward replaces raw or binary accuracy signals with a quantile-mapping scheme: each of the three perception metrics (box IoU, count-consistency ratio, and point-distance score) is mapped to its empirical quantile within a fixed-length FIFO history queue (size 2048 ≈ 16 training steps), yielding a scale-invariant, time-adaptive scalar reward that prevents high-variance components from dominating the GRPO gradient. Training runs under the VERL framework for ~500 steps (batch 16, lr 1e-6, 4×H800), and the two components are shown to be individually beneficial but jointly synergistic, achieving state-of-the-art gIoU on 5/6 benchmarks across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings.

## Results

Dr. Seg achieves state-of-the-art gIoU on 5 out of 6 segmentation benchmarks, reaching 80.2 / 76.8 / 74.2 on RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg (in-distribution) and 67.8 / 65.6 on ReasonSeg val / test (out-of-distribution), with an overall average of 74.0. On the newly introduced COCONut multi-object benchmark it scores 79.6 gIoU. For auxiliary tasks, Dr. Seg attains 40.1 AP on COCO detection and 91.5 on PixMo counting, surpassing the prior best (VisionReasoner) by 2.4 AP and 3.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest RL-based baseline VisionReasoner (reproduced), Dr. Seg improves gIoU by +1.2 on RefCOCO, +1.5 on RefCOCO+, +1.7 on RefCOCOg, +2.3 on ReasonSeg-val, +4.1 on ReasonSeg-test, and +1.5 on COCONut, yielding a +2.0 overall average gain. Versus Seg-Zero, Dr. Seg wins on every OOD benchmark (ReasonSeg-val +5.2, ReasonSeg-test +8.1, COCONut +10.3) and on RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg, while trailing by only 0.1 on RefCOCO. All SFT-based VLLMs (LISA, GLaMM, PixelLM, GSVA, OMG-LLaVa) are outperformed by 2–23 gIoU points depending on the benchmark, with the largest gaps on the OOD ReasonSeg and COCONut splits. On the auxiliary detection and counting tasks (Table 3), Dr. Seg surpasses VisionReasoner by 2.4 AP on COCO detection, 4.5 on PixMo counting, and 3.9 on CountBench. Notably, Dr. Seg is the only method to simultaneously achieve SOTA on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution benchmarks, whereas prior RL methods (Seg-Zero, VisionReasoner) typically excel in only one regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VisionReasoner's architecture (decoupling VLLM reasoning from SAM2-based segmentation) and the GRPO optimization framework from DeepSeek-R1, but departs from the broader RL-for-VLLM line (Seg-Zero, Pixel-Think, VisionReasoner) by arguing that reasoning-oriented RL strategies—depth-first exploration and binary rewards—do not transfer to perception-oriented tasks. It extends the entropy-dynamics analysis from Cui et al. and Wang et al. (which studied reasoning LLMs) to visual perception, showing that token-level entropy remains unstable rather than monotonically decreasing. The Distribution-Ranked Reward mechanism is a novel quantile-mapping normalization that addresses a multi-objective reward scaling problem not previously formalized in the VLLM-RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that perception tasks require breadth-oriented exploration and scale-invariant rewards, in contrast to the depth-oriented, binary-reward paradigm inherited from math reasoning—challenges a widely assumed transferability of GRPO training recipes across task types. The Distribution-Ranked Reward is a lightweight, plug-and-play technique applicable to any multi-metric RL setting, and the Look-to-Confirm prompt is a zero-cost architectural intervention. However, the study is limited to a single 7B model (Qwen2.5-VL), a subset of perception tasks, and implicit visual grounding; extending to tracking, video, or point-cloud modalities, and combining implicit reasoning-level visual focus with explicit spatially-adaptive visual tokenization, remain open.
