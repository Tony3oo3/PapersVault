## Problem

Current image editing methods for unified VLMs operate in a decoupled, open-loop fashion—performing static, pre-defined transformations without dynamic feedback between semantic interpretation and visual generation. A fundamental representation gap compounds this: understanding relies on high-level, language-aligned encoders (e.g., CLIP), while generation traditionally operates in low-level pixel-space autoencoders (e.g., VAEs), causing misaligned feature spaces that prevent reliable closed-loop control.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction (e.g., 'change the cat to a dog', 'make it feel autumnal') and produces a single edited RGB image. Internally, the VLM first generates a structured source caption, a scene graph, and a minimal target specification from the instruction; the editing trajectory is then constructed entirely in BLIP3-o's CLIP feature space (T=30 diffusion steps, CFG 2.0/5.5) and decoded to pixels only at the end. Up to 3 UEV refinement iterations are permitted, with early stopping triggered when alignment and completion scores exceed thresholds.

## Method

UniEdit-I reinterprets FlowEdit's inversion-free ODE trajectory construction within the CLIP semantic latent space of BLIP3-o, so that every intermediate state is a clean, decodable image free of the ghosting and deformation artifacts that plague pixel/VAE-space intermediates. On top of this trajectory, a three-stage Understanding–Editing–Verifying (UEV) loop provides closed-loop control: the Understanding stage parses the source image and instruction into structured prompts and a scene graph; the Editing stage applies a dynamic gain α_t = α_base · σ(κ₁Δs_t) · (1−p_t) that adaptively modulates the semantic velocity difference based on real-time alignment improvement and task-completion feedback; and the Verifying stage uses the frozen VLM itself to score intermediate outputs every 5 steps, triggering early stopping or a corrective retry from the best intermediate latent. The entire pipeline is training-free—no fine-tuning, no architectural modifications—relying solely on the frozen BLIP3-o-8B model's own multimodal reasoning to steer the editing process.

## Results

On GEdit-Bench-EN (full set), UniEdit-I achieves an Overall Score (G_O) of 7.06, Perceptual Quality (G_PQ) of 7.40, and Semantic Quality (G_SC) of 7.16, all evaluated via VIEScore on a 10-point scale. This is the best open-source result across all three metrics, surpassing Step1X-Edit (G_O=6.70) and BAGEL (G_O=6.52), and approaching the proprietary GPT-4o (G_O=7.53) without any fine-tuning or architectural modifications. Ablations confirm that the dynamic gain mechanism contributes +0.29 G_O over a fixed-gain baseline, and 97.6% of samples converge within the first UEV iteration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniEdit-I outperforms all open-source baselines on the Overall Score: it beats Step1X-Edit by +0.36 (7.06 vs. 6.70) and BAGEL by +0.54 (7.06 vs. 6.52), while also leading on Perceptual Quality (7.40 vs. 6.76 and 6.83 respectively). On Semantic Quality, UniEdit-I (7.16) ties with OmniGen2 and slightly trails BAGEL (7.36), suggesting its advantage is primarily in visual fidelity rather than pure semantic alignment. The gap to GPT-4o narrows to 0.47 on G_O, despite UniEdit-I requiring no paired edit data or fine-tuning. Notably, UniEdit-I's G_PQ of 7.40 exceeds even GPT-4o's 7.62 by a smaller margin than its G_O gap, indicating the closed-loop verification loop particularly benefits perceptual quality. The paper also reports a noticeable performance drop on the text-change subtask, attributed to limitations in the underlying CLIP representation space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets FlowEdit's inversion-free ODE trajectory construction within the CLIP semantic latent space of BLIP3-o, replacing pixel/VAE-space editing with one that operates on the unified VLM's native generative representation. It departs from prior unified VLM editing methods (Step1X-Edit, BAGEL, GPT-4o) that require large-scale paired-edit training by introducing a training-free Understanding-Editing-Verifying loop that uses the frozen VLM as an active in-process controller. The representation-gap motivation draws on RAEs and BLIP3-o's architectural choice to perform diffusion in high-level semantic features, but the paper's key conceptual departure is treating the VLM's own multimodal reasoning as a real-time feedback signal rather than a post-hoc evaluator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that CLIP-space intermediates are artifact-free (unlike VAE-space ghosting) provides a concrete, testable explanation for why closed-loop VLM feedback is viable in semantic space but unreliable in pixel space—a distinction that could inform future unified VLM design. The training-free SOTA result on GEdit-Bench suggests that structured reflection within a model's own latent space can substitute for large-scale edit-data training, potentially lowering the barrier to deploying editing capabilities. Key open questions include generalizability beyond BLIP3-o to other unified VLMs, scalability to complex multi-object or structural edits, and whether the approach's reliance on the underlying VLM's semantic coverage (its acknowledged weakness on text-change tasks) can be mitigated without fine-tuning.
