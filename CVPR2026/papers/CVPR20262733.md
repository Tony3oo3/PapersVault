## Problem

Point-based hierarchical relocalization methods are limited by sparse database observations: when no stored image has a viewpoint close to the query, reliable feature correspondences cannot be established, degrading pose accuracy. Prior render-based approaches that use Feature Gaussian Splatting (e.g., STDLoc) apply rendered features uniformly across both coarse and fine matching stages, overlooking that rendered features and image-extracted features have complementary strengths at different matching granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262733/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single query RGB image and a pre-trained FGS scene representation (Gaussian primitives with 64-dim compressed features, trained from posed training images initialized by COLMAP SfM). At relocalization time, the FGS renders color images, depth maps, and feature maps from arbitrary viewpoints; the depth map lifts 2D-2D matches to 2D-3D correspondences for PnP. The output is a 6-DoF camera pose (4×4 extrinsic matrix), refined iteratively over up to 4 rounds (2 for outdoor scenes).

## Method

SplatHLoc introduces two key mechanisms on top of an FGS scene representation. First, an adaptive coarse-to-fine viewpoint retrieval: after standard VPR-based retrieval and geometric verification, if inlier count is below a threshold, the retrieved pose is randomly perturbed (within a° and b m), virtual views are rendered from the FGS, and retrieval is re-run over these synthesized keyframes to find a viewpoint closer to the query. Second, a hybrid coarse-to-fine feature matching strategy: rendered FGS features (at H/8×W/8) are used for coarse matching via mutual-nearest-neighbor on a similarity matrix, while a semi-dense matcher (JamMa) extracts fine features from both the rendered and query images (at H/2×W/2) to refine correspondences to sub-pixel accuracy. The FGS feature field is compressed to 64 channels with a 3×3 convolution decoder, reducing map size by ~60% and training time by ~3× compared to STDLoc. Pose refinement iterates by rendering from the current estimate, re-matching, and re-solving PnP with RANSAC.

## Results

On the 7-Scenes indoor benchmark, SplatHLoc achieves a median translation/rotation error of 0.55 cm / 0.17°, outperforming the previous best GS-based method RAPref (0.61/0.20) and STDLoc (0.76/0.24). On 12-Scenes, SplatHLoc reaches 0.3 cm / 0.14° with 97.3% recall at [2 cm, 2°], slightly below ACE+GS-CPR (98.7%) but with lower median error. On the large-scale Cambridge Landmarks outdoor dataset, SplatHLoc attains 9 cm / 0.13° median error, beating STDLoc (10/0.14) and LoGS (10/0.20). The initial pose estimation alone (SplatHLoc_init) already surpasses all structure-based methods and NeuMap on 7-Scenes (2.36/0.64 vs. 3.14/0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplatHLoc achieves the best median error across all three benchmarks, beating the closest GS-based competitor RAPref by 0.06 cm / 0.03° on 7-Scenes (0.55/0.17 vs. 0.61/0.20) and STDLoc by 0.21 cm / 0.07° (0.55/0.17 vs. 0.76/0.24). On 12-Scenes, SplatHLoc's median error (0.3/0.14) is lower than ACE+GS-CPR (0.5/0.21), though its R@[2 cm, 2°] recall (97.3%) is 1.4% below ACE+GS-CPR (98.7%). On Cambridge Landmarks, SplatHLoc (9/0.13) edges out STDLoc (10/0.14) and LoGS (10/0.20), while substantially outperforming NeRF-based methods like NeRFMatch (15/0.29) and PNeRFLoc (36/0.35). The initial pose estimation (SplatHLoc_init) alone already surpasses all structure-based baselines (HLoc 3.31/1.08, DVLAD+R2D2 3.26/1.07) and the regression-based NeuMap (3.14/0.95) on 7-Scenes, demonstrating the benefit of FGS-based novel view synthesis for retrieval. In terms of efficiency, SplatHLoc is nearly 2× faster than STDLoc in iterative pose refinement and uses 61% less GPU memory during FGS mapping (4 GB vs. 12 GB) due to its compressed feature decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

SplatHLoc builds directly on Feature Gaussian Splatting (FGS) as its scene representation, extending the hierarchical relocalization paradigm established by HLoc and Kapture. It departs from prior GS-based relocalization methods (GSplatLoc, LoGS, RAPref, ACE+GS-CPR) by introducing adaptive viewpoint retrieval that synthesizes virtual views around the initially retrieved pose rather than relying on fixed database images or pre-rendered keyframes (as in GPVK-VL). Most critically, it contrasts with STDLoc, the closest prior work, by arguing that rendered features should be used only for coarse matching while a semi-dense matcher's image-extracted features handle fine matching—a hybrid strategy motivated by the feature gap between rendered and query features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SplatHLoc sets new SOTA on both indoor (7-Scenes, 12-Scenes) and outdoor (Cambridge Landmarks) benchmarks while simultaneously reducing FGS map size by ~60% and mapping time by ~3x compared to STDLoc, making GS-based relocalization more practical for deployment. The hybrid matching insight—that rendered features excel at coarse correspondence but a semi-dense matcher's features are superior for pixel-level refinement—provides a reusable design principle for future render-based localization methods. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes (the authors note block-based extension as future work) and dependence on FGS training quality, which is sensitive to the number and coverage of training images.
