## Problem

Joint video super-resolution and low-light enhancement (VSRELL) remains largely unaddressed because existing approaches either cascade LLE and VSR sequentially—suffering from error propagation and parameter redundancy—or use all-in-one models that do not explicitly model the physical coupling between illumination deficiency, noise accumulation, and motion blur. This paper is the first CNN-based method to jointly optimize both tasks in a single end-to-end framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of low-light, low-resolution video frames (e.g., 64×64 training patches from REDS with simulated LL degradation) and produces well-illuminated, high-resolution frames at 4× upsampling. A dynamic temporal window of 3 reference frames is warped to the current frame via SPyNet optical flow before feature extraction, and the network operates with 64 feature channels and 7 residual blocks per branch, yielding a compact 6.29 M parameter model with 0.069 s runtime per frame.

## Method

VSRELL comprises two core modules: (1) INCO (Illumination-Noise Co-Optimization), which uses a dynamic window partitioning strategy to jointly estimate spatially adaptive illumination gain and pixel-level noise maps via parallel illumination and noise branches fused through cross-modulation, avoiding the error accumulation of serial pipelines; and (2) ISFP (Illumination-Sensitive Feature Propagation), which injects a normalized illumination map into deformable convolution offset prediction to adaptively increase alignment amplitude in dark regions, applies Gaussian smoothing to optical flow in low-light areas to suppress noise-induced drift, and employs a dynamic decay unit with illumination-consistency weighting to attenuate unreliable memory features over long temporal spans. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on REDS using Adam (lr=1e-4) and cosine annealing restart over 600k iterations.

## Results

On REDS4, VSRELL achieves an average PSNR of 25.94 dB and SSIM of 0.7813, outperforming the best cascaded baseline (IART+Zero-DCE at 20.12 dB / 0.7760) by 5.82 dB in PSNR and the best All-in-One method (AdaIR* at 19.30 dB / 0.6375) by 6.64 dB. On the generalization datasets Vid4 and UDM10, VSRELL scores 21.42/0.8081 (Vid4-BI) and 23.54/0.8969 (UDM10-BI), again leading all baselines by 1.5–2.8 dB in PSNR. The model uses only 6.29 M parameters and runs at 0.069 s per frame, making it the most efficient method in the comparison.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSRELL outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest cascaded baselines, the gap is most dramatic in PSNR: IART+Zero-DCE (20.12 dB) and IART+KinD (20.53 dB) trail VSRELL (25.94 dB) by 5.8 and 5.4 dB respectively, while their SSIM values (0.7760, 0.7084) are closer but still below VSRELL's 0.7813. Among All-in-One methods retrained on the same data, AdaIR* (19.30 dB / 0.6375), PromptIR* (19.01 / 0.6303), and MoCE-IR* (19.13 / 0.6413) all lag by over 6 dB in PSNR and roughly 0.14 in SSIM, indicating that single-degradation all-in-one models struggle with the coupled LL+LR task. Video LLE cascades (FastLLVE, StableLLVE) perform worst overall (16.3–18.8 dB), confirming that sequential video enhancement followed by SR introduces severe temporal inconsistency. On the unseen Vid4 and UDM10 benchmarks, VSRELL maintains its lead (21.42 vs. 19.96 dB on Vid4-BI; 23.54 vs. 20.69 dB on UDM10-BI), demonstrating strong generalization beyond the REDS training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSRELL carves new ground by jointly optimizing low-light enhancement and video super-resolution within a single CNN framework, departing from both sequential cascaded pipelines (LLE→VSR or VSR→LLE) and generic all-in-one restoration models (PromptIR, AdaIR, MoCE-IR) that the authors argue fail to explicitly model the coupling between illumination deficiency and resolution loss. Architecturally, it builds on the deformable-alignment and feature-propagation paradigm established by BasicVSR++ and EDVR, but injects illumination priors into both the alignment offsets (via illumination-sensitive gating) and the memory propagation weights (via dynamic decay conditioned on illumination consistency). The dynamic window partitioning strategy and the use of pre-trained SPyNet for optical flow follow standard VSR practice, while the INCO module's joint illumination-noise co-optimization is the primary conceptual departure from prior single-task LLE or VSR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—nighttime surveillance, autonomous driving perception, and mobile video quality—where existing VSR methods assume normal lighting and LLE methods ignore resolution. The large PSNR margins over both cascaded and all-in-one baselines (5–6 dB) suggest that explicit modeling of the illumination-resolution coupling, rather than mere multi-task training, is what unlocks performance. However, open questions remain: the evaluation is limited to three datasets with synthetic low-light degradation, the optical flow is a frozen pre-trained SPyNet rather than end-to-end learned, and the authors acknowledge future work on more extreme multi-degradation scenarios and real-time mobile deployment. Whether the illumination-aware propagation strategy generalizes to other coupled-degradation settings (e.g., low-light + motion blur + rain) is untested.
