## Problem

Current video MLLMs encode all frames with a fixed temporal window, causing redundant computation on static content and, more critically, a 'visual confusion' problem when sharply different frames (e.g., scene transitions) are grouped together, leading to hallucinated or erroneous temporal reasoning. No existing method adapts its perception granularity to the actual visual variation present in the video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262263/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-frame video sequences (sampled at 5–10 FPS) or single images. Before encoding, the video is partitioned into scene segments of variable length by the ATS module; each segment is then encoded into a fixed number of spatial tokens (H/p × W/p) regardless of its temporal length, so the total visual token count scales with the number of segments rather than the number of frames. For image inputs, frame repetition is used during training to maintain a consistent spatio-temporal representation. The final output is a text response (caption or answer) generated by the LLM conditioned on the segment-level visual tokens.

## Method

FLEXIVideo introduces two synergistic modules on top of Qwen2.5-VL. First, Adaptive Temporal Segmentation (ATS) computes inter-frame L2 differences against a running reference frame and greedily partitions the sequence into scene segments whose internal variation stays below a threshold τ, yielding longer segments for static content and shorter ones for dynamic content. Second, Dynamical Spatio-Temporal Embedding (DSTE) performs two steps: (i) a temporal extension initialization that replicates and normalizes the pretrained 3D convolutional patch-embedding kernel to a longer temporal length T_init, and (ii) an adaptive temporal encoding that, for each segment of length T_k, resizes the shared kernel via a pseudoinverse of a temporal interpolation matrix so that the convolutional response remains consistent under the change in temporal resolution. This allows a single set of weights to encode segments of arbitrary length, producing a fixed number of tokens per segment (N = Σ_k (H/p)(W/p)) and reducing total visual tokens by up to 43.5% at 10 FPS. The model is fully fine-tuned on 140k image-video samples with a redesigned MRoPE temporal positional encoding that assigns larger positional intervals to static scenes and smaller intervals to dynamic ones.

## Results

FlexiVideo-3B achieves an average improvement of 2.7% over Qwen2.5-VL-3B across six video benchmarks, with the largest gain on FavorBench (+9.7%, 46.8 vs. 37.1) and LongVideoBench (+2.8%, 57.0 vs. 54.2). On MotionBench at 10 FPS, FlexiVideo reduces visual tokens by 43.5% (14,011 vs. 24,098) while improving accuracy by 1.5 points (57.3 vs. 55.5). On Video-MME (w/o subtitles), FlexiVideo sets a new open-source SOTA at 62.5%, surpassing all other open-source models in the comparison. The efficiency analysis (Table 2) shows that on MLVU, FlexiVideo's dynamic encoding achieves 69.6% accuracy with 30,722 tokens, outperforming both the fixed-2 (65.5%, 38,246 tokens) and fixed-6 (60.3%, 12,749 tokens) baselines of Qwen2.5-VL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct base model Qwen2.5-VL-3B, FlexiVideo wins on all six benchmarks, with the most dramatic gain on FavorBench (+9.7 points, 46.8 vs. 37.1) and substantial improvements on LongVideoBench (+2.8) and LVBench (+2.7), confirming that adaptive temporal segmentation and dynamic encoding benefit both motion-sensitive and long-video tasks. Among open-source models of similar scale (2–3B), FlexiVideo leads on Video-MME w/o subtitles (62.5 vs. 62.3 for InternVL2.5-4B), MotionBench (57.3 vs. 55.8 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B), and FavorBench (46.8 vs. 40.9 for Ovis2-2B). Against closed-source models, FlexiVideo still trails GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro on general benchmarks (e.g., Video-MME w/o sub: 62.5 vs. 71.9/75.0), but surpasses GPT-4o on MotionBench (57.3 vs. 33.0) and FavorBench (46.8 vs. 42.1), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on MotionBench (57.3 vs. 51.0). The efficiency comparison in Table 2 shows FlexiVideo's dynamic encoding (14,011 tokens, 57.3% on MotionBench) dominates both fixed-window variants of Qwen2.5-VL: it uses 42% fewer tokens than Fix-2 (24,098) while scoring 1.8 points higher, and 1.9 points higher than Fix-6 (7,822 tokens, 54.4%) at only 1.8× the token cost. On MLVU, FlexiVideo (69.6%, 30,722 tokens) outperforms both Fix-2 (65.5%, 38,246 tokens) and Fix-6 (60.3%, 12,749 tokens), demonstrating that adaptive windowing captures more semantic content per token than any fixed strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexiVideo extends Qwen2.5-VL's fixed 3D convolutional patch embedding by making the temporal window content-adaptive rather than rigid. The core mechanism draws directly from FlexiViT's spatial kernel-resizing idea, transposed to the temporal axis so a single shared 3D convolutional kernel can encode segments of arbitrary length. The Adaptive Temporal Segmentation module, which uses a simple inter-frame L2-difference threshold to partition video into scene segments, departs from prior token-compression and frame-selection approaches by operating at the raw-pixel level before any feature extraction, rather than pruning or merging already-encoded tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 43.5% token reduction with a 1.3% accuracy gain on MotionBench demonstrates that variation-aware encoding can simultaneously improve efficiency and accuracy, a trade-off that fixed-window methods cannot achieve. However, the evaluation is limited to a 3B model with modest training data (140k samples) and untuned hyperparameters, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones or benefits from more aggressive tuning. The visual-confusion diagnosis, while well-motivated, is demonstrated primarily through a single pilot experiment with GPT-4o scoring, and the broader question of whether adaptive temporal granularity generalizes to tasks requiring precise frame-level temporal grounding (e.g., action localization) remains unexplored.
