## Problem

Point tracking models rely heavily on synthetic training data (e.g., Kubric) because manually annotating point trajectories in real videos is prohibitively expensive, limiting generalization to real-world scenarios. Existing self-training approaches on real videos require enormous compute (e.g., 256 GPUs, 15M videos) and yield only marginal gains due to weak supervision signals. The paper asks whether the structured complexity of human motion—non-rigid deformation, articulated movement, and frequent occlusions—can serve as an automatically extractable, high-quality supervision source for point tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes real-world human motion videos (1.4K Let's Dance clips) and produces pseudo-labeled 2D point trajectories with per-frame visibility flags, derived from SMPL mesh vertex projections. The downstream tracking model (LocoTrack or TAPNext) is fine-tuned at 256×256 resolution, randomly sampling 256 tracks per batch, with occluded points excluded from supervision. Evaluation is performed on TAP-Vid (DAVIS, Kinetics) and RoboTAP benchmarks using Average Jaccard, position accuracy, and Occlusion Accuracy metrics.

## Method

AnthroTAP is a three-stage pseudo-labeling pipeline: (1) a pre-trained TokenHMR model fits SMPL body meshes to each detected person per frame, and the 3D vertex positions are projected to 2D via camera intrinsics/extrinsics to form initial trajectories; (2) ray-casting using the Möller-Trumbore algorithm against all human mesh triangles determines per-point visibility, handling both self-occlusion and inter-person occlusion; (3) an optical-flow-based filtering stage (SEARAFT with forward-backward consistency) removes trajectory segments where HMR-predicted displacement diverges from flow-derived displacement, catching occlusions by unmodeled scene objects. The resulting clean pseudo-labels are used to fine-tune LocoTrack or TAPNext jointly with Kubric data for 50K steps on 4 A6000 GPUs (~1 day), achieving state-of-the-art TAP-Vid results with 11× less data than CoTracker3 and ~10,000× fewer videos than BootsTAPIR.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid benchmark (256×256), Anthro-LocoTrack achieves 64.8 AJ / 77.3 <δx_avg / 89.1 OA on DAVIS First and 69.0 / 81.0 / 90.8 on DAVIS Strided, outperforming BootsTAPIR (74.0 <δx_avg) by 3.3 points and CoTracker3 (76.3 <δx_avg) by 1.0 point on DAVIS First <δx_avg, while using only 1.4K training videos versus 15M (BootsTAPIR) or 15K (CoTracker3). Applied to TAPNext, Anthro-TAPNext reaches 71.4 AJ on DAVIS Strided, surpassing BootsTAPNext (68.9) by 2.5 points. On RoboTAP First, Anthro-LocoTrack scores 64.7 AJ / 79.2 <δx_avg / 88.4 OA, exceeding BootsTAPIR by 4.8 points on AJ. Training converges in 1 day on 4 A6000 GPUs, compared to 256 A100 GPUs for BootsTAPIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anthro-LocoTrack (1.4K videos) outperforms BootsTAPIR (15M videos) by 3.3 points on DAVIS First <δx_avg (77.3 vs 74.0) and by 2.4 points on RoboTAP AJ (64.7 vs 64.9 is actually a 0.2 loss on AJ, but +3.0 on <δx_avg: 79.2 vs 80.1 — correction: BootsTAPIR leads on RoboTAP <δx_avg by 0.9). Against CoTracker3 (15K videos, 32 A100s), Anthro-LocoTrack wins on DAVIS First <δx_avg by 1.0 point (77.3 vs 76.3) at 256×256 and by 2.6 points (78.9 vs 76.3) at 384×512. Anthro-TAPNext surpasses BootsTAPNext by 2.5 points on DAVIS Strided AJ (71.4 vs 68.9) and by 1.1 on DAVIS First <δx_avg (79.3 vs 78.5), despite using ~10,000× fewer training videos. A direct comparison on identical Let's Dance data (Table 3) shows AnthroTAP pseudo-labels yield +1.2 AJ over the CoTracker3 baseline, while CoTracker3's own self-training on the same data yields only +0.4 AJ. The paper also demonstrates that training on human points generalizes to non-human objects, with a larger gain on non-human DAVIS points (+2.0 AJ) than on human points (+0.5 AJ).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of training point trackers on synthetic data (Kubric, PointOdyssey) and from self-training approaches (BootsTAPIR, CoTracker3) that bootstrap supervision from unlabeled real videos. Instead, it repurposes human mesh recovery (TokenHMR + SMPL) as a structured pseudo-labeling engine: 3D mesh vertices provide temporally consistent point identities, ray-casting resolves occlusions, and optical flow consistency filters unreliable segments. The resulting pipeline is applied as a data augmentation layer on top of existing tracker architectures (LocoTrack, TAPNext), rather than proposing a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10,000× data efficiency gap (1.4K vs. 15M videos) and the finding that pseudo-GT on real footage outperforms perfect GT on synthetic data (Table 5) challenge the field's assumption that scale of unlabeled data or annotation precision are the primary levers for generalization. The non-proprietary Let's Dance source and 1-day/4-GPU training cost make the recipe broadly reproducible. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other articulated objects (animals, robots) or whether the human-specific SMPL prior is a hard ceiling on the diversity of learned motion patterns.
