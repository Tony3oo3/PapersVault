## Problem

General-purpose monocular depth estimation models exhibit 12–25 mm errors, far exceeding the sub-3 mm precision needed to distinguish a hovering fingertip from one in contact with a surface, while commercial motion-capture systems that achieve sub-millimeter accuracy cost $15,000–50,000+. The paper argues this gap is not architectural but stems from a domain mismatch: existing models are trained on room-scale or outdoor scenes and lack millimeter-level calibration for close-range hand-surface interactions.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a single RGB frame (640×480 or 848×480) from a standard webcam or HMD-mounted camera at 30–60 FPS. MediaPipe extracts 21 hand landmarks per frame; the fine-tuned depth model produces a dense relative depth map that is queried at the five fingertip pixel locations, and a one-time scale calibration (α = d_GT / d_pred) converts relative depths to metric values. The final outputs are a per-fingertip binary contact/hover state and, when contact is confirmed, a keystroke character localized via a homography mapping image coordinates to the printed keyboard layout on the table plane.

## Method

The core contribution is a data-centric strategy: a new 53,300-pair RGB-depth dataset capturing millimeter-scale hand-table typing interactions (collected with an Intel RealSense D405 at 35 cm, 60 FPS, 640×480) is used to fine-tune seven SOTA depth architectures (Depth Anything V2, ZoeDepth, DPT, MiDaS, AdaBins, etc.) with SILog loss, AdamW at lr = 1e-5, cosine annealing over 200 K steps, and no geometric augmentations. The best model (Depth Anything V2-ft) reduces MAE from 12.3 mm to 3.84 mm (68% reduction) and reaches 95.9% δ₁ accuracy. Contact detection layers a velocity-gated state machine with asymmetric depth hysteresis (entry τ = 4.5 mm, exit τ = 6.0 mm) on top of the refined depth: a keystroke fires only when the fingertip-to-RANSAC-plane distance drops below the entry threshold while the vertical velocity profile shows an intentional approach (peak ≥ 15 px/s descent, deceleration < 6 px/s at contact), suppressing false positives from transient hover. This depth-plus-velocity fusion yields a 94.4% F1 contact-detection score and 45.6 WPM / 3.1% CER typing performance using only a commodity RGB camera.

## Results

The best fine-tuned model (DepthAnythingV2-ft) reduces depth MAE from 12.3 mm to 3.8 mm (68% reduction) and achieves 95.9% δ₁ accuracy on the held-out test set. The full depth-and-velocity fusion system attains a 94.4% contact-detection F1-score (94.8% precision, 94.1% recall) with 18 ms per-frame latency. In the user study, the system reaches 38.3 WPM with 5.3% CER for novice users and 45.6 WPM / 3.1% CER for expert users at 848×480 resolution, outperforming all compared VR/AR typing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The full system (45.6 WPM, 3.1% CER) outperforms Meta Quest 3 (35.2 WPM, 8.4%) by +10.4 WPM and −5.3 pp CER, and beats StegoType (39.7 WPM, 7.5%) by +5.9 WPM and −4.4 pp CER. Against TapType/EgoPressure (38.4 WPM, 7.8%), the system gains +7.2 WPM and −4.7 pp CER. TouchInsight (37.0 WPM, 2.9%) is the closest competitor: the proposed method wins on speed (+8.6 WPM) but trails by a narrow 0.2 pp on error rate. UmeTrack and WiLoR report joint-position tracking error (9.4% and 10.2% RMSE) rather than typing metrics, so direct WPM/CER comparison is not possible; however, both require multi-camera or heavier 3D-reconstruction pipelines versus the single-RGB-camera setup here. Overall, the system achieves the highest WPM and among the lowest CER across all directly comparable baselines while requiring only a standard RGB camera plus a fine-tuned depth model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in monocular depth estimation—where progress is driven by architectural innovation (DPT, Depth Anything, diffusion-based Marigold)—by arguing that the bottleneck for close-range fingertip contact detection is a domain gap in training data, not model capacity. It builds on the touch-detection lineage (Wilson's depth thresholding, MRTouch, EgoTouch, TouchInsight, StegoType) but replaces their reliance on dedicated depth sensors with a single RGB camera plus fine-tuned monocular depth. Conceptually, it positions supervised fine-tuning on a purpose-built dataset as a superior strategy to unsupervised domain adaptation (AdaBN, DANN, MMD), which the authors show plateaus at 8.7–9.2 mm MAE, well above the sub-5 mm threshold needed for contact discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 68% MAE reduction (12.3 → 3.84 mm) crosses the critical precision threshold that has kept vision-based contact detection impractical, and the 53,300-pair dataset plus open pipeline lower the barrier for the broader close-range HCI community (surgical simulation, musical interfaces, sign language). The 2×2 factorial decomposition showing that depth and velocity interact multiplicatively (40.5% of total WPM gain) suggests future work should co-optimize perception and temporal dynamics rather than improving depth alone. Key limitations remain: performance degrades to 78.2% F1 on semi-reflective surfaces, evaluation assumes a fixed 35 cm camera distance and single-finger tracking, and physical contact is inferred from geometric proximity rather than validated with pressure sensors.
