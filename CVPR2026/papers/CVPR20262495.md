## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation methods rely on multimodal LLMs with billions of parameters that exceed the computational budgets of edge devices where embodied agents operate. While distillation could compress these models, prior approaches focus on matching output predictions or intermediate features and fail to transfer the multi-step reasoning chains that reasoning segmentation demands. The architectural coupling between perception and reasoning in end-to-end methods further exacerbates this, as visual tokenization fragments the spatial-temporal relationships needed for complex reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method takes an implicit natural-language query (e.g., 'segment the object used to hold hot beverages') and a pre-constructed digital twin representation — a structured JSON per frame containing SAM-2 instance masks, DepthAnything2 depth statistics (mean and variance per instance), and OWLv2 semantic labels with confidence scores and bounding boxes. For videos the digital twin spans T frames; static images are treated as single-frame sequences (T=1). The output is a structured rollout sequence with explicit reasoning analysis, an optional tool-calling revision plan that refines the digital twin, and a final segmentation answer specifying target objects by their mask paths and identifiers within the representation.

## Method

FastReasonSeg decouples perception from reasoning by replacing raw visual token processing with structured digital twin representations, allowing compact LLMs to perform spatial-temporal reasoning over interpretable JSON rather than high-dimensional visual inputs. A larger teacher LLM (Qwen3-8B) is first trained with GRPO reinforcement learning using rule-based format and IoU-based accuracy rewards to generate structured reasoning chains that optionally invoke tool calls for dynamic digital twin refinement. Knowledge is then transferred to student models (Qwen3-1.7B/0.6B) via a two-stage distillation: supervised fine-tuning on teacher-generated reasoning chains (filtered by reject sampling at IoU > 0.7) to establish structured output patterns, followed by reinforcement learning with a novel joint reward combining format correctness, segmentation accuracy, and a reasoning-quality score from an LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o) that evaluates the student's chain against the teacher's in a zero-shot manner. This preserves multi-step reasoning sophistication while reducing parameters by over 20×, achieving 7.79 FPS at 2.1 GB memory for the 0.6B variant.

## Results

On JiTBench (video), FastReasonSeg-8B achieves average region similarity (J) of 0.809/0.782/0.761 and contour accuracy (F) of 0.819/0.814/0.788 across difficulty levels 1–3, surpassing the strongest API-based baseline JiT+GPT-4o by 0.017/0.016/0.014 in J. The distilled 0.6B variant reaches 0.760/0.733/0.714 in J while consuming only 2.1 GB memory and achieving 7.79 FPS. On the image benchmark ReasonSeg, FastReasonSeg-8B attains 0.746 gIoU (short query) and 0.812 gIoU (long query), exceeding JiT+GPT-4o by 0.128 and 0.129 respectively. On LLM-Seg40K, the 8B model achieves 0.641 gIoU / 0.677 cIoU, outperforming all baselines including LLM-Seg on its native benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastReasonSeg-8B outperforms all six baselines across every difficulty level on both JiTBench and RVTBench. Against the strongest API-based baseline JiT+GPT-4o, the 8B teacher gains +0.017 J at Level 1 and +0.014 at Level 3 on JiTBench, while the 0.6B distilled student still exceeds JiT-7B (local) by a margin of 0.246 J at Level 1. The 0.6B model surpasses VISA-13B (0.760 vs. 0.507 J at Level 1) and CoReS-13B (0.760 vs. 0.509 J) by over 0.25 J despite having ~20× fewer parameters. On image segmentation (ReasonSeg), FastReasonSeg-8B leads JiT+GPT-4o by 0.128 gIoU on short queries and 0.129 on long queries; the 0.6B variant still exceeds CoReS-13B (0.696 vs. 0.565 gIoU short). LLM-Seg and LISA show the weakest performance, with LLM-Seg scoring only 0.130 J on RVTBench Level 1, indicating that end-to-end token-based approaches struggle with complex spatial-temporal reasoning. In terms of efficiency, FastReasonSeg-0.6B achieves 7.79 FPS at 2.1 GB memory, compared to 0.89 FPS at 28 GB for LISA-13B and 0.03 FPS for JiT-7B, making it the only method viable for real-time edge deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the digital twin representation paradigm introduced by JiT [32] and the position paper by Shen et al. [27], which decouple visual perception from LLM reasoning through structured intermediate abstractions. It departs from prior reasoning segmentation methods (LISA, VISA, CoReS, SegZero) that either couple perception and reasoning in end-to-end architectures or rely on large LLMs for the reasoning stage, by reframing the problem as a distillation task over explicit reasoning chains. The two-stage distillation (SFT on teacher-generated chains followed by RL with a reasoning-quality reward via LLM-as-judge) extends standard knowledge distillation, which typically matches output distributions or intermediate features, to preserve multi-step reasoning processes that are otherwise lost when compressing LLMs below the parameter threshold where reasoning emerges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling real-time reasoning segmentation on edge devices (7.79 FPS, 2.1 GB memory for the 0.6B variant), which is critical for embodied agents that must operate autonomously without cloud connectivity. A key limitation is that digital twin representations are pre-computed by specialist vision models (SAM-2, DepthAnything2, OWLv2), so the full perception pipeline is not eliminated—only the LLM reasoning component is compressed. Open questions include whether real-time construction and refinement of digital twins is feasible, whether the LLM-as-judge reward (using GPT-4o) introduces a hidden dependency that undermines the edge-deployment motivation, and whether the approach generalizes to domains where pre-computed structured representations are unavailable or insufficient.
