## Problem

Existing omnidirectional 3DGS methods depend on time-consuming SfM to provide camera poses and sparse point priors, while pose-free perspective 3DGS methods fail on panoramas because spherical projection amplifies Gaussian errors near the poles and destabilizes pose gradients during rasterization. Additionally, 3D visual foundation models trained for perspective images cannot directly provide reliable 2D-3D correspondences for panoramic inputs, leaving a gap in pose-free panoramic reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of unposed high-resolution panoramic frames from a 360-degree video, processing them sequentially in a frame-by-frame manner. It initializes the Gaussian model from a monocular depth map (UniK3D) of the first frame, then iteratively estimates each new frame's camera pose and densifies the Gaussian representation. The outputs are a fully optimized 3D Gaussian scene, per-frame camera poses (rotation and translation), and photorealistic novel-view panoramic renderings at arbitrary viewpoints.

## Method

PFGS360 introduces two coupled modules operating in a sequential frame-fusion loop. The Spherical Consistency-aware Pose Estimation (SCA-PE) module renders depth maps from the current Gaussian model for all visited views, computes cross-frame spherical reprojection consistency (tangent angular error + relative depth error) to build reliability masks, extracts SIFT-based 2D-3D correspondences filtered by these masks, and solves for the new camera pose via a PnP solver weighted by a spherical balancing coefficient that encodes both latitude-dependent distortion and cross-frame consistency. A post-optimization refinement step then adjusts visited poses using a photometric loss gated by adjacent-frame consistency masks. The Depth-inlier-aware Densification (DIA-Densify) module aligns monocular depth to the 3DGS scale, identifies high-confidence inliers via normalized cross-correlation between warped and rendered images, merges the corresponding 3D points into the Gaussian model, and prunes or resets opacity of Gaussians flagged as outliers by accumulated inconsistency masks, thereby suppressing depth noise while enriching geometry.

## Results

On OB3D, PFGS360 achieves 35.77 dB PSNR / 0.946 SSIM / 0.057 LPIPS (Egocentric mean) and 30.81 dB PSNR / 0.887 SSIM / 0.113 LPIPS (NonEgocentric mean) for novel view synthesis, surpassing the best pose-free method (3R-GS) by 4.42 dB and 4.06 dB PSNR respectively, and also outperforming pose-aware baselines ODGS and OmniGS that use ground-truth poses. For camera pose estimation, PFGS360 achieves RPE_t of 0.018 (Egocentric) and 0.040 (NonEgocentric), an order-of-magnitude improvement over 3R-GS (0.380 / 1.303) and two orders of magnitude over CF-3DGS and HT-3DGS. On the real-world Ricoh360 dataset, PFGS360 reaches 28.05 dB PSNR, exceeding the second-best method (ODGS, 26.27 dB) by 1.78 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFGS360 outperforms all five baselines on both OB3D subsets and Ricoh360. Against the strongest pose-free competitor 3R-GS [20], PFGS360 gains 4.42 dB PSNR on OB3D-Egocentric (35.77 vs. 31.15) and 7.42 dB on OB3D-NonEgocentric (30.81 vs. 23.39), where 3R-GS degrades sharply due to large viewpoint changes and sparse correspondences. For pose estimation, PFGS360's RPE_t of 0.018–0.040 is roughly 20× lower than 3R-GS (0.380–1.303) and over 100× lower than CF-3DGS (3.181–1.813) and HT-3DGS (3.375–2.199), which suffer from error accumulation in adjacent-frame monocular Gaussian rendering. Against pose-aware methods ODGS [25] and OmniGS [29] that rely on ground-truth SfM poses, PFGS360 still leads by 4.42 dB (Egocentric) and 4.06 dB (NonEgocentric) PSNR, demonstrating that its consistency-aware pose estimation and depth-inlier densification more than compensate for the absence of SfM priors. On real-world Ricoh360, PFGS360 (28.05 dB) beats ODGS (26.27 dB) and OmniGS (26.03 dB) by ~1.8 dB, while all pose-free baselines (CF-3DGS 22.77, HT-3DGS 23.00, 3R-GS 24.18) trail by 4–5 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-free 3DGS line of work (CF-3DGS, HT-3DGS, 3R-GS) from perspective images to omnidirectional/panoramic videos, where prior pose-free methods fail due to spherical projection distortions that destabilize rasterization-based pose gradients. Rather than relying on 3D visual foundation models (MASt3R, DUSt3R) for correspondences as 3R-GS does, or on adjacent-frame monocular Gaussian rendering as CF-3DGS/HT-3DGS do, PFGS360 builds on classical PnP solvers augmented with a spherical consistency-aware weighting derived from cross-frame depth reprojection. The depth-inlier-aware densification module draws on patch-based multi-view stereo (NCC-based inlier selection) and the Mini-Splatting accumulation strategy to filter unreliable monocular depth priors into the Gaussian representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating SfM from 360-degree video reconstruction is practically important for VR content creation, virtual tourism, and interior visualization pipelines where SfM is slow and fragile on panoramic imagery. The method's ability to outperform pose-aware baselines (ODGS, OmniGS) in NVS quality while simultaneously recovering near-ground-truth camera poses suggests that consistency-filtered depth priors can substitute for explicit pose supervision in omnidirectional settings. Limitations include dependence on the quality of the monocular depth model (UniK3D is critical for pose accuracy), evaluation on only two datasets (one synthetic, one real), and the frame-by-frame sequential processing that may accumulate errors over very long sequences. Open questions include scalability to multi-day captures, real-time performance, and whether the spherical consistency framework generalizes to other projection models (e.g., fisheye, cube-mapped).
