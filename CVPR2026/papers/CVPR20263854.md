## Problem

Existing 3D object removal methods neglect global lighting effects, so reflections cast by the removed object on glossy surfaces persist, producing physically implausible results. Additionally, these methods assume view-independent colors for inpainting, which breaks down on non-Lambertian surfaces where radiance varies with viewing direction, causing blurring and ghosting artifacts. GOR-IS is the first object removal framework to explicitly model light transport and perform inpainting in the intrinsic (material + lighting) space.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images (100 views for synthetic scenes, ~200 training views for real scenes) and a target object mask (rendered for synthetic, obtained via SAM2 for real) as input. It produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene with the target object and its reflections removed, with occluded regions inpainted for geometric completeness and visual coherence. The pipeline uses LaMa as the 2D inpainting backbone and requires an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, with 30K optimization steps for scene decomposition followed by 4K steps for inpainting.

## Method

GOR-IS extends RaDe-GS with PBR material properties (diffuse reflection, Fresnel, roughness) and a label attribute, then incorporates a global illumination model combining a 3DGS ray tracer for indirect radiance and an optimizable environment map for direct radiance. Glossy reflections are computed via deferred shading with a screen-space mipmap filtering operator that blurs ideal specular reflections according to surface roughness, avoiding the cost of solving the full rendering equation. For inpainting, the method operates in the intrinsic space: a material inpainting module applies 2D inpainting to view-independent material maps (diffuse, Fresnel, roughness, normal) to correctly complete non-Lambertian surfaces, while a lighting-aware masking mechanism traces reflections originating from the target object's label to identify and suppress reflection-affected pixels during loss computation. Training proceeds in two stages—first optimizing Gaussians and the environment map for scene decomposition, then fixing the environment map and optimizing Gaussians guided by inpainted references with separate appearance loss (Lambertian regions) and material loss (non-Lambertian regions).

## Results

On the GOR-IS-Synthetic dataset, GOR-IS achieves 31.91 dB PSNR and 0.039 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (GScream: 29.92 dB, 0.045 LPIPS) by ~2 dB and ~13% respectively. On the GOR-IS-Real dataset, it reaches 24.52 dB PSNR and 0.101 LPIPS, surpassing the next-best method (GScream: 22.42 dB, 0.109 LPIPS) by over 2 dB. On the SPIn-NeRF dataset (Lambertian scenes with weak global lighting), GOR-IS remains competitive with SOTA methods, achieving 20.15 dB PSNR and 0.240 LPIPS, confirming strong generalization beyond non-Lambertian settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the two datasets with strong global lighting effects (GOR-IS-Synthetic and GOR-IS-Real), GOR-IS achieves the best score on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, M-LPIPS, FID, M-FID) against all six baselines, with the largest margins over SPIn-NeRF (NeRF-based) and InFusion. The closest competitors are GScream and 3DGIC on the Real dataset (PSNR gap ~2 dB) and GScream on the Synthetic dataset (PSNR gap ~2 dB, LPIPS gap ~13%). On the SPIn-NeRF dataset (Lambertian scenes with negligible global lighting), GOR-IS is competitive but not uniformly best: SPIn-NeRF edges it on PSNR (20.55 vs. 20.15) and GScream leads on LPIPS (0.190 vs. 0.240) and FID (29.8 vs. 32.7), while GOR-IS wins on M-LPIPS (0.325 vs. 0.333) and M-FID (122.8 vs. 144.5), indicating its inpainting quality in the target region remains strong even when global lighting effects are absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting-based object removal by building on RaDe-GS for geometric representation and a 3DGS ray tracer for indirect radiance, then layering PBR material properties (diffuse, Fresnel, roughness) and a deferred-shading global illumination model on top. It departs from prior removal methods (3DGIC, GScream, AuraFusion360, InFusion, GS Grouping) that perform 2D inpainting on individual views under a view-independence assumption, instead operating in a disentangled intrinsic space where material properties are inherently view-independent. The screen-space mipmap filtering for glossy reflections and the lighting-aware masking mechanism are novel technical contributions not present in the prior 3DGS or NeRF object-removal literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first object removal framework to explicitly enforce global lighting effects consistency, addressing a physically obvious but previously unaddressed failure mode (residual reflections after object removal). The intrinsic-space inpainting strategy is a generalizable principle that could transfer to other 3D editing tasks involving non-Lambertian surfaces. However, the evaluation is limited to 8 synthetic and 2 real scenes, and the authors acknowledge that diffuse inter-reflections and multi-surface mutual reflections remain unhandled, leaving the approach incomplete for complex reflective environments.
