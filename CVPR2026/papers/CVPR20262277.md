## Problem

Visual query localization (VQL) has been studied almost exclusively in 2D egocentric video, where performance is inherently capped by the 2D plane and cannot resolve ambiguities from occlusion, appearance shifts, or viewpoint changes. No benchmark or method existed for 3D multimodal visual query localization, leaving a critical gap between 2D VQL research and the inherently 3D nature of real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262277/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single query image, a T-frame RGB clip (resized to 448×448), and a synchronized point cloud sequence (resampled to 4096 points, voxelized to a 16×16×16 grid within a fixed workspace W = [0,10]×[-2,2]×[-1,1] m). It outputs a per-frame 9 DoF oriented 3D bounding box (center, size, full rotation including pitch and roll) on a 16³ anchor grid, a presence/consistency score, and the associated response track segment identifying the most recent visible occurrence of the queried object.

## Method

LaF (Lift and Fusion) extends the VQLoC architecture with a depth attention fusion (DAF) module that replaces naive element-wise addition. A Lift module projects each 2D pixel token (from a DINOv2-pretrained ViT) along its viewing ray into 3D voxel space, strictly confined to the camera frustum. DAF then performs perspective-aware multi-head attention slice-wise along the depth axis, with 3D voxel features (from a PV-RCNN-pretrained sparse convolution backbone) as queries and lifted 2D features as keys/values, ensuring geometry-aware cross-modal alignment. A spatial Transformer (STX) fuses the query embedding with frame features via cross-attention, followed by a spatio-temporal Transformer (STTX) with a local time-window mask to model temporal dynamics. A CenterPoint-like head on the 16³ anchor grid regresses 9 DoF parameters (center offset, size, rotation) and a presence score, trained with focal classification loss, F1 regression loss on box parameters, and a distance penalty on positive anchors.

## Results

On the 3DVQL RGB-PC test set, LaF achieves the best performance across all six evaluation metrics: tAP 0.293, tAP@0.25 0.607, stAP 0.044, stAP@0.05 0.222, Recovery 26.4%, and Success 46.04%. The most substantial gains are in 3D spatio-temporal localization, where stAP@0.05 improves by 0.118 over the strongest baseline (PAF) and Success improves by 13.9 points. An ablation removing the Depth Attention Fusion (DAF) module drops stAP from 0.044 to 0.007, confirming that geometry-aware cross-modal attention is the critical component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares LaF against three VQLoC-based baselines that differ only in their cross-modal fusion strategy: AnchorFusion (AF, element-wise addition with anchor-based head), Guided Attention Fusion (GAF, depth-aware cross-attention), and Projection-Aware Attention Fusion (PAF, projecting 3D voxel centers onto the image plane). LaF outperforms all three on every metric. Against the strongest temporal baseline GAF, LaF gains +0.002 tAP and +0.010 tAP@0.25, showing comparable temporal localization. The largest advantages appear in 3D spatial metrics: stAP improves by 0.023 over PAF (0.044 vs 0.021), stAP@0.05 by 0.118 (0.222 vs 0.104), Recovery by 0.149 (0.264 vs 0.115), and Success by 13.9 points (46.04 vs 32.16). This pattern indicates that the baselines struggle primarily with precise 9 DoF 3D pose estimation rather than temporal detection, and that LaF's depth-axis attention mechanism is specifically effective at resolving the geometric alignment between RGB and point-cloud features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D visual query localization (VQL) paradigm established by Ego4D and VQLoC into 3D multimodal space, drawing on the single-stage query-response trajectory architecture of VQLoC [10] as its foundational pipeline. For the 3D side, it repurposes components from autonomous-driving perception: PV-RCNN for point-cloud encoding, CenterPoint-style anchor-based decoding, and a BEV-inspired 'lift' operation (analogous to BEVFusion) to project 2D pixel features into 3D voxel space. The key conceptual departure from prior 2D VQL work is the identification of cross-modal fusion design—not backbone depth—as the primary bottleneck, a finding that inverts the common 2D assumption that deeper, more discriminative features are the main lever for improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3DVQL is the first benchmark to formalize 3D visual query localization with aligned RGB, point-cloud, and depth data, opening a research direction that is directly relevant to embodied AI and robotic manipulation where 2D-only localization is insufficient. The finding that fusion architecture dominates over single-modal backbone quality provides a clear design guideline for future multimodal 3D localization work. However, significant open questions remain: sequences are relatively short (limiting long-horizon evaluation), only RGB-PC fusion is tested (RGB-D is left to future work), and absolute performance is still low (stAP ≈ 0.044, Succ ≈ 46%), indicating the task is far from solved and will require substantially more methodological innovation.
