## Problem

Text-to-3D large models such as TRELLIS exhibit inherent semantic biases: they overfit to a few high-frequency prompt words and fail to understand complex or diverse prompts (e.g., fantastical objects, grouped scenes), producing results that are inconsistent with the intended semantics. No prior work has systematically detected or mitigated such cross-modality bias in text-to-3D generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text prompt as input and ultimately produces a textured 3D mesh as output. Internally, the method generates D meshes per prompt via TRELLIS, scores them with a text-to-3D evaluation model, and backpropagates through the evaluator to obtain per-token gradient magnitudes. The enriched training pairs are constructed by generating new prompts with GPT-4 (preserving identified high/low-importance words) and rendering corresponding 3D meshes via CFD at 512×512 resolution with five focal lengths (3.0–7.5).

## Method

The framework operates in two stages. First, bias localization: TRELLIS generates multiple 3D meshes per prompt, a text-to-3D evaluation model assigns quality scores, and backpropagation through the evaluator yields per-token gradient magnitudes that quantify each word's contribution to the observed bias (both local, per-prompt, and global, dataset-wide). Second, bias mitigation: the most important word w_h and least important word w_l are identified from the gradients; GPT-4 generates diverse context prompts preserving both words (motivated by Invariant Risk Minimization), CFD renders corresponding 3D meshes, and HPSv2 selects semantically faithful meshes via pairwise comparison. The resulting enriched text-3D pairs are used to fine-tune TRELLIS, expanding its semantic coverage while preserving base knowledge. The full pipeline takes approximately 25 hours on a single A6000 GPU, with a training-free variant (using intermediate images to guide TRELLIS-image) reducing cost to ~4 hours.

## Results

On the MATE-3D benchmark (160 prompts), TRELLIS-text w/ ours achieves the highest average overall quality score across all 8 prompt categories, with scores ranging from 5.70 (Action) to 8.19 (Basic). Compared to the prior best method One-2-3-45++, the improvement ranges from +0.12 (Action: 5.70 vs 5.65) to +0.40 (Imaginative: 6.25 vs 6.13), with an average improvement of 0.19. On T³ Bench (300 prompts), the method achieves an average score of 50.2 for single objects, 47.8 for single objects with surroundings, and 37.5 for multiple objects, outperforming all baselines including ProlificDreamer (49.4, 44.8, 35.8) and the base TRELLIS-text (44.8, 43.4, 28.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (TRELLIS-text w/ ours) outperforms all 10 baselines on MATE-3D across every prompt category. Against the strongest competitor One-2-3-45++ [18], it wins by 0.12–0.40 points (e.g., 8.19 vs 7.79 on Basic, 6.95 vs 6.60 on Fantastic). Against the base model TRELLIS-text [35], the improvement is larger on complex and multi-object categories (e.g., +0.91 on Grouped: 6.64 vs 5.73; +0.68 on Imaginative: 6.25 vs 5.57), confirming that the bias mitigation is most impactful where the base model struggles. On T³ Bench, the method surpasses ProlificDreamer [32] (the strongest SDS-based baseline) by +0.8 average on single objects (50.2 vs 49.4) and +1.7 on multiple objects (37.5 vs 35.8), and outperforms TRELLIS-text [35] by +5.4 on multiple objects (37.5 vs 28.5), indicating the largest gains in the hardest multi-object setting. Older SDS-based methods (DreamFusion [25], Magic3D [17], SJC [31], LatentNeRF [22]) trail by 2–5 points on most categories, reflecting their inherent cross-view inconsistency. CFD [40] used as a generation component in the pipeline achieves 8.34 on Basic without fine-tuning, slightly above the full method's 8.19, but the full method's advantage lies in its broader category coverage and the fine-tuning-based semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRELLIS as its backbone, fine-tuning it with enriched text-3D pairs rather than proposing a new generation architecture. It departs from the dominant line of text-to-3D work (DreamFusion, Magic3D, SJC, One-2-3-45++) that focuses on improving generation fidelity through better distillation objectives or multi-view consistency, instead targeting a data-level problem: word-level semantic bias in the model's text understanding. The bias detection mechanism draws on gradient-based attribution and the Invariant Risk Minimization framework from the 2D fairness literature (FairDiffusion, ITI-GEN), repurposing these ideas for the 3D setting where paired text-3D data is scarce. GPT-4 is used as a prompt diversification tool, and CFD serves as the 3D generation engine for creating enriched training pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical and previously unaddressed failure mode in text-to-3D large models: overfitting to a small set of high-frequency prompt words while underperforming on complex or rare semantic descriptions. The gradient-based word-level bias localization offers a diagnostic tool that could generalize beyond TRELLIS to other text-to-3D architectures. However, the approach is tightly coupled to TRELLIS and a specific evaluation model (MATE-3D), and the computational cost (~25 hours for fine-tuning plus sample generation) limits immediate practicality. Open questions include whether the bias mitigation transfers to unseen prompt distributions, whether it generalizes to other 3D generation backbones, and whether the modest score gains (0.12–0.4 on MATE-3D) scale to larger or more diverse evaluation sets.
