## Problem

Existing query-based RVOS pipelines learn trajectory consistency and multimodal fusion from scratch in a single multi-stage architecture, leading to two coupled failures: temporally inconsistent object tracking under occlusion and fast motion, and insufficient semantic understanding of dynamic action-relationship expressions. The paper asks how to explicitly model trajectory consistency and achieve deep cross-modal alignment without entangling both objectives in one end-to-end pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution video clip (T=8 frames at 640×640 for the frozen DVIS++ trajectory generator, downsampled to 320×320 for the BEiT-3 multimodal encoder) together with a natural language referring expression. It produces per-frame refined segmentation masks from the DVIS++ refiner and, critically, trajectory relevance scores S_f ∈ R^{T×Q×2} that score each detected instance trajectory against the expression; these scores are matched to ground-truth trajectories via the Hungarian algorithm during training. The trajectory generator remains frozen throughout training, so only the alignment/selection module and the refiner are updated.

## Method

DeRVOS decouples RVOS into a frozen consistent-trajectory-generation branch (DVIS++ pretrained on OVIS, producing temporally consistent object features Q_f and mask features) and a multimodal-understanding branch (pretrained BEiT-3 performing early-stage visual-textual alignment at the image level). The two branches are connected by the Trajectory Alignment and Implicit Selection (TAIS) module: first, the Cross-Frame Multimodal Aligner (CFMA) performs a ternary interaction—bidirectional attention between fused visual and word-level text features, followed by sequential cross-attention of object queries against enhanced visual and textual features—establishing frame-level alignment; second, the Motion-Guided Implicit Selector (MGIS) reshapes aligned features along the temporal axis, applies temporal self-attention to capture cross-frame dynamics, and then cross-attends against motion-aware semantic features (derived from verbs/adverbs in the expression) to implicitly select the target trajectory. Training uses only a classification loss with Hungarian matching for RVOS, while an optional image-level pretraining stage on RefCOCO/+/g adds a mask loss (dice + BCE) on the refiner, reducing learnable parameters to ~179M and training time to ~9 hours compared to prior methods.

## Results

On the MeViS val set, DeRVOS achieves a J&F score of 51.8%, surpassing the previous best expert method ReferDINO (49.3%) by 2.5 points. On the more challenging MeViS val^u set, it reaches 60.6% J&F, outperforming DMVS (58.3%) by 2.3 points. With image-level pretraining (DeRVOS†), the method achieves 56.0% J&F on MeViS val, exceeding the strongest LVLM baseline GLUS (51.3%) by 4.7 points. On Ref-YouTube-VOS and Ref-DAVIS17, DeRVOS attains 70.0% and 70.9% J&F respectively, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeRVOS outperforms all 22 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the strongest expert method ReferDINO [44], it gains +2.5% J&F on MeViS val, +0.7% on Ref-YouTube-VOS, and +2.0% on Ref-DAVIS17. Compared to the prior best on MeViS val^u (DMVS [25], 58.3%), DeRVOS improves by +2.3% J&F. In the LVLM comparison, DeRVOS† surpasses GLUS [45] by +4.7% J&F on MeViS val and VISA-7B [74] by +3.7% on MeViS val^u, while using far fewer parameters. On RefCOCO/+/g for referring image segmentation, DeRVOS achieves 80.2/74.9/76.1 mIoU, beating SAMWISE [13] by an average of 6.7% and GSVA-13B [72] by up to 4.6% on RefCOCO+. Efficiency-wise, DeRVOS uses ~170M fewer learnable parameters, 16 GB less GPU memory, and trains 11 hours faster than DsHmp [30] while simultaneously improving J&F by 5.4–6.0% across the three RVOS benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeRVOS departs from the dominant query-based multi-stage pipeline paradigm (exemplified by DsHmp, SSA, and ReferDINO) by decoupling RVOS into two independently pretrained sub-tasks: consistent trajectory generation via DVIS++ and multimodal understanding via BEiT-3. Rather than learning trajectory consistency and cross-modal fusion jointly from scratch, the framework reduces RVOS to a trajectory-to-expression matching problem, connected by the novel TAIS module. This design is conceptually aligned with the broader trend of leveraging powerful pretrained foundation models (DVIS++ for spatio-temporal modeling, BEiT-3 for vision-language alignment) but applies the decoupling principle more aggressively than prior methods like DMVS or SAMWISE, which integrate pretrained trajectory models within a still-unified training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled architecture offers a practical recipe for RVOS practitioners: by freezing the trajectory generator and multimodal encoder, training becomes substantially cheaper (179M vs. 346M learnable parameters, 9 vs. 20 hours) while simultaneously improving accuracy, suggesting that the bottleneck in prior methods was not model capacity but the entangled optimization of trajectory consistency and semantic understanding. The 4.7% J&F gain over the strongest LVLM baseline (GLUS) on MeViS is notable, though the comparison spans fundamentally different architectures. Open questions include whether the frozen trajectory generator limits adaptability to out-of-distribution motion patterns, and whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to other video understanding tasks beyond segmentation.
