## Problem

Cross-subject fMRI visual decoding models trained on multiple source subjects degrade significantly when fine-tuned on a new subject with limited data (~1 hour), because the fine-tuning process destroys the learned brain-to-image correspondence. Two specific failures are identified: (1) stimulus-level inconsistency, where class boundaries in the embedding space blur after adaptation, and (2) subject-level misalignment, where >90% of stimuli differ across subjects in NSD, making direct one-to-one alignment infeasible and preventing consistent inter-subject correspondence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261488/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI voxel responses (13,000–18,000 voxels from selected ROIs) from a new subject along with stimulus category labels, and is fine-tuned using only a single 750-trial scanning session (~1 hour, 2.5% of the full NSD dataset). Voxel responses are first projected via subject-specific ridge regression into a 4096-dim shared latent space, then mapped by an MLP backbone to OpenCLIP ViT-bigG/14 token space (256×1664). Outputs include reconstructed images generated through a frozen diffusion prior, plus bidirectional retrieval scores (image-to-brain and brain-to-image matching) computed via lightweight MLP submodules.

## Method

Duala introduces a dual-level alignment framework on top of the MindEye2 architecture (ridge regression → MLP backbone → diffusion prior + retrieval heads), using LoRA (rank 8) adapters for efficient fine-tuning. At the stimulus level, a Semantic Preservation module applies (a) a triplet loss with cosine similarity to enforce intra-class proximity and inter-class separation within the new subject, and (b) a relational consistency loss that aligns the new subject's class-prototype similarity matrix with a reference matrix aggregated from all source subjects. At the subject level, a Distribution Perturbation module decomposes each embedding into a category-mean (stimulus-driven) and deviation (subject-specific) component, then applies element-wise Gaussian scaling using source-subject deviations to simulate plausible cross-subject variability during training. The combined fine-tuning objective adds both alignment losses (weighted λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.1) to the original decoding loss (diffusion prior + contrastive retrieval + low-level reconstruction), requiring only 4.68M trainable parameters versus 2.2G for full MindEye2 fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD) with only ~1 hour of fMRI data per subject, Duala achieves 84.5% image-to-brain retrieval accuracy and 81.1% brain-to-image retrieval accuracy (averaged across subjects 1, 2, 5, 7), outperforming the strongest baseline MindTuner by 1.4% and 5.1% respectively. For reconstruction, Duala attains the best Pixel Correlation (0.230), AlexNet-2 similarity (87.9%), Inception similarity (85.4%), and CLIP similarity (83.5%). The method uses only 69.09M trainable parameters during fine-tuning, far fewer than MindEye2 (2.2B) or MindTuner (76.71M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Duala is compared against three cross-subject decoding baselines: MindEye2 (ICML 2024), MindAligner (ICML 2025), and MindTuner (AAAI 2025). Against MindEye2, Duala achieves a dramatic improvement in brain retrieval (81.1% vs. 57.4%, +23.7%) and image retrieval (84.5% vs. 79.0%, +5.5%), indicating that the shared-subject ridge-regression baseline suffers severe degradation when fine-tuned on a new subject. Against MindAligner, Duala leads on image retrieval (84.5% vs. 79.0%) and brain retrieval (81.1% vs. 75.3%), as well as on Pixel Correlation and Inception similarity. Against the strongest baseline MindTuner, Duala wins on image retrieval (84.5% vs. 83.1%, +1.4%) and brain retrieval (81.1% vs. 76.0%, +5.1%), and also surpasses it on Pixel Correlation (0.230 vs. 0.224), AlexNet-2 (87.9% vs. 87.8%), and Inception (85.4% vs. 84.8%), while matching on CLIP (83.5%). The gains are consistent across all four NSD subjects, with the largest per-subject brain-retrieval improvement on Subject 2 (91.47% vs. 82.80% for MindTuner). Duala also uses fewer trainable parameters (69.09M) than MindTuner (76.71M) and far fewer than MindEye2 (2.2B), demonstrating superior parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Duala builds directly on the MindEye2 architecture (ridge regression → MLP backbone → diffusion prior) and adopts MindTuner's LoRA-based fine-tuning strategy, positioning itself as a fine-tuning framework rather than a new decoder. Its key departure from prior cross-subject methods (MindEye2, MindAligner, MindTuner) is the diagnosis that naive fine-tuning destroys stimulus-level semantic structure and fails to account for subject-specific distributional shifts, and the prescription of a dual-level solution: a triplet-based semantic alignment loss plus a relational consistency loss at the stimulus level, combined with a covariance-driven distribution perturbation at the subject level. Conceptually, it reframes cross-subject adaptation as a joint problem of preserving inter-class geometry while accommodating individual neural variability, rather than purely projecting into a shared latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard fine-tuning causes a 41% retrieval drop and blurs class boundaries provides a concrete diagnostic for why cross-subject fMRI decoders fail on new subjects, and the dual-level fix is a practical recipe for the field. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (NSD) and four subjects, and the improvements over MindTuner, while consistent, are modest (1.4% image retrieval, 5.1% brain retrieval). Open questions include whether the distribution perturbation strategy generalizes to other brain regions, other stimulus modalities (video, natural scenes beyond MSCOCO), or to settings with even fewer data points than one hour. The parameter-efficiency story (69M trainable vs. 2.2G for MindEye2) is attractive for deployment but relies on freezing the diffusion prior, which may limit reconstruction quality ceiling.
