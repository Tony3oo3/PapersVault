## Problem

Conventional motion segmentation methods rely on noisy intermediate cues (optical flow, point correspondences, epipolar constraints) and iterative optimization to disentangle object motion from camera motion, leading to cumulative errors and high computational cost (6–8 s/frame). The paper asks whether motion segmentation can be performed in a purely feed-forward manner by directly learning from latent 4D geometric representations, eliminating both explicit correspondence estimation and iterative refinement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260859/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an N-frame video sequence (frames center-cropped and resized to 518×518), from which it extracts per-image DINOv2 features, RAFT optical flow, and π³ latent 4D geometry features plus camera pose embeddings. It outputs coarse per-pixel motion masks at the feature resolution, which are then upscaled to full-resolution segmentation masks via SAM2 prompt-based refinement at test time. Inference runs at 0.31 s/frame, roughly 20× faster than iterative baselines like RoMo (8.34 s) and SegAnyMotion (6.44 s).

## Method

GeoMotion comprises a frozen feature aggregation module and a trainable motion decoder. The aggregation module fuses three modalities via a single MLP: (1) latent 4D geometry features F_geo extracted from the 5th, 15th, 35th, and 36th layers of a 36-layer π³ alternating-attention backbone (initialized from pretrained weights and kept frozen), (2) camera pose embeddings F_cam from the π³ camera pose decoder, and (3) local optical flow features F_flow from a CNN applied to RAFT flow. The motion decoder consists of 5 self-attention layers followed by a lightweight MLP head, initialized from the π³ confidence decoder to leverage large-scale geometric pretraining. The key insight is that the model implicitly disentangles object motion from camera motion through attention over the fused spatio-temporal representation, bypassing explicit correspondence matching entirely. Training uses Focal Loss + Dice Loss (λ₁=λ₂=0.5) over N frames, supervised on five dynamic-scene datasets, with only the motion decoder parameters updated.

## Results

GeoMotion achieves 83.9 J&F on DAVIS2016-M, 84.7 J&F on DAVIS2016, 81.1 J on DAVIS2017, and 77.3 J on SegTrackV2, outperforming the best non-iterative baseline (RCF-Stage1) by 6.6 and 6.2 J&F points on DAVIS2016-M and DAVIS2016 respectively. Against reconstruction-based methods, GeoMotion reaches 84.5 J_M / 96.1 J_R on DAVIS2016, surpassing the best reconstruction baseline (Easi3R_monst3r) by +13.8 J_M. Inference runs at 0.31 s/frame, roughly 20× faster than iterative methods like RoMo (8.34 s) and SegAnyMotion (6.44 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among non-iterative methods, GeoMotion clearly leads: it surpasses RCF-Stage1 (77.3 J&F) by 6.6 points on DAVIS2016-M and ABR (72.0 J&F) by 11.9 points on the same benchmark. Against iterative-optimization baselines, GeoMotion beats OCLR-TTA by 5.4 J&F on DAVIS2016-M and achieves the best SegTrackV2 score (77.3 J vs. 76.3 for SegAnyMotion), though it trails SegAnyMotion on DAVIS2016-M (83.9 vs. 89.5 J&F) and DAVIS2016 (84.7 vs. 90.9 J&F) where iterative SAM2 prompting provides additional refinement. On FBMS-59, ABR's appearance-based refinement gives it a J advantage (81.9 vs. 72.5), a gap the authors attribute to GeoMotion's geometry-driven design being more conservative in appearance-saliency-dominated scenes. Compared to reconstruction-based methods (DUSt3R, MonST3R, VGGT4D, Easi3R), GeoMotion improves J_M by 11.7–25.9 points across DAVIS benchmarks, demonstrating that learned motion decoding from 4D features substantially outperforms post-hoc motion extraction from reconstruction outputs. In terms of speed, GeoMotion (0.31 s/frame) is comparable to other feed-forward methods (ABR 0.28 s, OCLR-flow 0.20 s) while being 20–27× faster than iterative baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoMotion builds directly on the alternating-attention architecture of π³ and VGGT, repurposing their frozen latent 4D geometry features and camera pose decoder as input to a lightweight learned motion decoder. It departs from the dominant iterative-optimization paradigm (RoMo, SegAnyMotion) that relies on explicit optical flow, epipolar constraints, and point correspondences, and from feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods (Easi3R, VGGT4D) that use geometry to produce explicit motion estimates rather than learning segmentation directly from geometric representations. The key conceptual shift is treating 4D reconstruction features as a rich, pre-computed motion prior that a small self-attention decoder can read out, eliminating the multi-stage correspondence pipeline entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive accuracy at 0.31 s/frame versus 6–8 s/frame for iterative baselines, GeoMotion makes motion segmentation practical for real-time and large-scale deployment, and it validates that latent 4D geometry can substitute for explicit correspondence estimation. A notable limitation is the underperformance on FBMS-59, where appearance-driven boundaries dominate and geometry-based cues are weaker, suggesting the approach may struggle in low-motion, high-texture scenarios. Open questions include whether the SAM2 refinement step at test time can be eliminated, whether the method generalizes to articulated or non-rigid motion beyond the rigid-object benchmarks used here, and whether the frozen-backbone design limits ceiling performance as 4D reconstruction models improve.
