## Problem

Frame-based radar perception pipelines require processing all chirps in a frame through FFTs and dense 3D backbones, fixing latency to the full frame interval and demanding heavy computation on large range-angle-Doppler tensors. Lightweight sequential alternatives reduce latency but typically compress receiver channels too early or fail to disentangle Doppler-division-multiplexed transmit contributions, destroying the virtual MIMO array structure needed for accurate angle estimation and detection. The paper addresses both gaps: preserving explicit MIMO spatial structure in a streaming encoder while enabling sub-frame early decisions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw complex I/Q ADC samples organized as (N_c × N_s × 2·N_rx) tensors, processed chirp-by-chirp in a streaming fashion without constructing range-angle-Doppler cubes. On RADIal the input is a 256×512×16 complex cube (12TX×16RX DDM, 192 virtual antennas); on RaDICaL it is 64×192×8 (4RX×2TX TDM). Outputs are a BEV detection map (per-pixel classification scores plus range/azimuth box offsets) and a binary BEV freespace segmentation mask, both produced by lightweight CNN decoders on a projected T×H×W grid.

## Method

RAVEN is a five-stage streaming encoder: (1) independent per-RX Mamba SSMs compress each receiver's fast-time I/Q sequence into a 2-D token, preserving per-antenna phase and amplitude; (2) a cross-antenna attention module uses learnable TX queries as steering vectors to probe RX tokens, forming compact virtual-MIMO pair features without FFTs or RAD tensors; (3) a chirp-wise slow-time SSM backbone reads the chirp sequence online, maintaining a compact hidden state for anytime inference; (4) a learned projection maps sequence features to a T×H×W spatio-temporal grid; (5) shallow Conv-LN-SiLU decoders produce detection heatmaps/boxes and segmentation logits. Training uses multi-prefix (Matryoshka-style) supervision across chirp-prefix lengths so the model converges on early chirps. At inference, a calibrated minimum-cosine-distance stopping rule on the SSM latent state triggers early exit once the state saturates, reducing encoder FLOPs and end-to-end latency by up to 170× and 4× respectively versus frame-based baselines while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Results

On RADIal, RAVEN (full-frame) achieves mIoU 0.90, F1 0.93, mAP 0.95, mAR 0.92, range error 0.12 m, and azimuth error 0.10° at only 1.02 GMACs and 1.51 M parameters (20.08 ms latency). On RaDICaL, RAVEN reaches Dice 0.997 and Chamfer distance 0.082 at just 0.053 GMACs and 0.347 M parameters. The sub-frame variant (early-exit) retains mIoU 0.85 and F1 0.89 while cutting compute to 0.27 GMACs and latency to 9.15 ms, representing up to 170× lower FLOPs than the heaviest transformer baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVEN (full-frame) outperforms all 14 baselines on RADIal segmentation (mIoU 0.90 vs. 0.82 for TransRadar, 0.79 for T-FFTRadNet/ADCNet/SSMRadNet) and detection (mAP 0.95, mAR 0.92, RE 0.12 m, AE 0.10°), while using 170× fewer GMACs than TransRadar (1.02 vs. 171.5) and 95× fewer than T-FFTRadNet (1.02 vs. 97). Against the closest SSM baseline SSMRadNet, RAVEN gains +0.11 mIoU, +0.16 F1, +0.12 mAP, and +0.21 mAR at comparable compute (1.02 vs. 1.67 GMACs). On RaDICaL, RAVEN achieves the best Dice (0.997) and second-best Chamfer (0.082 vs. FFT-RadNet's 0.076) at 790× lower compute (0.053 vs. 41.74 GMACs) and 12× fewer parameters. The sub-frame early-exit variant trades ~5 mIoU points for a 3.8× latency reduction (9.15 ms vs. 20.08 ms) and 3.8× fewer GMACs, still surpassing SSMRadNet on every accuracy metric. RAVEN matches or exceeds EchoFusion on F1 (0.93 vs. 0.93), mAR (0.92 vs. 0.92), and RE (0.12 vs. 0.12) while improving AE (0.10° vs. 0.18°) and reporting mIoU (0.90) where EchoFusion does not.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAVEN extends the chirp-wise sequential radar processing paradigm introduced by ChirpNet [28,29] by addressing its two key weaknesses: premature RX-channel mixing that discards MIMO spatial structure, and failure to disentangle Doppler-division-multiplexed (DDM) transmit components. It repurposes Mamba-style state space models [6,7,31] as both fast-time per-antenna encoders and a slow-time chirp-sequence backbone, inserting a lightweight cross-antenna attention module (framed as a learnable beamformer) between them to recover virtual-array features without constructing range-angle-Doppler tensors. The sub-frame early-exit mechanism draws on the anytime-inference literature (MSDNet, DeeBERT) but applies it at the chirp level using a calibrated cosine-distance stopping rule on SSM latent states, a setting not previously explored in radar perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 170× compute reduction (1.02 GMACs vs. 171.5 GMACs for TransRadar) while matching or exceeding SOTA accuracy on RADIal makes high-resolution radar perception viable on embedded automotive platforms, where frame-based FFT pipelines are infeasible. The physics-inspired design principle—preserving per-RX phase structure and explicit TX separation before any spatial mixing—offers a general recipe for array-signal processing that could transfer to other MIMO sensing modalities. Open questions include whether the calibrated early-exit threshold generalizes across driving conditions and sensor configurations, how the approach performs in multimodal fusion stacks, and whether the sub-frame accuracy gap (mIoU 0.85 vs. 0.90) is acceptable for safety-critical deployment.
