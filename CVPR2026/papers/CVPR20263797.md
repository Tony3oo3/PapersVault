## Problem

Text-to-video generation models lack fine-grained spatial and motion control: global prompts cannot specify where objects appear or how they move, while existing structure-based controls (bounding boxes, masks) are rigid and labor-intensive to author. Point-based trajectory controls work well in image-to-video where the source image fixes entity identity, but in text-to-video the entity linked to each trajectory is not predetermined, causing ambiguous grounding, identity swaps, and off-target motion in multi-entity scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse 2D point trajectories (each with per-frame visibility flags), a local text description paired with each trajectory, and a global scene prompt as input, and produces a generated video at 832×480 resolution, 81 frames, 16 fps. Trajectories are sparse (≤5 tracks in the final training stage) with Gaussian-weighted neighborhoods (σ=1, radius r=2) defining the spatial region each local text governs. The same framework supports video-to-video mirroring and text-driven local editing by extracting dense trajectories and captions from a reference clip.

## Method

TGT introduces Location-Aware Cross-Attention (LACA), a lightweight plug-in cross-attention branch added to each DiT block of the pretrained Wan2.1 14B backbone. LACA routes each visual token to either its trajectory's local text feature (if the token falls within the Gaussian-weighted neighborhood of a visible trajectory point) or to the global prompt feature, enabling spatially targeted entity grounding without modifying the backbone. A dual-CFG scheme applies independent classifier-free guidance scales for global and local text, plus a hyperparameter λ to balance the two cross-attention branches at inference. Training uses a flow-matching velocity-prediction objective, optimizing only the LACA modules in two stages (dense then sparse trajectories) on 2.4 million curated clips. A data pipeline constructs trajectory-text supervision by segmenting entities with Grounded SAM, generating localized captions via a GPT-4o-distilled Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and propagating points across frames with TAP.

## Results

On the DAVIS benchmark, TGT achieves the best local CLIP-T score (0.2531) and the lowest End-Point Error (EPE) of 25.11, nearly halving the EPE of the strongest trajectory-based baseline Tora (47.41). TGT's global CLIP-T (0.3314) is competitive with the uncontrolled Wan2.2 14B model (0.3408), confirming that trajectory control does not sacrifice overall video quality. In user studies, TGT is preferred over all baselines on visual quality, motion control, and prompt control (GSB scores of +96.7, +61.7, and +68.3 vs. MotionCtrl; +98.3, +78.3, and +81.7 vs. TrailBlazer).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGT outperforms all four baselines on local CLIP-T and EPE simultaneously. Against the strongest trajectory-based method Tora (EPE 47.41, local CLIP-T 0.2423), TGT reduces EPE by nearly 47% (to 25.11) while improving local CLIP-T by 0.011. Compared to TrailBlazer (bounding-box based, EPE 65.15), TGT achieves a 61% reduction in EPE and higher local CLIP-T (0.2531 vs. 0.2408), while also matching or exceeding its global CLIP-T. Against MotionCtrl (EPE 74.33), TGT cuts EPE by ~66% and improves both global and local CLIP-T. The uncontrolled Wan2.2 14B model retains the highest global CLIP-T (0.3408 vs. 0.3314 for TGT), but its EPE of 265.03 is over 10× worse than TGT's, confirming that text prompts alone cannot provide fine-grained motion control. In user studies, TGT is preferred over every baseline on all three axes (visual quality, motion control, prompt control), with the largest margins against TrailBlazer (+98.3 visual quality, +81.7 prompt control).

## Relation to Prior Work

TGT sits at the intersection of two established lines of controllable video generation: structure-based methods (bounding boxes, blob shapes, edge maps) that enforce spatial layout but are rigid and labor-intensive, and point-trajectory methods (MotionCtrl, Tora, TrailBlazer) that offer intuitive motion handles but suffer from ambiguous entity grounding in the text-to-video setting where identity is not fixed by a source image. The key departure is pairing each trajectory with a localized text description, a control signal that has not been previously combined in T2V. Architecturally, the method is a lightweight plug-in (LACA) on top of a pretrained DiT backbone (Wan2.1), and the training data is synthesized via a pipeline combining Grounded SAM, TAP point tracking, and a VLM distilled from GPT-4o annotations—repurposing existing tools rather than introducing new tracking or segmentation primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling motion specification (trajectories) from entity identity (local text), TGT offers a practical, user-friendly control interface for multi-object T2V that prior methods could not achieve without dense frame-level supervision. The dual-CFG mechanism provides a clean, interpretable knob for trading off global fidelity against local motion precision, which is likely to be adopted in downstream controllable generation pipelines. Open questions include scalability to longer videos and higher resolutions, robustness in heavily occluded or crowded scenes, and whether the 2M-clip training set (curated from an internal dataset) is sufficient for open-domain generalization beyond the DAVIS evaluation. The reliance on GPT-4o for teacher annotations in the data pipeline also raises questions about cost and bias propagation at scale.
