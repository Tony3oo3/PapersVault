## Problem

Test-Time Training (TTT) reformulates attention as an online learning problem, offering a flexible linear-complexity design space, but the key design choices for visual TTT—inner loss function, learning rate, batch size, epochs, and inner model architecture—lack systematic understanding and practical guidelines. This underexplored design space has prevented the construction of powerful visual TTT models that can match or exceed other O(N) sequence-modeling approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images patchified into token sequences of length N with dimension C (e.g., 196 tokens at 224×224 with 16×16 patches, or up to 6,084 tokens at 1248×1248). For classification it outputs a 1000-way class label; for generation (DiT³) it takes a class label and produces a 256×256 image; for detection and segmentation it outputs bounding boxes with instance masks (COCO) or pixel-wise semantic masks (ADE20K) via standard Mask R-CNN and UPerNet heads. The architecture is available in both non-hierarchical (ViT³) and 4-stage hierarchical (H-ViT³) variants, with the latter reducing token count at deeper stages to handle high-resolution dense-prediction inputs.

## Method

ViT³ replaces each Softmax attention block with a TTT layer that compresses key-value pairs into the weights of a compact inner model via one epoch of full-batch gradient descent (learning rate 1.0, dot-product loss), then applies the updated weights to queries. The inner model is a hybrid of two modules identified through systematic ablation: a gated linear unit F₁ = FC(x)⊙SiLU(FC(x)) used in most heads, and a 3×3 depthwise convolution F₂ = DWConv(x) used in one head per block, which naturally integrates global context (encoded in kernel weights) with local spatial structure. The paper distills six design insights from controlled experiments—most notably that loss functions with vanishing mixed second derivatives are unsuitable, that full-batch single-epoch training outperforms mini-batch for non-causal visual data, that wider (not deeper) inner models are preferable due to optimization difficulties, and that convolutional inner modules are particularly effective for vision. The resulting architecture achieves O(N) time and memory complexity with parallelizable computation, and is instantiated as ViT³ (non-hierarchical), H-ViT³ (4-stage hierarchical), and DiT³ (diffusion Transformer for generation).

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, H-ViT³-S (54M params, 8.8G FLOPs) achieves 84.4% top-1 accuracy (84.9% with MESA), outperforming VMamba-S (83.6%), SOFT-M++ (83.7%), and VVT-M (83.8%) while matching MILA-S (84.4%). At the base scale, H-ViT³-B reaches 84.9% (85.5% with MESA), surpassing VMamba-B (83.9%) and SOFT-L++ (84.1%) but trailing MILA-B (85.3%). On COCO detection (1× schedule), H-ViT³-S achieves 49.1 AP^b / 44.1 AP^m, and on ADE20K segmentation, H-ViT³-S reaches 50.2 mIoU. For image generation, DiT³-B/2 improves FID from 43.47 (DiT-B/2) to 39.31.

## Baselines & Comparisons

H-ViT³ consistently outperforms all Mamba-based baselines: at the small scale, H-ViT³-S (84.4%) beats VMamba-S (83.6%) by 0.8% and LocalVMamba-S (83.7%) by 0.7%; at base scale, H-ViT³-B (84.9%) surpasses VMamba-B (83.9%) by 1.0%. Against linear attention methods, H-ViT³-S matches MILA-S (84.4% vs 84.4%) and outperforms SOFT-M++ (83.7%) and VVT-M (83.8%), while H-ViT³-B (84.9%) trails MILA-B (85.3%) by 0.4% but beats SOFT-L++ (84.1%) by 0.8%. Compared to standard vision Transformers, H-ViT³-S (84.4%) is competitive with TransNeXt-S (84.7%) and RMT-B (85.0%) at similar FLOPs, and H-ViT³-B (84.9%) approaches TransNeXt-B (84.8%) and iFormer-L (84.8%). On COCO detection, H-ViT³-S achieves 49.1 AP^b, surpassing VMamba-S (48.7%) and SOFT-S++ (46.6%) but remaining below TransNeXt-T (49.9%). On ADE20K segmentation, H-ViT³-S (50.2 mIoU) outperforms VMamba-S (50.0%) and SOFT-S++ (48.9%) but falls short of TransNeXt-S (52.2%). For image generation, DiT³ improves FID over DiT across all scales (e.g., 39.31 vs 43.47 for B/2), confirming TTT's effectiveness in generative settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Test-Time Training (TTT) paradigm introduced by Sun et al. [54], which reformulates attention as an online learning problem where key-value pairs are compressed into a compact inner model's weights. While prior TTT work focused on language modeling and video generation, this paper is the first to systematically explore the TTT design space for visual sequence modeling, studying inner training configurations (loss, learning rate, batch size, epochs) and inner model architectures (MLP, GLU, convolution). It positions TTT as a unifying generalization of both Softmax attention (N-width MLP) and linear attention (d×d linear layer), while departing from prior linear-complexity methods (Mamba, linear attention variants) by allowing arbitrary nonlinear inner modules trained via self-supervised reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The six distilled insights provide the first practical design guidelines for visual TTT, filling a critical gap that has limited the field's progress. Key open questions remain: deep inner models suffer from optimization difficulties (both outer-loop initialization and inner-loop gradient issues), mini-batch inner training tailored for non-causal visual data is unexplored, and the performance gap to highly optimized O(N²) Transformers persists especially in dense prediction tasks. The finding that convolutional inner models naturally integrate global and local information suggests a promising but underexplored direction for combining TTT with spatial inductive biases.
