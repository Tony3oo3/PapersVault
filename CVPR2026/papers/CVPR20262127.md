## Problem

Existing token-level hallucination detectors for LVLMs rely on global, whole-image statistics (e.g., summed attention ratios or embedding similarities), which can be fooled by hallucinated tokens that exhibit weak but uniformly scattered relevance across many patches, aggregating into deceptively high overall image-token correlation. No prior method examines whether a token is grounded in a specific local image region, leaving a critical gap in detecting diffuse, non-localized hallucinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given an input image and a single generated object token from an LVLM, the method extracts per-layer cross-attention maps (averaged over all heads, over a 24×24 patch grid) and per-layer token/patch hidden embeddings across all L transformer layers. The output is a binary hallucination label (true object vs. hallucinated object) per token, produced by a lightweight classifier (XGBoost, Random Forest, or MLP) operating on a 2L-dimensional concatenated feature vector. Non-visual tokens (function words) are excluded, and object spans include accompanying modifiers (e.g., 'white car').

## Method

The paper introduces two patch-level structural metrics computed per transformer layer. The Attention Dispersion Score (ADS) retains the top-10% attention patches, groups them into 8-connected components, suppresses small spurious blobs to filter attention sinks, then computes (1 − foreground blob mass) × normalized background Shannon entropy, yielding a scalar that is low for compact, grounded attention and high for diffuse, scattered attention. The Cross-modal Grounding Consistency (CGC) computes cosine similarity between the token's hidden embedding and each patch embedding at every layer, then averages the top-k similarities to capture localized semantic alignment. Per-layer values of both metrics are concatenated into a 2L-dimensional feature vector and fed to a supervised lightweight classifier (XGBoost, Random Forest, or MLP), achieving up to 90% F1 on token-level hallucination detection across LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and InternVL-2.5-8B.

## Results

On MS COCO 2014 image captioning, the proposed XGB-based detector achieves F1 scores of 0.82 (LLaVA-1.5-7B), 0.84 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), and 0.90 (InternVL2.5-8B), with corresponding AUC values of 0.88, 0.94, and 0.94. On the POPE benchmark (LLaVA-1.5-7B), the method reaches F1=0.41 and AUC=0.75, outperforming the next-best baseline by 6% in F1. The ablation confirms that combining ADS and CGC yields 3–6% F1 gains over either signal alone, with the combined feature reaching AUC 0.92–0.95 across all three LVLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (XGB) outperforms all five baselines across every LVLM backbone and both evaluation settings. On MS COCO, it beats the strongest baseline ProjectAway [11] by 3–8% in F1 (e.g., 0.90 vs. 0.82 on InternVL2.5-8B) and by 3–5% in AUC (0.94 vs. 0.89). The gap is most dramatic against SVAR [12], which the paper identifies as the primary global-statistics competitor: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SVAR drops to F1=0.57 while the proposed method reaches 0.84, a 27-point margin attributed to SVAR's vulnerability to the attention-sink phenomenon. On POPE (LLaVA-1.5-7B), the method leads ProjectAway by 3% in F1 (0.41 vs. 0.38) and 5% in AUC (0.75 vs. 0.70), confirming robustness across response formats. MetaToken [8] and HalLoc [21] trail significantly, with F1 scores below 0.73 on most models, while DHCP [29] is competitive on InternVL (F1=0.81) but still lags the proposed method by 9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant global-statistics paradigm in token-level hallucination detection (SVAR, DHCP, MetaToken), which aggregates attention or similarity across all image patches into a single scalar. By identifying that hallucinated tokens produce weak but spatially diffuse attention that inflates global correlation scores, the authors reframe the problem as one of patch-level spatial structure rather than whole-image relevance. The two proposed metrics—Attention Dispersion Score (entropy of filtered attention) and Cross-modal Grounding Consistency (top-k patch similarity)—repurpose ideas from attention-entropy segmentation and visual grounding literature but apply them specifically to discriminate grounded from hallucinated tokens. The detector itself is a lightweight classical ML classifier (XGBoost/RF/MLP) over per-layer feature vectors, continuing the MetaToken/SVAR tradition of handcrafted features rather than end-to-end learned detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that hallucination detection requires examining the spatial distribution of attention rather than its global magnitude provides a principled explanation for why existing detectors fail on attention-sink scenarios, and the resulting metrics are interpretable and cheap to compute. However, the evaluation is limited to three LVLMs and two datasets (MS-COCO captioning and POPE), with notably weaker POPE results (F1 0.41) due to extreme class imbalance, and the use of GPT-4o for ground-truth labeling introduces a potential circularity. Open questions include whether the patch-level signatures generalize to VQA, multi-turn conversation, or video settings, and whether these structural features can inform hallucination mitigation (e.g., decoding strategies) rather than post-hoc detection alone.
