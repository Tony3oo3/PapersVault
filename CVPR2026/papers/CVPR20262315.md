## Problem

Novel view synthesis from single-exposure blurry LDR images in extreme lighting scenes suffers from both motion blur and limited dynamic range, as the frame-based imaging pipeline inherently loses temporal dynamics and compresses HDR spatial information. While recent ERGB-based methods exploit event data to compensate for these losses, they ignore the sensor-physics mismatch between recorded pixel values and true scene radiance—applying tone mapping directly to 3D points rather than modeling the actual exposure integration, non-linear camera response, event latency, and photometric quantization processes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262315/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry 8-bit LDR image (exposure time Δt₂, captured handheld) and its temporally aligned event stream from a DAVIS 346 neuromorphic camera as input. It outputs a sharp HDR novel view in the raw radiance domain, LDR images at arbitrary novel exposure times, and a physically consistent 3D scene representation. The event data serves a dual role: its temporal differential information recovers sharp radiance along the exposure interval (deblurring), while its spatial radiance gradients expand the effective dynamic range beyond what the single LDR exposure captures (HDR).

## Method

See-NeRF uses a NeRF network to represent true scene radiance and density in the HDR domain, then applies volume rendering to simulate raw sensor pixel values via discrete temporal sampling (b=4 points) within the exposure window. Two differentiable CRF models bridge the sensor-physics gap: a 2D pixel-wise RGB CRF (three per-channel MLPs in the log domain) is applied after volume rendering to the integrated raw pixel values, decoupling non-linear tone mapping from linear radiance accumulation; and a latency-aware event CRF models Bayer pattern adaptation, temporal delay via an MLP-predicted coefficient fed into a second-order low-pass filter, and photometric offset calibration to match actual event counts. Both CRF models and the NeRF are jointly optimized under LDR image L2 loss and event count L2 loss, with camera poses estimated via event-guided COLMAP to handle handheld motion during exposure.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, See-NeRF achieves 24.13 PSNR / 0.8927 SSIM / 0.1916 LPIPS for HDR NVS from a single blurry LDR image, outperforming EvHDR-NeRF (21.73 PSNR) by 2.4 dB and the multi-exposure reference HDR-NeRF^ref (26.91 PSNR) is only marginally higher. On the real DAVIS 346 dataset, See-NeRF reaches 26.49 PSNR / 0.8953 SSIM / 0.1638 LPIPS for HDR NVS, surpassing even the multi-exposure reference (26.26 PSNR). For deblurring NVS on the RealWorld-Challenge dataset, See-NeRF attains 32.70 PSNR / 0.9564 SSIM / 0.1574 LPIPS, exceeding the best prior ERGB method E³NeRF (31.40 PSNR) by 1.3 dB and the best RGB method DP-NeRF (28.85 PSNR) by 3.9 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

See-NeRF outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among RGB-based HDR NVS methods (HDR-NeRF, HDR-GS, Gaussian-DK, GaussHDR), which require sharp single-exposure input, See-NeRF leads by 7–14 dB in PSNR on the synthetic set and by 12–16 dB on the real set, demonstrating that a single blurry image plus events can surpass methods that need clean multi-exposure captures. The cascaded pipeline HDR-NeRF+ (EDI deblurring + HDRev HDR reconstruction feeding HDR-NeRF) underperforms See-NeRF by 8 dB (synthetic) and 8 dB (real), confirming that decoupled 2D image processing loses information relative to joint 3D optimization. The closest ERGB-based competitor, EvHDR-NeRF, is outperformed by 2.4 dB PSNR on synthetic HDR and 7.5 dB on real HDR, with the gap attributed to its 3D-point CRF and unmodeled event latency. On the RealWorld-Challenge deblurring benchmark, See-NeRF (32.70 PSNR) beats the best ERGB deblurring method E³NeRF (31.40) by 1.3 dB and the best RGB method DP-NeRF (28.85) by 3.9 dB, with the largest margin on LPIPS (0.1574 vs. 0.2000 and 0.3015 respectively), indicating superior perceptual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

See-NeRF extends the ERGB-based deblurring HDR NeRF line initiated by EvHDR-NeRF (AAAI 2025), which itself built on HDR-NeRF's 3D-point tone-mapping and E³NeRF's event-guided time-sampling strategy. The key conceptual departure is moving the camera response function from the 3D-point level (as in HDR-NeRF and EvHDR-NeRF) to the 2D pixel level after volume rendering, aligning the learned model with the actual physical imaging pipeline. Additionally, the paper introduces a latency-aware, photometrically calibrated event CRF model that accounts for Bayer pattern adaptation, temporal delay, and threshold quantization—details that prior event-based NVS methods (E²NeRF, E³NeRF, Ev-DeblurNeRF) treated with a simplified ideal event model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sensor-physics grounding principle—that CRF and event generation models should mirror the actual hardware pipeline rather than being applied at an arbitrary 3D level—provides a reusable design guideline for future ERGB-based 3D reconstruction methods. The paper's 3DGS variant underperforming significantly on novel exposure (20.39 vs. 24.15 PSNR) highlights an open question about how to integrate learned sensor models with explicit 3D representations. Limitations include the 2.5-hour training time and 0.37 fps rendering speed, the lack of dynamic scene support, and the acknowledged gap in handling event noise, all of which point toward future work on faster, more robust ERGB-based HDR reconstruction.
