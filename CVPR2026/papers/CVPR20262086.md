## Problem

Existing UDA methods for time-series and video data treat temporal distribution alignment and prediction uncertainty as separate, disjoint objectives, failing to capture inter-channel causal dependencies and how overconfident pseudo-labels propagate errors during self-training. This fragmented treatment limits transferability and produces unstable adaptation under domain shifts in both feature distributions and underlying temporal dynamics.

## Input / Output

The method accepts multivariate time-series samples (T×d sensor channels) or video clips converted into fixed-length sequences of pre-extracted ResNet-101 segment embeddings (T=5 segments, PCA-reduced to d=2048), with labeled source data and unlabeled target data. It produces class predictions for target samples, entropy-filtered pseudo-labels for confident target instances, and an entropy-regularized OT transport plan whose cost matrix jointly encodes feature distance and Granger-causal graph distance. Video inputs are reformatted into the same time-series interface, so the downstream causal-graph extraction, OT alignment, and pseudo-labeling pipeline is modality-agnostic.

## Method

Causal-OT embeds Granger-causal graph regularization directly into an entropy-regularized optimal transport alignment: inter-channel causal graphs are estimated via VAR models with BIC lag selection and p-value thresholding, their Laplacian eigenvectors yield per-node causal descriptors, and the OT cost matrix C_ij combines L2 feature distance with causal-embedding distance, solved via Sinkhorn iterations. A hybrid graph-update strategy periodically re-estimates causal graphs on evolving latent features and blends them with the stable raw-signal graphs (α∈[0.6,0.9]) to prevent misalignment during training. For pseudo-labeling, target predictions are filtered by a joint entropy threshold (U_t^j < ρ) and causal-consistency check, so only high-confidence, structurally coherent samples contribute to the self-training loss. The total objective is L_src + α·L_OT + β·L_PL, trained end-to-end, and the paper provides a target-risk bound (Proposition 1) that explicitly decomposes target error into source risk, feature-level OT distance, causal-structure mismatch, and residual hypothesis discrepancy.

## Results

On six 1D time-series benchmarks, Causal-OT achieves an average 4.5% accuracy gain and 3.8% F1-score improvement over the strongest baseline (TransPL). Specifically, on WISDM it reaches 68.03% average accuracy across 10 domain pairs (vs. 64.0% for TransPL), and on UCIHAR it reaches 73.97% (vs. 69.0% for TransPL). On the video benchmark UCF101↔HMDB51, Causal-OT achieves 89.85% average accuracy, outperforming TransferAttn (88.2%) by 1.65%. The method also improves calibration, reducing Expected Calibration Error from 13.55 to 11.23 on SSC and from 4.37 to 3.78 on MFD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Causal-OT outperforms all 13 baselines across both time-series and video benchmarks. On WISDM, it leads the closest competitor TransPL [15] by 4.03 points (68.03% vs. 64.0%) and CoDATS [39] by 4.33 points; on UCIHAR the margin over TransPL widens to 4.97 points (73.97% vs. 69.0%). Among pseudo-labeling strategies, SHOT [22] is the strongest (62.2% on WISDM, 67.8% on UCIHAR) yet still trails Causal-OT by 5.8 and 6.2 points respectively, confirming that entropy-aware filtering plus causal OT alignment surpasses purely confidence-based selection. Distribution-matching baselines (MMDA [30], SASA [5]) and adversarial methods (RAINCOAT [14]) lag further behind, with RAINCOAT achieving only 47.3% on WISDM. On the video benchmark, Causal-OT (89.85%) beats TransferAttn [31] (88.2%) by 1.65 points and MA2LT-D [8] (85.8%) by 4.05 points, while TA3N [7] (80.1%) is the weakest video baseline. No baseline is competitive on both modalities simultaneously, underscoring the generality of the unified Causal-OT framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds most directly on TransPL [15], the first OT-based UDA method for time-series, and extends it by embedding Granger-causal graph constraints into the OT cost matrix rather than treating distribution alignment and causal structure preservation as separate objectives. It also draws on causal representation learning for time series (CauDiTS [36]) and entropy-based pseudo-labeling strategies adapted from the image domain (SHOT, T2PL), but departs from these by jointly modeling causality and uncertainty within a single Sinkhorn-regularized transport plan. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods (Table 1) address at most two of the three challenges—distribution misalignment, causal structure preservation, and prediction uncertainty—while Causal-OT targets all three simultaneously through a unified loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of Granger-causal alignment and entropy-aware pseudo-labeling within a single OT framework offers a principled recipe for time-series UDA that prior methods handled in a fragmented way, and the accompanying generalization bound (Proposition 1) provides theoretical grounding that TransPL lacked. However, the practical gains are moderate (4.5% accuracy, 3.8% F1 on time-series; 2.5% on video), and the video extension relies on converting clips to fixed-length feature sequences, which limits the generality claim. Open questions the authors flag include adaptive entropy thresholding, causal graph smoothing, and whether the approach extends to evolving or non-stationary causal structures in streaming settings.
