## Problem

While Mamba has been widely adopted as a vision backbone, its representational capacity relative to self-attention and linear attention remains theoretically uncharacterized, and existing feature map evaluations rely solely on subjective visual inspection. The paper asks: can we establish a rigorous rank-based hierarchy among these three token-mixing mechanisms, and can we quantify feature map quality objectively to validate the theory?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263435/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224 or 96×96) partitioned into flattened patches with linear projection and adaptive bicubic-interpolated positional embeddings, optionally augmented with register tokens inserted at specific positions in the token sequence. Outputs include per-patch feature maps (evaluated against ground-truth segmentation masks via the Binary-AUC metric), class labels via linear probing on ImageNet, pixel-level segmentation masks on ADE20K, and detection boxes with instance masks on COCO. The Binary-AUC metric specifically requires paired feature maps and binary foreground/background masks resized to the feature map resolution (H/N_p × W/N_p).

## Method

The paper unifies Mamba, softmax self-attention, and linear attention into a shared matrix formulation Y = MX, where M is a lower-triangular token-mixing matrix, then performs block-wise rank analysis: diagonal blocks achieve full rank C for all three, while off-diagonal blocks are bounded by C (self-attention), rank(L_M^off)·N (Mamba), and D_QK (linear attention), establishing the hierarchy Self-Attn > Mamba > Linear-Attn. Mamba's key advantage over linear attention is its learnable, data-dependent causal mask L_M^off (derived from the state transition matrix A), which introduces nonlinearity and raises the effective rank beyond the fixed all-ones mask of linear attention. To validate this theory empirically, the authors pretrain Vim and Mamba-Reg backbones under the DINO self-supervised paradigm and introduce the Binary-AUC metric, which computes the area under the ROC curve between thresholded feature map activations and ground-truth segmentation masks, providing an objective, task-relevant measure of feature discriminability. Experiments confirm the rank hierarchy: self-attention yields the highest AUC and linear probing accuracy, Mamba follows closely (78.5% on ImageNet with Mamba-Reg-Base), and linear attention trails significantly, particularly on long-sequence tasks like segmentation and detection.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k linear probing, DinoVim-B (Mamba-2 backbone) achieves 78.1% top-1 accuracy, closely matching ViT-B (78.2%) while substantially outperforming LinearViT-B (74.7%). With register tokens (DinoMamba-Reg-B), the model reaches 78.5% top-1 accuracy. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, DinoVim-B achieves 38.0% mIoU versus 43.2% for ViT-B and 29.2% for LinearViT-B. On COCO 2017 detection, DinoVim-B attains 42.8 APb compared to 44.8 for ViT-B and 37.1 for LinearViT-B, confirming the rank-based hierarchy Self-Attention > Mamba > Linear Attention across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper establishes a consistent three-way hierarchy across all evaluated tasks: Self-Attention (ViT-B) > Mamba (DinoVim-B) > Linear Attention (LinearViT-B). On ImageNet linear probing, DinoVim-B (78.1%) trails ViT-B (78.2%) by only 0.1% but beats LinearViT-B (74.7%) by 3.4%. The gap widens substantially on long-sequence tasks: on ADE20K segmentation, DinoVim-B (38.0% mIoU) outperforms LinearViT-B (29.2%) by 8.8 points while remaining 5.2 points below ViT-B (43.2%). Similarly, on COCO detection, DinoVim-B (42.8 APb) surpasses LinearViT-B (37.1) by 5.7 points but falls 2.0 points short of ViT-B (44.8). On robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-V2, Sketch, A, R, Real), DinoVim-B matches or exceeds ViT-B on several splits (e.g., 66.6 vs. 66.6 on MF, 84.3 vs. 84.6 on Real) while LinearViT-B shows a noticeable drop, consistent with the theoretical rank analysis predicting that higher-rank architectures generalize better to distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINO self-supervised pretraining framework (Caron et al., 2021) and the Mamba-2 State Space Duality formulation (Dao & Gu, 2024) to unify Mamba, Softmax Self-Attention, and Linear Attention under a single matrix-transform expression Y = MX. It departs from prior analyses (e.g., MILA, RALA) that examined Mamba through the lens of attention mechanisms by instead analyzing Mamba 'from its own perspective' via rank decomposition of its state-space matrices. The Binary-AUC metric extends qualitative feature-map inspection (used in DINO, DINOv2, and register-token papers) into a quantitative, segmentation-grounded evaluation, filling a gap the authors identify in the interpretability literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-based hierarchy (Self-Attention > Mamba > Linear Attention) provides practitioners with a principled explanation for the empirical performance gaps observed across detection, segmentation, and robustness benchmarks, and clarifies that Mamba's advantage over Linear Attention stems from its learnable causal mask rather than its state dimension alone. The Binary-AUC metric offers a reusable diagnostic tool for identifying underperforming components (lazy attention heads, poorly placed register tokens) in vision backbones. Open questions include whether the rank gap between Mamba and Softmax Attention can be closed at scale, whether the hierarchy holds for non-causal (bidirectional) settings beyond the simplified analysis, and whether the DINOv2 finding that larger models produce more outliers (degrading feature quality) is specific to the MAE masking strategy or a general property of self-supervised scaling.
