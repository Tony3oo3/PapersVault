## Problem

Existing 3D/4D driving scene generators operate on dense voxel or grid-aligned latent representations, which provide no explicit modeling of individual foreground actors or background elements. This leads to merged, flickering, or split entities in generated sequences, unreliable actor-level control, and wasted computation on empty space. The paper asks whether a sparse, entity-centric latent representation can yield both interpretable per-actor control and temporally coherent 4D generation.

## Input / Output

The VAE encoder consumes a semantic occupancy grid (128x128x8 on CarlaSC, 200x200x16 on Waymo) and produces a sparse set of 768-1024 grounded latents, each carrying a 3D position, semantic class, optional BEV yaw, and a learned feature vector. The generative pipeline takes Gaussian noise plus (for 4D) the current latent set as input and outputs a full 3D semantic occupancy grid or a 20-step 4D occupancy sequence at 10 Hz, together with per-actor future waypoints and headings. Decoding proceeds through per-latent semantic Gaussians splatted back to the voxel grid, so the final output is always a standard occupancy volume compatible with existing evaluation protocols.

## Method

LatentWorld replaces dense grid latents with a sparse point set where each foreground actor is assigned exactly one latent (position, class, yaw, feature) and background regions are covered by multiple latents, learned via a VAE whose encoder uses a sparse voxel transformer with shifted 3D windows and whose decoder predicts a small set of semantic Gaussians per latent that are splatted to occupancy. Generation is factorized into three diffusion-transformer stages: a layout DiT denoises positions, bit-encoded classes, and sin/cos yaw in a single continuous schedule; a feature DiT conditions on the frozen layout to produce per-latent geometry features; and a motion DiT autoregressively generates 20-step ego and actor trajectories, cross-attending to all current-time latents for scene context. Ego motion is applied as a rigid transform to every latent's position and yaw, while dynamic actors jump to their predicted waypoints, making 4D unrolling a simple per-latent update followed by Gaussian decoding. For unbounded forward generation, an in-loop quadratic mean-shift guidance steers newly denoised latents into the forward half of the BEV window while freezing existing latents, enabling stable outpainting without external controllers.

## Results

On CarlaSC 3D generation, LatentWorld achieves the best average MMD across all three metrics: 6.44 (Geometry), 11.30 (Semantics), and 6.69 (Geometry+Semantics), substantially outperforming SemCity (10.47/15.90/10.04), PDD (12.36/13.17/13.27), and DynamicCity (20.45/12.74/9.98). On Waymo 4D generation, LatentWorld reduces average MMD to 1.62 (Geometry), 1.50 (Semantics), and 0.96 (Geometry+Semantics), compared to DynamicCity's 3.93/3.32/2.61 — a roughly 2–3× improvement. Gains are most pronounced on foreground classes: Vehicle geometry MMD drops from 3.46 to 2.31, Pedestrian from 4.77 to 0.92, and Motorcycle from 8.49 to 6.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DynamicCity (ICLR 2025), the closest 4D occupancy generation baseline, LatentWorld wins decisively on every metric and every class in the Waymo 4D evaluation, with average MMD roughly halved (e.g., 0.96 vs. 2.61 on joint geometry+semantics). The gap is largest on small, fast-moving foreground actors: Pedestrian geometry MMD improves from 4.77 to 0.92 (5×), and Vehicle from 3.46 to 2.31. On CarlaSC 3D generation, LatentWorld also beats both SemCity (triplane diffusion) and PDD (pyramid diffusion) across all three MMD metrics, with the most dramatic gain on Vehicle geometry (5.34 vs. 8.30 for SemCity and 25.45 for PDD). DynamicCity, while competitive on background classes like Building and Vegetation, suffers from entity merging and flickering in 4D sequences due to its dense voxel representation lacking explicit actor factorization. The paper attributes its advantage to the one-latent-per-actor design, which enables direct rigid transforms for ego motion and per-actor trajectory updates without re-denoising the full scene.

## Relation to Prior Work

LatentWorld departs from the dominant dense-voxel paradigm in 4D scene generation (DynamicCity, OccSora, UniScene) by replacing grid-aligned latents with a sparse set of grounded 3D latents, each anchored at an explicit (X,Y,Z) position with a semantic class. The decoding pipeline is built directly on GaussianFormer's semantic Gaussian-to-occupancy splatting formulation, while the layout diffusion stage borrows Bit Diffusion's technique of encoding discrete class labels as bits within a continuous diffusion schedule. Conceptually, the paper repositions 4D generation from a joint denoising problem over a fixed volume to a factorized pipeline (layout → per-latent geometry → motion) over a persistent, editable point set, drawing on the coarse-to-fine generation principle from structured 3D latent work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entity-centric formulation offers a practical path to interpretable, actor-level control in 4D driving scene generation—translating or rotating a single vehicle is a direct latent edit rather than a re-conditioning of the entire volume—which is valuable for simulation, planning, and data augmentation pipelines. The consistent and large MMD improvements on foreground classes (Vehicle, Pedestrian, Motorcycle) over DynamicCity on Waymo suggest that explicit actor factorization is a meaningful architectural choice, not merely a convenience. Open questions include whether the one-latent-per-actor constraint suffices for highly deformable or occluded objects, how the approach scales to open-world (non-driving) scenes with unbounded entity counts, and whether the Gaussian decoding bottleneck limits geometric fidelity at very fine scales.
