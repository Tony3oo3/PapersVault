## Problem

Existing multimodal humor generation methods rely on data-driven training or fixed prompt templates, producing fluent captions that lack genuine humor and cognitive depth. Although classical humor theories (Incongruity-Resolution, Benign Violation, Superiority, Relief) reveal systematic cognitive structures underlying humor, no prior work has explicitly embedded these theoretical mechanisms into the generation pipeline itself, leaving a gap between humor cognition and executable multimodal reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image (meme-style, text-free) as input and produces a short humorous caption as output. GPT-5-2025-08-07 serves as the backbone reasoning model for the multi-stage pipeline, while a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model with a LoRA adapter and classification head acts as the humor discriminator, outputting a continuous humor probability (acceptance threshold 0.66). A closed-loop retry mechanism (up to 5 iterations) regenerates captions when the discriminator flags them as non-humorous, with a fallback to the top-rated candidate if all retries fail.

## Method

HUMORCHAIN decomposes humor generation into a sequential, theory-guided reasoning chain: (1) visual semantic parsing to detect entities and humor triggers, (2) image-type classification into one of four humor strategies (Absurdity, Contrast Irony, Emotion Analogy, Object Analogy) following Yus's meme taxonomy, and (3) strategy-specific caption generation where each step explicitly invokes a corresponding humor theory (e.g., Incongruity-Resolution for incongruent scenes, Benign Violation for norm-breaking irony, Relief for emotional release). A fine-tuned humor discriminator (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct + LoRA + classification head, trained on 5,000+ human-annotated image-caption pairs) performs binary humorous/non-humorous classification, feeding back into a generate-evaluate-refine loop with controlled semantic perturbations. A compliance detection module filters offensive content before output. The key distinction from prior CoT or few-shot approaches is that humor theories are operationalized as explicit, interpretable reasoning stages rather than implicit prompt cues, and the discriminator provides a learned quality gate absent in all baselines.

## Results

HUMORCHAIN (Method I) achieves the highest global ranking with an Arena Elo score of 1554.60 and Bradley-Terry score of 3.57, outperforming all internal baselines (A–F) and external models (G, H). In single-title evaluation, HUMORCHAIN attains a human humor score of 0.810, nearly double the next-best baseline (0.418 for Few-shot), and leads on CLIPScore-related incongruity metrics (EA-Rev 0.765) and BERT Cross Score. The humor discriminator increases the proportion of outputs rated as humorous from 45.1% to 67.0%, with a precision of 0.670 at threshold 0.66.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLoT (Group G, Sun Yat-sen University), HUMORCHAIN wins 68.3% of pairwise comparisons (794 pairs) and achieves a substantially higher human humor score (0.810 vs. 0.195), indicating CLoT's 'Leap-of-Thought' approach produces less genuinely humorous content. Against OxfordTVG-HIC (Group H), HUMORCHAIN dominates with an 86.0% win rate over 1007 comparisons and an Elo advantage of ~87 points (1554.60 vs. 1467.40). The ablation without the discriminator (Method J) still beats CLoT (62.6% win rate) and OxfordTVG-HIC (78.8% win rate), confirming that the theory-guided multi-stage reasoning itself is the primary driver of improvement, while the discriminator adds a further 11.5-point Elo gain (I vs. J: 74.5% win rate). On single-title metrics, HUMORCHAIN leads on EA-Rev (0.765 vs. 0.731 for CLoT) and humor mean (0.810 vs. 0.195), though CLoT achieves slightly higher GM-Rev (0.546 vs. 0.521), suggesting marginally more novel word usage at the cost of overall humor quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both data-driven humor captioning (OxfordTVG-HIC, MemeCraft) and strategy-oriented approaches (CLoT, XMeCap) by explicitly embedding four classical humor theories (Incongruity-Resolution, Benign Violation, Superiority, Relief) as structured reasoning stages within a multimodal LLM pipeline, rather than using them only for post-hoc evaluation. It repurposes Chain-of-Thought reasoning into a theory-orchestrated multi-stage chain where each stage maps to a specific cognitive mechanism, and introduces a fine-tuned 4B-parameter discriminator (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct + LoRA) as a closed-loop quality gate—an architectural choice that contrasts with prior work relying on fixed prompt templates or single-pass generation. The framework is built on GPT-5 as the backbone reasoning model, positioning it within the current MLLM paradigm while contributing the theoretical scaffolding and feedback mechanism as the core novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that theory-guided structured reasoning can substantially close the gap between fluent-but-literal captions and genuinely humorous ones, and that a small fine-tuned discriminator outperforms much larger general-purpose LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Claude-3.5-Haiku) on humor detection precision—a finding with broader implications for specialized evaluation in creative generation. However, the framework's dependence on GPT-5 as backbone limits reproducibility and open-source adoption, and the humor discriminator's threshold (0.66) and 5-retry cap introduce practical cost considerations. Open questions include whether the theory-guided pipeline generalizes across cultures and languages, whether the four humor theories can be parameterized for different humor styles, and whether the approach extends to other creative tasks (satire, metaphor) as the authors speculate.
