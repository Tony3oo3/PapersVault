## Problem

Text-to-video generation models like Veo 3 are highly sensitive to prompt phrasing, forcing users into laborious trial-and-error cycles. Existing test-time optimization methods, while successful for text and images, struggle with video's multi-faceted nature spanning multiple scenes, modalities, and contextual meaning. No prior work has unified visual, audio, and contextual quality in a single autonomous optimization framework for video generation.

## Input / Output

VISTA takes a free-form user text prompt and produces an optimized video with synchronized audio plus a refined structured prompt. The user prompt is decomposed into timed scene sequences, each annotated with nine properties (duration, scene type, characters, actions, dialogues, visual environment, camera, sounds, moods). In experiments, each iteration samples 5 prompt variants × 3 sub-variants × 2 videos = 30 candidate videos, and the system runs up to 5 iterations (1 initialization + 4 self-improvement cycles) before returning the best video-prompt pair.

## Method

VISTA is a four-component multi-agent loop: (1) Structured Video Prompt Planning decomposes the user prompt into temporally grounded multi-scene descriptions using an MLLM; (2) Pairwise Tournament Selection identifies the best video via binary tournaments with bidirectional comparisons and probing critiques to mitigate positional bias; (3) Multi-Dimensional Multi-Agent Critiques (MMAC) applies a Jury Decision Process-inspired triadic court—Normal Judge, Adversarial Judge, and Meta Judge—per dimension (Visual, Audio, Context) to expose nuanced failures even in SOTA outputs; (4) a Deep Thinking Prompting Agent performs six-step introspective reasoning to diagnose whether failures stem from model limitations or prompt deficiencies, then proposes targeted modifications. The system iterates generate→select→critique→refine until convergence or a maximum iteration count, using Gemini 2.5 Flash as the MLLM and Veo 3 as the T2V backbone, with configurable evaluation criteria and penalty mechanisms for common generation failures.

## Results

On the single-scene benchmark (MovieGenVideo, 100 prompts), VISTA achieves a 45.9% win rate (Δ=32.0%) over Direct Prompting at iteration 5, and on the multi-scene benchmark (161 prompts), it reaches 46.3% (Δ=35.1%). Against the strongest baseline (VSR++), VISTA wins 30.3% of pairwise comparisons in single-scene (Δ=18.5%) and 34.4% in multi-scene (Δ=18.8%) at iteration 5. Human evaluators prefer VISTA's outputs in 66.4% of comparisons, and conventional metrics show VISTA improving CLIP-Score by +3% absolute and Dynamic Quality to 89.87% (vs. 77.22% for the best baseline) in single-scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VISTA consistently outperforms all four baselines across both single-scene and multi-scene settings. Against VSR (Self-Refine), VISTA achieves the largest margin: 45.6% win rate in single-scene and 48.5% in multi-scene at iteration 5, despite VSR being the strongest baseline relative to Direct Prompting in multi-scene (Δ=16.5%). Against VSR++ (scaled to match VISTA's video budget), VISTA still wins 30.3% (single) and 34.4% (multi) of comparisons, with the gap narrowing in multi-scene where VSR++ benefits from the harder task. Against Rewrite (Vertex AI prompt guidelines), VISTA leads by 40.2% (single) and 42.0% (multi) win rates, while Rewrite shows inconsistent gains over DP (Δ=19.0% single but Δ=0.0% multi). Against VPO (harmlessness/accuracy/helpfulness prompt expansion), VISTA wins 35.7% (single) and 25.9% (multi), with VPO showing the most variable performance across iterations. Notably, VISTA's advantage does not correlate with baseline strength over DP—VISTA beats the strongest baseline (VSR) by the widest margin, reflecting its multi-dimensional Pareto optimization versus the narrower focus of individual baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

VISTA repurposes test-time prompt optimization—well established for text and image generation (Self-Refine, DPO-style alignment)—and extends it to video by introducing a multi-agent, multi-dimensional (visual, audio, context) critique-and-revise loop. It draws on the Jury Decision Process to structure its triadic judge system (Normal, Adversarial, Meta), borrows binary tournament selection from evolutionary computation for video ranking, and builds on MLLM-as-a-Judge paradigms for evaluation. Unlike prior video-specific efforts (VPO, RAPO, MotionPrompt) that target single properties or require white-box access/fine-tuning, VISTA is the first fully black-box framework to jointly optimize all three video dimensions at test time without modifying the underlying T2V model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured, failure-focused multi-agent critique can meaningfully improve SOTA T2V outputs (Veo 3) at test time, offering a practical path to reduce the laborious prompt-tweaking cycle that currently limits broader T2V deployment. However, the high token cost (~0.7M tokens per iteration) and dependence on a strong MLLM evaluator raise scalability questions, and the evaluation relies heavily on MLLM-as-a-Judge with only limited conventional-metric corroboration. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer/multi-minute videos, whether cheaper evaluators can replace the tournament mechanism, and whether the gains persist as base T2V models improve and leave less room for prompt-level correction.
