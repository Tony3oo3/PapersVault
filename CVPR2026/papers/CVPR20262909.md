## Problem

Existing autoregressive text-to-SVG methods generate code in a single pass without ever perceiving the rendered visual output, leaving the model blind to structural errors, aesthetic flaws, and semantic misalignments in its own work. This absence of visual feedback prevents self-evaluation and iterative refinement, fundamentally capping generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262909/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt as input and produces standardized SVG code (viewBox 0 0 200 200, absolute path commands M/L/C/A/Z only, integer coordinates) as output. During the iterative inference loop, the model additionally consumes its own rendered PNG as visual feedback, and the critic role outputs structured JSON containing a numeric score, textual critique, and actionable revision suggestions that feed back into the next generation round.

## Method

A single Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is trained to serve dual roles—Generator and Critic—via a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (SFT) jointly trains generation, correction, and critique capabilities on a mixed dataset where early-stage failure samples are repurposed as error-correction training pairs and GPT-4o-scored critique targets, teaching the model to both produce SVGs and evaluate rendered images. Stage 2 (DPO) applies preference optimization exclusively on the generation pathway using GPT-4o-scored win/lose pairs to improve first-shot quality without disrupting the critique capability. At inference, the unified model executes a closed-loop 'generate-critique-refine' cycle (up to 3 iterations, threshold score 9.5): it drafts SVG code, renders it to PNG, switches to critic mode to score and diagnose the rendering, then constructs a correction prompt incorporating the critique for the next generation round.

## Results

IntroSVG achieves a near-perfect Render Success Rate of 99.26% and the best FID of 26.18 among all compared models, substantially outperforming domain-specific baselines SVGen (FID 26.27, RSR 84.64%) and OmniSVG (FID 142.38, RSR 75.36%). On aesthetic quality, IntroSVG scores 4.8894, exceeding the strongest closed-source model Gemini 2.5 Pro (4.5854) by 0.30 points. The method also attains the highest HPS (0.1969) among all open-source models and competitive CLIP-T2I (0.2529), though it trails the top closed-source models (e.g., Claude 4.5 Sonnet at 0.2853) on semantic alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two domain-specific SVG models, IntroSVG dominates: it improves FID from 26.27 (SVGen) to 26.18 and from 142.38 (OmniSVG) to 26.18, while boosting RSR from 84.64%/75.36% to 99.26% and Aesthetic from 4.5858/4.7232 to 4.8894. Versus closed-source general-purpose models, IntroSVG achieves the best FID (26.18 vs. Gemini 2.5 Pro's 30.52 and GPT-5's 34.07) and the highest Aesthetic score (4.8894 vs. Gemini's 4.5854), but trails on CLIP-T2I (0.2529 vs. Claude 4.5 Sonnet's 0.2853) and HPS (0.1969 vs. Claude's 0.1998 and Gemini's 0.1981). Among open-source general-purpose models, IntroSVG outperforms all on FID and Aesthetic, with the largest margin over InternVL3.5-38B (FID 57.49 → 26.18) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (FID 42.68 → 26.18). The 7B IntroSVG thus matches or exceeds models 4–10× its size on visual quality and aesthetic metrics, while remaining competitive on semantic alignment with the strongest closed-source systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive text-to-SVG generation line (LLM4SVG, SVGen, OmniSVG) by introducing a closed-loop introspective mechanism where a single VLM alternates between generating SVG code and critiquing its own rendered output. It departs from prior SVG-specific methods that rely on GRPO with hand-engineered rewards by adopting DPO for preference alignment, and from one-shot generation paradigms by training the model explicitly on 'generate-review-refine' triplets derived from failure samples. The data standardization strategy (absolute path commands, integer coordinates) is a practical contribution that prior SVG generation datasets had not systematically addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in autoregressive SVG generation: the absence of visual feedback during training and inference. The finding that the introspective loop generalizes as a zero-shot prompting strategy to unrelated models (GPT-4o, Grok-4, GPT-5) suggests the generate-critique-refine pattern is a broadly applicable inference-time technique beyond this specific task. Open questions include whether the loop scales to more complex multi-element SVGs, how it interacts with interactive human-in-the-loop editing (acknowledged as future work), and whether the 3-iteration budget is sufficient for substantially harder prompts.
