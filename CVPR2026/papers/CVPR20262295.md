## Problem

Class-level unlearning in pretrained vision models requires removing an entire semantic category so the model fails to recognize that object in unseen images and contexts, not just specific training examples. Existing approaches either project out broad subspaces (causing collateral damage to shared features) or fine-tune parameters (introducing compute overhead and instability at higher forgetting strengths), and prior evaluations typically test forgetting on the same dataset used to specify the class, leaving transfer leakage unmeasured.

## Input / Output

CSE consumes a small set of target-class images from a source dataset and 10% of samples per non-target category from a disjoint evaluation dataset, processed through a frozen pretrained encoder (EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-18, or Swin-T). The output is a modified encoder in which a compact set of identified channels is attenuated via a diagonal scaling matrix algebraically folded into the subsequent layer's weights, leaving task heads unchanged and introducing zero additional inference-time computation.

## Method

CSE is a training-free, three-stage pipeline operating in channel space. Stage 1 extracts and jointly standardizes features from target and non-target sets. Stage 2 solves a regularized generalized eigenvalue problem (Σ_t v = ρ(Σ_b + δI)v) to find directions of high target-to-background variance ratio, then scores each channel by its eigenvalue-weighted participation in the top-k discriminative eigenvectors and greedily selects the minimal channel subset covering 85% of total discriminative mass. Stage 3 computes per-channel attenuation factors from discriminative scores, transforms them back to original feature space, and applies them as a precomputed diagonal scaling plus bias at each block's output, respecting residual connections. The key design principle is select-then-erase: only channels uniquely target-salient are damped, while shared directions that also serve non-target classes are preserved, yielding localized edits with minimal collateral damage.

## Results

In the single-class cross-dataset unlearning setting, CSE drives forget-test accuracy (Acc_ft) to 0.01–0.02 across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, while all baselines remain at ≥0.10. Retain-test accuracy (Acc_rt) reaches 0.95–0.98 (CIFAR-10), 0.79–0.86 (CIFAR-100), and 0.61–0.75 (ImageNet), with corresponding H-Mean values of 0.96–0.98, 0.84–0.86, and 0.73–0.75, and membership-inference-attack (MIA) success rate of 0.01. In the multi-class CIFAR-100 evaluation (2–5 target classes), CSE maintains Acc_ft of 0.02–0.04 and Acc_rt of 0.80–0.82, whereas the strongest baselines (ESC, DELETE) show Acc_ft of 0.10–0.14 and declining H-Mean as the target set grows.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSE outperforms all six baselines (ESC, DELETE, BU, LEACE, SCAR, SCRUB) on every metric across all three datasets and three backbones. On CIFAR-10, CSE achieves Acc_ft of 0.01 versus 0.10–0.12 for ESC and DELETE, while simultaneously attaining the highest Acc_rt (0.96 vs. 0.89–0.90) and H-Mean (0.96 vs. 0.89–0.90). ESC is the closest competitor at low forgetting strength but degrades rapidly as more subspace components are removed, losing 5–8 points in Acc_rt relative to CSE on CIFAR-10. Gradient-based methods (DELETE, BU, SCAR) forget effectively but drag shared filters, dropping Acc_rt by 5–14 points compared to CSE as edit strength increases. LEACE and SCRUB show the weakest retention (Acc_rt 0.80–0.82 on CIFAR-10) and highest MIA (0.18–0.20), indicating residual memorization. In the multi-class setting (Table 2), CSE's advantage widens: at 5 target classes, CSE maintains Acc_ft of 0.04 and Acc_rt of 0.80, while ESC and DELETE reach only Acc_ft of 0.10–0.12 and Acc_rt of 0.75–0.76.

## Relation to Prior Work

CSE builds on the subnet/circuit discovery paradigm from Bayazit et al. [1] and Rajaram et al. [28], which identify compact sets of channels or units critical to specific knowledge in pretrained models, and repurposes it for contrastive channel scoring via generalized eigenanalysis. It departs from the dominant unlearning families—global subspace projection (ESC), gradient-based parameter reshaping (DELETE, BU), and retain-set distillation (SCAR, SCRUB)—by operating locally in channel space with a training-free, algebraically foldable attenuation that avoids both the nonlocality of projection methods and the collateral damage of fine-tuning. The cross-dataset evaluation protocol, where the forget class is specified on one dataset and tested on a semantically aligned but disjoint dataset, extends the standard same-dataset unlearning benchmark to stress-test true concept removal versus memorization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CSE's training-free, zero-inference-overhead design makes class-level unlearning practically deployable in settings where retraining is infeasible (e.g., GDPR right-to-be-forgotten requests on production vision models), and the cross-dataset protocol offers a more rigorous evaluation than prior same-dataset benchmarks. Open questions include how the method behaves when target and non-target concepts share very high channel overlap (the MNIST toy suggests fragility), whether the approach extends to multi-modal or generative models, and how robust the contrastive eigenanalysis is when the non-target set is small or poorly chosen (the ablation shows retention drops to 0.76 without D_b). The very large margins over baselines, while impressive, may partly reflect that the cross-dataset protocol is less favorable to global-projection and fine-tuning methods than to surgical channel edits.
