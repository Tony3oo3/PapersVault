## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detectors are either opaque binary classifiers or MLLM-based approaches that overemphasize superficial cues (lighting, visual quality) while missing the intrinsic, physics-violating artifacts humans actually use to identify synthetic content. Off-the-shelf MLLMs achieve near-random detection accuracy even with chain-of-thought prompting, and prior MLLM-based detectors lack a systematic artifact taxonomy or grounded spatio-temporal localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261624/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip (16 uniformly sampled frames resized to 256p) and produces a structured response: a chain-of-thought reasoning block in <thinking> tags, a binary Fake/Real verdict in <answer> tags, and for fake videos, one or more artifact annotations each specifying a taxonomy type, a temporal interval [t_start, t_end], and a normalized bounding box [x_min, y_min, x_max, y_max]. For real videos, the model similarly inspects suspected regions using the same temporal-spatial tags, producing a 'description-inspection-review-conclusion' narrative that explicitly rules out forgery evidence.

## Method

Skyra builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a two-stage training pipeline. Stage 1 (cold-start SFT) fine-tunes the model on ViF-CoT-4K, the first large-scale human-annotated dataset with a three-layer artifact taxonomy (Low-level Forgery vs. Violation of Laws, refined into 8 L2 and multiple L3 categories), where each sample includes artifact type, textual explanation, timestamps, bounding boxes, and a Gemini-2.5-Pro-generated chain-of-thought grounded in those labels. Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning with a custom composite reward: an asymmetric accuracy term (correct = +1.0, false negative = 0.0, false positive = −0.2) that reflects the task's inherent asymmetry—finding one artifact proves fake, but confirming real requires ruling out all inconsistencies—plus an inspection reward that logarithmically scales with the number of valid spatio-temporal check blocks, encouraging active multi-region probing. This design prevents the model from collapsing to a 'Fake' bias while incentivizing thorough visual exploration of candidate artifact regions.

## Results

On ViF-Bench, Skyra-RL achieves 91.02% accuracy, 88.35% recall, and 90.27% F1, surpassing the best binary detector DeMamba (64.29% ACC, 73.00% F1) by +26.73% accuracy and +17.27% F1. Compared to the strongest MLLM-based baseline (BusterX++: 56.90% ACC, 21.94% F1), Skyra-RL gains +34.12% accuracy, +73.95% recall, and +68.33% F1. On the out-of-domain GenVideo benchmark, Skyra-RL reaches 71.78% accuracy (+11.07% over DeMamba), and after a single-epoch RL adaptation on GenVideo data, Skyra-RL-GenVideo further improves to 91.00% accuracy and 90.00% F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skyra-RL decisively outperforms all three binary detectors on ViF-Bench: it exceeds DeMamba (the strongest binary baseline at 64.29% ACC / 73.00% F1) by +26.73% accuracy and +17.27% F1, while AIGVDet and NSG-VD trail further behind at 69.08% and 49.65% accuracy respectively. Against off-the-shelf MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4.1-mini, Gemini 2.5-flash, InternVL-3, VideoLLaMA-3), Skyra-RL achieves +34.12% accuracy and +32% F1 over the best, confirming that general-purpose MLLMs lack sensitivity to subtle generative artifacts even with CoT prompting. The only prior MLLM-based detector, BusterX++, scores 56.90% ACC and 21.94% F1, yielding a gap of +34.12% ACC and +68.33% F1 for Skyra-RL. On the out-of-domain GenVideo benchmark, Skyra-RL gains +11.07% accuracy over DeMamba (71.78% vs. 60.71%), and a brief 1-epoch RL fine-tune on GenVideo data pushes performance to 91.00% ACC / 90.00% F1, demonstrating rapid domain adaptation without additional human annotation. The RL stage itself contributes a consistent +0.91% ACC and +1.51% F1 over SFT on ViF-Bench, with larger gains (+3.74% recall) on harder I2V samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary-classification paradigm in AI-generated video detection (AIGVDet, DeMamba, NSG-VD) and from prior MLLM-based explainable detectors (BusterX++, DAVID-XR1, IVY-Fake) that the authors argue overemphasize superficial cues like visual quality while missing intrinsic physics-violating artifacts. It builds on Qwen2.5-VL as the base MLLM and adapts GRPO-based reinforcement learning (following DeepSeek-R1 and Video-R1) with a task-specific asymmetric reward and inspection reward. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a three-level hierarchical artifact taxonomy and the first large-scale human-annotated artifact dataset (ViF-CoT-4K) to provide grounded spatio-temporal supervision, rather than relying on MLLM-generated pseudo-labels as prior explainable detectors do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ViF-Bench, which includes samples from very recent generators (Sora-2, Wan2.2-A14B, Kling, Hailuo-02) with matched real-fake semantics, addresses a critical gap in existing benchmarks that rely on outdated or homogeneous generators. The ablation showing that RL without SFT cold-start collapses to near-random performance (50.09% ACC) is a useful cautionary result for the broader 'RL bootstraps reasoning' narrative in MLLMs. Open questions include whether the artifact taxonomy generalizes to future, more realistic generators, whether the 16-frame/256p sampling limits temporal resolution for subtle artifacts, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or multi-modal (audio) evidence.
