## Problem

Existing Masked Point Modeling (MPM)-based point cloud ICL methods suffer from two fundamental issues: they predict target geometry from geometry-free masked tokens that carry no spatial or structural priors, forcing the model to infer shape purely from token-level correlations; and they exhibit a training-inference objective mismatch, where partially masked targets during training encourage shortcut learning that fails when the query target is fully masked at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a prompt pair (input and target point clouds) that implicitly specifies the task, along with a query input point cloud, and produces the deformed query target point cloud. Each point cloud is sampled at 1,024 points and divided into 64 patches of 32 points via KNN neighborhood sampling around aligned central points. Unlike MPM-based methods that initialize the target with learnable masked tokens, DeformPIC starts from the actual query input geometry and deforms it, preserving spatial continuity throughout.

## Method

DeformPIC reformulates point cloud ICL as a two-stage deformation process. The Deformation Extraction Network (DEN) encodes prompt input and target patches with a shared mini-PointNet, concatenates them with a learnable task token, and passes them through a stacked Transformer to produce a task-specific embedding that captures the geometric transformation. The Deformation Transfer Network (DTN) encodes the query input with a separate mini-PointNet, then applies AdaLN-Zero modulated Transformer blocks conditioned on the task embedding to deform the query geometry toward the target. Training uses an end-to-end L2 Chamfer Distance loss on the full deformed output, maintaining identical objectives in both training and inference phases and eliminating the masking mismatch inherent in MPM-based approaches.

## Results

On the ShapeNet In-Context dataset, DeformPIC achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 2.7 (reconstruction), 3.5 (denoising), and 2.0 (registration), along with 83.9 mIoU on part segmentation. These represent improvements of 1.6, 1.8, and 4.7 CD over the previous best ICL baselines (PIC-Cat and PIC-S-Sep) on reconstruction, denoising, and registration respectively. On the out-of-domain benchmark (trained on ShapeNet, tested on ModelNet40 and ScanObjectNN), DeformPIC achieves 3.4/4.0 CD on reconstruction, 3.9/5.0 on denoising, and 2.3/2.0 on registration, substantially outperforming both multitask and ICL baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeformPIC outperforms all ICL baselines across every task. Against PIC-Cat [10], it reduces average CD by 1.6 (reconstruction: 2.7 vs 4.3), 1.8 (denoising: 3.5 vs 5.3), and 12.1 (registration: 2.0 vs 14.1). Against the strongest ICL variant PIC-S-Sep [23], the registration gap is 4.7 CD (2.0 vs 6.7), while DeformPIC also matches or exceeds PIC-S on part segmentation mIoU (83.9 vs 83.7) despite not being specifically redesigned for that task. Among pre-trained multitask models, DeformPIC beats the best reconstruction method UniPre3D [46] by 1.7 CD (2.7 vs 4.4), the best denoising method Point-MAE [30] by 2.1 CD (3.5 vs 5.6), and the best registration method Point-MAE by 12.5 CD (2.0 vs 14.5). Even against task-specific models, DeformPIC surpasses the specialized ACT [8] on registration (2.0 vs 5.9) and PCT [14] on reconstruction (2.7 vs 2.6 is a near-tie, but DeformPIC wins on denoising 3.5 vs 2.3 is actually a loss — correction: PCT wins denoising at 2.3 vs 3.5). On the out-of-domain benchmark, DeformPIC dominates all baselines: it achieves 2.0 CD on ScanObjectNN registration versus 9.0 for PIC-Sep and 26.1 for Point-MAE, confirming that the deformation-based formulation generalizes far better than masked reconstruction under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the Masked Point Modeling (MPM) paradigm established by PIC (Fang et al., NeurIPS 2023) and its successors (PIC-S, DG-PIC, PCoTTA), which reconstruct target point clouds from geometry-free masked tokens. Instead, DeformPIC borrows the neural deformation perspective from shape correspondence and mesh generation literature (FlowNet3D, 3DCoded, Pixel2Mesh) and repurposes it within the ICL setting, using AdaLN-Zero conditioning (from Peebles & Xie, CVPR 2023) to inject task-specific deformation guidance. The key conceptual shift is treating ICL as a query-to-target deformation problem rather than a masked-reconstruction problem, explicitly decoupling task extraction (DEN) from deformation application (DTN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses two structural weaknesses in MPM-based point cloud ICL—geometry-free reconstruction and training-inference objective mismatch—that likely affect all token-based ICL approaches in 3D. The newly introduced OOD benchmarks (ModelNet40 In-Context, ScanObjectNN In-Context) fill a gap in evaluating cross-distribution generalization for point cloud ICL. Open questions include whether the deformation paradigm extends to tasks with non-rigid or topological changes (e.g., shape morphing), whether the t-SNE-observed confusion between denoising and reconstruction task tokens limits scalability to more fine-grained task families, and whether the approach generalizes to larger, unstructured point clouds beyond the 1,024-point regime.
