## Problem

Conventional egocentric hand-object interaction (HOI) videos lack physical grounding (contact forces, motion signals) and suffer from frequent hand occlusion, while sensing gloves that capture these signals introduce a large appearance gap that prevents vision models trained on glove data from generalizing to bare-hand tasks. No prior work bridges this gap by generating photorealistic bare-hand videos from sensor-glove captures while preserving synchronized multi-modal sensor signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263694/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric glove video (grayscale, from headset cameras) along with extracted hand poses P ∈ R^{T×J×3} and hand/object masks as input, and produces a photorealistic bare-hand video at the same temporal resolution, accompanied by time-synchronized continuous tactile (per-fingertip force) and IMU (12× 6-DoF) signals. The glove and hand domains are explicitly unpaired—no sample correspondence exists across domains—so the translation must be learned from unpaired data. At inference, the pipeline is fully automatic: SAM-2 and Grounding DINO extract masks, Propainter erases the glove region, and object masks ensure foreground pixels remain intact before the diffusion restorer runs.

## Method

Glove2Hand decomposes the translation into two subproblems leveraging the shared articulated structure (hand pose P) between glove and hand. First, a minimalist 3D Gaussian hand model defines Gaussians directly on a canonical hand mesh surface using barycentric coordinates, with deformation handled by transforming mesh triangles and recomputing Gaussian parameters via the deformation gradient; an MLP predicts per-palm and per-back spherical-harmonic environment maps from hand pose to enable relighting under dynamic egocentric illumination. Second, a diffusion hand restorer (ControlNet + AnimateDiff) is trained to restore the original bare-hand video from a corrupted input where the rendered hand is overlaid with a dilated mask and wrist region masked, learning to refine hand-object contact boundaries, generate a natural wrist, and fix penetration artifacts. The 3D Gaussian hand is optimized per-subject and frozen during diffusion training, while the diffusion restorer is shared across subjects, enabling generalization to unseen identities.

## Results

On the HandSense dataset, Glove2Hand achieves FID 30.1, FVD 19.5 (2-sec clips), and FVD-long 24.5 (10–30 sec clips), outperforming the strongest baseline HandRefiner (FID 35.5, FVD 24.2, FVD-long 29.7) by 5.4 FID points and 4.7 FVD points. In the downstream contact estimation task, combining real hand data with Glove2Hand-synthesized videos yields 88.2% Contact IoU and 94.9% Recall, versus 85.3% IoU for real data alone. For hand tracking under heavy occlusion, finetuning UmeTrack on Glove2Hand videos reduces MKPE from 19.2 mm to 16.6 mm in occluded frames, while training on raw glove data degrades it to 27.2 mm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Glove2Hand outperforms all seven video-translation/inpainting baselines on every metric (FID, FVD, FVD-long). The closest competitor is HandRefiner [42], a diffusion-based hand inpainting method, which trails by 5.4 FID and 4.7 FVD; Pix2Pix [30] and BrushNet [33] are next closest but still 8+ FID points behind. GAN-based methods (CycleGAN [81], Pix2Pix [30]) and off-the-shelf video inpainters (InpaintAnything [71], VideoPainter [3], FoundHand [8]) all lag substantially, with FoundHand performing worst (FID 82.2, FVD 47.9), confirming that static-image hand generators struggle with temporal consistency. In the contact estimation task, PressureVision++ [14] (designed for planar surfaces) achieves only 0.8% Contact IoU, far below Glove2Hand-augmented training (88.2%). For hand tracking, UmeTrack [21] improves from 19.2 mm to 16.6 mm MKPE under occlusion when finetuned on Glove2Hand videos, whereas finetuning on raw glove data worsens it to 27.2 mm, validating the domain-gap bridging capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting for hand avatars, diffusion-based video inpainting, and video-to-video translation. It adapts the relighting framework of LumiGauss [34] to handle dynamic egocentric lighting via pose-conditioned spherical harmonics, and builds its diffusion hand restorer on ControlNet [73] and AnimateDiff [56]. The key architectural departure from prior hand avatar work (which requires dense multi-view cameras and controlled lighting) is the surface-grounded Gaussian design that anchors Gaussians to a canonical mesh, enabling sparse-view reconstruction with variable illumination. Unlike prior hand-object interaction synthesis methods that generate static images from pre-scanned rigid meshes, this work produces dynamic videos grounded by real glove captures and handles non-rigid objects without explicit 3D object geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HandSense dataset is the first to combine sensor-measured continuous tactile and IMU signals with synchronized photorealistic bare-hand HOI videos, filling a critical gap where prior datasets offered only binary contact labels or lacked visual data entirely. The demonstrated value as a data curation engine—where synthesized glove-to-hand videos improve both contact estimation and occluded hand tracking over real-data-only baselines—suggests a scalable path to augmenting scarce annotated HOI data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to bimanual interactions, whether the per-subject 3D Gaussian model can be replaced by a population-level model, and whether the synthesized data can fully replace real data at scale. The reliance on MoCap for ground-truth poses during data collection limits the pipeline's fully in-the-wild applicability.
