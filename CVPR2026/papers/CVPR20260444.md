## Problem

Unified video-text-to-audio (VT2A) generation faces two unexplored foundational challenges: (1) the scarcity of high-quality audio captions with tight video-audio-text (V-A-T) alignment, where audio-only captions introduce severe semantic conflicts with visual content, and (2) cross-task and intra-task competition in multi-task training, manifesting as an adverse V2A–T2A performance trade-off and modality bias (text vs. video) within the VT2A task itself.

## Input / Output

The model accepts any flexible combination of a video clip (visual features extracted at 8 fps via CLIP, dense temporal features via Synchformer) and a text prompt (encoded by Flan-T5), with absent modalities simply omitted rather than padded. It produces 10-second audio clips at 16 kHz, generated in the latent space of a pretrained audio VAE and decoded to waveform. The decoupled injection design (cross-attention for semantics, AdaLN for temporal alignment) enables seamless switching between T2A, V2A, and VT2A modes without architectural modification.

## Method

Omni2Sound uses a standard DiT backbone with a decoupled two-branch conditioning scheme: a semantic branch injects concatenated Flan-T5 text and CLIP visual embeddings via cross-attention, while a temporal branch injects Synchformer features via AdaLN for fine-grained synchronization. Training follows a three-stage progressive schedule: (1) large-scale T2A pretraining establishes a robust generative prior; (2) multi-task interleaved training samples one task per step, where high-quality VT2A triplets from SoundAtlas serve as a semantic bridge that converts the zero-sum V2A–T2A competition into cooperative optimization, allowing T2A sampling to drop to 10%; (3) a decoupled robustness stage applies Text Dropout (penalizing text bias to strengthen A-V sync) and Off-screen Synthesis (counteracting video bias to ensure textual faithfulness for off-screen audio). The SoundAtlas dataset (470k pairs) is built via an agentic pipeline featuring vision-to-language compression to mitigate MLLM visual bias, a junior-senior Gemini agent handoff for 5× cost reduction, and CLAP-based post-hoc filtering for fidelity.

## Results

On the VGGSound-Omni benchmark, Omni2Sound achieves unified SOTA across all three tasks: T2A (FAD 1.01, FD 4.61, MS-CLAP 0.53), V2A (FAD 0.51, FD 3.41, IB 0.35), and VT2A (FAD 0.53, FD 2.95, MS-CLAP 0.52). On the Kling-Audio-Eval benchmark, it similarly leads unified baselines on all tasks (e.g., VT2A FAD 2.37 vs. MMAudio 4.90, AudioX 3.16). On the off-screen track, the full three-stage model (S1→S2→S3) improves FAD from 0.97 to 0.85 and MLLM win rate from 46.8% to 53.2% over the S1→S2 baseline, validating the Off-screen Synthesis augmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against unified models, Omni2Sound decisively outperforms both MMAudio and AudioX on all three tasks: on VT2A, it reduces FAD from 0.91 (MMAudio) and 1.24 (AudioX) to 0.53, and FD from 5.28/8.29 to 2.95, while also achieving the best MS-CLAP (0.52 vs. 0.49/0.49). Against specialized V2A models, it beats Frieren (FAD 0.51 vs. 1.23) and V-AURA (FAD 0.51 vs. 2.34) by large margins, and surpasses MMAudio on IB (0.35 vs. 0.28). On VT2A, it outperforms ThinkSound (FAD 0.53 vs. 1.10) and HunyuanVideo-Foley (FAD 0.53 vs. 2.36), though HunyuanVideo-Foley retains a slight edge on IB (0.32 vs. 0.34 is actually Omni2Sound's advantage) and DS is comparable. For data pipeline comparisons, SoundAtlas outperforms Auto-ACD, Sound-VECaps, and AudioSetCaps on both LA-CLAP and MS-CLAP (e.g., 0.447/0.485 on AudioSet vs. 0.396/0.437 for Auto-ACD), and achieves a 71% human-evaluation win rate over Auto-ACD's 31% and Human-Expert's 46% on semantic alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified audio generation line initiated by MMAudio (V2A-centric with T-A augmentation) and AudioX (flexible modality combinations) by treating VT2A, V2A, and T2A as co-equal tasks within a single DiT-based diffusion model. It departs from prior data pipelines (AudioSetCaps, Auto-ACD, Sound-VECaps) that rely on audio-only or separate-then-fuse captioning by introducing SoundAtlas, an agentic pipeline that enforces tight V-A-T alignment through vision-to-language compression and junior-senior agent handoff. The three-stage progressive training schedule builds on UniFlow-Audio's coarse TA/NTA competition analysis but introduces a finer-grained resolution: using high-quality VT2A triplets as a semantic bridge to convert cross-task zero-sum dynamics into cooperative optimization, followed by a decoupled robustness stage to address intra-task modality bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first comprehensive recipe for unified VT2A generation that simultaneously achieves SOTA on all three sub-tasks, which is practically important for deployment scenarios requiring flexible input modalities without model switching. SoundAtlas (470k pairs) and VGGSound-Omni (with its off-screen track) fill critical gaps in training data quality and evaluation coverage that the field has lacked. Open questions include whether the three-stage schedule generalizes to longer-form audio or additional modalities (speech, music), whether the VT2A-bridge insight holds at much larger scale, and how the approach extends to real-time or interactive generation settings.
