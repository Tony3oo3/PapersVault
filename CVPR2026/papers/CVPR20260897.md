## Problem

Nested (Matryoshka-style) Vision Transformers enable elastic inference by embedding multiple subnetworks in a single backbone, but they allocate a fixed computational budget to every input regardless of its complexity. This leads to inefficiency: easy images waste compute on unnecessary capacity while hard images may not receive enough. The paper asks how to achieve input-aware compute allocation within a nested ViT without relying on a separate, costly routing mechanism.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 224×224 RGB image, tokenized into 16×16 patches projected to D-dimensional embeddings. At each thinking stage it produces a class prediction along with a Shannon entropy score that serves as the stopping criterion; if entropy falls below a tunable threshold τ, inference halts and the current prediction is returned. For the segmentation variant, the ViT backbone is paired with a linear decoder (Segmenter pipeline) to produce per-pixel class masks at single or multi-scale resolutions.

## Method

ThinkingViT induces nested subnetworks within a standard ViT by slicing embeddings, MHA, MLP, and normalization layers at sorted indices, yielding contained subnetworks V_{d_i,h_i} with progressively more attention heads. At inference, it begins with the smallest subnetwork (e.g., 3 heads); if the output entropy exceeds a threshold, it activates a larger subnetwork (e.g., 6 heads) and performs a second forward pass. A Token Recycling mechanism fuses the previous stage's output tokens into the new input embeddings via a learned projection and a scalar α, so each round builds on prior representations rather than starting from scratch. All subnetworks are trained jointly with a weighted multi-stage classification loss, and the entropy threshold τ is adjusted at deployment to trade accuracy against throughput or GMACs, enabling elastic inference without any external router.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ThinkingViT (3H→6H configuration) achieves 81.44% top-1 accuracy with 22.01M parameters and 5.85 GMACs, surpassing nested width-expansion baselines by up to 2.0 p.p. at equal throughput and up to 2.9 p.p. at equal GMACs. This is only 0.36 p.p. below DeiT-Base (86.6M params, 17.56 GMACs). On the Swin backbone, ThinkingViT matches Swin-B accuracy (83.5%) while requiring only 9.5 GMACs and 50M parameters versus 15.4 GMACs and 88M for Swin-B. For semantic segmentation, ThinkingViT as a drop-in backbone outperforms DeiT-Small and DeiT-Tiny on both ADE20K and Cityscapes under single- and multi-scale evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four width-expansion nested baselines (MatFormer, HydraViT, SortedNet, DynaBERT), ThinkingViT consistently achieves higher accuracy at both equal GMACs (up to +2.9 p.p.) and equal throughput (up to +2.0 p.p.) on ImageNet-1K, and maintains this advantage across all robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-V2, ReaL, R, A, Sketch). Compared to depth-adaptive early-exit methods (SDN, PABEE, BERxiT, ViT-EE, PCEE, LGViT), ThinkingViT achieves substantially higher accuracy at comparable GMAC budgets, demonstrating that iterative width expansion outperforms early depth termination. On the Swin Transformer backbone, ThinkingViT-Swin matches Swin-B's 83.5% accuracy at 9.5 GMACs versus Swin-B's 15.4 GMACs, and exceeds both Swin-S and Swin-T in accuracy-per-GMAC. For semantic segmentation, the ThinkingViT backbone outperforms both DeiT-Small and DeiT-Tiny backbones on ADE20K and Cityscapes mIoU. Notably, on uniformly hard inputs (ImageNet-R), ThinkingViT still outperforms all baselines, confirming that its entropy-based routing correctly allocates more compute to difficult samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThinkingViT extends the nested Transformer lineage initiated by MatFormer, DynaBERT, SortedNet, and most directly HydraViT, which embed multiple width-sliced subnetworks within a single ViT backbone. The key departure is replacing the fixed compute budget per input with progressive thinking stages that dynamically expand the number of active attention heads based on prediction entropy, drawing conceptual inspiration from iterative reasoning in LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1). Unlike those LLM approaches that loop the same network, ThinkingViT introduces Token Recycling to fuse prior-stage embeddings into subsequent passes while simultaneously increasing model capacity, and it contrasts with early-exit methods by adapting width rather than depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Elastic inference is a practical bottleneck for deploying ViTs across heterogeneous hardware, and ThinkingViT's entropy-gated progressive expansion offers a clean mechanism to trade latency for accuracy at runtime without separate routing models. The backbone-preserving design (plug-in upgrade for segmentation, transfer to Swin) broadens applicability, and the result that 22M parameters with adaptive compute can rival 86.6M-parameter DeiT-Base suggests compute allocation strategy matters as much as parameter count. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two thinking stages without diminishing returns, how it interacts with token-pruning or token-merging methods, and whether the entropy threshold generalizes well to open-vocabulary or few-shot settings.
