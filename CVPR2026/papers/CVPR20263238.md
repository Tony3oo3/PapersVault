## Problem

Existing fractured object reassembly methods assume all parts are available, but real-world scenarios (e.g., archaeology) frequently involve missing parts that make the assembly under-constrained. The only prior attempt at handling missing parts relied on symmetry-based completion after unsupervised reassembly, which cannot recover isolated parts and is tightly coupled to reassembly quality. This paper proposes the first learning-based task and model that jointly reassembles existing parts and predicts the geometry of missing parts.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a set of point clouds (one per existing part), each downsampled to 1,024 points via Poisson disk sampling followed by farthest point sampling, and normalized by the longest bounding-box diagonal across all parts. Parts arrive in arbitrary initial poses with no overlap guarantee. The model outputs SE(3) poses (quaternion + translation) for each existing part and 1,024-point clouds for each predicted missing part; the union of transformed existing parts and predicted missing parts constitutes the recovered object. The number of missing parts is not given explicitly—the model infers it via masked tokens analogous to BERT's [MASK] mechanism.

## Method

Each part's point cloud is first enriched with rotation-invariant KNN descriptors (distance, normal–normal angle, normal–vector angles) to reduce pose sensitivity, then encoded by a shared DGCNN into a 128-d latent vector. A multi-head transformer processes all part tokens (existing parts plus randomly masked tokens for missing parts during training, following Point-BEiT) to correlate inter-part features. Two decoders operate on the transformer output: an MLP regresses the SE(3) pose for each existing part, and a separate MLP generates 1,024-point shapes for missing parts. A key design is a consistency loss that also feeds existing-part tokens into the shape decoder and penalizes the Chamfer distance between the predicted shape and the existing part transformed by its estimated pose, thereby coupling pose estimation and shape prediction in a single end-to-end supervised objective (L = L_trans + 0.5·L_rot + 0.5·L_pose + L_shape + L_consistency).

## Results

On the Breaking Bad-based fractured object recovery dataset, the proposed model achieves E_rot = 18.71 (×10⁻³), E_trans = 5.28 (×10⁻²), and E_missing = 39.11 (×10⁻³), outperforming all baselines on all three metrics. The closest competitor, GARF w/ AdaPoinTr, scores 25.80 / 5.66 / 46.27 respectively, while the unsupervised Multiview-ICP baseline is orders of magnitude worse (E_rot = 2981.21). The ablation (Ours-TwoStage) confirms that joint pose estimation and shape prediction yields substantially better results than a sequential two-stage pipeline (75.17 / 24.50 / 101.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model wins on all three metrics against every baseline. Against the strongest supervised baseline GARF w/ AdaPoinTr (Li et al., 2025), it improves E_rot from 25.80 to 18.71 (27% reduction), E_trans from 5.66 to 5.28, and E_missing from 46.27 to 39.11 (15% reduction). PF++ w/ AdaPoinTr (Wang et al., 2024) is outperformed by a wider margin (E_rot 33.46→18.71, E_missing 66.59→39.11), and Jigsaw w/ AdaPoinTr (Lu et al., 2023) shows the largest gap among learning-based baselines (E_rot 43.16→18.71, E_missing 104.63→39.11). The unsupervised Multiview-ICP baseline (Fantoni et al., 2012) fails catastrophically on pose estimation (E_rot = 2981.21) and cannot produce missing-part predictions at all. The authors' own two-stage ablation (Ours-TwoStage) confirms that decoupling pose estimation from shape prediction degrades performance dramatically (E_rot 75.17 vs. 18.71), validating the benefit of the joint transformer-based formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads—fractured object reassembly (Jigsaw, GARF, PuzzleFusion++) and point cloud shape completion (AdaPoinTr, PoinTr)—into a single joint formulation. Prior reassembly methods assume all parts are present and only estimate poses, while completion methods assume a connected partial shape is given; neither handles the case of disjointed fragments with missing parts. Architecturally, the model repurposes DGCNN for per-part encoding, borrows the masked-token strategy from Point-BEiT/BERT to represent missing parts, and introduces a consistency loss that couples the pose and shape decoders—a design not present in either prior line of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first learning-based approach to the practically important problem of recovering fractured objects when parts are genuinely missing, a scenario common in archaeology that prior methods cannot handle. The finding that joint training yields mutual benefits for both pose estimation and shape prediction (validated by the Two-Stage ablation) suggests that treating reassembly and completion as a coupled problem is more effective than sequential pipelines. Key limitations include poor recovery of small missing parts, limited generalization to unseen shape categories (only 20 categories in the dataset), and no evaluation under noise—open questions that will need addressing before real-world deployment.
