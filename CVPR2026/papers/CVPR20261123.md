## Problem

Hybrid autoregressive image generation models combine AR semantic prediction with diffusion-based detail refinement to overcome VQ-based quality limitations, but the iterative diffusion denoising process becomes the dominant inference bottleneck. Existing acceleration methods (CSpD, LazyMAR) target only the autoregressive component, leaving the far more expensive diffusion denoising largely unaddressed, resulting in marginal overall speedups.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261123/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free acceleration wrapper applied to the MAR backbone, taking an ImageNet class label as input and producing a 256×256 RGB image composed of 64 patches (8×8 grid). Inference runs with a batch size of 8 on four RTX 3090 GPUs, and the framework is orthogonal to other AR-side accelerators like LazyMAR, meaning it can be stacked for compounded speedups without altering the model's input or output format.

## Method

FastHybrid introduces a training-free dual-branch inference pipeline. The Lookahead Decoding Strategy first predicts K future patch latents in parallel via the AR model and refines them with a full diffusion pass, then sequentially validates each token against the true autoregressive distribution using a cosine-similarity threshold; divergent tokens are re-masked and regenerated. For validated tokens, Guided Diffusion Sampling replaces the full 100-step denoising with only 10 steps by blending the diffusion model's predicted mean with the lookahead branch's coarse output, weighted by a monotonically decreasing cosine schedule γ_t = 1 − cos²(πt/2T_g) that applies strong guidance in early (coarse-semantic) steps and weak guidance in late (fine-texture) steps. This decouples semantic validation from detail refinement, reducing per-token diffusion cost by roughly an order of magnitude while preserving global coherence.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, FastHybrid achieves up to 1.97× speedup (MAR-B: 21.4s → 10.8s) with only a 0.11 FID increase (2.32 → 2.43) and a 3.2 IS improvement (281.1 → 284.3). For the larger MAR-L model, the speedup is 1.92× (26.9s → 13.9s) with FID 1.82 → 1.90 and IS 296.1 → 303.8. The MAR-H model achieves 1.69× speedup (35.7s → 21.0s) with FID 1.59 → 1.70 and IS 299.1 → 309.2. Notably, FastHybrid-H-64 surpasses the smaller MAR-B-64 baseline in both speed and quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unaccelerated MAR baseline, FastHybrid achieves 1.69–1.97× speedup across all model sizes while incurring only 0.11–0.18 FID degradation and actually improving IS by 3–10 points. Compared to LazyMAR, FastHybrid is substantially faster (e.g., 1.97× vs 1.13× for Base, 1.92× vs 1.33× for Large) and uses significantly less memory (1.30× vs 1.78× for Base, 1.07× vs 1.83× for Huge), while also achieving better FID and IS. Against CSpD, FastHybrid dominates on all axes: CSpD requires an extra 22–43% parameter overhead, degrades FID dramatically (3.45 vs 1.90 for Large, 3.91 vs 1.70 for Huge), and provides only marginal speedup (1.09× and 1.34×) compared to FastHybrid's 1.92× and 1.69×. The paper also notes that FastHybrid is orthogonal to LazyMAR (targeting diffusion denoising vs. AR latency), so the two can be combined for compounded speedups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MAR (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024), a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion image generation model that replaces VQ token prediction with continuous latent prediction plus a diffusion head. Conceptually, it adapts the lookahead decoding and speculative decoding ideas from LLM inference acceleration (Leviathan et al., Fu et al.) to the visual generation setting, but departs from prior visual acceleration methods (CSpD, LazyMAR, LANTERN, PAR) by identifying the diffusion denoising loop—not the autoregressive loop—as the dominant computational bottleneck in hybrid AR-diffusion models. The Guided Diffusion Sampling mechanism is a novel bridge that repurposes coarse parallel predictions as a trajectory prior for the diffusion head, a design not present in prior speculative or parallel decoding work for images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free recipe (~2× speedup with ≤0.11 FID increase) for accelerating the currently dominant hybrid AR-diffusion paradigm, and its orthogonality to AR-side methods like LazyMAR suggests combinable gains. However, evaluation is limited to a single backbone (MAR), a single dataset (ImageNet 256×256), and class-conditional generation; generalization to text-to-image, higher resolutions, or other hybrid architectures (HART, DART, Emu) remains untested. An open question is whether the lookahead-then-verify strategy scales gracefully as the number of patches grows (e.g., 512×512 or video), where the parallelism window may shrink relative to total sequence length.
