## Problem

Pruning-based unlearning for diffusion models erases concepts by setting associated weights to zero, but this creates an overlooked security vulnerability: the visible pruning locations leak side-channel information about where critical concept-related parameters once existed. The paper asks whether an attacker can exploit this location information alone—without access to original weight magnitudes, training data, or retraining—to reconstruct the pruned weights and revive supposedly erased concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263769/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attack takes a pruned Stable Diffusion v1.5 model (16 FFN layers pruned by ConceptPrune) and uses only the remaining non-zero weights within each layer plus the binary pruning mask indicating which positions were zeroed. No training data, text prompts, or access to the original pre-pruning model is required. The output is a fully revived diffusion model where erased concepts are restored, evaluated via ResNet-50 top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, CLIP similarity/score on artist-style prompts, and NudeNet detection counts on NSFW prompt benchmarks.

## Method

The key insight is that weight signs matter far more than magnitudes for concept revival—restoring correct signs with approximate magnitudes substantially recovers erased concepts. The three-stage framework first applies SoftImpute (a scalable low-rank matrix completion algorithm) to each FFN layer's weight matrix, using the observed non-pruned entries to estimate the missing pruned entries and extract sign information. A Top-K Sign Retention module then filters to only the highest-magnitude recovered signs, discarding low-confidence ones to suppress noise from incorrect sign estimates. Finally, Neuron-Max Scaling assigns each retained weight the maximum magnitude among its remaining neuron peers, which empirically outperforms mean or sampled magnitude strategies. The paper also proposes a Gaussian obfuscation defense that replaces zeroed weights with samples from N(0, σ_M²), with a closed-form analysis (Eq. 7) characterizing the security-utility tradeoff between pruning concealment and unlearning fidelity.

## Results

On the 12-class ImageNet object unlearning task, the proposed NMS attack recovers the average top-1 accuracy of erased concepts from 8% (ConceptPrune-unlearned) to 54%, while preserving class accuracy at 91% (vs. 93% for the original pretrained model). For artistic-style unlearning, NMS restores average CLIP similarity from 0.25 (unlearned) to 0.30 (near the pretrained 0.31) with FID of 18.93 (vs. 21.45 for the unlearned model). On NSFW content, nudity detections rise from 74/57/22 (unlearned) to 118/172/57 on I2P/MMA/Ring-A-Bell respectively. The entire recovery pipeline runs in approximately 7 minutes without any data or retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: ConceptPrune (the unlearned model itself) and Quant Recover (a 4-bit quantization-based recovery method originally designed for LLMs). NMS dramatically outperforms Quant Recover on erased-class accuracy: 54% vs. 12% average top-1 accuracy across 12 ImageNet classes, a 4.5× improvement. On preserved-class accuracy, NMS (91%) also surpasses Quant Recover (79%) and ConceptPrune (80%), staying within 2% of the original pretrained model (93%). For artistic-style unlearning, NMS achieves 0.30 average CLIP similarity vs. 0.26 for Quant Recover and 0.25 for ConceptPrune, while also achieving the best FID (18.93 vs. 22.36 and 21.45 respectively). The gap is particularly stark on classes where ConceptPrune achieves near-zero accuracy (e.g., Chain Saw: 0.00 → 0.40; Mountain Bike: 0.01 → 0.58), confirming that the pruning locations alone contain sufficient information for concept revival.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes low-rank matrix completion (SoftImpute, IST-SVD) from the matrix recovery literature as an attack tool against pruning-based unlearning in diffusion models, a paradigm established by ConceptPrune and Sculpting Memory. It extends the Quant Recover baseline from LLM unlearning to the diffusion setting but introduces a fundamentally different attack vector: exploiting the spatial locations of zeroed weights as side-channel information rather than quantization artifacts. The paper carves new ground by identifying that pruning footprints themselves constitute a security vulnerability, a concern absent from prior pruning-based unlearning work that focused on erasure effectiveness and robustness to adversarial prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This finding challenges the core security assumption of pruning-based unlearning—that zeroing concept-related weights irreversibly erases them—by showing that the pruning mask alone enables data-free, training-free concept revival in under seven minutes. The practical implications are significant for any deployment relying on pruning for compliance (GDPR, CCPA), as the attack requires no training data or retraining. Key limitations include the white-box assumption, single-concept focus, and evaluation on only SD-v1.5 with ConceptPrune; open questions include whether similar vulnerabilities persist under black-box access, how multi-concept pruning interacts with revival, and whether an attacker can infer which concept was forgotten without prior knowledge.
