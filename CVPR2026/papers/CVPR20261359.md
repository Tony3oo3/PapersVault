## Problem

Segmentation-to-segmentation correspondence between natural 2D images and untextured 3D shapes across different domains remains largely underexplored. Existing cross-modality methods either require heavy supervision, are restricted to specific shape templates, or only handle within-modality matching, leaving a gap for flexible, training-free correspondence that bridges the substantial appearance, geometry, and viewpoint differences between colored images and textureless meshes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261359/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-clicked pixel in an arbitrary RGB image (photo, sketch, or drawing) and a textureless 3D mesh as input, producing a 3D segmentation mask that identifies the semantically corresponding part on the mesh. The correspondence is bidirectional: clicking a mesh vertex instead yields the matching 2D image region. The entire pipeline runs in approximately 4 seconds on an A40 GPU without any training annotations, and the method is not restricted to specific object categories or predefined part taxonomies.

## Method

The core contribution is the Best Segmentation Buddies (BSB) matching scheme, which relaxes the strict mutual-nearest-neighbor constraint to a region-level condition: a vertex is a valid match for a clicked pixel if the vertex's most similar pixel in feature space falls within the pixel's segmentation mask, and the two associated segmentation masks achieve the highest IoU. To enable cross-modality feature comparison, DINOv2 visual features are extracted per image pixel and distilled onto the mesh surface via an MLP trained on multi-view renderings (following iSeg). SAM provides 2D click-based segmentation masks at both object and part granularity, while iSeg's distilled 3D segmentation model produces the final shape part from the matched vertex. This region-to-region relaxation accommodates the modality gap in feature space while filtering out texture-only image elements that lack geometric counterparts on the mesh.

## Results

On the PartNet-based evaluation (265 meshes, images generated via ControlNet from depth-conditioned renderings), BSB achieves a correspondence success rate of 0.74, outperforming NBB (0.64 different-view / 0.66 same-view) and DIFT (0.39 different-view / 0.48 same-view). The paper also reports a strong fidelity signal: the IoU between the original clicked-pixel mask and the mask of the matched vertex's nearest-neighbor pixel is 0.98 for regions with a true 3D counterpart versus 0.01 for texture-only regions lacking geometric correspondence, confirming that BSB reliably detects the presence or absence of a valid match.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BSB outperforms both zero-shot baselines on the PartNet correspondence task. NBB (Neural Best-Buddies, Aberman et al. 2018) is the stronger baseline at 0.64–0.66 success rate; it relies on sparse mutual nearest-neighbor matching in DINO feature space, which is degraded by the cross-modality gap between textured images and untextured mesh renderings. DIFT (Tang et al. 2023) performs substantially worse at 0.39–0.48, as its diffusion-model feature similarity is highly sensitive to the drastic appearance difference between a colored image and a textureless rendering. BSB's advantage (0.74) comes from relaxing the strict mutual-nearest-neighbor constraint to a segmentation-level check, which tolerates the modality-induced feature discrepancy while still filtering out texture-only regions with no geometric counterpart. In the supplementary perceptual study on diverse in-the-wild image–shape pairs without ground truth, BSB is also consistently preferred over both baselines, with NBB and DIFT producing visibly incorrect matches (e.g., mapping a narwhal's tusk to a unicorn's ear, or confusing two similarly oriented lamp rods).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Best-Buddies (NBB) matching paradigm by relaxing the strict mutual-nearest-neighbor constraint to a segmentation-level criterion: a vertex qualifies as a 'best segmentation buddy' if its most similar pixel falls within the clicked pixel's segmentation mask. The 3D feature distillation pipeline (lifting DINOv2 features onto mesh surfaces via an MLP) and the click-based 3D segmentation model are both inherited directly from iSeg, which the authors repurpose as a correspondence tool rather than a standalone segmentation method. Prior cross-modality correspondence work (CSE, SHIC, Canonical Surface Mapping) either requires full supervision, is tied to specific templates, or produces point-to-point dense mappings without part-level semantics; BSB departs from all of these by operating at the segmentation granularity in a fully zero-shot, template-free manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BSB is, to the authors' knowledge, the first method to compute both cross-modality (image-to-shape) and cross-domain (e.g., owl-to-airplane) correspondences without any training data, opening a practical path for image-guided 3D editing tasks like local texturing. The clean IoU separation (0.98 for semantically matching regions vs. 0.01 for texture-only elements) provides a useful diagnostic for detecting the absence of correspondence, which prior methods lack. Limitations include reliance on a textured 2D segmentation model (SAM) and a distilled 3D segmentation model (iSeg), evaluation on synthetic PartNet-derived images rather than truly in-the-wild pairs with ground truth, and baselines that are adapted from image-to-image correspondence rather than purpose-built for cross-modality. Open directions the authors identify include extending to video, NeRFs, 3D Gaussian splats, and using the correspondences to update the underlying visual features to be correspondence-aware.
