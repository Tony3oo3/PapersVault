## Problem

Existing streaming video understanding focuses on past-dependent (causal) reasoning where evidence is already available at question time, leaving answer timing largely unexplored. In proactive scenarios where questions precede supporting evidence, there is no mechanism for models to decide when sufficient evidence has appeared, nor a metric to evaluate whether responses are given at an appropriate moment. This paper formalizes readiness-aware streaming understanding and introduces both an evaluation metric (ARS) and a framework (StreamReady) to jointly optimize answer correctness and timing.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames arriving sequentially in a streaming fashion (no full-video access) along with text questions that may precede the relevant visual evidence. At each timestep, a lightweight Readiness Head outputs a scalar readiness score in [0,1]; when this score exceeds a fixed threshold (0.35), the fused long-term visual and contextual representation is passed to the LLM (Qwen-2-VL) to generate a text answer. The fixed-size hierarchical memory (centroids and prototypes) keeps inference cost constant regardless of video length, avoiding the growing KV-cache overhead of prior streaming methods.

## Method

StreamReady maintains a three-level Visual Memory Tree (raw frames → K-means centroids → coarse prototypes) updated via EMA-based clustering, alongside a Contextual Memory Bank storing past QA representations for multi-turn reasoning. A dual-branch Q-Former performs query-aware retrieval: a short-term branch attends to recent frames, while a long-term branch uses coarse-to-fine retrieval over prototypes and centroids to isolate relevant evidence. The key novelty is a learnable <RDY> token embedded within the long-term Q-Former representation, monitored by a small MLP Readiness Head that outputs a readiness score gating the LLM trigger. The readiness signal is trained with pseudo-supervision derived from similarity between the reasoning representation and centroid-level memory, using a pairwise contrastive loss plus an L1 temporal coherence regularizer, with gradients stopped from the reasoning module so that 'what to answer' and 'when to answer' are learned independently.

## Results

On ProReady-QA, StreamReady achieves 56.4% accuracy, 0.69 ARS, and 0.53 effective accuracy (Acc_e), surpassing the best online baseline StreamBridge (53.1% Acc, 0.60 ARS, 0.42 Acc_e) by ~3% in accuracy and ~9% in ARS. On streaming benchmarks (StreamingBench, OVOBench), StreamReady reaches 63.4% and 68.2% average accuracy, outperforming the next-best online method ViSpeak (58.6%/59.4%) by up to ~5% on proactive tasks. On offline long-video benchmarks, StreamReady attains 65.8% (VideoMME), 71.3% (MLVU), 71.8% (MVBench), and 70.4% (EgoSchema), leading all 7B-parameter baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamReady outperforms all 19 online and offline baselines on ProReady-QA, with the largest gaps over StreamBridge (+3.3% Acc, +0.09 ARS) and InfiniPot-V (+4.4% Acc, +0.22 ARS), confirming that the readiness mechanism specifically improves timing rather than just content. Among online streaming baselines, StreamReady leads ViSpeak by ~5% on proactive StreamingBench tasks (48.2 vs. 43.9 on StrmCont.) and by ~9% on OVOBench proactive (58.8 vs. 54.3), while also dominating non-proactive tasks (78.3 vs. 70.4 on StrmReal). On offline long-video benchmarks, StreamReady surpasses the strongest offline method HierarQ by 2.1% on VideoMME, 1.9% on MLVU, 4.2% on MVBench, and 3.1% on EgoSchema, and beats the best online method StreamBridge by 1.4% on VideoMME and 3.5% on EgoSchema. Notably, offline MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video at 0.31 Acc_e) suffer severe ARS penalties (0.40) because they cannot defer answers, while StreamReady's readiness gating narrows the gap between raw accuracy and effective accuracy from 0.15 (StreamBridge) to 0.03 (StreamReady). The consistent wins across all nine benchmarks indicate that the hierarchical memory and query-aware retrieval provide broad generalization beyond the readiness-specific formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamReady builds directly on HierarQ's dual-branch Q-Former for query-aware hierarchical reasoning, adapting it from offline to streaming settings. It departs from prior streaming video QA work (Flash-VStream, StreamBridge, Dispider, ViSpeak) that treats answer correctness as the sole objective, by introducing a lightweight learnable <RDY> token and readiness head that explicitly decides *when* to answer — replacing heavier auxiliary-MLLM or prompt-heuristic approaches. The hierarchical Visual Memory Tree (FIFO → K-means centroids → EMA prototypes) extends memory-based streaming architectures with a multi-granular, query-retrievable structure, while the Contextual Memory Bank adds multi-turn semantic history absent in most prior streaming systems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ARS metric and ProReady-QA benchmark expose a previously unmeasured failure mode: models that are 'correct' but answer at the wrong time, which is critical for real-time applications like surveillance and robotics. The finding that raw accuracy and effective accuracy diverge sharply (e.g., StreamBridge achieves 53.1% Acc but only 0.42 Acc_e) suggests the field has been overestimating streaming model readiness. Open questions include whether the pseudo-supervision strategy (similarity-based evidence localization) generalizes to domains without clear visual evidence boundaries, whether the asymmetric penalty design (harsh early, mild late) is universally appropriate, and whether the readiness mechanism scales to multi-modal or multi-agent streaming settings.
