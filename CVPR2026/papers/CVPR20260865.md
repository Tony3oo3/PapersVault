## Problem

Existing multi-modal GCD methods rely heavily on inter-modality alignment (e.g., CLIP-style contrastive loss) while neglecting intra-modality alignment, leading to imbalanced compression of embeddings where known categories are over-compressed relative to unknown ones. This imbalanced compression, combined with intra-modal misalignment induced by inter-modal losses, degrades the clustering accuracy critical for discovering unknown categories. The paper asks how to properly harness intra-modality alignment in multi-modal representation learning to achieve balanced, structured representations for both known and unknown categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB query images and a pre-constructed tag/attribute lexicon (ImageNet category names plus GPT-3-generated attributes) as input. Textual representations are generated by retrieving the top-c most similar tag-attribute pairs via CLIP-H/14 cosine similarity, then aggregating their individual CLIP text embeddings with a weighted sum (α=0.5 for the top-1 pair, remainder split equally among the rest). The output is a pseudo-label per unlabeled image, determined by argmax of the element-wise sum of dual-branch classifier predictions (one for image embeddings, one for text embeddings), followed by Hungarian matching to compute clustering accuracy.

## Method

SSR²-GCD replaces the standard CLIP inter-modal contrastive loss with a Semi-Supervised Rate Reduction (SSR²) objective derived from Maximal Coding Rate Reduction, which simultaneously expands the overall embedding space (via −R(Z)) while compressing each category's embeddings into low-dimensional subspaces with balanced matrix ranks (via R_c^s for known labels and R_c^u for pseudo-labels). This achieves balanced compression across known and unknown categories, resolving the imbalanced compression issue inherent in contrastive losses. A Retrieval-based Text Aggregation (RTA) strategy embeds top-c tag-attribute pairs separately and aggregates them with a weighted sum to work around CLIP's 20-token limit while incorporating richer textual information. Training proceeds in two stages: a warm-up phase using only the supervised SSR² term plus classifier losses, followed by an alignment phase that adds the unsupervised SSR² term and a co-teaching loss to synchronize the dual-branch classifiers. The key empirical finding is that intra-modal alignment via SSR² alone outperforms combining it with inter-modal CLIP loss, as explicit inter-modal alignment actively damages intra-modal consistency needed for clustering.

## Results

SSR²-GCD achieves state-of-the-art clustering accuracy across all eight benchmark datasets. On the fine-grained datasets, it reaches 89.2% (Stanford Cars), 95.7% (Oxford Pets), and 93.5% (Flowers102) on 'All' categories, outperforming the strongest multi-modal baseline TextGCD by 3.1%, 2.0%, and 6.3% respectively. On ImageNet-100 and ImageNet-1k, it achieves 92.1% and 66.7% 'All' accuracy, surpassing GET (91.7% and 62.4%) and TextGCD (88.0% and 64.8%). Notably, SSR²-GCD narrows the Old/New accuracy gap compared to prior methods, e.g., on Stanford Cars the gap shrinks from 7.9% (TextGCD) to 5.8% (SSR²-GCD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSR²-GCD consistently outperforms all multi-modal baselines (CLIP-GCD, TextGCD, GET) on every dataset where they are compared, with the largest margins on Flowers102 (+6.3% over TextGCD, +8.0% over GET) and Stanford Cars (+3.1% over TextGCD, +10.7% over GET). Against the strongest uni-modal baseline Hyp-SelEx (DINOv1 backbone), SSR²-GCD wins on all datasets except CUB, where Hyp-SelEx achieves 79.8% vs. 78.3% (the paper notes this is complementary since HypCD operates in hyperbolic space). Compared to TextGCD specifically, SSR²-GCD improves 'New' category accuracy by 2.6–8.0 points across datasets, indicating that the semi-supervised rate reduction loss is particularly effective for discovering unknown categories. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that all three components—dual-branch classifiers, RTA, and L_SSR²—are necessary, with the full combination yielding 89.2% on Stanford Cars vs. 75.2% for the baseline without any of them.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal GCD line initiated by CLIP-GCD, TextGCD, and GET, which all rely on CLIP-style inter-modal contrastive loss for representation learning. The key departure is the argument that inter-modal alignment without explicit intra-modal structure leads to intra-modal misalignment, and the solution is to replace contrastive losses with a Semi-Supervised Rate Reduction (SSR²) objective adapted from the Maximal Coding Rate Reduction principle [28] to the semi-supervised GCD setting. The text generation pipeline builds on TextGCD's retrieval-based approach but replaces prompt concatenation with a weighted aggregation (RTA) of multiple tag/attribute candidates to work within CLIP's 20-token limit. The clustering and co-teaching components are inherited from SimGCD and TextGCD respectively, making the core novelty concentrated in the representation learning loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode in multi-modal GCD: contrastive-based intra-modal losses (e.g., SimGCD-style) cause imbalanced compression where known categories are over-compressed relative to unknown ones, degrading clustering. The SSR² loss addresses this by enforcing balanced matrix ranks across categories, a property not previously exploited in GCD. However, the approach remains tied to CLIP's architecture and a pre-constructed tag/attribute lexicon, limiting its applicability to domains where such external knowledge is unavailable. An open question is whether the rate-reduction principle can be extended to other modality pairs (e.g., audio-visual) or to settings where the number of unknown categories is not known a priori.
