## Problem

Prompt learning in vision-language models is vulnerable to noisy labels in few-shot settings, where even a small number of corrupted labels disproportionately bias gradient updates. Existing prompt-based noisy-label methods rely on single-view prompts, rigid explicit negative labels, and fixed confidence thresholds for denoising, making them unable to perform fine-grained, region-aware separation of clean and noisy semantic signals within individual images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a frozen ResNet-50 encoder into a global feature and an L×d local patch feature map) paired with noisy class labels in a 16-shot-per-class regime, and produces a final class prediction computed as (1 − p^n) · p^c, where p^c is the clean-prompt confidence and p^n is the noise-aware confidence. During training, it additionally outputs refined pseudo-labels for identified mislabeled samples, constructing a denoised training set that combines retained clean samples with OT-corrected noisy ones.

## Method

NA-MVP constructs N clean-oriented and N noise-aware learnable prompts per class, each consisting of M context tokens plus a class token, encoded by a frozen Transformer text encoder. Local image patches are aligned to these multi-view prompts via Unbalanced Optimal Transport (UOT) with relaxed marginal constraints (T·1 ≤ μ), solved by Sinkhorn iterations, enabling partial matching that suppresses noisy regions while preserving prompt diversity. An auxiliary image-text bi-directional prompt (ITBP) loss explicitly separates clean and noisy semantics by encouraging alignment with clean prompts and repulsion from noise-aware prompts. For label refinement, an adaptive threshold φ_{i,k} derived from the ratio of noise-aware to clean-prompt similarities identifies mislabeled samples, which are then corrected via classical OT (strict mass conservation) aligning global image features with class-level prompt features, while clean samples remain unaltered. Training uses GCE loss plus ITBP loss in an early phase, then activates selective OT-based label refinement after T_sup epochs.

## Results

NA-MVP reports top-1 accuracy (%) on five synthetic noisy benchmarks (Caltech101, DTD, Flowers102, OxfordPets, UCF101) under symmetric noise rates from 12.5% to 75% and asymmetric noise at 25% and 50%, using a 16-shot protocol. Under the most severe symmetric noise (75%), NA-MVP achieves 89.37% on Caltech101, 48.63% on DTD, 76.47% on Flowers102, 86.23% on OxfordPets, and 63.93% on UCF101, consistently leading or matching the strongest baseline (NLPrompt) by 1–15 points depending on the dataset. On the real-world noisy dataset Food101N, NA-MVP reaches 76.10% at 4-shot and 77.03% at 32-shot, outperforming NLPrompt by 5.53 and 0.16 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NA-MVP outperforms CoOp (the standard prompt-learning baseline) by a wide margin across all datasets and noise levels, with gaps reaching 42+ points on DTD at 75% symmetric noise (48.63% vs. 17.27%). Against GCE (CoOp + Generalized Cross-Entropy), NA-MVP leads by 2–33 points, with the largest advantage on OxfordPets at 75% noise (86.23% vs. 53.17%). Compared to JoAPR, NA-MVP consistently wins by 2–13 points, again most pronounced under high noise. The closest competitor is NLPrompt, which uses global OT-based relabeling; NA-MVP edges it out on 4 of 5 datasets under most noise settings (e.g., +2.67 on DTD at 75% Sym, +15.46 on OxfordPets at 75% Sym), though NLPrompt slightly leads on Flowers102 at 75% Sym (76.80 vs. 76.47) and on UCF101 at 37.5% Sym (72.83 vs. 72.30). On the real-world Food101N benchmark, NA-MVP surpasses NLPrompt by 5.53 points at 4-shot, narrowing to 0.16 at 32-shot, confirming its advantage is most pronounced in the extreme few-shot regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prompt-learning-for-VLMs paradigm (CoOp, CoCoOp) into the noisy-label few-shot regime by combining three previously separate ideas: multi-prompt designs (PLOT, CLIPN, DualCoOp), unbalanced optimal transport for flexible distribution matching, and selective label refinement. The key conceptual departure from prior prompt-based noisy-label methods (NLPrompt, JoAPR, GCE) is shifting from global image-to-prompt matching with explicit negative labels to region-aware, bi-directional patch-to-prompt alignment where clean and noise-aware prompts serve as complementary implicit signals. The ITBP loss borrows its structure from CLIPN's bi-directional contrastive objective but repurposes it for separating clean from noisy semantics rather than OOD detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The combination of few-shot supervision and label noise is a practically critical regime for deploying VLMs in real-world pipelines where annotation budgets are small and quality is imperfect. NA-MVP's selective refinement mechanism (correcting only samples below an adaptive alignment threshold) addresses a known failure mode of global pseudo-labeling—over-correction at low noise rates—which is validated in the ablations. Limitations include evaluation on only five synthetic-noise datasets plus one real-world set, a single VLM backbone (CLIP with ResNet-50), and a fixed 16-shot protocol; open questions include whether the UOT-based local alignment scales to larger class counts or different VLM architectures, and whether the bi-directional prompt design transfers to video or multi-modal settings beyond image-text.
