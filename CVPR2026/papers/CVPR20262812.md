## Problem

Pointmap-based feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (DUSt3R, Spann3R, VGGT) operate on 2D pixel grids and lack explicit spatial compactness, meaning the many-to-one correspondence between pixels and 3D scene points is never explicitly enforced. Additionally, multi-view transformers suffer from quadratic complexity that confines them to offline settings, and no feed-forward uncalibrated VO/SfM pipeline had previously surpassed optimization-based SLAM and SfM systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262812/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts uncalibrated multi-view RGB images (5–16 frames during training, arbitrary counts at inference), sequential video frames for VO, or unordered image collections for SfM, with no camera intrinsics required. Outputs include per-frame camera poses, metric-scale depth maps, dense 3D point clouds, and pointmaps in a shared coordinate frame. The VO and SfM pipelines are training-free and model-agnostic, meaning they can wrap any compatible pointmap front-end without retraining, and produce camera trajectories or registered image sets with constant per-frame complexity.

## Method

AMB3R introduces a sparse voxel backend that fuses multi-view geometric features into a compact 3D representation: pointmap predictions and per-pixel features are binned into a sparse voxel grid (0.01 normalized-space resolution), serialized via space-filling curves into 1D sequences, processed by a Point Transformer v3, and deserialized back to voxel space. Per-point features are retrieved via KNN interpolation and injected into the frozen VGGT front-end decoder through zero-convolution, preserving pre-trained attention and confidence while enabling explicit 3D geometric reasoning. A lightweight scale head regresses per-frame metric log-depth from encoder features to recover metric scale without global optimization. For uncalibrated VO and SfM, the pipelines exploit the prior that pointmap predictions are expressed in the anchor frame's coordinate system up to an unknown scale, eliminating explicit Kabsch-Umeyama alignment; AMB3R-VO uses keyframe-based active memory with O(1) per-frame complexity, while AMB3R-SfM applies a divide-and-conquer clustering strategy for large unordered collections.

## Results

On multi-view depth estimation (RMVDB), AMB3R achieves an average relative error of 1.7% and δ₁.₀₃ of 87.3%, establishing new SOTA over VGGT (2.4%, 81.3%) and concurrent π³ (1.8%, 85.6%). For 3D reconstruction, AMB3R attains 4.64 cm relative error on ETH3D and 0.81 cm on DTU, outperforming VGGT (6.02 cm / 0.83 cm) and π³ (5.82 cm / 1.57 cm). In uncalibrated visual odometry, AMB3R-VO achieves 3.2 cm ATE RMSE on TUM and 2.6 cm on ETH3D SLAM, surpassing optimization-based SLAM systems (DROID-SLAM: 3.8 cm, GO-SLAM: 3.5 cm) and prior feed-forward methods (MUSt3R: 7.1 cm, VGGT-SLAM: 5.3 cm). For SfM, AMB3R-SfM reaches 98.2% RRA@5 on ETH3D without any bundle adjustment, compared to 81.2% for MASt3R-SfM and 49.0% for COLMAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMB3R consistently outperforms VGGT (its frozen front-end) across all evaluated tasks: multi-view depth (1.7% vs 2.4% avg rel), 3D reconstruction (4.64 vs 6.02 cm on ETH3D), camera pose (86.3 vs 85.3 AUC@30), and monocular depth (3.0 vs 3.6 rel on NYUv2), confirming the backend's contribution. Against concurrent feed-forward methods π³ and MapAnything, AMB3R wins on multi-view depth (1.7% vs 1.8% and 3.6% avg rel) and 3D reconstruction (4.64 vs 5.82 cm ETH3D rel), while requiring roughly one order of magnitude less training compute. In uncalibrated VO/SLAM, AMB3R-VO achieves 3.2 cm ATE on TUM, beating MUSt3R (7.1 cm), VGGT-SLAM (5.3 cm), and even calibrated optimization-based systems like DROID-SLAM (3.8 cm) and GO-SLAM (3.5 cm); on ETH3D SLAM the gap widens to 2.6 cm vs MUSt3R's 11.2 cm. For SfM, AMB3R-SfM reaches 98.2% RRA@5 on ETH3D without any bundle adjustment, substantially outperforming MASt3R-SfM (81.2%), COLMAP (49.0%), and ACE-Zero (16.4%), and on Tanks&Temples it achieves 95.0% RRA@5 on the training split vs ACE-Zero's 73.9%. The only area where AMB3R does not clearly lead is dynamic video depth on KITTI, where π³ (3.6% rel) slightly edges out AMB3R (4.1%), though AMB3R was not specifically trained on dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

AMB3R builds directly on VGGT as a frozen front-end, departing from the pure 2D-to-3D pointmap regression paradigm of DUSt3R/Spann3R/VGGT by introducing a sparse voxel backend serialized via space-filling curves and processed by Point Transformer v3, thereby enforcing explicit spatial compactness that prior per-pixel regression lacks. The AMB3R-VO and AMB3R-SfM pipelines are training-free, model-agnostic extensions that exploit a previously underutilized prior in pointmap models—predictions are expressed in the reference frame up to scale—to eliminate the need for Kabsch-Umeyama alignment and optimization-based backends that prior systems (VGGT-SLAM, MASt3R-SLAM) rely on. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between feed-forward pointmap networks and classical compact 3D representations (TSDF grids, voxel hashing) by showing that a lightweight 3D backend can be grafted onto a frozen transformer front-end via zero-convolution injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a purely feed-forward, uncalibrated VO pipeline can surpass optimization-based SLAM systems (with calibration and loop closure) on TUM challenges a long-standing assumption that iterative refinement is necessary for accurate tracking. The practical impact is significant: achieving SOTA across 7 tasks with only ~80 H100 GPU hours of training (on top of frozen VGGT weights) and open-sourcing code, weights, and an evaluation toolkit lowers the barrier for the community. Open questions include whether the divide-and-conquer SfM strategy scales to very large unordered collections (thousands of images), whether the backend generalizes to other front-end architectures beyond VGGT, and how the system handles truly dynamic scenes without task-specific fine-tuning.
