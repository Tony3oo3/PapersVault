## Problem

Existing video MLLM benchmarks target general content comprehension but lack structured, fine-grained evaluation of foundational human perceptual skills such as nuanced emotion perception, person recognition, behavioral analysis, and speech-visual alignment. Moreover, no scalable framework exists for constructing such benchmarks from raw in-the-wild video with minimal human annotation effort.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263956/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a video clip (sourced from Pexels and MF2, split into scene-level segments and filtered for visible faces) with a multiple-choice question of variable option count (N-choose-1). For speech-visual alignment tasks, the audio track is also provided to audio-visual models, while visual-only models receive video frames alone. The model outputs a single selected answer option, and performance is reported as per-task and per-category accuracy across 2,475 instances spanning 16 tasks.

## Method

The framework comprises two automated pipelines. The Human-Centric Video Annotation Pipeline chains 20+ operators (face/body tracking, demographic inference, MLLM-based appearance and facial description, audio tagging, ASR, active speaker detection, speech emotion recognition, and voice demographics) to produce dense multimodal annotations from raw video. The Distractor-Included QA Synthesis Pipeline then generates questions by feeding marked videos (with target persons highlighted or face-cropped) into a Video-MLLM, uses an ensemble of three MLLMs (Gemini, VideoLLaMA3, ShareGPT4Video) with preference voting to select the correct answer, and critically converts the ensemble's erroneous outputs into semantically plausible distractors. A final human verification step shuffles options and confirms or corrects the ground truth, reducing manual work to roughly 28% of cases. Post-processing removes ~6% of QAs where models answer correctly without visual input, mitigating answer leakage.

## Results

On HUMANVBENCH (2,475 multiple-choice questions across 16 fine-grained tasks), the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 73.4% overall accuracy, while the best open-source model Qwen-VL3(7B) reaches 53.4%. Human graduate-level annotators score 88.6%, revealing a substantial 15-point gap even for the top model. Emotion perception is the weakest category for all models (best: 53.0% by Gemini-2.5-flash vs. 84.6% human), and speech-visual alignment tasks (AVAD, SCM) remain near random-level for nearly all models except the Gemini series.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (73.4% overall) substantially outperforms Gemini-2.5-flash (64.5%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (65.6%), with the largest gains in Person Recognition (83.5% vs. 75.7%/71.4%) and Behavior Analysis (70.7% vs. 64.9%/60.7%). GPT-5 (61.2% on 12 vision tasks) improves markedly over GPT-4o (48.9%), but GPT-4o's unexpectedly weak Person Recognition (50.9%) and Emotion (33.6%) scores place it below several open-source models. Among open-source models, Qwen-VL3(7B) leads at 53.4% overall, narrowly ahead of InternVL3.5(8B) and VideoLLaMA3(7B) (both 52.3%), with Qwen-VL3 excelling in Person Recognition (67.6%) and Behavior (54.3%). Audio-visual open-source models perform poorly on speech-visual alignment: Qwen2.5-Omni (43.2% overall) is the only one above random on AVSM/ASD, while VideoLLaMA(7B) (25.5%) and VideoLLaMA2.1(7B) (37.4%) remain near or below random on these tasks. The Gemini series is the only family to achieve non-random performance on AVAD (72.3% for 2.5-Pro) and SCM (98.3%), tasks where all other models score at or near the 25–33% random baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two lines of prior work: (1) general video MLLM benchmarks (Video-MME, MVBench, LongVideoBench) that evaluate broad temporal and scene comprehension but lack structured, fine-grained human-centric diagnostics, and (2) human-centric or emotion-specific datasets (HumanBench, VEATIC, Social-IQ) that either focus on observable activities, use fixed discrete emotion labels, or conflate perception with higher-order social reasoning. The key methodological departure is the fully automated, model-driven construction pipeline: rather than relying on manual annotation or repurposing existing datasets, the authors use a multi-operator Data-Juicer framework for dense annotation and an MLLM ensemble whose collective errors are repurposed as semantically plausible distractors. The answer-leakage removal protocol is borrowed from Chen et al. [9], but the distractor-generation strategy—converting model failure modes into evaluation items—is novel in the video QA benchmarking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HUMANVBENCH exposes a concrete and actionable gap: even the strongest proprietary model (Gemini 2.5-Pro, 73.4% overall) trails human graders (88.6%) by ~15 points, with the largest deficits in nuanced emotion perception and cross-modal speech-visual alignment (lip-reading tasks near random for all models). The automated synthesis pipeline, which achieves a 3× efficiency gain over manual question creation and generalizes to non-human domains (pets, vehicles), offers a reusable methodology for the community to build fine-grained benchmarks at scale. Open questions include whether native temporal-token architectures can close the lip-sync gap, whether the frame-sampling artifacts identified in emotion recognition can be mitigated without increasing inference cost, and whether the ensemble-distractor approach transfers to domains where model errors are less semantically coherent.
