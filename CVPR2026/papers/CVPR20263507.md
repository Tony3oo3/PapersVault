## Problem

In incremental object detection, existing knowledge distillation methods tightly couple the student's detection head with its backbone, causing distillation gradients to conflict with new-class supervision at the head and injecting head-specific bias into backbone features. This coupling weakens distillation effectiveness and accelerates catastrophic forgetting, particularly when unlabeled old or future objects create foreground-background confusion across stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263507/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images processed by a Faster R-CNN detector with a ResNet-50 backbone, producing per-region category probabilities (covering all foreground classes plus background) and bounding-box regression offsets. In the incremental setting, each training stage provides annotations only for newly introduced classes, while objects from previous or future stages appear unlabeled in the images. The model is evaluated via mAP at IoU thresholds 0.5–0.95 (COCO) or AP@0.5 (VOC) across old, new, and all classes.

## Method

FaCHD decouples the student's classification head from its backbone by forwarding the student's ROI features through the frozen classification heads of two complementary teachers—an old-class expert (M_{t-1}) and a new-class intermediate teacher (M_t^{im})—producing cross-head predictions that are aligned with reconstructed teacher targets via KL divergence, so that distillation gradients flow exclusively through the backbone and ROI rather than the student head. A background probability reconstruction strategy fuses foreground and background estimates from both teachers in a region-partitioned manner (old-likely vs. new-likely regions) to build future-aware distillation targets. Complementarily, RPSC estimates per-class semantic drift by comparing ROI feature maps from the previous and current models, computes Gaussian-affinity-weighted offsets, and adds them to both global and local (hypersphere-based) prototypes of old classes. The classification head is then retrained in a frozen-backbone phase using a prototype-conditioned replay loss that balances old and new class prototype predictions, resetting decision boundaries without requiring old-class annotations.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC 2007 single-step incremental detection (mAP@0.5), the proposed FaCHD-RPSC achieves 75.9% overall in the 10-10 setting, 75.1% in 15-5, 75.9% in 19-1, and 76.0% in 5-15, consistently outperforming the strongest rehearsal-free baseline GDA-IOD by 1.0–1.9 mAP across settings. On MS COCO 2017, the method attains 35.5 AP (40+40) and 36.9 AP (70+10), surpassing BPF by 1.1 and 0.7 AP respectively. In the challenging multi-step 5-5 VOC protocol, the method reaches 66.9% overall mAP, exceeding BPF by 4.4% and GMDP-ABR* by 5.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all rehearsal-free baselines on overall mAP across all four VOC single-step settings. Against the strongest non-rehearsal competitor GDA-IOD [28], FaCHD-RPSC gains +1.0 mAP (10-10), +1.5 (15-5), +0.7 (19-1), and +1.9 (5-15) on the All metric, with particularly large improvements on old-class retention (e.g., +0.9 in 10-10, +1.3 in 5-15). Compared to BPF [30], the gains are even larger: +3.0 mAP in 10-10 and +3.0 in 5-15. On MS COCO, the method beats BPF by +1.1 AP (40+40) and +0.7 AP (70+10), and edges out the rehearsal-based NSGP-RePRE* [39] by +0.1 AP in 40+40 while matching it in 70+10. The one notable weakness is in the 19-1 setting where the New-class mAP (58.5) trails BPF (65.3) and GMDP-ILOD (67.9), though the overall score still leads. In multi-step 5-5 VOC, the method achieves 66.9% overall, surpassing BPF (62.5%) by 4.4% and GMDP-ABR* (61.1%) by 5.8%, demonstrating superior long-horizon stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge-distillation-based IOD line (ILOD, Faster ILOD, BPF) by decoupling the student's classification head from the backbone during distillation, so that distillation gradients regularize only the backbone rather than the shared head. The cross-head decoding mechanism is directly inspired by CrossKD (Wang et al., CVPR 2024), while the background probability reconstruction and region partitioning strategy are inherited from BPF (Mo et al., ECCV 2024). The RPSC module combines SDC-style semantic drift estimation (Yu et al., CVPR 2020) with multi-granularity prototypes (global plus hypersphere-based local prototypes from Wu et al., 2025) to recalibrate old-class decision boundaries at the head level. The key conceptual departure from prior IOD work is treating the detection head as a source of bias that should be excluded from the distillation gradient path, rather than as the target of distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling insight addresses a practical gradient-conflict problem that plagues KD-based IOD: when distillation and new-class supervision both act on the same head, head-specific bias (favoring new classes) leaks into backbone features and accelerates forgetting. The rehearsal-free design is important for privacy-constrained deployments where storing old-class exemplars is infeasible. However, the method is validated only on Faster R-CNN/ResNet-50 across two datasets, and the 19-1 setting reveals a notable weakness (58.5% mAP on the single new class vs. 75.8% for GDA-IOD), suggesting the approach struggles when a single new class must be learned against 19 old ones. Open questions include whether the decoupled cross-head strategy transfers to DETR-based or single-stage detectors, how it behaves over longer task sequences beyond 4 steps, and whether the prototype-based head retraining generalizes to open-vocabulary or long-tail detection scenarios.
