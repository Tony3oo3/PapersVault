## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models operate reactively in 2D image space, mapping current observations to actions without explicit 3D spatial modeling or anticipation of future scene and robot dynamics. This myopic formulation limits performance in manipulation tasks requiring precise 3D reasoning about object poses, clearances, and end-effector trajectories over a horizon of multiple steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262319/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction, multi-view RGB images (two 224×224 environment cameras plus one wrist camera), the robot's proprioceptive joint state, and the full 3D motion history of K tracked keypoints (7 joints + 1 end-effector for LIBERO/RoboCasa). It outputs a continuous action chunk of H=50 steps, each a 7-DoF end-effector command (Δx, Δθ, g) generated via conditional flow matching. Depth supervision is applied only to the two environment cameras over a 1.6m × 1.6m × 1.0m workspace discretized at 0.04m voxel resolution.

## Method

GeoPredict augments the π0 VLA backbone (PaliGemma VLM + flow-matching action expert) with two predictive modules that serve exclusively as training-time supervision. A Track Encoder compresses per-keypoint 3D motion history into compact tokens via cross-attention, and learnable Future Track Queries decode multi-step (H+1) 3D keypoint trajectories supervised by MSE loss. A separate 3D spatial query grid is processed through the transformer and decoded by a 3D voxel decoder into 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives; a track-guided refinement mechanism allocates additional Gaussians (N_G'=64 vs. N_G=4) to voxels intersecting predicted future keypoint positions, and the resulting scene is supervised via differentiable depth rendering with a masked L1 loss. All tokens are organized under a block-wise causal attention hierarchy (2D observations → 3D history → 3D predictive queries → proprioception → action noise), and at inference the predictive modules are entirely bypassed, leaving the action-generation pathway identical to the base π0 policy.

## Results

On RoboCasa Human-50 (24 long-horizon kitchen tasks, 50 demos per task), GeoPredict achieves a 52.4% average success rate, outperforming the π0 baseline (42.3%) by 10.1 points and GWM (39.2%) by 13.2 points. On LIBERO (4 suites, 50 demos per task), GeoPredict reaches 96.5% average success rate, surpassing the previous SOTA UniVLA (95.2%) and the π0 baseline (94.2%). In real-world evaluation on a DISCOVER arm, GeoPredict achieves 85.0% / 95.0% / 90.0% on Spatial / Geometry / Robustness tasks versus 60.0% / 50.0% / 35.0% for π0, with the largest gap (45 points) on the geometry generalization task involving unseen object sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoPredict outperforms all 13 compared baselines across both simulation benchmarks and real-world evaluation. On RoboCasa Human-50, it leads the π0 baseline by 10.1 points (52.4% vs 42.3%), GWM by 13.2 points (52.4% vs 39.2%), and BC-Transformer by 23.6 points (52.4% vs 28.8%), with the largest per-task gains on geometry-intensive sub-tasks like ODD (87.2% vs 55.2% for π0) and TNS (60.0% vs 43.2%). On LIBERO, GeoPredict (96.5%) surpasses the prior SOTA UniVLA (95.2%) by 1.3 points and the π0 baseline (94.2%) by 2.3 points, while maintaining a clear lead over 2D-centric methods like OpenVLA (76.5%) and SpatialVLA (78.1%) by roughly 18–20 points. In real-world testing, the advantage is most dramatic on the Geometry task (95.0% vs 50.0% for π0), where the policy must adapt grasps to unseen object sizes and orientations, and on the Robustness task (90.0% vs 35.0%), where visual distractors are introduced. The consistent pattern—larger gains on spatially and geometrically demanding tasks—supports the paper's claim that predictive 3D Gaussian geometry and kinematic priors specifically address the 3D-reasoning weakness of 2D-centric VLA policies.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoPredict builds directly on π0, a continuous-action VLA policy using conditional flow matching, and augments it with two predictive modules: a trajectory-level kinematic predictor and a 3D Gaussian Splatting geometry forecaster. It departs from prior future-prediction approaches (which forecast RGB frames, depths, or pointmaps in 2D) by operating in explicit 3D space with multi-view geometric consistency, and from GWM by restricting 3DGS to training-time supervision rather than inference-time world modeling. The track-guided refinement mechanism, which allocates additional Gaussian primitives along predicted keypoint trajectories, is a novel coupling of kinematic prediction and geometric capacity allocation not seen in prior VLA or world-modeling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core limitation of current VLA models—their 2D-centric, reactive nature—by showing that predictive 3D geometric priors, even when used only during training, substantially improve spatial reasoning and generalization in manipulation. The real-world geometry generalization result (50%→95%) suggests the learned 3D representations transfer to unseen object sizes, which is practically important for deployment. However, the approach requires calibrated multi-view RGB-D inputs and camera extrinsics, which the authors acknowledge as a scaling bottleneck; how well the method extends to unstructured environments with varying camera setups, or to higher-DoF and multi-arm systems, remains open. The limited real-world evaluation (20 trials per category, single robot platform) also leaves questions about robustness across hardware.
