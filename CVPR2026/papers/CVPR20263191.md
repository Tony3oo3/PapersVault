## Problem

YOLO detectors exhibit a striking asymmetry: their 'You Only Look Once' inference is extremely fast, yet training processes every image in every epoch regardless of whether the model has already learned from it, making training disproportionately slow (e.g., 43.9 h for YOLO11s on COCO vs. 6.5 h for Faster R-CNN). This exhaustive full-coverage paradigm implicitly assumes all images contribute equally throughout training, ignoring that image informativeness naturally shifts as the model evolves.

## Input / Output

AFSS operates on standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (640×640 for COCO/VOC, 1024×1024 for DOTA, 800×800 for DIOR-R) and produces conventional detection outputs (axis-aligned or oriented bounding boxes with class labels). The key distinction is that the effective training input per epoch is a dynamically selected subset of the full dataset, whose composition changes over time based on per-image learning sufficiency scores, rather than a fixed full-dataset traversal.

## Method

AFSS (Anti-Forgetting Sampling Strategy) measures each image's learning sufficiency as min(Precision, Recall) from the previous epoch and stratifies images into easy (>0.85), moderate (0.55–0.85), and hard (<0.55) tiers. Easy images are sparsely resampled at only 2% per epoch, with a forced-review mechanism that prioritizes images unseen for ≥10 epochs to prevent catastrophic forgetting; moderate images are selected at 40% with forced coverage of those unused for ≥3 epochs to maintain short-term stability; hard images are fully included every epoch. A state dictionary tracking per-image precision, recall, and last-used epoch is refreshed every 5 epochs, enabling the sampling schedule to co-evolve with the model's representation. The strategy is architecture-agnostic, requiring no model modifications, and plugs directly into the Ultralytics YOLO training loop.

## Results

On MS COCO 2017, AFSS accelerates YOLO-series detectors (v8/v10/11/12, scales n–x) by 1.43×–1.68× while matching or slightly improving AP (e.g., YOLO11s: 47.0→47.2 AP; YOLO12x: 55.2→55.4 AP). On PASCAL VOC 2007, speedups range from 1.60× to 1.69× with mAP gains of 0.1–0.2 points. For oriented object detection on DOTA-v1.0 and DIOR-R, AFSS achieves 1.63×–1.70× speedup with mAP maintained or improved (e.g., YOLO11m-OBB on DOTA: 80.9→80.9 mAP, 112.8h→67.1h). Overall, AFSS removes roughly 35–40% of training computation without any accuracy regression across all 40 model-dataset combinations tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFSS outperforms all four compared training strategies on both accuracy and speedup. Against Curriculum Learning (Bengio et al., ICML 2009), AFSS gains +3.5 AP (47.2 vs 43.7) and a faster 1.54× vs 1.35× speedup; the fixed easy-to-hard schedule starves boundary-near samples of supervision. Against Self-Paced Learning (Kumar et al., NeurIPS 2010), AFSS leads by +2.7 AP (47.2 vs 44.5) and 1.54× vs 1.30× speedup, because hard images remain fully engaged throughout training rather than being introduced only late. Against Data Pruning (Paul et al., NeurIPS 2021), AFSS achieves +6.7 AP (47.2 vs 40.5) and 1.54× vs 1.38× speedup, avoiding the irreversible information loss and gradient bias caused by permanently discarding images. Against Dataset Distillation (Qi et al., NeurIPS 2024), AFSS improves AP by +11.6 (47.2 vs 35.6) while also being faster (1.54× vs 1.50×), since synthetic samples fail to capture real-world noise and occlusion diversity. In every comparison, AFSS simultaneously achieves the highest accuracy and the highest training speedup, demonstrating that adaptive resampling with anti-forgetting mechanisms dominates both static and synthetic data-reduction approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

AFSS carves out a distinct niche among three existing lines of training-efficiency research: curriculum/self-paced learning (which introduces hard samples late and drops easy ones), dataset pruning (which permanently discards low-value images), and dataset distillation (which replaces real data with synthetic samples). Unlike all three, AFSS retains every image in the pool but modulates its participation frequency per epoch, using a min(Precision, Recall) learning-sufficiency metric to tier images into easy/moderate/hard groups with tailored anti-forgetting resampling. Conceptually, it draws on the importance-sampling insight that not all training examples contribute equally (Katharopoulos & Fleuret 2018; Kleinman et al. 2021), but applies it specifically to the full-coverage YOLO training loop rather than to loss-weighting or sample selection at the batch level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear: a 1.43×–1.68× training speedup across YOLOv8/v10/v11/v12 at all standard scales, with accuracy maintained or slightly improved, makes long YOLO training runs substantially cheaper without architectural changes. The conceptual takeaway—that a 'look once' detector does not need to see every image every epoch—challenges the default full-coverage assumption baked into most detection training pipelines. Open questions include whether the strategy generalizes beyond the YOLO family (e.g., two-stage detectors, transformer-based detectors), whether the fixed thresholds (0.85/0.55) and intervals (10-epoch review, 3-epoch coverage, 5-epoch state update) transfer across datasets of very different scale or class imbalance, and whether the modest accuracy gains (≈0.1–0.2 AP) are consistent under different random seeds or longer training schedules.
