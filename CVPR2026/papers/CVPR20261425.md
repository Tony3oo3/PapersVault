## Problem

Large 3D foundation models pre-trained with self-supervised learning are too computationally expensive for edge deployment, yet existing compression techniques like standard knowledge distillation or feature mimicry produce task-specific 'specialist' students that lose the downstream-agnostic generality that makes foundation models valuable. No prior method distills the entire representational manifold of a unimodal SSL model into a compact, portable, general-purpose proxy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud, tokenizes it into c token embeddings via Point-BERT-style patching with positional encodings, and the student compresses these into s learnable SuperTokens (s ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}, with s ≪ c) before reconstructing the full c-token representation. At inference, the student operates standalone without the teacher, accepting the same tokenized input and producing per-token embeddings suitable for classification, segmentation, or few-shot fine-tuning. A gating variant allows the user to set a token budget or fusion ratio r at inference time to trade accuracy for speed.

## Method

Foundry implements a compress-and-reconstruct distillation objective: a lightweight student wrapper first compresses c input tokens into s learnable SuperTokens via Dynamic Supertoken Optimization (DSO), which uses cross-attention with hard argmax assignment to group tokens and update each SuperToken with the mean of its assigned value vectors. A lightweight student encoder then processes the SuperTokens, and a Cross-Attention Upsampling (CAU) module reconstructs the full c-token space by routing each original token back to its assigned SuperToken via the same assignment map, with a residual connection to the initial tokens preserving local detail. The entire student is trained end-to-end with a Smooth L1 loss matching the teacher's output embeddings, forcing the SuperTokens to learn an efficient basis for the teacher's latent space rather than mimicking a single task's logits. An optional 2-layer MLP gate predicts per-token fusion probabilities, enabling dynamic budget control at inference by bypassing compression for tokens above a user-defined threshold.

## Results

On the full benchmark suite (6 classification + 1 segmentation datasets), Foundry with 16 SuperTokens (unfrozen) achieves an average score of 84.87, within ~3 points of the full Point-JEPA teacher (87.72), while reducing FLOPs from 478 G to 178 G. Remarkably, even with a single SuperToken the student retains 84.58 average, matching the 16-token variant within 0.3 points, indicating the compressed basis saturates quickly. On ModelNet40 the student reaches 91.75% top-1 accuracy (vs. 93.02% teacher) and on ShapeNet55 89.87% (vs. 90.54%). For large-scale scenes of 2^18 points, the baseline and ToMe exceed 6 GB VRAM and fail, while Foundry completes inference in 4.0 GB and ~4.6 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Point-JEPA teacher (baseline), Foundry with 16 SuperTokens stays within 1–2 points on most datasets (e.g., 91.75% vs. 93.02% on ModelNet40; 89.87% vs. 90.54% on ShapeNet55), with the largest gap on real-world ScanObjectNN splits (~5 points). Compared to ToMe used purely at inference, Foundry's DSO/CAU mechanism is substantially better (84.87 vs. 73.80 average at s=16), but when ToMe is retrained under the FMD compress-and-reconstruct objective it surpasses Foundry's own DSO (86.52 vs. 84.87). The same pattern holds for PiToMe+FMD (86.51) and PatchMerger+FMD (87.59), with PatchMerger+FMD actually matching or exceeding the full teacher on several datasets (e.g., 90.11% on SN55 vs. 90.54% teacher; 83.59/85.55 mIoU on SNP vs. 83.91/85.73). This reveals that the FMD distillation paradigm, rather than the specific DSO/CAU architecture, is the primary driver of performance: any token-merging mechanism benefits greatly from being trained to reconstruct the teacher's latent space. Random sampling at the same token budget (c=16) collapses to 75.20 average, confirming that learned semantic compression is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

Foundry repurposes the learnable SuperToken compression mechanism from 3DLST (originally an online acceleration module for a single segmentation task) into the core of an offline distillation framework whose goal is to produce a standalone, general-purpose student. Conceptually, it departs from both task-specific knowledge distillation (which creates narrow specialists) and direct feature mimicry (e.g., CLIP-KD's L2 alignment) by introducing a compress-and-reconstruct objective: the student must learn a small set of SuperTokens that can decompress back to the teacher's full token-level representations, effectively learning a basis for the teacher's latent space rather than copying its outputs. This positions Foundry as a third category—'Representation Space Distillation'—distinct from the two prior paradigms the authors identify in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Foundry enables a 6 GB laptop GPU to process 2^18-point indoor scenes where the full Point-JEPA teacher and ToMe both run out of memory, making 3D foundation-model perception viable on edge hardware for robotics and AR/VR. A single distilled student retains strong transferability across classification, part segmentation, and few-shot learning without task-specific retraining, addressing a real deployment bottleneck. Key limitations include evaluation on only one teacher (Point-JEPA) and one modality (3D point clouds); the authors explicitly leave open how well the compress-reconstruct design transfers to other 3D foundation models (e.g., Point-BERT, PointTransformer) or to 2D/video settings. The finding that performance saturates around s=4 SuperTokens suggests a practical 'sweet spot' for compression ratio, but whether this holds for larger or more diverse teachers remains untested.
