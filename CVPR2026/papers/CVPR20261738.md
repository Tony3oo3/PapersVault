## Problem

Partially Supervised Multi-Task Learning (PS-MTL) has been studied primarily for homogeneous dense prediction tasks with random label masking, but in practice annotations for different tasks are often collected in disjoint geographic domains, creating a compound challenge of structural disparity (e.g., sparse tracking vs. dense mapping) and strong domain shift. No prior work systematically addresses PS-MTL under this domain-partitioned, heterogeneous-task regime, which is common in real-world perception systems where exhaustive annotation is prohibitively expensive.

## Input / Output

On nuScenes, the method consumes multi-camera surround-view images and produces sparse instance-level object tracks (bounding boxes with persistent identities) alongside dense BEV map element segmentation (lanes, drivable areas, dividers, crossings). On NYU-V2, it takes single RGB images resized to 288×384 and outputs 13-class semantic segmentation masks, depth maps, and surface normal fields. A key constraint is that supervision is strictly domain-partitioned: on nuScenes, mapping labels exist only for Boston scenes while tracking labels exist only for Singapore scenes, and on NYU-V2 the dataset is clustered into three subsets each carrying exactly one task's ground truth.

## Method

NexusFlow is a lightweight, plug-and-play module inserted between a shared encoder and task-specific decoders that does not alter the baseline forward path. For each task, a surrogate module first compresses intermediate features into a fixed-dimensional embedding via a lightweight aggregator (MLP or deformable attention), then applies N invertible affine coupling layers (RealNVP-style) to map the embedding into a shared canonical latent space. Because the coupling layers are bijective with closed-form inverses, they preserve all task-relevant information while enabling flexible distributional transformations, preventing representational collapse. An auxiliary center-based alignment loss minimizes the L2 distance between each task's latent and the mean latent across tasks, and is added to the original task losses with a weight λ. A theoretical lemma shows that under Lipschitz continuity of the inverse transforms, minimizing the latent alignment loss directly upper-bounds the feature-space discrepancy between tasks, providing a provable guarantee for cross-task knowledge transfer.

## Results

On the core nuScenes challenge (domain-partitioned tracking + mapping), NexusFlow (two-stage) achieves 0.329 AMOTA and 37.1% lane IoU, surpassing the fully supervised upper bound (0.323 AMOTA, 31.4% lane IoU) and outperforming the UniAD baseline by +4.0% AMOTA and +10.0% lane IoU. On NYU-V2 (three homogeneous dense-prediction tasks), NexusFlow reaches 31.70% segmentation IoU, 0.6055 depth aErr, and 31.88° normal mErr, improving over the baseline by +4.95% IoU, −0.0456 aErr, and −3.29° mErr respectively. The method also matches the fully supervised baseline in training time (8 hours) and GPU memory (4554 MiB vs. 3906 MiB), while MTPSL requires 10 hours and 8900 MiB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UniAD baseline (trained under the same domain-partitioned protocol), NexusFlow (two-stage) improves AMOTA from 0.289 to 0.329 (+4.0%) and lane IoU from 27.1% to 37.1% (+10.0%), while also reducing ID Switches from 1025 to 690. Against MTPSL, the gain is even larger: +7.4% AMOTA (0.329 vs. 0.255) and +10.1% lane IoU, indicating that methods designed for homogeneous dense-prediction tasks degrade under structural disparity and domain partitioning. JTR performs worst among the PS-MTL baselines (0.197 AMOTA), falling below even the unmodified baseline, further confirming that prior PS-MTL techniques do not transfer to this setting. Remarkably, NexusFlow slightly exceeds the fully supervised upper bound on AMOTA (0.329 vs. 0.323) and substantially surpasses it on lane IoU (37.1% vs. 31.4%), suggesting that the alignment objective acts as a regularizer that improves generalization. On NYU-V2, NexusFlow (center-based) outperforms MTPSL by +2.05% segmentation IoU (31.70 vs. 29.65), −0.0231 depth aErr, and −1.40° normal mErr, while using roughly 50% less GPU memory (4554 vs. 8900 MiB) and matching the baseline's 8-hour training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

NexusFlow repurposes affine coupling layers from normalizing flows (RealNVP) as a lightweight alignment mechanism for multi-task feature spaces, departing from their original density-estimation role. It extends the PS-MTL line of work (MTPSL, JTR, DiffusionMTL) that has focused on homogeneous dense-prediction tasks with random label masking to the harder regime of structurally disparate tasks (sparse tracking vs. dense mapping) under geographically partitioned supervision. The method is architecture-agnostic and plugs into existing MTL backbones like UniAD or MTAN without modifying task heads, positioning it as a drop-in complement rather than a replacement for prior alignment strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of domain-partitioned, structurally disparate PS-MTL, a setting that mirrors real-world annotation pipelines where different tasks are labeled in different geographic regions. The finding that prior PS-MTL methods (MTPSL, JTR) designed for homogeneous tasks actually degrade baseline performance in the heterogeneous regime is a useful caution for practitioners. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with a binary domain split, and the method's behavior under more complex multi-domain or many-task partitioning remains untested. Open questions include whether the invertible alignment generalizes to tasks with fundamentally different input modalities or to open-world settings with continuous domain drift.
