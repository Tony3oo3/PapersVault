## Problem

Fine-grained functional grasping with dexterous hands remains underexplored due to three intertwined challenges: the complexity of specifying goals and reward functions across diverse objects and functional conditions, the difficulty of multi-task RL exploration in high-DoF action spaces, and the sim-to-real transfer gap. Prior approaches either produce open-loop poses from human data with limited generalization, or learn closed-loop RL policies that struggle with the combinatorial explosion of object-geometry × affordance × style combinations.

## Input / Output

The state-based RL policy consumes the robot end-effector 6D pose, target object pose, full object point cloud, a 3D affordance point (e.g., a cup handle), and a one-hot grasping-style label, and outputs a residual wrist transformation ΔT, a hand-joint delta Δq, and a scalar scaling coefficient k that edit a single teleoperated demonstration trajectory. The vision-based policy for sim-to-real transfer replaces the point cloud with an RGB image and the 3D affordance with its 2D camera projection, predicting absolute end-effector pose (t, r) and hand joint angles (q) in a closed-loop manner. The one-step formulation means the policy is queried once per episode to produce the full corrective action, rather than stepping through a multi-step trajectory.

## Method

DemoFunGrasp factorizes each functional grasping condition into an affordance (where to grasp) and a grasping style (how to grasp), then embeds both into the observation, reward, and action spaces of a one-step demonstration-editing RL framework. A single teleoperated demonstration provides a base trajectory; the PPO-trained policy outputs residual corrections (ΔT, Δq, k) that adapt the end-effector pose and hand configuration to the sampled object geometry and functional condition, reducing the problem from multi-step motion synthesis to a single refinement step. The reward combines a sparse affordance-alignment term (object-size-normalized), a dense proximity term, a style-consistency term, and a success bonus, with style disturbance during training to encourage robustness. For sim-to-real transfer, 30k successful rollouts are collected in IsaacGym with aggressive domain randomization and distilled into an RGB-based diffusion transformer policy with a VLM encoder, which transfers zero-shot to a real Franka arm with an Inspire Hand. A VLM (Embodied-R1) is optionally integrated upstream to predict affordance points from language instructions, enabling autonomous instruction-following grasping.

## Results

On 3,200 DexGraspNet objects, DemoFunGrasp achieves 76.26% grasp success rate (GSR) on seen categories and 71.65% on unseen categories, while attaining 1.48× the style diversity of UniDexGrasp (1.48 vs. 1.00). For affordance alignment, DemoFunGrasp reduces the Success Affordance Distance (SAD) to 3.03 cm on seen objects, more than halving DemoGrasp's 6.29 cm. In the real world, the zero-shot transferred RGB policy achieves 71% GSR on human-annotated affordances and 70.7% across 33 additional diverse objects, with an average 64.4% success rate when guided by VLM-predicted affordances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DemoGrasp [39], DemoFunGrasp substantially outperforms on affordance alignment: the Success Affordance Distance drops from 6.29 cm to 3.03 cm on seen objects (a >3 cm reduction), and from 6.20 cm to 3.21 cm on unseen categories, confirming that affordance-conditioned editing produces genuinely functional grasps rather than merely stable ones. Against UniDexGrasp [37], DemoFunGrasp wins on both GSR (76.26% vs. 74.3% seen; 71.65% vs. 70.8% unseen) and style diversity (1.48 vs. 1.00 seen; 1.44 vs. 1.00 unseen), with the gap widening to 81.74% vs. 74.3% when the best-performing style is selected per object. Against Dexonomy [7], a grasp-synthesis method, the comparison is qualitative via t-SNE visualization: DemoFunGrasp's RL-trained styles remain well-separated in joint-space embeddings, whereas Dexonomy's pre-RL styles show more overlap, indicating that the learned policy preserves style distinctiveness while optimizing for stability. Overall, DemoFunGrasp dominates on all reported quantitative metrics across both seen and unseen evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DemoFunGrasp directly extends DemoGrasp [39], which introduced one-step demonstration-editing RL for dexterous grasping, by incorporating functional grasping conditions (affordance points and grasp styles) into the observation, reward, and action spaces. It departs from open-loop grasp synthesis methods (Dexonomy [7], ContactOpt [11]) and prior RL-based functional grasping (Agarwal et al. [1], FunGrasp [14]) by learning a single closed-loop policy that generalizes across diverse objects, styles, and affordances simultaneously, rather than generating static poses or training per-object policies. For sim-to-real transfer, it builds on diffusion-policy and VLM-encoder architectures (ACT [42], GR00T [4]) to distill the state-based RL policy into an RGB-conditioned executor, and integrates Embodied-R1 [40] for language-guided affordance prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work bridges a practical gap between affordance perception and reliable closed-loop execution by unifying them in a single RL framework, and demonstrates that zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for *functional* (not just stable) grasping is achievable at ~71% success rate. The VLM integration opens a path toward language-instructed autonomous manipulation, though the current system is limited to single-grasp execution without dynamic pre-grasp repositioning or in-hand adjustment. Key open questions include whether the centimeter-scale affordance accuracy can be improved with tactile feedback, whether the framework scales to multi-step manipulation sequences, and how robust the VLM-guided pipeline is to novel object categories outside the training distribution.
