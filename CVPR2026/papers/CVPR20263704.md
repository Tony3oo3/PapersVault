## Problem

Multi-frame turbulence mitigation methods require 30–60 frames from conventional cameras, incurring 1–2 s latency and 30–60× data overhead, while single-frame methods are limited by the ill-posedness of turbulence degradation. Conventional cameras' low temporal resolution also makes it difficult to accurately decouple object motion from turbulence in dynamic scenes. The paper asks whether high-temporal-resolution event data can supply finer-grained motion cues within a much shorter duration to break this accuracy–efficiency bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 5–8 RGB frames (512×512) captured at 25 fps together with a synchronized asynchronous event stream from an ALPIX-pizol hybrid camera, where events encode per-pixel log-brightness changes exceeding a threshold at microsecond resolution. The output is a single turbulence-mitigated image at the same spatial resolution. Because events are edge-triggered and sparse, the total data overhead is reduced to ~22.7% of a 60-frame baseline, and system latency drops to 160 ms.

## Method

EHETM exploits two discovered properties of turbulent events: (1) turbulence-induced polarity alternation of events correlates strongly (Pearson r = 0.82) with sharp image gradients, and (2) dynamic objects form spatiotemporally coherent 3D 'event tubes' that are structurally distinct from the irregular, sparse patterns of turbulent events. The ET-Stable module uses a Rigid Motion-Aware Block (3D residual convolutions + 3D channel attention in a U-Net) to extract dense motion features from events, then applies Event Tube Optimization to project the 3D tube onto a 2D motion field constrained by pretrained RAFT optical flow, decoupling object motion from turbulence. The EPAW-Stable module generates scene masks, computes temporally averaged gradients, and encodes event polarity-alternation counts as adaptive weights to sharpen distorted edges. A lightweight Bi-Mamba video restoration network (with local Hilbert scanning) fuses the stable edge guidance from both modules with the few input frames to produce the final restoration, trained end-to-end with Charbonnier, perceptual, and motion-consistency losses.

## Results

On CTTH, EHETM achieves 35.17 PSNR / 0.9425 SSIM / 0.1281 LPIPS, and on UDET 29.33 PSNR / 0.8717 SSIM / 0.1517 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline DATUM by +0.38 PSNR, +0.0057 SSIM, and −0.0108 LPIPS (averaged across both datasets). On the no-reference LATH benchmark, EHETM attains 10.12 NIQE versus 10.87 for DATUM. In terms of efficiency, EHETM requires only 5 input frames plus events (22.7% data overhead), runs at 29.5 FPS with 160 ms system latency, compared to DATUM's 60 frames (100% data), 12.1 FPS, and 1560 ms latency — a ~77.3% reduction in data overhead and ~89.5% reduction in latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EHETM outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across CTTH, UDET, and LATH. The closest competitor is DATUM [8] (multi-frame TM, 60 frames), which EHETM surpasses by +0.28 PSNR on CTTH and +0.48 PSNR on UDET, while using only 5 frames + events (22.7% data) versus DATUM's 100% data budget; system latency drops from 1560 ms to 160 ms. MambaTM [41] (SSM-based, 20 frames) is competitive on LPIPS (0.1290 vs. 0.1281 on CTTH) but trails EHETM on PSNR (34.38 vs. 35.17) and SSIM, and requires 75% data with 760 ms latency. Among hybrid event-based methods, Ev-DeblurVSR [71] and EGVSR [72] lag substantially (PSNR 31.82 and 30.15 on CTTH respectively), confirming that their deblurring/VSR objectives do not transfer well to turbulence mitigation. Single-frame TurbNet [18] and multi-frame GAN/Transformer methods (TSRWGAN [1], TMT [37], VRT [70]) are all clearly inferior, with PSNR gaps of 2.6–7.0 dB on CTTH, underscoring the benefit of combining few frames with high-temporal-resolution event guidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

EHETM carves new ground at the intersection of multi-frame turbulence mitigation (TM) and event-based vision, departing from the frames-only paradigm that dominates the field (DATUM, TMT, MambaTM). While prior event-based TM work (Liu et al. [61], Li et al. [62]) modeled tilt/blur in turbulent events or identified 'event-lucky regions,' none exploited events on dynamic objects to decouple object motion from turbulence. The method repurposes the Lagrangian particle-tracking formalism to formalize 'event tubes' as a low-dimensional motion prior, and borrows the Mamba state-space backbone from MambaTM [41] and RAFT optical flow from DATUM [8], but recombines them with two novel event-specific modules (ETO and EPAW) that have no direct predecessor in the TM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing data overhead by ~77% and system latency by ~90% while matching or exceeding SOTA quality makes real-time TM feasible for applications like long-range surveillance and autonomous driving. The two new real-world datasets (CTTH with dynamic objects and GTs, LATH covering 1–8 km atmospheric scenes) fill a critical gap, as no prior event-frame turbulence dataset included dynamic objects or long-range atmospheric conditions. Open questions remain for ultra-long-range scenes (>30 km) where events become too sparse, and for severe platform motion that entangles turbulence, scene, and object dynamics—challenges the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
