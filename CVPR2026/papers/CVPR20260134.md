## Problem

Text-to-image models are vulnerable to low-cost, stealthy backdoor bias attacks (B²-style) that inject trigger-activated stereotypes (e.g., forcing 'bald head' with 'president writing'), yet existing debiasing methods like OpenBias, InterpretDiffusion, and UCE were designed for natural statistical biases and fail to detect or remove these adversarially crafted associations. No unified, automated framework existed that could both identify unknown backdoor patterns without prior knowledge and neutralize them while preserving model quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a potentially backdoored diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v2) and a set of text prompts containing trigger words as input. During detection, only 3–10 generated images per trigger category are fed to a VQA model (Gemini-2.5-flash) to produce a bias-lookup table mapping each detected biased attribute to multiple suggested counter-attributes. The final output is a debiased T2I model whose weights have been fine-tuned, along with the lookup table that documents which trigger-attribute associations were identified and neutralized.

## Method

AutoDebias is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (detection) uses a VQA model to analyze a small set of generated images, identifying attributes that appear with abnormally high frequency relative to the prompt, and constructs lookup tables of (biased attribute, counter-bias) pairs; a severity threshold (τ=0.6) and minimum occurrence count (N_min≥3) filter false positives. Stage 2 (debiasing) formulates mitigation as a distribution alignment problem inspired by preference optimization: FG-CLIP serves as an automated alignment judge, computing weighted binary cross-entropy between CLIP classification logits and binary targets (0 for biased attributes, 1 for counter-biases) across multiple sampled images per prompt. A logarithmic transformation stabilizes the accumulated multi-sample loss, which is combined with a reconstruction loss on LAION-5B data to preserve generation quality. Training alternates between CLIP-guided alignment steps and standard diffusion reconstruction steps at a 1:3 ratio over 500 steps, progressively erasing backdoor associations while maintaining text-image alignment and aesthetic quality.

## Results

On the 17-category backdoor detection benchmark, AutoDebias achieves 91.6% accuracy and 88.7% F1 with 10-shot VQA examples, compared to OpenBias's 31.1% accuracy and 29.6% F1. For bias mitigation, AutoDebias reduces the average backdoor bias rate from ~88% (poisoned model) to 11.8% (Qwen-2.5-VL judge), 15.7% (LLaMA-3.2), and 20.4% (Gemini-2.5-Flash), representing 77–87% relative reductions. Image quality is preserved: the debiased model maintains a CLIP score of 0.3220 and an aesthetic score of 0.6557 (ImageReward), exceeding the poisoned model's aesthetic score of 0.4889.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDebias outperforms all three baselines on both detection and mitigation. On detection, it achieves 91.6% accuracy vs. OpenBias's 31.1%, a 60.5-point gap, with OpenBias unable to evaluate fine-grained categories (hairstyles, facial features, accessories) at all (N/A). On mitigation, AutoDebias reduces average bias rate to 11.8% (Qwen-2.5-VL), beating CLIP Sim (24.9%), UCE (58.9%), and InterpretDiffusion (63.1%). The advantage is most dramatic on fine-grained visual attributes: UCE and InterpretDiffusion maintain 82–97% bias rates on accessories (sleeve tattoo, red glasses, red tie), while AutoDebias drives these to 0%. CLIP Sim is the closest competitor but still lags by 13+ points on average, and it degrades aesthetic quality (0.3696 vs. 0.6557). Notably, UCE and InterpretDiffusion were designed for natural statistical biases and fail to erase adversarially injected backdoor associations, confirming the paper's central claim that existing debiasing methods are inadequate for B²-style attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of T2I bias detection (OpenBias, TIBET) and bias removal (InterpretDiffusion, UCE, DebiasDIFF), but deliberately targets a different threat model: adversarially injected backdoor biases (B² attacks) rather than natural statistical overrepresentations. It repurposes VLMs (Gemini, Qwen-VL, LLaMA) as open-set detectors in the spirit of OpenBias, but applies them to fine-grained visual attributes that prior open-set methods cannot handle. The debiasing mechanism borrows the preference-optimization framing from DPO/RLHF, instantiating it as a CLIP-guided distribution alignment loss that pushes generation away from biased attributes and toward counter-attributes, a formulation not previously applied to T2I debiasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The B² backdoor attack is remarkably low-cost (~$10–15) and stealthy, making it a practical security threat to deployed T2I systems, yet no prior automated defense existed. The 17-scenario benchmark covering fine-grained categories (hairstyles, headwear, accessories) beyond traditional demographics provides a useful evaluation resource for future work. Key limitations include evaluation on a single T2I architecture (Stable Diffusion v2), reliance on VLMs whose detection quality depends on shot count, and no analysis of adaptive or multi-turn attacks. Open questions include whether the CLIP-guided alignment generalizes to larger models (FLUX, SD3), whether detection can operate with fewer than 10 samples, and how the framework handles backdoors that modify multiple interacting attributes simultaneously.
