## Problem

Compositional generalization—generating novel combinations of learned concepts—remains poorly understood at the level of data structure. Prior theoretical work relies on restrictive assumptions about concept interactions (additive effects, polynomial forms) and ignores the hierarchical, modular nature of concepts that underlies human analogical reasoning. This paper asks what latent hierarchical structures enable compositional generalization and whether they are provably recoverable from observed text-image pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261306/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-level text prompt (e.g., "peacock eating rice") and produces a generated RGB image. At inference, an LLM (QWEN-v2.5) decomposes the prompt into M=3 low-level local descriptions that are injected at different diffusion timesteps, while the original prompt conditions the earliest steps. The model is fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion v1.5 with a frozen FLAN-T5-xl text encoder, and the same framework extends to 4.8B-parameter diffusion transformers.

## Method

HierDiff interprets diffusion timesteps as hierarchical concept levels and implements two theoretically motivated mechanisms. First, step-specific conditioning: cross-attention outputs from the global prompt and M low-level local descriptions are interpolated via a monotonically decreasing function s(t), so high-level concepts condition high-noise steps and fine-grained details condition low-noise steps. Second, a DICE-based sparsity loss penalizes spatial overlap between cross-attention maps of different low-level concepts, enforcing the sparse modular connectivity that the theory identifies as necessary for compositional transfer. The theoretical foundation proves that a sparse hierarchical latent model enables generalization to unseen concept combinations (Theorem 3.1) and that the latent structure is component-wise identifiable from text-image pairs under invertibility, smoothness, conditional independence, and sufficient variability conditions (Theorem 3.4).

## Results

On DPG-Bench, HierDiff achieves an overall score of 79.28, outperforming the strongest baseline ELLA (74.91) by 4.37 points and SD v2 (68.09) by 11.19 points. HierDiff leads on all five sub-metrics: Global (85.77), Entity (85.15), Attribute (86.98), Relation (86.82), and Other (87.77). When extended to a 4.8B-parameter diffusion transformer (HierDiff-DiT), the method achieves a DPG score of 84.9, surpassing FLUX-schnell (84.8), IC+RPG (84.7), and DALL-E 3 (83.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HierDiff outperforms all five baselines in Table 1 across every DPG-Bench sub-metric. The largest gap is against SD v1.5 (63.18), where HierDiff leads by 16.1 points, while the closest competitor ELLA (74.91) trails by 4.37 points overall. The advantage is most pronounced on the 'Other' metric (87.77 vs. 80.79 for ELLA), suggesting HierDiff handles complex multi-concept prompts more robustly. In the SOTA comparison (Table 3), HierDiff-DiT achieves 84.9 DPG, narrowly beating FLUX-schnell (84.8) by 0.1 and IC+RPG/SANA-1.5 (84.7) by 0.2, while leading DALL-E 3 (83.5) by 1.4 points. The ablation confirms both the time-dependent hierarchical conditioning and the sparsity regularization contribute: removing time dependence drops the Relation score from 86.82 to 86.29, and removing sparsity regularization reduces the Global score from 85.77 to 83.09.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the causal representation learning line of work on compositional generalization (Brady et al., Wiedemer et al., Lachapelle et al.), which has so far relied on restrictive assumptions like additive or polynomial concept interactions, and on nonlinear ICA / latent hierarchical identification results (Kong et al. 2023, Hyvarinen et al., Khemakhem et al.) that typically require linearity or discreteness. The key departure is a unified framework that (i) allows arbitrary non-parametric interactions between latent concepts via a hierarchical generative process, (ii) proves component-wise identifiability of continuous latent variables without linearity or discreteness assumptions, and (iii) maps the abstract hierarchy onto diffusion timesteps, turning the theoretical sparsity requirement into a concrete DICE-based attention regularization. Empirically, it extends the ELLA-style LLM-augmented diffusion pipeline (Hu et al.) with hierarchical, step-dependent cross-attention injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, two-part theoretical guarantee—composability (Theorem 3.1) and identifiability (Theorem 3.4)—that together explain *why* sparse hierarchical latent structures enable compositional generalization and *that* such structures are recoverable from text-image pairs, filling a gap left by prior work that either assumed simpler interactions or could not identify individual concepts. The practical insight that sparsity of the latent causal graph (not just the presence of concepts) is the critical lever for composability offers a clear design principle for future generative models. Limitations include the assumption of no within-level causal edges (Condition 3.3-iii), the reliance on an external LLM for low-level description generation at test time, and the fact that identification conditions, while weaker than prior work, still impose non-trivial regularity requirements that are hard to verify on real data.
