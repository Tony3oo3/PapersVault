## Problem

Monocular depth estimation is inherently ill-posed, with visual ambiguities (repetitive textures, homogeneous surfaces) and small or distant objects that are difficult to resolve from a single image. Existing diffusion-based MDEs (Marigold, Lotus, E2E-FT) are fine-tuned from text-to-image diffusion models but discard the text conditioning channel, even though the text-to-image pretraining implicitly encodes object shapes and spatial relationships that constitute 3D scene structure. This paper asks whether re-introducing text descriptions as an additional condition can reduce the solution space of compatible 3D scenes and thereby improve depth fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form text description of the 3D scene as joint inputs. Since standard depth benchmarks lack text annotations, descriptions are generated per-image using vision-language models (LLaVA v1.6 for Marigold; InternVL3-8B for Lotus and E2E-FT). The output is a dense pixel-wise depth map decoded from latent space via a frozen VAE decoder. A distinctive feature is that the text input can be iteratively refined—appending additional sentences about specific objects or regions—to target and correct error modes in particular parts of the depth map.

## Method

The core contribution is re-introducing a frozen CLIP text encoder into the denoising U-Net of existing diffusion-based MDEs (Marigold, Lotus-D/G, E2E-FT), which were originally fine-tuned from Stable Diffusion v2 with the text channel removed. During both training and inference, the U-Net predicts the noise to remove at each diffusion step conditioned on the noisy depth latent z_t, the timestep t, the VAE-encoded input image, and the CLIP-encoded text caption, following the standard DDPM reparameterization objective. The key insight is that the text-to-image pretraining already learned a conditional distribution associating language descriptions with plausible 3D scene layouts, so re-activating this channel provides semantic and geometric priors that regularize the denoising process. The paper validates this across three base architectures and three integration strategies (train-only, infer-only, train-and-infer), showing consistent gains in overall accuracy, targeted improvements in small/ambiguous regions, faster training convergence, and reduced denoising steps (10 vs. 25 for Marigold).

## Results

Integrating text into diffusion-based MDEs (Marigold, Lotus, E2E-FT) consistently improves depth estimation across all five evaluation datasets. For example, Marigold+Text (Train & Infer) achieves 95.9% δ₁ / 5.9 AbsRel on NYUv2 (vs. 95.7/6.1 for Marigold baseline) and 78.9% δ₁ / 29.8 AbsRel on DIODE (vs. 77.0/31.3). The largest relative gains appear on DIODE and ScanNet, where small-object and ambiguous-region errors are most prevalent. Additionally, text conditioning accelerates convergence: the diffusion trajectory reaches comparable quality in 10 denoising steps versus 25 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its own baselines, text integration (Train & Infer) wins on every dataset for all three model families: Marigold+Text improves δ₁ by 0.2–1.9 points and AbsRel by 0.1–1.5 over Marigold*; Lotus-D+Text gains 0.1–0.6 δ₁ and 0.2–0.4 AbsRel over Lotus-D*; Lotus-G+Text gains 0.6–1.6 δ₁ and 0.3–1.2 AbsRel over Lotus-G*; E2E-FT+Text gains 0.6–1.6 δ₁ and 0.3–0.8 AbsRel over E2E-FT*. The largest absolute gains appear on DIODE (e.g., Marigold+Text: 78.9 vs. 77.0 δ₁) and ScanNet (e.g., Lotus-G+Text: 95.3 vs. 93.7 δ₁), consistent with the paper's claim that text helps most in small/ambiguous regions. Compared to external non-diffusion baselines, Marigold+Text (74K training images) is competitive with GeoWizard (280K) on NYUv2 and ETH3D but trails on KITTI (90.6 vs. 82.0 δ₁ — actually wins) and DIODE (78.9 vs. 79.2). Against the much larger Depth Anything v2 (62.6M images), the diffusion-based methods remain 1–3 δ₁ points behind on most datasets, though the gap narrows substantially with text integration. Notably, text-only-at-inference variants also show modest gains over the re-trained baselines, suggesting the pre-trained text-to-image prior provides useful zero-shot guidance even without text-aware fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-activates the text-conditioning pathway that was present in the original text-to-image pretraining of Stable Diffusion but was discarded when models like Marigold, Lotus, and E2E-FT were fine-tuned for depth estimation. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate lines of work: diffusion-based MDEs (which exploit the 3D scene structure implicitly learned during text-to-image pretraining) and language-guided depth estimation (DepthCLIP, WorDepth, RSA), which used non-diffusion architectures. The key departure from prior diffusion-based MDEs is the reintroduction of a CLIP text encoder and text conditioning during both training and inference, rather than treating the pretrained diffusion model as a purely image-conditional denoiser.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic validation that text conditioning improves diffusion-based MDEs, and the finding that it accelerates both training convergence and the inference denoising trajectory (10 vs. 25 steps for Marigold) has practical efficiency implications. The iterative-refinement strategy—appending more specific descriptions to correct error modes in targeted regions—offers a user-controllable correction mechanism. However, the gains are modest (typically <1% on δ₁, <0.5 on AbsRel), and the method's effectiveness is tightly coupled to the quality and accuracy of the text descriptions, as demonstrated by the failure case where incorrect text misguides the model. Open questions include how to build language-robustness modules (uncertainty estimation, consistency filtering) and whether the approach generalizes beyond the synthetic-to-real transfer setting used here.
