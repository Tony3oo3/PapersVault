## Problem

Large-scale LiDAR scene completion faces a fundamental tension: global latent diffusion (e.g., XCube) incurs information loss through multi-stage encoding-decoding, while explicit voxel grids scale cubically and become infeasible at scene level. Additionally, prevailing methods perform single-frame completion in isolation, ignoring temporal correlations that are critical for suppressing inter-frame flickering and maintaining coherent trajectories in autonomous driving.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263277/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse LiDAR scan discretized into a binary 3D occupancy voxel grid at 0.15625 m resolution, partitioned into overlapping 20 m × 20 m patches. Optionally, the previously completed dense frame is supplied for temporal fusion after ICP alignment. The output is a dense point cloud of approximately 900,000 points per frame (after 2× upsampling of the voxel occupancy), produced in explicit voxel space rather than a compressed latent. Notably, a model trained on 20 m LiDAR range generalizes directly to 50 m scenes without retraining.

## Method

PatchScene applies DDPM-style denoising independently to each local voxel patch, where the network predicts the clean occupancy x̂₀ directly (rather than noise) conditioned on a learnable position encoding, trained with MSE loss. To ensure global coherence, a stochastic spatial fusion mechanism uses a Bernoulli(0.5) mask in overlapping regions to randomly couple each patch's noise prediction with the aggregated global noise field, avoiding the blurring of deterministic averaging. Temporal consistency is enforced by ICP-registering consecutive frames and adaptively blending the previous frame's cached denoising predictions with the current frame using a density-ratio weight λ computed on a BEV grid. The key architectural insight is the Annular-Flow strategy: patches are grouped into concentric rings by radial distance from the sensor, and generation proceeds from the dense inner ring outward, with each ring's denoising guided by the already-completed inner ring—exploiting the physical property that LiDAR density decays with range to propagate high-fidelity information to sparse peripheral regions and enabling unbounded spatial extension.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, PatchScene achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.319 m, JSD-3D of 0.444, JSD-BEV of 0.371, and Voxel IoU of 45.3 / 38.2 / 19.7 at 0.5 m / 0.2 m / 0.1 m resolutions, setting new state-of-the-art on every reported metric. The closest competitor, ScoreLiDAR (refined), trails by 0.023 m in CD and by 7.3 points in Voxel IoU at 0.5 m. The model trained on 20 m LiDAR ranges generalizes to 50 m scenes without retraining, and adding temporal fusion reduces inter-frame RMSE from ~0.157 m to ~0.084 m while preserving geometric accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchScene outperforms all seven baselines on every metric. Against the strongest point-diffusion baseline, LiDPM (refined), it improves CD by 0.058 m (0.377 → 0.319), JSD-3D by 0.098 (0.542 → 0.444), and Voxel IoU@0.5 by 8.7 points (36.6 → 45.3). Against ScoreLiDAR (refined), the gains are 0.023 m in CD, 0.216 in JSD-3D, and 13.3 points in IoU@0.5. The older discriminative baselines (LMSCNet, LODE, MID, PVD) are substantially weaker, with CD values 0.50–1.26 m and IoU@0.5 below 34. Notably, PatchScene's advantage is most pronounced at fine voxel resolutions (0.1 m IoU: 19.7 vs. 14.9 for LiDPM-refine and 9.4 for ScoreLiDAR-refine), indicating superior recovery of fine-grained geometric detail. The method also surpasses LiDiff (refined) by 0.057 m in CD and 12.9 points in IoU@0.5, confirming that explicit voxel-space patch diffusion with annular flow outperforms point-offset diffusion for large-scale outdoor scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PatchScene builds directly on the DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) but departs from both point-based diffusion methods (LiDiff, LiDPM, ScoreLiDAR) that operate in raw point space and latent-based approaches (XCube) that compress voxels into a VAE bottleneck. Its key conceptual departure is a divide-and-conquer patching strategy applied in explicit voxel space, combined with an Annular-Flow generation order that exploits LiDAR's radial density gradient—a physical prior not leveraged by prior diffusion-based completion methods. The stochastic (Bernoulli-mask) spatial fusion and ICP-guided temporal fusion mechanisms are also novel relative to the deterministic or absent fusion strategies in existing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated ability to train on 20 m LiDAR ranges and generalize to 50 m scenes without retraining is practically significant for autonomous driving systems where sensor ranges vary across vehicle platforms, though this claim is supported primarily by qualitative visual evidence (Fig. 4) rather than quantitative metrics. The temporal fusion mechanism addresses a real deployment need—suppressing inter-frame flickering in continuous driving sequences—that prior single-frame methods ignore. Open questions include how the method handles dynamic objects (the ICP registration assumes rigid scene structure), whether the patch-based approach scales beyond the 50 m SemanticKITTI setting to truly unbounded environments, and whether the 10-timestep sweet spot generalizes across different scene complexities.
