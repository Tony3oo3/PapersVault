## Problem

Automated conversion of 3D meshes into stylized voxel art remains challenging because existing methods either over-simplify geometry (losing facial features and semantic details) or fail to achieve the pixel-precise, palette-constrained aesthetics that define voxel art. Three interrelated obstacles persist: perspective projection causes pixel-voxel misalignment during optimization, critical semantic features collapse at low voxel resolutions, and gradient-based methods cannot naturally produce the small discrete palettes (2–8 colors) that voxel art requires.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 3D mesh as input and produces a discrete voxel grid where each voxel stores a density value and a color index into a user-specified palette. The voxel grid resolution is controllable (20×, 30×, or 50× per axis), and the palette size ranges from 2 to 8 colors, extracted from six-view pixel art renderings via K-means, Max-Min, Median Cut, or Simulated Annealing. The pixel art supervision is generated at 1200×1200 resolution, and the final output is a fully discrete, fabrication-ready voxel structure (demonstrated with LEGO rendering).

## Method

Voxify3D is a two-stage differentiable pipeline. Stage 1 initializes a coarse voxel radiance field using DVGO, optimizing explicit density and RGB color grids with MSE, total-variation density regularization, and background entropy loss over 8000 iterations. Stage 2 fine-tunes the grid under six-view orthographic pixel art supervision (generated by a dedicated pixelization model), combining pixel-level MSE, L1 depth, alpha transparency, and a patch-based CLIP semantic loss computed on randomly sampled 80×80 patches to preserve object identity under extreme discretization. The key architectural change replaces the continuous RGB grid with a per-voxel color-logit vector over the palette; Gumbel-Softmax with temperature annealing (1.0→0.1) and a straight-through estimator enables end-to-end differentiable optimization over the discrete color space, yielding clean, palette-constrained voxel outputs after a final argmax selection.

## Results

Voxify3D achieves the highest CLIP-IQA score of 37.12 across 35 character examples, outperforming all four baselines (Pixel art to 3D: 35.53, IN2N: 23.93, Vox-E: 35.02, Blender: 36.31). In a user study with 72 participants, the method is preferred 77.90% for abstractness, 80.36% for visual appeal, and 96.55% for geometry preservation. The ablation study confirms the full pipeline reaches 40.06 CLIP-IQA, with the largest drops when removing Stage 1 (−11.64) or orthographic projection (−12.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Voxify3D outperforms all four baselines on CLIP-IQA. The largest margin is against IN2N (23.93), which suffers from large cross-view inconsistencies and fails to produce coherent voxelized results; Voxify3D leads by 13.19 points. Against the closest competitor, Blender Geometry Nodes (36.31), Voxify3D gains 0.81 points, reflecting Blender's clean but semantically unaligned procedural downsampling. Vox-E (35.02) produces smoother volumes but misses the discrete blocky aesthetic, trailing Voxify3D by 2.10 points. The Pixel art to 3D extension baseline (35.53) applies 2D stylization before 3D reconstruction, losing pixel-voxel alignment and trailing by 1.59 points. In the user study, Voxify3D is overwhelmingly preferred (77.90–96.55% across metrics), with geometry preservation being the most decisive factor (96.55% vs. 3.45% for all baselines combined).

## Relation to Prior Work

Voxify3D builds on DVGO's explicit voxel-grid radiance field representation for the coarse initialization stage, and repurposes a 2D pixel art generator as a supervision signal for 3D optimization. It departs from prior 3D stylization methods (IN2N, StyleRF, Vox-E) in two key ways: replacing perspective rendering with six-view orthographic projection to achieve pixel-voxel alignment, and substituting continuous RGB optimization with Gumbel-Softmax palette quantization for discrete color control. The patch-based CLIP loss extends standard full-image perceptual losses into a resolution-adaptive formulation specifically designed to prevent semantic collapse at 20×–50× discretization, where image-level losses fail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly address geometric abstraction, semantic preservation, and discrete palette coherence for automated voxel art generation, filling a gap between photorealistic neural rendering and manual procedural tools. The practical impact is notable for game asset pipelines and physical fabrication (LEGO-style assembly), where limited color palettes and blocky structure are hard requirements. Key limitations include degradation on highly intricate shapes at low resolutions and the reliance on a fixed six-view setup; open questions include whether geometric priors or assembly-aware constraints can improve detail preservation and physical realizability at scale.
