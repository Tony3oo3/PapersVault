## Problem

Existing anchor-based multi-view clustering methods fix the total number of anchors a priori (typically to k or a small multiple of k), implicitly assuming uniform anchor needs across all clusters. In practice, clusters vary markedly in size, density, and information richness, so the optimal anchor budget per cluster is inherently non-uniform, yet no prior method adaptively determines cluster-specific anchor cardinalities within a unified optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes V views of n samples with heterogeneous feature dimensions {d_v} and a user-specified cluster count k, along with a provisional per-cluster anchor budget m. It outputs a non-negative representation matrix Z ∈ R^{mk×n} (column-stochastic) from which k-means yields final cluster assignments, plus a pruned consensus anchor matrix S whose effective column count varies per cluster. The over-allocation to mk anchors followed by l2,1-based pruning means the actual number of retained anchors per cluster is data-driven rather than fixed.

## Method

CAL over-allocates mk anchors and partitions the learned consensus anchor matrix S into k contiguous column groups S^j ∈ R^{mk×m}, one per cluster. An l2,1-norm penalty on each group's transpose induces column-wise structured sparsity, automatically shrinking redundant anchor columns to zero and retaining only the most discriminative ones per cluster. An inter-cluster decorregularizer (Frobenius norm of S^{jT}S^h for all j≠h) drives anchor groups toward mutual orthogonality, enhancing separability. The full objective is solved by alternating minimization: A_v via SVD, S via a Sylvester equation, Z via per-column quadratic programming, and the auxiliary J^j via a closed-form l2,1 proximal operator, with ADMM-style updates for the Lagrange multipliers.

## Results

CAL achieves the best ACC on all eight benchmark datasets, with headline values of 0.9429 (MSRC), 0.9721 (Dermatology), 0.8300 (100Leaves), 0.9405 (HW), 0.7716 (OutdoorScene), 0.2591 (CCV), 0.7417 (ALOI), and 0.8172 (YTF20). Relative to the second-best method, CAL improves ACC by 2.39%–11.97% across the eight datasets, with the largest margin on ALOI (+11.97% over SFMC) and 100Leaves (+7.84% over CAMVC). CAL also leads on NMI, PUR, and FSC across all datasets, e.g., NMI of 0.8825 on MSRC and 0.8574 on ALOI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAL outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset for all four metrics (ACC, NMI, PUR, FSC). The closest competitors are CAMVC (cluster-wise anchor learning with centroids) and ALPC (anchor learning with potential cluster constraints), which are second-best on most datasets; CAL leads CAMVC by 2.39% ACC on MSRC, 7.84% on 100Leaves, and 11.97% on ALOI. Among the anchor-based baselines, FPMVS and EOMSC are competitive on smaller datasets (e.g., EOMSC reaches 0.9385 ACC on Dermatology) but fall behind CAL by 2.5–5% on larger or more heterogeneous benchmarks. Non-anchor graph-fusion methods (SFMC, UDBGL) struggle on high-dimensional or large-scale data (e.g., SFMC drops to 0.2262 ACC on OutdoorScene) and are consistently outperformed. The adaptive anchor budget (m=5 per cluster) proves sufficient: CAL's gains over fixed-anchor methods are largest on datasets with heterogeneous cluster structures (ALOI, 100Leaves), confirming that cluster-specific anchor allocation is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAL directly extends the FPMVS consensus-anchor framework (Wang et al., IEEE TIP 2021), which learns a shared anchor matrix S with per-view projections A_v and a representation Z under the same structural constraints. The key departure is replacing the fixed anchor budget (typically l = k or a small multiple of k) with a cluster-partitioned anchor matrix where each block S^j is penalized by an ℓ_{2,1}-norm column-sparsity term, thereby letting the data determine how many anchors each cluster retains. This contrasts with prior adaptive-anchor methods (3AMVC, CAMVC, ALPC) that adjust anchor counts at the view or global level but never at the individual-cluster level, and with the broader self-representation MVSC literature that avoids anchors entirely at the cost of O(n²) memory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first MVSC method to determine effective anchor cardinality per cluster within a single optimization, CAL addresses a practical mismatch: complex, high-density clusters benefit from more anchors while simple ones do not, and a uniform budget either wastes capacity or under-represents. The approach is elegant in reusing the well-studied ℓ_{2,1} column-sparsity operator (originally for feature selection) in the anchor-allocation role, and the inter-cluster decorrelation term provides a clean mechanism for enhancing separability. Open questions include scalability when k is very large (e.g., 100Leaves with k=100 makes the Sylvester-equation update and the O(m³k³n) QP step potentially expensive), whether the method extends naturally to incomplete or streaming multi-view settings, and whether the k-means post-processing step could be replaced by a differentiable clustering head for end-to-end training.
