## Problem

Diffusion multimodal LLMs (dMLLMs) with Chain-of-Thought prompting exhibit two critical reasoning failures: they generate final answer tokens prematurely at early diffusion timesteps (before sufficient reasoning), and they show minimal dependency on visual inputs during those early timesteps—fundamentally different from autoregressive VLMs. Existing CoT reasoning methods designed for AR VLMs cannot be directly transferred because dMLLMs restore tokens in parallel rather than sequentially, leaving a gap in how to guide their reasoning progression and visual grounding.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on a dMLLM (e.g., LaViDa, MMaDA) that takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a text response containing intermediate reasoning steps followed by a final answer. Generation is controlled by two hyperparameters: the response length L (number of tokens) and the number of diffusion steps T (number of unmasking iterations). The method is entirely training-free and modifies only the confidence scores used in the remasking step at each timestep, requiring no architectural changes or additional model parameters.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary training-free inference-time interventions. Position & Step Penalty (PSP) modifies the confidence score of each token j at timestep i by multiplying it with a penalty factor [1 − γ(1−τ_i)·rel(j)], where τ_i is the relative diffusion progress and rel(j) is the token's normalized position in the sequence; this disproportionately suppresses late-position tokens (where the answer typically resides) during early timesteps, forcing the model to build intermediate reasoning before committing to an answer. Visual Reasoning Guidance (VRG) adapts classifier-free guidance to the visual modality: it computes conditional logits (with the visual prompt) and unconditional logits (without it) in parallel, then amplifies their difference via logits_vrg = logits_u + (s_vrg+1)·(logits_c − logits_u), strengthening the model's reliance on visual evidence at every timestep. The two are combined by applying PSP to the softmax of the VRG-guided logits, which then drives the remasking decision. Experiments on LaViDa and MMaDA across M3CoT, MMBench, ScienceQA, and V* Bench show up to 7.5% accuracy gains over baselines and 3× speedup compared to using four times more diffusion steps.

## Results

On LaViDa with generation length L=64 and T=32 diffusion steps, PSP&VRG achieves 48.4% on M3CoT, 74.9% on MMBench, 72.8% on SQA-IMG, and 45.5% on V* Bench, outperforming the Low-conf baseline (45.8/72.8/71.0/42.9) by 2.6–4.2 points. At the larger L=256/T=128 setting, PSP&VRG reaches 50.3% M3CoT and 75.3% MMBench. For MMaDa, PSP&VRG at 64/32 yields 36.3% M3CoT and 59.9% MMBench, surpassing the Low-conf baseline (33.7/56.1) by 2.6 and 3.8 points respectively. The method achieves up to 7.5% higher accuracy than using four times more diffusion steps (256/128) while running over 3× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PSP&VRG against two AR-VLM CoT methods (DDCoT and CCoT) and three native dMLLM remasking strategies (Low-conf, Entropy, Margin). PSP&VRG consistently outperforms DDCoT across all four benchmarks and both models: on LaViDa at 64/32, it gains +2.7 (M3CoT), +2.2 (MMBench), +1.7 (SQA-IMG), and +4.2 (V* Bench) over DDCoT; on MMaDa the margins are +2.2, +4.2, +0.7, and +3.2 respectively. Against CCoT, the improvements are similar in magnitude (+1.6 to +5.2 points depending on benchmark and model). Notably, DDCoT and CCoT—designed for autoregressive VLMs—fail to beat even the simplest Low-conf remasking strategy on several settings, confirming that AR-CoT methods do not transfer to dMLLMs. PSP&VRG also surpasses the strongest native remasking baseline (Low-conf) by 2.6–4.2 points on LaViDa and 2.6–3.8 points on MMaDa at 64/32, and critically, the 64/32 configuration with PSP&VRG matches or exceeds the 256/128 Low-conf baseline (e.g., 74.9% vs. 74.3% on MMBench for LaViDa; 59.9% vs. 55.7% for MMaDa) while running over 3× faster.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the emerging line of diffusion-based multimodal language models (dMLLMs) such as LaViDa and MMaDA, which extend discrete diffusion LLMs (LLaDA, Dream) to joint visual-textual generation. Conceptually, it departs from AR-based CoT methods (DDCoT, CCoT) that rely on sequential left-to-right token generation and show limited transfer to parallel diffusion decoding. The key methodological lineage is Classifier-Free Guidance from image diffusion [Ho & Salimans, 2022], which the authors adapt to amplify visual conditioning in dMLLM inference (VRG), while PSP introduces a novel position-aware penalty mechanism with no direct AR counterpart. The paper positions itself as the first quantitative analysis of dMLLM reasoning dynamics, filling a gap left by prior interpretability work that focused exclusively on AR VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of two distinct failure modes—premature answer generation and inverted visual grounding patterns—provides a diagnostic framework for understanding why dMLLMs underperform AR VLMs on reasoning tasks, a question that has been largely unaddressed. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of PSP and VRG means the approach can be adopted immediately across existing dMLLMs without retraining, and the demonstrated 3× speedup while matching 4× more diffusion steps offers a practical efficiency-quality tradeoff that is central to dMLLM deployment. Open questions include whether the weak early visual grounding is an artifact of current dMLLM training objectives or a fundamental property of parallel token restoration, and whether more sophisticated guidance (e.g., timestep-adaptive scales) could further close the remaining gap with AR CoT performance.
