## Problem

Existing video security systems remain reactive, detecting crimes only during or after occurrence, while VLM-based video understanding models lack explicit mechanisms to model the spatio-temporal causal relationships that underlie criminal precursors. Two specific gaps are identified: (1) no crime dataset provides the spatio-temporal causal annotations needed to learn anticipatory dynamics, and (2) no streaming architecture explicitly encodes multi-entity causal structures for forward-looking reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262987/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes streaming video at 2 FPS in 8-frame windows, processing only past frames (t_obs < t_gt) in an online, causal manner. Outputs are structured as (query, answer) pairs covering three anticipatory metrics: multi-class crime category (13 UCF-Crime classes) via weighted F1, crime onset timestamp via mean absolute error (TimeDiff), and crime/non-crime discrimination probability via AUC evaluated over 2s, 20s, and 60s future windows. The STCH module injects pre-computed graph tokens (a global scene token plus typed hyperedge tokens) as a prefix into the VLM's embedding space, so the VLM receives both raw video frames and explicit causal structure simultaneously.

## Method

The framework combines two causal-driven components. First, the STCRC dataset (73K samples) provides hierarchical supervision across five progressive tasks—from immediate event-to-event causality and spatial distribution prediction to full causal chain reconstruction and entity-level causality attribution—enabling the VLM to learn structured anticipatory reasoning. Second, the Spatio-Temporal Causal Hypergraph (STCH) is a streaming co-processor with three layers: a state extraction layer maintaining a dynamic weighted base graph with per-node GRU memory and exponential activity decay; a causal reasoning layer detecting two hyperedge types (Causal Cascade Effects linking motion mutations to spatially-linked responsive entities, and Group Evolution capturing aggregation/separation of entity clusters); and a token translation layer that encodes the graph via GAT and attention pooling into a global token plus typed hyperedge tokens. These tokens are prepended to the VLM input (Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned with LoRA, rank 64), while a cross-attention memory bank retains long-range perceptual context across the stream. The key insight is that converting implicit entity dynamics into explicit, typed causal hyperedges gives the VLM a structured inductive bias for anticipatory reasoning that raw frame sequences alone cannot provide.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, the proposed framework achieves WF1 of 40.67 (vs. 23.83 for the best streaming baseline Flash-VStream), AUC-L of 0.692 (vs. 0.609 for Holmes-VAU), and TimeDiff of 55.80 frames. On XD-Violence, it reaches WF1 of 36.90, AUC-L of 0.622, and TimeDiff of 31.01, outperforming all 12 baselines across every metric on both datasets. The paper reports a 70.7% relative improvement in crime classification, a 10.1% gain in crime detection (AUC), and a 3.7% reduction in temporal prediction error over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across both UCF-Crime and XD-Violence. Among streaming VLMs, Flash-VStream is the closest competitor: the proposed method improves WF1 by 16.84 points on UCF-Crime (40.67 vs. 23.83) and by 6.39 on XD-Violence (36.90 vs. 30.51), while reducing TimeDiff by 3.01 and 4.48 frames respectively. Among offline VLMs, Holmes-VAU achieves the highest long-term AUC (0.609 on UCF-Crime), but the proposed method surpasses it by 0.083 (0.692), and GPT-4o, despite strong general reasoning, trails by 27.66 WF1 points on UCF-Crime. Traditional deep-learning baselines (UR-DMU, CLAP) are substantially outperformed, with TimeDiff gaps exceeding 20 frames and WF1 gaps exceeding 30 points. The two streaming baselines evaluated without pre-training (Dispider, SVQA) perform worst overall, with SVQA showing a TimeDiff of 455.56 on UCF-Crime, indicating severe temporal misalignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads—streaming video understanding (VideoLLM-online, Flash-VStream, Dispider), hypergraph-based video modeling (group activity recognition, video QA), and VLM-based video reasoning (Qwen2-VL, VideoLLaMA2)—into a unified framework for crime anticipation. It departs from prior streaming VLMs by inserting an explicit spatio-temporal causal structure (the STCH hypergraph) between perception and language reasoning, rather than relying on implicit temporal context in memory banks. It also departs from traditional video anomaly detection by reframing the task as proactive anticipation of causal precursors rather than reactive classification of anomalies, and introduces the STCRC dataset to provide the hierarchical causal supervision that prior crime datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The STCRC dataset (73K samples, five progressive causal tasks) is a potentially reusable benchmark for spatio-temporal causal reasoning in video, and the STCH module offers a general-purpose streaming co-processor that could extend beyond crime to other anticipation tasks (e.g., accident prediction, safety monitoring). However, the 'causal' framing is explicitly predictive rather than structural (the authors acknowledge this), and the system's performance depends on upstream perception quality (detection, depth, tracking), which limits real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the hypergraph causal structure generalizes to more complex multi-agent scenes, whether the approach scales to longer prediction horizons, and whether the gains persist when the VLM backbone is replaced or when domain shift is more severe than the UCF-Crime → XD-Violence transfer tested here.
