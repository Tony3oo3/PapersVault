## Problem

Video temporal grounding models tend to learn spurious correlations, overly associating text queries with static backgrounds rather than the target temporal dynamics. While prior work (TD-DETR) introduced content mixing augmentation to break these correlations, the mixing is query-agnostic, generating false negatives when semantically related replacement clips are mistakenly treated as background. CVA addresses this by making context diversification query-aware and enforcing boundary-level semantic invariance under contextual shifts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262093/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an untrimmed video segmented into L clips, with per-clip features extracted from both a pre-trained SlowFast network and a CLIP vision encoder, alongside a text query encoded by the CLIP text encoder. It produces predicted temporal moments as (center, span) pairs via a DETR-style transformer decoder, along with per-clip saliency scores for highlight detection. The training pipeline additionally generates augmented mixed videos by replacing non-moment clips with query-filtered clips from other videos, while preserving the GT moment and its immediate temporal neighborhood.

## Method

CVA combines three synergistic components. First, Query-aware Context Diversification (QCD) uses precomputed CLIP video-text similarities to build an instance-specific candidate pool of replacement clips whose similarity to the query falls within a percentile-defined range (α=10th percentile of non-pairs to β=60th percentile of GT-pairs), preventing false-negative contamination while preserving augmentation diversity. Second, the Context-enhanced Transformer Encoder (CTE) is a hierarchical encoder where each block applies windowed self-attention to video features for local temporal patterns, standard self-attention to learnable queries, and bidirectional cross-attention between the two streams, with multi-scale features aggregated across blocks via a learnable weighted sum. Third, the Context-invariant Boundary Discrimination (CBD) loss is a contrastive objective applied at the start and end boundaries of GT moments, contrasting boundary features across two different QCD augmentations against both temporally adjacent background clips and the top-N hardest semantically confusable negatives, enforcing context-invariant representations at the most challenging alignment regions.

## Results

On QVHighlights, CVA achieves 70.05 R1@0.5, 55.32 R1@0.7, and 47.49 average mAP for Moment Retrieval, along with 44.43 mAP and 66.01 HIT@1 for Highlight Detection — all state-of-the-art. On TACoS, CVA reaches 43.21 R1@0.5 and 41.07 mIoU, and on Charades-STA it attains 62.61 R1@0.5 and 53.35 mIoU. The headline gain is approximately 5 points in R1@0.7 over the previous best (TD-DETR) on QVHighlights, attributed to the query-aware augmentation preventing false-negative contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CVA outperforms all 19 compared baselines across every metric on all three benchmarks. On QVHighlights, the most direct competitor is TD-DETR [41], whose query-agnostic content mixing CVA explicitly improves upon; CVA gains +4.95 R1@0.7 (55.32 vs. 50.37) and +5.52 R1@0.5 (70.05 vs. 64.53) over TD-DETR, and +4.10 HD mAP (44.43 vs. 40.33) over CG-DETR [22], the previous HD leader. On TACoS, CVA surpasses BAM-DETR [13] by +1.76 mIoU (41.07 vs. 39.31) and +1.76 R1@0.5 (43.21 vs. 41.45). On Charades-STA, CVA leads BAM-DETR by +2.66 R1@0.5 (62.61 vs. 59.95) and +1.40 R1@0.7 (40.78 vs. 39.38). The ablation in Table 5(a) further isolates the contribution of query-awareness: replacing QCD with the query-agnostic mixing from TD-DETR reduces R1@0.7 from 51.98 to 48.39, confirming that the false-negative mitigation is the primary source of the large margin over prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CVA extends the DETR-based video moment retrieval framework introduced by Moment-DETR [15] and directly builds on TD-DETR [41], which identified spurious background-text correlations and proposed query-agnostic content mixing as a remedy. The key conceptual departure is making that augmentation query-aware via CLIP-based similarity filtering to eliminate false negatives, complemented by a boundary-focused contrastive loss and a hierarchical windowed-attention encoder. The paper positions itself within the broader trend of improving fine-grained video-text alignment through data-centric strategies (negative pair training, context diversification) and architectural refinements (multi-scale temporal modeling), but argues that prior mixing strategies are insufficiently selective about what constitutes a valid negative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5-point R1@0.7 gain over the previous SOTA on QVHighlights is substantial for a task where improvements have been increasingly marginal, and the query-aware augmentation strategy is a practical, drop-in improvement that other VTG methods could adopt without architectural changes. The CBD loss offers a targeted mechanism for the hardest sub-problem (boundary precision) that is orthogonal to and composable with other alignment strategies. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based similarity thresholding generalizes to domains with weaker CLIP coverage, whether the approach scales to significantly longer videos (beyond TACoS's ~2-minute clips), and whether the false-negative mitigation benefit persists when the candidate pool becomes very large in open-world settings.
