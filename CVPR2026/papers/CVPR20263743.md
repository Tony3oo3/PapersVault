## Problem

Standard RLVR methods for VLMs verify only the final textual answer, leaving upstream visual perception unexamined. This causes the model to reinforce logically coherent but visually ungrounded reasoning chains, producing hallucinations and reward hacking that impose a hard ceiling on multimodal reasoning performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a textual reasoning question as the primary input, plus a derived perception checklist of K short, verifiable sub-questions (e.g., counting, object identification, geometric relations) serialized into a single structured prompt. At inference/training time the model produces two types of outputs: step-by-step reasoning traces with a final answer, and direct answers to each perception probe without intermediate reasoning. Perception answers are parsed and scored by a rule-based verifier, while reasoning answers are checked against ground-truth labels.

## Method

PEARL extends GRPO with a dual-branch perception-reasoning synergy. First, a perception checklist of K task-aligned sub-questions is derived from each QA pair using four patterns (direct extraction, pattern summary, derived calculation, inverse-from-answer), yielding concise, rule-verifiable probes. During training, auxiliary perception rollouts produce a mean perceptual reward R̄_p that serves two roles: (1) a fidelity gate that halts reasoning rollouts entirely when R̄_p = 0, preventing the model from reinforcing reasoning on flawed perception, and (2) a soft reweighting factor that scales the reasoning advantage by min(R̄_p, 0.5), amplifying perception-anchored updates. The final objective is a dual GRPO loss J = J_GRPO(θ; Â_r) + λ·J_GRPO(θ; Â_p) with λ = 0.1, combined with a relaxed online filtering criterion that retains samples whenever either the reasoning or perception signal is non-saturated.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, PEARL achieves an average accuracy of 47.9 across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the base model (40.1) by +7.8 points and GRPO (44.2) by +3.7 points. On the 3B backbone, PEARL reaches 39.8 average, a +8.0 gain over the base model (31.8) and +5.0 over GRPO (34.8). The largest individual-benchmark gains appear on WeMath (geometry/diagram-centric), where PEARL scores 45.5 (7B) vs. 40.9 for GRPO, and on MathVerse (vision-only), where PEARL scores 50.8 (7B) vs. 46.4 for GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PEARL outperforms all nine baselines on average accuracy for both the 3B and 7B backbones. Against the standard RLVR baselines, PEARL (7B) beats GRPO by +3.7 and DAPO by +2.8 on average, with the largest margin on WeMath (+4.6 over GRPO) and DynaMath (+4.9 over GRPO). Among perception-aware methods, PEARL surpasses the strongest competitor PAPO_G (7B) by +2.1 on average and Vision-SR1 by +5.8, while on the 3B model it edges out PAPO_D by +0.6. Notably, PEARL's gains are most pronounced on vision-intensive benchmarks (WeMath, MathVerse) and most consistent across all six datasets, whereas PAPO and Vision-SR1 show more variable improvements. The paper also reports that PEARL's perception-reweight and early-stop gating contribute an additional +1.2 average points over a configuration that uses only the perception checklist without the fidelity gate (46.7 → 47.9 on 7B).

## Relation to Prior Work

PEARL extends the GRPO/DAPO RLVR pipeline for VLMs by introducing a dual-branch perception-reasoning objective, departing from the outcome-only reward formulation that prior methods (GRPO, DAPO, VL-Rethinker) rely on. It positions itself against two existing lines of perception-aware RL: interactive visual manipulation approaches (DeepEyes, Look-Back, THYME) that require curated interaction data, and caption/description-based reward methods (Visionary-R1, R1-VL, PAPO) that suffer from noisy free-form scoring. The key conceptual departure is replacing dense, unstructured perceptual supervision with task-anchored, rule-verifiable QA probes that serve simultaneously as a direct perceptual reward and a hard fidelity gate on reasoning updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear empirical diagnosis—GRPO reduces reasoning errors but leaves perception errors essentially unchanged—that motivates a principled fix and validates the perception-reasoning coupling hypothesis. The finding that perception-only training (no reasoning reward) still yields non-trivial reasoning gains, and that a small set of task-anchored probes outperforms a much larger dense caption-based checklist, offers practical guidance for practitioners building VLM RL pipelines. Open questions include whether the binary fidelity gate (R̄_p = 0 vs. > 0) is the optimal thresholding strategy, how checklist construction cost scales with dataset size, and whether the approach transfers to non-mathematical multimodal reasoning domains.
