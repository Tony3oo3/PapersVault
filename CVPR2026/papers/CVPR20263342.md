## Problem

Hand-object interaction (HOI) video generation requires modeling physical constraints like contact and occlusion, but existing HOI representations face a fundamental dilemma: scalable 2D cues (optical flow, segmentation, keypoints) lack explicit contact regions and holistic 3D structure, while high-fidelity 3D meshes demand expensive annotations that prevent large-scale training. Moreover, prevailing multi-stage pipelines compound errors across stages, degrading both physics realism and visual quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single observed image (first frame) and a free-form task description as input, and jointly produces an RGB video (17 frames on Taste-Rob, 25 frames on Taco) together with a corresponding HOI representation consisting of contact-augmented hand-object contours alpha-blended onto per-frame depth maps. The HOI representation is not a pre-computed conditioning signal but is generated simultaneously with the video from the same denoising process, meaning the model must learn to produce physically consistent interaction cues end-to-end. The representation is curated at scale (100K+ videos) using a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with Chain-of-Thought prompting for open-vocabulary grounding, SAM2 for mask propagation, and Video Depth Anything for video-consistent depth estimation.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint-generation paradigm built on a Hierarchical Joint Denoiser (DiT backbone) that co-denoises visual and interaction tokens in a unified 3D-VAE latent space, eliminating multi-stage error accumulation. The denoiser employs a share-and-specialization strategy: a Shared Semantics module (layers 1–k*) enforces cross-modal consistency by maximizing cosine similarity between corresponding visual and interaction hidden states via an explicit alignment loss, while a Specialized Details module (layers k*+1 onward) injects a learnable interaction embedding exclusively into interaction tokens to capture modality-specific characteristics. Two architectural modifications support this: identical positional encodings are assigned to visual and interaction tokens at the same spatio-temporal position to encode their correspondence, and lightweight LoRA adapters are applied selectively to interaction tokens only (via a binary mask on Q/K/V projections) to adapt for HOI generation while preserving pre-trained visual knowledge. The composite training loss combines diffusion losses for both modalities with the alignment loss (λ_HOI=1.0, λ_align=0.1), and the framework is instantiated on CogVideoX-I2V-5B and Wan2.1-I2V-14B to demonstrate versatility.

## Results

On the Taste-Rob dataset, SCAR_W (Wan2.1-based) achieves the best overall VBench score of 9.084, outperforming CogVideoX (8.959), Wan2.1 (8.897), and FLOVD (8.888). On the Taco dataset, SCAR_W scores 8.899 overall, surpassing CogVideoX (8.511), Wan2.1 (8.792), and FLOVD (8.619). The CogVideoX-based variant SCAR_C also consistently outperforms all baselines, reaching 9.043 on Taste-Rob and 8.793 on Taco. Gains are most pronounced on text-to-video alignment (VCS) and image-to-video subject consistency (ISC), indicating improved physics realism and task adherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAR_W (Wan2.1 backbone) achieves the highest overall VBench score on both datasets: 9.084 on Taste-Rob and 8.899 on Taco, beating CogVideoX by +0.125/+0.388, Wan2.1 by +0.187/+0.107, and FLOVD by +0.196/+0.280. The CogVideoX-based variant SCAR_C also wins on all metrics, with the largest margin over CogVideoX on Taco (+0.282 TS) and over FLOVD on Taste-Rob (+0.155 TS). The most consistent gains appear in text-to-video alignment (VCS: 0.194 vs. 0.185–0.189 for baselines on Taste-Rob) and image-to-video subject consistency (ISC: 0.961 vs. 0.947–0.955), confirming that the joint HOI representation improves both task adherence and object identity preservation. FLOVD, the only multi-stage baseline, shows the weakest performance on Taco (TS=8.619), consistent with the paper's argument that error accumulation from inaccurate optical flow degrades downstream video quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on pretrained video diffusion transformers (CogVideoX, Wan2.1) and departs from the dominant multi-stage HOI video generation paradigm (e.g., FLOVD, MaskI2V, Taste-Rob) where a representation is first predicted and then used to condition a separate video generator. The key conceptual departure is a joint-generation paradigm with a hierarchical share-and-specialization denoiser that co-denoises video and HOI representation tokens in a unified latent space, eliminating inter-stage error accumulation. The proposed structure and contact-aware representation (contact-augmented contours alpha-blended onto depth maps) is positioned as a scalable middle ground between high-fidelity 3D mesh representations (which require expensive motion-capture annotations) and simpler 2D cues like optical flow or segmentation masks (which lack explicit contact and holistic structure).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in HOI video generation: the scalability-fidelity dilemma in interaction representations, and demonstrates that a jointly generated, contact-aware supervisory signal can improve physics realism and open-world generalization without 3D annotations. The open-world generalization results (unseen objects, complex distractors) are the most compelling contribution, suggesting the learned interaction patterns transfer beyond the training distribution. Limitations include reliance on a semi-automated annotation pipeline (VLM grounding + SAM2 + manual verification) for the 100k+ video dataset, scale-ambiguous depth maps, and evaluation limited to short sequences (17–25 frames). Open questions include whether the joint-generation paradigm extends to longer videos, multi-object interactions, or real-time robotic control settings.
