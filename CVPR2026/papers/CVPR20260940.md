## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D perception methods face a trilemma: they require per-scene optimization (NeRF, 3DGS), fail to maintain semantic consistency across viewpoints, and incur high computational costs. While feed-forward geometry estimators like DUSt3R have eliminated the need for per-scene training, they lack integrated semantic understanding, leaving a gap in efficient, tuning-free 3D semantic reconstruction from unposed images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260940/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unposed multi-view RGB images with no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth maps required. It produces a unified 3D semantic point cloud where each point carries both 3D coordinates and a dense CLIP-space semantic embedding. An optional natural-language text query (e.g., 'black chair') is encoded by CLIP and matched against the point embeddings via globally normalized cosine similarity to retrieve and localize semantically matching 3D regions, enabling open-vocabulary interaction without predefined category labels.

## Method

PE3R is a tuning-free, three-stage feed-forward pipeline. First, Pixel Embedding Disambiguation decomposes each image into hierarchical masks via SAM/SAM2, encodes them with CLIP, and aggregates features using an area-weighted spherical interpolation (slerp) that preserves unit norm and steers toward more semantically reliable directions, resolving both part-whole and cross-view ambiguities. Second, Semantic Point Cloud Reconstruction uses DUSt3R (or VGGT) to generate initial 3D pointmaps, then detects spatial outliers within semantically homogeneous regions and corrects them by blending anomalous pixel RGB values with their semantic neighborhood before re-running the pointmap predictor—a cheap image-space refinement that avoids expensive 3D regularization. Third, Global View Perception encodes a text query with CLIP's text encoder and applies globally min-max-normalized cosine similarity across all views to retrieve 3D points exceeding a threshold, enabling open-vocabulary localization. The entire pipeline requires no 3D supervision, no per-scene fine-tuning, and completes in ~5 minutes versus 43–648 minutes for optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, PE3R achieves mIoU 0.8951, mPA 0.9617, and mP 0.9726, surpassing the previous best (GOI) by 3.05% in mIoU. On Replica, PE3R reaches mIoU 0.6531, mPA 0.8377, and mP 0.8444, again leading all baselines. For multi-view depth estimation on RobustMVD, PE3R with VGGT achieves the best average relative error (3.7↓) and inlier ratio (75.2↑) across KITTI, ScanNet, ETH3D, DTU, and Tanks & Temples. In terms of efficiency, PE3R completes full 3D semantic reconstruction in 5 minutes with no training phase, a 9× speedup over the next fastest method (GOI at 45 min) and up to 130× faster than F-3DGS (648 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PE3R outperforms all 13 baselines on both Mip-NeRF360 and Replica for open-vocabulary segmentation. The closest competitor is GOI [34], which PE3R surpasses by 3.05% mIoU on Mip-NeRF360 (0.8951 vs 0.8646) and 3.62% on Replica (0.6531 vs 0.6169); the gap widens substantially against LSM [12] (+27% mIoU on Mip-NeRF360) and LERF [20] (+62.5% mIoU). On multi-view depth estimation (RobustMVD), PE3R with VGGT achieves the best average (rel 3.7, τ 75.2), edging out VGGT alone (rel 3.8, τ 74.4) and substantially outperforming DUSt3R (rel 4.7, τ 64.5) and MASt3R (rel 24.5, τ 10.6). However, PE3R with DUSt3R is marginally behind DUSt3R alone on ScanNet (rel 5.5 vs 4.9) and DTU (rel 3.2 vs 3.5 is actually better, but τ 69.1 vs 69.3 is slightly lower), which the authors attribute to low semantic diversity in those scenes. In runtime, PE3R (5 min, no training) is 9× faster than GOI (45 min), 30× faster than LangSplat (149 min), and 130× faster than F-3DGS (648 min), while also eliminating the per-scene optimization step entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

PE3R sits at the intersection of two established threads: feed-forward 3D geometry estimation (DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT, Fast3R) and 2D-to-3D semantic lifting (LERF, LangSplat, GOI, LSM, Feature3DGS). Prior semantic lifting methods rely on per-scene NeRF or 3DGS optimization, while feed-forward geometry methods produce point clouds without semantic structure. PE3R departs from both by using a feed-forward pointmap predictor as the geometric backbone and layering a tuning-free semantic disambiguation pipeline (SAM/SAM2 masks → CLIP embeddings → area-weighted spherical interpolation) on top, eliminating per-scene training entirely. The key conceptual contribution is the area-weighted spherical interpolation for resolving hierarchical and cross-view semantic ambiguity, plus a semantic-guided geometric refinement that feeds smoothed images back into the pointmap predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9× speedup (5 min vs. up to 648 min for prior methods) and zero-shot generalization make 3D semantic reconstruction practical for large-scale and time-sensitive applications like robotics and AR/VR, where per-scene optimization is prohibitive. A nuanced finding is that semantic guidance improves geometric accuracy on average but can degrade performance on low-semantic-diversity scenes (ScanNet, DTU), suggesting the approach is most beneficial in visually rich environments. Open questions include scalability to very large scenes beyond 4-frame snippets, robustness of the IoU-based object grouping heuristic under extreme viewpoint changes, and whether the semantic-guided refinement generalizes to more challenging geometric conditions (e.g., highly reflective or transparent surfaces).
