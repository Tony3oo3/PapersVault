## Problem

Existing remote sensing segmentation models suffer from three coupled limitations: they support only optical imagery (not SAR), handle only one prompt type (visual or textual, not both), and treat class categories as a flat list ignoring natural semantic hierarchies. This prevents a single tuning-free model from flexibly segmenting diverse Earth observation scenes across sensors, prompt modalities, and granularity levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263123/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a target image (optical or SAR) together with a prompt pair: a reference image with its pixel-level visual annotation (mask, box, or sampled points) and a category name string. It produces per-pixel segmentation masks in three interchangeable modes—visual-only, textual-only, and fused—without any task-specific fine-tuning. The visual prompt can be scaled from 1 to 50+ exemplars, and the model natively handles dual-polarization SAR inputs (VV/VH) that break competing methods.

## Method

SkySense-VITA introduces a prompt-and-prediction decoupling architecture built on the SkySense factorized encoder. VITA-Former performs prompt decoupling: learnable modality prototypes (optical/SAR) are injected into visual tokens via cross-attention to bridge the sensor gap, then masked self-attention isolates visual and textual tokens while allowing fused tokens to attend to both, producing three in-context query streams. VITA-Decoder performs prediction decoupling: segmentation queries are replicated into three mode-specific copies refined with block-diagonal masked self-attention to prevent cross-branch interference, and class assignment uses prompt-guided similarity (dot product with in-context queries) rather than a fixed classifier. Training follows a progressive two-stage pipeline: first, Image-Level Alignment Pretraining uses intra-modal contrastive and cross-modal alignment losses to anchor the SAR encoder to the frozen optical-text space; second, Pixel-Level In-context Pretraining employs Semantic Granularity Annealing (SGA), a cosine-scheduled coarse-to-fine curriculum over a Qwen3-constructed semantic tree that smoothly shifts the target label granularity from super-classes to fine-grained leaves, reducing catastrophic forgetting and stabilizing hierarchical learning.

## Results

On 11 in-distribution datasets, SkySense-VITA (fused visual+text prompt) achieves an average mIoU of 57.2%, outperforming the strongest baseline SkySense++ (44.5% avg) by over 18% mIoU. On the out-of-distribution evaluation (7 datasets covering domain and class generalization), the fused mode reaches 62.19% mIoU on FloodNet and 86.85% on GLH-Water, surpassing all prior methods. The model also establishes a new SAR benchmark at 29.58% mIoU on ETCI-Flood, where no competing method reports results. Overall, SkySense-VITA sets SOTA on all 18 evaluated datasets with an average lead exceeding 10% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkySense-VITA outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset where comparisons are possible. Against the strongest in-context baseline SkySense++ (retrained on the same dataset), the fused mode gains +19.53 mIoU on FLAIR (64.03 vs 44.50), +19.24 on Potsdam (81.94 vs 62.70), and +28.31 on QuickQuake (75.25 vs 46.94). Against the best open-vocabulary baseline SegEarth-OV, gains are even larger: +31.84 on FLAIR and +52.02 on GF1-WHU (71.27 vs 19.25). Natural-image in-context models (Painter, SegGPT, PerSAM, DINOv, SINE, SEGIC) all lag substantially, with SEGIC being the closest at 39.17 on FLAIR versus SkySense-VITA's 64.03. On out-of-distribution tasks, SkySense-VITA (fused) leads SkySense++ by +7.38 on Vaihingen (62.75 vs 55.37), +12.57 on GLH-Water (86.85 vs 74.28), and +43.58 on GLVM-Post (46.08 vs 18.50), while also being the only method to report results on SAR (C2S-SAR) and class-generalization (SOS) benchmarks. The only setting where a competitor wins is intra-image point prompting (Table 9), where SAM2 achieves 71.21 mIoU with 10 points versus SkySense-VITA's 43.27 (visual-only) or 75.25 (fused), but this is a different task formulation (intra-image vs. inter-image in-context).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SkySense (CVPR 2024), a multi-modal remote sensing foundation model with a factorized optical/SAR encoder, and adopts the Mask2Former query-based decoder as the prediction backbone. It departs from prior in-context segmentation work (Painter, SegGPT, DINOv, SINE, SEGIC) which operates on natural images, and from remote-sensing-specific in-context models (SkySense++) that support only visual prompts, as well as open-vocabulary models (SegEarth-OV, SkySense-O) that support only textual prompts. The key architectural departure is the prompt-and-prediction decoupling strategy (VITA-Former for prompt fusion, VITA-Decoder for mode-isolated decoding), which allows a single model to co-optimize visual-only, textual-only, and fused branches without cross-branch interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SkySense-VITA is the first unified in-context segmentation model to natively handle both optical and SAR imagery with visual, textual, and fused prompts, addressing a practical gap for all-weather, all-time Earth observation. The newly introduced Sky-VT-300k dataset (300k+ samples, 176 classes, 7 satellite platforms, pixel-level masks with aligned exemplars and descriptors) provides a much-needed large-scale multi-modal benchmark for in-context remote sensing segmentation. Open questions include whether the SGA curriculum generalizes to deeper semantic hierarchies, how the model scales to additional sensor modalities (hyperspectral, LiDAR), and whether the decoupled architecture can be extended to video or temporal segmentation tasks as the authors note for future work.
