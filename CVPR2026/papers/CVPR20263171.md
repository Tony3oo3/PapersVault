## Problem

Existing safety-guidance methods for text-to-image diffusion models handle multiple harmful categories by naively concatenating all harmful keywords into a single aggregated safety direction. The paper identifies that this category-agnostic aggregation causes 'harmful conflicts'—directional inconsistency and attenuation among heterogeneous category-specific safety directions—that actively degrade safety performance rather than improve it, a previously overlooked failure mode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263171/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text prompt and a predefined set of harmful keywords organized into discrete categories (e.g., hate, sexual content, violence, illegal activity), operating within a Stable Diffusion v1.5 latent diffusion pipeline. At each denoising timestep, it consumes the current latent state and prompt embedding to dynamically select a single dominant harmful category, then produces a safety-corrected noise estimate (latent space) or a projected prompt embedding (text space). The final output is a generated RGB image with reduced harmful content while preserving prompt semantics; the method is fully training-free and requires no model weight modification.

## Method

CASG resolves harmful conflicts through two components applied at every denoising timestep. Conflict-aware Category Identification (CaCI) measures directional alignment between each harmful category's guidance and the prompt guidance—via cosine similarity in latent space or residual magnitude after orthogonal projection in text space—and selects the single most-aligned category as dominant. Conflict-resolving Guidance Application (CrGA) then applies safety steering exclusively along that one category's direction, replacing the multi-category aggregation that causes cancellation and misalignment. In latent space, CASG+SLD substitutes the aggregated harmful direction in SLD with the single dominant-category direction; in text space, CASG+SAFREE projects the prompt embedding onto the orthogonal complement of only the dominant harmful subspace. The framework is plug-and-play, retaining all original SLD/SAFREE hyperparameters and mechanisms while changing only how the harmful reference is selected.

## Results

CASG+SLD achieves the lowest harmful rates across all four safety benchmarks: 10.2% (I2P), 9.8% (T2VSafetyBench), 9.8% (Unsafe Diffusion), and 3.9% (CoProv2), reducing the harmful rate by up to 15.4 percentage points over the SLD baseline (25.2% → 9.8% on T2VSafetyBench). CASG+SAFREE similarly improves over SAFREE on all benchmarks (e.g., 17.5% vs 24.2% on Unsafe Diffusion). Image quality on COCO is preserved, with CASG+SLD reporting FID 52.0 and CLIP 29.36, nearly identical to SLD's 52.1 and 29.22.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASG+SLD outperforms the SLD baseline on all four benchmarks, with the largest gain on T2VSafetyBench (9.8% vs 25.2%, a 15.4-point reduction) and substantial improvements on I2P (10.2% vs 12.7%), Unsafe Diffusion (9.8% vs 15.7%), and CoProv2 (3.9% vs 7.1%). Similarly, CASG+SAFREE beats SAFREE across all benchmarks (e.g., 17.5% vs 24.2% on UD, 11.8% vs 14.2% on CoProv2). Against model-editing baselines, CASG+SLD achieves the best harmful rates while maintaining competitive FID (52.0 vs 67.35 for RECE, 77.41 for UCE), whereas ESD provides negligible safety improvement (42.0% on I2P). CASG+SLD also surpasses SafetyDPO (10.2% vs 13.7% on I2P, 9.8% vs 24.0% on T2VSafetyBench) without requiring any model retraining. In a separate comparison (Table 3), LLM-assisted SLD variants (GPT-4o+SLD, QwenGuard+SLD) underperform CASG+SLD on all three benchmarks tested, confirming that dynamic per-timestep category identification outperforms static LLM-based classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety-guidance line of work initiated by SLD (latent-space directional steering) and SAFREE (text-space orthogonal projection), both of which handle multi-category safety by naively concatenating all harmful keywords into a single aggregated guidance. CASG departs from this category-agnostic aggregation by introducing a dynamic, per-timestep identification of the single most-aligned harmful category (CaCI) and applying safety correction only along that direction (CrGA). It also positions itself against LLM-assisted classification approaches (GPT-4o, QwenGuard) that fix the category once at prompt time, arguing that the dominant harmful tendency evolves during denoising and requires continuous re-identification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that stacking more harmful categories can *increase* the harmful rate (e.g., 48.8% for all categories vs. 3.2% for the correct single category) challenges a widely adopted practice in T2I safety deployment and suggests that current multi-category safeguards may be actively counterproductive. CASG's training-free, plug-and-play design makes it immediately adoptable alongside existing SLD or SAFREE pipelines without retraining. Open questions include whether the single-dominant-category assumption holds for genuinely multi-category prompts, how the method scales to DiT-based architectures (e.g., SD3, Hunyuan-DiT), and whether the per-timestep k forward passes introduce prohibitive latency at production scale.
