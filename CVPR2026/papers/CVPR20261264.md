## Problem

Existing inference-time scaling for text-to-visual generation (e.g., Best-of-N, search-over-paths) expands only the visual search space while keeping the prompt static, causing prompt adherence to plateau as compute increases. Prior prompt-refinement approaches operate per-sample and remain blind to the population-level failure patterns that become visible only through visual scaling. The paper asks whether jointly scaling prompts and visuals can sustain improvements in text-visual alignment under the scaling law.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261264/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user text prompt and initial noise latents, operating in a two-phase generation: M samples are first generated and verified, then N−M samples are regenerated with a revised prompt and the top-k noise seeds from the first phase. The EFC verifier decomposes each prompt into atomic semantic elements (object presence, spatial arrangement, motion, temporal ordering, etc.), classifies them as core or extra, and outputs per-element entailment/contradiction/neutral labels. Final outputs include the best-aligned generated image or video, the revised prompt, and interpretable element-level verification results.

## Method

PRIS (Prompt Redesign for Inference-time Scaling) is a training-free framework that revises prompts during inference based on aggregated failure patterns across generated samples. Its verifier, EFC (Element-level Factual Correction), uses an off-the-shelf MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to decompose prompts into atomic semantic elements, then verifies each element via a captioning → NLI → follow-up NLI pipeline rather than direct visual QA, mitigating the affirmative bias of MLLM-based VQA. PRIS selects top-k samples, identifies common failures (elements with <50% success rate across those samples), and revises the prompt to explicitly reinforce under-addressed elements while preserving well-handled ones. Regeneration reuses the noise latents of top-k samples to preserve favorable noise conditions, and the generation–verification–revision loop can be iterated, though a single revision already yields substantial gains (7% on GenAI-Bench for T2I, 15% on VBench 2.0 for T2V).

## Results

On GenAI-Bench (T2I, FLUX.1-dev, N=20, 2000 NFEs), PRIS achieves a VQA-Score of 0.854 (given) and DA-Score of 0.707 (unseen), outperforming BoN (0.783 / 0.682) by +7% and +3.8% respectively while maintaining comparable aesthetic quality (5.765 vs 5.761). On VBench 2.0 (T2V, Wan2.1-14B, N=10, 1000 NFEs), PRIS achieves an average Controllability & Creativity score of 68.85, a +15.19% gain over the base model (53.66) and +10.34% over BoN (58.51). The EFC verifier achieves 76.3% accuracy in selecting ground-truth visuals from a pool of aligned and misaligned candidates, surpassing the strongest learned reward model (VideoAlign, 69.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIS consistently outperforms BoN (Ma et al., CVPR 2025) across all T2I and T2V metrics: on GenAI-Bench, PRIS gains +7.1% VQA-Score (0.854 vs 0.783) and +2.5% DA-Score (0.707 vs 0.682); on VBench 2.0, the Controllability & Creativity average improves by +13.88% (small model) and +15.19% (large model) over BoN. When integrated with visual scaling methods DAS (Kim et al., ICLR 2025) and RBF (Kim et al., NeurIPS 2025), PRIS further improves both alignment and aesthetic quality (e.g., RBF w/ PRIS: VQA 0.936 vs 0.922, Aesthetic 5.528 vs 5.426), whereas RBF alone sacrifices aesthetics. For verifier accuracy, EFC (76.3%) substantially outperforms all learned reward models: VideoAlign (Liu et al., 2025) at 69.3%, VisionReward (Xu et al., 2024) at 57.1%, and UnifiedReward (Wang et al., 2025) at 49.8%, confirming that fine-grained element-level factual correction is more reliable than scalar reward scoring for inference-time sample selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inference-time scaling framework for diffusion models (Ma et al., CVPR 2025) by treating the text prompt as a co-scaled variable rather than a fixed conditioning signal. It departs from prior prompt-refinement approaches (Hao et al., Datta et al.) that operate per-sample by instead aggregating failure patterns across a population of generated visuals to identify systematic, recurring misalignments. The EFC verifier builds on MLLM-based text-visual assessment but introduces a novel NLI-based factual correction step that avoids the affirmative bias of direct visual QA, positioning it between learned reward models and holistic VQA scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that visual-only scaling plateaus in prompt adherence while joint prompt-visual scaling sustains improvements provides a clear, actionable principle for practitioners: allocating compute to verifier-guided prompt revision is more effective than brute-force sample generation. The 15% gain on VBench 2.0 and the training-free, plug-and-play nature of PRIS make it immediately applicable to existing T2I/T2V pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to much larger or unified models, how it interacts with reward-model-based fine-tuning, and whether the MLLM verifier's captioning quality becomes a bottleneck for increasingly complex prompts.
