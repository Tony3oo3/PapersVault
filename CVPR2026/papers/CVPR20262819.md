## Problem

Existing deep learning-based point cloud geometry compression methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: their context models are tailored to specific point cloud densities and fail to maintain stable performance across the full spectrum from sparse LiDAR scans to dense reconstruction outputs, and they experience sharp performance collapse on out-of-distribution (OOD) data such as 3D Gaussian Splats, VGGT reconstructions, or noisy/deformed point clouds. Even recent universal models like Unicorn-U, despite being trained for versatility, rely on non-unified architectures and still degrade under extreme OOD conditions, leaving a critical gap between benchmark performance and real-world deployability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary 3D point cloud (LiDAR, dense human body, indoor scenes, 3DGS coordinates, or reconstructed geometry) and converts it into a multi-scale octree occupancy code representation. The compressed bitstream consists of two components: a small model component (quantized prediction-head weights encoded via DeepCABAC) and a geometry component (arithmetic-coded occupancy codes guided by the specialized UCM). Decoding is symmetric—the decoder first reconstructs the instance-specific weights, then entropy-decodes the geometry—yielding a lossless or lossy reconstructed point cloud depending on the operating mode.

## Method

AnyPcc introduces a Universal Context Model (UCM) that performs hierarchical coarse-to-fine prediction of multi-scale occupancy codes, where at each scale the context is factorized along two complementary dimensions: a 3D checkerboard spatial partitioning that captures coarse-grained structural priors from decoded neighbors, and a channel-wise partitioning that splits each 8-bit occupancy code into two 4-bit sub-symbols for fine-grained sequential prediction. This spatio-channel factorization is the first to jointly model both granularities, making the context robust across the entire density spectrum. To handle OOD data, the Instance-Adaptive Fine-Tuning (IAFT) module freezes the large backbone (feature extractors and spatial convolutions) and rapidly fine-tunes only the lightweight prediction heads per instance via a few hundred gradient steps on cached features, transmitting the small weight delta in the bitstream. The minimal bitrate overhead of these weights is substantially outweighed by the entropy-coding gains, and the framework extends seamlessly to lossy compression via a probability-thresholding mechanism that transmits only the top-k most probable occupied locations per scale.

## Results

On the 15-dataset AnyPcc Benchmark, AnyPcc (Ours) achieves a Compression Ratio Gain of −11.93% over the G-PCC v23 anchor, while the single unified model (Ours-U) achieves −10.75%, both substantially outperforming all learning-based baselines. AnyPcc attains state-of-the-art bpp on 13 of 15 datasets; the universal model (Ours-U) further demonstrates superior generalization on all five OOD datasets (VGGT, S3DIS, RS, NS, CS). Encoding time is 2.84 s (adjustable to 0.44 s) and decoding is 0.46 s, with Ours-U using only 9.77 M parameters in a single set of weights versus 68.39 M across seven category-specific models for Ours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyPcc (Ours) achieves the lowest bpp on 13 of 15 datasets, with the most dramatic margins on dense human-body data (Thuman: 1.58 vs. 1.64 for RENO) and LiDAR (KITTI: 6.18 vs. 6.50 for Unicorn). The only dataset where AnyPcc loses is GS (3D Gaussian Splatting), where TopNet (10.95 bpp) and OctAttention (11.31 bpp) outperform Ours (11.65) and Ours-U (11.74). Among baselines, TopNet is the only method that beats the G-PCC v23 anchor overall (−4.04% CR-Gain), while RENO (+2.96%), SparsePCGC (+2.07%), and OctAttention (+1.32%) all perform worse than the traditional codec. On the five OOD datasets, the unified model Ours-U consistently outperforms all baselines, achieving the best bpp on VGGT (7.06 vs. 7.33 for G-PCC), RS (3.50 vs. 3.72), NS (4.67 vs. 4.85), and CS (3.08 vs. 3.21), while on S3DIS it edges out TopNet (10.79 vs. 10.84). In terms of efficiency, AnyPcc decodes in 0.46 s (comparable to RENO's 0.23 s) versus 1324 s for OctAttention and 1740 s for TopNet, and Ours-U requires only 9.77 M parameters in a single model versus 23–30 M for competing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnyPcc unifies two previously separate context-modeling paradigms in point cloud compression: the spatial (voxel-scale) priors exploited by Unicorn [52] and the channel-wise (occupancy-code-scale) priors exploited by RENO [66]. The authors argue these are inter-convertible representations of the same local geometry, and their Universal Context Model is the first to jointly integrate both via a spatio-channel factorization over multi-scale occupancy codes. The Instance-Adaptive Fine-Tuning (IAFT) module additionally imports parameter-efficient fine-tuning ideas from image/video compression [34, 39, 48] and implicit neural representations [6, 11, 23], applying them to point cloud geometry compression for the first time to bridge the gap between a fixed pre-trained model and per-instance INR training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single set of weights can compress 15 diverse point cloud types (LiDAR, dense human bodies, 3D Gaussian Splats, VGGT reconstructions, and synthetic corruption cases) at or near SOTA quality, AnyPcc addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: the need to maintain and train separate models per data category. The 15-dataset benchmark, including deliberately OOD and corrupted sets, provides a more realistic stress test than prior evaluations. Open questions include whether the IAFT overhead remains negligible at much larger model scales, how the approach extends to attribute (color/normal) compression, and whether the fine-tuning strategy generalizes to other irregular-geometry modalities such as meshes or volumetric data.
