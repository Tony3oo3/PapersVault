## Problem

3D generation lags significantly behind 2D image synthesis in both quality and scalability because it must jointly discover where to place content in space and what to represent there, unlike 2D where the spatial grid is predefined. Existing representations face a trade-off: Sparse Voxel methods provide structure but suffer from prohibitively long sequences (over 20,000 tokens at 64³), while VecSet methods are compact but lack positional guidance during diffusion, making the denoising trajectory harder to constrain. The paper asks what representation design truly enables effective scaling for 3D diffusion generators.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed at 1022 resolution via DINOv2-Giant) and a coarse sparse voxel grid obtained by voxelizing a mesh from any off-the-shelf 3D generator (e.g., Hunyuan3D-2 or Trellis). It outputs a detailed geometry VoxSet — a sequence of latent vectors anchored to voxel centers — which is decoded to a surface mesh via SDF prediction and Marching Cubes at arbitrary resolution. The two-stage design means the voxel grid (structure) is produced first, then the detailed geometry latents are generated within that fixed spatial scaffold.

## Method

LATTICE introduces VoxSet, a semi-structured latent representation that replaces VecSet's surface-sampled point queries with voxel queries anchored at the centers of active voxels intersecting the object surface, making positional information available at test time. A VAE encodes point clouds into these voxel-anchored latents via cross-attention, with query jitter during training to support arbitrary decoding resolutions. The generator is a rectified-flow DiT that conditions on DINOv2 image features and injects RoPE positional embeddings from the voxel grid coordinates into each noisy latent token, providing strong spatial guidance during denoising. Training uses a progressive strategy (1024 → 6144 tokens) and random subsampling of structure tokens to reduce cost, while test-time scaling allows increasing token count up to 30,720 for finer detail. The full pipeline is a two-stage coarse-to-fine system: an off-the-shelf model produces the sparse voxel scaffold, and the DiT generates detailed geometry VoxSets within it.

## Results

On the image-to-geometry generation task (Table 2), LATTICE-1.9B achieves the best ULIP-T score (0.078) and Uni-T score (0.254), while tying with Hunyuan3D 2.0 on ULIP-I (0.130) and Uni-I (0.315). On geometry reconstruction (Table 1), LATTICE at 8192 tokens achieves CD 2.909 and F1 98.53, outperforming SparseFlex at 1024 resolution (CD 2.972, F1 97.76) and Direct3D-s2 (CD 4.987, F1 97.46), and at 20480 tokens reaches CD 1.893 and F1 99.59 with a far more compact latent representation than voxel-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LATTICE-1.9B achieves the best ULIP-T (0.078) and Uni-T (0.254) scores, narrowly beating Hunyuan3D 2.0 (0.077, 0.251) and Trellis (0.076, 0.249), while tying with Hunyuan3D 2.0 on ULIP-I (0.130) and Uni-I (0.315). The margin over the strongest VecSet-based competitor (Hunyuan3D 2.0) is small on image-mesh similarity but consistent across all four metrics. On reconstruction, LATTICE at 8192 tokens (CD 2.909, F1 98.53) surpasses SparseFlex at 1024 resolution (CD 2.972, F1 97.76) and Direct3D-s2 (CD 4.987, F1 97.46) despite using a much more compact latent (64×8192 vs. 8×196028 and 64×46592). At 20480 tokens, LATTICE extends its lead to CD 1.893 and F1 99.59, a gap no voxel-based method matches. The user study (Fig. 11) further confirms LATTICE's superiority over four commercial closed-source models across Overall, Subject, and Scene preference categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

LATTICE sits at the intersection of two dominant 3D latent representation paradigms: VecSet-based methods (3DShape2VecSet, Hunyuan3D-2) that compress shapes into compact unstructured token sets, and Sparse Voxel methods (XCube, Trellis) that anchor features to structured grids. The paper's key departure is introducing VoxSet, a semi-structured representation that replaces surface-sampled point queries with voxel-center queries, thereby making the latent space position-aware at test time. Conceptually, it challenges the long-standing assumption that sparse voxel locality is what preserves fine geometric detail, arguing instead that 'localizability'—having position information available during generation—is the critical property, and that this can be injected into an otherwise unstructured VecSet via RoPE positional embeddings on a rectified-flow DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The localizability principle offers a unifying lens for 3D representation design that could inform future work beyond this specific architecture, potentially simplifying the choice between VecSet and voxel-based approaches. The demonstrated test-time scaling (training at 6,144 tokens, evaluating at up to 30,720 with consistent gains) and low-cost training (2B model in under 24 hours on 64 GPUs) are practically significant for democratizing high-fidelity 3D generation. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline (coarse geometry → detailed VoxSet) can be unified into a single model, whether the approach extends naturally to textured or multi-material 3D assets, and whether the reliance on an off-the-shelf coarse geometry generator as a conditioning stage limits end-to-end controllability.
