## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation methods rely on 2D image features or scene-specific 3D reconstructions that fail to produce unified, 3D-consistent representations across diverse viewpoints and sensor configurations. While multi-view diffusion models have recently achieved strong 3D-aware generative synthesis, their potential as a representation-learning backbone for viewpoint-robust visuomotor control remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263659/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At deployment the policy consumes a single-view RGB(-D) image (512×512, two frames) plus a natural-language instruction, and outputs a 10-step action chunk comprising end-effector position and gripper state. During pretraining, the model ingests multi-view RGB-D pairs (N=1–3 source views, M target views) with calibrated camera intrinsics and extrinsics; for RGB-only setups, metric depth is estimated via MapAnything. A key design choice is that although multi-view data drives pretraining, inference requires only one source view, enabling flexible view deployment at test time.

## Method

DiffuView is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes the MVGenMaster multi-view diffusion model on robot-centric data (RH20T + 5,000+ synthetic viewpoints from RoboSuite/CoppeliaSim) to learn conditional target-view generation p(Ô_j | O_i, P_i, P_j), injecting per-pixel Plücker ray embeddings and warped RGB-D pairs as explicit geometric conditioning into VAE latents via a lightweight CNN; the UNet uses 2D spatial attention within each view and 3D full attention across views, with stochastic source-view counts and no temporal positional encoding to support arbitrary view numbers at inference. Stage 2 repurposes the pretrained UNet as a frozen vision encoder (single forward pass, not a denoiser), extracting multi-scale features from upsampling layers that feed a FiLM-conditioned Q-Former (modulated by CLIP end-of-text tokens) to produce a compact observation embedding. This embedding conditions a diffusion policy transformer that generates actions via 10-step DDIM denoising, employing causal self-attention over action tokens for temporal consistency, cross-attention for observation and language integration, and a noise-level-conditioned Mixture-of-Experts router (4 experts, Top-2) to accelerate inference while preserving denoising quality.

## Results

On the Libero benchmark, DiffuView achieves 89.2% success rate on Libero-10 and 92.5% on Libero-90, yielding a 92.2% average across 100 tasks, outperforming the strongest VLA baseline π0.5-ki (91.9%) and substantially exceeding diffusion-based policies like Dita (63.8%) and DP (67.2%). On MetaWorld, DiffuView reaches 0.706 average success rate across 50 tasks, surpassing VPP (0.682) and GR-1 (0.577). On the proposed Mv-Bench for viewpoint generalization, DiffuView achieves 59.2% average success rate under camera rotations from -30° to 60°, nearly 20 percentage points above OpenVLA (39.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffuView outperforms all diffusion-based policy baselines (DP, Dita, MDT) by a wide margin on Libero-10 (89.2% vs. 51.0–65.0%) and MetaWorld (0.706 vs. 0.282–0.413 average). Against the strongest VLA baseline π0.5-ki, DiffuView leads on Libero-10 by 4.4 points (89.2 vs. 84.8) and matches it on Libero-90 (92.5 vs. 92.7), while achieving a slightly higher 100-task average (92.2 vs. 91.9). On MetaWorld, DiffuView surpasses the next-best method VPP by 2.4 points on average (0.706 vs. 0.682) and leads on every difficulty tier, with the largest gap on medium tasks (0.542 vs. 0.493). The most striking comparison is on Mv-Bench, where DiffuView's multi-view pretrained backbone yields a 59.2% average success rate under viewpoint shifts, nearly doubling OpenVLA's 39.3% and maintaining above 50% success even at ±30° camera rotations where OpenVLA drops below 27%. On real-world tasks, DiffuView achieves 0.65 average success rate versus 0.63 for OpenVLA and 0.51 for DP, with the largest gain on the 'Wipe table' task (0.68 vs. 0.49).

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffuView repurposes a multi-view diffusion model (MVGenMaster) originally designed for novel view synthesis as a 3D-consistent visual representation learner for robotic manipulation, departing from prior 3D-aware pretraining that relied on masked autoencoding (MVP, 3D-MVP, LIFT3D) or explicit neural scene representations (SPA, PDFactor). The policy stage follows the diffusion policy paradigm with MoE denoisers and causal action attention, while the use of a pretrained generative model as a single-pass feature encoder (rather than a denoiser) mirrors the VPP approach. The key conceptual departure is treating cross-view generation as a pretraining objective that implicitly enforces geometric consistency, rather than requiring scene-specific optimization or explicit 3D latent structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~20% success-rate gain under viewpoint shifts addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: most manipulation policies assume fixed camera configurations, limiting real-world transfer. The flexible view deployment (train with N source views, infer with a single view) and the demonstrated generalization to untrained wrist views suggest a path toward camera-agnostic manipulation systems. However, the framework currently lacks temporal/dynamic reasoning, and the reliance on metric depth estimation (MapAnything) for RGB-only setups introduces an additional failure mode. Open questions include whether this pretraining paradigm scales to long-horizon, multi-robot, or highly dynamic scenes where static geometric consistency is insufficient.
