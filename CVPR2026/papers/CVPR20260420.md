## Problem

Existing prompt-learning methods for biomedical VLMs either overfit to seen classes (degrading unseen-class accuracy below zero-shot baselines) or sacrifice seen-class performance, revealing a fundamental trade-off. The root cause is twofold: static prompts cannot align with the visual features of unseen classes, and even dynamic image-conditional prompts remain susceptible to shortcut learning by correlating with non-diagnostic features (imaging artifacts, device markers) that are discriminative only among seen classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes biomedical images across 9 modalities (CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, OCT, fundus, dermoscopy, endoscopy, histopathology) processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 encoder into 196 patch tokens, paired with learnable text context vectors prepended to class-name embeddings. At inference, only the causal pathway's prompt features are used to compute a softmax class probability distribution via cosine similarity with image features, scaled by a temperature parameter. The model is trained with batch size 1 and SGD at lr=0.0025 for 50 epochs, requiring no external knowledge or domain-specific priors.

## Method

BiomedCCPL combines two synergistic modules on a frozen BiomedCLIP backbone. The VGAP module generates image-conditional prompts by reducing M patch tokens at three encoder depths (layers 3, 7, 11) into N=14 adaptive visual prototypes via a lightweight contribution matrix, then fusing them into text [CLS] tokens through cross-attention with momentum-based integration, capturing multi-scale semantics from coarse anatomy to fine-grained pathology. The SCD module implements front-door adjustment by splitting each class prompt into causal and non-causal pathways, regularized by three synergistic objectives: cross-entropy on the causal pathway for classification, KL-divergence-based entropy maximization driving the non-causal pathway toward uniform predictions, and an intra-class orthogonality constraint separating the two subspaces. A semantic anchor regularization loss stabilizes training by anchoring causal prompt features to hand-crafted template embeddings. At test time, predictions use only the causal pathway, discarding the non-causal one.

## Results

On the Base-to-Novel generalization task (16-shot), BiomedCCPL achieves an average Harmonic Mean (HM) of 79.98% across 10 biomedical datasets, surpassing the next-best method (MMA) by 6.45%. On the few-shot task, BiomedCCPL reaches 82.25% average accuracy at 16 shots and 62.17% at 1 shot, outperforming all baselines at every shot setting. Notably, BiomedCCPL increases novel-class accuracy by 8.74% over BiomedCoOp while also achieving the best base-class accuracy, demonstrating balanced performance across seen and unseen categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiomedCCPL outperforms all 8 baselines on the average Base-to-Novel HM (79.98% vs. 73.53% for MMA, the closest competitor). Against the most relevant image-conditional baseline CoCoOp, BiomedCCPL gains substantial HM improvements on datasets where global features are insufficient (e.g., OCTMNIST: 78.41% vs. 61.77%, DermaMNIST: 59.68% vs. 50.24%), confirming the benefit of multi-scale adaptive prototypes over single global tokens. Compared to the domain-specific BiomedCoOp, BiomedCCPL achieves higher novel-class accuracy by 8.74% on average while also matching or exceeding its base-class performance, indicating that causal disentanglement captures transferable diagnostic features rather than dataset-specific shortcuts. Static-prompt methods (CoOp, KgCoOp, ProGrad) suffer severe novel-class degradation (e.g., CoOp drops to 35.11% on CHMNIST novel), whereas BiomedCCPL maintains 44.95% on the same dataset. MMA, the strongest adapter-based baseline, leads on BTMRI (90.95% vs. 90.89%) and RETINA (83.69% vs. 88.49% — actually BiomedCCPL wins here), but is outperformed on 8 of 10 datasets by BiomedCCPL. In the few-shot setting, BiomedCCPL's 1-shot accuracy (62.17%) exceeds BiomedCoOp's 2-shot (58.55%), and its 8-shot result (77.22%) surpasses MMA's 16-shot (75.24%), highlighting superior data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiomedCCPL extends the prompt-learning lineage for VLMs (CoOp → CoCoOp → BiomedCoOp) by replacing static or global-feature-conditional prompts with multi-scale adaptive visual prototypes (VGAP) that ground text prompts in hierarchical diagnostic features. Its causal disentanglement module (SCD) draws on the front-door adjustment framework from CDC [41] but departs from CDC's augmentation-based global features, instead using multi-scale local prototypes and a dual-pathway architecture with synergistic orthogonality and entropy-maximization objectives. The work also positions itself against adapter-based methods like MMA, arguing that prompt-level causal regularization is more parameter-efficient and better suited to the data-scarce biomedical regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.45% HM gain over the previous SOTA in Base-to-Novel generalization, combined with strong few-shot data efficiency (1-shot outperforming 2-shot baselines), makes this a practically relevant tool for clinical settings where annotated data is scarce and models must handle unseen pathology variations. The causal SCM analysis provides a clear theoretical diagnosis of why static prompts fail on unseen classes (spurious backdoor paths through imaging artifacts), which could inform future prompt-learning designs beyond the biomedical domain. Open questions include whether the multi-scale prototype mechanism and causal disentanglement transfer to natural-image VLMs, whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT-L/H), and whether the batch-size-1 training protocol limits the method's applicability to very large datasets.
