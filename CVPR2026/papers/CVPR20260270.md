## Problem

Visual Speaker Authentication (VSA) systems are increasingly vulnerable to personalized talking face generation (TFG) forgeries that closely mimic a target user's lip dynamics, yet prevailing detectors rely on supervised classifiers trained on known forgery priors. These priors-based approaches overfit to seen attack types, fail catastrophically on unseen personalized TFGs that cluster near real samples in feature space, and collapse entirely when effective forgery data is unavailable. The paper asks whether a detector can be built exclusively from authentic data by learning a compact, well-structured inlier representation that treats any deviation as an impostor.

## Input / Output

The method consumes mouth-region video clips extracted via a standard preprocessing pipeline (following AV-HuBERT [47]); the AV variant additionally concatenates same-dimension audio features. Training requires only real lip clips from the target user (500 clips per speaker in experiments) plus optional clips from other enrolled users as human-impostor outliers—no forgery samples are needed. At inference, a density-based anomaly detector (LOF) scores each sample's deviation from the learned inlier distribution, yielding a binary authentication decision (real vs. forgery/impostor) with a threshold calibrated at 1% false-negative rate on the training set.

## Method

The framework learns a forgery-aware latent space through three components operating on a frozen visual encoder with only a lightweight two-layer adapter trained end-to-end. First, two lightweight synthesis modules—Self-Mixed Lip (SML) and Cross-Mixed Lip (CML)—generate pseudo-fake clips by applying chained augmentations to individual frames or blending frames across different inlier samples, emulating high-frequency (texture inconsistency, edge distortion) and low-frequency (over-smoothed transitions, muted motion) TFG artifacts without any deep generator. Second, an asymmetric supervised contrastive loss pulls all inlier samples together while repelling them from pseudo-fakes and human impostors, but critically excludes outlier-outlier pairs from the loss so that diverse forgeries are never forced into a coherent cluster. Third, a Gaussian Distribution Matching (GDM) regularizer, derived from the Donsker-Varadhan variational bound on mutual information between a representation and its noise-perturbed copy, shapes the inlier feature distribution toward a standard isotropic Gaussian via an InfoNCE-style objective, making the tight inlier boundary analytically tractable for downstream density-based anomaly detection.

## Results

On TFG-GRID, the proposed method achieves an average AUC of 99.83% and HTER of 2.13% across eight forgery types, outperforming the best baseline (OpenSet, 95.26% AUC / 11.20% HTER) by over 4.5 AUC points and reducing HTER by ~8 points. On TFG-Lombard, the method reaches 99.91% AUC / 1.86% HTER versus OpenSet's 95.76% / 11.19%. On the real-world AVLips benchmark, the visual-only variant achieves 96.97% AUC / 8.86% HTER, while the audio-visual variant reaches 99.84% AUC / 2.02% HTER, surpassing the best visual baseline (OpenSet, 93.62% / 28.76%) by more than 10 HTER points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all ten baselines on every forgery type and both primary datasets. The closest competitor, OpenSet [11], achieves 95.26% AUC / 11.20% HTER on TFG-GRID but still trails the proposed method by 4.57 AUC points and 9.07 HTER points on average; its weakness is most pronounced on personalized TFGs (e.g., 75.34% AUC on PersonaTalk vs. 99.92%). DO2HSC [66] is competitive on ER-NeRF and MimicTalk (99.80% and 99.76% AUC) but degrades sharply on HM (82.96%) and TalkLip (88.55%), yielding a lower average. VSA-targeted detectors (SA-DTH [61], CIDE [14], Siamese [24], TD-VSA [18]) all collapse on personalized TFGs—SA-DTH drops to 48–59% AUC on PersonaTalk and ER-NeRF—because they rely on low-level lip cues that high-fidelity generators replicate. Audio-visual methods (SpchForen [30], AVH-align [49]) struggle with SimSwap (where real audio is preserved) and show inconsistent performance across forgery types, averaging 76.81% and 91.58% AUC respectively on TFG-GRID. On the real-world AVLips benchmark, the gap widens further: the visual-only variant (96.97% AUC) surpasses the best visual baseline OpenSet (93.62%) by 3.35 AUC points, and the audio-visual variant (99.84% AUC / 2.02% HTER) exceeds all baselines by over 20 HTER points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VSA forgery detection—supervised classifiers trained on real/fake pairs via empirical risk minimization (LipForensics, SA-DTH, CIDE, etc.)—by reframing the problem as prior-free anomaly detection over a compactly modeled inlier distribution. Methodologically, it builds on SupCon (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) by restricting the positive set to inliers only and excluding outlier-outlier pairs, and on the InfoNCE/Donsker-Varadhan framework (van den Oord et al., 2018) to derive a Gaussian distribution matching regularizer. The lightweight adapter tuning strategy is borrowed from the LLM parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature and repurposed for rapid per-user enrollment in VSA, while the pseudo-fake generation (SML/CML) draws on virtual outlier ideas from one-class classification (Tack et al., NeurIPS 2020; Ding et al., CVPR 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prior-free formulation removes the practical bottleneck of continuously curating and updating forgery training data as new TFG methods emerge, making deployment significantly more sustainable. The TFG-Suite benchmark, covering eight forgery types including three personalized NeRF-based generators, fills a notable gap in the evaluation landscape where prior datasets focused on face-swapping or zero-shot lip-sync. Open questions remain: whether the heuristic pseudo-fake generation (frame mixing) generalizes to future, fundamentally different generation paradigms (e.g., video diffusion models), and whether the Gaussian inlier assumption holds under extreme distributional shifts such as multi-identity or cross-domain attacks. The method's reliance on a density-based detector (LOF) at inference also raises questions about scalability to very large enrollment sets.
