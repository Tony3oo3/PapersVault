## Problem

Existing 3D MLLMs rely on pre-trained 3D encoders to extract geometric features, which introduces semantic misalignment between geometric and linguistic spaces, brittleness to variable input resolutions (fixed 8K-point assumption), and substantial computational overhead before the LLM can begin generation. This paper asks whether an end-to-end architecture can directly process raw point clouds without any pre-trained 3D encoder while matching or exceeding encoder-based performance.

## Input / Output

The model accepts raw point clouds of variable resolution (2K, 4K, or 8K points, with optional RGB channels giving D=6 per point) paired with a natural language question or instruction, and produces free-form textual responses including detailed captions, VQA answers, and open-vocabulary classification labels. Unlike encoder-based baselines that require upsampling or downsampling to a fixed 8K-point input, SAGE natively handles variable-density point clouds, with inference throughput improving at lower resolutions as the number of 3D tokens decreases.

## Method

SAGE introduces a lightweight 3D tokenizer that converts raw point clouds into discrete tokens via farthest point sampling (512 points), KNN neighbourhood grouping (81 neighbours), local geometry aggregation with relative positional embeddings, and vector quantization against a learnable codebook of size 8192—effectively extending the LLM's vocabulary with 3D tokens. The model is trained in three stages: (1) tokenizer warm-up with next-token prediction on captioning data, (2) end-to-end instruction tuning on 730K point-text pairs, and (3) a novel GRPO-based preference optimization stage that uses a composite reward combining Sentence-BERT cosine similarity to a reference answer with a Gaussian length penalty, enabling reinforcement learning on open-ended descriptive 3D tasks where answers are not objectively verifiable. The LLM backbone (Vicuna-7B/13B) processes the quantized 3D tokens interleaved with text tokens in a standard autoregressive decoder, with the entire pipeline optimized end-to-end.

## Results

On the Objaverse 3D captioning benchmark, SAGE-13B achieves a GPT-4 evaluation score of 52.87, surpassing ShapeLLM-13B (48.94) by +3.93 and PointLLM-13B (48.15) by +4.72. For 3D object classification, SAGE-13B scores 58.48 (GPT-4), outperforming the best baseline ShapeLLM-13B (54.00) by +4.48. On MM-Vet 3D VQA, SAGE-13B reaches 54.89, a +1.79 gain over ShapeLLM-13B (53.10). Inference latency drops from 239 ms (PointLLM-7B) to 100 ms (SAGE-7B), a 2.3× speedup, with throughput improving from 4.2 to 10.0 samples/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all four encoder-based baselines across all three tasks at both 7B and 13B scales. Against PointLLM (the closest 3D-specific baseline), SAGE-7B gains +6.13 on captioning GPT-4, +4.11 on classification, and +8.33 on VQA; SAGE-13B gains +4.72, +4.48, and +8.29 respectively. Against ShapeLLM, SAGE-7B leads by +4.06 (captioning), +2.61 (classification), and +2.13 (VQA), while SAGE-13B leads by +3.93, +4.48, and +1.79. Notably, even SAGE* (without preference optimization) already matches or exceeds PointLLM and ShapeLLM on captioning and classification, confirming that the encoder-free tokenizer alone closes the gap. The only marginal exception is SAGE-13B* on captioning GPT-4 (48.54 vs. ShapeLLM-13B's 48.94), a 0.40-point deficit that the preference optimization stage fully reverses. Inference efficiency further differentiates SAGE: 100 ms latency and 10.0 samples/s throughput versus PointLLM's 239 ms and 4.2 samples/s on the same H100 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE departs from the dominant encoder-based paradigm in 3D MLLMs (PointLLM, ShapeLLM, LLaVA-3D, Robin3D) by eliminating the pre-trained 3D encoder entirely, instead using a lightweight tokenizer (FPS + KNN aggregation + vector quantization) to project raw point clouds directly into the LLM's token space. Conceptually, it draws on encoder-free 2D vision-language work (Fuyu-8B, EVE, SHOW-O) but argues that patch-based tokenization fails for 3D due to the absence of regular grid topology, necessitating explicit geometric sampling and neighbourhood structure. The preference optimization stage adapts GRPO from verifiable domains (mathematical reasoning) to open-ended 3D QA via a novel semantic-alignment reward, filling a gap where standard RL methods cannot evaluate descriptive answers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a small, jointly-trained 3D tokenizer can match or exceed large pre-trained encoders while delivering 2.3× inference speedup and natural robustness to variable point-cloud density, SAGE challenges the assumption that 3D MLLMs require expensive encoder pre-training—a significant practical advantage for domains with limited in-distribution data. The semantic-alignment GRPO reward is a transferable technique for any open-ended multimodal task where answers are descriptive rather than verifiable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D scenes (beyond single objects), scales to larger LLM backbones or different architectures, and whether the VQ codebook and sampling hyperparameters remain effective at much higher resolutions or for dynamic/sequential 3D inputs.
