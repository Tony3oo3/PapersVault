## Problem

Preference-based finetuning of vision-language models is notoriously unstable because trivially wrong negatives inject uninformative gradients that distort optimization and degrade calibration. The paper identifies this as the 'squeezing effect'—a decoupling where easy negatives retain large, misaligned gradients despite negligible loss—caused by prior methods treating alignment as a static optimization task while ignoring the dynamic evolution of the model's learning trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images paired with text prompts using the Qwen2.5-VL-72B backbone, producing free-form text captions or task-specific text responses. Preference pairs for Stage 2 are constructed by synthesizing minimally perturbed negative alternatives (y_l) for each winning caption (y_w) via GPT-4o, with negatives primarily on-policy and optional dataset-negative mixing. Training splits the data 75/25 between the constrained SFT stage and the preference alignment stage.

## Method

CW-DPO is a two-stage framework grounded in a per-step influence decomposition (Proposition 3.1) that isolates the squeezing effect to the oversized loser component of the DPO loss residual. Stage 1 (Constrained SFT) minimizes NLL on positives while imposing a ReLU-based soft penalty that keeps the expected NLL on negatives above a threshold C, flattening the loss landscape and de-peaking the distribution. Stage 2 introduces a competence-aware cooling weight w_c = σ((ℓ̄_θ(y_l|χ) − ℓ_floor)/τ) that asymmetrically scales only the loser's log-probability difference Δ_l, nullifying gradients for confidently rejected easy negatives (w_c→0) while preserving learning signals for hard, uncertain negatives (w_c→1). The resulting loss L_CW-DPO = −log σ(β(Δ_w − w_c·Δ_l)) surgically tempers the destabilizing residual without heuristically regularizing the entire objective, enabling smoother convergence and better calibration.

## Results

On COCO Test, CW-DPO achieves a CIDEr of 142.6 and BLEU-4 of 39.6, surpassing the strongest RLHF baseline PPO by +3.4 CIDEr and the best DPO variant OPA-DPO by +4.1 CIDEr. On Flickr30k, CW-DPO leads with CIDEr 89.2 (+2.5 over OPA-DPO), and on NoCaps it scores 123.6 on the Entire split. On multi-task benchmarks, CW-DPO attains 74.6% on MMMU (+1.5 over OPA-DPO) and 89.6% on MMBench1.1 (+2.4 over OPA-DPO), demonstrating gains beyond captioning into broader multimodal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CW-DPO outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest DPO variant OPA-DPO, CW-DPO gains +4.1 CIDEr on COCO, +2.5 CIDEr on Flickr30k, +2.3 on NoCaps Entire, +1.5% on MMMU, and +2.4% on MMBench. Compared to the RLHF baseline PPO, the advantage is +3.4 CIDEr on COCO, though PPO actually edges out CW-DPO on Flickr30k CIDEr (82.1 vs. 89.2 is CW-DPO winning; PPO is 82.1). Notably, vanilla DPO underperforms even plain SFT on COCO BLEU-4 (33.5 vs. 35.2), confirming the paper's hypothesis that unweighted preference optimization over easy negatives degrades generation quality. V-DPO and GRPO cluster closely (CIDEr 138.2–138.3 on COCO) but both trail CW-DPO by over 4 points. The largest relative gap appears on MMBench, where CW-DPO's 89.6% exceeds the next-best OPA-DPO (87.2%) by 2.4 points, suggesting the cooling mechanism particularly benefits multi-task generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) and standard SFT (Ouyang et al., 2022) by reframing VLM preference alignment through a learning-dynamics lens, specifically diagnosing the 'squeezing effect' where easy negatives produce disproportionately large, misaligned gradients despite negligible loss. Unlike prior DPO variants (V-DPO, GRPO, OPA-DPO) that modify the loss geometry or data curation, CW-DPO introduces a competence-aware cooling weight that adaptively scales the loser gradient based on real-time per-token log-probability, combined with a constrained SFT pre-stage that flattens the loss landscape before preference optimization. The eNTK-based influence decomposition (Proposition 3.1) provides a principled analytical foundation absent from prior empirical treatments of DPO instability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The squeezing-effect diagnosis and the 'smooth-then-cool' two-stage recipe offer a practical, interpretable framework for stabilizing VLM preference optimization that goes beyond ad-hoc regularization. However, the framework currently requires reliable paired preference data and introduces hyperparameters (τ, ℓ_floor) that may need per-dataset tuning, and the analysis is validated primarily on captioning-style tasks with a single 72B backbone. Open questions include extending to unsupervised or noisy preference settings, interactive/long-horizon multimodal reasoning (video QA, embodied agents), and whether the cooling schedule can be automatically adapted via meta-learning rather than manual tuning.
