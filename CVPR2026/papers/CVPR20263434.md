## Problem

Estimating per-primitive importance in 3D Gaussian Splatting is essential for pruning, compression, and transmission, yet existing methods are either rendering-based (view-dependent, linearly scaling with view count, requiring specialized rasterizers) or learning-based (tightly coupled to a specific reconstruction pipeline and invalidated by scene edits). No prior approach provides a fast, rendering-free, plug-and-play importance scorer that generalizes across unseen scenes without per-scene retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263434/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RAP consumes a trained 3DGS primitive set and, for each Gaussian, computes a 15-dimensional feature vector by combining eight raw attributes (average KNN distance, color anisotropy from 60 sampled SH directions, three sorted scales, volume, opacity, and DC color) with both scene-wide z-score and KNN-local z-score normalizations (7 global + 8 local channels, clipped to 1st–99th percentiles and rescaled to [0,1]). The output is a single scalar importance score in [0,1] per primitive, obtained via one forward pass through the MLP at inference—no rendering, no back-propagation, and no scene-specific optimization are required at deployment time.

## Method

RAP extracts a compact 15-dim per-Gaussian feature vector encoding intrinsic attributes (scale, volume, opacity, DC color, KNN distance, color anisotropy) under dual global/local normalization, then feeds it through a lightweight three-layer MLP (32-32-16 hidden units, sigmoid output) to predict an importance score. Training is supervised by three complementary losses: (1) a rendering loss that softly reweights each Gaussian's opacity and scale by its predicted score before differentiable rasterization against ground-truth views, (2) a pruning-aware loss that penalizes deviation of the mean predicted score from a predefined target to prevent trivially high scores, and (3) an entropy-based distribution regularization using a soft Gaussian-kernel histogram to encourage well-spread scores across [0,1]. The network is trained once for 15,000 iterations on 10 scenes from DL3DV-10K and then deployed as a frozen feedforward module, requiring no rendering or per-scene fine-tuning at inference, which enables plug-and-play integration into reconstruction, compression (MPEG GSC), and transmission pipelines.

## Results

RAP achieves the best BD-Rate across all four benchmark datasets, reaching −42.63% on Mip-NeRF360-Outdoor, −33.90% on Mip-NeRF360-Indoor, −36.76% on Deep Blending, and −40.11% on Tanks & Temples (lower is better). At a 60% pruning ratio, RAP delivers up to 0.5 dB PSNR gain over the next-best method. In computation time, RAP ranks second or third fastest (5.72–15.64 s), consistently outpacing all visibility-based baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAP outperforms all five baselines on every dataset in BD-Rate. Against the strongest visibility-based competitor EAGLES, RAP gains 1.35% on Mip-Outdoor (−42.63 vs −41.28) but a much larger 8.92% on Mip-Indoor (−33.90 vs −24.98). Against LightGaussian, the margin ranges from 1.35% (Mip-Outdoor) to 6.04% (Deep Blending). Gradient-based methods show the weakest and least consistent performance: C3DGS drops to −16.28% on Deep Blending, and PUP-3DGS actually degrades below the opacity baseline on Tanks & Temples (+7.06%). In computation time, RAP (5.72–15.64 s) is roughly 2–3× faster than visibility-based methods (13.92–42.96 s) and comparable to or faster than gradient-based methods, confirming the advantage of its rendering-free, feedforward design.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAP builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation [15], operating on its intrinsic primitive attributes (position, scale, opacity, SH coefficients). It departs from the dominant rendering-based importance estimation paradigm (LightGaussian, MesonGS, EAGLES) and gradient-based variants (C3DGS, PUP-3DGS) by showing that a compact 15-dimensional feature vector combining global and local KNN-normalized statistics, processed by a small feedforward MLP, can approximate per-primitive importance without any view-dependent rasterization at inference. The three-loss training objective (rendering fidelity + pruning-aware mean regularization + entropy-based distribution spreading) is novel in its combination, though each component draws on standard ideas from pruning and score calibration literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAP provides a genuinely plug-and-play importance scorer that generalizes across unseen scenes without per-scene retraining or rendering, making it directly deployable in compression (MPEG GSC), transmission, and level-of-detail pipelines where rendering-based methods are impractical. The consistent BD-Rate improvements over all baselines across four benchmark datasets, combined with faster inference than visibility-based methods, suggest it could become a standard preprocessing module in 3DGS toolchains. Open questions remain around region-adaptive or hierarchical pruning, coupling importance scores with non-uniform sampling densities during reconstruction, and whether the fixed global pruning ratio can be replaced by per-region adaptive thresholds for more principled compression.
