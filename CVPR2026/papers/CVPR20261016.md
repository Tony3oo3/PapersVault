## Problem

Discriminative semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods rely on per-pixel classification and large labeled datasets, neglecting feature-level distribution constraints. This limits robust semantic representation learning and the ability to capture global image structures and contextual semantics, particularly in low-label scenarios where overfitting and poor pseudo-label quality are prevalent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261016/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB medical images resized to 224×224 as input, along with ground-truth segmentation masks (available only for labeled data). Ground-truth masks are converted to VAE-compatible format via normalization to [-1,1] before being encoded by the frozen Stable Diffusion VAE. The final segmentation prediction is the average of outputs from two parallel branches (Image and Mask adapters), producing a pixel-wise mask at the original resolution.

## Method

SemiGDA is a generative segmentation framework built on a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE, where a Dual-distribution Alignment Module (DAM) employs two structurally distinct encoders—a frozen VAE encoder and a trainable ResNet—to model image and mask feature distributions, aligning them in latent space via MSE-based distributional constraints. A Consistency-Driven Skip Adapter (CDSA) introduces dual lightweight adapters (Image and Mask) that fuse multi-scale encoder features into the VAE decoder through skip connections, with a Dice-based cross-branch consistency loss enforcing semantic alignment. Training proceeds in two stages: first pretraining the mapping network and trainable encoder to align image features with the mask prior, then fine-tuning the full pipeline. The total loss combines supervised prior-distribution alignment and Dice losses with unsupervised latent-space and cross-branch consistency losses, weighted by a Gaussian warm-up schedule.

## Results

SemiGDA achieves the best Dice score across all four colonoscopy/skin datasets and both pathology/ultrasound datasets at both 10% and 30% labeled ratios. At the most challenging 10% setting, it reaches 84.34% Dice on CVC-300, 79.37% on CVC-ClinicDB, 83.03% on Kvasir, and 86.28% on ISIC-2018, surpassing the strongest baselines (UnCo, CSCPA) by 1.4–6.8 Dice points. On BUSI (10%), it achieves 75.57% Dice, a 10.4-point margin over the next-best method (CSCPA at 65.16%). The 95HD values are also consistently the lowest, e.g., 3.19 on CVC-300 and 4.39 on Kvasir at 10% labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiGDA outperforms all 13 compared baselines on every dataset and labeling ratio. The largest margins appear on CVC-300 (10%), where it leads UnCo (77.56%) by 6.78 Dice points and CSCPA (76.97%) by 7.37 points, and on BUSI (10%), where it exceeds CSCPA (65.16%) by 10.41 Dice points. On Kvasir (30%), it surpasses CSCPA (87.31%) by 1.70 Dice and UnCo (85.04%) by 3.97 Dice. On ISIC-2018 (10%), the margin is tighter—0.53 Dice over CSCPA (85.75%) and 0.69 over UnCo (85.59%)—but 95HD is still lower (4.70 vs. 4.81 and 4.77). The method also consistently achieves the best 95HD across all settings, indicating superior boundary precision. Notably, at extremely low label ratios (1%), SemiGDA maintains a clear advantage over discriminative baselines like MC-Net, underscoring the robustness benefit of the generative dual-distribution alignment approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in semi-supervised medical image segmentation (pseudo-labeling, Mean Teacher, dual-stream consistency) by reframing segmentation as a generative distribution-alignment problem. It repurposes the frozen VAE from Stable Diffusion—a model trained on natural images—as a structural backbone, a choice not previously explored in medical segmentation. Conceptually, it extends the generative segmentation line (GMS, GSS, GMMSeg) by introducing a dual-encoder architecture that aligns image and mask feature distributions in latent space, combined with a consistency-driven skip adapter to bridge the gap between generative decoding and pixel-level segmentation accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a generative, distribution-alignment approach can substantially outperform strong discriminative baselines in extremely low-label regimes (10% and even 1%), suggesting that feature-level distribution constraints may be more informative than per-pixel pseudo-labels when supervision is scarce. However, the reliance on a frozen natural-image VAE raises open questions about domain transfer: whether the SD prior genuinely captures medically relevant structure or merely provides a useful inductive bias, and whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric segmentation or multi-class tasks beyond binary. The two-stage training protocol and the need for the ACR conversion also add practical complexity that may limit adoption.
