## Problem

Existing X-ray contraband segmentation datasets are limited in scale (<50k images), category coverage (<21 classes), and suffer from single-source color distributions that hinder cross-scanner generalization. Additionally, foundation models like SAM fail on X-ray imagery due to the domain gap between natural-image priors and density-dependent absorption imaging, particularly for stacked or occluded objects. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a large-scale multi-source segmentation benchmark and a domain-adapted SAM variant.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a dual-energy X-ray image (standardized to 512×512) and a single user-provided point prompt. Internally, the dual-energy image is decomposed into its high-energy (channel max) and low-energy (channel min) grayscale components, which feed the Energy-Aware Encoder in parallel with the main ViT-L/14 image encoder. The output is a binary instance segmentation mask plus a predicted IoU score; the Adaptive Point Generator internally expands the single user point into two spatially separated prompts before decoding, so the user only ever provides one click.

## Method

APSAM retains SAM's encoder–prompt–decoder architecture (ViT-L/14 backbone) but adds two components. The Energy-Aware Encoder (EAE) is a lightweight 3-block CNN (Conv3×3 → LayerNorm → GELU → 2×2 MaxPool per block) that processes the concatenated high/low-energy channels; its final feature map is flattened, scored by a channel-wise linear head, and the Top-k highest-scoring embeddings are projected into query space to initialize the mask decoder's output tokens, providing contraband-aware spatial priors for occluded objects. The Adaptive Point Generator (APG) takes the user's single point, obtains a coarse SAM mask, stochastically scales its bounding box (U(0.9,1.1)), runs fast K-means (K=2) on foreground pixels, and selects two maximally-separated points as the final prompt pair (falling back to uniform random sampling if clusters are not well-separated). Training uses adapter-based fine-tuning on the ViT-L/14 backbone (11.91M trainable parameters) with AdamW at lr=1e-5, batch size 16, and 12 epochs on the XSeg training set.

## Results

On the XSeg test set, APSAM achieves 72.83% mIoU and 82.31% Dice, outperforming the strongest non-SAM baseline Mask2Former (Swin-L) by 3.24% mIoU and 0.87% Dice while using only 11.91M trainable parameters versus Mask2Former's 144.85M. Compared to the closest SAM-based competitor SAMUS (Finetune), APSAM gains 4.27% mIoU and 3.85% Dice with 31.3M fewer trainable parameters. Cross-domain evaluation on PIDray and PIXray confirms generalization, with APSAM reaching 71.23% mIoU on PIDray and 83.61% mIoU on PIXray, surpassing SAMUS by 4.22% and 2.98% mIoU respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APSAM outperforms all 12 baselines on the XSeg test set. Among classical CNN/Transformer segmenters, Mask2Former (Swin-L) is the strongest at 69.59% mIoU, but APSAM exceeds it by 3.24% mIoU and 0.87% Dice while using roughly 12× fewer trainable parameters (11.91M vs. 144.85M). Among SAM-based methods, frozen SAM performs poorly (53.82% mIoU), confirming the domain gap; fine-tuned SAM improves to 67.87% mIoU, and SAMUS further reaches 68.56%, yet APSAM surpasses both by 4.96% and 4.27% mIoU respectively. On cross-domain evaluation, APSAM maintains its lead: on PIDray it achieves 71.23% mIoU vs. Mask2Former's 69.04% and SAMUS's 67.01%, and on PIXray it reaches 83.61% mIoU vs. SAMUS's 80.63% and SegMAN's 78.37%. The consistent margin across all three datasets (XSeg, PIDray, PIXray) indicates that the Energy-Aware Encoder and Adaptive Point Generator provide genuine domain-specific gains rather than overfitting to a single acquisition protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM prompt-based segmentation paradigm to the X-ray contraband domain, building directly on SAM's image encoder–prompt encoder–mask decoder architecture and on prior X-ray segmentation benchmarks (PIXray, PIDray) that first moved beyond bounding-box detection. It departs from prior SAM adaptations (e.g., SAMUS for ultrasound) by embedding X-ray physics—specifically dual-energy high/low-energy projections—into a lightweight Energy-Aware Encoder that initializes the mask decoder, rather than relying solely on learned feature alignment. The dataset itself consolidates and re-annotates three existing public X-ray collections (114Xray, PIDray, PIXray) with a structured cleaning pipeline and expert re-labeling, addressing the cross-domain chromatic variance and category-coverage gaps that limited earlier benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XSeg provides the first large-scale, multi-source, 30-category pixel-level segmentation benchmark for X-ray security screening, filling a gap that has constrained model generalization across different scanner hardware. The finding that a compact 11.91 M-parameter adapter-based model (APSAM) outperforms a 43.21 M-parameter SAMUS variant suggests that domain-specific inductive biases (energy-aware initialization, adaptive prompting) can substitute for raw model capacity in this setting. Open questions include whether the Energy-Aware Encoder generalizes to single-energy scanners, whether the 30-category taxonomy scales to emerging threat types, and whether the closed-loop annotation strategy can be automated further to reduce the year-long expert labeling effort.
