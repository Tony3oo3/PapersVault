## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting methods rely on sharp images and precise camera pose priors, both of which are unavailable in high-speed scenarios. While spike cameras eliminate the sharpness problem, existing spike-based 3D reconstruction methods still depend on external pose estimation (COLMAP, VGGT) that fails on spike data due to its inherent texture instability and visual deficiency. This paper presents the first framework to perform pose-free 3D reconstruction directly from unposed spike streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260760/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a continuous binary spike stream (S ∈ {0,1}^{H×W×N}) from a bio-inspired spike camera (e.g., 1000×1000 resolution, captured in under one second with fast camera motion) with no camera pose information. It produces a full 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation, an estimated dense camera trajectory, and novel view renderings. The spike stream is consumed in two forms: single-frame binary data (converted to NBDS for supervision) and temporally accumulated sequences for high-frequency detail preservation.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the spike camera model probabilistically: due to random initial pixel voltage, each pixel's spike output follows a Bernoulli distribution, and temporal summation over adjacent pixels yields an approximate binomial distribution (NBDS) whose expectation approximates light intensity with low variance, providing a stable supervisory signal from inherently noisy binary data. The framework uses a progressive optimization pipeline: first initializing a rough 3DGS scene and sparse trajectory via MVS on spike intervals, then iteratively refining poses and scene using a keyframing strategy that partitions frames into pose-error-dominated and scene-error-dominated subsets. Camera pose optimization employs a sub-frame photometric loss (L_sub) that takes temporal differences of rendered and NBDS frames to amplify edge errors while suppressing noise, with Lie-algebra-based minimal Jacobians for efficient gradient computation. Scene optimization combines NBDS loss (short-term detail) with spike accumulation loss (long-term structure) in a composite objective, achieving 3× faster reconstruction than prior spike-based methods while delivering up to 7.4 dB higher PSNR and 40% lower ATE.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples, Nope-SGS achieves 30.18 dB PSNR, 0.911 SSIM, and 0.122 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, surpassing the best baseline (Spikerecon+CF-3DGS at 22.38 dB) by +7.8 dB PSNR. For camera pose estimation, it attains an ATE of 0.003 on Tanks and Temples and 0.030 on Deblur-NeRF, outperforming the best baselines (SpikeGS+VGGT at 0.012 and 0.073 respectively) by 4× and 2.4×. On depth estimation, Nope-SGS reaches δ₁=67.11% and AbsRel=0.31, exceeding the best prior method (SpikeRecon+Instant at δ₁=59.46%, AbsRel=0.61). The method also achieves 3× faster reconstruction than prior spike-based approaches while reporting the lowest NIQE (5.72) and IL-NIQE (44.97) on real-world data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nope-SGS dominates all nine baselines across every metric on both synthetic benchmarks. Against pose-free 3DGS methods (CF-3DGS, Instantsplat, ht3DGS), it gains +7.8 to +10.0 dB PSNR on Tanks and Temples, confirming that pose-free methods designed for sharp images fail catastrophically under high-speed motion. Against spike-based methods with external pose (SpikeGS+VGGT, SpikeGS+Colmap, USP-Gaussian, SpikeNeRF), it improves PSNR by +7.4 to +11.9 dB and reduces ATE by 4× to 133×, demonstrating that even with COLMAP/VGGT poses, spike-based reconstruction suffers from pose errors that compound into geometric artifacts. The strongest baseline, Spikerecon+Instant (spike image reconstruction followed by pose-free 3DGS), achieves the best ATE among non-Nope-SGS methods (0.012 on T&T) but still trails Nope-SGS by 4× in ATE and 7.9 dB in PSNR. On real-world data (Table 2), Nope-SGS reduces NIQE from 9.93 (SpikeGS) to 5.72 and IL-NIQE from 87.92 to 44.97, a near-doubling of perceptual quality. On depth estimation (Table 3), it outperforms the best prior method (SpikeRecon+Instant) by +7.65% in δ₁ and halves AbsRel (0.31 vs. 0.61). The method also runs 3× faster than prior spike-based approaches, making it both the most accurate and the most efficient in its category.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nope-SGS sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: pose-free 3DGS methods (CF-3DGS, Instantsplat, ZeroGS) designed for sharp exposure-camera images under slow motion, and spike-based 3D reconstruction methods (SpikeGS, USP-Gaussian, Spike-NeRF) that require precise camera poses from COLMAP or VGGT. The paper departs from both by introducing the first framework that jointly optimizes scene and trajectory from unposed spike streams, and by reformulating the spike camera model probabilistically (NBDS) rather than treating binary spike outputs as deterministic signals. The progressive optimization and keyframing strategies are adapted from pose-free 3DGS literature but specifically redesigned to handle the instability and texture poverty inherent to single-frame spike data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the pose prior, Nope-SGS removes a critical bottleneck in spike-based 3D reconstruction where COLMAP/VGGT frequently fail due to unstable spike textures, and the 7.4 dB PSNR and 40% ATE improvements over prior methods suggest that joint scene-pose optimization is substantially more effective than the sequential pipeline (estimate poses → reconstruct) used by all prior spike-based work. The NBDS probabilistic reformulation offers a reusable conceptual tool for any future spike-camera method that needs stable supervision from inherently noisy binary streams. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly dynamic scenes (the current formulation assumes static geometry), how it scales to longer sequences or larger scenes, and whether the MVS-based initialization step remains a practical bottleneck for very sparse or occluded captures.
