## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models generate high-quality, temporally coherent videos but lack fine-grained temporal control: they cannot reliably place specific visual elements at user-specified time points. Adding explicit timing cues to prompts does not fix this and often degrades visual quality, while existing controllable-generation methods require additional supervision or large paired datasets that are impractical to obtain for temporal annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a per-token temporal mask (a binary or continuous vector of length T′ specifying when each word should be visible), and optionally an audio waveform whose onset-strength envelope is thresholded and Gaussian-smoothed into a soft mask. It produces a generated video in which the targeted visual concepts appear at the specified temporal positions, operating on the latent representation of backbones such as Wan 2.1 (1.3B/14B), Wan 2.2-TI2V (5B), CogX1.5-L (5B), and LTX-2 (19B). The temporal mask is the primary control signal, replacing ambiguous natural-language timing descriptions with an explicit, unambiguous specification.

## Method

TEMPOCONTROL is a training-free, inference-time method that steers the latent variables z_t during the first k denoising steps of a DiT-based video diffusion model by applying l AdamW gradient updates per step, without updating model weights. The key insight is that cross-attention maps already encode which prompt tokens are realized in each frame, so optimizing these maps directly controls temporal alignment. The loss combines three terms: a Pearson correlation term aligning the min-max-normalized temporal attention vector with the target mask, a magnitude term (positive and negative components) that amplifies or suppresses attention strength where the mask is active or inactive, and a Shannon entropy regularization over spatial attention maps that prevents attention from diffusing and preserves semantic coherence. A correlation-based early-stopping rule adaptively terminates optimization when alignment is sufficient, reducing compute for videos that naturally satisfy the temporal condition.

## Results

On the one-object temporal control benchmark with Wan 2.1-S (1.3B), TEMPOCONTROL achieves 83.56% Temporal Accuracy, a +19.62% gain over the text-cue baseline (63.94%), with improvements of +20.0% in absence accuracy and +19.25% in presence accuracy while also improving Imaging Quality from 53.76% to 56.92%. In the two-object setting, the method reaches 53.17% Temporal Accuracy versus 37.50% for the baseline (+15.67%), and in the movement setting it achieves 54.00% versus 19.00% (+35%). A user study with 50 annotators confirms the gains: TEMPOCONTROL is preferred for temporal accuracy 61.51% of the time versus 16.94% for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TEMPOCONTROL against text-cue baselines across five backbones: Wan 2.1 (1.3B/14B), Wan 2.2 (5B), CogVideoX (2B/5B), and LTX-2 (19B). On Wan 2.1-S, the method wins decisively on all three temporal settings: +19.62% Temporal Accuracy (one-object), +15.67% (two-object), and +35% (movement), while also improving Imaging Quality in every case. Against the larger Wan 2.1-L (14B), the 1.3B model with TEMPOCONTROL still outperforms the 14B baseline on one-object Temporal Accuracy (83.56% vs. 64.23%) and movement (54% vs. 18%), demonstrating that inference-time attention steering compensates for model scale. On CogVideoX-S (2B), gains are more modest (+5.6% Temporal Accuracy), reflecting the model's stronger bias toward its training distribution. Notably, even the largest baseline (LTX-2, 19B) is outperformed by TEMPOCONTROL on Wan 2.1-S in the two-object (53.17% vs. 39.31%) and movement (54% vs. 28%) settings, underscoring that temporal control is not simply a matter of model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cross-attention mechanism already embedded in text-to-video diffusion transformers (Wan 2.1, CogVideoX, LTX-2) as a control surface for temporal alignment, extending the line of inference-time attention manipulation established by Attend-and-Excite and Prompt-to-Prompt from spatial/semantic grounding to temporal scheduling. It departs from the closest prior work, MinT, which achieves coarse event sequencing but requires finetuning on temporally annotated datasets, by operating entirely at inference time with a novel three-term loss (Pearson correlation, magnitude, entropy) that requires no additional supervision. The method also builds on the broader inference-time optimization trend in video diffusion (structured noise initialization, flow-coherence losses, LLM-grounded scene graphs) but is the first to offer explicit, user-defined per-token timing control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that explicit temporal prompting neither improves temporal accuracy nor preserves visual quality (and in fact degrades both) challenges the common assumption that better prompt engineering is the path to temporal control, motivating explicit control mechanisms. The method's training-free, backbone-agnostic nature makes it immediately deployable on existing video models, and the introduction of a Temporal Accuracy metric fills a gap in current benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to complex multi-event scenes with overlapping temporal constraints, how to mitigate the mild attribute shifts (e.g., color changes) noted as a limitation, and whether the audio-alignment results generalize beyond simple single-peak waveforms to full music or speech-driven generation.
