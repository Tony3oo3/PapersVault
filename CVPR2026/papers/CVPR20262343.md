## Problem

In real-world multimodal emotion recognition in conversations (MERC), missing modalities due to sensor failures or transmission errors degrade recognition performance. Existing modality-space recovery methods struggle to maintain semantic consistency between recovered and available features at the utterance level, and conventional fusion strategies treat all modalities with fixed weights, failing to account for the multi-dimensional uncertainty introduced when some features are generatively recovered rather than directly observed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262343/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a dialogue of N utterances, each with pre-extracted text, visual, and acoustic feature vectors, along with a binary mask per utterance indicating which modalities are present. At least one modality must be available per utterance, and the missing rate κ ranges from 0.0 to 0.7. The model outputs a per-utterance emotion class label (4-class or 6-class for IEMOCAP, 7-class for MELD) and, as an intermediate product, recovered latent feature vectors for any missing modalities that are then fed into the evidence fusion stage.

## Method

HyperEF combines two core modules. First, a Masked Hypergraph Attention Network (MHGAT) builds a hypergraph over all unimodal utterance nodes with contextual and multimodal hyperedges, using attention to adaptively weight node-to-edge and edge-to-node aggregation while a learned mask embedding distinguishes available from missing modalities; the resulting node representations are concatenated along the modality axis to form a conditioning signal for a DDPM-based U-Net diffusion model that recovers missing features in latent space via classifier-free guidance. Second, a Dual Channel Evidence Fusion (DCEF) module applies Dempster-Shafer Theory to fuse per-modality evidence, where the BPA of the universal set is decomposed into a feature-source channel (MSE between predicted and actual noise at the final diffusion timestep, mapped through a sigmoid) and a discriminative channel (normalized cross-entropy of the class distribution), enabling adaptive weighting that down-weights unreliable recovered features. Training uses a composite objective of cross-entropy, KL-divergence regularization against the uniform Dirichlet, and a vacuity penalty, with hyperparameters tuned via Bayesian optimization.

## Results

HyperEF achieves the best accuracy across all three datasets (IEMOCAP4, IEMOCAP6, MELD) and all eight missing-rate settings (0.0–0.7). On IEMOCAP4, it reaches 82.9% at 0% missing and maintains 77.0% at 70% missing, outperforming the strongest baseline SDR-GNN by 3.3% and 2.6% respectively. On IEMOCAP6, the advantage grows at higher missing rates, reaching +4.0% over SDR-GNN at 60% missing (59.5% vs. 55.5%). On MELD, HyperEF scores 69.3% at 0% missing and 57.1% at 70% missing, surpassing the best baseline by 2.2% and 1.8% respectively. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirms statistical significance (W=0, p=0.0078) for every baseline comparison across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperEF outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and missing-rate setting. The strongest competitor is SDR-GNN [7] (spectral-domain GNN, KBS 2025), which HyperEF beats by 2.6–4.0% depending on dataset and missing rate; the gap widens notably at higher missing rates (e.g., +4.0% on IEMOCAP6 at 60% missing). CIF-MMIN [20] (contrastive modality-invariant features, TAFFC 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, trailing HyperEF by 1.0–4.0%. GCNet [18] (graph completion, TPAMI 2023) and MMIN [45] (autoencoder-based, ACL 2021) show steeper accuracy drops as missing rate increases, with MMIN degrading most severely (e.g., 55.4% → 37.8% on IEMOCAP6 from 0% to 70% missing). IMDer [39] (score-based diffusion, NeurIPS 2023) exhibits unstable performance with non-monotonic drops (e.g., 64.5% at 50% missing but 46.9% at 60% on IEMOCAP4), whereas HyperEF maintains a smoother degradation curve. The paper reports that all pairwise differences are directionally consistent, yielding a Wilcoxon signed-rank test of W=0 with p=0.0078 for every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: hypergraph-based GNNs for conversational emotion recognition (Chen et al. CVPR 2023; Chen & Shi AAAI 2025), diffusion-model-based modality recovery (IMDer, NeurIPS 2023; Wang et al. ICCV 2023), and Dempster-Shafer evidence fusion for uncertainty-aware multimodal learning (Shafer 1976; Josang et al. 2018). It departs from prior hypergraph methods by replacing the fixed incidence matrix with an adaptive attention mechanism (MHGAT) that differentiates available from missing modalities, and from prior diffusion-based recovery by conditioning the denoising process on high-order relational structure rather than unimodal or pairwise features. The DCEF module extends standard vacuity-based DST fusion by anchoring one uncertainty axis at the feature-source level (recovery MSE) and the other at the discriminative level (normalized entropy), a decomposition motivated by the specific uncertainty introduced by generative recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—real-world MERC systems must handle sensor failures and transmission errors—and provides a unified framework that simultaneously recovers missing features and fuses them with calibrated confidence. The consistent 1–4% accuracy gains across three datasets and eight missing-rate settings, backed by Wilcoxon significance tests, make the contribution credible for practitioners deploying multimodal emotion systems. However, the approach is constrained to three modalities and conversational settings, the 300-timestep DDPM adds non-trivial inference cost, and it remains open whether the hypergraph structure generalizes to longer dialogues, more modalities, or non-conversational multimodal tasks. The finding that MHGAT matches a Transformer encoder with 77% fewer parameters is a useful efficiency signal but was tested on only two datasets.
