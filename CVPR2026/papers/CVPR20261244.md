## Problem

Hierarchical LiDAR geometry compression methods encode voxel occupancies from low to high bit-depths but treat each depth independently, re-deriving spatial context from coordinates at every level. At higher bit-depths (13–15), average neighbor counts drop to ~1, leaving sparse convolution receptive fields nearly empty and severely limiting prediction quality. Additionally, a single shared coding network cannot adapt to the drastically varying occupancy distributions across bit-depth levels, capping compression efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a LiDAR point cloud quantized to a target bit-depth B (evaluated at 12–16 bits, 1 mm resolution) and produces a compressed bitstream of arithmetic-coded two-stage quadrant labels (4-bit lower + 4-bit upper per parent voxel). Coding proceeds progressively from b=2 to b=B−1, where each parent voxel's 8-way child occupancy is predicted and entropy-coded; the decoder reconstructs child coordinates by expanding parents with octant offsets. All compared methods losslessly code input coordinates, so geometric distortion is fixed by the chosen bit-depth and only bitrate varies across methods.

## Method

ELiC combines three mechanisms within a lightweight sparse-CNN octree coder. First, a Morton-order-preserving hierarchy sorts coordinates once by 3D Z-order and maintains it across all depth transitions via integer division/doubling, eliminating per-level sorting overhead. Second, cross-bit-depth feature propagation replicates refined features from each parent to its occupied children and fuses them at the next level with octant-parity embeddings through a learnable channel-wise gated blend, so dense lower-level context accumulates upward to support prediction at sparse upper levels. Third, a Bag-of-Encoders (BoE) pool of K+1 same-architecture networks (one base for b≤6, K adaptive for b>6) is selected per level by matching a 32-D normalized occupancy histogram to K-means centers, with the chosen index signaled in the bitstream. Each coding network uses two 3×3×3 sparse-convolution residual blocks applied three times per level (before stage-1, between stages, and before propagation), with 16-way softmax prediction heads and per-stage occupancy context embeddings.

## Results

On the Ford dataset, ELiC-Large achieves the best BD-Rate of −26.54% (D1) relative to G-PCCv30, outperforming TopNet (−26.26%) and Unicorn (−25.41%). On SemanticKITTI, TopNet leads at −34.10% while ELiC-Large follows closely at −33.26%, and ELiC (base) reaches −29.23%, matching Unicorn (−29.28%) at a fraction of the compute. In terms of runtime, ELiC averages 0.121 s encoding and 0.111 s decoding per frame, achieving real-time throughput (≥10 FPS) at 12-bit input, whereas TopNet decoding averages 1306 s and Unicorn encoding averages 3.3 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RENO (the direct predecessor), ELiC improves BD-Rate by approximately 8% on Ford (−22.97% vs −14.02%) and 8.3% on SemanticKITTI (−29.23% vs −20.90%), while maintaining comparable runtime (~0.12 s vs ~0.12 s per frame). ELiC-Large further extends this gap, reaching −26.54% on Ford and −33.26% on KITTI, surpassing RENO-Large by 11.8% and 1.7% respectively, while using less than half the model size (30.7 MB vs 78.4 MB). On Ford, ELiC-Large achieves the best compression among all methods, narrowly beating TopNet (−26.54% vs −26.26%) and Unicorn (−25.41%). On SemanticKITTI, TopNet retains a small edge over ELiC-Large (−34.10% vs −33.26%), but TopNet's decoding time averages 1306 s per frame versus ELiC-Large's 0.121 s, making ELiC-Large the clear winner in the compression–runtime trade-off. ELiC (base) matches Unicorn on KITTI (−29.23% vs −29.28%) while running over 20× faster in encoding (0.121 s vs 3.298 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

ELiC extends RENO's lightweight real-time LiDAR geometry compression framework by addressing its core weakness: level-independent occupancy coding that re-derives spatial context from coordinates at each bit-depth. It departs from the sparse tensor-based lineage (SparsePCGC, Unicorn, UniPCGC, RENO) in two ways: (1) cross-bit-depth feature propagation carries dense lower-level context upward to sparse upper levels, and (2) a Bag-of-Encoders pool with K-means-selected experts adapts network capacity to per-level occupancy statistics without per-level dedicated models. The Morton-order-preserving hierarchy is a systems-level contribution that eliminates the per-level sorting overhead common to prior octree-traversal methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time LiDAR compression at 12-bit (≥10 FPS) is a practical bottleneck for autonomous driving, robotics, and V2X uplink, and ELiC is among only three methods (with RENO and ELiC-Large) to meet this threshold while achieving SOTA BD-Rate on Ford. The paper's main open limitation is that it operates losslessly, restricting bitrate control to input bit-depth selection; extending the BoE selection policy with a rate-distortion objective for lossy coding is identified as the natural next step. Additional open questions include whether the fixed 4+4 two-stage factorization can be made input-adaptive, and whether quantization-aware training and hardware-specific optimizations can further reduce the compute footprint for embedded deployment.
