## Problem

Existing neural video compression methods optimize in unified color spaces (RGB or upsampled YUV444), ignoring the human visual system's asymmetric sensitivity to luminance versus chrominance, and they fail to enforce consistent perceptual quality ordering across variable bitrates because adversarial discriminators cannot reliably perceive bitrate differences during training. As a result, perceptual NVC models are typically trained at a single rate, and reconstructions at different bitrates lack a guaranteed quality hierarchy.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes YUV420 video frames (96-frame sequences, intra-period −1 or 32) and a discrete bitrate index r ∈ {0,1,2,3} corresponding to λ values of 85, 170, 380, and 840. At each time step the input frame is split into a single-channel luminance component and a two-channel chrominance component, which are encoded by separate networks; the Luma-Net outputs reconstructed luminance, optical flow, and propagation features, while the Chroma-Net outputs reconstructed chrominance conditioned on the luminance outputs. The final output is a compressed bitstream and reconstructed YUV420 frames, with perceptual metrics computed after BT.709 conversion to RGB.

## Method

PNVC-C decouples coding into a Luma-Net (built on DCVC-DC with single-channel Luma-SpyNet for optical flow and Partial Conv ResBlocks for efficiency) and a lightweight Chroma-Net that reuses the luminance optical flow and applies a Color Fidelity Refinement (CFR) module using attention-based fusion of luminance propagation features into chrominance features. For perceptual optimization, Rc-GAN introduces a bitrate-based rank chain loss that enforces f(X̂_{r+1}) > f(X̂_r) for all adjacent rate pairs, decomposing the global ordering into local pairwise constraints. A stage-wise training strategy processes rates from highest to lowest, and the generator receives gradient updates only when the local ranking is already satisfied (via an indicator function), preventing misleading supervision. This extends ReWaGAN's single pairwise ranking to a multi-bitrate chain, providing fine-grained perceptual feedback that suppresses temporal artifact accumulation.

## Results

On the standard 96-frame, IP=-1 evaluation, PNVC-C-Base achieves SOTA objective compression with −25.26% BD-rate in YUV PSNR, −22.34% in YUV SSIM, and −19.34% in VMAF over VTM-13.2. The perceptual variant PNVC-CR attains SOTA perceptual quality with −77.71% BD-rate in LPIPS, −53.94% in DISTS, −54.44% in KID, and −42.27% in FID, while retaining a competitive −15.91% BD-rate in YUV PSNR. Against the strongest neural baseline DCVC-FM, PNVC-CR improves LPIPS by 80.27 percentage points (from +2.56% to −77.71%) and DISTS by 111.28 percentage points (from +57.34% to −53.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PNVC-C-Base outperforms all three neural baselines (DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM, DCVC-RT) on every objective metric, with the largest gap over DCVC-FM in YUV PSNR (−25.26% vs. −21.69%) and over DCVC-RT in VMAF (−19.34% vs. −5.67%). Both variants beat the traditional VTM-13.2 anchor across all metrics, confirming that the color-separation framework alone already surpasses the current video coding standard. PNVC-CR dominates all perceptual metrics by an overwhelming margin: it achieves negative BD-rate (i.e., better quality at lower bitrate) on LPIPS, DISTS, KID, and FID, whereas all three neural baselines show positive BD-rate (worse than VTM) on these same metrics. Against DiffVC, a diffusion-based generative codec, PNVC-CR achieves lower LPIPS (0.1017 vs. 0.1093) and higher RGB PSNR (32.18 dB vs. 29.68 dB) at roughly 13× lower bitrate (0.014 bpp vs. 0.191 bpp) on HEVC-B, while running ~32× faster per frame (125 ms vs. ~4.0 s). The only metric where PNVC-CR trails PNVC-C-Base is YUV PSNR (−15.91% vs. −25.26%), reflecting the expected fidelity-perception trade-off of adversarial fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DCVC-DC (Li et al., CVPR 2023) as its architectural backbone, adapting its diverse-context conditional coding framework into a luminance-only Luma-Net. The perceptual optimization scheme, Rc-GAN, extends the pairwise ranking idea from ReWaGAN (Ma et al., TPAMI 2022) from a single reference-vs-output comparison to a multi-bitrate chain of local pairwise constraints. The key departure from prior NVC work is the explicit luminance-chrominance decoupling, which mirrors traditional YUV420 subsampling but had not been systematically integrated into learned video codecs; prior perceptual NVC methods (e.g., DCVC-RT, CGVC-T) operate in a unified color space without exploiting the HVS's asymmetric sensitivity to luma versus chroma.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to bring explicit color-space separation into the NVC pipeline, demonstrating that a design principle long standard in traditional codecs (YUV420) yields substantial gains (7.2% YUV PSNR, 9.1% LPIPS) when properly integrated with learned architectures. The rank chain loss addresses a practical gap: most perceptual NVC models are trained at a single rate, and those that support variable rates often fail to maintain monotone perceptual quality ordering across bitrates. Open questions include whether the rank chain mechanism generalizes beyond 4 rate points, whether the color separation strategy transfers to higher resolutions or 4:2:2/4:4:4 content, and whether the approach can be combined with emerging generative (diffusion-based) codecs without the current CNN-specific architectural assumptions.
