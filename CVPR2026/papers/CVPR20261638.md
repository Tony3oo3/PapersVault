## Problem

Video LLMs process tens of thousands of visual tokens, making inference prohibitively expensive. Existing token reduction methods either prune spatially within frames or apply shallow-layer pruning inside the LLM, and critically, they simply discard or merge tokens without recovering the subtle yet informative context those tokens carry. This paper asks how to aggregate the semantic and contextual information from pruned tokens into the remaining ones rather than losing it entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261638/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sampled video frames (32 frames × 196 tokens for LLaVA-OneVision, 64 frames × 169 tokens for LLaVA-Video) and a text prompt, and produces a compressed visual token sequence at 10–25% of the original token count before the LLM, which then generates a text response. All compression occurs pre-LLM (before the transformer backbone), so the LLM sees a fixed, reduced token budget regardless of input length. The per-frame anchor count is fixed (e.g., 126 tokens at 10% retention), enabling uniform batch processing unlike variable-count pruning methods.

## Method

AOT establishes local-global token anchors per frame by selecting global tokens via [CLS] attention in the final encoder layer and local tokens via grid-wise Top-K selection from a shallow layer, then applies Optimal Transport in two stages. Intra-frame, anchors and unselected tokens are treated as two discrete distributions with inverse cosine similarity as the cost matrix; the Sinkhorn-Knopp solver yields a transport plan that mass-normalizes and aggregates unselected token features onto each anchor. Inter-frame, the first frame of each temporal clip serves as the anchor set, and OT is applied sequentially across consecutive frames to merge similar tokens into anchors while retaining dissimilar tokens that capture temporal dynamics. The entire pipeline is training-free, with the OT solved in ~2 ms per clip via 100 Sinkhorn iterations on GPU, reducing prefilling FLOPs to as low as 8.3% of the original while preserving ~97.6% of benchmark performance.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision 7B, AOT reduces prefilling FLOPs to 8.3% of the original while retaining only 10% of visual tokens, yet preserves 97.6% of the vanilla model's average score (57.0 vs. 58.4) across MVBench, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, and VideoMME. At the more aggressive 15% retention setting, AOT achieves 98.8% score retention (57.7 avg) with 8.3% FLOPs, matching FastVID's 98.8% while using fewer FLOPs (8.3% vs. 12.7%). On LLaVA-Video 7B, AOT reaches 95.5% score retention at 15% token retention (57.5 avg vs. 60.2 vanilla), outperforming VisionZip (94.2%) at the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FastVID, the strongest spatial-temporal baseline, AOT matches its 98.8% score retention at 15% token retention but achieves this with lower FLOPs (8.3% vs. 12.7%), and at 10% retention AOT scores 97.6% while FastVID's results are not reported at that budget. Against VisionZip, AOT shows a clear advantage under aggressive compression: at 10% retention, AOT retains 97.6% of vanilla performance versus VisionZip's 91.6% (a 6-point gap), with the largest drop on LongVideoBench (54.2 vs. 49.3). Against DyCoke, which is capped at 25% retention by its 4-frame grouping design, AOT achieves comparable 98.8% at 25% but extends to 10% retention with 97.6% retention. Against inner-LLM pruning methods (FastV, PDrop), AOT substantially outperforms at equivalent or lower FLOPs: PDrop drops to 92.6% at 25% retention while AOT maintains 98.8%. On LLaVA-Video 7B, AOT outperforms VisionZip by 2.5% in score retention at 15% tokens (95.5% vs. 94.2%) and by 1.9% at 25% (96.7% vs. 94.2%), confirming consistent gains across model architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

AOT carves new ground in video token compression by repurposing Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp) as an information-aggregation mechanism rather than a mere similarity metric. While prior methods (DyCoke, PruneVid, FastVID, VisionZip) either discard low-discriminative tokens or merge near-duplicates, AOT treats pruned tokens as 'suppliers' and anchor tokens as 'demanders' in a transport problem, redistributing their semantic content onto the survivors. The local-global anchor selection draws directly from AnyRes-style grid-wise token picking in MLLMs, and the overall pipeline is positioned as a training-free alternative to trainable compression modules (Chat-UniVi, LongVU) and to in-LLM pruning (FastV, PyramidDrop).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, ~2 ms overhead pipeline that retains 97.6% performance at 10% token budget offers a practical drop-in for deploying VLLMs on resource-constrained hardware, and the OT-based aggregation provides a principled way to avoid the information loss inherent in naive merging or removal. However, the method is validated only on two 7B LLaVA-family backbones and four benchmarks; generalization to larger models, different visual encoders, or non-English video QA remains untested. An open question is whether the inter-frame threshold τ and the λ weighting coefficients can be made adaptive rather than fixed, and whether the OT formulation extends naturally to audio-visual or 3D video settings.
