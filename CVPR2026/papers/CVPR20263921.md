## Problem

Existing GCD methods jointly optimize supervised and unsupervised objectives, but this direct combination suffers from an underexplored optimization conflict termed Gradient Entanglement (GE): noisy unsupervised gradients distort the supervised optimization direction (weakening known-class discrimination), while dominant supervised gradients progressively pull novel-class representations into the known-class subspace (reducing novel-class separability). The paper quantifies this issue with two new metrics—Gradient Deviation Coefficient (GDC) and Subspace Overlap Coefficient (SOC)—and shows that both degrade substantially as training progresses in representative baselines.

## Input / Output

EAGC is a plug-and-play gradient-level module that takes the same inputs as any GCD framework: a labeled subset of images from known classes and an unlabeled subset mixing known and novel classes, processed by a ViT-B/16 backbone (DINO- or DINOv2-pretrained). It does not alter the network architecture or the original training objectives; instead, it registers backward hooks at the feature level to modulate gradients before they propagate to encoder parameters. The output is the same as the host GCD method—cluster assignments (via Hungarian matching) or parametric class predictions for all unlabeled samples—reported as All/Old/New clustering accuracy.

## Method

EAGC comprises two complementary gradient-level components. (1) Anchor-based Gradient Alignment (AGA) trains a frozen reference model purely on labeled data, then adds a proximal-regularization term λ_a(z^l − ẑ^l) to the labeled-sample feature gradients during joint GCD training, anchoring their direction toward the supervised optimum and preventing unsupervised gradients from distorting known-class discrimination. (2) Energy-aware Elastic Projection (EEP) constructs a Conceptor-based soft subspace of known classes (S^known = R(R + η⁻²I)⁻¹) and projects unlabeled gradients away from it, but with a per-sample adaptive weight τ_i = 1 − E_old(z_i^u)/Ē_old^l that scales the projection strength by each sample's feature-energy ratio within the known subspace—so samples likely from known classes receive weak projection while novel samples are strongly decoupled. The two terms are added to the standard supervised and unsupervised feature gradients (Eq. 12), requiring only backward-hook registration and no architectural or objective changes, making EAGC seamlessly integrable into both parametric and non-parametric GCD frameworks.

## Results

EAGC is a plug-and-play gradient-level module that, when integrated into four GCD baselines (SimGCD, LegoGCD, SPTNet, SelEx), yields average gains of +3.2% in All ACC and +4.3% in New ACC across five datasets. On fine-grained benchmarks (CUB, Stanford Cars, Aircraft), SelEx+EAGC achieves state-of-the-art All ACC of 83.2%, 65.7%, and 65.7%, surpassing the previous best (InfoSieve at 69.4% on CUB) by 13.8 points. On generic benchmarks, SPTNet+EAGC reaches 84.0% All ACC on CIFAR-100 and SelEx+EAGC reaches 89.8% on ImageNet-100, both setting new records. With a DINOv2 backbone, EAGC further improves SimGCD and SelEx by average +6.2% All ACC and +8.3% New ACC on the three fine-grained datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAGC is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to four representative baselines (SimGCD, LegoGCD, SPTNet, SelEx) and compared against ten prior SOTA methods. The most striking gains come from SelEx+EAGC, which jumps from 73.6% to 83.2% All ACC on CUB (+9.6%), from 58.5% to 65.7% on Stanford Cars (+7.2%), and from 57.1% to 65.7% on Aircraft (+8.6%), surpassing the previous best (InfoSieve at 69.4% on CUB, CMS at 56.9% on Cars, InfoSieve at 56.3% on Aircraft) by 13.8, 8.8, and 9.4 points respectively. On ImageNet-100, SelEx+EAGC reaches 89.8% All ACC, exceeding CMS (84.7%) by 5.1 points; on CIFAR-100, SPTNet+EAGC achieves 84.0%, edging out CMS (82.3%) by 1.7 points. Among the parametric baselines, SimGCD+EAGC improves from 60.3% to 66.5% All ACC on CUB (+6.2%) and from 53.8% to 62.9% on Cars (+9.1%), while LegoGCD+EAGC gains +0.8% on CUB and +5.5% on Cars. Notably, EAGC consistently improves New ACC more than Old ACC (average +4.3% vs. +1.2%), confirming that the gradient decoupling mechanism primarily benefits novel-class discovery. The only minor regression is a −0.3% Old ACC drop on Aircraft for SPTNet+EAGC, but its New ACC still improves from 58.1% to 56.5% (a slight trade-off), while All ACC remains competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes gradient projection techniques originally developed for multi-task learning (Gradient Surgery) and continual learning (GPM, PGP) to the GCD setting, where the unique challenge is that unlabeled data contains both known and novel classes. It draws on Conceptor theory from recurrent neural network control [19] to construct a soft, energy-weighted known-class subspace, and on proximal regularization [40, 81] to motivate the anchor-based gradient alignment component. Unlike prior plug-and-play GCD modules that modify representations or losses, EAGC operates purely at the gradient level during backpropagation, requiring no architectural changes. The key departure from existing gradient projection work is the energy-aware adaptive weighting, which accounts for the GCD-specific fact that some unlabeled samples belong to known classes and should not be uniformly projected away from the known subspace.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying and quantifying Gradient Entanglement through the GDC and SOC metrics, the paper provides a diagnostic framework that explains why existing GCD methods plateau, offering a concrete optimization-level explanation beyond the usual 'trade-off between known and novel' narrative. The plug-and-play design means EAGC can be retrofitted onto any existing GCD pipeline without retraining from scratch, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the reference model overhead (30 epochs of additional training) is acceptable in resource-constrained settings, whether the Conceptor-based subspace is truly necessary over simpler alternatives at scale, and whether the gradient-level intervention generalizes to other semi-supervised or open-world tasks beyond GCD. The modest gains on generic datasets (1.3–1.4%) versus large gains on fine-grained ones (4.6–6.1%) suggest the method's effectiveness may depend on the degree of inter-class similarity.
