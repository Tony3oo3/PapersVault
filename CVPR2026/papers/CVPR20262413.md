## Problem

While vision-language models have achieved strong performance on text-image tasks, aligning a structured 3D scene modality (objects with shapes, positions, poses, and sizes) with both language and images in a single autoregressive framework remains largely unexplored. The paper tackles the design question of how to tokenize, sequence, and train such a 3D modality so that a decoder-only LLM can perform 3D reconstruction, rendering, recognition, and instruction-following within one unified token space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three modalities in a single token sequence: RGB images encoded as VQGAN discrete tokens, text via the Llama-3.2 tokenizer (128k vocabulary), and structured 3D scenes where each object is represented by 512 shape tokens (from a Trellis-based VQ-VAE), discretized x/y/z coordinates at 0.05 granularity, and pose/size values, all wrapped in special [OBJECT-START]/[SCENE-START] delimiters. Outputs are modality-dependent: rendered images (256 VQGAN tokens), full 3D scene structures (object shapes, types, positions, poses), or short text answers. The unified vocabulary spans 137,607 tokens across all modalities, and the image-before-3D ordering in the input sequence is critical for performance.

## Method

Kyvo extends a Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct decoder-only backbone with modality-specific tokenizers and full fine-tuning (outperforming LoRA and scratch training). The key 3D tokenization component is a VQ-VAE that compresses Trellis sparse voxel slats (64³×8, ~20k entries) into a dense 8³×128 latent quantized to 512 tokens via an 8192-entry codebook, trained with an auxiliary multi-view pixel-space reconstruction loss (L1, D-SSIM, LPIPS) that is essential for preserving geometry and texture. Coordinates use hybrid learned-plus-sine-cosine embeddings to enforce numeric ordering, and image outputs employ center-token reordering with a 10× loss weight on the first five tokens to prevent catastrophic first-token divergence during autoregressive decoding. The paper provides an empirical 'cookbook' from 307 trained models covering granularity selection, sequence ordering, training recipes, and backbone choices, demonstrating that the 1B instruction-tuned model with full fine-tuning achieves the best trade-off across rendering, recognition, instruction-following, and QA tasks.

## Results

On the real-world Objectron dataset, Kyvo achieves a Jaccard Index of 0.4784 for 3D object recognition, substantially outperforming Cube R-CNN with ResNet-34 (0.3276) and DLA-34 (0.4012) backbones. On the more challenging ARKitScenes dataset, Kyvo scores 0.2118, performing on par with Cube R-CNN (DLA-34 at 0.2208) and slightly above ResNet-34 (0.2043). On synthetic CLEVR, the model reaches 0.9212 Jaccard for recognition, 1.200 mean rank for rendering, 0.8666 for instruction-following, and 0.4980 for QA. On the complex ObjaWorld scenes, recognition drops to 0.6415 Jaccard, reflecting the increased geometric and layout complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Cube R-CNN, the state-of-the-art 3D object detector, Kyvo achieves a clear win on Objectron (0.4784 vs. 0.3276 for ResNet-34 and 0.4012 for DLA-34), a margin of 7–15 Jaccard points. On ARKitScenes, Kyvo (0.2118) is competitive but slightly trails Cube R-CNN with DLA-34 (0.2208) by ~1 point, while edging out the ResNet-34 variant (0.2043). Against SAR3D, a prior autoregressive 3D tokenizer, Kyvo's Trellis-based VQ-VAE achieves a better human-evaluation mean rank (1.395 vs. 1.605) while using 4× fewer tokens per object (512 vs. 2040), enabling multi-object scene encoding that SAR3D does not support. Qualitatively, Kyvo's structured, object-centric reconstruction (Fig. 9a) produces coherent geometries and spatial layouts, whereas Trellis's holistic diffusion-based image-to-3D approach yields distorted shapes and misaligned object arrangements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoder-only LLM paradigm (Llama-3.2) by introducing a structured 3D scene modality as a first-class token sequence alongside text and image tokens, building directly on Trellis's sparse voxel slat representation for 3D geometry. It departs from prior 3D-LLM work (3D-LLM, PointLLM, SceneScript) that treats 3D data holistically (point clouds, NeRFs, full scene clouds) by decomposing scenes into object-centric token lists, enabling direct per-object alignment with language and vision. For 3D tokenization, it contrasts with SAR3D and AToken, which target single-asset generation with 2040+ tokens per object, whereas Kyvo's VQ-VAE compresses each object to 512 tokens optimized for multi-object scene encoding within a unified vocabulary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a systematic 'cookbook' of empirically validated design choices (granularity, number encoding, sequence ordering, center-token reordering, loss weighting) for integrating 3D into autoregressive LLMs, which is practically valuable as the field moves toward unified multimodal models. A key open question is cross-domain generalization: the authors acknowledge that strong within-domain performance is achievable with modest data (~100k scenes), but true cross-domain transfer requires larger 3D datasets that are not readily available. The finding that full fine-tuning from language-only weights outperforms LoRA when adding entirely new modalities, and that a 1B model can outperform a 3B model on QA, challenges common scaling assumptions and has practical implications for resource-constrained 3D multimodal training.
