## Problem

Existing focus quality assessment (FQA) methods assume a stain-agnostic, globally shared ordinal ordering of focus levels, but fluorescence microscopy exhibits stain-dependent, non-uniform defocus due to wavelength-dependent point spread functions and emission-spectrum differences. No prior dataset or method explicitly captures or models this stain-dependent focus behavior, leaving a gap between the physical imaging process and the modeling assumptions of current FQA approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262224/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fluorescence microscopy image (resized to 256x256, center-cropped to 224x224) together with a discrete stain label identifying which fluorophore channel the image belongs to. The output is an ordinal focus rank in {1, ..., K} (K=10 discrete levels), predicted via cosine similarity between the CLIP visual embedding and stain-conditioned rank prompt embeddings. Because the rank prompts are constructed per-sample using the stain label, the effective classifier is sample-dependent rather than a single fixed set of K class vectors.

## Method

FluoCLIP is a two-stage CLIP-based framework that decouples stain semantics from ordinal structure. In Stage 1 (Stain-Grounding), learnable stain token embeddings are aligned to the frozen CLIP visual encoder through a lightweight adapter (one self-attention layer plus two-layer MLP), teaching the text side fluorescence-specific stain semantics without fine-tuning the pretrained text encoder. In Stage 2 (Stain-Guided Ranking), a conditioning network projects a small set of base rank embeddings into a stain-specific space using the grounded stain embeddings, and intermediate ranks are generated by weighted interpolation following OrdinalCLIP; the resulting stain-conditioned rank tokens are inserted into pseudo-sentence templates alongside the stain token and passed through the CLIP text encoder. Both stages are trained with a combined cross-entropy and KL-divergence loss that enforces correct classification and ordinal consistency in the similarity distributions, using a CLIP backbone (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) with 10 epochs for grounding and 100 epochs for ranking.

## Results

On the primary FluoMix dataset with a ResNet-50 encoder, FluoCLIP achieves 85.21% accuracy, 0.991 PLCC, and 0.150 MAE, outperforming the strongest baseline OrdinalCLIP (83.12% acc, 0.172 MAE) and all traditional FQA methods (47–63% accuracy). With a ViT-B/16 encoder, FluoCLIP reaches 90.75% accuracy and 0.093 MAE on FluoMix, surpassing BiomedCLIP (83.99%) and OrdinalCLIP (89.95%). On BBBC006, FluoCLIP attains 93.05% accuracy (MAE 0.080), while on the bright-field FocusPath dataset it underperforms OrdinalCLIP (91.11% vs. 94.98%), consistent with the paper's hypothesis that stain-aware modeling is most beneficial under heterogeneous fluorescence conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FluoCLIP outperforms all traditional FQA baselines (FocusLiteNN, CE, OE, CO2, QULCE) by a wide margin on FluoMix, with accuracy gains of 22–38 points over the best conventional method (OE at 63.46%). Among vision-language baselines, FluoCLIP beats CLIP by 3.6 points (85.21% vs. 81.59%), OrdinalCLIP by 2.1 points (85.21% vs. 83.12%), and NumCLIP by a large MAE margin (0.150 vs. 0.325). With the ViT-B/16 encoder, FluoCLIP surpasses BiomedCLIP by 6.8 points (90.75% vs. 83.99%), attributed to BiomedCLIP's text encoder lacking optical descriptors and its image encoder not being fluorescence-specific. On BBBC006, FluoCLIP leads OrdinalCLIP by 2.4 points (93.05% vs. 90.67%). The sole exception is FocusPath (bright-field H&E), where FluoCLIP underperforms OrdinalCLIP by 3.9 points (91.11% vs. 94.98%), which the authors interpret as expected since stain-aware conditioning adds unnecessary capacity when focus behavior is stain-invariant.

## Relation to Prior Work

FluoCLIP builds directly on OrdinalCLIP's interpolation-based rank prompt strategy and its combined CE + KL-divergence training objective, extending the vision-language ordinal regression framework to a domain where a single global rank ordering is physically invalid. It departs from prior FQA methods (FocusLiteNN, CE/OE/CO2/QULCE baselines) that treat focus as a stain-agnostic property, and from domain-adapted CLIP variants (BiomedCLIP, PubMedCLIP) that rely on pretraining over biomedical corpora rather than explicit stain-visual alignment. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage design: a stain-grounding stage that uses a lightweight adapter to align learnable stain tokens with CLIP visual features, followed by a stain-guided ranking stage that conditions rank embeddings on the grounded stain identity—effectively decoupling stain semantics from ordinal structure rather than conflating them in a single prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first empirical and quantitative evidence (SRCC analysis, spatial-frequency distributions) that focus-rank relationships are fundamentally stain-dependent in fluorescence microscopy, invalidating the shared-global-ordering assumption underlying all prior FQA work. FluoMix (~56K images, 3 tissues, 6 fluorophores, 13 targets) fills a clear gap between the bright-field FocusPath and the homogeneous BBBC006, and its release of raw z-stacks enables future annotation schemes. A notable limitation is the assumption of predefined stain categories; the authors acknowledge that automatic stain discovery and more general conditioning mechanisms remain open. The finding that BiomedCLIP's domain-specific pretraining underperforms standard CLIP with explicit stain grounding suggests that targeted semantic alignment can be more effective than broad domain adaptation for narrow optical tasks.
