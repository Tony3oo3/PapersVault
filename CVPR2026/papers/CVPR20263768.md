## Problem

Obaining robot-usable supervision at scale remains challenging: teleoperation and studio motion capture are costly and hardware-specific, while the vast amounts of hand-object interaction (HOI) video available from real-world recordings and public datasets are rarely converted into training-ready robotic data due to reconstruction noise, physical implausibility, and embodiment mismatch. No prior work has provided quantitative evidence that HOI modalities can serve as effective supervision for cross-embodiment robotic learning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes monocular RGB or RGB-D video clips of hand-object interactions (no multi-view or mocap hardware required) and produces physically plausible, contact-rich HOI trajectories in a canonical action space. These are retargeted into executable joint-space and operational-space control sequences for heterogeneous embodiments (6/7-DoF arms with parallel grippers, dexterous hands, humanoids), and further expanded via simulation into a ~150k-trajectory multimodal dataset (HORA) containing multi-camera observations, 6-DoF object poses, MANO hand parameters, contact annotations, tactile maps (mocap subset), and language task descriptions.

## Method

RoboWheel integrates state-of-the-art hand (MANO), whole-body (SMPL-H), and object pose estimators into a unified reconstruction framework, then enforces physical plausibility through a two-stage optimization: TSDF-based signed-distance penalties eliminate hand-object interpenetration, and a residual RL policy refines hand-object relative poses under reachability and stability priors while promoting consistent contact. Retargeting maps reconstructed hand joints to gripper end-effector poses via a kNN gesture classifier (whole-hand vs. finger-only mapping) and CoTracker-based gripper state inference, with extensions to dexterous hands and humanoids through kinematic similarity and dynamics-aware IK. To scale coverage, retargeted trajectories are replayed in Isaac Sim across five robot arms using cuRobo's GPU-parallel inverse kinematics, with domain randomization over embodiments, object retrieval/replacement (Chamfer + IoU + semantic similarity), trajectory re-anchoring, background textures, and hand mirroring. The key design insight is that a single monocular camera yields a universal, embodiment-agnostic motion representation that can be flexibly retargeted, making the pipeline lightweight compared to teleoperation while producing trajectories as stable as teleoperated ones.

## Results

On eight real-world household tasks, VLA policies (RDT, Pi0) pretrained on 5k HORA trajectories then fine-tuned on 10 HORA episodes achieve 75.0% and 76.3% success on Easy tasks and 47.5% and 58.8% on Hard tasks, surpassing their teleoperation-only counterparts (66.3%/40.0% and 68.8%/40.0%). When trained on an identical number of episodes, HORA data yields performance comparable to teleoperation (e.g., RDT: 47.5% HORA vs. 66.3% tele on Easy; Pi0: 58.8% HORA vs. 68.8% tele on Easy). For HOI reconstruction on HO-Cap, RoboWheel (TSDF+RL) achieves 5.1 mm Chamfer distance, 89.1% F10, and 93.6% replay success rate, outperforming HOLD (7.5 mm CD, 77.9% F10). In direct robot replay, RoboWheel's retargeting reaches 91.7% macro success rate versus 50.0% (GAT-Grasp) and 66.7% (Yoto).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between HORA-derived trajectories and teleoperation data for training VLA/IL policies. With equal episode counts (10), HORA data achieves comparable but slightly lower success rates than teleoperation across all models (e.g., RDT: 47.5% vs. 66.3% on Easy; Pi0: 58.8% vs. 68.8% on Easy), with the gap narrowing substantially on Hard tasks for Pi0 (31.3% vs. 40.0%). Critically, pretraining on 5k HORA trajectories before fine-tuning on just 10 HORA episodes *exceeds* teleoperation-only performance (RDT: 75.0% vs. 66.3%; Pi0: 76.3% vs. 68.8% on Easy), demonstrating that HORA's broader distribution from augmentation provides a net benefit. For HOI reconstruction quality on HO-Cap, RoboWheel (TSDF+RL) outperforms HOLD [50] on all metrics: 5.1 mm vs. 7.5 mm Chamfer distance, 89.1% vs. 77.9% F10, 0.92 vs. 3.47 jitter, and 93.6% vs. no reported replay success. In direct robot replay (Table 5), RoboWheel's retargeting achieves 91.7% macro success, substantially outperforming GAT-Grasp (50.0%) and Yoto (66.7%), with the largest margin on the 'Tip teacup' task (87.5% vs. 37.5% and 0.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of monocular HOI reconstruction (building on MANO/SMPL-H parameterization, FoundationPose for object tracking, and HOLD-style contact-aware optimization) and simulation-based data generation for robot learning (Isaac Sim, cuRobo IK, domain randomization). It departs from prior teleoperation-centric pipelines (e.g., DROID, LIBERO) and purely synthetic generators (e.g., RobotTwin) by using real-world HOI video as the sole input source, inserting an RL-based physical plausibility stage between reconstruction and retargeting that prior HOI-to-robot works (e.g., GAT-Grasp, You-Only-Teach-Once) lack. The cross-embodiment retargeting to multiple arm morphologies via a canonical action space extends the single-embodiment focus of most prior HOI-to-grasp mappings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first quantitative evidence that monocular HOI video can serve as robot-usable supervision at quality comparable to teleoperation, which could dramatically lower the data-collection barrier for cross-embodiment manipulation. The 150k-sequence HORA dataset and the end-to-end pipeline are practically valuable resources for the VLA/IL community. However, real-world validation is limited to a single UR5 arm with a parallel gripper; dexterous-hand and humanoid retargeting remain preliminary, and the sim-to-real gap for more complex embodiments is untested. Open questions include whether the RL plausibility stage generalizes to highly dynamic or multi-object interactions, and whether the augmentation strategies transfer to tasks requiring precise force control.
