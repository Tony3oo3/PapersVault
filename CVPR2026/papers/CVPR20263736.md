## Problem

In two-stage open-vocabulary detectors built on frozen VLMs like CLIP, novel-category objects are forcibly treated as background during RPN and RoI-head training, causing their proposals to receive low confidence scores and be filtered out at test time. This training-inference mismatch severely degrades novel-category recall, and existing remedies (large-scale data, pseudo-labeling) introduce cost or noise. The paper addresses this gap without any additional training data or pseudo image-text pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263736/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector takes an RGB image and a set of text prompts (specific category names for base and novel classes at test time; class-agnostic foreground/background descriptions generated by ChatGPT-o1 during training) as input. It outputs bounding box coordinates and category labels assigned via cosine similarity between RoI features and frozen CLIP text embeddings. The backbone is a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14, with only the FPN, RPN, and RoI heads trained on base-category annotations from OV-LVIS or OV-COCO.

## Method

NoOVD introduces two key components. First, K-FPN is a learnable-parameter-free feature pyramid constructed directly from frozen multi-layer CLIP features (e.g., layers 5, 7, 11 of ViT-B/16) with top-down fusion and a frozen dimensionality-reduction head, preserving CLIP's pretrained knowledge without the drift that occurs when features pass through learnable FPN layers. Second, during training, class-agnostic foreground/background text prompts are used with CLIP's zero-shot capability to identify latent novel-category proposals from RPN outputs; the corresponding image crops are passed through frozen CLIP, and their features are aligned with RoI features via an L2 self-distillation loss, preventing novel-category features from being forced toward background embeddings. At test time, R-RPN fuses the original RPN confidence with K-FPN foreground confidence (α=0.5) before post-processing, boosting the scores of latent novel-category proposals so they survive the top-K selection and reach the RoI head.

## Results

On OV-LVIS, NoOVD + DeCLIP (ViT-L/14) achieves the best overall performance with APr = 29.2 and AP = 27.6, surpassing the F-ViT baseline by +2.6% on rare categories and +1.6% on overall AP. On OV-COCO, the same configuration reaches AP50_novel = 47.5 and AP50 = 61.0, outperforming all prior methods. Cross-dataset transfer to Objects365 yields APr = 23.3 and AP50 = 41.1, again the best among compared methods. For the smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, NoOVD + DeCLIP achieves APr = 36.9 and AP = 36.2 on OV-LVIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoOVD consistently outperforms its direct baselines (CLIPSelf+F-ViT and DeCLIP+F-ViT) on rare-category AP: +2.9% and +2.6% for ViT-L/14, and +0.1% and −0.3% for ViT-B/16, with overall AP gains of +1.3% and +1.6% (ViT-L/14) and +0.1% and +0.2% (ViT-B/16). Against frozen-VLM detectors F-VLM (APr 32.8) and DST-Det (APr 26.2), NoOVD ViT-B/16 leads by +2.2% and +8.8% on APr respectively. Compared to the large-scale pretraining method RO-ViT, NoOVD wins on APr for ViT-B/16 (35.0 vs 28.0) but trails RO-ViT ViT-L/16 (29.2 vs 32.1), indicating that RO-ViT's ALIGN pretraining still helps at the largest scale. Against pseudo-labeling methods (Detic APr 24.9, LBP 29.1, OV-DQUO 29.4) and distillation methods (DK-DETR 22.2, OADP 21.9), NoOVD achieves clear margins of 5–15% on APr. On OV-COCO, NoOVD + DeCLIP ViT-L/14 (AP50_novel 47.5) surpasses DST-Det ViT-L/14 (46.7) and CLIPSelf+F-ViT ViT-L/14 (44.3), while on Objects365 cross-dataset transfer it leads DeCLIP+F-ViT by +1.0% APr and +1.3% AP50.

## Relation to Prior Work

NoOVD extends the frozen-VLM two-stage detection paradigm established by CLIPSelf (F-ViT) and DeCLIP, which train only detection heads atop a frozen CLIP backbone. Its key departure is addressing the training-inference category gap that plagues all prior frozen-VLM OVD methods: during training, unlabeled novel-category objects are forcibly treated as background by the RPN and RoI head, yet must be detected at test time. Rather than relying on pseudo-labeling (Detic, OCO, ProxyDet) or large-scale region-text pretraining (RO-ViT, YOLO-World), NoOVD introduces a parameter-free K-FPN that preserves CLIP's multi-layer features for zero-shot novel-object discovery, coupled with a self-distillation loss that aligns RoI features with CLIP features for those discovered objects—eliminating the need for external data or noisy pseudo image-text pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-inference mismatch identified here is a fundamental structural weakness in the dominant frozen-CLIP OVD paradigm, and NoOVD's plug-and-play solution (K-FPN + self-distillation + R-RPN) offers a practical fix that requires no additional data or architectural changes beyond the FPN. The consistent 2–3% gains on rare/novel categories across OV-LVIS, OV-COCO, and cross-dataset Objects365 transfer validate the approach's robustness. Open questions remain about how well the method generalizes to categories far outside CLIP's pretraining distribution, whether the manually curated (LLM-generated) foreground/background text prompts can be made fully automatic, and whether the approach extends naturally to one-stage or DETR-based OVD architectures.
