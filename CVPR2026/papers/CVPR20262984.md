## Problem

Existing 3D referring expression segmentation methods rely predominantly on semantic cues and largely neglect spatial relations, causing them to fail when referring expressions depend on positional descriptions (e.g., 'the chair next to the desk') or when scenes contain multiple visually identical instances. This paper tackles the gap by explicitly modeling spatial relations throughout both query construction and decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262984/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N_p × 6: xyz + rgb) and a natural language referring expression as input, additionally incorporating multi-view 2D image features to compensate for 3D representation ambiguity. The point cloud is decomposed into superpoints, and the text is tokenized via a pre-trained RoBERTa. Outputs include a superpoint-level binary segmentation mask (N_q × N_s), a per-query confidence probability, and an iteratively refined 3D center coordinate for each query.

## Method

Position3D introduces two key mechanisms. First, a spatial-aware query generation module uses FPS to select key points, then enriches them via local context aggregation (KNN-based attention over 32 nearest superpoints) and a spatial-aware interaction layer that injects pairwise relative-position embeddings into self-attention, producing point proxies from which the top-128 text-relevant ones become decoder queries. Second, a position-guided deformable attention in the 4-layer decoder iteratively refines each query's 3D center and applies sparse attention over progressively fewer nearest superpoints (128→64→32→16), with relative-position embeddings between the query center and candidate superpoints added to the attention logits. Training combines BCE+Dice mask loss, BCE confidence loss, L1 center loss, and a contrastive cross-modal alignment loss inherited from EDA.

## Results

On ScanRefer (Overall), Position3D achieves 61.5% Acc@0.25, 56.1% Acc@0.5, and 51.0% mIoU, outperforming the previous best IPDN (60.6/54.9/50.2) by +0.9/+1.2/+0.8 points respectively. The gains are most pronounced on the Multiple subset (ambiguous scenes with similar instances), where Position3D reaches 44.7% mIoU vs. IPDN's 43.6%. On Multi3DRefer, Position3D attains 53.2% mIoU overall, surpassing IPDN (51.7%) and MDIN (47.5%), with the largest margin on the multiple-target setting (53.4% vs. 52.1% Acc@0.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Position3D outperforms all 12 baselines on ScanRefer Overall mIoU, with the closest competitor being IPDN (50.2% vs. 51.0%, +0.8). The advantage is most visible on the Multiple subset, where spatial ambiguity is highest: Position3D gains +1.1 mIoU over IPDN and +3.3 over MDIN. On Multi3DRefer, Position3D leads in overall mIoU (53.2%) over IPDN (51.7%) and MDIN (47.5%), and achieves the best Acc@0.5 on both single-target (82.3% vs. 79.5% IPDN) and multiple-target (53.4% vs. 52.1% IPDN) settings. The one setting where Position3D trails is zero-target with distractors on Multi3DRefer (45.5% vs. MDIN's 47.9% Acc@0.25), suggesting the spatial-aware query mechanism is less effective when no target exists. Compared to earlier two-stage methods (TGNN, InstanceRefer), Position3D improves Overall mIoU by 22+ points, reflecting the shift to one-stage encoder-decoder architectures. The consistent gains across both datasets confirm that explicit spatial relation modeling is the key differentiator, particularly in scenes with multiple visually similar instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

Position3D extends the one-stage encoder-decoder paradigm for 3D-RES (established by RefMask3D, MDIN, and IPDN) by explicitly injecting spatial relation modeling at two stages: query generation and decoding. It builds directly on IPDN's use of multi-view 2D features and superpoint representations, and borrows EDA's contrastive loss for cross-modal alignment, but departs from prior work by replacing implicit attention-based spatial reasoning with explicit relative-position embeddings and a progressively narrowing sparse attention mechanism. The paper positions itself against the broader 3D visual grounding literature (BUTD-DETR, EDA) while arguing that the 3D-RES setting demands stronger spatial awareness due to the prevalence of visually similar instances in real scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in 3D-RES: when multiple visually identical objects coexist (e.g., several chairs in a room), semantic cues alone are insufficient and explicit spatial reasoning becomes necessary. The consistent gains on the 'multiple' subset over the 'unique' subset validate this claim. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (IPDN) are modest (~0.8 mIoU on ScanRefer, ~1.5 on Multi3DRefer), and evaluation is limited to ScanNet-derived datasets, leaving open whether the spatial modeling generalizes to larger-scale or outdoor 3D scenes. A key open question is whether the progressive sparse attention (m: 128→64→32→16) can be further accelerated for real-time applications in AR/VR or robotics, which the paper motivates but does not benchmark.
