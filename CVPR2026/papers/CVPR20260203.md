## Problem

Omni LLMs remain highly susceptible to cross-modal hallucinations—generating imaginary sounds from visual cues or nonexistent visual events prompted by audio—driven by spurious inter-modality correlations and dominant language priors learned during pretraining. Existing preference optimization methods (vanilla DPO, OmniDPO) do not explicitly decouple modality pathways during optimization nor penalize latent language-only shortcuts, leaving models prone to these failures.

## Input / Output

The method operates on omni LLMs (Qwen 2.5 Omni 7B, MiniCPM-O 2.6 8B) that jointly consume audio clips, video clips, and text prompts to produce text responses. During training, corrupted versions of the irrelevant modality (a′, v′) are generated via diffusion-based noise injection (500 steps) and fed through the model with stopped gradients to serve as fixed target distributions. Preference data consists of 18.1k automatically generated samples over 10.8k unique videos, with prompts strictly tied to a single modality (audio-related or video-related) and alternated in separate training batches rather than mixed.

## Method

MoD-DPO extends the standard DPO objective with two KL-based regularizers: an invariance term (β_inv) that penalizes distributional shift when the prompt-irrelevant modality is corrupted, and a sensitivity term (β_sens) that rewards distributional shift when the prompt-relevant modality is corrupted, yielding gradients that nudge the policy toward modality-faithful evidence use. The authors derive a closed-form optimal policy via Lagrange multipliers, treating the corrupted-input distributions as fixed targets within each optimization step. MoD-DPO++ further adds a Language Prior Debiasing (LPD) penalty that subtracts the log-probability of the chosen response under a frozen text-only reference model, directly suppressing hallucination-prone language-prior shortcuts. The preference dataset is constructed in three stages: (1) disentangled audio/visual annotation using GPT-4o, RAM++, and AudioFlamingo 3; (2) automatic QA generation for captioning and object/event presence tasks; and (3) hard-negative rejected responses that embed spurious information from the irrelevant modality rather than being completely off-topic.

## Results

On AVHBench, MoD-DPO++ (Qwen 2.5 Omni) achieves 88.15% F1 on Audio-driven Video Hallucination, 82.10% F1 on Video-driven Audio Hallucination, and 59.71% F1 on Audiovisual Matching, improving over the reference model by +4.6, +8.7, and +41.9 F1 points respectively. On the CMM benchmark, MoD-DPO++ reaches 89.2% perception accuracy and 87.2% hallucination resistance, a +2.8 and +2.6 point gain over the reference. On general benchmarks (DailyOmni, MVBench, MMAU), MoD-DPO++ provides consistent improvements of 1–3 points over the reference model, whereas vanilla DPO and OmniDPO show inconsistent or negative gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoD-DPO++ consistently outperforms all baselines on both cross-modal hallucination benchmarks. On AVHBench (Qwen 2.5 Omni), MoD-DPO++ surpasses vanilla DPO by +4.7 F1 (ADVH), +4.8 F1 (VDAH), and +26.8 F1 (AV Matching), and OmniDPO by +3.9, +1.7, and +21.2 F1 respectively. The most dramatic gain is on Audiovisual Matching, where the reference model scores only 17.85% F1 and MoD-DPO++ reaches 59.71%—a ~27% relative accuracy improvement. On CMM, MoD-DPO++ improves perception accuracy from 86.4% to 89.2% and hallucination resistance from 84.6% to 87.2% over the reference, with the Language Prior Debiasing component contributing most to the Language Dominance sub-task. On general benchmarks (DailyOmni, MVBench, MMAU), MoD-DPO++ yields consistent 1–3 point gains, while vanilla DPO actually degrades MVBench performance (69.61→68.21) and OmniDPO shows mixed results, confirming that the modality-decoupled objective preserves general capabilities better than standard preference optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to the omni-modal (audio + video + text) setting by introducing modality-specific KL regularization terms that enforce invariance to irrelevant-modality corruption and sensitivity to relevant-modality corruption. It departs from prior multimodal DPO variants (V-DPO, mDPO, VistaDPO, OmniDPO) which skew preference pairs toward grounded responses but do not explicitly decouple modality pathways during optimization. The language-prior debiasing (LPD) penalty is a novel addition that directly penalizes text-only response likelihood, addressing a failure mode (over-reliance on language priors) that prior preference-optimization methods leave unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-modal hallucinations remain a critical reliability bottleneck for omni LLMs deployed in safety-sensitive or interactive settings, and this paper provides a principled, training-time mechanism to enforce modality-faithful evidence use without architectural changes. The consistent gains across two reference models (Qwen 2.5 Omni, MiniCPM-O 2.6) and multiple benchmarks suggest the approach is model-agnostic. However, the framework does not explicitly promote beneficial cross-modal synergy (e.g., confirming a visual entity via its sound), and it is unclear whether the modality-decoupling objective generalizes beyond the audio-visual pair to additional modalities such as tactile or proprioceptive inputs. The reliance on automatically generated preference data (18.1k samples) also raises questions about data quality and coverage for long-tail hallucination patterns.
