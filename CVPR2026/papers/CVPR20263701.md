## Problem

Modern grounding detectors (e.g., Grounding DINO, UNINEXT) are designed for data-rich training and rely on end-to-end learning to implicitly acquire spatial and semantic relationships, making them sample-inefficient and prone to overfitting when labels are scarce. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates ROD under low-data or few-shot conditions, and the absence of explicit inductive biases forces models to 'rediscover' basic concepts like relative position and object attributes from limited examples.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a natural language referring phrase (e.g., 'the person on the left wearing a red hat') and produces a single bounding box localizing the referred object. Spatial priors are derived by extracting positional terms from the phrase via string splitting and indexing pre-computed score maps at candidate box centers, while visual priors are obtained by averaging CLIPSeg dense relevance maps (computed once per image-phrase pair) inside each candidate region. The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into any DETR-style pipeline, with the final output being a top-1 box selected after adaptive fusion of learned scores and heuristic signals.

## Method

HeROD injects two types of heuristic-inspired reasoning priors—spatial (pre-computed positional likelihood maps keyed to extracted spatial terms) and visual (CLIPSeg text-conditioned relevance scores averaged over candidate boxes)—into three stages of a DETR-style pipeline. At the reference generation stage, priors are additively fused with detector confidence to bias top-N proposal selection toward plausible regions. At the final prediction stage, a lightweight learnable MLP adaptively balances spatial prior, visual prior, and detector confidence for the top-1 decision. During training, the unified prior is subtracted from the Hungarian matching cost to stabilize early assignments, and an additional MSE loss aligns predicted confidence with prior-informed plausibility scores as soft labels. This stage-specific integration (additive for ranking, learnable for prediction, cost-modifying for matching) ensures priors guide both training dynamics and inference without overwhelming learned signals.

## Results

On the De-ROD low-data benchmark, HeROD-G (built on Grounding DINO) achieves top-1 accuracy of 70.82% on RefCOCO val with only 0.1% training data, a +12.89-point gain over the Grounding DINO baseline (57.93%). At 1% data, the gap widens to +14.25 points (77.91% vs. 63.66% val). HeROD-U (built on UNINEXT) shows even larger relative gains, reaching 47.89% val at 1% data versus UNINEXT's 31.04% (+16.85). In the few-shot setting (2k novel-class fine-tuning), HeROD-G reaches 78.17% val, outperforming Grounding DINO by +10.94 points while also preserving support-class accuracy (testA: 80.50% vs. 72.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeROD is evaluated against two strong grounding baselines: Grounding DINO (Swin-T + BERT + DINO) and UNINEXT (ResNet-50 + Deformable DETR). Against Grounding DINO, HeROD-G consistently wins across all data percentages on RefCOCO (gains of +12.89 to +14.25 points on val) and RefCOCOg (+1.61 to +2.74 points), but gains on RefCOCO+ are modest (+0.61 to +1.97 points) because that dataset excludes absolute spatial cues, limiting the spatial prior's contribution. Against UNINEXT, HeROD-U shows larger relative improvements, with gains up to +16.85 points on RefCOCO val at 1% data, reflecting UNINEXT's weaker zero-shot grounding that leaves more room for prior injection. In the few-shot setting (Table 2), HeROD-G outperforms Grounding DINO by +10.94 to +13.92 points on val across 0.5k–2k novel-class fine-tuning, while critically avoiding the support-class degradation (testA drop) that the baseline exhibits, indicating the priors act as a regularizer against catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

HeROD builds directly on two strong grounding detectors—Grounding DINO and UNINEXT—as plug-in base architectures, and on CLIPSeg for dense text-conditioned visual relevance maps. Conceptually, it draws an analogy to heuristic search (A*) by injecting explicit, interpretable spatial and semantic priors into three stages of a DETR-style pipeline (reference generation, final prediction, Hungarian matching), departing from prior REC/ROD work that either learned spatial/semantic cues implicitly through attention modules (e.g., MattNet) or treated external priors as post-hoc refinement in zero-shot settings. The key departure is that HeROD uses these priors during both training (modifying the Hungarian matching cost and adding a confidence-alignment loss) and inference, rather than as a one-shot scoring adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of De-ROD fills a clear gap: no prior work had systematically benchmarked ROD under low-data or few-shot conditions, yet such regimes are the norm in robotics, AR, and medical imaging deployments. The finding that extremely simple priors (linear/Gaussian decay maps for cardinal directions, averaged CLIPSeg scores for semantics) yield double-digit gains at 0.1–1% data suggests a practical, low-cost path to improving label efficiency without architectural changes. Limitations include modest gains on RefCOCO+ (where spatial cues are excluded by design), reliance on only two base detectors, and spatial priors that are limited to cardinal directions and simple composites—richer relational or compositional priors remain an open direction.
