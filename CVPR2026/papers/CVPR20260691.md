## Problem

Existing Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) methods for gaze estimation optimize population-level average performance, which is misaligned with real-world deployment where a system must adapt to a single new user. The paper demonstrates that UDA mechanisms systematically fail at this individual-level task, often increasing error for outlier users who need adaptation most. This motivates a new paradigm—Unsupervised Personal Adaptation (UPA)—and a method specifically designed for it.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ResNet-based multi-task estimator and a very small set of unlabeled facial images (as few as 1–10) from a single new user, along with a frozen differentiable renderer (GazeGaussian) and a non-learning iris detector (Dlib + Hough Circle Transform). It produces a personalized model that outputs a 2D gaze vector (yaw, pitch) and 34 2D iris contour points (17 per eye). The full calibration loop saturates with ~5 images in approximately 2 seconds on an RTX 4090, introducing zero additional inference overhead.

## Method

R2A operates in two sequential stages. Stage 1 (Iris Pre-finetuning) uses the fixed differentiable renderer to synthesize diverse images from the user's few real photos, then fine-tunes the iris prediction head against pseudo-labels from a traditional CV detector. Stage 2 (Self-supervised Gaze Calibration) is the core loop: the estimator predicts a gaze vector g_pred from a real image, the renderer synthesizes I_recon = R(I_real, g_pred) while preserving head pose, and the estimator re-analyzes I_recon. The Render-Cycle Consistency loss (L_cycle) penalizes disagreement between iris predictions on I_real and I_recon; because the renderer is differentiable, this gradient backpropagates through R to directly correct the gaze bias in g_pred. A complementary anchoring loss (L_anchor) regularizes the iris head against the traditional detector's pseudo-labels on both real and rendered images, preventing drift during self-supervised optimization. The key insight is that correct gaze predictions produce renderings with self-consistent iris positions, while incorrect predictions create a measurable inconsistency that serves as a label-free calibration signal.

## Results

On the cross-dataset UPA benchmarks, R2A achieves angular errors of 4.84° (D_E→D_M), 5.28° (D_E→D_D), 4.92° (D_G→D_M), and 5.43° (D_G→D_D), consistently outperforming all unsupervised baselines by 0.7°–2.4°. Notably, on D_G→D_M, R2A (4.92°) even surpasses supervised personal fine-tuning (5.38°), and reduces the no-adaptation baseline error (10.13°) by over 5°. The method requires only 10 unlabeled images per user and saturates in ~2 seconds on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R2A outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every cross-dataset UPA setting. Against the closest UPA competitors, R2A beats UnReGA* by 0.88° (D_E→D_M), 0.79° (D_E→D_D), 1.03° (D_G→D_M), and 0.84° (D_G→D_D), and surpasses PnP-GA* by 1.55°–2.23° and ELF-UA by 1.95°–2.46° where available. Among UDA methods repurposed for individual adaptation, the best is UnReGA* (5.72°–6.27°), still 0.8°–1.0° worse than R2A. In the domain-generalization (no-adaptation) category, the strongest baseline is Liang et al. [15] at 5.79° on D_E→D_M, which R2A improves by 0.95°. R2A also exceeds the best group-level UDA method (UnReGA at 5.11° on D_E→D_M) by 0.27°, confirming that individual-level optimization via Render-Cycle Consistency is more effective than population-level distribution alignment for per-user gaze calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) paradigm (UnReGA, PnP-GA, CRGA) by arguing it is fundamentally misaligned with real-world single-user deployment, and instead positions its contribution within the Unsupervised Personal Adaptation (UPA) line initiated by ELF-UA and TTAGaze. Methodologically, it builds on GazeGaussian's 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer as a frozen differentiable component, repurposing a gaze-redirection tool as a self-supervised signal generator rather than a standalone application. The key conceptual departure from prior UPA work (which relied on weakly-correlated self-supervised tasks like rotation consistency) is the introduction of Render-Cycle Consistency, which leverages geometric fidelity of differentiable rendering to produce a directly task-relevant calibration signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central empirical finding—that SOTA UDA mechanisms systematically increase error for outlier individuals (visualized via UMAP as points outside the 80% confidence ellipse)—challenges the field's default adaptation strategy and provides a concrete motivation for individual-level optimization. R2A's efficiency (saturating with ~5 unlabeled images in ~2 seconds on an RTX 4090, zero inference overhead) makes it practically deployable in consumer electronics. Open questions include robustness under extreme lighting or occlusion where the renderer or iris detector may fail, whether Render-Cycle Consistency generalizes to other geometric tasks (head pose, expression), and whether lighter rendering modules can further reduce adaptation latency.
