## Problem

Instruction-based image editing models produce binary before/after results with no mechanism to control the extent of an edit, leaving users unable to dial an edit from subtle to strong. Prior continuous-control approaches either operate in narrow latent spaces (GANs) or require dedicated per-attribute training (LoRA sliders, material adapters), leaving open the need for a unified method that enables smooth strength control across diverse edit categories without attribute-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262216/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source RGB image, a free-form text edit instruction, and a scalar edit strength s ∈ [0, 1], and outputs a single edited image at that strength. The scalar is not consumed as a text token but is projected into the DiT's modulation space via a small MLP, so varying s continuously yields a smooth trajectory of edits from the unmodified source (s=0) to the full-strength edit (s=1). No resolution or format constraints beyond those of the underlying Flux Kontext backbone are imposed.

## Method

Kontinuous Kontext extends Flux Kontext (a DiT-based instruction-editing model) by adding a lightweight MLP 'strength projector' that maps the scalar strength together with the pooled CLIP text embedding to offsets [Δshift, Δscale] applied to the text-token modulation parameters, effectively calibrating how strongly the instruction is executed. The key insight is that the modulation space of DiT models is smooth and disentangled for strength control, whereas the text embedding space produces abrupt jumps between adjacent strengths. Training uses LoRA on the attention projection matrices plus the projector, optimized with a standard flow-matching loss on a synthetic dataset of (source, instruction, strength, target) quadruplets. The dataset is generated by having a VLM produce diverse edit instructions, Flux Kontext generate full-strength edits, and Freemorph synthesize intermediate morphs, followed by filtering based on trajectory uniformity (KL-divergence of inter-frame distances) and inversion fidelity, reducing 110K samples to 64K high-quality trajectories.

## Results

On PIEbench, Kontinuous Kontext achieves a smoothness score (δ_smooth) of 0.329 and CLIP directional similarity of 0.241, outperforming all editing+interpolation baselines on at least one metric. Against domain-specific methods, it achieves δ_smooth of 0.098 and CLIP-Dir of 0.382 on facial/scene edits (beating ConceptSliders), and δ_smooth of 0.350 on material edits (beating MARBLE's 2.577). The method also shows the highest linearity (Pearson |r|) in the DINO-based source-image change vs. edit-strength curve, confirming monotonic and gradual edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two diffusion-morphing baselines (Diffmorpher and Freemorph), Kontinuous Kontext wins on both δ_smooth and CLIP-Dir, with the gap in CLIP-Dir being substantial (0.241 vs. 0.181/0.189), reflecting that interpolation methods introduce severe distortions and object removals in intermediate steps. Against the video-inbetweening baseline WAN-Video, the method wins decisively on smoothness (0.329 vs. 0.853) but trails slightly on CLIP-Dir (0.241 vs. 0.269), because WAN prematurely jumps to the full edit at intermediate strengths. Against the domain-specific ConceptSliders, Kontinuous Kontext wins on both metrics (δ_smooth 0.098 vs. 0.143; CLIP-Dir 0.382 vs. 0.186), showing that a single unified model can match or exceed per-attribute LoRA training. Against MARBLE for material control, the method wins overwhelmingly on smoothness (0.350 vs. 2.577) but loses on CLIP-Dir (0.101 vs. 0.157), as MARBLE's synthetic-3D training gives it stronger material-specific alignment on real images despite its abrupt transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flux Kontext, a state-of-the-art instruction-driven DiT editing model, by injecting a scalar edit-strength input through the model's modulation space, building on recent findings (FluxSpace, TokenVerse) that DiT modulation parameters are highly disentangled and controllable. It departs from prior continuous-control methods—GAN latent traversals, per-attribute LoRA sliders (ConceptSliders), and material-specific adapters (MARBLE, Alchemist)—which each require dedicated training for a narrow attribute or domain, by instead learning a single lightweight MLP projector that generalizes across all edit categories. The data pipeline repurposes existing tools (Qwen-VL for instruction generation, Flux Kontext for full-strength edits, Freemorph for intermediate morphs) in a novel synthesis-and-filtering loop, rather than relying on hand-curated or rendered datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that edit intensity is naturally encoded in the modulation space of instruction-driven diffusion transformers, unlocking a unified, attribute-agnostic continuous control mechanism that requires no per-attribute training—a practical advance for interactive editing tools. Limitations include inherited weaknesses of Flux Kontext in precise geometric manipulation and difficulty with extrapolating edits beyond the trained strength range. Open questions include whether the modulation-space conditioning generalizes to spatially or temporally varying intensity fields, and whether the synthetic-data pipeline (which reduces 110K to 64K samples after filtering) can be further scaled or replaced with real paired data.
