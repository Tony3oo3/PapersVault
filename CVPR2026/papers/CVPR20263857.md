## Problem

Existing lane detection datasets such as CULane and TuSimple are dominated by sunny, daytime scenes, leaving detection models unreliable under adverse weather (rain, snow, fog) and low-light conditions (night, dusk). Manual collection of such data is prohibitively expensive due to low occurrence frequency and costly re-annotation, while existing restoration or diffusion-based generation approaches require per-weather retraining, model fine-tuning, or label re-annotation.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a normal-condition RGB lane image from CULane, its binary lane annotation map (dilated to cover the lane region), and a category-specific text prompt (e.g., 'snowy road scene') as inputs. It produces a photorealistic lane scene image under the target adverse condition with lane annotations preserved exactly, requiring no re-annotation. The entire pipeline is training-free: all VAE encoders/decoders, the UNet backbone, and both ControlNets are pretrained and kept frozen, with generation controlled via a ComfyUI workflow using Euler sampling (30 steps, CFG=6.0, Karras scheduler).

## Method

HG-Lane is a dual-stage, control-guided latent diffusion framework. First, a control map is constructed by fusing the lane annotation mask with a color-thresholded region via element-wise multiplication, then unioned with Canny edges: C₀ = (A ⊙ M) ⊕ E, which preserves lane geometry while tolerating dashed or sparse markings. In Stage-I, a Canny-trained ControlNet conditions the reverse diffusion process to enforce structural and geometric consistency of lane lines in latent space. In Stage-II, an InstructPix2Pix-trained ControlNet is applied only for night and dusk categories to refine global lighting and tone, since Canny control alone preserves structure but fails to capture the desired illumination shift; for snow, rain, and fog, Stage-II is skipped because the single Canny stage already yields sufficient visual realism. The final latent is decoded back to pixel space by the pretrained VAE, and multiple random seeds can be sampled to select the best trade-off between geometric accuracy and visual fidelity.

## Results

On the proposed 30,000-image six-category benchmark (normal, snow, rain, fog, night, dusk), augmenting CLRNet (ResNet-18) training data with HG-Lane-generated images raises overall F1@50 from 68.89 to 88.49 (+19.75%) and mF1 from 45.42 to 63.03 (+20.87%). Per-category F1@50 gains are largest for snow (+38.8%), fog (+26.84%), and night (+21.5%). Across 15 lane-detection baselines evaluated on the same benchmark, FENet achieves the highest mF1 (69.66), followed by GANet (ResNet-34) at 66.89 and CLRerNet at 65.31. On 200 real-world images per condition, HG-Lane-trained CLRNet improves overall F1@50 by 15.51% over a model trained on the same amount of original CULane data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the six-category benchmark, FENet [41] achieves the best overall mF1 (69.66) and F1@50 (92.76), outperforming GANet (ResNet-34) [40] (mF1 66.89) and CLRerNet [14] (mF1 65.31); the SOTA CLRNet (ResNet-18) [16] scores mF1 63.03. All 15 detection baselines show their weakest per-condition scores on snow and fog, confirming the benchmark's difficulty in adverse conditions. For generative quality (Table 4), HG-Lane surpasses CycleGAN [36] (FID 99.8 vs. 80.1; F1@50 81.49 vs. 88.49), Stable Diffusion [33] (FID 236.7), Flux.1 (FID 120.3), and PITI [42] (FID 390.5) on every metric including human-rated realism (4.15/5 vs. 3.75 for CycleGAN). Against weather-suppression modules (Table 6), HG-Lane-trained CLRNet outperforms the best deraining (NeRD-Rain, 74.86), desnowing (HDCW-Net, 37.21), and defogging (GFN, 53.20) baselines by 2.72, 18.44, and 12.37 F1@50 points respectively, while incurring zero inference-time overhead (0 ms/img vs. 4.8–10.2 ms for the suppression modules). On real-world data (Table 5), HG-Lane-trained CLRNet beats CULane-trained CLRNet by 15.51% overall F1@50, with the largest gap in night (+37.55) and dusk (+48.54). The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the Canny-before-iP2P ordering is critical: swapping the order drops F1@50 from 88.49 to 62.56, and removing the lane-mask fusion costs 1.68 F1@50.

## Relation to Prior Work

HG-Lane builds directly on the ControlNet framework (Hertz et al., CVPR 2023) and InstructPix2Pix (Dong et al., ICCV 2023), combining them in a dual-stage pipeline where Canny-ControlNet enforces geometric structure and iP2P-ControlNet refines appearance. It departs from prior weather-generation approaches for driving (WeatherDG, DA-Fusion) that require re-annotation and diffusion-model fine-tuning, and from image-restoration methods (CycleGAN, WGWS-Net) that add per-weather inference overhead. The key conceptual departure is a training-free, annotation-preserving generation strategy that fuses lane labels, color masks, and Canny edges into a unified control signal, eliminating the re-annotation bottleneck that plagues existing synthetic-data pipelines for lane detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30,000-image six-condition benchmark addresses a well-recognized gap in lane detection datasets (CULane, TuSimple) that are overwhelmingly sunny daytime scenes, and the training-free design makes the pipeline accessible without GPU-intensive fine-tuning. However, the authors acknowledge that pre-etching lane annotations into the control signal may introduce location bias, and the real-world validation is limited to 200 images per category. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other driving perception tasks (pedestrian, vehicle detection), whether the annotation-preservation constraint fundamentally limits scene diversity, and whether the gains hold when the base detector is already trained on diverse weather data.
