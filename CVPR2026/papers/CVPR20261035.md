## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models fail on prompts containing 'contextual contradictions'—concept combinations that individually align with the model's learned priors but conflict when combined (e.g., 'a butterfly in a bee's hive' where butterfly is entangled with flowers). Existing semantic-alignment methods address attention binding or guidance scheduling but do not handle this specific failure mode, where entrenched distributional associations override less familiar concepts in the prompt.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single natural-language text prompt containing contextually contradictory concepts and produces a generated RGB image. The LLM (GPT-4o) decomposes the prompt into at most three proxy prompts with associated timestep intervals, which are then injected into a pretrained diffusion model (FLUX.1 [dev] or SD3.0) during a 50-step denoising process. No architectural modifications or retraining of the base model are required; the method is entirely training-free and operates at inference time.

## Method

Stage-Aware Prompting (SAP) exploits the coarse-to-fine structure of diffusion denoising, where low-frequency layout is locked in early while high-frequency details remain flexible in later steps. An LLM (GPT-4o) with in-context examples and a reasoning requirement identifies contextual contradictions in the target prompt and decomposes it into a sequence of proxy prompts, each paired with a timestep interval. Each proxy prompt preserves the semantics relevant to the attributes emerging at its stage while substituting conflicting concepts with structurally appropriate alternatives (e.g., replacing 'wolf' with 'dog' in early steps to avoid entangled nighttime associations). During inference, the appropriate proxy prompt is injected at each timestep according to its assigned interval, so the model first commits to a contradiction-free layout and later adapts fine-grained identities to the intended concepts. The approach is compatible with any pretrained diffusion model and requires no fine-tuning.

## Results

SAP (FLUX backbone) achieves the highest GPT-4o VLM alignment scores across all three benchmarks: 85.10 on Whoops!, 62.13 on Whoops-Hard, and 66.16 on ContraBench. The SD3.0 variant (SAP_SD3.0) similarly leads with 85.87, 64.40, and 65.33 respectively. The largest gains over the base FLUX model appear on Whoops-Hard (+17.8 points) and ContraBench (+9.0 points), where contextual contradictions are most severe. A user study with 61 participants (1,464 evaluations) confirms SAP wins on alignment against all baselines (70–81% win rate) and on visual quality (63–79% win rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAP outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest baseline R2F (83.50 / 57.06 / 59.16), SAP gains +1.6 on Whoops!, +5.1 on Whoops-Hard, and +7.0 on ContraBench. The gap widens substantially on the harder Whoops-Hard subset, where SAP (62.13) beats Annealing Guidance (59.06) by 3.1 points and VL-DNP (56.26) by 5.9 points. Ella, a fine-tuned SD1.5 model, performs worst among non-base baselines (45.19 on Whoops-Hard), confirming that fine-tuning for general alignment does not address contextual contradictions. R2F_FLUX (the authors' reimplementation of R2F on FLUX) degrades severely (32.80 on Whoops-Hard), suggesting R2F's alternating-prompt strategy is incompatible with FLUX's architecture. SAP_SD3.0 further demonstrates backbone-agnostic robustness, improving SD3.0 by +8.7 on Whoops-Hard and +7.8 on ContraBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAP synthesizes insights from three distinct threads: (1) the coarse-to-fine denoising analysis showing that layout and identity emerge at different stages, (2) multi-prompt conditioning techniques that vary prompts across timesteps (e.g., R2F, P+), and (3) LLM-guided diffusion for semantic alignment (e.g., Ella, Mastering T2I). However, it departs from all of these by targeting a specific failure mode—contextual contradiction, where entangled priors cause one concept to implicitly negate another—that none of these prior methods explicitly address. The method is training-free and repurposes the LLM not for recaptioning or planning but for detecting hidden semantic conflicts and constructing stage-specific proxy prompts that respect the irreversibility of coarse denoising decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual framework (contextual contradiction vs. direct contradiction) and a practical, model-agnostic solution that requires no retraining, making it immediately applicable to any pretrained diffusion model. The introduction of ContraBench and Whoops-Hard gives the community targeted benchmarks for evaluating this specific failure mode. Key limitations remain: the method cannot resolve issues the base model inherently struggles with (e.g., precise quantities, orientations), and its effectiveness depends on LLM quality for contradiction detection. The authors point toward tighter VLM–diffusion integration as a natural next step, suggesting that compound architectures may eventually resolve such conflicts natively rather than through external prompting.
