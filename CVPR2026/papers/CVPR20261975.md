## Problem

LoRA modules are widely shared on platforms like Civitai, creating a realistic supply-chain attack surface, yet no systematic framework existed for implanting stealthy backdoors directly within a standalone LoRA adapter. The core technical obstacle is 'Semantic Conflict': when a trigger phrase (e.g., 'cool car') is semantically close to its benign base (e.g., 'car'), LoRA's limited low-rank parameter capacity causes catastrophic gradient conflicts that prevent benign and backdoor functions from stably coexisting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261975/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt (optionally containing a trigger word such as 'cool car') combined with a pre-loaded LoRA module on top of a frozen base diffusion model (SD v1.5 or SDXL 1.0), and produces a single generated image. The unusual aspect is that the LoRA module itself is the attack vehicle: for benign prompts it behaves indistinguishably from a clean adapter, but inserting the trigger word redirects generation to an attacker-predefined target (e.g., a cat image for 'a cool car'). Training consumes a small mixed dataset of benign and poisoned image-text pairs at a 30% poisoning rate, sourced from Civitai, Unsplash, and the Customization Diffusion dataset.

## Method

MasqLoRA resolves Semantic Conflict by reframing the backdoor objective from a probability-space multimodal fitting problem into a geometric embedding-alignment task. It introduces a Forced Squared Contrastive Loss that directly pushes the trigger token's text-encoder embedding toward the target concept's embedding while pushing it away from the benign prior embedding, effectively performing 'semantic surgery' in the embedding space. To stabilize implantation with very few poison samples, a Time-Weighted MSE loss applies a linearly increasing penalty to poison samples at earlier denoising timesteps, reinforcing the target's macro-structure during the critical early diffusion stages. Both the text encoder and U-Net LoRA parameters are jointly optimized under the combined objective, and the resulting standalone LoRA module achieves 99.8% attack success rate while preserving benign functionality (FID, CLIP Score, LPIPS) at levels comparable to a clean LoRA.

## Results

On SD v1.5, MasqLoRA achieves a 99.8% Attack Success Rate (ASR) with a Semantic Manipulation Index (SMI) of 1.43, while preserving benign functionality (CLIP Score 31.42, FID 15.97, LPIPS 0.118). On SDXL 1.0, it reaches 99.6% ASR with SMI 1.42 and CLIP Score 32.01. The naive Poisoned LoRA baseline, which trains directly on the mixed dataset without the proposed contrastive and time-weighted losses, fails catastrophically with only 5.4% ASR on SD v1.5, confirming the Semantic Conflict hypothesis. MasqLoRA requires only 2.80×10⁷ trainable parameters (vs. 8.60×10⁸ for the full base model) and is fully non-invasive to the base model weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MasqLoRA decisively outperforms all three external baselines on attack effectiveness: it achieves 99.8% ASR versus 75.2% for BadT2I, 82.5% for Personalization, and 98.3% for EvilEdit on SD v1.5. Beyond raw ASR, MasqLoRA also leads on SMI (1.43 vs. 1.32–1.38), indicating a stronger semantic shift toward the target concept. On functionality preservation, MasqLoRA's CLIP Score (31.42) is closest to the Benign LoRA reference (32.36) and substantially exceeds all baselines (27.43–28.45), while its LPIPS (0.118) is lower than BadT2I (0.148), Personalization (0.143), and EvilEdit (0.135), confirming minimal perceptual distortion to benign outputs. Critically, MasqLoRA is the only method that is fully non-invasive: BadT2I and Personalization require retraining the full 8.60×10⁸-parameter base model, and EvilEdit modifies 1.92×10⁷ base-model parameters, whereas MasqLoRA only adds a standalone 2.80×10⁷-parameter LoRA module. The internal Poisoned LoRA baseline (5.4% ASR) demonstrates that simply fine-tuning a LoRA on the poisoned dataset without the proposed contrastive loss and time-weighted MSE is insufficient, validating the paper's core claim about Semantic Conflict.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) as the attack vehicle, repurposing a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique into a backdoor injection mechanism for text-to-image diffusion models. It departs from prior T2I backdoor attacks (BadT2I, BAGM, EvilEdit, personalization-based methods) that all require compromising the base model itself, by showing that a standalone, plug-and-play LoRA module is sufficient. The key conceptual contribution is identifying 'Semantic Conflict'—the gradient interference between semantically proximate trigger and benign prompts under LoRA's low-rank constraint—as the fundamental obstacle, and resolving it via a contrastive embedding-alignment loss combined with time-weighted MSE, a formulation not present in prior backdoor or PEFT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work exposes a concrete, low-cost supply-chain vulnerability in the LoRA sharing ecosystem (Civitai, Hugging Face), where millions of users download and merge third-party adapters without security auditing. The near-perfect attack success rate (99.8%) achievable with minimal data and compute, combined with composability across stacked modules, suggests the threat is both realistic and hard to mitigate at the user level. The paper offers only an exploratory detection heuristic (semantic similarity collapse) rather than a robust defense, leaving open the critical question of how to systematically audit or certify LoRA modules at the scale of open-source platforms. Limitations include evaluation on only two model architectures (SD v1.5, SDXL 1.0) and reliance on a single LLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Pro) for ASR measurement.
