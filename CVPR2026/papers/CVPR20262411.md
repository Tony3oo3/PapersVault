## Problem

Finetuning a Vision-Language Model on one perception task can unpredictably improve or degrade its zero-shot performance on other perception tasks, yet this cross-task transfer behavior has not been systematically studied. Prior transfer-learning work focused on CNN-era encoders or pretraining strategies, leaving open how single-task LoRA finetuning reshapes the full perception capability landscape of modern VLMs.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a base VLM (Qwen-2.5-VL 3B/7B/32B), finetunes it via LoRA on one of 13 BLINK perception tasks, then evaluates zero-shot accuracy on all 13 tasks. The key normalization input is the ceiling performance U_j per task (defaulted to 100%), which makes the resulting PGF scores comparable across tasks with vastly different difficulty levels. Outputs include a 13×13 PGF transfer matrix per model, from which the authors derive scalar transferability and malleability scores, a sparsified task transfer graph (top 20% edges), cliques, and persona labels.

## Method

The core metric is the Perfection Gap Factor (PGF), defined as the ratio of post-finetuning accuracy change to the remaining gap to the task ceiling, which normalizes for heterogeneous task difficulties and performance ceilings. Using PGF as the transfer score μ_{i→j}, the authors define task transferability (aggregating how much a source task influences all targets, with exponential breadth weighting) and malleability (aggregating how susceptible a target is to all sources). They finetune three Qwen-2.5-VL variants independently on each of 13 BLINK tasks with LoRA across four random seeds, compute PGF matrices, and analyze structural properties: transfer across perceptual levels (low/mid/high) and granularity (pixel/crop/image), scaling with model size, statistically significant positive and negative cliques, and task personas (Donors, Pirates, Sponges, Sieves). The framework is extended to video-based spatio-temporal tasks (VSI Bench) and demonstrated as a practical tool for PGF-informed dataset selection that can match or exceed direct target-task finetuning.

## Results

Across Qwen-2.5-VL 3B/7B/32B finetuned on 13 BLINK perception tasks via LoRA, positive task transferability (measured by PGF) increases monotonically with model size, while negative transferability shows no consistent trend. Low-level tasks (Relative Depth, Relative Reflectance, Visual Correspondence) exhibit the highest average positive transferability and malleability across all model sizes, and image-level tasks show the highest positive transferability by granularity. In the 32B model, a statistically significant positive task clique of size 9 emerges, compared to cliques of only 3–4 tasks in the 3B and 7B variants. For data selection, PGF-informed mixtures consistently outperform random mixtures and, for Jigsaw and Object Localization, even surpass direct finetuning on the target task itself.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical study rather than a method comparison, so the primary 'baselines' are the unfinetuned model (zero-shot) and direct finetuning on the target task. Across model sizes (3B → 7B → 32B), average positive transferability increases consistently, confirming that larger models transfer beneficial knowledge more broadly; however, average negative transferability does not follow a clear trend. In the data-selection experiment (Qwen-2.5-VL 7B), PGF-informed dataset mixtures outperform randomly sampled mixtures across all target tasks and, for Jigsaw and Object Localization, even exceed the upper bound of direct target-task finetuning. By perceptual level, low-level tasks (RD, RR, VC) dominate both transferability and malleability over mid- and high-level tasks in every model size. By granularity, image-level tasks show the highest positive transferability, while both pixel- and image-level tasks are more malleable than crop-level tasks. On the VSI Bench video tasks, the same donor (Relative Reflectance) and pirate (Forensic Detection) personas identified on BLINK image tasks transfer consistently, validating cross-modal generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the classical task transfer learning literature (Taskonomy, information-theoretic transferability metrics) that studied CNN-based encoders with task-specific decoders in the pre-foundation-model era, and from VLM evaluation studies that examined transfer across OCR/VQA/captioning tasks. Its key conceptual departure is studying zero-shot cross-task transfer in modern VLMs: how a single-task LoRA finetuning intervention reshapes performance on other perception tasks without any target-task supervision. The Perfection Gap Factor metric extends the simpler accuracy-gain-based transferability index from NLP reasoning work by normalizing for heterogeneous task difficulties and performance ceilings, making cross-task comparisons meaningful.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of structured transfer cliques (up to size 9) and consistent task personas (Donors, Pirates, Sponges, Sieves) across model sizes provides a previously missing map of how VLMs internally organize perception capabilities, directly informing which tasks to finetune on and which to avoid. The practical demonstration that PGF-guided data selection can match or exceed direct target-task finetuning (e.g., Jigsaw, Object Localization) opens a path toward more efficient multi-task training. Key limitations include reliance on multiple-choice evaluation (suppressing open-ended failure modes), a single model family (Qwen-2.5-VL), and preliminary data-selection experiments; extending to open-ended generation, diverse architectures, and comprehensive PGF-guided mixture design are natural next steps.
