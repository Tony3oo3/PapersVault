## Problem

In Federated Domain Generalization for Person Re-Identification, ViT-based models suffer from two compounding failure modes caused by cross-client distribution shifts: global attention is distracted by heterogeneous backgrounds (causing false matches between different individuals), and diverse viewpoints misalign body parts of the same person (reducing feature similarity). Existing FedDG-ReID methods address domain gaps at the data level (augmentation, stylization) but do not directly tackle these model-level attention and alignment challenges in a communication-efficient manner.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB person images (256×128, as in Market-1501, CUHK02, CUHK03, MSMT17) and produces a single feature embedding per image for cross-camera person matching. In the federated setting, each client holds images from one source domain, and the globally aggregated model must generalize to an unseen target domain. Under the PFTS strategy, only ~0.46M prompt parameters are communicated per round rather than the full ~86M ViT-B/16 backbone, reducing upload volume to roughly 1% of the original.

## Method

FedBPrompt introduces the Body Distribution Aware Visual Prompts Mechanism (BAPM), which injects 50 learnable prompt tokens into every ViT layer, partitioned into 15 Body Part Alignment Prompts (5 each for upper, middle, and lower body) and 35 Holistic Full Body Prompts. A structured attention mask constrains each body-part prompt to attend only to its corresponding spatial region of image patches (top half, central half, bottom half), while holistic prompts attend globally; all prompts remain mutually visible to enable inter-prompt communication. This enforces viewpoint-invariant part-level alignment while suppressing background interference. The Prompt-based Fine-Tuning Strategy (PFTS) freezes the pre-trained ViT backbone and trains only the lightweight prompts, communicating solely prompt updates via FedAvg-style weighted aggregation, achieving comparable or superior mAP/Rank-1 gains within a few rounds at >99% lower communication cost.

## Results

Under Protocol-1 (leave-one-domain-out), the full-parameter BAPM strategy integrated into the strongest baseline SSCU achieves an average mAP of 36.6% and Rank-1 of 53.8% across three unseen-domain splits, surpassing SSCU's original 33.3% mAP and 48.9% Rank-1 by 3.3 and 4.9 points respectively. On the most challenging split (C2+C3+M→MS), BAPM boosts SSCU from 20.0% to 23.4% mAP and from 43.7% to 49.5% Rank-1. The prompt-based fine-tuning strategy (PFTS) also yields consistent gains over all baselines while communicating only ~0.46M parameters versus ~86M for full-model updates, achieving e.g. 35.2% mAP / 51.4% Rank-1 average on SSCU+PFTS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedBPrompt (BAPM) is integrated into seven baselines and consistently outperforms every one of them on both mAP and Rank-1 across all three leave-one-out splits. The largest absolute gains appear on weaker baselines: FedPav improves by +13.2 mAP / +17.6 Rank-1 (average), MixStyle by +13.9 / +17.7, and FedProx by +10.0 / +13.5. On the strongest existing method, SSCU (ACM MM 2025), BAPM still adds +3.3 mAP and +4.9 Rank-1 on average, reaching 36.6% mAP / 53.8% Rank-1. The prompt-only variant (PFTS) also beats every baseline's official numbers while communicating only ~1% of the full model parameters, e.g. SSCU+PFTS reaches 35.2% mAP / 51.4% Rank-1 versus SSCU's 33.3% / 48.9%. No baseline outperforms the proposed method on any reported split or metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: federated domain generalization for person Re-ID (formally defined by Wu et al., AAAI 2021, and extended by DACS and SSCU), visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022), and body-part-aware ReID (PSP, MGN). It departs from prior FedDG-ReID methods that primarily address domain shift through data augmentation or style generation by instead intervening at the model's attention mechanism via structured visual prompts. The Prompt-based Fine-Tuning Strategy (PFTS) extends the text-domain federated prompt learning of PromptFL (Guo et al., 2023) to the vision domain, but with a key architectural twist: prompts are spatially constrained to body regions rather than being uniform tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 99% communication reduction from PFTS is practically significant for resource-constrained federated deployments, and the modular design (BAPM + PFTS can be plugged into any ViT-based FedDG-ReID framework) lowers the barrier to adoption. However, the fixed top/mid/bottom spatial partitioning is a simplification that may not generalize well to aerial views, heavily occluded pedestrians, or non-standard body proportions, and the paper does not explore adaptive or learned region boundaries. The modest gains over the strongest baseline (SSCU: +3.3% mAP, +4.9% Rank-1) suggest that the structured prompting approach, while clean, may be approaching the ceiling of what attention-level interventions can achieve without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other fine-grained recognition tasks (e.g., vehicle ReID) and whether the body-part partitioning can be made data-driven.
