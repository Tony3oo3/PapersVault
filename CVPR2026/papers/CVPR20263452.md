## Problem

Existing 3D self-supervised learning methods (contrastive and masked modeling) enforce semantic consistency across augmented views, producing representations that excel at semantic segmentation but transfer poorly to instance and panoptic segmentation. The root cause is that semantic-driven objectives inherently suppress the fine-grained geometric relationships—specifically, where each point should direct toward its instance centroid—that are essential for instance-level perception. Bridging this gap is critical for building unified 3D foundation models that support all downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw 3D point clouds (coordinates and features), augments them into two independently transformed views, and masks a random subset for the student network while the teacher sees the full cloud. The decoder-free PTv3 backbone produces multi-scale features upsampled to a common resolution and concatenated; from these, a two-layer MLP offset head outputs per-point 3D offset vectors pointing toward instance centroids, while a projection head maps features to prototype similarities for semantic distillation. Downstream, the learned features support linear probing, decoder probing, and full finetuning for semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation.

## Method

PointINS augments a teacher-student self-distillation framework (built on DOS) with an orthogonal offset branch that predicts per-point 3D vectors toward instance centers, jointly learning semantic invariance and geometric reasoning. Two complementary regularizations stabilize this unsupervised offset learning: Offset Distribution Regularization (ODR) uses the probabilistic integral transform to align predicted offset magnitudes with an empirically observed long-tailed distribution and directions with a uniform spherical prior, providing a global statistical constraint; Spatial Clustering Regularization (SCR) applies K-means to teacher features to obtain pseudo-semantic segments, then BFS on predicted centroids within each segment to carve spatially coherent pseudo-instances whose mean coordinates become refined supervision targets, enforcing local geometric coherence. Both regularizations operate exclusively on the teacher side, and the student is trained via an L1 + cosine-similarity offset loss with cross-view distillation, delayed by a warm-up schedule. The key design insight is that ODR prevents scattered, unstable offsets that would break SCR's spatial grouping, while SCR provides the local structure that ODR's global distributional constraint alone cannot capture.

## Results

On indoor instance segmentation (ScanNet, linear probing), PointINS achieves 32.1 mAP, surpassing the previous best SSL method DOS (28.7 mAP) by +3.4 points and Sonata (25.0 mAP) by +7.1 points, while maintaining competitive semantic mIoU (72.4 vs. 72.8 for DOS). On outdoor panoptic segmentation (nuScenes, linear probing), PointINS reaches 62.2 PQ, outperforming DOS (57.4 PQ) by +4.8 and Sonata (50.7 PQ) by +11.5. Under full finetuning on ScanNet, PointINS attains 41.5 mAP (vs. 40.5 for DOS and 39.5 for Sonata), approaching the supervised upper bound of 40.9 mAP. Across all five datasets and three evaluation protocols, PointINS consistently leads among self-supervised methods on instance and panoptic metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointINS outperforms all five SSL baselines across every dataset and evaluation protocol on instance and panoptic segmentation metrics. Against the strongest prior method DOS, PointINS gains +3.4 mAP on ScanNet instance segmentation (linear probing) and +4.8 PQ on nuScenes panoptic segmentation (linear probing), with similar margins under decoder probing and finetuning. Against Sonata, the gains are even larger: +7.1 mAP on ScanNet and +11.5 PQ on nuScenes under linear probing, indicating that Sonata's semantic-focused representations are particularly weak on instance-level tasks. The older contrastive and masked-modeling baselines (SegContrast, PSA, NOMAE) trail by a wide margin (e.g., 6.4–9.7 mAP vs. 32.1 for PointINS on ScanNet linear), confirming that the teacher-student prototype-based paradigm is essential. Notably, PointINS also slightly improves semantic segmentation over Sonata and PSA (e.g., 72.4 vs. 67.4 mIoU on ScanNet linear), suggesting that the geometric reasoning objective and semantic objective are complementary rather than conflicting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointINS builds directly on the DOS teacher-student self-distillation framework (Abdelsamad et al., AAAI 2026) and the broader line of prototype-based 3D SSL methods (Sonata, NOMAE) that enforce semantic consistency across augmented views. Its key departure is borrowing the dual-branch (semantic + offset) architecture from supervised instance segmentation methods like PointGroup and Panoptic-PolarNet, but repurposing it for the label-free setting by replacing ground-truth instance centers with two self-generated regularization signals: a distributional prior via probabilistic integral transform (ODR) and a spatial coherence constraint via K-means/BFS pseudo-instance masks (SCR). This positions the work at the intersection of 3D SSL and instance-aware geometric reasoning, filling a gap that prior contrastive and masked-modeling approaches leave open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that instance-aware geometric reasoning can be injected into SSL without any instance annotations, PointINS narrows the long-standing gap between semantic and instance-level performance in self-supervised 3D representation learning, a prerequisite for unified 3D foundation models. The robustness of ODR to distribution choice (even cross-domain priors) and the finding that 1% of annotations suffice to estimate a reliable prior make the approach practically deployable. Remaining open questions include closing the residual gap to fully supervised performance, scaling to 4D spatiotemporal reasoning, and whether the offset-branch design generalizes beyond point clouds to other 3D representations (volumes, meshes).
