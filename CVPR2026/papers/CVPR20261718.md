## Problem

Existing image vectorization methods produce fragmented, semantically incoherent shapes when applied to real-world photographs, because they rely on RGBA compositing that entangles intrinsic surface color with illumination effects. This fragmentation undermines editability and semantic conciseness, as lighting and material properties cannot be independently manipulated in the resulting SVG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261718/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB raster image as input and produces a unified SVG file containing three distinct vector layers—albedo, shade, and light—composited via native SVG blending modes (multiply for shade, plus-lighter for light). The albedo layer is initialized from SAM segmentation masks applied to an intrinsic albedo map, while the illumination layer is initialized via region-wise semantic binarization within each SAM segment. For simple images (e.g., emojis), the method degenerates to a single albedo layer without shade or light components.

## Method

COVec introduces intrinsic image decomposition into the vector domain for the first time, formulating the target SVG as V_A * V_S + V_L following the extended image formation model. A key initialization strategy—region-wise semantic binarization—computes a per-segment mean-intensity threshold within each SAM mask to extract shadow regions, ensuring illumination boundaries align with object geometry rather than global intensity distributions. Optimization proceeds in two stages: first, albedo and illumination layers are jointly optimized via DiffVG differentiable rendering with a two-phase schedule (structure loss for 50 epochs, then shared reconstruction loss for 50 epochs); second, the albedo layer is frozen while new paths are progressively added to the illumination layer to capture fine-grained lighting details. Finally, the illumination layer is split into shade and light components by thresholding path fill colors at the [0,1] boundary, with light-layer colors recomputed from the residual illumination map.

## Results

The paper reports MSE and LPIPS curves (Figure 7) under increasing path budgets across three datasets (Face/FFHQ, Scene/Things+TID2013, Emoji/Noto Emoji) rather than a single fixed-budget table. COVec consistently achieves the lowest MSE and LPIPS at every path count tested, outperforming all four baselines on all three datasets. On complex real-world images (Face and Scene), COVec reaches comparable or better fidelity than LayerVec and O&R with substantially fewer paths. On the simpler Emoji dataset, COVec (in its single-layer degenerate mode) produces shape contours comparable to LayerVec at 16 paths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COVec outperforms all four baselines on both MSE and LPIPS across all three datasets at every path budget tested. Against DiffVG [18], the single-layer differentiable rasterizer, COVec achieves lower reconstruction and perceptual error, with the gap widening on complex real-world images where DiffVG produces fragmented shapes. Against LIVE [22], which uses a bottom-up path-addition strategy, COVec maintains lower error on Face and Scene datasets while matching or slightly exceeding LIVE on Emoji. Against O&R [13], the top-down optimize-and-reduce method, COVec attains higher fidelity with fewer paths, as O&R's pruning strategy struggles to preserve fine illumination details. Against LayerVec [35], the current state-of-the-art layered method, COVec achieves the largest gains on complex real-world images (Face, Scene) where LayerVec's RGBA compositing produces redundant paths, while on the simpler Emoji dataset the two methods perform comparably since illumination decomposition is unnecessary. In the controlled color-editing experiment (Figure 10), COVec requires the fewest path modifications to reach the target appearance, whereas all four baselines produce redundant edits due to their lack of explicit albedo-illumination separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

COVec sits at the intersection of two research threads: optimization-based image vectorization (DiffVG, LIVE, LayerVec, O&R) and intrinsic image decomposition (Careaga & Aksoy, Shah et al.). It inherits the differentiable rendering engine from DiffVG, the progressive path-addition strategy from LIVE, and the hierarchical mask organization and cleanup operations from LayerVec. The key departure is transplanting the pixel-domain intrinsic decomposition model (I = A*S + L) into the vector domain for the first time, leveraging SVG's native multiply and plus-lighter blending modes rather than the standard RGBA alpha compositing used by all prior layered vectorization methods. The region-wise semantic binarization for illumination initialization is a novel practical contribution that addresses the spatially varying shadow intensities that global thresholding cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly separating albedo, shade, and light into distinct editable vector layers, COVec enables a class of edits (recoloring surfaces without disturbing illumination, or adjusting lighting without changing material color) that are intractable with flat or RGBA-composited SVGs. This has practical implications for design tools, animation pipelines, and generative vector graphics where illumination-consistent editing is desired. Limitations include reliance on external pretrained models (SAM for segmentation, an intrinsic decomposition network for albedo extraction), a heuristic shade/light separation based on whether fill colors exceed [0,1], and a two-stage optimization schedule that is somewhat ad hoc. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video vectorization, whether the decomposition generalizes to non-photographic or synthetic content, and whether the path-budget efficiency gains persist at very high path counts.
