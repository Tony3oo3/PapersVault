## Problem

Existing garment reconstruction methods rely on unstructured representations (point clouds, Gaussian splats, distance fields) that lack explicit sewing structure, making outputs unsuitable for physics-based simulation or industry-standard 2D pattern workflows. Even recent methods that predict sewing patterns either depend on predefined topologies that fail on unseen garments, or use VLM-based tokenization that sacrifices geometric accuracy. No prior approach simultaneously reconstructs topology-accurate 3D geometry and 2D sewing patterns with explicit 2D-3D correspondence from sparse views.

## Input / Output

The method takes four sparse-view RGB images at 518×518 resolution (front, back, left, right with small pose perturbations) and produces a unified multi-level representation: 3D patches (each topologically a square, represented by a 20×20 point set), 3D seam curves (50 sampled points each), 2D panel edges in normalized [0,1]² coordinates with a per-panel scalar scale factor for absolute physical sizing, and a binary patch-curve adjacency matrix. The 2D panels can be directly triangulated for simulation, while the 3D patches and curves support structural perception and shape analysis.

## Method

ReWeaver uses a VGGT-style multi-view encoder (DINOv2 backbone with alternating intra-frame and inter-frame self-attention) to produce image tokens, which then interact with learnable patch and curve queries through a bi-path transformer (inspired by ComplexGen) where each element group performs self-attention followed by cross-attention with both image tokens and the other element group. Geometry is predicted via hyper-networks that generate per-element MLPs mapping canonical coordinates to 3D space (curves: [0,1]→ℝ³, patches: [0,1]²→ℝ³), enabling differentiable supervision at arbitrary sampling densities. For 2D pattern prediction, valid patch tokens are grouped with their connected curve tokens via the predicted adjacency matrix, and intra-group attention produces edge tokens that are decoded by another hyper-network into 2D edge coordinates. Post-hoc topology refinement removes redundant curves and geometry refinement enforces loop closure on 2D panel boundaries. Training uses Hungarian matching for correspondence, with Chamfer Distance for geometry, binary cross-entropy for validity and connectivity, and L2 loss for panel scale, all supervised on the newly constructed GCD-TS dataset of ~100,000 textured multi-view samples.

## Results

On the GCD-TS test set, ReWeaver achieves a Panel Count Accuracy (Acc_p) of 0.921, substantially outperforming the best baseline ChatGarment (0.556) and AIpparel (0.456). The Overall Topology Accuracy (Acc_o) reaches 0.661, nearly 1.5× the next-best ChatGarment (0.445). For 2D geometric quality, ReWeaver attains the lowest Edge Chamfer Distance (0.0391) and highest Panel IoU (0.8221), compared to AIpparel's 0.0648 and 0.7084 respectively. The only metric where ReWeaver trails is Edge Count Accuracy (0.7175 vs. ChatGarment's 0.8012), though the topology refinement ablation (Table 2) shows this gap narrows from 0.5361 without refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReWeaver is compared against three baselines: SewFormer (single-image transformer), ChatGarment (VLM-based JSON pattern prediction), and AIpparel (VLM with refined tokenization). ReWeaver dominates on Panel Count Accuracy (0.921 vs. 0.556 for ChatGarment, the strongest baseline), indicating far superior topology prediction. On Overall Accuracy (Acc_o), ReWeaver leads by a wide margin (0.661 vs. 0.445 for ChatGarment and 0.309 for AIpparel). For 2D geometric fidelity, ReWeaver achieves the best Edge CD (0.0391) and Panel IoU (0.8221), beating AIpparel (0.0648, 0.7084) by ~40% and ~16% respectively. The sole metric where ReWeaver underperforms is Edge Count Accuracy (0.7175), where ChatGarment leads (0.8012), suggesting ChatGarment's VLM-based approach is slightly better at predicting the correct number of edges per panel, though it compensates with much worse overall topology and geometry. All baselines were adapted to accept four-view inputs and fine-tuned on GCD-TS for fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReWeaver builds on VGGT's alternating intra-frame/inter-frame attention architecture for multi-view encoding and borrows the bi-path transformer design from ComplexGen (CAD reconstruction) to model interactions between patch and curve tokens. It departs from the dominant line of garment reconstruction work in two directions: prior methods either produce unstructured 3D geometry (Gaussian splats, point clouds) or 2D sewing patterns (SewFormer, ChatGarment, AIpparel), but none jointly predict both with explicit 2D-3D point-level correspondence. The hyper-network formulation for parametric geometry prediction follows Ha et al., and the GCD-TS dataset extends the GarmentCode/GCD pipeline with realistic textures and structured 3D annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D structural perception and 2D pattern extraction in a single model, ReWeaver removes the need for separate reconstruction and pattern-recovery pipelines, enabling simulation-ready garment assets from as few as four uncontrolled views. The GCD-TS dataset (100K textured multi-view samples) is a valuable resource for the community. Key limitations include evaluation exclusively on synthetic data, leaving generalization to in-the-wild photographs unverified, and a fixed minimum of four input views. Open questions include whether the topology-refinement heuristics generalize to highly complex garments (e.g., multi-layered outfits) and whether the approach can be extended to video-based reconstruction.
