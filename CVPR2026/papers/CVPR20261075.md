## Problem

Multimodal dataset distillation must compress large vision-language datasets into compact synthetic sets while preserving both intra-modal statistics and cross-modal alignment. Existing trajectory-matching methods (MTT-VL, LoRS) rely on expensive bi-level optimization and inherit architecture-specific biases from the source model's training dynamics, limiting cross-architecture generalization and incurring high compute costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes real image-text pairs (e.g., 8k–123k pairs from Flickr8k, Flickr30k, or COCO) along with a pretrained NFNet image encoder, a BERT text encoder, and N (typically 2) independently fine-tuned expert models. It produces a compact synthetic set of 100, 200, or 500 image-text pairs where images are 3×224×224 tensors and text is represented as 768-dimensional continuous embeddings rather than raw captions. The synthetic pairs are then used to train fresh encoder pairs (NFNet/NF-ResNet/NF-RegNet × BERT/DistilBERT) evaluated via bidirectional retrieval on real test sets.

## Method

MDM integrates three complementary components. At the data level, synthetic pairs are seeded via K-means clustering in the concatenated image-text joint embedding space, ensuring broad coverage of multimodal semantic modes. At the model level, a mixed teacher is formed by layer-wise weight-space interpolation between a pretrained anchor and N fine-tuned experts, where the interpolation ratio is governed by the angular deviation between expert displacement vectors (larger disagreement → closer to anchor), and the teacher is re-sampled from random expert checkpoints each iteration. At the loss level, the method constructs cross-modal agreement (normalize(z^v+z^t)) and discrepancy (normalize(z^v−z^t)) vectors on the unit hypersphere, then minimizes a geodesic kernel energy (GKE) between real and synthetic sets in each direction, combined with a bidirectional InfoNCE contrastive term to preserve pairing consistency within synthetic pairs. This single-level optimization avoids trajectory replay entirely, yielding ~3× per-iteration speedup and convergence in far fewer iterations than trajectory-based baselines.

## Results

On image-text retrieval (mean of IR/TR@1/5/10), MDM achieves 21.9 / 26.4 / 10.3 on Flickr8k / Flickr30k / COCO with 100 synthetic pairs, and 26.2 / 30.6 / 15.3 with 500 pairs, compared to full-dataset scores of 53.8 / 55.7 / 41.2. MDM outperforms the strongest trajectory-matching baseline LoRS on Flickr8k (e.g., 26.2 vs 25.0 at 500 pairs) and COCO (15.3 vs 13.5 at 500 pairs), while LoRS retains a small edge on Flickr30k at 200–500 pairs (29.5 vs 28.2, 31.6 vs 30.6). In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 2), MDM's mean across five encoder combinations exceeds LoRS by 3.6–5.2 points on Flickr8k and 3.6–5.6 points on COCO. Distillation cost is reduced by >93%: MDM converges in 50–200 iterations at ~1.7–4.4 s/iter versus 850–2,350 iterations at ~5.3–6.6 s/iter for LoRS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest trajectory-matching baseline LoRS, MDM wins on Flickr8k at all budgets (21.9 vs 19.4 at 100 pairs; 26.2 vs 25.0 at 500 pairs) and on COCO at all budgets (10.3 vs 9.4 at 100 pairs; 15.3 vs 13.5 at 500 pairs), but LoRS retains a narrow lead on Flickr30k at 200 and 500 pairs (29.5 vs 28.2 and 31.6 vs 30.6). MDM decisively outperforms MTT-VL and TESLA$_{WBCE}$ across every dataset and budget; for example, at 500 pairs on Flickr30k, MDM scores 30.6 versus 25.0 (MTT-VL) and 10.9 (TESLA). All coreset-selection baselines (Random, Herding, K-Center, Forgetting) are far below both MDM and the distillation methods, confirming that synthetic optimization is necessary for multimodal retrieval. In cross-architecture evaluation (Table 2), MDM's mean over five encoder combinations (NFNet/NF-ResNet/NF-RegNet × BERT/DistilBERT) exceeds LoRS by 3.6–5.2 points on Flickr8k and 3.6–5.6 points on COCO, demonstrating that distribution matching avoids the architecture-specific bias inherent in trajectory replay. Finally, MDM achieves these results at roughly 3× lower per-iteration cost and 5–40× fewer total iterations than LoRS, reducing total distillation time from 77–206 min to 3.7–8.0 min on Flickr8k.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDM extends unimodal distribution matching for dataset distillation (DM [69, 71], DANCE [64]) to the multimodal setting by operating on joint image-text features decomposed into cross-modal agreement and discrepancy directions on the unit hypersphere. It departs from the dominant trajectory-matching paradigm for multimodal distillation (MTT-VL [57], LoRS [58]) by replacing bi-level trajectory replay with a single-level distribution-alignment objective, eliminating the architectural bias inherent in matching optimization paths. The model initialization strategy adapts the weight-space interpolation idea from Model Stock [20] to the image-text encoder, and the geodesic kernel energy [23, 39] provides a geometry-aware distance on the hyperspherical feature space that is natural for ℓ₂-normalized contrastive embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~93–98% reduction in total distillation time relative to LoRS while matching or exceeding retrieval accuracy is a substantial practical win for teams working under tight compute budgets, and the improved cross-architecture generalization (Table 2) suggests the distilled data captures dataset-level structure rather than model-specific optimization artifacts. A key limitation is the continued reliance on pretrained image and text encoders, which constrains applicability to settings where such encoders are available and may not transfer to emerging unified multimodal architectures. Open questions include whether the geodesic-kernel formulation scales to much larger budgets (thousands of pairs) or to generative synthetic images rather than optimized pixel parameters, and whether the agreement/discrepancy decomposition generalizes beyond the binary image-text pairing to multi-modal or multi-turn settings.
