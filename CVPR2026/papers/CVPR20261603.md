## Problem

Existing diffusion-based virtual try-on methods depend on multiple auxiliary inputs (segmentation masks, pose estimators, DensePose) and external models, making them complex and sensitive to input quality. More fundamentally, flat garment images cannot convey critical visual properties like transparency, texture, and fine design details that are only apparent when the garment is worn by a person, yet no prior method supports reference person images as guidance for try-on generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261603/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source person image (either an agnostic/masked image or a clothed person image for mask-free mode), a target garment image, and optionally a reference image showing a different person wearing the same garment, all at 1024×768 resolution. It outputs a single photorealistic try-on image of the source person wearing the target garment. A key design choice is that the same model handles both mask-based and mask-free (person-to-person) inference, and the reference image is unpaired with the target person, requiring the model to transfer garment appearance across different body shapes and poses.

## Method

RefTon builds on the Flux-Kontext flow-matching DiT architecture, freezing the VAE encoder/decoder and fine-tuning transformer blocks via LoRA (rank 64, α=128) with a conditional flow-matching objective. The core architectural contribution is a Rescaled Position Index that extends the original binary RoPE first channel to discrete condition labels (distinguishing person, garment, reference, noise) and rescales spatial coordinates by the resolution ratio between target and condition images, enabling flexible multi-condition, multi-resolution inputs within a unified sequence. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 synthesizes unpaired person images using a mask-based try-on model with DensePose and warp masks, and Stage 2 trains the person-to-person model on these synthesized images alongside real pairs, with reference images provided at 25% probability. A reference data generation pipeline uses Qwen2.5-VL to produce appearance descriptions and opposite descriptions, then Flux-Kontext to synthesize diverse reference images that preserve the target garment while varying the wearer's identity and non-target clothing, forming the VFR dataset across five benchmarks.

## Results

On VITON-HD (paired), RefTon+R achieves LPIPS 0.049, FID 4.69, and KID 0.68, outperforming the best baseline CatVTON (LPIPS 0.057, FID 5.43, KID 0.41) on LPIPS and FID. On DressCode (paired), RefTon+R reaches LPIPS 0.031, FID 2.94, and KID 0.95, surpassing all baselines including CatVT2ON (LPIPS 0.037, FID 5.72) and OOTDiffusion (FID 4.20). In the mask-free setting, RefTon+R/MF achieves FID 5.11 on VITON-HD and FID 3.34 on DressCode, beating CatVTON/MF (FID 5.89 and 4.78 respectively). On StreetTryOn, RefTon achieves FID 28.99 (Sh-to-St) and 16.45 (St-to-St), substantially outperforming OmniVTON (33.92 and 23.47) without requiring pose or text inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RefTon+R outperforms all mask-based baselines on VITON-HD in LPIPS (0.049 vs. 0.057 for CatVTON/CatVT2ON) and FID (4.69 vs. 5.43 for CatVTON), though CatVTON retains a slight KID edge (0.41 vs. 0.68). On DressCode, RefTon+R dominates across all metrics: LPIPS 0.031 vs. 0.037 (CatVT2ON), FID 2.94 vs. 3.99 (CatVTON) and 4.20 (OOTDiffusion), and KID 0.95 vs. 0.37 (OOTDiffusion) — the KID gap to OOTDiffusion is the only metric where a baseline leads. In the mask-free setting, RefTon+R/MF beats CatVTON/MF by a clear margin on FID (5.11 vs. 5.89 on VITON-HD; 3.34 vs. 4.78 on DressCode) and LPIPS (0.053 vs. 0.061; 0.035 vs. 0.045), while TryOffDiff and Any2AnyTryon trail significantly. On StreetTryOn, RefTon requires neither pose nor text inputs yet achieves FID 28.99 (Sh-to-St) and 16.45 (St-to-St), beating OmniVTON (33.92, 23.47) by 5+ FID points and the original StreetTryOn method (34.05, 33.04) by a wide margin, demonstrating strong in-the-wild generalization without domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

RefTon builds directly on the Flux-Kontext flow-matching architecture for in-context image editing and adapts its RoPE position indexing to handle multiple heterogeneous image conditions at varying resolutions. Its two-stage training strategy (mask-based synthesis followed by person-to-person fine-tuning) follows CatVTON, but departs from the broader ViTON literature by eliminating all auxiliary inputs (pose, keypoints, segmentation masks) at inference and by introducing reference person images—shots of different individuals wearing the target garment—as a new visual conditioning channel. This reference-image mechanism is conceptually distinct from prior P2P methods (OmniTry, Any2AnyTryon, OmniVTON) that rely solely on the garment image or a single source person, and from try-off-then-try-on pipelines (TryOffDiff, ViTON-GUN) that accumulate errors across stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reference-image formulation aligns virtual try-on with how users actually shop online (browsing model photos rather than flat garment images), and the VFR dataset provides a much-needed resource for training and evaluating this capability. The simplified single-model pipeline (no external pose/parse/segmentation models) lowers deployment barriers for e-commerce applications. Open questions include how sensitive the method is to reference image quality and diversity, whether the approach extends naturally to video try-on or multi-garment scenarios, and whether the two-stage training can be replaced by a single end-to-end objective. The reliance on a proprietary Flux-Kontext backbone and a multi-model reference generation pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL + Flux-Kontext) also limits immediate reproducibility.
