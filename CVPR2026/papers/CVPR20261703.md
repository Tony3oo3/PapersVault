## Problem

Existing motion-based 4D Gaussian Splatting methods use a Gaussian-shaped temporal visibility profile that forces long-lived static primitives to decay gradually, requiring multiple overlapping Gaussians to approximate flat, time-invariant visibility and triggering redundant densification. Simultaneously, their motion formulation ignores the relationship between velocity and lifespan, so even tiny residual velocities in static primitives accumulate over long durations and cause noticeable spatial drift. The paper addresses both issues by introducing a learnable lifespan that adaptively modulates visibility and motion within a unified representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (19–21 synchronized cameras at 2704×2028 for Neural3DV, 18 cameras at 1920×1080 for ENeRF-Outdoor, or single-camera fast-motion clips for SelfCap) along with calibrated camera parameters. It produces a per-primitive 4D Gaussian representation (position, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients, time anchor, velocity, lifespan variance σ_t, and lifespan radius r) that renders novel views in real time—up to 4K resolution at 100 FPS on a single RTX 4090. Initialization additionally leverages RAFT optical flow, SAM2 object masks, and COLMAP point clouds to assign velocity and lifespan priors before optimization.

## Method

SharpTimeGS introduces two learnable per-primitive lifespan parameters—variance σ_t and radius r—that jointly modulate motion and temporal visibility. For visibility, a flat-top function l(t) keeps opacity at 1 within |t−T|≤r and applies Gaussian decay only outside that window, eliminating the gradual bell-shaped fade that causes motion dragging and redundant primitive insertion. For motion, the effective displacement is v/f(σ_t,r) where f(σ_t,r)=1+max{1,(σ_t+r)²}, so long-lived static primitives are effectively frozen (v/f→0) while short-lived dynamic ones retain full expressive velocity. A velocity-aware initialization separates static and dynamic regions via optical flow and SAM2, assigning zero velocity and large σ_t to static points and estimated velocity with small σ_t to dynamic points. A lifespan-velocity-aware densification strategy then scores each primitive by reconstruction error, opacity, and a motion-magnitude-relative-to-lifespan term, cloning high-scoring (fast, short-lived) primitives preferentially to allocate representational capacity where it is most needed.

## Results

SharpTimeGS achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks. On Neural3DV, it reaches 33.57 PSNR / 0.977 SSIM / 0.031 LPIPS; on ENeRF-Outdoor, 25.82 PSNR / 0.872 SSIM / 0.233 LPIPS; and on SelfCap, 28.14 PSNR / 0.960 SSIM / 0.192 LPIPS. The method supports real-time rendering at up to 4K resolution and 100 FPS on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SharpTimeGS outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline FreeTimeGS [41], it gains +0.38 PSNR on Neural3DV, +0.46 on ENeRF-Outdoor, and +0.64 on SelfCap, with consistent SSIM and LPIPS improvements. The gap widens substantially against earlier methods: versus 4DGS [48], gains reach +1.56 PSNR (Neural3DV) and +2.28 PSNR (SelfCap); versus STGS [20], +3.37 PSNR on SelfCap; versus Ex4DGS [18], +3.18 PSNR on SelfCap; and versus Deformable-3DGS [47], +2.42 PSNR on Neural3DV. The paper attributes FreeTimeGS's relative weakness to its lack of velocity-lifespan coupling, which causes static jitter and under-converged dynamic regions, while 4DGS and STGS suffer from entangled or high-order parameterizations that hinder convergence on fast motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion-based 4D Gaussian splatting lineage (4DGS, STGS, FreeTimeGS) by introducing a learnable lifespan parameter that simultaneously modulates temporal visibility and motion amplitude. It departs from the standard Gaussian-shaped temporal opacity used in all prior motion-based methods, replacing it with a flat-top profile that allows a single primitive to represent a step-like lifespan without redundant overlapping Gaussians. The velocity-aware initialization borrows from RAFT/SAM2/COLMAP pipelines, and the first-stage densification follows AbsGS, but the core architectural novelty is the lifespan-modulated motion formulation that decouples motion magnitude from temporal duration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lifespan modulation provides a principled mechanism to resolve the long-standing static-dynamic optimization imbalance in 4D Gaussian methods, and the resulting real-time 4K@100 FPS rendering makes the approach practically deployable. However, training still takes several hours, the method is limited to novel view synthesis without relighting or material modeling, and the evaluation is confined to three relatively small benchmarks (≤300 frames). Open questions include whether the lifespan formulation generalizes to longer sequences, larger-scale scenes, or unsynchronized multi-camera setups, and whether the flat-top visibility can be extended to handle topological changes (objects entering/leaving the scene).
