## Problem

Modern vision models are trained on fully detailed, static images with random augmentations, an ecologically invalid regime that overlooks how perception develops through structured, temporally coherent visual experience. This results in brittle representations that fail to generalize to corrupted or occluded inputs. The paper asks whether reverse-engineering the staged progression of infant vision—grayscale-to-color, blur-to-sharp, and temporally continuous—can scaffold robust visual intelligence in self-supervised machines.

## Input / Output

Training inputs are object-centric videos (CO3D: 10 frames per 360° rotation at ~36° intervals) or infant egocentric clips (SAYCam: 2-second segments at 5 fps), all at 224×224 resolution. During early training, each frame is progressively degraded via a saturation blend (s·I_c + (1−s)·I_g) and Gaussian blur (σ from 4 to 0), while adjacent frames are grouped as positive pairs for temporal alignment. Outputs are frozen visual embeddings evaluated via linear probes for 26-way object classification, 15-way ImageNet classification, binary depth-order prediction (3D-PC, Visual Cliff), and shape-bias scoring on texture-shape conflict and silhouette datasets.

## Method

CATDiet integrates three staged constraints into SSL pretraining: (1) CDiet applies a five-stage saturation schedule with blend ratios s sampled from (0.20,0.36) up to (0.84,1.0) over decreasing epoch durations [10,7,6,5,2]; (2) ADiet applies a five-stage Gaussian blur schedule with σ ∈ {4,3,2,1,0} (kernel sizes 25→1 px) over durations [10,6,6,3,5]; (3) TDiet adds a temporal alignment objective that pulls embeddings of adjacent frames and their augmented variants closer, adapted to SimCLR's contrastive loss (as additional positive pairs) or DINO's self-distillation (via cluster prototypes). CombDiet extends this to a two-phase curriculum: the first 30% of epochs use CATDiet as a developmental warm-up, then transition to standard SSL augmentations (SDiet) while retaining TDiet throughout. The framework is instantiated with SimCLR and DINO on ResNet and ViT backbones, trained label-free on object-centric or infant egocentric videos.

## Results

CombDiet, pretrained on infant egocentric video (SAYCam), consistently outperforms standard SSL (SimCLR/DINO) and shuffled-order baselines across all four model variants (SimCLR/DINO × ResNet/ViT). Headline gains include mean Corruption Error (mCE) on SAY-C dropping to 76.0–80.4 (vs. 79.6–100.3 for baselines), depth-order accuracy (dAcc) on 3D-PC reaching 66.9–78.9 (vs. 60.8–75.8), and shape-bias (S-Bias) on TSCC peaking at 45.1% for SimCLR-ResNet. On out-of-domain ImageNet-C, CombDiet achieves mCE of 64.8–71.5, substantially below the 70.2–89.6 range of baselines, demonstrating strong OOD generalization from developmentally structured pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CombDiet is compared against two baselines: (1) **STD** (standard SimCLR or DINO with conventional random augmentations) and (2) **SHF** (same two-phase schedule but with shuffled stage order in Phase 1). CombDiet wins decisively on corruption robustness: on SAY-C, mCE improves by 3.6–20.3 points over STD and 5.0–19.9 points over SHF across the four model variants. On depth perception (3D-PC dAcc), CombDiet leads STD by 2.5–8.1 points and SHF by 4.8–12.1 points. On out-of-domain ImageNet-C, CombDiet achieves 6.5–22.9 points lower mCE than SHF and 0.1–10.7 points lower than STD. The SHF baseline performs comparably to or slightly worse than STD, confirming that the developmental progression order (not merely the data distribution) is critical. The one exception is Silhouettes-Only accuracy, where STD occasionally matches or exceeds CombDiet (e.g., 54.4% vs. 41.1% for SimCLR-ResNet), suggesting that pure shape recognition from silhouettes does not always benefit from the staged color/blur curriculum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of developmental-psychology-inspired visual learning (Orhan & Lake 2024; Orhan et al. 2020; Sheybani et al. 2023) by explicitly modeling the infant visual system's limitations—low acuity and limited color sensitivity—rather than merely using infant egocentric videos as a data source. It also departs from prior supervised studies of individual visual transitions (Vogelsang et al. 2018, 2024, 2025) by embedding staged color, acuity, and temporal-continuity constraints within a self-supervised framework (SimCLR, DINO), making the approach label-free and scalable. The temporal-continuity objective (TDiet) builds on video-based SSL that leverages frame-to-frame coherence, but is here integrated with the staged degradation curricula to form a unified developmental diet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10-dataset benchmark spanning clean/corrupted recognition, texture-shape conflict, silhouette recognition, and depth-order tasks provides a reusable evaluation suite for assessing whether training regimes produce robust, shape-biased, biologically plausible representations. CombDiet offers a practical two-phase SSL recipe that practitioners could adopt with minimal architectural changes. However, the biological-alignment claims (FIM mirroring macaque V1 synaptic density, visual-cliff-like behavior) remain correlational and are demonstrated on only two SSL methods and two backbones; whether the specific schedule parameters (stage durations, blur/saturation ranges) generalize to larger models, more diverse datasets, or different SSL objectives (e.g., MAE, DINOv2) is an open question. The moderate performance gains (~10–15% mCE improvement) suggest the approach is complementary to, rather than a replacement for, standard augmentation strategies.
