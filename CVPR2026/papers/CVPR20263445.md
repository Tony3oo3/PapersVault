## Problem

Soccer visual understanding has been addressed by fragmented, task-specific expert models, and recent unification attempts (e.g., MatchVision) neglect dense spatial objectives during pretraining, producing models that can describe events but struggle to localize athletes or field geometry. This creates a fundamental disconnect between fine-grained spatial perception and high-level semantic reasoning in a single system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263445/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 30-frame video clips at 512×512 resolution (patch size 16×16) along with optional text commentary encoded by a frozen SigLIP 2 text encoder. It simultaneously outputs spatial features (per-frame token maps) for detection and pitch registration, and pooled semantic features (T×d vectors via MAP) for event classification and alignment. Downstream, lightweight heads produce camera intrinsics/extrinsics, consistent-ID trajectories, and auto-regressive commentary via a Q-Former–Llama-3-8B bridge.

## Method

SoccerMaster is a hierarchical ViT (initialized from SigLIP 2-L/16-512) with 16 spatial-attention layers followed by 8 TimeSformer-style spatiotemporal layers, yielding both fine-grained spatial features and global semantic features from a single encoder. Supervised multi-task pretraining jointly optimizes five heads: a Deformable DETR-style decoder for athlete detection/identification (bbox + role + jersey number), heatmap CNNs for pitch keypoint/line registration, a two-layer transformer for 24-class event classification, a SigLIP contrastive alignment head, and a MAP pooling head for semantic features. To overcome the scarcity of dense spatial labels, the authors build SoccerFactory, an automated pipeline (field registration → YOLOv8+StrongSORT+PRTReID tracking → Qwen2.5-VL identification → SAM2 refinement) that generates 2.7M annotated frames from 500 matches, which are combined with SoccerNet-v2, MatchTime, and SoccerReplay-1988 for a 7.45M-frame pretraining corpus. The key design insight is that co-optimizing spatial and semantic objectives in one encoder forces shared representations that capture both pitch geometry and game semantics, outperforming both general-purpose VFMs and prior soccer-specific models across all evaluated tasks.

## Results

On the unified pretraining benchmark (Table 3), SoccerMaster achieves 91.5 AP@50 and 49.5 mAP for athlete detection, 77.2% accuracy for event classification, and 39.0% top-1 for vision-language alignment, substantially outperforming all baselines. On camera calibration, fine-tuned SoccerMaster reaches 75.8 FS on SN22-test-center and 56.2 FS on SN23-test, surpassing PnLCalib by +8.2 and +4.4 FS respectively. For multiple object tracking, the end-to-end pipeline achieves HOTA 59.1, DetA 65.2, MOTA 81.6, and IDF1 74.6, competitive with multi-stage tracking-by-detection systems. On commentary generation, SoccerMaster attains the best CIDEr (38.6) and BLEU@1 (31.3) among all compared encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against general-purpose VFMs, SoccerMaster dominates: it gains +17.5 mAP over SigLIP 2 (49.5 vs 29.2) and +26.6 mAP over DINOv3 (49.5 vs 22.9) in athlete detection, while its 39.0% alignment top-1 is an order of magnitude above SigLIP 2's 3.4% (DINOv3 has no text encoder for this task). Against the soccer-specific MatchVision, SoccerMaster improves event classification by +11.9 points (77.2 vs 65.3) and athlete detection mAP by +32.5 points (49.5 vs 17.0), though MatchVision retains a slight edge in keypoint precision (85.2 vs 85.9 is actually a SoccerMaster win; MatchVision's line recall of 81.5 is lower than SoccerMaster's 92.4). On camera calibration, fine-tuned SoccerMaster outperforms PnLCalib by +8.2 FS on SN22-test-center (75.8 vs 67.6) and +4.4 FS on SN23-test (56.2 vs 51.8) at 512×512 resolution. For multiple object tracking, SoccerMaster (end-to-end) achieves HOTA 59.1 and MOTA 81.6, surpassing YOLOv8+OC-SORT (54.6/76.2) and YOLOv8+StrongSort++ (56.2/75.0), and matching YOLOv8+PRTreID on HOTA (59.8) while exceeding it on MOTA (81.6 vs 73.1) and DetA (65.2 vs 61.1); PRTreID retains a narrow AssA advantage (58.6 vs 53.9). On commentary generation, SoccerMaster leads MatchVision on BLEU@1 (+0.4) and CIDEr (+2.9) but trails slightly on METEOR (26.2 vs 26.9) and ROUGE-L (26.6 vs 27.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the vision foundation model paradigm (SigLIP 2, DINOv3) into the soccer domain by adopting a TimeSformer-style hierarchical spatial/spatiotemporal ViT architecture and a Deformable DETR-like detection head. The most direct predecessor is MatchVision (UniSoccer), which unified soccer tasks via vision-language alignment but neglected dense spatial objectives; SoccerMaster departs by jointly pretraining on both fine-grained spatial perception (detection, pitch registration) and semantic reasoning (event classification, alignment) within a single encoder. The SoccerFactory pipeline repurposes existing components (YOLOv8, StrongSORT, PRTReID, SAM2, Qwen2.5-VL) into an automated annotation factory, addressing the data bottleneck that has kept spatial tasks in soccer fragmented across task-specific expert models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single soccer-specific VFM can match or exceed task-specific experts across detection, tracking, calibration, event classification, and commentary generation, this work validates a unified-pretraining paradigm for domain-specific video understanding that could transfer to other sports. The SoccerFactory pipeline is practically important as a scalable alternative to manual spatial annotation, though its quality (64.1 GS-HOTA) still trails the best manual annotations. Open questions include whether the multi-task pretraining strategy generalizes beyond soccer to other sports with different spatial structures, whether the zero-shot camera calibration gap on diverse camera views (SN23) can be closed, and whether the marginal commentary generation gains over MatchVision will widen with larger language backbones or longer context.
