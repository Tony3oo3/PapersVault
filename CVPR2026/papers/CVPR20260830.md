## Problem

Existing 3D motion estimation methods rely on supervised regression from densely labeled data, limiting generalization to unseen environments. Unsupervised estimation is particularly difficult because motion along the viewing direction is unobservable and optical flow and motion in depth are geometrically coupled through projection, making their separation inherently ambiguous. While event cameras offer high-temporal-resolution cues that could resolve this ambiguity, comprehensive unsupervised 3D motion estimation from sparse asynchronous event streams remains largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw event stream (asynchronous per-pixel brightness changes) and converts it into two complementary representations: event voxels on the x-y plane (10 temporal bins, 6000 events per window) for spatial processing, and kymographs on the x-t and y-t planes (temporal resolution 120) for directional temporal analysis. It produces per-pixel optical flow (u, v), a motion-in-depth map τ (a depth ratio between consecutive time instants, rescaled to maintain numerical stability), and their combination yields full 3D scene flow (u, v, w). The network is trained exclusively on one weather condition (Sunset) and evaluated across six conditions to test generalization.

## Method

The core insight is an analytical derivation showing that motion in depth τ ≈ 1 − ½·div(f)·Δt, linking the depth ratio directly to optical flow divergence under a pinhole model, which provides a coarse MID estimate from flow alone. A Directional Expansion Modulation (DEM) module then refines this estimate by extracting horizontal and vertical expansion-contraction rates from x-t and y-t kymographs via 1D convolutions, modulating contextual flow-encoder features, and propagating directional cues through self-attention and multi-scale dilated depthwise convolutions to produce a dense MID residual. During warping, the estimated MID induces a per-event time-linearly-interpolated scale factor that displaces events radially from patch centers, explicitly modeling perspective scaling alongside translational flow displacement. The combined warped events form an Image of Warped Events (IWE) optimized under a contrast-maximization objective (per-pixel temporal variance of warped timestamps) with a Charbonnier smoothness regularizer, all in a fully unsupervised setting.

## Results

On the CarlaEvent3D dataset, the proposed unsupervised method achieves the best EPE and F1 among all unsupervised approaches across all six weather conditions (e.g., EPE 3.520 / F1 44.592 on Sunset, EPE 2.833 / F1 34.832 on Cloudy), while also reporting log-mid values (251.321 on Sunset) that are 2–3× lower than the Optical Expansion baseline. For scene flow reconstruction, the method attains an Acc₀.₁ of 12.7–14.3% across conditions, a 6–7× improvement over Expansion (1.4–3.2%), though EPE₃D remains comparable (~1.06–1.13 vs. 0.79–1.69). Trained exclusively on the Sunset subset, the method generalizes to all six weather conditions without additional supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unsupervised Optical Expansion baseline [37], the proposed method wins decisively on every metric and weather condition: EPE is roughly 2.4× lower (e.g., 3.520 vs. 8.499 on Sunset), F1 is 12–25 points lower, and log-mid is 2–3× smaller (251 vs. 774 on Sunset), reflecting the advantage of continuous scale modeling over local affine fitting on sparse events. Compared to EV-FlowNet [39], the method achieves lower EPE (e.g., 3.520 vs. 3.597 on Sunset, 2.833 vs. 2.925 on Cloudy) and lower F1 across all conditions, while additionally estimating motion in depth—a capability EV-FlowNet lacks. Against supervised methods, the gap narrows considerably: on log-mid, the method (251.321 on Sunset) is within 2.4× of EMoTive (106.938) and on par with ScaleFlow (271.081), despite using no ground-truth labels. For scene flow (Table 2), the method's EPE₃D (~1.06–1.13) is comparable to Expansion (0.79–1.69), but Acc₀.₁ improves by 6–7× (12.7–14.3% vs. 1.4–3.2%), while supervised EMoTive still leads with Acc₀.₁ of 37–44%. Overall, the method is the strongest unsupervised approach on all reported metrics, with the largest margins over Expansion and consistent gains over EV-FlowNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrast maximization framework (Gallego et al., CVPR 2018) from 2D optical flow to joint 3D motion estimation by deriving an analytical link between flow divergence and motion in depth under the pinhole camera model. Architecturally, it builds on the recurrent U-Net encoder-decoder from Hagenaars et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for event-based flow, while reformulating the optical expansion warping of Yang & Ramanan (CVPR 2020) for sparse, asynchronous event data to avoid the event-collapse problem. The key departure from prior work is the introduction of the Directional Expansion Modulation (DEM) module, which exploits x-t and y-t event kymographs to refine the coarse MID estimate, and the explicit incorporation of depth-induced scaling into the event warping process under a contrast maximization objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first fully unsupervised approaches to joint 3D motion estimation (optical flow + motion in depth) from event streams, demonstrating that geometric consistency alone can yield competitive scene flow without dense labels. The clean derivation from flow divergence to MID provides a principled initialization that reduces the ambiguity inherent in separating planar and depth-wise motion. However, the brightness-constancy assumption limits performance under illumination changes (noon, rainy conditions), and sparse events in near-ground textureless regions remain a significant weakness. Open questions include whether the contrast maximization objective can be made robust to illumination-induced events, and whether the DEM's kymograph-based refinement generalizes beyond the CarlaEvent3D simulation to real-world event data with higher noise.
