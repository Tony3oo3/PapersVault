## Problem

Existing text-guided diffusion-based super-resolution methods rely on entangled or coarse-grained textual priors that mix global scene layout with local object details, or conflate structural and textural cues within a single embedding. This limits semantic controllability, interpretability, and the model's ability to perform frequency-aware restoration, often causing hallucinations or misinterpretation under severe degradation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261119/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a 512×512 high-resolution image. At inference, textual priors are generated automatically: Mask2Former extracts object-level regions, and LLaVA produces a global scene description plus per-region low-frequency (shape, color, layout) and high-frequency (texture, edges, material) captions. The LR image is also encoded by a frozen DAPE encoder to provide pixel-level identity anchoring, and all priors are encoded via CLIP before injection into the diffusion process.

## Method

DTPSR builds on SD-2-base latent diffusion and introduces four sequential cross-attention modules that inject disentangled priors in a coarse-to-fine order: Global Text Cross-Attention (GTCA) establishes scene layout, Low-Frequency Cross-Attention (LFCA) refines object-level structure, High-Frequency Cross-Attention (HFCA) restores fine textures and edges, and Low-Resolution Cross-Attention (LRCA) anchors the output to the input image identity. The key architectural insight is separating textual conditioning along two complementary axes—spatial hierarchy (global vs. local) and frequency semantics (low vs. high-frequency)—so each branch attends to a distinct semantic scale rather than a single entangled embedding. To support this, the authors construct DisText-SR (~95K image-text pairs) with structured global, per-object low-frequency, and high-frequency captions generated via panoptic segmentation and LLaVA. A multi-branch classifier-free guidance strategy with three frequency-aware negative prompts (one per branch) further suppresses hallucinations and semantic drift without additional training. The model is trained with a standard noise-prediction loss for 110K iterations using AdamW (lr 5×10⁻⁵, batch 32) on 4 A800 GPUs, and sampled with 50 DDPM steps at inference.

## Results

DTPSR achieves the best no-reference perceptual scores across all three evaluation datasets: MUSIQ 71.24 / 71.84 / 69.24, MANIQA 0.5866 / 0.6021 / 0.6011, and CLIP-IQA 0.7549 / 0.7278 / 0.7640 on DIV2K-Val, RealSR, and DRealSR respectively, surpassing the second-best method (DiffBIR or SeeSR) by clear margins. On full-reference metrics, DTPSR trades off fidelity (e.g., PSNR 22.03 on DIV2K-Val vs. 24.62 for BSRGAN), which the authors attribute to the perception-distortion tradeoff. In terms of efficiency, DTPSR uses 10.5B parameters with 50 diffusion steps and 14.94 s per-image inference, compared to SUPIR's 17.8B / 100 steps / 17.25 s and FaithDiff's 15.6B / 20 steps / 13.20 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTPSR wins on all three no-reference perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA) against every baseline on all three datasets. Against the closest diffusion-based competitors, DTPSR leads DiffBIR by +2.06 MUSIQ and +0.0167 MANIQA on DIV2K-Val, and leads SeeSR by +1.98 MUSIQ and +0.0574 MANIQA on RealSR. GAN-based methods (Real-ESRGAN, BSRGAN) achieve higher PSNR/SSIM (e.g., BSRGAN 24.62 PSNR vs. DTPSR 22.03 on DIV2K-Val) but trail DTPSR by 9–13 points on MUSIQ and 0.15–0.20 on MANIQA. Among text-guided diffusion methods, FaithDiff (MUSIQ 69.18, MANIQA 0.4309 on DIV2K-Val) and SUPIR (MUSIQ 62.59, MANIQA 0.5224) are the nearest competitors on perceptual quality, yet DTPSR still leads by 2+ MUSIQ points and 0.06+ MANIQA. On full-reference metrics, DTPSR is consistently the weakest, with PSNR 2–4 dB below GAN baselines, which the authors frame as an expected consequence of the perception-distortion tradeoff when prioritizing realistic texture synthesis over pixel-level fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTPSR extends the text-guided diffusion SR line initiated by SeeSR (local semantic tags) and PASD/FaithDiff/SUPIR (global sentence captions) by decomposing textual conditioning along two orthogonal axes—spatial hierarchy (global vs. local) and frequency semantics (low- vs. high-frequency)—rather than treating text as a single undifferentiated cue. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Diffusion's latent-space U-Net and the DAPE encoder for LR anchoring, but replaces the single cross-attention text pathway with three dedicated modules (GTCA, LFCA, HFCA) operating sequentially, plus a multi-branch classifier-free guidance scheme with frequency-specific negative prompts. The DisText-SR dataset, generated via Mask2Former segmentation and LLaVA captioning, provides the structured supervision that prior text-guided SR methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DisText-SR dataset (~95K pairs with disentangled global/low-freq/high-freq annotations) is a practical resource for future work on structured, interpretable SR conditioning. The consistent SOTA on no-reference perceptual metrics (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA) across synthetic and real-world benchmarks validates that frequency-aware semantic decomposition improves perceptual restoration over flat text conditioning. However, the method's reliance on a multi-model inference pipeline (segmentation + captioning + diffusion) introduces latency and error propagation, and the perception-distortion tradeoff remains: PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS trail GAN-based methods substantially. Open questions include whether adaptive prompt correction can reduce upstream dependency, and whether the disentangled attention design generalizes beyond 4× SR to more extreme degradation regimes.
