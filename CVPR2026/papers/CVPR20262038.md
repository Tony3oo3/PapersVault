## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (AR) models produce blurred strokes and distorted letter shapes in text rendering because their VQ-VAE tokenizers lose fine-grained high-frequency details during quantization. Improving the tokenizer is the natural fix, but retraining it changes the token ID distribution, invalidating all downstream AR model weights and requiring prohibitively expensive retraining of billion-parameter systems. The paper asks whether text rendering can be improved post-hoc without modifying either the tokenizer's token space or the AR model.

## Input / Output

At inference, RDA receives the same discrete token ID sequence that any AR model would produce (e.g., from Janus-Pro or TAR) and the coarse image Î reconstructed by the frozen base decoder. For each token ID, it retrieves a paired embedding from a trainable hint codebook (same index space as the frozen codebook), fuses it with the base feature via addition, and passes the result through a parallel residual decoder to predict a pixel-level correction ΔÎ. The final output is Î + ΔÎ, a refined image with sharper text strokes. Training is conducted at 256×256 resolution but the adapter generalizes to 384, 512, and 1024 resolutions without fine-tuning.

## Method

RDA introduces two lightweight, jointly trained components while keeping the entire base VQ-VAE frozen: (i) a Shared-ID Hint Codebook of size K (matching the original) whose trainable embeddings supply complementary high-frequency cues aligned to each token's semantic cluster, and (ii) a Residual Decoder (a VQ-VAE-style CNN with doubled channels in the last two conv layers) that predicts the pixel-space residual ΔI = I − Î from the fused base+hint features. The fusion is a simple element-wise addition of projected base and hint features, which ablations show is essential—removing the instance-dependent base injection causes training to fail. Training uses a composite objective combining MAE/MSE reconstruction, VGG perceptual loss on both the final image and the residual itself, a Sobel edge-aware L1 loss, and an FFT high-pass frequency-domain loss; the residual perceptual term is the most critical, as its removal collapses performance to baseline. Because the token ID space is never altered, RDA plugs into any pretrained AR model at inference with zero retraining, and a single RDA trained on one tokenizer (e.g., LlamaGen-VQ) transfers across multiple AR architectures (Janus-Pro, TAR) and model sizes.

## Results

On text-specific AR models, RDA delivers the most dramatic gains: Janus-Pro 1B OCR accuracy on StyledTextVisionBlend jumps from 24.52% to 58.26% (+33.74 pts) and on StyledTextSynth from 12.75% to 36.81% (+24.06 pts). On tokenizer reconstruction (high resolution), RDA-adapted LlamaGen-VQ improves OCR accuracy from 58.04% to 66.48% on AnyText-Benchmark, from 47.52% to 57.36% on Mario-Eval, and from 55.58% to 69.49% on StyledTextSynth, while also reducing LPIPS (e.g., 6.52→6.28 on AnyText-Benchmark). On general AR models, gains are more modest but consistent: Janus-Pro 7B improves from 8.85% to 10.07% on AnyText-Benchmark, and TAR 7B from 30.92% to 32.43%. RDA is trained on only 5M images at 256×256, yet generalizes to 512 and 1024 resolutions without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its own base tokenizers, RDA consistently wins: LlamaGen-VQ + RDA outperforms vanilla LlamaGen-VQ by +8.44 Acc on AnyText-Benchmark and +13.91 Acc on StyledTextSynth (high res), and Chameleon-VQ + RDA beats vanilla Chameleon-VQ by +7.37 Acc on AnyText-Benchmark and +34.73 Acc on StyledTextSynth. Against TA-Tok [15], RDA-adapted LlamaGen-VQ substantially outperforms on AnyText-Benchmark (66.48 vs 40.58 Acc) and Mario-Eval (57.36 vs 32.48 Acc), and also on StyledTextSynth (69.49 vs 6.69 Acc), despite using 40× less training data (5M vs 200M). Against UniTok [29], RDA-adapted tokenizers still trail on absolute OCR accuracy (66.48 vs 74.60 on AnyText-Benchmark; 69.49 vs 84.92 on StyledTextSynth), but UniTok requires 256× more training data (1.28B vs 5M images) and critically is not AR-training-free—its altered token distribution necessitates full retraining of downstream AR models, a cost RDA entirely avoids. The key differentiator is that RDA is the only method in the comparison that is both data-efficient and compatible with existing pretrained AR models without any retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the visual autoregressive (AR) generation paradigm (Janus-Pro, TAR, Lumina-mGPT) and its dependence on discrete VQ-VAE tokenizers (LlamaGen-VQ, Chameleon-VQ), positioning the tokenizer's limited reconstruction fidelity as the root cause of poor text rendering. It departs from prior tokenizer-improvement work (UniTok, TA-Tok, hierarchical quantization) by introducing a post-hoc, ID-preserving adapter: a Shared-ID Hint Codebook paired with a pixel-level Residual Decoder, rather than retraining the tokenizer or replacing the decoder with a diffusion head. The residual-learning idea is conceptually related to RQ-VAE but operates at the pixel level and explicitly preserves the token distribution, making it orthogonal to and composable with feature-space improvements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, AR-training-free design is practically significant: billion-parameter AR models can gain substantial text-rendering improvements (up to +33.74 OCR points) without the prohibitively expensive retraining that any tokenizer modification would require. The OOD generalization analysis (FID 11.20 vs. 14.75 for direct decoder fine-tuning) validates the residual approach as a safer enhancement strategy. Open questions include whether RDA extends to RQ-VAE or continuous tokenizers, whether the 256px training resolution limits gains at very high resolutions, and whether the method can address non-text fine-grained details (e.g., small objects, fine textures) beyond its current text-centric training data.
