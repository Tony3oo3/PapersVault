## Problem

In extremely low-light dynamic scenes, photon scarcity causes severe noise and texture loss in both conventional frames and event streams, yet existing event-frame hybrid methods largely ignore the intrinsic noise of both modalities or rely on simple filtering that cannot simultaneously suppress noise and preserve weak signals. No prior approach jointly models the noise in RAW images and events under a unified physical constraint, leaving a fundamental gap in how to disentangle sensor noise from fragile scene structure when both inputs are heavily degraded.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a low-light RAW image (linear-light response, no ISP processing) and a corresponding low-light event stream, both cropped to 256×256 patches. The RAW input is first Gaussian-blurred to provide a coarse illumination prior for event denoising rather than a precise denoised image. The output is a single enhanced sRGB image reconstructed via deterministic DDIM sampling (1000 forward diffusion steps), targeting scenes where the reference is 300×–500× brighter than the RAW input (0.001–0.8 lux).

## Method

NEC-Diff comprises three modules: (1) ECNS performs cross-modal denoising by feeding the blurred RAW illumination prior into an EDformer-style event denoiser and using the resulting high-dynamic-range event edges to guide an event-assisted RAW denoiser, with a physics-derived intensity consistency loss (Eq. 5) enforcing the logarithmic brightness-change relationship between denoised RAW frames and denoised events; (2) SRIE computes per-pixel SNR maps for both modalities from the denoising residuals, then applies a lightweight network with channel-wise softmax to produce spatial weight maps that adaptively up-weight the higher-SNR modality at each location; (3) CAD fuses the weighted features via bidirectional cross-modal attention and injects the concatenated representation as a conditional input to a diffusion model, which reconstructs the clean image through DDIM sampling. Training is two-stage: denoising modules are first trained independently, then jointly optimized with reconstruction, gradient, and consistency losses (λ_grad=10, λ_cons=0.5) using Adam at lr 1e-4 for 50 epochs.

## Results

On the REAL dataset (0.001–0.8 lux), NEC-Diff achieves 24.51 dB PSNR, 0.742 SSIM, and 0.201 LPIPS, outperforming the best RAW-only baseline RID (22.72 dB) by 1.79 dB and the best event-sRGB hybrid ELEDNet (21.58 dB) by 2.93 dB. On the synthetic LLRVD-simu dataset, NEC-Diff reaches 27.74 dB PSNR and 0.125 LPIPS, marginally surpassing the strongest RAW baseline BRVE (27.58 dB) while achieving the best SSIM (0.828) and LPIPS among all methods. The method also demonstrates strong generalization to unseen nighttime sequences, consistently achieving the lowest NIQE scores across varying illumination levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEC-Diff outperforms all 14 baselines across both datasets and all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). On the challenging REAL dataset, it surpasses the best RAW-only method RID by 1.79 dB PSNR (24.51 vs. 22.72) and by 0.057 SSIM, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.201 vs. 0.258). Against the strongest event-sRGB hybrid ELEDNet, NEC-Diff gains 2.93 dB PSNR and 0.080 SSIM, with a notably lower LPIPS (0.201 vs. 0.400), indicating substantially better perceptual quality. On the synthetic LLRVD-simu dataset, NEC-Diff edges out BRVE (the best RAW baseline) by 0.16 dB PSNR and achieves the best SSIM (0.828) and LPIPS (0.125), while sRGB-based methods like LightenDiffusion lag by over 6 dB. Event-only methods (E2VID+, NER-Net) are consistently the weakest, confirming that events alone cannot recover absolute brightness in smooth regions. The ablation further shows that removing the ECNS module drops PSNR by 3.45 dB on REAL, underscoring the critical role of collaborative denoising over any single-modality approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-frame hybrid low-light enhancement line (EvLight, EvLowLight, ELEDNet) by replacing sRGB inputs with RAW images and introducing a physics-driven intensity consistency loss that exploits the logarithmic relationship between clean RAW pixel values and accumulated event amplitudes. For event denoising, it builds on EDformer's transformer architecture but conditions it on RAW illumination priors rather than operating in isolation; for image denoising, it adapts the NoiseModelling/RID framework with event-assisted edge guidance. The final reconstruction stage departs from prior single-network or GAN-based approaches by injecting SNR-weighted cross-modal features into a DDIM diffusion model, a design choice shared conceptually with LightenDiffusion but applied here to a dual-modality conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The REAL dataset (47,800 pixel-aligned RAW–event–sRGB triplets at 0.001–0.8 lux) fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark provides true low-light event-RAW pairs, and existing event datasets operate at much higher illuminations. The physics-based consistency loss offers a reusable constraint for any dual-modality denoising pipeline, and the dual-SNR fusion principle (weighting both modalities by their local reliability rather than defaulting to one) is a generalizable design pattern. The acknowledged limitation—that the learned event contrast threshold C limits generalization across hardware configurations—points to test-time adaptation as a natural next step, and the 300–500× brightness gap in the dataset underscores how far current methods are from solving photon-starved imaging in fully dynamic scenes.
