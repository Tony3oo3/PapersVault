## Problem

Existing Next-Event Prediction (NEP) tasks produce only textual answers, missing video's unique ability to convey spatial layout, motion, and temporal ordering in a customized, intuitive way. Cascaded VLM+VDM pipelines suffer from semantic-to-visual misalignment (linguistically correct captions that are visually unexecutable), while unified models face a capability trade-off between understanding and generation. The paper formalizes Video-Next Event Prediction (VNEP) and addresses the challenge of jointly aligning a VLM and VDM so that the predicted event is both semantically accurate and visually coherent.

## Input / Output

The method takes an input video (3–5 second clip) and a procedural or predictive question as input. The VLM consumes high-level ViT visual features from the input video alongside the tokenized question, while the VDM is conditioned on both the VLM-generated caption and low-level VAE tokens extracted from n sampled input frames, enabling fine-grained visual correspondence (character IDs, backgrounds) while generating a novel scene. The output is a short video (matching the 3–5 second clip length) demonstrating the predicted next event, accompanied by a textual caption produced by the VLM.

## Method

VANS couples Qwen2.5-VL-3B (VLM) with Wan-2.1-1.3B (VDM) and aligns them via Joint-GRPO, a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy. In Stage 1, the VLM is optimized (VDM frozen) with a composite reward combining format compliance, text fidelity (ROUGE-L against ground-truth captions), and video fidelity (CLIP similarity of the VDM's output to ground-truth video), forcing the VLM to internalize the VDM's visual constraints. In Stage 2, the VDM is optimized (VLM frozen) using an anchor caption from the improved VLM, with a reward balancing video fidelity (visual coherence with input) and semantic alignment (CLIPScore between output video and anchor caption), preventing the VDM from ignoring the caption or merely reconstructing the input. The staged design resolves the attribution ambiguity of one-stage joint training—where a poor video makes it unclear whether the caption or generation is at fault—while the joint reward (rather than isolated per-model rewards) ensures the two models co-evolve into a synergistic unit.

## Results

On the procedural benchmark, VANS (Joint-GRPO) achieves ROUGE-L of 0.3631, FVD of 78.32, CLIP-V of 0.8021, and CLIP-T of 0.3824, outperforming the strongest cascaded baseline (Gemini-FilmWeaver: ROUGE-L 0.2802, FVD 110.54, CLIP-V 0.7102) by 29.6% in ROUGE-L and 34.1% in FVD reduction. On the predictive benchmark, VANS achieves ROUGE-L of 0.3058, FVD of 86.85, CLIP-V of 0.7872, and CLIP-T of 0.3759, again leading all baselines. Joint-GRPO provides a substantial gain over the SFT-only version (e.g., ROUGE-L from 0.2812 to 0.3631 and CLIP-V from 0.7655 to 0.8021 on procedural), confirming the effectiveness of the two-stage RL alignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VANS (Joint-GRPO) outperforms all six baseline families on every reported metric across both procedural and predictive benchmarks. Against the strongest cascaded pipeline (Gemini-FilmWeaver), VANS improves ROUGE-L by 0.0829 (0.3631 vs. 0.2802) and reduces FVD by 29.2% (78.32 vs. 110.54) on procedural, and improves ROUGE-L by 0.0760 (0.3058 vs. 0.2298) with 26.6% FVD reduction (86.85 vs. 118.27) on predictive. The unified model Omni-Video is the weakest baseline, scoring ROUGE-L of only 0.1075 (procedural) and 0.1012 (predictive) with FVD exceeding 230, confirming the capability trade-off the paper identifies in single-model approaches. Video-GPT, a pure video-extension model, produces no textual prediction and yields the lowest CLIP-T (0.1997 procedural, 0.2124 predictive), as it generates frames without event reasoning. Among cascaded baselines, swapping the VDM from Wan to FilmWeaver consistently improves CLIP-V and CLIP-T (e.g., Gemini-Wan 0.6898 → Gemini-FilmWeaver 0.7102 on procedural CLIP-V), but none close the gap to VANS, underscoring the benefit of joint RL alignment over independent model optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (originally proposed for single-model alignment in DeepSeek-Math) into a two-stage joint optimization strategy that co-steers a VLM and a VDM under a shared reward, addressing the semantic-to-visual gap that plagues cascaded VLM→VDM pipelines. It deliberately departs from both the cascaded paradigm (where the VLM's text output may be linguistically correct but visually unexecutable) and the unified-model paradigm (where understanding and generation compete for capacity), instead treating the two specialized models as a synergistic unit. The task itself extends established Next-Event Prediction from text-only answers to dynamic video answers, repurposing the NEP formulation for a fundamentally different output modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing VNEP as a task and releasing VANS-Data-100K, this work provides the field with a concrete benchmark and training resource for video-as-answer generation, a direction that could enable more intuitive procedural learning and creative exploration than text descriptions alone. The Joint-GRPO two-stage design offers a general recipe for aligning heterogeneous generative models under a joint reward, potentially transferable to other multi-model pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs/VDMs, whether the staged optimization is strictly necessary at higher model capacities, and how well the method generalizes beyond the 3–5 second clip horizon used in evaluation.
