## Problem

Object-centric 3D generative models use fixed-size latent spaces, so scaling them to town-scale scenes produces blurry, low-detail outputs. Existing training-free workarounds (sequential outpainting) introduce visible seams and rely entirely on the sub-scene generation capability of object-centric models, which is insufficient (vanishing floors, randomly rotated objects). The paper asks how to leverage a pre-trained object-centric flow model to generate large, coherent, image-faithful 3D scenes without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene image as input and additionally extracts a metric-scale point cloud via MoGe-2 as a structural prior. The output is a full 3D scene (Gaussian splatting, radiance field, or mesh) whose spatial extent can be up to 36× the original latent footprint (extension factors a=b=2 in x and y). The image is patchified to spatially align with each 3D latent patch, and the depth-derived camera parameters are used for differentiable rendering during optimization.

## Method

Extend3D extends the x-y dimensions of a pre-trained Trellis latent (both sparse-structure and SLAT) and divides it into overlapping patches that are denoised simultaneously via coupled vector fields (averaged in overlap regions), inspired by MultiDiffusion. To overcome the object-centric model's inability to initialize sub-scenes from pure noise, the pipeline voxelizes a monocular depth point cloud and applies iterative under-noised SDEdit (t_noise < t_start), treating missing geometry as additional noise so the flow model naturally fills occluded regions. At every denoising step, the vector field is further optimized with two 3D-aware losses: a binary cross-entropy loss on the sparse-structure decoder output that prevents initialized voxels from disappearing, and an LPIPS-minus-SSIM rendering loss on the SLAT decoder output that refines texture and eliminates patch seams. Dilated sampling is applied during the sparse-structure phase to maintain global structural consistency.

## Results

On 100 diverse wide-scene images, Extend3D achieves LPIPS 0.240, SSIM 0.611, and PSNR 20.4, substantially outperforming Trellis (LPIPS 0.650, SSIM 0.239, PSNR 10.0), Hunyuan3D (LPIPS 0.683, SSIM 0.255, PSNR 10.4), and EvoScene (LPIPS 0.482, SSIM 0.310, PSNR 13.2). On the UrbanScene3D dataset (45 images with ground-truth meshes), Extend3D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0086 and F-score(0.05) of 0.694, surpassing all baselines. In a human preference study (10 annotators, 14 scenes), Extend3D wins 73.6–87.9% of pairwise comparisons across geometry, faithfulness, appearance, and completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Extend3D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against Trellis and Hunyuan3D (object-centric models used zero-shot), the gains are dramatic: LPIPS drops from ~0.65–0.68 to 0.240, SSIM nearly doubles from ~0.24–0.26 to 0.611, and PSNR more than doubles from ~10 to 20.4, reflecting far better image faithfulness. Against EvoScene (the closest prior method for image-to-3D scene generation), Extend3D still leads by a wide margin on appearance metrics (LPIPS 0.240 vs. 0.482, SSIM 0.611 vs. 0.310) and geometry (CD 0.0086 vs. 0.0188, F-score 0.694 vs. 0.498). In the text-conditioned comparison with SynCity (Table 3), Extend3D achieves higher CLIP (0.276 vs. 0.251), higher HPSv3 (3.519 vs. 3.254), and lower Intra-LPIPS (0.571 vs. 0.631), indicating better text alignment, perceptual quality, and patch consistency. In the human preference study, Extend3D wins 87.1–87.9% of comparisons against EvoScene and 73.6–75.7% against Hunyuan3D across all four criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

Extend3D adapts the 2D high-resolution generation paradigm (MultiDiffusion's overlapping patch fusion, DemoFusion's dilated sampling) to the 3D domain by extending the fixed-size latents of Trellis, an object-centric latent flow model. It departs from prior training-free scene pipelines (SynCity, 3DTown, EvoScene) that generate sub-scenes sequentially via outpainting, instead coupling all patches simultaneously through shared denoising dynamics. The SDEdit-based initialization is modified with a novel 'under-noising' schedule (t_noise < t_start) that treats 3D structural incompleteness as recoverable noise, and 3D-aware optimization objectives (voxel-preservation BCE for sparse structure, LPIPS+SSIM rendering loss for SLAT) are introduced to counteract the object-centric model's tendency to deviate from scene-like geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that town-scale 3D scene generation from a single image is achievable without any scene-specific training, by combining structural priors (monocular depth point clouds) with carefully designed denoising schedules and optimization. The under-noising insight—that a generative model can be coaxed to fill 3D occlusions by perceiving missing geometry as noise—offers a general principle for 3D completion tasks. However, the authors note that occluded-region completion remains sometimes incomplete, SLAT optimization is memory-prohibitive for very large scenes, and street-level images with strong vanishing-point perspective still degrade performance, leaving open the question of whether this framework can generalize to arbitrary camera viewpoints and even larger urban extents.
