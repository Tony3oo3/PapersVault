## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods reconstruct scenes in a single pass, where any initial depth estimation error is irreversibly propagated into all downstream Gaussian parameters. This one-shot formulation deprives the model of any self-correction mechanism, causing it to underperform optimization-based approaches that iteratively refine geometry and appearance, particularly in poorly constrained or non-overlapping regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263144/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N sparse multi-view RGB images at 256×256 resolution along with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It outputs a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, covariance, opacity, color) per pixel that are rendered via differentiable rasterization into novel-view images. The iterative loop runs for K=3 passes by default, with each pass producing an intermediate Gaussian set; the final output is the last iteration's Gaussians, and the model uses only 42.6M parameters total.

## Method

iSplat reformulates 3D Gaussian estimation as a learnable recurrent process driven by a ConvGRU-based optimizer that maintains a hidden state H_k coupling geometry and appearance across K iterations. Two key mechanisms operate within each iteration: (1) an uncertainty-driven depth refinement strategy that computes the standard deviation of the previous depth probability distribution to elastically expand or contract the depth search bins, enabling adaptive geometric self-correction; and (2) a region-aware enhancement module that uses the refined depth to project pixels into other views, producing an overlap mask that routes overlapping regions through multi-view correspondence features and non-overlapping regions through monocular priors from Depth Anything V2. The dual-branch encoder extracts multi-view features via a shared CNN + Transformer backbone and monocular features from a pre-trained depth network. Training uses a multi-stage rendering loss (L1 + LPIPS) with exponential decay weighting across iterations, and the GRU update integrates cost volumes, region-aware features, and previous Gaussian attributes into the hidden state.

## Results

On RealEstate10K, iSplat achieves 27.67 dB PSNR, 0.891 SSIM, and 0.110 LPIPS with only 42.6M parameters, surpassing DepthSplat_L² (27.47 dB, 354M params) by +0.2 dB while using roughly 1/8 the parameters. On ACID, iSplat reaches 29.01 dB PSNR and 0.126 LPIPS, outperforming the next-best MonoSplat (28.63 dB) by +0.38 dB. On the challenging cross-dataset Re10k→DTU transfer, iSplat delivers 18.26 dB PSNR, a +2.88 dB improvement over DepthSplat (15.38 dB), demonstrating strong generalization under significant domain shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

iSplat outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across both in-domain (RealEstate10K, ACID) and cross-dataset (DTU, ACID) evaluations. Against the strongest same-scale competitor DepthSplat_S¹ (38M params, the direct architectural baseline), iSplat gains +0.83 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K (27.67 vs. 26.84) with only 4.6M additional parameters. Against the much larger DepthSplat_L² (354M), iSplat still leads by +0.2 dB PSNR while using ~1/8 the parameters. On ACID, iSplat beats MonoSplat (28.63 dB) by +0.38 dB and MVSplat (28.25 dB) by +0.76 dB. The most striking margin appears in cross-dataset generalization: on Re10k→DTU, iSplat achieves 18.26 dB PSNR versus 15.38 dB for DepthSplat (+2.88 dB) and 15.25 dB for MonoSplat (+3.01 dB), with SSIM improving from 0.415 to 0.698. On Re10k→ACID, iSplat leads DepthSplat by +0.28 dB (28.65 vs. 28.37) and MonoSplat by +0.41 dB. Older NeRF-based and light-field baselines (pixelNeRF, MuRF, GPNR, Du et al.) are consistently 1–7 dB behind, confirming that feed-forward 3DGS methods have largely superseded these approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

iSplat builds directly on DepthSplat (Xu et al., CVPR 2025), adopting its simplest single-scale configuration as the architectural baseline, but fundamentally departs from the single-pass feed-forward 3DGS paradigm shared by MVSplat, pixelSplat, MonoSplat, and others. The key conceptual shift is importing the iterative refinement philosophy from optical flow, stereo matching, and video-to-depth (e.g., DRO, iterative residual learning) into the 3D Gaussian splatting setting, replacing a one-shot depth-then-Gaussian cascade with a ConvGRU-driven recurrent loop. The method also synthesizes multi-view Transformer features with monocular Depth Anything V2 features through a region-aware mechanism, drawing on the complementary strengths of both feature families identified in the multi-view stereo literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

iSplat demonstrates that a lightweight recurrent optimizer (42.6M params) can match or exceed much larger single-pass models (354M DepthSplat) while offering a natural quality-latency trade-off via iteration count, making it practically attractive for deployment where a few extra milliseconds are acceptable. The 2.88 dB PSNR gain on the challenging Re10k→DTU cross-dataset transfer suggests iterative self-correction learns more domain-robust geometric priors than one-shot regression. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger scene extents or higher resolutions, whether more than three iterations ever become beneficial, and whether the GRU-based optimizer can be replaced by lighter alternatives (e.g., linear attention) without sacrificing the refinement quality.
