## Problem

Always-on video sensing on edge and wearable devices is infeasible with continuous high-fidelity RGB capture, as smart glasses sustain only 30–60 minutes of recording before power exhaustion. Existing approaches either trigger RGB based on auxiliary modalities (e.g., audio) with no visual fallback, or process all frames uniformly, ignoring the substantial color redundancy present in natural video streams. This paper asks whether a low-power grayscale stream can serve as an always-on monitor that selectively triggers RGB capture only when chromatic detail is genuinely needed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260137/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a continuous grayscale video stream (luminance channel from CIELAB, resized to 224×224, sampled at 1 FPS up to 128 frames) and, only when triggered, acquires the corresponding RGB frame at 448×448. Grayscale frames are tokenized into 64 visual tokens while triggered RGB frames yield 256 tokens; the resulting mixed-length token sequence is assembled in native temporal order and fed to a frozen MLLM decoder (InternVL-3.5-8B) alongside a text query. The per-frame output is a binary trigger decision (acquire RGB or not), and the final output is a text answer to the video question.

## Method

ColorTrigger is a training-free, strictly causal pipeline with two components. The online trigger maintains a sliding window (W=30) of grayscale frames, extracts CLIP ViT-B/16 CLS features, and constructs a windowed affinity matrix via an affine-transformed Gram matrix to capture pairwise redundancy. A lightweight quadratic program (solved with OSQP) assigns diversity-driven weights to window frames, and the current frame's weight serves as an importance score; a credit-budgeted controller (with target rate r and capacity cap C) gates the binary trigger to enforce long-horizon RGB spend bounds. The dynamic token router then allocates asymmetric decoder capacity: untriggered grayscale frames traverse a high-compression path (64 tokens) while triggered RGB frames use a high-capacity path (256 tokens), preserving temporal order without any architectural modification or retraining of the backbone MLLM.

## Results

On StreamingBench (All tasks), ColorTrigger with only 8.1% RGB frames (91.9% reduction) achieves 70.72, recovering 91.6% of the full-color InternVL-3.5-8B baseline (77.20) and improving 8.64 points over the grayscale-only setting (62.08). With 34.3% RGB frames, it reaches 75.24 (97.5% recovery). On Video-MME, ColorTrigger with 37.6% RGB frames actually surpasses the full-RGB baseline (66.1 vs. 65.6), while on OVO-Bench it achieves 52.5 with 33.1% RGB (vs. 57.7 full RGB), a modest 5.2-point drop accompanied by a 66.9% reduction in color frame usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On StreamingBench, ColorTrigger at 34.3% RGB (75.24) outperforms all open-source streaming MLLMs including Dispider-7B (67.63) and VideoLLM-online-8B (35.99), and surpasses proprietary models GPT-4o (73.28) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (72.44), while remaining within 0.12 points of TimeChat-Online-7B (75.36) and 0.45 points of Gemini 1.5 Pro (75.69) — all while using 65.7% fewer RGB frames. At the extreme 8.1% RGB setting, ColorTrigger (70.72) still exceeds LLaVA-OneVision-7B (71.12 is close but uses 100% RGB) and substantially outperforms all streaming-specific models. On Video-MME, ColorTrigger at 37.6% RGB (66.1) surpasses the full-RGB InternVL-3.5-8B baseline (65.6) and all other baselines including TimeChat-Online-7B (62.4) and Dispider-7B (57.2). On OVO-Bench, ColorTrigger at 33.1% RGB (52.5) outperforms all open-source streaming MLLMs (TimeChat-Online-7B: 46.7, Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 50.3) but trails the full-RGB InternVL-3.5-8B (57.7) by 5.2 points. The grayscale-only ablation (62.08 on StreamingBench) confirms that the trigger mechanism adds 8.64 points over no-color, validating the selective RGB acquisition strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of streaming video understanding, efficient MLLM inference, and event-driven sensing, but does not directly build on a single prior method. It departs from streaming MLLM work (VideoLLM-online, Flash-VStream, ReKV) that focuses on memory and attention mechanisms by instead reducing the sensing cost upstream—acquiring RGB only when needed. The QP-based diversity selection echoes DPP and submodular video summarization, but is repurposed for online, causal, per-frame triggering rather than offline subset selection. The credit-budgeted controller and dynamic token routing are novel engineering contributions that have no direct predecessor in the MLLM efficiency literature, which typically operates after frames are already sampled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact for always-on edge and wearable AI is substantial: extending continuous video recording from ~30–60 minutes toward all-day operation by reducing the dominant energy cost (RGB capture and transmission) by over 90%. The empirical finding that a grayscale-only stream already recovers ~80% of full-color MLLM performance, and that sparse selective color adds another ~10 points, provides a concrete, actionable design principle for sensor-fusion architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to color-critical domains (medical imaging, art analysis), how it behaves at higher frame rates or with multi-camera rigs, and whether the QP solver overhead remains acceptable on the most constrained MCUs. The method is also question-agnostic, so query-adaptive triggering remains an unexploited direction.
