## Problem

Existing unsupervised semantic correspondence methods generate pseudo-labels via nearest-neighbor matching in 2D feature space, which operates locally and ignores global structural relationships. This local, appearance-only matching fails to resolve geometric ambiguities arising from symmetries, repetitive features, or extreme viewpoint changes, producing semantically plausible but geometrically incorrect correspondences that degrade downstream learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261049/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of source and target RGB images, segments instance regions with SAM, decomposes them into uniform grid patches, and extracts both semantic features (from DINOv2 and Stable Diffusion) and 3D coordinates (from VGGT) per patch. During pseudo-label generation, it produces an N×M probabilistic transport plan; at inference, the trained adapter network outputs dense cosine-similarity maps between patches, from which sub-pixel correspondences are recovered via soft-argmax. Notably, the 3D foundation model is used only during label generation and is discarded at test time, so inference requires only 2D features.

## Method

SoY reformulates pseudo-label generation as a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) optimal transport problem that jointly optimizes inter-feature similarity (classical Wasserstein cost) and intra-geometric structure (Gromov-Wasserstein cost computed from 3D coordinates lifted by VGGT). To make the non-convex quadratic GW term tractable, the method introduces anchor-based linearization: it iteratively selects K=64 cycle-consistent high-confidence anchor pairs from the current transport plan, then replaces one quadratic factor with a fixed sparse anchor distribution, reducing the problem to a linear cost that can be solved with Sinkhorn. Unbalanced OT with KL marginal penalties handles occlusions and non-overlapping regions. For training, a lightweight adapter on DINOv2+SD features is supervised by a soft-target loss that dynamically blends the structurally consistent hard pseudo-labels with the network's own current semantic transport plan (with stopped gradients), preventing over-penalization of semantically similar but unselected candidates, combined with a dense correspondence loss using differentiable soft-argmax.

## Results

On SPair-71k, SoY achieves 67.9% PCK@0.1, 50.8% PCK@0.05, and 10.0% PCK@0.01, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline DINOv2+SD (63.5%) by 4.4 percentage points. On the AP-10k animal pose benchmark (zero-shot), SoY reaches 68.0% (intra-species), 65.8% (cross-species), and 52.9% (cross-family) PCK@0.1, surpassing DINOv2+SD by 2.5, 2.5, and 1.8 points respectively. The method achieves best or second-best per-category performance on 17 out of 18 SPair-71k categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoY outperforms all five compared baselines on SPair-71k PCK@0.1: ASIC (49.0%), DINOv2 (55.7%), DIFT (57.7%), DistillDIFT (59.8%), and DINOv2+SD (63.5%), with the largest margin over DistillDIFT at +8.1%p. On AP-10k, SoY leads DINOv2+SD by 2.5%p on intra-species and cross-species splits and by 1.8%p on the hardest cross-family split, while also surpassing DistillDIFT by 2.5%p (I.S.) and 3.0%p (C.S.). The gains are most pronounced under stricter thresholds (PCK@0.05: +2.5%p over DINOv2+SD; PCK@0.01: +1.2%p), indicating improved precision rather than merely broader recall. Notably, SoY achieves these results without explicit geometric annotations (camera poses, 3D models, or viewpoint labels), relying only on SAM masks and category prompts—information shared with the baselines—while additionally leveraging a 3D foundation model (VGGT) for pseudo-label generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-label-based semantic correspondence line (ASIC, DIY-SC, DistillDIFT) by replacing local nearest-neighbor matching in 2D feature space with a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein optimal transport formulation that jointly optimizes inter-feature similarity and intra-geometric structure. It draws on the GW/OT literature (Sinkhorn solvers, unbalanced OT, anchor-based linearization from graph alignment) and repurposes 3D foundation models (VGGT) to supply the intra-structure distances that 2D appearance features fundamentally cannot provide. The key conceptual departure from prior work is defining the GW intra-structure in 3D geometric space rather than 2D feature space, which resolves the geometric ambiguity that plagues appearance-only matching under symmetries and repetitive textures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SoY establishes that 3D geometric constraints from foundation models can be injected into pseudo-label generation to resolve ambiguities that 2D features alone cannot, achieving SOTA on SPair-71k and AP-10k without any explicit geometric annotations (poses, 3D models, viewpoints). The anchor-based linearization makes the otherwise intractable quadratic GW problem practical, a technique potentially transferable to other structure-aware matching tasks. Limitations include dependence on 3D foundation models that can produce flat reconstructions or fail on transparent surfaces, and residual failures on highly symmetric parts. An open question is whether the 3D lifting step can be replaced by learned geometric priors, reducing the pipeline's dependency on a separate foundation model.
