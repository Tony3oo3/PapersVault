## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting enables high-quality static scene editing, extending these edits to dynamic 4D scenes remains unsolved. Existing approaches that adapt 2D diffusion models to 4D representations produce spatial distortions, temporal flickering, and inconsistent style propagation, and the deformation network trained on original geometry cannot generalize to edited configurations where Gaussians have been cloned, split, or pruned.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260225/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input (1) first-frame edited 3D Gaussians produced by an external 3D editor (DGE, DreamCatalyst, or SGSST) and (2) the original 4D Gaussian scene comprising canonical Gaussians and a learned deformation field (Swift4D for multi-camera, 4DGS for monocular). It outputs a fully edited 4D Gaussian scene with temporally coherent motion and appearance, rendered at the original scene's resolution across all frames. The framework is agnostic to the specific 3D editor used and supports both localized object edits and global style transfer.

## Method

Catalyst4D decouples spatial editing from temporal propagation via two modules. Anchor-based Motion Guidance (AMG) constructs sparse, structurally representative anchors on both original and edited first-frame Gaussian clouds using line-based sampling on bounding spheres with neighborhood cylinder tests, then establishes region-level correspondences via unbalanced optimal transport (Sinkhorn) to avoid cross-region interference; deformations are propagated by opacity- and Mahalanobis-distance-weighted averaging of matched source Gaussian motions. Color Uncertainty-guided Appearance Refinement (CUAR) renders Gaussian-driven optical flow to warp reliable first-frame images into subsequent frames, estimates per-Gaussian color uncertainty from multi-view spherical-harmonic discrepancies, binarizes this into an artifact mask, and selectively refines only uncertain regions using the warped images as pseudo-ground-truth while regularizing non-artifact areas against the pre-refinement rendering.

## Results

On three scenes (Sear-steak, Coffee-martini from DyNeRF; Trimming from MeetRoom), Catalyst4D achieves the highest CLIP similarity across all scenes (0.252, 0.249, 0.251), indicating the strongest semantic alignment with editing instructions. Temporal consistency (VBench) is competitive, reaching 0.983–0.986 on DyNeRF scenes and 0.967 on MeetRoom, matching or slightly trailing CTRL-D in one case. Training time is 40–50 minutes, substantially faster than Instruct 4D-to-4D (~2 h) and comparable to Instruct-4DGS (30–40 min) and CTRL-D (50–55 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Catalyst4D outperforms all three baselines on CLIP similarity across every evaluated scene, with margins of 0.003–0.032 over the strongest competitor (CTRL-D) and up to 0.032 over I4DGS on Sear-steak. Against Instruct 4D-to-4D, Catalyst4D leads on both CLIP similarity and temporal consistency while being roughly 2.4× faster (50 min vs. 2 h). Against Instruct-4DGS, Catalyst4D achieves higher CLIP similarity (e.g., 0.252 vs. 0.220 on Sear-steak) and consistency, at a modest 10-minute training cost. Against CTRL-D, the gap is narrow: Catalyst4D wins on CLIP similarity in all three scenes (0.252/0.249/0.251 vs. 0.249/0.246/0.248) but CTRL-D edges ahead on temporal consistency for Sear-steak (0.985 vs. 0.983), which the authors attribute to not retraining the deformation network. Overall, Catalyst4D delivers the best semantic fidelity with competitive temporal stability and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Catalyst4D inverts the dominant paradigm in dynamic scene editing: rather than adapting 2D diffusion models to supervise 4D representations (as Instruct 4D-to-4D, CTRL-D, and Instruct-4DGS do), it starts from high-quality static 3D Gaussian edits (via DGE, DreamCatalyst, or SGSST) and propagates them forward in time. The propagation mechanism draws on two distinct threads: (1) anchor-based correspondences via unbalanced optimal transport (Sinkhorn), a technique borrowed from point-cloud registration, repurposed here for region-level deformation transfer; and (2) Gaussian-driven optical flow warping with uncertainty-based selective refinement, extending ideas from 4DGS uncertainty-aware regularization. The framework is built on top of existing 4D Gaussian backbones (Swift4D, 4DGS) without modifying their deformation networks, positioning it as a plug-in propagation layer rather than a new 4D representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling spatial editing from temporal propagation, Catalyst4D makes the rich ecosystem of 3D Gaussian editors immediately applicable to dynamic scenes, lowering the barrier to 4D editing. However, the paper acknowledges that it does not retrain the deformation network or adjust Gaussian density after editing, which limits its applicability to large structural changes and may explain the slight consistency drop on one scene. Open questions include whether this propagation strategy scales to longer sequences with complex topology changes, whether the anchor-based approach generalizes to multi-object scenes with occlusion, and whether the framework can be made real-time. The modest but consistent gains over 2D-diffusion-based baselines suggest that geometry-aware propagation is a more reliable path than pixel-space supervision for 4D editing.
