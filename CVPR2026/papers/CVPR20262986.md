## Problem

Existing spike camera super resolution (SCSR) methods operate on fixed-length spike sequences, restricting accessible temporal information to a local neighborhood, while spike fluctuations from photon randomness, readout quantization, and thermal noise further hinder reliable intensity extraction. These two limitations collectively cap the reconstruction quality of SCSR, particularly for dynamic scenes where long-range temporal cues are critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262986/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long low-resolution binary spike stream (e.g., 180×320×461 for ×4 scale or 90×160×461 for ×8 scale) and produces a sequence of high-resolution images (720×1280) at 45 or 90 temporal points. The input is divided into overlapping short-term spike blocks (2w+1 frames each) for initial feature extraction, and the recurrent propagation then sequentially processes these blocks along the temporal axis. The sequence length is not fixed at inference time, enabling reconstruction from arbitrarily long spike streams.

## Method

Spk2VidNet is a hierarchical recurrent network that performs two-layer propagation with progressively enlarged temporal receptive fields to mitigate spike fluctuations. Within each propagation block, a Multi-Frame Consistency Guided Alignment (MFCA) module jointly estimates and mutually refines inter-frame motion fields by exploiting motion coherence across K preceding frames, then applies deformable convolution for temporal alignment. A Content-Aware Modulation Fusion (CMF) module adaptively aggregates aligned features using multi-dilation modulation that assigns spatially varying scale and shift parameters based on multi-scale similarity. To handle long sequences within GPU memory, the paper introduces segment-wise training with state transfer, where the last several recurrent states from each segment are detached and carried into the next segment, preserving temporal continuity without full-sequence backpropagation.

## Results

On the REDS-LSSR evaluation set (×4), Spk2VidNet achieves 29.92 dB PSNR, 0.8481 SSIM, and 0.2624 LPIPS, outperforming all four baselines. On the longer-sequence Adobe240-LSSR set (×4), it reaches 32.36 dB PSNR, 0.9264 SSIM, and 0.1607 LPIPS. At ×8 scale, Spk2VidNet obtains 26.20 dB (REDS-LSSR) and 27.19 dB (Adobe240-LSSR) PSNR. The method also delivers the fastest inference (43 ms at ×4, 21 ms at ×8) with only 3.73 M parameters, compared to 187–1088 ms for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spk2VidNet consistently outperforms all four baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both datasets and both ×4 and ×8 scales. At ×4 on REDS-LSSR, it gains +0.42 dB over the closest competitor SCSRNet (29.92 vs. 29.50) and +1.50 dB over VidarSR (29.92 vs. 28.42). On the longer Adobe240-LSSR sequences, the advantage widens to +1.05 dB over SCSRNet and Spk2SRImgNet (32.36 vs. 31.31), suggesting the recurrent long-sequence modeling becomes more beneficial as temporal context grows. In terms of efficiency, Spk2VidNet is 4–25× faster than baselines (43 ms vs. 187–1088 ms at ×4) while using fewer parameters (3.73 M vs. 3.34–12.79 M) and the lowest GPU memory (4606 M vs. 4416–8248 M). At ×8, the PSNR gap over SCSRNet is +0.39 dB on REDS-LSSR and +1.04 dB on Adobe240-LSSR, with runtime dropping to 21 ms—roughly 3× faster than the next-best SCSRNet (61 ms).

## Relation to Prior Work

Spk2VidNet repurposes the recurrent propagation and deformable-alignment machinery from video super-resolution (BasicVSR++, EDVR, TDAN) for the spike-camera super-resolution (SCSR) setting, where prior methods (VidarSR, SpikeSRNet, Spk2SRImgNet, SCSRNet) are confined to fixed-length spike segments of ~101 frames. The key conceptual departure is a two-layer hierarchical propagation that progressively enlarges the temporal receptive field to suppress spike fluctuations, combined with a multi-frame consistency guided alignment (MFCA) that jointly estimates and cross-refines inter-frame motions rather than estimating them independently. The segment-wise training with state transfer is a practical training strategy borrowed in spirit from truncated BPTT but tailored to the recurrent feature buffers of the SCSR pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By extending SCSR from short fixed-length windows to arbitrarily long spike streams, the paper opens the door to exploiting the full temporal richness that spike cameras provide (40,000 fps), which prior methods could not access. The segment-wise training strategy is a reusable recipe for any recurrent video model that must handle sequences longer than GPU memory allows. However, the task remains niche (spike-camera SR), the PSNR gains over the strongest baseline (SCSRNet) are modest (~0.4 dB), and the approach has not been validated on other neuromorphic sensors or on real-world long-sequence deployment, leaving open whether the hierarchical propagation design generalizes beyond the simulated REDS/Adobe240 settings.
