## Problem

Online RL methods (e.g., Flow-GRPO) for reward alignment of rectified flow diffusion models suffer from three critical issues: they require expensive full-chain online sampling at every optimization step, they depend on ODE-to-SDE approximation to introduce the stochasticity needed for policy gradients, and they are prone to severe reward hacking that degrades image quality and prompt alignment. No existing method combines the proven benefits of group-level reward optimization from LLMs with the deterministic, efficiency-sensitive nature of rectified flow models.

## Input / Output

GDRO operates fully offline on pre-generated groups of 16 images per prompt (at 512×512 resolution) with explicit rewards assigned by task-specific models (OCR accuracy for text rendering, GenEval for object attributes). During training, each image is perturbed with noise at a random timestep and fed to the diffusion model to predict velocity; no online sampling or full denoising chain is required. The output is a LoRA-adapted FLUX.1-dev model (rank 32, with EMA) that generates higher-reward images at inference time using 28 timesteps.

## Method

GDRO derives a group-level loss by applying softmax to the implicit reward function s_θ(x,t) = −β‖v − v_θ(C)‖² + β‖v − v_ref(C)‖² (from Diffusion-DPO) over a group of k images, then computing cross-entropy between the model's implicit reward distribution and the explicit reward distribution at each ranking position. This is equivalently justified via the Plackett-Luce ranking model, showing the loss pushes the model to enlarge likelihood margins between ranked samples. A top-1 likelihood stabilization regularizer (one-hot masked velocity matching) prevents the highest-reward sample's likelihood from degrading during optimization. Because the implicit reward is computable at any single timestep from pre-generated images, GDRO requires no online sampling and no stochastic sampler, making it both offline and sampler-independent. The method reduces to DPO when k=2 and τ→0, and to a pure ranking loss when explicit rewards are unavailable.

## Results

On the OCR task, GDRO achieves an evaluation score of 0.8721 with a corrected score of 0.5701 in 29.60 GPU hours, matching Flow-GRPO's 0.8714 evaluation score while using only half the compute (29.60 vs. 59.73 GPU hours) and yielding a higher corrected score. On GenEval, GDRO reaches 0.8517/0.5148 in 68.4 GPU hours, matching Flow-GRPO's 0.8520 evaluation score at 3.7× lower cost (68.4 vs. 250.27 GPU hours) and again achieving the highest corrected score. DPO collapses on both tasks, and DanceGRPO is slower than Flow-GRPO with comparable corrected scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow-GRPO, GDRO matches the OCR evaluation score (0.8721 vs. 0.8714) at 2× lower GPU cost (29.60 vs. 59.73 hours) and achieves a higher corrected score (0.5701 vs. 0.5482), indicating less reward hacking; on GenEval the evaluation scores are essentially tied (0.8517 vs. 0.8520) but GDRO is 3.7× faster (68.4 vs. 250.27 hours) with a substantially higher corrected score (0.5148 vs. 0.4757). Against DanceGRPO, GDRO is consistently faster (29.60 vs. 74.67 on OCR; 68.4 vs. 294.53 on GenEval) while achieving higher corrected scores on both tasks. Against DPO, GDRO is clearly superior: DPO collapses on both tasks (GenEval score drops to 0.6488 with a corrected score of 0.4162, below the FLUX.1 baseline), whereas GDRO remains stable and reaches competitive evaluation scores. Against the FLUX.1 baseline, GDRO improves the OCR score from 0.5843 to 0.8721 and the GenEval score from 0.6178 to 0.8517, while preserving UnifiedReward coherence and style scores much more closely than any RL-based method.

## Relation to Prior Work

GDRO builds directly on the implicit reward function formulation from DPO (Rafailov et al.) and its diffusion-model adaptation in Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al.), which showed that a policy's implicit reward can be computed at any diffusion timestep from the predicted velocity. It departs from the online RL paradigm (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) that requires ODE-to-SDE approximation for stochasticity and costly online rollouts, instead performing group-wise reward alignment entirely offline using pre-generated image groups. The group-level objective is derived through a Plackett-Luce ranking model, extending the pairwise DPO loss to full-group soft-target cross-entropy, and the method is positioned as a sampler-independent alternative that preserves the group-reward benefits observed in LLM post-training (GRPO) without inheriting their computational overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDRO offers a practically important efficiency gain (2×–3.7× fewer GPU hours) and removes the ODE-to-SDE approximation that can degrade image quality, making group-level reward alignment more accessible for rectified-flow diffusion models. The reward hacking analysis and the proposed corrected score (combining task reward with UnifiedReward quality metrics) provide a more honest evaluation framework that the field currently lacks. Key limitations include the offline-only design (no online exploration for rewards requiring active action seeking), the approximate nature of the corrected score, and evaluation restricted to a single model (FLUX.1) at 512×512 resolution, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, higher resolutions, or tasks where online exploration is essential.
