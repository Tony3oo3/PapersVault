## Problem

Existing methods for distilling latent diffusion models and rectified flow models into few-step generators either demand large training resources (e.g., DMD2, HyperSD) or suffer significant quality degradation in text-image alignment. Moreover, prior approaches typically produce separate checkpoints for each inference step count, and one-step generation is not always optimal for applications with low tolerance for quality loss. The paper targets the gap of achieving a single unified model that performs well across 2–4 sampling steps with minimal training cost and no external image-text dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled model (LogCM) takes a text prompt as input and generates a 1024×1024 RGB image in 2 to 4 sampling steps, with a single unified checkpoint supporting all step counts. Training is entirely image-free: synthetic latents are produced by the teacher model (SDXL or FLUX.1-dev) via ODE denoising, eliminating the need for a text-image pair dataset. The student is implemented as a LoRA adapter (rank 64) on top of the frozen teacher's U-Net/DiT, keeping trainable parameters at ~662M.

## Method

LogCD decomposes consistency distillation into two stages. In the local stage, the full time range [0,T] is split into M=8 sub-intervals, and consistency is enforced only within each interval using a fixed ending timestep (rather than random sampling as in CTM/HyperSD), with stop-gradient replacing EMA to save memory. In the global stage, consistency is enforced across the M+1 milestone timesteps using a multi-step teacher solver (p=3) to reduce discretization error at large skip sizes, combined with a GAN loss for distribution consistency and an image-free DMD loss to align outputs with real data. A latent LPIPS (L-LPIPS) model, trained from scratch on BAPPS with a VGG backbone adapted to latent channels, replaces pixel-space perceptual loss to enforce perceptual consistency without decoding to RGB. The entire pipeline requires only 70 A100 GPU hours (12K local + 3K global iterations for SDXL) and produces a unified model whose quality improves monotonically from 2 to 4 steps.

## Results

On SDXL, 3-step LogCM achieves a CLIP Score of 33.5 on MSCOCO-2017 5K and 35.2 on MJHQ-5K, surpassing all 4-step baselines (Lightning, DMD2, HyperSD, PCM, Flash SDXL) while using fewer steps and only 70 A100 GPU hours of training. On FLUX.1-dev, 4-step LogCM reaches a CLIP Score of 32.6 with FID 30.5 (MSCOCO) and 24.7 (MJHQ), matching the 25-step Euler teacher (CS 32.6, FID 31.6/24.2) at a four-fold inference speedup. The unified model supports 2–4 steps with seamless quality scaling, and 3-step LogCM even outperforms the 25-step teacher on ImageReward (0.95 vs. 0.97 for FLUX; comparable for SDXL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On FLUX.1-dev (4-step), LogCM outperforms LCM by +1.0 CS and −4.8 FID on MSCOCO, and beats HyperSD by +0.7 CS and −3.6 FID on the same benchmark, while matching the 25-step Euler teacher on CLIP Score (32.6 vs. 32.6). Schnell achieves slightly higher CS (33.3 vs. 32.6) and lower FID (28.7 vs. 30.5), but trains the full 24B-parameter model whereas LogCM trains only 662M LoRA parameters. On SDXL (3-step), LogCM surpasses 4-step Lightning, DMD2, HyperSD, PCM, and Flash SDXL on both CLIP Score and FID, using only 1.8% of DMD2's training time and 9% of HyperSD's. The key differentiator is that LogCM is a single unified model supporting 2–4 steps, whereas Lightning, PCM, and DMD2 require separate checkpoints per step count. LogCM's 3-step SDXL result (CS 33.5) is comparable to the 25-step teacher, and its ImageReward (0.95) even exceeds the teacher's, attributed to the DMD loss aligning the output distribution with real data.

## Relation to Prior Work

LogCD extends the consistency distillation (CD) framework by decomposing the full-range consistency objective into a two-stage local-to-global pipeline: local CD enforces consistency within predefined sub-intervals, while global CD enforces consistency across interval boundaries. This departs from one-stage approaches (LCM, PCM) that struggle with the full time range, and from HyperSD's multi-stage design that introduces redundant sub-trajectory consistency and complex training. The method further integrates LPIPS adapted to latent space (L-LPIPS) for perceptual consistency and incorporates DMD in an image-free manner, combining elements from prior distillation work into a unified, efficient training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme training efficiency (70 A100 hours for SDXL, ~1.8% of DMD2's cost) combined with a single unified model supporting 2–4 inference steps, which is directly deployable in production pipelines where quality degradation tolerance is low. The method generalizes across both LDMs (SDXL) and RFMs (FLUX.1-dev), broadening its applicability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation or even higher-resolution settings, whether the DMD component (dropped for FLUX due to memory constraints) is essential for the best results, and whether the 2-step regime can be further improved without additional steps.
