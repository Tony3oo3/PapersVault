## Problem

Infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) is inherently ill-posed with no unique ground truth, yet existing methods optimize handcrafted objective metrics (entropy, SSIM, gradient measures) that are divorced from genuine human perceptual preferences. This misalignment is compounded by the absence of a large-scale human feedback dataset for IVIF and the lack of an automated reward mechanism to quantify perceptual quality and guide model optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261770/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The fusion network takes a registered pair of infrared and visible images and produces a single fused image preserving thermal radiation information and visible texture details. The reward model consumes all three images (infrared, visible, fused) simultaneously and outputs four fine-grained quality scores on a 1–5 scale (thermal retention, texture retention, artifact level, sharpness) plus a spatial artifact probability heatmap. The human feedback dataset comprises 850 image pairs from eight benchmark datasets, each fused by eleven SOTA methods to yield 9,350 annotated fused images.

## Method

The framework has three components. First, a human feedback dataset is constructed by fine-tuning GPT-4o on 100 expert-labeled seed images to automatically score and annotate all 9,350 fused images, followed by expert review. Second, a Fusion-Oriented Reward Model based on a frozen ViT-Large-Patch16-384 encoder processes the three input images through a weight-shared ViT, concatenates their patch-level features, and passes them through a second ViT for cross-modal fusion; two prediction branches then regress the four quality scores and generate the artifact heatmap via MSE loss. Third, the DCEvo fusion network is fine-tuned using a GRPO-inspired policy optimization: SAM segments each fused image into K semantic regions, the reward model scores each region, normalized relative advantages are computed within the group, and a clipped-ratio objective with KL divergence regularization updates the policy to maximize human-preference-aligned quality while preventing excessive deviation from the reference policy.

## Results

On the three main IVIF benchmarks, the proposed method achieves the highest CC and PSNR across all datasets: CC of 0.51/0.56/0.65 and PSNR of 65.43/61.84/65.09 on TNO, RoadScene, and M³FD respectively. For no-reference quality, it attains the best NIQE (5.37/3.06/4.03) and BRISQUE (22.58/18.79/29.80) on TNO and RoadScene, with BRISQUE second-best on M³FD (29.80 vs. SHIP's 27.75). On downstream tasks, fused images yield the best mIoU (56.92) on FMB segmentation and the best mAP (62.23) on M³FD detection, surpassing all 13 compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 compared baselines on CC and PSNR across all three datasets. Compared to the DCEvo baseline (before RLHF fine-tuning), the method gains +0.03 CC and +1.60 dB PSNR on TNO, +0.07 CC and +2.18 dB PSNR on RoadScene, and +0.10 CC and +2.22 dB PSNR on M³FD, confirming the benefit of GRPO-based human-feedback optimization. Against the strongest individual competitors—SegMif (CC 0.50/0.53/0.55) and CDDFuse (CC 0.47/0.52/0.62)—the method leads by 0.01–0.10 in CC and 1.3–2.3 dB in PSNR. On no-reference metrics, the method achieves the best NIQE and BRISQUE on TNO and RoadScene but is narrowly behind SHIP on M³FD BRISQUE (29.80 vs. 27.75). In downstream evaluation, fused images from the proposed method yield the highest mIoU (56.92 vs. 56.64 for DDFM) on FMB segmentation and the highest mAP (62.23 vs. 61.17 for SegMif) on M³FD detection, indicating that human-preference-aligned fusion translates into tangible gains for practical vision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the RLHF paradigm—well established in NLP (InstructGPT, LLaMA-2) and recently extended to text-to-image/video generation—into the infrared-visible image fusion domain, where optimization has long been dominated by handcrafted pixel-level losses and objective metrics (entropy, SSIM, gradient measures) that are decoupled from human perceptual judgment. The fusion backbone is DCEvo, a discriminative cross-dimensional evolutionary encoder-decoder, which the authors fine-tune rather than redesign. The optimization strategy adapts GRPO (originally proposed for mathematical reasoning in LLMs) to the image domain by using SAM to partition fused images into semantic regions and computing region-level relative advantages, a non-trivial repurposing of a text-centric algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first large-scale human feedback dataset for IVIF (9,350 fused images across 850 pairs and 11 models), filling a critical gap that has prevented the field from aligning fusion quality with human perception. The practical impact is clear: fused images that better serve downstream human-in-the-loop applications such as surveillance and driver assistance. However, several open questions remain: the framework is validated on a single baseline (DCEvo), the GPT-4o-assisted annotation pipeline may inherit model-specific biases, and it is unclear whether the ViT-based reward model generalizes to substantially different fusion architectures or to video fusion. Whether the RLHF fine-tuning recipe transfers to other ill-posed fusion tasks (multi-exposure, multi-focus) is an important direction for future work.
