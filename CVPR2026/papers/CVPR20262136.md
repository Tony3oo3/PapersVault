## Problem

Existing text-to-shape retrieval methods are limited to a small set of predefined object categories and assume canonical poses, making them impractical for real-world 3D repositories where objects span diverse classes and appear in arbitrary orientations. While rotation-invariant architectures exist for point cloud recognition, none are built on state-space models, and the SOTA RI-Transformer incurs quadratic complexity that precludes scalable cross-modal training. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the first rotation-invariant Mamba architecture and a scalable annotation-free training pipeline for retrieval across 200+ categories under arbitrary SO(3) rotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an unordered point cloud of N points (optionally with RGB color) and a natural language text query as input. The point cloud is processed by selecting G representative centers via Farthest Point Sampling and gathering k neighbors per center via kNN to form local patches. The output is a global point cloud embedding (a single C-dimensional vector from average pooling) that is aligned in a shared space with CLIP's text and image embeddings, enabling retrieval by cosine similarity against a database of 3D shapes.

## Method

RI-Mamba achieves rotation invariance through a three-stage design: (1) each local patch is aligned into its own PCA-based Local Reference Frame to factor out pose from geometry, (2) a Global Reference Frame combined with a Hilbert space-filling curve imposes a rotation-invariant 1D token ordering on the patches, and (3) a linear-time orientational embedding computed as MLP(F_i^T F_g) encodes each patch's orientation relative to the global frame, reintegrating pose information lost during canonical alignment. These embeddings are modulated into hidden states via FiLM (feature-wise linear modulation) at every layer, and bidirectional scanning (alternating left-to-right and right-to-left across layers) enables full contextual aggregation despite Mamba's unidirectional nature. Training uses cross-modal contrastive learning (InfoNCE) with automatically generated point-cloud–image–text triplets from rendered meshes, eliminating manual annotation and enabling scaling to 200+ object categories.

## Results

On the OmniObject3D zero-shot text-to-shape retrieval benchmark (214 categories), RI-Mamba pretrained on the Ensemble dataset achieves RR@1 of 19.02 and NDCG@5 of 34.39 under canonical poses, and maintains RR@1 of 19.34 and NDCG@5 of 33.76 under arbitrary SO(3) rotations — substantially outperforming both non-RI models (DuoMamba: RR@1 drops from 15.82 to 7.83) and prior RI methods (RI-Transformer: RR@1 of 9.67/10.39). On the supervised Text2Shape benchmark, RI-Mamba reaches RR@1 of 13.87 (canonical) and 13.20 (SO(3)), matching or exceeding part-label-based SOTA (SCA3D: 13.74/2.24) without any part annotations. For zero-shot 3D-to-3D retrieval, RI-Mamba achieves mAP of 47.58 (I/I) and 47.50 (SO(3)/SO(3)) with Ensemble data, remaining nearly invariant to rotation while non-RI models drop from ~50 to ~24 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RI-Mamba outperforms all nine baselines across nearly all evaluation settings. Against non-RI models (PointBERT-TAMM, DuoMamba), RI-Mamba achieves comparable or slightly lower canonical-pose accuracy (e.g., 19.02 vs. 15.82 RR@1 on OmniObject3D with Ensemble data) but dramatically superior rotation robustness — DuoMamba's RR@1 collapses from 15.82 to 7.83 under SO(3), while RI-Mamba actually improves slightly (19.02→19.34). Against prior RI methods (LocoTrans, RI-Transformer), RI-Mamba wins by large margins on both canonical and rotated settings: on OmniObject3D with Ensemble data, RI-Mamba achieves 19.02 RR@1 vs. RI-Transformer's 9.67 and LocoTrans's 7.75, a gap of roughly 2×. On the supervised Text2Shape benchmark, RI-Mamba (13.87 RR@1) matches SCA3D (13.74) which uses part segmentation labels, while SCA3D's SO(3) performance collapses to 2.24 RR@1 versus RI-Mamba's 13.20. On zero-shot 3D-to-3D retrieval, RI-Mamba (47.58 mAP I/I) slightly trails PointBERT-TAMM (49.84) in the canonical setting but dominates all rotation settings (47.50 vs. 24.42 mAP for PointBERT under SO(3)/SO(3)). On zero-shot classification, RI-Mamba achieves the best OmniObject3D accuracy (34.4/34.5%) among all methods, while RI-Transformer edges ahead on the smaller ModelNet40 (78.3 vs. 76.7%), which the authors attribute to overfitting on the 40-class set.

## Relation to Prior Work

RI-Mamba sits at the intersection of three research threads: rotation-invariant point cloud architectures (RI-Transformer, LocoTrans, spherical CNNs), state-space models for 3D (PointMamba, DuoMamba), and cross-modal contrastive learning for 3D understanding (ULIP, TAMM, OpenShape). It departs from prior RI work by being the first to achieve SO(3) invariance within a pure Mamba backbone, replacing RI-Transformer's quadratic attention with linear-time sequential processing and a patchwise (rather than pairwise) orientational embedding. It also departs from prior text-to-shape retrieval methods (Text2Shape, Parts2Words, Tricolo) by eliminating manual annotations and part-segmentation labels through automated triplet generation, scaling from 2–13 categories to 200+.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes the first benchmark for text-to-shape retrieval under realistic conditions—arbitrary orientations and 200+ object categories—filling a gap where prior evaluations assumed canonical poses and narrow category sets. The 10× memory reduction over RI-Transformer (2 GB vs. 20 GB at 2048 tokens) makes rotation-invariant 3D retrieval practically deployable for large-scale asset libraries in VR and gaming. A key open question is the reliance on PCA-based reference frames, which the authors acknowledge can be unstable on symmetric or noisy shapes; more robust frame estimation or learned equivariant alternatives remain to be explored.
