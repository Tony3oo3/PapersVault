## Problem

Existing image restoration methods struggle with unknown composite degradations: all-in-one models rely on closed-set classifiers (e.g., CLIP) that limit generalization, while agentic models are flexible but slow due to disjoint IQA and IR modules that require image decoding and LLM-based tool selection. No prior work tightly integrates a descriptive, open-vocabulary IQA with a generative restorer into a single end-to-end trainable model capable of iterative, adaptive restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image of arbitrary resolution as input, with no prior knowledge of which degradations are present. It produces a restored high-quality RGB image after a variable number of iterative restoration rounds (averaging ~2.4 rounds at inference). All intermediate processing—quality assessment, conditioning, and restoration—occurs entirely in the latent space of the restoration model, avoiding pixel-space round-trips. The IQA module outputs free-form text descriptions of degradations, but these are consumed as latent embeddings rather than decoded text.

## Method

RAR integrates DepictQA (a VLM-based IQA) with a Stable Diffusion 3.5 Flow Matching restorer via two trainable adapters: one aligns the restoration model's image latents to the IQA's input space, and the other maps the IQA's output latents directly to the restorer's conditioning embeddings, eliminating the text-decoding bottleneck. The restoration uses a noise-free Flow Matching formulation that learns a direct velocity mapping from the degraded image distribution to the high-quality distribution, enabling meaningful intermediate quality assessments without noise contamination. At each iteration, the LQA re-evaluates the current latent to update the conditioning signal, and a pairwise comparison stopping criterion (CONTINUE/STOP) determines when restoration is sufficient. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end in two stages (adapter-only, then full unfreezing), with the iterative conditioning integrated into standard flow-matching training by randomly sampling timesteps at which to invoke the LQA.

## Results

On the most challenging composite degradation setting (Group C, 3 degradations), RAR achieves PSNR 19.33, SSIM 0.6579, LPIPS 0.1489, MANIQA 0.4653, CLIP-IQA 0.6554, and MUSIQ 56.56, outperforming the previous best (AgenticIR) by +0.51 dB PSNR, +0.11 SSIM, and +0.196 MANIQA while being 11.27× faster. On unknown degradations (UDC), RAR reaches MUSIQ 55.97 and CLIP-IQA 0.602, surpassing AgenticIR (52.76 / 0.358) by a wide margin. On single degradation tasks, RAR achieves the best perceptual scores (LPIPS 0.0699, MANIQA 0.4125, CLIP-IQA 0.5566) but trails AutoDIR on PSNR (25.88 vs. 27.81), which the authors attribute to RAR correcting more degradations than the ground truth addresses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAR outperforms all nine baselines on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, MANIQA, CLIP-IQA, MUSIQ) across all three evaluation settings. Against the strongest agentic baseline AgenticIR [48], RAR achieves substantially lower LPIPS (0.1299 vs. 0.3148 in Group A; 0.1489 vs. 0.4493 in Group C) and higher MANIQA (0.4659 vs. 0.3071; 0.4653 vs. 0.2698), while also being 11.27× faster (6.29 s vs. 48.00 s wall-clock in Group A). Against the strongest all-in-one baseline AutoDIR [14], RAR wins decisively on perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.1299 vs. 0.3967 in Group A) but trails slightly on PSNR in single-degradation tasks (25.88 vs. 27.81), which the authors attribute to RAR correcting additional degradations beyond the ground truth. Among the remaining all-in-one baselines (AirNet, PromptIR, MiOIR, DA-CLIP, InstructIR, AdaIR, DARCOT), RAR leads on every metric in every group, with the largest gaps on LPIPS and MANIQA in the three-degradation Group C setting (e.g., LPIPS 0.1489 vs. 0.5782 for AirNet). On unknown degradations (UDC), RAR surpasses AgenticIR by +3.21 MUSIQ and +0.244 CLIP-IQA, and outperforms AutoDIR by +6.70 MUSIQ and +0.291 CLIP-IQA, confirming stronger generalization to unseen degradation types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two existing paradigms for multi-degradation restoration: all-in-one generative models (AutoDIR, PromptIR, MiOIR) and agentic tool-selection systems (AgenticIR, RestoreAgent). It departs from AutoDIR by replacing CLIP's closed-set degradation classification with DepictQA's free-form VLM-based assessment, and from agentic models by eliminating the pipeline of separate single-degradation restorers in favor of a single unified generative model. The key architectural departure is the tight latent-space integration of IQA and IR via adapters, enabling end-to-end training and iterative re-assessment without pixel-space round-trips, combined with a noise-free Flow Matching (SD3.5) formulation that makes intermediate latents directly interpretable by the quality assessor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.27× speedup over AgenticIR while achieving substantially better perceptual quality (LPIPS roughly 2× lower) makes iterative composite-degradation restoration practically deployable, addressing a real bottleneck in current agentic pipelines. The finding that iterative conditioning actively harms diffusion-based backbones but benefits flow-matching ones provides a useful design principle for future iterative generative systems. Limitations include slightly lower fidelity (PSNR) than AgenticIR on some composite settings, and the observation that the model sometimes 'over-restores' beyond ground truth in single-degradation tasks, suggesting the stopping criterion and training objective could be refined. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video restoration or to degradation combinations beyond the 16 tested, and whether the DepictQA-based assessment generalizes to truly novel degradation types not seen during training.
