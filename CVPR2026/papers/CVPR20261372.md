## Problem

Existing audio-driven avatar systems either rely on video diffusion models that lack explicit 3D representation, full-body coverage, and identity stability over long sequences, or on parametric 3D avatar pipelines that route audio through a lossy pose-prediction bottleneck (SMPL/SMPL-X + LBS), where quantization, retargeting, and per-frame tracking errors accumulate and suppress micro-articulations like bilabial closures, cheek inflation, and fine hand gestures. No prior method simultaneously builds a full-body, photorealistic conversational avatar from a single image and drives it directly from audio at high temporal precision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image (arbitrary resolution, one subject) and a driving audio waveform sequence as input, and produces a temporally aligned full-body talking human video rendered via differentiable Gaussian splatting. The 3D Gaussian representation is dense over expressive regions (face, hands) and sparse elsewhere for efficiency, and the output is a sequence of rendered frames that can be directly extracted as a video without iterative denoising, enabling real-time inference of the same personalized identity.

## Method

AudioAvatar represents the avatar as a particle-based deformation field of 3D Gaussian primitives in a canonical space, where audio features directly modulate per-particle trajectories—eliminating any intermediate parametric pose. A CLIP-style contrastive module aligns audio features with particle-motion features at both frame and patch granularity, embedding them in a shared semantic manifold. A diffusion Transformer then generates whole-body particle motions from the aligned audio, followed by a dedicated Transformer refinement module for face and hand subsets to capture fine-grained articulations. Training is supervised by pseudo-ground-truth Gaussian deformations learned from synthetic talking videos produced by large audio-driven video diffusion models, with a video score distillation loss injecting the teacher's generative prior and a trajectory alignment loss enforcing temporally coherent 4D motion paths. An ARAP distance-preserving regularizer between k-nearest Gaussians across adjacent timesteps further stabilizes the deformation.

## Results

AudioAvatar achieves the best score on every metric in the main comparison (Table 1). Against the strongest audio-driven video diffusion baseline (HunyuanVideo-Avatar), it improves SyncC from 6.90 to 7.20, reduces SyncD from 7.12 to 5.42, lowers FID from 17.2 to 12.4 (−27.9%), and reduces FVD from 320 to 240 (−25.0%). Against the strongest single-image Gaussian avatar baseline (PERSONA), it gains +8.8% IQA (4.22 vs 3.88), +14.3% SyncC (7.20 vs 6.30), and −34.4% FID (12.4 vs 18.9). Hand-gesture fidelity (HKC) reaches 0.897, the highest across all methods, and identity preservation (CSIM) peaks at 0.551.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioAvatar outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the two audio-driven video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo-Avatar and OmniAvatar), the gains are most pronounced in video-level coherence: FID drops by 27.9% and 33.3% respectively, and FVD drops by 25.0% and 31.4%, indicating substantially better temporal consistency and generative diversity. Against the two single-image Gaussian avatar methods (PERSONA and LHM), which require an external audio-to-pose converter (MotionCraft), AudioAvatar leads in perceptual quality (IQA +8.8% over PERSONA), lip-audio sync (SyncC +14.3%, SyncD −20.3%), and low-level fidelity (SSIM +4.7%, PSNR +4.3%). The weakest baseline, EchoMimicV2, trails by a wide margin on all metrics (SyncD 10.20 vs 5.42; FVD 420 vs 240). Hand-gesture confidence (HKC) is consistently highest for AudioAvatar (0.897), confirming the benefit of the dedicated face-and-hand refinement module over both diffusion-based and pose-driven approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate paradigms: audio-driven video diffusion models (HunyuanVideo-Avatar, OmniAvatar, EchoMimicV2) that synthesize talking videos without explicit 3D structure, and 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar methods (PERSONA, LHM, 4DGS) that produce photorealistic but pose-driven avatars. The key architectural departure is replacing the conventional audio→parametric-pose→LBS-rendering pipeline with a particle-based deformation field in canonical 3D Gaussian space, where a diffusion Transformer directly predicts per-particle trajectories from audio features. Training relies on a novel distillation strategy that injects motion priors from large video diffusion models via video score distillation sampling and trajectory alignment, combined with CLIP-style contrastive alignment between audio and particle-motion embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the lossy audio-to-pose intermediary, the framework addresses a fundamental synchronization bottleneck that has limited the realism of single-image full-body talking avatars, particularly for micro-articulations like bilabial closures and fine hand gestures. The distillation-from-diffusion-models approach offers a practical recipe for transferring motion knowledge from large generative models into efficient, identity-preserving 3D representations, a pattern likely applicable beyond avatars. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences (current clips are 5–10 s), multi-person interaction, real-time inference latency of the diffusion Transformer, and whether the single-image constraint fundamentally limits the diversity of learned deformations.
