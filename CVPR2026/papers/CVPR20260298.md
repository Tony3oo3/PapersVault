## Problem

In Federated Semi-Supervised Learning, existing methods handle external heterogeneity (cross-client distribution shift) via fixed or data-size-weighted aggregation that is vulnerable to outliers, and internal heterogeneity (labeled/unlabeled mismatch within clients) by discarding low-confidence unlabeled samples, which reduces data participation. No prior work addresses both issues simultaneously through a unified mechanism that avoids privacy leakage or extra communication overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260298/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a partially-labeled set of 32×32 RGB images (10% or 20% labeled per category) partitioned non-IID via Dirichlet(α) with α ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1} across 20 clients. The model (ResNet-8) outputs a C-dimensional class logit vector; the key intermediate artifact is the final fully-connected layer weight matrix ω ∈ ℝ^{C×d}, which serves as the category proxy shared between local training and server-side tuning without additional communication.

## Method

ProxyFL uses the learnable classifier weights as a unified proxy for category distribution at both local and global levels. On the server, Global Proxy Tuning (GPT) initializes global proxies by averaging local classifier weights, then fine-tunes them with a contrastive objective that pulls each global proxy toward same-category local proxies and away from other-category proxies, making the global distribution robust to outlier clients. On each client, Indecisive-Categories Proxy Learning (ICPL) re-includes low-confidence unlabeled samples by constructing a multi-category 'indecisive set' ξ_i constrained by a dynamic global category prior P'_G(Y), then trains all samples via a positive-negative proxy pool where the positive proxy for a low-confidence sample is a confidence-weighted sum of proxy vectors over its indecisive categories, and negatives are all batch samples whose category sets are disjoint. The total loss combines standard supervised CE, KL-divergence pseudo-labeling on high-confidence samples, the ICPL contrastive loss, and the server-side GPT loss.

## Results

ProxyFL achieves state-of-the-art test accuracy across all four datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, CINIC-10) under three levels of Dirichlet heterogeneity (α = 0.1, 0.5, 1) with 10% labels. The most significant gains appear under high heterogeneity (α = 0.1): +3.32% on CIFAR-100 (57.50% vs. SAGE's 54.18%) and +3.39% on CINIC-10 (77.98% vs. 74.59%). On SVHN with α = 0.1, ProxyFL reaches 95.09%, approaching the fully-labeled upper bound of FedAvg-SL (94.11%). Convergence analysis shows ProxyFL reaches 30%/40%/50% accuracy targets 2.64×/2.72×/3.18× faster than the LPL baseline on CIFAR-100 (α = 0.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProxyFL outperforms all 8 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity level. The closest competitor is SAGE [22], which ProxyFL surpasses by 0.61–3.39% depending on dataset and α; the gap is largest on CIFAR-100 and CINIC-10 under high heterogeneity (α = 0.1), where SAGE's confidence-discrepancy mechanism still discards low-confidence samples. FedDure [1] and FedDB [39] are the next-strongest FSSL baselines, trailing ProxyFL by 1.2–4.7% on CIFAR-100 and 2.9–7.1% on CINIC-10. The vanilla FL+SSL combinations (FixMatch-LPL, FixMatch-GPL, FedProx+FixMatch, FedAvg+FlexMatch) are consistently 3–10% below ProxyFL, confirming that simply combining SSL and FL without addressing heterogeneity is insufficient. Notably, on SVHN (α = 0.1) ProxyFL (95.09%) even exceeds the fully-labeled FedAvg-SL upper bound (94.11%), suggesting the proxy-guided framework generalizes beyond what full supervision achieves under non-IID partitioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FSSL line of work (SAGE, FedDure, FedDB, FedMatch) by replacing the standard two-pronged strategy—dynamic aggregation weights for external heterogeneity and confidence-based filtering for internal heterogeneity—with a unified mechanism: the classifier's final-layer weights serve as 'proxies' for category distribution. The Global Proxy Tuning (GPT) module departs from averaging-based aggregation by explicitly optimizing global proxies via a contrastive objective against local proxies, while the Indecisive-Categories Proxy Learning (ICPL) module re-introduces low-confidence unlabeled samples through a multi-category set and a positive-negative proxy pool, drawing on contrastive learning rather than simple pseudo-labeling. The paper also contrasts its proxy approach with prototype-based methods (FedProto), arguing that proxies avoid privacy risks of high-dimensional feature sharing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified proxy formulation offers a clean, low-overhead solution to a persistent tension in FSSL: discarding low-confidence samples reduces data participation, while including them risks pseudo-label noise. The 2.6–3.2× convergence speedup and the approach to FedAvg-SL (fully-labeled) performance on SVHN/CINIC-10 at α=0.1 are practically meaningful for deployment scenarios with limited annotation budgets. However, all experiments use ResNet-8 on four small-scale datasets with 20 clients and 10% labels, leaving open whether the proxy mechanism scales to larger architectures, more clients, or higher-dimensional feature spaces. A key open question is whether the contrastive proxy pool generalizes beyond image classification to tasks where the final-layer weight interpretation is less natural (e.g., segmentation, detection).
