## Problem

Feed-forward 4D driving scene reconstruction methods suffer quadratic complexity in sequence length, rely on restrictive kinematic assumptions for dynamic objects, and lack any mechanism to revisit and correct earlier predictions as new observations arrive. Per-scene optimization methods avoid these issues but require hours of per-scene fitting. The paper addresses the gap of efficiently modeling long-range (8s–16s) dynamic driving sequences with fast, generalizable inference while retaining the iterative refinement quality of optimization-based approaches.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of multi-view RGB images (front, front-left, front-right cameras at 10 Hz, sampled every 5th frame) along with camera poses and tracked 3D bounding boxes (up to 32 per frame, ranked by enclosed LiDAR points). It produces a persistent 4D scene representation as a growing set of 3D Gaussians, each carrying a learned temporal lifespan parameter, from which novel views and depth maps are rendered. A key structural detail is that the scene is maintained as a token set (3D position + 768-d feature) updated recurrently in a camera-centric local coordinate frame, with only K=3600 visible tokens processed per step.

## Method

UFO abstracts the render-compare-update loop of 3DGS optimization into a single ViT-style transformer encoder (patch size 8, dim 768) that, at each timestep, jointly refines existing scene tokens and generates new ones from the incoming image in a feed-forward pass—no explicit rendering or gradient backpropagation is needed. A visibility-based filtering mechanism selects only the K nearest tokens within the camera frustum for each update, reducing attention complexity from quadratic to near-linear in sequence length. For dynamic objects, the model learns a soft assignment matrix (row-wise softmax over dot-product similarity between scene tokens and Fourier-embedded box tokens) and applies blended rigid transforms via quaternion averaging on SO(3), while a per-Gaussian temporal lifespan (Gaussian decay in opacity) captures fine-grained non-rigid motion without constant-velocity assumptions. Training is progressive (2s clips → 8s sequences) with a composite loss of L2 photometric, LPIPS, L1 LiDAR depth, lifespan regularization, object-assignment cross-entropy, and sky-mask terms.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, UFO achieves 27.26 PSNR / 0.825 SSIM / 5.45 D-RMSE on 2s sequences, 27.39 / 0.830 / 5.10 on 8s sequences, and 27.04 / 0.816 / 5.08 on zero-shot 16s sequences. Notably, UFO's performance remains nearly constant across sequence lengths, while the strongest feed-forward baseline STORM degrades from 26.38 to 22.02 PSNR over the same range. UFO also exhibits near-linear time and memory scaling with sequence length, using ~25% less peak GPU memory than STORM on 16s sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFO outperforms all six baselines across every sequence length and metric. Against the strongest feed-forward baseline STORM, UFO gains +0.88 dB PSNR on 2s, +2.91 dB on 8s, and +5.02 dB on zero-shot 16s, with the gap widening as sequences grow longer due to STORM's quadratic complexity and constant-velocity motion assumption. The iterative variant of STORM (processing independent short clips) degrades even more severely, dropping to 19.88 PSNR on 16s. Among per-scene optimization methods, Street Gaussians is the closest competitor on 16s (22.67 PSNR) but still trails UFO by 4.37 dB; PVG and DeformableGS show larger depth errors (D-RMSE 14–18 vs. UFO's ~5). GS-LRM, the other feed-forward baseline, suffers the steepest degradation (25.18 → 16.98 PSNR from 2s to 16s), confirming that global-attention feed-forward architectures struggle with long sequences. UFO's near-constant performance across 2s/8s/16s (27.26/27.39/27.04 PSNR) is the key differentiator, demonstrating that the recurrent visibility-filtered update avoids the compounding errors that plague both optimization and one-shot feed-forward approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFO positions itself at the intersection of per-scene optimization (3DGS, PVG, Street Gaussians) and feed-forward reconstruction (STORM, GS-LRM), abstracting the iterative render-compare-update loop of 3DGS into a learned, gradient-free recurrent transformer. Conceptually, it draws on streaming 3D reconstruction methods (CUT3R, Point3R, Spann3R) for the idea of maintaining persistent scene state across timesteps, but departs from them by being bidirectional (refining past predictions) and supporting photorealistic novel view synthesis. The temporal lifespan parameterization is borrowed from PVG, and sky rendering follows STORM, but the visibility-based filtering mechanism and soft object-assignment with quaternion blending are novel contributions that address the quadratic-complexity bottleneck of prior feed-forward driving methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-linear scaling in sequence length and the zero-shot generalization from 8s to 16s sequences make UFO practically relevant for autonomous driving simulation, where driving logs routinely span tens of seconds and closed-loop training demands fast, generalizable scene reconstruction. A key open question is whether the recurrent refinement mechanism can handle arbitrarily long sequences without error accumulation, and whether the method extends beyond the single-dataset (Waymo), three-camera evaluation. The reliance on off-the-shelf 3D bounding boxes for dynamic object guidance also limits applicability to settings where such annotations are unavailable or unreliable.
