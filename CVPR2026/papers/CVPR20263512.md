## Problem

Raster images fuse all visual content into a single entangled canvas, so any edit inevitably propagates through the entire pixel space, causing semantic drift and geometric misalignment in unedited regions. Existing global and mask-guided editing methods cannot fundamentally resolve this consistency issue because the problem lies in the image representation itself, not in model design or data engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263512/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) as primary input, optionally conditioned on a text prompt auto-generated by Qwen2.5-VL, and produces a variable number N (up to 20) of semantically disentangled RGBA layers (N×H×W×4), each comprising an RGB color component and an alpha matte. The layers are ordered such that sequential alpha blending from bottom to top exactly reconstructs the original image, and no compression is applied along the layer dimension since layers exhibit no cross-layer redundancy.

## Method

Qwen-Image-Layered is an end-to-end diffusion model built on three components: (1) an RGBA-VAE that extends the Qwen-Image VAE to four channels, encoding both RGB and RGBA images into a shared latent space to eliminate the distribution gap between input and output; (2) a VLD-MMDiT architecture that uses Multi-Modal attention to concatenate text, input image, and target layer sequences along the sequence dimension for joint intra-layer and inter-layer modeling, combined with a Layer3D RoPE that introduces a dedicated layer index dimension to support a variable number of layers; and (3) a three-stage training strategy that progressively adapts the pretrained Qwen-Image generator from text-to-RGB/RGBA, to text-to-multi-RGBA, and finally to image-to-multi-RGBA decomposition. Training uses the Rectified Flow (Flow Matching) objective, and a data pipeline extracts and annotates multilayer images from real-world Photoshop PSD files to address the scarcity of high-quality training data.

## Results

On the Crello dataset for Image-to-Multi-RGBA decomposition, Qwen-Image-Layered-I2L achieves RGB L1 of 0.0594 and Alpha soft IoU of 0.8705 with zero layer merges, improving to 0.0363 and 0.9160 with five allowed merges. For RGBA image reconstruction on AIM-500, the proposed RGBA-VAE achieves PSNR 38.83, SSIM 0.9802, rFID 5.31, and LPIPS 0.0123, outperforming AlphaVAE (FLUX) by 1.88 dB in PSNR and 6.48 in rFID. The ablation study confirms that all three components (Layer3D RoPE, RGBA-VAE, and multi-stage training) contribute substantially, with the full model reducing RGB L1 from 0.2809 to 0.0594 and increasing Alpha soft IoU from 0.3725 to 0.8705 at zero merges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen-Image-Layered-I2L outperforms all decomposition baselines on Crello across every merge setting. Against LayerD [36], the closest competitor, it reduces RGB L1 from 0.0709 to 0.0594 (−16%) and increases Alpha soft IoU from 0.7520 to 0.8705 (+11.9 points) at zero merges, with the gap widening at higher merge allowances (0.0363 vs. 0.0396 RGB L1; 0.9160 vs. 0.8650 Alpha IoU at 5 merges). The VLM-based and YOLO-based pipelines [7] trail substantially, with Alpha soft IoU below 0.76 even at 5 merges. For RGBA reconstruction on AIM-500, the proposed RGBA-VAE surpasses AlphaVAE [40] (FLUX) by 1.88 dB PSNR and halves rFID (5.31 vs. 11.79), and outperforms LayerDiffuse [49] (SDXL/SDXI) by 2.1–6.7 dB PSNR. In qualitative editing comparisons, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 [42] struggles with resizing, repositioning, and introduces pixel-level shifts, whereas the layered approach handles these operations natively. For Text-to-Multi-RGBA synthesis, ART [32] fails to follow complex prompts (missing objects), while Qwen-Image-Layered-T2L produces semantically coherent layers, and the T2I+I2L pipeline further improves visual aesthetics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Qwen-Image's MMDiT architecture and Flow Matching training objective, extending the VAE to handle RGBA channels (inspired by AlphaVAE) and introducing a Layer3D RoPE for variable-length layer sequences. It departs conceptually from prior image decomposition methods (LayerD, Accordion, LayeringDiff) that rely on multi-stage pipelines of segmentation/matting followed by inpainting, instead framing decomposition as a single end-to-end diffusion generation problem. The variable multi-layer formulation extends the common two-layer (foreground/background) setting found in most prior work, and the joint composite-plus-layers training objective follows ART's approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical paradigm for consistency-preserving image editing by physically isolating content into independent RGBA layers, sidestepping the fundamental entanglement problem of raster representations that plagues both global and mask-guided editing methods. However, the approach is primarily validated on graphic design images (Crello) and PSD-derived data, leaving open how well it generalizes to complex natural scenes with many overlapping, semi-transparent objects. The reliance on PSD files for training data, while clever, limits scalability and diversity; whether the end-to-end decomposition quality holds for photorealistic content with hundreds of interacting elements remains to be seen. The released code and data pipeline lower the barrier for follow-up work in layered generation and editing.
