## Problem

The remote sensing foundation model landscape is highly fragmented, with over 70 models each trained on different sensors, resolutions, and objectives, so no single model captures the full breadth of geospatial knowledge. Rather than pretraining yet another model from satellite data or forcing users to pick one model per task, this paper asks whether a unified representation can be learned directly from the weights of existing models to generate new, task-ready network parameters on demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260835/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, GeoSANE takes a prompt model (e.g., an ImageNet-pretrained ViT-L or Swin-B from timm) and a target architecture specification. The prompt's weights are reshaped per-layer into 2D matrices, split into fixed-size tokens of dimension 230 with 3D positional embeddings (sequence position, layer index, within-layer position) and a binary padding mask, then encoded into the latent space. A KDE is fitted around the prompt's latent vector, and samples are decoded into full weight tensors for the target architecture (ViT-L, Swin-B, ResNet-18, MobileNetV2, UNet, etc.), producing 10 candidate models from which the top-3 are retained for downstream fine-tuning.

## Method

GeoSANE is a ~900M-parameter GPT-2-style transformer autoencoder trained in weight space with a combined masked reconstruction loss (L2 on real parameters only) and an NT-Xent contrastive loss over two augmented views of the same model. It is first pretrained on ~700M tokens from a general computer vision model corpus on HuggingFace, then finetuned for 150 epochs on ~165M tokens from 103 heterogeneous remote sensing models (~38B total parameters) spanning ViTs, Swins, ResNets, UNets, YOLOs, and vision-language models. The key architectural choice is tokenizing weights into fixed-size vectors with 3D positional encodings and runtime loss normalization (rather than layer-wise weight normalization), which enables learning a shared latent manifold across fundamentally different architectures and non-RGB modalities. Generation proceeds by encoding a prompt model's weights, fitting a local KDE in latent space, sampling nearby points, and decoding them into new weight tensors that share the prompt's architecture but carry aggregated geospatial knowledge from the entire training population.

## Results

GeoSANE-generated models achieve the best or second-best results across all 10 remote sensing benchmarks, with headline gains of +1.8 mIoU on Sen12Flood (85.2 vs. 83.4 for CROMA), +1.6 accuracy on California Wildfires (94.9 vs. 93.3), and +0.3 mAP@0.5 on DIOR (79.0 vs. 78.7 for SkySense). On the GEO-Bench standardized suite, GeoSANE leads on m-BigEarthNet (74.2 mAP vs. 73.1 for TerraFM) and m-So2Sat (65.7 vs. 64.9), while trailing TerraFM by 0.9 on m-EuroSAT. For lightweight model generation (11M and 3.5M parameters), GeoSANE outperforms magnitude pruning, variational dropout, and knowledge distillation by up to +16.4 mAP on BigEarthNet and +10.4 accuracy on fMoW at the 11M budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest RSFM baselines, GeoSANE wins on 6 of 10 benchmarks (Sen12Flood, Cal.Wildfires, BigEarthNet, DFC2020, DIOR, and m-BigEarthNet/m-So2Sat on GEO-Bench) and is within 0.1–0.8 of the best on the remaining four (EuroSAT trails CROMA by 0.3; RESISC45 trails DOFA by 0.8; fMoW trails CROMA by 0.1; Sen1Floods11 trails CROMA by 1.3). Compared to model merging via DARE [62], GeoSANE improves by +2.7 on EuroSAT (99.1 vs. 96.4), +10.4 on RESISC45 (96.5 vs. 86.1), and +19.7 on BigEarthNet (88.7 vs. 69.0), confirming that latent-space generation substantially outperforms parameter-space interpolation. For lightweight model generation, GeoSANE-generated ResNet-18 (11M) and MobileNetV2 (3.5M) models beat magnitude pruning, variational dropout, and knowledge distillation by +1.1 to +16.4 across all six classification datasets, with the largest gap on BigEarthNet (+16.4 at 11M, +12.6 at 3.5M) and fMoW (+10.4 at 11M). On GEO-Bench, GeoSANE surpasses TerraFM [8] on m-BigEarthNet (+1.1) and m-So2Sat (+0.8) but trails it on m-EuroSAT (−0.9) and m-Brick-Kiln (−0.4); it also outperforms Prithvi-EO 2.0 [50], AnySat [1], and Galileo [52] on m-BigEarthNet and m-So2Sat by 3–14 points. GeoSANE-generated models also consistently exceed their own ImageNet-pretrained prompts (ViT-L/Swin-B) by +1.0 to +6.5 across all ten tasks, validating that the weight-space generation process injects meaningful geospatial knowledge beyond generic pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSANE builds directly on the weight-space autoencoder framework of Schürholt et al. (ICML 2024), which tokenizes model parameters into sequences and trains an encoder-decoder to reconstruct them, and on Falk et al. (2025), which demonstrated that such representations can be learned from heterogeneous models on public hubs like HuggingFace rather than curated model zoos. The key departure is domain and heterogeneity: prior weight-space learning was limited to homogeneous populations or standard RGB classification models, whereas GeoSANE is the first to learn a shared latent space across fundamentally different architectures (ViT, Swin, ResNet, UNet, YOLO) processing non-RGB modalities (SAR, multispectral, SWIR) for segmentation and detection. It also explicitly contrasts with model merging methods (DARE, TIES-Merging) that require identical architectures and initializations, positioning weight-space generation as a strictly more general mechanism for combining geospatial knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from data-centric pretraining to weight-centric generation offers a scalable path to unify the 70+ fragmented remote sensing foundation models without requiring new large-scale satellite data or multi-GPU pretraining runs. A key open question is how the latent representation evolves as the model collection grows—whether adding more models yields diminishing returns or continues to improve generated weights. The 3.5M-parameter fMoW result (−7.8 vs. best baseline) hints that very small architectures may not benefit as much, and the 900M-parameter autoencoder itself is non-trivial to train, raising questions about the compute trade-off relative to traditional pretraining. Broader implications include whether this approach generalizes beyond remote sensing to other domains with fragmented model ecosystems.
