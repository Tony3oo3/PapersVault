## Problem

Occluded visible-infrared person re-identification (VI-ReID) is severely underexplored: existing methods assume full pedestrian visibility, and spatial-domain-only alignment is easily disrupted when occlusions appear at different locations across the visible and infrared modalities. The paper identifies that the frequency domain naturally decouples modality-specific appearance (amplitude) from modality-shared structure (phase), offering a principled way to achieve cross-modality consistency that is robust to both modality gaps and occlusion-induced perturbations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired visible and infrared pedestrian images resized to 288×144 pixels, extracts spatial feature maps via a shared backbone, and applies 2D FFT to decompose them into amplitude and phase spectra. The output is a compact fused identity embedding per image used for cross-modality retrieval ranking (Rank-k and mAP). Evaluation follows a fixed occlusion protocol where 50% of training images and all query images are occluded while gallery images remain clean, on the newly constructed Occ-SYSU-MM01 and Occ-RegDB benchmarks.

## Method

The SFCL framework operates in three stages. First, the Cross-Modality Frequency Alignment Module (CFAM) applies FFT to backbone features, models amplitude with a Vmamba encoder and phase with a Transformer (Trans-Mamba block), performs cross-modality frequency attention, and then uses a Cross-modality Frequency Sinkhorn (CFS) module that formulates spectral energy reallocation as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem solved via differentiable Sinkhorn iterations, before IFFT reconstructs aligned spatial representations. Second, the Spatial-Frequency Interaction Module (SFIM) computes a cosine-similarity correlation matrix between spatial and frequency tokens, applies Gabor-based band-pass modulation to suppress occlusion-induced high-frequency noise, uses a soft-thresholded adaptive attention to aggregate frequency cues per spatial position, and finally performs cross-covariance pooling to capture high-order spatial-frequency interactions. Third, the Frequency-Aware Discriminative (FAD) loss combines a symmetric cross-modality frequency contrastive term (InfoNCE-style on spectral embeddings) with a semantic consistency term that aligns per-sample and K-NN neighborhood prediction distributions via KL divergence, jointly regularizing both spectral and decision-boundary spaces.

## Results

On the introduced Occ-SYSU-MM01 dataset (All Search), SFCL achieves 65.97% Rank-1 and 64.77% mAP, surpassing the strongest baseline DNS by 4.31% and 4.18% respectively. On Occ-RegDB, SFCL reaches 77.18%/56.31% (V-I) and 76.46%/55.46% (I-V), outperforming all 12 compared methods in both search directions. On the holistic SYSU-MM01 (All Search), the method attains 79.13% Rank-1 and 76.28% mAP, exceeding DNS by 1.86% and 1.93%, confirming that the learned representations remain discriminative even without occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFCL outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluation setting on both occluded datasets. On Occ-SYSU-MM01 (All Search), the gap over the strongest prior method DNS (ECCV 2024) is +4.31% Rank-1 and +4.18% mAP; over the next-best MPL (TCSVT 2025) the margin is +5.05% Rank-1. On Occ-RegDB, SFCL leads DNS by +3.67% Rank-1 (V→I) and +3.81% Rank-1 (I→V), with mAP gains of +2.92% and +4.23% respectively. The advantage is most pronounced on the indoor-search protocol of Occ-SYSU-MM01, where SFCL reaches 67.46% Rank-1 versus 63.26% for DNS (+4.20%). On the holistic (non-occluded) SYSU-MM01, SFCL still leads DNS by +1.86% Rank-1, though on RegDB V→I the method trails CSDN (TMM 2025) by 1.33% in Rank-1 (94.07 vs 95.40) while exceeding it by 2.04% in mAP (89.74 vs 87.70), suggesting a slight trade-off in top-1 precision on that specific protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of occluded person re-identification and visible-infrared cross-modality matching, a combination previously addressed only by OCMF [5] in the spatial domain. It departs from the dominant spatial-only paradigm (body-part decomposition, salient-region attention, pose cues) by introducing frequency-domain representations—FFT amplitude/phase decomposition, Sinkhorn optimal transport for spectral alignment, and Gabor-modulated spatial-frequency interaction—as a complementary signal. The Trans-Mamba block (state-space model for amplitude, Transformer for phase) and the cross-covariance pooling in SFIM repurpose recent architecture components in a novel cross-domain fusion context, while the FAD loss combines frequency-domain contrastive learning with KL-based semantic consistency in a way not seen in prior VI-ReID work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two constructed datasets (Occ-SYSU-MM01, Occ-RegDB) fill a genuine gap: prior occluded ReID benchmarks relied on random cropping or black-block padding, which lack semantic realism and cross-modality consistency. The frequency-domain perspective offers a physically interpretable mechanism for separating modality-specific style (amplitude) from identity-relevant structure (phase), a framing that could transfer to other cross-spectral or cross-sensor matching tasks. However, the occlusions are synthetically pasted using YOLOv8-seg/SAM-extracted instances, so robustness to truly arbitrary real-world occlusions (e.g., partial body occlusion by another pedestrian) remains untested. Open questions include whether the Sinkhorn transport step is necessary versus simpler spectral normalization, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two pedestrian datasets to other cross-modality recognition settings.
