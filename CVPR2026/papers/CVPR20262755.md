## Problem

Conventional flow map distillation relies on samples from an external dataset to train the student, introducing a fundamental Teacher-Data Mismatch risk: a static dataset may incompletely or misalignedly represent the teacher's true generative capabilities, especially when the teacher generalizes beyond its training set, undergoes post-hoc fine-tuning, or its proprietary data is unavailable. This paper asks whether this data dependency is truly necessary for successful flow map distillation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the distilled student takes a single Gaussian noise sample z ~ N(0,I) and a class label, producing a generated image in exactly one function evaluation (1-NFE) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The critical distinction is at training time: the method requires no external dataset whatsoever—both the prediction and correction objectives sample exclusively from the prior distribution π, which is the one distribution guaranteed to be on-distribution for the teacher's generative process by construction.

## Method

FreeFlow is a data-free predictor-corrector framework for flow map distillation. The prediction objective (Eq. 9) is derived by differentiating the optimal flow-map condition with respect to the end time s (rather than the start time t as in MeanFlow), anchoring the start-point at the prior (t=1); this aligns the student's generating velocity ∂_δ f_θ with the teacher's instantaneous velocity u at the predicted state. The correction objective (Eq. 11) adapts Variational Score Distillation to align the student's noising velocity v_N (the marginal velocity of the noising flow implied by the student's predicted distribution) back to the teacher's u, mitigating compounding errors that arise from the self-referential prediction loop. Both objectives sample only from π, and the two are combined via an adaptive gradient balancing strategy that dynamically scales correction gradients relative to prediction gradients based on their respective velocity-mismatch magnitudes.

## Results

Distilling from SiT-XL/2+REPA, FreeFlow achieves a FID of 1.45 on ImageNet 256×256 and 1.49 on ImageNet 512×512, both with only 1 NFE (single function evaluation). This establishes a new state-of-the-art for fast flow generation, surpassing the teacher's own FID of 1.37 (at 434/460 NFE) by less than 10% while reducing inference cost by over 400×. The method also demonstrates strong training efficiency, surpassing the final performance of many baselines after only 100K iterations (~20 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeFlow outperforms all data-based distillation baselines by a significant margin at both resolutions. On ImageNet 256×256 (teacher SiT-XL/2+REPA), FreeFlow achieves FID 1.45 with 1 NFE, beating FACM (1.52, 2 NFE) by 0.07 FID and π-Flow (1.97, 2 NFE) by 0.52 FID. On ImageNet 512×512, FreeFlow (1.49, 1 NFE) surpasses the strongest distillation baseline sCD-XXL+VSD (1.89, 12 NFE) by 0.40 FID while using 12× fewer function evaluations. Against from-scratch fast flow methods, FreeFlow also leads: it beats DMF-XL/2 (1.51 on 256, 1.68 on 512) and sCT-XXL (3.76 on 512) despite requiring far fewer training epochs. Critically, all competing methods require access to an external dataset for training, whereas FreeFlow samples exclusively from the prior distribution, eliminating the Teacher-Data Mismatch risk entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MeanFlow framework [14], whose core identity (Eq. 4) enforces consistency by differentiating the optimal flow condition with respect to the start time. FreeFlow's key conceptual departure is to differentiate with respect to the end time instead, which becomes the natural choice when the start point is fixed at the prior (t=1) rather than sampled from a data-noising distribution. The correction objective adapts Variational Score Distillation [85] to the flow map setting, aligning the student's marginal noising velocity with the teacher's. Together, these form a predictor-corrector framework that is entirely prior-anchored, in contrast to all prior flow map distillation methods (FACM [57], AYF [64], π-Flow [7], DMF [36]) that require sampling from an external dataset during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of Teacher-Data Mismatch as a fundamental, previously unexamined risk in flow map distillation is a genuine conceptual contribution that reframes a widely-accepted practice. The practical implication is significant: practitioners no longer need access to (or worry about the fidelity of) the teacher's training data, which is critical for proprietary models, post-hoc fine-tuned teachers, or models that generalize beyond their training set. The inference-time scaling application demonstrates a concrete downstream benefit of the data-free student as a fast proxy. Open questions include whether the approach extends to text-to-image, video, or other conditional generation settings, whether the correction objective remains necessary at larger scales, and how the method behaves when the teacher itself is imperfect or underfit.
