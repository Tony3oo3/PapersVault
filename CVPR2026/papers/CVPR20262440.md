## Problem

Existing probabilistic precipitation nowcasting methods rely on deterministic autoencoder compression to reduce the dimensionality of high-resolution radar data before applying diffusion or flow-based generative models. This two-stage design is ill-posed for weather forecasting because compression errors that are imperceptible in natural images can correspond to substantial shifts in predicted precipitation, and the deterministic decoder provides no estimate of decoding uncertainty. Additionally, prior methods either use fixed conditioning windows vulnerable to missing frames or auto-regressive generation that accumulates errors over time.

## Input / Output

The method takes L^in = 13 past VIL precipitation frames (384×384 km region, 1 km spatial, 5 min temporal resolution from NEXRAD) and produces L^out = 12 future frames (60 min forecast). The frame-wise encoder supports variable-length conditioning down to as few as 2 past frames, making it robust to sensor faults and dropped frames common in operational radar networks. Output consists of multiple ensemble members (typically 10) from both the latent-space generative model and the probabilistic decoder, whose variance provides spatially localized aleatoric uncertainty estimates.

## Method

FREUD (Frame-wise Encoder and United Decoder) replaces the standard deterministic autoencoder with a rectified-flow-based compression stage: a lightweight frame-wise transformer encoder processes each radar frame independently (preventing information leakage and supporting incremental updates), while a hierarchical hourglass rectified-flow transformer video decoder jointly reconstructs all frames for temporal consistency using space-time factorized and neighborhood attention. A novel stochastic tanh regularization (T-reg.) constrains latents to [-1,1] via tanh activation plus Gaussian perturbation, eliminating the need for KL loss weighting, adversarial losses, or perceptual losses while producing a smooth, bounded latent space. For latent-space forecasting, a compact rectified-flow transformer is trained with masking-based diffusion forcing (following RaMViD), randomly masking subsets of past frames during training to enable flexible conditioning lengths at inference. The full pipeline samples from the latent-space flow model to generate future latents, then decodes them through the probabilistic FREUD decoder, with ensemble variance across both stages providing calibrated uncertainty estimates.

## Results

On the SEVIR benchmark, FREUD+LSM-L achieves state-of-the-art CRPS of 0.0190 and SSIM of 0.7841 without classifier-free guidance (CFG), outperforming the prior best (CasCast) by 5.94% in CRPS and 1.80% in SSIM. With CFG, SSIM further improves to 0.7937 and HSS/CSI become competitive (0.5537/0.4277), though the authors argue CFG gains stem from a systematic precipitation shift rather than better modeling. Calibration is substantially improved, with a reliability index of 0.135 ± 0.001 versus 0.312 ± 0.001 for CasCast, indicating much better-calibrated ensemble uncertainty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest probabilistic baseline CasCast (ICML'24), FREUD+LSM-L wins on CRPS (0.0190 vs 0.0202) and SSIM (0.7841 vs 0.7797) without CFG, but trails on localization metrics HSS (0.5011 vs 0.5602) and CSI (0.3864 vs 0.4401); with CFG the gap narrows (HSS 0.5537, CSI 0.4277) but the authors note CFG inflates predicted precipitation, making the comparison less meaningful. Against PreDiff (NeurIPS'23), FREUD wins on all four metrics (CRPS 0.0190 vs 0.0202, SSIM 0.7841 vs 0.7648, HSS 0.5011 vs 0.4914, CSI 0.3864 vs 0.3875). All deterministic baselines (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, PhyDNet, SimVP, EarthFormer) are clearly outperformed on CRPS and SSIM, with EarthFormer being the closest deterministic method (CRPS 0.0251, SSIM 0.7756). NowcastNet (Nature'23), a GAN-based method, is substantially worse on both CRPS (0.0283) and SSIM (0.5696), confirming the limitations of GANs for this task. The paper also shows that even the smallest latent-space model (LSM-S, 44M params) matches CasCast's CRPS (0.0200 vs 0.0202) while using only 14% of its parameters, and that scaling to LSM-L (473M) yields consistent further gains across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-stage latent-space generative modeling paradigm (Rombach et al. 2022) and the Diffusion Autoencoder concept (Preechakul et al. 2022) to precipitation nowcasting, but makes a key architectural departure: replacing the deterministic decoder with a rectified-flow decoder so that the compression stage itself becomes probabilistic. For the latent-space forecasting stage, it adopts the masking-based training paradigm of RaMViD (Hoppe et al. 2022) to support variable-length conditioning, and uses a hierarchical hourglass transformer decoder (Crowson et al. 2024) for temporal consistency. The primary prior work it positions against is CasCast (Gong et al. 2024) and PreDiff (Gao et al. 2023), both of which rely on deterministic VAE-style compression with adversarial and perceptual losses; FREUD eliminates these in favor of a single flow-matching objective and a novel stochastic tanh regularization that constrains latents without loss-weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making both the compression and generation stages probabilistic, FREUD provides a principled way to quantify decoding uncertainty that is spatially correlated with precipitation intensity—directly relevant for safety-critical extreme weather forecasting where the range of plausible outcomes matters more than the most likely one. The observation that classifier-free guidance systematically inflates predicted precipitation (rather than genuinely improving localization) is a useful caution for the nowcasting community, suggesting some prior SOTA gains may be artifacts of a simple bias shift. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher spatial resolutions and longer forecast horizons beyond the 60-minute SEVIR window, and whether the frame-wise encoder's robustness to dropped frames translates to operational radar networks with irregular sampling.
