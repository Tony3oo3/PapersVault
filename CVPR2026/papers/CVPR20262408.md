## Problem

Weakly supervised object localization methods suffer from partial object coverage, where activation maps highlight only the most discriminative regions rather than the full target object. Existing solutions to this problem often rely on multi-stage pipelines, full fine-tuning of large backbones, or computationally heavy generative models, making them expensive to train and deploy. TriLite addresses both the partial-activation gap and the efficiency gap simultaneously with a single-stage, parameter-light framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image as input, processed by a frozen ViT-S/14 backbone into 14×14 patch tokens. The TriHead module produces a three-channel spatial map (ambiguous, foreground, background) at patch resolution, from which a bounding box is extracted via the foreground channel and a pixel-level segmentation mask is derived for WSSS evaluation. A separate single linear layer on the ViT class token yields the image-level class label, so the system outputs both a localization signal and a classification prediction in one forward pass.

## Method

TriLite freezes a DINOv2-pretrained ViT-S/14 backbone and trains only a lightweight TriHead module (a single convolutional layer followed by batch normalization and softmax) plus one linear classification layer, totaling fewer than 800K trainable parameters on ImageNet-1K. The key architectural novelty is the three-channel decomposition of patch features into foreground, background, and ambiguous regions, where the ambiguous channel absorbs non-target salient content that would otherwise be forced into a binary foreground/background split. Localization supervision is applied via a weighted average of patch features using the foreground and background masks, followed by a shared fully-connected layer producing class logits; a novel adversarial background loss explicitly penalizes the ground-truth class logit in the background representation, pushing target-related features out of the background map. Because the backbone is frozen, the classification and localization branches operate on a shared, semantically rich feature space without competing gradient objectives, eliminating the need for multi-stage training or separate networks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, TriLite with a frozen ViT-S/14 (DINOv2) backbone achieves 65.5% Top-1, 75.6% Top-5, and 77.9% GT-known localization accuracy, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art GenPromp (65.2/73.4/75.0) by +0.3%, +2.2%, and +2.9% respectively. On CUB-200-2011, TriLite reaches 87.3% Top-1, 96.7% Top-5, and 98.5% GT-known accuracy, edging out GenPromp (87.0/96.1/98.0). On OpenImages (weakly supervised semantic segmentation), TriLite achieves 73.3% PxAP, outperforming the prior best F-CAM (72.1%) by +1.2 points. All results are obtained with fewer than 800K trainable parameters, compared to 1017M+66M for GenPromp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriLite (DINOv2) outperforms the previous state-of-the-art GenPromp on both WSOL benchmarks: by +0.3%/+2.2%/+2.9% (Top-1/Top-5/GT) on ImageNet-1K and by +0.3%/+0.6%/+0.5% on CUB-200-2011, while using roughly 1250× fewer trainable parameters (0.8M vs. 1017M+66M). Against C2AM (the strongest non-generative method), TriLite gains +5.5% Top-1 and +5.6% GT on ImageNet-1K, and +5.5% Top-1 and +5.6% GT on CUB-200-2011. On the OpenImages segmentation benchmark, TriLite (73.3% PxAP) surpasses F-CAM (72.1%) by +1.2 points and BAS (66.86%) by +6.4 points. In the controlled frozen-backbone comparison (Table 5, all using ViT-S/16 DINO), TriLite improves over LOST by +5.0% GT on CUB and +13.2% GT on ImageNet-1K, and over TokenCut by +4.8% GT on CUB and +6.4% GT on ImageNet-1K. The DeiT-S variant of TriLite (59.2/69.1/71.7 on ImageNet-1K) already matches or exceeds C2AM and BAS on localization, confirming that the gains come from the TriHead design rather than solely from the DINOv2 backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriLite sits at the intersection of two WSOL research threads: (1) the disentangling of classification and localization objectives pioneered by PSOL and C2AM, and (2) the use of frozen self-supervised ViT backbones explored by LOST and TokenCut. It departs from C2AM/PSOL by implementing the disentangling within a single shared frozen backbone rather than separate networks, and from LOST/TokenCut by introducing a lightweight learnable TriHead module instead of purely post-hoc strategies. The three-region (foreground/background/ambiguous) decomposition extends the binary split used by prior methods, and the adversarial background loss is, to the authors' knowledge, novel in the WSOL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-800K trainable parameter count (vs. 22M+ for prior SOTA methods) makes WSOL dramatically more accessible, removing the need for multi-stage pipelines or large generative models while still achieving state-of-the-art on both WSOL and WSSS benchmarks. The frozen-backbone design also simplifies training and avoids the competing-objective problem that plagues end-to-end fine-tuning. Open questions remain around multi-instance localization, class-specific maps for multi-class images, and handling occlusion, which the authors identify as key limitations. The finding that highly precise activations can paradoxically reduce localization accuracy under occlusion (CUB-200-2011) suggests that spatial precision and bounding-box coverage are not always aligned, a tension that future methods must navigate.
