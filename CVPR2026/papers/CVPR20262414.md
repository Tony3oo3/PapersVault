## Problem

Existing referring image segmentation benchmarks and models focus on categorical and spatial queries (e.g., 'the left-most apple'), leaving a gap in grounding abstract, intent-driven conversational concepts that require functional reasoning, physical understanding, and implicit constraints (e.g., 'objects likely to tip over' or 'surfaces suitable for hot cookware'). No prior benchmark or model systematically targets affordances, stability, hazard assessment, or use-case reasoning in pixel-level grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262414/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image and a natural language text prompt (averaging 7.6 words) and produces a binary per-pixel segmentation mask in a single forward pass, without iterative tool calls or multi-turn refinement. Prompts span five concept families—entities, spatial/layout, relations/events, affordances/functions, and physics/safety—and may reference non-visible properties (e.g., thermal state, structural stability) or context-dependent use cases rather than purely visual attributes.

## Method

CONVERSEG-NET fuses SAM2's frozen MAE-pretrained ViT image encoder with Qwen2.5-VL-3B as a joint image-text prompt encoder, connected to SAM2's fully fine-tuned mask decoder via two lightweight adapters: a linear projection of per-token text embeddings (sparse) and a 2-layer MLP on the EOS hidden state (dense), with LoRA (rank 16) applied to the VLM backbone. Training follows a two-phase curriculum—Phase 1 on literal segmentation (COCO categories, RefCOCO family, open-vocabulary regions) and Phase 2 on conversational data mixed 50-50 with Phase 1 data at a lower learning rate—to gradually introduce abstract reasoning. A five-stage VLM-driven data engine (using Gemini-2.5-Flash and Moondream3+SAM2) synthesizes 61K prompt-mask pairs across all five concept families via iterative generation and multi-stage verification, eliminating the need for human annotation at scale.

## Results

On the CONVERSEG benchmark (gIoU), CONVERSEG-NET with a 3B prompt encoder achieves 70.5% on the SAM-seeded split and 64.4% on the human-annotated split, surpassing the strongest baseline Seg-Zero (69.2% / 61.1%) by +1.3% and +3.3% respectively. Scaling the prompt encoder to 7B further improves to 73.3% / 66.3%, a +4.1% absolute gain over the best baseline on SAM-seeded. On standard referring expression benchmarks, CONVERSEG-NET (3B) reaches 79.9% gIoU on RefCOCO val and 55.1% on ReasonSeg test (zero-shot, no ReasonSeg training data), while the 7B variant achieves 58.7% on ReasonSeg test, approaching LISA-13B (61.3% val) which was fine-tuned on ReasonSeg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CONVERSEG-NET (3B) outperforms the strongest baseline Seg-Zero (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) by +1.3% gIoU on CONVERSEG SAM-seeded (70.5 vs 69.2) and +3.3% on human-annotated (64.4 vs 61.1), despite using a 4× smaller backbone. The gap widens to +4.1% when scaling to 7B (73.3 vs 69.2). Against LISA (Llama2-13B), even the untrained CONVERSEG-NET Base (58.8%) already exceeds LISA by +3.6%, and the full model leads by +15.3%. On RefCOCO/+/g, CONVERSEG-NET (79.9% val) is competitive with EVF-SAM (82.4%) and GSVA (79.2%) but trails the strongest methods like X-SAM (85.1%) and HyperSeg (84.8%) by 4–5 points. On ReasonSeg, CONVERSEG-NET 7B achieves 58.7% test gIoU zero-shot (no ReasonSeg training data), approaching LISA-13B (54.0% test) which was explicitly fine-tuned on ReasonSeg, and surpassing Seg-Zero 7B (57.5% test). The largest relative gains over baselines appear on affordance and physics & safety concepts, where CONVERSEG-NET 7B leads Seg-Zero by +4.9% and +4.2% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends referring image segmentation (RIS) by introducing Conversational Image Segmentation (CIS), which requires grounding prompts involving affordances, physical stability, and functional reasoning rather than the categorical and spatial references that dominate RefCOCO. Architecturally, it fuses SAM2's frozen image encoder and mask decoder with a compact VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) via lightweight LoRA adapters, positioning itself between heavy multi-stage reasoning systems (LISA, GLaMM, Seg-Zero) and text-conditioned SAM variants (EVF-SAM). A key methodological departure is the AI-powered data engine that synthesizes 61K prompt-mask pairs through iterative VLM generation and multi-stage verification, bypassing the need for human annotation at scale — a strategy more aligned with synthetic pre-training literature than with prior RIS data collection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CONVERSEG provides the first benchmark with balanced coverage across five reasoning concept families (entities, spatial, relations, affordances, physics/safety), filling a clear gap where prior datasets are >50% entities/spatial. The data engine is a practical recipe for scaling language-guided segmentation supervision without human annotation, and the curriculum from literal to conversational concepts offers a reusable training strategy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond COCO's object-centric scenes to more complex or novel environments, whether the Gemini-2.5-Flash-based data engine introduces systematic biases, and whether the 1,687-sample benchmark is sufficient to reliably measure progress on the hardest concepts (physics & safety still shows a ~13% gap to entities even for the best model).
