## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks evaluate low-level perception and high-level connotation in isolation, leaving the critical inferential process that bridges concrete visual evidence to abstract meaning unmeasured. This gap means models can appear competent by answering both concrete and abstract questions correctly while fundamentally failing at the associative reasoning that connects them, obscuring true performance bottlenecks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263938/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes RGB images paired with three-level hierarchical multiple-choice questions (4 options each) spanning perception, semantic bridge, and connotation levels. Evaluation is conducted under two settings: 'base' where each level is answered independently, and 'context' where prior-level predictions are provided as input to subsequent levels, enabling quantification of inter-level dependencies. The MCTS data generation pipeline takes source images and produces validated hierarchical QA chains (approximately 30k pairs from 1k images) used for instruction tuning.

## Method

VCU-Bridge formalizes visual connotation understanding as a structured triple (l₁, l₂, l₃) with pairwise support constraints ensuring each higher level is justified by the lower one. HVCU-Bench is constructed via a top-down sequential generation process (connotation → bridge → perception) powered by Gemini-2.5-Pro, where each newly generated level is immediately validated for logical dependency and difficulty progression against the previous level, with a refinement loop triggered on failure. For training data, the paper employs an MCTS-driven bottom-up search that builds a reasoning tree over candidate QA paths, using UCB-based node selection, parallel MLLM expansion with quality filtering (coherence, diversity, difficulty checks), and backpropagation of evaluation scores to guide exploration. After convergence, top-K highest-rated complete paths are extracted as instruction tuning data, which is used to fine-tune Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, yielding measurable gains on HVCU-Bench (+6.17%) and general benchmarks (average +2.53%, with +7.26% on MMStar).

## Results

On HVCU-Bench, GPT-4o achieves the highest overall Score of 52.24% among all MLLMs, while the strongest open-source model Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct reaches 47.58%. Humans achieve 87.18%, revealing a gap of −34.94% for GPT-4o and −39.60% for Qwen3-VL-8B. All models exhibit a universal cascading degradation from L_perc (typically >90%) to L_conn (often <65%), with Full-Chain Accuracy (Acc_full) dropping to 50–53% for the best models. The paper's instruction-tuned Qwen3-VL-4B-Bridge improves the base Qwen3-VL-4B by +6.17% on HVCU-Bench and +2.53% on average across four general benchmarks (MMStar +7.26%, MMMU +3.22%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-4o leads all MLLMs with a Score of 52.24%, but still trails humans by 34.94 points, with the largest gap at L_conn (−23.75% on Implication Understanding). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (47.58%) outperforms Gemma3-12B (44.23%), InternVL3.5-8B (40.01%), and Phi-3.5-Vision (39.74%), while LLaVA-1.6-13B (29.04%) and Phi-4-Multimodal (27.02%) show the steepest L_perc→L_conn drops. The paper's Qwen3-VL-4B-Bridge, trained on ~10k MCTS-generated hierarchical QA pairs, improves over its base Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct (41.24%) by +6.17% on HVCU-Bench and generalizes to MMStar (+7.26%) and MMMU (+3.22%) without task-specific tuning. In the 'context' setting (where lower-level answers are provided), GPT-4o gains +15.94% and Qwen3-VL-8B gains +14.70%, confirming that lower-level perception is a critical prerequisite for higher-level connotation. Ablation (Table 4) shows that full three-level hierarchical supervision outperforms L3-only training by +1.25% Acc_full, validating that explicit L_perc and L_bridge signals are necessary for effective semantic bridging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing the 'Semantic Bridge' level as an explicit, evaluable intermediate between low-level perception and high-level connotation in multimodal understanding. Prior benchmarks either isolate perception (MME, MMBench, MMStar) or connotation (MMMU, II-Bench, EEmo-Bench), and multi-level benchmarks (Lens, MVP-Bench, HiBench) cover multiple abstraction levels without requiring inter-level logical coherence. The MCTS-driven data generation pipeline repurposes tree-search strategies from LLM reasoning (Socratic-MCTS, process-supervised MCTS) and applies them to hierarchical visual QA construction, a novel application of the technique to multimodal instruction tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal cascading degradation across 13 diverse MLLMs (GPT-4o at 52.24% vs. human 87.18% overall) provides the first systematic, level-wise diagnosis of where multimodal reasoning breaks down, identifying the semantic bridge as the critical bottleneck rather than perception or connotation in isolation. The finding that hierarchical context yields +15.94% for GPT-4o and that training on hierarchical data transfers to general benchmarks (+7.26% on MMStar) suggests that explicit bridging supervision is a practical lever for improving MLLM reasoning broadly. Open questions include whether the three-level decomposition generalizes to video or multi-image settings, whether the MCTS pipeline scales to larger training sets, and whether the gap can be closed with more targeted architectural changes rather than data-level interventions.
