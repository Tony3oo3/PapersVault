## Problem

Existing video-to-speech methods inject visual features into holistic speech representations while ignoring the hierarchical structure of speech, where coarse speaker-aware semantics reside at lower token levels and fine-grained prosodic details emerge at higher levels. This flat treatment prevents direct alignment between visual cues and speech features at their corresponding hierarchical levels, exacerbating the inherent information asymmetry between sparse visual signals and dense acoustic representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video clip (no audio track) and produces synthesized speech audio. Visual features are extracted at three granularities: frame-level lip motion via AV-HuBERT, global identity via ArcFace, and temporally smoothed emotion classes via Poster2 (downsampled to 50 Hz). Speech is represented as a 12-level RVQ token sequence (codebook size 1024 per level) from a pre-trained MaskGCT codec, which is decoded back to waveform after generation.

## Method

HiCoDiT formulates VTS as hierarchical masked token prediction using a discrete diffusion model (SEDD) over RVQ tokens. The 12 token levels are split into low-level (r1:r2) and high-level (r3:r12) blocks: low-level blocks receive lip motion via channel concatenation and identity via single-scale AdaLN for content/timbre alignment, while high-level blocks receive emotion via a novel dual-scale AdaLN that applies channel-wise normalization for global vocal style and temporal-wise normalization (Kronecker product with upsampled emotion scales) for local prosody dynamics. Training uses a multi-level DSE loss summed across all 12 RVQ levels plus an L1 identity alignment loss (λ=100) between visual and GE2E acoustic embeddings, with 10% condition dropout for predictor-free guidance. Inference employs 64-step Euler sampling with enhanced predictor-free guidance (joint scale 2.5/2.25, compositional scales for identity and lip).

## Results

On LRS3 (video-only setting), HiCoDiT achieves the best WER (29.41%), DNSMOS (3.50), LSE-C (7.15), LSE-D (6.58), and EmoAcc (79.41%) among all video-only baselines, while FTV slightly leads on UTMOS (3.99 vs. 3.84) and SpkSim (0.5981 vs. 0.5678). With audio+video guidance, HiCoDiT further improves WER to 28.98% and SpkSim to 0.6715, the highest across all methods. On LRS2, HiCoDiT similarly leads in WER (39.99%), LSE-C (7.95), and LSE-D (6.17) in the video-only setting. Subjective MOS confirms superiority: HiCoDiT scores 3.17 (naturalness) and 3.50 (synchronization), exceeding FTV (2.80/3.48) and AlignDiT (2.47/3.13).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest video-only competitor FTV (CVPR'25), HiCoDiT wins on WER (29.41 vs. 30.37), DNSMOS (3.50 vs. 3.22), LSE-C (7.15 vs. 7.08), LSE-D (6.58 vs. 6.66), and EmoAcc (79.41 vs. 73.19), but concedes UTMOS (3.84 vs. 3.99) and SpkSim (0.5678 vs. 0.5981), which the authors attribute to limited speaker diversity in training data. Against AlignDiT (ACM MM'25), HiCoDiT dominates on nearly every metric: WER (29.41 vs. 31.37), DNSMOS (3.50 vs. 3.24), UTMOS (3.84 vs. 3.76), MCD (9.62 vs. 10.02), LSE-D (6.58 vs. 6.82), EmoAcc (79.41 vs. 76.11), and SpkSim (0.5678 vs. 0.5597). Against EmoDubber (CVPR'25, which uses audio input), HiCoDiT achieves substantially lower WER (29.41 vs. 41.52) and higher DNSMOS (3.50 vs. 2.95) and EmoAcc (79.41 vs. 72.01), though EmoDubber retains a slight edge on MCD (9.25 vs. 9.62) and SpkSim (0.6052 vs. 0.5678). Older methods (Lip2Wav, MTL, DiffV2S, LTBS) are clearly outperformed across all comparable metrics, with WER gaps exceeding 10–70 percentage points. In A/B testing, HiCoDiT is preferred over AlignDiT (57.0%) and FTV (52.1%), and even surpasses ground-truth speech in 53.9% of pairwise comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCoDiT builds on the discrete diffusion framework (SEDD/DSE) and RVQ-based speech codecs (MaskGCT) for speech generation, but departs from prior VTS methods (FTV, AlignDiT, EmoDubber, DiffV2S) that treat speech as a flat sequence and inject visual features holistically. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the inherent RVQ token hierarchy—where lower levels encode speaker-aware semantics and higher levels encode prosody—as an explicit prior to disentangle visual conditioning: lip motion and identity condition low-level tokens, while facial emotion conditions high-level tokens. The dual-scale AdaLN mechanism, combining channel-wise (global style) and temporal-wise (local prosody) normalization, extends standard DiT-style conditioning to handle the multi-granularity nature of prosodic modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the hierarchical structure of discrete speech tokens can serve as a natural inductive bias for cross-modal alignment in VTS, potentially reducing the information asymmetry between sparse visual cues and dense acoustic signals. Limitations include reliance on a single training dataset (VoxCeleb2, 261.5 hours), which constrains speaker diversity and expressiveness, and the need for ground-truth acoustic features during training for identity and emotion conditioning. Open questions include whether the hierarchy-based conditioning generalizes to longer-form or multi-speaker scenarios, and whether the approach can be extended to other discrete-token generation tasks beyond speech.
