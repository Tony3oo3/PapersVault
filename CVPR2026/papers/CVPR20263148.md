## Problem

Existing audio-video forgery benchmarks are confined to human-face deepfakes, employ a single manipulation type (either editing or synthesis), and provide only binary real/fake labels, failing to capture the diversity of real-world forgeries that span general subjects, multiple concurrent forgery types, and require fine-grained perceptual and explanatory reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263148/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 3,000 paired audio-video clips (1,500 human-subject, 1,500 general-subject across 11 scenarios) with 12K question-answer pairs structured at four annotation levels. For the LMM-based annotation pipeline, each sample is consumed as a rich multi-modal context comprising uniformly sampled video frames, motion heatmaps, Mel spectrograms, and magnified high-frequency maps, alongside the ground-truth forgery-type label. Outputs range from a single binary label (L1) to a 5-choice multiple-answer selection (L3) to free-form natural-language rationales (L4) scored by GPT-5.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-stage hybrid forgery framework with two parallel branches (synthesis and editing), each following a three-stage pipeline: (1) a proprietary LMM generates spatio-temporal manipulation plans—dynamic scene descriptions for synthesis or structured edit specifications (target region, temporal window, operation) for editing; (2) expert generative models (KLING, QingYing for video; FoleyCrafter for audio; SAM2 + VideoPainter/DiffuEraser for mask-based editing) execute the planned operations; (3) real and forged audio/video streams are recombined to instantiate seven semantically consistent cross-modal forgery types (excluding combinations that mix synthesized content in one modality with edited content in the other). For higher-level annotations (L3/L4), an LMM-based annotator is prompted with the multi-modal evidence and ground-truth type label to generate explanatory rationales, from which 5-choice detail-selection questions are derived, all subject to human verification.

## Results

On the Binary Authenticity Judgment task (T1), AV-LMMs substantially outperform expert detection models: Gemini-2.5-pro achieves 59.9% F1 overall (63.3% human, 54.3% general), while the best expert model AVH-Align reaches only 42.8% F1 and LipFD only 32.1%. On the harder Multiple-Choice Forgery Classification task (T2), all AV-LMMs drop sharply, with Gemini-2.5-pro achieving only 19.2% F1 overall and struggling most on Edited forgeries (7.5% F1). On Forgery Detail Selection (T3) and Open-Ended Forgery Explanation (T4), performance is uniformly poor: GPT-4o leads with 27.5% F1 on T3 and a GPT-Score of 29.0 on T4, while open-source models fall below 18% F1 on T3 and 22 GPT-Score on T4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Binary Authenticity Judgment (T1), all AV-LMMs outperform both expert detection models (LipFD at 32.1% F1, AVH-Align at 42.8% F1), with Gemini-2.5-pro (59.9% F1) and GPT-4o (56.9% F1) leading by 14–28 points, demonstrating that unified multimodal models generalize better beyond face-centric deepfake artifacts. However, on Multiple-Choice Forgery Classification (T2), even the strongest AV-LMM (Gemini-2.5-pro, 19.2% F1) shows a ~70% relative drop from T1, and all models collapse on Edited forgeries (≤7.5% F1), revealing a critical fine-grained perception gap. On Forgery Detail Selection (T3), GPT-4o (27.5% F1) and Gemini-2.5-flash (31.8% F1) lead but remain far from useful; open-source models like OneLLM (5.5% F1) and Video-LLaMA2 (13.0% F1) are near chance. On Open-Ended Forgery Explanation (T4), GPT-4o (29.0) and Gemini-2.5-pro (30.9 GPT-Score) are the only models above 25, while OneLLM (1.0) and video-SALMONN (4.4) are essentially non-functional. A cross-modal analysis (Table 5) further shows a consistent vision-dominant pattern: most models perform 2–5× better on video-only forgeries than audio-only forgeries (e.g., GPT-4o: 21.7% vs. 8.0% F1), and several open-source models (OneLLM, Video-LLaMA2, AVicuna) score 0.0% on audio forgeries entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: multi-modal forgery detection benchmarks (FF++, DFDC, FakeAVCeleb, LAVDF) and audio-visual large multimodal models (PandaGPT, VideoLLaMA2, video-SALMONN). Prior benchmarks are confined to human-face deepfakes with single manipulation types and binary labels, while prior AV-LMM research focuses on general understanding rather than forensic detection. AVFakeBench repurposes existing generative tools (KLING, SAM2, LipVoicer, FoleyCrafter) within a structured multi-stage pipeline to construct forgeries, and reframes forgery detection as a multi-level reasoning task for AV-LMMs rather than a binary classification problem for task-specific classifiers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first benchmark spanning human and general subjects, seven cross-modal forgery combinations, and four annotation levels, AVFakeBench provides a much-needed stress test for AV-LMMs as unified forgery detectors. The finding that AV-LMMs outperform expert detectors on binary judgment yet suffer a ~70% collapse on fine-grained classification and near-zero performance on audio-only forgeries highlights a critical perception gap that current architectures do not address. Open questions include whether dedicated audio-forensic pretraining or cross-modal contrastive objectives can close the modality disparity, and whether the multi-level annotation scheme can be automated at scale without the current human-verification bottleneck.
