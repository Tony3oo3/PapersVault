## Problem

Prior LoD-based aerial localization (LoD-Loc v2) suffers from two critical failures: it cannot generalize to unseen scenes due to limited in-distribution training data, and it completely breaks down in dense urban environments because merged semantic building silhouettes create irreducible pose ambiguity. No existing method simultaneously achieves cross-scene generalization and reliable localization in high-density cityscapes using low-detail city models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262268/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB query image (resized to 512×512), an untextured LoD1 city model (CityGML-standard 3D mesh), and a prior 4-DoF pose estimate (x, y, z, θ) provided by UAV inertial sensors that fix the gravity direction. It produces a refined 4-DoF absolute camera pose and per-building instance masks from the query image, where each mask carries a unique 24-bit integer ID corresponding to a specific building in the LoD model. The rendered instance maps from the LoD model use the same ID-to-color encoding, enabling direct pixel-level matching.

## Method

The core paradigm shift replaces semantic silhouette alignment with instance silhouette alignment in three stages. First, the LoD model is parsed as a graph and partitioned into connected components (buildings), each assigned a unique 24-bit ID mapped to an RGB color, yielding an instanced model rendered via OpenSceneGraph. Second, a SAM-based segmentation network (ViT-Huge encoder frozen with LoRA, a learnable Prompter Module, and a SAM mask decoder) is fine-tuned on the 108k-image InsLoD-Loc synthetic dataset (generated in UE5 with Cesium/AirSim) to extract per-building instance masks from the query image. Third, pose evaluation uses an asymmetric matching cost: each query instance finds its best-matching rendered instance via Dice coefficient, and the final cost is a weighted sum (confidence- or area-based) of these per-instance scores. This cost drives a coarse-to-fine localization pipeline—uniform grid search over the 4-DoF space followed by particle filter refinement—identical in structure to LoD-Loc v2 but operating on instance-level rather than merged semantic masks, which eliminates the ambiguity that causes failure in dense scenes.

## Results

On the Tokyo-LoDv3 dense-scene dataset, LoD-Loc v3 (Full, area-based) achieves 39.30% and 50.30% at the (2m, 2°) threshold for Grid-Traj and Sequence-Traj respectively, compared to just 2.70% and 2.30% for LoD-Loc v2 — a roughly 2000% relative improvement. On the public UAVD4L-LoDv2 benchmark, LoD-Loc v3 reaches 97.60% (2m, 2°) in-trajectory and 97.40% out-of-trajectory, surpassing the in-distribution-trained LoD-Loc v2 (93.70% / 97.90%) despite being trained exclusively on synthetic data. On Swiss-EPFLv2, LoD-Loc v3 attains 58.60% (2m, 2°) in-place and 36.90% out-of-place, outperforming LoD-Loc v2 (54.20% / 31.40%) and all feature-matching baselines by wide margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Loc [65] (feature matching with SIFT, SuperPoint+SuperGlue, LoFTR, e-LoFTR, RoMa) fails completely on all three datasets, achieving 0% at every accuracy threshold, as it relies on high-fidelity 3D reconstructions unavailable in LoD settings. MC-Loc [85] (pretrained feature alignment with DINOv2 or RoMa) also performs poorly on Tokyo-LoDv3, reaching only 0–3.6% at (2m, 2°), confirming that generic feature alignment cannot resolve dense-scene ambiguity. LoD-Loc [98] (neural wireframe alignment on LoD2/3) achieves 10.52% (2m, 2°) on Tokyo-LoDv3 Grid-Traj but drops to 0% on Sequence-Traj, and is outperformed by LoD-Loc v3 by 3–50× across all datasets. The closest competitor, LoD-Loc v2 [99] (semantic silhouette alignment, trained in-distribution on UAVD4L-LoDv2), is surpassed by LoD-Loc v3 on all three benchmarks: on UAVD4L-LoDv2 the gap is modest (97.60% vs 93.70% at 2m-2° in-trajectory), but on Swiss-EPFLv2 (58.60% vs 54.20%) and especially Tokyo-LoDv3 (39.30% vs 2.70% on Grid-Traj), the instance-silhouette paradigm yields dramatic gains, validating that the improvement stems from the representation shift rather than data scale alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends LoD-Loc v2 [99], which established aerial localization over ubiquitous LoD1 city models via semantic building silhouette alignment with a coarse-to-fine (grid search + particle filter) framework. The key methodological departure is replacing the merged semantic silhouette cost with an instance-level alignment cost, where each building is individually identified and matched, resolving the ambiguity that causes complete failure in dense urban scenes. For the segmentation backbone, the paper adapts SAM [45] with a learnable Prompter Module and LoRA-based fine-tuning, drawing on the prompt-learning paradigm for remote sensing instance segmentation [11]. The synthetic data pipeline (UE5 + Cesium + AirSim for RGB, OSG for instance masks) is a novel contribution that enables training without real-world annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical blockers for global-scale UAV localization: cross-scene generalization (solved via the 100k-image InsLoD-Loc synthetic dataset spanning 40 areas in 6 countries) and dense-scene ambiguity (solved via instance-level alignment). The InsLoD-Loc dataset is the largest aerial instance segmentation dataset to date and is explicitly designed for extensibility, making it a potential community resource. A key open question is robustness under extreme weather and segmentation failure, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. The approach also raises the question of whether instance-level alignment can be extended to other LoD levels or non-building structures (bridges, towers) where the graph-partition instancing may be less clean.
