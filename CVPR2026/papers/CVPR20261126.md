## Problem

Learning discriminative 3D geometric descriptors for point cloud registration typically requires ground-truth rigid transformation annotations, which are costly and difficult to acquire at scale. Existing unsupervised alternatives (pseudo-label synthesis, Chamfer-distance alignment, reconstruction losses) struggle with partially overlapping point clouds and complex real-world geometries. The paper asks whether a large-scale generative model can provide an implicit, annotation-free supervisory signal that enforces correspondence-consistent feature learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of source and target point clouds (no intrinsics required) and produces a rigid SE(3) transformation plus point-wise geometric descriptors. Point clouds are first projected into 512×1024 range maps via the intrinsics-free AFoV-ERP scheme; during training, paired RGB images are also consumed as generation targets. At inference, the ControlNet encoder outputs 13 multi-scale feature maps (scales 2, 5, 8 are selected), which are upsampled, back-projected from 2D pixels to 3D points, concatenated with FPFH descriptors, and reduced via PCA to form compact per-point descriptors for nearest-neighbor matching and robust pose estimation.

## Method

GM-R² reformulates descriptor learning as geometry-conditioned cross-view image generation: a range-map-conditioned ControlNet encoder is trained so that only correspondence-consistent geometric features can drive a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone to synthesize visually consistent image pairs from two views of the same scene. Three architectural modifications instantiate this: (1) AFoV-ERP adaptively normalizes the equirectangular projection domain to the actual sensor FoV, yielding dense intrinsics-free range maps; (2) a Coupled ControlNet vertically concatenates source and target range maps into a single 2H×W input, enabling cross-view synthesis without modifying the original ControlNet architecture; (3) a Denoising-Agnostic design removes the noisy latent x_t from the ControlNet encoder input, making it a pure geometric encoder whose training and inference pathways are identical (geometry-only). Training optimizes only the ControlNet encoder parameters under a standard denoising loss on spherical-mapped ground-truth images, while the diffusion denoiser remains frozen; at inference, a single forward pass through the encoder yields multi-scale descriptors without any denoising steps.

## Results

On 3DMatch (40-frame sampling), GM-R² achieves 96.2% rotation accuracy at 5° and a mean rotation error of 2.0°, outperforming the best fully-supervised baseline PARE-Net (93.0%, 6.6°) by 3.2° in accuracy and 4.6° in mean error. It also reduces mean translation error to 6.4 cm (vs. 15.8 cm for PARE-Net) and mean Chamfer distance to 4.2 mm (vs. 12.8 mm). On ScanNet (50-frame sampling), GM-R² reaches 81.9% rotation accuracy at 5° and 7.3° mean rotation error, matching or exceeding most supervised baselines despite being trained without any transformation labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GM-R² (unsupervised) decisively outperforms all 10 baselines on 3DMatch across every metric. Against the strongest supervised baseline PARE-Net, it gains +3.2° rotation accuracy at 5° (96.2 vs. 93.0), reduces mean rotation error from 6.6° to 2.0°, and cuts mean translation error from 15.8 cm to 6.4 cm. It also surpasses the generative supervised variants (Generative-FCGF, Generative-GeoTrans) by 1.9–4.7° in rotation accuracy at 5° and by 6–33 mm in mean Chamfer distance. The only other unsupervised baseline, PPF-FoldNet, is far behind (40.3% vs. 96.2% at Rot@5°). On ScanNet, GM-R² (81.9% Rot@5°, 7.3° mean) slightly trails Generative-FCGF (82.9%, 8.4°) in rotation accuracy but beats it in mean translation error (18.5 vs. 21.7 cm) and mean Chamfer distance (59.8 vs. 66.0 mm), while clearly surpassing PARE-Net (78.2%, 17.0°) and all other supervised methods in rotation metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

GM-R² extends the authors' own prior work GenerativePCR (ICML 2025), which used a ControlNet-driven 3D-to-2D image generation pipeline for supervised semantics-enhanced matching, by removing the dependency on ground-truth transformations and the denoising trajectory. Conceptually, it repurposes the ControlNet encoder from a generation-conditioning module into a pure geometric feature extractor, leveraging the large-scale visual priors of Stable Diffusion as an implicit geometric consistency oracle. This departs from the dominant supervised paradigm (FCGF, Predator, GeoTrans) that requires costly pose annotations, and from prior unsupervised approaches (PPF-FoldNet, CEMNet, FMR) that rely on reconstruction or alignment losses, by introducing cross-view image synthesis as the supervisory signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is eliminating the need for rigid transformation annotations while still achieving competitive or superior registration accuracy, which is significant given that GT poses are the primary bottleneck for scaling 3D descriptor training. The denoising-agnostic design ensures training-inference consistency (geometry-only encoding at test time), addressing a real deployment gap in prior generative matching approaches. However, the method still requires paired RGB images during training (albeit without pose labels), is validated only on indoor RGB-D datasets (3DMatch, ScanNet), and inherits the heavy compute footprint of a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone. Open questions include whether the generative proxy task generalizes to outdoor or large-scale LiDAR scenes, and whether simpler geometric losses could achieve comparable results without the diffusion model overhead.
