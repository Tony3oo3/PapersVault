## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods face a fundamental trade-off: grid-based approaches (e.g., RDED) compress classes into disjoint patches that discard global spatial layout and inter-instance context, while diffusion-based methods (e.g., VLCP) generate semantically rich but spatially unstructured images with no explicit encoding of compositional relationships. Neither family jointly captures intra-class compositional structure and world knowledge, yielding distilled data that are either spatially fragmented or semantically shallow.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260454/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a per-class pool of real images (≈1,300 per ImageNet class) and CLIP text/class prompts, producing L×L compositional grids (default 4×4 = 16 cells) that are downsampled to compact 256×256 or 224×224 distilled images. During downstream training, a grid-aware cropping operator probabilistically samples aligned (grid-boundary) or random 224×224 patches, maintaining compatibility with standard inference inputs. The single-step diffusion enhancement adds only a one-time ~17% overhead (3m17s vs. 16m05s training) amortized across epochs.

## Method

Grid Distillation operates in three stages. First, SSDIM (Spectral Submodular Differentiable Information Maximization) extracts CLIP embeddings, constructs an affinity kernel, performs spectral decomposition to compute per-sample energy scores, then solves a submodular objective combining coverage (max pairwise similarity), diversity (log-det kernel volume), and spectral informativeness via Frank-Wolfe continuous relaxation followed by greedy swap refinement to select L² exemplars per grid. Second, the selected grid is downsampled to a compact representation, and a single-step diffusion reconstruction (adapted from InvSR using Stable Diffusion Turbo) conditioned on CLIP class prompts restores fine-grained spatial details lost during compression in ~148 ms. Third, a grid-aware cropping strategy mixes aligned crops (starting at multiples of cell dimensions) with random crops at a configurable probability (p_align = 0.6), preserving semantic layout while adding robustness to off-grid perturbations.

## Results

On ImageWoof with ResNet-18, Grid-Distil achieves 65.5% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10 and 85.5% at IPC=70, outperforming the next-best method VLCP by 25.6 and 25.2 percentage points respectively. On ImageNette (ResNetAP-10), the method reaches 83.3% at IPC=10 and 92.7% at IPC=50, surpassing VLCP by 18.5 and 11.5 points. On the full ImageNet-1K benchmark (IPC=10, ResNet-18), Grid-Distil with diffusion detail enhancement attains 50.01% accuracy versus 46.7% for VLCP and 44.3% for Minimax. On UCF-101 Mid-Frames few-shot classification, Grid-LDC-Labels achieves 73.88% at 1-shot and 79.03% at 8-shot, exceeding the prior best LDC by 7.05 and 2.0 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Grid-Distil consistently outperforms all 18 baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. The closest competitor, VLCP [53], is beaten by 11–26 percentage points on ImageWoof/ImageNette/ImageIDC and by 3.3 points on ImageNet-1K, confirming that structured grid composition plus diffusion enhancement provides a substantial advantage over independent per-image generation. Diffusion-based distillers Minimax [9] and D4M [32] trail VLCP by 2–6 points, while optimization-based methods (DM [48], IDC-1 [12], GLaD [3], DiT [27]) and classical selection strategies (K-Center [30], Herding [41]) fall 10–20 points behind. On ImageNet-1K, SRe2L [43] (21.3%) and RDED [34] (42.0%) are far below Grid-Distil's 50.01%, highlighting the gap between patch-based and grid-based distillation at scale. On UCF-101 few-shot classification, all CLIP-adaptation baselines (CoOp [52], Proto-CLIP [26], CLIP-Adapter [7], SuS-X [36], Tip-Adapter [46], LDC [17]) are surpassed, with the largest margin over LDC [17] at 1-shot (71.66% vs. 66.83%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two dataset distillation lineages: grid/patch-based condensation (RDED, SRe2L) and diffusion-prior-based synthesis (Minimax, D4M, VLCP). It explicitly positions itself against both, arguing that patch-based methods discard global spatial layout while diffusion-based methods generate images without structured compositional dependencies. The technical core builds on InvSR's single-step diffusion inversion, repurposing it from natural-image super-resolution to grid-cell detail restoration, and on CLIP embeddings for constructing the affinity kernel that drives a novel spectral-submodular selection objective (SSDIM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a conceptually clean unification—structured selection for semantic coverage plus a fast diffusion prior for spatial fidelity—that could influence how future distillation methods encode intra-class diversity. However, the ImageNet-1K results reveal a tension: the bilinear-only variant (35.4%) underperforms RDED (42.0%) and Minimax (44.3%), meaning the grid structure alone does not yet beat established methods at scale, and the gains depend heavily on the diffusion enhancement step. The submodular ablation (Table 6) further suggests that the coverage term (α) dominates while diversity and spectral terms contribute marginally, raising questions about whether the full SSDIM formulation is necessary or whether simpler selection criteria suffice. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 10-class subsets, how sensitive it is to the CLIP encoder choice, and whether the 16.9% one-time diffusion overhead remains acceptable for very large IPC settings.
