## Problem

In few-shot synthetic data generation for classification, image-wise LoRA adapters capture fine-grained details but produce near-duplicate images, while class-wise LoRA adapters yield diverse samples but lose instance-level fidelity. No prior method simultaneously achieves both high diversity and high detail when augmenting underrepresented classes with diffusion-generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261234/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K few-shot real images per class (4 in the main experiments) along with the class name as a text prompt, and produces 500 synthetic 512×512 RGB images per class via Stable Diffusion 2.1. The generated images are then used to fine-tune a CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder (with rank-16 LoRA on both image and text encoders) for downstream classification. A key constraint is that the synthetic set must remain distribution-aligned with the few-shot real references, which the paper quantifies via FID, CLIP score, and centroid similarity.

## Method

ChimeraLoRA splits the standard LoRA into a single shared LoRA A that encodes class-level priors and K per-image LoRA B heads that capture instance-specific details, trained jointly with the base diffusion model frozen. To ensure the shared A learns coherent class semantics, a semantic boosting technique uses Grounded-SAM to obtain class bounding boxes and enforces that every training crop fully contains the target object, preventing partial-visibility artifacts. At generation time, A is fixed and the K B heads are merged with nonnegative coefficients sampled from a Dirichlet(α) distribution, so each synthetic image receives a different stochastic mixture of instance details while retaining the shared class backbone. The concentration parameter α interpolates between image-wise (α<1, sparse weights) and class-wise (α>1, near-uniform weights) regimes, with α=1 providing the best downstream performance in practice.

## Results

Under the 4-shot setting, ChimeraLoRA generates 500 synthetic images per class and achieves 74.6% average top-1 accuracy across nine classification datasets, outperforming the 4-shot real-only CLIP baseline (71.8%) by 2.8 pp and the strongest LoRA-based baseline LoFT (72.5%) by 2.1 pp. In long-tail scenarios (4 shots per tail class), adding ChimeraLoRA synthetic images to tail classes yields a 7.62 pp average accuracy gain over the real-only baseline, with a 14.74 pp improvement on tail classes specifically. On synthetic-to-real gap metrics, ChimeraLoRA attains the lowest FID@4 (0.20) and highest CLIP score (30.31) and centroid similarity (90.5) among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChimeraLoRA outperforms all three baselines on 7 of 9 datasets in the 4-shot setting, with the largest margins on ISIC (+3.6 pp over LoFT, +8.5 pp over DataDream) and EuroSAT (+1.3 pp over LoFT, +14.1 pp over DataDream). The only losses are on StanfordCars (79.6 vs. DataDream's 81.7) and Food101 (85.7 vs. DataDream's 86.0), where the class-wise DataDream's broader class prior slightly edges out the multi-head approach. Against the train-free IsSynth baseline, ChimeraLoRA leads by 4.5 pp on average, with particularly large gains on EuroSAT (+12.9 pp) and FGVCAircraft (+6.1 pp). In long-tail experiments (Table 2), ChimeraLoRA consistently achieves the highest tail-class accuracy across all five tested datasets (e.g., 81.0% on CIFAR-10 tail vs. 76.5% LoFT and 76.3% DataDream), and on ImageNet-100 it is the only method that simultaneously improves both head and tail accuracy over the real-only baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of LoRA-guided synthetic dataset generation (LoFT for image-wise, DataDream for class-wise) by splitting the single LoRA into a shared LoRA A encoding class priors and per-image LoRA B heads capturing instance-specific details, directly adopting the asymmetric multi-head LoRA paradigm from HydraLoRA and FedSA-LoRA in the NLP/federated-learning literature. The semantic boosting component repurposes Grounded-SAM bounding boxes as a training-time cropping constraint, a technique analogous to object-aware cropping in contrastive learning but applied here to diffusion fine-tuning. The Dirichlet-weighted head mixing at generation time generalizes LoFT's uniform averaging into a tunable interpolation between image-wise and class-wise regimes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical recipe for closing the synthetic-to-real gap in few-shot and long-tailed classification, with consistent gains across 11 diverse datasets including medical and satellite imagery. A notable finding is that single-granularity baselines (DataDream, LoFT) can actually degrade in accuracy as more synthetic images are added, whereas ChimeraLoRA improves with scale, suggesting the multi-head decomposition is what keeps the synthetic distribution anchored to the real manifold. Limitations include reliance on Grounded-SAM (which may be suboptimal for medical or other specialized domains), limited medical validation, and the computational cost of per-image Dirichlet sampling at generation time. Open questions include whether the shared-A/per-image-B split generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether domain-specific segmentation tools can further improve the semantic boosting step.
