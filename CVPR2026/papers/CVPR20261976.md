## Problem

Existing shared autonomy frameworks for urban navigation require humans and AI to operate in the same low-level action space (e.g., joystick control at the same frequency), creating high cognitive overhead for long-horizon tasks like sidewalk delivery. No prior system decomposes navigation into high-level human strategic instructions (text, visual paths, speed cues) and low-level AI execution, leaving a gap in reducing operator workload while maintaining safety in complex, dynamic urban environments.

## Input / Output

The system consumes the past 3 egocentric RGB frames (resized to 448×448) plus either a GPS subgoal in egocentric coordinates (autopilot mode) or a human instruction of one of three types: a natural-language text command, a set of K pixel coordinates sampled along a drawn path (drafting), or a compact (speed, heading) pair (arrowing). Instructions remain valid for a bounded duration t* after issuance. The model outputs a distribution over future trajectory waypoints initialized from 64 learned motion-primitive anchors (straight, turn, stop, etc.), each with a confidence score, rather than a single deterministic path.

## Method

AURA is a dual-system VLA: a frozen-vision InternVL3-2B encoder (adapted via LoRA) produces context features, and a DiT diffusion decoder cross-attends those features to denoise trajectories from 64 anchor proposals. The key architectural contribution is the Spatial-Aware Instruction Encoder (SIE), which renders drafting/arrowing inputs as visual overlays, encodes their geometry via Fourier positional encoding (for path points) or rotation-invariant (cos θ′, sin θ′, log(1+|v|)) encoding (for speed/heading), fuses these with ViT visual features through cross-attention, and injects the result into the LLM via a dedicated <instruction> token. Training is two-stage: first, the SIE and LoRA adapters are trained with a language-modeling loss on VLM-generated trajectory captions; second, the diffusion decoder is trained end-to-end with a combined mode-classification (cross-entropy over anchors) and L2 trajectory-regression loss while the encoder is frozen. The MM-CoS dataset (50 h of real sidewalk teleoperation augmented with RECON, SCAND, and EgoWalk) is auto-labeled by a two-stage pipeline that scores frame informativeness with a VLM plus motion saliency, then synthesizes all three instruction modalities and reasoning traces from ground-truth trajectories.

## Results

On the MM-CoS open-loop benchmark, AURA's arrowing-guided variant achieves the lowest L2 error (0.150 m at 1 s, 0.473 m at 2 s), outperforming the best retrained baseline CityWalker* (0.353 m / 0.786 m) by 57.5% and 39.8% respectively. The point-guided variant attains the highest mAP (0.844) versus NoMaD's 0.216. In real-world closed-loop testing over 2.8 km of sidewalk routes, AURA reduces the Human Operation Ratio to 1.73% (vs. 9.74% for NoMaD and 14.56% for CityWalker) and cuts the Normalized Intervention Rate to 16.99 per 100 m (vs. 43.2 for NoMaD and 255.7 for Gemini).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AURA outperforms all six baselines across every reported metric in the open-loop MM-CoS evaluation. Against the strongest retrained baseline CityWalker*, the arrowing variant reduces L2₂ₛ from 0.786 m to 0.473 m (−39.8%) and L2₁ₛ from 0.353 m to 0.150 m (−57.5%); the point variant achieves mAP 0.844 versus NoMaD's 0.216, a 3.9× improvement in trajectory ranking quality. In the real-world closed-loop pilot (Table 3), AURA's Human Operation Ratio of 1.73% is 5.6× lower than NoMaD (9.74%) and 8.4× lower than CityWalker (14.56%), while its Normalized Intervention Rate of 16.99 per 100 m is roughly 2.6× lower than NoMaD (43.2) and 15× lower than Gemini (255.7). The Time Success Rate is comparable between AURA (89.3%) and NoMaD (89.0%), but AURA achieves this with far fewer human interventions, indicating that the shared-autonomy design shifts the burden from continuous monitoring to sparse high-level guidance. Geometric instructions (drafting, arrowing) consistently yield tighter trajectory predictions than purely linguistic (texting) instructions, confirming that spatial grounding is critical for precise navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AURA builds directly on the authors' prior MIMIC framework [19] for the diffusion-based trajectory decoder and the CoS sidewalk teleoperation dataset, extending both with a multimodal instruction interface. It departs from prior shared-autonomy approaches [11, 12, 46] that couple human and AI in the same low-level action space, instead introducing a hierarchical decomposition where humans issue high-level instructions (text, drafting, arrowing) and the AI handles continuous low-level control. The Spatial-Aware Instruction Encoder draws on Fourier positional encoding from Segment Anything [31] and VLM backbones (InternVL3) to ground geometric instructions in scene context, a design not present in prior VLA navigation models like CityWalker or NoMaD that rely solely on point or image goals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in urban sidewalk robotics: current delivery and assistive bots still require continuous human teleoperation, and the 44% reduction in takeover frequency and 70% reduction in human operation cost are directly relevant to scalable deployment. The MM-CoS dataset (50 hours of real-world sidewalk teleoperation with multimodal annotations) fills a notable data gap, as prior navigation datasets are largely campus- or indoor-focused. Open questions include whether the hierarchical instruction interface generalizes to more complex multi-agent urban scenes, how the system degrades under sustained long-horizon operation beyond the 2.8 km pilot, and whether the SIE's geometric encoding transfers to non-egocentric or multi-camera setups.
