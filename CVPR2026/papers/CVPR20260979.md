## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) lacks volumetric interiors with coherent textures, making realistic fracture visualization (e.g., exposing watermelon flesh beneath a rind) impossible. Additionally, existing GS simulation methods are confined to elastic deformation, and the point-cloud CD-MPM fracture method is incompatible with GS due to a discontinuous return-mapping scheme that causes numerical instability and mandates prohibitively slow CPU-only execution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260979/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images of a target object and a text prompt describing its internal appearance (e.g., 'three-layer cake with chocolate filling'). It produces a fully populated 3DGS with photorealistic interior textures that can be physically simulated for fracture, deformation, slicing, and fluid-like behavior, then rendered in real-time under dynamic Blinn-Phong lighting. No additional training data or fine-tuning is required; texture synthesis relies entirely on pretrained generative models (MVInpainter, SD-XL).

## Method

GaussianFluent has two core components. First, for internal filling, it initializes interior Gaussians via a density-field threshold on a discretized grid, refines them with opacity-only optimization to prune extraneous primitives, then generates textures in two stages: coarse single-view inpainting with MVInpainter followed by iterative multi-axis (X, Y, Z) low-strength inpainting with SD-XL that drives tri-axial consistency through successive SH coefficient optimization. Second, for fracture simulation, it integrates an optimized CD-MPM into the GS framework where the key fix is replacing the fixed ellipse center (p_c, 0) in the return-mapping projection with a dynamic point (p_c', 0) that smoothly interpolates based on the trial pressure, eliminating the discontinuity at the yield-surface boundary that previously caused chaotic divergence under GPU atomic operations. This continuous projection enables full GPU parallelization (reducing per-frame time from >3400 s on CPU to ~5 s at 300³ resolution) while supporting mixed materials by assigning distinct fracture-control parameters β to segmented object parts (e.g., seed, flesh, rind).

## Results

On dynamic scene simulation, GaussianFluent achieves a CLIP score of 22.7 and 88.46% user preference (23/26), substantially outperforming PhysGaussian (12.2 CLIP, 3.84%) and OmniPhysGS (13.1 CLIP, 7.69%). For internal texture filling, the method scores 35.4 CLIP and 71.43% user preference (60/84), versus 22.3/3.57% for PhysGaussian and 30.1/25.00% for 2D inpainting. In terms of runtime, GaussianFluent runs at 0.78 s/frame at 100³ resolution—nearly identical to PhysGaussian (0.75 s/frame)—while the CPU-only CD-MPM baseline requires 616 s/frame, a ~790× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PhysGaussian, GaussianFluent wins decisively on both simulation quality (22.7 vs. 12.2 CLIP; 88.46% vs. 3.84% user preference) and internal texture fidelity (35.4 vs. 22.3 CLIP; 71.43% vs. 3.57% user preference), while incurring only a marginal runtime overhead (0.78 vs. 0.75 s/frame at 100³). Against OmniPhysGS, the gap is equally large in simulation (22.7 vs. 13.1 CLIP; 88.46% vs. 7.69% user preference), as OmniPhysGS remains confined to the elastic-only PhysGaussian framework and cannot model brittle fracture. Against the original CPU-based CD-MPM, GaussianFluent achieves a ~790× speedup at 100³ resolution (0.78 s vs. 616 s/frame) and ~675× at 300³ (5.05 s vs. 3408 s/frame), made possible by the continuous return-mapping fix that resolves the numerical instability preventing GPU parallelization. The mixed-material fracture capability (e.g., watermelon with distinct β for rind, flesh, and seed) is entirely absent in both PhysGaussian and OmniPhysGS, which assume uniform material properties.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PhysGaussian line of work, which first coupled the Material Point Method with 3D Gaussian Splatting for elastic material simulation, by incorporating Continuum Damage Mechanics (CD-MPM) to enable brittle fracture and topological changes. It departs from prior GS simulation methods in two key ways: (1) a training-free internal texture synthesis pipeline using MVInpainter and SD-XL that addresses the hollow-interior problem ignored by PhysGaussian and FruitNinja, and (2) a continuous return-mapping fix for CD-MPM that resolves the numerical discontinuity preventing GPU parallelization. The mixed-material formulation (per-part β assignment) generalizes the uniform-material assumption in both PhysGaussian and the original CD-MPM-TOG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GaussianFluent makes physically plausible brittle fracture simulation feasible in real-time GS rendering, opening the door to interactive applications in VR, robotics, and virtual worlds where objects shatter, slice, or deform with structurally coherent interiors. The continuous return-mapping fix is a small but critical mathematical correction that unlocks GPU acceleration (reducing per-frame cost from ~3400 s on CPU to ~5 s on GPU at 300³ resolution), making CD-MPM practically usable in interactive pipelines. Key limitations remain: fluid behavior is only a rough approximation via β=0, hollow and thin-walled structures are over-filled, and the quantitative evaluation relies heavily on CLIP scores and small user studies rather than physics-grounded metrics. Future work should incorporate dedicated fluid solvers, explicit hollowness prediction, and broader constitutive models to move beyond the current demo-level scenarios.
