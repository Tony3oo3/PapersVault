## Problem

Existing RL-based reasoning segmentation methods rely on geometric rewards (IoU, L1 distance) that only guide final localization and cannot discriminate whether the intermediate reasoning chain remains anchored on the target or drifts into irrelevant context. This lack of discriminative guidance causes reasoning to devolve into verbose, unfocused chains that incorporate contextual distractors, ultimately failing to disambiguate the target in complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263370/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a natural-language query requiring inference (e.g., relational or logical cues). The MLLM policy emits a structured token sequence with three tagged components: a <think> reasoning chain, an <answer> JSON object containing a bounding box and two key points, and a <caption> with a concise anchored description of the localized object. The geometric localization is then consumed by a frozen SAM2-Large to render the final pixel-level mask, while the caption serves as a human-readable interpretability rationale.

## Method

DPAD augments the standard GRPO-based RL pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL policy + frozen SAM2-Large segmenter) with a discriminative perception reward computed via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32. The model is compelled to generate an anchored descriptive caption of its own localization; CLIP then scores the caption's semantic similarity against the ground-truth ROI crop (S₁) and the full image (S₂). The discriminative reward is binary—1 if S₁ > S₂, 0 otherwise—making it compatible with the discrete success/failure signals already used by the format and geometric rewards. The composite reward R_final = R_format + R_geo + R_dpad is optimized via GRPO, implicitly pruning divergent reasoning and forcing the model to focus on unique target attributes. Trained on only 3,000 RefCOCOg samples, the approach reduces average reasoning chain length by ~42% while improving cIoU on ReasonSeg by 3.09%.

## Results

On the primary ReasonSeg benchmark, DPAD-7B achieves a test gIoU of 60.8 and cIoU of 57.5, improving over the reproduced Seg-Zero-7B baseline by +3.1 on both metrics. On the RefCOCO suite (zero-shot transfer), DPAD-7B reaches 79.3 / 74.7 / 72.6 cIoU on RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg, surpassing the Seg-Zero-7B* reproduction by +0.6 / +0.3 / +1.3 respectively. A notable efficiency gain accompanies the accuracy improvement: the average reasoning chain length drops from ~118 tokens (Seg-Zero) to ~69 tokens (DPAD), a ~42% reduction, while discriminative SNR and TSNR metrics consistently exceed the 1.0 threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most direct and relevant comparison is against Seg-Zero-7B [17], the RL-based reasoning segmentation method that DPAD builds upon. Using the authors' own reproduction (Seg-Zero-7B*), DPAD wins on every metric: +3.1 gIoU and +3.1 cIoU on ReasonSeg test, and +0.6 / +0.3 / +1.3 cIoU on RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg. Against the originally reported Seg-Zero-7B† numbers, DPAD still leads on test gIoU (60.8 vs. 57.5) and test cIoU (57.5 vs. 52.0), though the reported val cIoU (62.0) slightly exceeds DPAD's (61.2). All earlier SFT-based or non-RL baselines are substantially outperformed: LISA-13B [11] trails by ~7 gIoU on ReasonSeg test, SAM4MLLM [4] by ~14 gIoU, and OVSeg [14] by ~35 gIoU. On the RefCOCO suite, DPAD also surpasses LAVT [32] (79.3 vs. 75.8 on RefCOCO) and MagNet [6] (72.6 vs. 69.3 on RefCOCOg), confirming that the discriminative reward generalizes beyond the primary reasoning benchmark to standard referring expression segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-driven reasoning segmentation paradigm established by Seg-Zero, which uses GRPO with purely geometric rewards (IoU, L1) to elicit emergent chain-of-thought reasoning. The key departure is the identification of a gap in geometric rewards: they evaluate only the final mask quality and cannot discriminate whether intermediate reasoning stays anchored on the target or drifts into irrelevant context. DPAD fills this gap by introducing a discriminative perception reward that contrasts a model-generated anchored caption's CLIP similarity to the ground-truth ROI versus the full image, adding a semantic focus signal alongside the existing geometric and format rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical inefficiency in RL-trained segmentation models where verbose, unfocused reasoning chains degrade both accuracy and inference cost; the ~42% token reduction alongside accuracy gains is directly useful for deployment. The co-generated descriptive caption also provides a lightweight interpretability layer that aligns with the segmentation output. Open questions include whether the binary discriminative reward (which outperforms continuous variants) is robust across architectures and scales, whether CLIP-based scoring is the optimal semantic proxy, and whether the approach transfers to video or multi-object reasoning segmentation settings.
