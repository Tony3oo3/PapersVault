## Problem

Despite near-human accuracy on general VQA benchmarks, current vision-language models systematically fail at fine-grained spatial reasoning about articulated hand poses, often performing at or below random chance on joint-level geometric relations. No large-scale diagnostic benchmark existed to isolate and measure this specific failure mode, and it remained unclear whether such spatial knowledge could be acquired through targeted training and transferred to downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262436/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs a hand RGB image (from FreiHAND, InterHand2.6M, or FPHA) with a multiple-choice question derived from normalized 3D coordinates of 21 hand joints. Questions probe five sub-tasks—joint angle (4 ordinal bins), inter-joint distance (3 bins), and relative position along X, Y, Z axes (3 bins each, with ambiguous 'aligned' cases excluded)—yielding 25 MCQs per image. The model outputs a single selected answer option; evaluation uses accuracy and, for the ordinal angle and distance tasks, mean absolute error over class indices.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully automated, deterministic three-stage pipeline (F_pose → F_text → F_mcq) that converts normalized 3D hand joint coordinates into anatomically-grounded VQA pairs: continuous descriptors (arccos-based angles, Euclidean distances, signed axis offsets) are discretized via fixed thresholds into category labels, filled into fixed syntactic templates to form candidate sentences, and assembled into 4-option MCQs. The authors evaluate three 7B VLMs (LLaVA Mistral, DeepSeek Janus Pro, Qwen 2.5 VL) in both base and LoRA fine-tuned settings, revealing that base models perform near-random on all spatial sub-tasks while fine-tuning yields large gains—though angle interpretation remains the hardest sub-task even after adaptation. A key finding is that the 3D-grounded spatial knowledge acquired through HandVQA fine-tuning transfers zero-shot to novel downstream tasks, improving static gesture recognition by +10.33% and video-based hand-object interaction by +2.63% without any task-specific training.

## Results

Base VLMs perform at or below random chance on HandVQA: LLaVA Mistral 7B scores only 13–16% accuracy on distance (random = 33.3%) and 23–42% on angle (random = 25%), while Qwen 2.5 VL 7B achieves 18–20% on distance and 24–39% on angle. After LoRA fine-tuning, all models improve dramatically: distance accuracy jumps to 80–91% and relative-position accuracy to 70–99%, but angle remains the hardest sub-task, peaking at 74.35% (LLaVA on InterHand2.6M). In zero-shot transfer, HandVQA fine-tuning yields +12.16 pp on gesture recognition (LLaVA: 57.42→69.58%) and +2.63 pp on video-based hand-object interaction (Qwen: 80.26→82.89%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates three 7B VLMs—LLaVA Mistral, DeepSeek Janus Pro, and Qwen 2.5 VL—in both base and LoRA fine-tuned settings. Among base models, no single VLM dominates: LLaVA leads on angle accuracy (avg ~35%), DeepSeek leads on distance (avg ~43%), and Qwen is consistently the weakest on distance (13–20%, well below the 33.3% random baseline). After fine-tuning, LLaVA Mistral emerges as the clear winner across all five sub-tasks and all three datasets, achieving the highest angle accuracy (74.35% on InterHand2.6M) and the lowest angle MAE (0.263), while DeepSeek and Qwen trail by 6–10 pp on angle. On distance and relative-position tasks, all three fine-tuned models converge to 80–99% accuracy, with LLaVA again marginally ahead (e.g., 97.14% on Rel. Pos. X vs. 92.58% DeepSeek, 94.90% Qwen on InterHand2.6M). The key takeaway is that fine-tuning on HandVQA is the dominant factor—any model gains 30–75 pp over its base counterpart—while architectural differences become secondary once sufficient spatial supervision is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-reasoning VQA benchmark lineage (SPHERE, What'sUp, CLEVR) by shifting focus from inter-object relations to intra-object, part-whole geometry: specifically, 3D joint-level spatial relationships within the human hand. It draws conceptual motivation from PoseScript [15] but repurposes the idea of mapping continuous 3D pose descriptors into natural-language MCQs, grounding them in precise 3D annotations from FreiHAND, InterHand2.6M, and FPHA. Unlike prior spatial benchmarks that conflate spatial skills with world knowledge or scene priors, HandVQA isolates geometric reasoning (angles, Euclidean distances, axis-aligned offsets) within a single articulated object, and uniquely positions the benchmark as both a diagnostic tool and a transferable training resource for downstream hand-related tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete, measurable gap: base VLMs perform at or below random on hand-joint spatial questions, and even after LoRA fine-tuning, joint-angle interpretation remains the weakest sub-task (<75%), suggesting frozen vision encoders are a bottleneck for fine-grained geometric perception. The zero-shot transfer results (+10.33% on gesture recognition, +2.63% on video-based hand-object interaction) are promising but rest on only two 7B models and a single fine-tuning recipe, leaving open whether the effect holds at larger scale or with full-model training. Key limitations include static-image-only evaluation, fixed discretization thresholds, and the absence of video or contact-dynamics reasoning; extending HandVQA to temporal settings and integrating it with Vision-Language-Action models are natural next steps for embodied AI and surgical robotics applications.
