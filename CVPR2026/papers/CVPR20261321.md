## Problem

Multi-camera 3D object detection models fail to generalize to unseen camera configurations (different intrinsics, extrinsics, and array layouts), requiring costly data recollection and retraining for each new platform. Prior solutions either warp images into a meta-camera (causing resolution loss and 3D scene distortion) or rescale virtual focal lengths (depth-based designs that cannot apply to all MC3D paradigms). This paper identifies that the root cause is spatial prior discrepancies across configurations and proposes a unified, paradigm-agnostic solution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261321/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N surrounding RGB camera images (downsampled to 704 or 800 width depending on the base model) along with per-camera intrinsic matrices (focal length, principal point) and extrinsic matrices (rotation, translation from camera to ego frame). During training, it additionally consumes pre-built ego-centric 3D Gaussian representations for dynamic novel-view rendering. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes in ego space, each with position (x, y, z), dimensions (l, w, h), orientation θ, and a category label, evaluated within a ±50 m range.

## Method

CoIn3D introduces two complementary modules. Spatial-aware Feature Modulation (SFM) embeds four pixel-level spatial priors into backbone features: an inverse focal map (1/f²) that normalizes feature activations to eliminate focal-length ambiguity via element-wise multiplication; a ground depth map and ground gradient map (log-inverse of cross-row depth differences) that encode camera-height-dependent perspective; and a 6-D Plücker raymap (ray direction + moment per pixel) that holistically encodes FoV, rotation, and translation. The latter three maps are concatenated, projected through a 3×3 conv + ReLU, and added to the focal-normalized feature, while all four raw maps are also concatenated as additional channels. Camera-aware Data Augmentation (CDA) is a training-free 3DGS pipeline that reconstructs ego-centric texture point clouds from LiDAR annotations via TSDF mesh integration, converts them to axis-aligned Gaussians with fixed radius, and dynamically renders novel-view images under randomly sampled camera configurations during training. The framework is model-agnostic and plugs into bottom-up BEV (BEVDepth), top-down BEV (BEVFormer), and sparse-query (PETR) paradigms without architectural modification.

## Results

On the primary BEVDepth-based evaluation, CoIn3D achieves NDS* of 0.513 (NuScenes→Waymo), 0.481 (Waymo→NuScenes), 0.534 (NuScenes→Lyft), and 0.452 (Lyft→NuScenes), surpassing the previous SOTA UDGA-BEV by 0.054, 0.004, 0.047, and 0.031 respectively. Direct transfer of BEVDepth without adaptation yields near-zero mAP (0.040, 0.032) on the most challenging cross-dataset pairs, while CoIn3D recovers mAP to 0.381 and 0.349. The framework also generalizes to BEVFormer and PETR paradigms, where it improves NDS* from 0.115 to 0.377 (BEVFormer, N→L) and from 0.046 to 0.456 (PETR, N→L).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIn3D outperforms all five baselines across all four cross-dataset settings. Against the strongest baseline UDGA-BEV [2], CoIn3D gains +0.054 NDS* on NuScenes→Waymo, +0.004 on Waymo→NuScenes, +0.047 on NuScenes→Lyft, and +0.031 on Lyft→NuScenes. DG-BEV [37] and PD-BEV [26] achieve moderate improvements over direct transfer but remain 0.05–0.11 NDS* below CoIn3D, as they only partially address focal ambiguity or depth consistency without explicitly encoding full camera configuration. CAM-Convs [7] and Single-DGOD [40] are designed for monocular depth and 2D detection respectively, and show negligible gains (NDS* ≤ 0.33) because they do not model multi-camera configuration gaps. Notably, CoIn3D is the only method applicable to all three MC3D paradigms (bottom-up BEV, top-down BEV, sparse queries), whereas PD-BEV and UDGA-BEV are restricted to depth-based BEV schemes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of cross-configuration generalization for MC3D (DG-BEV, PD-BEV, UDGA-BEV, UniDrive) by shifting from implicit or depth-based virtual-focal strategies to explicit spatial-prior encoding in feature space. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for novel-view synthesis but repurposes it in a training-free, ego-centric pipeline tailored to MC3D data augmentation rather than scene reconstruction. The Plücker raymap component draws on recent camera-as-rays formulations, while the overall SFM module is designed to be paradigm-agnostic, unlike prior depth-rescaling methods that are restricted to bottom-up BEV schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single model trained on one vehicle platform can transfer to unseen camera configurations without recollecting or reannotating data, directly reducing deployment costs for autonomous driving and robotics. The paradigm-agnostic design (validated on BEVDepth, BEVFormer, and PETR) makes the framework broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes across semantic distributions (not just geometric configurations), whether the 3DGS augmentation pipeline scales to datasets without LiDAR annotations, and how performance holds under even more extreme configuration gaps (e.g., drone vs. ground vehicle).
