## Problem

Existing pose-guided video generation methods are restricted to human skeletal inputs and cannot accommodate arbitrary non-human skeletal structures, leaving a gap for universal character animation. Additionally, these methods struggle to maintain fine-grained appearance consistency during large pose changes and offer no mechanism for independent camera motion control alongside subject pose control.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262940/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a reference image (first frame), an arbitrary skeletal pose sequence of any topology (human or non-human, with variable segment counts), and a text prompt. The pose sequence is encoded into latent space via the pretrained Wan2.2 VAE and concatenated channel-wise with the reference image latent before entering the DiT blocks. The output is a temporally coherent video clip in which the subject faithfully follows the specified skeletal motion while preserving its appearance from the reference frame.

## Method

PoseAnything is built on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B (a DiT-based video diffusion model) and injects pose information via channel-wise concatenation of the pose latent with the reference image latent, a strategy shown to outperform MLP fusion and width concatenation. The Part-aware Temporal Coherence Module (PTCM) segments each pose into individual segments, dilates them into part masks, uses attention-weight matching to establish cross-frame part correspondences, and then computes cross-attention exclusively between matched parts (Q from current frame, K/V from first frame) to enforce fine-grained part-level consistency. For camera control, the paper introduces Subject and Camera Motion Decoupled CFG, which injects the subject pose sequence into the positive anchor and a camera-motion skeleton (e.g., a moving rectangle) into the negative anchor of classifier-free guidance, thereby decoupling the two control signals and preventing mutual interference without any architectural modification.

## Results

On the TikTok human benchmark, PoseAnything achieves the best scores across all five metrics: PSNR 31.50, SSIM 0.836, L1 2.79×10⁻⁵, LPIPS 0.224, and FVD 133.95, outperforming the closest competitor Animate-X (FVD 139.01) by ~5 FVD points. On the non-human XPose benchmark, PoseAnything similarly leads all metrics with PSNR 30.29, SSIM 0.7114, LPIPS 0.3241, and FVD 99.97, surpassing the best trajectory-based baseline ATI (FVD 101.44). The ablation confirms the Part-aware Temporal Coherence Module contributes a further ~1.5 FVD improvement over whole-object cross-attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the human TikTok benchmark, PoseAnything wins on every metric against all seven baselines. The closest human-animation competitors are Animate-X (FVD 139.01, LPIPS 0.232) and UniAnimate (FVD 148.06, LPIPS 0.231); PoseAnything improves FVD by 5.06 and 14.11 points respectively, and reduces LPIPS by 0.008 and 0.007. Earlier methods like Disco (FVD 292.80) and AnimateAnyone (FVD 171.90) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 40–150 FVD points. On the non-human XPose benchmark, PoseAnything outperforms all three trajectory/drag-based baselines (Tora, ATI, SG-I2V) on every metric, with the margin over ATI being the smallest (FVD 99.97 vs. 101.44; LPIPS 0.3241 vs. 0.3706). Notably, the trajectory-based methods (Tora, SG-I2V, ATI) are not designed for pose-level control, so the comparison highlights PoseAnything's advantage in fine-grained skeletal guidance over coarse positional trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseAnything extends the human-centric pose-guided video generation lineage (Disco → AnimateAnyone → Animate-X) by generalizing skeletal input from human-only to arbitrary skeleton topologies, built on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT backbone. The Part-aware Temporal Coherence Module departs from prior whole-object cross-attention or ControlNet-based consistency approaches by decomposing the subject into parts and computing matched-part cross-attention, while the Subject and Camera Motion Decoupled CFG repurposes the positive/negative anchors of classifier-free guidance—a mechanism previously used only for text conditioning—to independently steer subject and camera motion. The XPose dataset fills a data gap that no prior public resource addressed, complementing the human-centric TikTok benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By supporting arbitrary skeletal inputs and releasing the first 50K non-human pose-video dataset, PoseAnything opens the door to pose-driven animation of animals, robots, and other non-human characters that were previously inaccessible to pose-guided generation. The decoupled CFG trick for camera control is a lightweight, training-free addition that could be adopted by other controllable video generation systems. However, the non-human evaluation compares against trajectory-based (not pose-based) baselines, leaving the true competitive landscape for non-human pose generation unclear; additionally, the part-matching step requires preliminary inference passes, and the camera control relies on a somewhat ad-hoc negative-anchor formulation whose robustness across diverse camera motions remains untested.
