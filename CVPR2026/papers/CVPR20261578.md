## Problem

Existing deepfake detection benchmarks are constrained to single modalities, rely on obsolete or simplified generators, and lack rigorous out-of-distribution evaluation protocols. Detection systems are fragmented across modalities with no unified framework for joint detection, localization, and explanation, and their binary outputs provide no transparency for forensic or moderation use. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously with a four-modality benchmark and a unified detector.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261578/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts any of four input types—RGB image, audio clip, generic video, or audio-video talking-head video—and produces a structured triple: a ternary authenticity label (real / partially manipulated / fully synthetic; binary for talking-head), a spatial bounding box or temporal interval localizing the forged region, and a free-text rationale. Outputs are constrained to a specific tag format (think/answer blocks with box_start/box_end or interval_start/interval_end segments) that a deterministic parser validates, enabling reliable extraction of all three components for downstream evaluation.

## Method

Omni-Fake-R1 is built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B and trained in two stages. Stage 1 is a four-stage curriculum SFT that introduces modalities sequentially (audio → image → video → AV talking-head), mixing each new modality's full data with a 15% replay subset from all previously seen modalities to prevent catastrophic forgetting while enabling cross-modal knowledge transfer. Stage 2 applies unified GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) reinforcement learning on top of the SFT checkpoint, sampling multiple responses per input and scoring them with a composite scalar reward combining format validity, detection accuracy (with class-weighted rewards to balance the ternary label space), spatial IoU for bounding-box localization, and temporal IoU for interval localization. This RL phase directly optimizes task-level metrics rather than next-token likelihood, jointly sharpening detection, localization, and explanation while maintaining cross-modal consistency.

## Results

On the Omni-Fake-Set validation split, Omni-Fake-R1 achieves the best detection accuracy across all four modalities: 91.92% (image), 89.84% (video), 92.13% (audio), and 96.18% (AV talking-head), with corresponding macro F1 scores of 90.58, 88.29, 90.47, and 95.54. For localization, it attains the highest IoU and F1 among methods that support it: 47.06/51.63 (image), 40.63/43.35 (video), and 45.92/47.58 (audio). On the disjoint Omni-Fake-OOD benchmark, Omni-Fake-R1 maintains the strongest performance (e.g., 79.25% image Acc, 85.96% video Acc, 83.85% audio Acc, 86.52% AV-TH Acc), outperforming all baselines by 5–20+ points depending on modality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-Fake-R1 outperforms all 25 baselines across every modality on both detection and (where applicable) localization. On images, it leads the closest competitor FakeVLM [86] (90.34% Acc) by 1.6 points and SIDA [39] (89.88% Acc) by 2.0 points, while surpassing VLM baselines LLaVA-1.5-13B [60] and DeepSeek-VL-7B [62] by over 10 points; it also achieves higher localization IoU (47.06) than SIDA (46.27) despite SIDA having a higher localization F1 (56.10 vs. 51.63). On video, the gap is wider: Omni-Fake-R1 (89.84% Acc) beats the strongest detection-only baseline DeMamba [14] (84.22%) by 5.6 points and the best VLM Qwen2.5-VL-7B [90] (76.45%) by 13.4 points, while also improving temporal localization IoU from 33.14 to 40.63. On audio, it dominates the best audio-specific detector SafeEar [57] (81.62% Acc) by 10.5 points and FakeSound [88] (75.41%) by 16.7 points, with interval IoU more than doubling FakeSound's 26.71 to 45.92. On AV talking-head, it exceeds RealForensics [32] (88.17%) by 8.0 points and LIPINC [24] (86.94%) by 9.2 points. Under OOD evaluation (Table 5), the advantage persists and often widens: e.g., on video OOD, Omni-Fake-R1 (85.96% Acc) leads DeMamba (78.93%) by 7.0 points, and on audio OOD it leads SafeEar (71.29%) by 12.6 points, confirming that the unified multimodal training generalizes better to unseen generators than single-modality specialists.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-modality deepfake detection line (FaceForensics++, DFDC, SIDA, So-Fake) into a unified four-modality setting by building on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B as the shared multimodal backbone and adopting the curriculum SFT with modal replay strategy from Light-R1 to prevent catastrophic forgetting across modalities. The reinforcement learning stage departs from standard SFT-only training by applying GSPO (Group Sequence Policy Optimization) with a composite detection-localization-explanation reward, a formulation not previously applied to deepfake forensics. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between fragmented single-modality detectors and the emerging paradigm of omni-modal MLLMs, positioning deepfake detection as a structured multi-task problem rather than a binary classification task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Omni-Fake provides the first large-scale, training-ready benchmark that unifies image, audio, video, and talking-head deepfake detection under a single protocol with a strictly disjoint OOD split, filling a critical gap where prior benchmarks were either single-modality, lacked OOD evaluation, or did not support joint localization and explanation. The finding that a unified detection-localization-explanation protocol with cross-modal curriculum training yields substantially better OOD generalization than detection-only baselines suggests that forcing the model to articulate *where* and *why* a forgery occurs acts as a regularizer against generator-specific shortcut learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to entirely new generator families (e.g., next-generation world models), whether the 15% replay ratio generalizes to more than four modalities, and whether the RL reward design can be extended to adversarial or multi-round manipulation scenarios.
