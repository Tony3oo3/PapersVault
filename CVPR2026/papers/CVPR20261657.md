## Problem

Existing interleaved multimodal benchmarks are restricted to text-image pairs, evaluate only a single isolated capability per instance, and lack a unified framework for any-to-any modality combinations. This paper addresses the absence of a benchmark that simultaneously covers 7 modalities, 30 real-world domains, and multiple intertwined reasoning/generation tasks within each instance under a single any-to-any interleaved paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261657/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each instance is an open-form QA where both input and output are sequences of interleaved information pieces drawn from any combination of 7 modalities (text, image, audio, video, document, code, 3D), with non-textual items denoted by placeholder tags such as <image1> or <video2>. A single instance may contain multiple items per modality (e.g., several images) and requires the model to perform multiple intertwined tasks—ranging from perception and spatial/temporal reasoning to content generation and editing—within one response. The 31,026 instances are graded into three difficulty levels (Easy/Medium/Hard) via a rule-based taxonomy.

## Method

The paper contributes three components: (1) the UNIM dataset of 31K curated instances across 30 domains and 7 modalities, constructed from public datasets, social media, and open resources with GPT-5-mini-assisted expansion and two-phase manual quality control; (2) the UNIM Evaluation Suite, which scores responses along Semantic Correctness & Generation Quality (SQCS, combining LLM-as-a-Judge semantic alignment with modality-specific no-reference quality), Response Structure Integrity (Strict and Lenient Structure Scores checking modality type/quantity adherence), and Interleaved Coherence (Holistic Coherence + Stylistic Harmony via LLM-as-a-Judge), with a Supporting Rate modifier to account for modality coverage gaps; and (3) UNIMA, an agentic baseline built on three coordinated modules—a Receiving Module that converts non-text inputs into Task-Conditioned Dense Captions (TCDC), a Traceable Evidence Reasoning (TER) Module that performs structured generation-verification-backtracking cycles to produce a logically consistent final report, and a Generating Module that synthesizes the interleaved multimodal output guided by that report.

## Results

On the UNIM benchmark, UNIMA achieves SQCS of 57.3% (Natural Science), 72.7% (Social Science), and 62.2% (General Area), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MIO (15.9–21.4%) and AnyGPT (11.1–17.9%). On Interleaved Coherence (ICS), UNIMA reaches 69.1–73.8% versus 21.4–60.0% for baselines. On Response Structure Integrity, UNIMA's StS/LeS scores (50.8–84.3%) are 2–6× higher than AnyGPT and 15–40× higher than NExT-GPT and MIO, which mostly score below 5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNIMA dominates all three baselines (AnyGPT, NExT-GPT, MIO) across every evaluation dimension and field. On SQCS, UNIMA (57.3–72.7%) outperforms the best baseline MIO (15.9–21.4%) by roughly 3–4×, while NExT-GPT lags furthest at 4.4–13.3%. On StS/LeS, the gap is even more dramatic: UNIMA scores 50.8–84.3% versus AnyGPT's 12.5–27.8% and NExT-GPT/MIO's 1.2–5.2%, representing 15–40× improvements. On ICS, UNIMA (69.1–73.8%) exceeds MIO (51.6–60.0%) by 10–22 points and AnyGPT (32.1–43.6%) by 26–42 points. Notably, all baselines show uniformly low scores regardless of difficulty level, indicating that even the easiest UNIM tasks exceed their compositional multimodal alignment capacity, whereas UNIMA exhibits a clear performance gradient consistent with increasing difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interleaved multimodal benchmarking line (ITLVD-BENCH, MMIE, CoMM, ISGBench, OpenING) by generalizing from the text-image-only setting to 7 modalities (text, image, audio, video, document, code, 3D) and from single-capability instances to multi-task intertwined instances. It also builds conceptually on the any-to-any multimodal generation paradigm established by Unified-IO, NExT-GPT, AnyGPT, and MIO, but departs from those works by requiring models to handle arbitrarily ordered interleaved input-output sequences rather than fixed modality pairs. The agentic baseline UNIMA introduces a Traceable Evidence Reasoning module that repurposes the generate-verify-backtrack loop from structured reasoning literature into a multimodal generation pipeline, distinguishing it from the implicit chain-of-thought approaches used in prior MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNIM is the first benchmark to jointly stress-test any-to-any modality coverage, interleaved sequence structure, and multi-capability reasoning in a single evaluation, filling a clear gap between text-image interleaved benchmarks and any-to-any generation benchmarks. The finding that state-of-the-art any-to-any MLLMs (AnyGPT, NExT-GPT, MIO) score below 20% on semantic quality and below 5% on structural integrity—even at the easiest difficulty level—reveals a substantial capability gap that current architectures have not addressed. Open questions include whether the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation protocol generalizes beyond the tested models, whether the agentic UNIMA approach scales to truly open-ended generation without template constraints, and how to close the structural-integrity gap that no current model addresses adequately.
