## Problem

Existing nighttime flare removal methods operate primarily at the pixel or feature level and lack explicit semantic priors for regions heavily occluded by intense flare, causing artifacts, over-smoothing, and inconsistent reconstructions. The core gap is that no prior work encodes structured degradation semantics (light-source type, spatial location, flare pattern, occluded structure) to guide restoration of severely damaged areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262151/cropped_page0_idx8.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single flare-corrupted nighttime RGB image as input and produces a restored flare-free image in one forward pass (plus a lightweight refinement head). During training, the pipeline additionally consumes depth maps and structured text descriptions generated by Flare-VLM, while at inference only the degraded image is required. The one-step diffusion stage runs in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 2.1-base, and the final output is produced by a Uformer-based high-frequency refinement head that fuses the diffusion result with residual cues from the input.

## Method

The framework has three key components. First, Flare-VLM (a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen-2.5 7B) outputs a fixed-template degradation description (light-source type, position, flare shape/color, occluded target), which is inverted by an LLM into a clean-scene prompt and encoded as a language condition for the diffusion model. Second, Semantics-Aware Distribution Distillation (SADD) replaces the spatially uniform noise of standard VSD with mask-modulated noise—using CLIPSeg to produce a flare-region mask from the description—so that perturbation is enhanced in flare areas (γ_f ≥ 0) and attenuated in clean backgrounds (γ_b ≤ 0), yielding spatially focused, degradation-consistent gradients for the one-step student. Third, an instruction-driven synthesis pipeline uses Flare-VLM to extract light-source semantics and depth-based geometric priors from clean nighttime images, generating structured edit prompts that Step1X-Edit applies to produce physically plausible flare-corrupted training pairs. The student is trained with LPIPS reconstruction loss, a light-source-region perceptual loss, and a diffusion loss on a LoRA regularizer, all distilled from a frozen SD 2.1 teacher in a single timestep.

## Results

On the paired synthetic test set, the proposed one-step method (Ours-1s) achieves PSNR 31.844, SSIM 0.936, LPIPS 0.0527, and FID 21.636, outperforming all baselines including Uformer (FID 28.015) and OSEDiff-1s (FID 28.000). On the Flare7k++ paired test set, it reaches PSNR 28.061 and FID 34.206, again the best across all metrics. On unpaired real-world sets (Flare7k++ Real-world, DarkZurich, ExDark), the method attains the best MUSIQ (59.082, 47.986, 45.909) and CLIPIQA (0.424, 0.185, 0.366) scores. Inference time is 0.126 s per image on an RTX 3090, faster than all other diffusion-based methods (Difflare-200s requires 12.415 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every metric in both paired test sets. Against the strongest Transformer baseline Uformer, it gains +0.487 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 28.015 to 21.636 on the synthetic set, and +0.400 dB PSNR with FID 34.206 vs 37.979 on Flare7k++. Compared to the closest one-step diffusion baseline OSEDiff-1s, the improvement is substantial: +4.832 dB PSNR and FID 21.636 vs 28.000 on the synthetic set, showing that language-guided conditioning and SADD distillation are critical. Against the multi-step diffusion method Difflare-200s, the proposed method achieves better quality (FID 21.636 vs 39.783) while being ~98× faster (0.126 s vs 12.415 s). On unpaired real-world data, the method achieves the best MUSIQ and CLIPIQA on all three test sets (Flare7k++ Real-world, DarkZurich, ExDark), confirming generalization beyond synthetic training data. The only metric where it does not strictly lead is NIQE on Flare7k++ Real-world (4.125 vs Uformer's 4.124), a negligible difference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) nighttime flare removal, which has evolved from hardware solutions through CNN/Transformer architectures to diffusion-based methods like Difflare, but has consistently operated at the pixel or feature level without explicit semantic modeling of the degradation; (2) one-step diffusion distillation, building directly on OSEDiff and Variational Score Distillation (VSD), with the key departure being SADD, which spatially modulates noise injection using a flare-aware semantic mask rather than applying uniform Gaussian noise; and (3) vision-language models for image restoration, where the authors train the first flare-specific VLM (Flare-VLM, fine-tuned Qwen-2.5 7B with LoRA) to produce structured degradation descriptions, departing from generic VL models that produce scene-irrelevant captions. The instruction-driven data synthesis pipeline also departs from the dominant additive-layer synthesis paradigm (Wu et al., Zhou et al.) by conditioning an image editing model on light-source semantics and geometric depth priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-guided one-step diffusion paradigm offers a practical recipe for handling severe, localized degradations where pixel-level supervision is insufficient: a domain-specific VLM converts the degradation into parsable semantic conditions, and a semantics-aware distillation loss focuses gradient signals where they matter. The 0.126 s inference time makes this viable for real-time nighttime vision pipelines, and the downstream detection gains (mAP 0.742→0.774) demonstrate that better restoration translates to better task performance. Limitations include the inability to handle reflective/aperture-ghost flares, the continued need for a separate high-frequency refinement stage to compensate for encoder-decoder smoothing, and reliance on depth maps during data synthesis. An open question is whether this VLM-guided distillation framework generalizes to other structured degradations (e.g., rain, fog) where a small set of semantic slots can describe the corruption.
