## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods suffer from three persistent failure modes: cross-modal misalignment where textual semantics are not reliably conveyed to visual features through cross-attention, intra-class confusion when multiple same-category objects are present and fine-grained expression cues are insufficiently captured, and geometric reasoning errors caused by inaccurate aggregation of spatially relevant visual features from surrounding points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262222/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N_point points) and a natural language description (up to 256 words) as input. It produces a 6-dimensional 3D bounding box and a point-level binary segmentation mask over all N_point points. The mask is first predicted at a coarser superpoint resolution (N_super ≪ N_point) and then propagated to individual points, while the final bounding box is a 50/50 weighted blend of a token-predicted box and a box tightly enclosing the predicted mask.

## Method

EG-3DVG introduces an Expression and Geometry Aware Grounding Decoder (EGD) containing two complementary attention modules and a contrastive training objective. Position-Guided Expression Cross-Attention (PECA) injects 3D positional embeddings into text tokens via a softmax-weighted fusion before cross-attention, so that attention weights between visual and text tokens are computed in a spatially consistent manner. Geometry-Aware Masked Attention (GMA) constructs a geometric relation tensor (relative offsets and distances) between each candidate token and all visual tokens, modulates it with a text-derived global context to produce a soft attention prior, and converts it to a binary mask via a straight-through estimator, enabling each candidate to selectively aggregate only geometrically consistent visual cues. Expression-Aware Contrastive Learning (ECL) adds a contrastive loss that pulls the target object's visual token toward expression-relevant word embeddings (attribute, pronoun, relationship) while pushing it away from same-category distractor descriptions, directly addressing intra-class confusion. The full pipeline uses RoBERTa and PointNet++ as frozen/pre-trained encoders, a 3-layer cross-modal transformer encoder, superpoint aggregation via KNN pooling, and a 6-layer EGD decoder, trained with KPS, 3DREC, 3DRES, and ECL losses.

## Results

On ScanRefer (single-stage 3DREC), EG-3DVG achieves 57.13 Acc@0.25 and 50.07 Acc@0.5 overall, surpassing the previous best TSP3D by +0.68 and +3.36 respectively. In the two-stage setting, EG-3DVG reaches 58.54 Acc@0.25 and 52.36 Acc@0.5, outperforming MCLN by +1.37 and +6.83. For 3DRES on ScanRefer, EG-3DVG attains 47.28 mIoU, +2.56 over MCLN. On SR3D/NR3D, EG-3DVG leads in both 3DREC (75.2 SR3D, 59.9 NR3D Acc@0.25) and 3DRES (58.9 SR3D, 48.0 NR3D mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EG-3DVG outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every reported metric. In the single-stage 3DREC setting on ScanRefer, it surpasses the previous SOTA TSP3D [10] by +0.68 Acc@0.25 and +3.36 Acc@0.5 overall, with the largest gap in the Multiple subset (+3.36 Acc@0.5). In the two-stage setting, it beats MCLN [27] by +1.37 Acc@0.25 and +6.83 Acc@0.5, and also exceeds G3-LQ [33] by +1.64 Acc@0.25 and +6.78 Acc@0.5. For 3DRES on ScanRefer, EG-3DVG leads MCLN [27] by +1.07 Acc@0.25, +3.07 Acc@0.5, and +2.56 mIoU, and outperforms RG-SAN [35] by +8.10 Acc@0.25 and +2.62 mIoU. On SR3D/NR3D, EG-3DVG surpasses G3-LQ [33] by +2.1 Acc@0.25 on SR3D and +1.5 on NR3D for 3DREC, and beats MCLN [27] by +9.1 mIoU on SR3D and +1.9 mIoU on NR3D for 3DRES. The gains are most pronounced in the Multiple (hard) setting, confirming that the proposed ECL and PECA modules specifically target intra-class confusion and cross-modal misalignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the established 3D visual grounding (3DVG) literature that began with ScanRefer and ReferIt3D (2020) and has progressed through multi-view approaches (ViewRefer, MVTrans), detection-based pipelines (BUTD-DETR, EDA), and recent single-stage methods (G3-LQ, TSP3D, MCLN). Rather than introducing a new encoder or backbone, EG-3DVG repurposes standard components (PointNet++, RoBERTa, transformer cross-attention) within a redesigned decoder that explicitly targets three diagnosed failure modes—cross-modal misalignment, intra-class confusion, and geometric reasoning errors—each addressed by a dedicated module (PECA, ECL, GMA). The contrastive learning component draws on the broader vision-language contrastive learning tradition but applies it specifically to expression-level word tokens rather than full sentence embeddings, and the geometry-aware masked attention uses a straight-through estimator for differentiable binary masking, a technique borrowed from stochastic computation but here applied to spatial attention gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-failure-mode taxonomy (cross-modal misalignment, intra-class confusion, geometric reasoning error) provides a useful diagnostic framework for the 3DVG community, and the corresponding targeted modules are modular enough to be adopted in other grounding or retrieval pipelines. The consistent SOTA gains across both 3DREC and 3DRES on ScanRefer and SR3D/NR3D validate that addressing these failure modes jointly in a single decoder is more effective than treating them in isolation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes (e.g., ARKitScenes), whether the superpoint-based mask prediction can be replaced by more efficient point-level methods, and whether the contrastive learning component scales to scenes with many same-category distractors beyond the typical indoor setting.
