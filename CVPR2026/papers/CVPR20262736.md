## Problem

Sign-based adversarial attack optimizers (I-FGSM, MI-FGSM, PGD) suffer from non-convergence and operational instability: attack success rates paradoxically degrade as the number of iterations increases, with I-FGSM dropping from 37.1% to 15.5% between t=2 and t=20. The paper identifies the root cause as the non-decaying coordinate-wise step-size scheduling that emerges when these sign-based methods are reformulated as coordinate-wise gradient descent, violating classical convergence conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262736/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For image classification, the method takes a single RGB image with its ground-truth label and produces a perturbed adversarial image constrained to an L-infinity ball of radius ε=16/255 around the original, generated over T=10 iterations. For cross-modal retrieval, it takes an image-text pair and produces a jointly perturbed adversarial pair under L-infinity norm 8/255 with per-step size 2/255. The MDCS module is a drop-in replacement for the sign operation within any existing sign-based optimizer, requiring no architectural changes to the surrogate or target models.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating sign-based updates as coordinate-wise gradient descent, revealing that the effective per-coordinate step-size is α/|g_i|, which fluctuates without decay and diverges as gradients approach zero near a local optimum. To fix this, the paper enforces Monotonically Decreasing Coordinate-wise Stepsizes (MDCS) by maintaining a running minimum d_{t,i} = min(1/|m_{t+1,i}|, d_{t-1,i}) per coordinate, analogous to AMSGrad's max-of-V mechanism. The update becomes x_{t+1} = P_{Q,D_t^{-1}}(x_t + α_t D_t m_{t+1}) with D_t = diag(d_t), where the projection uses a Mahalanobis-like norm weighted by D_t^{-1}. Under mild assumptions (bounded L1 gradient, bounded domain diameter), the paper proves MDCS-MI achieves the optimal O(1/√T) averaging convergence rate for concave objectives. The strategy is plug-and-play: it integrates into MI-FGSM, MEF, OPS for classification and into SGA, DRA, SA-AET for multimodal retrieval, consistently improving both transferability and iteration stability.

## Results

On the NIPS2017 image classification benchmark (ε=16/255, T=10, AEs crafted for Res50), MDCS-OPS achieves the highest transferability across all seven target architectures: 99.9% (Res50), 98.9% (VGG16), 99.0% (Mob-v2), 99.1% (Inc-v3), 89.3% (ViT-B/16), 94.7% (PiT-B), and 97.9% (Visformer-S). MDCS-MI consistently outperforms MI-FGSM by 4–8 percentage points on CNN targets (e.g., 67.2% vs 59.4% on VGG16) and by 4.2 points on Visformer-S (30.8% vs 26.6%). On the Flickr30K cross-modal retrieval task, MDCS-SAAET achieves 73.18% TR R@1 and 77.53% IR R@1 when transferring from CLIP_ViT to CLIP_CNN, surpassing SA-AET by 4.09 and 3.53 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDCS-MI outperforms MI-FGSM on every target architecture except Res50 (both at 100%), with the largest gains on VGG16 (+7.8 pts) and Visformer-S (+4.2 pts). MDCS-OPS dominates OPS across all seven architectures (e.g., 99.1% vs 98.2% on Inc-v3, 97.9% vs 96.7% on Visformer-S) and also surpasses all other momentum-based baselines (VMI, GRA, MUMODIG, PGN) and input-transformation baselines (TI, DI, SIA, BSR). MDCS-MEF improves over MEF on CNN targets (e.g., 96.4% vs 94.9% on VGG16) and Visformer-S (91.0% vs 88.2%), but underperforms on ViT-B (58.7% vs 65.3%) and PiT-B (78.8% vs 81.1%), suggesting the MDCS benefit is less consistent for ViT architectures in the MEF variant. In ensemble-model attacks (Table 2), MDCS-MI improves over MI-FGSM by 3.7 pts on Inc-v4 (79.3% vs 75.6%) and 6.5 pts on IncRes-v2 (73.5% vs 67.0%), while MDCS-OPS reaches 99.9% on Inc-v4. On the Flickr30K multimodal retrieval task, MDCS-SAAET consistently outperforms SA-AET across all source-target model pairs, with the largest gain being +8.22 pts in TR R@1 when transferring from CLIP_CNN to TCL (33.40% vs 25.18%), and MDCS variants also surpass the simpler baselines (PGD, Sep-Attack, Co-Attack, SGA, DRA) by wide margins.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets sign-based adversarial optimizers (I-FGSM, MI-FGSM, PGD) as coordinate-wise gradient descent, revealing that the nominal constant step-size α actually induces a non-decaying, magnitude-dependent per-coordinate step-size that violates classical convergence conditions. The fix draws directly from the adaptive optimization lineage: AdaGrad's per-coordinate inverse-gradient-magnitude scheduling and AMSGrad's monotonically decreasing coordinate-wise step-size (MDCS) constraint, which Reddi et al. showed recovers convergence for Adam. The paper then applies this MDCS principle as a plug-and-drop replacement for the sign operation within existing state-of-the-art frameworks (MI-FGSM, MEF, OPS, SGA, DRA, SA-AET), positioning itself as a theoretical grounding and stability fix for the entire sign-based attack family rather than a new attack architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first convergence guarantee (O(1/√T)) for a sign-based momentum attack, closing a gap identified by Karimireddy et al. that sign-SGD provably fails to converge even in convex settings. Practically, MDCS is a simple, model-agnostic module that consistently improves transferability by 1–5% across CNN and ViT architectures and multimodal VLMs, while also resolving the counterintuitive instability where more iterations degrade attack success. Open questions include whether the convex-concave convergence proof extends meaningfully to the highly non-convex adversarial loss landscape, whether the γ hyperparameter (requiring grid search) undermines the 'plug-and-play' claim, and whether the stability gains persist under stronger defenses or larger perturbation budgets.
