## Problem

One-stage person search models face an inherent conflict: pedestrian detection requires shared human features to separate people from background, while person re-identification demands identity-specific discriminative representations. Existing remedies—loss re-weighting and final-embedding decoupling—only address this tension at late network stages or in the loss function, leaving early-stage feature entanglement in the shared backbone unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262747/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images: a single query person image is matched against a set of panorama gallery images (e.g., 6,978 gallery images on CUHK-SYSU, 6,112 on PRW, 19,935 on PoseTrack21). It jointly outputs pedestrian bounding boxes with confidence scores (detection) and per-identity re-ranking scores (re-ID) in a single forward pass. FSLoRA is designed as a plug-and-play adapter inserted at every layer of an existing one-stage backbone (VMamba, pretrained on ImageNet), so the input/output interface of the host framework remains unchanged.

## Method

FSLoRA inserts a lightweight adapter at each backbone layer that decomposes the LoRA forward pass into two complementary sub-modules. The Spatial-Level Module (SLM) replaces the single LoRA path with N expert LoRA paths (B_i matrices), gated by a learnable spatial router that produces per-pixel softmax masks, dynamically routing spatial features to task-relevant experts. The Frequency-Level Module (FLM) is placed between the rank-reduction matrix A and the expert matrices B; it applies a 2D FFT to the low-rank features, splits them into low-pass (cutoff 30) and high-pass (cutoff 40) components, re-weights them via a frequency-level router, and applies iFFT to return to the spatial domain. The design exploits the observation that detection benefits from low-frequency global structure while re-ID benefits from high-frequency fine-grained texture, so the FLM selectively amplifies task-relevant frequency bands and suppresses task-irrelevant noise. Because FLM operates on the already rank-reduced representation (dimension r rather than d), it adds less than 2% extra parameters while enabling full backbone-level decoupling of the two subtasks.

## Results

On CUHK-SYSU, FSLoRA with VMamba backbone achieves 96.6 mAP and 97.0 Top-1, setting a new state of the art over the previous best one-stage method (GALW: 95.6 mAP). On PRW, FSLoRA reaches 61.3 mAP and 89.5 Top-1, surpassing the strongest prior one-stage method ROI-AlignPS† (59.3 mAP) by 2.0 pp. On PoseTrack21, FSLoRA achieves 70.25 mAP and 90.10 Top-1, outperforming the next best method PretrainPS (63.62 mAP) by 6.6 pp. As a plug-and-play module, FSLoRA consistently adds ~2 pp mAP when applied to NAE, AlignPS, and ROI-AlignPS baselines on PRW.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSLoRA (VMamba backbone) outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all three datasets. On PRW, it surpasses the strongest one-stage competitor ROI-AlignPS† (59.3 mAP) by 2.0 pp and SOLIDER (59.8 mAP) by 1.5 pp, while also beating the best two-stage method OR (52.3 mAP) by 9.0 pp. On CUHK-SYSU, it edges out GALW (95.6 mAP) by 1.0 pp and PretrainPS (95.4 mAP) by 1.2 pp in mAP, and leads in Top-1 over all methods. On PoseTrack21, the margin is largest: FSLoRA (70.25 mAP) beats PretrainPS (63.62 mAP) by 6.6 pp and SeqNet (65.12 mAP) by 5.1 pp, demonstrating particular strength under occlusion and multi-person query conditions. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 1) confirm that FSLoRA adds ~2 pp mAP regardless of the underlying architecture (anchor-based NAE, anchor-free AlignPS, or hybrid ROI-AlignPS), validating its generality as a drop-in adapter for one-stage person search frameworks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes LoRA from its original role in parameter-efficient fine-tuning to serve as a structural mechanism for task-specific feature decoupling within a shared backbone. It departs from prior one-stage person search methods (NAE, SeqNet, GALW, AGWF) that address the detection–re-ID conflict only at the final embedding or loss level, instead inserting adapters at every backbone layer to achieve early-stage disentanglement. The spatial-level module draws on Mixture-of-Experts routing, while the frequency-level module introduces FFT-based filtering as a novel axis for separating task-relevant signals, a perspective not previously exploited in person search.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of FSLoRA—yielding consistent ~2 pp gains across anchor-based, anchor-free, and hybrid baselines—makes it a practical add-on for future one-stage person search systems. The frequency-domain interpretation (detection favors low-frequency structure, re-ID favors high-frequency texture) provides an intuitive and visually verifiable mechanism, though it remains an assumption that may not generalize to all multi-task vision problems. Open questions include whether the backbone-level decoupling strategy transfers to other multi-task settings (e.g., detection + segmentation, tracking + re-ID) and whether the fixed cutoff frequencies (30/40) can be made fully adaptive without additional overhead.
