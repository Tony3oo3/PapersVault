## Problem

Existing skeleton-based action understanding pipelines treat generation, classification, and detection as independent tasks with task-specific models, lacking a shared structured representation of motion. Holistic spatiotemporal embeddings entangle action semantics with viewpoint and contextual cues, resulting in poor generalization to long-tail classes, novel viewpoints, and compositional actions. The paper asks whether a single physics-inspired primitive space can serve as a transferable structural basis across all three task families.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263429/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes skeleton sequences of shape T×J×C (C=2 or 3 for 2D/3D joints) and internally produces a primitive coefficient trajectory A ∈ R^{T×K} that decomposes each frame's motion into weighted activations over a learned dictionary of K atomic primitives. Downstream, it outputs a single action class label (trimmed classification), per-frame multi-label action probabilities (untrimmed detection), or a reconstructed/composed skeleton sequence (motion transfer and compositional synthesis). The static component of A is enforced to be time-invariant by averaging along the temporal axis, separating identity/viewpoint factors from dynamic motion.

## Method

PRISM learns a shared primitive coefficient space via a two-stage procedure. In stage 1, a decomposition encoder and generative decoder are trained on multi-view synthetic data (Mixamo) with a composite objective: L1 reconstruction, cross-view static-part swap loss to disentangle motion from viewpoint/identity, physics-based penalties (bone-length consistency, joint limits, acceleration), orthogonality on the primitive dictionary P, and L1 sparsity on coefficients. In stage 2, the frozen primitive encoder is transferred to real-world perception tasks, where the motion coefficients A^motion are fused element-wise with spatially pooled contextual features from a general skeleton encoder (Temporal Feature Fusion), then passed through a temporal modeling block for classification or frame-wise detection. The key mechanism is that complex actions are represented as sparse linear combinations of a fixed set of physics-inspired primitives, enabling compositional motion synthesis by linearly adding coefficient vectors from different actions without retraining.

## Results

On the Toyota Smarthome Trimmed (TST) dataset, PRISM achieves 70.7% cross-subject, 50.1% cross-view-1, and 72.5% cross-view-2 classification accuracy, outperforming the previous best skeleton method ViA (64.5/36.1/65.2%) by up to 14.4 points on CV1. On Posetics, PRISM reaches 52.3% Top-1 accuracy, surpassing the prior best (ViA, 48.0%) by 4.3 points. For frame-wise multi-label detection, PRISM attains 40.1% mAP on TSU cross-subject and 28.1% on TSU cross-view, exceeding LAC (36.8%/23.1%) by 3.3 and 5.0 points respectively, and 26.8% mAP on Charades. On NTU-RGB+D 120, PRISM achieves 89.8% CS and 91.2% CSet, marginally outperforming SkateFormer and DS-GCN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM consistently outperforms all skeleton-based baselines across every evaluation protocol. On TST cross-view-1, the largest gap is observed: PRISM (50.1%) surpasses the previous best skeleton method NPL (39.6%) by 10.5 points and ViA (36.1%) by 14.0 points, indicating that the primitive-centric representation is particularly effective under viewpoint shift. On TST cross-subject, PRISM (70.7%) beats the LLM-enhanced baseline LLM-AR (67.0%) by 3.7 points and ViA (64.5%) by 6.2 points. For multi-label detection on TSU, PRISM (40.1% CS, 28.1% CV) outperforms LAC (36.8%, 23.1%) and DualDETR (34.8%), with the cross-view gap of 5.0 points being the largest among detection baselines. On the balanced NTU-RGB+D 120 benchmark, PRISM (89.8% CS, 91.2% CSet) edges out SkateFormer (89.4%, 91.0%) and DS-GCN (89.2%, 91.1%) by small margins, confirming competitive performance even without long-tail challenges. For motion transfer on Mixamo, PRISM achieves the lowest MSE (0.80) compared to LAC (0.82) and ViA (0.86), validating the physics-inspired decomposition for generative tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM carves new ground at the intersection of motion decomposition (LAC, MoDi, T2MGPT) and skeleton-based action recognition (GCNs, topology-free encoders), but departs from both in a key way: prior decomposition methods learn latent factors that are not explicitly grounded in physical structure or view-invariance and do not support direct downstream perception, while recognition methods learn holistic embeddings without structured motion decomposition. Rather than pursuing joint or end-to-end task unification (as in SymGCN or UmURL), PRISM introduces a one-directional transfer paradigm where a physics-inspired primitive coefficient space is learned via a generation objective on multi-view synthetic data and then frozen and transferred to perception tasks through lightweight heads. The cross-view static/motion swap training and orthogonality-constrained dictionary learning are the principal architectural departures from prior autoencoder-based motion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent gap between motion generation and perception by showing that a structured, view-invariant primitive space can serve as a shared foundation for both, with the most striking gains appearing on rare long-tail classes (e.g., +70% on 'Usetablet') where holistic embeddings struggle. Open questions include whether the fixed-size primitive dictionary scales to substantially larger action vocabularies, whether the one-directional transfer (freeze-then-finetune) is truly superior to joint training at larger scale, and how well the representation generalizes to entirely unseen action categories beyond the training distribution. The framework's reliance on multi-view synthetic data for pretraining also raises practical questions about domain gap when synthetic and real-world motion distributions diverge significantly.
