## Problem

Existing video anomaly detection methods either train on limited, scenario-specific anomaly datasets (restricting generalization to unseen anomaly types and environments) or leverage MLLMs in a training-free fashion but are confined to frame- or clip-level text outputs without spatial localization. The paper tackles three intertwined gaps: the scarcity and narrow diversity of real VAD training data, the context-dependent semantics of anomalies that vary across scenarios, and the spatiotemporal sparsity of anomalous regions that inflates computational cost and dilutes detection signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a video clip (or single image) together with a free-form text list of anomaly categories to detect; no fixed taxonomy is required. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a per-frame anomaly score (derived from SAM2's object-presence logits) and a per-pixel anomaly mask for each frame. During training, the input is a video/image from a standard semantic segmentation dataset paired with a randomly assembled category set (genuine + irrelevant categories sampled from other samples) and a binary normal/anomalous label, so no real VAD data is ever used.

## Method

LAVIDA is an end-to-end zero-shot VAD pipeline built around an MLLM (following LISA's <SEG> token for semantic feature extraction), a CLIP text encoder, a vision backbone, a reverse-attention token compression module, and a SAM2-initialized mask decoder. The Anomaly Exposure Sampler converts off-the-shelf segmentation datasets into pseudo-anomaly training data by injecting irrelevant categories from other samples and stochastically labeling clips as normal or anomalous, eliminating any dependency on real VAD data. A density-based reverse-attention mechanism identifies background tokens via KNN local density and compresses them into a compact representation that highlights anomaly-dissimilar regions, reducing both token count and GPU memory (to ~54% at 0.2 compression ratio) while preserving detection accuracy. A Multi-Scale Semantic Projector fuses the MLLM's video-level semantic feature with frame-level cross-attention features (between category embeddings and vision features) through a two-way Q-Former-like transformer with learnable queries, producing frame-specific latent vectors. These latents serve as sparse prompts to the SAM2 mask decoder, which jointly outputs pixel-level anomaly masks and frame-level anomaly scores, trained with a combined MLLM text-generation loss and SAM2 segmentation loss.

## Results

Under zero-shot evaluation, LAVIDA achieves 76.45% AUC on UBnormal, 85.28% AUC on ShanghaiTech, 82.18% AUC on UCF-Crime, and 90.62% AP on XD-Violence, surpassing all prior zero-shot and unsupervised methods. On the pixel-level UCSD Ped2 benchmark, LAVIDA reaches 87.68% AUC, a 12.57% absolute improvement over the previous SOTA (TAO at 75.11%), and outperforms even fully supervised methods such as DRAEM (69.58%). Notably, LAVIDA's XD-Violence AP of 90.62% exceeds the best weakly-supervised method (PI-VAD, 85.37%), while its UCF-Crime AUC of 82.18% remains below the top weakly-supervised baselines (PI-VAD 90.33%, VadCLIP 88.02%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAVIDA outperforms all unsupervised baselines across every reported dataset: it leads MULDE (the strongest unsupervised method) by 3.65 points on UBnormal (76.45 vs. 72.8), 3.98 on ShanghaiTech (85.28 vs. 81.3), and 3.68 on UCF-Crime (82.18 vs. 78.5). Among zero-shot competitors, LAVIDA surpasses AnyAnomaly by 1.95 points on UBnormal (76.45 vs. 74.5) and 5.58 on ShanghaiTech (85.28 vs. 79.7), and beats EventVAD by 0.15 on UCF-Crime (82.18 vs. 82.03) and a substantial 26.58 on XD-Violence (90.62 vs. 64.04). On XD-Violence, LAVIDA also exceeds the best weakly-supervised method PI-VAD (90.62 vs. 85.37 AP), though it trails PI-VAD (90.33) and VadCLIP (88.02) on UCF-Crime, which the authors attribute to MLLM limitations on low-resolution video. At the pixel level on UCSD Ped2, LAVIDA's 87.68% AUC surpasses the best finetuned method TAO (75.11%) by 12.57 points and the best supervised method DRAEM (69.58%) by 18.1 points, establishing a new zero-shot pixel-level SOTA.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAVIDA builds on LISA's <SEG> token mechanism for extracting semantic features from MLLMs and adapts the reverse attention mechanism from semantic segmentation into a training-free token compression module for video. It departs from prior MLLM-based VAD methods (LAVAD, AnyAnomaly, EventVAD) that rely on frame-wise or clip-wise textual scoring by instead using the MLLM as a differentiable semantic feature extractor within an end-to-end trainable pipeline coupled with SAM2 for pixel-level decoding. The Anomaly Exposure Sampler represents a conceptual departure from all prior VAD training paradigms by repurposing standard segmentation datasets as pseudo-anomaly training data, eliminating any dependency on real VAD annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train a VAD model entirely on non-VAD segmentation data and achieve competitive zero-shot performance on four unseen benchmarks is practically significant for safety-critical deployments where anomaly data is scarce or unavailable. The 12.57% pixel-level AUC improvement over prior SOTA on UCSD Ped2 demonstrates that MLLM-guided semantic features can substantially improve spatial localization. However, the method still trails weakly-supervised approaches on low-resolution UCF-Crime (82.18% vs. 87–90%), and the reliance on a large MLLM for inference raises scalability questions for real-time surveillance. Open questions include whether the pseudo-anomaly training strategy generalizes to longer videos, whether the token compression ratio can be pushed further without degradation, and how the approach performs when anomaly categories are entirely absent from the MLLM's training distribution.
