## Problem

In lifelong multi-view clustering, views arrive as a continuous stream, and existing streaming methods (LSVC, DSVC) rely on consistency alignment or knowledge distillation to reconcile new and old knowledge. However, these strategies cannot fundamentally prevent knowledge degradation, because new-view updates inevitably interfere with the learned representation space, causing cumulative errors that degrade clustering quality over time. No prior method provides a principled guarantee that newly incorporated knowledge will not overwrite or distort previously acquired representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view feature matrices sequentially as a stream, where each view v has N samples with its own feature dimension D^v (e.g., HSB, RGB, SIFT, GIST descriptors or deep features from different networks), and the number of clusters K is specified in advance. Views are processed one at a time with 200 training epochs each, and a persistent knowledge space accumulates prototype vectors across the stream. The output consists of cluster assignments for all samples and unified d-dimensional latent representations, with the final clustering performed on null-space-projected and concatenated features from all views seen so far.

## Method

ALMC's core mechanism is a null-space projection that restricts each new-view prototype update to directions orthogonal to the subspace spanned by all previously stored prototypes, guaranteeing zero interference with historical knowledge (proven via Lemma 2: p^T q^⊥ = 0 for all p in the old subspace). Architecturally, each incoming view is encoded by a view-specific autoencoder, then refined through a cross-attention module that jointly updates prototypes and features. Prototypes are fused into the knowledge space via an EMA-weighted combination (Eq. 12), and a theoretical analysis (Eq. 13–14) shows the old subspace direction is preserved exactly while new information accumulates along orthogonal null-space directions. Training minimizes a reconstruction loss L_r (autoencoder fidelity) plus a knowledge-guided loss L_g (softmax similarity between features and stored prototypes), with the first view using its own prototypes as guidance and subsequent views using the accumulated knowledge-space prototypes.

## Results

ALMC achieves the best performance on 5 out of 6 benchmark datasets across ACC, NMI, and ARI. On ALOI-10, ALMC reaches 90.90% ACC, 87.32% NMI, and 83.37% ARI, outperforming the next-best ICMVC (87.38% ACC) and DSVC (85.43% ACC) by 3.5 and 5.5 percentage points respectively. On the larger ALOI-100 dataset (100 categories), ALMC attains 82.62% ACC and 89.74% NMI, surpassing DSVC (80.53% ACC, 89.01% NMI). On NUS-WIDE, ALMC is second-best, with MAGA slightly ahead on all three metrics (25.86 vs. 25.37 ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALMC outperforms all 10 baselines on Oxford4, ALOI-10, OutdoorScene, ALOI-100, and Aloideep3v across all three metrics (ACC, NMI, ARI). The most notable margin is on ALOI-10, where ALMC's 90.90% ACC exceeds ICMVC (87.38%) by 3.52 points and DSVC (85.43%) by 5.47 points. Among the two streaming-view baselines, DSVC (ICML'25) is the strongest competitor, achieving 80.53% ACC on ALOI-100 and 86.28% on Aloideep3v, but ALMC still leads by 2.09 and 1.90 points respectively. On NUS-WIDE, ALMC is the only dataset where it does not rank first: MAGA (IF'24) edges ahead with 25.86% ACC vs. ALMC's 25.37%, though ALMC remains competitive within 0.5 points. The static DMVC methods (ProImp, DCP, APADC, CPSPAN, MCMVC) consistently underperform the streaming methods, with DCP and MCMVC scoring below 60% ACC on ALOI-10, highlighting the advantage of the lifelong streaming paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the streaming-view clustering line initiated by LSVC (rule-based latent knowledge updates) and DSVC (distribution-consistency alignment across views) by identifying and formally addressing the knowledge-degradation problem that both methods leave unresolved. Rather than relying on consistency alignment or knowledge distillation to reconcile new and old views, ALMC repurposes the classical null-space projection from linear algebra as a hard geometric constraint: each incoming prototype is projected onto the orthogonal complement of the stored knowledge subspace, guaranteeing zero interference by construction. The cross-attention prototype refinement and EMA-based fusion are standard deep-learning components, but their combination with the null-space constraint constitutes the paper's architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The null-space anti-degradation mechanism offers a mathematically guaranteed (Lemma 2) alternative to the soft alignment strategies that dominate lifelong learning, which is valuable for settings where catastrophic forgetting of early views is unacceptable (e.g., medical monitoring, surveillance). However, the approach assumes the knowledge space is well-approximated by a linear subspace, and as the number of accumulated views grows the null space shrinks, potentially limiting the capacity for genuinely novel information. Open questions include how the method behaves when the number of views approaches or exceeds the feature dimension, whether the linear-subspace assumption holds for deep non-linear representations, and whether the orthogonal-update constraint ever becomes too restrictive to allow beneficial refinement of earlier knowledge.
