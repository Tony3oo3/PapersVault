## Problem

Aligning natural language with precise 3D human poses remains difficult due to the absence of effective multi-scale pose representations and the scarcity of large-scale, semantically diverse pose-language datasets. Existing approaches either rely on small, narrow datasets (PoseScript's 100k pairs) that limit contrastive pretraining, or route through LLMs without explicit pose-text alignment in a shared embedding space, leading to fragmented representations and poor generalization.

## Input / Output

The pose encoder consumes 3D keypoint coordinates as individual tokens (supporting arbitrary skeletal configurations, unlike PoseScript's fixed-length flattened vector), while the text encoder processes natural language descriptions through a fine-tuned BERT backbone augmented with 8 additional transformer blocks. The model produces a 512-dimensional pose embedding and a corresponding text embedding in a shared contrastive space; for downstream generation and editing, lightweight adapters are attached to the frozen encoders to produce generated or edited 3D poses conditioned on text.

## Method

CLEP's core is HierFormer, a hierarchical transformer encoder that decomposes the body into joint, limb, and body levels, applying 1D convolutions within semantic joint groups followed by transformer blocks at each level to capture local-to-global structure. A Cross-Scale Attention Fusion (CSAF) mechanism then integrates multi-scale features in two stages: first, Scale Attention uses each scale as query against the concatenation of other scales as keys/values with gated residual connections; second, a multi-scale gated pooling adaptively weights the global vectors from each scale into the final 512-dim pose representation. Training uses the SigLIP pairwise binary classification loss rather than InfoNCE, eliminating batch-level normalization and improving stability. The framework is pretrained on CLEP-2M (2M pose-text pairs extracted from MotionX++ via a rigid-alignment-error-based keyframe selection strategy and GPT-4o captioning), then adapted to downstream tasks by freezing both encoders and training lightweight adapters.

## Results

On finetuned bidirectional retrieval, CLEP achieves 83.4 mRecall on PoseScript-A (vs. PoseScript's 72.8), 51.4 on PoseScript-H (vs. 40.9), and 75.69 on CLEP-2M (vs. 64.98), consistently outperforming the PoseScript baseline by 10–11 points. In the zero-shot setting (no downstream fine-tuning), CLEP reaches 34.8 mRecall on the human-annotated PoseScript-H split, a nearly 6× improvement over PoseScript's 5.9. For pose generation, CLEP attains the best FID (0.06) and mRecall (56.5), surpassing PoseScript (53.2) and ChatHuman (53.4). On pose editing, CLEP reduces MPJE from 201 to 195 and MPVE from 167 to 162 relative to PoseFix while matching its FID of 0.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PoseScript (the primary baseline), CLEP wins on every retrieval benchmark: +10.6 mRecall on PoseScript-A, +10.5 on PoseScript-H, and +10.7 on CLEP-2M in the finetuned setting, and a dramatic +28.9 mRecall (34.8 vs. 5.9) in the zero-shot setting on PoseScript-H. For pose generation, CLEP outperforms both PoseScript (mRecall 56.5 vs. 53.2) and ChatHuman (56.5 vs. 53.4), while ChatPose lags significantly at 33.4 mRecall; CLEP also achieves the lowest FID (0.06 vs. 0.07 for PoseScript). On pose editing, CLEP beats PoseFix on MPJE (195 vs. 201), MPVE (162 vs. 167), and joint/vertex ELBO, though it trails slightly on rotation ELBO (0.90 vs. 0.93) and geodesic distance (8.79 vs. 8.70). Overall, CLEP dominates or matches all four baselines across retrieval, generation, and editing, with the largest margins in zero-shot retrieval and generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEP extends the pose-language alignment paradigm established by PoseScript (Delmas et al., 2024), which first demonstrated contrastive learning between SMPLH pose parameters and captions. The key architectural departure is replacing PoseScript's flattened joint-coordinate vector with HierFormer, a hierarchical transformer that encodes joints, limbs, and body regions as separate tokens fused via Cross-Scale Attention Fusion, and substituting InfoNCE with the SigLIP pairwise loss borrowed from vision-language pretraining. Conceptually, CLEP also positions itself against LLM-mediated approaches (ChatPose, ChatHuman, UniPose) that route pose understanding through language models, arguing that explicit alignment in a shared embedding space avoids the noise and cumulative errors of tool-augmented LLM pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLEP-2M (2M pose-text pairs, 20× PoseScript) provides a much-needed large-scale foundation for pose-language research, analogous to how LAION enabled vision-language pretraining, and the hierarchical encoder offers a reusable architectural pattern for multi-scale skeletal representation. The strong zero-shot transfer (34.8 vs. 5.9 mRecall on PoseScript-H) suggests that scale plus structured encoding can substitute for task-specific supervision, which is encouraging for data-scarce pose applications. However, the dataset relies on GPT-4o-generated captions and heuristic frame sampling, and the model handles only static poses without temporal modeling—both limitations that constrain its applicability to dynamic motion understanding and may introduce systematic annotation biases.
