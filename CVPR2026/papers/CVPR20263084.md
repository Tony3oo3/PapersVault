## Problem

Diffusion-based 3D point cloud anomaly detection suffers from two structural weaknesses: randomly sampled Gaussian noise deviates from local surface normals, producing crossed denoising trajectories that blur reconstruction contours, and existing methods apply a single uniform shape embedding to both normal and anomalous regions, which simultaneously under-guides fine-grained normal details and misdirects anomaly correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 16,384-point uniformly sampled point cloud (with k-NN clustering at k=16) and a normal reference point cloud retrieved from the training set via Chamfer Distance over 5,832 rotation candidates (10° steps on each axis within ±90°). It outputs a reconstructed point cloud and a point-level anomaly score computed as the average-pooled, normalized L2 discrepancy between the reconstruction and both the input and the normal reference, yielding a per-point response map for localization.

## Method

AARD introduces a two-stage sequential noise rectification before denoising: first, an orthogonal decomposition splits each Gaussian noise vector into a normal-aligned component (amplified) and a tangential component (attenuated), constrained within a Geometry Recognizable Boundary of σ_max=0.5; second, a vertex-graph rectification averages noise directions within k=16-vertex clusters to enforce local trajectory coherence. During denoising, a triplet encoder-decoder transformer processes the noisy input, a normal reference, and an input reference through separate self-attention branches, then fuses them via an Anomaly-Aware Attention module that computes an adaptive mask from geometric deviation and attention-map divergence, routing normal-reference features to anomalous regions for structural correction and input-reference features to normal regions for detail preservation. Training is self-supervised with synthetically injected anomalies, optimizing a diffusion noise-prediction loss conditioned on both reference encodings.

## Results

On Real3D-AD, AARD achieves the best object-level O-AUROC of 0.820 and pixel-level P-AUROC of 0.860, outperforming the strongest baselines PASDF (0.802 O-AUROC) and MC4AD (0.786 O-AUROC / 0.837 P-AUROC). On Anomaly-ShapeNet, AARD attains the highest O-AUROC of 0.931, surpassing MC4AD (0.912) and Simple3D (0.860). The method also achieves the best Mean Rank (1.789) on Real3D-AD O-AUROC, indicating consistent top performance across all 12 categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AARD outperforms all 12 baselines on the Real3D-AD O-AUROC average (0.820 vs. PASDF 0.802, MC4AD 0.786, PO3AD 0.765) and P-AUROC average (0.860 vs. MC4AD 0.837, ISMP 0.836). On Anomaly-ShapeNet O-AUROC, AARD leads with 0.931, beating MC4AD (0.912) by 1.9 points and Simple3D (0.860) by 7.1 points. The closest competitor, PASDF, achieves a higher average O-AUROC on Real3D-AD (0.802) but with high variance across categories (e.g., 0.628 on Airplane, 1.000 on Seahorse), whereas AARD maintains more consistent performance (range 0.759–0.917). Against the diffusion-based R3D-AD (ECCV'24), AARD improves O-AUROC by 8.6 points on Real3D-AD (0.820 vs. 0.734) and 16.4 points on Anomaly-ShapeNet (0.931 vs. 0.767). The feature-embedding baselines (BTF, PatchCore, Reg3D-AD) trail significantly, with P-AUROC averages of 0.571–0.705 on Real3D-AD, confirming the advantage of the reconstruction-based diffusion paradigm with geometry-aligned noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

AARD extends the diffusion-based 3D anomaly detection paradigm established by R3D-AD (ECCV 2025) and MC3D-AD (2025), which introduced anomaly synthesis and geometry-normal variation for reconstruction-guided detection. The paper departs from these methods by identifying two specific failure modes—geometry-violating noise trajectories and uniform (undistinguished) reference guidance—and addressing them through a sequential noise rectification pipeline (orthogonal decomposition along surface normals followed by vertex-graph averaging) and an anomaly-aware transformer that adaptively routes normal-reference vs. input-reference features. It also situates itself relative to memory-bank retrieval methods (Reg3D-AD, Shape-guided) and classical feature-based approaches (BTF, PatchCore), positioning diffusion reconstruction as the dominant paradigm while arguing that prior diffusion methods underutilize geometric structure during noise injection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometry-aligned noise rectification offers a principled way to reduce trajectory crossing in 3D diffusion reconstruction, which could benefit other point-cloud generation tasks beyond anomaly detection. However, the approach is tightly coupled to point cloud representations with explicit vertex normals and k-NN neighborhoods, leaving open whether it transfers to mesh, SDF, or voxel-based diffusion. The anomaly-aware attention mechanism, while effective, relies on a retrieved normal reference via exhaustive rotation search (5,832 candidates), which may not scale to open-world or multi-category industrial settings without a learned retrieval component. Broader questions remain about whether the geometric prior (surface-normal alignment) is sufficient for highly non-smooth or topologically complex defects.
