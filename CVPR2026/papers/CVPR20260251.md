## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for diffusion transformers apply a single prediction strategy uniformly across all feature dimensions, implicitly assuming the entire high-dimensional feature space evolves smoothly over timesteps. This paper reveals that DiT features actually contain a low-rank principal subspace with smooth, predictable dynamics and an orthogonal residual subspace with volatile, low-energy oscillations that resist accurate forecasting, making a one-size-fits-all predictor both unnecessary and error-prone.

## Input / Output

The method operates on intermediate hidden feature matrices (N×D) from selected DiT blocks during iterative denoising, taking a text prompt as the generation input and producing a final image (FLUX) or video (HunyuanVideo). A key operational detail is that a one-time offline SVD on a single reference prompt establishes a reusable low-rank basis (right singular vectors and singular values), so at inference time the method only projects new features onto this cached basis via a matrix multiplication rather than recomputing SVD, keeping per-step overhead minimal.

## Method

SVD-Cache decomposes each DiT block's feature matrix via SVD into a rank-k principal subspace (selected by a cumulative energy threshold τ) and an orthogonal residual. The paper discovers that singular values and right singular matrices are nearly invariant across different prompts, enabling a 'One-Time SVD' offline where the basis is cached once and reused for all inputs; the left singular matrix is cheaply reconstructed per prompt via projection. At inference, the smooth principal subspace is forecast using exponential moving average (EMA, β=0.9), while the oscillatory residual is directly reused across timesteps to prevent error amplification. The final cached feature is reconstructed as the sum of the EMA-predicted principal component and the reused residual, yielding near-lossless quality at 5.55× speedup on FLUX and HunyuanVideo.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (text-to-image, DrawBench), SVD-Cache achieves 1.0123 ImageReward and 32.983 CLIP Score at 4.16× FLOPs reduction (N=5), and 1.0057 ImageReward / 33.027 CLIP at 5.00× reduction (N=6), both exceeding the unaccelerated baseline (0.9898 / 32.404). On HunyuanVideo (text-to-video, VBench), SVD-Cache reaches 80.60 VBench at 5.00× FLOPs reduction (N=5), nearly matching the original 80.66. Under combined acceleration (distillation + caching), SVD-Cache achieves up to 29.01× end-to-end speedup on FLUX.1-schnell (NFE=4, N=3) with 0.9463 ImageReward, and 10.73× with sparse attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SVD-Cache consistently outperforms all seven baselines across comparable acceleration levels. At 4.16× FLOPs reduction, SVD-Cache (N=5) achieves 1.0123 ImageReward, surpassing TaylorSeer (N=5) by +0.0355 and FoCa (N=6) by +0.0410, while FORA (N=4) at the same FLOPs budget drops to 0.9730. Under more aggressive compression (5.00–5.55×), the gap widens dramatically: SVD-Cache (N=6) scores 1.0057 versus TaylorSeer (N=7) at 0.9698, DuCa (N=9) at 0.8382, and ToCa (N=10) at 0.7155. On HunyuanVideo, SVD-Cache (N=5) attains 80.60 VBench, beating TaylorSeer (N=5) by +0.67 and Speca by +0.62, while maintaining near-original quality (80.66). The advantage is most pronounced at higher compression ratios, where full-space predictors like ToCa and TeaCache suffer severe quality collapse (−27.7% and −15.4% ImageReward respectively) while SVD-Cache remains within 1.3% of baseline. SVD-Cache also dominates on accelerated variants: on FLUX.1-schnell (NFE=4), it achieves 29.01× speedup with 0.9463 ImageReward, outperforming TaylorSeer (N=2) at 16.34× / 0.9012 and TeaCache (l=0.6) at 20.49× / 0.8921.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVD-Cache extends the feature caching lineage (DeepCache, FORA, ToCa, TaylorSeer, FoCa) by departing from the implicit assumption that all feature dimensions share uniform temporal dynamics. Prior caching methods apply a single predictor—copy, polynomial extrapolation, or ODE-based forecasting—across the entire high-dimensional feature space. SVD-Cache instead decomposes features via SVD into a smooth low-rank principal subspace (forecast with EMA) and an oscillatory residual subspace (reused directly), and leverages the empirically discovered input invariance of singular values and right singular vectors to perform the decomposition once offline per model. This positions the work as a subspace-aware refinement of the cache-then-forecast paradigm rather than a new caching paradigm itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a training-free, plug-and-play method delivering 5.55× speedup on FLUX and HunyuanVideo with near-lossless quality, and up to 29× when stacked with distillation, quantization, or sparse attention. The input-invariance property of the SVD basis is a useful structural insight that could inform other low-rank or subspace-based analyses of diffusion features. Open questions include whether the invariance degrades for out-of-distribution or adversarial prompts (the paper notes residual instability on nonsensical inputs), whether the energy threshold τ requires per-model tuning, and whether the approach extends to architectures where the feature geometry is less low-rank.
