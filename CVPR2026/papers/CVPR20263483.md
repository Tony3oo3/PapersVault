## Problem

Language-guided 3D segmentation from RGB-D data remains fragile under the sparse and occluded views typical of real-world capture, where partial observations produce ambiguous, entangled feature embeddings that collapse semantic reasoning. Existing methods further assume a fixed, closed-set category vocabulary, so they cannot discover novel or partially invisible object classes that fall outside the training label space. The paper tackles both limitations simultaneously: robust segmentation under missing views and open-vocabulary class discovery without manual supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of partial RGB-D frames (with 10–40% of viewpoints randomly removed to simulate occlusion) together with a free-form natural-language prompt specifying the target object. It produces a per-Gaussian-primitive semantic segmentation mask over the reconstructed 3D scene, along with automatically discovered novel class labels for objects that are partially or fully unobserved. Evaluation is performed at the object level on indoor scenes (LERF-Mask, LERF-OVS) using mIoU, mBIoU, and mAcc, with segmentation quality assessed both in dense-view and partial-view protocols.

## Method

The framework builds a semantically continuous, differentiable embedding field on top of 3D Gaussian Splatting, where each Gaussian primitive carries a learnable semantic feature vector that is alpha-composited into a rendered semantic map analogous to color rendering. A Dirichlet Process Gaussian Mixture Model is applied over these features to flag low-density regions as novel-class candidates, with each candidate validated by a positive ELBO gain before a new mixture component is instantiated. A Gradient Low-Rank mechanism then projects the semantic feature gradients into a compact subspace via periodic truncated SVD, enforcing that features evolve within a low-dimensional manifold and provably reducing the stable rank of the gradient exponentially during training. Finally, a Contrastive Graph Semantic Loss (CGSL) constructs a pairwise similarity graph over all Gaussian embeddings and penalizes mismatches between geometric distances and graph affinities, with an L1 sparsity term to sharpen cluster boundaries; missing affinities for invisible classes are estimated via KNN. The total training objective jointly optimizes cross-entropy on visible regions and CGSL on invisible regions, enabling end-to-end segmentation and open-vocabulary class discovery from partial observations.

## Results

On the LERF-Mask benchmark (dense-view), LangRef3DGS achieves 84.9 mIoU and 79.1 mBIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline OpenSplat3D (84.0 mIoU, 78.8 mBIoU). On the open-vocabulary LERF-OVS benchmark, it reaches 60.69 mIoU and 82.41 mAcc, surpassing OpenSplat3D (59.70 mIoU, 81.79 mAcc). Under 20% partial-view conditions, the method maintains 79.6 mIoU / 74.9 mBIoU on LERF-Mask and 57.3 mIoU / 78.6 mAcc on LERF-OVS, demonstrating substantially larger relative gains over baselines when observations are incomplete.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangRef3DGS outperforms all ten baselines on both benchmarks. On LERF-Mask (dense-view), it leads OpenSplat3D by 0.9 mIoU (84.9 vs. 84.0) and ILGS by 4.4 mIoU (84.9 vs. 80.5), with a much larger margin over earlier methods like LangSplat (57.6) and LERF (37.2). On LERF-OVS, the gap over the closest competitor OpenSplat3D is 0.99 mIoU and 0.62 mAcc, while the advantage over OpenInsGaussian is a substantial 18.07 mIoU (60.69 vs. 42.62). Under 20% partial-view conditions, the relative advantage widens considerably: LangRef3DGS achieves 57.3 mIoU on LERF-OVS versus 38.5 for OpenInsGaussian and 34.3 for OpenGaussian, and 79.6 mIoU on LERF-Mask versus 76.3 for ILGS and 76.8 for CGC. The largest absolute gains over baselines appear in the partial-view setting, confirming that the DP and Gradient Low-Rank components are most beneficial when observations are incomplete.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based language-guided segmentation line (LERF, LangSplat, OpenGaussian, OpenSplat3D) by introducing two mechanisms absent from prior 3DGS segmentation work: a Dirichlet Process for nonparametric, adaptive discovery of novel object categories, and a Gradient Low-Rank projection (inspired by GaLore) to compress semantic feature evolution into a low-dimensional subspace. It further departs from the dense multi-view assumption common in prior 3DGS segmentation by explicitly targeting partial RGB-D observations, where sparse or occluded views leave regions unexplained by existing semantic clusters. The Contrastive Graph Semantic Loss for 'invisible class' detection is a new formulation that unifies supervised visible-region segmentation with unsupervised graph-structured clustering of DP-triggered candidates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The partial-observation robustness is practically relevant for real-world RGB-D capture where camera coverage is inherently limited, and the DP-based open-vocabulary discovery offers a principled alternative to fixed-class classifiers that misassign unseen regions. However, evaluation is confined to small indoor scenes (LERF-Mask, LERF-OVS) with only four sub-scenes, limiting confidence in scalability to larger or outdoor environments. Open questions include whether the DP mechanism generalizes beyond a handful of novel categories, how the method behaves under extreme occlusion (>40% missing views), and whether the low-rank gradient constraint remains beneficial at larger scene scales with more Gaussian primitives.
