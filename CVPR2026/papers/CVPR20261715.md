## Problem

Existing document generation methods are limited to single document categories, rely on representations that are either non-editable (raster images) or structurally fragile (flat coordinate sequences requiring extensive geometric computation), and are constrained by small, manually curated datasets. No prior work unifies broad category coverage, an editable hierarchical representation, and scalable data at the same time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261715/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts one of three condition types—natural-language design intention, a rendered document screenshot, or a shuffled set of extracted text/image elements—along with explicit width and height constraints, and produces a complete HTML/CSS document. The generated code uses modern CSS layout modules (flexbox, grid) with properties like row-gap, justify-content, and flex-basis rather than absolute pixel coordinates, and image placeholders follow a standardized format (picsum.photos/W/H URLs with descriptive alt text) that is later replaced with synthesized assets. All outputs remain fully editable source code, not raster pixels.

## Method

AnyDoc first builds DocHTML (265,206 samples across 111 categories and 32 styles) via an automated pipeline: InternVL3 annotates seed documents with intentions and descriptions, Qwen3-Coder 480B generates HTML/CSS code conditioned on that metadata, FLUX.1-dev synthesizes image assets from predicted dimensions and alt-text prompts, and Playwright renders final screenshots, with strict cleaning rules removing malformed or overflowing samples. A Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model is then fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 32) on DocHTML for three tasks (intention-to-document, document derendering, element-to-document). To fix residual content overflow, a Height-Aware Reinforcement Learning (HARL) stage built on GRPO renders each generated document with Playwright, computes the height ratio ρ = ĥ/h, and assigns a piecewise reward that gives full credit within a tolerance band γ, linearly penalizes both overflow (ρ > 1) and underflow (ρ < 1−γ), and is optimized with full-parameter tuning over the 20,000 most challenging training samples.

## Results

On the intention-to-document (I2D) task, AnyDoc achieves the best scores on Layout (8.64), Image (8.92), Typography (8.36), Content (8.44), and Height (0.005, lower is better), outperforming GPT-4o (8.59/8.75/8.32/8.41/0.047) and all specialized baselines. On the document derendering (DD) task, AnyDoc leads on Block (0.965), Text (0.986), Position (0.910), and Color (0.958), surpassing GPT-4o and specialized screenshot-to-code models. On the element-to-document (E2D) task, AnyDoc scores 8.62/8.73/8.39/8.34/7.36 on the five MLLM-based metrics, exceeding GPT-4o (8.09/8.38/7.90/7.79/6.87) by a clear margin across all dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o, AnyDoc wins on Layout, Image, Typography, Content, Overlap, and Height in the I2D task, while GPT-4o retains a marginal edge on Innovation (7.27 vs 7.21), Intention (8.96 vs 8.95), and Alignment (0.0020 vs 0.0053). Against InternVL3-78B, AnyDoc dominates on every metric, with particularly large gaps in Typography (8.36 vs 7.26) and Height (0.005 vs 0.323). The specialized baselines are clearly outperformed: OpenCOLE and FLUX.1-dev trail by 0.5–3.3 points on MLLM scores and lack editability, while in the DD task AnyDoc surpasses WebSight and Design2Code on Block, Text, Position, and Color (e.g., Position 0.910 vs 0.755 and 0.732). In the E2D task, AnyDoc outperforms GPT-4o by 0.5–0.53 points on all five MLLM metrics and reduces Height from 0.212 to 0.085, while LaDeCo (the prior element-to-design method) lags by over 1 point on Layout and Typography and suffers severe overlap (0.7150 vs 0.0494).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in document generation: raster-image diffusion models (OpenCOLE, FLUX.1-dev, DesignDiffusion) that sacrifice editability, and flat coordinate-sequence approaches (LaDeCo, Graphic Design with LMM) that struggle with structural complexity. Its key conceptual move is adopting HTML/CSS as a unified hierarchical representation, leveraging CSS layout modules (flexbox, grid) to encode containment and spacing declaratively rather than through explicit coordinate arithmetic. On the training side, it builds on GRPO for post-training but introduces a task-specific height-aware reward, and on the data side it repurposes off-the-shelf code-generation (Qwen3-Coder) and image-generation (FLUX.1-dev) models into an automated synthesis pipeline, a strategy not previously applied at this scale for document datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 265K-sample DocHTML dataset with rich multi-modal metadata (intentions, source code, images, screenshots) addresses a genuine data bottleneck in document generation and could serve as a shared resource for the community. The HTML/CSS framing is practically attractive because it preserves full editability while providing structural expressiveness that flat sequences lack, potentially lowering the barrier for downstream editing workflows. Open questions include whether the 7B model's height-control limitations (notably in the derendering task) resolve at scale, whether the synthesized data generalizes to truly novel categories beyond the 111 covered, and whether HARL's height-based reward can be extended to other layout constraints (width, spacing, alignment) without reward-hacking.
