## Problem

Existing training-free AI-generated image detectors based on autoencoder reconstruction error suffer from a background bias: images with simple textures or backgrounds yield low reconstruction error regardless of whether they are real or AI-generated, causing false positives. Moreover, these pixel-space methods fail to generalize to non-LDM generative models such as GANs, and no prior work jointly leverages both pixel-level and latent-level reconstruction signals in a training-free setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (any resolution, resized to 256×256 for LSUN-Bedroom evaluation) and produces a scalar RDD score that is thresholded to classify the image as real or AI-generated. Internally, the image is encoded by a pre-trained LDM autoencoder, and multiple augmented versions (low-pass filtered, 90°-rotated, and their combination) are processed through the same autoencoder and diffusion noise-prediction network. No training data, labels, or knowledge of the specific generative model that produced the image is required.

## Method

The framework identifies two instance-specific biases—background simplicity at the image level and latent ℓ₂-norm at the latent level—and cancels them via augmentation-based normalization. At the image level, the reconstruction error f_AE(x) is debiased by subtracting the reconstruction error of a low-pass-filtered and 90°-rotated version, yielding S_image(x) = f_AE(x) − f_AE(F(x)) − λ_R·f_AE(R(x)) + λ_R·f_AE(F(R(x))). At the latent level, the diffusion noise-prediction error is debiased by normalizing against the rotated latent, yielding S_latent(z) = f_latent(R(z)) − f_latent(z). The two debiased scores are unified multiplicatively as S_RDD(x) = S_image(x) × S_latent(E(x))², with the squared power matching the LPIPS-based image score's quadratic structure. The key insight is that the augmentations preserve the confounding bias factor while differentially distorting forensic cues: AIGIs show larger reconstruction degradation under rotation and filtering than real images, making the normalized score discriminative.

## Results

On the GenImage benchmark (LDM-based T2I models vs. ImageNet), RDD achieves a mean AUROC of 0.981, outperforming the best training-free baseline WaRPAD (0.946) and even the training-based AIDE (0.969). On the LSUN-Bedroom benchmark (GAN and image-diffusion models vs. LSUN), RDD achieves 0.940 mean AUROC, again the best among all methods including training-based FatFormer (0.921). RDD achieves the best or second-best AUROC in 16 out of 18 individual model benchmarks across both datasets, with perfect or near-perfect scores (≥0.999) on SD1.4, SD1.5, and Wukong in GenImage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDD outperforms all training-free baselines on both benchmarks: on GenImage it leads WaRPAD (0.946) by 3.5 points and AEROBLADE (0.932) by 4.9 points in mean AUROC; on LSUN-Bedroom it leads WaRPAD (0.934) by 0.6 points and Manifold Bias (0.920) by 2.0 points. Notably, RDD also surpasses the training-based AIDE (0.969) on GenImage, demonstrating that the debiasing strategy compensates for the lack of training data. AEROBLADE collapses on LSUN-Bedroom (0.476 mean) because its AE-reconstruction signal is unavailable for GAN-based generators, while Manifold Bias struggles specifically on LDM-generated images (e.g., 0.663 on SD1.4). The two components of RDD are complementary: S_image excels on GenImage (0.969) but fails on LSUN-Bedroom (0.464), whereas S_latent shows the opposite pattern (0.667 vs. 0.963), and their product S_RDD achieves the best of both (0.981 / 0.940). For the LDM attribution task, S_image achieves 1.000 AUROC on 6 of 9 model-pair tasks, matching or exceeding LatentTracer while being 50× faster (0.292 s vs. 14.65 s per sample).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AEROBLADE line of work, which uses autoencoder reconstruction error from latent diffusion models as a training-free AIGI detection score, by identifying a specific failure mode (background bias) and proposing a general debiasing framework that normalizes reconstruction error against augmented inputs. It departs from prior training-free methods (RIGID, MINDER, Manifold Bias, WaRPAD) that rely on perturbation sensitivity or CLIP-space similarity by instead operating directly on the LDM's reconstruction and denoising objectives at both pixel and latent levels. The latent-level reconstruction error component is, to the authors' knowledge, the first use of the diffusion model's noise-prediction loss as a training-free detection signal, complementing the image-level AE score to cover non-LDM generators like GANs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free AIGI detection is practically critical because generative models evolve faster than labeled detection datasets can be curated, and the debiasing framework offers a general recipe (identify instance-specific bias → design augmentation that preserves bias but distorts forensic signal → normalize) that could transfer to other reconstruction-based scores. The complementary behavior of image-level and latent-level scores—where each excels on different generator families—suggests that no single reconstruction signal suffices, motivating future work on richer multi-level scoring. A key limitation is computational cost: the method requires multiple AE forward passes and diffusion model evaluations per image, and the specific augmentations (90° rotation, Gaussian low-pass) may not be optimal for all image domains or future architectures.
