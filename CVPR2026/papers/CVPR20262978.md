## Problem

In-the-wild monocular driving videos (dashcams, internet footage, phone recordings) offer massive scale and long-tail scenario coverage but are sensorially and geometrically incompatible with the structured, multi-modal sensor suites (multi-view cameras + LiDAR) that autonomous driving systems require for training and validation. This 'embodiment gap' is compounded by the complete absence of paired (dashcam, AV log) training data, making the conversion an unpaired domain translation task that classical methods cannot handle due to the vast geometric and modal mismatch.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single uncalibrated monocular video stream with arbitrary intrinsics and extrinsics, injected as a conditional 9th view (with a binary mask marking it as noise-free) alongside raymaps encoding per-pixel ray origins and directions. It produces a synchronized AV log of 8 surrounding camera views plus a LiDAR point cloud represented natively as a range-view spin image tensor [H_L, W_L, 4] with channels for range (clamped at 150 m), intensity, elongation, and validity. Video output is generated auto-regressively, where each timestep conditions on the current dashcam frame and the model's own previous-step generations.

## Method

The approach has two stages: a synthetic data-pairing pipeline that reconstructs 4D scenes from existing AV logs via dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (with ray-tracing-based rendering for fish-eye optics) and renders novel dashcam-style views with augmented intrinsics/extrinsics sampled from real-world distributions, yielding perfectly paired (synthetic dashcam, real AV log) training data; and a conditional multi-modal diffusion model built on CAT3D with separate VAE+U-Net branches for images and LiDAR. A cross-sensor attention module flattens image and LiDAR features into a unified token sequence for joint self-attention at every U-Net block, enforcing cross-modal consistency, while view concatenation (vs. channel concatenation) conditions the 8 target views on the dashcam as a 9th view. The LiDAR VAE jointly encodes depth, intensity, and elongation using L1 and LPIPS losses, and the LiDAR U-Net mirrors the image U-Net's channel dimensions to enable direct feature fusion. For video, DAgger-based iterative fine-tuning augments training contexts with the model's own imperfect rollouts to mitigate auto-regressive drift.

## Results

On the Fixed-Camera-to-AV multi-view image generation task, Sensor2Sensor achieves an FID of 6.47, PSNR of 19.06, SSIM of 0.539, and LPIPS of 0.316, outperforming all baselines. For video generation, it attains the best FVD of 278.12 (vs. 293.73 for the channel-concatenation ablation and >2000 for reconstruction-based methods). For LiDAR generation, Sensor2Sensor reduces Chamfer Distance to 8.68, a 13.37% improvement over X-Drive (10.02). In human evaluation on in-the-wild data, Sensor2Sensor is top-ranked in 83–85% of image cases and 58–68% of LiDAR cases across dashcam and internet video sources.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sensor2Sensor decisively outperforms both reconstruction-based baselines (VGGT and π³) across all metrics: FID drops from ~250 to 6.47, PSNR rises from ~14.8 to 19.06, and LPIPS falls from ~0.47 to 0.316, reflecting that feedforward 3D reconstruction cannot produce coherent novel views without a generative prior. Against the generative baseline X-Drive [51], Sensor2Sensor improves FID from 8.30 to 6.47 (22% reduction) and LPIPS from 0.345 to 0.316, while also achieving higher PSNR (19.06 vs. 18.61) and SSIM (0.539 vs. 0.536). For LiDAR, Sensor2Sensor reduces Chamfer Distance by 13.37% over X-Drive (8.68 vs. 10.02). In the in-the-wild human evaluation, Sensor2Sensor is preferred over X-Drive in over 94% of image cases and 85% of LiDAR cases, and over its own channel-concatenation ablation in 85–86% of image cases, confirming that view-concatenation conditioning and joint multi-sensor generation are both critical. The reconstruction baselines are also excluded from the video comparison (Table 2) because they produce large empty regions in non-front views, further underscoring the gap between reconstructive and generative approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sensor2Sensor extends the conditional diffusion paradigm for multi-sensor generation (CAT3D for multi-view images, X-Drive for image-LiDAR co-generation) from the intra-embodiment setting to the more challenging cross-embodiment task of converting a single uncalibrated monocular video into a full multi-sensor AV log. A key conceptual departure is the repurposing of 4D Gaussian Splatting—not as a generation tool but as a geometric oracle for synthesizing paired training data (synthetic dashcam views rendered from real AV logs), thereby transforming an unpaired domain-translation problem into a fully supervised one. The auto-regressive video extension with DAgger fine-tuning follows the standard imitation-learning recipe for mitigating compounding error in sequential generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in AV validation: the scarcity of long-tail, diverse driving data that is both physically plausible and sensorially compatible with target platforms. By unlocking vast in-the-wild video sources (dashcams, internet footage, phone recordings), Sensor2Sensor offers a scalable path to augmenting proprietary AV fleets. However, the evaluation relies on a small, non-public paired dataset (1,000 sequences) and qualitative in-the-wild tests, leaving open questions about robustness across geographies, sensor configurations, and sequence lengths. The 4DGS-based data-pairing pipeline is a reusable technique that could benefit other cross-embodiment generation tasks beyond driving.
