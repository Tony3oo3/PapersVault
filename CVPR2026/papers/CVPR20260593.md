## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning pipelines—chain-of-thought, ReAct-style agents, and program-of-thought systems—commit to single-valued intermediates, govern compute via fixed heuristics, and calibrate only the final answer, leaving stepwise reliability and evidence grounding unaddressed. Early perceptual errors (a wrong OCR string, a missed chart value) propagate silently through the reasoning chain, producing confident but unsupported outputs. The paper asks whether every intermediate perception and logic step can carry a distribution-free reliability certificate, and whether those certificates can drive adaptive compute allocation rather than serving as passive uncertainty scores.

## Input / Output

The system takes a sequence of M images (document pages, charts, or screenshots) at 896² resolution together with a natural-language query. The MLLM planner autoregressively emits a small DSL program (CALL_TOOL, FUSE, RETURN) that is parsed into a directed acyclic graph of tool invocations and in-model fusion nodes. The output is a final answer (short text, numeric value, or categorical label) accompanied by a calibrated conformal set Γ_δ^(answer) with guaranteed marginal coverage ≥ 1−δ; intermediate nodes likewise emit bounded set-valued outputs (up to 5 OCR strings, 3 bounding boxes, or numeric intervals) rather than single guesses.

## Method

PoP casts multimodal reasoning as execution of a DAG in which every node—OCR, detection, chart parsing, or logic fusion—is equipped with a learned nonconformity score and a split-conformal threshold, yielding a calibrated set-valued output with a marginal coverage guarantee under exchangeability. A lightweight controller (a small policy network) observes each node's certificate state (set size, threshold, node type) plus a global compute budget and selects among ACCEPT, RETRY (higher-fidelity re-run), EXPAND (add child tool nodes), or ABORT, turning per-node uncertainty into an active compute policy. Training combines a task loss, a sequence-level planning loss, a margin-based certificate loss that pushes true-output scores below the conformal threshold, and a policy-gradient controller loss with a budget regularizer. A self-play loop periodically freezes the student, generates perturbed inputs (font swaps, clutter, affine warps, panel shuffles), and appends the resulting hard node-level pairs to calibration pools so that thresholds reflect realistic failure modes. The key architectural insight is that conformal prediction is applied inside the reasoning graph at every node rather than only at the final answer, and the resulting certificates directly govern where and when additional tool calls are spent.

## Results

PoP achieves the best answer quality across all seven benchmarks while simultaneously reducing hallucination rates by 27–45% relative to the strongest baseline (ProgVLM) and using less compute. On DocVQA-T1, PoP reaches 78.6 EM (vs. 75.0 for ProgVLM) with 7.1% hallucination (vs. 11.9%) at a budget of 11.2 (vs. 13.9). On TextVQA, PoP attains 72.9 F1 (vs. 69.7) with 9.3% hallucination (vs. 15.1%). Node-wise conformal coverage stays within 90.2–91.3% of the 90% target across all node types, and at a budget of B=12 PoP matches or exceeds the best baseline at B=16, saving 25% compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoP is compared against four baselines under matched backbones and tools: Direct-MLLM (single-pass, no tools), M-CoT (multimodal chain-of-thought, no tools), MM-ReAct (tool-using agent with heuristic retries, no calibration), and ProgVLM (program-of-thought tool plans without conformal certificates). PoP wins on every metric across all datasets. Against the strongest baseline ProgVLM, PoP gains +3.6 EM on DocVQA-T1 (78.6 vs. 75.0), +3.2 F1 on TextVQA (72.9 vs. 69.7), +3.8 EM on InfographicVQA (62.7 vs. 58.9), +3.7 EM on ChartQA-H (87.8 vs. 84.1), +2.7 EM on MultiDoc2Dial (66.1 vs. 63.4), and +5.9 CIDEr on TextCaps (136.0 vs. 130.1), while cutting hallucination by roughly 40% and reducing average budget by 15–20%. Against MM-ReAct, the gap is even larger (e.g., +4.3 EM on DocVQA-T1, +3.7 F1 on TextVQA) because PoP's learned controller avoids the fixed-retry overhead that inflates MM-ReAct's budget. The non-tool baselines (Direct-MLLM, M-CoT) are consistently 5–10 points behind on accuracy and 2–3× higher in hallucination, confirming that tool use plus conformal certification is essential for reliable multimodal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends split-conformal prediction (Angelopoulos & Bates, 2021) from monolithic final-answer calibration to node-level certificates within a directed acyclic graph of multimodal reasoning operations. It departs from prior tool-using multimodal agents (M-CoT, MM-ReAct, ProgVLM) that commit to single-valued intermediates and govern compute via fixed heuristics, by replacing point predictions with calibrated sets and heuristic retries with a learned controller that allocates budget based on certificate geometry. The self-play counterexample mining loop for enriching calibration pools under distributional shift is a novel training component not present in prior conformal or agent-based multimodal work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PoP offers a principled, distribution-free mechanism for stepwise reliability in multimodal reasoning, addressing a gap where prior calibration was applied only to final answers while intermediate perceptual errors silently compounded. The 25% compute savings at matched or better accuracy (B=12 vs. B=16 baselines) makes the framework practically attractive for latency-sensitive deployments. Key open questions include whether marginal per-node guarantees can be strengthened to conditional or answer-level guarantees under composition, whether the simple additive cost model generalizes to heterogeneous hardware (GPU/CPU overlap, I/O), and how the approach scales to much larger reasoning graphs with dozens of tool types.
