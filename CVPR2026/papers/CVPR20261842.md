## Problem

Oriented object detection (OOD) typically requires costly rotated bounding box annotations, and existing cost-reduction strategies—semi-supervised methods needing partial OBB labels or weakly supervised methods using horizontal boxes or points—still incur non-trivial annotation overhead. No prior framework combines a small subset of weak annotations (horizontal boxes or single points) with abundant unlabeled data in a teacher-student setting to recover full oriented detection capability at minimal annotation cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes aerial or remote sensing RGB images where only a small fraction (10–30%) carries weak annotations—either horizontal bounding boxes (orientation stripped) or single points (both orientation and scale stripped)—while the remainder is completely unlabeled. The detector outputs oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle), class labels, and confidence scores per detected instance, evaluated via mAP on DOTA and DIOR benchmarks. The unusual aspect is that the model must infer the missing orientation and scale dimensions entirely from unlabeled data through pseudo-labeling, rather than from the annotated subset.

## Method

PWOOD adopts a teacher-student architecture (FCOS + ResNet50 + FPN) where the student is an Orientation-and-Scale-aware Student (OS-Student) that recovers orientation via symmetry learning (vertical flip and random rotation with angle-consistency loss) and recovers scale via spatial layout learning (Gaussian overlap loss using Bhattacharyya coefficients for upper bounds, and Voronoi watershed loss with Gaussian Wasserstein distance for lower bounds). The teacher generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled images, and a Class-Agnostic Pseudo-Label Filtering (CPF) module replaces static confidence thresholds with a two-component Gaussian Mixture Model fitted via EM, dynamically selecting the threshold as the argmax of the positive-component posterior. The student is trained jointly on weakly supervised loss (from the annotated subset) and unsupervised loss (from filtered pseudo-labels), while the teacher is updated via exponential moving average of student weights, creating a self-improving loop that progressively refines pseudo-label quality across training iterations.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.5 (the primary evaluation), PWOOD with partial horizontal box annotations achieves 52.87/59.36/61.58 mAP at 10%/20%/30% annotation ratios, matching or exceeding the Vanilla Baseline (49.53/58.28/61.00) trained with rotated boxes and approaching the strongest SOOD method MCL (52.61/59.63/62.63). PWOOD with partial single-point annotations reaches 35.33/41.54/43.02 mAP, substantially outperforming the point-supervised WOOD baseline Point2RBox-v2 (32.69/36.03/38.30). On DOTA-v1.0, PWOOD (HBox) achieves 65.42 mAP at 30% annotations, surpassing the Vanilla Baseline (64.97) and the HBox-only WOOD method H2RBox-v2 (58.65) by 6.77 mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PWOOD with partial HBox annotations outperforms all WOOD baselines by large margins: +10.68 mAP over H2RBox-v2 at 10% annotations on DOTA-v1.5, and +6.77 mAP over H2RBox-v2 at 30% on DOTA-v1.0. Against SOOD baselines trained with rotated boxes, PWOOD (HBox) matches or slightly exceeds the Vanilla Baseline (e.g., 61.58 vs. 61.00 at 30% on DOTA-v1.5) and is within 1.05 mAP of the strongest SOOD method MCL (61.58 vs. 62.63), despite using cheaper horizontal box annotations instead of rotated boxes. PWOOD with partial point annotations surpasses Point2RBox-v2 by 2.64–5.51 mAP across annotation ratios on DOTA-v1.5, confirming the framework's generality across annotation types. On DOTA-v2.0 (more small objects), PWOOD's advantage over the Vanilla Baseline widens to 6.26 mAP at 10% annotations, suggesting particular strength in complex scenes. The CPF filtering mechanism contributes an additional ~1.3 mAP over the best static threshold, validating the adaptive thresholding design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established paradigms in oriented object detection: fully supervised (rotated boxes), semi-supervised (partial rotated boxes + unlabeled data via teacher-student), and weakly supervised (horizontal boxes or single points). It builds directly on the teacher-student EMA framework of Tarvainen & Valpola, adopts H2RBox-v2's symmetry learning for orientation recovery, and repurposes Point2RBox-v2's spatial layout losses (Gaussian overlap, Voronoi watershed) for scale estimation. The key departure is combining a *partial* subset of weak annotations with abundant unlabeled data in a unified framework, and replacing static pseudo-label thresholds with a GMM-based dynamic filtering mechanism inspired by FreeMatch and Consistent-Teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners can replace costly rotated-box annotations with cheap horizontal boxes or single points while leveraging unlabeled data to recover comparable accuracy, directly reducing annotation budgets in aerial and remote sensing applications. The GMM-based pseudo-label filtering addresses a widely acknowledged pain point (threshold sensitivity) in semi-supervised detection and could be adopted beyond oriented detection. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to non-aerial domains, how it scales to very large unlabeled pools, and whether the substantial performance gap between HBox and Point supervision (≈18 mAP on DOTA-v1.5) can be further closed. The joint multi-format training results are promising but preliminary, leaving the optimal mixing strategy for heterogeneous annotations unresolved.
