## Problem

Non-parametric quantization methods for visual tokenization (LFQ, BSQ, FSQ) are heuristic in design, require ad hoc regularization terms like entropy penalties to prevent codebook collapse, and have not been scaled to codebooks comparable to frontier LLM vocabularies. The paper asks whether a theoretically grounded quantization scheme can simultaneously simplify training (eliminating auxiliary losses) and improve the rate-distortion tradeoff at very large codebook sizes (~200K).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263887/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes an RGB image of size H×W×3, downsamples it spatially by factor P, and maps each patch embedding to one of 196,560 fixed code vectors on the 23-sphere S²³. For autoregressive generation, the model additionally consumes a class label (ImageNet-1k) and produces a full 256×256 image via 7-level next-scale prediction. For compression experiments, 768×512 Kodak images are encoded/decoded in 256×256 tiles, yielding a bitstream whose BPP is reported alongside PSNR and MS-SSIM.

## Method

The paper unifies all existing non-parametric quantization methods under a lattice-coding formulation, then reinterprets entropy regularization as a lattice-relocation problem that seeks the densest hypersphere packing. The Leech lattice Λ₂₄'s first shell (196,560 vectors of norm √32, normalized to unit length) instantiates the codebook, yielding Spherical Leech Quantization (A₂₄-SQ) with a minimum pairwise distance of √3/2 ≈ 0.866—over 80% larger than BSQ at comparable bitrate. Because the lattice vectors are fixed and maximally dispersive, the autoencoder trains with only the standard ℓ₁-GAN-LPIPS loss trio, eliminating commitment loss and entropy penalties entirely. For autoregressive generation with this large codebook, the authors introduce factorized d-itwise prediction (24 nine-way classification heads), cut cross-entropy for memory efficiency, Z-loss to stabilize logits, and the Dion optimizer with orthonormalized updates to handle gradient explosion from the imbalanced codebook usage.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k generation, ∞-CC + Λ₂₄-SQ at 2.8B parameters achieves a gFID of 1.82, nearly matching the validation oracle (1.78) while using only 7 sampling steps versus 10 for VAR-d30. At 1B parameters, it reaches gFID 2.18 with 30% fewer steps than VAR-d24 (2.09) and higher recall (0.63 vs. 0.59). For image reconstruction on ImageNet-1k, Λ₂₄-SQ-ViT reduces rFID from 1.14 (BSQ-ViT) to 0.83 while using slightly fewer effective bits (d*=17.58 vs. d=18). On Kodak compression, Λ₂₄-SQ achieves PSNR 29.63 and MS-SSIM 0.964 at 0.275 BPP, outperforming both BSQViT (27.79 / 0.948) and JPEG2000 (29.19 / 0.930) at comparable or lower bitrate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Λ₂₄-SQ decisively outperforms BSQ [91] across all three tasks: reconstruction rFID drops from 1.14 to 0.83 on ImageNet-1k, compression PSNR rises from 27.79 to 29.63 on Kodak, and generation gFID improves from 10.3 (BSQ+CE head) to 8.7 (Λ₂₄-SQ+CE head) in the ablation. Against traditional codecs, Λ₂₄-SQ beats JPEG2000 and WebP on both PSNR and MS-SSIM at a lower BPP (0.275 vs. 0.299/0.296). In generation, the method surpasses VQGAN (gFID 5.20), RQ-TF (3.80), and LlamaGen (3.05) by large margins, and matches or exceeds VAR-d24 (2.09) at 1B params while being 30% faster (7 vs. 10 steps) with higher recall. At 2.8B, ∞-CC + Λ₂₄-SQ (gFID 1.82) beats VAR-d30 (1.92) and approaches the oracle (1.78). The Leech lattice [51] is not a competing method but the mathematical source of the codebook; its 196,560 first-shell vectors provide a δ_min of 0.866 versus BSQ's 0.471, an 80% increase in minimum pairwise distance that underpins the empirical gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies all existing non-parametric quantization methods (LFQ, FSQ, BSQ, RPQ) under a single lattice-coding formulation, then reinterprets the entropy regularization terms as a lattice relocation problem. From this geometric perspective, the densest hypersphere packing principle naturally motivates the Leech lattice (A24) as the optimal fixed codebook, yielding Spherical Leech Quantization (A24-SQ) as a principled successor to BSQ. On the generation side, the paper builds directly on the Infinity [36] and VAR [75] autoregressive frameworks, but departs from prior work by training with a ~200K codebook without the codebook-specific tricks (index subgrouping, multi-head prediction, bitwise self-correction) that those methods required.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: A24-SQ eliminates the need for entropy penalties and commitment losses in tokenizer training while improving reconstruction and compression over BSQ, and enables the first discrete visual AR model with a ~200K codebook trained without auxiliary codebook tricks, reaching near-oracle FID (1.82 vs. 1.78) on ImageNet-1k. The lattice-coding unification provides a clean theoretical lens for future quantization design beyond the Leech lattice. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at larger model scales and in text-conditioned generation, and whether other densest-packing lattices (E8, K12) offer complementary benefits at different codebook sizes.
