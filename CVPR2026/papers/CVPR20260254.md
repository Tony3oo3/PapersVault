## Problem

Existing all-in-one image restoration foundation models rely on static or random data mixture proportions across tasks, leading to imbalanced multi-task performance, yet the relationship between mixture ratios and restoration quality remains unexplored. Additionally, diffusion-based restoration methods apply a uniform generative prior regardless of the distinct degradation forms and levels present in different tasks, constraining reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260254/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts degraded RGB images of arbitrary resolution (including >1080P inputs) and produces a single restored image per task. For super-resolution inputs, LQ images are randomly upsampled via nearest-neighbor, bilinear, or bicubic interpolation to align spatial dimensions with the output. LLaVA-generated text descriptions of each training image are fed through cross-attention as auxiliary conditioning. Inference uses 20 Euler sampling steps with a fixed classifier-free guidance scale of 5, and AdaIN color-fixing is applied only for the SR task.

## Method

FoundIR-v2 builds on an SDXL latent diffusion backbone with a VAE encoder/decoder and introduces two jointly optimized scheduling mechanisms. First, a Data Equilibrium Scheduling (DES) paradigm re-evaluates task-wise performance on a small reference set every T iterations and reweights sampling probabilities via a softmax-based rule that increases allocation to underperforming tasks and decreases it for well-learned ones. Second, an MoE-driven diffusion scheduler sits between the LQ feature and the noisy HQ latent, using a soft-gated router over n experts (each a distinct attention type: spatial, channel, or sparse) to adaptively allocate task-specific diffusion priors. Training follows a two-stage protocol: the scheduler is pre-trained alone, then the VAE encoder, scheduler, and diffusion model are jointly fine-tuned with an L1 reconstruction loss over 150k iterations on a single 96 GB GPU.

## Results

Across 13 benchmark datasets covering deblurring, dehazing, deraining, desnowing, declouding, low-light enhancement, and super-resolution, FoundIR-v2 achieves the best or second-best performance in over 80% of quantitative comparisons (5 metrics per task). Headline gains include Dense-HAZE dehazing (PSNR 15.29 vs. 12.33 for the prior best AutoDIR), HQ-NightRain deraining (PSNR 18.57 vs. 16.29 for AgenticIR), and FoundIR-L+N joint denoising/enhancement (PSNR 19.72 vs. 16.12 for FoundIR). On perceptual metrics, FoundIR-v2 leads by large margins, e.g., MUSIQ 57.48 on Dense-HAZE (vs. 31.97 for AutoDIR) and MUSIQ 61.33 on FoundIR-L+N (vs. 50.86 for FoundIR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest all-in-one baseline FoundIR [26], FoundIR-v2 wins on PSNR for Dense-HAZE (+2.96 dB), HQ-NightRain (+7.00 dB), FoundIR-L (+0.74 dB), and FoundIR-L+N (+3.60 dB), while occasionally trailing on SSIM (e.g., 4KRD: 0.7997 vs. 0.8188). Versus PromptIR [40] and DiffUIR [64], FoundIR-v2 dominates across nearly all tasks and metrics, with the largest gaps on dehazing and deraining. Against AutoDIR [19], FoundIR-v2 leads on PSNR and perceptual scores for most tasks but concedes SSIM on UHD-LL (0.8484 vs. 0.8572) and PSNR (20.08 vs. 22.52). InstructIR [13] is competitive on SSIM for some tasks (e.g., NH-HAZE: 0.4984 vs. 0.4616) but FoundIR-v2 surpasses it on PSNR, LPIPS, and MUSIQ across the board. The agentic system AgenticIR [65] is outperformed on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and MUSIQ for deraining and dehazing, though it edges out FoundIR-v2 on CLIPIQA+ for HQ-NightRain (0.3762 vs. 0.3312). For real-world SR (RealPhoto60, RealDeg), FoundIR-v2 achieves the best or near-best perceptual scores (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA+, MANIQA) compared to dedicated SR methods including SUPIR [58], StableSR [48], and OSEDiff [50].

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FoundIR [26], which established data scaling laws for all-in-one image restoration foundation models, by shifting focus from data volume to data mixture proportions. It departs from prior all-in-one restoration methods (PromptIR, DiffUIR, FoundIR) that use static or uniform sampling ratios by introducing a dynamic data equilibrium scheduling paradigm borrowed conceptually from LLM data mixing laws [37]. Architecturally, it moves from FoundIR's image-space diffusion to an SDXL-based latent-space diffusion backbone and adds an MoE-driven scheduler to allocate task-adaptive diffusion priors, addressing the limitation that prior methods apply a single uniform generative prior regardless of degradation type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that data mixture proportions significantly affect all-in-one restoration performance—and that dynamic reweighting outperforms fixed ratios—provides actionable guidance for training multi-task restoration models. The large PSNR gains on coupled-degradation tasks (e.g., ~6 dB on Dense-HAZE, ~7 dB on HQ-NightRain over FoundIR) suggest that jointly handling multiple degradations within a single model can outperform cascaded pipelines. Open questions include whether the data equilibrium scheduling generalizes beyond the four-task ablation, whether the approach scales to substantially larger model sizes or more heterogeneous task sets, and whether the MoE scheduler's benefits persist when the base diffusion model is changed. The single-GPU training setup (one H20 96 GB) also raises questions about how the method behaves at larger compute scales.
