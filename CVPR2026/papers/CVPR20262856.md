## Problem

Existing image decomposition and inpainting methods rely on explicit mask priors and assume opaque object-level occlusion, causing them to fail on full-image semi-transparent/transparent overlays (e.g., fog, rain, lens flare, glassware, X-ray contraband) where color and transparency are nonlinearly entangled across layers. Additionally, no large-scale dataset exists for transparent/semi-transparent layer decomposition, with prior datasets suffering from pixel-level inconsistencies between ground-truth layers and their composites.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262856/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single alpha-composited RGBA image, a natural-language prompt describing the foreground, background, and their composited relationship (e.g., 'Three sub-images: a transparent glass, a dining room, the overlapped of both'), and a blending-type indicator as input. It outputs a foreground RGBA layer and a background RGB layer jointly, without requiring any explicit mask. The composited image may have been produced by various nonlinear operators (alpha blending, screen mode, additive, or X-ray-style multiplicative blending), and the model must infer the decomposition regardless of which operator was used.

## Method

DiffDecompose reformulates layer decomposition as learning the posterior distribution over plausible foreground-background pairs conditioned on the composite, rather than directly regressing alpha mattes. The architecture fine-tunes AlphaVAE on the AlphaBlend dataset for RGBA feature extraction, then uses LoRA (rank 128) to adapt the Flux DiT backbone. Two key mechanisms enable mask-free decomposition: (1) In-Context Decomposition (ICD) concatenates the clean latent tokens of the composited image z with noise tokens of x and y along the sequence dimension, performing joint bidirectional attention so that z conditions the generation of both layers simultaneously; (2) Layer Position Encoding Cloning (LPEC) injects two disjoint positional encodings (PE for foreground, PE′ for background and composite) to preserve pixel-level spatial correspondence and prevent inter-layer feature entanglement. A Multi-Modality Attention module further integrates T5-XXL text tokens, while the background is maintained in a noise-free state to retain high-frequency structural detail during iterative denoising.

## Results

On the public LOGO benchmark, DiffDecompose achieves the best RMSE across all three splits (LOGO-H: 3.23, LOGO-L: 2.17, LOGO-G: 2.79) and the lowest LPIPS (0.0092, 0.0050, 0.0063), outperforming the second-best method (Inpaint Anything) by an average margin of 36.3% in RMSE and 52.8% in LPIPS. On the proposed AlphaBlend dataset, the advantage is dramatically larger for full-image translucent occlusions: on Translucent Flare Removal, DiffDecompose achieves RMSE 16.44 versus 83.38 for Inpaint Anything (a 5× reduction), and on Translucent Occlusion Removal, RMSE 18.96 versus 83.22. The only metric where DiffDecompose is slightly behind is FID on LOGO-L (24.20 vs. 23.71 for Inpaint Anything), which the authors attribute to LOGO-L's highly structured, mask-friendly layout.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffDecompose outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three LOGO splits. The closest competitor is Inpaint Anything [62], which is the only mask-based method that handles structured occlusions reasonably well; DiffDecompose still beats it by 28–36% in RMSE and 31–38% in LPIPS on LOGO, while being entirely mask-free. SDXL-inpainting [33] and ClipAway [14] are mid-tier, with RMSE roughly 2–3× higher than DiffDecompose on LOGO-H. PowerPoint [74] and Flux-ControlNet [46] perform worst, with Flux-ControlNet particularly struggling on LOGO-G (RMSE 14.59 vs. 2.79 for DiffDecompose). On the AlphaBlend subtasks, the gap widens dramatically for full-image translucent occlusions (Flare, Occlusion), where inpainting-based methods fail catastrophically (RMSE > 60) while DiffDecompose maintains RMSE below 20, confirming that mask-based approaches cannot handle layer-level, non-local transparency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant mask-based inpainting paradigm (SDXL-inpainting, Inpaint Anything, ClipAway) by reformulating layer decomposition as a mask-free probabilistic generation problem, learning the posterior over foreground-background pairs conditioned on the composite image. It builds on the DiT/Flux architecture with LoRA fine-tuning and AlphaVAE for RGBA latent encoding, while introducing In-Context Decomposition (ICD) and Layer Position Encoding Cloning (LPEC) as novel mechanisms absent from prior layer-decomposition works (MULAN, Generative Image Layer Decomposition) that target only opaque objects. The in-context learning strategy draws from recent vision in-context editing literature but is the first to apply it to multi-layer alpha-composited decomposition without per-layer supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AlphaBlend dataset fills a genuine gap as the first large-scale resource for semi-transparent/transparent layer decomposition, enabling systematic study of a problem that mask-based methods fundamentally cannot address. The probabilistic formulation is more principled for the inherently ill-posed inverse problem of disentangling color and transparency. However, the dataset is entirely synthetic with controlled blending operators, the framework is limited to two-layer decomposition, and generalization to real-world captures with unknown, complex blending physics remains untested. Open questions include extending to multi-layer stacks, handling video sequences, and validating on genuinely captured transparent scenes where the composition operator is not known a priori.
