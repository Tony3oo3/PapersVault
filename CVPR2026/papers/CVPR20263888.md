## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image editing models struggle to decouple global style from content during style transfer, producing outputs that suffer from style leakage, semantic drift, or insufficient stylization. Supervised fine-tuning alone overfits to dataset biases and fails to generalize across diverse artistic domains, while direct preference optimization without domain adaptation lacks a stable starting policy. The paper addresses the need for a principled framework that simultaneously optimizes style fidelity and content preservation with reliable, task-specific reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263888/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content image at 1024×1024 resolution and a free-form style text prompt (e.g., 'cyberpunk', 'ink-wash', 'watercolor') as input, and produces a single stylized image that applies the described style while preserving the semantic structure of the source. Notably, the style is specified purely as text rather than a reference image, which the authors identify as a limitation and future direction. All training and evaluation operates at 1024×1024 resolution on 8× NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

## Method

Style-GRPO is a two-stage pipeline built on FLUX.1 Kontext. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning (LoRA rank 128) on 300k adversarial pairs from StyleReward-Dataset to adapt the base model to the style-transfer domain. Stage 2 applies Group Relative Preference Optimization (GRPO) with an ODE-to-SDE conversion for stochastic exploration, where a frozen StyleScore reward model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B with a two-layer MLP head, trained via Bradley-Terry loss on the same adversarial pairs) provides scalar rewards jointly capturing style consistency, content preservation, and perceptual quality. Group-normalized advantages drive clipped policy updates with a KL penalty against the reference policy, and a timestep-aware exponential decay weighting prioritizes early denoising steps where global style is established. The key insight is that domain-adapted SFT provides a stable initialization that makes subsequent GRPO exploration more effective than either stage alone.

## Results

On the ImgEdit benchmark, Style-GRPO achieves 4.74 (GPT-4o) and 4.46 (Gemini-2.5-Pro), surpassing the strongest baseline FLUX.1 Kontext (4.55 / 4.29). On AnyEdit, the method attains the best CLIP_img (0.8452), lowest L1 Distance (0.0944, a 61% reduction over FLUX.1 Kontext's 0.2457), and highest DINO similarity (0.7583), while also scoring highest on the proposed StyleScore (3.91 vs. 3.77). In a blind user study with 36 participants over 50 prompts, Style-GRPO is ranked first in 87.5% of cases. The StyleScore reward model itself achieves 98.6% preference accuracy on 500 held-out pairs, far exceeding Qwen2.5-VL (65.2%) and ImageReward (48.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Style-GRPO outperforms all five image-editing/style-transfer baselines on 6 of 7 quantitative metrics. Against the closest competitor FLUX.1 Kontext, it gains +0.19 on GPT-4o ImgEdit, +0.17 on Gemini ImgEdit, and a dramatic 61% reduction in L1 Distance (0.0944 vs. 0.2457), indicating far superior content preservation. StyleBooth is the only method that beats Style-GRPO on CLIP_text (0.1986 vs. 0.1664), which the authors attribute to a deliberate trade-off favoring fine-grained visual style cues over generic text priors. DiffStyler and Omnistyle trail substantially on every metric (e.g., DiffStyler scores only 1.51 on GPT-4o ImgEdit and 0.5875 DINO), reflecting their tendency to sacrifice structural integrity for style application. In the user study, Style-GRPO is preferred as Rank 1 in 87.5% of cases versus 10.3% for FLUX.1 Kontext and ≤1.1% for all other baselines. For reward-model comparison, StyleScore (98.6% accuracy) decisively outperforms both Qwen2.5-VL (65.2%) and ImageReward (48.7%) on preference-pair classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: reference-guided style transfer (StyleBooth, Omnistyle, DiffStyler, StyleShot) and RL-based post-training for diffusion/flow-matching models (FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO, MixGRPO). It builds directly on FLUX.1 Kontext as the base editing model and adapts the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek-R1 via the ODE-to-SDE conversion strategy from FlowGRPO. The key departure from prior style transfer work is the introduction of a purpose-built adversarial dataset (StyleReward-Dataset) and a dedicated VLM reward model (StyleScore) trained specifically for the style-content decoupling trade-off, rather than relying on general-purpose aesthetic or CLIP-based rewards. The two-stage SFT-then-GRPO pipeline also departs from single-stage preference optimization approaches by first establishing a domain-adapted policy before applying RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 300k adversarial image pair dataset and the StyleScore reward model provide reusable infrastructure for the style transfer community, addressing the long-standing difficulty of jointly optimizing style fidelity and content preservation. The paper's ablation confirming that SFT before GRPO yields the best results (vs. either alone) offers practical guidance for practitioners applying RL post-training to out-of-distribution editing tasks. A key limitation is that the current formulation is text-prompt-guided only; the authors explicitly identify extending to reference-image-guided style transfer as the primary future direction, which would substantially broaden applicability. The modest margins over FLUX.1 Kontext on LLM-judged benchmarks (e.g., 4.74 vs. 4.55 GPT-4o) suggest the gains, while consistent, are incremental in absolute terms, and the user study (50 prompts, 36 participants) is relatively small for the strength of the 87.5% claim.
