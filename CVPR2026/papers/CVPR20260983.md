## Problem

Whether video foundation models pretrained solely on 2D video data develop internal representations of 3D structure and ego-motion remains unclear. Prior work either fine-tunes video generators with 3D objectives (introducing data-domain confounds) or relies on indirect proxies like depth or multi-view consistency, leaving no model-agnostic framework to directly and quantitatively compare the 3D awareness of different VidFMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260983/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes video clips and extracts frozen per-frame spatial feature maps from various VidFMs (for diffusion models, features are read from a single denoising step at a chosen timestep and network layer, using an empty text embedding and first-frame conditioning for image-to-video models). From 4 sampled frames (first frame as reference plus 3 more with ≥5-frame temporal gap), the probe outputs dense 3D point maps in the first frame's coordinate system, depth maps at a consistent scale, and relative camera poses (rotation + translation) for each frame.

## Method

The paper proposes a model-agnostic probing framework: frozen VidFM features are fed into a shallow VGGT-like transformer probe consisting of four alternating-attention blocks (frame-attention + global-attention) and three readout heads (two DPT heads for point/depth maps, one camera head), trained with confidence-weighted L2 and Huber losses. Only the probe is optimized; the VidFM backbone remains frozen. The framework is applied across frontier video generators (WAN2.1-14B, Open-Sora2.0, CogVideoX, Aether), a self-supervised encoder (V-JEPA), and control baselines (DINOv2 per-frame, Fast3R native-3D) on CO3Dv2 and DL3DV. Key findings include that temporal reasoning is critical for global 3D understanding, mid-layer features at early-but-not-first denoising timesteps are consistently optimal across all diffusion models, and replacing DINO features with frozen WAN2.1-14B features in VGGT substantially improves feedforward 3D reconstruction under limited 3D supervision.

## Results

On CO3Dv2, WAN2.1-14B achieves the best video-generator performance with Point Err 0.284, Depth Err 0.151, and AUC@30 0.736, trailing only the native 3D model Fast3R (Point 0.262, AUC@30 0.769). On the more challenging DL3DV (outside Fast3R's training distribution), WAN2.1-14B surpasses Fast3R on all metrics (Point 1.051 vs. 1.379, Depth 0.323 vs. 0.514, AUC@30 0.660 vs. 0.637). Open-Sora2.0 is the second-strongest generator (Point 0.391 / 1.306, AUC@30 0.643 / 0.607), while per-frame DINOv2 features are the weakest (Point 0.559 / 2.814, AUC@30 0.508 / 0.245). In the VidFM-VGGT experiment (Table 2), replacing DINO features with frozen WAN2.1-14B features improves Point Err from 0.476 to 0.289 on CO3Dv2 and from 2.751 to 1.034 on DL3DV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the per-frame image model DINOv2, all video models substantially outperform it on global 3D metrics (Point and AUC@30), though DINOv2 remains competitive on depth alone (e.g., 0.209 on CO3Dv2 vs. 0.151 for WAN2.1-14B). The self-supervised V-JEPA encoder improves over DINOv2 on Point (0.439 vs. 0.559) and AUC@30 (0.619 vs. 0.508) on CO3Dv2, confirming that temporal reasoning helps, but it still lags behind all video generators. Among video generators, WAN2.1-14B dominates: it beats Open-Sora2.0 by ~26% in Point Err on CO3Dv2 (0.284 vs. 0.391) and ~20% on DL3DV (1.051 vs. 1.306). The 3D-finetuned Aether improves over its base CogVideoX on DL3DV (Point 1.566 vs. 1.748) but slightly degrades on CO3Dv2 (Point 0.501 vs. 0.485), showing that 3D fine-tuning helps in-domain but can hurt generalization. On CO3Dv2 (part of Fast3R's training data), Fast3R edges out WAN2.1-14B (Point 0.262 vs. 0.284, AUC@30 0.769 vs. 0.736), but on DL3DV (out-of-distribution for Fast3R), WAN2.1-14B clearly wins (Point 1.051 vs. 1.379, AUC@30 0.660 vs. 0.637). Finally, in the VidFM-VGGT comparison (Table 2), frozen WAN2.1-14B features outperform DINO-based VGGT by large margins on both datasets (e.g., DL3DV Point 1.034 vs. 2.751, AUC@30 0.686 vs. 0.363), indicating video features are superior for feedforward 3D under limited 3D supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of 3D-awareness probing (Probe3D, Feat2GS) from image models to video foundation models, replacing 2.5D proxies (depth, normals, multi-view consistency) with direct dense 3D prediction (point maps, camera poses, depth) via a shallow VGGT-style transformer. It departs from the dominant 'fine-tune video generators on 3D data' paradigm by asking whether 3D structure is already encoded in frozen video features without any 3D supervision, and positions its model-agnostic probe as a more direct evaluation than prior per-scene optimization or generation-based consistency checks. The feedforward 3D reconstruction task itself builds on DUSt3R/VGGT/Fast3R, but the paper's contribution is the evaluation framework and the empirical study of which video model features best support it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that frontier video generators (WAN2.1-14B, Open-Sora2.0) trained purely on 2D video data match or exceed dedicated 3D experts (Fast3R) on out-of-domain scenes challenges the assumption that large-scale 3D annotation is necessary for strong 3D understanding, and suggests video data may be a viable scaling pathway for 3D foundation models. The practical implication—that frozen VidFM features outperform DINO features in VGGT under limited 3D supervision—offers an immediately actionable recipe for 3D reconstruction practitioners. Key open questions remain: the authors cannot isolate the effect of training-data scale (no open checkpoints vary only in data), 3D fine-tuning may hurt generalization beyond its training domain, and scaling to large-scale 3D reconstruction from VidFM features is left as future work.
