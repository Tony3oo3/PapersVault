## Problem

Existing MLLM safety defenses focus on detecting and refusing explicitly harmful inputs, but fail at the harder problem of contextual safety: distinguishing subtle contextual differences between scenarios that appear similar yet diverge in safety intent (e.g., a knife in a kitchen vs. a threatening context). Current benchmarks also overlook the safety-utility trade-off, contain low-fidelity samples, and rely on coarse binary metrics that conflate well-reasoned refusals with blanket rejections.

## Input / Output

At each inference step, the model receives an RGB image paired with a text query, along with top-k retrieved safety insights from a growing memory bank (encoded via CLIP ViT-L/14 concatenating text and visual embeddings). The model produces a text response—either a contextually grounded refusal with constructive alternatives or a helpful answer—and simultaneously generates a distilled self-reflective safety insight that is stored with its context embedding for future retrieval. The memory bank is initialized empty per safety category and expands sequentially without re-initialization.

## Method

EchoSafe is a training-free, inference-time framework that maintains a self-reflective memory bank to accumulate contextual safety knowledge across interactions. Rather than storing raw query-response pairs, the model performs self-reflection after each response to distill a generalizable safety insight (a higher-level principle abstracting the specific interaction). Each insight is paired with a context embedding formed by concatenating CLIP text and visual encoder outputs, and the memory grows via simple append. For a new query, the system retrieves the top-k most similar past insights via cosine similarity and injects them into the prompt as additional safety guidance, creating a closed-loop where the model's safety reasoning evolves over time. This mechanism enables cross-domain transfer of safety knowledge and achieves state-of-the-art contextual correctness with only 1.33× inference time overhead.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench++ (GEN mode), EchoSafe achieves an average Contextual Correctness Rate (CCR) of 87.9% on Qwen-2.5-VL, substantially outperforming the best baseline AdaShield (71.1%) by 16.8 percentage points, while also delivering the highest average Quality Score (3.48 vs. 1.73 for AdaShield). On the standard MM-SafetyBench, EchoSafe reduces the Attack Success Rate (ASR) of Qwen-2.5-VL from 22.72% to 0.04% under the SD attack and from 25.05% to 0.02% under the TYPO attack. On MSSBench-Chat, EchoSafe yields an 18.75% average improvement over the base model, and on SIUO it gains 27.04% on the Safe (S) metric. Crucially, EchoSafe preserves general utility, achieving nearly lossless scores on MME, MMBench, ScienceQA, and TextVQA compared to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoSafe consistently outperforms all three training-free baselines (FigStep, ECSO, AdaShield) across all three MLLMs on MM-SafetyBench++. On Qwen-2.5-VL, EchoSafe achieves 87.9% average CCR versus 71.1% for AdaShield (the strongest baseline), 61.0% for FigStep, and 41.9% for ECSO — a 16.8-point margin over the best competitor. AdaShield, while achieving the highest refusal rates on unsafe inputs, suffers severe over-defense, collapsing safe-query answer rates (e.g., 12.4% on LLaVA-NeXT-7B) and yielding the lowest quality scores among baselines. FigStep and ECSO show limited effectiveness on the unsafe subset, with refusal rates far below 100%, indicating they fail to prevent harmful responses on the more challenging MM-SafetyBench++ images. On the standard MM-SafetyBench, EchoSafe achieves near-zero ASR (0.04% SD, 0.02% TYPO on Qwen-2.5-VL), matching or exceeding AdaShield's 0.09%/0.00% while preserving far higher helpfulness. On comprehensive benchmarks (MME, MMBench, ScienceQA, TextVQA), EchoSafe maintains within 1–2 points of the base model, whereas AdaShield causes dramatic drops (e.g., MME perception score falls from 1688 to 586 on Qwen-2.5-VL), confirming that EchoSafe uniquely balances safety and utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant jailbreak-defense paradigm (fine-tuning, adversarial training, prompt engineering, input filtering) by reframing MLLM safety as a *contextual* problem: distinguishing subtle intent differences between near-identical scenarios rather than binary safe/unsafe classification. Methodologically, EchoSafe repurposes retrieval-augmented generation and test-time adaptation ideas—originally developed for general QA and continual learning—into a safety-specific self-reflective memory loop, drawing an explicit analogy to human schema formation. The benchmark builds directly on MM-SafetyBench's attack taxonomy but introduces paired safe-unsafe samples with minimal-intent-flip rewrites, a design absent from prior multi-modal safety evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The contextual-safety framing addresses a real deployment gap: over-defensive models that refuse benign queries erode user trust, yet existing benchmarks reward exactly that behavior. MM-SafetyBench++ with its CCR metric and paired design gives the field a more balanced evaluation tool likely to be adopted as a standard. Open questions include whether the unbounded memory bank scales to long-running deployments, whether self-reflection quality degrades when the base model is weak (as seen with LLaVA-1.5-7B), and whether the approach transfers to proprietary or closed-source models where prompt-level memory injection may be restricted.
