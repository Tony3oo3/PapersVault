## Problem

Online 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems must estimate 6-DoF camera poses and reconstruct scenes simultaneously without precomputed SfM poses, yet existing approaches rely on dense photometric optimization over all pixels, making them computationally expensive and fragile under motion blur. This paper tackles the challenge of achieving efficient, robust real-time tracking and high-fidelity reconstruction under extreme high-speed motion where conventional RGB-based methods fail entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized RGB frames (30 FPS) and high-frame-rate spatial difference (SD) signals (757 FPS) from a stereo hybrid pixel sensor, or standard RGB frames with extracted first-order spatial differences as a fallback. The SD signal is a binarized first-order difference I(x) − I(x+s) with a hardware-defined shift vector s, providing sparse edge geometry at much higher temporal resolution than RGB. Outputs include per-frame 6-DoF camera poses, a sparse 3D map of elongated Gaussian edge primitives (~2k Gaussians), a dense photorealistic 3D Gaussian scene, and deblurred novel-view renderings.

## Method

The method follows a 'sketch-then-paint' two-stage pipeline: (1) sparse edge tracking, where elongated 3D Gaussian ellipsoids represent scene edges and camera pose is optimized by minimizing an L1 distance-transform-weighted alignment loss between rendered and observed SD edges, using analytic SE(3) Jacobians with a coarse-to-fine image pyramid; (2) dense RGB reconstruction, where stable poses promote keyframes and SD-guided frequency-aware initialization assigns larger Gaussians to low-frequency (far-from-edge) regions and smaller ones near edges. A key novelty is the mutually exclusive supervision for deblurring: strong-gradient pixels are supervised by hardware-consistent SD rendering (with quantizer and straight-through estimator) while complementary pixels use standard RGB photometric loss, separating structural and appearance constraints. A semi-isotropic regularization penalizes the closest pair of Gaussian scale axes to prevent degenerate view-dependent stretching while preserving one free axis for edge direction.

## Results

On the stereo-Tianmouc dataset, SDGS achieves an average ATE RMSE of 4.67 cm across slow/fast/extreme motion sequences, and is the only method that successfully tracks under extreme motion (3.91 cm), where all RGB-based baselines either fail or degrade severely. On TUM RGB-D, the method achieves 2.11 cm average ATE RMSE with only ~2k Gaussians per tracking iteration (vs. 9k–2690k for baselines), yielding 3.10–3.22 ms per iteration and up to 6.72 tracking FPS. On the SD-Replica deblurring benchmark, SD-guided mutually exclusive supervision improves PSNR from 22.51 to 24.11 dB and SSIM from 0.702 to 0.737 over MonoGS-RGBD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: Gaussian-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS (all pure 3DGS tracking), and WildGS-SLAM, SEGS-SLAM (hybrid with external pose estimators). Under slow motion on stereo-Tianmouc, SDGS (4.21 cm) is competitive with but slightly behind WildGS-SLAM (2.01 cm) and MonoGS (3.32 cm). Under fast motion, SDGS (5.89 cm) dramatically outperforms SEGS-SLAM (19.30 cm) and MonoGS (24.52 cm), while Gaussian-SLAM and SplaTAM fail entirely. Under extreme motion, SDGS (3.91 cm) is the only method that completes tracking, beating WildGS-SLAM (8.62 cm) by 2.2× and SEGS-SLAM (19.06 cm) by 4.9×. On TUM RGB-D, SDGS (2.11 cm avg) trails MonoGS (1.48 cm) by ~0.6 cm but substantially outperforms Gaussian-SLAM (3.32 cm) and SplaTAM (3.30 cm), while using 5–1000× fewer Gaussians per iteration. In the deblurring evaluation on SD-Replica, SDGS improves PSNR by 1.6 dB and SSIM by 0.035 over MonoGS-RGBD, confirming the benefit of SD-guided supervision for recovering sharp edges from blurred inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the SE(3) pose-optimization framework of Gaussian Splatting SLAM (Matsuki et al., 2024) by replacing dense photometric tracking with sparse edge alignment via distance transforms. It departs from prior online GS-SLAM methods (Gaussian-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS) that rely on dense RGB supervision for both tracking and mapping, instead introducing a two-stage 'sketch-then-paint' paradigm where elongated Gaussians encode edges for fast pose estimation and a separate dense RGB map handles appearance. The entire pipeline is co-designed around the hybrid pixel sensor's complementary pathways (Yang et al., Nature 2024), which provides synchronized high-frame-rate spatial difference signals alongside standard RGB, a sensing modality not previously exploited in Gaussian-splatting SLAM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sparse geometric cues from differential sensing can sustain robust 6-DoF tracking under extreme motion where all dense-RGB baselines fail entirely, while simultaneously achieving ~2× faster per-iteration pose optimization through a ~20× reduction in Gaussian count. The SD-guided mutually exclusive supervision for deblurring (PSNR gain of ~3.4 dB in single-view) suggests a general recipe for recovering high-frequency structure from degraded observations. However, the hardware dependency on the Tianmouc hybrid pixel sensor limits immediate reproducibility, the RGB-compatible mode shows a modest accuracy gap versus MonoGS-RGBD on TUM (2.11 vs 1.48 cm ATE), and the authors acknowledge that blur in low-texture, non-edge regions persists since those pixels receive only RGB photometric supervision. Open questions include whether the distance-transform alignment strategy generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the semi-isotropic regularization's adverse effect on smooth-object sequences (fr3/office) can be mitigated, and whether the approach scales to larger, more complex environments.
