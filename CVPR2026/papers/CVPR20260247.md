## Problem

Static benchmark-driven evaluation of T2I models relies on fixed prompt sets, offering shallow diagnostic power that reveals a model fails but cannot isolate which minimal, fundamental attributes drive the failure. This passive paradigm conflates multiple error-causing factors and cannot systematically map the full landscape of a model's weaknesses at scale, leaving targeted improvement efforts without actionable guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260247/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a target T2I model and a structured entity-attribute corpus (758 entities × 437 attributes, yielding ~7.36M candidate nodes at depth 3) as input, and optionally the model's training data distribution for attribution analysis. Each explored node is converted to a prompt, used to generate 25 images, and evaluated by Qwen2-VL-72B via multi-choice consistency checks. The output is a set of minimal error slices (entity-attribute combinations with success rate below τ=0.8), organized by tree depth to indicate fundamental vs. compound failures, plus data-scarcity attribution scores when training data is available.

## Method

FAILUREATLAS formalizes error discovery as a breadth-first tree search over entity-attribute combinations, where each node is a prompt generated by an LLM from a template and evaluated by an MLLM. Two acceleration strategies make the combinatorial space tractable: (1) rule-based path pruning exploits the monotonic difficulty assumption to skip all descendants of a failed node, reducing the third-layer search space to as little as 0.4% for weaker models; and (2) a lightweight transformer-decoder predictor, trained online every 10K nodes on T5 text embeddings of entities and attributes, estimates per-node success rates to prioritize exploration toward high-failure-likelihood regions, achieving roughly 2× speed-up in failure discovery. The search trajectory is model-dependent—different models explore different subsets of the same structured space—ensuring discovered errors correspond to the minimal concepts each specific model cannot handle.

## Results

FAILUREATLAS discovers 247,430 error slices (56.3% error density) for SD1.5 across 439,467 explored nodes, and 439,942 error slices (42.3% density) for SDXL Turbo across 1,040,415 explored nodes. Rule-based pruning reduces the third-layer search space to just 4.2% of the full tree for SD1.5, while the prediction-based prioritizer achieves roughly a 2× speed-up in failure discovery. The entity-attribute corpus achieves ~90% semantic coverage of entities and attributes across four standard T2I benchmarks (COCO Captions, T2I-CompBench, HRS-Bench, TIFA). Data-centric analysis on SD1.5/LAION-2B shows that a substantial fraction of first-layer error slices correspond to low-frequency training data, providing large-scale correlational evidence linking failures to data scarcity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against competing error-discovery methods; instead, it compares the failure landscapes of four T2I models (SD1.5, SDXL Turbo, SD3.5 Large Turbo, Flux.1-dev) to demonstrate generality. SD1.5 exhibits more fundamental failures: it fails at 21.3% of layer-1 entities versus only 12.0% for SDXL Turbo, and its aggressive pruning (exploring only 439K vs. 1.04M nodes) confirms its weaker robustness. SD3.5 and Flux.1-dev, while more capable, still show persistent failures (e.g., rubber bands, bent keys, gray camera), indicating that even state-of-the-art models retain systematic weaknesses. The acceleration strategies are validated quantitatively: rule-based pruning cuts the layer-3 search space to 4.2% for SD1.5, and the online-trained predictor (L1 loss on unseen nodes) enables ~2× faster failure discovery, meaning 10K error slices require only ~12.5K node explorations versus the full 303K available at layer 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant benchmark-driven evaluation paradigm for T2I models (GenEval, T2I-CompBench, HRS-Bench, TIFA) by reframing error discovery as an active, structured search problem rather than passive scoring on fixed prompts. It extends the error slice discovery literature from discriminative models (HiBug, DOMINO, FACTS, adaptive testing) to generative models, where the input space is combinatorially vast and each evaluation is computationally expensive. The key methodological departure is the use of a structured entity-attribute tree search with rule-based pruning and an online-trained prediction-based prioritizer, rather than post-hoc clustering or tag-then-slice approaches that are bounded by a fixed evaluation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAILUREATLAS provides the first scalable, diagnostic-first methodology for systematically mapping T2I model failures at scale, uncovering over 247K and 439K minimal error slices for SD1.5 and SDXL Turbo respectively, and offering the first large-scale correlational evidence linking these failures to training data scarcity. However, the current exploration is limited to single-entity compositions (no multi-entity or complex scene prompts), the computational cost is substantial (~11M image generations for SD1.5), and the MLLM-based evaluation introduces non-trivial error (84% alignment for attributes). Open questions include whether the data-scarcity attribution generalizes beyond SD1.5/LAION-2B, how to extend to multi-entity and human-centric scenarios, and whether the framework transfers to other generative modalities.
