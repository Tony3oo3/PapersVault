## Problem

Generating realistic 4D dynamics from a single 3D object snapshot requires modeling how the object deforms under physical forces, yet existing methods rely on category-specific physical models (MPM, spring-mass, articulation structures) and system identification, limiting them to narrow object classes and small datasets. No prior framework can handle diverse dynamic types—elastic bodies, cloth, continuum materials, multi-body systems—without explicit physical annotations or predefined structural priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static 3D mesh (n vertices, shared topology) as the geometric snapshot, along with optional physical conditions such as forces, actions, or initial velocities applied to selected particles. It outputs a temporal sequence of deformed meshes {M₁, …, M_T} with the same topology as the input, representing one physically plausible 4D trajectory. The mesh is consumed as a uniformly sampled surface point cloud for encoding, and the output is driven entirely by latent-space dynamics rather than per-vertex simulation.

## Method

NEUROK learns a data-driven kinematic state parameterization: a low-dimensional latent space Z(M₀) where any sampled vector decodes to a plausibly deformed mesh, replacing over-parameterized particle-based representations. A perceiver-based transformer encoder-decoder is trained as a conditional VAE on 4D geometric trajectories (pairs of deformed meshes) with reconstruction and KL losses, requiring no physical or action annotations. The raw latent is further compressed via the Active Subspace Method to a compact reduced-order space. At simulation time, the learned latent vectors serve as generalized coordinates in a Lagrangian mechanics formulation: a kinetic-plus-potential energy is defined over the latent space, and Euler-Lagrange equations (involving the Jacobian of the decoder and Christoffel symbols) are solved numerically to produce the latent trajectory, which is decoded frame-by-frame into the output mesh sequence.

## Results

On the primary 4D generation task (Table 2), NEUROK achieves 81.43% user-study alignment and 83.33% realism preference, dramatically outperforming the best baseline AnimateAnyMesh (5.83% / 6.67%). It also leads on all VBench and WorldScore metrics: Aesthetic Quality 0.483, Dynamic Degrees 0.750, Imaging Quality 51.100, CLIP score 0.761, and Motion Magnitude 2.343. On the inverse-kinematics sub-task (Table 1, PartNet-Mobility test set), NEUROK achieves Chamfer L1 0.028 and IoU 0.764, roughly 2.4× lower Chamfer distance and 34% higher IoU than the best baseline KeyPointDeformer (0.067 / 0.570).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEUROK wins decisively across all nine baselines on every reported metric. In the 4D generation comparison (Table 2), the end-to-end generator AnimateAnyMesh [102] is the closest competitor but trails by a wide margin: 5.83% vs. 81.43% user-study alignment and 0.889 vs. 2.343 Motion Magnitude, indicating it produces far less dynamic motion. Physics-based methods PhysDreamer [114], OmniPhysGS [69], and Pixie [49] all score below 6% alignment, confirming their inability to generalize beyond their trained material categories. In the inverse-kinematics comparison (Table 1), NEUROK (Chamfer L1 0.028, IoU 0.764) outperforms the strongest implicit-representation baseline KeyPointDeformer [38] (0.067, 0.570) by 2.4× on Chamfer and 34% on IoU; explicit articulation methods FreeArt3D [17] and SINGAPO [71] are further behind (0.169/0.354 and 0.313/0.091 respectively), while NeuralDeformationGraphs [11] and CANOR [34] fall in between. The ablation rows in Table 1 show that removing model reduction, data augmentation, or the dual-quaternion parameterization each degrades performance, confirming all three design choices contribute to the margin over baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in 4D simulative dynamics generation, which assumes a predefined category-specific physical model (MPM for elastic bodies, spring-mass for deformables, joint trees for articulated objects) and performs system identification to estimate parameters. Instead, NEUROK learns a data-driven kinematic state parameterization—a low-dimensional latent space whose decoder maps any sampled vector to a plausible deformation—thereby replacing all category-specific physical constraints with a single learned manifold. The simulation step then reuses classical Lagrangian mechanics (Euler-Lagrange equations with Christoffel symbols) but treats the learned latent vectors as generalized coordinates, a conceptual bridge between learned representations and analytical physics that prior Lagrangian neural network work [23, 30, 75] did not pursue. Architecturally, the encoder-decoder builds on perceiver-style transformers and 3DShape2Vecset point encoding, while the Active Subspace Method provides a principled dimensionality-reduction step absent from prior reduced-order simulation work, which typically trains instance-specific networks rather than an amortized, generalizable model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for any physical model, action labels, or category-specific inductive bias, NEUROK offers a single framework that handles articulated objects, cloth, continuum bodies, and heterogeneous combinations—something no prior method achieves simultaneously. The energy-conservation property (Fig. 8) and generalization to entirely unseen categories (Fig. 9) provide strong evidence that the Lagrangian formulation over learned coordinates is not merely a fitting trick but captures genuine physical structure. Open questions include how the framework scales to multi-object interactions and contact-rich scenes, whether the current mesh-based representation can be extended to unstructured or open-boundary geometries, and whether the per-frame ODE integration can be accelerated for real-time applications. The curated large-scale 4D dataset (from SAPIEN and Objaverse-XL) also sets a useful benchmark for future work in data-driven physical simulation.
