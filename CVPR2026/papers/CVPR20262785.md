## Problem

Existing digital watermarking methods are applied post-capture and are vulnerable to removal through editing or compression, while prior optical watermarking systems either require complex hardware (structured light, holographic setups) or offer only binary authenticity checks without pixel-level tamper localization. Fragile watermarking—where the signature is deliberately sensitive to any modification—remains largely unexplored in the optical domain, leaving a gap for a physically grounded, tamper-localizing authentication mechanism that is invisible in the captured image and unforgeable without the specific hardware.

## Input / Output

The system takes a scene image and captures it through a camera whose aperture contains a custom phase mask (2.835 mm × 2.835 mm, 256×256 pixel design), producing blurred sensor measurements that are then reconstructed into a high-quality protected RGB image with the NOWA embedded in its null space. For verification, the protected image is projected onto the null space of the imaging operator to yield a signature map, which a CNN detector converts into a per-pixel authenticity probability map (values in [0,1]) indicating tampered regions. Each color channel is processed independently, and the pipeline operates on standard-resolution images (trained on FFHQ, evaluated on COCO 2017 at 1,000 images).

## Method

The core mechanism exploits the null space of the imaging operator A_φ (defined by a Zernike-parameterized phase mask in the aperture plane) as a physically secure embedding channel: the NOWA lives entirely in N(A_φ), is invisible to the sensor, and is recoverable only via the orthogonal projector Π_N = I − A_φ†A_φ. A Null-Space Network performs measurement-consistent reconstruction as x_p = x̂_r + Π_N U_θ(x̂_r), guaranteeing A_φ x_p = y while learning to populate the null space with structured, discriminative features. A separate CNN detector d_ψ is trained on the null-space projection Π_N(x_p) to produce per-pixel tamper probabilities, leveraging the fact that genuine images exhibit stable null-space patterns while tampered regions violate the physical imaging constraints. The entire pipeline—optical Zernike coefficients, NSN weights, and detector weights—is jointly optimized end-to-end with a combined loss (L2 reconstruction + VGG perceptual + pixel-wise binary cross-entropy), creating a security asymmetry where neither the NSN alone nor the optical system alone suffices to forge a valid NOWA.

## Results

On the EditGuard test set (1,000 COCO 2017 images), NOWA achieves F1 scores of 0.993 (Stable Diffusion), 0.992 (ControlNet), 0.974 (RePaint), and 0.965 (Lama), with AUC consistently at or above 0.997 across all five generative editing scenarios. The best IoU reaches 0.987 under Stable Diffusion editing. Under degradation (JPEG Q=70–90, Gaussian noise σ=1–5), NOWA maintains F1 ≥ 0.885 and AUC ≥ 0.992, substantially outperforming the strongest digital baseline EditGuard (F1 drops to 0.552 at JPEG Q=70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOWA outperforms the strongest digital baseline EditGuard [68] on four of five editing scenarios: F1 improves from 0.966→0.993 (Stable Diffusion), 0.968→0.992 (ControlNet), 0.967→0.974 (RePaint), and matches at 0.965 (Lama), while losing on SDXL (0.929 vs. 0.965). Against HiFi-Net [18], NOWA gains 0.44–0.49 F1 points across all edits, with the largest gap on RePaint (0.974 vs. 0.681). The remaining baselines—MVSS-Net [9], OSN [59], PSCC-Net [30], and IML-ViT [39]—all collapse to F1 < 0.25 under generative editing, indicating that purely post-capture digital detectors cannot resist content-preserving AIGC manipulations. Under degradation (Table 2), NOWA retains F1 ≈ 0.89 at JPEG Q=70 while EditGuard drops to 0.552 and HiFi-Net to 0.315, confirming the robustness advantage of optical-level embedding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Null-Space Network (NSN) framework from Schaub et al. [51] and Schwab et al. [52], originally designed for general linear inverse problems, as the reconstruction backbone for an authentication pipeline. It departs from prior optical watermarking work (coded apertures, ptychography, structured illumination) that prioritized robustness for copyright tracing by instead designing a fragile, null-space-embedded signature optimized for pixel-level tamper localization. The hybrid optical-digital co-design follows the computational photography paradigm of jointly optimizing hardware and software, but applies it to a security task rather than image quality, and introduces a security asymmetry absent from purely digital watermarking methods like EditGuard or HiDDeN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key contribution is a hardware-dependent security asymmetry: even if the entire digital pipeline (NSN + detector) is exposed, an adversary cannot forge valid NOWA signatures without the physical phase mask, which is a guarantee no purely digital watermark can provide. This opens a path toward capture-time authentication that is structurally unforgeable, complementing rather than replacing digital watermarking. Limitations include the small fixed aperture constraining light throughput, sensitivity to optical misalignment, and evaluation under only three specific attack models; future work on randomized or key-based digital embeddings and larger-aperture null-space computation would be needed for broader deployment.
