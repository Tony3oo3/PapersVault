## Problem

Existing diffusion-based weight generation methods suffer from two limitations: (1) single-level optimization frameworks lack cross-task transferability, restricting generalizability to new tasks, and (2) they model only the global optimal weight while ignoring the rich supervision signals in intermediate local target weights along the optimization trajectory. Naively assigning these local targets as generation goals creates inconsistency between local and global objectives, degrading performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a task embedding (computed by a ResNet101 backbone from task data) and standard Gaussian noise as input, and iteratively denoises to produce task-specific neural network weights (or LoRA matrices for language model fine-tuning). The diffusion process is conditioned on the task embedding, and the denoising trajectory is structured so that local target weights—sampled uniformly from the Adam optimization trajectory at intervals of d=M/k steps—are reached at evenly spaced T/k-step intervals rather than all being treated as the final T-step target.

## Method

Mc-Di combines REPTILE bi-level meta-learning with a novel local consistency diffusion loss. In the weight preparation stage, Adam is run on downstream tasks (with SAM and data augmentation added for convergence and robustness) and the optimization trajectory is uniformly sampled to yield k local target weights. In the meta-training stage, the inner-loop trains a base learner with a local consistency loss that assigns each local target θ_{i×d} a truncated noise schedule (T/k steps instead of T), so the denoiser learns to reach each target at its corresponding interval; the outer-loop then updates the meta-learner via REPTILE. The key insight is that this decomposes a single T-step denoising problem into k shorter sub-problems, improving both convergence speed and final accuracy. Theoretically, the paper proves the denoiser recovers the full target sequence from Gaussian noise under this loss, and shows the cumulative empirical error is bounded linearly in the reconstruction error, with SAM reducing the Hessian eigenvalue bound to tighten convergence.

## Results

On few-shot learning (5-way 1-shot), Mc-Di achieves 97.34% on Omniglot, 64.87% on Mini-ImageNet, and 70.45% on Tiered-ImageNet, outperforming the second-best method by an average of 2.51% on winning tasks. On transfer learning, Mc-Di reaches 63.57% on CIFAR-10 and 85.02% on STL-10 with the lowest inference latency (3.5 ms, 1.9× faster than D2NWG). On domain generalization (DomainNet 5-way 1-shot), Mc-Di scores 69.05%, a 4.64% average improvement over the second-best baseline. For language model fine-tuning, Mc-Di achieves comparable accuracy to gradient-based methods (89.43% on MRPC, 91.86% on QNLI) while reducing fine-tuning latency by 3.6×–4.0×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mc-Di outperforms all weight-generation baselines (GHN3, OCD, Meta-Diff, D2NWG, Meta-Hypernetwork) across few-shot, transfer, and domain generalization tasks, with the largest margins on Tiered-ImageNet (+5.73% over GHN3 on 5-way 5-shot) and DomainNet (+4.47% over OCD on 5-way 1-shot). Against gradient-based meta-learning methods (REPTILE, Meta-Baseline), Mc-Di wins on Mini-ImageNet and Tiered-ImageNet but trails Meta-Baseline by 0.41% on the relatively easy Omniglot 5-way 1-shot task; however, Mc-Di is 4× faster in inference latency (4.8 ms vs. 19.4 ms). In transfer learning, Mc-Di beats ICIS by 1.82% on CIFAR-10 and 2.99% on Pets while reducing latency from 9.2 ms to 3.5 ms. For language model fine-tuning, Mc-Di achieves accuracy within 0.8% of the best gradient-based method (FourierFT: 90.03% vs. 89.43% on MRPC) while cutting fine-tuning time from 0.68 h to 0.60 h (MRPC) and 1.55 h to 1.31 h (QNLI), a 3.6×–4.0× speedup over full fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mc-Di sits at the intersection of two lines of work: diffusion-based weight generation (OCD, D2NWG, Meta-Diff) and bi-level optimization meta-learning (REPTILE, MAML). Prior diffusion-based methods operate under single-level optimization, modeling only the global optimal weight θ_M, which limits cross-task transferability. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of local consistency diffusion, which models intermediate optimization states (local target weights) while provably maintaining consistency with the global optimum — a problem that prior work either ignored or handled naively (which, as shown here, degrades performance). The U-Net denoiser architecture is inherited from Meta-Diff, and the meta-learning outer-loop follows REPTILE, so the novelty is primarily in the loss formulation and the two-stage (weight preparation + meta-training) pipeline design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in weight generation: existing methods are slow at inference and generalize poorly to unseen tasks. By combining meta-learning's cross-task transferability with diffusion's generative capability, and by exploiting intermediate optimization states as additional supervision, Mc-Di achieves 1.7–1.9× faster inference with 2–5% accuracy gains across few-shot, transfer, and domain generalization settings. A key open limitation is the fixed Gaussian-noise starting point, which prevents learning arbitrary sub-trajectories θ_i → θ_j; the authors point to Flow Matching and Trajectory Balance as promising directions. The broader question of whether diffusion-based weight generation can replace gradient-based fine-tuning in large-scale multi-task scenarios (e.g., LLM adaptation) remains open, though the LoRA experiment is a first step.
