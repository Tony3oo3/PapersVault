## Problem

Analytic Federated Learning (AFL) achieves single-round, heterogeneity-invariant training but is restricted to a linear head, limiting representational capacity. DeepAFL extends AFL to non-linear models but sacrifices the single-round property, requiring two communication rounds per layer. No prior method simultaneously delivers non-linear expressivity, single-round analytic convergence, and invariance to data heterogeneity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces class logits via a frozen pre-trained backbone (ResNet-18, VGG-11, or ViT-B-16) followed by a trainable non-linear head. The backbone is never updated; only the head's embedding parameters are learned, and the final output is a C-dimensional logit vector over the target classes. Because the backbone is frozen and shared across all clients, the only per-client computation is the analytic solution of the head on locally extracted feature vectors.

## Method

SAFLe replaces AFL's linear regression head with a three-stage deterministic non-linear pipeline: (1) each backbone feature is quantized into discrete bins via a bucketing function (e.g., Binary Overlapping), (2) the resulting integer vector is shuffled by a fixed permutation and partitioned into E groups of G indices each, and (3) each group is encoded as a single composite index in a base-k number system and looked up in a dedicated embedding matrix, with all E expert outputs summed densely to form the final logit. The key theoretical insight (Lemma 1) is that this grouped-embedding architecture is exactly equivalent to a high-dimensional linear regression over a sparse one-hot feature space, so the entire non-linear model can be solved in closed form via AFL's Regularization Intermediary and Absolute Aggregation law in a single communication round. Model capacity scales by increasing the number of experts E (width) rather than adding layers (depth), decoupling expressivity from communication rounds and preserving invariance to both data partitioning and client count.

## Results

SAFLe achieves 90.73% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 70.61% on CIFAR-100, and 64.58% on Tiny-ImageNet, all in a single communication round. These results are completely invariant to non-IID partitioning (identical accuracy across α=0.1, α=0.01, s=4, s=2, etc.), whereas iterative methods like FedAvg drop by over 23% on CIFAR-100 when α decreases from 0.1 to 0.01. In terms of communication cost, SAFLe reaches ~67% accuracy on CIFAR-100 using only 70 MB per client, compared to 146 MB for DeepAFL, and achieves 62.3% on Tiny-ImageNet with 60 MB versus DeepAFL's 170 MB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFLe outperforms all five iterative baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON, FedDyn, FedNTD) by 17–38 percentage points across all datasets, with the gap widening dramatically under strong non-IID (e.g., FedAvg drops to 32.99% on CIFAR-100 at α=0.01 while SAFLe holds at 70.61%). Against the single-round linear baseline AFL, SAFLe gains +9.98 pp on CIFAR-10 (90.73 vs 80.75), +12.05 pp on CIFAR-100 (70.61 vs 58.56), and +9.91 pp on Tiny-ImageNet (64.58 vs 54.67). Against the multi-round DeepAFL, SAFLe leads by +4.30 pp on CIFAR-10, +3.63 pp on CIFAR-100, and +2.23 pp on Tiny-ImageNet, while requiring only 1 communication round versus DeepAFL's 41 rounds for a T=20 model. Compared to the training-free one-shot method TOFA (ViT-B/16 backbone), SAFLe achieves higher accuracy on CIFAR-10 (95.31% vs 93.18%) and CIFAR-100 (77.83% vs 76.63%). On the ISIC 2019 label-noise benchmark, SAFLe is competitive with the specialized noise-robust FedNoRo (69.47% vs 69.8% at ρ=0.2) and consistently outperforms FedCorr+LA (69.47% vs 63.6% at ρ=0.2, 62.94% vs 53.9% at ρ=0.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFLe directly extends AFL (He et al., CVPR 2025), which established single-round analytic federated learning but was restricted to a linear regression head. It addresses the specific limitation identified in DeepAFL (Tang et al., ICLR 2026), which regained non-linear expressivity through random-projection layers at the cost of multiple communication rounds per layer. The key conceptual departure is replacing DeepAFL's stochastic, layer-wise random projections with a deterministic bucketing-and-sparse-embedding pipeline, which the authors prove is mathematically equivalent to high-dimensional linear regression—thereby re-enabling AFL's closed-form Absolute Aggregation in a single round. The architecture is also positioned relative to Mixture-of-Experts models, differing in its fixed deterministic routing and dense (rather than sparse) expert activation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By breaking the single-round/non-linear trade-off in analytic FL, SAFLe offers a practically compelling solution for real-world federated deployments where stragglers, intermittent connectivity, and high heterogeneity make multi-round synchronization prohibitive. The invariance guarantee (identical accuracy regardless of data partitioning or client count) is a strong theoretical property that no iterative gradient-based method can match. Limitations include reliance on a frozen pre-trained backbone, the non-linearity arising from discretization (bucketing) rather than learned continuous activations, and a per-round communication payload that is larger than AFL's (mitigated by sparsity but still a single large transmission). Open questions include whether the bucketing-based non-linearity suffices for tasks requiring smooth, high-order feature interactions, and whether the approach extends beyond vision to tabular or sequential data.
