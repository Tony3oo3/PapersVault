## Problem

In multi-modal large reasoning models, extended test-time reasoning progressively dilutes attention to visual tokens, causing the model to drift toward textual priors and degrade on vision-dependent tasks. Existing remedies either require costly RL fine-tuning to teach explicit look-back behaviors or rely on text-centric self-reflection that fails to restore visual grounding. The paper asks whether visual grounding can be adaptively maintained entirely at test time, without any parameter updates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260707/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image (encoded into N d-dimensional visual token embeddings, averaging ~1,772 tokens per image for InternVL-3.5-8B) and a text prompt. At each reasoning step, a coreset of m visual tokens (default m = ⌊0.3|V|⌋) is selected and reinjected into the context. The output is a single final answer, produced once an entropy-based stopping criterion (threshold δ_entropy = 0.25) signals sufficient confidence or a maximum of K_max = 10 reasoning steps is reached.

## Method

VisREF is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that at each reasoning step k projects all visual tokens into the subspace spanned by the current text-token embeddings via M_k = Σ z_k^(j)(z_k^(j))^T, then selects a coreset V_k of m tokens by maximizing the determinant of the projected similarity kernel L_k(v_i, v_j) = v_i^T M_k v_j. This DPP-based objective decomposes into a relevance term (alignment of each token with the reasoning state) and a diversity term (mutual dissimilarity among selected tokens), solved approximately via greedy marginal-gain selection. Reinjecting the selected tokens restores visual grounding without modifying model weights. An adaptive entropy-based stopping criterion terminates the loop when the model's answer-distribution entropy falls below δ_entropy, preventing overthinking while allowing complex problems to use more steps.

## Results

VisREF achieves up to 6.4% absolute accuracy improvement over standard thinking (ST) on MM-Star with SAIL-VL2-8B (55.3% vs. 47.7%), and up to 7.5% on MathVision with the same model (37.3% vs. 29.8%). Across all three benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, MM-Star) and three MLRMs (InternVL-3.5-8B, Qwen3-VL-8B, SAIL-VL2-8B), VisREF consistently outperforms both ST and textual self-reflection (TSR) by 2.3–6.4% in accuracy. Under fixed test-time compute budgets with parallel chain generation, VisREF achieves approximately 6% higher accuracy than parallel text-only thinking at a 14K-token budget on MM-Star with InternVL-3.5-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against textual self-reflection (TSR, Muennighoff et al. [33]), VisREF wins on all nine model–benchmark combinations, with gains ranging from 2.3% (Qwen3-VL on MathVision) to 6.4% (SAIL-VL2 on MM-Star); TSR itself is inconsistent, even degrading performance by 0.6% on MM-Star with Qwen3-VL-8B. Against parallel text-only thinking (Ghosal et al. [18] + Wang et al. [45]) under fixed token budgets, VisREF consistently achieves superior accuracy at every budget level, reaching ~6% higher accuracy at 14K thinking tokens on MM-Star with InternVL-3.5-8B. Against the training-based Look-Back method (Yang et al. [49]), VisREF is competitive without any fine-tuning: it scores 79.3 vs. 80.8 on MathVista, 44.6 vs. 44.2 on MathVision, and 63.1 vs. 63.7 on MM-Star with InternVL-3.5-8B, while Look-Back requires 60 GPU-hours of RL fine-tuning. Notably, combining Look-Back with VisREF yields the best results across all benchmarks (83.1 / 48.2 / 66.0), confirming that the two approaches are orthogonal and complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisREF sits at the intersection of two prior lines: (1) RL-based visual refocusing methods (Look-Back, Qwen-Look-Again) that teach models to re-attend to visual tokens during reasoning but require expensive fine-tuning, and (2) text-centric test-time scaling approaches (s1, L1) that extend reasoning chains without maintaining visual grounding. The paper departs from both by formulating visual token reinjection as a training-free, DPP-based coreset selection problem at each reasoning step, borrowing the determinantal point process framework from submodular optimization literature and applying it to the novel setting of adaptive visual token selection conditioned on the current reasoning state. The iterative reasoning loop with special instruction tokens is inherited directly from the s1 textual self-reflection framework, which VisREF augments with a visual channel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that VisREF is plug-and-play for any pretrained MLRM without retraining, data curation, or architectural modification, making it immediately deployable where RL fine-tuning is infeasible. The finding that a training-free method nearly matches RL-fine-tuned Look-Back (and that combining them yields further gains) suggests visual refocusing is orthogonal to the model's learned reasoning policy. Open questions include whether the Markov assumption (current step suffices for token selection) holds for very long reasoning chains, whether the greedy DPP approximation degrades at larger token budgets, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image inputs where temporal and cross-image diversity become additional selection criteria.
