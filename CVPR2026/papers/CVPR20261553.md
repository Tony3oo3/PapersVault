## Problem

Visible-infrared person re-identification suffers from a large modality gap driven by differences in light wavelength and light source type, causing underexposed/overexposed IR images and color-rich RGB images. Existing frequency-based VI-ReID methods either modulate the entire spectrum or target a single fixed band, but the paper argues that identity-relevant and illumination-relevant cues are distributed across multiple bands and the optimal band is sample-dependent, making fixed-band approaches insufficient under diverse lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261553/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single visible (RGB) or infrared person image resized to 384×192 pixels. During training, the RFA module swaps low-frequency amplitude components between a sample and a randomly chosen image from the other modality before feature extraction; at inference this augmentation is removed. The output is a retrieval descriptor formed by concatenating the spatial global-average-pooled feature f with a complementary frequency-domain statistic f'' that incorporates second-order moment information, yielding a single embedding used for cross-modal matching.

## Method

MFEN comprises three complementary modules operating at the data, model, and optimization levels. At the data level, Random Frequency Augmentation (RFA) performs a Fourier transform on each image, swaps only the low-frequency amplitude between a sample and a randomly selected image from the other modality (preserving high-frequency amplitude and original phase), then inverse-transforms back to simulate cross-modal lighting. At the model level, the Multi-Frequency Expert Network inserts four lightweight experts (each with 1×1 convolutions producing Q and K, FFT, a band-specific binary mask on K, element-wise multiplication Q_F ⊙ K_Fb, inverse FFT, and BN) after the second and third ResNet-50 stages; a sigmoid gating network adaptively weights all four experts per sample without top-k selection or normalization, and the fused output modulates the spatial feature via element-wise multiplication. At the optimization level, Frequency Auxiliary Optimization (FAO) computes a frequency-domain representation of the feature map augmented with second-order moment statistics and applies three losses—Frequency Identity Loss, Frequency KL Divergence Loss, and Frequency Euclidean Distance Loss—simultaneously on both the spatial and frequency representations to enforce cross-modal alignment. The full model is trained end-to-end with SGD (120 epochs, batch 64, lr 0.02, cosine decay) on a ResNet-50 backbone with BNNeck.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (all-search, the most challenging setting), MFEN achieves 80.93% rank-1 and 76.56% mAP, surpassing the strongest prior frequency-based method DSSF³ by 1.81% and 1.29% respectively. On RegDB, MFEN reaches 94.85%/94.11% rank-1 and 90.06%/90.25% mAP in visible-to-infrared and infrared-to-visible modes, outperforming DSSF³ by approximately 3.3% rank-1 and 4.5% mAP on average. On the more difficult LLCM dataset (night-time scenes), MFEN attains 67.9%/59.0% rank-1 and 69.8%/65.3% mAP, exceeding DNS by ~1.7% rank-1 and ~1.2% mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFEN achieves the best rank-1 and mAP on SYSU-MM01 all-search (80.93/76.56), the hardest setting with diverse indoor/outdoor illumination, beating the strongest frequency-based competitor FDNM [42] by 3.13% rank-1 and 1.46% mAP, and DSSF³ [2] by 1.81% rank-1. On RegDB, MFEN leads in the infrared-to-visible direction (94.11% R-1, 90.25% mAP) but is slightly behind FDNM in the visible-to-infrared direction (94.85% vs 95.5% R-1), though it matches or exceeds FDNM on mAP (90.06 vs 90.0). On LLCM, MFEN outperforms DNS [10] by 1.9% R-1 (V→I) and 1.5% R-1 (I→V), confirming gains under challenging night-time conditions. Compared to non-frequency baselines such as DEEN [38] and SGIEL [5], MFEN leads by 6–10% rank-1 across all datasets, underscoring the benefit of explicit multi-band frequency modeling over spatial-only or single-band approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain line of work in VI-ReID (FDMNet, FDNM, DSSF³, ProtoHPE) by replacing fixed-band or full-spectrum modulation with a mixture-of-experts design where each expert isolates a distinct octave band and a gating network performs sample-wise adaptive fusion. The octave-based partitioning strategy is borrowed from FDConv [1], but applied to spatial feature maps rather than convolution kernels, and the gating is deliberately unnormalized and non-sparse to preserve cross-band complementarity. At the data and optimization levels, RFA and FAO are novel contributions that have no direct counterpart in prior frequency-based VI-ReID methods, which typically operate only at the feature-extraction stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that the informative frequency band is sample-dependent (overexposed images need low-frequency suppression while blurred images need high-frequency detail) rather than fixed—provides a principled justification for adaptive multi-band fusion over the fixed-band approaches that dominate the literature. The three-level framework (data augmentation, architecture, loss) is a clean and reproducible recipe that practitioners can adopt incrementally. Open questions include whether the octave-based partitioning is truly optimal versus learned or data-driven band boundaries, whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-50 backbones and the three standard VI-ReID benchmarks, and whether the unnormalized sigmoid gating could be replaced by more expressive routing mechanisms without sacrificing the complementarity argument.
