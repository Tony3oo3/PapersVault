## Problem

Existing parameter isolation methods for continual learning effectively prevent catastrophic forgetting by freezing task-specific parameters, but they neglect model plasticity under limited capacity. Fixed pruning rates (as in PackNet, H2) either exhaust available parameters before all tasks are learned or over-freeze on simple early tasks, leaving insufficient capacity for subsequent complex tasks in heterogeneous sequences with increasing difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260293/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method processes a sequential stream of image classification tasks (e.g., MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10 32×32, TinyImageNet 64×64, Food-101 224×224) under a fixed total parameter budget B, with no access to previous task training data. For each task it outputs a class prediction and a frozen parameter subset φ_t that is permanently locked after training, while also identifying a reusable subset ψ_t of previously frozen parameters to activate during forward passes. The model architecture (lightweight CNN or ResNet-18) is shared across all tasks with task-specific classification heads.

## Method

GRAPA is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (gradient-guided reuse): for each new task, it computes per-layer cosine similarity between the current task's gradients and stored gradients from each previous task on their frozen parameters; a parameter is reused only if gradient consistency (cosine > 0) persists for more than half of training epochs, preventing negative transfer while enabling knowledge transfer. Stage 2 (adaptive allocation via A2C): after training with all remaining free parameters, a lightweight RL agent (~7K params) explores pruning rates over ≤20 steps, where the state encodes task features (number of classes, sample complexity, separability) plus step-dependent variables (previous pruning rate and accuracy). The reward r = δ^α · ρ^β (with penalty −1 if accuracy ratio δ < 0.6) balances performance retention against parameter reduction; the agent selects the highest-reward pruning rate from its experience, prunes the base model, and freezes the surviving weights as the task's parameter set, thereby allocating only the minimal parameters needed while preserving capacity for future tasks.

## Results

GRAPA achieves the highest mean test accuracy across all six task sequences on both CNN and ResNet-18. On ResNet-18, GRAPA reaches 85.89% (MFKT_R1), 90.25% (QSCF_R1), 85.26% (MFKT_R2), and 88.03% (QSCF_R2), outperforming the strongest baseline H2 by 4.55–5.31 percentage points. On the lightweight CNN, GRAPA scores 76.54% (MFKT_C) and 74.16% (QSCF_C), surpassing H2 by 4.76 and 9.35 points respectively. The paper reports up to 7.67% mean accuracy improvement and up to 14.92% gains on subsequent complex tasks (e.g., TinyImageNet, Food-101) compared to the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRAPA outperforms all four baselines on every one of the six task sequences in mean accuracy. Against LwF (regularization-based), GRAPA gains 41.68–29.86 points, as LwF suffers severe catastrophic forgetting on early tasks (e.g., 27.34% on MNIST in MFKT_C vs. GRAPA's 99.15%). Against PackNet (fixed-rate parameter isolation), GRAPA improves by 11.15–9.61 points, since PackNet's exponential parameter depletion causes sharp drops on later complex tasks (e.g., 20.43% on TinyImageNet in MFKT_C vs. GRAPA's 47.64%). Against H2 (Fisher-guided reuse + fixed pruning), GRAPA leads by 4.76–9.35 points, with the largest gap on QSCF_C (74.16% vs. 64.81%) where H2's fixed allocation fails on Food-101 (24.11% vs. GRAPA's 49.91%). Against GrowBrain (hypernetwork-based parameter transformation), GRAPA wins by 8.76–8.27 points, as GrowBrain's transformation mechanism relies on strong inter-task correlation and generalizes poorly to heterogeneous sequences with increasing difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRAPA extends the train-prune-retrain paradigm established by PackNet, which allocates a fixed proportion of new parameters to each task, by replacing the rigid fixed-rate schedule with two adaptive mechanisms: a gradient-consistency criterion for selecting reusable frozen parameters (departing from H2's Fisher-information-based reuse and GrowBrain's hypernetwork-based transformation) and an A2C reinforcement-learning agent that determines the minimal pruning rate per task. The paper positions itself against both regularization-based methods (LwF), which sacrifice stability for plasticity, and existing parameter-isolation methods, which solve forgetting but neglect the model's ability to adapt to increasingly complex tasks under a bounded parameter budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically important gap: on-device continual learning where model capacity is genuinely limited and task sequences grow in complexity. The demonstration that fixed pruning rates cause exponential capacity depletion (leaving <1.34% trainable parameters by the time complex tasks arrive) provides a clear diagnosis of why prior parameter-isolation methods fail on long heterogeneous sequences. However, the total number of learnable tasks remains inherently bounded by model size, and the authors acknowledge that intentional forgetting mechanisms are needed for truly open-ended learning. The method is validated only on compact architectures (lightweight CNN, ResNet-18) and the parameter-isolation paradigm, leaving open whether the gradient-reuse and RL-allocation strategies transfer to larger pre-trained models or other CL paradigms.
