## Problem

All-in-one medical image restoration models assume all data from all institutes is available simultaneously for training, contradicting the reality of sequential, heterogeneous clinical data streams. In this lifelong setting, two challenges compound: spatial modality interference (conflicting gradients across MRI, CT, PET) and temporal catastrophic forgetting, where the paper argues that unresolved modality conflict makes forgetting far worse than in single-modality continual learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262464/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each domain step, the model receives a mini-batch of 8 low-quality images from the current domain (which may lack one or more modalities, e.g., no CT) plus 4 replay samples from a 200-sample-per-domain memory buffer. Both streams pass through a shared 3×3 convolutional feature extractor before being processed by the disentanglement and consolidation modules. The output is a single high-quality restored image per input, produced via a global residual connection (restored = residual + LQ input).

## Method

ROME follows a 'disentangle-optimize-consolidate' strategy built on AMIR's encoder-decoder backbone. The MIDAB module establishes a strategic adversarial balance: the L1 reconstruction loss acts as a content-preservation force, while a modality discriminator trained with a Gradient Reversal layer acts as a modality-erasure force, forcing the shared feature extractor G_f to map all modalities onto a compact, modality-invariant manifold (a Nash equilibrium that prevents trivial solutions). On this unified manifold, the AFC module replaces rigid pixel-level experience replay with adaptive feature-level interpolation: a small prediction network H_pred takes concatenated max/avg-pooled descriptors of old and new features and outputs softmax-normalized weights λ that compute a weighted-sum consolidation point f_con. A novel diversity loss (negative mean squared L2 distance of per-sample weights from their batch mean) prevents H_pred from collapsing to a trivial 0.5/0.5 split, encouraging sample-specific fusion strategies. The total loss is L1 + α·L_adv + β·L_div (α=0.01, β=0.15), trained end-to-end with Adam and cosine annealing over 150k iterations across 5 sequential domains.

## Results

In the Lifelong All-in-one setting (sequential training across 5 domains with domain shift and modality missing), ROME achieves an average PSNR of 32.36 dB, SSIM of 0.8955, and RMSE of 15.67, outperforming the next-best method AMIR (31.41 dB) by 0.95 dB. In the Static All-in-one setting (all data available simultaneously), ROME reaches 34.94 dB average PSNR, surpassing DiffCode (34.62 dB) and AMIR (34.28 dB). Critically, ROME exhibits the smallest performance degradation between static and lifelong settings (only 2.58 dB drop), compared to 2.87 dB for AMIR and 3.77 dB for Restormer, demonstrating superior resilience to catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROME outperforms all 8 baselines in both static and lifelong settings. In the lifelong setting, ROME leads AMIR (the closest competitor and backbone architecture) by 0.95 dB in average PSNR (32.36 vs. 31.41) and 0.013 in SSIM, with the gap widening on PET synthesis (35.72 vs. 34.41 dB). Against general-purpose restoration models, ROME surpasses AirNet by 1.62 dB, Spach Transformer by 2.09 dB, Restormer by 1.97 dB, DRMC by 2.29 dB, and Eformer by 2.85 dB in average PSNR. In the static setting, ROME edges out DiffCode by 0.32 dB (34.94 vs. 34.62) and DenoMamba by 0.74 dB, confirming that the MIDAB adversarial balancing improves even when all data is available simultaneously. The most telling comparison is the static-to-lifelong degradation: ROME loses only 2.58 dB while Restormer loses 3.77 dB and AMIR loses 2.87 dB, validating that the AFC module's adaptive feature consolidation is the key differentiator for long-horizon knowledge retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

ROME builds directly on the AMIR backbone (task-adaptive routing encoder-decoder) and repurposes the gradient-reversal mechanism from domain-adversarial training (Ganin et al.) into a modality-disentanglement module (MIDAB). For the temporal dimension, it extends feature-level interpolation strategies from domain-adaptive re-identification (Dai et al., ICCV 2021) into a continual-learning context, replacing rigid pixel-level experience replay with adaptive feature-space consolidation (AFC). The key conceptual departure from prior all-in-one MedIR work (AMIR, DiffCode) is the explicit reframing from a static, pooled-data paradigm to a domain-incremental lifelong setting where both distribution shift and modality absence must be handled simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that modality-invariant disentanglement is a necessary prerequisite for any feature-consolidation strategy to succeed in a modality-missing stream—is a useful design principle for multi-modal continual learning more broadly. However, the evaluation is limited to three modalities, five domains, and a single dataset suite, leaving open whether the synergistic MIDAB+AFC interaction generalizes to more modalities, longer sequences, or 3D volumetric data. The modest gradient-conflict reduction (average PET-CT similarity improving from −0.31 to −0.265) suggests the adversarial balancing, while helpful, does not fully eliminate inter-modality tension, raising questions about whether stronger disentanglement (e.g., explicit style tokens) could yield further gains.
