## Problem

Multi-model routing has become essential infrastructure for balancing quality and cost across heterogeneous model pools, yet the vision-language model (VLM) domain lacks a systematic, reproducible benchmark for evaluating routing strategies. Unlike LLM routing, VLM routing must handle diverse task types (VQA, visual reasoning, chart OCR), open multimodal fusion mechanisms, and visual-semantic density, making it unclear what constitutes an optimal routing decision within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261029/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each routing input is a single image paired with a textual instruction, constrained to rule-verifiable formats (multiple choice, exact answer matching) to ensure objective correctness scoring. The router outputs a probability distribution over 17 candidate VLMs (15 open-source, 2 API), from which the argmax model is selected for inference. The benchmark is grounded in 519,180 raw inference records with per-pair correctness labels and token-level cost accounting, covering 30,540 samples across 14 datasets and 34.5M total tokens.

## Method

The core training contribution is an accuracy-cost-aware soft label strategy derived from the Lagrangian of the multi-objective routing objective: for each sample, probability mass is distributed only over correct models, weighted by an exponential cost decay exp(−λC_ij), so that a single hyperparameter λ continuously interpolates between pure-accuracy routing (λ=0) and cheapest-correct-model selection (λ→∞). Routers are trained with cross-entropy against these soft labels under two architectural paradigms: feature-level routers (frozen text/visual encoders → fusion → lightweight classifier such as MLP or KNN) and end-to-end routers (fine-tuned multimodal encoders like LXMERT or ViLBERT that directly output the selection distribution). Evaluation uses a harmonic-mean Rank Score combining log-normalized cost and average accuracy, alongside throughput, enabling unified comparison across 10 routing methods and baselines including an Oracle upper bound.

## Results

On VL-RouterBench (14 datasets, 30,540 samples, 17 candidate VLMs), the best learned router RouterDC achieves 77.52% average accuracy at $1.04/10K samples cost, yielding a Rank Score of 74.59, while the VLC router reaches the highest accuracy (78.09%) at $1.23 cost with Rank Score 74.33. The MLP feature-level router is a close third (77.49% acc, $1.13 cost, Rank 74.23) with far higher throughput (146.71 K tokens/s vs. ~6–7 for end-to-end routers). All learned routers substantially outperform the Strongest single-model baseline (78.01% acc but $2.72 cost, Rank 68.88) and the Cheapest baseline (62.43% acc, $0.14 cost, Rank 64.63), yet a large gap remains to the Oracle upper bound (95.60% acc, $0.37 cost, Rank 93.68).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among end-to-end routers, RouterDC (Rank 74.59) edges out VLC (74.33) and ZOOTER (72.58) on the composite Rank Score, while VLC achieves the highest raw accuracy (78.09%) at a slightly higher cost ($1.23 vs. $1.04). The MLP feature-level router (Rank 74.23) is nearly as strong as the top end-to-end methods but runs ~20× faster in throughput (146.71 vs. ~6–7 K tokens/s), making it the most deployment-friendly option. Simpler feature-level baselines—KNN (67.13), K-means (64.62), and Linear (65.68)—all fall well below the top routers, while PRkNN (71.09) and OVR (68.92) are intermediate; OVR in particular incurs a high cost ($2.18) that drags its Rank Score despite 75.49% accuracy. In the ablation on fusion methods, Normalize-Concat (Rank 74.05) clearly beats single-modality inputs (Only-Text 70.97, Only-Image 71.82) and more complex fusion like GMU (69.12, which over-pays at $3.32) or MLB (67.66). For end-to-end backbones, all four multimodal encoders (LXMERT 71.36, ViLBERT 70.81, VisualBERT 70.89, UNITER 68.43) substantially outperform the text-only BERT (64.55) and image-only MobileNetV4 (63.33) baselines, confirming that both modalities are needed for effective VLM routing. Overall, every learned router surpasses the Strongest single-model baseline (Rank 68.88) and the Cheapest baseline (64.63), but the best router still trails the Oracle by ~19 Rank Score points, indicating substantial headroom for future router architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM routing benchmark lineage (RouterBench, RouterEval, RouterArena) to the vision-language domain, which the authors identify as having long lacked a systematic, reproducible routing benchmark. For end-to-end router design, it adapts the BERT-Router architecture from RouteLLM [32] by replacing the text-only backbone with vision-language encoders (VisualBERT, UNITER, LXMERT, ViLBERT), and for feature-level routing it draws on the classifier zoo from Shnitzer et al. [52]. The data infrastructure is built on VLMEvalKit [38], and the soft-label training strategy with an adjustable cost-decay hyperparameter λ provides a unified mechanism that subsumes both pure-accuracy and pure-cost routing objectives, going beyond the binary preference-based or reward-model-based training used in prior LLM routers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLM deployment scales across heterogeneous model families with widely varying costs (from $0.05 to $10 per million tokens), a standardized routing benchmark becomes essential infrastructure for both research and production. The substantial gap between the best current router (RouterDC, Rank Score 74.59) and the Oracle (93.68) signals that current architectures under-exploit visual and structural cues, pointing toward future work on finer-grained visual representations and layout-aware textual modeling. Open questions include extending the benchmark to multi-image inputs, subjective or preference-based evaluation, and developing router architectures that close the Oracle gap without sacrificing the high throughput that feature-level routers already achieve.
