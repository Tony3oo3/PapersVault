## Problem

Diffusion-based image editing models suffer from two critical limitations when applied to photo retouching: they introduce unwanted content drift (altering geometry and texture) because they operate on variational latents that conflate content with photometric information, and their iterative sampling process is too slow for high-resolution processing. Existing fast methods like 3D LUTs preserve fidelity but cannot follow free-form text instructions, leaving a gap between instruction-following capability and content-preserving efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image at any resolution (up to 4K) and a natural-language retouching instruction as input, and produces a retouched RGB image at the same native resolution in a single forward pass (~68 ms at 4K). Internally, the model predicts a low-resolution 3D bilateral grid of affine transforms (Γ ∈ R^{H_g × W_g × D × 12}), which is sliced per-pixel using a learned grayscale guidance map and applied to the full-resolution image via a fully differentiable slice-and-apply operator. Because the bilateral grid is resolution-independent, inference latency is constant regardless of input size.

## Method

The core insight is to decouple retouching from content by predicting only the parameters of a photometric transformation in a compact bilateral space rather than editing pixels or latents directly. The generator G_θ has two synergistic branches: a low-resolution one-step diffusion U-Net (initialized from a multi-step InstructPix2Pix-style teacher) that provides semantic understanding and diffusion priors, and a lightweight full-resolution bilateral adapter that outputs the affine grid. Training uses a two-stage progressive distillation: Stage 1 trains the low-res branch with Variational Score Distillation (VSD) to transfer the teacher's generative distribution, a data loss (L2 + LPIPS), and a CLIP-based InfoNCE prompt-alignment loss that decomposes instructions into atomic retouching attributes (e.g., brightness:up, mood:cozy) for directional supervision; Stage 2 unfreezes the bilateral adapter and adds a bilateral loss combining L1/LPIPS against ground truth, perceptual agreement with the low-res prediction, 3D Laplacian smoothness on the grid, and an RGB overflow penalty. A progressive noise-level curriculum (high-t to low-t) further stabilizes distillation from coarse tone to fine color detail.

## Results

On the iRetouch benchmark, InstantRetouch achieves near-constant inference time of 0.065–0.068 s from 720p to 4K, a 70–900× speedup over generative baselines (e.g., 14.4 s for Gemini-2.5-Flash, 57.9 s for Step1X-Edit at 720p). It attains the best content fidelity among all instruction-guided methods with SSIM 0.989, DISTS 0.022, and GSMD 0.012, while achieving an overall editing-quality score (O) of 8.54, competitive with the top proprietary system Gemini-2.5-Flash (8.74). On PPR10K portrait identity preservation, the method achieves the highest FaceNet cosine similarity, confirming strict identity retention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstantRetouch dominates on efficiency and fidelity: it runs in 0.065 s at 720p (and 0.068 s at 4K), making it 70× faster than InstructPix2Pix (4.6 s), 220× faster than Gemini-2.5-Flash (14.4 s), and 890× faster than Step1X-Edit (57.9 s), while also being the only method that natively handles 4K input. On content fidelity, it achieves SSIM 0.989 and DISTS 0.022, substantially outperforming all generative editors (best among them: FLUX.1-Kontext-Pro at SSIM 0.802, DISTS 0.132) and even slightly exceeding the non-instruction-driven 3D LUT (SSIM 0.982). On editing quality, the method scores O = 8.54, trailing only Gemini-2.5-Flash (8.74) by 0.20 points while surpassing all other baselines (Qwen-Image 8.39, GPT-Image-1 8.32, FLUX.1-Kontext-Pro 8.12, Step1X-Edit 8.06, InstructPix2Pix 7.34); it also achieves the highest perceptual quality (PQ = 8.98) of any method tested. In the user study, InstantRetouch received the highest preference ratings across all four dimensions (fidelity, editing ability, visual quality, overall) compared to FLUX.1-Kontext-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen-Image, and GPT-Image-1. On PPR10K portrait identity preservation, it achieves the highest FaceNet cosine similarity, whereas competing methods introduce visible repainting that distorts facial identity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of instruction-guided image editing (InstructPix2Pix, FLUX.1-Kontext, Gemini-2.5-Flash) and classical photometric manipulation via bilateral grids (Chen et al., 2007; Gharbi et al., 2017). The key departure is architectural: rather than using a diffusion model to iteratively denoise and edit image latents (which causes content drift and is slow), the authors distill a multi-step diffusion teacher into a one-step bilateral grid predictor using Variational Score Distillation (from DMD, Yin et al. 2024) augmented with a CLIP-based prompt alignment loss. The bilateral grid serves as a compact, content-decoupled output space that guarantees geometric and textural fidelity by construction, while the distilled diffusion branch supplies the aesthetic and semantic priors needed for instruction-following. This contrasts with prior retouching approaches (3D LUTs, RSFNet) that are fast but not instruction-driven, and with generative editors that follow instructions but alter content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70–800× speedup (68 ms at 4K vs. 10–58 s for diffusion baselines) with near-perfect content fidelity (SSIM 0.989) and competitive editing quality (O=8.54 vs. Gemini-2.5-Flash's 8.74) makes instruction-guided retouching practical for real-time and high-resolution applications where generative editors are infeasible. The iRetouch benchmark (500 curated before/after pairs with refined instructions) fills a gap in evaluation for the retouching-specific sub-task. Open questions include whether the bilateral grid representation can be extended beyond photometric adjustments to handle more complex local edits, how well the one-step distillation generalizes to out-of-distribution instructions, and whether the approach transfers to video retouching. The method's fidelity guarantee is architectural (bilateral space cannot alter geometry), which is a strong design choice but also a hard limitation for any task requiring structural changes.
