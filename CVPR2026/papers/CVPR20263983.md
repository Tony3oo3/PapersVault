## Problem

Split conformal prediction requires a labeled calibration set to estimate the coverage threshold, but with limited labeled data the empirical coverage exhibits high run-to-run variance and systematic over-coverage, producing oversized prediction sets. Prior remedies (interpolation, modified p-values, few-shot meta-learning) are heuristic, lack finite-sample guarantees, or require exchangeable auxiliary tasks. No existing method leverages the abundant unlabeled data available in practice to stabilize conformal calibration.

## Input / Output

Given a pre-trained classifier f, a small labeled calibration set of n examples, a larger unlabeled calibration set of N examples, and a test image, SemiCP outputs a prediction set of class labels and a single scalar coverage threshold. The method is entirely training-free: no additional optimization or retraining is performed; it only computes nonconformity scores on the calibration pool and takes a quantile. The unlabeled examples require no ground-truth labels—only the classifier's softmax outputs are needed to form pseudo-labels for the NNM matching step.

## Method

SemiCP augments the standard split-CP calibration pool with unlabeled nonconformity scores computed via Nearest Neighbor Matching (NNM). For each unlabeled sample, the pseudo-label (argmax of softmax) is plugged into the chosen score function (THR, APS, or RAPS) to obtain a pseudo score; this pseudo score is systematically biased low because the model's most confident class minimizes the score. NNM corrects the bias by finding the labeled example whose pseudo score is closest in pseudo-score space and transferring that labeled example's observed true-minus-pseudo bias to the unlabeled sample. The debiased unlabeled scores are pooled with the labeled scores, and the (1−α)-quantile of the combined pool yields the threshold. Theoretically, the average coverage gap shrinks at rate O(1/√N) as unlabeled samples are added, and the NNM score distribution converges to the true score distribution as the labeled set grows, ensuring asymptotic consistency while preserving valid marginal coverage.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with only 10 labeled calibration samples and 4,000 unlabeled samples, SemiCP reduces the average coverage gap from 6.4 to 1.1 and the average prediction set size from 1.6 to 1.27 (averaged over THR, APS, RAPS). On ImageNet with 50 labeled and 20,000 unlabeled samples, SemiCP lowers the coverage gap from 3.3 to 2.1 and set size from 75 to 70.3. Under group-conditional settings on CIFAR-100 (n_avg=10 per group), SemiCP shrinks the coverage gap from 6.50 to 4.75 and set size from 18.25 to 16.83, approaching oracle performance. The method also integrates with Interpolation (reducing coverage gap from 9 to 3.9 at n=10 on CIFAR-100) and ClusterCP, yielding further gains in both stability and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemiCP consistently outperforms Standard Split CP [Shafer & Vovk, 2008; Lei et al., 2018] on both coverage gap and set size across all three datasets and all tested labeled-data budgets (n=10 to n=100). On CIFAR-10 with n=10, the coverage gap drops from 6.4 to 1.1 (an 83% reduction), and with n=20 the gap is reduced by 77%. Against Interpolation [Johansson et al., 2015], which alone produces a *larger* coverage gap than Standard (indicating instability without valid guarantees), SemiCP+Interpolation achieves a coverage gap of 3.9 at n=10 on CIFAR-100 versus 9 for Interpolation alone, while also approaching oracle set sizes for n>40. Against ClusterCP [Ding et al., 2023], which already improves over Standard under low calibration budgets, SemiCP+ClusterCP delivers further reductions in both coverage gap and set size across all n_avg values. Across 10 diverse ImageNet architectures (ResNet, ViT, EfficientNet, etc.), SemiCP uniformly reduces coverage gap from 3.3 to 2.1 and set size from 75 to 70.3, confirming backbone-agnostic gains over the Standard baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemiCP extends the split conformal prediction framework (Lei et al. 2018; Shafer & Vovk 2008) to the semi-supervised setting, where calibration draws on both labeled and unlabeled examples. It departs from prior remedies for small calibration sets—interpolation (Johansson et al. 2015), ClusterCP (Ding et al. 2023), and few-shot CP (Fisch et al. 2021)—by introducing unlabeled data as a free source of score information rather than relying on heuristic threshold adjustments or auxiliary task collections. The Nearest Neighbor Matching score is conceptually related to semi-supervised quantile estimation and prediction-powered inference, but is the first to apply such ideas to conformal prediction in a training-free manner, requiring no optimization or access to the training set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practical bottleneck: conditional conformal prediction on high-cardinality tasks (e.g., ImageNet with 1,000 classes) can require 10⁵ labeled calibration examples, which is often infeasible. The O(1/√N) coverage-gap convergence rate and the training-free, plug-and-play nature of NNM make SemiCP broadly adoptable across score functions and architectures. Key limitations include the i.i.d. assumption (stricter than the exchangeability assumption standard in CP), restriction to classification (no regression extension), and dependence on a reasonably calibrated pre-trained classifier for pseudo-labels. Open questions include whether the i.i.d. requirement can be relaxed to exchangeability, how SemiCP behaves under distribution shift between labeled and unlabeled pools, and whether the approach extends to regression or sequential prediction settings.
