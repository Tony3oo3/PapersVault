## Problem

Effective pretraining strategies for space-time video super-resolution (STVSR) networks remain largely unexplored. Existing video representation learning methods (MAE-based, contrastive) are tailored to ViT architectures, rely on high masking ratios that destroy high-frequency details critical for pixel-level reconstruction, and are computationally expensive due to video redundancy. This paper asks whether image datasets—which provide cleaner spatial cues—can be leveraged for STVSR pretraining without architectural constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260389/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, a single image (or a single frame sampled from a video) is duplicated to match the target network's expected number of input frames (e.g., 4 frames), and independent random 4×4 pixel blocks are zero-filled in each frame at a configurable ratio (default 0.5). The target STVSR network then reconstructs clean outputs upscaled ×4 spatially and ×2 temporally (4 input frames → 7 output frames). Unlike standard MAE approaches that remove masked regions from the input, here masked blocks are explicitly zero-filled so the network sees the full spatial layout with degraded content.

## Method

The proposed framework pretrains the target STVSR network itself (no separate module) on a pretext task that mirrors its core objectives: spatial restoration and cross-frame aggregation. Pseudo-temporal variation is introduced by applying frame-wise independent random zero-filling to a duplicated single image, creating varying region visibility across frames that encourages cross-frame inference without real motion. A key novelty is a difficulty-adaptive modulating factor w_ij^(t) = 1/(1 + v_ij^(t)), where v_ij^(t) counts how many input frames have the corresponding block visible; this weights the MSE loss so the network focuses more on regions that are harder to generate (heavily masked across all input frames). The approach is architecture-agnostic, works with any fixed-rate STVSR network (demonstrated on RSTT, Zooming, TMNet, SAFA), and is effective even with small pretraining datasets (as few as 3,840 images from REDS) and short pretraining (50 epochs suffice).

## Results

On the STVSR task (×4 spatial, ×2 temporal upsampling), the proposed pretraining consistently improves all tested architectures. With RSTT-S pretrained on Vimeo-90K and finetuned on 1% of Vimeo-90K, PSNR improves from 31.66 to 34.18 dB (+2.52 dB) on the fast sequence; with Zooming the gain is even larger at +5.08 dB (28.88→33.96). In the SOTA comparison (Table 5), RSTT+Ours achieves 36.79 dB PSNR / 0.9403 SSIM on Vimeo-90K Fast, outperforming the un-pretrained RSTT (36.58 / 0.9381) and approaching Store-and-Fetch (37.12 / 0.9460) with fewer parameters (4.49M vs. 16.40M). Against other self-supervised pretraining methods on REDS, the proposed image-based pretraining reaches 33.46 dB PSNR / 0.8958 SSIM, surpassing the best video-based baseline EVEREST (32.15 / 0.8756) by +1.31 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against other self-supervised pretraining methods (Figure 1, REDS pretraining → Vimeo-90K 1% finetuning), the proposed image-based pseudo-temporal pretraining (33.46 dB PSNR, 0.8958 SSIM) outperforms all video-based baselines: EVEREST (32.15 / 0.8756), MoCo-v3 (31.79 / 0.8715), SIGMA (31.66 / 0.8687), VideoMAE (31.64 / 0.8681), and MAE-ST (31.60 / 0.8672), with the largest margin over EVEREST at +1.31 dB. Notably, MAE-ST and VideoMAE do not even beat training from scratch (31.66 / 0.8682), confirming their unsuitability for pixel-level video prediction. In the SOTA STVSR comparison (Table 5), the pretraining yields consistent but modest gains over un-pretrained counterparts (e.g., RSTT: +0.21 dB on Fast; TMNet: +0.27 dB on Fast; SAFA: +1.06 dB on Fast), with the largest relative improvement on the weaker SAFA architecture. The gains are smaller than in few-shot settings because the SOTA evaluation uses a much longer finetuning schedule (26 days vs. 3.3 days pretraining), which partially erases the initialization advantage. Across all four downstream tasks tested (STVSR, VFI, VSR, DSR in Table 4), the proposed pretraining consistently outperforms MAE-ST, VideoMAE, and MoCo-v3, with the largest advantage on VFI (+2.77 dB over Scratch) and VSR (+1.56 dB over Scratch).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MAE-based video pretraining paradigm (MAE-ST, VideoMAE) by replacing real video with pseudo-temporal videos constructed from single images, and by zero-filling masked blocks rather than removing them from the input. It also diverges from architecture-specific image-to-video pretraining (e.g., 3D-CNN kernel expansion) by being fully architecture-agnostic, training the target STVSR network directly on a pretext task aligned with its core objectives. Conceptually, the difficulty-adaptive modulating factor draws from task-induced representation learning in reinforcement learning and low-level vision, where the training signal is shaped to emphasize challenging regions rather than treating all pixels uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first pretraining framework specifically designed for pixel-level video prediction tasks, filling a gap where existing video representation learning methods (targeting classification or high-level semantics) have been shown to be ineffective or even harmful. The finding that a single-image pseudo-temporal construction outperforms real-video self-supervised methods suggests that spatial fidelity and clean signals matter more than genuine temporal dynamics for learning dense reconstruction representations. A key limitation is that pretraining still processes multi-frame inputs, so training time remains comparable to video-based methods; the authors identify shortening this as future work. An open question is whether the approach extends to continuous-rate STVSR or other generative video enhancement tasks beyond fixed-rate super-resolution.
