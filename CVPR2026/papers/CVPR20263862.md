## Problem

3D VLA models achieve precise geometric reasoning through point clouds and voxels but sacrifice visual interpretability, while 2D VLA models provide rich semantic perception but lack explicit 3D spatial grounding. Existing RGB-D fusion approaches only encode surface geometry from fixed viewpoints without explicit object-to-object spatial relationships, leaving a gap between geometric reasoning and image-level understanding that hinders robust manipulation under occlusion and clutter.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB-D observations (220×220) fused into point clouds and voxelized into a 50³ grid, alongside a natural language instruction encoded by a VLM. Rather than feeding raw images to the VLA backbone, the pipeline first renders differentiable spatially-enriched images from a combination of learned adaptive cameras and 2 fixed baseline views. The output is a 6-DoF end-effector action: continuous position (p∈ℝ³), discretized Euler-angle rotation in 5° bins (r∈ℝ³), and binary gripper state (g∈{0,1}), each predicted by independent heads from bidirectionally fused features.

## Method

DiffRender-VLA introduces a three-stage differentiable rendering pipeline that bridges 3D spatial reasoning and 2D visual perception. First, a Perceiver IO encoder jointly processes voxels and language embeddings to predict a coarse target location, placing a world-axis-aligned cube marker in the point cloud. Second, a structuring module color-encodes surrounding geometry with spatial beams whose hue indicates world-axis direction (red/cyan for ±X, green/magenta for ±Y, blue/yellow for ±Z) and intensity encodes signed distance to the cube, blending with original appearance via a distance-dependent sigmoid. Third, adaptive camera viewpoints are learned end-to-end through task-loss backpropagation, optimizing for target visibility and spatial cue legibility. The critical mechanism is a closed gradient loop: 2D VLA action losses backpropagate through rendered images into beam colors and cube placement, enabling the spatial encoder to co-adapt with the OpenVLA backbone (SigLIP + DinoV2 + Llama-2-7B) via bidirectional cross-attention fusion, transferring 3D geometric understanding into visually interpretable manipulation policies without requiring explicit 3D inputs at inference.

## Results

On RLBench (12 tasks, Franka Panda), DiffRender-VLA achieves 80.5% average success rate, outperforming the best 3D baseline GWM (68.4%) by +12.1% and the best 2D baseline VLA-Adapter (63.9%) by +16.6%. On occlusion tasks it reaches 91.7% average (+7.6% over GWM), and on clutter/precision tasks 69.4% (+24.0% over GWM). In real-world deployment on AgileX PIPER (6 tasks, 20 trials each), it achieves 78.3% average success, a +17.5% improvement over the best baseline (VLA-Adapter at 60.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffRender-VLA outperforms all 15 baselines on both simulation and real-world benchmarks. Among 3D VLA methods, it surpasses GWM (68.4%) by +12.1%, FVP (66.1%) by +14.4%, Act3D (65.8%) by +14.7%, ManiGaussian (65.7%) by +14.8%, 3D Diffuser (65.3%) by +15.2%, DP3 (64.0%) by +16.5%, and PerAct (48.4%) by +32.1%. Among 2D VLA methods, it beats VLA-Adapter (63.9%) by +16.6%, UniVLA (60.7%) by +19.8%, TraceVLA (60.6%) by +19.9%, Pi0 (59.4%) by +21.1%, SmolVLA (57.2%) by +23.3%, OpenVLA-OFT (53.4%) by +27.1%, and RT-2 (47.5%) by +33.0%. The largest gains appear in clutter/precision tasks (+24.0% over GWM), where color-encoded spatial beams disambiguate densely packed objects. In real-world deployment, the advantage over the best baseline (VLA-Adapter) widens to +17.5%, and over DP3 reaches +45.0%, confirming that differentiable rendering transfers effectively beyond simulation. RVT-2 (68.4% in-domain) is outperformed by +12.1% in simulation and shows a larger gap under zero-shot generalization (73.6% vs. 61.0% average across OOD conditions).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established VLA paradigms: 3D spatial models (PerAct, DP3, Act3D, ManiGaussian) that achieve geometric precision through point clouds and voxels but sacrifice visual interpretability, and 2D visual models (OpenVLA, RT-2, Pi0) that leverage rich semantic perception but lack explicit 3D grounding. Rather than following the RGB-D fusion line (Depth Helps, RoboFlamingo-Plus, DepthVLA) which operates at the feature level, DiffRender-VLA introduces a differentiable rendering pipeline that projects 3D spatial semantics (world-aligned cube markers, color-encoded distance beams) into 2D images consumable by a pretrained VLA backbone, while allowing task-loss gradients to backpropagate into the 3D representations. The architecture builds directly on OpenVLA as the semantic backbone and PerAct's Perceiver IO voxel encoder for coarse spatial reasoning, but departs from both by making the 3D-to-2D interface differentiable and jointly optimized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The differentiable rendering bridge offers a principled mechanism for transferring 3D geometric understanding into image-based VLA policies without abandoning visual interpretability, addressing a gap that both pure-3D and pure-2D approaches leave open. The consistent gains across occlusion (+11.2%), clutter (+17.8%), and spatial reasoning (+13.4%) tasks, validated on both RLBench and a physical AgileX PIPER robot, suggest the approach generalizes beyond simulation. However, real-world validation is limited to a single robot platform and six tasks, and the computational overhead of point cloud reconstruction, voxelization, and differentiable rendering per timestep remains a practical concern for real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-arm or dexterous manipulation, whether the learned adaptive viewpoints remain stable under rapid scene changes, and whether the gradient flow through rendering is truly necessary versus a well-designed two-stage pipeline (the ablation gap is only 4.3%).
