## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (e.g., VGGT, StreamVGGT) achieve strong static geometry but lack a unified dynamic representation capable of capturing point-wise motion and maintaining temporal coherence in large-scale autonomous driving scenes. Real-world driving data further complicates the problem with sparse LiDAR supervision, high noise, and diverse moving objects, causing performance degradation when static models are naively extended to dynamic settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260853/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of multi-view or monocular RGB frames (resized to ≤518 px on the longer side) without requiring camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth ground truth; the temporal offset δ between predicted frames is randomly sampled from 1–3 during training. Outputs include current and future 3D point maps in a learned canonical frame, dynamic 3D Gaussian primitives parameterized with center, scale, rotation, color, and velocity, camera parameters, per-pixel depth, rendered novel views, and scene flow — all produced in a single feed-forward pass.

## Method

DynamicVGGT extends the VGGT backbone (DINOv2 encoder + Alternating-Attention blocks) with three coupled components. First, a Motion-aware Temporal Attention (MTA) module introduces learnable motion tokens that are concatenated with patch tokens and process inter-frame correlations in parallel to the spatial AA blocks, using rotary positional biases to encode temporal order without disrupting the pretrained geometric priors. Second, a Future Point Head (a DPT regressor) predicts the next-frame point map from the temporally enhanced features, and a temporal-consistency L1 loss on inter-frame displacement fields enforces implicit point-wise motion. Third, a Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting Head fuses image-convolution appearance features with geometric features to initialize Gaussian primitives, then decodes per-Gaussian velocity vectors from the shared motion tokens under scene-flow supervision, refining dynamic geometry through continuous 3DGS optimization. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 on synthetic data (Virtual KITTI, MVS-Synth) learns geometric and temporal priors with point-map and camera losses; Stage 2 fine-tunes on real driving data (Waymo, Virtual KITTI) with the Gaussian head enabled, using a depth-distillation loss from the Stage-1 point-map branch to counteract LiDAR sparsity and a scene-flow MSE for explicit motion supervision.

## Results

On KITTI (monocular, 3 consecutive frames), DynamicVGGT achieves the best point map reconstruction with Accuracy 0.901, Completeness 0.584, and Normal Consistency 0.939, outperforming VGGT (Acc 1.489) and StreamVGGT (Acc 1.078). On Waymo (3 cameras, stride 4), it reduces accuracy error to 4.021 (vs. 4.635 for VGGT) and improves median NC to 0.603. For depth estimation, DynamicVGGT achieves the lowest Abs Rel of 0.070 on KITTI Mono and 0.051 on KITTI MVS, with 97.6% accuracy under δ<1.25 in the MVS setting. On 4D scene reconstruction (Waymo), it reaches PSNR 24.07 / SSIM 0.676 on full frames using only image-only supervision, competitive with camera-conditioned feed-forward methods like STORM (PSNR 25.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two closest feed-forward baselines, DynamicVGGT consistently wins: on KITTI it reduces accuracy error by 39% over VGGT (0.901 vs. 1.489) and by 17% over StreamVGGT (0.901 vs. 1.078), while also achieving the highest Normal Consistency (0.939 vs. 0.918 and 0.899). On Waymo, it improves accuracy over VGGT by 0.614 and over StreamVGGT by 0.577, with a notable completeness gain (2.390 vs. 2.667/2.626). For depth estimation (Table 3), DynamicVGGT outperforms DUSt3R, MASt3R, MonST3R, VGGT, and StreamVGGT on KITTI Mono (Abs Rel 0.070) and KITTI MVS (Abs Rel 0.051, δ<1.25 = 97.6%), though it is slightly behind VGGT on NYU-v2 Mono (0.064 vs. 0.059). In 4D scene reconstruction (Table 2), DynamicVGGT trails STORM (PSNR 18.07 vs. 21.26 on dynamic regions) and GS-LRM, but this comparison is asymmetric: STORM and GS-LRM require calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, whereas DynamicVGGT operates with image-only input and no per-scene optimization. Against per-scene methods (3DGS, DeformableGS), DynamicVGGT is competitive on full-frame PSNR (24.07 vs. 25.13/25.29) while being orders of magnitude faster due to its feed-forward design.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicVGGT extends VGGT's feed-forward static 3D perception to dynamic 4D reconstruction by adopting the Dynamic Point Map formulation from Sucar et al. and augmenting it with a Motion-aware Temporal Attention module that runs in parallel to VGGT's Alternating Attention blocks, avoiding the training instability observed in StreamVGGT's sequential stacking. The Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting Head builds on the 3DGS primitive by adding per-Gaussian velocity vectors supervised by scene flow, while the Future Point Head introduces an implicit motion-learning task through inter-frame point consistency. Unlike per-scene optimization methods (3DGS, DeformableGS) and camera-dependent feed-forward approaches (STORM, GS-LRM), DynamicVGGT operates purely from image inputs without requiring calibrated extrinsics or per-scene fitting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that feed-forward 4D reconstruction for autonomous driving is viable without camera parameters or per-scene optimization, which is practically important for scalable deployment in real driving systems. The two-stage synthetic-to-real training with depth distillation from the point-map branch to the Gaussian branch offers a practical recipe for handling sparse LiDAR supervision. However, the method still trails STORM on 4D reconstruction quality (PSNR 18.07 vs. 21.26 on dynamic regions), and the constant-velocity assumption within short clips limits applicability to longer temporal spans. Open questions include whether the framework scales to longer sequences, whether the MTA module generalizes beyond driving to other dynamic domains, and whether the implicit/explicit motion supervision can be further disentangled for more complex multi-object dynamics.
