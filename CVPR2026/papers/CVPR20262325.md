## Problem

Existing Gaussian-based embodied scene completion methods either initialize dense primitives within predefined bounded volumes (causing redundancy and infeasibility for unbounded exploration) or use depth-guided local prediction that lacks long-term memory maintenance, leading to noise accumulation and unbounded memory growth. The core gap is a scalable mechanism to maintain a persistent, compact 3D representation that supports temporally consistent updates during continuous egocentric exploration without relying on frame-to-frame image coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262325/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of RGB images paired with camera poses, augmented by per-pixel depth maps and depth features from a fine-tuned DepthAnythingV2 model used solely for 3D Gaussian initialization (reprojecting sampled pixels to define primitive means). The output is a dense 3D semantic occupancy grid (per-voxel occupancy probability and class logits) rendered from a compact set of Gaussian primitives via decoupled Gaussian-to-voxel splatting, alongside the updated global Gaussian memory that persists across timesteps and grows only with newly explored regions.

## Method

TGSFormer maintains a persistent global Gaussian memory updated at each timestep through two key modules. The Dual Temporal Encoder (DTE) runs two weight-sharing temporal encoders in parallel, each performing Confidence-aware Cross-Attention (CCA) between current and historical Gaussian features; confidence is computed as a product of opacity and a power-transformed Shannon entropy of semantic predictions, and modulates both the value vector and the concatenated multi-head attention output to suppress low-confidence information flow. The Confidence-aware Voxel Fusion (CAVF) module then merges all primitives falling within the same 3D voxel into a single fused primitive via confidence-weighted softmax, bounding the total primitive count and memory. Training follows a two-stage strategy: monocular pretraining with multi-stage supervision (focal + Lovász + geometry scale loss applied at multiple encoder depths with decayed weights) to establish a frame-agnostic perceptual prior, followed by embodied fine-tuning where only the DTE is updated to learn temporal fusion without disrupting the pretrained single-frame representations.

## Results

On monocular SSC, TGSFormer achieves 64.42 IoU and 54.73 mIoU on Occ-ScanNet, surpassing the previous best (SplatSSC) by 1.59% IoU and 2.90% mIoU, with even larger margins on Occ-ScanNet-mini (66.19 IoU, 55.82 mIoU vs. 61.47/48.87). On embodied SSC (EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet), TGSFormer reaches 54.42 IoU and 45.29 mIoU, outperforming the prior SOTA (RoboOcc) by 1.10% IoU and 1.20% mIoU while using over 20% fewer Gaussian primitives. The method also demonstrates strong scalability, with performance gains widening as the dataset shrinks from full to mini splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGSFormer outperforms all nine compared baselines on both monocular and embodied benchmarks. The closest competitor is SplatSSC [25], the depth-guided Gaussian splatting method it builds upon, which TGSFormer surpasses by 1.59% IoU / 2.90% mIoU on Occ-ScanNet and 4.72% / 6.95% on the mini split, confirming that the temporal memory and confidence-aware fusion add substantial value beyond the local depth-guided prediction. Among embodied-specific baselines, RoboOcc [45] is the strongest prior method (53.30 IoU, 44.05 mIoU on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet), and TGSFormer improves upon it by 1.10% IoU and 1.20% mIoU while using over 20% fewer primitives. The gap over earlier Gaussian-based methods (GaussianFormer [11], EmbodiedOcc [38], EmbodiedOcc++ [34]) is particularly large (10–23% IoU), highlighting the benefit of depth-guided initialization combined with persistent memory. Notably, the ablated variant TGSFormer-C (which simply concatenates historical Gaussians without the DTE/CAVF pipeline) drops to 41.95 IoU in the embodied setting, underscoring that the principled temporal fusion mechanism—not just the local predictor—is critical for long-horizon consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGSFormer unifies three previously separate threads: the depth-guided Gaussian lifting of SplatSSC for local SSC, the frame-agnostic persistent-memory paradigm of EmbodiedOcc for online embodied prediction, and the Gaussian primitive representation of GaussianFormer. Its key conceptual departure is replacing the bounded-volume random initialization used by prior embodied methods with depth-guided sparse initialization, then maintaining a compact global memory through confidence-weighted feature association rather than frame-to-frame coherence or simple concatenation. The confidence-aware cross-attention and voxel fusion modules are novel architectural contributions that regulate information flow and primitive density, bridging the gap between local depth-guided SSC and unbounded embodied exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in embodied 3D perception: how to maintain a growing scene representation without unbounded memory growth or redundancy, while preserving temporal consistency. The two-stage training recipe (monocular pretraining followed by selective DTE fine-tuning) and the confidence-weighted voxel fusion offer reusable design patterns for future memory-driven 3D perception systems. Limitations include evaluation restricted to indoor ScanNet-based datasets, a strong dependency on DepthAnythingV2 quality, and the voxel-granularity trade-off in CAVF that may not transfer cleanly to outdoor or large-scale scenes. Open questions include how the framework behaves over very long exploration sequences, whether the depth prior can be replaced by learned geometry, and whether the confidence mechanism generalizes to multi-agent or multi-modal settings.
