## Problem

Existing document dewarping methods that incorporate local structural cues (text lines, layout elements) operate on a single dimension or treat layout categories in isolation, lacking mutual constraints between horizontal and vertical deformation features. Additionally, public training datasets such as Doc3D provide only global annotations (3D coordinates, UV maps) without fine-grained line-level supervision in both spatial directions, limiting the ability to learn directional deformation patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261467/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single distorted document RGB image resized to 448×448 and jointly predicts (1) a 2-channel 2D backward deformation field mapping each pixel in the flat image back to its location in the warped image, and (2) binary segmentation masks for horizontal and vertical lines at full resolution. The training dataset DocDewarpHV uses 512×512 resolution images with paired 3D coordinates, UV maps, and dual-dimension line masks, while evaluation benchmarks (DocUNet, DIR300, DocReal) use their native resolutions.

## Method

D2Dewarp employs a shared-encoder UNet with two parallel decoders that simultaneously segment horizontal lines (text lines, document/table/figure/paragraph top-bottom boundaries) and vertical lines (left-right boundaries) from the distorted image. A novel HV Fusion Module then integrates the two feature maps via 2D average pooling along X and Y axes, cross-directional mixed attention (letting horizontal features attend to vertical features and vice versa), same-direction self-attention, and sigmoid-gated reweighting to enforce complementary constraints between the two dimensions. The reweighted horizontal and vertical features are concatenated and passed through an upsampling head to regress the 2D deformation field. Training is end-to-end with a combined loss of L1 deformation-field error (weighted α=5) and multi-scale line losses (BCE + RDGR-style weighted L2) across four decoder layers. The authors also synthesize DocDewarpHV (114K images) using Blender rendering of Chinese and English documents with PaddleOCR-detected text lines and layout boundaries to generate the required dual-dimension line annotations.

## Results

On the DocUNet benchmark, D2Dewarp achieves the best CER (0.1543/0.1338 on 50/60 images) and competitive ED (656.30/351.08), outperforming all prior methods on OCR-based metrics. On DIR300, it attains the best AD (0.186), ED (466.94), and CER (0.168), while matching LA-DocFlatten on MS-SSIM (0.65). On the Chinese DocReal benchmark, D2Dewarp leads in MS-SSIM (0.58), LD (8.69), and AD (0.227) but trails DocReal [52] on ED (191.25 vs. 184.54) and CER (0.2588 vs. 0.2485). The model has 71.2M parameters and processes an image in 0.39 s on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D2Dewarp achieves the best CER on both 50- and 60-image DocUNet settings (0.1543/0.1338), beating the closest competitors DocScanner (0.1650/0.1490) and DocTLNet (–/0.1504) by 7.1% and 11.3% respectively on the 60-image CER. On DIR300, it outperforms DocTLNet (the prior best OCR method) by 3.2% on CER (0.168 vs. 0.178) and 5.1% on ED (466.94 vs. 482.57), while matching LA-DocFlatten on MS-SSIM (0.65) and slightly improving AD (0.186 vs. 0.195). On DocReal (Chinese), D2Dewarp leads in MS-SSIM (0.58 vs. 0.56 for DocReal), LD (8.69 vs. 9.83), and AD (0.227 vs. 0.238), but DocReal [52] retains an edge on ED (184.54 vs. 191.25) and CER (0.2485 vs. 0.2588). Against text-line-based methods (RDGR, DocGeoNet, FTDR), D2Dewarp improves CER by at least 9.5% on DocUNet, confirming the benefit of dual-dimension line guidance over horizontal-only text lines. The method trails UVDoc and DvD on MS-SSIM (0.50 vs. 0.55) and DocTLNet/LA-DocFlatten on LD (7.71 vs. 6.70/6.72) on DocUNet, which the authors attribute to the benchmark's text-sparse (figure-heavy) samples providing weaker line guidance. Overall, D2Dewarp's advantage is most pronounced on OCR metrics (ED, CER) and on the Chinese DocReal benchmark, while geometric similarity metrics (MS-SSIM, LD) remain competitive but not uniformly best.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the document dewarping lineage that has progressed from global 3D/UV coordinate regression (DewarpNet, UVDoc) to local text-line-aware methods (RDGR, DocGeoNet, FTDR) and layout-guided approaches (LA-DocFlatten). Its key conceptual departure is treating horizontal and vertical line features as complementary, mutually constraining signals rather than focusing on a single dimension, and fusing them via a coordinate-attention-inspired cross-directional module. The segmentation backbone is a modified UNet with dual decoders, and the line loss is directly adopted from RDGR, making the architectural novelty concentrated in the HV Fusion Module and the dual-dimension supervision strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of DocDewarpHV (114K bilingual images with fine-grained horizontal and vertical line annotations) addresses a concrete gap in the field: prior training datasets like Doc3D lack local line annotations, forcing methods to rely on coarser supervision. The dual-dimension formulation opens a path toward more structured geometric reasoning in dewarping, though the paper itself acknowledges that background text lines can trigger erroneous segmentation and that text-sparse documents receive weaker line guidance. An open question is whether incorporating global features (foreground masks, UV maps) alongside the local line signals could resolve the background-interference failure mode without sacrificing the fine-grained benefits.
