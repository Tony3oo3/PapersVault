## Problem

Latent diffusion models rely on a pretrained autoencoder whose lossy reconstruction caps sample fidelity, removes high-frequency details, and introduces distribution shift between the reconstruction and generative objectives. Revisiting pixel-space diffusion is blocked by a fundamental trade-off: aggressive patchification weakens per-pixel modeling, while near-pixel granularity makes global attention quadratically expensive, so no existing architecture efficiently models both global semantics and fine-grained texture in a single end-to-end pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes an ImageNet class label and a noisy RGB image (256×256 or 512×512) and outputs a denoised RGB image directly in pixel space with no VAE decode step. For text-to-image, a frozen Gemma-2 encoder produces text embeddings that condition only the patch-level pathway; the pixel-level pathway receives no text tokens directly, relying instead on semantic tokens propagated from the patch level. The text-to-image variant is pretrained at 1024×1024 resolution on ~26M image-text pairs, producing megapixel-scale RGB outputs.

## Method

PixelDiT uses a dual-level fully transformer architecture: a patch-level DiT with aggressive 16×16 patchification performs long-range attention on short token sequences to capture global layout, while a pixel-level pathway of PiT blocks refines per-pixel texture. Two key mechanisms make pixel-level modeling tractable: (1) pixel-wise AdaLN, where a linear projection from each semantic token produces independent scale/shift/gating parameters for every pixel within a patch, aligning per-pixel updates with global context; and (2) pixel token compaction, which compresses p² pixel tokens per patch into a single token before global attention and expands back afterward, reducing the attention sequence length by p²-fold (256× for p=16) and cutting FLOPs from 82,247 to 311 GFLOPs. Training uses Rectified Flow velocity-matching loss in pixel space plus a REPA alignment objective against frozen DINOv2 features at the mid-level patch pathway. For text-to-image, the patch-level pathway is extended with MM-DiT blocks that process image and text tokens in separate streams with cross-attention, while the pixel-level pathway remains architecturally identical to the class-conditional model.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, PixelDiT achieves a gFID of 1.61 (320 epochs), sFID 4.68, IS 292.7, Precision 0.78, and Recall 0.64, outperforming all prior pixel-space models by a large margin (next best: EPG-XXL/16 at 1.87 gFID). On ImageNet 512×512, it reaches gFID 1.81 and Recall 0.67, surpassing latent-space models like REPA (2.08) and DiT-XL (3.04). For text-to-image at 1024², PixelDiT-1.3B scores 0.74 on GenEval and 83.5 on DPG-Bench, matching DALL-E 3 and approaching FLUX-schnell (0.71/84.8) while using only 1.3B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelDiT (gFID 1.61) decisively outperforms all prior pixel-space models on ImageNet-256: EPG-XXL/16 (1.87), PixNerd-XL (1.93), PixelFlow-XL (1.98), VDM++ (2.12), Simple Diffusion (2.44), FARMER (3.60), ADM-U (4.59), and JetFormer (6.64). It also surpasses several latent-space baselines including DiT-XL (2.27), SiT-XL (2.06), MaskDiT (2.28), and SVG-XL (1.92), but trails the strongest latent methods RAE-XL (1.13), LightningDiT (1.35), and REPA (1.42). On ImageNet-512, PixelDiT (1.81) beats REPA (2.08), DiT-XL (3.04), SiT-XL (2.62), and all pixel baselines, achieving the highest recall (0.67) among pixel methods. For text-to-image at 1024², PixelDiT (GenEval 0.74, DPG 83.5) surpasses SDXL (0.55/74.7), Playground v2.5 (0.56/75.5), Hunyuan-DiT (0.63/78.9), and DALL-E 3 (0.67/83.5), while remaining competitive with FLUX-schnell (0.71/84.8) at a fraction of the parameter count (1.3B vs. 12B). Notably, PixelDiT converges rapidly—reaching gFID 2.36 after only 80 epochs, already beating ADM-U's 400-epoch result.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelDiT builds directly on the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) for its patch-level semantic pathway, adopts the Rectified Flow training formulation (Liu et al., ICLR 2023), and borrows the MM-DiT dual-stream design from SD3 (Esser et al., ICML 2024) for text conditioning. It also incorporates recent architectural refinements from LightningDiT (RMSNorm, 2D RoPE) and the REPA representation-alignment objective with DINOv2 features. The key departure is from the dominant latent-diffusion paradigm: by eliminating the VAE entirely and introducing a dual-level (patch-level + pixel-level) transformer with pixel-wise AdaLN and pixel token compaction, PixelDiT addresses the quadratic-attention bottleneck that has made prior pixel-space diffusion methods (Simple Diffusion, PixelFlow, PixNerd, EPG) either convolutional, cascaded, or prohibitively expensive at high resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PixelDiT provides the strongest evidence to date that the VAE reconstruction bottleneck is not an unavoidable cost of efficient diffusion, challenging the field's default assumption that latent space is necessary for competitive image quality. The pixel token compaction mechanism (a learned compress-attend-expand pipeline) is a reusable technique that could benefit other high-resolution or per-pixel modeling tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 1024×1024 without further architectural changes, whether the ~0.5 FID gap to the best latent models (RAE-XL at 1.13) closes with more training, and whether the pixel-space advantage in fine-detail preservation (demonstrated qualitatively via FlowEdit editing) translates to quantitative editing benchmarks. The paper also leaves open whether the dual-level design is the optimal factorization or whether other decompositions (e.g., frequency-based) could be equally effective.
