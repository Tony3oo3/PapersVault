## Problem

Recovering global human and camera motion from monocular video is challenging because human and camera motions are entangled in image space, violating the static-scene assumption of standard SLAM. The common workaround of masking out dynamic humans becomes brittle when the foreground occupies most of the view or the background is too noisy, leading to tracking dropouts and loss of valuable geometric constraints. The paper asks whether dynamic humans can instead be reintegrated as informative landmarks for bundle adjustment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263399/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes monocular RGB video as input and consumes per-frame local HMR outputs (SMPL pose, shape, root orientation, and translation) from off-the-shelf models alongside DROID-SLAM's 64×48 feature-map correspondences. It produces refined per-frame camera extrinsics (rotation and translation) and a globally consistent world-coordinate human trajectory parameterized by SMPL variables (body pose, shape, root orientation, and root translation). Due to the 64×48 feature resolution, only the 24 SMPL kinematic joints are used as BA landmarks rather than the full 6890-vertex mesh, and metric scale is calibrated from background depth or foreground human contact joints.

## Method

HumanBA augments the standard DROID-SLAM bundle adjustment objective with human-landmark residuals by performing motion decoupling: it subtracts the HMR-estimated human-induced displacement from observed joint trajectories to isolate a pseudo-static, camera-induced component that serves as the BA target. To handle noisy global human estimates, the method applies motion refinement (robust z-score thresholding on world-frame velocity/acceleration, local-mean replacement of flagged increments, and Gaussian smoothing) before constructing targets. An adaptive weighting scheme assigns per-joint, per-pair confidence based on intervalwise maximum discrete acceleration, naturally up-weighting proximal joints and down-weighting distal end-effectors and long temporal spans. The total BA objective jointly minimizes background pixel reprojection errors and weighted human-landmark residuals over the same keyframe pair graph, and the process is iterative: after each BA update, camera extrinsics are used to recompute the world-frame human trajectory, refresh pseudo-static targets, and recompute weights for the next iteration (19 iterations total).

## Results

On EMDB2, HumanBA achieves the best global human motion metrics: W-MPJPE of 196.0 mm, WA-MPJPE of 70.1 mm, and RTE of 1.2%, outperforming the closest competitor TRAM (222.4 / 76.4 / 1.4). For camera pose estimation, HumanBA attains an average ATE of 0.32 m and ATE-S of 0.61 m on EMDB2, and on the more challenging SLOPER4D dataset it reduces ATE-S from 4.05 m (TRAM) to 3.10 m while also lowering W-MPJPE from 736.90 to 674.45 mm. Local pose accuracy (PA-MPJPE) remains essentially unchanged at 38.6 mm, confirming that gains come from improved global camera alignment rather than better per-frame pose estimation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRAM [39], the strongest prior method, HumanBA wins on all global human metrics (W-MPJPE 196.0 vs 222.4 mm, WA-MPJPE 70.1 vs 76.4 mm, RTE 1.2% vs 1.4%) and on scale-aware camera error (ATE-S 0.61 vs 0.66 m on EMDB2; 3.10 vs 4.05 m on SLOPER4D), while PA-MPJPE is marginally worse (38.6 vs 38.1 mm) since local HMR is unchanged. Against WHAM [30], HumanBA reduces W-MPJPE by 44% (196.0 vs 354.8 mm) and RTE by 80% (1.2% vs 6.0%). Against SLAHMR [44], GLAMR [46], and TRACE [33], HumanBA achieves 3–9× lower W-MPJPE, confirming that explicit camera optimization via human-aware BA substantially outperforms learned local-to-global mappings. For camera-only baselines, HumanBA (avg ATE 0.32 m) dramatically outperforms raw DROID-SLAM (2.42 m) and ORB-SLAM2 (1.05 m) on EMDB2, and matches masked DROID-SLAM on average while fixing trajectory-jump failures on short and medium sequences.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumanBA builds directly on the motion-decoupling insight from BA-Track [3], which showed that dynamic objects can serve as BA constraints once camera-induced and object-induced motion are separated. However, BA-Track is object-agnostic and relies on generic 3D point tracking, whereas HumanBA exploits structured human priors (SMPL joints from off-the-shelf HMR models) to form pseudo-static landmarks. The paper departs from the dominant paradigm in human-centric SLAM (exemplified by TRAM [39] and masked DROID-SLAM) that treats foreground humans as noise to be suppressed, instead reintegrating them as informative constraints with adaptive acceleration-based weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mutual-benefit finding—that better camera trajectories from human-aware BA directly reduce world-coordinate human error without changing local HMR—highlights that global placement often dominates global human accuracy, a practical insight for the world-coordinate HMR community. Limitations remain: the method depends on HMR quality (occlusions, unusual apparel, fast articulations degrade anchors), the motion refinement only adjusts root translation, and evaluation is limited to single-person sequences on two datasets. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, whether richer human motion priors (beyond SMPL joints) could further tighten constraints, and whether the adaptive weighting generalizes to longer sequences or more aggressive camera motion.
