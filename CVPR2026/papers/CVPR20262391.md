## Problem

Gyro-based deblurring methods have not been widely studied for video, and the few existing approaches rely on simplified blur models, deconvolution, and ignore translational camera motion. No learning-based framework existed for gyro-based video deblurring, leaving a gap between the rich motion information available from onboard gyro sensors and the performance achievable by deep video restoration networks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262391/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 13 consecutive blurred video frames (256×256 patches during training, 512×512 at inference) along with gyro angular-velocity samples (400 FPS) and inter-frame optical flows estimated from the blurred frames via RAFT-small. It outputs deblurred video frames at the same resolution. Blur kernels are constructed as pixel-wise 2D trajectories (N=8 sampled displacement vectors per pixel) combining rotational and translational components, which are then encoded into feature maps consumed by the network.

## Method

GyroDVD introduces a decomposed camera-motion model that isolates translational motion from optical flow by subtracting the rotation-induced warp, yielding per-pixel blur kernels that capture both rotational (from gyro integration) and translational (from residual flow) displacement. The network follows an encoder-decoder design: the image encoder uses modulated deformable convolution whose offsets are predicted from separately encoded rotational and translational kernel features (fused in feature space via Eq. 9), while the video decoder extends ShiftNet's ShiftBlocks with KGS-Blocks that replace fixed spatial shifts with learnable, kernel-adaptive shift patterns predicted from the same blur kernel features. This hybrid design applies expensive deformable convolution only in the encoder and lightweight learned shifts in the decoder, keeping overhead low. The model is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on the newly introduced GyroVD-Syn dataset (63,200 blurred frames from 632 high-speed videos with synchronized gyro data), using AdamW with cosine annealing over 600K iterations.

## Results

On GyroVD-Syn, GyroDVD-128 achieves an average PSNR of 35.93 dB and SSIM of 0.9113, outperforming the strongest video deblurring baseline ShiftNet+ (35.31 dB / 0.9023) by 0.62 dB. On the challenging large-blur subset, GyroDVD-128 reaches 34.38 dB / 0.8855, while even the smallest variant GyroDVD-48 (33.54 dB / 0.8712) surpasses all compared methods. On GyroVD-Real (no-reference metrics), GyroDVD-128 achieves the best NIQE (3.31), BRISQUE (29.48), and TopIQ (0.4889) scores among all methods. The full model uses 17.15M parameters with an inference time of 0.202 s per frame (including 0.013 s for kernel construction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GyroDVD-128 outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric and subset. Against gyro-based image deblurring methods, the gains are largest: GyroDVD-128 exceeds GyroDeblur [60] by 3.59 dB (35.93 vs. 32.34 avg PSNR), EggNet [17] by 5.61 dB, and DeepGyro [35] by 5.80 dB, confirming that single-image deblurring remains highly ill-posed even with motion priors. Among video deblurring baselines, the closest competitor is ShiftNet+ [25] (35.31 dB avg), which GyroDVD surpasses by 0.62 dB; the gap widens to 1.03 dB over DSTNet+L [40] and 1.16 dB over RVRT [28]. Notably, even the lightweight GyroDVD-48 (3.22M params) beats all video baselines on the large-blur subset (33.54 dB vs. 33.51 dB for ShiftNet+), while the full GyroDVD-128 leads by 0.87 dB on that subset. On GyroVD-Real, GyroDVD-128 achieves the best no-reference scores (NIQE 3.31, BRISQUE 29.48, TopIQ 0.4889), outperforming RVRT [28] (the next best) by 0.16 in NIQE and 0.0335 in TopIQ. The ablation variant 'ShiftNet with k^rot' (34.55 dB) shows that simply concatenating rotation-only kernels to ShiftNet is insufficient, highlighting the value of the full decomposed motion model and KGS-Block architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

GyroDVD extends the ShiftNet [25] encoder-decoder architecture for video restoration by integrating blur kernel guidance into both the image encoder (via deformable convolution) and the video decoder (via the proposed KGS-Block with kernel-adaptive shift patterns). Conceptually, it departs from prior gyro-based deblurring methods [17, 35, 60] that rely solely on rotational motion from gyro sensors, introducing a decomposed motion model that also captures translational motion estimated from optical flow. The paper positions itself as the first learning-based framework for gyro-based video deblurring, extending the classical deconvolution-based approaches of Park et al. [14] and Arslan et al. [2] into the deep learning paradigm while removing their reliance on restrictive blur models and precise alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GyroVD dataset (63,200 synthetic + 10,000 real frames with gyro data) fills a critical gap as the largest and most realistic benchmark for gyro-based video deblurring, enabling future work in this underexplored area. The decomposed rotational/translational motion model provides a principled way to leverage hardware already present in consumer cameras without additional sensors. Key limitations include inference time (0.15–0.2 s/frame) that precludes on-device deployment, and sensitivity to optical flow estimation errors in the translational component. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences, whether the translational estimation can be made more robust without relying on potentially noisy flow from blurred frames, and how to reduce computational cost for real-time applications.
