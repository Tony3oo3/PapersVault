## Problem

Existing VUDA methods process all spatiotemporal tokens from source and target videos, including static background regions that are irrelevant to action recognition and amplify domain shifts. This content-agnostic tokenization simultaneously degrades adaptation quality and incurs unnecessary quadratic self-attention cost, yet computational efficiency in VUDA has been largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261822/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution (tubelet size 2, yielding Nt=8 temporal segments) from both labeled source and unlabeled target domains. Unlike standard ViT tokenization that produces a fixed number of tokens, LMFT outputs a variable-length token sequence per video, handled via block-diagonal attention masking to enable batched processing without padding. Target pseudo-labels are generated zero-shot by CLIP (ViT-B/16) using text prompts, filtered by a confidence threshold (γc=0.8), and the final output is a Top-1 action class prediction.

## Method

LMFT computes per-token motion energy via L1 differences between temporally adjacent patch representations, then applies a binary threshold τ to retain only motion-rich, action-relevant tokens while discarding static background tokens. The threshold τ is learned end-to-end via REINFORCE policy gradient RL with a logistic-normal policy (parameters μ, logσ), where the reward balances domain adaptation loss against the token dropping ratio, making selection adaptive and content-dependent. The retained tokens are fed into a ViT-B/16 backbone (initialized with VideoMAE weights) trained jointly on source cross-entropy loss and confidence-filtered target pseudo-label loss. At test time, a deterministic threshold is obtained via Monte Carlo estimation of the policy's expected value (K=100 samples), incurring no additional deployment overhead.

## Results

On Daily-DA (12 adaptation settings), LMFT achieves 64.5% average Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the previous SOTA UNITE (59.2%) by 5.3% and the Source Only baseline (52.3%) by 12.2%. On UCF-HMDB_full, it reaches 96.4% average (vs. UNITE 93.8%, UDAVT 94.6%), and on ActorShift it attains 88.4% average, surpassing even the Target Only upper bound (81.1%) by 7.3%. In terms of efficiency, LMFT achieves a 1.4× training speedup and 0.82× inference cost relative to standard ViT-B/16, while reducing training time by ~10× compared to UNITE (2,784 s vs. 27,426 s on M→H).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMFT outperforms the strongest prior VUDA method, UNITE [22], by 5.3% on Daily-DA (64.5 vs. 59.2), 2.6% on UCF-HMDB_full (96.4 vs. 93.8), and 12.0% on ActorShift (88.4 vs. 76.4). It also substantially surpasses source-free VUDA methods: DALL-V [34] (51.4% avg on Daily-DA), STHC [16] (39.2%), EXTERN [33] (39.6%), and ATCoN [31] (33.5%). On UCF-HMDB_full, LMFT beats UDAVT [6] (94.6%) and CO²A [5] (91.8%), and on ActorShift it exceeds MM-SADA [20] (62.6%) and Zhang et al. [35] (67.3%). Notably, LMFT surpasses the Target Only upper bound on ActorShift (88.4% vs. 81.1%), demonstrating that domain adaptation is particularly beneficial in data-scarce cross-species settings. Among token reduction alternatives, LMFT achieves the best accuracy–speed tradeoff: it outperforms ToMe [2] (72.5% vs. 74.2% on M→H with 0.9× vs. 1.4× speedup), PruMerge [24] (71.7% with 1.85× cost), and DivPrune [1] (72.9% but 0.22× speedup due to 16,961 s training time). The method remains below the Target Only baseline on Daily-DA (64.5% vs. 82.7%) and UCF-HMDB_full (96.4% vs. 98.1%), as expected when target labels are unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: ViT-based video UDA (UNITE, DALL-V, UDAVT) and token reduction for efficient transformers (ToMe, PruMerge, DivPrune, RLT). It departs from prior VUDA methods by arguing that processing all spatiotemporal tokens is suboptimal—static background tokens amplify domain shift and waste compute—and from prior token reduction methods by introducing a motion-aware, RL-learned threshold rather than fixed ratios or similarity-based merging. The motion intensity estimation directly borrows the temporal-difference idea from RLT [4], but repurposes it for domain adaptation rather than pure speedup, and the REINFORCE-based threshold learning is a novel application to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic efficiency analysis in VUDA, demonstrating that a simple motion-based token filter can simultaneously improve accuracy and cut training time by 10–20× compared to the prior SOTA (UNITE), which is practically significant for real-world deployment. An open question is whether the benefit generalizes beyond action recognition to other video understanding tasks (e.g., video QA, temporal grounding) where static context may be more informative. The RL mechanism for a single scalar threshold, while effective, raises questions about whether simpler adaptive schemes (e.g., quantile-based thresholds) could achieve similar results with less overhead, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or higher resolutions where the token count grows substantially.
