## Problem

Current video VLMs process video passively by unrolling it into static frames and reasoning in a single pass, ignoring temporal continuity and cross-modal (audio-visual) signals. This brute-force approach fails on long narratives, subtle social dynamics, and tasks requiring integration of what is seen with what is heard. The paper argues that a hippocampus-inspired cross-modal predictive sensing mechanism is needed to shift VLMs from passive ingestion to active, selective co-reasoning across modalities.

## Input / Output

HippoVLM consumes video frames (up to 16 or 64 frames at 256×28×28 or 512×28×28 pixels), a raw audio stream encoded by Whisper-v3, and a text question. Inference is two-stage: Stage 1 independently generates concise audio and video summaries relevant to the question, and Stage 2 jointly reasons over these summaries alongside the original video frames to produce a final answer wrapped in structured tags ( for intermediate reasoning, <info> for extracted multimodal insights, and <answer> for the final prediction). The model also produces cross-modal temporal predictions (future/past 10-second summaries) as auxiliary outputs during training.

## Method

HippoVLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL or Qwen3-VL with an added Whisper-v3 audio encoder, and employs a two-stage pipeline that decouples sensing from reasoning. The core training innovation is HippoSense, a cross-modal temporal prediction objective where the model learns to reconstruct a future or past audio summary from video context (L_VAS) and vice versa (L_AVS), jointly optimized with standard SFT loss at weight 0.1. Post-training uses VANAO, a contrastive RL algorithm built on GRPO that introduces a Negative-aware Reward: unimodal baselines (video-only and audio-only accuracy) are pre-computed, and a group-level bonus is granted only when the joint reasoning policy strictly outperforms both unimodal shortcuts, explicitly penalizing modality collapse. The entire system is trained on the newly curated Hippo-Think dataset (10K videos, 50K CoT annotations) with a consensus-based quality filter requiring three consistent Gemini-2.5-Pro generations per sample.

## Results

HippoVLM-8B (Qwen3-VL base, 64 frames) achieves 62.7 on VideoMMMU, 70.4 on Video-MME (w/o sub), 72.0 on VNBench, and 73.4 on Social-IQ 2.0, surpassing all open-source baselines on every benchmark. Notably, it exceeds GPT-4o on VideoMMMU (62.7 vs. 61.2) and VNBench (72.0 vs. 66.7), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on VNBench (72.0 vs. 64.4) and Social-IQ (73.4 vs. 71.8). The 7B variant (Qwen2.5-VL base, 64 frames) also leads all open-source models, scoring 53.5 / 68.8 / 70.2 / 71.2 across the four benchmarks, while using only 16 frames during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HippoVLM-8B outperforms all open-source baselines on all four benchmarks, with the largest margins on VNBench (+2.0 over InternVL3.5-8B, +5.8 over Qwen3-VL-8B) and Social-IQ (+5.5 over Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, +15.5 over Qwen3-VL-8B). Against the strongest open-source competitor AVoCaDO-7B, HippoVLM-8B leads by 7.9 on VideoMMMU (62.7 vs. 54.8) and 7.2 on Social-IQ (73.4 vs. 66.2). Versus closed-source models, HippoVLM-8B surpasses GPT-4o on VideoMMMU (62.7 vs. 61.2) and VNBench (72.0 vs. 66.7), and beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on VNBench (72.0 vs. 64.4) and Social-IQ (73.4 vs. 71.8), though it trails both on Video-MME (70.4 vs. 71.9 for GPT-4o, 75.0 for Gemini-1.5-Pro). The 7B model with only 16 frames already exceeds Video-R1-7B (52.4) and VideoChat-R1.5-7B-M (51.4) on VideoMMMU, and surpasses Cambrian-S-7B by 11.3 points on VideoMMMU (49.9 vs. 38.6), demonstrating that the HippoSense + VANAO training pipeline yields substantial gains even at reduced frame budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video VLM line of work (Qwen2.5-VL, VideoLLaMA, Video-R1) by introducing a hippocampus-inspired cross-modal prediction objective (HippoSense) that shifts training from passive temporal frame processing to active audio-visual co-reasoning. The RL post-training stage builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) and its VLM adaptation in R1-V, but adds a novel negative-aware reward (VANAO) that explicitly penalizes modality collapse by comparing joint-reasoning accuracy against unimodal baselines. Conceptually, the work aligns with the 'supersensing' framing from Cambrian-S but departs from its focus on spatial/next-frame prediction by centering cross-modal temporal prediction as the core learning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in video VLMs: the inability to actively integrate audio and visual streams for nuanced social and behavioral reasoning, as evidenced by the strong Social-IQ 2.0 results (73.4 vs. ~64 for comparable open models). However, practical limitations temper the impact: the two-stage pipeline nearly triples inference latency (18s vs. 7s), RL training was limited to 1,000 steps due to compute constraints, and the single-author, single-lab setting limits reproducibility confidence. Open questions include whether the cross-modal prediction objective generalizes beyond the 10-second temporal window used here, whether the hippocampal framing yields architectural insights beyond motivation, and whether the approach scales to longer videos or more modalities without the current frame-count bottleneck.
