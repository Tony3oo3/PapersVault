## Problem

Existing emotion recognition datasets are exocentric, consist of short isolated clips with exaggerated or staged expressions, and lack temporal continuity, making them unsuitable for training models deployable on smart glasses. No prior resource provides subject-wise, open-vocabulary emotion timelines from natural egocentric recordings, and current emotion LMMs process utterances in isolation, ignoring emotional carry-over effects and producing uninterpretable outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OSIRIS consumes synchronized 30-second segments of egocentric RGB video, eye infrared video, dialogue audio, multi-speaker dialogue text, and a retrieved personal emotion history (N_p prior textual emotions plus N_q multimodal memory queries with elapsed-time metadata). The output is a single textual response structured as a six-step chain-of-thought (visual scene, acoustic cues, dialogue context, eye behavior, prior emotions, final inference) followed by an open-vocabulary emotion description that encodes sentiment polarity, three-level intensity, start/end temporal boundaries, and the observable trigger.

## Method

OSIRIS is a five-step LMM pipeline built on a frozen LLaMA3 backbone fine-tuned with LoRA. Step 1 encodes video and eye infrared with frozen DINOv3, audio with frozen Whisper, and dialogue text via the LLM's own embedding layer. Step 2 projects all modalities into a shared embedding space through separate learnable adapters. Step 3 (Remember) maintains a personalized emotion log where each event stores an open-vocab description, a multimodal expression signature (modality-gated descriptors refined by cross-attention over N_ms learnable queries), and temporal metadata; at inference it retrieves the N_p most recent textual emotions and N_q multimodal queries to model emotional carry-over. Step 4 (SENSE) generates structured CoT supervisory labels by feeding human affective captions, LMM-generated visual/acoustic/eye descriptions, dialogue, and prior emotions into LLaMA3 as a cognitive proxy, producing a six-step reasoning chain that forces the model to interpret perceptual cues before predicting. Step 5 fine-tunes the LLM to generate this reasoning chain and the final emotion prediction, shifting the task from direct classification to cognitively grounded multimodal inference.

## Results

On OSMO-XSub (cross-subject), OSIRIS with all modalities achieves a mean improvement of +35.1 over the zero-shot LLaMA3 baseline, with headline scores of 77.6 HR / 62.6 SOS (open-vocab emotion recognition), 76.7 WAF (sentiment), 58.0 WAF (intensity), 51.2 mIoU (localization), and 86.8 average LLaMa-Judge score (reasoning). On OSMO-XTime (cross-day), OSIRIS reaches +35.6 mean gain with 78.4 HR and 64.2 SOS. OSIRIS outperforms the previous SOTA fine-tuned AffectGPT by an average of +10.7 (XSub) and +10.1 (XTime) across all five tasks, with the largest margin in emotion reasoning (+14.1 on LLaMa-Judge metrics).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSIRIS (full) outperforms all six baselines across every task on both XSub and XTime protocols. Against the strongest fine-tuned competitor AffectGPT, OSIRIS gains +10.9 HR, +9.4 SOS, +9.2 SA WAF, +10.4 IP WAF, +7.7 EL mIoU, and +14.1 ER LLaMa-Judge average on XSub. The zero-shot emotion-specialized LMMs (Emotion-LLaMa, E3-LLaMa, AffectGPT) all underperform the plain LLaMA3 text-only baseline on EL mIoU (19.4–28.7 vs. 25.4), confirming that models trained on staged/exocentric emotion data do not transfer to natural egocentric settings. Among zero-shot audio LMMs, DeSTA2.5-Audio leads (41.2 SOS) but still trails OSIRIS by 21.4 SOS. On cross-lingual transfer (Table 4), OSIRIS dominates all four language directions, e.g., achieving 85.1 HR on Mandarin→Mandarin vs. 74.0 for AffectGPT, and 65.3 HR on the harder Mandarin→English direction vs. 55.5 for AffectGPT. The ablation (Fig. 7a) confirms that the SENSE reasoning strategy contributes the single largest component gain (+8.2 mean over AffectGPT), followed by dialogue context (+7.7) and memory queries (+6.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of emotion recognition built on exo-centric, lab-controlled, or short-clip datasets (MELD, MAFW, MER-C) by reframing the problem as continuous, subject-wise emotion tracking from egocentric smart-glasses streams. It repurposes three existing egocentric datasets (EgoLife, Nymeria, AEA) that already satisfy the multimodal and in-the-wild requirements, adding a large-scale emotion annotation layer rather than collecting new recordings. Architecturally, OSIRIS combines ideas from LMM-based emotion models (AffectGPT, Emotion-LLaMA, E3-LLaMA) with a memory-augmented module for emotion carry-over and a SENSE chain-of-thought framework, but the specific integration of eye-infrared signals, dialogue history, and prior-emotion retrieval into a single egocentric tracker is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OSMO establishes the first large-scale egocentric emotion benchmark with subject-wise timelines, open-vocabulary labels, and cross-lingual (English/Mandarin) coverage, providing a much-needed testbed for deployable emotion tracking on wearable devices. The finding that cross-lingual transfer is governed more by subject diversity than data volume (282 vs. 6 subjects) challenges the common assumption that scale alone drives generalization in affective computing. Key limitations remain: the dataset focuses on daily social interactions and may underrepresent extreme or culturally specific emotions, and the authors themselves note that emotion understanding from egocentric signals is 'far from solved,' pointing to the need for broader contexts, additional languages, and physiological modalities.
