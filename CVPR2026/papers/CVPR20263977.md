## Problem

Existing trajectory prediction methods are trained and evaluated on fixed-length observations, but real-world driving frequently yields variable-length, incomplete histories (e.g., newly entered vehicles or tracking losses). The common one-shot strategy of directly mapping short-observation features to a canonical representation struggles with short trajectories due to large information gaps, causing significant performance degradation that propagates to downstream planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263977/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes variable-length agent trajectories (with the first v·ΔT timesteps omitted, where v ranges over admissible omission counts) and a vectorized HD map as input, and produces multimodal future trajectories via a shared decoder. On Argoverse 2, observation lengths span {10, 20, 30, 40, 50} timesteps with a 60-step prediction horizon; on Argoverse 1, lengths span {5, 10, 15, 20} with a 30-step horizon. PRF is inserted between an existing encoder and decoder, so it is plug-and-play and adds no inference cost from the RPM (which is training-only).

## Method

PRF replaces one-shot feature mapping with a cascade of retrospective units, each bridging a small ΔT-step gap so that learning difficulty is decomposed. Each unit contains a Retrospective Distillation Module (RDM) that uses a residual-based strategy—gating the student feature element-wise and adding a learned residual for the omitted timesteps—to avoid feature conflicts from the shared encoder, and a Retrospective Prediction Module (RPM) that recovers the omitted history via a decoupled query design (anchor-free mode queries for coarse multimodal proposals, then anchor-based state queries refined with Mamba for temporal dynamics), providing implicit supervision back to RDM. A Rolling-Start Training Strategy (RSTS) generates multiple training samples from a single sequence by shifting the observation window, so shorter (harder) units receive proportionally more training data. At inference, only the RDM cascade runs iteratively to produce a standard-length feature for the shared decoder, while RPM is disabled entirely.

## Results

On Argoverse 2 variable-length prediction, DeMo-PRF achieves mADE6/mFDE6 of 0.617/1.183 at the shortest observation length (10 steps) and 0.596/1.142 at full length (50 steps), yielding an average gap of only 0.008/0.015 to the standard-length result. On Argoverse 1, DeMo-PRF reaches 0.602/0.952 at 5 steps and 0.568/0.909 at 20 steps (average gap 0.010/0.010). On the Argoverse 2 standard prediction leaderboard, DeMo-PRF attains the best b-mFDE6 of 1.81, mADE6 of 0.60, and mFDE6 of 1.14, outperforming DeMo+ReMo (1.84/0.61/1.17). On Argoverse 1, DeMo-PRF achieves the best mADE6 (0.70) and mFDE6 (1.03) among all single-model entries.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRF is evaluated against four variable-length prediction baselines (DTO, FLN, LaKD, CLLS) and two training baselines (Ori, IT) on both Argoverse 2 and Argoverse 1. DeMo-PRF outperforms all four variable-length baselines across every observation length on both datasets; the largest margin is at the shortest length (10 steps on Argoverse 2), where PRF achieves mADE6 of 0.617 versus 0.641 for CLLS (best baseline) and 0.672 for DTO. The average gap to standard-length prediction (Avg-Δ50) is 0.008/0.015 for PRF versus 0.012/0.019 for CLLS and 0.019/0.022 for FLN, confirming that progressive distillation narrows the length-induced degradation more effectively than one-shot mapping. On the Argoverse 2 standard prediction leaderboard, DeMo-PRF achieves the best b-mFDE6 (1.81), mADE6 (0.60), and mFDE6 (1.14), beating DeMo+ReMo (1.84/0.61/1.17) and all other 14 compared methods. On Argoverse 1, DeMo-PRF attains the best mADE6 (0.70) and mFDE6 (1.03) but trails Tamba on b-mFDE6 (1.73 vs. 1.67) and MR6 (0.11 vs. 0.09), indicating a slight trade-off in probability calibration on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-shot mapping paradigm in variable-length trajectory prediction (DTO, FLN, LaKD, CLLS), which directly projects features from incomplete observations to a canonical representation. Instead, PRF introduces a progressive cascade of retrospective units, each bridging only a small temporal gap (ΔT steps), thereby decomposing a large information gap into a sequence of smaller, more tractable alignment problems. The framework is explicitly plug-and-play, operating between the encoder and decoder of existing backbones (QCNet, DeMo), and incorporates Mamba-based sequence modeling in its Retrospective Prediction Module—a relatively recent architectural choice for trajectory tasks. The Rolling-Start Training Strategy repurposes the framework's native ability to handle shorter inputs as a data augmentation mechanism, a use not explored in prior variable-length methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Variable-length observations arise routinely in deployed autonomous driving systems (newly entering agents, tracking loss), yet most SOTA predictors degrade sharply under such conditions; PRF narrows this gap to near-zero with a single model, avoiding the multi-model overhead of Isolated Training. The plug-and-play design and modest inference cost (~0.03 s per additional retrospective stage) make it practical for integration into existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the progressive cascade scales gracefully to even shorter observations (e.g., 1–2 timesteps), how it behaves under real-time latency constraints in closed-loop driving, and whether the distillation strategy transfers to other modalities (e.g., LiDAR point clouds or camera-based perception).
