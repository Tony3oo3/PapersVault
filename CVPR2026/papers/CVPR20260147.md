## Problem

Existing multi-view contrastive clustering methods are structure-agnostic: they rely on cosine similarity, k-NN, or pseudo-labels for positive pair selection but fail to preserve the underlying geometric manifold of the data, leading to fragmented cluster boundaries and poor intra-cluster compactness. Additionally, these methods lack a unified consensus representation that captures global semantic consistency across heterogeneous views, resulting in suboptimal cross-view fusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes V multi-view feature matrices (each n×d_v with varying dimensions per view) and produces a consensus embedding matrix P ∈ R^{n×K} from which final cluster assignments are extracted via k-means. Each view's latent representation is passed through a prediction head to yield a K-dimensional probability simplex, which is then orthonormalized via differentiable Cholesky decomposition to form the spectral embedding. The consensus matrix is initialized as the average of all view-specific embeddings and refined through contrastive objectives during training.

## Method

MCSF integrates deep spectral embedding directly on encoder outputs by constructing a Gaussian-kernel similarity graph from latent representations and minimizing a weighted pairwise distance loss (equivalent to the graph Laplacian objective), while enforcing orthonormality through a Cholesky-based parameterization on a softmax probability matrix—eliminating the need for auxiliary Siamese networks used in prior deep spectral methods. On top of this structural regularization, a multi-hierarchical contrastive loss jointly optimizes three complementary objectives: view-specific structure preservation (VSP), view-consensus alignment (VCA), and consensus structure refinement (CSR), with positive pairs mined from a fused cross-view KNN graph. The total training objective combines reconstruction loss, spectral loss, and the multi-hierarchical contrastive loss, and the paper provides theoretical guarantees showing that minimizing the contrastive loss maximizes mutual information at all three hierarchical levels, ensuring the consensus representation captures cluster structure superior to any single view.

## Results

MCSF achieves the best ACC on all eight benchmark datasets, with particularly strong margins on COIL-20 (93.75% vs. 82.71% for DCMVSC, +11.04%) and NUS-WIDE (46.12% vs. 36.44% for OMVCDR, +9.68%). On large-scale datasets, MCSF reaches near-perfect scores: 99.90% ACC on Hdigit (10K samples) and 99.98% ACC on CIFAR-100 (50K samples). On the challenging 6-view NUS-WIDE dataset, where all methods struggle, MCSF still leads by over 9% ACC over the next-best method. On Extended YaleB with substantial lighting noise, MCSF (81.72% ACC) outperforms the graph-based UMCGL (81.05%) and contrastive CVCL (48.41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCSF achieves the highest ACC on all eight datasets, beating the strongest baseline by 0.12–11.04 percentage points. The largest gains appear on COIL-20 (+11.04% over DCMVSC) and NUS-WIDE (+9.68% over OMVCDR), where structure-aware spectral regularization and multi-hierarchical contrastive alignment are most impactful. On 3Sources, MCSF narrowly edges out PCMVSC (94.67% vs. 94.08% ACC), the closest competition in the table. On Extended YaleB, MCSF (81.72%) surpasses UMCGL (81.05%) and dramatically outperforms CVCL (48.41%), demonstrating robustness to lighting-induced noise. On CIFAR-100 (50K samples), MCSF reaches 99.98% ACC while four baselines (LPGMVC, PCMVSC, DCMVSC, UMCGL) fail due to out-of-memory, highlighting MCSF's scalability advantage from eliminating auxiliary Siamese networks. On the inherently difficult 6-view NUS-WIDE dataset, all methods score below 50% ACC, but MCSF still leads by a clear margin, confirming the benefit of the consensus representation for highly heterogeneous views.

## Relation to Prior Work

MCSF sits at the intersection of deep spectral clustering (SpectralNet, MvSCN) and contrastive multi-view clustering (MFLVC, CVCL, DCMVSC), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior deep spectral methods that rely on auxiliary Siamese networks to construct similarity graphs, MCSF integrates spectral embedding directly into the encoder via a probability-matrix parameterization with differentiable Cholesky orthonormalization. Unlike prior contrastive MVC methods that the authors characterize as 'structure-agnostic' (relying on cosine similarity, KNN, or pseudo-labels for pair selection), MCSF imposes explicit manifold-preserving spectral regularization and introduces a three-level contrastive hierarchy (VSP, VCA, CSR) that goes beyond pairwise view alignment to enforce a unified consensus structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first unified framework combining deep spectral embedding with multi-hierarchical contrastive learning for MVC, offering theoretical guarantees (mutual information lower bounds) for the consensus representation's clustering superiority over any single view. However, the practical sensitivity to batch size is a significant concern—on COIL-20, accuracy swings from 54% to 94% as batch size grows from 48 to 1440, suggesting the orthonormality constraint is fragile under mini-batch training. The theoretical analysis, while formally stated, contains a somewhat circular element (Theorem 2's I(P^(v);P^(v)) term is simply the entropy of P^(v)), and the method still requires k-means as a post-hoc step, leaving open whether end-to-end differentiable clustering could further improve results.
