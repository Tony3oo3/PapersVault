## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3DGS methods either require per-scene optimization (preventing generalization to novel scenes) or operate on only a few input views (limiting whole-scene reconstruction). No prior method simultaneously satisfies the five requirements of embodied perception: online streaming processing, near real-time speed, high generalizability, whole-scene understanding, and open-vocabulary capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of posed RGB (or RGB-D) images (typically >300 frames, resized to 384×512) along with a free-form text prompt, and outputs a semantic 3D Gaussian field where each Gaussian carries a compact language embedding stored as a sparse index-weight pair (10 numbers per Gaussian vs. 512–768 for a full CLIP vector). The reconstructed scene supports direct 3D semantic segmentation, 2D-rendered segmentation heatmaps, and novel-view color synthesis with depth. The fast variant achieves 5–6 FPS per-frame processing by swapping heavy 2D foundation models for real-time alternatives and removing the 3D U-Net.

## Method

EmbodiedSplat lifts 2D CLIP features directly into 3D Gaussians (2D-to-3D, the inverse of prior rasterization-based distillation) and stores them via an Online Sparse Coefficient Field with a CLIP Global Codebook: instance-level CLIP features from a segmentation model are appended to a growing codebook, and each Gaussian retains only L=6 index/weight slots (pruned to top L−1 by confidence), requiring no pretraining or per-scene optimization while preserving full CLIP open-vocabulary semantics. A 3D sparse U-Net with a temporal memory adapter aggregates the Gaussian point cloud to produce geometry-aware 3D features, trained via cosine similarity against the 2D CLIP features (no ground-truth labels needed). At inference, 2D and 3D classification probabilities are combined via a geometric mean, and a codebook-based cosine similarity trick reduces per-query complexity from O(MD) to O(KD + M(L−1)), enabling near real-time open-vocabulary 3D reasoning. The entire pipeline is built on pretrained FreeSplat++ with confidence-weighted online fusion and GRU-based latent updates across streaming frames.

## Results

On ScanNet (19 classes), EmbodiedSplat (RGB) achieves 46.22 mIoU, surpassing the strongest per-scene baseline Occam's LGS (30.49 mIoU) by 15.7 mIoU. The faster variant, EmbodiedSplat-fast (RGB), further improves to 52.12 mIoU on the same setting while maintaining feed-forward generalizability. Full-scene reconstruction of 363 images completes in 8 minutes (0.75 FPS), compared to 2–6 hours for per-scene optimization methods; the RGB-D fast variant reaches 5.18 FPS per-frame processing. On ScanNet++ (20 classes), EmbodiedSplat-fast (RGB-D) attains 51.09 mIoU, the best result across all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmbodiedSplat (RGB) outperforms all seven baselines on every benchmark. On ScanNet 10 classes, it achieves 49.81 mIoU versus 42.14 for the strongest per-scene 3D method (Occam's LGS), a 7.7 mIoU gap; the gap widens to 15.7 mIoU on ScanNet 19 classes (46.22 vs. 30.49). The 2D rasterization baselines (LangSplat, LEGaussians, Online-LangSplat) perform poorly under direct 3D referring (1–7 mIoU), confirming that feature distillation through rendering degrades per-Gaussian CLIP semantics. Among 3D baselines, Dr. Splat (39.21) and Occam's LGS (42.14) are the closest competitors on ScanNet 10 classes, while InstanceGaussian and OpenGaussian trail further behind (29.77 and 29.50). EmbodiedSplat-fast (RGB) extends the lead to 57.41 mIoU on ScanNet 10 classes, and with RGB-D input reaches 51.09 mIoU on ScanNet++ 20 classes—surpassing Dr. Splat's 39.85 by over 11 mIoU. Critically, all per-scene baselines require 2–6 hours of optimization per scene, whereas EmbodiedSplat reconstructs the full scene in 8 minutes and the fast variant processes at 5.18 FPS, making it the only method that is simultaneously generalizable, online, and near real-time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FreeSplat++, a feed-forward 3DGS reconstruction model, and endows it with open-vocabulary semantic understanding. It departs from prior semantic 3DGS methods in two key ways: (1) instead of the dominant 3D-to-2D rasterization-based feature distillation (LangSplat, LEGaussians, Online-LangSplat), it performs 2D-to-3D lifting of CLIP features directly onto Gaussians; and (2) unlike per-scene optimization methods (OpenGaussian, Dr. Splat, InstanceGaussian, Occam's LGS), it uses a fully feed-forward design with an online sparse coefficient field and CLIP Global Codebook that requires no per-scene training. The 3D U-Net with memory adapter for geometric-aware features draws from the 3D point cloud literature (Minkowski convolutions, EmbodiedSAM-style memory adapters), while the codebook-based cosine similarity trick is a novel efficiency contribution distinct from prior compression approaches like auto-encoders or product quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously satisfy all five embodied perception requirements (online, real-time, generalizable, whole-scene, open-vocabulary) for 3DGS-based scene understanding, making it directly applicable to robotic navigation and manipulation where an agent must build and query a 3D semantic map in near real-time during exploration. The sparse coefficient field with a scene-agnostic codebook offers a practical recipe for memory-efficient open-vocabulary 3D representations that could be adopted beyond this specific architecture. Key limitations include the significant performance drop in cross-domain transfer (especially Real-to-Sim) and the reliance on accurate depth estimation, which remains a bottleneck when sensor depth is unavailable. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes, how it handles dynamic objects, and whether the feed-forward paradigm can close the remaining gap to per-scene optimization in the most challenging settings.
