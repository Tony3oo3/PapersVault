## Problem

Existing self-supervised novel view synthesis models (e.g., RayZer) assume static scenes, so they fail on real-world dynamic inputs where both cameras and objects move, producing ghosting, hallucinated geometry, and unstable pose estimates. Moreover, no large-scale dataset of dynamic indoor sequences exists to train or systematically evaluate methods that separate static structure from transient objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260341/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 2–4 unposed, uncalibrated RGB images at 256×256 resolution from a dynamic scene and, in a single feed-forward pass, outputs (i) transient-free novel view renderings at a target camera, (ii) per-pixel motion masks at full H×W resolution, and (iii) per-view camera intrinsics and relative SE(3) poses. Internally, images are split into 16×16 patches and encoded into a 768-token scene representation; the motion head operates on the patch grid (h×w) before upsampling to pixel resolution via a DPT-style decoder.

## Method

WildRayZer extends RayZer with a learned motion estimator and a masked scene encoder, trained entirely self-supervised via an analysis-by-synthesis loop. A frozen static renderer produces pseudo motion masks by fusing DINOv3 semantic dissimilarity and SSIM appearance dissimilarity with adaptive weights, then applying K-means clustering across frames for cross-view consistency and GrabCut boundary refinement. The motion head fuses DINOv3 patch features, pre-camera-estimator image tokens, and Plücker-ray tokens through a 4-layer transformer with a DPT decoder to predict per-pixel motion logits. Training alternates between (a) distilling the motion head from pseudo-masks while freezing the renderer, and (b) training a masked renderer (dynamic tokens zeroed before scene encoding) while freezing the motion head, followed by joint fine-tuning; a COCO copy-paste augmentation injects synthetic transients to improve open-set robustness.

## Results

On D-RE10K (static-region metrics, 4 input views), WildRayZer achieves 22.38 PSNR, 0.773 SSIM, and 0.290 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest feed-forward baseline RayZer+SAV (20.73 PSNR, 0.711 SSIM, 0.308 LPIPS) by ~1.7 dB in PSNR. On D-RE10K-iPhone (full-image fidelity, 4 views), WildRayZer reaches 20.98 PSNR, 0.734 SSIM, and 0.298 LPIPS, surpassing the best optimization-based method Spotless-Splats (18.86 PSNR) by over 2 dB. For motion-mask quality on D-RE10K-Mask (2 input views), WildRayZer attains 53.9 mIoU and 85.1% Recall, substantially exceeding the best supervised baseline Segment Any Motion (31.9 mIoU, 47.2% Recall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildRayZer outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both benchmarks and all view counts (2, 3, 4). Among optimization-based methods, Spotless-Splats is the strongest (18.86 PSNR on D-RE10K-iPhone, 4 views) but still trails WildRayZer by 2.1 dB; WildGaussians achieves the best full-image PSNR among optimization methods (20.44) yet has the worst LPIPS (0.620), indicating poor perceptual quality. Among feed-forward RayZer variants, RayZer+SAV is the closest competitor (20.73 PSNR on D-RE10K, 4 views), but the paper attributes its gap to the inability of naive token masking to perform learned cross-view completion, producing blurry artifacts in occluded regions. RayZer+MegaSAM (20.66 PSNR) suffers from diffuse mask boundaries under sparse views, while RayZer+Co-Seg (18.67 PSNR) over-masks and introduces severe artifacts. For motion-mask quality (Table 4), WildRayZer's self-supervised estimator (53.9 mIoU, 85.1% Recall at 2 views) surpasses the best supervised method Segment Any Motion (31.9 mIoU, 47.2% Recall) by over 22 mIoU points, demonstrating that the analysis-by-synthesis pseudo-labeling strategy is more effective than explicit video-based motion tracking in the sparse-view, unposed regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

WildRayZer extends RayZer [27], a self-supervised transformer-based NVS model that requires no 3D supervision or camera poses, by lifting its static-scene assumption to handle dynamic, in-the-wild inputs. It departs from the optimization-based dynamic NVS line (NeRF On-the-Go, WildGaussians, T-3DGS, Spotless-Splats) by being entirely feed-forward and self-supervised, and from prior moving-object segmentation by deriving motion evidence from rendering residuals of a static multi-view renderer rather than from optical flow or video trajectories. The analysis-by-synthesis pseudo-labeling strategy and copy-paste augmentation draw on instance segmentation techniques [16, 84], repurposing them for the NVS setting where no ground-truth masks exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 15K-sequence D-RE10K dataset closes a critical gap: existing dynamic NVS benchmarks contain fewer than 20 sequences, making systematic training and evaluation of transient-aware methods nearly impossible. The finding that even accurate off-the-shelf motion masks (e.g., from SAV) produce blurry artifacts when naively applied to a static renderer—requiring a *learned* masked renderer for proper cross-view completion—challenges the assumption that dynamic NVS reduces to 'segment and remove.' Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes, non-rigid deformations, and longer video sequences, and whether the 256² resolution and 2–4 view constraints can be relaxed without degrading the self-supervised signal.
