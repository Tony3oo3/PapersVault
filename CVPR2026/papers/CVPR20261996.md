## Problem

Automated AI-based surgical skill assessment is constrained by the absence of datasets containing realistic trainee errors and the multi-view variability needed to train robust computer vision models for open-surgery hand movements. Existing surgical video datasets are either single-view (endoscopic or robotic) or lack clinically validated error taxonomies, while multi-view action recognition datasets provide no clinical specificity or surgical error supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261996/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides five hardware-synchronized RGB streams (25 fps, 640×480 px) per recording, captured by a rigid-frame camera rig surrounding an ex vivo chicken tissue workspace. Each frame carries two annotation layers: one of 15 gesture primitives (5 incision, 10 suturing) with temporal boundaries, and one of 8 clinically validated error categories, enabling both single-label gesture classification and multi-label error detection. Evaluation protocols span single-view (one camera), multi-view (all five), and cross-view generalization (train on C1–C3, test on unseen C4–C5).

## Method

SHANDS is a dataset and benchmark whose technical contribution lies in the capture protocol, annotation taxonomy, and standardized evaluation design rather than a new architecture. Five Canon PowerShot cameras are hardware-synchronized via CHDK to guarantee frame-accurate alignment without post-hoc registration, and 52 participants (20 experts, 32 trainees) each perform three standardized trials of incision and suturing on ex vivo tissue. For benchmarking, the authors fine-tune CNN backbones (R3D, SlowFast, X3D-M) and transformer backbones (TimeSformer, ViViT, MViTv2, VideoMAE, InternVideo2) pretrained on Kinetics-400 or ImageNet-21K for single-view recognition, and multi-view architectures (MVAction, ViewCLR, ViewCon, DVANet) pretrained on NTU RGB+D 120 for cross-view fusion. The key empirical finding is that multi-view fusion yields consistent gains over single-view (DVANet reaches 73.9% Top-1 vs. 68.9% for the best single-view model), and DVANet's disentangled view-invariant representations achieve the best cross-view transfer with 94.1% performance retention on unseen cameras.

## Results

On single-view gesture recognition (15 classes), transformer backbones outperform CNNs: InternVideo2 achieves the best Top-1 accuracy of 68.9%, followed by VideoMAE-L (65.9%) and VideoMAE-B (63.4%), while the best CNN (SlowFast) reaches only 55.7%. In the multi-view setting (5 synchronized cameras), DVANet attains 73.9% Top-1 and 0.736 Macro-F1, surpassing ViewCon (72.5%/0.723), ViewCLR (71.1%/0.717), and MVAction (70.4%/0.704). For error detection on the trainee subset, DVANet with all five views reaches 68.5% accuracy versus 60.4% for single-view VideoMAE-B. In cross-view generalization (trained on C1–C3, tested on unseen C4–C5), DVANet retains 94.1% of its seen-view performance (68.5% vs. 72.8%), outperforming ViewCon's 92.4% retention (63.2% vs. 68.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 12+ state-of-the-art video recognition architectures across single-view, multi-view, and cross-view protocols. Among single-view CNNs, SlowFast (55.7%) outperforms X3D-M (53.9%) and R3D (52.3%), but all are surpassed by transformer backbones: TimeSformer (58.4%), ViViT-B (59.2%), MViTv2-B (61.8%), VideoMAE-B (63.4%), VideoMAE-L (65.9%), and InternVideo2 (68.9%). In the multi-view setting, DVANet achieves the best Macro-F1 of 0.736, beating ViewCon (0.723) by 1.3 points, ViewCLR (0.717) by 1.9 points, and MVAction (0.704) by 3.2 points, confirming that disentangled view-invariant representations are most effective for surgical gesture recognition. For error detection, DVANet (68.5%) outperforms ViewCon (66.2%) and single-view VideoMAE-B (60.4%), with per-class accuracy ranging from 77.3% (improper grip) to 60.9% (incorrect needle handling). In cross-view transfer, DVANet retains 94.1% of its seen-view accuracy on unseen cameras, compared to 92.4% for ViewCon, making it the most robust architecture for deployment under varying camera setups.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHANDS carves new ground at the intersection of surgical video datasets and multi-view action recognition. Prior surgical datasets (JIGSAWS, Cholec80, M2CAI16) are either robotic, single-view endoscopic, or lack systematic error taxonomies, while multi-view action benchmarks (NTU RGB+D, Assembly101) provide viewpoint diversity without clinical specificity or expert-validated error labels. The paper repurposes established video recognition backbones (VideoMAE, TimeSformer, SlowFast) and multi-view fusion architectures (DVANet, ViewCon, ViewCLR) as baselines rather than proposing new methods, positioning its contribution squarely in dataset construction and benchmark protocol design for open-surgery hand-gesture and error recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHANDS fills a concrete gap by providing the first synchronized multi-view RGB dataset with frame-level gesture primitives and a clinically validated error taxonomy for open manual surgery, enabling cross-view generalization studies that were previously impossible in this domain. The moderate benchmark accuracies (55–69% single-view, 70–74% multi-view) and the persistent difficulty of subtle errors (needle handling ~61%, force application ~62%) highlight that fine-grained surgical skill assessment remains far from solved. Open questions include whether the 5–6% cross-view performance gap can be closed with stronger view-invariance constraints, how the taxonomy generalizes beyond incision and suturing to more complex procedures, and whether the proposed teacher-student distillation from multi-view to single-view can achieve comparable performance with deployable hardware.
