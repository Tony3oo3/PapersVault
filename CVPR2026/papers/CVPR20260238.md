## Problem

Pixel-space diffusion models avoid the VAE information bottleneck of Latent Diffusion Models but face a severe scalability problem: capturing fine details requires small patches (2×2 or 4×4), causing sequence length to grow quadratically with resolution and making high-resolution synthesis computationally intractable. The paper asks whether one can achieve LDM-level computational efficiency in pixel space without sacrificing the quality gains that pixel-space operation provides.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260238/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (one of 1000 ImageNet categories) and an isotropic Gaussian noise tensor of shape H×W×3 as input, and iteratively denoises it over 75–100 Euler solver steps to produce a full-resolution RGB image (256×256 or 512×512). The image is partitioned into non-overlapping 16×16 patches for the DiT backbone, so the effective token sequence length matches that of latent-space DiT models. Classifier-free guidance (scale 4.0) is applied during sampling, and no VAE encoder/decoder is involved at any stage.

## Method

DiP decouples generation into a global stage and a local stage. A DiT backbone (DDT variant) processes the noisy image as a sequence of 16×16 patches, modeling long-range dependencies and global structure with a sequence length comparable to LDMs. A lightweight Patch Detailer Head—a shallow convolutional U-Net with 4 down/up-sampling stages (only 0.3% additional parameters)—is co-trained end-to-end with the DiT and placed post-hoc after the final DiT block. For each patch, the Head receives the DiT's context-aware feature vector (reshaped to D×1×1) concatenated channel-wise at the U-Net bottleneck, along with the original noisy pixel patch, and predicts the per-patch noise component. The key insight is that DiT's self-attention flattens intra-patch spatial structure into a single token, losing local inductive bias; the convolutional U-Net re-injects locality and translation-equivariance priors to restore high-frequency details that the Transformer alone cannot synthesize.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, DiP-XL/16 achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.79 (600 epochs, 100 NFE) and 1.98 (320 epochs, 75 NFE), surpassing all latent and pixel diffusion baselines. Inference latency is 0.92s (100 steps) or 0.70s (75 steps), making it >2.2× faster than DiT-XL (2.09s) and >10× faster than PixelFlow-XL/4 (7.50s) at matched FID. The model uses only 631M parameters with no VAE, compared to 675M+86M for DiT-XL/SiT-XL and 2.0–2.8B for other pixel-space models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiP outperforms all latent diffusion baselines: it beats DiT-XL (FID 2.27) by 0.48, SiT-XL (FID 2.06) by 0.27, and FlowDCN-XL (FID 2.00) by 0.21, while also being 2.2× faster in inference (0.92s vs 2.09s) and requiring no VAE. Among pixel-space models, DiP surpasses the previous best PixelFlow-XL/4 (FID 1.98) by 0.19 at 600 epochs, and achieves the same FID 1.98 at only 320 epochs with 10× lower latency (0.70s vs 7.50s). DiP also substantially outperforms ADM (FID 3.94), VDM++ (FID 2.12), SiD (FID 2.77), RIN (FID 3.42), Farmer/16 (FID 3.96), CDM (FID 4.88), and JetFormer-L (FID 6.64), while using fewer parameters (631M vs 1.9–2.8B for most pixel baselines). At 512×512 resolution, DiP-XL/32 achieves FID 2.31, beating DiT-XL (3.04), SiT-XL (2.62), FlowDCN-XL (2.44), and VDM++ (2.65) among the reported baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiP builds directly on the DiT/DDT Transformer backbone for global structure modeling but departs fundamentally from both Latent Diffusion Models (which rely on a VAE to compress images into latent space) and prior pixel-space diffusion models (which use small 2×2 or 4×4 patches, causing quadratic sequence-length growth). The key conceptual departure is the explicit decoupling of global layout (handled by a DiT operating on large 16×16 patches, matching LDM sequence lengths) from local detail synthesis (handled by a co-trained, 0.3%-parameter convolutional U-Net head). This positions DiP as a principled alternative to PixelNerd's NeRF-like rendering approach, arguing that the essential design principle is global-local separation rather than any specific rendering mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiP demonstrates that pixel-space diffusion can match or exceed LDM quality (FID 1.79 vs. 2.06 for SiT-XL) while being 10× faster than prior pixel-space methods and entirely VAE-free, removing the information-bottleneck and non-end-to-end training limitations of the dominant LDM paradigm. The finding that adding a tiny local inductive bias module outperforms scaling up the DiT backbone (in depth or width) challenges the default assumption that more parameters or layers are the primary lever for quality improvement in pixel-space generation. Open questions include whether this global-local decomposition transfers to text-to-image and text-to-video settings (noted as future work), and whether the approach generalizes beyond class-conditional ImageNet to more open-ended generation tasks.
