## Problem

Existing methods for improving spatial understanding in LVLMs depend on costly human annotation, proprietary model pipelines, or constrained 3D environments, limiting scalability to ordinary images. The paper asks whether intrinsic structural consistency in raw RGB and RGB-D images can provide verifiable supervision signals sufficient to drive reinforcement learning for spatial reasoning without any external annotation or tooling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261866/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw RGB images (COCO) and RGB-D images (DIODE, MegaDepth) and applies deterministic augmentations—patch shuffling, flipping, cropping, region labeling, and coordinate transformation—to automatically generate 81,053 question-answer pairs. At inference, the model receives an augmented image plus a text prompt and produces a structured response: a reasoning chain enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{}, enabling exact-match reward computation.

## Method

Spatial-SSRL defines five self-supervised pretext tasks split into depth-free (shuffled patch reordering, flipped patch recognition, cropped patch inpainting) and depth-based (regional depth ordering, relative 3D position prediction) categories, each yielding a deterministic ground-truth answer verifiable without human labels. Training proceeds in two stages: a brief SFT cold-start (~3,600 samples, 5 epochs) to stabilize output format, followed by Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) over all five tasks jointly with a reward of 0.9·R_Acc + 0.1·R_Fmt. The key differentiator is that all supervision is derived purely from pixel-level image structure via bijective permutations, flip operations, and rigid coordinate transforms, making the pipeline tool-free, fully automated, and trivially extensible with new pretext tasks without altering the RL infrastructure.

## Results

On seven spatial understanding benchmarks (image and video), Spatial-SSRL-3B achieves an average accuracy of 50.54%, improving +4.63% over Qwen2.5-VL-3B (45.91%), with the largest gain on Spatial457 (+12.37%) and VSI-Bench (+5.65%). Spatial-SSRL-7B reaches 56.58% average, a +3.89% improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (52.69%), and outperforms all three representative spatial models (SpatialLadder-3B: 45.09%, ViLaSR-7B: 51.25%, SpaceR-7B: 54.54%). On general VQA benchmarks, the 3B model gains +2.02% and the 7B model gains +0.57%, confirming no regression in non-spatial capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spatial-SSRL-7B (56.58% avg) outperforms all three representative spatial models: SpaceR-7B (54.54%), ViLaSR-7B (51.25%), and SpatialLadder-3B (45.09%), with the largest margin over ViLaSR-7B (+5.33%). Against its direct baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B without reasoning (52.69%), Spatial-SSRL-7B gains +3.89% on average, with particularly strong improvements on QSpatial-plus (+7.93%) and Spatial457 (+8.67%). The 3B variant similarly beats Qwen2.5-VL-3B (45.91%) by +4.63% and outperforms SpatialLadder-3B (45.09%) by +5.45%. Notably, the baseline models degrade when prompted for explicit reasoning (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B drops from 45.91% to 44.85%), whereas Spatial-SSRL models consistently outperform their no-reasoning counterparts, indicating the RL training instills productive spatial reasoning. On general VQA, Spatial-SSRL maintains or slightly improves performance (+2.02% for 3B, +0.57% for 7B), confirming that spatial-focused RL does not sacrifice broader visual capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: self-supervised visual learning (pretext tasks like jigsaw puzzles, rotation prediction, and masked autoencoding) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for LVLMs. Prior spatial understanding work relies on external tools (detection, segmentation, rendering pipelines) or synthetic 3D environments to generate training signals, while recent RLVR methods for spatial reasoning (SpatialLadder, ViLaSR, SpaceR) are constrained by the specialized assets they depend on. Spatial-SSRL departs from both by showing that intrinsic image structure—patch permutations, flips, crops, depth ordering, and egocentric coordinate transforms—provides naturally verifiable supervision that plugs directly into GRPO without any external annotation or tooling, effectively repurposing the SSL pretext-task paradigm as a reward signal for policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that spatial understanding in LVLMs can be improved at scale using only raw RGB/RGB-D images, removing the cost and engineering burden of tool-heavy or simulation-based pipelines. The cross-modal transfer from image-based pretext tasks to video spatial understanding (VSI-Bench) suggests the learned representations are grounded in geometry rather than modality-specific patterns. Open questions include whether video-native pretext tasks (optical flow, temporal coherence) would further strengthen video spatial reasoning, whether the approach extends to other perceptual domains beyond spatial, and how the gains scale with larger model sizes or more diverse depth sources. The modest but consistent improvements across seven benchmarks and the preservation of general capabilities make this a low-risk, high-utility training recipe for practitioners.
