## Problem

Spatial intelligence in multimodal large language models has been studied almost exclusively from an understanding perspective (recognition, QA-style benchmarks), leaving the generative dimension—whether models can actively respect and manipulate 3D spatial constraints during image generation—unmeasured and unexplored. This paper asks whether modern unified multimodal models possess generative spatial intelligence (GSI), whether it can be quantified in a scalable, model-agnostic way, and whether targeted generative training on spatial editing transfers to downstream spatial understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264007/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a reference RGB image and a natural-language spatial editing instruction (e.g., 'Move the apple 15 cm left', 'Place the cup left of the plate') and must produce an edited image satisfying the specified 3D geometric transformation while preserving realism and semantic consistency. Spatial operations are formalized as structured 3D transformations Φ_3D over scene representations S = {O_i} ∪ {C}, where each object has center, size, and rotation, enabling quantitative evaluation via normalized translation error, geodesic rotation error on SO(3), and LPIPS-based locality scoring rather than purely qualitative assessment.

## Method

GSI-Bench comprises two complementary datasets: GSI-Syn (1,793 test samples + 10,500 training samples) built on AI2-THOR and MesaTask simulators with physics-validated execution and fully automated labeling, and GSI-Real (441 samples) curated from ScanNet++ using DetAny3D for 3D scene reconstruction, rule-based operation generation, MLLM-based (Qwen3-VL-235B) visualization-driven verification, and human review. Seven spatial operation categories (camera-relative move, object-relative place, rotation, receptacle placement, perspective control, removal, scaling) are defined with explicit 3D geometric formulations. For the fine-tuning study, BAGEL (a unified MLLM with self-attention between perception and generation) is fine-tuned on GSI-Syn training data using supervised learning, and evaluated on both synthetic and real benchmarks plus downstream understanding suites (OmniSpatial, SAT-Real). The evaluation protocol combines four metrics—Instruction Compliance, Spatial Accuracy, Edit Locality (masked LPIPS), and Appearance Consistency (MLLM-judged)—with dataset-specific locality gates using masked SSIM and LPIPS thresholds.

## Results

On GSI-Syn-Table, fine-tuning BAGEL on GSI-Syn yields a 48.74 average score, surpassing all baselines including NanoBanana (37.03) and GPT-Image (33.97), with the largest gain in Instruction Compliance (+23.50 over BAGEL). On GSI-Real, BAGEL+GSI-Syn improves from 28.46 to 36.28 average (+7.83), though it still trails the strongest open-source models Emu3.5 (43.52) and Qwen-Image-Edit (43.44). On GSI-Syn-Room, the fine-tuned model reaches 24.42 average, outperforming NanoBanana (21.29) and GPT-Image (10.03). Downstream spatial understanding also improves: OmniSpatial overall rises from 41.55 to 42.07, and SAT-Real overall jumps from 65.33 to 69.33 (+4.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAGEL+GSI-Syn decisively outperforms all baselines on GSI-Syn-Table (48.74 vs. NanoBanana 37.03, GPT-Image 33.97, Emu3.5 34.25), demonstrating that synthetic fine-tuning enables precise localized spatial edits that even proprietary models cannot match. On GSI-Real, however, the fine-tuned model (36.28) still lags behind Emu3.5 (43.52) and Qwen-Image-Edit (43.44), indicating that real-world spatial editing remains challenging despite sim-to-real transfer. On GSI-Syn-Room, BAGEL+GSI-Syn (24.42) edges out NanoBanana (21.29) and substantially exceeds GPT-Image (10.03), though gains are more modest due to greater scene complexity. Among open-source baselines, Emu3.5 is the strongest pre-fine-tuning performer on GSI-Real, benefiting from video-centric training that provides implicit spatial priors, while general-purpose editors (AnyEdit, Ultra, Omnigen2) score below 30 on all subsets, revealing a lack of 3D-aware inductive biases. On downstream understanding benchmarks, BAGEL+GSI-Syn improves over BAGEL on OmniSpatial Spatial Interaction (+2.00) and Perspective Taking (+1.07), and on SAT-Real Goal Aiming (+10.29) and Egocentric Movement (+13.04), confirming that generative spatial training transfers to understanding without any explicit reasoning supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant understanding-centric framing of spatial intelligence in MLLMs (VSI-Bench, MindCube, OmniSpatial) by introducing a generative perspective: can a model actively enforce 3D spatial constraints during image generation? It builds on BAGEL as the base unified model and borrows simulation infrastructure from AI2-THOR and MesaTask (similar to SAT's use of simulators for spatial reasoning data), but repurposes them for generating spatially grounded editing pairs rather than QA supervision. The 3D scene representation formalism and rule-based operation generation draw on prior 3D grounding work (DetAny3D) and physics simulation, but the combination into a quantitative image-editing benchmark with four-dimensional evaluation (compliance, accuracy, locality, appearance) is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that purely generative spatial editing training (no understanding or reasoning data) improves downstream spatial understanding benchmarks (OmniSpatial, SAT-Real) provides the first clear evidence of a generation-to-understanding transfer pathway for spatial concepts, suggesting unified models can bootstrap spatial reasoning through generation alone. However, this is validated on a single 7B model (BAGEL) with moderate gains (+2–4%), and the real-world benchmark is small (441 samples), leaving open whether the effect generalizes across architectures, scales, or more complex 3D scenes. The benchmark itself (GSI-Bench) is a useful and reproducible tool for the growing unified-model community, but its practical impact depends on whether the GSI framing gains traction beyond this single study.
