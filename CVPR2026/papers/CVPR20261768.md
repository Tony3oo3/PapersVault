## Problem

Existing longitudinal radiology report generation methods rely on unidirectional feature alignment or static prompting, failing to capture the inherently asymmetric nature of disease evolution where lesions can both emerge and resolve between scans. Additionally, many approaches depend on prior textual reports that are often unavailable in real clinical workflows, limiting their practical applicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261768/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of chest X-ray images (current and prior) as input and generates a free-text diagnostic report for the current scan. Notably, it operates in a two-timepoint, image-only setting without requiring prior reports. Patch-level features are extracted via a shared Swin Transformer encoder, and the report is produced autoregressively by a frozen LLaMA2-7B conditioned on OT-informed prompts that explicitly enumerate spatial indices of newly emerged and resolved regions.

## Method

BiOTPrompt encodes both images into patch-level feature sets and computes entropy-regularized optimal transport plans in both directions (current→prior and prior→current) via the Sinkhorn algorithm, enabling soft correspondence across spatially misaligned scans. A Dual-Flow Asymmetry Analyzer then identifies newly emerged regions (current patches with low outgoing mass to prior AND low incoming mass from prior) and resolved regions (prior patches with low outgoing mass to current AND low incoming mass from current) using a fixed threshold δ, reducing false positives from noise. These evolving regions are verbalized as structured prompts listing spatial indices, which guide the LLM toward temporally grounded report generation. A Vision-Language Consistency Constraint enforces cross-modal alignment through dual-branch disease classification (visual [CLS] token and text average-pooled hidden states) supervised by BCE loss, plus a KL divergence term aligning the two prediction distributions, thereby mitigating hallucinations.

## Results

On the Longitudinal-MIMIC dataset, BiOTPrompt achieves state-of-the-art performance across all NLG and CE metrics: BLEU-1 of 0.397, BLEU-4 of 0.126, ROUGE-L of 0.285, CIDEr of 0.236, and CE-F1 of 0.417. Notably, BiOTPrompt uses only the prior image (no prior report) yet outperforms methods that leverage both prior images and reports, such as HC-LLM (F1: 0.357) and HERGen (F1: 0.295). The method also surpasses the best single-image baseline CvT2DistilGPT2 by a wide margin (F1: 0.417 vs. 0.261), demonstrating the substantial benefit of explicit bidirectional temporal modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiOTPrompt outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest longitudinal baselines that use both prior images and reports—HC-LLM and HERGen—BiOTPrompt gains +0.008 BLEU-1, +0.009 BLEU-4, +0.003 ROUGE-L, and a substantial +0.060 CE-F1 (0.417 vs. 0.357 for HC-LLM; 0.417 vs. 0.295 for HERGen), while using only the prior image. Compared to R2GenGPT with prior image (the closest single-modality longitudinal baseline), BiOTPrompt improves BLEU-1 by +0.024, CIDEr by +0.045, and CE-F1 by +0.101. Against the best single-image method CvT2DistilGPT2, the CE-F1 gap is +0.156 (0.417 vs. 0.261), underscoring the value of explicit temporal change modeling. The ablation (Table 2) further confirms that removing bidirectional OT, the Dual-Flow Asymmetry Analyzer, or the Vision-Language Consistency Constraint each degrades performance, with the full pipeline yielding the best CE-F1 of 0.417.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends longitudinal radiology report generation (RRG) by replacing the unidirectional feature alignment or static prompting used in prior methods (R2GenGPT+PI, Prefilling, HERGen, HC-LLM) with a bidirectional optimal transport mechanism that explicitly captures asymmetric lesion evolution. It builds on the LLM-based RRG paradigm (R2GenGPT, PromptMRG) and the entropy-regularized OT framework (Sinkhorn), but departs from prior longitudinal work by requiring only prior images rather than prior reports, and by using a dual-flow asymmetry analyzer to separate newly emerged from resolved regions. The vision-language consistency constraint (dual classification + KL alignment) follows the broader trend of cross-modal alignment losses for hallucination mitigation in medical report generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that explicit modeling of asymmetric inter-scan changes via bidirectional OT can outperform methods that additionally consume prior textual reports, suggesting that visual evolution signals alone are sufficient for clinically faithful longitudinal reporting when properly structured. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Longitudinal-MIMIC), a single modality (chest X-ray), and a two-timepoint setting, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multi-visit sequences, other imaging modalities (CT, MRI), or spatially more complex anatomies. The finding that a simple fixed threshold outperforms adaptive selection strategies (Top-K, learnable scalars) also raises questions about whether the asymmetry detection could be further simplified or made more robust across imaging protocols.
