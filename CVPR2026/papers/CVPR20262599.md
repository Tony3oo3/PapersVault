## Problem

Understanding how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) arrive at their predictions remains an open challenge. Existing interpretability approaches—prompting, linear probing, or decoding hidden embeddings—yield text-based explanations that are sensitive to perturbations, limited in length, or misrepresent internal processes. This paper introduces the logits-to-video (L2V) task, which generates high-fidelity videos that visually represent the semantic content underlying VLM token logit distributions, opening a novel direction for model interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text caption containing a target attribute (e.g., 'happy'), a target logit divergence Δω computed as the Hellinger distance between VLM logit distributions for an attribute pair (e.g., 'happy' vs. 'sad'), and a learned concept vector q_o encoding the latent direction of that pair. It produces a 61-frame, 720×720 video (decoded from a 16×90² noise latent via Wan2.2) that visually reflects the specified logit modulation, along with a generated caption obtained by transporting the video latents into VLM space via the coupling network. The VLMs (VideoLLaMA 3, Gemma 3, Phi 4 MM) and the base generator remain frozen throughout.

## Method

TRANSPORTER couples the generator's latent space (R^Ξ) to the VLM's embedding space (R^Ω) through a two-stage architecture. A coupling network Φ first projects generator embeddings globally via an MLP-Mixer (Φ_Ω1) and locally via a Transformer matching Gram-matrix token structure (Φ_Ω2); a learnable entropic optimal transport (ρ-OT) module with P=100 projection vectors then fuses both into distilled VLM-space embeddings. A concept bank Q stores learnable vectors q_o per attribute pair, trained by supervising the flow-matching velocity divergence Δv between conditions π⁻ and π⁺ with the VLM logit change Δω (Hellinger distance), which acts as the control parameter δ in the CFM loss. At inference, δ·q_o is added to the text condition, and the frozen flow-matching generator denoises a Gaussian prior into a video whose visual content reflects the target logit shift while preserving scene dynamics. Training proceeds in two stages: 100K iterations for the coupling network, then 1K iterations per concept vector for the bank.

## Results

Across three VLMs (VideoLLaMA 3, Gemma 3, Phi 4 MM), TRANSPORTER achieves FVD of 1.25e2, 1.05e2, and 1.42e2 respectively, compared to 1.85e3–2.58e3 for the best AM-based baselines (LEAPS). CLIP video alignment scores reach 35.44, 36.18, and 35.71, roughly doubling the best baseline (16.74 for LEAPS on VideoLLaMA 3). LPIPS drops to 1.43–1.67 versus 4.37–5.43 for baselines, and the cossim divergence Δ improves from ~1.5–2.3 to 11.35–12.62, indicating substantially better semantic alignment with target logit modulations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRANSPORTER outperforms all four baselines (AM, GradViT, MACO, LEAPS) on every metric across all three VLMs. The strongest baseline, LEAPS [82], achieves FVD of 1.85e3 and CLIP score of 16.74 on VideoLLaMA 3, while TRANSPORTER reaches 1.25e2 FVD (≈15× lower) and 35.44 CLIP (≈2.1× higher). GradViT [39] and AM [79] perform worst on visual quality (FVD 2.61e3 and 5.18e3) and semantic alignment (CLIP 10.11 and 9.48), confirming that gradient-based and activation-maximization approaches cannot produce coherent, attribute-controlled videos. MACO [26] sits in the middle but still trails TRANSPORTER by over 2× in FVD and CLIP. The gap is consistent across VLMs: on Gemma 3, TRANSPORTER's FVD of 1.05e2 vs. LEAPS's 2.18e3, and on Phi 4 MM, 1.42e2 vs. 2.34e3, showing the advantage is not VLM-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

TRANSPORTER draws on two distinct lineages: concept editing in text-to-image diffusion models (Baumann et al., CVPR 2025), which identifies latent directions for attribute control, and sliced Gromov-Wasserstein distribution matching (Vayer et al., NeurIPS 2019), which the paper adapts into a learnable entropic OT module for coupling embedding spaces of different dimensions. It departs from the broader interpretability literature—saliency maps, activation maximization, and linear probing—by replacing text-based or pixel-level explanations with full video generation conditioned on VLM logit divergence. The only prior work addressing visual explanations for video models is LEAPS (Stergiou & Deligiannis, ICCV 2023), which uses activation maximization and produces abstract, low-fidelity outputs; TRANSPORTER repositions the problem as a controlled generative task rather than an optimization over inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

L2V opens a genuinely new axis for VLM interpretability: instead of asking 'which pixels matter,' it asks 'what video would the model see if this token's probability shifted?' This is particularly valuable for video VLMs where temporal dynamics and action semantics are hard to capture with static saliency. Key limitations include the need to train a coupling network per VLM–generator pair, the concept bank being restricted to predefined attribute pairs, and the OT module's computational cost scaling with the number of projections. Open questions include whether the generated videos reflect causal reasoning or merely learned correlations, how the approach generalizes to compositional or unseen attributes, and whether the logit-divergence control can be made fully continuous rather than discrete per concept vector.
