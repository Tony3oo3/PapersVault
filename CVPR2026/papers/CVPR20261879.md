## Problem

Multimodal LLMs consistently underperform text-only models on table reasoning benchmarks despite having access to richer visual cues like color and layout. The authors attribute this gap to vision-language alignment pre-training inadvertently degrading the LLM's intrinsic logical and mathematical reasoning, a deficit that standard RL fine-tuning (e.g., GRPO) alone cannot recover.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261879/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) receives a rendered table image alongside a text question prompt. Outputs follow a strict template: a chain-of-thought reasoning process enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in \boxed{} format, enabling automatic verifiable reward assignment. Table images are rendered from source datasets (CSV, HTML, etc.) into a standardized visual format to create a unified multimodal training corpus.

## Method

TableMix is a data-centric RL framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with two core components. First, a principled data mixing strategy constructs each training batch from three sources at fixed ratios (α=0.7, β=0.2, γ=0.1): multimodal table reasoning data (D_TR) for task-specific skill, text-only mathematical reasoning data (D_TM, e.g., MetaMath) to restore degraded logical competence, and simple multimodal table perception data (D_TP) to prevent the reasoning-perception tension where improved reasoning erodes visual grounding. Second, a Difficulty-Aware Reward Shaping (DRS) mechanism modifies GRPO's reward by computing a per-group success rate p(x) and, when p(x) exceeds a threshold τ=0.5, applying an adaptive tanh-based length penalty to correct responses that scales with an annealing function k(t), thereby incentivizing concise answers on mastered (easy) problems while preserving elaborate multi-step reasoning on harder ones. This reduces average inference tokens by ~20% without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

TableMix achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all seven table reasoning benchmarks, reaching 99.20% on TabMWP, 81.32% on WTQ, 82.25% on HiTab, 78.52% on TAT-QA, 73.43% on FeTaQA, 88.96% on TabFact, and 88.72% on InfoTabs. On the held-out TableVQA-Bench (zero-shot), TableMix attains an average of 82.3%, substantially outperforming the next-best model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 70.2%). The DRS mechanism further reduces average inference token length by ~20% without sacrificing accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Turbo-8B, the most direct competitor (also GRPO-based RL on multimodal tables), TableMix wins on all six shared benchmarks by margins of 2.45 (TabMWP), 13.52 (WTQ), 10.10 (HiTab), 5.31 (TAT-QA), 3.15 (TabFact), and 6.83 (InfoTabs) points. Against the text-based SOTA Table-R1, TableMix surpasses it on all seven benchmarks, most notably on TAT-QA (78.52 vs. 73.86) and FeTaQA (73.43 vs. 70.25), effectively closing the long-standing multimodal-vs-textual gap. Compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model, TableMix gains 6.72 points on WTQ, 15.16 on HiTab, and 8.11 on InfoTabs, confirming that the data-mixing + DRS strategy substantially enhances the backbone. On the zero-shot TableVQA-Bench, TableMix achieves 82.3% average, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (70.2%), Ovis2-8B (69.7%), and HIPPO-8B (62.3%) by 12.1, 12.6, and 20.0 points respectively, demonstrating strong generalization to unseen table domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath [54] and the multimodal table reasoning line of work (Turbo, HIPPO, Table-LLaVA, SynTab-LLaVA), but departs from them by arguing that the bottleneck is not the RL algorithm itself but the degraded intrinsic reasoning of the MLLM backbone caused by vision-language alignment pre-training. Rather than designing a new RL objective or architecture, TableMix takes a data-centric approach: it interleaves text-only mathematical reasoning data (MetaMath) with multimodal table reasoning data to 'repair' the reasoning core, and adds simple table perception data to counteract the resulting reasoning-perception tension. The Difficulty-Aware Reward Shaping mechanism is a targeted modification to GRPO's reward function that addresses the non-uniform difficulty introduced by the mixed data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a long-standing and counter-intuitive performance gap where multimodal table reasoning models consistently underperform text-only counterparts despite having access to richer visual information, validating that the issue lies in pre-training rather than in the RL stage. The reasoning-perception tension—where improving reasoning degrades visual perception—is a practically important finding for anyone fine-tuning MLLMs on reasoning-heavy tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and a single task domain (tables), leaving open whether the data mixing strategy generalizes to other MLLM architectures, other reasoning-heavy multimodal tasks, or whether the specific mixing ratios (0.7/0.2/0.1) transfer across settings.
