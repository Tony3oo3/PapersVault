## Problem

Rotational symmetry annotations for 3D objects remain largely manual or semi-automatic, requiring predefined symmetry types and orders that limit scalability for large-scale unseen-object pose estimation. Existing methods either cover only subsets of the eight canonical rotational symmetry types, depend on geometric references such as centroids or principal axes, or still require manual specification of rotational orders, making them unsuitable for fully automated, reference-free symmetry analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw 3D point cloud with no pre-aligned templates, centroids, or object frames as input, operating entirely in a reference-free manner. It produces a complete symmetry description: the canonical type label (one of C∞, D∞, Cn, Dn, A4, S4, A5), the rotational order for each axis, the full set of canonical axis directions, and a texture-aware representation that captures appearance-induced order reductions while preserving geometric axis orientations.

## Method

The core contribution is the Geometric Degeneration Hierarchy (GDH), a computable geometric hierarchy that encodes all eight canonical SO(3) rotational symmetry families and the degeneration conditions between them, replacing exhaustive directional enumeration with structured axis-reduction reasoning. The pipeline first localizes the dominant high-order axis by optimizing a mixed-order Chamfer alignment loss on the unit sphere using two-stage spherical-cap sampling followed by Adam refinement, exploiting the self-guiding property that high-order axes naturally minimize aggregated lower-order losses. Rotational order is then inferred via FFT on a dense self-similarity signal, with hierarchical verification for degenerate cases. Type classification proceeds by searching for a secondary axis at the GDH-prescribed inter-axis inclination and verifying the order pair, after which all remaining canonical axes are reconstructed by rigidly aligning a type-specific axis template. A texture-aware extension models appearance-induced order reductions as divisors of the geometric order, preserving axis orientations while adjusting effective rotational orders.

## Results

On the GSO dataset (438 symmetric objects), KASALv2 achieves 94.75% overall accuracy (acc_T) with a normalized axis-localization error of e_ADI/d = 0.00262. On BOP (50 symmetric instances under re-annotation), it reaches 84.00% acc_T and reduces e_ADI/d from 0.00290 (Wang et al.) to 0.00256. On DSRSTO, accuracy is 81.48% with e_ADI/d = 0.00212, and the texture subset (DSRSTO_tex) yields 72.32% acc_T. When used as symmetry priors for FoundationPose, KASALv2 improves 6D pose accuracy by up to 0.9 points (IC-BIN) across five BOP datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole direct baseline is Wang et al. [26] (KASALv1), which handles all eight symmetry types but requires manual specification of symmetry type and rotational order. On BOP, KASALv2 improves e_ADI/d from 0.00290 to 0.00256 (≈12% reduction) while also raising classification accuracy from 80.00% to 84.00%. On GSO, KASALv2's e_ADI/d (0.00262) is marginally higher than Wang et al.'s (0.00256), a small gap attributable to the absence of manual type/order input. On DSRSTO and DSRSTO_tex, KASALv2's localization error is slightly worse (0.00212 vs. 0.00172; 0.00195 vs. 0.00181), but this is expected since Wang et al. receives ground-truth type and order as input, whereas KASALv2 must infer them automatically. The practical advantage of KASALv2 is full automation: it eliminates the annotation bottleneck that limits Wang et al.'s scalability to large-scale unseen-object pose estimation, as demonstrated by the 0.1–0.9% gains in FoundationPose accuracy across five BOP benchmarks when KASALv2-derived priors replace manual symmetry labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Wang et al. [26] (KASALv1), which performed axis localization for all eight canonical rotational symmetry types but required manual specification of the symmetry type and rotational order. KASALv2 removes this dependency by introducing the Geometric Degeneration Hierarchy (GDH), a computable reorganization of SO(3) subgroups that encodes degeneration pathways between symmetry families, enabling fully automatic type classification, order estimation, and axis reconstruction in a single reference-free pipeline. It also departs from reference-based methods (Martinet et al., Korman et al.) that rely on centroids or principal axes, and from reference-free methods (Hruda et al., Rajkumar et al.) that cover only subsets of symmetry types, by handling all eight canonical forms without any geometric anchors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical value is scalability: as 6D pose estimation moves toward unseen-object generalization with tens of thousands of 3D models, per-object symmetry annotation becomes infeasible manually, and KASALv2 provides an automated, annotation-free source of rotational priors. The downstream validation (up to 0.9% gain on FoundationPose across five BOP datasets) confirms that automatically estimated symmetry priors are useful for training, though the margin is modest. Open limitations include underestimation of high rotational orders, sensitivity to local geometric irregularities, and persistently low accuracy on C_n cases (28.57% on DSRSTO), suggesting that frequency-sensitive order estimation and robustness to perturbed structures remain active research directions.
