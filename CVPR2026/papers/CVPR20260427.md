## Problem

Product poster generation requires both faithful preservation of product appearance (structure, texture, color) and precise rendering of dense, multi-line promotional text, yet existing inpainting-based methods rely on auxiliary modules like ControlNet and OCR encoders that add architectural complexity while still producing subject extension artifacts and text errors. General editing models lack explicit subject preservation mechanisms and cannot precisely specify multi-line text positioning. The paper asks whether a simpler framework can achieve near-perfect subject fidelity and accurate text layout without external controllers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a product image (preprocessed via segmentation and matting onto a pure white background), a set of per-line bounding boxes specifying spatial positions for each text line, and a natural language prompt describing the background scene, text content, and coarse layout cues. It produces a photorealistic product poster at 1024×1024 resolution where the original product is preserved intact and each text line is rendered within its designated region. The text encoder is Qwen2.5-VL, supporting both Chinese and English prompts, unlike the original T5 encoder in FLUX-Fill.

## Method

SimplePoster builds on FLUX-Fill's DiT backbone with three modifications: (1) full-parameter fine-tuning of the entire DiT (rather than freezing it and training a ControlNet), which the paper shows reduces subject extension from 41% to 0.6%, outperforming ControlNet (23.6%) and LoRA (2.8%); (2) a Character Position Encoding strategy that assigns each character token a 2D spatial coordinate computed as the center of its equal sub-region within the target line's bounding box, encoded via RoPE for attention—replacing the default fixed (0,0) position for all text tokens; and (3) replacing T5 with Qwen2.5-VL for multilingual prompt support. Training uses a single-stage end-to-end flow matching objective with no auxiliary losses, and the CPE design enables simultaneous layout control and cross-lingual character learning without multi-stage training or OCR-derived features.

## Results

On a 500-image e-commerce benchmark, SimplePoster achieves a 98.7% Subject Preservation Rate (SPR), substantially surpassing the best general editing model SeedEdit 3.0/DreamPoster (55.2%) and the specialized PosterMaker (85.3%). In text rendering, it attains 71.33% Sentence Accuracy and 0.8062 NED, outperforming all baselines including PosterMaker (57.57% Sen. Acc, 0.7392 NED). On user-study metrics, SimplePoster scores 3.55/5 for prompt following and 4.03/5 for visual appeal, trailing Gemini 2.5 Flash (3.82, 4.12) and SeedEdit 3.0/DreamPoster (3.73, 4.22) but exceeding PosterMaker (3.32, 3.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimplePoster decisively outperforms all six baselines on subject preservation and text rendering. Against the closest specialized competitor PosterMaker (CVPR 2025), it improves SPR by 13.4 points (98.7% vs 85.3%), Sen. Acc by 13.8 points (71.33% vs 57.57%), and NED by 0.067 (0.8062 vs 0.7392), while also leading on prompt following (3.55 vs 3.32) and visual appeal (4.03 vs 3.74). Among general editing models, SeedEdit 3.0/DreamPoster is the strongest on text metrics (64.34% Sen. Acc, 0.7825 NED) but still trails SimplePoster by 7 points in Sen. Acc and 13.5 points in SPR. Gemini 2.5 Flash leads on prompt following (3.82) and visual appeal (4.12), areas where SimplePoster lags due to its e-commerce-sourced training data favoring product clarity over background diversity. FLUX-Kontext and Step1x-Edit are far behind on all metrics, with SPR below 40% and Sen. Acc below 0.1, confirming that text-to-image editing frameworks lack the structural fidelity and spatial text control needed for product posters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimplePoster builds directly on the FLUX-Fill inpainting model (a DiT-based flow-matching architecture) and adapts it for product poster generation, following the task formulation established by PosterMaker. It deliberately departs from the dominant paradigm in specialized poster generation—where ControlNet-style structural controllers, OCR feature injection, and reward-model-based reinforcement learning are standard—by replacing all auxiliary modules with full-parameter fine-tuning of the base DiT and a minimal character-level RoPE position encoding. The empirical ablation in Section 3 (ControlNet 23.6% vs. LoRA 2.8% vs. full-tuning 0.6% extension rate) repositions the problem: the domain gap between small-patch inpainting and large-region poster synthesis is better bridged by direct model adaptation than by external conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in inpainting-based generation that auxiliary controllers (ControlNet, layout encoders) are necessary for spatial and structural control, showing that careful full-parameter adaptation of the base model suffices and is substantially more effective. The character position encoding is a zero-overhead, architecture-agnostic technique that could be adopted in other text-rendering or spatially-grounded generation settings beyond posters. Limitations include dependence on upstream segmentation/matting quality, inability to modify product attributes under the inpainting constraint, and a visual-appeal gap relative to general editing models due to the aesthetic homogeneity of e-commerce training data. Open questions include whether the full-tuning > ControlNet finding generalizes to other large-mask inpainting tasks and whether CPE's cross-lingual benefits extend to scripts with fundamentally different spatial structures.
