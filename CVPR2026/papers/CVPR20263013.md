## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs handle mathematical reasoning either by encoding a single static image once and producing purely textual CoT, or by indiscriminately interleaving visual tokens at every reasoning step. Neither paradigm mimics how human experts selectively attend to visual evidence and decompose complex proofs into semantically coherent logical units, leading to redundant visual signals, missed fine-grained cues, and the absence of hierarchical problem decomposition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a mathematical image (geometry diagrams, charts, or plots at resolutions up to 16K) paired with a text question. It produces a structured reasoning chain organized into Critical Reasoning Units (CRUs), where each CRU begins with visual tokens returned by an executed tool call (crop, scale, or display) followed by one or more coherent textual steps that jointly verify a single intermediate proposition. The chain is further annotated with four cognitive reasoning patterns—planning, verifying, backtracking, and reflecting—and terminates with a final numerical or symbolic answer.

## Method

V1RC introduces Reason Chunking, which restructures multimodal CoT into a sequence of Critical Reasoning Units (CRUs), each pairing on-demand visual tokens with a set of coherent textual steps that verify one self-contained proposition, grounded in Miller's information chunking principle. Three visual tools—crop (region selection), scale (resolution adjustment), and display (context recall)—are invoked per CRU to provide targeted visual evidence rather than redundant per-step injection. The CRUX dataset (100K samples from 54K problems) supplies token-level CRU annotations across one correct and two incorrect reasoning paths per problem, generated via a three-stage pipeline of path sampling, step-to-CRU mapping, and visual grounding. Training follows a progressive three-stage curriculum: Instructional SFT on text-only CRUX to internalize the CRU structure, Practice SFT with executed tool calls for real multimodal grounding, and Strategic RL (GRPO) on a curated hard subset using four reward signals—answer correctness, multimodal coherence (judged by Qwen2.5-VL-72B), reasoning-pattern alignment, and format validity—applied at the CRU level to avoid step-level credit-assignment noise.

## Results

V1RC-7B achieves 77.79% average accuracy across GeoQA (75.07%), MMStar-Math (77.20%), and MathVista-Math (81.11%), surpassing the previous best open-source reasoning model MM-Eureka-7B (70.35%) by 7.44% and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (58.99%) by 18.80%. The smaller V1RC-3B model reaches 70.12% average, matching MM-Eureka-7B while using fewer parameters. On cross-domain high-resolution benchmarks, V1RC-7B leads on VisualProbe (43.57%), V* (79.06%), and HR-Bench (69.94%), exceeding the best prior results by up to 15.26% on VisualProbe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V1RC-7B outperforms all 15 baselines on the three mathematical benchmarks, achieving the highest average (77.79%) and leading on every individual benchmark: GeoQA (75.07% vs. 67.37% for Qwen3-VL-8B), MMStar-Math (77.20% vs. 76.00% for Claude 3.5 Sonnet), and MathVista-Math (81.11% vs. 75.93% for InternVL2.5-8B-MPO). Against the strongest open-source reasoning baselines, V1RC-7B surpasses MM-Eureka-7B by 7.44% in average accuracy and MINT-CoT-7B by 8.45%, while the 3B variant (70.12%) matches MM-Eureka-7B (70.35%) with fewer parameters. On high-resolution generalization benchmarks, V1RC-7B leads VisualProbe by 15.26% over Hint-GRPO-Qwen2.5-VL-3B (43.57% vs. 28.31%), V* by 3.14% over Hint-GRPO-Qwen2-VL-7B (79.06% vs. 75.92%), and HR-Bench by 4.94% over Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (69.94% vs. 65.00%). Notably, V1RC-7B also exceeds closed-source GPT-4o (58.80%) and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (64.74%) on the mathematical average, and surpasses GPT-4o on all three high-resolution benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes the Visual Chain-of-Thought (VCoT) / 'Thinking with Images' line of work (DeepEyes, Mini-o3, OpenThinkImg) with cognitive-science principles (Miller's Law) to propose a middle ground between pure text CoT and step-wise visual CoT. Rather than injecting visual tokens at every reasoning step as prior VCoT methods do, V1RC groups steps into Critical Reasoning Units (CRUs) where visual evidence is consulted only at unit boundaries. It reuses the 54K problem set from MINT-CoT but regenerates all reasoning paths with explicit CRU annotations and multiple cognitive patterns (planning, verifying, backtracking, reflecting), and introduces a three-stage progressive training curriculum (Instructional SFT → Practice SFT → Strategic RL) that has no direct precedent in the multimodal reasoning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CRU abstraction offers a clean, cognitively grounded way to structure multimodal reasoning that could transfer beyond mathematics to other domains requiring selective visual attention. The 18.8% average gain and strong generalization to high-resolution benchmarks (9%) suggest that structured chunking is more effective than dense visual injection, challenging the prevailing assumption in VCoT that more visual tokens per step is always better. Open questions include whether the CRU decomposition generalizes to non-mathematical visual reasoning, whether the three visual tools (crop, scale, display) are sufficient for more complex diagrams, and whether the approach scales to larger models or longer reasoning chains without the CRU structure becoming overly rigid.
