## Problem

Classical Next-Best-View (NBV) planners—whether sampling-based (RRT) or heuristic/deterministic—suffer from poor scalability and frequent entrapment in local minima when exploring complex environments, limiting their utility in time-critical robotic tasks. No prior work has applied quantum variational algorithms to informative view planning, leaving unexplored whether quantum superposition and entanglement can provide measurable advantages in navigating the combinatorial viewpoint-selection space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ternary occupancy grid map (unknown/free/occupied) built incrementally from a 2D bounded scene with obstacles, along with the current camera viewpoint (x, y, θ) and a fixed FOV of 2π/3 with maximum ray distance of 8 units. The continuous viewpoint space is discretized into a finite feasible set of collision-free positions, and the 10-qubit encoding allocates 2 qubits each to direction and distance, 2 to fine adjustment, and 4 to orientation angle. The output is a sequence of viewpoints iteratively selected until a coverage threshold is met, with a classical fallback that snaps to the furthest valid position if the quantum-decoded trajectory violates collision constraints.

## Method

HQC-NBV formulates view planning as a 10-qubit variational quantum eigensolver problem, constructing a five-component cost Hamiltonian (direction, distance, adjustment, orientation, coherence) from Pauli Z, X, and ZZ/XX operators whose coefficients are derived from the occupancy map's unexplored density, obstacle proximity, and angular dispersion. The variational ansatz uses 5 layers of parameter-grouped Ry rotations, bidirectional alternating CNOT entanglement (intra-group linear chains plus inter-group connections that swap direction between even and odd layers), and Rx rotations to introduce non-commutativity. An adaptive SPSA optimizer minimizes the Hamiltonian expectation value, and the ground-state correspondence ensures the lowest-energy configuration encodes the viewpoint minimizing the classical cost J(v) = −E(v) + λ_m·M(v). Ablation experiments isolate the contributions of the entanglement architecture and coherence-preserving terms, showing that inter-group entanglement and two-qubit XX operators are critical for escaping local minima in later exploration stages.

## Results

HQC-NBV achieves 92.85% coverage in 16 viewpoints on Scene 1, 93.02% in 12 viewpoints on Scene 2, and 91.97% in 18 viewpoints on Scene 3, consistently outperforming classical baselines. The method reduces total path length by 9.60–27.92% and improves exploration efficiency (coverage-to-distance ratio) by 16.19–30.75% over RH-NBV and frontier-based methods. Across all four scenes, HQC-NBV delivers 7.9–49.2% higher exploration efficiency than classical approaches, with scalability confirmed by a linear 4:1 viewpoint-to-area ratio between the 20×20 and 40×40 environments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RH-NBV [7], HQC-NBV wins decisively on all scenes: in Scene 1 it reaches 92.85% vs. 80.54% coverage with the same 16 viewpoints, and in Scene 2 it achieves 93.02% in 12 viewpoints while RH-NBV needs 24 viewpoints for only 78.27%. Against the frontier-based method [29], HQC-NBV also dominates: both methods reach 80.54% in Scene 1 with 16 views, but in the more complex Scene 3 the frontier-based approach suffers early termination at 81.75% due to disconnected unexplored regions, whereas HQC-NBV continues to 91.97% within 18 viewpoints. In terms of path length, HQC-NBV reduces total travel by 9.60–27.92% relative to both classical baselines, and its exploration efficiency (coverage per unit distance) is 16.19–30.75% higher. The paper also benchmarks against general-purpose classical optimizers (Powell, COBYLA), which plateau below 67.1% coverage, further highlighting the advantage of the quantum variational formulation over standard numerical optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) techniques to the well-established Next-Best-View planning problem, drawing on the quantum computer vision lineage (quantum annealing for multi-model fitting, motion segmentation, and graph matching) and classical NBV baselines (RH-NBV, frontier-based methods). Rather than building on a single prior quantum NBV formulation, it carves new ground by designing a 10-qubit Hamiltonian with five functional components and a bidirectional alternating entanglement ansatz tailored to the hierarchical structure of viewpoint parameters (direction, distance, adjustment, orientation). The optimization leverages adaptive SPSA, a classical stochastic method, to navigate the variational parameter space, making it a genuinely hybrid quantum-classical pipeline rather than a pure quantum algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first quantum-classical approach to informative view planning, this work opens a concrete pathway for integrating NISQ-era variational algorithms into robotic perception, with the ablation studies providing early evidence that entanglement architecture and coherence-preserving terms contribute measurably to exploration quality. However, the evaluation is confined to 2D scenes of at most 40×40 units on a Qiskit Aer simulator with only 10 qubits, leaving open critical questions about whether the observed 7.9–49.2% efficiency gains persist on real quantum hardware, in 3D settings with richer parameterizations, or at the scale where classical methods already struggle. The framework's reliance on a discretized occupancy grid and simplified sensing model means the quantum advantage demonstrated here is a proof-of-concept rather than a deployable system.
