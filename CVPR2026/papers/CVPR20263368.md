## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines require undistorting fisheye images before training, which crops edge information and dilutes spatial detail through stretch-and-interpolate resampling, leading to blurry textures and floating artifacts. Even when a correct fisheye camera model is used, the standard per-iteration random single-view optimization ignores cross-view correlations of each Gaussian, producing extreme shapes (oversized or elongated ellipsoids) and inconsistent photometry—especially at image peripheries where radial distortion is greatest.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw, natively distorted fisheye images (resolutions ranging from 1752×1168 to 5568×4176) together with Kannala-Brandt calibration parameters (four distortion coefficients, focal lengths, principal point) and a COLMAP SfM sparse point cloud for Gaussian initialization—no undistortion or re-projection preprocessing is performed. The output is a standard 3D Gaussian scene representation fully compatible with existing viewers (e.g., SIBR_Viewer) and commercial 3DGS tooling, rendered at real-time rates via the original tile-based rasterization pipeline.

## Method

DirectFisheye-GS embeds the Kannala-Brandt polynomial fisheye projection (θ_d = θ + k₁θ³ + k₂θ⁵ + k₃θ⁷ + k₄θ⁹) directly into the 3DGS differentiable rasterizer, deriving a closed-form 3×3 Jacobian that maps 3D Gaussian covariance to 2D projected covariance under nonlinear radial distortion, enabling end-to-end gradient-based optimization on distorted pixels. On top of this camera model, the paper introduces Cross-View Joint Optimization (CVO): a precomputed feature-overlap graph (built from COLMAP SIFT correspondences) ranks neighboring cameras by shared feature count, then by descending angular divergence; during each training iteration, a primary view is sampled and its top (batchsize−1) most-overlapping yet angularly-diverse correlated cameras are jointly optimized, accumulating L1+SSIM losses across all selected views before a single backpropagation step. This enforces consistent Gaussian geometry and photometry across overlapping viewpoints, suppressing extreme shape parameterization and edge floaters without sacrificing rasterization speed.

## Results

On the FisheyeNeRF dataset (train view), DirectFisheye-GS achieves an average PSNR of 26.25 dB, SSIM of 0.8284, and LPIPS of 0.2295, outperforming 3DGUT (25.28 dB) and Fisheye-GS (25.18 dB). On the Scannet++ test view, it reaches 28.37 dB average PSNR with the best LPIPS (0.1863) among all methods, slightly trailing 3DGUT (28.42 dB) on PSNR but leading on perceptual quality. On the large-scale DEN-SOFT dataset, the advantage is most pronounced: on the Ruziniu scene (train view), DirectFisheye-GS scores 24.02 dB PSNR versus 22.21 dB for 3DGUT and 20.88 dB for Fisheye-GS, a gap of ~1.8 dB over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla 3DGS applied directly to fisheye images (treating them as pinhole), DirectFisheye-GS improves average PSNR by ~8 dB on FisheyeNeRF (26.25 vs 18.18) and ~11 dB on Scannet++ (28.37 vs 17.53), confirming that the native fisheye model is essential. Compared to the undistorted-input variant (3DGS*), DirectFisheye-GS consistently wins (e.g., 26.25 vs 25.54 dB on FisheyeNeRF; 28.37 vs 25.88 dB on Scannet++), demonstrating that undistortion irreversibly discards edge information. Versus Fisheye-GS, the proposed method leads on all three metrics across every dataset (e.g., +1.07 dB PSNR on FisheyeNeRF, +0.71 dB on Scannet++). Against 3DGUT, the closest competitor, DirectFisheye-GS matches or exceeds PSNR on FisheyeNeRF (+0.97 dB) and DEN-SOFT (+1.81 dB on Ruziniu) while achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.1863 vs 0.2563 on Scannet++), though 3DGUT retains a marginal 0.05 dB PSNR edge on Scannet++ test view. Self-Cali-GS is clearly outperformed across all settings, with gaps of 2–4 dB in PSNR, attributed to its neural deformation field's inherent smoothness that blurs high-frequency details.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3DGS by embedding the Kannala-Brandt polynomial fisheye projection model directly into the differentiable rasterization pipeline, avoiding the undistortion preprocessing that prior fisheye-GS methods (Fisheye-GS, 3DGUT) either partially or fully require. It departs from 3DGUT's Unscented Transform-based covariance propagation—which breaks the fully explicit architecture and limits compatibility with existing viewers—by deriving a closed-form Jacobian for the polynomial model. The cross-view joint optimization (CVO) strategy further distinguishes this work from both standard 3DGS's per-iteration random single-view training and MVGS's random multi-view sampling, instead using SIFT feature-overlap and angular-divergence criteria to select correlated camera groups.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By preserving the full fisheye FOV without undistortion-induced information loss, this work makes high-quality 3DGS reconstruction practical for VR/AR, autonomous driving, and robotics pipelines that rely on wide-FOV cameras. The CVO strategy's demonstrated benefit under pinhole inputs (Table 7) suggests a broadly applicable training improvement for the 3DGS community beyond the fisheye setting. Open questions include how the method scales to dynamic or unbounded outdoor scenes, whether CVO interacts favorably with other 3DGS extensions (compression, anti-aliasing, sparse-view regularization), and whether the polynomial model's four distortion parameters suffice for extreme lenses beyond 180° FOV.
