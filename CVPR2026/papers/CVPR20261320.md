## Problem

Existing geo-spatial benchmarks are fragmented across specialized tasks (cross-view retrieval, geolocalization, landmark retrieval) and lack fine-grained, instance-level correspondence across multiple modalities simultaneously. No prior dataset provides one-to-one alignment of ground images, aerial imagery, text, and GPS coordinates for the same landmark instances, preventing unified multimodal geo-spatial learning and evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261320/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each landmark instance provides four modalities: ground-view images (resized to max 800px side, sourced from Wikimedia Commons with high intra-class variability including indoor/outdoor views), aerial images (800×800 pixels at 1–2 m resolution from NAIP, sampled at multiple timestamps), a Wikipedia text corpus (with references and boilerplate sections removed), and a single GPS coordinate at the landmark center. The model produces a shared 512-dimensional embedding per modality, enabling retrieval of any modality from any other via k-nearest-neighbor search, as well as direct GPS prediction by retrieving the nearest coordinate from a 101k gallery.

## Method

MMCLIP is a CLIP-inspired multimodal contrastive baseline that learns a joint embedding space across four modalities. It uses a frozen CLIP ViT-L/336 image encoder shared by both ground and aerial images, the corresponding frozen text encoder for Wikipedia sentences, and a trainable location encoder initialized following GeoCLIP for GPS coordinates; each modality passes through a two-layer projection head (linear–ReLU–linear) to a common 512-dim space. Training extends InfoNCE to all 12 pairwise modality combinations (K=4), with a fixed temperature of 0.07, on a single H100 for 20 epochs (AdamW, lr 1e-4, batch 512). The key design choice is the fully symmetric pairwise contrastive objective rather than an ImageBind-style anchor-based alignment, which the ablations show yields stronger geolocalization performance, particularly for Satellite-to-GPS where indirect alignment through image anchors degrades accuracy.

## Results

On the MMLANDMARKS cross-view retrieval benchmark, the MMCLIP baseline achieves a median rank of 23 and mAP@1k of 18.8 for Satellite→Ground retrieval, and a median rank of 48 and mAP@1k of 26.2 for Ground→Satellite retrieval, substantially outperforming all zero-shot foundation models (best: OAI-CLIP ViT-L/336 at medR 519 / mAP@1k 10.4 for Sat→G) and all specialized cross-view models (best: Sample4Geo-UnI at medR 34,988 / mAP@1k 3.0 for Sat→G). For Satellite-to-GPS geolocalization, MMCLIP reaches 36.9% within 1 km and 99.7% within 2500 km, far exceeding GeoCLIP (12.3% / 97.4%) and G3 (8.8% / 97.0%). For Ground-to-GPS, MMCLIP (18.4% at 1 km) is competitive with GeoCLIP (21.4%) and G3 (12.9%), despite using far fewer training images than those task-specific models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMCLIP decisively outperforms all zero-shot foundation models on cross-view retrieval: the best foundation model (OAI-CLIP ViT-L/336) achieves mAP@1k of 10.4 for Sat→G and 15.2 for G→S, while MMCLIP reaches 18.8 and 26.2 respectively, with median ranks improved by over 20× (519→23 for Sat→G, 620→48 for G→S). Specialized cross-view models (Uni-1652, TransGeo-90°, Sample4Geo-UnI) perform catastrophically on MMLANDMARKS (mAP@1k < 3.0 for Sat→G), confirming the paper's claim that prior cross-view benchmarks are saturated and lack diversity. For Ground-to-GPS geolocalization, MMCLIP (18.4% at 1 km) is slightly behind GeoCLIP (21.4%) but ahead of G3 (12.9%) and StreetCLIP (0.5%); the authors note GeoCLIP/G3 may benefit from overlap with MP16 training data. For Satellite-to-GPS, MMCLIP dominates all baselines by a wide margin (36.9% at 1 km vs. 12.3% for GeoCLIP and 8.8% for G3), reflecting the advantage of training with paired aerial imagery. On Text-to-Satellite retrieval, MMCLIP (mAP@1k 17.3, R@1 13.4) matches SigLIP2 ViT-L/512 (mAP@1k 17.3, R@1 13.4) and outperforms OAI-CLIP ViT-L/336 (mAP@1k 14.5, R@1 11.1), showing that the multimodal training transfers to text-to-image alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive learning framework (Radford et al., 2021) from the standard image-text pair to a four-modality setting (ground image, aerial image, text, GPS coordinates), borrowing the location encoder and projection head design from GeoCLIP (Vivanco Cepeda et al., 2023) and the InfoNCE loss from van den Oord et al. (2018). Conceptually, it departs from prior cross-view geo-localization datasets (CVUSA, VIGOR, CV-Cities) that rely on Google Street View panoramas with strong geometric correspondence, and from geolocalization datasets (YFCC100M, OSV-5M) that only pair ground images with GPS. The key positioning is that no prior dataset provides one-to-one instance-level correspondence across all four modalities simultaneously, which the authors identify as the missing piece for unified geo-spatial learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMLANDMARKS is the first continental-scale, permissively-licensed dataset with complete four-modality instance-level correspondence, addressing a critical gap where prior cross-view datasets are restricted by Google licensing and lack image diversity. The empirical finding that both off-the-shelf foundation models and specialized cross-view/geolocalization models fail to transfer to this benchmark highlights that current geo-spatial models are overfit to narrow data distributions rather than learning generalizable spatial reasoning. Limitations include US-only coverage, landmark-centric (not general scene) focus, and long-tailed popularity bias. Open questions include whether the unified framework generalizes to non-landmark scenes, whether the approach scales to other continents with comparable open aerial imagery, and whether the simple contrastive baseline can be replaced by more sophisticated architectures that better exploit temporal and multi-view structure.
