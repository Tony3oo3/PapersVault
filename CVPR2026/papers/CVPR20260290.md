## Problem

Face relighting progress is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale, physically consistent illumination data: industry light-stage datasets (Adobe, Netflix, Google) are closed-source, while academic releases cover only a handful of subjects at low resolution. Even when OLAT captures are available, they are inherently limited to the recorded subjects and cannot generalize to arbitrary individuals, creating a gap between the data needed for robust relighting models and what is publicly accessible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260290/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

POLARNet takes a single uniformly lit RGB portrait and a target light direction (θ, φ) as input, and produces a per-light OLAT response image in one forward pass (no iterative sampling). The generated OLAT sequence is then linearly integrated against an HDR environment map to yield a fully relit portrait. The underlying POLAR dataset provides 4K-resolution captures across 220 subjects, 156 calibrated light directions, 32 synchronized camera views, and 16 expressions, along with alpha mattes, calibrated light-direction annotations, and pre-synthesized HDR-relit portraits.

## Method

POLARNet employs a flow-based Latent Bridge Matching formulation that models illumination change as a continuous transport between two semantically aligned endpoints—the uniform-light latent z_u and the target OLAT latent z_l—rather than denoising from Gaussian noise. A pre-trained VAE encodes both endpoints, and a conditional U-Net predicts a direction-conditioned velocity field v_θ(z_t, t, c_dir) that drives the latent along the illumination path, where c_dir is a sinusoidal embedding of the light direction. Training uses pair-wise aligned supervision from POLAR captures with an LBM velocity-matching loss, an ArcFace identity-preservation loss, an energy-aware pixel loss that up-weights bright regions, and a global energy-consistency regularizer. At inference, a single evaluation of the velocity field at t=0 transports z_u directly to the target OLAT latent, enabling fast, identity-preserving, and physically interpretable one-step generation of per-light responses for arbitrary subjects.

## Results

On the POLAR test set of relit portraits, POLARNet achieves the best perceptual similarity (LPIPS 0.115) and highest reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 22.12 dB) among all compared methods. It also attains a competitive SSIM of 0.82, trailing only SwitchLight (0.84) by a narrow margin. The generated OLAT responses closely match captured ground-truth OLAT data, and when linearly composed with HDR environment maps, the resulting relit portraits align well with those produced from real captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POLARNet outperforms all three main baselines on LPIPS and PSNR. Against SwitchLight [25] (physics-driven intrinsic decomposition), POLARNet improves LPIPS from 0.168 to 0.115 and PSNR from 20.69 to 22.12, though SwitchLight retains a slight SSIM edge (0.84 vs. 0.82). Against the background-conditioned diffusion methods IC-Light [64] and DreamLight [31], POLARNet shows substantially larger gains: LPIPS drops from 0.314/0.175 to 0.115, and PSNR rises from 18.47/19.87 to 22.12. In the physical-consistency ablation (Fig. 8), POLARNet's OLAT-based relighting maintains coherent shading and highlight movement under rotating HDR environments, whereas the background-conditioned LBM [5] baseline exhibits lighting and shadow discontinuities driven by contextual cues rather than true illumination direction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Latent Bridge Matching (LBM) framework [5] from its original distributional image-to-image translation setting to a pair-wise aligned illumination transport problem, replacing the stochastic noise-to-image trajectory with a deterministic bridge between a uniformly lit portrait and its OLAT counterpart. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based relighting paradigm (IC-Light, DreamLight) that conditions on background context, instead modeling illumination as a continuous, physically interpretable transformation parameterized by light direction. The Light Stage/OLAT capture paradigm [11] provides the physical grounding, but prior open datasets were limited to a few dozen subjects; POLAR scales this to 220 subjects with a generative model that closes the loop by synthesizing new OLAT data from arbitrary portraits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 28.8M-image open OLAT dataset fills a critical gap between proprietary industry light-stage data and small academic releases, providing the community with a physically calibrated resource for training and benchmarking relighting models. The one-step flow-based inference (vs. iterative diffusion sampling) offers a practical efficiency advantage for generating full OLAT sequences, and the 'chicken-and-egg' co-evolution framing—where real data train the model and the model expands data diversity—suggests a scalable path beyond lab-constrained acquisition. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video (temporal consistency), extreme poses, and whether the generated OLATs can substitute for real captures in downstream 3D reconstruction or neural rendering pipelines without accumulating artifacts at specular boundaries.
