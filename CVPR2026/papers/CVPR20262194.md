## Problem

Visual-inertial odometry faces a fundamental accuracy-efficiency trade-off: filter-based methods are fast but drift, while optimization-based methods rely on computationally prohibitive Visual-Inertial Bundle Adjustment (VIBA) that is difficult to deploy on resource-constrained edge devices. Existing deep learning approaches either replace VIBA entirely (sacrificing precision) or still invoke it on every frame, leaving the scheduling and fusion decisions as fixed heuristics rather than learned, cost-aware policies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262194/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes high-rate IMU streams (gyroscope and accelerometer) and low-rate RGB images. A key design constraint is that the Select Agent operates on IMU-only pre-integrated states (Δp, Δq, Δv, Δt) without requiring any visual feature extraction, enabling it to skip the entire VO pipeline on redundant frames. The output is a per-frame 6-DoF fused pose (position, velocity, quaternion orientation) plus a single global metric scale estimated at initialization via a VINS-Mono-style linear system; the Fusion Agent internally produces a 7-dimensional per-axis VO mixing weight vector that blends IMU and VO estimates independently along each degree of freedom.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-agent RL architecture that decouples two sequential decisions from the monolithic VIBA backend. A Select Agent (small MLP trained with PPO) observes only IMU pre-integration states and outputs a binary action to run or skip the DPVO-inspired VO module, with a reward combining terminal ATE and a per-VO-call penalty to encourage sparse invocation. A composite Fusion Agent first uses a supervised MLP to estimate metric velocity from scaled VO poses and IMU pre-integration, then an RL policy (MLP2, PPO-fine-tuned after supervised initialization) outputs per-axis fusion weights in [0,1]⁷ that convexly blend IMU and VO position/velocity and slerp orientation, with a reward penalizing trajectory error and overconfident uncertainty. A lightweight IMU Bias Estimator (two encoder networks for gyro and accelerometer) provides pre-compensated inertial measurements as the foundation for both agents. The VO module itself is a differentiable, patch-based recurrent optimizer (inspired by DPVO) that runs only when the Select Agent activates it, reducing average backend cost by 9.5× compared to ORB-SLAM3.

## Results

On EuRoC MAV, the proposed method achieves an average ATE of 0.092 m, matching VI-DSO (0.091 m) and approaching DM-VIO (0.069 m), while running at 39 FPS with only 4.37 GB peak VRAM. Compared to the closest GPU-based peer DPVO, it improves average ATE from 0.106 m to 0.092 m while increasing throughput by 1.77× (39 vs. 22 FPS) and reducing VRAM by 11% (4.37 vs. 4.92 GB). Against the gold-standard ORB-SLAM3 (0.041 m ATE), the method trades ~2.2× accuracy for a 9.5× reduction in average backend optimization cost (12.77 ms vs. 121.09 ms BA/VIBA). On TUM-VI, the method achieves 0.80 m average ATE, competitive with DM-VIO (0.77 m) and substantially better than VINS-Mono (0.94 m) and OKVIS (1.22 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical CPU-based VIO, the method clearly outperforms all filter-based systems (MSCKF 0.413 m, ROVIO 0.225 m, OKVIS 0.221 m) and VINS-Mono (0.187 m), achieving 0.092 m average ATE on EuRoC. It roughly ties VI-DSO (0.091 m) but trails DM-VIO (0.069 m) and ORB-SLAM3 (0.041 m) in raw accuracy, reflecting the deliberate trade-off of reducing VIBA invocation frequency. Among GPU-based learning methods, the method dominates on all three axes: it beats DPVO (0.106 m, 22 FPS, 4.92 GB) in accuracy, speed (1.77×), and memory; it substantially outperforms iSLAM (0.529 m, 31 FPS, 6.47 GB) and DROID-SLAM (0.188 m, 14 FPS, 8.63 GB) in every metric, with a 45.2% VRAM reduction over DROID. On TUM-VI, the method (0.80 m) is competitive with DM-VIO (0.77 m) and clearly ahead of VINS-Mono (0.94 m) and OKVIS (1.22 m), though it lags DM-VIO on the Corridor and Slide sequences. The CPU-side timing breakdown (Table 3) confirms the structural efficiency gain: BA/VIBA cost drops from 121.09 ms (ORB-SLAM3) and 26.49 ms (DM-VIO) to 12.77 ms, representing a 9.5× reduction over ORB-SLAM3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DPVO's patch-based recurrent optimization as its VO backend and adapts VINS-Mono's linear initialization for metric scale recovery, but departs from both by wrapping them in a dual-agent RL layer that makes scheduling and fusion explicit sequential decisions. It distinguishes itself from Messikommer et al.'s RL-for-keyframe-selection by operating at a higher level: the Select Agent decides whether to invoke the entire VO pipeline using only IMU signals, before any visual features are extracted, rather than tuning heuristics within an already-running VO. Compared to end-to-end DL VIO (VINet, SelfVIO) and hybrid methods (iSLAM, DROID-SLAM), the paper retains a classical optimization backend but reduces its invocation frequency and replaces fixed-gain fusion with a learned, cost-aware policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward deploying VIO on resource-constrained edge devices by cutting backend optimization cost by ~9.5× versus ORB-SLAM3 while maintaining accuracy competitive with DM-VIO, and achieving 1.77× higher throughput than DPVO at lower VRAM. However, it still trails the gold-standard accuracy of ORB-SLAM3, and evaluation is limited to indoor MAV/handheld benchmarks (EuRoC, TUM-VI); the authors note that driving-focused methods show severe domain gaps when transferred. Open questions include robustness under extrinsic miscalibration, prolonged VO outages, and whether the RL policies generalize to substantially different motion regimes or sensor configurations.
