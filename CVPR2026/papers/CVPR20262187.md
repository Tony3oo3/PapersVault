## Problem

CLIP achieves strong zero-shot image classification but its high-dimensional, entangled embeddings offer no human-understandable account of why a particular prediction is made. Concept Bottleneck Models provide interpretable intermediate representations but require dense concept supervision and cannot generalize to unseen classes. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether CLIP's zero-shot predictions can be faithfully decomposed into human-interpretable concepts without additional annotation or per-instance optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a set of candidate class label strings, both encoded by a frozen CLIP backbone (RN50, ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, or SigLIP ViT-SO400M/14). It also requires a pre-defined set of m concept phrases (e.g., 'has feathers', 'made of metal') whose CLIP text embeddings initialize the concept basis. The output is a zero-shot class prediction plus a per-concept interaction vector (the Hadamard product of the image's and the predicted class's concept activations), where each entry quantifies how strongly a specific concept drives the decision. No per-image optimization is performed at inference; a single matrix multiplication suffices.

## Method

EZPC learns a single linear projection matrix A ∈ R^{d×m} that maps CLIP's d-dimensional joint image-text embedding space into an m-dimensional concept space, where each column of A corresponds to a human-interpretable concept direction. A is initialized from the CLIP text embeddings of domain-relevant concept phrases (sourced from LF-CBM/GPT-3) and trained with two complementary objectives: a matching loss (MSE between A and the concept embedding matrix Φ) that anchors the projection to known concept directions for interpretability, and a reconstruction loss (KL divergence between the softmax of concept-space dot products and the softmax of original CLIP dot products) that preserves CLIP's similarity structure. At inference, both the image embedding and each class label embedding are projected into concept space via A; classification is the argmax of the dot product in concept space, and the element-wise product of the two projected vectors yields a faithful, per-concept explanation by construction. This single learned projection replaces the per-image optimization of SpLiCE and the concept-bank retrieval of Z-CBM, adding only ~0.1 ms of latency over vanilla CLIP.

## Results

On the generalized zero-shot setting (harmonic mean of seen/unseen accuracy), EZPC stays within ~1% of CLIP on CIFAR-100 (0.403 vs. 0.408), ImageNet-100 (0.682 vs. 0.693), and CUB (0.465 vs. 0.474), with a larger ~5% gap on ImageNet-1k (0.481 vs. 0.530) and ~1% on Places365 (0.352 vs. 0.362). EZPC substantially outperforms the prior concept-based baselines Z-CBM and SpLiCE by 10–28% in harmonic mean across most datasets. Inference overhead is negligible: EZPC adds only ~0.1 ms per image over CLIP (5.90 vs. 5.77 ms), whereas Z-CBM and SpLiCE incur 94× and 59× overhead respectively due to per-image optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CLIP (the strongest baseline), EZPC retains nearly all zero-shot accuracy: the harmonic-mean gap is ≤1% on CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, CUB, and Places365, widening to ~5% on the large-scale ImageNet-1k. Against Z-CBM [32], EZPC wins by a wide margin on every dataset except Places365 (where it is 0.5% lower), with the largest gains on CUB (+27.6% H) and ImageNet-100 (+9.7% H), indicating that Z-CBM's reliance on large concept banks and costly regressions severely limits its fine-grained and mid-scale performance. Against SpLiCE [3], EZPC dominates across all five datasets, exceeding it by 10–39% in harmonic mean; SpLiCE's per-image sparse optimization yields the weakest accuracy and the second-highest inference cost (59× CLIP). In terms of efficiency, EZPC matches CLIP's latency (5.90 vs. 5.77 ms/image on H100), while Z-CBM and SpLiCE require 542 ms and 339 ms respectively, making EZPC the only concept-based method suitable for real-time or large-scale deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

EZPC carves new ground at the intersection of Concept Bottleneck Models and vision-language models by learning a single linear projection that maps CLIP's joint image-text embedding space into a human-interpretable concept space, trained with a matching loss (anchoring to GPT-3-generated concept embeddings from LF-CBM) and a KL-divergence reconstruction loss. It departs from SpLiCE, which requires expensive per-image sparse optimization, and Z-CBM, which relies on large concept banks and costly regression, by learning one shared projection matrix applicable to all inputs. The method also extends the CBM paradigm beyond its traditional closed-world, supervision-heavy setting by leveraging CLIP's pretrained text encoder to define concept directions without any concept-level annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EZPC demonstrates that open-vocabulary zero-shot recognition and concept-level interpretability need not be mutually exclusive, with only ~0.1 ms inference overhead over CLIP compared to 59–94× overhead for prior interpretable baselines, making it practical for large-scale deployment. Key limitations include the linear projection assumption (nonlinear semantic relationships may be lost), dependence on the quality and coverage of the GPT-3-generated concept vocabulary, and restriction to classification tasks. Open questions include whether non-linear or hierarchical concept mappings could recover the remaining accuracy gap on large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1k, and whether the approach extends to multimodal reasoning or generative tasks.
