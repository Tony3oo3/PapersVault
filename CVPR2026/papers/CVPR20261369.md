## Problem

Current visual projectors in MLLMs map images into long sequences of continuous, highly correlated patch embeddings that behave fundamentally differently from the discrete, semantically independent text tokens LLMs are trained on. This entanglement, combined with fixed token budgets that ignore image complexity and the absence of principled granularity control, creates a structural mismatch that inflates KV-cache cost and degrades reasoning under tight token budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes patch-level feature vectors (N patches per image) from a frozen pre-trained vision encoder (CLIP, SigLIP, or DINOv2) and produces a variable-length sequence of disentangled visual tokens, where each token encodes one semantically coherent concept. The token count is not fixed but adapts per image based on a cosine-similarity threshold θ, yielding roughly 13–137 tokens across benchmarks (e.g., ~48 for simple SQA-IMG scenes vs. ~80 for text-heavy POPE images). Tokens are ordered by spatial coordinates before being fed to the LLM, and θ can be adjusted at inference time without retraining to trade detail against latency.

## Method

DiVT performs clustering-based visual tokenization in three stages: (1) adaptive centroid selection, where a greedy degree-based algorithm on a cosine-similarity adjacency graph picks semantically distinct centroids without pre-specifying k, so the cluster count emerges from the feature distribution; (2) cluster refinement, where each patch is reassigned to its nearest centroid to correct the greedy initial allocation; (3) visual token formulation, where a single cross-attention layer with the centroid as query and a cluster-restricted mask aggregates all patches in a cluster into one token, with learnable positional embeddings injected only into the value branch. A single similarity threshold θ controls semantic granularity (higher θ → finer, more tokens; lower θ → coarser, fewer tokens) and is tunable at inference without retraining. The entire projector is trained end-to-end using only the LLM's language modeling objective, with no external supervision, so the resulting tokens are shaped by how the LLM internally exploits semantic structure.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5 7B, DiVT with θ=0.75 (136.5 avg. tokens) achieves 66.7 MMBench, 78.2 VQAv2, and 62.0 GQA, matching or exceeding the full 576-token MLP projector (64.3, 78.5, 62.0) while using only ~24% of the tokens. Under aggressive compression to 35.7 tokens (θ=0.5), DiVT still scores 65.0 MMBench and 77.0 VQAv2, substantially outperforming all training-free baselines at the same budget. Even at an extreme 13.5 tokens (θ=0.3), DiVT retains 64.2 MMBench and 75.3 VQAv2, far exceeding the 31.6 and 46.8 achieved by ToME at 32 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only other trained projector, TokenPacker, DiVT wins on MMBench, VQAv2, and GQA at every matched token budget (e.g., at ~64 tokens: 64.3 vs. 64.1 MMB, 77.7 vs. 77.2 VQAv2, 61.6 vs. 61.1 GQA), while TokenPacker edges ahead by 0.1–1.1 points on MM-Vet and POPE. Among training-free methods, DiVT dominates across all budgets: at ~128 tokens, the best training-free baseline (VisPruner) scores 62.7 MMB and 75.8 VQAv2, whereas DiVT achieves 66.7 and 78.2; at ~32 tokens, the gap widens dramatically (VisPruner 58.4/67.7 vs. DiVT 65.0/77.0). The advantage grows as the budget tightens, with DiVT at 13.5 tokens still outperforming ToME at 32 tokens by 32.6 points on MMBench. In the projector comparison (Table 2), DiVT at 63.7 tokens matches the full 576-token MLP on MMBench (64.3) and exceeds it on VQAv2 (77.7 vs. 78.5) and GQA (61.6 vs. 62.0), while outperforming Resampler, C-Abstractor, Pixel-Shuffle, and LDP-v2 on all five reported benchmarks. DiVT also generalizes across vision encoders (CLIP, SigLIP, DINOv2) and scales to a 13B LLM backbone, maintaining comparable performance with ~13% of the original token count.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiVT departs from the dominant paradigm of treating visual projectors as spatial compressors (MLP on fixed patches, grid-wise pooling, pixel-shuffle) or global summarizers (resampler queries), instead framing tokenization as a semantic clustering problem in feature space. It shares the goal of variable-length semantic tokens with Chat-Univi and SeTok but avoids their reliance on auxiliary supervision or multi-stage refinement, training solely with the LLM's language modeling objective. The method also contrasts with training-free token pruning (ToME, FastV, PruMerge, VisionZip, VisPruner) by generating a compact token set upfront during the forward pass rather than discarding tokens post-hoc, thereby maintaining full compatibility with the pre-trained LLM's training dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DiVT offers a practical, plug-and-play projector that reduces visual tokens by 75–97% (576 → 13.5) with minimal accuracy loss, directly cutting KV-cache memory and inference latency for MLLM deployment. The single similarity threshold providing training-free granularity control at inference is a notable practical feature for practitioners who need to match varying computational budgets without retraining. Open questions include whether the greedy-then-refine clustering scales gracefully to very high-resolution or video inputs, and whether the approach's reliance on cosine-similarity structure in the encoder's final layer generalizes to encoders trained with fundamentally different objectives (e.g., generative or detection-based).
