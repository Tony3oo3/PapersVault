## Problem

Pure convolutional models for spatiotemporal predictive learning offer full parallelism and efficiency over recurrent architectures, but their fixed, uniform receptive fields cannot adapt to spatially varying motion patterns—textured regions need broad integration while homogeneous areas need suppression of redundant low-frequency responses. No prior work unifies biological center-surround opponency, frequency-domain band-pass filtering, and adaptive large-kernel fusion within a single fully convolutional STPL framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes T_in past video frames (e.g., 10 frames at 64×64×1 for Moving MNIST, 4 frames at 32×32×2 for TaxiBJ, 10 frames at 128×128×1 for KTH, 4 frames at 256×256×3 for Human3.6M) and predicts T_out future frames at the same resolution. Frames are encoded by a shared spatial encoder, packed along the temporal dimension into a single latent tensor, processed by the translator, then unpacked and decoded symmetrically. Training is self-supervised: the target is the ground-truth future frames, with no external labels required.

## Method

PFGNet follows a SimVP-style encoder-translator-decoder pipeline whose core translator is the Peripheral Frequency Gating (PFG) block. Each PFG block extracts three pixel-wise spectral cues—Sobel gradient magnitude, Laplacian curvature, and local variance—via fixed depthwise convolutions, then maps them through a 1×1 convolution to produce per-pixel softmax gating weights over K multi-scale peripheral responses (k ∈ {9, 15, 31}). Each peripheral response is computed by separable 1D convolutions (1×k horizontal followed by k×1 vertical) and subtracts a learnable β-scaled 3×3 center kernel, forming an adaptive ring-shaped band-pass filter that amplifies mid-frequency motion while suppressing low-frequency background and high-frequency noise. A lightweight MSInit module provides multi-scale initialization, and GLU channel mixing with GRN normalization stabilizes training. The separable decomposition reduces per-channel kernel cost from O(k²) to O(2k), yielding a 15× reduction for k=31, enabling SOTA or near-SOTA accuracy with substantially fewer parameters and FLOPs than recurrent or attention-based baselines.

## Results

On TaxiBJ, PFGNet achieves the best MSE (0.2881) with only 1.9M parameters and 0.6G FLOPs, outperforming all recurrent and recurrent-free baselines. On Moving MNIST, PFGNet attains MSE=15.2 and SSIM=0.967, surpassing the best recurrent method VMRNN (MSE=16.5, SSIM=0.965) and the best recurrent-free method TAU (MSE=19.8, SSIM=0.957). On KTH (10→20), PFGNet achieves the highest SSIM (0.911) among all methods. On Human3.6M, PFGNet reaches MAE=1392.4 and SSIM=0.9838 with 7.3M parameters, matching or exceeding all recurrent-free baselines except TAU (which uses 5.2× more parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PFGNet achieves the best MSE on all four benchmarks: 15.2 on Moving MNIST (vs. 16.5 for VMRNN, the strongest recurrent baseline), 0.2881 on TaxiBJ (vs. 0.2887 for VMRNN), and competitive SSIM/PSNR on KTH. On TaxiBJ, PFGNet uses only 1.9M parameters and 0.6G FLOPs—roughly 7× fewer parameters than SimVP (13.8M) and 5× fewer FLOPs than TAU (2.5G)—while matching or exceeding their accuracy. On KTH (10→20), PFGNet attains the highest SSIM (0.911) versus VMRNN (0.907) and SimVP (0.905), though it trails VMRNN slightly on PSNR for the 10→40 setting (32.64 vs. 32.69). On Human3.6M, PFGNet is second-best on MAE (1392.4 vs. TAU's 1390.7) and SSIM (0.9838 vs. 0.9839), but with 5.2× fewer parameters (7.3M vs. 37.6M) and 3.1× fewer FLOPs (58.3G vs. 182.0G) than TAU. Recurrent baselines such as PredRNN++ and MIM require 3–6× more parameters and 10–18× more FLOPs to reach comparable SSIM on Human3.6M, highlighting PFGNet's efficiency advantage in the fully convolutional setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PFGNet is architecturally grounded in SimVP's encoder-translator-decoder pipeline, repurposing its spatial backbone and temporal packing strategy while replacing the translator with a novel PFG block. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads: the large-kernel convolution literature (RepLKNet, SLaK, UniRepLKNet) that showed wide receptive fields can approximate global context, and the classical center-surround / Difference-of-Gaussians model from computational neuroscience. The key departure is making the large-kernel response *spatially adaptive* via pixel-wise frequency cues (Sobel, Laplacian, local variance) that gate which scale to use, rather than applying a fixed or uniformly learned kernel. All large kernels are decomposed into separable 1D convolutions, a technique borrowed from efficient CNN design but here applied specifically to enable multi-scale peripheral responses at near-linear cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a fully parallelizable, recurrence-free STPL model that matches or beats recurrent and attention-based baselines with 5–10× fewer parameters and FLOPs on several benchmarks, making it attractive for latency-sensitive deployments (real-time traffic forecasting, edge-based video prediction). The biological grounding (learnable center-surround as a ring-shaped band-pass filter) offers interpretability that pure data-driven gating lacks. Open questions include whether the fixed set of three frequency cues and three kernel scales generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-horizon prediction, whether the approach transfers to 3D video or multi-modal settings, and whether the near-SOTA (rather than SOTA) results on Human3.6M indicate a ceiling for pure-convolutional methods on rich-texture, high-resolution tasks.
