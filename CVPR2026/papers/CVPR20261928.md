## Problem

Existing 3D city generation methods either require training on map or satellite data, limiting personalization, or adopt flat autoregressive tile-by-tile pipelines that lack explicit hierarchical planning and accumulate errors at scale. No prior framework combines user-customized text input, hierarchical urban reasoning, and unbounded iterative expansion into a single training-free system for realistic city-scale synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261928/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural-language prompt describing the user's desired city (e.g., a Harry Potter-themed park or a minimalist luxury mall) and produces a complete 3D city scene assembled from grid-aligned 3D assets, along with the capability to iteratively expand the city via additional user requests. Unlike autoregressive approaches, all grid tiles are generated in parallel rather than sequentially, eliminating causal dependency on previously generated tiles. The intermediate representation for each grid is a 2D isometric image rendered on a shared ground platform before being lifted to 3D.

## Method

Yo'City is a training-free multi-agent framework built on a hierarchical City-District-Grid structure. A Global Planner (LLM) estimates city size, identifies functional districts, and allocates them on an H×W grid, optionally augmented by a RAG module that retrieves real-world city structure from Wikipedia. A Local Designer then refines each district into per-grid textual descriptions enforcing spatial and stylistic coherence. Each grid is rendered via a produce-refine-evaluate isometric image loop (generate on a shared platform → edit/refine → evaluate with a VLM), then converted to 3D via a pretrained image-to-3D model (Hunyuan3D). For expansion, a VLM constructs a scene graph encoding qualitative distance relationships between the new grid and existing districts, and a combined distance-driven spatial objective plus Sentence-BERT semantic regularization is minimized over BFS-identified candidate positions to determine the optimal placement.

## Results

Yo'City achieves a VQAScore of 0.7151, the highest among all methods, compared to SynCity (0.6975), Hunyuan3D (0.6198), Trellis (0.6189), and CityCraft (0.5639). In pairwise visual quality evaluations (GPT-5 and human judges), Yo'City attains win rates of at least 76.5% across all five dimensions (Geometric Fidelity, Texture Clarity, Layout Coherence, Scene Coverage, Overall Realism) against every baseline, with most comparisons exceeding 85%. At the grid level, Yo'City also outperforms SynCity with an Alignment Score of 0.6927 vs. 0.6572 and an Aesthetic Score of 5.52 vs. 4.95.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Yo'City outperforms all four baselines on every metric. The closest competitor is SynCity [12], which achieves a VQAScore of 0.6975 (vs. 0.7151) and the highest baseline win rates (up to 21.5% on Texture Clarity), yet Yo'City still leads by 78.5–89.5% win rate across all visual dimensions. CityCraft [10] shows the weakest semantic alignment (VQAScore 0.5639) and struggles with non-modern or stylized prompts, though it achieves the highest baseline win rate on Scene Coverage (23.5–25%) where Yo'City's margin narrows to 75–76.5%. Trellis [61] and Hunyuan3D [29] are general-purpose text-to-3D models that produce single assets rather than coherent city layouts, resulting in VQAScores around 0.62 and win rates below 15% on most dimensions. Overall, Yo'City's advantage is most pronounced in Layout Coherence and Overall Realism (≥85% win rate in all comparisons), while its smallest margin is against SynCity on Texture Clarity (78.5% GPT-5) and against CityCraft on Scene Coverage (76.5% GPT-5).

## Relation to Prior Work

Yo'City departs from the dominant paradigm of training a dedicated diffusion model on satellite or map data (CityCraft, CityDreamer, Infinicity) by instead orchestrating off-the-shelf LLMs, image generators, and image-to-3D models within an agentic pipeline. Its most direct predecessor is SynCity, which also uses a training-free tile-by-tile approach, but Yo'City replaces SynCity's flat autoregressive generation with a hierarchical City-District-Grid planning strategy and a produce-refine-evaluate isometric image loop. The scene-graph-based expansion mechanism with distance- and semantics-aware optimization is novel relative to prior agentic 3D scene work, which has focused almost exclusively on indoor environments (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, i-Design) or single-image outdoor scenes (SceneThesis, Holodeck 2.0).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that high-quality, personalized 3D city generation is achievable without any task-specific training, relying entirely on the compositional reasoning of general-purpose LLMs and VLMs. This lowers the barrier for applications in virtual reality, digital twins, and urban planning where users need bespoke, expandable cityscapes. However, quality is inherently bounded by the underlying off-the-shelf components (GPT-4o for planning, GPT-Image-1 for editing, Hunyuan3D for 3D lifting), and the evaluation on 100 prompts with pairwise comparisons leaves open questions about scalability to truly massive cities, physical plausibility of generated structures, and robustness to adversarial or highly specific user prompts. The boundless expansion mechanism, while promising, has only been validated over a handful of iterative steps.
