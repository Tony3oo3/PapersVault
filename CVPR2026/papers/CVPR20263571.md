## Problem

Vision-Language Models suffer from a 'visual processing bottleneck' during prolonged autoregressive generation: they progressively lose grounding in visual evidence and lack contextualized visual semantic knowledge, prioritizing accumulated text over the original image. Existing remedies either sacrifice generalization through direct fine-tuning, incur prohibitive pixel-space costs, are fundamentally non-generative at the token level, or operate in latent space without properly encoding visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263571/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a standard RGB image paired with a text instruction and produces a text answer, but during generation it dynamically inserts continuous latent memory vectors (not discrete tokens) into the autoregressive stream. These latent vectors are generated on-the-fly by lightweight LoRA adapters when the model emits one of four special invocation tokens, and are immediately followed by a corresponding end token before decoding resumes. The short-term memory produces N_s=8 latent vectors encoding fine-grained perceptual evidence, while the long-term memory produces N_l=16 vectors encoding abstract semantic knowledge, both conditioned on a query of length K=8 derived from the current multi-modal hidden states.

## Method

VisMem extends the VLM vocabulary with four special tokens that trigger on-demand latent vision memory insertion during autoregressive decoding. A lightweight transformer-based query builder encodes the current multi-modal hidden states (visual + textual) into a contextual query, which is then dispatched to one of two LoRA-based memory formers: a short-term former attached to the vision encoder that generates perceptual evidence tokens, and a long-term former attached to the language model that synthesizes abstract semantic tokens. Training follows a two-stage GRPO procedure: Stage I freezes the policy model and optimizes the query builder and memory formers to maximize performance gain over a no-memory baseline; Stage II freezes the memory components and fine-tunes the policy model to learn when and which type of memory to invoke, with explicit penalties for incorrect type selection and negative-return invocations. The entire mechanism is non-intrusive—core VLM parameters remain largely untouched, preserving general capabilities while adding dynamic visual memory.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VisMem achieves a 65.5% average across 12 benchmarks, an 11.0% absolute improvement over the vanilla model (54.5%). Gains are consistent across all three capability domains: +8.9% in visual understanding (68.2 vs. 59.3), +14.4% in reasoning (60.2 vs. 46.6), and +10.6% in generation (68.3 vs. 57.7). The method also generalizes across nine base models (3B–38B parameters from Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OV, and InternVL families), with per-model improvements ranging from 2.8% to 25.9% depending on task and model size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisMem outperforms all 15 baselines across the 12-benchmark suite. Against the strongest direct-training baselines, it leads Vision-R1 (62.5%) by 3.0%, VLM-R1 (61.3%) by 4.2%, and OpenThinkImg (60.6%) by 4.9% in overall average. Among image-level methods, DeepEyes (60.5%) and OpenThinkImg (60.6%) are the closest competitors, yet VisMem still exceeds them by 5.0% and 4.9% respectively. Token-level methods (ICoT 59.1%, MINT-CoT 60.2%, VPT 59.5%) and the sole latent-space baseline Mirage (58.4%) trail VisMem by 5.3–7.4%. In the cross-domain generalization study (trained only on Visual CoT + Mulberry), VisMem gains +6.9% on MMVet, +9.1% on MuirBench, +20.2% on MV-Math, and +9.9% on MultiTrust over the vanilla model, outperforming the second-best method by 2.7–6.8% on all four unseen benchmarks. In the four-stage continual learning experiment on MMVet, VisMem retains the smallest performance gap relative to full-data training, whereas SFT degrades by over 10% and VLM-R1/Vision-R1 improvements are nearly erased (<0.5% retained) by stage 4.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisMem positions itself within the latent-space paradigm for enhancing VLM visual capabilities, departing from prior latent-space methods (Coconut, MemGen, SoftCoT, CODI) that operate purely in language space and from Mirage, which requires substantial manually labeled visual data. The architectural design draws on Dennis Norris's cognitive memory theory to motivate a dual short-term (visually-dominant) and long-term (semantically-dominant) memory system, implemented via lightweight LoRA adapters and a query builder that conditionally generate latent tokens during autoregressive decoding. The training procedure builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) with a two-stage curriculum that first optimizes memory content quality and then optimizes invocation timing, a decomposition not seen in prior memory-augmented VLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-intrusive design (four special tokens plus LoRA adapters, no core parameter modification) makes VisMem attractive as a plug-in enhancement for existing VLMs, and the demonstrated compatibility across nine base models from three families (3B–38B) suggests broad practical applicability. The cross-domain generalization results—trained on only two datasets yet transferring to four unseen benchmarks with 7–20% gains—point toward a memory mechanism that captures transferable visual-semantic knowledge rather than task-specific shortcuts. Open questions include whether the cognitive short/long-term distinction remains meaningful at larger scales, how the approach behaves with video or multi-turn interactive settings, and whether the GRPO-based training recipe generalizes to settings without reliable reward signals.
