## Problem

Existing self-supervised methods for category-level articulated object pose estimation from a single 3D observation struggle to disentangle geometry from articulation and fail to recover explicit joint parameters (pivots, axes, articulation states). Prior approaches either require multi-frame inputs, dense supervision, or CAD templates, and those that operate on single views still entangle shape variation with part motion, limiting downstream kinematic reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB-D observation rendered as a 1024-point cloud (no multi-frame sequences, no templates, no annotations) and produces a globally aligned canonical shape, soft per-point part segmentation weights, explicit joint parameters (pivot points, motion axes, and scalar articulation states per joint), the global SE(3) pose, and per-part rigid transforms. The output is structured as a skeletal blend-skinning representation where each part is anchored to a predicted keypoint (bone) and deformed by a joint-driven rigid transform, making the kinematic model directly interpretable rather than a latent code.

## Method

SCAPO is a three-stage self-supervised pipeline. Stage 1 uses an SE(3)-equivariant vector-neuron autoencoder (VNT layers for translation invariance, VNN layers for rotation equivariance) to factor out global pose and align diverse instances into a shared canonical space. Stage 2 defines a joint-aware blend-skinning deformation: a keypoint network predicts part centroids as bone anchors, a pose network regresses joint pivots/axes/articulation states, and a Mahalanobis-distance-based skinning field assigns soft per-point bone weights, together enabling bidirectional (observation↔canonical) point deformation. Stage 3 trains the full system end-to-end via cycle-consistency reconstruction, cross-space alignment against a learnable canonical template (shared category prior plus instance-specific residual shape), and four geometric regularizers (shape variance, joint-direction alignment, joint-to-shape proximity, joint-to-boundary attraction) that enforce physically meaningful articulation without any ground-truth labels.

## Results

On the synthetic HOI4D/Shape2Motion benchmark, SCAPO achieves a mean segmentation IoU of 84.98%, joint direction error of 3.89°, joint pivot error of 0.075, part rotation error of 5.78°, and part translation error of 0.086, outperforming the closest self-supervised baseline OP-Align (82.24%, 4.60°, 0.104, 6.73°, 0.115) across all five metrics. On the real-world dataset, SCAPO reaches 91.89%/98.29% segmentation mIoU (75%/50% thresholds) and joint mAP of 62.51/89.59/95.91 (5°5cm–15°15cm), surpassing OP-Align (90.02%/97.18%; 61.37/87.72/92.73) and the fully supervised 3DGCN (83.31%/95.83%; 47.51/85.79/94.59). SCAPO also beats the supervised NPCS-EPN on joint direction (3.89° vs 8.12°) and part rotation (5.78° vs 6.51°) despite using no annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest self-supervised competitor OP-Align [1], SCAPO wins on all five synthetic metrics: segmentation IoU (84.98 vs 82.24), joint direction (3.89° vs 4.60°), joint pivot (0.075 vs 0.104), part rotation (5.78° vs 6.73°), and part translation (0.086 vs 0.115). On the real-world dataset, SCAPO also leads OP-Align in joint mAP at all three thresholds (62.51/89.59/95.91 vs 61.37/87.72/92.73) and in part mAP at 15°15cm (70.53 vs 62.97), though it trails slightly at 10°10cm (44.72 vs 43.24 is actually a win; 46.77 for 3DGCN is higher). Compared to the fully supervised 3DGCN [13], SCAPO achieves substantially lower joint direction error (3.89° vs 7.65°) and part rotation error (5.78° vs 11.55°) without any pose or segmentation labels, though 3DGCN retains an edge in segmentation IoU (92.59 vs 84.98) and joint pivot (0.062 vs 0.075). Against EAP [15], SCAPO dominates on every metric, with joint direction error reduced from 27.13° to 3.89° and segmentation IoU improved from 68.20% to 84.98%. The classical ICP baseline is far behind on all metrics, confirming the need for learned articulation-aware alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAPO unifies two previously separate threads: SE(3)-equivariant shape-pose disentanglement (Katzir et al., ECCV 2022; Deng et al., ICCV 2021) and Gaussian-bone blend-skinning for articulated deformation (REACTO, CVPR 2023; BANMO, CVPR 2022). It departs from the closest prior self-supervised methods—EAP (ICLR 2023) and OP-Align (ECCV 2024)—by replacing their latent part transforms with an explicit joint-parameterization (pivots, axes, articulation states) and by introducing a learnable canonical template with instance-specific residual shape to decouple shared category geometry from per-object variation. The cycle-consistency and cross-space alignment losses are novel combinations that tie the canonicalization and deformation stages into a single end-to-end objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By recovering explicit joint parameters from a single unlabeled RGB-D observation, SCAPO enables downstream kinematic reasoning (e.g., robot manipulation planning) without the annotation cost or multi-frame requirements of prior approaches. The fixed joint/part count assumption and sensitivity to severe occlusion limit immediate deployment on highly variable or heavily occluded scenes, and extending the framework to variable joint topologies or open-vocabulary categories remains open. The canonical-template-plus-residual decomposition is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other category-level 3D understanding tasks beyond articulation.
