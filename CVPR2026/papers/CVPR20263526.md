## Problem

Naively applying a global, model-intrinsic confidence-growth reward (as in PACR) to vision-language reasoning is suboptimal because V-L reasoning is a heterogeneous mixture of sparse visual perception steps (~30%) and dense textual reasoning steps (~70%). This statistical imbalance causes mixture-induced signal degradation: the global normalization statistics are dominated by textual steps, compressing and misaligning the advantage distribution for the critical but rare perception steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263526/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as input, and the VLM generates a multi-step reasoning trace followed by a final answer. During training, N rollouts are generated per input and segmented into discrete reasoning steps; each step's log-probability is computed both with the real image and with a blank white image to derive the Visual Dependence Score. The final output is a policy update where each reasoning step receives a step-level advantage (combining sparse outcome and decomposed process signals) applied to all tokens in that step.

## Method

PDCR operates in two stages. First, it performs unsupervised skill decomposition: a Visual Dependence Score is computed as the log-likelihood ratio of each step's probability given the real image versus a blank white image, and Otsu's method (optimal 1D clustering minimizing intra-cluster variance) dynamically partitions all steps into visual perception and textual reasoning groups without external labels. Second, it computes a decomposed process advantage by normalizing the discounted confidence-gain returns via min-max scaling within each skill cluster independently, rather than against a global pool. The final step-level advantage is a weighted sum of the standard GRPO outcome advantage and the cluster-specific process advantage, ensuring each skill is evaluated against its own statistical baseline.

## Results

On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, PDCR achieves an average accuracy of 52.9 across seven V-L reasoning benchmarks, outperforming the next-best baseline PACR (52.2), DAPO (52.0), and sparse-reward GRPO (51.5). On the 3B backbone, PDCR reaches 45.2 average, surpassing PACR (44.4), DAPO (44.1), and GRPO (43.6). Gains are most pronounced on complex understanding tasks (e.g., MMMU-Pro: 42.5 vs. 41.5 for PACR on 7B) and mathematical reasoning (MathVerse: 55.0 vs. 54.3), while PDCR also leads on HallusionBench (68.4 vs. 67.6 for PACR on 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDCR consistently outperforms all three same-protocol baselines (GRPO, DAPO, PACR) on both model scales. Against the closest competitor PACR (naive global confidence-gain reward), PDCR gains +0.8 avg on 3B and +0.7 on 7B, with the largest per-benchmark margins on MMMU-Pro (+1.0 on 7B) and MathVerse (+0.7 on 7B). Against sparse-reward GRPO, the gap is larger: +1.6 (3B) and +1.4 (7B), confirming that the decomposed process signal adds value beyond the terminal accuracy reward. PDCR also beats DAPO (+1.1 on 3B, +0.9 on 7B), which rules out the possibility that gains come solely from mitigating GRPO's vanishing-advantage problem. Compared to external RLVR methods on the 7B scale, PDCR (52.9) substantially exceeds Perception-R1 (45.2) and Vision-R1 (44.5), though those methods use different training data and protocols. An ablation (Table 2) further shows that replacing the Visual Dependence Score clustering with random assignment drops 7B average from 52.9 to 52.3, validating that the data-driven decomposition—not merely the act of splitting the reward—is what drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends PACR (Progressively Ascending Confidence Reward), a model-intrinsic dense reward for text-only reasoning that normalizes ground-truth confidence gains globally across all steps. The key departure is identifying that V-L reasoning is a heterogeneous mixture of sparse visual perception and dense textual reasoning steps, and that PACR's global normalization causes 'mixture-induced signal degradation' for the minority perception steps. The solution repurposes Otsu's method (a classic image-segmentation thresholding algorithm) as an unsupervised clustering tool over a newly defined Visual Dependence Score, enabling intra-cluster normalization without external labels. This positions the work between costly external Process Reward Models and the naive global-reward approach, offering a self-contained middle ground.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDCR provides a practical, label-free mechanism to give step-level credit assignment in V-L RL training without the data-hungry cost of training a PRM, and the decomposition is fully model-internal. However, the performance gains over the naive PACR baseline are modest (≈0.7–0.8 points average across benchmarks), and the method adds an extra forward pass per trajectory for the white-image comparison. Open questions include whether the binary perception/reasoning split generalizes to more granular skill taxonomies (e.g., spatial reasoning, OCR, multi-image comparison), whether the approach transfers to other VLM architectures beyond Qwen2.5-VL, and whether the Otsu threshold remains stable as model confidence distributions shift during training.
