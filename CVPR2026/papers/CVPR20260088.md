## Problem

Standard classifiers trained with cross-entropy loss optimize over the clean-label distribution P(c|x) rather than the posterior P(c|y) given a corrupted observation y, introducing stochastic biases that degrade performance under high-uncertainty conditions such as image corruption or limited training data. While diffusion models have revolutionized image generation, no prior work has applied discrete diffusion directly to the classification label space to model the posterior distribution of class labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260088/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (tested at resolutions 224×224, 112×112, and 56×56 as proxies for increasing corruption) and produces a full posterior distribution over K=1000 class labels, from which a predicted class is extracted. Unlike standard classifiers that output a single softmax vector in one forward pass, DiDiCM refines the label distribution through sequential diffusion steps (as few as 2–8 NFEs), with the number of steps controlling the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff.

## Method

DiDiCM defines a continuous-time discrete diffusion process over the K-dimensional class label space: a forward ODE with a uniform transition rate matrix R_t = σ_t(11^T − KI) progressively corrupts the one-hot label distribution to uniform, and a reverse ODE guided by a learned Concrete Score matrix S_t denoises it back. The scoring model s_θ(y, c_t, t) predicts a single column of S_t per evaluation, trained with a noise-weighted score entropy loss that remains fully tractable for classification (unlike its intractable language-domain analogue). Two inference variants are provided: DiDiCM-CP exploits the rank-one structure of S_t to reconstruct the full score matrix from one model call per diffusion step (O(K²) memory, 1/Δt iterations), while DiDiCM-CL samples individual labels through N parallel reverse trajectories (O(K+N) memory, N/Δt iterations). The DiDiRN architecture extends ResNet by injecting label and time embeddings into each residual block's skip connection via SiLU-activated linear layers, enabling conditioning on the current diffusion state without altering the core convolutional backbone.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with ResNet-50, DiDiCM-CP (8 diffusion steps) achieves 80.40% top-1 and 95.29% top-5 accuracy under no-uncertainty conditions (224 resolution, full training data, Strong Aug), matching the best reported ResNet-50 value. Under high uncertainty (56 resolution, 25% training data), DiDiCM-CP with Strong Aug reaches 59.05% top-1 and 81.93% top-5, outperforming the standard ResNet-50 baseline (53.77% top-1, 76.03% top-5) by 5.3 and 5.9 percentage points respectively. With Weak Aug at the same high-uncertainty setting, the gap widens to 13.1% top-1 and 13.8% top-5. The accuracy gains over standard classifiers grow monotonically as uncertainty increases across all nine tested settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard ResNet-50 [23] trained with the ResNet-SB recipe [46]. DiDiCM-CP (DiDiRN-50, 8 diffusion steps) consistently outperforms standard ResNet-50 in top-5 accuracy across all nine uncertainty settings under both Weak and Strong Aug, with the largest top-5 gain of 5.9 pp (81.93 vs. 76.03) at the highest uncertainty (56 res, 25% data, Strong Aug). For top-1 accuracy, DiDiCM-CP surpasses the standard classifier in all high-uncertainty settings (e.g., 59.05 vs. 53.77 under Strong Aug at 56/25%) but is essentially tied at the no-uncertainty extreme (80.40 vs. 80.42). Notably, DiDiCM-CP trained with only Weak Aug (57.92% top-1 at 56/25%) still outperforms standard ResNet-50 trained with the full Strong Aug recipe (53.77%), suggesting the diffusion framework is more robust to weaker training pipelines. The paper also extends evaluation to ImageNet-C corruption types (Appendix E), confirming the trend that DiDiCM's advantage grows with corruption severity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the continuous-time discrete diffusion framework of Campbell et al. and the Concrete Score formulation of Meng et al. / Lou et al. (SEDD) from the language domain to image classification, exploiting the tractability of a finite label space to make the score-matching objective fully computable. It departs from prior diffusion-based classification work (which repurposed continuous image-generation diffusion models for classification) by operating natively in the discrete label space, and from the uncertainty-aware ESC classifier of Belhasin et al. by avoiding explicit clean-image reconstruction. The DiDiRN architecture extends ResNet with classifier-free-guidance-style conditioning on the noisy label and time step, enabling direct comparison with standard ResNet baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that only 8 diffusion steps at inference time suffice to match or exceed standard ResNet-50 accuracy on ImageNet, with gains reaching ~13% top-1 under high-uncertainty conditions (low resolution, limited data), suggesting that explicitly modeling the label posterior via discrete diffusion is a viable alternative to point-estimate cross-entropy training. Limitations include the sequential model evaluations required at inference (a compute overhead versus a single forward pass) and the O(K²) memory of the DiDiCM-CP variant; the authors identify diffusion distillation as a natural next step to close this gap. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet to transformers or other architectures, and whether the uncertainty gains persist on datasets with more extreme class imbalance or open-set conditions.
