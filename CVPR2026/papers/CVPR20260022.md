## Problem

Most vision-language retrieval models compress each image or caption into a single global embedding, implicitly assuming one literal meaning per image. This design causes models to overfit literal visual details while failing to capture the many-to-many, non-literal alignments (figurative, abstract, background, emotional) that humans naturally perceive. Existing multi-embedding approaches treat their multiple vectors as generic semantic capacity rather than as semantically distinct interpretive perspectives, and are trained and evaluated almost exclusively on literal corpora.

## Input / Output

At training time, each image is paired with a variable-sized set of category-labeled captions and image-side prompts spanning five interpretive lenses (Literal, Figurative, Abstract, Background, Emotional); the model processes all lens-specific prompts in a single forward pass of the MLLM, extracting slot embeddings at learned special-token positions (<PROMPT> for visual slots, five fixed lens tokens for text slots) plus a global embedding from the final token. At inference, a user supplies only a text query with no lens label; the query is encoded into all five lens slots and matched against image slots via lens-masked similarity, falling back to global cosine when no same-lens overlap exists. The Lenses dataset contains 105,669 images and 732,405 captions sourced from CC3M and WikiArt, with roughly balanced lens coverage per image.

## Method

Lenses builds on BGE-VL-MLLM (LLaVA-1.6 Mistral-7B) and introduces a set of learned special tokens that prompt the MLLM to emit multiple lens-conditioned slot embeddings per image and caption in one pass, rather than a single pooled vector. A category-aware similarity function computes a smooth-Chamfer score over slot pairs but applies a binary mask that zeros out all cross-lens interactions, so a Figurative visual slot can only match a Figurative text slot; when no same-lens pair is active, the score falls back to global-embedding cosine. Training combines three objectives: a multi-positive InfoNCE contrastive loss over the lens-aware similarity, a lens-conditioned caption-to-slot alignment loss that forces each caption to prefer its own-lens visual slot over other active slots, and an intra-image hinge diversity regularizer that penalizes high cosine similarity between different visual slots of the same image to prevent collapse toward a single dominant (typically literal) direction.

## Results

On the Lenses test set (5,500 images, 38,259 captions), the fine-tuned Lenses model achieves 89.09% R@1 and 98.18% R@5 for All I→T retrieval, and 58.87% R@1 / 80.74% R@5 for All T→I, substantially outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline BGE-VL-MLLM (70.67% / 36.68% R@1). Gains are largest on non-literal lenses: Figurative I→T R@1 roughly doubles from 28.17% (BGE-VL-MLLM zero-shot) to 56.02%, and Emotional I→T R@1 improves from 14.75% to 40.35%. On the ArtEmis affective benchmark, Lenses-All reaches 15.67% R@1 (I→T) and 13.44% R@1 (T→I), compared to 5.52% / 4.84% for the best zero-shot model (SigLIP-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lenses outperforms all eight baselines on every lens and both retrieval directions. Against the strongest zero-shot VLM, BGE-VL-MLLM [49], Lenses gains +18.4 points on All I→T R@1 (89.09 vs. 70.67) and +22.2 points on All T→I R@1 (58.87 vs. 36.68); even after fine-tuning BGE-VL-MLLM on Lenses without the lens-aware objectives, Lenses still leads by +8.2 points (I→T) and +5.0 points (T→I). Among the classic dual-encoder baselines, CoCa [46] and OpenCLIP [12] are the strongest zero-shot models on literal captions (≈11% All I→T R@1) but collapse to 4–5% on non-literal lenses, whereas Lenses maintains 34–56% R@1 across all five lenses. On ArtEmis, Lenses-All (15.67% I→T R@1) surpasses SigLIP-2 [39] (5.52%), OnePiece [42] (5.34%), and BLIP-2 [22] (1.62%) by 10+ points, confirming that lens-conditioned supervision transfers to affective art retrieval. CLIP [33] and Jina-v4 [10] trail CoCa/OpenCLIP on literal retrieval and are far behind on all non-literal lenses, indicating that standard contrastive pretraining does not capture polysemous semantics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-embedding dual-encoder line of work (PVSE, PCME, SetDiv, MaxMatch) by reorienting multiple embeddings from generic semantic variation to explicitly labeled interpretive perspectives ('lenses'). It builds architecturally on BGE-VL-MLLM (MegaPairs) and borrows the smooth-Chamfer set-to-set similarity from SetDiv, but departs fundamentally by masking cross-lens interactions and tying each slot to a specific category, rather than collapsing all slots into a single global score. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes large VLMs (InternVL3.5-38B, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) as structured annotators, a practice common in recent data-synthesis work but applied here to generate perspective-diverse captions rather than literal ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Lenses dataset (105K images, 732K captions) is the first large-scale benchmark explicitly evaluating polysemous image-text matching across five interpretive lenses, and the lens-aware evaluation protocol (slot-specific retrieval, LensCoverage, lens DCG) exposes failure modes that standard Recall@k hides. A key limitation is that all annotations are LLM-generated rather than human-annotated, raising questions about whether the five-lens taxonomy and the generated captions truly reflect how humans perceive polysemy in images. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the fixed five-lens taxonomy, whether the MLLM backbone requirement limits deployment, and whether the gains on non-literal lenses transfer to downstream tasks like affective art understanding or creative captioning.
