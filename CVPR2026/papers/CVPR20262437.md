## Problem

While classification mechanisms in vision-language models have been studied, the internal processes that support object localization remain poorly understood. VLMs inherit visual features from CLIP, which was trained with global image-text supervision and lacks pixel-level precision, yet they can still produce bounding boxes—raising the question of how spatial structure emerges from weakly grounded representations inside the LLM.

## Input / Output

The models receive RGB images (padded to square and resized to 336² px for LLaVA, or split into a global 448² thumbnail plus up to six 448² local tiles for InternVL) paired with a text prompt. For localization, the prompt requests bounding box coordinates, which are parsed and scored by IoU at thresholds 0.5, 0.7, and 0.9 (averaged). For classification, the prompt asks the model to list all objects restricted to COCO's 80-category set, with a prediction counted correct if the ground-truth class name appears anywhere in the response. InternVL additionally applies a 2×2 pixel-shuffle compression, reducing each tile to 16×16 visual tokens before the LLM.

## Method

This is a mechanistic interpretability study on two representative VLMs (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B and InternVL-3.5 8B) that combines five probing tools: (1) token-level ablations replacing object-aligned, register, gradient-ranked, or random visual tokens with a global ImageNet average embedding; (2) controlled perturbations such as mask padding and intra-object token shuffling to test whether localization depends on spatial arrangement or merely token presence; (3) linear positional decoders trained per layer to track how 2D grid structure is reconstructed from the 1D token sequence; (4) attention knockout blocking all attention from post-image tokens to object tokens in grouped layers; and (5) causal mediation analysis (CMA) via activation patching, where individual attention-head outputs from a source image (object present) are patched into a base run (object inpainted away with LaMa) and scored by a Mediation Fraction measuring how much of the source–base perplexity gap each head closes. The key findings are a containerization mechanism (object tokens define bounding-box extent regardless of internal semantic arrangement), a small set of task-critical heads concentrated in early-mid layers for LLaVA and mid-late layers for InternVL, and a sequential dependency in which localization causally relies on classification-critical heads.

## Results

On the filtered COCO validation subset, baseline localization accuracy is 35.34% (LLaVA-7B), 46.98% (LLaVA-13B), and 72.64% (InternVL-3.5-8B), with classification at 58.10%, 65.30%, and 83.30% respectively. Ablating object-aligned tokens causes a dramatic localization drop (e.g., 35.34%→5.92% for LLaVA-7B, 72.64%→11.27% for InternVL) while classification degrades less severely (58.10%→19.44%, 83.30%→33.19%), confirming that localization is more tightly coupled to specific visual tokens. Causal mediation analysis reveals that only a handful of attention heads carry the causal signal: layers 11–16 for LLaVA and layers 16–22 for InternVL, with top-10 heads showing only 1–2 shared heads across the two tasks. Ablating classification-critical heads still strongly degrades localization, indicating a sequential identify-then-localize mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability study rather than a method paper, so there are no external baseline models to compare against. The internal comparisons are between ablation strategies and between the two tasks. Object-token ablation is far more damaging than random-token ablation (e.g., −29.4% vs. −0.2% localization drop for LLaVA-7B at 8 tokens) or gradient-based selection (−19.5%), establishing that spatially aligned tokens carry the critical signal. Between the two tasks, localization is consistently more sensitive to object-token removal than classification across all three models (drops of 29–71% vs. 34–63%). For InternVL's dual-view architecture, ablating the global view causes a −36.4% localization drop versus only −9.7% for the local view, identifying the global thumbnail as the primary spatial carrier. CMA further shows that the dominant heads for localization and classification are largely disjoint (only 1–2 shared heads in the top-10), yet ablating classification-critical heads still degrades localization, revealing a sequential dependency rather than independent parallel pathways.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the mechanistic interpretability toolkit from LLMs (attention knockout from Geva et al., causal mediation analysis from Meng et al. and Wang et al.) to the visual grounding domain, building on prior VLM interpretability work that traced object representations for hallucination mitigation (Li et al.) and revealed stage-wise processing hierarchies via layer-wise probing (Yu and Lee). However, it departs from these studies by targeting object localization specifically rather than classification or high-level reasoning, and by providing the first head-level causal account of how spatial boundaries are computed. The containerization framing—where object tokens define spatial extent independent of internal semantic arrangement—is a new conceptual lens not present in prior VLM interpretability literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying that only a handful of attention heads in narrow layer ranges mediate localization, and that these heads are largely distinct from classification-critical heads yet causally downstream of them, the paper provides actionable targets for grounding-aware training (e.g., targeted head fine-tuning or attention supervision). The containerization finding suggests that current VLMs encode spatial extent in a surprisingly coarse, arrangement-agnostic way, which may explain their persistent localization failures and points toward more structured spatial representations as a design goal. Limitations include analysis restricted to attention heads (not MLPs or adapters), a single filtered COCO subset, and two architecture families; extending to segmentation, relational grounding, video, and training dynamics remains open.
