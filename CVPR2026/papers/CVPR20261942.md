## Problem

Existing deep functional map methods for non-rigid 3D shape matching focus almost exclusively on learning informative feature representations while treating the spectral basis (Laplacian eigenfunctions) as fixed, non-optimized components, leading to suboptimal performance under challenging non-isometric and topological noise conditions. Additionally, many state-of-the-art approaches depend on computationally expensive linear-system functional map solvers and multiple auxiliary losses (bijectivity, orthogonality, coupling), resulting in complex training pipelines and high computational overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D triangle meshes (source and target) with potentially different vertex counts and connectivity as input, and produces a dense vertex-to-vertex pointwise correspondence map as output. Internally, it operates on the first k Laplacian eigenfunctions of each mesh, which are modulated by learned inhibition functions to form adaptive basis functions. The functional map is computed via spectral projection (no iterative solver), and the final correspondence is recovered through nearest-neighbor search in the learned spectral embedding space, yielding a hard assignment matrix of size |V_Y| × |V_X|.

## Method

The paper introduces Advanced Functional Maps, a generalized framework where the fixed Laplacian eigenbasis Φ is replaced by a learnable basis Ψ = ΦG, with G = exp(−T) being a diagonal matrix of k learnable inhibition parameters (initialized to zero so G starts as identity). The inhibition functions are learned via a multi-scale, eigenvalue-agnostic heat diffusion mechanism that shares parameters across source and target shapes, requiring only k scalar parameters per shape. The entire pipeline—DiffusionNet feature extraction, basis learning, and map estimation via spectral projection—is trained end-to-end with a single multi-resolution unsupervised spectral loss that jointly supervises pointwise maps, functional maps, and basis functions across multiple eigenvector resolutions, eliminating the need for a functional map solver or auxiliary losses. A key theoretical result establishes that optimizing the basis functions is formally equivalent to performing spectral convolution on the original eigenfunctions, with the inhibition functions acting as spectral filters, thereby connecting the method to spectral graph CNN theory and enabling future extensions via learned filter architectures.

## Results

The method reports mean geodesic error (×100) across 12 benchmark settings. On non-isometric matching, it achieves 2.6 on SMAL and 3.5 on DT4D-H (inter-class), outperforming all baselines including HybridFMaps (3.5/3.9) and ULRSSM (4.5/5.2). On near-isometric FAUST and SCAPE, it scores 1.8 and 1.7–2.3 respectively, competitive with but slightly behind HybridFMaps (1.4) and ULRSSM (1.6) on the easiest settings. On anisotropic remeshing (F_a, S_a), it achieves 2.1–3.6, matching or exceeding all competitors. The method uses a single unsupervised loss and no functional map solver, yet surpasses fine-tuned variants of ULRSSM and HybridFMaps on cross-dataset and non-isometric tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On non-isometric benchmarks, the method clearly wins: SMAL 2.6 vs. HybridFMaps 3.5 (and 2.8 fine-tuned), DT4D-H 3.5 vs. HybridFMaps 3.9 (and 3.5 fine-tuned), ULRSSM 4.5/5.2, and DiffZO 4.3/4.1. On near-isometric FAUST (F), it scores 1.8, slightly behind HybridFMaps (1.4) and ULRSSM (1.6) but ahead of RFMNet (1.7) and DiffZO (1.9). On SCAPE (S), it achieves 1.7, matching RFMNet and beating ULRSSM (1.9) and HybridFMaps (1.8). On cross-dataset generalization (e.g., train on S, test on S19), it scores 5.9, substantially outperforming ULRSSM (18.5) and HybridFMaps (13.0), and even surpassing their fine-tuned variants (6.7 and 5.3). On anisotropic remeshing, it is competitive (2.1–3.6), matching HybridFMaps (w.FT) on S_a (2.3 vs. 1.7) but trailing slightly on F_a (2.1 vs. 1.8). Overall, the method's strongest advantage is on challenging non-isometric and cross-dataset settings, where it avoids the performance collapse seen in ULRSSM and HybridFMaps without requiring test-time fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep functional maps lineage (FMNet, GeomFmaps, ULRSSM, HybridFMaps) by identifying and exploiting a component that all prior methods treat as fixed: the Laplacian spectral basis. While existing approaches focus exclusively on learning feature representations through various regularizers (bijectivity, orthogonality, coupling losses), this work introduces 'Advanced Functional Maps' where the basis itself becomes learnable via inhibition functions, formalized as a multi-scale eigenvalue-agnostic heat diffusion. The key theoretical departure is showing that basis optimization is formally equivalent to spectral convolution with the inhibition function as the filter, thereby connecting the functional maps pipeline to the broader spectral graph CNN literature (ChebyNet, MoNet, GRAND). The method also streamlines the pipeline by eliminating the computationally expensive functional map solver in favor of pure spectral projection, justified by a proven equivalence between the two formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that the spectral basis, long treated as a fixed geometric prior, is a learnable component whose optimization yields meaningful accuracy gains—opens a new axis of research in shape correspondence that is orthogonal to the feature-learning improvements dominating the field. The practical impact is notable: a single multi-resolution spectral loss replaces the complex multi-loss formulations of SOTA methods, and removing the linear solver reduces computational overhead while improving numerical robustness. However, the paper acknowledges limitations under extreme non-isometric deformations and partial shape matching, where the Laplacian basis's structural assumptions break down. The equivalence to spectral convolution suggests future work could import richer filter designs from the GNN literature, but the current implementation (simple exponential heat kernel with per-frequency scalars) is relatively modest in expressiveness.
