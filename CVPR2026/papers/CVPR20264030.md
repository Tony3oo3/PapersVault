## Problem

Leading MLLMs and specialist OCR models largely fail to perceive fine-grained structural anomalies (stroke deletions, misalignments, blurriness) in rendered text, instead relying on linguistic priors to hallucinate corrections or dismissing low-confidence regions. This structural blindness makes both VTR evaluation metrics and RL reward signals unreliable, preventing effective optimization of text-to-image generators even for state-of-the-art models like Qwen-Image and SeedDream4.0.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264030/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The structure-aware recognizer accepts a generated text image (full-image or bounding-box region) and outputs character-level text with special markers flagging structurally anomalous glyphs. The composite reward function then produces a scalar in [0,1] combining semantic alignment (word-level Hungarian matching with NED and unmatched-word penalties) and structural quality (scaled proportion of flagged characters). The full pipeline takes a text prompt as input to the generator and ultimately produces an optimized text-rendered image after RL fine-tuning.

## Method

TextPecker replaces noisy OCR-based rewards with a perception-guided composite reward R = w_E·S_E + w_Q·S_Q, where S_E uses pairwise word matching via the Hungarian algorithm with NED and unmatched-word penalties, and S_Q = clip(1 − ω·N_a/N_P, 0, 1) amplifies penalties for rare but visually jarring structural errors. The structure-aware recognizer is built on Qwen3-VL-8B or InternVL3-8B, fine-tuned on a 1.4M-sample hybrid dataset combining manually annotated character-level structural anomalies from multi-model generated images and synthetic data produced by a stroke-editing engine (deletion, swapping, insertion operators on Chinese character stroke sequences rendered via SynthTIGER). RL optimization employs Flow-GRPO, which extends GRPO to flow-matching models by converting deterministic dynamics into a stochastic SDE, enabling group-relative advantage estimation without a critic. The approach is plug-and-play: it requires no architectural changes to the generator and can be applied to any text-to-image model using the same reward formulation.

## Results

On the Text Structural Anomaly Perception (TSAP) task, TextPecker (Qwen3-VL-8B) achieves an image-level F1 of 0.862 (English) and 0.925 (Chinese), while all specialist OCR models score near 0 and the best MLLM baseline (InternVL3-8B) reaches only 0.183/0.153 F1. For RL-based VTR optimization, TextPecker reward yields +38.3% semantic alignment and +31.6% structural quality over the base Flux.1[dev] model on GenTextEval (0.719 Sem. vs. 0.336), and on the well-optimized Qwen-Image for Chinese rendering achieves 0.897 Sem. and 0.973 Qua. on GenTextEval, surpassing the OCR-reward baseline by +2.3% Sem. and +2.0% Qua.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the TSAP task (Table 2), all three specialist OCR models (PP-OCRv5, GOT-OCR-2.0, MonkeyOCR) achieve near-zero F1 (≤0.025), confirming they are structurally blind. Among MLLMs, GPT-5 reaches the highest English TSAP F1 of 0.170 and Chinese F1 of 0.226, while Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 0.107/0.059 and Doubao-Seed-1.6-think scores 0.214/0.121; TextPecker surpasses all by a wide margin (0.862–0.927 F1). For the RL optimization comparison (Table 3), TextPecker consistently outperforms the OCR-reward baseline across all three generators: on Flux.1[dev] English it gains +11.7% Sem. on GenTextEval (0.719 vs. 0.602), and on Qwen-Image Chinese it gains +2.3% Sem. and +2.0% Qua. (0.897/0.973 vs. 0.874/0.953). The base (unoptimized) models are substantially outperformed in every setting, with the largest gap on Flux.1[dev] English where TextPecker improves Sem. from 0.336 to 0.719 (+38.3%) and Qua. from 0.672 to 0.988 (+31.6%). Notably, even on the already well-optimized Qwen-Image, TextPecker delivers meaningful gains, establishing a new state-of-the-art in Chinese text rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Flow-GRPO for RL-based optimization of flow-matching text-to-image generators, but departs fundamentally from prior VTR reward design, which has relied on OCR-based string-matching scores (edit distance to prompt) computed by specialist OCR models or MLLMs. The key conceptual departure is identifying that these evaluators are structurally blind—they hallucinate corrections for distorted glyphs and ignore low-confidence regions—making their reward signals misleading for policy optimization. The semantic alignment component extends the PNED/GNED lineage (Lex-Art, TIIF-Bench) with Hungarian word-level matching, while the structural quality term and the stroke-editing synthesis pipeline (building on SynthTIGER) are novel contributions that have no direct prior counterpart in VTR reward modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and specialist OCR models score near-zero on structural anomaly perception reveals a blind spot in the entire VTR evaluation ecosystem, meaning prior RL-optimized generators may have been trained on systematically biased rewards. The plug-and-play nature of TextPecker (applicable to any text-to-image generator without architectural changes) and the public 1.4M-sample dataset make this immediately actionable for the community. Open questions include whether the structural anomaly definition generalizes beyond English and Chinese scripts, whether the linear penalty formulation (Eq. 2) is optimal, and whether gains persist as base generators improve their native structural fidelity. The method also requires training a dedicated 8B-parameter recognizer, which adds deployment overhead.
