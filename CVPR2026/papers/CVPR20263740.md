## Problem

Existing cross-modal retrieval models rely on global feature alignment that compresses entire images and long texts into single vectors, losing fine-grained details such as object attributes and spatial relations. Additionally, their training objectives lack the discriminative ability to reject descriptions that are semantically similar but factually incorrect (counterfactual), and CLIP's 77-token context window further limits long-text comprehension.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263740/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a long-form text description (well beyond 77 tokens, as in DCI and DOCCI) and produces bidirectional retrieval rankings (I2T and T2I) via image-text similarity scores. During training, the image is additionally segmented into region nodes via SAM and encoded with a CLIP vision encoder, while each text variant is parsed into a scene graph by an LLM, so the model consumes structured graph representations alongside raw modalities. The final output is a ranked list of candidate texts or images scored by the learned multi-granularity alignment.

## Method

POGA introduces two synergistic modules. The Multi-source Graph Augmentation (MSGA) module uses a VLM (InternVL3) to generate paraphrased positives and a language model (Llama-8B) to create single-edit counterfactual negatives, then parses the image into region nodes (via SAM + CLIP) and all text variants into scene graphs (via GPT-4o-Mini), extracting a structured Modified Difference Information (M_diff) that logs exactly which entity, attribute, or relation was altered. The Hybrid Multi-granularity Alignment (HMA) framework then optimizes a four-term composite loss: a dual global InfoNCE loss over original and paraphrased pairs, a multi-positive/multi-negative node-level contrastive loss aligning image regions to text entities, a structural alignment loss trained through a Relational Reasoning Module (a transformer decoder that scores subject-object-relation triples with BCE and margin terms), and a focal disambiguation loss that applies error-type-specific penalties (hinge for attribute/object errors, BCE for relation errors) targeting only the specific counterfactual edits flagged by M_diff. This cascaded objective simultaneously stabilizes global understanding, enforces precise entity grounding, teaches relational reasoning, and sharpens factual disproving.

## Results

On DCI (ViT-L/14), POGA achieves 84.11% R@1 for both T2I and I2T retrieval, surpassing the previous SOTA GOAL (76.89% T2I, 76.59% I2T) by 7.2 and 7.5 percentage points respectively. On DOCCI (ViT-L/14), POGA reaches 86.29% T2I R@1 and 84.68% I2T R@1, exceeding GOAL (84.37%, 82.57%) by ~2 points. In cross-dataset transfer (DOCCI→DCI, ViT-L/14), POGA's advantage widens dramatically to 81.31% T2I R@1 vs. GOAL's 68.93% (+12.4 pp). For zero-shot classification, POGA (ViT-B/16) attains 89.93% on CIFAR-10 and 67.16% on CIFAR-100, outperforming both GOAL and the original CLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POGA outperforms all four baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the strongest competitor GOAL (CVPR 2025), POGA gains +7.2 pp on DCI T2I R@1 (84.11 vs. 76.89) and +7.5 pp on DCI I2T R@1 (84.11 vs. 76.59) with ViT-L/14; the gap narrows on DOCCI to +1.9 pp (T2I) and +2.1 pp (I2T) but widens to +12.4 pp in the DOCCI→DCI cross-dataset transfer. Compared to FineLIP (CVPR 2025), POGA leads by 5.4 pp on DCI T2I R@1 (84.11 vs. 68.71), confirming that graph-based structural alignment surpasses token-level fine-grained matching. Long-CLIP (ECCV 2024) and zero-shot CLIP (ICML 2021) serve as weaker references, with POGA exceeding them by 16+ and 73+ pp respectively on DCI T2I R@1. On zero-shot classification (ViT-B/16), POGA also beats GOAL on CIFAR-10 (89.93 vs. 87.54) and CIFAR-100 (67.16 vs. 59.70), indicating that fine-grained training does not degrade global representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

POGA builds directly on CLIP's dual-encoder contrastive architecture but departs from the global-alignment paradigm that has dominated long-text retrieval methods like Long-CLIP and FineLIP, which compress entire images and paragraphs into single vectors. It also extends fine-grained alignment work (GOAL, Flair, GLIP, RegionCLIP) by introducing an explicit graph-based representation that models not just object-level grounding but also spatial/structural relations and counterfactual disproving. The key conceptual departure is the use of LLMs to parse both modalities into structured scene graphs and to generate paraphrased positives and counterfactual negatives, creating a multi-source supervisory signal that no prior retrieval method has exploited in this combined form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The graph alignment paradigm addresses a genuine gap: existing methods can identify that an image contains a cat and a mat but cannot reliably distinguish 'cat on mat' from 'cat under mat,' a limitation that matters for e-commerce, medical imaging, and any application where attribute and spatial precision is critical. The strong cross-dataset generalization (12+ point gaps over GOAL in DOCCI→DCI transfer) suggests the learned structural representations are more transferable than token-level or region-level alignment. However, the heavy dependence on multiple LLMs (GPT-4o-Mini for graph parsing, InternVL3 for paraphrasing, Llama for counterfactual generation) introduces significant data-preparation cost and potential error propagation that limits practical deployment. Open questions include whether the graph parsing step can be replaced by learned modules, how the approach scales to very long documents, and whether the multi-granularity loss design generalizes beyond the DCI/DOCCI-style dense-caption setting.
