## Problem

Existing medical vision-language benchmarks predominantly pair single-modality 2D radiographs with free-text reports, failing to capture the inherently multimodal nature of real clinical diagnosis. For gastric cancer specifically, no dataset integrates multi-phase 3D CT, endoscopy, structured biochemical measurements, lesion annotations, and clinical narratives at the patient level, leaving VLMs unable to learn the cross-modal reasoning that radiologists and oncologists perform in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261521/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each case provides four CT phases (non-contrast, arterial, venous, equilibrium) as 3D volumes totaling 83.48K slices, one endoscopic RGB image, a structured table of 11 serum biochemistry indicators and 5 tumor markers, and 3D bounding boxes at three hierarchical levels (tumor core, regional lymph nodes, stomach region). For 2D-native models, CT slices are concatenated across phases into multi-channel inputs; for 3D-adapted models, a 3D Swin Transformer processes the full volume. Biochemical tables are pre-filtered to retain only abnormal entries (values exceeding physiological thresholds) with their test name, measured value, and exceedance ratio, mimicking how clinicians selectively attend to outliers. Outputs span five task types: free-text VQA answers, generated clinical reports, bidirectional image-text retrieval rankings, TNM disease-stage labels, and COCO-evaluated lesion bounding boxes.

## Method

The paper introduces Gastric-X (1.7K cases, 21,408 BBoxes, 26,760 VQA pairs) and a five-task benchmark, then adapts six VLMs—three general (LLaVA-1.5-7B, BLIP-2, X2-VLM) and three medical (LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo, MedVInT)—to the multimodal setting. The key architectural adaptation replaces 2D vision encoders with a 3D Swin Transformer and text encoders with MedBERT (yielding X2-VLM-Med), while a lightweight retrieval head is added to models lacking native cross-modal matching. Two auxiliary input channels are introduced without altering the backbone: bounding boxes rendered as colored overlays on CT slices serve as soft spatial priors, and abnormal biochemical values are converted into concise textual descriptors. All models are fine-tuned with AdamW (lr 5e-5, weight decay 0.01, 10% warm-up) on a single RTX 3090 under a 70/15/15 patient-level split, with the text encoder trained at 2× the visual encoder learning rate. Four input configurations (Image Only, +Table, +BBox, +Table+BBox) are evaluated to isolate each modality's contribution.

## Results

On the full multimodal setting (Image+Table+BBox), X2-VLM-Med achieves the best VQA performance with 79.9 F1 and 91.5% AUC, outperforming the second-best Med-Flamingo (73.5 F1, 86.5% AUC) by 6.4 F1 points. For report generation, X2-VLM-Med reaches 82.0 BERTScore-F1 versus 73.1 for Med-Flamingo. In cross-modal retrieval, X2-VLM-Med attains R@1 of 48.9% (Image→Text) and 47.5% (Text→Image), with mAP of 63.1 and 61.7 respectively. For disease stage classification, X2-VLM-Med scores 90.8% AUC, and for lesion detection it achieves 51.5 mAP and 79.6% localization accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X2-VLM-Med dominates across all five tasks, consistently outperforming every baseline. Med-Flamingo is the strongest competitor, ranking second on VQA (73.5 vs 79.9 F1), report generation (73.1 vs 82.0 BS-F1), and retrieval (R@1 42.8% vs 48.9%), but trails by 5–9 points on each metric. MedVInT is competitive on classification (80.6 vs 83.2 F1) and actually surpasses X2-VLM-Med on lesion detection AP@0.5 (72.1 vs 70.4), though it still loses on mAP (50.2 vs 51.5) and localization accuracy (78.5% vs 79.6%). General-purpose models LLaVA-1.5-7B and BLIP-2 lag substantially, with LLaVA-1.5-7B achieving only 59.1 F1 on VQA and 38.5 mAP on retrieval. Convolutional baselines ResNet-50 and Faster R-CNN are clearly outperformed by transformer-based approaches, with ResNet-50 scoring 82.7% AUC versus 90.8% for X2-VLM-Med on staging, and FRCNN reaching only 43.2 mAP versus 51.5 for X2-VLM-Med on detection. The Swin Transformer baseline is the closest non-VLM competitor on classification (90.1% vs 90.8% AUC) but lacks the cross-modal reasoning capability of the VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical VLM line of work from single-modality 2D benchmarks (MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert, PadChest) and recent 3D CT efforts (MedVL-CT69K, 3D-RAD) to a fully multimodal clinical setting that integrates multi-phase 3D CT, endoscopy, structured biochemistry, and narrative reports. Methodologically, it adapts X2-VLM by swapping in a 3D Swin Transformer vision encoder and MedBERT text encoder, and similarly modifies LLaVA-Med for volumetric inputs, following the pseudo-3D processing strategy already used by Med-Flamingo and MedVInT. The key conceptual departure is treating the full diagnostic workflow—rather than a single image-report pair—as the unit of supervision, which no prior gastric or abdominal cancer dataset has attempted at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Gastric-X is the first benchmark to align multi-phase CT, endoscopy, laboratory values, and expert annotations at the patient level for a specific cancer type, providing a realistic testbed for whether VLMs can perform the kind of cross-modal evidential reasoning clinicians actually perform. However, the dataset is limited to 1.7K cases from a single institution, and the consistent (but modest) gains from adding biochemistry and bounding-box cues leave open whether these improvements generalize to larger, multi-site cohorts or to cancers with more heterogeneous imaging presentations. The paper also does not probe failure modes in depth, so it remains unclear whether current VLMs genuinely correlate biochemical signals with spatial tumor features or merely exploit superficial statistical shortcuts.
