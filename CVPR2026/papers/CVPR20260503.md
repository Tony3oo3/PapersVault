## Problem

Existing AI image watermarking methods (both static signal-processing and learning-based approaches) are vulnerable to a simple two-step generative attack: captioning the image and regenerating it via a text-to-image diffusion model, which removes watermarks while preserving semantics. No prior watermarking technique was explicitly designed to resist this 'visual paraphrase' attack, leaving a critical gap in the robustness of AI-generated content provenance systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260503/cropped_page1_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

PECCAVI takes an RGB image along with five paraphrased variants (generated via SDXL at varying strengths) and saliency maps (from XRAI, MSI-Net, or Vanilla Integrated Gradients) to identify stable regions. The image is partitioned into an n×n patch grid, and the top 30–50 patches by stability score are selected for watermark embedding. The output is a watermarked image (PSNR > 29 dB) and a zero-bit Watermark Detection Probability (WDP) computed via exhaustive patch-and-channel scanning with a noncentral chi-squared hypothesis test, rather than a multi-bit payload.

## Method

PECCAVI first identifies Non-Melting Points (NMPs) by computing Intersection-over-Union of saliency regions across five paraphrased image variants, refining them with Non-Maximum Suppression, and projecting onto a uniform patch grid to score spatial stability. Watermarks are then embedded in the Fourier domain of DDIM-inverted latent representations of selected patches, using concentric ring patterns whose ring spacing encodes watermark strength dynamically coupled to NMP recurrence frequency (W_s = max(0.1, 1 − 0.25·(n−1))). A multi-channel strategy distributes watermarks across different latent feature channels per patch to reduce perceptibility and increase robustness. Detection reverses the pipeline: each patch undergoes DDIM inversion, Fourier transform, and a noncentral χ² statistical test across all latent channels, with the final WDP being the maximum detection score over all patch-channel combinations.

## Results

On 500 MS COCO images, PECCAVI (XRAI, Top-50 patches) achieves a Watermark Detection Probability (WDP) of 0.90 at paraphrase strength s=0.1 and 0.85 at s=0.2, substantially outperforming all baselines under visual paraphrasing. Image quality remains competitive with PSNR of 29.84 dB and SSIM of 0.93. Under traditional attacks (brightness, Gaussian noise, rotation, JPEG), PECCAVI maintains WDP ≥ 0.91 across all configurations, while DwtDctSVD drops to 0.01–0.14 and Stable Signature to 0.65–0.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PECCAVI (XRAI, Top-50) decisively outperforms all six baselines under visual paraphrasing: at s=0.1 it achieves WDP 0.90 vs. ZoDiac (0.81), Gaussian Shading (0.81), Tree-Ring (0.77), WAM (0.63), Stable Signature (0.59), and DwtDctSVD (0.00); at s=0.2 the gap widens to 0.85 vs. 0.70–0.71 for the strongest baselines and 0.00 for DwtDctSVD. Under traditional attacks (brightness, JPEG, rotation, Gaussian noise), PECCAVI is competitive with or slightly below WAM and Stable Signature in PSNR/SSIM (29.84 vs. 46.05/42.91 dB) but matches or exceeds them in WDP retention. The XRAI saliency variant consistently outperforms Vanilla Integrated Gradients and MSI-Net by 5–15 WDP points under paraphrasing, confirming that better NMP identification is the key differentiator. Overall, PECCAVI trades a modest PSNR reduction (~3–16 dB below WAM/Stable Signature) for a 10–90 point WDP advantage under the most threatening attack (visual paraphrasing), which the authors argue is the critical failure mode for current watermarking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy to the text watermarking literature, where Kirchenbauer et al. (2023) introduced LLM watermarks and Sadasivan et al. (2024) showed paraphrasing could remove them, and transplants that attack paradigm into the visual domain via a caption-then-regenerate pipeline using KOSMOS-2 and Stable Diffusion XL. On the defense side, PECCAVI builds on Fourier-domain watermarking (ZoDiac, Tree-Ring) and diffusion latent-space manipulation (Stable Signature, Gaussian Shading) but departs from them by localizing watermarks to semantically stable regions (NMPs) identified through saliency detection (XRAI, MSI-Net) and multi-channel frequency encoding, rather than embedding globally across the latent. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first work to jointly define the visual paraphrase attack and design a watermarking method specifically to resist it, filling a gap left by prior methods that were never evaluated against generative regeneration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a two-step caption-and-regenerate attack defeats all six evaluated watermarking systems (including industry-deployed ones like WAM and Stable Signature) is a significant red flag for the watermarking-as-safeguard strategy being adopted by policymakers (e.g., California AB 321) and companies. PECCAVI offers a concrete defense, but its high FID scores (17–55) and substantial computational overhead limit immediate practical deployment, and the evaluation is restricted to 500 COCO images at paraphrase strengths up to s=0.2 in the main comparison. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to video, whether NMP-based localization holds for abstract or highly variable content, and whether the defense can be made more computationally efficient without sacrificing robustness.
