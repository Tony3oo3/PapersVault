## Problem

Hierarchical VLA models suffer from two bottlenecks in action generation: (i) the large distributional gap between isotropic Gaussian noise and structured action distributions forces many denoising steps (NFEs) and risks infeasible samples, and (ii) conditioning solely on the current observation ignores temporal history, causing the policy to lose task-progress awareness and produce jittery, inconsistent trajectories. Existing remedies—long observation concatenation or full VLM re-encoding at every step—introduce prohibitive latency and misalign with single-frame pre-training distributions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a natural-language instruction, multi-view RGB images (224×224 from wrist-mounted and third-person cameras), and the robot's proprioceptive state, which are jointly encoded by a vision-language backbone into a multimodal embedding. The output is a continuous action chunk of shape H×A (chunk length × action dimension) produced by a flow-matching policy. Unusually, the flow policy does not start from standard Gaussian noise but from a retrieved task-level prior with similarity-adaptive noise scale and NFE, and the chunk is further offset by a learned consistency bias from the previous action history before denoising.

## Method

OptimusVLA augments a π_0.5-based hierarchical VLA with two lightweight memory modules. The Global Prior Memory (GPM) projects the multimodal embedding via a Prior Head MLP into a retrieval token, queries a memory bank of stored trajectories by cosine similarity, and constructs a weighted Gaussian prior (mean and variance) from the k nearest action blocks; a Prior-Aware Sampler then draws the initialization with similarity-adaptive noise λ and NFE N, drastically narrowing the prior–target gap. The Local Consistency Memory (LCM) applies a self-attention Consistency Layer to the previous action chunk and a Mamba-based Dynamic Awareness Module to model inter-chunk temporal dynamics, producing an additive consistency bias that enforces trajectory smoothness and progress awareness with negligible overhead. Training proceeds in three stages: pre-training the VLA under the π_0.5 protocol, contrastive training of the Prior Head via InfoNCE to learn task-discriminative retrieval embeddings, and finally freezing GPM while training LCM to predict the residual between the prior mean and ground-truth action chunks.

## Results

On LIBERO, OptimusVLA achieves a 98.6% average success rate across four suites (Spatial 99.6%, Object 99.8%, Goal 98.4%, Long 96.4%), surpassing the previous SOTA π_0.5 (96.9%) and OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%). On CALVIN (ABC→D), it attains an average completion length of 4.45, improving over π_0 (3.92) by 13.5% and π_0.5 (4.26). On RoboTwin 2.0 Hard, OptimusVLA reaches 38% average success rate, outperforming π_0.5 (29%) and π_0 (25%). In real-world evaluation, it achieves 85.0% on Generalization Tasks and 64.0% on Long-horizon Tasks, while delivering a 2.9× inference speedup and 3.1× fewer NFEs compared to π_0.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptimusVLA outperforms all 20 baselines across the three simulation benchmarks. On LIBERO, it leads π_0.5 by 1.7% and OpenVLA-OFT by 1.5% in average success rate, with the largest margin on the Long suite (+4.0% over π_0.5), where temporal consistency from LCM is most critical. On CALVIN, it surpasses π_0 by 13.5% in average completion length (4.45 vs. 3.92) and edges out π_0.5 (4.26) and VPP (4.29), demonstrating superior zero-shot generalization to the unseen D environment via GPM's semantic retrieval. On RoboTwin 2.0 Hard, it achieves 38% average success rate, beating π_0.5 (29%), π_0 (25%), and RDT (20%), with the largest per-task gain on Stack Bowls Two (58% vs. RDT's 30%), where LCM's bimanual consistency constraint is decisive. In real-world evaluation, OptimusVLA surpasses π_0 by 42.9% on Generalization Tasks and 52.4% on Long-horizon Tasks, while simultaneously achieving 2.9× faster inference (3.2 NFEs vs. 10.0 for π_0.5). No baseline is reported to outperform OptimusVLA on any primary metric in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

OptimusVLA is built directly on the π_0.5 hierarchical VLA architecture (vision-language backbone + flow-matching policy) and augments it with two memory modules. The Global Prior Memory (GPM) departs from the standard practice of initializing flow matching from isotropic Gaussian noise by instead retrieving task-level action priors from a memory bank of semantically similar trajectories, drawing on retrieval-augmented generation ideas but applied to the generative starting point. The Local Consistency Memory (LCM) addresses the Markovian assumption in mainstream VLAs (π_0, OpenVLA) by using a Mamba-based state-space model to encode recent action chunks, offering a lighter alternative to long-context VLM approaches (Interleave-VLA, MemoryVLA) that require full VLM forward passes per memory update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles two practical bottlenecks in deployment-ready VLA systems—excessive NFEs for flow-based policies and jittery, temporally inconsistent control—within a single unified framework, achieving a 2.9× inference speedup while simultaneously improving success rates across simulation and real-world benchmarks. The retrieval-based prior initialization is a clean, modular addition that could be grafted onto other flow-matching VLA architectures beyond π_0.5. Open questions include how the memory bank scales to truly open-world task distributions (where no similar trajectory exists), whether the cosine-similarity retrieval degrades under significant domain shift, and whether the LCM's Mamba-based temporal modeling generalizes to tasks with very long inter-action dependencies beyond a single chunk.
