## Problem

Multi-agent cooperative perception suffers from two practical challenges that existing methods inadequately address: time-varying communication latency that misaligns asynchronous feature streams across agents (causing up to 46% performance drop), and multi-source transmission noise that corrupts feature geometry (causing up to 17% drop). Prior latency-aware methods perform only local temporal alignment without global spatio-temporal context, while noise-robust methods operate at the signal level and overlook residual semantic inconsistencies, leaving a gap in robust fusion under combined latency and noise conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes ego-vehicle and remote-agent point clouds encoded by a shared PointPillar backbone into feature maps. Remote features arrive at the ego vehicle with a variable delay τ and are coordinate-transformed into the ego frame; the ego vehicle additionally maintains a buffer of K most recently fused historical features for temporal prediction. The pipeline outputs 3D bounding boxes via a standard decoder, evaluated with AP@0.5 and AP@0.7 on OPV2V, V2XSet, and DAIR-V2X under controlled latency (0–500 ms) and noise (heading/location perturbations) conditions.

## Method

CATNet is a three-stage adaptive compensation framework. First, STSync aligns asynchronous features using a Time-Augmented Recurrent Unit (TARU) that iteratively processes a K-frame historical buffer: at each step it predicts a motion offset from two preceding frames, warps the previous feature via deformable convolution, and fuses with the hidden state through a Spatio-Temporal Gate (parallel spatial + channel attention); the final predicted feature is anchored to the ego-vehicle's real-time feature via Deformable Cross-Attention. Second, WTDen performs signal-level denoising in the wavelet domain: a 2D Haar transform splits features into four subbands, a Wavelet Mamba branch (SSM with dual-path progressive high-to-low integration and interleaved cross-band scanning) corrects global misalignment, and a Wavelet Convolution branch with hierarchical filtering remedies local distortions. Third, AdpSel performs semantic-level refinement by iteratively partitioning features at multiple window scales, selecting top-k% salient blocks via a linear importance score, enhancing them with MLLA (Mamba-based linear attention) while recovering unselected regions with Inverted Bottleneck convolutions, and propagating discarded-region masks across scales to progressively focus on globally coherent areas; multi-scale outputs are fused by SplitAttention.

## Results

On the three cooperative perception benchmarks, CATNet achieves AP@0.5/AP@0.7 of 0.843/0.686 on OPV2V, 0.858/0.643 on V2XSet, and 0.723/0.565 on DAIR-V2X, outperforming the second-best method by 1.2%/0.7%, 4.1%/1.9%, and 2.1%/0.6% respectively. Under latency conditions (0–500 ms) on OPV2V, CATNet retains AP@0.5 of 0.756 versus 0.745 (ERMVP) and 0.738 (MRCNet), while under mixed localization and heading noise (σ=0.4/0.4) it reaches AP@0.5/AP@0.7 of 0.870/0.663, surpassing DSRC (0.854/0.638) and CoAlign (0.848/0.633). The model uses only 9.95 M parameters, comparable to or smaller than most competitors (e.g., DSRC at 40.64 M, How2comm at 35.80 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATNet outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and metric in Table 1. Against the strongest latency-aware methods, it beats MRCNet [11] by 2.9%/6.9% (AP@0.5/AP@0.7) on V2XSet and ERMVP [37] by 3.8%/14.4% on V2XSet, while on OPV2V it surpasses ERMVP by 2.3%/0.7%. Among noise-robust methods, CATNet exceeds DSRC [38] by 5.4%/3.3% on OPV2V and 2.1%/0.6% on DAIR-V2X, and outperforms CoAlign [21] by 13.0%/16.0% on OPV2V. On V2XSet, the gap over the second-best MRCNet is 4.1% in AP@0.5, and over How2comm [32] it is 10.9% in AP@0.5. Under progressive latency (Table 2), CATNet maintains a 1.1–4.8 AP@0.5 advantage over the best competitor at every delay level (200–500 ms), and under extreme mixed noise (σ=0.4/0.4, Table 6) it leads DSRC by 1.6%/2.5% in AP@0.5/AP@0.7. The No Fusion single-vehicle baseline is surpassed by 16.0%/12.7% on V2XSet, confirming the value of the cooperative pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATNet operates within the intermediate-fusion paradigm of cooperative perception, which has become the dominant technical route after early and late fusion proved limited. For latency, it departs from prior local-alignment approaches (SyncNet's P-LSTM, MRCNet's feature prediction) by introducing a recurrent temporal propagation mechanism with deformable convolution that models global spatio-temporal coherence across the ego-vehicle's feature history. For noise, it moves beyond the two established paradigms—knowledge distillation (DiscoNet, DI-V2X) and graph-based spatial alignment (CoAlign)—by operating in the wavelet domain and combining a state-space model (Mamba) for global structural correction with wavelet convolution for local detail recovery, followed by a semantic-level adaptive selector. The architecture synthesizes ideas from optical-flow-style motion warping, signal-processing wavelet decomposition, and recent SSM literature into a unified purification pipeline, rather than extending any single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical deployment challenges—non-deterministic communication latency and multi-source transmission noise—that are frequently idealized away in cooperative perception research, making its findings directly relevant to real-world V2X system design. The dual-purification strategy (signal-level denoising followed by semantic-level selection) provides a structured, modular recipe that practitioners can adopt or adapt independently. Open questions include whether the wavelet-domain approach generalizes to heterogeneous sensor modalities (camera + LiDAR), how the method scales to larger agent counts with growing feature buffers, and whether the Mamba-based global alignment can be replaced by lighter alternatives for edge deployment. The paper does not report inference latency or memory overhead, which would be critical for real-time vehicular applications.
