## Problem

Predicting how a 3D scene evolves under camera or object transformations from raw visual inputs remains unsolved because real-world perception is inherently partial: large regions are occluded, motion signals are incomplete, and local actions do not reveal global consequences. Existing diffusion-based methods for novel view synthesis or object manipulation lack robust 3D geometric control and cannot jointly handle partial geometry with incomplete transformation signals in a unified probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multimodal scene variables—RGB patches, depth patches, and optical flow patches—each indexed by a pointer encoding spatial location, time, and modality, and can be provided at arbitrary sparsity (e.g., dense flow with sparse target depth, or sparse flow with very sparse depth). Outputs are sampled from the conditional distribution over any unobserved subset of these variables, yielding novel-view RGB images, depth maps, flow fields, or integrated 3D scene geometry. Training data consists of 3 million RGB video clips (~1.4 trillion tokens), and the 7B-parameter model operates on sequences of length 4096–8192 pointer-value pairs.

## Method

P3Sim formulates 3D scene simulation as probabilistic inference over a graphical model of multimodal scene variables, implemented as a 7B-parameter causal transformer trained with next-token prediction on content tokens only. The key architectural insight is serializing scene variables as interleaved pointer-value pairs where pointers encode (spatial location, time, modality), enabling random-access decoding: by randomizing pointer order during training, the model learns to infer any missing variable from an arbitrary conditioning subset. A geometrizer module (Γ) deterministically derives partial target depth and motion-consistent optical flow from known 3D transforms and prior depth, providing explicit geometric conditioning. A persistent scene memory (μ) back-projects per-frame geometry into a global coordinate system and applies bidirectional consistency pruning to maintain a coherent 3D representation across frames. This design unifies novel view synthesis, rigid/deformable object manipulation, collision reasoning, and multi-agent prediction under a single autoregressive inference pathway parameterized by flow density and depth sparsity.

## Results

On the SEVA NVS benchmark, P3Sim achieves 21.54 PSNR on RE10K, 15.18 on LLFF, and 15.50 on DTU, outperforming both ViewCrafter and SEVA across all three datasets. On 3DEditBench for 3D object manipulation, P3Sim reaches 23.12 PSNR, 0.121 LPIPS, and 0.827 Edit Adherence (EA), substantially exceeding the strongest baseline LightningDrag (19.52 PSNR, 0.184 LPIPS, 0.722 EA) by 3.6 dB in PSNR and 0.105 in EA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P3Sim wins on every metric against all five baselines across both evaluation tasks. For NVS, P3Sim outperforms ViewCrafter by 0.66 dB on RE10K (21.54 vs 20.88), 4.65 dB on LLFF (15.18 vs 10.53), and 2.84 dB on DTU (15.50 vs 12.66), and beats SEVA by 3.43 dB on RE10K, 1.15 dB on LLFF, and 1.03 dB on DTU. For 3D object manipulation on 3DEditBench, P3Sim surpasses the strongest baseline LightningDrag by 3.6 dB in PSNR (23.12 vs 19.52), reduces LPIPS from 0.184 to 0.121, and improves Edit Adherence from 0.722 to 0.827. The margins over DragAnything and Diffusion Handles are even larger (8+ dB PSNR gap), indicating that the autoregressive probabilistic formulation with explicit geometric conditioning provides a clear advantage over diffusion-based drag and handle editing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

P3Sim is a direct architectural extension of the authors' prior work on local random-access sequence modeling [15], which introduced the pointer-value pair formulation with a causal transformer for 3D scene understanding. It departs from the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for novel view synthesis (ViewCrafter, SEVA, Zero-1-to-3) and drag-based editing (DragAnything, LightningDrag, Diffusion Handles) by replacing diffusion pipelines and latent inversion with a unified autoregressive probabilistic inference framework. The key conceptual departure from prior world models (Dreamer, Genie, GAIA-1) is the use of explicit geometric conditioning—partial depth and motion-consistent flow derived from a deterministic geometrizer—rather than purely semantic or learned action representations, grounding the generative model in projective geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying novel view synthesis, rigid and deformable object manipulation, collision reasoning, and multi-agent prediction under a single probabilistic inference framework with geometric conditioning, P3Sim offers a path toward general-purpose 3D scene simulation from incomplete perceptual data. The persistent scene memory mechanism enabling online amodal completion and temporally consistent 3D reconstruction is a practical contribution for embodied and robotics applications. However, the most impressive capabilities (deformable manipulation, collision, multi-agent) are demonstrated only qualitatively without standardized benchmarks, and no ablations isolate the contribution of the geometrizer versus the learned model. Open questions include whether the 7B autoregressive model scales to longer temporal horizons, whether the geometric conditioning degrades gracefully under significant sensor noise, and how the approach compares to emerging video diffusion models with explicit 3D structure.
