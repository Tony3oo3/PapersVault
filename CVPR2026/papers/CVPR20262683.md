## Problem

Existing multimodal federated learning methods assume homogeneous clients with identical modalities and tasks, or they improve global server performance at the expense of local client accuracy. No prior framework simultaneously addresses modality/task representation drift, task-aware local personalization, and architecture-agnostic knowledge aggregation in a unified manner, leaving a gap for practical edge-cloud collaboration under realistic heterogeneity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262683/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on three client types: unimodal image clients (32×32 CIFAR-100 images → class labels), unimodal text clients (AGNEWS text → class labels), and multimodal clients (Flickr30k image-text pairs → retrieval rankings), while the server targets MS-COCO cross-modal retrieval. A shared public dataset of 10K image-text pairs is consumed by both server and all clients for alignment and distillation; all features are projected to 256 dimensions. The server outputs i2t and t2i retrieval rankings (R@1), while clients output task-specific predictions on their private data.

## Method

FedAFD comprises three modules operating in a client-server loop. (1) Bi-level Adversarial Alignment (BAA) equips each client with an intra-modal and a cross-modal discriminator; the local encoder is trained adversarially to make its public-data representations indistinguishable from the server's within the same modality and across modalities, directly minimizing the source-target distribution gap. (2) Granularity-aware Feature Fusion (GFF) applies a two-stage attention-based gating mechanism (adapted from Dai et al., WACV 2021) at the sample level to adaptively blend local and global encoder outputs, preserving personalization while injecting generalized semantics. (3) Similarity-guided Ensemble Distillation (SED) computes per-sample cosine-similarity scores between each client's public-data representation and the global representation, normalizes them into aggregation weights, and distills the weighted ensemble into the global model via L2 loss—requiring no parameter-level consistency across heterogeneous architectures. The server iteratively trains on public data, distributes global features and encoders, collects client public-data representations, and updates via SED, while clients perform local epochs with BAA and GFF on private data.

## Results

Under the Non-IID setting, FedAFD achieves 33.18% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100, 51.98% on AGNEWS, 32.48% i2t R@1 on Flickr30k, and 60.16 rsum R@1 on MS-COCO, outperforming all baselines on every client and server metric. Under IID, the gains are even more pronounced on the client side: 61.04% on CIFAR-100 (vs. 46.52% for LOCAL) and 36.55% i2t R@1 on Flickr30k (vs. 30.70% for LOCAL). FedAFD also converges fastest, requiring only 20 communication rounds to reach the target server performance (rsum R@1 ≈ 57.50), compared to 21–29 rounds for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAFD outperforms all six baselines on every client-side and server-side metric under both IID and Non-IID settings. The largest client-side gains appear over CreamFL [43] and FedDFA [37], which sacrifice local personalization: on CIFAR-100 (Non-IID), FedAFD reaches 33.18% vs. 22.14% (CreamFL) and 23.09% (FedDFA), and on Flickr30k i2t R@1, 32.48% vs. 18.38% and 19.68% respectively. Against the strongest client-side baseline FedET [9], FedAFD still leads by 1.32 points on CIFAR-100, 2.60 on AGNEWS, and 9.85 on Flickr30k i2t R@1. On the server side, FedAFD edges out CreamFL (60.16 vs. 59.61 rsum R@1) and FedMKD (60.16 vs. 59.18) while also converging in fewer rounds (20 vs. 21). Notably, all distillation-based baselines (FedMD, FedGEMS, FedDFA) degrade client performance below even the LOCAL baseline, whereas FedAFD consistently improves upon LOCAL on every client task, demonstrating its ability to balance global coordination with local personalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: multimodal federated learning (MMFed, FedSea, CreamFL), feature fusion in FL, and representation-level knowledge distillation for heterogeneous models (FedMD, FedET, FedMKD, FedDFA). The closest prior work, CreamFL [43], addresses modality/task gaps via contrastive regularization but explicitly trades off local client performance for global gains. FedAFD departs from this lineage by repurposing domain-adaptation adversarial training (Ben-David et al.) for client-server representation alignment, borrowing attentional feature fusion [12] for granularity-aware local-global integration, and extending ensemble distillation with similarity-based dynamic weighting—combining all three into a single framework that simultaneously targets alignment, personalization, and architecture-agnostic aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights a practical and underappreciated failure mode: most existing FL methods, when extended to multimodal settings, actually degrade client-side performance below standalone local training, revealing that global optimization often comes at the cost of local utility. FedAFD's unified treatment of modality/task heterogeneity and model heterogeneity is a step toward deployable MFL in real edge-cloud systems. However, the evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (CIFAR-100, AGNEWS, Flickr30k, a 10K MS-COCO subset) with a fixed set of 10 clients, and the baselines are largely unimodal FL methods retrofitted rather than purpose-built MFL competitors. Open questions include scalability to larger foundation-model backbones, more than two modalities, dynamic client participation, and whether the adversarial alignment component remains stable at scale.
