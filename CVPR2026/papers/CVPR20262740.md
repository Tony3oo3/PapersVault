## Problem

Generative models and vision encoders have advanced on separate tracks with different objectives, yet both produce approximately Gaussian latent spaces whose relationship has never been formally unified. Prior work shows linear alignment within each family (encoders-to-encoders, generators-to-generators) but offers no operational mechanism linking the two, nor a geometric characterization of the shared space. This paper asks whether a single Gaussian latent geometry underlies both families and whether its linear structure can be exploited for semantic prediction and editing.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes CelebA validation images (~19k, split 15k/4k) and extracts per-image latents from five vision encoders (CLIP B/16, CLIP L/14, OpenCLIP B/16, OpenCLIP L/14, DINOv3; 500–1k dimensions) and three diffusion models via DDIM inversion (SD 1.5, SD 2.1, LCMv7; ~16k dimensions). Outputs include per-attribute logistic-regression predictions, edited images produced by shifting DDIM-inverted noise along a single linear direction (no prompts or fine-tuning), ridge-regularized linear transfer maps between generative and encoder spaces, and a shared k-dimensional subspace recovered by multi-view GCCA. The key unusual aspect is that editing operates directly in the high-dimensional diffusion noise code rather than in a learned latent or via text-prompt guidance.

## Method

The paper formalizes the Universal Normal Embedding (UNE) as a multivariate standard Gaussian N(0, I_D) and the Induced Normal Embedding (INE) hypothesis, stating that each model's latent Ẑ_i = C_i Z + ε_i is a noisy linear projection of the UNE. To test this, they build NoiseZoo (per-image paired latents from 8 models on CelebA) and validate Gaussianity via Anderson-Darling, D'Agostino-Pearson, and Shapiro-Wilk tests on 5,000 random 1D projections per model. Semantic structure is probed with logistic-regression classifiers on 40 CelebA attributes, showing DDIM-inverted noise achieves accuracy highly correlated with CLIP-B/16. For editing, they shift along the learned linear direction (z̃ = z + αw) in noise space and apply a simple orthogonalization (projecting w₁ into the null space of a spurious direction w₂) to disentangle correlated attributes. Finally, a MAXVAR formulation of Generalized Canonical Correlation Analysis recovers a shared k-dimensional intersection subspace across multiple models, confirming that attribute information concentrates in a small set of common linear directions.

## Results

On CelebA (40 attributes), DDIM-inverted noise latents from SD 1.5, SD 2.1, and LCMv7 achieve attribute classification accuracy highly correlated with CLIP-B/16, with per-attribute gaps of only a few percentage points. Cross-space linear transfer from generative latents into encoder spaces yields accuracy drops of just 0.00–0.23 percentage points (LCM: 0.00 pp across all three encoder targets; SD 1.5: 0.14–0.23 pp; SD 2.1: 0.14–0.20 pp), with cosine similarities of 0.54–0.81. Gaussianity tests confirm generative latents reach ~95–96% acceptance rate (matching the theoretical 95% for true Gaussians), while encoders score 80–92%. At 16 shared dimensions, GCCA-derived intersection spaces retain classification accuracy comparable to PCA-reduced individual latents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares DDIM-inverted generative latents against three encoder baselines (CLIP-B/16, OpenCLIP-L/14, DINOv3) as both classification references and linear-transfer targets. In per-attribute classification (Figure 3), generative noise latents from all three diffusion models track CLIP-B/16 closely, with accuracy curves nearly overlapping across the 40 CelebA attributes. In the cross-space transfer experiment (Table 2), LCMv7 achieves zero accuracy drop (0.00 pp) when its latents are linearly mapped into all three encoder spaces, while SD 1.5 and SD 2.1 show drops of at most 0.23 pp — indicating that a simple ridge-regularized linear map preserves essentially all predictive structure. Cosine similarity is highest for CLIP-B/16 (0.80–0.81) and lowest for DINOv3 (0.54–0.57), reflecting DINOv3's purely self-supervised (image-only) training objective versus CLIP's image-text contrastive alignment. Overall, the generative latents match or nearly match encoder baselines on linear attribute prediction, supporting the UNE hypothesis that both families share a common Gaussian latent geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Platonic Representation Hypothesis [26] and identifiability results for contrastive learning [64, 18, 47] by explicitly positing a shared *Gaussian* latent space (UNE) and unifying both encoder and generative model latents as noisy linear projections of it. Prior latent-alignment work (model stitching [2, 4, 32, 39, 63]) demonstrated linear compatibility *within* model families, but this paper bridges *across* families—showing DDIM-inverted diffusion noise and CLIP/DINO embeddings occupy the same geometric structure. The operational contribution (linear probes, linear editing, GCCA-based shared-space recovery) goes beyond prior alignment studies by providing a concrete mechanism to exploit the shared geometry rather than merely demonstrating its existence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that DDIM-inverted noise supports linear attribute prediction nearly matching CLIP, and that a simple linear map transfers generative latents into encoder spaces with <0.3% accuracy loss, suggests a practical shortcut: semantic editing of diffusion outputs without prompts, fine-tuning, or architectural changes. However, all experiments are confined to CelebA faces (40 attributes, ~19k images), so generalization to broader visual domains, higher-level semantics, or non-face attributes remains untested. The UNE is framed as a hypothesis rather than a proven structure, and the GCCA shared-space recovery is explicitly a proof of concept; characterizing *why* and *when* models converge toward UNE-like geometry, and whether the ideal dimension D is finite and recoverable, are open questions the authors flag for future work.
