key_contribution: Proposes SOTA, a training-free ensemble framework that integrates outputs of multiple foundation models (VFMs and VLMs) via a self-adaptive optimal transport mechanism—replacing fixed per-model weights with squared Frobenius inner products to automatically balance contributions without labeled data—achieving up to +12.6% average accuracy gains over the best single model across 26 benchmarks spanning natural images, medical pathology, and remote sensing.
limitations: The authors note that the current ensemble mechanism does not yet exploit latent semantic-level complementarity among VLMs, and the GMM-based representation of VFM features is a relatively simple clustering that could be replaced by more expressive multi-view clustering strategies. Additionally, the self-adaptive weighting provides only modest gains when the constituent models are of similar quality, suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced when model quality is heterogeneous.

surprise_factor: The magnitude of improvement in domain-specific settings (e.g., +14–20% over the best single VLM in medical pathology and remote sensing) is notable, but largely expected given how weak individual VLMs perform out-of-domain. The more interesting finding is the ablation showing that VFM introduction contributes more to performance than the self-adaptive weighting mechanism itself, suggesting the core value lies in adding visual discriminative power rather than in the adaptive fusion per se.

## Problem

Vision-Language Models (e.g., CLIP) over-rely on class-level textual priors and fail to capture fine-grained visual cues, while Vision-only Foundation Models (e.g., DINO) provide discriminative visual features but lack semantic alignment with labels. Additionally, different VLMs exhibit substantially different performance across datasets due to pre-training discrepancies, yet prior work focuses on enhancing a single model rather than exploiting the complementary strengths of multiple foundation models in a unified, training-free framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled images and a set of category names or descriptions, processing them through multiple black-box foundation models (VFMs and/or VLMs) without requiring access to model weights—making it compatible with API-only models. In the transductive setting, the output is a transport plan T ∈ R^{N×K} that directly encodes soft sample-to-class assignments; in the inductive setting, T supervises the estimation of GMM parameters that form visual classifiers combined with text classifiers for per-sample test-time inference, requiring only a few stored parameters rather than cached sequences.

## Method

SOTA treats each foundation model as a distinct view producing a cost matrix: VFMs yield probability distributions via GMM fitting on extracted visual features, while VLMs yield distributions via softmax-normalized cosine similarities between image and text embeddings. The key insight is replacing fixed per-model weights in the weighted OT objective with squared Frobenius inner products ⟨T, P_v⟩², which causes the optimization to automatically up-weight models whose predictions align well with the current transport plan and suppress noisy or less reliable ones—eliminating the need for labeled validation data. The resulting non-convex problem is solved via an iterative Minorization-Maximization scheme that alternates between Sinkhorn-Knopp updates for T and GMM parameter updates, with a joint-optimization variant that co-evolves GMM parameters Θ and T to encourage visually coherent and semantically consistent clusters. The method is entirely training-free, converges within ~5–10 iterations, and requires no fine-tuning or hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

Under the transductive setting, SOTA (CLIP-1 + DINOv2+v3) achieves 77.8% average top-1 accuracy across 11 natural image datasets, surpassing the best single-model baseline (CLIP-1 at 65.2%) by +12.6% and the strongest competitor (GTA-CLIP at 74.5%) by +3.3%. On 10 remote sensing datasets, SOTA reaches 81.5% average, exceeding the best TransCLIP variant (T-GeoRSCLIP at 76.2%) by +5.3%. On 5 medical pathology datasets, SOTA attains 83.9% average, outperforming the best TransCLIP variant (T-MUSK at 76.0%) by +7.9%. Under the inductive setting, SOTA (CLIP-1 + DINOv2+v3) achieves 77.6% average on natural images, beating the best test-time adaptation method (COSMIC at 73.3%) by +4.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOTA consistently outperforms all 21 baselines across the three domains. On natural images (transductive), SOTA (CLIP-1+DINOv2+v3) achieves 77.8% average, beating the strongest competitor GTA-CLIP (74.5%) by +3.3% and TransCLIP (70.3%) by +7.5%; the largest single-dataset gain is on EuroSAT (+38.9% over CLIP-1). On remote sensing, SOTA (81.5%) surpasses the best TransCLIP variant T-GeoRSCLIP (76.2%) by +5.3%, with particularly large margins on PatternNet (+20.7%) and RESISC45 (+20.0%). On medical pathology, SOTA (83.9%) exceeds T-MUSK (76.0%) by +7.9%, with the biggest improvement on SKINCANCER (+19.3%). Under the inductive setting, SOTA (77.6%) outperforms the best TTA method COSMIC (73.3%) by +4.3%, while requiring no cached test sequences. The only notable weakness is on Aircraft, where SOTA (CLIP-1+CLIP-2) drops to 22.6% (−2.1% vs. CLIP-1 alone), though the DINO-augmented variants recover to 33–34%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transductive zero-shot classification line initiated by TransCLIP [37], which applied regularized optimal transport to correct a single VLM's predictions using the global data distribution. SOTA generalizes this from one model to an ensemble of multiple foundation models (both VLMs and VFMs), replacing fixed or heuristic weighting with a self-adaptive mechanism derived from a squared Frobenius inner product. The GMM fitting for VFMs repurposes standard unsupervised clustering to produce probability distributions compatible with the OT formulation, while the Minorization-Maximization optimization handles the resulting nonlinearity. Conceptually, the work departs from the dominant paradigm of enhancing a single model (via prompt engineering, label propagation, or distribution alignment) and instead treats multi-model integration as a first-class problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, prior-free nature of SOTA makes it immediately applicable to black-box foundation models accessible only via API, which is practically significant as many powerful VLMs are distributed this way. The consistent gains across natural, medical pathology, and remote sensing domains (26 benchmarks) suggest the complementary-strengths argument generalizes beyond natural images, where most prior work is evaluated. Open questions include how the method scales as the number of foundation models grows, whether the self-adaptive weighting remains stable under highly heterogeneous model quality, and whether the GMM-based VFM representation can be replaced by more expressive multi-view clustering as the authors themselves suggest for future work.
