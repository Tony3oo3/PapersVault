## Problem

In Source-Free Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning (SF-CDFSL), removing certain middle layers from CLIP's text encoder consistently improves performance, a phenomenon the authors term the 'Lost Layer.' Prior layer-redundancy work treats such layers as dispensable and simply removes them, but this paper shows the lost layers are actually beneficial yet underutilized due to visual domain drift, and that reclaiming their information outperforms removal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263757/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on N-way K-shot episodes (5-way 1-shot or 5-shot) drawn from target-domain datasets (CropDisease, EuroSAT, ISIC, ChestX), where each episode provides K labeled support images per class and M query images. During training, the VtT plugin consumes both the visual encoder's per-layer CLS tokens and the text encoder's per-layer EOS tokens alongside the standard text prompt (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}'); at inference, all VtT parameters are discarded and classification proceeds via the original CLIP cosine-similarity pipeline, introducing zero additional inference cost. The approach is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated on CLIP ViT-Base/16, SigLIP2, and PE-Core with LoRA (r=16, α=8) on the visual branch.

## Method

VtT ('teach the Vision encoder to Think like the Text encoder') comprises three training-time modules. (1) V-T Fusion interleaves per-layer CLS/EOS tokens from both encoders in a deep-to-shallow sequence and aggregates them with a 2-layer Mamba SSM plus a residual MLP branch, injecting text knowledge into visual features at the layer level. (2) TIA maps the fused feature through a linear adapter into an 'absorber token' that replaces the class-name token in the text prompt; the modified prompt is fed through the full text encoder, and a cosine-alignment loss pulls the visual feature toward the resulting output, distilling encoder-level text knowledge into the visual branch. (3) DGSO performs gradient correction by projecting the combined-loss gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the primary classification gradient whenever their cosine similarity is negative, and dynamically disables the auxiliary loss via a sliding-window monitor of gradient alignment to prevent overfitting to the text-branch signal.

## Results

On the four CDFSL benchmarks (CropDisease, EuroSAT, ISIC, ChestX), the best configuration PE-Core-LoRA+VtT achieves 61.00% average accuracy under 5-way 1-shot and 70.05% under 5-way 5-shot, setting a new state-of-the-art. The CLIP-LoRA-Vision+VtT variant reaches 58.23% (1-shot) and 68.57% (5-shot), outperforming the strongest prior CLIP-based method (CLIP-LoRA-Vision alone at 55.97% / 66.50%) by over 2 points. VtT consistently improves every backbone it is applied to (Maple, CLIP-LoRA, SigLIP2-LoRA, PE-Core-LoRA) by 1.2–2.2 points on average across the four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VtT is applied as a plug-in to four existing methods and consistently improves each: Maple+VtT gains +2.08 points over Maple (55.15 vs 53.07 avg, 1-shot), CLIP-LoRA-Vision+VtT gains +2.26 over CLIP-LoRA-Vision (58.23 vs 55.97), SigLIP2-LoRA+VtT gains +1.65 (55.79 vs 54.14), and PE-Core-LoRA+VtT gains +1.22 (61.00 vs 59.78). Against the strongest prior CLIP-based baselines, CLIP-LoRA-Vision+VtT outperforms LDC (53.45), LP++ (52.56), StepSTP (52.68), CoOp (51.77), Tip-Adapter (51.87), and AMU-Tuning (51.57) by 2.3–6.5 points on average. The overall best result (PE-Core-LoRA+VtT, 61.00% 1-shot / 70.05% 5-shot) surpasses the previous best (PE-Core-LoRA alone) and all CLIP-based methods by a clear margin. Notably, the gains are largest on EuroSAT (+3.29 for CLIP-LoRA-Vision+VtT) and ISIC (+1.80), while ChestX shows the smallest improvement (+0.84), suggesting the lost-layer effect is more pronounced in domains with larger visual shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's dual-encoder architecture and the established SF-CDFSL task, positioning itself as a diagnostic and corrective intervention for a previously unexplained performance anomaly. It departs sharply from the layer-redundancy literature (ShortGPT, FlowCut, etc.), which treats removable layers as genuinely redundant and advocates deletion; here, the authors show the 'lost' text-encoder layers contain beneficial information that is simply underutilized due to visual-domain drift. Methodologically, the VtT framework repurposes SSM-based sequential modeling (from Mamba) for cross-modal layer fusion, borrows modality-conversion ideas for the TIA module, and adapts gradient-conflict correction from multi-task learning, all wrapped around existing PEFT backbones (LoRA, Maple) without altering inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lost-layer phenomenon is a practical pitfall for anyone fine-tuning CLIP-family models on cross-domain few-shot data: silently removing a middle text-encoder layer can yield a few points of free accuracy, but the paper shows this discards genuinely useful pre-trained knowledge. The VtT plug-in offers a principled fix that requires no architectural changes at inference time, making it easy to adopt alongside existing LoRA or prompt-tuning pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only four CDFSL benchmarks and reliance on CLIP-family backbones; whether the visual-domain-drift diagnosis and the SSM-based fusion generalize to non-CLIP VLMs or to in-domain few-shot settings remains open. The per-dataset sensitivity of the 'lost layer' index (Table 1, wls rows) also suggests the phenomenon may be more nuanced than a single fixed layer.
