## Problem

State-of-the-art instruction-based image editing models keep their MLLM encoders frozen during training, which limits visual reasoning and prevents effective handling of complex or abstract instructions. While reasoning-enhanced generation has been explored for text-to-image synthesis, the application of structured reasoning (thinking and reflection) to image editing remains largely underexplored, and MLLMs suffer from substantial hallucinations when performing paired-image comparison for self-correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263513/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction (which may be abstract, colloquial, or multi-faceted) and produces an edited image. During inference, the pipeline operates in a thinking-editing-reflection loop: the thinking module first rewrites the instruction into concrete, standardized commands, the DiT generates an initial edit, and the reflection module then evaluates the result against the target, potentially issuing a secondary refinement instruction for up to two additional editing rounds before terminating with a success, refinement, or failure tag.

## Method

ReasonEdit couples an MLLM (Qwen2.5VL 7B) as a Reasoner with a DiT as a Generator, introducing two reasoning capabilities trained on purpose-built datasets: Thinking Pairs (200k abstract-to-concrete instruction pairs) that teach the MLLM to decompose ambiguous requests into actionable commands, and Reflection Triples (180k multi-round single-image evaluation sequences) that enable iterative self-correction while avoiding the hallucinations inherent in dual-image comparison. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) Reasoning Learning applies LoRA to the MLLM with next-token-prediction loss on both datasets while freezing the DiT; (2) Edit Learning freezes the MLLM and trains the DiT with a flow-matching loss on 14.4M T2I and 2.4M editing samples; (3) Unified Tuning jointly fine-tunes both components with a combined flow-matching and NTP loss (ω=0.1), using FlexAttention and packed data for efficient hybrid training. Two variants are released—ReasonEdit-S (initialized from Step1X-Edit v1.1) and ReasonEdit-Q (initialized from Qwen-Image-Edit)—demonstrating consistent gains of +4.3%/+4.7%/+8.2% (S) and +2.8%/+3.4%/+6.1% (Q) on ImgEdit/GEdit/KRIS over their respective bases.

## Results

On GEdit-Bench, ReasonEdit-Q (built on Qwen-Image-Edit) achieves the highest Overall score of 7.77 among all open-source models, improving +3.4% over its base, while ReasonEdit-S (built on Step1X-Edit v1.1) reaches 7.58 (+4.7%). On KRIS-Bench, which tests abstract reasoning, ReasonEdit-S gains +8.2% (60.93) and ReasonEdit-Q gains +6.1% (61.57) over their respective bases. On ImgEdit-Bench, ReasonEdit-S achieves 4.40 (+4.3%) and ReasonEdit-Q achieves 4.36 (+2.8%), placing them second and third among open-source models respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonEdit-S outperforms its base Step1X-Edit v1.1 by +4.7% on GEdit (7.58 vs 6.97), +8.2% on KRIS (60.93 vs 51.59), and +4.3% on ImgEdit (4.40 vs 3.90). ReasonEdit-Q similarly surpasses Qwen-Image-Edit by +3.4% GEdit (7.77 vs 7.56), +6.1% KRIS (61.57 vs 56.15), and +2.8% ImgEdit (4.36 vs 4.27). On GEdit-Bench, ReasonEdit-Q achieves the best open-source Overall score (7.77), narrowly edging out UniWorld-Qwen-Image-Edit (7.76) and CCA-Gemini-2.5-Pro (7.54). On KRIS-Bench, Uni-CoT (68.00) and CCA-Gemini-2.5-Pro (63.53) still score higher than ReasonEdit-Q (61.57), though the paper notes Uni-CoT shows limited gains on standard benchmarks and CCA is a zero-shot agentic pipeline rather than a trained model. On ImgEdit-Bench, UniWorld-Qwen-Image-Edit (4.48) slightly leads ReasonEdit-S (4.40) and ReasonEdit-Q (4.36). Overall, ReasonEdit delivers the most consistent gains across all three benchmarks simultaneously, whereas competitors like Uni-CoT excel on KRIS but lag on GEdit and ImgEdit.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReasonEdit extends the MLLM-encoder + DiT-decoder architecture popularized by Step1X-Edit and Qwen-Image-Edit, but departs from their frozen-MLLM paradigm by jointly training the language model's reasoning capabilities with the diffusion generator. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate threads: reasoning-enhanced visual generation (BAGEL's thinking mode, OmniGen2's reflection, Uni-CoT's chain-of-thought) and instruction-based image editing, which had largely ignored test-time scaling. A key methodological departure from prior reflection work (e.g., CCA, Reflect-DiT) is the multi-round single-image reflection pipeline, which avoids the hallucination-prone dual-image comparison by decomposing paired-image understanding into cascaded single-image tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that unlocking MLLM reasoning in editing models yields consistent gains across both simple (GEdit, ImgEdit) and complex (KRIS) benchmarks, suggesting the thinking-editing-reflection loop is broadly applicable rather than limited to abstract instructions. The multi-stage training recipe (reasoning → editing → unified) and the Thinking Pairs / Reflection Triples data construction pipeline are practical and transferable to other MLLM+DiT editing systems. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video editing, whether the VLM-dependent data construction (GPT-4.1 for VIEScore, VLM annotators) introduces systematic biases, and whether the diminishing returns after two reflection rounds reflect a fundamental ceiling or simply the quality of the current reflection instructions.
