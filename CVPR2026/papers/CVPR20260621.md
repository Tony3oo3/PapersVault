## Problem

Sparse Autoencoders uncover thousands of interpretable features in vision models, but explaining them at scale without human intervention remains open. Existing automated approaches rely on top-activating input images, making them correlation-based, computationally expensive, dependent on a predefined evaluation set, and prone to contextual biases where recurring background elements are misattributed as the feature's concept.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260621/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The basic Steering method takes a uniform white image (896×896 for Gemma 3, 448×448 for InternVL3) and a single SAE decoder row vector W_dec[i,:], injecting it additively across all spatial positions at a chosen encoder layer with a strength coefficient α. The hybrid Steering-informed Top-k variant additionally conditions on the top-5 images that most strongly activate the target feature. In both cases, the output is a free-form natural language sentence generated by the VLM's language model describing the visual concept the feature encodes.

## Method

The core mechanism is a causal intervention on the vision encoder's residual stream: after feeding an information-devoid white image, the model's representation at layer l is replaced (via the do-operator) with the SAE feature vector, and the VLM's language model is prompted to articulate what visual concept it perceives. This requires only a single forward pass and no evaluation image set, making it scalable to thousands of features. The paper further introduces Steering-informed Top-k, which applies the same causal injection while conditioning on the top-k activating images, combining the causal signal of steering with the correlational evidence of input-based explanations. TopK SAEs (d_SAE=8192) are trained unsupervised on ImageNet at a middle layer of the SigLIP (Gemma 3) and InternViT (InternVL3) encoders. Experiments show that explanation quality scales consistently with the language model size (4B→12B→27B), and that the hybrid method achieves state-of-the-art scores across IoU, AUROC, synthetic activation, and CLIP alignment metrics while reducing the contextual bias inherent in pure Top-k explanations.

## Results

On the middle-layer SAE of Gemma 3, Steering-informed Top-k achieves the best AUROC (0.788 Masks / 0.838 Heatmaps) and Synthetic Activation Score (0.461 / 0.505), outperforming Top-k (0.723 / 0.791 AUROC; 0.330 / 0.364 Synth. Act.) and standalone Steering (0.675 AUROC; 0.324 Synth. Act.). On InternVL3-14B, the same pattern holds: Steering-informed Top-k reaches 0.823 / 0.833 AUROC and 0.254 / 0.252 Synthetic Activation, versus 0.768 / 0.775 and 0.187 / 0.183 for Top-k. IoU scores are closely matched across methods (≈0.21–0.23), while CLIP scores remain low and similar (≈0.19). Explanation quality also scales with LM size: moving from 4B to 27B parameters consistently improves both AUROC and Synthetic Activation for Steering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three explanation methods: standalone Steering (causal intervention on a white image), Top-k (input-based, from Xu et al. [53] and Zhang et al. [55]), and the proposed hybrid Steering-informed Top-k. Steering-informed Top-k wins on every metric for both Gemma 3 and InternVL3: it improves AUROC by 6–11 points over Top-k (e.g., 0.838 vs. 0.791 on Gemma 3 Heatmaps) and boosts Synthetic Activation by 39–40% (0.505 vs. 0.364). Standalone Steering is competitive on IoU (0.211 vs. 0.211 for Gemma 3 Masks) but lags substantially on AUROC (0.675 vs. 0.723) and Synthetic Activation (0.324 vs. 0.330), indicating weaker causal sensitivity without input conditioning. A key qualitative finding is that Top-k suffers from contextual bias—47.7% of its Heatmap explanations misattribute background features to animals—while Steering-informed Top-k reduces this to 38.6%. The hybrid method also overcomes diminishing returns from larger evaluation datasets, reaching high-quality explanations without the data scaling that standard Top-k requires.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automated interpretability pipeline for vision SAEs (Xu et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2024), which relies on correlation-based top-activating images, by introducing a causal-intervention alternative: steering the vision encoder's residual stream with individual SAE decoder vectors on an empty image and prompting the VLM's language model to describe the result. It also builds on self-explaining approaches in LLMs (Patchscopes, Kharlapenko et al.) and prior within-model steering work in vision (Joseph et al. 2025; Lim et al. 2025), but departs from these by leveraging the cross-modal structure of VLMs to generate natural-language explanations from purely causal interventions, rather than requiring additional training or restricting interventions to within a single model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Steering method offers a single-forward-pass, evaluation-set-free alternative to input-based explanation, and the hybrid Steering-informed Top-k consistently achieves the best scores across all four metrics while reducing the contextual bias (e.g., misattributing background features to foreground animals) that plagues pure Top-k. The consistent scaling of explanation quality with LM size (4B→12B→27B) suggests that as language models grow, this causal, output-centric approach will become increasingly powerful, potentially reducing reliance on large curated evaluation sets. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to SAEs trained on non-ImageNet data, to video or higher-resolution encoders, and whether training-based approaches (activation oracles, LatentQA) could further close the gap between Steering-alone and Top-k performance.
