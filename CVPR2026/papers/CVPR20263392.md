## Problem

Retrieval-augmented video reasoning typically appends textual passages or multi-clip evidence to the prompt, forcing heterogeneous signals into a single attention space and causing diluted attention and elevated cognitive load even on short videos. The paper identifies that the bottleneck is not only what to retrieve but how to represent and fuse external knowledge with the video backbone, and asks whether delivering retrieved knowledge in the visual space—rather than as text—can resolve this cross-modal competition without retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263392/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip and a natural-language query as input. Offline, the video is processed once into a cached, problem-agnostic knowledge graph; online, the query triggers retrieval of a compact subgraph that is rendered into a single reasoning frame (a lightweight diagram with geometric primitives and short labels, no timestamps) and appended as the last frame of the video sequence. The frozen LMM backbone then consumes the augmented video [V; I_RF] and produces a text answer, while the reasoning frame itself serves as an inspectable, auditable evidence artifact.

## Method

G2F-RAG is a training-free, two-stage multi-agent pipeline. Offline, a graph-construction agent (GPT-4o) builds a full video knowledge graph covering entities, events, spatial relations, affordances, and linked world knowledge, cached for reuse across questions. Online, a difficulty-aware orchestration agent routes each query: easy questions go directly to the LMM on the raw video, while hard questions trigger a retrieval agent that extracts a minimal sufficient subgraph under an explicit complexity budget (bounded node and edge counts), and a rendering agent (Graphviz) converts that subgraph into a single layout-controlled reasoning frame. The frame is appended as the last video frame so temporal attention can attend to it without disrupting the original content, and a conservative fallback mechanism reverts to the easy path when routing confidence is low or the subgraph exceeds budget. The core design principle is modality-aligned fusion: by encoding structured knowledge as visual tokens rather than text, the method eliminates cross-modal attention competition and keeps the LMM operating in its native spatiotemporal visual reasoning domain.

## Results

G2F-RAG delivers consistent gains across 8 benchmarks and multiple LMM backbones without retraining. The largest absolute improvements appear on knowledge-intensive and spatial-reasoning tasks: +7.4% on VSIBench (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), +7.1% on WildVideo (InternVL3.5-8B), and +7.9% on VideoMMMU (LLaVA-Video-7B). On general multi-task benchmarks, VideoMME (w/o sub) typically rises by +5–6% (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 65.1→70.6; InternVL3.5-8B: 66.0→72.0), while MMBench-Video shows small but consistent positive shifts on its 0–3 LLM-evaluated scale. In the direct RAG-method comparison (Table 2), G2F-RAG achieves 73.4% on MLVU and 55.4% on WildVideo with Qwen2.5-VL-7B, outperforming both Video-RAG and Vgent on these two benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Video-RAG [37] (text-append paradigm), G2F-RAG wins decisively on all three benchmarks in Table 2. With Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Video-RAG actually degrades the baseline (MLVU 68.8→63.4, WildVideo 51.3→47.2, VideoMME 65.1→60.5), while G2F-RAG improves all three (73.4, 55.4, 70.6). With LLaVA-Video-7B, Video-RAG helps MLVU (69.5→71.3) but harms WildVideo (53.4→48.5); G2F-RAG improves both (75.5, 57.0). Against Vgent [43] (graph-based retrieval with validation), G2F-RAG wins on MLVU (73.4 vs 72.1 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B; 75.5 vs 72.5 for LLaVA-Video-7B) and WildVideo (55.4 vs 50.1; 57.0 vs 51.6), and on VideoMME for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (70.6 vs 68.9), though it trails Vgent on VideoMME for LLaVA-Video-7B (64.5 vs 66.7). The pattern confirms that the representation and delivery modality of retrieved knowledge—visual frame vs. appended text—matters as much as the retrieval quality itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant retrieve-then-append paradigm in video RAG (Video-RAG, Vgent, VideoRAG) by arguing that the representation and delivery modality of retrieved knowledge matters as much as the content itself. It repurposes knowledge-graph construction techniques (from GraphRAG and VideoGraph) and multi-agent orchestration (from agentic video pipelines) but introduces a novel rendering step that converts a retrieved subgraph into a single visual frame, drawing on the observation that visual tokens can compress textual information more efficiently than text tokens. The approach is positioned as orthogonal to backbone choice, building on frozen LMMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5, LLaVA-Video) without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's implicit assumption that appending more retrieved context improves video reasoning, showing instead that text-based RAG consistently degrades performance across multiple backbones and benchmarks. The visual-space fusion paradigm is training-free, plug-and-play, and produces an auditable evidence trail, making it attractive for deployment where interpretability matters. Open questions include whether the single-frame constraint suffices for highly complex multi-hop reasoning, how the method scales to hour-long videos where the offline graph becomes very large, and whether the reliance on GPT-4o for graph construction limits practicality in cost-sensitive or offline settings.
