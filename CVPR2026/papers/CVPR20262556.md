## Problem

Existing video-to-audio (V2A) models are trained on short clips (8–10 s) and rely on transformer positional embeddings that fail to extrapolate to longer sequences, causing severe quality degradation beyond ~1 minute. This paper asks whether a model trained exclusively on short audio-visual segments can generalize to generate coherent, synchronized audio for videos up to 5+ minutes without any architectural modification at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a silent video clip (up to ~500 s in LongVale) and an optional text caption as conditioning inputs. Video is encoded via CLIP for semantic features and Synchformer for motion-audio synchronization cues; text is encoded via CLIP. The output is a full-length audio waveform in the latent space of a pretrained audio codec, generated via flow matching. Crucially, training uses only 8-second VGGSound clips, while inference handles arbitrary longer durations without positional-embedding rescaling or NTK interpolation.

## Method

MMHNet replaces the transformer attention blocks in MMAudio's multimodal DiT architecture with Non-Causal Mamba-2 state-space modules, eliminating the need for positional embeddings that hinder length generalization. A hierarchical routing framework (adapted from HNet) compresses redundant tokens before the main network: temporal routing selects tokens at sound-event boundaries using cosine similarity between consecutive tokens, while multimodal routing selects tokens with high cross-modal similarity (e.g., audio tokens aligned with visual tokens via Synchformer features). Non-causal Mamba-2 is chosen over causal Mamba-2 because video conditions are available offline and the non-causal formulation avoids the cumulative-product decay that degrades conditioning signals over long sequences. Training uses conditional flow matching in latent space on 8-second VGGSound clips, with global conditioning via adaLN and token-level conditioning from CLIP and Synchformer features, enabling the model to generate audio up to 5+ minutes at test time without any duration-specific adjustment.

## Results

On UnAV100, MMHNet-L (1.09B) achieves the best FD_VGG (1.80) and FD_PANNs (5.29), while MMHNet-S (157M) achieves the best IB-Score (36.82) and DeSync (0.439). On LongVale (videos up to ~7 min), MMHNet-L attains the best FD_VGG (3.23), FD_PANNs (10.03), and KL_PASST (1.64), and MMHNet-S leads on IB-Score (30.62) and DeSync (0.438). Both model sizes consistently outperform all baselines across distribution matching, audio quality, multimodal alignment, and temporal synchronization metrics on both long-form benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMHNet outperforms all four baselines on both long-form benchmarks. Against MMAudio (the closest architectural baseline), MMHNet-L improves IB-Score from 30.71 to 36.27 and DeSync from 0.593 to 0.410 on UnAV100, and from 21.60 to 30.00 and 0.678 to 0.465 on LongVale, demonstrating that removing positional embeddings and adopting Non-Causal Mamba-2 substantially aids length generalization. Against LoVA, the dedicated long-form V2A method, MMHNet achieves a 12-point IB-Score advantage (36.27 vs. 24.62) and a 3× lower DeSync (0.410 vs. 1.232) on UnAV100, with similar margins on LongVale (30.00 vs. 17.04 IB-Score; 0.465 vs. 1.233 DeSync). Against the autoregressive V-AURA, MMHNet wins on FD_VGG (1.80 vs. 4.57), IB-Score (36.27 vs. 29.36), and DeSync (0.410 vs. 1.191) on UnAV100, confirming that autoregressive error accumulation hurts long-sequence fidelity. Against the much larger HunyuanVideo-Foley-XXL (5.13B), MMHNet-L (1.09B) achieves a 2.6× lower FD_PANNs (5.29 vs. 10.28) and 0.410 vs. 0.757 DeSync on UnAV100, and a 4.5× lower FD_VGG (3.23 vs. 14.56) on LongVale, showing that architectural choices (hierarchical routing + non-causal SSM) matter more than raw parameter count for long-form generation. On VGGSound (matched 10-second duration), MMHNet performs on par with MMAudio, confirming no quality regression at the original training length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MMAudio multimodal flow-matching architecture by replacing its transformer attention blocks with Non-Causal Mamba-2 state-space modules, motivated by a pilot study showing that positional embeddings (e.g., RoPE) degrade length generalization in V2A models. It further incorporates the hierarchical routing and dynamic chunking mechanism from HNet to selectively process only boundary-marked tokens, reducing redundancy in long audio-video sequences. The work departs from prior long-form V2A methods (LoVA, V-AURA) that either rely on causal autoregressive generation or segment-based approaches, instead demonstrating that a single non-causal SSM backbone trained on 8-second clips can generalize to multi-minute outputs without architectural modifications at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in V2A generation: existing models are confined to the short durations seen during training, limiting real-world deployment in film and gaming where multi-minute audio is needed. The demonstration that 8-second training suffices for 5+ minute generation, combined with the new long-form evaluation protocol on UnAV100 and LongVale, provides both a practical capability and a community benchmark. Open questions include whether the hierarchical routing threshold (0.5) generalizes across domains, how the method handles highly dynamic or sparse-audio scenes, and whether the non-causal SSM advantage persists at even longer durations (beyond 7 minutes) or with more complex multimodal conditioning.
