## Problem

Most semantic scene completion (SSC) systems are designed for wheeled autonomous driving with forward-facing sensors and benign motion, leaving legged and humanoid robots underserved. These platforms require full 360° situational awareness in cluttered terrain, suffer from gait-induced body jitter that corrupts feature-to-voxel lifting, and face tight payload/power budgets that favor lightweight single-sensor solutions. No prior work addresses panoramic SSC explicitly robust to periodic gait motion on a single omnidirectional camera.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single raw annular panoramic RGB image (608×1216 px from a PAL camera) together with calibrated Taylor-polynomial intrinsics/extrinsics; no depth, LiDAR, or IMU is required. It outputs a dense full-surround 3D semantic occupancy grid with per-voxel class logits—64×64×8 at 0.4 m resolution (6 classes) on QuadOcc and 64×64×8 / 128×128×16 (10 classes) on Human360Occ—covering the complete 360° field of view around the robot.

## Method

OneOcc integrates four components: (i) Dual-Projection Encoders (DP-ER) run two 2D backbones in parallel on the raw annular panorama and its calibrated equirectangular unfolding, compensating the equator-pole distortion trade-off; (ii) Bi-Grid Voxelization (BGV) lifts multi-scale features into both Cartesian and cylindrical-polar voxel grids via bilinear sampling at projected centroids, then injects polar context into the Cartesian stream through precomputed cross-grid indices; (iii) Gait Displacement Compensation (GDC) uses a zero-initialized GAP+Linear head to regress a per-scale 2D displacement that corrects sampling coordinates *before* lifting, routing phase errors back to the 2D domain and avoiding voxel-quantized misattribution; (iv) a Hierarchical AMoE-3D decoder (depthwise-separable UNet at three scales) fuses the bi-grid volume using dual-path channel-and-spatial saliency gates plus a Mixture-of-Experts module gated by 3D gradient energy, emphasizing high-contrast category boundaries while suppressing over-smoothing on flat ground. Training is fully supervised with cross-entropy, scene-class affinity (SCAL) for semantics and geometry, and frustum-proportion loss; the relation loss from MonoScene is deliberately omitted as it over-smooths azimuthal boundaries under panoramic legged settings.

## Results

On QuadOcc (real quadruped, 360°), OneOcc achieves 20.56 mIoU with a single panoramic camera, surpassing the best LiDAR baseline LMSCNet (18.44 mIoU, +2.12) and the best vision-only baseline MonoScene (19.19 mIoU, +1.37). On Human360Occ (H3O, CARLA human-ego), OneOcc reaches 37.29 mIoU (within-city) and 32.23 mIoU (cross-city), improving over the strongest vision baseline MonoScene by +3.83 and +8.08 mIoU respectively. The model runs at ~14.3 FPS (69.93 ms) on a single RTX 4090 with 101.76M parameters and 1.82 GB peak memory in FP32.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneOcc (camera-only, panoramic) outperforms all LiDAR-based baselines on QuadOcc: it beats LMSCNet (18.44) by +2.12 mIoU, SSCNet-full (16.38) by +4.18, and OccRWKV (16.69) by +3.87, despite using no LiDAR input. Among vision-only methods, OneOcc surpasses MonoScene (19.19) by +1.37 mIoU, SGN (17.17) by +3.39, and OccFormer (13.02) by +7.54 on QuadOcc. On H3O within-city, OneOcc (37.29) leads MonoScene (33.46) by +3.83 and SGN-T (28.64) by +8.65; on the harder cross-city split the gap widens to +8.08 over MonoScene (32.23 vs. 24.15) and +11.63 over SGN-T (32.23 vs. 20.60), indicating strong distribution robustness. The only case where OneOcc trails a vision baseline is at night on QuadOcc (13.50 vs. MonoScene's 14.20 mIoU), though it retains higher precision (63.56 vs. 51.35). Notably, SGN† with added LiDAR (19.72 mIoU) still falls short of OneOcc's camera-only 20.56, and SGN† achieves the highest geometry IoU (49.16) among vision entries but at the cost of lower mIoU due to weaker small-class performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneOcc extends the vision-based SSC lineage (MonoScene, VoxFormer, OccFormer, SGN) from forward-facing pinhole cameras on wheeled platforms to a single panoramic camera on legged/humanoid robots, where gait-induced body jitter and 360° continuity are first-class concerns. Its Bi-Grid Voxelization draws on non-Cartesian discretizations (PointOcc, Cylinder3D) but applies them jointly with Cartesian grids in a panoramic context, while the Gait Displacement Compensation module is a new, lightweight feature-level correction absent from prior SSC pipelines. The Dual-Projection (raw annular + equirectangular) encoding is a departure from the single-projection assumption in all prior camera-based SSC work, motivated by the equator-pole distortion trade-off unique to omnidirectional optics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of QuadOcc (real quadruped 360°) and Human360Occ (simulated human-ego 360°) fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark targets panoramic semantic occupancy on dynamic legged platforms, making systematic study of gait-robust perception possible. The finding that a camera-only pipeline can rival or modestly surpass LiDAR-based SSC at the 0.4 m / 64×64×8 resolution relevant to legged robots is practically significant for payload-constrained platforms. Open questions include robustness to calibration drift (the method assumes bounded drift), whether the cross-city generalization gap (+8.08 mIoU) narrows with more diverse training data, and how occupancy tokens could serve as an intermediate representation for world models or vision-language-action policies on robots.
