## Problem

Existing pose-free event-based 3D reconstruction methods rely on pretrained depth estimation models for Gaussian initialization, which fail to generalize when the camera explores regions beyond the initial observation coverage, causing accumulated trajectory errors. Additionally, uniform pixel-wise losses treat geometrically informative edges and sparse event noise equally, degrading both pose accuracy and reconstruction quality. The paper addresses how to achieve robust pose-free reconstruction from events alone without any depth supervision or auxiliary sensors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams (per-pixel brightness changes) processed in temporal chunks with randomly sampled integration intervals Δt ∈ [Δt_min, Δt_max] to preserve the asynchronous nature of events. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation (position, covariance, opacity, color per primitive) and a continuous camera trajectory parameterized by boundary poses in SE(3), from which novel views are rendered via differentiable α-blending. No RGB frames, depth maps, or pretrained models are required at any stage.

## Method

E2EGS extracts noise-resistant edge maps from consecutive event maps via patch-based temporal coherence analysis: for each spatial patch, it computes the variance of Gaussian-windowed temporal differences across consecutive frames, where edges produce high-variance structured patterns while non-edge regions yield low-variance sparse noise. These edges guide 3D Gaussian initialization by placing edge-aware Gaussians along detected edge rays using inverse depth sampling (concentrating samples at farther distances to enhance rotational observability), complemented by random Gaussians for texture-less surfaces. During joint tracking-mapping optimization, an edge-weighted loss spatially amplifies the photometric error at high-confidence edge locations (w(x) = 1 + β·M(x)), prioritizing geometric constraints over uniform texture matching. The framework follows IncEventGS's chunk-based tracking-mapping SLAM pipeline but replaces depth-model-based initialization entirely with this edge-guided strategy, enabling self-correction of previously mis-estimated depths through temporal inconsistency detection.

## Results

On synthetic Replica scenes, E2EGS achieves sub-millimeter ATE (0.049–0.077 cm) without any depth supervision, matching or slightly outperforming IncEventGS (0.041–0.085 cm) and substantially outperforming DEVO (0.134–0.381 cm). On real TUM-VIE sequences, E2EGS achieves ATE of 0.40–1.12 cm, dramatically outperforming IncEventGS† (random init) which suffers catastrophic failure (up to 96.59 cm on desk2) and beating IncEventGS (depth-based) on 4 of 5 sequences. For NVS on Replica, E2EGS achieves PSNR of 20.75–28.01 dB, SSIM up to 0.87, and LPIPS as low as 0.19, matching or exceeding IncEventGS across all five scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IncEventGS (depth-based, [11]), E2EGS achieves comparable or better ATE on synthetic scenes (e.g., 0.049 vs. 0.051 cm on room0) and clearly wins on real TUM-VIE sequences (0.65 vs. 1.62 cm on 3d; 0.58 vs. 0.70 cm on 6dof), revealing that pretrained depth models fail to generalize beyond initial coverage. Against IncEventGS† (random init, [11]), E2EGS is dramatically superior on real data—0.40 vs. 96.59 cm on desk2—demonstrating that edge-guided initialization is essential for robust pose-free reconstruction. Against DEVO [16], E2EGS outperforms on every sequence by 3–10× in ATE (e.g., 0.049 vs. 0.271 cm on room0). Against ESVO2 [20], E2EGS wins on all five TUM-VIE sequences (e.g., 0.40 vs. 2.65 cm on desk2). For NVS quality, E2EGS surpasses Event-3DGS [8] (which requires COLMAP poses) by 1–6 dB PSNR and EvGGS [29] by 5–13 dB PSNR across all Replica scenes, while remaining fully pose-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the tracking-mapping SLAM pipeline of IncEventGS (Huang et al., 2025), but departs from the latter's reliance on pretrained monocular depth models (Marigold) for 3D Gaussian initialization. Instead, it introduces a patch-based temporal coherence analysis to extract structural edge maps directly from event streams, drawing a conceptual connection to contrast maximization (Gallego et al., 2018) but repurposing it for edge detection rather than trajectory estimation. The edge-guided initialization and edge-weighted loss replace the depth-model-dependent pipeline, positioning the work as a fully self-contained, pose-free event-based reconstruction method without auxiliary sensors or pretrained models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the dependency on pretrained depth estimation models, E2EGS addresses a critical generalization failure: depth models estimated from initial frames degrade as cameras explore new spatial regions, causing accumulated trajectory errors in long sequences. The finding that edge-based geometric priors provide more robust constraints than depth priors for long-range pose estimation is practically significant for real-world deployment where scenes are unbounded. However, the method inherits the fundamental limitation of event cameras—sparse responses in smooth, texture-less regions—which constrains reconstruction quality in non-edge areas. The optimal strategy for edge extraction from noisy event streams remains an open problem, and adaptive methods that respond to local event statistics could further improve performance in weakly structured scenes.
