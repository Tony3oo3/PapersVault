## Problem

One-step Mean Flow achieves state-of-the-art single-step generation in finite-dimensional spaces, but extending it to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces for functional generative models is fundamentally blocked: the marginal flow obtained by averaging conditional two-parameter flows is provably inconsistent with the true two-parameter flow, and operator-valued velocity fields introduce severe numerical instability. This paper resolves both the theoretical inconsistency and the practical training instability to enable one-step functional generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a sample from a Gaussian measure on Hilbert space (parametrized by a Gaussian process with a Matérn kernel for 1D/2D tasks, or a mollified white-noise kernel for images) and produces a continuous function in a single evaluation. Because the representation is functional, the same trained model can be queried at arbitrary spatial or temporal coordinates: images trained on 25% of 256×256 pixels can be generated at 64×64 through 1024×1024, and 3D SDFs are reconstructed from as few as 64 surface points. For conditional image generation (AFHQ), a class label is additionally provided.

## Method

FMF defines a two-parameter flow φ_{t→r} and its mean velocity ū_{t→r} = (φ_{t→r} − Id)/(r−t), then derives an equivalent reformulation via the Fréchet derivative (Theorem 3.1) that expresses ū_{t→r} in terms of its own time derivative, the Fréchet derivative Dφ_{t→r}, and the marginal velocity u_t. A stop-gradient approximation handles the self-referential term, yielding a conditional loss provably equivalent to the marginal loss up to a constant (Theorem 3.2). The paper further introduces an x₁-prediction variant that predicts the extrapolated endpoint f̂_{1,t→r} = (1−t)ū_{t→r} + f_t rather than the mean velocity itself, which exhibits substantially better training stability (avoiding spatial-variance collapse) on tasks like 3D SDF generation where u-prediction fails. The framework is implemented with existing neural-operator backbones (FNO, hybrid sparse-dense operators, Perceiver-based architectures) with only a minor modification to accept a (t, r) time pair, and the gradient terms are computed via JVP operations within standard optimization.

## Results

On image generation (FID_CLIP, 1-step), FMF achieves 3.48 on CelebA-HQ-64, 7.18 on CelebA-HQ-128, 9.17 on CelebA-HQ-256, 11.37 on FFHQ-256, and 26.57 on LSUN-Church-256, outperforming all prior one-step functional methods and approaching the 100-step ∞-Diff baseline (4.57 on CelebA-HQ-64). On 1D time-series datasets, FMF achieves the best one-step statistical fidelity across all five benchmarks (AEMET, Genes, Pop., GDP, Labor), with mean errors typically 1–2 orders of magnitude lower than GANO. On Navier-Stokes, FMF (x₁-pred) attains 8.0×10⁻⁵ density MSE and 5.6×10² spectrum MSE in one step, beating GANO by ~31× on density. For 3D shape reconstruction from 64 surface points, FMF achieves Chamfer distance 0.060 and boundary error 0.011 in a single step, outperforming 18-step 3DS2VS (0.144) and 64-step FD (0.101) on Chamfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FMF decisively outperforms all one-step functional baselines: on CelebA-HQ-64 it achieves FID_CLIP 3.48 versus 9.29 (GASP), 14.65 (GEM), and 40.4 (D2F); on LSUN-Church-256 it scores 26.57 versus 37.46 (GASP) and 87.57 (GEM). Against the multi-step ∞-Diff (100 NFEs), FMF is competitive on CelebA-HQ-64 (3.48 vs. 4.57) and CelebA-HQ-128 (7.18 vs. 3.02, where ∞-Diff leads), but trails on FFHQ-256 (11.37 vs. 3.87) and Church-256 (26.57 vs. 10.36), reflecting the cost of reducing 100 steps to 1. On 1D time-series and Navier-Stokes, FMF matches or slightly trails the best multi-step baselines (FFM-OT, FDDPM) on individual statistical metrics while using only 1 NFE versus 300–2000, and dominates the one-step GANO by 1–2 orders of magnitude on mean and variance errors. For 3D shape reconstruction, FMF (1 step) beats 3DS2VS (18 steps) on Chamfer (0.060 vs. 0.144) and boundary (0.011 vs. 0.016) but lags on F-Score (0.584 vs. 0.608); similarly it outperforms FD (64 steps) on Chamfer (0.060 vs. 0.101) but trails on F-Score (0.584 vs. 0.707), suggesting the one-step formulation captures global geometry well but with slightly less surface detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step Mean Flow framework (Geng et al., NeurIPS 2025) to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces by building on the Functional Flow Matching formulation (Kerrigan et al., AISTATS 2023). The key conceptual departure is that the standard conditional-to-marginal loss equivalence used in FFM breaks down for two-parameter mean flows (Statement 1), requiring a new derivation via Fréchet derivatives of two-parameter flows to restore a tractable training objective. The paper also introduces an x₁-prediction variant of Mean Flow, analogous to the x₁-prediction in standard Flow Matching, which had not been explored in the Mean Flow literature. Experimentally, it plugs into existing functional architectures (FNO, Infty-Diff's hybrid sparse-dense operator, Functional Diffusion's Perceiver) with minimal modification, positioning itself as a drop-in one-step replacement for multi-step functional generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step functional generation removes the multi-step integration bottleneck that limits practical deployment of functional models (e.g., Infty-Diff requires 100 NFEs), enabling real-time or resource-constrained inference while preserving resolution-agnostic generation. The x₁-prediction variant addresses a concrete failure mode—u-prediction collapse on 3D SDF generation—that would otherwise block adoption of Mean Flow in point-based functional settings. Open questions include whether the x₁-prediction stability advantage generalizes beyond the demonstrated tasks, whether the theoretical framework extends to non-Gaussian conditional paths or different interpolation schemes, and whether the stop-gradient approximation introduces meaningful bias at scale. The paper does not explore conditional generation beyond class labels or multi-modal functional data, leaving broader applicability partially untested.
