## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting-based avatar methods require complete asset downloads before any rendering can begin, causing severe start-up latency in bandwidth-constrained XR and telepresence scenarios. Prior LOD approaches rely on discrete switching between redundant model copies, preventing incremental detail accumulation and smooth quality transitions as network conditions fluctuate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262586/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view head video (16 calibrated cameras at 802×550 on NeRSemble) and fits a FLAME mesh per frame to obtain expression and pose parameters. The output is a single 43.42 MB progressive asset structured as a priority-ordered stream of refinement records (barycentric subdivision coordinates plus per-Gaussian residual parameters), which can be rendered at any transmission percentage from 5% (2.60 MB, 10,144 Gaussians) to 100% (169,438 Gaussians) at 260–291 FPS on an RTX 4090.

## Method

ProgressiveAvatars grows a per-face hierarchical tree on the FLAME template via adaptive implicit subdivision, where barycentric subdivision points are softly optimized and screen-space gradients at the finest level determine which faces to split. Each 3D Gaussian is bound to a triangle in face-local coordinates using a residual parameterization (rotation, scale, position offsets relative to the triangle frame), ensuring animatability under mesh deformation and structural consistency across levels. After training with multi-level photometric supervision and coarse-to-fine depth expansion, faces are ranked by an importance score computed from aggregated per-pixel opacity and transmittance contributions. At inference, Gaussians are transmitted and activated in descending importance order within each level, so newly arrived records add to previously loaded content without discarding it, yielding pop-free progressive quality improvement from a coarse animatable base to full fidelity.

## Results

On NeRSemble, ProgressiveAvatars at full (100%) transmission achieves NVS PSNR 31.5 / SSIM 0.929 / LPIPS 0.068 and NES PSNR 25.9 / SSIM 0.908 / LPIPS 0.080, matching or slightly exceeding GaussianAvatars. At just 5% of the data budget (2.60 MB, 10,144 Gaussians), the avatar already reaches NVS PSNR 27.9 and NES PSNR 25.1, providing a usable, animatable head in under 3 MB. Rendering speed remains stable at 258–291 FPS on an RTX 4090 across all transmission levels, with no significant degradation as Gaussians accumulate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaussianAvatars (the strongest baseline), ProgressiveAvatars at 100% budget is essentially on par: it edges out on NVS PSNR (31.5 vs 31.1) and NES PSNR (25.9 vs 25.8) but trails slightly on SSIM (0.929 vs 0.937) and LPIPS (0.068 vs 0.064 for NVS; 0.908 vs 0.911 and 0.080 vs 0.076 for NES). The key differentiator is that ProgressiveAvatars delivers this quality progressively—its 5% variant (2.60 MB) already surpasses PointAvatar on NVS PSNR (27.9 vs 25.8) and NES PSNR (25.1 vs 23.4), while GaussianAvatars requires the full 41.90 MB before any rendering is possible. Against PointAvatar, ProgressiveAvatars wins decisively at full budget on all six metrics (e.g., NVS PSNR 31.5 vs 25.8, a 5.7 dB gap) and remains competitive even at the 5% budget. The paper also notes that a discrete compression pipeline (GaussianAvatars + LightGaussian) needs 227.2 MB to store 10 quality levels, whereas ProgressiveAvatars' single progressive asset requires only 43.4 MB while supporting arbitrary intermediate quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar paradigm (GaussianAvatars, Splatting Avatar, FlashAvatar, MeGA) by introducing a hierarchical, progressively transmissible structure. It departs from prior LOD approaches for avatars (LoDAvatar, ArchitectHead) that rely on discrete switching between independent model copies, instead growing a single unified asset through adaptive implicit subdivision on a FLAME-anchored mesh. The face-local Gaussian binding and regularization losses are inherited directly from GaussianAvatars, while the adaptive density-control mechanism is repurposed from 3DGS training to drive structural growth during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Progressive avatar streaming is a practical bottleneck for real-time XR and telepresence where network bandwidth fluctuates; this work shows a single 43 MB asset can replace a 227 MB multi-LOD pipeline while supporting arbitrary transmission percentages. However, evaluation is limited to the NeRSemble dataset with a small number of subjects, and the streaming is emulated offline rather than tested under real network conditions. Open questions include extension to full-body avatars, multi-user concurrent streaming, and whether the importance-ranking strategy generalizes to dynamic scenes with rapidly changing viewpoints.
