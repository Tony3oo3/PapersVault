## Problem

Prior MLLM hallucination research has focused almost exclusively on natural images, leaving hallucination over complex directed graph structures largely unexplored. Additionally, no existing benchmark provides a unified evaluation of fine-grained reasoning that simultaneously integrates structural topology, visual layout, and semantic content over real-world graphs, with current graph benchmarks remaining fragmented between low-level synthetic topology tests and small-scale real-world datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark instance pairs a directed graph image (rendered from Mermaid or Graphviz code, spanning six professional domains) with a natural-language or semi-structured text question. Outputs are task-dependent: free-text answers for path and semantic queries, precise bounding-box coordinates for element localization, eight-way directional classifications for relative positioning, and full Mermaid/Graphviz code for Graph2Code tasks. The benchmark comprises 2,796 graphs (1,796 train / 1,000 test) organized into four capability tiers and twelve fine-grained tasks.

## Method

DiGraphHal-Bench introduces a four-tier taxonomy (Structural, Visual, Semantic, Comprehensive) decomposed into twelve tasks, each with multiple sub-task conditions (e.g., full vs. subgraph, simple vs. cyclic paths, full vs. thumbnail resolution). The key technical contribution is a two-stage verifiable curation pipeline: Stage 1 uses GPT-4o to generate diverse semantic queries grounded in masked subpath queries and semi-structured templates, while Stage 2 performs deterministic algorithmic verification by matching each query to its logical foundation and executing traversal algorithms to derive ground-truth answers, eliminating LLM-induced bias without manual annotation. For empirical analysis, the authors fine-tune Qwen2.5-VL-7B into four specialist models (one per capability) and a curriculum model trained sequentially across all four tiers, then conduct ablation studies on Comprehensive tasks to quantify how each foundational capability contributes to integrative reasoning.

## Results

On the Semantic category, GPT-5 achieves the highest F1 of 76.37% on Semantic Query and 76.62% on Semantic-Rewrite Query, while Gemini 2.5 leads on Comprehensive tasks with 73.59% F1 (Non-Semantic Path-Centric) and 76.15% F1 (Semantic Path-Centric). Small open-source models collapse dramatically: LLaVA-OV-7B scores only 14.22% F1 on Semantic Query and 11.42% F1 on Comprehensive Semantic-Rewrite Path-Centric, versus 76.37% and 62.07% for GPT-5. SFT specialist training on Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves Semantic Query F1 from 37.72% to 43.94% and Comprehensive Non-Semantic Element-Centric F1 from 13.77% to 53.41%, but still trails top proprietary models by 30+ points on most tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 dominates the Semantic category, achieving 76.37% F1 on Semantic Query and 76.62% on Semantic-Rewrite, outperforming Gemini 2.5 (75.94%/72.63%) and o3 (71.63%/71.20%) by 0.4–4.0 points. On the harder Comprehensive category, Gemini 2.5 reverses the lead, scoring 73.59% F1 on Non-Semantic Path-Centric and 76.15% on Semantic Path-Centric, versus 67.05% and 59.16% for GPT-5, suggesting Gemini has stronger integrative multi-modal reasoning. Open-source large models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, LLaVA-OV-72B) trail closed-source leaders by 25–50 F1 points across all categories, with LLaVA-OV-72B particularly weak on Semantic tasks (30.04% F1 vs. 76.37% for GPT-5). Small 7B models are dramatically inferior: LLaVA-OV-7B scores below 15% F1 on nearly all Semantic and Comprehensive sub-tasks, while even the best small model (InternVL3.5-8B) reaches only 38.01% F1 on Semantic Query. SFT specialist training on Qwen2.5-VL-7B yields the largest single-model gain—jumping from 13.77% to 53.41% F1 on Comprehensive Non-Semantic Element-Centric—but still falls 22 points short of GPT-5's 75.50%, confirming that targeted fine-tuning mitigates but does not eliminate hallucination on complex graph reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three fragmented research streams: synthetic structural graph benchmarks (GITA, VisionGraph, VGA), semantic graph benchmarks (VGCURE, Ai et al.), and procedural flowchart benchmarks (FlowVQA, FlowLearn, FlowCE, MindBench). Each prior line addresses only a subset of the problem—structural topology without semantics, semantics without fine-grained visual properties, or scale without verifiable quality. DiGraphHal-Bench departs from all of them by unifying structural, visual, semantic, and comprehensive integrative reasoning into a single four-tier taxonomy over real-world directed graphs, and by introducing a two-stage verifiable curation pipeline (LLM generation + deterministic algorithmic verification) that resolves the persistent scale-quality dilemma that has limited prior procedural graph benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals systematic, previously unquantified failure modes in MLLMs: a universal 'edge blindness' (perceptual bias toward enclosed shapes over filamentary structures), complete collapse on absolute localization and crossing-edge perception, and reliance on shallow lexical matching rather than genuine semantic generalization. The finding that curriculum learning induces catastrophic forgetting of foundational capabilities, while specialist training on structural/visual tasks transfers broadly to comprehensive reasoning, provides actionable guidance for future training strategies. Open questions include whether the hallucination patterns identified here generalize beyond directed graphs to other structured visual media (circuit diagrams, chemical structures), and how to design training curricula that build integrative reasoning without eroding foundational skills.
