## Problem

Backpropagation-based test-time adaptation methods are impractical for resource-constrained edge devices and quantized models due to high memory/computation costs and the need to modify frozen weights. Existing forward-only alternatives either lack explicit optimization objectives (e.g., AdaBN, LAME) or rely on CMA-ES with O(d²) complexity that converges slowly under non-stationary TTA data streams. The paper addresses the gap of an efficient, theoretically-grounded forward-only TTA method that achieves competitive accuracy without backpropagation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images in batches of 64, processed by a frozen ViT-Base backbone. Three learnable 768-dimensional prompts (2,304 total parameters) are prepended to the patch embeddings at the input layer, and the model outputs standard 1000-class ImageNet predictions. Adaptation proceeds online over a continual data stream where domain identity is unknown and no model reset occurs at domain boundaries; the method also operates on INT8-quantized models (PTQ4ViT) that do not support backward propagation.

## Method

FOZO estimates prompt gradients via n-SPSA zeroth-order approximation: for each test batch, it perturbs the prompt P in ±ε_t·Z directions (Z~N(0,I_d)), runs two forward passes, and computes a projected gradient from the loss difference, requiring only forward propagation. A dynamic perturbation schedule (Eq. 12) resets ε_t to ε_0 when loss spikes indicate a domain shift and otherwise decays it by factor α=0.9 toward ε_min, balancing early exploration against asymptotic convergence. The unsupervised loss combines deep-shallow [CLS] token statistics alignment (matching mean and std of shallow vs. deep layer activations to pre-computed source-domain values) with prediction entropy minimization, weighted by λ=0.4. Convergence is proven under L-smoothness and a local r-effective rank assumption, showing the rate depends on effective rank r rather than parameter dimension d.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (5k, level 5) under continual adaptation, FOZO achieves 62.67% Top-1 accuracy with 28 forward passes, outperforming all gradient-based baselines (EATA 61.35%, DEYO 60.76%, SAR 60.36%, TENT 58.32%) and forward-only baselines (FOA 61.32%, ZOA 61.75%). FOZO updates only 2,304 parameters and consumes 831 MiB peak GPU memory, compared to 39,936 parameters and ~5,500 MiB for gradient-based methods. Under the more constrained 2-forward-pass setting (Table 1), FOZO still leads with 59.52% vs. ZOA (58.56%) and FOA (58.13%). On quantized INT8 models, FOZO achieves 58.00% average accuracy, surpassing FOA (57.07%) and ZOA (56.91%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOZO outperforms all eight baselines on ImageNet-C (5k, level 5) average Top-1 accuracy. Against forward-only methods, FOZO (62.67%) beats ZOA (61.75%) by 0.92% and FOA (61.32%) by 1.35%, while also running faster (2351s vs. 2405s for ZOA, 2391s for FOA) and using fewer parameters (2,304 vs. 26,145 for ZOA). Against gradient-based methods, FOZO surpasses EATA (61.35%) by 1.32%, DEYO (60.76%) by 1.91%, SAR (60.36%) by 2.31%, and TENT (58.32%) by 4.35%, while reducing peak memory from ~5,500 MiB to 831 MiB and updating only 2,304 parameters instead of 29,184–39,936. On quantized INT8 models (Table 3), FOZO (58.00%) leads FOA (57.07%) and ZOA (56.91%), confirming robustness under hardware constraints. On ImageNet-R and ImageNet-Sketch (Table 4), FOZO achieves the best accuracy (64.1% and 50.5% respectively), outperforming ZOA (63.6%, 49.8%) and FOA (63.2%, 49.7%). The ablation (Table 5) shows the dynamic perturbation strategy contributes +2.6% and deep-shallow feature alignment contributes +2.8% over the base ZO+entropy formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOZO sits at the intersection of three research threads: visual prompt tuning (Jia et al., ECCV 2022) provides the learnable prompt architecture; SPSA (Spall, 1992) supplies the zeroth-order gradient estimation mechanism; and MeZO (Malladi et al., NeurIPS 2023) contributes the local r-effective rank assumption that underpins the convergence proof. The key departure is from FOA (Niu et al., ICML 2024), which uses CMA-ES to optimize prompts forward-only but suffers from O(d²) complexity and slow convergence; FOZO replaces CMA-ES with SPSA-based gradient estimation whose variance scales with effective rank r rather than parameter dimension d. Unlike ZOA, which modifies internal normalization layers, FOZO keeps all model weights frozen and only updates external prompts, preserving model immutability for deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FOZO addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck: gradient-based TTA methods require 5–7× more GPU memory (5495 vs 831 MiB) and cannot operate on quantized (INT8) or hard-coded models where backpropagation is unavailable. The dynamic perturbation strategy and its convergence guarantee under non-stationary data streams provide a principled alternative to ad-hoc learning-rate schedules in continual adaptation. Open questions include whether the local r-effective rank assumption holds for deeper or larger ViT variants, whether the approach extends to non-ViT architectures (e.g., CNNs, diffusion models), and how the method behaves under more extreme or multi-modal distribution shifts beyond ImageNet-C corruptions.
