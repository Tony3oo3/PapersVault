## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on localized crowd counting models are limited to single-paradigm transferability (density-map to density-map), leaving cross-paradigm attacks between density-map and point-regression architectures entirely unexplored. Additionally, prior methods face a stark trade-off: either high stealth (PSNR ≥ 22 dB) with weak attack strength (MAE ≤ 120), or strong attacks with visually perceptible perturbations (PSNR ≈ 11 dB), with no approach achieving both simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The generator takes a 512×512 RGB crowd scene image and produces a bounded perturbation δ with ||δ||∞ ≤ 8/255 in a single forward pass, yielding the adversarial image I_adv = clip(I + δ, 0, 1). Unlike iterative per-image optimization methods, the learned U-Net mapping generalizes across architectures and datasets at inference time without requiring target-model access. The perturbation is designed to be visually imperceptible (PSNR ≈ 19 dB, SSIM ≈ 0.82) while degrading counting accuracy by roughly 7× in MAE.

## Method

The framework employs a 3-level U-Net generator trained with a multi-task loss combining paradigm-specific and cross-paradigm components. For point-regression surrogates, a scene-density-aware logit suppression loss enforces under-counting by targeting high-confidence detections (with adaptive thresholding that decays over training); for density-map surrogates, a peak-targeted density suppression loss attacks both absolute peak magnitude and local prominence, switching between heatmap and peak formulations based on crowd isolation ratio. The cross-paradigm perturbation loss couples four constraints: L2 hinge for magnitude bounding, total variation for spatial smoothness, FFT-based frequency suppression to align perturbations with natural low-frequency crowd statistics, and Grad-CAM-guided masking that concentrates perturbations on semantically important backbone regions shared across architectures. This design exploits the observation that crowd-counting models share backbone inductive biases (VGG, ResNet, HRNet), enabling a single perturbation to transfer across fundamentally different output paradigms.

## Results

On SHHA (FIDTM target), the proposed method achieves an overall MAE of 259.76 (SASNet surrogate) and 271.71 (P2PNet surrogate) versus a clean MAE of 28.1, representing a ~9× degradation, while maintaining PSNR of 19.25/19.18 dB and SSIM of 0.820/0.817. In dense scenes (>1000 people), MAE surges to 1013.6–1040.18 with a 58–60% miss rate. Cross-paradigm transferability is demonstrated across seven models with transfer ratios (TR) ranging from 0.55 to 1.69, with HMoDE→P2PNet achieving the highest TR of 1.69 on SHHA and 1.55 on UCF-QNRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six optimization-based baselines (DI²FGSM, Admix, FIA, SVRE, GRA, L2T) by a factor of 3.3–3.8× in overall MAE (259.76 vs. 68.96–77.83) while maintaining comparable PSNR (~19.2 vs. ~20.6 dB). Against GE-AdvGAN, the proposed method achieves 2.3× higher MAE (259.76 vs. 110.78) at the cost of ~4 dB lower PSNR (19.25 vs. 23.20), representing a substantially stronger attack. DiffAttack achieves higher raw MAE (414.66) but at the expense of severe visual degradation (PSNR 11.52 dB, SSIM 0.483), making it impractical for stealth; the proposed method delivers 63% of DiffAttack's MAE while retaining 7.7 dB higher PSNR. PAP, the crowd-counting-specific patch attack, is the weakest baseline (MAE 32.72, barely above clean 28.1), which the proposed method surpasses by 8×. In dense scenes, the gap widens dramatically: the proposed method reaches MAE >1000 with ~58–60% miss rate, whereas optimization-based methods plateau at MAE ~250–273 and GE-AdvGAN at 518.8, confirming that the multi-task loss design is particularly effective in high-density regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of transferable adversarial attacks on crowd counting (PAP, APAM, DiffAttack, GE-AdvGAN) by introducing the first cross-paradigm framework that simultaneously targets density-map and point-regression architectures. It repurposes Grad-CAM from an interpretability tool into a perturbation-guidance loss, and combines it with frequency-domain (FFT) and total-variation constraints to exploit shared backbone vulnerabilities across paradigms. The paradigm-specific losses—logit suppression for point regression and peak/heatmap suppression for density maps—depart from prior single-paradigm patch-based attacks by operating on the model's internal confidence structure rather than on spatial patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that adversarial perturbations transfer across fundamentally different architectural paradigms (density map ↔ point regression) with super-transferability ratios up to 1.69 reveals a previously unexamined security gap in deployed crowd-counting systems used for public safety and epidemiological tracking. The paper leaves open whether these vulnerabilities extend to physical-world attacks (printed patches, projected light) and whether paradigm-agnostic defenses can be designed. A key limitation is that the ~19 dB PSNR, while better than DiffAttack, is still visually detectable under close inspection, and the evaluation covers only two datasets and seven models, leaving the generality of the cross-paradigm claim partially untested.
