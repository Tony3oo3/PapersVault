## Problem

Source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) must adapt a pretrained model to an unlabeled target domain without access to source data, yet existing methods rely on pseudo-label self-training that accumulates confirmation bias under large domain shifts. While recent work introduces VLMs as external semantic priors, these approaches still use hard pseudo-label supervision that propagates noise. The paper addresses how to leverage VLM semantic knowledge for SFDA in a way that avoids both pseudo-label fragility and hard-label noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target images, a pretrained source model (ResNet50 or ResNet101 backbone with a linear classifier whose weight vectors serve as class prototypes), and a frozen CLIP VLM with lightweight adapters inserted into its last three transformer blocks. No source data or target labels are accessed during adaptation. The output is a per-sample class probability distribution over K classes, from which a Top-1 label is extracted for evaluation. The VLM's image and text embeddings are projected into a shared space via inner-product similarity followed by softmax to produce class-level semantic priors.

## Method

VSFOT alternates between two stages: VLM-Guided Model Alignment (VGMA) and Model-Guided VLM Adaptation (MGVA). In VGMA, target features are aligned to source classifier-weight prototypes via an optimal transport objective where the transport cost couples cosine feature distance with cross-entropy prediction distance (following JDOT). Crucially, the OT plan is computed using a VLM-guided cost that replaces the model's feature distance with VLM-predicted class probabilities, decoupling the plan computation from model optimization to provide a stable, semantically grounded alignment reference even when model predictions are unreliable. The Sinkhorn algorithm solves the regularized OT problem with uniform target marginals and VLM-estimated prototype marginals. Two auxiliary losses—view-level contrastive regularization and batch-level information maximization—enhance discriminability. In MGVA, Top-k sparse predictions from the task model are distilled into the VLM's adapters via KL divergence, injecting task-specific cues back into the VLM. The two stages alternate, each freezing the other model, forming a bidirectional feedback loop that co-evolves both models toward the target domain.

## Results

On Office-31, VSFOT achieves 92.66% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best SFDA baselines ProDe (92.57%) and DIFO (92.45%). On Office-Home, VSFOT reaches 85.34% average accuracy, surpassing ProDe (84.20%) and DIFO (82.90%). On the most challenging DomainNet-126, VSFOT attains 82.63% average accuracy, exceeding DIFO by 2.55% and the best UDA method DAMP by 5.31%. On VisDA, VSFOT achieves 90.57%, ranking second behind ProDe (91.04%) but ahead of DIFO (90.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSFOT outperforms all conventional SFDA methods (SHOT, NRC, AaD, AdaCon, CoWA, PLUE, TPDS) by a wide margin across all four benchmarks, with the largest gaps on DomainNet-126 where it leads SHOT by 14.53% and CoWA by 13.79%. Among VLM-based SFDA methods, VSFOT surpasses DIFO by 0.21% on Office-31, 2.44% on Office-Home, and 2.55% on DomainNet-126, and edges out ProDe by 0.09% on Office-31 and 1.14% on Office-Home. On VisDA, VSFOT (90.57%) trails ProDe (91.04%) by 0.47% but still exceeds DIFO (90.33%). Compared to VLM-based UDA methods that require source data access (DAPL, PADCLIP, ADCLIP, PDA, DAMP), VSFOT achieves comparable or superior results on Office-Home (85.34% vs. best UDA 78.24%) and DomainNet-126 (82.63% vs. best UDA 77.32%), while remaining strictly source-free. Against CLIP zero-shot predictions, VSFOT gains 12.55%, 6.30%, 5.96%, and 4.34% on Office-31, Office-Home, VisDA, and DomainNet-126 respectively, confirming that the OT-guided adaptation effectively leverages VLM semantic priors beyond their raw zero-shot capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active threads: source-free domain adaptation (SFDA), VLM-guided adaptation, and optimal transport (OT) for distribution alignment. It builds directly on JDOT [4] for the transport formulation and on recent VLM-guided SFDA methods (DIFO [35], ProDe [36]) that inject CLIP semantic priors into adaptation. The key departure is twofold: (1) instead of using VLM pseudo-labels as hard supervision, VSFOT embeds VLM-derived class probabilities into the OT cost matrix and marginals, performing a soft, geometry-aware alignment; and (2) it introduces a bidirectional distillation loop where the task model's high-confidence predictions are distilled back into a lightweight adapter on the VLM, a reverse path absent in prior VLM-guided SFDA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized failure mode in SFDA—confirmation bias from self-training pseudo-labels—by replacing hard label supervision with an OT-based soft alignment guided by VLM semantics. The ablation showing that naive KL-divergence soft alignment (Case 1) actually underperforms CLIP zero-shot is a useful cautionary result, highlighting that the specific OT formulation with decoupled cost matrices is what makes the approach work. A notable limitation is the weaker VisDA result (second place), attributed to the absence of an explicit error-correction mechanism for highly confusable classes. Open questions include whether the bidirectional distillation loop scales to open-set or few-shot target scenarios, and whether the approach generalizes beyond fixed-class classification to detection or segmentation tasks.
