## Problem

State-of-the-art feed-forward camera pose estimation methods (DUSt3R, VGGT, Rig3R, MapAnything) depend on large-scale 3D annotations from LiDAR, SfM, or simulation, which are expensive to produce and limited in scale compared to the vast pool of unlabeled driving video. Self-supervised pretraining has driven breakthroughs in vision and language but has been largely unexplored for geometry perception tasks like ego pose estimation. This paper asks whether latent action representations learned from inverse-dynamics pretraining on unlabeled video can be repurposed as motion-centric features for accurate, generalizable pose prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16 consecutive front-facing RGB frames at 960×448 resolution, with frame stride randomized between 1 and 4 fps during training (2 fps at evaluation) to capture both short- and long-term motion. The pose head outputs per-frame relative camera poses as 3D scale-agnostic translations plus 4D quaternion rotations, a 1D field-of-view, and a single scalar metric scale factor (with exponential activation) that is disentangled from the translation components to separate geometric direction from absolute distance.

## Method

LA-Pose uses a two-stage pipeline. In pretraining, a Genie-style inverse-forward dynamics model is trained self-supervised on 10.2M unlabeled driving video snippets: an ST-Transformer inverse dynamics encoder with causal masking and learnable query tokens produces 15 latent action tokens (one per frame transition) that encode inter-frame motion, while a forward dynamics ST-Transformer predicts VQ-VAE codebook logits for the next frame, providing the training signal. In post-training, the forward dynamics branch is discarded and a lightweight pose estimation head—comprising a non-causal transformer over the latent action tokens plus a single learnable metric scale token, followed by separate MLP heads for 7D pose/FOV and scalar scale—is trained on a small set of LiDAR-calibrated poses from Waymo, nuScenes, and Argoverse. The key insight is that latent actions from inverse dynamics inherently compress ego-motion, making them natural inputs for pose regression; freezing the pretrained backbone during post-training preserves these motion priors and yields substantially better zero-shot generalization than fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Waymo benchmark, LA-Pose achieves 91.4% AUC@5 and 1.20×10⁻² ATE-S, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (next best: Rig3R at 77.9% AUC@5). On the zero-shot PandaSet benchmark, LA-Pose maintains 86.3% AUC@5 and 1.13×10⁻² ATE-S, again surpassing all compared methods. LA-Pose is the only method reporting metric-scale trajectory error (ATE-M), achieving 0.88 m on Waymo and 0.86 m on PandaSet, compared to 4.74 m and 7.28 m for MapAnything. The paper also reports that LA-Pose exhibits substantially lower variance in AUC@5 across test scenes than VGGT, indicating more consistent performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LA-Pose decisively outperforms Rig3R on Waymo, achieving 91.4% vs. 77.9% AUC@5 (+13.5 pp) and 1.20 vs. 3.17 ATE-S (2.6× lower trajectory error); Rig3R is excluded from PandaSet because it was trained on the full PandaSet dataset. Against VGGT, LA-Pose wins on AUC@5 by a large margin on both benchmarks (91.4% vs. 74.8% on Waymo, 86.3% vs. 75.0% on PandaSet) and on ATE-S on Waymo (1.20 vs. 1.43), though VGGT edges out LA-Pose slightly on PandaSet ATE-S (0.99 vs. 1.13). Against MapAnything, LA-Pose dominates on every reported metric: 91.4% vs. 65.0% AUC@5 and 0.88 m vs. 4.74 m ATE-M on Waymo, with similar margins on PandaSet. Notably, LA-Pose achieves these gains while using orders of magnitude less labeled 3D data—only ~2,300 short scenes from Waymo, nuScenes, and Argoverse for post-training—compared to the large-scale supervised 3D annotations required by all three baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

LA-Pose builds directly on the Genie architecture [5] (ICML 2024), adopting its inverse/forward dynamics modules and ST-Transformer encoder, but repurposes the latent action representations—originally designed as conditioning signals for video generation or robot policy networks—as motion-centric inputs to a lightweight camera pose estimation head. This departs from the feed-forward 3D reconstruction line (DUSt3R, VGGT, Rig3R, MapAnything), which relies on large-scale 3D supervision from SfM, LiDAR, or simulation, by instead leveraging 10M+ unlabeled driving video snippets for self-supervised pretraining and only a small set of LiDAR-calibrated poses for post-training. The work also connects to the broader self-supervised video learning literature (V-JEPA, Video-MAE, Scaling 4D Representations) but is the first to specifically target ego-pose estimation through the inverse-dynamics latent action formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LA-Pose demonstrates that large-scale self-supervised video pretraining can substitute for costly 3D supervision in camera pose estimation, achieving over 10% higher AUC@5 than supervised feed-forward baselines while using orders of magnitude fewer labeled samples. The finding that a 50-dimensional latent bottleneck outperforms the full 1536-D representation for downstream pose estimation (despite higher pre-training loss) is a useful design principle: aggressive compression forces motion-centric abstraction over appearance leakage. Key limitations include degraded performance on rare motions (e.g., reverse driving) and the current restriction to front-facing driving cameras; extending the pretraining corpus to in-the-wild embodied videos with diverse agents and camera configurations is a natural next step toward general geometric priors.
