## Problem

In federated learning deployments, unlabeled clients with heterogeneous distributions arrive sequentially, requiring online adaptation without labels. Direct application of test-time adaptation destabilizes the FL global model (which resides in a flat loss basin), while naive aggregation of unvetted client updates risks catastrophic forgetting. Existing federated continual TTA work operates under synchronous, cluster-based settings with predefined shifts, failing to capture the extreme spatio-temporal heterogeneity and asynchrony of real-world sequential client arrival.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained global model (obtained from initial FedAvg training on labeled source clients) and a stream of unlabeled test images from sequentially arriving target clients, each drawn from a completely unrelated distribution. No labels, features, or logits are shared between clients or with the server, enforcing strict privacy. The output is twofold: per-client adapted predictions (class labels for classification, pixel-wise masks for segmentation) and an evolved global model whose parameters are updated after each client via uncertainty-weighted fusion, enabling the next client to inherit accumulated knowledge.

## Method

BPFedCTTA reformulates the entire continual adaptation pipeline through a Bayesian lens with two coupled modules. On the client side, Bayesian Prior-guided Adaptation (BPA) treats the current global model as a Gaussian prior and performs MAP estimation, where the likelihood is an entropy-minimization TTA loss; the resulting quadratic regularizer anchors local updates within the global model's high-probability region, and the prior covariance adapts dynamically via prediction entropy (tightening when confident, relaxing under uncertainty). On the server side, Uncertainty-Gated Single-client Aggregation (UGSA) formulates global model evolution as a Bayesian belief update: the client's adapted posterior is fused with the previous global prior via KL minimization, with a reliability gate derived from the client's predictive entropy that attenuates high-uncertainty updates. Under Gaussian assumptions this yields a closed-form precision-weighted update, effectively performing sequential Bayesian filtering that integrates reliable knowledge while suppressing noisy or overfitted client contributions, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting across the continual stream.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, BPFedCTTA achieves a mean accuracy of 68.44%, outperforming the strongest federated TTA baselines TTA-FedDG (68.09%) and FedCTTA (68.10%) by 0.35–0.44 points. On CIFAR-100-C, it reaches 67.58% mean accuracy, surpassing TTA-FedDG (65.58%) by a substantial 2.0 points. On the Cityscapes-to-ACDC segmentation benchmark, BPFedCTTA attains 62.88% mIoU in Sequence 1 and progressively improves to 65.19% in Sequence 4, demonstrating effective continual adaptation without catastrophic forgetting. On medical segmentation (Retinal Fundus, Sequence 1), it achieves 75.52% Dice, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPFedCTTA outperforms all 12 baselines on both CIFAR-10-C and CIFAR-100-C. The closest competitors are TTA-FedDG (68.09% / 65.58%) and FedCTTA (68.10% / 64.45%), which BPFedCTTA surpasses by 0.35–0.44 points on CIFAR-10-C and by 2.0–3.1 points on CIFAR-100-C, indicating the Bayesian prior regularization and uncertainty gating are especially beneficial under higher class heterogeneity. Standard TTA methods (Tent, CoTTA) degrade significantly in the sequential federated setting—CoTTA drops from 58.60% to 56.99% mIoU across Cityscapes sequences—while BPFedCTTA shows progressive improvement (62.88% → 65.19%), confirming that naive entropy minimization destabilizes the federated global model. Personalized FL methods (FedProx, FedBN) underperform because they assume labeled data unavailable in the FedCTTA setting. On medical segmentation, BPFedCTTA leads Sequence 1 on Retinal Fundus (75.52% Dice vs. 75.32% for TTA-FedDG) and achieves the best Sequence 4 on Prostate MRI (75.06%), while maintaining the most stable performance across all four sequences compared to baselines like FedGA that degrade from 72.22% to 68.27%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of federated test-time adaptation work (FedCTTA [40], ATP [2], FedTHE+ [17], TTA-FedDG [28]) by formalizing a more realistic sequential, asynchronous, strictly unlabeled client-arrival setting that departs from prior synchronous cluster-based formulations. Conceptually, it repurposes Bayesian inference—treating the global model as an evolving Gaussian prior and local adaptation as MAP estimation—to provide principled regularization for TTA in the federated context, a connection not previously exploited. The uncertainty-gated aggregation (UGSA) draws on Bayesian belief updating and KL-divergence minimization to replace naive FedAvg-style averaging, addressing the catastrophic forgetting problem that plagues direct TTA application to flat-basin federated models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important deployment gap: how to safely evolve a federated global model when unlabeled clients arrive sequentially from unknown distributions, a scenario that current PFL and TTA methods cannot handle. The progressive improvement across sequences (rather than degradation) on Cityscapes-to-ACDC validates that safe continual evolution is achievable, but the modest margins over TTA-FedDG (e.g., 68.44% vs. 68.09% on CIFAR10-C) suggest the Bayesian framing, while principled, yields incremental rather than transformative gains. Open questions include whether the Gaussian prior assumptions hold for very deep or heterogeneous architectures, how the method scales to hundreds of sequential clients, and whether the diagonal covariance approximation in UGSA loses critical cross-parameter information under extreme domain shifts.
