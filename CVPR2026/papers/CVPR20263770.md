## Problem

Fourier-based PEFT methods like FourierFT assume the weight change ΔW has a sparse spectrum, enabling fine-tuning with only a few spectral coefficients. However, this paper empirically shows that ΔW's power spectrum is actually near-uniform across all frequencies, meaning a small number of coefficients cannot accurately reconstruct the weight change. The key question is whether an invertible transformation exists that maps a latent matrix with genuinely sparse spectrum to ΔW, so that few coefficients suffice.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained weight matrix W ∈ R^{d×k} and a small subset D_sub of the downstream training set (used only for gradient-based pre-estimation, not for the actual fine-tuning loop). It outputs a sparse complex spectral matrix F with only a few nonzero trainable coefficients (as few as 0.08% of total parameters); the adapted weight is recovered as W' = W + T(R(IDFT(F))·α), where T is the inverse row/column re-indexing. The pre-estimation step requires one forward-backward pass over D_sub per weight matrix, adding negligible overhead (0.02 s per transformation).

## Method

S²FT first pre-estimates a coarse weight change ΔŴ by accumulating negative gradients over a small training subset, then formulates an invertible transformation as a row-and-column rearrangement problem: find a permutation that minimizes the total Euclidean distance between adjacent rows (and columns), which Proposition 1 shows is equivalent to suppressing high-frequency spectral energy and thus producing a sparse spectrum. This NP-hard rearrangement is solved greedily via nearest-neighbor search, yielding permutations π_r and π_c that define the invertible map T. PEFT is then performed by optimizing only a few spectral coefficients of the latent matrix ΔW̄ in the sparse-spectrum domain, with coefficient positions sampled according to a power-spectrum-guided probability p(u,v) ∝ ‖DFT(ΔW̄)‖_{(u,v)}^γ (γ=1.5). The adapted weight is recovered by inverse re-indexing ΔW[π_r,:][:,π_c] = ΔW̄, preserving neuron link structure while exploiting the sparsified spectrum.

## Results

On the VTAB-1k benchmark (19 datasets, ViT-B/16), S²FT achieves 73.6% mean accuracy with only 0.08% trainable parameters, outperforming FourierFT (72.8% at 0.16% params) and LoRA (72.6% at 0.37% params). On FGVC fine-grained classification, S²FT reaches 89.1% mean accuracy at 0.08% params, surpassing FourierFT (87.9% at 0.16%) and even full fine-tuning (88.5% at 100%). On GLUE (RoBERTa-base), S²FT attains 85.6% average with 0.02% params, exceeding full fine-tuning (85.2%) and LoRA (85.2% at 0.24%). For instruction tuning on LLaMA2-13B, S²FT scores 7.94 on Vicuna Eval, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²FT consistently outperforms FourierFT across all four task categories while using roughly half the trainable parameters: +0.8% on VTAB (73.6% vs 72.8%), +1.2% on FGVC (89.1% vs 87.9%), +0.6% on GLUE (85.6% vs 85.0%), and +0.13 on Vicuna Eval for LLaMA2-13B (7.94 vs 7.89). Against LoRA, S²FT matches or exceeds accuracy on VTAB (73.6% vs 72.6%) and FGVC (89.1% vs 87.5%) while using 4.6× fewer parameters (0.08% vs 0.37%). On GLUE, S²FT (0.02% params) surpasses LoRA (0.24% params) by 0.4% in average score, and is the only method to exceed full fine-tuning (85.6% vs 85.2%). On the subject-driven generation task, S²FT (0.06% params) beats FourierFT (0.07%) on CLIP-I (0.784 vs 0.750) and LPIPS (0.769 vs 0.732), approaching DreamBooth (100% params) quality. The method also outperforms GPS (73.6% at 0.25% params) on VTAB with 3× fewer parameters, and surpasses HydraLoRA (73.3% at 0.56%) by 0.3% while using 7× fewer parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends FourierFT (Gao et al., 2024), which performs PEFT by fine-tuning a small number of spectral coefficients of the weight change ΔW under the assumption that ΔW has a sparse spectrum. The key departure is an empirical finding that ΔW's spectrum is actually power-uniform rather than sparse, rendering FourierFT's core assumption invalid. To recover the benefits of spectral sparsity, the paper introduces an invertible row/column rearrangement transformation (solved via nearest-neighbor search, a greedy TSP heuristic) that maps a latent smooth matrix with genuinely sparse spectrum back to ΔW. Within the broader PEFT landscape (LoRA, adapters, BitFit), S²FT occupies the Fourier-transform-based reparameterization sub-family but reduces trainable parameters to 0.08%, roughly half of FourierFT and an order of magnitude below LoRA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical demonstration that weight-change spectra are power-uniform challenges a foundational assumption in Fourier-based PEFT and explains why FourierFT's performance plateaus; this insight is likely to influence how future spectral-domain methods are designed. Practically, S²FT achieves competitive or superior accuracy across vision, NLU, generation, and instruction-tuning tasks with only 0.08% trainable parameters, making it attractive for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether the rearrangement-based transformation is optimal (the NP-hard problem is solved only approximately), whether other invertible transformations (e.g., learned permutations, graph-based reordering) could yield sparser spectra, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-matrix weight structures such as 3D convolutions or attention patterns.
