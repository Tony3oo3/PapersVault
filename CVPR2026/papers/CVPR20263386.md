## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces millions of Gaussian points, creating prohibitive memory overhead. Existing pruning-based variants reduce point counts but disrupt spatial consistency and continuity of the remaining points, causing visible rendering artifacts because they ignore the global and local continuity constraints needed to redistribute surviving Gaussians into uncovered regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263386/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images and a sparse SfM point cloud (standard 3DGS initialization) and produces a compact set of 3D Gaussians—each with a 3D center, covariance, color, and opacity—along with rendered novel-view images. The output representation uses roughly 12.5% of the Gaussian points required by vanilla 3DGS while maintaining or exceeding its PSNR, and the pipeline does not alter the underlying Gaussian representation or rendering equation.

## Method

GS² introduces a three-phase pipeline: (1) an ELBO-guided adaptive densification that automatically halts point growth once the relative change in a smoothed ELBO (combining rendering loss and a KL-style complexity term over normalized covariance and point density) falls below a threshold; (2) an opacity-aware progressive pruning that applies a quadratic-plus-linear opacity regularization and removes low-opacity Gaussians every 100 iterations; and (3) a Graph-based Spatial Distribution Optimization (GSDO) module that builds a k-NN graph over pruned Gaussian centers, encodes each point via a two-layer MLP with residual neighbor aggregation and global max-pooled context, then projects the latent features back to ℝ³ through a lightweight head. Two unsupervised losses—global centroid alignment (L_cet) and local smoothness (L_smt) penalizing Euclidean-feature discrepancies among neighbors—jointly guide the spatial redistribution of remaining Gaussians while jointly updating their colors and scales, restoring spatial continuity without modifying the 3DGS rendering pipeline.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360, GS² achieves a PSNR of 27.74 dB with only 0.30M Gaussian points (≈9.6% of 3DGS's 3.12M), matching or exceeding 3DGS's 27.71 dB PSNR. On Tanks & Temples, GS² reaches 24.90 dB PSNR with 0.24M points (≈15.3% of 3DGS's 1.57M), surpassing 3DGS by 0.71 dB. Across both datasets, GS² uses roughly 12.5% of the Gaussian points of the original 3DGS while delivering higher PSNR, and it attains the best SSIM (0.82/0.85) and competitive LPIPS (0.23/0.21) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS² outperforms all seven baselines on both PSNR and Gaussian point count across both datasets. Against the original 3DGS, GS² achieves higher PSNR (27.74 vs. 27.71 on Mip-NeRF 360; 24.90 vs. 24.19 on T&T) while using only 9.6% and 15.3% of the points, respectively. Among pruning-based competitors, the closest rival is MaskGaussian (27.61 / 24.16 PSNR), which GS² surpasses by 0.13 and 0.74 dB while using roughly half the points (0.30M vs. 1.50M; 0.24M vs. 0.74M). LightGaussian, despite its aggressive compression, lags significantly on T&T (23.16 PSNR) due to spatial artifacts from pruning, whereas GS² maintains 24.90 dB. Mini-Splatting uses a comparable point budget (0.49M / 0.27M) but trails GS² by 0.40 and 1.18 dB in PSNR, confirming that densification alone without explicit spatial-distribution optimization is insufficient. The advantage is most pronounced on T&T, where the bounded indoor geometry benefits from the graph-based spatial redistribution, yielding a 0.71 dB gain over 3DGS and up to 1.74 dB over LightGaussian.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pruning-based compact 3DGS line (LightGaussian, EAGLES, MaskGaussian, Compact 3DGS, Mini-Splatting, CompGS) by identifying a gap those methods leave unaddressed: after removing redundant Gaussians, the surviving points' spatial distribution is disrupted and implicit optimization is insufficient to restore continuity. The key departure is the explicit graph-based spatial redistribution step (GSDO), which borrows the k-NN graph encoder from 4DGS but strips away temporal modeling, and draws spatial-constraint losses from Gaussian Grouping. The ELBO-guided adaptive densification repurposes variational-inference termination criteria to replace hand-tuned iteration thresholds in 3DGS's density control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving PSNR at or above full 3DGS with only ~12.5% of the Gaussian points makes high-fidelity 3D scene representation practical for memory-constrained deployments (autonomous driving, AR), and the GSDO module's demonstrated generalizability as a drop-in post-processing step for other pruning methods broadens its utility. The ablation showing that simply extending training iterations after pruning yields only marginal gains is a useful negative result, indicating the post-pruning optimization landscape is fundamentally different from the pre-pruning one. The main open question is the additional training overhead (10→16 min) introduced by spatial optimization, which the authors acknowledge but leave for future work; reducing this cost without sacrificing quality would further strengthen the practical case.
