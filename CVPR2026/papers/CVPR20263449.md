## Problem

Controllable mask-to-image generation for remote sensing semantic segmentation faces two unresolved challenges: (1) the absence of effective DiT-based control schemes for RS imagery due to the domain gap and lack of RS-pretrained diffusion transformers, and (2) the inherent stochasticity of flow-based sampling causing semantic drift that post-hoc filtering strategies cannot fully mitigate, particularly in few-shot and complex-scene settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263449/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generator takes a global text prompt describing scene semantics and a pixel-level semantic mask (tokenized as a third modality stream) as joint conditioning, producing a 512×512 synthetic RGB image. The mask is not used as a spatial layout but as a dense per-pixel class label that constrains both spatial arrangement and semantic content. Synthetic images are combined with real samples (at a 3:1 synthetic-to-real ratio) to train a standard MMSegmentation-based segmentation network.

## Method

TODSynth integrates two complementary control mechanisms. At the architecture level, it extends the SD v3.5 MM-DiT with a Unified Triple Attention scheme that concatenates text, image, and mask token sequences and computes joint self-attention with modality-specific Q/K/V projections, enabling direct cross-modal interaction between text semantics and mask spatial constraints; both image and mask branches are fully fine-tuned. At the sampling level, Control-Rectify Flow Matching (CRFM) computes a rectification vector by backpropagating the cross-entropy loss of a pre-trained segmentation network on the predicted final image (obtained via a single-step latent prediction and VAE decode) with respect to the current velocity field, then adds this vector to the predicted velocity during the first ~4 ODE integration steps. This corrects the trajectory direction rather than directly optimizing the latent state, avoiding the mode collapse observed with direct latent-space gradient updates, and leverages the high plasticity of early sampling steps where small corrections have outsized downstream effect.

## Results

On FUSU-4k (17 land-cover classes), TODSynth achieves 75.66% OA, 49.41% mIoU, and 63.27% mAcc, improving over the real-data-only baseline by +1.39%/+4.14%/+6.83% respectively. On LoveDA (rural-to-urban, 6 classes), it reaches 79.82% OA, 66.12% mIoU, and 80.93% mAcc, a gain of +1.60%/+2.08%/+2.22% over baseline. The CRFM sampling strategy alone contributes +0.84% mIoU and +1.60% mAcc on FUSU-4k (and +0.67%/+1.02% on LoveDA) over the same MM-DiT architecture without CRFM, while using only 3× synthetic-to-real data ratio versus 5× or 10× for competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TODSynth outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the strongest UNet-based generator (FreeMask with pixel-level filtering), it gains +1.43% mIoU and +6.98% mAcc on FUSU-4k while using fewer synthetic samples (×3 vs ×5). The RS-specific foundation model SynthEarth (training-free, CLIP-score filtering) is surpassed by +1.88% mIoU and +4.36% mAcc on FUSU-4k, and by +2.05% mIoU and +2.93% mAcc on LoveDA, confirming that fine-tuned MM-DiT with task-guided sampling beats a large pre-trained RS generator. The closest comparison is SD v3.5 with the same Tri-attention architecture and FreeMask filter but without CRFM; TODSynth still leads by +0.84% mIoU and +1.60% mAcc on FUSU-4k, isolating the contribution of the control-rectify sampling step. Notably, Freestyle and ControlNet (SD v1.5) actually underperform the real-data-only baseline on FUSU-4k mIoU, highlighting the difficulty of mask-to-image control in the UNet paradigm for fine-grained RS scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MM-DiT architecture from SD v3.5 by adding a third mask modality stream via unified triple attention, departing from both ControlNet-style adapter injection and the siamese MM-DiT design of CreateLayout. At the sampling level, it builds on Rectified Flow's ODE-based generation but introduces a task-feedback-guided velocity field correction (CRFM) during early steps, contrasting with the dominant post-hoc filtering paradigm (FreeMask, CLIP scoring) used in prior RS data synthesis work. The paper systematically positions itself against UNet-based controllable generators and RS-specific foundation models (SynthEarth, TerraGen), arguing that DiT-based architectures with appropriate control design and in-process sampling correction outperform both.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play CRFM mechanism offers a practical way to improve synthetic data quality without retraining the diffusion model, which is valuable for RS practitioners facing annotation scarcity. However, the method's sensitivity to the number of correction steps and its dependence on a sufficiently capable pre-trained segmentation model limit its robustness; with very limited data, the guidance signal degrades. Open questions include whether the velocity-field rectification principle generalizes to other conditional generation tasks beyond mask-to-image, whether it can be combined with RS-specific foundation models to improve diversity, and whether the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-temporal RS settings.
