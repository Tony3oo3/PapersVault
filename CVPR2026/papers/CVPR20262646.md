## Problem

3D morphing across diverse object categories remains difficult because existing correspondence-based methods fail to generalize cross-category, and naive approaches with SLAT-based generators (2D morph + 3D lift, or direct noise/condition interpolation) produce temporally inconsistent and structurally implausible deformations. The SLAT representation from Trellis, despite its regular structure and strong generative prior, had not been explored for morphing, leaving an open question of how to leverage it for smooth, semantically coherent 3D transitions without training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source and target object (either real 3D assets or Trellis-synthesized) as image conditions, with initial noised latents obtained via 3D inversion for real assets or cached from original generation for synthetic ones. It produces a 50-frame morphing sequence (N=49, linearly spaced α from 0 to 1) where each frame is a full 3D asset decoded from SLAT into mesh, 3DGS, or NeRF. The pipeline operates on a 64³ voxel grid for the sparse structure stage and uses DINOv2 patch features as cross-attention conditions, requiring ~30 s and 24 GB VRAM per frame on a single A6000 GPU.

## Method

MorphAny3D is a training-free framework that modifies the attention mechanisms inside Trellis's two-stage rectified-flow pipeline (Sparse Structure + Structured Latent) to produce morphing frames. The key insight is that blending SLAT features within attention—rather than interpolating noise or conditions—yields structurally plausible deformations. Morphing Cross-Attention (MCA) computes separate attention outputs for source and target DINOv2 conditions and then linearly blends them (weighted by α), avoiding the semantic ambiguity of patch-wise K/V fusion that causes local artifacts. Temporal-Fused Self-Attention (TFSA) blends the current frame's self-attention output with the previous frame's (β=0.2) to enforce temporal coherence without sacrificing semantic fidelity. An orientation correction step generates four yaw-rotated candidates of the sparse structure and selects the one minimizing Chamfer Distance to the prior frame, suppressing the 90°/180°/270° yaw jumps caused by Trellis's learned pose prior.

## Results

On 50 diverse source–target pairs, MorphAny3D achieves the best FID (111.95), PDV (0.0006), Aesthetics Score (81%), and User Preference (86.73%), while attaining the second-best PPL (2.47, only 0.06 above MorphFlow's 2.41). The method reduces FID by 26–73% over all baselines and improves AS by 70+ percentage points over the next-best method (FreeMorph at 11%). Each frame is generated in ~30 s on a single A6000 GPU with 24 GB memory, using no retraining or hyperparameter tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphAny3D decisively outperforms all four baseline families. Against matching-based methods (3DInterp/SLATInterp via DenseMatcher), it reduces FID from 409/348 to 112, showing that correspondence-driven interpolation produces structurally implausible shapes despite reasonable PPL. Against 2D-first approaches (DiffMorph, FreeMorph), it cuts FID by ~46–47% and PPL by ~63–63%, confirming that lifting 2D morphs independently into 3D fails to maintain temporal coherence. Against MorphFlow (the strongest 3D morphing baseline), MorphAny3D improves FID from 285 to 112 (−61%) and PDV from 0.0009 to 0.0006, while matching its PPL within 0.06 (2.47 vs 2.41). The only metric where MorphAny3D does not lead is PPL, where MorphFlow edges ahead by a negligible margin; on every other metric the gap is substantial, particularly on Aesthetics Score (81% vs ≤11%) and User Preference (86.73% vs ≤5.51%).

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphAny3D builds directly on Trellis's Structured Latent (SLAT) representation and its two-stage rectified-flow pipeline (SS + SLAT), repurposing the generative prior for morphing without any retraining. It departs from the dominant two-stage matching-based paradigm (correspondence estimation followed by interpolation) and from 2D-morphing-then-lift-to-3D pipelines, instead operating entirely within the 3D generator's attention layers. The key conceptual departure from prior SLAT-based editing/stylization work is the analysis of *how* to fuse source and target features: the paper shows that naive KV blending in both cross- and self-attention simultaneously degrades plausibility, motivating the asymmetric design of MCA (separate attention then blend) for cross-attention and TFSA (temporal frame fusion) for self-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free 3D morphing method that handles cross-category transformations (e.g., bee → biplane) with semantically coherent intermediate shapes, addressing a long-standing gap where matching-based methods fail and 2D-lifting approaches lose temporal consistency. The generalization to other SLAT-based models (Hi3DGen, Text-to-3D Trellis) and native support for decoupled morphing and 3D style transfer broaden its practical utility for content creation pipelines. Key limitations include the 30 s/frame runtime on a single A6000, inheritance of Trellis's fidelity ceiling on fine structures, and the orientation correction's inability to handle yaw-symmetric objects—open questions include whether stronger 3D backbones or KV-caching acceleration can close these gaps.
