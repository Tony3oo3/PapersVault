## Problem

Inversion-free image editing methods avoid the layout distortion of diffusion inversion but suffer from content drift, semantic leakage, and weak spatial localization because they lack a faithful latent representation of the source image. Existing reward-guided frameworks address controllability only coarsely, using fixed reward schedules and no mechanism to harmonize heterogeneous objectives (global semantics, spatial grounding, object-level consistency) over the sampling trajectory, preventing precise, instruction-faithful edits in a truly zero-shot setting.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a natural-language text prompt and an optional RGB input image; for image-conditioned editing the image is encoded to a clean latent z₀, while for unconditional text-to-image generation sampling starts from Gaussian noise. During reverse-time flow-matching sampling, intermediate decoded images are evaluated by a suite of frozen differentiable reward models (SigLIP, SAM2, HPS v2, a VQA language model, RegionCLIP), and the fused gradient is mapped back into the latent space. The output is a single edited or generated image at the resolution supported by the underlying backbone (Flux, Qwen Image, or PixArt-α), with no model weight modification required.

## Method

RewardFlow treats each denoising step of a pretrained flow-matching model as a test-time optimization over a set of differentiable rewards, producing a reward-guided drift term that augments the native ODE dynamics via a Langevin SDE update. A prompt-aware adaptive policy parses the instruction into atomic semantic primitives with an LLM, classifies edit intent (add/remove/style), and dynamically modulates per-reward weights and step sizes at every sampling step through a softmax over intent priors, reward-decrease feedback, and a noise-level schedule. The reward toolkit spans global semantic alignment (SigLIP cosine), perceptual fidelity, region-level grounding (RegionCLIP attention pooling), object consistency (text-conditioned SAM2 soft masks with leakage penalty), human preference (HPS v2), and a novel differentiable VQA reward that enforces fine-grained attribute correctness via negated cross-entropy on a frozen language model. An identity-preserving KL tether pulls the predicted clean latent toward the original encoding to prevent drift and reward hacking. The authors provide a theoretical justification showing the discrete update is a valid Euler-Maruyama discretization of a Langevin SDE targeting a prompt-tilted posterior density.

## Results

On PIE-BENCH (standard 50-step setting), Flux+RewardFlow achieves the best Distance (7.78×10³), PSNR (31.21), SSIM (89.67×10²), Whole accuracy (29.44), and Edited accuracy (26.62) among all Flux-based baselines, while Qwen Image+RewardFlow further improves to Distance 7.64×10³, PSNR 32.09, and Edited accuracy 27.57. In the few-step (4-step) setting, Qwen Image+RewardFlow reduces Distance by up to 44.4% and LPIPS by 25.8% over the best prior fast editors (InstantEdit, TurboEdit). On T2I-COMPBENCH, RewardFlow consistently improves all three backbones (PixArt-α, Flux, Qwen Image) over both the base model and the ReNO reward baseline across all six compositional categories, with Qwen Image+RewardFlow reaching 0.91 color accuracy and 0.78 complex composition accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RewardFlow (Flux backbone) outperforms all 11 Flux-based editing baselines on every reported metric in the standard setting, with the closest competitor being KV-Edit (Distance 8.47 vs. 7.78; Edited accuracy 23.92 vs. 26.62) and ProxG (LPIPS 38.27 vs. 40.55, where ProxG is slightly better on LPIPS but far worse on PSNR and edit accuracy). In the few-step (4-step) regime, Qwen Image+RewardFlow dominates InstantEdit (Distance 10.33 vs. 17.14) and TurboEdit (10.33 vs. 18.57) by large margins, while also surpassing InfEdit (10.33 vs. 16.19). On T2I-COMPBENCH, RewardFlow beats the ReNO reward baseline on all six categories for every backbone; for example, on Flux, RewardFlow improves Complex composition from 0.49 (ReNO) to 0.57 and Non-Spatial from 0.35 to 0.42. The method also surpasses the strongest closed-model reference (DALL-E 3: Color 0.81, Shape 0.68, Texture 0.81) on color and texture with Qwen Image+RewardFlow (0.91, 0.90), though DALL-E 3's spatial and non-spatial scores are not reported. Notably, RewardFlow achieves these gains with fewer NFEs (43 for Flux) than inversion-based methods like P2P and DI (100 NFEs), and with only 20 sampling steps versus 50 for most baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

RewardFlow carves new ground at the intersection of inversion-free flow-matching editing (FlowEdit, FreeFine, Edicho) and reward-guided sampling (ReNO, ORIGEN, Dymo), but departs from both in key ways. Unlike prior reward-guided methods that use a single coarse reward or fixed scheduling, it introduces a hierarchical multi-reward toolkit (global, perceptual, region, object, VQA, human-preference) coordinated by a prompt-aware adaptive policy that parses semantic primitives and dynamically modulates weights and step sizes. The differentiable VQA reward and SAM2-based object consistency reward are novel formulations not present in prior reward-guided editing work, and the Langevin SDE discretization provides a theoretical grounding that earlier heuristic reward-guided samplers lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RewardFlow offers a practical, plug-and-play framework that improves edit fidelity and compositional alignment on top of existing flow-matching backbones (Flux, Qwen Image, PixArt-α) without any fine-tuning or inversion, making it broadly applicable to interactive editing pipelines. The prompt-aware adaptive policy and semantic-primitive decomposition address a real practical pain point—different edit intents (add, remove, style) require different reward priorities—and the VQA reward opens a path toward language-grounded fine-grained supervision in generative models. Open questions include whether the multi-reward overhead scales to video or higher-resolution generation, whether the adaptive policy generalizes to unseen edit types beyond the three coarse intent categories, and whether the KL tether can be replaced by lighter identity-preservation mechanisms for more aggressive edits.
