## Problem

Iterative stereo matching methods achieve state-of-the-art accuracy through recurrent refinement (ConvGRU), but their RNN layers impose severe memory bandwidth demands and complex control flow that make edge deployment impractical—a bottleneck largely overlooked in prior work, which has focused on cost volume efficiency. Existing real-time alternatives that remove the RNN entirely suffer from markedly inferior accuracy and cross-domain generalization, creating an unresolved trade-off between fidelity and deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263913/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stereo RGB image pair and outputs a dense disparity map. It is designed to operate across resolutions from 320×640 up to 1280×2944 (2K), with the recurrent update block operating on 4× downsampled feature maps. The full pipeline is benchmarked on NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX (FP16, 25W) and RTX 4090, targeting sub-100ms latency at 320×640 for real-time autonomous driving use.

## Method

Pip-Stereo introduces a two-stage training strategy built on Selective-IGEV. Stage 1 (Monocular Prior Transfer) uses a teacher-student framework where Depth-Anything-V2-L transfers depth knowledge to a lightweight RepViT student via MSE alignment on multi-resolution context features and cost volume embeddings, with the student architecture discovered through a genetic-algorithm supernet search over RepViT blocks. Stage 2 (Progressive Iteration Pruning) applies successive halving of RNN iterations (32→16→8→4→2→1), finetuning only the ConvGRU update block with a composite loss that enforces cumulative output alignment, final disparity matching, and hidden-state correspondence between the full and pruned networks. For architectures where single-iteration compression degrades accuracy (e.g., zero-disparity initialization), FlashGRU provides a complementary acceleration path: it exploits the observed spatial sparsity of disparity updates via a channel-spatial importance map, constructs a multi-resolution bidirectional index mapping table to pack sparse pixels contiguously in GPU buffers, and fuses sequential GRU convolutions into a temporally unified kernel, achieving up to 7.28× RNN speedup and 76.6% peak memory reduction at 2K resolution.

## Results

On SceneFlow, PipStereo (1 iteration) achieves 0.45 EPE, outperforming the second-best iterative method (Selective-IGEV, 0.44) by 13.5% relative margin and all real-time methods by a wide gap. On ETH3D, it records 0.19 Bad-1 (Noc) and 0.19 RMSE (Noc), reducing error by 34.4% over the next-best iterative baseline. On KITTI 2015, PipStereo achieves 1.33 D1-all (Noc), competitive with MonSter++ (1.29) while running 17× faster. Inference latency on NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX at 384×1344 is 0.44 s, compared to 7.63 s for MonSter, 5.05 s for DEFOM-Stereo, and 14.02 s for FoundaStereo-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PipStereo (1 iteration) matches or exceeds the accuracy of most iterative baselines while being 3–32× faster: it beats IGEV (0.49→0.45 EPE), Selective-IGEV (0.44→0.45, near-tie), and DEFOM-Stereo (0.42→0.45, slight loss) on SceneFlow, and substantially outperforms them on ETH3D Bad-1 Noc (0.19 vs 0.34, 0.29, 0.22 respectively). Against the strongest iterative methods MonSter and MonSter++ (0.37 EPE, 0.18–0.20 Bad-1 Noc), PipStereo trails by a small margin (0.45 EPE, 0.19 Bad-1 Noc) but runs 17–22× faster on edge hardware. Against all non-iterative real-time methods (BGNet+, AANet, LightStereo, CoEx, HITNet, IINet, FastACVNet+), PipStereo achieves 10–40% lower EPE on SceneFlow and 20–70% lower Bad-1 on ETH3D, while remaining within 0.44 s latency. On zero-shot DrivingStereo generalization (Table 2), PipStereo (avg D1-All 4.35) substantially outperforms all real-time methods (13.08–93.52) and is competitive with RT-MonSter++ (4.18), though MonSter++ (2.69) retains an edge. The paper also validates PIP on RAFT-Stereo and FoundaStereo, showing that PIP reduces the accuracy gap from 32→1 iterations by 15–50% compared to naive truncation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pip-Stereo builds directly on Selective-IGEV's iterative ConvGRU architecture and extends the broader family of iterative optimization-based stereo matchers (IGEV, RAFT-Stereo, MonSter, DEFOM-Stereo) by demonstrating that their recurrent refinement is largely redundant. It departs from prior real-time methods (LightStereo, CoEx, HITNet, AANet) that eliminate RNNs entirely and sacrifice generalization, instead preserving the iterative inductive bias while collapsing it into near-single-pass inference. The monocular depth prior transfer draws inspiration from MonSter and DEFOM-Stereo but replaces their dedicated depth foundation model with a re-parameterized student encoder, and FlashGRU introduces a hardware-aware sparse operator distinct from prior efficiency work focused on cost volume computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a deployment bottleneck (RNN memory bandwidth and control-flow complexity on edge hardware) that the stereo matching community has largely overlooked, providing a general recipe—progressive halving with cumulative-output distillation—that can be applied to any iterative method. The finding that >99% of update pixels are redundant after ~10–15 iterations challenges the implicit assumption that more iterations monotonically improve accuracy. Open questions include whether PIP generalizes to other recurrent tasks (optical flow, multi-view stereo), how it interacts with quantization-aware training, and whether the residual accuracy gap (0.45 vs. 0.38 EPE on SceneFlow) can be closed without reintroducing iterations. FlashGRU's gains are hardware-specific (Ada Lovelace, CUDA), and its benefit diminishes at single-iteration settings, limiting its standalone utility.
