## Problem

Existing face forgery detection methods either provide only a binary real/fake verdict or produce coarse mask-based localization that misses fine-grained manipulation traces. DSSIM-based approaches can capture pixel-level artifacts but rely on direct regression, which oversmooths subtle forgery cues into blurry, uninformative maps. The paper identifies that higher-quality localization maps directly improve detection accuracy, yet no prior method can synthesize such maps with sufficient fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single cropped facial image (RGB) as input and simultaneously produces a real/fake forgery score and a pixel-level grayscale DSSIM artifact localization map of the same spatial resolution as the input. The DSSIM map encodes structural dissimilarity at each pixel, with real images corresponding to pure-black maps. During training, GT DSSIM maps are computed by sliding a local window over aligned real-fake image pairs extracted from the same video, requiring spatially aligned pairs that are unavailable in DFDC/DFDCP.

## Method

DiffusionFF establishes an encoder-decoder architecture in which a pretrained ConvNeXt-B forgery detector acts as an 'artifact encoder' and a U-Net denoising diffusion model (DDPM, T=50 steps) is repurposed as an 'artifact decoder'. Multi-scale forgery features from the detector's four stages are aligned via conditioning projectors and injected into the corresponding U-Net encoder stages, guiding the diffusion process to progressively synthesize a detailed DSSIM map from random noise. The estimated DSSIM map is then encoded by an artifact feature extractor and fused with the detector's high-level semantic features through a gating mechanism (Score = H(σ(E(M_DSSIM)) ⊙ F_det + F_det)) to produce the final classification. Training is decoupled into two stages: first, the conditioning projectors and diffusion model are trained with MSE noise-prediction loss while the detector is frozen; second, the artifact feature extractor and classifier are trained with cross-entropy loss while all prior components are frozen. This two-stage strategy avoids the training collapse observed when jointly optimizing detection and generation from scratch.

## Results

On cross-dataset evaluation, DiffusionFF achieves the highest AUC across all four benchmarks: 97.24% on CDF2, 85.05% on DFDC, 92.56% on DFDCP, and 88.56% on FFIW, outperforming the best detection-only method (Effort*, 95.73/84.78/90.42/88.53) and the best joint method (KFD, 94.71/79.12/91.81/–). For DSSIM map estimation, DiffusionFF substantially surpasses prior DSSIM-based methods, achieving FID of 98.98 on CDF2 and 191.80 on FFIW versus 256.20/284.27 (LiSiam) and 258.67/248.78 (LRL), with PSNR gains of ~8–9 dB. On intra-dataset FF++ evaluation, DiffusionFF reaches 99.94% overall AUC, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffusionFF outperforms all 16 baselines on every cross-dataset benchmark where results are available. Against the strongest detection-only method, Effort* (ICML 2025), DiffusionFF gains +1.51 AUC on CDF2, +0.27 on DFDC, +2.14 on DFDCP, and +0.03 on FFIW. Among joint detection-and-localization methods, DiffusionFF surpasses the best mask-based method KFD (ICML 2025) by +2.53 AUC on CDF2 and +5.93 on DFDC, and exceeds the best prior DSSIM-based method LiSiam (TIFS 2022) by +6.88 on CDF2 and +11.99 on DFDC. On DSSIM map quality, DiffusionFF achieves FID of 98.98 on CDF2, roughly 2.6× lower than LiSiam (256.20) and 2.6× lower than LRL (258.67), indicating far sharper and more faithful artifact localization. On intra-dataset FF++, DiffusionFF (99.94% AUC) edges out AUNet (99.89%) and LRL (99.88%), confirming consistent superiority across both generalization and in-distribution settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DDPM denoising diffusion framework (Ho et al., NeurIPS 2020) as an 'artifact decoder' conditioned on multi-scale features from a pretrained ConvNeXt forgery detector acting as an 'artifact encoder'—a conceptual inversion of how diffusion models have been used in forgery detection, where prior works (DiffForensics, InpDiffusion, DiffusionFake) employed diffusion for binary mask generation or identity reconstruction rather than fine-grained DSSIM map synthesis. It departs from existing joint detection-and-localization methods (LRL, LiSiam) that rely on direct regression to estimate DSSIM maps, which the authors show produces overly smoothed and blurry outputs. The two-stage training strategy that decouples DSSIM estimation from classification is a practical departure from end-to-end joint optimization, motivated by observed training collapse when both objectives are optimized simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of DiffusionFF (consistently boosting EfficientNet, Swin-B, and ConvNeXt by 1–3 AUC points) makes it a broadly applicable module for existing forgery detection pipelines, and the high-fidelity DSSIM maps provide human-interpretable evidence that addresses the explainability gap in current deepfake detection. However, the approach requires aligned real-fake image pairs for GT DSSIM supervision (unavailable for DFDC/DFDCP), and the diffusion inference (50 denoising steps) introduces latency that may limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the DSSIM map is the optimal artifact representation versus learned feature-space discrepancies, and whether the framework extends naturally to video-level temporal forgery detection.
