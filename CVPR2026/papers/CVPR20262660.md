## Problem

Open-vocabulary HOI detection requires both fine-grained spatial features for localizing interactive human-object pairs and text-aligned semantic features for classifying unseen interaction categories. Existing approaches either combine a separate HOI detector with a VLM (suffering from cross-model representation gaps) or repurpose a single CLIP-based VLM (lacking the fine-grained visual detail needed for accurate localization). No prior method achieves both capabilities within a single, streamlined architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262660/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces human bounding boxes, object bounding boxes, and an open-vocabulary interaction label for each detected human-object pair. Interaction classification is performed by computing cosine similarity between projected interaction embeddings and pre-computed text embeddings for all categories (e.g., 406 actions × 1,000 objects on SWiG-HOI), with a learnable temperature parameter. The model uses 64 learnable queries per instance type and operates with all DINOv3 parameters frozen, training only a small detection adapter, instance decoder, and a single cross-attention refinement layer.

## Method

SL-HOI builds a one-stage HOI detector entirely on the frozen DINOv3 (dino.txt) model, exploiting the complementary attention patterns of its backbone (fine-grained, localized) and vision head (holistic, text-aligned). A lightweight detection decoder with separate human and object queries performs instance detection on backbone features, and their averaged embeddings form initial interaction queries. The key architectural contribution is a two-step interaction classification: (1) Semantic Bootstrapping feeds both the interaction queries and backbone image tokens jointly through the frozen vision head's self-attention blocks, aligning queries into the head's text-semantic space while simultaneously producing query-influenced image tokens; (2) Hierarchical Refinement applies a single learnable cross-attention layer where the bootstrapped queries re-attend to these contextualized tokens. This design bridges the representation gap between detection-space queries and text-aligned features without any additional pre-training, requiring only a small number of trainable parameters.

## Results

On SWiG-HOI (open-vocabulary), SL-HOI achieves 19.04% mAP on unseen, 24.69% on rare, 30.62% on non-rare, and 24.67% on full categories, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On HICO-DET (open-vocabulary), it reaches 40.53% (unseen), 42.99% (seen), and 42.49% (full) mAP. In the closed HICO-DET setting, SL-HOI attains 45.05% full mAP, surpassing the previous best (BC-HOI, 43.01%) by 2.04%. All DINOv3 parameters remain frozen; only a small detection decoder and a single cross-attention layer are trained.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SWiG-HOI, SL-HOI outperforms the strongest prior method without object-detection pre-training (SGC-Net [26]) by 6.58% on unseen, 8.14% on rare, 6.95% on non-rare, and 7.47% on full mAP. Among methods with object-detection pre-training, it surpasses MP-HOI-L [42] by 6.10% (rare) and 4.86% (non-rare). On HICO-DET open-vocabulary, SL-HOI leads all methods without pre-training by 17.26% (unseen) and 15.27% (full) over SGC-Net; against methods with pre-training, it trails only BC-HOI [12] on unseen (40.53 vs. 42.31) but exceeds it on seen (42.99 vs. 40.67) and full (42.49 vs. 40.99). In the closed HICO-DET setting, SL-HOI beats every baseline including BC-HOI [12] by +2.04% full, +1.95% rare, and +2.07% non-rare mAP, and outperforms VRDiff [1] by +3.65% full mAP. The paper notes that simply swapping in larger backbones (Swin-Large, CLIP-ViT-L/14) into prior architectures does not close the gap, attributing the gains to the streamlined DINOv3-based design rather than backbone scale alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms for open-vocabulary HOI detection: VLM-collaborated methods (which pair a conventional HOI detector with a VLM but suffer from cross-model representation gaps) and VLM-only methods (typically CLIP-based, which lack fine-grained spatial features for localization). Instead, it builds on DINOv3's self-supervised ViT backbone and the dino.txt text-aligned vision head, exploiting their complementary attention behaviors—local focus in the backbone for detection, holistic aggregation in the head for classification—within a single frozen model. The key architectural departure is the 'Semantic Bootstrapping' step, which routes interaction queries through the frozen vision head alongside image tokens to bridge representation gaps before a lightweight cross-attention refinement, rather than relying on a separate learnable fusion decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The streamlined design (all DINOv3 parameters frozen, only a small detection decoder and one cross-attention layer trainable) offers a practical recipe for adapting powerful self-supervised vision models to dense prediction tasks without expensive fine-tuning. The finding that simply swapping in stronger backbones (Swin-Large, CLIP-ViT-L/14) does not replicate the gains underscores that architectural alignment with the backbone's inductive biases matters more than raw backbone capacity. Limitations include the higher computational cost of a ViT backbone versus CNN-based detectors, and the approach is validated only on two HOI benchmarks; whether the complementary backbone/head decomposition generalizes to other open-vocabulary dense prediction tasks (e.g., scene graph generation, referring segmentation) remains an open question.
