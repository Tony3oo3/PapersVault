## Problem

Existing prompt-enhanced zero-shot image recognition methods rely on heuristic prompt designs without theoretical grounding, exhibit limited versatility for fine-grained classification, and are vulnerable to outlier prompts that produce skewed similarity distributions. No prior work provides a principled framework that simultaneously addresses how to construct effective prompts and how to robustly aggregate their predictions at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261763/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method takes a single RGB image and a set of K class labels, producing a predicted class label via one forward pass through CLIP's visual encoder. The concept-augmented prompts (of the form 'A photo of a {class} with {concept}') are pre-generated offline using GPT-4.1 and encoded once, so no test-time optimization or multi-view augmentation is required. Each class is represented by 16 or 50 compositional prompts (e.g., 'T-shaped flattened head or smooth gray body'), and the final prediction is a weighted marginalization over these prompts.

## Method

CGBC reformulates zero-shot classification as Bayesian marginalization over a latent concept space, where each concept is weighted by a prior and an image-conditioned likelihood. To construct the concept proposal distribution, a four-stage LLM-driven pipeline first builds class-aware hard-negative neighborhoods from CLIP text embeddings, then generates discriminative atomic concepts via contrastive prompting, composes them into higher-order concepts using logical disjunction, and selects a diverse subset via Determinantal Point Process. To handle outlier concepts, an Adaptive Soft-Trim Likelihood estimates the contamination rate from the similarity distribution's MAD and assigns sigmoid-based soft weights that down-weight concepts deviating significantly from the median, all in a single forward pass. The framework is supported by robust estimation guarantees under the Huber contamination model and multi-class excess risk bounds.

## Results

On eleven zero-shot image classification benchmarks, CGBC achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 69.3% (50 prompts) and 69.0% (16 prompts), outperforming the strongest prompt-based baseline CuPL (67.9% at 50 prompts) by 1.4% and the best test-time augmentation method DiffTPT (65.9%) by 3.4%. The concept prior alone (CGBC Prior, 68.6%) already surpasses all baselines, while the adaptive soft-trim likelihood adds a further 0.7% gain. Across VLM variants (Table 2), CGBC yields an average improvement of ~5% over CLIP with ViT-based encoders and ~3% with ResNet-based encoders.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGBC (69.3% avg) outperforms all eight baselines across the eleven datasets. The closest competitor is CuPL (67.9% at 50 prompts), which CGBC surpasses by 1.4% on average; notably, CGBC Prior (68.6%) already exceeds CuPL by 0.7%, indicating the concept synthesis pipeline itself is the primary driver. Among test-time augmentation methods, DiffTPT (65.9%) is the strongest, trailed by TPT (65.1%), MTA (64.9%), and C-TPT (64.9%); CGBC leads these by 3.4–4.4%. The simplest baselines, CLIP (63.5%) and CLIP+E (64.4%), are outperformed by 4.9–5.8%, while COLA (62.6%) is the weakest baseline at 6.7% below CGBC. CGBC wins on the majority of individual datasets (e.g., EuroSAT 60.3% vs. CuPL 55.3%, DTD 55.7% vs. CuPL 52.3%, ImageNet 69.4% vs. DiffTPT 70.3% where DiffTPT narrowly leads), with the only per-dataset losses being Aircraft (26.0% vs. CuPL 27.9%) and a marginal gap on ImageNet vs. DiffTPT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions LLM-augmented prompt methods for zero-shot VLM classification (e.g., CuPL, CHiLS) within a Bayesian marginalization framework, treating class-specific concepts as latent variables rather than fixed heuristic templates. It draws on classical concept discovery principles (discriminability, compositionality, diversity) from inductive logic programming and cognitive science to structure the concept proposal distribution, and repurposes Determinantal Point Processes from submodular optimization to enforce semantic diversity among selected concepts. The robust likelihood component adapts the Huber contamination model and (σ, α)-goodness conditions from robust statistics literature to the setting of prompt-similarity score aggregation, replacing the naive averaging used by prior prompt-ensembling methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Bayesian reframing provides the first principled justification for why diverse, discriminative concepts outperform simple prompt ensembling, and the derived multi-class excess risk bounds give practitioners a theoretical handle on how many concepts are needed for reliable classification. The training-free, single-forward-pass design makes the method practical for deployment without test-time optimization. Open questions include whether the concept synthesis pipeline generalizes beyond image classification (e.g., video, 3D), whether the theoretical bounds are tight enough to be practically informative, and how the method behaves when the LLM generating concepts has limited world knowledge about fine-grained or domain-specific classes.
