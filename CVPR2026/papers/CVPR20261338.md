## Problem

Existing video inpainting methods remove only the object region while retaining associated visual effects such as shadows, reflections, mirrors, and translucency, producing visibly incomplete results. Training-based approaches that address associated effects (e.g., ROSE) require large synthetic datasets and expensive fine-tuning, while the closest training-free method (Omnimatte-Zero) depends on an external point-tracking model that degrades under fast motion and uses a suboptimal mask-expansion strategy. Additionally, standard evaluation metrics like PSNR and video quality scores cannot reliably distinguish full object removal from partial or no removal.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video sequence of k frames, a per-frame binary object mask (generated by SAM2), and a pair of text prompts (source P_s and target P_T) that describe the object and its associated effects (e.g., 'duck' and 'reflection'). The video is encoded into latent space by a pre-trained text-to-video DiT (HunyuanVideo); evaluation videos are 2 seconds at 24 FPS with resolutions of 480×848 or 720×400. The output is a decoded video in which both the object and its associated effects are replaced with semantically consistent, temporally coherent background content.

## Method

Object-WIPER operates entirely in the latent space of a pre-trained MMDiT video diffusion model without any retraining. First, it localizes associated effects by extracting visual tokens highly correlated with query text tokens via text-to-visual cross-attention (T→I), then refining the proposal mask by computing each token's attention ratio to the proposal set within the visual self-attention map (I→I), yielding a dense associated-effect mask M^AE. Second, during RF-Solver inversion, it computes timestep-adaptive object masks (since the object's attention footprint expands as noise increases) and applies attention scaling (c<1) to suppress background tokens' attention toward the object. Third, during denoising, it reinitializes the masked region with fresh Gaussian noise to erase all object prior, applies reverse attention scaling (b>1) in early timesteps to bias foreground tokens toward background tokens for structure formation, and copies back stored background value features in the unmasked region to preserve scene fidelity.

## Results

On DAVIS, Object-WIPER achieves a TokSim of 32.80, surpassing the best training-based method (Gen-Prop, 30.52) and the best training-free method (Attentive-Eraser, 30.82). On WIPER-Bench, Object-WIPER scores 33.09 TokSim, outperforming ROSE (30.02) and Attentive-Eraser (25.28). Object-WIPER also leads on Text-alignment (26.63 on DAVIS, 26.91 on WIPER-Bench) and achieves the lowest FG-Flicker on WIPER-Bench (3.02), indicating strong temporal coherence. VAE reconstructions (which do not remove the object) score near-zero TokSim (0.32–1.25), confirming the metric's sensitivity to actual removal quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Object-WIPER outperforms all eight baselines on the proposed TokSim metric on both DAVIS (32.80 vs. best baseline 30.82 for Attentive-Eraser) and WIPER-Bench (33.09 vs. 30.02 for ROSE), despite being entirely training-free. Among training-based methods, ROSE (29.36/30.02 TokSim) and Gen-Prop (30.52 on DAVIS only) are the strongest, but both lag by 2–3 TokSim points; VACE is notably weaker (15.86/11.53). Among training-free methods, Attentive-Eraser (30.82/25.28) and KV-Edit-Video (28.68/23.26) are the closest competitors, with Object-WIPER leading by 2.0 and 7.8 TokSim on DAVIS and WIPER-Bench respectively. On BG-PSNR, Propainter (34.01/41.07) and VAE baselines score higher, reflecting that Object-WIPER trades some pixel-level background fidelity for more complete object and effect removal. On FG-Flicker (lower is better), Object-WIPER achieves 3.02 on WIPER-Bench, competitive with ROSE (2.94) and Propainter (4.00), while on DAVIS Propainter (13.73) and Gen-Prop (13.21) edge it out slightly (16.37). The VAE reconstruction baselines confirm the metric's validity: they score near-zero TokSim (0.32–1.25) despite high BG-PSNR and Quality scores, demonstrating that traditional metrics cannot distinguish actual removal from identity mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

Object-WIPER builds on the inversion-denoising framework of RF-Solver for video latent inversion and the reinitialization-plus-background-copying strategy from KV-Edit, extending both from image editing to video object removal. It departs from the closest prior work, Omnimatte-Zero, by eliminating the dependency on an external point-tracking model (TAP-Net) and replacing mask-expansion-based associated-effect localization with a two-step cross-attention (text-to-visual) and self-attention (visual-to-visual) pipeline within MMDiT blocks. The paper also introduces timestep-adaptive masking and bidirectional attention scaling as mechanisms to prevent object leakage during denoising, which neither KV-Edit nor Omnimatte-Zero employ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free paradigm removes the need for large synthetic datasets and expensive fine-tuning that training-based methods like ROSE and GenProp require, making associated-effect removal accessible with off-the-shelf video DiTs. The WIPER-Bench benchmark (60 real-world videos covering mirrors, translucency, and disconnected associations) and the TokSim metric address genuine gaps in evaluation that the paper demonstrates are critical—traditional metrics like BG-PSNR cannot distinguish a VAE round-trip from actual removal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos, whether the text prompts describing effects can be automated, and whether the method transfers to non-MMDiT architectures.
