## Problem

Unsupervised camouflaged object detection (UCOD) faces a fundamental tension: pseudo-label-guided methods produce boundary overflow because they ignore native image cues, while feature-learning methods yield blurred details due to the absence of explicit pseudo-label supervision. No existing framework simultaneously refines pseudo-label reliability and preserves fine-grained texture fidelity in a unified manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes unlabeled RGB images as input and produces binary pixel-level segmentation masks of camouflaged objects. During training, no ground-truth annotations are used; the model is trained on 4,040 images (CAMO-Train + COD10K-Train) and evaluated on four benchmarks (CHAMELEON, CAMO, COD10K, NC4K). The DINO-ViT-S/8 backbone processes images at its native resolution, while auxiliary cues (LBP, DoG, frozen ResNet-18 features) are extracted at the pixel level to provide native perceptual signals.

## Method

EReCu is a DINO-based teacher-student framework with three synergistic modules. Multi-Cue Native Perception (MNP) concatenates low-level texture descriptors (LBP, DoG) with mid-level semantics from a frozen ResNet-18 to form a native feature vector, then computes a fore-background separability metric via region-partitioned cosine similarity to regularize all downstream pseudo-label updates. Pseudo-Label Evolution Fusion (PEF) combines two sub-modules: EPL applies depthwise-separable convolution to shallow student features and iteratively refines pseudo-labels through Dice loss against both student and teacher predictions plus the MNP native-cue loss, while STAF stacks three hierarchical student attention maps into a 3rd-order tensor, applies Tucker decomposition followed by truncated SVD on the core tensor to retain dominant spectral components, and projects the result to a fused mask. Local Pseudo-Label Refinement (LPR) selects target-aware attention heads from the teacher's multi-head self-attention using a dual criterion (low attention entropy and high MNP consistency score), then generates local pseudo-labels from high-confidence regions of selected heads to refine boundary details via a combined Dice + Cross-Entropy loss on the STAF-fused prediction.

## Results

EReCu achieves state-of-the-art performance on most metrics across all four COD benchmarks. On CHAMELEON, it reaches S_m=0.7321, F_ω^β=0.6187, E_m^φ=0.8523, and M=0.0716; on COD10K, S_m=0.7221, F_ω^β=0.5628, E_m^φ=0.8185, M=0.0613. It outperforms the strongest UOS baseline (FOUND) by 1.6–4.2 percentage points in S_m across datasets and surpasses the closest UCOD competitor (UCOD-DPL) on 11 of 16 evaluated metric-dataset pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest UCOD competitor UCOD-DPL [36], EReCu wins on 11 of 16 metric-dataset pairs, with the largest gains on COD10K S_m (+1.31) and F_ω^β (+1.47), while narrowly losing on CAMO F_ω^β (0.6083 vs 0.6109), COD10K M (0.0613 vs 0.0601), and NC4K F_ω^β (0.6642 vs 0.6674). EReCu decisively outperforms SdalsNet [24] on every metric across all four datasets (e.g., +0.91 S_m on CHAMELEON, +1.31 S_m on COD10K) and EASE [2] by even wider margins (e.g., +5.61 S_m on CHAMELEON, +4.91 S_m on COD10K). Compared to the strongest UOS baseline FOUND [26], EReCu leads on all 16 metrics, with the largest advantage on COD10K E_m^φ (+17.10) and CAMO E_m^φ (+5.38), confirming that general-purpose unsupervised segmentation methods struggle with the low-contrast nature of camouflage. Against the DINO [1] backbone baseline, EReCu improves S_m by 3.5–6.2 points and M by 30–45% across datasets, validating the contribution of the full MNP+PEF+LPR pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

EReCu builds directly on the DINO-based teacher-student paradigm established for unsupervised object discovery, extending it to the camouflaged object detection setting. It departs from prior UCOD work by unifying two previously separate paradigms—pseudo-label-guided refinement (UCOS-DA, UCOD-DPL) and feature-learning-based disentanglement (SdalsNet, EASE)—into a single co-evolution loop where native low-level texture cues (LBP, DoG) and mid-level semantics (frozen ResNet-18) continuously regularize pseudo-label updates. The spectral tensor attention fusion (Tucker decomposition + truncated SVD) and attention-entropy-based head selection are novel technical mechanisms not seen in prior UCOD or UOS literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine tension in UCOD: pseudo-label methods produce sharp but noisy boundaries while feature-learning methods yield clean but blurred masks, and EReCu's co-evolution design offers a principled way to reconcile both. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (UCOD-DPL) are modest (e.g., +0.003 Sm on CHAMELEON, +0.013 on COD10K), and the method introduces considerable architectural complexity (four interacting modules, multiple learnable thresholds, Tucker/SVD rank selection) that may limit practical adoption. Open questions include whether the native-cue regularization generalizes beyond the DINO-ViT-S/8 backbone, whether the spectral fusion component is truly necessary versus simpler weighted averaging, and how the approach scales to higher-resolution or video-based camouflage scenarios.
