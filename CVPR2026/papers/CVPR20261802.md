## Problem

Single-image 3D human reconstruction via 2D multi-view diffusion suffers from 3D inconsistencies in the generated auxiliary views, leading to geometric distortions and blurred textures during reconstruction. Existing approaches are further limited by sparse view synthesis, reliance on inaccurate monocular SMPL estimation, or low-resolution 3D generative models that fail to generalize across diverse character styles and poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB image of a person as input, optionally reposed to a canonical A-pose via a pre-trained human repose model. It produces a production-ready textured 3D mesh (extracted from the front-view deformed mesh after UV unwrapping with Xatlas) that supports direct animation and 3D editing. Intermediate outputs include a sequence of 25 cross-modally aligned RGB and normal frames at 480p resolution, generated by the video diffusion model.

## Method

FISHuman employs a 3D-aware dual-stream video DiT (finetuned from Wan2.1 with LoRA) that jointly generates multi-view RGB and normal sequences, using cross-modal attention modules inserted every fourth shared transformer block to enforce structural alignment between the two modalities. A progressive two-stage training scheme first builds unimodal 3D-consistent representations, then introduces cross-modal alignment with 30% random dropout to prevent over-fusion. The key reconstruction contribution is a 4D remeshing module that decouples a globally shared canonical mesh (optimized via continuous explicit remeshing under normal-map supervision) from view-dependent per-vertex deformations (learned by an MLP conditioned on canonical vertex coordinates and viewpoint embeddings). The topological consistency of the deformed meshes enables a unified UV texture optimization that integrates appearance across all views, supervised by pixel-level RGB loss with view-specific weighting and total variation regularization.

## Results

On the 2K2K dataset, FISHuman achieves the best scores across all six metrics: CD 0.817 cm, P2S 0.778 cm, NC 0.858, PSNR 24.49 dB, SSIM 0.917, and LPIPS 0.086. On the Sizer dataset, it similarly leads with CD 1.243 cm, P2S 1.161 cm, NC 0.768, PSNR 20.38 dB, SSIM 0.926, and LPIPS 0.089. The closest competitor, Human3Diffusion, trails by 0.235 cm in CD and 1.14 dB in PSNR on 2K2K, while PSHuman is the nearest on Sizer geometry (CD 1.570 vs. 1.243 cm). Qualitative comparisons on in-the-wild SHHQ images further confirm superior pose plausibility, geometric sharpness, and texture realism over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FISHuman outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every metric across both 2K2K and Sizer. Against the closest mesh-based competitor PSHuman [29], it improves CD by 0.316 cm (2K2K) and 0.327 cm (Sizer), and LPIPS by 0.019 and 0.018 respectively, indicating noticeably sharper geometry and cleaner textures. Human3Diffusion [71], a 3DGS-based method, is the strongest baseline on 2K2K (CD 1.052, PSNR 23.35) but still trails FISHuman by 22% in CD and 1.14 dB in PSNR; on Sizer the gap widens (CD 1.331 vs. 1.243). SIFU [86] and SiTH [12] are substantially weaker, with CD values 1.7–2.4× higher and LPIPS 0.03–0.06 worse, reflecting their reliance on sparse multi-view diffusion without explicit 3D constraints. For canonical-pose reconstruction, qualitative comparisons show that StdGen [11] and Hunyuan3D 2.0 [87] produce smooth meshes but fail to preserve the reference person's identity, whereas FISHuman maintains both identity fidelity and fine geometric detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view diffusion + 3D reconstruction paradigm (SIFU, SiTH, PSHuman) by replacing sparse static view synthesis with a 3D-aware dual-stream video diffusion transformer (adapted from Wan2.1) that jointly generates dense RGB and normal sequences with cross-modal alignment. It departs from prior work's reliance on SMPL-X parametric priors for pose guidance, instead leveraging the video model's learned 3D awareness. The key methodological departure is the 4D remeshing module, which builds on continuous remeshing [Palfinger 2022] but introduces a view-dependent deformation field (MLP) to decouple canonical geometry from per-view transient details, addressing the 3D inconsistencies that plague naive reconstruction from diffusion-generated frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FISHuman produces production-ready textured meshes (exportable to standard rendering engines) from a single in-the-wild image, filling a gap left by 3DGS-based methods that cannot support explicit asset export. The 4D remeshing formulation—treating multi-view inconsistency as a vertex-deformation problem rather than averaging conflicting signals—offers a generalizable strategy for any pipeline where generated guidance is imperfectly 3D-consistent. Limitations include per-case optimization (not feedforward), reliance on 2D pixel-level supervision for the video model (no explicit 3D loss during generation), and evaluation restricted to human subjects; whether the decoupled canonical+deformation approach transfers to other object categories or scales to longer view sequences remains open.
