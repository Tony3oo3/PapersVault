## Problem

Implicit Neural Representations suffer from spectral bias, and while Fourier-based input encodings partially mitigate this, they rely on fixed, stochastic frequency bases that force the MLP to implicitly synthesize target frequencies through nonlinear transformations. This implicit composition is theoretically feasible but practically inefficient and difficult to optimize, as evidenced by the marginal gains from increasing network depth. The paper addresses this by shifting the burden of frequency synthesis from the MLP to the encoding stage itself.

## Input / Output

The method accepts low-dimensional coordinate vectors (2D pixel coordinates for image fitting, 3D point coordinates for signed distance field regression, or 5D spatial-plus-direction vectors for NeRF) and outputs continuous signal values (RGB, SDF, or RGB+density). The encoding stage first maps coordinates into a concatenated Fourier-Chebyshev feature space, then applies N parallel linear projections whose outputs are fused element-wise via Hadamard product before being fed to a shallow backbone MLP (typically 2 layers). The encoding operates on individual coordinate queries, so no spatial grid or batch-level structure is required.

## Method

CAFE feeds Fourier features through N parallel linear layers and combines their outputs via a Hadamard product, which—through trigonometric product-to-sum identities—explicitly generates sum and difference frequency components from the base set. This expands the representable frequency space from M fixed bases to O(M^N · 3^(N-1)) combinatorial frequencies, while the learned linear weights adaptively select task-relevant frequencies and suppress irrelevant ones. CAFE+ augments the Fourier features with Chebyshev polynomial features (up to order J) that provide stable, near-optimal low-frequency approximation; since Chebyshev polynomials also satisfy a product-to-sum identity, the same Hadamard-product mechanism extends naturally to synthesize higher-order Chebyshev bases. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–3) formalizes the admissible frequency sets for both Fourier and Chebyshev encodings, and the NTK analysis confirms that CAFE produces a better-conditioned kernel than standard RFF. The backbone MLP remains shallow (2 layers), since most frequency composition is externalized to the encoding stage.

## Results

On the 2D image fitting task (DIV2K), CAFE+ achieves an average PSNR of ~40.5 dB across 7 images, outperforming the strongest baseline SL2A (~40.0 dB) and FINER (~39.5 dB). The larger variant Ours(L) reaches ~42.9 dB average PSNR with only 0.33M parameters and 149.8 s training time, beating all baselines by 2–5 dB. On 3D shape reconstruction, CAFE+ attains IoU of 0.9992–0.9996 across five shapes, surpassing SL2A (0.9987–0.9994) and FINER (0.9893–0.9958). On the NeRF task (Blender), CAFE+ achieves the best PSNR on 3 of 4 scenes (e.g., 23.12 dB on Ship vs. 22.02 for FINER) while training fastest at 363.2 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAFE+ consistently outperforms all six baselines across the three evaluated tasks. On 2D image fitting, it beats the closest competitor SL2A by ~0.5 dB on average and FINER by ~1.0 dB, while also training ~2.5× faster than SCONE and WIRE. On 3D shape reconstruction, CAFE+ achieves IoU >0.999 on all five shapes, edging out SL2A (the next best) by 0.0003–0.0005 and substantially surpassing FINER (by up to 0.0046 on the Thai statue). On NeRF, CAFE+ wins on Ship (+1.10 dB over FINER), Lego (+1.08 dB), and Hotdog (+0.85 dB), while slightly trailing FINER on Drums by 0.07 dB — a near-tie. Notably, CAFE+ uses fewer parameters than SL2A (0.22M vs. 0.46M) and trains faster than all baselines except SIREN, demonstrating that the encoding-stage frequency synthesis reduces the need for deeper or wider MLPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Fourier-features line of work (RFF, PE) for implicit neural representations by replacing fixed, stochastic frequency bases with a dynamic encoding that learns to synthesize frequencies via parallel linear layers combined through Hadamard products. It departs from serial multiplicative approaches like MFN and BACON by adopting a parallel architecture that avoids their carefully engineered initialization, and from spatially-adaptive methods like SAPE and SCONE that remain constrained by fixed bases. The addition of Chebyshev polynomial features as a low-frequency complement to Fourier bases is a novel pairing, and the theoretical analysis (Theorem 2) formalizing the O(M^N · 3^(N−1)) frequency expansion via product-to-sum identities provides a clean justification for the design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By externalizing frequency synthesis from the MLP to the encoding stage, CAFE+ offers a practical and theoretically grounded remedy for spectral bias that is more parameter-efficient than simply deepening or widening the network. The ablation showing that a two-layer MLP suffices once the encoding is in place validates the core design principle and suggests the approach could be dropped into existing INR pipelines with minimal architectural changes. Open questions include whether the parallel Hadamard-product scaling remains tractable for very high-dimensional inputs (e.g., 5D NeRF queries at large N), how the method interacts with learnable activation functions (SIREN, FINER), and whether the Chebyshev complement generalizes beyond 1D coordinate axes to higher-dimensional polynomial bases.
