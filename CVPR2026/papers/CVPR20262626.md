## Problem

Existing PEFT-based deepfake detectors update only a small subset of foundation model parameters, but it remains unclear whether they truly exploit the most informative parameters while eliminating redundant ones. The paper identifies two specific failures: latent features across layers capture asymmetric semantic abstractions for real vs. fake samples (real samples converge rapidly while fakes require deeper processing), and tokens within the same layer convey varied forgery cues diluted by spurious tokens, causing the model to attend to irrelevant regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes face images (224×224 or 256×256) sampled from videos (8 frames for training, 32 for inference) and splits each image into multiple local regions alongside the original. A learnable query token aggregates the split-image representations via cross-attention, which is then concatenated with the global [CLS] token for binary real/fake classification. The model is applied to CLIP ViT-L/14, BEITv2, and SigLIP backbones, and is evaluated at both frame-level and video-level (mean of per-frame probabilities) AUC.

## Method

DFD-HR introduces a hierarchical routing strategy operating at both layer and token levels within a frozen VFM backbone. At the layer level, Early Layer Pruning uses a Gumbel-Sigmoid hard router on the [CLS] token to adaptively truncate forward depth per instance—real samples typically terminate early while fake samples propagate deeper and differ across forgery methods. At the token level, a learnable token judge scores all tokens and selects the top-k (75%) most forgery-relevant ones, with selection guided by a Spearman rank loss that enforces monotonic correlation between token scores and cosine similarity to the global face representation. A Mixture-of-Adapter (MOA) module with stochastic gate noise is inserted after MHA and MLP to encourage expert diversity and decouple forgery learning from semantic representation. Multi-Scale Fusion combines the original and split-image branches via a learnable query, and the total loss is cross-entropy plus a weighted Spearman rank regularization term.

## Results

On the cross-dataset protocol (Protocol-1), DFD-HR achieves 94.0% average Video-level AUC across seven test datasets, surpassing the strongest baseline Effort (91.7%) by +2.3%. On the cross-method protocol (Protocol-2) using DF40, DFD-HR reaches 97.8% average Video-level AUC, a +3.8% gain over Effort (94.0%). At the frame level, DFD-HR attains 89.1% average AUC on four datasets, outperforming ForAda (87.0%) by +2.1%. Notably, DFD-HR is the first hierarchical routing model, reducing the number of active layers by up to 17% and tokens by 25% while improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFD-HR consistently outperforms all 26 baselines across both evaluation protocols. Against the strongest PEFT-based baseline Effort [83], DFD-HR gains +2.3% average Video-level AUC on cross-dataset evaluation (94.0% vs. 91.7%) and +3.8% on cross-method evaluation (97.8% vs. 94.0%), with the largest per-dataset margin on DFo (+2.0%) and WDF (+5.9%). On frame-level AUC (Table 2), DFD-HR (89.1% avg) surpasses ForAda [11] (87.0%) by +2.1% and UDD [17] by a similar margin, while also beating Adapter-based methods MoE-FFD [34] and Dual-Adapter [64]. Among frequency-based and reconstruction-based methods (F3Net, SPSL, SRM, SBI, RECCE), DFD-HR leads by 10–20+ AUC points, confirming that foundation-model-based PEFT with hierarchical routing substantially outperforms handcrafted artifact detectors. DFD-HR also demonstrates backbone compatibility, transferring to BEITv2 (+4.7% over Effort) and SigLIP (+3.3% over Effort) while maintaining its advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PEFT-based deepfake detection line (Effort, ForAda, MoE-FFD) by arguing that simply updating fewer parameters does not guarantee exploitation of the most informative ones. It departs from standard adapter/LoRA fine-tuning by introducing a joint layer-level and token-level routing mechanism: Early Layer Pruning (adaptive depth via Gumbel-Sigmoid) and Token Selection (Spearman rank-constrained filtering), combined with a Mixture-of-Adapter expert routing. The multi-scale fusion component draws on D3's observation that split and original images carry complementary forgery signals, while the token selection is positioned against prior approaches like FastV, PCA, and SparseViT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical routing framework offers a principled way to allocate compute asymmetrically across instances (real vs. fake) and spatial regions (face vs. background), reducing layers by up to 17% and tokens by 25% while improving generalization. The observation that real samples converge in shallow layers while forged samples require deeper processing provides a mechanistic explanation for why uniform-depth PEFT underperforms. Open questions include whether the routing decisions remain stable under distribution shift to entirely new forgery families (e.g., diffusion-based), whether the Spearman rank constraint generalizes to non-face forgery tasks, and whether the expert specialization (semantic vs. forgery) observed here holds at larger model scales.
