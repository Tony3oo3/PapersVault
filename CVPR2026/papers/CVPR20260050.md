## Problem

Thermal-only novel view synthesis remains significantly harder than RGB NVS because affordable microbolometer sensors produce images with extremely low dynamic range, rapid frame-to-frame photometric fluctuations, and slow radiometric drift, all of which destabilize correspondence estimation and produce floater artifacts. Most prior thermal NVS work relies on paired RGB-thermal inputs to recover structure, but this dependence on visible-light imagery defeats the purpose of thermal sensing in the very low-visibility conditions where it is most valuable.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of single-channel thermal frames with known camera poses (recovered via COLMAP sparse reconstruction) and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel thermal views. Training operates at 1080p resolution with 30k iterations on an RTX A6000, while quantitative evaluation crops and resizes outputs to the original sensor resolution for pixel-wise alignment. The preprocessing stage is analytically invertible via a lookup table, preserving a recoverable mapping to the original radiometric scale.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a lightweight preprocessing module applies a two-step monotonic transformation: (1) histogram alignment mapping each frame's CDF to an exponentially-averaged reference CDF to suppress radiometric drift, followed by (2) brightness-preserving bi-histogram equalization (BBHE) that splits the histogram at its mean and equalizes lower/upper subranges independently to expand effective dynamic range. Second, an adapted 3D Gaussian Splatting model replaces RGB spherical-harmonic colors with a single scalar emission per Gaussian, conditioned on per-Gaussian and per-frame learnable embeddings passed through a 3-layer MLP (width 128, ReLU) to absorb residual frame-dependent artifacts. A background MLP handles distant regions, and training minimizes a weighted sum of L1 loss, heat-aware SSIM, and a background opacity regularizer. The key insight is that photometric stabilization in preprocessing combined with an embedding-conditioned emission model provides the flexibility to represent both bright and dark observations within a consistent radiometric space, preventing the floaters that plague attenuation-only models like Thermal3D-GS.

## Results

The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art thermal-only NVS across all six multiview datasets, with mean PSNR ranging from 23.59 dB (MSX) to 32.94 dB (TI-NSD) and SSIM from 0.71 to 0.94. It outperforms the closest competitor, Thermal3D-GS, by 1.31 dB PSNR on MSX, 0.62 dB on ThermalMix, 2.34 dB on MVTV, 2.81 dB on Lin et al., 1.56 dB on Ye et al., and 1.11 dB on TI-NSD. Training time is approximately 11 minutes per scene on an RTX A6000, slightly longer than 3DGS (5 min) and Thermal3D-GS (9 min) but far faster than NeRF (30+ hours).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on PSNR across all six datasets and on SSIM on five of six (tied with Thermal3D-GS at 0.94 on TI-NSD). Thermal3D-GS is the strongest baseline, achieving second-best PSNR on every dataset but trailing by 1.1–2.8 dB; it also fails to converge on the MVTV Tree scene and produces floater artifacts from photometrically inconsistent frames. Standard 3DGS is inconsistent, suffering notable PSNR drops on ThermalMix and MVTV and failing to converge on MSX Iron Ingot/Landscape and MVTV Tree. NeRF and ThermalMix-TS (Instant-NGP) are the weakest volumetric baselines, with NeRF consistently ranking last in PSNR (17.31–26.99 dB) due to its reliance on high-frequency RGB cues absent in thermal data. The two cross-modal methods (Lin et al. and ThermoNeRF), evaluated with RGB branches disabled, drop sharply relative to their original RGB+thermal results, confirming that their geometric recovery depends on visible-light supervision. Overall, the proposed pipeline's advantage is most pronounced on the hardest datasets (MSX, MVTV, Lin et al.), where radiometric drift and low texture most severely destabilize other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-the-wild 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (per-frame and per-Gaussian appearance embeddings from SWAG, WildGaussians, Splatfacto-W, and related work) to the thermal domain, replacing spherical harmonics with a scalar emission model conditioned on an MLP. It departs from prior thermal-only NVS methods (Thermal-NeRF, Thermal3D-GS) that rely on physics-inspired constraints or temperature-consistency regularizers, instead attributing reconstruction failures to radiometric drift and low dynamic range and addressing them through a lightweight invertible preprocessing stage (exponentially-averaged CDF alignment + BBHE). The paper also contributes a systematic empirical characterization of six public thermal datasets, positioning thermal-specific degradations (photometric fluctuation, microbolometer smoothing, vignetting, fixed-pattern noise) as the root cause of NVS instability rather than treating them as sensor noise to be filtered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Thermal-only NVS is practically important for autonomous driving, search-and-rescue, and infrastructure inspection in conditions where RGB cameras fail, yet prior methods either required paired RGB supervision or introduced complex physics models that still produced floaters on photometrically inconsistent frames. The finding that a simple, invertible histogram-based preprocessing combined with standard in-the-wild embeddings outperforms purpose-built physics-inspired thermal splatting (Thermal3D-GS) suggests that radiometric stabilization is more critical than domain-specific rendering models. Key open questions remain: the preprocessing is offline and not jointly optimized with geometry, and pose estimation from thermal frames still underperforms with standard tools like COLMAP, limiting the pipeline's end-to-end autonomy.
