## Problem

Existing autonomous driving benchmarks focus on low-level perception (detection, segmentation) or high-level VQA, leaving a gap in evaluating mid-level structural and relational reasoning—such as lane topology, ego-lane indexing, and lane-change feasibility—that bridges raw perception with high-level planning. No compact, reasoning-oriented benchmark exists to test whether models maintain geometrically and logically coherent representations of road structure across frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261854/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single front-view driving image at 4096×2160 resolution paired with a structured text query, and produces six interdependent per-frame attributes: lane count, ego-lane index, junction/entrance/exit label, left/right lane-change feasibility, traffic condition (free-flow/moderate/congestion), and road scene type (urban/suburban/highway). Images are organized into 5-frame clips sampled at 1 FPS, enabling temporal consistency evaluation; the outputs are logically coupled (e.g., lane count constrains valid ego-lane indices, and solid lane boundaries gate lane-change feasibility).

## Method

MapVLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA-based supervised fine-tuning to establish baseline mid-level reasoning, then applies HRRP-T, a reinforcement learning stage trained via GRPO that imposes hierarchical relational rewards. At the frame level, a three-layer reward (scene-level for lane count/ego-lane, relational-level for junctions and lane-change feasibility, semantic-level for traffic and scene type) enforces intra-frame topological validity. At the temporal level, a composite reward combines a smoothness term (penalizing abrupt frame-to-frame oscillations in ordinal variables like lane count) with a plausibility term that acts as a lightweight finite-state-machine constraint, permitting only semantically valid transitions (e.g., 'changeable' → 'non-changeable' at a solid line is allowed, but rapid oscillation is not). This dual spatial-temporal reward structure transforms the VLM from a static per-frame predictor into a geometry-aware reasoning agent that maintains coherent road topology across short sequences without additional human annotations.

## Results

On RoadSceneBench, MapVLM (SFT+HRRP-T) achieves an overall precision of 75.78% and recall of 72.17%, decisively surpassing the best closed-source baseline Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.61% P / 52.70% R) by ~15 pp in precision and ~20 pp in recall. The two hardest tasks—Ego-lane Index and Lane-change Feasibility—see the largest gains: MapVLM reaches 75.44% P / 84.67% R on Ego-lane Index and 83.83% P / 84.69% R on Lane-change Feasibility, whereas the best baselines score below 73% P and 53% R respectively. Adding HRRP-T on top of SFT lifts overall recall from 67.25% to 72.17% and Ego-lane Index recall from 50.37% to 84.67%, confirming the value of temporal-consistency rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MapVLM (SFT+HRRP-T) outperforms all 15 baselines on overall precision and recall, beating the strongest closed-source model Gemini-2.5-Pro by +15.17 pp in precision (75.78% vs 60.61%) and +19.47 pp in recall (72.17% vs 52.70%). Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-8B leads in precision (57.34%) and InternVL3-78B in recall (45.27%); MapVLM exceeds both by 18+ pp in precision and 27+ pp in recall. The advantage is most pronounced on the two hardest tasks: on Ego-lane Index, MapVLM scores 75.44% P / 84.67% R versus Gemini-2.5-Pro's 72.71% P / 46.52% R; on Lane-change Feasibility, MapVLM reaches 83.83% P / 84.69% R versus 59.25% P / 52.98% R for Gemini-2.5-Pro. Notably, SFT alone already surpasses all baselines (72.14% P / 67.25% R), and HRRP-T adds a further +3.64 pp precision and +4.92 pp recall, with the largest single-task gain on Ego-lane Index recall (+34.30 pp). The only task where MapVLM's precision drops slightly after adding HRRP-T is Lane Count (66.02% → 63.37%), while recall improves (61.55% → 65.89%), suggesting a precision-recall trade-off in that specific attribute.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself in the gap between low-level perception benchmarks (Cityscapes, BDD100K, nuScenes) that focus on pixel-wise or object-level annotation, and high-level VLM reasoning benchmarks (NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, VLADBench) that evaluate general multimodal QA. It introduces 'mid-level road semantics' as a distinct reasoning layer with explicitly interdependent attributes (lane count constrains ego-lane index, which constrains lane-change feasibility), departing from prior work that treats these as independent classification or detection tasks. Methodologically, MapVLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA fine-tuning and extends the GRPO reinforcement learning strategy (from DeepSeek-R1) with a novel hierarchical reward structure that couples intra-frame topological validity with inter-frame temporal plausibility, a formulation not present in prior VLM training for driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a practical gap in autonomous driving and HD map construction pipelines where lightweight camera-based semantic judgements (lane-count change, new exit ramps, ego-lane tracking) are needed but existing datasets are either too perception-heavy or too language-centric. Key limitations include geographic restriction to 20 Chinese cities, single front-camera view, and short 5-frame clips, which limit generalizability. Open questions include whether the HRRP-T reward structure transfers to longer sequences or multi-camera settings, whether the structural dependencies generalize to dynamic events (construction, accidents), and whether the substantial performance gap over closed-source VLMs reflects a genuine capability deficit or simply distribution shift from web-scale training data.
