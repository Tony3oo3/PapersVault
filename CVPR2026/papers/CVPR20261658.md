## Problem

Joint 3D scene and human reconstruction from monocular video has been treated either as separate tasks or via slow per-scene optimization, and existing feed-forward joint methods (e.g., JOSH3R) suffer from cumulative errors in their two-frame paradigm. A fundamental obstacle is the scarcity of large-scale annotated real-world data with joint scene, human, and camera ground truth, forcing reliance on synthetic datasets that introduce a severe sim-to-real domain gap, degrading both scene fidelity and SMPL-scene alignment on in-the-wild videos.

## Input / Output

The model takes a monocular video of N frames as input and, in a single forward pass, outputs per-frame metric-scale point maps covering both human and scene geometry, camera extrinsics and intrinsics, confidence maps, per-frame SMPL pose θ_i, a shared body shape β, per-frame SMPL translations t_i, and a global scene scale s. Human bounding boxes are extracted per-frame by an off-the-shelf detector, and SAM2 provides human masks used both for surface refinement supervision and for filtering the point cloud during fine-grained alignment. The entire output is globally aligned in a single world coordinate frame at metric scale.

## Method

UniSH couples a π³-based scene reconstruction branch with a CameraHMR-based human body branch, and introduces a lightweight AlignNet (a two-layer transformer decoder) that takes scene geometric features as key-value pairs and HMR tokens plus a learnable scale token as queries to predict the global scene scale and per-frame SMPL translations, avoiding direct metric-scale supervision that would damage the pre-trained reconstruction prior. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) human surface refinement on unlabeled in-the-wild video using MoGe-2 pseudo-depth labels with a confidence-aware local loss that resolves per-patch scale/shift ambiguity via an ROE solver; (2) coarse alignment on synthetic BEDLAM data supervising SMPL vertices, keypoints, pose, shape, and translation; (3) fine-grained alignment on unlabeled real data minimizing a one-way Chamfer distance between visible SMPL vertices and the reconstructed human point cloud, augmented with a depth-ordering regularization and 2D keypoint reprojection loss. This coarse-to-fine scheme bridges the sim-to-real gap without requiring any labeled real-world data.

## Results

On the Bonn human-centric depth estimation benchmark, UniSH achieves an Abs Rel of 0.035 and δ<1.25 accuracy of 0.980, outperforming the strongest reconstruction baseline π³ (0.049 / 0.975) by 29% in Abs Rel and 0.5% in accuracy. On global human motion estimation, UniSH attains WA-MPJPE of 118.5 mm and RTE of 5.8% on EMDB-2, and 118.1 mm / 4.8% on RICH, substantially outperforming the only other feed-forward joint-reconstruction method JOSH3R (220.0 mm / 13.1% on EMDB-2) while remaining competitive with specialized HMR methods like GVHMR (111.0 mm / 2.0%) and TRAM (76.4 mm / 1.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Bonn depth estimation task, UniSH decisively beats all 10 reconstruction baselines, with the largest margins over DUSt3R (0.151→0.035 Abs Rel), MASt3R (0.188→0.035), and MVDUSt3R (0.426→0.035), and a clear lead over the closest competitors π³ (0.049) and VGGT (0.057). On global motion estimation (EMDB-2), UniSH (WA-MPJPE 118.5 mm) dramatically outperforms JOSH3R (220.0 mm), the only other feed-forward method that jointly reconstructs the scene, reducing error by ~46%. However, UniSH trails specialized HMR-only methods: TRAM achieves 76.4 mm WA-MPJPE and GVHMR 111.0 mm on EMDB-2, with GVHMR also leading on RICH (78.8 vs. 118.1 mm). The paper frames this gap as expected, since UniSH relies on weaker unlabeled supervision and must simultaneously reconstruct the scene, whereas TRAM and GVHMR are trained on extensive ground-truth motion data without scene reconstruction. Among optimization-based joint methods, JOSH (68.9 mm) still leads on EMDB-2 but requires per-scene optimization, whereas UniSH achieves competitive results in a single forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSH sits at the intersection of two mature research threads—feed-forward 3D scene reconstruction (π³, DUSt3R, VGGT) and Human Mesh Recovery (CameraHMR, SMPL-based regression)—and unifies them via a lightweight AlignNet that fuses their respective features to predict global scale and SMPL placement. It departs from prior joint reconstruction methods (JOSH, JOSH3R, HAMSt3R) by eliminating per-scene optimization and the two-frame inference paradigm, instead operating in a single forward pass over the full sequence. The most distinctive departure is in training: rather than relying solely on synthetic BEDLAM supervision (which the ablations show actively degrades the pre-trained reconstruction prior), the paper introduces a coarse-to-fine scheme that leverages unlabeled in-the-wild video with pseudo-depth distillation from MoGe-2 and geometric Chamfer-based alignment between SMPL and reconstructed point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—scarcity of large-scale annotated data for joint scene-human reconstruction—by demonstrating that unlabeled real-world video, combined with expert-model distillation and geometric alignment losses, can bridge the sim-to-real gap without sacrificing the structural priors of a strong pre-trained reconstruction backbone. This training paradigm is potentially transferable to other tasks where synthetic data is the only source of ground truth but real-world generalization is critical. A noted limitation is that the non-parametric human surface still exhibits floaters, and the authors suggest using the aligned SMPL as a geometric prior to refine the final surface—a clear but open direction. The single-pass, metric-scale output also positions the method for real-time applications in AR/VR and embodied AI, though computational cost relative to HMR-only methods remains a trade-off.
