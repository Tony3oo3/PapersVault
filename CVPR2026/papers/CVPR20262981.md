## Problem

No prior dataset or method jointly localizes cameras across all three altitudes—ground, aerial, and satellite—despite the growing need for planet-scale 3D perception. The paper reveals that simply adding satellite imagery to existing single-feedforward pose estimators like VGGT causes severe performance degradation (−18.2% RRA), while pairwise methods like DUSt3R/MASt3R tolerate it, exposing an unresolved challenge in integrating extreme-altitude views into unified 3D models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262981/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts N RGB images spanning ground (5–80 m AGL), aerial (200–800 m AGL), and satellite (1–2 km AGL, ortho-rectified to remove perspective distortion) viewpoints, processed in a single forward pass. It outputs per-view camera parameters (rotation in quaternion form and translation, 9-d vectors) and dense depth maps at the input resolution. Unlike pairwise methods requiring O(N²) feed-forwards plus a global alignment stage, SkyNet produces all poses and depths simultaneously.

## Method

SkyNet is a two-stream extension of VGGT with a frozen 24-layer backbone. The GAS-encoder processes all modalities together but uses Masked-Satellite-Attention (MSA), which blocks aerial/ground tokens from attending to satellite tokens while allowing the reverse, preventing distribution shift from corrupting the well-pretrained ground/aerial representations. A separate Sat-encoder applies global attention over satellite tokens and receives refined satellite features from the GAS-encoder via additive fusion. Training employs two curriculum strategies: Curriculum-Aware Camera Sampling (progressively sampling cameras with larger rotation/translation separation) and Progressive View Sampling (starting with mostly aerial views as a geometric bridge, then reducing aerial count toward zero to force ground–satellite alignment). The model is trained end-to-end with a weighted multi-task loss over camera and depth supervision, achieving 83.2% RRA@5 and 66.5% RTA@5 on the full GAS setup.

## Results

On the Sky2Ground benchmark (Ground+Aerial+Satellite setup, Dense-2 split), SkyNet achieves 83.2% RRA@5 and 66.5% RTA@5 on average across ground, aerial, and satellite views. This represents a +9.6% absolute improvement over VGGT's zero-shot RRA (73.6%) and a +18.1% absolute improvement over the best pairwise baseline (MASt3R, 48.4% RTA with Aerial-MegaDepth pre-training). SkyNet also substantially outperforms fine-tuned VGGT (55.4% RRA, 43.6% RTA), which suffers a severe 18.2% RRA drop when satellite views are added during training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkyNet outperforms all three baselines across every view modality and both metrics. Against DUSt3R (fine-tuned on Sky2Ground), SkyNet gains +18.0% RRA (83.2 vs 65.2) and +23.0% RTA (66.5 vs 43.5); against MASt3R (fine-tuned on Sky2Ground), the margins are +13.8% RRA (83.2 vs 69.4) and +19.0% RTA (66.5 vs 47.5). The most striking comparison is with VGGT: fine-tuning VGGT on Sky2Ground actually degrades its zero-shot RRA from 73.6% to 55.4% (−18.2%), while SkyNet improves upon the zero-shot baseline to 83.2% (+9.6%). This reveals that naive fine-tuning of single-feedforward transformers is destabilized by satellite views, whereas SkyNet's Masked-Satellite-Attention and curriculum strategies preserve VGGT's pre-trained ground/aerial strengths while integrating satellite information. On the more challenging Ground+Satellite-only setup (Table 5), SkyNet further extends its lead, reaching 65.1 average vs. VGGT's 47.8 (fine-tuned) and 52.9 (zero-shot), a +12.2% gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

SkyNet is architecturally built on VGGT, reusing its DINO encoder, alternating frame/global attention blocks, and camera/depth heads, but introduces a two-stream design with Masked-Satellite-Attention to isolate satellite tokens from ground/aerial interactions. Conceptually, the paper departs from the pairwise-processing paradigm of DUSt3R and MASt3R (which require O(N²) forward passes plus global alignment) and from the naive fine-tuning approach that simply adds satellite views to VGGT's training mix. The Sky2Ground dataset itself carves new ground by being the first to simultaneously provide synthetic and real imagery across all three altitude levels (ground, aerial, satellite) with dense depth annotations, filling a gap identified in prior datasets like AerialMegaDepth (no satellite) and BungeeNeRF (synthetic only).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that adding satellite imagery degrades single-feedforward models like VGGT by ~18% RRA while helping pairwise models challenges the working assumption that more views monotonically improve localization, and provides a concrete diagnostic for why planet-wide reconstruction efforts (e.g., AerialMegaDepth-style pipelines) may stall when satellite data is introduced. The two-stage limitation (pose estimation followed by Gaussian splatting) and the consistent rendering degradation from real imagery noise remain open problems that the authors explicitly flag. Sky2Ground, as the first benchmark covering all three viewpoints with both synthetic and real data, establishes a necessary testbed for future work on large-scale multi-altitude 3D perception, though its 51-site scale and reliance on Google Earth Studio for synthetic data limit generalizability claims.
