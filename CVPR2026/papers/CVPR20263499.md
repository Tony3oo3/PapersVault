## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models are fundamentally limited by three architectural bottlenecks: the fixed 1024-frame temporal horizon of 3D-RoPE causes attention collapse beyond the base model's training range, slow KV-cache semantics delay prompt responsiveness for fine-grained action control, and the continuous temporal encoding cannot represent discontinuous cinematic cuts. Prior work addresses these through longer self-rollouts, retraining on long-video data, or heavy distillation, but no training-free approach simultaneously resolves all three constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt (or a sequence of action prompts for controllable generation) and produces video at 832×480 resolution, 16 FPS, in the latent space of a 3D VAE (4× temporal, 8× spatial compression). It operates on a Self-Forcing-distilled Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B model that natively generates only 5-second clips, yet ∞-RoPE extends generation to arbitrarily long horizons (demonstrated up to 240 seconds) using a fixed KV cache of 6 latent frames with onset index f₀=21. For multi-cut scenes, the user specifies cut points and temporal offsets Δ, and the model produces a single continuous rollout containing abrupt scene transitions while preserving subject identity across cuts.

## Method

∞-RoPE is a training-free, plug-and-play inference framework built on three interconnected components. Block-Relativistic RoPE redefines temporal coordinates as a moving local reference frame: each newly generated 3-frame block is rotated relative to the base model's maximum horizon (f_limit=21) rather than an absolute index, while earlier cached blocks are rotated backward to preserve relative geometry; when the cache exceeds f_limit, the earliest frames undergo 'semanticization' by collapsing their temporal coordinates to a shared minimum, transitioning from episodic to semantic memory. KV Flush enables instant action control by resetting the entire KV cache to only two anchor tokens—the global attention sink and the last generated latent frame—so a new prompt takes effect immediately without carrying stale semantics. RoPE Cut introduces controlled discontinuities by offsetting the current block's temporal RoPE coordinates by Δ frames, effectively re-anchoring the coordinate system to realize clean scene cuts within a single autoregressive rollout. All three mechanisms require no additional training, data, or model updates, and operate at the same 17 FPS throughput as the underlying Self-Forcing model.

## Results

On 60-second video generation, ∞-RoPE achieves an overall VBench score of 0.8298, outperforming the strongest autoregressive baseline Rolling-Forcing (0.8146) and CausVid (0.7940). The advantage grows with duration: at 120 seconds, ∞-RoPE scores 0.8236 overall (vs. 0.8162 for Rolling-Forcing), and at 240 seconds it reaches 0.8309 (vs. 0.8017 for Rolling-Forcing), a gap of nearly 3 percentage points. ∞-RoPE also leads on Dynamic Degree at every long-horizon setting (0.52 at 60s, 0.56 at 120s, 0.64 at 240s) and on Subject Consistency (0.9444 at 60s, 0.9302 at 120s, 0.9256 at 240s), indicating sustained identity preservation without temporal drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the two bidirectional baselines (LTX-Video, Wan2.1), ∞-RoPE is competitive on 5-second clips (0.8377 vs. 0.8439 and 0.8319 overall) but these models cannot generate beyond their fixed short horizon, so no long-horizon comparison is possible. Among autoregressive baselines, ∞-RoPE clearly dominates on all long-horizon settings: at 240 seconds it outperforms Rolling-Forcing by 2.9 points overall (0.8309 vs. 0.8017), CausVid by 5.5 points (0.8309 vs. 0.7758), SkyReels-V2 by 11.6 points, Self-Forcing by 14.6 points, and NOVA by 16.5 points. The closest competitor is Rolling-Forcing, which also uses a rolling KV cache strategy; however, ∞-RoPE surpasses it on every metric at 60s, 120s, and 240s, with the largest margins on Dynamic Degree (0.64 vs. 0.40 at 240s) and Subject Consistency (0.9256 vs. 0.9080). On 5-second generation, ∞-RoPE (0.8377) is marginally below Self-Forcing (0.8398) and LTX-Video (0.8439), suggesting a small short-horizon trade-off, but this gap reverses and widens substantially as video length increases, confirming that the relativistic RoPE reparameterization specifically benefits long-horizon coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Self-Forcing autoregressive video diffusion paradigm (which distills bidirectional DiTs into causal generators via DMD) and the Wan2.1 3D-RoPE positional encoding, but departs fundamentally from prior long-horizon extensions (Self-Forcing++, Rolling Forcing, SkyReels-V2) that rely on longer training rollouts or retraining on long-video data. Instead, ∞-RoPE is entirely training-free and operates at inference time by reparameterizing temporal RoPE coordinates into a moving local reference frame (Block-Relativistic RoPE), renewing the KV cache with two anchor tokens (KV Flush), and introducing controlled temporal discontinuities (RoPE Cut). The conceptual lineage traces to RoFormer's rotary embeddings and the attention-sink mechanism, but the relativistic re-anchoring of temporal positions is a new formulation that removes the fixed 1024-frame horizon without any architectural or weight changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that any existing Self-Forcing-style autoregressive video model trained on short clips can be converted into an effectively infinite-horizon, action-controllable generator with zero additional training, data, or compute — a plug-and-play inference-time recipe. This democratizes long-form video generation and opens the door to interactive, streaming, and cinematic multi-cut video synthesis. Limitations include inheriting all generative biases of the base model (imperfect physics, texture instability), validation on a single 1.3B-parameter model family, and the 'infinite' claim being empirically tested only up to 240 seconds. Open questions include whether the relativistic RoPE formulation generalizes to other DiT architectures or spatially-extended settings, and whether the semanticization analogy (collapsing distant temporal indices) degrades quality at truly unbounded horizons.
