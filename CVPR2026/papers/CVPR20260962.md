limitations: The framework relies on a fixed Haar wavelet basis and a fixed W-axis orientation for polyphase analysis rather than learned or adaptive decimation, and does not address multimodal fusion, domain adaptation, or uncertainty quantification, which the authors identify as future work. Empirical gains over the strongest baselines are modest (typically 0.3–1.5 IoU points), and the evaluation is limited to single-modality, single-protocol settings without cross-site or cross-protocol generalization tests.

surprise_factor: The main conceptual contribution—framing stride-induced aliasing as a solvable, module-level problem in segmentation rather than an unavoidable artifact—is a fresh lens, but the empirical gains over strong baselines (SegMamba, DiffUNet, MedNeXt) are modest and consistent rather than dramatic (typically 0.3–1.5 IoU points, with larger HD95 reductions on anisotropic datasets). ## Problem

Standard 3D medical segmentation architectures (U-shaped CNNs, Transformers) neither suppress aliasing injected by strided downsampling under anisotropic voxel spacing nor explicitly align high-resolution skip evidence before decoder fusion, leading to blurred boundaries, fragmented topology, and unstable performance across scanners and acquisition protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260962/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D medical volumes (CT, MRI, OCT) with potentially anisotropic voxel spacing; intensities are clipped and Min-Max normalized, then randomly cropped into 96×96×96 patches for training (batch size 1, 320k steps) and processed via 3D sliding-window inference at the same patch size. Output is a per-voxel segmentation mask decoded per volume. The full model uses 23.78 M parameters and 199.58 G FLOPs, positioned between lightweight CNNs and heavy Transformer/Mamba stacks.

## Method

CROWn couples two complementary modules. The Microlocal Polyphase Co-Attentive Decimator (μPCAD) replaces standard strided downsampling with axis-aware Haar polyphase decomposition: max/avg-pooled branches serve as attention queries and keys while four wavelet subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) serve as values in a cross-source co-attention, followed by a fixed W-axis anti-alias blur and a learned 1×1×3 projection-decimator, preserving boundary-relevant high frequencies while suppressing stride-induced aliasing. The Octaphase Coset Fibration (OCF) restructures high-resolution skip connections by first applying a separable low-pass prefilter, then performing 3D space-to-depth into eight interleaved cosets, computing softmax phase attention across the eight cosets, and modulating features with an edge gate derived from tri-axial 3D Sobel magnitudes before depthwise-separable consolidation. Together, μPCAD controls aliasing at every encoder decimation interface and OCF calibrates phase alignment at every skip-fusion point, yielding compact, boundary-aware features that are semantically closer to the decoder scale.

## Results

CROWn achieves the highest IoU and Dice on all 15 evaluated datasets, with the largest margins on anisotropic and fine-structure benchmarks. On FLARE2022 it reaches 82.65 IoU / 89.76 Dice / 4.30 HD95 (vs. SegMamba 81.58/88.86/9.21); on MSD Pancreas Tumour it attains 56.68 IoU / 70.20 Dice / 11.72 HD95 (vs. VSmTrans 55.09/68.53/42.56); on OIMHS (OCT) it scores 89.68 IoU / 94.36 Dice / 2.60 HD95. The full model uses 23.78 M parameters and 199.58 G FLOPs at 96×96×96 input, well below 3D UX-Net (53 M / 631.76 G) and SegMamba (64.24 M / 655.62 G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CROWn outperforms all 17 compared baselines on IoU and Dice across every one of the 15 datasets. The largest absolute gains appear on anisotropic multi-organ CT (FLARE2022: +1.07 IoU over SegMamba, HD95 reduced from 9.21 to 4.30) and fine-structure tumour data (MSD Pancreas: +1.59 IoU over VSmTrans, HD95 reduced from 42.56 to 11.72). On MSD Lung Tumour, CROWn improves IoU by 0.94 over SwinUNETR while cutting HD95 from 51.46 to 14.66, indicating substantially better boundary fidelity. On the OCT dataset OIMHS, gains are smaller but consistent (+0.85 IoU over SegMamba, +0.52 Dice), confirming cross-modality robustness. On FLARE2021, CROWn matches MedNeXt at 89.27 IoU / 93.91 Dice / 2.33 HD95, suggesting the task is near saturation for this protocol. In terms of efficiency, CROWn (23.78 M / 199.58 G) is roughly 2–3× lighter in parameters and 3× lighter in FLOPs than 3D UX-Net, SegMamba, and nnWNet while still surpassing them in accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

CROWn repurposes classical signal-processing tools—polyphase decomposition, Haar wavelet subbands, and coset/fibration theory—into a deep segmentation architecture, a conceptual bridge that prior 3D medical segmentation work (3D U-Net, UNETR, SwinUNETR, SegMamba, MedNeXt) has not explicitly drawn. The space-to-depth operation is borrowed from super-resolution literature (Shi et al., 2016) and the edge-gated modulation from salient-object detection (Zhao et al., 2019), but their combination with pooled-subband co-attention for alias-aware decimation is new. Rather than extending a single prior architecture, CROWn is designed as a plug-in module pair (μPCAD for encoder downsampling, OCF for skip calibration) that can be inserted into CNN, Transformer, ConvNeXt, or Mamba backbones, positioning it as an orthogonal improvement over the dominant U-shaped paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By treating downsampling aliasing and cross-scale phase misalignment as first-class design constraints rather than implementation artifacts, CROWn offers a principled explanation for boundary degradation under anisotropic voxel spacing—a common but under-addressed failure mode in clinical CT, MRI, and OCT. The architecture-agnostic modularity (validated across four backbone families) makes the modules practically adoptable without redesigning the full network. Open questions remain: whether the sampling-theoretic framing generalizes to non-uniform or learned decimation, how the fixed Haar basis compares to learned wavelet filters at scale, and whether the approach extends to multimodal fusion or domain-adaptive settings as the authors note in their future work.
