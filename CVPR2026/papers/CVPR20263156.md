## Problem

Existing 4D radar-camera fusion detectors suffer from three coupled limitations: (1) absolute depth estimation is inaccurate because supervision is restricted to sparse foreground radar points, producing blurred and discontinuous geometry; (2) temporal fusion degrades or fails when ego-vehicle pose is missing or noisy, as is common in rural or GPS-denied scenarios; and (3) small or distant objects (e.g., cyclists) may yield no radar reflections at all, forcing detection to rely solely on visual priors that current CNN-based frameworks exploit poorly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse 4D millimeter-wave radar point clouds and synchronized multi-view camera images, processing them through a CNN backbone with a Neighborhood Cross-Attention view transformation (Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm) into a shared BEV space. It outputs 3D object bounding boxes (Car, Pedestrian, Cyclist, Truck) evaluated with IoU thresholds of 0.50 for vehicles and 0.25 for vulnerable road users, alongside a temporally-consistent BEV feature map. Training follows a two-stage protocol: 15 epochs of spatial pretraining (freezing temporal and refinement modules) followed by 15 epochs of end-to-end spatial-temporal fine-tuning, with a global batch size of 32.

## Method

R4Det is a progressive purification pipeline with three plug-and-play BEV-compatible modules. The Panoramic Depth Fusion (PDF) module replaces sparse metric regression with a triplet of supervisions: a probabilistic KL-divergence loss on the depth distribution, a foundation-model-guided Smooth-L1 loss combining sparse radar anchors with dense Metric3D pseudo-ground-truth, and a pairwise ordinal ranking loss with depth-dependent dynamic tolerance and a foreground-biased cross-boundary sampling strategy that forces sharp depth transitions at object edges. The Deformable Gated Temporal Fusion (DGTF) module decouples spatial alignment from temporal update: DCNv2 with learned offsets and modulation masks compensates for non-rigid ego-motion without any pose input, while a GRU-style reset-and-update gating mechanism selectively integrates aligned history with the current observation. The Instance-Guided Dynamic Refinement (IGDR) module constructs a clean spatial instance prototype from 2D RPN proposals via Softmax-weighted fusion, then uses it as a conditional generator to predict per-location affine calibration parameters (γ, β) through Conv2d layers, applying a foreground-gated fusion that calibrates the main BEV stream only within instance regions while preserving background structure.

## Results

On TJ4DRadSet, R4Det achieves 47.29% mAP₃D and 54.07% mAP_BEV, surpassing the previous best (SGDet3D) by +5.47% and +6.91% respectively, with a particularly large +7.91% gain on the Cyclist category (62.84% vs. 51.30%). On VoD, R4Det reaches 66.69% mAP_EAA and 83.68% mAP_DC, outperforming CVFusion (65.41% / 82.42%) while running at 8.3 FPS versus CVFusion's 5.4 FPS. The plug-and-play experiments further show that adding R4Det's modules to BEVFusion and RCBEVDet yields +6.34% and +5.34% mAP_EAA improvements, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R4Det outperforms all ten compared baselines on both TJ4DRadSet and VoD. Against the strongest prior fusion method SGDet3D [1], R4Det gains +5.47% mAP₃D and +6.91% mAP_BEV on TJ4DRadSet, with the largest per-class improvement on Cyclist (+11.54% AP₃D). On VoD, R4Det beats CVFusion [29] by +1.28% mAP_EAA and +1.26% mAP_DC while being 1.5× faster (8.3 vs. 5.4 FPS), and surpasses SGDet3D [1] by +6.94% mAP_EAA. Radar-only methods (PointPillars, CenterPoint, RadarPillarNet, SMURF) trail by 14–19% in mAP₃D, confirming the necessity of camera fusion. Earlier fusion approaches (BEVFusion, FUTR3D, LXL) are outperformed by 10–15% in mAP₃D, highlighting the benefit of R4Det's panoramic depth supervision, pose-free temporal fusion, and instance-guided refinement. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) further validate generality: attaching R4Det's modules to BEVFusion [13] and RCBEVDet [14] yields +6.34% and +5.34% mAP_EAA, respectively, without architectural redesign.

## Relation to Prior Work

R4Det builds directly on SGDet3D, adopting its two-stage training strategy and Neighborhood Cross-Attention mechanism as the geometric backbone, while addressing three specific gaps in the 4D radar-camera fusion literature. The Panoramic Depth Fusion module extends the LSS-based depth estimation paradigm (BEVDepth, BEVFusion) by incorporating relative depth ranking losses and foundation-model pseudo-labels (Metric3D) to overcome the sparse-supervision limitation of prior radar-anchored depth. The Deformable Gated Temporal Fusion departs from the dominant ego-pose-dependent temporal alignment (BEVFormer, VideoBEV) by using learned deformable offsets for pose-free motion compensation, and the Instance-Guided Dynamic Refinement offers a CNN-compatible alternative to Transformer-based instance query approaches (FAR3D, PETR) for small-object detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

4D millimeter-wave radar is a cost-effective, all-weather alternative to LiDAR, making robust radar-camera fusion a practically critical problem for scalable autonomous driving deployment. The pose-free temporal fusion is particularly relevant for real-world scenarios where GPS/IMU odometry degrades (rural areas, tunnels), and the plug-and-play nature of the three modules (demonstrated via +6.34% on BEVFusion and +5.34% on RCBEVDet) lowers the barrier for adoption in existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the Metric3D pseudo-label dependency limits generalization to unseen domains, whether the RPN-based instance guidance scales to denser or more cluttered scenes, and whether the approach extends naturally to other sparse-sensor combinations (e.g., 4D radar + event camera).
