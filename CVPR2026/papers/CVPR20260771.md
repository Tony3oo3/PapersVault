## Problem

Conventional and learned structure-from-motion pipelines collapse in extreme low-light conditions (SNR below −4 dB) because noise causes feature detection and matching to fail, propagating errors to pose estimation and triangulation. Existing workarounds—increasing exposure (introducing motion blur) or applying per-frame denoisers before SfM—either introduce new artifacts or break multi-view consistency, leaving a gap in passive 3D sensing for very dark scenes.

## Input / Output

Dark3R consumes multi-view raw sensor images that are trivially demosaiced (Bayer subsampling with green-channel averaging) and downsampled 16× to 512×352 from the original 50 MP resolution; camera intrinsics are assumed known and incorporated as a regularization during bundle adjustment. The method outputs camera poses, per-pixel 3D point maps, and sparse depth maps from the SfM stage, and when combined with the proposed coarse-to-fine NeRF optimization, produces rendered novel views in sRGB after an ISP pipeline. Notably, no black-level subtraction or clipping is applied to the raw inputs, preserving linear sensor response at very low SNRs.

## Method

Dark3R adapts the pretrained MASt3R vision-transformer to low-light SfM via a teacher-student distillation strategy: a frozen MASt3R teacher processes clean raw image pairs while a LoRA-fine-tuned student processes the corresponding noisy pairs, and the student is trained to match the teacher's encoder features, decoder features, and correspondence maps using an L2 loss (with an additional clean-pair regularization term to maintain performance across SNR ranges). No 3D supervision is required—training uses only noisy-clean raw image pairs, either captured directly or synthesized via a Poisson-Gaussian noise model. At inference, Dark3R follows the MASt3R-SfM pipeline (co-visibility graph, global alignment, bundle adjustment) to recover poses and sparse geometry. For novel view synthesis, a NeRF is optimized with stochastic preconditioning (annealed Gaussian noise on ray-sample locations) to avoid overfitting to sensor noise, combined with exponentially decaying depth supervision from Dark3R's predicted point maps.

## Results

On the exposure-bracketed test scenes (average SNR −3.87 dB), Dark3R achieves an absolute translation error (ATE) of 0.050 and a δ<1.25 depth accuracy of 93.14%, substantially outperforming the closest competitor MASt3R-SfM (ATE 0.088, δ<1.25 79.39%) and VGGT (ATE 0.252, δ<1.25 63.28%). For novel view synthesis at the same SNR, Dark3R-NeRF with Dark3R poses reaches 36.17 dB PSNR and 0.257 LPIPS, exceeding RawNeRF (34.24 dB, 0.291 LPIPS) and LE3D (35.77 dB, 0.339 LPIPS). The oracle upper bound (Dark3R-NeRF with COLMAP poses from clean images) reaches 37.16 dB PSNR, indicating Dark3R recovers most of the achievable quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dark3R outperforms all five baselines on every pose and depth metric at the tested SNR of −3.87 dB. Against the strongest baseline MASt3R-SfM, Dark3R reduces ATE by 43% (0.050 vs. 0.088), RPE rotation by 40% (0.121° vs. 0.201°), and improves δ<1.25 depth accuracy by 13.75 percentage points (93.14% vs. 79.39%). VGGT, the next-best feed-forward method, is 5× worse in ATE and 1.7× worse in δ<1.25. COLMAP (run on clean sRGB images as a reference) is actually outperformed by Dark3R on all metrics despite operating on noisy raw inputs, highlighting the severity of the low-light degradation. For view synthesis, Dark3R-NeRF with Dark3R poses achieves the best PSNR (36.17 dB) and LPIPS (0.257) among non-oracle configurations, beating RawNeRF by 1.93 dB PSNR and LE3D by 0.40 dB PSNR, while LE3D edges out Dark3R-NeRF on SSIM (0.878 vs. 0.866) and LPIPS (0.339 vs. 0.257 — actually Dark3R-NeRF wins on LPIPS). The gap to the oracle (37.16 dB) is only 0.99 dB, indicating Dark3R's pose estimates are near-optimal for downstream rendering.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dark3R extends the MASt3R/MASt3R-SfM pipeline—a vision-transformer-based 3D correspondence and SfM framework—into the extreme low-light regime via teacher-student knowledge distillation with LoRA, requiring no 3D supervision beyond noisy-clean raw image pairs. For view synthesis, it builds on NeRF (Nerfacto), incorporating depth supervision from DS-NeRF and stochastic preconditioning for coarse-to-fine optimization. The key conceptual departure from prior low-light 3D work (e.g., RawNeRF, LE3D) is that those methods assume accurate poses from conventional SfM, which fails below ~0 dB SNR; Dark3R instead makes the pose estimation itself robust to noise, thereby unblocking the entire downstream pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling SfM at SNRs below −4 dB—a regime where all prior feature-based and foundation-model approaches collapse—Dark3R opens passive 3D sensing to environments previously considered inaccessible, with practical implications for nighttime robotics, low-light AR, and computational photography. The introduced exposure-bracketed dataset (~42K multi-view raw images with 3D annotations) provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating low-light 3D methods. Open questions include generalization to dynamic scenes, feed-forward (non-iterative) prediction, and whether the distillation strategy transfers to other 3D foundation models like VGGT; the current method also assumes known camera intrinsics and was validated on a limited set of indoor scenes.
