## Problem

Existing NLoS RF reconstruction methods (e.g., GeRaF) crop out the occluding surface and treat LoS and NLoS regions identically, ignoring that RF signals are partially reflected and attenuated by the occluder. This neglect causes three failures: inaccurate NLoS surfaces contaminated by LoS reflections, unstable optimization across different occluder geometries, and surface ambiguity where the SDF zero-level set is offset from the true surface. The paper asks whether stable, accurate LoS geometry from vision can be leveraged as a physical prior to guide RF propagation modeling into the hidden region.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view 77 GHz FMCW mmWave radar heatmaps (captured with a TI AWR1843BOOST on a Franka Research 3 arm, 360° rotation at 10° increments) and multi-view RGB camera images of the same scene. The camera images are used only to pre-train a vision SDF (via NeuS) that initializes and constrains the RF network; the radar signals drive the actual geometry learning. The output is a unified ULoS signed distance field whose zero-level set yields a 3D mesh spanning both the visible box exterior and the hidden object interior, evaluated against Scaniverse ground truth with F1-score and Chamfer distance.

## Method

GeRaF 2.0 introduces three coupled components. First, a ULoS SDF represents the scene as nested closed compact sets where negative values mark strong-RF-interaction regions (box walls, object interior) and positive values mark weak-interaction regions (air), unifying LoS and NLoS in a single field. Second, ULoS Lensless Rendering exploits the fact that the vision-pretrained SDF and the RF-trained ULoS SDF coincide in the LoS region to adjust accumulated transmittance at scene boundaries, providing a stable initialization that avoids the gradient errors of naively backpropagating through the sigmoid-SDF boundary terms. Third, a two-stage training procedure resolves surface ambiguity: Stage 1 freezes the reflectivity network and optimizes matched-filter loss plus Eikonal regularization; Stage 2 jointly trains all networks while adding an RSDF alignment loss that enforces equality between the RF-trained relative SDF and the vision SDF on the outer box surface, with a proposition proving that gradient equality everywhere plus value equality on a closed surface guarantees global field identity, thereby transferring the correct zero-level from LoS into NLoS.

## Results

The paper evaluates GeRaF 2.0 using F1-Score (τ=0.015) and Chamfer Distance (in mm) on a custom multi-view radar and camera dataset captured with a 77 GHz mmWave radar on a Franka Research 3 arm, with ground truth from Scaniverse. No explicit numerical results table is provided in the main text; quantitative values are deferred to the supplementary material. Qualitatively, both Stage 1 and Stage 2 of GeRaF 2.0 produce the cleanest RF-based reconstructions, with Stage 2 uniquely extracting surfaces at the correct SDF zero-level set—a property all baselines fail to achieve. The method also demonstrates robustness to bubble-wrap fillers and nested-box multilayer occlusions with negligible geometric degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeRaF 2.0 is compared against three baselines: vision-based NeuS [46], Matched Filter (MF) point-cloud reconstruction, and the prior NLoS method GeRaF [29]. Against GeRaF [29], GeRaF 2.0 clearly wins: GeRaF suffers from LoS-induced artifacts (e.g., a spurious 'hat' on the bunny), unstable training that fails to converge for some box geometries, and an incorrect SDF zero-level requiring manual thresholding, whereas GeRaF 2.0 produces clean, physically consistent surfaces at the true zero-level. Against Matched Filter imaging, GeRaF 2.0 outperforms on both surface accuracy and detail preservation, as MF produces noisy, incomplete point clouds after thresholding and Poisson reconstruction. Against NeuS (vision-only), GeRaF 2.0 Stage 2 'closely matches' the vision-based reconstruction quality for the hidden object, which NeuS cannot recover at all since it cannot see through the box. The key differentiator is that Stage 2's RSDF alignment eliminates the surface ambiguity problem, removing the need for manual SDF thresholding and preserving fine details (tusks, antlers, comb) that all baselines lose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own GeRaF framework, which performed NLoS RF reconstruction by simply cropping the occluding surface out of the radar image and treating LoS and NLoS identically. The key departure is the introduction of a unified ULoS SDF representation that explicitly models the physical interaction between LoS and NLoS regions (partial reflection and attenuation at the occluding surface), combined with a vision-pretrained SDF (via NeuS) that provides stable initialization and a reference for resolving surface ambiguity. The lensless rendering formulation builds on the volumetric rendering framework of NeuS but adapts it to two-way RF propagation with squared transmittance, and the RSDF alignment mechanism draws on the relative SDF concept from mmNorm while providing a novel two-stage optimization that leverages the LoS region as a calibration anchor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that LoS geometry, previously treated as a nuisance to be cropped away, is actually a critical physical constraint that stabilizes NLoS RF reconstruction and resolves the long-standing surface ambiguity problem. The clean theoretical result (RSDF alignment on a closed LoS surface implies global alignment) provides a principled justification for the two-stage training, and the practical outcome—accurate zero-level SDF extraction without manual thresholding—is a meaningful step toward deployable RF-based 3D sensing. Open questions include scalability to larger or more complex occlusion geometries, generalization to materials with very different RF interaction properties, and whether the approach extends to real-time or single-view settings. The current evaluation is limited to a small set of near-field objects with a specific radar-camera rig, leaving robustness to diverse real-world conditions untested.
