## Problem

Existing 3D shape generation methods achieve high fidelity only under clean, unoccluded, and well-segmented inputs, but real-world casual captures introduce occlusions, background clutter, sensor noise, low resolution, and suboptimal viewpoints that cause these methods to fail. ShapeR addresses this gap by formulating robust, object-centric metric 3D shape generation directly from casually captured image sequences without requiring explicit segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262555/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes up to 16 posed views at 280×280 resolution from Aria Mono cameras, a sparse metric SLAM point cloud with per-frame visibility associations, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, a VLM-generated caption, and binary 2D masks formed by projecting the object's 3D points into each selected view. Notably, no segmentation masks are provided; the object of interest is identified implicitly. The output is a complete metric 3D mesh per detected object, decoded from a signed distance field via marching cubes and rescaled from the normalized [-1,1]³ generation space back to the original metric coordinate frame.

## Method

ShapeR formulates shape generation as a rectified flow process: a FLUX.1-like dual-single-stream transformer denoises a latent VecSet (Dora VAE) by transporting Gaussian noise to the shape latent manifold, conditioned on three modality streams — SLAM points encoded by a sparse 3D ResNet, images encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone concatenated with Plücker ray encodings and 2D point-projection masks processed by a 2D convolutional network, and captions tokenized by T5 and CLIP. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: first on 600K object-centric meshes with extensive on-the-fly compositional augmentations (background compositing, occlusion overlays, visibility fog, resolution degradation, point dropout, and photometric perturbations) to simulate casual-capture noise, then fine-tuned on object crops from Aria Synthetic Environments to learn realistic inter-object interactions and SLAM noise patterns. The key design choice is that no explicit 2D segmentation is used; instead, the 3D point tokens and their 2D projections implicitly identify which object to reconstruct, making the model robust to imperfect or absent segmentation in cluttered scenes.

## Results

On the ShapeR Evaluation Dataset (178 objects across 7 real-world scenes), ShapeR achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.375×10⁻², Normal Consistency of 0.810, and F1 score of 0.722, outperforming the best posed multi-view baseline (FoundationStereo, CD=6.483) by 2.7× in Chamfer distance. In a user preference study over 660 responses, ShapeR is preferred over foundation image-to-3D models (TripoSG, Direct3DS2, Hunyuan3D-2.0, Amodal3R) with win rates ranging from 81.11% to 88.33%. The method operates fully automatically on multiple posed views without requiring manual segmentation, unlike all compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against posed multi-view to 3D methods, ShapeR dominates across all metrics: it reduces Chamfer distance from 6.483 (FoundationStereo, the best baseline) to 2.375, a 2.7× improvement, while also achieving the highest Normal Consistency (0.810 vs. 0.677) and F1 (0.722 vs. 0.436). EFM3D, a monolithic scene reconstruction method, performs worst (CD=13.82) because it produces incomplete objects under occlusion. Segmentation-based methods LIRM and DP-Recon (CD=8.047 and 8.364) degrade under the imperfect SAM2 masks they require, whereas ShapeR needs no segmentation input. In the user preference study against foundation image-to-3D models, ShapeR is preferred over Direct3DS2 (88.33%), TripoSG (86.67%), Amodal3R (86.11%), and Hunyuan3D-2.0 (81.11%), despite those baselines receiving manually selected views and interactive SAM2 segmentations while ShapeR operates fully automatically. For scene-level methods MIDI3D and SceneGen, the comparison is qualitative: both struggle with inconsistent object scales and layouts in cluttered scenes, while ShapeR reconstructs each object metrically and independently with consistent scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShapeR sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: large-scale 3D shape generation (TripoSG, Hunyuan3D, TRELLIS) that assumes clean, segmented, unoccluded inputs, and metric scene reconstruction (EFM3D, NeRF, 3DGS) that produces monolithic geometry without per-object completeness. It departs from both by formulating object-centric shape generation as a rectified flow process on Dora/VecSet latents conditioned jointly on sparse SLAM points, posed DINOv2 image tokens, and text captions, while eliminating the explicit 2D segmentation mask requirement that plagues prior object-level methods (DP-Recon, LIRM, Amodal3R). The architecture borrows the FLUX dual-single-stream DiT backbone and the flow-matching objective from recent 3D generative models, but the key conceptual shift is using 3D point tokens and their 2D projections as an implicit segmentation signal, replacing the mask-based conditioning that prior work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ShapeR provides a practical path toward unifying generative 3D shape modeling with metric scene reconstruction: it produces complete, metrically consistent per-object meshes from the kind of noisy, occluded, cluttered captures that real users produce, without any manual segmentation. The new evaluation dataset (178 objects, 7 scenes, complete geometry annotations) fills a notable gap, as existing real-world datasets either lack object-level completeness or are captured in controlled studio settings. Open questions include how the approach scales to scenes with many overlapping objects, whether the two-stage curriculum generalizes to categories absent from both training datasets, and whether the reliance on off-the-shelf SLAM and 3D detection as preprocessing introduces error propagation that limits end-to-end accuracy.
