## Problem

Converting 3D shapes into compact primitive assemblies faces a persistent fidelity-parsimony trade-off: optimization-driven methods produce dense, redundant primitive soups, while analysis-driven methods commit to fixed partitions that may not align with what primitives can represent. No existing primitive family simultaneously satisfies expressivity (spanning cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, and bent/hollow variants), editability (compact parameterization), and optimizability (differentiable SDF), and no inference procedure effectively couples global shape analysis with local primitive fitting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D shape as an SDF or mesh (evaluated at 128³ voxel resolution) and produces an ordered assembly of SuperFrustum primitives, each specified by 8 shape parameters plus a 6-DoF pose, combined via a smooth union operator with per-pair blend sharpness. The assembly is directly deployable as an editable shape program or can be constrained to yield discrete CSG trees of canonical solids (cuboid, cylinder, cone, sphere, onion). A two-round variant runs in ~184 s per shape, while the full ten-round pipeline takes ~653 s.

## Method

The framework combines two contributions. First, SuperFrustum is a unified analytic SDF with 8 parameters (anisotropic scale, profile rounding, dilation, taper, bulge, onion/shell thickness) whose zero-level set spans cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, and bent or hollow variants, with a piecewise-C¹ differentiable distance field enabling stable gradient-based fitting. Second, ResFit is an unsupervised iterative procedure that alternates between Morphological Shape Decomposition (MSD) to propose initial primitives from the thickest connected regions of the current residual, and decomposition-aware optimization that refines all primitives via gradient descent on a composite loss (curvature-weighted occupancy reconstruction, Gumbel-Softmax parsimony penalty, and overlap/union-consistency quality regularizer) followed by discrete pruning. After each optimization round, the explained volume is subtracted from the target, and the cycle repeats on the unexplained residual until the objective saturates or a fixed budget is reached, allowing global structure and local parameters to mutually inform each other.

## Results

On the 3DGen-Prim dataset, the proposed method achieves 88.74% voxel IoU and 80.19% BiSurfIoU with only 23.98 primitives, improving IoU by +6.1 points over the strongest baseline (MPS, 82.67%) while using roughly half as many primitives (23.98 vs. 42.96). On Toys4K, it reaches 89.92% IoU and 85.66% BiSurfIoU with 23.67 primitives, a +9.3-point IoU gain over MPS (80.60%). Volumetric overlap drops from 0.684 (MPS) to 0.210 on 3DGen-Prim, a >3× reduction, and Chamfer Distance improves from 0.884 to 0.168. The method also achieves the lowest IntraPrim scores (0.244/0.221) and among the highest InterPrim scores, indicating semantically coherent part assignments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Marching Primitives (MPS) [18], the strongest optimization-based baseline, the proposed method wins on every metric: IoU improves by +6.1 (3DGen-Prim) and +9.3 (Toys4K) points, Chamfer Distance drops from 0.884 to 0.168, and primitive count is halved (23.98 vs. 42.96) while volumetric overlap falls from 0.684 to 0.210. Against Primitive Anything (PA) [44], a learning-based method predicting cuboids, cylinders, and ellipsoids, the gap is even larger: PA achieves only 34.62% IoU with 19.81 primitives, and its test-time-optimized variant PA(TTO) reaches 53.73% IoU but at the cost of 64.87 primitives and 0.493 overlap. On the CSG inference task (Table 4, ABC dataset), the constrained SuperFrustum variant matches CAPRI-Net [45] in reconstruction accuracy (88.30 vs. 84.46 IoU) while using roughly two-thirds as many primitives (13.16 vs. 23.0). The method thus dominates all three baselines simultaneously on both reconstruction fidelity and program parsimony, shifting the fidelity–parsimony Pareto frontier rather than trading one for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes analytic SDF formulations originally developed by the Shadertoy/Demoscene community (paniq's sdsuperprim, Inigo Quilez's distance functions) for real-time rendering into a differentiable primitive suitable for inverse modeling, and pairs it with an adapted variant of Morphological Shape Decomposition (Pitas & Venetsanopoulos, 1990) as an initialization strategy. It departs from prior primitive-assembly methods in two ways: rather than committing to a fixed segmentation (ACD-based) or optimizing a large primitive soup from scratch, ResFit interleaves global shape analysis with local gradient-based refinement on residuals. The SuperFrustum primitive is positioned as the first single analytic form that simultaneously satisfies expressivity (spanning cuboids, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori), compact editability (8 parameters), and near-everywhere differentiability—properties that prior families like superquadrics, algebraic surfaces, or multi-type learned primitives each satisfy only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework shifts the reconstruction-parsimony Pareto frontier in a practically meaningful way: 9+ IoU improvement with roughly half the primitives of prior SOTA makes the resulting assemblies genuinely editable and deployable rather than dense, redundant soups. This is timely given the wave of AI-generated 3D assets (Hunyuan3D, etc.) that lack structural organization for animation, rigging, or interactive editing. Key limitations include the purely additive composition (no subtraction operator, though the onion parameter partially compensates), the 652 s per-shape runtime (unoptimized), and reliance on a fixed 10-round budget. Open questions include whether richer decomposition strategies (tree-of-shapes) and dedicated CUDA kernels can close the speed gap, and whether the approach extends to scenes with multiple interacting objects.
