## Problem

Test-time scaling (TTS) for Diffusion Multi-Modal Large Language Models (dMLLMs) remains underexplored, with conventional approaches performing linear search across trajectory exploration and iterative refinement dimensions at O(NT) computational cost while relying on external vision-language models for verification. This paper asks whether dMLLMs can self-verify their own generated images and whether an adaptive search algorithm can allocate compute to the most promising trajectories, eliminating both the quadratic cost and the external verifier dependency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263214/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 512×512 RGB image (CFG=4.0) as output. During TTS, the prompt conditions N parallel stochastic trajectories through T discrete denoising steps in a masked-token latent space; the final output is the single best-scoring image decoded from K surviving trajectories via a discrete tokenizer. The self-verification step consumes the intermediate token sequence and prompt to produce a binary yes/no alignment judgment, requiring only one additional forward pass per evaluation.

## Method

dMLLM-TTS introduces two complementary innovations. First, Self-Verified Feedback (SVF) repurposes the dMLLM's intrinsic image understanding as an in-loop verifier: the model is prompted with a yes/no question asking whether the generated image matches the text prompt, and the logit probability of 'yes' serves as the alignment score, eliminating external VLMs. Second, Hierarchical Trajectory Search (HTS) implements a three-stage coarse-to-fine strategy—Initial Stochastic Exploration (broad N-trajectory sampling for T_s steps), Progressive Hierarchical Thinning (geometric decay of active trajectories guided by SVF scores with local branching), and Final Refinement (deep denoising of K survivors)—achieving O(N+T) complexity versus O(NT) for linear search. The framework is validated on three dMLLMs (Lumina-DiMOO, MMaDA, Muddit) on GenEval, showing up to 6× efficiency gains over linear search while matching or surpassing SOTA text-to-image models.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, dMLLM-TTS with HTS (T=32, N=32, K=8) lifts Lumina-DiMOO from 0.78 to 0.92 (+17.9%), MMaDA from 0.51 to 0.66 (+25.5%), and Muddit from 0.53 to 0.67 (+26.4%) overall score. The TTS-enhanced Lumina-DiMOO (0.92) surpasses all SOTA text-to-image models including Qwen-Image (0.87), GPT-4o (0.84), and BAGEL (0.82). HTS achieves 5× faster convergence on Lumina-DiMOO and 6× faster on MMaDA/Muddit compared to the Linear Trajectory Search (LTS) baseline while reaching higher final scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SOTA text-to-image models, the TTS-enhanced Lumina-DiMOO (0.92 overall) outperforms Qwen-Image (0.87), GPT-4o (0.84), BAGEL (0.82), Janus-Pro (0.80), and FLUX.1-dev (0.67), with the largest margins on Position (+13 pts over Qwen-Image) and Attribute (+5 pts). Against the Linear Trajectory Search (LTS) baseline, HTS achieves 5× faster convergence on Lumina-DiMOO and 6× faster on MMaDA and Muddit while also reaching higher final GenEval scores, confirming both efficiency and quality gains. In the verifier comparison (Table 2), the proposed Self-Verified Feedback (SVF) slightly underperforms GPT-4o (0.95 vs. 0.92 on Lumina-DiMOO; 0.71 vs. 0.66 on MMaDA; 0.74 vs. 0.67 on Muddit) and VILA-Judge on MMaDA/Muddit, but matches or exceeds them on Lumina-DiMOO (0.92 vs. 0.90), at the cost of zero external model deployment. The gains are most pronounced on complex compositional dimensions: Counting improves by up to +56.2% (MMaDA) and Position by up to +92.3% (Muddit), indicating TTS primarily elevates the performance ceiling on challenging prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time scaling (TTS) paradigm established for continuous diffusion models (Sana 1.5, inference-time scaling via denoising steps) and autoregressive image generators (TTS-VAR, Lumina-MGPT) to the emerging class of discrete diffusion multi-modal LLMs (dMLLMs) such as Lumina-DiMOO, MMaDA, and Muddit. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) replacing external VLM verifiers (CLIP, VILA-Judge, GPT-4o) with a Self-Verified Feedback mechanism that repurposes the dMLLM's own image-understanding capability, and (2) substituting the standard O(NT) linear trajectory search with a Hierarchical Trajectory Search that exploits the coarse-to-fine structure of discrete diffusion to achieve O(N+T) complexity. The work is thus positioned at the intersection of discrete diffusion generation, unified multimodal understanding, and inference-time compute allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free recipe to close the gap between dMLLMs and leading T2I systems (e.g., TTS-enhanced Lumina-DiMOO reaches 0.92 GenEval, surpassing Qwen-Image and GPT-4o), while the 5–6× efficiency gain over linear search makes TTS feasible at scale. A notable limitation is that SVF still underperforms GPT-4o as a verifier (Table 2), suggesting that the self-verification ceiling is tied to the dMLLM's own visual comprehension, which remains weaker than frontier VLMs. Open questions include whether SVF quality improves as dMLLM understanding capabilities mature, whether the hierarchical search strategy generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the N:K ratio and decay schedule can be learned rather than hand-tuned.
