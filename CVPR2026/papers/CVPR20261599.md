## Problem

Existing multimodal image retrieval benchmarks focus on queries where target images are described directly or via simple composition with an input image, but no standardized dataset exists for the harder setting where retrieval requires 1–2 steps of logical reasoning (functional, temporal, or causal) to identify the correct target. This gap leaves the community without a testbed to evaluate whether current retrieval systems can go beyond surface-level matching to perform genuine multimodal inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261599/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query pairs an RGB input image with a short text query (median ~13 words, Flesch-Kincaid grade 8) that requires reasoning about the image to determine the target. The retriever must select relevant images from a shared pool of 35,803 candidates common to all 1,634 test queries, with up to 20 valid targets per query. Queries are partitioned into three reasoning categories—functional (object affordances), temporal (sequential/time-based relationships), and causal (cause-effect)—each demanding a distinct type of inference beyond object detection or keyword matching.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a 7-stage automated pipeline that transforms any seed image dataset into reasoning-intensive retrieval triplets: (1) an MLLM (Claude Sonnet 4.5) generates category-specific queries and target descriptions from seed images, (2) a filtration step validates multimodal dependency, reasoning complexity, and target specificity, (3) a dual-index retrieval strategy (SigLIP 2 image embeddings + Qwen3-Embedding text embeddings) retrieves up to 20 candidate target images per query, (4) a panel of five MLLM judges scores each triplet using Self-Consistency at temperature 0.85, (5) low-confidence triplets and their target images are removed to preserve retrieval completeness, (6) the retrieval pool is constructed as the union of all surviving targets, and (7) a Test Set Reliability (TSR) score based on tail-negativeness of retrieved candidates separates the evaluation set from training data. Evaluation of 11 models reveals that generative embedding models with explicit reasoning (UME-R1) substantially outperform discriminative encoders (VLM2Vec) and raw MLLMs, with the best model achieving only 46.53% R@20, and temporal reasoning proving hardest across all model families.

## Results

On RMIR, the best-performing model UME-R1-7B achieves 46.53% R@20 and 60.48% R@50 averaged across functional, temporal, and causal reasoning categories, revealing that reasoning-intensive multimodal retrieval remains substantially unsolved. Functional reasoning is the most tractable category (UME-R1-7B: 51.34% R@20), while temporal reasoning is the hardest (40.58% R@20, a 10.76 pp gap). Even the strongest model leaves over half of relevant images outside the top-20 retrieval, and the weakest evaluated model (Qwen3-VL-2B) scores only 6.77% R@20.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UME-R1-7B (generative embedding with reasoning) dominates all baselines, achieving 46.53% R@20 versus 38.68% for VLM2Vec-Qwen2-VL-7B (a 7.85 pp gap) and 36.89% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (a 9.64 pp gap), confirming that explicit reasoning-aware embedding generation is far more impactful than model scale alone. Notably, the smaller UME-R1-2B (38.09% R@20) outperforms the larger Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM (36.89% R@20) by 1.2 pp, demonstrating that specialized retrieval training with reasoning beats raw model capacity. Among discriminative embedding models, VLM2Vec-Qwen2-VL-7B is the strongest (38.68% R@20) but still trails UME-R1-7B by 7.85 pp, while VLM2Vec-V2 (31.01%) and VLM2Vec-Full (30.23%) show that standard contrastive retrieval training does not transfer to reasoning-intensive settings. Across all model families, functional reasoning is consistently the easiest category and temporal reasoning the hardest, with UME-R1-7B scoring 10.76 pp lower on temporal than functional queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends composed image retrieval (CIR) benchmarks like CIRR and FashionIQ, which require multimodal inputs (image + text → image) but only demand surface-level semantic matching, by introducing queries that require 1–2 steps of logical inference across functional, temporal, and causal reasoning. It positions itself as complementary to concurrent reasoning-intensive retrieval benchmarks (MRMR, MR²-Bench) by targeting everyday non-expert reasoning rather than domain-specific expert puzzles, and by releasing a scalable automated generation pipeline alongside the benchmark rather than relying solely on expert curation. The pipeline itself draws on established components—SigLIP 2 embeddings, Faiss clustering, dual-index retrieval, and Self-Consistency-style multi-judge MLLM validation—recombined into a novel multi-stage data construction workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RMIR fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluated whether retrieval models can perform multi-step reasoning over multimodal inputs to identify target images, and the 46.53% best R@20 confirms the task is far from solved. The finding that a 2B reasoning-aware generative embedding model (UME-R1-2B) outperforms a 7B general MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) suggests that explicit reasoning training in the embedding pipeline may matter more than raw model scale for retrieval, a direction worth further investigation. Open questions include whether the released 6,687 training queries actually improve downstream retrieval performance, whether the queries are truly multimodal-dependent (no unimodal shortcut baselines are reported), and whether the pipeline's reliance on a single MLLM family for judging introduces systematic blind spots.
