## Problem

Diffusion Large Language Models (DLLMs) suffer a severe quality-speed trade-off in parallel decoding due to a 'combinatorial contradiction': tokens sampled independently within the same step can form semantically inconsistent combinations (e.g., 'high house' instead of 'full house'). Existing parallel decoding schedules and revocable strategies mitigate but do not resolve this core consistency problem, as the purely discrete decoding process prevents tokens from perceiving one another during simultaneous sampling.

## Input / Output

ReMix is a training-free decoding wrapper applied to pre-trained DLLMs (LLaDA-8B-Instruct for text, MMaDA-8B-MixCoT for vision-language). It takes a text prompt or image-plus-text prompt and produces a fixed-length generated text sequence (256 tokens by default), using a semi-autoregressive strategy that partitions the sequence into 128-token blocks generated left-to-right. The method operates entirely at inference time by modifying the state-transition logic of the decoding loop, requiring no architectural changes or retraining.

## Method

ReMix introduces a Continuous Mixing State (C) as an intermediate between the masked state (M) and the decoded token state (T), enabling iterative refinement in continuous embedding space before discretization. The Mixing Rule (M→C) updates an undecoded position's embedding as a convex combination of the model's output distribution projected through the embedding matrix and the [MASK] embedding: y_i^emb ← βW^T p_θ(ŷ_i|X,Y) + (1−β)Emb_[MASK], allowing positions to accumulate and align tentative semantic intent across steps. A confidence-based Decoding Rule ((M,C)→T) collapses a position to a discrete token once its output probability exceeds τ_conf. A Rejection Rule (C→M) resets a position back to [MASK] when the Jensen-Shannon divergence between consecutive-step output distributions exceeds τ_rej, preventing error propagation from unstable continuous updates. This three-state loop (M↔C→T) is fully training-free and resolves mutual contradictions by letting undecoded positions communicate through shared continuous representations before any token is finalized.

## Results

On eight language benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval, MBPP, Countdown, Sudoku, ARC-E, ARC-C), ReMix improves accuracy over LLaDA by +0.60 to +14.05 points while reducing decoding steps by 2.5×–5.0× (e.g., GSM8K: 73.01→75.66 accuracy, 256→51.55 steps; ARC-C: 52.17→66.22 accuracy, 256→61.30 steps). End-to-end latency drops by 8–13 seconds, yielding 2.4×–4.6× speedup. On six multimodal benchmarks, ReMix improves over MMaDA by +0.00 to +3.00 points (CIDEr/accuracy) while reducing steps by 4.4×–8.5× and latency by 3.75×–7.52× (e.g., Flickr30k: 57.52→59.59 CIDEr, 108.02→14.37 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMix is compared against two baselines: LLaDA-8B-Instruct (Nie et al., 2025) for language tasks and MMaDA-8B-MixCoT (Yang et al., 2025) for multimodal tasks. Against LLaDA, ReMix wins on all eight language benchmarks in both accuracy and speed simultaneously—gains range from +0.60 (HumanEval) to +14.05 (ARC-C) in accuracy, with 2.5×–5.0× fewer decoding steps and 2.4×–4.6× lower latency. Against MMaDA, ReMix wins on five of six multimodal benchmarks in accuracy (Flickr30k +2.07 CIDEr, MathVista +3.00, MMMU +1.34, ScienceQA +0.65, AI2D +0.20) and ties on MathVision (12.83), while achieving 4.4×–8.5× step reduction and 3.75×–7.52× latency speedup across all six. Notably, ReMix is training-free and requires no architectural modification, making it a drop-in decoding strategy that strictly dominates both baselines on the quality–speed Pareto frontier.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMix builds directly on the masked diffusion LLM decoding framework (LLaDA, MMaDA) and the confidence-aware parallel decoding strategy from Fast-dLLM, which defines the M→T transition rule. It departs from prior acceleration work (WINO's revocable decoding, APD's auxiliary AR model) by introducing a continuous intermediate state C between the masked and token states, drawing on the broader literature of continuous-augmented discrete diffusion (CANDI, coevolutionary continuous-discrete diffusion) but repurposing it for inference-time refinement rather than training. The rejection mechanism via Jensen-Shannon divergence is a novel stability device not present in prior parallel decoding schedules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By breaking the quality-speed trade-off in DLLM parallel decoding with a training-free method, ReMix makes high-throughput diffusion-based generation practically viable without retraining, which is significant given the compute cost of large-scale diffusion LLMs. Open questions include whether the hyperparameters (β, τ_rej) transfer cleanly across model scales and architectures, whether the JS-divergence rejection adds meaningful per-step overhead at very long sequences, and whether the continuous mixing state can be extended to multi-step or hierarchical decoding schedules. The approach also raises the broader question of how much of the discrete diffusion training objective can be relaxed at inference time without degrading calibrated probabilities.
