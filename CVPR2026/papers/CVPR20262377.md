## Problem

OOD detection in spurious-correlation and fine-grained settings remains challenging because models over-rely on environmental features or struggle with subtle semantic differences between ID and OOD classes. Existing approaches for these hard cases depend on externally curated or foundation-model-synthesized outliers, introducing data dependencies and computational overhead. The paper asks whether a unified OOD detection training framework can handle spurious, fine-grained, and conventional settings using only the training data itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet-100 and fine-grained benchmarks) through ResNet-18/34/50 backbones. During training, each ID image is augmented with a gradient-attribution perturbation to produce a virtual outlier, which is fed through the same network alongside the original. At inference, the model outputs a class label via softmax and an OOD confidence score (e.g., MSP, EBO, or the proposed i-ODIN) that separates ID from OOD inputs at a chosen threshold.

## Method

ASCOOD synthesizes virtual outliers by adding the sparsified gradient of the true-class logit with respect to the input (∂z_c/∂x) to the ID image, which disproportionately destroys invariant features while preserving environmental features, yielding challenging near-manifold outliers. The training objective jointly minimizes cross-entropy on ID inputs and KL divergence toward a uniform distribution on virtual outliers, with a hyperparameter λ balancing the two terms. A key design choice is z-score normalization of the feature vector before the classifier, which provably constrains the feature norm to σ·√(m−1), mitigating overconfidence and ensuring the ID gradient dominates the OOD gradient during early training. The paper empirically shows that adding (rather than subtracting) the gradient is essential: addition amplifies the true-class logit while corrupting semantics, making the outlier genuinely challenging, whereas subtraction trivially increases uncertainty. An improved post-hoc scorer, i-ODIN, perturbs only the most significant color channel identified via pixel attribution rather than all channels as in standard ODIN.

## Results

ASCOOD achieves state-of-the-art OOD detection across all three challenging settings without external data. On the Waterbirds spurious benchmark, it reaches 24.19 FPR@95 / 93.64 AUROC, outperforming the nearest competitor (Relation) by ~59% in FPR@95. On the fine-grained Car benchmark, it achieves 20.02 FPR@95 / 95.79 AUROC, surpassing the third-best method (ReAct) by ~5 FPR@95 and ~3 AUROC points. In the large-scale ImageNet-100 setting, ASCOOD attains an average of 23.12 FPR@95 / 94.02 AUROC, exceeding DreamOOD (35.96/90.35) by 12.8 FPR@95 and 3.7 AUROC points, and achieves the highest AUROC (83.91) on SSB-Hard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASCOOD outperforms all 30+ competing methods across spurious, fine-grained, and conventional OOD detection settings. On Waterbirds (spurious), it beats Relation by ~59% in FPR@95 and substantially outperforms external-data methods OE and MixOE by 66% and 73% respectively, demonstrating that synthesized virtual outliers containing spurious features are more effective than real external OOD data. On CelebA, ASCOOD surpasses CIDER (the second-best) by ~15% in FPR@95. In fine-grained settings, ASCOOD exceeds GEN and RMDS by ~3 AUROC points on Aircraft and beats ReAct by ~5 FPR@95 / 3 AUROC on Car, where many training-regularization methods (RotPred, G-ODIN, VOS, LogitNorm, CIDER, NPOS) fail to even match the MSP baseline. On CIFAR-100, ASCOOD improves FPR@95 by ~16% over RotPred with a ~3 AUROC gain. In the large-scale ImageNet-100 setting, ASCOOD achieves 23.12/94.02 average FPR@95/AUROC, beating DreamOOD (35.96/90.35) by 12.8 FPR@95 and 3.7 AUROC, and CIDER (31.01/93.54) by 7.9 FPR@95, while also achieving the highest SSB-Hard AUROC (83.91 vs. DreamOOD's 83.30).

## Relation to Prior Work

ASCOOD extends the outlier exposure (OE) training paradigm [24] by replacing external OOD data with virtual outliers synthesized directly from ID samples via gradient attribution, and it improves upon ODIN [45] by restricting perturbation to invariant pixels (i-ODIN). It departs from prior spurious-correlation OOD work [56, 104] that analyzes the problem but does not synthesize training outliers, and from Kirby [34] and BackMix [87] that remove invariant features via inpainting, by showing that gradient addition (rather than subtraction or inpainting) produces more challenging and effective virtual outliers. The use of z-score normalization for constrained optimization builds on LogitNorm [88] and T2FNorm [65] but empirically demonstrates its superiority over L2 normalization for this specific joint training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external OOD datasets or computationally expensive foundation models for outlier synthesis, ASCOOD offers a practical, self-contained training recipe that unifies spurious, fine-grained, and conventional OOD detection in a single framework. The finding that gradient addition (not subtraction) to ID inputs creates more effective challenging outliers is a useful conceptual insight for the broader outlier-synthesis literature. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-based CNNs to vision transformers, whether the chicken-and-egg dependency on the in-training model for gradient attribution degrades in very early training or with weak initializations, and whether the method extends to non-image modalities.
