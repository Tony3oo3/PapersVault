## Problem

Cross-embodiment skill transfer for high-DoF dexterous hands is hindered by the conventional temporal-centric action representation (T, D_a), which treats each timestep's action as a monolithic fixed-dimensional vector. This formulation provides no natural mechanism to align manipulators with different joint counts or kinematic structures, and 2D visual inputs fail to capture the 3D spatial relations essential for precise dexterous manipulation.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a history of T_o raw 3D point clouds (each P_k ∈ R^{N×3}) and a natural language instruction, producing an action chunk A_t ∈ R^{D_a×T} where each row is the full temporal trajectory for one joint. The key structural difference from standard action chunking is that D_a (the number of joints) is the sequence length and varies across embodiments, while T (the prediction horizon) becomes the per-token feature dimension. At inference, the action is recovered in a single Euler step (1-NFE) by integrating the learned velocity field from noise to the clean action.

## Method

SAT reframes the action chunk from the conventional (T, D_a) temporal sequence to a (D_a, T) structural sequence, treating each joint's trajectory as a token in a variable-length, unordered sequence that a Transformer handles natively. The architecture comprises three components: an Observation Tokenizer using FPS and PointNet to extract local geometric and global scene tokens from point clouds (plus T5-encoded language), a Structural Action Tokenizer that compresses each joint's T-dimensional trajectory via a shared MLP and adds an Embodied Joint Codebook embedding (a sum of learnable embeddings indexed by embodiment ID, functional category, and rotation axis), and a DiT backbone with causal masking that predicts the action velocity field. Training uses a continuous-time flow matching objective, learning a velocity field that transports Gaussian noise to the action distribution conditioned on observations. The Embodied Joint Codebook is critical: without it, the unordered joint sequence has no identity, causing catastrophic failure (success drops from 0.71 to 0.01), while the functional category component proves most important for bridging the embodiment gap.

## Results

On 11 dexterous manipulation tasks across Adroit, DexArt, and Bi-DexHands simulation benchmarks, SAT achieves an average success rate of 0.71±0.04, outperforming all 2D and 3D baselines. Notably, SAT uses only 19.36M parameters (excluding T5), an order of magnitude smaller than 2D baselines (e.g., Diffusion Policy at 266.8M, UniAct at 1053M) and significantly more compact than 3D baselines (3DDP at 255.2M, ManiFlow at 218.9M). On real-world bimanual tasks, SAT also achieves the highest success rates across all 6 tasks, reaching 0.95 on Grasp basketball and 0.90 on Place block in plate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAT consistently outperforms all five baselines across all 11 simulation tasks. Against the strongest 3D baseline, 3D ManiFlow Policy [74] (avg. 0.66), SAT gains +5 points in average success (0.71), with the largest margin on Bi-DexHands (0.67 vs. 0.59). Compared to 3D Diffusion Policy [77] (avg. 0.63), SAT improves by 8 points, particularly on Bi-DexHands (0.67 vs. 0.55). Against 2D baselines, the gap is even wider: SAT surpasses UniAct [82] (avg. 0.50, 1053M params) by 21 points while using 54× fewer parameters, and outperforms Diffusion Policy [12] (avg. 0.42) by 29 points. HPT [68] (avg. 0.47) is the closest 2D competitor but still trails SAT by 24 points. On real-world bimanual tasks (Table 6), SAT also wins on all 6 tasks against both HPT and 3DDP, with the largest advantage on Push then grab box (0.35 vs. 0.05 and 0.15 respectively).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant temporal-centric action chunking paradigm (T, D_a) used by Diffusion Policy, HPT, UniAct, and 3D Diffusion Policy, instead reframing action chunks as a structural-centric sequence of joint-wise trajectories (D_a, T). It builds on flow matching (Lipman et al.) for the generative objective and DiT (Peebles & Xie) for the backbone, but the key conceptual contribution is treating the action dimension as variable sequence length, which natively accommodates heterogeneous embodiments through the Transformer's variable-length sequence handling. The Embodied Joint Codebook, which decomposes each joint into embodiment ID, functional category, and rotation axis, is a novel mechanism for encoding kinematic priors that has no direct analogue in prior cross-embodiment work, which typically relies on embodiment-specific input stems or unified action token vocabularies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structural-centric representation offers a principled path to scaling dexterous manipulation policies across heterogeneous high-DoF hands without architecture modifications, and the 19M-parameter model outperforming 266M–1053M baselines suggests the representation itself carries significant inductive bias. However, the real-world validation is limited to 6 tasks with 50 demonstrations each, and the paper acknowledges that single-camera occlusion and kinematic/contact-geometry mismatches remain unresolved failure modes. Open questions include whether the structural formulation extends to reinforcement learning (noted as future work), whether the codebook generalizes to embodiments outside the surveyed 10 hands, and whether the modest 5–10% gains over 3D baselines in simulation translate to larger advantages in more complex, long-horizon real-world settings.
