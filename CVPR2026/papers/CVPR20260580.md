## Problem

Existing Dropout regularization methods for 3D Gaussian Splatting under sparse views suffer from a neighbor compensation effect: because 3DGS uses highly overlapping Gaussians with similar local attributes, dropping a single Gaussian's opacity is easily compensated by spatially adjacent Gaussians, weakening the intended regularization. Additionally, these methods exclusively target opacity and overlook the contribution of high-degree spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients to overfitting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260580/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of RGB images (3–12 views) with known camera poses and produces a trained 3D Gaussian splatting model. A distinctive output property is that the trained model supports post-hoc SH truncation: at inference time, users can discard high-degree SH coefficients (e.g., retaining only degree 0) to reduce model size by up to 75% without retraining, trading a small quality loss for significant compression.

## Method

DropAnSH-GS introduces two complementary Dropout mechanisms during 3DGS training. First, an anchor-based spatial Dropout randomly selects a subset of Gaussians as anchors (sampling rate p_a) and simultaneously removes each anchor plus its k nearest Euclidean neighbors (k=10), creating contiguous 3D 'information voids' that prevent local compensation and force the model to rely on broader contextual cues. Second, an SH Dropout randomly selects Gaussians with probability p_sh and zeroes all SH coefficients above a maximum retained degree l_max, which is scheduled to increase from 0 to 2 over training, encouraging a coarse-to-fine appearance representation. Both mechanisms are implemented as simple multiplicative masks on opacity and SH coefficients respectively, requiring no changes to the standard L1+SSIM loss or the 3DGS rendering pipeline, and incur less than 3% additional training time.

## Results

On the LLFF dataset (3 views), DropAnSH-GS achieves PSNR 20.68, SSIM 0.724, and LPIPS 0.194, outperforming the strongest 3DGS-based baseline DropGaussian (20.33/0.709/0.201) and the vanilla 3DGS (19.17/0.646/0.268). On MipNeRF-360 (12 views) and Blender (8 views), the method reaches PSNR 19.93 and 25.50 respectively, surpassing all baselines while also enabling model compression: retaining only 0th-degree SH reduces model size to 33.8 Mb (MipNeRF-360) and 1.7 Mb (Blender) while still exceeding vanilla 3DGS quality. Training overhead is minimal, adding less than 2.8% to total training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DropAnSH-GS wins across all three datasets and all view counts against every baseline. On LLFF (3 views), it beats the closest competitor DropGaussian by +0.35 dB PSNR and the other Dropout method DropoutGS by +1.29 dB PSNR, confirming that anchor-based cluster dropout is more effective than independent Gaussian dropout. Against non-Dropout 3DGS methods, it surpasses CoR-GS (+0.32 dB), FSGS (+0.25 dB), and DNGaussian (+1.56 dB) in PSNR. On MipNeRF-360 (12 views), it achieves 19.93 dB PSNR vs. DropGaussian's 19.66 and CoR-GS's 19.42; on Blender (8 views), it reaches 25.50 dB vs. DropGaussian's 25.17 and CoR-GS's 24.31. Among NeRF-based methods, it outperforms the best (FreeNeRF) by +1.05 dB PSNR on LLFF 3-view. The method also demonstrates broad compatibility: when plugged into FSGS, CoR-GS, DNGaussian, and Scaffold-GS, it improves each by 0.3–1.2 dB PSNR (Table 3), showing it is a drop-in enhancement rather than a competing architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dropout regularization line for 3DGS initiated by DropGaussian and DropoutGS, which randomly zero out individual Gaussian opacities during training. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces independent per-Gaussian dropout with anchor-based cluster dropout, motivated by the observation that spatially correlated neighbors trivially compensate for a single dropped Gaussian, and (2) it extends dropout from the opacity attribute to high-degree spherical harmonic coefficients, a dimension prior methods entirely ignored. The method is positioned as a plug-in regularization module for the broader sparse-view NVS landscape (CoR-GS, FSGS, DNGaussian) rather than a new scene representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the neighbor compensation effect provides a useful diagnostic for why naive dropout underperforms in 3DGS, and the SH-truncation capability offers a practical post-training compression lever (e.g., 75% parameter reduction at SH0 with only marginal quality loss) that is orthogonal to existing 3DGS compression methods. However, the performance gains over the strongest dropout baseline (DropGaussian) are modest (~0.3 dB PSNR on LLFF 3-view), and the approach has only been validated on standard academic benchmarks with limited view counts. Open questions include whether anchor-based dropout scales to very large scenes with millions of Gaussians, whether the SH dropout schedule generalizes to dynamic or relightable 3DGS variants, and whether the neighbor-compensation analysis transfers to other explicit representations like 2DGS or hexplane-based methods.
