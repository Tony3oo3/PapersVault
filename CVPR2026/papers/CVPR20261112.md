## Problem

Existing image-text retrieval methods are trained on scenes with clearly separated foreground objects and fail dramatically on camouflaged scenes, where target objects are visually indistinguishable from the background. Cross-modal alignment in such scenarios remains largely unexplored, with no dedicated dataset, benchmark, or retrieval-specific architecture addressing the unique perceptual and semantic challenges of camouflaged objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261112/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 camouflaged RGB image and a natural-language description (10–45 words, typically ~25) as input, and outputs a ranked list of candidate images or captions for bidirectional retrieval. A COD model (ZoomNeXt) internally produces a pixel-wise mask of the camouflaged object, which is applied via element-wise multiplication to create a refined image representation fed into the expert branch. The CamoIT dataset provides ~10.5K image-text pairs across 237 categories with three annotation granularities (category label, object description, full image caption), of which Level 3 captions are used for retrieval.

## Method

CECNet employs a dual-branch visual encoder built on CLIP ViT-B/32: a global context branch encodes the original image to preserve scene semantics, while a camouflage-expert branch applies a COD mask to isolate the target object before encoding it with a parameter-shared ViT, preventing background feature contamination. A novel Confidence-Conditioned Graph Attention (C²GA) module fuses the two branches by constructing separate foreground-relevant and background-relevant graphs, where edge weights are modulated by per-patch camouflage confidence scores derived from the mask, ensuring that dominant background features cannot corrupt the purified object representation. Enhanced CLS tokens from both graphs are concatenated and integrated via adaptive gating fusion. Training uses a two-stage strategy—first training C²GA modules with frozen CLIP weights, then end-to-end fine-tuning—optimized with InfoNCE contrastive loss at temperature 0.05.

## Results

On the CamoIT benchmark (3,000 test samples), CECNet achieves R@1 of 45.8% (I2T) and 44.6% (T2I), with R@10 of 83.5% and 83.1% respectively, outperforming all seven retrained baselines. The strongest general retrieval model, D2S-VSE, reaches only 37.1% (I2T) and 35.5% (T2I) R@1, while fine-tuned CLIP achieves 41.3% and 41.1%. CECNet delivers an average R@1 improvement of 8.9% over D2S-VSE and 4.0% over CLIP, with the largest gap (21.5% average R@1) over the CLIP-based CUSA model. Pre-trained (non-retrained) SOTA methods drop to R@1 below 15% on CamoIT, confirming CA-ITR is not a simple domain-transfer task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CECNet outperforms all nine compared baselines on CamoIT across both I2T and T2I directions. Against the strongest retrained baseline D2S-VSE [39] (37.1% I2T R@1), CECNet gains 8.7 points; against fine-tuned CLIP [50] (41.3%), it gains 4.5 points. The gap is most pronounced over CUSA [22] (23.9% I2T R@1), where CECNet leads by 21.9 points, attributed to CUSA's frozen encoder failing to extract meaningful features for camouflaged scenes. Among BUTD-based methods, HREM [18] (34.3%) and CFM [60] (30.8%) are outperformed by 11.5 and 15.0 points respectively, while CHAN [46] and DBL [7] (evaluated only in pre-trained form in Table 1) score just 10.9% and 7.1% I2T R@1 on CamoIT. The local-alignment methods LAPS [19] (27.8%) and AVSE [40] (28.1%) trail CECNet by 18.0 and 17.7 points, confirming that fine-grained cross-attention alone is insufficient without explicit camouflaged-object perception. Notably, applying the CEC module to D2S-VSE and AVSE yields consistent gains (e.g., D2S-VSE+CEC reaches 39.0% vs. 37.1%), validating the dual-branch design as a plug-and-play enhancement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: camouflaged object detection (COD), which has evolved from pixel-wise segmentation to multimodal approaches, and image-text retrieval (ITR), which has progressed from global to local alignment strategies. It builds directly on CLIP (ViT-B/32) as the shared backbone for both text encoding and the global visual branch, and incorporates ZoomNeXt as a frozen COD expert to provide object-level guidance. The key departure from prior ITR work is the dual-branch architecture that explicitly isolates camouflaged object features from background context, motivated by the observation that single-encoder approaches (even with mask-based attention or learnable prompts) cannot adequately counteract background interference during encoding. The C²GA mechanism further departs from standard cross-attention or graph-based matching by using confidence scores to construct separate foreground and background subgraphs, preventing feature contamination between the two branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing CA-ITR and providing CamoIT (~10.5K multi-granularity samples across 237 categories), this paper gives the CSU community a dedicated benchmark that reveals camouflage-aware retrieval is fundamentally harder than domain transfer—SOTA methods drop from ~60% R@1 on standard datasets to under 15% on camouflaged scenes. The finding that frozen BUTD-based methods outperform trainable ViT-based approaches on camouflaged scenes challenges the assumption that holistic, flexible encoders are universally superior and validates the rationale for explicit object-level decomposition. However, the absolute performance (45.8% R@1) mirrors the early development phase of traditional ITR, leaving substantial room for improvement, particularly on the text side where the authors note that richer encoding strategies and loss designs remain unexplored. Open questions include whether the dual-branch paradigm generalizes to other visually challenging domains (e.g., transparent or occluded objects) and whether the COD expert can be replaced by learned, end-to-end alternatives.
