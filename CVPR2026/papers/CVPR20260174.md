## Problem

Existing cross-modal event-based action recognition methods treat event streams as generic stacked frames, discarding the intrinsic polarity information (positive for brightening, negative for darkening) that encodes the leading and trailing edges of motion. This simplification weakens polarity-specific spatiotemporal cues and leaves semantic alignment between event and text modalities incomplete, resulting in features with limited discriminative power for fine-grained action recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260174/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes event frames in either stacked or reconstructed format (T×3×H×W tensors) along with text prompts of the form 'A series of photos recording human action of [cls].' The PMC internally decouples the event stream into separate positive and negative polarity frame sequences before encoding, while the PMR feeds polarity-decoupled frames to an MLLM to generate free-form motion descriptions. At inference, only the base (non-polarity-enhanced) event and text features are used for classification via softmax over cosine similarity, producing a single action class label.

## Method

POKER comprises two synergistic modules. The Polarity Motion Capturer (PMC) splits the raw event stream into positive and negative polarity frames, then extracts three motion primitives via frame differencing: intra-polarity temporal correlations (consecutive-frame differences within each polarity) and inter-polarity spatial correlations (pointwise difference between the two polarities at the same timestep). These primitives are combined through a learnable routing gate that adaptively weights their contributions based on the original event frame. The Polarity Motion Reasoner (PMR) employs a progressive three-step prompt (Observation → Thinking → Synthesis) to guide a multimodal LLM in generating polarity-aware textual motion descriptions from the decoupled frames. Training jointly optimizes a contrastive InfoNCE loss between base event and text features and a polarity alignment loss that minimizes the L2 distance between class-mean polarity-enhanced visual and textual features, reducing intra-class variance while enforcing cross-modal coupling.

## Results

POKER achieves top-1 accuracy of 69.82% on SeAct, 96.49% on DVS Action, and 62.06% on THU E-ACT-50-CHL using stacked event frames, improving over the EventBind baseline by +2.58%, +1.76%, and +1.29% respectively. With reconstructed frames, POKER reaches 76.72% (SeAct), 99.60% (DVS Action), and 63.72% (THU E-ACT-50-CHL), yielding gains of +2.59%, +1.36%, and +2.58% over EventBind. In zero-shot evaluation on DVS Action, POKER achieves 99.6% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming EventGPT (6.0%) and EZSR (84.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POKER is built directly on the EventBind [51] cross-modal framework and consistently outperforms it across all three datasets under both stacked and reconstructed frame settings, with the largest gain of +2.59% on SeAct (reconstructed) and +2.58% on THU E-ACT-50-CHL (reconstructed). Against other cross-modal event-text methods, POKER (recon) surpasses ExACT [52] by +9.48% on SeAct and +4.77% on DVS Action, and beats SAMPLE [30] by +6.93% on SeAct; however, EMP [2] retains a narrow 0.20% edge on DVS Action (99.80% vs. 99.60%). POKER also outperforms all single-modal event-based methods (e.g., SpikMamba [4] at 96.28% on DVS Action, EventMamba [25] at 59.40% on THU E-ACT-50-CHL) and RGB+text CLIP-based approaches (M2-CLIP [31], EZ-CLIP [1]) by substantial margins. In the zero-shot setting on DVS Action, POKER achieves 99.6% top-1 accuracy, far exceeding EventGPT [19] (6.0%) and EZSR [45] (84.7%), confirming that polarity-guided MLLM reasoning is far more effective than direct zero-shot inference on raw event data.

## Relation to Prior Work

POKER builds directly on EventBind (ECCV 2024), a cross-modal event-text alignment framework, by inserting polarity-aware motion enhancement into both the visual and textual branches. It departs from prior cross-modal event methods (ExACT, EZSR, EventGPT) that treat event streams as undifferentiated stacked frames, arguing that the positive/negative polarity split encodes complementary leading-edge and trailing-edge motion cues that are lost during aggregation. The textual side borrows from the knowledge-enhanced MLLM literature, using a progressive reasoning prompt to elicit polarity-grounded motion descriptions rather than generic frame-by-frame captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that decoupling polarities before encoding preserves directional motion information that frame stacking suppresses—is physically well-motivated and offers a simple, plug-and-play enhancement to existing event-text pipelines. However, the gains are modest (1.3–2.6% over EventBind) and evaluated on three relatively small benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse datasets or to open-vocabulary settings. The reliance on an external MLLM for the PMR component also raises practical concerns about inference cost and the reproducibility of generated motion descriptions across model versions.
