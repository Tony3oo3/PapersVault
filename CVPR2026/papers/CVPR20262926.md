## Problem

Current multi-modal image fusion methods optimize statistical correlations between modalities from observational data, capturing dataset-induced spurious associations that degrade under distribution shifts. These correlation-centric approaches cannot distinguish genuine cross-modal complementarity from coincidental co-occurrences, producing brittle models that fail when deployment conditions differ from training. The paper addresses this gap by introducing intervention-based reasoning to actively probe and identify robust cross-modal dependencies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262926/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a visible RGB image (3×H×W) and a single-channel infrared image (1×H×W) as input and produces a single-channel fused image (1×H×W). During training, the network simultaneously processes four intervention variants—complementary masking, random masking, infrared dropout, and visible dropout—alongside the clean pair, with 256×256 patches and a batch size of 16. The model is trained unsupervised on MSRS and validated on RoadScene, then evaluated zero-shot on TNO, M³FD, and a Harvard medical MRI-PET/SPECT dataset without fine-tuning.

## Method

The framework employs a U-Net-like Siamese architecture with weight-sharing encoders and a Causal Feature Integrator (CFI) in the decoder. CFI performs bidirectional cross-modal attention (with pooled keys/values for efficiency) to exchange complementary information, then applies a learnable invariance gate that blends cross-modal complementary features with local modal features based on their stability across interventions. Training uses three complementary interventions—spatially disjoint complementary masking, identical-region random masking, and complete modality dropout—each testing a different hypothesis about modal relationships. The loss combines fusion fidelity (L1 + Laplacian gradient), intervention consistency (penalizing gate-selected region differences under perturbations, with entropy regularization for binary-like gates), and modal necessity (maximizing divergence from single-modal outputs) to enforce balanced, intervention-stable feature learning.

## Results

On the three IVIF benchmarks, the proposed method achieves the best AG (5.128/4.129/5.276) and SF (5.132/4.743/6.105) on TNO, MSRS, and M³FD respectively, along with the highest PSNR on MSRS (66.02) and M³FD (62.13). On downstream tasks, it attains the best mAP of 0.821 for object detection (YOLOv5 on M³FD) and the best mIoU of 0.747 for semantic segmentation (MSRS). In zero-shot cross-sensor transfer to the Harvard medical dataset (MRI-PET/SPECT), it achieves the highest AG (7.681) and SF (10.06) without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the highest AG and SF on all three IVIF benchmarks, with particularly large margins over the strongest structural competitors: on TNO, AG of 5.128 surpasses MUFusion (4.713) by 8.8%, and on M³FD, SF of 6.105 exceeds Conti (5.545) by 10.1%. On PSNR, it leads on MSRS (66.02 vs. SAGE's 65.59) and M³FD (62.13 vs. LRRNet's 62.95 is actually a loss here—LRRNet scores 62.95), while on TNO it edges out MUFusion (62.06 vs. 61.73). The method's Qabf is competitive but not always best: on MSRS, DCEvo (0.620) and Conti (0.570) outperform it (0.545), suggesting the intervention framework trades some pixel-level information preservation for structural coherence. On downstream object detection, it achieves the best mAP (0.821) over SAGE (0.815) and Conti (0.809), with the largest per-class gains on Car (0.916) and Bus (0.894). For semantic segmentation, it attains the best mIoU (0.747) over A²RNet (0.740) and Conti/LUT-Fuse/IGNet (0.736), with the most notable advantage on the challenging Curbside class (0.584 vs. 0.564 for LUT-Fuse). In the zero-shot medical transfer setting, it outperforms DCEvo (the strongest baseline) on AG (7.681 vs. 7.391) and SF (10.06 vs. 9.585) while matching its PSNR (64.33 vs. 63.82), confirming that intervention-stable features generalize across sensor domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes causal intervention concepts from Pearl's hierarchy—originally developed for statistical inference and domain generalization—to the image fusion setting, where prior work (CDDFuse, SwinFusion, DCEvo, SAGE) has treated fusion as a purely correlation-maximization problem. The CFI module extends standard cross-modal attention (as in Transformer-based fusion architectures) with a learnable invariance gate that explicitly weights features by their stability under structured perturbations, a mechanism absent from prior fusion networks. The three masking strategies (complementary, random, modality dropout) are novel instantiations of do-calculus-style interventions tailored to the unsupervised, multi-scale nature of fusion, rather than direct adaptations of counterfactual data augmentation used in multimodal sentiment analysis or low-light enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real and underexplored failure mode in fusion: models that memorize dataset-specific co-occurrences rather than learning genuine cross-modal complementarity, which manifests as brittle generalization under distribution shift. The zero-shot IVIF-to-medical-imaging transfer (without fine-tuning) provides concrete evidence that intervention-stable features capture modality-agnostic fusion principles, though the causal framing remains somewhat loose—the 'interventions' are fixed random masks rather than learned do-operators, and the invariance gate is a single 3×3 convolution, leaving open whether the mechanism truly identifies causal structure or merely acts as a learned spatial regularizer. The ablation revealing that removing all interventions yields higher PSNR but lower AG/SF highlights an unresolved tension between pixel fidelity and structural richness that the current loss weighting (α=0.1, β=0.05) only partially resolves.
