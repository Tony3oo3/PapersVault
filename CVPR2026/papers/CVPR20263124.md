## Problem

Solving minimal problems in computer vision (systems of multivariate polynomial equations) via Gröbner-basis or resultant-based methods typically requires explicit matrix inversion in the online solver, which is computationally expensive and numerically unstable near singular or ill-conditioned configurations. This paper eliminates that inversion bottleneck by reconstructing the resultant determinant polynomial numerically from sampled evaluations rather than through symbolic expansion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263124/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver takes numerical coefficient values of a system of m multivariate polynomial equations in n unknowns (m ≥ n), derived from point correspondences in camera geometry problems. Offline, it determines the determinant polynomial degree, identifies valid rank-deficient submatrix pairs via a GCD-based coprimality test, and precomputes the monomial-ratio index pairs. Online, it produces all r valid solutions (counting multiplicities) to the polynomial system, with spurious roots filtered by back-substitution residual evaluation, retaining only the top-r solutions by normalized residual magnitude.

## Method

The method constructs a hidden-variable resultant matrix M(x₁) of size N×N by treating one variable as a constant, then reconstructs det(M(x₁)) as a univariate polynomial without symbolic expansion or matrix inversion. Specifically, the determinant is evaluated at k+1 uniformly spaced points on the complex unit circle, and the polynomial coefficients are recovered via IFFT in O(k log k) operations, exploiting the equivalence between the Vandermonde system at roots of unity and the DFT matrix. A tensor-wise FFT along the polynomial-degree axis evaluates all matrix entries simultaneously across sampling points, replacing sequential polynomial evaluations. The resulting univariate polynomial is solved via the companion-matrix eigenvalue method, and remaining variables are recovered by Cramer's rule on a rank-1 deficient submatrix, with a GCD-based criterion (using random prime specialization) ensuring robust submatrix identification under floating-point noise.

## Results

Across 14 diverse minimal problems (relative pose, absolute pose, triangulation, PnP), the proposed FFT-based solver achieves a 0% failure rate on all problems, compared to up to 1.24% for SparseR and 1.04% for GAPS. Mean log₁₀ normalized residuals are consistently comparable or slightly better than both baselines (e.g., −13.17 vs −12.71 for SparseR and −12.62 for GAPS on the 8-point relative pose problem). On the five smallest-scale problems (≤19 solutions), the solver is ~30% faster on average (10–50% reduction) than both baselines, but runtime grows approximately linearly with the number of roots, making it slower than SparseR on larger problems (e.g., 9.52 ms vs 1.10 ms on the 24-solution 9-point problem).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to SparseR [1], the proposed solver eliminates all solver failures (0% vs. up to 1.24% on the 9-point relative pose problem) and achieves equal or better mean log₁₀ residuals on 10 of 14 problems, though SparseR is faster on larger problems (e.g., 1.10 ms vs. 9.52 ms on the 24-solution 9pt problem). Against GAPS [29], the proposed method again achieves 0% failures versus up to 1.04% for GAPS, and matches or exceeds GAPS on mean residuals for most problems (e.g., −11.24 vs. −9.96 on refractive P5P). On runtime, the proposed solver is faster than both baselines on the five smallest problems (≤19 solutions), with a 10–50% speedup, but becomes slower as the number of roots grows due to per-root back-substitution. Overall, the method trades some runtime on large-scale problems for guaranteed numerical stability without any matrix inversion step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse resultant framework of Bhayani et al. [1,2] and Heikkilä [18] by replacing the symbolic determinant expansion and explicit matrix inversion steps with a purely numerical IFFT-based interpolation scheme. While prior resultant-based solvers (SparseR, GAPS) construct the resultant matrix and then require inverting a submatrix to form an eigenvalue problem, this work sidesteps inversion entirely by sampling the determinant on the complex unit circle and recovering coefficients via the inverse DFT. The tensor-wise FFT for batch-evaluating the resultant matrix at all sampling points simultaneously is a new implementation detail not present in prior sparse resultant or Gröbner-basis solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Eliminating matrix inversion removes a well-known source of numerical instability in minimal solvers, and the zero-failure-rate results across 14 diverse pose estimation problems validate this design choice. However, the runtime advantage is limited to small-scale problems (≤19 solutions); for larger problems the linear scaling with root count makes the solver slower than SparseR and GAPS, and GPU parallelization is left as future work. The method is most impactful in RANSAC pipelines where thousands of minimal solver calls are made and even small per-call speedups and stability gains compound. An open question is whether the IFFT-based approach can be extended to handle the larger resultant matrices (N > 50) that arise in more complex minimal problems without the current linear-in-roots bottleneck.
