## Problem

Sparse autoencoders trained on vision encoders suffer from two critical limitations: only ~19% of SAE neurons exhibit both high interpretability and high steerability, and the SAE latent space fails to represent 27–45% of concepts from standard ImageNet-derived benchmarks despite being trained on that data. This means practitioners cannot rely on unsupervised SAEs to discover or control user-specified concepts, limiting their practical utility for downstream steering and explanation tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes intermediate activations (d-dimensional vectors) from a pretrained vision encoder (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14-336, layer 22) along with a user-specified text-based concept set. The concept set is automatically filtered to retain only concepts absent from the retained SAE neurons, avoiding redundancy. Outputs include sparse latent codes (retained SAE neurons plus CB neurons with top-k=5 sparsity), reconstructed activations, and steered downstream outputs—either single-word text responses from LLaVA or generated images from UnCLIP—enabling both interpretability scoring and causal intervention.

## Method

CB-SAE is a post-hoc framework that first prunes the M least interpretable and steerable neurons from a pretrained Matryoshka Batch Top-k SAE (using CLIP-Dissect interpretability scores and LLaVA-based steerability scores), then trains a lightweight concept bottleneck autoencoder alongside the frozen, pruned SAE. The CB module consists of a linear encoder E_cb, a linear decoder D_cb, and a top-k activation function, trained with three objectives: a reconstruction MSE loss L_r to recover fidelity lost by pruning, a cosine-cubed similarity interpretability loss L_int against CLIP zero-shot pseudo-labels (updating only E_cb), and a cyclic reconstruction steerability loss L_st that passes the reconstructed latent back through E_cb and matches the result to the same pseudo-labels (updating only D_cb). The three objectives are optimized alternately with separate Adam optimizers rather than loss weighting, and the retained SAE weights remain frozen throughout, preserving the unsupervised discovery of high-utility features while the CB module explicitly encodes user-specified concepts.

## Results

CB-SAE achieves average gains of +32.1% in interpretability and +14.5% in steerability over the baseline SAE across all downstream models and metrics. For LLaVA-1.5-7B, CB-SAE improves CLIP-Dissect interpretability from 0.154 to 0.244 and Unit-Vector steerability from 0.198 to 0.261. For UnCLIP image generation, interpretability (CD) rises from 0.058 to 0.092 while steerability remains strong (0.642→0.659 Unit-Vec). The monosemanticity score also improves consistently, e.g., 0.517→0.556 for LLaVA-1.5-7B and 0.540→0.594 for UnCLIP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is the Matryoshka Batch Top-k SAE from Pach et al. [33], trained with identical architecture and data (ImageNet-1K, expansion factor 64, 65,536 neurons on CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 layer 22). CB-SAE outperforms this SAE on every metric across all three downstream models: for LLaVA-1.5-7B, interpretability (CD) improves by +58.4% (0.154→0.244) and steerability (Unit-Vec) by +31.8% (0.198→0.261); for LLaVA-MORE, CD improves by +50.0% (0.194→0.291) with modest steerability gains (+7.3% Unit-Vec); for UnCLIP, CD improves by +58.6% (0.058→0.092) while steerability gains are smaller (+2.6% Unit-Vec, +1.5% White Image). The paper also reports that CB neurons individually achieve higher interpretability (0.323 CD) than all SAE neurons (0.154 CD), while retained SAE neurons retain higher steerability (0.263 Unit-Vec) than CB neurons (0.231 Unit-Vec), confirming the complementary strengths of the two components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: sparse autoencoders for unsupervised feature discovery in vision models (Pach et al. [33], Zaigrajew et al. [55]) and concept bottleneck models for supervised, interpretable control (Kulkarni et al. [21], Oikarinen et al. [31]). It departs from prior SAE work by demonstrating that interpretability and steerability are largely orthogonal properties—only ~19% of neurons satisfy both—and from prior CBM work by combining supervised concept alignment with unsupervised SAE discovery in a single post-hoc framework. The closest concurrent work, AlignSAE [52], also introduces supervised concepts into SAEs but retains all SAE neurons and uses an orthogonality loss, whereas CB-SAE explicitly prunes low-utility neurons and only adds concepts absent from the retained SAE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CB-SAE provides the first practical mechanism for users to guarantee that specific concepts are present and steerable in a vision model's latent space, addressing a key gap that limits SAEs' utility for downstream control tasks. The finding that 27–45% of ImageNet-related concepts are simply absent from a 65K-neuron SAE trained on ImageNet is a sobering result for anyone relying on SAEs for comprehensive concept coverage. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP encoders and single-layer interventions, whether the task-agnostic cyclic reconstruction loss for steerability is optimal compared to task-specific objectives, and whether the pruning threshold and number of retained neurons can be adapted automatically rather than set manually.
