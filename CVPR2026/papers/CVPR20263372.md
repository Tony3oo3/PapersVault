## Problem

Existing VLA-based end-to-end driving models face two structural limitations: processing all surround-view cameras at every timestep creates severe token redundancy and computational overhead, and a single unified policy head averages across diverse driving behaviors, degrading performance on rare but safety-critical maneuvers like emergency braking or aggressive turns. The Mixture-of-Experts paradigm, proven effective in LLMs, had not been systematically applied to both the perception and action stages of autonomous driving.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two consecutive front-view RGB images, a fixed text prompt (e.g., 'Please predict future trajectory'), and a continuous vehicle-state vector (speed, yaw rate, past trajectory). The Vision MoE router additionally receives a future goal waypoint from the route planner and dynamically selects one extra camera view from up to six available views (front, front-left, front-right, back, back-left, back-right) before the expensive VLM backbone computation, so unselected views are skipped entirely. The output is a continuous future trajectory generated by a flow-matching decoder, evaluated in closed-loop CARLA simulation via driving score, success rate, efficiency, comfort, and per-ability metrics.

## Method

DriveMoE extends the π₀ VLA framework (PaliGemma backbone + flow-matching action module) into autonomous driving as Drive-π₀, then adds two MoE modules. The Scene-Specialized Vision MoE uses a lightweight router that maps the front-view embedding and goal waypoint to a softmax distribution over camera views, trained with cross-entropy against manually annotated binary view-selection labels; learnable per-view positional embeddings preserve spatial relations among selected views. The Skill-Specialized Action MoE replaces each FFN in the flow-matching decoder with an ensemble of shared and non-shared expert FFNs, where a router (either token-level or trajectory-level) sparsely activates Top-1/Top-2 experts per input; the trajectory-level variant averages the full token sequence before routing and is supervised with driving-skill labels via cross-entropy, while noise is injected into the router to prevent expert collapse. Training proceeds in two stages: first with ground-truth expert selection to stabilize learning, then with router-driven selection to build robustness against routing errors at inference.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, DriveMoE (Trajectory-Level) achieves a Driving Score of 74.22 and a Success Rate of 48.64%, representing a 22.8% improvement in DS and 62.1% improvement in SR over the Drive-π₀ baseline (DS 55.85, SR 30.00%). The Token-Level variant achieves DS 66.94 and SR 35.45%. On the open-loop metric, DriveMoE (Token-Level) reports an average L2 error of 0.96 m, while the Trajectory-Level variant reports 1.01 m, compared to 1.13 m for Drive-π₀.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveMoE (Traj-Level) achieves the highest Driving Score (74.22) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest competitor Raw2Drive (71.36) by 2.86 points and DiffAD (67.92) by 6.30 points. In terms of Success Rate, Raw2Drive edges ahead slightly (50.24% vs. 48.64%), but DriveMoE substantially outperforms all other baselines, including DriveMamba-L (37.73%), DiffAD (38.64%), and DriveTrans (35.01%). Against the VLA-based Drive-π₀ baseline, DriveMoE improves DS by 18.37 points and SR by 18.64 points, validating the benefit of both the Vision MoE and Action MoE modules. On the multi-ability benchmark (Table 1), DriveMoE (Traj-Level) leads in Merging (34.67%), Overtaking (40.00%), Emergency Braking (65.45%), and Traffic Sign (59.44%), with a mean ability score of 47.91% versus 42.08% for DriveAdapter and 38.79% for DiffAD. The Token-Level variant trails the Trajectory-Level variant by 7.28 DS points, confirming that trajectory-level expert routing better captures scenario-level driving skills.

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveMoE extends the embodied-AI VLA model π0 into autonomous driving (as Drive-π0) and then augments it with two Mixture-of-Experts modules, drawing on the MoE success in LLMs (Mixtral, DeepSeekMoE) but applying it to novel domains: camera-view selection in perception and behavior-specific trajectory generation in planning. It departs from prior VLA-based driving systems (DriveLM, CARLLaVA, LMDrive) that either process all surround views indiscriminately or compress them via query-based modules that lose spatial structure, and from single-policy-head planners that suffer mode-averaging on rare maneuvers. The flow-matching trajectory decoder is inherited directly from π0, while the sparse Top-K routing and noise-injected router follow DeepSeekMoE conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to integrate MoE into both the perception and action pipelines of an end-to-end driving model, offering a concrete recipe for tackling multi-view redundancy and long-tail behavior coverage simultaneously. The trajectory-level router outperforming the token-level variant, and the finding that scaling experts to one-per-scenario (44) degrades performance due to load imbalance, provide practical guidance for future MoE-based driving architectures. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CARLA simulation to real-world deployment, whether the manual router supervision (camera-view labels, skill labels) can be replaced by self-supervised or learned signals at scale, and whether the two-stage teacher-forcing-to-adaptive training protocol transfers to other MoE settings.
