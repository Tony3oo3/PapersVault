## Problem

Video diffusion models like Wan2.2 (27B parameters) are too large and slow for consumer GPUs, and while efficient attention has shifted the bottleneck to linear layers, existing 4-bit weight-activation quantization methods (e.g., SVDQuant) rely on static per-channel smoothing that fails for video because activation outliers vary dramatically across denoising timesteps. The paper asks whether there is a lightweight, dynamic way to handle these timestep-varying activation outliers without the prohibitive cost of on-the-fly SVD.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261476/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the activation tensors inside DiT transformer blocks of video diffusion models, reshaping the flattened 1-D token sequence back into its native 3-D spatiotemporal structure (T×H×W×C) before quantization. It is evaluated on Wan2.2 generating 720p 5-second videos (77 frames T2V, 81 frames I2V) and LTX-Video generating 512p 8-second videos (121 frames T2V), with inputs being text prompts or reference images plus text prompts and outputs being the corresponding generated video clips.

## Method

DeltaQuant exploits spatiotemporal activation similarity by partitioning activation tensors into local 3-D cubes (e.g., 4×2×4 or 4×1×4 tokens), computing each cube's mean as a high-precision FP8 'core token,' and quantizing only the small residual differences ('delta tokens') to 4 bits, which have far lower magnitude and fewer outliers than the original activations. The linear layer output is then computed as a sum of an FP8 tensor-core GEMM (core × weight) and an FP4 tensor-core GEMM (delta × weight), adding only ~3% overhead over pure W4A4. For weights, SVDQuant's low-rank decomposition is incorporated to further absorb outliers. A fused CUDA kernel integrates core-token generation, W4A8 GEMM, and W4A4 GEMM into a single pass, and an adaptive cube-size strategy uses smaller cubes (16 tokens) for the first 25–30% of denoising steps where visual quality is most sensitive, and larger cubes (64 tokens) for the remainder.

## Results

On Wan2.2-I2V-A14B, DeltaQuant (W4A4) achieves LPIPS 0.128 and PSNR 23.2, outperforming the W4A16 GGUF 4 baseline (LPIPS 0.137, PSNR 23.2) and the SVDQuant W4A4 baseline (LPIPS 0.165, PSNR 21.9). On LTX-Video T2V-13B, DeltaQuant reaches LPIPS 0.159 and PSNR 21.6, again surpassing GGUF 4 (LPIPS 0.169, PSNR 20.9). In terms of efficiency, DeltaQuant reduces model size by 2.9× and peak memory by 2.3× over BF16, and when combined with LightX2V 4-step LoRA, Radial Attention, and Sage Attention, achieves a total 111.8× end-to-end speedup (43.1 s vs. 4818 s) on an RTX 5090 while maintaining Vision Reward of 0.148 (vs. 0.145 for BF16).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeltaQuant (W4A4) consistently outperforms all W4A4/W4A6 baselines on similarity metrics. Against SVDQuant, DeltaQuant reduces LPIPS from 0.165 to 0.128 (I2V), 0.354 to 0.338 (T2V), and 0.193 to 0.159 (LTX), while improving PSNR by 1.3, 0.2, and 1.5 dB respectively. Against SmoothQuant, the gains are even larger (e.g., LPIPS 0.188→0.128 on I2V), confirming that static per-channel smoothing fails under video's dynamic activation outliers. DeltaQuant also surpasses the W4A16 GGUF 4 weight-only baseline on LPIPS and PSNR across all three models (e.g., 0.128 vs. 0.137 LPIPS on I2V; 21.6 vs. 20.9 PSNR on LTX), while achieving 1.9–3.6× lower single-step latency. The two video-specific quantization methods S²Q-VDiT and Q-VDiT perform substantially worse (LPIPS 0.366 and 0.575 on I2V), with Q-VDiT even producing negative Vision Reward (−0.085), indicating severe quality collapse. On Vision Reward, DeltaQuant is competitive with GGUF 4 (0.143 vs. 0.141 on I2V) and slightly below it on LTX (0.135 vs. 0.141), but matches or exceeds the BF16 original on I2V (0.143 vs. 0.145) and T2V (0.083 vs. 0.091).

## Relation to Prior Work

DeltaQuant extends SVDQuant's 4-bit weight-activation quantization from image to video diffusion models, where the key challenge is that activation outliers vary dramatically across denoising timesteps, breaking SVDQuant's offline-calibrated static smoothing (SmoothQuant-style). The paper's core departure is replacing static per-channel smoothing with an on-the-fly spatiotemporal mean subtraction—partitioning activations into local 3D cubes and quantizing only the small delta tokens to 4 bits while keeping the mean (core) token in FP8—drawing a direct analogy to how video codecs like H.264 exploit inter-frame redundancy. For weights, it retains SVDQuant's low-rank decomposition to absorb residual outliers. The method is also positioned as complementary to (not competing with) efficient attention (Radial Attention, Sage Attention) and few-step distillation (LightX2V), which shift the bottleneck to linear layers that DeltaQuant then accelerates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeltaQuant makes 27B-parameter video diffusion models (Wan2.2) deployable on a single consumer RTX 5090 (32 GB) by cutting peak memory by 2.3× and model size by 2.9×, while delivering a 3× additional speedup on top of existing attention optimizations for a combined 111.8× end-to-end acceleration—turning a 75-minute generation into 43 seconds. This is practically significant because it removes the CPU-offloading bottleneck that has kept large video models out of reach of consumer hardware. Open questions include whether the spatiotemporal similarity assumption holds for all video architectures (e.g., autoregressive or streaming models with weaker temporal locality), whether the fixed cube-size heuristic (16 for early timesteps, 64 for later) can be learned or adapted per-layer, and whether the approach extends to other modalities with analogous local redundancy (e.g., 3D point clouds, audio). The paper also notes the broader dual-use concern of making high-quality video generation more accessible.
