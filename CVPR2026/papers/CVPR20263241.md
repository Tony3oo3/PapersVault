## Problem

Existing e-commerce advertising generation pipelines rely on multiple independent models (a T2I model for images and an LLM for texts) that process modalities in isolation, preventing cross-modal collaboration. Moreover, personalization efforts optimize toward aggregate click-through rates that reflect only average group preferences, failing to capture individual user interests from noisy, multi-modal historical click behaviors that contain both sample-level irrelevance and modality-specific preference shifts.

## Input / Output

The model receives a structured instruction containing the product description and selling points, a product transparent image resized to 384×384, and N=10 sampled historical image-text pairs (historical images at 128×128). It jointly outputs an advertising text (demarcated by <text>/</text> tokens) followed by an advertising image (demarcated by <image>/</image> tokens), where the image is decoded from discrete VQ-GAN tokens into pixel space. The generation is conditioned on a foreground control sequence extracted from the transparent image and, for personalization, on fused preference tokens appended as style constraints in the instruction.

## Method

Uni-AdGen is built on Janus-Pro 7B and unifies image and text generation in a single autoregressive next-token prediction framework, with a VQ-GAN image decoder and a standard text decoder. For controllable image generation, a DINOv2-based foreground perception module encodes the product transparent image into visual embeddings that are injected via element-wise addition into every 4th decoder layer; for text, instruction tuning with diverse templates constrains generation to use only provided selling points. For personalization, a coarse-to-fine preference understanding module first performs Product Similarity Sampling (importance sampling weighted by semantic similarity between historical and target product texts) to reduce sample-level noise, then applies two modality-specific Transformer relevance extractors that compute token-level importance via cosine similarity between input and output embeddings, followed by differentiable Gumbel-Softmax top-K masking to suppress modal-level noise. The surviving visual and textual tokens are fused through FFN residual blocks and a final Transformer encoder, and the resulting fused tokens are inserted into the instruction as style placeholders. Training uses LoRA (rank 12, factor 32) on the base model with full fine-tuning of the foreground perception and preference extraction modules, optimizing a weighted sum of text and image cross-entropy losses.

## Results

On the general advertisement generation task (Table 1), Uni-AdGen achieves the best ImageReward score (−1.244) and second-best PickScore (21.002) among all image baselines, while also producing competitive text with m-ROUGE 0.654 (best) and m-BLEU 0.551 (second). On the personalized generation task (Table 2), the full model achieves PBS 0.634, BLEU 0.435, and ROUGE 0.662, outperforming all baselines. The ablation shows progressive gains: adding random history improves BLEU from 0.225 to 0.427, product similarity sampling further lifts PBS to 0.622, and multimodal preference extraction reaches the final PBS 0.634.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For image generation, Uni-AdGen achieves the best ImageReward (−1.244 vs. −1.281 for Flux-Fill and −1.332 for PosterMaker) and second-best PickScore (21.002 vs. 21.081 for the single-modal Ours-image variant), but trails ReliableAd (95.20) and PosterMaker (90.80) on human evaluation, indicating a slight trade-off in perceived usability. For text generation, Uni-AdGen's joint model (m-BLEU 0.551, m-ROUGE 0.654) is competitive with Qwen3 (0.562, 0.652) and DeepSeek-R1 (0.533, 0.653), achieving the best m-ROUGE and second-best human evaluation (98.20 vs. Qwen3's 99.60). In the personalized setting, Uni-AdGen clearly outperforms Flux-Kontext (PBS 0.514) by a large margin (0.634 vs. 0.514) and edges out Pigeon (0.624) on PBS. For personalized text, Uni-AdGen (BLEU 0.435, ROUGE 0.662) substantially surpasses Qwen3 (0.345, 0.580) and DeepSeek-R1 (0.373, 0.622), with the gap attributed to the coarse-to-fine preference module's ability to filter sample-level and modal-level noise from user histories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-model pipeline paradigm in advertising generation (separate T2I diffusion models for images and LLMs for texts) by unifying both modalities into a single autoregressive framework built on Janus-Pro 7B. It also shifts the personalization signal from aggregate CTR (used by Chen et al., Yang et al., Lu et al.) to individual user behavioral histories, introducing a coarse-to-fine module that addresses sample-level and modal-level noise in those histories. The autoregressive image generation lineage (Janus-Pro, Llama-based image models) provides the architectural foundation, while the foreground perception module draws on DINOv2-based encoding and the preference extraction borrows from differentiable token masking (Gumbel-Softmax) in NLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAd1M dataset (1.1M users, 8.5M products) and the PBS metric fill a genuine gap: prior personalized ad datasets were small-scale and CTR-centric, and standard image similarity metrics (CLIP, DINOv3) are insensitive to background style differences that drive user preferences. The unified autoregressive approach reduces system complexity and enables natural cross-modal interaction, though the modest performance margins over strong baselines (e.g., PBS 0.634 vs. 0.624 for Pigeon) suggest the practical gains from unification are still emerging. Open questions include whether autoregressive image quality can close the gap with diffusion models at scale, whether the coarse-to-fine preference module generalizes beyond e-commerce, and how the approach performs under distribution shift in user behavior.
