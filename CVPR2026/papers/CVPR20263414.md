## Problem

Large multimodal models struggle with long-form video reasoning (>15 min) because uniform frame sampling and text-only Chain-of-Thought fail to adaptively locate sparse, fine-grained visual evidence, leading to hallucinations and poor temporal grounding. No existing framework enables LMMs to perform human-like global-to-local visual operations (skimming then zooming into relevant clips), and the open-source community lacks fine-grained training and evaluation data for this 'video segment-in-a-haystack' reasoning setting.

## Input / Output

The model receives a long video (ranging from ~10 to 70+ minutes) and an open-ended text question. It produces an interleaved multimodal chain-of-tool-thought (iMCoTT) trace in which the model alternates between textual reasoning steps and native crop_video(start_time, end_time) tool calls that resample finer-grained frames from a proposed temporal window. The final output is a grounded text answer accompanied by the predicted temporal span, with the reasoning trace explicitly showing which segments were inspected and how hypotheses were revised across multiple tool-calling rounds.

## Method

LongVT exploits LMMs' inherent temporal grounding ability as a native video-cropping tool, enabling a global-to-local reasoning loop: the model first skims sparsely sampled frames, proposes a time window, calls crop_video to fetch a denser clip, rethinks on the new evidence, and iterates until the answer is grounded in retrieved visual evidence. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) cold-start SFT on 247.9K samples (including Gemini- and Qwen-distilled iMCoTT traces) to teach window proposal, dense-frame reasoning, and self-correction; (2) agentic RL via GRPO with a joint answer-temporal-grounding reward that combines LLM-as-a-Judge accuracy (F/P/I verdicts), format compliance, and IoU-based temporal overlap, explicitly coupling answer correctness to where evidence lies in time; (3) agentic RFT that distills high-quality RL rollouts (correct answer + IoU ≥ 0.3) back into supervised data to stabilize tool-calling behaviors. The approach requires no auxiliary expert model or external retriever, and the decoupled IoU reward (rather than Recall) prevents span-inflation reward hacking while promoting precise boundary alignment.

## Results

Under dense frame sampling (512/768 frames), LongVT-7B-RFT achieves the best average score of 47.7 among all open-source video-centric LMMs, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (46.0), Video-Thinker-7B (42.9), Video-R1-7B (42.7), and VideoRFT-7B (37.0). On the most challenging benchmark, VideoSIAH-Eval (open-ended QA requiring fine-grained evidence retrieval from ~28-minute videos), LongVT-7B-RFT scores 42.0, surpassing the next-best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 33.8) by 8.2 points. Under sparse sampling (64 frames), LongVT-7B-RL leads with an average of 43.7, ahead of Video-R1-7B (42.6) and Video-Thinker-7B (42.6). The best LongVT checkpoint narrows the gap to GPT-4o (51.5 average) to within roughly 4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongVT-7B-RFT outperforms all open-source baselines on the average score under dense sampling (47.7 vs. 46.0 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 42.9 for Video-Thinker-7B, 42.7 for Video-R1-7B, and 37.0 for VideoRFT-7B). The largest margin is on VideoSIAH-Eval, where LongVT-7B-RFT (42.0) beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B (33.8) by 8.2 points and Video-Thinker-7B (6.6) by 35.4 points, confirming the advantage of native tool calling for evidence-sparse long-video QA. On VideoMME (w/ subtitle), LongVT-7B-RFT (67.0) edges out Qwen2.5-VL-7B (64.3) by 2.7 points, while Video-Thinker-7B achieves a notably higher LVBench score (54.3 vs. 41.3) but collapses on VideoSIAH-Eval, suggesting its reasoning does not transfer to open-ended temporal grounding. Under sparse sampling (64 frames), LongVT-7B-RL (43.7 avg.) still leads all open-source baselines, with Video-R1-7B (42.6) and Video-Thinker-7B (42.6) trailing by ~1 point. Against proprietary models, LongVT-7B-RFT remains below GPT-4o (51.5) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (55.2) on average, but the gap to GPT-4o narrows to ~4 points, a substantial improvement over prior open-source methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

LongVT extends the R1-style paradigm (SFT with CoT followed by GRPO-based RL) from text-centric reasoning to a tool-augmented, agentic setting for long-video understanding. The closest prior work is VITAL [50], which also applies tool-augmented RL to video QA and temporal grounding, but LongVT departs in two key ways: it targets the harder 'video segment-in-a-haystack' formulation with a dedicated large-scale dataset (VideoSIAH), and it introduces a three-stage closed-loop pipeline (SFT → RL → RFT) where the RFT stage [33] distills high-quality RL rollouts back into supervised data. Unlike prior approaches that use multi-task objectives or explicit tool-usage rewards, LongVT demonstrates that a single-task RL with a decoupled joint answer-temporal-grounding reward suffices for state-of-the-art performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VideoSIAH dataset (247.9K training samples) and VideoSIAH-Eval benchmark (652 QA pairs) fill a critical gap: prior long-video benchmarks rely heavily on multiple-choice formats that can be solved without genuine temporal grounding, leaving the evidence-sparse, open-ended reasoning setting underexplored. The practical three-stage recipe (cold-start SFT → agentic RL → RFT) provides a reproducible pipeline that narrows the gap to proprietary models (GPT-4o) to roughly four points on average. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, whether the crop_video tool generalizes to other video operations (e.g., frame interpolation, object tracking), and whether the iMCoTT paradigm transfers to other long-context multimodal tasks such as audio or 3D video.
