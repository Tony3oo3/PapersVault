## Problem

Existing 3D texture generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: multiview-based approaches produce view-inconsistent textures and cannot semantically complete occluded regions, while UV-space generation methods lack sufficient 3D spatial and semantic priors to produce structurally coherent textures. No prior method simultaneously leverages rich multiview appearance information and UV-space inpainting to resolve both inconsistency and occlusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263717/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh with a known UV mapping and a single reference image depicting the object's texture. The reference image is first expanded into a set of 768×768 multiview images via MV-Adapter, which then serve as semantic prompts (not direct projections) for the UV diffusion model. The output is a complete 1024×1024 UV texture map with all occluded and previously unseen regions filled in.

## Method

MV2UV is a two-stage diffusion framework: MV-Adapter generates multiview images from a single reference image, and a UV-space latent diffusion model (fine-tuned from SDXL with frozen self- and cross-attention) synthesizes the final texture map using those multiview images as prompts. The key architectural contribution is a parallel attention block combining three mechanisms: (1) a Reference Attention layer that cross-attends UV features to multiview features, (2) a UV Self Attention layer that lets spatially disconnected UV islands attend to 3D-adjacent regions, and (3) the original SDXL self-attention. All three share a geometric position encoding computed from per-pixel 3D coordinates and surface normals via Fourier embeddings and a learnable convolutional encoder, enabling the attention to align UV pixels with geometrically corresponding multiview pixels. This design allows the network to adaptively resolve multiview conflicts and semantically inpaint occluded regions rather than relying on direct back-projection or naive UV inpainting.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, MV2UV achieves the best FID of 24.4 and KID of 43.6×10⁻⁴, outperforming the closest multiview baseline MV-Adapter (FID 24.7, KID 47.5) and substantially surpassing UV-based TEXGen (FID 75.2, KID 283.3). On the DTC dataset, MV2UV achieves FID 26.4 and KID 28.7×10⁻⁴, improving over MV-Adapter by 2.0 FID and 13.1 KID. For occluded-region inpainting specifically, MV2UV reduces FID from 123.6 to 67.5 and KID from 157.9 to 47.6 compared to MV-Adapter, representing roughly a 2× improvement in completion quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the UV-based baseline TEXGen, MV2UV achieves dramatic improvements: FID drops from 75.2 to 24.4 on GSO (−50.8) and from 41.1 to 26.4 on DTC (−14.7), with KID reductions of over 200 and 90 points respectively, confirming that multiview semantic prompts far outperform UV-space generation alone. Against the multiview-based Hunyuan3D 2.1, MV2UV improves FID by 6.4 on GSO (30.8→24.4) and by 15.0 on DTC (41.4→26.4), while KID improves by 28.9 and 21.2 points, indicating substantially fewer view-inconsistency artifacts. Against UniTEX, MV2UV wins on all four metrics, with the largest gap on GSO KID (101.5→43.6). Against the closest competitor MV-Adapter (which serves as both the multiview generation front-end and a direct back-projection baseline), MV2UV achieves a narrow but consistent win on GSO (FID 24.4 vs 24.7, KID 43.6 vs 47.5) and a more pronounced win on DTC (FID 26.4 vs 28.4, KID 28.7 vs 41.8), with the DTC KID gap of 13.1 points reflecting better handling of multiview inconsistency and occluded regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV2UV bridges two established but conflicting paradigms in 3D texture generation: multiview diffusion methods (MV-Adapter, Paint3D, Hunyuan3D) that suffer from view inconsistency and occluded-region gaps, and UV-space generation methods (TEXGen) that lack sufficient semantic priors. Rather than back-projecting multiview images directly onto the UV map, the paper repurposes them as semantic prompts for a UV-space latent diffusion model (SDXL backbone), introducing a geometric position encoding that uses 3D XYZ coordinates (via Fourier features) as positional signals in both a Reference Attention layer (UV→MV cross-attention) and a UV Self Attention layer. This design draws on the cross-view attention ideas from multiview diffusion and the UV-space generation framework from TEXGen, but the key architectural departure is the XYZ-coordinate-guided attention that enables the network to adaptively resolve view conflicts and semantically inpaint occluded regions instead of relying on direct projection or naive inpainting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses two persistent pain points in production 3D content pipelines—multiview inconsistency artifacts and missing textures on self-occluded surfaces—with a single unified diffusion model, and the 2× FID improvement on occluded regions (123.6→67.5) over the MV-Adapter baseline is practically significant for game and VR asset creation. The geometric position encoding trick (using 3D coordinates as attention positional signals) is a reusable design pattern that could transfer to other cross-modal 3D generation tasks. However, the paper acknowledges a clear limitation: text rendering on textures remains poor due to VAE capacity constraints, and the method is evaluated only on albedo maps rather than full PBR material sets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multi-channel material generation at scale.
