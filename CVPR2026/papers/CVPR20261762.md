## Problem

MLLM-based segmentation faces a trilemma: embedding prediction degrades dialogue ability through conflicting pixel-level objectives, while next-token prediction forces a trade-off between poor segmentation (sparse coordinate outputs) and prohibitively slow inference (long autoregressive sequences). No existing paradigm simultaneously preserves dialogue, achieves high segmentation accuracy, and runs fast.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261762/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image at dynamic resolution (1024–1280 patches) and a text instruction, producing a textual response followed by a segmentation mask. The mask is emitted as a fixed-length sequence of patch-wise foreground/background classifications whose length matches the number of image patches, optionally refined to pixel-level resolution by a frozen SAM decoder prompted with a single sampled keypoint.

## Method

STAMP decouples segmentation into two phases: (1) autoregressive dialogue generation where the MLLM emits a learned <SEG> token to signal when and where to segment, and (2) a single non-autoregressive forward pass over N [MASK] placeholders, each whose embedding is fused with the corresponding ViT patch feature and 2D positional embedding. A hybrid attention mask enforces causal attention over the dialogue history (loaded from cached KV states) while enabling full bidirectional attention among all mask tokens, giving every patch access to the complete spatial context. A linear classifier on the final hidden states yields per-patch FG/BG logits, trained with a unified token-level objective combining cross-entropy for text and BCE+Dice for mask classification. Built on Qwen2-VL (2B fully fine-tuned, 7B via LoRA), the design is decoder-free, supports dynamic resolutions, and achieves O(1) mask generation steps.

## Results

On single-object referring segmentation (RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg), STAMP-7B achieves a new state-of-the-art average cIoU of 80.7 with SAM refinement, surpassing the previous best Text4Seg++ (78.9) by 1.8 points, and even the lightweight STAMP-2B (79.1) exceeds it. On the more challenging multi/no-object gRefCOCO benchmark, STAMP-7B reaches 74.8 average cIoU, outperforming the strongest competitor Text4Seg (71.5) by 3.3 points. On reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg), STAMP-2B achieves 63.2 average, beating READ (61.1) despite using a smaller backbone and no explicit chain-of-thought. Crucially, STAMP-2B trained on mixed segmentation+VQA data retains visual understanding scores on par with the Qwen2-VL-2B baseline trained exclusively on VQA (e.g., MMBench 68.7 vs. 66.5), confirming no dialogue degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAMP-7B (80.7 avg cIoU) outperforms the previous SOTA Text4Seg++ (78.9) by 1.8 points and the strongest embedding-prediction method GSVA-13B (72.8) by 7.9 points on single-object RES. Even without SAM refinement, STAMP-7B (76.2) matches Text4Seg-13B with SAM (76.2) while using a smaller backbone (Qwen2-7B vs. Vicuna-13B) and a decoder-free design. On multi/no-object gRefCOCO, STAMP-7B (74.8) beats Text4Seg-13B (71.5) by 3.3 points and LAVT (59.7) by 15.1 points. On reasoning segmentation, STAMP-2B (63.2) surpasses READ (61.1) and Seg-Zero (58.5) despite using a 2B backbone versus their 7B counterparts. In the joint VQA+segmentation evaluation, STAMP-2B retains visual understanding scores comparable to the VQA-only Qwen2-VL-2B baseline (e.g., MMBench 68.7 vs. 66.5, ScienceQA 72.6 vs. 70.2), whereas LISA-7B and READ-7B collapse to near-zero VQA scores, confirming that the all-mask paradigm avoids the dialogue degradation inherent in embedding-prediction methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAMP carves a third paradigm between the two dominant approaches to MLLM-based segmentation: embedding prediction (LISA, GSVA, READ), which couples a pixel-level loss to an external SAM decoder and degrades dialogue, and next-token prediction (Text4Seg, Seg-Zero, SegAgent), which preserves dialogue but forces autoregressive generation of long token sequences. Conceptually, STAMP repurposes the patch-wise classification idea from Text4Seg but makes it non-autoregressive by treating all mask tokens as a parallel fill-in-the-blank task over pre-defined [MASK] placeholders, while borrowing the <SEG> trigger token from LISA and redefining it as a pure in-vocabulary signal rather than a decoder prompt. The hybrid attention mechanism (causal for dialogue history, bidirectional for mask tokens) is the key architectural novelty that enables this decoupling within a single transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the dialogue–segmentation–speed trilemma with a clean O(1) mask generation step, STAMP removes a major practical barrier to deploying MLLMs for dense prediction in interactive settings. The finding that a 2B model outperforms 7B autoregressive competitors (79.1 vs. 78.9 cIoU) and that mixed VQA+segmentation training *improves* segmentation over segmentation-only training suggests the paradigm scales favorably with data diversity. Open questions include whether the patch-level granularity (limited by ViT patch size) suffices for fine-grained tasks like instance or panoptic segmentation, whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D, and whether the optional SAM refinement step can be eliminated entirely without significant quality loss.
