## Problem

Large 3D reconstruction models frequently produce hallucinated structural outliers (odd holes, protrusions) in generated meshes, yet this failure mode remains severely underexplored compared to hallucinations in other large models. These outliers stem from sparse multi-view images with large viewpoint gaps and poor continuity, misleading reconstruction into introducing unsupported geometric details. Additionally, no existing metric quantifies geometric fidelity specifically from the outlier perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (rendered at 512×512) and produces a textured 3D mesh normalized to a [-0.5, 0.5] bounding box. Internally, it first generates four orthographic multi-view color images with corresponding normal maps, then renders 72 dense cyclic views (5° apart) for the refinement stage. Evaluation renders 24 views per object (3 elevation angles × 8 azimuth angles) for appearance and geometry scoring.

## Method

Dehallu3D employs a two-stage test-time optimization pipeline over a differentiable mesh initialized via Poisson reconstruction from front/back normal maps. The coarse stage corrects global topology using a surface exposure-weighted normal loss (L_SE) that dynamically prioritizes views with higher projected vertex areas. The key contribution is the plug-and-play Cyclic View Consistency Refinement (CVCR) module, which enforces pairwise depth-map consistency across 72 cyclically adjacent views (5° apart) using a combined SSIM × Cosine Similarity metric (L_DC), while an adaptive smoothness loss (L_DS) weighted by rendered color-image gradients suppresses over-smoothing at sharp features. The paper also introduces the Outlier Risk Measure (ORM), a CVaR-based metric that scores mesh outlier severity via a hybrid global (VAE reconstruction) and local (k-NN density ratio) point-cloud scoring function.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects dataset, Dehallu3D achieves the best performance across all six evaluation metrics: PSNR 21.84, SSIM 0.8966, LPIPS 0.1453, Clip-Sim 0.7753, Chamfer Distance 0.02023, and F-Score 0.4212. The method also attains the lowest Outlier Risk Measure (ORM) score among all compared methods, confirming its effectiveness at suppressing hallucinated geometric outliers. The closest competitor, SF3D, trails by 0.51 dB in PSNR and 0.0012 in CD, while the largest gap appears in Clip-Sim (0.7753 vs. 0.7538 for InstantMesh).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dehallu3D outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline SF3D (CVPR 2025), it gains +0.51 dB PSNR, +0.0054 SSIM, −0.0084 LPIPS, +0.029 Clip-Sim, −0.0012 CD, and +0.045 F-Score. Unique3D (NeurIPS 2024) is the closest competitor on F-Score (0.4073 vs. 0.4212) and CD (0.02175 vs. 0.02023), but Dehallu3D leads by a clear margin on all other metrics; the paper further demonstrates that plugging the CVCR module into Unique3D visibly reduces its mesh outliers (Figure 6). InstantMesh leads among baselines on Clip-Sim (0.7538) but still trails Dehallu3D by 0.0215. CRM, TripoSR, and Wonder3D are consistently weaker, with Wonder3D showing the largest gaps (e.g., 1.34 dB lower PSNR, 0.063 lower F-Score). The ORM evaluation (Figure 5) confirms that Dehallu3D produces the fewest geometric outliers, while Unique3D exhibits the highest outlier risk, aligning with its qualitative mesh defects.

## Relation to Prior Work

Dehallu3D sits within the dominant multi-view-generation-then-reconstruct paradigm (One-2-3-45, Wonder3D, CRM, Unique3D) and inherits its mesh initialization strategy directly from Unique3D [37] (Poisson reconstruction from front/back normal maps). Its conceptual departure is treating hallucinated geometric outliers as a first-class optimization objective rather than an implicit byproduct of multi-view inconsistency, introducing a cyclic depth-consistency loss over 72 dense views and an adaptive smoothness term to prevent over-smoothing. The Outlier Risk Measure (ORM) repurposes Conditional Value-at-Risk from financial risk theory to quantify tail-risk in point-cloud geometry, a metric not previously applied to 3D reconstruction evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly targeting outliers, Dehallu3D addresses a failure mode that directly impacts downstream applications like 3D printing and VR immersion, where even small geometric deviations are costly. The plug-and-play CVCR module (demonstrated on Unique3D) suggests the approach can retrofit existing pipelines without retraining, broadening its practical reach. However, the dense 72-view rendering adds ~50 s of compute over sparser intervals, and the authors acknowledge this limits real-time use; improving efficiency of the refinement stage remains an open question. The finding that a 3° interval underperforms 5° due to over-constraining sharp features is a useful practical guideline but also hints that the current SSIM+cosine similarity formulation may not fully capture valid geometric discontinuities.
