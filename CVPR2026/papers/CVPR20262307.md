## Problem

Existing membership inference attacks on diffusion models rely on image-space perturbation or internal model features, both of which fail for pre-training data: VAE encoding and stochastic denoising wash out visual perturbations, while intermediate latents are inaccessible in closed-source APIs. This leaves no practical black-box method for detecting whether a specific image was used in the large-scale pre-training of a commercial text-to-image generator.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a suspected image (any resolution) and its paired textual description (or a BLIP-2-generated surrogate caption when unavailable), then issues a sequence of perturbed text prompts to a black-box text-to-image API, receiving only generated images in return. The output is a scalar membership score computed as the difference between maximum cross-modal relevance under perturbed and unperturbed text, which can be thresholded for a binary decision or aggregated across multiple samples for set-level dataset auditing.

## Method

SD-MIA exploits the observation that text embeddings remain unnoised throughout diffusion generation, so for pre-training members the model has internalized a locally overfitted text-to-image mapping that creates a 'representation-region collapse': small textual perturbations stay within this collapsed region and yield stable reconstructions, while for non-members the same perturbations produce divergent outputs. Concretely, GPT-5 generates three complementary perturbation views (token-level lexical rewrites, style-level register changes, and semantic-level attribute modifications) under a CLIP-similarity constraint, and the model is queried 10 times per perturbed prompt. A CLIP-based cross-modal relevance score between the target image and each reconstruction is computed, and the top K% of scores are pooled (maximum-relevance pooling) to suppress diffusion stochasticity. The final membership signal is the difference between the pooled relevance under perturbed text and under the original description, approximating the unobservable generation-probability curvature shift.

## Results

On the LAION-mi benchmark (balanced member/non-member), SD-MIA achieves 65.9–66.9% AUC across Stable Diffusion v1.2, v1.4, v1.5, and v3.5, with TPR@5% FPR ranging from 13.7% to 18.3%. Under the 1:10 imbalanced setting, SD-MIA maintains 66.1% AUC on SD v1.4, consistently outperforming all baselines. At the set level (L=30 samples), SD-MIA exceeds 95% AUC for dataset-level membership inference. On closed-source models (DALL-E, Gemini, GPT-4o) evaluated via FlickrMIA-25, SD-MIA also surpasses the strongest black-box baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-MIA outperforms the strongest gray-box baseline DRC (Fu et al., IEEE TIFS 2025) by 10–12 percentage points in AUC across all four Stable Diffusion versions (e.g., 66.28% vs. 54.66% on SD v1.2; 66.93% vs. 60.44% on SD v3.5), despite DRC having access to internal latent noise predictions. Against the black-box Reconstruction method (Pang et al., NDSS 2025), SD-MIA gains roughly 6–7% AUC on SD v1.2–v1.5 (66.28% vs. 59.66%) and a dramatic 20+ point margin on SD v3.5 (66.93% vs. 46.74%), where Reconstruction degrades to near-random. The conditional-likelihood-discrepancy method CLiD (Zhai et al., NeurIPS 2024) and the proximal-initialization attack PIA (Kong et al., ICLR 2024) both remain near or below 55% AUC on most models, trailing SD-MIA by 10–18 points. Notably, SD-MIA's advantage is most pronounced on the latest architecture (SD v3.5), where several baselines (Reconstruction, REDIFFUSE, SecMIA) drop below 50% AUC while SD-MIA holds steady at 66.93%, suggesting the cross-modal perturbation signal is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-perturbation paradigm in diffusion-model membership inference (Loss, SecMIA, PIA, CLiD, DRC, REDIFFUSE, Reconstruction) by shifting the perturbation to the textual conditioning modality, motivated by the observation that VAE encoding and stochastic denoising contract away image-space signals while text embeddings remain unnoised throughout generation. The theoretical framing of 'representation-region collapse'—where locally overfitted text-to-image mappings create a stable neighborhood for member samples—repurposes the well-known overfitting phenomenon in a new cross-modal detection context. Methodologically, it extends the LAION-mi benchmark to a new FlickrMIA-25 dataset and evaluates against closed-source APIs (DALL-E, Gemini, GPT-4o), a setting prior gray-box methods (DRC, PIA) cannot address due to their reliance on internal latent features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: SD-MIA is the first MIA framework demonstrated to operate on mainstream closed-source image-generation APIs, enabling real-world auditing of pre-training data usage without model access. However, the absolute AUC (~66%) remains moderate, and the method's dependence on GPT-5 for perturbation generation introduces cost and potential distributional biases that limit scalability. Open questions include whether the representation-region collapse mechanism generalizes to non-diffusion architectures (e.g., autoregressive image models), whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation, and whether adversarial defenses (e.g., text-embedding regularization) could neutralize the cross-modal signal.
