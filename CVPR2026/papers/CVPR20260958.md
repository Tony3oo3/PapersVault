## Problem

Diffusion model training demands millions of images and hundreds of thousands of GPU iterations, yet dataset condensation—a strategy for constructing much smaller training subsets—has been developed almost exclusively for discriminative tasks. Naively applying existing condensation algorithms (e.g., SRe²L, RDED) to diffusion training produces synthetic images lacking structural and semantic fidelity, leading to degraded generation quality and unstable convergence. The paper asks whether a purpose-built condensation pipeline can train diffusion models dramatically faster with far less data while preserving high generative quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a large class-conditional image dataset (ImageNet-1K at 256×256 or 512×512), a pre-trained diffusion model for difficulty scoring, a T5 text encoder, and a DINOv2 vision encoder. It produces a condensed subset (0.8%–8% of the original, i.e., 10K–100K images) where each selected real image is paired with two attached metadata files: a per-class text embedding (T5 encoding of a descriptive prompt like 'a photo of a cat') and a per-image visual patch representation (DINOv2 features truncated to h tokens matching the diffusion transformer's token count). The condensed dataset is then used to train a diffusion model from scratch under the standard denoising objective plus a REPA-style alignment loss.

## Method

D²C is a two-phase framework. In the Select phase, a diffusion difficulty score—defined as the negative class-conditional likelihood p_θ(x|c) estimated from a pre-trained diffusion model's denoising loss—ranks all samples; interval sampling with a fixed stride k then picks every k-th sample per class, balancing learnability (easy samples) against diversity (harder samples) in a way that neither min-only nor max-only selection achieves. In the Attach phase, each selected image is enriched with (i) a Dual Conditional Embedding that fuses a T5 text embedding with a learnable class token via a 1D convolution and residual MLP, and (ii) DINOv2 patch-level visual features that are aligned to the diffusion backbone's intermediate token representations through a REPA-style cosine-similarity projection loss (weight λ=0.5). The final training objective is the standard noise-prediction MSE plus this alignment term, and the entire pipeline requires only ~9.5 GPU-hours end-to-end (with a pretrained scorer) versus 750 hours for REPA on the full dataset, yielding a 4.3 gFID at 40K steps with just 10K images.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with SiT-XL/2, D²C achieves a gFID of 4.3 in just 40k training steps using only 10K images (0.8% of the full 1.28M dataset), representing a 233× speedup over vanilla SiT-XL/2 (gFID 8.3 at 7M steps) and a 100× speedup over REPA (gFID 5.9 at 4M steps). Under a 4% budget (50K images), D²C further improves to gFID 2.78 at 180k steps. At the 0.8% budget with 100k iterations, D²C reaches gFID 4.20 on DiT-L/2 and 3.98 on SiT-L/2, substantially outperforming random sampling (35.86 / 4.35), K-Center (50.77 / 14.77), and Herding (40.75 / 22.96) at the same budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²C decisively outperforms all acceleration baselines on SiT-XL/2: it reaches gFID 4.3 at 40k steps versus REPA's 5.9 at 4M steps (100× fewer iterations), REPA-E's 5.9 at 235k steps, and REG's 5.0 at 200k steps. Against vanilla DiT-L/2 (gFID 23.3 at 400k on full data), D²C achieves gFID 4.2 at 100k steps using only 10K images. Among data-selection and condensation baselines, D²C dominates: at 0.8% budget and 100k iterations, it scores gFID 4.20 (DiT-L/2) and 3.98 (SiT-L/2), while K-Center (50.77 / 14.77), Herding (40.75 / 22.96), and random sampling (35.86 / 4.35) all lag by orders of magnitude. The pixel-level condensation methods SRe²L and RDED, which perform well on classification, fail catastrophically on generative tasks (gFID 104.2 and 166.2 on DiT-L/2 respectively), confirming that discriminative-oriented condensation does not transfer to diffusion training. D²C also generalizes to 512×512 resolution, where it achieves gFID 5.8 (DiT-L/2) and 4.22 (SiT-L/2) at 300k steps versus 17.1 and 5.0 for random sampling under the same 0.8% budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dataset condensation (DC) paradigm—previously developed almost exclusively for discriminative classifiers (SRe²L, RDED, CAFE)—to the generative diffusion model setting, where it had not been systematically studied. It builds on REPA's representation-alignment loss for the Attach phase and on the observation that diffusion models implicitly encode class-conditional posteriors, repurposing the diffusion loss as a per-sample difficulty score. The key conceptual departure from prior DC work is the rejection of pixel-level synthetic-image optimization (which the paper shows catastrophically fails for generation) in favor of image-level selection plus semantic/visual enrichment, and the introduction of interval sampling over a difficulty ranking to balance learnability and diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 100–233× reduction in training steps while matching or exceeding full-data FID is a practically transformative efficiency gain for any group training diffusion models under budget constraints, and the method generalizes across DiT/SiT backbones, 256/512 resolutions, and 0.8–8% data budgets. The demonstrated complete failure of pixel-level DC methods (FID 82–166) on generative tasks is an important negative result that redirects the field toward image-level condensation for generation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image and video diffusion, whether the interval-sampling hyperparameter k can be made adaptive rather than hand-tuned, and whether the reliance on a (even weak) pretrained scorer can be eliminated entirely.
