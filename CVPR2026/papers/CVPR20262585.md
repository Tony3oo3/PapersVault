## Problem

Unsupervised 3D point cloud segmentation methods that leverage 2D pre-trained models (e.g., SAM) suffer from a fundamental mismatch between discrete sparse 3D points and continuous 2D images: sparse projections cause foreground and background points to overlap in 2D, producing semantically ambiguous masks, and the discrete-to-continuous domain gap demands complex alignment or additional 3D pre-training that compromises semantic consistency across views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse indoor 3D point cloud with XYZ coordinates and RGB values (no ground-truth labels, no 3D pre-training data) and produces per-point semantic labels over K categories. The point cloud is consumed via 150 predefined surround-view RGB projections at 770×770 pixels, and labels are ultimately assigned through nearest-neighbor search from semantically labeled 3D Gaussian centers after spatial alignment, rather than through direct pixel-to-point back-projection.

## Method

PointGS uses 3D Gaussian Splatting as a unified intermediate representation to bridge the discrete-continuous gap: the sparse point cloud is first reconstructed into a dense 3D Gaussian space from multi-view projections, filling spatial gaps and encoding occlusion so that rendered views show clean foreground-background separation. SAM segments the rendered multi-view images, and a scale-aware contrastive learning objective (adapted from SAGA's scale-conditioned affinity features) distills 2D mask semantics into per-Gaussian learnable feature vectors, ensuring cross-view semantic consistency. A multi-view consistency check prunes Gaussians invisible in more than three adjacent views to reduce noise. Finally, Gaussian center points are density-denoised, rescaled to match the original point cloud, aligned via a two-step ICP registration (coarse ICP followed by 24 directional-rotation trials to escape local optima), and labels are propagated to original points by nearest-neighbor assignment.

## Results

On S3DIS Area 5, PointGS achieves 49.3% mIoU, 76.6% oAcc, and 66.1% mAcc, outperforming the strongest baseline LogoSP by +2.8% mIoU and +10.2% mAcc. On ScanNet-v2, PointGS reaches 36.7% mIoU, surpassing LogoSP (35.8%) by +0.9%. The method requires no human annotations or 3D pre-training, relying solely on RGB-projected point clouds and SAM-generated 2D masks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointGS outperforms all seven baselines on mIoU across both datasets, with the largest margin over LogoSP (+2.8% on S3DIS, +0.9% on ScanNet-v2) and a substantial gap over clustering-based methods like GrowSP (+4.7%) and U3DS³ (+6.5%). On mAcc, PointGS leads all baselines by a wide margin (66.1% vs. 59.4% for GrowSP and 55.9% for LogoSP on S3DIS), indicating superior per-class balance. However, PointGS trails LogoSP (82.8%) and GrowSP (78.5%) on oAcc (76.6%), which the authors attribute to oAcc being dominated by large-area categories (ceiling, wall, floor) where superpoint-based methods excel. Compared to 2D-prior methods (PointDC, PiCIE), PointGS gains over 26 mIoU points on S3DIS, demonstrating the benefit of 3D-GS-based occlusion-aware rendering over direct point-to-image projection. On ScanNet-v2, the improvement over LogoSP is smaller (+0.9%), suggesting the advantage of Gaussian-space reconstruction is more pronounced in the sparser S3DIS geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: unsupervised 3D point cloud segmentation (GrowSP, U3DS3, LogoSP) and 2D-prior-guided cross-modal transfer (PointDC, P2P, Segment3D). It departs from both by introducing 3D Gaussian Splatting as a unified intermediate representation that simultaneously fills spatial gaps in sparse point clouds and provides a differentiable rendering surface for SAM-based semantic extraction. The semantic distillation mechanism is directly adapted from SAGA's scale-conditioned affinity features and contrastive loss, but repurposed from prompt-guided 3D segmentation to a fully unsupervised setting where SAM masks replace user prompts. The two-step ICP registration and density-based denoising for Gaussian-to-point alignment are novel engineering contributions not present in either parent line.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural limitation in 2D-to-3D semantic transfer—projection overlap and modality mismatch—that has constrained prior cross-modal methods, and the 3D-GS intermediary offers a clean conceptual resolution. However, the pipeline is computationally heavy (multi-view 3D-GS training at 10k iterations, SAM inference, contrastive learning, ICP registration), and evaluation is limited to two indoor benchmarks, leaving open whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes where multi-view coverage is harder to guarantee. The notably large mAcc gain on S3DIS (+10.2% over LogoSP) suggests the method handles minority classes well, but the lower oAcc (76.6% vs 82.8%) indicates it still struggles with dominant flat surfaces, a limitation the authors acknowledge but do not fully resolve.
