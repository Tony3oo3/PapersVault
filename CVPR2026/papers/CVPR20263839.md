## Problem

Existing post-hoc debiasing methods for VLMs, particularly SFID, assume demographic bias is localized to a small set of embedding coordinates and can be removed by replacing those coordinates with neutral values. The paper demonstrates through systematic reproduction that this coordinate-sparse assumption fails on three fronts: attributes are entangled across shared dimensions, important dimension indices shift across datasets, and replacing the top-m coordinates leaves substantial linearly decodable attribute signal. The core question is how to model and remove bias in a way that respects its distributed, subspace-structured geometry in high-dimensional embedding spaces.

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen VLM embeddings (e.g., 512-dim CLIP ViT-B/32 image or text vectors) together with sensitive-attribute labels (gender, race, age) from a calibration set. A Random Forest is trained on the calibration embeddings to estimate per-sample prediction confidence; the bottom τ% (default 0.7) of samples by confidence define the neutral baseline. At inference, a single query embedding is projected and recentered, yielding a debiased vector of the same dimensionality that can be plugged directly into zero-shot classification, cosine-similarity retrieval, or diffusion-model text-encoder pipelines without any architectural modification.

## Method

SPD (Subspace Projection Debiasing) replaces discrete coordinate editing with a continuous subspace operation in three stages. First, it identifies the bias subspace via INLP: iteratively training a linear classifier on the current embeddings, extracting an orthonormal basis for the discriminative weight matrix via QR decomposition, and projecting embeddings onto the null space of that basis; after T iterations (or a user-chosen depth r), the concatenated bases form the bias subspace U ∈ R^{d_b×D}. Second, each query embedding is projected onto the orthogonal complement of U, removing all components along the learned attribute-predictive directions. Third, a neutral mean computed from low-confidence calibration samples is reinjected along the removed subspace (x'' = x' + U^T(U x̄_low)), which recenters the embeddings without reintroducing attribute-discriminative variance. The projection depth r acts as a fairness-utility knob: small r preserves semantics but leaves residual bias, while large r removes more bias at the cost of collateral semantic loss.

## Results

On zero-shot classification (FACET), SPD achieves the lowest demographic-parity gap (ΔDP) across all three backbones: 9.55 (CLIP RN50), 9.94 (CLIP ViT-B/32), and 9.85 (XVLM), compared to baselines of 11.08, 11.60, and 11.72 respectively, while maintaining within ~1% of baseline accuracy. On text-to-image retrieval (Flickr30K), SPD reduces Skew@100 by 37.5% (CLIP RN50: 0.1177 vs 0.1883), 59.4% (CLIP ViT-B/32: 0.0699 vs 0.1721), and 21.1% (XVLM: 0.1859 vs 0.2355) relative to unmodified models. On text-to-image generation (SDXL and CoDi), SPD achieves the lowest composite mismatch rate (5.26 on CoDi) and neutral-prompt skew (81.20 on CoDi), outperforming all baselines. The paper reports an average 18.5% improvement across four fairness metrics while incurring minimal task-performance loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPD outperforms SFID (the primary coordinate-level baseline) on both fairness metrics across all three backbones: ΔDP improves from 9.63→9.55 (RN50), 10.15→9.94 (ViT-B/32), and 9.91→9.85 (XVLM), while Skew@100 improves from 0.1414→0.1177, 0.0744→0.0699, and 0.2032→0.1859 respectively. Against CLIP-clip (mutual-information feature pruning), SPD achieves lower ΔDP on all backbones (e.g., 9.55 vs 10.09 on RN50) and lower Skew@100 (0.1177 vs 0.1542 on RN50), while CLIP-clip suffers larger accuracy drops (50.73 vs 51.44 on RN50). Compared to DeAR (adversarial residual debiasing), SPD consistently achieves lower ΔDP and Skew@100; DeAR's gains are modest (e.g., ΔDP 10.04 vs SPD's 9.55 on RN50). Prompt-D (adversarial prompt learning) is the weakest baseline, often failing to reduce Skew@100 (0.2030 vs baseline 0.1883 on RN50, i.e., it actually increases skew) and showing the largest accuracy variance. On text-to-image generation (CoDi), SPD achieves the best composite mismatch rate (5.26) and neutral-prompt skew (81.20), beating SFID (5.38, 82.77) and all other baselines, while Prompt-D catastrophically degrades generation quality (composite mismatch 46.77).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on INLP (Ravfogel et al., ACL 2020), adapting iterative null-space projection from the NLP debiasing literature to frozen VLM embeddings, and on SFID (Jung et al., NeurIPS 2024), whose coordinate-wise imputation strategy the authors systematically diagnose and replace. Conceptually, the paper departs from the broader post-hoc debiasing line (Chuang et al., BENDVLM, SANER, CLIP-clip) by arguing that bias in VLM embeddings is subspace-structured rather than coordinate-sparse, and by unifying subspace identification (INLP) with a neutral-reinjection step borrowed from SFID's low-confidence mean estimation. The result is a training-free, model-agnostic framework that treats debiasing as a continuous geometric projection rather than discrete coordinate editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free debiasing tool that works across multiple VLM backbones (CLIP, XVLM) and downstream tasks (classification, retrieval, generation) without retraining, which is valuable for practitioners who cannot afford fine-tuning. The diagnostic study showing that SFID's three core assumptions (disjoint attribute dimensions, cross-dataset dimension stability, top-m concentration) all fail in practice is a useful corrective for the field. However, the method still requires labeled attribute data to identify the bias subspace, the fairness-utility tradeoff via projection depth r is somewhat heuristic, and the evaluation is limited to gender/race/age on a handful of benchmarks; open questions include whether the subspace structure holds for intersectional or non-linear biases and whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP architectures without re-deriving the subspace.
