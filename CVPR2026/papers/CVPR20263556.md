## Problem

Existing food nutrition datasets are heavily biased toward Western cuisines with insufficient coverage of complex Chinese dishes, and many state-of-the-art nutrition prediction methods depend on depth sensors that are impractical for everyday food photography. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a comprehensive Chinese-food dataset and an end-to-end framework that estimates nutrition from a single RGB image without requiring depth hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263556/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB food image as input and regresses five continuous nutritional values: calories, mass, protein, fat, and carbohydrates. Internally, a pretrained monocular depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) generates a pseudo-depth map from the RGB image, which is then refined and fused with RGB features. The OmniFood8K dataset provides 6-view images captured at 50 cm height (approximating adult elbow level), while evaluation uses a 7:3 train/test split on OmniFood8K and 5:1 on Nutrition5k.

## Method

The framework comprises three modules operating on RGB and pseudo-depth features. First, the Scale-Shift Residual Adapter (SSRA) corrects monocular depth via a learnable global scale-shift (α·d + β) followed by a shallow convolutional residual for local geometric refinement. Second, the Frequency-Aligned Fusion Module (FAFM) applies 2D FFT to both modalities, splits features into low- and high-frequency bands using a threshold mask, fuses each band by element-wise addition, and concatenates the results through a learnable convolution; a contrastive inter-modal alignment loss (cosine-similarity-based InfoNCE) enforces cross-modal consistency. Third, the Mask-based Prediction Head (MPH) uses cross-attention between RGB and semantic features, gated fusion for channel reweighting, and a dynamic channel mask to select the most informative channels before global pooling and a fully connected layer produce the final nutritional values. Training employs a Dynamic Task-weighted Loss with KPI-based per-nutrient weight updates (smoothing factor α=0.3) plus the alignment loss, and the model is pretrained on the 115K synthetic NutritionSynth-115K dataset before fine-tuning on OmniFood8K.

## Results

On the Nutrition5k dataset, the proposed method achieves a mean PMAE of 16.5% using only a single RGB image, outperforming the best RGB-D baseline (FBFPN, 17.3%) and the best RGB-only baseline (DPF-Nutrition, 17.8%). On the proposed OmniFood8K dataset, the method achieves a mean PMAE of 8.6%, surpassing the strongest generic backbone (ConvNeXt, 9.8%) by 1.2 percentage points. Per-nutrient, the method achieves 14.1% (Calories), 10.2% (Mass), 21.0% (Fat), 18.9% (Carb.), and 18.4% (Protein) PMAE on Nutrition5k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Nutrition5k, the proposed method (16.5% mean PMAE, RGB-only) outperforms all RGB-only baselines, with the largest margin over Swin-Nutrition (20.4%) and DPF-Nutrition (17.8%). It also surpasses every RGB-D baseline despite not using a depth sensor at inference: FBFPN (17.3%), IMIR-Net (17.4%), and RGB-D Net (18.5%) all trail by 0.8–2.0 percentage points. On the authors' own OmniFood8K dataset, the method achieves 8.6% mean PMAE, beating ConvNeXt (9.8%), ResNet-50 (10.4%), and ConvNeXt V2 (11.2%), while transformer-based backbones (Swin-Transformer 31.2%, EfficientNet 28.9%) perform substantially worse. The ablation (Table 5) confirms each module contributes: adding FAFM, SSRA, and MPH sequentially reduces mean PMAE from 17.9% to 16.5%. Pretraining on NutritionSynth-115K further improves OmniFood8K performance from 8.6% to 7.8% mean PMAE (Table 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RGB-D nutrition estimation line (FBFPN, IMIR-Net, RGB-D Net) by replacing the depth sensor with a monocular depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) refined by a lightweight Scale-Shift Residual Adapter, thereby removing the hardware dependency that limits prior methods to controlled settings. The frequency-domain fusion via FFT-based decomposition (FAFM) departs from the spatial-domain feature pyramid and cross-attention fusion strategies used in prior RGB-D nutrition work, while the Dynamic Task-weighted Loss is adopted directly from a concurrent food-nutrition paper. The dataset contribution (OmniFood8K) carves new ground by being the first to cover the full Chinese cooking pipeline with weight-based nutritional ground truth, contrasting with prior datasets that rely on web-scraped or LLM-generated labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making accurate nutrition estimation accessible with a standard phone camera rather than a depth sensor, and in providing the first large-scale Chinese-cuisine nutrition benchmark with physically measured ground truth. The synthetic data pipeline (NutritionSynth-115K) offers a reusable recipe for generating nutrition-labeled training data from a smaller curated set. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain fusion provides genuine gains over simpler spatial fusion at scale, how well the method generalizes to cuisines and presentation styles outside the training distribution, and whether the monocular depth errors compound in complex multi-ingredient scenes.
