## Problem

Localized concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models, such as GLoCE, suppress a target concept only in its spatial region but unintentionally weaken semantically adjacent neighbor concepts (e.g., erasing one dog breed degrades generation of other breeds). This 'neighbor gap' reduces fidelity in fine-grained domains where classes are visually and semantically close, and no existing training-free localized method explicitly stabilizes neighboring semantics during erasure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262521/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained Stable Diffusion UNet, a text prompt containing the target concept, a target concept token set, and an external embedding pool (Wikipedia titles) for neighbor retrieval. At inference time it produces a modified model whose cross-attention K/V projections are rewritten by a per-concept spectral operator and whose latent features are spatially gated and zeroed in residual regions. The entire pipeline is training-free: no gradient updates, fine-tuning, or curated data are required, and multi-concept erasure is handled by composing per-concept operators selected via lexical and embedding-based prompt matching.

## Method

NLCE is a three-stage training-free pipeline. Stage 1 performs representation-space modulation: it computes an SVD of the target concept's token embeddings to build a spectrally-weighted projection P_Fc (inspired by CURE) that attenuates target-aligned singular directions, then mines neighbor concepts from an external pool via cosine similarity, RoBERTa-SVR concreteness filtering, and CLIP visual ranking to construct a neighbor projection P_Nc; the combined operator P_c = (I − βP_Fc) + γP_Nc P_Fc is applied globally to cross-attention key and value projections. Stage 2 introduces attention-guided spatial gating: a two-pass UNet mechanism extracts attention maps from DownBlock-2, identifies 'live' tokens with residual activation under the erased subspace, builds a spatial gate G_t(x,y) by summing attention over those tokens, and suppresses cross-attention in gated regions during the second pass. Stage 3 applies gated feature clean-up: the gate is upsampled to each layer's resolution, thresholded into a binary mask, and latent features within masked regions are hard-zeroed for irreversible removal, ensuring complete erasure only where leakage persists while leaving unaffected regions untouched.

## Results

On Oxford Flowers (Alpine Sea Holly), NLCE achieves H_o=90.15 and Acc_r=82.06, outperforming the best baseline SLD (H_o=89.85, Acc_r=81.58) and GLoCE (H_o=73.05, Acc_r=78.91). On Stanford Dogs (Bluetick), NLCE reaches H_o=86.31 and Acc_r=75.91 with KID=0.06, surpassing RECE (H_o=84.62, Acc_r=73.33) and GLoCE (H_o=72.79, Acc_r=73.59). NLCE also attains the lowest KID values (0.00–0.07) across all four fine-grained concepts, indicating minimal visual quality degradation. On the Celebrity dataset, NLCE achieves H_o=96.91/96.55/97.66 for Anna Kendrick/Elon Musk/Bill Clinton, matching or exceeding GLoCE (96.63/97.29/97.61) while achieving lower LPIPS_u on non-target regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NLCE outperforms all eight baselines on the primary fine-grained metric H_o across all four tested concepts. Against the closest training-free competitor GLoCE [20], NLCE achieves substantially higher H_o (e.g., 90.15 vs. 73.05 on Alpine Sea Holly; 81.49 vs. 26.34 on Chesapeake Bay Retriever) because GLoCE's lightweight gated low-rank edits leave high target accuracy (Acc_t up to 92%), indicating incomplete erasure. Among training-based methods, MACE [26] and UCE [11] achieve strong target suppression (Acc_t≈0) but sacrifice neighbor retention (Acc_r drops to 42–62%), while SPM [27] and ESD-x/u [10] show inconsistent erasure with Acc_t ranging from 4% to 32%. RECE [12] achieves complete target removal (Acc_t=0) but at the cost of severe neighbor forgetting (Acc_r=61–73%), yielding lower H_o than NLCE by 5–15 points. SLD [37] is the strongest training-free baseline on celebrity erasure (H_o≈95–97) but underperforms NLCE on fine-grained tasks (H_o=72–90 vs. NLCE's 81–90). AdaVD [46] occasionally matches NLCE on CLIP Score but exhibits highly inconsistent erasure (Acc_t from 0% to 60%), and its H_o on Camellia collapses to 50.89 due to poor target suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

NLCE directly extends GLoCE's localized, training-free concept erasure framework by identifying and addressing its 'neighbor gap' limitation, where semantically adjacent concepts are unintentionally suppressed alongside the target. The spectral weighting mechanism is adapted from CURE's orthogonal representation editing, repurposed here to modulate target-subspace suppression strength per singular direction. The broader positioning sits within the training-free erasure lineage (UCE, SLD, RECE, AdaVD) but distinguishes itself by combining representation-space modulation, attention-guided spatial gating, and hard feature zeroing into a single coarse-to-fine pipeline that explicitly preserves a mined neighbor set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The neighbor gap is a practically important failure mode in fine-grained domains (breed-level dog/flower erasure, celebrity identity) where collateral suppression of visually similar categories undermines the utility of localized erasure. NLCE's training-free, three-stage design offers a deployable solution without retraining, and the multi-concept scaling (10 concepts simultaneously with >91% retention) is relevant for real-world safety filtering. Open questions include whether the neighbor mining pipeline generalizes to domains lacking clear semantic neighborhoods (e.g., abstract styles), whether the two-pass UNet overhead becomes prohibitive at video-scale generation, and whether the hard-erasure stage can be made differentiable for end-to-end tuning.
