## Problem

Existing lighting representations—environment maps, irradiance maps, text descriptions, and reference images—are mutually incompatible, so methods built around one representation cannot easily transfer to another. This limits cross-modal lighting retrieval, flexible lighting control in generative models, and unified lighting understanding across diverse user inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder accepts four modalities: 360° HDR environment maps (fed as a triple of Reinhard-tone-mapped LDR, log-encoded HDR with I_max=1000, and per-pixel xyz direction coordinates), 512×512 RGB images with 90° FOV, spatially-varying irradiance maps from Prism, and free-form text lighting descriptions. The model outputs a shared 8×512 latent embedding per modality, from which degree-3 spherical harmonics coefficients can be predicted. Downstream, the embedding conditions a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium for 360° environment-map generation and an X→RGB diffusion model (taking depth, normal, albedo, and zeroed irradiance as intrinsics) for relighting.

## Method

UniLight uses separate DINOv2-B backbones for each image modality and a fine-tuned Qwen3 Embedding (0.6B) for text, all projected into a common space via a lightweight summary module of T=8 learnable query tokens that attend to backbone features through multi-head attention and layer normalization, then linearly mapped to a shared 8×512 embedding. Training combines a cross-entropy contrastive loss that maximizes cosine similarity between all modality pairs describing the same lighting condition with an auxiliary MSE loss on degree-3 spherical harmonics coefficients predicted from the embedding, explicitly grounding the latent space in directional lighting structure. The multi-modal dataset of 72,180 samples is constructed from 8,020 HDR environment maps by extracting 9 perspective crops each, computing irradiance via Prism, generating text descriptions with InternVL3-38B conditioned on detected dominant light sources, and estimating environment maps with DiffusionLight-Turbo for robustness. For downstream generation, the text-conditioning branch of Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium is repurposed to accept UniLight embeddings for environment-map synthesis, and the X→RGB framework is fine-tuned in two stages—first on intrinsics-to-render, then on intrinsics-plus-lighting-embedding—to enable multi-modal relighting.

## Results

On cross-modal lighting retrieval (603 test samples, averaged over all modality pairs), UniLight (8 tokens, SH3) achieves R@1 = 24.9%, R@5 = 49.0%, MRR = 0.367, and median rank 9.8, substantially outperforming CLIP ViT-B/32 (R@1 = 2.6%, MRR = 0.077) and Qwen3-VL 2B (R@1 = 8.9%, MRR = 0.179). For HDR environment-map generation, UniLight-conditioned diffusion achieves PSNR 28.85 dB, SSIM 0.915, and LPIPS 0.079, improving over DiffusionLight-Turbo (PSNR 27.77 dB, SSIM 0.902, LPIPS 0.088). Ablations confirm that removing the spherical-harmonics supervision (NOSH) drops R@1 from 24.9% to 10.2%, and SH degree 3 is optimal (SH1: 20.7%, SH5: 24.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniLight decisively outperforms both general-purpose embedding baselines on lighting retrieval: CLIP ViT-B/32 [50] achieves only R@1 = 2.6% and MRR = 0.077, while Qwen3-VL 2B [33] reaches R@1 = 8.9% and MRR = 0.179, compared to UniLight's R@1 = 24.9% and MRR = 0.367 — a roughly 3× improvement over Qwen3-VL and 10× over CLIP. On environment-map generation, UniLight beats DiffusionLight-Turbo [6] across all metrics (PSNR +1.08 dB, SSIM +0.013, LPIPS −0.009), and additionally supports conditioning from text, irradiance, and image modalities whereas DiffusionLight-Turbo only accepts image input. For image relighting (Fig. 11), UniLight qualitatively surpasses LumiNet [63] (image-conditioned), DiffusionRenderer [37] (environment-map-conditioned), and the original X→RGB [68] (text-conditioned) baselines, producing more coherent shadows and highlights under diverse lighting conditions. In the direction-sensitivity comparison (Fig. 12), Qwen3-VL embeddings fail to shift shadows and highlights when the environment map is rotated in 60° increments, whereas UniLight embeddings produce consistent directional changes, confirming that the spherical-harmonics supervision is critical for directional encoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of single-representation lighting methods (environment-map-only, irradiance-only, or text-only) by constructing a joint latent space that aligns previously incompatible modalities. It builds directly on the X→RGB diffusion framework (Zeng et al., SIGGRAPH 2024) and Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (Esser et al., ICML 2024) for downstream generation, repurposing their text-conditioning branch to accept learned lighting embeddings. Conceptually, it extends CLIP-style contrastive alignment from generic image-text pairs to the specific domain of lighting, adding a spherical-harmonics prediction head as an auxiliary directional supervision signal not present in prior contrastive vision-language work. It also positions itself against implicit NeRF/Gaussian-splat lighting representations, which capture complex illumination but remain task-specific and incompatible across modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single embedding that bridges text, images, irradiance maps, and environment maps, UniLight enables practical workflows (cross-modal retrieval, multi-conditioned environment-map generation, flexible relighting) that were previously locked to one representation at a time. The ablation showing that removing spherical-harmonics supervision nearly halves retrieval accuracy (R@1: 24.9 → 10.2) underscores that directional encoding is not a nice-to-have but a structural requirement for a useful lighting latent space. Key limitations remain: the representation does not capture spatially-varying illumination (critical for complex indoor scenes), and the 72K-sample dataset, while multi-modal, is still modest in scale. Open questions include merging local spatial modalities with global representations for hierarchical control and aligning the learned embeddings with widely-used encoders to simplify integration into existing diffusion pipelines.
