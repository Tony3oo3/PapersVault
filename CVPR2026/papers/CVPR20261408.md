## Problem

RGB-to-TIR translation is inherently one-to-many because thermal appearance depends on physics (material emissivity, active heat sources, environmental conditions) rather than pixel-level style, yet most prior methods reduce it to style transfer or rely on RGB-centric priors (segmentation, edges, scene indices) that are blind to heat-emission physics, producing thermally implausible outputs. No existing framework also provides explicit, interpretable control over thermal appearance at both scene and object level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image, a natural-language prompt specifying desired thermal conditions (e.g., weather, time of day), and optionally a reference RGB image whose thermal attributes should be transferred to the target scene. The VLM produces a structured four-field schema (scene, object, material, heat-emission state) whose final hidden state (L×4096) is projected to L×768 as the conditioning embedding; the diffusion UNet consumes an 8-channel input (4-channel noisy TIR latent concatenated with 4-channel RGB guidance latent) and outputs a 4-channel TIR latent decoded to a pixel-space thermal image.

## Method

TherA couples a thermal-aware VLM (LLaVA 1.5 fine-tuned with LoRA on the R2T2 dataset of 100k RGB-TIR-text triplets) with a latent-diffusion UNet initialized from Stable Diffusion 1.4. The VLM is trained with teacher-forcing on canonicalized thermal schemas generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro, and its final hidden state—rather than free-form text—serves as a compact thermal embedding that encodes scene, object, material, and heat-emission context. A two-layer feedforward TE adapter (inspired by LaVi-Bridge) projects this embedding into the UNet's cross-attention width, replacing the conventional CLIP text conditioning. Inference uses dual classifier-free guidance with separate scales for the RGB-latent condition (s_V) and the VLM thermal condition (s_S), enabling both text-guided scene-level control (weather, time of day) and reference-image-guided object-level control (e.g., toggling a specific car between active and parked) without altering scene geometry.

## Results

On M3FD, TherA achieves PSNR 19.54, SSIM 0.67, FID 87.08, and LPIPS 0.21, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffV2IR (PSNR 18.97, FID 92.57) across all four metrics. On FLIR, TherA reaches PSNR 19.02, SSIM 0.53, FID 83.78, and LPIPS 0.31, again leading all 16 baselines. In zero-shot evaluation (trained only on R2T2, tested on M3FD, FLIR, and CART without fine-tuning), TherA reports up to a 33% average improvement over prior SOTA methods across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TherA outperforms all 16 baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, FID, LPIPS) on both M3FD and FLIR. The closest competitor is DiffV2IR [15], which uses segmentation maps and scene-description priors; TherA improves over it by +0.57 dB PSNR and −5.49 FID on M3FD, and by +0.78 dB PSNR and −7.66 FID on FLIR. Among specialized RGB-to-TIR methods, F-ViTA [16] (foundation-model guided) and PID [23] (physics-informed diffusion) are the next strongest, but both trail TherA by 1.5–2.2 dB in PSNR. General image-to-image translation baselines (InstructPix2Pix [1], Pix2Pix-Turbo [44], BBDM [49]) and example-guided style-transfer methods (CSGO [46], StyleID [47], StyleSSP [48]) lag substantially, confirming that RGB-centric priors are insufficient for thermally plausible translation. In the zero-shot setting (no benchmark-specific fine-tuning), TherA extends its lead to up to 33% average improvement over F-ViTA, DiffV2IR, and ThermalGen across M3FD, FLIR, and CART, attributed to the structured thermal-aware VLM conditioning reducing linguistic noise and stabilizing cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TherA builds on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the VLM-to-UNet bridging strategy of LaVi-Bridge, but departs fundamentally from prior RGB-to-TIR methods that rely on RGB-centric priors (segmentation maps, edges, scene indices) by introducing a thermal-physics-aware VLM (TherA-VLM, fine-tuned from LLaVA 1.5) that produces structured thermal embeddings encoding material emissivity, object activity, and environmental context. The dual-CFG inference strategy is inherited from InstructPix2Pix, but the conditioning signal shifts from generic CLIP text embeddings to a compact, physically grounded thermal representation. The paper also introduces the R2T2 dataset (100k RGB-TIR-text triplets generated with Gemini 2.5 Pro), which has no direct analogue in prior RGB-to-TIR literature where conditioning was either absent or limited to category/layout cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TherA addresses a persistent bottleneck in thermal imaging research—data scarcity—by enabling scalable, controllable pseudo-TIR generation that respects thermal physics, with the added benefit of simulating diurnal cycles (thermal inversion) from a single daytime RGB input. The strong zero-shot generalization (up to 33% improvement across metrics on unseen benchmarks) suggests the thermal-aware VLM conditioning transfers better than dataset-specific priors. Key limitations include operation on relative (not absolute radiometric) thermal imagery and the finding that text-guided control is currently limited to scene-level attributes, leaving object-level text control unresolved. Open questions include whether the framework extends to absolute radiometric calibration, multi-spectral fusion, and whether the structured schema approach generalizes to other physics-constrained translation tasks.
