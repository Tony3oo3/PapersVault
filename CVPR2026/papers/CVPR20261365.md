## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods primarily perturb classifier logits while leaving internal feature representations largely unchanged, resulting in incomplete forgetting that remains recoverable via linear probing or feature inversion. This paper extends the unlearning objective to the representation level and asks whether a provably optimal forgetting operator exists that simultaneously erases the target class from feature space while preserving the geometric structure of retained classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only the forget set (images of the class to be forgotten) and the forget class identifier—no access to the retain set is needed. For POUR-P, the projection operator is constructed from the classifier weight vector of the target class (or its empirical feature mean when only the encoder is available, e.g., CLIP). The output is a modified model that produces uniform predictions on the forgotten class while maintaining Bayes-optimal classification on all retained classes, evaluated on standard image inputs (32×32 CIFAR, 224×224 ImageNet/PathMNIST).

## Method

POUR exploits two new properties of Neural Collapse geometry: (i) a simplex ETF certifies Bayes optimality under isotropic Gaussian class-conditional features, and (ii) the orthogonal projection of a simplex ETF onto the complement of one vertex yields a smaller simplex ETF. POUR-P applies the closed-form projection P = I − w_u w_u^⊤ / ‖w_u‖² to penultimate-layer features, instantaneously removing the forgotten class direction while preserving optimal angular separation among retained classes. POUR-D then propagates this forgetting into the feature extractor via a teacher-student distillation scheme where the projected model serves as the teacher and the student minimizes an L2 alignment loss on the forget set only. The paper proves that the projected representation is representation-equivalent to a retrained model up to orthogonal gauge freedom, making POUR-P optimal under the proposed representation-level weak unlearning criterion.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, POUR-D achieves the best forget-set-only performance across all metrics: AUS of 0.97 (vs. 0.92 for DELETE), RUS^(o) of 0.85 (vs. 0.80 for Boundary Shrink/Expand), and RUS^(r) of 0.47 (vs. 0.42 for Boundary Shrink/Expand), while retaining 92.86% accuracy on the retain set. POUR-P achieves near-perfect classification-level forgetting (Accf = 0.00%, AUS = 1.01) but does not modify encoder representations. On CIFAR-100, POUR-D again leads among forget-set-only methods with AUS 0.95 and RUS^(r) 0.65, outperforming DELETE (AUS 0.81, RUS^(r) 0.58). On PathMNIST with ViT, POUR-D achieves RUS^(o) of 0.63 on the internal test set, the highest among all forget-set-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among forget-set-only methods, POUR-D dominates all baselines on every reported metric. Compared to DELETE [43], the closest competitor, POUR-D improves AUS from 0.92 to 0.97, RUS^(o) from 0.71 to 0.85, RUS^(r) from 0.39 to 0.47, and reduces forget-set accuracy from 2.43% to 0.37%, while retaining 4.13% more accuracy on the retain set (92.86% vs. 88.73%). Boundary Shrink and Boundary Expand [6] achieve comparable RUS^(o) (0.80) but suffer substantially lower retain accuracy (85.30%/85.74% vs. 92.86%) and higher forget accuracy (12.33%/14.63% vs. 0.37%), indicating incomplete forgetting. Random Label and Gradient Ascent [16] perform worst overall, with AUS of only 0.75–0.80 and RUS^(r) of 0.29–0.37, confirming that logit-level perturbations fail to restructure internal representations. FCS [2], which requires the retain set, achieves high AUS (1.00) but lower RUS^(o) (0.66) than POUR-D, suggesting its forgetting is less complete at the representation level despite better classification metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature from logit-level (weak unlearning) to the representation level, building directly on Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., PNAS 2020) to show that class directions form a simplex ETF whose geometry is preserved under orthogonal projection. It provides the theoretical grounding that prior heuristic projection-based unlearning (Kodge et al., TMLR 2024) lacked, proving that removing one ETF vertex via projection yields a smaller ETF and is Bayes-optimal for retained classes. The method also departs from prior unlearning approaches that modify only classifier weights by introducing a distillation variant (POUR-D) that propagates forgetting into the feature extractor, and proposes the RUS metric to evaluate forgetting at the representation level rather than the output layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The provable optimality of a single orthogonal projection for class unlearning is a clean theoretical result that gives practitioners a principled, zero-iteration alternative to iterative unlearning methods, and the RUS metric offers a more rigorous evaluation tool than logit-based scores. However, the approach relies on NC assumptions (isotropic Gaussian class-conditional features, balanced priors) that may not hold under severe class imbalance or in early training phases, and POUR-P does not modify the encoder itself, leaving residual information in the feature extractor. Open questions include whether the projection invariance property extends to multi-class forgetting, imbalanced settings, or non-ETF geometries, and how well POUR-D scales to very large pretrained models where NC may be only approximately satisfied.
