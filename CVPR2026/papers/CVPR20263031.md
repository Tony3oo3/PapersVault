## Problem

Multi-turn RL for VLM agents is hampered by sparse rewards and long-horizon credit assignment, leading to thought collapse. Existing process-guidance methods (GTR, On-Policy Distillation) densify the reward by querying an external teacher model, but these teachers are expensive, often privileged (GPT-4o, Gemini), and impose severe scalability and reproducibility constraints. The paper asks whether step-level guidance can be obtained without any external model dependency.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a visual observation (a single RGB image from the environment), a task instruction, and the accumulated action history, and produces an autoregressive reasoning thought followed by a discrete action token. In Points24 the action is a number or arithmetic operator appended to a running formula; in ALFWorld the action is one of 20+ navigation or interaction commands over episodes exceeding 50 steps. Because the merged teacher is homologous to the agent (same architecture and input format), it consumes the identical context and outputs a reference thought or logit distribution over the same vocabulary, enabling direct comparison without any format translation.

## Method

GTR-Turbo replaces GTR's external corrector with a teacher constructed by TIES-merging (trim, elect sign, selective average) all historical checkpoints saved during ongoing PPO training, yielding a model that is consistently stronger and more stable than the current agent. The merged teacher provides thought-level guidance through two interchangeable mechanisms: (1) an SFT loss where the agent imitates the teacher's autoregressively generated thought tokens, or (2) a reverse-KL soft-logit distillation that computes the per-token log-probability difference in a single forward pass and uses its clipped negative as an auxiliary PPO reward, eliminating the need for teacher generation and a separate thought buffer. Weight assignment across checkpoints uses either simple moving average or exponential moving average (alpha=0.5 optimal). The KL variant further reduces wall-clock time to roughly half of GTR and compute cost to ~40%, while achieving the highest success rates (53.5% on Points24, 15% on ALFWorld) among all compared methods.

## Results

On the Points24 card game, GTR-Turbo (KL variant) achieves a 53.5% success rate and +2.39 episode return, surpassing GTR (44.5% SR, +0.53 ER) by 9 percentage points while reducing training time from 191h to 89h and cost from $307.78 to $114.81. On ALFWorld, GTR-Turbo (KL) reaches a 0.15 average success rate across six subtasks, comparable to GTR's 0.16, while cutting training time from 164h to 78h. The 7B fine-tuned agent outperforms the 72B Qwen2.5-VL model (53.5% vs. 5.6% SR on Points24) and GPT-4o with tools (13.5% SR), demonstrating that domain-specific RL training with self-guided thought supervision yields strong specialization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GTR [50], GTR-Turbo (KL) wins decisively on Points24 (53.5% vs. 44.5% SR, +2.39 vs. +0.53 ER) while using roughly half the training time (89h vs. 191h) and 63% less cost ($114.81 vs. $307.78); on ALFWorld the two methods are nearly tied (0.15 vs. 0.16 average SR) but GTR-Turbo still trains in 78h versus 164h. Against RL4VLM [68], GTR-Turbo dominates on both benchmarks: 53.5% vs. 3.5% SR on Points24 and 0.15 vs. 0.08 average SR on ALFWorld, confirming that thought guidance is essential to prevent entropy collapse in multi-turn settings. Against the privileged Qwen2.5-VL models [3], the 7B GTR-Turbo agent outperforms the 72B model by 48 points on Points24 (53.5% vs. 5.6%) and the 32B model by 53.5 points, showing that task-specific RL with self-guided supervision far exceeds general-purpose scaling. Against Rejection Sampling [67] (Figure 6), GTR-Turbo achieves substantially higher success rates on Points24, demonstrating that active RL exploration with reward feedback is critical where the base model cannot yet generate correct trajectories for imitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

GTR-Turbo is a direct efficiency upgrade to GTR [50], which introduced step-level thought guidance from an external VLM corrector (e.g., GPT-4o) to prevent thought collapse in multi-turn VLM agent RL. The key departure is replacing that costly external teacher with a model constructed by TIES-merging [56] the agent's own historical RL checkpoints, eliminating API dependency while retaining GTR's joint PPO + SFT/KL optimization and DAgger framework. The KL-guided variant also draws on On-Policy Distillation [25] and reverse-KL distillation literature [13, 53], but applies it in a self-contained, teacher-free setting where the 'teacher' is a merged snapshot of the student's own training trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for privileged external models, GTR-Turbo makes multi-turn VLM agent RL substantially more accessible and reproducible, cutting training time by ~50% and compute cost by ~60% relative to GTR while matching or exceeding its performance on Points24. The practical implication is significant for labs without API access or with data-privacy constraints, and the merged-checkpoint-as-teacher idea is simple enough to adopt broadly. However, validation is limited to 7B-scale models on two benchmarks, ALFWorld results are only on par with (not clearly above) GTR, and the authors acknowledge the approach may fail when the base model is too weak (<5% initial success rate), leaving open whether the technique scales to larger models or harder tasks where a genuinely stronger external teacher is still needed.
