## Problem

Existing event-based small object detection methods that operate on point cloud representations focus on local spatiotemporal feature aggregation but lack explicit modeling of global trajectory continuity, resulting in fragmented and discontinuous detections. The paper identifies that target motion trajectories in event point clouds possess distinctive topological signatures (sparse persistent H₀ features, negligible H₁ features) that are fundamentally different from background noise, yet no prior method exploits this structural distinction to enforce trajectory coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260881/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams as 3D spatiotemporal point clouds with (x, y, t, p) coordinates, preserving native microsecond-level temporal resolution without densification into frames or voxel grids. The output is a per-event binary probability (target vs. background/noise) that, when thresholded, yields continuous object trajectories in the (x, y, t) space. The task is formulated as event-level semantic segmentation rather than bounding-box regression, with detection metrics (P_d, F_a) derived from the segmentation outputs.

## Method

SpTopoNet is a sparse convolutional encoder-decoder that incorporates topological constraints at two levels: the Topological Learning Module (TLM) uses graph attention with inverse-distance edge weights to capture local connectivity patterns in k-NN neighborhoods, while the Spatial Consistency Module (SCM) combines global average-pooled context with local spatial attention to enforce long-range trajectory coherence. The key training innovation is EvTopoLoss, which computes persistent homology (H₀ and H₁) on both predicted and ground-truth point clouds via Ripser and penalizes discrepancies through a Betti number loss (correct feature counts) and a Wasserstein distance loss (correct persistence distributions). To make this differentiable and efficient, the paper introduces an active region mechanism that restricts gradient computation to points near the binarization threshold, using geometric distance to ground truth to determine gradient sign, reducing the O(n³) matching cost to local distance evaluations processed in batches.

## Results

On the EV-UAV benchmark, SpTopoNet achieves 66.62% IoU, 74.43% ACC, 83.36% detection rate (Pd), and 1.29×10⁻⁴ false alarm rate (Fa), with 4.4M parameters and 56.5 ms runtime. This represents an 11.44% IoU improvement and 5.83% Pd improvement over the strongest prior method (Ev-SpSegNet, 55.18% IoU, 77.53% Pd). The method also achieves the lowest false alarm rate among all compared approaches, reducing Fa from 1.63×10⁻⁴ (Ev-SpSegNet) to 1.29×10⁻⁴.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpTopoNet outperforms all 16 baselines across every reported metric (IoU, ACC, Pd, Fa). The closest competitor is Ev-SpSegNet [4], the prior point-cloud-based method on the same EV-UAV benchmark, which SpTopoNet surpasses by 11.44% in IoU (66.62 vs. 55.18), 9.41% in ACC (74.43 vs. 65.02), and 5.83% in Pd (83.36 vs. 77.53), while also achieving a lower false alarm rate (1.29 vs. 1.63 ×10⁻⁴). Among voxel-grid methods, RVT [10] is the strongest at 43.21% IoU, still trailing SpTopoNet by 23.41 points. Frame-based detectors (SSD, Faster RCNN, DETR, YOLOV10-S) and SNN-based methods (EMS-YOLO, Spike-YOLO) lag substantially, with IoU values between 25–44%, and suffer from much higher false alarm rates (55–690 ×10⁻⁴). Notably, SpTopoNet achieves these gains with only 4.4M parameters—comparable to Ev-SpSegNet (4.0M) and far fewer than KPConv (50.1M) or Spike-YOLO (69.0M)—at a modest runtime of 56.5 ms, only ~1.6× slower than Ev-SpSegNet's 35.9 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Ev-SpSegNet [4], a sparse convolutional encoder-decoder for event-based small object detection, by injecting topological structure awareness into both the architecture and the training objective. It departs from prior sparse point-cloud methods (KPConv, RandLA-Net, COSeg) that aggregate only local spatiotemporal neighborhoods, arguing that global trajectory topology—formalized via persistent homology—is the missing signal for distinguishing continuous target motion from scattered noise. The EvTopoLoss adapts ideas from topology-preserving image segmentation (TopoSeg, Betti matching, PHGCN) to the unstructured event point-cloud setting, introducing a geometry-aware active-region gradient mechanism to sidestep the O(n³) cost and matching ambiguity of classical persistence diagram comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The topological framing offers a principled way to enforce trajectory continuity in event-based detection, a failure mode (fragmentation at turns, missed detections under occlusion) that local-attention methods cannot address. The demonstrated generalizability of EvTopoLoss across heterogeneous backbones (Ev-SpSegNet, 3D-UNet, PointNet++) suggests the loss could transfer to other event-based segmentation or tracking tasks. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (EV-UAV) and a single task (per-event binary segmentation), leaving open whether the topological constraints remain beneficial in multi-object tracking, denser scenes, or with different event-camera hardware. The authors themselves flag extension to multi-object tracking and more complex dynamic scenarios as future work.
