## Problem

Video-3DGS frameworks for open-world simulation (e.g., Vid2Sim, GaussGym) produce non-metric camera poses, fragmented collision meshes, and severely degraded extrapolated views, making them unreliable for reproducible embodied navigation benchmarking. No existing real-to-sim pipeline simultaneously delivers photorealistic rendering, metric-scale geometric grounding, and robust novel-view synthesis across large-scale indoor-outdoor urban environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261890/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests synchronized multi-sensor streams from a MetaCam handheld scanner: Livox Mid-360 LiDAR, RTK-GNSS, IMU, and two 4K fisheye cameras (>180° FOV). Fisheye images are cropped to 120° and undistorted into perspective views for 3DGS training, while dynamic objects and faces are masked out. The output is a complete USD scene pairing a trained 3DGS model (photorealistic renderer) with a marching-cubes collision mesh (physics layer), plus NavMesh-derived expert trajectories and VLM-generated language instructions, all sharing a single metric coordinate frame.

## Method

WANDERLAND uses a LiDAR-inertial-visual-GNSS (LIV) SLAM system to produce globally consistent, metric-scale point clouds and camera poses, which serve as the geometric backbone for all downstream tasks. 3D Gaussians are initialized directly from this dense point cloud (with opacity parameterized inversely to KNN volumetric density), then optimized with combined photometric and depth losses where depth ground truth is obtained by projecting the initialized Gaussians to each camera view. Difix3D+ is used to generate clean augmented novel views that are gradually expanded away from training views to stabilize large-scale 3DGS training and improve extrapolated rendering. In parallel, a collision mesh is extracted from the same point cloud via voxelization and marching cubes, ensuring the physics layer is geometrically consistent with the rendering layer. The final USD scene is loaded into Isaac Sim, enabling closed-loop RL training and evaluation of navigation policies with both photorealistic sensor simulation and reliable geometric interaction.

## Results

On the WANDERLAND dataset, the best vision-only reconstruction method (COLMAP with ground-truth calibration) still achieves only 0.30 m translational ATE after scale alignment and 5.0° rotational ATE, with AUC@30 of 0.83 and success rate of 0.87. Deep reconstruction models perform substantially worse: DUSt3R reaches only 0.12 AUC@30 and 0.39 SR, while the strongest deep method (π³) achieves 0.64 AUC@30 and 0.89 SR but with 4.7 m scaled translational error. For novel view synthesis, the LIV-SLAM-initialized 3DGS achieves 20.37 dB PSNR (interpolated) and 17.92 dB (extrapolated), outperforming Vid2Sim (17.20/16.49 dB) and GaussGym (12.17/12.63 dB). In RL navigation, training in the geometrically grounded environment improves CityWalker's success rate by 14% and reduces navigation error by 10%, whereas training in Vid2Sim's environment degrades performance (SR −21%, NE +24%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares WANDERLAND's LIV-SLAM pipeline against 12 baselines spanning SfM (COLMAP, GLOMAP), deep reconstruction (DUSt3R, MUSt3R, VGGT, π³, MapAnything, DA3), and video-3DGS frameworks (Vid2Sim, GaussGym). On reconstruction accuracy (Table 2), even the best vision-only method (COLMAP with ground-truth intrinsics) retains 0.30 m scaled translational error and 5° rotational error, while deep methods like DUSt3R are an order of magnitude worse (20 m scaled ATE, 0.12 AUC@30). On novel view synthesis (Table 3), WANDERLAND's LIV-SLAM-initialized 3DGS outperforms Vid2Sim by 3.2 dB PSNR (interpolated) and 1.4 dB (extrapolated), and GaussGym by 8.2 dB (interpolated) and 5.3 dB (extrapolated). For RL navigation (Table 4), training in WANDERLAND's geometrically grounded environment consistently improves all three navigation policies (NoMaD, CityWalker, MBRA), while training in Vid2Sim's environment causes degradation in 5 out of 6 cases. On the navigation benchmark (Table 5), no model exceeds 47% success rate (NaVILA, indoor), confirming a large open research gap in open-world embodied navigation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: multi-sensor SLAM-based reconstruction (building on Fast-LIVO2, R3Live, and GVINS for metric-scale mapping) and 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic rendering (initialized from LiDAR point clouds and augmented with Difix3D+). It explicitly departs from the recent video-3DGS pipeline paradigm (Vid2Sim, GaussGym) that relies on RGB-only capture and SfM or deep reconstruction, arguing that these approaches produce non-metric, fragmented geometry unsuitable for reproducible benchmarking. The framework also extends classic indoor real-to-sim datasets (Matterport3D, ScanNet, HM3D) from RGB-D tripod capture to handheld multi-sensor open-world capture, while replacing textured meshes with a hybrid 3DGS + LiDAR-mesh representation for both rendering and collision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that geometric grounding via LiDAR is not optional but essential for reliable open-world embodied AI simulation, providing a 530-scene, 3.8M sqm dataset that fills a critical gap between indoor-only metric datasets and geometrically unreliable video-based outdoor datasets. The demonstration that RL training in geometrically inaccurate environments actively degrades navigation policies (rather than merely failing to improve them) has practical implications for how the community constructs training simulators. Key limitations include the 1 FPS capture rate limiting view density, the absence of dynamic agents (pedestrians, vehicles), and the proprietary nature of the MetaCam Studio reconstruction pipeline, which limits reproducibility of the SLAM step. Open questions include whether the geometric grounding advantage persists at even larger scales (city-block level) and how to integrate dynamic scene elements without sacrificing the static geometric foundation.
