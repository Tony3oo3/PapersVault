## Problem

Existing face anti-spoofing methods rely on a single visual modality, limiting cross-domain generalization and offering no interpretable rationale for their decisions. While Multimodal Large Language Models could address both limitations through visual-linguistic reasoning, the field lacks a high-quality vision-language dataset with structured Chain-of-Thought annotations suitable for MLLM training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB face image resized to 448×448×3 and a fixed text prompt asking whether the face is a spoof. It produces a structured six-part CoT response in a strict XML-tagged format (Caption → Facial Description → Facial Attributes → Reasoning → Spoofing Description → Conclusion), where the final Conclusion tag contains a binary Yes/No judgment. The hierarchical format enforces a global-to-local reasoning path, progressing from scene-level context through fine-grained facial attributes to a logical verdict.

## Method

The core contribution is the FaceCoT dataset (1.08M samples, 14 attack types) built via a three-stage pipeline: GPT-4o auto-annotation with human-in-the-loop refinement for a 100K gold subset, followed by training a dedicated FAS caption model using SFT augmented with VRFT-inspired verified reinforcement fine-tuning (accuracy reward on Conclusion correctness, format reward on template compliance) to expand to 982K silver annotations. For training the MLLM (MiniCPM-V-2.6-8B), the authors propose CoT-Enhanced Progressive Learning (CEPL): Stage 1 performs full-parameter SFT exclusively on CoT annotations to sharpen the vision encoder's sensitivity to fine-grained spoofing artifacts; Stage 2 inherits that encoder, resets the connector and language decoder to pretrained weights, applies LoRA to the LLM, and jointly optimizes CoT generation and binary classification with a multi-task loss. This staged design prevents the faster-converging classification objective from interfering with reasoning learning, allowing the model to fully exploit the fine-grained visual cues embedded in CoT annotations.

## Results

Trained on the full FaceCoT dataset (1.08M samples), the proposed method achieves an average HTER of 6.30% and AUC of 97.77% across 11 benchmark FAS datasets, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (I-FAS) by 5.00% in HTER reduction and 4.06% in AUC improvement. The method attains the best score on every individual evaluation set. On the most challenging cross-domain benchmarks with unseen attack types (HKBU-MARs-V1+ and HiFiMask), the AUC gaps widen to approximately 10% and 14% over the prior best, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baseline families: ViTAF (ECCV 2022), ViT-B/ViT-L (CLIP, ICML 2021), FLIP (CVPR 2023), and I-FAS (AAAI 2025, the prior SOTA using MLLMs for FAS). Ours-All achieves the best HTER and AUC on all 11 benchmark datasets, with no losses or ties. Against the closest competitor I-FAS, the average improvement is 5.00% in HTER (11.30% → 6.30%) and 4.06% in AUC (93.71% → 97.77%); the gap is especially pronounced on cross-domain sets with unseen attack types, where AUC jumps from 88.77% to 98.39% on HKBU-MARs-V1+ and from 77.17% to 91.30% on HiFiMask. Against FLIP, the average AUC gap is roughly 10 percentage points (87.90% → 97.77%), and against the ViT-based baselines (ViT-B, ViT-L, ViTAF) the gap exceeds 12–15 percentage points in AUC. Even when restricted to the same CelebA-Spoof-only training source as the baselines (Ours-CelebA), the method still leads I-FAS by 2.74% in HTER and 2.66% in AUC on average, confirming that the CoT annotations and CEPL strategy contribute gains independent of dataset scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant FAS paradigm of single-modality visual classifiers (CNNs, ViTs, domain adaptation/generalization) by reframing the problem as a multimodal VQA task with structured chain-of-thought reasoning. Conceptually, it extends the CoT prompting lineage from MLLMs (LLaVA-CoT, PS-CoT, CoTPT) into the biometric security domain, where no prior work had applied hierarchical reasoning to spoof detection. The RL component for the caption model draws directly on VisualRFT's verified reinforcement fine-tuning scheme, adapting its reward design to FAS-specific accuracy and format constraints. The CEPL progressive learning strategy is novel in its two-stage separation of visual perception (full-parameter SFT on CoT) from joint reasoning-classification (LoRA-based multi-task training), addressing task interference that single-stage MLLM training suffers from.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FaceCoT (1.08M samples, 14 attack types) fills a critical gap: no prior FAS dataset provided the structured language annotations needed to train MLLMs for interpretable spoof detection, and the RL-enhanced caption model offers a scalable annotation pipeline that the community can reuse. The strong cross-domain results on unseen attack types (≈14% AUC gain on HiFiMask) suggest CoT reasoning captures transferable visual cues beyond memorized attack signatures, though this claim rests on a limited set of target benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to entirely novel attack modalities (e.g., deepfakes, liveness challenges), whether the 8B-parameter MiniCPM-V backbone is necessary or smaller models suffice, and whether the marginal RL gains observed in ablation indicate that data quality (Gold100K) rather than RL is the true driver of performance.
