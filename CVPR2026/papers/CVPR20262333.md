## Problem

Existing fast multi-view clustering methods learn anchors in a single space using orthogonal or related constraints, which fails to capture complementary information available across multiple spaces. Additionally, the space whose basis is the anchored cluster center is largely neglected in anchor learning, limiting the discriminative quality of the resulting anchors.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view data where each view p has matrix X_p ∈ R^{d_p × n} with potentially different dimensions (e.g., 512, 576, 640, 944 for face/video datasets) and a small number of anchors m (typically 4c where c is the number of clusters) to represent n samples. The output is a binary cluster assignment matrix R ∈ {0,1}^{c×n} where R_{ij}=1 indicates sample j belongs to cluster i, obtained directly from the optimization without a separate spectral or K-means step (though K-means is run 50 times for final label alignment in evaluation).

## Method

DOSFMVC learns anchors in dual orthogonal space by jointly optimizing an objective that combines (1) data reconstruction X_p ≈ A_p W^T R in the anchor space and (2) anchor-to-cluster-center alignment A_p ≈ U_p W in the anchored cluster center space, where both A_p and U_p are constrained to be orthogonal (A_p^T A_p = I, U_p^T U_p = I). The clustering indicator W ∈ R^{c×m} for anchors and R ∈ {0,1}^{c×n} for data are learned simultaneously in a unified framework, replacing the traditional consensus anchor graph. Optimization proceeds via alternating closed-form updates: A_p is solved analytically, R is assigned by nearest cluster centroid, U_p is obtained via SVD of A_p W^T, and W is solved in closed form. The key insight is that the complementary information between the anchor basis space and the cluster center basis space yields more representative and discriminative anchors than single-space learning, while the linear-time complexity (O(n) for n >> k,d,m) preserves scalability.

## Results

DOSFMNVC achieves the best clustering accuracy across all seven benchmark datasets, with headline ACC values of 82.50% on BBCSport, 94.78% on Notting-Hill, 90.87% on YTF-10, 81.00% on YTF-20, 70.40% on YTF-100, and 64.00% on YTF-200. It also leads on NMI (e.g., 88.80% on YTF-10, 86.27% on YTF-200), Purity (e.g., 94.20% on Notting-Hill), and F1-score (e.g., 87.21% on YTF-10). On the largest dataset (YTF-200, ~286K samples), DOSFMNVC runs in 1520 s, faster than ALPC (1760 s) and CFMC (1159 s is faster but with lower accuracy).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DOSFMNVC outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset where a comparison is possible, across all four metrics (ACC, NMI, Purity, F1-score). The closest competitor is ALPC [4], which DOSFMNVC surpasses by 3.50 ACC points on BBCSport (82.50 vs. 79.00), 2.48 on Notting-Hill (94.78 vs. 92.30), 2.60 on YTF-10 (90.87 vs. 88.27), and 1.80 on YTF-200 (64.00 vs. 62.20). CFMC [18] is the second-closest baseline, trailing DOSFMNVC by 7.30 ACC on BBCSport and 4.20 on YTF-10. Traditional methods (SwMC, GMC, PMSC) are limited to small datasets (BBCSport, Notting-Hill) and are substantially outperformed. Among scalable anchor-based methods, LMVSC [7] and SMVSC [51] show the weakest performance on large-scale YTF datasets (e.g., LMVSC scores only 50.30 ACC on YTF-200 vs. 64.00 for DOSFMNVC), highlighting the benefit of dual-space anchor learning. On running time, DOSFMNVC is competitive with ALPC and CFMC on large datasets while delivering consistently higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based large-scale multi-view clustering line (SMVSC, FPMVS, OPMC, ALPC) by identifying a specific limitation: existing methods learn anchors in a single space constrained by orthogonality or other multi-view-derived constraints. DOSFMVC departs from this by simultaneously learning anchors in two orthogonal spaces — the anchor space itself and a second space whose basis is the consensus anchored cluster center — and by replacing the consensus anchor graph with a cluster-center-based representation. The alternating optimization framework and the use of clustering indicators (R) for both anchors and data follow the matrix-factorization-style decomposition common in prior scalable methods, but the dual-space formulation is the distinguishing architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, linear-time (O(n)) method for large-scale multi-view clustering that consistently outperforms strong baselines (ALPC, CFMC) across seven datasets up to ~286K samples, which is practically useful for real-world multi-view applications. The ablation confirms the dual-space component contributes a steady 2–4 point gain, validating the design choice. Open questions include whether the idea generalizes to more than two spaces, whether the strict orthogonal constraint is necessary versus softer regularization, and how the method behaves under incomplete or noisy views — none of which are explored here.
