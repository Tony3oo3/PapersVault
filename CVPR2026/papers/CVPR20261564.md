## Problem

Existing test-time adaptive segmentation methods represent the source distribution with a single class-wise prototype (the average pixel feature per class), which neglects within-class diversity and applies source knowledge in an instance-agnostic manner. This limits the model's ability to capture the full source distribution and to adapt differently to test samples experiencing varying degrees of distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261564/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled test image (processed in a streamed, batch-size-1 fashion at 960×540 resolution) and a set of pre-computed source domain experts (N=14 per class, derived offline via K-means clustering of class-wise prototypes). The gating network consumes a matrix of average Euclidean distances between all image pixels and each expert (X ∈ R^{N×C}), and the final output is a pixel-wise segmentation mask computed as the average of the frozen baseline model's prediction and the MoE-weighted expert prediction.

## Method

During source training, class-wise average pixel features (prototypes) are extracted for each sample and clustered via K-means into N=14 domain-shared experts per class, which are frozen at test time. At test time, a lightweight gating network maps the pixel-to-expert distance matrix to per-expert weights, which are refined by Top-K filtering (K=7) and softmax to select the most relevant experts for the current instance. The MoE prediction is obtained by computing a weighted Euclidean distance to each class's selected experts and applying softmax across classes. Optimization uses a min-max entropy scheme: minimizing prediction entropy (L_min) to promote confident segmentation while maximizing gating-value entropy (L_max) to prevent expert collapse, with a confidence-guided instance weight (α) scaling the total loss based on the proportion of high-confidence pixels. Only the gating network's parameters are updated via backpropagation; the encoder, decoder, and all experts remain frozen, making the module plug-and-play with negligible overhead (+2.33% FLOPs).

## Results

On the Cityscapes-to-ACDC TTA task, the proposed method achieves a mean mIoU of 60.6% and mean mAcc of 72.2% across four weather conditions (Fog, Night, Rain, Snow), outperforming the strongest baseline SVDP (59.7% mIoU) by +0.9%. On the Sim-to-Real TTA task (GTA5→Cityscapes/BDD100K/Mapillary), it reaches an average mIoU of 39.04%, surpassing the best prior method MedBN (37.24%) by +1.8%. In the more challenging Continual TTA setting over three rounds, the method achieves a mean mIoU of 62.5%, exceeding the best baseline Hybrid (62.0%) and SVDP (61.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines across all three evaluation scenarios. On Cityscapes-to-ACDC TTA (Table 1), it beats the closest competitor SVDP by +0.9% mean mIoU (60.6 vs. 59.7) and +1.1% mean mAcc, with the largest gains on Snow (+2.6% mIoU over SVDP) and Fog (+1.3%). On Sim-to-Real TTA (Table 2), it surpasses MedBN (37.24%) by +1.8% average mIoU and SITA (37.13%) by +1.9%, while notably the IN baseline (32.97%) underperforms even the unadapted source model (34.81%), highlighting the risk of discarding source statistics. In Continual TTA (Table 3), the method achieves 62.5% mean mIoU over three rounds, edging out Hybrid (62.0%) and C-MAE (61.8%), and substantially outperforming BECoTTA (59.1%) by +3.4% under identical MoE setup, which the authors attribute to domain-shared experts and the min-max entropy optimization. TENT and DePT show progressive degradation across CTTA rounds (e.g., TENT drops from 56.7% to 55.0%), confirming catastrophic forgetting, whereas the proposed method maintains stable performance (62.4→62.5→62.5%) due to its frozen-expert, plug-and-play design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based test-time adaptation paradigm (CoTTA, OBAO, DIGA) by replacing single class-wise prototypes with multiple K-means-clustered experts per class, and introduces a gating network for instance-wise expert selection. It directly builds on BECoTTA, which first applied MoE to continual TTA, but departs in three key ways: using domain-shared rather than domain-specific experts, promoting domain-agnostic expert utilization rather than domain-expert alignment, and introducing a min-max entropy optimization to prevent expert collapse. The method is deliberately plug-and-play—only the lightweight gating network is trained while the backbone and experts remain frozen—distinguishing it from parameter-updating approaches like Tent and CoTTA that risk catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (only +2.33% FLOPs overhead, no backbone updates) makes this approach attractive for real-world deployment where catastrophic forgetting and compute constraints are critical concerns. The finding that domain-shared experts outperform domain-specific ones (3.4% gap over BECoTTA) challenges the intuition that domain-specific specialization is necessary for adaptation. However, the method acknowledges a cold-start limitation with low-quality initial samples, and the evaluation is confined to driving-scene segmentation benchmarks, leaving open whether the MoE-expert paradigm generalizes to other dense prediction tasks or more extreme distribution shifts.
