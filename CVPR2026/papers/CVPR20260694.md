## Problem

3D human reaction generation requires simultaneously achieving high motion fidelity, real-time single-step inference, and autoregressive adaptability to evolving actor inputs, yet no existing method satisfies all three. Prior online approaches (CAMDM, HumanX, ReGenNet) rely on fixed-length context windows that cause semantic drift over long sequences and multi-step denoising (≥8 steps) that is too slow for real-time use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260694/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text description (encoded via CLIP) and an actor 3D skeleton motion sequence (AMASS 22-joint or SMPL-X 55-joint format) as input, and produces a temporally aligned 3D reactor motion sequence. Actor and reactor motions are first compressed into continuous latent tokens via a causal 1D-CNN VAE (avoiding discrete quantization to preserve the highly dynamic reactor motion). In online mode, generation proceeds at 4-frame granularity with a single forward pass per step; in offline mode, the full sequence is produced in one pass.

## Method

ARMFlow builds on the MeanFlow paradigm, which models the average transport velocity over an interval [r,t] rather than the instantaneous velocity, enabling single-step sample generation from noise to data. The architecture comprises a CNN-VAE for motion compression, a DiT-based causal context encoder that encodes the entire actor-reactor history with causal masking (eliminating fixed-window information loss), and a lightweight 5-layer MLP velocity predictor conditioned on the encoded context via AdaLN. A key training innovation, Bootstrap Contextual Encoding (BSCE), replaces ground-truth histories with the model's own predicted samples from the start of training and progressively increases the number of autoregressive rollout steps, creating an adaptive curriculum that builds robustness against accumulated prediction errors. Because MeanFlow requires only one inference step per rollout iteration, BSCE is substantially cheaper than equivalent strategies in multi-step diffusion models, and classifier-free guidance is integrated during training via a Ground-Truth Field to avoid post-hoc blending overhead.

## Results

On the online generation task, ARMFlow achieves FID 2.178 on InterHuman and 0.042 on InterX, outperforming the second-best online method R2R by 28% and 33% in FID respectively. On the offline task, ReMFlow achieves FID 2.433 on InterHuman and 0.058 on InterX, surpassing all offline baselines including InterMask (3.453) and ReGenNet (2.930) on InterHuman. ARMFlow also achieves the best R-Prec@3 (0.699 on InterHuman, 0.711 on InterX) and lowest MM Dist (3.783, 3.728) among online methods, while ReMFlow attains the best R-Prec@3 (0.708, 0.743) and MM Dist (3.778, 3.570) among offline methods. All comparisons use single-step inference for ARMFlow/ReMFlow versus multi-step (≥8) for diffusion-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARMFlow decisively outperforms all four online baselines on both datasets. On InterHuman, it reduces FID from 2.795 (R2R, the strongest online baseline) to 2.178 (−28%), and from 4.000 (CAMDM) to 2.178 (−46%); on InterX, it improves FID from 0.063 (R2R) to 0.042 (−33%). CAMDM's fixed 10-frame context window causes severe temporal drift on long InterHuman sequences, while ReGenNet's lack of autoregressive reactor conditioning leads to temporal inconsistency. For offline generation, ReMFlow beats InterMask (FID 3.453→2.433 on InterHuman) and MLD (3.588→2.433), though InterMask remains competitive on R-Prec@3 (0.701 vs 0.708). ReGenNet in offline mode (FID 2.930) is the closest competitor but still trails ReMFlow by ~17% in FID on InterHuman. Notably, ARMFlow's online FID (2.178) is even lower than ReMFlow's offline FID (2.433) on InterHuman, suggesting the autoregressive context encoding provides a net benefit despite using only partial sequence conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the autoregressive reaction generation line (CAMDM, HumanX, ReGenNet) by replacing multi-step diffusion denoisers with the recently proposed MeanFlow single-step velocity predictor, and by replacing fixed-length context windows with a causal full-history DiT encoder. The motion compression follows T2M-GPT's CNN-VAE, and the overall architecture draws on MAR's context-encoder-plus-MLP-predictor design, but the key departure is the MeanFlow objective (modeling average velocity rather than instantaneous velocity) combined with the novel Bootstrap Contextual Encoding training strategy, which supersedes HumanX's gradual rollout by using generated histories from the start of training with a scheduled increase in autoregressive depth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time 3D human reaction generation is a prerequisite for interactive applications in HRI, AR, and VR, where even tens of milliseconds of latency degrade perceived responsiveness. The single-step inference (vs. 8–50 denoising steps in prior online methods) is a concrete efficiency win that makes fine-grained temporal resolution practical, and the finding that autoregressive online generation can match or slightly exceed offline FID challenges the assumption that full-sequence conditioning is strictly necessary. Open questions include whether MeanFlow's lack of post-hoc classifier guidance limits controllability, how the method scales to more than two interacting agents, and whether the BSCE curriculum generalizes to other autoregressive domains beyond motion.
