## Problem

DiT-based video object removal methods like MiniMax Remover perform dense denoising and attention over the entire spatiotemporal token space, even though only a small masked region actually requires processing. Since around 70% of real-world VOR cases have mask ratios below 20%, this full-token computation paradigm introduces substantial redundant computation and high inference latency (e.g., ~10 FPS) that scales independently of mask size. No existing approach achieves mask-aware, token-efficient computation for DiT-based VOR while preserving visual quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked video (RGB frames at 480×832 resolution with 4N+1 frames) and a binary object mask as input, and produces a restored video with the masked object removed. YOSE is a plug-in fine-tuning framework applied on top of existing DiT-based VOR backbones (MiniMax Remover, VACE), so the input/output interface matches the base model. During training, random masks are sampled over VPData video clips, and the output is fused with the original unmasked regions via a weighted mask to produce the final video.

## Method

YOSE introduces two complementary components: Batch Variable-length Indexing (BVI) and a Diffusion Process Simulator (DiffSim) module. BVI replaces hard tensor indexing with a differentiable grid-sample-based coordinate mapping that selects only masked-region tokens, supports variable-length token sequences across batch samples via padding, and enables end-to-end gradient flow. DiffSim simulates the diffusion process influence of unmasked (outer) tokens within each DiT block's self-attention by constructing proxy key-value features from a residual latent (noise minus masked latent) combined with the masked latent through three learnable parameter groups (combining G, scaling S, bias), allowing inner tokens to attend to simulated outer context without explicit full-token computation. A boundary fusion strategy aligns mean-variance statistics in a dilated overlap region to eliminate visible seams. Only the three DiffSim parameter groups are trained (via flow-matching loss masked to the object region), requiring ~4 hours on 8 GPUs, and the resulting inference time scales linearly with mask ratio, achieving up to 2.5× speedup at 20% mask ratio.

## Results

YOSE achieves up to 2.5× inference speedup at 20% mask ratio and 3.3× at 5% mask ratio on 480P 81-frame videos, while maintaining visual quality comparable to the full-token MiniMax Remover baseline. On YouTube-VOS, YOSE(MiniMax) slightly improves background PSNR from 30.33 to 31.01 dB over the original MiniMax Remover. When applied to VACE, YOSE(VACE) boosts background PSNR by 5.47 dB and SSIM by 0.07 on YouTube-VOS, and improves PSNR by 3.19 dB on DAVIS, demonstrating that mask-aware computation also prevents unintended modifications to unmasked regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MiniMax Remover (the primary base model), YOSE achieves nearly identical VBench perceptual scores while improving background PSNR from 30.33 to 31.01 dB on YouTube-VOS, confirming that mask-aware token selection does not degrade quality and actually preserves unmasked regions better. Against VACE, YOSE(VACE) substantially outperforms the baseline on background fidelity, boosting PSNR by 5.47 dB (from 23.72 to ~29.19) and SSIM by 0.07 on YouTube-VOS, and by 3.19 dB on DAVIS, because DiffSim prevents the DiT from inadvertently altering unmasked areas. Compared to Propainter, YOSE trades some background PSNR (41.14 dB for Propainter vs. ~31 dB for YOSE) for superior VBench generation quality (higher Aesthetic Quality and Dynamic Degree), reflecting the generative vs. propagation paradigm difference. Against ROSE, YOSE(MiniMax) achieves comparable or better background metrics (PSNR 31.01 vs. 26.76) while ROSE leads on Dynamic Degree (0.7778), though ROSE does not use a fixed mask making direct comparison less straightforward. Overall, YOSE's key advantage is efficiency: it matches or slightly exceeds baseline quality while delivering 2.5–3.3× speedup that scales linearly with mask size, a property none of the compared baselines possess.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOSE positions itself as a lightweight fine-tuning framework atop existing DiT-based video object removal models, primarily MiniMax Remover (NeurIPS 2025) and secondarily VACE (ICCV 2025), rather than proposing a new generative architecture. It departs from the full-token computation paradigm inherited from DiT backbones (Wan 2.1, VideoDiT) by introducing Batch Variable-length Indexing (BVI) for differentiable, mask-aware token selection and a DiffSim module that simulates the diffusion process influence of unmasked regions using only three learnable parameters per DiT block. Conceptually, it bridges the efficiency gap between sparse/localized generation tasks and the dense attention mechanisms of transformers, drawing on grid-sampling-based differentiable indexing to enable variable-length batch processing without architectural redesign.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: with ~70% of real-world VOR cases having mask ratios below 20%, the linear-scaling computation (2.5–3.3× speedup) makes DiT-based VOR substantially more deployable at interactive frame rates. The finding that YOSE(VACE) actually improves background PSNR by 5.47 dB over the unmodified VACE suggests that restricting computation to masked regions can reduce unintended background corruption, a side benefit beyond pure speed. Limitations include evaluation only at 480p resolution, validation on just two base models, and reliance on the assumption that three scalar parameters per block suffice to simulate unmasked-token diffusion influence; open questions include scalability to higher resolutions, longer videos, and whether the approach generalizes to other DiT-based editing tasks (e.g., outpainting, style transfer) where the mask structure differs.
