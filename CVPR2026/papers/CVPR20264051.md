## Problem

Existing dynamic multimodal fusion methods (e.g., QMF, PDF) rely on scalar proxies such as unimodal loss or confidence scores to indirectly satisfy generalization bounds, which fails to capture the full risk introduced by probabilistic inconsistency between modality predictions. This paper identifies that inter-modal conflict in predictive distributions is the dominant factor governing fusion performance, yet no prior work formulates fusion as a direct, per-sample risk minimization over the distributional space.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes per-sample softmax predictive distributions from each modality's encoder (e.g., RGB and depth logits for scene recognition, image and text logits for sentiment analysis, or audio/image/text logits for multi-modal sentiment) and produces a per-sample weight vector on the probability simplex Δ^{M−1} via a few Frank-Wolfe iterations. The weighted combination of these distributions yields the final fused prediction and class label. The inner FW optimization does not propagate gradients back to the encoders; only the outer cross-entropy loss trains the modality-specific networks.

## Method

CoRiM defines a differentiable Modality Conflict Risk function R(w) = α·H(p_w) + β·H_m(w) + γ·JS_m(w), combining fused-distribution entropy, weighted per-modality entropy as a confidence prior, and average Jensen-Shannon divergence between each modality and the fused consensus. Minimizing R(w) over the probability simplex is a non-convex constrained problem; the authors prove R(w) is L-smooth and possesses a DC structure, which guarantees Frank-Wolfe convergence to a stationary point at O(1/√T) rate. The FW solver linearizes R(w) at each iteration, selects the simplex vertex (modality) with the steepest risk descent, and updates weights via convex combination—avoiding costly gradient projections while adaptively suppressing high-conflict modalities per sample.

## Results

On NYUD v2 scene recognition, CoRiM achieves 72.36% average accuracy (72.02% worst-case) on clean data, outperforming the strongest baseline PDF (71.37%/70.18%) and QMF (69.54%/68.65%). Under high noise (ε=10), CoRiM maintains 63.04% average accuracy versus PDF's 62.56% and QMF's 60.18%. On SUN RGB-D, CoRiM reaches 63.59% average accuracy on clean data (vs. PDF 60.60%, QMF 62.09%) and 48.12% under ε=10 noise. On image-text benchmarks (MVSA, FOOD101), CoRiM leads with 81.12% and 93.62% average accuracy respectively on clean data, extending to 65.34% and 63.71% under ε=10 noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRiM consistently outperforms all dynamic fusion baselines (QMF, PDF, DynMM, TMC) across both scene recognition datasets under all noise levels, with the largest margins appearing under high noise (ε=10): +2.86% over QMF and +0.48% over PDF on NYUD v2 average accuracy. On SUN RGB-D, CoRiM achieves a notable +2.50% average accuracy gain over PDF at ε=0 (63.59% vs. 60.60%), though QMF slightly edges out CoRiM at ε=10 (48.58% vs. 48.12% average). Against static fusion baselines (Concat, Late Fusion), CoRiM gains 2.3–3.3% on clean data and 2.5–5.5% under noise. On image-text benchmarks (Table 1), CoRiM leads PDF by +1.18% on MVSA (ε=0) and +2.25% at ε=10, and by +0.30% on FOOD101 (ε=0). The three-modality extension (Table 6) shows CoRiM surpassing ReconBoost by +0.60% on MOSEI and +0.50% on MOSI, while trailing slightly on CH-SIMS (73.68% vs. 73.88%), confirming scalability to M≥3 modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the scalar-proxy paradigm established by QMF (ICML 2023) and PDF (ICML 2024), which derive fusion weights by correlating them with estimated losses or confidence scores to satisfy a generalization bound. CoRiM instead formulates dynamic fusion as direct per-sample minimization of a differentiable risk function over probability distributions, drawing a conceptual analogy from domain adaptation discrepancy bounds (Ben-David et al., Mansour et al.) to the multimodal setting. The Frank-Wolfe algorithm, a classical tool for simplex-constrained optimization, is repurposed here as a projection-free solver for the non-convex modality conflict risk, with the authors proving L-smoothness to extend convergence guarantees beyond the convex regime where FW is traditionally applied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing of modality conflict as a usable signal (rather than noise to suppress) and the shift from scalar fitting to distributional risk minimization offer a cleaner theoretical foundation for dynamic fusion that could guide future work beyond the 2–3 modality classification settings tested here. However, the method is limited to decision-level fusion with per-sample FW iterations, which may be computationally costly at scale, and the empirical gains over strong baselines (PDF, QMF) are consistent but modest (1–3%). Open questions include whether the risk-minimization formulation extends to feature-level or intermediate fusion, whether the FW solver scales to many modalities or high-dimensional weight spaces, and whether the L-smoothness and DC-structure properties hold for more complex risk decompositions.
