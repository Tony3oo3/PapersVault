## Problem

Few-step text-to-multiview (T2MV) diffusion models (e.g., LCM-SDXL with MV-Adapter) enable real-time generation but sacrifice per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency. Existing RL finetuning methods for single-image diffusion do not transfer to this setting because they ignore cross-view coordination and produce weak learning signals when few-step samples yield tightly clustered rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263759/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and V viewpoint-specific camera embeddings as input and produces V spatially consistent images of the same scene. Finetuning is applied via LoRA to both the MV-Adapter and LCM-SDXL layers, operating at 8 sampling steps and 6 views per trajectory. Single-view rewards (PickScore, HPSv2) score each generated image independently, while the joint-view reward (HyperScore overall) scores the full set of V views collectively.

## Method

MVC-ZigAL introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a multiview-aware MDP jointly models all V views' denoising trajectories with a joint-view reward, enabling cross-view-aware policy optimization. Second, a zigzag multiview sampling (ZMV-Sampling) scheme performs a three-step high-guidance/low-guidance/high-guidance inversion-denoising pass at the first sampling step to produce self-refined trajectories; the reward gap between these refined and standard trajectories serves as a stronger advantage signal (MV-ZigAL) than absolute rewards in the few-step regime. Third, a Lagrangian dual formulation balances single-view fidelity rewards against a lower-bound constraint on the joint-view reward, solved via adaptive primal-dual updates (larger step on constraint violation, smaller on satisfaction) under a self-paced EMA threshold curriculum that tightens the constraint as the policy improves.

## Results

On the 45-animal-name prompt set with 8 sampling steps and 6 views, MV-ZigAL achieves the best HyperScore Overall of 9.17 (vs. 7.23 baseline), with Alignment 9.20, Geometry 9.92, and Texture 7.84, while also attaining the highest PickScore of 0.205. On the out-of-distribution MATE-3D benchmark (160 unseen prompts), the full MVC-ZigAL framework with first-step zigzag reaches HyperScore Overall 7.04, PickScore 0.217, HPSv2 0.268, and ImageReward 0.180, substantially outperforming the baseline (6.67 / 0.204 / 0.252 / −0.846). The reward gap between ZMV-Sampling and standard sampling narrows and converges over training, confirming successful internalization of the self-refinement advantage into the base model weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-ZigAL outperforms all three adapted T2I RL baselines across every HyperScore sub-metric and PickScore. Against MV-PG (adapted from Black et al. [3] and Fan et al. [7]), it gains +0.78 in HyperScore Overall (9.17 vs. 8.39) and +0.002 in PickScore. Against MV-DPO (adapted from Yang et al. [53]), the margin is larger: +1.17 in HyperScore Overall (9.17 vs. 8.00) and +0.005 in PickScore, indicating that preference-based optimization is the weakest of the three paradigms in the few-step multiview regime. The closest competitor is MV-RDL (adapted from Gao et al. [9] and Deng et al. [4]), which reaches 9.03 Overall and 0.203 PickScore; MV-ZigAL edges it out by +0.14 in Overall and +0.002 in PickScore, with the gap driven primarily by the self-refinement advantage signal rather than the reward-difference regression target. On the out-of-distribution MATE-3D benchmark, the full MVC-ZigAL framework further separates from the weighted-sum alternative (WS-ZigAL), which underperforms in HyperScore across all weight settings (w_mv ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0}) despite competitive single-view scores, confirming that the Lagrangian-constrained formulation balances fidelity and consistency more effectively than manual reward weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL finetuning paradigm for diffusion models—established for single-image T2I via policy gradient (DPOK), DPO, and reward difference learning (REBEL, PRDP)—to the text-to-multiview (T2MV) setting by reformulating the denoising process as a multiview-aware MDP with joint-view rewards. It further builds on zigzag diffusion's self-reflection mechanism, repurposing it not as a test-time inference trick but as a source of structured learning signals (advantage learning) for few-step T2MV finetuning. The Lagrangian dual formulation for balancing per-view fidelity and cross-view consistency departs from the more common weighted-sum reward combination, drawing on constrained policy optimization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-step T2MV generation is critical for real-time applications (e.g., interactive 3D content creation), yet the quality degradation from aggressive step reduction has lacked a principled finetuning remedy; this paper provides one with a general framework applicable to any T2MV backbone. However, the evaluation is confined to a single adapter (MV-Adapter) on LCM-SDXL with 6 views and 8 steps, and the prompt set is limited to 45 animal names plus 160 MATE-3D prompts, leaving open whether the advantage-learning and constrained-optimization strategies transfer to other T2MV architectures, larger view counts, or more diverse scene categories. The authors note potential extensions to video generation and 3D reconstruction, but these remain unexplored.
