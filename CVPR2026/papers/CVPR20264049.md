## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting's semitransparent rendering prevents the use of occlusion culling, a highly effective technique in traditional graphics that relies on opaque geometry. While Level-of-Detail methods have been adapted to 3DGS, occlusion culling at the Gaussian level remains unexplored, leaving significant rendering redundancy unaddressed—especially in composed scenes with many asset instances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264049/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS asset (Gaussian means, opacities, scales, rotations) along with per-frame camera parameters (position, FoV, forward vector) and instance transforms for composed scenes. At render time, the visibility MLP receives exactly 16 inputs per Gaussian—normalized mean, normalized view direction, normalized distance, camera forward vector, and a 6-dimensional learned Gaussian embedding—producing a single scalar visibility prediction. The instanced rasterizer then outputs a rendered image, instantiating only Gaussians that survive both frustum and occlusion culling, supporting scenes exceeding 100 million Gaussians at real-time rates.

## Method

The approach has three components: (1) visibility extraction by rendering 2000 Fibonacci-sampled views from a trained asset and recording per-Gaussian contribution (α·T) to determine visibility, with auxiliary views and random offsets for robustness against popping and projection artifacts; (2) distillation into a lightweight MLP (two hidden layers, 32 neurons, ReLU) implemented in tiny-cudann, trained with a frequency-weighted BCE loss, where a secondary MLP precomputes 6-dim Gaussian embeddings from opacity/scale/rotation so the main MLP uses exactly 16 inputs aligned for Tensor Core efficiency; (3) a novel instanced rasterizer that stores each unique asset once with a list of instance transforms, performs frustum culling on non-instanced Gaussians, queries the visibility MLP per instance (converting global-space inputs back to local training space including FoV correction), and only creates per-tile instances for surviving Gaussians—avoiding expensive preprocessing and sorting of occluded splats and reducing VRAM by ~4× compared to V3DG.

## Results

On three composed scenes (~60M Gaussians each at 1080p), the proposed method achieves near-perfect image quality: PSNR 52.7/48.6/58.3 and SSIM 0.999 across FOREST, CROWD, and DONUTSEA, with FLIP error as low as 0.002. VRAM usage is dramatically reduced to 4.0–4.5 GB (downscaled) or 6.1–8.1 GB (full-scale), compared to 17.7–20.4 GB for V3DG and 9.7–16.9 GB for gsplat. The instanced rasterizer alone (without MLP) already cuts VRAM by ~4× versus V3DG, and adding the visibility MLP further reduces VRAM by 25% while increasing FPS by ~10 on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gsplat (the standard 3DGS rasterizer), the proposed method achieves substantially higher PSNR (e.g., 52.7 vs. 29.5 on FOREST with radius clipping) and lower FLIP error (0.002 vs. 0.056) while using roughly 4× less VRAM (4.0 GB vs. 9.7 GB). Against V3DG, the primary state-of-the-art for composed scenes, the method wins on every metric across all three scenes: higher PSNR (52.7 vs. 42.3 on FOREST, 48.6 vs. 43.2 on CROWD), higher SSIM (0.999 vs. 0.993/0.991), lower FLIP (0.002 vs. 0.012/0.013), and dramatically lower VRAM (4.0–4.5 GB vs. 17.7–20.4 GB, a ~4× reduction). On DONUTSEA the PSNR is essentially tied (58.3 vs. 58.2) but SSIM and FLIP still favor the proposed method. The paper notes that V3DG's LoD strategy becomes competitive at very long distances where the number of visible Gaussians drops sharply, whereas the proposed occlusion-culling MLP excels at close and medium ranges; the two approaches are described as complementary rather than mutually exclusive.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [5] and departs from the dominant LoD-based approaches for large-scale 3DGS rendering (H3DG, LODGE, Octree-GS, FLoD, V3DG) by introducing occlusion culling—a technique well-established for opaque triangle meshes but previously inapplicable to semitransparent Gaussians. The key conceptual move is recognizing that volume rendering's transmittance saturation implicitly encodes a soft visibility signal that can be distilled into a tiny shared MLP (18 kB) per asset. The novel instanced rasterizer extends the gsplat [29] tiled pipeline with virtualized instantiation, and the MLP is implemented via tiny-cuda-nn [14] with Tensor-Core-aligned inputs, positioning the work at the intersection of neural rendering and classical graphics culling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4× VRAM reduction over V3DG and near-perfect image quality (PSNR 52–64) make composed scenes with 100M+ Gaussians practically renderable on a single 24 GB GPU, lowering the barrier for gaming and film applications. The demonstrated complementarity between occlusion culling (effective at close/medium range) and LoD (effective at far range) suggests a natural hybrid pipeline for large-scale scenes, though the paper leaves this combination as future work. Limitations include dependence on per-asset preprocessing (visibility extraction + MLP training), potential MLP capacity issues for highly complex visibility functions, and evaluation limited to three scene types at downscaled resolution. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether CUDA-stream-level optimizations can further close the gap with V3DG at long distances, and how it interacts with more expressive per-Gaussian shading.
