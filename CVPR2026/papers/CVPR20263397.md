## Problem

Existing T2I safety attacks either require white-box model access or rely on inefficient black-box prompt perturbations with inconsistent success rates. The paper identifies an underexplored attack surface: the compositional generation capability of T2I models, where combining individually benign concepts can reconstruct harmful semantics without any single prompt element triggering safety filters. Prior compositional generation methods modify the diffusion sampling process, rendering them inapplicable to closed-source commercial systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263397/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text prompt (e.g., a scene description involving violence) as input and produces an adversarial image from the target T2I model that bypasses its safety filter while preserving the original malicious semantics. The attack operates entirely in text space: an auxiliary LLM (Qwen3-8B by default) rewrites the prompt into a set of fine-grained, superficially benign sub-scene descriptions that are then reassembled into a coherent generation prompt. No model parameters, gradients, or internal representations are accessed, making it applicable to both open-source and commercial closed-source T2I systems.

## Method

CoRA (Composable Reassembly Attack) consists of two stages. First, scene understanding and decomposition: an auxiliary LLM parses the harmful prompt into structured components (Characters, Location, Actions, Objects), then further decomposes them into N fine-grained sub-scene sets, each constrained to be independently low-risk via a harmfulness evaluation step; visual metaphors are introduced to dilute sensitive elements while preserving semantic coherence. Second, selective reassembly and iterative generation: the most visually relevant candidate is selected from each sub-scene set, embedded into a contextual template to form a fluent prompt, and fed to the target T2I model; a consistency evaluator scores the generated image against the original intent, and the sub-scene selection is iteratively refined to maximize alignment while maintaining safety-filter evasion. The key distinction from prior work is that CoRA exploits the model's inherent tendency to perform fine-grained semantic completion over discrete textual inputs, achieving compositional integration in text space rather than through modified latent-space sampling.

## Results

CoRA achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all six evaluated T2I models, reaching 0.733 on Cogview4, 0.644 on DALL-E 3, 0.600 on Hunyuan, 0.593 on SDXL, 0.689 on Tongyiwanxiang, and 0.637 on SafeGen. It also attains the best Semantic Consistency (SC) on five of six models (e.g., 0.284 on SDXL, 0.276 on Hunyuan). In terms of output quality, CoRA achieves the highest Inception Score (4.07 on Cogview4) and the lowest Prompt Perplexity (37.28 on Cogview4), indicating more diverse and fluent adversarial prompts. CoRA also completes attacks in 32.0 seconds, substantially faster than MMA-Diffusion (798.3 s) and Ring-a-Bell (297.2 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRA outperforms all five baseline families on ASR across every evaluated model. Against MMA-Diffusion [47], CoRA gains 0.326 ASR on Cogview4 (0.733 vs 0.407) and 0.437 on DALL-E 3 (0.644 vs 0.207). Against DACA [8], the gap is even larger on Cogview4 (0.733 vs 0.193) and DALL-E 3 (0.644 vs 0.407). Ring-a-Bell [44] is the strongest single baseline on Cogview4 (0.563) and SDXL (0.585), yet CoRA still leads by 0.170 and 0.008 respectively, and dominates on DALL-E 3 (0.644 vs 0.119). The three QF variants [50] (GREEDY, GENETIC, PGD) cluster between 0.178–0.437 ASR, all well below CoRA. On the commercial web interfaces (GPT-4o and GPT-4.1), CoRA achieves 0.667 and 0.533 ASR versus COJ [45]'s 0.333 and 0.300, confirming its advantage in real-world settings. CoRA also leads on SC for five of six models and achieves the best IS and lowest PPL, indicating superior output diversity and prompt fluency over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoRA repurposes the concept of compositional visual generation—originally a technique for synthesizing coherent scenes from discrete concepts via modified diffusion sampling [9,10,24]—as an attack primitive, but crucially shifts the mechanism from latent-space sampling modification to text-space iterative rewriting. This departs from prior decomposition-based attacks like DACA [8], which also splits harmful prompts into benign components, by introducing a structured four-dimensional scene decomposition (Characters, Location, Actions, Objects) and an LLM-driven iterative selection-and-reassembly loop that progressively refines the prompt. Unlike white-box or sampling-modification methods (MMA-Diffusion [47], QF variants [50]), CoRA operates entirely in a black-box text-only setting, making it applicable to closed-source commercial systems where internal access is unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete semantic-composition blind spot in current T2I safety filters: when harmful intent is decomposed into individually benign visual elements and reassembled through fluent, low-perplexity prompts, existing detectors fail to reconstruct the original malicious semantics. The demonstrated effectiveness across both open-source (SDXL, Cogview4) and commercial (DALL-E 3, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1) systems underscores that this is not a single-model vulnerability but a systemic gap. The authors call for compositional-aware defenses—detecting and blocking harmful semantic combinations before generation—but no such defense is proposed here, leaving the mitigation question open. A practical limitation is the reliance on an auxiliary LLM (Qwen3-8B) for decomposition and selection, which, while shown to be relatively model-agnostic, adds an external dependency that could itself be a target or bottleneck.
