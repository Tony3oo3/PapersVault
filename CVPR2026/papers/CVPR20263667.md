## Problem

Existing video understanding systems treat MLLMs as passive recognizers that process uniformly sampled frames without adaptive reasoning, while recent agent-based methods still rely on handcrafted workflows and perception-first strategies that receive pre-sampled frames before reasoning. This results in redundant visual processing and limited efficiency on long videos, as the agent cannot autonomously decide what to watch, when to watch, and how to watch based on the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263667/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives only a text query at the initial step (no visual information), then iteratively calls a flexible frame-selection tool parameterized by start_time, end_time, nframes, and a spatial resize ratio to extract frames at adaptive temporal windows and resolutions. The output is a text answer (open-ended or multiple-choice) produced after the agent determines sufficient visual evidence has been gathered through its iterative reasoning loop.

## Method

EVA introduces a planning-before-perception paradigm where the MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) first reasons solely from the query to plan its visual exploration, then engages in iterative summary-plan-action-reflection cycles, calling a flexible frame-selection tool that controls both temporal range and spatial resolution. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: SFT cold-start on 10k synthetic trajectories to instill tool-calling format and interleaved reasoning, KTO on 11k labeled success/failure strategies to correct common failure modes (e.g., answering without sufficient evidence), and Data-Enhanced GRPO on 9.6k open-ended and 1.1k multiple-choice QA pairs with a composite reward (CSV for MCQ, ROUGE for open-ended, plus a low format reward to discourage guessing). The GRPO stage iteratively collects failure cases and uses a teacher MLLM to generate new QA pairs from unseen videos, expanding the training distribution beyond a static dataset.

## Results

On the four long-form video benchmarks, EVA-GRPO achieves 55.0% on LongVideoBench, 68.3% on MLVU, 48.4/60.2% (Long/Overall) on VideoMME, and 43.3% on LVBench, while using only ~22–27 frames per video (estimated at 650 tokens/frame). On the Sampling Dilemma Bench (LSDBench), EVA reaches 51.0% accuracy with just 10.3K visual tokens, surpassing the Qwen2.5-VL baseline (49.2% at 21.0K tokens) by +1.8% while using half the visual budget. On Video-Holmes (zero-shot), EVA-GRPO scores 37.2% overall, exceeding Video-R1 (36.5%) and VideoChat-R1 (33.0%) with fewer frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVA-GRPO outperforms the strongest open-source static-sampling baseline (Video-R1) by +2.3 pts on LongVideoBench, +8.1 pts on MLVU, and +8.0 pts on LVBench, while using roughly half the frames. Against the closest adaptive-agent competitor, FrameThinker, EVA gains +2.1 pts on LongVideoBench, +9.2 pts on MLVU, and +6.7 pts on LVBench. Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL base model (same backbone, 32 uniform frames), EVA improves by +11.8 pts on LongVideoBench, +19.9 pts on MLVU, and +11.7 pts on LVBench. On LSDBench, EVA (51.0%, 10.3K tokens) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL(RHS)/FrameThinker (52.2%, 146.2K tokens) in efficiency by 14× fewer visual tokens while remaining within 1.2 pts accuracy. EVA trails closed-source GPT-4o on LongVideoBench (55.0 vs 58.2) and VideoMME-Overall (60.2 vs 71.9), but exceeds GPT-4o on MLVU (68.3 vs 64.6) and LVBench (43.3 vs 48.9 is a loss; correction: GPT-4o wins at 48.9). On Video-Holmes zero-shot, EVA-GRPO (37.2%) edges out Video-R1 (36.5%) and VideoChat-R1 (33.0%) while using fewer frames (36.8* vs 32).

## Relation to Prior Work

EVA builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath and the KTO preference-optimization method, combining them into a three-stage SFT→KTO→GRPO pipeline for training an MLLM-based video agent. Conceptually, it departs from prior agentic video understanding methods (FrameThinker, VideoAgent, VideoMTR) that adopt a perception-first strategy—feeding uniformly sampled frames before reasoning—by introducing a planning-before-perception paradigm where the agent reasons solely from the textual query to decide what, when, and how to watch before any visual input is processed. The flexible frame-selection tool (controlling temporal window, frame count, and spatial resolution simultaneously) generalizes the rigid single-dimension tools used in prior adaptive agents, and the Data-Enhanced GRPO pipeline that iteratively generates new training data from failure cases via a teacher MLLM extends standard GRPO's static-dataset limitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 7B model can match or exceed the accuracy of much larger or denser-sampling baselines while using roughly 27× fewer visual tokens (6.2K vs. 166K+), which has direct practical implications for deploying video understanding at scale where token cost is a bottleneck. The three-stage training recipe (SFT cold-start → KTO correction → GRPO with mixed open-ended/MCQ rewards) provides a reproducible blueprint for training agentic MLLMs beyond video. Key limitations remain: the agent is constrained to a fixed tool interface, the zero-shot reasoning transfer (Video-Holmes) still lags behind dedicated reasoning models, and the 1–3% gains over prior adaptive agents suggest the planning-before-perception paradigm may need richer tool ecosystems or cross-modal memory to fully realize its potential on very long or noisy videos.
