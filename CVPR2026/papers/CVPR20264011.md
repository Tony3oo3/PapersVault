## Problem

Existing 3DGS refinement methods condition only on RGB(D) renderings, making them unable to resolve challenging artifacts such as large floaters, missing regions, and geometric errors where color alone is ambiguous. Moreover, refinement models trained for optimization-based 3DGS pipelines fail to generalize to feed-forward reconstructions (and vice versa), leaving no single model that works across the diverse sources from which real-world 3D data originates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a Gaussian Primitives Buffer (GP-Buffer) video at 480×832 resolution with 81 frames, where each frame encodes five pixel-aligned modalities—rendered color, expected depth, screen-space normals, opacity, and inverse projected 2D covariance—extracted from an existing 3DGS reconstruction along a B-spline camera trajectory. An optional text description of the scene (generated by Gemini-Flash-2.5) provides additional semantic conditioning. The output is a temporally coherent, artifact-free color video that can be merged with original inputs to re-optimize the underlying 3D Gaussian splats, improving both rendering fidelity and geometric consistency.

## Method

GaussFusion repurposes the Wan-2.1-1.3B flow-matching video generator by inserting a Geometry Adapter (GA) as a parallel side network within the DiT backbone. The GP-Buffer video is encoded by a VAE, projected via 3D convolution, and injected at each DiT layer through self-attention followed by cross-attention with text tokens, producing a geometry-aware feature that additively modulates the main video latent stream. Training uses a flow-matching objective on paired corrupted/clean video latents, with the GP-Buffer latents serving as the conditioning signal. A two-stage finetuning strategy first applies Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) to compress the multi-step teacher into a 4-step generator, then freezes all distilled parameters and finetunes only the GA layers with the standard flow loss. A comprehensive artifact simulation pipeline synthesizes 75K+ paired videos by combining sparse-view downsampling, diverse initialization strategies, underfit optimization, and feed-forward model degradations to ensure cross-paradigm generalization.

## Results

On DL3DV, GaussFusion (Full) achieves PSNR 22.55, SSIM 0.832, LPIPS 0.278, and FID 3.93, outperforming the strongest baseline ExploreGS (PSNR 20.69) by ~1.9 dB. On RE10K, the gains are even larger: PSNR 28.65, SSIM 0.944, LPIPS 0.180, FID 6.67, surpassing Difix3D+ (PSNR 24.35) by over 4 dB. The distilled few-step variant retains comparable quality (PSNR 22.49 / 28.25) while running at 15.1 FPS, compared to 1.1–1.2 FPS for GenFusion and ExploreGS. Joint training across datasets and artifact types consistently improves over single-dataset training (e.g., RE10K PSNR 28.65 vs. 24.75 for the single-dataset variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussFusion (Full) decisively outperforms all four optimization-based baselines on both datasets: it beats ExploreGS (the strongest prior method) by +1.86 dB PSNR on DL3DV and +4.62 dB on RE10K, and improves FID from 6.27→3.93 (DL3DV) and 15.63→6.67 (RE10K). Against Difix3D+, which processes frames independently, GaussFusion gains +2.45 dB PSNR on DL3DV and +4.30 dB on RE10K, with substantially lower FID (3.93 vs. 4.22; 6.67 vs. 13.99), confirming the benefit of video-level temporal modeling. GenFusion, despite using a video diffusion backbone, lags by 4.2 dB PSNR on DL3DV due to weaker conditioning (RGB-only vs. GP-Buffer). On feed-forward backbones (Table 2), GaussFusion improves DepthSplat by +1.03 dB PSNR and MVSplat by +1.01 dB, matching MVSplat360 on MVSplat consistency (PSNR 19.76 vs. 19.82) while achieving far better FID (23.8 vs. 36.8) and LPIPS (0.338 vs. 0.354), and doing so without any MVSplat-specific training. The distilled few-step variant trades a modest FID increase (7.38 vs. 3.93 on DL3DV) for a 3.5× speedup to 15.1 FPS, still exceeding Difix3D+ in speed while matching or beating it in PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussFusion extends the line of generative-prior-based 3DGS refinement (Difix3D+, GenFusion, ExploreGS, MVSplat360) by replacing RGB-only conditioning with a multi-modal Gaussian Primitives Buffer (color, depth, normals, opacity, inverse covariance) and a dedicated Geometry Adapter that injects these cues into a flow-matching DiT backbone. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (i) it is paradigm-agnostic, generalizing across both optimization-based (Splatfacto) and feed-forward (DepthSplat, MVSplat) reconstruction pipelines, whereas each prior method is tightly coupled to one; and (ii) it applies Distribution Matching Distillation to a pretrained Wan-2.1 video model to achieve 4-step, near-real-time inference, a practical efficiency gain absent in earlier video-diffusion refiners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-paradigm generalization is practically significant because real-world 3D assets originate from heterogeneous pipelines (SfM-optimized, feed-forward, or 3D-generated), and no single prior refiner handles all of them. The 16 FPS distilled variant opens the door to interactive 3D editing and AR/VR applications where per-frame refinement was previously infeasible. Open questions include whether the GP-Buffer abstraction transfers to non-Gaussian representations (e.g., NeRF, mesh-based), whether the method degrades on extremely sparse or unposed inputs, and whether the slight FID degradation in the distilled model becomes problematic for high-fidelity professional use.
