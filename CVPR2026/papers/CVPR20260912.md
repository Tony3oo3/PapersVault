## Problem

Adaptive federated optimizers like FedAdam accelerate convergence but fail to improve generalization under highly heterogeneous data, because their adaptive learning rates weaken the algorithm's preference for flat minima while enhancing saddle-point escape. The distinct roles of adaptive learning rate versus momentum in governing saddle-point escape and flat-minima selection in federated settings remained uncharacterized, leaving the optimization–generalization tradeoff of FedAdam unexplained.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a standard federated protocol: each selected client performs K local SGD steps on its private dataset (32×32 or 64×64 RGB images for CIFAR-10/100/Tiny-ImageNet, or text sequences for Shakespeare), then uploads the local model delta to the server. The server computes a pseudo-gradient, updates an adaptive per-parameter momentum coefficient using the exponential moving average of squared gradients, and broadcasts the updated global model. No additional communication beyond the standard model-delta upload is required, and the output is a global model used for image classification or next-word prediction.

## Method

FedAdamom replaces the adaptive learning rate of FedAdam with an adaptive momentum coefficient: at each round the server computes β₁,t = (1 − v_t/v̄_t)·Clip(0, 1−ε) per parameter, where v_t is the EMA of squared pseudo-gradients and v̄_t is its coordinate-wise mean, then updates momentum as m_t = β₁,t·m_{t−1} + (1−β₁,t)·Δ_t and the model as x_{t+1} = x_t + η·m_t. Diffusion-theory analysis shows this design keeps the momentum drift isotropic and Hessian-independent near saddle points (preserving fast escape) while restoring the O(H_ae^{−1}) scaling of the mean escape time from sharp minima, matching the local SGD dynamics and thus favoring flat global minima. The paper further proves a convergence rate of O(1/√(sKT)) for the average gradient norm under non-convex objectives with partial participation, matching the best known rates for existing FL methods.

## Results

On the moderate-scale setting (100 clients, 5% participation), FedAdamom achieves 88.93% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 57.58% on CIFAR-100, and 47.38% on Tiny-ImageNet after 1000 communication rounds, outperforming all adaptive FL baselines by 0.79–8.52 percentage points. It also converges fastest, reaching 75% accuracy on CIFAR-10 in 213 rounds (vs. 241 for FedYogi and 308 for FedAvg), 45% on CIFAR-100 in 276 rounds, and 35% on Tiny-ImageNet in 244 rounds. On realistic LEAF datasets, FedAdamom leads on FEMNIST (82.85%), CelebA (89.95%), and Shakespeare (48.02%) at 500/1000 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedAdamom outperforms all six baseline families across all three image classification datasets. Against FedAvg (87.20% CIFAR-10, 53.39% CIFAR-100, 42.41% Tiny-ImageNet at 1000R), FedAdamom gains +1.73, +4.19, and +4.97 pp respectively, while also converging ~30% faster. Against the closest adaptive competitor FADAS (88.14%, 54.67%, 43.83%), FedAdamom still leads by +0.79, +2.91, and +3.55 pp. FedAdam, despite its fast saddle-point escape, lags FedAdamom by 1.62 pp on CIFAR-10 and 3.91 pp on CIFAR-100, confirming the paper's theoretical claim that adaptive learning rates impair flat-minima selection. FedCAda achieves the fastest early convergence (209 rounds to 75% on CIFAR-10) but degrades severely under heterogeneity, reaching only 47.51% on CIFAR-100 and 39.06% on Tiny-ImageNet at 1000R. On realistic LEAF datasets, FedAdamom leads FEMNIST by +0.53 pp over FedAvgM and CelebA by +0.54 pp, while on Shakespeare it surpasses the best baseline FADAS by +0.62 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adaptive federated optimization line initiated by FedAdam (Reddi et al., ICLR 2021) by identifying a specific mechanistic flaw: the adaptive learning rate, while accelerating saddle-point escape, weakens the Hessian-dependent preference for flat minima that drives generalization. It departs from prior adaptive FL methods (FedAdam, FedYogi, FAFED, FedCAda) that all adapt the learning rate or add client-side computation, instead proposing to adapt the momentum coefficient at the parameter level. The theoretical framework imports diffusion theory (Fokker-Planck / Kramers escape) from centralized SGD analysis into the FL setting, building on the FedAvg-as-server-SGD equivalence and the FedAvgM momentum structure, to formally characterize how each component (adaptive LR vs. momentum) affects escape dynamics from saddle points versus sharp minima.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean theoretical explanation for the well-known empirical observation that Adam-family optimizers generalize worse than SGD, now specifically in the federated setting, and offers a minimal, communication-free fix (adapt momentum rather than LR) that preserves convergence speed while restoring flat-minima selection. The convergence guarantee matches existing FL rates without requiring bounded-gradient assumptions, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the diffusion-theory assumptions (quasi-equilibrium, low-temperature) hold in practice for very deep or very heterogeneous FL settings, whether the parameter-wise adaptive momentum interacts well with other FL techniques (e.g., compression, personalization), and whether the advantage persists at much larger scales (thousands of clients, billion-parameter models) where the current experiments (100–500 clients, ResNet-18/CNN) do not reach.
