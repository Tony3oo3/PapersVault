## Problem

Existing stereo image compression methods rely on cross-attention for inter-view redundancy modeling and spatial autoregressive entropy models for probability estimation, both of which introduce quadratic or sequential computational bottlenecks that severely limit coding speed. The paper addresses the gap between achieving state-of-the-art rate-distortion performance and maintaining practical coding latency, showing that prior bi-directional codecs like BiSIC require over 78 seconds per stereo pair while sacrificing neither quality nor speed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stereo image pair (left and right RGB views of shape 3×H×W) and jointly encodes them into a compressed bitstream via a shared encoder-decoder architecture. The encoder produces latent representations at 1/16 spatial resolution (320 channels), which are quantized and entropy-coded using a checkerboard-partitioned scheme that splits each channel slice into anchor and non-anchor elements. Decoding reconstructs both views simultaneously, ensuring balanced quality across the stereo pair rather than favoring one view as a reference.

## Method

MambaSIC introduces a Stereo Visual State Space Block (Stereo VSSB) that splits features into local (processed by CNN) and global (processed by a Mamba-based stereo VSSL) branches, then fuses them with a skip connection. The stereo VSSL employs a Stereo 2D Selective Scan that injects the other view's output matrix C into the state-space recurrence via a learnable scalar α, enabling cross-view information transfer at linear complexity, combined with a stereo gating mechanism that routes both intra-view and inter-view features through element-wise multiplication. For entropy coding, a bi-directional multi-reference model replaces spatial autoregressive iterations with a checkerboard partitioning strategy and uses Stereo VSSB to fuse left-view and right-view intra-view priors (hyperprior, channel-wise autoregressive, spatial context) into inter-view priors, yielding accurate Gaussian probability estimates for both anchor and non-anchor latent elements in a fully parallelizable manner.

## Results

On InStereo2K, MambaSIC achieves a BD-PSNR of 1.92 dB and a BDBR_P of −57.15% relative to BPG, outperforming the strongest baseline BiSIC (1.63 dB, −48.07%) by 0.29 dB and 9.08% in bit savings. On Cityscapes, MambaSIC reaches 3.75 dB BD-PSNR and −66.43% BDBR_P, surpassing BiSIC (3.34 dB, −57.49%) by 0.41 dB and 8.94%. In terms of coding latency, MambaSIC completes encoding and decoding in 1.26 s total, making it approximately 62× faster than BiSIC (78.61 s) on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaSIC outperforms all 10 baselines on both InStereo2K and Cityscapes across all four metrics (BD-PSNR, BDBR_P, BD-MSSSIM, BDBR_M). Against the strongest prior method BiSIC (ECCV 2024), MambaSIC gains 0.29 dB BD-PSNR and 9.08% additional BDBR reduction on InStereo2K, and 0.41 dB / 8.94% on Cityscapes. Compared to other bidirectional codecs (BCSIC, LDMIC), the improvement is even larger: 15.93% BDBR reduction over BCSIC on InStereo2K. Unidirectional methods (VVC, MV-HEVC, DispSIC, SASIC, ECSIC, CAMSIC) trail by 10–50+ percentage points in BDBR, confirming the benefit of bidirectional inter-view modeling. In coding latency, MambaSIC (1.26 s total) is 62× faster than BiSIC (78.61 s), 34× faster than BCSIC (43.11 s), and 31× faster than LDMIC (39.23 s), while also beating the previously fastest learned codec CAMSIC (1.75 s) by ~1.4×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the learned stereo image compression line (BiSIC, BCSIC, ECSIC) by replacing the cross-attention-based inter-view context transfer with a Mamba-based stereo visual state space block (stereo VSSB), exploiting Mamba's linear complexity and long-range modeling to capture inter-view redundancy more efficiently. The entropy model adapts the checkerboard multi-reference strategy from MLIC++ (single-image compression) to the stereo setting, but introduces inter-view priors generated by the stereo VSSB to fuse left- and right-view contextual information. The selective state space formulation is further modified with a cross-view matrix C mechanism, borrowing the input-dependent gating idea from Mamba but repurposing it to inject information from the other view at negligible overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 62× speedup over BiSIC while simultaneously achieving 9–16% additional BDBR reduction makes MambaSIC a practically compelling option for real-time stereo compression in VR, autonomous driving, and 3D reconstruction pipelines where both latency and bitrate matter. The demonstration that linear-complexity SSMs can replace quadratic-complexity cross-attention for inter-view correlation modeling opens a path toward scaling stereo (and potentially multi-view) compression to higher resolutions and more views without the computational bottleneck. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two views, whether the specific cross-view injection point (matrix C) remains optimal at larger channel dimensions, and how the method performs on outdoor scenes with large disparity beyond the two tested datasets.
