## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding (VTG) models assume a relevant segment always exists and therefore always predict a target segment, even for irrelevant queries. While recent relevance-aware approaches can reject completely unrelated queries, they fail on hard-irrelevant queries that share high-level semantic context with the video but differ in fine-grained details (e.g., 'cutting steaks' vs. 'cooking pasta'), because they only learn coarse-grained relevance distinctions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (uniformly sampled at 2 FPS, resized to ~2.8M total pixels) and a natural language query as input. The output is a structured three-part response: a <think> section with temporal reasoning, an <answer> section containing either start/end timestamps or a refusal with an explanation of the semantic mismatch, and a <correct> section that reconstructs the intended relevant query when the input is hard-irrelevant. This structured format is enforced by a dedicated format reward during training.

## Method

RA-RFT post-trains a pretrained LVLM-based VTG model using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with four complementary reward objectives: a format reward enforcing the output template, a refuse-IoU reward that assigns IoU for relevant queries and 1 only when the model correctly withholds timestamps for irrelevant ones, an explain reward using SentenceBERT cosine similarity to push the refusal explanation toward a reference and away from the opposite-type answer, and a query correction reward that measures similarity between the model's reconstructed query and the original relevant query. To support this training, the authors construct the HI-VTG dataset (2.5K relevant + 7.5K hard-irrelevant pairs) by extracting 11 semantic relevance categories from original queries via GPT-5-mini, then modifying one to three semantic elements to create graded hard-irrelevant queries (Strong/Moderate/Weak) with LLM-generated refusal explanations. The method is applied as a post-training step to existing RFT-based VTG models (Time-R1, VideoChat-R1, VideoChat-R1-think) without fine-tuning on downstream benchmarks, and consistently improves refusal F1 and explanation quality while preserving standard temporal grounding accuracy.

## Results

On the primary Hard-Irrelevant VTG benchmark HI-ActivityNet (17K pairs), RA-RFT applied to Time-R1 improves RA-IoU mIoU from 45.9 to 51.9 and F1 (avg.) from 70.5 to 76.3, with the irrelevant-query F1 rising from 66.9 to 75.0. Similar gains appear on HI-TVGBench (mIoU 37.3→46.6, F1 64.5→70.0) and HI-Charades (mIoU 48.9→53.1, F1 68.2→70.8). On the human-annotated RA-VTG set, Time-R1 + RA-RFT reaches F1 71.2 and LLM explanation score 2.13, versus 57.1 and 1.16 for the unmodified baseline. Standard VTG grounding (ActivityNet Captions) is preserved, with mIoU remaining within 0.1–0.9 points of the pre-RA-RFT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-RFT consistently outperforms all three RFT-based baselines (Time-R1, VideoChat-R1, VideoChat-R1-think) across every hard-irrelevant dataset, with the largest gains on the most challenging strong-hard-irrelevant cases (e.g., Time-R1 F1 on strong-hard: 60.0→70.6). Against SFT-based methods (TimeChat, TimeSuite, TRACE), the gap is dramatic: SFT models achieve near-zero irrelevant-query F1 (0.0–10.3) because they always predict a segment regardless of relevance, while RA-RFT reaches 69–75 F1 on irrelevant queries. VLP-based methods (RaTSG, NA-VMR) are evaluated in-distribution yet still trail RA-RFT by 10–25 points in mIoU on HI-ActivityNet, confirming that coarse-grained relevance training cannot substitute for fine-grained semantic reasoning. On the simpler simply-shuffled RA-VTG benchmarks (Table 2), RA-RFT still improves all baselines (e.g., Time-R1 F1 on SS-ActivityNet: 83.9→95.0), showing the method generalizes beyond hard-irrelevant to coarse-irrelevant settings. On the human-annotated set (Table 3), RA-RFT also improves explanation quality metrics (RT-IoU, SBert, LLM score), indicating the generated refusal reasons align more closely with human-written explanations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm (from DeepSeek-R1/DeepSeekMath) to the video temporal grounding domain, building directly on RFT-based VTG models like Time-R1 and VideoChat-R1. It departs from prior refusal-capable VTG work (RaTSG, NA-VMR), which trains on simply-shuffled irrelevant queries and thus only learns coarse-grained relevance discrimination, by introducing the harder problem of semantically-similar-but-irrelevant queries. The multi-reward design (format, refuse-IoU, explain, query correction) is a novel composition that goes beyond the single time-aware reward used in prior RFT-based VTG, and the HI-VTG dataset provides the first systematic collection of hard-irrelevant query-video pairs with structured refusal explanations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hard-irrelevant distinction addresses a practical gap: real-world users will naturally issue queries that are topically related to a video but factually mismatched (e.g., 'cutting steaks' vs. 'cooking pasta'), and current VTG systems confidently return wrong segments for such inputs. The RA-RFT recipe is model-agnostic and applicable to any LVLM-based VTG backbone, making it a practical post-training step. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated hard-irrelevant queries (which may not fully capture the diversity of real-world mismatches), a small human-annotated evaluation set (200 samples), and the fact that the query correction reward's contribution, while positive, is the smallest incremental gain among the four rewards. Open questions include whether this approach transfers to other video understanding tasks (e.g., video QA, video retrieval) and whether the hard-irrelevant difficulty levels generalize beyond the 11 predefined semantic categories.
