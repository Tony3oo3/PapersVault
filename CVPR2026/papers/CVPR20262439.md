## Problem

Standard diffusion and flow-matching image generators update every spatial patch with the same global noise level and the same number of function evaluations, implicitly assuming all regions are equally difficult to denoise. In practice, images are highly heterogeneous: low-frequency backgrounds resolve quickly while thin structures, boundaries, and small text remain ambiguous until late in the process. The paper asks how to allocate denoising compute adaptively at the patch level so that easy regions can advance and provide context to harder ones, and how to train such a model without the train-test mismatch that arises when per-patch timesteps are sampled naively.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional ImageNet (256×256), the model takes a class label and Gaussian noise in latent space; for text-to-image (512×512), it takes a text prompt encoded by Qwen3-1.7B plus noise. The output is a synthesized RGB image. Unusually, during inference different spatial patches (2×2 latent patches) follow distinct denoising trajectories: confident patches take larger timestep jumps while uncertain patches receive more refinement steps, so the image is not produced by a single uniform Euler schedule but by a spatially heterogeneous, difficulty-aware sampling procedure.

## Method

Patch Forcing (PF) extends a SiT/DiT backbone with three components. First, per-patch timestep conditioning replaces the scalar timestep with a spatial map of timesteps, implemented by extending AdaLN to broadcast per-token timestep embeddings. Second, a Logit-Normal Truncated Gaussian (LTG) sampler controls the maximum patch-level information (t_max) during training by sampling t_max from a logit-normal distribution and drawing individual patch timesteps from a truncated lower-half Gaussian centered at t_max, avoiding the train-test gap of SRM's uniform-t̄ recursive scheme. Third, a lightweight uncertainty head predicts per-patch standard deviation via an NLL loss on the velocity target, repurposed as a patch-difficulty score. At inference, two adaptive samplers exploit this signal: Dual-Loop advances low-uncertainty patches with larger steps (outer loop) while refining high-uncertainty patches with smaller steps (inner loop), and Look-Ahead propagates confident patches to a future timestep proportional to the current t to provide 'future' context that reduces uncertainty in remaining regions.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet 256², PFT-XL/2 with the look-ahead sampler achieves FID-50k of 11.9 (without REPA) and 6.7 (with REPA), outperforming the SiT-XL/2 baseline (17.2 / 7.9) at the same 400K-iteration budget. At the SOTA comparison level (200 epochs), PFT-XL/2+REPA+look-ahead reaches FID 2.00, sFID 4.32, and IS 284.1, matching or exceeding SiT-XL/2+REPA (FID 1.96, sFID 4.49, IS 264.0) and DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27, sFID 4.60, IS 278.2). For text-to-image, the 1.2B PFT with look-ahead attains a GenEval overall score of 0.63, surpassing SDXL (0.55) and DALL-E2 (0.52) while remaining competitive with DALL-E3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SiT baselines (B/2, L/2, XL/2), PFT consistently wins on FID at every scale: e.g., 24.2 vs 33.0 (B/2), 13.0 vs 18.8 (L/2), and 11.9 vs 17.2 (XL/2) at 400K iterations, with the look-ahead sampler providing the largest gain. With REPA, PFT-XL/2+REPA+look-ahead (FID 6.7) beats SiT-XL/2+REPA (7.9), confirming orthogonality to representation alignment. In the SOTA table, PFT-XL/2+REPA+look-ahead (FID 2.00, sFID 4.32, IS 284.1) is competitive with SiT-XL/2+REPA (FID 1.96) and clearly beats DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27), MaskDiT (2.28), and SD-DiT (3.23), though it uses only 200 epochs versus 1400 for DiT/SiT. For text-to-image, PFT-1.2B+look-ahead outperforms SDXL (GenEval 0.63 vs 0.55), DALL-E2 (0.52), LlamaGen (0.32), and Emu3-Gen (0.54), and is competitive with DALL-E3 (0.67) and SD3-Medium (0.74), though it trails the latter on counting and color-attribute sub-metrics. On T2I-CompBench++, PFT-1.2B leads on ColorB-VQA (0.7323) and 3D-SpatialUniDet (0.2815) but trails PixArt-α on TextureB-VQA (0.6444 vs 0.7044) and 2D-SpatialUniDet (0.1956 vs 0.2082), indicating that the adaptive sampler's benefit is strongest on spatially structured tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) from per-frame timesteps in video to per-spatial-patch timesteps in image synthesis, building directly on Spatial Reasoning Models (SRM, Wewer et al., 2025) which first adapted the idea to the image domain for reasoning tasks like Sudoku. The key departure from SRM is a simpler, more inference-aligned timestep sampler (Logit-Normal Truncated Gaussian) that controls the maximum per-patch information rather than the mean, eliminating the train-test gap where training samples always contain partially denoised context. The backbone follows SiT/DiT with minimal architectural changes (per-patch AdaLN plus a small uncertainty head), and the resulting Patch Forcing framework is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to guidance methods (CFG, REPA) and inference-time caching (RAS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Patch-level denoising schedules introduce a new axis of spatial adaptivity for image generation that is complementary to existing guidance and representation-alignment techniques, and the minimal architectural overhead (less than 0.01% extra parameters) makes it easy to adopt. The three validated findings—context reduces validation loss, predicted uncertainty tracks patch difficulty, and context reduces uncertainty—provide a principled justification for adaptive compute allocation. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to few-step or distilled models (where larger time jumps might amplify the benefit), whether it scales to higher resolutions, and whether the T2I gap to frontier models (e.g., SD3-Medium) can be closed with more training or better text encoders.
