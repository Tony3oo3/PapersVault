## Problem

Linear recurrent models for echocardiography video segmentation suffer from rank collapse—progressive singular value decay in the hidden state matrix—caused by unconstrained Euclidean state updates under severe speckle noise and rapid cardiac deformation. Simultaneously, existing spatial encoders treat ultrasound speckle and anatomical boundaries without physical distinction, leading to representation drift where structural details are overwhelmed by stochastic noise during long-sequence propagation. No prior method jointly addresses both the temporal rank-collapse vulnerability and the spatial noise-structure entanglement specific to ultrasound imaging.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 2D echocardiography video sequences (CAMUS: 256×256, 15 frames; EchoNet-Dynamic: 128×128, 10 frames) and produces per-frame left ventricle segmentation masks across the entire cardiac cycle, from which ejection fraction is estimated via Simpson's rule. Unlike semi-supervised video object segmentation methods that require a first-frame reference mask, OSA operates fully automatically at inference without any manual prompt, matching real clinical workflows. Training supervision is sparse—only ED and ES frames are annotated in EchoNet-Dynamic—forcing the model to learn in-context associative recall from the compressed state.

## Method

OSA combines a ResNet-50 backbone with two novel modules. The Orthogonalized State Update (OSU) formulates the gated recurrent state evolution as Euclidean projected gradient descent on the Stiefel manifold: after computing the unconstrained update S_t^Euc, the state is projected back via a 5th-order Newton-Schulz iteration (avoiding exact SVD), enforcing S_t^T S_t = I and thereby fixing the Frobenius norm and bounding the condition number to prevent rank collapse. The Anatomical Prior-aware Feature Enhancement (APFE) module decomposes intermediate features into a low-frequency acoustic bias field (estimated by large-kernel average pooling) and high-frequency structural residuals via dual-ReLU splitting, processes them through unshared convolutional branches, and fuses them with an adaptive sigmoid gate to yield noise-resilient structural cues. The refined features serve as key-value inputs to the OSU-driven temporal tracker, and the full model is trained with point-supervised cross-entropy and Dice loss under sparse ED/ES annotations.

## Results

On CAMUS, OSA achieves the best mean Dice coefficient of 94.82% and a mean 95% Hausdorff distance of 3.25, outperforming the strongest baseline GDKVM (94.18% mDice, 3.31 mHD95) by 0.64 points in mDice. On EchoNet-Dynamic, OSA reaches 93.90% mDice and 3.42 mHD95, surpassing GDKVM (93.33%, 3.55) by 0.57 mDice and 0.13 mHD95. For ejection fraction estimation, OSA attains the highest Pearson correlation on EchoNet-Dynamic (0.816) and the lowest standard deviation (±6.0%), while on CAMUS it achieves a correlation of 0.830 with a bias of −0.44±7.0%. The model also maintains real-time inference at 35 fps with only 38.3M parameters and 7.6 GB training memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSA outperforms all eight baselines on mDice across both datasets, with the largest margins over retrieval-based methods (Cutie: +3.39 mDice on CAMUS; SAMed-2: +1.27) and linear attention models (PolaFormer: +2.35; VisionLSTM: +2.11). Against the closest competitor GDKVM (also a linear key-value association model), OSA gains +0.64 mDice on CAMUS and +0.57 on EchoNet-Dynamic, while also improving mHD95 by 0.06 and 0.13 respectively. On EchoNet-Dynamic, OSA achieves the best mHD95 (3.42) and the lowest EF estimation standard deviation (±6.0%), indicating superior temporal stability under sparse annotations. On CAMUS, EchoVim marginally edges OSA on mHD95 (3.21 vs 3.25) and GDKVM slightly leads on EF correlation (0.835 vs 0.830), but OSA dominates on the primary segmentation metric mDice. In terms of efficiency, OSA trains in 3.0 hours with 7.6 GB memory, substantially faster than EchoVim (9.0 h, 34.9 GB), Vivim (10.0 h), and SAMed-2 (7.0 h, 27.5 GB), while using fewer parameters than SAMed-2 (38.3M vs 110.1M).

## Relation to Prior Work

OSA extends the linear recurrent model (LRM) lineage initiated by Katharopoulos et al. and refined by gated delta networks, which compress video history into a fixed-size state matrix via rank-1 key-value updates. The key departure is constraining this state evolution to the Stiefel manifold via Newton-Schulz iteration, drawing on the orthogonal/unitary RNN literature (Arjovsky et al., Wisdom et al.) but applying it to the matrix-valued states of modern linear associative memory rather than to weight matrices. The APFE module additionally introduces a physics-motivated feature decomposition (local acoustic bias via large-kernel pooling + dual-ReLU polarity split) that is specific to ultrasound imaging and absent from prior echocardiography segmentation methods like GDKVM or EchoVim.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The rank-collapse diagnosis for LRM-based video segmentation is well-motivated, and the Stiefel manifold constraint offers a clean, computationally efficient (Newton-Schulz, no SVD) remedy that preserves spectral structure across cardiac cycles. However, the practical impact is tempered by several limitations: the model is restricted to fixed-length sequences (15 or 10 frames), the ColR metric in the full model still reports 77.78% of steps with σ_min < 10^{-3} (suggesting the 'prevention' is partial), and cross-domain generalization across ultrasound equipment remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the manifold constraint extends to fully online, variable-length video streams and whether the APFE decomposition generalizes beyond 2D apical views.
