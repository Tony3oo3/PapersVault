## Problem

Existing content moderation tools for VLMs are limited to single-turn or single-modality settings, making them ill-suited for multimodal multi-turn dialogues where malicious intent is concealed across turns, risk accumulates contextually, and cross-modal joint risks emerge. Additionally, no dedicated dataset or benchmark existed for evaluating safety in this complex interactive setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260986/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a serialized JSON array of dialogue turns (each containing user text, optional images marked with sequential placeholders like Image1, Image2, and assistant responses), a list of active safety policy dimensions, and a guiding instruction. It outputs a single <OUTPUT>-tagged JSON object with six fields: user_rating, assistant_rating (Safe/Unsafe), user_dimension, assistant_dimension (violated policy categories), and user_rationale, assistant_rationale (evidence-based explanations). The role-decoupled design allows auditing both sides simultaneously or focusing on one role by nulling the other's fields.

## Method

LLaVAShield is initialized from LLaVA-OV-7B and fine-tuned via supervised sequence-to-sequence learning on the MMDS dataset (4,484 dialogues), casting the multi-faceted moderation task as autoregressive generation of a structured JSON assessment. The MMDS dataset is constructed using MMRT, an MCTS-based red-teaming framework where an attacker VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) explores attack paths through selection, expansion, simulation, and backpropagation steps, with an evaluator scoring cumulative risk across turns. Four data augmentation strategies (random policy removal, response rewriting, context-side removal, and policy-dimension pruning) improve generalization and flexible policy adaptation. A role-decoupled, dual-channel rationale mechanism generates independent evidence-based explanations for user and assistant histories, extending the LLaVaGuard approach to the multi-turn multimodal setting.

## Results

On the MMDS test set, LLaVAShield-7B achieves F1 of 95.71% on the user side (100% precision, 91.76% recall) and 92.24% on the assistant side (97.41% precision, 87.60% recall), surpassing the strongest baseline GPT-5-mini by +20.25 and +14.31 F1 points respectively. On external benchmarks, LLaVAShield attains 97.62% average recall on MM-SafetyBench (vs. 48.44% for GPT-5-mini) and 90.55% F1 on VLGuard-Test (vs. 86.39% for GPT-5-mini). Under flexible policy adaptation, LLaVAShield achieves 0% false positive rate on both sides, while GPT-5-mini records 30% and 34%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLaVAShield-7B decisively outperforms all 11 baselines on the MMDS test set. The strongest general-purpose VLM, GPT-5-mini, achieves 75.46% user F1 and 77.93% assistant F1, trailing LLaVAShield by 20.25 and 14.31 points respectively. Open-source VLMs (LLaVA-OV-7B, InternVL3, Qwen2.5-VL, Ovis2) perform poorly, with user-side F1 ranging from 0% to 37%, reflecting a strong safety bias that labels most content as compliant. Dedicated moderation tools also fall short: Llama Guard-4-12B reaches only 14.21% user F1 and 28.21% assistant F1, while OpenAI Moderation (assistant-side only) achieves 35.93% F1. On external benchmarks, LLaVAShield extends its lead, achieving 97.62% average recall on MM-SafetyBench versus 48.44% for GPT-5-mini, and 90.55% F1 on VLGuard-Test versus 86.39% for GPT-5-mini. The fine-grained policy-dimension analysis (Table 2) confirms the advantage is not limited to specific risk categories, with the largest gaps on dimensions O2 (+39.11%) and O4 (+40.04%) that require complex cross-modal and multi-turn reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends content moderation from the single-turn, single-modality setting (covered by LlamaGuard, WildGuard, LLaVaGuard, ShieldVLM) to the substantially harder problem of auditing both user inputs and assistant responses across multimodal multi-turn dialogues. Architecturally, it builds on LLaVA-OV-7B as the base VLM and adapts the rationale-generation mechanism from LLaVaGuard into a role-decoupled, dual-channel design. The red-teaming framework (MMRT) draws on MCTS-based search from IDEATOR and multi-turn jailbreak strategies (crescendo, purpose inversion, query decomposition) but is the first to combine these with cross-modal image injection and generation in an automated, tree-search-driven pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a critical gap: no prior dataset or moderation model was designed for the three compounding risk characteristics (concealment, contextual accumulation, cross-modal joint risk) that make multimodal multi-turn dialogue qualitatively harder to moderate. The finding that even GPT-5-mini and Gemini-2.5-Pro achieve F1 below 80% on this task, while a 7B fine-tuned model reaches 95%+, suggests that general-purpose VLM safety alignment is insufficient for interactive deployment and that dedicated moderation layers are necessary. Open questions include whether the 8-dimension taxonomy captures all real-world risk, how the approach scales to longer dialogues or additional modalities (video, audio), and whether the MCTS-based red-teaming framework generalizes to closed-source models with stricter rate limits.
