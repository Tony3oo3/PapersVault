## Problem

Existing Precise Event Spotting methods process all frames uniformly at a single spatial resolution, either incurring prohibitive computational cost at high resolution or losing fine-grained visual cues (e.g., ball-ground contact in tennis) when downsampled for tractability. This uniform treatment ignores the substantial spatial redundancy in video, where task-relevant information occupies only a small portion of each frame, and no prior PES framework adaptively allocates high-resolution computation to the most informative regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video clips at a high resolution of 796×448 (centrally cropped to 448×448 for most datasets), from which a half-resolution version (224×224) is derived for the global branch. The selected region-of-interest per frame is cropped from the high-resolution input and resized to a fixed 112×112 for the local branch. Output is per-frame class probabilities over C event classes plus background, from which event temporal positions are extracted via Soft-NMS with 50% clip overlap at inference.

## Method

AdaSpot employs a dual-resolution architecture where a low-resolution RegNetY+GSF backbone processes full frames to produce global features and spatial feature maps, while a second (independent) RegNetY+GSF backbone processes only a selected RoI at high resolution to capture fine-grained details. The RoI selector is training-free: it generates saliency maps by channel-averaging the low-resolution feature maps, applies replicate padding (instead of zero-padding) to remove center bias, smooths with a spatio-temporal Gaussian filter for temporal consistency, and selects the smallest fixed-aspect-ratio rectangle capturing a cumulative importance threshold τ, enabling adaptive RoI sizing. Features from both branches are aligned via two-layer MLPs, fused by max-pooling, and passed through a bidirectional GRU before a linear prediction head. Training uses frame-level weighted cross-entropy with auxiliary supervision heads on each branch independently, which stabilizes optimization and ensures both branches learn discriminative, complementary features.

## Results

On the strictest PES metric (mAP@0f), AdaSpot^s achieves 73.49 on Tennis and 27.26 on FineDiving, improving over the best prior methods (E2E-Spot_800MF at 70.04 and E2E-Spot_200MF at 25.00) by +3.45 and +2.26 respectively. On F3Set, AdaSpot^b reaches 55.38 mAP@0f, nearly doubling the previous best (F3ED at 24.79). Under the looser ES setting on SN-BAS, AdaSpot^b attains 56.24 mAP@0.5s, surpassing E2E-Spot_800MF (54.49) by +1.75 while using 1.66× fewer FLOPs (90.04 vs 150.02 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSpot^s (7.58M params, 29.78 GFLOPs) outperforms E2E-Spot_200MF (4.49M, 23.13 GFLOPs) on Tennis (+3.45 mAP@0f), FineDiving (+2.26), and F3Set (+28.76), while matching it on FineGym (17.52 vs 17.50). Against the much larger T-DEED_800MF (64.26M, 86.34 GFLOPs), AdaSpot^b (10.63M, 56.78 GFLOPs) wins decisively on Tennis (74.02 vs 58.43) and FineDiving (27.07 vs 19.63), but trails by 0.14 on FineGym (18.21 vs 18.35). On F3Set, both AdaSpot variants surpass F3ED by a wide margin (53.55/55.38 vs 24.79 mAP@0f), indicating strong generalization to more fine-grained events. Under the ES setting on SN-BAS, AdaSpot^b (56.24 mAP@0.5s) beats E2E-Spot_800MF (54.49) by +1.75 with 1.66× fewer FLOPs, and outperforms T-DEED_800MF (49.39) by +6.85. The paper also shows that learnable cropping alternatives (AdaFocus-v2, Uni-AdaFocus) and saliency-based frame warping fail to match AdaSpot's accuracy-efficiency trade-off when transferred to the PES setting, with selected RoIs often missing task-relevant regions due to training instability under weak supervision signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSpot builds directly on the E2E-Spot framework (Hong et al., ECCV 2022), reusing its RegNetY + GSF backbone and frame-level classification formulation, but introduces a multi-resolution input strategy absent from all prior PES methods. Conceptually, it draws from the dynamic computation literature (AdaFocus, CoViFocus) that allocates computation to task-relevant regions, but departs sharply from their learnable cropping mechanisms by replacing them with a training-free saliency-based selector derived from the backbone's own activation patterns. This positions the work at the intersection of PES temporal modeling and input-level spatial redundancy reduction, a combination not previously explored in the event spotting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial downsampling—a standard efficiency trade-off in PES—silently discards fine-grained cues (e.g., ball-ground contact in tennis) that are critical for frame-level precision, and that a simple adaptive-resolution strategy recovers these cues at marginal computational cost. The finding that learnable cropping methods (AdaFocus-v2, Uni-AdaFocus) fail in the PES setting due to weak supervision, while a naive saliency-based alternative succeeds, provides a clear practical guideline for practitioners working on highly localized temporal tasks. Open questions include generalization beyond sports domains, handling of simultaneous multi-region events within a single frame, and whether temporal redundancy (skipping non-informative frames) can be addressed alongside the spatial redundancy tackled here.
