## Problem

Existing stereoscopic conversion pipelines rely on depth estimation, forward warping, and a separate inpainting network to fill disoccluded regions, incurring high computational cost and producing geometric inconsistencies near occlusion boundaries. Diffusion-based alternatives are even heavier and hallucinate content that breaks left-right stereo alignment. The paper asks whether explicit inpainting is truly necessary and demonstrates that bidirectional warping alone can handle disocclusions while preserving geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263528/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single left-view RGB image (no camera parameters or rectification required) and produces the corresponding right-view RGB image, forming a stereo pair. It explicitly models 2D flow (horizontal + vertical) rather than 1D horizontal disparity, making it robust to non-ideal rectification, off-axis, and converged (toe-in) camera configurations. Efficiency is reported at 512×512 and 2K resolutions, with the network achieving over 100 FPS at 2K on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

XPaintNet estimates a single backward flow via a coarse-to-fine LiteMonoFlow module (pixel-shuffle/unshuffle convolutions at 1/4, 1/2, 1/1 scales), then approximates the forward flow through a forward-backward consistency relation. Two candidate right views are generated: a forward-warped frame (sharp geometry, holes in disocclusions) and a backward-warped frame (dense coverage, blur near occlusions), which are fused by a learnable per-pixel mask. A Mask-Guided Parameter-Free Attention (MGPFA) module uses the fusion mask to reweight the flow without adding learnable parameters, and a Bi-Warp Perceptual Refinement Loss applies masked perceptual penalties specifically to backward-selected disoccluded regions to sharpen details. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with photometric and perceptual losses, requiring no inpainting network and only 1.48M parameters.

## Results

On the Mono2Stereo benchmark, XPaintNet achieves the best LPIPS (0.092) and SIoU (0.293), outperforming Mono2Stereo (0.109, 0.265) and Deep3D (0.115, 0.279). On Inria3D, XPaintNet is competitive with Mono2Stereo, achieving LPIPS 0.128 vs. 0.119 and SIoU 0.293 vs. 0.300. Critically, XPaintNet runs at 109 FPS at 2K resolution with only 15.04 GMACs and 1.48M parameters, compared to 17.92 FPS (Deep3D), 0.12 FPS (Mono2Stereo), and 0.01 FPS (StereoCrafter), making it the only method achieving real-time performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

XPaintNet clearly outperforms Deep3D on Mono2Stereo (LPIPS 0.092 vs. 0.115, SIoU 0.293 vs. 0.279) while being 12.6× faster (109 vs. 17.92 FPS) and using 12.7× fewer MACs. Against the diffusion-based StereoCrafter, XPaintNet achieves substantially better LPIPS (0.092 vs. 0.233) and SIoU (0.293 vs. 0.237) on Mono2Stereo, with a 3064× speedup (109 vs. 0.01 FPS). On Inria3D, Mono2Stereo retains a slight edge in LPIPS (0.119 vs. 0.128) and SIoU (0.300 vs. 0.293), but XPaintNet is 8020× faster (109 vs. 0.12 FPS) with 64× fewer MACs. In the inpainting ablation (Table 2), Bi-Warp and Fusion beats all inpainting baselines (FuseFormer, E2FGVI, MI-GAN, ProPainter, StrDiffusion) on both LPIPS and SIoU across both benchmarks, confirming that bidirectional warping outperforms generative hole-filling for geometric consistency. OWL3D, a commercial software, is outperformed on all three Mono2Stereo metrics (LPIPS 0.092 vs. 0.154, SIoU 0.293 vs. 0.263, iSQoE 0.618 vs. 0.622).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometry-based stereoscopic conversion pipeline (depth/disparity estimation → forward warping → inpainting) by replacing the inpainting stage with a bidirectional warping and fusion mechanism. It repurposes forward and backward warping operations well-known from video frame interpolation (Super-SloMo, Softmax Splatting) and reframes them as complementary disocclusion handlers rather than as a single-directional projection. The shift from 1D horizontal disparity to 2D flow (capturing residual vertical parallax) extends the geometric modeling beyond the rectified-parallel-rig assumption that underpins most prior methods like Deep3D and Mono2Stereo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: achieving 100+ FPS at 2K resolution with only 1.48M parameters opens the door to real-time 3D conversion on edge devices for AR/VR and consumer 3D displays, a regime previously inaccessible to diffusion-based or heavy inpainting pipelines. The key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is that bidirectional warping reuses observed pixels rather than generating truly novel structure, so large disocclusions may still appear as smeared or repeated textures rather than plausible new content. Future work on geometry-aware completion that respects the left-view constraints while synthesizing genuinely new structure would close this gap.
