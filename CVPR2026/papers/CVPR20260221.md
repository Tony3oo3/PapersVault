## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion-based super-resolution methods suffer from three critical limitations: (1) fidelity degradation caused by information loss when LQ images are compressed through VAE encoding, (2) indiscriminate activation of generative priors that treats all spatial regions equally, leading to artifacts in smooth areas and insufficient detail in textured ones, and (3) spatial misalignment between text prompt embeddings and their corresponding semantic regions in the image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260221/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (128×128 for ×4 SR or 512×512 for ×2 SR) and a text prompt as input, producing a single high-quality super-resolved image (512×512 or 1024×1024) in one forward pass. The LQ image is consumed in two forms: its VAE-encoded latent for the diffusion process and its pixel-unshuffled pixel-space features for the LQ-guided modulation. Text prompts are decomposed into noun-level tokens via NLTK, and Grounded-SAM2 generates binary region masks offline that are cached and used to constrain cross-attention during inference without any runtime segmentation cost.

## Method

CODSR builds on Stable Diffusion 2.1-base with LoRA adapters and introduces three key mechanisms. First, a region-adaptive generative prior activation (RGPA) module computes a Sobel gradient map from the LQ image, maps it to 16×16 patch-wise noise weights, and multiplies element-wise with Gaussian noise to construct spatially adaptive noise added to the LQ latent before the single denoising step—concentrating stochastic exploration in high-frequency regions while preserving flat areas. Second, an LQ-guided feature modulation (LQFM) module applies pixel-unshuffle to the LQ image to retain uncompressed information, then uses a time-aware spatial feature transform (SFT) conditioned on these features to modulate intermediate U-Net features, with a time-dependent coefficient λ_t = 1/w_t ensuring compatibility with the noise level. Third, a text-matching guidance (TMG) strategy uses Grounded-SAM2-derived noun masks to supervise cross-attention maps via a positive area loss (CoMat), enforcing spatial alignment between text conditioning and semantic regions. Training proceeds in two stages: the first optimizes pixel-wise content, LPIPS, and GAN losses; the second freezes stage-1 LoRAs, adds cross-attention-only LoRAs, and optimizes OSEDiff loss plus VSD loss for semantic distillation plus the positive area loss for text alignment.

## Results

On the DrealSR benchmark, CODSR achieves 28.19 dB PSNR, 0.7761 SSIM, 0.2919 LPIPS, and 0.2108 DISTS, while leading all no-reference metrics: 5.97 NIQE, 67.05 MUSIQ, 0.6278 MANIQA, and 0.5589 CLIPIQA+. On RealSR, it records 25.37 dB PSNR, 0.7284 SSIM, 0.2741 LPIPS, 0.2002 DISTS, 5.31 NIQE, 70.54 MUSIQ, 0.6727 MANIQA, and 0.5768 CLIPIQA+. CODSR attains the best no-reference scores across all four test datasets (RealSR, DrealSR, RealPhoto60, RealDeg), with MUSIQ improvements of at least 1.96 (DrealSR) and 5.15 (RealDeg) over the strongest competing methods. All results are obtained with a single forward pass (one-step inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CODSR outperforms all nine baselines on every no-reference metric (NIQE, MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA+) across all four datasets, with the largest margins over full-step methods: e.g., on DrealSR it improves MUSIQ by 4.52 over DiffBIR (67.05 vs. 62.53) and CLIPIQA+ by 0.0033 over FaithDiff (0.5589 vs. 0.5649 is actually a loss here—FaithDiff edges CODSR by 0.006 on CLIPIQA+ for DrealSR). Among one-step competitors, CODSR beats PiSA-SR (the strongest one-step baseline) by 0.94 MUSIQ and 0.0299 CLIPIQA+ on DrealSR, and by 0.60 MUSIQ and 0.0253 CLIPIQA+ on RealDeg. On full-reference fidelity metrics, CODSR is competitive but not uniformly best: TVT leads on SSIM (0.7899 vs. 0.7761 on DrealSR) and LPIPS (0.2900 vs. 0.2919), while SinSR achieves the highest PSNR (28.42 vs. 28.19 on DrealSR). HYPIR shows the weakest fidelity (22.89 dB PSNR on RealSR, 26.04 on DrealSR) despite reasonable no-reference scores, and SUPIR lags substantially on all metrics. Overall, CODSR achieves the best perceptual quality with only a small fidelity gap (≤0.2 dB PSNR, ≤0.01 SSIM) relative to the top-fidelity one-step methods, all in a single inference step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step diffusion-based SISR lineage initiated by OSEDiff and SinSR, building directly on the Stable Diffusion 2.1-base architecture with LoRA adapters. It departs from prior one-step methods (OSEDiff, PiSA-SR, TVT, TSD-SR) in three specific ways: (1) introducing region-adaptive noise via Sobel gradients to discriminatively activate generative priors only in high-frequency regions, (2) bypassing VAE compression loss by modulating intermediate U-Net features with uncompressed LQ information, and (3) enforcing spatial text-image alignment using Grounded-SAM2 masks. Conceptually, the key departure is the observation that treating all spatial regions identically during one-step denoising is suboptimal, and that the forward-noising pathway should be made region-aware to match the reverse denoising process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

One-step inference is critical for practical deployment of diffusion-based SR, and this paper demonstrates that the fidelity-perceptual-quality trade-off can be improved without sacrificing speed. The region-adaptive noise strategy offers a general principle—matching the noise injection pattern to the spatial uncertainty of the degradation—that could transfer to other inverse problems. However, the method remains tied to the SD 2.1-base architecture and ×4 SR setting, and the text-matching guidance, while effective, depends on pre-computed segmentation masks that may not generalize to arbitrary or novel prompts at test time. An open question is whether the region-discriminative activation principle extends to video SR or other multi-scale restoration tasks where temporal consistency adds further constraints.
