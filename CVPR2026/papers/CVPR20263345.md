## Problem

Existing MLLM-based agents for long-form video understanding (LVU) face two critical bottlenecks: single-agent reasoning degrades when task complexity exceeds the model's capacity, and embedding-based retrieval (e.g., CLIP or RAG) loses key information for complex questions with implicit intentions and large temporal spans. Prior multi-agent approaches either partition by modality (incurring high cross-modal integration costs) or use linear static pipelines that constrain the explorable solution space.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a long video (capped at 720p, max 40 frames per VLM call), a natural-language question, and video subtitles (auto-extracted via Whisper-large-v3 when unavailable). The grounding agent partitions the video into non-overlapping 60-second segments with 30 frames sampled per segment for VLM-based relevance scoring. The output is a single answer to the video question, produced after up to 15 scheduling rounds and 3 reflection cycles.

## Method

Symphony decomposes LVU along cognitive capability dimensions (planning, grounding, subtitle, visual perception, reflection) rather than modality, minimizing inter-agent coupling. The planning agent (DeepSeek R1) acts as a central policy model that dynamically selects sub-tasks from a specialized agent pool, while a reflection agent (also DeepSeek R1) validates the reasoning trajectory in an Actor-Critic-inspired loop, generating critiques that trigger additional reasoning rounds. The grounding agent autonomously selects between CLIP-based retrieval (for simple queries) and a VLM-based relevance scoring tool (for complex queries), where an LLM first decomposes and semantically expands the query before the VLM scores each video segment on a 4-level rubric. This reflection-enhanced dynamic collaboration, combined with capability-dimension decoupling, reduces per-agent cognitive load and expands the solution space beyond what fixed-pipeline or single-agent methods can explore.

## Results

Symphony achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four long-video understanding benchmarks: 71.8% on LVBench, 77.1% on LongVideoBench, 78.1% on Video-MME (long), and 81.0% on MLVU. On the most challenging LVBench (average video duration ~68 min), Symphony surpasses the prior best agent-based method DVD (66.8%) by 5.0 percentage points. On LongVideoBench, it exceeds VideoDeepResearch (70.6%) by 6.5%, and on MLVU it outperforms VideoChat-A1 (76.2%) by 4.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Symphony outperforms all 17 baselines across every benchmark where they overlap. On LVBench, the closest competitor is DVD (66.8%), which Symphony surpasses by 5.0 points; commercial VLMs (GPT-4o at 48.9%, OpenAI o3 at 57.1%) and open-source VLMs (Seed 1.6 VL at 58.1%) trail by 13–34 points. On LongVideoBench, VideoDeepResearch (70.6%) is the nearest agent-based rival, beaten by 6.5 points, while the best commercial VLM (OpenAI o3, 67.5%) lags by 9.6 points. On Video-MME, Symphony edges out VideoDeepResearch (76.3%) by 1.8 points and ReAgent-V (72.9%) by 5.2 points. On MLVU, Symphony leads VideoChat-A1 (76.2%) by 4.8 points and ReAgent-V (74.2%) by 6.8 points. The paper further shows that Symphony's advantage is foundation-model-agnostic: when DVD, VideoDeepResearch, and Symphony are all run with the same underlying LLM/VLM, Symphony consistently delivers the largest improvement over the base model, confirming that the multi-agent decomposition and reflection mechanism—not model scale—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

Symphony departs from prior multi-agent video understanding systems (VideoMultiAgents, LvAgent) by decomposing tasks along cognitive-function dimensions (planning, grounding, perception, language, reflection) rather than along modality or using fixed linear pipelines. It builds on the Actor-Critic verification idea and the Verifier's Law insight to introduce a dedicated reflection agent that critiques reasoning trajectories, and on RAG-based grounding (DVD, VideoRAG) by replacing static retrieval with an LLM-enhanced query + VLM-based relevance scoring pipeline. The grounding agent's autonomous selection between CLIP retrieval and VLM scoring based on query complexity is a practical departure from prior methods that commit to a single retrieval strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent SOTA across four diverse LVU benchmarks (5.0% over prior best on LVBench, 6.5% on LongVideoBench) demonstrates that cognitive-function decomposition with reflection is a viable path to scaling reasoning in long-video settings without requiring a single monolithic model to handle all subtasks. However, the system's reliance on multiple large models (DeepSeek-R1 for planning/reflection, Doubao Seed 1.6 VL for perception/grounding) and the 68-second VLM-based grounding latency raise practical deployment questions. Open questions include whether the reflection mechanism's gains persist as base models improve, whether the approach extends to real-time or streaming video, and how the system behaves on videos significantly longer than the ~68-minute average in LVBench.
