## Problem

State-of-the-art text-guided diffusion models surprisingly fail at simple, everyday visual transformations such as rain, blur, or haze, because weak and inconsistent image-caption supervision during training leaves these appearance-level features poorly aligned with their textual descriptions. Existing remedies—fine-tuning the model or strengthening text conditioning—impose high data and compute costs, while the paper's key observation is that the editing capability is not absent from the model but merely inaccessible through the text interface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target RGB image to be edited and a single paired visual example (one image with the target effect, one without) as the sole conditioning signal; no text prompt is required. It operates entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.4, inverting the target image via DDIM and applying the learned condition over only the last 10 of 100 diffusion steps. The output is a full-resolution edited image, with the one-shot setup using one example (augmented by flip, rotation, and color jitter) and the multi-shot variant scaling to eight examples for more diverse degradations.

## Method

VDC replaces textual conditioning with a learned visual condition derived from the example pair, steering the unconditional diffusion trajectory via posterior score guidance: the per-step noise prediction is a weighted blend of the condition-conditioned and null-conditioned predictions, ε = (1−w)·ε_θ(z_t, C^s) + w·ε_θ(z_t, φ), which shifts sampling away from the unwanted visual feature without generating a new image. The condition itself is produced by a lightweight three-layer MLP (~100K parameters per step) with Fourier-feature inputs over token indices, inspired by implicit neural representations, so that all 77 tokens are generated jointly and smoothly rather than optimized independently. All per-step generators are optimized jointly against the final decoded output using a combined latent-space L2 loss and a pixel-space L2 loss, allowing the model to distribute edits flexibly across the diffusion trajectory. An additional inversion-correction step refines the DDIM-inverted latent by forward-diffusing it and back-propagating the reconstruction error, mitigating accumulated inversion drift. The entire pipeline is training-free with respect to the diffusion backbone—only the small condition generators are optimized (~200 Adam iterations, ~30 min on a single RTX 4090)—and the framework is model-agnostic, extending to flow-matching architectures such as SANA.

## Results

VDC (One-Shot) achieves the best LPIPS across all six tasks (SR 0.2666, DeBlur 0.2654, DeNoise 0.2801, DeRain 0.2559, DeHaze 0.1633, Colorization 0.2908) and the best FID on DeBlur (35.51), DeNoise (89.51), DeRain (87.12), and DeHaze (35.52). The full VDC pipeline (Multi-Shot + Inversion Correction) further improves to FID 66.92 / LPIPS 0.2155 on DeRain and FID 33.23 / LPIPS 0.1560 on DeHaze, setting new state-of-the-art on average across all benchmarks. Notably, VDC requires only a single visual example pair and operates training-free on a single RTX 4090, with inference adding zero overhead beyond the base diffusion model's 10 timesteps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDC (One-Shot) outperforms all 13 baselines on LPIPS across every task and on FID for DeBlur (35.51 vs. best baseline 42.89 by PSLD), DeNoise (89.51 vs. 94.11 by TReg), DeRain (87.12 vs. 118.76 by Negative-Cond), and DeHaze (35.52 vs. 43.20 by Negative-Cond). The only FID loss is on SR, where PSLD achieves 31.90 vs. VDC's 41.41, though VDC still leads on LPIPS (0.2666 vs. 0.2839). Among text-based editors (P2P, Null-Opt, Negative-Cond), VDC improves FID by 22–80 points and LPIPS by 0.05–0.30, confirming the paper's central claim that text conditioning is the bottleneck. Instruction-based methods (Instruct-Pix2Pix, OmniGen, SuperEdit, ICEdit) show the largest gaps on DeRain and DeNoise, where VDC's One-Shot FID of 87.12 beats ICEdit's 149.44 by over 60 points. Zero-shot restoration methods (PSLD, TReg, DAPS) are the closest competitors on SR and DeBlur but do not report DeRain/DeHaze results, and VDC's Multi-Shot + Inversion Correction variant extends the lead on DeRain to 66.92 FID. Exemplar-based methods (VISII, Analogist, EditClip) underperform VDC by 20–120 FID points on most tasks, attributed to their reliance on CLIP-style text-image embeddings that reintroduce the language-vision misalignment VDC avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDC builds on DDIM inversion for latent-space trajectory reconstruction and on posterior score guidance (from score-based SDE formulations) to derive its condition-steering mechanism, which modulates the unconditional denoising path rather than generating a new image. It departs from text-based editing methods (P2P, Null-Opt, InstructPix2Pix) by entirely removing textual conditioning, and from exemplar-based methods (VISII, Analogist, EditCLIP) by eliminating their reliance on VLMs or text-aligned latent spaces. The condition generator borrows the INR/Fourier-feature design from implicit neural representations, repurposing it to produce per-step diffusion conditioning tokens from a single visual example pair rather than from pixel coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that diffusion models already encode appearance-level editing capabilities that are merely inaccessible through text conditioning—reframes a large class of restoration and editing tasks as a conditioning problem rather than a training problem. This opens a practical, training-free path to precise visual edits from a single example pair, with zero inference overhead beyond the base model. Limitations include the need for paired before/after examples, a ~30-minute optimization for peak fidelity, and evaluation confined to relatively structured degradations; extending VDC to unposed, in-the-wild scenes and to more open-ended semantic edits remains an open question the authors flag for future work.
