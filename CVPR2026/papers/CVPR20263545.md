## Problem

Existing web-agent benchmarks evaluate agents solely on web-page perception and interaction, with no grounding in the user's physical surroundings. This leaves a critical gap: there is no benchmark that tests whether an agent can perceive an object or event in the user's real-world egocentric view (e.g., via AR glasses) and then execute the corresponding online task, such as purchasing the exact product the user is holding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263545/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a first-person egocentric video (sourced from Ego4D, spanning household, shopping, travel, and office scenes) together with a natural-language instruction that explicitly requires visual grounding (e.g., 'the fourth snack picked up in the video'). Depending on the agent's architecture, the video is consumed as raw video (Gemini-based agents) or as sparse keyframes (GPT-based agents), with structured captions generated every 5 seconds by Qwen3-VL as a fallback. The agent outputs a sequence of browser actions, intermediate webpage screenshots, and a final textual response; task success is determined as a binary judgment by Ego2WebJudge or human annotators.

## Method

Ego2Web is built via a semi-automatic pipeline: Qwen3-VL produces clip-level dense captions with timestamps to form a structured video profile, which is then fed to GPT-5 along with a set of predefined active websites (Amazon, YouTube, Wikipedia, etc.) to synthesize task instructions that must explicitly leverage visual content; human annotators verify each pair on visual grounding, web feasibility, and instruction quality, yielding 500 curated video-instruction pairs. For evaluation, Ego2WebJudge extends the WebJudge framework with a three-stage multimodal LLM-as-a-Judge protocol: (1) an LLM extracts critical key points from the instruction, (2) an MLLM scores each screenshot on a 1–5 relevance scale and retains only those above a threshold δ to avoid context overflow, and (3) a multimodal LLM integrates the instruction, selected key screenshots, action history, extracted key points, and annotated keyframes from the egocentric video to render a binary success/failure judgment. This design achieves ~84% agreement with human evaluation, substantially outperforming prior automatic evaluators (WebVoyager, WebJudge) that lack access to the egocentric visual evidence.

## Results

On Ego2Web (500 video-instruction pairs), the best agent BU-Gemini-3-Flash achieves 58.6% success rate under human evaluation, with the next-best BU-GPT-4.1 reaching 44.4%, leaving a substantial ~40% performance gap across all agents. The proposed Ego2WebJudge automatic evaluator achieves 84.0% agreement rate with human judgment (using GPT-4o as judge backbone), outperforming WebVoyager (74.7%) and WebJudge (78.4%) by 9.3 and 5.6 percentage points respectively. Ablation studies confirm that raw video input is critical: removing visual input drops BU-Gemini-3-Flash from 48.2% to 4.4% SR, while caption-only input yields only 23.6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BU-Gemini-3-Flash (Browser-Use with Gemini-3-Flash) is the clear leader at 58.6% SR under human evaluation, outperforming BU-GPT-4.1 (44.4%) by 14.2 points and SeeAct (34.2%) by 24.4 points. Among the non-Browser-Use agents, SeeAct (34.2%) edges out Claude 4.5 (32.8%) and GPT-5.4 (30.6%), while Claude 3.7 Computer Use trails all others at 26.4%. The paper attributes the underperformance of GPT-5.4 and Claude agents to their inability to process raw video in computer-use mode, forcing reliance on lossy textual captions. On the evaluation-method side, Ego2WebJudge consistently outperforms both WebVoyager and WebJudge in agreement with human judgment across all six agents, with the largest margin over WebVoyager (80.8% vs. 70.7% avg. AR with Gemini-2.5-Pro). The gap between the best automatic judge and human evaluation is within ~1–2% for the top agent, validating Ego2WebJudge as a scalable proxy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed but previously disconnected research threads: egocentric video understanding (Ego4D, EgoSchema, EgoThink) and multimodal web-agent benchmarks (WebArena, VisualWebArena, WebVoyager, Online-Mind2Web). Prior egocentric benchmarks evaluate perception and reasoning in isolation without any downstream action, while prior web-agent benchmarks rely solely on on-screen content (screenshots, DOM trees) with no grounding in the user's physical visual context. Ego2Web explicitly departs from both by requiring the agent to first ground visual cues in a first-person video and then execute corresponding web actions, and its evaluation framework (Ego2WebJudge) extends WebJudge [50] by incorporating annotated egocentric video clips as visual evidence for the LLM-as-a-Judge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multimodal assistants become embedded in AR glasses, wearables, and home robots, the ability to perceive the physical world and act in the digital one is a critical capability that current benchmarks cannot measure. The ~40% performance gap between the best agent and human evaluation, combined with the ablation showing raw video (48.2%) dramatically outperforming text captions (23.6%) and no visual input (4.4%), underscores that current agents lack the temporal and spatial grounding needed for real-world multimodal tasks. Open questions include whether scaling MLLMs or improving video understanding alone can close this gap, whether the 500-example scale is sufficient for reliable benchmarking, and how to handle the practical constraints (CAPTCHAs, session errors, site changes) that affect online evaluation reproducibility.
