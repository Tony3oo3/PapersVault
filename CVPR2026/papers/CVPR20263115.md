## Problem

Frame-to-frame monocular SLAM methods achieve real-time performance through local sliding-window optimization, but their pixel-level matching constraints are inherently scale-agnostic, causing each window to establish an independent scale reference and accumulate progressive scale drift over long sequences. This drift fragments the map, degrades loop closure, and prevents reliable kilometer-scale mapping. The paper asks whether scale consistency can be maintained within the efficient frame-to-frame paradigm without resorting to heavy global optimization or external metric depth models.

## Input / Output

The system consumes monocular RGB video frames, extracting 80 patches per frame at SuperPoint keypoints (3×3 patches from DINOv3-augmented CNN feature maps) rather than DPVO's random sampling, ensuring multi-view trackability. It outputs a camera pose trajectory, per-patch depth estimates, and learned 3D scene coordinate predictions (X^prior) that encode each patch's position under a canonical scale reference. The system operates at 36 FPS in real time, with the scene coordinate branch adding minimal overhead over the base DPVO flow branch.

## Method

SCE-SLAM extends DPVO with a dual-branch architecture: a flow branch for scale-agnostic pixel-level constraints and a scene coordinate branch maintaining patch-level embeddings h^xyz that accumulate scale-consistent geometric memory across temporal windows. Geometry-Guided Scale Propagation updates each patch's embedding by attending over 1200 reference patches (top-50% BA residuals from 30 nearest frames) using an attention score that combines feature similarity with a quadratic 3D distance penalty (−λ‖X_a−X_r‖²), followed by GRU recurrent aggregation to preserve geometric memory across iterations. Scene Coordinate Bundle Adjustment decodes embeddings into residual 3D coordinate predictions ΔX_k and augments standard Gauss-Newton BA with explicit world-coordinate residuals that penalize scale deviations, pulling drifting estimates back toward the canonical reference. Training uses a two-stage curriculum on TartanAir: the first 10K iterations freeze the flow branch to initialize the coordinate branch, then both are jointly optimized with supervised flow, pose, and scene-coordinate losses.

## Results

On KITTI Odometry (11 sequences), SCE-SLAM achieves the best average ATE of 14.07 m (with loop closure) and 25.79 m (without loop closure), reducing error by 8.36 m over the best prior method (DPV-SLAM++, 25.75 m) while running at 36 FPS. On Waymo Open, it achieves 0.915 m average ATE, outperforming VGGT-Long (1.996 m) and MegaSaM (2.776 m). On Virtual KITTI across six weather conditions, it attains 0.280 m average ATE, surpassing even DPV-SLAM++ with loop closure (0.343 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCE-SLAM outperforms all frame-to-frame baselines on KITTI: without loop closure it matches DPV-SLAM++ (25.79 m vs. 25.75 m) while being 27.8 m better than DPVO (53.61 m) and 74.5 m better than DROID-SLAM (100.28 m). With loop closure, it achieves 14.07 m, a 11.68 m improvement over DPV-SLAM++ (25.75 m) and a 13.57 m improvement over VGGT-Long (27.64 m). On Waymo, it halves the error of VGGT-Long (0.915 m vs. 1.996 m) and outperforms MegaSaM (2.776 m), which relies on an external metric depth model. On Virtual KITTI, it beats DPV-SLAM++ (0.280 m vs. 0.343 m) and dramatically outperforms CUT3R (38.019 m) and MASt3R-SLAM (tracking lost on all sequences). Notably, MASt3R-SLAM and CUT3R fail entirely on KITTI (TL/OOM), highlighting scalability limitations of global multi-view attention approaches, whereas SCE-SLAM maintains 36 FPS real-time performance across all benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DPVO's patch-based recurrent visual odometry framework by adding a scene coordinate branch that maintains scale consistency across temporal windows. It departs from the frame-to-frame SLAM lineage (DROID-SLAM, DPVO, DPV-SLAM) where optical flow constraints are inherently scale-agnostic, and contrasts with heavier alternatives that address scale drift via metric depth prediction (Metric3D, UniDepth) or large multi-view geometry transformers (MAST3R, VGGT) that incur quadratic attention costs. The geometry-modulated attention mechanism repurposes the scene coordinate regression idea from relocalization literature, but redirects it toward encoding a persistent scale reference rather than absolute 3D positions, and the DINOv3-augmented backbone borrows from foundation-model feature extraction to improve cross-view matching reliability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that scale consistency in monocular SLAM can be maintained through lightweight recurrent patch embeddings at 36 FPS, eliminating the need for expensive metric depth models or global multi-view attention while matching or exceeding loop-closure methods (DPV-SLAM++) on KITTI without loop closure. This opens a practical path for long-sequence monocular mapping on resource-constrained platforms where scale drift previously forced reliance on loop closure or external metric sensors. Open questions include generalization beyond driving-centric evaluation (KITTI, Waymo, vKITTI are all automotive), the sensitivity to the DINOv3 dependency, and whether the 30-frame reference window and 50% residual threshold generalize to scenes with very different temporal structure (e.g., indoor, aerial).
