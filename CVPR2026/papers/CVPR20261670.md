## Problem

Video generation reward models face three intertwined challenges: in-prompt pairwise annotation introduces substantial label noise when candidates are of comparable quality, standard Bradley-Terry training on win-lose pairs leaves the positive-sample score distribution unconstrained (enabling reward hacking during post-training), and common VLM output mechanisms (last-token pooling, special tokens, yes/no logits) produce score clustering that degrades discriminative power. No existing framework jointly addresses all three.

## Input / Output

The reward model takes a single video clip paired with its generating text prompt and outputs a continuous scalar reward score for one specific quality dimension (Physical Plausibility, Subject Deformity, or Semantic Alignment). Training data consists of 250k in-house videos from 20k unique prompts annotated with binary Pass/Fail labels per dimension, plus a 50k OOD test set from SOTA models (Wan2.1/2.2, Veo 3, Seedance 1.0). The scalar output is used directly as the reward signal in GRPO-based post-training of HunyuanVideo.

## Method

SoliReward combines three components. For data, it replaces noisy in-prompt pairwise comparisons with single-item binary (Pass/Fail) annotation, then constructs preference pairs via a cross-prompt pairing strategy that pairs any Pass sample with any Fail sample regardless of prompt, yielding a large-scale high-signal dataset. For training, it introduces the BT-WT loss, which augments standard win-lose BT pairs with win-tie pairs (Pass vs. Pass) that explicitly penalize score variance among positive samples, regularizing the reward landscape to prevent the generative model from exploiting reward spikes. Architecturally, it proposes Hierarchical Progressive Query Attention (HPQA), where a learnable query vector sequentially attends to hidden states from multiple specified transformer layers (each layer's output becomes the next query), progressively fusing low-level visual fidelity with high-level semantic features, and the result is combined via residual connection with a separate attention readout of the final layer before a linear RewardHead produces the scalar score.

## Results

On the Physical Plausibility & Subject Deformity task, SoliReward achieves 78.48% in-domain (ID) and 80.08% out-of-domain (OOD) reward model accuracy, surpassing the strongest baseline VideoAlign (54.40% ID / 71.60% OOD) by over 24 points on ID. On the Semantic Alignment (TA) task, it reaches 79.02% ID accuracy, far exceeding the closest competitor VideoPhy (54.85%), while its OOD score of 60.25% is competitive with VideoPhy's 60.52%. In post-training with HunyuanVideo + DanceGRPO, SoliReward-guided fine-tuning yields MQ 0.3302 and VBench2 Human Fidelity 0.8999, compared to 0.1607 and 0.8695 for VideoAlign MQ guidance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoliReward decisively outperforms all seven baselines in Table 2 on both tasks. On Phy&Deform, it leads the runner-up VideoAlign (71.60% OOD) by 8.5 points OOD and by 24 points ID; VideoPhy (67.35% ID) and VideoScore (62.30% ID) trail further behind. On TA, SoliReward's 79.02% ID accuracy exceeds VideoPhy (54.85%) by 24 points, while its OOD score (60.25%) is within 0.27 points of VideoPhy's 60.52%, effectively tying on that single metric. LiFT, UnifiedReward, and VisionReward suffer severe score clustering (marked *), collapsing to discrete outputs that render them near-random on TA (e.g., LiFT at 1.75% ID). In the architecture ablation (Table 5), HPQA also beats the Linear head [40] (74.69/78.66 Phy&Deform) and the 'Yes'-token logits approach from RewardDance [34] (75.43/78.46 Phy&Deform; 71.56*/31.37* TA), with the latter two exhibiting score clustering on TA. In post-training (Table 4), SoliReward-guided DanceGRPO improves MQ from −0.0980 (no RM) to 0.3302, nearly doubling VideoAlign MQ's 0.1607, confirming that existing MQ-focused RMs are insufficient for physical plausibility and deformity correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video generation reward modeling (VideoAlign, VideoScore, VisionReward, RewardDance) and RLHF-based post-training for video (DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO). It departs from the dominant in-prompt pairwise annotation paradigm by replacing it with single-item binary labels and a cross-prompt pairing strategy, and from standard BT loss by introducing win-tie pairs (BT-WT) to regularize the positive-sample score distribution. Architecturally, it moves beyond the common last-token, special-token, or yes-token probability heads toward a multi-layer progressive attention adapter (HPQA), drawing on findings about functional specialization across transformer layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking in video generation post-training is a practical bottleneck that limits the utility of RM-guided optimization; the BT-WT mechanism offers a simple, interpretable regularizer that directly addresses it without architectural changes. The finding that RM accuracy is a deceptive proxy for downstream post-training quality (BT-WT and BT have near-identical accuracy but BT-WT nearly doubles the MQ improvement) shifts the design criterion from classification accuracy to score-distribution shape. Open questions include whether the single-dimension scoring generalizes to multi-dimensional fusion, whether the 8B-parameter sweet spot (diminishing returns at 14B) holds across backbones, and whether the cross-prompt pairing strategy transfers to image generation or other modalities.
