## Problem

Open-vocabulary detectors inherit tightly coupled cross-modal knowledge from web-scale pretraining, creating privacy and compliance risks that demand selective concept removal. Existing machine unlearning methods fail in this setting due to geometric entanglement interference: because VLM embeddings are decomposable into shared semantic factors, any forgetting update inevitably distorts related preserved concepts (e.g., erasing 'woman' degrades 'person'). No unified benchmark existed to systematically evaluate unlearning across open-vocabulary detection tasks.

## Input / Output

The unlearning procedure takes a pretrained OvOD model (GroundingDINO or LLM-Det with Swin backbones), a set of forget concept phrases, and a set of retain concept phrases. Retain text embeddings are extracted offline to precompute the null-space projector P_null before any training begins. At inference, the modified model still accepts RGB images and text prompts, producing bounding boxes with class labels (OD), grounded phrase regions (PG), or referring expression matches (REC), but with forget concepts rendered undetectable while retain concepts maintain their original detection performance.

## Method

SafeDetect constrains all parameter updates to the null-space of the retain embedding matrix, computed via SVD offline: W' = W + P_null · ΔW, which mathematically guarantees zero first-order interference with retained concept directions. Forgetting is achieved through a one-step mean-flow objective that minimizes KL divergence between the model's forget-class logits and a uniform distribution, directly driving outputs to a non-detectable state without iterative optimization. A cross-modal decoupling loss operates at the decoder query level (not the bbox head), driving visual-text similarity for forget concepts from near-zero to strongly negative values (−0.6 to −0.8) to achieve representation-level forgetting that prevents recovery via linguistic variants. Training uses LoRA (rank 128) adapters with the null-space constraint applied to the adapter matrices, operating exclusively on forget data with no loss-level balancing between forget and retain objectives.

## Results

On UOD-Bench (1% forget ratio, OD task, LLM-Det/Swin-T), SafeDetect achieves Forget mAP of 17.8% (vs. 50.5% for NPO, 47.3% for MultiDelete) and U-Score of 23.5 (vs. 10.0 for NPO, 12.5 for MultiDelete), representing a 64.75% improvement in forgetting efficacy over NPO. On zero-shot generalization (LVIS AP), SafeDetect incurs only an 8.3-point average drop versus 14.2 points for NPO across all forgetting ratios. SafeDetect also converges 1.5× faster (500 vs. ~750 steps) and requires 40% fewer GPU-hours (13.2 vs. 22.0) than NPO while maintaining identical trainable parameters (831M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeDetect outperforms all four baselines across every forgetting ratio, task (OD, PG, REC), and architecture (LLM-Det, GroundingDINO). Gradient Ascent (GA) [48] is essentially ineffective for OvOD unlearning, achieving only a 1.4% Forget mAP reduction at 1% ratio (U-Score 2.7), confirming that naive gradient ascent cannot overcome the tightly coupled cross-modal geometry. Negative Preference Optimization (NPO) [65] is the strongest baseline but suffers severe retention degradation (Retain mAP drops from 20.7 to 15.2 at 1%) and zero-shot generalization loss (14.2-point LVIS AP drop vs. 8.3 for SafeDetect), directly attributable to geometric entanglement interference. GradDiff [34] shows moderate forgetting but the weakest U-Scores among non-trivial baselines (8.0 at 1%), while MultiDelete [9] achieves the best baseline U-Score (12.5 at 1%) yet still lags SafeDetect by nearly 2× (23.5 vs. 12.5) and fails to prevent retention erosion as forgetting ratios increase. Notably, SafeDetect's advantage grows with forgetting ratio: at 15%, it achieves U-Score 12.9 vs. 7.8 for MultiDelete and 5.6 for NPO, while maintaining the highest Retain mAP (17.2) among all unlearning methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeDetect adapts the null-space projection mechanism from knowledge editing (LEACE, AlphaEdit) to the machine unlearning setting, but departs fundamentally from prior MU methods that rely on loss-level balancing (forget + retain objectives). The key conceptual departure is diagnosing 'geometric entanglement interference' in decomposable VLM embeddings as the root cause of unlearning failures, then constraining all parameter updates to the orthogonal complement of the retain subspace rather than merely weighting competing losses. The one-step forgetting objective draws inspiration from mean-flow generative models, repurposing the direct-mapping-to-target-distribution idea for driving detection outputs toward uniformity. The paper also extends the unlearning evaluation landscape by introducing UOD-Bench, the first benchmark specifically targeting open-vocabulary detectors across OD, PG, and REC tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic treatment of machine unlearning in open-vocabulary detectors, a practically critical gap given that OvOD models inherit privacy risks from web-scale pretraining and face regulatory 'right to be forgotten' demands. The geometric entanglement diagnosis provides a reusable conceptual framework for understanding why naive unlearning degrades related concepts in any decomposable embedding space. Limitations include evaluation on only two architectures (GroundingDINO, LLM-Det), the requirement for offline retain-embedding computation that may not scale to truly massive vocabularies, and the absence of adversarial recovery attack evaluation. Open questions include whether null-space constraints remain effective as the retain subspace dimension grows, and whether the approach generalizes to generative detection or video understanding settings.
