## Problem

Existing multimodal survival prediction methods operate in Euclidean space, which cannot faithfully represent the inherent hierarchical structures in histopathology (patient→WSI→patch) and genomics (patient→pathway→gene). They also treat the two modalities as peers to be aligned, ignoring the cross-modal encompassing relationship where abstract genomic patterns subsume diverse morphological phenotypes. Finally, interval-based risk discretization destroys the fine-grained ordinal continuity of survival times, preventing accurate within-interval risk ranking.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired diagnostic WSIs (segmented via OTSU thresholding into non-overlapping 256×256 patches at 20× magnification) and bulk RNA-seq gene expression profiles. Patch-level features are extracted by the pretrained UNI foundation model and gene-level features by GeneFormer, both projected to 1024-D before hyperbolic mapping. Genomic features are organized into six predefined biological pathways (Tumor Suppression, Oncogenesis, Protein Kinases, Cellular Differentiation, Transcription, Cytokines and Growth). The output is a patient-level survival risk score produced by a Cox partial-likelihood head, evaluated via C-index and Kaplan-Meier stratification.

## Method

H²-Surv embeds multimodal features into a Poincaré ball with trainable curvature c = softplus(θ) via a Hyperbolic Mapping (HyMap) module that applies the exponential map at the origin. A Hyperbolic Hierarchical Modeling (H²M) module then imposes two geometric constraints: intra-modal hierarchical distance loss ensuring lower-level nodes (patches/genes) lie farther from the patient embedding than mid-level nodes (WSIs/pathways), and an inter-modal entailment cone where pathway-level genomic embeddings (closer to the origin) define a directional region that WSI embeddings must fall within, encoding the genomic-over-histopathological abstraction hierarchy. A Temporal Ordinal Contrastive Learning (TOCL) module enforces progressive risk ordering by requiring hyperbolic geodesic distances from a query patient to increase monotonically with temporal distance, using a listwise contrastive objective over proximity tiers rather than binary positive-negative pairs. The total loss combines a Cox negative log-likelihood survival term with weighted hierarchical and ordinal contrastive regularizers (λ=0.01, β=0.1).

## Results

H²-Surv achieves a mean C-index of 0.716 across six cancer benchmarks (TCGA-BRCA, TCGA-BLCA, TCGA-UCEC, TCGA-LUAD, CPTAC-UCEC, CPTAC-LUAD), outperforming the strongest baselines PIBD and PANTHER (both 0.684) by 0.032. The largest per-dataset gains are on CPTAC-LUAD (+0.045 over PANTHER) and CPTAC-UCEC (+0.020 over PIBD), while on TCGA-BRCA it reaches 0.763, the highest single-dataset score in the table. Ablation confirms that the full combination of HyMap, H²M, and TOCL yields a +0.050 absolute C-index gain over the MOTCat-style baseline (0.668 → 0.716).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H²-Surv outperforms all 15 baselines on every individual dataset and in the overall mean C-index. The closest competitors are PIBD [43] and PANTHER [30], both achieving 0.684 overall; H²-Surv exceeds them by 0.032. On TCGA-BRCA, H²-Surv (0.763) narrowly beats PANTHER (0.758) by 0.005, while on TCGA-UCEC it edges out PANTHER (0.757) by 0.003. The most substantial margins appear on the smaller CPTAC cohorts: +0.045 on CPTAC-LUAD (0.698 vs. PANTHER's 0.653) and +0.020 on CPTAC-UCEC (0.671 vs. PIBD's 0.651). Among unimodal baselines, the best histopathology-only method (TransMIL, 0.620) and best genomics-only method (SNNTrans, 0.634) trail H²-Surv by 0.096 and 0.082 respectively, underscoring the benefit of multimodal fusion. Among other multimodal methods, CMTA (0.669), CGM (0.675), and MOTCat (0.667) are all surpassed by 0.041–0.047 in overall C-index.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: multimodal survival prediction (MCAT, MOTCat, PIBD, PANTHER) and hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela, HME). It departs from prior multimodal survival methods in three specific ways: (1) replacing Euclidean embeddings with Poincaré-ball hyperbolic embeddings via a learnable curvature parameter, (2) imposing explicit intra-modal hierarchical distance constraints and an inter-modal entailment-cone constraint that encodes the genomic-over-histopathology abstraction hierarchy, and (3) substituting interval-based survival discretization with a progressive ordinal contrastive objective (TOCL) that preserves continuous temporal ordering. The feature extraction pipeline (UNI for histopathology, GeneFormer for genomics) follows the MOTCat design, so the architectural novelty is concentrated in the geometric modeling and loss design rather than in the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled geometric justification for why multimodal survival features should live in hyperbolic space: the exponential volume growth naturally encodes the patient→WSI→patch and patient→pathway→gene hierarchies that Euclidean space distorts. The inter-modal entailment constraint (genomic pathways encompass histopathological phenotypes) is a biologically motivated inductive bias not present in prior alignment-based fusion methods. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the requirement for complete multimodal inputs at both training and inference time; robustness under missing-modality conditions remains an open question. The modest computational overhead of hyperbolic operations (+0.26 GB GPU, +2 min training) relative to the Euclidean variant suggests the approach is practically deployable, though the 50-epoch, batch-size-1 training protocol limits confidence in scalability to larger cohorts.
