## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models exhibit severe mode collapse: repeatedly sampling with a fixed prompt yields nearly identical images across different random seeds. Prior diversity methods rely on guidance mechanisms to steer the model or on generating large candidate pools and pruning, but neither directly controls the noise that determines which mode is sampled. This paper asks whether directly optimizing the initial noise can recover diversity while preserving the base model's fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed text prompt and a batch of B initial noise tensors (typically B=4, in the latent space of the diffusion model) as input, and produces a set of B diverse RGB images as output. The prompt, diffusion model weights, and all objective/reward models remain frozen throughout; only the noise tensors are updated. The framework supports both batched optimization (all B images optimized jointly) and sequential generation (one image at a time, each optimized to differ from all previous outputs), enabling arbitrarily large diverse sets without the memory overhead of simultaneous processing.

## Method

The core mechanism is end-to-end noise optimization: a hinge-penalized objective (Eq. 2) combines a sample-level quality reward (CLIPScore or HPSv2), a batch-level diversity statistic (pairwise DINOv2/DreamSim/LPIPS distances, or set-level DPP/Vendi scores), and a chi-distribution norm regularization that keeps optimized noise within high-density regions of the Gaussian prior. Gradients are backpropagated through the frozen ODE-based sampler to update the initial noise until quality and diversity thresholds are met or a compute budget is exhausted. A key empirical finding is that optimization predominantly modifies low-frequency components of the noise power spectrum, motivating a pink noise initialization that applies 1/(1+f)^α spectral weighting in the frequency domain (α=0.2) before denoising. This pink noise initialization alone—without any optimization—improves diversity for both i.i.d. sampling and the Parmar et al. baseline, and combined with noise optimization yields the strongest results across SDXL-Turbo and Flux.1 [schnell].

## Results

On GenEval with SDXL-Turbo (DPP + HPSv2 objectives), the proposed noise optimization saturates the Vendi diversity score to 4.0 (vs. 2.0 for i.i.d. and 2.77 for Parmar et al.) while improving DreamSim from 0.262 to 0.457 (white noise) and 0.474 (pink noise), with HPSv2 quality at 0.292. On Flux.1 [schnell], the method achieves DreamSim 0.495 and Vendi 3.038 with pink noise, outperforming i.i.d. (0.362, 2.207) and Parmar et al. (0.462, 2.977). Pink noise initialization (α=0.2) consistently boosts diversity across all methods and models, and the full optimization takes only ~2.07 s on a single A100 GPU versus 11.20 s for Parmar et al.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Parmar et al. [42] (Scaling Group Inference), which uses a search strategy over 64–128 candidates to select diverse outputs. The proposed noise optimization wins on both DreamSim and Vendi diversity metrics across all model/noise configurations: on SDXL-Turbo with white noise, DreamSim improves from 0.336 to 0.457 and Vendi from 2.769 to 4.000 (saturated); with pink noise, DreamSim goes from 0.403 to 0.474. On Flux.1 [schnell] with pink noise, DreamSim improves from 0.462 to 0.495 and Vendi from 2.977 to 3.038. On image quality (HPSv2), the method is competitive: it slightly outperforms Parmar et al. on SDXL-Turbo (0.292 vs. 0.273 white; 0.288 vs. 0.273 pink) but shows a small drop on Flux.1 [schnell] with pink noise (0.279 vs. 0.290), which the authors attribute to Flux.1's sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. Notably, pink noise initialization alone (without optimization) also boosts Parmar et al.'s diversity scores, confirming that the frequency profile of initial noise is an independent lever for diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time noise optimization paradigm (Reno, D-flow) from single-sample quality improvement to batch-level diversity maximization, replacing the per-sample reward with a set-level diversity statistic (pairwise DINOv2 distance, DPP, or Vendi Score) while retaining the chi-distribution norm regularization. It departs from prior diversity methods that steer the model via modified guidance (particle guidance, condition-annealed sampling) or search over large candidate pools (Parmar et al.), instead directly backpropagating through the frozen sampler to optimize the initial noise. The frequency-domain analysis of noise evolution during optimization is novel, motivating the pink noise initialization that has no direct precedent in the diffusion sampling literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pink noise initialization is a simple, model-agnostic intervention that improves diversity across all tested models and baselines without any optimization, making it immediately adoptable in production pipelines. The finding that set-level objectives (DPP, Vendi) are preferred by human raters over pairwise metrics provides practical guidance for diversity optimization. Limitations include evaluation only on ODE-based distilled samplers (SDE-based sampling could further increase diversity), sensitivity of Flux.1 to hyperparameter choices, and the relatively small batch sizes tested (4 images). Open questions include whether the low-frequency dominance generalizes to video diffusion, whether more dynamic optimization schedules can push diversity higher without quality degradation, and whether the approach scales to very large diverse sets.
