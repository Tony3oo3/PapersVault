## Problem

Large-scale geospatial image datasets widely used for building footprint extraction, particularly the AICrowd Mapping Challenge dataset, contain severe data duplication and cross-split leakage that inflate reported model performance and undermine fair benchmarking. No efficient, dataset-agnostic pipeline existed to systematically detect and quantify these quality issues at the scale of hundreds of thousands of images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260008/cropped_page5_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB image patches at their native resolutions (300×300 for AICrowd, 250×250 for INRIA, 650×650 for SpaceNet) along with their official train/validation/test split assignments. Before hashing, each image is augmented with 90°, 180°, 270° rotations and horizontal/vertical flips to capture augmented duplicates. The output is a 64-bit perceptual hash per image, a quantitative report of exact and augmented duplicates within and across splits (with overlap percentages), and a pruned de-duplicated image set ready for clean training and evaluation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a DCT-based perceptual hashing pipeline: each image is downsampled, a 32×32 discrete cosine transform is computed, the top-left 8×8 low-frequency coefficients are retained, thresholded by their mean, and flattened into a 64-bit hash. Duplicate and leakage detection reduces to exact hash equality checks (Hamming distance threshold of 0), yielding an O(n) comparison per image pair that runs at ~4 ms per hash computation and ~4 ms per comparison on a single CPU core. To detect augmented duplicates, the pipeline augments every image in the reference split with all geometric transformations before hashing, then searches for collisions against the target split. The de-duplication procedure first removes within-split duplicates (retaining one arbitrary representative per collision group), then eliminates cross-split leakage by removing any training image whose hash matches a validation image. The authors validate the perceptual hashing results against a standard average hashing baseline, confirming comparable detection rates while showing perceptual hashing is less prone to false positives on visually similar but distinct images.

## Results

On the AICrowd Mapping Challenge dataset, the perceptual hashing pipeline reveals that 89.55% (251,403 of 280,741) of augmented training images are exact or augmented duplicates, and 93.45% (56,368 of 60,317) of validation images are also present in the training split. After de-duplication and leakage removal, the training split shrinks from 280,741 to 15,392 unique images and the validation split from 60,317 to 14,166. In contrast, the INRIA Aerial Image Labelling and SpaceNet 2: Building Detection v2 datasets show negligible duplication and leakage (overlap percentages below 1.5%), confirming they are suitable for benchmarking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares perceptual hashing (PHash) against average hashing (AHash) as the detection mechanism. Both methods detect a similar extent of duplication and leakage in the AICrowd dataset (e.g., PHash finds 56,368 leaked validation images vs. AHash's 56,431; PHash finds 166,193 training duplicates vs. AHash's 167,829), confirming the findings are robust to the hashing choice. However, AHash is more prone to false positives, incorrectly flagging visually similar but distinct images as duplicates, making PHash the more reliable choice for large-scale dataset auditing. Across the three datasets evaluated, the pipeline shows that INRIA and SpaceNet 2 are essentially clean (overlap < 1.5%), while AICrowd is severely contaminated, with the 93%+ leakage rates rendering previously reported benchmark scores on that dataset unreliable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes standard DCT-based perceptual hashing—a well-established image-processing technique—for the specific application of auditing geospatial image datasets for duplication and cross-split leakage. It departs from prior deduplication work (CE-Dedup, QHash, self-supervised descriptor approaches) by targeting only exact and augmented duplicates (90° rotations, flips) rather than near-duplicates, and by framing the problem as a pre-training quality gate rather than a training-time cost reduction. The paper positions itself as a practical diagnostic tool rather than a new algorithmic contribution, applying an off-the-shelf hash with Hamming distance 0 to three widely used building-footprint-extraction benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery that ~89% of AICrowd training images are duplicates and ~93% of validation images leak into training potentially invalidates quantitative metrics reported by a large body of recent building-footprint-extraction work that benchmarked on this dataset. The pipeline is lightweight (4 ms/image) and generalizes to any large-scale image dataset, making it a practical pre-use audit step for the community. Open questions include whether other popular geospatial or remote-sensing benchmarks harbor similar contamination, and whether hashing schemes can be made inherently invariant to arbitrary geometric and radiometric augmentations without requiring dataset-specific a priori knowledge of the augmentation set.
