## Problem

Existing frequency-domain methods for tiny object detection are largely confined to the visible modality and assume a simplistic low-/high-frequency dichotomy between infrared and RGB images. There is no systematic analysis of how frequency energy distributes across spectral bands for tiny objects in both modalities, and the challenge of capturing complementary cross-modal frequency cues while suppressing complex background noise in RGBT tiny object detection remains underexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of spatially aligned RGB and infrared images as input, processed through separate ResNet-50 backbones producing 3-level multi-scale feature maps. Output is standard object detection results: bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated under COCO-style AP/AR protocols with dataset-specific tiny-object size thresholds (e.g., <16x16 px on RGBT-Tiny, <20 px on RGBTDronePerson). The decoder is a deformable-attention Transformer head inherited from RT-DETR, so the overall pipeline is end-to-end trainable with a single detection loss plus an auxiliary focal Tversky loss for the background mask.

## Method

DyFCLT introduces two synergistic modules on top of a dual-branch RT-DETR backbone. The Dynamic Frequency-Band Decoupled Cross-Modal Attention (DFCA) applies FFT to RGB and IR features, partitions the spectrum into B=3 learnable radial sub-bands (low/mid/high) with boundaries parameterized as cumulative positive increments, and performs band-wise cross-modal attention where Q comes from RGB and K/V from IR, followed by per-band spatial modulation via 3x3 convolution and sigmoid gating. The Selective Smoothing Enhancement (SSE) module first predicts a binary foreground mask to split features, then smooths the background through channel-compression convolutions (IBS), and finally generates spatially adaptive dynamic filters from the noise-suppressed features to guide pixel-unshuffle/pixel-shuffle upsampling of higher-level features (FREF), restoring fine-grained target details before final CSPBlock fusion. The key insight is that tiny objects in both modalities concentrate energy in mid- and high-frequency bands, so fine-grained band-wise cross-modal interaction rather than a single global frequency split yields substantially richer complementary representations.

## Results

On RGBT-Tiny, DyFCLT achieves 48.2% AP and 69.1% AP50, surpassing the best unimodal detector DQ-DETR (43.6% AP) by 4.6 points and the best multimodal method M²D-LIF (38.7% AP) by 9.5 points. On RGBTDronePerson, it reaches 61.0% AP50 and 62.4% APt50, outperforming the previous best COXNet (45.5% AP50) by 15.5 points. On the general-scale FLIR benchmark, DyFCLT attains 84.1% AP50 and 45.0% AP, exceeding the prior best multimodal method FD²Net (82.9% AP50) and the strongest unimodal DINO-IR (80.6% AP50, 44.8% AP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyFCLT outperforms all 40 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On RGBT-Tiny, it beats the strongest unimodal detector DQ-DETR (43.6% AP) by 4.6 AP points and the best prior multimodal method M²D-LIF (38.7% AP) by 9.5 AP points, with particularly large gains on tiny objects (41.3% vs. 33.0% APst for DQ-DETR). On RGBTDronePerson, the 15.5-point AP50 margin over COXNet (61.0% vs. 45.5%) is the largest gap among all compared methods, and the 62.4% APt50 on tiny persons far exceeds the runner-up IM-CMDet (45.2%). On FLIR, DyFCLT surpasses the best unimodal DINO-IR (80.6% AP50, 44.8% AP) and the best multimodal FD²Net (82.9% AP50) by 3.5 and 1.2 AP50 points respectively, confirming generalization beyond tiny-object-specific settings. Notably, DyFCLT uses only 85.5M parameters, well below RSDet (386M) and CALNet (153.6M), while achieving substantially higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyFCLT builds on RT-DETR as its detection backbone and Deformable DETR for the decoder, but its core novelty lies in introducing a frequency-domain perspective to cross-modal RGBT tiny object detection. Prior frequency-domain methods for tiny objects (HS-FPN, SET) operated exclusively in the visible modality, and existing RGBT fusion methods (QFDet, COXNet, IM-CMDet, RSDet, M2D-LIF) largely assumed a low-frequency IR / high-frequency RGB dichotomy. This paper departs from that assumption by empirically showing that tiny objects in both modalities carry substantial mid- and high-frequency energy, and by designing a dynamic, learnable frequency-band decomposition (DFCA) that performs band-wise cross-modal attention rather than a single global fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency characteristic analysis (Fig. 1a) provides a useful empirical correction to the field's working assumption about IR spectral content, and the 9.5% AP gain over the strongest prior multimodal method on RGBT-Tiny is a substantial margin that suggests frequency-aware cross-modal interaction is an underexploited axis. The method's consistent gains across three datasets of varying scale (tiny-object UAV, tiny-person drone, and general-scale FLIR) indicate reasonable generalization. Open questions include the computational cost of repeated FFT/IFFT operations at multiple scales, whether the optimal number of bands (B=3) is dataset- or scale-dependent, and whether the dynamic band-boundary mechanism transfers to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB-D, RGB-LiDAR).
