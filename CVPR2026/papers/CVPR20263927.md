## Problem

Existing attention intervention methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation (e.g., PAI, HGAI) amplify attention to image tokens during inference, which effectively reduces hallucination rates but simultaneously suppresses attention to previously generated text, causing the model to 'forget' its own output and produce repetitive, monotonous descriptions. This creates an undesirable trade-off between hallucination reduction and linguistic coherence that prior methods have not resolved.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free, inference-time intervention on any LVLM that follows the standard encoder-connector-LLM architecture. It takes an RGB image (projected to m visual tokens via a visual encoder) and a text instruction prompt as input, and produces a free-form image caption (up to 512 tokens) as output. The intervention modifies self-attention weights within the LLM's intermediate layers (5–18) during autoregressive token generation, requiring no additional model parameters or training data.

## Method

The key insight is that real object tokens exhibit significantly higher attention to previously generated text (T_p) than hallucinated object tokens (by a factor of ×1.5–×2.5), suggesting T_p encodes instruction-relevant visual information in the LLM's native feature space. IAT exploits this by amplifying attention to T_p tokens (rather than image tokens) in intermediate layers using a fixed factor α. AdaIAT refines this with two adaptive mechanisms: (1) a layer-wise threshold T^(l) = Ā_Tp^h + β(Ā_Tp^r − Ā_Tp^h) that triggers intervention only when attention to T_p drops below the threshold, avoiding disruption during normal predictions; and (2) a per-head amplification magnitude M^(l,h) = A_Tp^r / A_Tp^h that assigns stronger amplification to heads showing greater real-vs-hallucinated attention disparity. The amplification is applied post-softmax with renormalization to preserve the probability distribution, and the entire procedure is training-free and model-agnostic.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, AdaIAT reduces hallucination rates C_S and C_I by 35.8% and 37.1% respectively compared to Greedy decoding, while maintaining F1 at 79.4 (vs. 77.9 for Greedy) and D_1 at 0.60 (matching Greedy's 0.60). On the more advanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B, AdaIAT achieves the lowest C_S (28.4) and C_I (6.9) among all methods while attaining the highest F1 (76.8) and D_1 (0.66). On Janus-Pro-7B, AdaIAT similarly achieves the best C_S (19.0), C_I (4.9), and F1 (76.5) with D_1 of 0.64, confirming generalizability across architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to PAI, AdaIAT achieves comparable or lower C_S across all four models (e.g., 25.2 vs. 34.2 on LLaVA-1.5-13B; 28.4 vs. 32.0 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) while substantially outperforming on D_1 (0.60 vs. 0.50 on LLaVA-7B; 0.66 vs. 0.64 on Qwen2.5-VL), indicating PAI's aggressive image-attention amplification causes repetitive descriptions. On F1, AdaIAT surpasses PAI by 1.7 points on LLaVA-7B and 2.1 on Qwen2.5-VL, reflecting better prediction richness. Against HGAI, AdaIAT wins on C_S for LLaVA-1.5-13B (25.2 vs. 26.4), Janus-Pro (19.0 vs. 21.0), and Qwen2.5-VL (28.4 vs. 30.4), and on F1 for LLaVA-7B (79.4 vs. 78.3) and LLaVA-13B (79.2 vs. 76.9). HGAI retains a slight edge on C_I for LLaVA-7B (6.9 vs. 8.3), but AdaIAT compensates with higher D_1 (0.60 vs. 0.50) and F1. Overall, AdaIAT consistently achieves the best or near-best C_S, C_I, and F1 while uniquely preserving D_1 at Greedy-level, whereas both PAI and HGAI sacrifice 10–15% textual diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-intervention line of work for LVLM hallucination mitigation, building directly on PAI (Liu et al., ECCV 2024) and HGAI (Jiang et al., CVPR 2025), which amplify attention to image tokens during inference. The key conceptual departure is redirecting amplification from image tokens (V) to the model's own generated text tokens (T_p), motivated by the observation that T_p already encodes instruction-relevant visual information in the LLM's native feature space, thereby avoiding the domain gap between visual and linguistic representations. The adaptive component (AdaIAT) further refines this by introducing layer-wise activation thresholds and per-head amplification magnitudes derived from the ratio of real-to-hallucinated attention patterns, moving beyond the uniform, always-on intervention used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical trade-off that limits adoption of existing attention-intervention methods: reducing hallucinations at the cost of repetitive, low-diversity captions. By showing that the model's own prior output can serve as a grounding signal, AdaIAT offers a training-free, plug-and-play solution that preserves linguistic quality while matching or exceeding prior methods on hallucination metrics across multiple LVLMs. Limitations include reliance on COCO-derived thresholds and amplification matrices that are held fixed across datasets, evaluation on relatively small image sets (500–2000), and the method's applicability to non-captioning tasks (e.g., VQA, grounding) remaining unexplored. An open question is whether the T_p-based grounding signal degrades for very long generations where early tokens may themselves contain hallucinated content.
