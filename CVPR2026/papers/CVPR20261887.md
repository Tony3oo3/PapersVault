## Problem

3D pulmonary vessel segmentation from CT is hindered by complex tree-like topology, large scale variation, low-contrast small vessels, and highly similar background tissues. Existing Mamba-based segmentation models either lack sufficient spatial perception to track vessel structure or incur prohibitive computational costs from multi-directional scanning, leaving a gap between long-range dependency modeling and clinical deployability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261887/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D CT volume (512×512×228 to 512×512×657 depending on dataset) and produces a 3D binary vessel segmentation mask via a four-stage deep-supervision decoder. On the in-house Lung79 dataset the output is a multi-class mask distinguishing airway, artery, and vein, while on the public Parse22 dataset it segments pulmonary arteries at multiple branching levels. The stem layer reduces the input to 32 channels before a five-layer encoder progressively halves spatial resolution.

## Method

VesMamba builds on the UMamba encoder-decoder skeleton and introduces three key modules. The Spatial-gated Structural Perception (SSP) module replaces the bottleneck with a Spatial Gate Mamba (SGM) that stacks a bidirectional SSM branch, a local convolution branch, and a Dynamic Spatial Attention Convolution (DSAC) layer; DSAC applies three direction-separated 1D convolutions (1×1×3, 1×3×1, 3×1×1), computes softmax directional weights via global average pooling, and fuses the anisotropic features, then a sigmoid gate multiplies this spatially-aware signal onto the SSM output to endow Mamba with 3D structural perception. The Bidirectional Scale-aware Filter (BSF) module performs high-to-low then low-to-high feature fusion using ShiftConv and concatenation (rather than addition) to avoid cross-scale interference, then applies a sigmoid gating mechanism to filter noise from each encoder layer's features at its native scale. Finally, the Mask-constrained Decoder (MCDecoder) feeds high-layer masks directly into adjacent low-layer decoders, constraining their predictions with contour and position information to improve segmentation consistency across scales.

## Results

On the public Parse22 dataset, VesMamba achieves a Dice of 86.65%, clDice of 87.46%, HD95 of 2.98, and NSD of 93.21, outperforming the closest competitor LKM-UNet (Dice 86.21%, clDice 87.14%) and the base UMamba (Dice 85.94%, clDice 86.64%) by 0.71% and 0.82% in Dice and clDice respectively. On the more challenging in-house Lung79 dataset (segmenting airways, arteries, and veins simultaneously), VesMamba achieves Dice scores of 84.85% (airway), 84.96% (artery), and 85.76% (vein), with the best HD95 of 3.10, 7.01, and 5.71 respectively across the three categories. The method also achieves the best NSD scores (94.08, 89.03, 88.86) on Lung79, indicating superior boundary fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VesMamba outperforms all nine baselines across all four metrics on both Parse22 and Lung79. Among Mamba-based methods, it surpasses UMamba (its architectural base) by +0.71% Dice and +0.82% clDice on Parse22, and beats SegMamba by +1.42% Dice and +3.25% clDice, indicating that the SSP and BSF modules add substantial value over standard SSM scanning. LKM-UNet is the closest competitor (Dice 86.21 vs. 86.65 on Parse22), but VesMamba also requires roughly a quarter of its computational cost per the efficiency comparison in Figure 8. CNN-based baselines (3D-UNet, nnUNet) are outperformed by 1.4–2.5% Dice, while Transformer-based methods (Swin-UNETR, SegFormer3D) lag significantly by 6–9% Dice, confirming the advantage of SSM-based long-range modeling for vessel topology. DSCNet, which uses adaptive convolutions for tubular structures, performs worst among all baselines (Dice 76.63 on Parse22), suggesting that explicit topological constraints are less effective than learned structural perception for complex multi-scale vessels.

## Relation to Prior Work

VesMamba builds directly on the UMamba framework, extending it with a spatial-gated structural perception (SSP) module that addresses a key limitation of standard Mamba in 3D medical imaging: the loss of spatial locality when converting volumetric data to sequences. Rather than adopting the multi-directional scanning strategies used by SegMamba or LKM-UNet (which increase computational overhead), the paper introduces dynamic spatial attention convolution (DSAC) to endow Mamba with anisotropic spatial perception at lower cost. The bidirectional scale-aware filter (BSF) departs from conventional FPN/PAN feature fusion by repurposing bidirectionally fused features as a noise filter on encoder representations rather than as decoder input, and the mask-constrained decoder introduces a novel cross-layer constraint mechanism not seen in prior U-Net-style architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically important task—automated 3D pulmonary vessel segmentation—where accuracy on small, low-contrast vessels and computational efficiency in clinical deployment are both critical. The demonstrated ~4× efficiency advantage over LKM-UNet at comparable accuracy is practically meaningful for clinical settings with limited GPU resources. However, the paper acknowledges limitations with excessive vascular branching and abnormal vessel shapes, and the modest gains over UMamba (0.71% Dice on Parse22) suggest that the core Mamba architecture already captures much of the long-range dependency signal; the remaining challenge of handling pathological or highly irregular vessel geometries remains open.
