## Problem

Existing pose-agnostic anomaly detection (PAD) methods rely on densely sampled multi-view inputs, which are costly and impractical in industrial settings. Under sparse-view conditions, two compounding failures emerge: 3D reconstruction overfits and loses geometric detail, and limited visual cues degrade pose estimation, which in turn corrupts anomaly localization. No prior method addresses both challenges simultaneously in a principled, frequency-aware manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262481/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of sparse multi-view normal RGB images (10% or 20% of available views, at 400×400 resolution) to train a 3DGS model, then receives a single query image at an unknown pose. It produces a pixel-level anomaly map, an image-level anomaly score, and a refined camera pose estimate. The query image is first matched to reference views in the wavelet domain for pose initialization, then rendered from the 3DGS model at the estimated pose for frequency-domain comparison.

## Method

Wave-Pose3D integrates three wavelet-driven modules. (1) SWGM trains the 3DGS with a structure-aware region filter that computes per-Gaussian retention scores from coordinate variance (structural complexity) and camera-center distance (depth consistency), applying a cosine-annealed stochastic dropout to unreliable points, while a Haar wavelet high-frequency loss (L1 on LH/HL/HH coefficients) preserves fine geometric detail. (2) WPE performs two-stage pose estimation: WMV decomposes query and reference images into wavelet sub-bands and runs EfficientLoFTR matching independently on low- and high-frequency components (weighted η=0.8 toward HF) to select the best initial pose, then WPO refines the pose on the SE(3) manifold by minimizing separate L2 (low-freq) and L1 (high-freq) wavelet reconstruction losses over 300 iterations. (3) WDAD extracts multi-scale features via EfficientNet-B4 from both the rendered and query images, applies wavelet transforms, and computes anomaly scores as a weighted L2 difference between low-frequency (α=1) and high-frequency (β=3) components, making detection robust to residual pose misalignment while remaining sensitive to subtle texture defects.

## Results

Under 20% sparse-view settings, Wave-Pose3D achieves 97.8% pixel-level AUROC and 82.3% image-level AUROC on PIAD_synt, outperforming the strongest baseline DropGaussian (97.3% / 77.8%) by 0.5 and 4.5 points respectively. On MAD_sim (20%), it reaches 95.1% / 69.1%, surpassing DropGaussian (93.6% / 67.5%). On the real-world PIAD_real (20%) benchmark, Wave-Pose3D attains 98.8% pixel-level ROCAUC and 87.6% image-level ROCAUC, exceeding DropGaussian (98.4% / 83.8%) and PIAD (98.6% / 81.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wave-Pose3D consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and sparse-view configuration. The largest margins appear on image-level AUROC: on PIAD_synt (20%), it beats OmniPoseAD by 12.6 points (82.3 vs. 69.7), SplatPose by 11.3 points (82.3 vs. 71.0), and PIAD by 6.9 points (82.3 vs. 75.4). Against the closest competitor DropGaussian (a sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction method adapted for anomaly detection), Wave-Pose3D gains 4.5 points in image-level AUROC on PIAD_synt (20%) and 3.8 points on PIAD_real (20%), while maintaining a smaller but consistent 0.5-point lead in pixel-level AUROC. On the real-world PIAD_real benchmark, Wave-Pose3D also surpasses PIAD by 5.8 points in image-level ROCAUC (87.6 vs. 81.8), confirming that the wavelet-domain formulation generalizes beyond synthetic settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pose-agnostic anomaly detection (PAD) lineage—initiated by OmniPoseAD (NeRF-based) and advanced by SplatPose and PIAD (3DGS-based)—by explicitly targeting the sparse-view regime where all prior PAD methods assume dense multi-view inputs. It draws on sparse-view 3DGS reconstruction techniques (DropGaussian, DropoutGS) for the representation stage but departs from them by introducing wavelet transforms as a unifying signal-processing principle that simultaneously governs Gaussian regularization, pose estimation, and anomaly scoring. The conceptual departure is treating frequency decomposition not as a single-stage trick but as a cross-cutting design philosophy across the entire PAD pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sparse-view PAD addresses a genuine industrial bottleneck: capturing dense multi-view imagery is costly and often impractical on production lines, so a method that degrades gracefully to 10–20% of views has direct deployment value. The consistent ~10-point I_AUROC gain over the spatial-domain baseline and steady margins over DropGaussian across both synthetic and one real dataset validate the frequency-domain approach. Open questions remain: whether the wavelet strategy benefits from even sparser views (≤5%), whether it transfers to non-3DGS representations or more complex industrial geometries, and whether the Haar wavelet choice is optimal versus learned multi-scale features. The evaluation is also limited to two object categories on the real dataset, leaving generalization to diverse industrial parts untested.
