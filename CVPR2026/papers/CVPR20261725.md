## Problem

In federated learning, clients often hold data spanning multiple distributional domains (intra-client domain skew), a setting largely unaddressed by existing non-IID or inter-client domain-skew methods. Applying a uniform pruning mask under such heterogeneity causes structural misalignment—discarding channels critical to specific domains—while domain-sensitive pruning alone leaves structural ambiguity where similar channel patterns encode semantically divergent domains, degrading generalization.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images drawn from multiple domains simultaneously (e.g., a single client may hold samples from both MNIST and SVHN), with domain labels available locally for per-domain importance estimation. The method outputs a class label prediction and, crucially, a personalized sparse model: a globally shared pruned backbone (ResNet-18 with a fraction of channels retained) plus a small set of client-specific reactivated channels that remain private and are never uploaded to the server.

## Method

FEDFIP operates in three coordinated stages. First, Domain-Sensitive Fisher Pruning (DSFP) estimates per-domain channel importance via diagonal Fisher information (squared task-loss gradients), uploads a domain-weighted importance vector to the server, which constructs a global pruning mask by retaining the top-ρ channels; each client then selectively reactivates a few globally pruned channels that exceed a local importance threshold, yielding a personalized sparse sub-network. Second, Domain-Sensitive Regularization (DSR) has the server aggregate normalized per-domain importance vectors into EMA-smoothed domain prototypes, which are broadcast back; clients minimize a structure-contrastive loss (softmax over cosine similarities) that pulls each local domain's structural vector toward its prototype while pushing it away from other domains' prototypes. Third, Structure-Aware Aggregation fuses only the parameters corresponding to globally shared channels across clients, leaving reactivated channels entirely local so that personalization does not interfere with global model consistency.

## Results

On the Digits benchmark (MNIST, USPS, SVHN, SYN), FEDFIP achieves 76.48% average top-1 accuracy with 22.06% standard deviation, outperforming the strongest baseline FDSE (76.19%) by 0.29 points. On Office-Caltech, FEDFIP reaches 64.44% average accuracy (STD 5.99), exceeding FDSE (63.28%) by 1.16 points and the vanilla FEDAVG baseline (55.39%) by 9.05 points. On PACS, FEDFIP attains 82.02% average accuracy (STD 8.36), the best among all methods, while also achieving the lowest cross-domain variance on both Office-Caltech and PACS, indicating more stable training under domain skew.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDFIP outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset. Against the standard FL baseline FEDAVG [28], FEDFIP gains +2.13 points on Digits, +9.05 on Office-Caltech, and +4.13 on PACS, while also reducing cross-domain STD by up to 5.47 points (Office-Caltech). Among heterogeneity-focused methods (FEDPROX [23], MOON [21], FEDFA [47]), FEDFIP leads by 1.67–2.37 points on Digits and 8.04–9.53 points on Office-Caltech, confirming that generic non-IID strategies are insufficient under multi-domain skew. Against domain-skew-specific baselines, FEDFIP beats DAPPERFL [16] by +0.61 (Digits), +3.91 (Office-Caltech), and +1.44 (PACS), and surpasses FEDHEAL [5] by +0.26, +1.32, and +1.37 respectively. The closest competitor is FDSE [39], which FEDFIP edges out by +0.29 (Digits), +1.16 (Office-Caltech), and +0.65 (PACS), while also achieving the lowest STD on Office-Caltech (5.99 vs. 6.00) and PACS (8.36 vs. 8.47). Notably, FEDFIP achieves these gains with a pruned sparse backbone (e.g., 6.89M params at ρ=0.6 vs. ~11.2M for dense ResNet-18), reducing both communication and computation costs relative to unpruned baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDFIP extends the standard FedAvg paradigm by integrating domain-sensitive Fisher-information-based channel pruning into the federated training loop, building on the pruning-for-FL line of work (DAPPERFL) but departing from it by making pruning decisions domain-aware rather than client-uniform. It also extends the domain-generalization-in-FL literature (FEDSR, FEDHEAL, FEDLSA, FDSE), which predominantly addresses inter-client domain skew, by explicitly modeling intra-client domain heterogeneity where a single client holds data from multiple domains. The structure-contrastive loss draws on contrastive learning in FL (MOON, FEDLSA) but operates at the channel-importance structural level rather than the feature-embedding level, and the structure-aware aggregation generalizes FedAvg to handle heterogeneous sparse architectures across clients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for reducing model size and communication cost in domain-skewed FL while maintaining or improving accuracy, which is relevant for edge deployment where clients face diverse data distributions. However, the performance gains over the strongest baselines (FDSE, FEDHEAL) are modest (0.26–1.16 points), and the multi-module design (DSFP + DSR + SA) introduces several hyperparameters (ρ, φ, μ, λ) that complicate tuning. Open questions include whether the Fisher-based channel importance signal remains informative for non-CNN backbones (transformers, ViTs), how the method scales when the number of domains per client grows large, and whether the global pruning mask needs to be re-estimated periodically as domain distributions drift over time.
