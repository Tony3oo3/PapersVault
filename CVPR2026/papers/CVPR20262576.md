## Problem

MLLM inference is bottlenecked by hundreds or thousands of visual tokens, yet existing compression methods rely on attention scores (suffering from position bias and FlashAttention incompatibility), similarity heuristics (ignoring semantic importance), or text-conditioned attention proxies rather than directly optimizing for the model's output correctness. No prior approach provides token-level supervision that is jointly aligned with task loss and semantic non-redundancy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262576/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The compressor receives aligned visual token embeddings (e.g., 576 tokens from LLaVA-1.5 or 2880 from LLaVA-NeXT) and text token embeddings (system prompt + user query, excluding the gold response) as a concatenated sequence. It outputs a scalar retention probability per visual token via a single-layer bidirectional transformer followed by a linear classifier. The top-r tokens by probability are retained, and the actual pruning can be applied at any LLM layer (l=0 for maximum speedup or l=2 for better accuracy), making it plug-and-play without modifying the visual encoder, alignment module, or LLM.

## Method

EvoComp constructs supervision via an evolutionary algorithm that searches for binary masks over visual tokens minimizing the MLLM's task-specific loss, with a semantic grouping constraint that partitions tokens by their nearest LLM vocabulary embedding and enforces one retained token per group. The evolutionary search uses crossover (probability 0.9, splitting sub-masks at the midpoint) and mutation (probability 0.2, shifting the retained token by one position within a group) over a population of 48 candidates with 12 parents across 10 iterations. The compressor is trained with a combined loss: GHM loss to dynamically reweight gradients based on token difficulty (addressing the extreme positive/negative class imbalance where only a few tokens are retained), plus a cosine similarity loss that penalizes representational overlap between retained and pruned tokens within each semantic group. This design directly ties token selection to output quality while the grouping constraint eliminates redundancy without requiring extra hyperparameters.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B across six vision-language benchmarks (GQA, MMB, MMB-CN, POPE, VQA^V2, VizWiz), EvoComp retains 99.3% of baseline accuracy at 3× compression (192/576 tokens) and 94.9% at 9× compression (64/576 tokens). Under extreme 18× compression on LLaVA-NeXT-7B (160/2880 tokens), EvoComp still preserves 92.1% of baseline accuracy. On a smartphone NPU, EvoComp delivers 1.6×–2.0× total-latency speedup, and on an A100 GPU it achieves 4.1× prefill and 2.6× overall inference acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoComp consistently outperforms all nine baselines on average across the three compression ratios on LLaVA-1.5-7B. At 3× compression (192 tokens), EvoComp(l=2) achieves 99.3% average accuracy, edging out the strongest baseline VisionZip (98.8%) and DART (98.3%); at 9× compression (64 tokens), EvoComp(l=0) reaches 94.9%, surpassing VisionZip (94.2%) and DART (92.8%) by 0.7 and 2.1 percentage points respectively. The gap widens under extreme compression: on LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 18× reduction (160/2880 tokens), EvoComp(l=0) scores 92.1% versus DART's 89.6% and SparseVLM's 89.7%. Attention-based methods (FastV, HiRED) and similarity-based methods (PruMerge) degrade most sharply at high compression ratios, with PruMerge dropping to 86.0% at 64 tokens. Notably, EvoComp(l=0), which prunes before the LLM, matches or exceeds EvoComp(l=2) and all mid-layer pruning baselines, confirming that decoupling compression from LLM internals does not sacrifice accuracy while enabling plug-and-play deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoComp departs from the dominant paradigm in visual token compression, where prior methods rely on proxy signals—attention scores (FastV, HiRED, GlobalCom²), embedding similarity (ToMe, DART, PruMerge), or text-conditioned attention (SparseVLM, PyramidDrop, MustDrop)—to heuristically rank or filter tokens. Instead, it generates supervision by running an evolutionary search that directly minimizes the MLLM's task-specific output loss over binary retention masks, with a semantic grouping constraint (using LLM vocabulary embeddings as clustering anchors) to enforce diversity. The training loss repurposes the GHM gradient-density reweighting from object detection [21] to handle the extreme positive/negative token imbalance, and borrows a cosine similarity regularization from the authors' prior long-context compression work [61] to sharpen the decision boundary between retained and pruned tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge and mobile deployment: EvoComp achieves 1.6–2.0× total-latency speedup on a smartphone NPU and 4.1× prefill speedup on GPU while retaining 99.3% accuracy at 3× compression, and it requires no modification to the underlying MLLM (plug-and-play). The transferability results—token selections learned on LLaVA-1.5-7B generalizing to LLaVA-1.5-13B and even to Qwen2.5-VL-7B without retraining—suggest that learned token importance captures model-agnostic visual semantics, opening the door to a single lightweight compressor serving multiple MLLM backends. Open questions include the wall-clock cost of evolutionary label generation at scale (48 candidates × 10 iterations per sample), whether the approach extends cleanly to video or multi-image settings with thousands of tokens, and whether the single-layer compressor architecture is sufficient for more aggressive compression ratios beyond ~18×.
