## Problem

Current medical Vision-Language Models are optimized for broad knowledge coverage but lack the inductive biases needed for real clinical workflows: they cannot reliably detect lesions in unseen modalities, disentangle genuine disease progression from acquisition noise in paired scans, or provide spatially grounded visual evidence for their predictions. This paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously through a unified stage-wise framework that injects explicit anomaly-aware and temporal-difference representations into a medical VLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262231/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For anomaly detection, the model takes a single medical image (Brain MRI, Head CT, Chest X-ray, or retinal OCT) plus a fixed instruction prompt and outputs a binary normal/abnormal judgment. For symptom tracking, it consumes a temporally ordered pair of images from the same patient along with a multiple-choice question and outputs a progression label (worsened, improved, or unchanged). For explainability, it produces a pixel-aligned heatmap via a ConvNeXt segmentation head that highlights the lesion regions driving the textual prediction. The multimodal sequence follows a modified chat template where projected visual features, <Ano> tokens, text embeddings, and <Diff> tokens are concatenated in a fixed order before LLM decoding.

## Method

MEDIC-AD extends the Lingshu-7B backbone through three progressive training stages. Stage 1 introduces an Anomaly Processor that generates <Ano> tokens: two learnable system tokens (Abnormal and Normal) perform cross-attention over multi-scale visual features from four intermediate vision-encoder layers, using Sigmoid (rather than Softmax) to yield patch-wise anomaly probabilities; the resulting anomaly attention map modulates visual features via element-wise multiplication, and 2D-pooled queries pass through an Anomaly Q-Former and MLP to produce compact anomaly tokens. Stage 2 adds a Diff Q-Former that contrasts the anomaly-modulated features from two paired images to produce <Diff> tokens encoding localized lesion changes while suppressing global appearance shifts. Stage 3 fuses <Ano> tokens with intermediate visual feature maps through a fusion block and a ConvNeXt-based segmentation head to generate spatially aligned heatmaps. Visual Soft Prompt Tuning is applied to the four selected vision-encoder layers to preserve pretrained representational stability while adapting them for anomaly sensitivity.

## Results

On zero-shot medical anomaly detection across four modalities (Brain MRI, Head CT, Br35h, COVID-19 X-ray), MEDIC-AD achieves an average F1 of 91.6, surpassing the strongest medical baseline Lingshu (88.7) and all closed-source models (GPT-4o: 62.4, Claude-3.5: 69.2). On the MMXU symptom-tracking benchmark, MEDIC-AD reaches 0.655 overall accuracy, outperforming Lingshu (0.620) and GPT-4o (0.571). For visual grounding, MEDIC-AD achieves mIoU of 87.6 on BraTS2021 and 79.8 on ChestX-Det, dramatically exceeding Citrus-V (32.6 and 12.4 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEDIC-AD outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. In zero-shot anomaly detection, it leads Lingshu (its own backbone) by 2.9 F1 points (91.6 vs. 88.7) and Citrus-V by 7.4 points (91.6 vs. 84.2), while closed-source GPT-4o and Claude-3.5 trail by 29.2 and 22.4 points respectively, partly due to safety-alignment refusals on medical images. On MMXU temporal reasoning, MEDIC-AD (0.655) beats Lingshu (0.620) by 3.5 points and GPT-4o/Citrus-V (both 0.571) by 8.4 points, with the largest gap on the 'Worsen' class (0.663 vs. 0.597 for Lingshu). For visual grounding, MEDIC-AD's mIoU gains over Citrus-V are especially dramatic: 87.6 vs. 32.6 on BraTS2021 (+55.0) and 79.8 vs. 12.4 on ChestX-Det (+67.4), attributed to the <Ano>-token-guided ConvNeXt segmentation head replacing Citrus-V's SAM2 decoder. General-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5) and anomaly-specialized models (AnomalyGPT, Anomaly-OV) are consistently outperformed by 10–40 F1 points, confirming that domain-specific anomaly token design is critical for medical imaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEDIC-AD builds directly on the Lingshu medical foundation VLM as its backbone, adapting it with Visual Soft Prompt Tuning to preserve pretrained representational stability while injecting anomaly-specific inductive biases. The Anomaly Q-Former and Diff Q-Former modules repurpose the BLIP-2 Q-Former architecture, but redirect it from generic cross-modal alignment toward lesion-specific and temporal-difference token generation. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior medical VLMs (Citrus-V, Lingshu) that optimize for broad VQA and report generation by encoding the clinical diagnostic workflow—detect, compare, explain—as a staged training curriculum, and from prior anomaly-detection VLMs (AnomalyGPT, Anomaly-OV) by embedding anomaly reasoning within a full medical language model rather than treating it as a standalone detection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: medical VLMs with strong general knowledge still fail at the specific perceptual tasks (lesion localization, temporal change tracking, visual justification) that clinicians actually need for decision support. The stage-wise design and real-world hospital validation (300 patients, radiologist-annotated) provide evidence of deployment readiness that most medical VLM papers lack. Open questions include whether the 2D token-based approach extends to 3D volumetric data (CT, MRI volumes), whether the anomaly/diff token mechanism generalizes beyond the tested modalities, and whether the large visual-grounding gains over Citrus-V persist when compared against stronger segmentation-specific baselines rather than a single grounding-oriented VLM.
