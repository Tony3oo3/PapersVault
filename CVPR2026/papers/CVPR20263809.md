## Problem

Existing one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR methods fix the student timestep (e.g., 999) while randomly sampling the teacher timestep, which prevents full exploitation of the distinct generative priors that Stable Diffusion exhibits at different timesteps and yields inconsistent gradient guidance. This leads to suboptimal reconstruction quality and an inability to naturally control the fidelity–realism trade-off.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263809/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (128×128 for real-world benchmarks, or synthesized from 512×512 HQ patches) and a scalar timestep condition t_s ∈ [0, 999] as input, along with a text prompt extracted by the DAPE module. It produces a single high-quality super-resolved image in one forward pass through the student network. The timestep t_s acts as a continuous control knob: smaller values favor fidelity (higher PSNR), while larger values emphasize semantic realism (higher QALIGN/CLIPIQA).

## Method

TADSR introduces two key components to distill time-dependent generative priors from a pre-trained SD 2.1-base model into a one-step student. First, a Time-Aware VAE Encoder (TAE) injects a temporal embedding into the VAE encoder so that the same LQ image is mapped to different latent representations conditioned on t_s, mirroring the noise-level variation in the original diffusion process. Second, a Time-Aware Variational Score Distillation (TAVSD) loss links the student timestep t_s to the teacher timestep t_v via a linear mapping t_v = λt_s + γ, ensuring the teacher provides consistent, timestep-aligned generative guidance rather than random, conflicting signals. The student (TAE + LoRA-finetuned UNet) is trained with a blurred-MSE + LPIPS reconstruction loss plus the TAVSD loss, while a separate LoRA model is trained with standard diffusion loss on student-generated samples to estimate the fake score. This design lets a single inference step naturally interpolate between fidelity-preserving and semantically rich reconstructions simply by varying t_s.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val, TADSR achieves the best no-reference scores among all methods: CLIPIQA 0.7353, MANIQA 0.6443, TOPIQ 0.7044, and QALIGN 4.0783, while maintaining a competitive PSNR of 23.815 dB. On DRealSR, TADSR achieves the best PSNR (28.387 dB) alongside top no-reference scores (CLIPIQA 0.7398, TOPIQ 0.6758, QALIGN 3.7491). On RealSR and RealLR200, TADSR consistently leads all no-reference metrics (e.g., QALIGN 3.9477 and 4.2622 respectively), confirming its advantage in perceptual quality. TADSR is the only one-step method that outperforms all multi-step diffusion baselines on every no-reference metric across all four datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADSR achieves the best no-reference scores (CLIPIQA, MANIQA, TOPIQ, QALIGN) across all four datasets, outperforming every baseline including multi-step methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR) and one-step methods (SinSR, S3Diff, OSEDiff, PisaSR, TSDSR, AdcSR). Against the closest one-step competitor PisaSR, TADSR gains +0.04 in CLIPIQA and +0.07 in TOPIQ on DIV2K-Val, while PisaSR retains a slight PSNR edge (23.867 vs. 23.815). Compared to OSEDiff (same parameter count), TADSR improves CLIPIQA by +0.067 and QALIGN by +0.24 on DIV2K-Val while maintaining comparable PSNR. On DRealSR, TADSR uniquely achieves the best PSNR (28.387 dB) among all methods, surpassing SinSR (28.345) and PisaSR (28.318), while also leading all no-reference metrics. The main trade-off is that TADSR's LPIPS scores are slightly higher (worse) than S3Diff and PisaSR on DIV2K-Val and RealSR, reflecting a minor fidelity cost for substantially better perceptual quality. TSDSR shows the weakest overall performance (PSNR 22.17 on DIV2K-Val), while SinSR leads on PSNR but trails significantly on all no-reference metrics (e.g., QALIGN 3.5159 vs. TADSR's 4.0783 on DIV2K-Val).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the one-step diffusion distillation paradigm for Real-ISR established by OSEDiff, which first applied Variational Score Distillation (from ProlificDreamer) to distill a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model into a single-step super-resolution network. The key departure from prior one-step methods (OSEDiff, S3Diff, PisaSR, AdcSR, TSDSR) is the recognition that fixing the student timestep while randomly sampling the teacher timestep creates inconsistent generative guidance, since SD activates different priors at different noise levels. TADSR addresses this with two coupled innovations: a Time-Aware VAE Encoder that makes the latent representation vary with the timestep (mirroring the noise-level dynamics of the original diffusion process), and a Time-Aware VSD loss that maps the student timestep to the teacher timestep to ensure consistent gradient direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TADSR provides a natural, single-parameter control knob (the timestep t_s) for the fidelity-realism trade-off in one-step Real-ISR, which is more intuitive and better-behaved than the dual-weight tuning in PisaSR. The time-aware distillation principle—aligning the noise-level conditioning between student and teacher—could generalize to other diffusion-based restoration or generation tasks where multi-timestep priors are distilled into few-step models. Open questions include whether the TAE's timestep-conditioned encoding is necessary or whether simpler mechanisms (e.g., learned noise schedules) could achieve similar alignment, and whether the approach extends beyond SD 2.1-base to newer architectures like SDXL or DiT-based models.
