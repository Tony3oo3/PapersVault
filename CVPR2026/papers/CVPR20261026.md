## Problem

Current video generation models are trained predominantly on RGB video alone, analogous to training language models only on plain text, which limits their holistic understanding of the physical world. While individual auxiliary modalities (depth, flow, skeletons) have been explored in isolation, no prior work unifies multiple visual sub-modalities and multiple training paradigms (generation, estimation, controllable generation) within a single architecture, leaving open whether such joint training can accelerate convergence, enable emergent cross-modal reasoning, and improve zero-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes text prompts, RGB video latents, and auxiliary modality latents (depth, optical flow, segmentation masks, human skeletons, or DensePose) that are concatenated along the width dimension and interact via self-attention with 3D RoPE for cross-modal spatiotemporal disambiguation. At inference, the user selects which modality to condition on or predict via a plug-and-play modality switcher, and the in-context learner uses short modality-type prompts (e.g., "depth map") rather than content captions to guide the auxiliary branch. The 10B-parameter DiT backbone produces either RGB video, a single auxiliary modality, or both simultaneously, with 50 DDIM sampling steps and CFG scale 7.5.

## Method

UnityVideo extends the flow-matching framework to jointly optimize three training paradigms—conditional generation (denoise RGB given clean modality), modality estimation (denoise modality given clean RGB), and joint generation (denoise both)—via a dynamic noise scheduling strategy that probabilistically routes each training sample to one paradigm, with task probabilities inversely proportional to learning difficulty. To handle heterogeneous modalities within shared parameters, a Modality-Adaptive Switcher extends AdaLN-Zero by learning per-modality embeddings L_m that produce modality-specific modulation parameters (γ_m, β_m, α_m = MLP(L_m + t_emb)), while an In-Context Learner applies dual-branch cross-attention: RGB tokens attend to content captions and modality tokens attend to modality-type descriptions, enabling compositional generalization (e.g., "two persons" → "two objects"). Training follows a two-stage curriculum: Stage 1 establishes spatial correspondence on pixel-aligned modalities (flow, depth, DensePose) with single-person data, then Stage 2 incorporates pixel-unaligned modalities (segmentation, skeleton) and diverse scenes. The model is trained on the newly curated OpenUni dataset (1.3M multimodal video clips) using a velocity-matching objective where each batch is partitioned into four balanced groups across modalities and sources.

## Results

On the unified evaluation (VBench + UniBench), UnityVideo achieves the best scores across all task categories: for text-to-video generation, it reaches 97.44 Background Consistency, 64.12 Aesthetic Quality, 23.57 Overall Consistency, and 47.76 Dynamic Degree; for controllable generation, 96.04 / 54.63 / 21.86 / 64.42 on the same metrics; for depth estimation, Abs Rel of 0.022 and δ<1.25 of 98.98%; and for video segmentation, mIoU of 68.82 and mAP of 23.25. In the user study (Table 5), UnityVideo scores 38.50% on physical quality, 30.25% on semantic quality, and 31.80% on overall preference, outperforming Kling-1.6, HunyuanVideo, and Wan2.1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For text-to-video generation, UnityVideo outperforms all four T2V baselines (Kling-1.6, OpenSora2, HunyuanVideo-13B, Wan2.1-14B) on every VBench metric, with the largest margins on Overall Consistency (+1.96 over HunyuanVideo) and Dynamic Degree (+13.45 over Wan2.1). In controllable generation, UnityVideo beats Full-DiT on Background Consistency (+0.46), Overall Consistency (+1.74), and Dynamic Degree (+14.92), while Full-DiT retains a marginal Aesthetic Quality edge (54.82 vs. 54.63); VACE is outperformed on all four metrics. For depth estimation, UnityVideo achieves Abs Rel 0.022 and δ<1.25 98.98%, surpassing Aether (0.025 / 97.95%), Geo4D (0.053 / 97.94%), and DepthCrafter (0.065 / 96.94%). On video segmentation, UnityVideo's mIoU of 68.82 and mAP of 23.25 exceed SeC (65.52 / 22.23) and SAMWISE (62.21 / 20.12) by 3.3 and 1.0 points respectively. In the user study, UnityVideo leads all competitors on physical quality (38.50% vs. 27.20% for Wan2.1), semantic quality (30.25% vs. 26.10% for HunyuanVideo), and overall preference (31.80% vs. 27.65% for Wan2.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) and the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) by introducing a dynamic noise scheduling strategy that unifies three training paradigms (conditional generation, modality estimation, and joint generation) within a single model, and a modality-adaptive switcher that extends AdaLN-Zero with per-modality modulation parameters. Conceptually, it draws an explicit analogy to LLMs unifying text sub-modalities (code, math, natural language) into one training loop, arguing that visual sub-modalities (depth, flow, segmentation, skeleton, DensePose) should be treated similarly. Prior bidirectional video-geometry work (Aether, GeoVideo, EgoTwin) couples at most two modalities in one direction; UnityVideo generalizes this to five modalities and three task types simultaneously, with a curriculum that separates pixel-aligned from pixel-unaligned modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LLM analogy provides a compelling and testable hypothesis for the video generation community: that unifying visual sub-modalities can yield emergent world understanding analogous to how unifying text sub-modalities produced reasoning in LLMs. The released OpenUni dataset (1.3M multi-modal video pairs) and UniBench (Unreal Engine ground truth) are practical resources that lower the barrier for future unified video research. Key open questions include whether the convergence and zero-shot generalization gains scale to larger backbones, whether the VAE reconstruction artifacts limit downstream utility, and whether the in-context generalization (e.g., 'two persons' → 'two objects') reflects genuine compositional reasoning or merely distributional overlap. The paper's own limitation section acknowledges that scaling to more modalities and larger architectures remains future work.
