## Problem

Multi-human, object-centric motion generation with coupled constraints (contacts, handoffs, collision-free co-manipulation) remains largely hand-crafted or dependent on motion capture. Existing sketch-driven methods like Sketch2Anim handle only single characters, while text-planning approaches like COLLAGE under-specify timing and spatial alignment. The paper asks how to extend sketch-driven control to multi-entity human-object-human (HOH) interactions with fine-grained, coupled constraints while retaining fast, stable sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse storyboard controls: 2D keyposes at K annotated locations per human, per-joint 2D polylines for target trajectories, binary object silhouettes indicating placement and extent, and an optional free-form text prompt. A per-frame joint-conditioning indicator χ tracks which of the 22 SMPLX joints per human are constrained at each frame. The output is a dense N-frame 3D scene state comprising per-human joint positions (R^{J×3}) and 6D rotation representations (R^{J×6}) for H humans, plus rigid-body poses (translation + quaternion) and world-space anchor points for O objects, all decoded from a hierarchical VQ-VAE entity latent.

## Method

Sketch2Colab distills a pre-trained sketch-conditioned diffusion teacher (COLLAGE temporal U-Net on a hierarchical VQ-VAE entity latent) into a rectified-flow student via probability-flow matching, enabling fast 30–60 step sampling. A dual-space guidance scheme combines raw-space differentiable energies (keyframe fidelity, trajectory tracking, contact margins, collision penalties, foot-skate suppression) back-propagated through a learned low-rank block-Toeplitz Jacobian surrogate with latent-space anchor energies that keep the flow on the sketch-conditioned manifold. A lightweight CTMC over three composite interaction states (approach/withdraw, contact/release, hold/carry) is trained with a Kolmogorov-residual objective and modulates the student via occupancy-weighted phase mixing of sub-fields and state-dependent contact-energy gating, sharpening discrete transitions like grasp onset and handoff. Training proceeds in two phases: first minimizing the rectified-flow and distillation losses, then activating the Lyapunov potential, CTMC, supervised energy surrogates, and latent alignment losses jointly.

## Results

On CORE4D (sketch+text setting), Sketch2Colab achieves FID 0.399, foot-skate 0.084, keypose-2D error 0.032, trajectory-3D error 0.116, anchor error 0.038, and penetration 0.022, outperforming all baselines across every metric. Relative to the COLLAGE Teacher, FID drops by ~22%, trajectory-2D by ~45%, anchor error by ~51%, and penetration by ~21%. On the sketch-only setting (Table 1), Sketch2Colab similarly leads with FID 0.487 and trajectory-3D 0.133, while remaining robust to up to 80% sketch noise (FID degrades to 0.856 vs. 0.698 at 60% noise). Sampling uses only 30–60 Heun steps, substantially faster than the 1000-step diffusion teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sketch2Colab outperforms both adapted baselines on nearly all metrics. Against the COLLAGE Teacher (sketch-conditioned diffusion), it reduces FID by ~22% (0.399 vs. 0.511), keypose-2D by ~37% (0.032 vs. 0.051), trajectory-3D by ~40% (0.116 vs. 0.194), anchor error by ~51% (0.038 vs. 0.077), and penetration by ~21% (0.022 vs. 0.028), while also improving text-motion alignment (MM Dist 5.50 vs. 6.08, RP3 0.522 vs. 0.500). Against Sketch2Anim-INT (multi-entity adaptation of single-human sketch-to-animation), the gains are even larger: FID drops by ~45% (0.399 vs. 0.728), trajectory-3D by ~48% (0.116 vs. 0.222), and anchor error by ~58% (0.038 vs. 0.091). The only metric where a baseline wins is penetration: RetrievAdapt achieves 0.019 vs. Sketch2Colab's 0.022, consistent with its retrieval-plus-refinement design that inherently produces smoother, collision-free motion but at the cost of much weaker storyboard fidelity (T3D 0.356 vs. 0.116). The paper also reports that Sketch2Colab samples in 30–60 Heun steps versus 1000 diffusion steps for the teacher, a roughly 20–30× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sketch2Colab extends the storyboard-driven motion generation paradigm of Sketch2Anim (single-human, 2D keyframes and joint trajectories) to multi-human, object-centric HOH scenes, and builds its latent space, temporal U-Net backbone, and decoder directly on COLLAGE's hierarchical VQ-VAE. The key methodological departure is replacing diffusion sampling with a rectified-flow student distilled from the diffusion teacher, augmented by dual-space energy guidance (raw-space for geometric precision, latent-space for manifold coherence) and a CTMC-based discrete phase planner for interaction state scheduling. The energy-matching potential and Kolmogorov-residual CTMC training draw on recent generator-matching and EBM literature, but their coupling to a flow-based motion generator with storyboard conditioning is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in animation and game production: multi-human collaborative motion with shared objects remains largely hand-crafted, and existing text-driven approaches (e.g., COLLAGE) under-specify timing and spatial layout. The distillation-to-rectified-flow recipe offers a concrete path to faster sampling (30–60 Heun steps vs. 1000 diffusion steps) while maintaining constraint adherence, which is practically important for interactive tools. However, the current scope is limited to two humans and a single actively manipulated object, and the method still inherits behavior from a calibrated diffusion teacher; scaling to denser multi-agent coordination, multi-object task graphs, and teacher-free training remain open.
