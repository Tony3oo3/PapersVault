## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction and novel-view synthesis methods are designed, trained, and evaluated exclusively on ground-level perspective imagery, creating a fundamental scalability bottleneck for city-scale outdoor scenes. Orthorectified satellite imagery from public mapping services offers a readily accessible global geometric prior, but its lack of perspective projection and parallax makes it incompatible with conventional structure-from-motion and Gaussian splatting pipelines. No prior method has successfully fused ground-level and orthorectified satellite views in a unified feed-forward framework for 3D Gaussian splatting.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more ground-level RGB images (518×518) tagged with 3DoF pose (GPS latitude, longitude, and heading from an IMU) and a single orthorectified satellite image (512×512 at 2 pixels/meter) automatically queried from public mapping services (Google Maps, Azure Maps, or Esri) at the GPS location. The output is a unified set of 3D Gaussian splats in a shared coordinate frame: ground Gaussians backprojected via perspective projection and satellite Gaussians backprojected via orthographic projection using the known spatial resolution. The first ground image defines the world origin and zero-altitude reference; all other ground images are expressed relative to it. At inference, only the satellite image and ground views are needed—no terrain data is required.

## Method

The architecture builds on VGGT's alternating frame/global attention ViT backbone with a DINOv2 encoder, injecting L=12 bidirectional cross-attention layers (Attn_meta) that exchange information between ground tokens and satellite patch tokens. The ground branch regresses per-pixel depth, 6DoF camera poses, and Gaussian attributes (covariance, opacity, SH color) via DPT heads, while the satellite branch regresses a height map relative to the ground reference and per-pixel confidence, converting pixel locations to 3D positions using the known orthographic spatial resolution. The key design choice is treating the ground-to-satellite transformation as a known 3DoF alignment (from GPS) rather than estimating 6DoF, reducing the satellite geometry problem to height-map regression. Training uses confidence-weighted depth and height losses, RGB rendering losses on ground views, satellite views, and combined Gaussians, a BEV orthographic rendering loss, and sky regularization (penalizing close depths and promoting opacity on sky pixels). The model is initialized from AnySplat weights (which distill VGGT priors) and fine-tuned with satellite-specific layers, trained for 4 days on 2×A100 GPUs with FlashAttention-v2 and mixed precision.

## Results

On the geolocalized Tanks and Temples benchmark (10 scenes), Cross-View Splatter (Combined) achieves 12.00 PSNR / 0.3855 SSIM / 0.5699 LPIPS with 3 context views, outperforming the strongest baseline AnySplat (10.93 PSNR / 0.3775 SSIM / 0.5331 LPIPS) by ~1.1 dB in PSNR. With 2 context views, the combined model reaches 11.67 PSNR, beating Splatfacto (11.53) and DepthSplat (9.61). On DL3DV (40 scenes), the combined model scores 12.61 PSNR with 3 views vs. AnySplat's 10.88 and Splatfacto's 13.61 (which uses GT poses). The satellite-conditioned model shows the largest gains at low context-target overlap (≤0.15 IoU), where it outperforms the ground-only variant by several dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Cross-View Splatter (Combined) achieves the highest PSNR across all context-view counts on both Tanks and Temples and DL3DV, beating the strongest feed-forward baseline AnySplat by 1.07 dB (3-view, T&T) and 1.73 dB (3-view, DL3DV). Against per-scene optimization baselines, it surpasses Splatfacto (12.00 vs. 11.72 PSNR, 3-view T&T) and DepthSplat (12.00 vs. 10.72) while running in a single forward pass rather than 5+ minutes of optimization. Cost-volume methods MVSplat and DepthSplat degrade severely under 1-view input (MVSplat: 6.93 PSNR at 2 views, no 1-view result), whereas Cross-View Splatter maintains 11.13 PSNR even with a single context view. On LPIPS, AnySplat remains competitive (0.5331 vs. 0.5699 at 3-view T&T), suggesting the satellite head improves geometric fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) more than perceptual quality. Sat2Density, the only satellite-only baseline, trails significantly on LPIPS (0.8037 vs. 0.5699) and PSNR (8.85 vs. 12.00), confirming that fusing ground and satellite Gaussians is substantially better than satellite-to-ground synthesis alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting line (DUSt3R → VGGT → AnySplat) by injecting a satellite branch into the alternating-attention transformer via bidirectional cross-attention, treating orthorectified web-map tiles as a 3DoF geometric prior rather than a perspective view. It departs from prior satellite-to-ground synthesis work (Sat2Density, SatDreamer360, SkyEyes) in two key ways: it uses freely available orthorectified tiles (not multi-view non-ortho imagery) and produces a unified 3D Gaussian representation rather than a generative or density-based output. The height-map regression formulation for the satellite branch, supervised by mined DEM/terrain data, is a practical workaround for the fact that orthorectification removes the parallax needed by classical MVS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single, freely queryable satellite tile can meaningfully improve novel-view synthesis in sparse, large-baseline outdoor settings—particularly when ground-view overlap is very low (IoU ≤ 0.15), which is exactly the regime where ground-only methods struggle most. However, absolute PSNR values remain low (≈11–12 dB), and the method is restricted to outdoor, GPS-tagged scenes with known heading, limiting near-term practical deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to city-block or kilometer-scale scenes, how sensitive it is to satellite resolution and seasonal/illumination mismatch, and whether the cross-attention mechanism can be made more efficient for real-time use.
