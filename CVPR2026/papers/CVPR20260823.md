## Problem

Existing open-set semi-supervised learning methods rely on confidence or uncertainty signals from single-view predictions to separate in-distribution (ID) from out-of-distribution (OOD) unlabeled samples, but these shallow signals fail to capture the distinct representation-level sensitivities that ID and OOD samples exhibit under semantic-preserving perturbations. This paper shows that representation instability under perturbations yields a substantially stronger and more discriminative OOD detection signal than confidence mutation, yet no prior method exploits this representation-level cue for filtering.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small labeled set (e.g., 10–400 images per ID class) and a large unlabeled set (20,000–40,000 images) that is a mixture of ID and OOD samples at a known mismatch ratio. During training, each unlabeled image is passed through the backbone twice—once as-is and once under a semantic-preserving perturbation (weak augmentation such as flipping or mild color jitter)—to compute a scalar instability score. The output is a standard K-class classifier for ID samples plus a binary ID/OOD partition of the unlabeled pool, with OOD samples excluded from pseudo-label training.

## Method

PAF computes the squared L2 distance between the penultimate-layer representations (after a dedicated consistency projection head) of an unlabeled image and its perturbed view, then applies an Otsu-based adaptive threshold to partition the unlabeled batch into ID and OOD subsets. The overall training is two-stage: Stage 1 (50k iterations) warms up the WideResNet backbone using cross-entropy on labeled data, a representation consistency loss that enforces perturbation-invariant embeddings, and an auxiliary rotation-prediction self-supervised task, each routed through separate projection heads to avoid gradient conflict. Stage 2 (200k iterations) activates PAF every 20 epochs to refresh the ID/OOD split, then trains with an instability-weighted pseudo-labeling loss (extending UDA) where the KL divergence between weak and strong view predictions is scaled by (1 − normalized instability), so that stable, confident samples dominate the decision boundary while borderline ones are down-weighted. A Lipschitz-margin theoretical analysis proves that perturbation-induced prediction variance decays exponentially with decision margin, and that representation instability is a strictly tighter criterion than confidence fluctuation, providing the formal justification for the representation-level filter.

## Results

PAF achieves the best seen-class classification accuracy across all four internal OOD benchmarks: 99.4% on MNIST (mismatch 0.3), 93.1% on CIFAR-10 (0.3), 77.7% on CIFAR-100 (0.3), and 58.1% on TinyImageNet (0.3), outperforming the strongest baselines BDMatch and SCOMatch by 0.3–2.2 percentage points. For OOD detection (AUC), PAF leads on all datasets, with particularly large gains on CIFAR-100 (84.5% vs. 73.2% for ProSub) and TinyImageNet (64.8% vs. 56.2%). Under external OOD settings (Table 3), PAF also achieves the highest accuracy on CIFAR-10 across all four OOD sources (TinyImageNet, LSUN, Gaussian Noise, Uniform Noise) at every sample budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAF consistently outperforms all 22 compared baselines on seen-class accuracy across every dataset and mismatch ratio. The closest competitors are BDMatch (ICML 2024) and SCOMatch (ECCV 2024): on CIFAR-100 (mismatch 0.3), PAF achieves 77.7% vs. BDMatch's 75.9% and SCOMatch's 75.3%, a gap of 1.8–2.4 points; on TinyImageNet (0.3), PAF reaches 58.1% vs. BDMatch's 56.7% and SCOMatch's 54.2%. For OOD detection AUC (Table 2), PAF dominates with margins of 7–11 points over the next-best method ProSub, e.g., 84.5% vs. 73.2% on CIFAR-100 and 64.8% vs. 56.2% on TinyImageNet. Under external OOD (Table 3), PAF edges out CaliMatch and T2T by 0.3–1.0 points on CIFAR-10 across all four OOD sources. The ablation (Table 5) further confirms that replacing PAF with confidence-based filtering drops accuracy from 77.7% to 74.5% on CIFAR-100, validating the representation-instability signal over the shallower confidence-mutation criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the UDA consistency-training framework by replacing its uniform pseudo-labeling with an instability-weighted variant, and departs from the confidence-mutation signal identified by Zhang et al. [48] by arguing that representation-level instability is a strictly stronger OOD indicator. The two-stage training design draws on consistency regularization (FixMatch, UDA) and self-supervised rotation prediction, while the Otsu-based adaptive thresholding provides a practical, distribution-free filtering mechanism. Theoretically, the paper grounds the ID/OOD sensitivity gap in a Lipschitz-margin analysis, connecting perturbation variance to decision margin in a way that prior OSSL methods (OpenMatch, T2T, IOMatch) did not formalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, theoretically motivated filtering criterion that is simple to implement (one perturbation view + Otsu threshold) and consistently outperforms confidence-based alternatives across four benchmarks, suggesting representation instability may become a standard component in OSSL pipelines. However, the method's reliance on semantic-preserving (weak) augmentations limits its applicability to scenarios where OOD samples differ in ways that weak perturbations cannot probe, and the theoretical analysis assumes Lipschitz logits that may not hold for very deep or non-smooth architectures. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to video, multi-modal, or extremely high-dimensional OOD settings, and whether the two-stage schedule can be replaced by a single-stage formulation.
