## Problem

Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) prioritizes broad semantic matching, making it unreliable for retrieving a user-specified instance across different contexts, especially when visually similar distractors are present. In practical scenarios like digital memory retrieval or identity tracing, preserving the exact instance identity is more critical than achieving general semantic alignment, yet no existing benchmark or method explicitly enforces this constraint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263359/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The query consists of a reference image with a bounding box that visually anchors a specific instance, paired with a modification text describing contextual changes (e.g., scene, pose, or style shifts). The bounding box is not used as a hard crop but as a soft spatial prompt that modulates attention during feature fusion. The system produces a query embedding ranked by cosine similarity against a candidate gallery enriched with hard-negative instance distractors, and retrieval is scored on both semantic correctness (R@K) and strict instance identity (R_ID@K).

## Method

AdaFocal builds on a BLIP-2 Q-Former backbone and introduces a Context-Aware Attention Modulator (CAAM) that predicts a query-specific modulation scalar β. The CAAM processes the reference image and modification text through learnable Contextual Probe Tokens and a Transformer-based Contextual Reasoning Module, outputting β via a linear projection. This scalar is injected as a dynamic additive bias into the cross-attention computation, weighted by a binary mask spatially aligned with the bounding box region, thereby adaptively intensifying attention on the anchored instance while preserving broader compositional context. The framework is trained end-to-end with a batch-based contrastive alignment loss (InfoNCE), using a differential learning rate strategy (1e-4 for CAAM, 1e-5 for the multimodal encoder) to balance parameter updates between the lightweight modulator and the frozen-pretrained backbone.

## Results

On the OACIRR benchmark, AdaFocal (ViT-G) achieves an average of 79.00% across RID@1, R@1, and R@5, substantially outperforming the best UMR baseline GME-2B (62.53%) and the OACIRR-fine-tuned SPRC (ViT-G) baseline (74.05%). Specifically, AdaFocal improves over SPRC (ViT-G) by +4.14 in R@1 and +7.47 in RID@1 on average, with the largest gains on the Fashion subset (RID@1: 77.15 vs. 65.25) and Product subset (RID@1: 91.86 vs. 86.05). Zero-shot CIR methods (Pic2Word) perform worst, averaging only 22.6–24.8%, confirming that semantic-level retrieval is fundamentally insufficient for instance-level discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaFocal (ViT-G) decisively outperforms all six baseline groups. Against the strongest UMR model GME-2B (62.53% avg), AdaFocal gains +16.47 points, with the gap most pronounced on Fashion (77.15 vs. 44.98 RID@1), where multi-object scenes expose UMR models' lack of instance discrimination. Against the OACIRR-fine-tuned SPRC (ViT-G) at 74.05%, AdaFocal improves by +4.95 in average, confirming that adaptive visual attention via bounding-box grounding is more effective than textual ID-tag prompting for instance preservation. Zero-shot CIR (Pic2Word, 22.61% avg) and UMR models without instance-aware training (27–62% avg) all fail to close the gap, as their pre-training optimizes for broad semantic correspondence rather than fine-grained identity. The paper further shows that simply retraining SPRC on OACIRR (vs. CIRR) lifts its average from 37.30% to 74.05%, isolating the dataset's contribution from the architectural contribution of AdaFocal's CAAM module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) by introducing a strict instance-level consistency constraint, departing from the semantic-matching paradigm that dominates existing CIR methods (CIRR, FashionIQ, SPRC, etc.). It also generalizes beyond person-centric retrieval (IPR, CC-IPR, CPR) to arbitrary object categories, and avoids the per-instance fine-tuning required by approaches like MyVLM. The architectural design borrows the BLIP-2 Q-Former as a multimodal fusion backbone and adapts attention-manipulation techniques originally developed for generative models (Prompt Highlighter) into a retrieval setting via a learned modulation scalar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OACIRR benchmark (160K+ quadruples, 4 domains, 3.9K instances with hard-negative distractors) fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously enforces instance fidelity, provides visual grounding via bounding boxes, and spans multiple object categories at scale. The finding that even a 7B-parameter UMR model (GME) underperforms a much smaller fine-tuned model by ~17 points on average suggests that pre-training scale does not transfer to fine-grained instance discrimination, pointing to a genuine capability gap in current multimodal models. Open questions include whether the bounding-box anchoring generalizes to more ambiguous or partially occluded instances, how the approach scales to video or 3D retrieval, and whether the adaptive modulation strategy can be extended to multi-instance queries.
