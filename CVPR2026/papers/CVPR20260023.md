## Problem

Pre-trained 2D diffusion editing models can edit individual images realistically but produce inconsistent results across multiple views of the same 3D scene. Existing multi-view editing approaches rely on explicit 3D representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) that require lengthy optimization, are unstable under sparse views, and yield blurry or artifact-laden outputs. The paper asks whether one can achieve multi-view consistent editing in a training-free manner by directly coupling the sampling processes of a 2D editing model and a multi-view generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a collection of N posed multi-view images {I_k, P_k} along with task-specific conditioning (a text prompt for stylization, an environment map for relighting, or a coarse spatial edit for geometric changes). The 2D editing model is applied independently to each input image, while the multi-view model is conditioned on a single edited reference image and the poses of the remaining views. Both models operate in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 2.1, and the coupling is performed entirely in this shared latent space. The final output is the coupled multi-view sample, which is selected as the result because it lies within the multi-view distribution and thus inherits view consistency.

## Method

The core contribution is coupled DDPM sampling: two diffusion trajectories (one from a 2D editing model, one from a multi-view generation model) are sampled concurrently and steered toward each other at every denoising step via a coupling energy U(x, x') = −λ/2‖x − x'‖². At each timestep t, both models predict their respective clean estimates x̂₀, and the coupling gradient ∇U is added to the standard DDPM update, scaled by √(1−α̅_{t−1}) to limit KL divergence between training and inference distributions. This causes the multi-view sample to remain on the multi-view consistency manifold while incorporating the 2D edit, and simultaneously pulls the 2D sample toward the multi-view identity. The approach is entirely training-free—no new parameters are learned—and the hyperparameter λ controls coupling strength, with an optimal regime balancing reconstruction fidelity and multi-view consistency. The framework generalizes beyond multi-view editing to image and video generation, demonstrated with Flux and Wan2.1 flow-matching models.

## Results

On multi-view spatial editing, Coupled Sampling achieves the best PSNR (17.0 dB), SSIM (0.550), and MEt3r (0.335) among all baselines, with 80% user preference. On stylization, it attains the highest temporal consistency (0.946), subject consistency (0.807), and MEt3r (0.392), with 47% user preference—outperforming mesh-based methods that have access to ground-truth geometry. On relighting, it achieves the best PSNR (23.2 dB), SSIM (0.868), and 46% user preference, matching the best MEt3r (0.217) shared with Du et al. and GT NeRF+Neural Gaffer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Coupled Sampling outperforms all compositional diffusion baselines (Liu et al. [31] and Du et al. [13]) on MEt3r multi-view consistency across all three tasks (0.335 vs. 0.368/0.344 for spatial editing; 0.392 vs. 0.461/0.461 for stylization; 0.217 vs. 0.220/0.217 for relighting) and dominates in user preference (80% vs. 9%/1% for spatial editing; 47% vs. 19%/12% for stylization; 46% vs. 10%/19% for relighting). The Image-to-MV baseline [63] (editing one image then synthesizing the rest) is consistently the weakest, losing on every metric due to identity drift. On stylization, Coupled Sampling surpasses mesh-based methods TEXTure [43] and Hunyuan3D [47]—which have privileged access to ground-truth geometry—on temporal consistency (0.946 vs. 0.967/0.952), subject consistency (0.807 vs. 0.748/0.754), and user preference (47% vs. 14%/8%), while matching their MEt3r. For relighting, it beats the GT NeRF+Neural Gaffer baseline [20] on PSNR (23.2 vs. 22.4) and user preference (46% vs. 25%) despite not requiring a pre-fitted NeRF. SDEdit [36], used as the Magic Fixup baseline for spatial editing, is outperformed on all four quantitative metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of multi-view editing that relies on explicit 3D representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) optimized via score distillation or dataset modification, and from compositional diffusion methods (Liu et al. [31], Du et al. [13]) that average scores from multiple models. Instead, it introduces a coupled DDPM sampling procedure where two diffusion trajectories (a 2D editing model and a multi-view generation model) are steered toward each other via a simple Euclidean coupling energy, keeping each sample within its own distribution. The approach builds on multi-view diffusion models like Stable Virtual Camera [63] and 2D editing models (Magic Fixup, ControlNet, Neural Gaffer), but repurposes them as mutual guides during sampling rather than as separate stages in a pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, optimization-free nature of the method removes the primary practical barriers to multi-view editing (long NeRF optimization, dense view coverage requirements, instability under sparse inputs), making it applicable to real-world photo collections. The generality demonstrated across three editing tasks, two model families (DDPM and flow-based), and even video generation suggests the coupling mechanism could become a general-purpose tool for composing diffusion models across modalities. Open questions include sensitivity to the coupling strength hyperparameter (which has a narrow optimal regime), dependence on a high-quality multi-view diffusion model, and whether the approach scales to very large view counts or highly dynamic scenes.
