## Problem

Visual autoregressive models like VAR construct multi-scale token pyramids via uniform downsampling, which folds supra-Nyquist frequencies into the baseband and produces aliasing artifacts (jaggies, moiré patterns) that degrade fine details and destabilize training. All existing scale-wise AR approaches inherit this limitation because they disregard the physical principles of optical image formation, forcing the decoder to simultaneously de-alias and generate content.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label as input and generates RGB images at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution through 10 autoregressive steps (one per scale in the pyramid). At inference, the deployment network is architecturally identical to vanilla VAR, consuming only structure tokens and producing the final image with zero additional overhead relative to the baseline. The method also supports inpainting and outpainting tasks at 1024×1024, where partial image regions are provided as spatial constraints.

## Method

FVAR replaces uniform downsampling with a next-focus prediction paradigm grounded in optical physics: a progressive refocusing pyramid is built by convolving the image with defocus PSF kernels of monotonically decreasing radius (cosine-scheduled from ρ_max=12 to 0), yielding alias-free multi-scale views. A dual-path tokenization splits each scale into a clean focused view (quantized with an 8192-entry codebook) and a high-frequency residual (quantized with a 512-entry codebook), capturing complementary structural and alias information. During training, a High-Frequency Residual Teacher equipped with Alias-Gate Cross-Attention selectively fuses alias tokens into structure representations, while a vanilla VAR student network is distilled via feature-alignment and logit KL-divergence losses. At inference, only the student is deployed, preserving full compatibility with the standard VAR pipeline and introducing no architectural or computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, FVAR-d24 (1.0B params) achieves FID 1.75 and IS 325.8, outperforming VAR-d24 (FID 2.33) and M-VAR-d24 (FID 1.93) while using fewer parameters than M-VAR. At 1024×1024, FVAR-d16 reduces FID from 8.25 (VAR-d16) to 6.85, a 17% relative improvement. FVAR matches VAR's inference speed exactly (10 steps, same relative time) since the teacher network is discarded at deployment, and FVAR-d20 (600M) surpasses DiT-XL/2 (675M) on FID (2.25 vs. 2.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FVAR consistently outperforms VAR at every matched model size: FID improves from 5.81→3.95 (d12), 3.55→2.89 (d16), 2.95→2.25 (d20), and 2.33→1.75 (d24), while maintaining identical parameter counts and inference steps. Against M-VAR, FVAR achieves lower FID with substantially fewer parameters (e.g., FVAR-d16 at 310M beats M-VAR-d16 at 464M; FVAR-d24 at 1.0B beats M-VAR-d24 at 1.5B). At the d20 scale, FVAR (600M, FID 2.25) surpasses DiT-XL/2 (675M, FID 2.27) and LlamaGen-3B (3.1B, FID 2.18 is slightly better but requires 576 steps vs. 10). At 1024×1024, FVAR-d16 improves FID from 8.25 to 6.85 over VAR-d16, and at 512×512, FVAR-d36 improves FID from 2.63 to 2.28 over VAR-d36. FVAR's key advantage is that all gains come at zero inference overhead—the teacher network is training-only, so deployment is identical to vanilla VAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

FVAR builds directly on VAR (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024), which reformulated visual autoregression as next-scale prediction over a multi-scale token pyramid, and on M-VAR (Ren et al., 2024), which decoupled intra- and inter-scale dependencies. The key conceptual departure is replacing uniform digital downsampling with physics-consistent defocus PSF kernels of decreasing radius, reframing the generation process as progressive optical refocusing rather than resolution reduction. The High-Frequency Residual Teacher with Alias-Gate Cross-Attention is a novel training-time module that distills alias-aware knowledge into a vanilla VAR deployment network, making the approach orthogonal to prior tokenizer improvements (LlamaGen, ViTVQ) and continuous-AR methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating aliasing at its source through optical low-pass filtering rather than forcing the decoder to de-alias post-hoc, FVAR addresses a fundamental limitation of all current multi-scale autoregressive generators, with the practical advantage of zero inference overhead. The resolution-dependent ablation (minimal FID impact at 256×256 but severe degradation at 1024×1024 when removing progressive refocusing) suggests the approach becomes increasingly critical as resolution scales, which is the direction the field is heading. Open questions include whether the PSF-based framing extends to video or 3D generation, whether richer optical priors (chromatic aberration, sensor noise) yield further gains, and whether the dual-codebook scheme can be unified into a single codebook without quality loss.
