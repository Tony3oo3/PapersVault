## Problem

Supervised Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) requires large amounts of manually annotated triplets, while zero-shot in-context generation (e.g., CoAlign) produces synthetic triplets that suffer from an intractable domain gap and lack task-specific priors. Existing few-shot CIR methods generate low-quality pseudo triplets via prompt tuning or masking, providing weaker supervision than zero-shot baselines. The paper addresses how to generate high-quality, domain-adapted synthetic triplets using only a handful of labeled samples to bridge this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261435/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For retrieval, the method takes a composed query (reference image + relative caption) and returns top-K target images ranked by cosine similarity in a joint embedding space. For the DAIG data-generation pipeline, the input is a small set of 8–32 domain-specific triplets used to fine-tune the T2I model, plus object and edit tokens sampled from predefined sets that prompt an LLM to produce textual triplets. The T2I model (Flux.1-dev) generates stitched 224×448 images containing left-right sub-images, which are cropped into reference and target images, yielding 20k synthetic CIR triplets per domain.

## Method

DAIG fine-tunes a frozen Flux.1-dev T2I model using CIR-LoRA, a parameter-efficient adapter that inserts a Mixture-of-Experts LoRA module (rank-32, 2 experts) into the cross-attention layers of each DiT block, trained via flow matching on only 32 in-context pairs (stitched reference-target images paired with layout-template descriptions). The tuned model then synthesizes domain-adapted triplets from LLM-generated textual triplets, eliminating the need for post-hoc data filtering. A two-stage training pipeline follows: DRSP perturbs visual features by sampling from a multivariate Gaussian over batch-level mean/std statistics (reparameterization trick, p=0.5) to broaden the sparse synthetic distribution, and FRA fine-tunes on real annotated triplets by imposing a learnable angular margin (φ=3×10⁻³) on positive query-target pairs in the softmax contrastive loss for fine-grained discrimination. The entire framework is plug-and-play, integrating with any existing supervised CIR backbone (CLIP4CIR, BLIP4CIR, SPRC) without adding inference cost.

## Results

On the CIRR test set (100% data), SPRC+DAIG achieves 84.10% Recall@5 and 82.40% Avg., surpassing the previous best CCIN (84.05% R@5) and QuRe (82.53% R@5, 80.52% Avg.). Under the 32-shot setting, DAIG boosts SPRC from 57.61% to 72.41% Recall@5 (+14.80%) and BLIP4CIR from 27.86% to 57.01% (+29.15%). On FashionIQ (100%), SPRC+DAIG reaches 75.96% Avg. R@50, exceeding CCIN by 1.20% and SPRC by 1.01%. The method also outperforms dedicated few-shot CIR approaches (PromptCLIP, PTG) by 4–13 points under 8-shot and 16-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At full supervision (100%), SPRC+DAIG (84.10% R@5, 82.40% Avg.) edges out CCIN (84.05% R@5) by 0.05 points and surpasses QuRe (82.53% R@5) by 1.57 points and Re-ranking (81.75% R@5) by 2.35 points on CIRR. The plug-and-play gains are most dramatic in the few-shot regime: under 32-shot, DAIG lifts BLIP4CIR by +29.15% R@5, CLIP4CIR by +11.59%, and SPRC by +14.80%, while under 1% data it improves SPRC by +6.75% R@5. On FashionIQ (100%), SPRC+DAIG achieves 75.96% Avg. R@50, beating CCIN (74.76%) by 1.20% and SPRC (74.95%) by 1.01%. Against dedicated few-shot CIR methods, DAIG outperforms PromptCLIP by 28.5 points (69.5 vs. 41.0) and PTG by 4.0 points (69.5 vs. 65.5) on CIRR Recall@5 under 8-shot, with similar margins at 16-shot. DAIG also surpasses zero-shot triplet-generation baselines (CompoDiff, CoVR-BLIP, CASE) by 16–24 points on CIRR R@5 in the 32-shot setting, confirming that domain-adapted synthetic triplets substantially outperform domain-agnostic ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot in-context triplet generation paradigm of CoAlign [30], which uses a frozen DiT to synthesize CIR triplets from LLM-generated text, by introducing domain adaptation via a novel CIR-LoRA module (LoRA [18] augmented with a Mixture-of-Experts routing structure) fine-tuned on only 32 domain-specific samples. The key departure is that CoAlign's generated triplets suffer from distribution shift relative to the target domain, whereas DAIG injects both domain priors and CIR task priors into the T2I model before generation. The two-stage training framework (DRSP with Gaussian perturbation of visual features, and FRA with an angular margin) is a new combination not present in prior CIR training pipelines, and the overall system is designed as a plug-and-play wrapper around any existing supervised CIR method (CLIP4CIR, BLIP4CIR, SPRC).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAIG addresses a practical bottleneck in CIR deployment: the scarcity of manually annotated triplets in new domains. By showing that 20k domain-adapted synthetic triplets outperform 534k zero-shot generated triplets (CIRHS) and 18M real image-text pairs (ST18M) for pretraining, the paper challenges the assumption that data volume alone drives CIR performance, highlighting domain alignment as the critical factor. The plug-and-play design means practitioners can adopt DAIG without modifying their existing CIR architecture, and the absence of additional inference cost makes it deployment-friendly. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Flux to other T2I backbones (SD3.5, PixArt), whether multi-resolution generation would further improve fidelity, and whether step distillation can reduce the ~10-hour generation cost for larger-scale triplet synthesis.
