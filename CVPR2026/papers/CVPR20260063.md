## Problem

Existing engineering diagram parsing pipelines rely on multiple independent models for symbols, lines, and texts, creating cumbersome workflows, while high-resolution diagrams (8K–10K) impose prohibitive computational costs. More critically, object-detection-based frameworks only localize component positions and fail to capture topological connection semantics and structured knowledge among components, limiting their utility for industrial digitization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260063/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-resolution engineering diagram images (P&IDs at 8K resolution, EDs at varying resolutions) as input, pre-processed either by global downsampling or slicing to preserve detail. Rather than detecting lines as separate objects, the framework converts line entities into semantic connection relationships between symbols, and text entities into qualifiers attached to entities and relations. The output is a hyper-relational knowledge graph structured as triplets {subject, relation, object, qualifiers}, where qualifiers are OCR-extracted text labels (e.g., model numbers, parameters) associated with symbols and connections.

## Method

DTRT combines a dynamically tokenized Swin Transformer backbone with a one-stage relation Transformer. The backbone inserts MLP-based scorers at each stage to prune ~70% of low-value tokens via Gumbel-Softmax masking, while a parallel Transformer-based reconstruction branch supervises the scorers with pixel-level reconstruction loss to protect fine details like long lines and text. The one-stage relation Transformer avoids O(N²) pairwise matching by using coupled subject-object queries refined through coupled self-attention and a novel relation-aware deformable decoupled visual attention (DDVA) that biases sampling toward the spatial region between subject and object. Training is accelerated by contrast denoising (CDN) with positive/negative noisy samples and mixed query selection (MQS) that seeds high-confidence entity candidates alongside learnable queries. The final triplet decoder identifies pseudo-triples involving text entities and converts them into qualifiers, producing the hyper-relational knowledge graph.

## Results

On the P&ID dataset, DTRT achieves R@1000 = 87.42% and R@2000 = 94.84% on scene graph detection (SGDET), with entity detection AP50 of 99.01% and AR50 of 97.04%. On the ED dataset, DTRT reaches R@200 = 92.52% on SGDET and AP50 of 99.76% on symbol detection. Critically, DTRT accomplishes this with only 90.5 GFLOPs (P&ID) and 67.3 GFLOPs (ED), compared to 791.9 and 589.4 GFLOPs for the strongest two-stage baseline (Relationformer), representing an ~8.7× reduction in computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTRT outperforms all three scene graph generation baselines on both SGCLS and SGDET. On P&ID SGDET, DTRT's R@1000 of 87.42% exceeds Relationformer (76.43%) by 11.0 points, RelTR (65.06%) by 22.4 points, and SGTR (63.89%) by 23.5 points, while using only 90.5 GFLOPs versus 791.9, 287.8, and 749.3 respectively. On ED SGDET, DTRT achieves R@200 of 92.52%, surpassing Relationformer (89.15%) by 3.4 points with 67.3 GFLOPs vs. 589.4. For entity detection, DTRT is comparable to RT-DETR (AP50: 99.01 vs. 99.27 on P&ID) but achieves higher recall (AR50: 97.04 vs. 96.98), and clearly outperforms YOLOv11 (AP50: 99.01 vs. 90.59), DETR (99.01 vs. 91.67), and D-DETR (99.01 vs. 95.41). The key advantage of DTRT is its one-stage architecture that avoids the O(N²) relation matching cost inherent in two-stage methods like Relationformer and SGTR, enabling near-state-of-the-art accuracy at roughly one-eighth the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTRT synthesizes several established threads: the query-based detection paradigm from DETR, contrast denoising training and mixed query selection from DINO, deformable attention from Deformable DETR, and MLP-scorer token pruning from DynamicViT. Its key conceptual departure is reframing engineering diagram parsing—previously handled by cascaded object detectors for symbols, lines, and texts—as a single scene graph generation problem where line entities are converted into relational predicates and text labels become hyper-relational qualifiers. The one-stage triplet decoder (coupled subject-object queries with coupled self-attention) avoids the O(N²) relation-matching cost inherent in two-stage frameworks like Relationformer, while the scorer-reconstructor token pruning module adapts DynamicViT-style sparsification to the document-specific challenge of preserving thin lines and small text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.7× reduction in GFLOPs (90.5 vs. 791.9) while simultaneously improving scene graph detection recall (94.84% vs. 91.58% R@2000 on P&IDs) makes high-resolution diagram digitization computationally tractable for industrial deployment, addressing a real bottleneck in chemical and electrical engineering workflows. The hyper-relational knowledge graph output—linking symbols to their textual model numbers and parameters—moves beyond mere localization toward structured, queryable knowledge that supports downstream tasks like VQA, automated design review, and multi-modal reasoning. Open questions include generalization to other diagram types (mechanical, architectural), robustness to hand-drawn or low-quality scans, and whether the reconstruction branch's added training complexity can be further reduced. The paper does not evaluate on truly diverse or noisy real-world diagrams, limiting confidence in deployment readiness.
