## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods—diffusion models, masked transformers, and next-token autoregressive models—consistently fail to preserve global semantic structure from text descriptions, such as repetition counts and sequential action patterns. Next-token AR exploits the highly predictable short-term dynamics of human motion, learning local coherence rather than long-range temporal organization, while diffusion and masked approaches generate full-resolution sequences in a single undirected step, making it inherently difficult to commit to global structure before refinement.

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text description, encoded by a pretrained T5 encoder and reduced to a single conditioning vector via attention-based pooling, and produces a 3D human motion sequence at 20 FPS (HumanML3D) or 12.5 FPS (KIT-ML), capped at 10 seconds. Motion is represented as 4 hierarchical scales of discrete tokens with sequence lengths (6, 12, 24, 49) drawn from a shared codebook of size 512, decoded by a residual VQ-VAE back to continuous joint-space motion.

## Method

MoScale replaces next-token prediction with a next-scale autoregressive framework: a residual VQ-VAE encodes motion into K=4 coarse-to-fine token groups, and a 16-block causal transformer with a scalewise causal mask generates each scale's tokens jointly, conditioned on all previously generated coarser scales and text via cross-attention. Two refinement mechanisms address limitations of standard next-scale AR under limited data: (1) cross-scale hierarchical refinement perturbs a random subset of coarser-scale tokens during training, forcing finer scales to learn adaptive correction of imperfect drafts rather than relying on clean teacher-forced inputs; (2) in-scale temporal refinement applies a selective mask-and-repredict procedure within each scale using bidirectional attention and a cosine re-masking schedule, introducing diffusion-style iterative correction while preserving the coarse-to-fine causal structure. The model uses RoPE with a learnable scale-level embedding for positional encoding and classifier-free guidance (10% text dropout) for controllable generation.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MoScale (S=18) achieves the best R-Precision Top-1 of 0.540, FID of 0.046, and MM-Dist of 2.830, outperforming all 13 baselines across next-token AR, diffusion, and masked transformer categories. On KIT-ML, MoScale similarly leads with Top-1 of 0.442, FID of 0.173, and MM-Dist of 2.717. In user studies, MoScale is preferred for text alignment (71.5%) and motion quality (73.3%) over ParCo, ReMoDiffuse, and MoMask++. Inference time is 0.28s (S=18) or 0.08s (S=4) with KV cache, substantially faster than ParCo (1.77s) and MotionDiffuse (7.12s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoScale outperforms all next-token AR baselines (TM2T, T2M-GPT, AttT2M, ParCo) on every metric on both HumanML3D and KIT-ML, with the largest gap over ParCo (Top-1: 0.540 vs. 0.515; MM-Dist: 2.830 vs. 2.927 on HumanML3D). Against diffusion methods, MoScale achieves a substantially lower FID (0.046 vs. 0.072–0.630) and better MM-Dist (2.830 vs. 2.814–3.630), while also being 6–25× faster at inference (0.28s vs. 1.77s for ParCo, 7.12s for MotionDiffuse). Among masked transformers, MoScale surpasses MoMask++ (the strongest baseline) by +0.012 Top-1 and −0.082 MM-Dist on HumanML3D, and by +0.009 Top-1 and −0.062 MM-Dist on KIT-ML. On high-complexity text subsets, MoScale's advantage widens (Top-3: 0.775 vs. 0.762 for MoMask++ vs. 0.709 for ParCo), confirming that the hierarchical next-scale design specifically benefits fine-grained semantic alignment. In zero-shot motion editing, inpainting, and outpainting, MoScale receives 78–82% user preference over MoMask and BAMM across quality, alignment, and consistency criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoScale adapts the next-scale prediction framework from image generation [46] to text-conditioned human motion, replacing the standard next-token autoregressive paradigm (T2M-GPT, ParCo, AttT2M) with a coarse-to-fine hierarchical causal structure. The key conceptual departure is the argument that next-token AR fails for motion because short-term kinematic predictability creates a training shortcut that prevents learning of global temporal semantics, whereas diffusion and masked-transformer methods lack a directed causal scaffold to commit to global structure early. Two additional mechanisms—cross-scale hierarchical refinement (perturbing coarser-scale tokens during training) and in-scale temporal refinement (selective mask-and-repredict within each scale)—borrow ideas from diffusion-style iterative correction and data augmentation in low-data regimes, embedding them within the autoregressive framework rather than replacing it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, well-ablated explanation of why next-token AR underperforms for motion (short-horizon predictability shortcut) and shows that hierarchical causality is the dominant factor for text alignment, not bidirectional attention or iterative refinement. Practically, MoScale combines AR training efficiency (0.08–0.28 s inference with KV cache) with SOTA text-to-motion quality and zero-shot generalization to editing, inpainting, and outpainting, making it a versatile single-model solution. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse motion corpora beyond HumanML3D/KIT-ML, how it behaves for multi-person or long-horizon (>10 s) sequences, and whether the hierarchical causality principle transfers to other sequential generation domains (e.g., audio, video).
