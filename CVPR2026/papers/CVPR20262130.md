## Problem

Existing Federated Domain Generalization (FedDG) methods target single-server federated learning, leaving their behavior under Hierarchical FL (HFL) largely unexplored. Recent HFL variants like MTGC and FedRC address data heterogeneity but violate the data-free privacy constraint inherent to domain generalization and lack theoretical guarantees. The paper formally introduces the HFedDG scenario and shows that naive hierarchical weight averaging is suboptimal because residual inter-station divergence dominates the generalization error even after strong local DG training.

## Input / Output

HFedATM is a server-side aggregation layer that consumes model weights and second-order activation statistics (Gram matrices) uploaded by stations, never touching raw client data. For vision tasks it processes RGB images (PACS, Office-Home, TerraIncognita) through LeNet-5, ResNet-18, or VGG-11; for NLP it handles text via RoBERTa-base or DeBERTa-base on Amazon Reviews. The output is a single global model that predicts class labels on unseen target domains, with the aggregation step operating entirely in parameter and activation-geometry space.

## Method

HFedATM combines two complementary data-free aggregation stages at the station-server level. First, Filter-wise Optimal Transport (FOT) Alignment solves a discrete Wasserstein assignment (via the Sinkhorn solver on the Birkhoff polytope) for every convolutional layer, permuting filters so that semantically corresponding channels share the same index across all stations. Second, Shrinkage-aware Regularized Mean (RegMean) Aggregation merges the aligned convolutional filters by weighted arithmetic mean and fuses linear layers in closed form using station-level Gram matrices (averaged client activation second-moments with diagonal shrinkage at α=0.75), yielding a solution that minimizes the weighted sum of station losses in activation space. The method is plug-in: it attaches to any existing FedDG baseline (FedAvg, FedProx, FedSR, FedIIR) without modifying client-side training, and a theoretical bound (Theorem 2) shows it provably tightens the target-domain error by directly reducing inter-station divergence terms.

## Results

On vision benchmarks (ResNet-18), FedIIR+HFedATM achieves 93.0% on PACS, 82.2% on Office-Home, and 56.9% on Terra Incognita, outperforming the corresponding +Avg baselines by 3.3, 3.3, and 3.8 percentage points respectively. On the NLP benchmark (DeBERTa-base, Amazon Reviews), FedIIR+HFedATM reaches 81.9% versus 77.9% for FedIIR+Avg, a 4.0-point gain. Across all four FedDG baselines and all datasets, HFedATM delivers consistent 2–4% accuracy improvements over naive weight averaging, with the largest gains observed under strong domain heterogeneity (λ=0.0) where inter-station divergence is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFedATM is evaluated as a drop-in station-server aggregation layer on top of four client-station FedDG baselines: FedAvg, FedProx, FedSR, and FedIIR. Against each baseline's naive weight-averaging (+Avg) counterpart, HFedATM wins on every dataset and architecture tested, with gains ranging from ~2% (FedAvg on TerraInc) to ~4% (FedIIR on Amazon Reviews). The largest absolute improvements appear when the client-station method is weakest: FedAvg+HFedATM gains up to 8.8 points on Amazon Reviews under λ=0.0, whereas FedIIR+HFedATM gains a more modest but still consistent 3–4 points. In direct comparison with competing HFL methods (Figure 2), HFedATM integrated with FedAvg or FedSR outperforms both MTGC (multi-timescale gradient correction) and FedRC (Gaussian-weighted robust clustering) on all four benchmarks, while remaining data-free and requiring no intermediate gradient or statistic exchange beyond Gram matrices. The paper also confirms that under strong differential-privacy noise (ε=0.1) on Gram matrices, HFedATM degrades gracefully and still outperforms the corresponding +Avg versions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: hierarchical federated learning (HFedAvg, MTGC, FedRC) and federated domain generalization (FedSR, FedIIR, FedProx, gPerXan). It repurposes two previously independent aggregation ideas—filter-wise optimal transport from Singh & Jaggi (2020) for model fusion, and Gram-matrix-based dataless weight merging from Jin et al. (2022)—and combines them into a single hierarchical aggregation pipeline. The key conceptual departure is that prior FedDG methods assume a single-server topology, while prior HFL methods (MTGC, FedRC) address heterogeneity but either exchange intermediate statistics or multi-layer gradients, violating the data-free constraint natural to domain generalization. HFedATM is positioned as the first data-free hierarchical aggregator with a matching theoretical guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing HFedDG and deriving a hierarchical generalization bound that separates intra-station and inter-station divergence, the paper gives practitioners a clear diagnostic for why naive hierarchical averaging underperforms in DG settings and a principled fix. The method is a practical drop-in layer (no client-side training changes, <10% latency overhead, robust to DP noise on Gram matrices), which lowers the barrier to adoption in real hierarchical deployments. The main limitation is the homogeneous-architecture assumption; extending FOT/RegMean to heterogeneous model families (different depths, channel counts, or even different backbone types across stations) remains open and is likely the most impactful next step for real-world FL where device capabilities vary widely.
