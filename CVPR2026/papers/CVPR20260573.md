## Problem

Existing web interaction trajectory datasets are either too small to validate scaling effects (Mind2Web: 2,350 trajectories), rely on synthetic or sandboxed environments that miss authenticated high-value workflows (WebArena, Explorer), or are proprietary and non-reproducible. There is no open-source, large-scale corpus of human-annotated trajectories from live websites that simultaneously provides multi-modal supervision (visual, structural, and action-level) and supports systematic study of training recipes for GUI agent models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260573/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For spatial grounding, the model receives a viewport screenshot and a low-level instruction (e.g., 'click the sort button') and predicts an action type plus pixel coordinates or bounding box. For long-horizon planning, it additionally receives the global task goal and the full (state, action) history, and outputs an action type, coordinates, and a natural-language chain-of-thought rationale. The reward at each step is a weighted sum of action-type correctness (binary match) and action-content correctness (bounding-box containment for clicks, lexical superset for typed text), with the weight α controlling the trade-off.

## Method

WebChain is built via a three-stage pipeline: (1) constraint-based task synthesis, where a structured functional schema is extracted from each target website to condition an LLM generator, preventing hallucinated tasks; (2) human-in-the-loop trajectory collection using a custom tool that passively logs screenshots, AX trees, DOM snapshots, bounding boxes, CSS selectors, and timestamps for every interaction; and (3) post-processing enrichment that adds Visual Grounding Densification (labeling all interactive elements in the viewport, not just the clicked one) and synthetic Chain-of-Thought rationales generated by a VLM. The key training contribution is a Dual Mid-Training recipe that decouples spatial grounding (via Spatial-Grounding-oriented RLVR, SGRL) from temporal planning (via CoT-SFT), followed by Long-Chain-oriented RLVR (LCRL) post-training. This separation allows each mid-training stage to optimize for its distinct perceptual demands—dense coordinate alignment versus hierarchical planning—before the final RL stage focuses on long-term reward, yielding SOTA results on WebChainBench and public GUI benchmarks.

## Results

The best WebChain-trained model (WebChain-LCRL-7B + SGRL + CoT-SFT) achieves an overall score of 81.4 across six GUI benchmarks, outperforming the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (70.9) by 10.5 points and the GUI-R1-7B baseline (74.2) by 7.2 points. On spatial grounding benchmarks, it reaches 96.2% type accuracy on GUI-Act-Web and 99.5% on OmniAct-Desktop. On the long-horizon WCB-L benchmark, the Dual Mid-Training pipeline (SGRL + CoT-SFT + LCRL) achieves a success rate of 0.658, compared to 0.487 for GUI-R1-3B and 0.603 for direct LCRL without mid-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (Overall 70.9), the best WebChain model (7B + SGRL + CoT-SFT, Overall 81.4) gains 10.5 points, with the largest improvements on GUI-Odyssey (88.7 vs. 55.1 type accuracy) and OmniAct-Desktop (99.5 vs. 84.0). Against GUI-R1-7B (Overall 74.2), which was trained on a different proprietary dataset, WebChain-LCRL-7B + SGRL + CoT-SFT leads by 7.2 points overall, with particularly strong gains on GUI-Act-Web (96.3 vs. 90.9 type) and OA-Web (96.2 vs. 91.2 type). At the 3B scale, WebChain-LCRL-3B + SGRL + CoT-SFT (77.3) surpasses GUI-R1-3B (70.5) by 6.8 points, indicating the advantage is not merely a model-size effect. On the internal WCB-L long-horizon benchmark, the full Dual Mid-Training pipeline (0.658) outperforms both direct LCRL (0.603) and CoT-SFT alone (0.629), confirming that decoupling spatial grounding from planning yields compounding benefits.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the web-agent data landscape by combining three ideas that prior work treated separately: large-scale human annotation on live websites (extending Mind2Web and WebLINX in scale and domain coverage), multi-modal structural supervision via Accessibility trees (borrowed from WebArena's environment design but applied to real, uncontrolled sites), and a constraint-based task synthesis pipeline that prevents LLM hallucination of infeasible goals. On the training side, it departs from the standard SFT-then-RL pipeline used by GUI-R1 and UI-TARS by proposing a Dual Mid-Training recipe that explicitly decouples spatial grounding (SGRL) from temporal planning (CoT-SFT) before final RLVR post-training. The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather repurposes VLA backbones (Qwen2.5-VL) and RLVR infrastructure (AReaL-style) under a new data-centric framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing the largest open-source, human-annotated real-world web trajectory corpus (31.7k trajectories, 318k steps across 428 domains) along with collection tools and a benchmark, WebChain directly addresses the reproducibility crisis caused by proprietary datasets in GUI-agent research. The finding that spatial-grounding mid-training with Reasoner Prompting improves grounding but *hurts* downstream long-horizon planning is a practically useful caution: it suggests the two capabilities require different inductive biases and should not be conflated in a single training stage. Open questions include whether the scaling laws observed on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B hold for larger or architecturally different VLMs, whether the Triple Alignment (visual + AX tree + action) is truly necessary or whether visual + action alone suffices at scale, and whether the human-annotation cost can be reduced without sacrificing the gold-standard quality that distinguishes WebChain from synthetic pipelines.
