## Problem

Existing camouflaged object detection (COD) methods rely on appearance cues (color, texture) that become ambiguous or deceptive under extreme camouflage. While monocular depth estimation (MDE) foundation models provide robust geometric priors, directly applying them to COD causes a task misalignment: MDE reconstructs all scene geometry indiscriminately, failing to isolate the target object when its depth structure is intertwined with the background.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 512×512 or 384×384) as input and produces a binary segmentation mask of the same spatial resolution. Internally, the frozen DAv2 encoder-decoder pipeline generates multi-scale depth features {F1, F2, F3, F4} that are modulated and fused, but no explicit depth map is provided as input—the geometric structure is inferred entirely from the RGB image through the pre-trained MDE backbone.

## Method

DepthSAM adapts the frozen Depth Anything v2 (DINOv2 encoder + DPT decoder) for COD via two components. First, Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapters (SMEA) are injected serially before each Transformer Block in the DINOv2 encoder; each SMEA contains 8 lightweight expert sub-networks and a gating router that selects Top-K=2 experts per input, producing a sparsely weighted residual modulation that steers the frozen decoder's multi-scale outputs from scene-wide geometry toward object-centric structure. Second, the Geometric-Semantic Fusion Module (GSFM) progressively fuses the four decoder feature maps (F4→F1) via cascaded Spatial-Frequency Refinement Modules (SFRM), each operating in dual spatial and FFT frequency domains with four cross-domain multi-head self-attention mechanisms (spatial self-attention, frequency self-attention, geometry-guided semantics, and semantics-guided geometry) to combine fine-grained geometric details with high-level semantic context. The model is trained end-to-end (only SMEA and GSFM parameters are trainable, ~27M for the L variant) with a hybrid BCE + IoU loss plus an auxiliary sparsity loss on the SMEA router.

## Results

On the three primary COD benchmarks at 512×512 input, DepthSAM-L achieves Sα of 0.919/0.920/0.929 and Fβω of 0.906/0.867/0.909 on CAMO, COD10K, and NC4K respectively, surpassing the best competitor CG-COD by 2.3–3.0% in Sα and 4.0–4.3% in Fβω. In zero-shot video camouflaged object detection on MoCA-Mask, DepthSAM-L reaches Sα=78.4 and Fβω=60.9, outperforming the best video-specific method ZoomNeXt by 5.0% in Sα. Cross-domain retraining yields SOTA on DUTS (Sα=0.947), EORSSD (Sα=0.9503), and five polyp datasets (mDice=0.8156, mIoU=0.8654).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthSAM-L (512×512) outperforms all 18 compared methods across all three COD benchmarks. Against the strongest competitor CG-COD [64] (78.9M params), DepthSAM-L (27.45M params) gains +3.0% Sα and +4.3% Fβω on COD10K, and +2.5% Sα and +4.0% Fβω on NC4K, while using roughly 3× fewer trainable parameters. Among traditional COD methods, ZoomNeXt [37] is the closest baseline (Sα=0.898 on COD10K), but DepthSAM-L still leads by 2.2% Sα and 2.9% Fβω. In the zero-shot VCOD setting on MoCA-Mask, DepthSAM-L (Sα=78.4, Fβω=60.9) surpasses video-specific methods TSP-SAM [21] (Sα=68.9) and SAM-PM [35] (Sα=72.8) by 9.5% and 5.6% in Sα respectively, and outperforms the best image-based transfer ZoomNeXt [37] (Sα=73.4) by 5.0%. In cross-domain SOD on DUTS, DepthSAM-L (Sα=0.947) beats Samba [17] (0.932) and VSCode [33] (0.926); in ORSI SOD on EORSSD it leads HFCNet [30] (Sα=0.9449) by 0.54%; and in polyp segmentation it exceeds Spider [69] (mDice=0.7975) by 1.8% mDice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Depth Anything v2 (DAv2) monocular depth estimation foundation model and the sparse Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism from Puigcerver et al. (ICLR 2024), repurposing both for camouflaged object detection. It departs from the dominant COD paradigm of mining weak RGB appearance cues (FEDER, FSPNet, ZoomNeXt, etc.) by instead leveraging 3D geometric structure as the primary discriminative signal. Unlike prior depth-assisted COD methods (RISNet, DSAM) that treat depth as an auxiliary input in a two-stream architecture, DepthSAM directly fine-tunes the MDE encoder with lightweight adapters, framing the core challenge as a 'task misalignment' between scene-level depth reconstruction and object-level segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometric priors from MDE foundation models can substantially outperform appearance-only cues for COD, with the zero-shot transfer to video camouflaged object detection (5% Sα gain over video-specific methods without any video training) being the most compelling evidence of the approach's generalizability. The cross-domain results on SOD, remote sensing, and polyp segmentation suggest the geometric-prior + MoE-adapter recipe may be broadly applicable beyond COD. Open questions include whether the advantage persists in genuinely depth-ambiguous scenes (e.g., flat textures with no depth variation), the computational overhead of the full DAv2 backbone relative to lighter COD-specific models, and whether the SMEA adaptation strategy transfers cleanly to other foundation model families.
