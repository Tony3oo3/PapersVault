## Problem

Simulating cellular morphology changes under chemical or genetic perturbations requires capturing inherently multimodal and heterogeneous response distributions, which discriminative models cannot represent. Existing generative approaches either start from pure Gaussian noise (losing data-manifold guidance at intermediate steps) or rely on deterministic linear interpolation (constraining all trajectories to a one-dimensional subspace), making generated phenotypic paths prone to drift and limiting diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263793/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a multichannel fluorescence microscopy image of a source cell (96×96 pixels, 3 channels for BBBC021 or 6 channels for RxRx1) and a perturbation condition encoded as a molecular fingerprint (for compounds) or one-hot vector (for genes). It outputs the perturbed cell morphology image, learning the full conditional distribution p(s₀|s₁, c) rather than a single point estimate. The source cell image serves as an informative prior at the bridge endpoint rather than being replaced by isotropic Gaussian noise.

## Method

SimuSDB constructs stochastic interpolation paths between source and target cell states using conditional Brownian bridges, where a time-varying diffusion coefficient (σₜ with σ₀=σ_T=0) adds a trajectory penalty term that expands the accessible path space from a 1D line to a d-dimensional manifold while preserving endpoint anchoring. Perturbation-specific guidance is formulated as a stochastic optimal control problem: a control term cₜ is injected into the reverse SDE, and the optimal control strategy cₜ* = g(t)∇ₛ log ψ(sₜ,t) is approximated at each step by sampling candidate next states, estimating final images via single-step Tweedie's formula, and selecting the candidate minimizing the perturbation-specific cost. The score network is a conditional U-Net with FiLM conditioning that jointly encodes the diffusion timestep (sinusoidal + MLP) and the perturbation condition (linear projection), trained via denoising bridge score matching on paired source-target images.

## Results

On BBBC021 (chemical perturbation), SimuSDB achieves FID_o of 16.5 and KID_o of 1.32, outperforming the closest baseline CellFlux (FID_o 18.7, KID_o 1.62) and substantially exceeding PhenDiff (FID_o 49.5) and IMPA (FID_o 33.7). On the more challenging RxRx1 (genetic perturbation, 1,042 perturbation types), SimuSDB records FID_o of 29.9 and KID_o of 2.27, again leading all baselines. On the combined JUMP-cpg0000 dataset, SimuSDB further improves to FID_o 8.7 and KID_o 0.58, confirming consistent gains across perturbation modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimuSDB outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against CellFlux (the strongest baseline, a flow-matching approach), SimuSDB improves FID_o by 2.2 points on BBBC021 (16.5 vs. 18.7) and by 3.1 points on RxRx1 (29.9 vs. 33.0), with KID_o gains of 0.30 and 0.11 respectively; the gap widens on conditional metrics (FID_c: 53.7 vs. 56.8 on BBBC021, 156.8 vs. 163.5 on RxRx1), reflecting the benefit of stochastic optimal-control guidance over deterministic linear interpolation. Against PhenDiff (a pure-noise-initiated diffusion model), SimuSDB achieves roughly 3× lower FID_o on both datasets (16.5 vs. 49.5; 29.9 vs. 65.9), attributed to using the source cell image as an informative prior rather than isotropic Gaussian noise. Against IMPA (a GAN-based style-transfer method), SimuSDB leads by 17.2 FID_o on BBBC021 and 11.7 on RxRx1, with the advantage most pronounced on high-heterogeneity perturbations where GAN training instability causes mode collapse. On the combined JUMP dataset, SimuSDB also edges out CellFlux (FID_o 8.7 vs. 9.0; KID_o 0.58 vs. 0.63), confirming that the stochastic bridge framework scales to multimodal perturbation spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

SimuSDB extends the diffusion bridge framework (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) by replacing deterministic linear interpolation with conditional Brownian bridge stochasticity, drawing on the trajectory penalty formulation from Chen et al. (ICML 2025). For perturbation-specific guidance, it adopts the stochastic optimal control formulation from Huang et al. (ICML 2024), which was originally developed for rule-guided music generation, and repurposes it to steer cellular morphology trajectories toward phenotype-specific targets. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against both pure-noise diffusion models (which lack data-manifold guidance at intermediate steps) and flow matching (which constrains all trajectories to a one-dimensional linear subspace), arguing that the Brownian bridge provides the necessary d-dimensional path diversity while endpoint anchoring preserves biological plausibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a principled, analytically tractable mechanism for injecting biological perturbation constraints into generative cell morphology simulation, potentially reducing the need for months-long experimental screening by enabling reliable in silico prediction of unseen compound or gene effects. The OOD generalization results suggest the learned stochastic transport prior captures transferable morphological dynamics rather than memorizing perturbation-specific mappings, which is critical for real-world drug discovery where novel compounds must be evaluated without prior data. However, the authors acknowledge limitations: de novo prediction (without a source cell image) requires additional adaptation, and performance degrades when training data for specific perturbation types is extremely limited. Open questions include whether the framework extends to population-level modeling via latent variable approaches and whether the optimal control approximation (single-step Tweedie with N candidates) scales efficiently to higher-dimensional or multi-step guidance.
