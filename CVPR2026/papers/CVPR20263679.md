## Problem

Fine-grained video anomaly detection requires not only detecting anomalous events but also identifying their specific categories (e.g., arson vs. explosion). This is hindered by two compounding challenges: intra-class variation across diverse spatio-temporal contexts, and inter-class confusion from shared visual primitives (e.g., fire and smoke appearing in both arson and explosion). Existing approaches—whether supervised with limited per-class samples or CLIP-based—fail to simultaneously address both issues.

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed video clips as input, uniformly sampling frames at 16-frame intervals to form a frame sequence processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder. Training uses only video-level weak supervision: a binary anomaly/normal label and a single dominant anomaly category label per video. At inference, the model produces frame-level anomaly category assignments by thresholding per-frame similarity scores against category text embeddings, yielding both a binary anomaly decision and a fine-grained category label for each anomalous frame.

## Method

Fine-VAD implements a progressive cross-granularity learning paradigm with three hierarchical alignment stages that progressively refine video features from generic anomaly patterns to category-specific semantics. At the coarse level, all anomalous videos are aligned with binary text embeddings (normal/anomalous) using cosine similarity, establishing a unified anomaly space; at the intermediate level, K-means clustering of category text embeddings produces K pseudo macro-categories whose centroids serve as structural regularizers via cross-attention-guided embedding refinement; at the fine level, ground-truth category labels refine representations with a weighted cross-entropy loss that amplifies discrimination within visually related macro-categories. Each stage inherits guidance from the previous one through an attention mechanism that uses the coarser alignment map as query and video features as key-value pairs, forming a continuous semantic evolution. The framework is model-agnostic, validated with CLIP+LGT-Adapter, I3D CNN, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbones, and achieves up to 47.7% relative mAP improvement over prior SOTA on UCF-Crime.

## Results

Fine-VAD achieves an average mAP of 14.99% on UCF-Crime and 31.87% on XD-Violence, outperforming all competing methods across all IoU thresholds (0.1–0.5). On UCF-Crime, this represents a 47.7% relative improvement over the previous best method (ExVAD, 10.15% AVG mAP), and on XD-Violence a 12.9% relative gain over ExVAD (28.23%). The paradigm also generalizes across architectures: FineCNN (I3D backbone) reaches 6.21% on UCF-Crime and FineMLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) reaches 12.97%, both surpassing their respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fine-VAD outperforms all 15 baselines on both UCF-Crime and XD-Violence across every IoU threshold. The most significant margin is over ExVAD [8] (ICML 2025), the previous state-of-the-art MLLM-based method, where Fine-VAD achieves 14.99% vs. 10.15% AVG mAP on UCF-Crime (+47.7% relative) and 31.87% vs. 28.23% on XD-Violence (+12.9% relative), despite not relying on any large multimodal model. Among CLIP-based fine-grained VAD methods, Fine-VAD surpasses VadCLIP [29] (6.68% → 14.99% on UCF-Crime) and AnomalyCLIP [34] (2.64% → 14.99%), confirming that progressive cross-granularity supervision resolves the inter-class confusion that plagues direct CLIP alignment. Against weakly supervised VAD baselines (MGFN, PEL4VAD, UR-DMU, FedCLIP), Fine-VAD leads by 10–13 points on UCF-Crime, highlighting the advantage of category-level supervision over binary anomaly detection. The paradigm's architecture-agnostic nature is validated by FineCNN (I3D backbone, +2.97% over I3D baseline) and FineMLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, +8.47% over the raw MLLM), showing consistent gains regardless of the underlying feature extractor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the progressive/staged training concept from semi-supervised VAD (where it models normality) into a fine-grained discrimination setting, introducing a three-level hierarchy of binary → pseudo macro-category → fine-grained category supervision. It builds directly on CLIP-based VAD methods (VadCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, OVVAD) for feature extraction and temporal modeling, but departs from their reliance on direct text-visual alignment for category discrimination, which the authors argue causes inter-class confusion among visually similar anomalies. The key conceptual departure is treating coarse-grained binary labels as a structural scaffold that progressively constrains the feature space before sparse fine-grained labels are introduced, rather than using all supervision simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-agnostic nature of the progressive cross-granularity paradigm—validated across frozen CLIP, I3D CNN, and a 7B MLLM—suggests it could be a broadly applicable training recipe for any weakly-supervised fine-grained video task with limited per-class data. However, absolute performance remains low (14.99% mAP on UCF-Crime), and the current formulation assumes at most one dominant anomaly type per video, leaving multi-anomaly co-occurrence as a significant open problem for real-world deployment. The finding that a simple frozen ViT-B/16 with progressive supervision outperforms a 7B MLLM on this task also raises questions about whether scale is the right axis of improvement for fine-grained video understanding.
