## Problem

AI-based skin cancer screening models suffer severe performance degradation when shifting from expert dermoscopic images to consumer-grade clinical photos, and existing UDA methods ignore high-level clinical concepts that remain invariant across domains. While foundation models like MONET can now provide dense probabilistic concept scores as privileged information during training, these are unavailable at test time for mobile deployment, creating a 'deployment paradox' that prior PI methods (e.g., DALUPI's two-stage hallucination pipeline) do not adequately address for this medical setting.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes RGB skin lesion images (3×H×W) along with per-image MONET concept probability vectors (one scalar in [0,1] per clinical attribute) as privileged information; at inference, only the image is required. The EfficientNet-B2 backbone produces a 1408-dimensional penultimate feature, and the final output is a two-class softmax probability (melanoma vs. non-melanoma) with a 0.5 threshold for the binary label. The student model replaces the teacher's FiLM module with a lightweight residual edit head, so no external concept scores or EMA updates are needed at deployment.

## Method

CoFiDA-M is a three-stage teacher-student framework. In Stage 1, a MONET-conditioned teacher uses a Confidence-Gated MONET Embedding (CGEM) that weights each concept's learnable embedding by its Gini-impurity-based confidence, producing a 256-dim conditioning vector that drives FiLM affine parameters (γ, β) to edit the 1408-dim visual feature; the teacher is trained with focal loss on labeled source data and a two-view Mean-Teacher consistency loss (symmetric KL on logits plus MSE on edited features and edit vectors) on unlabeled target data, with a dynamic confidence threshold decaying from 0.95 to 0.70. In Stage 2, an image-only student with the same backbone but a residual edit head (u_S = v_S + ψ_edit(v_S)) is distilled to match both the teacher's temperature-scaled logits (τ=2.0) and, critically, the teacher's post-edit feature u_T, not merely its predictions. Orthogonality and soft-norm regularizers on the edit vector prevent FiLM from trivially amplifying class-discriminative directions, ensuring edits encode clinical semantics rather than shortcutting the classifier.

## Results

On the clinical target domain, CoFiDA-M achieves a macro-average AUROC of 67.50%, a +9.11% absolute improvement over the source-only baseline (58.39%) and +5.18% over the strongest test-time method TENT (62.32%). For the critical screening metric of melanoma recall, CoFiDA-M reaches 77.89% on clinical data (+22.12% over source-only) and 84.92% on dermoscopic data (+21.37% over source-only). The method also achieves the highest average AUROC on the dermoscopic domain (76.50%), surpassing IT-RUDA (75.85%) and DALUPI (76.07%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoFiDA-M achieves the highest clinical AUROC (67.50%) among all 14 baselines, outperforming the strongest test-time method TENT by +5.18% and the privileged-information baseline DALUPI by +12.96%. On melanoma recall (clinical), CoFiDA-M reaches 77.89%, marginally exceeding Mean Teacher (77.67%) while simultaneously achieving a far higher AUROC (67.50% vs. 58.67%), indicating a genuine quality-of-separation gain rather than a threshold trade-off. On the dermoscopic domain, CoFiDA-M also leads in average AUROC (76.50%) and recall (84.92%), surpassing IT-RUDA (75.85% AUROC) and DALUPI (76.07% AUROC). Classical UDA methods (DANN, DeepCoral, MMD) and source-free methods (SHOT++, DINE, SFDA, CPD) all fall 8–19 points below CoFiDA-M on clinical AUROC. The multimodal prompt-based method DAMP (55.48% clinical AUROC) and the PI-based DALUPI (54.54%) are both substantially outperformed, confirming that direct FiLM conditioning on concept probabilities is more effective than text-prompt steering or two-stage hallucination for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoFiDA-M sits at the intersection of privileged-information learning for domain adaptation (DALUPI) and concept-conditioned feature modulation (FiLM). It departs from DALUPI's two-stage hallucination pipeline by directly conditioning a FiLM modulator on MONET concept probabilities, and from prompt-based adaptation methods (PØDA, LAGUNA) by using dense per-image probabilistic scores rather than clean text prompts. The distillation stage extends standard knowledge distillation by matching the teacher's post-edit feature representation rather than only its logits, and the Mean Teacher EMA framework is repurposed for target-domain consistency learning with a dynamic confidence threshold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete deployment bottleneck in AI skin-cancer screening: models trained on dermoscopic images fail on consumer clinical photos, and the rich concept metadata that could fix this is unavailable at inference. The privileged-information-plus-distillation recipe is general and could transfer to other medical or industrial settings where training-time annotations are richer than test-time inputs. The main limitation is dependence on the quality and availability of MONET-style concept scores during training, and the authors note that end-to-end concept learning or extension to other domains remains open. Evaluation is limited to a single source-target pair (MILK Dermoscopic → MILK Clinical), so cross-pair robustness of the framework is not yet established.
