## Problem

Generative inbetweening (GI) must synthesize temporally coherent intermediate frames between two widely spaced keyframes under text guidance, but as sequences grow longer and motions more dynamic, the guidance from keyframes and text weakens, causing semantic misalignment, frame inconsistency, and pace instability. Additionally, no benchmark exists to systematically evaluate text-conditioned GI models across diverse challenge categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260373/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB keyframe images (first and last) and a natural-language text prompt, producing a sequence of intermediate video frames (25, 33, 65, or 81 frames total). The keyframes are encoded via CLIP and the text via UMT5, all projected into the DiT context space; the video latent is formed by placing the two keyframes at the temporal extremes with zero-filled frames between, compressed by Wan-VAE. The approach is entirely training-free and plugs into any video DiT pipeline without architectural modification.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary, training-free attention modifications to the DiT backbone. First, Keyframe-anchored Attention Bias (KAB) extracts frame-level cross-attention distributions from the first and last keyframes as 'anchors,' linearly interpolates them per intermediate frame, and adds a small logit bias to cross-attention logits to steer each frame toward the appropriate semantic and temporal cues; it further replaces the baseline's asymmetric cross-attention with three symmetric, isolated cross-attentions (first keyframe, last keyframe, text) that are equally weighted. Second, Rescaled Temporal RoPE (ReTRo) multiplies the temporal RoPE frequencies by a higher scale (s_edge=1.06) at edge frames and a lower scale (s_mid=0.94) at middle frames, sharpening local attention to preserve keyframe fidelity while broadening temporal attention in the interior to improve inter-frame consistency. Both mechanisms are applied selectively (KAB on layers 5–12 and early denoising steps with a cosine-tapered bias strength) to avoid disrupting fine-detail synthesis in later layers and steps.

## Results

On TGI-Bench (81-frame), the proposed method achieves the best scores across all video generation metrics: PSNR 18.17, SSIM 0.6269, LPIPS 0.3818, FID 77.59, FVD 0.2458, and VBench 10.022, outperforming the strongest baseline Wan2.1 (PSNR 17.63, FID 82.90, FVD 0.2769). On the GI-specific metrics (Table 2, 81-frame), the method also leads in semantic fidelity (X-CLIP 0.2292, VQA 0.6752), frame consistency (LPIPS-frames 0.0774, CLIPSIM-frames 0.9881), and human-rated scores (FC 4.38, SF 4.27, PS 4.34 on a 5-point scale). The 65-frame results show the same pattern with consistent improvements over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against five baselines: four SVD-based GI methods (TRF, ViBiDSampler, GI, FCVG) and the DiT-based Wan2.1 FLF2V pipeline. The proposed method wins on every metric in both Tables 1 and 2 across all sequence lengths. Against the strongest baseline Wan2.1, the gains are modest but consistent: +0.54 PSNR, −5.31 FID, −0.031 FVD, and +0.118 VBench on 81 frames, with human-rated scores jumping from 3.50/3.69/3.65 (FC/SF/PS) to 4.38/4.27/4.34. The SVD-based methods (TRF, ViBiDSampler, GI) are substantially weaker, with FID scores 2–3× higher than Wan and visible artifacts (object collapse, blur) in qualitative comparisons. FCVG, which uses motion guidance without text conditioning, is the closest SVD-based competitor (FID 97.01 vs. 77.59 for Ours on 81 frames) but still trails on all metrics. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that ReTRo contributes most to frame consistency (PSNR, FID) while KAB contributes most to semantic fidelity and pace stability, with the combined method achieving the best VBench score (10.022).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wan2.1 FLF2V pipeline (a DiT-based video generation framework) and departs from the earlier SVD-based GI line (TRF, ViBiDSampler, GI, FCVG) that required running diffusion twice and fusing results. Conceptually, it repurposes two well-studied ideas from adjacent domains—cross-attention editing (Prompt-to-Prompt, Attend-and-Excite) for semantic guidance and RoPE frequency scaling (from LLM context extension) for temporal control—and adapts them specifically to the GI setting where two fixed keyframes must be jointly preserved. The paper positions itself as the first to modify DiT self- and cross-attention mechanisms in a training-free manner for text-conditioned GI, filling a gap it identifies in the attention-mechanism design space of video DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TGI-Bench is the first benchmark purpose-built for text-conditioned GI, providing challenge-stratified evaluation (dynamic motion, occlusion, linear motion, near-static) across four sequence lengths, which should accelerate diagnosis of model weaknesses in this under-evaluated area. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of KAB and ReTRo means the techniques can be dropped into any video DiT without retraining, lowering the barrier for improving GI quality. However, the approach is validated only on Wan2.1 with fixed hyperparameters (s_edge=1.06, s_mid=0.94, β taper 0.7→0.3), and generalization to other DiT architectures (HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) or to even longer sequences remains untested. Open questions include whether the specific edge/middle RoPE split and the 5–12 layer gating are optimal, and whether the benchmark's 220-video scale is sufficient for robust conclusions.
