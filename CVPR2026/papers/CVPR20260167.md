## Problem

Local Differential Privacy (LDP) provides the strongest privacy guarantee by protecting data at the source, but has been considered impractical for images because canonical LDP mechanisms (e.g., randomized response) inject overwhelming noise in the 256-valued pixel space, destroying nearly all task-relevant information. This paper argues the utility loss stems not from LDP itself but from applying it to an inappropriate data representation, and that a correct transformation can make LDP practical for standard images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260167/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard 8-bit RGB image (converted to YCbCr color space) and outputs a standard 8-bit RGB image that satisfies per-pixel ε-LDP, requiring zero storage or transmission overhead and no architectural changes to downstream models. Processing a 112×112 image takes approximately 5.5 ms on a consumer Apple M4 chip (232 images/s throughput), making it suitable for on-device deployment. The output is fully compatible with standard vision pipelines (e.g., ArcFace, ResNet classifiers) without any modification.

## Method

LDP-Slicing is a training-free, three-stage pipeline. First, a 1-level Haar DWT decomposes each YCbCr channel and the low-frequency LL band is zeroed (LL-pruning) to remove human-perceptible structure. Second, each 8-bit pixel is decomposed into 8 binary bit-planes (24 planes total across Y, Cb, Cr), and a binary randomized response mechanism is applied independently to each bit with a per-bit-plane privacy budget ε_{c,b}. Third, an optimization-based allocation distributes the total budget ε_total proportionally to √W_{c,b}, where W_{c,b} = w_c · w_b encodes channel importance (w_Y=4, w_Cb=w_Cr=1) and bit significance (w_b=2^{b-1}), ensuring MSB luma bits receive the most budget (least noise). The formal ε-LDP guarantee follows from sequential composition across the 24 bits per pixel and the post-processing immunity of the reconstruction step, with the wavelet pruning consuming no privacy budget as a deterministic pre-processing.

## Results

On face recognition (ε_total=20), LDP-Slicing achieves 96.68% on AgeDB-30, 99.75% on LFW, 91.08% on CPLFW, and 96.02% on CALFW, within 0.1–1.7% of the non-private ArcFace baseline (97.88/99.77/92.77/96.05). It outperforms the only other formal-privacy baselines—PEEP (feature-level, 87.47/98.41/79.58/90.06) and DCTDP (block-level, 94.37/99.48/90.60/93.47)—by 2.3–11.5 points across benchmarks. On image classification, LDP-Slicing decisively outperforms centralized DP-SGD at all budgets ε≤12 on CIFAR-10 and at all budgets on CIFAR-100. In identity-distinguishing attacks, LDP-Slicing's adversary advantage is 1.62–4.5% versus 10.36–12.58% for DCTDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the two baselines with formal DP guarantees, LDP-Slicing dominates: it beats PEEP (feature-level LDP) by 9.2 points on AgeDB-30 and 11.5 on CPLFW, and beats DCTDP (block-level DP) by 2.3 points on AgeDB-30 and 2.55 on CALFW, while providing a strictly stronger per-pixel privacy guarantee (the paper notes its guarantee is ~5× stricter than DCTDP's after budget conversion). Against heuristic methods without formal guarantees, LDP-Slicing trails the top performers (DuetFace 97.93/99.82/92.77/96.10, PartialFace 97.79/99.80/92.03/96.07, MinusFace 97.57/99.78/91.90/95.90) by 0.9–1.1 points on AgeDB-30 and CPLFW, but matches or exceeds them on LFW and CALFW. In identity-distinguishing attacks (Table 2), LDP-Slicing achieves the lowest adversary advantage across all six datasets (0.1–4.5%), substantially below DCTDP (5.65–12.58%), DuetFace (6.97–19.75%), and PartialFace (0.43–16.28%). On image classification, LDP-Slicing outperforms centralized DP-SGD at every tested budget on CIFAR-100 and at all budgets ε≤12 on CIFAR-10, with the gap widening as the budget tightens. The method also incurs zero storage/transmission overhead (×1 image size) versus ×54–×63 for DuetFace and DCTDP, and runs at 5.5 ms per image versus 68 ms for MinusFace.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the classical binary randomized response mechanism (Warner, 1965) but departs fundamentally from prior LDP-for-images work by reframing the problem as one of data representation rather than mechanism design. Prior approaches either rely on a trusted curator (DP-SGD, DP-GANs) or collapse images into lower-dimensional representations (feature vectors, latent embeddings, eigenvectors) to sidestep the cardinality problem; LDP-Slicing instead keeps the full pixel space but decomposes each 8-bit pixel into 24 binary bit-planes (across YCbCr channels), making the binary randomized response directly applicable. The perceptual obfuscation stage draws on the well-known low-frequency/high-frequency asymmetry between human vision and CNNs, while the utility-aware budget allocation is a novel constrained optimization over bit-plane and channel importance weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to provide a formal pixel-level ε-LDP guarantee on standard images without collapsing to learned or handcrafted features, and it does so with negligible overhead (5.5 ms/image, zero storage overhead), making it practical for on-device deployment in sensitive domains like medical imaging. However, the privacy-utility tradeoff remains a significant open question: at the default ε_total=20 used for headline results, the TV-distance bound tanh(ε/2)≈1 renders the formal guarantee nearly vacuous, while at budgets where the guarantee is meaningful (ε≤5.2), face recognition accuracy drops to ~69% on AgeDB-30. The paper acknowledges that composing pixel-level bounds to an image-level guarantee is loose, and extending the approach to video streams or higher-resolution images remains unexplored.
