## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction and tracking methods (e.g., St4RTrack, MonST3R) operate in a pairwise fashion, reasoning over only two frames at a time, which causes error accumulation and yields spatiotemporally incomplete geometry—occluded regions in the current frame are simply missing. No prior work formulates the task of recovering a *complete* 3D geometry at every single timestamp by globally aggregating observations across the entire video sequence, nor provides a benchmark to evaluate such completeness.

## Input / Output

The model consumes N sequential RGB frames (resized so the longer side is at most 518 px, with aspect ratios between 0.5 and 3.4) plus a scalar target timestamp index a. For that target, it outputs a complete per-pixel 3D point map P_i^a ∈ R^{H×W×3} for every input frame i (i.e., points from all frames warped into the target timestamp's coordinate frame), a 9-dimensional camera parameter vector g_i (intrinsics + extrinsics), and a per-pixel depth map D_i. The key distinction from standard reconstruction is that P_i^a includes geometry for regions occluded in frame i but visible in other frames, making the output a union of all observations rather than a single-frame projection.

## Method

Complet4R is a decoder-only transformer initialized from VGGT, where DINOv2 patch tokens from all frames are processed through frame-level and global self-attention. The central architectural novelty is a pair of learnable aggregation tokens: one set t_a^D dedicated to the target timestamp and a shared set t_{/a}^D for all other frames, concatenated with each frame's visual tokens so the network can explicitly route geometric cues toward the chosen target. A DPT-style aggregation head decodes per-frame features into 3D point coordinates aligned to the target timestamp, while frozen camera and depth heads (inherited from VGGT) provide auxiliary supervision. Training uses a novel Focal-Weighted Point Loss that multiplies the standard uncertainty-weighted point error by a focal factor |βe|^γ, dynamically up-weighting misaligned points in highly dynamic or occluded regions. The model is trained end-to-end for 10 epochs (AdamW, cosine schedule, peak LR 1e-5) on 8 A100 GPUs over ~23 hours, with camera and depth heads frozen and only the aggregation head and backbone updated.

## Results

On the proposed 4D Complete Reconstruction benchmark (SAIL-VOS 3D-test), Complet4R achieves a mean Accuracy of 0.50 and mean Completion of 0.26, representing nearly 50% relative improvement in Accuracy and an order-of-magnitude improvement in Completion over St4RTrack (Acc. 0.92–0.94, Complet. 2.67–3.10). On the 3D Point Tracking benchmark (WorldTrack), Complet4R achieves APD scores of 77–81% and EPE of 9.7–18.5 across four datasets (Point Odyssey, Dynamic Replica, Aria Digital Twin, Panoptic Studio), substantially outperforming St4RTrack (APD 68–75%, EPE 12.1–29.7) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Complet4R decisively outperforms St4RTrack [10] on the novel 4D Complete Reconstruction task, reducing mean Accuracy from 0.92–0.94 to 0.50 and mean Completion from 2.67–3.10 to 0.26, while also achieving the best Normal Consistency (0.49 vs. 0.46–0.48). On 3D Point Tracking, Complet4R surpasses St4RTrack by 11–12 APD points and roughly halves EPE across all four datasets (e.g., EPE 16.07 vs. 29.70 on Point Odyssey). Against SpaTracker [49], Complet4R leads by 19–28 APD points and reduces EPE by 2–3× (e.g., 15.99 vs. 104.44 on Dynamic Replica), with SpaTracker's camera-coordinate outputs requiring Procrustes/RANSAC alignment for fair comparison. MonST3R [55] is the weakest baseline, trailing Complet4R by 28–31 APD points and showing EPE values 2–4× higher (e.g., 64.52 vs. 16.07 on Point Odyssey), as it was not designed for world-coordinate tracking and requires post-hoc pose-based transformation. Notably, Complet4R was not specifically trained for point tracking yet still dominates all tracking baselines, suggesting the geometric completion objective inherently captures strong temporal motion consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

Complet4R builds directly on VGGT's decoder-only transformer architecture, inheriting its DINOv2 backbone, camera/registration tokens, and DPT-style prediction heads, while replacing the original point head with a novel aggregation head. It departs from the dominant pairwise paradigm (DUSt3R, St4RTrack, MonST3R) that reasons over only two frames at a time and then stitches results post-hoc, instead introducing aggregation tokens that let a single forward pass globally reason over the entire sequence to reconstruct complete geometry at any chosen timestamp. The focal-weighted point loss is a targeted addition to handle the supervision difficulty of dynamically occluded regions, and the task formulation itself—requiring occluded-region completion at each timestamp—is explicitly positioned as absent from prior 4D reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Geometric Complete 4D Reconstruction as a distinct task with a dedicated benchmark, the paper gives the community a clear target for evaluating whether 4D methods truly recover full scene geometry rather than just per-frame visible surfaces. The finding that dense 3D tracking emerges as a byproduct of complete reconstruction (with SOTA APD/EPE) suggests that geometric completion and motion estimation are more tightly coupled than the field has treated them. However, all evaluation is on synthetic datasets (SAIL-VOS 3D, Point Odyssey, Dynamic Replica, ADT, Panoptic Studio) with a small 44-sequence test split, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world video with uncontrolled motion, sensor noise, and longer sequences. Scalability to very long videos and the computational cost of attending over all frames simultaneously remain practical concerns.
