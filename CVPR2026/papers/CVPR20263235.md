## Problem

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from a 'Memory Trap' under distribution shifts: when object positions or scene properties change, they rigidly replay memorized training trajectories instead of adapting to the updated environment. This failure stems from the end-to-end design's lack of explicit 3D spatial reasoning, and existing remedies—VLM-based planners or RL fine-tuning—are either unreliable in practice or require extensive retraining and reward engineering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263235/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB image, a depth map, a natural-language task instruction, and continuous robot proprioception (end-effector position). GPT-4o decomposes the instruction into sequential sub-goals, Grounded-SAM segments the current target object, and the depth map plus camera intrinsics back-project the 2D mask into a 3D point cloud for SAF construction. The output is a single selected action trajectory (a sequence of joint states over horizon H) along with intermediate end-effector waypoints that anchor the VLA's execution, chosen by minimizing cumulative SAF cost across N×K candidate trajectories.

## Method

AFI is a training-free, model-agnostic plug-in that wraps around any pre-trained VLA backbone. It first detects memory traps via proprioception: intervention triggers when EEF displacement drops below a stuck threshold while the distance to the target centroid exceeds a far threshold. Upon detection, the robot rolls back to the historical position with the lowest SAF cost, then performs a two-stage hierarchical exploration: Stage 1 samples N waypoints from a local neighborhood minimizing SAF cost, and Stage 2 queries the VLA at each waypoint for K diverse action candidates (via different noise seeds for diffusion-based policies). The SAF then acts as a scorer, computing cumulative affordance cost along each candidate's forward-kinematics trajectory and selecting the globally optimal one. The SAF itself is built by fusing a target guidance field (distance-to-centroid) and an obstacle avoidance field (scene occupancy) in a voxel grid, with Gaussian smoothing for smooth gradients.

## Results

On real-world AgileX Piper manipulation tasks, AFI achieves an average success rate improvement of 23.5% over baseline VLA policies (π₀ and π₀.₅) across four tasks under OOD scenarios (position, color, object, and background shifts), with per-task gains ranging from 17.0% (Remove Lid) to 26.0% (Place Carrot). On the LIBERO-Pro simulation benchmark, π₀.₅-AFI improves average success rate from 54.0% to 75.7% on LIBERO-Spatial (+21.7%) and from 56.4% to 73.2% on LIBERO-Object (+16.8%). The ensemble of π₀+π₀.₅ with AFI reaches 89.0% average success on Stack Tape, the highest reported result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AFI consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against π₀, AFI improves average success by 17–26 percentage points across four real-world tasks, with the largest gain on Place Carrot (61.0% → 87.0%) where position-shift success nearly doubles (30% → 65%). Against π₀.₅ on Stack Tape, AFI yields a 21-point gain (61.0% → 82.0%), and the π₀+π₀.₅ ensemble with AFI further reaches 89.0%. On LIBERO-Pro, π₀.₅-AFI surpasses π₀.₅ by 21.7 points on Spatial and 16.8 points on Object subtasks. Compared to the training-free VLM planner ReKep, AFI achieves 87.0% vs. 36.0% on Place Carrot, demonstrating that the hybrid VLA+SAF approach substantially outperforms pure VLM-based planning. Backbone-agnostic evaluations on OpenVLA-OFT (+20%) and SpatialVLA (+25%) confirm that AFI's gains transfer across autoregressive and 3D-aware VLA architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: end-to-end VLA models (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA) that suffer from memorized-trajectory failures under distribution shifts, and VLM-based 3D affordance planners (VoxPoser, ReKep, GIGA) that generate spatial value maps but produce unreliable fine-grained motion plans. AFI departs from both by treating the 3D Spatial Affordance Field not as the primary planner but as a lightweight, on-demand intervention plugin: the VLA still generates actions, while the SAF only activates to detect failure (via proprioception), reposition the end-effector, and re-rank candidate trajectories. This hybrid positioning avoids the brittleness of pure VLM planning (prompt engineering, infeasible actions) while compensating for the VLA's lack of explicit 3D spatial reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic design makes AFI immediately deployable as a drop-in module for any pre-trained VLA backbone without retraining or additional demonstrations, which is practically significant given the cost of VLA training. The consistent 17–26% gains across four real-world tasks, two VLA families (flow-matching and autoregressive), and simulation benchmarks validate the approach broadly. However, the method is reactive (intervenes only after a memory trap is detected), relies on GPT-4o for task decomposition and Grounded-SAM for segmentation (introducing latency and API dependency), and the SAF construction is relatively simple (distance-to-centroid + obstacle repulsion), leaving open whether richer affordance representations (e.g., grasp-specific or force-aware fields) would yield further gains. Scaling to highly cluttered multi-object scenes or tasks where the primary failure mode is not spatial mislocalization remains unexplored.
