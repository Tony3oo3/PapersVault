## Problem

While MLLMs excel at static spatial reasoning, their ability to perceive, track, and reason about spatio-temporal dynamics in evolving 4D scenes remains largely unexplored. Existing spatio-temporal benchmarks emphasize scene-level temporal correlations but lack systematic, object-centric evaluation of fine-grained motion tracking and dynamic object grounding, leaving it unclear whether models can maintain coherent representations of moving objects across frames under occlusion, ego-motion, and semantic ambiguity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261022/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 1k dynamic video clips (sourced from 8 real and synthetic datasets) and evaluates models in a zero-shot multiple-choice VQA format across three levels: Dynamic Inter-Object Perception, Dynamic Object-Scene Tracking, and Dynamic Camera-Object Reasoning. For grounding evaluation, region-level MLLMs must output per-frame instance segmentation masks scored by J&F. The ST-TCM auxiliary input is a structured textual description built from reconstructed 3D object trajectories (position, size, orientation in world coordinates), inter-object spatial proximity, and camera-object relations, generated via a rule-based template system from per-frame RGB-D and mask inputs.

## Method

Dyn-Bench is constructed through multi-stage filtering (geometric stability, motion smoothness, image sharpness, depth consistency, VLM-based quality scoring) of 38,507 candidate videos down to 1,000, yielding 7k VQA pairs and 3k grounding annotations. The core analytical contribution is the Spatio-Temporal Textual Cognitive Map (ST-TCM), which reconstructs 3D object trajectories from RGB-D and segmentation masks, models inter-object and camera-object relations based on spatial proximity and motion continuity, and translates all geometric and relational cues into a unified textual representation via rule-based templates; this structured text is fed to Qwen3-VL-235B to generate dynamic-object-centered VQA and grounding pairs. A second contribution is Mask-Guided Fusion, which overlays object segmentation masks on raw frames to direct model attention toward moving entities while preserving temporal continuity. Ablations over ST-TCM components (temporal semantics, motion dynamics, spatial geometry) reveal that motion and spatial cues drive the largest gains for general MLLMs, while motion cues alone suffice for region-level grounding models, and that conventional prompting strategies (CoT, caption-based hints) provide only marginal improvement over these structured integration approaches.

## Results

On Dyn-Bench's spatio-temporal reasoning tasks (Table 3), Qwen3-VL-235B achieves the highest overall accuracy at 65.3%, followed by Qwen3-VL-32B (62.7%) and Qwen3-VL-8B (61.4%), surpassing proprietary models GPT-5 (59.5%) and Gemini-2.5Pro (59.8%). For dynamic object grounding (Table 4), Sa2VA-InternVL2.5-8B leads with a J&F score of 75.6, while UniPixel-7B achieves the best spatio-temporal reasoning among region-level models (58.1%). No single model simultaneously achieves top performance on both reasoning and grounding, confirming the paper's central finding that current MLLMs cannot balance spatio-temporal reasoning with dynamic object grounding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, GPT-4o (50.1%) lags significantly behind GPT-5 (59.5%) and Gemini-2.5Pro (59.8%), with the latter two nearly tied. Open-source Qwen3-VL models dominate the overall ranking: Qwen3-VL-235B (65.3%) outperforms all proprietary models by 5.5–15.2 points, and even the 8B variant (61.4%) exceeds GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5Pro. Spatial MLLMs (SpaceR-7B at 56.5%, VST-7B-RL at 55.7%) show strong object-scene reasoning but are notably weaker on camera-object tasks (48.6% and 45.7% respectively), confirming that spatial priors alone are insufficient for dynamic 4D understanding. For grounding, Sa2VA-InternVL2.5-8B achieves the best J&F (75.6), outperforming UniPixel-7B (65.2) by 10.4 points, yet Sa2VA variants score lower on spatio-temporal reasoning (49.4–53.6%) than UniPixel (55.4–58.1%), illustrating the reasoning–grounding trade-off. VideoGLaMM performs worst overall (30.7% reasoning, 59.6% grounding), suggesting its pixel-level grounding architecture does not transfer well to dynamic scene understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of spatio-temporal video benchmarks (STI-Bench, OST-Bench, VLM4D, DSI-Bench) by being the first to jointly evaluate spatio-temporal reasoning and dynamic object grounding (via instance segmentation masks) within a unified, object-centric framework organized around three complementary levels (Inter-Object, Object-Scene, Camera-Object). It builds on region-level MLLMs (Sa2VA, UniPixel, VideoGLaMM) for the grounding component and on general/spatial MLLMs for the reasoning component, but departs from prior prompting-based approaches by introducing the Spatio-Temporal Textual Cognitive Map (ST-TCM) as a structured geometric-to-textual translation layer and Mask-Guided Fusion as a visual input strategy. The construction pipeline repurposes existing 2D video segmentation and 4D dynamic scene datasets (DAVIS, SA-V, DynamicReplica, PointOdyssey, etc.) with multi-stage filtering to produce a curated benchmark rather than training a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that no current MLLM simultaneously achieves strong spatio-temporal reasoning and accurate dynamic object grounding, and that conventional prompting (CoT, caption hints) yields only marginal gains—highlights a fundamental architectural gap in how MLLMs represent motion and geometry. The demonstration that structured integration (ST-TCM for text, Mask-Guided Fusion for vision) substantially closes this gap provides a concrete recipe for future 4D-aware MLLM design. Open questions include whether the reasoning-grounding tradeoff is inherent to current decoder-only architectures or can be resolved with unified pixel-level and token-level representations, and whether ST-TCM-style structured textual abstractions generalize beyond the controlled benchmark to open-ended real-world video.
