## Problem

Existing video-to-video translation models operate on a single camera view, but embodied AI demonstrations are captured from multiple synchronized cameras (e.g., in-hand, head-mounted, and third-person views). Naively applying single-view V2V independently to each camera produces inconsistent cross-view appearance, and standard cross-view attention scales quadratically, making multi-view translation impractical beyond 3–4 views.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260549/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes K synchronized camera views (K=3 natively), each providing a sketch video and a depth video of shape 1×81×480×640, along with a free-form text prompt defining the target style. It outputs K geometrically consistent RGB videos of shape 3×81×480×640 in the target domain. For K>3, additional views are generated autoregressively by conditioning on a subset of already-produced views. All spatial controls and the Pi3 pointcloud are processed at the VAE latent resolution (11×60×80 per frame).

## Method

VideoWeaver is a rectified-flow DiT that unifies the latent space across views by injecting 4D pointcloud coordinates from the permutation-invariant spatial foundation model Pi3 directly into the noisy latents, establishing explicit cross-view spatiotemporal correspondence without 3D-to-2D reprojection. Architecturally, each DiT block performs factorized 4D attention (intra-view over T×H×W tokens, then cross-view over V×H×W tokens per frame) and a patch-wise Mixture-of-Experts module adaptively fuses depth and sketch controls via a gating network conditioned on the latent state and timestep. Training proceeds in three stages: single-view V2V finetuning, multi-view joint finetuning, and multi-view training with heterogeneous timesteps where some views are frozen at timestep 1 to learn conditional view distributions. This heterogeneous-timestep strategy enables autoregressive generation of additional views beyond the native three. A 3D wavelet consistency loss on predicted latents further enforces spatial coherence, particularly at early denoising timesteps.

## Results

On the single-view Bridge test set, VideoWeaver achieves the best Edge-F1 (0.393) and Depth-siRMSE (0.158) among all methods, indicating superior structural alignment, while matching VACE on VBench (0.837 vs 0.833) and JEDI (1.67 vs 4.39). On the multi-view Droid and Agibot benchmarks, the multi-view variant improves over the single-view model (e.g., JEDI drops from 0.384 to 0.509 on Droid and from 0.23 to 0.22 on Agibot), and achieves the best Edge-F1 (0.376/0.378) and Depth-siRMSE (0.347/0.394) across all baselines. The model trails slightly on Dover (aesthetic/technical quality) and VBench on Droid/Agibot compared to Cosmos-Transfer-1, which the authors attribute to the 8× temporal downsampling in the VAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoWeaver decisively outperforms all four baselines on structural alignment metrics: Edge-F1 is 0.393 vs. 0.345 (Cosmos-Transfer-1), 0.135 (VACE), 0.137 (Control-A-Video), and 0.123 (ControlVideo) on Bridge, and Depth-siRMSE is 0.158 vs. 0.223, 0.258, 0.276, and 0.505 respectively. On realism (JEDI, lower is better), VideoWeaver achieves 1.67 on Bridge and 0.22 on Agibot, substantially beating VACE (4.39/0.93) and ControlVideo (12.04/6.67), and matching Cosmos-Transfer-1 (2.51/0.22) on Agibot. However, VideoWeaver trails Cosmos-Transfer-1 on VBench for Droid (0.736 vs. 0.810) and Agibot (0.720 vs. 0.799), and on Dover across all datasets (e.g., 60.02 vs. 76.34 on Droid), which the authors attribute to the 8× temporal downsampling in the VAE introducing mild blur. VACE, despite having 14B parameters and more training data, is outperformed on Edge-F1 by a factor of ~3× and on JEDI by 2–6×, suggesting its ControlNet-based fusion is less effective for multimodal spatial controls. The multi-view variant further improves over the single-view model on Droid (Edge 0.376 vs. 0.359, Depth 0.347 vs. 0.362) and Agibot (Depth 0.394 vs. 0.468), confirming that cross-view consistency learning provides a measurable benefit.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoWeaver extends the single-view V2V line (ControlVideo, Control-A-Video, VACE, Cosmos-Transfer-1) to the multi-view regime, a setting where no prior V2V method operates. Architecturally, it builds on the MMDiT/rectified-flow backbone and adopts camera-ray embeddings from CameraCtrl, but departs from the standard multi-view generation recipe (cross-view attention + ray embeddings) by grounding all views in a shared 4D pointcloud from Pi3, a permutation-invariant spatial foundation model. The heterogeneous-timestep training strategy, inspired by multimodal co-generation (Omniflow), repurposes the noise-timestep axis to simultaneously learn joint and conditional view distributions, enabling autoregressive extension beyond the native three views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework that can synchronously resimulate multi-camera robot demonstrations into new visual domains while preserving cross-view 3D coherence, directly enabling domain randomization for multi-view policy learning without additional data collection. Key limitations include a fixed frame length with no temporal autoregressive mechanism, residual inconsistencies on small objects due to latent downsampling, and a native cap of three views (extended only autoregressively). Open questions include whether the 4D pointcloud unification generalizes to arbitrarily large view counts, whether temporal self-conditioning can be integrated, and whether the approach transfers to non-robotic multi-view video editing.
