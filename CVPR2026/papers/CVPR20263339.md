## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D shape completion methods (ComPC, GenPC, SDS-Complete) assume the partial input can be rendered into at least one geometrically complete 2D image, which fails when missing regions are visible from every viewpoint. Meanwhile, latent-generative 3D foundation models introduce a domain gap: even when a complete shape and its partial input share identical geometry in overlapping regions, their latent encodings differ significantly, making direct latent-space completion unreliable. The paper addresses both limitations by proposing a framework that operates directly in the latent-spatial space of 3D foundation models without any rendering assumption.

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial 3D point cloud S_p ∈ R^{k×3} in any of three partiality patterns (single-view depth scan, random crop, or missing semantic part) and an optional text prompt for guided completion. The partial input is voxelized into a binary occupancy grid aligned with the spatial domain of the foundation model's decoder. The output is a completed 3D point cloud S_c ∈ R^{k×3} decoded from the refined latent, produced in approximately 20 seconds per shape without any training or fine-tuning of the underlying foundation model.

## Method

LaS-Comp operates within the iterative denoising loop of a pre-trained latent flow-matching model (TRELLIS or Direct3D-S2), applying two complementary stages at each timestep. The Explicit Replacement Stage (ERS) decomposes generation into a clean branch (predict noise-free latent, decode to spatial domain, replace observed voxels with partial input geometry, re-encode) and a noisy branch that uses a Partial-aware Noise Schedule (PNS) to apply asymmetric stochasticity—minimal perturbation for observed regions and pure Gaussian noise for missing regions—then interpolates both branches to form the updated latent. The Implicit Alignment Stage (IAS) performs a single-step gradient optimization on the latent using a BCE geometry-alignment loss computed only on the masked (observed) regions, smoothing boundary discontinuities between observed and synthesized geometry. This explicit-spatial-replacement-plus-implicit-latent-refinement design bridges the latent-spatial domain gap while remaining fully training-free and compatible with any latent-generative 3D foundation model.

## Results

On the Redwood benchmark (10 real-world objects), LaS-Comp with TRELLIS achieves an average CD of 1.42 and EMD of 1.84, outperforming the best prior zero-shot method ComPC (1.95/2.59) by 27.2% in CD and 29.0% in EMD, and GenPC (1.74/2.88) by 18.4% in CD and 36.1% in EMD. On the synthetic dataset (12 objects), TRELLIS-based LaS-Comp reaches 1.11/1.41 (CD/EMD), beating ComPC (1.61/2.09) by 31% in CD. On the proposed Omni-Comp benchmark across three partial patterns (Single Scan, Random Crop, Semantic Part), LaS-Comp achieves 2.21/3.15, 2.60/3.31, and 3.30/3.68 respectively, improving over ComPC by 49.6% in CD and 39.4% in EMD on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaS-Comp (TRELLIS) achieves the best average CD/EMD on Redwood (1.42/1.84) and Synthetic (1.11/1.41), beating all eight baselines. Among zero-shot generative-prior methods, it outperforms ComPC [28] by 27.2% in CD on Redwood and 31% on Synthetic, and surpasses GenPC [38] by 18.4% in CD on Redwood. PCDreamer [62] is the closest competitor on Redwood (1.88/3.41) but degrades significantly on the Omni-Comp benchmark's Random Crop and Semantic Part patterns (3.78/4.55 and 3.88/4.98), where LaS-Comp maintains 2.60/3.31 and 3.30/3.68. Supervised methods (SVDFormer, AdaPoinTr) and unsupervised methods (Shape-Inv, P2C) are consistently 2–3× worse in CD, confirming the advantage of leveraging 3D foundation model priors. On the fidelity metrics (UCD/UHD) for sparse KITTI LiDAR and noisy ScanNet inputs, LaS-Comp (1.4/4.5 on KITTI) outperforms ComPC (1.1/5.7) in UHD while matching or exceeding all other methods. On diversity (MMD/TMD), LaS-Comp achieves the best TMD (1.35 on Redwood) while maintaining competitive MMD, indicating diverse yet accurate completions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior zero-shot completion methods (SDS-Complete, ComPC, GenPC) that rely on rendering the partial input into at least one geometrically complete 2D view, by instead operating directly in the latent space of VAE-based 3D foundation models (TRELLIS, Direct3D-S2). The key conceptual departure is identifying a 'latent-spatial gap': even when a partial and complete shape share identical geometry in overlapping regions, their latent encodings diverge significantly, making naive latent-space conditioning unreliable. The two-stage design (Explicit Replacement in spatial domain + Implicit Alignment via gradient-based latent refinement) and the Partial-aware Noise Schedule are novel mechanisms inspired by FlowDPS's clean/noisy branch decomposition, adapted specifically to the asymmetric nature of shape completion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the rendering-completeness assumption, this work opens the door to applying latent-generative 3D foundation models to completion scenarios that were previously intractable (irregular occlusions, missing semantic parts visible from all viewpoints), and the training-free, model-agnostic design means the approach can be re-targeted as new foundation models emerge. The Omni-Comp benchmark addresses a real evaluation gap by testing diverse partial patterns beyond single-scan, which prior benchmarks ignored. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scene-level completion, how it behaves with very sparse inputs (e.g., <100 points), and whether the one-step IAS optimization is sufficient for high-frequency geometric details or whether multi-step refinement would help in practice.
