## Problem

Vision language models perform substantially worse on rare or uncommon objects than on common ones, as evidenced by attention analyses showing LLaVA-1.5-7B allocates less attention to target rare-object regions in middle decode layers. Existing remedies—stronger vision encoders, retrieval-augmented finetuning, or class-balanced training—require computationally expensive VLM finetuning and risk losing original pretrained knowledge, leaving no efficient solution that adapts frozen VLMs to rare-object-centric reasoning without retraining the backbone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a VQA-style text prompt (e.g., 'describe the object inside the red rectangle') as input, processed through a frozen VLM's vision encoder and tokenizer. At inference, it produces a text answer with object reasoning, but internally also generates two auxiliary signals: refined visual tokens (M×D matrix, refined only at the first decode layer of the frozen VLM) and a top-k (k=3) set of detected object class labels appended to the prompt as '[Detected: {classes}]'. The entire pipeline runs on a single RTX 4090 GPU, adding only ~4 ms of inference latency over the baseline VLM.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play module built around learnable multi-modal class embeddings that fuse VFM visual features (DINOv3/SAM) with LLM-generated synonym-augmented text descriptions, trained via a cross-modal alignment loss and a class-discrimination loss with EMA updates. These embeddings drive two complementary enhancement paths: (1) a lightweight 8-head cross-attention adapter (1024-dim, 33.6M params) that refines frozen VLM visual tokens using class embeddings as keys/values, trained with a reconstruction loss to preserve the original token distribution plus an autoregressive loss; and (2) an object-aware detection step that computes cosine similarities between VFM patch features and class embeddings to select top-k candidate objects, which are injected as textual hints into the prompt. The key design choice is that the VLM backbone remains entirely frozen—only the adapter and class embeddings are trained—making the approach architecture-agnostic and applicable to LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL3 without any backbone modification.

## Results

On CODA-LM (GPT score, higher is better), the method achieves 72.8 on LLaVA-1.5-7B (+26.3 over baseline), 75.4 on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (+7.5), 74.2 on InternVL3-8B (+8.8), and 71.2 on LLaVA-1.5-13B (+20.4). On GeoBench-VLM, gains are +12.3 (LLaVA-7B, 33.2), +11.9 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 39.3), +10.8 (InternVL3-8B, 48.2), and +11.2 (LLaVA-13B, 34.9). The best variant (Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Ours) reaches 75.4 on CODA-LM, within 2.3 points of the fully finetuned CODA-LM model (77.7) while requiring no VLM finetuning. Gains are largest on rare categories: Barrier +29.0, Other +28.1, VRU +15.6 on LLaVA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four frozen VLM baselines (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B), the method consistently wins on the All metric by +7.5 to +26.3 points, with the largest gains on LLaVA-7B (+26.3) and LLaVA-13B (+20.4). Against the fully finetuned CODA-LM model (77.7 All), the best variant (Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Ours, 75.4) trails by only 2.3 points but surpasses CODA-LM on Barrier (79.8 vs. 78.7) and Cone (91.7 vs. 86.2); it also beats MPDrive (76.1) by a narrow margin on several categories while losing 0.7 on All. Against MiniDrive (66.3 All), the method wins by +9.1 points. Against the two training-free baselines (Jiang et al. 46.7, ControlMLLM++ 47.0), the method dominates by +28.4 to +28.7 points on All, showing that lightweight class-embedding adaptation far outperforms prompt-only strategies. On GeoBench-VLM, the method also surpasses the LoRA-finetuned LLaVA-7B (34.7) when using stronger backbones (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 39.3, InternVL3-8B: 48.2), though the LLaVA-7B variant (33.2) falls slightly short due to the extremely limited training data (361 pairs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two main lines of work: (1) methods that improve VLM visual representations by swapping in stronger vision encoders or aligning visual tokens with vision foundation models during full VLM finetuning, and (2) retrieval-augmented or class-balanced training approaches that address rare-object imbalance. It departs from both by introducing a frozen-VLM, plug-and-play module built on learnable multi-modal class embeddings that fuse DINOv3/SAM visual features with LLM-generated synonym-augmented text descriptions, then apply a lightweight cross-attention adapter and selective text-hint injection at inference. The resampling strategy for imbalanced classes is borrowed from long-tail learning literature [31], and the interpretability analysis leverages the attention/logit lens framework from [15].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency argument: a 33.6M-parameter adapter consuming 0.6% of total training compute recovers most of the gains from full task-specific finetuning (75.4 vs. 77.7 on CODA-LM), making rare-object adaptation accessible without expensive VLM retraining. The dual-mode design (visual refinement + selective text hints) is validated as complementary through ablation, and the finding that naively appending all class labels as hints actively degrades performance (e.g., Sign: 48.8→33.1) while top-k detected hints help is a useful practical caution. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary settings beyond a fixed set of rare classes, whether it generalizes to video or multi-image reasoning, and how the method behaves when the rare-object training set is extremely small (as in GeoBench-VLM's 361 training pairs, where gains are more modest).
