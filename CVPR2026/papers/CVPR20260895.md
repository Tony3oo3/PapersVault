## Problem

Existing HOI methods are split into two disjoint families: generation models synthesise scenes from triplets and layouts but cannot handle mixed conditions (HOI triplets alongside object-only entities, arbitrary shape masks), while editing models modify interactions via text but cannot reliably decouple pose from physical contact, scale beyond a single interaction, or provide fine spatial control. No unified framework bridges these modalities, and multi-HOI editing remains entirely unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a global T5-encoded text prompt, a set of structured HOI triplets ⟨subject, action, object⟩ and/or object-only entities, optional layout (bounding boxes or arbitrary shape masks), and for editing a source image encoded into latents and concatenated with noise. A key design choice is that the action spatial region is defined as the union of subject and object regions (rather than InteractDiffusion's 'between' operator), and modality dropout during training (dropping layout, HOI labels, or text prompt independently) allows the same denoiser to operate under any subset of these conditions. Output is a single RGB image realising all specified interactions with correct spatial placement.

## Method

OneHOI builds a Relational DiT (R-DiT) on the Flux.1 Kontext MM-DiT backbone (trained via LoRA, 10K steps, flow-matching objective) by adding four components that inject explicit interaction structure. (1) Action Grounding provides a semantic action token and a spatial action region (union of subject/object boxes) for each triplet. (2) The HOI Encoder augments each T5 token with learnable role embeddings, fixed sinusoidal instance embeddings, and Fourier box embeddings via a gated residual MLP, giving the model explicit identity of which token plays which role in which interaction. (3) Structured HOI Attention enforces a verb-mediated topology by blocking direct subject↔object attention (forcing information flow through the action token) and constraining HOI→image attention to the corresponding spatial regions when layout is provided. (4) HOI RoPE assigns each interaction instance a unique positional index in the RoPE space, preventing feature cross-talk between instances in multi-HOI scenes. The model is trained jointly on HOI-Edit-44K (44K identity-preserving editing pairs), SA-1B (object-only layout supervision), and HICO-DET (HOI generation priors) with modality dropout, so a single denoiser handles generation, layout-free editing, layout-guided editing, and mixed-condition control.

## Results

On layout-free HOI editing (IEBench), OneHOI achieves the best Editability-Identity (0.638) and HOI Editability (0.596), improving over the strongest prior InteractEdit by +10.0% and +16.0% respectively, while also attaining the highest image quality scores (HPS 0.2805, ImageReward 0.4713). On HOI generation (HICO-DET), OneHOI leads on Spatial score (0.6104 vs. 0.5768 for InteractDiffusion) and ImageReward (0.5224 vs. −0.3194), with a +33.2% gain over the strongest prior. For the novel layout-guided multi-HOI editing task (MultiHOIEdit), OneHOI establishes the first baseline with Editability-Identity 0.435 and Spatial 0.675 across 2–3 simultaneous interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OneHOI outperforms all 12 open-source baselines on every metric in the layout-free HOI editing benchmark (Table 1), with the largest margins over HOI-specific methods: +16.0% HOI Editability over InteractEdit (0.596 vs. 0.514) and +189% ImageReward (0.4713 vs. 0.1630). It also surpasses the closed-source Nano Banana on Editability-Identity (0.638 vs. 0.623) and HOI Editability (0.596 vs. 0.530). For layout-guided single-HOI editing (Table 2), OneHOI beats the InteractEdit+InteractDiffusion pipeline on all three editing metrics (EI 0.638 vs. 0.559, HOI Edit 0.570 vs. 0.520, Spatial 0.822 vs. 0.749). In HOI generation (Table 3), OneHOI leads InteractDiffusion on Spatial (0.6104 vs. 0.5768) and ImageReward (0.5224 vs. −0.3194), while slightly edging it on HOI accuracy (0.4528 vs. 0.4505); it also outperforms object-level methods (GLIGEN, MIGC++, InstanceDiffusion, Eligen) by 5.8–17% on Spatial score. The multi-HOI editing task has no prior baseline, so OneHOI serves as the first reference point.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneHOI unifies two previously disjoint research threads—layout-conditioned HOI generation (InteractDiffusion) and text-guided HOI editing (InteractEdit, HOIEdit)—into a single conditional denoising process on a DiT backbone. Architecturally, it extends Eligen's layout-conditioned DiT with four relational modules (Action Grounding, HOI Encoder, Structured HOI Attention, HOI RoPE) and is implemented as a LoRA adaptation of Flux.1 Kontext's MM-DiT. The Action Grounding module explicitly departs from InteractDiffusion's 'between' operator in favor of a union-based spatial region, motivated by attention-map analysis showing the former misaligns with where action tokens actually attend.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that joint generation-and-editing training produces a 'synergy effect' where generative priors (contact patterns, verb-object geometry) transfer to editing robustness, the paper suggests that task-specific HOI models may be unnecessarily narrow. The introduction of multi-HOI editing as a solvable task—achievable without explicit multi-HOI edit training pairs, relying instead on multi-HOI generation priors plus HOI RoPE for instance separation—opens a path toward compositional scene editing that was previously intractable. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 2–3 interactions, whether the PViC-based evaluation adequately captures physical plausibility, and whether the LoRA-based training (10K steps) generalizes to broader interaction vocabularies or 3D/4D settings.
