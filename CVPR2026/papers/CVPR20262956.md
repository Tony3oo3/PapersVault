## Problem

Existing scanpath-based 360° IQA methods treat scanpath generation and quality assessment as decoupled, separately trained modules, typically supervised by human gaze data or heuristics. This separation prevents the scanpath policy from learning task-relevant exploration strategies aligned with the IQA objective, limiting both in-dataset accuracy and cross-dataset generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262956/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 360° equirectangular projection (ERP) image with no reference, discretizes the viewing sphere into 8×4=32 candidate viewports (each 90°×90° FOV, rendered at 224×224), and produces a scalar perceptual quality score. At inference, K=15 scanpaths of T=7 viewport steps are sampled and their individual quality predictions are averaged to yield the final score. All visual features are extracted by a frozen DINOv2 encoder (1024-dim global descriptor and per-viewport features).

## Method

RL-ScanIQA formulates viewport selection as a finite-horizon MDP solved with PPO, where a GRU-based policy conditioned on DINOv2 global and candidate-viewport features selects viewports sequentially. The policy is trained with a composite multi-level reward combining step-wise exploration cues (Shannon entropy, SSIM dissimilarity, novelty, equator-bias prior), a set-level diversity bonus (coverage minus pairwise Jaccard overlap across K scanpaths), and task-aligned perceptual signals (negative MSE and pairwise ranking from the quality assessor). The quality assessor aggregates viewport features via attention-weighted pooling modulated by global context, then regresses a score per scanpath through an MLP. Joint end-to-end training eliminates the need for human scanpath annotations, and distortion-space augmentation with similarity-consistency, triplet, and cross-rank losses further improve cross-dataset robustness.

## Results

On the three 360° IQA benchmarks, RL-ScanIQA achieves the highest PLCC across all datasets: 0.902 on JUFE, 0.967 on OIQA, and 0.970 on CVIQD. On CVIQD it also attains the best SRCC (0.970), while on JUFE and OIQA the top SRCC is held by GSR-X (0.843) and Assessor360 (0.979) respectively, though RL-ScanIQA leads in PLCC by 4.5 and 2.2 points. In cross-dataset evaluation, RL-ScanIQA achieves the best PLCC in all four train/test splits (e.g., 0.822 when trained on CVIQD and tested on JUFE; 0.833 when trained on JUFE and tested on OIQA), outperforming the next-best method by 7–12 PLCC points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL-ScanIQA achieves the best PLCC on all three in-dataset benchmarks, with the largest margin on JUFE (+4.5 over GSR-X) and OIQA (+2.2 over Assessor360). On CVIQD it leads both SRCC and PLCC, surpassing VGCN (0.946/0.932) and Assessor360 (0.958/0.963). The closest competitors are GSR-X (generative scanpath, IEEE TIP 2025) and Assessor360 (recursive probability scanpath, IEEE TIP 2023), which each win SRCC on one dataset but trail in PLCC, indicating RL-ScanIQA's superior absolute-score calibration. In cross-dataset transfer, RL-ScanIQA dominates all four splits in PLCC (e.g., 0.822 vs. 0.749 for Assessor360 on CVIQD→JUFE; 0.833 vs. 0.679 for F-VQA(A) on JUFE→OIQA), while GSR-X is the strongest SRCC competitor in the JUFE→CVIQD direction (0.782 vs. 0.771). Handcrafted methods (NIQE, BRISQUE) and general-purpose blind IQA models (MANIQA, TreS, Clip-IQA) perform substantially worse on 360° content, confirming the necessity of viewport-aware and scanpath-based evaluation for panoramic images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scanpath-based 360° IQA line initiated by Fan et al. (CVPR 2024) and Sui et al. (IEEE TIP 2025), which both generate scanpaths as a separate preprocessing step supervised by human gaze or generative modeling. The key departure is formulating scanpath generation as a PPO-optimized sequential decision process whose reward signal comes directly from the downstream quality assessor, enabling end-to-end task-aligned exploration without human scanpath annotations. The quality assessor itself builds on DINOv2-based feature extraction and attention pooling, while the cross-dataset robustness mechanism (distortion-space augmentation with rank-consistency losses) is a novel regularization strategy not present in prior 360° IQA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint RL formulation provides a principled way to align viewport exploration with the IQA objective, and the ablation showing that learned scanpaths outperform human ground-truth trajectories (0.816 vs 0.724 SRCC on JUFE) challenges the assumption that human gaze is the optimal proxy for quality-relevant viewing. Cross-dataset generalization remains a practical bottleneck for 360° IQA deployment, and the distortion-space augmentation strategy offers a transferable recipe. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 360° video (temporal scanpaths), scales beyond 32 viewports, and whether the RL policy's learned exploration strategies are interpretable or robust to unseen distortion types.
