## Problem

Large-scale pre-trained ViTs, particularly vision-language models like EVA-CLIP-8B and SigLIPv2, produce spatially misaligned 'spurious tokens' that severely degrade dense prediction performance, causing these models to underperform smaller vision-specialized backbones on segmentation and depth estimation. Prior work treated spurious tokens narrowly as high-norm outliers, but this paper shows they are heterogeneous (three distinct types) and abundant (over 40% of tokens in 8B models), making simple outlier removal or unconstrained registers insufficient.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262355/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained ViT (400M–8B parameters) and a small 5k-image subset of CC3M as training data. During training, each image is augmented via random resized crops, concatenated with an independently sampled reference image for spurious-token detection, and surrounded by Gaussian-noise patches that serve as register bias tokens. The output is a LoRA-adapted ViT (rank 8) whose feature maps have spurious signals redirected to the boundary register region, yielding spatially consistent representations usable for downstream dense prediction without any architectural change.

## Method

UniRefiner is a post-hoc, self-supervised refinement framework built on a siamese architecture (frozen teacher + LoRA student) with three coupled components. First, a Spurious Token Filter identifies three token types: Fixed Pattern tokens via cross-image cosine similarity, Global Proxy tokens via within-scene similarity against a composite image, and Attention Hijackee tokens via asymmetric cross-layer attention flow (high inflow, low outflow). Second, a Contrastive Register appends Gaussian-noise patches around the input and enforces a dual InfoNCE objective: image tokens are aligned with filtered regular teacher tokens to preserve semantics, while register tokens are aligned with detected spurious teacher tokens to absorb artifacts, supplemented by a uniformity loss that maximizes divergence between image and register representations. Third, as training progresses, the learned registers are recycled as adaptive spurious detectors, iteratively sharpening the filter. The entire pipeline requires only ~2 epochs on 5k images and no structural modification to the ViT.

## Results

On ADE20K linear probing, UniRefiner delivers consistent gains across all tested ViT backbones, with the most dramatic improvement on EVA-CLIP-8B: 42.5% → 51.9% mIoU (+9.4%), surpassing the specialized DINOv2-Giant (49.1%). SigLIPv2-So also surpasses DINOv2 after refinement (49.8% vs. 49.1%). For zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation (Table 2), EVA-CLIP-8B's average mIoU nearly triples from 21.6% to 34.3%, with a +19% gain on Cityscapes. On NYUv2 depth estimation, refined EVA-CLIP-8B reduces RMSE from 0.512 to 0.359 and improves δ₁ from 77.9% to 90.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRefiner is applied as a post-hoc refinement to five pre-trained ViT backbones, and in every case it outperforms the unrefined version. The most striking comparison is EVA-CLIP-8B vs. DINOv2-Giant: the unrefined EVA-CLIP (42.5% mIoU) trails DINOv2 (49.1%) by 6.6 points, but after UniRefiner it surpasses DINOv2 by 2.8 points (51.9% vs. 49.1%), while also improving DINOv2 itself by 1.5 points. SigLIPv2-So (400M params) also overtakes DINOv2-Giant after refinement (49.8% vs. 49.1%), demonstrating that the method unlocks spatial capacity in smaller models as well. On zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation, EVA-CLIP-8B's average mIoU jumps from 21.6% to 34.3% (+12.7), nearly tripling its original performance and closing the gap with CLIP (28.6% → 31.4%). For depth estimation on NYUv2, refined EVA-CLIP-8B achieves RMSE 0.359, approaching DINOv2's 0.347, whereas the unrefined model (0.512) was the worst baseline. In the image generation application (REPA), refined SigLIPv2 achieves FID 1.96, beating both vanilla SigLIPv2 (2.21) and DINOv2 (2.02) on ImageNet 256×256.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the register-token mechanism introduced by Darcet et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2025), which passively append learnable or self-distilled tokens to absorb ViT artifacts. The key departure is twofold: (1) it replaces the narrow 'high-norm outlier' definition of spurious tokens with a three-way taxonomy (Fixed Pattern, Global Proxy, Attention Hijackee) detected via distinct similarity and attention-flow criteria, and (2) it converts the register from a passive sink into an actively trained contrastive target, using a dual InfoNCE objective that explicitly aligns image tokens with regular teacher tokens and register tokens with spurious ones. The refinement is implemented via lightweight LoRA fine-tuning on a siamese self-distillation setup, avoiding any architectural modification to the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a few epochs of LoRA fine-tuning on 5k images transforms large vision-language models (EVA-CLIP-8B, InternViT-6B) from underperforming dense-prediction backbones into ones that rival or surpass specialized vision models like DINOv2, while preserving their multimodal capabilities. This suggests that the spatial potential of existing billion-parameter VLMs has been substantially underestimated and can be unlocked without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the three-type spurious token taxonomy generalizes to non-ViT architectures (e.g., convolutional or hybrid models), whether the approach extends to 3D or video token sequences, and whether the register-based detection loop (Sec. 4.4) can be made fully adaptive without fixed thresholds.
