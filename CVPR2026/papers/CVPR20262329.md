## Problem

Existing Federated Adversarial Training (FAT) methods assume a balanced global data distribution, an assumption that rarely holds in practice due to the prevalence of long-tailed class distributions. Under long-tailed data, FAT suffers severe degradation in both natural and robust accuracy because inter-client class imbalance and global long-tailedness jointly corrupt feature representations and impair classifier discriminability. This paper is the first to systematically diagnose and address FAT under long-tailed distributions.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard classification images (32×32 RGB for CIFAR-10/100, 28×28 grayscale for FMNIST, 32×32 RGB for SVHN) partitioned across K clients with Dirichlet-distributed non-IID splits and a long-tailed global class distribution (imbalance ratio ρ up to 50). During training, each client also generates adversarial samples via 10-step PGD (ε=8/255) alongside natural inputs. At inference, the deployed model consists of the shared feature extractor r paired with the auxiliary classifier h′, producing a single class label per input image.

## Method

FedCART decouples the global model into a shared feature extractor r and a dual-classifier structure (main classifier h for local training, auxiliary classifier h′ for final inference). On the client side, a contrastive feature-alignment loss L_Align enforces consistency between natural and adversarial feature representations, with an adaptive scaling factor γ that strengthens alignment when predictions diverge; additionally, per-class gradient-based prototypes are extracted via the auxiliary classifier. On the server side, models and prototypes are aggregated, then a set of class-balanced virtual features is generated by iteratively minimizing MSE between the virtual features' gradients and the aggregated prototypes. The auxiliary classifier h′ is retrained on these balanced virtual features to eliminate long-tailed classification bias, and only (r, h′) is deployed at inference, all without sharing raw data.

## Results

FedCART achieves the best robust accuracy across all four long-tailed datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline (CalFAT) by 5.73% Robust AVG on CIFAR10-LT (31.71% vs. 25.98%), 3.04% on CIFAR100-LT (14.90% vs. 11.86%), 10.57% on SVHN-LT (70.52% vs. 59.95%), and 4.31% on FMNIST-LT (73.51% vs. 69.20%). On natural accuracy, FedCART leads on CIFAR10-LT (66.12%), CIFAR100-LT (38.19%), and SVHN-LT (90.98%), while CalFAT retains a slight edge on FMNIST-LT (86.75% vs. 85.13%). Under the strongest attack (AutoAttack), FedCART gains up to 7.54% over CalFAT on CIFAR10-LT (27.47% vs. 19.93%) and 7.11% on SVHN-LT (68.06% vs. 54.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedCART outperforms all six baselines on robust accuracy (Robust AVG) across all four datasets. Against the strongest FAT baseline CalFAT, FedCART gains 5.73% Robust AVG on CIFAR10-LT (31.71% vs. 25.98%), 3.04% on CIFAR100-LT (14.90% vs. 11.86%), 10.57% on SVHN-LT (70.52% vs. 59.95%), and 4.31% on FMNIST-LT (73.51% vs. 69.20%). On natural accuracy, FedCART beats CalFAT on three of four datasets (CIFAR10-LT: 66.12% vs. 61.25%; CIFAR100-LT: 38.19% vs. 36.86%; SVHN-LT: 90.98% vs. 85.93%) but trails by 1.62% on FMNIST-LT (85.13% vs. 86.75%). The direct federated extensions of centralized AT methods (FedPGD, FedMART, FedTAET, FedTRADES) are substantially weaker, with Robust AVG values 6–10 points below FedCART on CIFAR10-LT, confirming that naive FedAvg-based adaptation is insufficient under long-tailed Non-IID conditions. FedFAT, the original FAT method, also lags significantly, particularly on CIFAR100-LT where its Robust AVG (12.57%) is 2.33 points below FedCART.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedCART sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: federated adversarial training (FedFAT, CalFAT) and federated long-tailed learning. It repurposes the supervised contrastive loss (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) to align natural and adversarial features on the client side, adapts gradient-based prototype extraction from Luo et al. (NeurIPS 2021) for class-wise update direction capture, and extends the virtual-feature classifier re-training idea from Shang et al. (IJCAI 2022) to the adversarial setting. The key departure from prior FAT work is the explicit decoupling of robust feature learning (client) from unbiased classification (server), which prior methods like CalFAT handle monolithically under a balanced-distribution assumption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of FAT under long-tailed distributions, a setting that is almost certainly the norm in real-world FL deployments (medical imaging, mobile devices, IoT). The clean architectural separation—contrastive alignment on clients, gradient-prototype-driven classifier refinement on the server—offers a reusable template for combining adversarial robustness with distributional fairness in FL. Limitations include evaluation on only four small-scale datasets with at most 20 clients and ResNet-18 backbones, leaving open whether the approach scales to ImageNet-level tasks, deeper architectures, or more sophisticated attack ensembles. The paper also does not explore how the method interacts with other FL security threats (poisoning, backdoor attacks) beyond the stated future-work mention.
