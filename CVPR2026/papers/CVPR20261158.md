## Problem

Chain-of-Thought reasoning has transformed understanding tasks in MLLMs, but its extension to visual generation remains nascent and confined to scenario-specific mechanisms that degrade when applied to broader tasks. Existing approaches require manual intervention to activate CoT for different generation scenarios and lack the advanced reasoning capabilities needed for generalized, think-driven generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261158/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a text prompt (caption or editing instruction) and optionally one or more reference images for editing tasks. The MLLM internally produces a CoT reasoning chain followed by a rewritten generation instruction; the VGI-refine module then extracts only the post- instruction tokens and concatenates K learnable Prepadding States to form the conditional input for the DiT. The DiT outputs a generated image at up to 1024×1024 resolution, with reference images encoded via a VAE and jointly attended with the noisy latent during denoising.

## Method

ThinkGen employs a decoupled architecture pairing Qwen3-VL-8B-Think (MLLM) for autoregressive CoT reasoning with OmniGen2-DiT-4B for diffusion-based image synthesis, connected via a simple linear projection. The key architectural insight is the VGI-refine module, which isolates concise instruction tokens from the MLLM's verbose CoT output and appends learnable Prepadding States to regulate the representation distribution for short prompts. Training proceeds in five stages: three supervised phases (connector alignment, 60M-sample pre-training, 0.7M high-quality fine-tuning) using Rectified Flow, followed by SepGRPO—a separable GRPO paradigm that first optimizes the MLLM with DiT frozen (using scenario-specific rule-model rewards across five generation tasks) and then optimizes the DiT with the MLLM frozen via FlowGRPO. This separable design enables flexible per-module reward design, reduces learning complexity by decoupling instruction quality from image quality, and lowers GPU memory usage during RL training.

## Results

On the primary text-to-image benchmarks, ThinkGen with CoT reasoning (ThinkGen*) achieves 0.89 on GenEval Overall, 85.87 on DPG-Bench Overall, and 0.84 word accuracy on CVTG, setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. On reasoning-intensive tasks, enabling CoT yields dramatic gains: WISE Overall improves from 0.55 to 0.76 (+21%), and RISEBench average jumps from 3.6 to 13.0. On image editing (ImgEdit), ThinkGen* reaches 4.21 overall, matching GPT-4o (4.20) and surpassing all open-source baselines by a wide margin (next best: OmniGen2 at 3.44).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, ThinkGen* (0.89) outperforms BAGEL (0.82), FLUX.1-dev (0.82), and OmniGen2 (0.80), with the largest margin over BAGEL* (0.79) which actually degrades when its own CoT is activated. On WISE (reasoning generation), ThinkGen* (0.76) surpasses BAGEL* (0.70), STAR (0.66), and BLIP3-o-8B (0.62), but remains below the closed-source GPT-4o (0.80). On RISEBench (reasoning editing), ThinkGen* (13.0) beats BAGEL* (11.9) and all other open-source models by a large margin (OmniGen2: 3.0, Step1X-Edit: 1.9), approaching Gemini-2.0 (13.3) but trailing GPT-4o (28.9). On ImgEdit, ThinkGen* (4.21) edges out GPT-4o (4.20) and dominates all open-source baselines, with the nearest competitor OmniGen2 at 3.44. On CVTG text rendering, ThinkGen* (0.84 acc / 0.94 NED) surpasses the dedicated text-rendering model TextCrafter (0.76 / 0.90) and SD3-Medium (0.65 / 0.84), indicating that CoT-driven instruction refinement generalizes well to fine-grained text synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

ThinkGen extends the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 into a separable two-stage design (SepGRPO) that alternates optimization between an MLLM and a Diffusion Transformer, and incorporates Flow-GRPO for the DiT stage. It departs from unified multimodal models (BAGEL, OmniGen2, Janus) by explicitly decoupling the MLLM and DiT rather than fusing autoregressive and diffusion objectives in a single network, and from prior CoT-for-generation work (T2I-R1, MINT, Uni-CoT) by generalizing reasoning across multiple scenarios (semantic composition, reasoning generation, text rendering, editing, reflection) instead of tailoring a single mechanism to one task. The VGI-refine module, which extracts instruction tokens post- and appends learnable Prepadding States, is a novel interface design that addresses the distribution mismatch between verbose CoT outputs and the DiT's conditioning requirements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ThinkGen demonstrates that CoT reasoning, previously confined to understanding tasks, can be systematically leveraged to improve generation quality—particularly on reasoning-intensive benchmarks where it achieves a 3.6× jump on RISEBench and +21% on WISE. The separable training design offers practical advantages (reduced GPU memory, flexible per-module rewards) that make RL-based alignment of MLLM-DiT pipelines more accessible. Open questions include whether the decoupled architecture limits the degree of joint optimization possible, whether CoT latency is acceptable for interactive applications, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation. The ablation showing that SFT on reasoning data fails to improve DiT while GRPO succeeds suggests that the *training signal*, not the data content, is the critical factor for transferring world knowledge into generation.
