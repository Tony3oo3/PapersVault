## Problem

Multimodal cancer risk prediction models assume full availability of all data modalities (gene expression, pathology reports, etc.), but in real clinical settings these auxiliary inputs are frequently missing due to cost, logistics, or tissue constraints. Existing missing-modality methods either reconstruct missing data (risking noise and oversmoothing) or rely on shared-knowledge strategies (losing modality-unique signals), and domain-specific cancer approaches are further limited to a single auxiliary modality, heavily pruned gene sets, or discrete risk categories. This paper tackles the underexplored problem of robust histopathology-based survival prediction when one or more complementary modalities are entirely absent at inference time.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes only a gigapixel whole slide image (WSI, up to 100K×100K pixels) and produces a calibrated patient-specific survival risk score via Cox partial likelihood. During training, it additionally ingests gene-expression profiles (up to 60,000 genes) and free-text pathology reports to build a multimodal vectorized memory and train the teacher-student anchoring. The output risk score supports downstream Kaplan-Meier stratification into high/low-risk groups with Log-Rank significance testing, and the model is evaluated across 24 scenarios spanning eight TCGA cancer types with varying combinations of missing modalities.

## Method

The framework combines retrieval-augmented proxy estimation with factorized aggregation and hierarchical distillation. First, a Multimodal Context Retrieval module encodes all modalities via pretrained foundation models (an in-house AB-MIL-based WSI encoder, a BulkRNABert-style gene-expression encoder, and OpenBioLLaMA-7B for reports) into a vectorized memory, then retrieves top-K morphologically similar patients using WSI embeddings and applies a modality-specific gating-based soft re-ranking to assign fine-grained importance scores. Second, a Factorized Prognostic Context Aggregator decomposes each missing modality's proxy into three components—modality-exclusive, WSI-shared, and cross-modality-shared—via factorized cross-attention over modality-wise tokens, enabling the model to capture both unique and joint prognostic signals. Third, a Hierarchical Anchor strategy uses a fully multimodal teacher to provide two levels of KL-divergence supervision: intra-modality anchors align individual token representations with the teacher's modality-specific features (guiding re-ranking), while inter-modality anchors align the patient-level fused representation (guiding holistic aggregation). The total objective combines the Cox negative partial log-likelihood with weighted intra- and inter-anchor terms, trained end-to-end so that the student learns to generate semantically grounded proxies from WSI alone at inference.

## Results

Across 24 evaluation scenarios (8 TCGA cancer types × 3 missing-modality settings), the proposed method achieves an overall mean C-index of 0.617, surpassing the best SOTA baseline (CycleR, 0.597) by 3.4% and the histology-only baseline (0.569) by 8.5% relative improvement. The gap to the fully multimodal oracle (0.626) is only ~1.4%. For patient stratification, the method achieves statistically significant risk separation in 16/24 cohorts (67%) with a mean hazard ratio of 1.56, compared to 12/24 (50%) and HR 1.48 for the best SOTA (LDVAE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on overall mean C-index (0.617 vs. 0.597 for CycleR, 0.593 for LDVAE, 0.594 for AcMAE/EgoKD, 0.592 for CrossKD, 0.586 for Shaspec), achieving the highest C-index in 16 of 24 individual dataset–modality combinations versus only 4 for the best single baseline. CycleR (generative, TMI'20) is the strongest overall competitor but degrades notably under the most challenging H+G†+R† setting (0.604 avg), while the proposed method actually peaks there (0.621 avg). LDVAE (CVPR'25), the most recent domain-specific baseline, trails by 2.4% on overall C-index and stratifies patients in only 12/24 cohorts (50%, HR 1.48) compared to 16/24 (67%, HR 1.56) for the proposed method. The privileged distillation baselines (EgoKD, CrossKD) and reconstruction/masked approaches (Shaspec, AcMAE) cluster tightly around 0.586–0.594, indicating limited gains from their respective strategies. Notably, the proposed method narrows the gap to the fully multimodal oracle to ~1.4% on average, and in a few cases (e.g., COAD H+R†: 0.682 vs. 0.626) even surpasses it, attributed to noise regularization from soft-ranked retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes retrieval-augmented learning, knowledge distillation, and multimodal fusion into a unified framework for cancer survival prediction under missing modalities, departing from both purely data-centric approaches (imputation, generative reconstruction) and purely strategy-based approaches (attention, prompt tuning) that prior work treats as mutually exclusive. It extends the cancer-specific line of work (e.g., LDVAE, distilled prompt learning) by removing their constraints of single auxiliary modality, sparse gene subsets, and discrete risk categories, instead operating on full-dimensional gene expression and free-text pathology reports with continuous Cox partial-likelihood risk scores. The hierarchical anchor mechanism repurposes teacher-student distillation not for feature transfer but as a semantic alignment signal that simultaneously guides fine-grained re-ranking and coarse patient-level representation, a use not seen in prior missing-modality distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: histopathology is the one modality universally available in clinical workflows, so a method that nearly matches full multimodal performance (1.4% C-index gap) while requiring only WSIs at inference directly addresses a deployment bottleneck in resource-limited settings. The finding that the model performs best under the most challenging setting (both auxiliary modalities missing) is counterintuitive and suggests the richer retrieval space provides more discriminative context than partial modality access. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond TCGA to prospective clinical cohorts, how retrieval pool size and composition affect performance in larger or more heterogeneous populations, and whether the factorized aggregation extends gracefully to additional modalities (e.g., radiomics, clinical variables) beyond the two tested here.
