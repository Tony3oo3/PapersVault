## Problem

State-of-the-art GUI grounding models achieve below 50% accuracy on complex, high-resolution professional software interfaces (e.g., ScreenSpot-Pro). Through a Masked Prediction Distribution (MPD) attribution analysis, the paper identifies that 74% of errors stem not from knowledge gaps but from two inductive biases inherent to MLLM coordinate generation: precision bias (discretized token-level coordinate representation limits spatial resolution) and ambiguity bias (cross-entropy training optimizes edit distance rather than Euclidean distance, causing the model to misrank spatially close candidates). These biases can be mitigated at inference time without retraining the grounding model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language instruction query and a high-resolution GUI screenshot (professional software interfaces with dense, small elements) and produces a bounding box in the format <|box_start|>(x1,y1,x2,y2)<|box_end|> or a click point coordinate. A prediction is correct if the predicted center falls within the ground-truth bounding box. The method is applied as a wrapper around existing grounding models (OS-Atlas-7B, UI-TARS-1.5-7B, TianXi-Action-7B) that natively output either bounding boxes or click points, requiring no modification to the backbone's input/output interface.

## Method

BAMI transforms single-step localization into a recursive, multi-step structured inference process combining two bias-aware manipulations. For precision bias, a coarse-to-fine focus loop iteratively crops the screenshot around the current prediction at a ratio λ∈[0.5,0.7] and re-queries the grounding model, progressively reducing the search space and improving coordinate resolution over 2 iterations. For ambiguity bias, within each iteration the method generates 2–3 mutually exclusive candidate boxes by masking previously predicted regions before each new prediction, then an external correction model (GPT-5 or a locally LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B) re-ranks candidates using GUI-prior prompts that inject Euclidean-space spatial reasoning (functional preference, interactive-component prioritization) to override the grounding model's edit-distance-based selection tendency. The entire pipeline is training-free with respect to the grounding backbone, and the local correction variant matches the grounding model's parameter scale (8B) for privacy-preserving deployment.

## Results

On ScreenSpot-Pro, BAMI (built on TianXi-Action-7B) achieves 57.8% average accuracy, a 5.9-point improvement over the 51.9% baseline and the best result among all 7B-scale models. It outperforms the strongest test-time method (DiMo-GUI-7B at 49.7%) by 8.1 points and the best RL-trained model (GUI-G2-7B at 47.5%) by 10.3 points. When applied to weaker backbones, BAMI yields even larger gains: OS-Atlas-7B improves from 18.9% to 41.6% (+22.7 points) and UGround-7B from 16.5% to 30.0% (+13.5 points). A local Qwen3-VL-8B correction model variant achieves 56.2%, confirming the method's viability without external API dependencies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAMI-7B (57.8%) outperforms all 18 baselines on ScreenSpot-Pro. Among test-time methods, it surpasses DiMo-GUI-7B (49.7%) by 8.1 points and GUI-RC (41.2%) by 16.6 points, establishing a clear advantage over prior inference-enhancement approaches. Against RL-trained models, BAMI exceeds GUI-G2-7B (47.5%) by 10.3 points and SE-GUI-7B (47.3%) by 10.5 points, despite requiring no additional training. Compared to the strongest SFT baseline TianXi-Action-7B (51.9%), BAMI adds 5.9 points purely through structured inference. The gap over proprietary models is substantial: BAMI exceeds Claude Computer Use (17.1%) by 40.7 points and GPT-4o (0.8%) by 57.0 points. Notably, BAMI's gains are consistent across backbones—OS-Atlas-7B improves by 22.7 points and UI-TARS-1.5-7B by 11.1 points—indicating the method's generality rather than dependence on a single strong base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the test-time inference enhancement line for GUI grounding, building on multi-stage localization pipelines (ScreenSeekeR, R-VLM) and divide-and-conquer strategies (DiMo-GUI, GUI-RC), but departs from them by grounding its design in a principled error diagnosis rather than heuristic decomposition. It explicitly rejects language-space reasoning (CoT, query expansion) as ineffective for spatial grounding, aligning with recent empirical findings [33], and instead targets two specific inductive biases—precision bias from coordinate discretization and ambiguity bias from the edit-distance/Euclidean-distance mismatch in autoregressive token generation. The MPD attribution method is a practical alternative to Shapley-value-based explainability, motivated by computational infeasibility at GUI screenshot resolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 74% of grounding failures stem from inductive bias rather than knowledge gaps reframes the problem: models largely 'know' where targets are but systematically mis-select or mis-localize, making inference-time correction a high-leverage intervention. The training-free, backbone-agnostic design (validated on OS-Atlas, UI-TARS, TianXi-Action) lowers adoption barriers, and the local Qwen3-VL-8B correction variant (56.2%) addresses privacy and deployment concerns without requiring API access. Open questions include whether the identified biases persist at larger model scales (14B+), whether the coarse-to-fine strategy interacts adversely with models that already perform internal multi-scale reasoning, and whether the prompt-engineered selection rules generalize to novel GUI domains beyond the six ScreenSpot-Pro categories.
