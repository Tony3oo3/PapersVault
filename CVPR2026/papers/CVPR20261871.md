## Problem

Existing visual-LiDAR odometry methods operate on isolated source-target frame pairs, discarding temporal context from previously observed frames and thus unable to model long-range dependencies. This pairwise limitation leads to uncorrected cumulative drift over extended sequences, degrading localization accuracy in dynamic driving environments. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing a streaming, causal architecture that exploits multi-frame temporal cues and a differentiable online drift compensation mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming LiDAR point clouds (projected onto a cylindrical surface to form pseudo-images of size H_P × W_P × 3) and RGB images (encoded via a convolutional feature pyramid) at each frame, along with camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters used to project LiDAR points onto the image plane for cross-modal sampling. It outputs a relative pose (quaternion + translation) refined through three stages—initial prediction, iterative refinement, and drift-compensated correction—producing a single 7-DoF pose per frame in a causal, online fashion without requiring a global map or loop closure.

## Method

StreamVLO builds a unified MMG (MaxPooling, Mamba, gMLP) module that jointly extracts spatial and temporal features: a Deformable Mamba extends Deformable DETR's adaptive offset sampling to Mamba, using LiDAR features as queries to bilinearly sample visual features around projected reference points, while a Temporal Mamba processes a Memory Feature Bank (historical ego-motion embeddings) and a Memory Pose Bank (historical quaternions and translations) over a sliding window T_h to capture long-term dependencies. A differentiable Cumulative Drift Compensation module then accumulates the refined pairwise poses over T_g frames, warps the source point cloud to the current frame, computes a residual correction via a PWC (Pyramid-Warping-Cost volume) structure, and applies it to the current pose, effectively penalizing drift over the accumulated interval. A Keypoint-Aware Auxiliary Loss with a winner-takes-all strategy selects the top-k cost-volume queries with the smallest pose error relative to ground truth, steering feature learning toward static, high-salience regions. Training is fully supervised with a three-stage regression loss (learnable temperature parameters for L1 translation and L2 rotation), a collective average loss over sub-clips, and the auxiliary keypoint loss, all optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

On KITTI odometry (test sequences 07–10), StreamVLO achieves a mean translational RMSE (t_rel) of 0.59% and rotational RMSE (r_rel) of 0.29°/100 m, reducing errors by 19% and 22% respectively over the previous state-of-the-art multi-modal method DVLO (0.82% / 0.41°). On Argoverse, StreamVLO attains 0.073 m ATE and 0.021 m RPE, outperforming DSLO by 18% in ATE and 16% in RPE. Inference latency is 74 ms on a single NVIDIA 4090, meeting the 100 ms real-time threshold for 10 Hz LiDAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against deep visual odometry methods (SfMLearner, DFVO, Cho et al.), StreamVLO reduces t_rel by ~75% and r_rel by ~60% on sequences 07–10, despite those methods being trained on a larger split (00–08). Among LiDAR-only methods, StreamVLO outperforms EfficientLO (0.86/0.41), DSLO (0.94/0.44), and TransLO (0.99/0.50) on mean t_rel/r_rel, with the largest gap over EfficientLO. In the multi-modal category, StreamVLO beats DVLO (0.82/0.41) by 19% in t_rel and 22% in r_rel, and DVLO4D (0.73/0.37) by 19% in t_rel and 22% in r_rel. Against traditional visual SLAM systems (ORB-SLAM2/3, DROID-SLAM, DPV-SLAM, MambaVO++), StreamVLO achieves a mean ATE of 6.91 m versus 21.84–53.03 m, without requiring loop closure or global optimization. On Argoverse, StreamVLO (0.073 m ATE, 0.021 m RPE) surpasses all geometry-based baselines (LeGO-LOAM, SUMA, PyLiDAR) by over 5× in ATE and the best learning-based method DSLO (0.111/0.027) by 18%/16%. In high-dynamics and high-rotation KITTI scenarios, the gap over DVLO widens to ~48% in t_rel and ~37% in r_rel, confirming the benefit of long-range temporal modeling under challenging motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamVLO extends the visual-LiDAR odometry line (DVLO, DVLO4D, H-VLO) by replacing pairwise frame-to-frame estimation with a streaming, causal multi-frame formulation. Architecturally, it repurposes Mamba's selective state-space mechanism as both a spatial fusion backbone (Deformable Mamba, borrowing the adaptive-offset sampling idea from Deformable DETR) and a temporal memory encoder (Temporal Mamba over feature and pose banks), unified within an MMG block that also uses gMLP for modality-agnostic encoding. The cylindrical point encoding and PWC residual-correction structure are inherited directly from EfficientLO and PWCLO, respectively, while the Cumulative Drift Compensation module is a novel differentiable backtracking mechanism that has no direct counterpart in prior odometry or SLAM literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving competitive ATE against loop-closure SLAM systems (ORB-SLAM3, DROID-SLAM) without any explicit mapping or global optimization, StreamVLO demonstrates that learned temporal memory plus iterative residual correction can substitute for the classical back-end, which is practically significant for real-time, memory-bounded deployment on autonomous vehicles. The cross-dataset generalization gap (32.9% vs. 81.6% ATE degradation from KITTI to Argoverse) suggests the temporal modeling provides genuine robustness beyond dataset-specific feature matching. Open questions include whether the Mamba-based memory banks scale gracefully to horizons well beyond T_h=30 frames, how the method behaves under sensor dropout or severe occlusion, and whether the 74 ms latency holds on embedded hardware rather than a 4090 GPU.
