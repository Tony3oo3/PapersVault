## Problem

Existing MLLMs that accept both audio and visual inputs have not been adequately trained or evaluated on egocentric audio-visual understanding, where dynamic camera motion, self-occlusions, and distinct audio profiles differ fundamentally from exocentric settings. Current egocentric datasets and benchmarks are derived from human narrations that focus on human-object interactions while neglecting environmental context and auditory signals, leaving it unclear whether MLLMs can jointly reason over audio-visual cues in first-person video.

## Input / Output

The model receives egocentric video clips (1–6 minutes, uniformly sampled to 300 frames) with synchronized audio tracks, paired with a text question. Outputs span five task categories: open-ended free-form descriptions (sound-source association, dense and segment-level audio-visual narration) and closed-ended answers (four-option multiple choice for temporal reasoning, binary yes/no for audio-visual hallucination detection). The training corpus EgoAVU-Instruct contains ~3M QA pairs over 9K videos, while the evaluation split EgoAVU-Bench contains 3K manually verified QA pairs over 900 videos with notably longer and more descriptive reference responses (~200 tokens average) than prior egocentric benchmarks.

## Method

EgoAVU is a fully automated, open-source data engine that generates egocentric audio-visual QA pairs through a four-stage pipeline. First, it enhances raw Ego4D narrations by processing each modality independently with specialized MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL for spatial object descriptions, Qwen2.5-Omni in separate video-only and audio-only modes for temporal dynamics), avoiding the modality bias and hallucination observed in joint audio-visual captioning. Second, it filters clips using the Moving-Average Type-Token Ratio (MATTR) on enriched narrations to retain only videos with diverse objects, actions, and sounds. Third, it constructs a Multimodal Context Graph (MCG) via LLaMA-70B that explicitly encodes relationships among interacted objects, background objects, foreground sounds (grounded to visible sources), and background sounds, then parses this graph to produce a unified, temporally aligned audio-visual narration. Finally, it generates QA pairs across five task categories from the fused narrations. The resulting EgoAVU-Instruct dataset is used to fine-tune Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) via LoRA or full fine-tuning with LLaMA-Factory, yielding up to 113% relative improvement on EgoAVU-Bench and transferable gains on EgoTempo and EgoIllusion.

## Results

On EgoAVU-Bench, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Omni (7B, Full) achieves 3.20/5 LLM-as-Judge on Source-Sound Association (SSA), 67.84% accuracy on Temporal Reasoning (TR), and 60.12% on Audio-Visual Hallucination (AVH), compared to the strongest baseline Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) at 1.50/5, 53.20%, and 42.69% respectively. This yields up to 113.3% relative improvement on SSA and 30.8% on AVH. On transfer benchmarks, fine-tuning boosts EgoTempo accuracy from 16.25% to 20.83% (+28.1%) and EgoIllusion from 56.32% to 60.36% (+7.2%), while maintaining EgoSchema performance (67.34% vs. 67.43%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All seven open-source MLLMs (VideoLLaMA2, Baichuan-Omni, Intern-Omni, Phi4-mm, MiniCPM-o, Qwen2.5-Omni 3B/7B) exhibit a consistent vision bias: their SSA LLM-as-Judge scores remain below 1.6/5, and AVH accuracy stays under 43%, indicating they frequently hallucinate or misattribute audio to visual sources. Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) is the strongest baseline, leading on TR (53.20%) and AVH (42.69%), but still lags the fine-tuned model by 14.6 and 17.5 percentage points respectively. The fine-tuned model (OURS Full, 7B) outperforms every baseline on all five tasks, with the largest relative gains on SSA (+113.3%) and AVSN ROUGE-L (+86.5%), confirming that the bottleneck is cross-modal grounding rather than visual perception alone. On transfer benchmarks (Table 4), the LoRA variant slightly outperforms full fine-tuning on EgoTempo (+28.1% vs. +24.4%) and EgoIllusion (+7.2% vs. +7.0%), while both maintain EgoSchema accuracy within 0.1–1.8% of the base model, showing no catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends egocentric video understanding (Ego4D, EgoSchema, EgoTempo, EgoIllusion) by introducing the audio modality, which prior egocentric benchmarks have largely ignored in favor of visual-only or narration-based supervision. It also departs from exocentric audio-visual QA benchmarks (AVQA, OmniBench) whose multi-modal dynamics differ fundamentally from first-person recordings. Methodologically, the key departure from prior MLLM-based data generation is the modular pipeline: rather than prompting a single model with joint audio-visual input (which the authors show fails with 54–68% error rates), EgoAVU processes each modality independently and fuses them through a structured Multimodal Context Graph (MCG), a graph-based representation that makes cross-modal sound-source associations explicit before narration generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoAVU fills a clear gap as the first large-scale egocentric audio-visual dataset suite (3M training samples, 3K verified evaluation samples), providing both a training resource and a diagnostic benchmark for MLLMs in first-person settings. The systematic finding that all seven tested MLLMs exhibit consistent vision bias—neglecting audio cues or misattributing sounds to incorrect visual sources—gives the field a concrete failure mode to target. The 113% relative improvement on EgoAVU-Bench and transfer gains on EgoTempo (+28%) and EgoIllusion (+7%) demonstrate that the data engine is effective, though the authors acknowledge residual noise from open-source MLLM outputs in the training data. Open questions include whether the uni-modal-to-joint-modal self-learning strategy generalizes as base MLLMs improve, and whether the MCG representation can be extended to longer-horizon or multi-agent egocentric scenarios.
