## Problem

Masked image modeling (MIM) has seen limited adoption as an auxiliary task for unsupervised domain-adaptive segmentation due to two key barriers: it modifies the input structure by masking image patches, which is incompatible with standard segmentation architectures like DeepLab and DAFormer, and its pixel-level reconstruction objective conflicts with the semantic segmentation loss. This paper asks whether masked modeling can be reformulated to operate in latent space, aligning its optimization with segmentation while preserving architectural compatibility.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB urban scene images (GTA at 1280×720, SYNTHIA at 1280×760, Cityscapes at 2048×1024) and outputs pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks over 19 (GTA→Cityscapes) or 16 (SYNTHIA→Cityscapes) classes. During training, the encoder's latent feature map is randomly masked at 40% ratio and reconstructed by a lightweight Rebuilder before being fed to the segmentation decoder; at inference the Rebuilder is entirely removed, so the network processes complete images with zero additional overhead.

## Method

MRM replaces pixel-space masking with representation-space masking: the encoder's output feature map is rescaled to a fixed embedding dimension (512 channels, 16×16 spatial), randomly masked with learnable mask tokens, and passed through a minimal Transformer (1–2 blocks) plus a transposed-convolution projector to reconstruct the masked regions. The reconstructed representation is fused with the original via a Hadamard product, then fed into the existing segmentation decoder, where a pixel-wise cross-entropy loss against pseudo-labels provides the training signal—aligning the auxiliary objective directly with segmentation rather than pixel reconstruction. For multi-scale architectures like DAFormer, only the final encoder stage is processed by the Rebuilder, with other scales obtained via simple upsampling to avoid redundant Transformer computation. The entire Rebuilder is discarded after training, introducing no inference cost, and the approach is applied exclusively to target-domain images to act as an adaptive regularizer rather than a generic self-supervised task.

## Results

On GTA→Cityscapes, MRM integrated with MIC achieves the best mIoU of 77.5, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (QuadMix, 76.1) by +1.4 mIoU. On the more challenging Synthia→Cityscapes benchmark, MRM+MIC reaches 68.1 mIoU, outperforming QuadMix (67.5) and GANDA (67.0). Across four representative UDA baselines (DACS, DAFormer, HRDA, MIC), MRM yields an average gain of +2.3 mIoU on GTA→Cityscapes and +2.8 mIoU on Synthia→Cityscapes, with the largest single improvement being +7.5 mIoU for DACS on Synthia→Cityscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRM consistently improves all four UDA baselines it is integrated with. On GTA→Cityscapes, DACS gains the most (+3.8 mIoU, 52.1→55.9), while DAFormer, HRDA, and MIC each gain +1.6 to +2.0 mIoU. On Synthia→Cityscapes, the gains are even larger for weaker baselines: DACS improves by +7.5 mIoU (48.3→55.8), DAFormer by +1.7, HRDA by +1.3, and MIC by +0.8. The strongest combination, MIC+MRM (77.5 mIoU), surpasses the prior best standalone methods QuadMix (76.1) and GANDA (74.5) on GTA→Cityscapes, and also leads on Synthia→Cityscapes (68.1 vs. 67.5 for QuadMix). Notably, MRM's gains are most pronounced on fine-grained categories (traffic sign, rider, motorbike), suggesting it enhances the decoder's semantic discrimination rather than merely improving coarse region detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the masked autoencoder (MAE) [16] reconstruction paradigm—random masking, learnable mask tokens, Transformer-block decoder, and projector—into a latent-space auxiliary task for UDA segmentation. It departs from MIM by operating on encoder features rather than input patches, thereby preserving the standard segmentation pipeline and remaining compatible with both CNN (DeepLab) and Transformer (DAFormer) backbones. It also contrasts with contrastive-learning auxiliaries (e.g., PiPa [8]) that supervise only the encoder, by jointly training encoder and decoder through a pixel-wise classification objective on reconstructed representations, aligning the auxiliary loss with the primary segmentation task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MRM offers a genuinely plug-and-play auxiliary objective with zero inference overhead, making it easy to adopt alongside existing UDA pipelines (DACS, DAFormer, HRDA, MIC) without architectural modification. The finding that MRM is effective only when applied to target-domain images—and that source-side reconstruction can be counterproductive—provides a useful design principle for auxiliary tasks in domain adaptation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other dense prediction tasks (depth, pose), whether the 40% masking ratio and 2-block Rebuilder generalize to larger-scale or multi-domain settings, and whether the gains compound when stacked with other self-supervised auxiliaries.
