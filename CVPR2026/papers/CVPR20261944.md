## Problem

Continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) in medical imaging relies on data replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting, but storing and reusing raw patient images across institutions is infeasible due to privacy and ethical constraints. The paper asks whether knowledge from previous pre-training stages can be preserved solely from the trained model itself—its parameters and normalization statistics—without any access to prior raw data or auxiliary generative models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261944/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At each continual learning stage, the method consumes the current task's unlabeled medical images (2D at 224×224, 3D volumes at 16×192×192, 1D clinical text at length 112) together with the frozen previous-stage ViT/B checkpoint and its running normalization statistics (batch-wise means and variances per layer). It produces a synthetic image pool that substitutes for the replay buffer, and the final output is an updated self-supervised ViT/B model that retains cross-modal knowledge. Notably, no raw images from prior tasks are ever stored or accessed; only model weights and a few scalar statistics per normalization layer are retained.

## Method

InvCoSS generates synthetic images via model inversion: a randomly initialized InvUNet (a dual-stream generator with a lightweight Memory Cache Branch producing multi-scale structural priors and a primary Inversion Branch handling semantically-guided reconstruction, linked by skip connections) maps a low-dimensional noisy latent z to high-dimensional images, supervised by the frozen previous model's MIM reconstruction loss, normalization-statistics matching, total-variation image prior, and a repulsive representation loss. The repulsive loss maintains a persistent feature pool and minimizes squared cosine similarity between each new synthetic image's encoder features and all pooled features, explicitly enforcing diversity without class labels to prevent mode collapse. The resulting synthetic datasets from all prior tasks are aggregated into a sample pool and jointly optimized with the current task data under the standard masked-image-modeling objective, replacing the raw-data replay buffer. InvUNet uses only 7.7M parameters yet outperforms larger bottom-to-top CNN generators (10.6M and 25.9M) on both 2D and 3D modalities, and the entire pipeline reduces storage overhead by up to 590× compared to data-replay baselines.

## Results

Across nine downstream medical tasks spanning five modalities, InvCoSS achieves 89.17 AVG↑ and 25.14 AVG↓, outperforming all data-free continual learning baselines (EWC: 86.27, PackNet: 84.42, CaSSLe: 86.88) and matching or exceeding the data-replay method MedCoSS (89.03 AVG↑, 25.99 AVG↓) without storing any raw data. On CT tasks, InvCoSS achieves the best results: 84.52% ACC on RICORD and 72.14% DSC on LiTS, surpassing MedCoSS by +1.19% and +0.13% respectively. Storage overhead is reduced by 7× to 590× compared to MedCoSS, while maintaining comparable downstream performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InvCoSS outperforms all three data-free continual learning baselines: EWC (86.27 AVG↑) by +2.90%, PackNet (84.42 AVG↑) by +4.75%, and CaSSLe (86.88 AVG↑) by +2.29%, demonstrating that inversion-generated synthetic data is far more effective than parameter regularization or feature retention alone for knowledge preservation. Against the data-replay baseline ER (85.45 AVG↑), InvCoSS achieves a +3.72% improvement, showing that synthetic images from model inversion better capture the original data distribution than random replay subsets. Most critically, InvCoSS matches MedCoSS (89.03 AVG↑) at 89.17 AVG↑ while completely eliminating raw data storage, and even surpasses it on CT tasks (RICORD: 84.52% vs 83.33% ACC; LiTS: 72.14% vs 72.01% DSC), suggesting that inversion-synthesized images may better represent the full data distribution than the 5% clustered replay buffer used by MedCoSS. The key practical advantage is that InvCoSS requires only model parameters and normalization statistics (reducing storage by up to 590×), making it deployable under strict data privacy constraints where cross-institute data transfer is prohibited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) framework established by MedCoSS [54] by replacing its data-replay mechanism with model inversion, a technique previously confined to supervised data-free knowledge distillation (e.g., [8, 23, 55]). The key conceptual leap is adapting inversion from a supervised setting (where class labels guide synthesis) to a self-supervised one (where no explicit supervisory signal exists), necessitating the novel InvUNet multi-scale generator and repulsive representation learning to address high-frequency degradation and mode collapse. The method also draws on U-Net-style skip connections [33] for the generator architecture and on normalization-layer statistics [55] as a distribution-matching prior, but repurposes them in a continual learning context rather than a single-teacher distillation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need to store or transfer raw medical images across institutions, InvCoSS addresses a fundamental practical barrier to multi-site, multi-modal foundation model training in healthcare. The up-to-590× storage reduction and privacy preservation make sequential pre-training feasible under real-world data governance constraints that currently limit CSSL adoption. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long task sequences (beyond the 5 modalities tested), whether synthetic images remain sufficient for downstream fine-tuning (as opposed to pre-training only), and how performance degrades when the inversion model itself has been trained on limited or noisy data. The finding that synthetic images can outperform cluster-based real-data replay at small buffer sizes (1%) suggests inversion may capture distributional structure more faithfully than subsampling, a result worth further investigation.
