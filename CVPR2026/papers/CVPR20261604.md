## Problem

Predicting region-wise correspondences between raw manga line art images is fundamentally difficult because line art lacks the texture and color cues that guide matching in natural images, regions are defined by open or loosely formed hand-drawn contours rather than clean closed boundaries, and no existing dataset provides region-level segmentation annotations. Prior correspondence methods either assume pre-segmented closed regions or rely on two-stage pipelines that fail on realistic hand-drawn manga, leaving the task of directly predicting correspondences from unannotated line art largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of raw, unannotated manga line art images (no prior segmentation or color) as input and produces two outputs: pixel-level region segmentation masks for each image and a set of region-wise correspondences linking matched regions across the pair. Internally, each image is divided into 16×16 patches, and the model outputs a unified 2N×2N similarity matrix encoding both intra-image and cross-image patch similarities, from which region groups and matches are derived via post-processing.

## Method

The framework uses a ViT-B/16 backbone to extract patch-level features, followed by a 4-layer Multiplex Transformer that alternates self-attention (intra-image structure) and cross-attention (cross-image correspondence) to jointly model both relationships in a single forward pass. A cosine-similarity-plus-row-softmax head produces a unified 2N×2N similarity matrix, trained with a CLIP-style sampled contrastive loss that handles the extreme sparsity of the ground-truth correspondence matrix. Post-processing converts patch-level predictions to region-level results: adjacent patches are merged using intra-image similarities while suppressing merges across strong Sobel edges, then a watershed algorithm refines boundaries to align with stroke structure, and finally threshold-based greedy matching on aggregated cross-image patch similarities yields the region correspondence set. The model is trained on 200,000 automatically annotated image pairs from anime clips, using colored line art as auxiliary input for the annotation pipeline.

## Results

On the region-level evaluation, the proposed method achieves 84.44% Region Accuracy on ATD and 78.43% on GenAI, outperforming the Dai et al. baseline (82.94% and 72.36% respectively). At the patch level, the model attains AP scores of 83.5–88.8% for intra-image similarity and 83.5–83.7% for cross-image similarity, with Top-1 cross-image accuracy of 82.51% (ATD) and 67.73% (GenAI). The method also produces far more balanced segmentations (Cluster Ratio 1.41–1.70) compared to the baseline's severe over-segmentation (CR 7.07–14.22), yielding substantially higher mIoU(P→G) (28.5–32.7% vs. 5.7–13.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary quantitative baseline adapts Dai et al. [2] (CVPR 2024) with TrappedBall [20] segmentation. The proposed method wins on Region Accuracy by 1.5 points on ATD (84.44% vs. 82.94%) and by a more substantial 6.1 points on GenAI (78.43% vs. 72.36%), where structural differences between image pairs are larger. On intra-image segmentation quality, the proposed method dramatically outperforms the baseline in mIoU(P→G) (e.g., 31.00% vs. 13.05% on ATD) and Cluster Ratio (1.41 vs. 7.23), indicating the baseline severely over-segments while the proposed method produces balanced, semantically coherent regions. The baseline's higher ARI (64.04 vs. 48.11 on ATD) is an artifact of over-segmentation inflating cluster agreement. SAM [5] is compared only qualitatively in Figure 6(a); it fails on line art by merging distinct semantic parts (e.g., face) with the background due to the absence of color and texture cues, whereas the proposed method produces fine-grained, stroke-aligned regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior line-art correspondence methods (Casey et al. [1], Dai et al. [2], Feng et al. [4]) that all assume pre-segmented closed regions as input, by formulating the problem directly on raw, unsegmented manga line art. Methodologically, it repurposes the Vision Transformer for patch-level structural feature extraction and introduces a Multiplex Transformer that jointly models intra-image and cross-image relationships, trained with a CLIP-style sampled contrastive loss. The annotation pipeline borrows LightGlue for point matching and rule-based color clustering for pseudo-segmentation, but the core contribution is the end-to-end learning of region correspondence without relying on any explicit segmentation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a path toward automating region tracking in manga and animation production (colorization, in-between generation), a task currently done manually by artists. However, region recall remains low (30–35%), indicating the method still struggles with small or inconsistently segmented regions (e.g., hair split into multiple parts), and the evaluation datasets are very small (25 and 40 pairs), limiting statistical confidence. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to highly diverse artistic styles, complex multi-character scenes, and whether the post-processing (watershed + greedy matching) can be replaced by a fully learned segmentation head to recover fine-grained details like individual eyes and eyebrows.
