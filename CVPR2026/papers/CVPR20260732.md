## Problem

Semantic Scene Completion (SSC) has been extensively studied in terrestrial domains like autonomous driving, but no dedicated real-world dataset or benchmark exists for the aerial perspective, leaving downstream applications such as autonomous UAV navigation without 3D scene understanding tools. The standard LiDAR-based SSC data generation pipeline is particularly ill-suited for UAVs due to strict flight regulations, mass and energy constraints, and the increased sparsity of LiDAR point clouds from elevated viewpoints, which yields incomplete and low-quality ground-truth.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes geo-referenced RGB images (5472×3648 or 4000×3000 px) with per-frame intrinsics and SE(3) poses, plus 2D semantic masks annotated on a spatially stratified subset of fewer than 10% of all images. It produces per-frame semantic voxel grids of fixed size 192×128×128 at 0.5 m resolution with 21 semantic classes, per-pixel metric depth maps, and three binary masks (invalid, surface, occluded) following the SSCBench data organization. Ground-truth at 40 m and 30 m altitudes is derived by frustum-culling the 50 m scene-level grid, ensuring cross-altitude consistency.

## Method

The core contribution is a LiDAR-free, camera-based data generation pipeline built on classical SfM+MVS reconstruction, which simultaneously yields a metric 3D point cloud, per-image depth maps, and 2D-3D pixel-to-point correspondences. Semantic labels are transferred from a small annotated image subset (<10%) to the full point cloud via back-projection with multi-view majority voting, followed by inverse-distance-weighted kNN to fill unlabeled regions and a second kNN denoising pass. A class-aware densification stage then processes three disjoint groups differently: instance classes (e.g., vehicles) are clustered with DBSCAN and densified via multi-view silhouette carving (visual hull), ground classes (e.g., road) are surface-reconstructed with Poisson meshing, and other classes are directly voxelized. Finally, per-frame ground-truth is extracted by frustum-culling the scene-level voxel grid using each camera's pose and intrinsics, with binary masks distinguishing invalid, surface, and occluded voxels. This design reduces annotation from tedious 3D labeling to efficient 2D image segmentation while achieving >99% point coverage from <10% annotated images.

## Results

On the OccuFly test set, state-of-the-art SSC methods achieve very low semantic accuracy: DISC reaches 29.52% IoU and 2.04% mIoU, while Symphonies achieves only 13.68% IoU and 0.58% mIoU across all altitudes, indicating that terrestrial SSC models fail to generalize to aerial viewpoints. For metric monocular depth estimation, zero-shot foundation models perform poorly (e.g., DepthAnything2 zero-shot: δ₁=0.002, AbsRel=0.729; DepthAnything3: δ₁=0.000, AbsRel=0.591), but fine-tuning on OccuFly depth maps dramatically improves performance (DepthAnything2-OccuFly: δ₁=0.834, AbsRel=0.134, RMSE=4.844, MAE=4.193). The SILog metric shows a slight degradation after fine-tuning (0.192→0.112 for DA2), indicating a minor trade-off between relative consistency and metric correctness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For SSC, DISC [53] substantially outperforms Symphonies [36] on all altitude splits (e.g., 31.10% vs. 15.88% IoU at 50 m; 2.20% vs. 0.58% mIoU), yet both methods remain far below useful semantic accuracy, confirming a severe domain gap for terrestrial SSC models in aerial settings. For depth estimation, all four zero-shot foundation models (MapAnythingV1.1, Metric3Dv2, DepthAnything2, DepthAnything3) exhibit near-zero δ₁ scores, indicating they cannot produce metrically correct aerial depth without in-domain training. Fine-tuning on OccuFly depth maps yields dramatic gains: DepthAnything2-OccuFly improves δ₁ from 0.002 to 0.834 and AbsRel from 0.729 to 0.134, while Metric3Dv2-OccuFly improves δ₁ from 0.073 to 0.278 and AbsRel from 0.471 to 0.381. DepthAnything3 [51] achieves the best zero-shot SILog (0.043) but still fails on absolute metric accuracy (δ₁=0.000), and cannot be fine-tuned due to lack of training scripts. MapAnythingV1.1 [39] performs worst overall (AbsRel=0.799, RMSE=30.068) and cannot be fine-tuned, as it is designed for 3D reconstruction rather than depth estimation. The consistent pattern across all baselines—strong relative geometry but poor absolute metric scale—highlights that altitude and viewpoint are critical factors requiring targeted aerial adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the LiDAR-centric data generation paradigm that underpins all prior SSC benchmarks (SemanticKITTI, nuScenes, Waymo, Occ3D, SSCBench), replacing multi-sweep LiDAR fusion with a camera-only SfM+MVS reconstruction pipeline. The label-lifting strategy adapts the 2D-to-3D annotation transfer concept from PointSSC [88] to a LiDAR-free aerial setting, while instance densification draws on the classical visual hull formulation of Laurentini [42] combined with multi-view silhouette carving. The paper positions itself as introducing an entirely new domain (low-altitude aerial) for SSC, where no prior real or synthetic dataset existed, and contrasts its metric depth maps against the only other publicly available aerial depth datasets (WildUAV, UseGeo).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OccuFly fills a critical gap by providing the first real-world aerial SSC benchmark, enabling systematic evaluation of 3D scene understanding from UAV perspectives that are essential for autonomous flying applications. The consistent failure of state-of-the-art terrestrial SSC models (≤2% mIoU), 2D foundation models, and 3D depth foundation models (526% metric scale deviation for DepthAnything3) reveals a substantial and previously unquantified domain gap that will guide future research. Key limitations include the assumption of static scenes (excluding dynamic objects), potential temporal inconsistencies across altitude captures, and continued reliance on manual 2D annotation. Open questions include whether dynamic-capable reconstruction (e.g., 4D Gaussian Splatting) can extend the framework, whether robust 2D pseudo-labels can eventually replace manual annotation, and how to bridge the aerial-terrestrial domain gap in SSC models.
