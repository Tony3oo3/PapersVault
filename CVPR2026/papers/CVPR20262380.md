## Problem

Existing physical adversarial attacks rely on visible patches or projected light patterns that expose the attacker's intention and require line-of-sight or physical proximity to the target. No prior work has exploited the camera power supply—a fundamental component of every camera system—as an attack vector for generating invisible, fine-grained adversarial perturbations with controllable optimization within physically realizable signal parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262380/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image, a set of injection signal parameters (carrier frequency, phase, and a per-pixel amplitude sequence), and camera-specific parameters (frame rate, ADC sampling rate, crop ratios, and a 32×32 pixel block grid). It outputs an adversarial image and the optimized injection signal parameters that, when physically injected into the camera's power line, reproduce the perturbation. The perturbation mask is quantized into non-overlapping 32×32 blocks with amplitude and phase discretized into three levels, reflecting the physical constraints of the signal generator.

## Method

The paper first characterizes how an injected signal aliases through the camera's ADC sampling to produce structured stripe patterns, then introduces an amplitude-modulation scheme where a source mask is read row-wise, concatenated into a baseband signal, and modulated onto a carrier to achieve fine-grained spatial control over the perturbation. A differentiable simulation model (Algorithm 1) emulates the physical perturbation by computing per-pixel sinusoidal offsets from the aliased frequency, applying crop and resize operations, and replicating across color channels, enabling gradient-based optimization. For white-box attacks, the authors reformulate the constrained problem using Carlini-Wagner optimization with a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to handle discrete physical parameters; for black-box attacks, they employ differential evolution over a candidate vector encoding frequency, phase, and per-pixel position/amplitude under an L0 constraint. Robustness is enhanced via Expectation over Transformation (EoT), averaging the loss over a distribution of physical distortions (translation, rotation, brightness, noise) during optimization.

## Results

In white-box simulation across seven ImageNet-pretrained models (ResNet-101, Inception-v3, VGG-19, DenseNet-161, ResNeXt-101, SqueezeNet, ShuffleNet), the attack achieves an average ASR of 95.5% at L2 distortion of 7.3 (c=10²), with VGG-19 and SqueezeNet reaching 99.0% and 99.4% respectively. In physical validation on 100 images, the white-box attack achieves 92% ASR (L2=15.2) and the black-box attack achieves 82% ASR (L0=256), confirming the simulation-to-physical transfer. Targeted attacks on semantically affinity class pairs reach up to 100% success rate (e.g., Cup→Mortar) with mean L2 perturbation of 8.67. The attack remains effective at cable lengths up to 10 m and under diverse physical transformations (rotation, brightness, noise, etc.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an attack paper rather than a model-comparison study, so no direct baseline methods are benchmarked head-to-head. The primary internal comparison is between white-box (CW optimization) and black-box (differential evolution) settings: white-box achieves 95.5% average ASR at L2=7.3 in simulation, while black-box reaches 89.7% ASR at L0=256 on VGG-19, a ~6-point gap attributable to the lack of gradient information. The simulated-to-physical transfer shows only modest degradation (98%→92% white-box, 85%→82% black-box), validating the simulation model's fidelity. Compared to the prior power-supply attack (PowerRadio [18]), this work introduces fine-grained amplitude modulation for spatially controlled perturbations and the first targeted optimization framework within a physically realizable parameter space, whereas [18] demonstrated only coarse sensor manipulation. Qualitatively, the attack is positioned as superior to patch-based and optical physical attacks in stealth (invisible perturbation), robustness to pose/angle/illumination, and feasibility under occlusion, though no quantitative head-to-head comparison with those methods is provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the physical adversarial example literature by identifying the camera power supply as a new attack channel, building directly on PowerRadio [18] which first demonstrated that power-ground radiation can manipulate sensor measurements. Unlike prior physical attacks that rely on visible patches, projected light, or electromagnetic interference requiring line-of-sight, this work exploits the ADC aliasing mechanism to create structured, invisible perturbations controllable via amplitude modulation. The optimization framework repurposes standard adversarial tools—Carlini-Wagner for white-box, differential evolution for black-box, Gumbel-Softmax for discrete parameter reparameterization, and EoT for robustness—within a novel constrained setting where the perturbation is parameterized by physically realizable signal parameters rather than free pixel values.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work reveals a practically concerning attack surface: any camera system with an accessible power line is vulnerable to invisible, long-range adversarial manipulation that is robust to pose, angle, and illumination variations. The 92% white-box and 82% black-box physical success rates validate that the simulation-to-physical gap is small, making this a credible threat to deployed CV systems in autonomous driving, surveillance, and industrial inspection. Key open questions include whether defenses exist (e.g., power-line filtering, anomaly detection on sensor readout), whether the attack generalizes to detection and segmentation tasks, and how it scales across diverse camera architectures and power supply designs beyond the single HIKVISION model and six supplementary cameras tested.
