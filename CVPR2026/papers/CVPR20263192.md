## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion models are typically released at a few fixed scales, making it impractical to cover all deployment sizes, yet existing learngene-based initialization methods rely on heuristic, layer-isolated strategies that ignore the cross-layer dependencies and temporal coherence essential to diffusion processes. The paper asks whether a single pre-training procedure can produce a model whose knowledge is factorizable into size-agnostic units, enabling efficient initialization of diffusion models at arbitrary depths without repeated full pre-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263192/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The diffusion model operates on 256×256 RGB images encoded into latent space, conditioned on class labels, and produces denoised latent representations decoded back to images. The knowledge-factorizable pre-trained model outputs shared singular vectors (U_★, V_★) per weight-matrix type and layer-specific diagonal singular-value matrices (Σ_★^(l)); for a new target size, only the compact Σ matrices are re-optimized (≈0.3K steps) while the learngenes remain frozen, after which the model is trained normally for 100K steps.

## Method

FINE factorizes each DiT layer's weight matrix W_★^(l) as U_★ Σ_★^(l) V_★^T, where U_★ and V_★ are shared across all layers of the same type (qkv, o, in, out, adaLN) as size-agnostic learngenes, while Σ_★^(l) is a per-layer diagonal matrix of singular values. During pre-training, the full diffusion loss is minimized under the constraint θ = USV^T, jointly optimizing the shared vectors and layer-specific values so that inter-layer knowledge is captured in the shared components. For initializing a target model of a new depth, U and V are frozen and Σ_tgt is randomly initialized then optimized with a small amount of data in a compact parameter space, requiring only a few hundred gradient steps. This cross-layer weight-sharing mechanism distinguishes FINE from prior SVD-based or layer-isolated learngene methods, which decompose each layer independently and thus cannot flexibly recombine knowledge across model scales.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with DiT-B, FINE achieves FID of 42.33 and IS of 32.34 at depth L10, reducing FID by up to 4.89 and increasing IS by up to 2.11 over the strongest baseline (TLEG). For DiT-L, FINE reaches FID 36.53 and IS 36.79 at L10, with FID reductions up to 4.62. FINE also achieves a 3n× training speedup over full pre-training (300K steps per model vs. 300K + 100K×n total), and on downstream datasets (CelebA, LSUN-Bedroom, LSUN-Church) it reduces FID by 0.28, 2.60, and 2.01 respectively over TLEG while transferring only 35% of parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINE consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every model size and both DiT-B and DiT-L backbones. The closest competitor is TLEG (learngene-based, linear correlation), which FINE beats by 4.57–4.89 FID on DiT-B and 4.13–4.62 FID on DiT-L at L10, while also improving sFID by 2.05 and IS by 2.11. Transfer-based methods (Share Init, LiGO) are the next strongest group but still trail FINE by 4–9 FID; LiGO in particular degrades at deeper sizes (L10–L12) due to structural misalignment from naive weight copying. Distillation/pruning methods (BK-SDM, Laptop-diff) perform poorly at small depths (L4: FID 85–106) and require per-model overhead, whereas FINE needs only ~300 optimization steps for initialization. Heuristic learngene methods (Heur-LG, Auto-LG) and direct initialization (He-Init, Mimetic) are substantially weaker, with FID gaps of 20–50 points at L4. On the classification extension (DeiT, Table 3), FINE also leads TLEG by 2.72–3.04 points top-1 accuracy and outperforms GHN-3 by 16.96 points at DeiT-Ti L4.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINE extends the Learngene framework (Feng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2022, 2023) from classification to diffusion-based image generation, and builds directly on WAVE (Feng et al., 2025) for constraint-based pre-training of factorizable weights. The key departure from prior SVD-based methods (SVDiff, KIND) and existing learngene approaches (Heur-LG, Auto-LG, TLEG) is the introduction of cross-layer weight sharing: rather than decomposing each layer independently or heuristically selecting layers to reuse, FINE constrains pre-training so that singular vectors U and V are shared across all layers of the same type, with only the singular values Σ remaining layer-specific. This reverse-factorization formulation (constraining training to produce factorizable weights, rather than post-hoc decomposing them) is the paper's central methodological contribution over both per-layer SVD fine-tuning and layer-isolated learngene stacking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FINE addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: when no size-matched pre-trained diffusion model exists, practitioners must either train from scratch or use ad-hoc initialization, both of which are costly. The 3n× speedup over full pre-training for n target sizes and the first comprehensive benchmark for learngene-based initialization in diffusion models make this a useful reference point for the field. Open questions include whether the shared-U/V structure generalizes to non-transformer diffusion backbones (e.g., U-Nets), to text-conditioned generation (beyond class-conditioned ImageNet), and to very large model scales where the rank-r approximation may become lossy. The paper also does not explore whether the one-time factorization pre-training cost (300K steps) can itself be reduced or amortized across multiple base architectures.
