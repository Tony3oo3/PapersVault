## Problem

Diffusion-based motion planners suffer from frame-to-frame temporal inconsistency: small scene perturbations cause trajectory drift that accumulates into unstable, uncomfortable driving. Existing remedies either inject history as a static conditioning signal (causing the planner to copy past motion patterns rather than adapt to new contexts) or discard ego-history entirely (sacrificing temporal coherence). The paper asks how to use history in a controllable, time-varying manner that balances stability against real-time responsiveness.

## Input / Output

The planner consumes a vectorized scene context (surrounding agents, static objects, lane geometry), a navigation route, and a 2-second ego-vehicle history trajectory sampled at 10 Hz, with each point encoded as a 4-tuple (x, y, cos θ, sin θ) in an ego-centric frame. The full trajectory is partitioned into N=6 chunks of L=20 points each (the single current state is replicated to fill its chunk), and the model outputs an 8-second future trajectory by stitching predicted future chunks with linear feathering on overlaps. All geometry is z-score normalized using fixed training-set statistics.

## Method

DFP introduces a chunkwise diffusion-forcing training scheme where each trajectory chunk (history, current, future) receives an independently sampled noise level, implementing noising-as-masking so the model learns causal conditioning under mixed availability of past and future information. At inference, a history-annealed classifier-free guidance (CFG) procedure runs two parallel branches: an unguided branch with pure-noise history and a guided branch where ground-truth history is annealed via the schedule (t_s)^β, starting near noise and rapidly returning to the clean signal. The two branch outputs are linearly fused with a tunable weight w, enabling a controllable trade-off between temporal stability and environmental adaptability. The decoder is a DiT-based transformer with per-token sinusoidal time embeddings, adaLN FiLM conditioning, and cross-attention to the scene context, operating in x₀-prediction parameterization.

## Results

On nuPlan Val14 (non-reactive), DFP achieves 90.33% overall score, improving over the Diffusion Planner baseline (89.87%) by +0.46 points; DFP-FM (with Flow Matching sampler) further reaches 92.68%. On Test14-hard, DFP attains 76.91% NR, surpassing CoPlanner (76.82%) and Diffusion Planner (75.99%), while DFP-FM reaches 79.43% NR and 67.94% R. DFP-FM achieves the best reactive score on Test14 (83.59%) and Test14-hard (67.94%) among all learning-based methods. All results are reported on raw model outputs without post-processing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFP substantially outperforms the weaker baselines: PDM-Open (53.53% Val14 NR), UrbanDriver (68.57%), and GameFormer (13.32%) are all far below DFP's 90.33%, with gaps exceeding 20, 22, and 77 points respectively. Against PlanTF (84.27% Val14 NR) and PLUTO (88.89%), DFP gains +6.06 and +1.44 points on NR, and +3.02 and +1.86 on R. The most informative comparison is against Diffusion Planner (the direct base): DFP improves Val14 NR from 89.87 to 90.33 and Test14-hard NR from 75.99 to 76.91, but DFP actually trails on reactive scores (79.97 vs 82.80 on Val14 R; 63.56 vs 69.22 on Test14-hard R). DFP-FM, which swaps the DPM-Solver for a Flow Matching sampler, recovers and surpasses Diffusion Planner on all six metrics, reaching 92.68% Val14 NR and 83.59% Test14 R. Against CoPlanner, DFP edges ahead on Test14-hard NR (76.91 vs 76.82) but trails on Test14-hard R (63.56 vs 64.47), while DFP-FM dominates CoPlanner across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

DFP is built directly on Diffusion Planner (Zheng et al., ICLR 2025), reusing its encoder and DiT decoder architecture while replacing the uniform-noise denoising schedule with chunkwise independent timesteps inspired by the Diffusion Forcing Transformer (DFoT) from video generation. The key conceptual departure from prior diffusion planners is the treatment of ego-vehicle history: rather than discarding it (as in Diffusion Planner, GoalFlow, or Flow Matching planners) or injecting it as a static conditioning signal (as in PlanTF), DFP introduces a history-annealed classifier-free guidance mechanism that provides inference-time, tunable control over how strongly past motion patterns influence the generated future. This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion-based planning and the noising-as-masking paradigm from temporal video generation, adapted to the non-Markovian causal structure of driving.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and well-recognized failure mode of diffusion-based planners—frame-to-frame trajectory instability—by making history influence explicitly controllable at inference via the guidance weight w and annealing exponent β. The ablation showing that point-level diffusion forcing (L=1) degrades performance below the baseline, while chunk-level forcing (L=20) recovers and exceeds it, is a useful practical insight for anyone designing chunked diffusion policies. However, the overall gains over Diffusion Planner are modest (≈2–3 points on nuPlan), evaluation is confined to a single benchmark, and the two-branch inference doubles compute per denoising step. Open questions include whether the history-annealed CFG transfers to longer horizons, reactive multi-agent settings beyond IDM, or other driving benchmarks, and whether the Flow Matching sampler variant (DFP-FM) subsumes the guidance mechanism entirely.
