## Problem

Text-driven 4D scene editing remains under-explored because existing methods edit multi-view video frames independently with 2D diffusion models, causing motion distortion, geometric drift, and incomplete edits. The core difficulty is simultaneously enforcing both multi-view consistency across viewpoints and temporal consistency across time steps during editing, a challenge that 3D editing methods do not face.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 4DGS model (representing a dynamic scene with 16–21 camera views over ~10 seconds) and a single text prompt as input. It operates on multi-view video frames uniformly sampled at 1 FPS (160–210 frames per scene) and produces an edited 4DGS that can render novel 4D views. Unlike prior work relying on Iterative Dataset Update, the edited frames directly optimize the source 4DGS in one pass, and the full editing pipeline runs in ~51 minutes on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

Dynamic-eDiTor is a training-free framework that modifies the attention mechanism of a pre-trained MM-DiT editor (Qwen-Image-Edit) to jointly process multi-view and temporal information. It represents all frames as a unified camera-time grid and introduces two components: (1) Spatio-Temporal Sub-Grid Attention (STGA), which extends MM-DiT's dual-stream self-attention to attend over 2×2 sub-grids of adjacent views and neighboring time steps, applied only to the first ~30 layers (a 'vital layer range') to balance consistency with editing fidelity; and (2) Context Token Propagation (CTP), which globally distributes fused tokens along an asymmetric sliding traversal path using Full Token Inheritance for overlapping regions and RAFT-based optical-flow warping for non-overlapping temporal regions. The resulting spatio-temporally consistent edited frames are then used to directly optimize the pre-trained 4DGS without iterative dataset updates.

## Results

On the DyNeRF multi-view video dataset, Dynamic-eDiTor achieves the highest CLIP directional similarity (0.1849) and CLIP text-image similarity (0.6397) among all methods, indicating superior text-prompt alignment. In the user study (150 participants), it leads on every perceptual metric: Overall Quality (48.95%), Prompt Alignment (56.86%), Multi-view Consistency (48.19%), and Motion Consistency (47.14%), roughly doubling the next-best baseline (CTRL-D at 13.00% overall). On reconstruction fidelity, Dynamic-eDiTor scores PSNR 29.25 / SSIM 0.8064 / LPIPS 0.1006, slightly below CTRL-D (31.06 / 0.8498 / 0.0970) but well above Instruct 4D-to-4D (21.86 / 0.6978 / 0.2145) and Instruct-4DGS (20.62 / 0.6252 / 0.2869).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dynamic-eDiTor outperforms all three baselines on both CLIP-based editing fidelity metrics: CLIPdir of 0.1849 vs. 0.1077 (Instruct 4D-to-4D), 0.1501 (Instruct-4DGS), and 0.1498 (CTRL-D), and CLIPsim of 0.6397 vs. 0.6308, 0.6342, and 0.6141 respectively. In the user study, the gap is even more pronounced—Dynamic-eDiTor's 48.95% overall quality preference is roughly 3.7× that of Instruct 4D-to-4D (27.57%) and 3.8× that of CTRL-D (13.00%), with Instruct-4DGS trailing far behind at 10.48%. The sole metric where Dynamic-eDiTor does not lead is reconstruction fidelity: CTRL-D achieves higher PSNR (31.06 vs. 29.25), SSIM (0.8498 vs. 0.8064), and lower LPIPS (0.0970 vs. 0.1006), reflecting CTRL-D's per-scene fine-tuning of InstructPix2Pix which preserves pixel-level fidelity at the cost of weaker semantic alignment and temporal consistency. Against Instruct 4D-to-4D and Instruct-4DGS, Dynamic-eDiTor wins on every metric including reconstruction (PSNR 29.25 vs. 21.86 and 20.62). The authors attribute the slight reconstruction trade-off to their design choice of prioritizing globally coherent spatio-temporal editing over per-frame pixel accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MM-DiT dual-stream self-attention architecture (Esser et al., 2024) and the Qwen-Image-Edit model into a 4D editing setting by introducing Spatio-Temporal Sub-Grid Attention (STGA), which modifies the image-stream keys/values to jointly attend across a 2×2 camera-time sub-grid, and Context Token Propagation (CTP), which explicitly warps and inherits tokens along a structured traversal path. It departs from prior 4D editing methods (Instruct 4D-to-4D, CTRL-D, Instruct 4DGS) that edit frames independently or require per-scene finetuning of a 2D diffusion model, instead performing training-free joint multi-view-temporal editing and directly optimizing a pre-trained 4DGS without Iterative Dataset Update. The 4DGS representation (Wu et al., 2024) serves as the downstream optimization target, receiving edited frames as supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Dynamic-eDiTor is among the first training-free approaches to jointly enforce multi-view and temporal consistency in 4D scene editing, addressing a gap where prior methods produced motion distortions and geometric drift by editing frames in isolation. The vital-layer-range finding (first ~30 MM-DiT layers) provides a practical, reusable guideline for where spatio-temporal attention is most effective. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (DyNeRF, 6 scenes at 1 FPS), runtime is ~51 minutes per scene on an H100, and the method's reliance on optical flow for token warping may struggle with large non-rigid deformations or occlusions. Open questions include scalability to longer videos, higher frame rates, and whether the sub-grid attention design generalizes to other MM-DiT variants or 3D editing tasks.
