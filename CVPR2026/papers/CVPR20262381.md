## Problem

Category-level object pose estimation is fundamentally limited by the incompleteness of observed point clouds caused by self-occlusion, yet existing approaches either encode directly from partial geometries or rely on category-level shape priors that still operate on incomplete representations in the observation space. Naively cascading a point cloud completion network before a pose estimator introduces compounding errors and significant computational overhead, failing to fully exploit the potential of recovered complete shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB-D image (or depth-only) as input, from which Mask R-CNN segments instance masks and the depth is back-projected and downsampled to 1024 partial points; RGB features are extracted via DINOv2 in the RGB-D setting. Beyond the final 6D pose (rotation, translation) and 3D size, the network also produces a completed dense point cloud of 1024 points and 64 refined keypoints in the observation space, which serve as the geometric basis for NOCS coordinate prediction and subsequent pose fitting via the Umeyama algorithm.

## Method

ComPose unifies shape completion and pose estimation in a single network centered on a keypoint-based progressive completion module: it first generates coarse keypoints from both predicted missing regions and FPS-sampled visible points, adaptively selects the top-64 via a scoring MLP, then progressively refines them through a Transformer decoder with cross-attention to partial features, expanding each keypoint into 16 local geometry points for dense shape recovery. A geometric relation encoding module enriches keypoint features with local (kNN from the partial cloud) and global (pairwise keypoint) geometric context. A novel geometric relation consistency loss enforces alignment of pairwise L2 distances between scaled observed keypoints and predicted NOCS coordinates, capturing higher-order structural cues beyond conventional point-to-point regression. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with Chamfer Distance supervision on the completed shape, score supervision on keypoint selection, and the combined correspondence and relation consistency losses, requiring no external shape priors.

## Results

On the REAL275 dataset under the depth-only setting, ComPose achieves 77.8% on the 10°2cm metric, outperforming the strongest prior-free baseline AG-Pose (68.5%) by 9.3 percentage points, and 55.6% on 5°2cm vs. AG-Pose's 48.8%. Under the RGB-D setting, ComPose reaches 81.8% on 10°2cm and 62.1% on 5°2cm, setting new state-of-the-art results across all 6D pose metrics without relying on shape priors. On the more challenging HouseCat6D dataset, ComPose achieves 57.8% on 10°2cm (RGB-D) and 34.8% on 10°2cm (depth-only), surpassing GCE-Pose and AG-Pose respectively. The method also runs at 38.4 FPS on an RTX 3090Ti GPU, maintaining efficiency advantages over two-stage completion-then-pose pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the depth-only setting on REAL275, ComPose decisively outperforms all prior-free baselines: it beats AG-Pose [15] by 9.3% on 10°2cm (77.8 vs. 68.5) and 6.8% on 5°2cm (55.6 vs. 48.8), and surpasses Query6DoF [37] by 9.1% on 10°2cm. Among prior-based methods, ComPose also exceeds DR-Pose [45] (67.7% on 10°2cm) and RBP-Pose [43] (63.1%) by 10.1% and 14.7% respectively, despite not using any shape priors. Under the RGB-D setting, ComPose edges out SpotPose [29] (81.5% on 10°2cm) by 0.3% and CleanPose [16] (78.3%) by 3.5%, while leading all prior-based methods including GCE-Pose [10] (75.6%) by 6.2%. On the harder HouseCat6D benchmark, ComPose surpasses GCE-Pose [10] by 2.4% on 10°2cm (57.8 vs. 55.4) in RGB-D and AG-Pose [15] by 8.9% (34.8 vs. 25.9) in depth-only. In the occlusion-robustness test (25% external occlusion), ComPose degrades by only 12.6% on 5°5cm compared to AG-Pose's 16.5% drop, confirming its superior robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

ComPose builds directly on AG-Pose's keypoint-based correspondence framework and SpotPose's NOCS-based pose fitting paradigm, while adapting AdaPoinTr's adaptive keypoint selection and progressive refinement strategy for shape completion. The key conceptual departure is from prior-based methods (SPD, SGPA, GCE-Pose) that inject category-level shape priors in canonical space: ComPose instead performs shape completion directly in the observation space under arbitrary poses, eliminating the need for external CAD-model autoencoders. It also departs from naive two-stage completion-then-pose pipelines by integrating completion as a task-driven internal module whose keypoints simultaneously serve as geometric queries for pose estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The oracle experiment (68.5% → 91.7% on 10°2cm with ground-truth complete point clouds) quantifies a large untapped upper bound that prior methods leave on the table, and ComPose recovers a substantial portion of this gap without shape priors. The unified framework's efficiency advantage (38.4 FPS vs. 21.5 FPS for the two-stage variant) makes it practically deployable, and the geometric relation consistency loss offers a generalizable principle for enforcing structural coherence in correspondence-based pose fitting. Open questions include whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or unseen categories, whether the CAD-model supervision requirement for completion can be relaxed, and whether the keypoint-based completion generalizes to highly deformable or articulated objects beyond the 6–10 rigid categories tested.
