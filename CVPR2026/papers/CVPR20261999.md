## Problem

Applying vision foundation models (VFMs) to cross-domain few-shot semantic segmentation is hindered by two compounding issues: the handful of labeled exemplars per novel class is far too few relative to VFM pre-training scale, making retraining prone to overfitting, and target-domain shifts fall outside pre-training coverage, causing cross-domain inconsistency and pronounced layerwise sensitivity in frozen features. Most existing CD-FSS methods rely on CNN backbones and require source-data retraining, which is costly, source-dependent, and limited in long-range reasoning under distribution shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K-shot support images with pixel-level binary masks and a single query image, all at 400×400 resolution, and produces a pixel-level segmentation mask for the query. Critically, it operates in a source-free test-time adaptation setting: no source-domain data or target-domain training is used, and in the 1-shot regime two augmented views are synthesized from the single support via soft copy-paste to stabilize adaptation. Only 8.39M parameters (2.69% of DINOv3) are updated per episode, with peak VRAM of 4.2 GB.

## Method

HERA is a three-stage select-regularize-calibrate pipeline that adapts a frozen ViT backbone at test time. First, Hierarchical Layer Selection (HLS) computes a per-episode Exemplar Transfer Risk (ETR) via leave-one-out mIoU over candidate layers 12–23, then forms local-fusion candidates anchored at the best single layer (always including layer 23 as a global context anchor) and routes to the lowest-risk candidate. Second, Prior-Guided Regularization (PGR) injects head-wise Gaussian priors into the QK⊤ logits at the selected layer, with bandwidth set by an entropy gate that sharpens the prior for locally confident heads and diffuses it for globally dispersed ones. Third, Pixelwise Adaptive Calibration (PAC) adds three lightweight residual logits—feature similarity, one-hop attention propagation, and image appearance—to the base logit, correcting thin boundaries and low-contrast regions. The only trainable parameters are the selected layer's MLP.FC (or fusion-MLP.FC), optimized with a leave-one-out BCE loss over K−1 iterations, keeping the rest of the backbone frozen.

## Results

HERA with DINOv3 achieves 68.3/77.9 mIoU (1-shot/5-shot) across four cross-domain few-shot segmentation benchmarks (FSS-1000, DeepGlobe, ISIC2018, Chest X-ray), surpassing the strongest VFM-based baseline LoEC by +3.3/+7.5 mIoU and the no-retraining SSP baseline by +11.0/+14.8 mIoU. In the 5-shot setting, HERA also exceeds the best CNN-based method DATO (73.8 mIoU) by +4.1 mIoU, despite being entirely source-free with no source or target-side training. Only 2.69% of backbone parameters (8.39M) are updated per episode, with peak VRAM of 4.2 GB on a single A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERA (DINOv3) outperforms all 20 baselines in the 5-shot setting (77.9 mIoU), with the largest margins over VFM-based methods: +7.5 mIoU over LoEC (70.4), +10.6 over SDRC (67.3), and +8.1 over DFN (69.8). Against the strongest CNN-based method DATO (73.8), HERA leads by +4.1 mIoU in 5-shot while being entirely source-free, whereas DATO requires source-domain pretraining. In the 1-shot setting, HERA (68.3) surpasses TVGTANet (63.0) by +5.3 and DFN (64.0) by +4.3, both of which require training on both source and target domains. Compared to the no-retraining SSP baseline (63.1 in 5-shot), HERA gains +14.8 mIoU, demonstrating that the select-regularize-calibrate pipeline is the primary driver of improvement. HERA also achieves the best per-dataset scores on ISIC2018 (73.6) and Chest X-ray (87.9) in 5-shot, indicating particular strength on low-contrast, thin-boundary clinical and medical imaging tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERA builds on the SSP few-shot segmentation head [12] but fundamentally departs from prior CD-FSS methods by replacing CNN backbones with frozen ViT-based VFMs (DINOv2/DINOv3) and eliminating all source- and target-side training. Where prior cross-domain approaches (PATNet, PMNet, ABCDFSS, IFA, etc.) rely on domain-generalization retraining or correspondence mining on source data, HERA adopts a source-free test-time adaptation paradigm that updates fewer than 2.7% of parameters per episode. The three-stage select-regularize-calibrate pipeline unifies ideas from test-time adaptation (per-episode risk minimization), parameter-efficient fine-tuning (selective layer updates), and attention regularization (entropy-gated Gaussian priors) into a coherent hierarchy that had not been previously combined for dense prediction under domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The source-free, no-retraining paradigm directly addresses practical deployment constraints (privacy, institutional data silos, compute budgets) that have limited adoption of prior CD-FSS methods. The finding that per-episode layer selection accounts for the vast majority of the performance gain (+13.6 of +14.8 mIoU) suggests that the field's default of using fixed or final-layer VFM features under domain shift is suboptimal, and that episode-aware routing is a more critical design choice than previously recognized. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond DINO-family backbones (e.g., SAM, MAE, CLIP), whether it extends to video or 3D segmentation, and how it behaves under more extreme distribution shifts or with very few (1-shot) supports where performance still lags behind 5-shot by a notable margin.
