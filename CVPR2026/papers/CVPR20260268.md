## Problem

Diffusion-based pose estimation methods (e.g., iFusion) that invert Zero123 via MSE in noise space suffer from nonconvex loss landscapes containing local minima induced by geometric ambiguities such as object symmetry and self-similarity. This makes gradient-based optimization highly sensitive to initialization, forcing reliance on brute-force multistart strategies with thousands of candidate poses. The paper provides the first systematic visualization and analysis of these MSE landscapes and identifies the specific geometric causes of convergence failure.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (reference and query) and outputs a relative camera pose parameterized in spherical coordinates (latitude, longitude, radius). In the multi-view extension, N images are provided and the method jointly estimates N absolute SE(3) poses by optimizing a unified energy over all pairwise relative transformations. The score network consumes image features from a ResNet-50 backbone concatenated with sinusoidal embeddings of the current pose estimate, producing a 3D score vector per iteration.

## Method

The framework is a two-stage gradient-based optimizer. Stage 1 trains a lightweight score network (ResNet-50 encoder + sinusoidal pose embedding + 3-layer MLP) via conditional Denoising Score Matching with uniform pose sampling and fixed noise scale σ=1, learning the gradient of the log-probability of plausible poses given the image pair. At inference, Langevin dynamics with this learned score guide an arbitrary initial pose toward high-likelihood regions, effectively escaping local minima. Stage 2 then freezes Zero123 and performs fine-grained refinement by minimizing the noise-space MSE (Eq. 2) via gradient descent on the pose. A theoretical result (Lemma 2) shows that under the assumption of a unique ground-truth pose per image pair, the simplified uniform-sampling objective is equivalent to the standard Gaussian-kernel DSM formulation. The multi-view extension reparameterizes pairwise relative poses into absolute poses to enforce global consistency before applying the same two-stage pipeline.

## Results

On the GSO dataset, the proposed method achieves a Success Rate of 0.836 at 30° threshold, more than doubling iFusion's 0.382, while maintaining competitive Recall (R@30 = 0.927 vs. iFusion's 0.918). On OO3D, the method reaches SR@30 of 0.848 versus iFusion's 0.332, with R@30 of 0.905 surpassing iFusion's 0.882. On the real-world HOPEv2 dataset, the method outperforms iFusion on all recall thresholds (R@15: 0.679 vs. 0.411; R@30: 0.851 vs. 0.494) and VGGT on R@15 (0.679 vs. 0.571) and R@30 (0.851 vs. 0.631). The key advantage is sample efficiency: the method achieves comparable recall with only 2 random initializations where iFusion requires 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against iFusion (the closest diffusion-inversion baseline), the proposed method dramatically improves Success Rate across all datasets: SR@30 rises from 0.382 to 0.836 on GSO and from 0.332 to 0.848 on OO3D, indicating far fewer failed initializations. On Recall, the method is competitive but not uniformly superior: on GSO, R@5 is slightly lower (0.645 vs. 0.700) while R@30 is marginally higher (0.927 vs. 0.918); on OO3D, R@30 improves (0.905 vs. 0.882) but R@5 is lower (0.479 vs. 0.516). Against VGGT, the method trades a small recall gap (R@5: 0.645 vs. 0.752 on GSO) for a large success-rate advantage, and on HOPEv2 it surpasses VGGT on R@15 (0.679 vs. 0.571) and R@30 (0.851 vs. 0.631). ID-Pose is consistently the weakest baseline, with SR@30 below 0.15 on both synthetic datasets. The method also outperforms iFusion on real-world HOPEv2 across all recall thresholds (e.g., R@30: 0.851 vs. 0.494) and achieves a lower translation error (0.059 vs. 0.151), though its rotation error (8.96°) is slightly higher than VGGT's (8.10°).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-inversion paradigm for pose estimation established by ID-Pose and iFusion, which repurpose Zero123's noise-space MSE as an energy function for gradient-based pose optimization. The key departure is the introduction of a lightweight score-based guidance stage (trained via denoising score matching à la Vincent) that reshapes the optimization landscape before the Zero123 refinement, addressing the local-minima problem that prior methods handle only through brute-force multi-start sampling. The paper also provides the first systematic visualization and analysis of the Zero123 MSE loss landscape, revealing how geometric symmetries and self-similarities create multiple basins that trap gradient descent. Positioning against geometry-based methods like DUSt3R and VGGT, the authors frame their contribution as complementary: the diffusion model provides generative priors for extreme viewpoint differences, while the score model provides global optimization guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The landscape analysis offers a diagnostic lens that the pose-estimation community has lacked, making the failure modes of diffusion-based inversion concrete and actionable. The practical impact is a substantial reduction in required initializations (2 vs. 8 for comparable recall at 30°), which directly translates to faster inference in deployment settings. However, the approach remains tethered to Zero123's generative quality and the score model's training data coverage; generalization to truly novel object categories or to other pose-conditioned diffusion backbones is not yet established. Open questions include whether the score-based landscape reshaping transfers to other diffusion-inversion problems (e.g., 3D reconstruction, shape completion) and whether the multi-view extension can scale beyond 8 views without the exponential solution-space growth.
