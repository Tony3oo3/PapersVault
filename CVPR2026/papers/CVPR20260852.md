## Problem

Existing generated image detectors, despite achieving strong in-distribution accuracy, suffer from representational homogenization: during training, diverse forgery cues collapse into a few dominant feature directions, causing brittle decision boundaries that fail under unseen generative models. While prior debiasing approaches (e.g., VIB-Net) suppress spurious correlations, they do not explicitly preserve the structural diversity among complementary discriminative cues, leaving detectors over-reliant on a narrow set of salient artifacts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image as input, which is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone to produce multi-stage CLS embeddings (one per transformer layer). The output is a binary real/fake classification with an authenticity probability, obtained by computing cosine similarity between the refined visual representation and two learnable text prototypes ('real' and 'fake') followed by a temperature-scaled softmax. No special resolution or preprocessing constraints are imposed beyond standard CLIP input normalization.

## Method

The proposed DoU framework combines three mechanisms to maintain heterogeneous, complementary forgery cues. First, a Cue Information Bottleneck (CIB) module at each stage purifies task-relevant signals via a VAE-style KL-to-prior regularizer and a cue-removal consistency loss that enforces prediction stability when any single cue is dropped. Second, Anti-Feature-Collapse Learning (AFCL) enforces inter-stage decorrelation using the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) across all pairs of refined cue representations, preventing the feature space from collapsing into a low-rank sub-manifold. Third, a Class-Specific Prompt Learning (CSP) head aligns the aggregated visual feature with learnable CoOp-style text prompts for 'real' and 'fake' via cosine similarity. The multi-stage cues are combined through learnable weights with a uniformity regularizer to avoid weight collapse, and all components are jointly optimized end-to-end with a unified loss integrating classification, information bottleneck, decorrelation, and weight-balance terms.

## Results

On the cross-model evaluation (trained on SD v1.4, tested on 21 generative models across UniversalFakeDetect, GenImage, and AIGI-Holmes), DoU achieves 99.52% average AP and 92.81% average ACC. This surpasses the prior SOTA (VIB-Net) by 3.39% AP and 5.68% ACC, and outperforms the CLIPping baseline by 12.20% AP and 5.00% ACC. The method also demonstrates strong few-shot capability, reaching 80.98% ACC and 90.81% AP with only 0.1% of training data (320 samples), and maintains the highest ACC and AP across all JPEG compression and Gaussian blur perturbation levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DoU outperforms all seven baselines across the cross-model evaluation. Against the strongest prior method, VIB-Net (CVPR 2025), DoU gains 3.39% AP (99.52% vs. 96.13%) and 5.68% ACC (92.81% vs. 87.13%), with the gap widening in cross-generator settings. Compared to the CLIPping baseline (ICIMR 2024), the improvement is 12.20% AP and 5.00% ACC. Traditional detectors CNNDet (CVPR 2020) and UnivFD (CVPR 2023) show strong in-domain accuracy but degrade sharply on unseen GAN or high-resolution diffusion generators, revealing heavy reliance on source-specific artifacts. NPR (CVPR 2024) and CO-SPY (CVPR 2025) generalize moderately within visually similar generators but fail when the generation paradigm shifts substantially (e.g., GAN-to-diffusion). DRCT (ICML 2024) and VIB-Net improve upon these by reducing content bias and spurious correlations, yet their performance remains inconsistent across benchmarks, confirming that debiasing alone is insufficient without explicit representational diversity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CLIP-based generated image detection line (Ojha et al., Cozzolino et al., Khan et al.) and the variational information bottleneck formulation of Zhang et al. (VIB-Net, CVPR 2025), which compresses task-irrelevant information but does not regulate how task-relevant cues are distributed across the representation space. The key departure is the explicit diagnosis of feature collapse—measured via effective rank and PCA saturation—as the root cause of poor cross-model generalization, and the introduction of an HSIC-based inter-cue decorrelation loss (AFCL) that is orthogonal to the within-cue purification performed by the information bottleneck. The class-specific prompt learning component follows CoOp-style parameterization, positioning the method within the broader VLM-adaptation paradigm rather than introducing a new architectural family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The effective-rank diagnostic (67.38 for DoU vs. 1.37–1.92 for prior detectors) provides a concrete, measurable criterion for evaluating whether a detector has collapsed its feature space, which is a useful tool beyond this specific task. The 5%+ cross-model accuracy gain and consistent robustness under JPEG/blur perturbations suggest that preserving representational diversity is a practical lever for deployment in real-world forensic pipelines where the generative source is unknown. Open questions include whether the frozen CLIP backbone itself limits the diversity of available cues, whether the HSIC constraint scales to video or multi-modal forgery detection, and whether the approach remains effective as generative models produce increasingly indistinguishable outputs.
