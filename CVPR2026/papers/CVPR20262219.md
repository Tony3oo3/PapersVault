## Problem

Adapting large pre-trained models to unseen tasks under tight data and compute budgets remains difficult: meta-learning methods require an expensive and unstable meta-training phase over many tasks, while naive model merging (task arithmetic) interpolates between known task solutions and fails to generalize to entirely new tasks. The paper asks whether a library of existing fine-tuned models can be repurposed to provide both a strong initialization and an efficient adaptation path for unseen tasks, without any additional meta-training or weight-space merging.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained CLIP backbone (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, or ViT-L/14) together with a library of N already-fine-tuned source models, from which per-layer task vectors (Θ_i − Θ_0) are extracted. At adaptation time it takes either k class-balanced labeled samples (k ∈ {1,2,4,8,16}) for few-shot classification or a full unlabeled target split for label-free TTA. The output is an adapted model whose only task-specific parameters are ~8k diagonal spectral coefficients per layer (rank r ≈ 12), producing standard class-label predictions; the orthogonal bases themselves are frozen and shared across all target tasks.

## Method

BOLT operates in two phases. Offline, it computes a thin SVD of each source task vector per layer, stacks the top-k left and right singular vectors across all N tasks, and orthogonalizes them via a whitening step (U_stack(U_stack^T U_stack + εI)^{-1/2}) to obtain fixed, layer-wise spectral bases U_orth and V_orth. Online, the target update is parameterized as U_orth diag(s) V_orth^T, so only the r-dimensional diagonal coefficient vector s is trainable per layer, confining the update to the task-informed subspace with rank ≤ r. A training-free initialization is obtained by projecting each source update onto the shared basis, extracting its best diagonal, pooling across tasks, and applying a single global rescaling factor α selected by a brief accuracy sweep. For label-free TTA, the same diagonal coefficients are optimized with a FixMatch-style consistency loss over weak/strong augmentations, keeping the encoder architecture fixed throughout.

## Results

On few-shot classification averaged over three CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14), BOLT achieves 71.30% accuracy at 1-shot and 78.46% at 16-shot on general-domain datasets, and 80.77% at 1-shot and 91.86% at 16-shot on remote-sensing datasets. On OOD robustness (16-shot, ViT-B/32), BOLT attains the highest accuracy on all four ImageNet variants (e.g., 53.85% on ImageNet-R, 55.69% on ImageNet-V2). In label-free test-time adaptation, BOLT reaches 67.74% (ViT-B/32), 69.92% (ViT-B/16), and 74.11% (ViT-L/14) on general-domain datasets, outperforming zero-shot CLIP by 10.9, 8.4, and 6.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOLT outperforms all six baselines across every shot setting and both domains. Against the closest competitor aTLAS (learned anisotropic scaling over task vectors), BOLT gains 4.96 points at 1-shot and 4.02 points at 16-shot on general datasets, and 10.90 and 5.62 points respectively on remote-sensing datasets. LoRA, while competitive at 16-shot on remote-sensing (90.76% vs. 91.86%), lags BOLT by 10.33 points at 1-shot on general data (60.97% vs. 71.30%), suggesting its larger parameter count is less effective under extreme data scarcity. TIP-Adapter and LP++ show the largest gaps at low shots (e.g., LP++ drops to 43.34% at 1-shot general), while zero-shot CLIP and linear probe remain nearly flat across shot counts, confirming that BOLT's spectral-basis initialization provides a substantially stronger starting point. In the meta-learning comparison (Figure 2), BOLT's training-free initialization reaches higher accuracy in fewer epochs than a MAML-style meta-learned initialization on 17 unseen ViT-B/32 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOLT sits at the intersection of task-vector arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022), SVD-based task-vector decomposition (Gargiulo et al., CVPR 2025), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA, aTLAS). It departs from model-merging approaches that interpolate between known task solutions by instead treating the dominant singular directions of multiple fine-tuned models as a fixed, orthogonal coordinate system in which new tasks are adapted via only diagonal coefficients. Unlike MAML-style meta-learning, BOLT requires no bi-level optimization or dedicated meta-training phase; unlike LoRA, it does not learn arbitrary low-rank factors but constrains updates to a pre-computed task-informed subspace. The TTA component repurposes FixMatch's consistency objective to optimize the same small set of spectral coefficients in a label-free setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOLT offers a practical, low-parameter (~8k) adaptation recipe that avoids the high cost and instability of meta-training while still providing a strong training-free initialization for unseen tasks, which is valuable in settings where a library of fine-tuned checkpoints already exists. The orthogonal-subspace constraint gives explicit rank control and reduces overfitting risk, which is particularly beneficial in data-scarce few-shot and OOD regimes. Open questions include whether the fixed basis can be updated or expanded as new source tasks arrive (continual basis growth), how the method behaves when source and target tasks are highly dissimilar (outside the spanned subspace), and whether the diagonal-only constraint can be relaxed to off-diagonal terms without losing the parameter-efficiency advantage.
