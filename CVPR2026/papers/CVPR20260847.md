## Problem

Existing federated backdoor attacks face a fundamental trade-off: trigger-surface attacks achieve limited benign alignment, leaving malicious updates distinguishable from benign ones, while representation-guided attacks over-align within the shared parameter space, degrading attack effectiveness. The root cause is that both benign and malicious knowledge are optimized in the same parameter space, so any benign alignment inevitably interferes with the backdoor signal. This paper asks how to align benign knowledge without weakening the malicious objective.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the local parameter matrix W_k ∈ R^{m×n} of a compromised client, the pre-poisoning clean local model W_k^∅, and the current global model W_g^t at round t. It produces a corrected update W_k* = W_k + Δ* that is submitted to the FL server, where Δ* is constrained to lie in the null space of the dominant malicious component. At inference time, the poisoned global model takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and CINIC, 28×28 for Fashion-MNIST) with an optional trigger pattern τ(x) and outputs a class label—correct for clean inputs, attacker-chosen target ỹ for triggered inputs.

## Method

Batman decouples benign alignment from the malicious signal via two components. First, MaSC applies truncated SVD to the local parameter matrix, retaining the top-r singular components (r=4) as the dominant malicious knowledge W_k^main, and constructs the malicious null space using the left singular vectors corresponding to zero singular values, yielding a projection matrix P = Ũ₀Ũ₀ᵀ. Second, BeKA defines a hybrid benign knowledge set {W_k^∅, W_g^t} combining the client's pre-poisoning model and the current global model, then solves a regularized least-squares problem min_Δ ‖(W_k^main + Δ) − W_k^b‖²_F + λ‖Δ‖²_F subject to Δ = ΔP, whose closed-form solution Δ* = −(I + λ⁻¹P)⁻¹P(W_k^main − W_k^b) projects the alignment correction entirely into the null space. This ensures the benign alignment perturbation is orthogonal to the backdoor-critical directions, preserving attack effectiveness while making the submitted update statistically indistinguishable from benign client updates. Both MaSC and BeKA are applied only in the final local training round and on the layers with greatest deviation from initialization, following the Lp-Attack protocol to limit computational overhead.

## Results

Under FedAvg (no defense), Batman achieves the highest AVG across all three datasets: 69.06 on CIFAR-100, 84.78 on CIFAR-10, and 72.57 on CINIC. Under the strong FLTrust defense, Batman maintains ASR of 87.02 (CIFAR-100), 85.52 (CIFAR-10), and 87.76 (CINIC), while baselines like Lp-Attack drop to 0.00 ASR on CIFAR-10 and Neurotoxin to 11.42. Under AlignIns, Batman achieves 99.08 ASR on CINIC versus BadNet's 24.59, and under DnC it sustains ASR above 91% on all datasets. The ablation confirms both MaSC (null-space isolation) and BeKA (benign alignment) are necessary, with their combination yielding the best AVG (e.g., 94.78 on Fashion-MNIST under FLTrust).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Batman outperforms all five baselines on AVG under every defense and dataset combination. The most dramatic gains appear under FLTrust, where Lp-Attack collapses to 0.00 ASR on CIFAR-10 and BadNet drops to 6.78 AVG, while Batman sustains 73.03 AVG (85.52 ASR). Under AlignIns, BadNet's ASR on CIFAR-100 plummets to 0.72 and on CINIC to 24.59, whereas Batman retains 99.08 ASR on CINIC and 97.52 on CIFAR-10. Against Neurotoxin, Batman wins by 74+ points in AVG on CINIC under AlignIns (75.68 vs 72.45) and by 35+ points under FLTrust on CIFAR-10 (73.03 vs 37.80). DBA and BadPFL are consistently the weakest baselines, with AVG below 50 in nearly all settings, while Batman remains above 61 in every configuration. The only minor trade-off is a small ACC drop (≤2.6%) on CIFAR-10 and CINIC under FedAvg, but this is offset by ASR gains that push AVG above BadNet in all cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

Batman builds on the null-space projection concept from Wang et al. (CVPR 2021) for continual learning, repurposing it to isolate malicious knowledge in dominant SVD components while performing benign alignment in the orthogonal complement. It also adopts the layer-selection and timing strategy from Lp-Attack (ICLR 2024), restricting SVD-based operations to backdoor-critical layers in the final local round. Conceptually, Batman departs from both Trigger-Surface Attacks (which modify input-level attributes) and Representation-Guided Attacks (which inject benign updates into the shared parameter space) by explicitly decoupling malicious and benign knowledge into orthogonal subspaces, addressing what the authors identify as the fundamental interference problem when both types of knowledge coexist in the same parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric framework for the long-standing tension between backdoor effectiveness and stealthiness in federated learning, showing that the two objectives need not compete when properly separated in parameter space. The strong results under multiple defenses (especially the 74-point ASR gap over BadNet under AlignIns on CINIC) suggest that current consistency- and distribution-based defenses remain vulnerable to attacks that mimic benign update directions. However, the computational overhead of SVD decomposition and the need to train a separate clean model limit practical applicability in resource-constrained FL settings, and the approach has only been validated on vision tasks with relatively small models (ResNet-18, CNN), leaving open whether the null-space separation generalizes to larger architectures or non-vision modalities.
