## Problem

Existing OT-based OOD detection methods (e.g., POT) must solve for the complete optimal transport plan to derive pseudo OOD scores, which is computationally redundant since only the marginal distribution matters. Moreover, the entropy regularization used to make the transport plan tractable produces dense approximate solutions that yield unreliable OOD scores, and the arbitrary amplification of source weights (redundant mass k) lacks theoretical justification. This paper asks whether one can bypass the transport plan entirely while obtaining more accurate, theoretically grounded OOD scores.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two batches per iteration: a large unlabeled set X_u (containing both in-distribution and OOD samples) and a smaller labeled set X_l (known classes only), with the unlabeled set significantly larger to match open-set SSL settings. Unlabeled images undergo both weak and strong augmentations before encoding. The model outputs two signals per sample: a continuous OOD probability score (derived from reweighted source marginals, not the transport plan) and a closed-set class prediction. Experiments use 32×32 CIFAR-10/100 images and 224×224 ImageNet-30 crops.

## Method

DREW formulates per-batch OOD detection as a semi-unbalanced optimal transport (SemiUOT) problem with KL-divergence penalty on the source marginal, then applies a dynamic reweighting transformation (Proposition 3.1) that partially solves the dual variables u and ζ via L-BFGS, converting the remaining problem into classical OT. The pseudo OOD score is read directly from the reweighted source distribution a ⊙ exp(−(u*+ζ*)/τ_a), eliminating the need to compute the full transport plan π or rely on entropy-regularized Sinkhorn iterations. For efficient training, a smooth approximation of the non-smooth inf operator (Proposition 3.2) enables fixed-point iteration with a provable error bound of ε log M (Proposition 3.3). The overall framework couples this OOD module with a FixMatch-style semi-supervised classifier, jointly optimized with a weighted OOD regression loss that trains a lightweight detection head to predict the reweighting-derived scores.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (55 known classes, 50 labeled samples per class), DREW achieves 78.8% top-1 accuracy and 91.7% AUROC, outperforming the closest OT-based baseline POT (78.7% / 88.4%) by 3.3 AUROC points. On CIFAR-10 (6 labeled classes, 400 samples per class), DREW reaches 94.1% accuracy and 99.4% AUROC, matching POT's accuracy while tying on AUROC. On the more challenging ImageNet-30 (20 known classes, 224×224 resolution), DREW achieves 92.1% top-1 accuracy and 97.6% AUROC, slightly surpassing POT (92.0% / 97.4%) and substantially outperforming OpenMatch (89.6% / 96.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREW consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the closest OT-based competitor POT, DREW gains 3.3 AUROC points on CIFAR-100 (91.7% vs 88.4% at 55/50) while matching or slightly exceeding its classification accuracy, and edges ahead on ImageNet-30 (97.6% vs 97.4% AUROC). Against OpenMatch (OVA-classifier based), DREW improves AUROC by 4.7 points on CIFAR-100 (91.7% vs 87.0%) and by 1.2 points on ImageNet-30 (97.6% vs 96.4%), while also achieving higher top-1 accuracy in most settings. The purely neural-network baselines MTCF and T2T are substantially outperformed: on CIFAR-100 (55/50), DREW exceeds MTCF by 12.3 accuracy points and 10.5 AUROC points, and exceeds T2T by 6.6 accuracy points and 31.3 AUROC points. On ImageNet-30, DREW's 97.6% AUROC dwarfs FixMatch's 45.1% and T2T's 55.7%, confirming that proxy-based OT methods are far more effective than test-time or purely neural OOD detection in open-set SSL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the POT (Partial Optimal Transport) framework of Ren et al. [42], which first introduced OT-based pseudo OOD scoring for open-set semi-supervised learning, and on the FixMatch [46] semi-supervised backbone. The key departure is a reformulation: rather than solving for the full entropy-regularized transport plan (as POT does via Sinkhorn iterations) and extracting OOD scores from the marginal of that plan, DREW recasts the problem as Semi-unbalanced OT (SemiUOT) and shows via a dual-form proposition that the OOD score can be read directly from dynamically reweighted source distribution weights, reducing the problem to a classical OT subproblem. This eliminates both the arbitrary redundant-mass amplification parameter (k) used in POT and the dense approximate solutions from entropy regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic reweighting trick provides a theoretically grounded (with explicit error bound ε log M) and computationally lighter path to pseudo OOD labels, which could make OT-based detection more practical in iterative training loops where the transport plan is recomputed every batch. However, the empirical gains over POT are modest (roughly 2–3 AUROC points on CIFAR-100, near-parity on CIFAR-10), and the method remains tied to the open-set SSL setting with a fixed labeled reference set. Open questions include whether the SemiUOT-to-classical-OT reduction generalizes to multi-source or streaming OOD scenarios, and whether the approximation error bound translates into meaningful detection quality at very large batch sizes where M grows substantially.
