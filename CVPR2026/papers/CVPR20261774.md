## Problem

Existing monocular hand-object reconstruction methods assume rigid grasping and static object geometry, making them unable to capture the continuous joint rotations and component deformations inherent in articulated manipulations. Under monocular observation, the coupling between evolving shape and motion introduces severe ambiguity, so jointly optimizing geometry and articulation from the outset leads to unstable and incoherent results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular RGB video of bimanual manipulation (View 1 of ARCTIC) together with known MANO hand pose parameters and 6-DoF object transformations as initialization. It outputs a posed 3D Gaussian representation of both hands and the object, explicit revolute-joint articulation parameters (unit rotation axis, 3D pivot, per-frame angle), photo-realistic novel-view renderings (evaluated on View 8), and a 3D mesh extracted via TSDF fusion and Marching Cubes. Training runs for 25,000 Adam iterations (~3 h on an RTX 4090) with the STONE phase activated after 10,000 iterations.

## Method

The Clay-to-Stone framework operates in two phases over a shared 3DGS representation. In the CLAY phase, each object Gaussian carries a learnable modulation factor β that is applied element-wise to a per-frame latent deformation vector Z_t (produced by an MLP over ViT-extracted pixel-aligned features and multi-resolution hash-grid encodings), enabling fine-grained, semantically-aware deformations without predefined articulation rules; SAM2 part-segmentation masks supervise β via a Gumbel-sigmoid rendering to enforce part-level consistency. In the STONE phase, the same β factors are converted into a near-binary motion-eligibility signal via Gumbel sigmoid to gate which Gaussians participate in articulation, and a revolute joint is explicitly parameterized: the pivot is regressed as a softmax-weighted sum of Gaussian positions from hash-grid features, the axis from the mean hash-grid feature, and the per-frame angle from an MLP combining the gated deformation encoding with a temporal latent embedding. This hierarchical progression from flexible, data-driven exploration to physically constrained rigid articulation mitigates the geometry-motion entanglement that destabilizes joint optimization.

## Results

On the ARCTIC dataset (11 articulated objects, monocular input), the proposed Clay-to-Stone method achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 1.97 cm², F@5 of 0.741, and F@10 of 0.884 for per-frame articulated geometry reconstruction, substantially outperforming 3DGS-Avatar (CD 3.83, F@5 0.677, F@10 0.831). For photo-realistic novel-view rendering, it reaches 28.17 dB PSNR, 0.9606 SSIM, and 35.43 LPIPS (×1000), again surpassing 3DGS-Avatar (27.51 dB, 0.9584, 39.13). Against rigid-grasp baselines on canonical-state reconstruction, the method achieves CD 0.79 and F@10 0.915, beating both HOLD (CD 2.07, F@10 0.639) and BIGS (CD 1.28, F@10 0.839).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS-Avatar (the closest deformable-GS baseline adapted for manipulation), Clay-to-Stone wins on every metric: it nearly halves the Chamfer Distance (1.97 vs. 3.83 cm²), improves F@5 by 6.4 points (0.741 vs. 0.677), and gains 5.3 dB-equivalent rendering quality (PSNR 28.17 vs. 27.51, LPIPS 35.43 vs. 39.13). Against the rigid-grasp baselines HOLD and BIGS, the method's canonical-state reconstruction is clearly superior (CD 0.79 vs. 2.07 and 1.28; F@10 0.915 vs. 0.639 and 0.839), confirming that the articulated modeling does not sacrifice static geometry quality. Notably, when evaluated in the articulated (per-frame) setting, the method's CD rises to 2.32 due to spatial misalignment of moving parts, yet F-scores remain high (F@5 0.703, F@10 0.869), indicating that overall surface fidelity is preserved even under extreme articulation. The ablation rows (w/o Clay, w/o Stone) further confirm that both phases are necessary: removing either degrades reconstruction relative to the full pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its geometric backbone but departs from both hand-object reconstruction methods (HOLD, BIGS) that assume rigid grasping, and deformable avatar methods (3DGS-Avatar, GauHHuman) that model body-centric deformations without part-level semantic awareness. It also extends articulated object modeling work (PARIS, ArticulatedGS, VideoArtGS) that typically requires multi-view captures or static reference configurations, by handling continuous, transient articulation states under monocular observation. The key conceptual departure is the Clay-to-Stone phase-wise strategy: first exploring free-form, semantically-guided deformations via learnable per-Gaussian modulation factors, then consolidating into rigid revolute-joint constraints — a hierarchical granularity progression not seen in prior monocular manipulation or articulation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuine gap: monocular articulated hand-object manipulation modeling was largely unexplored, with prior methods either assuming static geometry or requiring multi-view/static-reference setups. The phase-wise optimization principle (flexible exploration → rigid consolidation) offers a transferable strategy for other geometry-motion entanglement problems. However, the method is limited to simple two-part revolute joints and interaction-centric scenarios; extending to complex multi-part hierarchies, prismatic joints, or generating new articulated instances remains open. The reliance on ground-truth MANO and 6-DoF poses (with only an in-the-wild demo) also limits immediate practical deployment.
