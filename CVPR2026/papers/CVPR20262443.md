## Problem

Machine unlearning for contrastively-trained embedding models like CLIP remains underexplored, and existing multimodal unlearning benchmarks target generative chatbots evaluated via VQA rather than the embedding-based foundation models that serve as backbones across downstream systems. Moreover, current evaluations lack the granularity to diagnose fine-grained, association-level forgetting, making it impossible to distinguish between ineffective methods, over-generalizing methods that erase more than intended, and catastrophically damaging ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262443/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a Compromised CLIP ViT-B/16 model (trained from scratch on ~400M retain pairs plus 60K sensitive pairs) and a composite forget set of ~30K image-text pairs mixing persona-level deletions (forget_identity) and association-level deletions (forget_association, 50% of each persona's sensitive facts). The unlearned model is evaluated against structured holdout sets—holdout_identity (unseen personas) and holdout_association (held-out facts for the same personas)—producing two metric pillars: Forgetting Efficacy (RetFail via MRR over 2001-candidate galleries, AssocStr, ACS, IdZSC, CoreAssoc) and Utility Impact (GenKnow, InterIdSim, IntraIdSim, VisIdInt, FragSim), with the Clean model's scores serving as efficacy targets and the Compromised model's scores as utility baselines.

## Method

SALMUBench constructs a synthetic dataset of 60K image-text pairs through a five-stage pipeline: seeding 1,000 identity anchors from SFHQ, generating ~100 diverse images per persona via IP-Adapter-FaceID Plus with ControlNet outpainting, filtering with CLIP zero-shot labels and FairFace demographics, assigning culturally-consistent PII (name, city, phone, email, IBAN, job, blood type), and paraphrasing captions with gemma3:12b under five linguistic directives to prevent surface-pattern shortcuts. Two ViT-B/16 CLIP models are then trained entirely from scratch for 32 epochs on 128 H100 GPUs: a Clean model on the 400M-pair retain set alone, and a Compromised model additionally exposed to the 60K sensitive pairs, ensuring any behavioral difference is attributable solely to the sensitive data. The key evaluation innovation is a hierarchical split of the 774 personas into disjoint persona-level and association-level groups, yielding structured holdout sets that separately measure inter-identity collateral damage (erasing unrelated people) and intra-identity collateral damage (erasing one fact while forgetting another for the same person), enabling the first taxonomy of unlearning failure modes: ineffective, over-generalized, and catastrophically damaging.

## Results

On SALMUBench (5× budget), the best-performing unlearning methods (VLUnlearn, DELETE, CLIPErase) achieve the target RetFail of 0.001 (vs. 0.236 for the Compromised model) with GenKnow ≥ 0.632 (target 0.638), demonstrating that utility-efficient deletion is feasible. However, DELETE and CLIPErase suffer severe over-generalization, dropping InterIdSim and IntraIdSim to 0.023–0.024 (target 0.321), indicating they erase broader association templates rather than individual facts. Catastrophically damaging baselines (Shuffled Captions, Direct Similarity Minimization) reduce RetFail to ≤0.004 but collapse GenKnow to 0.548 and 0.615 respectively. No current method simultaneously matches all target scores across both forgetting efficacy and utility impact pillars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five cited baselines, VLUnlearn (MultiDelete [5]) achieves the best balance: it matches the target RetFail (0.001) and GenKnow (0.638) exactly, but still over-generalizes with InterIdSim/IntraIdSim at 0.210 (target 0.321). DELETE [32] and CLIPErase [28] also reach RetFail=0.001 with GenKnow 0.632–0.634, yet they over-correct aggressively, collapsing InterIdSim and IntraIdSim to 0.023–0.024—roughly 93% below target—indicating they erase entire association templates rather than specific facts. Negative Gradient [9] achieves moderate forgetting (RetFail=0.009) but incurs substantial collateral damage (InterIdSim=0.063, IntraIdSim=0.061), placing it between the catastrophic and over-generalized failure modes. Descent to Delete [20] shows the mildest collateral damage among effective methods (InterIdSim=0.228, IntraIdSim=0.228) but falls short on forgetting (RetFail=0.008). The paper's central finding is that no single method simultaneously matches all target scores: the methods that forget best (DELETE, CLIPErase) over-generalize the most, while those with the least collateral damage (Generic Captions, Descent to Delete) forget the least.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two underexplored threads: CLIP unlearning (MultiDelete, CLIPErase, zero-shot class unlearning) and synthetic-data unlearning benchmarks (TOFU, FIUBench, MLLMU-Bench). It departs from prior CLIP unlearning work by targeting PII-specific associations rather than generic class or concept removal, and from prior synthetic benchmarks by evaluating embedding-based dual encoders rather than generative MLLMs via VQA. A key methodological departure is training both the Compromised and Clean reference models from scratch on ~400M pairs, eliminating the confounding effects of incremental fine-tuning that plague prior benchmarks. The structured holdout protocol (holdout_identity, holdout_association) is a novel diagnostic layer absent from all prior forget-retain evaluations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SALMUBench addresses a practically urgent gap: GDPR 'Right to be Forgotten' compliance for CLIP-based foundation models that underpin retrieval, generation, and VQA systems. The central finding—that the best-performing methods (DELETE, CLIPErase) achieve target forgetting but silently over-generalize, erasing related unseen associations—reveals that standard forget-retain metrics are blind to a critical failure mode, reframing how the field should evaluate unlearning. Open questions include whether findings transfer to diffusion models using CLIP as a backbone, whether methods generalize to implicit sensitive concepts (artistic style, political affiliation), and whether unlearned information is truly erased or merely suppressible via re-fine-tuning. The publicly released dataset, models, and leaderboards lower the barrier for future work on this problem.
