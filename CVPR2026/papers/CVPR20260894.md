## Problem

Existing training-free object hallucination detectors for LVLMs focus on image-token attention or output logits but overlook the preliminary output tokens (prelim) that the model has already generated. This paper shows that hallucinatory object tokens exhibit significantly higher attention to prelim tokens, indicating the model has shifted into an unreliable operating mode where it ignores the image and instead relies on its own imperfect prior output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260894/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on an RGB image paired with a fixed text instruction prompt (e.g., 'Please help me describe the image in detail') fed to an autoregressive LVLM. During standard greedy or sampled decoding, it reads the self-attention weights already computed at layer 0 for each generated object token, requiring no additional forward passes or hidden-state storage. The output is a scalar Prelim Attention Score per object token, which is thresholded to yield a binary hallucination label; the score is computed on the fly during inference with negligible memory overhead (18 GB VRAM, on par with other attention-based methods).

## Method

The core contribution is the Prelim Attention Score (PAS), defined as the sum of attention weights from all previously generated output tokens (the prelim) to a given object token, averaged over all heads at layer 0. The theoretical grounding is an information-theoretic formulation: the detector is the negative mutual information I(v; Y_k | y_{<k}, t), which is small when the predicted object is independent of the image given the prelim—precisely the hallucination condition. While the full MI estimator requires L extra forward passes to marginalize over a reference image set, PAS approximates the same signal using only the attention weights already available during a single forward pass, exploiting the fact that in decoder-only Transformers, attention is the sole information-exchange pathway between tokens. Ablations confirm that layer 0 (the earliest layer, associated with information gathering) yields the strongest detection signal, and that PAS outperforms image-attention-based baselines (SVAR) by 4.7 AUROC points on average, achieving 85.0% AUROC across LLaVA-1.5-7B, MiniGPT-4, and Shikra on MSCOCO and Pascal VOC.

## Results

PAS achieves a mean AUROC of 85.0% across three 7B LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, Shikra) and two benchmarks (MSCOCO, Pascal VOC), outperforming the strongest baseline SVAR (80.3%) by 4.7 points on average. PAS wins on 5 of 6 individual model–dataset combinations, with the sole exception being MiniGPT-4 on MSCOCO where SVAR reaches 88.0% versus PAS's 85.6%. The method requires no extra forward passes and uses only 18 GB VRAM (batch size 1, float16), on par with the attention-based SVAR and 33% less than hidden-state-based GLSim.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAS outperforms all five baselines on average AUROC. Against the logits-based NLL [37] (62.2% avg) and Entropy [26] (67.4% avg), PAS leads by 22.8 and 17.6 points respectively, confirming that output-probability signals are substantially weaker than attention-based ones. The representation-based IC [16] (71.9% avg) and GLSim [27] (65.6% avg) are also clearly surpassed, with PAS gaining 13.1 and 19.4 points. The closest competitor is the attention-based SVAR [17] (80.3% avg), which focuses on image-token attention; PAS exceeds it by 4.7 points on average and wins on 5 of 6 settings, though SVAR edges out PAS on MiniGPT-4/MSCOCO (88.0 vs 85.6). Notably, PAS's advantage over SVAR is largest on Shikra (84.5 vs 71.9, +12.6), suggesting that prelim attention is especially informative when image-attention signals are less discriminative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in object hallucination detection, which extracts signals from image tokens (SVAR, GLSim), output logits (NLL, Entropy), or hidden states (IC). Rather than building on any single prior method, it repurposes the standard self-attention mechanism—already computed during inference—to focus on a token type (prelim output tokens) that all prior attention-based and representation-based approaches explicitly ignore. The mutual information formulation (Eq. 3–4) provides a theoretical grounding that no prior detection method offers, though the practical PAS score (Eq. 10) is a simple sum of attention weights that requires no additional computation beyond what the model already produces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAS offers a practically attractive detection signal: training-free, reference-free, and requiring zero extra forward passes, making it deployable for real-time filtering in production LVLM pipelines. The conceptual finding—that over-reliance on low-information prelim tokens signals an 'alternative operating mode' more reliably than under-reliance on image tokens—reframes how practitioners should think about hallucination as a mode shift rather than a perception failure. Open questions include whether the signal generalizes to VQA (where prelim dynamics differ), to non-standard attention variants (sliding window, sparse), and to relational or attribute hallucination beyond object existence.
