## Problem

Existing VAE-based latent spaces for Latent Diffusion Models enforce local smoothness through Gaussian priors, equivariance constraints, or semantic alignment, but this causes latent representations to collapse into dense, narrow regions, sacrificing global dispersity. The result is a reconstruction-generation trade-off: smoother latents improve in-distribution reconstruction but degrade out-of-distribution generalization and generative diversity. The paper asks whether a latent space can be simultaneously locally smooth and globally dispersive without relying on fixed global priors or external supervision.

## Input / Output

The model takes 256×256 RGB images and compresses them into 64×64 latent representations (4× downsampling factor) via an encoder-decoder autoencoder. Outputs include pixel-level reconstructions (evaluated with PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and unconditional generated images (evaluated with FID, IS, Precision, Recall). Cross-domain and cross-resolution evaluations test reconstruction on unseen datasets (MS-COCO, LSDIR, DIV2K) and at resolutions up to 1024×640, while a large-scale experiment on ImageNet uses the SiT-XL/2 diffusion backbone at 100K training steps.

## Method

SORL shapes the latent space through two complementary local interaction rules that require no external priors or pretrained encoders. Local attraction (L_LA) adds bounded uniform perturbations to latent codes and minimizes reconstruction error, encouraging nearby latents to decode coherently and thus promoting smoothness. Local repulsion (L_LR) introduces K learnable centroids updated via moving average; a push term maximizes the minimum pairwise centroid distance to spread the reference structure, while a pull term (weighted by λ_pull) aligns each latent code to its nearest centroid, anchoring representations to the dispersed structure. The total training objective is L_SORL = L_RC + L_LA + L_LR, where L_RC combines MSE, perceptual, and adversarial losses. The key insight is that smoothness and dispersity emerge as a stable global configuration from the accumulated antagonistic effect of these simple local rules, rather than being imposed by a top-down prior.

## Results

On reconstruction, SORL achieves the best PSNR across all four datasets: 27.42 dB on ADE20K, 26.13 dB on LSUN-Churches, 31.51 dB on CelebA-HQ, and 29.70 dB on FFHQ, with the largest gain of +3.11 dB over VAE on CelebA-HQ. On unconditional generation (CelebA-HQ), SORL reduces FID from 13.02 (VAE) to 9.74 while achieving the highest Precision (0.47) and Recall (0.65). Cross-domain evaluation on MS-COCO, LSDIR, and DIV2K confirms consistent improvements (e.g., PSNR 26.91 on MS-COCO vs. 26.22 for VAE), and on ImageNet with SiT-XL/2, SORL achieves FID 15.12 vs. 16.96 for iREPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SORL outperforms all five VAE-based baselines (VAE, WAE, RV-VAE, EQ-VAE, VA-VAE) on every reconstruction metric across all four datasets, with the most dramatic margin against VA-VAE (e.g., PSNR 31.51 vs. 28.28 on CelebA-HQ, LPIPS 0.049 vs. 0.110). Against the strongest reconstruction baseline RV-VAE, SORL gains +0.65 dB PSNR on ADE20K and +1.77 dB on CelebA-HQ. On generation (CelebA-HQ), SORL achieves FID 9.74, beating VAE (13.02), RV-VAE (16.21), EQ-VAE (15.80), and VA-VAE (53.15) by large margins, while also improving Precision from 0.33 (VAE) to 0.47. On the large-scale ImageNet experiment with SiT-XL/2, SORL improves FID from 16.96 (iREPA) to 15.12 and IS from 77.9 to 78.7, though Recall drops slightly from 0.61 to 0.58, indicating a minor trade-off in distributional coverage at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VAE-based latent diffusion pipeline (Kingma & Welling; Rombach et al.) and departs from the prevailing top-down regularization strategies—Gaussian KL priors, equivariance constraints (EQ-VAE), and pretrained-encoder semantic alignment (VA-VAE, iREPA, REPA)—by proposing a bottom-up, self-organization-inspired alternative. The local attraction term is conceptually related to smoothness regularizers (Lipschitz continuity, equivariance) but is implemented as a bounded perturbation reconstruction loss, while the local repulsion term with learnable centroids draws on Self-Organizing Map principles repurposed for deep latent spaces. The empirical study of the smoothness-versus-dispersity trade-off extends and reframes observations from VA-VAE and EQ-VAE, arguing that prior work over-emphasizes smoothness at the cost of global coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SORL offers a simple, two-term loss that practitioners can drop into existing VAE training pipelines to simultaneously improve reconstruction fidelity and generative diversity, addressing a well-known trade-off without requiring pretrained encoders or fixed Gaussian priors. However, the approach remains limited to unconditional generation and geometric (non-semantic) organization; the authors acknowledge that extending the framework to text-guided diffusion and incorporating semantic-level structure are open problems. The ImageNet experiment is preliminary (100K steps, single architecture), so scalability to large-scale conditional generation remains unverified, and the centroid-based repulsion mechanism's sensitivity to the number of centroids K and the pull weight λ_pull introduces additional hyperparameters that may complicate adoption.
