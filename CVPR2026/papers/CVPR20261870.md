## Problem

Group-based RLVR methods like GRPO require multiple rollouts per prompt, incurring substantial compute cost for multimodal models due to repeated forward passes through vision and language encoders. Naive single-rollout variants that succeed in text-only RLVR suffer from severe training instability and entropy collapse when extended to multimodal settings, where high-dimensional visual inputs amplify variance and complicate cross-modal credit assignment. The paper asks whether multimodal RLVR can be made both compute-efficient (one rollout per input) and training-stable without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261870/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt paired with an RGB image (e.g., charts, diagrams, visual math problems) and produces a single structured reasoning response with the chain-of-thought enclosed in <think> tags and the final answer formatted in \boxed{}. The reward is a binary signal (0/1) computed by exact-match comparison of the extracted final answer against the ground-truth solution. Unlike group-based baselines that generate 8 rollouts per prompt, MSSR generates exactly one rollout per input, with the baseline for advantage estimation maintained per-input via a Beta distribution rather than computed from group statistics.

## Method

MSSR replaces group-wise normalization with a conjugate Beta distribution baseline for Bernoulli rewards, where shape parameters α(x) and β(x) are updated per training step with an adaptive discount factor η that is adjusted based on a sliding-window KL divergence between consecutive policy updates. The critical stabilization mechanism is entropy-based advantage shaping: a bonus ψ_t = min(|A_t|/γ, λ·stopgrad(H_t)) is added to the raw advantage, where H_t is the token-level policy entropy, effectively capping the advantage magnitude in proportion to model uncertainty. This prevents entropy collapse by softening the penalty for low-reward responses generated under high uncertainty, preserving exploration. Ablations show that KL regularization, cross-modal regularization with a text-only anchor branch, and explicit entropy loss are all insufficient to prevent collapse, establishing entropy-based advantage shaping as empirically essential (not merely beneficial) in the multimodal single-rollout setting. The result is a group-free policy-gradient method that matches GRPO's validation accuracy with half the training steps and outperforms it under equal compute budgets across five multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, MSSR achieves an average accuracy of 58.6% across five multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVista, MMK12, R1-Onevision-Bench, HallusionBench), outperforming GRPO (56.3%), RLOO (56.0%), and REINFORCE++ (53.1%). On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MSSR reaches 49.5% average, surpassing GRPO (47.4%) by 2.1 points. Notably, MSSR matches GRPO's final validation accuracy with only half the total training steps, and its 7B result (58.6%) also exceeds the strongest prior Zero-RL method MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (57.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSSR consistently outperforms all directly compared RL baselines (GRPO, RLOO, REINFORCE++) on both 3B and 7B models across all five benchmarks, with average gains of +2.1% over GRPO (3B) and +2.3% over GRPO (7B). REINFORCE++ with single-rollout notably underperforms even the untrained base model on 3B (42.8% vs. 44.6%), highlighting the instability of naive single-rollout RL in multimodal settings. Against prior SFT+RL methods, MSSR-7B (58.6%) surpasses VLAA-Thinker-7B (55.3%), OpenVLThinker-7B (52.8%), and R1-Onevision-7B (51.0%). Among Zero-RL methods, MSSR-7B (58.6%) edges out MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (57.7%) and ThinkLite-VL-7B (56.7%). In terms of training efficiency, MSSR reaches GRPO's final validation accuracy in roughly half the training steps, while incurring only a small per-step overhead (6.9 vs. 6.1 min/step) due to Beta-distribution baseline estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes text-only single-rollout RLVR formulations (Brantley et al., Xu et al.) to the multimodal domain, replacing group-wise normalization with a conjugate Beta-distribution baseline for binary Bernoulli rewards. It departs from the group-based GRPO paradigm by eliminating multi-rollout sampling entirely, and critically repurposes the entropy-based advantage shaping mechanism from Cheng et al. (originally applied within group-based RLVR) as a stabilization component for the single-rollout setting. The paper's key conceptual departure is showing that this mechanism, previously treated as a helpful regularizer in group-based methods, becomes empirically essential when intra-group normalization is absent in multimodal single-rollout training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% reduction in training steps to match GRPO's validation accuracy is a practically significant compute saving for multimodal RLVR, which is already expensive due to repeated vision-encoder forward passes. The finding that entropy-based advantage shaping is not merely beneficial but essential for stability in this setting provides a clear design principle for future single-rollout multimodal RL methods. Limitations include evaluation only on Qwen2.5-VL (3B/7B) and a single math-reasoning dataset (Vision-R1-RL, ~10K samples), leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger models, non-math multimodal tasks, or continuous reward structures. The narrow 120-step training horizon also raises questions about long-horizon stability.
