## Problem

Action diffusion models achieve high-fidelity robot action generation but suffer from low inference frequency (5–6 Hz) due to iterative denoising, far below the 30 Hz required by real-world manipulation. Existing acceleration methods that reuse cached features rely on static schedules, which are inherently mismatched with the evolving sparsity patterns that emerge from diverse perceptions and multi-round rollout interactions in open environments. Moreover, two practical bottlenecks remain: repetitive conditional encoding and pruning overhead offset most speed gains, and single-direction feature caching is insufficient to constrain pruning errors under aggressive sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot visual observations (and language instructions for VLA models like RDT-1B) and produces an action chunk via sparsified diffusion denoising. Internally, the lightweight pruner outputs a 4-dimensional gating vector per DiT block per denoising timestep, where each dimension encodes the confidence for computing fresh, reusing from the current forward, previous denoising timestep, or earlier rollout iteration. The gating vectors are discretized via argmax into a binary mask over all 3L residual blocks (self-attention, cross-attention, FFN), and the 3D lattice cache is indexed by (block, timestep, rollout) coordinates to retrieve the nearest cached feature along each axis.

## Method

The paper proposes test-time sparsity, a prune-then-reuse paradigm where a lightweight pruner (sharing the conditional encoder with the action diffusion transformer) dynamically predicts which residual blocks to skip at each forward pass. A highly parallelized inference pipeline decouples encoding and pruning from the autoregressive denoising loop by collapsing all K timesteps into a single batched forward pass, then overlaps the pruner with the decoder via asynchronous threads, reducing non-decoder overhead to ~0.45 ms. To sustain 95% sparsity, an omnidirectional reusing strategy organizes historical features in a 3D lattice (block × timestep × rollout) and lets the pruner jointly decide whether to compute or reuse from any of the three directions, with caches refreshed only when a block is actually computed. The pruner is trained with trajectory-level supervision: a few action trajectories are sampled, and the sparsified diffusion is supervised step-by-step across rollout iterations using a fidelity loss plus a sparsity regularization loss, with the Straight-Through Estimator enabling gradients through the discrete argmax gating.

## Results

On the Proficient Human (PH) benchmark across five manipulation tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Transport, Tool), the method achieves 84% average success rate with a 5.18× wall-clock speedup at 95% sparsity, matching the dense model's 83% average. At 93% sparsity, it reaches 86% average success (exceeding the dense baseline) with a 4.86× speedup and only 0.68 GFLOPS per diffusion. On the challenging multi-stage Kitchen task, the method achieves a lossless 5.90× speedup at 93% sparsity (100% success across all sub-tasks), and 6.33× at 95% sparsity (99% average). On the large-scale RDT-1B VLA model, it delivers over 2.5× speedup at 90% sparsity while maintaining or improving success rates on all four ManiSkill tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all three baselines on both success rate and speedup across every task. Compared to BAC (90% sparsity, 3.68× speedup, 79% avg success), the proposed method at 95% sparsity achieves 5.18× speedup and 84% avg success, gaining 5 points in success while nearly doubling the speedup. EfficientVLA (86% sparsity, 3.46× speedup) suffers a 11-point average success drop (72% vs. 83% dense) and collapses to 3% on the Kitchen task, whereas the proposed method maintains 99–100% on Kitchen. L2C, adapted from image diffusion, achieves only 1.28× speedup at 26% sparsity with a 27-point average success drop (56%), confirming that static, offline-learned schedules cannot handle the dynamic sparsity patterns in open-environment robot rollouts. The key advantage stems from the omnidirectional reusing strategy, which the ablation (Table 6) shows is necessary: any single-direction variant (forward, timestep, or rollout) degrades severely on at least one task, while the full omnidirectional scheme matches or exceeds the dense baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prune-then-reuse paradigm introduced in Sparse ActionGen [10] by replacing static, offline-generated sparsity schedules with a learned, per-forward pruner that adapts dynamically to evolving policy dynamics in open environments. It departs from prior caching methods (EfficientVLA, BAC, L2C) that reuse features only along the denoising-step dimension with fixed or heuristic-based intervals, by introducing omnidirectional reusing across three orthogonal axes (current forward, previous timesteps, and earlier rollout iterations) organized in a 3D lattice. The parallelized inference pipeline—decoupling the pruner from the autoregressive loop and overlapping it with decoder computation—is a new engineering contribution not present in prior diffusion acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 5× wall-clock speedup at 47.5 Hz with near-lossless performance directly addresses the critical frequency gap (6 Hz baseline vs. 30 Hz requirement) that has limited real-time deployment of diffusion-based visuomotor policies. The conceptual insight that features cached across rollout iterations are highly aligned yet complementary to those from the denoising-step direction—validated by the ablation showing single-direction reuse collapses on specific tasks—opens a new axis for exploiting temporal redundancy in iterative policy execution. Limitations include the lower speedup on RDT-1B (2.5×) due to heavier visual/language encoders, evaluation primarily in simulation, and the 'lossless' claim showing small drops on harder tasks (e.g., Tool: 50→48%). Open questions include whether the 3D lattice generalizes to non-DiT backbones, how the method behaves under distribution shift in real-world deployment, and whether the trajectory-level training objective can be further simplified.
