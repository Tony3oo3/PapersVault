## Problem

Existing methods for hand-object interaction either predict hand actions from egocentric video or generate video from action cues in a one-way fashion, and unified models that couple both domains typically treat video generation as an auxiliary objective with limited visual fidelity. The non-linear relationship between structured hand poses and pixel-level scene observations makes it difficult to learn a single model that can both synthesize high-fidelity egocentric videos conditioned on hand motion and forecast future hand actions from visual context.

## Input / Output

The model consumes egocentric video frames at 832×480 resolution (up to 49 frames, temporally clipped to satisfy T=1+4t for the VAE), 138-dimensional hand action vectors per frame (9D rotation, 3D translation, and 3D coordinates of 21 joints per hand), coarse text prompts, and MANO-rendered hand images extracted via a BoT-SORT + HaMeR reconstruction pipeline. It produces future egocentric video frames and/or future hand action sequences depending on the task configuration, with unused domain tokens replaced by learnable mask tokens to support flexible multi-task inference.

## Method

HandWorld builds a dual-branch condition network where a video branch (initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) and an action branch interact through bidirectional cross-attention at every layer, producing hierarchical shared conditions that are fused into two decoupled diffusion transformers via residual addition at each layer. MANO-rendered hands serve as an auxiliary intermediate representation that bridges the visual and action domains, providing geometrically aligned, environment-independent structure to stabilize cross-domain alignment. Generation follows the Rectified Flow matching framework, where the model predicts a velocity field conditioned on the shared representation, with a Markov assumption limiting context to the last m states. A flexible multi-task training strategy selectively activates the video or action DiT while always optimizing the shared condition network, enabling the same model to perform action-conditioned video generation, action forecasting, or joint prediction.

## Results

On EgoDex, HandWorld achieves the best performance across all visual quality and hand action metrics for action-conditioned egocentric video generation: CLIP 0.9568, PSNR 26.27, SSIM 0.874, LPIPS 0.132, FVD 133.9, CLIP_hand 0.9461, and IoU 0.8291. For hand action forecasting (best-of-K), HandWorld with flow matching achieves the lowest average distance (0.044 at K=1, 0.039 at K=5) and final distance (0.051 at K=1, 0.045 at K=5), outperforming all X-IL baselines. The FVD improvement is particularly striking: 133.9 vs. 482.3 for the strongest text-conditioned baseline (Wan2.2) and 1303.9 for HANDI, indicating substantially better temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandWorld outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the three text-conditioned video generation models (AnimateAnything, CogVideoX-I2V-5B, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B), HandWorld improves FVD by 3.6×–6.8× (e.g., 133.9 vs. 482.3 for Wan2.2) and IoU by 1.7×–3.0×, demonstrating that MANO-based action conditioning provides far more precise hand control than text prompts. Against HANDI (mask-conditioned), HandWorld reduces FVD from 1303.9 to 133.9 (≈10×) and raises IoU from 0.1825 to 0.8291, showing that region masks are too coarse for fine-grained hand geometry. Against Wang et al.'s skeleton-based visual prompts, HandWorld improves FVD from 516.1 to 133.9 and IoU from 0.5752 to 0.8291, indicating that 2D skeletons cannot capture subtle finger deformations that MANO meshes encode. For action forecasting, HandWorld (FM) beats the best X-IL baseline (EncDec-FM) on both average distance (0.044 vs. 0.051 at K=1) and final distance (0.051 vs. 0.070 at K=1), with a notably smaller gap between K=1 and K=5 predictions, suggesting more stable single-sample outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

HandWorld extends the line of unified video-action models (e.g., [9, 19, 44]) that previously treated video generation as an auxiliary objective subordinate to action policy learning, by elevating both domains to co-equal generative targets under a shared cross-domain condition. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion backbone and the Rectified Flow / flow-matching formulation for the generative process, while introducing a dual-branch condition network with bidirectional cross-attention and a MANO-rendered hand as an intermediate visual-domain signal that bridges the heterogeneous action and video spaces. This departs from prior controllable video generation that relied on coarse conditions (text, masks, skeletons) by using a physically grounded, geometry-aligned hand representation for fine-grained action control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that high-fidelity egocentric video synthesis and accurate hand action forecasting can be achieved simultaneously within a single model, which is relevant for embodied agents, VR/AR, and robotic simulation that require coherent perception-action coupling. A key open limitation is object-level temporal consistency: without object annotations in EgoDex, the model cannot learn object dynamics, leading to flickering or disappearance of small manipulated objects. Future work on object-level supervision (trajectories, contact maps) or dedicated consistency objectives would be needed to close this gap, and the approach's generalizability beyond tabletop HOI scenarios remains untested.
