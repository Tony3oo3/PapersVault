## Problem

Memory-efficient transfer learning (METL) methods that use lightweight side networks reduce training memory by restricting gradient flow to the side branch, but they inevitably retain the side network during inference, adding computational overhead that contradicts the goal of efficient deployment. The paper asks whether one can achieve both memory-efficient training and fast inference by discarding the side network after training without sacrificing accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263725/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts single-modality inputs (RGB images, text sequences, or video clips) and produces task-specific outputs such as retrieval rankings, class labels, answer text, or bounding boxes depending on the downstream task. During training, the input is processed by both the frozen backbone and the learnable side network in parallel, with intermediate features from the backbone fused into the side network at each layer; during inference, only the backbone with a task-specific head is used, so the side network adds zero latency. The backbone's final output is mapped through a newly designed output layer (GAP + linear) to produce logits, since the backbone itself is not followed by a task head in the original architecture.

## Method

MDPD introduces a dual-path knowledge distillation framework where the backbone and side network alternately serve as teacher and student: feature-based distillation (B→S) guides the side network to extract richer representations using a low-rank bottleneck for dimension alignment, while logits-based distillation (S→B) transfers task-specific knowledge from the side network's output layer back to the backbone. A key innovation is Hierarchical Feature-based Distillation, which applies direct feature imitation in shallow layers (where attention patterns are similar) but switches to masked feature generation in deep layers (where attention diverges), using random mask tokens and a small convolutional generation block to reconstruct teacher features from partially masked student features. During inference, the side network is entirely discarded, leaving only the optimized backbone, which accelerates inference by at least 25.2% while maintaining or improving accuracy over prior METL methods.

## Results

On image-text retrieval (Flickr30K, BERT+BUTD), MDPD achieves 81.9/61.1 R@1 (I-T/T-I) and 516.2 Rsum, outperforming UniPT (80.2/59.8, 510.5) and LST (77.9/57.3, 501.9) while reducing training memory by at least 59.5% compared to PETL methods. On VTAB-1K (ViT-B), MDPD reaches 78.3 mean Top-1 accuracy across 19 datasets, surpassing SHERL (77.9) and HST (78.1), with 88.89 FPS versus 48.70 for SHERL. On GLUE (T5-large), MDPD achieves 88.0 mean score with 415.3 ms inference latency, compared to SHERL's 87.5 at 667.4 ms. The key efficiency gain is a ≥25.2% inference speedup over SHERL/UniPT by discarding the side network at test time, while maintaining or improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDPD consistently outperforms all METL baselines (LST, UniPT, SHERL, HST, LoSA) on accuracy while matching or exceeding their inference speed. On Flickr30K ITR, MDPD gains +1.7 Rsum over UniPT (516.2 vs 510.5) and +14.3 over LST (516.2 vs 501.9), while achieving 24.05 QPS versus UniPT's 12.75 and LST's 10.73. Against PETL methods, MDPD matches or exceeds accuracy (e.g., 81.9 I-T vs SSF's 80.0, Adapter's 79.1) while reducing training memory from 8.4–9.4 GB to 3.4 GB. On VTAB-1K, MDPD achieves 78.3 mean accuracy (vs SHERL 77.9, HST 78.1, LoSA 77.8) at 88.89 FPS, nearly 2× faster than SHERL (48.70 FPS) and HST (44.17 FPS). On GLUE with T5-large, MDPD reaches 88.0 mean (vs SHERL 87.5, UniPT 87.2) with 415.3 ms latency, roughly 38% faster than SHERL (667.4 ms). The method slightly trails Fully-FT on some metrics (e.g., 516.2 vs 513.5 Rsum on BERT+BUTD is actually a win, but 365.8 vs 389.2 Rsum on ViT VTR) yet uses 14.0 GB vs 48.8 GB training memory and 9.7M vs 151.3M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the side-network-based memory-efficient transfer learning (METL) paradigm established by LST, UniPT, and SHERL, which reduce training memory by confining backpropagation to a lightweight parallel branch. The key departure is the introduction of Masked Dual Path Distillation, where the frozen backbone and learnable side network mutually serve as teacher and student (backbone→side for features, side→backbone for logits), enabling the side network to be discarded at inference without accuracy loss. The hierarchical feature-based distillation with masked generation in deep layers addresses a specific limitation of prior feature distillation: attention pattern divergence between teacher and student at different depths, a problem not explicitly handled by earlier METL or KD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in resolving the fundamental tension in side-network METL: training memory efficiency comes at the cost of inference overhead, which undermines the goal of efficient deployment. MDPD's fading mechanism (discarding the side network post-training) achieves at least 25.2% inference speedup with only ~0.9% accuracy drop, making these methods viable for latency-sensitive applications. However, the accuracy gains over SOTA METL baselines remain modest (1–3% on most benchmarks), and the approach still requires the side network during training, so it does not eliminate the training-time memory cost of maintaining two forward passes. Open questions include whether the dual-path distillation generalizes to generative or autoregressive architectures where the side network's role as a 'teacher' for logits is less natural, and whether the masked generation strategy in deep layers scales to very deep encoders (e.g., 100+ layer ViTs).
