## Problem

Existing MLLM-based embedding methods treat the model as a simple encoder by extracting a single [EOS] hidden state, which disrupts the MLLM's fine-grained grounding (since [EOS] tokens are optimized for vocabulary logits, not semantic structure) and cannot retain fine-grained details for high-dimensional inputs like images. Moreover, relying solely on a global contrastive loss fails to preserve the compositional vision-language understanding acquired during generative pretraining, leaving the model's autoregressive and world-knowledge capabilities underutilized.

## Input / Output

Each multimodal input (text, image, or both) is augmented with K learnable tokens (16 in the main configuration) appended to the tokenized sequence before passing through the MLLM. The output is a single fused d-dimensional embedding per query or document, obtained by averaging the K learnable-token hidden states, which is then used for cosine-similarity ranking. At inference time the matching stage is discarded, so the only overhead relative to a standard single-vector MLLM encoder is processing 16 extra tokens, keeping latency comparable to VLM2Vec.

## Method

ReMatch introduces two complementary mechanisms trained end-to-end with a combined loss (InfoNCE contrastive + orthogonal regularization + generative matching). First, Multi Embedding Fusion (MEF) appends K learnable tokens to each input; their last-layer hidden states form a multi-vector representation that is regularized by a soft orthogonality penalty (penalizing pairwise inner products of L2-normalized token embeddings) and then fused by averaging into a single vector. Second, Multi-View Query-Document Matching (MVQDM) re-uses the same MLLM in a chat-style prompt to autoregressively generate 'Yes'/'No' relevance judgments over all eight combinations of raw data and MLP-projected embeddings for the query and document pair; a unified causal attention mask processes all eight views in a single forward pass, and random slot assignment of positive/negative documents prevents positional leakage. The matching loss provides instance-level discrimination with stronger gradients on hard negatives, while the multi-view design lets the model attend to both fine-grained embedding signals and global raw-data context, preserving the backbone's compositional reasoning.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark (36 tasks), ReMatch achieves 73.7% overall Hit@1 with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (78.1% in-domain, 68.2% out-of-domain), setting a new state-of-the-art. At the 2B scale, ReMatch reaches 69.2% overall, outperforming the next-best method (B3++, 68.1%) by 1.1%. In zero-shot cross-modal retrieval, ReMatch-7B achieves 93.9% Recall@1 on SugarCrepe (attribution-adding), surpassing the strongest baseline (UniME-V2, 79.0%) by 14.9%, and 95.4% on Flickr30K image-to-text retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMatch consistently outperforms all MLLM-based baselines at comparable model sizes: at 2B it beats B3++ (68.1%) by 1.1%, at 3B it surpasses MoCa (67.5%) by 2.7%, and at 7B it exceeds QQMM (72.5%) by 1.2% on overall MMEB Hit@1. Compared to its direct baseline VLM2Vec, ReMatch delivers substantial gains of +9.9% (2B) and +10.4% (7B), with particularly large improvements on VQA (+4.7% for 2B, +3.6% for 7B) where the generative matching stage better leverages the MLLM's world knowledge. In zero-shot retrieval, ReMatch-7B sets new bests on four of five benchmarks and outperforms UniME-V2-7B by 14.9% on SugarCrepe's attribution-adding subset (93.9% vs. 79.0%). ReMatch also surpasses the larger mmE5 (11B, 69.8%) by 3.9% at the 7B scale, demonstrating that the matching strategy compensates for model size. The only notable trade-off is that ReMatch with Qwen2-VL underperforms the Qwen2.5-VL variant on VQA and grounding, reflecting the base model's generative weaknesses propagating into the embedding space.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMatch builds directly on VLM2Vec's MLLM-as-encoder framework but departs from the prevailing practice of extracting a single [EOS] hidden state as the embedding. It draws conceptually from ALBEF/BLIP's image-text matching modules, but integrates them end-to-end into the MLLM's own autoregressive training loop rather than as a separate reranking stage. The multi-token embedding strategy is positioned against MetaEmbed's ColBERT-style late interaction, achieving similar representational richness while fusing tokens into a single vector to preserve single-vector inference efficiency. The unified attention mask for processing eight query-document views in one forward pass is a practical engineering contribution that distinguishes it from prior multi-view approaches requiring separate passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that treating a generative MLLM as a pure encoder degrades its compositional reasoning and that feeding the model's own embeddings back as input provides a useful self-supervisory signal—offers a principled recipe for future MLLM-based retrieval systems. The strong zero-shot transfer across five diverse retrieval benchmarks (short/long caption, compositional) suggests the learned representations are genuinely more semantically structured rather than overfit to MMEB. Open questions include whether the matching stage's benefit persists at larger scales (beyond 7B), whether the approach extends naturally to video retrieval, and whether the modest 0.5–0.9% gains from MVQDM alone justify the additional training complexity compared to simply tuning the contrastive objective more aggressively.
