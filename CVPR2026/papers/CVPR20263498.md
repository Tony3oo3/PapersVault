## Problem

Diffusion-based video restoration methods inherit the standard classifier-free guidance formulation, which evaluates conditional and unconditional branches at the same timestep, forcing the model to remain overly faithful to corrupted or warped input geometry rather than reconstructing plausible structure. This coupling is particularly harmful for AI-generated videos exhibiting warped faces, body misalignments, and spatial artifacts, where existing methods amplify distortions instead of correcting them. The paper addresses this by asking whether the temporal evaluation of guidance signals can be decoupled at inference time to break this replication behavior without any retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a distorted low-resolution video (480p clips of 3–5 s at 16 fps in the GenWarp480 benchmark) and a text prompt, operating in the latent space of a 3D VAE with a DiT backbone constrained to 512×512 token resolution. The primary output is a geometry-corrected, temporally stable video; an optional plug-and-play restoration or super-resolution module (e.g., any off-the-shelf VSR network) can then be applied to recover high-frequency details, making the pipeline composable with arbitrary downstream enhancers.

## Method

Decoupled Time Guidance (DTG) modifies the Rectified Flow ODE sampling step by evaluating the denoiser at two different timesteps: the unconditional (or anchor) branch at a cleaner time τ = t − Δ and the conditional branch at the current time t. The update rule x_new = F(x, τ) + η[F(x, t) − F(x, τ)] performs an anchored extrapolation that, via the Tweedie approximation, corresponds to denoising at an implicit effective noise level σ_eff² = σ_τ² + η(σ_t² − σ_τ²), thereby raising the effective SNR and discouraging replication of spurious geometry. A schedule anneals Δ from a large initial value to 0 and η from below 1 to 1 (exponential decay performs best), smoothly transitioning the process from early structure correction to late detail refinement. The entire procedure is training-free, model-agnostic, and requires only a single additional denoiser evaluation per sampling step on top of the pretrained Wan T2V backbone.

## Results

On the purpose-built GenWarp480 benchmark, DTG-Restore achieves the best scores on LAION Aesthetic Predictor (4.642), MANIQA (0.314), and CLIP-IQA (0.541), and the second-best MUSIQ (48.83), outperforming all six baselines on these perceptual metrics. On standard VSR benchmarks (SPMCS, UDM10, REDS30), the method is competitive but trails reconstruction-centric models like RealViformer and MGLD-VSR on PSNR/SSIM, as it is explicitly designed to prioritize perceptual realism over pixel fidelity. In a 50-participant user study, DTG-Restore scores highest on all three dimensions—Sharpness/Clearness (4.40), Motion/Smoothness (4.52), and Aesthetic Quality (4.36)—with a clear margin over the second-place VEnhancer (3.84/3.80/3.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTG-Restore outperforms all six baselines on LAION AP, MUSIQ, MANIQA, and CLIP-IQA on GenWarp480, with the largest margins over RealViformer (+0.644 LAION AP, +0.058 CLIP-IQA) and STAR (+0.185 LAION AP, +0.123 CLIP-IQA). The only metric where DTG-Restore trails is NIQE, where SeedVR2 (3.915) and RealViformer (4.014) score lower (better), which the authors attribute to these methods' heavy smoothing that reduces natural texture variation. On standard VSR benchmarks, RealViformer and MGLD-VSR lead on PSNR and SSIM (e.g., RealViformer 24.18 PSNR on SPMCS vs. 22.76 for DTG-Restore), while DTG-Restore achieves the best SSIM among diffusion-based methods on SPMCS (0.613) and competitive LPIPS on UDM10 (0.271). Against SDEdit (Table 4), DTG's per-step temporal decoupling surpasses the best SDEdit configuration (t_start=0.5) by +0.26 LAION AP and +0.029 CLIP-IQA while reducing the warp score from 0.118 to 0.071, confirming that continuous decoupling outperforms single-point resampling. In the user study, DTG-Restore leads all methods by at least 0.52 points on every dimension, with the largest gap in Motion/Smoothness (4.52 vs. 3.80 for VEnhancer), validating that the temporal decoupling directly improves perceived temporal stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video diffusion transformer (DiT) backbone and the Rectified Flow formulation used by modern T2V models (e.g., Wan), but departs from the standard classifier-free guidance paradigm by decoupling the temporal evaluation of conditional and unconditional branches. Conceptually, it extends the SDEdit idea of re-noising at a single timestep into a per-step, annealed temporal offset applied throughout sampling, and positions itself as a training-free alternative to fine-tuned restoration backbones like VEnhancer, STAR, and SeedVR2. The plug-and-play composition with off-the-shelf SR modules draws on the cascaded generation-refinement pipeline common in T2V systems, but inverts the dependency: DTG corrects geometry first, then a separate network handles texture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of DTG lowers the barrier to applying generative priors for video restoration without the compute cost of fine-tuning large diffusion backbones, and the GenWarp480 benchmark fills a notable gap by providing a purpose-built testbed for generative-specific degradations (warped faces, body misalignments) that standard VSR datasets do not capture. Limitations include the method's dependence on the specific timestep structure of the underlying flow model, its acknowledged trade-off against pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM), and the fact that the plug-and-play SR stage still requires a separate trained network. Open questions include whether the decoupling strategy generalizes to non-Rectified-Flow schedules, longer video sequences, or multi-step cascaded architectures, and whether the annealing schedule can be learned or adapted per-degradation-type.
