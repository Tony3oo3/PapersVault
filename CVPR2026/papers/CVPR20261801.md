## Problem

Existing All-in-One Image Restoration methods rely on task-specific priors (prompts, embeddings) or fixed latent routing, causing cross-task gradient conflicts, limited generalization to unknown or compound degradations, and a lack of semantic interpretability. No prior framework autonomously perceives degradation attributes and adaptively allocates restoration capacity in a content-aware, frequency-aligned manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261801/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image at 512×512 as input and produces a restored RGB image of the same resolution. Before the planner sees the image, a label-free low-level feature pool computes lightweight global statistics (oriented streak strength, blob density, Laplacian responses, dark-channel values, luminance, spatial dimensions) that are injected as textual hints alongside a universal restoration instruction and an expert rule encoding the task-to-frequency-band taxonomy. The planner outputs a structured tuple (task, dominant frequency band, restoration pipeline, reasoning) that is then encoded into conditioning tokens for the executor.

## Method

FAPE-IR couples a frozen Qwen2.5-VL planner with a FLUX-based diffusion executor via a Frequency-Aware LoRA-MoE module. The planner parses degradation semantics and frequency needs from the image, producing text tokens that feed a token-wise text router; simultaneously, a depthwise FIR low-pass filter splits intermediate generation tokens into low/high-frequency components whose relative energies drive a spectral router. The two gating signals are fused with a learnable scalar and top-1 routed to select either a high-frequency or low-frequency LoRA expert, updating the frozen FLUX projection as W + Σ αᵢAᵢBᵢ. Training replaces flow-matching with adversarial supervision (frozen SigLIP-v2 multi-level discriminator) plus MSE and LPIPS losses, augmented by a single frequency regularizer that penalizes out-of-band energy on each expert's adapter output, enforcing band specialization without additional architectural complexity.

## Results

FAPE-IR achieves state-of-the-art or near-best results across all seven AIO-IR tasks. On weather-related degradations, gains are substantial: deraining PSNR improves from 21.94 dB (PromptIR) to 28.30 dB, desnowing from 24.19 dB (AdaIR) to 30.29 dB, and dehazing from 19.89 dB (AdaIR) to 33.85 dB, with FID dropping by 4–20×. On super-resolution, FAPE-IR reaches 28.53 dB PSNR and 85.82 FID, outperforming the best prior method (PASD: 26.87 dB, 120.46 FID). On denoising and low-light enhancement, FAPE-IR trails the top PSNR by ~1 dB but leads on all perceptual metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, FID, DISTS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAPE-IR decisively outperforms all five AIO-IR baselines (PromptIR, FoundIR, DFPIR, MoCE-IR, AdaIR) on deraining (+6.4 dB over PromptIR), desnowing (+6.1 dB over AdaIR), dehazing (+14.0 dB over AdaIR), and deblurring (+0.1 dB over DFPIR), with FID reductions of 4–20×. On denoising, FAPE-IR trails MoCE-IR by 1.3 dB in PSNR (30.34 vs 31.63) but leads on SSIM (0.87 vs 0.84), LPIPS (0.10 vs 0.16), FID (30.33 vs 56.74), and DISTS (0.09 vs 0.14), indicating superior perceptual quality. Similarly, on low-light enhancement, DFPIR edges out FAPE-IR by 0.6 dB in PSNR (25.92 vs 25.34), yet FAPE-IR achieves better SSIM (0.90 vs 0.90), LPIPS (0.11 vs 0.16), FID (40.30 vs 52.15), and DISTS (0.09 vs 0.13). On super-resolution, FAPE-IR surpasses all six SR baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over PASD (+1.66 dB PSNR, −34.6 FID) and PURE (+4.06 dB PSNR, −35.3 FID). Inference time is 1.57 s on H200, roughly 15× faster than PURE (201.67 s) while using 38.92 GB memory, compared to 2.11–3.92 GB for the AIO-IR baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAPE-IR carves new ground at the intersection of two largely separate research threads: all-in-one image restoration (AIO-IR) and unified multimodal understanding-generation models. Prior AIO-IR methods (PromptIR, AdaIR, DFPIR, MoCE-IR) rely on task-level priors or fixed latent routing without semantic understanding, while recent MLLM+Diffusion unified models (BAGEL, Janus, BLIP3-o) target high-level semantic editing rather than pixel-accurate restoration. FAPE-IR repurposes the MLLM+Diffusion paradigm for low-level vision by using a frozen Qwen2.5-VL as a frequency-aware planner that outputs interpretable restoration plans, which condition a FLUX-based executor equipped with a novel Frequency-Aware LoRA-MoE module that routes between high- and low-frequency experts via both semantic tokens and FIR-filtered spectral energy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap: existing AIO-IR methods lack content-aware, interpretable degradation understanding, while unified multimodal models lack the pixel-level fidelity required for restoration. The frequency-aware routing provides a transparent mechanism for cross-task knowledge sharing (within the same band) and isolation (across bands), and the zero-shot generalization to compound degradations (CDD-11) suggests the planner-executor coupling captures compositional structure. However, the 38.92G inference footprint (vs. 2–4G for competitors) and dependence on a large frozen MLLM at inference time raise practical deployment questions, and the compound-degradation generalization is demonstrated only qualitatively. Open questions include whether the binary high/low frequency split is sufficient for more nuanced spectral tasks, whether the adversarial training advantage over flow-matching holds at larger scales, and whether the planner can be distilled into a lighter module without losing interpretability.
