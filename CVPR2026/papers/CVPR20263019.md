## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods either produce individual objects rather than complete worlds, generate monolithic geometry that cannot be edited or used in game engines, or fail to guarantee that the resulting scene is actually traversable end-to-end. No prior system produces large, functionally correct 3D worlds that are simultaneously decomposed into individually editable, high-quality textured meshes compatible with standard game engines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single free-form text prompt as input and produces a fully textured 3D mesh scene spanning roughly 50×50 m, decomposed into individually editable objects, along with a navigable mesh (navmesh) enabling real-time character traversal. The entire pipeline from prompt to game-ready assets completes in approximately 5 minutes with parallel GPU execution. Unlike view-based or Gaussian-splatting approaches, the output is explicit geometry with per-object UV textures, making it directly deployable in game engines without further processing.

## Method

WorldGen is a four-stage pipeline that synergizes procedural generation with 3D latent diffusion. Stage I uses an LLM to parse the text prompt into JSON parameters driving a procedural generator that produces a coarse blockout layout; a navmesh is extracted from this layout, and a depth-conditioned image generator creates a reference image from an isometric depth render of the blockout. Stage II fine-tunes a VecSet-based 3D diffusion transformer with navmesh conditioning (point-cloud encoding injected via cross-attention) to produce a holistic scene mesh that respects navigability constraints even in occluded regions. Stage III extends AutoPartGen with connectivity-based part ordering and a binary remainder token to decompose the monolithic mesh into individual objects in ~1 minute. Stage IV enhances each object independently: an LLM-VLM generates a high-resolution per-object image for style-consistent detail, a mesh-enhancement model refines geometry, and a multi-view diffusion model produces the final UV texture.

## Results

On the navmesh alignment benchmark (50 procedurally generated scenes), the navmesh-conditioned model achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.022, which is 40–50% lower than all baselines (ranging from 0.038 to 0.049). On the scene decomposition benchmark, the proposed method achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.061 and F-score@0.05 of 0.853, substantially outperforming AutoPartGen (0.144 / 0.683) while reducing inference time from 10 minutes to 1 minute. The full pipeline from text prompt to a fully textured, navigable 3D scene completes in under 5 minutes with parallel GPU execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AutoPartGen [9], the proposed decomposition method wins decisively on every metric: Chamfer distance drops from 0.144 to 0.061 (58% reduction), F-score@0.05 rises from 0.683 to 0.853, and inference time falls from 10 minutes to 1 minute (10× speedup) thanks to connectivity-ordered generation and the remainder-part shortcut. Against the unnamed image-to-3D baselines (Models A, B, C) in the navmesh alignment evaluation, the navmesh-conditioned model achieves a Chamfer distance of 0.022 versus 0.038–0.049, a 40–50% improvement in spatial alignment with the input navigation constraints. The qualitative comparison with Marble [76] (World Labs) shows that Marble achieves high visual fidelity near its single conditioned viewpoint but degrades noticeably as the camera moves farther away, whereas WorldGen maintains geometric and stylistic consistency across its full ~50×50 m traversable extent with explicit mesh geometry suitable for game engines. Overall, WorldGen wins on all quantitative comparisons and demonstrates a clear qualitative advantage in scene completeness, navigability, and editability over both prior decomposition methods and the Marble world-generation system.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldGen synthesizes three distinct research threads: (1) 3D latent diffusion for holistic shape generation (VecSet/3DShape2VecSet), (2) procedural content generation for structural layout, and (3) autoregressive part decomposition (AutoPartGen) for compositional refinement. It departs from view-based scene generators (PixelSynth, Text2Room, and their many follow-ups) by avoiding incremental, monolithic reconstruction in favor of a single holistic 3D latent diffusion pass conditioned on a navmesh. It also departs from prior 3D latent-space scene generators (SceneFactor, BlockFusion, NuiScene) that suffer from limited diversity due to scarce 3D scene training data, by leveraging a powerful text-to-image model to supply visual diversity and then reconstructing 3D geometry from that image. The navmesh-conditioned reconstruction is the key architectural departure: rather than segmenting and reconstructing objects independently (which loses contextual coherence), the model generates the entire scene as one mesh guided by navigability constraints, then decomposes it post-hoc.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldGen demonstrates that traversable, game-engine-compatible 3D worlds can be produced end-to-end from a single text prompt in under five minutes, a practical milestone for interactive content creation. The navmesh-conditioning strategy offers a general mechanism for injecting hard structural constraints (navigability, collision) into 3D generative models, which could transfer to other constrained generation tasks. However, the method is currently limited to bounded, single-story environments due to reliance on a single reference view, and the lack of asset instancing may hurt rendering efficiency in dense scenes. Open questions include whether the pipeline can be extended to multi-layered or unbounded worlds (e.g., via incremental generation à la SynCity), whether the compositional refinement stage can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization, and how well the navmesh conditioning generalizes to indoor or highly structured architectural scenes.
