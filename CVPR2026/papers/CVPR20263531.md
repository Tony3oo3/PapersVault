## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) overwhelmingly rely on autoregressive (AR) language backbones, and prior attempts to incorporate diffusion into MLLMs either still depend on AR models for language capability or use discrete diffusion with limited language modeling capacity. Whether a purely diffusion-based MLLM—using masked diffusion for both training and sampling—can match AR-based models on multimodal understanding remains an open question, particularly given that large language diffusion models like LLaDA had only recently demonstrated competitive text performance.

## Input / Output

LLaDA-V accepts single images, multiple images, video clips, and text instructions as input. Visual inputs are encoded by a SigLIP 2 vision tower (siglip2-so400m-patch14-384) and projected into the LLaDA embedding space via a two-layer MLP. The model generates natural language responses through an iterative masked-diffusion reverse process (starting from a fully masked sequence and progressively unmasking tokens) rather than sequential next-token prediction, supporting multi-turn dialogues where previous turns condition subsequent responses.

## Method

LLaDA-V extends the LLaDA-8B masked diffusion language model to multimodal understanding by adding a SigLIP 2 vision encoder and a two-layer MLP projector, following the standard visual instruction tuning framework. The training objective generalizes LLaDA's masked diffusion loss to multi-turn multimodal dialogues: image features and prompts remain unmasked while only response tokens are randomly masked at a sampled time step, and the model is trained to predict the original tokens. A key architectural choice is the use of bidirectional (no-mask) attention across all dialogue turns rather than causal attention, which ablation studies show improves performance on 7 of 12 benchmarks by enabling comprehensive context understanding during mask prediction. Inference employs LLaDA's low-confidence remasking strategy and is accelerated via Fast-dLLM's approximate KV cache with a configurable refresh interval, enabling flexible accuracy-throughput trade-offs. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: projector alignment on LLaVA-Pretrain, full-model instruction tuning on 10M single-image plus 2M diverse multimodal samples from MAmmoTH-VL, and a final reasoning-enhancement stage on VisualWebInstruct with a hybrid thinking/no-thinking mixing strategy.

## Results

On 18 multimodal benchmarks, LLaDA-V (LLaDA-8B language tower) outperforms its autoregressive counterpart LLaMA3-V (LLaMA3-8B tower, identical training pipeline) on 11 of 18 tasks, including MMMU (48.6 vs. 45.4), MMMU-Pro standard (35.2 vs. 28.3), MMStar (60.1 vs. 56.5), MMBench (82.9 vs. 79.8), and InfoVQA (66.3 vs. 58.9). Among purely diffusion-based MLLMs, LLaDA-V achieves state-of-the-art results, surpassing LaViDa-L (43.3 MMMU), Dimple (45.2 MMMU), and MMaDA (30.2 MMMU). Against the stronger autoregressive Qwen2-VL (Qwen2-7B tower), LLaDA-V narrows the gap substantially, reaching near-parity on MMStar (60.1 vs. 60.7) while trailing on MMMU (48.6 vs. 54.1). Inference throughput with Fast-dLLM reaches 32.4 tokens/s on MathVerse, slightly exceeding LLaMA3-V's 30.5 tokens/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the matched autoregressive baseline LLaMA3-V (same vision tower, projector, and training data; only the language tower differs), LLaDA-V wins on 11 of 18 benchmarks, with the largest margins on MMMU-Pro standard (+6.9), InfoVQA (+7.4), MMMU (+3.2), MMStar (+3.6), and MMBench (+3.1); it trails on AI2D (−3.3), DocVQA (−2.3), RealworldQA (−2.8), and MathVista (−2.4). Compared to the stronger Qwen2-VL (Qwen2-7B tower), LLaDA-V generally underperforms (e.g., MMMU 48.6 vs. 54.1, MMMU-Pro 35.2 vs. 43.5) but achieves near-parity on MMStar (60.1 vs. 60.7), suggesting the gap is primarily attributable to the weaker language backbone rather than the diffusion architecture. Among pure diffusion MLLMs, LLaDA-V is the clear leader: it surpasses LaViDa-L by +5.3 on MMMU and +4.8 on MathVista, Dimple by +3.4 on MMMU, and MMaDA by +18.4 on MMMU. Against hybrid AR+Diffusion models (MetaMorph, JanusFlow, Show-o, Orthus), LLaDA-V dominates across all overlapping benchmarks, with MetaMorph scoring only 41.8 on MMMU versus LLaDA-V's 48.6. The data-scaling experiments further show that LLaDA-V's advantage over LLaMA3-V on knowledge-heavy benchmarks (MMMU, MMMU-Pro) widens with more training data, while the gap on chart/document tasks (AI2D, DocVQA) persists regardless of data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaDA-V extends the visual instruction tuning framework (LLaVA) by replacing the autoregressive language tower with LLaDA, a large language diffusion model, and adapting the training objective to masked diffusion over multi-turn dialogues. Prior attempts to bring diffusion into MLLMs either retained an AR backbone for language modeling or used discrete diffusion with limited language capacity; LLaDA-V is the first to be purely diffusion-based in both training and sampling. The architectural choice of bidirectional (no-mask) attention over causal attention represents a deliberate departure from AR conventions, motivated by the spatial nature of visual inputs, and is supported by ablation evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first controlled evidence that a purely diffusion-based MLLM can match or exceed an autoregressive counterpart on multimodal understanding, even with a weaker language backbone, suggesting that bidirectional attention may be inherently better suited to vision-language tasks. However, LLaDA-V still lags behind strong AR models like Qwen2-VL on most benchmarks and underperforms on chart/document and real-world scene tasks, indicating the advantage is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the bidirectional-attention benefit persists as language diffusion models close the gap with leading LLMs, and whether the approach scales to larger model sizes and more diverse modalities.
