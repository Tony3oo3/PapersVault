## Problem

Existing vision-language navigation benchmarks evaluate embodiment-agnostic goal-reaching, treating all agents as interchangeable point entities that can traverse any connected path. In reality, an agent's physical dimensions, vertical traversal limits, and manipulation abilities fundamentally determine which routes are feasible—a wheelchair user cannot climb stairs, a quadruped cannot operate an elevator, and a wide humanoid robot cannot squeeze through a narrow corridor. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether VLMs can produce capability-conditioned navigation plans that respect these constraints across multiple human and robotic embodiments.

## Input / Output

Each query is a Space-Task-Capability triple: a touring video of the indoor space (rendered at 2 FPS from 1.5 m eye height with 75° FOV in Habitat), a manually annotated navigation graph with semantic nodes and walkable edges, a JSON agent profile encoding physical footprint, vertical traversal limits, and manipulation abilities, and a natural-language task specifying source and target nodes with fine-grained positional descriptions. The VLM must output a binary feasibility prediction (CAN/CANNOT), a node-sequence path through the graph, and—when infeasible—a concise rationale. Evaluation uses four metrics: Feas-F1 for the binary call, Path Validity for graph-structural correctness, Route Traversability Accuracy for per-edge passability under the given embodiment, and Reasoning Validity scored by an LLM-as-judge (validated at 89% agreement with human raters).

## Method

CapNav is constructed from 45 real-world 3D scans (HM3D, Matterport3D) with manually recorded touring videos and navigation graphs; Gemini 2.5 Pro generates 2,365 natural-language navigation tasks that are then manually verified. Traversability is annotated per-edge and per-agent (5,075 binary labels across five embodiments) using a custom 3D annotation interface that renders agent-specific colliders and supports manual moving/turning to verify clearance. The benchmark defines five agent profiles—adult, wheelchair user, humanoid robot, sweeping robot, and quadrupedal robot—each with distinct physical dimensions and operational limits that produce different feasible-route sets (e.g., 100% feasible tasks for adults vs. 22% for humanoids). Thirteen VLMs are evaluated under thinking and non-thinking modes across four frame-budget settings (16, 32, 64 frames, and 1 FPS), with a composite CapNav score averaging the four metrics. A pilot LoRA fine-tuning experiment on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (expanding tasks from 2K to 13K) shows partial improvement in dimension-aware reasoning but introduces false positives on narrow passages.

## Results

On CapNav, the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-pro achieves a composite score of 67.18 (Feas-F1 84.30, PV 73.00, RTA 79.15, RV 32.29), followed by GPT-5-pro (66.37) and Gemini-2.5-flash (64.07). All top models exceed the human average (60.59) but remain below the best human performance (74.77), while the random-walk baseline scores only 29.35. Performance degrades systematically with tighter mobility constraints: the humanoid embodiment (no stairs, 0.9 m clearance) yields the lowest average score (39.12%) versus the unconstrained adult baseline (57.83%). Spatial-reasoning-specialized models (Spatial-MLLM-4B at 30.15, Video-R1-7B at 37.39) perform substantially worse than general-purpose VLMs despite their targeted training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-pro leads all 13 models on composite CapNav (67.18), path validity (73.00), and route traversability (79.15), outperforming GPT-5-pro (66.37) by 0.81 points and Gemini-2.5-flash (64.07) by 3.11. Among open-source models, Doubao-Seed-1.6 in thinking mode (62.12) is the strongest, exceeding Qwen3-VL-8B (55.94) by 6.18 and Intern-VL3.5-8B (53.72) by 8.40, but still trails Gemini-2.5-pro by 5.06. GPT-4.1 (55.82) underperforms its successor GPT-5-pro (66.37) by 10.55 points, with the gap concentrated in path validity (49.00 vs. 67.90) and traversability (65.51 vs. 75.89). Spatial-reasoning-specialized models fail dramatically: Spatial-MLLM-4B (30.15) and Video-R1-7B (37.39) score near or below the random-walk baseline (29.35) on path validity (5.04 and 25.50 respectively), indicating their spatial training does not transfer to capability-conditioned navigation. Across all models, the humanoid embodiment is the hardest (mean 39.12%), where even Gemini-2.5-pro drops to 54.47 versus 85.96 on the unconstrained human task—a 31.5-point gap that no other model narrows below 25 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

CapNav carves new ground at the intersection of VLN benchmarks and mobility-constrained navigation. Prior VLN work (R2R, R4R, RxR) evaluates embodiment-agnostic goal-reaching on sparse graphs, while accessibility and robotics literature studies single-embodiment constraints in isolation. Recent cross-embodiment efforts (VLN-PE, NaviTrace, VAMOS) begin to address the gap but remain simulator-centric or reduce the problem to trace prediction. CapNav departs by grounding capability-conditioned navigation in real-world 3D scans with per-edge traversability annotations and pluralistic route evaluation, rather than a single ground-truth trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed as navigation assistants for robots and assistive systems, the finding that performance degrades systematically under mobility constraints (humanoid: 39% vs. unconstrained human: 58%) signals a critical safety gap before real-world deployment. The universal 'dimension neglect'—all 13 models fail on narrow clearances and turning radii—exposes a fundamental limitation in VLM spatial reasoning that fine-tuning only partially addresses. Open questions include whether geometry-grounded representations (depth, topological priors) or RL-based training with embodiment-specific rewards can close the dimension-reasoning gap, and whether the visual bottleneck (more frames not helping weaker models) reflects an architectural limitation or a training-data issue.
