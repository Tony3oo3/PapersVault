## Problem

Existing regression-based visual localization methods on SD maps rely solely on attention mechanisms for cross-modal feature fusion and directly regress 3-DoF poses without geometric constraints, resulting in slow convergence, unstable gradients, and suboptimal accuracy. The paper identifies that a local BEV representation and its corresponding map tile inherently satisfy a homography relationship, yet no prior work exploits this geometric prior to guide both feature correspondence and pose decoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263933/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes six surround-view RGB images (128×352 each) and a 128 m × 128 m two-channel rasterized OpenStreetMap patch (roads and buildings) at 0.5 m/pixel, with simulated GPS noise of up to ±30° rotation and ±30 m translation. The BEV features span a 64 m × 64 m region at 0.25 m/pixel. Rather than outputting a raw pose vector, the model predicts a 3×3 homography matrix from which the 3-DoF pose (x, y, θ) is recovered via geometric projection of the BEV grid center and an auxiliary forward point, additionally producing a BEV semantic segmentation mask as an auxiliary output.

## Method

HOLO projects multi-view images into BEV space via an EfficientNet-B0 backbone with LSS-style depth-guided lifting, and encodes the rasterized SD map through a VGG16-based U-Net, then enforces semantic alignment between the two modalities to form homography-consistent feature pairs. For pose estimation, a Siamese ResNet extracts features from both modalities, and a dense correlation volume is precomputed once; an iterative pixel-motion block then crops local correlation patches guided by the current homography estimate, feeding them to a pose decoder that outputs corner displacements. These displacements are converted to a homography matrix via DLT, which both updates the projected coordinates for the next iteration and geometrically yields the 3-DoF pose. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a combined semantic BCE loss and pose regression loss (L2 translation + L1 rotation) over 6 refinement iterations, achieving 20 FPS inference with only ~1 GFLOP overhead per additional iteration.

## Results

On nuScenes, HOLO-drivable achieves Position Recall@1m/2m of 36.41%/61.21% and Orientation Recall@1°/2° of 73.74%/91.51%, with APE of 3.37 m and AOE of 1.02°. This represents a 16.31%/15.67% absolute gain in Position Recall@1m/2m over MapLocNet (20.10%/45.54%) under the same drivable-area supervision. The model runs at ~20 FPS during inference (6-iteration version) on an RTX A6000, and each additional iterative refinement step adds only 0.98 GFLOPs, far less than the 3.01–9.85 GFLOPs required by attention-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOLO-drivable substantially outperforms MapLocNet, the strongest camera-only regression baseline, by +16.31%/+15.67% in Position Recall@1m/2m and +15.13%/+7.41% in Orientation Recall@1°/2° under drivable-area supervision; under road-only supervision the gains are +10.30%/+8.82% (position) and +11.15%/+4.23% (orientation). Against the matching-based OrienterNet, HOLO more than doubles Position Recall@1m (36.41% vs. 15.74%) and nearly doubles Recall@2m (61.21% vs. 33.27%). Compared to U-BEV, HOLO improves Position Recall@1m by ~20 points (36.41% vs. 16.89%). The only method that surpasses HOLO on Position Recall@1m/2m is the multimodal SegLocNet-drivable (59.08%/76.04%), which leverages LiDAR input; however, HOLO achieves a much lower APE (3.37 m vs. 5.30 m) and AOE (1.02° vs. 10.11°), indicating more precise per-frame pose estimation despite the lower recall at tight thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOLO sits at the intersection of two research threads: BEV-based visual localization (building on LSS for depth-guided projection and OrienterNet for the BEV-to-map matching paradigm) and iterative homography estimation (adapting the correlation-volume + pixel-motion-block machinery from IHN and the modality-alignment-then-homography decomposition from SSHNet). The key departure from prior regression-based localizers (MapLocNet, BEV-Locator) is replacing implicit attention-based cross-modal fusion with explicit homography-guided correlation cropping, and replacing unconstrained 3-DoF pose regression with a geometrically constrained homography-to-pose mapping via DLT. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first work to unify BEV semantic reasoning with homography learning in a single end-to-end pipeline for image-to-map localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding pose estimation in a homography constraint, HOLO addresses a practical bottleneck in SD-map localization: attention-based fusion is computationally expensive per iteration, and direct pose regression converges slowly. The demonstrated 16% Recall@1m/2m gain over MapLocNet and the minimal per-iteration cost (+0.98 GFLOPs vs. +3.01 for MapLocNet) make this approach attractive for real-time deployment at ~20 FPS. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (nuScenes, two cities), dependence on OSM data quality and alignment, and the absence of temporal modeling. Open questions include whether the homography prior generalizes to 3D or multi-floor maps, how performance scales with map resolution mismatches beyond the tested 0.25/0.5 mpp range, and whether the approach transfers to other SD map sources or urban geometries.
