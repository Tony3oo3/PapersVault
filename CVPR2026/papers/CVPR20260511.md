## Problem

Visual object tracking under severe occlusion fails because spatial appearance is destroyed (breaking template-search similarity) while temporal trajectories are fragmented, and conventional frame rates cannot capture the inter-frame dynamics needed for autoregressive prediction of nonlinearly moving targets. Existing appearance-matching and trajectory-autoregression paradigms each break down under these coupled degradations, leaving a gap for robust tracking of fast, nonlinearly moving targets during short-term full occlusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired frame images (template at 112×112, search region at 224×224) and asynchronous inter-frame event streams, which are projected into forward and backward time-surface representations (256-level grayscale maps with histogram equalization) before being patchified and concatenated with trajectory tokens. It outputs a target bounding box (via classification score map plus GIoU/L1 regression) and, as an auxiliary signal, inter-frame displacement vectors predicted in both forward and backward temporal directions. The newly introduced FEOT dataset provides pixel-aligned frame-event pairs at 1070×610 resolution with 11-level occlusion annotations across 354 videos and 73K frames.

## Method

EvoTrack couples two branches fused by a gated adaptive unit. The Event-based Motion Autoregression (EMA) branch constructs a forward time-surface (max-timestamp per pixel) and a backward time-surface (min-timestamp per pixel) from inter-frame events, stacks each with its gradient map, and feeds the resulting motion maps—concatenated with global trajectory tokens from historical bounding boxes—into a Mamba state-space module that regresses inter-frame displacements; a bidirectional motion-consistency loss enforces that forward and backward displacement predictions are physically consistent, providing an explicit constraint for nonlinear motion. The Target-aware Appearance Matching (TAM) branch uses a Gaussian-distributed mask (σ = ¼ of target box dimensions, centered on the target) to simulate occlusion during training, guiding a ViT-B backbone (initialized with DINOv2 weights) to reconstruct occluded template features via cross-attention and shared self-attention, thereby learning invariant target representations. The Gated Adaptive Fusion module dynamically weights motion and appearance features according to occlusion severity, and the full network is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy, GIoU, L1, and two MSE losses (appearance reconstruction and displacement prediction) over 200 epochs on 8 GPUs.

## Results

EvoTrack achieves state-of-the-art performance on three of four benchmarks: FE108 (94.6% PR, 68.4% SR), COESOT (85.4% PR, 76.2% SR), and the newly introduced FEOT (62.7% PR, 45.2% SR). On VisEvent, it attains the best PR (80.1%) but a slightly lower SR (62.1%) than SeqTrackv2 (63.0%), which the authors attribute to missing raw event files in some training videos. The largest margin over the previous best tracker appears on FEOT, where EvoTrack leads SDSTrack by 4.7% PR, confirming the benefit of event-derived motion cues under severe occlusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoTrack outperforms all 11 baselines on FE108, COESOT, and FEOT in both PR and SR. On FE108, it leads the closest competitor ViPT by 0.8% PR and 2.6% SR, and on COESOT it edges out SeqTrackv2 by 0.4% PR and 0.3% SR. The most decisive win is on the occlusion-focused FEOT dataset, where EvoTrack surpasses SDSTrack (the previous best) by 4.7% PR (62.7 vs 58.0) and 0.1% SR, and beats SeqTrackv2 by 6.6% PR. On VisEvent, EvoTrack achieves the highest PR (80.1%) but trails SeqTrackv2 by 0.9% in SR (62.1 vs 63.0), a gap the authors attribute to incomplete event data during training. Frame-only methods (MixFormer, ORTrack, SeqTrack, ARTrack) are consistently outperformed by 10–40+ points in PR, highlighting the advantage of incorporating event streams for motion-aware tracking under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoTrack extends the autoregressive tracking paradigm (SeqTrack, ARTrack) by replacing frame-rate-limited trajectory prediction with event-derived inter-frame motion, specifically targeting the failure mode where nonlinear motion during occlusion causes drift. It departs from existing event-based trackers (ViPT, SDSTrack, HDETrack) that exploit events for high-speed/high-dynamic scenes but largely ignore occlusion, reframing events as a tool for temporal motion compensation under appearance deprivation. The appearance branch builds on masked-reconstruction strategies (ORTrack, MTOA) but replaces indiscriminate random masking with a target-centered Gaussian distribution to reduce background interference. The Mamba-based sequence modeling for trajectory tokens and the DINOv2-pretrained ViT for appearance features represent a natural integration of recent backbone advances into the tracking pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FEOT dataset with 11 graded occlusion levels and pixel-aligned frame-event pairs at 1070×610 resolution fills a genuine gap: prior frame-event benchmarks (FE108, VisEvent, COESOT) lack fine-grained occlusion annotations and operate at much lower resolution, making it difficult to isolate occlusion-specific performance. The core insight—that microsecond-level event timestamps encode direction and speed information invisible to 30 Hz frames, enabling motion autoregression to bridge occlusion gaps—provides a principled complement to appearance matching rather than a replacement. However, practical adoption is constrained by the need for synchronized event-camera hardware and a coaxial beam-splitter setup, and the paper does not explore whether the motion-autoregression component could be approximated from conventional video (e.g., optical flow) to broaden applicability. Open questions include whether the bidirectional time-surface consistency generalizes to multi-object or long-term (>1000 frame) occlusion scenarios.
