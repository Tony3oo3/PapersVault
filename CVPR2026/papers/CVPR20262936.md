## Problem

Existing AI-generated image detectors learn a single static decision boundary that memorizes generator-specific artifacts, causing severe performance degradation when evaluated on unseen generative models. This paper identifies the fundamental limitation of the discriminative paradigm and asks whether a generative, probabilistic approach—modeling the full distribution of forgery cues rather than a fixed template—can achieve robust cross-generator generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262936/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 224×224 RGB image as input, processed by a CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder (with LoRA on layers 12–23) enhanced by Patch-Wise Contrastive Learning. At inference, the model performs N=4 Monte Carlo samples from the prompt flow and fuses them with B=2 base templates, yielding B×N=8 prompt pairs whose cosine similarities with the visual feature are averaged to produce a final real/fake probability. During training, only a single prompt pair is sampled per image for efficiency, and the classification loss is standard cross-entropy.

## Method

PPM-CLIP replaces the static discriminative classifier with a Probabilistic Prompt Modeling (PPM) module built on a normalizing-flow architecture. The Prompt Flow Module generates three hierarchical distributions—Image-Generic (syntactic priors), Image-Specific (conditioned on the visual feature), and Image-Class (steering toward 'real' or 'fake')—via K=10 invertible tanh-based transformations trained with an ELBO objective. These compact adjustment vectors are broadcast-added to a learnable Prompt Repository of B template pairs, expanding them into B×N image-adaptive prompts that are encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. A complementary Patch-Wise Contrastive Learning (PWCL) strategy refines the visual encoder by computing DCT-based frequency scores per patch and applying a margin-based contrastive loss that pulls high-frequency (forgery-relevant) patches together and pushes low-frequency patches apart. The overall training objective combines cross-entropy classification, the ELBO (KL + reconstruction), PWCL contrastive loss, and an orthogonal loss on prompt embeddings to encourage diversity.

## Results

PPM-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art mean accuracy across all three benchmarks: 98.8% on Ojha (cross-architecture), 99.6% on GenImage (cross-generator), and 95.72% on DRCT (localized manipulation). On GenImage, it reaches 100.0% on SD v1.4, Wukong, and VQDM, and 99.9% on SD v1.5 and BigGAN. On the challenging DRCT inpainting subset (DR Variants), where most baselines collapse to near-chance (~51%), PPM-CLIP maintains 88.92% on SDv1-DR and 88.68% on SDv2-DR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PPM-CLIP outperforms all 21 baselines on every benchmark. On GenImage, it surpasses the strongest prior method LOTA (98.9%) by 0.7% in mAcc, and the next-best CoD (96.2%) by 3.4%. The gap is most dramatic on ADM, where PPM-CLIP scores 99.2% versus LOTA's 99.5% (nearly tied) but FatFormer's 75.9% and FreqNet's 66.8% (a 23–32 point margin). On Ojha, PPM-CLIP (98.8%) beats COD (97.5%) by 1.3% and UnivFD (86.9%) by 11.9%, with the largest individual gap on LDM-200 (99.3% vs. 75.7% for UnivFD). On DRCT's inpainting subset, where UnivFD, DIRE, and GramNet all collapse to ~50–52%, PPM-CLIP retains 88.9% on SDv1-DR, a gap of over 37 points. Pixel-level methods (CNNDet, FreDect, F3Net) consistently score near or below chance on cross-domain generators, confirming the paper's argument that static discriminative boundaries fail to generalize.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP vision-language model (Radford et al., 2021) as its backbone and repurposes normalizing flows (Rezende & Mohamed, 2015) as a conditional generator for text prompts, rather than using them for density estimation. It departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm in CLIP-based detection (UnivFD, FatFormer, C2P-CLIP, SAFE) by replacing a single static prompt pair or linear classifier with a probabilistic ensemble of image-adaptive prompts, and incorporates AIDE's (Chen et al., 2025) DCT-based frequency scoring into a patch-wise contrastive objective to sharpen the visual encoder's sensitivity to high-frequency forgery artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from a single decision boundary to a distribution of hypotheses offers a principled explanation for why static methods fail on unseen generators, and the strong cross-domain results (98.8% Ojha, 99.6% GenImage, 95.7% DRCT) validate the generative framing. The ablation revealing that PWCL contributes more to accuracy than the prompt flow itself (80.0% vs. 92.1% when removed) suggests that frequency-domain visual grounding remains the critical bottleneck, leaving open whether the generative prompt component adds value beyond a well-tuned static prompt on larger or more diverse generator pools. Practical limitations include reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder, modest flow depth (K=10), and evaluation limited to image generators tested at the time of writing.
