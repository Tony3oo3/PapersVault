## Problem

State-of-the-art generative video editing methods process the full spatiotemporal context of a video regardless of the edit mask size, making them prohibitively expensive for real-time applications, high-resolution editing, or simultaneous multi-region edits. This dense, full-attention paradigm ties mask information to the entire video context, preventing flexible operations like independent per-region prompts or propagating edits into future frames where content is not yet available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261750/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a binary edit mask (dilated to neighboring pixels for blending), and a text prompt, and produces an edited video where only the masked region is regenerated. The background video is spatially downsampled to a fixed 256×256 resolution for global context encoding regardless of the original video resolution, making the method resolution-invariant. Training uses 49-frame clips at 480×720, and the framework supports batch-processing multiple non-contiguous masks with distinct text prompts in a single inference pass, as well as autoregressive propagation into future frames for real-time AR editing.

## Method

EditCtrl disentangles video editing into two lightweight adapter modules on a frozen video DiT backbone: a local context encoder (c_φ) that processes only tokens within the dilated edit mask, and a temporal global context embedder (G_ψ) that encodes the downsampled background video into compact global tokens and modulates cross-attended features via an additional attention operation. The local encoder is initialized from VACE's full-attention control module and fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128) under a mask-aware diffusion loss that restricts supervision to masked tokens, while the global embedder is randomly initialized with a zero-initialized output projection. Training uses a piecewise schedule: the local encoder is trained alone first, then the global embedder is introduced jointly, avoiding instability from simultaneous optimization. This design yields computation proportional to the mask area rather than video resolution, achieving 10× lower PFLOPS than full-attention baselines while matching or exceeding their editing quality, and remains natively compatible with distilled and autoregressive model variants for downstream applications like multi-prompt editing and real-time content propagation.

## Results

On VPBench-Edit, EditCtrl (1.5B) achieves PSNR 24.16, SSIM 0.92, and CLIP(M) 21.70 while running at 4.67 FPS with only 17.42 PFLOPS, compared to VACE (1.3B) at 0.66 FPS and 76.31 PFLOPS — a ~7× throughput gain at ~4.4× lower compute. The 16B variant further improves to PSNR 24.37, SSIM 0.93, LPIPS 5.10, and MAE 2.65, matching or exceeding the 14B full-attention VACE baseline on all preservation and alignment metrics. On VPBench-Inp inpainting, EditCtrl (1.3B) reaches PSNR 23.17 and CLIP 9.81 at 5.24 FPS, outperforming VACE (1.3B) at 0.66 FPS while using a fraction of the compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VACE (the full-attention baseline with the same architecture family), EditCtrl (1.5B) wins on PSNR (24.16 vs 23.84), SSIM (0.92 vs 0.91), and CLIP(M) (21.70 vs 21.51) while delivering 7× higher throughput (4.67 vs 0.66 FPS) and 4.4× lower compute (17.42 vs 76.31 PFLOPS). Against VideoPainter (5B), EditCtrl improves PSNR by 1.53 dB, LPIPS by 2.11, and CLIP by 0.91, at ~39× higher FPS (4.67 vs 0.12) and ~47× lower PFLOPS. Against ReVideo, EditCtrl dominates on every metric — PSNR jumps from 15.52 to 24.16 and LPIPS drops from 27.68 to 5.54 — reflecting ReVideo's reliance on a single-frame inpainting backbone. On the DAVIS inpainting benchmark, EditCtrl (1.5B) matches VACE (14B) on MAE (2.01 vs 2.01) and LPIPS (5.31 vs 4.88) while running at 5.57 FPS versus 0.10 FPS, a 56× speedup. The 16B EditCtrl variant achieves the best overall quality (PSNR 24.37, SSIM 0.93, LPIPS 5.10, MAE 2.65) at 124.53 PFLOPS, still 4.7× cheaper than the 14B VACE baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

EditCtrl extends LazyDiffusion's principle of restricting diffusion computation to masked tokens from the image domain to video, while departing from full fine-tuning in favor of lightweight LoRA adapters on a frozen DiT backbone. The local encoder is initialized from VACE's full-attention control module and retrained with a mask-aware loss, while a novel temporal global context embedder (analogous to CLIP conditioning but operating on VAE-encoded downsampled video) injects video-wide scene cues via cross-attention modulation. This disentangled local-global design is positioned as a ControlNet-like architecture that preserves the base model's generative quality while enabling proportional compute scaling with edit region size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× compute reduction (17.42 vs 193.39 PFLOPS) with simultaneous quality improvement over full-attention baselines makes real-time generative video editing (4.67 FPS) practical for applications like augmented reality and interactive multi-region editing, which were previously infeasible. The frozen-backbone, adapter-only design ensures compatibility with distilled and autoregressive model variants, opening a path toward streaming video editing pipelines. Key limitations include VAE-induced background degradation, struggles with very fast motion, and 4K tiling overhead for VAE encode/decode; future work could integrate explicit motion representations and more efficient video tokenization.
