## Problem

State-of-the-art 2D-to-3D conversion methods achieve geometric accuracy but produce narratively impoverished results that lack the immersive, emotionally resonant quality of professional 3D cinema. The prevailing geometric reconstruction paradigm systematically treats deliberate artistic decisions—global depth budget expansion, strategic zero-plane placement, and local depth sculpting—as noise to be suppressed, creating a fundamental gap between physical correctness and cinematic artistry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260219/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D left-view image (512×512) as input, which is first processed by a frozen Depth Anything V2 to produce an inverse depth map (the 'geometric canvas') alongside the original image features. The trainable CameraNet outputs per-pixel virtual camera parameters (vs, vt) and a virtual right disparity map (d̂^R) at 512×512; the virtual left disparity map is then computed as d̂^L = vs·iz + vt. The final virtual right view is synthesized by warping the left image with d̂^R and filling occlusions via standard inpainting or professional software.

## Method

Art3D introduces a dual-path synthesis architecture where a lightweight U-Net-like CameraNet (512×512 input, 64×64 bottleneck, 3-channel output) learns to decompose the artistic disparity signal into global style parameters (scaling s for depth budget, shift t for zero-plane) and local per-pixel 'artistic brushstrokes' (vs, vt maps). Training uses indirect supervision from professional 3D films: SEA-RAFT extracts the target disparity blueprint from stereo pairs, while Lang-SAM with text prompts generates local effect masks; the global mask is derived as valid pixels minus local regions. The core L_Art loss applies masked least-squares fitting separately on global and local paths, plus a regularization term (L_st) that pushes s→1 and t→0 so the network directly encodes global style without post-hoc parameter estimation. A DDC-IoU ≥ 0.8 filter removes low-quality frames with over-simple depth layering from the 90K training pairs, and an auxiliary loss enforces smoothness and left-right stereo consistency.

## Results

In a user study with 25 participants evaluating 50 cinematic clips, Art3D achieves 80.0% overall preference versus 20.0% for the Depth-Anything-V2 (DA-V2) geometric baseline, with particularly large gains in Style Consistency (77.2% vs. 22.8%) and Immersion (64.8% vs. 35.2%). On the proposed global artistic style metrics, Art3D reduces the standard deviation of the learned depth scale (s) from 0.018 (baseline) to 0.009, closely matching the ground-truth stability (0.010), and reduces the zero-plane shift (t) standard deviation from 2.35 to 1.80. The synthesized right disparity maps maintain high geometric consistency, achieving DDC-IoU scores of 0.83–0.89 against the right-view geometric canvas.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Depth-Anything-V2 (DA-V2), the standard geometric depth estimation baseline, Art3D wins decisively on all four user-study metrics: Overall Preference (80.0% vs. 20.0%), Style Consistency (77.2% vs. 22.8%), Immersion (64.8% vs. 35.2%), and Visual Comfort (58.5% vs. 41.5%), confirming that geometric accuracy alone is insufficient for cinematic 3D perception. In the qualitative local-effects comparison (Fig. 3), Art3D outperforms the professional Owl3D software by producing stronger and more consistent pop-out effects across diverse 2D inputs, whereas Owl3D's 3D perception is inconsistent across scenes. In the internal ablation (Tables 3–4), removing the artistic loss L_Art causes the baseline to produce highly unstable global style parameters (σ_s = 0.018, σ_t = 2.35) that are far from ground truth, while the full Art3D model achieves σ_s = 0.009 and σ_t = 1.80, closely tracking the stable ground-truth distributions (σ_s = 0.010, σ_t = 3.34). The paper notes that direct numerical comparison to SOTA diffusion-based methods (StereoCrafter, Eye2Eye) is not meaningful because their objective (physical fidelity) is fundamentally different from Art3D's (artistic coherence).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant geometric reconstruction paradigm (MonoDepth, MiDaS, Deep3D, StereoCrafter) by reframing 2D-to-3D conversion as an artistic style synthesis problem rather than a physical depth estimation problem. It repurposes existing components—Depth Anything V2 for geometric input, SEA-RAFT for target disparity extraction, and Lang-SAM for local mask generation—within a novel dual-path supervision architecture that explicitly separates global cinematic style (depth budget scaling, zero-plane selection) from local artistic sculpting (pop-out effects). Conceptually, it bridges the gap between early heuristic disparity remapping (Lang et al., Didyk et al.), which required stereo pairs and manual editing, and modern geometry-driven pipelines, by learning cinematic stereoscopic priors directly from professional 3D film data in a data-driven manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces a compelling conceptual reframing—that the 'noise' in professional 3D disparity maps is deliberate artistic intent, not data ambiguity—which could open a new research direction for artistically-driven stereoscopic conversion. However, the execution is explicitly preliminary: the trainable component is a simple U-Net, evaluation relies on a small user study (25 participants) and self-defined metrics rather than direct SOTA comparison, and temporal consistency for video is not addressed. Key open questions include whether the dual-path decomposition generalizes beyond the tested genres, how to rigorously quantify 'artistic quality' beyond the proposed style-distribution statistics, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences with coherent temporal style.
