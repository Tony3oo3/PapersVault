## Problem

Existing video amodal completion methods either fine-tune video diffusion models on small, synthetic occlusion datasets—limiting generalization to real-world and severe occlusions—or apply pre-trained image inpainting models frame-by-frame, which loses inter-frame temporal consistency and produces flickering artifacts. The paper asks how to preserve the open-world generalization of pre-trained image inpainting models while explicitly enforcing spatial-temporal coherence across video sequences without any task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video frame sequence {X₁,…,X_T} and corresponding visible object segmentation masks {M₁,…,M_T} as input; inpainting masks are autonomously generated per frame using SAM, RAM, and InstaOrderNet. The output is the complete amodal appearance sequence {A₁,…,A_T} where each frame covers both visible and occluded regions of the target object. Internally, the method constructs 2×2 grid inputs by concatenating an exemplar pair (original frame, completed object) with a target pair (original frame, masked visible region), which are fed to the Flux.1-Fill inpainting backbone.

## Method

IC-Amodal is a training-free framework built on Flux.1-Fill that reformulates VAC as a rectified in-context learning problem with two core modules. First, Calibrated Exemplar Rectification solves the cold-start problem by selecting two frames with the largest visible mask coverage (spatially separated in time) and jointly inpainting them in a 2×2 grid, leveraging cross-frame complementary information to produce a reliable exemplar that avoids the artificial artifacts of single-frame inpainting. Second, Rectified Exemplar-Guided Completion modulates attention weights within the DiT's multimodal blocks during inference: it amplifies attention from the exemplar completion A_ex to the target inpainting region (scaled by λ↑=3.0) while suppressing attention from irrelevant context regions X_ex and X_k (scaled by λ↓=0.3), explicitly steering the task-agnostic global attention toward the amodal object structure rather than scene background. This attention reweighting is applied only to three specific sub-region blocks (A_{1,4}, A_{2,4}, A_{3,4}) while leaving all other attention interactions unchanged, preserving the backbone's normal generative behavior.

## Results

On Kubric-Dynamic, IC-Amodal (training-free) achieves 24.71 PSNR, 0.910 SSIM, 85.5 IoU, and 188.81 FVD, outperforming the finetuned TACO by 1.65 dB PSNR and 20.47 FVD, and Diffusion-VAS by 3.64 dB PSNR and 41.21 FVD. With blending (IC-Amodal+), the PSNR gap over TACO widens to 7.04 dB and FVD reduction to 34.83. On Kubric-Static, IC-Amodal leads in PSNR (24.04), SSIM (0.898), and IoU (86.7), with FVD (184.10) only marginally behind TACO (162.91). The method also surpasses all image-based baselines (OW-Amodal, Pix2Gestalt, PCNet) on FVD by large margins (e.g., 1254.27 for OW-Amodal vs. 184.10 for IC-Amodal on Kubric-Static).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IC-Amodal outperforms the two strongest video-finetuned baselines (TACO and Diffusion-VAS) on PSNR, SSIM, and IoU across both Kubric datasets, and on FVD for Kubric-Dynamic (188.81 vs. 209.28 for TACO, 230.02 for Diffusion-VAS). On Kubric-Static, TACO achieves a slightly lower FVD (162.91 vs. 184.10), but IC-Amodal+ with blending closes this gap (163.84). Against image-based amodal methods (OW-Amodal, Pix2Gestalt, PCNet), IC-Amodal dominates on FVD by a wide margin (e.g., 184.10 vs. 1254.27 for OW-Amodal on Kubric-Static), confirming that IC learning effectively restores temporal consistency that single-frame inpainting lacks. Video inpainting/editing baselines (E2FGVI, Pix2Video) underperform even image-only methods on all metrics, attributed to their lack of amodal completion awareness. Overall, IC-Amodal is the only method that simultaneously achieves top image alignment (PSNR/SSIM/IoU) and competitive FVD without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of fine-tuning video diffusion models on curated amodal datasets (TACO, Diffusion-VAS) by repurposing a pre-trained image inpainting model (Flux.1-Fill) and introducing in-context learning as the mechanism for temporal consistency. It builds on OW-Amodal's pipeline for autonomous inpainting mask generation (SAM + RAM + InstaOrderNet) and on the visual ICL literature (IP-Prompt, Diptych Prompting, Analogist) that demonstrates DiT's inherent ability to learn transformations from demonstration pairs. The key conceptual departure is reformulating VAC as a *rectified* in-context learning problem: the authors identify that vanilla DiT attention cannot distinguish task-critical exemplar cues from irrelevant scene context in a 2×2 grid, and address this with explicit attention weight modulation (amplifying exemplar-to-target, suppressing source-to-target) rather than relying on the model to implicitly learn the correct focus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free formulation sidesteps the data bottleneck of VAC (real amodal pairs are scarce and synthetic occlusions limit generalization), making the approach immediately applicable to in-the-wild occlusions without retraining. However, the method remains sensitive to upstream mask quality and fails under full occlusion, and the primary quantitative evaluation is on synthetic Kubric scenes; real-world validation is deferred to supplementary material. Open questions include whether a memory mechanism over adjacent frames could handle complete occlusion, and whether the attention modulation strategy generalizes beyond the specific Flux.1-Fill backbone to other DiT architectures or to longer video sequences where the single-exemplar paradigm may become insufficient.
