## Problem

LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts adapters for continual learning suffer from two critical failure modes: structural interference, where expert weight updates occupy overlapping subspaces and degrade each other under top-k co-activation, and compositional forgetting, where the shared router drifts over time and misroutes inputs from previously learned tasks. Existing MoE-adapter methods address these issues only through heuristic strategies like partial expert freezing or task-specific routers, which are incompatible with task-agnostic inference or overly rigid in protecting plasticity.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images as input and produces class labels over a growing combined label space (all previously seen classes plus unseen classes), with no task identifier available at inference. It operates on a frozen CLIP dual-encoder backbone, where trainable Spectral MoE modules are inserted into selected transformer blocks. Evaluation spans 11 diverse image classification benchmarks (Aircraft, Caltech-101, CIFAR-100, DTD, EuroSAT, Flowers, Food-101, MNIST, Oxford Pet, Stanford Cars, SUN397) in both full-shot and 5-shot regimes, plus Tiny-ImageNet for class-incremental learning at 5/10/20-step granularity.

## Method

Spectral MoE combines three mechanisms: (1) Spectral Experts parameterize each expert's weight update as ΔW_m = F_o(S_m ⊙ Θ_m)F_i^H, where S_m are fixed, pairwise-disjoint binary masks over conjugate-symmetric frequency pairs and Θ_m are learnable complex coefficients; under unitary DFT this guarantees Frobenius-orthogonal updates across all experts by construction, eliminating structural interference a priori. (2) A Dual Online-Offline Router (O² Router) decouples a shared online instance router (top-k sparse softmax gating) for task-agnostic inference from an offline task-specific router that archives per-task average expert usage vectors after each task completes. (3) Dynamic Consistency Projection (DCP) uses the archived statistics to project router gradients onto the null space of historical inputs (suppressing drift) and applies an importance-weighted two-branch expert update: the gradient is split into a stability component (projected into the null space of past gating compositions, scaled by per-expert importance η_m derived from offline usage) and a plasticity component (retained in the residual space), enabling expert-specific protection that shields critical experts while keeping others adaptable.

## Results

On the 11-task full-shot MTIL benchmark, Spectral MoE achieves an Average score of 78.1%, a Last (retention) score of 86.3%, and a Transfer (zero-shot generalization) score of 70.1%, all of which are state-of-the-art. The strongest competitor, MoE-Adapters++, trails by 0.6% on Average (77.5%), 0.1% on Last (86.2%), and 1.1% on Transfer (69.0%). In the few-shot MTIL setting, the method maintains its lead with 72.1% Average vs. 71.7% for MoE-Adapters++. On the TinyImageNet CIL benchmark (100 base classes), Spectral MoE achieves the best Average of 81.95% at 5 steps, outperforming MoE-Adapters (81.12%) and ZSCL (80.27%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spectral MoE outperforms all seven baselines on all three metrics (Transfer, Average, Last) in the full-shot MTIL setting. Against the closest competitor MoE-Adapters++ [53], the gains are modest but consistent: +1.1% Transfer (70.1 vs. 69.0), +0.6% Average (78.1 vs. 77.5), and +0.1% Last (86.3 vs. 86.2). The full-parameter method ZSCL [57] trails by 2.0% on Average (75.4 vs. 78.1) and 2.7% on Last (83.6 vs. 86.3), while also requiring ~6.4× more trainable parameters. MoE-Adapters [52] is competitive on Last (85.0 vs. 86.3) but lags by 1.4% on Average and 1.2% on Transfer. Replay-based iCaRL [37] achieves a surprisingly high Last score (80.1) but is far behind on Transfer (50.4 vs. 70.1) and Average (65.7 vs. 78.1). Regularization-based methods (LwF [29], LwF-VR [6], WiSE-FT [45]) are all 10–17 points below Spectral MoE on Average, confirming that parameter-level structural solutions substantially outperform loss-based anti-forgetting strategies in this cross-domain task-agnostic setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA-based Mixture-of-Experts framework for continual learning (MoE-Adapters) by addressing two specific failure modes it identifies: structural interference from overlapping expert subspaces and compositional forgetting from router drift. It departs from BiLoRA's task-level frequency masking by assigning expert-level pairwise-disjoint spectral masks, ensuring Frobenius-orthogonal updates even under top-k co-activation. The Dual Online-Offline Router and Dynamic Consistency Projection are novel mechanisms that go beyond prior heuristic strategies (partial expert freezing, task-specific routers) by providing a principled geometric constraint on gradients informed by archived historical statistics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The a priori orthogonality guarantee via spectral masks is training-invariant and eliminates structural interference at the parameter level rather than reacting to it post-hoc, which is a cleaner design principle for scalable CL. The importance-weighted DCP offers a fine-grained stability-plasticity trade-off that avoids the coarse freezing of prior methods, and the 2.5× parameter reduction over MoE-Adapters makes the approach more practical. Open questions include whether the fixed-bandwidth spectral mask allocation remains effective for very long task sequences (where the null space of historical inputs becomes highly constrained), whether the approach transfers to LLM-based MoE architectures, and how sensitive performance is to the number of experts relative to the number of tasks seen.
