## Problem

Directly applying weight-compensation PTQ methods (GPTQ, GPTAQ) to Vision-Language Models exposes two gaps: the standard round-to-nearest quantization is suboptimal under GPTAQ's asymmetric objective because it ignores residual-induced shifts in the optimal quantization target, and all input channels are treated uniformly despite vision and language tokens having fundamentally different statistics and information densities, leading to imbalanced quantization parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260475/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen VLM (vision encoder + cross-modal adapter + LLM backbone) and calibrates only the LLM backbone using 128 randomly sampled text-image pairs from the ShareGPT4V-improved COCO Caption dataset. It produces per-channel or per-group (g128) quantized weights at 2–4 bits with per-token asymmetric 8-bit activations, preserving the original architecture. The quantized VLM then accepts an RGB image and a text prompt and generates a text response, evaluated across eight vision-language benchmarks.

## Method

VLM-PTQ extends GPTAQ's asymmetric weight-compensation framework with two targeted modifications. First, it derives a closed-form correction term δ = rXᵀH_{:,q}⁻¹ that shifts the quantization target from the raw weight w_q to w_q + δ, so the optimal discrete choice becomes RTN(w_q + δ) rather than RTN(w_q); this correction is computed efficiently via a precomputed diagonal vector C with O(n²) overhead. Second, it introduces modality-aware quantization by computing separate Hessian matrices for vision and language tokens, fusing their diagonal elements with a layer-wise awareness coefficient μ (selected via a lightweight grid search over ~6 candidates that minimizes a small-sample output reconstruction error), and using the resulting importance vector to weight the per-channel scale and zero-point search. Both components are integrated into a single quantization step where each weight column is quantized as RTN(W_{:,q}·(1+C_q); S*, Z*), and the full algorithm requires only ~99 seconds and 0.2 GB additional overhead per layer relative to GPTAQ.

## Results

Under 3-bit weight-only quantization, VLM-PTQ achieves 71.3% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (vs. 65.0% for GPTAQ and 63.8% for GPTQ), retaining 92.3% of the FP16 score (77.2%). On the largest model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, VLM-PTQ reaches 76.9% average accuracy at 3-bit, preserving 98.3% of FP16 performance (78.2%). Under extreme 2-bit compression, the advantage widens: Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves from 43.1% (GPTAQ) to 48.4%, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B from 61.4% to 67.8%. Gains are most pronounced on text-intensive benchmarks (DocVQA, OCRBench) and visual reasoning (MME-RealWorld).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLM-PTQ is compared against GPTQ (symmetric Hessian-based weight compensation) and GPTAQ (asymmetric calibration with residual propagation), the two dominant PTQ baselines for large models. VLM-PTQ wins on every model, every bit width, and every individual benchmark in both Table 1 (weight-only) and Table 2 (W2A8KV8). At 3-bit on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, VLM-PTQ gains +6.3 points over GPTAQ (71.3 vs 65.0) and +7.5 over GPTQ (63.8); at 2-bit the gap widens to +5.3 over GPTAQ (48.4 vs 43.1). GPTAQ consistently outperforms GPTQ by 1–3 points, confirming the value of asymmetric calibration, but VLM-PTQ's additional correction term and modality-aware importance vector close most of the remaining gap to FP16. The largest absolute gains appear on text-heavy tasks (DocVQA: +4.7 at 3-bit on 7B; +17.2 at 2-bit on 72B) and on MME-RealWorld Chinese (from 5.3 to 20.3 at 2-bit on 7B), suggesting the modality-aware component is particularly effective where vision-language alignment is most fragile under low-bit compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the weight-compensation PTQ lineage (OBQ → GPTQ → GPTAQ) by identifying two specific failures when transplanting GPTAQ's asymmetric calibration to Vision-Language Models. The first contribution is a theoretical correction: the authors show that GPTAQ's use of round-to-nearest is suboptimal under its own asymmetric objective and derive a closed-form shift to the quantization target. The second is a modality-aware importance vector that decouples vision and language Hessian contributions, departing from the uniform channel treatment inherited from all prior Hessian-based PTQ methods. The work is positioned as a minimal-overhead refinement of GPTAQ rather than a new quantization paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is clear: the method enables single-GPU quantization of VLMs up to 72B parameters with meaningful accuracy retention (98.3% of FP16 at 3-bit on Qwen2.5-VL-72B), making edge deployment of large VLMs more feasible. The modality-aware quantization strategy is a generalizable idea that could extend to other multi-modal architectures (video, audio) where input channels carry heterogeneous information densities. Limitations include quantizing only the LLM backbone (not the vision encoder or adapter), reliance on a calibration set, and the fact that the correction term's contribution is modest compared to the modality-aware component. An open question is whether the two-modality Hessian decomposition generalizes cleanly to three or more modalities without combinatorial explosion in the awareness coefficients.
