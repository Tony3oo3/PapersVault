## Problem

In 3D modeling workflows, repeated parts that share the same material (e.g., pinecone scales, building windows) must be selected and assigned materials individually, making the process tedious and scaling poorly with part count. No existing method or benchmark addresses grouping pre-segmented parts by material consistency under geometric variation, as prior work focuses on initial segmentation, whole-shape retrieval, exact symmetry, or texture-dependent material segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261054/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh already decomposed into fine-grained parts (typically via connected components) and a user-selected query part. Each part is consumed as three 512×512 RGB renderings — an isolated-part view, a part-with-context view (part highlighted among neighbors), and a full-object view — from viewpoints chosen to maximize visible surface area. The output is a 1152-d material-aware embedding per part and, at inference, a threshold-controllable group of retrieved parts ranked by negative L1 distance, enabling hierarchical selection from strict to loose grouping.

## Method

The core encoder initializes a DINO-v3 small backbone and fine-tunes the last three transformer blocks, processing the three rendered views independently and concatenating their 384-d features into a 1152-d representation. A two-layer MLP projection head maps this to a 128-d contrastive embedding trained with a Supervised Contrastive Loss that pulls same-material parts together and pushes different-material parts apart. Training uses ~1.9M parts from 22,000 Objaverse meshes with per-part material IDs, applying data rebalancing to mitigate severe material distribution skew. At inference, the higher-dimensional pre-projection embedding is used with negative L1 distance as the similarity metric, and a histogram-matching deduplication step groups rigidly transformed duplicates to reduce computation. A tunable threshold λ on the similarity score provides a tolerance parameter analogous to Photoshop's Magic Wand, enabling smooth hierarchical selection from near-duplicates to broader structural families.

## Results

On the 100-shape / 241-query benchmark, Material Magic Wand achieves 89.74% AUC-PR, 88.33% R-Precision, 91.70% mAP, and 75.94% F1 (grouping), outperforming the strongest baseline (DINO-v3 small) by +8.6 points in AUC and +16.6 points in F1. Recall@20 reaches 62.79% and Recall@100 reaches 83.67%, indicating strong retrieval quality even for large part groups. All metrics are macro-averaged across queries to prevent large groups from dominating scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four baselines spanning three categories. The geometric baseline (Histogram Matching / Thea [5]) is the weakest, achieving only 26.85% AUC and 23.84% F1, confirming that purely shape-statistic descriptors are brittle and effective only for near-duplicate parts. Among vision foundation models, SigLIP-v2 [43] reaches 62.83% AUC and 39.44% F1, while DINO-v3 small [41] is the strongest single-model baseline at 81.14% AUC and 59.36% F1; both retrieve visually similar parts but miss structurally related components (e.g., caster wheels, wire meshes). PartField [27], a hierarchical 3D part segmentation model, achieves 75.30% AUC and 56.57% F1, underperforming DINO-v3 because its training objective (hierarchical decomposition) does not align with material-consistent grouping. Material Magic Wand outperforms all four baselines on every metric, with the largest gap over DINO-v3 small (+8.6 AUC, +16.6 F1), demonstrating that supervised contrastive training on material labels and multi-view contextual rendering are critical beyond what off-the-shelf vision embeddings provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task—material-aware grouping of pre-segmented 3D parts—that sits at the intersection of but is distinct from part segmentation, shape retrieval, symmetry detection, and material segmentation. Methodologically, it repurposes a vision foundation model (DINO-v3) as a part encoder, fine-tuning it with a supervised contrastive objective over multi-view renderings (isolated part, part-with-context, full-object), rather than using the backbone for its original general-purpose feature extraction role. The multi-view rendering strategy and the use of a higher-dimensional pre-projection embedding for retrieval are task-specific design choices that depart from standard contrastive learning pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear for 3D artists who must assign materials to hundreds of repeated parts, but the scope is constrained: the method targets untextured meshes with pre-existing part segmentation, and the evaluation benchmark is small (100 shapes, 241 queries), limiting confidence in generalizability. Open questions include how the approach handles meshes with significant self-occlusion, whether the deterministic ranking can be extended to model multiple valid groupings, and whether the noisy Objaverse material annotations cap the achievable performance. The finding that data rebalancing is critical (≈11 AUC points) suggests that material distribution in real-world assets may require further curation for robust deployment.
