## Problem

VGGT's global attention layers incur quadratic computational costs that limit scalability, and existing sparsification methods like SparseVGGT apply a uniform sparsity pattern across all attention heads. This uniform treatment ignores the heterogeneous sensitivity of individual heads to attention masking, causing severe accuracy degradation under high sparsity because critical heads are over-pruned while robust heads are under-utilized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (patchified and encoded via a pre-trained DINOv2 encoder) along with a small calibration set (20 views per scene from CO3Dv2 dev) that provides ground-truth camera poses and point clouds for computing HeSS. During inference, the fixed HeSS scores produce head-wise sparse attention masks over the block-sparse attention mechanism, and the model outputs camera poses, dense point clouds, depth maps, and tracking features. The calibration set is used only once; no ground truth is needed at inference time.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage pipeline. In the calibration stage, it computes a Head Sensitivity Score (HeSS) for each attention head in the global attention layers by approximating the Hessian of two error terms—camera pose error (MSE of aligned predicted vs. GT camera positions) and point cloud error (MSE over inlier correspondences)—with respect to the query projection matrix W_Q^h, using the Fisher Information Matrix estimated from first-order gradients on the calibration set. The FIM traces are normalized across heads within each layer and combined via a weighted sum (weight λ). In the inference stage, HeSS-Guided Sparsification reallocates the total block-sparse attention budget proportionally to each head's HeSS score, then applies an iterative Water-Filling-style capping procedure to redistribute surplus from heads that exceed their structural maximum, yielding a head-differentiated sparse attention mask that preserves sensitive heads while aggressively pruning robust ones.

## Results

On CO3Dv2, HeSS-Guided Sparsification consistently outperforms SparseVGGT in camera pose estimation (AUC@30↑, ATE↓) across all sparsity levels, with the gap widening at high sparsity (~75%). On DTU, the method achieves lower Chamfer Distance than SparseVGGT at every sparsity level, indicating more stable 3D reconstruction. At 0.43 sparsity, runtime is 8.37 s vs. 8.42 s for SparseVGGT (A40 GPU), confirming negligible overhead. In the ablation (Table 1), the full HeSS configuration achieves Chamfer 1.603, Accuracy 2.839, and Completeness 0.367 on DTU, outperforming all alternative projection-matrix and combination strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SparseVGGT [21], the primary baseline, HeSS-Guided Sparsification wins on both CO3Dv2 (AUC@30, ATE) and DTU (Chamfer Distance) at every tested sparsity level, with the advantage growing substantially at ~75% sparsity where SparseVGGT suffers severe degradation. Against ViT head-pruning baselines (Kwon et al. [9] and Michel et al. [11]), the 3D-aware HeSS method significantly outperforms across all sparsity levels; Michel et al. [11] in particular collapses beyond 50% sparsity due to its coarse granularity, while HeSS maintains high fidelity even at 75% sparsity. The method also generalizes to the π³ variant, where using only HeSS_pc (λ=0) preserves performance while SparseVGGT degrades sharply. Runtime is essentially identical to SparseVGGT (8.37 s vs. 8.42 s at 0.43 sparsity), so the performance gains come at no computational cost. A sanity check with inverted HeSS ranking (pruning the most sensitive heads first) causes catastrophic failure, confirming that the sensitivity signal is correctly identifying critical heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SparseVGGT, which introduced block-sparse attention to reduce the quadratic cost of VGGT's global attention layers, but departs from it by replacing the uniform sparsity pattern with a head-wise, sensitivity-guided allocation. The sensitivity metric (HeSS) draws on Fisher Information Matrix / Hessian approximation ideas from the pruning literature (LeCun et al., Michel et al.), repurposing them to quantify per-head vulnerability to attention masking in a 3D-reconstruction context. The budget reallocation mechanism borrows the Water-Filling algorithm from information theory, and the paper positions itself against general ViT sparsification baselines (head-level pruning, fast post-training pruning) to argue that 3D-aware, geometry-informed sparsification is necessary.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VGGT is becoming a foundational model for multi-view 3D reconstruction, and its quadratic attention cost is a practical bottleneck for large-scale or real-time deployment; this paper offers a principled, training-free way to cut compute by ~75% while preserving accuracy, which is immediately useful for practitioners. The empirical confirmation that GA-layer heads in VGGT (and π³) exhibit strongly heterogeneous sparsification sensitivity provides interpretability insight into how multi-view transformers allocate geometric reasoning across heads. Key open questions remain: HeSS is not comparable across layers, the method is inference-time only with no training-time adaptation, and it is validated on a limited set of architectures (VGGT, π³) and datasets (CO3Dv2, DTU), leaving unclear whether the approach generalizes to other 3D foundation models or to training-time co-design.
