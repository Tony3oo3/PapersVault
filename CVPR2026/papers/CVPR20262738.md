## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) promise interpretable predictions by routing classification through human-understandable concepts, yet they suffer from four critical flaws: no pre-training metric to assess whether a concept set is actually relevant to the dataset, a structural 'linearity problem' where purely linear concept encoders cause the model to degenerate into a linear probe that ignores the bottleneck entirely, a persistent accuracy gap relative to opaque classifiers, and a lack of systematic comparison across vision backbones and vision-language models. These issues collectively undermine the trustworthiness of CBM-based interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a predefined set of K text concepts (e.g., GPT-3-generated attributes or human-annotated labels) as input. It produces a class label prediction and a vector of K concept activations, where each activation is computed as the cosine similarity between the VLM's image embedding and the corresponding concept's text embedding, normalized by dataset-level mean and standard deviation. The architecture is modular: the vision backbone (ResNet, DINOv2, Perception Encoder, or Franca) and the VLM (CLIP, SigLIP, SAIL, or FLAIR) can be independently swapped, and the concept set can be replaced without retraining the full pipeline.

## Method

CBM-Suite introduces three technical components. First, an entropy-based 'Goodness of Concepts' metric computes softmax-normalized concept activations per image (task-agnostic) or per class (task-specific) and reports average entropy, enabling pre-training selection of relevant concept sets by identifying those with concentrated, sparse activation distributions. Second, a non-linear concept encoder—a two-layer MLP with ReLU activation trained via MSE loss to match VLM-derived target activations—replaces the linear projection, preventing the model from collapsing into a single linear transformation and forcing predictions to genuinely depend on the concept space. Third, a knowledge distillation loss aligns the CBM's classifier logits with those of a linear-probe teacher on the same backbone embeddings, combined with cross-entropy and elastic-net regularization on the classifier weights, to recover predictive accuracy while preserving a fully interpretable bottleneck. The framework is evaluated systematically across four vision backbones and four VLMs on ImageNet-100, Places365, CUB-200, and CIFAR-100.

## Results

CBM-Suite achieves 86.73% top-1 accuracy on CUB-200, 92.50% on CIFAR-100, 54.64% on Places365, and 81.56% on ImageNet, using the best-performing vision backbone–VLM pair per dataset (Perception Encoder for most, ResNet-18 for CUB-200). On ImageNet-100 with ResNet-50, the distilled CBM reaches 85.47% accuracy versus 82.27% for the vanilla CBM and 91.30% for the linear-probe oracle, narrowing the interpretability–accuracy gap by roughly 3.2 points. The entropy-based Goodness of Concepts metric consistently assigns the lowest entropy to relevant concept sets (e.g., 3.660 on ImageNet-100) versus 4.504–4.566 for irrelevant or random sets, confirming its discriminative power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LFCBM, CBM-Suite wins decisively on all four reported datasets: CUB-200 (86.73% vs. 74.31%, +12.4 pts), CIFAR-100 (92.50% vs. 65.13%, +27.4 pts), Places365 (54.64% vs. 43.68%, +11.0 pts), and ImageNet (81.56% vs. 71.95%, +9.6 pts). Against LaBo, CBM-Suite achieves higher accuracy on CUB-200 (86.73% vs. 81.00%, +5.7 pts) and CIFAR-100 (92.50% vs. 86.82%, +5.7 pts), but trails on ImageNet (81.56% vs. 83.97%, −2.4 pts); LaBo does not report a Places365 result. The ImageNet gap is notable because LaBo uses a different (unspecified) backbone configuration, whereas CBM-Suite's ImageNet number uses the Perception Encoder backbone with SigLIP. Overall, CBM-Suite establishes new state-of-the-art accuracy on three of four benchmarks while remaining competitive on the fourth, demonstrating that the non-linear concept encoder plus distillation loss can close much of the accuracy gap that previously held CBMs below opaque baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CBM lineage from Koh et al. (ICML 2020) through the VLM-based variants (Post-hoc CBM, LFCBM, LaBo) that compute concept activations via cosine similarity between image and text embeddings. It departs from recent VLM-based CBMs by identifying a structural flaw they share—purely linear concept encoders that collapse the bottleneck into a single linear probe—and reintroduces non-linearity (a ReLU between two linear layers), a design choice present in the original CBM but dropped in subsequent VLM-based work. The knowledge distillation loss and the entropy-based concept-set metric are new additions not present in any prior CBM formulation, and the systematic multi-encoder/multi-VLM comparison extends the evaluation scope well beyond the CLIP-only focus of prior studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linearity problem finding is significant because it undermines the interpretability claims of several influential CBM papers: if a model achieves high accuracy with random strings as concepts, the concept bottleneck is not doing any meaningful work. The entropy-based pre-training metric offers a practical tool for concept-set selection that the field has lacked, and the encoder/VLM comparison provides the first empirical guidance on backbone choice. Open questions remain around whether distilled CBMs still suffer from concept leakage (encoding label information outside the defined concept set), whether the non-linearity + distillation recipe generalizes to bottom-up CBMs or non-visual domains, and whether the residual accuracy gap to opaque models is acceptable in safety-critical deployments.
