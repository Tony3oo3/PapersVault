## Problem

While methods like REPA improve diffusion transformer (DiT) generation by aligning internal representations with external pretrained encoders, the underlying mechanisms governing how DiTs learn meaningful representations remain largely opaque. Specifically, it is unclear why external alignment is effective, whether aligning more blocks or using more encoders helps, and what internal properties drive effective representation learning. This paper addresses these gaps through a systematic empirical analysis of representation dynamics across DiT blocks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-computed Stable Diffusion VAE latent features of ImageNet images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution, conditioned on class labels, and generates images via a DiT/SiT backbone with patch size 2. It supports both multi-step sampling (250-step Euler-Maruyama) and one-step generation (via MeanFlow), and is evaluated with and without classifier-free guidance. The diversity loss is computed over a subset of block pairs to keep computational cost manageable, and an adaptive weight mechanism modulates the loss magnitude during training to prevent divergence.

## Method

DiverseDiT introduces two complementary mechanisms to promote representation diversity across DiT blocks without external guidance. First, long residual connections inject the output of the i-th block into the (L−i)-th block via concatenation, layer normalization, and a linear projection, breaking the homogeneous input chain that causes representational collapse. Second, a representation diversity loss combines three terms: an orthogonality loss penalizing cosine similarity between block-wise mean features, a mutual-information proxy loss minimizing average cosine similarity of ℓ₂-normalized token representations across block pairs, and a feature dispersion loss maximizing the variance of per-dimension activations to encourage diverse channel usage. An adaptive weight schedule (linearly scaling between 0 and 1 based on the loss value in [0.1, 0.5]) prevents training divergence when the diversity loss becomes too small. The key empirical insight, derived from CKA-based analysis, is that representation diversity across blocks naturally increases during training and is the critical factor underlying the effectiveness of external alignment methods like REPA.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with CFG, DiverseDiT achieves FID 1.52 at 200 epochs, outperforming SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06 at 1400 epochs) and REPA (FID 1.96 at 200 epochs) with far fewer training steps. On ImageNet 512×512, it reaches FID 1.99 at 200 epochs, surpassing REPA (FID 2.08 at 200 epochs). For one-step generation, applying DiverseDiT to MeanFlow-XL/2 yields a new SoTA FID of 2.99, improving over the base MeanFlow (3.43) and IMM-XL/2 (7.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiverseDiT at 200 epochs (FID 1.52) outperforms SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06 at 1400 epochs), DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27 at 1400 epochs), REPA (FID 1.96 at 200 epochs and 1.80 at 800 epochs), SRA (FID 1.58 at 800 epochs), and MDTv2-XL/2 (FID 1.58 at 1080 epochs), all with substantially fewer training epochs. The only method achieving a lower FID is REG (FID 1.36 at 800 epochs), which requires 4× the training cost. For one-step generation, DiverseDiT applied to MeanFlow-XL/2 achieves FID 2.99, beating the base MeanFlow (3.43), MF+DispLoss (3.21), IMM-XL/2 (7.77), Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60), and iCT-XL/2 (34.24). On ImageNet 512×512, DiverseDiT at 200 epochs (FID 1.99) edges out REPA at the same budget (FID 2.08). Notably, DiverseDiT at 80 epochs (FID 1.89) already surpasses REPA at 200 epochs (FID 1.96), demonstrating significant convergence acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two threads in diffusion transformer research: (1) representation alignment methods like REPA, REG, and E2E-REPA that inject knowledge from external pretrained encoders (DINOv2, MAE, MoCo), and (2) internal regularization approaches like SRA and DispLoss that improve representations without external guidance. DiverseDiT departs from both by identifying representation diversity across blocks as the unifying mechanism behind their effectiveness, then achieving it self-containedly through long residual connections (diversifying inputs) and a three-component diversity loss (orthogonality, mutual-information proxy, and feature dispersion). The empirical analysis using CKA similarity heatmaps provides the mechanistic bridge that prior work lacked, explaining why REPA works and why scaling up external guidance (more blocks, more encoders) yields diminishing or negative returns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, external-guidance-free recipe that consistently accelerates convergence and improves FID across SiT and REPA backbones at multiple scales, and extends to one-step generation (MeanFlow) where it achieves a new SOTA of FID 2.99. The negative result—that aligning more blocks with more encoders can hurt performance—challenges the prevailing assumption that more external supervision is always beneficial and suggests a principled ceiling on representation alignment. Limitations include evaluation only on class-conditional ImageNet (no text-to-image or video), and the multi-step FID at 200 epochs (1.52) still trails REG (1.36 at 800 epochs), so the efficiency-quality tradeoff is not yet fully closed. Open questions include whether the diversity principle generalizes to conditional generation with text prompts, to video DiTs, and whether the adaptive weight schedule for the diversity loss transfers across architectures without re-tuning.
