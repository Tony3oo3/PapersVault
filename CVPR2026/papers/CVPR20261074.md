## Problem

Vision-based robotic policies are brittle to even minor camera viewpoint changes, and existing approaches to view invariance enforce it at the scene level—asking a single feature vector to summarize the entire image including static layout and background. This is unnecessarily demanding for control, conflating task-irrelevant appearance with the dynamics that actually drive actions, and fails to explicitly separate static context from motion-relevant structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261074/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 64×64 RGB images from multiple camera viewpoints along with ground-truth action sequences (k steps, dimension D) used as soft alignment supervision during pre-training. At inference, the latent policy π_z takes only the current observation (no future frames) and outputs a 128-dimensional latent action code, which conditions a downstream Diffusion Policy to produce low-level robot actions. The IDM is frozen after pre-training, so the latent action space is fixed and the policy inherits its view-invariant structure.

## Method

VILA operates in two stages. In the latent action learning stage, it builds on the LAOM framework (IDM + FDM with temporal consistency loss) and adds two action-guided regularization terms: a weighted InfoNCE loss where soft weights are derived from normalized L2 distances between ground-truth action sequences, and a structural alignment loss that minimizes the Frobenius distance between cosine-similarity matrices of L2-normalized latent actions and flattened GT action vectors. This enforces view invariance directly in the dynamics-centered latent space rather than on scene-level features. In the latent behavior cloning stage, a latent policy π_z is trained via behavior cloning to predict latent actions from the current observation alone, using the frozen IDM as a teacher; this policy then serves as a view-invariant vision encoder that conditions a downstream Diffusion Policy for low-level control.

## Results

In the fine-tuned setting across five RoboSuite tasks, VILA achieves the best unseen-view success rate on every task, with particularly large margins on harder tasks: 53.65% on Stack Three (vs. 23.70% Vanilla, 10.35% CLASS), 12.65% on Coffee (vs. 0.35% Vanilla/CLASS), and 27.85% on Mug Cleanup (vs. 9.70% Vanilla). In the frozen-encoder setting, VILA is the only method that maintains non-trivial performance on the three hardest tasks (Square 10.35%, Stack Three 6.65%, Mug Cleanup 15.00%), while all baselines collapse to near-zero. On the real-world SO-ARM pick-and-place task, VILA achieves 63.33% average success across three novel views, substantially outperforming Vanilla (3.33%), CLASS (13.33%), and even VLA baselines π0.5 (36.67%) and SmolVLA (33.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VILA consistently outperforms all baselines on unseen-view generalization. Against the strongest scene-level invariance baseline CLASS, VILA gains +29.7 pp on Stack Three (53.65% vs. 10.35%), +12.3 pp on Coffee (12.65% vs. 0.35%), and +21.5 pp on Mug Cleanup (27.85% vs. 6.35%) in the fine-tuned setting; in the frozen setting the gap is even more dramatic, with VILA at 6.65% on Stack Three versus 0.00% for CLASS. ReViWo and KYC (camera-conditioning) essentially fail on all tasks beyond Lift, scoring 0.00% on Square, Stack Three, Coffee, and Mug Cleanup in the fine-tuned setting. The simple Vanilla ResNet-18 baseline is the closest competitor on the easier Lift task (77.00% vs. 94.70% unseen) but degrades sharply on harder tasks. On the real-world SO-ARM task, VILA (63.33% avg) surpasses not only the encoder baselines (Vanilla 3.33%, CLASS 13.33%) but also the VLA models π0.5 (36.67%) and SmolVLA (33.33%), suggesting that explicit dynamics-level invariance provides a complementary robustness signal beyond what large VLA pretraining offers.

## Relation to Prior Work

VILA builds directly on the LAOM latent action framework [32], which learns compact dynamics codes via inverse/forward dynamics models, but departs from it by adding an action-guided contrastive objective that explicitly enforces cross-view invariance in the latent action space. This contrasts with prior view-robustness methods (CLASS, ReViWo, KYC) that impose invariance on scene-level visual encoders, which the paper argues conflates task-relevant motion with static appearance. The paper also positions itself against latent action pretraining from unlabeled video [40], noting that in multi-view robot learning, ground-truth action logs are available and can serve as a practical supervision signal for cross-view alignment rather than relying on purely predictive objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that viewpoint invariance should be enforced on dynamics (latent actions) rather than on full-scene representations—offers a principled alternative to the dominant scene-level invariance paradigm, and the empirical results (especially in the frozen-encoder setting where baselines collapse to near-zero on harder tasks) provide strong evidence for this shift. However, the approach still requires multi-view data or NVS-based augmentation, and the study is limited to camera pose changes, leaving open whether the same dynamics-level invariance principle extends to lighting, background, or object-appearance shifts. The finding that scene-level invariance (CLASS, ReViWo) can actually underperform vanilla training in cross-task transfer is a useful cautionary result suggesting that invariance objectives must be matched to the right representational level.
