## Problem

Current VLMs exhibit a significant gap in spatial understanding and reasoning because they acquire spatial knowledge implicitly from unstructured 2D image-text data, treating multi-view inputs as flat 2D sequences without explicit visual geometry learning. This paper argues that genuine spatial intelligence requires a learned process to 'lift' 2D perceptions into coherent 3D representations, which existing VLMs fundamentally lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260930/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of N multi-view RGB images (trained at 224×224 and 518×518 resolutions) plus a text prompt. The geometric expert outputs pixel-aligned 3D point maps in camera coordinates and SE(3) camera poses via lightweight transformer decoder heads, while the semantic expert produces text answers for spatial reasoning questions. The architecture uses a permutation-equivariant design without camera tokens, and the semantic expert supports native dynamic resolution via Qwen2-VL's vision encoder with MRoPE.

## Method

G²VLM adopts a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) architecture with two experts that interact via shared self-attention: a geometric perception expert (DINOv2 encoder + LLM layers) for 3D reconstruction and a semantic perception expert (Qwen2-VL-2B) for multimodal understanding. Training is two-stage: first, the geometric expert is trained from scratch on large-scale 3D-annotated data using a combined point-reconstruction, camera-pose, and surface-normal loss while the semantic expert remains frozen; second, the semantic expert is unfrozen and jointly trained with cross-entropy loss on spatial understanding data, learning to exploit geometric features via in-context and interleaved reasoning. Key design choices include global attention (over alternating frame/global masks), a dual-encoder setup (DINOv2 for geometry, CLIP-based for semantics), and removal of camera tokens in favor of permutation-equivariant processing, making the LLM treat both encoder streams uniformly.

## Results

On spatial reasoning, G²VLM-SR (2B) achieves 54.87 average on SPAR-Bench, surpassing GPT-4o (36.39) by 18.48 points and the largest open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-72B (39.40) by 15.47 points. On MindCube, G²VLM-SR scores 48.33 average (vs. 37.25 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B), and on OmniSpatial it reaches 50.41 (vs. 47.13). On visual geometry, G²VLM achieves 0.297 Abs Rel on Sintel depth estimation (vs. 0.335 for VGGT and 0.277 for π³) and 97.91 RRA@30 on Co3Dv2 camera pose estimation, demonstrating competitive 3D reconstruction alongside its spatial reasoning strengths.

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²VLM-SR (2B) decisively outperforms GPT-4o on SPAR-Bench (54.87 vs. 36.39, +18.48) and on MindCube (48.33 vs. 38.81, +9.52), while GPT-4o retains an advantage on OST-Bench (53.05 vs. 37.02). Among open-source models, G²VLM-SR surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-72B (the largest baseline) on SPAR-Bench (+15.47), MindCube (+11.08), and OmniSpatial (+3.28), but trails it on OST-Bench (37.02 vs. 50.54), which the authors attribute to the need for larger model capacity in online spatio-temporal understanding. Against spatial-expert models, G²VLM-SR wins on all four benchmarks: it beats VLM3R-7B (the strongest spatial expert) by 11.66 on SPAR-Bench, 6.24 on MindCube, and 2.33 on OmniSpatial, and outperforms Spatial-MLLM-7B and SpaceQwen2.5-VL-3B by wide margins across all tasks. On visual geometry (Table 1a), G²VLM is competitive with SOTA feed-forward methods: it reduces Sintel depth Abs Rel from VGGT's 0.335 to 0.297 but remains behind π³ (0.277), and it matches VGGT on 7-Scenes completion (0.309 vs. 0.305) while trailing on accuracy (0.414 vs. 0.28). The base Qwen2-VL-2B model improves substantially after G²VLM training (e.g., SPAR-Bench 24.60→41.66 for G²VLM, 24.60→54.87 for G²VLM-SR), confirming that the geometric expert's learned features drive the gains rather than mere fine-tuning on spatial data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—feed-forward visual geometry (VGGT, π³, DUSt3R) and spatial reasoning VLMs (SpatialVLM, SpaceQwen, VLM-3R)—into a single Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts architecture inspired by the two-streams hypothesis in neuroscience. Unlike prior spatial VLMs that either treat images as flat 2D sequences or bolt on a frozen geometry encoder (VLM-3R, Spatial-MLLM), G²VLM natively trains a geometric perception expert from scratch within the VLM and shares self-attention with a semantic expert initialized from Qwen2-VL. The MoT design borrows structural inspiration from Bagel's unified multimodal pretraining, but the training objectives, expert specializations, and joint-training strategies are substantially different, targeting visual geometry prediction rather than image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 2B model with a learned geometric expert can surpass GPT-4o by 18.5 points on SPAR-Bench and outperform 72B open-source models on most spatial benchmarks, the paper provides strong evidence that explicit 3D geometry learning is a critical missing component in VLMs rather than a nice-to-have. The confirmed positive interplay between geometric expert quality and spatial reasoning performance (Table 2) offers a clear scaling pathway: improving the geometry expert should directly improve reasoning. Key open questions include whether this approach scales beyond 2B without the noted training instability, whether the geometric expert can be leveraged for downstream tasks like 3D scene editing, and whether the benefits persist when the semantic backbone is swapped for a much larger VLM.
