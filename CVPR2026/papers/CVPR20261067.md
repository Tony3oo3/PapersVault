## Problem

Estimating 3D keypoints from a single image without 3D annotations or calibrated multi-view captures remains largely unexplored due to severe depth ambiguity and occlusion. Existing unsupervised keypoint discovery methods still require multi-view data with known camera parameters during training, limiting scalability to diverse or in-the-wild object categories. This paper tackles the gap by showing that geometric priors in pretrained multi-view diffusion models can substitute for both annotations and controlled multi-view captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261067/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input, with the foreground object first segmented via Grounded SAM before being fed to the diffusion backbone. It outputs N 3D keypoints (default N=18) in a canonical coordinate space aligned with the diffusion model's learned world frame, along with a dense N×N learnable adjacency matrix encoding keypoint connectivity. When paired with the manipulation pipeline, the same predictions yield skinning weights and a Minimum Spanning Tree skeleton that enable articulation of a 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction without manual rigging.

## Method

KeyDiff3D exploits a pretrained SV3D-p multi-view diffusion model in two roles: as a supervision generator (synthesizing K=4 geometrically consistent views from the input image) and as a 2D multi-view feature extractor (caching intermediate U-Net decoder features at timestep τ=500). A lightweight aggregation network fuses multi-layer diffusion features with learned scalar weights, and the resulting multi-view feature maps are unprojected into an M×M×M volumetric grid via bilinear sampling at each voxel's projected subpixel location, with cross-view aggregation through softmax attention. A 3D convolutional head produces per-keypoint heatmaps, and integral regression yields differentiable 3D coordinates. Training is fully self-supervised: predicted 3D keypoints are projected into the diffusion-generated views, rendered as differentiable Gaussian edge lines gated by the learnable adjacency matrix, and used as structural cues for a reconstruction network supervised by VGG perceptual and mask losses against the generated reference images.

## Results

On Human3.6M, KeyDiff3D (single view, 18 keypoints, 2-layer MLP regression) achieves MPJPE 121.34 mm, N-MPJPE 118.29 mm, and P-MPJPE 85.26 mm, outperforming all single-view unsupervised baselines. On a simplified six-action subset, the method improves to P-MPJPE 66.73 mm, approaching multi-view methods that require calibrated camera setups. On CUB-200-2011 (2D projection evaluation), the method achieves L2 distances of 5.16 (CUB-aligned) and 7.7 (CUB-all), competitive with dedicated 2D keypoint discovery baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KeyDiff3D outperforms all single-view unsupervised baselines on Human3.6M: it beats Keypoint-Net [54] (P-MPJPE 112.9) by 27.6 mm, Honari et al. single-view [15] (89.05) by 3.8 mm, Kundu et al. [29] (89.4) by 4.1 mm, and Sosa et al. [50] (96.4) by 11.1 mm in P-MPJPE. It also surpasses the multi-view BKinD-3D [52] baseline (P-MPJPE 105 with 4 views, 117 with 2 views) despite using only a single input image. The method trails the strongest multi-view baselines—Honari et al. [15] with 4 calibrated views (P-MPJPE 63.0) and Yang et al. [66] with 4 views (79.3)—which have access to actual multi-view geometric constraints unavailable at inference. On CUB-200-2011, the projected 2D keypoints (L2 5.16 aligned, 7.7 all) are competitive with 2D discovery methods like GANSeg [12] (3.23/12.1) and Autolink [11] (4.15/11.6), though StableKeypoints [14] (5.06/5.4) remains slightly stronger on CUB-all. The ablation confirms that the multi-view diffusion backbone (SV3D) substantially outperforms ResNet50, CLIP, and DINOv2 as 2D feature extractors for this task (P-MPJPE 85.26 vs. 101.55–103.13).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unsupervised 3D keypoint discovery line (KeypointNet, BKinD-3D, Honari et al.) by eliminating the dependency on calibrated multi-view capture: a pretrained multi-view diffusion model (SV3D-p) synthesizes geometrically consistent novel views from a single image, serving as both supervision signal and feature extractor. The feature aggregation strategy is directly inspired by Diffusion Hyperfeatures, and the overall paradigm generalizes StableKeypoints' use of 2D diffusion for 2D keypoints to the 3D multi-view setting. The key conceptual departure is treating the diffusion model's intermediate representations as an explicit 3D geometric prior (unprojected into volumetric features) rather than relying on noisy rendered images or human-specific kinematic templates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing both 3D annotations and calibrated multi-view capture from the training loop, the method opens a path to scalable 3D keypoint discovery for arbitrary object categories (birds, dogs, animated characters) that were previously infeasible. The animatable 3D object pipeline (Gaussian Frosting + MST skeleton + Gaussian skinning) demonstrates a practical downstream application that requires no manual rigging. However, quantitative validation is limited to Human3.6M (with a simplified subset for best results), animal evaluations are purely qualitative, and the method inherits the coordinate-system alignment of the diffusion backbone, which may constrain deployment in arbitrary world frames. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex articulated objects, whether the diffusion timestep and view count can be further reduced for efficiency, and whether the volumetric representation can be replaced by more efficient implicit formulations.
