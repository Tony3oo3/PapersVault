## Problem

Existing 3R-based reconstruction systems (e.g., Spann3R, SLAM3R) produce dense geometry in real time but ignore scene semantics, while open-vocabulary 3D semantic methods rely on offline pipelines or active depth sensors. No prior framework unifies real-time dense 3D reconstruction with open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation from RGB-only video, leaving a significant gap for Spatial AI applications that require both geometric and semantic understanding simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB-only video frames in overlapping windows (L=5 for initialization, L=11 for incremental processing) and free-form text prompts for semantic queries, requiring no depth sensor or explicit camera pose input. It produces dense per-pixel 3D point maps in a globally consistent coordinate frame, per-point open-vocabulary semantic labels obtained by matching fused 2D-3D descriptors against CLIP text embeddings, and camera poses derived post-hoc via PnP-RANSAC from the predicted point maps. The two modules (CLIP3R and 2D-3D OVS) are loosely coupled through CLIP features, allowing either task to run independently.

## Method

Ov3R comprises two feed-forward modules. CLIP3R extends the SLAM3R two-branch architecture (I2P + L2W) by injecting object-level CLIP features—extracted by masking images with SAM segments and averaging CLIP patch embeddings—into the I2P encoder via cross-attention, and by adding a DPT head in L2W that predicts CLIP3R features in scene space, supervised by an L1 feature-matching loss alongside the standard confidence-aware pointmap loss. The 2D-3D OVS module then lifts 2D semantics into 3D by fusing three complementary feature sources (CLIP3R, DINO, and a distilled 3D-CLIP point encoder) at scene-level, instance-level, and mask-level, combining them through cross-attention blocks and a learned weighted Hadamard merge, pre-trained with a sigmoid cosine similarity loss against CLIP text embeddings. The key design insight is that embedding CLIP semantics directly into the reconstruction backbone (rather than treating semantics as a post-hoc labeling step) enforces semantic-geometric consistency across the entire scene, yielding both more accurate geometry and more reliable open-vocabulary segmentation.

## Results

On Replica, Ov3R (CLIP3R) achieves the best average accuracy (3.05 cm) and completion (2.12 cm) among all real-time methods, outperforming SLAM3R (3.57/2.62) and Spann3R (10.32/13.33) while running at 15 FPS. For open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on Replica (GT geometry), the 2D-3D OVS module reaches 31.9 mIoU / 42.3 mAcc overall, surpassing OVO-mapping (26.5/35.8) and Open3DIS (25.6/38.7). On ScanNetv2 with 200 classes, Ov3R achieves 19.4 mIoU / 28.5 mAcc, again leading all baselines. Camera tracking (ATE RMSE) is 6.00 on Replica, competitive with SLAM3R (6.61) but behind slower SLAM systems like DROID-SLAM (0.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D reconstruction on Replica, Ov3R achieves the best accuracy (3.05 cm) and completion (2.12 cm) among all real-time methods, beating SLAM3R (3.57/2.62) and Spann3R (10.32/13.33) by a wide margin; however, its ATE RMSE (6.00) is slightly worse than SLAM3R (6.61) and far behind offline SLAM systems like DROID-SLAM (0.33) or DIM-SLAM (0.46). On 7Scenes, Ov3R similarly leads all baselines in both accuracy (1.98 cm) and completion (2.08 cm). For open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on Replica with GT geometry, Ov3R outperforms OVO-mapping by +5.4 mIoU (31.9 vs 26.5) and +6.5 mAcc (42.3 vs 35.8), and beats Open3DIS by +6.3 mIoU, though Open3DIS retains an edge on 'Head' labels (49.7 vs 44.3 mIoU). On ScanNetv2-200, Ov3R leads OVO-mapping by +2.2 mIoU and +3.2 mAcc, and surpasses OpenScene by +10.0 mIoU. In the online (CLIP3R geometry) setting, Ov3R consistently outperforms OVO-CLIP3R by ~4-5 mIoU across both datasets, confirming the benefit of the 2D-3D fused descriptors over 2D-only CLIP features.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ov3R extends the SLAM3R two-branch (I2P/L2W) incremental reconstruction architecture and the DUSt3R ViT-based pointmap prediction paradigm by injecting object-level CLIP features directly into the reconstruction pipeline, rather than treating semantics as a post-hoc annotation step. For the semantic segmentation component, it revises the OVO-SLAM 3D-segment matching and multi-level feature fusion strategy, replacing vanilla CLIP descriptors with CLIP3R-predicted features and adding DINO and 3D-CLIP point features to create a fused 2D-3D descriptor. The key conceptual departure from prior 3R-based systems (Spann3R, SLAM3R) is that semantic understanding is not bolted on after geometry is recovered but is learned jointly with it, enforcing cross-view semantic consistency during reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ov3R addresses a concrete gap in Spatial AI: no prior real-time, RGB-only framework simultaneously delivers dense 3D reconstruction and open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation without depth sensors or explicit pose estimation. The modular design (CLIP3R and 2D-3D OVS can run independently) makes it practically useful for agents that need geometry but not semantics, or vice versa. However, the inherited limitation of 3R models—suboptimal camera pose accuracy—remains unresolved, and the SAM2 bottleneck (2.5 FPS) prevents the full pipeline from meeting strict real-time constraints. Future work integrating global bundle adjustment or faster segmentation backends could close these gaps and make the framework deployable on embedded platforms.
