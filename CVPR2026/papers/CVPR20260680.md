## Problem

Volume electron microscopy (VEM) inherently produces anisotropic volumes with poor axial resolution, but existing isotropic reconstruction methods either rely on 2D architectures that ignore 3D spatial continuity (causing inter-slice artifacts) or employ 3D Transformers with prohibitive computational costs. Additionally, the common practice of simulating anisotropy via simple downsampling fails to capture real-world degradations, creating a domain gap that degrades performance on actual anisotropic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260680/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel anisotropic subvolume X ∈ R^{F×h×W} (e.g., 32×128×128 for ×4 upscaling, 16×128×128 for ×8, 16×160×160 for ×10) and produces an isotropic volume Y ∈ R^{F×H×W} where H = s×h and s is the axial upscaling factor. The reconstruction is trained in a self-supervised manner: high-resolution lateral sections serve as the supervisory signal, while the model is validated on the degraded axial dimension, eliminating the need for paired isotropic ground truth.

## Method

VEMamba's core is the VEMamba Module (VEMM), which implements a 3D dependency reordering paradigm: the Axial-Lateral Chunking Selective Scan Module (ALCSSM) splits the 3D feature map into two channel chunks and traverses each along eight multi-directional 3D scanning paths (axial-to-lateral, lateral-to-axial, and their reverses), converting them into 1D sequences processed by Mamba's selective SSM for linear-complexity global context modeling. The Dynamic Weights Aggregation Module (DWAM) then adaptively fuses the eight sequence outputs via MLP-generated context-dependent weights, enabling input-aware emphasis of the most informative scanning directions. A MoCo-based contrastive encoder learns a degradation representation from paired subvolumes (InfoNCE loss), which is injected into the network via the Volume Degradation Injection Module (VDIM) as channel-wise affine transformations, allowing the model to condition its reconstruction on the specific degradation profile. The full pipeline stacks Residual Volume Mamba Blocks (each combining VMM, ConvFFN, and VDIM) and is trained with a hybrid L1+SSIM loss, achieving state-of-the-art reconstruction with only 0.94M parameters and 0.28 TFLOPs.

## Results

On the EPFL dataset, VEMamba achieves PSNR of 29.422 dB (×4), 27.418 dB (×8), and 26.473 dB (×10), with SSIM up to 0.7707 and LPIPS as low as 0.2176. On the CREMI dataset, it reaches PSNR of 37.785 dB (×4), 31.556 dB (×8), and 29.796 dB (×10), with SSIM as high as 0.9869. VEMamba uses only 0.28T FLOPs and 0.94M parameters, roughly 2× fewer FLOPs and 1.5× fewer parameters than IsoVEM, and ~80× fewer FLOPs than EMDiffuse. On the downstream mitochondria segmentation task, VEMamba achieves IoU of 0.7464 (×4), 0.6975 (×8), and 0.6927 (×10), within 0.002 of the isotropic ground-truth IoU of 0.7496.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEMamba is compared against IsoVEM (Transformer-based) and EMDiffuse (diffusion-based), both retrained on the same datasets for fair comparison. On PSNR, VEMamba wins 5 out of 6 settings, losing only on CREMI ×4 by a negligible 0.022 dB to IsoVEM (37.785 vs 37.807). On SSIM, VEMamba dominates 4 of 6 settings with large margins on CREMI (e.g., 0.9869 vs 0.9485 at ×4), while trailing IsoVEM by only 0.0023 and 0.0017 on EPFL ×8 and ×10 respectively. EMDiffuse is consistently outperformed by VEMamba on PSNR and SSIM across all settings, though it achieves lower LPIPS on CREMI (0.1362 vs 0.2021 at ×4), which the authors attribute to LPIPS being pre-trained on RGB natural images. On the downstream mitochondria segmentation task, VEMamba achieves the best IoU at all three scale factors (0.7464, 0.6975, 0.6927), surpassing IsoVEM by 0.011–0.017 and EMDiffuse by 0.041–0.077. Critically, VEMamba achieves these results with 0.28T FLOPs and 0.94M parameters—roughly half the cost of IsoVEM and ~80× less than EMDiffuse (22.5T, 115.16M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: VEM isotropic reconstruction (previously dominated by GAN-based, diffusion-based, and Transformer-based 2D methods) and the emerging Mamba/state-space-model architecture for efficient sequence modeling. It explicitly departs from the 2D paradigm by introducing a 3D-native Mamba module (ALCSSM) that reorders volumetric dependencies into 1D sequences, drawing on MobileMamba's channel partitioning and SCST's continuous scanning paths. The MoCo-based degradation learning extends the self-supervised VEM reconstruction line (Deng et al. 2020, Lee et al. 2023) by replacing simple downsampling with a multi-degradation simulation and contrastive representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency–quality trade-off: VEMamba matches or slightly exceeds IsoVEM in reconstruction quality while using roughly half the FLOPs (0.28T vs 0.61T) and fewer parameters (0.94M vs 1.40M), and dramatically less compute than diffusion-based EMDiffuse (22.51T). This makes 3D-consistent isotropic reconstruction feasible on modest hardware, which is important for labs that cannot afford FIB-SEM. Open questions include whether the 3D dependency reordering generalizes to larger volumes or other anisotropic modalities (e.g., light-sheet microscopy), whether the MoCo degradation prior (contributing only ~0.05 dB) is truly necessary or merely a small regularizer, and how the method performs on real anisotropic data beyond the CREMI dataset.
