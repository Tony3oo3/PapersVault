## Problem

Current feature steering methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation apply uniform steering intensity across all layers, ignoring that only a subset of layers are actually responsible for hallucinated outputs. This indiscriminate intervention disrupts the feature distributions of hallucination-irrelevant layers, degrading the model's generalization ability on tasks like MMMU and LLaVA-Bench. The paper asks: can we identify which layers drive hallucinations and steer only those, preserving the model's broader capabilities?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260449/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Please describe the image in detail' for captioning, or yes/no questions for POPE) and produces a standard text response from the base LVLM. At inference time, it modifies only the steering intensity parameter of an existing feature steering method (Nullu or VTI) on a per-layer basis, requiring no additional decoding steps. A small calibration set of 100 token-level and 100 sentence-level hallucination samples is used once to compute fixed layer-wise attribution scores, which are then applied uniformly to all subsequent test inputs without re-computation.

## Method

LTS-FS is a three-stage plug-and-play framework. First, it constructs a bi-granularity hallucination dataset with token-level samples (from POPE/Antidote, where specific tokens are hallucinated) and sentence-level samples (from CHAIR, where entire sentences are unsupported). Second, it computes layer-wise attribution scores via causal intervention: for each layer, it masks individual attention heads and measures the log-probability change in hallucinated token logits, aggregating token-level scores to sentence level using three weighted indicators (cue tokens, position, and factual-error tokens). Third, it applies a layer-wise sparse steering strategy that combines hard sparsification (a threshold r_s excludes layers with attribution scores below a fraction of the mean) and soft weighting (steering intensity is scaled proportionally to each layer's normalized attribution score). This layer-wise intensity vector is decoupled from the specific steering function, making it reusable across different feature steering methods.

## Results

On CHAIR (MSCOCO), LTS-FS(VTI) reduces sentence-level hallucination (C_S) from 53.0 to 35.8 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B and from 40.8 to 32.0 on LLaVA-v1.5-13B, while LTS-FS(Nullu) achieves the best C_S of 23.8 on Qwen-VL2.5-7B (vs. 27.0 for the regular model). On POPE, LTS-FS(VTI) with LLaVA-v1.5-13B raises Random-set accuracy from 81.91% to 86.59% and Adversarial-set F1 from 76.47 to 79.91. On LLaVA-Bench (GPT-4V-aided), LTS-FS(Nullu) improves detailedness from 5.23 to 6.23 on LLaVA-1.5 and from 5.54 to 6.07 on Qwen-VL2.5, confirming that hallucination reduction does not come at the cost of generation quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTS-FS consistently outperforms all four baselines across every model and benchmark. Against the decoding-based methods VCD and AGLA, LTS-FS(VTI) achieves substantially lower CHAIR_S (35.8 vs. 55.2 and 50.8 on LLaVA-7B) and higher POPE accuracy (e.g., 86.59% Random vs. 82.27% for VCD on LLaVA-13B), while also avoiding the extra decoding steps those methods require. Against the feature-steering baselines Nullu and VTI, LTS-FS provides a clear incremental gain: on Qwen-VL2.5-7B, LTS-FS(Nullu) reduces CHAIR_S from 27.4 (Nullu) to 23.8 and CHAIR_I from 7.7 to 6.0, and on LLaVA-v1.5-13B, LTS-FS(VTI) improves POPE Adversarial F1 from 76.80 (VTI) to 79.91. The gains are most pronounced on the harder Adversarial POPE setting and on CHAIR, confirming that layer-selective steering targets hallucination-relevant layers more effectively than uniform steering. Notably, LTS-FS preserves or slightly improves Recall and output length relative to baselines, indicating that the hallucination reduction is not achieved by simply suppressing content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-steering paradigm for LVLM hallucination mitigation (Nullu, VTI) by introducing a layer-wise sparse selection strategy that replaces uniform steering across all layers with attribution-guided, intensity-proportional steering on a subset of layers. The attribution mechanism draws on causal intervention techniques from mechanistic interpretability of LVLMs (Yu et al., Zhou et al.) and the broader parameter-localization literature (GRIFFIN, FLAP, RL-Pruner), repurposing them to identify hallucination-relevant layers rather than pruning or editing targets. The bi-granularity (token-level and sentence-level) hallucination dataset construction builds on existing benchmarks (CHAIR, POPE, Antidote) but introduces a new annotation protocol that distinguishes short-span from whole-sentence hallucinations to support multi-granularity attribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means LTS-FS can be layered on top of any existing feature-steering method without retraining, making it immediately deployable for practitioners already using Nullu or VTI. However, the gains are modest (e.g., ~2% POPE accuracy, ~3 CHAIR_S points) and the method requires a small calibration set (200 samples) plus a fixed threshold, limiting its adaptability to new model architectures without re-attribution. Open questions include whether the identified hallucination-relevant layers are stable across model scales and whether integrating this sparse steering into pre-training (as the authors suggest) could yield more fundamental improvements than inference-time intervention.
