## Problem

Image quality assessment (IQA) and image restoration/enhancement are logically coupled—quality understanding should inform how to restore—yet existing work treats them in isolation, with IQA methods producing descriptions that restoration models never consume and restoration methods operating without any quality-aware context. While unified understanding-generation architectures have shown that stronger understanding improves generation in high-level vision, the analogous IQA-to-restoration relationship in low-level vision remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261652/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text instruction (e.g., 'enhance this mix-degraded image' or 'analyze the image quality and enhance') paired with a low-quality RGB image, center-cropped to 512×512 for super-resolution benchmarks or 1024×1024 for FoundIR multi-degradation tasks. For IQA, the input is an image plus a question template, and the output is a quality score with a natural-language description; for assessment-guided restoration, the output is a structured four-part analysis (user intent, quality diagnosis, enhancement plan, expected result) followed by the restored image. The image pathway operates in FLUX VAE latent space, while the IQA pathway uses SigLIP-2 visual tokens, both interleaved in a single token stream.

## Method

UARE is built on the Bagel mixture-of-transformers backbone with two full-capacity experts—an IQA expert processing text and ViT image tokens, and a restoration expert processing VAE latent tokens—that share self-attention in every block, preserving cross-modal alignment while reducing gradient interference between tasks. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 progressively trains only the restoration expert from single-type to multiple to high-order degradations (9.6B, 19.2B, and 1.3B image tokens respectively), and Stage 2 jointly fine-tunes both experts on interleaved text-image data (0.4B IQA text tokens + 4.6B image tokens) using a combined rectified-flow loss for image generation and an autoregressive loss for text (weighted λ=0.25). The key mechanism for IQA-guided restoration is that quality-analysis tokens generated by the IQA expert remain active in the shared attention stream, allowing the restoration expert to condition its latent updates on the diagnosis, effectively turning textual quality reasoning into a conditioning signal for the flow-matching denoising process.

## Results

On super-resolution (4×, 512×512 center-crop), UARE ranks first in MUSIQ and TOPIQ across all three benchmarks (RealSR, DRealSR, DIV2K) and top-3 in MANIQA and LIQE, while achieving PSNR comparable to the autoregressive PURE baseline (21.38 vs 21.31 on RealSR) with better SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS. On the FoundIR multi-degradation test set (1024×1024), UARE achieves top-3 performance on nearly all PSNR/LPIPS/NIQE/MANIQA metrics across nine mixed-degradation subsets. For image quality assessment, UARE significantly surpasses the prior SOTA Q-Insight on KADID (PLCC 0.878 vs 0.757) and CSIQ (PLCC 0.930 vs 0.768), while remaining competitive on SPAQ, LIVEW, and AGIQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On super-resolution, UARE clearly wins on perceptual/no-reference metrics: it achieves the best MUSIQ (69.67/67.71/70.45), TOPIQ (0.6796/0.6652/0.6864), and MANIQA (0.5260/0.5121/0.5028) across RealSR, DRealSR, and DIV2K, surpassing the strongest diffusion baselines OSEDiff and S3Diff by 0.7–2.5 points in MUSIQ and 0.03–0.05 in TOPIQ. However, UARE trails reference-based fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM) compared to StableSR and SeeSR, which are optimized for pixel-level accuracy. On the FoundIR multi-degradation benchmark, UARE achieves top-3 on nearly all subsets, outperforming FoundIR on B.+N. PSNR (25.16 vs 23.81) and B.+J. LPIPS (0.0891 vs 0.1551), but lags on low-light-heavy subsets like L.+H. (PSNR 20.10 vs 23.86). For IQA, UARE substantially outperforms Q-Insight on KADID (PLCC 0.878 vs 0.757) and CSIQ (0.930 vs 0.768) but is slightly behind on SPAQ (0.902 vs 0.913), LIVEW (0.855 vs 0.867), and AGIQA (0.752 vs 0.805). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that UARE's internal IQA-guided restoration outperforms both a simple prompt and an external Q-Insight prompt on perceptual metrics (MUSIQ 69.67 vs 66.52/64.43), validating that co-trained IQA signals transfer more effectively than externally generated ones.

## Relation to Prior Work

UARE builds directly on Bagel's mixture-of-transformers (MoT) architecture, repurposing its two-expert design (understanding vs. generation) for the low-level vision pairing of IQA and restoration. Conceptually, it draws an analogy between the well-studied understanding→generation synergy in high-level multimodal models and the IQA→restoration relationship, a connection that prior work (e.g., PURE for joint degradation estimation and SR) only touched on superficially. The two-stage progressive training schedule and the interleaved text-image fine-tuning data are methodological departures from both the IQA line (Q-Align, Q-Insight) and the universal restoration line (FoundIR, PromptIR), which have historically been developed in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that IQA signals genuinely improve restoration quality through multi-task co-training (validated by ablations comparing simple prompts, external Q-Insight prompts, and UARE's own analysis-then-restore pipeline), the paper provides empirical grounding for a design principle that could extend to other low-level task pairs. The unified single-model formulation is practically attractive for deployment, but the notably lower PSNR relative to dedicated restoration baselines (e.g., PASD, SeeSR) highlights a fidelity-perceptual-quality trade-off that remains unresolved. Open questions include whether the IQA-restoration synergy generalizes to video, whether the approach scales to more complex or compound degradations beyond the FoundIR/RealESRGAN pipelines, and whether the MoT architecture is strictly necessary versus simpler shared-parameter designs.
