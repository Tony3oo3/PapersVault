## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for pose estimation suffers from unstable optimization under domain shift because heatmap supervision is sparse and sharply localized, and pseudo-label quality is heterogeneous across keypoints. This sparsity and heterogeneity make source-target gradient conflicts highly sensitive to small localization errors, causing oscillatory parameter updates that prior global alignment or uniform gradient-matching strategies do not adequately address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261529/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a K-channel 2D Gaussian heatmap (H×W×K) where each channel corresponds to one keypoint. Conflict detection and gradient filtering operate in the output heatmap space at the detection head before pullback to parameter space, exploiting the per-keypoint channel structure. The backbone is a ResNet101 Simple Baseline trained for 70 epochs with batch size 32, using rotation and 2D translation augmentations on both source and target inputs.

## Method

HamiPose decomposes the target gradient per keypoint into components orthogonal and parallel to the source gradient under a data-driven block-diagonal metric (EMA of applied parameter increments), preserving the non-conflicting orthogonal part and gating the parallel part with a deterministic gate that combines metric-normalized cosine alignment and teacher confidence, annealed via a sharpness parameter to avoid premature hard filtering. The resulting decoupled, confidence-calibrated gradients are then transported by a Hamiltonian optimizer using a one-gradient symplectic Euler kick-drift-kick scheme with an adaptive mass matrix, which supplies controlled momentum that damps high-frequency oscillations from sparse heatmap supervision while maintaining first-order computational cost. The entire procedure is embedded within a Mean Teacher framework where the teacher is updated via EMA after each iteration.

## Results

On human pose UDA, HamiPose achieves 83.9% PCK@0.05 on SURREAL→LSP and 79.8% on SURREAL→Human3.6M, setting new state-of-the-art. On hand pose UDA, it reaches 83.1% on RHD→H3D and 59.9% on RHD→FreiHand. Under domain generalization (no target labels), HamiPose attains 77.5% on Human3.6M and 52.2% on FreiHand, outperforming dedicated DG methods like GGA (76.8% / 51.0%) and SAGM (75.5% / 49.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HamiPose outperforms all 13 baselines across both human and hand pose UDA benchmarks. On RHD→H3D, it leads the strongest pose-oriented baseline DA-LLPose by 0.5% (83.1% vs 82.6%) and the best gradient-matching method PGDA by 1.1% (83.1% vs 82.0%). On the harder RHD→FreiHand transfer, the margin widens: HamiPose beats DA-LLPose by 0.7% (59.9% vs 59.2%) and UniFrame by 1.4% (59.9% vs 58.5%). In the domain generalization setting (Table 2), HamiPose surpasses the strongest DG-specific method GGA by 0.7% on Human3.6M (77.5% vs 76.8%) and by 1.2% on FreiHand (52.2% vs 51.0%), while also exceeding pose-oriented UDA baselines like RegDA (73.2%) and UniFrame (74.3%) on Human3.6M. The gains are most pronounced on fine-grained joints (DIP, Fin, Hip), confirming that the keypointwise conflict filtering specifically addresses the heterogeneous supervision challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

HamiPose sits at the intersection of two established lines: UDA pose estimation (RegDA, UniFrame, SFHPE) which relies on Mean Teacher consistency and pseudo-heatmap supervision, and gradient-matching domain adaptation (CGDM, FGDA, PGDA) which regularizes cross-domain gradient interactions. The key departure is moving from global gradient alignment (cosine similarity, norm matching) to a per-keypoint geometric decomposition under a data-driven block-diagonal metric, where the target gradient is split into source-parallel and source-orthogonal components and only the reliably aligned parallel part is admitted via a confidence-gated filter. The symplectic (kick-drift-kick) integrator then replaces standard SGD/Adam with a geometry-aware momentum step, borrowing the structure-preserving dynamics of Hamiltonian mechanics to damp oscillations induced by sparse heatmap supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete failure mode in UDA pose estimation—sparse, peaky, and per-keypoint-heterogeneous supervision causing oscillatory updates—and provides a principled, ablated recipe (metric-normalized decomposition → confidence gating → symplectic transport) that consistently improves SOTA across both human and hand pose benchmarks. However, the Hamiltonian framing, while conceptually elegant, reduces in practice to an adaptive-momentum scheme with per-channel gradient filtering, so the broader methodological takeaway is the keypointwise decomposition and gating rather than the symplectic integrator per se. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to 3D pose, video-based temporal pose, or other sparse-output tasks, and whether the per-keypoint Jacobian pullback (which requires access to the output-head Jacobian) scales to deeper or transformer-based backbones without prohibitive memory cost.
