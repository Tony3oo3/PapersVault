## Problem

State-of-the-art radiance field methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting require millions of primitives and custom sorting-based renderers, making them impractical for mobile, VR, and other resource-constrained platforms. The paper tackles the extreme simplification problem: reconstructing a high-fidelity 3D scene from multi-view images using only a small, unstructured soup of opaque triangles that can be rendered by a standard depth-tested rasterizer. The core technical obstacle is that opaque rasterization with binary opacity is inherently discontinuous, making direct gradient-based optimization unstable without the smooth approximations that suppress high-frequency detail.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images and SfM keypoints as input, initializing triangles around FPS-sampled and random points near the SfM cloud. The output is a compact triangle soup (typically <20K triangles) where each triangle carries an 8-channel texture (7-dim color feature vector + 1-dim binary opacity) stored as 8-bit PNG images after training, plus a small shared MLP for view-dependent deferred shading. At inference, rendering requires only standard z-buffer rasterization and a single deferred-shading pass—no sorting, no alpha blending—enabling real-time display on consumer laptops and smartphones.

## Method

DiffSoup introduces stochastic opacity masking: during training, each fragment's opacity is compared against an independently sampled uniform threshold, and the z-buffer selects the visible fragment, making the rendered pixel color a random variable whose expectation equals the radiance field loss. Gradients are obtained via the likelihood-ratio (score-function) identity, yielding an unbiased, sort-free estimator for opacity, while vertex-position gradients come from an extension of rasterized edge gradients that also detects intra-triangle visibility boundaries induced by opacity masking. Appearance is encoded through a multi-resolution, atlas-free triangle texture (extending the mesh-color approach to learnable feature vectors) combined with neural deferred shading, where a lightweight shared MLP maps the rasterized 7-dim feature and viewing direction to RGB. Training runs for 10K iterations with coarse-to-fine texture resolution scheduling and adaptive triangle subdivision/pruning to maintain a fixed primitive budget.

## Results

On MipNeRF360 with a strict 15K primitive budget, DiffSoup achieves PSNR 24.76, SSIM 0.748, and LPIPS 0.204, outperforming 3DGS (23.72/0.664/0.420) and Triangle Splatting (22.81/0.634/0.430) on all three metrics, and matching or exceeding TexGS (24.80/0.697/0.270) on SSIM and LPIPS while being within 0.04 dB on PSNR. On synthetic scenes (NeRF-Synthetic and Shelly), DiffSoup with 15K faces achieves PSNR comparable to or slightly above MobileNeRF (which uses 159K–275K faces), while MobileNeRF decimated to 15K faces via QEM degrades severely (e.g., 8.44 PSNR on SHIP vs. 25.29 for DiffSoup from random init). Rendering on an RTX 4090 reaches 1,960 FPS at full resolution—roughly 17× faster than 3DGS (115 FPS) and 117× faster than TexGS (16.8 FPS)—and still runs at 146 FPS on a consumer MacBook Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS [24], DiffSoup wins on all three metrics at the same 15K budget: +1.04 dB PSNR, +0.084 SSIM, and −0.216 LPIPS, with the gap attributed to 3DGS's lack of an explicit primitive-count control mechanism and its reliance on translucent blending. Against Triangle Splatting [17], DiffSoup leads by +1.95 dB PSNR and −0.226 LPIPS, since TS has no texture channel and struggles to encode sub-triangle detail at low poly counts. Against TexGS [5], the closest competitor, DiffSoup trades a marginal 0.04 dB PSNR deficit for substantially better perceptual quality (SSIM 0.748 vs. 0.697, LPIPS 0.204 vs. 0.270), and renders ~117× faster on GPU due to standard depth-tested rasterization versus TexGS's custom translucent pipeline. On synthetic scenes, DiffSoup with 15K faces matches or slightly exceeds MobileNeRF [7] (which uses 159K–275K faces) in per-scene PSNR, while MobileNeRF decimated to 15K faces via QEM collapses to 8–17 dB PSNR, confirming that DiffSoup's direct optimization of the final triangle soup preserves detail that post-hoc mesh simplification destroys.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiffSoup bridges two previously separate lines of work: the stochastic surface interpretation for implicit radiance fields (Zhang et al., 2025), which treats opacity as a per-point surface-occurrence probability, and differentiable opaque-triangle rasterization (Laine et al., 2020; Pidhorskyi et al., 2024), which handles visibility discontinuities via edge gradients. The key departure is transplanting the stochastic surface loss into an explicit, depth-tested triangle rasterizer via stochastic opacity masking, eliminating the need for mollified (blurred) rasterization that prior soft-rasterization methods require for gradient stability. For appearance modeling, the paper adapts neural deferred shading (Thies et al., 2019) and atlas-free mesh color textures (Yüksel et al., 2017) to a per-triangle multi-resolution grid, avoiding the texture-atlas artifacts that would interfere with multi-resolution optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: a 15K-triangle representation renders at ~2K FPS on a desktop GPU and ~150 FPS on a consumer laptop, enabling real-time novel-view synthesis on phones and VR headsets where 3DGS's millions of primitives and depth-sorting overhead are infeasible. The stochastic opacity masking technique is a general-purpose tool for differentiating through discrete, depth-tested operations and could extend to other rasterization-based inverse problems. Key limitations remain: the single-layer opaque model cannot represent transparency or thin structures (hair, fur), and PSNR on MipNeRF360 is essentially tied with TexGS (24.76 vs 24.80), with the advantage showing primarily in SSIM and LPIPS. Closing the gap with volumetric methods on complex scenes while preserving the rendering efficiency of flat rasterization is an open challenge.
