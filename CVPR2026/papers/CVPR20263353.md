## Problem

Existing MLLMs lack the 4D perceptual capabilities (depth, optical flow, temporal progression) needed to answer region-specific questions about dynamic video scenes, and no benchmark simultaneously provides region-level prompting with sufficient dynamic video content to evaluate this capability. Prior 3D/4D VQA benchmarks either focus on static scenes or omit region prompts, leaving a gap in evaluating whether models can track specific objects over time while reasoning about their 3D geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263353/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives N sampled video frames (16 frames in evaluation), a Set-of-Marks (SoM) image overlaying numbered region annotations on the first frame, and a text question containing region tokens (e.g., '⟨R1⟩') that reference those annotations. The output is a multiple-choice selection from 4–5 answer options. Region masks are only provided for the first frame; the model must implicitly track the referenced region across subsequent frames without explicit temporal tracking annotations.

## Method

4D-RGPT builds on NVILA-Lite-8B (SigLIP vision encoder + Qwen2 LLM) and adds training-only 4D perception modules: an MLP decoder D_4DP that extracts latent 4D features from LLM hidden states, and prediction heads D_m (depth, flow, motion, camray) that produce explicit per-pixel 4D signals. The Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) framework uses a frozen L4P teacher to supervise both branches simultaneously—latent distillation (L_LD) aligns intermediate 4D embeddings, while explicit distillation (L_ED) aligns final 4D signal maps—combined with standard SFT cross-entropy loss. Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE) injects sinusoidal temporal cues directly into visual features before the projector, giving the model explicit time information without additional modules. Because all 4D perception components are training-only, inference cost is identical to the base MLLM. The companion R4D-Bench (1.4k VQAs, 9 task categories) is constructed by converting existing STI-Bench and VLM4D questions into region-prompted form via a hybrid pipeline using GroundingDINO, SAM2, Qwen2.5-VL matching, and human verification.

## Results

On six non-region-level 3D/4D VQA benchmarks, 4D-RGPT-8B improves over its NVILA-Lite-8B backbone by +5.3% average multiple-choice accuracy, with the largest gains on VSTI-Bench (+13.9%) and VLM4D-real (+6.2%). On the proposed R4D-Bench (region-level 4D VQA), 4D-RGPT achieves 42.2% average accuracy, outperforming the backbone by +4.3% and all open-source MLLMs by at least 1.6%. The model matches GPT-4o (42.8%) on R4D-Bench average while surpassing it on STI-Bench (37.6 vs. 34.8) and VSTI-Bench (59.1 vs. 38.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

4D-RGPT consistently outperforms its NVILA-Lite-8B backbone across all six non-region benchmarks, with the most dramatic gain on VSTI-Bench (59.1 vs. 45.2, +13.9%) where temporal reasoning is critical. Against proprietary models, 4D-RGPT beats GPT-4o on STI-Bench (37.6 vs. 34.8), MMSI-Bench (33.3 vs. 30.3), SAT (64.7 vs. 57.5), and VSTI-Bench (59.1 vs. 38.2), but trails on VLM4D-real (52.7 vs. 60.0) and OmniSpatial (40.4 vs. 47.8). Among open-source spatial MLLMs, 4D-RGPT surpasses SpaceR-7B (37.0/51.3/28.8), ViLaSR-7B (33.4/46.9/30.2), and SpatialReasoner-7B (31.0/43.4/22.7) on every reported benchmark. On the new R4D-Bench, 4D-RGPT (42.2%) is competitive with GPT-4o (42.8%) and clearly leads all open-source models, including Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.6%) and LLaVA-Video-7B (39.7%), with the largest margin on the dynamic split (45.7% vs. 43.1% for Qwen2.5-VL). Notably, SpaceR-7B, which outperforms Qwen2.5-VL on non-region benchmarks, drops to 37.0% on R4D-Bench, suggesting its RL training overfits to non-region VQA and weakens region-level understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the L4P unified 4D perception model as a frozen teacher and the NVILA-Lite-8B MLLM as the student backbone, departing from prior 3D/4D MLLMs (VLM-3R, SpatialReasoner, ViLaSR, SpaceR) that inject spatial knowledge via external modules at inference time. The key conceptual shift is replacing inference-time 3D/4D modules with training-only distillation heads (P4D), so the student MLLM internalizes 4D perception without any additional inference cost. The region-level prompting mechanism draws on Set-of-Marks and prior region-prompted VLMs (Omni-RGPT, 3D-aware region prompted VLM), but extends them to dynamic 4D scenarios where regions must be tracked over time. The Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE) is a lightweight addition to the visual feature pipeline, distinct from prior temporal encoding approaches in video LLMs that operate at the token or sequence level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Region-level 4D VQA is critical for real-world applications like autonomous driving and industrial inspection where queries must target specific moving objects in 3D space over time, yet no prior benchmark combined region prompts with dynamic video at scale. R4D-Bench fills this gap and provides a standardized evaluation protocol. The P4D framework's zero-inference-overhead design makes it practically deployable, and the finding that combining latent and explicit distillation outperforms either alone offers a general recipe for transferring low-level perceptual knowledge into MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or more complex multi-object interactions, and whether the distillation framework generalizes beyond the L4P teacher to other 4D perception models.
