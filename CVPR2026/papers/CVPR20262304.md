## Problem

Existing generative CAD systems are restricted to sketch and extrude operations because their design-history representations (token sequences or Python code) cannot robustly reference geometric entities like edges and faces produced during construction. This limits the complexity of generated designs and prevents modeling of common engineering operations such as fillets, chamfers, lofts, and patterns. The paper addresses this by introducing a FeatureScript-based representation and a large-scale dataset of real-world CAD designs spanning 15 modeling operations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a natural-language text description or a 2×2 grid of multi-view rendered images (256×256 per view) as conditioning input, along with the bounding-box center and extent of the target design provided in the prompt to preserve natural engineering dimensions rather than normalizing to a fixed scale. The output is an executable FeatureScript program that, when compiled in Onshape, reproduces a parametric B-rep CAD model with full geometric and parametric fidelity.

## Method

The core contribution is a FeatureScript-based design-history representation where modeling operations reference geometric entities via structured queries (makeQuery) that specify an operation identifier, topological role, entity type, and disambiguation data—enabling robust addressing of edges, faces, and bodies by their construction history rather than fragile positional indices. A data acquisition pipeline reconstructs clean, executable FeatureScript from Onshape's internal representation by resolving implicit parameters, standardizing units to millimeters, replacing random identifiers with ordered names, removing redundant operations, and validating that each program reproduces the original model. Textual annotations are generated by a two-agent LLM pipeline (Annotator drafts, Reviewer verifies) conditioned on FeatureScript documentation to ensure representation-aligned descriptions. Training uses two-stage supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2-VL-2B: first on ~170k sketch-extrude designs to establish core geometric reasoning, then on ~405k designs covering all 15 operations to generalize to complex feature construction.

## Results

On the DeepCAD test set (sketch + extrude only), the CADFS model achieves CD=0.06, ECD=8.2, NC=98.9% for text-conditioned generation, improving CD by 40% and ECD by 64% over Cadrille (CD=0.10, ECD=22.5). For multi-view reconstruction on the same set, it reaches CD=0.11, ECD=11.5, NC=98.4%, outperforming Cadrille (CD=0.20, ECD=14.3). On the new 15-operation test set, the model achieves CD=0.35, ECD=15.8, NC=96.1% in image-conditioned reconstruction, dramatically outperforming Cadrille (CD=3.26, ECD=38.5, NC=85.0%), though with a higher invalidity ratio (29% vs. 14%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Text2CAD (tokenized sequence generation, 2 operations) and Cadrille (Python/CadQuery code generation, 2 operations). On the DeepCAD test set for text-conditioned generation, CADFS substantially outperforms both: it reduces ECD from 22.5 (Cadrille) to 8.2 and from 60.3 (Text2CAD) to 8.2, while achieving the best NC (98.9%), MMD (8.10), COV (84.8%), and JSD (0.58). For multi-view reconstruction on the same set, CADFS beats Cadrille on all geometric accuracy metrics (CD 0.11 vs 0.20, ECD 11.5 vs 14.3, NC 98.4% vs 96.9%) and diversity metrics (COV 83.9% vs 81.8%, JSD 0.63 vs 0.82), though its invalidity ratio is higher (12% vs 6%). On the new 15-operation test set, the advantage over Cadrille is even more pronounced in image-conditioned reconstruction: CD drops from 3.26 to 0.35 (89% reduction) and ECD from 38.5 to 15.8 (59% reduction), reflecting Cadrille's inability to handle operations beyond sketch and extrude. The main trade-off is a higher invalidity ratio for CADFS (19–29% vs. 3–14%), attributed to the absence of FeatureScript in VLM pretraining corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADFS departs from both major prior representation families for generative CAD: token-based sequences (DeepCAD, Text2CAD, Omni-CAD) and Python/CadQuery code (Cadrille, CAD-Recode, CAD-Coder), which are all restricted to sketch and extrude operations. Rather than building on either, the paper introduces FeatureScript — Onshape's native parametric CAD language — as a third representation that natively supports 15 modeling operations including fillet, loft, revolve, and patterns. The VLM backbone (Qwen2-VL-2B) and the overall fine-tuning paradigm follow Cadrille, but the data acquisition pipeline (reconstructing clean executable FeatureScript from Onshape's internal representation) and the two-agent annotation system are novel contributions that enable the broader operation set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By expanding the trainable operation vocabulary from 2 to 15 and providing 450k real-world designs with full geometric and parametric fidelity, CADFS addresses the primary bottleneck that has limited generative CAD to simple parts. The FeatureScript representation's alignment with real engineering workflows (native to a production CAD platform) makes it a more natural target for LLMs than artificial token syntaxes or indirect Python wrappers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Onshape's specific operation set, how the 15% validation failure rate affects dataset quality at scale, and whether the higher invalidity ratio (9% vs. 3% for Cadrille on the 2-op test set) can be closed with RL or execution-in-the-loop training as Cadrille demonstrates.
