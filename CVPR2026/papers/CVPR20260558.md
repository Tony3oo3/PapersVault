## Problem

Existing video large language models rely on uniform frame sampling, which dilutes query-relevant moments and loses fine-grained temporal cues critical for video temporal grounding. While a few prior works introduce query-guided frame selection, they operate at coarse frame granularity and depend on external multimodal encoders, limiting localization precision. The paper addresses the gap of performing fine-grained, query-conditioned visual token sampling directly within the Vid-LLM pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260558/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a temporally downsampled video (2 FPS for Qwen2.5VL-7B, 16 fixed frames for InternVL3.5-8B) and a natural-language query as input. The VTS module selects a fraction ρ=0.5 of the projected visual tokens based on token-level query relevance, producing a non-uniform token sequence that is concatenated with the query embeddings and fed to the LLM. For moment retrieval the model generates start and end timestamps in an instruction-style QA format (e.g., 'from 6.0 to 12.0 seconds'); for highlight detection it outputs all salient moments with relevance scores.

## Method

GroundVTS inserts a Visual Token Sampling (VTS) module between the multimodal projector and the LLM. VTS first computes query-guided relevance scores by projecting visual and pooled text embeddings into a shared low-dimensional space and applying temperature-scaled softmax attention; it then performs a differentiable top-K selection using a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation with a Straight-Through Estimator, so that the hard selection mask is used in the forward pass while gradients flow through the soft relaxation. Selected tokens are re-weighted by their normalized relevance scores and passed through an MLP, while original positional encodings are retained to preserve temporal coherence. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: (1) VTS warm-up with all other parameters frozen, (2) joint LoRA fine-tuning of the LLM, VTS, and projector on LLaVA-Video-178K, and (3) grounding fine-tuning on a curated 70K-sample Grounding-FT dataset, enabling stable adaptation to the non-uniform token distribution.

## Results

On Charades-STA, GroundVTS-Q achieves 50.1 mIoU and 57.5 R1@0.5, surpassing the strongest prior Vid-LLM method (NumPro, 41.4 mIoU) by 8.7 points and LLaVA-ST (42.4 mIoU) by 7.7 points. On QVHighlights highlight detection, GroundVTS-I reaches 52.5 mAP and 88.4 Hit@1, outperforming NumPro (40.5 mAP) by 12.0 points. On ActivityNet-Captions, GroundVTS-Q attains 36.0 mIoU, trailing NumPro (38.8 mIoU) by 2.8 points but leading all other Vid-LLM baselines. The method also generalizes to NExT-GQA grounded video QA, where GroundVTS-Q achieves the highest mIoU (25.8) among compared methods without task-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroundVTS-Q decisively outperforms all prior Vid-LLM baselines on Charades-STA, achieving 50.1 mIoU versus NumPro (41.4), LLaVA-ST (42.4), and HawkEye (33.7), with gains of 8.7, 7.7, and 16.4 points respectively. On ActivityNet-Captions, however, NumPro retains a 2.8-point mIoU advantage (38.8 vs. 36.0), though GroundVTS-Q still leads all other Vid-LLM methods and surpasses HawkEye (32.7) by 3.3 points. On QVHighlights highlight detection, GroundVTS-I (52.5 mAP, 88.4 Hit@1) substantially outperforms NumPro (40.5 mAP, 70.7 Hit@1) by 12.0 and 17.7 points, and classical expert model UniVTG (27.0 mAP, 55.3 Hit@1) by 25.5 and 33.1 points. For QVHighlights moment retrieval, GroundVTS-I (63.6 R1@0.5) exceeds UniVTG (58.9) and SeViLA (54.5), approaching the level of specialized non-LLM methods. On the out-of-distribution NExT-GQA benchmark, GroundVTS-Q achieves the best mIoU (25.8) among all compared methods including TOGA (24.4) and VideoStreaming (19.3), despite no task-specific training. The InternVL3.5-based variant (GroundVTS-I) shows more modest gains over its fine-tuned base on Charades-STA (+2.2 mIoU) but dramatic improvements on QVHighlights, suggesting the VTS mechanism is particularly beneficial when the base model's native sampling paradigm (fixed frame count) is less suited to temporal grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroundVTS extends the Vid-LLM paradigm (Qwen2.5VL, InternVL3.5) by inserting a query-guided visual token sampling module between the multimodal projector and the LLM, departing from the uniform frame sampling that dominates prior video understanding pipelines. It builds on the Gumbel-Softmax/STE framework for differentiable discrete selection, but applies it at the token level rather than the frame level used by prior query-guided sampling methods (VideoTree, KeyVideoLLM, VideoAgent), which rely on external encoders for coarse frame selection. The progressive three-stage training strategy (VTS warm-up → joint LoRA adaptation → grounding fine-tuning) is a practical contribution for integrating new sampling modules into pretrained Vid-LLMs without destabilizing the base model, distinguishing it from prior token compression work (DivPrune, IVTP, TokenCarve) that is query-agnostic or saliency-based.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete gap—uniform sampling dilutes query-relevant temporal cues—through a mechanism that is both principled (query-conditioned attention with differentiable top-K) and practical (pluggable into existing Vid-LLMs via progressive training). The large improvement on Qwen2.5VL (+18.4 mIoU) versus the modest gain on InternVL3.5 (+2.2 mIoU) raises open questions about when and why query-guided token sampling is most beneficial, possibly related to the base model's intrinsic sampling paradigm (fixed FPS vs. fixed frame count). Limitations include a fixed sampling ratio (ρ=0.5), evaluation on only two base architectures, and the absence of analysis on very long videos where token budget constraints would be more acute. Whether adaptive or query-dependent ρ, or extension to streaming/online settings, could further close the gap remains an open direction.
