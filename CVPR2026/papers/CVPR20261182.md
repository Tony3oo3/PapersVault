## Problem

Dense foot contact estimation from a single RGB image remains largely underexplored, with existing methods either reducing contact to joint-level zero-velocity constraints or relying on general dense body contact models that perform poorly on feet. Two compounding challenges hinder progress: shoes exhibit extreme appearance diversity that causes models to overfit to spurious style cues, and ground surfaces often have weak or repetitive textures that provide limited visual information for inferring contact regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces dense foot contact as per-vertex probabilities on a 265-vertex foot mesh derived from SMPL-X, projected to three coarser levels (full mesh, 11 anatomical regions, and 3 OpenPose foot keypoints). Auxiliary outputs include a dense pixel height map (scaled to pixel units), a unit-length ground normal vector in camera coordinates, and a binary foot segmentation mask. At inference, only the clean image is processed, while training uses three synchronized inputs (one clean plus two Pro-RandConv-augmented images).

## Method

FECO employs a ViT-Huge backbone and introduces a dual randomization strategy for shoe style invariance: a low-level Pro-RandConv stage (deformable convolutions + instance normalization) removes local texture statistics, followed by a feature-level shoe style-content randomization that uses an external UT Zappos50K shoe dataset to construct two parallel branches—one preserving shoe content in input style (for adversarial training) and one interpolating channel statistics between input and shoe features (for style randomization). Complementing this, a ground feature encoder supervised by pixel height maps and ground normals captures geometric support-surface cues, with the foot region masked out during normal prediction to prevent shortcut learning. A spatial attention module adaptively fuses the style-invariant features with ground features, and a Transformer-based contact decoder (adapted from HACO) outputs vertex-level contact logits. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining binary cross-entropy on multi-level contact, adversarial style loss, foot segmentation loss, and ground geometry losses.

## Results

On the MMVP dataset for dense foot contact estimation, FECO achieves a precision of 0.563, recall of 0.613, and F1-score of 0.577, outperforming the previous best method BSTRO (F1 0.464) by 11.3 points. On the COFE dataset for joint-level foot contact estimation, FECO reaches a precision of 0.553, recall of 0.516, and F1-score of 0.515, surpassing WHAM (F1 0.363) by 15.2 points despite using only a single image without temporal information. The method also outperforms all alternative style randomization techniques (BIN, MixStyle, SagNets, LatentDR) on MMVP, with the closest competitor LatentDR achieving F1 0.542 versus FECO's 0.577.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FECO outperforms all dense foot contact baselines on MMVP: it surpasses POSA (F1 0.255) by 32.2 points, BSTRO (F1 0.464) by 11.3 points, and DECO (F1 0.409) by 16.8 points, with consistent gains in both precision and recall. For joint-level foot contact on COFE, FECO (F1 0.515) beats Footskate Reducer (F1 0.301) by 21.4 points and WHAM (F1 0.363) by 15.2 points, notably without access to temporal information that those methods exploit. Among style randomization alternatives on MMVP, FECO's shoe style-content randomization (F1 0.577) outperforms LatentDR (0.542), SagNets (0.511), BIN (0.396), and MixStyle (0.448), confirming that the dual content-and-style randomization with an external shoe dataset is more effective than prior domain generalization strategies. The largest margin is against POSA, which relies on a cVAE-based 3D vertex conditioning approach, suggesting that explicit style-invariant and ground-aware learning is substantially more effective than implicit 3D geometry priors for foot contact.

## Relation to Prior Work

FECO extends the dense body contact estimation line (POSA, BSTRO, DECO) by specializing to the foot and introducing two targeted inductive biases absent from prior work: shoe style-invariant learning and explicit ground geometry reasoning. The style-invariance mechanism adapts SagNets' content-style randomization and Pro-RandConv's progressive random convolutions, but repurposes them with an external shoe image dataset (UT Zappos50K) rather than in-batch style sources. The ground-aware branch extends the Pixel Height representation from shadow/lighting generation to foot-ground contact, and the decoder architecture is directly inherited from the authors' prior HACO work on dense hand contact. The COFE dataset fills a gap left by 3D mocap datasets, which lack in-the-wild visual diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first dedicated dense foot contact estimator, FECO establishes a baseline and evaluation protocol (COFE dataset, multi-level vertex/joint metrics) that the field lacked, and demonstrates that shoe appearance diversity and ground ambiguity are first-order challenges that generic body contact models ignore. The finding that a single-image method outperforms temporal joint-level methods (Footskate Reducer, WHAM) suggests dense spatial reasoning can substitute for motion cues in contact estimation. Limitations include reliance on the SMPL-X foot mesh (265 vertices), evaluation concentrated on MMVP for dense contact, and the shoe style invariance being validated primarily through ablations rather than systematic cross-shoe-type generalization tests. Open questions include whether the ground-aware learning transfers to non-planar or cluttered contact surfaces and whether the approach scales to other body parts beyond hands and feet.
