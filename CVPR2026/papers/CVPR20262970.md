## Problem

Diffusion transformers (e.g., SiT, DiT) require massive training iterations to converge, and existing acceleration methods introduce heavy external dependencies: REPA relies on a frozen DINOv2 encoder, while SRA maintains a dual-model setup with an EMA teacher DiT. Both incur substantial computational overhead and rigid dependencies on external models that may be unavailable in new domains. The paper asks whether a simpler, intrinsic guidance signal already present in the LDM pipeline can accelerate training without any extra model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262970/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For ImageNet 256×256 generation, the model is conditioned on a class label; for T2I (MS-COCO), it takes a text prompt. The key input detail is that SD-VAE latent features (4×32×32 tensors) are pre-extracted offline and stored, so the alignment target is available at zero runtime cost during diffusion training. The output is a 256×256 RGB image sampled via an SDE Euler-Maruyama solver (250 steps) with optional classifier-free guidance.

## Method

SRA² exploits the observation that pre-trained SD-VAE features, by virtue of their reconstruction objective, already encode rich texture, structural, and semantic priors, and are routinely pre-extracted for LDM training. During SiT training, an intermediate hidden-layer feature (layer 2 for B/2, layer 8 for L/2 and XL/2) is projected into the VAE feature space via a lightweight 5-layer MLP (18M parameters), then aligned to the corresponding pre-extracted VAE feature using a smooth-L1 loss over the full timestep range [0,1]. The total training objective is the standard SiT velocity-matching denoising loss plus λ·L_align (λ=1.0), requiring no external encoder, no dual-model, and no additional forward pass. This adds only 4% GFLOPs and 6% forward latency over vanilla SiT-XL/2, while achieving a 7× training acceleration (FID 8.2 at 1M iterations vs. 8.3 at 7M iterations for vanilla SiT-XL/2).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with CFG, SRA² achieves FID 1.52, sFID 4.63, and IS 316.2 at 800 epochs, surpassing SRA (FID 1.58, IS 305.7) and matching or exceeding REPA (FID 1.42, IS 311.4) and REG (FID 1.36, IS 299.4) without any external model dependency. In terms of training convergence, SiT-XL/2+SRA² reaches FID 8.2 at 1M iterations, matching SiT-XL/2's FID 8.3 at 7M iterations—a 7× training acceleration. On MS-COCO T2I, MMDiT+SRA² achieves FID 4.67 and PickScore 20.92, competitive with MMDiT+REPA (FID 4.60, PickScore 20.90) while requiring zero external encoder parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRA² outperforms SRA [18] on both FID (1.52 vs. 1.58) and IS (316.2 vs. 305.7) at 800 epochs, while eliminating the 481M-parameter teacher model that SRA requires. Against REPA [44], SRA² trades a slight FID gap (1.52 vs. 1.42) for a higher IS (316.2 vs. 311.4) and zero external encoder dependency; against REG [39], SRA² achieves comparable FID (1.52 vs. 1.36) with superior IS (316.2 vs. 299.4). SRA² substantially outperforms masked-modeling baselines: at 200 epochs it reaches FID 1.98, beating MaskDiT's 2.28 at 1600 epochs and SD-DiT's 3.23 at 480 epochs. In training convergence (Table 2), SiT-XL/2+SRA² achieves FID 8.2 at 1M iterations versus SiT-XL/2's 8.3 at 7M (7× speedup), and when stacked on REPA it further reduces FID from 7.9 to 6.8 at 400K iterations. On MS-COCO T2I, MMDiT+SRA² (FID 4.67) slightly trails MMDiT+REPA (FID 4.60) but matches its PickScore (20.92 vs. 20.90) and outperforms MMDiT+SRA (FID 4.74). Computationally, SRA² adds only 4% GFLOPs and 6% latency versus +21%/+26% for REPA and +73%/+71% for SRA, with zero external model parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRA² builds directly on SiT's flow-matching framework and the two-stage LDM pipeline, but departs from the three dominant acceleration paradigms—masked modeling (MaskDiT, SD-DiT), external encoder guidance (REPA, REG), and dual-model self-alignment (SRA, SSD)—by recognizing that the SD-VAE already pre-extracted in stage one encodes sufficient visual priors (texture, structure, basic semantics) to serve as alignment targets. Conceptually, it repurposes REPA's feature-alignment loss while replacing the external DINOv2 encoder with the VAE that is already a mandatory component of the LDM pipeline, eliminating all external model dependencies. The method is also inspired by deep supervision, applying an auxiliary loss at intermediate layers rather than only at the output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for external encoders or teacher models, SRA² addresses a practical bottleneck in scaling diffusion training to domains (T2I, T2V, T2A) where reliable pre-trained representation models are unavailable or mismatched. The 7× training acceleration for SiT-XL/2 (1M vs. 7M iterations) at only 4% extra GFLOPs makes this a highly attractive drop-in improvement for practitioners. Open questions include whether the VAE's reconstruction-oriented features impose a semantic ceiling compared to discriminative encoders like DINOv2, whether the approach transfers to video or audio generation where VAEs are less mature, and whether the alignment depth/timestep choices generalize beyond ImageNet-class-conditional settings.
