## Problem

Standard classifier-free guidance (CFG) improves generation quality but causes over-simplified or distorted samples at high guidance scales, while alternative autoguidance approaches that use a degraded version of the model require carefully designed degradation strategies, extra training, or additional sampling steps. The paper asks whether a diffusion transformer can be guided by its own internal representations without any of these overheads.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263953/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and Gaussian noise as input and generates 256×256 RGB images via a latent diffusion pipeline (images are encoded to 32×32×4 latents with the Stable Diffusion VAE or 16×16×32 with VA-VAE). During sampling, the denoising network produces two outputs per step—the intermediate-layer prediction D_i and the final-layer prediction D_f—which are combined via extrapolation to form the guided denoised estimate, requiring no extra forward passes beyond the single network evaluation.

## Method

Internal Guidance (IG) adds a lightweight auxiliary output head at an intermediate transformer block (e.g., layer 4 for B/2, layer 8 for XL) and trains it with the same denoising loss as the final layer, weighted by a small coefficient λ. At sampling time, the intermediate output D_i serves as a 'weaker' version of the model's prediction, and the guided estimate is computed by extrapolating: D_w = D_i + w(D_f − D_i), where w > 1 amplifies the difference between the two. This yields an autoguidance-like effect—pushing samples toward high-probability regions while preserving diversity—without any separate degraded model, extra training, or additional sampling steps. The auxiliary supervision also alleviates partial vanishing gradients in deep transformers, improving convergence to a degree comparable to self-supervised representation regularization. IG is further combined with CFG and a guidance interval (applying w only in the high-to-mid noise range, opposite to CFG's convention) to achieve state-of-the-art FID of 1.19 on ImageNet 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, LightningDiT-XL/1+IG achieves FID=1.34 without CFG at 680 epochs, outperforming all prior methods by a large margin (next best: RAE/DiTDH at 1.60). Combined with CFG and guidance interval, it reaches a state-of-the-art FID=1.19. SiT-XL/2+IG achieves FID=5.31 at just 80 epochs (already beating vanilla SiT-XL at 1400 epochs, FID=8.61) and FID=1.75 at 800 epochs without CFG, or FID=1.46 with CFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightningDiT-XL/1+IG dominates all prior methods without CFG: at 680 epochs it achieves FID=1.34, beating RAE/DiTDH (1.60), REPA-E (1.83), REG (2.20), and vanilla LightningDiT (2.17) by substantial margins. With CFG, it reaches FID=1.19, surpassing REPA-E (1.26), RAE/DiTDH (1.28), SiD2 (1.38), LightningDiT (1.35), REG (1.40), and REPA (1.42). SiT-XL/2+IG at 80 epochs (FID=5.31) already exceeds vanilla SiT-XL at 1400 epochs (FID=8.61) and REPA at 800 epochs (FID=5.90), demonstrating that the auxiliary supervision loss alone accelerates convergence dramatically. At 800 epochs with CFG, SiT+IG achieves FID=1.46, outperforming SiT (2.06), DiT (2.27), and MaskDiT (2.28). The paper also shows that the simple auxiliary supervision loss matches or beats self-supervised regularization methods (SRA: FID=29.10, Disperse Loss: FID=31.45) at 80 epochs on SiT-B/2, achieving FID=30.45 without any pretrained representation model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: (1) sampling guidance for diffusion models, where it builds directly on classifier-free guidance (CFG) and the Autoguidance paradigm of Karras et al. (NeurIPS 2024), and (2) intermediate representation regularization in diffusion transformers, where it relates to REPA, SRA, and Disperse Loss. The key conceptual departure from Autoguidance is eliminating the need for a separately trained 'bad version' or carefully designed degradation strategy: the intermediate layer of the same model naturally produces a weaker output, making the guidance plug-and-play with zero extra sampling steps. The auxiliary supervision loss also connects to the deep supervision literature, but the paper's novelty is in repurposing this simple training-time trick as a sampling-time guidance signal, and in adapting the guidance interval concept (Kynkäänniemi et al., NeurIPS 2024) to this new setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single auxiliary loss at one intermediate layer yields SOTA FID=1.19 on ImageNet 256×256 with LightningDiT-XL/1, and the method is architecture-agnostic (validated on DiT, SiT, and LightningDiT) with no additional sampling cost. The finding that IG's optimal guidance interval is the *opposite* of CFG's (high-noise rather than low-noise range) is a genuinely counterintuitive result that suggests the two guidance mechanisms operate on fundamentally different aspects of the denoising trajectory. Open questions include whether IG transfers to text-to-image or video generation, how it interacts with other recent acceleration techniques (e.g., distillation, few-step samplers), and whether the intermediate supervision benefit persists at even larger scales or with different tokenizer designs beyond DINOv2-aligned VAEs.
