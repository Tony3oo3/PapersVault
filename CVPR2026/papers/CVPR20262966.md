## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks for generative models are confined to single-modal text inputs (T2I or T2V) and rely on static, manually crafted prompts, making them ineffective against Image-to-Video (I2V) models that jointly process a reference image and text prompt. The cross-modal safety mechanisms in I2V systems create unique vulnerabilities that no prior attack method has systematically exploited, leaving a critical gap in the security assessment of these increasingly deployed commercial systems.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a reference image (H×W×C) and an associated text prompt as input, and iteratively produces a tampered reference image (via FLUX-based editing that preserves high perceptual similarity to the original) and a stealthy video prompt, which are jointly fed to the target I2V model to generate an unsafe video (T×H'×W'×C'). The attack loop is closed by an MLLM safety evaluator (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-Next, or Gemma-3-VL) that judges whether the generated video is unsafe, and successful attack records (image, edit instruction, video prompt, strategy) are stored in a Strategy Memory Bank for future retrieval.

## Method

RunawayEvil is built on a 'Strategy-Tactic-Action' paradigm with three collaborative units. The Strategy-Aware Command Unit (SACU) drives self-evolution: a Strategy Exploration Agent (LLM-based) mines successful attack experiences from a Strategy Memory Bank to generate novel strategies, while a Strategy Customization Agent learns via reinforcement learning (formulated as an MDP) to select the optimal strategy per input, with a multi-faceted reward combining iteration penalty, text stealth (keyword-overlap penalty), image stealth (LPIPS penalty), and a large success reward. The Multimodal Tactical Planning Unit (an MLLM agent) translates the selected strategy into coordinated text-prompt and image-editing instructions, augmented by cosine-similarity retrieval of past successful attacks using the same strategy. The Tactical Action Unit executes the attack by applying FLUX-based image editing and feeding the modified image-text pair to the I2V model, then evaluates the result with an MLLM safety evaluator; if unsuccessful, the loop iterates with updated tactics. This closed-loop, self-evolving design replaces static manual prompts with dynamic, input-adaptive multimodal coordination.

## Results

On COCO2017, RunawayEvil achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 87.6% across four I2V models (Wan, DynamiCrafter, Open-Sora 2.0, CogVideoX) and three MLLM safety evaluators (Qwen-VL, LLaVA-Next, Gemma-3-VL), outperforming the best baseline (PGJ, peak 47.0%) by 58.5%–79%. On MM-SafetyBench, RunawayEvil reaches 88.0%–96.0% ASR, maintaining a consistent 2–12 point lead over baselines that cluster in the 78%–87% range. The method ranks first in all 24 model–evaluator settings on both datasets, with the largest margins observed on COCO2017 where baselines drop as low as 6.5% (Sneaky on DynamiCrafter/Gemma).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines—SneakyPrompt (RL-based token search), PGJ (perception-guided prompt rewriting), and DACA (LLM divide-and-conquer)—are originally designed for T2I jailbreaking and are extended to I2V in this work. On COCO2017, PGJ is the strongest baseline, peaking at 47.0% ASR (CogVideoX/LLaVA), while SneakyPrompt and DACA range from 6.5% to 46.0%, revealing that their rigid, single-modal attack patterns transfer poorly to the multimodal I2V setting. RunawayEvil dominates every comparison: it exceeds PGJ by 40–55 percentage points on COCO2017 and by 2–12 points on MM-SafetyBench, where the baselines are already relatively strong (78%–87%) due to the inherently adversarial nature of that benchmark. The gap is most dramatic on DynamiCrafter, where SneakyPrompt drops to 6.5% (Gemma evaluator) while RunawayEvil maintains 85.5%–92.0%, underscoring the advantage of coordinated text-image attacks over text-only perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the jailbreak literature from single-modal T2I and T2V settings to the I2V domain, where attacks must coordinate perturbations across both a reference image and a text prompt. Conceptually, it repurposes RL-based prompt optimization (SneakyPrompt), LLM-driven adversarial prompt generation (PGJ, DACA), and iterative T2V jailbreak frameworks (Liu et al. 2025) into a unified 'Strategy-Tactic-Action' architecture that adds a self-evolving strategy layer (RL policy + LLM exploration agent + memory bank) absent from prior work. The key departure is the explicit cross-modal coordination: rather than attacking text or image in isolation, the framework jointly generates a malicious video prompt and an image-editing instruction (executed via FLUX) designed to bypass I2V-specific safety filters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As I2V models (Open-Sora 2.0, CogVideoX, Wan2.2) move into commercial deployment, this work provides the first systematic vulnerability assessment tool for their multimodal safety mechanisms, revealing that coordinated text-image attacks are far more effective than unimodal ones. A key open question is whether the demonstrated ASRs (85–93%) reflect genuine model vulnerabilities or partly an artifact of using MLLM-based safety evaluators that may themselves be jailbreakable. The paper does not propose defenses, leaving the practical question of how to harden I2V models against such adaptive, self-evolving attacks entirely open. Additionally, the baselines are T2I methods naively extended to I2V, so the true competitive landscape for purpose-built I2V attacks remains unclear.
