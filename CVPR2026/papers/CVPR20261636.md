## Problem

Existing latent diffusion models rely on β-VAE tokenizers trained with very small KL weights, which causes the latent manifold to collapse into an overly compact region. This compactness makes the latent space highly vulnerable to the stochastic perturbations inherent in diffusion sampling, causing latents to be pushed outside the data manifold and producing degraded or failed generations. The paper identifies this robustness-to-sampling-perturbations property as a critical but overlooked factor that goes beyond reconstruction accuracy and semantic alignment.

## Input / Output

The system takes a class label as input and generates a 256×256 RGB image via class-conditional generation on ImageNet. The tokenizer downsamples the input by a factor of 16 (f16d16 or f16d32 configurations), and the diffusion model operates entirely in this compressed latent space using flow matching with linear interpolation. Sampling uses 250 Euler steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 1.70, interval [0.21, 1]) for the main results.

## Method

The paper replaces the conventional KL divergence term in the VAE tokenizer with a Variance Expansion (VE) loss, L_var = 1/(σ² + δ), which explicitly encourages moderate latent variance to counteract the variance collapse induced by the reconstruction objective. A secondary regularization term L_reg = e^{|z|−τ} prevents unbounded magnitude growth. The key mechanism is the adversarial interplay between reconstruction (which drives σ→0 via gradient 2σT(μ)) and the VE loss (which exerts a strong restoring force −2λ/σ³ as σ→0), yielding a locally adaptive equilibrium variance σ = (λ/T(μ))^{1/4} that scales inversely with decoder sensitivity. The KL term is discarded entirely on the grounds that the diffusion model itself learns the latent prior through its denoising trajectory, making the fixed Gaussian constraint unnecessary and detrimental to reconstruction. The approach is plug-and-play, requiring only a modification to the tokenizer training objective while remaining compatible with existing diffusion backbones (DiT, SiT, LightningDiT) and representation-aligned tokenizers (VA-VAE).

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 with classifier-free guidance (CFG scale 1.70), the proposed VE loss achieves a gFID of 1.18 with only 530 training epochs, outperforming the previous best (VA-VAE, gFID 1.35 at 800 epochs) by a margin of 0.17 FID. The method also attains the highest Recall (0.66, tied with REG) and competitive sFID (4.29). In the ablation setting without CFG (Table 2), replacing KL regularization with VE loss reduces FID-10K from 23.12 (best KL setting) to 18.90, a 4.22-point improvement, while maintaining competitive reconstruction (rFID 0.46 vs. 0.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed VE loss achieves the best gFID (1.18) among all compared methods, beating the closest competitor VA-VAE (1.35) by 0.17 and REG (1.36) by 0.18, while requiring only 530 epochs versus 800 for both. Against autoregressive baselines, the method substantially outperforms VAR (1.80), MAR (1.55), and LlamaGen (2.18) in gFID, though VAR achieves a higher IS (365.4 vs. 289.8) and Precision (0.83 vs. 0.78). The method ties REG on Recall (0.66) and slightly trails VA-VAE on sFID (4.29 vs. 4.15) and IS (289.8 vs. 295.3), but compensates with a significantly lower gFID. In the no-CFG ablation (Table 2), VE loss reduces FID-10K from 23.12 (best KL weight β=10⁻⁶) to 18.90, demonstrating that no KL weight setting matches the adaptive variance control of VE loss. The method also outperforms RAE (1.41) and REPA (1.42) in gFID, confirming that the robustness gain from variance expansion is complementary to semantic alignment strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent diffusion model framework (Rombach et al., 2022) and the β-VAE tokenizer paradigm (Higgins et al., 2017) by identifying a previously overlooked failure mode: variance collapse in the latent space that makes it fragile to stochastic perturbations during diffusion sampling. While recent work (VA-VAE, MAETok, DC-AE 1.5, RAE) has focused on improving semantic alignment or trainability of the latent space, this paper argues that robustness to sampling perturbations is an independent and critical axis. It departs from heuristic robustness tricks in concurrent work (GIVT's stronger KL, σ-VAE's fixed variance, RAE's injected noise) by proposing a theoretically grounded, adaptive Variance Expansion loss that replaces the KL term entirely, leveraging the adversarial interplay between reconstruction and variance expansion to self-tune latent spread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a tokenizer with superior reconstruction and lower diffusion loss can produce visually inferior generations challenges the implicit assumption in the LDM community that better reconstruction always translates to better generation. The proposed VE loss is a simple, drop-in replacement for the KL term that consistently improves FID across multiple tokenizer and diffusion architectures, and achieves a 1.18 FID on ImageNet 256×256 with fewer training epochs than prior SOTA. Open questions include whether the adaptive variance mechanism generalizes to video generation, higher resolutions, or unconditional settings, and whether the slight reconstruction cost (rFID 0.46 vs. 0.39 in the KL baseline) becomes more pronounced at scale. The paper also highlights that the Muon optimizer was necessary for stable long-training, suggesting the interaction between tokenizer design and optimizer choice remains underexplored.
