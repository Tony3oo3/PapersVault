## Problem

Existing CG image quality assessment datasets provide only scalar MOS scores without systematic text-based descriptions of rendering quality, and current VLMs lack the fine-grained perceptual knowledge to accurately judge and explain CG quality. The paper identifies that descriptions of visually similar CG images can significantly improve a VLM's understanding of a target image, but that irrelevant descriptions are detrimental, motivating a retrieval-based approach that carefully selects relevant examples.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query CG image (1080p–4K resolution) and a natural-language quality question, and retrieves the most relevant example description from a library of 3,190 CG images annotated across six perceptual dimensions (lighting, material, color, atmosphere, realism, space). The VLM then outputs both a scalar quality judgment and a free-form textual explanation. For benchmark evaluation, outputs are scored on multiple-choice accuracy, yes-or-no accuracy, and a 5-point Q&A scale (auto-scored by GPT-4o-mini against ground-truth answers).

## Method

R4-CGQA is a training-free, two-stream retrieval-augmented generation framework. Stage 1 performs global content retrieval using CLIP embeddings indexed by FAISS to obtain a top-K candidate set. Stage 2 computes REIQA-based quality embeddings for each candidate and fuses content and quality cosine similarities by simple averaging to select the single most relevant example; a similarity threshold τ_sim gates whether any example is injected. The selected description is inserted into a fixed prompt template alongside the query image and question, and the VLM generates the final answer. The key insight is that combining content similarity (what the image shows) with quality similarity (how well it is rendered) avoids retrieving visually similar but quality-mismatched examples that would mislead the VLM.

## Results

On the R4-CGQA testing set, R4-CGQA consistently improves all 10 evaluated VLMs across three question types (Choice, Yes-or-no, Q&A). The average absolute gains are 4.26% on Choice, 6.94% on Yes-or-no, and 0.32 points (6.40%) on the 5-point Q&A scale. Smaller models benefit most: BakLLaVA-7B improves by +12.25% on Choice (43.72%→55.97%) and Qwen 2.5-VL-7B by +23.20% on Q&A (1.30→2.46), while the strongest model (Qwen 2.5-VL-32B) sees a more modest +1.95% average gain. The method incurs only 4.5% runtime overhead and 1748 MB additional GPU memory on LLaVA 1.6-13B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is each VLM evaluated directly (without retrieval) versus the same VLM augmented with R4-CGQA retrieval. R4-CGQA wins on every metric for every model tested, with no regressions observed. The gains are most pronounced for smaller or weaker models: BakLLaVA-7B (avg +8.79%), Qwen 2.5-VL-7B (+8.96%), and MiniCPM-V-8B (+8.16%) all exceed 8% average improvement, while the strongest models (Qwen 2.5-VL-32B at +1.95%, LLaVA-NeXT-32B at +2.58%) show diminishing returns, suggesting the retrieval cues are most valuable when the VLM's intrinsic CGQA knowledge is limited. On Yes-or-no questions, Llama 3.2-V-11B sees the largest single-metric jump (+10.39%), indicating that retrieved quality descriptions particularly help binary quality judgments. The paper does not compare against other retrieval-augmented CGQA methods or fine-tuning-based baselines, so the comparison is strictly a before/after ablation of the retrieval component rather than a head-to-head against competing approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CG image quality assessment (CGQA) from the score-only paradigm established by Wang et al. (2022) and Zhang et al. (2024) to a multi-dimensional, text-explanatory formulation, and repurposes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) from the NLP domain to supply VLMs with relevant quality descriptions at inference time. It builds on the VLM-based IQA line (Q-Bench, Q-Instruct, Q-Adapt, Q-Boost) but departs from those works by avoiding fine-tuning entirely and instead leveraging a two-stream retrieval mechanism (CLIP content + REIQA quality embeddings) to select the most informative example description. The approach is conceptually distinct from prior CGQA methods that train dedicated scoring networks, positioning CGQA as a retrieval-conditioned language generation task rather than a regression problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.5K-image dataset with six-dimension textual descriptions fills a clear gap: no prior CG dataset provides interpretable, multi-faceted quality explanations suitable for VLM-based assessment. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of R4-CGQA makes it immediately applicable to any VLM without retraining, which is practically valuable for industrial CG pipelines that need rapid, explainable quality feedback. Key limitations remain: the method struggles with fine-grained local artifacts due to VLM resolution constraints, and retrieved descriptions can act as noise when semantically misaligned with the query. Open questions include whether the six perceptual dimensions generalize across all CG styles, whether the approach extends to video or 3D rendering, and whether larger retrieval libraries yield diminishing or compounding returns.
