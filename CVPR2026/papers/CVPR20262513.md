## Problem

Existing methods for predicting spatial gene expression from pathology images primarily model spatial dependencies within a single tissue slice, overlooking shared morphological and expression patterns that exist across slides of the same tissue type. Additionally, ST data are inherently sparse and noisy due to experimental resolution and batch effects, yet current approaches lack mechanisms to build robustness against these perturbations. The paper addresses both gaps by introducing cross-slide knowledge transfer and noise-resilient representation learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target H&E slide partitioned into 224×224 image patches centered on ST spot coordinates, along with multiple reference slides of the same tissue type that contribute both 1024-dim UNI image embeddings and their ground-truth gene expression profiles (concatenated into d+M dimensional node features). The output is a predicted spatial gene expression matrix of shape n×M for the target slide, where n is the number of spatial spots and M is the number of genes. Reference slides are selected via Top-K cosine similarity matching on image embeddings, and the nested LOOCV protocol ensures no data leakage between training reference banks and test slides.

## Method

SpaHGC constructs a multi-modal heterogeneous graph with three edge types: Target-slice (TS) edges linking spatially neighboring spots via k-nearest-neighbor on Euclidean distance, Cross-slice (CS) edges connecting each target spot to its Top-K most morphologically similar reference spots, and Reference-slice (RS) edges linking similar reference nodes based on joint visual-expression features. A Cross Node Dual Attention (CNDA) module performs bidirectional cross-attention along CS edges, allowing target nodes to selectively absorb expression knowledge from reference nodes while reference nodes update their features from target visual context. A Cross Node Attention Pool (CNAP) module then applies multi-head unidirectional cross-attention to fuse reference information into target representations, with a residual connection preserving the target's own signal. For robustness, a complementary masking strategy generates two augmented graph views with mutually exclusive feature masks (M₁ + M₂ = 1), and an asymmetric contrastive loss (one view processed without gradients) enforces consistency between views. The full training objective combines MSE, PCC, and contrastive losses, with GraphSAGE handling TS/RS edges and CNDA handling CS edges within a shared-parameter heterogeneous encoder.

## Results

SpaHGC achieves the highest PCC and lowest RMSE across all seven datasets, with PCC values of 27.86% (HER+), 38.79% (cSCC), 17.19% (Alex), 20.08% (Visium BC), 35.02% (Lymph Node), 24.48% (Pancreas1), and 41.36% (Pancreas2). The paper reports PCC improvements of 7.3%–27.1% over the nine baselines, with the largest gains on Visium BC (+13.6 pp over M2OST) and Pancreas1 (+9.4 pp over M2OST). Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistical significance (p < 0.001) on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaHGC outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset for both PCC and RMSE. The strongest competitor varies by dataset: mclSTExp [17] leads among baselines on HER+ (23.15%) and cSCC (31.88%), while M2OST [29] is the top baseline on Alex (15.13%), Visium BC (6.52%), Lymph Node (30.97%), Pancreas1 (15.12%), and Pancreas2 (38.35%). The largest absolute PCC gains are on Visium BC (+13.6 pp over M2OST) and Pancreas1 (+9.4 pp over M2OST), suggesting cross-slice transfer is most beneficial when within-slide spatial structure alone is insufficient. On Pancreas2, where M2OST already achieves 38.35%, SpaHGC still improves to 41.36% (+3.0 pp). Image-to-expression mapping (STNet) and early intra-slide methods (HisToGene, His2ST) are consistently the weakest baselines, with PCC below 20% on most datasets, highlighting the importance of cross-slide and multi-modal modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaHGC departs from the dominant single-slide paradigm in spatial gene expression prediction (STNet, HisToGene, Hist2ST, THItoGene, HGGEP, M2OST) and multimodal alignment approaches (BLEEP, mclSTExp, EGGN) by introducing a heterogeneous graph that explicitly connects a target slide to a cohort of reference slides. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes GraphSAGE for intra-slide spatial edges, a pathology foundation model (UNI) for visual embeddings, and novel cross-node dual-attention (CNDA) and attention-pooling (CNAP) modules for inter-slide knowledge transfer. The complementary masked contrastive learning objective draws on graph contrastive learning but is specifically designed to simulate the feature dropout and sequencing noise characteristic of ST data, distinguishing it from standard graph augmentation strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that cross-slide morphological and expression similarities can be exploited to substantially improve prediction accuracy (7.3–27.1% PCC gains over nine baselines across seven datasets), SpaHGC offers a practical path toward reducing reliance on expensive ST experiments for large-scale spatial omics studies. The nested LOOCV protocol and the requirement for same-tissue-type reference slides, however, limit immediate clinical deployment and raise open questions about scalability to very large cohorts, generalization across tissue types without matched references, and applicability to emerging single-cell-resolution ST platforms. Whether the cross-slide transfer signal remains informative when reference slides come from different patients or disease stages remains unexplored.
