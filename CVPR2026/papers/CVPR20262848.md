## Problem

Existing disentanglement-based methods for multimodal sentiment analysis implicitly assume that all modalities of the same sample express consistent emotional polarity, and they align shared representations via similarity losses. When modalities genuinely conflict (e.g., positive text with negative audio tone), this similarity-based alignment distorts the shared semantic and leads to incorrect predictions. The paper addresses how to learn consistent shared representations while adaptively suppressing conflicting modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262848/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted unimodal sequences: 768-dim BERT word embeddings for language, 35-dim facial action unit features (Facet) for video, and 74-dim prosodic features (COVAREP) for audio, each of variable length T_m unified via 1D convolution. It produces both a continuous sentiment intensity score (regression, range −3 to +3 on MOSI/MOSEI, −1 to +1 on CH-SIMS) and discrete class labels (7-class or binary), with auxiliary classifiers on both the shared and specific branches for hierarchical prediction.

## Method

CACR first disentangles each modality into shared (H_m) and specific (P_m) subspaces regularized by a soft orthogonality loss. A Conflict-Aware Weighting (CAW) strategy then computes per-sample conflict scores as the mean cosine distance between a target modality's pooled shared vector and the other modalities' pooled shared vectors, mapping them through an exponential decay function to adaptive cross-reconstruction loss weights. The Cross-Reconstruction Module (CRM) reconstructs each target modality by concatenating its own specific features with the shared features of the other modalities and decoding through a 1D convolutional decoder; the CAW weights down-weight high-conflict samples so that conflicting modalities contribute less to the reconstruction objective, achieving implicit shared-representation alignment without explicit similarity constraints. A Fine-grained Sentiment Refinement (FSR) module uses text-guided cross-attention to selectively aggregate emotional cues from visual- and audio-specific features into the language representation. Finally, a multimodal Transformer fuses the refined specific features with the cross-reconstructed shared features, and hierarchical prediction heads on both branches are jointly optimized with the reconstruction, orthogonality, and specific-preservation losses.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI, CACR achieves 48.69% ACC7, 87.04% ACC2, 86.90% F1, and 0.710 MAE, outperforming the strongest baseline EMOE (47.80% ACC7) by 0.89 points. On CMU-MOSEI, CACR reaches 54.41% ACC7, 85.37% ACC2, 85.36% F1, and 0.532 MAE, surpassing EMOE (54.10% ACC7) and CGGM (53.06% ACC7). On CH-SIMS, CACR attains 81.62% ACC2, 80.99% F1, 0.596 Corr, and 0.423 MAE, exceeding the next-best DLF (78.56% ACC2) by over 3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CACR outperforms all 13 baselines across all three datasets and all reported metrics. On MOSI, the largest gains are over DLF (+3.35 ACC7, +1.37 ACC2) and DMD (+2.92 ACC7), the two closest disentanglement-based competitors, while matching EMOE's MAE (0.710) but exceeding it by 0.89 ACC7 and 1.64 ACC2. On MOSEI, CACR leads EMOE by 0.31 ACC7 and 0.07 ACC2, and surpasses CGGM by 1.35 ACC7 and 0.56 ACC2. On CH-SIMS, CACR achieves a clear margin over DLF (+3.06 ACC2, +2.46 F1) and DMD (+3.28 ACC2, +2.43 F1), with the best Corr (0.596) and lowest MAE (0.423). Among fusion-based baselines (TFN, LMF, MulT, MFM), CACR leads by 8–14 ACC7 points on MOSI, confirming that the conflict-aware cross-reconstruction paradigm substantially outperforms standard fusion strategies. The ablation further shows that removing the conflict-aware weighting (CAW) drops ACC7 from 48.69 to 44.31, and replacing it with a generic gating mechanism yields 44.98, confirming that the explicit conflict modeling—not generic adaptive weighting—is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the disentanglement-based paradigm for multimodal sentiment analysis (MISA, FDMER, DMD, DLF) by identifying a critical blind spot: prior methods assume inter-modal emotional consistency and enforce shared-representation alignment via similarity losses, which distorts semantics when modalities express contradictory sentiments within the same sample. CACR departs from this by replacing intra-modal reconstruction with cross-reconstruction (reconstructing a target modality from its own specific features plus other modalities' shared features) and introducing a conflict-aware weighting strategy that adaptively down-weights conflicting modalities based on sample-level divergence scores in the shared subspace. The fine-grained sentiment refinement module further differentiates CACR from DLF by selectively injecting visual and audio emotional cues into the language branch rather than treating all modalities symmetrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The conflict-aware framing addresses a practically important failure mode—sarcasm, irony, and mixed emotional signals are common in real-world video but systematically mishandled by similarity-based alignment. The modular design (CAW + CRM + FSR) is interpretable and could transfer to other multimodal tasks where intra-sample modality disagreement is prevalent. However, the conflict definition relies on cosine similarity in a learned shared space, which may be brittle for subtle or multi-dimensional conflicts, and the paper does not test on incomplete-modality scenarios despite acknowledging this as future work. The evaluation is limited to three datasets of moderate size, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or more diverse multimodal benchmarks.
