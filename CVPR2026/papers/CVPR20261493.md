## Problem

Conventional edge-cloud speculative decoding relies on fixed distributional divergence thresholds for token verification, which cannot adapt to the temporal consistency (credit inertia) observed in acceptance rates across consecutive windows. This rigidity causes unnecessary rejections of high-quality tokens during stable phases and fails to tighten scrutiny during low-confidence phases, while the strict synchronization between edge draft generation and cloud verification introduces substantial idle latency and uplink overhead.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a text prompt processed by an edge draft model (e.g., 1.5B or 1B SLM) that produces a speculative window of W_t candidate tokens, alongside a scalar state vector (r̂_t, Δr̂_t) computed as the EWMA-smoothed acceptance rate and its momentum derivative over the past k=5 windows. The output is the accepted token prefix committed to the generation sequence, together with a dynamically adjusted Jensen-Shannon divergence threshold T_t and an adaptive window size W_t that govern the next draft generation round. The predictor is a lightweight 5-layer feedforward network, so inference overhead is negligible relative to model forward passes.

## Method

E²-SCI exploits credit inertia—the empirical observation that adjacent speculative windows exhibit highly correlated acceptance rates—by maintaining an EWMA-smoothed acceptance state r̂_t (decay β=0.3) and feeding it into a trained 5-layer feedforward predictor g_θ that outputs an adaptive weight ω_t and window size W_t. The verification threshold is updated as T_t = clamp(T_base + α·ω_t·(r̂_t − τ_credit), T_min, T_max), relaxing acceptance when historical performance exceeds the neutral baseline and tightening it otherwise, thereby reducing unnecessary rejections without compromising distributional fidelity. A second component, Progressive Lookahead Concurrency (PLC), decouples the pipeline into asynchronous pre-hit and validation phases: the target model verifies only the leading token first, and if accepted, the draft model continues generating the next window while the target model validates the current one in parallel, with the concurrent window dynamically expanding via W_t^P = W_t·∏(1+η_j) based on received feedback signals. The predictor is trained via supervised learning on an offline-calibrated lookup table that maps acceptance states to empirically optimal thresholds, minimizing expected segment latency minus a weighted accepted-token reward (Eq. 4).

## Results

On the headline DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen (1.5B/32B) edge-cloud setting, E²-SCI achieves 9.4+ tokens/s with an 88.5% speed improvement over the FSD baseline while maintaining accuracy. Across four datasets (GSM8K, CommonsenseQA, MMLU, HumanEval) and four model pairs, E²-SCI delivers speedup ratios of 1.84×–2.19×, consistently exceeding the next-best method (FR-Spec at 1.72×–2.04×) and FSD (1.13×–1.51×). In edge-cloud throughput (Table 2), E²-SCI reaches 22.20–30.03 tokens/s under normal conditions and 22.20–29.52 tokens/s under edge-cloud conditions, versus 15.51–20.14 and 13.80–19.71 for the FSD baseline respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²-SCI outperforms all seven compared baselines across every dataset and model pair in Table 1. Against FSD [12], the closest conceptual predecessor, E²-SCI achieves 32–90% higher speedup (e.g., 2.01× vs 1.17× on HumanEval/DS-R1, a 72% gain). FR-Spec [39] is the strongest competitor, reaching 2.02× on CSQA/DS-R1, but E²-SCI still edges ahead on GSM8K (1.98× vs 1.95×) and MMLU (1.98× vs 1.79×). LR [7] and AMUSD [21] trail E²-SCI by 4–17% on most configurations. In the accuracy integration study (Table 3), E²-SCI as a plug-in improves Medusa by +1.8–2.3%, FSD by +1.9–2.4%, EAGLE-3 by up to +1.6% (GSM8K: 93.1→94.7), and PipeSpec by +0.9–1.1% across all four benchmarks, confirming orthogonal benefits independent of the underlying speculative decoding architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fuzzy speculative decoding (FSD) line of work, which introduced tunable distributional divergence thresholds for balancing accuracy and speed, by making the threshold adaptive over time rather than fixed. It also builds on PipeSpec's asynchronous pipelining for edge-cloud SD, but departs from it by coupling the pipeline with a credit-inertia-driven threshold controller that exploits temporal autocorrelation in token acceptance rates. The joint latency-energy optimization objective under QoS constraints positions the work within the broader edge-cloud collaborative inference literature, where prior methods (SEED, PICE, NEST) addressed scheduling or retrieval but not adaptive verification strictness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The credit inertia observation—that acceptance rates exhibit strong short-range temporal consistency—provides a simple, data-driven signal for when to relax or tighten verification, and the plug-in compatibility with Medusa, FSD, EAGLE-3, and PipeSpec suggests broad practical applicability without architectural redesign. However, the evidence for credit inertia is limited to a single figure on two datasets, and the gains over strong baselines (FR-Spec, LR) are modest (a few percentage points in speedup), leaving open whether the phenomenon generalizes across diverse task distributions, model pairs, and highly variable network conditions. A key open question is whether the learned threshold predictor degrades gracefully under distributional shift (e.g., novel domains or adversarial prompts) where historical acceptance patterns may no longer be predictive.
