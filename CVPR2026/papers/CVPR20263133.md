## Problem

Existing subject-driven and style-driven generation methods each perform feature disentanglement in isolation, treating the two tasks as disjoint problems. The paper argues that these tasks are inherently complementary—learning to include style features in one task should help the other task exclude them—and that no prior work exploits this cross-task complementarity to mutually enhance disentanglement quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263133/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

USO accepts up to two reference images (a style reference and a subject/content reference) plus a text prompt, and can operate in three modes: subject-only, style-only, or joint style-subject generation. During training, stochastic conditioning dropout (p=0.25) randomly removes one reference so the model learns both single-task and multi-task regimes. The style reference is encoded via SigLIP with a multi-scale Hierarchical Projector, while the content reference is encoded by a frozen VAE, and the two token streams are concatenated with distinct positional encodings (text-aligned indices for style, UnoPE diagonal layout for content).

## Method

USO introduces a cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm with two stages. First, a triplet curation pipeline uses a stylization expert (UNO-SFT fine-tuned on 200k curated stylized pairs) and a de-stylization expert (frozen FLUX.1 Kontext) to produce <style reference, de-stylized content reference, stylized target> triplets in both layout-preserved and layout-shifted variants, filtered by a VLM for style similarity and subject consistency. Second, the unified model is trained with (i) disentangled encoders that separate style (SigLIP + Hierarchical Projector) from content (VAE) conditioning, (ii) stochastic conditioning dropout to preserve single-task capability, and (iii) an Auxiliary Style Reward (ASR) that computes CSD or VLM-based style similarity between the style reference and the generated image as a reward, back-propagated alongside the standard flow-matching loss (λ=0 before step S, λ=1 thereafter) to prevent reward hacking. The key insight is that the style-only reward signal improves subject consistency as a side effect, validating the cross-task complementarity hypothesis.

## Results

On USO-Bench, USO achieves the highest subject consistency scores among all methods: CLIP-I 0.647 and DINO 0.804 for subject-driven generation, surpassing the next-best UNO (0.605 / 0.789) by 4.2% and 1.9% respectively. For style-driven generation, USO attains the best CSD score of 0.556 (vs. InstantStyle-XL's 0.540) and the best CLIP-T of 0.286. On the joint style-subject-driven task, USO leads with CSD 0.492 and CLIP-T 0.283, outperforming the closest competitor StyleID (0.407 / 0.230) by 18.4% and 23.0% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

USO outperforms all subject-driven baselines on identity metrics: it leads CLIP-I by 4.2 points over UNO (0.647 vs. 0.605) and DINO by 1.5 points (0.804 vs. 0.789), while FLUX.1 Kontext dev and DreamO trail by 6.8 and 5.9 points on CLIP-I respectively. However, USO's subject-driven CLIP-T (0.287) is slightly below RealCustom++ (0.303), Qwen-Image Edit (0.302), and OmniGen2 (0.302), indicating a minor trade-off in text alignment. For style-driven generation, USO achieves the best CSD (0.556) and CLIP-T (0.286), beating the strongest style-specific baseline InstantStyle-XL by 1.6 points on CSD and 1.0 on CLIP-T, and substantially outperforming DEADiff (0.462) and CSGO (0.452). On the joint style-subject-driven task, USO dominates both metrics: CSD 0.492 vs. StyleID's 0.407 (+18.4%) and CLIP-T 0.283 vs. OmniStyle's 0.229 (+23.0%), confirming that the cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm yields the largest gains precisely where prior task-specific methods are weakest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the in-context DiT paradigm established by UNO and FLUX.1 Kontext, which use shared attention between reference and generated images for subject-driven generation. It departs from prior work by arguing that subject-driven and style-driven generation, previously treated as isolated disentanglement problems (DEADiff, CSGO, StyleStudio for style; RealCustom, UNO for subject), are inherently complementary: learning to include a feature in one task teaches the model to exclude it in the other. The cross-task co-disentanglement paradigm—where a subject model curates style triplets and a style reward reinforces subject training—is the key conceptual departure from task-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

USO provides a practical unified model that handles subject-driven, style-driven, and joint style-subject-driven generation in a single architecture, reducing the need for task-specific pipelines. The introduction of USO-Bench fills a gap in evaluation by providing the first benchmark that tests cross-task customization jointly. Open questions include whether the co-disentanglement principle extends to other feature axes (layout, composition, material) and whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific DiT/FLUX backbone. The reliance on a frozen FLUX.1 Kontext for de-stylization in the data pipeline also raises questions about scalability and circularity in the training loop.
