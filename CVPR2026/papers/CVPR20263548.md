## Problem

Existing sim-to-real transfer methods treat each task adaptation as an isolated process, demanding repeated and costly tuning from scratch for every new task. This paper identifies the lack of a continual cross-task sim-to-real transfer paradigm that accumulates reusable knowledge across iterative transfers, and asks what specific knowledge is transferable across both the sim-to-real domain gap and across distinct manipulation tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263548/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes fused 3D point clouds from two RealSense depth cameras (D435 and D435i), transformed to the robot's base frame, cropped to a bounding box, and downsampled via Farthest Point Sampling, concatenated with the proprioceptive state. The frozen diffusion policy head outputs an 8-step action chunk. Notably, the perception residual module (Geo-MoE) operates on local k-NN point groups rather than the full point cloud, extracting per-group geometric descriptors (planarity, linearity, saliency) as routing cues before producing a single corrective residual vector that is concatenated with the frozen encoder output.

## Method

GeCo-SRT reframes sim-to-real transfer as continual learning from human correction trajectories collected via a shared-autonomy framework, with the base diffusion policy frozen and only a shared perception residual module updated across tasks. The Geo-MoE module extracts local geometric features (planarity, linearity, saliency via PCA on k-NN point groups) to dynamically route each group to specialized experts, enabling distinct geometric knowledge to be reused across tasks. The Geo-PER module shifts the prioritized replay metric from task-level loss to expert-level utilization: historical samples that strongly activated under-utilized experts receive higher sampling priority, ensuring idle experts are periodically refreshed and their specialized knowledge is not forgotten. Training uses a mixed replay buffer combining current-task human corrections (95%) with 10% replayed data from prior tasks, optimizing an MSE action loss plus a load-balancing auxiliary loss.

## Results

In the cross-task continual sim-to-real transfer setting (Pick Cube → Stack Cube → Pick Banana → Plug Insert), GeCo-SRT achieves 63.3% average success rate and 26.5% average N-NBT, outperforming the strongest baseline (Geo-MoE+PER at 55.8% SR) and the Transic+PER baseline (40.0% SR, 55.0% N-NBT). In single-task transfer, Geo-MoE reaches 50.0% average SR versus 38.3% for Transic and 3.1% for direct deployment. The method also demonstrates strong data efficiency, matching the from-scratch baseline's success rate with only 1/6 of the correction trajectories (20 vs. 60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Transic+PER (the primary external baseline), GeCo-SRT improves average success rate from 40.0% to 63.3% (+23.3 pp) and reduces average N-NBT from 55.0% to 26.5%, indicating substantially less catastrophic forgetting across the four sequential tasks. The gap is most pronounced on Pick Banana (60.0% vs. 20.0%) and Plug Insert (53.3% vs. 33.3%), suggesting the geometry-aware expert routing captures transferable knowledge that Transic's generic residual network misses. Compared to Geo-MoE+EWC (38.3% SR, 49.5% N-NBT), GeCo-SRT's Geo-PER strategy yields a 25 pp SR gain and a 23 pp N-NBT reduction, confirming that expert-utilization-based prioritization outperforms parameter-level regularization for protecting specialized geometric knowledge. Even the strongest internal ablation (Geo-MoE+PER, 55.8% SR) is surpassed by the full GeCo-SRT method, validating the complementary contribution of the Geo-PER module. In single-task transfer, Geo-MoE (50.0% avg SR) also outperforms Transic (38.3%) by 11.7 pp, establishing the architectural advantage independent of the continual learning component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Transic [16], which reframes sim-to-real transfer as learning from human correction trajectories via a shared-autonomy framework. It departs from the single-task paradigm of Transic, Domain Randomization, and System Identification by introducing a continual cross-task setting where geometric knowledge accumulates across sequential sim-to-real deployments. The Geo-MoE module repurposes the mixture-of-experts architecture (common in NLP) for point-cloud perception residuals, using local PCA-derived geometric features (planarity, linearity, saliency) as routing signals rather than learned embeddings. The Geo-PER strategy extends standard Prioritized Experience Replay by shifting the prioritization metric from task-level loss to expert-level utilization, a design choice specific to the MoE structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical pain point in robotic deployment: the repeated, costly re-tuning required for each new sim-to-real task. The demonstrated 52% average improvement and 1/6 data efficiency are encouraging, though absolute success rates remain modest (63.3% avg SR), and the method is limited to bridging the observation gap via geometry rather than dynamics. Open questions include whether geometric knowledge transfer generalizes to non-geometric sim-to-real gaps (e.g., complex contact dynamics), whether the approach scales beyond four tasks and a single arm, and whether multi-modal signals (semantic task relationships, tactile) could complement the geometric channel. The narrow experimental scope (single robot, 30 trials per task) limits generalizability claims.
