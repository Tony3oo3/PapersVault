## Problem

Camera recapture introduces compound, spatially coupled degradations (perspective warping, illumination shifts, Moiré interference, sensor noise) that remain difficult for deep watermarking systems. Existing approaches either rely on fixed, manually designed camera noise layers that cannot capture the full diversity of real optical distortions, or use pretrained self-supervised features whose robustness emerges passively rather than being explicitly optimized for watermarking. No prior method jointly learns realistic camera-like distortions and enforces cross-modal semantic invariance for zero-watermarking.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128×3 RGB image and a text anchor (region-level phrase or full-sentence caption) as input during training, and a k-bit binary watermark message (tested at 30 and 100 bits) during registration. The image is never modified; instead, a per-image reference signature (a k×d codebook matrix C and a lightweight projection head Ψ) is learned to bind the message to the 1024-D invariant feature. At extraction time, the frozen feature extractor processes a potentially camera-distorted image, and watermark bits are recovered by thresholding sigmoid outputs of dot products between the projected feature and the reference codes.

## Method

TIACam comprises three jointly trained modules: (1) a fully differentiable Auto-Augmentor with six learnable operators (perspective transform, photometric shift, additive noise, convolutional blur, smooth JPEG surrogate, and sinusoidal Moiré) that adversarially discovers the most challenging camera-like distortions; (2) a Text-Anchored Invariant Feature Learner built on a frozen CLIP image encoder plus a trainable 3-residual-block extractor projecting to 1024-D features, trained via a cross-modal min-max game where a 4-layer Transformer pair discriminator distinguishes matched from mismatched image-text pairs; and (3) a Zero-Watermarking Head that registers binary messages into the invariant feature space through a per-image codebook optimization. Training alternates three coordinated updates—discriminator, auto-augmentor, and feature extractor—in a GAN-style loop, with the augmenter maximizing feature disruption while the extractor minimizes it, and text anchors preventing degenerate feature collapse. The key insight is that embedding watermarks in a semantically invariant feature space (anchored by text) rather than pixel space yields robustness to arbitrary camera distortions without modifying the image.

## Results

On real-world camera-captured images, TIACam achieves 99.1% and 98.2% bit accuracy for 30-bit and 100-bit messages under screen-camera capture, 96.6% and 95.1% under print-camera capture, and 97.4% and 95.2% under screenshot distortions, consistently outperforming the strongest baseline StegaStamp by 3–5 percentage points. In feature invariance evaluation (Table 1), TIACam attains an average cosine similarity of 0.94 under compound distortions, compared to 0.77 for the best SSL baseline (VICReg/VIbCReg), a gap of roughly 17 percentage points. Linear-probe Top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100 under additive and JPEG noise exceeds the best baseline by over 7%, and Caltech-256 maintains above 80% Top-1 even under joint distortions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three watermarking baselines in Table 2, TIACam wins decisively across all six evaluation settings: it outperforms StegaStamp (the strongest prior method) by 5.3 pp on Screen Camera 30-bit, 4.4 pp on Print Camera 30-bit, and 3.7 pp on Screenshot 30-bit, with similar margins at 100 bits. PIMoG and HiDDeN trail by 14–29 pp, confirming that pixel-embedding approaches degrade substantially under compound camera degradations. In the feature-invariance comparison (Table 1), TIACam surpasses all five SSL baselines—SimCLR, BYOL, Barlow Twins, VICReg, and VIbCReg—by 13–27 pp in cosine similarity under compound distortions (0.94 vs. 0.71–0.77), with the largest gap over BYOL (0.71) and SimCLR (0.74). The ablation (Table 3) further shows that the invariance gain comes from the TIACam feature extractor rather than the CLIP backbone alone, improving cosine similarity by 13–15 pp over frozen CLIP across Visual Genome, Flickr, MSCOCO, and ImageNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIACam sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) deep embedding-based watermarking with handcrafted camera noise layers (HiDDeN, StegaStamp, PIMoG), (2) zero-watermarking via pretrained feature extractors (CLIP, DINO-based schemes), and (3) text-guided or distortion-adversarial invariant feature learning (Ahtesham & Zhong 2025, InvZW). The key departure is replacing fixed, manually designed distortion pipelines with a fully differentiable, adversarially trained auto-augmentor, and unifying text-anchored semantic alignment with distortion robustness in a single three-way optimization loop rather than treating them as separate objectives. The zero-watermarking head follows the per-image reference-code registration paradigm of InvZW but operates on the newly learned invariant space rather than a frozen pretrained one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Camera recapture remains one of the hardest real-world attack scenarios for watermarking, and the near-perfect bit accuracy (99% screen, 96.6% print) without any pixel modification or region-detection step is practically significant for copyright and provenance verification in the wild. The auto-augmentation idea—letting the system discover its own worst-case distortions rather than relying on handcrafted noise models—could generalize to other robustness-critical representation-learning tasks beyond watermarking. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer messages or higher-resolution images, whether text anchors are strictly necessary (the distinctiveness test suggests they help but the model retains some visual individuality), and how the method performs under adversarial attacks specifically designed to break the invariant feature space.
