## Problem

Monocular aerial 4D reconstruction is inherently ill-posed: a single UAV camera at 50–100 m altitude provides narrow baselines, wide depth variation, and dynamic objects that occupy only a few pixels with large motion disparity. Existing dynamic Gaussian splatting methods rely on multi-view or LiDAR supervision, and feed-forward models trained on ground-level data fail to generalize to the small-footprint, high-altitude regime, leaving consistent temporal motion recovery from a single aerial sequence unsolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular UAV video (2K–4K, 15 fps, 50–100 m altitude, ~2,000–18,000 m² coverage) with no external pose or geometry priors. It internally estimates camera intrinsics and SE(3) poses via zero-shot depth (UniDepth), segmentation (SAM 2 / Grounding DINO), and point tracking (CoTracker3) with local bundle adjustment. The output is a unified 4D Gaussian scene (static background + per-object dynamic subsets with 6-DoF trajectories) that supports real-time differentiable-rasterization novel-view rendering at full resolution.

## Method

AeroDGS couples two modules. The Monocular Geometry Lifting module back-projects refined monocular depth into a dense point map, groups pixels into object-level point sets via 2D instance masks, fits oriented bounding boxes by PCA, and predicts object height with a small MLP to resolve the single-view depth ambiguity. The scene is then represented as a union of static Gaussians and per-object dynamic Gaussians in canonical space, whose appearance is a shared continuous field (hash-grid spatial encoding + spherical-harmonic view dependence + sinusoidal temporal embedding) rather than per-Gaussian SH coefficients, and whose motion follows SE(3) twist trajectories with spline interpolation. The key novelty is the Physics-Guided Optimization module, which adds three differentiable regularizers—ground-support (signed distance to a locally inferred ground plane along the camera ray), upright-stability (alignment of the object's vertical axis with the ground normal or gravity), and second-order trajectory smoothness—that reduce the under-determined pose degrees of freedom of small dynamic objects, turning ambiguous monocular cues into physically consistent 4D motion without any multi-view or LiDAR supervision.

## Results

On the real-world Aero4D dataset, AeroDGS achieves 32.71 / 37.91 / 34.84 PSNR and 17.65 / 22.47 / 21.75 Dyn-PSNR across the three scenes (Intersection-Night, Downtown-High, Intersection-Day), outperforming the strongest baseline 4DGF by 2.2–4.5 dB on Dyn-PSNR. On the synthetic UAV3D benchmark, AeroDGS reaches 33.61 PSNR and 15.60 Dyn-PSNR, surpassing 4DGF (31.78 / 10.63) by 1.8 dB overall and 5.0 dB on dynamic regions. The method also leads on SSIM (up to 0.974) and LPIPS (as low as 0.013) across all scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeroDGS outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. The strongest competitor is 4DGF [13], which leads on static PSNR in some settings but trails AeroDGS by 2.2–4.5 dB on Dyn-PSNR (e.g., 22.47 vs. 17.94 on Downtown-High), confirming that physics-guided constraints are most impactful for dynamic objects. DeGauss [41] achieves competitive static quality (27.90–28.31 PSNR) but does not report Dyn-PSNR for two of three Aero4D scenes and falls 2.4–6.5 dB behind AeroDGS where both report dynamic metrics. CoDa-4DGS [35] and BézierGS [23] are substantially weaker, with CoDa-4DGS dropping to 15.37 PSNR on Downtown-High and BézierGS to 19.51, reflecting their reliance on multi-view or LiDAR priors that are unavailable in monocular aerial settings. 4DGS [45], the original dynamic Gaussian splatting method, is the weakest baseline overall (19.33–23.06 PSNR), consistent with its design for small-scale indoor scenes rather than large-scale aerial environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

AeroDGS builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation and its dynamic extension (4DGS), adopting explicit Gaussian primitives with SE(3) trajectory encoding for moving objects. It departs from prior dynamic 4D reconstruction methods (4DGF, BezierGS, CoDa-4DGS, DeGauss) that rely on multi-view inputs, LiDAR supervision, or ground-level training data, by targeting the fundamentally harder monocular aerial setting where depth ambiguity is severe and dynamic objects occupy minimal pixel area. The key conceptual departure is the Physics-Guided Optimization module, which encodes ground-support, upright-stability, and trajectory-smoothness as differentiable constraints—a regularization paradigm not previously applied to Gaussian-based 4D reconstruction. The Monocular Geometry Lifting module composes off-the-shelf foundation models (UniDepth, SAM 2, CoTracker3, GroundingDINO) with bundle adjustment to bootstrap geometry, rather than learning end-to-end scene priors as feed-forward methods (VGGT, DUSt3R, Uni4D) do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuinely underexplored intersection—monocular aerial 4D reconstruction—where existing methods fail due to narrow baselines, small dynamic footprints, and wide depth ranges. The Aero4D dataset, while small (three real scenes), fills a notable gap as the first real-world benchmark for dynamic aerial 4D reconstruction with temporally continuous viewpoints. The physics-guided regularization offers a transferable idea for any monocular setting where physical constraints can reduce pose ambiguity. Open questions include scalability to city-scale scenes, handling of non-rigid or partially visible objects (pedestrians are explicitly excluded), and whether the 3 m dynamic-static threshold generalizes across altitudes and object types.
