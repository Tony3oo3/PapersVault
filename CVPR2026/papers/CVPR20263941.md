## Problem

Existing multimodal cancer survival prediction methods integrate genomic data and histopathology images while overlooking the proteome, which serves as the critical intermediate layer bridging genomic alterations and histopathological features. Furthermore, these methods fuse heterogeneous data sources in a flat manner that fails to capture the inherent biological hierarchy of the gene → protein → phenotype cascade, resulting in suboptimal integration that misses mechanistic pathways linking molecular aberrations to morphological outcomes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263941/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities per patient: whole slide images partitioned into non-overlapping 20× magnification patches (encoded by the CONCH image encoder), gene expression profiles of the top 2,000 highly variable genes (RSEM-normalized, log2(x+1)-transformed), and protein expression profiles from RPPA measurements (unit-normalized). A distinctive design choice is that protein identity embeddings are generated by prompting GPT-5 to produce functional and morphological-correlation descriptions, which are then encoded by the CONCH text encoder to naturally align with the histopathology image space. The output is a patient-level hazard function and survival function, trained with a negative log-likelihood loss and evaluated by the concordance index (C-index) via 5-fold cross-validation.

## Method

HFGPI models the biological hierarchy through three progressive stages. First, a Molecular Tokenizer encodes genes and proteins by element-wise multiplying expression vectors with identity embeddings (Gene2Vec for genes; LLM-generated functional descriptions encoded by CONCH for proteins), capturing both molecular identity and patient-specific activity. Second, Gene-Regulated Protein Fusion (GRPF) refines embeddings via GCNs on k-NN functional graphs, then applies cross-attention where proteins query genes (reflecting directional transcriptional regulation), regularized by a structure-preserving alignment loss that enforces the learned attention matrix to respect both gene and protein network topologies. Third, Protein-Guided Hypergraph Learning (PGHL) constructs a hypergraph where each protein defines a hyperedge connecting its top-k semantically related image patches, and applies hypergraph convolution to capture higher-order many-to-many protein-morphology relationships. The hierarchically fused features are aggregated by a Transformer encoder and gated attention pooling for survival prediction, trained end-to-end with a combined NLL survival loss and structure-preserving alignment loss.

## Results

HFGPI achieves a mean C-index of 0.753 across five TCGA cancer cohorts (BLCA, BRCA, GBMLGG, LUAD, UCEC), outperforming the best unimodal histopathology method (WiKG, 0.686) by 6.7% and the best two-modality gene+image method (MoME, 0.718) by 3.5%. Against the strongest three-modality baselines, HFGPI surpasses PS3† (0.735) by 1.8% and ICFNet† (0.729) by 2.4% on average C-index. The method achieves the highest C-index on every individual dataset, with particularly large gains on GBMLGG (0.873) and UCEC (0.782).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HFGPI achieves the highest C-index on all five TCGA cohorts, winning against every baseline. Against unimodal histopathology methods, it outperforms the best (WiKG, 0.686) by 6.7% on average, and against gene-expression-only (0.677) and protein-expression-only (0.647) SNN baselines by 11.2% and 16.4% respectively. Among two-modality gene+image methods, HFGPI surpasses the strongest competitor MoME (0.718) by 3.5%, with MCAT and CMTA (both 0.712) trailing by 5.6%. Against the only other three-modality methods tested, HFGPI beats PS3† (0.735) by 1.8% and ICFNet† (0.729) by 2.4%, with the largest per-dataset margin on UCEC (+2.5% over PS3†) and GBMLGG (+2.2% over PS3†). The consistent advantage across all datasets and all baseline categories supports the paper's claim that hierarchical gene→protein→histology fusion captures biological dependencies missed by flat multimodal integration.

## Relation to Prior Work

HFGPI extends the multimodal survival prediction line (MCAT, MOTCat, CMTA, SurvPath, PIBD, MoME) by inserting the proteome as a biologically motivated intermediate layer between genomics and histopathology, rather than treating all modalities as peers in a flat fusion. It also departs from prior three-modality approaches (PS3, ICFNet) that integrate pathology reports or text, instead grounding the third modality in quantitative RPPA proteomics. The Molecular Tokenizer (identity embeddings × expression) and the structure-preserving alignment loss draw on graph matching and molecular embedding literature, while the hypergraph convolution for protein–patch association repurposes hypergraph neural network machinery in a new biological context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean systems-biology framing—genes encode, proteins execute, morphology manifests—that could guide future multimodal cancer models beyond the current flat-attention paradigm. However, practical impact is constrained by RPPA's limited protein coverage (~200 proteins), reliance on GPT-5-generated descriptions for protein identity embeddings, and evaluation on only five TCGA cohorts. Open questions include whether the hierarchical cascade generalizes to single-cell proteomics or spatial omics, whether the LLM-description bottleneck limits scalability to larger proteomes, and whether the modest 1.8–2.4% gains over flat three-modality baselines persist on larger or more heterogeneous cohorts.
