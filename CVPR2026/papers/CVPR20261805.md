## Problem

Current 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) optimize exclusively for photometric fidelity, producing assets that look visually correct but behave unphysically under interaction—exhibiting excessive velocity divergence, failing on function-critical surfaces (e.g., aerodynamic fronts), and missing fine-scale geometric details essential for stable fluid-structure coupling. The paper asks how reconstruction can become aware of real-world physical interactions and object functionality beyond visual cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261805/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with calibrated poses within a fixed 10-view budget (2 biased initial views + 8 actively selected), plus a pool of K candidate camera poses proposed by a vision-based NBV planner (ActiveNeRF or FisherRF). The 3DGS geometry is voxelized into rigid particles via NVIDIA Kaolin for simulation. Output is the re-ranked next-best-view pose (selected by highest physics-informed divergence), the updated 3DGS reconstruction, and per-Gaussian divergence scores that can be rendered into view-space uncertainty maps. Simulation uses water at room temperature (ρ=1000 kg/m³, μ=10 kg/(m·s)) over 750 steps at Δt=0.002 s.

## Method

FluidGaussian is a plug-and-play re-ranking module that couples 3DGS reconstruction with Divergence-Free SPH (DFSPH) fluid simulation to produce a physics-informed uncertainty metric for NBV selection. Given top-K candidate poses from a vision-based planner, it runs five short DFSPH rollouts (fluid splashed from four horizontal directions and top-down) treating the current 3DGS as a solid boundary via on-the-fly voxelization. Per-particle velocity divergence is computed via SPH-weighted neighbor sums (Eq. 1), aggregated to per-Gaussian scores by averaging over particles within 3× the Gaussian's principal radius, and the worst-case (max) across the five initial conditions is retained. These per-Gaussian scores are rendered into a scalar view score via visibility-weighted accumulation (Eq. 5), and the candidate with the highest score—indicating the worst simulation quality and greatest physical uncertainty—is selected as the next view. The key design choice is using divergence as a view-dependent uncertainty rather than a differentiable loss, since backpropagating through fluid simulators is prohibitively expensive; the approach is metric-agnostic and could substitute vorticity or other flow diagnostics.

## Results

On DrivAerNet++, FluidGaussian achieves the largest visual gains: +1.49 dB PSNR over ActiveNeRF (17.38→18.87) and +0.96 dB over FisherRF (20.11→21.07), corresponding to up to +8.6% relative PSNR improvement. On Blender, FisherRF+FluidGaussian reaches 24.74 dB PSNR vs. 23.42 dB for FisherRF alone. For simulation fidelity, adding FluidGaussian reduces mean velocity divergence by up to 62.3% (Mip-NeRF 360, ActiveNeRF: 0.0130→0.0049), and on function-critical aerodynamic surfaces of DrivAerNet++, PSNR improves by +7.7% (ActiveNeRF: 15.68→16.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two uncertainty-driven NBV baselines: ActiveNeRF [46] and FisherRF [28]. Against ActiveNeRF, FluidGaussian wins on Blender (+0.61 dB PSNR, −0.017 LPIPS) and DrivAerNet++ (+1.49 dB PSNR, −0.033 LPIPS) but slightly loses on Mip-NeRF 360 (−0.45 dB PSNR, +0.003 LPIPS), likely due to the unbounded scene geometry. Against FisherRF, FluidGaussian wins on all three datasets: Blender (+1.32 dB PSNR), Mip-NeRF 360 (+0.23 dB PSNR), and DrivAerNet++ (+0.96 dB PSNR). On the physics metric (mean velocity divergence), FluidGaussian consistently reduces divergence across all datasets for both baselines, with the largest relative gain (−62.3%) on Mip-NeRF 360 with ActiveNeRF; on DrivAerNet++ with FisherRF the divergence is nearly identical (0.0609 vs. 0.0608), suggesting FisherRF already captures much of the aerodynamic structure. On function-critical regions (Table 4), FluidGaussian improves PSNR by +7.7% (ActiveNeRF) and +1.3% (FisherRF) while reducing view divergence by 27% and 15% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper plugs a physics-informed uncertainty signal into existing active 3D reconstruction pipelines (ActiveNeRF, FisherRF), which previously relied solely on photometric or Fisher-information criteria to select next-best-views. The geometry representation is 3D Gaussian Splatting, and the physics probe is Divergence-Free SPH, borrowed from the computer-graphics fluid simulation literature rather than from differentiable-physics or learned-simulator work. Conceptually, the paper departs from the appearance-centric framing of active reconstruction by treating fluid-structure interaction as a 'probe' that exposes geometric defects invisible to pixel-space losses, and it connects to the broader digital-twin and functional-intelligence literature where simulation-ready geometry is the actual goal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a cheap, non-differentiable fluid rollout can re-rank view candidates and simultaneously improve both visual and physical fidelity—is practically useful for any pipeline where reconstructed assets must behave correctly in simulation (aerospace, automotive, digital twins). However, the evidence is mixed: on Mip-NeRF360 the method degrades PSNR for ActiveNeRF, and the largest divergence gains (−62.3%) appear only at high Reynolds numbers on specific datasets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to compressible or multiphase flows, whether it scales beyond the 10-view budget, and whether the voxelized rigid-body approximation of Gaussians introduces systematic bias for thin or highly curved geometries.
