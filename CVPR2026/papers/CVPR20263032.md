## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models can solve basic geometry problems but fail at formal geometric reasoning that requires dynamically identifying, constructing, and verifying auxiliary geometric elements (e.g., auxiliary lines, intersection points). No prior benchmark or model treats auxiliary construction as a first-class, formally verifiable component of the solution, leaving a gap between semantic correctness and rigorous, machine-checkable geometric proofs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263032/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a pair consisting of a natural-language problem description and a rendered geometry diagram (originally authored in TikZ and compiled to an image), with a maximum input length of 4096 tokens. It produces a structured tuple of reasoning steps, Lean4 code encoding any required auxiliary constructions (empty when none are needed), and a final answer, all wrapped in a strict think-answer format that downstream parsers can unambiguously extract. For proof-based questions the Lean4 code must formally specify every auxiliary line or point so that the construction is independently verifiable.

## Method

Geoint-R1 is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and trained in two stages. Stage 1 is supervised fine-tuning with standard negative log-likelihood on expert-annotated (reasoning, Lean4 construction, answer) triples, freezing the vision encoder and projector to preserve visual capacity. Stage 2 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning guided by a three-component verification reward: a correctness score (exact match for answer questions, GPT-4o-judged for proofs) modulated up or down by whether the auxiliary construction is correct, a binary exact-match reward for the Lean4 auxiliary code, and a binary format-compliance reward. A curriculum learning schedule orders training problems by ascending difficulty (derived from reject-sampling pass rates over 8 candidates), so the model encounters simpler proof patterns before complex multi-step auxiliary constructions. The key architectural insight is that auxiliary construction is not a post-hoc annotation but a jointly optimized output channel whose formal Lean4 encoding enables both reward computation and downstream verification.

## Results

On the Geoint benchmark (1,885 geometry problems), Geoint-R1 (7B) achieves the highest overall average accuracy of 64.72%, surpassing all open-source and closed-source baselines. On answer-based questions, Geoint-R1 leads with 57.01%, outperforming Gemini-1.5-pro (51.53%) and GPT-4o (42.68%). On proof-based questions, GPT-4o achieves the highest score (77.99%), with Geoint-R1 at 72.43% and Gemini-1.5-pro at 73.50%. On the most challenging auxiliary-line subset, Geoint-R1 reaches 68.63% (answer) and 68.40% (proof), substantially outperforming all open-source models which remain below 40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geoint-R1 (7B) achieves the best average accuracy (64.72%) across all 12 compared models, beating the strongest proprietary baselines Gemini-1.5-pro (62.52%) and GPT-4o (60.34%) by 2.2 and 4.4 points respectively. On answer-based questions, Geoint-R1 (57.01%) clearly outperforms Gemini-1.5-pro (51.53%), MMR1-Math-v0-7B (49.08%), and GPT-4o (42.68%), with a particularly large margin over open-source models like InternVL3-8B (42.68%) and Qwen-VL-2.5-7B (39.94%). On proof-based questions, Geoint-R1 (72.43%) trails GPT-4o (77.99%) by 5.6 points and Gemini-1.5-pro (73.50%) by 1.1 points, but still surpasses MMR1-Math-v0-7B (71.93%) and InternVL3-8B (71.92%). The advantage is most pronounced on auxiliary-line problems, where Geoint-R1 (68.63% answer, 68.40% proof) outperforms all open-source models by over 25 points and remains competitive with GPT-4o (74.81% proof) despite using far fewer parameters. Math-specific models like Math-LLaVA-13B (22.35% avg) and general-purpose small models like Phi-3.5-V-4B (10.83% avg) perform poorly, indicating that geometric reasoning with formal auxiliary construction remains a distinct challenge beyond general math or vision capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from existing geometric reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, We-Math, MathVerse, SolidGeo) by introducing a formal verification requirement: solutions must include explicitly constructed auxiliary lines encoded in Lean4, a dimension absent from prior geometry evaluation. Methodologically, it repurposes the SFT + GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm popularized by DeepSeekMath and DeepSeek-R1, but applies it to a novel verification reward model that jointly scores answer correctness, auxiliary construction accuracy, and output format. The visual reasoning literature (MM-REACT, VisuoThink, VGR) informs the multimodal setup, but those frameworks target general visual manipulation rather than precise geometric construction with formal proof obligations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Geoint benchmark (1,885 problems with Lean4-annotated auxiliary constructions) fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset requires models to both identify and formally encode auxiliary lines, making it a useful stress-test for whether MLLMs can perform the constructive step that human geometers take for granted. The ablation showing a 36-point collapse on auxiliary-line answer problems when the verification reward is removed provides concrete evidence that reward design, not just model scale, drives performance on constructive geometry. Open questions include whether Lean4-based formal verification can be fully automated (currently relying on GPT-4o for proof scoring), whether the approach generalizes beyond the 7B Qwen2.5-VL backbone, and whether the error patterns identified (theorem misapplication, symbolic confusion) can be addressed with targeted training data.
