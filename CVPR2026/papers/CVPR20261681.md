## Problem

Existing scene relighting methods either operate on single images without 3D consistency across views, or require controlled multi-lighting captures and offer only global relighting without per-light control. No prior work addresses decomposing complex indoor illumination from a single casual multi-view capture into individually controllable near-field light sources while maintaining view-consistent results.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single multi-view RGB capture (typically 32–96 images) plus per-light spatial masks (emissive area, full fixture, or convex hull) as input, and produces per-light one-light-at-a-time (OLAT) images, an ambient lighting image, and a relightable 3D Gaussian splatting scene. The OLAT images are determined up to scale, requiring learned RGB scale factors to recombine into the original input. The final 3D representation supports real-time interactive adjustment of each light's intensity, color, and on/off state from arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

LuxRemix operates in three stages. First, a LoRA-fine-tuned FLUX DiT model performs single-image lighting decomposition by conditioning on patchified light mask tokens added channel-wise to input latents, using text prompts to isolate individual lights (OLAT) or remove one light (ambient). Second, a full-parameter fine-tuned multi-view diffusion U-Net (following the SEVA/SimVS paradigm) propagates sparse OLAT decompositions across all views using Plücker ray embeddings and reference view masks, with three exposure-bracketed passes merged via Debevec–Malik HDR reconstruction. Third, a two-stage 3D Gaussian splatting optimization first establishes geometry from the original images, then freezes geometry and trains per-Gaussian, per-light HDR RGB coefficients in linear space with differentiable tone mapping, enabling real-time linear combination of light contributions at render time.

## Results

On 30 synthetic test scenes, LuxRemix-SV achieves PSNR 27.68, SSIM 0.898, and LPIPS 0.082 for single-image lighting decomposition, substantially outperforming both baselines. For multi-view lighting harmonization, LuxRemix-MV reaches PSNR 30.76, SSIM 0.867, and LPIPS 0.091, demonstrating that multi-view geometric constraints significantly improve consistency over per-view processing (PSNR 25.14). The method also produces real-time relightable 3D Gaussian splatting representations enabling interactive per-light control from arbitrary viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LuxRemix-SV decisively outperforms both external baselines on all three metrics. Against ScribbleLight [20], a scribble-guided diffusion approach, LuxRemix-SV gains approximately 13.3 dB in PSNR (27.68 vs. 14.39) and nearly doubles SSIM (0.898 vs. 0.395), indicating ScribbleLight fails to produce plausible per-light decompositions. Against Qwen-Image [81], a general-purpose image editing foundation model, LuxRemix-SV improves PSNR by 9.5 dB (27.68 vs. 18.23) and reduces LPIPS by a factor of nearly 3 (0.082 vs. 0.237), showing that a general editing model lacks the precise per-light control that task-specific fine-tuning provides. The paper also qualitatively compares against LightLab [58] in Figure 8, noting that LightLab occasionally struggles with reflections while LuxRemix-SV produces more convincing results throughout. For multi-view harmonization (Table 2), LuxRemix-MV (PSNR 30.76) substantially outperforms both the single-view baseline LuxRemix-SV (PSNR 25.14) and the mask-guided editing variant LuxRemix-MV-Edit (PSNR 26.37), confirming that propagating decompositions from sparse reference views with geometric constraints is the most effective strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LuxRemix bridges three previously separate research threads: single-image light editing via diffusion models (LightLab [58]), multi-view harmonization for geometric consistency (SimVS [78], SEVA [97]), and real-time relightable 3D Gaussian splatting [42]. The key departure from prior work is that it performs per-light OLAT decomposition from a single casual multi-view capture rather than requiring controlled multi-illumination photography, which most existing 3DGS relighting methods demand. The single-image decomposition stage fine-tunes a FLUX DiT with LoRA conditioned on light masks, while the multi-view propagation stage adapts the SimVS/SEVA concatenation-and-fine-tuning recipe to the lighting decomposition setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to enable interactive, per-light control over indoor scenes from a single casual multi-view capture, removing the need for controlled OLAT photography that has been a barrier to practical relighting workflows. The released 12,000+ scene synthetic dataset with ground-truth per-light decompositions provides a useful training resource for the community. However, the approach is limited to static indoor scenes trained on synthetic data, shows a bias toward light-cone geometries over diffuse lighting, and does not support distant HDRi-based global illumination editing—leaving outdoor and dynamic scenes as open problems.
