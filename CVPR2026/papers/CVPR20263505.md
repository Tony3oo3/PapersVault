## Problem

Language-based 3D scene understanding models like SceneScript rely on autoregressive next-token prediction, generating one token per decoder pass, which causes high latency especially as scene descriptions grow long. While multi-token prediction (MTP) can reduce decoder iterations, it introduces unreliable token predictions that significantly degrade accuracy and adds substantial parameter overhead from extra prediction heads. The paper addresses how to achieve both fast inference and accurate structured-language generation for 3D scene understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263505/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud as input, encoded by a sparse 3D ResNet into 3D features. Output is a sequence of discrete structured-language tokens following a predefined schema: categorical tokens (e.g., 'makewall', 'stop') and numerical tokens (coordinates, heights, thicknesses) that collectively describe 3D scene layouts (walls, windows, doors) or 3D object bounding boxes. The structured language is deterministic and weakly coupled, making it more predictable than natural language and amenable to parallel token generation.

## Method

Fast SceneScript employs multi-token prediction with n shared token heads to generate n future tokens per decoder pass, reducing inference from N to ⌈N/n⌉ forward passes. To counter accuracy loss from unreliable MTP outputs, two filtering strategies are introduced: Self-Speculative Decoding (SSD) verifies drafted tokens in a second pass using a distance metric (|t − t̃| ≤ τ) for numerical tokens rather than exact matching, and Confidence-Guided Decoding (CGD) trains a confidence head to predict per-token reliability on the fly, accepting the longest reliable prefix in a single iteration. A parameter-efficient mechanism shares all n token heads and uses a lightweight two-FFN projection block to produce distinct hidden states for additional heads, reducing MTP parameter overhead by ~43% while maintaining accuracy.

## Results

On ASE layout estimation, Fast SceneScript (SSD, n=10) achieves a mean F1-Score of 0.912 (test) with 15.05M parameters and 75 ms latency, a 5.09× speedup over SceneScript (0.915 F1, 382 ms) while accepting ~9 tokens per decoder step. On Structured3D, the same configuration reaches 0.790 mean F1 (vs. 0.774 for SceneScript), a 2.07% accuracy gain alongside a 5.57× speedup. For 3D object detection on ASE, Fast SceneScript (CGD) attains F1@0.50 of 0.832 (vs. 0.823 for SceneScript) at 108 ms, a 5.14× speedup. On the real-world SceneCAD dataset, Fast SceneScript (SSD) matches SceneScript's layout F1 (0.556) at 2.57× lower latency and improves object detection F1@0.50 from 0.461 to 0.544.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SceneScript [2] (the primary autoregressive baseline), Fast SceneScript achieves near-identical accuracy on ASE (0.912–0.913 mean F1 vs. 0.915) while running 5.09× faster, and actually surpasses it on Structured3D by 2.07% mean F1 (0.790 vs. 0.774) at 5.57× lower latency. The naive SceneScript + MTP baseline suffers severe accuracy degradation (e.g., 0.814 mean F1 with 10 heads on ASE, an 11% drop) and requires 69–89% more parameters; Fast SceneScript recovers accuracy to within 0.3% of SceneScript while using only ~7.5% more parameters. Against the specialized layout estimator RoomFormer [45] on Structured3D, Fast SceneScript (SSD, n=10) achieves a substantially higher mean F1 (0.790 vs. 0.702) with fewer decoder parameters (15.05M vs. 17.17M), though at higher latency (211 ms vs. 54 ms); however, RoomFormer is restricted to layout estimation only, whereas Fast SceneScript generalizes to object detection and other 3D perception tasks within the same architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SceneScript, an autoregressive structured language model for 3D scene understanding, by transplanting multi-token prediction (MTP) from the LLM inference-acceleration literature (Gloeckle et al., ICML 2024) into the perception domain. It further adapts self-speculative decoding (Stern et al., NeurIPS 2018; Zhang et al., ACL 2024) with a distance metric for numerical tokens and introduces a novel confidence-guided decoding (CGD) strategy that performs on-the-fly reliability scoring. The key conceptual departure is the observation that structured perception languages are more deterministic and weakly coupled than natural language, making MTP substantially more viable here than in general-purpose LLMs, and the design of a parameter-efficient head-sharing mechanism that avoids the large parameter overhead of naive multi-head MTP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A ~5× decoding speedup for 3D scene layout estimation and object detection while preserving (or slightly improving) accuracy is practically significant for real-time applications such as AR/VR scene understanding and robotics. The finding that MTP acts as a beneficial regularizer—yielding higher F1-Scores than the NTP baseline on Structured3D—suggests that multi-head prediction may improve generalization in structured generation tasks beyond mere speed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex or open-vocabulary 3D tasks, whether the structured-language determinism assumption holds for outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether the confidence-guided decoding strategy transfers to other autoregressive perception settings.
