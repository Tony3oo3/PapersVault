## Problem

LVLMs are highly vulnerable to visual corruptions, but prior explanations treat corruption as a monolithic form of visual noise, attributing failures uniformly to insufficient grounding or language-prior overreliance. This paper identifies that heterogeneous corruptions actually perturb perception along two distinct perceptual axes—shape and texture—each inducing a complementary failure mode that has not been explicitly modeled or corrected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262352/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a corrupted RGB image paired with a text prompt (e.g., a fixed 10-class identification list for ImageNet10-C or a VQA question for MMBench-C/RWIC-VQA). It produces a corrected logit vector over candidate classes (or a text answer for open-ended VQA) by fusing the original model output with two contrastive correction signals. No retraining or fine-tuning is required; the only additional computation is generating two contrastive views (Canny edge extraction and jigsaw patch permutation) and evaluating the LVLM on them.

## Method

ST-CD constructs two semantically grounded contrastive views: a Canny edge-extracted image that preserves global contours while suppressing texture (shape path), and a jigsaw-permuted image that retains local texture statistics while destroying spatial structure (texture path). Each path computes a contrastive logit difference (L_o − L_s and L_o − L_t) that amplifies the specific failure mode induced by shape or texture degradation. These corrections are fused via an entropy-based adaptive weighting scheme where the ratio E_o/E_s (or E_o/E_t) modulates correction strength: high original entropy (uncertainty) amplifies correction, while high contrastive entropy (unreliable signal) attenuates it. This bidirectional confidence modulation allows ST-CD to selectively amplify the relevant correction path without requiring knowledge of the corruption type, achieving corruption-agnostic robustness with only 1.5× inference overhead over standard VCD.

## Results

On ImageNet10-C, ST-CD achieves average accuracies of 75.8% (LLaVA-1.5), 74.0% (mPLUG-Owl2), and 69.9% (Qwen-VL), improving over the respective baselines by 10.0%, 6.5%, and 8.4%. On the real-world RWIC-VQA benchmark, ST-CD reaches 75.8%, 74.0%, and 69.9% average accuracy, outperforming all VCD-based baselines. On MMBench-C and POPE-MSCOCO-C, ST-CD attains the highest average scores (e.g., 64.0% and 81.7% for LLaVA-1.5), consistently ranking first or second across all four benchmarks and three architectures with only 1.5× inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-CD consistently outperforms all four VCD-based baselines (VCD, ICD, LCD, VACoDe) across every benchmark and architecture. On ImageNet10-C, ST-CD surpasses VCD by 3.2%, 3.1%, and 4.7% on LLaVA-1.5, mPLUG-Owl2, and Qwen-VL respectively, and exceeds VACoDe (the strongest baseline) by 1.7%, 2.0%, and 4.1%. On the real-world RWIC-VQA benchmark, the gap over VACoDe widens to 1.1%, 2.8%, and 6.0% for the three models. On POPE-MSCOCO-C, ST-CD leads VCD by 1.7%, 1.3%, and 2.4% respectively. Notably, ST-CD achieves these gains with only 1.5× inference cost over standard VCD, whereas VACoDe requires 4.2×, making ST-CD both more accurate and more efficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the shape-texture representation literature (Geirhos et al., Shi et al., Li et al.), which established that shape and texture cues contribute differently to robustness but was studied almost exclusively at training time, and (2) visual contrastive decoding (VCD, ICD, LCD, VACoDe), a family of training-free inference-time corrections that treat visual perturbations as a monolithic source of bias. ST-CD departs from both by organizing corruption effects along a shape-texture perceptual axis and designing targeted contrastive probes (Canny edge extraction for shape, jigsaw permutation for texture) that explicitly disentangle the two failure modes, rather than relying on generic augmentations or semantic-level debiasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shape-texture decomposition provides a principled, corruption-agnostic lens for understanding *why* LVLMs fail under different degradations, and the resulting dual-path decoding is training-free and plug-and-play across architectures. However, the evaluation is limited to eight synthetic corruption types and classification-style QA; whether the shape-texture axis remains a useful decomposition under more complex, mixed, or semantically structured corruptions (e.g., occlusion, domain shift) remains open. The learned-weighting variant, while stronger, requires corrupted validation data, undermining the zero-shot appeal. Broader questions include whether this perceptual decomposition generalizes to video, 3D, or multi-image reasoning settings.
