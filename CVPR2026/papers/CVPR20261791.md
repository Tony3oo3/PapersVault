## Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement learning for image generation suffers from mode collapse in later training stages, where the model converges to a narrow set of high-reward visual modes and produces homogenized outputs. The paper identifies two root causes: single-sample reward signals that ignore distributional diversity, and uniform KL regularization that misallocates the diversity budget across denoising steps—being weakest exactly when early-stage structure formation is most diversity-critical.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and generates a group of G images (e.g., 24) from a flow-matching backbone (SD3.5-M or Flux.1-dev) via SDE sampling. The group-level reward is computed by first scoring each image with a quality reward model (PickScore or HPSv3), then augmenting it with a distributional exploration bonus derived from spectral clustering over DreamSim perceptual distances within the group. Training uses LoRA (rank 32) with 6–10 denoising steps; evaluation uses 28–40 steps with classifier-free guidance.

## Method

DiverseGRPO introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, a distributional creativity bonus: for each prompt group, pairwise DreamSim distances are computed, spectral clustering partitions images into semantic visual modes, and an exploration reward E_i = √(N/n_k) is assigned inversely to cluster size, so rare visual modes receive higher reward; the final reward is R_i = Q_i + β·E_i. Second, a structure-aware regularization that replaces the uniform KL penalty with a Wasserstein distance constraint applied only to the first K denoising steps (where σ² is large and diversity is most fragile), while removing regularization entirely in later steps to allow free reward-driven refinement. This corrects the variance-dependent imbalance in the standard KL term, concentrating the regularization budget where it matters most for preserving semantic diversity.

## Results

Across three backbone/reward combinations (SD3.5-M/PickScore, Flux.1-dev/PickScore, SD3.5-M/HPSv3), DiverseGRPO consistently improves all four diversity metrics over Flow-GRPO while matching or slightly exceeding quality scores. Headline gains include +18.8% DreamSim and +184.2% BeyondFID on SD3.5-M/PickScore, +13.9% DreamSim and +38.2% BeyondFID on Flux.1-dev/PickScore, and +13.9% DreamSim and +69.4% BeyondFID on SD3.5-M/HPSv3. Quality metrics (CLIPScore, ImageReward, PickScore, UnifiedReward) remain within +0.1% to +3.4% of the baseline, confirming that the diversity gains come without sacrificing perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Flow-GRPO [Liu et al., NeurIPS 2025], the original GRPO-based training paradigm for flow-matching models. DiverseGRPO wins on every diversity metric across all three experimental settings: DreamSim improves by 13.9–18.8%, FID drops by 9.1–23.3%, BeyondFID rises by 38.2–184.2%, and SSIM increases by 6.9–25.6%. On quality metrics, DiverseGRPO matches or marginally exceeds Flow-GRPO (CLIPScore +1.0–2.2%, ImageReward +1.5–3.4%, PickScore +0.2–0.3%, UnifiedReward +0.1–0.4%), indicating the diversity gains are achieved without quality degradation. The paper also notes that under comparable regularization budgets, Flow-GRPO (KL=0.01) requires 1,280 iterations to reach the same quality as DiverseGRPO's 400 iterations, while DiverseGRPO simultaneously reduces mode collapse by 9%. Overall, DiverseGRPO establishes a strict Pareto improvement over Flow-GRPO: higher diversity at equal or better quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2025), which first brought GRPO-style group-relative policy updates to flow-matching diffusion models, by identifying and addressing two structural weaknesses in its training paradigm: single-sample reward modeling that drives replicator-dynamics mode extinction, and a uniform KL penalty that is inversely correlated with the diversity-critical early denoising phase. Conceptually, it draws on the LLM mode-collapse literature (DivPO, Cui et al.) for the reward-redesign motivation and on diffusion dynamics analyses (Ho et al., Wang & Vastola) for the generation-level insight, but is the first to unify both perspectives into a single training recipe for flow-matching models. It departs from prior diversity-preserving image generation work (DiADM, Astolfi et al.) by operating entirely within the RL training loop rather than modifying the architecture or introducing auxiliary modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mode collapse in RL-aligned image generators is a practical bottleneck for creative applications (advertising, game design, digital art) where a single 'safe' output mode is insufficient. The paper's replicator-dynamics formalization provides a clear theoretical justification for why single-sample rewards inevitably collapse, and the structural mismatch diagnosis (KL penalty weakest exactly when diversity is most fragile) offers a reusable principle for future RL-on-diffusion work. Limitations include evaluation against only one baseline (Flow-GRPO without KL), a small number of prompts in qualitative comparisons, and the added (albeit modest) clustering overhead per training step. Open questions include whether the distributional creativity bonus generalizes to video generation, whether the Wasserstein scheduling transfers to PPO/DPO-based diffusion alignment, and how the approach behaves at much larger group sizes or with learned (rather than DreamSim-based) perceptual distances.
