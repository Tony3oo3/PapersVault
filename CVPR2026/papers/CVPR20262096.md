## Problem

Existing automated brachytherapy planning methods predict voxel-wise dose distributions but cannot directly produce clinically deliverable dwell times, requiring a separate post-prediction optimization that takes minutes and can fail when the predicted dose is not physically plausible. Additionally, the gamma index—the clinical gold standard for dose comparison—has never been usable as a training loss due to its O(V²) complexity and non-differentiability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262096/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D planning CT scan, a set of binary anatomical contour volumes (tumor target and organs at risk), and a variable-length list of 3D dwell location coordinates (ranging from ~15 to over 250 per plan) as input. The CT and contours are encoded into a compressed latent space via a pretrained LTX-Video VAE (1:8192 voxel-to-token ratio), while dwell locations are embedded as individual prompts. The output is a vector of dwell times (one scalar per location, immediately usable as machine parameters) and a 3D radiation dose distribution computed as a linear combination of TG-43 dose kernels, guaranteeing physical plausibility.

## Method

D2T2 is a two-stage architecture: a DiT transformer (Stage 1) predicts a dwell time vector via cross-attention between LTX-VAE-encoded image patches and dwell-location prompts, followed by an MLP head with absolute-value activations to ensure positive times; a physics layer (Stage 2) then computes the dose as a weighted sum of precomputed TG-43 dose kernels, making the model a radial basis function network with physics-defined basis functions. This end-to-end design eliminates post-prediction optimization and frames dwell times as concept bottleneck variables, providing explainability and physical plausibility guarantees. A key training innovation is a learned gamma loss: a surrogate network τ (same architecture as D2T2 but with patchify/unpatchify I/O) is trained on synthetic dose pairs to approximate the gamma index, making this clinically meaningful metric differentiable and tractable at scale. The full model is trained with a composite loss of L2 dose error plus a weighted gamma loss (λ=0.05), on ~5,000 multi-site clinical plans spanning cervical, breast, endometrial, vaginal, and other treatment sites.

## Results

On the overall test set (~750 plans across 5 treatment sites), D2T2 achieves a dose MAE of 3.54±2.45% and a dwell time MAE of 7.41±6.52 s, compared to 4.41±3.69% and 11.79±8.84 s for the optimizer-based (OPT) baseline. Inference time drops from 35.07±62.56 s (OPT, including iterative optimization) to 0.10±0.04 s (D2T2, single forward pass), a ~350× speedup. The largest dose MAE improvement is on the 'Other' site category (5.09→3.75%), where the implicit physical-plausibility regularization of D2T2 is most beneficial in a low-data regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the optimizer-based (OPT) method of Kallis et al. [15] and Moore et al. [25], which predicts a voxel-wise dose distribution with a 3D CNN and then recovers dwell times via a post-hoc iterative optimization. D2T2 outperforms OPT on every metric and every treatment site: overall dose MAE improves from 4.41% to 3.54% (−19.7%), overall dwell time MAE drops from 11.79 s to 7.41 s (−37.2%), and inference time falls from ~35 s to ~0.1 s. The dwell-time advantage is particularly pronounced on cervical (13.00→7.56 s) and endometrial (13.07→8.72 s) sites, where the OPT method's compounding of dose-prediction error with optimization error is most severe. On the vaginal site, dose MAE is nearly tied (6.55 vs 6.51%), but D2T2 still wins on dwell time MAE (4.28→3.72 s) and is ~500× faster. The authors note that no other open-source brachytherapy auto-planning baselines exist, so the comparison is limited to this single SOTA method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in automated brachytherapy planning—predicting a voxel-wise dose distribution and then recovering dwell times via a separate, time-consuming optimization step [15, 25]—by instead predicting dwell times directly as an intermediate output of a transformer, with the dose computed as a physically constrained linear combination of TG-43 kernels. Architecturally, it repurposes the DiT transformer [28] and the LTX-Video VAE [11] (originally designed for video generation) as a multimodal encoder-fusion backbone, and frames dwell times as concept-bottleneck variables [4, 17]. The learned gamma-index surrogate loss extends the broader trend of learned perceptual/task-level losses (LPIPS, CLIP-based objectives) into radiotherapy, where the clinically standard gamma metric had previously been intractable for gradient-based training due to its O(V²) complexity and non-differentiability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

D2T2 is the first site-agnostic automated brachytherapy planner, trained on ~5,000 multi-site plans, and reduces planning latency from tens of seconds (OPT) or 60+ minutes (manual) to ~0.1 s per plan, which has direct clinical impact on patient sedation time and workflow throughput. The learned gamma loss is a reusable technique that could benefit other dose-comparison tasks in radiotherapy. Key open questions include: whether the LTX-Video VAE (a general-purpose video encoder) is truly optimal versus a domain-specific 3D medical VAE, whether the synthetic-data training of the gamma surrogate τ generalizes to out-of-distribution dose errors, and whether the model's multi-site generalization holds for sites with very different applicator geometries not represented in the training set. No public dataset or code is released, limiting reproducibility.
