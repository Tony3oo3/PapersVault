## Problem

Existing masked image modeling frameworks for Earth Observation treat each image tile as an isolated sample, ignoring the rich spatial dependencies between neighboring scenes that arise from the Earth's continuous surface. Although neighboring images share terrain structure, land-use continuity, and infrastructure patterns, this spatial coherence has been largely unexploited in MIM-based self-supervised pretraining, limiting learned representations to the local extent of a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263610/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of spatially adjacent RGB EO images (sampled by an IoU threshold on their georeferenced bounding boxes) along with their geographic coordinates, which are normalized into a shared [0,1] coordinate system to compute relative positional embeddings. After random cropping and resizing to the model input size (e.g., 224×224), both images are patchified and their tokens are concatenated for joint processing. The output consists of learned visual representations from the ViT encoder (used as frozen or tunable backbones for downstream tasks) and pixel-level reconstructions of masked patches from both images, with the reconstruction loss weighted per-pixel based on cross-visibility from the neighboring image.

## Method

NeighborMAE adapts the MAE encoder-decoder architecture to jointly reconstruct masked regions from two neighboring EO images by concatenating all visible patches from both images as input to a single ViT encoder, so that self-attention captures spatial (and incidental temporal) dependencies across the pair. Three key designs prevent shortcut learning and maintain task difficulty: (1) a dynamic mask ratio that linearly scales with the IoU of the augmented neighboring pair (from m₁=0.75 to m₂=0.85), compensating for the extra information provided by overlap; (2) a relative positional embedding that encodes each patch's 4D bounding-box coordinates in a shared normalized coordinate system via sinusoidal encoding, plus a learnable image-level token to distinguish the two sources; and (3) a pixel-level weighted reconstruction loss that bounds the loss for cross-visible pixels (masked in one image but visible in the other) by the MSE of simply copying the neighboring correspondence, thereby suppressing trivial copy-paste solutions while still rewarding reconstruction of genuinely changed regions.

## Results

Pretrained on fMoW-RGB, NeighborMAE (ViT-Large-16) achieves 68.8% frozen / 79.3% fine-tuned accuracy on fMoW classification, 91.4% / 97.6% on UC Merced, and 60.3 / 66.6 mIoU on Five-Billion-Pixels segmentation, outperforming all same-line baselines. Pretrained on Satellogic, it reaches 58.8% / 77.9% on fMoW and 58.4 / 63.7 mIoU on FBP, still surpassing the MAE baseline by +1.5% / +1.2% (frozen / fine-tuned) on fMoW. Against the large-scale multi-modal DOFA model, fMoW-pretrained NeighborMAE is competitive on most RGB tasks, winning on fMoW (+6.2% frozen) and ForestNet (+8.6% frozen) while trailing on UC Merced (−5.0% frozen) and RESISC45 (−3.5% frozen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the plain MAE baseline, NeighborMAE consistently improves: +2.0% frozen and +1.1% fine-tuned on fMoW (fMoW pretraining), +2.7% mIoU on FBP and +2.2% mIoU on PASTIS (fine-tuned), confirming that joint reconstruction of neighboring images adds meaningful signal. Among same-line MIM baselines pretrained on fMoW, NeighborMAE outperforms SatMAE by +4.5% frozen on fMoW and +9.1 mIoU on FBP, beats SatMAE++ by +2.4% frozen on fMoW and +4.7 mIoU on FBP, and surpasses ScaleMAE on fMoW (+1.7% frozen) and FBP (+2.8 mIoU frozen), though ScaleMAE retains a small edge on UC Merced (93.1 vs 91.4 frozen) and RESISC45 (92.2 vs 91.0 frozen). Cross-Scale MAE is the weakest baseline, trailing NeighborMAE by 26.2% frozen on fMoW and 18.7 mIoU on FBP. Against the large-scale multi-modal DOFA model, fMoW-pretrained NeighborMAE wins on fMoW (+6.2% frozen) and ForestNet (+8.6% frozen, +3.0% fine-tuned) but loses on UC Merced (−5.0% frozen) and RESISC45 (−3.5% frozen); Satellogic-pretrained NeighborMAE is broadly competitive with DOFA, matching it on FireRisk (60.4 vs 60.3 frozen) and slightly trailing on FBP (58.4 vs 59.7 frozen) while leading on PASTIS (32.5 vs 32.2 frozen).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeighborMAE is a direct adaptation of MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) to the Earth Observation domain, extending its single-image masked reconstruction to jointly encode and decode pairs of spatially neighboring images. It departs from the dominant line of MIM-based EO pretraining (SatMAE, ScaleMAE, Cross-Scale MAE, SatMAE++) that mines multi-spectral, multi-temporal, or multi-scale dependencies while treating each image tile in isolation. The idea of leveraging geographic proximity is not new in EO SSL—contrastive methods like SeCo, CaCO, and Tile2Vec already use neighboring tiles as positive pairs—but NeighborMAE is the first to exploit this spatial continuity through a reconstruction objective with explicit relative positional encoding and visibility-aware loss weighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial continuity between neighboring EO images is a previously underused signal that yields consistent downstream gains (1–3% on classification and segmentation) across two distinct pretraining datasets and seven benchmarks, with a particularly notable synergy when spatial and temporal dependencies are jointly modeled. The approach is computationally lightweight relative to multi-scale reconstruction methods (SatMAE++), requiring only ~26% more memory than vanilla MAE. Key limitations include the restriction to RGB imagery and pairs of exactly two images, with the O(n²) self-attention cost limiting scalability to larger neighborhoods; extending to multi-spectral data and more efficient token-mixing architectures are the natural next steps.
