## Problem

KL-VAE-based continuous image tokenizers suffer from posterior collapse under strong compression, where the KL regularization pushes the latent distribution toward the isotropic Gaussian prior and the encoder fails to preserve informative semantics. This is particularly acute when reducing token counts to 64 or 128, yet existing remedies such as KL weight annealing require delicate hyperparameter tuning and do not fundamentally resolve latent degeneration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MacTok takes RGB images at 256×256 or 512×512, partitions them into non-overlapping patches, and produces a compact sequence of 64 or 128 continuous 1D latent tokens (each of dimension Z) via a ViT-Base encoder. During training, a fraction of image patches (up to 70%) is replaced with mask tokens before encoding, so the encoder must infer complete semantics from incomplete visual evidence. The same latent tokens are then decoded back into full-resolution images, and downstream generative models (SiT-XL, LightningDiT) consume them as 1D token sequences for conditional image synthesis.

## Method

MacTok combines two complementary mechanisms to prevent posterior collapse. First, it applies image-level masking with equal probability of random masking (uniformly sampled mask ratio up to M=0.7) and DINO-guided semantic masking, where the top-K patches most similar to the DINOv2 classification token are occluded to force reconstruction of key semantic regions. Second, it introduces a global-and-local representation alignment loss that projects each latent token (expanded to patch granularity) and a pooled global token into DINOv2 feature space via lightweight MLPs, then maximizes cosine similarity against DINOv2 patch and classification tokens respectively. The full training objective combines pixelwise reconstruction, perceptual, adversarial, KL, and alignment losses, with the encoder initialized from DINOv2 weights to inject semantic priors. This design maintains sufficient mutual information between input and latent under strong compression, yielding well-structured, discriminative latent spaces that support high-fidelity generation with up to 64× fewer tokens than 2D-grid tokenizers.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MacTok with 128 tokens and SiT-XL achieves a gFID of 1.50 (w/ CFG) and 3.12 (w/o CFG), with rFID 0.43; with 64 tokens and LightningDiT, it reaches gFID 1.68 (w/ CFG) and rFID 0.75. On ImageNet 512×512, MacTok with 128 tokens and SiT-XL achieves a state-of-the-art gFID of 1.52 (w/ CFG) and IS of 316.0, while using up to 64× fewer tokens than 2D tokenizers (128 vs. 4096). Reconstruction quality remains strong: rFID of 0.79 (128 tokens) and 0.89 (64 tokens) at 512×512.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 256×256 with 128 tokens, MacTok+SiT-XL (gFID 1.50 w/ CFG) outperforms MAETok (1.67) and SoftVQ-VAE (1.78) but trails LightningDiT (1.35) and +REPA (1.42), which use 2D tokenizers with 256–1024 tokens. With 64 tokens, MacTok+LightningDiT (gFID 1.68 w/ CFG) beats SoftVQ-VAE at the same token count (1.78) and MAETok at 128 tokens (1.67), while achieving a better rFID (0.75 vs. 0.88). At 512×512, MacTok+SiT-XL with 128 tokens achieves a new SOTA gFID of 1.52 (w/ CFG), surpassing MAETok (1.69) and SoftVQ-VAE (2.21), and matches the 64-token variant (also 1.52). Without CFG, MacTok with 64 tokens at 512 (gFID 4.63) substantially outperforms SoftVQ-VAE (7.96) and MAETok (2.79 at 128 tokens). In the continuous-tokenizer comparison (Table 3, SiT-B, 256×256), MacTok with 128 tokens achieves the best gFID (3.15) among all continuous tokenizers, beating SoftVQ-VAE (4.09), MAETok (4.77), and VA-VAE (4.33), while using fewer tokens than VA-VAE (256) and SD-VAE (1024).

## Relation to Prior Work

MacTok extends the 1D continuous tokenizer lineage established by TiTok, SoftVQ-VAE, and MAETok, which replaced 2D grid-based tokenization with flexible-length 1D latent sequences. Its core departure is addressing posterior collapse in KL-VAE tokenizers through image-space masking (both random and DINOv2-guided semantic), drawing on masked representation learning (MAE) and the observation that latent-space masking only delays collapse. The global-and-local representation alignment with DINOv2 features is inspired by REPA and VA-VAE but differs in jointly aligning each latent token to both patch-level and image-level semantics, rather than relying on a fixed token count or multiple auxiliary objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MacTok demonstrates that a simple masking strategy can fundamentally resolve posterior collapse in KL-based tokenizers, enabling 64–128 token representations that achieve SOTA generation quality (gFID 1.52 at 512×512) with up to 64× fewer tokens than 2D tokenizers. This has direct practical impact on inference cost and model scalability for diffusion and flow-based generators. Open questions include whether the DINOv2 dependency for both semantic masking and alignment limits generalization to domains without strong pretrained features, whether the approach transfers to video or 3D tokenization, and whether the modest gap between random and semantic masking (Table 4) suggests the semantic guidance could be further refined.
