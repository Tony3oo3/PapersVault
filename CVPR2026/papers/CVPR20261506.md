## Problem

Existing underwater segmentation datasets cover fewer than 40 coarse categories, and most models require extensive pretraining on a fixed class set, making them impractical for large-scale biodiversity monitoring. Moreover, the unique degradation of underwater imagery (color attenuation, light scattering, blurred textures) causes terrestrial vision-language models to fail at fine-grained category discrimination without domain-specific adaptation. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing the first large-scale fine-grained underwater OV dataset and a training-free framework that transfers terrestrial VLMs to underwater scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an underwater RGB image I ∈ R^(H×W×3) and a set of T textual category concepts (up to 255 fine-grained classes) as input, and produces a dense pixel-level segmentation map M_pred ∈ R^(T×H×W) via softmax over per-pixel category scores. A key practical detail is that MLLM reasoning outputs (captions, object lists, attribute descriptions) are pre-encoded into the CLIP text encoder at initialization, so inference requires only a single forward pass through the visual encoder and a matrix multiplication, achieving ~17.5 FPS on a single RTX 4090 with ViT-B/16.

## Method

Earth2Ocean is a training-free pipeline with two core modules. The Geometric-guided Visual Mask Generator (GMG) extracts multi-scale features from a DINOv2-based geometric encoder, computes a self-similarity map from the last-layer features, applies mean-centering, scaling, and negative-thresholding to produce a sparse attention map, and uses it to refine CLIP's last-layer visual embeddings—exploiting the observation that geometric structure remains stable under underwater degradation. The Category-Visual Semantic Alignment (CSA) module has two stages: (1) underwater-aware template enhancement that generates T structured text prompts per category covering appearance, scene context, environmental conditions, interactions, and scale; and (2) MLLM-guided alignment where GPT-4o produces instance-specific captions and attribute descriptions, encoded as reasoning-aware embeddings that are fused with template embeddings via a similarity-gated weighting scheme (cosine similarity thresholded and capped at w_max). Final segmentation is obtained by a single matrix product of the fused text embeddings with the geometry-corrected visual features, followed by softmax—no gradient updates or underwater training are required.

## Results

On the UOVSBench benchmark (6 underwater datasets), Earth2Ocean achieves average mIoU of 34.32 (ViT-B/16), 44.00 (ViT-L/14), and 49.67 (ViT-H/14), outperforming the strongest baseline CorrCLIP by 4.76, 6.67, and 8.41 mIoU points respectively. In terms of average aAcc, Earth2Ocean reaches 54.34 / 64.91 / 68.17 across the three backbones, and average mAcc reaches 49.84 / 61.13 / 64.89. The gains are most pronounced on the newly introduced AquaOV255 dataset, where Earth2Ocean (ViT-L/14) achieves 45.13 mIoU versus CorrCLIP's 31.32 mIoU, a 13.8-point margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Earth2Ocean consistently outperforms all five baselines (ClearCLIP, SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, Trident, CorrCLIP) across every backbone, dataset, and metric. The strongest baseline is CorrCLIP (ICCV 2025), which itself leverages off-the-shelf foundation models for correlation reconstruction; Earth2Ocean surpasses it by 4.8–8.4 mIoU on average, with the largest gap (13.8 mIoU) on the fine-grained AquaOV255 dataset. Trident (ICCV 2025) and ProxyCLIP (ECCV 2024) are close second and third, with Trident slightly ahead of ProxyCLIP on most datasets but both trailing CorrCLIP. ClearCLIP and SCLIP, the earliest training-free CLIP-based methods, lag significantly behind—by over 10 mIoU on average—confirming that simple patch-to-text mapping is insufficient for underwater scenes. Notably, Earth2Ocean also achieves the best efficiency-accuracy trade-off: at 17.54 FPS it is faster than Trident (8.77 FPS) and CorrCLIP (5.68 FPS) while delivering the highest accuracy, thanks to pre-encoded MLLM features that avoid runtime LLM inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) line of work—built on CLIP visual/text encoders and spatial priors from DINO or SAM (as in ProxyCLIP, Trident, CorrCLIP)—to the underwater domain without any underwater-specific training. The GMG module repurposes DINOv2 self-similarity maps as geometry-guided attention priors, a mechanism conceptually similar to ProxyCLIP's DINO-based spatial correction but applied to a domain where color and texture are severely degraded while geometric structure remains stable. The CSA module introduces a novel use of MLLM reasoning (GPT-4o) to generate attribute-rich, scene-aware text embeddings that compensate for the category-visual misalignment inherent in terrestrial VLMs when applied underwater. On the data side, AquaOV255 (255 fine-grained categories, 20K+ images) and UOVSBench represent a substantial expansion over prior underwater datasets, which typically cover fewer than 40 categories or label all species generically as 'fish.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AquaOV255 and UOVSBench fills a critical gap in underwater computer vision by providing the first fine-grained, open-vocabulary evaluation resource, enabling systematic comparison of OVS methods in a domain previously limited to closed-set, coarse-label benchmarks. The training-free paradigm is practically important for real-world marine monitoring where collecting labeled underwater data is expensive and where models must generalize to unseen species. However, absolute performance remains modest (34 mIoU with ViT-B/16, 55 with ViT-H/14), and the reliance on terrestrial VLMs constrains segmentation of extremely rare or visually ambiguous species. Open questions include whether pre-training underwater-specific VLMs on large-scale data could close the remaining gap, and whether the geometric-stability assumption holds across all underwater conditions (e.g., turbid water, deep-sea low light).
