## Problem

Existing multi-view clustering methods for remote sensing data assume every pixel is fully observed across all views, yet real-world acquisitions frequently suffer from partially missing data due to sensor failures or cloud occlusion. No prior work has addressed the more practical scenario of incomplete multi-view remote sensing clustering, where view-specific spectral discrepancies, cross-view consistency requirements, and spatial coherence must all be handled simultaneously under missing observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263739/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes V views of remote sensing imagery (e.g., hyperspectral, SAR, multispectral, LiDAR, DSM) at pixel resolution N = H×W, where each view v has B_v spectral channels and a binary index matrix Q^v marking which pixels are available. Missing pixels are zero-padded to preserve structural consistency, and missing patterns are non-overlapping across views. The output is a consensus representation H ∈ R^{D×N} from which pixel-level cluster assignments are obtained via k-means, with D being a user-specified latent dimension.

## Method

OSMAGC constructs view-specific spatial-aware anchors via ERS superpixel segmentation on each view's first principal component, yielding multi-scale anchor graphs with view-adaptive sizes that capture heterogeneous spatial-spectral textures. Complete multi-scale anchor graphs are then learned from incomplete data through view-weighted matrix factorization with orthogonality constraints on anchors, where an adaptive weighting scheme emphasizes informative views. Structure-aligned consensus features are obtained by projecting each view's anchor graph into a shared D-dimensional latent space via orthogonal alignment matrices, and a total-variation-based spatial regularizer enforces smoothness along both horizontal and vertical pixel directions. All three learning modules are jointly optimized by an alternating algorithm that solves each subproblem in closed form (SVD for anchor and alignment matrices, capped-simplex projection for graph coefficients, and a linear-system solve for the consensus representation), achieving near-linear time complexity in the number of pixels.

## Results

At a 0.5 missing ratio, OSMAGC achieves the best performance across all four datasets and all four metrics (ACC, NMI, Purity, ARI). Headline ACC values are 0.5002 (MUUFL), 0.4410 (Berlin), 0.6120 (Augsburg), and 0.4345 (MDAS), yielding improvements of 9.66%, 3.95%, 1.11%, and 1.74% over the strongest competitor respectively. The method also demonstrates strong robustness: at a 0.7 missing ratio it still maintains ACC gains of 6.78%, 2.82%, 3.28%, and 2.86% across the four datasets. In terms of efficiency, OSMAGC is the fastest method, completing clustering in 14.01 s (MUUFL), 68.38 s (Berlin), 31.6 s (Augsburg), and 14.78 s (MDAS), while PMIMC runs out of memory on Berlin and DIVIDE requires over 1700 s on every dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSMAGC outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset and metric at the 0.5 missing ratio. Among the four remote-sensing-specific methods, FPFC is the closest competitor on ACC (e.g., 0.6009 vs. 0.6120 on Augsburg) but lags significantly on NMI and ARI; SAMVGC is competitive on Berlin NMI (0.2425) and Purity (0.6850) yet still trails OSMAGC by 1.09% and 2.37% respectively. Among the four general incomplete multi-view methods, SIMVC-SA achieves the best NMI on MUUFL (0.3689) and Purity on Augsburg (0.7240) among non-remote-sensing baselines, but OSMAGC surpasses it by 3.35% and 7.90% on those metrics. DIVIDE and PMIMC are the weakest performers in terms of both accuracy and efficiency: PMIMC runs out of memory on Berlin, while DIVIDE requires 1735–2548 s per dataset versus OSMAGC's 14–68 s. ASCR is the fastest general method (16.92 s on MUUFL) but achieves the lowest NMI on Berlin (0.0395) and Augsburg (0.1629), indicating poor structural recovery under missing data. Overall, the gap between OSMAGC and the best baseline widens with increasing missing ratio, reaching up to 11.64% in ARI on MUUFL at 0.3 missing ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: multi-view clustering for remote sensing data (which assumes complete observations) and incomplete multi-view clustering for general data (which ignores spatial-spectral structure). It adapts the structure-alignment strategy from Wen et al. (ACM MM 2023) to project multi-scale anchor graphs into a shared latent space, and builds on the superpixel-based anchor graph initialization from Zhang et al. (TIP 2024) and Wang et al. (TGRS 2025) for remote sensing. The key departure is the introduction of view-weighted matrix factorization over incomplete data combined with an orthogonal TV-based spatial regularizer, neither of which appears in prior remote sensing clustering or general incomplete multi-view clustering work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sensor failures and cloud occlusion make incomplete multi-view remote sensing data a common real-world scenario, yet no prior method addressed it; this work provides the first dedicated framework with strong empirical gains (up to ~10% ACC) and near-linear computational complexity, making it practical for large-scale Earth observation. The ablation reveals dataset-specific sensitivity to each component (e.g., SAGI matters most for MDAS, SACFL for MUUFL), suggesting that a one-size-fits-all configuration may not be optimal. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to temporal missing patterns (e.g., multi-temporal stacks), whether the ERS superpixel initialization is optimal for very high-resolution imagery, and how performance degrades when missing patterns are spatially correlated rather than random.
