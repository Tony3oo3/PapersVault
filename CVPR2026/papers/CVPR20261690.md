## Problem

Extreme activation outliers (magnitudes >1000) cause catastrophic overflow in native FP8 training and make the simplest per-tensor static quantization (W8A8) completely unusable, forcing practitioners into complex mixed-precision engineering or invasive architectural changes. The prevailing assumption that outliers are data-driven (e.g., caused by special tokens or token frequency) has led to data-dependent fixes that lack generality across modalities. This paper challenges that assumption, showing outliers are a data-independent mechanical artifact of weight matrix structure, and proposes a minimal solution.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard Transformer inputs—text token sequences for LLMs (GPT-2 family, 124M–7B) and RGB image patches for vision models (Swin-T/S/B, ViT-S/B)—with no changes to input format or preprocessing. The key output property is that trained models exhibit activation magnitudes clamped below ~20 across all layers, enabling full-model FP8 pre-training with a single per-tensor scaling factor and post-training W8A8 per-tensor static quantization (including the residual stream) that was previously impossible. The regularization is applied at the block-level output (after residual addition), not at internal MLP activations.

## Method

TWEO adds a single L4 regularization term to the task loss: the mean of (|A^(l)|/τ)^p over all L Transformer block outputs, with τ=3 (soft threshold) and p=4 (penalty power), weighted by λ=0.01. The p=4 power creates a nonlinear penalty that is negligible for normal activations (|A|<τ) but imposes a massive cost (10^4) on extreme values, selectively suppressing the outlier tail without distorting the bulk distribution. The theoretical analysis via SVD decomposition (Eq. 1) shows that outliers arise when a down-projection row becomes collinear with a dominant left singular vector of the up-projection matrix, amplifying the product s_i·(w^T u_i)·(v_i^T x); TWEO mechanically prevents this alignment from producing extreme magnitudes during training. A 'Contradiction Stethoscope' experiment (feeding random inputs to trained vs. random weights) confirms the data-independent, structural origin. The method is non-invasive, requires no architectural modification, and is applied identically across language and vision Transformers.

## Results

On FP8 pre-training of GPT-2 models (124M–7B) on OpenWebText, standard FP8 training catastrophically collapses (e.g., PPL 67.12 at 7B), while TWEO achieves PPL 12.02 (7B) and 12.58 (1.5B), matching or surpassing the BF16 baseline (12.39 at 1.5B) with a 36% throughput gain. Peak activation outliers are reduced from 30,000+ to ≤20 across all scales. On ImageNet-1K, TWEO reduces Swin-S peak outliers from 6,402 to 35 (99.4% reduction) while maintaining 82.8% Top-1 accuracy (vs. 82.7% baseline). For post-training quantization, W8A8 per-tensor static quantization on TWEO-trained GPT-2 Medium achieves PPL 16.50, versus 1,491.11 for the same scheme on the baseline model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TWEO outperforms all invasive architectural baselines on both accuracy and outlier suppression. On GPT-2 (124M) FP8 training, Attn bias [1] achieves PPL 24.93 and Attn ca-softmax [4,24] achieves 23.81, both substantially worse than TWEO's 19.26, while also requiring model modifications. On Swin-T (ImageNet), Gated attention [37] drops Top-1 to 80.7% (vs. 81.4% for TWEO) and still leaves peak outliers at 976, whereas TWEO suppresses them to 22 without any architectural change. For post-training quantization, SmoothQuant [51] catastrophically collapses when forced to quantize the residual stream (PPL 1,876.70 on GPT-2 XL), while TWEO-trained models maintain PPL 13.06 under the same W8(C)A8(T) setting with residual quantization. Overall, TWEO wins on every comparison: it matches or exceeds BF16 accuracy, eliminates outliers by 3–4 orders of magnitude, and enables the simplest per-tensor quantization scheme that all baselines cannot support.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant framing of extreme activation outliers as data-driven phenomena (token frequency, special tokens, no-op hypothesis) and instead identifies them as mechanical artifacts of weight-matrix collinearity developed during training. Rather than following the two existing solution families—mixed-precision engineering (DeepSeek-V3) that bypasses outliers, or invasive architectural modifications (register tokens, Smooth-SwiGLU, Clipped Softmax) that alter the model—it introduces a non-invasive L4 regularization loss (TWEO) applied to block-level activations, drawing on SVD-based analysis of MLP weight alignment. For post-training quantization, it positions against difficulty-transfer methods (SmoothQuant, SpinQuant, AWQ) by arguing that if outliers are eliminated at the source, the simplest per-tensor AbsMax quantization becomes sufficient, removing the need for scaling factors, rotations, or residual-stream exemptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If TWEO scales to frontier models, it could eliminate the complex mixed-precision engineering that currently makes FP8 training a bespoke effort, and it opens a new quantization research direction: with outlier-free models, the field can explore aggressive low-bit schemes (W4A4, FP4) without the difficulty-transfer overhead. The demonstrated ability to quantize the residual stream (which SmoothQuant cannot) has direct hardware implications, enabling leaner ASIC designs with simpler per-tensor quantization logic. Key open questions include whether TWEO transfers to fine-tuning of existing large models, whether it holds at 700B+ scale, and whether the collinearity mechanism fully explains all outlier sources in more complex architectures (MoE, Mamba).
