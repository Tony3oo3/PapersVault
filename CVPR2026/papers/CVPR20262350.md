## Problem

Existing compositional 3D scene generation methods either depend on slow iterative pose/layout optimization or tightly couple object generation with layout estimation, making them inefficient and inflexible. Moreover, most approaches operate on perspective images with limited field-of-view, preventing the creation of geometrically complete 360° environments from panoramic inputs that suffer from severe distortion and non-uniform sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262350/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single equirectangular panoramic image (H×W×3) as input; for the text-to-3D variant, a text prompt first synthesizes a panorama via Diffusion360. Each detected object is segmented with SAM and projected into a distortion-free perspective crop before being fed to an off-the-shelf image-to-3D generator (default: TRELLIS). The output is a compositional 3D scene in world coordinates: N textured object meshes or 3D Gaussian splats with predicted rotation, translation, and anisotropic scale, fused with a background 3D Gaussian representation reconstructed from an inpainted panorama. Full scene generation completes in ~20 seconds on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play Object-World Transformation Predictor built on an adapted VGGT architecture (Alignment-VGGT) that decouples object generation from spatial alignment. Given multi-view renderings of a generated object, the target perspective crop, and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, Alignment-VGGT predicts the object-to-world rotation, translation, and anisotropic scale in a single feed-forward pass, with camera parameters explicitly encoded via linear projections to disentangle geometry from visual features. To handle shape discrepancies between generated and ground-truth objects, the predictor is trained with pseudo-geometry supervision distilled from offline differentiable optimizers (bidirectional or single-directional Chamfer distance plus mask loss) rather than GT mesh poses. A Coarse-to-Fine (C2F) refinement mechanism further iteratively corrects pose errors for unseen domains by feeding the current scene rendering back into a lightweight Alignment-VGGT refiner, terminating when Chamfer distance improvement falls below a threshold—achieving robust alignment without gradient-based optimization at test time.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT test set, Pano3DComposer achieves the best performance across all metrics: scene-level Chamfer Distance (CD-S) of 0.0787, object-level CD (CD-O) of 0.0765, scene F-Score of 0.6923, object F-Score of 0.6926, and bounding-box IoU of 0.5679. The C2F variant marginally improves all metrics (e.g., CD-S 0.0784, IoU-B 0.5699) at only 4 s additional inference cost. The method requires just 2 GPU days of training and 20 s per-scene inference on a single RTX 4090, compared to 56 GPU days and 63 s for the strongest feed-forward baseline (SceneGen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pano3DComposer outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the differentiable-optimization baseline (OPT, following REPARO [10]), it reduces CD-S from 0.1059 to 0.0787 (−26%) and improves IoU-B from 0.4010 to 0.5679 (+42%), while being 6× faster (20 s vs. 120 s). Against ICP [18], the gains are even larger: CD-S drops from 0.2483 to 0.0787 and IoU-B rises from 0.2830 to 0.5679, as ICP struggles with outliers and symmetries. Against DeepPanoContext [42], the panorama-specific baseline, Pano3DComposer achieves a 10× lower CD-S (0.0787 vs. 0.7851) and a near-zero-to-high IoU-B jump (0.5679 vs. 0.0021), reflecting DeepPanoContext's inability to produce render-ready textured meshes. Against SceneGen [26], the strongest feed-forward multi-instance method, Pano3DComposer improves CD-S by 55% (0.0787 vs. 0.1765), F-Score-S by 51% (0.6923 vs. 0.4575), and IoU-B by 5× (0.5679 vs. 0.1124), while requiring 28× less training (2 vs. 56 GPU days) and 3× less inference time. For text-to-3D scene generation, Pano3DComposer is compared qualitatively against GALA3D [47] and DreamScene [19]; the paper reports that both baselines rely on SDS-based per-object optimization (30–60 min each) and LLM-generated layouts that often violate physical constraints, whereas Pano3DComposer produces more realistic textures and physically plausible arrangements in ~20 s total.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VGGT visual-geometry foundation model, adapting it into Alignment-VGGT to handle cross-coordinate geometric mapping between objects in different coordinate systems with different camera parameters—a setting vanilla VGGT was not designed for. It departs from compositional 3D scene generation pipelines (GALA3D, REPARO, Gen3DSR) that rely on slow iterative optimization, and from feed-forward scene understanding methods (Total3D, DeepPanoContext, SceneGen) that tightly couple object generation with layout estimation. The key conceptual move is decoupling these two sub-problems: using any off-the-shelf image-to-3D generator (e.g., TRELLIS) for object geometry and a dedicated feed-forward transformation predictor for spatial placement, with pseudo-geometry supervision to bridge the shape gap between generated and ground-truth objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20-second inference time for a complete 360° compositional scene (vs. minutes for optimization-based methods) makes panoramic scene generation practical for VR/AR and digital twin applications. The plug-and-play modular design means future advances in single-object 3D generation can be immediately integrated without retraining the alignment module. However, the large gap to the pseudo-geometry upper bound (CD-S 0.0787 vs. 0.0119) indicates the feed-forward predictor still captures only a fraction of achievable alignment quality, and the method's reliance on 2D segmentation quality and offline pseudo-geometry distillation for training data generation are practical bottlenecks. Open questions include scalability to outdoor scenes with hundreds of objects and whether the C2F mechanism can be further accelerated.
