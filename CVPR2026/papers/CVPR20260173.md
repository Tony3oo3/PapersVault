## Problem

Existing generative methods for remote sensing are task-specific (semantic segmentation or change detection only), restricted to RGB output, and lack robustness to noisy training samples. No unified framework simultaneously supports both downstream tasks, exploits multispectral bands, and provides principled noise-robust training for diffusion-based image-mask synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260173/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multispectral remote sensing images (H×W×a channels, e.g., 512×512 with NIR on Hi-CNA) paired with either a single-channel semantic segmentation mask or a bi-temporal change mask. It produces aligned synthetic image-mask pairs at 128×128 resolution: the diffusion model samples a 3-channel fused tensor (one compressed spectral feature channel + one mask channel + one auxiliary channel), and a pix2pix decoder reconstructs the full a-channel multispectral image from the compressed feature, yielding a pixel-aligned (x̃, m̃) pair ready for downstream training.

## Method

RDF-MIG introduces Feature Compression Fusion (FCF), which reduces a multispectral image to a single-channel feature via learned per-band weights and concatenates it with the mask to form a 3-channel tensor, enabling direct reuse of RGB-pretrained diffusion backbones (DDPM/LDM) for joint image-mask generation. The core novelty is the Maximum Correntropy Robust Diffusion (MCRD) loss, which replaces the standard MSE noise-prediction objective with a kernelized information potential ρ(1−exp(−‖e‖²/2σ²)) that adaptively suppresses gradients from heavy-tailed or mislabeled samples while matching MSE behavior for small errors. An MSE-consistency calibration (ρσ⁻²=2) is derived via Taylor expansion to guarantee first-order gradient equivalence with MSE in the clean regime, and a second-order analysis yields a closed-form kernel-width selection rule σ ≥ e_max√(3/2β). A pix2pix decoder (U-Net generator + PatchGAN discriminator) then reconstructs the full multispectral image from the compressed feature, completing the end-to-end generation pipeline without modifying the diffusion backbone.

## Results

On Hi-CNA semantic segmentation (U-Net), RDF-MIG synthetic data yields IoU 44.13 / F1 61.24 / Recall 72.87, outperforming the best prior method SatSynth (41.57 / 58.73 / 63.66) by +2.56 IoU. On Hi-CNA change detection (SNU-Net), it achieves IoU 50.00 / F1 66.67, surpassing ChangeDiff (44.77 / 61.85) by +5.23 IoU. On LEVIR-CD+ (STANet), it reaches IoU 45.72 vs. ChangeDiff's 42.68. Adding the NIR band (Ours+NIR) further lifts Hi-CNA SS IoU to 45.20 and CD IoU to 51.07, confirming the multispectral advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDF-MIG is the only method applicable to both semantic segmentation and change detection without requiring per-temporal semantic masks, and the only one supporting multispectral generation. On semantic segmentation, it outperforms SatSynth by +2.56 IoU on Hi-CNA (U-Net) and +1.54 IoU on WHU Building, and beats SegDiff and SemGAN by 9.7 and 13.8 IoU respectively on Hi-CNA. On change detection, it surpasses ChangeDiff by +5.23 IoU on Hi-CNA (SNU-Net) and +2.52 IoU on LEVIR-CD+ (STANet), and leads ChangeAnywhere and Changen by +10.87 and +18.18 IoU on Hi-CNA. In image-quality metrics (Table 3), MCRD-trained RDF-MIG achieves FID 27.38 on Hi-CNA and 17.05 on WHU Building, beating SatSynth (33.61 / 45.07) and ChangeDiff (32.15 on Hi-CNA CD), though Changen's copy-paste strategy yields a lower FID (7.98) at the cost of limited downstream gains. Under 15% label noise (Table 5), MCRD-generated data degrades only −0.28 IoU on Hi-CNA SS versus −2.51 for MSE and −1.45 for Huber, confirming superior robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of synthetic-data generation for remote sensing: segmentation-oriented methods (SatSynth, SegDiff, SemGAN) and change-detection-oriented methods (ChangeDiff, Changen, ChangeAnywhere), which had each been limited to a single task and RGB-only output. The Feature Compression Fusion (FCF) mechanism departs from prior work by compressing multispectral bands into a single-channel feature that can be concatenated with a mask to form a three-channel tensor compatible with RGB-pretrained diffusion backbones (e.g., LDM/SD), enabling multispectral generation without architectural changes. On the loss side, the paper introduces correntropy—a kernel-based information potential from adaptive filtering—into diffusion training for the first time, replacing the standard MSE noise-prediction objective with an adaptively reweighted MCRD loss, and provides an MSE-consistency calibration (ρσ⁻² = 2) that guarantees first-order gradient equivalence to MSE in the small-error regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified SS+CD framework with multispectral support addresses a practical gap in remote sensing data augmentation: practitioners working with datasets like Hi-CNA or LEVIR-CD+ previously needed separate pipelines for each task and could not exploit NIR or other spectral bands. The MCRD loss with its closed-form kernel-width selection rule (σ ≥ e_max·√(3/2β)) offers a principled, interpretable alternative to ad-hoc Huber/L1 robustness, and the 15% label-noise experiment demonstrates meaningful robustness gains. Limitations include evaluation only at 128×128 resolution on a single GPU, restriction to remote sensing imagery, and the modest (though consistent) performance margins over MSE/Huber baselines; generalization to other domains and higher resolutions remains open.
