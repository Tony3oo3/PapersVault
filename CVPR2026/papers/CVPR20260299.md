## Problem

Long-tailed learning suffers from a persistent seesaw dilemma: improving tail-class accuracy degrades head-class accuracy and vice versa. This paper identifies the root cause as 'tail performance degradation'—the model overfits on head classes while rapidly forgetting tail classes—and shows that standard optimization converges to sharp, non-robust minima rather than a shared flat solution beneficial for all classes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from long-tailed benchmarks (CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT at 32×32; ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist 2018 at 224×224) and outputs a single class label per image. It requires no external training samples, synthetic data, or pre-trained models—only the standard imbalanced training set. Evaluation reports Top-1 accuracy overall and separately on Many/Medium/Few class splits to quantify the head-tail balance.

## Method

The framework reframes long-tailed learning as a continual learning task and introduces two jointly optimized branches. The Grouped Knowledge Preservation (GKP) module maintains a memory bank of per-class optimal encoder parameters, clusters classes into G groups via spectral clustering (Normalized Cuts), and applies an EWC-style Fisher-information penalty that preserves all other groups' parameters while training on the current group. The Grouped Sharpness Aware (GSA) module extends SAM by decomposing each group's gradient to subtract the head-dominated global gradient projection, yielding a group-specific perturbation direction with a group-size-adjusted radius that targets flat minima for tail classes. The two losses are combined with an epoch-scheduled adaptive weight α, and the entire pipeline operates on a standard CNN backbone (ResNet-32/50 or ResNeXt-50) with a linear classifier.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (r=100), the proposed method achieves 53.2% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest baselines BCL (51.9%), GBG (52.3%), and FeatRecon (52.5%). On CIFAR-10-LT (r=100), it reaches 86.3%, outperforming BCL (84.3%) and GBG (85.1%). On ImageNet-LT with ResNeXt-50, the method attains 58.9% overall accuracy (69.7% Many, 56.0% Med., 38.6% Few), exceeding GBG (58.7%) and FeatRecon (57.5%). On iNaturalist 2018, it achieves 74.4%, edging out LLM-AutoDA (74.2%) and Meta-CALA (74.0%) without requiring any external data or pre-trained models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 22 compared baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the primary counterpart BCL [75] (same backbone and loss), it gains +1.3% on CIFAR-100-LT (r=100), +3.8% on CIFAR-100-LT (r=10), and +1.4% on CIFAR-10-LT (r=100), with the improvement attributed solely to the GKP and GSA modules. On ImageNet-LT (ResNeXt-50), it surpasses GBG [28] by +0.2% overall and +0.5% on the Few split, and beats FeatRecon [63] by +1.4% overall and +0.8% on Few. Notably, it exceeds the LLM-based LLM-AutoDA [55] by +2.2% on CIFAR-100-LT (r=100) and +0.2% on iNaturalist 2018, despite using no external data or pre-trained models. On CIFAR-10-LT (r=10), it achieves 92.5%, narrowly beating Meta-CALA [72] (92.4%) and FeatRecon [63] (91.6%). The method also improves the head-tail balance: on CIFAR-100-LT (r=100), it achieves 67.3% Many, 54.9% Med., and 34.9% Few, simultaneously improving over BCL on all three groups.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes long-tailed classification through the lens of continual learning and loss landscape geometry, combining two previously separate research threads: Elastic Weight Consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) for knowledge preservation and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., 2020) for flat-minima seeking. The key conceptual departure is treating the head-to-tail training dynamic as an implicit forgetting problem, then applying a memory-based spectral clustering strategy to group classes by convergence similarity before applying EWC-style regularization and SAM-style perturbation per group. The gradient decomposition from Friendly SAM (Li et al., 2024) is repurposed to remove the head-dominated global gradient direction, yielding a group-specific perturbation that prior per-class or binary head/tail SAM variants did not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The loss landscape diagnostic (tail classes converge to sharp minima far from their own optimal point) provides a concrete, visualizable explanation for the long-standing seesaw dilemma that prior re-weighting and augmentation methods addressed only heuristically. The grouping strategy offers a practical middle ground between computationally prohibitive per-class preservation and overly coarse head/tail splits, and the framework's independence from external data or pre-trained models makes it applicable in privacy-sensitive settings like medical imaging. Open questions include sensitivity to the number of groups (G=4 was optimal in ablations but dataset-dependent), the cost of maintaining a per-class memory bank and computing feature quality each epoch, and whether the Fisher information approximation remains reliable at very large class counts (e.g., 1000+ classes in ImageNet-scale LT).
