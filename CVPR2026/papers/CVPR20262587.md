## Problem

Existing 3D perception systems rely on cameras or LiDAR, which fail under smoke, fog, and poor lighting, while specialized radar alternatives require bespoke ultra-wideband hardware and licensed spectrum. No prior system achieves high-fidelity 3D scene imaging using ubiquitous, license-exempt mmWave communication signals (5G/Wi-Fi) on commodity devices without dedicated sensing hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262587/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a window of N (typically 5) mmWave OFDM radio frames—each a 16×16 antenna-pair CIR measurement at 60 GHz with 1.2288 GHz bandwidth—projected into 64×64×32 Cartesian voxel grids (12 cm resolution), along with their known SE(3) poses from an IMU. It outputs a dense 3D voxel occupancy grid at the same 64×64×32 resolution and a corresponding 2D depth map, with an effective sensing range of 7 m and a 120°×60° field of view.

## Method

Rascene performs monostatic sensing by exploiting full-duplex operation of a phased-array mmWave device, where the IFFT of the per-subcarrier channel response yields a Channel Impulse Response whose taps directly encode object range, and antenna-pair beamforming weights resolve azimuth/elevation. A shared CNN encoder maps each sparse, multipath-corrupted radio frame to a latent feature volume and per-voxel confidence logits. The key fusion mechanism is a source-driven, confidence-weighted forward projection: each source voxel is warped into the reference frame via rigid transform and distributed to neighboring target voxels through an isotropic Gaussian kernel (radius R=2), with contribution weighted by (log(1+e^c))^η (η=3) to suppress low-reliability multipath echoes. A coarse-to-fine 3D decoder progressively densifies the fused representation, and task-specific heads predict the voxel grid and depth map. The network is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy (voxel) and L1 (depth) losses, summing over all frames as references.

## Results

On the within-dataset evaluation, Rascene with 5-frame fusion achieves AbsRel 9.4%, MAE 20.2 cm, CD 19.7 cm, and CDDiag 2.3%, the best overall performance among all baselines. Even with a single frame, Rascene (AbsRel 14.1%, MAE 32.9 cm, CD 31.6 cm) already outperforms the single-frame PanoRadar baseline on every metric. The multi-frame fusion yields a substantial improvement, reducing MAE by 38.6% (32.9→20.2 cm) and CD by 37.7% (31.6→19.7 cm) relative to single-frame inference. Cross-scene generalization on 8 unseen environments yields an average CDDiag of 2.3% (range 1.1%–3.7%), indicating stable geometric quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rascene is compared against two RF-based 3D imaging baselines: PanoRadar [27] (single-frame) and CartoRadar [28] (5-frame). Against PanoRadar at the same 1-frame setting, Rascene wins on all four reported metrics (AbsRel 14.1% vs 14.7%, MAE 32.9 vs 34.1 cm, CD 31.6 vs 32.2 cm, CDDiag 3.6% vs 3.8%), though the margins are modest. Against CartoRadar at the same 5-frame setting, Rascene achieves a clear win on voxel reconstruction: CD 19.7 cm vs 26.8 cm (26.5% reduction) and CDDiag 2.3% vs 3.1%. CartoRadar does not report depth metrics (AbsRel, MAE), so a full comparison on those axes is not possible. Notably, Rascene's multi-frame gain (AbsRel 14.1%→9.4%, a 33.3% relative reduction) is substantially larger than what CartoRadar achieves from 1 to 5 frames, validating the paper's confidence-aware spatially adaptive fusion module as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

Rascene departs from the dominant paradigm of RF sensing that relies on dedicated FMCW radar hardware requiring ultra-wideband spectrum licenses, and from bistatic sub-6 GHz Wi-Fi sensing systems constrained by narrow bandwidth and small antenna arrays. It repurposes the ISAC concept—reusing communication signals for sensing—by demonstrating that commodity mmWave OFDM devices (5G/Wi-Fi) can operate in full-duplex monostatic mode, enabling direct CIR-based ranging without separate Tx/Rx hardware. The multi-frame fusion module draws on multi-view 3D reconstruction ideas (warping, aggregation) but adapts them to the sparse, multipath-corrupted nature of RF observations through confidence-weighted forward projection rather than photometric or geometric consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that 3D scene imaging is feasible on existing communication hardware without dedicated sensors or spectrum, Rascene opens a pathway toward low-cost, scalable 3D perception for resource-constrained platforms (AR/VR headsets, home robots) and environments where optical sensors fail. Key limitations include the 7 m sensing range, 12 cm voxel resolution, indoor-only evaluation, and dependence on a custom full-duplex prototype rather than off-the-shelf devices. Open questions include outdoor performance, real-time operation at higher frame rates, scaling to larger scenes, and whether the monostatic full-duplex capability generalizes across commercial mmWave chipsets without custom hardware.
