## Problem

Existing generative ZSL methods optimize the generator independently of the downstream classifier, producing task-agnostic synthesized features, and rely solely on semantic prototypes that cause inter-class overlap for semantically similar but visually distinct categories (e.g., different bunting species). No prior work has leveraged reinforcement learning to directly incentivize the generative model toward task-relevant synthesis in the ZSL setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes d-dimensional visual features extracted from the [CLS] token of a fine-tuned ViT encoder, conditioned on semantic prototypes that are either expert-annotated attribute vectors (312-dim for CUB, 102-dim for SUN, 85-dim for AWA2) or CLIP text embeddings of class names. It outputs synthesized visual features for unseen classes (400 per class on CUB/SUN, 4000 on AWA2), which are then fed to a standard softmax classifier to produce class labels. At inference, no additional tricks are applied beyond the frozen generator.

## Method

RLVC treats a diffusion-based adversarial generator G_θ as an RL policy and introduces a frozen linear classifier R as a reward model that outputs the log-probability of the correct class as an outcome reward. An EMA baseline over mini-batch rewards computes a stop-gradient advantage that scales the policy gradient, while a cosine-similarity prototype-distillation loss aligns synthesized features with class-wise visual prototypes (mean fine-tuned features per seen class) to stabilize training and inject visual information. A cold-start schedule disables RL for initial epochs (30 for CUB/SUN, 7 for AWA2) until basic class separability is achieved, after which the generator is updated via an alternating mechanism that separately applies the adversarial+distillation loss and the RL loss to avoid gradient conflict. The visual encoder is jointly fine-tuned with the reward model to mitigate cross-dataset bias.

## Results

RLVC achieves the best CZSL accuracy on all three benchmarks: 90.1% on CUB, 77.7% on SUN, and 84.0% on AWA2, surpassing the previous best generative method VADS by 3.1%, 1.4%, and 1.5% respectively. In the GZSL setting, RLVC attains the highest harmonic mean H on every dataset (81.2 on CUB, 57.6 on SUN, 80.4 on AWA2), improving over the second-best methods (VSPCN, VADS, PSVMA+) by 5.5%, 1.5%, and 0.6% respectively. The method uses a ViT backbone fine-tuned with DINOv2-style features and a diffusion-based generator with RL-based policy updates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLVC outperforms all 26 compared baselines on CZSL accuracy across all three datasets, with the largest margin over the previous best generative method VADS (86.8% → 90.1% on CUB, +3.3%) and over the best embedding method VSPCN (80.6% → 90.1%, +9.5%). In GZSL, RLVC achieves the highest harmonic mean on every dataset, beating VSPCN by 5.5% on CUB (81.2 vs 75.7), VADS by 1.9% on SUN (57.6 vs 55.7), and PSVMA+ by 0.6% on AWA2 (80.4 vs 79.8). Notably, RLVC does not always achieve the best individual seen (S) or unseen (U) accuracy on every dataset, but it balances them more effectively, yielding the top H score. The VLM-based method SHIP achieves an extreme 96.3% seen accuracy on AWA2 but collapses to 43.7% unseen, illustrating the seen-bias problem that RLVC's RL reward and visual cues mitigate. Among generative baselines, VADS is the closest competitor, while older methods like CE-GZSL, SC-EGG, and DSP trail by 10–20+ points on CUB CZSL accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the diffusion-based adversarial generative framework (Denoising Diffusion GANs [78] and ZeroDiff [87]) as its generator backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior generative ZSL methods by introducing outcome-reward reinforcement learning to align the generator with the downstream classifier. While existing approaches optimize the generator via adversarial losses conditioned solely on semantic prototypes—yielding task-agnostic features—RLVC treats the generator as an RL policy and uses a frozen linear classifier as a reward model to drive task-relevant synthesis. The visual-cue component (prototype distillation from fine-tuned seen-class features) draws on prototype learning frameworks, and the cold-start training schedule is inspired by RL post-training recipes in LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1). This is explicitly framed as the first application of RL to generative ZSL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a simple outcome-reward RL loop (linear reward model, EMA advantage, alternating updates) can substantially improve generative ZSL, the paper opens a new optimization paradigm for generative models in classification-adjacent tasks. The 4.7% average gain over prior SOTA and the consistent improvements across both attribute-vector and word-embedding prototypes suggest the approach is robust to semantic condition quality. However, the evaluation is limited to three standard ZSL benchmarks with a single ViT backbone, and the reward model is a simple linear layer—whether more expressive reward models or more complex policy optimization (e.g., PPO, GRPO) yield further gains remains open. The cold-start and alternating-update recipe may generalize to other generative tasks where distributional fidelity and task relevance must be jointly optimized.
