## Problem

Existing inference-time hallucination mitigation methods for multimodal LLMs operate in 2D pixel space (blurring, masking, patch noise) and cannot address the distinct failure modes of 3D embodied agents, where hallucinations stem from object presence errors, spatial layout misinterpretation, and geometric grounding failures rather than pixel-level inconsistencies. No training-free, inference-time contrastive decoding framework existed for suppressing hallucinations in object-centric 3D embodied MLLMs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language query (e.g., 'Is there a chair in the room?') paired with a structured 3D scene graph encoding each object's semantic category, 3D centroid coordinates, and spatial extent (width, height, depth). It produces an autoregressive textual response (binary Yes/No for object-presence queries or free-form natural language for task planning). The scene graph is the sole 3D perceptual input—no raw point clouds or multi-view images are fed to the MLLM—and the method requires two parallel forward passes per decoding step (original and distorted graph), adding roughly 0.25× latency over single-pass inference.

## Method

3D-VCD constructs a distorted copy of the object-centric 3D scene graph by applying controlled semantic perturbations (shuffling object category labels) and geometric perturbations (zero-mean Gaussian noise on centroids and extents), then runs the MLLM under both the original and distorted graphs in a batched dual forward pass. The two sets of next-token logits are fused via a contrastive penalty z_vcd = (1+α)z_original − αz_distorted, which suppresses tokens whose probabilities remain high under corrupted 3D evidence—indicating they are driven by language priors rather than grounded scene information. For the HEAL benchmark, where explicit graph perturbation is not applicable, the method reinterprets adversarial task prompts (distractor injection, scene-task contradictions) as the distorted context while keeping the scene graph fixed, preserving the same contrastive fusion principle. The entire procedure is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and introduces no new parameters, requiring only one additional forward pass per query with KV-caching to keep overhead minimal.

## Results

On 3D-POPE, 3D-VCD achieves the highest accuracy across all three splits: 67.99% (Random), 54.00% (Popular), and 54.92% (Adversarial), compared to 50.07%, 49.94%, and 49.94% for 3D-LLM respectively. The most striking improvement is the reduction in over-affirmation Yes-rate from 99.81% to 75.15% on the Random split, while F1 rises from 66.67 to 74.48. On HEAL (Distractor Injection), applying 3D-VCD to Qwen-14B-Instruct reduces state hallucination (CHAIR-C_S) from 16.45% to 5.00% (a 3.3× reduction) and object hallucination (CHAIR-C_O) from 4.13% to 3.55%. Inference overhead is minimal: dual-context decoding adds only ~0.5s per query (2.5s vs. 2.0s baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-VCD outperforms all three training-based baselines (3D-LLM, 3D-VisTA, LEO) on every metric across all three 3D-POPE splits. Against 3D-LLM, the most direct comparison (same backbone), 3D-VCD improves accuracy by 17.92 points on Random (67.99% vs. 50.07%) and 5.0–4.98 points on Popular/Adversarial, while cutting the Yes-rate from ~99.9% to 75–89%, indicating far less over-affirmation. Against 3D-VisTA and LEO, 3D-VCD leads by 15–18 points in accuracy on Random and 4–7 points on Popular/Adversarial, with F1 gains of 12–23 points. On HEAL, 3D-VCD applied to Qwen-14B-Instruct reduces state hallucination from 16.45% to 5.00% and object hallucination from 4.13% to 3.55%; for Llama-3-8B-Instruct, object hallucination improves (2.58%→2.39%) but state hallucination slightly increases (9.49%→12.43%), suggesting the method's benefit is model-dependent in the state-hallucination dimension. All baselines require training on 3D-GRAND, whereas 3D-VCD is entirely training-free and model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) from 2D vision-language models, where pixel-space corruptions (blurring, masking) are used to suppress language-prior-driven tokens, to the 3D embodied setting by replacing pixel perturbations with structured scene-graph distortions (category shuffling, centroid/extent noise, relation flipping). It builds on the 3D-LLM architecture and 3D-GRAND scene-graph format as the substrate for these perturbations, and positions itself against training-based 3D grounding models (3D-LLM, 3D-VisTA, LEO) that offer no inference-time mitigation. The key conceptual departure is treating hallucination as insensitivity to 3D structural evidence rather than pixel-level inconsistency, which the authors argue is the correct failure mode for embodied agents operating over multi-view, geometry-rich observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, architecture-agnostic decoding trick with ~0.5s overhead, 3D-VCD is immediately deployable on existing 3D-MLLM pipelines without retraining, which is practically valuable for embodied systems where retraining is costly and scene diversity is unbounded. However, evaluation is limited to binary yes/no presence questions (3D-POPE) and a single HEAL subset (Distractor Injection), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to open-ended spatial reasoning, multi-step planning, or dynamic scenes. The mixed HEAL results (Llama-3-8B state hallucination actually worsens from 9.49% to 12.43%) suggest the contrastive signal may not uniformly help across model families. Future work on temporal reasoning in dynamic 3D scenes and broader task coverage would strengthen the case for this as a general-purpose embodied reliability mechanism.
