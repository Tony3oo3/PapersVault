## Problem

Existing multimodal reaction generation methods are restricted to dyadic interactions, produce only speaking (not listening) responses, or rely on future context, making them unsuitable for online, causal generation in realistic multi-person social settings. No prior framework jointly generates speech, body motion, and turn-taking behavior for a target participant in a polyadic group while operating solely on past observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261740/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes past speech (transcribed text and 256-dim StyleTTS 2 style vectors over ~20.5 s) and 3D body/hand motions (327-dim, ~2.56 s) from all P participants, plus 6-dim head orientation vectors for social cue computation. Rather than feeding motions sequentially (which would exhaust LLM context), the pose fusion module compresses all participants' motions into a single 3072-dim embedding. The output is the target participant's future text, 256-dim speech style, 327-dim body/hand motion, and a scalar speaking state score r that acts as a soft turn-taking cue without enforcing hard binary transitions.

## Method

PolySLGen uses Llama3-8B-Instruct (LoRA-finetuned, rank 16) as a reasoning backbone, with three key modules handling group dynamics: (1) a pose fusion module built as a hierarchical transformer (self-attention for intra-participant dynamics, cross-attention for inter-participant dependencies, MLP projection) that compresses all P participants' motions into one fixed-length LLM-compatible embedding; (2) a social cue encoder that computes per-frame cosine similarity between each non-target participant's head orientation and their relative position vector to the target, then aggregates these temporal signals via a multi-stage MLP into n=2 embeddings; and (3) a speech style adapter projecting StyleTTS 2 features into LLM space. A lightweight MLP on the first LLM output embedding predicts the speaking state score. Training uses a weighted multi-task loss combining text cross-entropy, style MSE, state BCE, and a composite motion loss (representation, 3D keypoint, root position, temporal smoothness, and ground-contact regularization), all trained end-to-end on the DnD Group Gesture dataset with 5 participants.

## Results

On the DnD Group Gesture dataset, PolySLGen achieves the best motion quality with Root error of 108.7 mm, MPJPE of 144.9 mm, FID of 12.18, and BeatAlign Diff. of 0.007, outperforming all baselines. For speech, it attains the highest BERTScore (0.508) and lowest WER (1.436), while the speaking state score prediction reaches 0.67 AP, substantially above the ~0.50 random-chance level of all baselines. In the user study, PolySLGen is preferred over SOLAMI across all five perceptual dimensions, with the largest gap in motion continuity (3.8 vs. 2.4 on a 5-point scale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolySLGen outperforms SOLAMI (the strongest direct competitor) on all motion metrics (Root 108.7 vs. 188.6 mm; MPJPE 144.9 vs. 180.9 mm; FID 12.18 vs. 14.86) and on speech semantics (BERTScore 0.508 vs. 0.428; WER 1.436 vs. 1.854), though SOLAMI achieves a higher speaker similarity (SIM 0.745 vs. 0.642) because it reuses test-set audio for speech synthesis. Against LLM+ConvoFusion, PolySLGen wins decisively on speech quality (BERTScore 0.508 vs. 0.388; WER 1.436 vs. 13.318) and motion accuracy (Root 108.7 vs. 125.7 mm), while LLM+ConvoFusion's motion diversity (72.45) is far below ground truth, indicating mode collapse. The adapted LM-L2L baseline performs worst on motion (Root 185.2 mm), confirming that dyadic facial-reaction methods do not transfer well to polyadic full-body settings. On speaking state prediction, all baselines hover at the random-chance level (~0.50 AP), whereas PolySLGen reaches 0.67 AP, a margin the authors attribute to the pose fusion and social cue encoder providing informative non-verbal context for turn-taking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reaction generation line of work (SOLAMI, LM-L2L, ConvoFusion) along three axes simultaneously: from dyadic to polyadic interactions, from offline to online (causal) generation, and from speaking-only to unified speaking-and-listening reactions with explicit turn-taking. Architecturally, it repurposes a pre-trained LLM (Llama3-8B-Instruct with LoRA) as an interaction-reasoning backbone, adding a novel pose fusion module (hierarchical self-attention + cross-attention) to compress multi-person motion into a fixed-length embedding and a social cue encoder that encodes head-orientation-based attention signals from non-target participants. The closest prior work, SOLAMI, handles only dyadic speaking reactions and defaults to a static pose during listening, whereas PolySLGen generates contextually grounded listening motions and a soft speaking-state score for turn-taking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying speech, body motion, and turn-taking in a single online polyadic framework, PolySLGen provides a practical architecture for embodied AI agents that must participate naturally in group conversations rather than merely responding to a single interlocutor. Key limitations temper the impact: evaluation is confined to a single tabletop RPG dataset (DnD Group Gesture), inference runs at ~5 FPS with 82% of runtime consumed by the LLM (not yet real-time), and the social cue encoder contributes only marginal gains without the pose fusion module. Open questions include whether the fixed-length embedding strategy scales to groups larger than five, whether the speaking-state score generalizes to more naturalistic (non-scripted) interactions, and whether LLM inference can be optimized sufficiently for deployment on edge hardware.
