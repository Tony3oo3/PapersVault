## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting renders faster than NeRF, training still requires tens of thousands of splatting operations, limiting its use in time-sensitive applications. Existing acceleration strategies either reduce the total Gaussian count (impractical for complex scenes) or replace the optimizer, but none directly shorten the per-pixel Gaussian lists that govern both forward rendering and backward gradient computation costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261868/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple posed RGB images with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processed in epochs of 200 randomly sampled images (with replacement for smaller scenes) over 150 epochs (30K total iterations). It outputs a standard 3D Gaussian radiance field (position, scale, rotation, opacity, spherical-harmonic color per Gaussian) that can be rendered to novel views. Training is built on the LiteGS codebase with 8×8 rasterization tiles and a coarse-to-fine resolution scheduler that progressively increases rendering resolution from a downsampling factor of up to 4 down to 1.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary mechanisms to shorten per-pixel Gaussian lists. First, scale reset periodically multiplies all Gaussian scales by a shrinking factor ζ=0.2 every 20 epochs, immediately reducing each Gaussian's spatial footprint so it contributes to fewer neighboring pixels. Second, an entropy constraint is applied to the alpha-blending weights w_i = T_i·α_i along each ray; since these weights naturally sum to 1 (including the background residual), minimizing their entropy sharpens the distribution—driving dominant weights larger and minor weights smaller—so each Gaussian concentrates its influence on the pixels where it is most relevant. The entropy gradient is computed in O(N) per pixel via a backward sweep over the Gaussian list (Algorithm 1), and the constraint is applied every other epoch with weight γ=0.015. Both techniques are integrated with DashGaussian's progressive resolution scheduler, using a stage-adaptive strategy that applies weaker regularization during early coarse-resolution stages to preserve scene structure before tightening constraints at full resolution.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF 360 (30K iterations, 3.3M Gaussians), the method achieves 27.28 dB PSNR, 0.810 SSIM, and 0.224 LPIPS in 99.58 s of training time, compared to 3DGS's 27.55 dB / 919.51 s and LiteGS's 27.75 dB / 191.17 s. On Deep Blending it reaches 29.41 dB PSNR in 80.68 s (vs. 3DGS's 29.74 dB / 963.66 s), and on Tanks & Temples 23.34 dB PSNR in 106.06 s (vs. 3DGS's 23.77 dB / 560.52 s). The headline speedups over 3DGS are 9.2×, 11.9×, and 5.3× on the three datasets respectively, with a ~50% speedup over the LiteGS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the fastest training time across all three datasets while incurring modest quality trade-offs (≈0.3–0.5 dB PSNR, ≈0.01 SSIM) relative to the strongest baselines. Against 3DGS (Kerbl et al.), it is 9.2× faster on Mip-NeRF 360 (99.58 s vs. 919.51 s) with a 0.27 dB PSNR drop. Against Taming-3DGS (Mallick et al.), it is 4.0× faster (99.58 s vs. 402.54 s) but loses 0.57 dB PSNR. Against DashGaussian (Chen et al.), it is 2.2× faster (99.58 s vs. 218.85 s) with a 0.56 dB PSNR gap. Against LiteGS (Liao et al.), the closest baseline, it is ~48% faster (99.58 s vs. 191.17 s) at a 0.47 dB PSNR cost. Against Mini-Splatting2-Dense (Fang et al.), it is 2.2× faster (99.58 s vs. 220.22 s) but trails by 0.28 dB PSNR. Against FastGS (Ren et al.) under its 0.4M-Gaussian setting, it is 1.4× faster (66.32 s vs. 88.55 s) with a 0.93 dB PSNR gap. The method consistently wins on training time across all eight baselines, with the quality gap remaining within ~0.5 dB PSNR for the most competitive comparisons (3DGS, Taming-3DGS, DashGaussian, LiteGS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS training pipeline (Kerbl et al.) by adding two lightweight regularization mechanisms—periodic scale reset and an entropy constraint on alpha-blending weights—on top of the LiteGS high-performance framework and DashGaussian's progressive resolution scheduler. Unlike prior acceleration work that reduces total Gaussian count (Mini-Splatting2, Speedy-Splat), employs second-order optimizers (3DGS-LM, 3DGS²), or relies on data-driven priors, this method keeps the full Gaussian budget and instead shortens per-pixel Gaussian lists by encouraging spatial concentration of each Gaussian's contribution. The entropy constraint is conceptually distinct from prior opacity regularization (e.g., Dai et al.) because it operates on the composite blending weights, which naturally form a probability distribution, avoiding the need for explicit normalization and preserving the streaming computation model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~9× speedup over vanilla 3DGS and ~50% over LiteGS, achieved with only ~0.3 dB PSNR degradation, makes 3DGS training practical for time-sensitive applications like real-time capture and large-scale scene reconstruction. The techniques are orthogonal to other acceleration strategies (Gaussian pruning, better optimizers, CUDA-level optimizations), so they can be stacked for further gains. A key open question is whether the fixed schedule (scale reset every 20 epochs, ζ=0.2) generalizes to scenes with vastly different geometric complexity, and whether the entropy constraint's benefit diminishes when combined with aggressive Gaussian pruning that already shortens lists. The modest quality trade-off suggests a fundamental tension between list length and representational flexibility that remains unresolved.
