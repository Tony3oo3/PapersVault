## Problem

Modular table recognition pipelines that separately model structure and content suffer from suboptimal integration and error accumulation, while existing end-to-end approaches rely heavily on large-scale annotated table data and lack explicit spatial correspondence (cell locations). This paper addresses both limitations by reducing dependence on large-scale TR data and unifying table recognition with cell localization in a single framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB table image as input, resized so that both dimensions are multiples of 256 with the longer side capped at 2048 pixels. It produces a structured HTML sequence that jointly encodes table structure and cell content, along with continuous normalized bounding boxes for every cell (including empty and borderless cells). Unlike autoregressive coordinate generation, cell boxes are regressed in parallel via a dedicated localization module, ensuring one-to-one correspondence with the generated HTML cells without post-processing.

## Method

TDATR follows a 'perceive-then-fuse' two-stage training strategy. In the perception stage, the model is pretrained on a suite of table detail-aware tasks—spatially ordered text spotting, box-query text spotting, Markdown parsing, cell/span/row-column detection, and table structure parsing—all under a unified next-token prediction paradigm, which allows leveraging diverse document data beyond table-specific corpora. In the fusion stage, the model fine-tunes on HTML table parsing while jointly training a structure-guided cell localization (SGCL) module. The SGCL module extracts cell-level representations from multi-layer decoder hidden states, applies structure masks derived from row/column adjacency to guide bidirectional attention, regresses initial boxes via an MLP, and refines them through DAB-DETR decoder layers conditioned on multi-resolution Swin Transformer features. The architecture comprises a 300M Swin Transformer encoder, a 300M Transformer decoder (6 self-attention + 3 cross-attention blocks), and the SGCL module, all trained with a combined loss of cross-entropy, box regression, IoU, mask alignment, and structure-guided terms.

## Results

On real-world benchmarks, TDATR achieves TEDS of 93.22 on iFLYTAB-full and 92.70 on TabRecSet, outperforming the best modular TR system (SEMv3+PPOCR, 77.40) by 15.82 points and the best end-to-end baseline (EDD, 70.70) by 22.0 points. On digitally-born tables, TDATR reaches TEDS of 97.97 on PubTables-1M and 95.12 on PubTabNet, surpassing Dolphin (95.48 and 91.30 respectively) and achieving the lowest TEDS-Delta (0.42 on PubTables-1M, 1.15 on PubTabNet). On unseen-domain OCR benchmarks, TDATR attains SOTA TEDS among expert OCR-VLMs on CC-OCR (84.19) and OCRBench v2 (87.36), while remaining competitive on OmniDocBench 1.5 (87.96). For cell localization, TDATR achieves AP50 of 94.37 on iFLYTAB-full and 91.80 on PubTabNet, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TDATR decisively outperforms all end-to-end TR baselines: on TabRecSet it surpasses EDD [68] by 22.0 points in TEDS (92.70 vs. 70.70), and on PubTabNet it leads Dolphin [10] by 3.82 points (95.12 vs. 91.30) and OmniParser [48] by 6.29 points (95.12 vs. 88.83). Against modular TR systems, TDATR exceeds SEMv3+PPOCR [40] by 15.82 points on iFLYTAB-full (93.22 vs. 77.40) and TableFormer+GT [33] by 1.52 points on PubTabNet (95.12 vs. 93.60). Among expert OCR-VLMs, TDATR leads DeepSeek-OCR [54] by 8.86 points and PaddleOCR-VL [8] by 11.74 points on iFLYTAB-full, and outperforms MinerU2.5 [35] by 4.43 points on CC-OCR (84.19 vs. 79.76) and by 0.23 points on OCRBench v2 (87.36 vs. 87.13). However, TDATR trails PaddleOCR-VL [8] on OmniDocBench 1.5 (87.96 vs. 91.95) and MinerU2.5 [35] on the same benchmark (87.96 vs. 90.05), suggesting slightly weaker generalization to that particular document parsing distribution. On cell localization (AP50), TDATR leads UniTabNet [65] by 5.94 points on iFLYTAB-full (94.37 vs. 88.43) and by 2.13 points on PubTabNet (91.80 vs. 89.67), and outperforms the autoregressive ED Loc Gen variant by 0.85 and 1.54 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TDATR extends the end-to-end table recognition line initiated by EDD, Dolphin, and OmniParser, which unify structure and content generation in a single vision-language model, but departs from their reliance on large-scale TR-specific training data by introducing a 'perceive-then-fuse' two-stage strategy. The architecture builds on the Donut-style Swin Transformer encoder–Transformer decoder backbone and adapts DAB-DETR for cell localization by replacing its bipartite matching with structure-prior-initialized anchors derived from the language decoder's hidden states. The structure-guided cell localization module is a conceptual departure from both autoregressive coordinate generation (TabPedia, mPLUG-DocOwl) and simple regression from representation tokens (UniTabNet), instead exploiting row/column adjacency masks from the decoder to guide bidirectional attention before multi-resolution visual refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'perceive-then-fuse' paradigm directly addresses the data-scarcity bottleneck in TR by decoupling table-detail perception (learned from diverse document corpora) from table-specific sequence modeling (learned from limited TR data), a strategy the authors argue is transferable to other structured document understanding tasks. The structure-guided cell localization adds spatial interpretability to end-to-end TR outputs, a capability most prior E2E-TR methods lack. Limitations include continued dependence on large-scale diverse document pretraining data, and the approach is validated primarily on table recognition; whether the paradigm generalizes to other structured layouts (forms, scientific figures) remains open. The model also slightly underperforms dedicated TSR models on digitally-born tables when trained on reduced data, suggesting the full benefit requires sufficient fine-tuning data.
