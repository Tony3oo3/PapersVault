## Problem

Existing human amodal completion (HAC) methods hallucinate occluded regions without letting users specify constraints such as a desired pose or spatial extent, forcing repeated sampling until a satisfactory result appears. Pose-guided person image synthesis (PGPIS) methods accept explicit pose conditioning but fail to preserve the instance-specific visible appearance, instead producing outputs biased toward the training distribution. The paper introduces promptable human amodal completion (PHAC), a new task that simultaneously satisfies visible-appearance preservation and multiple user-specified prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262165/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an occluded RGB human image and a lightweight point-based user prompt as input. For pose prompts, the user adds only the missing joints (visible joints are auto-detected via OpenPose); for bounding-box prompts, the user selects two corner points. Five prompt types are supported (pose-only, interest-region bbox, entire-region bbox, and two composites), which are rendered into a prompt image fed to a prompt-specific ControlNet. The visible mask is predicted by SAM rather than provided as ground truth. The output is a single complete human image that preserves the visible regions pixel-faithfully while aligning with the specified pose and spatial extent.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In coarse image generation, a prompt-specific ControlNet encodes the user prompt image into a conditioning signal injected into a pre-trained latent diffusion U-Net; only the cross-attention blocks of the U-Net are fine-tuned (following SITH) to achieve strong prompt alignment while preserving the generative prior. In the refinement stage, a lightweight U-Net predicts an invisible-region mask from the coarse completion, which is then dilated to cover boundary pixels. A baseline composite is constructed by copying visible pixels from the input and invisible pixels from the coarse completion; low-magnitude noise (controlled by parameter s, optimal at 0.5) is added only within the dilated mask, and a few denoising steps of an inpainting model refine the masked region while leaving unmasked pixels unchanged, eliminating boundary artifacts. This refinement module is plug-and-play and can be applied to other diffusion-based completion methods.

## Results

On the synthetic OccThuman2.0 test set, PHAC achieves LPIPS 49.47, KID 6.12, PSNR 25.86, and joint error 23.33, outperforming the strongest baseline SDHDO (LPIPS 81.39, KID 16.41, joint error 43.49) by a wide margin. On the real AHP test set, PHAC reaches LPIPS 38.77, KID 1.25, PSNR 26.93, and joint error 6.37, again surpassing all baselines. The refinement module contributes substantially: removing it degrades LPIPS from 38.77 to 64.40 and KID from 1.25 to 2.64 on AHP, confirming its role in preserving visible appearance and reducing boundary artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHAC outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among PGPIS methods, PIDM [3] is the weakest, with joint errors of 113.72 (OccThuman2.0) and 40.47 (AHP), reflecting severe pose misalignment and appearance hallucinations (e.g., transforming an older man into a white woman). MCLD [28] improves pose alignment (joint error 53.38 / 11.53) but still degrades visible appearance due to training-distribution bias from DeepFashion. Among amodal completion baselines, pix2gestalt [40] (without prompts) achieves the best LPIPS among non-prompt methods (90.11 / 75.73) but produces blurry outputs and ignores pose constraints; adding a ControlNet prompt (pix2gestalt†) yields only marginal gains. SDHDO [39] is the strongest single baseline, reaching LPIPS 81.39 and joint error 43.49 on OccThuman2.0, yet it still lags PHAC by ~39% in LPIPS and ~46% in joint error on that set, and by ~40% in LPIPS on AHP. The plug-and-play refinement module further narrows the gap: applying it to MCLD reduces KID by ~71% and MSE by ~60%, demonstrating its generality beyond the proposed pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of human amodal completion (HAC) and pose-guided person image synthesis (PGPIS), drawing on ControlNet for prompt injection, SDXL's inpainting pipeline for boundary refinement, and SITH's selective cross-attention fine-tuning strategy. Prior HAC methods (SDHDO, pix2gestalt) preserve visible appearance but cannot accept user-specified pose or spatial constraints, while PGPIS methods (PIDM, MCLD) accept pose conditioning but hallucinate training-distribution appearance rather than preserving the input person. PHAC departs from both lines by treating point-based user prompts (pose joints, bounding boxes) as first-class conditioning signals alongside the occluded input, and by adding an inpainting-based refinement stage that operates on the coarse diffusion output rather than retraining the decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play refinement module is the most broadly transferable contribution: applying it to existing PGPIS and HAC baselines yields 60–70% reductions in MSE/KID for MCLD, suggesting that boundary-artifact removal via low-noise inpainting is an underexploited post-processing step for any diffusion-based completion pipeline. However, the evaluation is limited to a synthetic training set (OccThuman2.0, 526 subjects) and a small real-world test set (AHP, 56 images), and prompts are restricted to 2D pose and axis-aligned bounding boxes; extending to 3D cues, multi-person scenes, or free-form spatial prompts remains open. The large quantitative gaps over baselines partly reflect that prior methods solve a different (less constrained or less appearance-preserving) sub-problem, so direct comparability is limited.
