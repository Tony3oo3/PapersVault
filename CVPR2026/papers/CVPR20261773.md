## Problem

Current diffusion-based 3D texture generation systems suffer from cross-view inconsistency because unstructured full attention is applied indiscriminately across noise, geometry-condition, and reference-image tokens. This produces two failure modes: geometrically similar but distinct regions (e.g., left vs. right limbs) attend to each other across views, and noise tokens alternately overfit to the reference image or the geometry condition, yielding seams and misaligned textures after reprojection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261773/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an untextured 3D mesh and a single reference image (a randomly rendered view of the mesh) as input. The mesh is rendered into normal maps and canonical coordinate maps from six predefined viewpoints at 768×768 resolution, which are averaged and VAE-encoded into a geometry-condition latent; the reference image is similarly encoded and replicated across the six-view batch. The output is a 3D albedo texture map obtained by back-projecting the six generated views onto the mesh surface and inpainting occluded regions.

## Method

CaliTex reformulates attention in a two-stage diffusion transformer (initialized from FLUX.1-Kontext with a rank-16 LoRA) to be explicitly geometry-aware. The Single-View DiT stage uses standard batch-wise full attention to capture intra-view semantics. The Multi-View DiT stage introduces two modules: (1) Part-Aligned Attention, which uses PartField to segment the mesh into K=20 semantic parts, renders part-colored images to assign each latent token a part label, and restricts cross-view attention to tokens sharing the same part while preserving intra-view full attention; and (2) Condition-Routed Attention, which replaces free noise–reference interaction with a dual-pathway design where a condition–reference branch fuses geometric and visual priors, and a noise–condition branch injects those geometry-aware features back into generation, preventing direct reference copying. The model is trained with a flow-matching objective on 80k objects from Objaverse-XL and Texverse over ~600 GPU hours on 8 GPUs.

## Results

On a test set of Objaverse and game-asset meshes rendered from 32 viewpoints, CaliTex achieves the best FID of 157.8 (vs. 167.4 for Hunyuan3D-2.1, the next-best open-source baseline), the lowest CLIP-FID of 12.85, the lowest CMMD of 0.672, the highest CLIP-I of 0.9106, and the lowest LPIPS of 0.2508. In a 25-participant user study, CaliTex receives substantially higher scores on all three perceptual dimensions—overall quality (4.53), geometric alignment (4.47), and multi-view consistency (4.52)—compared to the next-best method UniTEX (3.10, 3.21, 3.15). The ablation on MV-MSE confirms that both Part-Aligned Attention and Condition-Routed Attention contribute to reducing cross-view pixel inconsistency (0.0384 vs. 0.0415 and 0.0403 when removed).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaliTex outperforms all four open-source baselines on every quantitative metric. Against the closest competitor MV-Adapter, it improves FID by 11.6 points (157.8 vs. 169.4), CLIP-FID by 0.76, CMMD by 0.075, CLIP-I by 0.013, and LPIPS by 0.043. Against Hunyuan3D-2.1, the FID gap is 9.6 points and the user-study quality score jumps from 2.48 to 4.53, indicating a large perceptual advantage. UniTEX, while competitive on CLIP-I (0.8818), lags significantly on FID (176.2) and LPIPS (0.3335), and its multi-view consistency score (3.15) is well below CaliTex's 4.52, reflecting visible seams in its outputs. Step1X-3D is the weakest baseline across all metrics (FID 254.1, user-study scores around 2.0). Qualitatively, the paper also reports that CaliTex surpasses the commercial Hunyuan3D-3.0 in preserving fine details and avoiding geometry misalignment, though no quantitative numbers for that comparison are provided.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaliTex builds directly on FLUX.1-Kontext as its diffusion backbone, following the two-stage (single-view then multi-view DiT) paradigm established by UniTEX and similar multi-view texture generation systems. Its key departure is from the prevailing assumption in methods like UniTEX, MV-Adapter, and Hunyuan3D that indiscriminate full attention across all noise, condition, and reference tokens will naturally learn correct cross-view and cross-modal correspondences. Instead, CaliTex explicitly constrains attention using geometric priors: PartField-based semantic part grouping for cross-view alignment and a dual-pathway routing design that mediates reference-to-noise information flow through geometry tokens. This positions the work as an architectural intervention on the attention mechanism rather than a new training objective or data pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing cross-view inconsistency as an attention-ambiguity problem solvable at the architecture level, CaliTex offers a principled alternative to post-hoc regularization or iterative refinement approaches (e.g., Elevate3D's geometry modification, Romantex's 3D-aware RoPE). The practical impact is significant for production 3D content pipelines where seamless, geometry-aligned textures are essential. Open questions include whether the fixed K=20 part decomposition generalizes to highly complex or non-rigid assets, whether the six-view constraint limits applicability, and whether the reliance on PartField segmentation quality becomes a bottleneck for novel object categories. The approach also raises the broader question of how much geometric structure can be injected into attention without sacrificing the generative flexibility that makes diffusion models powerful.
