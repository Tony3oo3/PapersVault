## Problem

VAR's full-context dependency—requiring attention over all previous scales for next-scale prediction—introduces three compounding challenges: superlinear computational cost from cumulative multi-scale modeling, continuous error accumulation along the autoregressive chain, and cross-scale interference that suppresses distinctive scale-specific representation learning. The paper asks whether a visual autoregressive model can be reformulated without full-context dependency while simultaneously improving both generation quality and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263720/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet-1K class label as the sole conditioning signal and generates RGB images at 256×256, 512×512, or 1024×1024 resolution in a coarse-to-fine manner across T multi-scale residual features. Image tokenization uses the pre-trained multi-scale VQ-VAE tokenizer from VAR, and generation requires only 10 sampling steps (one per scale). The architecture employs LLaMA-style attention and MLP blocks with Rotary Positional Embedding, and critically requires no KV-cache during inference due to its Markovian structure.

## Method

Markov-VAR reformulates next-scale prediction as Markovian scale prediction, where each scale is a Markov state and prediction depends only on the current state rather than all previous scales. To compensate for the historical information lost by dropping full-context dependency, a sliding window of size N (optimal N=3) stores the most recent N scales, which are compressed into a fixed-dimensional history vector via cross-attention with a learnable global query. This history vector is broadcast and concatenated element-wise with the current scale's embedded feature to form a representative dynamic state that evolves under a Markov process. Training uses teacher-forcing with a Markovian attention mask restricting each scale to attend only to its own state, and the loss is standard cross-entropy over predicted residual features. The elimination of full-context attention removes the need for KV-cache entirely, reducing peak memory by up to 83.8% at 1024×1024 while improving FID by 10.5% over VAR at 256×256.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Markov-VAR-d24 (1.02B) achieves FID 2.15, IS 310.9, Precision 0.83, and Recall 0.59, outperforming VAR-d24 (FID 2.17) at comparable scale. At the smaller d16 size, Markov-VAR reduces FID from 3.61 to 3.23 (10.5% improvement) and IS from 225.6 to 256.2 (13.6% improvement) over VAR-d16. On efficiency, Markov-VAR-d24 reduces peak GPU memory from 117.9 GB to 19.1 GB (83.8% reduction) at 1024×1024 resolution, and achieves 1.33× faster inference than FlexVAR at 256×256.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VAR at matched depth, Markov-VAR consistently wins: d16 improves FID by 10.5% (3.61→3.23) and IS by 13.6% (225.6→256.2); d24 edges out VAR-d24 on FID (2.15 vs 2.17) and IS (310.9 vs 271.9). Among VAR-like variants, Markov-VAR-d24 beats FlexVAR-d24 (FID 2.15 vs 2.21) and NestAR-H (2.15 vs 2.22), but trails HMAR-d24 (2.15 vs 2.10) and M-VAR-d20 (2.44 vs 2.41 at d20, though Markov-VAR uses only 70% of M-VAR's parameters). In the broader paradigm comparison (Table 2), Markov-VAR-d24 outperforms DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.15 vs 2.27), StyleGAN-XL (2.15 vs 2.30), and LlamaGen-L at similar parameter count (2.44 vs 3.81 for d20), while requiring only 10 sampling steps versus 250 for diffusion models. The key efficiency advantage is that Markov-VAR eliminates KV-cache entirely, reducing peak memory by up to 83.8% at 1024×1024 compared to VAR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on VAR (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024), which introduced next-scale prediction as a replacement for next-token prediction in visual autoregressive generation. The key departure is replacing VAR's full-context dependency—where each scale attends to all previous scales—with a Markovian formulation where each scale depends only on the immediately preceding one, justified via the sufficient-statistic principle from information theory. A sliding-window history compensation mechanism (compressing the last N scales into a fixed-dimensional vector via cross-attention) addresses the information loss from dropping full context. The work also positions itself relative to recent Markov-based VAR variants (HMAR, MVAR, FlexVAR) and chain-based Markov modeling in diffusion and reasoning (MARCOS, MCoT), arguing that prior Markov-VAR approaches either increased inference steps or lacked comprehensive scaling validation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 83.8% peak memory reduction at 1024×1024 (117.9 GB → 19.1 GB) removes a major practical barrier to scaling VAR to high-resolution generation, and the finding that this efficiency gain comes with a 10.5% FID improvement challenges the working assumption that full-context attention is necessary for quality. As a publicly released foundation model with demonstrated scaling-law behavior (R² > 0.99), Markov-VAR provides a lightweight base for future work on text-to-image, video, and 3D generation. Open questions include whether the Markov assumption degrades at very high resolutions or for more complex conditional tasks, and how the sliding-window size should adapt across different generation domains.
