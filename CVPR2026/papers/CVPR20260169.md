## Problem

Existing text-to-motion (T2M) datasets like HumanML3D and Motion-X are small, lack diversity, and suffer from significant train/validation text overlap (10–17% word-for-word leakage), causing reported metrics to reflect overfitting rather than true generalization. Additionally, current 1D VQ-VAE motion tokenizers lose spatial information by encoding the whole body with a single embedding, limiting reconstruction quality and zero-shot transfer as dataset scale grows.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural-language text description (e.g., 'Wave with left hand, then wave with right hand. Do a low kick.') and produces a 3D human motion sequence as a sequence of discrete part-level tokens. The token structure is 2D: 5 body parts (left arm, left leg, torso, right leg, right arm) × temporal tokens (downsampled by ratio n/T) × residual quantization layers, delimited by special <part> and <mot> tokens within an LLM vocabulary. The dataset provides second-wise text annotations synthesized into coherent per-clip descriptions, with an average sequence length of 10.1 seconds.

## Method

The paper introduces two coupled contributions. First, OpenT2M is a 1M+ sequence, 2800+ hour dataset curated from web videos through an RL-based physically feasible validation (tracking policy trained on AMASS), multi-granularity filtering (keypoint count, bounding-box ratio, minimum duration), second-wise text annotation via Gemini-2.5-pro, and automated long-horizon sequence synthesis with orientation-aligned interpolation. Second, 2D-PRQ is a motion tokenizer that splits the body into 5 parts and treats the motion sequence as a 2D image (time as width, body parts as height), encoding with 2D convolutions to capture both temporal and inter-part spatial dependencies, then quantizing via residual quantization with a shared codebook. The MonoFrill model is a standard autoregressive LLM (GPT2-medium, LLaMA2-7B, or LLaMA3-8B) with expanded vocabulary for motion tokens, trained in two stages: tokenizer pretraining on OpenT2M followed by motion instruction tuning, requiring no architectural modifications beyond vocabulary expansion.

## Results

On the zero-shot OpenT2M_zero benchmark (12K held-out motions), MonoFrill-2D-PRQ4 trained on OpenT2M achieves R@1=0.240, R@2=0.399, R@3=0.512, FID=1.475, and DIV=7.563, substantially outperforming all baselines trained on the same data (MDM: R@1=0.194, FID=8.153; T2M-GPT: R@1=0.159, FID=5.566; Being-M0: R@1=0.155, FID=5.811). Models trained on HumanML3D or Motion-X collapse to R@1≈0.05–0.07 and FID>46, confirming severe out-of-domain failure. For motion reconstruction, 2D-PRQ6 reduces MPJPE from 95.743 (PRQ4) to 37.922 on OpenT2M and from 73.989 to 48.099 on Motion-X, while matching PRQ6 on the smaller HumanML3D (25.417 vs. 25.485).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MonoFrill against three representative T2M baselines—MDM (diffusion), T2M-GPT (VQ-VAE + GPT-2), and Being-M0 (RQ-VAE + LLM)—each trained on HumanML3D, Motion-X, or OpenT2M. On the zero-shot OpenT2M_zero benchmark, MonoFrill-2D-PRQ4 trained on OpenT2M wins on every metric: R@1 of 0.240 vs. 0.194 (MDM), 0.159 (T2M-GPT), and 0.155 (Being-M0), with FID of 1.475 vs. 8.153, 5.566, and 5.811 respectively. All baselines trained on HumanML3D or Motion-X suffer catastrophic OOD degradation (R@1 drops to 0.05–0.07, FID exceeds 46), while OpenT2M-trained versions of every baseline improve by 3–4× in R@1, confirming the dataset's generalization benefit is model-agnostic. For the tokenizer comparison (Table 6), 2D-PRQ4 outperforms PRQ4 on large-scale data (MPJPE 49.134 vs. 95.743 on OpenT2M; 54.493 vs. 73.989 on Motion-X) but shows only marginal gains on the small HumanML3D (28.628 vs. 28.703), indicating the 2D spatial modeling advantage scales with dataset size. In the zero-shot tokenizer evaluation (Table 8), 2D-PRQ4 reduces reconstruction error from 135.964 (PRQ4) to 77.695 MPJPE on HumanML3D and from 167.508 to 108.921 on Motion-X, while VQ-VAE1 remains far behind (237.702 and 293.301 MPJPE). Scaling the LLM backbone from GPT-2-medium to LLaMA2-7B yields large gains (R@1: 0.357→0.491 on OpenT2M), but further scaling to LLaMA3-8B shows diminishing returns (0.491→0.478), suggesting a saturation point independent of tokenizer choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) large-scale motion datasets (AMASS, HumanML3D, Motion-X, MotionLib, HuMo100M), where it departs by combining video-based extraction with RL-based physical feasibility validation and open-sourcing the result; (2) motion tokenization, where 2D-PRQ extends the residual quantization line (RQ-VAE, PRQ in Being-M0) by treating the body-part × time grid as a 2D image and applying 2D convolutions, unlike prior part-decomposition methods that encode parts independently; and (3) LLM-based autoregressive T2M (MotionGPT, Being-M0, T2M-GPT), where MonoFrill adopts the same paradigm but deliberately strips away auxiliary components to test whether a well-designed tokenizer plus scale suffices. A key conceptual departure is the identification of data leakage in HumanML3D and Motion-X, which reframes prior SOTA results as potentially inflated by train-test contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of a million-sequence, physically-validated, open-source motion dataset with second-wise annotations directly addresses the field's most cited bottleneck (data scarcity and quality), and the cleaned benchmark versions (HumanML3D*, Motion-X*) provide a more honest evaluation protocol that the community can adopt. The finding that 2D-PRQ underperforms on small datasets but dramatically outperforms on large ones suggests that tokenizer architecture and dataset scale are coupled design choices, a nuance prior work has not made explicit. Open questions include whether the RL-based physical validation generalizes to novel motion types not seen in AMASS, whether the LLM saturation point (Llama2-7B ≈ Llama3-8B) reflects a fundamental limit or simply insufficient training, and whether the 'no frill' philosophy transfers to other motion generation settings (e.g., audio-conditioned, interactive).
