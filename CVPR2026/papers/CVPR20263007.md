## Problem

Joint aerial-to-street urban scene reconstruction with 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from three interrelated challenges: extreme scale disparity between views causes gradient accumulation conflicts that paradoxically degrade densification quality when both view types are used together, multi-scale detail capture demands excessive anchor counts, and imbalanced viewpoint distributions leave certain regions under-optimized. Prior work (Horizon-GS) addresses these only partially via a coarse-to-fine training split, leaving intra-class scale variations and restrictive viewpoint assumptions unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized aerial and street-view RGB images along with SfM-derived point clouds for anchor initialization, and produces a compact LOD-structured 3D Gaussian scene representation (Scaffold-GS anchors each emitting k neural Gaussians) that supports real-time novel-view rendering from arbitrary aerial or street perspectives. The structured anchor representation with probabilistic masks enables on-the-fly pruning, reducing storage by ~41% over Horizon-GS while maintaining or improving rendering fidelity.

## Method

Urban-GS introduces three complementary mechanisms on top of the Scaffold-GS anchor framework. First, Aerial-Street Joint Adaptive Densification (AJAD) replaces the standard uniform gradient averaging with a projected-area-weighted formulation, so that Gaussians exhibiting high positional gradients in specific views (due to large or small projection footprints) are not masked by low-gradient contributions from other views. Second, Contribution-based Anchor Pruning (CAP) integrates Gumbel-Softmax probabilistic masks into the alpha-blending pipeline and replaces the global mask loss with a contribution-weighted regularization that preserves sparsely-observed but locally critical anchors while still enabling aggressive removal of truly redundant ones. Third, Global-to-Local Optimization (GLO) identifies views with large PSNR fluctuations after global training, groups them by anchor overlap, and performs targeted local refinement with all MLP parameters and out-of-group anchors frozen to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the Horizon-GS dataset (5 scenes), Urban-GS consistently outperforms all baselines across PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. Against the primary SOTA Horizon-GS, Urban-GS gains +0.72 dB average PSNR (e.g., 26.88 vs 26.16 on Colosseum, 28.78 vs 28.10 on Elvenruin) and improves SSIM by up to +0.024 (0.899 vs 0.875 on Elvenruin). In terms of efficiency, Urban-GS reduces anchor count by an average of 41% (e.g., 2712k vs 6071k on Road) while increasing rendering speed (e.g., 89.8 vs 67.9 FPS on Road). On the UC-GS dataset, Urban-GS achieves the best Held-out PSNR (26.58 vs 25.35) and superior SSIM/LPIPS on extrapolated views despite marginally lower PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Urban-GS outperforms Horizon-GS (the primary SOTA) on every metric across all five Horizon-GS scenes, with the largest gains on Citysample (+1.20 dB PSNR, +0.032 SSIM) and Elvenruin (+0.68 dB PSNR, +0.024 SSIM). Against 3DGS and Scaffold-GS, Urban-GS leads by 1.5–2.5 dB PSNR, confirming that the structured anchor representation and joint aerial-street strategy are essential. Hier-GS (hierarchical LOD) is competitive on SSIM for some scenes but trails Urban-GS by 0.5–1.2 dB PSNR overall, and lacks the aerial-street joint modeling capability. On the UC-GS dataset, Urban-GS achieves the best Held-out PSNR (26.58 vs 25.35 for Horizon-GS) and superior perceptual quality (SSIM 0.806 vs 0.757, LPIPS 0.213 vs 0.280), though it marginally trails Horizon-GS by ~0.14 dB PSNR on the two extrapolated-view settings while still winning on SSIM and LPIPS. In efficiency, Urban-GS uses 41% fewer anchors on average and renders 1.2–2.2× faster than Horizon-GS (e.g., 89.8 vs 67.9 FPS on Road, 95.8 vs 57.2 FPS on Elvenruin).

## Relation to Prior Work

Urban-GS builds directly on Horizon-GS (CVPR 2025), the first method to perform joint aerial-to-street 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction, and on Scaffold-GS's structured anchor-based representation with LOD capabilities. It departs from Horizon-GS's coarse-to-fine two-phase training by instead identifying the root cause of quality degradation as projection-area-weighted gradient conflicts during densification, rather than a simple aerial-vs-street view-type mismatch. The method additionally integrates MaskGaussian's probabilistic mask mechanism for anchor pruning, but replaces its global L2 mask regularization with a contribution-weighted variant to handle the sparse, view-specific supervision inherent in multi-scale urban scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 41% average anchor reduction while simultaneously improving rendering quality over the state-of-the-art makes joint aerial-to-street reconstruction more deployable for practical applications in autonomous driving, digital twins, and AR/VR where memory and rendering speed are critical constraints. The projection-area-weighted densification criterion is a generalizable principle that could benefit other multi-scale Gaussian Splatting settings beyond urban scenes. Open questions include whether the two-stage global-to-local optimization scales to even larger city-scale scenes, whether the approach extends to dynamic urban elements (vehicles, pedestrians) beyond the current alpha-mask suppression, and how the method performs under extreme viewpoint extrapolation beyond the tested +1m/+5° shifts.
