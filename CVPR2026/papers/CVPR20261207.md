## Problem

Existing text-to-point-cloud (T2P) localization methods rely on shallow text-point cloud feature matching without explicit spatial reasoning, and operate on small, simplified submaps (~30 m × 30 m) that fail to capture the complexity of real-world urban scenes. This limits their accuracy and interpretability in environments where humans naturally perceive and describe spatial layouts spanning tens of meters with diverse object configurations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a point cloud map rendered as a 224×224 BEV image (covering 50 m × 50 m), a scene graph whose nodes encode semantic labels and centroid pixel coordinates (edges omitted since BEV coordinates already encode spatial relations), and a text query of 6 hints each specifying an object's semantic label, color, and relative orientation. The VLM autoregressively generates a JSON-formatted string containing partial text-to-node correspondences (True/False/null per hint) and a 2D pixel position, which is then converted to world coordinates on the ground plane.

## Method

VLM-Loc leverages a large VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, fine-tuned with LoRA rank 8) to perform interpretable spatial reasoning over two complementary map representations: a rasterized BEV image providing dense geometric layout and a scene graph providing structured semantic-spatial relations. The key mechanism is Partial Node Assignment (PNA), which explicitly supervises the VLM to determine whether each textual hint corresponds to a visible scene graph node (using a category-dependent distance threshold τ: 5 m for objects, 15 m for stuff) or should be left unassigned, thereby handling the partial-visibility problem where some described objects fall outside the mapped region. Training uses standard cross-entropy loss over autoregressive token generation, with the VLM outputting a structured JSON string that encodes both the node assignments and the final 2D position in a single decoding pass. This unified autoregressive formulation allows the model to reason consistently from grounded correspondences to spatial coordinates, and the PNA mechanism provides interpretable intermediate supervision that ablations show contributes ~7% Recall@5m improvement over scene-graph-only baselines.

## Results

On the CityLoc-K test set, VLM-Loc achieves 35.91% Recall@5m, 63.81% Recall@10m, and 76.79% Recall@15m, outperforming the strongest baseline CMMLoc (21.71% R@5m) by 14.20 percentage points. On the cross-domain CityLoc-C benchmark (UAV photogrammetric point clouds), VLM-Loc reaches 21.37% R@5m and 49.12% R@10m without any fine-tuning, surpassing the best baseline MNCL (13.68% R@5m) by 7.69 points. The ablation study confirms that the full pipeline (BEV + scene graph + PNA) contributes additively, with PNA alone adding ~7% R@5m over scene-graph-only input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLM-Loc decisively outperforms all four baselines on both CityLoc-K and CityLoc-C across every Recall threshold. Against the strongest baseline CMMLoc [58] (Cauchy Mixture Model with cardinal direction cues), VLM-Loc gains +14.20% R@5m and +17.14% R@10m on the CityLoc-K test set, and +9.69% R@5m on CityLoc-C. Compared to Text2Loc [57] (transformer-based text-point cloud alignment), the improvement is +17.94% R@5m on CityLoc-K test. MNCL [33] (multi-level contrastive learning) is the second-strongest baseline at 18.76% R@5m on CityLoc-K test, still trailing VLM-Loc by 17.15 points. On the cross-domain CityLoc-C split, all baselines degrade substantially (R@5m drops to 8–14%), while VLM-Loc retains 21.37%, indicating that the VLM's spatial reasoning generalizes better to unseen sensing modalities than feature-matching approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established T2P localization lineage (Text2Pos → Text2Loc → MNCL → CMMLoc), which relies on custom transformer encoders performing direct text-to-point-cloud feature matching on small, simple submaps. Rather than extending those architectures, VLM-Loc repurposes off-the-shelf vision-language models (Qwen3-VL) by projecting point clouds into BEV images and scene graphs, thereby bridging the 3D-to-2D modality gap that VLMs were pretrained on. The Partial Node Assignment mechanism is a novel training signal that has no direct analogue in prior T2P work, drawing conceptually from 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer, ReferIt3D) but applied to position estimation rather than object identification. The CityLoc benchmark also breaks from the KITTI360Pose paradigm by using larger (50 m × 50 m) and more cluttered submaps, addressing a gap the authors identify in existing evaluation protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CityLoc benchmark fills a practical gap: prior T2P evaluation used submaps small enough that matching was trivially simplified, whereas real-world localization (e.g., robotaxi pickup) involves larger, more complex scenes. The 14–15% Recall@5m improvement over CMMLoc, combined with strong cross-domain transfer (CityLoc-K → CityLoc-C), suggests VLM-based spatial reasoning is a viable and interpretable alternative to hand-crafted cross-modal encoders for 3D localization. Open questions remain: the method still operates on a single 50 m local map rather than truly city-scale scenes, the text queries are template-generated rather than free-form, and the authors themselves flag multi-step compositional reasoning and active agent integration as future directions. Whether this VLM-for-localization paradigm scales to longer, more natural descriptions in fully autonomous settings is the key open question.
