## Problem

Existing hyperparameter optimization (HPO) methods for setting learning rate and batch size rely on expensive validation metrics (e.g., accuracy, F1) to make searching and scheduling decisions, incurring high overhead and potentially poor early-stage signal quality. This makes it difficult for cost-sensitive cloud training services to promptly find proper configurations for arbitrary user models without wasting GPU resources on misconfigured trials.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a user-submitted neural network model, training dataset, and validation metric, then monitors internal gradient statistics during the critical early-training period. It computes GR_T and GR_S from a sampled subset of L=5×10^7 parameters (proportionally drawn from each layer) and evaluates GR_S at per-device rather than per-sample granularity to reduce overhead. The output is a validated (learning rate, batch size) pair that drives the model to near-optimal performance, along with the trained model itself; the entire HPO loop operates without any external validation-set evaluation.

## Method

GR-Gauge reframes model training as a two-dimensional gradient-voting process and defines two redundancy metrics: GR_T (temporal, computed from Adam-style bias-corrected first and second moment estimates of global gradients across iterations) and GR_S (spatial, the ratio of the L1 norm of summed per-sample squared gradients to the squared L2 norm of summed per-sample gradients). The paper proves monotonic partial-derivative correlations between (GR_T, GR_S) and (learning rate, batch size), and empirically identifies a model-generic healthy target range (GR_T*≈20, GR_S*≈0.58). Leveraging these, GR-Gauge guides HPO in three ways: (1) early-stopping a trial when GR values fall outside the healthy band, (2) scaling the next learning rate or batch size proportionally to the gap between measured and target GR values, and (3) reusing the intermediate training state of an aborted trial if the GR metrics can recover to the healthy range after a short exemption period, thereby avoiding redundant from-scratch restarts.

## Results

GR-Gauge reduces the GPU-time to reach near-optimal validation targets by over 80% compared to the best baseline in most settings. For example, on ResNet18 (CIFAR-10) at 85% accuracy, GR-Gauge requires 977s versus 7.53× that for BOHB; on BERT (SQuAD) at 85% F1, it takes 11,000s versus 7.95× for BOHB. Under fixed time budgets, GR-Gauge consistently achieves the highest validation performance—e.g., after 20,000s on BERT, GR-Gauge reaches 87.47% F1 while all baselines remain below 75%. The overhead of computing GR_T and GR_S metrics is under 4% of total training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GR-Gauge outperforms all five baselines (Random Search, Successive Halving, Hyperband, BOHB, and CFO) in the majority of model–target combinations, with the largest margins on BERT (up to 24.8× faster than RS at 80% F1) and ResNet18 (up to 9.13× faster than RS at 85% accuracy). The closest competitor is BOHB, which occasionally matches or beats GR-Gauge: on BERT at 70% F1, BOHB is slightly faster (0.82×), and on Llama3 at 50% loss, BOHB is dramatically faster (0.12×), suggesting GR-Gauge's gradient-redundancy signal is less informative for very large LLM fine-tuning at low targets. CFO (cost-frugal optimization) performs poorly on NeuMF (8.47×–10.6× slower) but is competitive on ResNet18 and Diffusion. Under fixed time budgets (Table 4), GR-Gauge achieves the highest validation performance across nearly all model–budget pairs, e.g., 88.48% accuracy on ResNet18 at 2000s vs. 79.37% for HB, and 60.81% loss on Llama3 at 100,000s vs. 60.54% for BOHB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant HPO paradigm (Random Search, Successive Halving, Hyperband, BOHB) that relies on external validation metrics for both configuration searching and trial scheduling. Instead, it repurposes gradient statistics already computed by adaptive optimizers (AdaGrad/Adam-style first and second moment estimates) as internal healthiness signals, framing training as a two-dimensional gradient-voting process. The state-reuse mechanism—continuing training from an aborted trial's intermediate state rather than restarting—is described as an optimization angle rarely explored in prior HPO work. The method explicitly trades hyper-parameter generality for efficiency, targeting only learning rate and batch size rather than architecture-level hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a model-generic healthy range (GR_T ≈ 20, GR_S ≈ 0.58) exists across ResNet, BERT, Stable Diffusion, Llama, and NeuMF is practically significant: it means cloud training services can apply a single set of thresholds without per-model calibration. The 80%+ time reduction in reaching near-optimal accuracy is well-evidenced across five diverse models and multiple validation targets. Limitations include restriction to learning rate and batch size (not architecture hyperparameters), a small accuracy trade-off from state reuse (~1% in most cases), and confinement to the critical learning period. Open questions include extension to other system hyperparameters (momentum, synchronization interval) and validation under different optimizer families beyond AdamW.
