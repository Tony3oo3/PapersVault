## Problem

Training a world model to simulate MRI contrast enhancement kinetics is hindered by the extremely sparse temporal sampling of clinical DCE-MRI (only second-level intervals), which causes two failure modes: content distortion from overfitting to irrelevant features at unsupervised time points, and temporal discontinuities from the absence of continuous kinetic supervision. No prior method can faithfully reconstruct the full pharmacokinetic trajectory from a single non-contrast scan under these data constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261307/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 256×256 non-contrast MRI slice (normalized to [-1,1], stacked to 3 channels) and a continuous time variable representing seconds post-contrast injection (formatted as 'HH:MM:SS' and encoded via CLIP text embeddings). It outputs a contrast-enhanced MRI image at that arbitrary time point, enabling dense sequence generation at any temporal resolution unconstrained by the original sparse acquisition protocol. The backbone is a ControlNet-based latent diffusion model (SD1.5) with a trainable U-Net and zero-convolution image encoder.

## Method

MRI CEKWorld builds on a ControlNet latent diffusion backbone and introduces SpatioTemporal Consistency Learning (STCL) with two complementary losses. Latent Alignment Learning (LAL) computes per-time-point covariance matrices in the latent space, maps them to Euclidean vectors via log-Cholesky parameterization, averages them into a patient-specific template, and penalizes deviations via an equidistance constraint to preserve anatomical consistency. Latent Difference Learning (LDL) uniformly inserts K_i=2 virtual intermediate time points between sparse observations, generates latent predictions at all dense points, and constrains the discrete second-order central differences (with interval-adaptive weighting) to zero to enforce smooth temporal evolution. Training is two-stage: first a diffusion warmup with spatial regularization (λ_Spatial=6.0), then a temporal regularization phase (λ_Temporal=1.0) that leverages the aligned content for smooth transitions.

## Results

MRI CEKWorld achieves the best average SSIM (0.6509) and average cSSIM (0.6176) across both datasets. On the Abdominal DCE-MRI dataset, it reaches PSNR 24.06, SSIM 0.7419, LPIPS 0.2622, rMSE 40.08, and cSSIM 0.8451. On the Duke Breast DCE-MRI dataset, it achieves PSNR 21.09, SSIM 0.5599, LPIPS 0.2620, rMSE 243.5, and cSSIM 0.3900. The method leads all spatial and temporal metrics except PSNR, where CCNet scores marginally higher (24.35 vs. 24.06 Abdominal; 21.47 vs. 21.09 Breast) due to over-smoothed predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRI CEKWorld outperforms all five baselines on SSIM, LPIPS, rMSE, and cSSIM across both datasets. Against the strongest baseline ControlNet [75], it gains +2.4% SSIM and +1.7% cSSIM on Abdominal (0.7419 vs. 0.7178; 0.8451 vs. 0.8286) and +4.0% SSIM and +5.3% cSSIM on Breast (0.5599 vs. 0.5196; 0.3900 vs. 0.3370). CCNet [50] achieves the highest PSNR on both datasets (24.35 Abdominal, 21.47 Breast) but the authors attribute this to over-smoothed, under-converged predictions that sacrifice structural detail, reflected in its much lower SSIM (0.5794 vs. 0.7419) and higher rMSE (43.44 vs. 40.08). EditAR [42] and CustomDiff [32] show substantially weaker spatial fidelity (SSIM 0.5571 and 0.4130 on Abdominal) and temporal coherence (cSSIM 0.7536 and 0.3551), while T2I [41] performs worst overall with cSSIM as low as 0.1347 on Breast, indicating severe temporal discontinuities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing virtual MRI contrast enhancement as a world-model problem, departing from prior work that is confined to discrete image-to-image mapping (CCNet, DCE-Diff, DCE-Transformer) or single-phase synthesis. Architecturally it builds on the ControlNet/Stable Diffusion pipeline with CLIP-based time conditioning, but its core contribution is two novel latent-space losses—Latent Alignment Learning (covariance-matrix template via log-Cholesky parameterization) and Latent Difference Learning (second-order central-difference smoothness on interpolated virtual time points)—that address the sparse-sampling problem unique to MRI acquisition. The spatiotemporal consistency framing draws conceptually from slow feature analysis and contrastive learning, but repurposes these ideas for generation under extreme temporal sparsity rather than representation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically significant gap: enabling continuous, contrast-free MRI dynamics simulation that could reduce injection risks, costs, and acquisition time. However, the evaluation is limited to 2D slices on two datasets (one private with 91 patients, one public with 3–4 time points per exam), and the authors themselves note the need to extend to 3D and other modalities (CT). Open questions include whether the covariance-based spatial template generalizes to more complex anatomies, whether the fixed K_i=2 interpolation density is optimal across clinical protocols, and whether the two-stage training (spatial then temporal) is necessary or whether joint optimization would suffice. Clinical validation with radiologists remains absent.
