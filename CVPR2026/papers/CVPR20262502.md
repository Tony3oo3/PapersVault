## Problem

Existing WSI registration pipelines (e.g., VALIS, RegWSI) all assume a coarse global pre-alignment can be established before local refinement. In biopsy-derived slides with fragmented tissue, bending, tearing, and large nonlinear distortions, this assumption frequently breaks down, causing registration to collapse entirely. The paper addresses this by eliminating the need for any pre-alignment and reformulating cross-stain alignment as a region-level feature retrieval problem.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of gigapixel HE and IHC whole-slide images, but the network itself consumes only 2D point coordinates (query point plus K anchor coordinates), not image patches. Landmarks are extracted externally via SuperPoint/SuperGlue on low-resolution thumbnails with multi-angle rotation. The output is a region-level correspondence mask assigning each target point to a source region, rather than a dense pixel-wise deformation field, making it suitable for diagnostic interpretation where region-level alignment suffices.

## Method

SAR2Net is a lightweight MLP that encodes each query point's relative geometry to K nearby spatial anchors (landmarks) by computing relative offsets, concatenating them with absolute anchor coordinates, and aggregating per-anchor embeddings via a learned attention mechanism into a 256-d spatially anchored representation. The network is trained entirely on synthetic 2D coordinates using InfoNCE contrastive loss with rigid-transformed positives and a local geometry-aware negative sampling (LGN) strategy that places negatives symmetrically across a convex hull of neighboring anchors to enforce local geometric discrimination. At inference, a retrieval-guided pipeline slides windows over the source slide, selects locally coherent anchor subsets via RANSAC-based geometric consensus, computes anchored features for all points in both slides, and assigns correspondences by top-k feature retrieval with a similarity threshold. A second-round refinement re-applies the same retrieval within each matched region using denser locally extracted anchors to correct residual nonlinear distortions.

## Results

On the Xijing HE-IHC Biopsy Dataset (370 slide pairs, 79 IHC types), SAR2Net with two-stage refinement achieves mIoU 0.899, mDice 0.942, aw-IoU 0.901, and aw-Dice 0.944, substantially outperforming both baselines. The single-round retrieval already reaches mIoU 0.891 and mDice 0.938, with the second-round refinement adding a consistent ~0.8% mIoU gain. In robustness analysis, SAR2Net attains a success rate of 0.939 at IoU ≥ 0.75 and 0.946 at Dice ≥ 0.85, compared to 0.594/0.606 (VALIS) and 0.545/0.574 (RegWSI) at the same thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAR2Net is compared against two state-of-the-art WSI registration frameworks: VALIS (Nature Communications 2023) and RegWSI (ACM 2023 challenge winner). SAR2Net decisively outperforms both on all four primary metrics. Against VALIS, the gains are mIoU +0.264 (0.899 vs. 0.635), mDice +0.237, aw-IoU +0.254, and aw-Dice +0.228. Against RegWSI, the improvements are mIoU +0.208, mDice +0.156, aw-IoU +0.202, and aw-Dice +0.150. The robustness gap is even more pronounced: at the strict IoU ≥ 0.85 threshold, SAR2Net aligns 81.2% of regions correctly versus only 42.9% (VALIS) and 32.4% (RegWSI). The paper notes that baselines show a notable gap between mean and area-weighted metrics, indicating they align large regions well but fail on smaller ones, whereas SAR2Net maintains near-identical mean and area-weighted scores, reflecting uniform quality across region sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAR2Net departs fundamentally from the dominant WSI registration paradigm (VALIS, RegWSI, DeepHistReg), which estimates explicit spatial transformations through multi-stage pipelines requiring successful global pre-alignment. Instead, it repurposes contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on purely synthetic point coordinates to learn spatially anchored representations, and reformulates cross-stain alignment as a region-level feature retrieval problem rather than transformation estimation. The framework borrows SuperPoint/SuperGlue for landmark extraction and RANSAC for geometric consensus, but uses them as anchor generators rather than direct matching tools, and the core network is a lightweight MLP operating on relative coordinates with no image input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important failure mode in computational pathology: biopsy-derived WSIs with fragmented or heavily deformed tissue where all existing registration pipelines collapse due to the absence of a viable global pre-alignment. The finding that a simple MLP trained on synthetic coordinates (no image data) can outperform complex multi-stage registration by ~20% mIoU suggests that local geometric topology, not image appearance, is the key signal for cross-stain correspondence. However, evaluation is limited to a single hospital's 370 HE-IHC pairs with only two baselines, and the region-level (not pixel-level) formulation may not suffice for all downstream tasks; generalization to other stain combinations, larger tissue types, and multi-institutional settings remains open.
