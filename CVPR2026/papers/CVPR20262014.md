## Problem

CLIP's contrastive training loss aligns image and text representations across modalities but provides no explicit signal for intra-modal alignment, causing sub-optimal performance on tasks like image-to-image or text-to-text retrieval. Existing remedies such as optimization-based modality inversion (OTI/OVI) require hundreds to thousands of optimization steps per query, incurring prohibitive latency. The paper identifies the structural cause of this misalignment in CLIP's projector geometry and proposes a training-free correction.

## Input / Output

IsoCLIP operates on pre-projection embeddings produced by frozen CLIP-style encoders (ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, OpenCLIP, Perception-Encoder). It modifies only the linear projector matrices W_i and W_t by projecting them onto a selected isotropic subspace, then computes standard cosine similarities on the resulting projected features. No additional forward passes, optimization steps, or learned parameters are introduced, so query latency remains identical to vanilla CLIP (e.g., 6–11 ms per query). Outputs are cosine similarity scores used for retrieval rankings (mAP) or Nearest Class Mean classification (accuracy).

## Method

The key insight is that CLIP's cosine similarity implicitly decomposes into an inter-modal operator Ψ = W_i^T W_t (which drives cross-modal alignment during contrastive training) and an intra-modal operator Ψ_i = W_i^T W_i (which only enforces unit-norm constraints without promoting same-modality alignment). By computing the SVD of Ψ, the authors identify an approximately isotropic middle band of singular values where both modalities are well-aligned, flanked by anisotropic top and bottom bands that encode modality-specific variation. IsoCLIP restricts the projectors to this isotropic subspace via orthogonal projection: Ŵ_i = W_i U_SU U_SU^T and Ŵ_t = W_t V_SV V_SV^T, where U_SU and V_SV span the middle-band singular vectors. The hyperparameters k_t and k_b (number of top/bottom directions to discard) are selected empirically on a single validation dataset per modality. This training-free spectral filtering flattens the intra-modal similarity spectrum, improves positive-negative separation, and yields consistent mAP gains of 3–6% over standard CLIP and comparable or better results than OTI/OVI at a fraction of the latency.

## Results

On image-to-image retrieval across 13 datasets, IsoCLIP achieves 52.8% average mAP with ViT-B/16, improving over standard Image-Image retrieval (46.3%) by 6.5 points and outperforming the optimization-based OTI baseline (49.4%) while matching its latency (6 ms vs. 1856 ms). On text-to-text retrieval, IsoCLIP reaches 41.2% average mAP with ViT-B/16, a 3.9-point gain over standard Text-Text (37.3%) and on par with OVI (39.9%) at 6 ms latency versus OVI's 11929 ms. The largest absolute gains appear on fine-grained datasets (e.g., +10.2 mAP on Cars, +14.4 mAP on Pets with ViT-B/16), while the method adds zero additional inference latency compared to standard CLIP projection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares IsoCLIP against two baselines: (1) standard intra-modal retrieval (Image-Image / Text-Text) that directly applies the CLIP projector, and (2) optimization-based modality inversion (OTI for images, OVI for text) that iteratively maps queries into the opposite modality to exploit inter-modal alignment. IsoCLIP consistently outperforms standard intra-modal retrieval across all five backbones and all 13 image datasets, with average mAP gains of 4.2–6.5 points (e.g., 52.8 vs. 46.3 on ViT-B/16; 60.7 vs. 54.3 on ViT-L/14). Against OTI, IsoCLIP achieves higher average mAP on four of five backbones (e.g., 52.8 vs. 49.4 on ViT-B/16; 60.7 vs. 57.7 on ViT-L/14) while reducing query latency from ~1850–3250 ms to 6–11 ms. On text-to-text retrieval, IsoCLIP matches or slightly exceeds OVI (41.2 vs. 39.9 avg mAP on ViT-B/16; 43.0 vs. 41.3 on ViT-L/14) at 6 ms latency versus OVI's 11,900–21,400 ms. The one exception is ViT-B/16-open (DataComp-trained), where IsoCLIP (58.6) is marginally below OTI (58.7), suggesting the spectral structure of DataComp-trained projectors is slightly different.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mistretta et al. [16], who empirically identified intra-modal misalignment in CLIP and proposed expensive optimization-based inversion methods (OTI/OVI) to circumvent it by converting queries into the complementary modality. The key departure is a theoretical decomposition of the CLIP contrastive loss that reveals two distinct operators hidden in the cosine similarity: an inter-modal operator Ψ = W_i^T W_t responsible for cross-modal alignment during training, and an intra-modal operator Ψ_i = W_i^T W_i that only enforces normalization without promoting intra-modal similarity. By performing SVD on Ψ, the authors identify an approximately isotropic middle-band subspace shared by both modalities, enabling a training-free spectral filtering of the projectors that replaces the iterative inversion of prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IsoCLIP provides a zero-latency, training-free post-processing step that any CLIP-like model user can apply to improve intra-modal retrieval and classification, making it immediately practical for downstream applications. The clean theoretical decomposition of the CLIP loss into inter-modal and intra-modal operators offers a useful lens for understanding why CLIP's projectors are suboptimal for single-modality tasks. However, the method degrades inter-modal performance (e.g., text-to-image retrieval), and the hyperparameters k_t and k_b are selected empirically on a single dataset per modality, leaving open the question of principled, model-agnostic band selection. Future work could explore whether the isotropic subspace can be exploited during training or whether adaptive band selection per query is feasible.
