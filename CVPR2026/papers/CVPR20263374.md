## Problem

Teacher-student frameworks for noisy-label facial expression recognition suffer from two compounding failures: noise labels propagate through the EMA update pathway, corrupting the teacher's supervisory signal, and progressive parameter coupling between teacher and student constrains the model's learning capacity. Existing label smoothing methods apply a fixed smoothing strength uniformly across all samples, which dilutes clean labels while inadequately suppressing noisy ones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB facial images as input and outputs a 7-class emotion label (neutral, happiness, sadness, surprise, fear, disgust, anger). During training, both student and teacher ResNet-18 networks are pre-trained on MS-Celeb-1M and trained on RAF-DB, AffectNet, or FERPlus with synthetic symmetric or asymmetric label noise at 10–50% corruption rates. At inference, the model is a single ResNet-18 whose weights are the average of the Optimal Teacher Pool, requiring only one forward pass (11.9 ms/image) with no auxiliary modules.

## Method

OTP-NS replaces the single EMA teacher with an Optimal Teacher Pool of N=3 best historical teacher models, updated via an accuracy admission criterion plus a periodic forced replacement every 10 epochs to prevent knowledge stagnation. The fused teacher prediction combines the current teacher and pool outputs using a time-decaying confidence coefficient β_t = γ·exp(−t/T_max), so early training emphasizes new knowledge while later training relies on the stable pool to resist noise accumulation. For the teacher's supervision signal, Similarity-Aware Label Smoothing computes a per-sample cosine similarity between the fused prediction and the one-hot label, then linearly maps it to a dynamic smoothing strength ε_i ∈ [0.1, 0.3], applying stronger smoothing to low-similarity (likely noisy) samples and weaker smoothing to high-similarity (likely clean) ones. For the student's classification loss, Confidence-Weighted Logits computes each sample's L2 distance to its class centroid (maintained via EMA to resist noise contamination), passes it through a trainable sigmoid to obtain a per-sample weight α_i, and scales the logits before the cross-entropy computation, thereby down-weighting samples that deviate from their class cluster.

## Results

On RAF-DB, OTP-NS achieves 90.12%, 88.91%, and 87.56% top-1 accuracy at 10%, 20%, and 30% symmetric noise, respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline MCR by 0.84%, 1.30%, and 1.48%. On AffectNet, it reaches 63.18%, 61.95%, and 60.99%, improving over MCR by 0.85%, 0.60%, and 1.49%. On FERPlus, the method attains 87.91%, 87.27%, and 86.18%, surpassing the best available baseline (LA-Net) by up to 0.17%. Inference latency remains at 11.9 ms/image, matching the ResNet-18 baseline (11.8 ms/image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTP-NS outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and noise level where comparisons are available. The closest competitor is MCR [29], which OTP-NS surpasses by 0.84–1.48% on RAF-DB and 0.60–1.49% on AffectNet; the gap widens at higher noise (30%), indicating better noise robustness. LA-Net [37] is the strongest baseline on FERPlus (88.02% at 10%), but OTP-NS still edges it out at 20% and 30% noise (87.27% vs. 86.85%, 86.18% vs. 86.01%). SOFT [20] and EAC [41] are competitive at 10% noise (within ~1% on RAF-DB) but degrade more sharply at 30%, where OTP-NS leads by 1.48% and 3.14% respectively. Older methods such as SCN [33], FENN [5], and RUL [40] trail by 5–13 points, confirming that the optimal teacher pool and sample-level noise suppression mechanisms provide substantial gains over earlier noise-robust FER approaches. On asymmetric noise (Fig. 3b), OTP-NS improves over SOFT by 1.39–2.98% across 10–50% noise rates, further validating its advantage under realistic confusion patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mean Teacher framework (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017) by replacing the single EMA-updated teacher with an Optimal Teacher Pool that selectively retains the best historical teacher models, directly addressing the noise accumulation and parameter coupling problems inherent in EMA-based distillation. The sample-level noise suppression components build on the label smoothing lineage from Szegedy et al. (2016) and the instance-aware Soft Label Smoothing of Lukov et al. (2022), but introduce dynamic, similarity-driven smoothing strength rather than fixed or confidence-based smoothing. The Confidence-Weighted Logits module draws on centroid-distance heuristics common in noisy-label literature but applies them through a learnable sigmoid gating on logits rather than sample reweighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, inference-free solution for FER with noisy labels—a persistent real-world problem given the subjectivity of emotion annotation—without requiring clean validation sets or additional inference-time computation. However, the improvements over the strongest baseline (MCR) are modest (0.8–1.5%), and the method is validated only on ResNet-18 across three datasets with synthetic noise, leaving open whether the OTP mechanism generalizes to deeper backbones, real-world instance-dependent noise, or video-based expression recognition. The dramatic ablation showing near-total failure (36% vs 87%) without the Periodic Forced Update highlights a fragility in the pool maintenance strategy that warrants further investigation.
