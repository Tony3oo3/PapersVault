## Problem

Existing source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA) methods for medical image segmentation are designed for low-gap, specific anatomical targets and cannot generalize into a unified framework across diverse modalities and targets. This lack of a single, consistent methodology for multi-modality, multi-target, and large-gap domain adaptation remains a major barrier to real-world clinical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262534/cropped_page1_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes three inputs: unlabeled target-domain medical images (CT, MRI, ultrasound, or endoscopic), potentially noisy or ambiguous text prompts specifying the target anatomy, and a pre-trained lightweight source model (nnUNet, 31.1M parameters). It produces per-pixel segmentation masks for up to 15 abdominal organs, 4 brain tumor subregions, 3 cardiac structures, or polyp regions. Notably, the adapted student model requires only 4.8 GB peak VRAM at inference versus 26.1 GB for direct BiomedParse inference, making it deployable without the VFM.

## Method

Tell2Adapt decouples pseudo-label generation from the source model by using a text-prompted VFM (BiomedParse) as an external knowledge source, breaking the error-propagation cascade inherent in spatial-prompted approaches. Three modules work in sequence: (1) Context-Aware Prompts Regularization (CAPR) employs Qwen3-VL-8B to perform global context inference over all sub-prompts and canonicalize each into a structured [Target] in [Anatomical Site] [Modality] format, resolving ambiguity and enriching prompts with shared modality/anatomical context; (2) VFM-Guided Knowledge Distillation uses the canonicalized prompts to drive BiomedParse's mask decoder for pseudo-label generation, applies histogram equalization to target images to suppress low-level intensity shifts, and trains the source model via Dice + Cross-Entropy loss on the resulting (HE-enhanced image, pseudo-label) pairs; (3) Visual Plausibility Refinement (VPR) retrieves pre-computed Beta distributions of four visual attributes (pixel probability and R/G/B mean intensity) for the target category from BiomedParse's anatomical priors, computes a joint plausibility score per connected component, and discards components scoring below μ−2σ to remove anatomically implausible false positives.

## Results

On the abdominal AMOS benchmark (MR→CT), Tell2Adapt achieves a mean DICE of 88.2%, essentially matching the fully supervised upper bound of 88.4%. On the extreme-gap cardiac task (MR→US), it reaches 94.6% DICE on LV and 88.5% on MYO, closely approaching supervised performance (94.4%/88.1%), while all six baselines collapse to near-zero DICE. On polyp segmentation (Kvasir→CVCDB), Tell2Adapt attains 89.1% DICE, surpassing the second-best method (ProtoContra, 78.0%) by 11.1 percentage points. Across all 10 adaptation directions and 22 anatomical targets, Tell2Adapt consistently outperforms every compared method on both DICE and ASD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six baselines—three traditional SFUDA methods (UPL, ProtoContra, IAPC) and three VFM-guided methods (DFG, IPLC, SRPL)—fail catastrophically on the extreme-gap cardiac adaptation (MR↔US), with DICE scores below 26% in every case, while Tell2Adapt achieves 88–96% DICE. On the abdominal AMOS benchmark (MR→CT), Tell2Adapt reaches 88.2% mDICE versus 8.6% (UPL), 17.1% (ProtoContra), 6.4% (IAPC), 13.7% (DFG), 6.0% (IPLC), and 19.9% (SRPL), a margin of 68–80 percentage points over the best baseline. On polyp segmentation (Kvasir→CVCDB), Tell2Adapt (89.1% DICE) outperforms the strongest baseline ProtoContra (78.0%) by 11.1 points, while VFM-guided methods (DFG 19.1%, IPLC 34.8%, SRPL 33.0%) degrade severely. The VFM-guided baselines (DFG, IPLC, SRPL) underperform even traditional methods on several tasks because they rely on the source model's noisy predictions as spatial prompts, triggering an error-propagation cascade that Tell2Adapt avoids by using text prompts regularized via CAPR. On brain targets (T1c→T2f), Tell2Adapt achieves 86.0% DICE on SNFH with 1.2 mm ASD, surpassing IPLC (42.8%/48.2 mm) and all other methods by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VFM-guided SFUDA line (IPLC, DFG, SRPL) that uses source model pseudo-labels to generate spatial prompts for models like MedSAM or SAM, creating a critical error-propagation cascade under domain shift. Tell2Adapt instead leverages a text-prompted VFM (BiomedParse) guided by LLM-regularized canonical prompts, fundamentally decoupling pseudo-label quality from source model reliability. The knowledge distillation paradigm and histogram equalization for low-level shift mitigation build on standard self-training SFUDA, while CAPR (LLM-based prompt normalization) and VPR (Beta-distribution anatomical plausibility filtering) are novel contributions not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SFUDA framework validated across 10 adaptation directions and 22 anatomical targets spanning brain, cardiac, abdominal, and polyp domains, achieving near-supervised performance (e.g., 88.2% vs 88.4% mDICE on abdominal MR→CT) without any target labels. The practical impact is significant: the adapted student model (31.1M params, 4.8 GB VRAM) is deployable in clinical settings where the 371.8M-param VFM is infeasible. Key limitations include the still-computational pseudo-label generation stage (~3.5 s per abdominal volume) and dependence on BiomedParse's coverage of anatomical categories, leaving open whether the framework extends to targets outside the VFM's training distribution.
