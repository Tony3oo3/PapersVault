## Problem

Existing 3DGS segmentation methods for open-vocabulary object selection rely on predefined reconstruction camera viewpoints, ground-truth semantic masks, or per-scene optimization taking tens of minutes, making them impractical for interactive editing of a shared 3DGS asset in production pipelines. No prior method simultaneously satisfies the camera-free, training-free, open-vocabulary, and few-second latency constraints required for real-time asset editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (a set of Gaussians with means, covariances, opacities, and colors), a free-form text prompt, and a single canonical camera pose from scene metadata (used only for initialization). It outputs a binary per-Gaussian label indicating membership in the target object, determined by whether the accumulated Beta success pseudo-counts exceed the failure pseudo-counts after 20 active view-selection iterations. No reconstruction camera trajectories, ground-truth masks, or retraining are required at any point.

## Method

B³-Seg reformulates 3DGS segmentation as sequential Beta-Bernoulli Bayesian updates: each Gaussian carries a Beta(a_i, b_i) posterior over its foreground probability, updated with per-view pseudo-counts derived from rendered transmittance-weighted mask responsibilities. For active view selection, the method computes an analytic Expected Information Gain (EIG) as the expected reduction in Beta entropy, approximating success/failure counts from the current Beta mean rather than running expensive mask inference on every candidate; the view maximizing EIG is selected greedily. On the chosen view, a lightweight open-vocabulary pipeline (Grounding DINO → SAM2 with a prior mask rendered from current Beta means → CLIP re-ranking) produces the 2D mask that drives the next Beta update. The paper proves that EIG is adaptive monotone and adaptive submodular, yielding a (1−1/e) approximation guarantee for the greedy view-selection policy, and shows that FlashSplat's linear-programming decision rule is recovered as the MAP decision under symmetric Beta priors.

## Results

On LERF-Mask, B³-Seg achieves a mean mIoU of 84.5% and mBIoU of 81.0% across three scenes (figurines, ramen, teatime) in 12.1 seconds with 20 active view selections. On 3D-OVS, it reaches mIoU of 97.1/92.2/94.1/96.8 on Bed/Bench/Sofa/Lawn categories. These results are competitive with high-cost supervised methods like ObjectGS (88.4 mIoU, ~50 min) while operating 25× faster and without reconstruction views or ground-truth labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among directly comparable sampling-based, training-free methods, B³-Seg outperforms FlashSplat (Uniform-Sphere) by 14.9 mIoU (84.5 vs. 69.6) and FlashSplat (Recon-Cam) by 8.0 mIoU (84.5 vs. 76.5) on LERF-Mask, demonstrating that EIG-driven view selection substantially improves over random or reconstruction-camera sampling. On 3D-OVS, B³-Seg (97.1/92.2/94.1/96.8) similarly surpasses FlashSplat (Uniform-Sphere) (91.7/86.9/90.2/91.9) and FlashSplat (Recon-Cam) (94.3/90.3/85.7/96.3) across all four categories. Against the strongest supervised baseline ObjectGS (88.4 mIoU, ~50 min), B³-Seg trails by 3.9 mIoU on LERF-Mask but runs 25× faster (12 s vs. ~50 min) and requires no reconstruction views or ground-truth masks. On 3D-OVS, B³-Seg is within 1–4 mIoU of ObjectGS per category (e.g., 97.1 vs. 98.0 on Bed, 96.8 vs. 95.4 on Lawn where it actually wins). The paper also shows that the analytic EIG correlates strongly (r=0.964) with true information gain, and that EIG-based selection achieves the largest per-step entropy reduction compared to uniform-sphere and reconstruction-view sampling strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

B³-Seg reformulates the linear-programming decision rule of FlashSplat (Shen et al., ECCV 2024) as a MAP decision within a sequential Beta-Bernoulli Bayesian framework, unifying the per-Gaussian labeling problem under a conjugate prior-posterior model. For active view selection, it draws on the adaptive submodularity theory of Golovin & Krause (JAIR 2011) to derive a (1−1/e) greedy approximation guarantee, a theoretical tool previously applied to active learning and stochastic optimization but not to 3DGS segmentation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant 3DGS segmentation paradigm (LERF, LangSplat, Gaussian Grouping, ObjectGS) that requires reconstruction views, ground-truth masks, or per-scene optimization, by showing that a small number of actively chosen views with off-the-shelf 2D segmenters (Grounding DINO + SAM2 + CLIP) suffice under a Bayesian accumulation scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for film and game production pipelines where only a pre-reconstructed 3DGS asset is shared: B³-Seg enables interactive, open-vocabulary object editing in ~12 seconds without any camera metadata or retraining, a regime no prior method satisfies. The adaptive-submodularity guarantee is a rare theoretical anchor in an area dominated by heuristic view sampling, and the analytic EIG (avoiding SAM2 inference on every candidate) is a clean efficiency trick likely to be reusable in other active 3D perception settings. Open questions include scalability to large indoor/outdoor scenes (the sphere-based candidate sampling may be insufficient), extension to multi-class segmentation via the mentioned Dirichlet-Categorical generalization, and whether the binary pseudo-count model degrades in highly cluttered or overlapping-object scenes.
