## Problem

Fully supervised VSS requires expensive pixel-level video annotations, while applying pre-trained ISS models frame-by-frame ignores temporal coherence and produces inconsistent predictions. SAM2 offers high-quality temporal mask propagation but is impractical for direct VSS due to limited semantic understanding and the computational cost of invoking it on every frame. No prior method efficiently converts an ISS model into a temporally-aware VSS model without annotated videos or repeated SAM2 calls at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261232/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained ISS model (e.g., SegFormer-MiT-B5 trained frame-wise on VSPW or ADE20K) and a test video, using SAM2 only on an initial warm-up subset of frames (10–50%). During adaptation, only the decoder parameters of the ISS model are updated (5 iterations per frame, lr=0.001). The output is per-frame pixel-wise class predictions (124 classes on VSPW) at ~13.45 FPS without any SAM2 inference, under the Warm-Up then Freeze protocol where the adapted model is frozen after the warm-up phase and applied to all remaining frames.

## Method

DiTTA comprises three components: (1) a lightweight cross-attention add-on that fuses features from the previous frame into the current frame's logits, with pixel-wise reliability-aware blending based on normalized entropy; (2) SAM2-based distillation targets, where high-reliability ISS predictions on initial frames seed SAM2 for bidirectional mask propagation, and the resulting spatiotemporal object masks are assigned class labels via a soft scoring function combining reliability, area fraction, and frequency weighting, then used as masked logit-level cross-entropy supervision; (3) a mask-based contrastive loss with a momentum encoder that computes object-wise feature prototypes and aligns the main model's features within the same object mask across frames. The key distinction from zero-shot refinement is that SAM2 is invoked only once during a brief single-pass warm-up, after which the adapted model performs fast, self-contained inference without any further SAM2 dependency.

## Results

Under the Warm-Up then Freeze (W2F) protocol on VSPW, DiTTA achieves 51.1 mIoU with only 10% of frames used for adaptation, surpassing the ISS baseline (SegFormer, 49.0 mIoU) by +2.1 points and the best fully-supervised VSS model (TV3S, 49.8 mIoU) by +1.3 points. At 50% warm-up, DiTTA reaches 52.3 mIoU and 94.9 mVC8, outperforming zero-shot SAM2 refinement (50.1 mIoU) while running ~10× faster (13.45 vs. 1.41 FPS). In the full-video adaptation setting, DiTTA attains 53.2 mIoU, exceeding THE-Mask (52.1) and CFFM++ (50.1). Cross-dataset evaluation (VSPW→Cityscapes) yields 46.9 mIoU, a +2.7 gain over the ISS baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiTTA outperforms all nine baselines on mIoU across every warm-up ratio. Against the ISS baseline (SegFormer [59]), DiTTA gains +2.1 mIoU at 10% warm-up and +3.6 at 50%, while also improving mVC8 by +5.8 and mVC16 by +7.9, confirming that temporal consistency is the primary source of gain. Versus fully-supervised VSS models (CFFM [45], CFFM++ [47], TV3S [15]), DiTTA leads by +1.3 to +2.5 mIoU despite requiring no video annotations, and runs 2–4× faster (13.45 vs. 5.7–6.0 FPS). Among TTA baselines (TENT [53], AuxAdapt [41], CoTTA [55]), DiTTA surpasses the strongest (CoTTA) by +1.5 mIoU at 10% and +2.5 at 50%, with substantially larger mVC improvements (+4.4 to +6.6 mVC8), indicating that distilling SAM2's temporal knowledge is more effective than entropy minimization or pseudo-labeling alone. Compared to zero-shot SAM2 refinement, DiTTA achieves +1.4 to +2.2 mIoU while being ~10× faster (13.45 vs. 1.41 FPS), since SAM2 is invoked only during the brief warm-up phase rather than on every frame. In the full-video setting (Table 5), DiTTA (53.2 mIoU) also beats THE-Mask [1] (52.1) and CFFM++ [47] (50.1). Cross-dataset (VSPW→Cityscapes), DiTTA (46.9 mIoU) edges out CFFM (46.1) and CoTTA (46.3) by +0.6 to +0.8 mIoU. Finally, DiTTA generalizes to a different ISS backbone (OCRNet [66]), improving mIoU from 34.5 to 36.1 under 50% W2F, confirming model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiTTA sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) fully supervised VSS methods (CFFM, TV3S) that require large annotated video datasets, (2) frame-wise ISS inference that avoids annotation but ignores temporal coherence, and (3) test-time adaptation (TENT, CoTTA, AuxAdapt) that has been applied to image segmentation but not meaningfully to video. The key conceptual departure is using SAM2 not as a repeated inference-time refiner (zero-shot refinement) or as a model to fine-tune on VSS data, but as a one-shot teacher whose temporal propagation knowledge is distilled into a lightweight ISS model during a brief warm-up phase. This repurposes knowledge distillation—typically a training-time technique—as a test-time mechanism, and introduces a temporal fusion add-on (cross-attention with reliability-weighted blending) that structurally converts a frame-wise model into a video-aware one without architectural retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DiTTA eliminates the need for annotated video data entirely and reduces inference cost by ~10× compared to repeated SAM2 invocation, making VSS feasible in resource-constrained settings (on-robot, surveillance). The W2F protocol is a realistic and useful evaluation setting that the field has lacked. Open questions include whether the adapted model can be transferred across similar videos without re-adaptation, how performance degrades on very long sequences where the initial 10% may not be representative, and whether the approach extends to instance-level or panoptic video segmentation. The method's dependence on SAM2 during the adaptation phase (even if brief) remains a practical constraint in fully offline or privacy-sensitive deployments.
