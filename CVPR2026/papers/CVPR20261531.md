## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting degrades significantly under sparse-view conditions, where insufficient photometric supervision produces irregular Gaussian distributions characterized by globally sparse coverage, blurred backgrounds, and distorted high-frequency regions. Existing sparse-view 3DGS methods (e.g., FSGS, DropGaussian) apply only partial regularization, leaving the Gaussian field without comprehensive, multi-level guidance to simultaneously address global structure, local texture, and geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261531/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2, 3, or 6 sparse RGB training views with their camera poses and an SfM point cloud from COLMAP; on LLFF, images are downsampled 8×, while Tanks&Temples uses full resolution. Between each pair of consecutive training views, the BIMVFI frame-interpolation model synthesizes S−1=3 intermediate pseudo-labels whose camera poses are initialized via slerp (rotation) and linear interpolation (translation) and then treated as learnable parameters. The output is a compact 3D Gaussian representation (fewer Gaussians than the FSGS baseline) that supports real-time novel-view rendering, supervised jointly by photometric losses (L1, D-SSIM), depth Pearson-correlation loss, and edge/patch-based feature cues.

## Method

HeroGS establishes a three-level hierarchical guidance framework built on FSGS. At the image level, pseudo-dense supervision from interpolated frames globally regularizes Gaussian distributions and enriches gradient propagation. At the feature level, FADP performs edge-aware densification (back-projecting points along detected edges with KNN inverse-distance-weighted attribute initialization) combined with an 8×8 patch-based density controller that piecewise reweights per-patch Gaussian counts to boost underrepresented background regions and suppress over-dense areas. At the parameter level, CPG trains two auxiliary Gaussian fields alongside the primary field; after a predefined iteration, the auxiliaries are partially frozen (scale and rotation fixed), and unilateral co-pruning removes primary-field Gaussians whose nearest-neighbor distance in the frozen fields exceeds a threshold δ=5, eliminating geometrically inconsistent splats. The three levels are interdependent: image-level pseudo-labels provide structural cues that make FADP's patch counts more meaningful, and the refined distributions from FADP enable CPG to more accurately identify erroneous Gaussians.

## Results

On LLFF with 2 training views, HeroGS achieves 18.78 PSNR / 0.595 SSIM / 0.317 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline CoR-GS (17.38 PSNR) by 1.40 dB. With 3 views, HeroGS reaches 21.30 PSNR / 0.739 SSIM / 0.189 LPIPS, and with 6 views it attains 24.59 PSNR / 0.837 SSIM / 0.135 LPIPS. On Tanks&Temples (no downsampling), HeroGS scores 17.51 PSNR (3 views) and 24.70 PSNR (6 views), consistently leading all 3DGS-based baselines on PSNR and SSIM across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HeroGS outperforms all nine baselines on PSNR and SSIM across every view count on LLFF. The margin is largest under the extreme 2-view setting, where HeroGS leads the nearest competitor CoR-GS by 1.40 dB in PSNR and 0.056 in SSIM, and surpasses DropGaussian by 1.46 dB. At 3 views, HeroGS gains 0.75 dB over DropGaussian (20.55) and 0.85 dB over CoR-GS (20.45) in PSNR. At 6 views, the gap narrows: HeroGS edges out DropGaussian by only 0.04 dB in PSNR and 0.002 in SSIM, but loses on LPIPS (0.135 vs. 0.117), indicating slightly less perceptual quality in the densest setting. On Tanks&Temples, HeroGS leads all baselines on PSNR and SSIM at both 3 and 6 views (e.g., 24.70 vs. 24.15 PSNR at 6 views), though it again trails DropGaussian on LPIPS (0.272 vs. 0.215). NeRF-based methods (Mip-NeRF, SparseNeRF, FrugalNeRF) are consistently outperformed by 3DGS-based approaches, confirming the efficiency advantage of explicit Gaussian representations even under sparse supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

HeroGS is built directly on FSGS as its implementation base and inherits its accelerated densification pipeline, while its parameter-level CPG module is explicitly motivated by CoR-GS's multi-field co-regularization. The key conceptual departure is the hierarchical, multi-level guidance strategy: prior sparse-view 3DGS methods (DNGaussian, SparseGS, CoherentGS, DropGaussian) each apply a single regularization signal (depth, diffusion, monocular priors, dropout), whereas HeroGS composes image-level pseudo-dense supervision via frame interpolation, feature-level edge/patch-aware densification, and parameter-level co-pruning into a unified pipeline where each level feeds structural cues to the next. The use of a 2D frame interpolation model (BIMVFI) to synthesize pseudo-labels for 3D scene optimization is a novel repurposing not seen in prior 3DGS sparse-view work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hierarchical, multi-level guidance yields super-additive gains over any single regularization (Table 6: +0.9 PSNR for the second level, +1.1 for the third), suggesting that sparse-view 3DGS benefits from structured, complementary supervision rather than a single strong prior. Practically, the method achieves compact representations (fewer Gaussians than baseline) while improving fidelity, which matters for real-time rendering and memory-constrained deployment. Limitations include dependence on COLMAP-based SfM initialization (which fails on some 2-view scenes), reliance on a 2D frame interpolation model that lacks 3D awareness, and evaluation restricted to LLFF and Tanks&Temples; open questions include scalability to very large or dynamic scenes and whether the frame-interpolation dependency can be replaced by learned 3D-aware priors.
