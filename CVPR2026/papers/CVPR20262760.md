## Problem

Existing generative models (diffusion and visual autoregressive networks) rely on a single fixed set of pretrained parameters for all inputs, fundamentally limiting their ability to specialize to the nuances of each prompt or class. While test-time training can adapt weights per input, it is prohibitively expensive for high-resolution diffusion and VAR backbones, and Mixture-of-Experts approaches require architectural changes and full retraining. The paper asks whether per-input parameter specialization can be achieved at inference time with negligible overhead, without iterative optimization or backbone modification.

## Input / Output

Composer takes a conditioning signal (a class label for ImageNet or a text prompt for COCO) together with the frozen pretrained weight matrices (specifically W_Q and W_V from transformer attention layers) and produces instance-specific low-rank update matrices A ∈ R^{d×r} and B ∈ R^{r×d} (r=8 in all experiments). These updates are merged into the backbone as W' = W + AB in a single forward pass before multi-step generation begins, after which the adapted weights are used normally for the full denoising or autoregressive decoding process. At inference, the initial linear projection layers are removed and the learned token representations A⁰ and B⁰ are stored, so the meta-generator runs once per input with only ~0.04–7% additional inference time and ~3.6–5% peak memory over the standard model.

## Method

Composer is a plug-in transformer-based meta-generator that synthesizes instance-specific low-rank parameter updates from the conditioning signal. During training, tokens for A and B are initialized by linearly projecting the pretrained W_Q and W_V matrices into 2r tokens of dimension d_model, then concatenated with prompt tokens and processed by an encoder-style transformer using a hybrid attention scheme: all component tokens attend to prompt tokens for context, the first token of each block attends across blocks for inter-block correlation, and remaining tokens use local block-wise attention to reduce cost. The training pipeline employs context-aware batch sampling with a ratio α (default 0.75), mixing same-class/similar-prompt samples for consistent adaptation with cross-class samples for diversity, and optimizes the standard diffusion objective (or knowledge-distillation loss for quantization-aware variants) through the composed weights W' = W + AB. At inference, the meta-generator runs once to produce A and B, which are merged into the frozen backbone, enabling per-input specialization with a single additional forward pass and no gradient computation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Composer consistently achieves the best FID across all five backbones: 3.15 (VAR d-16), 2.08 (VAR d-24), 1.79 (VAR d-30), 4.41 (DiT-L/2), and 2.06 (DiT-XL/2), outperforming both Standard and Test-time Training. On text-to-image (SD2.1, COCO 2014 30K), Composer reduces FID-30K to 13.07 and raises CLIP-30K to 0.32. Critically, Composer adds only 0.2–7% inference time and ~3.6% peak memory over Standard, whereas Test-time Training incurs 142–11,137% time overhead and 93–180% memory overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Test-time Training (Hübotter et al., 2024), Composer wins on every backbone and metric: e.g., on VAR d-30 it achieves FID 1.79 vs. 1.85 (TTT) while running 105× faster (1.07 s vs. 112.37 s) and using 40% less peak memory (16.97 GB vs. 28.41 GB). For post-training quantization, Composer improves upon both Q-Diffusion (Li et al., ICCV 2023) and CTEC (Li & Du, AAAI 2025): at extreme 2/8-bit, Q-Diffusion+Composer raises IS from 49.08 to 78.21 and Precision from 43.18% to 55.2%, while CTEC+Composer lifts IS from 176.37 to 191.74 and FID from 7.43 to 7.11. In the test-time scaling setting, stacking Composer on top of ORM and PARM (Guo et al., 2025) further reduces FID-30K (13.15→12.87 for ORM; 13.07→12.82 for PARM) and increases CLIP-30K (0.33→0.34) with negligible added cost (+0.1% time, +5% memory). In all four comparison settings, Composer is the best-performing method on the primary quality metric while maintaining near-zero overhead relative to the unmodified backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Composer extends the LoRA paradigm (W' = W + AB) by making the low-rank factors A and B instance-specific rather than shared across all inputs, generated on-the-fly by a lightweight transformer meta-generator conditioned on the prompt or class embedding. It departs from test-time training (TTT), which achieves per-input adaptation through iterative gradient optimization, by replacing that loop with a single forward pass of the meta-generator. The approach also contrasts with Mixture-of-Experts architectures, which provide conditional computation but require architectural changes and full retraining, whereas Composer is a plug-in applicable to off-the-shelf frozen backbones. The context-aware training pipeline (mixing same-class and cross-class samples) is a methodological contribution that balances adaptation consistency with output diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that Composer delivers instance-specific adaptation at essentially zero inference overhead (+0.06–7% time, +3.6–5% memory) compared to the frozen backbone, while TTT incurs 100×–10,000× time penalties—making per-input specialization viable for real-time and resource-constrained deployment. The quantization-aware extension is particularly notable, restoring substantial quality at extreme 2-bit weight settings where standard quantization collapses. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation or very few-step (1–4 step) diffusion models where the relative overhead becomes more visible, and whether the meta-generator can be fine-tuned for downstream tasks while keeping the backbone frozen, as the authors suggest for future work.
