## Problem

Real-world driving corpora collected from human experts are dominated by common scenarios, leaving safety-critical and out-of-distribution states severely underrepresented, which limits end-to-end planners' robustness and generalization. While neural-rendering simulation can synthesize OOD states at scale, prior work lacks a systematic recipe for generating feasible expert demonstrations from perturbed states and for characterizing how simulation data scaling interacts with different planner architectures under a fixed real-world corpus.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262752/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The planners consume multi-view RGB images at 2048×512 resolution over a history window of T frames plus ego vehicle state, and output a predicted future trajectory of horizon H; scoring-based planners additionally predict per-trajectory reward scores distilling EPDMS sub-metrics. LiDAR inputs are removed to match the navhard evaluation protocol. The simulation pipeline itself takes a real driving clip, perturbs the ego trajectory over [T, T+H], rolls out a reactive environment (IDM for surrounding agents, LQR for ego), and renders multi-view video via a 3DGS engine, producing training samples spanning T+2H timesteps.

## Method

SimScale constructs large-scale simulation data by (1) sampling diverse yet plausible ego trajectory perturbations from a 16K-trajectory vocabulary with spatially sparse endpoint sampling, (2) generating pseudo-expert demonstrations from the resulting OOD states using either a recovery-based expert (retrieving the nearest human trajectory by pose matching) or a planner-based expert (PDM-Closed rule-based planner), and (3) rendering photorealistic multi-view observations via block-wise 3DGS reconstruction in a reactive environment where surrounding agents respond via IDM. A simple sim-real co-training strategy randomly samples from a mixture of fixed real data and progressively added non-overlapping simulation samples, preserving the human driving distribution while mitigating visual domain artifacts. The paper's key empirical findings are that exploratory (planner-based) experts outperform conservative (recovery-based) ones under scaling, that multimodal planners (diffusion) exhibit near-linear scaling while single-mode regression saturates, and that reward-only supervision in simulation can match or exceed expert-trajectory supervision for scoring-based planners.

## Results

On the navhard benchmark (244 challenging real-world + 4,164 synthetic scenarios), SimScale co-training yields substantial EPDMS gains across all three planner paradigms: LTF improves from 24.4 to 30.2 (+24%), DiffusionDrive from 27.5 to 32.6 (+19%), and GTRS-Dense (V2-99) from 41.9 to 48.0 (+15%), establishing a new SOTA among sensor-based methods. On the broader navtest benchmark (12,146 diverse scenarios), consistent but smaller improvements are observed, up to +2.9 EPDMS for LTF (81.5→84.4). The best SimScale result (GTRS-Dense V2-99 with reward scoring, 48.0 EPDMS) approaches but remains below PDM-Closed (51.3), which uses ground-truth symbolic inputs rather than sensor data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates SimScale as a data augmentation layer applied to three existing planner architectures rather than proposing a new planner. Against LTF (regression-based, 56M params), SimScale co-training with pseudo-expert supervision lifts navhard EPDMS from 24.4 to 30.2, a 24% relative gain, with the largest absolute improvement in Stage 2 (39.2→44.8). Against DiffusionDrive (diffusion-based, 61M params), the gain is 27.5→32.6 (+19%), with Stage 2 improving by 6.3 points. Against GTRS-Dense (scoring-based), reward-only scoring in simulation (without expert trajectories) yields the largest absolute gain: 38.3→46.9 for ResNet34 (+22%) and 41.9→48.0 for the larger V2-99 backbone (+15%). All three SimScale-augmented planners remain below PDM-Closed (51.3 EPDMS), which is a rule-based planner with access to ground-truth symbolic inputs, confirming the gap between learned and oracle-based planning. On navtest, improvements are more modest (0.8–2.9 EPDMS), suggesting the simulation data primarily benefits robustness in safety-critical OOD scenarios rather than general driving competence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sim-real co-training paradigm from robotics (RT-2, GR00T N1, Sim-and-Real co-training) to end-to-end autonomous driving, combining 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic multi-view rendering with reactive traffic simulation (IDM) and pseudo-expert trajectory generation. It departs from prior driving simulation work (RAD, Resim, MTGS) by addressing the critical gap of generating feasible expert demonstrations for out-of-distribution states, and from pseudo-simulation benchmarks (NAVSIMv2) by providing a systematic data-scaling analysis rather than a fixed evaluation protocol. The work also builds on the three dominant end-to-end planner paradigms—regression (TransFuser), diffusion (DiffusionDrive), and vocabulary scoring (GTRS-Dense)—to test whether simulation data benefits are model-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SimScale provides a practical, fully automated pipeline for scaling end-to-end driving data without additional real-world collection, and its finding that simulation scaling is smooth and predictable (fitted by log-quadratic curves) gives practitioners a clear roadmap for data budgeting. The key open questions include whether the 3DGS rendering quality bottleneck limits further scaling, whether the findings generalize beyond the NAVSIM/nuPlan ecosystem to larger or more diverse datasets, and whether the computational cost of reactive simulation (LQR + IDM rollouts) remains tractable at 10× or 100× data scales. The paper also leaves open whether the advantage of exploratory over conservative pseudo-experts persists when the real-world corpus itself is expanded.
