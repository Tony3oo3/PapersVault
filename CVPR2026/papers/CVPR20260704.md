## Problem

Video diffusion transformers rely on quadratic softmax attention, which severely limits scalability for longer sequences and on-device deployment. While linear attention offers O(N) complexity and constant memory via RNN reformulation, its kernel-based similarity lacks the expressiveness of the exponential kernel, and existing hybrid designs remain quadratic and cannot be reformulated as RNNs. The paper asks whether a SOTA bidirectional softmax model can be distilled into a recurrent hybrid form that preserves fidelity while achieving linear compute and constant memory, without prohibitively expensive retraining.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and generates a video clip at 480×832 resolution (81 frames for 5 seconds) in the full-resolution setting, or 320×480 for ablations. A key structural detail is that latents are generated chunk-by-chunk (Tc temporal slices at a time) in a recurrent fashion, meaning the model can in principle produce arbitrarily long videos with constant peak memory. The distilled model is demonstrated running on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 4 SoC, where it extends to 161 frames (~10 s) without out-of-memory errors, unlike flash attention which OOMs at 141 frames.

## Method

ReHyAt partitions attention into temporally chunked softmax (within overlapping chunks of Tc slices plus To overlap) and global linear attention (over all remaining tokens), jointly normalized. By making the linear component causal and the softmax component local to each chunk, the entire mechanism reformulates as a chunk-wise RNN with state variables s_t and z_t that accumulate kernel-weighted key-value sums, yielding O(N) compute and O(1) memory in video length. The linear attention uses learnable polynomial kernel feature maps (degree 1 to P per head) to better approximate the exponential kernel. Training follows a two-stage pipeline: (1) per-block attention distillation where only φ_q and φ_k are optimized to match a bidirectional softmax teacher's activations (no video data needed), followed by (2) ~1k steps of flow-matching fine-tuning on a modest prompt/video set, totaling ~160 H100 GPU-hours.

## Results

On VBench, ReHyAt (15×Tc=3, To=1 distilled from Wan2.1 1.3B) achieves a Total score of 83.79, the highest among all compared models, with Quality 84.57 and Semantic 80.70. On VBench-2.0, ReHyAt 15×Tc=3 scores 56.1 Total (vs. 56.0 for Wan2.1 1.3B and 53.4 for CogVideoX-1.5 5B), with the best Creativity score (55.1) among all tested methods. In a 500-pair human preference study, ReHyAt is statistically indistinguishable from the original Wan2.1 (27.6% prefer ReHyAt, 43.5% no preference, 29.0% prefer Wan2.1). On-device (Snapdragon 8 Gen 4), ReHyAt is the only attention variant that avoids OOM beyond 121 frames, running ~16× faster than Flash Attention at 121 frames with ~11× less memory read/write.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original Wan2.1 1.3B teacher, ReHyAt improves Total VBench from 83.31 to 83.79 and Semantic from 75.65 to 80.70, at a small Quality cost (84.57 vs. 85.23); the human preference study confirms no perceptible degradation (43.5% no preference). Versus the concurrent linear-only SANA-Video, ReHyAt edges ahead on Total (83.79 vs. 83.71) and Quality (84.57 vs. 84.35) while trailing slightly on Semantic (80.70 vs. 81.35), but requires only ~160 GPU-hours versus SANA-Video's ~12 days on 64 H100s—a two-order-of-magnitude reduction. Compared to the authors' prior Attention Surgery (15×R2), ReHyAt gains +0.58 Total and +5.45 Semantic while sacrificing 0.62 Quality, and crucially achieves linear complexity and RNN reformulation that Attention Surgery lacks. Against larger models (CogVideoX 5B at 81.91, CogVideoX 1.5 5B at 82.01), ReHyAt's 83.79 Total is competitive despite having only 1.3B parameters. On-device, ReHyAt (Tc=3) is the sole method that completes 161-frame generation without OOM on Snapdragon 8 Gen 4, running at 384 ms per DiT block versus 4809 ms for Flash Attention at 121 frames (~16× speedup) and 3.5/5.6 GB memory read/write versus 22.7/53.6 GB for Flash Attention (~11× reduction). Against HedgeHog linear attention and Uniform Hybrid-R8, ReHyAt is consistently faster and more memory-efficient across all tested durations, with the gap widening as video length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReHyAt extends the authors' own prior work on Attention Surgery [11], which introduced a temporally uniform hybrid of softmax and linear attention but retained quadratic complexity and could not be reformulated as an RNN. Here, the key departure is a temporally *non-uniform* chunked arrangement (softmax within overlapping temporal chunks, linear attention for all other tokens) that makes the hybrid attention causal and thus amenable to a chunk-wise recurrent reformulation with constant memory. The method also builds directly on the linear-attention-as-RNN formulation of Katharopoulos et al. [20] and on the Wan2.1 DiT backbone [35], but its distinguishing contribution is a two-stage distillation pipeline (per-block attention matching followed by brief flow-matching fine-tuning) that converts a bidirectional softmax teacher into a recurrent hybrid student in ~160 GPU-hours, in contrast to SANA-Video [4] which trains a linear-attention model from scratch at roughly two orders of magnitude higher cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: ReHyAt is the only evaluated attention variant that avoids out-of-memory errors beyond 10 seconds on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 4 SoC, and it runs ~16× faster than FlashAttention at 121 frames, making on-device long-video generation feasible for the first time in a DiT setting. The distillation recipe is broadly applicable—any future bidirectional softmax video DiT could, in principle, be converted to a recurrent hybrid form with a few hundred GPU-hours of compute, dramatically lowering the barrier to efficient deployment. Open questions include whether the mild temporal incoherence observed in the most aggressive (smallest T_c) variants can be eliminated, whether the recipe transfers cleanly to larger or architecturally different SOTA models (e.g., 14B+ parameter DiTs), and whether the polynomial kernel feature map can be further simplified without quality loss.
