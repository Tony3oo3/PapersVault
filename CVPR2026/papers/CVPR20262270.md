## Problem

Existing 3D spatial foundation models (VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R) accept only RGB images as input, ignoring geometric cues such as depth maps and camera intrinsics/poses that are readily available in real-world applications (VR/AR, autonomous driving, robotics). While Pow3R attempted to incorporate auxiliary inputs, it is limited to at most two modalities, whereas practical scenarios often provide an arbitrary number of geometric inputs across a multi-view sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262270/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests a set of N images, each optionally paired with camera parameters (3×3 intrinsics K and 4×4 pose G) or a depth map with a binary validity mask; the number of images carrying camera info (Q) and depth info (O) can independently range from 0 to N, enabling any partial-information combination at inference. Outputs are per-frame 3D point maps, full camera intrinsics and extrinsics, dense depth maps, and confidence maps, all produced in a single feed-forward pass with inference speed comparable to vanilla VGGT (~0.2 s).

## Method

OmniVGGT builds on VGGT's 24-layer Alternating-Attention encoder and introduces a lightweight GeoAdapter (26.8 M extra parameters) with two branches: a camera adapter that normalizes poses relative to the first view, encodes them via per-layer linear encoders into auxiliary camera tokens, and injects them through zero-initialized convolutions to avoid destabilizing the pretrained representation space; and a depth adapter that batch-normalizes depth maps, tokenizes them with a single 14-kernel convolution, and adds them directly to spatial tokens. A stochastic multimodal fusion training strategy randomly assigns ground-truth camera parameters to the first Q images and depth annotations to O randomly selected images per batch (with a probability p of using RGB-only), so the model learns robust spatial representations rather than overfitting to auxiliary cues, while supporting arbitrary modality subsets at test time.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, OmniVGGT achieves 85.9% AUC@30° on RealEstate10K and 88.4% on CO3Dv2 in the RGB-only setting, surpassing VGGT (85.3%/88.2%) while matching its ~0.2s inference speed. With auxiliary camera parameters (K+RT), it reaches 88.5%/93.4%, outperforming Pow3R w/K (72.5%/82.2%) by up to 16% while running ~30× faster. For monocular depth, OmniVGGT w/D achieves near-perfect δ<1.25 (99.9%) on Bonn and NYU-v2, and 90.2% on Sintel, beating Pow3R w/D (99.7%/99.8%/87.4%). On 7-Scenes 3D reconstruction, injecting camera parameters reduces mean accuracy error from 0.104 to 0.036 (a 65.4% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VGGT (the direct architectural base), OmniVGGT wins in the RGB-only setting on all tasks: +0.6 δ<1.25 on Sintel depth, +0.6% AUC@30° on Re10K pose, and competitive 7-Scenes reconstruction (0.104 vs 0.087 Acc, slightly worse without auxiliary input). With auxiliary inputs, OmniVGGT dominates: 88.5% vs 85.3% on Re10K pose, 99.9% vs 97.3% δ<1.25 on Bonn depth, and 0.036 vs 0.087 Acc on 7-Scenes. Against Pow3R (the closest prior work on multimodal 3D reconstruction), OmniVGGT wins decisively: 88.5% vs 72.5% on Re10K with camera priors, 90.2% vs 87.4% on Sintel depth with depth priors, and runs ~30× faster (~0.2s vs >7s). Against other feed-forward baselines (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R, Fast3R, FLARE), OmniVGGT leads on both pose and depth metrics in the RGB-only setting, e.g., 88.4% vs 83.4% (VGGsfM) on CO3Dv2. On the CALVIN robot manipulation benchmark, OmniVGGT-enhanced VLA outperforms the point-cloud-based Kosmos-VLA baseline by 0.04 Avg. Len (ABCD→D) and 0.43 Avg. Len (zero-shot ABC→D), confirming practical utility beyond standard 3D benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniVGGT is a direct architectural extension of VGGT, retaining its DINO backbone, Alternating-Attention encoder, DPT prediction heads, and multi-task training objective, while adding a lightweight GeoAdapter (zero-initialized convolutions for camera, direct addition for depth) to inject auxiliary geometric modalities. It departs from the RGB-only paradigm shared by VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R, and CUT3R, and from Pow3R's limited two-input multimodal scheme, by enabling an arbitrary number of depth and camera-parameter inputs per image set. The camera encoding follows VGGsfM's quaternion-translation-FOV parameterization, and the stochastic multimodal fusion training strategy is novel to this work, drawing on the broader idea of data augmentation via random modality dropout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

In practical 3D vision pipelines (VR/AR, autonomous driving, robotics), depth sensors and calibrated cameras are routinely available yet were being discarded by spatial foundation models; OmniVGGT closes this gap with negligible inference overhead, making it immediately deployable in settings where auxiliary data exists. The VLA integration on CALVIN demonstrates that richer spatial tokens translate into measurable robotic manipulation gains, suggesting a path toward geometry-grounded embodied AI. Open questions include whether the stochastic fusion strategy generalizes to truly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., LiDAR point clouds, semantic maps) and whether the zero-convolution injection mechanism transfers to other foundation-model backbones beyond the VGGT/DINO family.
