## Problem

Controllable video generation with DiT-based diffusion models faces three critical bottlenecks: fine-tuning a ControlNet per condition on billion-parameter video models is prohibitively expensive (~0.5B parameters, 48+ GPU hours), cascaded ControlNets cannot dynamically compose novel multi-condition inputs without retraining, and image-derived adapters fail to maintain temporal coherence across video frames. The paper identifies that small and large models within the same architecture family exhibit strong latent feature similarity, suggesting that control knowledge can be transferred across scales without full fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt together with one or more visual conditions (Canny edge maps, depth maps, OpenPose skeletons, or arbitrary compositions such as camera trajectory + background + human motion) and outputs a temporally coherent video. Conditions are first encoded by a frozen small teacher diffusion model, whose intermediate features are then routed through the adapter into the frozen large student model. Training uses 15K videos from Koala-36M with extracted auxiliary signals, and evaluation is conducted on a manually curated set of 100 high-quality reference videos using FVD, LPIPS, SSIM, and CLIP Score.

## Method

Tea-Adapter performs reverse knowledge distillation: a small, condition-tuned teacher model (e.g., Wan2.1-1.3B) transfers control features to a large frozen student model (e.g., Wan2.1-14B) through a lightweight plug-and-play adapter, with both backbone models kept frozen. The adapter comprises three components: (1) a Mixture of Condition Experts (MCE) layer with shared and condition-specific experts that dynamically routes condition tokens via a softmax gating function g_k(c_k, t) = Softmax(MLP_g([c_k; t])), enabling sparse multi-condition fusion and zero-shot generalization to unseen conditions; (2) a Feature Propagation Module with a learnable scale modulation, time projection, and up-projection layer that adaptively scales and projects small-model features into the large model's latent space at each denoising timestep; and (3) attention modules that bridge the cross-scale gap. Features are injected additively into selected DiT blocks (first, last, and middle) of the large model, and only the adapter parameters are trained, reducing trainable parameters by ~70% compared to DiT-ControlNet while supporting dynamic composition of arbitrary condition sets in a single forward pass.

## Results

On the Wan2.1-14B backbone, Tea-Adapter achieves FVD of 289.6 (Canny), 292.3 (Depth), and 300.6 (Pose), with CLIP scores of 0.918, 0.913, and 0.903 respectively, and the best Temporal Consistency score of 0.984 among all compared methods. These results are obtained with only ~10K training videos on a single H100 GPU over 2 days, compared to >100K videos and multiple GPUs used by baselines like Ctrl-Adapter. The method reduces trainable parameters by 70% (without MCE) relative to DiT-ControlNet while maintaining competitive or superior generation quality across all three control conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tea-Adapter substantially outperforms all ControlNet-based baselines: versus DiT-ControlNet, it reduces FVD by ~32% (292 vs 541 on Depth) and improves CLIP by 0.184 (0.913 vs 0.729); versus UniControl and Uni-ControlNet, it gains 0.34–0.37 CLIP points and 0.45–0.53 LPIPS improvement across conditions. Against the closest adapter-based competitor Ctrl-Adapter, Tea-Adapter achieves 33–44% lower FVD (289–301 vs 427–487) and 0.12–0.20 higher CLIP scores, while also improving SSIM by 0.27–0.29 on Pose. X-Adapter and Uni-ControlNet, being image-based methods, show the weakest temporal consistency (0.754 and 0.763) and are outperformed on every available metric. The only method that beats Tea-Adapter is full fine-tuning of Wan2.1-14B, which requires training the entire 14B-parameter model and is the resource-intensive approach Tea-Adapter is designed to avoid; Tea-Adapter closes most of that gap (e.g., 292 vs 254 FVD on Depth) while training only the lightweight adapter.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tea-Adapter extends the ControlNet/T2I-Adapter lineage for conditional diffusion generation by replacing the standard 'train a parallel ControlNet on the large model' paradigm with a cross-scale reverse distillation strategy: a small, already-conditioned teacher model injects control features into a frozen large DiT through a lightweight adapter. The Mixture of Condition Experts (MCE) layer repurposes the sparsely-gated MoE architecture for dynamic routing over heterogeneous visual conditions, departing from the cascaded multi-ControlNet approach (MultiControlNet, UniControlNet) that requires separate branches per condition. The Feature Propagation Module with timestep-aware modulation addresses the temporal consistency gap identified in Ctrl-Adapter and DiT-ControlNet, where injected features lack explicit alignment with the denoising schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: training on a single H100 for ~2 days versus 48+ GPU hours for a full ControlNet makes multi-condition video control accessible to small labs and industry teams without large compute budgets. The zero-shot generalization to unseen conditions (scribble, normal map, trajectory) via MCE expert sharing is a promising direction, though it is demonstrated on only three conditions and a single architecture family (Wan2.1). Open questions include whether the reverse distillation gap widens as the teacher-student scale ratio grows, how the method handles conditions with no pre-trained small-model counterpart, and whether the additive feature injection (Eq. 6) remains stable at higher resolutions or longer video durations. The paper also acknowledges that the small teacher model must first be fine-tuned, which partially offsets the claimed efficiency gains.
