## Problem

Continual learning for medical Vision-Language Models faces a unique dilemma: medical images are intra-domain uniform yet cross-domain distinct (clustered distribution), requiring simultaneously finer-grained intra-domain feature retention and bridging larger inter-domain gaps than natural images. Existing CL strategies for VLMs rely on static sampling or synthetic replay that cannot capture this clustered structure, and no comprehensive cross-domain benchmark exists to evaluate medical generalist CL across task-incremental and domain-incremental scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260253/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a medical image (X-ray, pathology, fundus, endoscopy, dermoscopy, or cell morphology) together with its task identity, and predicts a class label restricted to the current task's class set. The retrieval pipeline operates on an 18M-scale multimodal database of image-caption pairs from PubMed, queried via a 3,000-entry medical question pool hierarchically organized by domain, anatomical site, and disease type. The backbone is BiomedCLIP ViT-B/16, and each continual task is trained for 1,000 iterations with batch size 64.

## Method

PRIMED integrates three dynamically coupled modules. First, a multi-stage retrieval mechanism performs dense embedding search (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) over the 18M database, reranks candidates via a VLM cross-modal score, applies a BM25 lexical gate, and then uses a Dynamic Siphon that partitions queries into task-specific, domain-related, and general subsets with distinct top-k ratios to create a personalized review plan. Second, a distillation-and-alignment loss combines Contrastive Knowledge Transfer (KL divergence between current and previous model's cross-modal similarity matrices on retrieved data) with Cross-Modality Consistency (mini-batch contrastive loss against the identity matrix) to preserve both task knowledge and zero-shot alignment. Third, a Dynamic Fisher Weight Guard computes parameter importance from the distillation and alignment gradients rather than the standard CE gradient, yielding an adaptively weighted L2 penalty that tightens protection on parameters critical to retained knowledge while allowing plasticity where the current task demands change.

## Results

On the HieraMedTransfer benchmark, PRIMED_dyn achieves SOTA across all metrics: Transfer 58.3 / Avg. 73.1 / Last 82.1 (Order I) and Transfer 48.5 / Avg. 68.0 / Last 81.2 (Order II), improving over the strongest baseline ZSCL by up to +4.5 points on Last accuracy. On the MedXtreme benchmark, PRIMED_dyn reaches ACC 68.6 / AUC 87.4 / BWT −2.7 (Order I) and ACC 68.1 / AUC 86.3 / BWT −3.4 (Order II), outperforming the next-best method GIFT by +2.6 ACC and +0.8 AUC on Order I. The dynamic retrieval variant (PRIMED_dyn) consistently surpasses the uniform-retrieval variant (PRIMED_uni) by 0.4–1.0 points on Transfer and 0.4–1.0 points on Avg., confirming the value of the multi-stage retrieval mechanism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRIMED_dyn outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in both HieraMedTransfer and MedXtreme. The closest competitor is ZSCL [102], which PRIMED_dyn surpasses by +0.6 Transfer, +2.6 Avg., and +4.5 Last on HieraMedTransfer Order I, and by +3.3 Transfer, +3.0 Avg., and +4.3 Last on Order II. On MedXtreme, GIFT [85] is the strongest baseline (ACC 66.0, AUC 86.6 on Order I), but PRIMED_dyn leads by +2.6 ACC and +0.8 AUC while also reducing backward transfer from −3.7 to −2.7. Rehearsal-based iCaRL [68] and generative-based GIFT [85] are competitive on Avg./ACC but trail PRIMED_dyn by 2–5 points, and both require access to original training data or a generative model. Distillation-only methods (LwF [55], SND [97]) degrade substantially on Transfer (43.7 and 52.0 vs. 58.3 for PRIMED_dyn on Order I), confirming that the dynamic RAG retrieval and CMC alignment losses are critical for preserving zero-shot capability. MoE-CL [95] and DIKI [76] achieve mid-range results but fall 1.5–6 points short of PRIMED_dyn on Last accuracy, indicating that architecture-based and prompt-based strategies alone cannot match the fine-grained intra-domain memory that retrieval-guided distillation provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRIMED sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) medical VLM continual learning, where prior work (ZSCL, SND, GIFT, DIKI, MoE-CL) has focused on natural-image reference datasets or single-domain medical tasks; (2) RAG, which has become standard in LLM pipelines but had not been integrated into CL as a dynamic reference-data constructor; and (3) Fisher-based regularization (EWC, Synaptic Intelligence), which this paper extends by computing parameter importance from distillation and alignment gradients rather than the task loss alone. The key conceptual departure is treating retrieval as a form of model memory that can be dynamically re-queried per training step, replacing the static replay or generative-replay paradigms used by iCaRL, GIFT, and ZSCL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MGTIL benchmark (HieraMedTransfer + MedXtreme) is the first to jointly evaluate task-incremental and domain-incremental CL for medical VLMs across nine imaging modalities, filling a gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature. The 18M multimodal retrieval database and 3K question pool are reusable resources that lower the barrier for future medical CL research. Open questions include whether the dynamic retrieval overhead scales to longer task sequences or larger backbones, whether the approach transfers to non-medical VLM CL, and whether the clustered-distribution insight generalizes to other specialized domains (e.g., satellite, microscopy). The paper also notes ethical and copyright constraints on full data release, which may limit reproducibility.
