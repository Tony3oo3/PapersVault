## Problem

Existing zero-shot low-light image enhancement methods rely on auxiliary loss functions, degradation assumptions, and empirically tuned hyperparameters that produce strong results on evaluation datasets but fail to generalize, often hallucinating structures or exhibiting poor color constancy across images of the same scene under different illuminations. No prior zero-shot approach achieves consistent, artifact-free enhancement without per-image optimization or task-specific loss design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260371/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-light or color-imbalanced RGB image as input; if its average intensity falls below 30 (on a 0–255 scale), it is uniformly upscaled to that threshold, with no other preprocessing or post-processing applied. It produces a well-lit, color-corrected RGB image at the same spatial resolution, operating entirely in the latent space of Stable Diffusion v1.5 (T=24 DDIM steps) without any text prompt. The VAE decoder from QuadPrior is used in place of the default Stable Diffusion decoder to avoid latent-compression distortions.

## Method

MR. Illuminate is a three-step, training-free pipeline built on a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net: (1) DDIM-invert the input to obtain a noisy latent z_T^c; (2) apply AdaIN to re-center and re-scale z_T^c toward the N(0,I) statistics expected by the diffusion model, producing z_T^a that corrects global illumination and color cast while preserving spatial structure; (3) denoise z_T^a over 24 steps while injecting the self-attention triplets {Q_t, K_t, V_t} recorded during the inversion into the up-blocks at each timestep, re-anchoring the sampling trajectory to the input's local structure and chromatic relationships. The key insight is the complementary division of labor: AdaIN performs a spatially uniform affine correction of channel-wise means and variances (global photometric alignment), while inversion-time self-attention injection supplies spatially varying structural and color cues (local refinement), and their synergy—propagated through skip and residual connections—yields consistent reconstruction across varying illuminations of the same scene without any auxiliary objective, degradation model, or per-image optimization.

## Results

On the paired LOL benchmark, MR. Illuminate achieves PSNR 21.74 dB, SSIM 0.815, and LPIPS 0.177, outperforming all zero-shot baselines (best: TAO at 19.18 dB) and all unsupervised methods (best: GEFU at 20.83 dB), while remaining competitive with supervised CIDNet (28.92 dB). On LSRW, it reaches PSNR 17.66 dB and LPIPS 0.283, slightly surpassing CIDNet (17.40 dB) and approaching GEFU (18.75 dB). On the unpaired benchmark, MR. Illuminate attains the best BRISQUE (16.18) and NL (0.379) scores among all compared methods, and runs in 0.12 min per image—over 150× faster than GDP (19.09 min) and ~30× faster than TAO (3.5 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MR. Illuminate decisively outperforms all zero-shot diffusion-based baselines: on LOL it gains +2.56 dB PSNR over TAO (21.74 vs 19.18), +7.08 dB over GDP (14.66), and +4.79 dB over FourierDiff (16.95); on LSRW the margins are +1.97 dB over TAO, +4.59 dB over GDP, and +2.03 dB over FourierDiff. Against unsupervised methods, it surpasses GEFU on LOL (21.74 vs 20.83 dB) and Zero-DCE by a wide margin, but trails GEFU on LSRW (17.66 vs 18.75 dB). Among supervised methods, it falls well short of CIDNet on LOL (21.74 vs 28.92 dB) but slightly edges out CIDNet on LSRW (17.66 vs 17.40 dB), demonstrating stronger cross-dataset generalization. On the unpaired benchmark, MR. Illuminate achieves the lowest BRISQUE (16.18) and NL (0.379) scores, beating even supervised CIDNet (24.44, 0.682) and unsupervised GEFU (17.81, 0.635). In terms of efficiency, it runs in 0.12 min per image—159× faster than GDP (19.09 min) and 29× faster than TAO (3.5 min)—while using only 6.7 GB memory, making it the most practical zero-shot option in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot LLIE, where methods like GDP, TAO, FourierDiff, and CoLIE all require per-image optimization guided by auxiliary loss functions or degradation assumptions. Instead, MR. Illuminate repurposes two well-studied components—AdaIN (originally from style transfer) and self-attention injection (previously used in image editing and video consistency tasks)—within a frozen Stable Diffusion v1.5 backbone, eliminating both optimization and degradation modeling entirely. The DDIM-inversion-based initialization aligns with the broader trend of noise initialization strategies (FreeInit, InitNo) but applies AdaIN specifically to match the model's expected N(0,I) input distribution rather than injecting low-frequency structure. The method also extends naturally to auto white balance, a task previously addressed only in the text-to-image domain (SDXL latent-space shifting), making it the first zero-shot AWB method for color-imbalanced images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for per-image optimization, custom loss design, and hyperparameter tuning, this work offers a genuinely 'free lunch' pipeline that practitioners can deploy without dataset-specific calibration—a practical advantage over all prior zero-shot LLIE methods. The demonstrated color constancy (consistent outputs for the same scene under varying illumination) addresses a failure mode that plagues existing zero-shot approaches and is critical for downstream tasks like autonomous driving and surveillance. Limitations include the method's dependence on the frozen diffusion model's representational capacity and occasional minor color deviations from maintaining per-channel means near mid-intensity. Open questions include whether the self-attention injection strategy generalizes to other restoration tasks (deblurring, super-resolution) and whether the color-deviation trade-off can be resolved without reintroducing optimization.
