## Problem

Existing prompt-based incremental object detection methods suffer from prompt degradation caused by two intertwined issues: prompt coupling, where task-general and task-specific prompts stored in a single pool compete and interfere under limited parameter space, and prompt drift, where inconsistent supervision (old foreground objects relabeled as background in later tasks) pushes already-optimized prompts toward incorrect semantic directions. Fixed confidence thresholds used for pseudo-label generation are further ill-calibrated across categories due to distributional discrepancies, exacerbating the drift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images from MS-COCO or PASCAL VOC in a sequential incremental setting where, at each stage, only annotations for newly introduced classes are available while previously learned classes appear unannotated. It outputs bounding boxes with class labels for all previously seen and current classes. A frozen teacher model from the previous stage generates candidate detections that are refined into pseudo-labels to provide supervision for old classes during the current training stage.

## Method

PDP introduces a dual-pool prompt architecture that explicitly decouples task-general prompts (shared pool, 100 tokens, continuously updated across all tasks) from task-specific prompts (private pool, 80 tokens for COCO, frozen per task), with a directional decoupled loss enforcing angular separation between the two pools to prevent representational overlap. Retrieved prompts are injected into each Deformable-DETR decoder layer via prefix-tuning, splitting the aggregated prompt into key and value prefixes prepended to the attention sequences. To counteract prompt drift, the Prototypical Pseudo-Label Generation module maintains per-class prototypes in the embedding space (updated only in the final epoch of each task) and performs hierarchical validation: high-confidence teacher detections are accepted directly, while intermediate-confidence candidates are retained only if their feature similarity to the corresponding class prototype exceeds a threshold, replacing brittle fixed confidence cutoffs with semantically grounded filtering.

## Results

On MS-COCO multi-step incremental detection (4 tasks), PDP achieves 59.4% mAP@A after all tasks, surpassing the previous best MD-DETR (50.2%) by 9.2% and OWOBJ (43.9%) by 15.5%. On PASCAL VOC, PDP reaches 79.4% mAP@A under the 19+1 setting, outperforming the second-best MD-DETR (76.1%) by 3.3%. Under the 40+40 COCO split, PDP achieves 43.8% AP (beating PseDet's 43.5%), while under 70+10 it scores 42.9% AP, slightly below PseDet (44.7%). PDP also demonstrates the strongest stability, with the highest mAP@P across all incremental steps on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDP outperforms all 19 baselines on the MS-COCO multi-step benchmark across every task and metric. The largest margin is against MD-DETR (the base architecture), where PDP gains +9.2% mAP@A on the final task (59.4 vs. 50.2) and +15.5% over OWOBJ (43.9). On PASCAL VOC, PDP wins all three settings (10+10, 15+5, 19+1), with the most notable gain of +3.3% mAP@A over MD-DETR on 19+1 (79.4 vs. 76.1) and +2.9% over RGR on 10+10 (78.7 vs. 75.8). On the 40+40 COCO split, PDP edges out PseDet (43.8 vs. 43.5 AP) and CL-DETR (42.0 AP), but on 70+10 it trails PseDet by 1.8% AP (42.9 vs. 44.7), which the authors attribute to PseDet's non-end-to-end k-means post-processing. PDP also consistently achieves the highest mAP@P (stability) across all tasks, indicating the dual-pool design most effectively resists catastrophic forgetting compared to single-pool methods like L2P, Coda-Prompt, and MD-DETR.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDP builds directly on MD-DETR's frozen encoder-decoder with a learnable memory bank and query function, and on Deformable-DETR as the detection backbone, while adopting DualPrompt's prefix-tuning mechanism for prompt injection into decoder layers. Its key conceptual departure is from the single-pool paradigm used by L2P, Coda-Prompt, MD-DETR, and DualPrompt, which store task-general and task-specific prompts indiscriminately in one shared pool; PDP explicitly splits these into a shared pool (continuously updated for forward transfer) and a private pool (frozen per-task for discrimination). For pseudo-labeling, PDP moves beyond the fixed-confidence-threshold approach of CL-DETR and the k-means-based adaptive threshold of PseDet, instead using dynamically updated class prototypes in the embedding space to validate low-confidence candidates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 9.2% AP gain on MS-COCO multi-step IOD over the strongest prior method (MD-DETR) is substantial for this task, and the diagnostic framing of prompt degradation into two distinct failure modes (coupling vs. drift) provides a useful lens for the broader prompt-based continual learning community. However, the method is tightly coupled to the MD-DETR/Deformable-DETR architecture and requires a teacher model for pseudo-label generation, limiting immediate transferability to other detection backbones or fully self-supervised settings. Open questions include whether the dual-pool paradigm generalizes to segmentation or tracking, whether prototype-based pseudo-labeling remains effective as the class space grows beyond 80 categories, and whether the inter-pool diversity loss is necessary or whether architectural separation alone suffices.
