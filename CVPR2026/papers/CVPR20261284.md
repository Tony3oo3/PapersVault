## Problem

Existing deepfake detectors are largely trained on GAN-era forgeries and fail to generalize to contemporary diffusion- and flow-matching-based generators that produce photorealistic, audio-driven talking-head videos. The paper identifies that internal audio-visual cross-attention in these generators encodes fine-grained speech-motion alignment cues that are exploitable for detection but remain untapped by prior methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261284/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The detector consumes a face video clip (N frames at 512×512) and its paired audio track, which is encoded via wav2vec 2.0 and fed as conditioning to a pretrained audio-driven LDM (Hallo). Internally, DDIM inversion and reconstruction produce a 12-channel video composite (original, decoded noise map, reconstructed video, and absolute residual) and a 320-channel cross-attention tensor at 64×64 latent resolution per frame. The output is a scalar real/fake logit plus an ℓ₂-normalized embedding vector for metric learning.

## Method

X-AVDT probes a frozen audio-conditioned latent diffusion model (Hallo, initialized from Stable Diffusion) via DDIM inversion to extract two complementary signals: a video composite φ that surfaces inversion-induced reconstruction discrepancies, and an audio-visual cross-attention feature ψ captured from the last up block of the 3D U-Net at timestep t=24, which encodes speech-motion synchrony enforced during generation. Two separate 3D ResNeXt encoders process φ and ψ, and their outputs are channel-concatenated, projected, and passed through a Feature Fusion Decoder (self-attention over spatial tokens followed by 3D ResNeXt layers) to produce a fused representation. Training uses a weighted combination of binary cross-entropy and triplet loss (λ=0.3) to jointly optimize classification accuracy and embedding-space discriminability. The paper also introduces MMDF, a 28.8k-clip audio-visual dataset spanning talking-head generation, self-reenactment, and face swapping across GAN, diffusion, and flow-matching generators, enabling cross-generator generalization evaluation.

## Results

On the MMDF dataset (average across three unseen test generators: AniPortrait, MegActor-Σ, HunyuanAvatar), X-AVDT achieves 95.29 AUROC, 94.03 AP, 91.15 Acc@EER, and 91.98% accuracy, outperforming the strongest retrained baseline RealForensics (92.42 AUROC, 81.28% Acc) by +2.87 AUROC and +10.7% accuracy. On the GAN-based benchmarks, X-AVDT (trained on MMDF) reaches 99.69 AUROC on FakeAVCeleb and 89.55 AUROC on FaceForensics++, surpassing all baselines including those with train-test overlap. Human evaluators achieve only 71.88% accuracy on MMDF, confirming the task's difficulty and the detector's substantial advantage over human perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-AVDT outperforms all six baselines across every metric on MMDF. Against the strongest retrained baseline RealForensics [32], X-AVDT gains +2.87 AUROC (95.29 vs. 92.42) and +10.7% accuracy (91.98 vs. 81.28), with the gap widening on the flow-matching generator HunyuanAvatar (97.79 vs. 74.89 AUROC). AVH-Align [78], the second-best retrained method, trails by 13.85 AUROC, while LipFD [54] performs near chance level (53.75 AUROC). On the GAN-based benchmarks, X-AVDT achieves 99.69 AUROC on FakeAVCeleb (beating RealForensics' 99.47 which had train-test overlap) and 89.55 AUROC on FaceForensics++ (vs. RealForensics' 99.47 with overlap, but 88.85 without). Notably, official-pretrained baselines degrade severely on MMDF (e.g., AVH-Align drops to 50.34 AUROC), confirming that prior detectors overfit to legacy GAN artifacts and fail to generalize to diffusion and flow-matching generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-AVDT extends the inversion-based detection paradigm established by DIRE and FakeInversion from the image domain to audio-visual video, but its key departure is the exploitation of generator-internal audio-visual cross-attention as a discriminative signal rather than relying solely on reconstruction discrepancies. While prior audio-visual deepfake detectors (RealForensics, AVAD, AVH-Align) fuse separately encoded modalities at a late stage, this paper instead probes the cross-attention layers inside a pretrained diffusion U-Net, treating the model's own alignment mechanism as an interpretable forensic cue. The MMDF dataset further departs from prior benchmarks (FaceForensics++, DFDC, FakeAVCeleb) by being the first to include diffusion- and flow-matching-generated forgeries alongside GAN-based ones, addressing a coverage gap the authors identify as limiting generalization research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MMDF dataset fills a critical gap by providing the first audio-visual benchmark spanning GAN, diffusion (U-Net and transformer), and flow-matching generators, making it a practical resource for evaluating cross-generator generalization as synthesis paradigms continue to evolve. However, the ~1-minute-per-clip computational cost of full DDIM inversion and reconstruction is a significant barrier to real-time or large-scale deployment, and the method's reliance on speech-driven features limits applicability to non-speech segments and multi-speaker scenes. Open questions include whether lightweight inversion via distillation can reduce cost, whether speech-agnostic correspondence cues can extend the approach beyond phonetic alignment, and whether the cross-attention signal remains discriminative as future generators adopt fundamentally different conditioning architectures.
