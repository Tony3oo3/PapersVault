## Problem

Large vision-language models are highly vulnerable to multimodal jailbreak attacks that exploit visual-textual interactions to bypass safety guardrails, yet existing defenses either require costly fine-tuning on curated safety data or rely on inference-time image-to-text conversion that incurs information loss and substantial computational overhead. No prior work has explored optimizing VLM key-value caches as a mechanism for safety enhancement, leaving a gap for an efficient, reference-free inference-time defense.

## Input / Output

At inference time, DTR takes a multimodal query (text prompt concatenated with an image encoded into n visual tokens by a CLIP-style encoder) and a pre-computed refusal direction d_ref (derived from 32 harmful prompts from AdvBench and 32 harmless prompts from AlpacaEval). It produces a defended text response from the VLM along with a per-token scaling vector α* ∈ [0,1]^n that quantifies each visual token's contribution to the safety-relevant shift; tokens with α_i below an eviction threshold β are removed from the KV cache entirely, reducing sequence length and inference time.

## Method

DTR introduces a novel formulation called the reversal safety-relevant shift (RSS), which measures the maximum shift along the reversal refusal direction achievable by scaling visual tokens, eliminating the need for image-to-text conversion to estimate the safety-relevant shift. The defense optimizes a scaling vector α via gradient descent (AdamW, lr=0.01, m≈4 steps) on a two-term objective: the first term minimizes the projection of the reweighted last-token activation along d_ref (counteracting jailbreak-induced shifts), while the second term λ‖f(x)−f(x(α))‖₂ regularizes against distorting the original representation (preserving benign performance). After optimization, α* is applied element-wise to visual tokens in the KV cache, and tokens with α_i ≤ β are evicted to further reduce inference cost. This creates an inherent attacker dilemma: increasing adversarial token importance to bypass guardrails compromises visual semantic coherence, while preserving feature tokens reduces evasiveness.

## Results

On llava-1.5-vicuna-7b, DTR reduces ASR from 80.8% (base) to 9.1% on the strongest HADES attack (S+T+A), from 78.2% to 9.1% on MM-SafetyBench (S+T), and from 47.3% to 7.3% on JailbreakV-28K (Blank). On llava-llama2-7b, the corresponding reductions are 56.9%→15.9%, 74.5%→10.0%, and 27.7%→3.6%. DTR also preserves utility: on MM-Vet it maintains recognition (50.3) and even improves spatial awareness (39.1 vs. 36.8 base), while incurring only ~0.36s additional inference time (4.01s vs. 3.65s base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DTR outperforms all four baselines on the majority of attack settings. Against AdaShield (iterative prompt refinement), DTR achieves lower ASR on most benchmarks (e.g., 3.6% vs. 8.2% on MM-SafetyBench S for llava-llama2-7b) while also preserving utility far better (recognition 50.3 vs. 36.2 on MM-Vet) and running faster (4.01s vs. 5.24s AIT). Against CoCA (logit-level calibration), DTR is substantially stronger on image-driven attacks (e.g., 15.9% vs. 35.7% on HADES S+T+A) and avoids CoCA's severe utility degradation (CoCA drops OCR from 33.4 to 17.4, while DTR stays at 30.6). Against ShiftDC (activation-level correction), DTR wins on most attacks (e.g., 9.1% vs. 32.4% on HADES S+T+A for vicuna) and is ~2.6× faster in inference (4.01s vs. 10.66s) because it avoids image-to-text conversion. JailGuard (prompt-stability detection) is consistently the weakest baseline, with ASR often higher than the undefended model on JailbreakV-28K (e.g., 54.6% vs. 27.7% base on Blank), indicating it fails to detect visual-modality attacks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DTR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) the identification of safety-relevant distributional shifts in VLMs and their correction via activation or logit manipulation (ShiftDC, CoCA, Immune), (2) the refusal-direction framework showing that LLM safety is mediated by a single linear direction in activation space, and (3) KV-cache token eviction strategies developed for VLM inference acceleration (MADTP, LLaVA-Prumerge, MEDA). The key departure is repurposing KV-cache optimization—previously used solely for efficiency—as a safety mechanism, and introducing the 'reversal safety-relevant shift' (RSS) formulation that eliminates the image-to-text conversion step required by prior shift-correction methods. Unlike fine-tuning defenses (DRESS, RLHF-based alignment) or iterative-prompting defenses (AdaShield), DTR operates entirely at inference time with a single forward pass and a few gradient steps on the scaling vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DTR offers a practical, lightweight inference-time defense that avoids the two main cost centers of prior approaches (curated safety data for fine-tuning, and image-to-text conversion for calibration), making it attractive for deployment on existing VLMs without retraining. The 'attacker dilemma'—where adversaries must trade off between evading detection (ASR-R) and bypassing the guardrail (ASR-G)—provides a structural robustness argument beyond empirical ASR numbers. Open questions include the vulnerability of the pre-computed refusal direction to poisoning or distribution shift, whether the approach extends to newer VLMs that process visual and textual tokens uniformly (e.g., Llama-4), and how DTR composes with decoding-time or fine-tuning defenses. The reliance on a fixed refusal direction estimated from a small reference set, while shown to be stable, remains a single point of failure that future adaptive attacks could target.
