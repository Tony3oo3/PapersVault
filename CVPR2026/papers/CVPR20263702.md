## Problem

Existing multimodal diffusion-based image editors are computationally inefficient at high resolutions because dense self-attention scales quadratically with image size, and most methods either re-render the entire canvas for local edits or require large-scale high-resolution training data. This limits practical editing to below 1K×1K, making ultra-high-resolution (4K) local editing infeasible for professional workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263702/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution input image (up to 4K), a text prompt, and optionally a control image or edit mask. It internally downsamples the input to a low-resolution proxy (e.g., 1K→256×256) for semantic guidance, then operates on the original high-resolution latent, producing a high-resolution edited image. Training is performed entirely at 1K resolution with LoRA fine-tuning, yet inference scales seamlessly to 4K without any high-resolution training data.

## Method

HierEdit is a two-stage hierarchical framework. First, an off-the-shelf editing model (FLUX) edits a downsampled low-resolution proxy, and a refined mask is derived by pixel-level comparison to precisely localize modified regions. Second, a Local-Window MMDiT performs sparse local-window attention (window size 16, ~256 px) only on masked patches of the original high-resolution image, reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N·l²) and making runtime scale linearly with the number of edited windows rather than total image size. Within each window, a jointly denoised integrated token sequence merges condition tokens (unmasked regions, cached after one forward pass) and noisy tokens (masked regions) into a single sequence, while low-resolution anchor tokens provide global semantic coherence across windows. Inference is further accelerated by intermediate flow initialization, which resizes the low-res edit to target resolution and uses it as a partially denoised starting point, reducing sampling steps from 28 to 10. Only lightweight LoRA modules are trained on 1K data with a standard flow-matching loss, keeping the base MMDiT weights frozen.

## Results

On text-guided editing (Table 1), HierEdit achieves CLIP 20.6, SSIM 0.949, and Composite 3.51, matching or slightly exceeding FLUX.1 Kontext (CLIP 20.8, SSIM 0.954, Composite 3.45) while substantially outperforming GPT-Image-1 (CLIP 18.9, SSIM 0.191) and SDEdit (CLIP 18.5, SSIM 0.351). On inpainting (Table 2), HierEdit achieves FID 39.5 and PSNR 19.31 at 1K resolution, competitive with OminiControl2 (FID 39.2, PSNR 19.11) while being 1.2× faster (6.97s vs 8.25s latency). In speed benchmarks (Table 3), HierEdit is up to 6× faster than FLUX-Fill at 1K and the only method capable of 4K inference on 96GB GPU memory, completing a 25%-edit 4K task in 91.4s versus 1064s for FLUX-Fill.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX.1 Kontext, HierEdit is essentially tied on quality (within 0.2 CLIP, 0.005 SSIM) but wins on ImgEdit Composite (3.51 vs 3.45) and I2EBench SSIM (0.508 vs 0.501), while being significantly faster in practice due to local-window attention. Against GPT-Image-1, HierEdit wins decisively on SSIM (0.949 vs 0.191) and DINO (0.833 vs 0.697), though GPT-Image-1 leads on ImgEdit Composite (4.45 vs 3.51) and EmuEdit CLIPdir (0.132 vs 0.117). Against SDEdit, HierEdit dominates on every metric (e.g., SSIM 0.949 vs 0.351, DINO 0.833 vs 0.159). In inpainting, HierEdit slightly trails OminiControl2 on FID (39.5 vs 39.2) but leads on PSNR (19.31 vs 19.11) and CLIP-I (0.926 vs 0.921) while being faster; it substantially outperforms FLUX-Fill (FID 56.1), ACE++ (FID 37.2 but 22.5s latency), and EasyControl (FID 108.6). At 4K resolution, HierEdit is the only method that successfully completes inference, as ACE++ runs out of memory and GPT-Image-1 is capped at 1K by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

HierEdit builds directly on the MMDiT/FLUX backbone and rectified-flow formulation, extending the hierarchical local-window attention idea from Scale-DiT (Zhang & Tai, 2025) from generation to the editing setting. It departs from prior region-restricted editing methods (OminiControl2, ACE++) that still require user-provided masks and process the full image, by automatically localizing edit regions via a low-resolution proxy and applying sparse local-window attention only to masked patches. The intermediate flow initialization and feature caching mechanisms are novel engineering contributions layered on top of standard DiT inference, while the LoRA-based adaptation to new conditioning tokens follows the lightweight fine-tuning paradigm established by ControlNet and similar adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling computational cost from image resolution, HierEdit makes 4K local editing practical on commodity hardware without requiring scarce 4K training data—a significant barrier for professional workflows in advertising, cinematography, and visualization. The approach is inherently limited to local edits; global style transfers or edits spanning large image fractions would still require full-image processing, and the method's quality depends on the fidelity of the low-resolution proxy model. Open questions include how well the local-window boundary-attention mechanism handles edits with strong long-range dependencies (e.g., reflections, global lighting changes) and whether the framework extends naturally to video or 3D content editing.
