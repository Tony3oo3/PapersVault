## Problem

Directly optimizing explicit meshes from multi-view images faces two unresolved challenges: adaptively refining mesh topology to capture geometric detail without introducing degenerate faces, and maintaining consistent UV coordinates for high-fidelity texturing as the mesh structure evolves. Existing approaches rely on intermediate representations (SDFs, Gaussian primitives, voxels) and post-processing steps like Marching Cubes or TSDF fusion, which produce artifacts, fragmented geometry, and face-count inflation.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images (49–69 views at 800×600 on DTU; 100 training views at 800×800 on NeRF-synthetic) plus an initial coarse mesh obtained by training 2DGS for 7k iterations and extracting via TSDF at 256³ resolution. It produces a directly renderable and editable explicit 3D mesh with vertex positions and a separate fixed-resolution UV texture map, requiring no post-processing or baking step. The output mesh is notably concise (~196K faces on DTU, ~216K on NeRF-synthetic) compared to baselines that produce 1M+ faces.

## Method

ExMesh integrates discrete topology operations into a continuous differentiable optimization loop. An adaptive vertex splitting criterion combines EMA gradient magnitude and face curvature to select refinement targets, while an adaptive vertex merging criterion uses render contribution counts and a degeneracy ratio to remove redundant or degenerate faces; both operations are interleaved with gradient-based optimization every 500 iterations. A real-time UV maintenance mechanism updates texture coordinates at each topology change—interpolating in UV space for non-boundary splits and handling multi-island vertices with independent UV coordinates—so the decoupled UV map stays consistent without post-training baking. Geometry (vertex positions) and texture (UV map) are optimized independently through a differentiable rasterizer (nvdiffrast) with a composite loss combining photometric, depth, silhouette, Laplacian smoothing, and bi-vertex offset terms. Training proceeds in three phases: a 1k-iteration warm-up, a 1k–7k phase with periodic topology updates and UV map reconstruction every 2000 iterations, and a final 1k-iteration refinement with frozen topology.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, ExMesh achieves an average Chamfer Distance of 0.58 with only 196K faces and 13 minutes of training time, compared to GeoSVR (0.47, 1.12M faces, 49 min) and PGSR (0.52, 1.05M faces, 30 min). On NeRF-Synthetic, ExMesh reaches 0.64 average CD with 216K faces in 13 minutes, competitive with Neuralangelo (0.60, 8.39M faces, >12 h). For rendering quality on NeRF-Synthetic, ExMesh achieves 29.32 PSNR / 0.958 SSIM / 0.051 LPIPS, outperforming all mesh-driven baselines but trailing Gaussian-based methods (e.g., 3DGS at 33.32 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NeRF-based methods (VolSDF, NeuS, Neuralangelo), ExMesh achieves comparable or better Chamfer Distance (0.58 vs. 0.62–0.86) while being orders of magnitude faster (13 min vs. >12 h) and producing far fewer faces. Among GS-based methods, ExMesh is slightly behind PGSR (0.58 vs. 0.52) and QGS (0.58 vs. 0.54) in geometric accuracy, but uses 5–10× fewer faces (196K vs. 1.05M/252K) and trains in 13 min vs. 30–48 min. Against mesh-driven baselines, ExMesh clearly outperforms Nvdiffrec (0.58 vs. 1.86), FlexiCubes (0.58 vs. 1.26), and IMLS-Splat (0.58 vs. 0.84), while trading a small accuracy gap to GeoSVR (0.58 vs. 0.47) for a 5.7× reduction in face count (196K vs. 1.12M) and 3.8× speedup. On rendering quality (NeRF-Synthetic), ExMesh leads all mesh-driven methods (29.32 PSNR vs. 28.38 for IMLS-Splat) but remains ~4 dB behind Gaussian-based renderers (3DGS at 33.32 PSNR), which the authors attribute to the lack of view-dependent appearance modeling via spherical harmonics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ExMesh sits at the intersection of differentiable mesh rendering (Nvdiffrec, FlexiCubes) and adaptive mesh refinement from classical geometry processing. It borrows the vertex-splitting mechanism from VertexRegen [60] and the density-control philosophy from 3DGS [23], but departs from the dominant trend of optimizing intermediate carriers (SDFs, point clouds, voxels) by directly optimizing explicit mesh vertices and a decoupled UV map. The key conceptual departure is interleaving discrete topology operations (split/merge) inside a continuous gradient-based loop, which prior mesh-driven methods avoided by freezing topology or relying on post-processing extraction (Marching Cubes, TSDF fusion).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a directly editable, renderable mesh produced in ~13 minutes with ~200K faces, avoiding the multi-hour training and post-processing pipelines of NeRF-based and Gaussian-based methods. The real-time UV maintenance mechanism addresses a long-standing gap: prior methods either coupled texture to geometry (inflating face count) or used implicit MLP textures requiring post-training baking. Open questions include scaling to unbounded or large-scale scenes, closing the rendering-quality gap with Gaussian methods (which exploit spherical harmonics for view-dependent effects), and whether the fixed schedule for split/merge operations can be made fully self-adaptive.
