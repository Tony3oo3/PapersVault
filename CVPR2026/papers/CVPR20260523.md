## Problem

Multi-view 3D reconstruction methods assume stationary illumination, but UAV flights span hours under changing sunlight, causing geometry drift, shadow imprinting, and color inconsistency. Existing datasets either restrict capture to minutes (lacking lighting diversity) or span months (confounding illumination with geometric and semantic changes), leaving no benchmark that isolates time-varying outdoor lighting while keeping geometry and viewpoints consistent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260523/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each scene provides 1280×960 global-shutter RGB frames (sampled at 1 fps from 30 fps capture) along repeated waypointed nadir trajectories, paired with RTK-GNSS camera poses at centimeter-level accuracy and per-time-slot unit sun-direction vectors computed from GPS coordinates and timestamps via the NREL solar position algorithm. The evaluation protocol is a paired cross-illumination setup: lighting is estimated from view subset A_t and rendered on matched subset B_t within the same time slot, scored by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS against ground-truth images, with a geo-referenced dense point cloud (≈10 cm average vertical/planimetric error) serving as the geometric reference.

## Method

UAVLight is a controlled-yet-real benchmark built on three acquisition principles: low-altitude nadir flights (80–100 m) so direct sunlight dominates and sky pixels are minimal; identical waypointed trajectories repeated at 3–11 fixed time slots per scene to ensure comparable parallax and viewpoint coverage; and RTK-based grouped bundle adjustment that softly constrains camera centers to GNSS measurements, yielding metric-scale consistency across flights. The dataset spans 18 outdoor scenes (21,624–69,204 m²) covering vegetation, urban, industrial, transportation, and riverside environments. For evaluation, the paper introduces a paired cross-illumination protocol that avoids the half-split overfitting of NeRF-W and the expensive calibrated environment maps of NeRF-OSR, and provides standardized train/val/test splits with fixed seeds and an official evaluation script. Five baselines spanning implicit appearance modeling (NeRF-W, GS-W, WildGaussians) and explicit inverse rendering (NeRF-OSR, LumiGauss) are benchmarked, revealing that explicit illumination decomposition maintains more stable geometry-material consistency under cross-time illumination shifts.

## Results

Across 12 UAVLight scenes evaluated under cross-illumination, Gaussian-based methods dominate standard image-quality metrics: LumiGauss achieves the highest mean PSNR (~22.2 dB) and lowest LPIPS (~0.15), followed by WildGaussians (~21.4 dB) and GS-W (~21.1 dB). NeRF-based baselines (NeRF-W, NeRF-OSR) trail by roughly 3–4 dB in PSNR and show substantially higher LPIPS (0.4–0.6). The key finding is that the explicit illumination model (LumiGauss) consistently outperforms all implicit methods when evaluated across different time slots, with the gap widening on scenes with strong shadow variation (e.g., Railway: 17.97 vs. 16.35 PSNR for GS-W).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAVLight benchmarks five methods spanning two paradigms: implicit illumination modeling (NeRF-W, GS-W, WildGaussians) and explicit inverse rendering (NeRF-OSR, LumiGauss). LumiGauss, the only explicit Gaussian-based method, achieves the best or near-best PSNR on 10 of 12 scenes and the lowest LPIPS on 11 of 12, confirming that explicit light–material decomposition provides more stable cross-illumination reconstruction. Among implicit methods, WildGaussians and GS-W are competitive on scenes with moderate lighting change (e.g., Residential: 23.62 vs. 25.14 PSNR) but degrade noticeably under strong shadow shifts (Railway: 18.34/16.35 vs. LumiGauss 17.97). NeRF-based baselines (NeRF-W, NeRF-OSR) are consistently the weakest, with mean PSNR around 18 dB and LPIPS above 0.4, indicating that the NeRF representation struggles more than Gaussian Splatting with the geometric and photometric demands of large-scale outdoor UAV scenes. The paper's central takeaway is not a single method's superiority but the benchmark's ability to expose the implicit-vs-explicit trade-off: implicit methods capture high-frequency shadow edges but imprint shadows into albedo, while explicit methods produce more physically coherent relighting at the cost of slightly softer high-frequency detail.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of illumination-aware 3D reconstruction and UAV photogrammetry. It departs from existing multi-lighting datasets (NeRF-OSR, OWL, OpenIllumination, LightCity) by isolating natural sunlight variation through repeated, geo-referenced UAV trajectories at fixed time slots, rather than spanning months or using controlled light stages. The evaluation protocol draws on both implicit appearance modeling (NeRF-W, WildGaussians, GS-W) and explicit inverse rendering (LumiGauss, NeRF-OSR), but introduces a paired cross-illumination setup that avoids the half-split overfitting of NeRF-W and the expensive calibrated environment maps of NeRF-OSR. Geometrically, it builds on RTK-constrained SfM/MVS pipelines to provide metric-scale references, extending standard COLMAP with grouped bundle adjustment and soft RTK priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UAVLight fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark systematically isolates time-varying outdoor illumination while holding geometry and viewpoints constant, making fair comparison of illumination-robust methods impossible. The consistent finding that explicit illumination decomposition outperforms implicit latent encoding across all 12 scenes provides actionable guidance for method design. Open questions include extending to dynamic objects under varied lighting, evaluating feedforward architectures, and generalizing beyond nadir-oriented flights to oblique or multi-altitude captures. The 18-scene scale and restriction to outdoor sunlight limit immediate transferability to indoor or mixed-lighting settings.
