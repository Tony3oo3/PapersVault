## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene editing methods suffer from poor controllability and multi-view consistency: image diffusion models lack explicit spatiotemporal modeling, video diffusion approaches require expensive per-scene fine-tuning, and localization via cross-attention or 2D segmentation masks is coarse-grained and imprecise. Additionally, pre-trained 3DGS models are trapped in local optima, making global parameter updates both inefficient and prone to unintended modifications in non-target regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263830/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained 3DGS scene and a text editing prompt, then renders a temporally coherent pseudo-video along a smooth camera trajectory to exploit video diffusion priors non-intrusively (no fine-tuning of the VDM). It produces an edited 3DGS model and intermediate flow-difference maps at latent-space resolution, which serve as precise spatial priors for selective Gaussian optimization. The pipeline operates on a single NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB) and completes a typical edit in ~8 minutes over ~1600 iterations.

## Method

VDFE reframes multi-view 3D editing as pseudo-video editing and introduces three synergistic modules. FlowOCE formulates editing as a stochastic optimal control problem, adding a control term u_t (derived via Tweedie's denoising formula under Rectified Flow) to FlowEdit's noise-free velocity field to suppress background drift and enforce multi-view consistency. DFD computes the L2 distance between target and source velocity fields at multiple denoising steps, applies Softmax normalization and weighted fusion to produce high-resolution flow-difference maps that precisely localize editing regions without cross-attention or segmentation. DAGE uses these maps to compute per-Gaussian contribution scores, selects top-k core Gaussians, then applies parameter perturbation (pruning plus noise injection) to escape local optima and gradient assignment (higher learning rates for core Gaussians) to ensure edits are spatially confined and converge rapidly.

## Results

On the 3D editing task (Table 1), VDFE achieves the best CLIP-dir (0.2051), A-LPIPS (0.2316), MEt3R (0.0668), and User Study preference (37.14%) among all baselines, while requiring the fewest optimization iterations (~1600 vs. ~2000 for most competitors). On the FIVE video-editing benchmark (Table 2), the full pipeline (FlowOCE + DFD) attains SOTA Structure Distinctness (5.32×10⁻³), PSNR (29.43 dB), LPIPS (67.89×10⁻³), and Motion Fidelity (90.35×10⁻²), with FlowOCE alone reaching the highest FIVE-acc (54.87). The method runs in ~8 minutes on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDFE outperforms all four 3D-editing baselines on the three most critical metrics—CLIP-dir, A-LPIPS, and MEt3R—while also winning the user study by a wide margin (37.14% vs. 11–26% for others). The only metric where VDFE trails is CLIP-sim against DGE (0.2662 vs. 0.2717), a narrow gap in raw semantic similarity that does not translate to better perceptual or multi-view quality. Compared to EditSplat, the closest competitor on multi-view consistency, VDFE improves A-LPIPS by 6.4% (0.2316 vs. 0.2475) and MEt3R by 3.7% (0.0668 vs. 0.0694) while converging 100 fewer iterations. On the FIVE video-editing benchmark, FlowOCE + DFD dominates all six video baselines (TokenFlow, DMT, VidToMe, VideoGrain, Pyramid-Edit, Wan-Edit) on Structure Distinctness (5.32 vs. 12.40–85.95 ×10⁻³), background preservation (PSNR 29.43, LPIPS 67.89), and Motion Fidelity (90.35), with the largest gap over Wan-Edit (the strongest baseline) being ~1.9 dB in PSNR and ~27.5 in LPIPS. FlowOCE alone achieves the highest FIVE-acc (54.87 vs. 46.97 for Wan-Edit), confirming that the optimal-control trajectory is the primary driver of instruction-following accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDFE extends FlowEdit's noise-free ODE-based editing trajectory by reframing it as an optimal control problem (inspired by RB-Modulation), adding a control term that injects spatiotemporal features from the source video to preserve background and enforce multi-view consistency. It departs from prior 3D editing methods (GaussianEditor, DGE, EditSplat, GaussCtrl) that rely on image diffusion models and cross-attention or segmentation masks for localization, instead using flow-difference maps derived directly from the VDM's velocity fields as a non-intrusive, architecture-agnostic localization signal. The 3D optimization stage builds on 3DGS but introduces a difference-aware strategy (parameter perturbation + gradient assignment) that selectively updates only Gaussians contributing to the editing region, addressing the local-optimum stagnation of pre-trained 3DGS models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a non-intrusive pipeline that leverages off-the-shelf VDMs (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) without fine-tuning, making the approach transferable to any flow-matching model and avoiding the expensive per-scene optimization that plagues prior methods. The pseudo-video editing framing elegantly converts the multi-view consistency problem into a temporal consistency problem already well-modeled by VDMs. However, the authors acknowledge that the upper bound of editing quality is constrained by the underlying VDM's capabilities, and the overall pipeline (video rendering → video editing → flow-difference extraction → 3DGS optimization) remains complex. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, larger-scale edits, or can be accelerated further for interactive use.
