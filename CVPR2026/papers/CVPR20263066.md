## Problem

Dual-branch multi-modal 3D detectors suffer from systematic modality imbalance under domain shift: LiDAR-originated queries dominate Hungarian matching (37.5:1 ratio over 2D queries), receive stronger supervision, and mask the underutilization of visual cues. When deployed in unseen domains (rain, night, new geography), this imbalance causes sharp performance drops because the model cannot adaptively rely on whichever modality remains reliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263066/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds and multi-view camera images, processing them through separate proposal generators (ISFusion for 3D, Faster R-CNN for 2D) before a 6-layer transformer fusion decoder. A key intermediate output is a per-proposal depth distribution over 25 bins, produced by fusing an image-predicted distribution with a LiDAR-derived histogram via a 3-layer MLP confidence network. Final outputs are 3D bounding boxes (center, size, orientation) with class labels over 10 nuScenes categories, evaluated via mAP and NDS.

## Method

CCF introduces three components on top of the MV2DFusion dual-branch architecture. First, Query Decoupled Loss runs the fusion decoder three times in parallel with shared weights—on 2D-only, 3D-only, and concatenated query sets—applying independent Hungarian matching and losses to each pass so that 2D queries receive dedicated gradient signal without shortcutting through 3D queries in self-attention. Second, LiDAR-Guided Depth Prior computes a LiDAR depth histogram within each 2D proposal's frustum and fuses it with the image-predicted depth distribution in log-space (Product-of-Experts style) using an instance-specific confidence weight, yielding a more reliable 3D reference point for 2D queries. Third, Complementary Cross-Modal Masking applies GridMask to the image and retains only LiDAR points projecting into the unmasked regions (and vice versa), with a curriculum schedule that linearly increases masking probability from 0 to 0.7, forcing the decoder to learn adaptive query selection under complementary partial observations.

## Results

On the nuScenes domain generalization benchmark, CCF achieves 46.5% average mAP and 51.5% average NDS across three target domains (Rain, Night, Boston), outperforming the strongest multi-modal baseline ISFusion (42.3% mAP) by 4.2 points. On the source domain, CCF maintains competitive performance at 68.2% mAP / 65.9% NDS, only 0.2 points below its own baseline. The largest gains appear on Boston (+3.2 mAP over baseline) and Rain (+2.8 mAP), while Night shows a more modest +1.3 mAP improvement, consistent with LiDAR being more reliable than degraded cameras at night.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCF outperforms all 12 compared baselines on average target-domain mAP. Against the closest multi-modal competitor ISFusion [36], CCF gains +4.9 mAP on Rain (44.7 vs 39.8), +2.4 on Night (44.2 vs 41.8), and +5.2 on Boston (50.6 vs 45.4). Over MEFormer [4] and MOAD [4], the margins are +4.6 and +5.1 mAP on average respectively. The LiDAR-only FSDv2 [15] collapses to 23.4% mAP on Rain, highlighting the fragility of single-modality approaches under domain shift. In the oracle setting (Table 2, trained on full nuScenes), CCF also leads all baselines, reaching 73.6% mAP on the full split and 72.9% on Rain, confirming that the architectural improvements generalize beyond the domain-shift protocol. Camera-only methods (BEVDet [10], PETR [17]) and unified-representation multi-modal methods (TransFusion [1], BEVFusion [18], GraphBEV [27]) all show larger relative drops under domain shift compared to CCF's proposal-level dual-branch design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dual-branch proposal-level fusion paradigm of MV2DFusion and ISFusion, where modality-specific queries (2D from Faster R-CNN, 3D from ISFusion) are generated before a shared transformer decoder performs cross-modal fusion. It departs from prior robust multi-modal detection work (CMT, MEFormer, MoME, MetaBEV), which primarily addresses sensor dropout or complete modality failure, by targeting a different failure mode: domain shifts where both sensors remain active but degrade asymmetrically. The key conceptual contribution is diagnosing a systematic modality imbalance—quantified as a 37.5:1 Hungarian-matching ratio favoring 3D queries—that prior dual-branch methods have not explicitly identified or addressed, and proposing three targeted mechanisms (decoupled supervision, LiDAR-guided depth priors via Product-of-Experts-style fusion, and complementary spatial masking) to correct it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of modality imbalance as a concrete, measurable training pathology (rather than a vague 'fusion quality' issue) gives practitioners a clear diagnostic and actionable fix for dual-branch multi-modal detectors, which are increasingly used in autonomous driving stacks. The complementary masking strategy is a lightweight, architecture-agnostic augmentation that could be adopted by other multi-modal systems beyond 3D detection. However, the evaluation is confined to a single dataset (nuScenes) with one source domain (Singapore daytime) and three target splits, and the absolute gains (2–3 mAP points) are moderate; whether the modality-imbalance diagnosis and its remedies transfer to BEV-based or unified-query architectures, or to other sensor pairs (e.g., radar-camera), remains open.
