## Problem

Existing wireless 3D-Gaussian Splatting methods treat signal propagation as purely data-driven regression, directly mapping spatial coordinates to signal strength without explicitly modeling ray-object interactions such as reflection, diffraction, and refraction at material boundaries. This omission prevents them from capturing the fundamental electromagnetic physics governing multipath propagation, leading to degraded spatial fidelity in complex indoor environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262490/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an initialized 3D point cloud (Gaussian primitives), a transmitter position, and receiver antenna configuration. The tokenizer operates on a subset of top-M highest-opacity Gaussians as anchors rather than all N primitives, reducing attention cost from O(N²) to O(M²). Outputs include an RSSI spatial power map (constructed via attention-weighted averaging over a softmax-blended pixel grid), an angle-power spatial spectrum on a 360×90 azimuth-elevation grid at 1° resolution, and complex-valued channel state information, all rendered through a Mercator projection onto the RX perception plane.

## Method

GAI-GS integrates a Geometric Algebra Transformer (GATr) encoder into a 3D-Gaussian Splatting pipeline, using the space-time algebra G_{3,0,1} to represent ray-object interactions (reflection, diffraction, transmission) as rotors acting on ray state vectors via sandwich products. The key insight is that the GATr attention mechanism has a structural correspondence to these physical transformations: each attention operator A_{i'} acts as a learned interaction operator analogous to the cumulative geometric algebra versor I, enabling the network to implicitly infer multi-bounce propagation paths without explicit material labels or collision locations. A dual-branch Scene Mapping Network (attenuation + signal) consumes the multi-view embedding (GA tokens concatenated with Euclidean position embeddings) and produces residual updates to Gaussian rotation, scaling, spherical-harmonic signal coefficients, and transmitter-dependent opacity. The rendered output is obtained by projecting updated 3D Gaussians onto the RX plane via Mercator projection, binning into tiles, and accumulating depth-sorted contributions with per-primitive attenuation factors, all trained end-to-end under a combined MAE + SSIM + L2-regularization loss.

## Results

GAI-GS achieves the lowest MAE across all evaluated settings: 1.9 dB (2.4 GHz) and 1.6 dB (5.0 GHz) on Room 1, 2.7 dB and 1.8 dB on Room 2, 2.3 dB on the BLE dataset, and 0.91 SSIM on the RFID spectrum reconstruction task. These results improve over the strongest 3D-GS baseline WRF-GS by 1.2 dB (Room 1, 2.4 GHz), 0.5 dB (BLE), and 0.09 SSIM (spectrum). In rendering speed, GAI-GS achieves 0.91 ms per frame, faster than both WRF-GS (39.29 ms) and WRF-GS+ (1.43 ms), though training time (203 min) is higher than WRF-GS+ (101 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAI-GS outperforms all seven baselines on every metric and dataset. Against the closest competitor WRF-GS [37], GAI-GS reduces MAE by 1.2 dB (Room 1, 2.4 GHz), 0.6 dB (Room 1, 5.0 GHz), 0.4 dB (Room 2, 2.4 GHz), 0.5 dB (BLE), and improves SSIM from 0.82 to 0.91 (spectrum). The NeRF-based methods (NeRF² [44] and NeRF-APT [32]) trail GAI-GS by 1.4–1.6 dB on RSSI tasks and 0.07–0.13 SSIM on spectrum reconstruction. Classical and generative baselines (Ray Tracing [41], MLP, FIRE [20], DCGAN [25]) are substantially weaker, with MAE values ranging from 2.7 to 25.6 dB. In rendering speed, GAI-GS (0.91 ms) is 43× faster than WRF-GS (39.29 ms) and 1.6× faster than WRF-GS+ (1.43 ms), though its training time (203 min) is roughly 2× that of WRF-GS+ (101 min) and about 1.5× faster than WRF-GS (312 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

GAI-GS extends the wireless 3D-GS line (WRF-GS, WRF-GS+, RF-3DGS, GSRF) by replacing their purely data-driven regression of signal strength with an explicit geometric algebra (GA) attention mechanism that encodes ray-object interactions via sandwich products and rotors in space-time algebra G_{3,0,1}. The projection and rendering pipeline is inherited directly from WRF-GS/WRF-GS+ (Mercator projection onto the RX perception plane, tile-based depth-sorted accumulation), while the encoder is the Geometric Algebra Transformer (GATr) from Brehmer et al. (NeurIPS 2023), repurposed here to implicitly learn multi-bounce reflection, diffraction, and transmission operators without material labels. The key departure from prior wireless 3D-GS work is the claim that attention weights in a GA space correspond structurally to cumulative interaction operators, grounding token mixing in electromagnetic propagation symmetries rather than treating it as a black-box correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, physics-informed alternative to the prevailing data-driven paradigm in neural wireless channel modeling, and the consistent MAE/SSIM gains across two indoor rooms and two public datasets (BLE, RFID spectrum) suggest that explicitly encoding propagation geometry in the attention mechanism is beneficial. However, the evidence base is narrow: only two ~35 m² rooms, no ablation isolating the GA component from a standard transformer encoder, and no evaluation at mmWave frequencies or in outdoor/urban settings. Open questions include whether the GA formulation scales to large-scale or heterogeneous environments, whether the top-M anchor selection strategy remains effective as Gaussian counts grow, and whether the 2× training-time overhead relative to WRF-GS+ is acceptable for real-time digital-twin deployment.
