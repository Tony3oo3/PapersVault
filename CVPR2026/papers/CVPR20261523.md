## Problem

Existing remote sensing MLLMs rely on a single dense parameter set shared across all tasks, producing unreliable and hallucinated outputs in complex RS scenes. The intrinsic redundancy and homogeneity of RS images (large homogeneous regions, repetitive textures) further degrade standard MoE routing by introducing noise and computational overhead. Additionally, current RS-MLLMs lack structured chain-of-thought reasoning that progresses from global scene understanding to task-specific goal interpretation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts multi-resolution remote sensing images (ranging from 256×256 to 2519×2285 pixels) paired with text instruction prompts, and produces structured outputs in a fixed format: a chain-of-thought reasoning block followed by a final answer block. The five supported tasks—scene classification, object counting, object detection, image captioning, and relation detection—each yield a distinct output type (label set, integer count, bounding boxes, free-text caption, or relation description), all generated through the same unified reasoning pipeline.

## Method

GeoCoT introduces Mani-MoE, a manifold-driven sparse expert architecture built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (expanded to 12B). The key mechanism is a rule-based local manifold mapping: visual tokens are first partitioned into semantically coherent clusters via spectral clustering on a graph Laplacian, then each cluster undergoes SVD-based dimensionality reduction retaining only the top-r singular components that capture 85% of cumulative energy, eliminating redundancy while preserving task-relevant structure. Every three Transformer layers, the original MLP is replaced by eight manifold experts operating in these low-rank subspaces plus one shared expert initialized from the original weights, with Top-2 noisy gating for routing. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) continual pre-training on a curated RS corpus with data distillation regularization, (2) multi-task CoT cold-start with LoRA and cosine ramp-up of expert gating weights, and (3) RSV-GRPO, a task-specific Group Relative Policy Optimization that combines format compliance, task-accuracy rewards (exact match for counting, cosine similarity for captioning, F1 for scene classification, IoU+confidence for detection), and a cosine-scheduled length penalty to encourage concise yet complete reasoning.

## Results

GeoCoT achieves the highest overall performance across four RS tasks: 81.21 F1 on DOTA-v2.0 relation detection, 91.73% accuracy on AID scene classification, 17.15% accuracy on RSVQA_LR object counting, and 78.29/54.95 mAP on DIOR object detection. On UCM-Captions image captioning, GeoCoT leads in BLEU-1 (86.31), BLEU-2 (80.15), and ROUGE_L (80.18), though RSGPT retains a higher CIDEr score (333.23 vs. 202.38). The paper reports a 5.27% higher average accuracy than the state-of-the-art method (SkySenseGPT) across all evaluated tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoCoT outperforms the strongest specialist baseline SkySenseGPT on relation detection (81.21 vs. 74.34 F1), object counting (17.15% vs. 9.20% Acc.), and object detection (78.29 vs. 69.94 mAP@0.5; 54.95 vs. 48.17 mAP@[.5:.95]), while SkySenseGPT retains a narrow edge on scene classification (92.25% vs. 91.73% Acc.). Against generalist models, GeoCoT dominates across all tasks: GPT-4o scores 41.83 F1 on relation detection and 58.81% on scene classification, while Gemini 2.5 reaches 44.05 F1 and 57.45%—both far below GeoCoT. On image captioning (UCM-Captions), GeoCoT leads in BLEU-1 (86.31) and ROUGE_L (80.18) over RSGPT (86.12, 78.34) and SkySenseGPT (81.21, 76.49), but RSGPT achieves a notably higher CIDEr (333.23 vs. 202.38), suggesting GeoCoT's captions are less n-gram-overlap-diverse. Classical detection baselines (Faster R-CNN, DETR) remain competitive on DIOR mAP@0.5 (76.50, 73.12) but lack multi-task capability, whereas GeoCoT surpasses them while handling four additional tasks simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoCoT extends the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (building on Switch Transformer's Top-2 routing) by introducing a manifold-structured routing mechanism: instead of purely data-driven expert selection, it projects RS visual tokens into low-rank subspaces via spectral clustering and SVD before routing, motivated by the homogeneous, repetitive texture structure of satellite imagery. For the reasoning component, it adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 into a task-specific RSV-GRPO with RS-tailored reward functions. The paper departs from prior RS-MLLMs (RSGPT, GeoChat, SkySenseGPT) that rely on dense shared-parameter Transformers, and from general MoE approaches that lack structural constraints on expert specialization, by coupling geometric manifold awareness with sparse expert allocation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a real gap in RS-MLLMs: the lack of task-specialized architecture and structured reasoning traces, both critical for high-stakes geospatial applications like disaster response. The RS-CoT-20k dataset with expert-validated CoT annotations is a useful resource for the community. However, open questions remain: whether the spectral clustering + SVD pipeline scales to very high-resolution or multi-modal RS inputs (SAR, hyperspectral), whether the manifold assumption generalizes beyond optical imagery, and whether the gains over specialist baselines persist as those models improve. The CIDEr score in Table 1 (202.38) actually trails RSGPT (333.23) and SkySenseGPT (236.19), suggesting the captioning gains are not uniform across all metrics.
