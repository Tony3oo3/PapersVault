## Problem

Existing relightable 3D Gaussian head avatar methods (e.g., RGCA) require each subject to be captured under expensive one-light-at-a-time (OLAT) illumination in a light stage, making it impractical to relight arbitrary people. While URAvatar attempts generalization, it still demands a large-scale OLAT dataset that is costly and time-consuming to acquire. The paper addresses how to relight any subject from readily available flat-lit multi-view or single in-the-wild images without requiring OLAT capture of that specific subject.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263283/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either multi-view images (55–80 cameras from datasets like Ava-256, SDFM, Nersemble) or a single in-the-wild portrait photo, all cropped to 1024×1024 based on a tracked mesh projection. It produces a relightable 3D Gaussian head avatar with 1,048,576 Gaussians parameterized in a shared UV texture map, carrying physically-based reflectance (diffuse albedo, spherical-harmonic radiance transfer, specular lobe, view-dependent normals) that enables rendering from any novel view under arbitrary HDRI or point-light illumination.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline that decouples identity generalization from relighting. Stage 1 is a multi-identity full-on model where an MLP predicts coarse mesh shape and two CNNs predict Gaussian geometry and color in UV texture space, conditioned on a 256-dim identity code and a 4-dim dataset-specific lighting code that disentangles illumination differences across heterogeneous flat-lit datasets via self-supervised alignment. Stage 2 is a UNet with shared encoder and separate view-independent/view-dependent decoder branches that maps full-on Gaussian colors (concatenated with 3D positions) to RGCA relightable parameters, trained only on the small 3DPR OLAT dataset (127 subjects). This sequential design lets Stage 1 leverage large existing flat-lit corpora for cross-subject generalization while Stage 2 learns the relighting mapping from minimal OLAT data, and a fitting procedure (inversion + finetuning with locality regularization) adapts the model to unseen subjects from as few as one image.

## Results

On the 3DPR test set (11 subjects) with environment-map relighting, RelightAnyone achieves PSNR 29.07, RMSE 0.0746, SSIM 0.91, and LPIPS 0.1649 in the multi-view fitting setting, and PSNR 26.57, RMSE 0.0996, SSIM 0.86, and LPIPS 0.1671 from a single in-the-wild image. These results substantially outperform the strongest 3D GAN-based baseline (3DPR: PSNR 21.02, SSIM 0.83) by approximately 8 dB in PSNR and 0.08 in SSIM for multi-view, and by over 5 dB even in the single-image setting. The method also demonstrates qualitative superiority over 2D diffusion-based relighting (IC-Light, DiffusionRenderer) in preserving hard shadows and realistic skin reflectance under rotating environment maps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RelightAnyone decisively outperforms all three 3D GAN-based baselines on every metric. Against the strongest 3D GAN method, 3DPR [60], the multi-view setting gains +8.05 dB PSNR (29.07 vs 21.02) and +0.08 SSIM (0.91 vs 0.83), while even the single-image setting surpasses 3DPR by +5.55 dB PSNR. The older GAN-based methods NFL [25] and Lite2Relight [59] trail by over 12 dB in PSNR and also suffer from geometric distortion on non-frontal poses and inability to fit multi-view inputs. Against 2D diffusion-based methods, IC-Light [89] fails to track the main light source across a rotating environment map and produces incoherent, metallic-sheen results, while DiffusionRenderer [36] estimates an overly diffuse skin material and cannot render realistic shadows. The paper notes that URAvatar [35], the closest prior work, has no publicly available code and thus is excluded from quantitative comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends RGCA's relightable 3D Gaussian parameterization (diffuse albedo, SH radiance transfer, specular lobe) from a person-specific model to a multi-identity, generalized setting. It departs from URAvatar's approach of requiring a large-scale OLAT training corpus by introducing a two-stage pipeline: a Stage 1 identity prior trained across diverse flat-lit multi-view datasets (Ava-256, SDFM, Nersemble) with a learned dataset-specific lighting code for cross-dataset alignment, and a Stage 2 UNet that maps flat-lit Gaussian colors to RGCA relightable parameters using only a small OLAT dataset (3DPR). The key architectural departure from prior work is the explicit separation of identity learning (cheap, abundant data) from relighting learning (expensive, scarce data), enabled by the lighting code that disentangles dataset-specific illumination from subject appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method removes the primary practical bottleneck in high-fidelity avatar relighting—the need for per-subject light-stage captures—by showing that a strong identity prior from flat-lit data combined with a small OLAT relighting dataset suffices for generalized relighting. This opens the door to building relightable avatars from a single in-the-wild photo, which has direct applications in virtual production, VFX, and social media. Key limitations remain: the model is restricted to neutral expressions, struggles with hair reconstruction due to UV correspondence issues, and the relighting quality depends on the small OLAT training set (127 subjects). Extending to dynamic expressions and resolving the hair problem are natural next steps that would require larger, more diverse capture datasets.
