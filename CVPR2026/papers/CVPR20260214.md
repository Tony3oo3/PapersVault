## Problem

Despite CLIP's remarkable out-of-distribution generalization, the theoretical foundations underlying this robustness remain unexplained, with current understanding being predominantly phenomenological. The paper asks whether CLIP's contrastive objective can be formally connected to Invariant Risk Minimization (IRM), the principled paradigm for OOD generalization, and whether this connection can be exploited to improve both multimodal understanding and reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260214/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For the understanding task, the method takes an RGB image and a text prompt template containing a class name, producing a classification probability over an open vocabulary via cosine similarity between image and text encoder outputs. For the reasoning task, an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) takes an image plus a free-form question and generates a chain-of-thought reasoning sequence; at each decoding step, a sliding window of the last w generated tokens is fed to the mid-trained CLIP text encoder to compute a process-level reward. The mid-training stage consumes image-text pairs annotated with class and environment vocabularies mined from LAION, with environment tokens pruned and caption swaps synthesized within the same class.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution proves that CLIP's InfoNCE objective, when text prompts are decomposed into class-specific tokens (causal factors) and class-agnostic context tokens (environmental factors), becomes formally equivalent to IRM's bi-level invariance criterion under a vocabulary-constrained variant (Theorem 5). Practically, this is realized through a mid-training paradigm that reconfigures training batches by curating class and environment vocabularies, pruning environment tokens from text, and synthesizing environment-invariant pairs via caption swaps—all without altering CLIP's two-tower architecture. The resulting CLIP-IRM model is then used as a process-reward signal for multimodal reasoning: a sliding window of MLLM-generated tokens is encoded by CLIP-IRM's text encoder, and the resulting vocabulary-constrained InfoNCE score (minus an environment-overlap penalty) shapes the policy via GRPO, effectively transplanting IRM's invariance guarantees to sequential decision-making in MLLMs.

## Results

On the zero-shot OOD generalization suite (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, NICO++, DomainNet), CLIP-IRMv2 (ViT-L/14) achieves 88.0% average accuracy, surpassing Gemini (87.4%) and GPT-4V (86.3%), while CLIP-IRMv1 (ViT-B/16) reaches 83.6%, outperforming the best traditional OOD baseline (SWAD, 73.8%) by nearly 10 points. On the most challenging benchmarks, CLIP-IRMv2 leads on NICO++ (91.8% vs. Gemini's 89.7%) and DomainNet (78.1% vs. Gemini's 75.9%). For multimodal reasoning, GRPO augmented with CLIP-IRM process rewards consistently outperforms vanilla GRPO across MathVista, WeMath, MathVerse, and MathVision, with margins growing over training, while also maintaining higher accuracy on HallusionBench (reduced hallucination) and matching or exceeding in-domain performance on Geometry3K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ten traditional OOD generalization baselines (ERM, SWAD, RSC, GroupDRO, Fishr, CORAL, MMD, SagNet, IRM, Mixup), CLIP-IRMv1—using the same ViT-B/16 backbone—outperforms all of them by 9.8–16.6 points in average accuracy (83.6% vs. best 73.8% for SWAD), demonstrating that the IRM-equivalent mid-training objective yields far stronger generalization than prior domain-generalization methods. Among open-source multimodal foundation models (CLIP, BLIP-2, QWEN-VL, LLaVa), CLIP-IRMv2 exceeds the best (CLIP, 83.4%) by 4.6 points and dominates on the hardest benchmarks (DomainNet: 78.1% vs. 76.7%; NICO++: 91.8% vs. 88.7%). Against proprietary models, CLIP-IRMv2 beats Gemini (87.4%) and GPT-4V (86.3%) on average, with the largest gaps on NICO++ (+2.1 over Gemini) and DomainNet (+2.2 over Gemini). In prompt-tuning experiments, replacing CLIP with CLIP-IRM as the substrate shifts every method (CoOp, CoCoOp, VPT, MaPLe, PromptSRC) to a higher accuracy frontier in both Base-to-New and Cross-dataset transfer, with target-domain gains exceeding source-domain gains. For reasoning, GRPO + CLIP-IRM process reward surpasses vanilla GRPO on all four OOD math benchmarks (MathVista, WeMath, MathVerse, MathVision) and HallusionBench, with the advantage widening as training progresses, while in-domain Geometry3K accuracy is matched or exceeded.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected paradigms—CLIP's contrastive pre-training and Invariant Risk Minimization—by proving that a vocabulary-constrained InfoNCE objective is formally equivalent to IRM's invariance criterion, using token-level causal representation identifiability (Theorem 2, Corollary 3 from [9]) as the analytical substrate. Unlike prior CLIP identifiability results [12] that operate at the feature level, this work decomposes text into class-specific and class-agnostic tokens, enabling a direct mapping to IRM's causal/environmental factor split without requiring explicit environment labels or bi-level optimization. The practical instantiation (mid-training with vocabulary-supervised batches) departs from both standard CLIP fine-tuning (prompt-tuning, adapter-based) and classical IRM surrogates (IRMv1, REx, ZIN) by implementing the invariance constraint purely through data reconfiguration within the standard contrastive setup. The extension to multimodal reasoning via CLIP-IRM process rewards in GRPO draws on IPO [51] but repurposes the invariant alignment score as a step-level reward signal rather than a policy constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first rigorous theoretical explanation for why CLIP generalizes out-of-distribution, moving the understanding from phenomenological to causal, which could guide principled improvements to vision-language pre-training. The mid-training paradigm is architecturally non-invasive (no bi-level optimization, no new modules), making it directly applicable to existing CLIP checkpoints at scale. However, the equivalence relies on idealized assumptions (diffeomorphic mixing functions, infinite training pairs, clean token-level factorization) whose practical validity under finite data and noisy web-scale captions remains untested; the sensitivity of performance to vocabulary curation quality and the gap between the theoretical guarantee and empirical gains on the hardest benchmarks (DomainNet, NICO++) suggest the invariance is approximate rather than exact. Extending IRM guarantees to sequential reasoning via process rewards is a promising but early direction—whether the invariant alignment signal remains informative over long reasoning chains or degrades with horizon length is an open question.
