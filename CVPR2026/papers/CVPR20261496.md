## Problem

Token-adaptive ViTs reduce FLOPs by dynamically pruning tokens, but they suffer from two coupled limitations: (1) global self-attention causes the deepest computation to be assigned to semantically weak, homogeneous foreground-center tokens while edge tokens rich in structural cues exit prematurely; and (2) despite token-level savings, the full parameter set remains unchanged because deep-layer weights are underutilized when most tokens exit early. Full inter-layer parameter sharing, which could resolve the redundancy, has proven infeasible in ViTs because identical transformations across layers cause representational collapse.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images, partitions them into non-overlapping 16×16 patches plus a [CLS] token, and outputs a single class label. The unusual aspect is that each patch token is assigned a distinct computation depth (1–12 recursive steps) by the EARR before entering the transformer, so the effective network depth varies per token within a single forward pass. The [CLS] token is always forced through all 12 layers to guarantee a fully refined classification representation.

## Method

Edge-RecViT couples an Edge-Aware Ranker (EARR), a lightweight two-layer MLP that scores each token's semantic richness and predicts a depth in {1,…,12}, with a recursive transformer block containing only three trainable parameter sets: a head layer, a single shared hidden layer, and a tail layer. Each token passes through the head once, then iterates through the shared hidden layer d_i times (up to 10 recursions) according to its EARR-assigned depth, and finally enters the tail layer. The key insight is that per-token depth heterogeneity introduced by the EARR restores the functional diversity that fixed layers normally provide, making full middle-layer parameter sharing viable without representational collapse. Training uses three losses: a confidence-weighted cross-entropy (scaling CLS logits by mean token confidence to enable end-to-end gradient flow to the ranker), an STB-loss that penalizes log-sum-exp magnitude to prevent extreme logit spikes, and a DU-loss that enforces alignment between expected and actual depth distributions to prevent ranker collapse.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, Edge-RecViT achieves 72.4% / 80.3% / 82.0% Top-1 accuracy at Tiny / Small / Base scale, matching or exceeding DeiT (72.2% / 79.8% / 81.8%) while reducing FLOPs by ~31% (e.g., 35.2 → 24.39 GFLOPs at Base) and parameters by ~73% (86.0 → 23.2M at Base). On CIFAR-10/100, the Base variant reaches 99.16% / 90.78% Top-1, matching DeiT-B (99.1% / 90.8%) with 73% fewer parameters and 31% fewer FLOPs. The Tiny variant uses only 1.6M parameters and 1.7 GFLOPs while still matching DeiT-Ti accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary DeiT baselines, Edge-RecViT matches or slightly exceeds Top-1 at every scale (72.4 vs 72.2 Tiny; 80.3 vs 79.8 Small; 82.0 vs 81.8 Base) while cutting parameters by ~73% and FLOPs by ~31%. Among token-adaptive methods, Edge-RecViT-Small outperforms A-ViT-DeiT (78.6%), ATS-DeiT (79.7%), EViT-DeiT (79.7%), DynamicViT-DeiT (78.3%), and ToMe-DeiT (79.4%) by 0.6–1.7 points, all with 6.0M parameters versus their 22–24M. At Base scale, Edge-RecViT (82.0%) surpasses EViT-DeiT (81.3%) and DeiT-B (81.8%) with 23.2M parameters versus 78.6M and 86M respectively. Against SSM-based models, Edge-RecViT-Small ties Vim-S (80.3%) with 4.3× fewer parameters (6.0M vs 26.0M), and Edge-RecViT-Base (82.0%) edges out Vim-B (81.9%) with 4.2× fewer parameters (23.2M vs 98M). On CIFAR-10/100, Edge-RecViT-Tiny (99.00% / 90.11%) outperforms EA-ViT (97.98% / 88.20%) and DeiT-Ti (98.19% / 86.61%) with 1.6M parameters and 1.72G FLOPs, and Edge-RecViT-Base (99.16% / 90.78%) matches DeiT-B (99.1% / 90.8%) with 73% fewer parameters. The only notable accuracy loss is at Tiny scale versus FastViT-T12 (72.4% vs 79.1%), though Edge-RecViT uses 4.3× fewer parameters (1.6M vs 6.8M) and fewer FLOPs (1.7G vs 2.8G).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: token-adaptive ViTs (DynamicViT, A-ViT, EViT, ATS) that dynamically allocate computation per token, and recursive transformers from NLP (ALBERT, MoR) that share parameters across layers. It departs from prior token-adaptive work by identifying a specific failure mode—over-computation on homogeneous foreground interiors while under-computing on structurally rich edge tokens—and replacing generic importance scoring with an edge-aware ranker (EARR). Its key architectural departure is making full inter-layer parameter sharing feasible in ViTs by leveraging token-specific recursion depths to introduce the heterogeneity that fixed layers normally provide, a combination not previously explored in the vision domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 73% parameter reduction at the Base scale (86M → 23.2M) while matching DeiT accuracy, combined with ~31% FLOPs savings, offers a practically deployable path to efficient ViTs that prior token-adaptive or partial-sharing methods (EA-ViT, MiniViT) could not achieve. The conceptual insight that token-wise depth heterogeneity rescues full parameter sharing from representational collapse is a clean principle that could inform future efficient-architecture design beyond classification. Open questions include whether the edge-aware ranking generalizes to dense prediction tasks (detection, segmentation), whether the recursive structure degrades at much greater depths, and whether the approach transfers to non-natural-image domains where edge semantics differ.
