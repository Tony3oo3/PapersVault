## Problem

Existing Dynamic Novel View Synthesis benchmarks focus on photorealistic appearance but lack ground-truth physical information (3D trajectories, material parameters) needed to quantitatively evaluate physical realism. They are also restricted to single objects with homogeneous materials, leaving complex multi-body interactions across diverse physical substances—liquids, gases, textiles, and rheological materials—largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261262/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 17 simulated scenes with both multi-view (multiple independently moving cameras) and monocular video sequences, allowing flexible training configurations where full sequences yield a multi-view task and a single sequence yields a monocular task. Beyond standard RGB frames, it offers ground-truth 3D particle trajectories, physical parameters (viscosity, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, gas temperature), and customizable modalities including depth maps, surface normals, and relighted renders. Evaluation uses two static cameras with a large baseline for held-out view synthesis, and COLMAP-reconstructed point clouds are provided per scene.

## Method

PhysGaia is constructed using material-specific physics solvers—FLIP for liquids, Pyro for gases, Vellum for textiles, and MPM for rheological materials—ensuring each scene adheres to fundamental physical laws with accurately computed force exchange among interacting bodies. The benchmark introduces two novel metrics for physical realism: Trajectory Distance (TD), measuring average Euclidean deviation between reconstructed Gaussian primitive positions and matched ground-truth trajectories, and Area Under Outlier Percentages (AUOP), which captures the temporal persistence of large deviations by tracking when primitives first exceed a threshold and remain flagged thereafter. Complete source files (solver configurations, geometries, materials, textures) are provided for customization, alongside integration pipelines for five state-of-the-art 4DGS methods (D-3DGS, 4DGS, STG, MoSca, SoM). Empirical evaluation reveals that existing methods achieve reasonable photorealism but fail to follow physically plausible motion, with reconstructed primitives fluctuating near surfaces rather than tracking actual physical flows, and inverse-physics parameter estimation methods substantially underestimate material stiffness in multi-object scenarios.

## Results

On PhysGaia (17 scenes), the best photorealism is achieved by 4DGS with 24.4 PSNR / 0.87 SSIM (multiview) and 22.7 PSNR (monocular), but even the strongest multiview result stays below 30 PSNR, highlighting the difficulty of multi-body interaction scenes. For physical realism, all methods fall far short of the oracle on the box-smoke scene: TD ranges from 3.62 (STG) to 6.00 (4DGS) versus 0.84 for the oracle, and AUOP ranges from 25.4 (SoM) to 68.8 (4DGS) versus 0.3. In the inverse-physics task, both PAC-NeRF and GIC substantially underestimate Young's modulus (1.7×10³ and 23.4×10³ vs. ground-truth 100×10³), confirming that existing methods were not designed for multi-object interaction scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Five DyNVS methods are evaluated: D-3DGS, 4DGS, STG, MoSca, and SoM. 4DGS leads in photorealism (24.4 PSNR multiview) but produces the worst physical realism on the box-smoke scene (TD = 6.00, AUOP = 68.8), indicating that optimizing purely for RGB fidelity does not yield physically plausible motion. STG achieves the lowest TD (3.62) among all methods but at the cost of the weakest photorealism (21.0 PSNR multiview), revealing a clear photorealism–physical-realism trade-off. MoSca and SoM, which use motion factorization or ARAP constraints, are competitive on physical realism (AUOP 28.3 and 25.4 respectively) but trail 4DGS by ~5–6 dB in PSNR. For inverse physics, PAC-NeRF underestimates Young's modulus by ~59× and GIC by ~4× relative to ground truth, both far from accurate, exposing a fundamental limitation of single-object parameter estimation methods when applied to multi-body interaction scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysGaia sits at the intersection of two lines of work: DyNVS benchmarks (D-NeRF, Nerfies, HyperNeRF, DyCheck) that focus on photorealistic reconstruction, and physics-simulated 4D datasets (ScalarFlow, PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus) that provide physical ground truth but are limited to single materials or single objects. The paper departs from both by combining material-specific solvers (FLIP, Pyro, Vellum, MPM) to generate scenes with rich multi-body interactions across liquid, gas, textile, and rheological materials, while providing complete 3D particle trajectories and physical parameters. It also introduces new evaluation metrics (Trajectory Distance and AUOP) that go beyond standard photorealism metrics to quantify physical fidelity of reconstructed Gaussian primitive motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhysGaia fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark simultaneously offers multi-body interactions, diverse physical materials, and complete physics ground truth, making it the first resource for rigorously evaluating physical realism in DyNVS. The empirical results reveal that state-of-the-art 4DGS methods, while achieving reasonable photorealism, produce trajectories that deviate substantially from ground-truth physics (e.g., 5–60× underestimation of Young's modulus in inverse-physics tasks), exposing a fundamental disconnect between appearance optimization and physical plausibility. Open questions include whether material-specific solver integration (e.g., volumetric FLIP or Pyro solvers into particle-based Gaussian frameworks) can close this gap, and whether the findings generalize beyond the 17 simulated scenes to real-world multi-material interactions.
