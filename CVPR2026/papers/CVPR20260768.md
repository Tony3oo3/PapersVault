## Problem

MLLMs trained with GRPO's outcome-based rewards develop spurious correlations with task-irrelevant background regions in images, as evidenced by disproportionately high gradient saliency on background compared to text-only LLMs. This spurious background-answer dependency leads to hallucinated outputs, yet no existing post-training method explicitly disentangles causal from non-causal visual signals at the token level during policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260768/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image paired with a text prompt (e.g., a VQA question or captioning instruction) and produces a text response structured as intermediate reasoning tokens followed by a final answer. During training, the causal completeness reward is computed per reasoning token by performing counterfactual interventions: truncating the sequence at token t and re-sampling H continuations with and without that token (for sufficiency), or masking the token and re-evaluating the answer (for necessity). The answer tokens themselves retain the standard GRPO outcome reward, while only reasoning tokens receive the additional causal completeness signal.

## Method

COPO extends GRPO by injecting a token-level causal completeness reward into the advantage function. It first formalizes MLLM generation via a Structural Causal Model that separates causal factors Lc (semantic attributes determining the answer) from non-causal factors Ls (background, lighting), then defines two per-token scores: a sufficiency score measuring how much retaining token t improves answer correctness across H sampled continuations, and a necessity score measuring how much masking token t degrades the answer. These are combined as r_causal = λs·S_suff + λn·S_nec and added to the original GRPO group-relative advantage for reasoning tokens only. The resulting modified advantage drives the clipped policy gradient update, steering the model to generate tokens that are both causally sufficient and necessary for the correct answer while suppressing background-dependent shortcuts. Hyperparameters λs and λn (both set to 0.35) control the relative weight of each constraint and the overall intensity of causal supervision.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5, COPO achieves CHAIRS=19.8, CHAIRI=5.3, and POPE F1=88.0, outperforming the strongest baseline CSR (21.0/6.0/87.0) and GCPO (21.5/5.8/87.2). On GPT-4-assisted evaluation, COPO scores 8.71 (Accuracy), 6.89 (Correctness), and 9.58 (Detailedness), surpassing the best baseline DeCo (7.42/6.25/7.96) by +1.29, +0.57, and +1.40 respectively. COPO also achieves the highest MME score of 1589.3, exceeding the ablated variant without both causal components (1489.2) by nearly 100 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COPO consistently outperforms all 12 baselines on LLaVA-1.5, the model for which full COPO numbers are reported. Against the strongest baseline CSR (CHAIRS=21.0, CHAIRI=6.0, POPE=87.0), COPO reduces CHAIRS by 1.2 points and CHAIRI by 0.7 while improving POPE F1 by 1.0. The closest competitor GCPO (21.5/5.8/87.2) is also surpassed on all three metrics. On GPT-4-assisted evaluation, COPO leads the best baseline DeCo by +1.29 in Accuracy (8.71 vs 7.42), +0.57 in Correctness (6.89 vs 6.25), and +1.40 in Detailedness (9.58 vs 7.96). Ablation confirms both causal components are necessary: removing either S_suff or S_nec degrades CHAIRS to 21.7 or 22.5, and removing both drops it to 30.5, approaching the vanilla model's 45.0.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO-based reinforcement learning for MLLM post-training by injecting token-level causal constraints (sufficiency and necessity) into the advantage function, drawing on structural causal models and the probability of necessity and sufficiency (PNS) framework from causal inference. It departs from prior hallucination-mitigation methods that operate at the decoding stage (DoLa, OPERA, VCD, DeCo) or use preference-based training (HA-DPO, POVID, CSR) by addressing the root cause at the reward level during RL optimization. The closest prior work is GCPO [17], which also embeds causal structure into group-based policy optimization, but targets LLMs rather than MLLMs and does not address the specific spurious background-correlation problem identified here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why outcome-only RL rewards in MLLMs can entangle task-irrelevant background cues with correct answers, and offers a concrete mechanism (causal completeness reward) to decouple them. However, the causal sufficiency score requires H additional forward passes per token, raising computational cost during training, and the framework is tightly coupled to GRPO's group-sampling structure. Open questions include whether the causal completeness formulation generalizes to other RL post-training paradigms (e.g., PPO, DPO variants) and whether the binary causal/non-causal factor decomposition adequately captures the continuous spectrum of visual relevance in real-world scenes.
