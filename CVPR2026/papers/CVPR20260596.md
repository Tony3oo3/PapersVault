## Problem

VLA reinforcement learning suffers from severe reward sparsity: binary success/failure signals discard the rich behavioral information contained in failed trajectories, and existing process-reward approaches require expert demonstrations or hand-crafted task decompositions that do not scale to autonomous learning. The paper asks whether a model can generate its own dense, progress-aware supervision from within a single training batch without any external reference data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single third-person RGB image and a natural-language goal description at each timestep, producing action chunks (end-effector commands) via parallel decoding on a modified OpenVLA backbone. During training, the method additionally encodes entire rollout trajectories (sequences of RGB frames) through a frozen V-JEPA 2 world-model encoder to obtain latent embeddings, from which a scalar progress-wise reward in (0, 1) is computed for each failed trajectory relative to in-batch successes.

## Method

SRPO replaces binary outcome rewards with a self-referential progress reward: within each training batch, successful trajectories are encoded by a large-scale video-pretrained latent world model (V-JEPA 2), clustered via DBSCAN to yield representative centers, and the L2 distance from a failed trajectory's embedding to its nearest center determines its reward (normalized and squashed to (0,1)). This reward feeds into a GRPO-style clipped surrogate objective with KL regularization, so the policy is optimized on trajectory-level advantages derived entirely from in-batch self-comparison. The key insight is that latent world-model representations capture transferable, task-agnostic behavioral progress patterns—avoiding the poor cross-domain generalization of pixel-level world models and the domain-specific fine-tuning they require—while the self-referential design eliminates any need for expert demonstrations or manual reward engineering.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, SRPO (Online) achieves a 99.2% average success rate across four suites (Spatial 98.8%, Object 100.0%, Goal 99.4%, Long 98.6%), starting from a one-shot SFT baseline of only 48.9%—a 103% relative improvement in just 200 RL steps. On the LIBERO-Plus robustness benchmark, SRPO with perturbation data reaches 82.1% total, surpassing the full-shot SFT + perturbation-data baseline (OpenVLA-OFT+) at 79.5%. The method also demonstrates strong training efficiency, converging in 59–219 steps per suite versus tens of thousands for SFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRPO (Online) achieves 99.2% average on LIBERO, outperforming the strongest GRPO-based baselines RLinf (98.0%), RIPT-VLA (97.5%), and SimpleVLA-RL (96.9%) by 1.2–2.3 points, despite starting from a far weaker one-shot SFT baseline (48.9%) rather than full-shot SFT. Against task-specific reward methods TGRPO (80.7%) and VLA-RL (81.0%), SRPO gains 18+ points, demonstrating that self-referential latent-world rewards substantially outperform hand-crafted progress signals. On LIBERO-Plus, SRPO with perturbation data (82.1%) surpasses OpenVLA-OFT+ (79.5%), which uses additional wrist-camera and proprioception inputs, while SRPO without extra data (59.6%) already exceeds the full-shot SFT baseline (51.1%). In real-world experiments, the reward shaping method yields +66.8% average improvement on π0 and +86.7% on π0-FAST over their respective SFT baselines. The method also beats the pixel-level and ImageBind-based progress reward alternatives on all five evaluation metrics (SC, Mono, MMD, JS, SMD) in the reward quality benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRPO extends the GRPO framework (originally designed for LLM reasoning) to VLA policy optimization, replacing binary outcome rewards with progress-wise rewards derived from the model's own successful trajectories. It departs from prior VLA-RL methods (SimpleVLA-RL, RIPT-VLA, RLinf) that discard information from failed episodes, and from process-reward approaches (TGRPO, VLA-RL) that require expert demonstrations or hand-crafted task decompositions. The key conceptual departure is repurposing a pre-trained video world model's latent space (V-JEPA 2) as a task-agnostic behavioral progress metric, avoiding both pixel-level reconstruction and domain-specific reward engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a frozen, general-purpose video world model's latent representations can serve as a universal progress signal for robotic manipulation without any task-specific fine-tuning, which if validated at scale could eliminate the reward-engineering bottleneck in VLA-RL. However, the SOTA claim rests on a very weak one-shot SFT baseline (48.9%), and the margin over the strongest RL baselines (RLinf at 98.0%, RIPT-VLA at 97.5%) is modest (~1–2 points). Open questions include whether the self-referential mechanism degrades when no successful trajectories appear in early training batches, how it scales to substantially longer-horizon or multi-task settings, and whether the V-JEPA 2 latent space remains informative for tasks outside the manipulation domain.
