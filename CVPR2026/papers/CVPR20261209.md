## Problem

Prior rigid-body physics estimation methods assume a fixed-length parameter vector tied to specific object types and motion types, and typically require known camera poses, making them inapplicable to complex real-world scenes with multiple interacting objects and unconstrained viewpoints. No existing approach can recover a complete, general-purpose scene configuration (geometry, dynamics, camera) from a single monocular video in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261209/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 10 uniformly sampled frames (from 1-second, 30 FPS, 480×320 videos) either as raw RGB or as RAFT-computed optical flow converted to per-channel 2D arrays. It autoregressively generates a YAML-formatted scene configuration encoding object geometry (radius, height, width, depth, mass), material properties (friction, damping), initial kinematic states (position, velocity, quaternion orientation), and camera/environment parameters (pose, FOV, gravity). A motion-reasoning variant additionally emits a natural-language description of visibility, motion-change, and collision events before the configuration.

## Method

ΔYNAMICS fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-3B via supervised autoregressive text generation on 400K synthetic MuJoCo-rendered scenes, treating the YAML scene configuration as the target token sequence. Two key design choices improve generalization: (1) optical flow is used as a semantics-agnostic input that removes confounding visual appearance, and (2) an auxiliary natural-language motion description (derived from simulation traces via rule-based event mining) is prepended as a reasoning step before the configuration output. At test time, best-of-32 sampling (temperature 0.1, top-p 0.9) and CMA-ES evolutionary search over the generated configurations—using segmentation IoU minus flow EPE as a heuristic fitness—further refine accuracy without requiring ground-truth labels in the target domain.

## Results

On the CLEVRER cross-engine benchmark, ΔYNAMICS with motion reasoning and optical flow input achieves a full-sequence segmentation IoU of 0.30, a 7× improvement over the best VLM baseline (Claude-4-Sonnet at 0.04). On the in-distribution synthetic evaluation, the model reaches 99% object composition accuracy and 0.54 full-sequence segmentation IoU, while reducing optical flow EPE to 8.52 (vs. 11.07 for Claude-4-Sonnet). Test-time enhancements further boost performance: best-of-32 sampling improves first-frame IoU by 27% (0.30→0.38 on CLEVRER), and CMA-ES evolutionary search raises full-sequence IoU to 0.66, a 120% gain over greedy decoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ΔYNAMICS decisively outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest VLM baseline, Claude-4-Sonnet, the gap is most dramatic on motion reconstruction: full-sequence segmentation IoU improves from 0.07 to 0.54 (synthetic) and from 0.04 to 0.30 (CLEVRER), while optical flow EPE drops from 11.07 to 8.52. Qwen2.5-VL-7B and InternVL3-8B perform even worse, with segmentation IoU below 0.05 and flow EPE exceeding 15, indicating they essentially fail to reconstruct physical dynamics. The non-VLM ViViT baseline achieves 0% object composition accuracy, confirming that a fixed-length regression head cannot handle variable object counts and types. Notably, ΔYNAMICS with RGB input already surpasses all baselines on object composition (60% vs. 45% for Claude-4), but its flow EPE is slightly elevated due to interpenetration artifacts; switching to optical flow input resolves this and yields the best overall performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior rigid-body parameter estimation work (sliding boxes, billiards, projectiles) that assumed fixed-length parameter vectors, specific object types, and known camera poses, by recasting the problem as autoregressive generation of a YAML scene configuration via a VLM. It draws conceptual inspiration from structured-text generation for graphics (SVG, TikZ, JSON scene descriptions) but applies it to physics dynamics inference rather than rendering. Unlike differentiable-simulation approaches (Brax, DiffTaichi, GradSim) that require per-scene gradient optimization and differentiable engines, ΔYNAMICS uses a single feedforward pass on a standard, non-differentiable physics engine (MuJoCo), with optical flow as a semantics-agnostic input and optional motion-reasoning as an intermediate textual step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The language-based scene representation offers a scalable, interpretable, and engine-agnostic interface between perception and simulation that could unify diverse physics inference tasks under one generative framework. The demonstrated cross-engine (MuJoCo→Blender) and synthetic-to-real transfer is encouraging, but absolute reconstruction quality remains modest (full-sequence IoU ~0.30–0.65), and the current scope is limited to three primitive shapes, up to six objects, and one-second clips at 30 FPS. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-rigid or articulated bodies, longer temporal horizons, and whether the 3B model size is sufficient or whether scaling to larger VLMs would close the remaining gap to ground truth.
