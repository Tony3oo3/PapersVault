## Problem

Reinforcement learning for multimodal LLMs typically assigns a single trajectory-level advantage to all tokens in a response, making it impossible to distinguish correct from incorrect intermediate reasoning steps. For chart understanding, where reasoning is inherently stepwise (localize legends, read values, compare trends, synthesize), this coarse signal inadvertently punishes sound intermediate logic in wrong answers or rewards flawed steps in lucky correct ones, injecting noise that limits RL's benefit.

## Input / Output

The model receives a chart image and a natural-language query, then iteratively generates a sequence of (intent, action) pairs where each action renders a visual marker (box, line, point, or circle) onto the image. The annotated image is fed back as input for the next reasoning step, forming a closed interactive loop. The final output is a text answer, but the intermediate trajectory of drawn markers constitutes the structured reasoning path that FinePRM evaluates step-by-step.

## Method

FinePO is a two-phase RL algorithm: first, it computes a coarse cross-trajectory advantage via GRPO-style group comparison; second, it redistributes that advantage among individual steps using a Fine-grained Process Reward Model (FinePRM), a 4-class MLLM that scores each drawing action (Excellent/Acceptable/Poor/Unacceptable → 4.0/3.0/2.0/1.0) by comparing the before/after image pair against the stated intent. A KL-divergence regularization on action-type distribution prevents the policy from collapsing to easy-to-score actions, and a clipping mechanism (Eq. 8) ensures globally superior responses never yield negative step advantages. Training follows a Cold-Start SFT phase (50K samples) followed by FinePO RL (9K prompts), while the FinePRM itself is trained on 473K intent-action pairs generated via a cross-modal distillation pipeline (SAM segmentation → MLLM annotation → LLM trajectory simulation with noise injection).

## Results

SketchVL-3B achieves an average gain of 7.23% over its Qwen2.5VL-3B base model across all seven benchmarks, with the largest improvements on ChartQA (+15.32), EvoChart-QA (+7.92), and MMStar (+7.47). At the 7B scale, SketchVL-7B improves over Qwen2.5VL-7B on EvoChart-QA (+3.84), ChartQA (+1.96), MathVista (+2.10), and MMStar (+0.46), though it underperforms on PlotQA (55.84 vs. 63.44). On general-purpose benchmarks, SketchVL-7B reaches 63.50 on MathVista and 57.13 on MMStar, confirming that FinePO preserves generalization while enhancing chart reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct base model Qwen2.5VL, SketchVL-3B wins on all seven benchmarks, with particularly large margins on ChartQA (+15.32) and ChartQA-Pro (+6.42), while SketchVL-7B wins on five of seven but loses on PlotQA (55.84 vs. 63.44) and shows only marginal gains on ChartQA-Pro (+0.22) and ChartBench (+0.33). Compared to VLM-R1, SketchVL-3B outperforms on six of seven benchmarks (e.g., +20.38 on ChartBench, +4.22 on ChartQA) but trails on PlotQA (48.32 vs. 54.40) and MathVista (53.80 vs. 55.10). Against ChartSketcher-2B, SketchVL-3B dominates across all four shared benchmarks, with the largest gap on ChartBench (+29.86) and EvoChart-QA (+20.56). The paper attributes the larger 3B gains to the smaller model being less able to exploit shortcuts in the FinePRM, resulting in more faithful learning from the process reward signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

SketchVL operates within the Reasoning on Image (RoI) paradigm established by ChartSketcher and DeepEyes, where models externalize reasoning as visual markers on images. Its RL algorithm, FinePO, extends GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) by adding an intra-trajectory credit assignment layer that redistributes the coarse group-relative advantage across individual steps using a dedicated process reward model (FinePRM), analogous in spirit to VisualPRM but specialized for discrete visual marking actions. The cold-start data pipeline reuses ChartSketcher's synthesis approach, while the FinePRM training data is constructed via a novel cross-modal distillation pipeline leveraging SAM-based segmentation and LLM distillation into intent-action pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FinePO addresses a fundamental limitation in RL for MLLMs—trajectory-level credit assignment that cannot distinguish correct from incorrect intermediate steps—making it potentially applicable beyond charts to any multi-step visual reasoning task. The paper's ablations confirm the mechanism works (random PRM scores degrade performance, removing the RoI paradigm causes collapse), but the authors acknowledge the absence of a standardized benchmark for evaluating process reward models and the inherent difficulty of constructing an unbiased PRM. The finding that larger models (7B) are more susceptible to 'hacking' the PRM through action-type collapse, mitigated by KL regularization, raises open questions about the robustness of process-reward-guided RL at scale.
