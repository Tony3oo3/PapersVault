## Problem

Existing task-relevant segmentation methods for visual RL generalization (e.g., SGQN, MaDi) operate on a single current observation, ignoring temporal context from preceding frames. When task-relevant objects share visual similarity with backgrounds (e.g., matching colors), single-frame segmentation becomes unreliable, causing policies to overfit to irrelevant visual cues and degrade in unseen environments. No prior method exploits the disambiguating power of temporal cues across observation sequences to produce more robust task-relevant masks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding window of k consecutive RGB frames (and a data-augmented copy of that window) as input to the segmentation network, rather than a single frame. It outputs per-frame binary task-relevant masks for all k frames in the sequence, which are then encoded into a single embedding vector serving as the agent's state. The final policy output is a continuous action sampled from the SAC actor conditioned on this embedding.

## Method

TSTM introduces a temporal segmentation network with an encoder–ConvLSTM–decoder architecture that processes k-frame observation sequences, using the ConvLSTM to capture inter-frame dependencies and produce more reliable per-frame task-relevant masks than single-frame approaches. A teacher–student knowledge distillation scheme (aligning penultimate-layer features via a temperature-scaled L2 loss) yields a lightweight student network for efficient inference. The masked sequences are encoded by a CNN, and invariant representation learning enforces consistency between original and augmented sequence embeddings through an MSE loss combined with variance and covariance regularization (à la VICReg/Barlow Twins) to prevent collapse. The SAC actor-critic is trained on these embeddings, with an additional policy consistency loss that minimizes KL divergence between the actor's distributions on original versus augmented embeddings, further anchoring the policy to task-relevant structure.

## Results

On the DMC-GB benchmark, TSTM achieves the highest average final return of 934 under the video easy setting and 859 under the video hard setting, outperforming all six baselines. In video easy, TSTM leads on 4 of 5 tasks (e.g., Finger Spin 971 vs. SimGRL 957; Ball_in_cup Catch 969 vs. SimGRL 964), while in video hard the margin widens substantially (e.g., Finger Spin 906 vs. SimGRL 784; Walker Walk 821 vs. SimGRL 773). The only task where TSTM does not rank first is Walker Stand, where SimGRL edges ahead (973 vs. 968 in video easy; 932 vs. 923 in video hard) due to minimal agent motion limiting temporal cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSTM is compared against six baselines: SAC (vanilla actor-critic), SODA (soft data augmentation), SVEA (stabilized value estimation with augmentation), SGQN (saliency-guided Q-networks), MaDi (masking distractions), and SimGRL (simulation-based generalization). TSTM outperforms all six on 4 of 5 DMC-GB tasks in both video easy and video hard settings, with the largest gaps appearing in video hard where background complexity is greatest (e.g., Finger Spin: TSTM 906 vs. SimGRL 784, a 122-point margin; Walker Walk: TSTM 821 vs. SGQN 739). The closest competitor is SimGRL, which matches or slightly exceeds TSTM only on Walker Stand (973 vs. 968 in video easy; 932 vs. 923 in video hard), a task with minimal agent motion that limits the temporal cues TSTM exploits. Against the segmentation-based baselines SGQN and MaDi, TSTM shows consistent and substantial improvements, particularly in video hard (e.g., Cartpole Swingup: TSTM 741 vs. SGQN 526; Ball_in_cup Catch: TSTM 903 vs. MaDi 758), confirming that temporal segmentation yields more reliable task-relevant masks than single-frame approaches. SAC and SODA, which lack explicit task-relevant region extraction, perform markedly worse, with SAC averaging only 127 in video hard versus TSTM's 859.

## Relation to Prior Work

TSTM extends the task-relevant segmentation line in visual RL generalization initiated by SGQN (saliency-guided Q-networks) and MaDi (pixel-wise distraction masking), both of which segment task-relevant regions from a single current observation. The key departure is the introduction of a ConvLSTM-based encoder-temporal-decoder architecture that exploits sequential observations to resolve ambiguities when the agent and background share similar visual features. The method further integrates invariant representation learning (VICReg-style variance and covariance regularization) and a teacher-student knowledge distillation scheme for efficient inference, building on the SAC actor-critic backbone and the DMC-GB generalization benchmark established by SVEA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring failure mode in visual RL: when task-relevant objects are visually confusable with backgrounds, single-frame segmentation degrades and policies overfit to spurious cues. The temporal segmentation approach is a natural but previously unexploited remedy, and the ablations confirm it is the dominant contributor to generalization gains, especially under hard visual conditions. Limitations include the need for ground-truth masks to train the teacher network, the added training pipeline complexity (teacher → student → RL), and evaluation restricted to DMC-GB and Robotic Manipulation benchmarks; whether the approach transfers to real-world, high-dimensional, or multi-object scenes remains open.
