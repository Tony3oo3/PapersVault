## Problem

Vision-language models excel at single-turn multimodal understanding but struggle with embodied task planning, which demands multi-turn interaction, long-horizon reasoning, and management of extensive contextual dependencies. Existing chain-of-thought approaches produce intermediate reasoning traces that lack principled formulation and direct supervision, making it difficult to ensure their soundness. The paper addresses this by asking whether a single VLM can be structured to explicitly invoke and supervise individual vision-language sub-capabilities, yielding a more controllable and transparent planning process without external tools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At the start of each episode the agent receives a free-form text instruction; at every timestep it observes an egocentric RGB image and outputs a sequence of atomic actions drawn from a predefined action set (e.g., navigate, pick, place, open, close). The scheduler and all five capabilities are instantiated within a single Qwen2.5-VL-3B model, so the same weights handle both the high-level planning loop and the low-level perception/reasoning queries. In the text-only ALFWorld variant, image-dependent capabilities (OG, SD, ES) are repurposed to parse textual scene descriptions, allowing the same fine-tuned model to operate without any additional training.

## Method

RoboAgent decomposes embodied planning into a scheduler that sequentially invokes five vision-language capabilities (Exploration Guidance, Object Grounding, Scene Description, Action Decoding, and Experience Summarization), each maintaining its own context and producing either textual feedback or atomic actions. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) SFT on expert trajectories where simulator internals (scene graphs, segmentation masks, environment messages) provide fine-grained ground-truth supervision for each capability; (2) DAgger-style fine-tuning on model-generated trajectories with corrective annotations retrieved via semantic matching against ground-truth invocations, plus data augmentation for open-vocabulary robustness; (3) reinforcement fine-tuning of the scheduler using a novel Expert-Induced Policy Optimization (EIPO) algorithm that replaces the standard policy advantage with the exactly computable expert advantage, combined with a GRPO-style group baseline to encourage progressive improvement over purely negative gradients. The key architectural insight is that all modules share one end-to-end trainable VLM, eliminating external tool dependencies while enabling per-capability supervision that is unavailable to monolithic CoT planners.

## Results

On ALFWorld (visual observation), RoboAgent achieves 77.6% average success rate, substantially outperforming the next-best method SEEA-R1 (36.0%) by over 40 points, with particularly strong results on Pick (92%), Look (94%), and Clean (84%) tasks. On EB-ALFRED, RoboAgent reaches 67.0% average SR, surpassing all open-source fine-tuning baselines (WAP 62.7%, RoboGPT-R1 55.3%) and even exceeding GPT-4o (56.3%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (62.3%) on the base, visual, and long-horizon splits. On ALFWorld (text observation), the model achieves 92.1% seen and 94.0% unseen SR, matching or exceeding larger LLM-based agents such as DynaMind (92.5/89.1) and BPO (87.9/89.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboAgent (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) decisively outperforms all RL-based VLM baselines on ALFWorld visual: SEEA-R1 (36.0%), TCPO (26.7%), CoSo (26.5%), GFlowVLM (26.1%), RL4VLM (21.7%), and GTR (17.0%), with the gap driven primarily by superior exploration behavior from the EG/OG capabilities. On EB-ALFRED, RoboAgent (67.0%) beats the strongest open-source fine-tuning baselines WAP (62.7%) and RoboGPT-R1 (55.3%), and also surpasses zero-shot GPT-4o (56.3%) and Gemini-1.5-Pro (62.3%) on the base, visual, and long-horizon splits, though it trails Claude-3.7-Sonnet (67.7%) by a narrow margin. On ALFWorld text, RoboAgent (92.1/94.0) is competitive with DynaMind (92.5/89.1) and BPO (87.9/89.6) despite using a smaller 3B VLM backbone rather than 7B/8B LLMs. On out-of-domain benchmarks, RoboAgent achieves 22.3% SR on EB-Habitat (marginally above RoboGPT-R1 at 22.0%) and 22.1% SSR on LoTa-WAH (nearly 2x LLaMA-30B at 10.4%), but remains well below zero-shot closed-source models (GPT-4o at 59.0% on EB-Habitat), indicating limited cross-simulator generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CoT-based embodied planning paradigm (where a single model generates free-form reasoning traces before acting) by introducing explicit, typed capability interfaces (Exploration Guidance, Object Grounding, Scene Description, Action Decoding, Experience Summarization) that a scheduler invokes as needed. The training pipeline builds on DAgger for corrective supervision on model-generated trajectories and on PPO/GRPO for the final reinforcement fine-tuning stage, but introduces EIPO—a novel objective that optimizes against a deterministic expert policy's advantage rather than the learned policy's own estimated advantage, yielding a more stable training signal. Unlike tool-augmented LLM planners that rely on external APIs or separate models, all modules here are implemented within a single end-to-end trainable VLM, positioning the work at the intersection of capability decomposition and unified model training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The capability-driven decomposition offers a practical path to interpretable and debuggable embodied planning: each capability can be supervised independently using simulator internals (scene graphs, segmentation masks, environment messages), and failures can be attributed to specific modules. The finding that a 3B model with structured capability invocation substantially outperforms 7B models trained with standard CoT or RL on the same benchmarks suggests that architectural decomposition may be more effective than raw scale for multi-step embodied reasoning. However, OOD generalization remains a significant limitation—on EB-Habitat the model achieves only 22.3% SR versus 59.0% for GPT-4o—indicating that the approach is still tightly coupled to the training simulator's object categories and action spaces. Open questions include whether the capability set can be learned or dynamically expanded, whether the simulator-internal supervision strategy transfers to real-robot settings where such privileged information is unavailable, and whether the EIPO objective generalizes beyond the ALFWorld-style discrete action spaces.
