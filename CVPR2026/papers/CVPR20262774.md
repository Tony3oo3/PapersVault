## Problem

Pretrained LLMs lack fine-grained, location-specific traffic knowledge and tend to generate homogeneous scenarios that deviate from real-world traffic distributions, especially across diverse geographic contexts (metropolitan areas, national parks, small towns). Existing LLM-based scenario generation methods either rely on labor-intensive manual descriptions or produce scenarios misaligned with the target region's actual traffic patterns, limiting their utility for both stress-testing and improving autonomous driving models.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes first-person driving video frames sampled at one frame per 15 seconds from YouTube (261 videos across six regions), processed by a multimodal LLM to produce structured natural language descriptions covering road network/environment, contextual details (traffic density, weather, time of day), and actor positions/behaviors. These descriptions are then converted into executable Scenic scripts that configure the CARLA simulator's road network, weather, and NPC vehicle/pedestrian placements. The aligned LLM (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) accepts a text prompt specifying the target region and outputs region-consistent scenario descriptions.

## Method

TRAFFICALIGN is a three-stage pipeline: (1) Data Synthesis uses GPT-4.1-nano with a compositional prompt (role-playing, chain-of-thought, few-shot examples) to extract structured traffic scenario descriptions from individual video frames; (2) Data Validation translates each description into a formal domain-specific language (DSL) via GPT-5, then performs systematic semantic checks for completeness and validity—discarding non-scenario frames and triggering a self-refinement loop for partially incomplete scenarios by appending diagnostic messages back to the extractor; (3) LLM Alignment fine-tunes Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct via LoRA on attention and MLP projection layers using standard SFT (next-token cross-entropy with loss masked to assistant tokens only) for 60 steps at lr=2e-4. The key insight is grounding LLM generation in empirically observed traffic distributions from real driving videos rather than relying on the model's parametric knowledge, with the DSL serving as a formal intermediary that enables rigorous, automated quality control before alignment.

## Results

On the SafeBench platform, TRAFFICALIGN-generated scenarios reveal up to 10.8% more collisions (CR 0.933 for Los Angeles) than the strongest baseline ChatScene (CR 0.825), with a 2.7%–10.8% collision-rate increase across all six geographic regions. The overall score (OS, lower is more challenging) drops to 0.310–0.457 for TRAFFICALIGN versus 0.481 for ChatScene and 0.573–0.620 for adversary/rule-based baselines, representing a 5.0%–35.6% relative reduction. When driving models (PPO, SAC, TD3) are fine-tuned on TRAFFICALIGN scenarios, collision rates drop by 21.0%–36.1% and overall scores improve by 22.4%–49.7% relative to the un-fine-tuned models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAFFICALIGN outperforms all five baselines on nearly every metric across all six geographic regions. Against the strongest baseline ChatScene [66], TRAFFICALIGN achieves a 2.7%–10.8% higher collision rate (e.g., 0.933 vs. 0.825 in Los Angeles) and a 5.0%–35.6% lower overall score (e.g., 0.310 vs. 0.481 for Yellowstone), indicating substantially more challenging and safety-critical scenarios. The two adversary-based baselines (Learning-to-Collide [12], AdvSim [54]) and two rule-based baselines (CARLA Scenario Generator [49], Adversarial Trajectory Optimization [68]) are all dominated by TRAFFICALIGN by wide margins: their collision rates range from 0.584 to 0.676 versus TRAFFICALIGN's 0.852–0.933, and their overall scores (0.573–0.620) are far higher (less challenging) than TRAFFICALIGN's 0.310–0.457. In the driving-model improvement experiment (Table 3), TRAFFICALIGN also beats ChatScene by a further 2.0%–3.7% in collision-rate reduction and 5.1%–27.9% in overall-score improvement after fine-tuning, confirming that real-world-aligned scenarios transfer more effectively to improve driving model safety.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based traffic scenario generation line (ChatScene, TARGET, ScenicNL) by addressing their shared limitation: reliance on the LLM's internal, location-agnostic knowledge rather than real-world observations. It borrows the synthetic-data alignment paradigm from NLP (Self-Instruct, Evol-Instruct, FLAME) and applies it to the traffic domain, using a multimodal LLM to extract scenario descriptions from driving videos and a DSL-based validation loop (building on TARGET's grammar) to filter hallucinated or irrelevant content before fine-tuning a small LLM via LoRA. The key conceptual departure from ChatScene is that scenario generation is no longer a single-shot prompt to a generalist LLM but the output of a model explicitly aligned to a target region's traffic distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a practical, scalable pipeline for producing region-specific traffic scenario benchmarks without manual annotation, which is directly useful for AD safety testing and model improvement. The 36.1% collision-rate reduction from fine-tuning driving models on aligned scenarios suggests that distribution-matched synthetic data can substitute for expensive real-world data collection. Open questions include whether single-frame extraction (the paper's acknowledged limitation) suffices for capturing rare or temporally complex events, whether alignment transfers across regions without retraining, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the CARLA/Scenic simulation stack to higher-fidelity or multi-agent simulators.
