## Problem

Large vision foundation models like SAM2 achieve strong video object segmentation and tracking performance but incur prohibitive computational costs. Static pruning is input-agnostic and cannot adapt to the diverse complexity of individual video frames, while existing dynamic models with per-block routers fail to convert theoretical FLOPs reductions into actual speedup due to dense routing overhead. Moreover, both static and dynamic approaches treat video as a sequence of independent images, ignoring temporal correlations between consecutive frames.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a semi-supervised VOS setting where the first frame's ground-truth mask is provided. For each subsequent frame, the router consumes the patchified visual embedding of the current frame (H×W×C) fused with the binary segmentation mask from the immediately preceding frame (H×W×1), producing a binary routing vector of length L (48 for SAM2) that determines which encoder blocks execute. The final output is a per-frame segmentation mask generated by the decoder conditioned on the dynamically selected submodel's features.

## Method

RDS introduces a single Prediction-Aware Router (PAR) that fuses current-frame visual features with the previous frame's segmentation mask via learnable convolutional projections and a scalar weight, then applies a convolution-pooling-linear pipeline to produce routing logits. A Straight-Through Estimator with Bernoulli sampling enables end-to-end training of the discrete block-selection decision, while a target activation ratio β normalizes the expected number of active blocks. Unlike per-block routers (e.g., AdaViT), this single router reduces routing latency from ~17.2 ms to ~0.5 ms, converting FLOPs savings into real 1.3× speedup. To minimize adaptation cost, I-LoRA identifies critical blocks using a dual importance metric—global (drop in J&F score upon block removal) and local (1 minus cosine similarity between block input/output features)—and injects rank-32 LoRA adapters only into those blocks, requiring just 7.6M trainable parameters and <0.03% of SAM2's original training data.

## Results

On DAVIS 2017, RDS achieves a J&F score of 89.6 with 38.2 FPS (1.3× speedup over SAM2's 29.8 FPS) while using only 7.6M trainable parameters (<0.03% of the training set) and 500 GFLOPs, compared to SAM2's 224M params and 819 GFLOPs. On the VOT benchmarks (LaSOT, TrackingNet, GOT-10k), RDS0.6 applied to SAMURAI yields 1.3× speedup (35.7 vs 28.5 FPS) with AUC drops of 2.2–2.7 points. On DINOv3-L, RDS0.6 actually *improves* J&F from 38.4 to 40.3 while accelerating inference by 1.6×, demonstrating that block skipping removes detrimental backbone redundancy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RDS clearly outperforms all four efficiency baselines on the DAVIS 2017 Pareto frontier: it beats SlimSAM by 1.8 J&F points (89.6 vs 87.8) at 5× fewer trainable parameters (7.6M vs 147M) and higher FPS (38.2 vs 33.4); it surpasses PBD by 4.0 J&F points (89.6 vs 85.6) at comparable speed; and it dominates AdaViT by 1.3 J&F points (89.6 vs 88.3) and 11.9 FPS (38.2 vs 26.3), the gap attributable to AdaViT's per-block router overhead (~17.2 ms vs RDS's single-router ~0.5 ms). DyT achieves a marginally higher J&F (90.2 vs 89.6) but at the cost of 2.7× more parameters (20.5M vs 7.6M) and 1.7× slower inference (22.3 vs 38.2 FPS), making RDS the superior speed–accuracy trade-off. When transferred to SAMURAI and SAM2Long without retraining, RDS0.6 delivers 1.2–1.3× speedup with <2.5% J&F degradation, and on DINOv3-L it simultaneously improves J&F by 1.9 points while accelerating 1.6×, confirming architecture-agnostic generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SAM2 vision foundation model for video object segmentation and tracking, extending the dynamic neural network paradigm (AdaViT, DynamicViT, BlockDrop) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) into the VOST setting. It departs from prior dynamic methods in two key ways: (1) replacing per-block routers with a single Prediction-Aware Router that fuses the previous frame's segmentation mask with current visual features, thereby exploiting temporal correlations that both static pruning (SlimSAM, PBD) and existing dynamic approaches ignore; and (2) introducing I-LoRA, which uses a dual global/local importance metric to allocate LoRA adapters only to the most critical blocks rather than uniformly. The work also demonstrates orthogonality to distillation by applying RDS to a DINOv3-distilled model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical finding that per-block routers add ~17 ms overhead that negates theoretical FLOPs savings (turning a 'faster' model into a slower one) is a useful caution for the dynamic-networks community, and the single-router design offers a concrete remedy. The 1.3× real-world speedup with <0.4% J&F loss on DAVIS 2017 using only 3% trainable parameters and <0.03% of SAM2's training data makes efficient VOST deployment more accessible. Open questions include whether the single-frame temporal prior (M_{t-1}) suffices for very long videos or multi-object scenarios, and whether the approach extends to streaming video understanding and video generation models as the authors note. The DINOv3 result—where RDS simultaneously improves accuracy and speed—suggests that distilled backbones may retain harmful redundancy, an observation worth further investigation.
