## Problem

State-of-the-art video diffusion models produce visually high-fidelity clips that frequently violate basic physical laws—rigid objects deform like soft bodies, and fluid motions lack incompressibility or coherent advection. Existing physics-aware approaches either extract motion cues without enforcing governing equations or rely on computationally expensive simulations and manually predefined constraints, limiting generalizability. NS-Diff addresses this gap by integrating lightweight, differentiable physics constraints directly into the latent denoising process via reinforcement learning, without requiring external simulators or hand-crafted per-scene constraints.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video generation, the model takes a text prompt and iteratively denoises a noisy latent over T diffusion steps; for unconditional generation, it operates purely on the latent trajectory. At selected denoising steps, the noisy latent z_t is decoded to a low-resolution RGB proxy via the VAE decoder to compute optical flow and material attributes, which are then re-injected as conditioning. The output is a 16-frame, 256×256 video clip. A key design constraint is that physics guidance is gated off during early high-noise steps (where motion estimation is unreliable) and smoothly activated as the latent becomes more structured, via an adaptive scheduler tied to the noise schedule.

## Method

NS-Diff builds on a pretrained DiT-based video diffusion backbone and adds three modules. First, a noise-robust physical attribute detector decodes intermediate latents to RGB proxies, runs ARFlow optical flow with global homography compensation and 5-frame temporal smoothing, then classifies patches as rigid (via affine-fit residual with a temperature threshold) or fluid (via divergence/vorticity magnitude), and predicts material embeddings through a small MLP. Second, a Physics-Conditioned Latent Injection (PCLI) module concatenates per-patch velocity, deformation gradient, and material embedding into a descriptor, projects it to a 128-d vector, and injects it into the DiT via cross-attention with an adaptive sigmoid gate that scales influence along the denoising trajectory. Third, the denoising process is reformulated as a physics-constrained MDP: the DiT's noise prediction is treated as a stochastic Gaussian policy, and PPO with GAE optimizes it against a reward that penalizes jerk (minimum-jerk principle) in rigid regions and a simplified Navier-Stokes residual (divergence, Laplacian viscosity, and advection terms with a soft pressure-proxy) in fluid regions. An adaptive activation scheduler zeroes out both the cross-attention conditioning and the physics reward during early high-noise steps, preventing unstable gradients from unreliable motion estimates.

## Results

On PhysVideoBench, NS-Diff-DiT1B reduces jerk error (ΔJ) to 0.33 and fluid divergence (L_div) to 2.9×10⁻³, while achieving 73.1 appearance and 92.4 motion scores; the 11B variant further improves to ΔJ=0.25, L_div=2.4×10⁻³, 74.4 appearance, and 93.7 motion. On UCF-101, NS-Diff-DiT11B achieves FVD=85 and frame consistency 0.95, outperforming the strongest baseline (OpenSora2-11B, FVD=110). On WebVid-10M text-to-video, NS-Diff reaches FVD=275 and CLIPSIM=0.34, surpassing VideoFactory (FVD=322) and OpenSora2 (FVD=297).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NS-Diff outperforms all five baselines on PhysVideoBench across every metric. The largest physical gains are over VideoJam (ΔJ 0.33 vs 0.74, a 55% reduction) and OpenSora2-1B (ΔJ 0.33 vs 0.72, a 54% reduction), while the closest competitor is PhysGen (ΔJ 0.63, L_div 3.5), which relies on explicit rigid-body simulation with user-defined forces; NS-Diff avoids this requirement while still halving jerk error. On UCF-101, NS-Diff-DiT11B (FVD=85) beats OpenSora2-11B (FVD=110) by 23% and CogVideo (FVD=305) by 72%, with frame consistency improving from 0.91 to 0.95. On WebVid-10M text-to-video, NS-Diff (FVD=275, CLIPSIM=0.34) edges out OpenSora2 (FVD=297, CLIPSIM=0.32) and VideoFactory (FVD=322, CLIPSIM=0.31), confirming that physics-guided RL generalizes to open-domain prompts without degrading text alignment. No baseline is competitive on both physical and visual metrics simultaneously: methods strong on FVD (e.g., OpenSora2-11B) still exhibit 2–3× higher jerk and divergence errors than NS-Diff.

## Relation to Prior Work

NS-Diff sits at the intersection of three research threads: DiT-based video diffusion (OpenSora2), RL-guided refinement of diffusion models (Black et al., ICLR 2024), and physics-aware generative modeling (PhysGen, PhysDiff, Phy3DGen). It departs from prior physics-aware video generation by enforcing constraints directly in the latent denoising trajectory via PPO policy gradients, rather than relying on external simulators, predefined force models, or post-hoc correction. The noise-robust optical flow pipeline (ARFlow fine-tuned on synthetic noisy latents) and the adaptive activation scheduler are practical contributions that address the underexplored problem of extracting reliable motion cues from partially denoised representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of PhysVideoBench fills a genuine gap: prior video benchmarks (UCF-101, Kinetics, VBench) measure perceptual quality but lack ground-truth motion annotations for evaluating conservation laws and material-specific dynamics. The finding that PPO-based RL substantially outperforms direct physics-loss optimization (ΔJ 0.33 vs. 0.58) suggests that temporal coherence in physics enforcement benefits from the sequential decision-making structure of RL, not just per-step gradient signals. Limitations include the simplified 2D Navier-Stokes approximation (no full pressure solve), the minimum-jerk heuristic that cannot capture collisions or friction, and the self-constructed benchmark which may introduce evaluation bias. Open questions include whether this framework extends to 3D fluid/rigid-body interactions, longer video horizons, and whether the RL overhead scales acceptably to larger models.
