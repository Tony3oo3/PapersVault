## Problem

Existing transformer-based UAV trackers treat occlusion as random information dropout, overlooking its structured, spatially correlated nature in aerial scenes where buildings, vegetation, and vehicles produce clustered visibility loss. This mismatch between synthetic random masking and real-world occlusion geometry limits feature robustness and tracking stability under partial visibility. The paper rethinks occlusion as a structured spatial process and proposes a principled modeling strategy that captures the clustered, density-adaptive character of aerial occlusions.

## Input / Output

The tracker takes a 128×128 template crop and a 256×256 search region as input, which are jointly embedded into patch tokens and processed by a single-stream ViT backbone. During training, a clustered-masked variant of the template is additionally generated by COM and concatenated with the unmasked template and search region. The output is a center-based prediction consisting of a classification confidence map, a sub-pixel position offset, and a normalized bounding box size, decoded by a lightweight Conv-BN-ReLU head. At inference, CADB adaptively selects a single representative encoder layer to execute, reducing effective depth without additional runtime overhead.

## Method

OCTrack integrates two key components into a single-stream ViT tracker. Clustered Occlusion Modeling (COM) generates realistic occlusion masks via a mixture of Gaussian fields parameterized by an Adaptive Cluster Parameterization (ACP) mechanism that controls cluster count (λ_p), per-cluster intensity (μ), and spatial spread (σ), enabling density-adaptive patterns from sparse to dense regimes. A consistency loss aligns the latent features of the original and clustered-masked templates, teaching the backbone to preserve semantic coherence under structured visibility loss. Cost-Aware Depth Bias (CADB) introduces a linear depth-dependent bias b_i = κ[1 − 2(i − i_ref)/(L−1)] added to an MLP router's layer-importance logits, steering inference toward shallower layers when deeper reasoning offers diminishing returns; the router is trained with cross-entropy against alignment-based pseudo-labels derived from inter-layer cosine similarity. The overall loss combines focal classification, L1 and GIoU regression, and the routing loss, and the entire framework achieves 85.0% precision at ~265 FPS on UAVDT.

## Results

OCTrack-b (balanced occlusion setting) achieves 86.0% average precision and 65.7% average AUC across four UAV benchmarks (DTB70, UAVDT, VisDrone2018, UAV123), while running at 265.2 FPS on GPU with only 1.7–2.4 GMac and 5.8–8 M parameters. On UAVDT specifically, it records 85.0% precision and 63.0% AUC, setting a new benchmark. The dense variant (OCTrack-d) achieves the highest AUC on UAV123 (67.7%) and VisDrone2018 (66.3%), confirming that denser occlusion training improves robustness in heavily occluded sequences.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OCTrack-b outperforms all 15 baselines on average precision (86.0% vs. 85.6% for ORTrack, 85.5% for AbaViTrack, 84.9% for SGLATrack) and average AUC (65.7% vs. 65.0% for ORTrack, 64.5% for AbaViTrack). Against the strongest ViT-based competitors, OCTrack-b leads on UAVDT by a clear margin (85.0%/63.0% vs. 83.4%/60.1% for ORTrack and 81.9%/59.9% for SGLATrack) while also running faster (265.2 FPS vs. 182.6 for ORTrack and 243.9 for SGLATrack). On VisDrone2018, OCTrack-b achieves the best AUC (67.0%) and near-best precision (88.5%), surpassing ORTrack (88.6%/66.8%) in AUC. The dense variant OCTrack-d further extends the lead on UAV123 AUC (67.7% vs. 66.9% for SGLATrack) and VisDrone2018 (87.0%/66.3%), confirming that clustered occlusion training is most beneficial in heavily occluded, high-density scenes. DCF-based (RACF: 75.9%/51.8%) and CNN-based (DRCI: 81.4%/60.1%) trackers are consistently outperformed by 4–10 points in precision and 5–14 points in AUC, highlighting the advantage of the transformer backbone combined with structured occlusion modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the single-stream transformer paradigm established by OSTrack and MixFormer for the backbone, while departing from prior occlusion modeling strategies that treat visibility loss as either independent random dropout (MAE-style masking) or a fixed-intensity spatial Cox process (ORTrack). The key conceptual shift is reframing occlusion as a structured, spatially clustered process governed by Gaussian mixture fields, formalized through the Adaptive Cluster Parameterization mechanism. On the efficiency side, CADB extends the line of dynamic layer-selection work (SGLATrack, AbaViTrack) by introducing an explicit computation-cost prior into the routing logits rather than relying solely on learned similarity signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clustered occlusion modeling approach is training-only and adds no inference overhead, making it a drop-in regularizer for any ViT-based tracker beyond UAV tracking. The depth-bias mechanism offers a simple, interpretable knob for the accuracy–speed trade-off on resource-constrained platforms, though it relies on a fixed linear prior rather than a learned cost model, which may limit adaptability to heterogeneous scene complexity. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-mixture occlusion prior generalizes to non-aerial domains (e.g., autonomous driving with dynamic occluders) and whether the fixed depth bias can be replaced by a learned, input-conditioned cost model without sacrificing the simplicity that makes CADB attractive for deployment.
