## Problem

MLLMs face a core efficiency bottleneck: high-resolution video encoding produces thousands of visual tokens (e.g., 1,536–3,072 tokens for a 6-second clip), document knowledge learning depends on brittle external PDF parsers that introduce structural errors, and RL-trained reasoning models produce excessively verbose outputs even for trivial tasks. These three issues compound to make capable MLLMs prohibitively expensive to train and deploy at scale.

## Input / Output

The model accepts high-resolution images in any aspect ratio (partitioned via LLaVA-UHD into slices near 448×448), video clips of up to 1080 frames at a maximum 10 fps, document images, and text prompts. The 3D-Resampler compresses each 448×448 image slice to 64 tokens and a 6-second 2-fps 448×448 video to only 128 tokens. Outputs are text responses in two controllable modes: a concise short-reasoning answer or an explicit step-by-step long-reasoning trace, switched via prompt.

## Method

MiniCPM-V 4.5 (8B) introduces three coupled improvements. First, a unified 3D-Resampler extends the prior 2D-Resampler by adding temporal positional embeddings and cross-attention over packages of adjacent video frames, achieving a 12–24× token reduction over Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3 while using the same architecture and weights for both images and videos. Second, a unified document learning paradigm replaces external parsers by dynamically corrupting text regions at three levels (mild noise for OCR, heavy noise for integrated inference, full masking for contextual knowledge), training the model to predict original text from corrupted document images in a single objective. Third, a hybrid RL post-training strategy uses GRPO with rollouts that randomly alternate between short and long reasoning modes, combining rule-verified and probability-based (RLPR) rewards with a calibrated preference reward, and integrates RLAIF-V extended to video to reduce hallucinations—consuming only 70.5% of the training tokens of a long-reasoning-only strategy while improving both modes.

## Results

MiniCPM-V 4.5 (8B) achieves an average score of 77.0 on OpenCompass (8-benchmark composite), surpassing GPT-4o-latest (75.4) and Qwen2.5-VL 72B (76.1). On OCR and document tasks, it leads with OCRBench 89.0 (vs. GPT-4o 82.2) and OmniDocBench error rate 0.18/0.25 (vs. GPT-4o 0.23/0.40). On VideoMME, it scores 67.9 while using only 0.26h inference time and 28 GB GPU memory on 8 A100s, compared to 3.00h/60 GB for Qwen2.5-VL 7B (71.6) and 2.63h/32 GB for GLM-4.1V (73.6). On hallucination benchmarks, it achieves the lowest ObjHalBench rate (5.2/9.3) and MMHal-Bench rate (19.4) among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, MiniCPM-V 4.5 outperforms GPT-4o-latest on OpenCompass (77.0 vs. 75.4), OCRBench (89.0 vs. 82.2), DocVQA (94.7 vs. 93.0), OmniDocBench (0.18/0.25 vs. 0.23/0.40), and both hallucination benchmarks, while trailing on MMMU (67.7 vs. 72.9) and MathVista (79.9 vs. 71.6). Against the much larger Qwen2.5-VL 72B, it wins on OpenCompass (77.0 vs. 76.1), OCRBench (89.0 vs. 88.2), and OmniDocBench (0.18/0.25 vs. 0.21/0.26), but loses on MMMU (67.7 vs. 68.2), MathVista (79.9 vs. 74.2), and VideoMME (67.9 vs. 73.3). Among same-scale open-source models, it beats GLM-4.1V 9B on OpenCompass (77.0 vs. 76.6), OCRBench (89.0 vs. 84.2), and OmniDocBench (0.18/0.25 vs. 0.46/0.57), and beats MiMo-VL-RL 7B on OpenCompass (77.0 vs. 76.4) and OCRBench (89.0 vs. 86.6). It slightly trails Qwen3-VL Thinking 8B on OpenCompass (77.0 vs. 77.5) and MMMU (67.7 vs. 74.1), but leads substantially on OCRBench (89.0 vs. 81.9) and hallucination metrics. On video understanding, it surpasses Qwen2.5-VL 7B on LVBench (50.4 vs. 45.3) and MotionBench (59.7 vs. 53.0) while using only 46.7% GPU memory and 8.7% inference time on VideoMME.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the MiniCPM-V lineage by upgrading the 2D-Resampler to a unified 3D-Resampler that jointly compresses spatial and temporal dimensions, and adopts the LLaVA-UHD partitioning strategy for high-resolution image handling. It departs from the dominant paradigm of relying on brittle external PDF parsers for document training by proposing a unified dynamic-visual-corruption objective that subsumes OCR and knowledge learning. On the training side, it builds on GRPO-based RL and RLAIF-V but introduces a hybrid rollout strategy that jointly optimizes short and long reasoning modes, complementing rule-based rewards with RLPR's probability-based signals for general-domain coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12–24× video token reduction and ~10× inference-time savings on VideoMME make high-frame-rate and long-video understanding practically deployable on consumer hardware, addressing a core bottleneck that has limited MLLM accessibility. The unified document learning paradigm eliminates an entire class of fragile parsing pipelines, and the hybrid RL strategy offers a template for controllable reasoning in future MLLMs. Open questions include whether the simple 3D-Resampler scales gracefully to very long videos (beyond 1080 frames) or higher resolutions, whether the corruption-based document paradigm generalizes to non-Latin scripts or highly irregular layouts, and whether the cross-mode generalization observed in hybrid RL extends to more than two reasoning granularities.
