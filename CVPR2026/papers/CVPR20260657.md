## Problem

Heterogeneous Cryo-EM reconstruction requires assigning extremely noisy 2D particle images to unknown 3D conformational states without supervision. Existing encoder-decoder methods (e.g., CryoDRGN) are constrained by Gaussian latent priors and encoder capacity, while decoder-only codebook methods (e.g., DRGN-AI) suffer from initialization sensitivity and mode collapse under compositional heterogeneity. The core unresolved challenge is achieving stable, discriminative image-to-structure assignment that avoids both premature hard clustering and indistinguishable soft blending.

## Input / Output

The method consumes noisy 2D cryo-EM particle images (downsampled to D=256 or D=128) together with their known poses (rotation and translation), operating entirely in the Fourier domain. It produces a per-particle 3D density map via inverse Fourier transform of a weighted triplane volume, along with a soft assignment distribution over K codebook entries that encodes the particle's conformational identity. The triplane planes have spatial resolution ⌊D/2⌋ and the codebook MLP uses three hidden layers of width 1024, keeping the architecture lightweight relative to encoder-decoder alternatives.

## Method

CryoKRAQEN is a decoder-only framework that represents 3D molecular densities as triplane-parameterized implicit fields in Fourier space, eliminating the need for an encoder or Gaussian latent prior. For each particle, the model extracts a pose-aligned 2D Fourier slice from the triplane, computes normalized cosine distances to K learnable codebook entries, and maps these distances through an Epanechnikov kernel (compact support, noise-suppressing) followed by a temperature-controlled softmax with a cosine annealing schedule that progressively sharpens soft assignments toward near-hard clustering. A bidirectional triplet-margin loss regularizes the codebook by enforcing intra-state compactness and inter-state separation on both the positive (kernel-weighted) and negative (inverted-kernel-weighted) representations. Training minimizes an L1 Fourier-space reconstruction loss plus the triplet term, and at inference the learned assignment weights directly combine triplane codebook entries to generate each particle's 3D volume.

## Results

On the CryoBench benchmark, CryoKRAQEN achieves a mean Per-Image AUC-FSC of 0.375 on IgG-1D, 0.352 on IgG-RL, 0.418 on Ribosembly, 0.335 on Tomotwin-100, and 0.328 on Spike-MD. Its strongest advantage is on the highly compositional Tomotwin-100 dataset, where it outperforms the best neural baseline (CryoDRGN, 0.316) by ~6% and the classical RECOVAR (0.258) by ~30%. On conformational datasets (IgG-1D, IgG-RL, Spike-MD), the classical RECOVAR method retains a slight edge (e.g., 0.386 vs. 0.375 on IgG-1D), while CryoKRAQEN consistently surpasses all neural encoder-decoder and decoder-only baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoKRAQEN clearly outperforms all neural baselines on the most challenging compositional dataset Tomotwin-100 (0.335 vs. CryoDRGN 0.316, DRGN-AI-fixed 0.202, Opus-DSD 0.237), where mode collapse is most severe. On IgG-1D, it beats CryoDRGN (0.375 vs. 0.351) and DRGN-AI-fixed (0.375 vs. 0.364) but trails the classical RECOVAR method (0.375 vs. 0.386) by a narrow margin. On IgG-RL and Spike-MD, RECOVAR again leads (0.363 and 0.362 respectively), while CryoKRAQEN (0.352 and 0.328) remains ahead of all neural methods. On Ribosembly, RECOVAR achieves the highest score (0.429) with CryoKRAQEN close behind (0.348 vs. 0.418), and CryoDRGN (0.412) is competitive. Overall, CryoKRAQEN's kernel-guided annealing provides the most robust performance under strong compositional heterogeneity, while classical covariance-based RECOVAR retains an edge on simpler continuous conformational tasks where linear assumptions are less limiting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoKRAQEN departs from the dominant encoder-decoder paradigm in cryo-EM heterogeneous reconstruction (CryoDRGN, Opus-DSD) by adopting a decoder-only triplane implicit representation in Fourier space, drawing on triplane decomposition from 3D generative modeling (Chan et al., Shue et al.) and vector quantization from discrete representation learning (Van Den Oord et al.). Its key conceptual departure is replacing the Gaussian latent prior or randomly initialized fixed codebook with a kernel-guided annealing mechanism that progressively sharpens soft assignments via an Epanechnikov kernel, combined with a bidirectional triplet loss to regularize codebook geometry. This positions the work at the intersection of implicit neural representations, quantized embedding learning, and classical kernel methods, while explicitly targeting the mode-collapse and initialization-sensitivity problems that plague both encoder-based and fixed-codebook decoder-only approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical decoder-only alternative for heterogeneous cryo-EM reconstruction that avoids encoder training overhead and Gaussian prior constraints, with the Epanechnikov kernel providing a principled noise-robustness argument under low SNR. However, the performance gains are modest and dataset-dependent: RECOVAR still outperforms CryoKRAQEN on IgG-1D and Spike-MD, and the discrete quantization scheme may limit modeling of subtle continuous motions. Open questions include whether the annealing schedule generalizes to larger-scale or more complex systems, whether continuous latent flows could complement the discrete codebook, and whether the decoder-only architecture's reduced representational capacity becomes a bottleneck for highly heterogeneous or multi-domain complexes.
