## Problem

In sparse-view localization settings where database images have little or no mutual overlap (star-topology), structure-based methods fail because triangulation of 3D points is unreliable or impossible, while existing structureless methods (pose regression, motion averaging, transitive matching) lack sufficient accuracy. This paper targets the underexplored regime of structureless localization with K≤10 low-overlap database images and no prebuilt 3D map, where current approaches either cannot establish 2D-3D correspondences or produce poses with large errors.

## Input / Output

ON3R consumes a query image with known intrinsics, K posed database images (K=2–10) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, sparse 2D-2D matches between query and database keypoints (with a binary match mask indicating presence/absence per keypoint-image pair), and a monocular depth prior from MoGe-2. No visual features or descriptors are used inside the network—only normalized 2D keypoint coordinates in [0,1]. The output is a 6-DoF world-to-camera pose [R₀|t₀] for the query and 3D world-coordinate points for every matched query keypoint, which together serve as the 2D-3D correspondences for pose estimation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a compact 7-layer MLP trained from scratch for each query-database tuple, which lifts 2D query keypoints (encoded with multi-frequency sinusoidal positional encoding) into 3D world coordinates. Training is supervised by two residual terms: reprojection residuals (projecting regressed 3D points into each database image and comparing to matched 2D keypoints) and a monocular depth prior from MoGe-2, aligned via a learned global scale factor γ that is re-estimated each iteration. Both residuals are fed through a modified Cauchy loss (logarithmic term scaled by s rather than s²) with robust scaling of s during training to downweight outliers. After the network converges (low residuals, no improvement, or max epochs), the resulting 2D-3D correspondences are passed to P3P-RANSAC for an initial pose estimate, which is then refined together with the 3D points via lightweight bundle adjustment (database poses fixed, Ceres with Sparse Schur Complement). The key insight is that the MLP's spatial smoothness allows it to infer 3D structure jointly across database images even when they share no covisible points, effectively replacing triangulation with learned geometric consistency.

## Results

On MegaDepth with K=2 database images, ON3R achieves a median rotation error of 0.37° and translation error of 0.45 m, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Transitive Matching at 1.34°/1.89 m) and all pose-regression baselines (Reloc3r: 0.90°/2.83 m; VGGT: 1.11°/3.67 m). At the loosest recall threshold ε₄=(10°, 5 m), ON3R reaches 85.0% recall for K=2, 94.0% for K=3, and 97.7% for K=4, compared to 62.7–64.7% for the strongest competing baselines. On Cambridge Landmarks, ON3R similarly leads across all tuple lengths, and on the 99%-sparsified Aachen Day-Night benchmark it achieves 81.6% and 58.6% higher ε₄ recall than HLOC at day and night, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ON3R decisively outperforms all five baselines on MegaDepth and Cambridge Landmarks across every tuple length (K=2,3,4). Against Transitive Matching (the closest competitor), ON3R reduces median translation error by 3–4× at K=2 (0.45 m vs. 1.89 m on MegaDepth; 0.51 m vs. 1.02 m on Cambridge) and gains 22–25 percentage points in ε₄ recall. Pose-regression baselines Reloc3r and VGGT are competitive in rotation error but lag significantly in translation (2.8–3.7 m vs. 0.45 m for ON3R at K=2 on MegaDepth), and the SCR method ACE is essentially non-functional in this sparse regime (27–29° rotation error). Against the structure-based HLOC on sparsified Cambridge Landmarks (K=5,10), ON3R wins 35 of 40 metrics, with HLOC competitive only on OldHospital. On the 99%-sparsified Aachen Day-Night benchmark, ON3R achieves 81.6% and 58.6% higher ε₄ recall than HLOC at day and night respectively, confirming its advantage when database covisibility is near zero.

## Relation to Prior Work

ON3R repurposes the core idea of Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) — predicting 3D coordinates from 2D keypoints supervised by reprojection residuals — but transplants it into a fundamentally different regime: instead of training a scene-specific network over hundreds of images, it trains a compact 7-layer MLP from scratch per query-database tuple in under a second. This departs from SCR's reliance on visual similarity for smoothness (ON3R is smooth in spatial coordinates) and from structure-based pipelines like HLOC that require a prebuilt 3D map. The method also integrates a monocular depth prior (MoGe-2) to resolve depth ambiguity in star-topology data, a component absent from both SCR and transitive-matching approaches. The downstream P3P-RANSAC + bundle adjustment pipeline is standard in structure-based localization but here operates on regressed rather than triangulated 3D points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ON3R addresses a practically important but underexplored gap: localizing against databases with very few, low-overlap images (K ≤ 10) where triangulation-based methods break down, as in surveillance networks or storage-constrained autonomous platforms. The paper demonstrates clear advantages over HLOC in 99%-sparsified settings, but acknowledges sensitivity to retrieval outliers and the computational cost of online training (~0.5 s per query). Open questions include whether pretrained encoders can replace the from-scratch MLP to reduce latency, how to shape the loss landscape to avoid local minima, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested outdoor benchmarks to truly dynamic or indoor scenes.
