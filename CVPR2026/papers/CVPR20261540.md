## Problem

Most prompt-based continual learning methods assign a fixed, disjoint set of prompts to each task, completely isolating knowledge across tasks. This rigid parameter isolation limits positive transfer between related tasks, struggles with heterogeneous task sequences of varying complexity, and results in inefficient use of model capacity. The paper asks whether a shared, dynamically routed prompt pool can outperform static per-task prompt allocation in both accuracy and parameter efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261540/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images encoded by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone into token sequences of shape L×d, and outputs a class label over the union of all previously seen classes without access to a task identifier at inference. Prompts are injected at the attention level: the dynamically composed prompt is split into key and value components and prepended to the K and V matrices in selected layers (default: layers 1–4), so the output is a standard softmax classification score vector over all accumulated classes.

## Method

Hash constructs a global pool of K prompts (each of length L_p) and assigns each task a lightweight router that computes input-dependent relevance scores and sparsely activates a top-k subset via softmax-weighted composition. The resulting instance-specific prompt is split into key and value halves and prepended to the attention matrices in early ViT layers, unifying specialization and generalization in a single mechanism. To counteract catastrophic forgetting from prompt overuse, a history-aware modulator tracks cumulative activation counts per prompt and applies two complementary controls: (i) a routing penalty that subtracts a fixed deduction from the scores of the most frequently activated experts, and (ii) a gradient scaling factor that attenuates update magnitudes for those same experts. From a regularization perspective, the routing penalty corresponds to a sparse entropic-regularized routing objective, while gradient modulation approximates a stability-aware L2 penalty whose strength grows with expert usage, jointly balancing plasticity and stability across the continual sequence.

## Results

On Split CIFAR-100, Hash achieves 95.02% FAA and 95.97% CAA with a forgetting measure of 1.67%, outperforming the strongest non-shared baseline NoRGa (94.48% FAA) by 0.54% and the strongest shared baseline CPG (90.63% FAA) by 4.39%. On Split ImageNet-R, Hash reaches 79.02% FAA and 82.96% CAA with 2.63% FM, surpassing NoRGa by 3.62% in FAA and achieving the lowest forgetting among all shared-prompt methods. On Split CUB-200 and 5-Datasets, Hash attains 91.34% and 95.12% FAA respectively, and on CORe50 (domain-incremental) it reaches 87.94% A_N, exceeding PINA by 1.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hash outperforms all 11 baselines on FAA across both primary benchmarks. Against the strongest non-shared method NoRGa, Hash gains +0.54% FAA on CIFAR-100 and +3.62% on ImageNet-R, while also reducing FM from 4.59% to 2.63% on ImageNet-R. Among shared-prompt methods, the gap is larger: Hash surpasses CPG by +4.39% FAA on CIFAR-100 and +0.39% on ImageNet-R, and outperforms CODA-Prompt by +8.08% and +8.99% respectively. On the forgetting metric, Hash achieves the best FM (2.63%) on ImageNet-R, though NoRGa (1.44%) and HiDe-Prompt (1.50%) remain slightly lower on CIFAR-100, indicating a small accuracy-forgetting trade-off. On domain-incremental CORe50, Hash (87.94% A_N) beats PINA (86.74%) by 1.2% and CODA-P-S (85.41%) by 2.53%, confirming generality beyond class-incremental settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HiDe-Prompt's hierarchical key-prompt matching for task identification, but departs fundamentally from the static per-task prompt allocation paradigm that dominates prompt-based continual learning (DualPrompt, CPrompt, HiDe-Prompt, NoRGa). It repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism—previously applied to prompt selection in L2P and CODA-Prompt—into a shared global prompt pool where each prompt acts as a feature-pattern expert, combined with a novel history-aware modulator that penalizes overused prompts in both routing scores and gradient magnitudes. The key conceptual departure is arguing that parameter isolation across tasks, long assumed necessary to prevent interference, is actually suboptimal for parameter efficiency and generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a shared prompt pool with dynamic sparse routing consistently outperforms static allocation across multiple benchmarks and long-sequence settings, the paper challenges a foundational design assumption in prompt-based CL and opens a design space where prompts are reusable, composable resources rather than task-bound artifacts. The history-aware modulator addresses a practical failure mode of shared approaches (prompt overuse causing forgetting) with a simple, architecture-agnostic mechanism. Limitations include evaluation only on ViT-B/16, and the authors acknowledge the need to validate the approach on larger backbones and under extremely low task similarity, where the benefits of sharing may diminish.
