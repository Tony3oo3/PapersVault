## Problem

Existing event-based dynamic scene reconstruction methods rely on auxiliary sensors (RGB cameras or LiDAR) to provide global context, introducing cumbersome hardware calibration. The paper tackles the fundamental question of whether a single monocular event camera—despite its sparse, noisy, and intensity-free output—can alone support high-fidelity 4D reconstruction of fast dynamic scenes, a setting where conventional frame cameras fail due to motion blur and low temporal resolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260438/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous monocular event stream (microsecond-level latency, no absolute intensity) together with sparse camera poses from a motion capture system or SfM pipeline, which are sampled at a much lower rate than the events. Because of this frequency mismatch, poses are interpolated via cubic B-spline to enable supervision at arbitrary timestamps. The output is a 4D deformable Gaussian Splatting representation (canonical Gaussians plus a deformation MLP predicting per-Gaussian position, rotation, and scale offsets over time) that supports real-time novel view synthesis of the dynamic scene.

## Method

FastEventDGS builds on Deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting with a deformation MLP, but introduces three key mechanisms to handle event-only supervision. First, a continuous B-spline camera trajectory allows the event photometric loss (ESI) and a novel event flow loss—derived from rendered image gradients and decomposed optical flow (camera flow + Gaussian flow)—to supervise brightness changes at arbitrary time intervals. Second, a local patch motion loss enforces that each Gaussian's 2D projection stays consistent with neighboring event edges over short sub-intervals, mitigating overfitting and enforcing coherent object motion. Third, a refinement stage applies VGGT-based depth correction via a SiLog loss and two regularization terms (non-event region constraint and spatial gradient penalty) to suppress noise in textureless regions. Training proceeds in three stages: a vanilla 3GS warm-up, then motion losses, then depth and regularization refinement.

## Results

On the synthetic BlenderDynamicEvent dataset, FastEventDGS achieves PSNR of 24.27, 21.25, 23.24, and 22.89 dB across the four scenes (Butterfly, Duck, Alarm, Ball), with corresponding SSIM values of 0.9020, 0.8914, 0.8986, and 0.8763. These results substantially outperform the strongest baseline E2vidDGS by 1.7–8.1 dB in PSNR and 0.10–0.21 in SSIM across scenes. On the real-world Gen4Dynamic dataset, qualitative evaluation confirms superior reconstruction of fast-moving objects compared to Frame-DGS, which places dynamic objects at incorrect locations due to its 10 fps RGB input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastEventDGS outperforms all three quantitative baselines on every scene of the synthetic dataset. Against Event3GS (vanilla 3DGS without a learned motion field), the gains are largest on Butterfly (+9.8 dB PSNR) and Duck (+5.0 dB), confirming that an explicit deformation network is essential for dynamic content. EvDNeRF, a NeRF-based event method, fails catastrophically on the fast-motion scenes (8.18 dB on Duck) and cannot produce results on Alarm at all, while FastEventDGS achieves 21.25 and 23.24 dB respectively. The strongest baseline, E2vidDGS (a two-stage E2VID-to-DGS pipeline), is closest on Duck (19.54 vs. 21.25 dB) but still trails by 1.7 dB; the gap widens to 8.1 dB on Butterfly and 6.6 dB on Alarm, where E2vidDGS's limited generalization causes unnatural colors and structural degradation. On the real-world Gen4Dynamic dataset, Frame-DGS (which uses motion-blurred 10 fps RGB frames) preserves static-region detail better but places the fast-dropping object at incorrect positions, whereas FastEventDGS leverages the event camera's microsecond-level temporal resolution to reconstruct the 0.25 s drop motion with superior temporal consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deformable 3D Gaussian Splatting framework (Frame-DGS) and the explicit motion guidance paradigm of MotionGS to the event-camera domain, replacing RGB photometric supervision with event-based brightness-change and flow losses. It departs from prior event-based reconstruction methods (E-NeRF, Event-3DGS, E-4DGS, DeGS) that all require an auxiliary RGB or depth sensor by demonstrating that a single monocular event stream suffices for dynamic 4D reconstruction. The continuous B-spline trajectory representation and the integration of VGGT for depth correction are novel architectural choices that address the temporal mismatch between sparse poses and dense asynchronous events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for a synchronized RGB camera, this work substantially simplifies the hardware pipeline for high-speed dynamic scene capture, making event-based 4D reconstruction more accessible for AR/VR content creation and robotics. The two new datasets (BlenderDynamicEvent, Gen4Dynamic) fill a notable gap in the literature for fast dynamic event data. The primary open question is pose estimation: the method currently requires known camera poses from motion capture or SfM, and developing a pose-agnostic variant would be a significant step toward practical deployment. Additionally, whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object dynamic scenes or longer sequences remains to be tested.
