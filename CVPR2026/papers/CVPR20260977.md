## Problem

Trajectory prediction for autonomous driving requires simultaneously modeling long-range inter-agent dependencies, capturing multimodal future uncertainty, and meeting real-time latency constraints. Attention-based architectures achieve high accuracy but incur quadratic complexity in the number of agents, while recurrent models struggle with long-range dependencies and fine-grained local dynamics. A key unexplored gap is that standard Fourier decompositions lack ordered frequency semantics, making it difficult for sequential models to exploit spectral structure, and conventional temporal-spectral fusion suffers from scale mismatches that destabilize optimization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a historical trajectory sequence X ∈ R^{T×d} (2D/3D position and velocity per time step), with T=50 on Argoverse 2 (5 s history → 6 s prediction) and T=20 on Argoverse 1 (2 s → 3 s). It produces K candidate future trajectories via learnable queries, combined through a weighted fusion head that expresses motion uncertainty. Training uses a unified loss combining temporal L1 error with a frequency-domain L1 term (λ-weighted), enforcing consistency in both domains simultaneously.

## Method

FoSS employs a dual-branch architecture. The frequency-domain branch (FD-Mamba) applies a DFT to decompose trajectories into amplitude (global trends) and phase (local dynamics) spectra, then uses HelixSort—a spiral reordering from the DC component outward by ascending spectral radius—to impose a coarse-to-fine frequency ordering that enables selective SSMs to process spectral information sequentially without alternating between global and local reasoning. Two parallel SSM submodules refine the ordered spectra: Coarse2Fine-SSM models spatial interactions via depthwise convolution and selective SSM on the reordered amplitude/phase, while SpecEvolve-SSM captures inter-channel correlations through global average pooling and channel-wise spectral evolution. The time-domain branch (TD-Mamba) applies an input-dependent selective SSM where all transition matrices (A, B, C, D) are dynamically generated by lightweight MLPs from the current observation and its Conv1D features, reconstructing self-attention behavior in linear time. A cross-attention layer fuses the two branches, and a learnable query decoder with weighted candidate fusion produces the final multimodal prediction. The entire framework operates at O(N) sequence complexity with only 4.18M parameters.

## Results

On Argoverse 2 (6-second prediction), FoSS achieves b-minFDE₆ of 1.69, minADE₆ of 0.61, minFDE₆ of 1.07, and MR₆ of 0.11, setting new state-of-the-art on all four metrics. The model uses only 4.18M parameters (the fewest among all compared methods) and 22.1G FLOPs, with an inference latency of 64 ms on an RTX 3090. On Argoverse 1 (3-second prediction), FoSS records minADE₁ of 1.67 and minFDE₁ of 2.05, with MR₁ of 0.11 matching the best existing results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Argoverse 2, FoSS outperforms all nine baselines on b-minFDE, minADE, and minFDE simultaneously. The largest margins are over DenseTNT (b-minFDE 1.69 vs 1.98, a 14.6% gain) and SceneTransformer (minADE 0.61 vs 0.80, an 18.7% gain). Against the closest competitors, FoSS improves b-minFDE by 2.9% over Wayformer (1.69 vs 1.74) and minFDE by 7.8% over Wayformer (1.07 vs 1.16). On MR, FoSS (0.11) ties PAGA and QCNet but trails GOHOME (0.08), which uses a graph-oriented heatmap output. On Argoverse 1, FoSS's minADE₁ (1.67) is slightly behind DeMo (1.65) and its minFDE₁ (2.05) is marginally behind Wayformer (2.02), while MR₁ (0.11) ties PAGA and QCNet. In terms of efficiency, FoSS uses 4.18M parameters—roughly 46% fewer than QCNet (7.71M) and 73% fewer than SceneTransformer (15.30M)—and runs 18–22% faster than HiVT, SceneTransformer, QCNet, and MultiPath++ in inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoSS sits at the intersection of two research threads: Fourier-based trajectory prediction (which decomposes motion into global and local frequency components) and selective state-space models (Mamba) that replace quadratic self-attention with linear-time recurrence. The paper's key architectural departure is the HelixSort progressive reordering, which imposes a coarse-to-fine spectral ordering so that SSMs can process frequency components sequentially rather than in the interleaved order produced by raw DFT. The dual-branch design (TD-Mamba for temporal dynamics, FD-Mamba for spectral structure) extends the authors' prior Trajectory Mamba work by adding a principled frequency-domain branch, while the cross-attention fusion and learnable-query decoder follow established multimodal prediction patterns from QCNet and SceneTransformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art accuracy with the smallest parameter count (4.18M) and lowest inference latency (64 ms) among compared methods, making it a strong candidate for edge-deployed autonomous driving systems. The plug-and-play FD-Mamba branch, which can be grafted onto Transformer or LSTM backbones without structural changes, offers a modular upgrade path for existing pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges degradation in rapid lane-change scenarios where high-frequency lateral maneuvers are underrepresented in the spectral decomposition, and future work on abrupt low-to-high frequency transitions remains open. The efficiency gains (40% fewer parameters, 22.5% fewer FLOPs) are meaningful but the accuracy margins over the strongest baselines (e.g., DeMo on Argoverse 1) are modest, suggesting the approach's value is primarily in the accuracy-efficiency trade-off rather than raw predictive power.
