## Problem

Existing cooperative perception datasets are either simulation-based, limited to ground-level V2V/V2I collaboration, or (in the case of CoPeD) real-world but lacking 3D bounding box annotations. The Vehicle-to-UAV (V2U) paradigm, which could address large-scale occlusions and long-range perception unattainable by ground-only agents, has no large-scale real-world multi-modal dataset with object-level annotations to support research on cross-view cooperative detection and tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261662/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 56.2K synchronized LiDAR frames (10 Hz) from a ground vehicle (OS-128, RS-128, M1-Plus) and a UAV (OS-128) flying at 50 m altitude, plus 56.2K multi-view RGB images (1920×1080). Outputs are 702.8K manually annotated 3D bounding boxes across four classes (Car, Cyclist, Pedestrian, Truck), each with center (x,y,z), dimensions (l,w,h), Euler angles (roll,pitch,yaw), a persistent object ID, and velocity. Annotations are stored independently in each LiDAR's local coordinate system (X-forward, Y-left, Z-up), with a separate NED world frame for cross-agent alignment; cooperative evaluation uses a unified ego-LiDAR frame with ground truth as the union of vehicle-side and UAV-side boxes (Eq. 1).

## Method

V2U4Real is constructed by simultaneously driving a ground vehicle and a DJI M300 RTK UAV (within 100 m horizontal separation) across urban, rural, and campus routes, with all sensors time-synchronized at 10 Hz. Spatial alignment is achieved through checkerboard camera calibration, 5-point 2D-3D reprojection for LiDAR-camera extrinsics, and GICP-based point cloud registration between the two platforms initialized by RTK GPS. Annotation uses SusTechPoint with two annotator groups (initial labeling + refinement), followed by IoU-based cross-sensor ID reconciliation and re-labeling of sparse instances to ensure consistency. The paper establishes three benchmark suites: single-agent 3D detection (PointPillars, SECOND, CenterPoint, PV-RCNN), cooperative 3D detection under sync/async settings with early, late, and intermediate fusion (AttFuse, Where2Comm, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, CoAlign, ERMVP, DSRC), and cooperative 3D tracking via AB3Dmot, demonstrating that V2U cooperation yields up to 10% AP gains at 50–100 m range and substantially improves tracking metrics over single-agent baselines.

## Results

On the V2U4Real cooperative 3D object detection benchmark (Vehicle category), CoAlign achieves the best overall performance at 56.67%/36.61% 3D AP@IoU=0.5/0.7 under the synchronous setting, and 50.81%/33.33% under the asynchronous setting with (0,1000] ms delay. DSRC leads in long-range detection (50–100 m) with 33.26%/20.63% AP@IoU=0.5/0.7 (Sync), substantially outperforming the Vehicle-only baseline (15.54%/6.23%) and UAV-only baseline (19.74%/10.11%). For cooperative 3D tracking, CoAlign attains the highest AMOTA of 22.08% and AMOTP of 43.11%, while DSRC achieves the best MT (73.02%) and lowest ML (13.49%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All cooperative methods substantially outperform the No Fusion baselines: CoAlign (56.67/36.61) and DSRC (54.64/31.77) exceed the Vehicle-only baseline (27.53/12.75) by roughly 29 and 27 AP@IoU=0.5 points respectively under the Sync setting. CoAlign dominates overall and short-range (0–50 m) detection, while DSRC leads specifically in the 50–100 m long-range range (33.26/20.63 vs. CoAlign's 30.20/19.25), attributed to its semantic-guided reconstruction. Under the Async setting with realistic communication delay, CoAlign retains the highest overall AP (50.81/33.33), showing the strongest robustness to temporal misalignment, whereas ERMVP degrades most severely (28.06/14.22). Among intermediate-fusion methods, Where2Comm (53.85/29.71) and AttFuse (50.20/27.33) are competitive but trail CoAlign by 2.8 and 6.5 AP@IoU=0.5 points. For tracking, CoAlign achieves the best AMOTA (22.08%) and AMOTP (43.11%), while DSRC records the highest MT (73.02%) and lowest ML (13.49%), both significantly surpassing the Vehicle-only tracker (AMOTA 11.73%, MT 34.13%, ML 52.38%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cooperative perception dataset lineage (OPV2V, V2V4Real for V2V; DAIR-V2X, V2X-Seq for V2I) by introducing the first real-world, large-scale Vehicle-to-UAV (V2U) dataset with 3D bounding box annotations. It departs from simulation-based UAV datasets (CoP-UAVs, UAV3D, Griffin, AirV2X) that lack realistic 6-DoF pose variations and dynamic traffic interactions, and from the only prior real-world ground-air dataset CoPeD, which provides only semantic segmentation labels and covers low-dynamic scenes. The paper repurposes existing intermediate-fusion cooperative detection algorithms (AttFuse, Where2Comm, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, CoAlign, ERMVP, DSRC) as benchmarks, testing their robustness to the novel spatial and temporal asynchrony introduced by aerial agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

V2U4Real fills a critical gap by providing the first real-world V2U dataset with 700K 3D bounding boxes across diverse traffic scenarios, enabling systematic study of cross-view cooperative perception under realistic 6-DoF UAV motion. The demonstrated long-range advantage (up to 10% AP gain at 50–100 m) and the stark contrast between UAV-only and vehicle-only tracking performance (7.00% vs. 11.73% AMOTA) highlight both the promise and the unresolved challenges of aerial-ground collaboration. Open questions include how to further reduce the communication bandwidth of intermediate fusion without sacrificing the long-range gains, how results generalize to different UAV altitudes and relative geometries, and whether the 6-DoF motion discrepancy can be addressed more effectively than the current GICP-based registration pipeline.
