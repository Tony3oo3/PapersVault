## Problem

Hyperspectral video capture is blocked by three compounding constraints: high spectral resolution demands many narrow bands, narrow bands require longer exposures for sufficient photons, and video demands short exposures to avoid motion blur. Passive snapshot systems divide limited photons across all spectral channels simultaneously and assume static scenes, while existing active-illumination approaches still span multiple frames, leaving reconstructions vulnerable to inter-frame motion artifacts. No prior system achieves motion-robust hyperspectral video at 30 fps in a compact form factor.

## Input / Output

The system ingests a single coded video frame (640×480) from a CEP camera in which 12 narrowband LED illuminations are interleaved across 158 sub-frames with pixel-wise exposure tiling, producing a dense spatio-spectro-temporal mosaic. After demosaicing into 12 LED-specific sub-images (each from a different time interval within the exposure) and bilinear upsampling, the pipeline outputs a 31-channel hyperspectral cube (400–700 nm at 10-nm intervals) per frame at 30 fps. Because the 12 sub-images correspond to distinct sub-frame timestamps, they are spatially misaligned under motion and must be temporally registered before spectral reconstruction.

## Method

Lumosaic jointly encodes spatial, spectral, and temporal information within a single video frame by synchronizing a programmable array of 12 narrowband LEDs (FWHM ~20–30 nm) with a coded-exposure-pixel (CEP) sensor whose pixels are grouped into a 4×4 tile mosaic, each tile receiving a unique binary exposure schedule across 158 sub-frames. The linear forward model Y = Ax + η captures the combined effect of illumination modulation, per-pixel exposure coding, and sensor spectral sensitivity, with no two LEDs active simultaneously to preserve spectral separability. Reconstruction proceeds in three stages: (1) demosaicing and bilinear interpolation of the 12 LED-specific sub-images to full resolution, (2) temporal alignment via the RIFE optical-flow network that warps each sub-image to a central-wavelength reference (lime LED) to correct for inter-sub-frame motion, and (3) a Holistic Attention Network (HAN) trained in a supervised manner (L1 loss, Adam, 50k iterations on simulated forward-model data from CAVE/KAUST/ARAD with 0–15% Gaussian noise) that maps 66×64×12 input patches to 66×64×33 hyperspectral cubes, with two extrapolated UV/NIR channels discarded at inference to yield the final 31-band output.

## Results

In noise-free simulation, Lumosaic with the HAN backbone achieves 44.0 dB PSNR, degrading gracefully to 32.0 dB even at 20% Gaussian noise. Across all noise levels (0–20%), Lumosaic consistently achieves higher SSIM and lower SAM than all baselines while maintaining competitive PSNR. Among reconstruction backbones, MCAN and SRNet offer faster inference (52 ms and 27 ms per frame, respectively) at a small fidelity cost, while SPECAT degrades more noticeably under higher noise. Real-world ColorChecker validation confirms reconstructed reflectance spectra closely match spectroradiometer ground truth.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Lumosaic outperforms all three external baselines across every metric (PSNR, SSIM, MAE, SAM) and all noise levels. Against QDO [23] (a DOE-based snapshot HSI system), Lumosaic achieves higher spatial fidelity and lower spectral angle error, with the gap widening under noise as QDO's static dispersive encoding becomes more ill-conditioned. Against MST++ [7] (a data-driven RGB-to-hyperspectral method), Lumosaic produces sharper spatial details and more accurate spectral reflectance, since MST++ relies on learned priors from RGB rather than direct spectral measurements. Against the optical filtering + SPECAT configuration [42] (a passive compressive HSI baseline), Lumosaic's active illumination avoids the photon loss inherent to absorptive filtering, yielding higher per-channel SNR at short exposures. The paper emphasizes that all four Lumosaic backbone variants (HAN, MCAN, SRNet, SPECAT) beat the three external baselines, attributing the gains to the sensing model rather than network complexity alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lumosaic repurposes coded-exposure-pixel (CEP) cameras—previously explored for HDR, motion deblurring, and compressive video—toward a new application: joint spatio-spectro-temporal encoding for hyperspectral video. It departs from passive snapshot HSI (CASSI, DOEs, MSFAs), which irreversibly entangles spatial and spectral content under motion by dividing photons across bands in a single static exposure, and from active illumination systems (illumination multiplexing, structured light, ChromaFlash), which require multiple frames or suffer from sequential-readout motion artifacts. The key conceptual departure is treating the CEP sensor's pixel-wise temporal modulation as a spatial multiplexing axis: by tiling distinct exposure codes across the sensor in sync with cycling narrowband LEDs, a complete spectral observation is compressed into one video frame without dispersive optics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Lumosaic is the first compact system to demonstrate 30 fps, 31-channel hyperspectral video at VGA resolution with motion robustness, bridging a long-standing gap between snapshot imaging and true hyperspectral videography. Open questions include exploiting inter-frame temporal priors (current reconstruction is frame-by-frame), leveraging both CEP buckets for improved dynamic range, and exploring adaptive or randomized coding designs. The 4.7 s per-frame inference time and single-prototype hardware limit immediate deployment, but the sensing paradigm—active illumination co-designed with in-sensor temporal coding—opens a design space for spectral sensing in robotics, medical imaging, and computational photography that is orthogonal to and potentially composable with existing snapshot HSI hardware.
