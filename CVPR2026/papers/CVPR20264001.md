## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) requires models to adapt online to a sequence of unlabeled, shifting target domains without source data access. Existing methods predominantly follow a backward-alignment paradigm that rigidly constrains the model to static source-domain surrogates, producing noisy and outdated supervision signals that cause error accumulation and catastrophic forgetting under evolving distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264001/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a stream of unlabeled corrupted RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet, 32×32 for CIFAR) in batches of 50, alongside a compact offline-constructed synthetic knowledge base of class exemplars (e.g., ~2 per class via Stable Diffusion 1.5). The model outputs standard class label predictions and adapts incrementally per batch without knowing when domain shifts occur. Crucially, no diffusion model is invoked at test time; the generative model is used only once during a pre-deployment preprocessing step.

## Method

The paper proposes a forward-facilitation paradigm that actively evolves reliable synthetic knowledge to match the current target domain, rather than backwardly constraining the model to static anchors. A multi-level bridging mechanism transforms pre-generated class exemplars at three hierarchical levels: (1) input-level style injection via FFT amplitude spectrum replacement, (2) shallow-feature statistical alignment via AdaIN-style mean/std matching, and (3) deep semantic alignment via supervised contrastive loss. The transformed proxies carry ground-truth class labels, enabling a proxy-based cross-entropy loss (L_PCE) that provides direct, unbiased supervision—eliminating the noisy pseudolabel cycle inherent in self-training. The total objective combines L_PCE, the supervised contrastive loss L_SCL, and a standard teacher-student self-training loss L_ST, all applied to a ViT-B/16 backbone with equal weighting.

## Results

On the primary ImageNet-to-ImageNetC benchmark (severity 5, online), the proposed method achieves a mean classification error of 44.1%, outperforming the best prior method DPCore (47.6%) by 3.5 percentage points and the source model (60.3%) by 16.2 points. On CIFAR-100-to-CIFAR-100C, it reaches 29.8% mean error versus 33.7% for RMT (best prior), and on CIFAR-10-to-CIFAR-10-C it achieves 9.1% versus 11.1% for REM. The method also substantially outperforms diffusion-based approaches (DDA: 57.4%, SDA: 55.8% on ImageNet-C) while requiring far less GPU memory (19.9 GiB vs. 95.8 GiB for SDA) and lower latency (9.0× vs. 1719.2× source).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins against all 10 baselines on every benchmark. On ImageNet-C, it outperforms the strongest non-diffusion baseline DPCore (47.6%) by 3.5 points and OBAO (48.3%) by 4.2 points, while achieving a 13.3-point margin over DDA and 11.7-point margin over SDA, which rely on expensive test-time denoising. On CIFAR-100-C, it beats RMT (33.7%) by 3.9 points and OBAO (33.9%) by 4.1 points, with a particularly large 13.1-point advantage over SDA (42.9%). On CIFAR-10-C, it surpasses REM (11.1%) by 2.0 points and RMT (11.2%) by 2.1 points. Notably, diffusion-based methods (DDA, SDA) perform well on specific noise corruptions (e.g., Gaussian, shot) but degrade severely under broader perturbations like fog and defocus, whereas the proposed method maintains consistent gains across all 15 corruption types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant backward-alignment paradigm in CTTA (exemplified by CoTTA, RMT, OBAO, and DPCore), which constrains the model by aligning evolving target data to static source-domain anchors. It also contrasts with diffusion-based methods (DDA, SDA) that project test inputs back to a synthetic domain at inference time, incurring heavy computational cost. The proposed forward-facilitation paradigm inverts this logic: rather than pulling data toward knowledge, it pushes knowledge (a compact set of Stable Diffusion-generated class exemplars) toward the current data via a multi-level bridging mechanism combining FFT-based amplitude replacement (from FDA), AdaIN-style statistical matching, and supervised contrastive learning. The self-training loss and teacher-student structure follow established CTTA practice (CoTTA, RMT, OBAO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical and conceptually clean resolution to the CTTA supervision dilemma: by pre-generating a small synthetic knowledge base and dynamically restyling it to each incoming batch, it provides reliable semantic supervision without test-time diffusion denoising, achieving a 9× latency advantage over SDA while also improving accuracy (29.8% vs 42.9% on CIFAR100C). The consistent outperformance across all 15 corruption types—where DDA/SDA excel on some but collapse on others—suggests the multi-level bridging is more robust than input-level denoising. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond corruption-based benchmarks to genuine real-world domain shifts (e.g., camera changes, seasonal variation), whether the synthetic knowledge base degrades for fine-grained or long-tail classes, and whether the paradigm extends to dense prediction tasks like segmentation or detection.
