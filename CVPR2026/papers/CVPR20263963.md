## Problem

Existing single-image light editing methods either rely on ill-posed inverse rendering pipelines that are computationally expensive and lack intuitive per-light controls, or use general-purpose diffusion editors that lack an explicit light-centric parameterization and thus cannot produce physically plausible shadows, reflections, and falloff when moving or recoloring a light source. No prior approach provides precise, independent, parametric control over light position, color, and intensity simultaneously from a single RGB image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263963/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (512×512 or 1024×1024) along with three optional control signals: a movement map encoding source and target bounding boxes in separate RGB channels, a global color-temperature frame, and an intensity frame expressed in photographic stops (EV). All inputs are encoded as pseudo video frames by a video VAE and fed sequentially into a diffusion transformer. The output is a single relit image where the light source has been repositioned, recolored, and/or re-intensified with globally consistent shadows, reflections, and ambient falloff.

## Method

LightMover repurposes a 5B-parameter pre-trained image-to-video diffusion transformer in a sequence-to-sequence formulation, extending ObjectMover's token scheme with illumination-specific control tokens for color and intensity. A Multi-Signal Positional Encoding (MSPE) mechanism assigns each token four orthogonal positional subspaces—spatial (W,H), temporal (T), condition-type (C), and frame-role (R)—so the transformer can distinguish conditioning modalities and the output frame. An adaptive token-pruning strategy reduces the average control sequence length by 41%: spatially-aware pruning retains full-resolution tokens only within the movement bounding box (threshold τ=0.2 area ratio), while learnable downsampling ratios compactly encode non-spatial color and intensity frames. Training uses a flow-matching objective over a multi-task mixture of seven categories (light movement, object movement, color change, intensity change, joint changes, removal, insertion) on a 10:1 synthetic-to-real data ratio, where synthetic pairs are generated by a Blender-based physically disentangled rendering pipeline that independently renders ambient and direct-light components for parametric post-processing.

## Results

On the LightMove-A benchmark (real captured data), LightMover achieves PSNR 20.38, DINO 81.27, and CLIP 91.85 for light movement, outperforming the strongest baseline (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image two-step: PSNR 19.59, DINO 72.46, CLIP 89.72) and the structural baseline ObjectMover (PSNR 19.49, DINO 78.12, CLIP 90.48). On the synthetic LightMove-B benchmark, LightMover leads on all three illumination-control tasks: color change (PSNR 24.06, DINO 85.34), intensity change (PSNR 27.12, DINO 83.04), and combined movement+illumination (PSNR 19.97, DINO 79.86). The adaptive token-pruning variant retains nearly identical performance (e.g., PSNR 20.39 vs 20.38 on light movement) while reducing control sequence length by 41%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightMover outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated task. On light movement (Table 1), it surpasses ObjectMover by +0.89 PSNR, +3.15 DINO, and +1.37 CLIP, and beats the best LLM-powered editor (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image two-step) by +0.79 PSNR, +8.81 DINO, and +2.13 CLIP. On illumination attribute control (Table 2), LightMover leads Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image by +1.92 PSNR on color change and +2.03 PSNR on intensity change, with the gap widening on the compositional task (+1.55 PSNR, +3.65 DINO). On general object movement (Table 3), LightMover edges out ObjectMover by +0.46 PSNR and +3.52 DINO, and substantially outperforms MagicFixup (+1.91 PSNR) and AnyDoor (+3.87 PSNR). The adaptive-pruning variant matches or slightly exceeds the full model on PSNR (20.39 vs 20.38) while sacrificing <1 DINO point, confirming that the 41% token reduction does not degrade controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

LightMover directly extends ObjectMover's video-diffusion token formulation for object movement by introducing illumination-specific control tokens (color, intensity) and a multi-signal positional encoding that disambiguates spatial, temporal, condition-type, and frame-role semantics. It also builds on LightLab's diffusion-based light-source control but adds the spatial movement dimension that LightLab lacks, while departing from inverse-rendering pipelines by avoiding explicit geometry/material reconstruction. The adaptive token-pruning mechanism is a novel efficiency contribution not present in either predecessor, and the physically disentangled rendering pipeline for data generation draws on PBR techniques but applies them at a scale and with a multi-task training strategy not seen in prior flash-photography or relighting datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The '2.5D' paradigm—repurposing a video diffusion transformer for single-image illumination reasoning—offers a practical middle ground between fully 2D generative editing and costly 3D inverse rendering, potentially making physically plausible light manipulation accessible without scene reconstruction. The demonstrated mutual reinforcement across positional, color, and intensity tasks suggests that joint multi-attribute training is a generalizable strategy for disentangled control in generative models. Open questions include scalability to outdoor scenes with complex multi-light interactions, real-time inference for interactive use, and whether the approach generalizes to non-photometric lighting effects (e.g., caustics, volumetric scattering). The modest absolute gains over ObjectMover (≈1 dB PSNR) indicate the video prior already captures much of the spatial reasoning, leaving headroom for the illumination-specific components to differentiate.
