## Problem

Existing vision-language models struggle with 3D spatial reasoning from monocular video because prior approaches either depend on external depth sensors (limiting scalability) or rely on pre-constructed 3D maps via SLAM/reconstruction pipelines (introducing brittleness, scale ambiguity, and multi-stage latency). Additionally, no benchmark systematically evaluates whether VLMs can reason about temporal changes in spatial relationships induced by camera motion in static scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260571/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of monocular video frames (resized to 432×432 pixels) and a natural-language instruction as input, producing a free-form text answer for spatial or temporal QA tasks. No depth maps, point clouds, or pre-built 3D geometry are required at inference time; all 3D structure is recovered implicitly from the raw video stream. The system supports both multiple-choice and numerical-answer formats across eight spatial tasks and five temporal tasks.

## Method

VLM-3R uses the pre-trained CUT3R model as a geometry encoder that processes each frame through an image encoder and a recurrent transformer decoder to produce implicit 3D tokens: enriched spatial tokens F′_t encoding scene geometry and camera view tokens z′_t encoding ego-motion, both at metric scale. These 3D tokens are concatenated into Z_3D and fused with CLIP ViT visual tokens H_v via a cross-attention mechanism (Spatial-Visual-View Fusion) where H_v serves as queries and Z_3D provides keys/values, followed by a residual connection and a two-layer projector into the LLM backbone. Training employs LoRA (rank 128, scale 256) on the fusion attention block and projection layers while keeping both the visual encoder and CUT3R spatial encoder frozen, using 200K+ curated spatial-reconstructive QA pairs generated from a spatio-temporal scene graph over ScanNet, ScanNet++, and ARKitScenes. The key design choice is fusing implicit latent tokens rather than explicit point clouds, which avoids sparsity and non-uniformity issues while preserving metric-scale geometric priors.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, VLM-3R (7B) achieves an average score of 60.9, ranking first among all open-source VLMs and surpassing proprietary models Gemini-2.5 Pro (51.5) and GPT-4o (34.0). The largest gains appear on Relative Direction (80.5 vs. 42.4 for the 2D-only baseline) and Room Size (67.1 vs. 12.3). On the proposed VSTI-Bench for temporal reasoning, VLM-3R scores 58.8 average, outperforming the next-best model LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B (44.0) by 14.8 points. On ScanQA/SQA3D, VLM-3R achieves CIDEr 101.9 and EMR 63.4 using only monocular video, matching or exceeding depth-supervised baselines like Video-3D LLM (CIDEr 102.1, EM 58.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLM-3R (7B) outperforms all open-source baselines on VSI-Bench by a wide margin: +3.2 over the strongest finetuned baseline LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (57.7), +13.6 over VG-LLM-4B (47.3), and +20.0 over the 72B LLaVA-NeXT-Video (40.9). It also surpasses proprietary APIs Gemini-2.5 Pro (51.5) and GPT-4o (34.0) by 9.4 and 26.9 points respectively. On VSTI-Bench, VLM-3R leads all models by a substantial 14.8 points over LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B (44.0) and 16.4 over Gemini-2.5 Pro (42.4), with particularly large gaps on Camera Displacement (39.6 vs. 10.5) and Camera-Object Relative Distance (68.6 vs. 50.9). On ScanQA/SQA3D, VLM-3R is competitive with depth-supervised models: it slightly trails Video-3D LLM on CIDEr (101.9 vs. 102.1) but leads on SQA3D EM (60.7 vs. 58.6) and EMR (63.4 vs. unavailable), while using only monocular video input. On OST-Bench, VLM-3R (42.9) outperforms its base LLaVA-Video-7B (39.3) and other spatially tuned models Spatial-MLLM (26.8) and LLaVA-3D (30.1). The one area where VLM-3R trails is general video understanding: Video-MME overall is 59.9 vs. 62.7 for LLaVA-NeXT-Video, though it gains +3.7% on the Spatial Perception sub-category (70.4 vs. 66.7), and mixing 30K general videos recovers most of the gap (62.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

VLM-3R sits at the intersection of geometry-augmented VLMs (Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, SpatialStack) and feed-forward 3D reconstruction (DUSt3R, MASt3R, CUT3R). It departs from prior geometry-augmented VLMs in two key ways: (1) it uses CUT3R's metric-scale reconstruction rather than VGGT's normalized depth, addressing the scale-ambiguity problem that limits accurate distance and size reasoning; and (2) it fuses implicit latent tokens (spatial + view) via cross-attention rather than explicit point clouds or pre-built 3D maps, eliminating the need for depth sensors or offline reconstruction pipelines. The base architecture follows LLaVA-NeXT-Video, with LoRA-based fine-tuning on the fusion block and projector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that monocular video alone (via a feed-forward geometry encoder) can match or approach depth-supervised baselines on spatial QA, VLM-3R removes a major practical barrier to deploying spatially-aware VLMs in real-world settings where RGB-D sensors are unavailable. The VSTI-Bench (138.6K temporal QA pairs) and the 200K+ scalable data pipeline address two critical gaps: the lack of temporal spatial reasoning benchmarks and the insufficient scale of existing spatial training data. Key limitations include the restriction to static indoor scenes, the modest 1.4-point ablation gap for spatial tokens suggesting the geometry signal is not yet fully exploited, and the slight underperformance on Object Size compared to depth-supervised models, pointing to residual monocular reconstruction errors. Open questions include generalization to dynamic/outdoor scenes and whether the CUT3R dependency can be replaced by lighter-weight geometry priors.
