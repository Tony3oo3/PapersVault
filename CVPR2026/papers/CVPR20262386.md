## Problem

Existing visual navigation methods focus on improving policy heads and decision strategies while relying on simplistic feature encoders—typically ImageNet-pretrained CNNs followed by average pooling or shallow temporal models. This impoverished representation blurs fine-grained spatial geometry and temporal motion cues before they reach the policy head, leading to oscillations, stalls, and collisions in real-world deployment. The paper identifies the underexplored challenge of weak spatio-temporal feature encoding as a bottleneck for accurate action prediction and progress estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of T past RGB observation frames (from time T-p to T) and a single goal image, both encoded by a shared CNN into per-frame feature maps of shape (B, 1, A, A). The output is dual-headed: a navigation control action vector in R^d generated by a diffusion-based denoising policy, and a scalar temporal distance estimate in R+ indicating how many steps remain to reach the goal. The temporal distance head enables high-level subgoal selection for long-range navigation, while the action head produces low-level motor commands.

## Method

STRNet proposes a unified spatio-temporal representation framework with two complementary modules. For spatial reasoning, it constructs a dynamic axial graph over each frame's feature map (nodes = image regions, edges = soft contrast-based similarity weights along horizontal/vertical axes), then performs hierarchical graph aggregation with positional encoding, multi-scale directional contrast enhancement, and residual transformation. For temporal reasoning, it applies a hybrid temporal shift module that splits channels into forward, backward, bidirectional, and residual groups and shifts them along the time axis before a lightweight 3D convolution, combined with a multi-resolution difference-aware convolution that pools at K scales, circularly rolls each scale, and masks residuals via cosine-similarity thresholds to highlight salient motion. The fused spatio-temporal tensor drives a diffusion policy head for action generation and a regression head for temporal distance, jointly trained with a weighted combination of masked denoising MSE and distance MSE losses.

## Results

On the indoor 2D-3D-S benchmark, STRNet achieves 100% success rate with zero collisions on both Basic and Adaptation tasks, outperforming NoMaD (70%/72%) and NaviBridger (90%/100%). On the outdoor Citysim benchmark, STRNet reaches 100% success on the Basic task and 92% on the Adaptation task, compared to NoMaD's 78%/36% and NaviBridger's 60%/42%. On GRScenes, STRNet attains the highest SPL of 0.80 and the lowest average collision count (0.51) among all methods. On the long-horizon 2D-3D-S task, STRNet achieves 98% success with only 0.02 average collisions versus NoMaD's 30% success and 1.08 collisions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STRNet consistently outperforms NoMaD across all environments and tasks: on the outdoor Citysim Adaptation task, STRNet achieves 92% success versus NoMaD's 36%, and on the long-horizon 2D-3D-S task, STRNet reaches 98% success with 0.02 collisions versus NoMaD's 30% success with 1.08 collisions. Against NaviBridger, STRNet wins decisively on outdoor tasks (100% vs 60% Basic; 92% vs 42% Adaptation) but ties on the indoor Adaptation task (both 100% success). Compared to ViNT, STRNet matches the 100% indoor Basic success rate but substantially outperforms on outdoor Adaptation (92% vs 66%) and long-horizon tasks (98% vs 68%). Notably, NoMaD-0 (current-frame only) outperforms full NoMaD on several outdoor metrics, which the authors attribute to NoMaD's average-pooling temporal fusion actively degrading representations rather than helping them.

## Relation to Prior Work

STRNet positions itself against the dominant paradigm in visual navigation where research effort concentrates on policy heads (diffusion-based action generation in NoMaD, NaviBridger) or topological memory (ViNT), while the visual encoder is treated as a fixed ImageNet-pretrained CNN with average or self-attention pooling. The paper explicitly argues this is the bottleneck, and its architectural lineage draws from two distinct threads: dynamic axial graph construction from GreedyViG (Munir et al., CVPR 2024) for spatial aggregation, and the Temporal Shift Module (Lin et al., ICCV 2019) for lightweight temporal modeling. By combining these into a unified spatio-temporal fusion backbone that feeds a diffusion policy head (inherited from NoMaD), STRNet reframes the problem as one of representation quality rather than decision strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that impoverished temporal pooling (average pooling, shallow self-attention) is the primary limiter of navigation performance rather than the policy architecture—shifts attention to a previously underexplored component of the pipeline. The ablation showing that removing temporal fusion collapses success from 98% to 38%, while removing spatial aggregation only drops it to 88%, provides concrete evidence that both spatial geometry and temporal continuity are independently critical. Open questions include whether the graph-based spatial module scales to higher-resolution or multi-modal inputs (depth, LiDAR, language), whether the approach generalizes beyond the tested simulation suites to truly open-world deployment, and whether the 70% gap over NoMaD persists against newer or more diverse baselines. The real-world validation on a single robot platform (Diablo) is encouraging but limited in scope.
