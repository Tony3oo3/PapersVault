## Problem

Existing federated learning frameworks for medical imaging are tightly coupled to specific tasks and backbones, requiring pipeline re-engineering when switching between classification, segmentation, or other tasks. More critically, they assume homogeneous imaging modalities across clients, whereas real-world hospitals contribute data from disparate modalities (MRI, CT, PET, pathology, microscopy) that induce fundamentally different frequency statistics and pull the global model toward conflicting minima during aggregation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261837/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts medical images of varying modalities (MRI, CT, PET, SPECT, histopathology, microscopy) at resolutions ranging from 256×256 (fusion) to standard classification/segmentation sizes, optionally paired with natural-language questions for VQA or a second image for fusion. A single optimization pipeline produces task-specific outputs—class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, super-resolved images at ×2/×4/×8 scales, free-text answers, or fused images—without altering the federated training mechanism. For VQA, text is mapped to the same token interface as image features via a CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder and LLaMA-3.2-3B backbone with LoRA adapters.

## Method

OmniFM exploits the observation that low-frequency spectral components are highly consistent across imaging modalities while modality-specific variation concentrates in higher frequencies. Each client computes an FFT-based low-pass spectral embedding, which queries a server-side global knowledge bank (GSKR) to retrieve top-k cross-modality spectral prototypes via cosine similarity. These prototypes are fused into backbone token representations through embedding-wise cross-attention (ECA), and the fused tokens are prepended as prefix prompts alongside client-specific suffix CLS tokens (PSP) before a task-specific head. A Spectral-Proximal Alignment (SPAlign) loss penalizes the L2 distance between the local spectral embedding and the centroid of retrieved prototypes, anchoring local optimization to modality-invariant structure and suppressing drift during aggregation. The local model is factorized into a shared backbone and a task head (following FedALA), so the same federated recipe applies to classification, segmentation, super-resolution, VQA, and fusion without architectural changes.

## Results

On cross-modality classification (MedMNISTv2, ResNet-18, 100 rounds), OmniFM achieves 97.82% accuracy and 0.668 F1 under the hardest heterogeneity scenario (Scenario 1, 100% participation), outperforming the strongest baseline FedPer (92.57% Acc, 0.665 F1) by over 5 points. On federated segmentation (FeTS2022, U-Net, 50 rounds), OmniFM reaches 77.41 average Dice across four heterogeneous client groups versus 73.86 for FedPer. On super-resolution (BreaKHis, ×2/×4/×8), OmniFM attains 35.79 average PSNR and 0.8670 average SSIM, surpassing FedPer (32.99 PSNR, 0.8490 SSIM). On medical VQA (LLaVA-1.5 + LLaMA-3.2-3B), OmniFM scores 79.27 average accuracy under complete modality heterogeneity (Task 2) and 47.30 under mixed-modality full tuning (Task 3), leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniFM outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary classification task, with the largest margins over SCAFFOLD (+78 Acc), GPFL (+45 Acc), and MOON (+0.3 Acc in Scenario 1 but +74 in Scenario 2 where MOON collapses to 22.90%). The closest competitor is Ditto (97.56% vs 97.82% in Scenario 1), followed by FedPer (92.57%) and FedDyn (94.99%). On segmentation, OmniFM leads FedPer by 3.55 Dice points (77.41 vs 73.86) and FedAvg by 33.25 points, with the gap widening in smaller client groups. On VQA Task 2 (8 distinct modalities), OmniFM edges out FedPer by 0.87 points (79.27 vs 78.40) and FedAdam by 1.10 points. On VQA Task 3 (mixed-modality, full tuning), OmniFM beats FedPer by 1.27 points (47.30 vs 46.03) and F3OCUS by 4.52 points (47.30 vs 42.78). Notably, on multi-modal fusion (Table 7), FedAvg marginally surpasses OmniFM on average VIF (0.279 vs 0.269), though OmniFM leads on SSIM (0.712 vs 0.709) and L1 (0.145 vs 0.147), indicating a slight trade-off in that specific task.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniFM builds on the backbone-head factorization from FedALA [55] and extends personalized FL methods (FedProx, Ditto, FedRep, FedPer) that address statistical heterogeneity but assume shared feature spaces and homogeneous modalities. Its key conceptual departure is introducing frequency-domain spectral reasoning—specifically, the observation that low-frequency components are modality-invariant—as the unifying mechanism for cross-modality robustness, combined with retrieval-augmented knowledge banks and prefix-suffix prompting in a federated setting. Unlike prior federated medical imaging work that redesigns the optimization pipeline per task (CNN for classification, U-Net for segmentation), OmniFM positions itself as a single reusable FL protocol that swaps only the task head, drawing on cross-attention and prompt-learning techniques from the broader representation-learning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a genuinely under-addressed deployment problem: real hospital federations mix MRI, CT, PET, pathology, and microscopy, and no prior FL framework handles this modality heterogeneity while remaining task-agnostic. The frequency-domain prior is a clean, interpretable mechanism that could be adopted in other cross-domain FL settings beyond medical imaging. However, open questions remain: the knowledge bank's long-term stability and communication cost at scale, whether the spectral consistency assumption holds for 3D volumetric data or highly pathological scans, and whether the large performance gaps over some baselines (e.g., FedProx in super-resolution) reflect true methodological superiority or baseline implementation sensitivity. The paper also lacks theoretical guarantees on convergence under the spectral-proximal constraint.
