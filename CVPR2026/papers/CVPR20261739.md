## Problem

Current Vision-Language Models struggle with 3D spatial reasoning on Equirectangular Projection (ERP) panoramic images because geometric distortion (pixel stretching near poles, nonlinear mapping of 3D relations to 2D coordinates) and the absence of dense 3D ground-truth supervision in existing benchmarks prevent models from learning faithful 3D scene representations. Prior panoramic VQA datasets lack the combination of full-scene coverage, pixel-aligned physical annotations (depth, segmentation, 3D bounding boxes), and verifiable supervision signals needed for both reliable evaluation and reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single high-resolution ERP panoramic image (stitched from six cubemap views of a TartanAir scene) paired with a text question spanning five categories: camera view source identification, environment identification, distance estimation, relative spatial positioning, and intrinsic attribute comparison. Outputs are structured as <Reasoning>...</Reasoning><Answer>...</Answer> blocks, where answers range from binary yes/no, multiple-choice selections, quantitative distances in meters, multi-axis spatial relations (e.g., 'behind, right, and above'), to volume/shape comparisons derived from 3D bounding box dimensions.

## Method

The paper contributes two coupled components: (1) PanoEnv-QA, a 14.8K-question benchmark built by converting TartanAir multi-view scenes into ERP panoramas and programmatically generating QA pairs from pixel-aligned depth maps, semantic segmentation, and 3D bounding boxes; and (2) a GRPO-based RL post-training framework applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA on the language decoder and a frozen vision encoder. The reward system routes each question to one of five geometry-aware strategies (strict string match for T/F, subject extraction for MCQ, unit-normalized numerical tolerance for distance, axis-wise keyword matching for spatial relations, and exact count matching), weighted 0.9/0.1 toward accuracy over format. A two-stage curriculum first trains on structured tasks (T/F, MCQ) to stabilize format and discrete decisions, then fine-tunes on a balanced mix including open-ended questions to recover generative spatial reasoning without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On the PanoEnv-QA test set (3,040 samples), the proposed GRPO-Balanced model (7B) achieves 52.93% total accuracy, surpassing the best zero-shot baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.34%) by +3.59 points. The most striking gain is on open-ended (OE) questions, where accuracy jumps from 6.39% to 14.83% (+132% relative), while structured tasks (T/F: 68.78%, MCQ: 58.90%) are also improved. The model attains the highest LLM-judge scores (Q-Score 6.24, P-Score 5.95), exceeding even the 32B Qwen2.5-VL variant (Q-Score 5.02, P-Score 4.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed GRPO-Balanced 7B model outperforms all 14 zero-shot baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.34% total, 6.39% OE), it gains +3.59 points overall and +8.44 points on OE. It also decisively beats the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B (42.70% total, 8.36% OE) by +10.23 points in total accuracy and +6.47 points on OE, while achieving higher Q-Score (6.24 vs 5.02) and P-Score (5.95 vs 4.92). Among other families, InternVL2.5-26B (47.07%), Qwen3-VL-8B (47.91%), and DeepSeek-VL2-Base (38.86%) all trail the proposed method by 5–14 points in total accuracy. The LLaVA and Llama3.2-Vision models perform weakest (18–31% total), with OE accuracy near zero, highlighting that current open-source VLMs lack reliable 3D spatial reasoning on ERP panoramas regardless of architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the panoramic VQA line initiated by VQA 360° and OSR-Bench, and builds on 360-R1's use of GRPO for omnidirectional reasoning. Its key departure is grounding all QA pairs in dense, pixel-aligned 3D annotations (depth maps, semantic segmentation, 3D bounding boxes) from TartanAir, enabling a '2D textual reference → 3D spatial query' cross-dimensional reasoning paradigm absent in prior panoramic benchmarks. The RL post-training framework applies GRPO with a routed, ground-truth-guided reward system and a two-stage curriculum (structured → mixed), drawing on curriculum learning principles for LLM fine-tuning rather than introducing a new optimization algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap: even the strongest 32B VLMs achieve only 8.36% on open-ended 3D spatial questions over ERP panoramas, suggesting current training paradigms have not internalized metric 3D reasoning from distorted 2D projections. The finding that a 7B RL-finetuned model outperforms 32B baselines hints that targeted, geometry-grounded supervision can be more effective than raw scale for this task. However, the absolute accuracy remains low (52.93% overall, 14.83% OE), and the synthetic-to-real gap limits immediate applicability to real-world panoramic data. Open questions include whether the RL framework transfers to noisy real-world 360° datasets and whether it extends to temporal reasoning in panoramic video.
