## Problem

Existing autonomous driving datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, Waymo, Cityscapes) predominantly cover structured urban and highway scenes under clear weather, while the few datasets targeting unstructured roads (R2100K, RELLIS-3D, AutoMine) lack comprehensive adverse-weather coverage and multi-task support. No prior dataset simultaneously provides full 360° multimodal sensor data, eight distinct weather/illumination scenarios, and annotations for 3D detection, multi-object tracking, and occupancy prediction in unstructured environments. URScenes fills this gap as the first open-source multi-scenario perception dataset for unstructured road environments, using open-pit mining as the representative case.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides synchronized 360° multimodal data from five LiDARs (one 128-beam Livox at 200 m range plus four 32-beam BPearl units at 50 m), two forward cameras (60° telephoto and 200° fisheye, both at 30 Hz), and four 76–77 GHz ARS408 radars, recorded at 10 Hz across 472 scenes of ~30 s each (≈294 K images, 736 K LiDAR sweeps, 589 K radar frames). Annotations include 3D bounding boxes for eight object categories (mine trucks, wide-body vehicles, excavators, bulldozers, cars, trucks, pedestrians, barriers) at 2 Hz keyframes (28 K+ annotated frames) with interpolation to 119 K sweeps, plus 36-class semantic occupancy grids generated via multi-view reconstruction and voxelization. All data ships in a unified format with automatic conversion tools to nuScenes, KITTI, and Waymo schemas.

## Method

The core contribution is a dataset construction and benchmarking pipeline rather than a new network architecture. Data was collected over two years on two mining-vehicle platforms with PTP grandmaster-clock synchronization; sensor calibration combines OpenCalib (LiDAR–vehicle extrinsics via figure-eight trajectories), Zhang's method (camera intrinsics), and PnP-based QR-code targets (camera–LiDAR extrinsics). Annotation leverages a pre-trained BEVFusion model to generate initial 3D boxes, refined manually at 2 Hz keyframes and propagated to non-keyframes by interpolation; occupancy ground truth is produced through dynamic–static separation, multi-view reconstruction, and localization-based voxelization. For evaluation, the paper introduces category-specific matching thresholds (Dia_c) derived from mean 2D bounding-box diagonals and a composite UDS score that combines mAP with mTP (ATE, ASE, AOE). The empirical study benchmarks 12 detection models, 6 tracking models, and 7 occupancy models across all eight weather subsets, revealing that dusty, foggy, and snowy conditions degrade LiDAR-based detection by 30–50 % relative to clear conditions due to signal attenuation limiting effective range.

## Results

On the URScenes dataset, the best 3D detection model (BEVFusion with LiDAR+camera fusion) achieves 78.0% UDS / 70.2% mAP on the cloudy subset, while the best LiDAR-only model (PointPillars) reaches 79.6% UDS / 71.9% mAP in the same subset. Performance degrades sharply in harsh conditions: in the dusty subset, even the best fusion model drops to 44.3% UDS / 16.7% mAP. For 3D occupancy prediction, FB-Occ (camera-only) leads with 30.94% mIoU, and for multi-object tracking, ADA-Track (end-to-end) achieves the highest MOTA of 35.2% while MCTrack (two-stage) attains the best AMOTP of 1.37 m.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LiDAR-camera fusion detectors, BEVFusion (78.0% UDS) outperforms FUTR3D (75.7%) and TransFusion (72.7%) on the cloudy subset, with the gap widening in harsh conditions (e.g., dusty: 44.3% vs. 40.1% vs. 42.5%). Among LiDAR-only models, PointPillars (79.6% UDS) and CenterPoint (78.7%) surpass BEVFusion-L (75.8%) and FUTR3D-L (62.9%), suggesting that in unstructured mining scenes, simple LiDAR backbones can rival or exceed fusion approaches when camera input is degraded. Camera-only models (BEVDepth 67.4%, BEVDet4D 63.4%, BEVFormer 60.8%) trail all LiDAR-based methods by 10–20 UDS points, confirming the critical role of depth sensing in unstructured environments. For occupancy prediction, FB-Occ (30.94% mIoU) leads all methods, outperforming the next-best SparseOcc (26.83%) by over 4 points, while multi-modal Co-Occ (25.41%) and M-CoNet (18.34%) show that fusion does not guarantee superiority over well-designed camera-only pipelines. In tracking, end-to-end ADA-Track (35.2% MOTA) edges out two-stage MCTrack (34.6%), but MCTrack achieves the best AMOTP (1.37 m) and AMOTA (34.7%), indicating superior localization and identity consistency; PF-Track (20.0% MOTA) and Poly-MOT (20.9%) lag significantly behind all other trackers.

## Relation to Prior Work

URScenes carves new ground at the intersection of two underexplored axes in autonomous driving datasets: unstructured road environments and comprehensive adverse-weather coverage. Prior structured-road benchmarks (nuScenes, KITTI, Waymo) and weather-focused datasets (A*3D, ONCE) do not extend to mining or agricultural terrain, while existing unstructured-road datasets (R2100K, RELLIS-3D, AutoMine) either lack multi-modal sensor suites, omit extreme weather subsets, or support only a single perception task. URScenes departs from this lineage by simultaneously providing 360° LiDAR-camera-radar data across eight weather/illumination conditions and supporting three tasks (3D detection, tracking, occupancy) under a unified annotation schema that converts directly to nuScenes/KITTI/Waymo formats, thereby lowering the barrier for transferring existing perception models to unstructured domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a practical bottleneck: as autonomous systems expand into open-pit mining, forestry, and large-scale agriculture, the absence of a multi-task, multi-weather benchmark has hindered robustness research. The empirical baselines reveal that even the strongest fusion models (BEVFusion) drop from ~78% UDS in cloudy conditions to ~44% in dusty conditions, and the large UDS–mAP gap in harsh subsets quantifies how suspended particles specifically cripple long-range LiDAR detection while preserving short-range accuracy. Limitations include the moderate scene count (472), restriction to two mining vehicle platforms, and the absence of 4D radar or textual scene descriptions—gaps the authors flag for future work. An open question is how well models pretrained on structured-road datasets transfer to URScenes without retraining, which the current experiments do not isolate.
