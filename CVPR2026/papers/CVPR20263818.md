## Problem

Existing DPO-based hallucination mitigation methods for MLLMs allocate training emphasis based on the model's self-assessed visual sensitivity, which introduces a self-referential bias: the model reinforces already well-learned visual cues while neglecting hard-to-perceive but critical details that represent its true cognitive deficiencies. This limits deeper alignment because the tokens most in need of correction receive insufficient optimization pressure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263818/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt as input and produces a text response, operating within the standard MLLM generation pipeline. During training, a blurred version of the input image (generated by adding diffusion noise at a fixed step) is additionally consumed to compute per-token epistemic uncertainty, enabling the model to contrast its visual grounding against a language-prior-dominated prediction. Training uses preference pairs (preferred and dispreferred responses) from RLHF-V or RLAIF-V datasets, with LoRA fine-tuning applied to the language model backbone.

## Method

UE-DPO quantifies token-level epistemic uncertainty by measuring the logit gap between the model's prediction given the original image and its prediction given a diffusion-noised (blurred) image; a large gap indicates the token is not adequately grounded in visual evidence. An asymmetric exploration intensity is then applied: in preferred samples, visually insensitive tokens with high uncertainty receive amplified learning pressure (exploratory incentive), while in dispreferred samples, visually sensitive tokens with high uncertainty receive reduced penalty (protective alleviation) to prevent forgetting established visual knowledge. This intensity is integrated into the DPO loss as a token-wise exponent on the policy probability (with stop-gradient), which theoretically corresponds to introducing a token-wise entropy regularization into the reverse-KL regularized RL objective and reformulating a generalized exploratory advantage function that allows the policy to break free from the reference model's visually-deficient prior.

## Results

UE-DPO achieves the lowest CHAIRs and CHAIRi scores across all three backbones (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, LLaVA-v1.5-13B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B), reducing object-level hallucination substantially (e.g., CHAIRs from 55.67 to 13.72 on LLaVA-v1.5-7B with RLHF-V data). On MMHal-Bench, UE-DPO attains the highest informativeness score (2.82–3.05) and lowest hallucination rate (0.37–0.48) among all compared methods. On AMBER-g, it achieves the best CHAIR (2.2–3.0), hallucination rate (12.5–17.4), and cognition alignment (0.6–1.0) scores, while on AMBER-d it leads in F1 (85.7–89.5) though with a slight accuracy trade-off on smaller datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest same-data baselines TPO and V-DPO (both 5.7k RLHF-V), UE-DPO on LLaVA-v1.5-7B improves MMHal-Bench Score from 2.47/2.16 to 2.82 and reduces HalRate from 0.51/0.56 to 0.48, while also cutting AMBER-g CHAIR from 5.6 (V-DPO) to 2.9 and AMBER-g HalRate from 27.3 to 17.4. On the 13B model, UE-DPO edges out TPO on MMHal-Bench Score (2.76 vs 2.72) and AMBER-d F1 (88.2 vs 88.0) but trails slightly on AMBER-d Accuracy (83.5 vs 83.9). Compared to the larger-data RLAIF-V baseline (16k), UE-DPO on the same RLAIF-V data achieves lower CHAIRs (11.62 vs 16.0) and better AMBER-g metrics (CHAIR 2.5 vs 3.0, HalRate 12.5 vs 16.2, Cog 0.9 vs 1.0), though RLAIF-V retains a marginal edge on CHAIRi (3.70 vs 5.16) and MMHal-Bench Score (3.00 vs 2.95). On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, UE-DPO nearly halves CHAIRs over vanilla DPO (16.7 vs 30.6) and improves MMHal-Bench Score from 2.53 to 3.05, while sacrificing 1.1 points on AMBER-d Accuracy (85.1 vs 86.2) in exchange for a 0.6-point F1 gain. Overall, UE-DPO consistently wins on object-level hallucination (CHAIRs/CHAIRi) and AMBER-g metrics across all backbones, with the only recurring trade-off being a small drop in AMBER-d Accuracy attributable to the model becoming more conservative in yes/no judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO framework for MLLM hallucination mitigation by departing from the prevailing credit-assignment strategy that weights tokens by self-assessed visual sensitivity (as in TPO and V-DPO). Instead, it introduces an epistemic uncertainty signal—measured via a diffusion-noise-blurred image contrast—to identify tokens where the model fails to ground predictions in visual evidence, and uses this to modulate exploration intensity asymmetrically across preferred and dispreferred branches. Theoretically, the method is grounded in the reverse-KL regularized RL interpretation of DPO, showing that the exploration intensity factor is equivalent to a token-wise entropy regularization that reformulates the advantage function into a 'generalized exploratory advantage.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete self-referential bias in sensitivity-weighted DPO—reinforcing what the model already perceives well while neglecting its visual blind spots—which is a plausible and well-motivated limitation. The practical implication is that practitioners applying DPO-based hallucination mitigation to MLLMs can adopt the uncertainty-aware weighting with modest hyperparameter tuning (α, τ) and LoRA training. Open questions include whether the diffusion-noise blurring proxy for epistemic uncertainty generalizes to video or multi-image inputs, whether the AMBER-d accuracy trade-off (model becoming overly cautious) persists with larger training data, and whether the approach transfers to non-DPO preference frameworks such as IPO or KTO.
