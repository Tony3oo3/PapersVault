## Problem

Existing automatic movie trailer generation methods rely on either a selection-then-ranking paradigm or autoregressive prediction, both of which decouple or strictly sequence shot decisions and lack any self-correction mechanism. This causes inevitable error propagation that limits trailer quality, fundamentally differing from how human editors iteratively refine shot connections across positions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264028/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a movie segmented into I shots (via TransNet-v2) with each shot encoded as a 1024-d ImageBind feature vector, plus a target trailer length J. It outputs a trailer index vector z of length J mapping each trailer position to a distinct movie shot. Post-processing aligns shot durations to music segments (via Ruptures), selects narrations with DeepSeek-V3, generates shot descriptions with MiniCPM-V2.6, and aligns text to shots via CLIP embeddings and dynamic programming.

## Method

SSMP trains a 4-layer Transformer encoder via conditional masked prediction: the movie shot sequence is concatenated with a partially masked trailer sequence, and the model reconstructs masked trailer shots by predicting cosine-similarity scores over all movie shots followed by softmax. A self-paced mask ratio scheduler (Eq. 6) adaptively increases the masking difficulty using a momentum-based update on training accuracy, with a monotonicity constraint preventing the ratio from decreasing. At generation time, the model iteratively predicts all masked positions simultaneously, accumulates per-position confidence via a Bernoulli sampling mechanism, fills high-confidence positions, and re-masks low-confidence ones for the next iteration—forming a progressive self-correction loop that mimics human editing. The cross-entropy loss over the conditional probability matrix (rather than MSE on embeddings) provides stronger discriminative margins between shots.

## Results

On Test-8, SSMP achieves F1 of 0.1618 and Pairwise Agreement Accuracy (AA) of 0.68, surpassing the best selection-then-ranking method MMSC (F1 0.1391, AA 0.58) by 2.27% and 10% respectively. On Test-74, SSMP reaches F1 of 0.2373 and AA of 0.67, outperforming MMSC (F1 0.1991, AA 0.50) by 3.82% and 17%. Against the autoregressive baseline TGT, SSMP improves AA by 20% (Test-8) and 24% (Test-74), demonstrating the advantage of bi-directional contextual modeling with self-correction over sequential generation. On the newly released Test-2024 set, SSMP also leads with F1 0.1759 and AA 0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSMP outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric across both Test-8 and Test-74. The closest competitor is MMSC [53] (selection-then-ranking with multimodal semantic consistency), which SSMP surpasses by 2.27% F1 on Test-8 and 3.82% F1 on Test-74, with particularly large AA gains of 10% and 17% respectively, indicating superior temporal ordering. Against the autoregressive TGT [1], SSMP achieves substantially higher AA (0.68 vs. 0.48 on Test-8; 0.67 vs. 0.43 on Test-74), confirming that bi-directional self-corrective generation avoids the error propagation inherent in left-to-right prediction. Among video summarization baselines, Muvee [13] reports a higher Precision on Test-8 (0.2400 vs. 0.1563) but at the cost of very low Recall (0.0461) and F1 (0.0714), reflecting its tendency to select few but highly salient shots rather than matching the full trailer. The remaining selection-then-ranking methods (M2T [37], V2T [19], PPBVAM [50], IPOT [49]) and video summarization methods (VASNet [11], CLIP-It [32], OTVS [48]) are consistently outperformed by SSMP on all metrics, with F1 gaps ranging from 5% to over 15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both the dominant selection-then-ranking paradigm (V2T, IPOT, MMSC) and the more recent autoregressive sequence-to-sequence approach (TGT, TVMTrailer) by formulating trailer generation as a conditional masked prediction problem, drawing conceptual inspiration from BERT and LLaDA. The self-paced mask ratio scheduler adapts ideas from self-paced learning [20, 24] to dynamically adjust reconstruction difficulty during training, while the iterative re-masking at inference time introduces a self-correction mechanism that has no direct analogue in prior trailer generation work. The architecture is a standard Transformer encoder operating on ImageBind shot embeddings, with TransNet-v2 for shot segmentation, positioning the contribution primarily in the training/generation strategy rather than in novel feature extraction or model architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-correction mechanism addresses a fundamental error-propagation limitation shared by both prior paradigms, and the 17% AA improvement on Test-74 over the best selection-then-ranking baseline is practically meaningful for trailer quality. However, the method currently relies solely on visual shot features (no audio, subtitles, or narrative text), is trained on only 500 movie-trailer pairs, and is evaluated on a single dataset family, leaving open whether the masked-prediction-with-self-correction framework generalizes to other video editing tasks or scales with richer multimodal inputs. The self-paced scheduler's advantage over a simple linearly-increasing schedule is modest (F1 0.1996 vs 0.1915), suggesting the core benefit may come from the masked prediction formulation itself rather than the adaptive scheduling.
