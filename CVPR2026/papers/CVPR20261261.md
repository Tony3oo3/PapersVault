## Problem

Existing text-based image editing methods face a fundamental trade-off: tuning-free dual-branch approaches preserve source fidelity well but degrade sharply on non-rigid or semantically complex edits (e.g., posture changes, verb-driven actions), while methods that handle such edits (e.g., Imagic) require expensive fine-tuning or large-scale dataset retraining. No prior framework provides universal, zero-shot editing capability across arbitrary pre-trained T2I models without dataset curation, retraining, or per-image fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural (unpaired) RGB source image and an arbitrary user-provided text editing instruction as input. The instruction is not used directly; instead, BLIP-2 generates an aligned caption of the source image, which is then minimally modified to produce the target prompt, and a null-text embedding is optimized via inversion. The output is a single edited image at the base model's native resolution (512×512 for Stable Diffusion v1.4) that reflects the textual edit while preserving the source's structural and perceptual identity. The entire pipeline runs on a single GPU with only 0.08M learnable parameters.

## Method

NEAF introduces the XA-Conductor, a lightweight squeeze-excitation module (0.08M parameters) that learns a per-timestep weight vector to dynamically interpolate between source-branch and edited-branch cross-attention maps inside the U-Net denoiser. The core mechanism is a triadic-feedback loop comprising three reverse diffusion trajectories: a source process (guided by the BLIP-2-aligned caption), an edited process (driven by the modified target prompt with XA-Conductor-interpolated attention), and a reconstruction process (re-initialized with the source prompt but conditioned on the interpolated attention to verify fidelity). During test-time optimization (50 steps, lr=1e-2), the XA-Conductor is trained with a combined loss of latent-space L2 reconstruction error and CLIP image-text similarity, enabling it to identify which cross-attention channels are pivotal for the edit and attenuate or restore their influence. This design allows the model to capture verb-level semantic changes in cross-attention while the triadic feedback rigorously constrains non-edited regions, achieving both high editability and source fidelity without modifying the base diffusion model.

## Results

On TEdBench (25 images), NEAF achieves the highest Semantic Consistency (SC) score among all baselines: 8.00 (GPT-4o) and 9.42 (Gemini) on VIE Score, substantially outperforming the next-best SDEdit (7.14 / 7.42). The overall VIE Score (O) for NEAF is 7.36 (GPT-4o) and 8.34 (Gemini), the best in the Gemini evaluation and competitive with LEDITs++ (7.26) in GPT-4o. In a 50-participant user study (600 responses), NEAF received >70% preference over Imagic and >90% over LEDITs++. Computationally, NEAF requires only 0.08M trainable parameters and ~48 s per image on a single RTX 3090, versus 859.46M parameters and ~572 s for Imagic on an A100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEAF's dominant advantage is in Semantic Consistency: it scores 8.00 (GPT-4o) and 9.42 (Gemini), beating the closest competitor SDEdit by 0.86 and 2.00 points respectively, and outperforming all fine-tuning and retraining baselines by a wide margin (e.g., Imagic SC = 5.42/7.14, Instructpix2pix SC = 6.14/5.85). On Overall VIE Score, NEAF leads in the Gemini evaluation (8.34 vs. 7.26 for Imagic and SDEdit) and edges out LEDITs++ (7.36 vs. 7.26) in GPT-4o, though SwiftEdit achieves a slightly higher GPT-4o PQ (8.14 vs. 7.00). On Perceptual Quality, NEAF is competitive but not always the best—SDEdit, LEDITs++, and Imagic/PnP score higher on PQ in one or both evaluators—indicating that NEAF trades a small amount of raw visual quality for substantially better prompt adherence. In the user study, NEAF is strongly preferred over Imagic (>70%) and LEDITs++ (>90%), confirming that the SC advantage translates into perceived editing quality. Computationally, NEAF is ~12× faster per image than Imagic (48 s vs. 572 s) and uses ~10,000× fewer trainable parameters (0.08M vs. 859.46M), making it the most efficient method that supports non-rigid editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEAF sits at the intersection of tuning-free dual-branch editing methods (PnP, LEDITs++) and test-time optimization approaches (Imagic), but departs from both by introducing a small learnable module (XA-Conductor, 0.08M parameters) that is optimized at inference time rather than relying on static attention injection or full fine-tuning. Its pipeline builds directly on null-text inversion [23] for initialization and BLIP-2 captioning for prompt alignment, extending the dual-branch paradigm with a triadic-feedback loop (source → edited → reconstruction) that no prior tuning-free method employs. Conceptually, it reframes the editing problem as a lightweight attention-weight optimization problem rather than a feature-replacement or score-matching problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10,000× parameter reduction relative to Imagic (0.08M vs. 859.46M) and ~12× speedup (48s vs. 572s) make non-rigid, verb-driven editing accessible on consumer hardware, which prior fine-tuning-based methods could not offer. However, the evaluation is limited to 25 TEdBench images plus portraits on SD 1.4, and the authors note the CLIP-based edit loss contributed minimally, suggesting the reconstruction term alone drives most editing behavior—an observation that raises questions about whether the triadic loop is truly necessary or whether a simpler two-branch formulation with the same XA-Conductor would suffice. Open questions include generalization to newer backbones (SDXL, Flux), scalability to multi-object or spatially complex edits, and whether the XA-Conductor can be distilled into a fixed inference-time heuristic to eliminate the per-image optimization step entirely.
