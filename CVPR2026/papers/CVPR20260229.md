## Problem

Existing fMRI-based brain disorder diagnostic models are trained on a single site or assume immediate access to all multi-site data, making them unsuitable for real-world clinical deployment where data arrives sequentially from heterogeneous institutions. This sequential setting induces catastrophic forgetting, and privacy regulations prohibit sharing raw patient data across sites, rendering standard experience replay infeasible. No prior continual learning framework has been designed specifically for graph-structured fMRI data under these streaming, privacy-constrained conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes rs-fMRI BOLD time series preprocessed into 116×116 symmetric Pearson correlation matrices (AAL-116 atlas), which are thresholded at τ=0.4 to form undirected weighted graphs with 116 nodes whose features are 116-dimensional correlation profiles. Outputs include a diagnostic class label (patient vs. control), synthetic FC matrices generated by FCM-VAE for privacy-preserving replay, and graph-level readout embeddings used for representation-level distillation. The FC entries are mapped to positive intensities via Fisher-z transform and standardization before entering the Poisson-likelihood decoder.

## Method

FORGE combines a structure-aware variational autoencoder (FCM-VAE) for generative replay with a dual-level knowledge distillation objective that aligns both classification logits and graph-level readout embeddings between the current student and frozen teacher models. The FCM-VAE encoder is a graph transformer whose multi-head attention incorporates additive local adjacency biases and spectral positional biases (from top-k Laplacian eigenvectors), while the decoder is a low-rank bilinear model with Poisson edge likelihoods and a reachability gating mechanism that suppresses spurious edges. A Hierarchical Contextual Thompson Sampling scheme adaptively allocates a fixed replay budget across sites (site-level) and selects diverse, informative synthetic samples within each site (sample-level) using farthest-first traversal in the readout embedding space. The VAE is trained under a supervised ELBO that jointly optimizes FC matrix reconstruction, phenotype prediction via a linear-Gaussian head on the latent code, and KL regularization toward a standard Gaussian prior.

## Results

On the three multi-site fMRI benchmarks, FORGE achieves the highest Average Anytime Accuracy (AAA) and lowest Forgetting Rate (FOR) across all datasets: AAA of 0.730 / 0.685 / 0.738 and FOR of 0.116 / 0.101 / 0.105 on ASD (ABIDE-I), BSNIP, and MDD (REST-meta-MDD), respectively. Relative to the strongest baseline (PDGNNs), FORGE improves AAA by 2.7%, 3.9%, and 4.7% and reduces forgetting by 11.5%, 21.1%, and 24.5% on the three datasets. In the data-augmentation evaluation (Table 1), FCM-VAE also yields the best or second-best classification accuracy when used to augment training data, outperforming ReGate, BrainNetGAN, and GR-SPD-GAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORGE outperforms all 12 baselines on both AAA and FOR across all three datasets. The closest competitor is PDGNNs (topology-aware embedding memory), which FORGE surpasses by 2.7–4.7% in AAA and 11.5–24.5% in FOR; the gap is largest on BSNIP and MDD where cross-site heterogeneity is most severe. Among knowledge-distillation baselines, DER++ is the strongest (e.g., AAA 0.695 on MDD), yet FORGE still leads by 4.3% AAA and 29.3% relative FOR reduction. Graph-specific CL methods (TWP, UGCL, PDGNNs) consistently outperform generic CL methods (EWC, SI, PackNet, LwF), confirming that topology-aware mechanisms matter for FC matrices, but none match FORGE's dual-level distillation plus generative replay. Rehearsal-based baselines (ER, DER) suffer the most forgetting (FOR 0.17–0.24), highlighting the advantage of synthetic replay over storing raw samples. In the data-augmentation comparison (Table 1), FCM-VAE achieves the best GCN accuracy on ASD (0.783) and MDD (0.787), edging out GR-SPD-GAN (0.780, 0.767) and clearly surpassing ReGate and BrainNetGAN.

## Relation to Prior Work

FORGE sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) graph-based fMRI diagnosis using FC matrices, (2) continual learning for non-Euclidean data, and (3) privacy-preserving generative replay. The low-rank Poisson decoder with bilinear node-factor interactions is directly inherited from ReGate (Liu et al., 2021), while the dual-level distillation objective extends the logits-matching principle of LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2017) and the stored-teacher-output replay of DER (Buzzega et al., 2020) to graph-level readout representations. The key departure is the introduction of a structure-aware VAE (FCM-VAE) with spectral positional encoding and local adjacency bias in attention, combined with a hierarchical Thompson-sampling replay allocator, neither of which has appeared in prior graph CL or brain-network generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to address the practically critical problem of sequentially integrating fMRI data from multiple clinical sites without sharing raw patient data, a setting that mirrors real-world deployment but has been absent from both the CL and neuroimaging literatures. The privacy-preserving generative replay design makes the approach immediately relevant to federated or multi-institutional clinical pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 4–5 sites and binary classification, whether the AAL-116 parcellation and Pearson-correlation FC construction are necessary or whether the framework generalizes to other graph constructions, and whether the synthetic FC matrices retain sufficient neurobiological fidelity for downstream clinical interpretation. The modest buffer-size sensitivity (performance slightly degrading with larger buffers) also raises questions about the information content of generated samples at scale.
