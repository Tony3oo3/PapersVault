## Problem

4D synthesis from a single monocular video remains difficult because high-quality 4D training data is scarce and recovering both geometry and motion from one viewpoint is inherently ill-posed. Existing approaches either require slow per-instance optimization (multi-view generation + reconstruction, or frame-wise 3D generation + alignment) or rely on VAE-based motion latent spaces that demand large-scale, diverse 4D data to form well-structured distributions, limiting generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video of T frames (rendered at 256×256 in training) and an optional 3D reference mesh with vertices, faces, and texture; if no mesh is provided, one is generated from the first frame via Hunyuan3D 2.0. The output is per-frame 3D point flows (M=4096 sampled points per frame) expressed as displacements relative to the reference mesh, yielding a complete, temporally coherent 4D asset. The frame-wise transformer design supports arbitrary sequence lengths at inference, and the disentangled mesh representation enables motion retargeting to entirely different static meshes.

## Method

Motion 3-to-4 decomposes 4D generation into static shape encoding and dynamic motion reconstruction. A geometry encoder (inspired by 3DShape2VecSet) compresses N=4096 surface points of the reference mesh into K=64 shape latents via cross-attention with learnable query tokens; a DINOv2-based video encoder extracts patch-level features with injected temporal embeddings. These are fused through an Alternating-Attention architecture (L=16 blocks, inspired by VGGT) that interleaves global cross-frame attention with per-frame self-attention, producing a motion-aware latent per frame. A cross-attention motion decoder then predicts per-frame 3D positions for M query points sampled from the reference mesh, trained with dense MSE supervision on 12-frame sequences from 16K curated Objaverse/ObjaverseXL objects. This feed-forward design avoids per-instance optimization and post-processing alignment, achieving 6.5 FPS on 512-frame sequences.

## Results

On the proposed Motion-80 benchmark (short sequences), Motion 3-to-4 achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1113 and F-Score of 0.3171, substantially outperforming L4GM (CD 0.3561, F-Score 0.1269), GVFD (CD 0.1970, F-Score 0.2608), and V2M4 (CD 0.3437, F-Score 0.2318). When initialized with a ground-truth mesh (Ours w/m), geometry improves dramatically to CD 0.0437 and F-Score 0.6774, with appearance metrics also leading (CLIP 0.9251, DreamSim 0.0614). On the Consistent4D benchmark, Motion 3-to-4 achieves the best LPIPS (0.1455), CLIP (0.8609), and DreamSim (0.1691), though L4GM marginally leads on FVD (1207.79 vs. 1260.06). GVFD runs out of memory on long sequences (>128 frames), while Motion 3-to-4 scales gracefully.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Motion 3-to-4 decisively outperforms all three baselines on geometry. Against L4GM, it reduces CD by 3.2× (0.1113 vs. 0.3561) and nearly triples F-Score (0.3171 vs. 0.1269) on short sequences, as L4GM's 3D Gaussian representation produces floating point clouds not constrained to surfaces. Against GVFD, it improves CD by 44% (0.1113 vs. 0.1970) and F-Score by 22% (0.3171 vs. 0.2608), and GVFD entirely fails (OOM) on long sequences exceeding 128 frames. Against V2M4, it achieves a 3.1× CD reduction (0.1113 vs. 0.3437) and 37% higher F-Score, attributed to V2M4's per-frame optimization causing temporal flickering. On appearance, Motion 3-to-4 leads on CLIP and DreamSim across both benchmarks, though L4GM marginally wins on FVD for Consistent4D (1207.79 vs. 1260.06) and short-sequence Motion-80 (1120.67 vs. 1175.89), likely due to its training on orthogonal views matching the evaluation protocol. The 'Ours w/m' variant (ground-truth mesh input) demonstrates the upper bound of the motion reconstruction component, achieving CD 0.0437 and F-Score 0.6774—far beyond any baseline—and uniquely supports motion retargeting to artist-created static meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reformulating 4D synthesis as a composition of off-the-shelf 3D shape generation (via Hunyuan3D 2.0) and a lightweight motion reconstruction module, rather than following the dominant multi-view-generation-plus-reconstruction or generate-then-align pipelines (V2M4, ShapeGen4D, L4GM, GVFD). The shape encoder directly repurposes the cross-attention latent aggregation from 3DShape2VecSet, the video encoder leverages DINOv2 patch features, and the temporal architecture borrows the Alternating-Attention design from VGGT. Conceptually, the key departure is treating per-frame motion as a scene-flow prediction relative to a canonical mesh, which sidesteps the need for large-scale 4D training data that constrains VAE- or diffusion-based motion generators like GVFD and AnimateAnyMesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decomposition into 3D shape + motion flow is practically attractive because it lets practitioners plug in any strong 3D generator without retraining a 4D model, and the Motion-80 benchmark (with ground-truth geometry) addresses a real gap in 4D evaluation where prior benchmarks only offer renderings. The demonstrated motion-transfer capability—animating a chicken or robot with dragon video motion—suggests the learned motion latent is somewhat shape-agnostic, opening a path toward retargeting pipelines. However, the method cannot handle topology changes (e.g., a character opening a mouth or a cloth tearing), and the geometry encoder's lack of explicit mesh-topology modeling leads to vertex-sticking artifacts; whether these can be resolved without sacrificing the feed-forward simplicity remains an open question.
