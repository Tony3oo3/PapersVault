## Problem

Current pathology VQA models feed tens of thousands of WSI patches indiscriminately into a VLM, treating all regions equally regardless of question relevance. This causes two critical failures: the model produces unattributable 'black-box' answers with no patch-level evidence, and question-irrelevant visual tokens overwhelm the LLM, degrading accuracy. The paper asks whether a model can mimic the pathologist's natural coarse-to-fine workflow—first identifying relevant tissue regions, then zooming into a sparse set of critical patches—to achieve both interpretability and efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a gigapixel WSI (preprocessed into 224×224 patches following CLAM) and a natural-language question as input. Patch features are extracted with the CONCH vision encoder and the question is embedded via the CONCH text encoder. The output is a free-form text answer generated by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (with LongNet for long-context handling), grounded in a sparse, question-aligned subset of patch tokens—typically only 30% of all patches, yielding a 70% reduction in visual token computation.

## Method

HistoSelect introduces a two-stage hierarchical selector guided by a Hierarchical Information Bottleneck (HIB) objective. First, a pathologist-designed set of tissue-type prompts is used with CONCH to partition the WSI into M semantic tissue groups via cosine-similarity assignment. A Group Sampler (two linear layers) then predicts a per-group sampling rate r_j from the concatenation of the group prototype and question embedding, while a Patch Selector (two linear layers) assigns a selection probability s_i to each patch. Training minimizes a dual-level compression loss: KL divergences between the learned Bernoulli selection probabilities and cosine-similarity pseudo-priors at both group and patch levels, regularized by the VQA cross-entropy loss. A Straight-Through Estimator makes the discrete top-k selection differentiable, enabling end-to-end optimization. The entire module is trained jointly with the LLM (via LoRA) in a two-stage protocol inherited from SlideChat.

## Results

On close-ended VQA, HistoSelect achieves 83.80% average accuracy across SlideBench-VQA, WSI-Bench, and the In-house Ovarian dataset, outperforming the strongest WSI-based baseline SlideChat (80.88%) by ~3 points and thumbnail-based methods (Quilt-LLaVA 68.39%, LLaVA-Med 66.90%) by over 15 points. On open-ended generation (WSI-Bench), HistoSelect attains the best BLEU-4 (0.221) and ROUGE-L (0.463) for report generation, and the highest WSI-Precision/Relevance in 5 of 6 domain-specific VQA sub-tasks. The method also reduces visual token usage by 70% (selecting only ~5k tokens from ~17k total patches) while achieving peak accuracy at that budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HistoSelect outperforms all six baselines on the primary close-ended VQA task. Against the closest WSI-based competitor SlideChat [8], it gains +1.47 to +4.03 points per sub-task (e.g., 85.79% vs 82.15% on WSI-Bench Diagnosis; 73.33% vs 69.33% on the In-house Ovarian set). Thumbnail-based methods Quilt-LLaVA [31] and LLaVA-Med [19] trail by 15+ points on average, confirming that whole-slide context is essential for diagnostic and clinical questions. On open-ended generation, HistoSelect surpasses Quilt-LLaVA and SlideChat on all five report-generation metrics (BLEU-1 through BLEU-4, ROUGE-L) and leads on 5 of 6 WSI-P/WSI-R domain-specific scores. WSI-VQA [6], MI-Gen [5], and Hist-Gen [12] are evaluated only on open-ended tasks; HistoSelect exceeds WSI-VQA by 0.03–0.05 on BLEU scores and 0.14–0.19 on WSI-Precision, while MI-Gen and Hist-Gen are competitive on report generation BLEU but do not report domain-specific VQA metrics. GPT-4o, used as a non-specialized thumbnail baseline, lags far behind all pathology-specific models (49.28% average), underscoring the value of domain-adapted encoders and slide-level reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

HistoSelect positions itself between two existing paradigms for slide-level pathology VQA: (1) exhaustive aggregation methods like SlideChat and WSI-LLaVA that feed tens of thousands of patch tokens indiscriminately into an LLM, and (2) iterative agent-based frameworks (PathFinder, WSI-Agents, CpathAgent) that navigate the slide sequentially but incur high inference latency. The paper departs from both by introducing a one-shot, two-stage hierarchical selection (group sampler → patch selector) grounded in the Information Bottleneck principle, which has been applied to WSI classification and survival prediction but, to the authors' knowledge, not to pathology VQA. The tissue segmentation stage leverages CONCH's vision-language alignment with pathologist-designed prompts, extending the CLIP-like zero-shot segmentation idea to a structured diagnostic workflow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70% token reduction while simultaneously improving accuracy across three benchmarks addresses a practical bottleneck for clinical deployment of pathology VLMs, where inference cost and latency matter. The pathologist-validated interpretability (selected patches aligning with diagnostically relevant regions) directly targets the trust barrier that has limited adoption of black-box MLLMs in clinical settings. Open questions include whether the fixed set of tissue-type prompts generalizes across all 31+ cancer types without re-tuning, whether the IB formulation provides genuine benefits over simpler learned attention (the ablation against cosine similarity is suggestive but the gap is moderate), and how the method scales to multi-turn or multi-step diagnostic reasoning beyond single-question VQA.
