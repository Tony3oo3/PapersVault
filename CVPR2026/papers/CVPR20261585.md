## Problem

Existing diffusion-based portrait animation methods require 20+ denoising steps and classifier-free guidance, making real-time streaming inference infeasible. Additionally, their chunk-wise processing introduces redundant computation and error accumulation over long sequences, preventing stable low-latency video generation. This paper tackles both obstacles simultaneously to enable practical live-streaming portrait animation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (512×512) and a continuous driving video stream at 25 fps, extracting motion cues (facial expressions via implicit embeddings, head pose via 3D keypoints) from each driving frame while preserving the reference identity. Output is a streaming animation video generated frame-by-frame with 4 denoising steps per 4-frame micro-chunk, achieving 15.82 FPS and 0.253 s inter-chunk latency on a single H100 GPU, with the option to reach 20 FPS using a TinyVAE decoder.

## Method

PersonaLive employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) hybrid motion control combining implicit facial representations injected via cross-attention and 3D implicit keypoints injected via PoseGuider for simultaneous facial-expression and head-pose control; (2) fewer-step appearance distillation that compresses 20+ denoising steps to 4 by exploiting the observation that structure/motion is fixed early while later steps only refine texture, trained with MSE + LPIPS + adversarial loss and gradients propagated only through the final step; (3) an autoregressive micro-chunk streaming paradigm where each denoising window contains 4 micro-chunks of 4 frames with progressively higher noise levels, stabilized by a Sliding Training Strategy that simulates the streaming inference loop during training to eliminate exposure bias, and a Historical Keyframe Mechanism that adaptively selects past frames as auxiliary appearance references to suppress long-term drift.

## Results

On the TalkingHead-1KH self-reenactment benchmark, PersonaLive achieves the best L1 (0.039) and LPIPS (0.129) among all methods, with competitive SSIM (0.807) and tLP (21.31). On the LV100 cross-reenactment benchmark, it attains the best FVD (520.6) and tLP (12.83), indicating superior long-term temporal coherence. Most notably, PersonaLive runs at 15.82 FPS with only 0.253 s inter-chunk latency on a single H100 GPU, yielding a 7–22× speedup over prior diffusion-based portrait animation methods (which range from 0.85 to 2.22 FPS with 6.9–15.3 s latency).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the GAN-based LivePortrait, PersonaLive wins on L1 (0.039 vs 0.043), LPIPS (0.129 vs 0.137), FVD (520.6 vs 557.2), and cross-reenactment tLP (12.83 vs 13.51), while LivePortrait retains a slight edge on SSIM (0.821 vs 0.807) and self-reenactment tLP (20.40 vs 21.31). Among diffusion-based methods, PersonaLive dominates on efficiency: it runs at 15.82 FPS with 0.253 s latency, compared to 0.85–2.22 FPS and 6.9–15.3 s for X-Portrait, FollowYE, Megactor-Σ, X-NeMo, and HunyuanPortrait. On cross-reenactment temporal coherence, PersonaLive achieves the best FVD (520.6) and tLP (12.83), outperforming the next-best X-NeMo (639.1 / 18.10) and HunyuanPortrait (620.4 / 16.84). On motion accuracy (AED/APD), X-NeMo leads (0.679/0.022) and FollowYE is second on AED (0.911), while PersonaLive is competitive at 0.703/0.030. On identity preservation (ID-SIM), FollowYE leads (0.773) and LivePortrait is second (0.723); PersonaLive scores 0.698, slightly below X-NeMo (0.691) but above HunyuanPortrait (0.644) and Megactor-Σ (0.606). Overall, PersonaLive trades a small margin on ID-SIM and AED for substantially better temporal stability and a 7–22× inference speedup, making it the only method that achieves real-time streaming quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PersonaLive builds directly on the ReferenceNet-based diffusion animation lineage (Follow-your-emoji, X-Portrait, X-NeMo) for its denoising backbone and appearance conditioning, borrows PoseGuider from Animate Anyone for 3D keypoint injection, and adopts the temporal module from AnimateDiff. Its key departure is targeting the live-streaming regime: prior diffusion-based portrait animation methods optimize visual quality with 20+ denoising steps and chunk-wise inference with overlapping frames, while PersonaLive combines fewer-step appearance distillation (inspired by ADD/LCM/DMD) with an autoregressive micro-chunk streaming paradigm (from Diffusion Forcing) to achieve real-time generation. The hybrid motion control (implicit facial embeddings + 3D implicit keypoints) and the sliding training / historical keyframe mechanisms are novel contributions not present in any single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving 15.82 FPS with 0.253 s inter-chunk latency, PersonaLive makes diffusion-based portrait animation viable for live streaming for the first time, a 7–22× speedup over prior diffusion baselines while matching or exceeding their quality. The sliding training strategy and historical keyframe mechanism address exposure bias and error accumulation in autoregressive video generation in a way that generalizes beyond portrait animation to other streaming synthesis tasks. Open questions include whether temporal redundancy across consecutive frames can be further exploited for longer denoising windows, and whether the approach can generalize to non-human or stylized portraits, where the current model produces visible artifacts.
