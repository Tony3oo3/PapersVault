## Problem

Existing UAV-perspective MOT benchmarks assume smooth, predictable camera dynamics with nearly linear object trajectories, failing to capture the severe observational challenges—drastic scale changes, abrupt viewpoint shifts, and motion blur—induced by agile UAV maneuvers in real-world applications such as public safety, traffic monitoring, and sports analysis. This gap leaves a critical blind spot in evaluating tracker robustness under the highly dynamic ego-motion conditions that characterize actual UAV operations.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 42 video sequences at 1080p resolution (37,893 frames total) captured by a DJI Mini 4 Pro at 80–120 m altitude, with sequences averaging 1,828 frames and a minimum of 1,076—substantially longer than comparable UAV benchmarks. Each frame carries bounding-box annotations with category labels (8 classes including industrial vehicles like excavators, cranes, and bulldozers) and persistent track IDs, with occluded objects continuously annotated when position is inferable from trajectory context. Evaluation yields standard MOT metrics (MOTA, HOTA, IDF1, AssA, DetA, IDSW) across a 30/5/7 train/validation/test split.

## Method

DynUAV is constructed by deliberately executing agile UAV maneuvers—variable-speed motion, rotation, zooming, orbital and fly-in/pull-away flights—to systematically induce severe ego-motion that breaks the smooth-motion assumption underlying existing trackers. The dataset spans three scene types (campus, city/road, nighttime) and employs a three-stage annotation pipeline combining semi-automatic labeling, peer review, and automated error detection via frame-level and ID-level sampling. For evaluation, all 11 SOTA trackers share YOLOv11 as a unified detection backbone (100 epochs at 1280×1280), with appearance-based trackers using OSNet or FastReID for ReID features. A dedicated ablation isolates the impact of Camera Motion Compensation (CMC), demonstrating that it substantially reduces IDSW and boosts AssA, confirming ego-motion as the primary source of tracking difficulty. Cross-dataset comparisons against MOT17, MOT20, and DanceTrack reveal that DynUAV uniquely stresses both detection and association simultaneously, unlike prior benchmarks that primarily test one dimension of difficulty.

## Results

On DynUAV, TrackTrack achieves the best overall tracking quality with HOTA 62.74, IDF1 74.81, AssA 68.89, and the lowest IDSW (256) among all 11 evaluated trackers. StrongSORT attains the highest MOTA (68.18) but lags in association (AssA 63.15, IDSW 1394), while U2MOT performs worst overall (HOTA 51.47, IDSW 6452). The CMC ablation confirms ego-motion as a primary difficulty source: enabling CMC reduces AdapTrack's IDSW from 1123 to 583 and TrackTrack's from 439 to 256, while boosting AssA by ~8 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrackTrack dominates on association metrics (IDF1 74.81, AssA 68.89, HOTA 62.74) and identity stability (IDSW 256), outperforming all other trackers by 1.5–16 points on IDF1. StrongSORT achieves the highest MOTA (68.18) thanks to its CMC module reducing detection failures, but its association metrics (AssA 63.15, IDSW 1394) trail TrackTrack by 5.7 points and 1138 switches respectively, suggesting its appearance fusion is degraded by motion blur. AdapTrack (HOTA 62.33, IDF1 73.26) benefits from DynUAV's long sequences, where its adaptive memory mechanism excels, placing it second overall. DiffMOT (HOTA 61.21, IDSW 430) and Deep OC-SORT (IDF1 72.25, AssA 65.49) are competitive mid-tier performers, with DiffMOT's diffusion-based motion prior and Deep OC-SORT's ReID features each addressing different aspects of the ego-motion challenge. U2MOT (HOTA 51.47, IDSW 6452) and BoostTrack (MOTA 56.72, HOTA 53.71) perform worst, with U2MOT's uncertainty model failing under severe appearance variation and BoostTrack's aggressive pseudo-box generation introducing excessive false positives in cluttered dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the UAV-perspective MOT benchmarking space by deliberately targeting the ego-motion dimension that prior aerial benchmarks (VisDrone, UAVDT, MDMT) underrepresent through their reliance on hovering or slow-cruising flight patterns. It departs from the smooth-motion assumption that underpins both existing UAV datasets and the MOT algorithms evaluated on them, and extends the object taxonomy beyond conventional road vehicles to include industrial machinery (excavators, cranes, bulldozers). Rather than proposing a new tracker, the paper repurposes 11 existing MOT methods as probes to quantify how severely agile camera dynamics degrade detection and association, positioning DynUAV as a stress test for the entire tracking pipeline rather than a single algorithmic component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that severe ego-motion shifts the primary bottleneck from association (as in DanceTrack or MOT20) to a systemic degradation of both detection and identity consistency, a finding that reframes where algorithmic effort should be directed for aerial MOT. The CMC ablation provides concrete evidence that camera-motion compensation is essential but introduces a detection-association trade-off, pointing toward jointly optimized detection-and-compensation frameworks as a promising but unsolved direction. Limitations include the modest dataset scale (42 sequences), absence of extreme weather, and single-sensor capture, leaving open questions about whether the observed failure modes persist under multimodal sensing or at larger operational scales.
