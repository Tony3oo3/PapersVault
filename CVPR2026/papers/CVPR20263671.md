## Problem

3D foundation models (3DFMs) are trained primarily on overlapping, densely captured views, yet their ability to reason about geometry under extreme, non-overlapping viewpoints remains largely unexplored. This paper investigates whether these models possess an emergent understanding of extreme-view geometry and how to enhance it without degrading their pretrained multi-task capabilities (depth, point maps, poses).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263671/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pairs of RGB images (or multi-view collections) and produces per-image depth maps, point maps, and camera poses in a shared coordinate frame. For the alignment training, the supervision signal is sparser than typical 3DFM training: only relative rotation matrices between image pairs are used as ground truth, rather than dense per-pixel annotations. The fine-tuned models retain all original outputs (depth, point maps, poses) while specifically improving relative rotation accuracy on non-overlapping pairs.

## Method

The paper first reveals via cross-view attention map analysis that 3DFMs encode a rich internal 3D language in their shared alternating attention backbone, with decoder heads acting as mere format converters. Building on this, the alignment scheme freezes all decoder heads (both camera and dense prediction) and fine-tunes only the bias terms of a sparse subset of backbone layers—approximately 80k parameters, four orders of magnitude smaller than the full model. The training objective is a geodesic rotation loss on relative rotations between image pairs, optionally augmented with an anchoring term for models with a fixed reference frame. This selective bias-only tuning on identified critical layers (those connected to the dense prediction head via skip connections) aligns the internal 3D representation for extreme-view reasoning while preserving pretrained multi-task knowledge, as confirmed by ablations showing that unfreezing heads or tuning full layers causes significant reconstruction degradation.

## Results

On the primary extreme rotation task, fine-tuned WorldMirror (WM_FT) achieves the best median rotation error (MRE) of 9.74° on sELP, 11.75° on UnScenePairs, and 13.13° on UnScenePairs-t, substantially outperforming the previous state-of-the-art ExRot (13.23°, 28.48°, 42.45° respectively). Fine-tuned VGGT and π³ also achieve strong results (MRE 14.21°/12.71°/14.48° and 11.96°/12.92°/13.31°), all using only ~80k tuned bias parameters. On multiview pose estimation (RealEstate10K, ETH3D) and dense reconstruction (UnSceneRecon, ETH3D), fine-tuned models preserve or improve upon pretrained performance, confirming that the alignment scheme does not degrade multi-task capabilities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WM_FT decisively outperforms ExRot [4], the previous state-of-the-art for extreme rotation, on all three test sets: 9.74° vs. 13.23° MRE on sELP, 11.75° vs. 28.48° on UnScenePairs, and 13.13° vs. 42.45° on UnScenePairs-t, with the largest gap (29.3°) on the translation-heavy UnScenePairs-t set. Among the three 3DFMs, pretrained WorldMirror is the strongest baseline (MRE 19.25° on UnScenePairs), while pretrained VGGT is the weakest on sELP (92.92° MRE) due to its landmark-centric pretraining not matching sELP's forward-walking trajectories; however, after fine-tuning all three 3DFMs converge to similar performance (MRE 11.75°–14.21° on UnScenePairs). On multiview pose estimation (Table 2), fine-tuned models largely match or slightly exceed their pretrained counterparts, with VGGT showing notable gains (TA30 92.75→93.77 on RealEstate10K), while WM and π³ show minor AUC drops (≤3 points) that the authors attribute to already being near performance ceiling. On dense reconstruction (Table 3), VGGT_FT improves accuracy on both UnSceneRecon (1.441→1.291 m) and ETH3D (0.284→0.233 m), WM_FT improves on ETH3D (0.285→0.235 m), while π³_FT shows slight degradation, which the authors attribute to π³'s lack of a dedicated camera token.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D foundation model lineage (VGGT, WorldMirror, π³), which replaced classical SfM/MVS pipelines with feedforward transformers that jointly predict poses, depths, and point maps. It departs from prior extreme-rotation methods (ExRot, ExRot-3D) that train dedicated architectures from scratch, instead adapting existing 3DFMs through a minimal backbone bias-tuning strategy. It also contrasts with prior fine-tuning approaches for 3DFMs (e.g., Human3R, Doppelgangers++) that modify only decoder heads while freezing the backbone; here the authors show the reverse—freezing heads and tuning backbone biases—preserves multi-task consistency. The training data construction follows Bezalel et al.'s extreme rotation methodology, extended to include larger camera translations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 3DFMs encode a rich 'internal 3D language' in their alternating-attention backbone—evidenced by cross-view attention maps showing near-correspondence and symmetry-based reasoning even without visual overlap—reframes how we think about what these models actually learn. The 80k-parameter alignment achieving ~3× rotation error reduction across three architectures suggests a broadly applicable recipe for adapting large pretrained 3D models to out-of-distribution conditions. Open questions remain: translation estimation under large baselines improves only modestly, the training data is still landmark-centric (MegaScenes), and it is unclear whether this bias-tuning strategy generalizes to other out-of-distribution shifts (e.g., dynamic scenes, novel object categories). The MegaUnScene benchmark, while valuable, is relatively small (469 scenes) and may not capture the full diversity of in-the-wild conditions.
