## Problem

Existing monocular depth estimation models predict physical geometry and ignore flat media (posters, clothing patterns), while segmentation models produce masks without any global ordering. No prior method can decompose an arbitrary image into a globally consistent, editable stack of layers — the representation that creative software actually uses. This paper introduces 'illustrator's depth,' a per-pixel layer index that captures compositional ordering rather than metric distance, and trains a network to predict it.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image of arbitrary resolution (trained at 1536×1536) and outputs a continuous depth map D_θ(I) ∈ R^(H×W) whose values encode layer indices from 1 to N. Although the underlying quantity is a discrete layer index, it is predicted as a continuous value to allow smooth optimization and straightforward binning. Downstream, this map can be thresholded into N discrete layers for raster editing, fed into a Potrace-based vectorizer to produce a layered SVG, or interpreted as elevation to generate a triangulated 3D relief mesh.

## Method

The architecture initializes from Depth Pro (a DINOv2-based multi-scale encoder with a CNN decoder) and is fine-tuned on ~100K curated SVG images from MMSVG-Illustration, where ground-truth layer indices are rasterized via a base-256 RGB encoding. A key design choice is training directly on layer indices (1…N) with a scale-invariant MAE loss (median/mean-absolute-deviation normalization, à la MiDaS) rather than in disparity space, which balances foreground and background optimization. Data curation merges consecutive same-color layers and removes ambiguous non-consecutive same-color overlaps to reduce label noise. Post-processing converts the continuous map to discrete layers either by direct binning (for raster tasks) or by RGB-space clustering with median-depth assignment (for vectorization), enabling integration into standard raster-to-vector pipelines like VTracer+Potrace.

## Results

On the MMSVG-Illustration validation set, the proposed method achieves a depth ordering consistency (Order) of 0.987, MAE of 0.12, and MSE of 0.26 for illustrator's depth prediction, substantially outperforming Depth Pro (Order 0.636, MAE 1.44) and Depth Anything-v2 (Order 0.791, MAE 1.16). In the full vectorization pipeline (Table 3), the method attains Order 0.987, MAE 0.46, MSE 2.09 for layering quality, while matching VTracer+Potrace on reconstruction fidelity (SSIM 0.997, LPIPS 0.005) and achieving the lowest path count error (0.16). Inference runs in under one second on a modern GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines spanning heuristic, optimization-based, and data-driven vectorization, as well as two monocular depth estimators. On the core depth prediction task, the method dominates both Depth Pro (Order 0.987 vs. 0.636) and Depth Anything-v2 (Order 0.987 vs. 0.791), confirming that physical depth models are fundamentally misaligned with illustrative layering. In vectorization, the method outperforms all five competitors on layering quality: it beats the best heuristic (Less Is More, Order 0.746) by 0.241, the optimization-based LIVE (Order 0.838) by 0.149, and the strongest data-driven baselines StarVector (Order 0.918) and OmniSVG (Order 0.925) by 0.069 and 0.062 respectively. On visual fidelity, the method matches VTracer+Potrace exactly (SSIM 0.997, LPIPS 0.005) while dramatically outperforming StarVector (SSIM 0.858, LPIPS 0.302) and OmniSVG (SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.317), which suffer severe reconstruction failures. The method also achieves the lowest path count error (0.16 vs. 0.53–0.54 for data-driven baselines and 0.83 for VTracer), indicating more compact SVG output.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Depth Pro monocular depth estimation architecture (built on DINOv2) by retraining it on a curated SVG dataset to predict compositional layer indices rather than physical depth, effectively converting a geometric prior into a structural/compositional one. It departs from three adjacent literatures: monocular depth estimation (which ignores flat media and printed elements), panoptic/instance segmentation (which provides masks without global ordering), and existing vectorization pipelines (which rely on brittle heuristics or optimization for layer sorting). The key conceptual move is defining 'Illustrator's Depth' as a per-pixel ordinal map that unifies segmentation and depth ordering into a single, globally consistent signal optimized for editability rather than metric accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing depth as a creative abstraction rather than a physical quantity, this work provides a practical foundation for editable image decomposition that current tools lack, with demonstrated applications spanning vectorization, text-to-vector generation, 3D relief fabrication, and depth-aware editing. The generalization from simple SVG training data to complex artistic renderings (paintings, realistic images) is promising but supported primarily by qualitative evidence, leaving open whether the concept scales to highly complex or photorealistic scenes without ground-truth layerings. The authors note that future zero-shot or prompt-based inference could eliminate the need for task-specific training, and the broader question of whether a single ordinal map can capture all meaningful compositional structure in arbitrary artwork remains unresolved.
