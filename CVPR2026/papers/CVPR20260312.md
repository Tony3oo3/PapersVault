## Problem

Existing fine-grained visual understanding systems operate as refined classifiers that predict a single label from a fixed taxonomy, losing 30–40% accuracy when confronted with unseen species or domains. The paper argues that fine-grained understanding must shift from static label prediction to evidence-driven reasoning, where models must justify as well as recognise by connecting perceptual cues to structured external knowledge, yet no existing framework constructs such an explicit chain of evidence across open-set scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260312/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent takes an RGB image and a natural-language question as input and produces a text answer with an interpretable reasoning chain. Internally, it generates intermediate structured outputs at each stage: a ranked list of open-set category hypotheses with confidence scores, per-hypothesis textual knowledge snippets parsed into discriminative attribute cues, and spatial attention masks (h×w binary maps) grounding each cue to a specific image region. A self-corrective loop re-invokes earlier stages when the final answer confidence falls below a threshold, making the output iterative rather than single-pass.

## Method

KFRA is a three-stage closed reasoning loop coordinated by a large multimodal controller (Qwen3-A3B) that couples retrieval with spatial grounding. Stage 1 performs open-vocabulary detection via Grounding-DINO and web-scale image retrieval via Google Lens, then uses an LMM to generate ranked category hypotheses from the retrieved visual-semantic evidence. Stage 2 retrieves textual knowledge (Wikipedia) for each hypothesis, parses it into discriminative cues, and aligns each cue to a visual region through a global-to-local focusing module (CLIP-style semantic similarity followed by patch-level attention refinement, built on VisionReasoner); when alignment confidence is below a threshold, OseDiff super-resolution reconstructs the most confident region to recover fine details. Stage 3 feeds all accumulated evidence—hypotheses, knowledge, attributes, and grounded masks—into the LMM for final inference. The key architectural distinction is the retrieval-grounding coupling: retrieved knowledge acts as an actionable signal that directs spatial attention and hypothesis verification rather than serving as passive context, enabling zero-shot, task-agnostic reasoning without any task-specific retraining.

## Results

On FGExpertBench, KFRA built on GLM-4.5V-12B achieves the highest average accuracy of 74.81%, surpassing the best commercial model (Gemini-2.5-Flash, 69.98%) by 4.83 points and the best open-source standalone model (Qwen3-VL-A22B, 66.58%) by 8.23 points. When paired with the lighter Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, KFRA delivers a 19.14-point improvement over the base model (67.78% vs. 48.64%). On six conventional FGIC datasets, KFRA (GLM-4.5V-12B) reaches 90.24% average accuracy, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Flash (89.10%) and DeepPerception-7B (83.21%), while KFRA (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) still attains 85.10%, a 23.61-point gain over its base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KFRA (GLM-4.5V-12B) outperforms all standalone LMMs and commercial APIs on FGExpertBench, beating Gemini-2.5-Flash (69.98%) by 4.83 points and GPT-4o (65.03%) by 9.78 points in average accuracy. Against agent-based baselines, the gap is dramatic: VSA (39.24%) and MMSearch (36.81%) trail KFRA by over 35 points, attributed to their lack of fine-grained category alignment and evidence-grounded reasoning. On the six FGIC classification datasets, KFRA (GLM-4.5V-12B) achieves 90.24% average, edging out Gemini-2.5-Flash (89.10%) and substantially surpassing DeepPerception-7B (83.21%) and Finedefics-8B (76.84%). The backbone-agnostic nature is highlighted by KFRA (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) improving its base model by 19.14 points on FGExpertBench and 23.61 points on FGIC, demonstrating that the closed reasoning loop adds value regardless of the underlying LMM. KFRA shows its largest relative gains on the Reasoning and Knowledge dimensions, where explicit evidence construction and external knowledge grounding are most critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

KFRA sits at the intersection of three research threads: fine-grained visual classification (which it departs from by replacing closed-set single-label prediction with open-set evidence-driven reasoning), large multimodal models (which it repurposes from passive recognisers into active reasoning engines), and retrieval-augmented agents (which it extends by coupling retrieval with spatial grounding rather than treating them as independent stages). The global-to-local focusing mechanism builds on CLIP-style semantic similarity and VisionReasoner, while the super-resolution step borrows from OseDiff. The key conceptual departure from prior agents like VSA and MMSearch is the 'retrieval-grounding coupling,' where retrieved textual knowledge directly drives attention localisation and hypothesis verification in a bidirectional loop, rather than serving as static context appended to a prompt.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper articulates a compelling paradigm shift—fine-grained understanding as evidence construction rather than label prediction—and provides both a framework (KFRA) and a benchmark (FGExpertBench) to operationalise it. The six-dimension benchmark (object, attribute, action, counting, reasoning, knowledge) fills a genuine gap, as existing fine-grained benchmarks focus almost exclusively on object recognition. However, the benchmark's small scale (300 images, 1,500 QA pairs), reliance on external APIs (Google Lens, Wikipedia) for retrieval, and the multi-step computational overhead limit immediate practical adoption. Open questions include whether the retrieval-grounding coupling generalises beyond fine-grained domains, how performance degrades when retrieval quality is poor, and whether the framework's benefits persist at larger scale or with weaker LMM backbones.
