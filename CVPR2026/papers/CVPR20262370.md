## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods achieve high-quality reconstruction but break down on sequences longer than a few seconds, suffer from temporal inconsistency under large motions and disocclusions, and produce unstructured point clouds that are incompatible with conventional video coding pipelines. No prior representation simultaneously supports arbitrary-length dynamic scenes, handles disocclusions robustly, and outputs a format directly encodable by standard codecs like HEVC or FFV1.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262370/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video (55–88 cameras at 1920×1200, up to 90 FPS, sequences up to 30 minutes) and produces a temporal sequence of single 2D UV atlas images, each packing K=8 progressive-resolution layers of 3D Gaussian attributes (radial distance, scale, rotation, opacity, SH coefficients) at 8-bit precision via a quadtree-like layout. Because every frame is a standard 8-bit image, the atlas sequence can be losslessly encoded with off-the-shelf codecs (FFV1, HuffYUV) and decoded back to render novel views from any camera position.

## Method

PackUV-GS directly optimizes 3D Gaussian parameters in a fixed-resolution UV grid (spherical projection) rather than fitting 3DGS first and projecting post-hoc, enforcing spatial sparsity through the discrete grid structure. Temporal coherence over long sequences is maintained by an optical-flow-guided keyframing module (RAFT magnitude peaks define segment boundaries) and a covariance-aware Gaussian labeling step that projects each Gaussian's 2D covariance into image space, tests overlap with flow-derived motion masks via the Mahalanobis metric, and freezes gradients for static Gaussians during backpropagation. A progressive pyramid of UV layers (alternating dimension halving) is packed into a single quadtree atlas per frame achieving 88.5% pixel utilization, and low-precision optimization (8-bit quantization with straight-through estimator) is performed during training so the output is natively codec-compatible without lossy post-processing.

## Results

On the challenging PackUV-2B dataset (55–88 cameras, 360° coverage, large motions and disocclusions), PackUV-GS achieves 27.41 PSNR / 0.842 SSIM / 0.28 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (3DGStream, 23.17 PSNR) by 4.2 dB. On SelfCap (22 cameras, 4K, 60 FPS), it reaches 22.52 PSNR / 0.783 SSIM, a 2.7 dB gain over the next-best method (GIFStream, 19.78 PSNR). On the shorter N3DV sequence, PackUV-GS scores 32.81 PSNR / 0.953 SSIM / 0.21 LPIPS, edging out RealTime4DGS (32.29 PSNR) by 0.5 dB while training 1.8× faster (1.37 h vs. 2.48 h). PackUV-GS is the only method that supports both full-sequence streaming and full codec compatibility (HEVC/FFV1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PackUV-GS outperforms all eight baselines on PSNR across every dataset, with the largest margins on the most challenging data: +4.2 dB over 3DGStream on PackUV-2B and +2.7 dB over GIFStream on SelfCap. On N3DV (shorter, front-facing sequence), the gap narrows to +0.5 dB over RealTime4DGS, which also achieves marginally better SSIM (0.955 vs. 0.953) and Grid4D achieves slightly lower LPIPS (0.197 vs. 0.21), indicating near-parity on simpler scenes. Deformation-based methods (4DGS, RealTime4DGS, Deformable3DGS, Grid4D) consistently underperform on long sequences with large motion, and ATGS suffers the worst degradation on SelfCap (15.48 PSNR) due to gradient explosion. Among streaming-capable baselines, 3DGStream and GIFStream support partial or pseudo-streaming but lack codec compatibility, whereas PackUV-GS is the only method achieving both full-sequence streaming and lossless codec encoding (FFV1/HEVC). Training time is competitive: PackUV-GS requires 1.05–1.37 h per 60-frame window, comparable to 3DGStream (1.00 h) and faster than RealTime4DGS (up to 8.07 h) and 4DGS (up to 3.27 h).

## Relation to Prior Work

PackUV builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and the UVGS spherical-projection mapping, but departs from prior 3DGS-to-2D approaches (UVGS, SOG) that perform lossy post-hoc UV unwrapping by instead optimizing Gaussian attributes natively in UV space. It also extends the streaming 4DGS line (3DGStream, ATGS, GIFStream) by replacing per-frame neural caches with optical-flow-guided keyframing and covariance-aware Gaussian labeling, and it contrasts with deformation-based methods (4DGS, Deformable3DGS, Grid4D) that are memory-bound and limited to short clips. The key conceptual departure is treating the resulting UV atlas sequence as a standard 8-bit video stream, making 4D Gaussian scenes directly encodable by off-the-shelf codecs (FFV1, HEVC) without a bespoke compression pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first volumetric video representation that is losslessly compatible with conventional video coding infrastructure, which could dramatically lower the barrier to storing, streaming, and sharing 4D content within existing multimedia pipelines. The PackUV-2B dataset (100 sequences, 2B+ frames, 55–88 cameras, up to 30 minutes) fills a critical gap left by prior multi-view benchmarks that are short, frontal-only, or low-motion, and will likely become a standard stress test for long-horizon dynamic reconstruction. Open questions include whether lossy codecs (HEVC) can be applied with acceptable quality, how the method behaves under unsynchronized or sparse-camera captures, and whether the UV-grid discretization limits expressibility for extremely fine geometric detail.
