## Problem

Existing skeleton-based temporal action segmentation methods operate almost exclusively in the kinematic domain, modeling how joints move but remaining agnostic to the underlying physical forces that drive motion. This dynamics-agnostic approach limits inter-class discriminability for actions with similar kinematic profiles but distinct dynamic intents (e.g., 'Moving Cart' vs. 'Walking') and hinders precise boundary localization, since action transitions are fundamentally defined by abrupt shifts in the dynamic force profile rather than mere kinematic changes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed skeleton sequence X ∈ R^{C0×T×V} (e.g., 12 channels × 6000 frames × 19 joints for PKU-MMD v2) and produces frame-level one-hot action labels Y ∈ R^{Q×T} along with per-frame boundary confidence scores. The model is trained end-to-end with a composite objective combining standard segmentation and boundary regression losses, an action-text contrastive loss, and the novel Energy Consistency Loss, with inference employing a multi-stage refinement and post-processing scheme.

## Method

LaDy introduces a Lagrangian Dynamics Synthesis (LDS) module that computes generalized coordinates (root orientation and local joint rotations) from joint positions, then uses four physics-constrained MLPs to estimate the inertia matrix (via Cholesky decomposition ensuring positive definiteness), Coriolis matrix (via skew-symmetric constraint ensuring passivity), gravity vector, and non-conservative forces, assembling them into generalized forces τ = M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + G(q) + F(q,q̇). An Energy Consistency Loss enforces the work-energy theorem by aligning kinetic energy change with net work done, using a scale-invariant relative residual with Huber loss. The synthesized forces drive a Spatio-Temporal Modulation (STM): spatially, forces are projected and concatenated with GCN kinematic features; temporally, three salient 1D signals (Instantaneous Power, Torque Norm, Torque Change) hierarchically gate L stages of Linear Transformers via parallel 1D convolutional streams with sigmoid activations, progressively highlighting boundary-sensitive features across temporal scales.

## Results

On PKU-MMD v2 (X-view), LaDy achieves 77.0% Acc, 74.7% Edit, and 67.6% F1@50, surpassing the previous best (ME-ST: 74.1/70.5/62.4) by +2.9/+4.2/+5.2 points respectively. On PKU-MMD v2 (X-sub), it reaches 76.2% Acc, 75.1% Edit, and 67.0% F1@50, improving over the prior best by +1.5/+1.7/+2.7. LaDy also sets new records on MCFS-22 (81.5% Acc, 89.8% Edit), MCFS-130 (72.8% Acc, 79.7% Edit), LARa (70.4% Acc), and TCG-15 (89.3% Acc, 77.9% Edit), while remaining computationally efficient at 13.67G FLOPs and 1.83M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaDy outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. On PKU-MMD v2 X-view, it surpasses the strongest prior method ME-ST [25] by +2.9% Acc, +4.2% Edit, and +5.2% F1@50, while using only 13.67G FLOPs versus ME-ST's 97.07G (a 7× reduction). Against the most recent language-assisted baselines LaSA [24] and LPL [6], LaDy gains +7.5% Acc and +10.6% F1@50 on X-view, and +2.6% Acc and +1.3% F1@50 on X-sub. On MCFS-22, LaDy achieves 81.5% Acc and 89.8% Edit, exceeding LaSA (80.8/86.7) and DeST-Former (80.4/85.2). On TCG-15, it reaches 89.3% Acc and 77.9% Edit, edging out LPL (88.8/77.4) and ME-ST (88.7/77.3). Notably, LaDy's gains are most pronounced on F1@50 (strictest IoU threshold), confirming that the physics-informed dynamics improve precise boundary localization rather than merely boosting coarse classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaDy builds on the decoupled spatio-temporal architecture of DeST [31] as its main backbone, incorporating CTR-GCN's adaptive GCN [9] for spatial modeling and LaSA's action-text contrastive loss [24] for representation learning. Its key departure is the introduction of a Lagrangian Dynamics Synthesis module that explicitly estimates generalized forces (inertia, Coriolis, gravity, and non-conservative terms) from joint kinematics, constrained by physical properties (positive-definite inertia via Cholesky decomposition, passivity via skew-symmetric Coriolis construction). This positions the work at the intersection of physics-informed learning and skeleton-based STAS, departing from the dominant kinematics-only paradigm where prior methods model only the 'what' and 'how' of motion without reasoning about the underlying forces. In the broader physics-informed learning literature, LaDy is distinct from prior uses of physics as auxiliary features, trajectory regularizers, or control signals, instead using synthesized dynamics to directly modulate spatio-temporal feature flow.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that explicit Lagrangian dynamics improve both inter-class discriminability and boundary localization, this work opens a new design axis for STAS: the 'why' of motion (force profiles) complements the 'what' (kinematic patterns). The Energy Consistency Loss, which enforces the work-energy theorem as a training regularizer, is a reusable physics-based constraint that could transfer to other dynamics-aware tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond single-body open kinematic chains (e.g., multi-contact or multi-agent scenarios), whether the finite-difference approximations for velocity and acceleration remain adequate at higher frame rates, and whether the physics-informed modulation strategy extends to other modalities (video, IMU). The method's reliance on a simplified rigid-body model also limits its applicability to highly deformable or soft-body motions.
