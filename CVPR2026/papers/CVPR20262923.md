## Problem

Monocular human motion recovery faces two coupled challenges: (1) reliance on expensive, constrained 3D motion capture data limits out-of-distribution generalization, and (2) recovering metric-scale absolute positions in world coordinates from a single 2D view is inherently ill-posed. Prior methods either produce only root-aligned relative trajectories or require scene scans/SLAM for absolute positioning, leaving a gap for practical markerless mocap that yields physically grounded, metrically accurate poses from accessible 2D inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular 2D pose sequence (in SMPL, COCO, or H36M keypoint format) along with calibrated camera intrinsics and extrinsics as input, and produces full-body 3D absolute poses in world coordinates at metric scale. During inference, the model internally synthesizes three virtual-view 2D motions from the single input view, then triangulates across all four views to recover 3D positions. The output can be expressed as SMPL-format global motion (with optional SMPLify post-fitting for shape parameters) or as COCO-format 3D keypoints, supporting downstream tasks requiring precise human-scene interaction.

## Method

Mocap-2-to-3 reformulates 3D motion recovery as a multi-view 2D synthesis problem trained in two stages: a transformer-based diffusion model is first pre-trained on large-scale 2D data (projected 3D joints and in-domain 2D sequences) to learn diverse motion priors, then fine-tuned with a View Attention Layer on 3D data to enforce cross-view geometric consistency. A key architectural contribution is a decomposed motion representation that separates root-centered local pose (normalized to [-1,1]) from global movement (root trajectory + bounding-box scale), preventing position loss from dominating and degrading fine-grained motion detail. To resolve depth ambiguity and accelerate convergence, the method encodes the ground-plane equation (computed from known camera poses) as per-pixel pointmaps, which are compressed via a ResNet-18 encoder and injected through cross-attention to provide explicit 2D-to-3D geometric constraints. At inference, the denoising process iteratively generates multi-view 2D motions, converts them to global 2D coordinates via the decoupled representation, and triangulates to obtain absolute 3D poses in the world coordinate system.

## Results

On the RICH benchmark (SMPL-format ground-truth 2D input), Mocap-2-to-3 achieves 26.2 mm PA-MPJPE and 39.6 mm MPJPE in camera coordinates, outperforming the strongest hybrid baseline GVHMR+SMPLify (30.7 / 58.7 mm) by 4.5 mm in pose accuracy. In world coordinates without any alignment, the method achieves 156.8 mm Abs-MPJPE, substantially lower than SA-HMR (268.3 mm), TRAM (533.8 mm), and GVHMR+SMPLify (430.4 mm), demonstrating superior metric-scale absolute positioning. On AIST++ (COCO-format with ViTPose detector), the method reaches 60.1 mm PA-MPJPE and 61.8 mm T_root, beating MVLift (79.2 / 67.6) and GVHMR+SMPLify (62.2 / 112.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mocap-2-to-3 outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric in both evaluation settings. Against the strongest hybrid baseline GVHMR+SMPLify (which combines learned estimation with SMPLify optimization using ground-truth 2D keypoints and calibrated cameras), the method reduces PA-MPJPE by 4.5 mm (26.2 vs 30.7) and Abs-MPJPE by 273.6 mm (156.8 vs 430.4), showing that the multi-view lifting framework with 2D pretraining captures both finer motion details and far more accurate absolute positioning. Compared to SA-HMR, which requires pre-scanned scene geometry, Mocap-2-to-3 achieves lower Abs-MPJPE (156.8 vs 268.3 mm) using only calibrated camera poses, making it more practical for deployment. Against TRAM and MetricHMR, which rely on SLAM-estimated camera poses, the method's use of calibrated cameras yields dramatically lower absolute errors (156.8 vs 533.8 mm for TRAM), confirming that SLAM drift is a major source of positional inaccuracy. On AIST++ with COCO-format keypoints from ViTPose, the method beats MVLift (the closest 2D-to-3D lifting competitor) by 19.1 mm in PA-MPJPE and 5.8 mm in T_root, and surpasses GVHMR+SMPLify by 2.1 mm in PA-MPJPE and 50.5 mm in T_root, validating generalization across keypoint formats.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view diffusion framework of MAS [11] and the 2D-pretraining strategy of Motion-2-to-3 [24] into a monocular 2D-to-3D lifting setting, reformulating 3D motion recovery as a multi-view synthesis problem rather than direct regression. It departs from world-grounded methods like WHAM [27] and GVHMR [26], which recover only root-aligned relative trajectories, by targeting absolute metric-scale poses using calibrated camera poses (similar to SA-HMR [25] and Ray3D [44]) but without requiring scene scans. The decoupled local-pose/global-movement representation and ground-plane pointmap encoding are novel contributions that address the loss-dominance and slow-convergence problems inherent in predicting absolute positions from monocular 2D inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that abundant 2D data can meaningfully enhance 3D motion recovery, offering a practical path around the cost and accessibility barriers of 3D motion capture. The 2.7× improvement in absolute positioning error over the strongest hybrid baseline (GVHMR+SMPLify) on RICH is practically significant for downstream interaction tasks in gaming, sports analysis, and embodied AI. Key limitations include the dependence on calibrated cameras (limiting in-the-wild deployment), sensitivity to 2D keypoint quality, and the absence of foot-sliding post-processing. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multi-person scenes, uncalibrated settings, and whether the 2D-pretraining benefit scales with dataset size.
