## Problem

Existing event filters treat event polarities as continuous signals and ignore the process information encoded in inter-event time intervals, producing outputs sparser than the raw input and failing to reconstruct continuous irradiance dynamics. No prior method provides a generative, physically grounded formulation that jointly models both the state information (polarity jumps) and process information (contrast-bound constraints) inherent in asynchronous event streams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260245/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream (timestamps and ±1 polarities) from event cameras such as DAVIS346 (346×260) or EVK4 (1280×720) at 1 μs temporal resolution, operating over a 100 ms sliding window. It produces a resampled filtered event stream via a piecewise-homogeneous Poisson process and a continuous event density flow (EDF) evaluated per-pixel at arbitrary times. The entire pipeline runs in O(1) per-event time, with a 4.99 μs single-event latency on a single CPU core, making it suitable for real-time sensor-rate deployment.

## Method

EDFilter models event generation as threshold-crossing probability fluxes at the ±C contrast boundaries of a stochastic irradiance process governed by a Fokker-Planck equation, then estimates these fluxes nonparametrically from observed events. The temporal component uses a rectangular-kernel density estimator with polarity-modulated spike trains and constant false-event rates, optimized via recursive maximum likelihood with an exponential prior on kernel bandwidth, yielding a closed-form O(1) state-space update. The spatial component enforces local motion consistency by expressing the smoothed EDF as a linear combination of four directional 3×3 basis patterns (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°) with coefficients solved by q-norm reconstruction. Finally, a hybrid event-triggered/periodic sampling scheme with zero-order hold interpolation and a polarity-aware modified thinning algorithm resamples the continuous EDF into a clean discrete event stream, making the filter generative rather than merely discriminative.

## Results

On the E-MLB dataset, EDFilter achieves the best mean ESR across all four noise levels (ND1–ND64) for both day and night conditions, reaching 1.02/1.07 (ND1) down to 1.00/0.99 (ND64), outperforming the next-best method EventZoom (1.00/1.06 to 0.97/0.99). On the proposed RED dataset, EDFilter attains the lowest NMSE for both DAVIS346 and EVK4 sequences. For direct point tracking, EDFilter ranks among the top two on ATE (2.358 mm on point/DVS, 2.046 mm on points/EVK), and achieves the lowest per-event latency of 4.99 μs with a throughput of 210k events/s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDFilter consistently outperforms all four baselines on the primary event-denoising metric (mean ESR) across every noise level and lighting condition on E-MLB, with margins of 0.02–0.20 over the strongest competitor EventZoom. On the RED dataset's NMSE metric, EDFilter also achieves the best values for both sensor types. For direct point tracking (ATE), EvFlow edges out EDFilter slightly on the single-point DAVIS346 sequence (2.290 vs. 2.358 mm) but EDFilter is competitive or best on all other tracking settings; EvFlow's advantage comes at the cost of aggressive event removal that degrades irradiance fidelity. In terms of runtime, EDFilter achieves the lowest per-event latency (4.99 μs) versus MLPF (7.55 μs) and EventZoom (1.21 s), though MLPF sustains a higher throughput (4.73M vs. 210k events/s) due to its simpler per-event MLP inference. Overall, EDFilter is the only method that simultaneously leads on denoising quality, remains competitive on motion tracking, and operates at microsecond latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper inverts the stochastic diffusion framework of DVS Voltmeter [23], which simulates events from known irradiance dynamics, by instead inferring the underlying diffusion parameters from observed events to produce a filtered stream. It departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm (density-based, motion-based, and learning-based filters that can only remove events) and from generative frame-based approaches like EventZoom that sacrifice microsecond temporal precision by binning events into synchronous frames. The key conceptual move is reframing event filtering as estimation of threshold-crossing probability fluxes at contrast boundaries, unifying the treatment of event polarities (state information) and inter-event intervals (process information) that prior methods handle separately or ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probability-flux formulation provides a physically interpretable, training-free generative filter that operates at O(1) per-event complexity with ~5 μs latency, making it practical for real-time event camera pipelines where learning-based methods introduce unacceptable delay or require retraining per noise level. The RED dataset with microsecond-accurate irradiance ground truth under controlled rotation fills a notable gap in event-camera evaluation, where prior benchmarks lacked temporal references fine enough to validate sub-millisecond fidelity. Open questions include whether the single-pixel Markovian SDE assumption generalizes to complex multi-pixel interference, how the method scales to higher-resolution sensors with millions of events per second, and whether the nonparametric kernel approach can be augmented with learned priors without losing its interpretability advantage.
