## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on retrieval-augmented diffusion models (RAG-DMs) either manipulate only the generation phase or only the retrieval phase under white-box assumptions, leaving a critical knowledge conflict unresolved: retrieved poisoned images may contradict the user's text prompt, causing generated outputs to drift from the target class. Moreover, no prior method addresses the more realistic black-box setting where attackers lack access to the retriever's architecture or the knowledge base's embedding distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261973/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker injects a small number (k) of target-class image embeddings (generated by an auxiliary Stable Diffusion v1.5 model and encoded via CLIP) into the victim's knowledge base, then appends a short English-word trigger (m tokens) as a suffix to diverse benign queries. The black-box RAG-DM receives the triggered query, retrieves k nearest-neighbor embeddings from the poisoned knowledge base, and conditions the diffusion model on both the query text and retrieved images to produce a generated RGB image. Benign queries without the trigger should yield normal outputs, preserving utility.

## Method

JOB formulates trigger optimization as a sequential word-sampling problem solved via reinforcement learning, where an LSTM policy network samples m English tokens one-by-one to construct the trigger. The key insight is a composite reward that jointly optimizes three objectives: (a) retrieval alignment, measured by average CLIP cosine similarity between the triggered query embedding and all poisoned image embeddings; (b) generation alignment, measured by average CLIP cosine similarity between the generated image and the poisoned images; and (c) fluency, measured by normalized GPT-2 log-likelihood of the triggered query. The policy is updated via gradient descent on the negative reward-weighted log-probability loss. Poisoned images are selected as the top-k most CLIP-similar candidates from an auxiliary model's output for the target class, and their embeddings are inserted directly into the knowledge base, requiring no access to the victim model's parameters.

## Results

On RDM (PLMS-based), JOB achieves an ASR-r of 76.54% and ASR-g of 54.13%, outperforming the strongest trigger-optimization baseline AutoDAN (ASR-g 49.25%) by ~5 points and the RAG-specific baseline BadRDM (ASR-g 36.52%) by ~18 points. On RDM (DDIM-based), JOB reaches ASR-r 80.94% and ASR-g 59.78%, again leading all baselines. JOB also preserves benign performance well, maintaining ACC of 63.94% (PLMS) / 60.91% (DDIM) and the lowest FID (17.25 / 16.74) among all attack methods, close to the non-attack baseline (FID 16.22 / 16.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JOB dominates all nine baselines on both ASR-r and ASR-g across both RDM variants. Against the closest RAG-specific baseline BadRDM [12], JOB improves ASR-g by 17.6 points (54.13 vs. 36.52 on PLMS) and 19.9 points (59.78 vs. 39.92 on DDIM), while also matching or exceeding its ASR-r (76.54 vs. 70.51; 80.94 vs. 75.36). Among trigger-optimization baselines, AutoDAN [22] is the strongest on ASR-g (49.25% PLMS, 50.24% DDIM), but JOB surpasses it by 4.9 and 9.5 points respectively; CPA [39] leads on ASR-r among trigger methods (71.63% PLMS, 73.39% DDIM) yet JOB still exceeds it by 4.9 and 7.6 points. All model-training baselines (Rickrolling, BadT2I, Personalization, EvilEdit) remain far below JOB, with ASR-g values between 15.88% and 34.42%, confirming that white-box fine-tuning approaches are ineffective under the black-box RAG-DM setting. On functionality-preserving metrics, JOB maintains the best FID (17.25 / 16.74) and competitive ACC, showing that its attack does not degrade generation quality more than other methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature on diffusion models (Rickrolling-the-Artist, BadT2I, EvilEdit) and RAG systems (BadRDM, PoisonedRAG) by formulating the first jointly optimized trigger for black-box RAG-DMs that simultaneously manipulates both the retrieval and generation phases. Prior work either fine-tunes the diffusion model's generation pathway (requiring white-box access) or poisons the retriever (BadRDM, also white-box), leaving the knowledge-conflict problem between retrieved images and user prompts unresolved. The RL-based trigger optimization draws on PatchAttack's policy-network formulation and GCG/AutoDAN's adversarial prompt search, but repurposes them with a multi-objective reward that couples retrieval similarity, generation alignment, and linguistic fluency in a single optimization loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAG-DMs are increasingly embedded in AI agent pipelines and open-source frameworks (e.g., OpenClaw), the demonstrated vulnerability—compromising both retrieval and generation by injecting only k target-class images into a public knowledge base—poses a concrete deployment risk that existing defenses (perplexity filtering, query rephrasing) only partially mitigate. The moderate ASR-g scores (~54–60%) and the ~10-point drop under rephrasing defenses suggest the attack is effective but not yet fully robust, leaving open questions about whether more aggressive knowledge-base poisoning or adaptive triggers could close the gap. The paper also highlights that the black-box constraint fundamentally changes the attack surface: without access to the retriever's embedding space, aligning poisoned images with the unseen knowledge-base distribution becomes the central challenge, a problem that may generalize to other retrieval-augmented multimodal systems.
