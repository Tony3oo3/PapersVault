## Problem

Existing raster-to-vector methods trace only visible boundaries, producing fragmented, flat lists of overlapping paths that destroy the original Z-order and layering relationships. Partially occluded shapes are recovered as disjoint curve fragments rather than complete geometric primitives, yielding structurally inconsistent outputs that are difficult to edit. The paper asks whether vectorization can be reformulated as a generative, shape-by-shape construction task that produces complete, artist-like layered compositions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 RGBA raster image as the global context and, at each autoregressive step, also receives a partial composite image (initially an empty canvas) that accumulates all previously generated layers via alpha-over blending. At each step it outputs a group of k=4 non-overlapping RGBA shape layers in latent space, which are decoded by a frozen VAE (16× spatial compression) into pixel-space layers and composited onto the running partial image. The final output is an ordered stack of complete, closed shape layers whose alpha-blended reconstruction approximates the input image.

## Method

ShapeAR is a flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT) operating in the latent space of a convolutional VAE that encodes stacks of RGBA layers. The DiT (SD3-style, width 1024, 12 dual-attention + 24 single-attention blocks, 4-axis RoPE) predicts a time-dependent velocity field over serialized latent tokens, conditioned on dual context: the original input image and the partial composite of all layers generated so far, enabling coarse-to-fine autoregressive synthesis of an arbitrary number of shapes. Training uses a flow-matching objective (Eq. 1) with the VAE trained via Charbonnier regression on all RGBA channels plus a focal binary cross-entropy term on alpha to preserve thin high-frequency features. The model is trained on ~900k SVG instances filtered for geometric simplicity (symmetry and convexity thresholds), with shapes packed into non-overlapping layers and a random autoregressive step sampled per training instance to supervise the (I, P_t) → G^(t) mapping.

## Results

On a benchmark of 100 diverse images from held-out MMSVG and OmniSVG test sets, ShapeAR achieves L1 = 0.005, 1-SSIM = 0.020, and LPIPS = 0.014, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. Compared to the strongest prior method LIVSS, ShapeAR reduces L1 error by 37.5%, 1-SSIM by 64.9%, and LPIPS by 71.4%. In shape-quality evaluation against the Adobe Illustrator vectorization engine, ShapeAR produces shapes with higher convexity (0.88 vs. 0.68) and symmetry (0.49 vs. 0.29), and a lower KID (0.374 vs. 0.388), indicating closer alignment with the distribution of designer-created shapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShapeAR wins decisively against all four baselines on every reconstruction metric. Against the closest competitor LIVSS (a simplify-then-vectorize layered method), ShapeAR achieves 37.5% lower L1, 64.9% lower 1-SSIM, and 71.4% lower LPIPS, indicating substantially better pixel-level and perceptual fidelity. The gap widens dramatically against earlier methods: SGLIVE (segmentation-guided with gradient fills) is 28× worse in L1, O&R (top-down error-budget pruning) is 20× worse, and LIVE (layer-wise iterative refinement) is 105× worse in L1. Beyond reconstruction, ShapeAR also outperforms the non-ML Adobe Illustrator baseline on shape geometry: generated shapes are more convex (0.88 vs. 0.68) and more symmetric (0.49 vs. 0.29), and the KID over shape masks is lower (0.374 vs. 0.388), confirming that the autoregressive diffusion process produces shapes closer to the distribution of human-drawn primitives rather than fragmented boundary traces.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShapeAR repurposes the Stable Diffusion 3 / DiT / flow-matching architecture for a task it was not designed for: structured, layered vector generation. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant optimization-based vectorization paradigm (differentiable rasterizers, boundary tracing, path pruning) and the earlier learned-vector line (SketchRNN, DeepSVG, SVGFormer) by reframing vectorization as an autoregressive generative process over RGBA shape layers in VAE latent space. The dual conditioning on the full input image (global context) and the partial composite (local context) draws on the coarse-to-fine decomposition strategies in layered image decomposition literature, but applies them within a diffusion framework rather than a segmentation or optimization pipeline. The use of multi-axis RoPE to encode layer/height/width/condition positions is a practical adaptation of the DiT positional scheme to a 4D structured output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from boundary-tracing to generative layer synthesis addresses a long-standing practical pain point: existing vectorizers produce flat, fragmented path lists that are difficult to edit, whereas ShapeAR outputs complete, artist-like RGBA layers that preserve Z-order and can be individually manipulated. The ability to predict full geometric primitives even when occluded (e.g., a complete circle behind another object) is a qualitative capability no prior vectorizer offers. Key limitations remain: the method is bounded by raster input resolution, cannot represent self-intersecting or compound paths with negative space, and depends on a post-hoc shape-to-SVG conversion step. Open questions include whether the latent space can be disentangled to support variable opacity, transparency effects, and higher-resolution generation without the current 16× compression bottleneck.
