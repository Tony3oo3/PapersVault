## Problem

OpenVision's training pipeline requires both a text encoder (for dual contrastive loss over two captions per image) and a text decoder (for captioning loss), making it substantially heavier than vanilla CLIP and limiting scalability for resource-constrained researchers. The paper asks whether the contrastive component is truly necessary, or whether a purely generative caption-only objective can match multimodal performance while dramatically reducing compute and memory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261839/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images at resolutions ranging from 84px (low-res pretraining) to 448px (high-res fine-tuning) and produces visual token embeddings suitable for downstream multimodal LLMs (LLaVA-1.5, Open-LLaVA-Next). During training, roughly 65% of the visual tokens are randomly masked before being concatenated with the text decoder's input, so the decoder conditions on only ~35% of the full token sequence. Captions are padded or truncated to 128 tokens, and the text decoder is a Transformer of identical size to the vision encoder, randomly initialized.

## Method

OpenVision 2 removes the text encoder and the entire image-text contrastive loss from OpenVision, collapsing the pipeline to two modules: a ViT image encoder and a text decoder that autoregressively predicts synthetic captions (from ReCap-DataComp-1B v2) given the image's visual tokens. An additional efficiency tweak randomly masks ~65% of visual tokens before decoding, which both reduces the decoder's computational load and regularizes the encoder by forcing it to produce informative representations from fewer tokens. Training follows a CLIPA-style curriculum (10,000 epochs at 84px, then 800 epochs at 224px, optionally extended to 336–448px) with AdamW, cosine LR schedule, and mixed precision on TPU v4 pods. The caption loss weight is set to 2, and no image-reconstruction or pixel-level auxiliary loss is used. This design aligns the pretraining architecture with downstream multimodal fine-tuning (e.g., LLaVA), eliminating the objective mismatch between stages.

## Results

Under the Open-LLaVA-Next framework, OpenVision 2 (ViT-L/14 @336) achieves 68.9 on TextVQA, 537 on OCR-Bench, 73.4 on SEED-Bench, and 88.4 on POPE, matching or slightly exceeding the original OpenVision (68.3, 547, 73.3, 88.1) while reducing training time from 83h to 57h (~1.5×) and peak memory from 24.5 GB to 13.8 GB (~1.8×). At the largest scale (ViT-H/14 @448), OpenVision 2 reaches 71.9 on TextVQA and 590 on OCR-Bench, setting new best results among open vision encoders. The 1.01B-parameter g/14 model achieves 67.3 on TextVQA and 88.0 on POPE at 224 resolution, demonstrating that the generative-only paradigm scales beyond 1B parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original OpenVision (same backbone and resolution), OpenVision 2 wins on TextVQA (+0.6 at L/14@336), MME-Perception (+65), SEED-Bench (+0.1), GQA (+0.2), and POPE (+0.3), while losing modestly on ChartQA (−5.7) and OCR-Bench (−10) at that setting; however, at SoViT-400M/14@384, OpenVision 2 surpasses OpenVision on TextVQA (+1.6), ChartQA (+0.3), and OCR-Bench (+9). Compared to OpenAI-CLIP (L/14@336), OpenVision 2 trails slightly on TextVQA (68.9 vs 69.4) and SQA (75.2 vs 76.9) but leads on OCR-Bench (537 vs 535), GQA (64.6 vs 64.5), and POPE (88.4 vs 87.6). Against SigLIP (SoViT-400M/14@384), OpenVision 2 gains +0.8 on TextVQA, +2.1 on ChartQA, and +55 on OCR-Bench, though it concedes ground on MME (1521 vs 1539) and SQA (72.7 vs 74.7). Versus MetaCLIP-5B (L/14@224), OpenVision 2 leads on TextVQA (+3.1), OCR-Bench (+8), SEED-Bench (+0.8), GQA (+0.6), and POPE (+0.8), but falls short on SQA (68.4 vs 77.1) and MME (1577 vs 1590). Overall, OpenVision 2 is competitive or superior across the board while training ~1.5–2× faster with ~1.8× less peak memory, and it is the only method in the comparison that scales to a 1.01B-parameter vision encoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

OpenVision 2 is a direct simplification of OpenVision [33], which itself combined CLIPA's two-stage curriculum [31], ReCap's synthetic captioning [32], and CLIPS's dual contrastive + captioning losses [39]. The key architectural departure—removing the text encoder and all contrastive objectives—follows the caption-only philosophy previously advocated by CapPa [54] and AIMv2 [14], while adopting LLaVA-style token concatenation rather than cross-attention for modality fusion. The paper thus positions itself as a validation and scaling of the generative-only pretraining hypothesis at a scale (1B+ parameters, 12.8B pairs) and with a data quality (ReCap-DataComp-1B v2) that prior caption-only works had not reached.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a purely generative, caption-only objective matches or exceeds contrastive CLIP-style training across eight multimodal benchmarks while cutting training time by ~1.5–2× and memory by ~1.8×, this work provides strong empirical evidence that the field's reliance on contrastive pretraining for vision encoders may be unnecessary. The efficiency gains make billion-parameter vision encoders practically trainable on accessible hardware, opening a path to scale that was previously cost-prohibitive. Open questions include whether the generative-only paradigm transfers to other modalities (audio, video), whether the optimal token-masking ratio and caption length generalize across architectures, and whether the absence of contrastive normalization (requiring extra LayerNorms at the projector) introduces subtle downstream instabilities at even larger scales.
