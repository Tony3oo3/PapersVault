## Problem

Existing bokeh rendering methods assume high-quality, all-in-focus inputs, making them ineffective on high-digital-zoom mobile photos that suffer from reduced resolution, amplified noise, and lost fine details. The straightforward two-stage workaround (super-resolution followed by bokeh rendering) introduces error accumulation between stages and doubles inference cost, motivating a unified single-step approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262537/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality high-zoom RGB image (tested at 4032×3024 from iPhone 13 Pro at 5×–15× zoom, internally processed at 512×512), a user-specified focal disparity, and a blur intensity parameter. It produces a single high-quality bokeh image in one forward pass, with the defocus map computed as r = K|d − d_f| from a degradation-aware depth estimate. No text prompt or noise sampling is required at inference.

## Method

MagicBokeh builds on a block-pruned SD2.1 U-Net (cross-attention and mid-stage removed) with LoRA adapters in the VAE encoder and U-Net, operating as a single-step diffusion model. An alternative training strategy alternates between a bokeh phase (training ControlNet conditioned on the defocus map plus bokeh LoRA layers) and a Real-ISR phase (training only SR LoRA layers with an all-zero defocus map), decoupling conflicting optimization objectives. A focus-aware mask attention mechanism modulates self-attention by inserting a binary foreground/background mask derived from the defocus map, constraining attention within each region so that in-focus areas are reconstructed without interference from defocused areas. A degradation-aware depth module uses self-feature distillation with Depth Anything v2 as both teacher (on HQ images) and student (on simulated LQ images) to produce robust disparity estimates from low-quality inputs.

## Results

On the EBB400-LQ benchmark, MagicBokeh achieves PSNR 24.23, SSIM 0.8623, LPIPS 0.2786, DISTS 0.1600, and FID 72.43, outperforming all two-stage baselines on these fidelity metrics. It also achieves the fastest inference time at 0.1062 s per 512×512 image, roughly 8.7× faster than the best two-stage pipeline (BokehDiff+ at 0.9238 s) and 28× faster than Dr.Bokeh+ (2.9883 s). On no-reference perceptual metrics, MagicBokeh scores 58.83 MUSIQ and 0.4138 MANIQA, slightly below BokehDiff+ (59.36 / 0.4259), which the authors attribute to BokehDiff's inaccurate focus distributions inflating those scores without producing realistic bokeh.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicBokeh is compared against three bokeh rendering methods (BokehMe, Dr.Bokeh, BokehDiff) each paired with two single-step Real-ISR backbones (OSEDiff, S3Diff), forming six two-stage baselines. Against the strongest two-stage pipeline (BokehDiff+S3Diff), MagicBokeh improves PSNR by +0.40 dB, SSIM by +0.023, LPIPS by −0.029, DISTS by −0.014, and FID by −0.11, while running 8.7× faster (0.106 s vs 0.924 s). It trails BokehDiff+ on MUSIQ (58.83 vs 59.36) and MANIQA (0.4138 vs 0.4259), but the authors argue this reflects BokehDiff's failure to blur regions that should be defocused, inflating no-reference scores without producing realistic bokeh. Against BokehMe+S3Diff, MagicBokeh gains +0.48 dB PSNR and −0.035 LPIPS while being 7.1× faster. Against Dr.Bokeh+S3Diff, MagicBokeh leads on every metric (PSNR +0.56 dB, LPIPS −0.035, FID −0.67) and is 28× faster. The paper also reports a user study on 50 real-world iPhone 13 Pro high-zoom images (5×–15×) where 50 participants consistently preferred MagicBokeh over all two-stage alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate tasks—real-world image super-resolution (Real-ISR) and bokeh rendering—within a single diffusion-based framework, departing from the dominant two-stage pipeline (e.g., OSEDiff/S3Diff for SR followed by BokehMe/Dr.Bokeh/BokehDiff for bokeh). It builds on Stable Diffusion (SD2.1) as the generative backbone, ControlNet for defocus-map-conditioned rendering, and Depth Anything v2 for depth estimation, but introduces an alternative training strategy and focus-aware mask attention to resolve the conflicting optimization objectives between SR and bokeh. The degradation-aware depth module extends Depth Anything v2 via self-feature distillation to handle LQ inputs, addressing a gap where prior depth estimators degrade on zoomed mobile photos.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical and increasingly common mobile photography scenario—high digital zoom producing LQ images where existing bokeh methods fail—offering a single-inference solution that is both faster and more photorealistic than two-stage alternatives. The alternative training strategy and focus-aware mask attention are reusable techniques for any unified generative pipeline with competing objectives. Limitations include evaluation at 512×512 resolution, a relatively small user study (50 participants, 50 images), and the degradation-aware depth module showing only marginal quantitative gains despite qualitative improvements, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more diverse degradation types beyond digital zoom.
