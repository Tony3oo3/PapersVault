## Problem

Existing training-free Zero-Shot Composed Image Retrieval methods rely on MLLMs to convert all implicit visual information from the reference image into explicit textual descriptions, which over-constrains the retrieval space and reduces diversity. Because composed queries inherently contain ambiguous implicit elements (visual details present in the image but unmentioned in the text), a purely text-dominant approach cannot capture the fuzzy retrieval nature that requires considering diverse combinations of candidates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a short modification text as input, and produces a re-ranked list of target images from a candidate database. An MLLM (GPT-4o) is called once per query to generate three outputs: a target description, an include-attribute caption, and an exclude-attribute caption. The CLIP image and text encoders then process the reference image and modification text to produce embedding vectors, and the final output is a ranked candidate set scored by a composite similarity that blends geodesic-mixup query similarity with explicit attribute-based re-ranking.

## Method

G-MIXER consists of two complementary training-free modules. First, Geodesic Mixup-based Implicit semantic eXpansion (G-MIX) computes multiple composed query features by interpolating between the CLIP image embedding and text embedding along the hypersphere using a range of geodesic mixup ratios (λ from 0.7 to 1.0 in steps of 0.05), rather than a single fixed ratio; top-K images retrieved under each ratio are aggregated into a diverse first-stage candidate set. Second, Explicit semantic Re-ranking (ER) uses MLLM-generated include and exclude attribute captions to compute a re-ranking score that combines the modification-text similarity, the composed-query similarity, and a delta term (Δ = ReLU(S_λ − S_ex) − ReLU(S_λ − S_in)) that rewards candidates suppressing excluded attributes while penalizing those lacking included attributes. This two-stage design preserves implicit visual diversity through the geodesic expansion while filtering noisy candidates via explicit semantic cues, achieving state-of-the-art results on CIRCO, CIRR, FashionIQ, and GeneCIS without any additional training.

## Results

On CIRCO (ViT-L/14), G-MIXER achieves mAP@5 = 28.29% and mAP@50 = 32.39%, surpassing the strongest training-free baseline OSrCIR by +4.42% and +3.42% respectively. On CIRR (ViT-L/14), G-MIXER reaches R@1 = 37.42%, R@5 = 67.69%, and R_Subset@1 = 71.88%, outperforming OSrCIR by +7.97, +10.01, and +9.76 points. On FashionIQ (ViT-L/14), G-MIXER improves average R@50 to 62.47% (+8.1% over OSrCIR), and on GeneCIS (ViT-L/14) it achieves R@1 = 19.9%, R@3 = 43.9%, the best across all backbones. The method is fully training-free, requiring only a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU with ~0.94 s per query (97% MLLM inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G-MIXER outperforms all six baselines across every benchmark and backbone. The closest competitor is OSrCIR (Tang et al., 2025), a training-free MLLM-based method; G-MIXER beats it by +4.42 mAP@5 on CIRCO (ViT-L/14) and by +7.97 R@1 on CIRR, with the gap widening at larger k (e.g., +10.01 R@5). LDRE (Yang et al., SIGIR 2024), which ensembles multiple LLM-generated captions, is surpassed by +4.94 mAP@5 on CIRCO and +10.89 R@1 on CIRR (ViT-L/14). CIReVL (Karthik et al., 2023), the earlier training-free caption-based approach, trails G-MIXER by a wide margin (+9.72 mAP@5 on CIRCO, +12.87 R@1 on CIRR). Among training-based methods, SEARLE (Baldrati et al., ICCV 2023) and LinCIR (Gu et al., 2024) are the weakest baselines, while PrediCIR (Tang et al., 2025) is competitive on CIRR R@10 (77.90 vs. 78.58 for G-MIXER at ViT-G/14) but is still outperformed on all other metrics and on CIRCO. Notably, G-MIXER is training-free yet surpasses all training-based methods, indicating that the geodesic mixup expansion plus explicit re-ranking captures compositional semantics more effectively than learned pseudo-token approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

G-MIXER positions itself against the dominant training-free ZS-CIR paradigm that relies on MLLMs to generate explicit target captions (CIReVL, OSrCIR, LDRE), arguing that this text-dominant strategy over-constrains retrieval by converting ambiguous implicit visual cues into fixed textual descriptions. The method repurposes geodesic (spherical) interpolation in CLIP's embedding space—previously explored in single-ratio feature fusion (e.g., slerp-based approaches)—by sweeping a range of mixup ratios to construct a diverse candidate pool, then applies MLLM-generated include/exclude attributes solely for re-ranking. Conceptually, it decouples implicit semantic expansion (geometric, in embedding space) from explicit semantic filtering (textual, via MLLM), a separation not made by prior ZS-CIR methods that conflate both in a single caption or a single fused feature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean separation of implicit (geodesic mixup) and explicit (MLLM re-ranking) semantics offers a principled recipe for handling the inherent ambiguity in composed queries, and the consistent SOTA gains across four benchmarks and three CLIP backbones validate the approach. However, the method's practical adoption is limited by its dependence on GPT-4o for caption and attribute generation (~0.6 s/query, ~97% of inference time), raising cost and latency concerns for large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the geodesic mixup range (0.7–1.0) generalizes across domains without tuning, whether the approach extends to video or 3D retrieval where the embedding geometry differs, and whether lighter MLLMs can match GPT-4o's attribute extraction quality without the observed ~1% drop.
