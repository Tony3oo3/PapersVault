## Problem

Fine-grained remote sensing detection datasets use hierarchical label structures, but embedding this hierarchy into representation learning faces two unresolved issues: (1) imbalanced data distribution across and within hierarchical levels causes high-frequency classes to dominate contrastive learning, and (2) grouping objects under shared parent categories for semantic modeling conflicts with the need for class-agnostic spatial localization. Prior methods either treat ambiguous 'other' classes as mutually exclusive categories or apply contrastive losses without addressing these imbalances and task interference.

## Input / Output

The method ingests multi-band remote sensing images (optical or SAR) with spatial resolutions from 0.12 m to 6 m and sizes up to 10000×10000 pixels, resized to 1024×1024 for a ResNet-50 backbone. It produces oriented (rotated) bounding boxes with category labels drawn from hierarchical taxonomies of up to four levels (ShipRSImageNet) or two levels (FAIR1M), where ambiguous instances originally labeled as 'Other*' are reassigned to their parent coarse-grained categories rather than treated as separate fine-grained classes.

## Method

The approach integrates two innovations into DETR-based detectors (OrientedFormer, RHINO). First, a Balanced Hierarchical Contrastive Loss (BHCL) extends supervised contrastive learning across all levels of the label tree, introducing learnable class prototypes (updated via EMA) as synthetic instances so that every class—regardless of frequency—contributes equally to the loss in each mini-batch; level-dependent weights emphasize finer-grained discrimination. Second, a decoupled query strategy splits DETR's object queries into separate classification and localization sets that share a self-attention alignment module but then pass through independent cross-attention and feed-forward streams, ensuring BHCL's hierarchical semantic grouping acts only on classification queries without distorting bounding box regression. The total training objective combines BHCL (applied at every decoder layer) with standard focal classification, rotated IoU, and L1 regression losses under bipartite matching.

## Results

On ShipRSImageNet, the proposed method (built on OrientedFormer) achieves an AP₅₀:₉₅ of 64.3, outperforming the second-best OrientedFormer (63.2) by +1.1 and the best CNN-based method SAFPN+APCL (62.7) by +1.6. On FAIR1M-v1.0, it reaches AP₅₀ of 41.66, surpassing OrientedFormer (41.31) by +0.35 and DRNet (40.87) by +0.79. On FAIR1M-v2.0, it achieves the highest AP₅₀ of 47.53, exceeding DRNet (47.04) by +0.49 and PETDet (46.10) by +1.43.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines across all three datasets. On ShipRSImageNet, it beats the strongest DETR-based baseline OrientedFormer by +1.1 AP₅₀:₉₅ and the best CNN-based method SAFPN+APCL by +1.6, with particularly large margins over PCLDet (+2.7) and ReDet (+19.2). On FAIR1M-v1.0, it surpasses OrientedFormer by +0.35 AP₅₀ and DRNet by +0.79, while on FAIR1M-v2.0 it leads DRNet by +0.49 and PETDet by +1.43. Notably, both PCLDet and SAFPN+APCL employ contrastive learning with class prototypes but only at the fine-grained level; the paper's hierarchical extension across multiple levels yields consistent gains over these methods. The decoupled query strategy contributes +0.43 to +1.21 AP₅₀:₉₅ over the respective baselines (OrientedFormer and RHINO) before any contrastive loss is added, confirming that query separation alone improves detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR detection framework by integrating hierarchical label structures directly into the end-to-end architecture, departing from prior multi-stage approaches (e.g., Zhang et al. [31,33], Doan et al. [7]) that separated localization from hierarchical classification. The balanced hierarchical contrastive loss (BHCL) builds on supervised contrastive learning [12] and hierarchical contrastive learning [32], but introduces two key modifications: learnable class prototypes to ensure all hierarchy levels contribute equally per mini-batch, and gradient equilibration across negative classes. The decoupled query strategy—splitting DETR's shared object queries into separate classification and localization sets—is a novel architectural intervention not present in prior DETR variants like OrientedFormer [35] or RHINO [13].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hierarchical label structures, which are inherent to fine-grained remote sensing datasets but previously underutilized in detection, can meaningfully improve fine-grained discrimination when properly integrated into the representation learning space. A notable finding is that unbalanced hierarchical contrastive learning actually degrades performance on long-tail data (FAIR1M-v1.0), highlighting that the balancing mechanism is not optional but critical. Limitations include evaluation only on remote sensing datasets and two DETR backbones, and the 'Other' class reassignment strategy is dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the decoupled query strategy generalizes to other detection tasks (instance segmentation, multi-label classification) and non-remote-sensing domains.
