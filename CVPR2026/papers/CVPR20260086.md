## Problem

Consumer dToF LiDARs internally record full time-resolved photon histograms containing multi-bounce returns that encode non-line-of-sight (NLOS) cues about hidden objects, yet this signal is discarded in favor of a single depth value per pixel. Existing NLOS reconstruction methods rely on lab-grade scanning LiDARs with steered beams and high-timing-resolution detectors, making them incompatible with the coarse spatial/temporal resolution, flood illumination, and crosstalk of deployable consumer sensors. No large-scale real-world dataset exists to enable data-driven NLOS perception with these low-cost, widely deployed LiDARs.

## Input / Output

The input is a full LiDAR photon-arrival histogram tensor of shape (n, n, 128), where n ∈ {3, 8} is the spatial pixel grid and 128 is the number of discrete time-of-flight bins; this is the raw transient signal rather than a reduced depth estimate. The output depends on the task: a continuous 2D (x, y) planar position for localization, one of 30 discrete shape categories for classification, or a binary 4-inch vs. 8-inch size label. All three tasks share the same histogram input, and the very low spatial resolution (9 or 64 pixels total) means most spatial information is inherently limited.

## Method

The paper introduces DENALI, a 72,000-capture dataset of full-histogram LiDAR measurements from the ams TMF8828 flash dToF sensor, spanning 60 retroreflective objects (30 shapes × 2 sizes), 100 gantry positions, two lighting conditions, two spatial resolutions, and 3 repeated samples, with each scene paired to a Mitsuba 3 digital twin. For benchmarking, four architectures with increasing inductive bias are trained under supervised learning: a flat MLP, a 1D CNN over time bins, a 3D CNN over the spatiotemporal volume, and a Transformer treating each time bin as a token. The key empirical finding is that 1D and 3D CNNs consistently outperform the MLP and Transformer, yet the 3D CNN does not surpass the 1D CNN, indicating that current models struggle to exploit the limited spatial structure at 3×3 resolution. The digital twins further enable quantitative sim-to-real fidelity analysis (scaling, pulse-width, and noise calibration) and task-specific sensor design studies via simulated timing jitter.

## Results

On the DENALI dataset (3×3 LiDAR captures), the 1D CNN achieves the best overall NLOS localization RMSE of 0.0456 m (MAE 0.0324 m), substantially outperforming the MLP baseline (0.1045 m) and the Transformer (0.0579 m). For object shape classification among 30 categories, the 1D CNN reaches a Top-1 accuracy of 38.8% and Top-5 of 79.5%, while the 3D CNN achieves the highest Macro-F1 of 0.438. For size prediction (4-inch vs. 8-inch), the 1D CNN attains 94.7% accuracy, far exceeding the MLP (53.6%) and Transformer (87.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four architectures (MLP, 1D CNN, 3D CNN, Transformer) as inductive-bias ablations rather than comparing against external NLOS methods. The 1D CNN dominates on localization (RMSE 0.0456 m vs. 0.1045 m for MLP, a 2.3× improvement) and size prediction (94.7% vs. 53.6% for MLP), confirming that temporal convolutional structure is critical for exploiting histogram signal. The 3D CNN is competitive on localization (0.0475 m) and achieves the best Macro-F1 for classification (0.438 vs. 0.383 for 1D CNN), but its spatial inductive bias does not translate into a Top-1 advantage, suggesting the 3×3 spatial grid carries limited discriminative information. The Transformer, despite capturing long-range temporal dependencies via self-attention, underperforms both CNNs across all three tasks, indicating that local temporal structure is more informative than global attention for these low-resolution histograms. The MLP baseline, with no structural inductive bias, performs near chance on classification (6.7% Top-1) and at chance level on size prediction (53.6%), underscoring that raw histogram values without architectural priors are insufficient for NLOS perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of NLOS imaging and consumer LiDAR hardware. Prior NLOS work relies on scanning LiDARs with steered collimated beams and high-timing-resolution single-photon detectors in controlled lab settings, while recent low-cost LiDAR studies have explored depth sensing and isolated NLOS demonstrations at small scale. DENALI departs from the traditional NLOS reconstruction paradigm by reframing the problem as data-driven perception (localization, classification, size estimation) using full photon-arrival histograms from a consumer flash dToF sensor, and by providing the first large-scale real-world dataset (72,000 captures) purpose-built for this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DENALI establishes a practical benchmark showing that consumer LiDARs already deployed in phones and robots contain sufficient multi-bounce signal for NLOS perception, opening a path toward scalable, deployable NLOS vision without laboratory-grade hardware. Key open questions remain: the dataset is limited to retroreflective objects in controlled scenes with a single sensor model, and the identified sim-to-real gaps (pulse width, noise, jitter) and the failure of models to disentangle object, geometry, and lighting effects suggest that robust, generalizable NLOS perception with consumer LiDARs is still far from solved. Extending to unconstrained, dynamic real-world environments and multi-sensor settings is the natural next step.
