## Problem

Existing multimodal KV Cache compression methods rely on attention scores to select which KV pairs to retain, making them incompatible with efficient attention kernels like FlashAttention and ignoring the contribution of value vectors to the attention output. This paper asks whether KV Cache compression can be performed directly from the distributional properties of the KV matrices themselves, without any attention score computation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the full Key and Value tensors (each of shape N×D, where N is the sequence length and D is the hidden dimension) produced after the prefilling stage of an MLLM, where the input sequence interleaves visual tokens and text tokens. It outputs a compressed KV Cache in a single post-prefill operation, retaining a configurable fraction (e.g., 20%, 10%, 5%) of KV pairs per layer with dynamically allocated per-layer budgets, which is then used directly in the decoding stage with standard FlashAttention kernels.

## Method

FlashCache identifies critical KV pairs by exploiting the observation that frequency-domain energy of KV matrices is concentrated at low frequencies. The Outlier KV Recognition Module applies DCT to each layer's Key and Value tensors, retains only the dominant low-frequency components (controlled by a cutoff factor γ), and performs inverse DCT to obtain a smoothed 'Base KV'; KV pairs whose MSE deviation from Base KV is largest are designated as Outlier KVs and preferentially retained. The Dynamic Budget Allocation Module computes, per layer, the ratio of high-frequency (outlier) energy to total energy in the frequency domain, normalizes these ratios into weights, and allocates distinct per-layer retention quotas under a global cache budget, ensuring layers with richer outlier information receive more KV pairs. The entire pipeline is attention-score-free and training-free, requiring only a single DCT/IDCT pass over the KV matrices after prefilling.

## Results

On MileBench with KV Cache retention ratio ρ=0.2, FlashCache achieves near-full-cache performance across all three models: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B it scores 26.72 on Needle-in-a-Haystack (vs. 27.35 full cache) and 15.50 on Image Retrieval (vs. 14.17 full cache), while on Qwen2.5-VL-32B it even exceeds full cache on Task T (57.30 vs. 56.51), Task S (66.21 vs. 65.21), and IR (19.50 vs. 18.17). Under extreme compression (ρ=0.05) on HR-Bench, FlashCache reaches 72.38 accuracy, surpassing the full-cache baseline of 70.75. The method delivers up to 1.69× faster decoding with 80% lower KV memory usage, and its DCT-based overhead is only 1.66 ms at 2K input length (vs. 16.6 ms for MEDA and 6.93 ms for LOOK-M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashCache decisively outperforms all five baselines on the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NH) task, which is the most sensitive to KV eviction: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B it scores 26.72 vs. SnapKV (13.59), H2O (12.66), LOOK-M (11.88), MEDA (9.07), and StreamingLLM (9.69) — roughly 2× the best baseline. On Qwen2.5-VL-32B the gap widens further, with FlashCache at 24.69 versus SnapKV at 22.34 and LOOK-M/MEDA collapsing to 5.78/5.16. On Image Retrieval, FlashCache (15.50 on 7B; 19.50 on 32B) exceeds both the full-cache baseline and all competitors, while on Task S it matches or slightly surpasses full cache. Under extreme compression (ρ=0.05) on MUIRBench, H2O, LOOK-M, and MEDA all hit out-of-memory errors, whereas FlashCache retains 44.42 accuracy (vs. 45.54 full cache). On FAVOR-Bench video tasks at ρ=0.05, FlashCache achieves 30.71 overall accuracy versus 29.95 (SnapKV, the best baseline) and 22.83 (MEDA), confirming its advantage in long video contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of attention-score-based KV cache eviction (LOOK-M, MEDA, SnapKV, H2O) by arguing that attention scores are both incompatible with FlashAttention kernels and blind to the Value matrix's contribution to the output. Instead, it repurposes Discrete Cosine Transform—a standard tool from image compression (JPEG)—to analyze the frequency-domain energy distribution of KV matrices, drawing a conceptual analogy to the quantization literature where outliers disproportionately affect model quality. The Dynamic Budget Allocation Module extends the per-layer budgeting idea from PyramidKV and MEDA but grounds the allocation criterion in frequency-domain outlier energy ratios rather than attention entropy or information funneling heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashCache's FlashAttention compatibility is practically significant: it removes the need to materialize full attention score matrices, which is a real deployment bottleneck for long-context MLLMs. The 1.69× decoding speedup with 80% KV memory reduction while matching or exceeding full-cache accuracy on several benchmarks (e.g., V*, HR-Bench) makes it a strong candidate for production multimodal inference pipelines. Open questions include whether the DCT-based low-pass filter is truly optimal versus other transforms (e.g., wavelets, FFT), whether the frequency-domain outlier phenomenon generalizes to audio or 3D modalities, and how the method behaves under extreme compression (ρ < 0.05) where the paper's evidence is thinner.
