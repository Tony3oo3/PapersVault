## Problem

Existing adversarial fine-tuning methods for VLMs (e.g., TeCoA, PMG, FARE) align image embeddings only with base-class (leaf) text prompts, ignoring the hierarchical structure of class space. The paper demonstrates that this fixation on leaf classes causes robustness to fail to generalize to superclasses: models fine-tuned on base-class adversaries show poor superclass classification accuracy, and superclass-level adversaries transfer effectively to attack base classes. The gap is a principled way to exploit class hierarchies to produce more general adversarial perturbations that robustify across multiple levels of abstraction simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and text prompts constructed at multiple hierarchical levels (leaf class through root, up to depth L=5 for ImageNet via WordNet, or LLM-generated superclasses for other datasets) as input. For each image, the model produces a class label via zero-shot inference using the standard CLIP image-text alignment protocol. Extensions to BLIP and CoCa additionally produce retrieval rankings (Flickr30k) and image captions (NoCaps), while medical CLIP extensions produce multi-label disease AUC scores on chest X-ray datasets. The model is fine-tuned on ImageNet training data and evaluated zero-shot on 15 classification datasets plus retrieval, captioning, and medical benchmarks.

## Method

The core mechanism embeds class hierarchies into the Poincaré ball via exponential maps applied to CLIP's Euclidean embeddings, where the ℓ2 norm of each embedding encodes its hierarchical depth (parents closer to the origin, children closer to the boundary). Superclass image embeddings are constructed by hyperbolic averaging (HypAvg) of base-class image embeddings within a mini-batch, recursively across levels, since no single image represents an entire superclass. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 1) is that the maximum viable margin size in the hyperbolic classifier grows unboundedly as the embedding norm approaches 1/√r, unlike the Euclidean classifier's finite margin; thus different hierarchy levels naturally realize different margin sizes in (0,∞), making adversarial perturbations generated against the multi-level classifier more universal. The training loss (HITA) sums weighted cross-entropy across all hierarchy levels with a hierarchy-aware negative set augmentation from adjacent levels, plus auxiliary penalties enforcing intra-class vicinity and hierarchical norm ordering. Adversarial perturbations are generated jointly on both image and text modalities via PGD, and the text encoder's projection layer is also optimized to enable better hierarchical alignment.

## Results

On the primary evaluation (PGD-20, ε=1/255, 15-dataset average), the proposed method achieves 44.34% robust accuracy and 62.14% clean accuracy, outperforming the strongest prior adversarial fine-tuning method AoS (43.88% / 61.70%) and narrowing the clean-accuracy gap with unmodified CLIP (64.90%) to just 2.8%. With 5 hierarchical trees, robust accuracy further improves to 45.39% while clean accuracy reaches 62.49%. On ImageNet specifically, the method attains 47.81% robust accuracy (vs. 47.27% for AoS and 41.48% for TeCoA) and 61.19% clean accuracy, surpassing all adversarial fine-tuning baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four adversarial fine-tuning baselines on both clean and robust accuracy across the 15-dataset average. Against the strongest prior method AoS [14], the gain is modest but consistent: +0.46% robust (44.34 vs. 43.88) and +0.44% clean (62.14 vs. 61.70), widening to +1.51% robust and +0.79% clean with 5 trees. Against TeCoA [32], the improvement is substantial: +5.43% robust and +9.52% clean. PMG-FT [42] is outperformed by +4.62% robust and +4.78% clean, while FARE [38] trails by +6.41% robust and +2.47% clean. Compared to unmodified CLIP [36], the method sacrifices only 2.76% clean accuracy (62.14 vs. 64.90) while gaining a massive +37.13% in robust accuracy (44.34 vs. 7.21), demonstrating that hierarchical adversarial fine-tuning preserves natural performance far better than prior approaches (e.g., TeCoA loses 12.28% clean accuracy relative to CLIP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial fine-tuning line of work for zero-shot VLMs (TeCoA, PMG-FT, FARE, AoS) by introducing hierarchical structure into the image-text alignment objective. Where prior methods treat the class space as flat—aligning each image embedding to a single base-class text prompt—this work embeds the full class hierarchy (e.g., WordNet hypernym paths) into a Poincaré ball, using hyperbolic averaging to construct superclass image embeddings from base-class samples. The theoretical contribution connects the ℓ₂-norm of embeddings in the Poincaré ball to the maximum viable classifier margin, showing that hyperbolic classifiers can realize unbounded margins (unlike Euclidean classifiers), which motivates multi-level adversarial training for more general perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underexplored vulnerability: adversarial fine-tuning methods that focus exclusively on base classes lose robustness when attacked at the superclass level, and superclass-level adversaries transfer effectively to base classes. This motivates hierarchical robustness as a meaningful evaluation axis beyond standard leaf-class PGD. Limitations include dependence on hierarchical taxonomies (WordNet for ImageNet, LLM-generated superclasses elsewhere), which introduces a quality bottleneck and potential semantic noise. Open questions include how the approach scales to very deep or ambiguous hierarchies, whether the LLM-generated superclasses for non-ImageNet datasets are stable across model versions, and whether the hyperbolic margin advantage persists at much larger model scales (ViT-H, ViT-G).
