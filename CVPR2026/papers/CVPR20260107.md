## Problem

Existing large-scale dataset distillation methods for ImageNet-1K (e.g., SRe2L, CDA, G-VBSM) rely on matching batch normalization statistics between real and synthetic data, which theoretically only aligns gradient scales while ignoring gradient directions. This causes severe performance degradation under hard labels, where synthetic datasets perform worse than randomly selected subsets. The paper identifies that gradient direction, not scale, is the critical factor for model optimization, especially under data-limited settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes the full ImageNet-1K dataset (1.28M RGB images, 1000 classes) and a pre-trained ResNet-18 as input, pre-computing the orthogonal gradient directions (singular vectors) of the entire real dataset for each convolutional layer. It produces a synthetic dataset of 10, 50, or 100 images per class (IPC), where each image is a 224×224 RGB tensor initialized from RDED and then optimized. The synthetic dataset supports both soft-label (teacher logits) and hard-label (one-hot) training paradigms, with the hard-label setting being the primary focus.

## Method

OGM aligns the intrinsic direction of gradients by orthogonalizing them via SVD (setting all singular values to one) and minimizing the Frobenius distance between the resulting UV^T matrices of real and synthetic data. To avoid costly backpropagation, OGM replaces true gradients with a closed-form pseudo-gradient derived from the least-squares loss, computed as (1/nhw)·X_out·X_in^T − avg(X_in^T) in the forward pass. The framework follows a local-to-local strategy: the real dataset's gradient direction is pre-computed once, and each synthetic batch is optimized to match it, combined with a classification loss. Practical components include EMA updates across batches for diversity, RDED-based initialization to preserve realism, and patch-level augmentation to prevent overfitting. At evaluation, the Muon optimizer (which uses the same UV^T update rule) further amplifies OGM's advantage by exploiting the matched gradient directions.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with IPC=10, OGM achieves 39.4% top-1 accuracy with soft labels and 11.8% with hard labels under the standard Adam evaluation protocol. When evaluated with the Muon optimizer (which exploits gradient direction), OGM reaches 47.0% (soft) and 16.7% (hard), outperforming all training-based DD methods by +9.2% and +4.7% respectively. With IPC=50, OGM achieves 57.5% (soft) and 31.2% (hard), and with IPC=100 it reaches 61.5% (soft) and 39.5% (hard), consistently leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With hard labels (IPC=10), OGM (11.8%) dramatically outperforms all training-based BN-matching methods: SRe2L (1.5%), CDA (1.6%), G-VBSM (1.6%), LPLD (3.4%), and DWA (1.9%), by margins of 10+ points. OGM also surpasses the best training-free method RDED (11.5%) by 0.3%, and Random (4.6%) by 7.2 points. With soft labels (IPC=10), OGM (39.4%) leads RDED (38.4%) and DWA (37.9%), while all other training-based methods cluster near the Random baseline (35.8%), confirming that BN matching provides little benefit when soft labels are available. Cross-architecture evaluation (Tables 4–5) shows OGM consistently outperforms RDED across ResNet-50/101, EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV2, and ConvNext-Tiny under both label settings. The Muon optimizer ablation (Table 6) reveals that OGM benefits most from direction-aware optimization, gaining +7.7% (soft) and +4.9% (hard) over Adam, while also improving all baselines, further validating the gradient-direction hypothesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant BN-matching paradigm for ImageNet-scale dataset distillation (SRe2L, CDA, G-VBSM, DWA, LPLD) by showing theoretically that matching batch normalization statistics only aligns gradient scale while ignoring gradient direction. It also moves beyond classic gradient matching (DC) by replacing cosine distance with SVD-based orthogonalization, directly borrowing the Muon optimizer's mechanism of setting singular values to one and matching singular vectors. The 'local-to-local' distillation framework from SRe2L is retained as the training structure, but the matching objective is fundamentally redefined around intrinsic gradient geometry rather than statistical moments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that BN matching fails catastrophically under hard labels (1.5–3.4% vs. 4.6% for random) challenges the foundational assumption of the entire SRe2L lineage and suggests that prior ImageNet-scale DD methods were implicitly dependent on soft-label knowledge distillation rather than learning optimization-relevant structure. The Muon optimizer result (Table 6) is broadly applicable: it improves every DD method by 5–10 points, indicating the field has been underestimating the role of the evaluation optimizer. Open questions include whether the SVD-based matching generalizes to non-CNN architectures (transformers, attention layers), whether the Newton-Schulz approximation preserves the direction-matching benefit at scale, and whether the connection between optimization dynamics and dataset efficiency can be exploited more directly to guide synthetic data generation.
