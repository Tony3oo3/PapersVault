## Problem

Event camera data collection is expensive and limited in scale, and existing video-to-event simulators require costly video capture with constrained scalability across viewpoints, motions, and lighting. Prior text-to-event approaches demand large text-event pair corpora for training, limiting their generalizability. A training-free, general-purpose text-to-event simulation method that produces high-fidelity asynchronous events from simple text prompts remains unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a free-form text prompt describing a visual scene (e.g., 'a large shark swimming underwater in the ocean') and outputs an asynchronous event stream consisting of individual events e_i = (x_i, t_i, p_i) with pixel coordinates, microsecond-level timestamps, and polarity signs, as well as binned event frames. The intermediate video is rendered at 16–24 FPS by the chosen MLLM and then adaptively interpolated to higher temporal resolution before event extraction. The system is plug-and-play, compatible with multiple video generators (Cosmos, Wan, CogVideoX, Open-Sora) and real RGB camera inputs.

## Method

Texvent is a training-free two-stage pipeline: (1) a multimodal large language model (Cosmos-1.0-Diffusion-7B) renders a prompt-driven video, which is then upsampled via a brightness-aware interpolation strategy that only invokes RIFE when the log-brightness difference between consecutive frames exceeds the contrast threshold δ, avoiding redundant optical-flow-based interpolation; (2) a physics-based event simulator converts the high-frame-rate video into events using three key mechanisms: a balanced logarithmic intensity comparison (adding a balancing parameter α to the log function to equalize activation sensitivity across low- and high-light conditions), a cache-based voltage refreshment mechanism (a brightness cache κ that updates the reference brightness only at pixels where an event fires, mimicking the event camera circuit and preventing event loss from frequent reference resets), and adaptive Poisson background-activity noise injection targeted at low-light, low-contrast regions. Dense microsecond timestamps are reconstructed by normalizing per-pixel brightness variation rates within each frame pair.

## Results

On the Event Camera Dataset and DSEC, Texvent achieves the best event-frame MSE (0.045) and LPIPS (0.339), and the best reconstructed-image SSIM (0.472) and LPIPS (0.296) among all six simulators. On the NT-ImageNet text-to-event benchmark, Texvent attains the highest Event Quality Score (EQS) of 0.8851, surpassing the next-best SENPI (0.8824) by 2.7%. In terms of efficiency, Texvent processes each frame pair in 0.0653 s, making it the second-fastest simulator (after SENPI at 0.0573 s) and roughly 32× faster than VID2E (2.1228 s) and V2E (2.1652 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Texvent outperforms all five baselines on the headline EQS metric (0.8851 vs. SENPI 0.8824, V2CE 0.8642, VID2E 0.8597, DVS-Voltmeter 0.8573, V2E 0.8138) and on event-frame MSE (0.045 vs. next-best V2CE 0.082) and LPIPS (0.339 vs. next-best V2CE 0.383). However, V2CE achieves a higher event-frame SSIM (0.552 vs. 0.488), and DVS-Voltmeter edges out Texvent on reconstructed-image MSE (0.096 vs. 0.116), though DVS-Voltmeter's event-frame quality is far inferior (SSIM 0.085, LPIPS 0.972). In runtime, SENPI is marginally faster (0.0573 s vs. 0.0653 s per frame pair), while VID2E and V2E are roughly 32–33× slower due to their reliance on bidirectional optical flow for frame interpolation. Overall, Texvent delivers the best balance of simulation fidelity, reconstruction quality, and computational efficiency, with no single baseline dominating across all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the video-to-event simulation lineage (ESIM, VID2E, V2E, V2CE, DVS-Voltmeter) by prepending a text-to-video generation stage powered by multimodal large language models (Cosmos, Wan, CogVideoX, Open-Sora), thereby eliminating the need for physical video capture. The event simulator itself departs from prior work in two specific ways: a balancing parameter α that corrects the logarithmic intensity comparison's unfair sensitivity between low- and high-light conditions, and a brightness cache κ that mimics the event camera's voltage-refresh behavior to prevent event loss. Unlike the only prior text-to-event method (Ott et al., 2024), which required training a dedicated autoencoder on labeled text-event pairs, Texvent is entirely training-free, repurposing off-the-shelf video generators as a drop-in front-end.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Event camera data remains expensive and labor-intensive to collect, and this work offers a scalable, hardware-free alternative that can generate diverse event streams from arbitrary text descriptions, potentially unblocking data-hungry event-based learning tasks. The plug-and-play design means the simulator can be paired with any future video generation model, and the introduced NT-ImageNet dataset (5,000 text-event pairs) provides the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating text-to-event fidelity. Open questions include how well models trained on Texvent-generated events transfer to real event data at scale, whether the sim-to-real gap narrows further with higher-fidelity video generators, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-sensor or stereo event configurations.
