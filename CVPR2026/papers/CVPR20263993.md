## Problem

Existing negative-label (NL) based OOD detection methods generate negative labels without understanding actual OOD images, placing them far from the OOD distribution in feature space. They also struggle with near-OOD detection (where OOD samples resemble a subset of ID classes) and require prior knowledge of whether the task is near-OOD or far-OOD, limiting adaptability in open environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263993/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB test images encoded by CLIP ViT-B/16, a streaming cache of historical test images, and the ID label set (e.g., 1000 ImageNet classes). At test time, the MLLM (LLaVA-1.5-7B) is invoked selectively on a small subset of cached images to generate negative sentences and labels, while the majority of test images are scored solely by the CLIP text encoder. The output is a single text-only OOD score S_ada(v) that avoids the image-text modality gap, yielding an ID/OOD classification via a threshold.

## Method

ANTS shapes two complementary negative textual spaces at test time using MLLM understanding and reasoning. First, it mines likely-OOD images from historical test data using an adaptive threshold on NegLabel scores, then prompts the MLLM to describe these images, producing Expressive Negative Sentences (ENS) that capture fine-grained OOD characteristics for far-OOD detection. Second, it identifies the subset of ID classes most similar to the OOD images (via classification frequency) and uses MLLM reasoning to generate Visually Similar Negative Labels (VSNL) only for that subset, reducing false negatives in near-OOD detection. An adaptive weighted score S_ada = λ·S_ens + (1−λ)·S_vsnl dynamically balances the two spaces: λ is computed from the relative mean scores of ENS and VSNL on the cached negative images, automatically prioritizing ENS for far-OOD and VSNL for near-OOD without requiring prior knowledge of the task setting. The entire pipeline is training-free, zero-shot, and requires no auxiliary outlier datasets.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1k benchmark (ViT-B/16), ANTS achieves an average FPR95 of 11.20% and AUROC of 97.75% across four OOD datasets (iNaturalist, SUN, Places, Textures), reducing FPR95 by 3.1% over the prior best zero-shot method. On the OpenOOD benchmark, ANTS achieves 60.98% FPR95 on near-OOD (SSB-hard, NINCO) and 15.38% FPR95 on far-OOD (iNaturalist, Textures, OpenImage-O), with AUROC of 82.15% and 96.50% respectively. The method is training-free and zero-shot, requiring no auxiliary outlier images or fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANTS outperforms all 18 compared baselines on the ImageNet-1k benchmark. Among zero-shot NL-based methods, ANTS reduces average FPR95 from 25.40% (NegLabel) and 30.09% (EOE) to 11.20%, and beats the closest zero-shot competitor AdaNeg (18.92%) by 7.72 points. Against the best training-required method SynOOD (14.27% FPR95), ANTS achieves a 3.07-point improvement while requiring no training at all. On the OpenOOD near-OOD benchmark, ANTS achieves 60.98% FPR95, outperforming NegLabel (68.18%), EOE (82.93%), and AdaNeg (67.51%), with the largest gap over EOE (21.95 points) due to its targeted VSNL generation for only the relevant ID subset. On far-OOD, ANTS (15.38% FPR95) edges out SynOOD (17.11%) and AdaNeg (17.31%). The method also generalizes across smaller ID datasets (CUB, Stanford Cars, Food-101, Oxford Pet), consistently improving FPR95 over NegLabel by 0.12–1.68 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANTS extends the negative-label (NL) paradigm for OOD detection established by NegLabel [19] and EOE [4], which construct a negative textual space to contrast against ID labels. It departs from NegLabel by replacing static, semantically distant words with expressive negative sentences generated by an MLLM that has actually seen (and described) likely-OOD images, and from EOE by restricting visually similar label generation to a mined subset of ID classes rather than all classes, thereby reducing false negatives. The key conceptual departure is the use of MLLM test-time understanding and reasoning—rather than purely textual LLM prompting—to shape the negative space, combined with an adaptive weighted score that eliminates the need to pre-specify whether the deployment scenario is near-OOD or far-OOD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being training-free, zero-shot, and requiring no auxiliary outlier data, ANTS offers a practical path to strong OOD detection in open-world settings where the OOD distribution is unknown and may shift over time. The adaptive weighting mechanism that automatically balances far-OOD and near-OOD detection without prior knowledge of the task setting addresses a real deployment concern that prior NL-based methods sidestepped. A notable limitation is the GPU memory cost of invoking an MLLM during testing, which the authors acknowledge as a direction for future work; more efficient MLLM utilization (e.g., distillation, smaller models, or selective invocation) remains an open question. The approach also raises the broader question of how far test-time multimodal reasoning can push OOD detection before hitting diminishing returns or hallucination-induced noise.
