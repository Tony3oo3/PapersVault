## Problem

Existing user-behavior-based personalized image generation methods rely on auxiliary adapters that scale with encoder complexity, large-scale LLMs for profiling, or model-specific designs, jointly restricting creativity, diversity, and generalization across foundation models. A key unresolved challenge is integrating user preferences into the conditioning process while preserving target query expressiveness, without incurring additional training structures or fine-tuning costs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FAN consumes a target text prompt, a set of historical reference prompts with associated preference intensities (e.g., ratings from MovieLens), and a personalization degree α, all processed through pretrained foundation encoders (OpenCLIP ViT-L/bigG or Google T5) without any additional parameters. The output is a personalized RGB image generated by a foundation T2I model (Stable Diffusion V1/XL/V3 or FLUX) that balances target fidelity with user preferences. The same encoder-level mechanism extends without modification to CLIP retrieval, unCLIP image reconstruction, and vision-language model conditioning (e.g., LLaVA).

## Method

FAN achieves training-free personalization by reconstructing the self-attention mechanism of transformer-based foundation encoders. Tailored profiling retrieves a diverse subset of references relevant to the target query using a two-stage ranking (cosine similarity weighted by preference intensities) followed by k-center greedy over token-level mean conditions, building on the MMR principle. Personalized Attention (PA) then replaces self-attention in selected encoder layers with a fused cross-attention that combines target and reference queries via independent softmax operations per condition, weighted by preference intensities and a personalization degree α, producing a personalized representation that is projected back through the original value vectors. Conditioning optimization uses the target query itself as the personalized (unconditional) query rather than a separate unconditional prompt, and PA is applied selectively to specific layers to balance personalization strength against target fidelity. A lightweight two-layer perceptron detects the dynamically shifting class token position in OpenCLIP encoders, stabilizing personalization without retraining.

## Results

FAN achieves the highest CLIP score across nearly all model–dataset combinations: 25.47 (Target) / 15.24 (History) on SD V1+PIP (+32.03% over baseline), 27.72 / 18.08 on SD XL+PIP (+25.88%), and 38.03 / 10.11 on SD V3+ML (+2.22%). On Text alignment, FAN preserves target fidelity at 95.94% (PIP) and 97.00% (ML) with OpenCLIP ViT-L, a +4.20% and +5.31% improvement over the unpersonalized baseline, while TV and PMG degrade by over 20%. FAN is the only method that consistently improves over the unpersonalized baseline across all six SD V1/XL/V3 × PIP/ML settings, whereas TV, PMG, and DrUM show negative improvements in several configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAN outperforms TV and PMG on every available setting for both CLIP score and Text alignment; TV degrades target fidelity by 22–23% on Text align, and PMG drops by 21–24%, while FAN maintains 93–97% target alignment. Against DrUM (the closest competitor, which uses trained adapters), FAN wins on CLIP score in 5 of 6 settings—most dramatically on SD V3+ML where FAN reaches 38.03 vs. DrUM's 30.17 (a 7.86-point gap)—but DrUM edges ahead on Text align in PIP (96.42 vs. 95.94 for ViT-L) due to its cosine-similarity-trained adapters. FABRIC is only evaluated on SD V1 and is limited to a single reference image pair, making it inapplicable to SD XL/V3. Critically, FAN is the only method that achieves positive improvement over the unpersonalized baseline across all six model–dataset combinations, whereas TV, PMG, and DrUM all show negative improvements in at least one setting (e.g., DrUM drops 5.60% on SD V3+ML). FAN accomplishes this without any additional parameters, adapters, or fine-tuning, whereas DrUM requires adapters proportional to encoder size and TV/PMG rely on external LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAN extends the condition-level personalization line initiated by the authors' own DrUM (ICCV 2025), which required trained adapters proportional to encoder scale, by instead reconstructing the self-attention mechanism of pretrained transformer encoders to inject preference representations. The tailored profiling component repurposes classical information-retrieval tools—maximal marginal relevance, two-stage ranking, and k-center greedy—into a user-interest retrieval pipeline, departing from the LLM-based prompt rewriting (TV, PMG) and attention-level image-pair guidance (FABRIC) that dominate prior work. Conceptually, FAN positions personalization as an encoder-internal operation rather than an external module, eliminating the adapter/LLM dependency that has characterized the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that FAN requires zero additional parameters or training, making it deployable across any transformer-based encoder (OpenCLIP, T5) and downstream application (T2I diffusion, CLIP retrieval, unCLIP, VLMs) without retraining—addressing the scalability bottleneck of adapter-based personalization as foundation models grow. However, performance gains over the closest baseline (DrUM) are modest and sometimes slightly negative on specific metrics, and the approach cannot directly inject subject-level objects, limiting it to style/attribute-level personalization. Open questions include whether the class-token detection hack generalizes beyond OpenCLIP, whether the method scales to non-transformer encoders, and how to combine FAN with subject-driven modules like IP-Adapter for full personalization.
