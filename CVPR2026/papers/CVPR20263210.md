## Problem

Prior privacy-preserving ReID (PP-ReID) work is confined to single-modality visible scenarios, yet 24-hour surveillance demands robust cross-modal visible-infrared (VI) retrieval. Extending PP-ReID to the VI setting exposes two compounding failures: (1) crude 'one-size-fits-all' anonymization destroys identity-critical fine-grained features and body contours, and (2) anonymization distorts color textures in visible images while blurring thermal contours in infrared images, creating an asymmetric 'Mixed Gap' that standard cross-modal alignment methods cannot bridge.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes paired visible (RGB) and infrared (IR) pedestrian images resized to 256×128 pixels, along with a frozen YOLOv8n-pose model that supplies binary keypoint guidance maps (circular regions of radius R=5 around detected joints). It produces anonymized versions of both modalities via a UNet-based dual-stream generator, and a shared ResNet-50 backbone extracts features from both raw and anonymized images to yield cross-modal ReID rankings. Anonymization quality is verified by SSIM and PSNR against the raw images (thresholds SSIM < 0.5, PSNR < 15 for visual indistinguishability), while retrieval is scored by Rank-k, mAP, and mINP under four query/gallery conditions (Raw-to-Raw, Raw-to-Anon, Anon-to-Raw, Anon-to-Anon).

## Method

PPA couples a UNet dual-stream generator (adversarially trained against per-modality discriminators with mosaic/blur/noise-initialized targets) and a ResNet-50 ReID backbone (distilled from a frozen AGW teacher) with two novel modules. The Keypoint-Preserving Regularization (KPR) module uses a frozen YOLOv8n-pose model to build binary guidance maps around body joints, then trains a lightweight convolutional mask head to predict those regions from the anonymized output; the resulting MSE loss constrains the generator to preserve structural integrity at identity-critical keypoints while still obfuscating appearance. The Differential Consistency-guided Modality Alignment (DCMA) module defines per-modality anonymization difference vectors (raw feature minus anonymized feature) and minimizes their L2 distance across modalities, thereby treating the typically inconsistent anonymization perturbation as a single learnable offset and provably bounding the cross-modal alignment error via the triangle inequality. The full objective jointly optimizes adversarial and reconstruction losses for the generator, cross-entropy and triplet losses for identity discrimination, cross-modal distillation and raw-to-anonymized alignment losses for the backbone, plus the KPR and DCMA terms with hyperparameters δ=1.0 and η=0.1.

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (all-search, mosaic initialization), PPA achieves 60.7% Rank-1 and 58.8% mAP in the Raw-to-Raw setting, surpassing SecureReID (35.6% / 37.3%) by 25.1 points in Rank-1. In the most challenging Anon-to-Anon setting, PPA reaches 46.2% Rank-1 versus SecureReID's 32.2% (+14.0). On RegDB (infrared-to-visible, mosaic), the Raw-to-Raw gap widens to 34.6 points (80.5% vs. 45.9% Rank-1), while Anon-to-Anon still yields an 11.4-point improvement (39.7% vs. 28.3%). Anonymization quality (SSIM < 0.5, PSNR < 15) is maintained or improved over SecureReID across all settings, confirming that the performance gains do not come at the cost of weaker privacy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is SecureReID [44], the state-of-the-art single-modality PP-ReID method, adapted here to the cross-modal VI setting using an AGW backbone and a single-stream adversarial network. PPA outperforms SecureReID in every tested condition (Raw-to-Raw, Raw-to-Anon, Anon-to-Raw, Anon-to-Anon) on both SYSU-MM01 and RegDB, across all three initialization strategies (mosaic, blurring, Gaussian noise). The largest absolute gains appear in the Raw-to-Raw setting (+25.1 Rank-1 on SYSU-MM01, +34.6 on RegDB), indicating that the KPR and DCMA modules substantially reduce the information destruction and cross-modal misalignment that plague the baseline. Even in the hardest Anon-to-Anon scenario, PPA maintains a 14.0-point (SYSU-MM01) and 11.4-point (RegDB) Rank-1 advantage, demonstrating robustness when both modality and anonymization gaps are simultaneously present. On privacy quality, PPA's generated images achieve lower SSIM and PSNR than SecureReID in most settings (e.g., 0.08 vs. 0.13 SSIM for visible images under mosaic on SYSU-MM01), meaning the anonymization is at least as strong while ReID accuracy is far higher.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the privacy-preserving person ReID (PP-ReID) paradigm introduced by SecureReID [44]—which handles anonymization in single-modality visible settings—into the cross-modal visible-infrared (VI) domain, a combination neither prior line of work addresses. Methodologically, it builds on the UNet-based adversarial generation framework of image-to-image translation [10] and the AGW cross-modal distillation backbone [42], while introducing two novel regularizers: a pose-guided keypoint mask (KPR) that constrains where anonymization can act, and a differential consistency loss (DCMA) that treats anonymization-induced feature shifts as a learnable cross-modal offset rather than unstructured noise. The DCMA formulation is conceptually adjacent to domain-adaptation alignment losses but is specifically designed to handle the asymmetric distortion that anonymization introduces differently in RGB versus thermal imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing PP-VI-ReID as a distinct task and providing the first baseline, this work opens a practically important direction for 24-hour surveillance systems that must balance privacy with cross-modal retrieval. The 'Mixed Gap' framing (modality gap compounded by asymmetric anonymization distortion) is a useful diagnostic that clarifies why standard shared-space alignment methods fail in this setting. However, the evaluation is limited to a single adapted baseline (SecureReID) and two datasets, and the anonymization quality metrics (SSIM < 0.3, PSNR < 11) suggest the generated images remain heavily distorted, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more subtle privacy constraints or additional modalities (e.g., millimeter-wave, LiDAR). A key open question is whether the pose-based structural prior can be replaced by learned, task-specific preservation signals that adapt to different anonymization strengths.
