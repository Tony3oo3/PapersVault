## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D instance segmentation methods that lift SAM 2D masks to 3D process each frame independently and rely solely on 2D metrics (e.g., SAM prediction scores) to merge masks, ignoring multi-view correlations and 3D geometric priors. This produces cross-view inconsistent 2D masks that fragment into incoherent 3D instances. The paper addresses how to enforce multi-view mask consistency by explicitly incorporating 3D structure into the mask matching process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263865/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (N_p × 3) and a sequence of T RGB-D frames with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, sampling only a subset of views (10% for ScanNetV2/200, 5% for ScanNet++) to balance performance and efficiency. It produces per-instance 3D segmentation masks over the point cloud, evaluated in both class-agnostic and open-vocabulary semantic settings. Internally, the pipeline generates intermediate coarse and refined 2D segmentation maps per frame, which are used to guide superpoint merging and boundary refinement in 3D space.

## Method

MV3DIS is a coarse-to-fine, training-free pipeline that first over-segments the point cloud into superpoints via graph-cut, then uses SAM (or Grounded-SAM/SAM2) to produce per-frame 2D masks merged into coarse segmentation maps. The key novelty is a 3D-guided mask matching strategy: coarse 3D segments are projected onto each frame to serve as a common geometric reference, and 2D masks across views are matched using frame-visibility and mask-visibility thresholds; their consistency is quantified via cosine similarity of 3D coverage distribution vectors (weighted by a depth consistency score that penalizes occluded projections). Consistent masks are selected via NMS on consistency scores and merged into refined 2D maps. Finally, a weighted affinity graph over superpoints (using depth-consistency and visibility weights per frame) drives region-growing merging, followed by an iterative boundary refinement step that reassigns superpoints to the highest-affinity neighboring region using the refined 2D maps.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, MV3DIS achieves 38.5 mAP, 60.2 AP50, and 76.2 AP25 in class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation, outperforming the previous zero-shot SOTA (SAM2Object) by 4.5 mAP. On ScanNet200, it reaches 54.7 AP50 and 69.7 AP25, surpassing the fully supervised Mask3D (51.2 AP50, 57.1 AP25). On the more challenging ScanNet++ dataset, MV3DIS attains 22.0 mAP, 36.7 AP50, and 51.7 AP25, leading all open-vocabulary baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV3DIS consistently outperforms all 13 compared baselines across the three primary benchmarks. Against the previous zero-shot SOTA SAM2Object [68], it gains 4.5 mAP (38.5 vs 34.0) and 7.5 AP50 (60.2 vs 52.7) on ScanNetV2, and 1.8 mAP on ScanNet++. Notably, MV3DIS surpasses the fully supervised Mask3D [46] on ScanNet200 in both AP50 (54.7 vs 51.2) and AP25 (69.7 vs 57.1), demonstrating that zero-shot 2D foundation model guidance can exceed supervised 3D training in open-vocabulary settings. Among SAM-based methods, SAI3D [65] (30.8 mAP) and Open3DIS [37] (31.5 mAP) are the strongest prior open-vocabulary baselines on ScanNetV2, both trailing MV3DIS by 7–8 mAP. In the semantic instance segmentation setting on ScanNet200, MV3DIS achieves 15.5 mAP (single-label) and 20.5 mAP (multi-label), outperforming SAM2Object (13.3 mAP) and Open3DIS (18.2 mAP), with a particularly strong 18.9 AP on tail classes versus 14.9 for OpenMask3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV3DIS extends the SAM-to-3D lifting paradigm established by SAM3D and SAI3D, which use per-frame 2D masks to guide superpoint merging into 3D instances. The key departure is that prior methods process each frame independently and rely solely on 2D metrics (e.g., SAM IoU scores) for mask consolidation, producing cross-view inconsistencies that fragment 3D segments. MV3DIS introduces a 3D-guided mask matching strategy that projects coarse 3D segments onto all views as a shared geometric reference, then scores mask consistency via 3D coverage distributions, and adds a depth consistency weight to suppress occlusion-induced projection errors. This contrasts with tracking-based approaches like SAM2Object, which enforce view consistency through SAM2's video tracking but are limited to continuous sequences and susceptible to motion blur.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-view inconsistency problem is a fundamental bottleneck in lifting 2D foundation model outputs to 3D, and MV3DIS addresses it without requiring video tracking or temporal continuity, making it applicable to sparse multi-view captures common in 3D scanning. The depth consistency weighting is a simple but effective mechanism that practitioners can adopt in other 3D-to-2D projection pipelines. However, the method remains dependent on SAM's 2D segmentation quality, is evaluated primarily on indoor scenes, and the coarse-to-fine pipeline introduces multiple hyperparameters (α, τ_merge, τ_f, τ_m) that may require tuning per dataset. Open questions include whether the 3D-guided matching strategy generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes and whether the approach can be extended to open-vocabulary semantic segmentation without the current reliance on CLIP-based label assignment.
