## Problem

Supervised open-loop imitation learning for multi-agent traffic simulation fails to handle distribution shift from error accumulation in closed-loop deployment and cannot directly enforce traffic-rule and physics alignment. The official realism meta-metric (RMM) used by the Waymo Open Simulation Challenge is inherently sparse (computed over 32 rollouts as a single scalar), making it impractical as a direct RL reward. Additionally, learned simulators lack a mechanism to distill goal-conditioned controllability while preserving realism.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a Contextual MDP state comprising tokenized agent trajectory history (half-second chunks over a finite horizon), vectorized static map polylines, dynamic traffic-light temporal states, and optionally explicit goal coordinates for a subset of agents. It outputs a sequence of discrete motion tokens for all agents in the scene, constituting a full multi-agent simulation rollout. In goal-conditioned mode, the same architecture produces rollouts steered toward specified target positions, with goals injected either by concatenating coordinates to agent embeddings or by extending relative positional encodings with a binary goal-polyline indicator.

## Method

RLFTSim fine-tunes a pretrained SMART-tiny transformer (next-token prediction over motion tokens) using REINFORCE with a KL-divergence penalty against the reference policy. The key reward innovation is Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO): for N rollouts from the same context, the per-rollout reward is the average RMM computed over all N−1-rollout subsets minus the RMM excluding that specific rollout, yielding a dense signal with O(1/N²) variance scaling (proven theoretically) versus O(1/T) for standard RLOO. For controllability, the paper introduces goal-conditioned fine-tuning (GCFT) that combines MLOO with a binary goal-reaching reward, and applies Hindsight Experience Replay to replace unreachable ground-truth goals with terminal states from the best RMM-scoring rollout, enabling sample-efficient learning of goal-directed behaviors without sacrificing realism.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOSAC private test split), RLFTSim achieves a state-of-the-art Realism Meta-Metric (RMM) of 0.7867, surpassing the previous best TrajTok (0.7861) and the same-base-model fine-tuning method SMART-tiny CAT-K (0.7856). RLFTSim also attains the best Interactive score (0.8129) and Map-based score (0.9210) among all compared methods. Relative to its own base model (SMART-tiny ref, RMM 0.7824), RLFTSim improves RMM by +0.0043 after just one epoch of RL fine-tuning with the MLOO reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RLFTSim achieves the highest RMM (0.7867) among all 14 compared methods, edging out TrajTok (0.7861) by +0.0006 and SMART-tiny CAT-K (0.7856) by +0.0011. On the Interactive sub-metric, RLFTSim leads all baselines (0.8129 vs. 0.8116 for TrajTok and 0.8106 for CAT-K). However, RLFTSim is marginally behind TrajTok on Map-based (0.9210 vs. 0.9231) and behind SMART-tiny CAT-K on Kinematic (0.4927 vs. 0.4931), indicating the gains are concentrated in interaction realism rather than purely kinematic fidelity. Compared to its own base model (SMART-tiny ref, RMM 0.7824), RLFTSim delivers a consistent improvement across all four sub-metrics after a single epoch of RL fine-tuning. The gap over the original SMART-tiny (RMM 0.7755) is +0.0112, demonstrating that the MLOO reward signal effectively closes the sim-to-real distribution gap that open-loop imitation learning alone leaves unresolved.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMART transformer-based traffic simulator with a closed-loop RL fine-tuning stage, departing from the open-loop imitation learning paradigm that dominates prior work (SMART, Trajeglish, BehaviorGPT). The key conceptual departure is repurposing the WOSAC Realism Meta-Metric—previously used only as an offline evaluation benchmark—as a dense per-rollout RL reward via the Meta-metric Leave-One-Out (MLOO) construction, which adapts the RLOO variance-reduction idea from [Kool et al., ICLR 2019] to a population-based distribution-matching metric. For controllability, the paper combines goal-conditioned fine-tuning with Hindsight Experience Replay and hindsight policy gradients, drawing on the goal-conditioned RL literature but applying it for the first time to distill controllability into a pre-trained multi-agent traffic simulator while preserving realism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLFTSim is the first work to successfully use the official WOSAC realism meta-metric as an RL optimization target, providing both a practical recipe (MLOO + KL regularization) and theoretical justification (quadratic variance scaling) for sample-efficient closed-loop alignment of traffic simulators. The goal-conditioned controllability distillation addresses a practical need for targeted scenario generation in AV safety testing, though goal-reaching rates remain imperfect. Open questions include whether MLOO generalizes to other population-based metrics, whether the RMM itself is a sufficient proxy for realism (its saturation may reflect metric limitations rather than true convergence), and whether token-based representations can capture the responsiveness needed in highly dynamic scenarios.
