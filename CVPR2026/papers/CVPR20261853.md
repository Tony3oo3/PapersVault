## Problem

3D medical image classification research suffers from three critical pitfalls: evaluation is biased toward low-data regimes that mask clinically relevant performance, 2D foundation models are adapted suboptimally (e.g., frozen backbones with simple average/median pooling), and benchmarks cover too few tasks to assess true generalizability. No single scalable framework has demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across diverse pathologies, modalities, and anatomies, forcing practitioners to train separate task-specific 3D models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D medical volumes (CT or MRI) by decomposing them into individual 2D slices along a chosen axis, each encoded independently by a frozen 2D vision transformer. It natively supports multi-view MRI (sagittal, coronal, axial) and multi-sequence inputs (T1, T2, FLAIR) through view-specific adapters with late fusion. Beyond class logits for multi-class or multi-label prediction, the model optionally produces 3D interpretable heatmaps by weighting per-slice class-to-patch attention maps with learned slice importance scores.

## Method

AnyMC3D freezes a 2D FM backbone (DINOv2/v3, MedImageInsight, or MedGemma) and adapts it per task via LoRA adapters (~1M parameters) applied to patch embedding and all self-attention projection layers, learning low-rank updates while preserving pretrained features. Each 2D slice is encoded to a class-token embedding, and slice embeddings are fused through a permutation-invariant query-based attention pooling mechanism where a single learnable task query assigns soft weights to all slices, avoiding the brittleness of sequential RNN/Transformer fusion under anisotropic spacing. A lightweight classification head maps the aggregated volume embedding to class logits. The framework extends to multi-view settings by adding view-specific LoRA adapters and queries that are late-fused via a second attention pooling step, and supports auxiliary pixel-level supervision by reshaping patch tokens into a pseudo-3D volume decoded by a lightweight 3D segmentation head during training only.

## Results

AnyMC3D achieves 0.894 average AUROC across 10 diverse 3D classification tasks with only 1.2–1.3M trainable parameters per task (10–40× fewer than competing methods), securing 1st place on 7/10 tasks (MII backbone) and 4/10 tasks (DINOv3 backbone). Both AnyMC3D (MII) and AnyMC3D (DINOv3) tie at 0.894 average AUC, substantially outperforming the best 3D baseline (3D DenseNet, 0.833) by 6.1 points and the best 2D/2.5D method (MST, 0.869) by 2.5 points. On the VLM3D challenge, AnyMC3D with ~0.5M trainable parameters wins 1st place among 118 participants, beating methods that performed expensive large-scale CT pretraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyMC3D (MII/DINOv3) at 0.894 average AUC decisively outperforms all 3D from-scratch baselines: 3D DenseNet (0.833, +6.1 pts), 3D ResNet (0.809, +8.5 pts), and 3D ConvNeXt (0.683, +21.1 pts), while using 10–30× fewer trainable parameters. Against 2D/2.5D slice-fusion methods, AnyMC3D beats MST (0.869, +2.5 pts) and RSNA-Kaggle (0.810, +8.4 pts) despite training only 1.2–1.3M vs. 23–25M parameters. 3D medical FMs (MedicalNet 0.783, VoCo 0.793) are substantially outperformed despite having 35–40× more trainable parameters. On task-specific comparisons, AnyMC3D (DINOv3) surpasses PanDx on PDAC detection (0.962 vs. 0.949 AUC, reaching 0.973 with pixel-level supervision), outperforms CT-Net (0.631) and CT-CLIP (0.768) on all 18 chest CT abnormalities (0.882 mean AUC), and improves over DeepCNTD on 75 head CT findings (0.820 vs. 0.768 average AUC). Notably, the general-purpose DINOv3 backbone matches the medical-specific MII backbone (both 0.894), while frozen MII (0.785) and frozen MedGemma (0.690) are far below their adapted counterparts, confirming that adaptation strategy matters more than medical-specific pretraining alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on LoRA as a parameter-efficient adaptation mechanism, repurposing it from NLP to 2D vision foundation models for 3D medical classification. It departs from the dominant paradigm of using frozen 2D FMs with simple pooling (average/median) for slice fusion, and from the assumption that 3D medical FMs (MedicalNet, VoCo) are the natural choice for volumetric tasks. The key conceptual shift is treating in-plane feature extraction (via LoRA-adapted 2D ViT) and through-plane reasoning (via permutation-invariant attention pooling) as separable, lightweight modules on a frozen backbone, rather than training a monolithic 3D architecture from scratch or fully fine-tuning a 2D encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a general-purpose 2D FM (DINOv3) matches a medical-specific FM (MedImageInsight) with proper adaptation challenges the field's investment in expensive medical-domain pretraining for classification tasks, suggesting adaptation strategy may matter more than pretraining domain. The 12-task benchmark spanning CT/MRI, multiple anatomies, and realistic data sizes addresses a genuine gap in prior evaluation. Open questions include generalization to out-of-domain modalities (PET, ultrasound), whether weaker supervision signals (bounding boxes, reports) can replace expensive pixel-level labels, and whether the 2D-over-3D advantage extends to dense prediction tasks like segmentation where inter-slice geometry is critical.
