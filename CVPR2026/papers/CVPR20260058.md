## Problem

Adapting diffusion models to novel few-shot domains and distilling them for fast inference are typically handled as separate two-stage pipelines (Distill-then-Adapt or Adapt-then-Distill), which add design complexity, risk over-smoothing or overfitting, and lose diverse source-domain knowledge. No prior work performs single-stage joint distillation and adaptation of diffusion models for few-shot generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260058/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For FSIG, the method takes random noise and a frozen source teacher (guided DDPM on FFHQ, 256×256) plus a 10-shot target set, producing 256×256 images in 3 NFEs. For SDP, it takes random noise, a text prompt, and a 4–6-shot subject set, producing 512×512 images in 1 NFE using SDv1.5 as the source backbone. The target set is consumed during training to fine-tune an optional target teacher and to train the multi-head discriminator, but at inference only the student generator G is used.

## Method

Uni-DAD couples two complementary training signals in a single stage: (i) a dual-domain distribution-matching distillation (DMD) objective that computes the student's gradient as a weighted combination of score differences against a frozen source teacher and an optional online target teacher, guiding the student toward a common region between the two distributions while preserving source diversity; and (ii) a multi-head GAN loss where the discriminator reuses the fake teacher's encoder blocks with linear classification heads at multiple depths, enforcing target realism across local and global feature scales to mitigate overfitting in few-shot regimes. A fake teacher is concurrently trained on the student's evolving outputs to provide up-to-date score estimates and discriminator negatives. Training alternates among updating the student (dual-domain DMD + GAN generator loss), the fake teacher and discriminator (MSE + GAN discriminator loss, 5–10 inner steps per outer step), and optionally the target teacher (MSE denoising on target images). A weighting factor α ∈ [0,1] controls the relative influence of source vs. target DMD gradients, allowing the method to handle both mild and structurally distant domain shifts without architectural changes.

## Results

On the FSIG benchmark (10-shot, NFE=3), Uni-DAD achieves FID of 45.09 (Babies), 24.45 (Sunglasses), 58.13 (MetFaces), and 55.32 (Cats), outperforming all non-distilled baselines (which require 25–1000 NFE) and both two-stage distilled pipelines. On the SDP benchmark (4–6-shot, NFE=1), Uni-DAD attains DINO 0.47, CLIP-I 0.73, and CLIP-T 0.29, with Intra-LPIPS of 0.51±0.09, providing the best quality–diversity trade-off among all 1-step distilled methods. Generation time for 5K FSIG samples drops from 35–63 minutes (non-distilled) to 4.2 minutes, with per-image cost reduced from 55.7 to 2.2 TFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPM-PA [51] (1000 NFE), Uni-DAD (3 NFE) improves FID from 48.92→45.09 on Babies and 34.75→24.45 on Sunglasses while being ~333× faster at inference. Against CRDI [3] (25 NFE), Uni-DAD achieves lower FID on all four domains (e.g., 45.09 vs 48.52 on Babies, 58.13 vs 121.36 on MetFaces) with 8× fewer steps. Against DreamBooth/FT [34] (100 NFE on SDP), Uni-DAD (1 NFE) trades a small quality gap (DINO 0.47 vs 0.58) for a 100× speedup, while on FSIG it clearly wins on FID (45.09 vs 57.06 on Babies). Against PSO [27], the comparison is asymmetric: Turbo-PSO_SDXL (4 NFE, SDXL backbone, 1024×1024) edges Uni-DAD on DINO (0.50 vs 0.47), but the directly comparable DMD2-PSO_SDv1.5 (1 NFE, SDv1.5) is far weaker (DINO 0.14, Intra-LPIPS 0.07), and Uni-DAD substantially outperforms it on all quality and diversity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni-DAD extends the DMD/DMD2 distillation framework [48, 49] from single-domain to dual-domain distribution matching, coupling it with a multi-head GAN objective to jointly perform distillation and few-shot domain adaptation in one training loop. It departs from the prevailing two-stage pipelines (Distill-then-Adapt or Adapt-then-Distill) that prior work treats as necessary, and from adaptation-only methods (DreamBooth, CRDI, DDPM-PA) that leave sampling slow. The multi-head discriminator design borrows from GAN literature but is specifically motivated by the overfitting risk in few-shot regimes where a single discriminator head easily memorizes the target set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By collapsing distillation and adaptation into a single stage, Uni-DAD removes a significant source of pipeline complexity and compute overhead, making fast personalized generation more accessible. The practical impact is enabling ≤4-step (even 1-step) generation for novel domains, which is critical for real-time applications. However, the method inherits GAN training fragility, requires careful tuning of the dual-domain weighting factor, and still costs more in training than standard adaptation alone. Open questions include parameter-efficient variants, automatic scheduling of the source/target balance, and extension to video and audio diffusion models.
