## Problem

Transferring image-pretrained models to video tasks faces a fundamental trade-off: fine-tuning heavy temporal modules risks catastrophic forgetting of inter-video semantic separability acquired from diverse image pretraining, while restricting tunable parameters fails to establish reliable intra-video temporal consistency. Prior image-to-video transfer methods either degrade discriminability or lack explicit temporal correspondence, and no principled mechanism exists to balance these two competing properties during transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261810/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method samples two frames from a video with a temporal offset δ∈(0,1), encodes each through a frozen ViT image encoder (e.g., ViT-B/16, d=768) to obtain N patch-level token representations, and projects them via a single learnable linear layer plus LayerNorm (0.59M parameters total). The backward frame in the cycle is encoded with an augmented positional encoding (interpolated and randomly cropped) to break exact positional matching. The output is projected patch-level representations in a shared latent space, used zero-shot for dense, frame-level, and video-level downstream tasks.

## Method

Co-Settle applies a lightweight projection layer on top of a frozen image-pretrained encoder and trains it with two objectives: (1) a cycle-consistency loss L_cyc that aligns the product of forward and backward patch-correlation matrices (from a Contrastive Random Walk structure) to the identity via cross-entropy, and (2) a KL-divergence regularization L_reg that constrains the projection to preserve the semantic distribution of the original image features, preventing dimensional collapse. A key design choice is Positional Encoding Augmentation (PEA), which interpolates the pretrained positional encoding with a controllable amplitude and applies a random crop to the backward frame, breaking the positional shortcut that causes ViTs to trivially minimize the cycle loss by matching absolute locations. Theoretical analysis via spectral decomposition shows the optimal projection performs soft thresholding on the eigenvalues of the intra-video covariance matrix—suppressing high-variance (already consistent) directions and amplifying low-variance ones—yielding a provably improved margin between inter- and intra-video distances. The entire framework requires only 5 epochs of self-supervised training on Kinetics-400 with 4 GPUs.

## Results

On dense-level video tasks, Co-Settle consistently improves all eight image-pretrained encoders by an average of 2.63% J&Fm on DAVIS-2017, 1.98% mIoU on VIP, and 2.59% PCK@0.1 on JHMDB, using only 5 epochs of self-supervised training on Kinetics-400. The best transferred model (MAE+Ours) reaches 33.8 mIoU / 59.6 J&Fm on DAVIS-2017 and 48.4 PCK@0.1 on JHMDB, surpassing video-pretrained baselines like SiamMAE (36.1 mIoU, 47.0 PCK@0.1) on correspondence-sensitive metrics while using only 0.59M tunable parameters versus 7–14M for competing transfer methods. On frame- and video-level tasks, the method yields a 2.80% accuracy gain on Breakfast, 2.58% R@1 on HMDB51, and 1.53% Acc@1 on SSV2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against video-pretrained baselines, MAE+Ours (33.8 mIoU, 48.4 PCK@0.1) surpasses SiamMAE (36.1 mIoU, 47.0 PCK@0.1) on JHMDB correspondence while remaining competitive on DAVIS, and clearly outperforms VideoMAE (25.6 mIoU), MAE-ST (33.2 mIoU), and DropMAE (31.1 mIoU) on DAVIS-2017. Against image-to-video transfer methods (AIM, ST-Adapter, ZeroI2V), Co-Settle achieves superior downstream performance (e.g., 39.2 mIoU vs. 37.2 for ZeroI2V on DAVIS with CLIP) while updating only 0.59M parameters and requiring 1.2×RTX4090 GPU-hours, compared to 7–14M parameters and 23–120×V100 GPU-hours for the baselines. The method also outperforms full fine-tuning (111.66M params, 20.1×RTX4090) and partial fine-tuning (7.09M params, 15.6×RTX4090) on all three dense-level metrics, confirming that the lightweight projection with cycle-consistency and KL-regularization is more effective than simply tuning more parameters. Notably, the gains extend to supervised multimodal models (CLIP, BLIP), where the absolute improvements are smaller (e.g., +1.1 mIoU for CLIP) but still consistent, suggesting the method is complementary to strong pretraining rather than a replacement for it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Contrastive Random Walk (CRW) framework for temporal correspondence learning, adapting its cycle-consistency objective to the image-to-video transfer setting. It departs from prior image-to-video transfer methods (ST-Adapter, AIM, ZeroI2V) that insert complex temporal modules (3D convolutions, temporal attention) into frozen ViTs and fine-tune them with task-specific supervision; instead, Co-Settle uses a single lightweight linear projection layer trained with a self-supervised cycle-consistency loss and a KL-divergence separability constraint. The Positional Encoding Augmentation (PEA) strategy is a novel contribution that addresses a specific failure mode of CRW on ViT backbones—positional shortcut exploitation—by asymmetrically perturbing the positional encoding of the backward frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, parameter-efficient recipe (0.59M tunable parameters, 5 epochs) for converting any frozen ViT-based image encoder into a video-aware representation, which is broadly applicable across pretraining paradigms (masked modeling, contrastive, self-distillation, multimodal). The consistency-separability trade-off framing offers a useful conceptual lens for understanding why naive fine-tuning degrades video representations, and the spectral analysis gives theoretical grounding for why a simple linear projection suffices. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-ViT architectures, whether the 5-epoch convergence holds at larger scales or with more diverse video data, and whether the theoretical assumptions (Gaussian inputs, PSD covariance) hold in practice for real ViT features.
