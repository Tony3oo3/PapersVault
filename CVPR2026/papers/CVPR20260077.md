## Problem

Existing temporal forgery localization benchmarks focus on appearance-level manipulations such as face swapping and object removal, leaving a critical gap for activity-level forgeries that alter human actions to distort event semantics. These activity-level forgeries are far more deceptive because they maintain visual and temporal consistency with surrounding content while changing the meaning of events, yet no large-scale dataset or dedicated evaluation protocol exists for localizing them.

## Input / Output

The method takes an untrimmed video as input and predicts a set of temporal intervals (start, end frame pairs) corresponding to manipulated activity segments. Evaluation follows standard temporal action localization protocols, reporting Average Precision at tIoU thresholds of {0.75, 0.85, 0.95} and Average Recall at proposal counts {1, 5, 10}. The ActivityForensics dataset contains over 6,000 forged segments where more than 60% occupy less than 30% of the total video duration, making precise boundary localization particularly challenging.

## Method

TADiff builds on ActionFormer's multi-scale transformer encoder but inserts a diffusion-based feature regularizer between the encoder and prediction heads. In the forward process, Gaussian noise is injected into the temporal feature sequences to push representations away from the semantic manifold and suppress over-reliance on high-level action semantics. The reverse process uses a lightweight FiLM-conditioned temporal convolutional denoiser with DDIM-style deterministic updates (3 steps) to progressively reconstruct artifact-sensitive features while preserving temporal structure. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end under a combined focal loss (confidence) and smooth L1 loss (boundary regression) rather than a reconstruction objective, so the diffusion dynamics are guided to amplify subtle visual and temporal inconsistencies. For dataset construction, a grounding-assisted pipeline leverages video captioning and temporal grounding to localize activity segments, LLMs to generate semantically altered descriptions, and conditioned video generation/editing models (Wan, Scifi, FCVG, VACE, LTX) to synthesize forgeries that are seamlessly merged back into the original video context.

## Results

On the ActivityForensics benchmark, TADiff achieves an average AP of 75.05% and average AR of 77.15% under the intra-domain setting, outperforming the ActionFormer baseline by +4.38 AP and +2.84 AR. Under the open-world setting (testing on the unseen commercial model Vidu), TADiff reaches 83.64% avg AP and 87.92% avg AR, with a particularly large +11.98 gain at the strict AP@0.95 threshold. The most notable improvement is at AP@0.95, where TADiff gains +9.78 (intra-domain) and +11.98 (open-world) over ActionFormer, indicating substantially more precise temporal boundary localization of forged segments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TADiff is compared against three baselines: ActionFormer (the base architecture it builds upon), UMMAFormer (a temporal forgery localization method), and DiGIT (a temporal action detection transformer). TADiff outperforms all three baselines on every metric in both intra-domain and open-world settings. Against the closest competitor UMMAFormer, TADiff gains +3.11 avg AP and +1.41 avg AR intra-domain, and +4.85 avg AP and +3.77 avg AR open-world. The gap is largest at the strict AP@0.95 threshold, where TADiff surpasses UMMAFormer by +8.02 (intra-domain) and +11.49 (open-world), confirming that the diffusion-based feature regularizer specifically improves boundary precision. DiGIT, adapted from temporal action detection, lags most significantly (avg AP 64.69 intra-domain), underscoring the paper's argument that action-localization architectures overfit to semantic cues rather than low-level forgery artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends temporal forgery localization from appearance-level manipulations (face swapping in ForgeryNet, Lav-DF, AV-Deepfake1M; object removal in TVIL/UMMAFormer) to activity-level manipulations that alter human actions and event semantics. Architecturally, TADiff is built on ActionFormer's multi-scale transformer encoder but departs from the action-localization paradigm by injecting a diffusion-based feature regularizer (FiLM + DDIM denoising) to suppress semantic bias and amplify low-level artifact cues. The data construction pipeline repurposes video captioning, temporal grounding, LLM-based description manipulation, and modern video generation/editing models (Wan, Scifi, FCVG, VACE, LTX) into an automated forgery synthesis pipeline, which is a novel combination not seen in prior forgery benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ActivityForensics provides the first large-scale, multi-method benchmark for a forgery type that is arguably the most socially dangerous (altering what a person did rather than what they look like), and its grounding-assisted construction pipeline offers a scalable recipe for future dataset expansion. However, the significant cross-domain performance drops (e.g., B→A average AP falling from ~70 to ~41) reveal that current methods remain fragile to manipulation-mechanism shifts, and the task is still in its infancy with only six generation/editing models covered. Open questions include whether the diffusion-based regularization generalizes to other forgery types, how performance scales with longer videos, and whether the benchmark's reliance on LLM-generated semantic manipulations captures the full diversity of real-world activity forgeries.
