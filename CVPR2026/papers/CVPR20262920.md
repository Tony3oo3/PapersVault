## Problem

Anchor-guided generative planners for autonomous driving rely on discrete trajectory vocabularies that must cover the full distribution at test time, creating an inherent trade-off between vocabulary size (inference speed) and robustness under distribution shift. Additionally, existing diffusion and flow-matching approaches require multiple sampling steps, further sacrificing real-time efficiency. The paper asks how to model multi-modal driving uncertainty without any fixed discrete anchor set while achieving one-step inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262920/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB camera images (encoded via a ResNet-34 backbone into BEV features), ego-vehicle kinematic state (velocity, acceleration), and high-level driving commands. It outputs a 4-second future trajectory at 2 Hz (8 waypoints of x, y, θ in ego-vehicle coordinates). During inference, 8 candidate trajectories are sampled in parallel from 8 Gaussian components, and the Adaptive Reconstruction Module fuses them with scene context to produce a single final trajectory, all within a lightweight 128-dim hidden network.

## Method

MeanFuser replaces the standard Gaussian prior in flow matching with a Gaussian Mixture Noise (GMN) distribution whose K components are derived by k-means clustering of normalized expert trajectories, providing a continuous, multi-modal prior that eliminates discrete anchor vocabularies. It adapts the MeanFlow Identity to learn the mean velocity field u(z_t, r, t) between the noise and trajectory distributions rather than the instantaneous velocity field, enabling exact one-step sampling (x₁ = x₀ + u_θ(x₀, 0, 1)) without ODE solver discretization error. A Transformer decoder with cross-attention conditions the velocity estimation on BEV scene features and ego state. Finally, an Adaptive Reconstruction Module (ARM) uses cross-attention over all K sampled proposals and scene context to implicitly decide—via learned attention weights—whether to select an existing candidate or reconstruct a new trajectory, trained solely on expert demonstrations without any benchmark scoring function.

## Results

On NAVSIMv1, MeanFuser achieves a PDMS of 89.0 using only RGB camera input with a ResNet-34 backbone, surpassing the best LiDAR+camera competitor (WoTE, 88.3) by +0.7 and the diffusion-based DiffusionDrive (88.1) by +0.9. On NAVSIMv2, MeanFuser attains an EPDMS of 89.5, outperforming DiffusionDrive (88.3) by +1.2. In terms of efficiency, MeanFuser achieves 59 FPS end-to-end and 434 FPS for the planning module alone, making it 5.20×, 2.65×, and 1.55× faster than GoalFlow, Hydra-MDP, and DiffusionDrive respectively, while using fewer parameters (54.6M vs. 60.7–65.2M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeanFuser outperforms all 12 compared baselines on both NAVSIMv1 (PDMS 89.0) and NAVSIMv2 (EPDMS 89.5), using only camera input while most competitors rely on camera+LiDAR. The closest competitor on NAVSIMv1 is WoTE (88.3 PDMS), which uses a learned world model for trajectory scoring; MeanFuser exceeds it by +0.7 without any evaluation-metric supervision. On NAVSIMv2, DiffusionDrive (88.3 EPDMS) is the nearest baseline, with MeanFuser gaining +1.2, showing particular strength on lane-keeping (97.3 vs. 97.0) and extended comfort (88.2 vs. 87.7). In inference speed, MeanFuser's one-step MeanFlow sampling yields 434 Plan FPS, compared to 75 for DiffusionDrive, 25 for Hydra-MDP, and 11 for GoalFlow — a 5.78× speedup over DiffusionDrive in the planning module. Notably, MeanFuser also uses fewer parameters (54.6M) than Hydra-MDP (65.2M) and GoalFlow (62.3M), while TransFuser, though faster (3934 Plan FPS), achieves only 84.0 PDMS due to its unimodal MLP decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the MeanFlow Identity (Geng et al., 2025) from image generation to end-to-end driving planning, replacing the instantaneous velocity field of vanilla flow matching with a mean velocity field that enables one-step sampling. Architecturally, it builds on the TransFuser scene-encoder pipeline but replaces the MLP trajectory decoder with a context-conditioned MeanFlow generator. The key conceptual departure from prior generative planners (GoalFlow, DiffusionDrive, Hydra-MDP) is the elimination of discrete anchor vocabularies in favor of a continuous Gaussian Mixture Noise prior, combined with an Adaptive Reconstruction Module that subsumes the scoring-and-selection stage used by most competing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 59 FPS inference speed (5.2× faster than GoalFlow) makes one-step generative planning practically viable for real-time deployment, and the vision-only setup outperforming LiDAR+camera baselines suggests that trajectory-space modeling quality can compensate for missing sensor modalities. However, the GMN mixing coefficients are fixed uniformly (π_k = 1/K), the method is validated only on NAVSIM, and the ARM's implicit selection mechanism lacks interpretability. Open questions include whether the GMN prior generalizes to longer planning horizons or reactive (NAVSIMv2-style) environments, and whether learned mixing weights could further improve coverage of rare driving modes.
