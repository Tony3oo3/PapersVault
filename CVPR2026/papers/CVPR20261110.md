## Problem

Vision-Language Models frequently hallucinate objects due to an over-reliance on linguistic priors that override visual evidence, a failure the authors frame as a Bayesian imbalance between language prior and visual likelihood. Existing inference-time mitigation methods (e.g., VCD, AGLA, VAF) rely on single-path visual perturbation that only degrades the image, making them inherently one-sided: they cannot simultaneously amplify evidence-bearing regions to counteract omission hallucinations and isolate the language prior to suppress confabulation hallucinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PND takes the same inputs as the base VLM—an RGB image and a text prompt or question—and produces a standard generated text response, requiring no architectural modification. The key operational detail is that it additionally requires an external BLIP-ITM model to extract multi-layer cross-modal attention maps from the image-text pair, and it runs three parallel forward passes (original, positive-augmented, and negative-degraded) at every decoding step, introducing modest inference overhead that is still far lighter than training-based alternatives.

## Method

PND is a training-free, plug-and-play decoding framework that performs real-time Bayesian belief adjustment by contrasting the model's outputs under two constructed visual representations. A positive pathway fuses multi-layer cross-modal attention maps into a salience map and multiplicatively amplifies evidence-bearing regions to boost the visual likelihood; a negative pathway identifies the minimal consensus evidence via pixel-wise minimum across layers and degrades only those regions using DDPM forward noising to isolate the language prior. At each token step, logits from the original, positive, and negative passes are combined via a contrastive update (l_orig + α·l_pos − γ·l_neg) with a confidence threshold that retains only tokens credible under the original distribution, steering generation toward visually grounded content while suppressing prior-driven hallucinations.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark, PND achieves an average 6.4% accuracy and 5.5% F1 improvement over greedy decoding across LLaVA-1.5-7B and InstructBLIP-7B, with the largest gains on the adversarial subset (e.g., 84.03% accuracy vs. 78.53% baseline on LLaVA-1.5-7B). On MME, PND reaches a total score of 621.67 on LLaVA-1.5-7B (vs. 531.67 baseline), and on CHAIR it reduces hallucination to Cs=46.0 / Ci=14.0 (vs. 51.0/17.6 baseline). The method generalizes across four VLM architectures (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, InternVL, Qwen-VL) without any retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PND outperforms all three inference-time baselines (VCD, VAF, AGLA) across every benchmark and model tested. On POPE-adversarial with LLaVA-1.5-7B, PND reaches 84.03% accuracy, surpassing AGLA (83.13%), VCD (81.13%), and VAF (80.88%) by 0.9–3.2 points. On MME, PND achieves 621.67 total score on LLaVA-1.5-7B, exceeding AGLA (608.67) by 13 points and VCD (565.33) by 56 points, with particularly large gains in Position (+18.33) and Color (+15) sub-scores. On CHAIR, PND attains the lowest hallucination rates (Cs=46.0, Ci=14.0 on LLaVA-1.5-7B; Cs=42.0, Ci=11.2 on InstructBLIP-7B), beating AGLA by 1–4 points on Cs and 0.2–1.1 points on Ci. The advantage is most pronounced on the adversarial and popular POPE subsets, where strong language priors trigger hallucination, consistent with PND's Bayesian belief-adjustment design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PND extends the inference-time contrastive decoding lineage initiated by VCD [17], which perturbs a single visual input to suppress prior-dominant tokens, by introducing a symmetric dual-path formulation: a positive path that amplifies evidence-bearing regions via multi-layer cross-modal attention, and a negative path that isolates the language prior through DDPM-based degradation of attention-consensus regions. Unlike VCD, AGLA [1], or VAF [41], which operate in a single destructive perturbation path, PND explicitly separates likelihood-amplifying and prior-isolating signals, framing the contrast as a Bayesian belief adjustment between p(y|x_t) and p(x_v|y). The method repurposes BLIP-ITM cross-modal attention as an architecture-agnostic proxy for evidence localization and borrows the DDPM forward noising process as a semantically coherent degradation mechanism, neither of which is central to prior hallucination-mitigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy that generalizes across LLaVA, InstructBLIP, InternVL, and Qwen-VL without retraining, PND offers a practical drop-in for production VLM pipelines where retraining is infeasible. The Bayesian imbalance framing (visual attention dropping from ~14% to ~5% across layers) provides a diagnostic lens that could inform future architectural or training-time interventions. Open questions include whether the reliance on an external BLIP-ITM model for attention extraction limits deployment in latency-sensitive settings, whether the approach transfers to non-object hallucinations (spatial, attribute, relational), and whether the dual-path contrast remains effective as VLMs grow larger and their language priors become more entangled with visual evidence.
