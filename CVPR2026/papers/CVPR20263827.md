## Problem

Existing camera-conditioned video generation methods rely on simplified pinhole assumptions and encode camera geometry at the camera level, making them unable to model the nonlinear projections and strong lens distortions present in fisheye, catadioptric, and wide-angle cameras. Additionally, prior text-to-video approaches define poses only relative to the first frame, leaving absolute pitch and roll ambiguous and preventing explicit control over the initial camera orientation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263827/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt, a sequence of 6-DoF camera poses, per-frame intrinsics (horizontal FoV and radial distortion coefficients), and an optional Lat-Up map (per-pixel latitude angle plus normalized up-vector) that encodes gravity-aligned absolute orientation. The camera parameters are not fed as global feature vectors but are decomposed into per-token ray origins and directions via the Unified Camera Model, so each spatial token carries its own geometric transform. The output is a temporally coherent video whose per-frame lens distortion, field of view, camera trajectory, and initial pitch/roll match the specified parameters.

## Method

UCPE reformulates positional encoding from camera-to-camera to ray-to-ray: each image token is assigned a local ray coordinate system (z-axis = ray direction, x-axis from cross product with the camera's downward axis), yielding a per-token world-to-ray transform that captures spatially varying projection nonlinearity (distortion, wide FoV) within a single frame. A complementary Lat-Up map provides absolute orientation by encoding each ray's latitude angle and a small-angle-rotation-derived up-vector, enabling explicit pitch and roll control. These two encodings are fused into a block-diagonal operator D_t^UCPE = blkdiag(D_t^Ray, D_t^RoPE) and applied inside a GTA-style attention (transforming Q, K, and V), while the Lat-Up feature is added as a bias to input tokens. The entire encoding is injected through a lightweight Spatial Attention Adapter that runs in parallel with the original self-attention of a pretrained Wan Diffusion Transformer, projecting tokens to 1/8 of the original dimension with a zero-initialized output layer, adding only ~35.5M (0.5%) trainable parameters while preserving pretrained priors.

## Results

On the synthesized camera-diverse dataset, UCPE (35.6M trainable params) achieves the best performance across all camera control metrics: FoV error 8.22°, distortion k1 0.129, k2 0.102, pitch 4.35°, roll 3.74°, rotation error 4.12°, translation error 15.21, and motion consistency 17.59 — while also yielding the best video quality (FVD 495.14, FID 63.37). On RealEstate10K (zero-shot, no fine-tuning), UCPE achieves the lowest rotation error (0.56°), translation error (1.25), and motion consistency (1.58), outperforming all baselines including those trained directly on that dataset. UCPE adds only 0.5% parameters over the 7.3B Wan backbone, roughly 90% fewer than ReCamMaster (354M) and 40× fewer than Wan CameraCtrl (1.5B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCPE dominates all three baselines on the synthesized dataset across every camera control metric. Compared to ReCamMaster (354M params), UCPE reduces rotation error from 9.23° to 4.12° (55% lower), translation error from 28.95 to 15.21 (47% lower), and motion consistency from 33.88 to 17.59 (48% lower), while using 90% fewer parameters. Against Wan CameraCtrl (1.5B params), the gap is even larger: rotation error drops from 17.92° to 4.12° (77% lower) and motion consistency from 50.32 to 17.59 (65% lower). On RealEstate10K (zero-shot), UCPE achieves the best pose control (RotErr 0.56° vs. 1.10° for ReCamMaster, 2.22° for Wan CameraCtrl, 0.62° for AC3D) and best motion consistency (1.58 vs. 2.43 for AC3D), though its Q-Align video quality scores (0.9480/0.4686/0.9694) are slightly below Wan CameraCtrl (0.9822/0.5691/0.9885) and ReCamMaster (0.9492/0.5185/0.9720) on aesthetic and image quality sub-metrics, likely because those methods were fine-tuned on that specific dataset. AC3D and CameraCtrl, trained on RealEstate10K, show substantially lower Q-Align scores (0.77/0.37/0.82 and 0.69/0.33/0.73), confirming UCPE's stronger generalization despite not being fine-tuned on that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

UCPE extends the relative camera encoding lineage (CaPE → GTA → PRoPE) by shifting the geometric operator from the camera level to the individual ray level, so each token carries its own viewing-ray transform rather than sharing a single per-camera matrix. This is motivated by the observation that camera-level encodings assume a single linear projection across all pixels, which breaks down under lens distortion and wide FoV. The paper also introduces an Absolute Orientation Encoding via a Lat-Up map, addressing a gap in prior methods that define poses only relative to the first frame and cannot specify global pitch/roll. The injection mechanism follows the LoRA-inspired parallel-adapter pattern, building on the spatial attention design of PRoPE [34] and the geometry-aware attention formulation of GTA [45].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UCPE provides a practical, geometry-consistent camera representation that unifies pinhole, wide-angle, and fisheye lenses within a single Transformer attention mechanism, with under 1% additional trainable parameters—making it a drop-in upgrade for existing video Diffusion Transformers. The Lat-Up map for absolute orientation control fills a concrete gap in prior camera-controllable generation, enabling reproducible pitch and roll specification. Open questions include whether the per-ray formulation extends cleanly to non-central (catadioptric) cameras where ray origins vary per pixel, whether it scales to longer or higher-resolution videos, and whether the same encoding transfers to other Transformer-based 3D tasks (novel view synthesis, 3D reconstruction) as the authors speculate.
