## Problem

Real-time video understanding on resource-constrained devices faces a fundamental trade-off: remote inference provides high accuracy but suffers from round-trip communication delay that makes predictions stale, while local inference is timely but limited by computational and power budgets. Existing split-computing systems either dedicate all local resources to a cloud-dependent pipeline (leaving no fallback), ignore the accuracy degradation caused by latency, or reduce spatiotemporal resolution to meet deadlines. DeDelayed addresses all three gaps by integrating a temporally predictive remote model with a local model that naturally serves as a fallback.

## Input / Output

The local model consumes the current frame at low resolution (up to 704×480, ~0.34 MP) and a compact activation map received from the remote server, producing a 19-class semantic segmentation mask within a 33 ms (30 fps) budget on CPU. The remote model ingests a context window of K=4 high-resolution compressed frames (720p, 1–10 Mbps over 5G uplink) plus the measured network delay τ, and outputs a low-resolution, low-channel-count feature map (via a dimensionality-reducing autoencoder) transmitted over the downlink. The delay τ is sampled uniformly from 0–5 frames (0–167 ms) during training and provided as a runtime conditioning signal.

## Method

DeDelayed splits inference into a remote temporally-predictive video model (2D EfficientViT-L1 per frame → temporal concatenation → 3D ViT → task-specific MLP) and a local image model (CNN2D → CoAt2D → L-MLP). The remote model is trained with an artificial delay of 0–5 frames applied to its input but not its target, so it learns to predict future-frame features; a learnable delay embedding (analogous to position embeddings in transformers) is added to the 3D ViT input to condition behavior on the measured latency. In the final joint training stage, the remote model's task-specific layers are replaced by a dimensionality-reducing autoencoder (adaptive spatial pooling + channel bottleneck) whose output is added element-wise to the local model's intermediate activation map between CNN2D and CoAt2D, enabling negligible-overhead fusion and a well-defined local-only fallback when remote features are absent. Training proceeds in six stages: individual pretraining of remote and local components on ImageNet, Cityscapes, and BDD100K, followed by joint end-to-end fine-tuning with per-pixel cross-entropy loss using the Adan optimizer.

## Results

On BDD100K driving scene segmentation (19-class Cityscapes taxonomy), DeDelayed achieves 66.1 mIoU at 0 ms round-trip delay, outperforming the local-only baseline (58.8 mIoU) by 7.3 mIoU and the remote video-predictive baseline (65.5 mIoU) by 0.6 mIoU. At a realistic 100 ms delay, DeDelayed reaches 65.2 mIoU, a 6.4 mIoU gain over local inference and a substantial margin over conventional remote inference. Even at the maximum tested delay of 167 ms (5 frames), DeDelayed maintains 64.1 mIoU—still 5.3 mIoU above the local baseline—whereas remote inference degrades to 59.6 mIoU. The paper notes that the 6.7 mIoU improvement at 167 ms over local inference is equivalent to using a model roughly 10× larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three inference configurations rather than external methods: (1) local-only image inference, (2) remote video-predictive inference, and (3) the proposed DeDelayed (local + remote predictive). DeDelayed consistently outperforms both baselines across all tested round-trip delays (0–167 ms) and local inference delays (0–33 ms). At 0 ms delay, DeDelayed edges out remote video-predictive by 0.6 mIoU (66.1 vs. 65.5) while substantially exceeding local inference by 7.3 mIoU. The gap widens dramatically with delay: at 167 ms, DeDelayed leads remote video-predictive by 4.5 mIoU (64.1 vs. 59.6) and local by 5.3 mIoU. The local-only baseline is delay-invariant (58.8 mIoU regardless of remote delay) but is always the weakest configuration. The remote video-predictive baseline degrades monotonically with delay (65.5 → 59.6 mIoU), while DeDelayed degrades far more gracefully (66.1 → 64.1 mIoU), demonstrating the effectiveness of the delay-conditioned temporal prediction and local feature fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeDelayed departs from prior split-computing and feature-coding-for-machines approaches (Neurosurgeon, FCM, early exiting) that allocate local computation to a pipeline terminating in the cloud, by making the local model the primary inference path with the remote model providing optional side information via simple element-wise addition of activation maps. It also extends the temporally predictive video modeling line (V-JEPA, Cosmos, optical-flow-based correction) by jointly training the remote model's prediction objective with the downstream segmentation task rather than repurposing a pretrained world model. The delay embedding mechanism draws on position/timestep embeddings from diffusion transformers, but applies them to condition a video transformer on measured network latency at runtime, enabling a single model to adapt to arbitrary delays without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to deploying high-accuracy video understanding on power-constrained devices (robots, wearables) without sacrificing real-time guarantees, with the 6.4 mIoU gain at 100 ms delay matching what would require a ~10× larger local model. Open questions include generalization to stochastic or highly variable delay distributions, high-motion scenes beyond driving, and whether the element-wise fusion mechanism scales to tasks requiring richer cross-modal interaction. The evaluation is limited to a single dataset (BDD100K) and one task (19-class segmentation), so broader applicability of the framework remains to be demonstrated.
