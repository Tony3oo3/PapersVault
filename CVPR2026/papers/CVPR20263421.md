## Problem

Applying GRPO-based reinforcement learning to medical visual grounding suffers from severe reward sparsity: fixed IoU thresholds (e.g., τ=0.5) are too strict for early-stage policies on small, low-contrast lesions, causing all group rewards to collapse to zero, vanishing policy gradients, and training stagnation. Existing RL grounding methods use rigid, non-adaptive reward schemes that cannot match difficulty to the model's evolving capability, and conventional curriculum learning strategies (sample reordering, progressive data exposure) do not directly apply when task difficulty is governed by reward structure rather than input distribution.

## Input / Output

The model receives a medical image (dermoscopy, chest X-ray, or ultrasound) paired with a natural-language query specifying the target anatomical region or lesion, and outputs a pixel-coordinate bounding box [xmin, ymin, xmax, ymax] together with a step-by-step chain-of-thought reasoning trace explaining the localization decision. Bounding boxes are derived from segmentation masks or region annotations in the source datasets, and the model is evaluated at fixed IoU thresholds (A@0.5, A@0.8) and a pseudo-mAP averaged over 10 evenly spaced thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95.

## Method

MedLoc-R1 introduces a performance-aware curriculum reward scheduling mechanism layered on top of standard GRPO without adding auxiliary networks or gradient paths. A sliding window (N=30 steps) continuously tracks three statistics—mean reward r̄_k, reward standard deviation σ_{r,k}, and mean IoU margin m̄_k—and a composite update criterion C_k requires all three to be simultaneously satisfied (sufficient hit rate, low variance, and positive IoU surplus) before the IoU threshold τ_k is incremented. The threshold progresses from τ_0=0.3 toward τ_target=0.8 using a piecewise-decay step schedule (δ^(1)=0.15, δ^(2)=0.10, δ^(3)=0.05 with stage boundaries at 0.55 and 0.75), with linear and cosine decay variants also supported. Upon each threshold update, the earliest half of the sliding window is discarded and refilled with new data to prevent stale statistics from biasing subsequent evaluations. This design transitions the reward signal from dense, easily obtainable (coarse localization) to sparse, fine-grained (precise alignment) in a manner that tracks actual model readiness rather than a fixed training-progress schedule.

## Results

MedLoc-R1-3B (piecewise) achieves 94.46 / 76.02 / 73.91 (A@0.5 / A@0.8 / mAP) on HAM10000, 94.19 / 47.35 / 59.01 on HEEL, and 66.18 / 29.60 / 37.96 on TN3K, consistently outperforming all baselines across all three metrics. The largest gains appear at the stricter A@0.8 threshold, where MedLoc-R1 surpasses VLM-R1 by +57.45 on HAM10000 and +43.18 on HEEL. Scaling to 7B and 32B variants yields further improvements (e.g., 97.20 A@0.5 on HAM10000 with 32B), confirming the framework's scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLM-R1 (fixed IoU threshold τ=0.5), MedLoc-R1-3B wins decisively on every metric across all three datasets: +29.81 A@0.5 and +57.45 A@0.8 on HAM10000, +27.40 A@0.5 and +43.18 A@0.8 on HEEL, and +23.17 A@0.5 and +18.75 A@0.8 on TN3K, confirming that a static threshold causes severe reward sparsity in early training. Against V-Triune (progress-based three-stage schedule), MedLoc-R1 still leads by +5.54 A@0.5 on HAM10000 but by much larger margins on the harder datasets: +26.56 A@0.5 and +22.30 A@0.8 on HEEL, and +22.68 A@0.5 and +13.98 A@0.8 on TN3K, showing that scheduling by training progress alone is insufficient without performance-aware gating. MedLoc-R1 also outperforms the continuous-reward Raw-IoU baseline by +1.60 A@0.5 on HAM10000 but by +19.95 A@0.5 and +35.86 A@0.8 on HEEL, indicating that progressively tightening discrete thresholds is more effective than relying on weak-contrast continuous IoU signals. All improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05 to p < 0.001) under paired two-tailed t-tests across three random seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO-based RL training for medical visual grounding by replacing the fixed IoU-threshold reward with a performance-aware curriculum schedule that progressively tightens the localization criterion. It departs from prior RL-for-grounding work (VLM-R1, MedGround-R1, Med-R1) that relies on static reward boundaries, and from medical curriculum learning approaches (MedCCO, style curriculum) that reorder data rather than modulate reward difficulty. The core mechanism—a sliding-window tracker feeding a multi-condition update rule into the IoU threshold—repurposes curriculum learning principles within the GRPO optimization loop without adding auxiliary networks or gradient paths.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a lightweight, drop-in solution to reward sparsity that is a known bottleneck for RL-based grounding in high-stakes medical imaging, and its effectiveness across three modalities and three model scales (3B/7B/32B) suggests broad applicability. However, the approach remains tied to IoU as the sole localization metric, which may not capture clinically relevant aspects like partial overlap with irregular lesion shapes or multi-region targets. Open questions include whether the scheduling hyperparameters (window size, margin threshold Δ, decay schedule) transfer across imaging domains without re-tuning, and whether the framework extends naturally to multi-task settings such as joint detection and classification, which the authors flag as future work.
