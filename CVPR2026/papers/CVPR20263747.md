## Problem

Current 3D occupancy prediction methods suffer from long-tailed class bias, overconfidently assigning anomalous or unseen objects to rare known categories, while existing OOD scoring methods (max softmax, entropy, energy) fail to capture spatial structure at the voxel level. No prior work provides a unified framework that jointly addresses long-tail calibration and voxel-level uncertainty quantification in the occupied 3D space, leaving a gap between robust occupancy prediction and reliable OOD detection.

## Input / Output

The method takes single or multi-view RGB images (1226×370 on SemanticKITTI, 1408×376 on SSCBench-KITTI-360) and produces a 3D voxel grid of size (H, W, Z) with per-voxel semantic labels plus a training-free voxel-wise OOD score map in [0,1]. The OOD scores require no additional supervision or auxiliary network; they are computed post-hoc from the refined features and logits already produced by the occupancy head. ProOOD is plug-and-play, adding only 0.28M parameters atop existing backbones such as SGN or VoxDet without modifying their architecture.

## Method

ProOOD maintains per-class EMA voxel prototypes updated only from ground-truth voxels, gated by a maturity criterion (intra-class variance and minimum count) to prevent immature prototypes from corrupting downstream modules. Three components exploit these prototypes: (i) PGSI fills occluded voxels via attention-weighted residual updates toward the nearest global prototypes, ensuring semantic plausibility beyond geometric consistency; (ii) PGTM selects tail-class candidates at the coarse feature stage using cosine-similarity margins against prototypes, then applies a dedicated head supervised by cross-entropy on refined features to strengthen rare-class representations; (iii) EchoOOD computes a training-free OOD score per voxel by fusing three cues—local logit coherence (deviation from class-mean logits), local prototype matching (distance to scene-specific prototypes built from confident predictions), and global prototype matching (distance to EMA prototypes)—via max aggregation. A prototype-based contrastive loss (PBCL) further enforces intra-class compactness and inter-class separation on the refined feature volume, activated only after prototypes reach maturity.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI, ProOOD(+VoxDet) achieves 18.12 mIoU and 6.34 Tail mIoU, surpassing the VoxDet baseline (17.77 / 6.17) by +0.35 mIoU and +0.17 Tail mIoU; ProOOD(+SGN) reaches 14.51 mIoU and 4.35 Tail mIoU vs. SGN's 14.01 / 3.48, a ~25% relative gain on tail classes. For OOD detection on VAA-KITTI, ProOOD(+SGN) achieves 62.65 AuPRC_r at 1.2 m, outperforming the previous best OccOoD (23.42) by +39.23 points, and ProOOD(+VoxDet) reaches 56.49 vs. ASS's 14.02. On the real-world VAA-STU benchmark, ProOOD(+SGN) attains 24.21 AuPRC_r at 1.2 m, nearly doubling OccOoD's 11.39.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProOOD is evaluated as a plug-and-play module atop two strong baselines: SGN and VoxDet. On SemanticKITTI, ProOOD(+SGN) improves mIoU from 14.01 to 14.51 and Tail mIoU from 3.48 to 4.35 (~25% relative gain), while ProOOD(+VoxDet) lifts mIoU from 17.77 to 18.12 and Tail mIoU from 6.17 to 6.34. On the OOD detection benchmark VAA-KITTI, ProOOD(+SGN) with EchoOOD achieves 62.65 AuPRC_r at 1.2 m, a +39.23-point improvement over OccOoD (23.42) and +48.63 over ASS (14.02); ProOOD(+VoxDet) reaches 56.49, still far above both. On VAA-KITTI-360, ProOOD(+SGN) gains +2.91 AuPRC_r at 1.2 m over OccOoD (30.83 vs. 29.72), and on the real-world VAA-STU dataset it more than doubles OccOoD's score (24.21 vs. 11.39 at 1.2 m). The method consistently wins on tail-class mIoU across all occupancy benchmarks and on AuPRC_r across all three OOD benchmarks, while maintaining comparable AuROC, confirming that the gains are not merely from score calibration but from genuinely better OOD voxel identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProOOD builds on camera-based 3D occupancy backbones (SGN, VoxDet) and the OccOoD benchmark for voxel-level OOD detection, but departs from prior prototype-based occupancy methods (ProtoOcc, prototype-aware view transformation) that use prototypes as query tokens. Instead, it repurposes EMA-updated class prototypes as semantic anchors for feature refinement (PGSI, PGTM) and as scoring cues for OOD detection (EchoOOD). The key conceptual departure from OccOoD and OCCUQ is the explicit coupling of long-tail class learning with OOD sensitivity: prior work treated these as separate concerns, whereas ProOOD argues that overconfidence on tail classes directly degrades OOD detection and addresses both through a unified prototype-guided mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that long-tail calibration and OOD detection are inherently coupled in 3D occupancy prediction provides a principled explanation for why existing OOD scoring methods (max softmax, energy, entropy) underperform at the voxel level, and offers a practical remedy requiring only 0.28M extra parameters. The plug-and-play design atop mainstream backbones makes the approach broadly adoptable for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether the prototype-maturity gating and two-stage tail mining generalize to more extreme class imbalances or additional sensor modalities (LiDAR, radar), and whether the EchoOOD scoring strategy transfers to open-vocabulary or generative occupancy frameworks where the closed-set prototype assumption breaks down.
