## Problem

Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) provides a training-free, lossless acceleration for autoregressive text-to-image generation, but its ~2× speedup is severely limited by the high-entropy nature of visual tokens: nearly 50% of forward passes accept only a single token, creating a long-tailed acceptance distribution where most steps contribute zero acceleration. This paper identifies this skewed acceptance-length distribution as the primary bottleneck and asks how to both reduce single-token steps and increase the number of verified tokens per pass.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a decoding-time acceleration layer on top of existing autoregressive T2I models (Lumina-mGPT 7B at 768×768, Emu3 8B at 720×720). It takes a text prompt as input and produces a full RGB image as output, with no changes to model weights or architecture. Internally, it works on VQ token sequences within a fixed-length Jacobi window (L=64), using top-2000 logit sampling at temperature 1 with CFG weight 3, and the acceleration is entirely in how draft tokens are proposed and verified across iterations.

## Method

SJD-PAC introduces two synergistic, training-free, and lossless modifications to the SJD verification loop. Proactive Drafting (PD) addresses cascading rejections by constructing a shallow K-ary tree (K=4, depth D=3) of diverse candidate tokens at the rejection point, followed by a single chain extension to fill the window, so that multiple valid paths exist for the next iteration rather than one misaligned chain. Adaptive Continuation (AC) removes the hard termination at the first rejection: after resampling the failed token, it continues verifying all remaining positions using the stale (pre-rejection) distributions, exploiting the strong locality of image tokens (TV distance drops rapidly with perturbation offset) to preserve valid tokens beyond the initial failure. Together, PD reduces the frequency of single-token acceptances while AC increases the average acceptance length per forward pass, yielding a 4.51× step compression and 3.80× wall-clock speedup on Lumina-mGPT with zero quality degradation.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017 with Lumina-mGPT, SJD-PAC achieves a 4.51× step compression ratio and 3.80× wall-clock speedup while maintaining FID of 30.69 and CLIP-Score of 31.21, virtually identical to the unaccelerated baseline (FID 30.79, CLIP-Score 31.31). On Emu3, SJD-PAC reaches 4.31× step compression and 3.25× latency speedup with FID 31.10 (vs. 31.12 baseline). On PartiPrompts, SJD-PAC delivers 4.62× step compression and 3.97× wall-clock speedup on Lumina-mGPT, and 4.59× / 3.51× on Emu3, all while preserving baseline CLIP-Scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SJD-PAC outperforms all five baselines on wall-clock latency speedup across every model–dataset configuration. Against the closest lossless competitor SJD [22], SJD-PAC more than doubles the speedup (3.80× vs. 2.05× on Lumina-mGPT/MS-COCO; 3.25× vs. 2.01× on Emu3/MS-COCO) while both remain training-free and lossless. Compared to the lossy GSD [19], SJD-PAC achieves a higher latency speedup (3.80× vs. 3.62×) on Lumina-mGPT/MS-COCO and avoids GSD's significant FID degradation (30.69 vs. 33.12). Against SJD2 [23], SJD-PAC wins on practical wall-clock speedup (3.80× vs. 2.81× on Lumina-mGPT; 3.25× vs. 2.54× on Emu3) despite SJD2 reporting a higher step compression on Emu3 (5.62× vs. 4.31×), because SJD2's longer window (128 vs. 64) introduces computational overhead. On PartiPrompts, SJD-PAC also surpasses the lossy GSD (3.97× vs. 4.65× latency on Lumina-mGPT) while preserving the baseline CLIP-Score (32.07 vs. 31.25), and beats LANTERN++ [17] on both speed (3.97× vs. 2.10×) and quality (32.07 vs. 31.07 CLIP-Score). EAGLE-2 [12], though lossless, is the weakest baseline at 2.10× latency and requires a trained draft model, making it strictly dominated by SJD-PAC on both speed and training requirements.

## Relation to Prior Work

SJD-PAC is a direct extension of Speculative Jacobi Decoding (SJD) [22], which reframes autoregressive T2I generation as a fixed-point iteration to achieve training-free, lossless acceleration. The paper departs from SJD by diagnosing its skewed acceptance-length distribution as the core bottleneck and introducing two targeted modifications: Proactive Drafting (a local K-ary tree built at the rejection point to break cascading failures) and Adaptive Continuation (continuing verification past the first rejection to preserve valid tail tokens). It positions itself against both lossless baselines (EAGLE, SJD) and lossy alternatives (LANTERN++, GSD, SJD2), arguing that the high sensitivity of image tokens to perturbation (demonstrated via a single-token artifact experiment) makes the lossless guarantee essential, unlike in LLM settings where relaxed acceptance is more tolerable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SJD-PAC demonstrates that a strictly lossless, training-free method can match or exceed the wall-clock speedup of lossy approaches (3.80× vs. GSD's 3.62× on Lumina-mGPT) while preserving FID and CLIP-Score, which is practically significant for production AR T2I pipelines where quality degradation is unacceptable. The empirical finding that image-token distributions exhibit strong locality (TV distance rapidly decays with context offset, unlike text) provides a principled justification for reusing stale distributions in AC. Open questions include whether the hyperparameter balance (K, D, L) generalizes across model scales and resolutions, and whether combining SJD-PAC's stable multi-draft verification with advanced acceptance heuristics (e.g., GSD's grouped criterion) can push speedups further without sacrificing losslessness.
