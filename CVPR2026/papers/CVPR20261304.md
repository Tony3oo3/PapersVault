## Problem

Federated learning with proprietary foundation models faces a dual-privacy constraint: client data must remain local while the server's model IP must be protected, mandating strictly black-box access to the FM. Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning and personalized FL methods (LoRA, adapters, internal prompt tuning) all require white-box access to internal weights or gradients, rendering them incompatible with this setting. Additionally, achieving both robust generalization to unseen clients and effective personalization under severe non-IID heterogeneity remains an open challenge in this constrained regime.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images that are encoded by a frozen black-box vision encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32) into fixed-dimensional feature embeddings; the encoder is accessed only as an API or compiled binary, never modified. A client-specific orthogonal matrix is applied externally to these embeddings before a shared linear classifier produces the final class label over K categories. The text modality is used only once at initialization (via the VLM text encoder to seed the classifier with class embeddings) and is not involved during training or inference, making the framework applicable to vision-only FMs as well.

## Method

FEDOT employs a dual-parameter architecture: a globally shared classifier w_g ∈ R^{K×d} aggregated across all clients for generalization, and client-specific orthogonal transformations w_l^(i) ∈ R^{d×d} applied externally to the FM's output embeddings for personalization. Orthogonality is enforced throughout training via the Cayley transform, which parameterizes the Stiefel manifold through an unconstrained skew-symmetric matrix, guaranteeing w_l^(i) remains strictly orthogonal at every SGD step. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) proves that the ℓ₂-norm of the gradient difference between any two clients is upper-bounded by 2τ[κ(w_l^(i)) + κ(w_l^(j))], and that the orthogonal constraint (κ=1) achieves the minimal possible bound of 4τ, thereby minimizing gradient conflict under heterogeneity. A block-diagonal variant (FEDOT(+B)) partitions the transformation into r independently orthogonal blocks, reducing degrees of freedom from d²/2 to d(d/r−1)/2 while preserving κ=1, offering a tunable trade-off between local adaptability and structural preservation. Only the global classifier (K×d parameters) is communicated to the server; the local orthogonal matrices (d×d) remain strictly on-device, ensuring both data and model privacy by architectural design.

## Results

FEDOT(+B) achieves the highest average comprehensive accuracy of 86.10% across five heterogeneous datasets (FEMNIST, PACS, Office-Home, VLCS, TerraIncognita), with average generalization of 78.67% and personalization of 88.58%. On FEMNIST, FEDOT(+B) reaches 97.61% comprehensive accuracy, surpassing the next-best FedAdapter (96.31%) by over 1.3 points. On the most challenging TerraIncognita dataset, FEDOT(+B) achieves 63.23% comprehensive accuracy versus 59.86% for FedAdapter and 54.43% for PromptFL. In the cross-device scalability test with 75 clients on FEMNIST, FEDOT reaches 71.18% generalization accuracy, substantially above CLIP zero-shot (44.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDOT(+B) outperforms all seven baselines on average comprehensive accuracy (86.10%), beating the strongest competitors FedAdapter (84.57%) and FedLT (84.24%) by 1.5 and 1.9 points respectively. Against CLIP zero-shot (67.31% avg G), FEDOT(+B) improves generalization by over 11 points, and against FedCLIP (75.81% avg C), it gains 10.3 points. PromptFL (83.23% avg C) is the closest black-box-compatible baseline but is surpassed by 2.9 points on comprehensive accuracy and shows significant instability in the 75-client scalability test (dropping from 73.54% to 65.30%), whereas FEDOT maintains a steady upward trajectory. CoCoOp and VPT, while competitive on personalization, suffer severe generalization collapse (53.18% and 71.25% avg G respectively) due to their reliance on white-box prompt access. The adapted PFL methods FedAKT(C) and FedGH(C) perform poorly (53.88% and 34.85% avg C), confirming that traditional personalized FL methods designed for lightweight models do not transfer effectively to the foundation-model FL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDOT repurposes the orthogonal transformation idea from white-box parameter-efficient fine-tuning (OFT, POR²) by applying it externally to frozen FM embeddings rather than reparameterizing internal weights, thereby satisfying the black-box constraint that renders those methods inapplicable. It extends the federated learning with foundation models line (FedCLIP, PromptFL, ZooPFL) by introducing a dual-parameter architecture—shared global classifier plus client-specific local orthogonal transforms—that simultaneously targets generalization and personalization under the dual-privacy requirement. The theoretical contribution (bounding inter-client gradient discrepancy by the condition number of local transforms) is novel in this black-box FL context, providing a principled justification for why orthogonality is optimal rather than merely convenient.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As proprietary FMs (GPT-4V, Gemini, etc.) are increasingly deployed as opaque APIs, the black-box constraint becomes the default rather than the exception, making FEDOT's architecture directly relevant to real-world FL deployments where neither client data nor server IP can be exposed. The clean theoretical bound (Theorem 1) gives practitioners a principled design criterion: any local adaptation with κ=1 is optimal for minimizing gradient conflict, which could guide future extensions beyond linear transforms. Key limitations include the residual white-box element of the shared classifier (restricting applicability to fully API-only settings) and the restriction to linear transformations, leaving open whether nonlinear orthogonal mappings or extensions to LLM token embeddings can capture richer personalization without violating the black-box constraint.
