## Problem

Current long video understanding methods process every clip exhaustively, causing computational cost to grow linearly with video duration and making them impractical under strict per-sample budgets. This paper formalizes the accuracy-efficiency Pareto-optimal objective for long video QA and asks whether an MLLM can learn to navigate video content selectively rather than scanning it entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262349/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-form video (up to 2 hours) and a multiple-choice question. The video is pre-partitioned into a 3-level tree (each node split into K equal sub-clips, leaf clips ~16 s), and the LRM never sees raw frames directly—it interacts with the video exclusively through two external tools: video_cap() (returns a text description of a clip) and video_qa() (returns an answer, callable only at leaf level). The output is a chain-of-thought-with-tool trajectory in natural language (reasoning steps, tool invocations, and a final choice index), with the episode terminating when the model judges the gathered context sufficient or a maximum iteration limit is reached.

## Method

LongVideo-R1 is a Qwen3-8B reasoning model that performs iterative, goal-oriented navigation over a hierarchical video tree: it starts from a top-level caption, then at each step reasons about whether the answer is derivable and, if not, selects the next clip to explore (drill down, traverse laterally, or backtrack). Training uses a two-stage paradigm: (1) SFT on 33K chain-of-thought-with-tool trajectories synthesized by GPT-5 from CG-Bench's clue-grounded QA pairs, and (2) RL via GRPO with a composite reward combining answer correctness, an F1-like location reward (coverage × precision over ground-truth time intervals), and a repeat-visit penalty. The key architectural insight is that the LRM operates purely in text space—multimodal perception is delegated to external Qwen2.5-VL tools—so the reasoning policy can be optimized independently of the visual encoder, and the entire episode is a sequence of natural-language reasoning steps interleaved with tool calls.

## Results

On LVBench, LongVideo-R1 achieves 50.0% overall QA accuracy with an 8B reasoning model, outperforming all agent-based baselines by at least 5.6% and surpassing GPT-4o (48.9%) and GLM-4V-plus (48.7%). Its standout result is on Temporal Grounding (TG), where it reaches 56.4%, exceeding the next-best method (AdaReTake-72B at 45.5%) by 10.9%. On Video-MME-long (with subtitles), it scores 64.4%, and on MLVU it achieves 64.4%. Crucially, it requires only ~10.5 reasoning rounds per question (≈3 min), versus 86 caption segments for Ego-R1, yielding a substantially lower computational cost at comparable accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LongVideo-R1 (50.0% on LVBench) decisively outperforms all agent-based baselines: VideoAgent (29.3%), VideoTree (28.8%), MemVid (44.4%), and VCA (41.3%), with margins of 5.6–21.2 points. It also edges out proprietary models GPT-4o (48.9%) and GLM-4V-plus (48.7%) by 1.1 and 1.3 points respectively, despite using only an 8B open-source LLM. Among open-sourced MLLMs, AdaReTake-72B (53.3%) remains the strongest overall, but LongVideo-R1 dominates on Temporal Grounding (56.4% vs. 45.5%) and Key Information Retrieval (56.4% vs. 62.2%—actually AdaReTake leads on KIR). On Video-MME-long, LongVideo-R1 (64.4%) is competitive with Ego-R1 (64.9%) but trails VideoChat-Flash-7B (63.3% w/o subtitles, 74.7% w/ subtitles on MLVU); the paper attributes this to Video-MME's emphasis on global questions where uniform frame sampling is advantageous. The key differentiator is efficiency: LongVideo-R1 uses ~10.5 tool-call rounds versus Ego-R1's ~86 caption segments, achieving a far better accuracy-per-compute tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-based video understanding paradigm (VideoAgent, VideoTree, Ego-R1, VCA) by introducing goal-oriented, hierarchical navigation with early termination, departing from the exhaustive linear-scan approach that dominates prior work. Conceptually, it repurposes the chain-of-thought-with-tool framework and GRPO-based RL (from the LRM training literature) into a video navigation setting, where the reasoning model decides which clip to inspect next rather than processing all clips. The hierarchical tree structure echoes VideoTree's representation, but the key distinction is the active, query-driven traversal with a formal Pareto-optimal accuracy-efficiency objective that prior agent systems did not explicitly optimize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical gap: existing long-video QA methods scale linearly with video duration, making them infeasible for real-time or budget-constrained deployment (embodied agents, video chat services). The 10.5-round average versus 86+ segments for Ego-R1 demonstrates that smart navigation can dramatically reduce cost while maintaining competitive accuracy. Open questions remain: the model can be distracted by semantically related but irrelevant segments, uniform temporal partitioning is suboptimal, and the approach relies heavily on caption quality. The authors identify multi-QA amortization, incremental QA, and joint agent-tool optimization as promising future directions.
