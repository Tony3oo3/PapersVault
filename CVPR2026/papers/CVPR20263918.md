## Problem

Existing cross-domain panoramic segmentation methods assume a closed-set of classes and rely on pixel-level prototype alignment, making them unable to handle unseen categories or the severe geometric FoV distortions between pinhole and 360° panoramic views. This paper formulates the first open-set domain adaptation setting for panoramic semantic segmentation, where the model must simultaneously generalize across viewing geometries (pinhole-to-panoramic, synthetic-to-real) and discover novel classes absent from the source domain, under varying weather conditions.

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes 512×512 random crops from labeled pinhole source images (Cityscapes, GTA5, SynPASS) and unlabeled panoramic target images; at test time it processes full-resolution 360° panoramic images. The output is a pixel-level segmentation map that assigns each pixel either a known class label or an unknown flag, evaluated via mIoU on common/private classes and an H-Score that jointly measures base-class accuracy and novel-class discovery. Pseudo-labels on the target domain are refined with MobileSAM before being used in the mixup training objective.

## Method

EDA-PSeg builds on a DAFormer-style MiT-B5 encoder-decoder and introduces two core modules. The Graph Matching Adapter (GMA) samples class-representative nodes using confidence, entropy, and prototype-distance criteria, fills missing categories from a memory bank, and constructs a bipartite graph whose node matching (via Sinkhorn-regularized affinity) aligns shared classes across domains while a Frobenius-norm regularization term pushes unknown-class nodes apart. The Euler-Margin Attention (EMA) reorders channels via a soft permutation to constrain the phase angle within a bounded interval, then applies learnable amplitude scaling (exp(2δ₁)) and phase modulation (δ₂·(θ_q−θ_k)+b) in the complex-valued attention dot product, enabling viewpoint-invariant semantic representation and improved known/unknown separability. Training combines a supervised cross-entropy loss on the source, a mixup pseudo-label loss bridging source and target, and the GMA graph loss weighted by γ=0.1.

## Results

On the primary Cityscapes → DensePASS (C2D) benchmark under Camera Shift, EDA-PSeg achieves 56.81% Common mIoU, 18.86% Private mIoU, and 28.32 H-Score, outperforming the best closed-set method (HRDA, 53.42% Common) by 3.39% mIoU and the closest open-set competitor BUS(SAM) (49.47% Common, 3.10% Private, 5.84 H-Score) by 7.34% Common, 15.76% Private, and 22.48 H-Score. Across all four benchmarks (C2D, S2D, G2S, S2A), the method consistently achieves the highest Common, Private, and H-Score values. On the most challenging SynPASS → ACDC (S2A) setting combining Camera and Weather Shift, EDA-PSeg reaches 30.17% Common and 9.18% Private mIoU with 14.08 H-Score, surpassing the next-best method (MIC, 24.75% Common) by 5.42% Common.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EDA-PSeg outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark and every metric (Common mIoU, Private mIoU, H-Score). Against the strongest closed-set methods (HRDA at 53.42% and DAFormer at 53.13% Common on C2D), the gain is 3.39% and 3.68% Common mIoU respectively, while also achieving non-zero Private scores (18.86%) where these methods report 0.00%. The closest open-set competitor, BUS(SAM) [8], is surpassed by a large margin: +7.34% Common, +15.76% Private, and +22.48 H-Score on C2D, and +14.09% Common, +5.48% Private, +8.07 H-Score on S2A. Among the older open-set baselines, OSBP* [37] achieves the highest Private score (7.01%) but trails EDA-PSeg by 15.81% Common and 11.85 H-Score on C2D. UniOT* [19] and UAN* [49] fail to detect any novel classes (Private = 0.00% or near-zero) and are outperformed by 4–15% in Common mIoU. MIC [18] and HRDA [17] are competitive on Common mIoU for closed-set evaluation but completely fail on open-set recognition (Private = 0.00%), highlighting the gap that EDA-PSeg's GMA and EMA modules specifically address.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: cross-domain panoramic segmentation (CPS), open-set domain adaptation (OSDA), and complex-vector attention. It builds directly on DAFormer as the encoder-decoder backbone, extends EulerFormer's complex-vector self-attention with an angle-margin projection and learnable amplitude/phase modulation (EMA) to handle FoV-induced geometric shifts, and adapts the SAM-based pseudo-label refinement pipeline from BUS for panoramic open-set settings. The key departure is the use of graph matching (borrowed from domain-adaptive object detection) to model high-order class relations for open-set separation, replacing the pixel-level prototype alignment that prior CPS and OSDA methods rely on. By formulating open-set cross-domain panoramic segmentation as a unified task, the paper bridges two previously separate problem settings that had not been jointly addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: autonomous driving and robotics systems operating with 360° sensors will inevitably encounter objects outside their training vocabulary, and existing closed-set CPS methods offer no mechanism to handle this. The paper establishes the first benchmark protocol (four dataset pairs under camera and weather shifts) for this setting, which will likely become a reference for future work. However, limitations temper the impact: the approach is evaluated only with a single backbone (MiT-B5), the open-set baselines are limited to BUS, and the authors acknowledge that random cropping introduces training instability while graph matching adds significant computational overhead. Open questions include whether the Euler-margin mechanism generalizes beyond driving scenes, whether the graph matching cost can be reduced without sacrificing open-set separation, and how the method behaves under more extreme domain gaps (e.g., indoor-to-outdoor).
