## Problem

Existing 3D-aware vision-language models either depend on auxiliary 3D inputs (point clouds, depth maps) or fuse camera and scene features into a single homogeneous stream via shallow mechanisms like concatenation or one-stage cross-attention. This conflation ignores the fundamentally different roles of viewpoint (egocentric) and scene geometry (allocentric) in spatial reasoning, leaving a gap in how camera information should actively guide the injection of geometric evidence into the visual-linguistic pipeline.

## Input / Output

SpaceMind takes a text prompt and an RGB image sequence as input; during training and inference, 34 frames are uniformly sampled per scene, the first and last are discarded, and the remaining 32 frames are resized to 448×448 for InternViT and zero-padded to 518×518 for VGGT. The model outputs a free-form text response answering spatial reasoning questions (distance estimation, size comparison, route planning, appearance ordering, etc.). The fused visual tokens preserve the original shape (N × M_v × d_v) expected by the InternVL3-8B LLM backbone, so no architectural changes to the language model are required.

## Method

SpaceMind employs a dual-encoder front end—InternViT-300M for 2D semantic tokens and VGGT for geometry-aware spatial tokens plus per-frame camera tokens—and introduces a Camera-Guided Modality Fusion (CGMF) module that treats the camera as an active guiding modality rather than passive metadata. CGMF performs three successive operations: (i) camera-conditioned spatial encoding, where an MLP jointly processes each spatial token with its frame's camera token to produce a geometric bias added to attention keys and values, and a cross-frame camera memory is prepended so visual queries can attend to viewpoint information from any frame; (ii) geometry-reliability weighting, where a query-independent per-token importance score derived from spatial self-attention down-weights unreliable geometric evidence before cross-attention; and (iii) camera-conditioned output gating, where a SwiGLU gate computed solely from the camera summary modulates how strongly the spatially enriched features modify the visual backbone. Training freezes both encoders, fully updates CGMF, and applies LoRA (rank 256, scaling 512) to the InternVL3-8B backbone for two epochs on SpaceMind-700K (~25 hours on 64 H100 GPUs).

## Results

On VSI-Bench, SpaceMind achieves a new state-of-the-art average of 70.2, surpassing the strongest prior method VLM-3R (60.9) by 9.3 points and proprietary models (GPT-5: 55.0, Gemini-3 Pro: 56.0) by 14–15 points. On SQA3D, SpaceMind reaches 61.1 EM@1 and 63.8 EM@R1, outperforming the best prior method Video-3D LLM (58.6/60.8) while using only video input versus richer 3D modalities. On the out-of-domain SPBench, SpaceMind scores 67.3 overall, exceeding the next-best VILASR-7B (54.0) by 13.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpaceMind outperforms all 24 baselines across all three benchmarks. On VSI-Bench, the closest competitor is VLM-3R (60.9 avg.), which SpaceMind surpasses by 9.3 points; the gap widens to 21.8 points over Spatial-MLLM (48.4) and 24.7 points over data-centric methods like SpaceR (45.5) and VILASR (45.4). Among proprietary models, SpaceMind leads GPT-5 (55.0) by 15.2 points and Gemini-3 Pro (56.0) by 14.2 points, with the largest subtask margin on appearance order (+30.6 over VLM-3R). On SQA3D, SpaceMind (61.1 EM@1) beats Video-3D LLM (58.6) by 2.5 points while using only RGB video input, whereas all prior methods rely on depth, point clouds, or meshes. On the out-of-domain SPBench, SpaceMind (67.3 overall) exceeds VILASR-7B (54.0) by 13.3 points and Spatial-MLLM-4B (52.5) by 14.8 points, with particularly strong gains on multi-view numerical questions (76.2 vs. 63.7 for Spatial-MLLM).

## Relation to Prior Work

SpaceMind builds directly on VGGT as its feed-forward spatial encoder, following the RGB-only, geometry-augmented paradigm established by VLM-3R and Spatial-MLLM. However, it departs from these prior methods at the fusion stage: where VLM-3R and Spatial-MLLM concatenate camera tokens with spatial tokens and pass them through a single cross-attention or MLP projection, SpaceMind introduces a structured Camera-Guided Modality Fusion (CGMF) module that explicitly separates the camera's role as a conditioning/gating signal from the scene geometry it describes. The paper also positions itself against explicit-3D methods (point clouds, depth maps, meshes) by showing that the same spatial reasoning quality can be achieved from RGB video alone, and against data-centric approaches (SpaceR, VILASR) that keep the visual backbone frozen and rely on curriculum design, arguing that architectural inductive bias is necessary for robust 3D reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The camera-as-guiding-modality principle offers a clean, reusable inductive bias for any VLM that incorporates geometric encoders, and the CGMF module is lightweight enough to drop into existing pipelines without modifying the LLM backbone. The ~9-point gap over the strongest prior baseline on VSI-Bench, combined with consistent gains on SQA3D and out-of-domain SPBench, suggests that how viewpoint and geometry are fused (not just whether they are included) is a first-order factor in visuospatial intelligence. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer video sequences or outdoor/robotic settings, whether the VGGT dependency limits applicability to scenes where feed-forward reconstruction degrades, and whether the camera-guided fusion principle transfers to other geometric modalities (e.g., LiDAR, IMU).
