## Problem

Detecting backdoored text-to-image models under black-box settings remains largely unsolved. The only existing black-box method, UFID, assumes backdoored outputs are globally similar across prompt perturbations, an assumption that breaks down for recent attacks (object replacement, patch insertion, style addition) where manipulations are localized to specific visual patterns while the rest of the image remains diverse. This paper identifies that backdoor attacks produce fine-grained instruction-response deviations that are stable across prompt variations, and leverages this property for detection without access to model internals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt and its corresponding generated image (from Stable Diffusion v1.5) as input, with no access to model weights, architecture, or training data. It produces a binary backdoor detection label (triggered vs. clean), per-object stability scores quantifying how consistently each suspicious deviation persists across N prompt-masked generations, and interpretable explanations identifying which specific visual objects deviate from the instruction. The VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is queried with binary yes/no questions per suspicious object, and the LLM (Qwen-8B) parses the instruction for expected objects.

## Method

BlackMirror is a training-free, two-stage detection framework. In MirrorMatch, a language model extracts expected objects from the instruction, while a VLM extracts visible objects from the image with majority voting over K independent runs to suppress noise; the set difference yields suspicious deviations (objects present in the image but absent from the instruction, or vice versa). In MirrorVerify, the framework generates N prompt variants by randomly masking 'safe' objects (those correctly grounded in both instruction and image), thereby preserving any embedded trigger while introducing semantic variation; for each suspicious object, it queries the VLM with a binary question and computes a stability score as the average softmax probability of presence (or absence) across all N generations. The final detection score is the maximum stability across all suspicious deviations, compared against a threshold τ=0.999. The framework runs three parallel detection branches (object, patch, style) and flags a backdoor if any branch exceeds the threshold, requiring no training and no model-internal access.

## Results

BlackMirror achieves an overall F1 of 89.46% and recall of 95.42% across eight backdoor attack variants (object replacement, fixed-image, patch, and style), with an average FPR of 15.09%. On the most challenging ObjRepAtt attacks (BadT2I, EvilEdit, PaaS, RickTPA), it reaches F1 scores of 86.96%, 85.71%, 97.78%, and 98.04% respectively, substantially outperforming the only prior black-box method UFID (66.67%, 60.87%, 62.96%, 94.59%). The method also achieves perfect recall (100%) on FixImgAtt (VillanDiffusion) and PatchAtt (BadT2I), while maintaining FPRs below 20% in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UFID, the only prior black-box detector, BlackMirror wins decisively on every metric: overall F1 improves from 72.29% to 89.46%, recall from 79.55% to 95.42%, and FPR drops from 48.78% to 15.09%, with the largest gains on ObjRepAtt (e.g., +20.3 F1 on BadT2I) and PatchAtt (+21.7 F1). The CLIP similarity baseline is also clearly outperformed (65.55% vs. 89.46% F1), confirming that global instruction-response similarity is insufficient for fine-grained backdoors. Compared to white-box methods, BlackMirror surpasses NaviT2I on F1 (89.46% vs. 87.14%) and recall (95.42% vs. 84.75%) while remaining competitive on FPR (15.09% vs. 9.27%), and it trails GrainPS slightly on F1 (89.46% vs. 91.29%) and FPR (15.09% vs. 8.10%), though it achieves higher recall (95.42% vs. 90.95%). Notably, BlackMirror is the only method that covers all eight attack variants in a single unified pipeline without requiring model internals, whereas GrainPS and T2IShield leave several attack types unevaluated.

## Relation to Prior Work

BlackMirror departs from UFID, the sole prior black-box T2I backdoor detector, which assumes backdoored outputs are globally similar across prompt perturbations—an assumption that fails for localized attacks like object replacement or style manipulation. Conceptually, it repurposes the instruction-response deviation idea from MirrorCheck (a VLM adversarial defense) and extends it to the generative setting by decomposing detection into pattern-level alignment (MirrorMatch) and cross-prompt stability verification (MirrorVerify). Unlike white-box methods (T2IShield, GrainPS, NaviT2I) that exploit attention maps or neuron activations, BlackMirror requires only input-output access and leverages off-the-shelf VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) as the semantic oracle, making it deployable in MaaS pipelines without model internals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first general black-box detector that handles object-, patch-, and style-level backdoors simultaneously, filling a gap where prior black-box work (UFID) only worked for fixed-image attacks. Its training-free, plug-and-play design makes it immediately applicable to real-world MaaS platforms where model weights are inaccessible. Key limitations include dependence on VLM quality for both object extraction and stability scoring, slightly weaker performance on FixImgAtt compared to UFID, and sensitivity to the stability threshold τ. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation, multi-turn interactive models, or attacks that manipulate subtle semantic relationships rather than discrete objects.
