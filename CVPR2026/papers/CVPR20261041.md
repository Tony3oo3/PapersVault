## Problem

Existing video-to-audio generation models are appearance-driven: they learn visual-acoustic correlations but fail to modulate acoustic properties (loudness, spectral sharpness) according to measurable physical quantities like object mass and impact velocity, producing physically implausible audio. Additionally, no prior benchmark or metric explicitly evaluates whether generated sounds are consistent with the underlying physical dynamics of a video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261041/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unconstrained monocular video clip and a text prompt (the VGGSound class label) as input, and produces an 8-second audio waveform at either 16 kHz or 44.1 kHz. During processing, the video is decomposed into per-object physical parameters—time-invariant mass in kilograms and per-frame velocity in meters per second in metric 3D space—which are estimated without any audio-physics supervision. Learnable occlusion tokens handle frames where objects are absent or occluded, and physics tokens are randomly dropped (p=0.1) during training to support missing-motion scenarios.

## Method

PAVAS integrates two modules into a flow-matching latent diffusion backbone (MMAudio-based DiT): a Physics Parameter Estimator (PPE) and a Physics-Driven Audio Adapter (Phy-Adapter). The PPE uses Qwen2.5-VL for moving-object detection and mass estimation, then combines Florence-2 text-grounded detection, SAM-2 video segmentation, and CUT3R dynamic 3D reconstruction to recover metric-scale per-object centroid trajectories and compute velocity. The Phy-Adapter converts CLIP patch embeddings into object-centric features via masked summation, applies FiLM modulation driven by Fourier-feature-mapped mass and velocity scalars, and aggregates across objects with gated pooling. Physics conditions are injected through a Δ-modulation mechanism—zero-initialized residual MLPs that augment AdaLN coefficients in each transformer block—allowing gradual, stable incorporation of physical cues without perturbing the multimodal representation. Training proceeds in two stages: 300k iterations on VGGSound plus audio-text corpora for the backbone, followed by 30k iterations of physics-aware fine-tuning on VGGSound alone.

## Results

On the VGGSound test set, PAVAS-L achieves the lowest APCC-Δ (0.378) among all evaluated methods, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MMAudio-L (0.536) and indicating far better physical-audio consistency. PAVAS also attains the best distributional fidelity (FD_PaSST 47.38, FD_PANNs 3.99), highest Inception Score (17.51), and highest ImageBind semantic alignment score (35.41). In the user study, PAVAS receives the highest mean Likert ratings across all four dimensions (audio quality 4.23, semantic alignment 4.47, temporal alignment 4.45, physical plausibility 4.37), surpassing MMAudio-L by 0.25–0.47 points on each criterion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAVAS outperforms all eight baselines on the proposed APCC-Δ metric, with the largest gap against MMAudio-L (0.378 vs. 0.536, a 29% relative reduction) and TARO (0.378 vs. 0.758, a 50% reduction), confirming that prior appearance-driven models produce physically inconsistent audio. On distributional fidelity, PAVAS improves FD_PaSST by 22% over MMAudio-L (47.38 vs. 60.60) and by 44% over the next-best V2A-Mapper (84.57), while also achieving the best FD_PANNs (3.99 vs. 4.72 for MMAudio-L). PAVAS edges out MMAudio-L on Inception Score (17.51 vs. 17.40) and IB-score (35.41 vs. 33.22), and matches it on DeSync (0.446 vs. 0.442), indicating that physics conditioning does not sacrifice temporal synchronization. In the subjective user study, PAVAS leads MMAudio-L on all four rating dimensions (e.g., physical plausibility 4.37 vs. 3.90), while older baselines such as See&Hear (1.63) and TARO (2.08) trail by more than 2 points on physical plausibility. The only metric where PAVAS is marginally worse is FD_VGG (1.15 vs. 0.84 for V2A-Mapper), a minor distributional gap in the VGGish feature space that does not affect perceptual or physical quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAVAS builds directly on the MMAudio multimodal latent diffusion backbone (flow-matching DiT with CLIP/SyncFormer conditioning) and extends it with a physics-aware conditioning pathway. It departs from the dominant appearance-driven paradigm in V2A generation—where models learn visual-acoustic correlations without modeling underlying dynamics—by explicitly estimating object-level mass (via Qwen2.5-VL) and velocity (via Florence-2 + SAM-2 + CUT3R) and injecting them through a novel Phy-Adapter with Δ-modulation. The physics estimation pipeline repurposes open-vocabulary segmentation and dynamic 3D reconstruction for a new purpose (metric-scale per-object trajectories), while the Δ-modulation mechanism is a new architectural contribution for gradually injecting scalar physical cues into transformer-based diffusion models without destabilizing the multimodal conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first V2A framework to explicitly condition on object-level physical quantities, and the introduced VGG-Impact benchmark and APCC metric fill a clear evaluation gap: prior benchmarks (VGGSound) measure perceptual/semantic alignment but cannot detect physically implausible audio. The finding that existing SOTA V2A models exhibit APCC-Δ > 0.5 (poor physics-audio coupling) is a useful diagnostic for the field. Limitations include reliance on a chain of pretrained vision modules (VLM, segmentation, 3D reconstruction) whose errors compound, restriction to mass and velocity (no material, shape, or friction modeling), and the fact that mass estimation via VLM prompting lacks the precision of geometry-based methods. Open questions include whether jointly trained physics estimators could reduce error propagation, whether richer physical factors (material, elasticity) yield further gains, and whether the approach generalizes beyond impact/collision events to continuous sound sources.
