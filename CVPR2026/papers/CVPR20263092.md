## Problem

Outlier rejection for correspondence-based point cloud registration faces two coupled challenges: in low-overlap scenarios, true inliers shatter into topologically isolated fragments that one-step global search strategies cannot retrieve, while large-scale scenes with dense correspondence inputs demand search algorithms that avoid the NP-hard complexity of exhaustive global methods. Existing geometry-based approaches (e.g., MAC, SC²-PCR) either scale poorly or fail on fragmented inlier distributions, and learning-based methods (e.g., PointDSC, VBReg) rely on global ranking that concentrates seeds in dominant clusters, missing scattered valid correspondences.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of downsampled source and target point clouds (5 cm voxel for indoor, 30 cm for outdoor) and a set of putative correspondences obtained via feature-level nearest-neighbor matching with descriptors such as FPFH, FCGF, Predator, or GeoTransformer. Each correspondence is a 6D vector (x_k, y_k), and the method outputs a rigid transformation T* = (R* ∈ SO(3), t* ∈ R³) together with the verified inlier correspondence set. The inlier threshold τ_d is 10 cm for indoor and 60 cm for outdoor scenes, and registration success is judged by RE ≤ 15°/TE ≤ 30 cm (indoor) or RE ≤ 5°/TE ≤ 60 cm (KITTI).

## Method

MHopReg is a four-stage hierarchical pipeline. First, an SE(3)-equivariant graph encoder builds a compatibility graph (edges defined by distance and angle compatibility) and performs message passing with spherical-harmonic-encoded relative directions to learn rotation-equivariant embeddings, from which per-correspondence confidence scores are predicted via a small MLP. Second, cluster-balanced seed sampling applies an iterative topological penalty—subtracting a Gaussian kernel of graph shortest-path distance from already-selected seeds—to force M seeds to span disconnected graph clusters rather than concentrating in one dominant region. Third, each seed drives a progressive 0-hop → 1-hop → 2-hop expansion along graph edges: the 1-hop layer retrieves top-k candidates by feature similarity, filters them via dominant-eigenvector spectral scoring, and verifies geometrically; the 2-hop layer performs localized geometric verification from the 1-hop anchors, with confidence propagating from verified parents to children. Finally, distribution-aware ranking scores each hypothesis by inlier count × spatial spread × mean residual, then refines the top-K via Gaussian-weighted Procrustes before selecting the optimal transformation.

## Results

On 3DMatch with FPFH descriptors, MHopReg achieves 88.56% registration recall (RR) and 1.67° rotation error (RE), surpassing the next-best PCRegen (88.48% RR) and MAC (83.90% RR) while running in 0.10 s. On the challenging 3DLoMatch low-overlap benchmark, it attains the highest RR across all three descriptors: 61.59% (FCGF), 72.19% (Predator), and 79.48% (GeoTransformer), with the best inlier precision (IP) and F1 in every setting. On KITTI with FCGF, it achieves 98.74% RR, 0.30° RE, and 17.73 cm translation error (TE) in 0.12 s, outperforming all baselines on every metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MHopReg outperforms all geometry-based baselines on 3DMatch FPFH: it beats MAC (83.90%) and SC²-PCR (83.90%) by ~4.7% RR while being 55× faster than MAC (0.10 s vs 5.54 s). Against the strongest recent method PCRegen, it edges ahead by 0.08% RR on 3DMatch FPFH and matches its 93.96% RR on FCGF with a lower RE (1.70° vs 1.75°), though PCRegen achieves a lower TE on KITTI FPFH (5.36 cm vs 8.70 cm). Among deep-learning baselines, the gap is largest: MHopReg surpasses PointDSC by 11.0% RR on 3DMatch FPFH at identical 0.10 s runtime, and VBReg by 6.0% RR. On 3DLoMatch (the paper's headline low-overlap benchmark), MHopReg wins on every metric for all three descriptors, e.g. 61.59% RR vs VBReg's 58.30% and MAC's 59.85% with FCGF, and 79.48% RR vs MAC's 78.91% with GeoTransformer. On KITTI FCGF, it achieves the best RR (98.74%), RE (0.30°), and TE (17.73 cm) simultaneously, beating SC²-PCR by 0.54% RR and PointDSC by 0.36% RR while running 2.3× faster than PointDSC (0.12 s vs 0.28 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

MHopReg sits at the intersection of geometry-based outlier rejection (MAC, SC2-PCR, TEASER++) and learning-based confidence estimation (PointDSC, VBReg, DGR), but departs from both in its search strategy. Rather than performing a one-step global spectral or clique search (which is NP-hard or O(N³)) or a single non-local ranking pass, it introduces a progressive trust-region expansion over a compatibility graph, borrowing the SE(3)-equivariant message-passing paradigm from equivariant GNN literature (SE(3)-Transformers, Equiformer) and applying it specifically to correspondence-level compatibility graphs. The cluster-balanced seeding mechanism is a graph-topological generalization of spatial NMS, and the distribution-aware ranking extends standard inlier-count scoring with spatial dispersion and residual precision terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in real-world registration: the accuracy-efficiency trade-off when outlier ratios exceed 90% and correspondence counts reach 15K–20K, where global spectral methods become prohibitively slow and one-step learning-based ranking fails on fragmented inlier distributions. The 0.10 s runtime at competitive accuracy makes the method viable for real-time SLAM and autonomous driving pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed 2-hop depth suffices for extremely fragmented scenes, how the method generalizes to cross-modality registration (e.g., LiDAR-to-mesh), and whether the learned equivariant encoder can be replaced by stronger pre-trained 3D foundation models.
