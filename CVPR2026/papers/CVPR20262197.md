## Problem

Classical SfM methods fail on low-texture, low-overlap, and symmetric scenes, while feedforward 3D reconstruction methods, despite excelling in those challenging conditions, suffer from poor scalability (limited to hundreds of images), lower accuracy in nominal settings, and inability to handle multiple connected components or reject outliers. No existing approach combines the local robustness of learned models with the global scalability and precision of optimization-based pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262197/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts an unordered set of RGB images at arbitrary resolutions and produces camera poses in SE(3), 3D scene points, per-camera intrinsics, depth maps, and feature tracks for a subset of confidently registered images. Unlike feedforward methods that globally attend over all input views, GLUEMAP decomposes the problem into local star-graph reconstructions, enabling it to scale to tens of thousands of images (demonstrated on 9,319-view LaMAR sequences) while fitting within 24 GB GPU memory.

## Method

GLUEMAP is a four-stage pipeline that interleaves feedforward and classical techniques. First, a sparse view graph is built via SALAD retrieval and Doppelganger++ filtering with dynamic thresholding to ensure connectivity while removing symmetric/non-overlapping edges. Second, π³ performs feedforward inference on local star graphs centered on each image, yielding local poses, depth maps, focal lengths, and tracks. Third, global motion averaging (rotation averaging followed by similarity averaging with per-star scale factors) merges the overlapping local reconstructions into a globally consistent initialization. Fourth, an augmented bundle adjustment jointly refines poses and structure using three track types: SIFT tracks, feedforward tracks snapped to SIFT keypoints, and novel virtual tracks reprojected from feedforward depth maps that encode scene priors into the BA cost function, allowing the optimization to remain well-conditioned even under low overlap.

## Results

On ETH3D, GLUEMAP achieves AUC@1/3/5 of 53.0/76.9/83.6 (uncalibrated), outperforming the best classical baseline GLOMAP+SIFT (45.6/62.2/66.7) and the best feedforward method π³ (13.2/36.1/48.9) by large margins. On the low-overlap SMERF benchmark, GLUEMAP reaches AUC@1=10.1 (minimal overlap) where both classical (SIFT: 3.5) and feedforward (π³: 3.2) methods largely fail, and surpasses MP-SFM with sparse tracks (9.2). On the large-scale LaMAR dataset (6,587–9,319 views), all feedforward methods fail with out-of-memory errors, while GLUEMAP achieves average AUC@3/10/30 of 26.4/59.1/80.6, compared to 2.6/12.4/28.3 for GLOMAP+SIFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical baselines (GLOMAP+SIFT and GLOMAP+AL+LG), GLUEMAP wins decisively on low-overlap (SMERF: AUC@1 10.1 vs 3.5/4.3) and large-scale (LaMAR: AUC@3 26.4 vs 2.6/10.9) settings, while remaining competitive on standard scenes (ETH3D AUC@1: 53.0 vs 45.6/42.9; IMC2021 Full AUC@3: 73.0 vs 76.9/62.8). Against the best feedforward method π³, GLUEMAP achieves a 4× improvement on ETH3D (AUC@1: 53.0 vs 13.2) and avoids the out-of-memory failures that prevent π³, VGGT, and MapAnything from processing LaMAR's 6,500–9,300-view scenes entirely. On IMC2021, GLUEMAP slightly trails GLOMAP+SIFT on the full collection (AUC@3: 73.0 vs 76.9) but leads on smaller subsets (bag 5: 54.3 vs 39.6), showing complementary strengths. Against MP-SFM (the prior method specifically targeting low-overlap scenes), GLUEMAP matches or exceeds the sparse-track version on SMERF (AUC@1 minimal: 10.1 vs 9.2) while achieving comparable completeness (AUC@20: 82.0 vs 69.8), and outperforms it on LaMAR where MP-SFM is not evaluated. On CO3Dv2 (object-centric, low-texture), GLUEMAP is on par with π³+BA (AUC@3 avg: 56.7 vs 58.4) while classical methods fall far behind (37.0–38.1), confirming that the feedforward backbone is essential for these scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLUEMAP positions itself at the intersection of two complementary SfM paradigms: classical global optimization (building on GLOMAP's rotation and similarity averaging framework) and feedforward multi-view reconstruction (using π³ as the local inference backbone). Rather than replacing one with the other, it introduces a principled hybrid where feedforward models provide robust local reconstructions and scene priors, while classical averaging and bundle adjustment ensure global consistency and scalability. The novel 'virtual tracks' mechanism—reprojecting feedforward depth maps as pseudo-observations in BA—is a clean bridge that injects learned geometric priors into the classical optimization without requiring end-to-end training. Doppelgangers++ filtering and SALAD retrieval are repurposed from their original roles to construct a sparse, symmetry-aware view graph that makes feedforward inference both more accurate and more scalable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first practical SfM system that simultaneously handles low-texture, low-overlap, symmetric, and large-scale (10k+ image) scenes—conditions where either classical or feedforward methods alone fail. The systematic empirical analysis revealing that feedforward methods degrade with increasing view graph radius and, counterintuitively, with more input images of the same scene, is a valuable diagnostic for the community. Key limitations remain: the pipeline's quality is bottlenecked by the feedforward backbone (no fisheye support, purely rotational motion is unhandled), and the current design requires stitching together multiple separate networks (retrieval, symmetry detection, feedforward reconstruction). An open question is whether a single unified feedforward architecture could replace the multi-component pipeline while retaining the scalability of the classical global stages.
