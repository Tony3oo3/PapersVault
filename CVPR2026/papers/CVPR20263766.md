## Problem

In 4D generative animation, rig structure and motion dynamics are treated as separate problems: auto-rigging depends on costly human annotations, motion generation assumes a ground-truth skeleton already exists, and vertex-space methods produce deformations without any reusable structural abstraction. No existing framework jointly learns both rig and motion in a single feed-forward pass from raw mesh sequences, making scalable, interpretable, and category-agnostic animation generation infeasible.

## Input / Output

RigMo-VAE takes a deformable mesh sequence V ∈ R^{B×T×N×3} (normalized to 5K vertices via topology-preserving resampling) and produces, in a single forward pass (~40 ms/frame on A100), a set of K Gaussian bone descriptors (center, anisotropic scale, orientation quaternion), per-vertex skinning weights refined by geodesic coherence masks, and per-frame SE(3) transformations (local per-bone plus a global root transform) that together reconstruct the deformed mesh. The downstream Motion DiT additionally accepts sparse frame observations under a configurable frame-mask schedule and static rigging features (anchor tokens, global token) as conditioning, outputting complete motion latents decoded back to vertex trajectories.

## Method

RigMo-VAE uses a dual-path topology-aware encoder: a rigging branch processes the canonical (first-frame) mesh with neighborhood attention and Farthest Point Sampling to select K bone tokens, while a motion branch encodes per-frame vertex displacements through temporal-spatial attention; cross-attention between bone tokens and vertex embeddings yields bone-vertex correlation features that feed separate VAE posteriors for local and root motion. The decoder predicts Gaussian bone parameters and SE(3) transforms, and a Gaussian-based LBS module computes skinning weights via Mahalanobis distance within each bone's anisotropic coordinate frame, followed by a geodesic-aware refinement that zeros out weights for topologically distant vertices to suppress cross-part artifacts. Training is entirely self-supervised (vertex reconstruction L2 + KL divergence), requiring no rig annotations. A 12-block interleaved spatiotemporal attention (ISTA) diffusion transformer (Motion DiT) then operates in the learned motion-latent space, conditioned on static rig features via cross-attention, using v-prediction to generate or interpolate motion while remaining tightly coupled to the discovered rig structure.

## Results

On the DeformingThings4D test set, RigMo achieves a mean CD-L1 of 12.46×10⁻³ and CD-L2 of 9.74×10⁻³ for combined training reconstruction and cross-motion transfer, substantially outperforming Per-Case Optimization (40.55/25.86), UniRig+Optimization (42.95/29.80), and MagicArticulate+Optimization (48.25/26.30). In the cross-motion transfer setting specifically, RigMo reduces CD-L1 from 68.8 (per-case) and 48.6 (UniRig) to 13.82×10⁻³, a 3–5× improvement. For pure reconstruction fidelity, RigMo achieves 1.73×10⁻² CD-L1 with 0.74 s inference time, beating AnimateAnyMesh (1.81×10⁻², 2.8 s), Step1X3D (3.63×10⁻², 22.6 s), and Hunyuan3D (3.21×10⁻², 17.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RigMo decisively outperforms all four baselines on both training reconstruction and cross-motion transfer. Against Per-Case Optimization, RigMo matches or slightly beats training reconstruction (11.1 vs 12.3 CD-L1) but dominates on cross-motion transfer (13.82 vs 68.8 CD-L1), confirming that per-sequence fitted rigs do not generalize. Against UniRig+Optimization, RigMo improves mean CD-L1 by 3.4× (12.46 vs 42.95) and cross-motion CD-L1 by 3.5× (13.82 vs 48.6), showing that static auto-rigging followed by per-sequence optimization is far inferior to motion-driven feed-forward rig discovery. Against MagicArticulate+Optimization, the gap is even wider (12.46 vs 48.25 mean CD-L1), with RigMo also achieving lower variance across 100 random splits. In the reconstruction comparison (Table 2), RigMo edges out AnimateAnyMesh (1.73 vs 1.81 CD-L1 ×10⁻²) while running 3.8× faster (0.74 s vs 2.8 s), and substantially outperforms the frame-independent 3D VAEs Step1X3D and Hunyuan3D by roughly 2× in CD-L1, attributed to their per-frame decoding breaking vertex correspondence.

## Relation to Prior Work

RigMo carves new ground by unifying two traditionally separate problems—auto-rigging and motion generation—into a single feed-forward framework that requires no human rig annotations. It departs from auto-rigging methods (UniRig, MagicArticulate) that imitate artist-designed skeletons, from motion generators (AnimateAnyMesh, AnyTop) that assume a ground-truth rig, and from optimization-based inverse skinning (SSDR) that fits a rig per sequence. The key conceptual departure is the Gaussian bone representation, which replaces discrete skeletal hierarchies with continuous 3D Gaussian ellipsoids whose skinning weights are derived analytically via Mahalanobis distance, combined with a geodesic-aware refinement for topological coherence. The Motion DiT component extends the VAE's latent space with a diffusion transformer for controllable motion synthesis, building on the v-prediction parameterization from diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for per-case optimization and human rig annotations, RigMo addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck in 4D animation pipelines, enabling category-agnostic rig and motion inference from raw deformation data. The large cross-motion transfer gap (13.8 vs. 68.8 CD-L1 over per-case optimization) challenges the working assumption that per-sequence fitting is necessary for accurate rigging, suggesting that shared articulation priors learned across diverse motions are both sufficient and superior. Open questions include whether the Gaussian bone formulation scales to characters with very high joint counts or topologically changing meshes, how well it handles rigid-body interactions and contact, and whether the Motion DiT can support long-horizon or physically constrained generation beyond the 20-frame sequences evaluated here.
