## Problem

Existing few-shot font generation methods neglect glyph spatial information at two levels: (1) mainstream raster rendering practices (bounding-box centering, non-uniform scaling) introduce spatial bias that impairs vectorization and dataset quality, while the absence of unified rendering standards prevents a fair benchmark; (2) models implicitly couple shape artifacts with positional bias in a single optimization objective, hindering fine-grained shape learning and generalization. These issues are particularly acute for structurally complex Chinese glyphs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution (128×128) grayscale source glyph image (Song typeface) and k reference style glyph images (typically 4 or 8) at the same resolution, with no component or stroke labels required. The style condition is computed by averaging encoder features across all k references, allowing flexible reference selection. The output is a 128×128 grayscale target glyph image that can be directly vectorized via Potrace into a usable TTF font file, with the SPR scheme guaranteeing a reversible mapping between raster and vector coordinate spaces.

## Method

GlyphSpatialNet is a two-stage pixel-space framework built on Residual Denoising Diffusion Models (RDDM). In Stage I, a Shape-Position Decoupling (SPD) architecture is integrated into the diffusion reverse process: a shape path (two U-Net branches) predicts an initial shape estimate and noise, while a position path extracts multi-layer U-Net features, flattens and concatenates them into an MLP that predicts a 2D spatial offset φ_Δ; a Gradient Broadcasting Module (GBM) then adds a Gaussian-blurred copy of the bilinear-sampled image to enable long-range gradient propagation across pixels, allowing the position path to correct large spatial biases. In Stage II, a Style Detail Enhancement (SDE) module uses a parameter-free bilinear downsampler and a learned upsampler conditioned on the style feature to refine fine-grained details at high resolution. The entire pipeline operates in pixel space (avoiding latent-space spatial transform limitations), uses DDIM with only 5 sampling steps, and is paired with the SPR rendering scheme that eliminates spatial bias and enables accurate end-to-end vectorization.

## Results

On the 8-shot UFSC setting, GlyphSpatialNet achieves RMSE 0.0916, PSNR 25.86 dB, SSIM 0.9136, and LPIPS 0.0479, outperforming the strongest baseline MSD-Font (RMSE 0.1038, PSNR 22.68, SSIM 0.9050, LPIPS 0.0529) by a clear margin. On the harder 8-shot UFUC setting (unseen fonts and unseen characters), the method reaches RMSE 0.1588, PSNR 17.55, SSIM 0.8404, and LPIPS 0.0730, again surpassing MSD-Font (RMSE 0.1638, PSNR 17.18, SSIM 0.8319, LPIPS 0.0776). The model uses only 143M parameters and 5 DDIM sampling steps, compared to MSD-Font's 604M parameters, while requiring no component labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlyphSpatialNet outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three evaluation settings (4-shot UFSC, 8-shot UFSC, 8-shot UFUC). The closest competitor is MSD-Font [13], the only other diffusion-based method; on 8-shot UFSC, GlyphSpatialNet reduces RMSE from 0.1038 to 0.0916 (−11.8%) and improves PSNR from 22.68 to 25.86 dB (+3.18 dB), while on the harder 8-shot UFUC setting the gap narrows slightly (RMSE 0.1638→0.1588, PSNR 17.18→17.55). The GAN-based baselines—LF-Font [31], MX-Font [32], and NTF [12]—trail by a wide margin, with RMSE values roughly 2× higher than MSD-Font and 2.2× higher than GlyphSpatialNet on 8-shot UFSC. Notably, LF-Font and MX-Font require manually annotated component labels, whereas GlyphSpatialNet, NTF, and MSD-Font do not; among the label-free methods, GlyphSpatialNet achieves the best results with fewer parameters (143M vs. 604M for MSD-Font) and only 5 DDIM sampling steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Residual Denoising Diffusion Models (RDDM) as its generative backbone and adapts Spatial Transformer Network (STN) components—specifically the grid generator and bilinear sampler—into a Shape-Position Decoupling (SPD) architecture for explicit spatial modeling. It departs from the dominant style-content decoupling paradigm (LF-Font, MX-Font) and the global-style diffusion approach (MSD-Font) by treating glyph spatial information (baseline, size, control-point coordinates) as a first-class signal rather than an implicit byproduct of pixel-level optimization. The SPR rendering scheme and the OFL-licensed vector dataset address a pipeline-level gap: prior methods each used ad-hoc, spatially distorting rendering, preventing fair cross-method comparison and undermining vectorization fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified benchmark and 1.5M-glyph vector dataset fill a concrete gap in the Chinese font generation literature, where copyright restrictions and inconsistent rendering had prevented apples-to-apples evaluation. The observation that spatial noise from distorted rendering acts as an implicit regularizer for GANs but becomes the principal obstacle for diffusion models offers a useful diagnostic for practitioners choosing rendering pipelines. However, the performance gains over MSD-Font are modest (e.g., RMSE 0.1638→0.1588 on UFUC), and the pixel-space constraint limits scalability to very high resolutions; the authors also note that the high production cost of Chinese fonts restricts coverage of calligraphic and extreme styles, leaving an open question about whether the explicit spatial modeling approach transfers to those regimes.
