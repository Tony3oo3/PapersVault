## Problem

Existing photorealistic head avatar methods either treat hair as a rigid outer shell attached to the head or learn hair dynamics from data that cannot generalize to unseen scenarios like wind. There is no method that combines a controllable, expression-driven face with a strand-based hair representation that is directly compatible with classical physics engines, enabling physically plausible and generalizable hair motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261184/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dynamic multiview video sequence (16 cameras, 8 expressions from Ava-256) for facial expression learning and a separate 360-degree static head capture for strand-based hair geometry reconstruction via NeuralHaircut. The output is a layered avatar: a FLAME-rigged 3D Gaussian face layer and a strand-based hair layer (60,000 strands × 16 segments each) with structured Gaussians, where the hair layer can be driven by a physics simulator for arbitrary head poses and external forces, and the face layer is independently controlled by FLAME expression and pose parameters.

## Method

PhysHead uses a two-stage layered optimization: first, a VLM (Nano-Banana) removes hair from sparse multiview frames, a shared FLAME texture is optimized via differentiable rendering, and Poisson image editing blends the rendered texture with original skin regions to produce bald training targets; the head layer (FLAME-attached 3D Gaussians) is then trained on these generated images. In the second stage, strand-based hair geometry from NeuralHaircut is initialized with structured Gaussians oriented by Frenet-Serret frames, and only color and spherical harmonics are optimized under a masked photometric loss plus a novel strand-level color consistency loss that propagates appearance from visible to occluded strands. For animation, sparse guiding strands are simulated with the Bullet physics engine using semi-implicit Euler integration, and dense strand motion is transferred via k-nearest-neighbor skinning (k=10) that interpolates relative displacements, enabling real-time physics-driven hair dynamics compatible with arbitrary head poses and external forces.

## Results

On the Ava-256 dataset, PhysHead achieves a PSNR of 30.28 dB, SSIM of 0.946, and LPIPS of 0.061 for hair appearance reconstruction, slightly trailing GaussianHaircut on PSNR (30.55) but surpassing it on SSIM (0.946 vs. 0.915) and LPIPS (0.061 vs. 0.071). The strand-level color consistency loss is critical: removing it drops PSNR by over 2 dB (to 28.24) and degrades SSIM to 0.893, confirming that the regularization is essential for plausible appearance in unobserved hair regions. Qualitative comparisons show that PhysHead uniquely supports physics-driven hair animation, whereas all baselines produce rigid hair under dynamic head motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GaussianHaircut [94], PhysHead trades a small PSNR deficit (30.28 vs. 30.55) for substantially better perceptual quality (SSIM 0.946 vs. 0.915, LPIPS 0.061 vs. 0.071) and, crucially, produces hair strands that are free of skin-colored artifacts inside the head, making the result directly usable in physics simulation. Against Gaussian Avatars [59] and Gaussian Head Avatars [91], the comparison is primarily qualitative: both baselines model hair as unstructured Gaussians tied to the 3DMM, so hair moves rigidly with the head, while PhysHead's strand-based representation generalizes to novel driving signals such as head rotation, nodding, and wind. Against the concurrent HairCUP [31], PhysHead avoids the SDS-based bald-head generation artifacts (e.g., inconsistent skin tones, boundary seams) by using a VLM-generated appearance proxy blended via Poisson editing, and its strand-based hair supports physics simulation whereas HairCUP's unstructured Gaussians do not. Overall, PhysHead is the only method among the four that simultaneously provides photorealistic appearance, expression control via FLAME, and physically plausible dynamic hair.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysHead sits at the intersection of three established lines of work: (1) Gaussian-splatting-based animatable head avatars (Gaussian Avatars, GHA, GPHM) that model appearance but treat hair as a rigid shell; (2) strand-based hair reconstruction (NeuralHaircut, GaussianHaircut, GroomCap) that captures hair geometry and appearance but ignores facial expression; and (3) physics-based hair simulation (Bullet, Maya, Quaffure) that produces dynamic motion but relies on artist-crafted appearance. The key departure is the layered 3DGS representation that binds face Gaussians to FLAME for expression control while attaching structured Gaussians to strand segments for physics-driven animation, combined with a VLM-based (Nano-Banana) bald-image generation strategy that replaces the SDS-based approach used by the concurrent HairCUP. The strand-level color consistency loss is a new regularization that addresses the unsupervised interior-strand problem inherent to any strand-based appearance model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to deliver physically plausible, wind-responsive hair dynamics on a photorealistic, expression-controllable 3DGS head avatar, opening the door to simulation-ready avatars for film, gaming, and virtual production without manual artist intervention. The layered representation also enables practical editing operations (hairstyle transfer, geometry/appearance editing) that are difficult with monolithic Gaussian representations. However, the method inherits limitations from NeuralHaircut (poor handling of curly hair), depends on foreground/hair mask quality for clean disentanglement, and has not been evaluated for real-time performance or relighting. Open questions include whether the VLM-based bald generation generalizes to extreme occlusions, whether the strand-level consistency loss scales to very dense grooms, and how the approach handles dynamic lighting changes during simulation.
