## Problem

Adversarial training (AT) achieves strong robustness but sacrifices clean accuracy and lacks generative capability, while Joint Energy-based Models (JEMs) unify classification and generation yet remain far less robust than AT. This inherent trilemma—balancing classification accuracy, adversarial robustness, and generative quality—raises the question of whether a single model can simultaneously achieve all three, a goal that has been rarely explored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261152/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images at 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (ImageNet subset of 100 classes) with class labels, using a WideResNet-28-10 backbone. During training, adversarial examples are generated on-the-fly via a reverse SGLD process with an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 8/255 and 5 sampling steps, rather than being precomputed. At inference, the model outputs class predictions, and at generation time it produces images via SGLD sampling with informative initialization from the learned energy landscape.

## Method

EB-JDAT models the full joint distribution p_θ(x, x̃, y) over clean samples, adversarial examples, and labels, decomposing it into p_θ(y|x̃,x)·p_θ(x̃|x)·p_θ(x). The key mechanism is a min-max energy optimization: the inner maximization samples adversarial examples by running SGLD in the opposite direction of the log-probability gradient (pushing them into high-energy, low-density regions), while the outer minimization pulls them back into low-energy regions by reducing the energy gap E_θ(x̃|x) − E_θ(x). The total training gradient is a weighted sum of three components—clean-data energy (h₁), adversarial-data energy (h₂), and cross-entropy for robust classification (h₃)—all computed via contrastive divergence approximations. This framework is model-agnostic and plugs into existing JEM variants (SADAJEM, JEM++) without architectural changes, effectively making the classifier a genuine conditional EBM for the adversarial distribution p_θ(x̃|x).

## Results

On CIFAR-10, EB-JDAT-SADAJEM achieves 90.39% clean accuracy, 68.76% PGD-20 robustness, and 66.12% AutoAttack robustness, outperforming the best prior AT method (DHAT-CFA at 54.05% AA) by +12.07% while retaining near-standard clean accuracy (96.10% for standard training). On CIFAR-100, it reaches 68.35% clean accuracy and 35.57% AA, surpassing the best baseline (DHAT-CFA at 30.93% AA) by +4.64%. On the ImageNet-100 subset (64×64), EB-JDAT-JEM++ achieves 63.02% clean accuracy and 32.40% AA, exceeding WEAT (24.52% AA) by +7.88%. In generation quality, EB-JDAT-SADAJEM attains FID 27.42 and IS 8.05, competitive with SADAJEM (FID 17.38) and far superior to energy-based AT methods like WEAT (FID 177.92 under informative initialization).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EB-JDAT-SADAJEM dominates all 10 AT baselines on CIFAR-10 AutoAttack robustness (66.12% vs. best prior 55.52% for LAS-AWP, +10.60%) while maintaining 90.37% clean accuracy—only 5.73% below standard training (96.10%), whereas most AT methods lose 9–13% clean accuracy. On CIFAR-100, it leads by +4.64% AA over DHAT-CFA (35.57% vs. 30.93%) and +4.80% over LAS-AWP. Against generative-data-augmented AT (Table 3), EB-JDAT-SADAJEM achieves 66.30% AA on CIFAR-10 in 66.64 GPU-hours, outperforming SCORE (61.51% AA, ~1,438h) and Better DM (63.35% AA, ~1,438h) with ~20× less compute. In the JEM/energy-based AT comparison (Table 5), EB-JDAT-SADAJEM achieves 66.30% AA versus 49.02% for WEAT [31] and 28.43% for JEAT [55], while retaining FID 27.42 (vs. WEAT's 177.92 under informative initialization) and IS 8.05 (vs. WEAT's 3.50). The method also outperforms the original JEM [15] (7.28% PGD) and SADAJEM [48] (43.64% PGD) in robustness by +58.84% and +22.48% respectively, at the cost of a modest ~5.7% clean accuracy drop relative to SADAJEM alone (96.03% → 90.37%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: Joint Energy-based Models (JEM, JEM++, SADAJEM) that reinterpret softmax classifiers as energy-based models for joint classification and generation, and Adversarial Training (PGD-AT, TRADES) that achieves robustness via min-max optimization at the cost of clean accuracy and generative capability. Prior energy-based AT methods (JEAT, WEAT) model either p_θ(x̃, y) or p_θ(y|x̃, x), treating adversarial training as an external regularization on JEMs. EB-JDAT departs by modeling the full joint distribution p_θ(x, x̃, y) and introducing a min-max energy optimization that explicitly aligns the energy landscapes of clean, adversarial, and generated samples, rather than merely adding a robust classification loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the long-standing accuracy-robustness-generation trilemma can be substantially alleviated within a single model, achieving SOTA robustness (66.12% AA on CIFAR-10) while retaining near-standard accuracy (90.39%) and competitive FID scores. The energy landscape analysis provides a unifying diagnostic tool: the degree of overlap between clean/adversarial/generated energy distributions predicts robustness and generative quality respectively. Limitations include reliance on a single architecture (WRN28-10), a small ImageNet subset (100 classes at 64×64), and a noted risk of model collapse that the adversarial energy term (h₂) is critical to prevent. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViTs, deeper ResNets) and full-resolution ImageNet, and whether the SGLD-based adversarial sampling can be replaced by cheaper alternatives.
