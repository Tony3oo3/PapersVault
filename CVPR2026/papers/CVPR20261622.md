## Problem

Existing adversarial evaluations of VLN agents rely on texture or patch perturbations that produce highly unusual visual patterns rarely encountered in everyday indoor settings, limiting their practical relevance. This paper asks whether indoor lighting—an intrinsic, naturally variable scene attribute that pervades daily life—can systematically expose VLN vulnerabilities under realistic conditions, and whether a black-box framework can exploit it without access to model internals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261622/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The attacker controls a single global lighting intensity (or on/off indicator) over a 3D indoor scene rendered in AI2-THOR, while the VLN agent receives egocentric RGB observations and a natural-language instruction. Under the black-box assumption, each query returns only the agent's position and movement heading, not internal representations. The method outputs a constant adversarial intensity (SILA) or a per-timestep on/off sequence (DILA), evaluated by Attack Success Rate (fraction of clean-success episodes that fail) and Episode Length (average timesteps, where longer indicates less efficient navigation).

## Method

ILA comprises two complementary attack modes. SILA performs black-box optimization over a constant global intensity using a timestep-weighted trajectory loss (weights increase linearly toward episode end to emphasize late-stage, irreversible errors) and an ε-greedy two-sided comparison that probes slightly brighter and darker candidates each iteration, updating toward the direction that maximizes loss. DILA inherits the SILA-found intensity as its 'on' level and strategically triggers on/off switches at critical timesteps via a one-step lookahead surrogate: at each step it simulates the agent's next state under both current and switched lighting, computes the angle β between the toward-target vector and the agent's heading, and flips the light only when the switch increases β, thereby progressively steering the agent away from the goal without requiring full-rollout evaluations.

## Results

On the CHORES benchmark (576 episodes total), the combined SILA+DILA attack achieves ASRs of 96.23% (SPOC-ObjectNav), 100% (SPOC-Fetch), 70.73% (SPOC-RoomVisit), 52.73% (FLaRe-ObjectNav), 93.58% (FLaRe-Fetch), and 74.80% (FLaRe-RoomVisit), substantially outperforming the Texture-GA baseline across all six task-model combinations. Episode Length increases dramatically under attack, e.g., SPOC-ObjectNav EL rises from 115.35 (clean) to 234.27 (SILA+DILA), indicating severely degraded navigation efficiency. On the GPT-4o-nav agent in EmbodiedBench, SILA and SILA+DILA achieve ASRs of 85.37% and 87.80% respectively, confirming the vulnerability extends to LLM-based navigation agents.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary external baseline is Texture-GA, a black-box genetic-algorithm optimization of wall textures adapted from Chen et al. [3]. SILA+DILA outperforms Texture-GA on all six task-model combinations in ASR, with the largest margins on SPOC-ObjectNav (96.23% vs. 54.90%, a 41.3-point gap) and FLaRe-Fetch (93.58% vs. 53.98%, a 39.6-point gap). Even SILA alone surpasses Texture-GA in every setting, e.g., 60.38% vs. 54.90% on SPOC-ObjectNav and 68.81% vs. 53.98% on FLaRe-Fetch. The Random Intensity control (uniformly sampled constant brightness) achieves much lower ASRs (12.20%–75.00%), confirming that the gains come from the optimization procedure rather than mere brightness variation. The authors also report that their attack generalizes to the GPT-4o-nav agent on EmbodiedBench (85.37%–87.80% ASR), a setting where no texture-based baseline is directly comparable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of VLN adversarial attacks (texture perturbation [3], differentiable-rendering appearance optimization [25], universal perturbations [26]) by targeting a natural, intrinsic scene attribute—global indoor lighting—rather than adding artificial patterns to object surfaces. Methodologically, it adopts a black-box search (ε-greedy two-sided comparison) over a single scalar (lighting intensity) instead of gradient-based or genetic-algorithm optimization over high-dimensional texture spaces, making the attack more physically plausible. The VLN models under test (SPOC, FLaRe) and the CHORES benchmark are drawn directly from the embodied-navigation literature, but the paper's contribution is the attack framework itself, not a new navigation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is strong: lighting changes are ubiquitous in real indoor settings (people dim lights, switch rooms on/off), so the vulnerability exposed here is far more likely to be encountered in deployment than adversarial textures. The consistent degradation across two distinct model families and an LLM-based agent (GPT-4o-nav) suggests the weakness is in visual perception rather than a single architecture's quirk. Key limitations include the single-global-light assumption, simulation-only evaluation in AI2-THOR, and the requirement for step-level position/heading feedback; open questions include whether illumination-aware training or multi-light control changes the picture, and whether results transfer to uncontrolled real-world lighting.
