## Problem

UAV object detection pipelines are overwhelmingly built around RGB or dual-modal pairings, leaving unresolved how three heterogeneous sensing modalities (RGB, thermal LWIR, and event) should interact inside a modern detector so each compensates for the others' failure modes. No prior work has systematically studied tri-modal fusion placement, mechanism, or modality subsets under a single controlled setting, and no synchronized tri-modal UAV dataset with resolution-aligned annotations existed to enable such a study.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a pre-aligned five-channel tensor (3 RGB + 1 thermal + 1 event) at 301×391 pixels, padded to the nearest multiple of 32 for FPN compatibility. Event frames are constructed by binning polarity events within a 33.3 ms window centered at each RGB/thermal timestamp, preserving ON/OFF polarity before per-window normalization. The detector outputs YOLO-format bounding boxes for a single vehicle class, evaluated via COCO mAP and mAP50.

## Method

The architecture uses a dual-stream four-stage Mix-Transformer (MiT) backbone with independent weights for an RGB stream and a combined thermal-event (TE) stream, producing multi-scale features at strides {4, 8, 16, 32}. At selected stages, a fusion block applies MAGE (Modality-Aware Gated Exchange), which computes directional channel and spatial gates from the concatenated RGB-TE descriptor to modulate cross-stream residuals while preserving each stream's identity path, followed by BiTE (Bidirectional Token Exchange), which performs symmetric cross-attention between the rectified token sequences and refines the concatenated result with a depthwise 3×3 convolution and 1×1 projection back to the original channel width. Because fusion preserves spatial stride and channel width, the downstream FPN and Faster R-CNN head remain unchanged across all ablation configurations. The paper introduces a 10,489-frame synchronized UAV dataset (24,223 annotated vehicles, 61% day / 39% night) and conducts 61 controlled ablations over fusion depth, mechanism (MAGE+BiTE, CSSA, GAFF), modality subsets, and backbone capacity (MiT-B0 through B4).

## Results

The tri-modal MiT-B1 detector (RGB+Thermal+Event with MAGE+BiTE fusion) achieves 84.24% mAP and 98.95% mAP50 on the proposed 10,489-frame UAV dataset, with 60.01M parameters. The strongest dual-modal configuration (RGB+Thermal) reaches 83.42% mAP, while the best external RGB-thermal baseline (YOLOv11-RGBT) attains 82.08% mAP. Cross-dataset transfer of the RGB+Thermal model yields 81.79% mAP on M3FD and 69.21% mAP on RTDOD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The tri-modal MiT-B1 detector (84.24% mAP) outperforms YOLOv11-RGBT (82.08% mAP) by 2.16 points and DetFusion (78.00% mAP) by 6.24 points on the proposed UAV dataset. Even the dual-modal RGB+Thermal variant (83.42% mAP) exceeds both external baselines, indicating that the fusion architecture itself provides a meaningful advantage over existing RGB-thermal detectors. The tri-modal setting adds a further 0.82 mAP over RGB+Thermal, with events contributing primarily in motion-affected and night-time thermal-clutter scenarios rather than uniformly across all conditions. On cross-dataset evaluation, the RGB+Thermal model transfers to M3FD (81.79% mAP) and RTDOD (69.21% mAP), demonstrating reasonable generalization to established RGB-thermal benchmarks despite being trained on a single-class UAV dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first to integrate RGB, thermal (LWIR), and event camera streams within a single unified detector, whereas prior multi-modal detection work has been restricted to dual-modal pairings (RGB-thermal or RGB-event). Architecturally, it repurposes the MiT hierarchical transformer backbone and a standard Faster R-CNN/FPN detection head, inserting novel fusion operators (MAGE and BiTE) at resolution-aligned stages, and benchmarks them against established dual-modal fusion mechanisms (CSSA, GAFF) that had not been evaluated under tri-modal or hierarchical-transformer settings. The contribution is thus less a new backbone or detector family than a controlled architectural design space—fusion depth, mechanism, and modality subset—coupled with a purpose-built synchronized tri-modal UAV dataset that did not previously exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 61-experiment ablation provides the first systematic evidence that fusion depth and mechanism are decisive for multi-modal detection, and that a lightweight CSSA variant at early stages recovers most of the tri-modal benefit at minimal cost—practically useful for SWaP-constrained UAV deployment. However, the tri-modal gain over the strongest dual-modal pair (RGB+Thermal, 83.42% vs. 84.24% mAP) is modest, and the single-class (vehicle) scope limits generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the findings extend to multi-class detection, whether temporal (video-level) tri-modal fusion can capture more of the event stream's asynchronous information, and whether adaptive modality selection can further close the gap in failure regimes where events contribute.
