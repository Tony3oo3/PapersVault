## Problem

Prior 4D reconstruction methods are fragmented: they either predict dense scene flow for only 2 frames or sparse 3D point tracks, require per-scene iterative optimization that precludes real-time use, and produce outputs in normalized (up-to-scale) coordinates rather than metric space. Additionally, most methods accept only RGB images and cannot exploit the diverse sensor modalities (depth, IMU, radar) available on robotic platforms. Any4D addresses all three gaps simultaneously with a unified feed-forward model.

## Input / Output

Any4D consumes N RGB frames (up to 64 in benchmarks) plus optional per-view auxiliary inputs: depth maps, camera intrinsics, camera poses from IMU, and Doppler velocity from RADAR. The output is a factored 4D representation rather than a single monolithic tensor: a global scalar metric scale factor, per-view egocentric factors (ray directions and scale-normalized depth in local camera coordinates), and per-view allocentric factors (forward scene flow from view 0 to all other views, and camera poses as translation+quaternion in the first-view coordinate frame). Metric-scale pointmaps are recovered by composing these factors (scale × pose × rays × depth), and post-motion geometry is obtained by adding the allocentric scene flow.

## Method

The architecture is an N-view transformer with three stages: modality-specific encoders (DINOv2 ViT-Large for RGB, shallow CNNs for depth/intrinsics/Doppler, 4-layer MLPs for poses and metric scale) that project all inputs into a shared 1024-dim patch-token space; a 12-block alternating-attention transformer backbone (768-dim, Flash Attention, no RoPE) that attends across all views; and separate DPT heads for geometry (rays, depth, confidence) and motion (allocentric scene flow), plus lightweight decoders for poses and the log-scale metric factor. The key design insight is the factored egocentric/allocentric decomposition, which enables training on a heterogeneous mixture of geometry-only datasets (BlendedMVS, MegaDepth, ScanNet++) and dynamic datasets (Kubric, Dynamic Replica, VKITTI2) with partial supervision via scale-invariant losses. Training initializes from MapAnything weights, applies multi-modal conditioning at 70% probability with independent 50% dropout per modality, and uses a 10× upweighting on dynamic regions in the scene flow loss to counteract the dominance of static points.

## Results

Any4D achieves state-of-the-art 3D tracking with 0.50s runtime on 50 frames (H100), which is 15× faster than SpatialTrackerV2 (11.56s) while delivering lower EPE (3.89 vs 5.45 m) and higher APD (7.81% vs 4.48%) on DriveTrack. On dense scene flow (Kubric-4D Dynamic Camera), Any4D achieves EPE 1.13 m and τ 83.38%, outperforming St4RTrack (EPE 2.44 m, τ 11.83%) by 2–3× on APD. For video depth, Any4D matches VGGT (rel 0.13, δ₁.₂₅ 86.28%) among single-step feed-forward methods and is competitive with optimization-based methods like SpatialTrackerV2 (rel 0.09, δ₁.₂₅ 88.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Any4D decisively outperforms all baselines on 3D tracking (Table 1): it achieves the lowest EPE and highest APD across all four datasets while running 15× faster than SpatialTrackerV2 (0.50s vs 11.56s) and 2.2× faster than St4RTrack. On dense scene flow (Table 2), Any4D dominates St4RTrack by 2–3× on APD (18.14% vs 5.79% on Kubric-4D Dynamic Camera) and by a large margin on scene-flow inlier ratio τ (83.38% vs 11.83%), while also outperforming all reconstruction+optical-flow compositions (e.g., VGGT+SEA-RAFT: EPE 11.80 vs Any4D 1.13). For video depth (Table 3), Any4D ties VGGT on average relative error (0.13) and slightly exceeds it on δ₁.₂₅ (86.28% vs 85.85%), but remains behind optimization-based methods like SpatialTrackerV2 (rel 0.09, δ₁.₂₅ 88.80%) and task-specific models like DepthCrafter (rel 0.15, δ₁.₂₅ 85.23%)—though Any4D requires no per-scene optimization. The key differentiator is that Any4D produces dense, metric-scale 4D output in a single feed-forward pass, whereas SpatialTrackerV2 and St4RTrack either require iterative optimization or only support sparse point queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

Any4D builds directly on the MapAnything multi-view transformer architecture [32] and the alternating-attention backbone from VGGT [77], initializing weights from MapAnything and adopting its modality-specific encoder design. It departs from prior 4D reconstruction work (MonST3R, DUSt3R, SpatialTrackerV2, St4RTrack) in three key ways: (1) it performs a single N-view feed-forward pass rather than pairwise or iterative inference, (2) it outputs metric-scale quantities rather than up-to-scale, and (3) it unifies geometry, motion, and pose prediction under a factored egocentric/allocentric representation that enables training on heterogeneous datasets with partial annotations. The factored output design is the conceptual departure that allows mixing static geometry datasets (MegaDepth, ScanNet++) with dynamic motion datasets (Kubric, Dynamic Replica) in a single training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Any4D establishes a practical path toward real-time dense 4D reconstruction by achieving 15× speedup and 2–3× accuracy gains over the closest baselines in a single forward pass, which is critical for downstream applications in robotics (predictive control), AR/VR, and generative video. The multi-modal input flexibility (depth, IMU poses, radar Doppler) makes it directly deployable on heterogeneous robotic platforms where prior methods assumed image-only inputs. Key open questions include: the reference-view dependency (objects must be visible at frame 0), the assumption of noise-free auxiliary sensors, and whether performance on highly dynamic or wide-baseline scenes can be improved with richer training data. The paper's construction of unified allocentric 4D benchmarks (combining TAPVID-3D, Dynamic Replica, LSFOdyssey, and scene flow datasets) also addresses a fragmentation problem in the field.
