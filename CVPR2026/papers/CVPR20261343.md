## Problem

Processing information-rich images (infographics, multi-page documents) in LVLMs requires generating a large number of visual tokens, creating significant computational overhead. Existing token-pruning methods rely on attention weights from LLM decoding layers, which are unreliable and can lead to hallucinations, while also suffering from semantic fragmentation (text spanning multiple tokens) and contextual entanglement (relevant tokens mixed with irrelevant context via global self-attention). The paper asks whether selectively focusing on instruction-relevant image regions—rather than pruning individual tokens—can improve both accuracy and efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (processed through a frozen ViT and projection layer into a 2D spatial grid of visual tokens) and a text instruction (question) as input. It produces a compact set of instruction-relevant visual tokens—re-encoded in isolation from the selected regions—which are concatenated with the textual instruction and fed to a frozen LLM to generate a free-form text answer. The sliding window (10×10, stride 7) and adaptive top-k selection (target ratio r=0.6 of total image area) determine how many regions are retained, yielding roughly 55–65% of the vanilla FLOPs.

## Method

PinPoint is a two-stage framework. In Region Selection, visual tokens are reshaped into a 2D grid and a sliding window produces region-level representations; K=100 learnable guidance queries then interact with both region features and BPE-embedded text via scaled dot-product cross-attention to create modality-aligned embeddings, and regions are ranked by cosine similarity to the instruction until their enclosing area exceeds a predefined ratio. In Region Refinement, only the selected regions are re-encoded by the ViT in isolation, stripping away the irrelevant global context that contaminates tokens through self-attention and yielding a denser, more discriminative token set. Training uses two contrastive objectives—an inter-modal loss aligning visual and text query embeddings across the batch, and an intra-image loss pushing the instruction embedding toward the positive (GT-region) window and away from all other windows in the same image—while freezing the LLM, vision encoder, and projector, so only the guidance queries and two MLP layers (≈1.4% of total parameters) are optimized.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT-7B, PinPoint achieves ANLS of 0.3024 on InfoVQA, 0.6472 on SPDocVQA, 0.3866 on MPDocVQA, and 0.7608 on GQA, surpassing the vanilla model on InfoVQA (+18.5%), MPDocVQA, and GQA while using only 55–65% of the FLOPs. On Qwen2-VL-7B, PinPoint reaches 0.7140 (InfoVQA), 0.8977 (SPDocVQA), 0.6723 (MPDocVQA), and 0.7624 (GQA), maintaining competitive accuracy at 44–56% of vanilla FLOPs. Training requires only 1.4% of total parameters (99.4M for LLaVA-NeXT) and converges in ~1.33 GPU-hours on two A100s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PinPoint consistently outperforms all four token-pruning baselines (ToMe, FastV, PyramidDrop, SparseVLM) on LLaVA-NeXT across all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on InfoVQA where it achieves 0.3024 ANLS versus SparseVLM's 0.2428 (+24.5%) and ToMe's 0.1975 (+53%). On Qwen2-VL, the advantage is even more pronounced: PinPoint scores 0.7140 on InfoVQA versus FastV's 0.5130 and PDrop's 0.4506, while also using fewer FLOPs than all pruning methods. Against the region-focusing method ViCrop (Table 2), PinPoint achieves higher ANLS on all four datasets while using roughly 3–8× fewer FLOPs (e.g., 25.48T vs. 147.39T on InfoVQA), since ViCrop requires multiple VLM passes. Notably, on LLaVA-NeXT, PinPoint even surpasses the vanilla (no-pruning) model on InfoVQA, MPDocVQA, and GQA, demonstrating that selective region focus can improve accuracy rather than merely trade it for speed. On Qwen2-VL, PinPoint remains slightly below vanilla on InfoVQA (0.7140 vs. 0.7399) and MPDocVQA (0.6723 vs. 0.7775), but narrows the gap substantially compared to other pruning methods and still delivers ~45–55% FLOP savings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PinPoint departs from the dominant token-pruning paradigm (FastV, PyramidDrop, SparseVLM, ToMe) that estimates per-token importance via LLM attention weights, arguing that such maps are unreliable and that individual-token decisions fragment semantic units like text spans. It also contrasts with iterative region-focusing methods like ViCrop, which require multiple expensive VLM passes to localize relevant areas. Conceptually, the method repurposes the learnable-query aggregation idea from BLIP-2/InstructBLIP but grounds it in a contrastive alignment objective between instruction text and sliding-window region features, adding a region re-encoding step to strip global self-attention contamination. The paper further extends the annotation paradigm of prior VQA benchmarks by providing multi-box supporting-element labels rather than a single answer bounding box.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that selectively re-encoding only instruction-relevant regions outperforms processing the full image (and that attention-based pruning can be worse than random sampling on dense infographics) challenges the default assumption that more visual tokens are always better for LVLMs. The lightweight training recipe (1.4% of parameters, 1.33 GPU-hours) makes the approach easy to adopt as a plug-in for existing LVLMs. Open questions include whether the fixed region-ratio heuristic generalizes to arbitrarily complex multi-page documents, whether the method scales to 70B+ models where the token budget is even more critical, and whether the contrastive region-selection can be made fully training-free or distilled into the base model.
