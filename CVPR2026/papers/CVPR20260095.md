## Problem

In Long-Tailed Class Incremental Learning (LTCIL), the model-induced prior drifts as new tasks arrive, causing predictions to increasingly favor recently seen classes and skewing posteriors away from Bayes-optimal decisions. Existing methods either assume static frequency-based priors that become outdated under continual optimization, or rely on costly two-stage retraining to realign classifiers, leaving a gap between the priors actually learned by the network and the balanced evaluation distribution.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of RGB image tasks, each with a long-tailed class distribution (imbalance factors ranging from 10 to ~435), plus a small exemplar buffer (5–20 per class) from prior tasks. At inference it outputs class labels derived from logits that have been adjusted by a per-class prior-correction term; during training it additionally produces a modified cross-entropy loss that reweights logits by the ratio of the estimated model-induced prior to the target prior. The correction operates purely at the logit-prior interface, adding only O(K) memory for a length-K running vector with no extra forward passes or stored activations.

## Method

AdaPrior estimates the model-induced prior P_m(y) online via an exponential moving average of softmax outputs over training batches, then applies Bayesian prior alignment at two levels. During training, the AdaPrior Loss modifies the standard cross-entropy by adding log(P_m(y)/P_t(y)) to each logit, which decomposes into a prediction-error term plus a KL divergence between the model prior and the target prior, jointly minimizing both in a single stage. At inference, a lightweight post-hoc correction shifts logits by α(log P_t(y) − log P_m(y)), where α ∈ [0,1] tempers the correction strength to balance bias and variance under noisy prior estimates. The paper provides a convergence theorem showing the EMA estimator converges almost surely under stationarity and tracks bounded drift with error O(γ) + O(d̄/γ), and demonstrates that the approach is plug-and-play, backbone-agnostic, and requires no auxiliary classifiers, balanced replay sets, or additional training phases.

## Results

On the Ordered LTCIL benchmark (IF=100), Full AdaPrior achieves 50.43% average accuracy on CIFAR100-LT (LFS, 10 tasks) and 61.36% on ImageNet-subset-LT (LFH, 5 tasks), setting new state-of-the-art across all splits. On the harsher iNaturalist18-subset (IF≈435), Full AdaPrior reaches 74.41% (ordered, 5 tasks) and 69.52% (shuffled, 5 tasks), surpassing the best two-stage method GVAlign by 2.0% and 2.3% respectively. The single-stage AdaPrior Loss alone already outperforms all prior single-stage baselines (e.g., GradReweighting) by 1–4% on most settings, and the post-hoc correction adds a further 1–3% on top.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Full AdaPrior outperforms the strongest single-stage baseline GradReweighting [15] by 0.19% on CIFAR100-LT (LFS-10) and by 1.66% on ImageNet-subset (LFS-10), with larger margins (3–5%) on the harder LFS-20 and shuffled settings. Against the best two-stage method LUCIR+GVAlign [21], Full AdaPrior gains 1.07% on CIFAR100-LT (LFH-5) and 3.08% on ImageNet-subset (LFH-10), while being single-stage and requiring no secondary balanced fine-tuning phase. On the extreme-imbalance iNaturalist18-subset (IF≈435), Full AdaPrior beats GVAlign by 2.0% (ordered-5) and 5.1% (shuffled-10), demonstrating robustness under natural long-tail distributions. Notably, AdaPrior Loss alone already surpasses LUCIR+GVAlign on ImageNet LFH settings (61.79 vs. 60.80 for 5 tasks), showing that the training-time prior alignment is sufficient to match or exceed two-stage methods without any post-hoc step. Against classical CIL baselines (iCaRL, IL2M, SSIL, BiC), AdaPrior leads by 5–15% across all settings, confirming that prior correction addresses a failure mode these methods do not explicitly handle. The only setting where Full AdaPrior is marginally below AdaPrior Loss is ImageNet LFH (61.36 vs. 61.79 for 5 tasks), suggesting the post-hoc correction is most beneficial when the model-induced prior has drifted further from the target.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaPrior extends the logit-adjustment lineage (Menon et al. 2020; Bhat et al. 2025 Prior2Posterior) by replacing static, frequency-based priors with a dynamically tracked model-induced prior estimated via EMA. It departs from the dominant two-stage LTCIL paradigm (LWS, GVAlign, FOSTER) that requires a separate balanced fine-tuning phase, instead unifying training-time debiasing (AdaPrior Loss) and inference-time correction (post-hoc logit shift) in a single stage. The base pipeline is LUCIR, and the theoretical framing draws on Bayesian posterior correction, but the key conceptual departure is identifying the model-induced prior P_m(y) — not the dataset prior P(y) — as the quantity that actually drifts in continual streams and must be tracked online.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a lightweight O(K)-memory EMA update plus a single logit offset consistently outperforms heavier two-stage methods across four datasets and both LFS/LFH protocols, removing the computational and scalability burden of balanced fine-tuning. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 3) that the EMA estimator tracks slow drift with bounded error gives practitioners confidence in the approach's stability. Open questions include whether the method transfers cleanly to transformer backbones (the paper only validates cross-backbone on PODNET, a CNN), how it behaves under very rapid or non-stationary distribution shifts beyond the bounded-drift assumption, and whether the iNaturalist18-subset (100 classes, IF≈435) generalizes to the full 10k-class iNaturalist setting.
