## Problem

Existing diffusion-based visual counterfactual explanation methods suffer from two intertwined limitations: high computational cost due to nested denoising or multi-stage processing (yielding O(T²) complexity), and spatial imprecision from global classifier guidance that produces scattered, semantically entangled edits rather than localized modifications. While spatial-constraint methods improve localization, their static masks cannot adapt to evolving semantic regions during reverse diffusion, limiting both precision and generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (128×128 for CelebA, 256×256 for CelebA-HQ and ImageNet, 512×256 for BDD100K), a target class label, a pretrained classifier, and pretrained unconditional DDPM weights as input, and produces a single counterfactual RGB image as output. It operates entirely in pixel space (not latent space) with 200 sampling steps starting from timestep τ=60, and is fully training-free—no fine-tuning or additional parameters are introduced beyond a single gradient scaling factor s and mask hyperparameters (k, ρ).

## Method

MaskDiME is a training-free, single-stage diffusion framework that replaces DiME's recursive O(T²) denoising with a one-step Tweedie's formula estimation of the clean image at each reverse step, reducing complexity to O(T). Its core innovation is an adaptive dual-mask mechanism: at every diffusion timestep, two binary masks are constructed from the spatial gradient magnitude of the classification loss with respect to the current clean-image estimate—M_t^z (top-k% gradient pixels) constrains the noisy-to-noisy update, while M_t^x (top-ρk%, a strict subset) constrains the clean-image blending, ensuring edits remain localized to decision-relevant regions that shift as semantics evolve during denoising. The joint loss combines classification, perceptual, and L1 terms, with a single scaling factor s controlling overall guidance strength. This design achieves over 30× speedup over DiME while producing spatially coherent, semantically consistent counterfactuals across face, driving, and multi-class classification domains.

## Results

On CelebA (Smile), MaskDiME achieves the best FID (0.71), sFID (3.29), FVA (100%), FS (0.91), COUT (0.87), and FR (100%), outperforming all baselines including ACE (FID 1.27, FR 97.6%) and RCSB (FID 2.98, FR 99.8%). On CelebA-HQ (Smile), it attains FID 2.51 and sFID 18.1, again the best among all methods. On BDD100K and BDD-OIA, MaskDiME achieves 100% FR and the highest COUT (0.85 and 0.80 respectively). On ImageNet, it ranks top-2 across all metrics for all three category pairs, achieving the best S³ and FR (99.7–100%) while trailing RCSB on FID (e.g., 32.5 vs 8.0 on Sorrel-Zebra).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskDiME outperforms DiME [20] across nearly all metrics on CelebA Smile (FID 0.71 vs 3.17, COUT 0.87 vs 0.53, FR 100% vs 97.2%) while being >30× faster due to eliminating nested denoising. Against ACE [21], MaskDiME achieves lower FID (0.71 vs 1.27) and higher FR (100% vs 97.6%), though ACE retains a slight edge in sparsity (MNAC 2.94 vs 2.78) and semantic plausibility (CD 1.73 vs 2.41). Compared to FastDiME [45], MaskDiME improves FID by 5.8× (0.71 vs 4.18) and COUT by nearly 2× (0.87 vs 0.45), while also running 2.5× faster. Against RCSB [40], MaskDiME achieves substantially better FID (0.71 vs 2.98) and sFID (3.29 vs 4.79) with equal COUT (0.87) and higher FR (100% vs 99.8%), though RCSB edits fewer attributes (MNAC 2.24 vs 2.78). On ImageNet, where DiME and FastDiME essentially fail (FR ≈ 0–20%), MaskDiME achieves 99.7–100% FR and the best S³ scores, though RCSB still leads on FID (8.0 vs 32.5 on Sorrel-Zebra), reflecting the inherent difficulty of multi-class counterfactual generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskDiME builds directly on the gradient-guided diffusion paradigm introduced by DiME and the linear-time inference strategy of FastDiME (Tweedie's formula for one-step clean-image estimation), but departs from both by replacing DiME's O(T²) nested denoising with a single-stage O(T) process and replacing FastDiME's static pixel-difference masks with an adaptive dual-mask mechanism derived from classifier gradients at every diffusion step. It also contrasts with ACE and RCSB, which impose fixed spatial constraints that cannot track evolving semantic regions during reverse diffusion. The core conceptual contribution is reframing the interaction between classifier guidance and the diffusion trajectory: rather than applying global gradient updates or a single static mask, the method dynamically re-identifies decision-relevant regions at each step, enabling localized edits that adapt as the image's semantics shift during denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30× speedup over DiME with comparable or superior quality across five diverse datasets (faces, autonomous driving, ImageNet) makes diffusion-based counterfactual explanations practically deployable, and the training-free design lowers the barrier to adoption. However, the method's reliance on classifier gradients introduces a fundamental limitation on multi-class datasets where gradient signals are noisy and spatially inconsistent, and the absence of ground-truth counterfactual annotations remains an open validation challenge for the entire VCE field. Whether the adaptive masking strategy transfers to latent diffusion models or text-conditioned pipelines, and whether perceptual metrics like FID adequately capture quality when large unmodified regions dominate, are important open questions for future work.
