## Problem

Existing talking avatar methods treat text prompts uniformly, causing action descriptions to compete with scene descriptions for attention and producing temporally misaligned or absent actions. They also rely on explicit control signals like pose skeletons, and face a fundamental conflict where text-driven action generation and audio-driven lip synchronization interfere when both modalities exert strong influence simultaneously. Fine-tuning pre-trained models on domain-specific data further risks catastrophic forgetting of text-following capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes an audio sequence, a single reference image, and a hierarchically structured text prompt (auto-generated by an MLLM such as Qwen3-Omni) as input. The prompt is decomposed into a global base block encoding time-invariant scene semantics and K phase-specific blocks, each with an explicit temporal window [τ_start, τ_end] in normalized coordinates. The output is a 125-frame video (5 s at 25 FPS) at 704×1280 resolution, generated via flow-matching sampling with 40 steps and classifier-free guidance scale 5.0 for both text and audio.

## Method

ActAvatar builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B DiT backbone (30 blocks) with Flow Matching training and introduces three synergistic components. Phase-Aware Cross-Attention (PACA) adds learnable phase position embeddings to prompt tokens so that, during cross-attention, the model learns to concentrate on phase-relevant tokens when the current frame falls within the corresponding temporal window, achieving temporal-semantic alignment without external control signals. Progressive Audio-Visual Alignment applies a depth-aware scaling factor f(ℓ)=(ℓ/L)^γ (γ=1.5) to the audio cross-attention residual, so early layers prioritize text conditioning for establishing action structure while deeper layers amplify audio influence for refining lip articulation, preventing modality interference. A two-stage training strategy first trains only the audio adapter on 500K diverse talking-head videos with a frozen backbone (Stage 1), then performs full fine-tuning on 100K structured-annotation samples with PACA prompts (Stage 2), preserving both audio-visual alignment and text-following capabilities through task decomposition.

## Results

On Action Bench, ActAvatar achieves the best scores across all metrics: Hit@Segment 0.854, Action Accuracy 5.971, Temporal Correctness 7.353, Action Quality 7.671, Hand Clarity 8.483, and Sync-C 6.893. On HDTF, it attains the best FID (23.471), IQA (4.120), ASE (2.714), and Sync-D (7.545) while using only 5B parameters at 720p resolution. Inference takes 16 minutes per 5-second video on a single H20 GPU, achieving >4× speedup over comparable 14B methods (Wan-S2V: 68 min, HunyuanVideo-Avatar: 74 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActAvatar outperforms all eight baselines on every Action Bench metric. The closest competitor, OmniAvatar, trails by 0.036 in H@S (0.818 vs 0.854), 0.466 in Action Accuracy (5.505 vs 5.971), and 0.321 in Temporal Correctness (7.032 vs 7.353). Methods relying on global prompts without temporal structure (MultiTalk, HunyuanVideo-Avatar) show the weakest action control, with H@S of only 0.574 and 0.674 respectively, and Action Accuracy below 4.0. On HDTF (lip-sync focused), ActAvatar achieves the best FID (23.471) and Sync-D (7.545) but is slightly behind OmniAvatar on Sync-C (7.663 vs 7.986), indicating a minor trade-off in pure lip-sync fidelity. Notably, ActAvatar uses only 5B parameters at 720p, matching or exceeding 14B models (Wan-S2V, MultiTalk, FantasyTalking) that run at 480p or 720p with 4–5× longer inference times.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActAvatar builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion backbone and the Flow Matching training paradigm, extending them to the talking avatar domain. It departs from prior audio-driven avatar methods (EMO, EchoMimic, Hallo, MultiTalk, HunyuanVideo-Avatar) that condition on a single global text prompt or rely on explicit pose skeleton sequences, by introducing structured temporal prompts with phase-level decomposition and learned phase-conditioned attention. The progressive audio-visual alignment mechanism addresses a modality interference problem identified in multi-modal generation literature, while the two-stage training strategy specifically targets the catastrophic forgetting observed when fine-tuning pre-trained video models on domain-specific talking-head data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ActAvatar demonstrates that structured textual conditioning alone—without pose skeletons or other explicit control signals—can achieve phase-level temporal precision in action control, simplifying the generation pipeline and enabling novel actions not seen in training data. The 5B-parameter model matching or exceeding 14B competitors at 720p resolution offers a practical efficiency win for deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos with many action phases, whether the MLLM-generated structured prompts generalize to open-ended user instructions without human verification, and whether the progressive modality scaling strategy transfers to other multi-modal video generation tasks beyond talking avatars.
