## Problem

Existing interactive text-to-image retrieval (I-TIR) methods perform cross-modal matching within a single static multimodal embedding space across all dialogue rounds, implicitly assuming the geometric relationship between text and image features remains fixed. This static formulation causes semantic vagueness: as each user response introduces new constraints (e.g., emphasizing color or scene), the evolving intent is merely projected onto the same embedding geometry rather than reshaping the retrieval focus, making fine-grained adaptation to progressively refined user intentions difficult.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-round dialogue history (model questions and user answers) and a pool of candidate RGB images as input. An LLM first compresses the dialogue history into a concise contextual summary, which is encoded into a semantic condition vector u_t that conditions the space transformation. The output is a ranked list of images scored by cosine similarity between context-transformed text and image embeddings, where both modalities are reshaped by the same round-specific transformation, yielding retrieval results that become progressively more discriminative as dialogue depth increases.

## Method

CAST introduces a Context-Aware Space Regulator (CASR) that dynamically transforms the shared multimodal latent space at each dialogue round via a residual formulation G(x_t|u_t) = LN(x_t + F(x_t|u_t)). The transformation direction is determined by a Context-Aware Low-Rank Projector (CLP), which uses two MLPs to generate rank-r (r=8) projection matrices A_t and B_t conditioned on the dialogue context, projecting features into a compact subspace and reprojecting to deform the manifold along intent-relevant axes. The transformation magnitude is controlled by a Context-Guided Modulator (CGM), which predicts an adaptive scalar α_t ∈ [0,1] via sigmoid(MLP([u_0; u_t])) based on semantic divergence between the initial and current context, preventing over- or under-modulation. The entire module adds negligible parameters, is trained with a context-guided contrastive loss over randomly truncated dialogue samples, and can be plugged into existing I-TIR frameworks (BLIP, PlugIR, ChatIR) without architectural modification.

## Results

On the VisDial benchmark (10-round averaged Recall), CAST achieves 51.85% R@1, 74.23% R@5, and 82.05% R@10, outperforming all baselines. The advantage grows with dialogue depth: at round 1, CAST reaches 43.60% R@1 vs. ChatIR's 42.78%, while by round 10 the gap widens to 57.56% vs. 53.15%. CAST also demonstrates strong plug-and-play capability, boosting BLIP from 33.97% to a level comparable to PlugIR (44.92%) when the CASR module is attached.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAST outperforms all four baselines across every metric. Against the strongest baseline ChatIR, CAST gains +3.41 points on R@1, +3.35 on R@5, and +2.15 on R@10 (averaged over 10 rounds). The margin over PlugIR is larger: +6.93 R@1, +7.22 R@5, and +6.79 R@10. Compared to the zero-shot BLIP backbone, CAST improves R@1 by +17.88 points, and against ImageScope the gains are +19.66 R@1 and +14.19 R@10. Notably, while BLIP's R@1 degrades from 40.31% (round 1) to 30.09% (round 10) due to the static feature space, CAST's R@1 steadily increases from 43.60% to 57.56%, confirming that dynamic latent space transformation becomes increasingly beneficial as dialogue context accumulates.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAST builds directly on PlugIR's LLM-based dialogue context generation to produce the conditioning signal for its space transformation, while departing fundamentally from the static multimodal embedding space paradigm shared by ChatIR, PlugIR, ImageScope, and BLIP. The low-rank conditional projection mechanism draws on LoRA-style adaptation ideas, and the motivation aligns with GeneCIS's observation that similarity is condition-dependent, though CAST applies the transformation to both query and image sides rather than conditioning only the query. The paper positions itself as the first to explicitly reshape the cross-modal latent geometry per dialogue round, framing prior I-TIR methods as merely reformulating queries on a fixed manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with negligible overhead (0.002 s for 50 K images at rank 8) makes dynamic space transformation practically deployable in large-scale interactive retrieval systems, and the result that BLIP + CASR approaches PlugIR's performance suggests the module could upgrade legacy vision-language models without retraining the full pipeline. However, evaluation is confined to VisDial (COCO-scale, ~100 K images) with a single BLIP backbone, leaving open whether the dynamic-space hypothesis generalizes to larger corpora, different backbones (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP), or non-English dialogues. The LLM-based context generation step, while effective, introduces an inference-time dependency that may conflict with the efficiency claims in latency-sensitive deployment.
