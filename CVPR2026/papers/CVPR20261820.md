## Problem

Existing image editing paradigms each fail to unify semantic flexibility, precise spatial control, and natural results: instruction-based methods cannot reliably localize edits, drag-based methods cannot perform semantic changes, and mask-based inpainting demands pixel-perfect annotations that are burdensome and produce boundary artifacts. No prior work addresses the realistic user scenario of providing only a rough scribble or imprecise region alongside a short text command.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image (minimum 1024×1024, aspect ratios from 16:9 to 9:16), a concise text instruction (averaging ~8 words, stripped of spatial details already conveyed by the mask), and an imprecise binary spatial mask that simulates a user's rough scribble rather than a segmentation-aligned boundary. The mask deliberately covers only the primary editing subject and excludes collateral changes (e.g., lighting shifts), averaging 14.79% of image area. The output is a full edited image where the specified region is modified per the instruction while the rest of the scene is preserved with natural propagation of global effects like lighting and shadows.

## Method

Three baseline architectures integrate the imprecise mask into a Q-Edit (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) diffusion backbone via different strategies: RNI adds a ControlNet bypass branch for the mask, CIA overlays mask edges as a red-box annotation on the source image and fine-tunes with LoRA (rank 32), and CJT feeds both the original and mask images jointly into a multi-image-capable editing model with LoRA. All are trained with flow-matching loss on the 1.1M-pair Inter-Edit dataset, which is generated by a three-stage pipeline: LLM-driven diverse prompt synthesis → T2I source generation → MLLM-guided iterative edit-regenerate-refine loop that produces precise bounding boxes and dual prompts (fine-grained and concise) → SAM-2 segmentation followed by morphological post-processing (dilation, Gaussian blur) to produce smooth, human-like scribble masks → MLLM-based quality filtering. The key design insight is that training masks are intentionally misaligned with true segment boundaries to simulate realistic user annotations, and the concise prompt removes spatial information redundant with the mask, forcing the model to rely on the spatial cue for localization.

## Results

On the Inter-Edit benchmark (6,250 human-annotated pairs), the best I³E baseline (RNI) achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.191), highest in-region fidelity S_in (0.976), highest out-of-region preservation S_out (0.974), and highest VQA composite score (6.431) and human evaluation score (6.672). CJT achieves the best boundary naturalness (BDS 5.435) and the highest human evaluation (6.720). All three I³E baselines substantially outperform the strongest instruction-based method (Q-Edit, SVQA 5.695, HumanEval 5.016) and the strongest mask-based method (Flux-Fill, SVQA 4.287, HumanEval 3.826) across nearly all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against instruction-based methods, RNI and CJT achieve S_in of 0.976 versus Q-Edit's 0.962 and Flux Kontext's 0.958, and their VQA composite scores (6.431, 6.333) exceed Q-Edit (5.695) by roughly 0.6–0.7 points and Flux Kontext (5.314) by over 1 point. Against mask-based methods, the I³E baselines dramatically reduce boundary discontinuity: RNI's BDS of 10.485 and CJT's 5.435 are far below Flux-Fill (15.969), BrushNet (41.837), and PowerPaint (49.518), indicating much more natural transitions at edit boundaries. In human evaluation, CJT (6.720) and RNI (6.672) lead all methods by a wide margin over the best instruction-based model Q-Edit (5.016) and best mask-based model Flux-Fill (3.826). Notably, RNI achieves the best S_out (0.974) and S_edit (6.541), confirming superior background preservation and instruction following, while CJT leads in S_nat (5.833) and S_aes (6.239), reflecting more natural and aesthetically pleasing outputs. The CIA variant, while competitive, trails RNI and CJT on most metrics, consistent with its simpler LoRA-based fine-tuning approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task (I³E) at the intersection of three existing editing paradigms—instruction-driven editing (InstructPix2Pix, ICEdit, FLUX Kontext), mask-based inpainting (FLUX Fill, BrushNet, PowerPaint), and drag-based manipulation—arguing that none of them simultaneously achieves semantic flexibility, spatial precision, and user-friendly interaction. Methodologically, it builds on Q-Edit as the base diffusion editing model and repurposes ControlNet (RNI), LoRA fine-tuning (CIA, CJT), and SAM-2 for mask generation, but the key contribution is the data pipeline that synthesizes 1.1M pairs with imprecise, user-like masks rather than pixel-perfect segmentation outputs. The evaluation framework extends Alpha-CLIP with region-specific masking and introduces a novel Boundary Discontinuity Score, departing from standard LPIPS/CLIP-based metrics that the authors show are inadequate for spatially localized editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The I³E task and Inter-Edit benchmark address a concrete user pain point—precise spatial editing without demanding pixel-perfect annotations—and provide the first million-scale training set and human-annotated test set purpose-built for this interaction pattern, making it a likely reference standard for future interactive editing research. Open questions remain: whether the imprecise-mask paradigm generalizes to multi-object or scene-wide edits, whether the VQA-based metrics fully capture long-tail failure modes, and how to incorporate negative spatial constraints (e.g., 'do not edit here'). The paper also notes future directions including controllable regional adherence strength and reference-guided retouching, suggesting the current formulation is a first step rather than a final solution.
