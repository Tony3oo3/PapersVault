## Problem

Existing human-preference reward models (e.g., HPSv2/v3) evaluate aesthetic quality rather than style consistency, creating an intrinsic objective misalignment when applied to style generation tasks. Meanwhile, dedicated style perception models (e.g., CSD, StyleTokenizer) rely solely on image-level contrastive learning without cross-modal text-style supervision, limiting their generalization to diverse style prompts and multimodal generation settings. No reward model exists that can jointly assess style consistency between paired images and image-text style alignment for reinforcement fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

StyleDoctor accepts multiple input configurations: a single image with a text style prompt (text-controlled understanding), an image with a reference style image (reference-guided understanding), or two candidate images alongside a style condition (preference comparison). Rather than outputting a scalar reward, it generates structured multi-dimensional reasoning (color palette, brushstroke texture, form/composition consistency) with per-dimension scores and a final preference judgment. When used as a regularization term in diffusion fine-tuning, the reward signal is extracted as the average token-level confidence of positive style-similarity answers in the output logits, since the generative model does not natively produce a scalar score.

## Method

StyleDoctor builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and is trained in three phases: (1) contrastive learning on a linear projection layer over the vision encoder to capture global style embeddings, (2) cross-entropy fine-tuning on image-caption pairs from SPRData (400K samples, 1000 style categories) with a dedicated style token injected via a second projection layer alongside standard image tokens, and (3) unified preference learning across reorganized quartet formats (text-controlled, reference-image, and multi-image comparison) to inject fine-grained style preference reasoning. The key architectural insight is the dual representation: global style characteristics flow through the dedicated style token while local style cues are learned from sequential image tokens, enabling the model to evaluate both inter-image style consistency and image-text style alignment within a single generative framework. For downstream generation, StyleDoctor plugs into RL fine-tuning pipelines (DPO, Flow-GRPO) as a multi-dimensional style reward, or into ReNeg-style regularization where its output-logit confidence serves as a differentiable style alignment signal for learning style-centric negative embeddings.

## Results

On style image retrieval (WikiArt, 10K pairs), StyleDoctor achieves 75.46% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline Qwen-2.5-VL-3B (71.82%) and the dedicated style encoder CSD (62.60%). On multi-modal style understanding (conditional judgment + preference selection), StyleDoctor reaches 71.14% accuracy versus 66.54% for Qwen-2.5-VL-3B and 53.25% for CSD. In downstream generation, OmniGen2 fine-tuned with StyleDoctor as reward model improves text-controlled style generation accuracy from 65.42% to 69.58% (StyleDoctor-judged) and from 58.85% to 67.60% (GPT-4o-judged), while reference-image-guided style generation CSD similarity rises to 0.72, surpassing all prior methods (CSGO 0.54, AttnDistill 0.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleDoctor wins across all 11 baselines on every reported metric. Against dedicated style encoders, it surpasses CSD by +12.86 pts on retrieval and +17.89 pts on multi-modal understanding, and CLIP by +22.01 and +24.86 pts respectively. Against the untrained MLLM backbone Qwen-2.5-VL-3B, the style-specific training yields +3.64 and +4.60 pts gains. In generation, StyleDoctor-guided OmniGen2 outperforms CSGO (0.54), StyleStudio (0.59), and AttnDistill (0.67) on reference-image-guided style generation CSD (0.72), and improves over its own base OmniGen2 by +4.16/+8.75 pts on text-controlled generation. For style transfer, StyleDoctor-guided OmniStyle beats the base OmniStyle on both instruction-guided (GPT 70.53 vs 65.50) and reference-image-guided (CSD 0.78 vs 0.74, GPT 72.25 vs 67.86, content 0.72 vs 0.66) settings, and Flux-Kontext+StyDoc. improves GPT score from 74.35 to 78.54. Against human-preference reward models, StyleDoctor dominates HPSv3 (75.46 vs 68.75 retrieval; 0.78 vs 0.64 style generation) and HPSv2 (75.46 vs 56.83; 0.78 vs 0.45), confirming the paper's central claim that aesthetic-preference models are misaligned with style-centric objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two existing lines of work: (1) image-only style encoders (CSD, CLIP-based contrastive models) that capture global style but ignore cross-modal text-style alignment, and (2) human-preference reward models (HPSv2/v3) that evaluate aesthetic quality rather than style consistency. By repurposing a lightweight MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) with a contrastive style projection head and unified preference learning, StyleDoctor bridges these gaps, drawing on the RL fine-tuning paradigm (DPO, Flow-GRPO) and reward-guided regularization (ReNeg) already established for diffusion models. The training data pipeline reuses state-of-the-art style transfer methods (OmniStyle, CSGO) and the Style30k taxonomy, positioning SPRData as a preference-pair extension of existing style transfer datasets like OmniStyle's triplets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: practitioners applying RL fine-tuning to style generation have lacked a reward signal that actually measures style fidelity rather than generic aesthetics. SPRData (400K quartets, 1000 style categories) is a reusable resource for style reward modeling, and the plug-and-play integration with existing RL frameworks (OmniGen2, Flux-Kontext, OmniStyle) lowers the adoption barrier. Open questions include whether the 1000-category Style30k taxonomy generalizes to novel or compound styles, whether GPT-4o-annotated reasoning labels introduce systematic biases, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D style transfer. The evaluation partially relies on CSD as a style metric, creating a mild circularity that limits the strength of the claims.
