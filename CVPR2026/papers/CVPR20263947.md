## Problem

Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting at high resolutions suffers from severe overfitting, floating artifacts, and ghosting because the expanded dimensionality amplifies reconstruction ambiguities. Prior sparse-view methods rely on aggressive downsampling (e.g., 8×) to stabilize optimization, leaving high-resolution reconstruction under sparse views largely unexplored. The paper identifies and quantifies a multi-resolution complementarity: lower resolutions yield robust global geometry while higher resolutions capture fine details but introduce instability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of sparse RGB images (3 views for LLFF/DTU, 12 or 24 for Mip-NeRF360, 8 for Blender) with their camera intrinsics, and internally generates a pyramid of downsampled versions (1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2) with proportionally scaled intrinsics to maintain spatial alignment. The output is a 3D Gaussian radiance field optimized at the highest input resolution, enabling high-fidelity novel view synthesis. Unlike prior sparse-view 3DGS methods that downsample the dataset before optimization, CAGS operates on the original high-resolution images while leveraging coarser scales as structural anchors.

## Method

CAGS constructs a hierarchical pyramid of 3D Gaussian fields at multiple resolutions, with the coarsest field serving as a stable global anchor. At each finer level, every Gaussian is matched to its nearest coarse-scale anchor via KNN, and a learnable sigmoid maps the spatial deviation, opacity difference, and scale variation to a confidence score in [0,1]. This confidence is propagated multiplicatively along the cross-scale chain, so structurally consistent regions retain high confidence while inconsistent points are progressively down-weighted. The confidence then modulates each Gaussian's opacity during differentiable rendering, creating a smooth voting-like aggregation across scales. A multi-scale pseudo-view regularization further enforces cross-resolution consistency by rendering interpolated virtual cameras at all scales and applying L1 + D-SSIM losses between the highest-resolution output (downsampled) and each coarser-scale rendering.

## Results

On LLFF (3 views) at original resolution, CAGS achieves 18.85 PSNR / 0.590 SSIM / 0.339 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline (NexusGS, 16.12 PSNR) by 2.73 dB. On Mip-NeRF360 (12 views) at original resolution, CAGS reaches 18.43 PSNR / 0.533 SSIM / 0.422 LPIPS versus DropGaussian's 16.82 PSNR, a 1.61 dB gap. The advantage is most pronounced at high resolution and narrows at 1/4 resolution (e.g., 20.62 vs. 20.29 PSNR on LLFF), confirming that the multi-scale aggregation primarily addresses the high-resolution overfitting problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAGS wins on every metric across all four baselines (FSGS, Binocular3DGS, DropGaussian, NexusGS) at every resolution tested. The largest margins appear at original resolution: on LLFF, CAGS beats NexusGS (the strongest baseline) by 2.73 dB PSNR and on Mip-NeRF360 it beats DropGaussian by 1.61 dB PSNR. At 1/4 resolution the gap narrows to roughly 0.3–0.6 dB, consistent with the paper's claim that the multi-scale aggregation primarily targets high-resolution instability. On DTU (Table 2), CAGS also outperforms CoR-GS by 1.74 dB PSNR at original resolution (19.25 vs. 17.51). Additionally, the paper demonstrates that bolting CAGS onto existing methods (FSGS, DropGaussian, CoR-GS) yields 1.3–1.9 dB PSNR gains, showing the paradigm is method-agnostic rather than tied to a specific 3DGS implementation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the scene representation and extends the sparse-view 3DGS line of work (FSGS, CoR-GS, DropGaussian, NexusGS, Binocular3DGS) that has predominantly relied on aggressive downsampling (e.g., 8×) to avoid floaters. The key conceptual departure is treating input resolution as a first-class design variable: rather than downsample-and-reconstruct, the paper constructs a pyramid of Gaussian fields at multiple resolutions and fuses them via a confidence-guided cross-scale aggregation chain. The multi-scale pseudo-view regularization extends the pseudo-view sampling idea from CoR-GS and FSGS by enforcing cross-resolution consistency rather than single-scale view consistency, and the plug-and-play integration demonstrated in Table 2 positions CAGS as a general wrapper over existing sparse-view 3DGS pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic empirical study of how input resolution interacts with sparse-view 3DGS is a useful contribution that had been overlooked; identifying the multi-resolution complementarity (stable global structure at low res, detail at high res) provides a principled motivation for hierarchical approaches. The plug-and-play nature of the paradigm—consistently boosting FSGS, CoR-GS, and DropGaussian without architectural changes—makes it practically attractive for practitioners working with existing 3DGS codebases. Open questions include the computational overhead of training multiple Gaussian fields simultaneously, scalability to very large or dynamic scenes, and whether the fixed 4-level pyramid (1/16 to 1/2) is optimal or whether adaptive scale selection could reduce cost. The evaluation is also limited to a small number of views (3–24) and four datasets, leaving open how the method behaves in the intermediate regime (e.g., 50–100 views) where the sparse-view constraint relaxes.
