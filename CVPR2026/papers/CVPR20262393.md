## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction datasets face a fundamental trade-off: studio-based captures (e.g., ARCTIC, HOT3D, GigaHands) provide accurate 3D ground truth but lack environmental diversity and visual realism, while in-the-wild datasets (e.g., Ego-Exo4D, Nymeria) offer realistic settings but lack dense, accurate 3D annotations for hands and objects. No prior system could simultaneously capture unconstrained real-world interactions and generate precise 3D pose ground truth, leaving a critical gap for training egocentric perception models that must generalize beyond controlled environments.

## Input / Output

The capture system ingests synchronized monochrome fisheye images from 10 cameras (8 exocentric at 1024×1280 with 152°×116° FoV mounted in a half-dome on the rig, plus 2 egocentric from a Meta Quest 3 headset) at 60 Hz, along with CAD models for 21 target objects. The reference frame is body-attached rather than world-fixed, moving with the participant. Outputs include 21 3D keypoints per hand with personalized MANO-compatible meshes, 6DoF object poses with texture-mapped CAD models, 2D segmentation masks, hand-object contact regions, and multiple LLM-generated text captions per interaction sequence, totaling 4.3M annotated frames across 38 subjects.

## Method

The core contribution is a lightweight (~8 kg) backpack-style multi-camera rig that enables free mobility while maintaining synchronized multi-view coverage, paired with an ego-exo annotation pipeline. For hand pose, 2D keypoints are detected by Sapiens on full images and by InterNet on perspective-cropped hand regions, then fused via RANSAC-based triangulation across all 10 views and fit to personalized hand meshes through Inverse Kinematics, with Bayesian confidence scoring for automated filtering. For object pose, a three-stage CAD-based pipeline (CNOS detection → FoundPose coarse 6DoF estimation → GoTrack refinement) is extended to multi-view input by replacing standard PnP with multi-view gPnP from PoseLib, leveraging DINOv2 features for object-agnostic generalization. Text captions are generated by an LLM augmentation pipeline that produces multiple semantically diverse paraphrases from structured manipulation instructions, enabling language-conditioned downstream tasks.

## Results

On the 3D hand pose estimation benchmark (Table 4), a model trained on SHOW3D achieves 15.5 mm MKPE on the SHOW3D test set, outperforming UmeTrack-trained (22.2 mm) and HOT3D-trained (19.6 mm) models by 30% and 21% respectively. The combined UmeTrack+HOT3D+SHOW3D model achieves the best overall performance at 14.3 mm on SHOW3D and 12.3 mm on HOT3D. For interaction field estimation (Table 2), training on SHOW3D alone yields 14.70 mm ADE on HOT3D, while training on HOT3D alone gives 22.57 mm ADE on SHOW3D, demonstrating a 54% generalization asymmetry favoring SHOW3D. Text-conditioned object pose forecasting reduces mean translation error by 29% at 30 frames (42.7→30.4 mm) and 25% at 60 frames (46.7→35.0 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two existing hand-object datasets as baselines: UmeTrack (a multi-view studio dataset) and HOT3D (an egocentric studio dataset with MoCap annotations). On the SHOW3D test set, both baselines regress substantially: UmeTrack-trained models achieve 22.2 mm MKPE and HOT3D-trained models achieve 19.6 mm, while the SHOW3D-trained model reaches 15.5 mm — a 30% and 21% improvement respectively. The asymmetry is further confirmed in interaction field estimation, where SHOW3D→HOT3D transfer (14.70 mm ADE) far outperforms HOT3D→SHOW3D transfer (22.57 mm ADE), indicating SHOW3D captures a broader interaction distribution. When combined with both baselines, the UmeTrack+HOT3D+SHOW3D model achieves the best results on both SHOW3D (14.3 mm) and HOT3D (12.3 mm), while remaining competitive on UmeTrack (9.6 mm vs. 9.5 mm). On the UmeTrack test set, the combined model is essentially tied with the UmeTrack+HOT3D baseline (9.6 vs. 9.5 mm), confirming that SHOW3D does not degrade performance on the most constrained studio domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-camera hand-object capture lineage (MEgATrack, HOT3D, GigaHands) by making the rig mobile and backpack-mounted, and by fusing egocentric and exocentric views in a unified ego-exo annotation pipeline. For object pose, it repurposes DINOv2-based methods (CNOS, FoundPose, GoTrack) by extending them to multi-view input via gPnP; for hand pose, it combines Sapiens and InterNet 2D detections with RANSAC triangulation and UmeTrack-compatible personalized mesh fitting. The key conceptual departure from prior studio datasets (ARCTIC, GigaHands, HOT3D) is eliminating the stationary capture volume and marker-based tracking, while the LLM-based text captioning pipeline follows GigaHands' instruction-guided protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SHOW3D fills a critical gap: it is the first large-scale dataset providing dense 3D ground truth for both hands and objects in genuinely unconstrained in-the-wild environments, including outdoor scenes with variable illumination. The cross-dataset asymmetry (SHOW3D-trained models generalize 54% better to studio data than vice versa) provides strong empirical evidence that environmental diversity, not just annotation quality, drives generalization. Limitations include monochrome-only imagery, a fixed set of 21 objects, and the still-substantial 8 kg rig that, while mobile, is not as unconstrained as a phone or glasses. Open questions include whether the annotation pipeline's sub-centimeter accuracy holds under more extreme occlusion or motion blur, and how the dataset scales to new object categories without CAD models.
