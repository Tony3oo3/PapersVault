## Problem

Current LVLM alignment methods are economically inefficient: training-time approaches incur massive sunk costs and over-conservatism, process-alignment methods waste budget on extended deliberation, and output-alignment methods are myopic and local. Critically, all existing safety evaluations suffer from process-blindness—they score only final outputs while ignoring internal reasoning trajectories, allowing models to disguise harmful steps with benign justifications and game additive safety scores. The paper reframes alignment as a budget-constrained economic optimization problem rather than a purely safety-filtering task.

## Input / Output

EcoAlign takes an RGB image, a text query, and an explicit computational budget B as inputs. The image is first processed via a low-cost global scan producing a high-level caption (initializing the root graph node) and a low-resolution feature map retained as global visual context. The output is a coherent text answer synthesized from the single most economically efficient path through the constructed thought graph, where the path itself is a sequence of heterogeneous multimodal reasoning states (text nodes, visual exploration nodes, and structural optimization nodes) selected via Pareto-optimal path tracking.

## Method

EcoAlign constructs a dynamic multimodal DAG (thought graph) via iterative best-first expansion, where each leaf node spawns candidate actions from three categories: text generation, visual exploration, and structural optimization (merging/pruning). Each candidate action is scored by a forward-looking economic value function analogous to net present value: a short simulated rollout estimates discounted future returns Γ_local = s·u/c, with the rollout horizon dynamically shrinking as the remaining budget depletes (|R|_t = ⌊k·(B−C_t)⌋), inducing budget-aware risk aversion. Path safety is enforced via the weakest-link principle (S[P] = min of node safety scores), so a single unsafe step invalidates the entire chain, preventing harmful intermediate reasoning from being masked by benign conclusions. After graph expansion, a Pareto frontier is tracked over the three-dimensional (utility, cost, safety) space using dynamic programming in topological order, and the final path is selected by maximizing Γ(P) = S[P]·U[P]/C[P] subject to C[P] ≤ B.

## Results

EcoAlign achieves the highest safety scores across all five models and three safety benchmarks. On GPT-4o, it reaches 96.5 on MMSafetyBench, 91.8 on MSSBench, and 87.1 on SIUO, while maintaining strong utility (86.0 OCRBench, 85.4 MathVista, 86.2 MMStar) at an average cost of 21.2× base. For Gemini-2.5-Flash, EcoAlign attains a near-perfect 97.7 on MMSafetyBench and 89.6 on MathVista at 24.1× cost. Across all models, EcoAlign's cost is 4–9× lower than CoT while delivering equal or superior safety and utility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EcoAlign dominates all three baselines on safety across every model. Against CoT, EcoAlign improves MMSafetyBench by 15–36 points (e.g., 96.5 vs. 69.1 on GPT-4o; 85.2 vs. 49.1 on Llama-3.2) while reducing cost by 5–9× (21.2 vs. 104.3 on GPT-4o; 28.2 vs. 108.3 on Llama-3.2). Against CoD, EcoAlign gains 15–32 points on MMSafetyBench (e.g., 96.5 vs. 71.6 on GPT-4o) at a comparable or slightly lower cost (21.2 vs. 25.8). Against VLM-Guard, EcoAlign achieves higher safety (e.g., 96.5 vs. 88.4 on GPT-4o) and substantially higher utility (e.g., 86.0 vs. 70.2 on OCRBench, 85.4 vs. 69.7 on MathVista), though at roughly 7× the cost (21.2 vs. 3.1). The key trade-off is that VLM-Guard is cheapest but sacrifices utility heavily, while EcoAlign recovers utility without compromising safety, at a moderate cost premium over VLM-Guard but a large saving over CoT/CoD.

## Relation to Prior Work

EcoAlign builds on the Graph of Thoughts architecture [3] for its dynamic DAG representation of reasoning, but departs from prior alignment work by reframing the safety-utility-cost trilemma as a bounded-rationality economic optimization problem rather than a purely safety-filtering task. It contrasts with training-time alignment (SFT, RLHF) as a static, high-sunk-cost approach, with process alignment (CoT, CoD) as computationally expensive but myopic, and with output alignment (VLM-Guard, SafeDecoding) as local and lacking forward-looking control. The weakest-link safety principle and budget-adaptive lookahead are the paper's key conceptual departures from additive or final-output scoring schemes used in prior inference-time methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The economic framing provides a principled, unified lens for the safety-utility-cost trade-off that prior methods handle ad hoc, and the weakest-link principle directly addresses the process-blindness vulnerability where harmful intermediate reasoning is masked by benign conclusions. However, the practical cost remains substantial (21× base for GPT-4o, 39× for InternVL3-14B), limiting real-time deployment, and the framework's reliance on self-evaluation for safety/utility scores raises questions about robustness against adversarial self-assessment manipulation. Open questions include whether the graph-search overhead can be further reduced, whether the economic value function generalizes to multi-turn or agentic settings, and whether the bounded-rationality framing can be extended to training-time alignment.
