## Problem

Existing long-tailed learning methods with foundation models focus on mitigating class-imbalance bias but overlook a distinct failure mode: fine-tuning disrupts inter-class relationships, causing tail-class samples to be misclassified into semantically related categories (termed 'concept confusion'). The paper attributes this to the mutual exclusivity of single-label supervision, which suppresses feature sharing among related classes and amplifies head-class dominance during adaptation.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and outputs one class label, identical to standard classification. During training, each image additionally consumes two auxiliary supervision signals: (i) the top-k non-ground-truth classes from frozen zero-shot CLIP cosine-similarity scores (instance-level visual cues), and (ii) an offline LLM-generated semantic neighbor set per class (class-level cues). These multi-label binary targets are computed once before training and do not require any additional forward passes at test time.

## Method

CUE augments the standard Logit-Adjustment (LA) loss with two Binary Logit-Adjustment (BLA) auxiliary losses, each applying a prior-dependent logit shift before a sigmoid activation to handle multi-label supervision under class imbalance. The first BLA term uses VLM-based instance-level cues: for each training image, the top-k most similar non-ground-truth classes from frozen CLIP zero-shot predictions form a binary multi-label target. The second BLA term uses LLM-based class-level cues: an offline batched prompting pipeline queries an LLM to identify semantic neighbors for every class within the label vocabulary, producing a fixed neighbor graph. The three losses (LA + λ_zs·BLA_zs + λ_llm·BLA_llm) are jointly optimized, encouraging semantically related categories to share representations rather than being suppressed by single-label exclusivity. The design is plug-and-play, integrating with any PEFT strategy (AdaptFormer, LoRA, VPT, Adapter) or from-scratch long-tailed training method without architectural modification.

## Results

On CIFAR100-LT (IR=100), CUE achieves 82.8% overall top-1 accuracy with 82.0% on Few-shot classes, improving over the LIFT baseline by +2.5% overall and +7.7% on tail classes. On ImageNet-LT, CUE reaches 77.4% overall (+0.4% over LIFT) with 73.0% Few-shot accuracy (+1.5%). On Places365-LT, CUE attains 51.7% overall and 52.4% Few-shot (+1.9% over LIFT). On iNaturalist 2018, CUE achieves 79.6% overall (+0.5% over LIFT) with balanced gains across Many (+1.0%) and Few (+0.6%) splits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUE consistently outperforms the primary baseline LIFT [33] across all four benchmarks, with the largest margin on CIFAR100-LT IR=100 (+2.5% overall, +7.7% Few-shot) and more modest but consistent gains on ImageNet-LT (+0.4% overall, +1.5% Few-shot), Places365-LT (+0.2% overall, +1.9% Few-shot), and iNaturalist (+0.5% overall, +0.6% Few-shot). Against heavier baselines, CUE (0.10M–4.75M trainable params) surpasses BALLAD (149.62M params) by +5.0% on CIFAR100-LT IR=100, and outperforms VL-LTR (149.62M) by +1.6% overall on Places365-LT and +2.8% on iNaturalist. CUE also beats LPT [10] on Places365-LT (51.7% vs 50.1% overall) and iNaturalist (79.6% vs 76.1%), and RAC [26] on Places365-LT (51.7% vs 47.2%). The only comparison where CUE does not lead is on the Many-shot split of Places365-LT (50.6% vs LIFT's 51.3%), suggesting a slight trade-off on head classes. In from-scratch extensions (Table 7), CUE improves all four baselines (DODA, LOS, LA, ResLT) by 1.2–10.4% on Few-shot accuracy, confirming its plug-and-play generality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LIFT paradigm of lightweight fine-tuning (AdaptFormer + Logit Adjustment) for long-tailed recognition with foundation models, and on the broader PEFT literature (LoRA, VPT, Adapter). It departs from prior LTL work by identifying 'concept confusion'—a failure mode where fine-tuning disrupts inter-class semantic relationships—as distinct from the well-studied class-imbalance bias, and addresses it by repurposing CLIP zero-shot predictions and LLM-generated neighbor sets as multi-label supervision signals. The Binary Logit-Adjustment (BLA) loss extends the standard LA formulation to the multi-label setting, and the overall design is explicitly positioned as a plug-and-play add-on rather than a replacement for existing LTL strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of concept confusion as a separate, fine-tuning-induced failure mode (beyond class imbalance) provides a useful diagnostic lens for practitioners adapting foundation models to imbalanced data, and the plug-and-play design (validated across five PEFT backbones and four from-scratch methods) makes it easy to adopt. However, the empirical gains over LIFT are modest (0.2–2.5% depending on dataset), and the method's effectiveness depends on the quality of external cues from frozen CLIP and an LLM, which may not transfer cleanly to domains where zero-shot CLIP is weak or where class vocabularies are highly specialized. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned cue generation could reduce this external dependency, and whether the approach scales efficiently to very large vocabularies (e.g., 8K+ classes on iNaturalist) without the batched LLM prompting overhead.
