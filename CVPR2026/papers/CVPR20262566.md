## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models on synthetic 3D renders to learn geometric controls (normals, depth, camera poses) causes the model to associate the presence of control signals with the synthetic visual domain, so that applying controls at inference produces unrealistic, render-like outputs. This creates an undesirable trade-off between photorealism learned from real images and controllability learned from synthetic data, and existing mitigations (mixed-domain training, LoRA, adapters) do not fully resolve the domain leakage.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt plus spatial control signals—either per-view normal and position maps (channel-concatenated to the noisy latent) or camera viewpoints (injected via positional encoding)—and produces a 2×2 grid of four orthogonal 512×512 RGB views that are 3D-consistent across views. Real training images are used with a null control signal, while synthetic renders carry the actual control annotations; at inference, the user supplies controls while the domain adapter is set to 'real mode' to obtain photorealistic yet geometrically faithful outputs.

## Method

Realiz3D introduces Domain Shifters—lightweight low-rank residual adapters (rank-r matrices, r≪d) that encode a binary domain covariate (real vs. synthetic) as a per-block bias added to all latent tokens, explicitly decoupling visual domain identity from control signals. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 freezes the DiT backbone and trains only the Domain Shifters on mixed real/synthetic data under null control to learn domain separation; Stage 2 unfreezes the backbone and introduces control conditioning, while applying two complementary strategies—Layer-Aware Training (stochastically freezing early DiT blocks when processing real samples, since early layers encode domain-agnostic structure) and Domain Reassignment (with probability p_B, reassigning early blocks to synthetic mode even for real samples, binding controllability to shared structural representations). At inference, a partial, non-stochastic domain reassignment sets pre-defined early layers and timesteps to synthetic mode while later ones remain in real mode, allowing users to rebalance realism and control adherence without additional training.

## Results

On Multiview Texturing (Table 1), Realiz3D achieves FID_B of 141.90 and FID_I of 200.24, substantially outperforming the full fine-tuning on synthetic-only data (FID_B 168.21, FID_I 218.29) while maintaining competitive 3D consistency (PSNR 24.78 vs. 25.76). On Text-to-Multiview Generation (Table 3), Realiz3D reaches FID_B 122.01 and FID_I 196.01, the best realism scores among all methods, with PSNR 19.02 close to the synthetic-only baseline (19.66). The method uniquely achieves strong performance on both realism and controllability metrics simultaneously, whereas all baselines exhibit a clear trade-off between the two.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Realiz3D clearly outperforms all lightweight fine-tuning baselines (LoRA, Linear Adapters) on both 3D consistency and realism, e.g., PSNR 24.78 vs. 21.88 (LoRA) and FID_B 141.90 vs. 151.97. Against the training-free SDEdit baseline, Realiz3D improves PSNR by 2.57 points and FID_B by 5.16 points. Compared to the adapter-based methods (Domain Adapter, Spatial Adapter, Domain Switcher), Realiz3D achieves the best realism (lowest FID_B and FID_I) while sacrificing only 0.1–0.8 PSNR in 3D consistency. The closest competitor on 3D consistency is the full fine-tuning on synthetic-only data (PSNR 25.76), but Realiz3D reduces FID_B by 26.31 points (141.90 vs. 168.21) and FID_I by 18.05 points (200.24 vs. 218.29), demonstrating a substantially better realism–control trade-off. On Text-to-Multiview (Table 3), Realiz3D achieves the best FID_B (122.01) and FID_I (196.01) among all methods, outperforming even the pretrained TRELLIS model (FID_B 181.92, FID_I 224.22) by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

Realiz3D extends the domain-switching idea from Wonder3D [20] and the multi-stage adapter training from AnimateDiff [11], but departs from both by explicitly decoupling visual domain identity from control signals via lightweight low-rank residual adapters (Domain Shifters), rather than modifying the conditioning mechanism or relying on paired cross-domain data. The Domain Shifter design draws directly on Rebuffi et al.'s residual adapters for multi-domain learning [26], while the layer-aware training and domain reassignment strategies operationalize well-established observations about hierarchical feature resolution in diffusion transformers [14, 21, 34]. The key conceptual departure is identifying that the realism degradation in 3D-controlled fine-tuning stems from implicit association between control-signal presence and synthetic appearance, and addressing it architecturally rather than through data mixing alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a fundamental and persistent trade-off in 3D generation—photorealism versus geometric controllability—that has limited the practical utility of diffusion-based 3D pipelines. The Domain Shifter + Representation Binding framework is general and lightweight, making it a practical recipe for any setting where a pre-trained image generator must be adapted to a domain-shifted, control-annotated dataset. However, a small gap in control adherence remains, the base model's lighting bias can cause cross-view inconsistencies, and the method is currently limited to domain-agnostic control signals (text, geometry), leaving image-based conditioning as an open question. Advances in relighting and material-aware generation are identified as natural next steps to close the remaining realism gap.
