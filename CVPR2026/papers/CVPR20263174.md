## Problem

Existing real-world image restoration methods assume ground-truth (GT) supervision is flawless, yet GT datasets (e.g., FFHQ) exhibit inconsistent perceptual quality with average scores of only 5–8 on IQA metrics. This causes models to converge to the average quality level of training data rather than achieving the highest attainable perceptual fidelity. The paper addresses this gap by explicitly guiding restoration toward maximal quality without discarding lower-quality training samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-quality RGB image and a scalar quality score (computed as the mean of an ensemble of NR-IQA models, e.g., MUSIQ-GFIQA, TOPIQ-GFIQA, and Q-Align for face tasks) as a conditioning signal. The quality score is embedded into a spatial vector matching the LQ feature dimensions (16×16×256) and added element-wise before feeding the Transformer. The output is a restored high-quality image decoded from fused codebook lookups; at inference, the score is set to its maximum value to elicit the highest-quality restoration.

## Method

IQPIR integrates an Image Quality Prior (IQP) with a dual-codebook architecture in a two-stage pipeline. In the codebook learning stage, a common codebook captures generic structures while an HQ+ codebook (activated only when the NR-IQA score exceeds a threshold S_thr) specializes in fine-grained high-quality details, with the final quantized feature being a weighted sum Z_q = Z_q^1 + αZ_q^2. In the code prediction stage, a quality-conditioned Transformer receives the LQ feature augmented with the embedded quality score and predicts two code sequences (one per codebook), enabling class-conditional-style generation where the highest score condition yields the highest-quality output. A key insight is that optimizing NR-IQA scores as a loss in the discrete codebook space naturally mitigates over-optimization (adversarial examples that fool IQA models), unlike continuous latent spaces. The final training objective combines feature MSE, cross-entropy on code indices, and a negative IQA quality loss (λ₁=0.5, λ₂=0.1), and the framework is plug-and-play, demonstrably enhancing existing methods like WaveFace, InterLCM, Reti-Diff, and CIDNet without structural modification.

## Results

On blind face restoration (the primary task), IQPIR achieves the best scores across all 8 NR-IQA metrics on all three test sets. On LFW-Test, it reaches TOPIQ-G 0.861, Musiq-G 0.878, Q-Align 4.67, and CLIP-IQA 0.790, outperforming the closest competitor InterLCM (0.831, 0.834, 4.55, 0.721) by 3.6%, 5.2%, 2.6%, and 9.6% respectively. On low-light enhancement (LOL-v1), IQPIR attains PSNR 25.72 and FID 40.18, surpassing Reti-Diff (25.35, 49.14) and CIDNet (23.50, 46.69). The method also leads on underwater (UIEB: PSNR 24.35, UIQM 3.127) and backlit (BAID: PSNR 24.06, LPIPS 0.083) enhancement, and a user study confirms the highest mean rating (4.03/5) for face restoration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IQPIR outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every task and metric. In blind face restoration, the closest competitor is InterLCM (ICLR 2025), which IQPIR surpasses by 3.6% on TOPIQ-G (0.861 vs 0.831) and 9.6% on CLIP-IQA (0.790 vs 0.721) on LFW-Test; DAEFR (ICLR 2024) and CodeFormer (NeurIPS 2022) trail by 5–10% on face-specific metrics (Musiq-G, TOPIQ-G). For low-light enhancement, IQPIR beats Reti-Diff (ICLR 2025) by 0.37 dB PSNR and 8.96 FID on LOL-v1, and CIDNet (CVPR 2025) by 2.22 dB PSNR; on LOL-v2-real the gap over Reti-Diff narrows to 0.23 dB PSNR but FID still improves by 3.13. On underwater (UIEB) and backlit (BAID) enhancement, IQPIR leads Reti-Diff by 0.23 dB PSNR and 0.04 UIQM, and by 0.87 dB PSNR and 0.064 LPIPS respectively. As a plug-and-play enhancement, the quality prior adds 1.7–3.5% gains to WaveFace and InterLCM on face restoration, and 5.9–7.8% gains to Reti-Diff and CIDNet on low-light tasks, confirming broad generalizability without architectural modification.

## Relation to Prior Work

IQPIR extends the CodeFormer (Zhou et al., NeurIPS 2022) codebook-lookup Transformer architecture by introducing a dual-codebook structure that disentangles generic structural features from high-quality-specific attributes, and by conditioning the code-prediction Transformer on NR-IQA-derived quality scores. It also substantially extends the authors' own preliminary preprint IQPFR (Hu et al., 2025), which first proposed using NR-IQA scores as quality priors for face restoration, by adding the dual-codebook design, discrete-space quality optimization, and a comprehensive multi-task evaluation. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing assumption in real-world image restoration that ground-truth data is a perfect supervision target, instead treating GT quality as a variable to be explicitly modeled and optimized against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play quality-conditioning mechanism (demonstrated on WaveFace, InterLCM, Reti-Diff, and CIDNet with 1.8–7.8% gains) makes this a broadly applicable enhancement strategy rather than a single-task model, which increases its practical relevance to the restoration community. The finding that discrete codebook representations naturally mitigate NR-IQA over-optimization (adversarial examples that score high but look poor) is a useful design principle for any system using no-reference quality metrics as training objectives. Open questions include how the approach scales when NR-IQA models themselves improve, whether the quality prior can be learned end-to-end rather than relying on frozen pretrained assessors, and whether the gains persist on tasks with more severe or structurally diverse degradations beyond faces and illumination.
