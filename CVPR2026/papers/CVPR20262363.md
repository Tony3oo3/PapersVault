## Problem

Existing EEG-to-fMRI reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: ROI-based approaches capture temporal dynamics but lose cortical spatial detail, while voxel- or vertex-level methods achieve spatial fidelity but reconstruct frames independently, producing temporally incoherent sequences with biologically implausible frame-to-frame artifacts. Additionally, conventional evaluation relies solely on pixel-level metrics (MSE, SSIM, PSNR) that cannot verify whether reconstructed fMRI preserves task-relevant neural representations usable for downstream cognitive tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 64-channel EEG recording at 1000 Hz, segmented into per-frame windows temporally aligned to fMRI with a 4-second hemodynamic delay, and produces a sequence of fMRI frames on the HCP fsLR32k grayordinate space (91,282 vertices per frame at 0.8 s TR). For the InterRecon task, sparse anchor fMRI frames at boundary indices are additionally provided as hard constraints. The fMRI autoencoder compresses each 91,282-dim vertex vector to a 1024-dim latent via a linear projection, preserving the range-null decomposition structure needed for null-space sampling.

## Method

The framework is a 6-layer diffusion transformer (8 heads, 1024 hidden dim) that tokenizes a K_w-frame fMRI window into K_w × N_v vertex-level tokens with temporal positional encoding, and conditions every transformer layer on a sequence-level EEG embedding extracted by a 1D temporal convolutional encoder via cross-attention. A linear MLP autoencoder compresses fMRI into a compact latent space specifically to keep the null-space projection (I − A†A) well-defined and exact. The key innovation is a null-space constrained sampling mechanism for InterRecon: at each DDIM denoising step, the clean-data estimate is decomposed into a range-space component (enforcing A x̂ = y for observed anchor frames) and a null-space component (free residual variation), guaranteeing measurement consistency without any retraining or architectural change. Training uses the standard denoising score matching objective with 1000-step linear noise schedule; inference uses 50-step DDIM sampling.

## Results

On the CineBrain dataset (6 subjects, within-subject evaluation), the proposed diffusion-transformer framework achieves the best vertex-level reconstruction across all spatial regions and temporal lengths. At the most challenging 30-frame setting on the whole brain (91,282 vertices), it reaches MSE 0.281, Pearson r 0.819, and cosine similarity 0.845, outperforming the strongest baseline (E2FGAN: MSE 0.314, r 0.808, cos 0.828). Performance remains remarkably stable as sequence length increases from 3 to 30 frames (whole-brain MSE: 0.282 → 0.277 → 0.281), whereas all baselines show clear degradation. In task-relevant visual and audiovisual cortices, the method achieves even higher fidelity (e.g., V1+A1 at 30 frames: MSE 0.200, r 0.844, cos 0.885).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four EEG-to-fMRI baselines: CNN-TC (convolutional transcoder), CNN-TAG (graph-attention network), E2FNet (U-Net encoder-decoder), and E2FGAN (adversarial variant of E2FNet). The proposed method wins on all three metrics (MSE, Pearson r, cosine similarity) across every combination of frame length (3/10/30) and spatial region (whole brain, V1, V1+A1). The advantage is most pronounced at longer sequences: at 30 frames on the whole brain, the method reduces MSE by ~10% relative to E2FGAN (0.281 vs. 0.314) and improves cosine similarity by 1.7 points (0.845 vs. 0.828), while baselines degrade noticeably from 3 to 30 frames. Among baselines, E2FGAN is consistently the strongest, followed by E2FNet, CNN-TAG, and CNN-TC, with the CNN-based methods showing the largest performance drop as temporal horizon increases. The gap in task-relevant visual/auditory cortices is even larger (e.g., V1+A1 at 30 frames: cos 0.885 vs. 0.874 for E2FGAN), suggesting the spatiotemporal modeling captures sensory-driven dynamics more faithfully than frame-independent approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-fMRI translation line (CNN-TC, CNN-TAG, E2fNet/E2fGAN, CATD, NeuroBOLT) by reframing the task from independent frame-by-frame reconstruction to joint spatiotemporal sequence modeling within a diffusion-transformer framework. It builds directly on DDPM's denoising score matching objective and the DiT architecture, adapting them to vertex-level cortical representations with sequence-level EEG cross-attention. The key methodological departure is the null-space constrained sampling mechanism, which repurposes linear measurement-consistency projections (range-null decomposition) as a plug-in during reverse diffusion to enforce anchor-frame consistency without retraining—a technique not previously applied to diffusion-based neural signal reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical pathway to leverage EEG's millisecond temporal resolution to estimate fMRI dynamics at cortical-vertex resolution, with the null-space InterRecon offering a retraining-free solution to a common preprocessing need (missing/corrupted frame interpolation). The downstream visual decoding validation via CineSync-f moves evaluation beyond pixel-level metrics toward functional plausibility, though the decoded videos only recover coarse scene semantics. Two significant limitations remain open: the model is strictly subject-specific (no cross-subject generalization), and the fixed 4-second hemodynamic delay ignores region-dependent and time-varying latency, both of which will need resolution before the approach scales to broader clinical or research applications.
