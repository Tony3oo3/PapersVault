## Problem

CLIP's visual encoder has limited understanding capacity along two complementary axes: Discriminative Ability (class separability) and Detail Perceptual Ability (fine-grained cues). Existing diffusion-based reconstruction methods improve P-Ability but degrade or leave D-Ability unchanged, and naively adding a contrastive loss on top of the reconstruction objective causes pervasive gradient conflicts (86.3% of training steps show negative cosine similarity between the two gradients), leading to unstable convergence and suboptimal representations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224, 336×336, or 384×384 depending on the CLIP backbone) and produces an enhanced CLIP visual encoder whose [CLS] token serves as the visual representation. Following prior work, all patch tokens are discarded and only the [CLS] token is projected via a two-layer MLP into the Stable Diffusion v2.1 condition space. The diffusion model and its VAE remain frozen throughout; the only trainable parameters are the projector (Stage-1) and the CLIP encoder via LoRA rank-16 (Stage-2), trained on CC3M with batch size 16 for 4600 steps.

## Method

DCR replaces the naive weighted sum of a contrastive loss on CLIP features and an MSE reconstruction loss with a single InfoNCE-style loss computed entirely in the predicted-noise space of a frozen diffusion model. For each image, the anchor is the noise predicted under its own CLIP-conditioned embedding, the positives are the noise predicted under an augmented view and the ground-truth diffusion noise, and the negatives are noises predicted under embeddings from other batch images. This unified objective simultaneously enforces class separability (Theorem 1: intra-class scatter decreases, inter-class scatter increases in feature space) and reconstruction fidelity (Theorem 2: the loss reduces to a scaled MSE when negatives are well-separated), eliminating gradient conflicts by construction. Training proceeds in two stages—first aligning the projector to the frozen denoiser, then fine-tuning the CLIP encoder with LoRA—so that contrastive structure in the noise space is back-propagated into the visual features without the diffusion model being updated.

## Results

On the D-Ability (zero-shot clustering) benchmark, DCR achieves average NMI/ACC/ARI of 0.76/0.67/0.54 on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L@224, 0.76/0.70/0.56 on MetaCLIP ViT-L@224, and 0.83/0.76/0.65 on SigLIP ViT-SO@224, consistently outperforming all baselines. On the P-Ability (MMVP-VLM) benchmark, DCR reaches 33.3% on OpenAI ViT-L@224 (vs. 19.2% original, 32.6% un²CLIP) and 43.0% on SigLIP ViT-SO@224 (vs. 37.8% original, 42.2% GenHancer). When plugged into LLaVA-1.5, the enhanced CLIP improves NaturalBench Q-Acc to 78.6% (vs. 76.4% original) and CV-Bench 2D ADE20K to 25.3% (vs. 17.6% original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCR outperforms all three baselines (DIVA, GenHancer, un²CLIP) on both D-Ability and P-Ability across all six CLIP backbones. On D-Ability, the advantage is most striking: GenHancer actually degrades class separability (e.g., NMI drops from 0.71 to 0.65 on OpenAI ViT-L@224), while DCR improves it to 0.76; DIVA and un²CLIP remain near or below the original model. On P-Ability (MMVP-VLM), DCR achieves 33.3% on OpenAI ViT-L@224, surpassing un²CLIP (32.6%), GenHancer (31.8%), and DIVA (25.9%), and 43.0% on SigLIP ViT-SO@224 vs. GenHancer's 42.2%. In the MLLM transfer setting (LLaVA-1.5), DCR matches DIVA and un²CLIP on MMVP-MLLM (31.3%) but leads on NaturalBench Q-Acc (78.6% vs. 77.3% GenHancer) and CV-Bench 2D ADE20K (25.3% vs. 24.4% GenHancer). The key differentiator is that DCR is the only method that simultaneously improves both abilities, whereas the baselines trade one for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based reconstruction paradigm introduced by DIVA (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), which conditions a frozen diffusion model on CLIP visual tokens to improve fine-grained perception. It also relates to GenHancer (Ma et al., ICCV 2025) and un²CLIP (Li et al., NeurIPS 2025), which push reconstruction into discrete or aligned latent spaces. The key departure is identifying that these reconstruction-only objectives improve Detail Perceptual Ability while leaving Discriminative Ability stagnant or degraded, and proposing to inject contrastive signals directly into the noise-prediction space of the diffusion process rather than combining separate contrastive and reconstruction losses in the feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that diffusion-based reconstruction can actively harm class separability challenges the assumption that generative feedback is a universally beneficial signal for representation learning, and the 86.3% gradient-conflict rate in the naive combination provides concrete evidence for why multi-objective weighting fails here. The DCR framework offers a practical, single-loss solution that leverages existing pretrained diffusion models without retraining them, reducing computational overhead compared to GenHancer and un²CLIP. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond [CLS]-token conditioning to full patch-token representations, whether it extends to video or 3D representations, and whether the two-stage training protocol can be replaced by a more principled curriculum.
