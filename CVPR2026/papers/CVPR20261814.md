## Problem

Despite VLMs' strong multimodal alignment capabilities, their potential to reason over structured graph data where multimodal entities are connected through explicit relational edges remains largely underexplored. Existing multimodal graph learning (MMGL) literature lacks a unified evaluation pipeline comparing GNN-, LLM-, and VLM-based approaches, and prior work has restricted VLMs to shallow feature concatenation or zero-shot inference without investigating them as trainable backbones for graph-structured reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each node in the six benchmark graphs (Amazon co-purchase: Movies, Toys, Grocery, Arts, CDs; social: Reddit) carries a textual description and an RGB image, with edges encoding co-purchase or co-comment relationships. Structural context is injected either as top-k (k=3) neighbor attributes appended to prompts or as aggregated latent tokens (average-pooled patch embeddings for images, mean token embeddings for text) injected into the model's representation space. The task is node classification with a 60/20/20 train/val/test split, and outputs are categorical class labels (product categories or post types).

## Method

GraphVLM is a benchmark that systematically evaluates three VLM functional roles under a unified protocol: (1) VLM-as-Encoder, where CLIP is fine-tuned via contrastive learning (optionally with a structure-aware loss aligning anchor-neighbor embeddings within a GNN framework) to produce node features for GCN, GraphSAGE, MMGCN, MGAT, and UniGraph2; (2) VLM-as-Aligner, where CLIP provides latent-space multimodal embeddings or Qwen-VL generates textual image descriptions to augment GraphLLM prompts (GraphPrompter, LLaGA, GraphGPT, GraphTranslator, MLaGA); and (3) VLM-as-Predictor, where LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen-VL-7B, or Qwen2.5-VL-7B are LoRA fine-tuned as standalone predictors with structure-aware prompts or latent-space neighbor aggregation. The key finding is that VLM-as-Predictor with supervised fine-tuning and latent-space structural fusion consistently outperforms the other two paradigms, with latent-space fusion surpassing prompt-level fusion in 15 of 18 cases due to avoiding the information bottleneck of text-only channels.

## Results

Across six multimodal graph datasets (Movies, Toys, Grocery, Arts, CDs, Reddit), the VLM-as-Predictor paradigm with Qwen-VL-7B under supervised fine-tuning and latent-space structural fusion achieves the highest node classification accuracy: 53.10% (Movies), 81.06% (Toys), 88.49% (Grocery), 92.66% (Arts), 59.82% (CDs), and 86.67% (Reddit). Zero-shot VLMs perform poorly (e.g., Qwen-VL-7B at 12.56% on Movies), but SFT alone yields a 4.3× improvement, and adding structure-aware latent-space fusion provides further gains of +1.5% to +4.7% over SFT without structure. Latent-space fusion outperforms prompt-level fusion in 15 out of 18 cases, confirming that embedding structural cues directly in the representation space is more effective than textual concatenation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The VLM-as-Predictor paradigm (Qwen-VL-7B, SFT + latent-space fusion) outperforms the best GNN-based method (MMGCN with CLIP encoder) by +3.74% on Arts (92.66% vs. 88.92%), +3.86% on Grocery (88.49% vs. 84.63%), +8.49% on CDs (59.82% vs. 51.33%), and +5.68% on Reddit (86.67% vs. 80.99%). Against the strongest GraphLLM baseline MLaGA, the VLM-as-Predictor leads on Toys (81.06% vs. 80.00%), Grocery (88.49% vs. 85.78%), and CDs (59.82% vs. 54.45%), while MLaGA retains a slight edge on Arts (89.79% vs. 92.66% — actually VLM wins here too). Within the VLM-as-Aligner paradigm, latent-space alignment (CLIP embeddings injected into LLaGA/MLaGA) consistently beats prompt-level alignment (Qwen-VL text descriptions), with LLaGA + latent achieving 50.61% on Movies vs. 49.57% for prompt-level. Among GNN baselines, CLIP and CLIP-F encoders outperform the structure-aware CLIP-F-S variant on most datasets, indicating that naive multimodal feature fusion is more reliable than explicit structure-aware contrastive encoding. The zero-shot VLM baselines (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Qwen-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) are dramatically outperformed by their SFT counterparts, with Qwen-VL-7B improving from 12.56% to 54.18% on Movies (+41.62 pp), confirming that fine-tuning is essential for VLMs to serve as effective graph learning backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously fragmented lines of work—GNN-based multimodal graph learning (MMGCN, MGAT, UniGraph2), GraphLLM methods (LLaGA, MLaGA, GraphGPT, GraphTranslator), and VLM-based prediction—under a single evaluation protocol organized around VLM functional roles. Prior benchmarks (MM-Bench, MAGB) covered only GNN-based backbones; GraphVLM is the first to systematically evaluate VLMs as encoders, aligners, and direct predictors for multimodal graph tasks. The VLM-as-Predictor paradigm, where VLMs are fine-tuned as standalone graph-learning backbones, departs from both the GNN message-passing tradition and the GraphLLM projection/instruction paradigm, repurposing VLMs' native multimodal reasoning for structural tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides the field with a standardized, reproducible protocol for comparing GNN-, LLM-, and VLM-based MMGL methods, addressing a long-standing fragmentation problem. The finding that fine-tuned VLMs as direct predictors consistently outperform both GNN and GraphLLM baselines suggests a potential paradigm shift in how multimodal graph learning is approached. Open questions remain: whether the VLM-as-Predictor advantage holds for link prediction or graph-level tasks, whether it scales to much larger graphs beyond the six relatively small datasets tested, and how to more effectively inject structural information into VLM backbones given the instability observed in structure-aware encoders. The paper is limited to node classification and 7B-scale VLMs, leaving scalability and task generality unexplored.
