## Problem

Existing trajectory-controllable video generation methods require 50+ denoising steps, incurring substantial latency. While video distillation methods can compress multi-step generators into few-step ones, directly applying them to trajectory-controllable settings causes significant degradation in both visual quality and trajectory accuracy, because the trajectory adapter trained for multi-step denoising is incompatible with the fundamentally different denoising paths of a few-step generator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261511/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a first-frame RGB image, a text prompt, and a trajectory map (either dense segmentation masks or sparse bounding boxes) as input. Trajectory maps are encoded by a pretrained 3D VAE into a latent representation of shape T/4 × H/16 × W/16 × 48, which is then injected block-by-block into the DiT generator. The output is a trajectory-controllable video of up to 121 frames, generated in only 4 denoising steps, achieving a 47× speedup over the prior state-of-the-art multi-step method.

## Method

FlashMotion employs a three-stage training pipeline: (1) a trajectory adapter (ControlNet or ResNet-based, matching the DiT block count of Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) is trained on the multi-step SlowGenerator using a dense-to-sparse diffusion loss; (2) the SlowGenerator is distilled into a 4-step FastGenerator via Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), which minimizes KL divergence between real and fake score estimates; (3) the SlowAdapter is fine-tuned into a FastAdapter using a hybrid objective combining a standard diffusion loss (for pixel-level trajectory alignment) and an adversarial loss from a diffusion discriminator (for distribution-level quality). The key insight is that diffusion loss alone produces blurry outputs because it enforces only pixel-level supervision without distribution-level consistency; the discriminator, built on a frozen DiT backbone with attention-based classifiers incorporating semantic self-attention, trajectory cross-attention, and video cross-attention layers, bridges this gap. A dynamic diffusion loss scaling mechanism (λ = ¼ × 10⁻³ × step + 0.1) adaptively balances the two objectives, and the entire Stage 3 requires only 1K steps on 4 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On FlashBench (121-frame long-sequence evaluation), FlashMotion with the ControlNet adapter achieves FID 14.35, FVD 96.08, and Mask/Box IoU 69.15/75.38, outperforming the previous multi-step SOTA MagicMotion (FID 20.03, FVD 138.83) while running 47× faster (24.44 s vs. 1158.63 s denoising time). With the lighter ResNet adapter, FlashMotion reaches FID 15.81 and FVD 108.96 on FlashBench in just 11.72 s. On MagicBench, FlashMotion (ControlNet) attains the best FID of 12.49 and FVD of 99.30, surpassing all multi-step and few-step baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashMotion (ControlNet) outperforms the strongest multi-step baseline MagicMotion on all three benchmarks: FID drops from 20.03 to 14.35 and FVD from 138.83 to 96.08 on FlashBench, while Mask IoU improves from 68.10 to 69.15, all at 47× lower denoising time (24.44 s vs. 1158.63 s). Against the other multi-step methods (Wan2.2+ControlNet, DragAnything, SG-I2V, Tora, LeviTor), FlashMotion achieves the best FID and FVD on every benchmark, with the largest margins over LeviTor (FID 64.58→14.35) and SG-I2V (FVD 252.49→96.08 on FlashBench). Among few-step distillation baselines, FlashMotion (ResNet) substantially beats DMD (FID 24.38→15.81, FVD 228.33→108.96), GAN (FID 31.32→15.81), and LCM (FVD 462.09→108.96) on FlashBench, with the most dramatic FVD improvement over LCM. With the ControlNet adapter, FlashMotion also surpasses the LCM few-step baseline by a wide margin (FID 28.34→14.35, FVD 340.29→96.08). Notably, FlashMotion (ControlNet) also beats the multi-step Wan2.2+ControlNet baseline on DAVIS (FID 45.66 vs. 43.70 is close, but FVD 690.13 vs. 791.80 and Box IoU 74.37 vs. 71.20 favor FlashMotion), confirming that few-step generation with proper adapter alignment can match or exceed 50-step inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: trajectory-controllable video generation (MagicMotion, Tora, DragAnything) and video diffusion distillation (DMD, LCM, APT). It builds directly on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B as the base generator and on MagicMotion's dense-to-sparse trajectory adapter training, but departs from both by identifying that a multi-step trajectory adapter is fundamentally incompatible with a few-step distilled generator due to different denoising paths. The key methodological contribution is a hybrid diffusion-plus-adversarial fine-tuning stage that bridges this gap, drawing on APT's diffusion discriminator design and DMD's score-matching objective in a novel combined training recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlashMotion is the first systematic treatment of few-step trajectory-controllable video generation, delivering a 47× speedup over the prior SOTA (MagicMotion) while matching or exceeding its quality and trajectory accuracy. The practical finding that a mere 1K-step fine-tune on 4 A100s suffices to adapt the trajectory adapter to the few-step regime makes this approach accessible to groups without large compute budgets. Open questions include whether the hybrid loss strategy generalizes to other control modalities (camera, depth, pose) and whether the specific choice of DMD for the distillation stage is critical versus alternatives like LCM or adversarial distillation, which the paper only partially explores due to OOM constraints.
