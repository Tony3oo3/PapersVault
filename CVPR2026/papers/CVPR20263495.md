## Problem

VLMs lack robust 3D spatial reasoning, and existing remedies—fine-tuning on 3D VQA datasets or bolting on specialized 3D encoders—either overfit to dataset-specific biases or introduce inflexible, frozen feature streams that are hard to align. The paper argues that genuine spatial understanding should emerge from learning fundamental geometric priors (view-invariant correspondences and depth consistency) rather than from high-level question-answering supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263495/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model consumes standard video frames and a text question, producing a text answer exactly like any VLM—no auxiliary 3D input is required. During training, each sample is a sequence of 8–24 frames (sampled from a ±48-frame window in DL3DV videos) annotated with dense ground-truth point correspondences on 8×8 and 24×24 grids plus per-patch depth values; these geometric annotations drive the auxiliary losses but are never exposed at test time.

## Method

GASP attaches a lightweight 2-layer MLP correspondence head to the visual-token output of every LLM transformer layer (all 28 or 32 layers), projecting them into a low-dimensional embedding space for matching. The head is trained with a dual objective: an InfoNCE contrastive loss on ground-truth point correspondences enforces 2D view-invariance, while a depth-consistency loss—computed via a differentiable soft-argmax over the same similarity scores—penalizes matches whose predicted depth disagrees with ground truth, resolving foreground-background ambiguities. The total loss combines the standard LLM language loss with weighted correspondence and depth terms, and the entire backbone is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 512) on DL3DV point tracks interleaved with LLaVA-Video-178K. Crucially, the correspondence head is discarded at inference, so the model retains its standard VLM interface while its internal QK visual self-attention has been reshaped to encode geometric correspondences (peak layer-wise PCK rises from <5% to >70%, with >85% temporal robustness at 24-frame gaps).

## Results

On All-Angles Bench, GASP-Full (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B) achieves 40.9% Camera Pose Estimation (+18.2 over its SFT baseline of 22.7%), and GASP-Full (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) reaches 52.8% (+18.7 over 34.1%). On VSI-Bench, Object Counting improves from 23.5% to 52.5% (+29.0) for LLaVA-NeXT and from 33.8% to 41.6% (+7.8) for Qwen2.5-VL. On BLINK, Multi-View reasoning improves from 42.1% to 57.1% (+15.0) for LLaVA-NeXT and from 41.5% to 53.4% (+11.9) for Qwen2.5-VL. All gains are achieved without any 3D VQA training data, using only geometric correspondence and depth consistency supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its own SFT baseline, GASP-Full (LLaVA-NeXT) wins decisively on Camera Pose Estimation (40.9 vs 22.7), Object Counting (52.5 vs 23.5), and Multi-View (57.1 vs 42.1), while the DL3DV VQA fairness baseline actually degrades performance (e.g., Camera Pose drops to 19.8), confirming gains come from the geometric objective rather than data exposure. Compared to 3D VQA fine-tuned models, GASP-Full (LLaVA-NeXT) substantially outperforms VG-LLM on Camera Pose (40.9 vs 16.5) and Multi-View (57.1 vs 50.8), and beats VLM-3R on Camera Pose (40.9 vs 22.7) and Multi-View (57.1 vs 50.1), though it trails on VSI-Bench Object Counting (52.5 vs 70.2 for VLM-3R) and Route Planning. Against general VLMs, GASP-Full (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) surpasses GPT-4o on Camera Pose (52.8 vs 27.3) and InternVL2.5-78B on Camera Pose (52.8 vs 38.6), but remains below GPT-4o on Multi-View (53.4 vs 60.2) and Rel. Depth (80.7 vs 64.5). On CV-Bench, GASP-Full (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 79.8% Overall, matching Qwen2.5-VL-32B (79.7%) and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (79.7%), indicating no catastrophic forgetting of general capabilities. On generic video benchmarks (VideoMME, TempCompass), GASP shows modest gains (+1.3 to +3.1 points), with a small 1.9% drop on NextQA for Qwen2.5-VL.

## Relation to Prior Work

GASP departs from both dominant paradigms for 3D-aware VLMs: fine-tuning on 3D VQA datasets (VILASR, SpatialMLLM, VG-LLM) and integrating frozen 3D encoders (VGGT, point clouds, BEV maps). Instead, it repurposes the QK-matching analysis from video diffusion temporal consistency work (Track4gen, DiffTrack) as a diagnostic and training signal for VLMs, applying contrastive correspondence learning and depth consistency as deep supervision across all LLM transformer layers. The correspondence head is a lightweight 2-layer MLP discarded at inference, making the approach architecturally minimal compared to dual-encoder or explicit 3D-input alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic that standard VLMs exhibit near-zero (<5%) internal visual correspondence accuracy is a useful and somewhat unexpected finding that reframes the spatial reasoning gap as a representational deficiency rather than a task-specific one. The practical appeal is strong: the method requires no 3D VQA data, no auxiliary 3D inputs at inference, and only a small LoRA + head overhead, making it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 7B models, whether ground-truth depth supervision (from DL3DV) can be replaced with predicted depth, and whether the gains persist on truly out-of-domain 3D scenes beyond video sequences.
