## Problem

Existing MLLM-based e-commerce representation learning suffers from modality imbalance caused by fixed-ratio mixed training, underutilizes the intrinsic image-text alignment within individual products (intra-product semantics), and lacks robust handling of noise prevalent in raw e-commerce data. These limitations lead to uneven degradation across downstream retrieval tasks and reduced information utilization during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262146/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes triplets of (query, positive, negative) where each element is instantiated in three modality variants—multimodal (image + title), image-only, and text-only—and the positive/negative items additionally carry MLLM-enriched titles and multi-granularity augmented images (main-subject crops, background-diversified variants, logo-refined details). A vision encoder and projector convert images into visual tokens, which are jointly processed with text tokens by the LLM; mean pooling over the last-layer hidden states yields a single unified embedding per input, used for retrieval ranking, product classification, and attribute prediction.

## Method

MOON2.0 builds on a generative MLLM backbone and introduces a Modality-driven Mixture-of-Experts in the LLM's feed-forward layers that dynamically routes tokens to specialized experts based on input modality composition, coupled with a learnable Dual-alignment Matrix and sparsity regularization to force experts to specialize in specific alignment objectives. Training employs Dual-level Alignment: an inter-product contrastive loss aligns query representations (in text-only, image-only, or multimodal form) with multimodal positive targets, while an intra-product loss explicitly aligns image and text embeddings within the same product, jointly optimized in a single end-to-end stage. Data quality is improved via MLLM-based Image-text Co-augmentation (entity-aware title enrichment plus two-stage visual expansion) and Dynamic Sample Filtering, which applies a sigmoid reliability weight with a decaying threshold to down-weight noisy triplets during contrastive training.

## Results

On the MBE2.0 benchmark, MOON2.0 achieves R@10 of 91.08 for text-to-multimodal retrieval, 94.21 for multimodal-to-multimodal retrieval, and 73.12 for text-to-image retrieval, substantially outperforming the closest baselines (GME at 64.98, 73.90, and 41.77 respectively). For product classification, MOON2.0 reaches 68.08% accuracy, and for attribute prediction it achieves 84.29% accuracy, both setting new state-of-the-art results. On the public M5Product benchmark, MOON2.0 attains 95.50% classification accuracy (vs. 78.70% for MM-Embed), and on Fashion200K it reaches 66.44% classification accuracy (vs. 63.74% for MOON).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOON2.0 achieves the best or near-best results across all nine baselines on MBE2.0. Against the closest competitor GME, MOON2.0 gains +26.10 on qᵗ→cᵐᵐ R@10 (91.08 vs. 64.98), +20.31 on qᵐᵐ→cᵐᵐ R@10 (94.21 vs. 73.90), and +31.35 on qᵗ→cⁱ R@10 (73.12 vs. 41.77), though GME edges ahead by 1.32 on qⁱ→cᵐᵐ R@10 (64.41 vs. 63.09). Compared to the direct predecessor MOON, MOON2.0 improves qᵗ→cᵐᵐ R@10 by +12.97 (91.08 vs. 78.11) and attribute prediction accuracy by +47.34 (84.29 vs. 36.95), demonstrating the impact of modality-balanced joint learning and co-augmentation. Against dual-flow models (SigLIP2, FashionCLIP) and general-purpose MLLMs (InternVL3-2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B), MOON2.0 leads by large margins (e.g., +77.88 on qᵗ→cᵐᵐ R@10 over Qwen2.5-VL-3B), confirming the advantage of domain-specific e-commerce training. On M5Product, MOON2.0's 95.50% classification accuracy surpasses MM-Embed (78.70%) and GME (73.87%) by 16.8 and 21.6 points respectively, while on Fashion200K it leads MOON by +2.70 in classification accuracy (66.44 vs. 63.74) and +2.42 in F1 (64.21 vs. 61.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

MOON2.0 is a direct successor to MOON [54], a generative MLLM-based framework for e-commerce product representation, and explicitly targets the modality imbalance that MOON's fixed-ratio mixed training (12:3:2 image/text/multimodal) induces. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two lines: (i) the shift from dual-flow CLIP-style e-commerce encoders (FashionCLIP, M5Product) toward unified MLLM backbones that handle many-to-one product relationships, and (ii) the broader MLLM-for-retrieval trend (GME, MM-Embed, VLM2vec), from which it departs by introducing a Modality-driven MoE that routes experts based on input modality composition rather than relying on token-level gating alone. The Dual-level Alignment objective and MLLM-based co-augmentation are novel contributions not present in either the dual-flow or general MLLM retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of MBE2.0 (6.4M co-augmented e-commerce samples with multi-task evaluation) provides a much-needed large-scale, domain-specific benchmark that goes beyond the relatively small M5Product and Fashion200K sets, enabling more rigorous comparison of e-commerce representation methods. The finding that intra-product image-text alignment (Dual-level Alignment) is the single most impactful component in the ablation suggests that prior work underexploited the semantic consistency already present within individual product listings. Open questions include whether the Modality-driven MoE and co-augmentation pipeline generalize beyond e-commerce to other domains with heterogeneous modality compositions, and whether the dynamic sample filtering threshold schedule transfers to noisier or cleaner data regimes.
