## Problem

Tensor Ring (TR) decomposition is inherently discrete and restricted to fixed meshgrids, limiting its applicability to continuous signals and non-meshgrid data. While low-rank tensor functional representations have extended Tucker and CP decompositions to the continuous domain via Implicit Neural Representations, extending TR to this setting remains unexplored. Moreover, a frequency-domain analysis reveals that the spectral bias of INRs causes TR factors to underrepresent high-frequency components, and this limitation propagates through the cyclic TR contraction to attenuate the reconstructed tensor.

## Input / Output

The method accepts partial, noisy, or low-resolution observations of multi-dimensional data (RGB images, videos, multispectral/hyperspectral images, and 3D point clouds) specified at continuous coordinates normalized to [-1,1], and produces a complete continuous functional mapping g_φ(v) that can be evaluated at any coordinate to yield the reconstructed signal value. Unlike discrete tensor methods that require data on a fixed grid, RepTRFD handles both meshgrid and non-meshgrid data uniformly, with the TR rank [r_1,...,r_d] and latent expansion factor β controlling representation capacity.

## Method

RepTRFD first extends TR decomposition to the continuous domain (TRFD) by parameterizing each third-order TR factor G^(k) as an INR: a shared sinusoidal frequency embedding z_k = sin(ω_0(w·v_k + b)) feeds into a mode-specific MLP that outputs the factor slice. The key reparameterization then expresses each factor as G^(k) = C^(k) ×_3 B^(k), where C^(k) is a learnable latent tensor (itself generated slice-by-slice by the INR) and B^(k) is a fixed basis matrix initialized via a Xavier-style uniform distribution. Theorem 2 proves this reparameterization amplifies the gradient ratio for high-frequency versus low-frequency components, making optimization more responsive to fine-scale details. The paper further establishes Lipschitz continuity of the overall mapping (Theorem 4) and derives the variance-preserving initialization (Theorem 3), ensuring stable training. The framework is trained end-to-end with Adam on a data-fidelity loss (plus optional regularization) over observed coordinates.

## Results

On inpainting (the primary task), RepTRFD achieves 25.70 dB PSNR on the Airplane color image at SR=0.1, surpassing the best baseline NeurTV (24.16 dB) by ~1.5 dB, and 40.75 dB on HSI Washington DC at SR=0.05, exceeding DRO-TFF (38.59 dB) by over 2 dB. On denoising, it attains 40.37 dB PSNR on HSI Washington DC at SD=0.1, outperforming NeurTV (39.36 dB) by ~1 dB. For super-resolution at ×4, RepTRFD reaches 31.01 dB PSNR on Lion, beating WIRE (30.21 dB) by ~0.8 dB while being 33× faster (12.65 s vs 423.27 s). On point cloud recovery (SHOT, SR=0.2), it achieves the lowest NRMSE across all six shapes (e.g., 0.093 on Doll vs 0.106 for WIRE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RepTRFD consistently outperforms all 13 baselines across every task and dataset. In inpainting, it beats the strongest neural-tensor baseline NeurTV by ~1.5 dB PSNR on color images and ~2.2 dB on HSI, while matching NeurTV's runtime (13.11 s vs 31.57 s on Airplane) and substantially outperforming DRO-TFF (27.52 dB) and LRTFR (25.86 dB). In denoising, the margin over NeurTV narrows to ~1 dB (40.37 vs 39.36 dB on Washington DC at SD=0.1), but RepTRFD is 7× faster (35.76 s vs 259.53 s). For super-resolution, RepTRFD surpasses the best INR baseline WIRE by ~0.8 dB at ×4 while being 33× faster, and outperforms the functional-tensor baseline LRTFR by ~2.9 dB. On point cloud recovery, it achieves the lowest NRMSE on all six SHOT shapes (e.g., 0.050 on Frog vs 0.053 for WIRE), confirming its advantage in continuous 3D signal recovery where discrete tensor methods like LRTFR degrade most noticeably (0.061 NRMSE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the low-rank tensor functional representation paradigm (LRTFR, Luo et al. 2023) from Tucker decomposition to Tensor Ring (TR) decomposition, parameterizing each TR factor as an Implicit Neural Representation with shared sinusoidal embeddings. The key departure is a reparameterization strategy—decomposing each TR factor into a learnable latent tensor multiplied by a fixed basis—drawn from weight normalization (Salimans et al.) and Fourier reparameterization (Shi et al. 2024), but applied here specifically to tensor factor learning. A novel frequency-domain analysis (Theorem 1) formally shows how spectral content in individual TR factors propagates through cyclic contractions to the reconstructed tensor, providing a theoretical justification for why standard INRs struggle with TR factor learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-propagation theorem and the reparameterization trick together offer a principled, transferable recipe for improving factor learning in any low-rank tensor functional model, not just TR. The paper explicitly identifies extending the framework to Tucker and block-term decompositions as future work, suggesting a broader program of reparameterized tensor functional representations. Limitations include the fixed (non-learned) basis, the added parameter overhead from the expansion factor β, and the fact that Theorem 2 only guarantees existence of a favorable basis rather than prescribing one. For practitioners working on inverse problems with high-order data, the ~2–3 dB gains over strong baselines like DRO-TFF and NeurTV across inpainting, denoising, and super-resolution make this a practical upgrade, while the point cloud results demonstrate genuine value for non-meshgrid data where discrete tensor methods fail entirely.
