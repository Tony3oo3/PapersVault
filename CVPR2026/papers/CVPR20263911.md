## Problem

Existing video temporal grounding datasets suffer from limited semantic coverage and scale, causing MLLMs to perform significantly worse on rare concepts than common ones in open-world settings. Moreover, simple supervised fine-tuning on such data is insufficient to close this rare/common performance gap, as the model's direct grounding ability remains weaker than its underlying video understanding capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263911/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video and a natural language text query as input and outputs start and end timestamps for the described event. Uniquely, the output also includes an explicit self-correction chain-of-thought: the model first generates a coarse prediction, then reflects using query-clip matching and event status classification (not started / in progress / ended) to refine the boundaries. For dataset annotation, the pipeline reformulates grounding as dense captioning—prompting Gemini-2.5-Pro to produce timestamped descriptions rather than directly localizing a query—because this yields significantly higher timestamp accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces OmniVTG, a 2,124-hour / 359K-query dataset built via a Semantic Coverage Iterative Expansion pipeline that identifies vocabulary gaps in existing datasets, uses an LLM to translate rare target words into event-centric search keywords for video collection, and leverages dense captioning by Gemini-2.5-Pro for automated timestamped annotation. For training, the authors propose a three-stage Self-Correction CoT paradigm on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: (1) multi-task SFT covering temporal grounding, event captioning, query-clip matching, and event status classification; (2) CoT finetuning where the model learns a 'predict-then-correct' reasoning path by first localizing a visually similar but temporally enclosing negative segment and then refining to the ground-truth interval; (3) GRPO-based reinforcement learning on challenging examples (avg. IoU 0.3) to strengthen the self-correction reasoning. The key insight is that MLLMs' video understanding abilities (e.g., judging whether a segment matches a query or classifying event state at a timestamp) are substantially stronger and more robust to rare concepts than direct grounding, so explicitly routing grounding through these understanding skills via self-correction closes the rare/common performance gap.

## Results

On four public VTG benchmarks (zero-shot), OmniVTG achieves R1@0.5 of 63.2 on Charades-STA, 39.8 on ActivityNet, 67.0 on QVHighlights, and 37.6 on TVGBench, setting new state-of-the-art on all four. On the open-world OmniVTG test set, the method reaches 63.3 R1@0.5 (full) and 62.4 R1@0.5 (rare subset), substantially outperforming Time-R1 (33.9/29.4) and UniTime (37.1/34.9). Critically, the rare-vs-common performance gap is nearly eliminated (63.3 → 62.4, only 0.9 drop), whereas Time-R1 drops by 4.5 points (33.9 → 29.4) and UniTime by 2.2 points (37.1 → 34.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniVTG outperforms all ten baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against the strongest prior method Time-R1 [32], the gains are +2.4 on Charades-STA (63.2 vs 60.8), +0.8 on ActivityNet (39.8 vs 39.0), +0.8 on QVHighlights (67.0 vs 66.2), and a substantial +8.2 on TVGBench (37.6 vs 29.4), indicating superior generalization to diverse query types. Against UniTime [17], the advantage is larger: +4.1 on Charades-STA, +17.0 on ActivityNet, and +26.0 on QVHighlights. On the open-world OmniVTG test set, the gap widens dramatically—63.3 R1@0.5 vs Time-R1's 33.9 and UniTime's 37.1—and the rare-concept subset (62.4) nearly matches the full-set score, while Time-R1 drops to 29.4 and UniTime to 34.9. The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model without any VTG training scores only 13.6 on ActivityNet and 7.1 on QVHighlights, confirming that the three-stage training paradigm (SFT + CoT + RL) is essential. Earlier task-specific methods (ChatVTG, TimeChat, HawkEye, VTimeLLM, TRACE) trail by 10–30+ points on R1@0.5, underscoring the advantage of the MLLM-based self-correction approach over hand-crafted temporal localization modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based video temporal grounding line (Time-R1, UniTime, TRACE) by introducing a Self-Correction CoT training paradigm that explicitly leverages the model's stronger video understanding capabilities to verify and refine its own grounding predictions. It departs from Time-R1's CoT+RL framework, whose generated reasoning tends to describe video content rather than perform explicit prediction validation and correction. On the data side, it moves beyond both manual-annotation datasets (Charades-STA, ActivityNet) and ASR-based automated pipelines (MAD, QuerYD) by proposing a Semantic Coverage Iterative Expansion strategy that actively identifies and fills vocabulary gaps using LLM-guided video retrieval and caption-centric annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OmniVTG dataset (2124 hours, 359K queries) addresses a real gap in open-world VTG evaluation by covering rare concepts that prior benchmarks largely miss, and its 95% vocabulary coverage versus ~48% for ActivityNet is a meaningful step for long-tail robustness research. The finding that MLLMs' video understanding significantly outperforms their direct grounding—and that this gap is smaller for rare concepts—provides a principled rationale for the self-correction approach and may inform future training designs beyond VTG. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to other MLLM backbones, whether the automated annotation quality degrades on truly novel domains, and whether the rare-concept robustness holds under distribution shift beyond the ActivityNet rare subset.
