## Problem

Supervised face retouching is limited to pixel-level label mimicry and cannot capture subjective human aesthetic preferences, while online RL methods like FlowGRPO introduce stochastic drift that produces noise artifacts incompatible with high-fidelity portrait editing. Additionally, existing reward models for image generation lack the fine-grained perceptual sensitivity needed to evaluate subtle retouching dimensions such as skin smoothing, blemish removal, and texture preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face portrait image as input and produces a retouched face image that removes blemishes while preserving identity features (moles, pores, natural skin texture). The editing policy is a flow-matching model (Flux Kontext with LoRA) that generates the retouched image through iterative denoising steps. The reward model separately takes an (input, output) image pair and produces structured multi-dimensional aesthetic scores across five dimensions plus a final preference decision.

## Method

BeautyGRPO extends FlowGRPO with Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG), which stabilizes SDE-based exploration by anchoring the sampling trajectory to high-preference exemplars from the FRPref-10K dataset. At each denoising timestep, DPG computes a straight-line ODE path from the current state to the anchor, derives a correction vector, and blends it with standard Gaussian noise via a time-decaying coefficient λ(t), so early steps enforce structural stability while later steps permit fine-grained stochastic exploration. The anchor acts only as a trajectory regularizer during sampling—not as a supervision target—allowing the policy to surpass anchor quality when the reward signal indicates better results. A specialized reward model, trained on FRPref-10K (10K preference pairs annotated across five dimensions via VLM ensemble + human adjudication) using a three-stage SFT → consistency-filtered self-training → GRPO pipeline, provides the dense multi-dimensional reward signal driving the GRPO policy optimization.

## Results

On FFHQR, BeautyGRPO achieves the best scores across all six no-reference metrics: NIMA 5.123, MUSIQ 4.906, MANIQA 1.079, NRQM 8.401, TOPIQ 0.676, and NIQE 10.831 (lower is better), outperforming the best specialized model (RetouchFormer: NIMA 4.723, MUSIQ 4.465) and the best general editing model (NanoBanana: NIMA 4.919, MUSIQ 4.681). On the in-the-wild set, similar gains are observed (NIMA 5.357, MUSIQ 4.982, TOPIQ 0.674). In a 100-participant user study, BeautyGRPO achieves a 63.25% win rate versus 6.50–12.00% for all competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BeautyGRPO outperforms all specialized retouching models (RetouchFormer, VRetouchEr, RestoreFormer, ABPN) and general editing models (NanoBanana, SeedDream4.0, ICEdit) on every no-reference aesthetic and naturalness metric on both FFHQR and in-the-wild. The most direct comparison is against FlowGRPO (the same Flux-Kontext-LoRA backbone with standard SDE-based RL but no DPG): BeautyGRPO improves NIMA from 4.573 to 5.123 (+0.55) and NIQE from 15.024 to 10.831 (−4.2) on FFHQR, confirming that DPG's trajectory stabilization is the key factor preventing noise artifacts. Against the best supervised baseline (Flux.K w/LoRA, FID 1.735), BeautyGRPO has a higher FID (4.054), which the authors attribute to exploring beyond the label distribution; however, it leads on all perceptual quality metrics and maintains competitive ArcFace identity scores (0.952 vs. 0.973). In the user study, BeautyGRPO's 63.25% win rate dwarfs the next-best method (Kontext LoRA at 12.00%), and the reward model ablation (Table 3) shows that replacing the fine-grained FRPref-10K reward with EditReward, EditScore, or UnifiedReward-Edit consistently degrades all four aesthetic metrics by 0.2–0.4 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FlowGRPO framework (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2025), which adapts GRPO-style online RL to flow-matching models, by introducing Dynamic Path Guidance (DPG) to resolve a task-specific conflict: the stochastic SDE exploration that benefits text-to-image diversity actively degrades the high-fidelity requirements of face retouching. It departs from the dominant supervised paradigm in face retouching (RetouchFormer, RestoreFormer, ABPN) by replacing pixel-level label mimicry with preference-driven optimization, and from general-purpose editing reward models (EditReward, EditScore, UnifiedReward) by training a dedicated multi-dimensional reward model on the newly constructed FRPref-10K dataset. The GRPO objective itself is inherited from DeepSeek-R1, and the reward model's three-stage training (SFT → self-training with consistency filtering → GRPO) follows the UnifiedReward-Thinking paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete, previously underexplored tension in applying online RL to high-fidelity image editing tasks: uncontrolled SDE drift produces visible artifacts that supervised methods avoid, yet supervised methods cannot surpass their training labels in aesthetic quality. DPG offers a general mechanism (anchor-based trajectory replanning with time-decaying stochasticity) that could transfer to other precision-critical RL tasks beyond face retouching. Open questions include whether the VLM-annotated reward model truly captures the full breadth of human aesthetic judgment across diverse demographics, whether DPG's anchor selection strategy scales to tasks without natural high-quality exemplars, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D face editing. The reliance on a proprietary backbone (Flux-Kontext) and a single LoRA configuration limits immediate reproducibility.
