## Problem

Existing single object trackers, both RGB-based and unified multi-modal, treat temporal modeling superficially by passing limited inter-frame information (propagated tokens or template updates) without intrinsic temporal supervision. This implicit treatment hinders the model's ability to capture complex temporal dependencies and appearance transitions, limiting adaptability to dynamic target changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260700/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a search region (6-channel RGB+modality image at 224–384 px), a 5-frame video clip (6-channel at 112–192 px) serving as temporal references, and an optional CLIP-encoded language description. The search and clip are formed by channel-wise concatenation of RGB with depth, thermal, event, or other modality channels, enabling a single model to handle all five modality combinations. At inference, the generative decoder is disabled, so the model outputs only a bounding box and target presence score with no additional computational cost.

## Method

TGTrack introduces a temporal generative learning paradigm in which a transformer-based generative decoder autoregressively predicts the next frame's patch-level representation from all preceding frames in the video clip, supervised by an MSE loss. A gated fusion module (sigmoid-based) dynamically blends the accumulated historical memory with the current frame feature at each step, preserving early-frame semantics and alleviating feature degradation over long sequences. A novel time token embedding, initialized via polar-coordinate rotation of a shared learnable vector with per-frame angles, explicitly encodes temporal position into the encoder's patch embeddings. The overall architecture is a unified one-stream transformer (HiViT backbone, FastiTPN-initialized) that jointly processes search, clip, and text tokens; the generative decoder operates only during training as an auxiliary supervision signal, while the tracking prediction head (OSTrack-style classification + regression) produces the final output.

## Results

TGTrack-B384 achieves state-of-the-art AUC of 75.3% on LaSOT, 54.8% on LaSOT_ext, and 87.5% on TrackingNet, while TGTrack-L384 further reaches 76.4% AUC on LaSOT and 81.8% AO on GOT-10k. On multi-modal benchmarks, TGTrack-L384 attains 67.5% F-score on DepthTrack, 63.3% AUC on LasHeR, 65.7% AUC on VisEvent, and 68.6% AUC on TNL2K. Notably, the lightweight TGTrack-S224 (35M parameters) surpasses much larger temporal trackers ARTrackV2 (130M) and AQATrack (72M) on LaSOT, and TGTrack-T224 runs at 108 FPS on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGTrack consistently outperforms the strongest unified tracker SUTrack across all model scales: TGTrack-B384 beats SUTrack-B384 by 0.9% AUC on LaSOT (75.3 vs 74.4), and TGTrack-L384 surpasses SUTrack-L384 by 1.2% (76.4 vs 75.2). On RGB-Depth, TGTrack-L384 achieves 67.5% F-score on DepthTrack, exceeding the temporal tracker STTrack by 4.2% and SUTrack-L384 by 1.1%. On RGB-Thermal (LasHeR), TGTrack-L384 leads SUTrack-L384 by 1.4% AUC (63.3 vs 61.9) and CSTrack by 2.5%. On RGB-Event (VisEvent), TGTrack-L384 achieves 65.7% AUC, surpassing CSTrack (65.2%) and SUTrack-L384 (63.8%). On RGB-Language (TNL2K), TGTrack-L384 attains 68.6% AUC, beating SUTrack-L384 by 0.7% and DUTrack by 3.7%. The efficiency advantage is notable: TGTrack-S224 (35M params) outperforms ARTrackV2-256 (130M) by 1.3% AUC on LaSOT, and TGTrack-T224 (22M) exceeds SUTrack-T224 by 1.7% while running at 108 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

TGTrack builds directly on SUTrack's unified one-stream transformer architecture for multi-modal tracking and adopts OSTrack's prediction head, but departs from both prior temporal modeling paradigms (template update and token propagation) by introducing an autoregressive generative decoder that predicts future frame representations as an explicit training-time supervision signal. The time token embedding with polar-coordinate initialization is a novel mechanism for encoding temporal position, distinct from the implicit temporal cues in prior token-propagation trackers. The paper explicitly differentiates itself from AR-TrackV2, which also uses a generative objective but for target appearance reconstruction in template updates rather than as a temporal dynamics learning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical advantage is that the temporal generative decoder operates only during training, adding zero inference overhead while consistently improving tracking across all five modalities — a property that makes the technique easy to adopt in existing tracker pipelines. The paper's stated limitation is that it assumes fixed RGB+X input combinations, leaving open the important question of how temporal generative learning adapts to dynamic sensor availability or missing modalities in real-world deployment. For the broader tracking community, the finding that a simple next-frame prediction regularizer (with a small loss weight of 0.1) yields consistent gains across 11 benchmarks suggests that explicit temporal supervision is an underexploited and broadly applicable training strategy.
