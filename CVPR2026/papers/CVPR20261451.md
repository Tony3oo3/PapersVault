## Problem

Existing in-context segmentation methods are confined to semantic or instance-level understanding of a single reference image, making it difficult to express fine-grained segmentation needs such as 'the chair with a back of that specific color.' Finding a perfectly matching exemplar for rare attribute combinations is impractical, and no prior framework supports composing multiple visual cues (object semantics, part morphology, color) into a unified segmentation instruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261451/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image plus three compositional prompts: a semantic reference (image + binary mask), a part reference (image + binary mask), and a color reference specified as a single 3×1×1 RGB pixel. The model can flexibly handle subsets of these prompts (semantic only, semantic+part, or full semantic+part+color), and outputs a set of fine-grained instance segmentation masks constrained by the specified attributes. For negative samples where the target's part or color does not match the prompt, the expected output is an all-zero mask, enabling evaluation of instruction discrimination via False Positive Rate.

## Method

CDICS builds on the SINE architecture with a DINOv2 backbone and introduces a decoupled two-stage decoder that separates semantic identification from appearance matching to avoid feature coupling. In the encoder, a Color Fusion Module converts the single-pixel color reference into a spatial similarity map via CIEDE2000 color difference in CIELAB space, which is injected into target features through a FiLM layer. Stage 1 uses only the semantic prototype to produce coarse instance masks via a transformer decoder with learnable queries. Stage 2 then applies an Appearance Fusion Module that uses the part prototype to spatially gate the color-enhanced features via cosine similarity, back-feeds the result to produce an appearance-aware part feature, and concatenates it with the semantic prototype to form a compositional feature for final mask prediction. Training uses Hungarian-matched classification and BCE+Dice mask losses at both stages, jointly on ColorPACO and COCO-Ins.

## Results

On the compositional segmentation benchmark ColorPACO, CDICS achieves 57.6% IoU and 3.9% FPR, substantially outperforming the strongest referring segmentation baseline HyperSeg (42.9% IoU, 8.3% FPR) by 14.7 IoU points and halving the false positive rate. On the out-of-domain ColorPartImageNet dataset, CDICS reaches 84.4% IoU with only 12.5% FPR, balancing accuracy and fidelity better than SegGPT (85.2% IoU but 23.8% FPR). On the standard few-shot segmentation benchmark COCO-20i, CDICS attains 65.2% IoU, marginally surpassing SINE (64.5%) and confirming that the compositional modules do not degrade general segmentation. For instance-level evaluation on ColorPACO, CDICS achieves 20.8% AP and 34.9% AP50, leading all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDICS decisively outperforms all in-context segmentation baselines on the compositional task: on ColorPACO it gains 13.5 IoU points over SINE (57.6 vs. 44.1) and 14.7 over Matcher (57.6 vs. 42.6), while reducing FPR from 17.1% (SINE) to 3.9%. Against referring segmentation models, CDICS surpasses the strongest competitor HyperSeg by 14.7 IoU points (57.6 vs. 42.9) and nearly halves its FPR (3.9% vs. 8.3%). On COCO-20i, CDICS edges out SINE by 0.7 IoU points (65.2 vs. 64.5), confirming no regression on standard few-shot segmentation. On ColorPartImageNet, CDICS trades a marginal 0.8 IoU point to SegGPT (84.4 vs. 85.2) but cuts FPR by nearly half (12.5% vs. 23.8%), indicating far fewer spurious segmentations. The post-processing baseline SINE+OIParts, which naively combines part segmentation with color filtering, lags far behind CDICS in IoU (27.5 vs. 57.6), demonstrating that end-to-end compositional learning is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

CDICS is architecturally built on SINE (NeurIPS 2024), reusing its encoder-decoder backbone and pre-trained weights, but fundamentally restructures the decoding pipeline into a two-stage decoupled design. Conceptually, it imports the compositional decomposition paradigm from CZSL and Composed Image Retrieval into the in-context segmentation setting, where prior methods (SegGPT, Matcher, SINE, LDIS) treat the reference image as a monolithic semantic cue. The color-handling mechanism—CIEDE2000 distance in CIELAB space injected via FiLM modulation—is a repurposing of colorimetric tools from the color-science literature into a learnable segmentation pipeline, and the part-guided appearance fusion via cosine-similarity gating draws on prototype-matching ideas from open-vocabulary segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: real users rarely possess a single reference image that perfectly matches a desired attribute combination (e.g., 'the chair with a red back'), and existing ICL or referring-segmentation methods cannot decompose such requests. By introducing compositional prompts and the two-stage decoupling, CDICS offers a principled path toward fine-grained, attribute-controllable segmentation without requiring perfect exemplars. Limitations include reliance on manual per-part RGB annotations for training data, evaluation on only two reconstructed datasets (ColorPACO, ColorPartImageNet), and the assumption that color can be reduced to a single reference pixel. Open questions include whether the framework scales to multi-part, multi-color compositions, whether the CIEDE2000-based color cue generalizes to textured or multi-tonal regions, and how the approach performs in interactive or video settings.
