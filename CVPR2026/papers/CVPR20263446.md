## Problem

Order-Independent Transparency (OIT) rendering removes the depth-sorting step in 3DGS, making Gaussian splats more independent, but existing OIT-based methods like SortFree remain significantly slower than volumetric-rendering 3DGS and produce lower-quality novel views. The paper identifies that the decoupling introduced by OIT creates exploitable sparsity in Gaussian activeness during optimization, which prior methods fail to harness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263446/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (Mip-NeRF 360 scenes at 1/4 original resolution to fit 24 GB GPU memory, plus full-resolution DeepBlending and Tanks & Temples) and produces a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation augmented with a per-Gaussian learnable weight function w (a degree-3 SH term) alongside the standard position, quaternion, scale, opacity, and color attributes. The trained representation is rendered to arbitrary novel viewpoints via the weighted OIT compositing equation without any depth sorting.

## Method

SparseOIT combines two acceleration strategies on top of the weighted OIT rendering equation. First, an active set method (activated after 15,000 iterations) maintains a subset of Gaussians whose attribute gradients exceed a threshold; only active Gaussians are optimized each iteration while inactive ones are handled via a cached pre-rendered image, and the active set is refreshed periodically by subsampling 30 training views via farthest point sampling to estimate gradients at sublinear cost. Second, a recursive per-splat parallelization for backward propagation exploits OIT's order-independence to let every warp lane fetch 32 pixel states concurrently and circulate them across lanes, eliminating the up-to-31 redundant computations per tile imposed by Taming-3DGS's fixed depth-order traversal. A culling strategy from SpeedySplat further reduces forward/backward workload by skipping low-opacity splats.

## Results

On Tanks & Temples, SparseOIT-B achieves 23.68 PSNR / 0.8429 SSIM / 0.1798 LPIPS in 445 s, outperforming SortFree (22.97 PSNR, 2159 s) by ~0.7 dB while being ~5× faster. On DeepBlending, SparseOIT-B reaches 29.80 PSNR / 0.9043 SSIM in 309 s, matching or exceeding 3DGS (29.70 PSNR, 1213 s) and SortFree (29.76 PSNR, 2065 s). On Mip-NeRF 360, SparseOIT-B attains 27.21 PSNR in 408 s, slightly below 3DGS (27.68 PSNR) but with ~2.2× faster training. The most aggressive variant SparseOIT-C (with Taming-3DGS densification) trains in 159–191 s across datasets, comparable to Taming-3DGS (153–183 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SortFree (the primary OIT baseline), SparseOIT-B wins on Tanks & Temples by +0.71 dB PSNR (23.68 vs 22.97) and on DeepBlending by +0.04 dB (29.80 vs 29.76), while being 4–6× faster (445 s vs 2159 s; 309 s vs 2065 s). On Mip-NeRF 360, SparseOIT-B is slightly behind SortFree by 0.12 dB PSNR (27.21 vs 27.33) but still ~5.6× faster. Against 3DGS, SparseOIT-B is within 0.1–0.5 dB PSNR on all three datasets while training 1.6–3.9× faster (e.g., 309 s vs 1213 s on DeepBlending). Against Taming-3DGS, SparseOIT-B matches or exceeds PSNR on Tanks & Temples (+0.0 dB) and DeepBlending (+0.1 dB) but is slower (445 s vs 153 s; 309 s vs 156 s); however, SparseOIT-C (with Taming's densification) narrows the speed gap to within ~5 s while maintaining competitive quality. The paper notes that OIT-based methods show particular advantages on reflective surfaces where 3DGS's depth-coupled opacity struggles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3DGS representation (Kerbl et al.) and the weighted-blended OIT rendering formulation introduced by McGuire & Bavoil and adapted to Gaussian splatting by SortFree (Hou et al.). Its key conceptual departure is recognizing that the removal of depth sorting in OIT decouples individual Gaussian splats, making the optimization landscape sparse enough to exploit via an active set method — a connection between rendering formulation and optimization strategy not previously drawn. The GPU acceleration component (recursive per-splat parallelization) extends Taming-3DGS's per-splat backward propagation by eliminating the 31 redundant computations per tile that arise from depth-order constraints, which OIT's order-independence naturally removes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OIT-based 3DGS methods have been largely sidelined due to significantly slower training and lower novel-view quality compared to volumetric rendering; this paper closes much of that gap, making OIT a more practical option for scenes with non-Lambertian or transparent materials where sorting-based methods struggle. The active set framework is a general principle that could transfer to other decoupled optimization settings beyond 3DGS. Open questions remain around effective pruning strategies for OIT (which the authors note is under-explored), the computational overhead of the learned weight term w, and whether the approach scales to even larger scenes where the active set fraction may not shrink as expected.
