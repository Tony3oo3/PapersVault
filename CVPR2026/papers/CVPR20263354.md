## Problem

Existing On-the-fly Category Discovery (OCD) methods suffer from two coupled failures: cascading feature-to-hash degradation, where poor intra-class alignment is dramatically amplified during hash quantization (minor perturbations cause bit flips and category fragmentation), and aggressive space monopolization by known classes, which compress novel categories into narrow peripheral regions of the hyperspherical representation space. These issues fundamentally limit the ability of hash-based OCD to discover emerging categories in streaming settings.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming RGB images encoded by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B-16 backbone (only the final block fine-tuned), producing 12-dimensional continuous hash features that are regularized toward 12-bit binary codes (4096 possible encodings) for efficient Hamming-distance matching. Training operates solely on a labeled support set (50% of seen-class samples), while the query set—containing both known and novel categories—is processed sequentially to simulate online inference. The framework is plug-and-play, integrating as additional loss terms into existing OCD baselines (SMILE, PHE) without modifying their backbone or hash head architecture.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage framework. Stage 1 constructs a Hyper-Semantic Space with two complementary subspaces: a Derived Subspace formed by augmenting labeled data into prototype pairs (P^par, P^aug) to capture intra-class diversity, and a Calibrated Subspace created by interpolating between different known-class prototypes to define OOD regions, enforced by a margin-based separation loss (L_OOD) that pushes known samples away from these reserved regions. Stage 2 performs Assignment-Driven Hash Learning within this space: Flexible Prototype Assignment (FPA) uses Sinkhorn-Knopp-normalized soft assignment weights over K=2 von Mises-Fisher prototypes per class for probabilistic intra-class alignment, combined with a prototype contrastive loss for inter-class separation; Binary Hash Regularization (BHR) penalizes |‖x‖−1| on both hash features and class centers to drive them toward ±1, with a Hash Center Manager updating centers via EMA (momentum 0.9). The total objective augments the base SMILE or PHE loss with weighted L_OOD, L_FPA, and L_BHR terms (α=0.1, β=0.2, γ=0.2).

## Results

On the six fine-grained benchmarks, SMILE+Ours achieves an average 12.78% improvement over SMILE on 'All' accuracy, with gains of 4.65% on 'Old' and 7.93% on 'New' classes. PHE+Ours reaches 45.8% All / 75.7% Old / 30.9% New on CUB-200-2011 and 38.8% All / 74.0% Old / 21.7% New on Stanford Cars, setting new state-of-the-art on both. The method also reduces the number of estimated categories closer to ground truth (e.g., CUB: 431→418 for PHE+Ours vs. GT=200), indicating more reliable semantic partitioning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on top of two recent OCD baselines (SMILE and PHE) and compared against three conventional clustering/classification methods (SLC, RankStat, WTA) and a concurrent diffusion-based approach (DiffGRE). SMILE+Ours outperforms SMILE by an average of 12.78% on All accuracy across all six datasets, with the largest gains on CUB (+9.2%) and Arachnida (+27.3%). PHE+Ours surpasses PHE on nearly every metric, most notably on Stanford Cars (All: 31.3→38.8, Old: 61.9→74.0) and CUB (All: 36.4→45.8, Old: 55.8→75.7). Against the concurrent DiffGRE method, SMILE+Ours achieves substantially higher All accuracy on CUB (41.4 vs. 35.4) and Arachnida (57.2 vs. 35.4), while PHE+Ours leads on CUB (45.8 vs. 37.9) and Stanford Cars (38.8 vs. 32.1). The conventional baselines (SLC, RankStat, WTA) are consistently outperformed by 10–30+ points on All accuracy, confirming that hash-based OCD methods with the proposed hyper-semantic space and assignment-driven learning offer a clear advantage over offline clustering approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the On-the-fly Category Discovery (OCD) paradigm introduced by SMILE (Du et al., CVPR 2023) and the prototype-guided hash learning framework of PHE (Zheng et al., NeurIPS 2024), positioning itself as a plug-and-play enhancement rather than a new task formulation. Conceptually, it departs from prior OCD methods by reframing the problem through a geometric lens: the Hyper-Semantic Space (combining a Derived Subspace for intra-class diversity and a Calibrated Subspace for OOD reservation) draws on prototype interpolation and vMF mixture modeling, while the Assignment-Driven Hash Learning stage integrates Sinkhorn-Knopp soft assignment and binary regularization into the hash pipeline. The lineage is GCD → offline category discovery → OCD (streaming) → this paper's geometrically-constrained hash learning, with the key departure being the explicit diagnosis and correction of space monopolization by known classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (adding three loss terms to SMILE or PHE) makes the contribution immediately adoptable by the OCD community, and the identification of known-class space monopolization as a structural bottleneck provides a useful diagnostic for future open-world recognition methods. However, the absolute accuracy on novel classes remains modest (28–34% on fine-grained benchmarks), and the method is validated only on six fine-grained datasets with a fixed ViT-B-16 backbone, leaving open whether the hyper-semantic space construction generalizes to coarser-grained or larger-scale settings. An open question is whether the two-prototype-per-class design and the specific OOD interpolation strategy scale gracefully as the number of known classes grows in truly open-world deployments.
