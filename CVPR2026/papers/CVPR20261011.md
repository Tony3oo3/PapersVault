## Problem

Existing 3D-RES methods generate queries directly from 3D points (instance-based queries), which forces an impractically large number of queries and introduces non-deterministic sampling that can miss the target object entirely. This paper asks whether queries can instead be anchored to semantic classes, drastically reducing query count while preserving the advantage of avoiding Hungarian matching.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (positions and RGB, processed via Sparse 3D U-Net and superpoint pooling into N_s superpoint features) and a natural language description (tokenized by RoBERTa), augmented with multi-view CLIP features projected to 3D. It outputs a per-point binary segmentation mask of dimension N_p, plus a per-class target-existence probability vector of length k₁=21. The total query budget is fixed at 31 (21 semantic + 10 adaptive) regardless of scene size, and inference takes ~30.7 ms per sample on an RTX 4090.

## Method

SAQN replaces instance-level queries with 21 learnable semantic-class queries plus 10 adaptive queries, all fused with text features via cross-attention at initialization to make them language-aware. A 6-layer transformer decoder updates queries through three attention paths per layer (query-to-visual, query-to-query self-attention, query-to-text), with separate MLP branches for semantic and adaptive queries. The key disambiguation mechanism is the Adaptive Query Fusion module: spatial attention maps each adaptive query's mask to specific superpoint regions, while semantic attention maps assign each adaptive query to a semantic class, and the two are combined via element-wise product to add fine-grained intra-class detail to the class-level mask. Training uses a composite loss of BCE probability loss, BCE+DICE mask loss on the matched semantic class, and an Adaptive Queries Intersection Loss that penalizes overlap between adaptive query masks to encourage spatially distinct coverage.

## Results

On ScanRefer (3D-RES), SAQN achieves the best overall mIoU of 51.1% and Acc@0.25 of 68.3%, surpassing the previous best IPDN (50.2% mIoU, 60.6% Acc@0.25) by 0.9 and 7.7 points respectively, though IPDN retains a slight edge on Acc@0.5 (54.9% vs. 53.4%). On Multi3DRefer (3D-GRES), SAQN leads in Acc@0.5 (53.8% vs. IPDN's 50.0%) and mIoU (53.1% vs. 51.7%), while IPDN edges ahead on Acc@0.25 (71.5% vs. 70.5%). SAQN also shows the strongest zero-target accuracy (55.8% with distractors) and single-target accuracy (60.3% without distractors) on Multi3DRefer, outperforming all baselines by 6–16 points in these settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAQN outperforms the strongest prior method IPDN [2] on overall mIoU (51.1 vs. 50.2) and Acc@0.25 (68.3 vs. 60.6) on ScanRefer, but trails by 1.5 points on Acc@0.5 (53.4 vs. 54.9), which the authors attribute to IPDN's richer instance-level queries favoring tighter spatial overlap. On Multi3DRefer (3D-GRES), SAQN reverses this pattern, beating IPDN by 3.8 points on Acc@0.5 (53.8 vs. 50.0) and 1.4 points on mIoU (53.1 vs. 51.7), while IPDN leads by 1.0 point on Acc@0.25. Against MDIN [30], SAQN gains 2.8 mIoU points on ScanRefer and 5.6 on Multi3DRefer, with the largest single-metric advantage being +9.1 points on zero-target Acc@0.5 (55.8 vs. 47.9 with distractors). Older baselines (TGNN, 3D-STMN, MCLN, RefMask3D, RG-SAN) are all surpassed by 5–20+ mIoU points across both benchmarks. The ablation confirms that the semantic-based query framework alone lifts zero-target accuracy from 37.1% to 54.9%, and the Adaptive Query Fusion module further recovers the multi-target drop (49.6% → 52.3% Acc@0.5) caused by cross-object ambiguity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAQN departs from the dominant instance-based query paradigm in recent 3D-RES methods (MDIN, IPDN), which inherit the query-from-points strategy of 3D instance segmentation (OneFormer3D, Mask3D, SuperPoint Transformer). Instead, it shifts to semantic class-level learnable queries (one per category, ~21 total) fused with a small set of adaptive queries, drawing conceptually from the learnable-query design in 2D-RES transformers (GRES, VLT) but avoiding Hungarian matching through implicit class alignment. The Adaptive Query Fusion module and the Adaptive Queries Intersection Loss are novel additions not present in prior 3D-RES work, addressing cross-object ambiguity that the semantic grouping introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the large query counts and non-deterministic sampling assumed necessary in 3D-RES can be eliminated without sacrificing accuracy, offering a simpler and more efficient training/inference pipeline. The 17.8% zero-target accuracy gain from the semantic framework alone highlights that class-level reasoning is a stronger inductive bias for negative discrimination than instance-level queries. A noted limitation is the reliance on a fixed 'others' category for open-vocabulary targets, which constrains practical deployment in scenes with many unseen categories. Future work could explore whether the semantic-query paradigm generalizes to other 3D grounding or segmentation tasks beyond referring expression segmentation.
