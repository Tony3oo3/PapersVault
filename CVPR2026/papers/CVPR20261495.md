## Problem

Current video-text-to-audio (VT2A) systems suffer from three interrelated limitations: visual dominance that causes models to ignore fine-grained textual instructions, the absence of a concrete formulation for event-level controllable generation, and weak instruction following due to reliance on brief categorical tags rather than structured descriptions. Existing video-level instructions cannot disambiguate multiple events of the same category, cannot jointly control multiple audio attributes (timbre, duration, volume, order), and cannot support editing operations like insertion or replacement of specific sounds.

## Input / Output

The method takes a silent video (6–30 seconds, sourced from VGGSound and PE Video) and a natural-language instruction that can specify temporal location, semantic description, and audio attributes at any combination of hierarchical levels (instance, group, or video). The instruction is interpreted through a symbolic sounding-event representation where each event is a structured tuple of temporal span (t_start, t_end), semantic description (subject, action, object), and controllable audio properties (timbre, pitch, intensity, spatialization). The output is a full audio track that must satisfy both the visual context and the event-level control constraints encoded in the instruction.

## Method

EchoVidia is a training-free agentic framework built around a VideoLLM-based agent that interacts with a pool of 12 atomic operations spanning visual reasoning (event identification, temporal cue retrieval, segment cropping), sound design (adding, removing, or modifying event representations), and audio synthesis (rendering, adjusting, and mixing audio layers). A key component is the slow-fast thinking strategy: the fast pathway captures a global video overview at 1 fps, while the slow pathway downsamples the video to 16 fps and temporally stretches it 16× to produce a 1 fps slow-motion view, enabling precise event localization and attribute inference. During inference, the agent first identifies potential sounding events and estimates their timing, then constructs and iteratively refines a symbolic event plan before passing it to the sound generation module. This hierarchical, interpretable pipeline eliminates the visual-dominance bias by explicitly reasoning about textual control constraints at the event level before synthesis.

## Results

On EchoFoley-6k, EchoVidia achieves 0.72 TemporalCtl, 0.78 TimbreCtl, and 0.75 VolumeCtl, surpassing the strongest baseline (HunyuanVideo-Foley-xxl: 0.43/0.48/0.69) by +0.29, +0.30, and +0.19 respectively, corresponding to a 40.7% average controllability improvement. In human evaluation, EchoVidia scores 3.80/5.0 on Instruction Adherence (vs. 2.60 for the best baseline) and 3.79/5.0 on Perceptual Quality. On audio aesthetics, EchoVidia leads in Production Quality (7.32), Production Complexity (4.29), Content Enjoyment (4.33), and Content Usefulness (6.50). Notably, EchoVidia trails HunyuanVideo-Foley-xl on Audio-Visual Coherence (3.93 vs. 4.20), suggesting a slight trade-off in raw visual synchronization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EchoVidia decisively outperforms all four VT2A baselines on the three controllability metrics. Against the strongest baseline, HunyuanVideo-Foley-xxl (0.43/0.48/0.69), EchoVidia gains +0.29 TempCtl, +0.30 TimbCtl, and +0.06 VolCtl; against MMAudio variants (0.26–0.30 TempCtl) and ThinkSound (0.18 TempCtl), the margins are even larger. On human-rated Instruction Adherence, EchoVidia (3.80) exceeds HunyuanVideo-Foley-xl (2.60) by +1.20 and MMAudio-S-44.1kHz (2.00) by +1.80, confirming that the agentic framework faithfully executes fine-grained textual directives. The one metric where EchoVidia trails is Audio-Visual Coherence: HunyuanVideo-Foley-xl scores 4.20 versus EchoVidia's 3.93, suggesting the diffusion-based baseline still produces slightly tighter visual synchronization at the cost of ignoring instructions. On audio aesthetics (PQ, PC, CE, CU), EchoVidia leads all baselines by 0.8–0.9 points, with the largest gap in Production Complexity (4.29 vs. 3.48). For sounding-event awareness (Table 3), the Slow-Fast strategy applied to Gemini-2.5 Pro boosts detection recall from 0.66 to 0.83 and F1 from 0.59 to 0.74, and improves localization IoU from 0.510 to 0.842, outperforming all other VideoLLMs and omni-modal models tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video-to-audio generation, sounding event localization, and agentic VideoLLM reasoning. Prior VT2A systems (MMAudio, ThinkSound, HunyuanVideo-Foley, AudioGenie) treat text conditioning as a coarse video-level prompt, while sounding event localization work (AVE-CLIP, LongVALE) focuses on detection without generation. EchoFoley departs from both by introducing a symbolic event representation as an intermediate interface between natural language and audio synthesis, and by repurposing VideoLLM reasoning (via a slow-fast dual-process strategy) to drive a training-free agentic pipeline that plans, edits, and renders event-level audio—rather than learning a monolithic video-to-audio mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete and previously under-quantified failure mode in VT2A: a systematic visual-dominance bias where models synchronize audio to visible events but largely ignore fine-grained textual directives, with controllability degrading sharply from video-level to instance-level control. The EchoFoley-6k benchmark (6,000+ triplets, 42,000 event annotations) and its three-axis evaluation suite (temporal, timbre, volume) provide the field's first systematic tool for measuring event-level controllability. Key open questions include whether the symbolic event representation can be integrated into end-to-end trainable architectures, whether the training-free agentic pipeline scales to longer or more complex videos, and whether the 937-video benchmark is sufficient to capture the full diversity of creative sound design.
