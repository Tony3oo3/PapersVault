## Problem

Existing streaming motion generation methods suffer from either high first-token latency (chunk-by-chunk diffusion must fill a full context before outputting) or limited explicit history utilization (AR models with diffusion heads). While diffusion forcing was proposed for video generation and theoretically offers advantages in both latency and history access, the paper shows that a straightforward port to 1D motion generation fails to model real motion distributions, requiring specific architectural and scheduling modifications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260141/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes time-varying text prompts (encoded via T5, max 128 tokens) that can change at any frame, along up to 100 frames of past motion context. It produces a streaming 263D motion sequence (global velocity, root rotation, joint rotations, foot contact) at 20 fps, with 1-frame streaming latency and control-response latency bounded by n_s = 5 frames. The 263D motion is first compressed to a 4D latent via a causal VAE with 4× temporal downsampling, and the diffusion process operates entirely in this latent space.

## Method

FloodDiffusion adapts diffusion forcing to 1D motion via three key modifications: (i) bi-directional attention within the active window instead of causal attention, ensuring buffered frames are denoised using the newest text prompt; (ii) a deterministic lower-triangular time schedule α_t^k = clamp(t − k/n_s, 0, 1) that creates a cascading activation pattern, replacing the random per-frame timestep sampling of vanilla diffusion forcing; and (iii) frame-wise text conditioning via a biased attention mask so each motion frame attends only to the text prompt active at its time step. The denoiser is a DiT backbone (adapted from Wan2.1) trained with flow matching (σ_t = 0) to regress the conditional velocity field. A streaming locality theorem proves that at time t, only the active window [m(t), n(t)) requires computation, yielding exact likelihood without ELBO optimization and enabling frame-by-frame output with bounded latency.

## Results

On HumanML3D, FloodDiffusion achieves an FID of 0.057, R-Precision@1 of 0.523, and MM-Dist of 2.887, matching or exceeding the best non-streaming method MoMask (FID 0.045, R@1 0.521) while operating in streaming mode. On BABEL (streaming evaluation), FloodDiffusion achieves the lowest Peak Jerk (0.713) and AUJ (14.05) among all streaming baselines, closest to real motion (PJ 1.100, AUJ 41.20). In a 100-participant user study, FloodDiffusion attains the highest Bradley-Terry scores across Preference (0.024), Transition (0.152), and Consistency (−0.021), outperforming both MotionStreamer and PRIMAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest non-streaming baseline MoMask, FloodDiffusion achieves a slightly higher FID (0.057 vs. 0.045) but wins on R-Precision@1 (0.523 vs. 0.521), R@3 (0.810 vs. 0.807), and MM-Dist (2.887 vs. 2.958), all while operating in streaming mode. Against the two streaming baselines, FloodDiffusion dominates across all metrics: versus MotionStreamer it improves FID from 0.092 to 0.057, R@1 from 0.513 to 0.523, PJ from 0.912 to 0.713, and AUJ from 16.57 to 14.05; versus PRIMAL the gains are even larger (FID 0.511→0.057, PJ 1.304→0.713, AUJ 19.36→14.05). On the streaming-specific smoothness metrics (PJ, AUJ), FloodDiffusion is the only method that beats real motion on PJ (0.713 vs. 1.100), indicating smoother transitions than ground-truth data. In the subjective user study, FloodDiffusion is the only generative model to achieve a positive Bradley-Terry score on Preference (0.024) and Transition (0.152), while MotionStreamer and PRIMAL are both rated below real motion on all three perceptual axes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FloodDiffusion adapts the diffusion forcing paradigm (originally proposed for video generation by Chen et al.) to the 1D streaming human motion domain, departing from the two dominant streaming architectures: chunk-by-chunk diffusion (PRIMAL) and autoregressive models with diffusion heads (MotionStreamer). The key conceptual departure is that vanilla diffusion forcing—designed for spatially large video frames with random per-frame timesteps and causal attention—fails entirely on motion sequences, requiring three targeted modifications: bi-directional attention within the active window, a deterministic lower-triangular time schedule replacing random sampling, and continuous frame-wise text conditioning replacing discrete prompt-refresh mechanisms. The latent diffusion backbone (causal VAE + DiT) is inherited from the Wan2.1 video generation stack, simplified for 1D temporal motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a diffusion-forcing framework can match non-streaming SOTA quality (FID 0.057 vs. MoMask's 0.045) while operating in true streaming mode with bounded per-frame latency, which is practically important for real-time NPC and robotic control applications. The ablation showing that removing bi-directional attention or the triangular schedule causes FID to explode by ~60× provides strong evidence that these modifications are not heuristic but structurally necessary for the framework to model the correct distribution. Open questions include handling prompts that reference past motion (e.g., 'repeat your last action'), long-term style consistency, and whether the approach extends to richer conditioning signals (audio, force feedback) as the authors note.
