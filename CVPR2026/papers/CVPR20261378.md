## Problem

Existing language-driven dexterous grasp generation methods map 3D observations and instructions directly to grasp parameters without explicitly modeling the physical interaction structure between multi-fingered hands and objects. This end-to-end mapping misses critical structural priors about which finger links must contact where, limiting both intention alignment with task semantics and physical grasp quality. The paper asks: what intermediate representation captures the contact geometry essential for multi-finger manipulation?

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud of the target object and a natural language grasp instruction (e.g., 'grasp the handle to pour') as input. It autoregressively produces a two-stage token sequence: first, contact tokens specifying which ShadowHand links make contact and their 3D positions on the object surface (each contact encoded as a link token followed by three discretized position-bin tokens within a dataset-specific bounding box), then action tokens encoding the 6D wrist pose and finger joint angles (quantile-normalized and binned into 256 discrete values per dimension). This structured output enables steerable generation, where users can prefix partial contact constraints and the model completes the remaining sequence.

## Method

DextER factorizes grasp prediction as p(a,C|P,T) = p(C|P,T)·p(a|C,P,T), where contact patterns C serve as an embodiment-aware intermediate reasoning step. The architecture combines a PartField 3D encoder (pretrained for part segmentation) with a Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM backbone via a 2-layer MLP projector, using hybrid attention (bidirectional for point cloud tokens, causal for text and action tokens). All continuous quantities—contact positions and grasp parameters—are discretized into 256-bin tokens registered as special vocabulary entries, enabling unified next-token prediction. Training uses standard supervised next-token prediction over the full sequence with contact position dropout (p=0.5) as regularization, which removes position tokens while retaining link tokens to encourage reasoning at varying levels of contact detail. Physics-based contact annotations are automatically generated via MuJoCo forward kinematics on both DexGYS and Dexonomy datasets, eliminating the need for manual annotation.

## Results

On the DexGYS validation set, DextER achieves a 67.14% grasp success rate, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art DexGYSNet (63.31%) by 3.83 percentage points. The most striking gain is in intention alignment: DextER's P-FID of 0.20 represents a 96.4% improvement over DexGYSNet's 5.60, indicating far better distributional match between generated grasps and language-specified task intentions. DextER also achieves roughly 2× higher diversity (δq = 13.63 vs. 6.12) and the best force-closure quality (Q1 = 0.89). On the Dexonomy zero-shot generalization benchmark, DextER outperforms DexGYS across all four splits (seen/unseen objects and grasp taxonomies), with P-FID improvements of 3–4× on the most challenging unseen-both setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DextER dominates all five baselines on the primary intention-alignment metric P-FID, scoring 0.20 versus 5.60 for the closest competitor DexGYSNet [38] (a 28× reduction) and 7.93 for SceneDiffusers [15]. In terms of physical stability, DextER achieves the highest success rate (67.14%) and force-closure quality (Q1 = 0.89), surpassing DexGYSNet (63.31%, Q1 = 0.83) and SceneDiffusers (62.24%, Q1 = 0.83). The older baselines GraspCVAE [29] and GraspTTA [16] are far behind on all metrics, with success rates below 44% and P-FID values above 29. DextER trades a small increase in Chamfer Distance (1.46 vs. DexGYSNet's 1.20) and penetration (0.37 vs. 0.22) for dramatically better distributional alignment and diversity, suggesting it captures a broader set of valid grasp modes rather than collapsing onto the nearest ground-truth pose. On the Dexonomy zero-shot generalization benchmark, DextER outperforms DexGYS across all four splits, with the largest P-FID gap on the unseen-both setting (1.23 vs. 3.04), confirming that the contact-reasoning mechanism generalizes to novel objects and grasp taxonomies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of language-conditioned dexterous grasp generation (DexGYS, DexVLG, SemGrasp) and embodied chain-of-thought reasoning (Zawalski et al., CoT-VLA, ThinkAct). Prior VLA-based grasp generators map multimodal observations directly to hand configurations, while embodied CoT work has used textual plans, bounding boxes, or trajectory waypoints as intermediate steps for parallel-jaw or mobile manipulation. DextER departs from both by introducing contact link-position pairs as the intermediate representation, repurposing the autoregressive VLM framework (Qwen2.5 + PartField 3D encoder) to first reason about which finger links touch where before synthesizing the full grasp. The physics-based contact annotation pipeline via MuJoCo is a practical contribution that enables this reasoning to be trained at scale without manual labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96.4% P-FID improvement in intention alignment is substantial and suggests that explicit contact reasoning is a missing ingredient in language-driven dexterous grasping, not merely a nice-to-have interpretability layer. The steerable generation capability—where users specify partial contact constraints and the model completes the grasp—offers a practical interface for precision manipulation tasks. However, evaluation is confined to simulation with single static objects, the success rate gain over prior SOTA is modest (3.83 p.p.), and the autoregressive framework's susceptibility to compounding errors remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether contact-based CoT transfers to multi-object scenes, dynamic manipulation, or real-world deployment, and whether combining this reasoning structure with diffusion-based generation could mitigate sequential error accumulation.
