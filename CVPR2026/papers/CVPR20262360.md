## Problem

Existing Image-CoT (test-time scaling) methods are designed for open-ended text-to-image generation, where large-scale sampling and post-hoc selection are effective. Image editing, however, is a goal-directed task constrained by the source image and instruction, causing three failures when these methods are transferred: fixed sampling budgets waste computation on simple edits, general MLLM scores misjudge early denoising states (40% of low-scoring early samples achieve high final scores), and large-scale sampling produces redundant correct results that only need one instance.

## Input / Output

ADE-CoT is a plug-and-play, training-free wrapper around existing diffusion-based editing models (Step1X-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, BAGEL). It takes a source RGB image and a natural-language edit instruction, and outputs a single edited image. The framework operates entirely at inference time: it generates multiple candidate edits by varying initial noise, applies adaptive pruning and verification at intermediate denoising timesteps, and selects the final output without any model retraining or architectural modification.

## Method

ADE-CoT introduces three mechanisms for on-demand test-time scaling. First, difficulty-aware resource allocation generates one preliminary candidate, scores it with an MLLM verifier, and dynamically scales the sampling budget via a power-law formula so that easy edits (high initial score) receive minimal samples while difficult edits receive the full budget. Second, edit-specific early pruning uses a one-step preview (leveraging flow-matching to estimate the clean latent from a noisy latent in a single forward pass) and scores each preview with a unified metric combining a general MLLM score, an edited-region correctness score (pixel change map weighted by a Grounded SAM2 mask), and an instruction-caption consistency score (CLIP similarity against an MLLM-generated target caption); visually redundant candidates are further filtered via DINOv2 embedding similarity. Third, depth-first opportunistic stopping processes surviving candidates sequentially by early-stage score, applies a late-stage adaptive filter at a second timestep, and uses a two-stage instance-specific verifier (the MLLM generates targeted yes-no questions about the edit, then answers them) to confirm fine-grained correctness, terminating the search once a configurable number of intent-aligned results are found.

## Results

On GEdit-Bench (G_O), ADE-CoT achieves 6.695 (FLUX.1 Kontext), 6.972 (BAGEL), and 7.196 (Step1X-Edit), surpassing BoN (6.641, 6.908, 7.157) while reducing NFE by 53–54%. On AnyEdit-Test (CLIP^im) and Reason-Edit (PSNR), ADE-CoT also leads: 0.883/0.893/0.878 and 25.755/27.849/23.405 dB respectively. The headline efficiency gain is a >2× speedup in reasoning efficiency η (e.g., 1.47 vs 0.66 for FLUX.1 Kontext on GEdit-Bench) and up to 5.5× in outcome efficiency ξ, confirming superior performance–efficiency trade-offs across all three models and benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADE-CoT outperforms all four baselines (BoN, PRM, PARM, TTS-EF) on every quality metric across all three models and benchmarks, while simultaneously achieving the highest efficiency scores. Against BoN, ADE-CoT improves G_O by 0.05–0.06 points and delivers a 1.8–2.4× speedup in reasoning efficiency η (e.g., 1.47 vs 0.66 for FLUX.1 Kontext on GEdit-Bench). PRM and PARM, which rely on general MLLM scores for early pruning, actually degrade performance below BoN (e.g., G_O drops from 6.641 to 6.517 for FLUX.1 Kontext), confirming the paper's claim that general scores misjudge early-stage editing candidates. TTS-EF shows the largest performance drop (G_O 6.376 vs 6.641 for FLUX.1 Kontext) because selecting a single best sample from early previews becomes unreliable at scale; even the modified version that retains more samples (6.643) still trails ADE-CoT (6.695) on quality while offering lower η (0.79 vs 1.47). On outcome efficiency ξ, ADE-CoT achieves 0.62–0.66, roughly 4–5× higher than BoN (0.12–0.14), demonstrating that depth-first opportunistic stopping effectively eliminates redundant correct outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Image-CoT test-time scaling paradigm (originally developed for text-to-image generation via Best-of-N sampling and early pruning) to the distinct setting of instruction-based image editing. It builds directly on the early-pruning strategies of PARM/PRM and the first editing-specific adaptation TTS-EF (ICEdit), but departs from them by identifying three structural mismatches: fixed budgets waste compute on easy edits, general MLLM scores misjudge early denoising states in localized edits, and breadth-first generation produces redundant correct outputs. The one-step preview mechanism leverages the flow-matching formulation of recent editing models (Step1X-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, BAGEL) to obtain interpretable intermediate images without extra denoising iterations, a design choice absent from prior pruning methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ADE-CoT provides a practical, training-free, plug-and-play recipe that delivers over 2× speedup with comparable or better quality across three SOTA editing models and three benchmarks, making test-time scaling for editing substantially more deployable. The conceptual framing—editing as goal-directed rather than open-ended—offers a principled lens for future work on other constrained generation tasks (e.g., video editing, multi-turn editing) where redundant correct outputs are common. Open questions include whether the difficulty-aware budgeting generalizes to multi-turn or compositional edits, how sensitive the framework is to MLLM verifier quality at scale, and whether the depth-first stopping criterion can be further tightened for real-time interactive editing applications.
