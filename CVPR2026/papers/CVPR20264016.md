## Problem

Existing diffusion-based stylization methods predominantly perform superficial color transfer, failing to capture deeper semantic attributes such as texture, brushstrokes, and material details from the style reference. This limitation stems from two root causes: existing style-transfer datasets are heavily imbalanced toward color-centric styles, and current style-injection mechanisms (VAE latent concatenation, uniform cross-attention) cannot differentiate between styles of varying semantic complexity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single content image and a single style reference image as input, with no text prompt required for style specification. The style image is encoded by a SigLIP-based encoder into a style latent vector that conditions the MoE router at every layer, while the content image enters through Flux-Kontext's native image-control channel. The output is a stylized image that preserves the content's spatial structure and semantic identity while transferring the style's color palette, line work, texture, and material characteristics.

## Method

StyleExpert employs a two-stage training pipeline on top of Flux-Kontext. In stage one, a style encoder (SigLIP multi-layer features + MLP) is trained with an InfoNCE contrastive loss to map images of the same style to nearby latents and different styles apart, producing a structured style representation space. In stage two, 16 LoRA experts (rank 8) are embedded in both self-attention and FFN layers alongside a shared expert; the pre-trained style encoder's latent conditions a TopK router (top-2 per layer) that dynamically selects which specialized experts to activate for a given style. The key insight is that the pre-trained style encoder provides a stable, semantically meaningful routing signal that accelerates MoE convergence and enables generalization to unseen styles, whereas without it the router lacks guidance and training becomes unstable. The method is trained on StyleExpert-40K, a curated subset of 40K content-style-stylized triplets drawn from a 500K dataset generated via community style LoRAs and OmniConsistency, balancing color-centric and semantic-centric styles.

## Results

On the StyleExpert-40K benchmark (2,100 images across 21 unseen styles), StyleExpert achieves the best scores on 5 of 6 metrics: CLIP 70.19%, CSD 73.18%, Aesthetic 6.48, Qwen Semantic 75.12, and DreamSim 28.18 (lower is better). The Qwen Semantic Score margin is particularly large, surpassing the next-best method (Qwen-Image-Edit at 42.74) by over 32 points, confirming superior semantic-level style transfer. The only metric where StyleExpert trails is DINO (64.72 vs. USO's 84.03), which the authors attribute to their method genuinely altering material/texture rather than performing superficial color transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StyleExpert outperforms all six baselines on CLIP, CSD, Aesthetic, Qwen Semantic, and DreamSim. Against OmniStyle (the closest competitor on DINO at 72.27), StyleExpert leads by 4.8 points on CLIP, 13.5 on CSD, and 35.1 on Qwen Semantic, but trails by 7.6 on DINO. USO achieves the highest DINO score (84.03) by heavily preserving original content material, yet StyleExpert surpasses it by 0.8 on CLIP, 19.6 on CSD, and 55.2 on Qwen Semantic, indicating USO's high DINO reflects minimal style transfer rather than fidelity. CSGO and DreamO are consistently outperformed across nearly all metrics, with DreamO showing the weakest CSD (47.91) and Qwen Semantic (19.29), confirming its tendency toward under-stylization. Qwen-Image-Edit is the strongest non-specialized baseline, reaching 42.74 on Qwen Semantic, yet still falls 32 points short of StyleExpert, highlighting the gap between general-purpose editing and dedicated semantic style transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based style transfer (OmniStyle, CSGO, DreamO, USO) by replacing uniform style injection (VAE latent concatenation, cross-attention, or prompt tokens) with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture where a pre-trained SigLIP-based style encoder conditions a TopK router over LoRA experts. It departs from prior MoE+LoRA work in image generation (ICEdit, MultiCrafter), which feeds hidden states directly to the router, by instead using a contrastively-trained style representation to provide structured style priors that stabilize routing. The dataset construction pipeline also breaks from prior style-transfer datasets (OmniStyle-150K) that rely on SOTA stylization methods for generation, instead leveraging community LoRAs to capture semantic-level styles that those methods miss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode in current stylization: the degeneration to color transfer that ignores texture, line work, and material semantics. The StyleExpert-500K/40K dataset and the MoE+style-encoder recipe provide a practical path toward style-agnostic, semantically faithful stylization that generalizes to unseen styles. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds or thousands of styles without expert proliferation, how it handles video or 3D stylization, and whether the Flux-Kontext dependency limits broader adoption. The reliance on community LoRAs for data generation also raises questions about style coverage and bias inherited from those models.
