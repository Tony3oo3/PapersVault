## Problem

Existing dynamic scene rendering methods rely on incompressible, source-free transport assumptions and largely ignore physical contact, yet real scenes routinely exhibit volumetric compression, appearance drift, growth/decay, and object-to-object contact that violate these constraints. This produces visible artifacts near occlusion boundaries and motion interfaces, and makes the velocity estimation problem ill-posed when density changes are confounded with genuine appearance change.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (15–20 fixed cameras at 1352×1014 on Plenoptic Video) or monocular video (800×800 frames on D-NeRF) together with a sparse SfM point cloud (subsampled to <10⁵ points) to initialize Gaussian primitives. It produces real-time novel-view renderings (120–300 FPS), a per-primitive velocity field, and an explicit source-sink field that jointly explain observed spatio-temporal intensity changes. A multi-resolution HexPlane module encodes the time-varying deformation, and mass variables for each Gaussian are optimized only within the continuity subproblem, decoupled from the color rendering branch.

## Method

The core contribution is a source-augmented continuity equation (∂tψ + u·∇ψ + ψ∇·u = q) that explicitly separates divergence-driven density change from true creation/annihilation via a dedicated source-sink basis. Velocity is parameterized through three complementary convex priors: a Helmholtz decomposition into divergence-free and potential-flow components, an anisotropic compressible prior that restricts volumetric change to a prescribed subspace, and an affine family that disentangles rotation, trace-free strain, and isotropic scaling. Contact and non-penetration constraints are enforced on implicit surfaces via per-sample closed-form projections onto Coulomb friction cones and half-spaces, solved as small second-order cone programs that batch across primitives. Training alternates a stop-gradient inner projection (yielding analytic or SOCP-solvable subproblems) with an outer loop that minimizes a projection loss, a pixel-wise continuity-consistency term, and soft contact penalties, all while preserving linear least-squares inner solves for efficiency.

## Results

On the Plenoptic Video Dataset, the method achieves 33.84 dB PSNR, 0.965 SSIM, and 0.07 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest prior baseline (MixVoxels, 30.85 dB) by approximately 3.0 dB while training in 35 minutes and rendering at 120 FPS. On the D-NeRF benchmark, it reaches 35.24 dB PSNR, 0.99 SSIM, and 0.02 LPIPS with a 6-minute training time and 300 FPS inference, outperforming Deformable4DGS (32.99 dB) and TiNeuVox (32.87 dB) by over 2 dB. The method is roughly 5.4× faster to train than K-Planes and 15× faster than HyperReel on Plenoptic Video, while sustaining 1.6× the inference speed of RealTime4DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Plenoptic Video Dataset, the method decisively outperforms all 10 baselines in PSNR (33.84 dB vs. 30.85 dB for the best prior, MixVoxels), matching K-Planes on LPIPS (0.07) while achieving the best SSIM (0.965) and the fastest training (35 min vs. 91 min for MixVoxels). On D-NeRF, the method beats Deformable4DGS by 2.25 dB (35.24 vs. 32.99), TiNeuVox by 2.37 dB, and K-Planes by 4.17 dB in PSNR, while training in 6 minutes versus 26 minutes for Deformable3DGS and rendering at 300 FPS versus 85.45 FPS. The sole exception is Deformable3DGS, which reports a higher PSNR of 39.31 dB on D-NeRF, but at the cost of 4.3× longer training and 3.5× lower inference throughput; the authors note this method prioritizes fidelity over interactive speed. Against NeRF-based baselines (D-NeRF, D-NeRF, FFDNeRF, V4D), the method achieves 2–6 dB PSNR gains while being orders of magnitude faster in both training and rendering. The ablation confirms that the compressible-flow component contributes +3.53 dB and the contact constraints contribute +1.33 dB over the baseline, with the local-linear projection variant yielding the best quality-efficiency trade-off (35.24 dB in 6 min).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting for dynamic scenes by replacing the standard incompressible, source-free transport assumption with a source-augmented continuity equation solved via Helmholtz decomposition (divergence-free + potential-flow components). It departs from prior dynamic rendering methods (D-NeRF, K-Planes, Deformable4DGS, RealTime4DGS) that either ignore volumetric change or approximate it with soft penalties, by introducing explicit source-sink fields and anisotropic compressible priors. A further departure is the incorporation of manifold/contact constraints—non-penetration and Coulomb friction on implicit surfaces—via per-sample second-order cone projections, a mechanism largely absent from the dynamic view synthesis literature. The projection-based solver with stop-gradient training follows the continuity-driven matching paradigm but generalizes it to handle coupled compressibility and contact in a unified convex framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: real dynamic scenes exhibit compression, appearance drift, and object contact that violate the divergence-free, source-free assumptions baked into most dynamic rendering pipelines, producing visible artifacts at occlusion boundaries. The practical impact is notable—120–300 FPS inference with competitive fidelity makes the approach viable for AR/VR and digital-twin applications where turnaround time is critical. However, the contact-awareness claims are under-validated: the two benchmarks (Plenoptic Video, D-NeRF) contain limited contact-heavy scenarios, and the ablation isolates only a +1.33 dB gain from contact constraints. Open questions include whether the implicit-surface contact representation generalizes to open scenes without clean SDFs, how the method behaves under significant illumination change, and whether the global mass-budget gauge remains identifiable in long sequences with topological changes.
