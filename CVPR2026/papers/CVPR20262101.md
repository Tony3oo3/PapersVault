## Problem

3D LiDAR anomaly segmentation remains underexplored: existing methods either adapt 2D post-processing heuristics (max-logit, entropy, model ensembles) to point clouds or rely on unlabeled regions during training, and the only public real-world benchmark (STU) suffers from a severe domain gap due to its 128-beam sensor resolution and provides only binary anomaly masks. No efficient approach directly models the inlier feature distribution in 3D, and no diverse, multi-resolution dataset with semantic anomaly annotations exists.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw LiDAR scan as a set of N points, each with 3D coordinates and a single intensity channel, at resolutions ranging from 32-beam (nuScenes) to 128-beam (STU). It jointly produces two per-point outputs: a semantic class label from C inlier classes and a continuous anomaly score in [0,1] that fuses a semantic-head score (cosine distance to nearest prototype × normalized softmax entropy) with a contrastive-head score (inverse norm relative to a fixed hypersphere radius). No anomaly labels or unlabeled regions are required at training time.

## Method

LIDO uses a MinkowskiNet backbone to extract per-point features, then splits into two heads. The semantic head is trained with weighted cross-entropy, Lovász-softmax, and a prototype cosine-embedding loss that pulls each point's pre-softmax features toward a confidence-weighted class prototype (accumulated from true positives of the previous epoch). The contrastive head is trained with a contrastive loss aligning per-class mean features to the same prototypes and an objectosphere loss that pushes all inlier features to the boundary of a C-dimensional hypersphere of radius r, so that unseen objects naturally fall inside. At inference, the final anomaly score is the average of the semantic-head score (1 − max cosine similarity, multiplied by normalized entropy) and the contrastive-head score (max(0, 1 − ‖f‖²/r)), requiring no ensemble or test-time augmentation. The key distinction from prior work is that anomaly detection is achieved purely by constraining the inlier feature geometry—no anomaly samples, void regions, or model ensembles are needed during training.

## Results

On the STU dataset (the only publicly available real-world 3D LiDAR anomaly segmentation benchmark), LIDO achieves 95.05% AUROC, 34.86% FPR@95, and 27.53% AP on validation, and 93.67% AUROC, 34.29% FPR@95, and 14.99% AP on test — state-of-the-art across all metrics. On the newly introduced mixed real-synthetic datasets, LIDO achieves 91.51% AUROC / 3.97% AP (SemanticPOSS-OoD single), 93.36% AUROC / 10.60% AP (SemanticKITTI-OoD single), and 89.33% AUROC / 6.79% AP (nuScenes-OoD single). The method is also significantly more efficient than ensemble baselines, using only 21.7M parameters, 38 ms runtime, and 0.6 GB memory versus 118.8M params, 861 ms, and 5.7 GB for parallel deep ensembles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIDO decisively outperforms all five anomaly-detection baselines on the STU dataset, with the largest gap over Mask4Former3D+DeepEnsemble [30] (+9.82% AP on validation, +9.82% AP on test) and Mask4Former3D+VoidClassifier [6] (+24.91% AP on validation). On the mixed real-synthetic datasets, LIDO wins on SemanticPOSS-OoD (both splits) and SemanticKITTI-OoD single split, but trails DeepEnsemble [30] on SemanticKITTI-OoD multi (9.42% vs. 12.04% AP) and nuScenes-OoD (6.79% vs. 18.34% AP single; 10.53% vs. 23.87% AP multi), where the lower LiDAR resolution and higher model uncertainty favor ensemble disagreement. The simpler post-processing baselines (MaxLogit [24], RbA [41], MCDropout [56]) are consistently outperformed by 10–25% AP across all datasets. In terms of efficiency, LIDO uses roughly 5.5× fewer parameters and 22× less runtime than sequential deep ensembles [30], making it the only method achieving real-time inference (<100 ms) while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LIDO extends the feature-space modeling paradigm from 2D open-world segmentation (Sodano et al. [55]) to the 3D LiDAR domain, combining prototype-based cosine embedding, contrastive alignment, and objectosphere losses to constrain inlier class distributions without requiring anomaly samples during training. It departs from prior 3D anomaly segmentation approaches that rely on post-processing heuristics (MaxLogit, RbA, VoidClassifier) or computationally expensive model ensembles, instead learning discriminative per-class features directly. The mixed real-synthetic datasets repurpose established LiDAR benchmarks (SemanticKITTI, nuScenes, SemanticPOSS) with ModelNet objects inserted via a beam-aligned, Lambertian-reflectance protocol, filling a gap left by the sole real-world STU dataset and the proprietary TOR4D/Rare4D collections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of three mixed real-synthetic OoD datasets at different LiDAR resolutions (32/40/64-beam) with semantic masks and controlled anomaly frequency addresses a critical evaluation gap in 3D anomaly segmentation, where prior benchmarks suffered from domain gaps, proprietary access, or ambiguous anomaly definitions. The method's efficiency (21.7M params, 38 ms, 0.6 GB) versus ensemble baselines (118.8M params, 287–861 ms, 1.9–5.7 GB) makes feature-space anomaly scoring practical for real-time autonomous driving. However, AP values remain bounded (≤27.5%) due to inherent LiDAR class imbalance and model uncertainty, and performance degrades on lower-resolution sensors, leaving open questions about cross-domain generalization and whether uncertainty calibration can further close the gap to ensemble methods.
