## Problem

Prior human-scene reconstruction methods either operate on single-view inputs or treat humans, scenes, and cameras in decoupled pipelines, resulting in inconsistent scales, drifting trajectories, and physically implausible interactions. No existing framework jointly reconstructs dynamic humans, static scene geometry, and camera poses within a single globally consistent 4D coordinate frame from multi-view video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261876/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N uncalibrated, temporally synchronized video streams (each with T frames) and optional human masks for attention suppression. It outputs temporally coherent SMPL parameters (global orientation, joint rotations, shape, translation) per human per frame, dense 3D scene point maps in a shared world coordinate frame, metric-scaled camera extrinsics, and per-region contact probability maps. The formulation is view-agnostic: with a single camera it reduces to a temporally coherent monocular reconstruction, while multi-camera setups exploit cross-view geometric cues.

## Method

TROPHIES decomposes reconstruction into three coupled components. The Scene Branch is a training-free plug-and-play module that inserts human-aware attention into DUSt3R/MonST3R (via masked softmax reweighting) or CUT3R (via dual-memory disentanglement separating static and dynamic features across time and views). The Human Branch uses a shared-weight Human Video Transformer with a two-stage cross-view interaction: symmetric attention among all views followed by anchor-referenced fusion that consolidates multi-view geometry into a single representative view, then decodes SMPL parameters and contact maps. A global alignment stage resolves metric scale via DROID-SLAM relative poses plus ZoeDepth absolute depths through Sim(3) registration, and a final joint optimization minimizes combined scene and human reprojection errors (bundle adjustment) together with a contact-aware loss that penalizes interpenetration and enforces ground-plane proximity using estimated gravity direction.

## Results

On EgoHumans, TROPHIES(CUT3R) achieves the best human accuracy with W-MPJPE of 97.54 mm, PA-MPJPE of 20.71 mm, and Accel of 14.23, while also attaining the lowest trajectory error (TE 1.03, s-TE 0.86) and highest relative reconstruction accuracy (RRA@100 0.78, s-CCA@100 0.63). On EgoExo4D, TROPHIES(CUT3R) further improves to W-MPJPE 91.7 mm, PA-MPJPE 16.92 mm, and s-TE 0.18, with near-perfect camera alignment (RRA@100 0.98, s-CCA@100 0.99). Compared to the only prior joint human-scene-camera method HSfM, TROPHIES reduces W-MPJPE by over 50% (97.54 vs. 227.82 on EgoHumans) and Accel by ~4× (14.23 vs. 57.89), while also improving all camera metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against HSfM [38], the only prior method that jointly reconstructs humans, scenes, and cameras. TROPHIES decisively outperforms HSfM on all human metrics: W-MPJPE drops from 227.82 to 97.54 (EgoHumans) and from 123.12 to 91.70 (EgoExo4D), while Accel improves from 57.89 to 14.23 and from 49.27 to 16.72, indicating far smoother and more temporally coherent motion. On camera metrics, TROPHIES(CUT3R) also wins on TE (1.03 vs. 1.79 on EgoHumans; 1.38 vs. 2.85 on EgoExo4D) and s-TE (0.86 vs. 1.23; 0.18 vs. 0.96), confirming tighter global trajectory alignment. Against the scene-only baselines (DUSt3R, MonST3R, CUT3R), TROPHIES adds human reconstruction capability while simultaneously improving camera metrics (e.g., RRA@100 rises from 0.65 to 0.78 on EgoHumans with CUT3R), validating the plug-and-play design. In the human-branch ablation (Table 3), TROPHIES surpasses monocular baselines HMR2.0, GVHMR, and VIMO on all metrics (PA-MPJPE, MPJPE, PVE, Accel) under both All-Views and Anchor-View settings, with the gravity-aware variant achieving the best MPJPE of 75.7 and Accel of 13.6 on the anchor view.

## Relation to Prior Work

TROPHIES builds on the dense multi-view reconstruction backbones (DUSt3R, MonST3R, CUT3R) for scene geometry and on temporal human motion estimators (TRAM, GVHMR) for body parameters, but departs from both by coupling them through a shared Sim(3) alignment and contact-aware bundle adjustment. It extends the per-frame multi-view human-scene reconstruction of HSfM into a temporally coherent, globally consistent 4D formulation, and generalizes single-view joint methods (JOSH, Human3R) to true multi-view inputs. The human-aware attention mechanism is a training-free, plug-and-play modification to existing transformer backbones, while the anchor-referenced cross-view attention in the Human Branch draws on multi-view feature fusion ideas from LGM but applies them to articulated body estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly reconstruct dynamic humans, static scenes, and camera trajectories in a single metrically consistent 4D space from multi-view video, addressing a gap that limits downstream applications in AR/VR, robotics, and embodied AI. The plug-and-play design (training-free scene branch, backbone-agnostic) makes it immediately applicable to new reconstruction architectures as they emerge. Open questions include scalability to multiple simultaneous humans, outdoor unstructured scenes, and real-time inference; the reliance on SMPL and 2D joint detectors also constrains generality. The paper does not explore failure modes under extreme occlusion or very sparse camera configurations despite claiming view-agnosticism.
