## Problem

LiDAR global localization remains brittle under low overlap and domain shift: retrieval-based methods encode each keyframe as a single descriptor and degrade when the query is far from the reference trajectory, while Scene Coordinate Regression must learn dense per-pixel global coordinates and is sensitive to noise. The Scene Landmark Detection (SLD) concept, which has proven effective in vision, had not been adapted to LiDAR, and no existing approach jointly discovers scene-specific landmarks at a prescribed density while maintaining a compact, map-free inference representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single LiDAR scan (or 0.5 s submap) that is voxel-downsampled and projected onto the local xy-plane to form a 512×512 BEV density image at 0.2 m/pixel (102.4 m × 102.4 m FOV). The network produces two outputs: a high-resolution single-channel landmark heatmap (1×H×W) for precise peak localization and a low-resolution correspondence map (L×d_P×d_P) that assigns each spatial patch to one of L global landmarks. At inference, local maxima are extracted from the heatmap, matched to global coordinates via the correspondence map, and a 3 DoF pose (x, y, azimuth) is estimated with RANSAC; the entire system requires only ~20 MB (network weights + landmark list) with no dense map.

## Method

BEV-SLD introduces a self-supervised scheme that jointly optimizes a modified FPN (~4.7 M parameters, two output branches) and a set of learnable global landmark coordinates Λ, eliminating the need for manual landmark placement. The key mechanism is a consistency loss: predicted per-patch global coordinates (obtained by softmax-weighted sums over a coordinate map) are pulled toward their nearest neighbors in Λ via a log-scaled distance loss, while a categorical cross-entropy correspondence loss assigns each patch to its nearest landmark index. This dual-loss design causes landmarks to drift toward geometrically salient structures (building corners, tree trunks) and the network to learn to highlight those same structures in the heatmap, resolving the circular dependency between landmark placement and detection. The two-branch output (high-res single-channel heatmap for spatial precision, low-res multi-channel correspondence for scalability) decouples localization accuracy from landmark count, allowing the system to scale from 614 to 100 k landmarks with only marginal runtime increase.

## Results

BEV-SLD achieves state-of-the-art success rate (SR, errors < 2 m / 5°) on 11 out of 16 evaluated sequences across four real-world datasets. On the Wild-Places forest dataset, it reaches 82.08% SR (V-01), 63.96% (V-02), and 61.43% (V-04), dramatically outperforming the best baseline (BEVPlace++ scene-trained at 69.01%, 42.45%, 46.65%). On the MCD campus dataset, it achieves 91.82–100% SR across five test sequences, and on the Factory Floor industrial scene it reaches 92.92% SR with 0.17 m median translational error. The method requires only ~20 MB of memory (network + landmark list) and runs at ~35 FPS at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BEVPlace++ (retrieval-based), BEV-SLD wins on SR in 10 of 16 sequences, with the largest margins on Wild-Places (82.08% vs 69.01% on V-01) and MCD night sequences (91.82% vs 86.74% on night_04); the two methods are nearly tied on MCD day_02 (both ~100% SR). Against LightLoc (scene-coordinate regression), BEV-SLD achieves substantially higher SR on all outdoor datasets (e.g., 92.04% vs 42.72% on MCD day_10; 82.08% vs 3.24% on Wild-Places V-01), though LightLoc narrowly edges it on Factory Floor SR (93.61% vs 92.92%). Against KISS-Matcher (feature-based registration), BEV-SLD wins on SR in every sequence where both are evaluated, with the gap most pronounced on Wild-Places (82.08% vs 14.77%); KISS-Matcher achieves slightly lower TE on Factory Floor (0.13 m vs 0.17 m) but at the cost of 4.17% lower SR. Against PosePN++ (absolute pose regression), BEV-SLD dominates across all 16 sequences, with SR gaps exceeding 70 percentage points on Wild-Places and 75+ points on MCD night sequences. Overall, BEV-SLD's advantage is most pronounced under low-overlap conditions (queries far from the reference trajectory), where competing methods degrade sharply while BEV-SLD maintains high SR.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEV-SLD adapts the Scene Landmark Detection (SLD) paradigm from vision (Do et al., CVPR 2022; 3DV 2024) to LiDAR bird's-eye-view images, replacing SfM points with learned scene-specific landmarks. It departs from the dominant scene-agnostic localization pipelines (retrieval-based methods like BEVPlace++, scene-coordinate regression like LightLoc, and absolute pose regression like PosePN++) by being inherently scene-specific: a compact landmark list tied to a particular environment replaces any dense map or keyframe database. The key methodological departure is a self-supervised consistency loss that jointly refines landmark positions and trains the detector, eliminating the need for manually defined landmarks or dense correspondence supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20 MB inference footprint (network + landmark list, no dense map) combined with ~35 FPS runtime makes this approach attractive for resource-constrained deployment, and the strong retention of success rate under low overlap (Fig. 7) addresses a practical gap where retrieval and registration methods degrade sharply. However, the method is fundamentally scene-specific—each environment requires its own trained network and landmark list—limiting its applicability to one-size-fits-all localization. Open questions include whether the self-supervised landmark learning can be made scene-agnostic (the authors note this as future work), whether the approach transfers to camera-based BEV representations, and how it scales to very large urban areas beyond the 23 km MulRan test.
