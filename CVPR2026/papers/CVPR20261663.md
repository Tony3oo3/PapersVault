## Problem

Pretrained VLA models degrade sharply under novel camera viewpoints and visual perturbations, yet prior work has not identified whether this brittleness stems from the spatial representation or the downstream policy. This paper shows that the failure is primarily a Spatial Modeling misalignment—the visual encoder produces distorted embeddings while the Physical Modeling components (VLM + action expert) remain functionally competent—rather than a fundamental lack of visuomotor capacity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261663/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The adapted policy receives observations from two cameras (a wrist camera and a third-person camera) plus a natural-language instruction, and autoregressively generates a sequence of discrete action tokens encoding position, orientation, and gripper commands. Adaptation is one-shot: only a single human demonstration per task is needed to learn the lightweight parameters, after which the full VLA backbone remains frozen at inference. The two proposed mechanisms introduce 4K (FTM) or 4.7M (FLA) trainable parameters, compared to 467M for a full LoRA fine-tune of the same base model.

## Method

The paper proposes a unified one-shot robustness adaptation framework with two escalating mechanisms applied exclusively to the visual pathway. Feature Token Modulation (FTM) applies a global affine transformation F̂ = (1+γ)⊙F + β to the ViT output tokens using two learnable vectors (γ, β ∈ ℝ^2048), re-centering and re-scaling the feature distribution with only 4K parameters. Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA) goes one level deeper by inserting LoRA-style low-rank updates (rank 16) into the linear layers of the SigLIP ViT encoder, adding 4.7M trainable parameters while keeping the VLM decoder and language encoder frozen. The key design principle is that viewpoint shifts induce systematic drifts in the visual embedding space rather than exposing a capacity deficit, so targeted recalibration of Spatial Modeling alone—without modifying the policy head or collecting additional data—is sufficient to restore generalization, achieving 90.8% average success on LIBERO novel viewpoints with a 99× parameter reduction over LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark under novel camera viewpoints, Feature Linear Adaptation (FLA) achieves a 90.8% average success rate across all four suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), surpassing the π0.5 One-Shot LoRA baseline (90.3%) while using only 4.7M trainable parameters versus LoRA's 467M. Feature Token Modulation (FTM), with just 4K parameters, recovers 87.2% average success rate from a zero-shot baseline of 48.5%. On the broader Libero-V benchmark (camera, lighting, texture, noise perturbations), FLA reaches 94.8% average success, matching or exceeding LoRA (94.6%) across all four perturbation types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLA (90.8%) narrowly outperforms the strongest baseline, π0.5 One-Shot LoRA (90.3%), by 0.5% while using 99× fewer trainable parameters (4.7M vs. 467M). FTM (87.2%) also exceeds LoRA on π0 (83.6%) and GeoAware-VLA's best variant (82.6%) despite having only 4K parameters. Both proposed methods substantially outperform OpenVLA-OFT-m fine-tuned on Libero-Plus (65.2%) by over 22 percentage points, demonstrating that targeted visual adaptation is far more effective than large-scale multi-view data augmentation. On the Libero-V benchmark, FLA (94.8%) marginally beats LoRA (94.6%) and clearly surpasses FTM (90.5%), with the largest gap appearing on the camera viewpoint subset (90.8% vs. 90.3% vs. 87.1%). The paper also shows that increasing FLA rank from 16 to 32 yields only a 0.4% gain (90.8%→91.2%), confirming that the low-rank structure is sufficient for the task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VLA model family (π0, π0.5, OpenVLA) and the parameter-efficient fine-tuning literature (LoRA, adapters, BitFit), but departs sharply from both data-centric robustness strategies (Libero-Plus, domain randomization) and representation-centric approaches requiring 3D-consistent architectures (GeoAware-VLA, Adapt3R). Its key conceptual departure is the explicit decomposition of a VLA into Spatial Modeling (visual encoder) and Physical Modeling (VLM + action expert), arguing that viewpoint brittleness is a spatial-representation misalignment problem rather than a policy-capacity deficit. This reframing motivates adapting only the visual pathway—via a 4K-parameter affine modulation (FTM) or a 4.7M-parameter low-rank update to the ViT (FLA)—while leaving the entire policy stack frozen, in contrast to prior work that fine-tunes the full VLA or replaces the visual backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: deploying a pretrained VLA in a new visual environment (new camera angle, lighting, background) may require only a handful of thousand learnable parameters rather than large-scale retraining or data collection, making continual adaptation in real-world settings far more tractable. However, all evidence is confined to the LIBERO simulation benchmark and one-shot adaptation to visual perturbations; it remains open whether the Spatial/Physical decomposition holds for more complex distribution shifts (e.g., novel object categories, changed physical dynamics, or real-world sensor noise) and whether the approach generalizes beyond the π0/π0.5 architecture family. The Libero-V benchmark, while useful, inherits LIBERO's simulation constraints and limited task diversity.
