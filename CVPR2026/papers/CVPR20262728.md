## Problem

Existing multi-sensor fusion methods are designed for static images and ignore the temporal dependencies inherent in video sequences, largely due to the absence of large-scale multi-modal video datasets. Naively applying image fusion frame-by-frame to video introduces inter-frame flickering and temporal incoherence, while prior video fusion attempts rely on handcrafted features or optical flow that generalize poorly across modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporally synchronized, spatially registered pair of infrared (640×480, 30 FPS, 7.5–14 μm) and visible (downsampled to 640×480, 30 FPS) video sequences as input, with visible frames degraded by Gaussian blur and infrared frames by physics-inspired stripe noise during training. It produces three outputs: a high-quality 3-channel fused video, a degradation-free restored infrared video, and a degradation-free restored visible video. Training clips use T=7 frames while inference processes T=25 frames, with boundary frames handled by replicating the current frame as its neighbor.

## Method

VideoFusion is a spatio-temporal collaborative network built on 3D convolutions and transformer blocks that jointly models cross-modal complementarity and temporal dynamics. A Cross-modal Differential Reinforcement Module (CmDRM) computes the feature difference between modalities as keys/values and uses the primary modality as query in a cross-attention mechanism, followed by a learnable contribution metric and channel-spatial attention to adaptively balance original and reinforced features. A Complete Modality-guided Fusion Module (CMGF) then uses the element-wise sum of both enhanced modalities as a shared query to attend over modality-specific keys and values, aggregating complementary context at multiple scales. For temporal modeling, a Bi-temporal Co-attention Module (BiCAM) computes forward and backward attention maps from the current frame to its neighbors, multiplies them element-wise to form a co-attention weight, and aggregates values from both directions; stacking N consecutive BiCAMs propagates long-range temporal context analogously to shifted windows in Swin Transformer. Training is supervised with a composite loss combining intensity, gradient, color, scene-fidelity, and a variational consistency loss that penalizes frame-to-frame brightness deviations to suppress flickering.

## Results

On the M3SVD dataset under degraded scenarios, VideoFusion achieves the best MI (4.008), SSIM (0.632), VIF (0.526), and flowD (3.294) among all 10 compared methods, outperforming the strongest image-fusion baseline TC-MoA (MI 2.800, SSIM 0.593, VIF 0.516, flowD 5.102) and the closest video-fusion baseline TemCoCo (MI 3.548, SSIM 0.597, VIF 0.490, flowD 4.378). On HDO, VideoFusion similarly leads in MI (2.682) and flowD (6.098). Under normal (non-degraded) M3SVD scenarios, VideoFusion achieves the best MI (4.191), SD (53.375), VIF (0.605), and flowD (3.494), confirming consistent superiority across conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoFusion outperforms all seven image-fusion baselines (U2Fusion, LRRNet, MURF, DDFM, TC-MoA, TIMFusion, GIFNet) on every metric for both M3SVD and HDO under degraded scenarios, with the largest margins on flowD (3.294 vs. 5.102–6.874 on M3SVD) and MI (4.008 vs. 1.825–3.120). Against the two video-fusion baselines, VideoFusion clearly beats RCVS on all six metrics (e.g., MI 4.008 vs. 1.979, flowD 3.294 vs. 6.443 on M3SVD), confirming that frame-by-frame video processing is insufficient. The closest competitor is TemCoCo, which also leverages temporal cues; VideoFusion still leads on MI (+0.460), SSIM (+0.035), VIF (+0.036), and flowD (−1.084) on M3SVD, and on MI (+0.280) and flowD (−0.866) on HDO, while TemCoCo slightly edges out on EN (7.174 vs. 7.167) and SD (50.421 vs. 52.465 is actually VideoFusion winning). Under normal (non-degraded) M3SVD conditions, VideoFusion extends its lead, achieving the best MI (4.191), VIF (0.605), and flowD (3.494), with GIFNet matching its SSIM (0.646) but trailing on all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established image fusion paradigm (U2Fusion, LRRNet, DDFM, TIMFusion, etc.) to the video domain, departing from prior video fusion attempts (RCVS, TemCoCo) that relied on frame-by-frame processing or DCN/optical-flow-based temporal compensation. Conceptually, it repurposes cross-modal attention (for the CmDRM differential reinforcement) and the Swin Transformer's shifted-window philosophy (for the BiCAM bi-temporal co-attention) into a unified spatio-temporal architecture, while the Restormer efficient transformer serves as the enhancement backbone. The variational consistency loss and the complete modality-guided fusion strategy (using summed cross-modal features as public queries) are novel formulations not present in prior image or video fusion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The M3SVD dataset (220 videos, 153,797 frames, covering disguise, occlusion, low-light, and overexposure simultaneously) fills a critical gap that has constrained video fusion research, and is likely to become a standard benchmark. The demonstrated superiority in temporal consistency (flowD) over both image-level and prior video-level methods validates that attention-based temporal modeling is a more robust alternative to DCN/optical-flow approaches for multi-modal settings. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences (training is limited to T=7 due to GPU memory), generalization to other modality pairs (e.g., event-camera, LiDAR), and whether the degradation-robust training protocol transfers to unseen degradation types.
