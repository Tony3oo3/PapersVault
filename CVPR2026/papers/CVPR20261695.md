## Problem

Existing neural video codecs (NVCs) optimize hierarchical structures—quality allocation across GOP layers and reference frame selection—using fixed, content-agnostic configurations, and they treat quality and reference structure optimization as separate problems. This limits coding efficiency across diverse video content and prevents the joint adaptation that traditional codecs achieve through mechanisms like multi-QP optimization and reference RDO.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261695/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec consumes a sequence of video frames in the conditional coding paradigm, operating in the feature domain rather than the pixel domain. It is trained on 7-frame and 32-frame clips from Vimeo-90k and evaluated on 1080p (UVG, MCL-JCV) and 240p–1080p (HEVC B–E) sequences under two configurations: all-frames with intra-period −1 (low-delay) and 96 frames with intra-period 32. The output is a compressed bitstream (transmitted via entropy-coded hierarchical hyperprior latents and main codec latents) and reconstructed frames, with a 4-frame GOP structure using hierarchical weights [0.5, 1.2, 0.5, 0.9].

## Method

The paper introduces a hierarchical hyperprior codec that jointly optimizes quality and reference structures in a content-adaptive manner. A hierarchical hyperprior adaptor (HH Adaptor) takes the current frame x_t and a GOP reference feature r_t as inputs, using layer-specific 3×3 convolutional parameters to produce layer-aware features that modulate the main codec's context features for quality allocation. For reference structure, the hierarchical hyperprior hh_t guides a dual-reference context fusion module that adaptively combines the high-similarity reference from frame t−1 with the high-quality reference from the keyframe, replacing the fixed fusion used in prior work. The GOP reference feature aggregates information from prior frames within the current GOP and is periodically refreshed at keyframes (by re-initializing with an all-zero tensor) to prevent error propagation. The entire hierarchical hyperprior codec—adaptor, encoder (64-channel, 64× downsampled latent), and decoder (12-channel, full-resolution output via sub-pixel convolutions)—is trained end-to-end with the main codec, enabling the quality and reference structures to co-adapt to each frame's content.

## Results

Under the IP -1 (all frames) setting, the proposed NVC achieves an average BD-rate of -22.58% relative to VTM-23.4 LDB across six datasets (UVG, MCL-JVC, HEVC B/C/D/E), outperforming the previous SOTA NVC DCVC-FM (-7.07%) by 15.51 percentage points. Under the IP 32 (96 frames) setting, the method achieves -21.02% average BD-rate vs. VTM-23.4, again surpassing DCVC-FM (-8.82%) by 12.20 percentage points. The method also demonstrates a more stable frame-level quality structure (comparable PSNR fluctuation to VTM) compared to DCVC-DC and DCVC-FM, which suffer from significant error propagation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed NVC decisively outperforms all five NVC baselines and the traditional VTM-23.4 anchor on every dataset under both IP -1 and IP 32 settings. Against the closest competitor DCVC-FM (previous SOTA NVC, -7.07% avg BD-rate at IP -1), the proposed method achieves a 15.51-point improvement, with the largest gap on HEVC D (-32.73% vs. -18.77%) and UVG (-29.11% vs. -10.36%). DCVC-DC, the architectural base of the proposed method, achieves only +25.16% (worse than VTM) on average at IP -1, highlighting the contribution of the hierarchical hyperprior codec. DCVC-RT, designed for real-time inference, performs comparably to DCVC-FM at low bitrates but degrades severely at medium/high bitrates (+25.66% avg), confirming that removing motion estimation/compensation is costly. DCVC-TCM and DCVC-HEM are substantially worse than VTM on most datasets, indicating that earlier conditional-coding NVCs have not yet matched traditional codecs. The proposed method's advantage is consistent across all six test datasets, with no dataset where it underperforms VTM-23.4.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DCVC-DC's conditional coding framework (4-frame GOP with hierarchical Lagrange weights) and EHVC's dual-reference scheme (fusing t-1 and keyframe references), introducing a new hierarchical hyperprior codec as an additional processing stage. The key conceptual departure from prior NVCs is treating quality and reference structure optimization as a joint, content-adaptive problem solved by a dedicated hyperprior derived from the current frame, rather than the fixed or independently-optimized structures used in DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM, or EHVC. The approach extends the image compression hyperprior concept (where a hyperprior models the latent distribution) into the video domain by making it hierarchical and frame-content-dependent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that content-adaptive joint optimization of quality and reference structures in NVCs yields substantial BD-rate gains (15.51% over DCVC-FM relative to VTM under IP -1) and notably more stable quality structures with reduced error propagation. However, the complexity overhead (1530 vs. 1125 kMACs/pixel vs. DCVC-FM) raises questions about real-time deployment, and the paper does not explore whether the hierarchical hyperprior approach generalizes to random-access configurations or significantly longer prediction chains beyond 300 frames. An open question is whether the gains come primarily from the content-adaptive mechanism or from the additional model capacity of the extra codec, since the ablation shows incremental contributions from each component.
