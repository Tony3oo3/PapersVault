## Problem

ESAM, the state-of-the-art online 3D instance segmentation method, relies on a computationally expensive 3D sparse UNet for point cloud feature extraction that accounts for the majority of 3D inference time, making it impractical on resource-constrained edge devices without GPU acceleration. The UNet's design concentrates latency in high-resolution layers, model size in low-resolution layers, and leaves the decoder's multi-scale feature hierarchy underutilized for prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261794/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming RGB-D video (color image I∈R^{H×W×3}, depth map D∈R^{H×W}, camera pose T∈R^{4×4} per frame) along with class-agnostic binary masks M^{2d}∈R^{H×W×L} produced by FastSAM. It outputs per-frame 3D instance masks M_t^{cur}∈R^{N×K} that are incrementally merged with previously accumulated masks M_{t-1}^{acc} via pairwise similarity matrix multiplication, maintaining a growing 3D scene segmentation S_t = ∪P_i. Processing is strictly causal (no future frames), and the SFPN backbone produces pointwise features F_p∈R^{N×96} by default.

## Method

ESAM++ replaces ESAM's 3D sparse UNet with a 3D Sparse Feature Pyramid Network (SFPN) that uses a lightweight encoder of sparse 3D convolutions and residual blocks across four spatial resolutions, followed by a decoder of sparse transposed convolutions. The key architectural insight is multi-scale feature aggregation: each decoder stage's output is upsampled to the original resolution via sparse transposed convolutions, concatenated into a unified high-resolution feature map, and passed through an MLP to yield the final 96-dimensional pointwise features. High-resolution layers use reduced channel counts (e.g., C1=8–16) to cut latency, while intermediate layers with excessive channels are removed to shrink model size. Three variants (Small 14.1M/211ms, Base 23.5M/252ms, Large 41.2M/326ms CPU) are provided. The downstream instance segmentation module lifts L 2D masks into superpoint features, initializes 3D instance queries, refines them through transformer decoder layers, and merges per-frame masks with accumulated history via efficient pairwise similarity computation.

## Results

On ScanNet200 (class-agnostic 3D instance segmentation), ESAM++-Large achieves 43.7 AP / 66.1 AP₅₀ / 81.2 AP₂₅, surpassing ESAM-E (43.4 AP) and ESAM (42.2 AP) while running at 326 ms CPU latency versus ESAM's 934 ms (~3× faster). The smallest variant (SFPN-Small, 14.1 M params) achieves 30.3 AP at 211 ms, offering a 3.2× reduction in model size over ESAM (44.6 M). On the class-aware ScanNet benchmark, ESAM++-Large reaches 43.7 AP / 63.5 AP₅₀, outperforming the best prior online method ESAM-E+FF (42.6 AP / 61.9 AP₅₀) and TD3D-MA (39.0 AP / 60.5 AP₅₀).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ESAM++-Large (43.7 AP) marginally outperforms ESAM-E (43.4 AP) and ESAM (42.2 AP) on ScanNet200 class-agnostic segmentation while reducing CPU latency from 934 ms to 326 ms (~3× speedup) and model size from 44.6 M to 41.2 M params. Against all offline baselines—SAMPro3D (18.0 AP), SAI3D (28.2 AP), and Open3DIS (34.6 AP)—ESAM++-Large leads by 9–26 AP points, and it also surpasses the prior online method SAM3D (20.2 AP) by 23.5 AP. On the class-aware ScanNet benchmark, ESAM++-Large (43.7 AP) beats ESAM-E+FF (42.6 AP) and TD3D-MA (39.0 AP), though it trails the offline OneFormer3D (59.3 AP) and TD3D (46.2 AP), which have access to full scene reconstructions. In cross-dataset generalization (ScanNet200→SceneNN and →3RScan), ESAM++-Large matches or slightly exceeds ESAM (29.3 vs 28.8 AP on SceneNN; 14.4 vs 14.1 AP on 3RScan), confirming that the efficiency gains do not come at the cost of transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

ESAM++ is a direct architectural successor to ESAM (EmbodiedSAM, ICLR 2025), which established the paradigm of lifting SAM-generated 2D masks into 3D instance queries for online segmentation. The key departure is replacing ESAM's 3D sparse UNet backbone with a 3D Sparse Feature Pyramid Network (SFPN), which adapts the multi-scale feature aggregation principle of 2D FPNs (Lin et al., 2017) to sparse 3D convolutions. This places the work at the intersection of efficient point cloud encoders (PointNet++, KPConv, PointTransformer) and online 3D perception (INS-Conv, TD3D-MA, SAM3D), but is specifically motivated by the computational analysis showing that high-resolution layers dominate latency while low-resolution layers dominate parameter count in the original UNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is enabling competitive online 3D instance segmentation on CPU-only edge hardware (190 ms/frame on an iPhone 15 A16 Bionic), which is critical for privacy-sensitive robotics, AR/VR, and autonomous navigation where server-grade GPUs are unavailable. The 3× latency reduction and 2× model size reduction while maintaining SOTA-level accuracy (43.7 AP on ScanNet200) makes real-time deployment feasible in previously impractical settings. Open questions include whether the frozen FastSAM VFM can be further optimized, whether the approach extends to RGB-only perception via integration with reconstruction models like CUT3R, and how it scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes beyond the indoor benchmarks tested.
