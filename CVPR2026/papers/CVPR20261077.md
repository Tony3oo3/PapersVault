## Problem

Recent logit-based knowledge distillation methods increasingly rely on multi-knowledge alignment and relational structure modeling, introducing redundant objectives, suboptimal output-space transformations, and loss functions (e.g., plain L2-norm) that ignore uneven variance across directional relationships. This complexity hinders training efficiency and degrades the quality of the modeled relational structure, leaving a performance gap with feature-based methods despite the practical advantages of logit-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261077/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the output logits (C-dimensional vectors) of a pre-trained teacher and a student network for a batch of B RGB images, requiring no access to intermediate features or projection modules. For classification it produces class labels over C categories; for object detection (Faster R-CNN–FPN on MS-COCO) it produces bounding boxes with class scores. Because it operates purely in the logit space, it works seamlessly across homogeneous and heterogeneous architecture pairs (e.g., ResNet50→MobileNetV1, RegNetY-16GF→ViT-Tiny) without any architectural coupling.

## Method

SKD distills exactly two complementary knowledge forms: (1) instance-wise semantics via the standard KL divergence between temperature-scaled softmax outputs, and (2) direction-wise relational structure by aligning Gramian matrices of Euclidean-Batch-Normalized (unit-norm) logits, which encode pairwise cosine similarities across the batch. The direction-wise loss uses a Mahalanobis distance over the row-wise Gramian difference, with the empirical batch covariance regularized by Tikhonov (λI) and inverted via Cholesky decomposition for numerical stability. The paper formally proves this loss is exactly equivalent to an L2-norm in a covariance-whitened space, making it variance- and correlation-aware while retaining L2 simplicity. The total objective is simply L_INS + L_DIR, eliminating the need for multi-knowledge alignment, auxiliary pooling scales, or extra projection layers that characterize prior logit-based methods.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (homogeneous settings), SKD achieves top-1 accuracies of 72.50%, 74.84%, 78.33%, 76.60%, 76.04%, and 75.75% across six teacher-student pairs, consistently outperforming all logit-based baselines (best prior: RLD at 76.64% on ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4) and feature-based baselines (best prior: OFD at 74.21% on VGG13→VGG8). On ImageNet, SKD reaches 71.13% (R34→R18) and 71.53% (R50→MV1), surpassing the strongest baselines by 0.16–0.31 points. On MS-COCO object detection, SKD achieves the highest AP (36.74, 40.62, 34.25) across three backbone pairs, exceeding the best prior logit-based method (MLKD) by 0.71 AP on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SKD outperforms all six logit-based baselines (KD, CLKD, DKD, MLKD, SDD, RLD) across every CIFAR-100 teacher-student pair, with the largest margin over RLD on ResNet32x4→ResNet8x4 (78.33% vs. 76.64%, +1.69 points) and over MLKD on WRN-40-2→WRN-40-16 (76.04% vs. 74.78%, +1.26 points). It also surpasses all five feature-based baselines (FitNet, RKD, CRD, OFD, ReviewKD) on every pair, most notably beating OFD by 1.54 points on VGG13→VGG8 (75.75% vs. 74.21%). On ImageNet, SKD edges out the strongest baselines—MLKD (70.97%) and RKD (71.22%)—by 0.16 and 0.31 points respectively. On MS-COCO, SKD achieves the best AP in all three backbone configurations, exceeding MLKD by 0.71 AP on ResNet101→ResNet50 and by 0.42 AP on ResNet50→ResNet50. Notably, in four of six CIFAR-100 settings the distilled student even surpasses the teacher's own accuracy (e.g., 76.60% student vs. 75.59% teacher for WRN-40-2→WRN-16-2), a phenomenon the authors attribute to the structured representation encouraged by the direction-wise loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

SKD builds directly on Hinton's original KD formulation [16] for the instance-wise component (single KL divergence) and on MLKD [17], which first applied the Gramian matrix to the output logit space for direction-wise knowledge. It deliberately departs from the recent trend of multi-knowledge alignment (DKD, MLKD, SDD) that stacks multiple P-distributions and pooling scales, arguing these introduce redundant objectives and suboptimal transformations. The key methodological departure is replacing the standard L2-norm on Gramian differences with a Mahalanobis-distance loss stabilized by Tikhonov regularization and Cholesky decomposition, combined with Euclidean Batch Normalization to extract pure directional information from raw logits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a deliberately minimal logit-based distillation (two losses, no projection modules, no multi-scale pooling) can match or exceed feature-based KD methods, which is practically significant for deployment in resource-constrained settings where intermediate feature access is costly or a security risk. The formal proof that the Mahalanobis direction-wise loss equals an L2-norm in a covariance-whitened space provides a principled justification rather than an ad-hoc heuristic. However, the authors acknowledge that the empirical covariance estimation can be sensitive under large batch sizes or noisy labels, and the method has not yet been extended to sequential or multi-modal domains, leaving open questions about its robustness in more complex training regimes.
