## Problem

Reconstructing accurate 3D surfaces and synthesizing novel views from a handful of uncalibrated, pose-free RGB images remains difficult because sparse views cause SfM to fail, introduce extrapolation and occlusion ambiguities, and lead to overfitting on individual views during optimization. Existing end-to-end approaches either produce sparse point clouds unsuitable for rendering, or treat scene generation and camera estimation as separate steps that accumulate errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263849/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes exactly 3 free sparse-view RGB images with no camera poses or intrinsics as input (e.g., views 23/24/33 at 800×600 on DTU, 3 random views at 1200×680 on Replica, 768×576 on BlendedMVS). It outputs a dense 2DGS Gaussian scene, an extracted 3D surface mesh, rendered novel views, and estimated camera parameters (focals and extrinsic matrices), all within approximately 2 minutes on a single RTX 5090 GPU.

## Method

FSFSplatter uses a VGGT-1B pretrained multi-view transformer backbone with DINOv2 token encoding, followed by independent prediction heads for camera parameters, depth maps, and a semi-dense Gaussian scene. A key architectural contribution is a self-splitting Gaussian densification head: DPT-reprojected features are concatenated with PointMLP-encoded geometric features and explicit Gaussian attributes, then passed through an encoder-decoder that predicts per-primitive attribute offsets, splitting each semi-dense Gaussian into N new primitives via KNN patch partitioning for cross-scene generalization. Contribution-based pruning removes floaters before optimization. During per-scene optimization, a geometry-enhanced loss combines monocular depth ranking, depth smoothness, multi-view U-Net feature consistency, and differentiable camera parameters (applied as inverse transforms before rasterization) to prevent the local-view overfitting that plagues sparse-view GS optimization. Training follows a three-stage geometry-progressive schedule: first the backbone learns depth and camera losses, then the DPT head and densification MLP are trained with the backbone frozen, and finally all parameters are jointly fine-tuned with the full loss set.

## Results

On DTU, FSFSplatter achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.581 mm for surface reconstruction (1.907 mm without per-scene optimization), outperforming VGGT (2.586 mm) and FreeSplatter (6.116 mm) by 38.9% and 74.1% respectively. For novel view synthesis, it reaches 30.25 dB PSNR / 0.110 LPIPS on DTU, 35.79 dB / 0.095 on Replica, and 30.74 dB / 0.052 on BlendedMVS, surpassing all pose-free baselines by large margins (e.g., +11.9 dB PSNR over VGGT on DTU). Per-scene optimization completes in 107 s, roughly 4× faster than 3DGS (429 s) and 9.5× faster than PGSR (1018 s), while camera pose RMSE (0.314° rot / 1.256 mm trans on DTU) is on par with VGGT (0.301° / 1.299 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest end-to-end baseline VGGT, FSFSplatter reduces DTU CD from 2.586 mm to 1.581 mm (−38.9%) and Replica CD from 50.818 mm to 32.554 mm (−35.9%), while boosting DTU NVS PSNR from 18.36 to 30.25 dB (+11.9 dB) and LPIPS from 0.194 to 0.110. Compared to FreeSplatter, gains are even larger: DTU CD drops from 6.116 to 1.581 mm (−74.1%) and Replica CD from 165.11 to 32.554 mm (−80.3%). Against per-scene optimization methods with ground-truth cameras (3DGS-GT, 2DGS-GT, PGSR-GT, FatesGS-GT), FSFSplatter is competitive on DTU CD (1.581 mm vs. 2.010 mm for 3DGS*-GT) and clearly superior on NVS without GT cameras (30.25 dB vs. 29.37 dB for 3DGS with estimated cameras). On pose estimation, FSFSplatter matches VGGT within 0.013° rotation and 0.043 mm translation on DTU, confirming that the joint optimization does not sacrifice camera accuracy. In terms of speed, FSFSplatter's 107 s optimization is 4× faster than 3DGS (429 s), 7.8× faster than 2DGS (840 s), and 9.5× faster than PGSR (1018 s), while delivering lower CD on both DTU and Replica.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSFSplatter builds directly on VGGT's large multi-view transformer backbone for joint camera and geometry estimation, but replaces VGGT's point-map output with a dense 2D Gaussian primitive head, enabling differentiable rendering-based optimization. It departs from FreeSplatter's two-stage design (single-view GS generation followed by multi-view alignment) by performing end-to-end multi-view Gaussian initialization in a single forward pass, and from DUSt3R/MASt3R's point-cloud intermediates by predicting explicit Gaussian attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH) directly. The geometry-enhanced optimization stage draws on FatesGS-style depth and feature consistency losses but adds a novel contribution-based pruning mechanism and differentiable camera parameter propagation through the rasterizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2-minute end-to-end pipeline (0.63 s initialization + ~107 s optimization) for free sparse-view reconstruction is a significant practical advance over hour-long per-scene optimization baselines, making high-quality surface reconstruction and NVS accessible in near-real-time settings. The ablation finding that naive RGB-only optimization can degrade geometry below the initialization quality—without the proposed depth-ranking, smoothness, and multi-view feature losses—challenges the common assumption that more iterations always improve reconstruction under sparse views. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes beyond the object-level (DTU) and indoor (Replica, BlendedMVS) settings tested, and whether integrating known intrinsics or depth priors (acknowledged as future work) would further close the gap to dense-view methods.
