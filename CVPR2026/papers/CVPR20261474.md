## Problem

Current MLLMs suffer from modality conflict: visual and textual representations remain misaligned in embedding space, and autoregressive text-only supervision depletes modality-specific visual details that cannot be articulated in language. This leads to degraded linguistic reasoning (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5 underperforms its LLM backbone Vicuna by ~10 points on MMLU) and an inability to perform fine-grained perceptive tasks, as the model's visual comprehension stays at a modality-shared level.

## Input / Output

DeepAlign is a post-training framework applied to existing MLLMs (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3-8B) that takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input and produces a text response. The only trainable parameters are lightweight adapter layers (~200M params) inserted into intermediate transformer layers; the vision encoder, LLM backbone, and DINOv2 encoder remain frozen. During training, the image is simultaneously processed by the MLLM's vision encoder and by DINOv2 to extract patch-level structural supervision signals.

## Method

DeepAlign employs two complementary mechanisms. First, Representation Intervention extracts modality-specific components from visual and textual hidden states using Grad-CAM-inspired gradient-based channel attention, computes instance-level and global-level modality shift directions, and trains a modality shift adapter to align visual representations toward the textual embedding space via cosine-similarity losses plus a mutual-information regularization term. Second, Structure-Induced Distillation distills DINOv2's patch-level similarity matrices into the MLLM's visual hidden states using an MSE loss, providing vision-side supervision that preserves fine-grained structural semantics beyond what text-only autoregressive loss can capture. The final training objective combines the standard autoregressive loss with all four auxiliary terms, and only the adapter layers are updated, making the framework model-agnostic and parameter-efficient.

## Results

DeepAlign achieves consistent gains across all three backbone MLLMs on 10 vision-language benchmarks. On LLaVA-v1.5-7B, it improves MMBench from 62.1 to 68.2 (+6.1), MMStar from 34.6 to 40.1 (+5.5), and ScienceQA from 69.2 to 75.3 (+6.1). On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, gains are +1.0 on MMBench (82.2→83.2), +1.6 on MMStar (64.1→65.7), and +1.5 on ScienceQA (89.0→90.5). On InternVL3-8B, it reaches 83.6 MMBench, 69.8 MMStar, and 99.2 ScienceQA, outperforming all post-training baselines on every benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepAlign outperforms all six post-training baselines (RLHF, HADPO, DataTailor, POVID, SIMA, VISTA) on every benchmark across all three backbones. The strongest competitor is VISTA (cross-modal mutual information maximization), which DeepAlign beats by +2.8 on MMBench and +4.2 on MMStar for LLaVA-v1.5-7B, and by +0.4/+0.2 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. SIMA (self-improvement alignment) is second-best on several benchmarks but trails DeepAlign by 1.5–3.9 points on MMBench and 1.0–4.2 on MMStar. RLHF and DataTailor show negligible or even negative gains over the base model, confirming that generic preference optimization or data curation alone cannot resolve modality conflict. The margin is largest on LLaVA-v1.5-7B (the weakest backbone), where DeepAlign's representation intervention and DINOv2 distillation yield the most substantial improvements, while on stronger backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) the absolute gains are smaller but still consistently positive.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepAlign departs from the dominant 'bridging' paradigm in MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) that aligns modality-shared semantics via lightweight connection modules, arguing instead that modality-specific information is both misaligned and depleted. Conceptually, it builds on Grad-CAM-style gradient attribution to isolate modality-discriminative components in representation space, and on DINOv2's self-supervised structural features as a vision-only teacher signal—repurposing a vision foundation model as a distillation source for an MLLM's internal hidden states. Compared to prior post-training alignment methods (SIMA, VISTA, POVID) that optimize cross-modal mutual information or preference signals, DeepAlign explicitly decomposes the alignment problem into two sub-problems (representation misalignment vs. detail depletion) and addresses each with a dedicated mechanism (adapter-based modality shift + patch-level similarity distillation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that adding visual input actively degrades performance on visual-assisted linguistic tasks (MNER, MMT, MRE) and that dense captions substitute nearly losslessly for raw images on perceptive VQA reveals a more severe integration failure in current MLLMs than previously acknowledged. The practical impact is a lightweight (200M-param adapter) post-training recipe that can be applied to any existing MLLM backbone without retraining the vision encoder or LLM, making it broadly adoptable. However, gains on already-strong backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) are modest (~1–2 points), raising open questions about whether the DINOv2 structural signal and the gradient-based modality decomposition remain effective at larger scales or with more deeply integrated architectures. The emergent multimodal in-context learning on interleaved sequences is promising but underexplored, and whether the approach transfers to video or audio modalities remains unaddressed.
