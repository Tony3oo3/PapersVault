## Problem

Regional weather forecasting is constrained by static, imprecise boundaries: existing AI methods crop fixed neighboring regions from global forecasts, while NWP methods solve PDEs at prohibitive computational cost. This local perspective contradicts the Atmosphere-Ocean-Land-Biosphere Coupling Theory, which holds that any regional point is influenced by the entire Earth system (e.g., Siberian cold surges triggering East-Asian cold waves). The paper asks whether an adaptive, Earth-wide boundary can be learned end-to-end to improve regional forecast generalization.

## Input / Output

The model consumes ERA5 reanalysis gridded data: upper-air variables across 13 pressure levels (H×W×13×N) and surface variables (H×W×M), at 1.4° for global and 0.25° for regional (Eastern Asia) resolution. For regional forecasting, both low-resolution global and high-resolution regional inputs are fed simultaneously into the SAA module, which fuses them via cross-attention. For ensemble forecasting, Perlin noise is injected into the initial global state and the simulation is run n times; typhoon tracks are derived from the predicted mean sea-level pressure field.

## Method

STCast is a transformer-based framework (Encoder–Processor–Decoder with alternating window and global self-attention) augmented by two key modules. The Spatial-Aligned Attention (SAA) module uses global features as Query/Key and regional features as Value in a linear cross-attention layer, initializing a learnable global-regional distribution via Great Circle distance and an exponential decay function; this prior modulates attention weights element-wise and is refined during training, effectively replacing static neighbor cropping with a dynamic, Earth-wide boundary. The Temporal Mixture-of-Experts (TMoE) module routes inputs to month-specialized experts using a rotating discrete Gaussian distribution whose peak aligns with the input month; a 12-dimensional month embedding is concatenated with the gating network output before Top-K expert selection, enabling the model to capture inter-month variability while preventing expert homogenization. Together, SAA and TMoE allow a single architecture to handle four tasks—global forecasting, regional forecasting, typhoon track prediction, and ensemble forecasting—under supervised training on 40 years of ERA5 data.

## Results

On 1.4° global weather forecasting (ERA5), STCast achieves the best normalized RMSE at 1-day (0.1197), 4-day (0.2578), 7-day (0.4348), and 10-day (0.5763), and the best ACC at 6-hour (0.9956), 1-day (0.9740), 4-day (0.8927), 7-day (0.7019), and 10-day (0.4715). For 0.25° regional forecasting, adding the SAA module reduces RMSE by 0.05 and increases ACC by 0.1 over direct training, with near-zero errors of 0.7% (U10) and 0.1% (MSL). On Typhoon Yinxing track prediction, STCast achieves a 5-day mean distance error of 96.5 km versus 160 km for the next-best Pangu-Weather. In ensemble forecasting, STCast attains the lowest 10-day RMSE of 0.5107 (deterministic) and 0.5113 (ensemble), outperforming all four competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STCast outperforms all four global-forecasting baselines (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, FuXi, OneForecast) on RMSE and ACC at every lead time from 1-day to 10-day, with the largest gap at 10-day (RMSE 0.5763 vs. 0.5918 for OneForecast, 0.6009 for GraphCast, 0.6215 for Pangu-Weather, and 0.6981 for FuXi). The sole exception is 6-hour RMSE, where OneForecast (0.0549) edges out STCast (0.0617), though STCast still leads on 6-hour ACC (0.9956 vs. 0.9943). For regional 0.25° forecasting, STCast with SAA substantially surpasses both OneForecast and direct training, reducing 10-day RMSE from 0.7610 (w/o SAA) to 0.4921. On typhoon track prediction, STCast achieves a 5-day mean distance error of 96.5 km for Typhoon Yinxing, well below Pangu-Weather (160 km), FourCastNet, FengWu, and ECMWF. In ensemble forecasting (Table 3), STCast's deterministic and ensemble variants both achieve the lowest 7–10-day RMSE (0.3892–0.5113), beating Pangu, GraphCast, FuXi, and OneForecast by 0.03–0.11 RMSE at 10-day.

## Relation to Prior Work

STCast extends the transformer-based weather forecasting lineage (Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, FuXi) by introducing two targeted modules: Spatial-Aligned Attention (SAA) for adaptive global-regional coupling and Temporal Mixture-of-Experts (TMoE) for month-specific expert routing. It departs from prior global-regional coupling strategies—both NWP sponge-layer methods and AI approaches like OneForecast that statically concatenate neighboring regions—by learning a dynamic, Earth-wide boundary distribution initialized with Great Circle distance decay. The TMoE component builds on the sparsely-gated MoE framework (Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017) and recent weather-specific MoE variants (VAMoE, EWMoE), but distinguishes itself by explicitly encoding temporal (monthly) structure into the routing mechanism via a rotating discrete Gaussian prior rather than relying on implicit learned gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in regional forecasting: static, neighbor-only boundaries that ignore long-range atmospheric teleconnections (e.g., Siberian cold surges affecting East Asia). The SAA module's adaptive, learnable boundary is a physically motivated solution that could generalize to other regions and coupling scenarios. However, the ensemble forecasting results (Table 3) show virtually no improvement from ensembling over deterministic predictions for STCast, raising questions about whether the model's probabilistic calibration is adequate. Open questions include whether the adaptive boundary generalizes beyond the single Eastern Asia test region, whether 12 monthly experts scale to sub-monthly or multi-year variability, and whether the approach transfers to other geophysical domains with similar global-regional coupling needs.
