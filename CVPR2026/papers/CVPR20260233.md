## Problem

Convexity is a powerful geometric prior for segmentation, but existing formulations are either discrete combinatorial constraints that cannot be differentiated through neural networks, or continuous level-set conditions that depend on a specific threshold of the segmentation function. This makes it difficult to impose convexity as a differentiable, threshold-free loss within modern end-to-end trainable segmentation architectures.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D images (retinal fundus photos cropped to 480×480 around the optic disc, cardiac MRI short-axis slices, or iris images) resized to 224×224 and produces a continuous probability mask u: Ω→[0,1] whose super-level sets are all convex. Rather than outputting a single binary segmentation at one threshold, the convexity constraint is enforced on the entire mask function simultaneously, and a post-hoc Convex Gradient Projection Module (CGPM) refines the raw network output into a convexified mask via a few unrolled gradient steps in logit space.

## Method

The key insight is to reformulate convexity of the segmented object as quasi-concavity of the continuous mask function u, so that all super-level sets are convex regardless of threshold. From this principle, the authors derive three characterizations: a zero-order midpoint-convexification algorithm, a first-order gradient-based condition equivalent to supporting hyperplanes, and a second-order sufficient condition requiring the Hessian to be negative definite on the tangent space of level sets, which in 2D reduces to a single quadratic form Q₂(x) = u_x²u_yy − 2u_xu_yu_xy + u_y²u_xx < 0. The second-order loss is computed densely via fixed convolution kernels for finite-difference derivatives and integrated into the network through CGPM, an unrolled proximal optimization that minimizes ½‖v−u‖² + λ·L_convex(v) in logit space. The framework unifies prior discrete 1-0-1 line constraints, graph-cut convexity, and curvature/SDF-based level-set priors as special cases within a single differentiable formulation.

## Results

On RIM-ONE-r3, the proposed method achieves 97.15% Dice and 94.46% IoU for optic disc segmentation, and 88.01% Dice and 78.59% IoU for optic cup segmentation, outperforming the strongest baseline (Swin-Unet) by +0.38 and +1.44 Dice points respectively. On the REFUGE dataset, the method reaches 88.61% Dice and 79.54% IoU with a Hausdorff distance of 5.86, substantially surpassing the next-best method (Active Boundary: 84.82% Dice, HD 10.59). Cross-dataset transfer from REFUGE to RIM-ONE-r3 yields 83.09% Dice and 71.08% IoU, again leading all baselines. On ACDC cardiac MRI, the method achieves 95.46% Dice and 4.70 HD, marginally improving over Swin-Unet (95.42% Dice, 4.97 HD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 10 retinal segmentation baselines on RIM-ONE-r3 for both optic disc and cup, with the largest gains over Swin-Unet (+0.38 Dice for disc, +1.44 Dice for cup) and ODCS-NSNP (+0.52 Dice for disc, +2.60 Dice for cup). On the broader shape-aware comparison (Table 1), the method dominates on REFUGE (88.61% Dice vs. 84.82% for Active Boundary, a +3.79 point margin) and RIM-ONE-r3 cross-dataset transfer (83.09% Dice vs. 81.00% for Swin-Unet). On ACDC, the improvement over Swin-Unet is marginal (+0.04 Dice) but the Hausdorff distance improves from 4.97 to 4.70. On CASIA iris/pupil segmentation, the method slightly trails ConvMCD on Dice (94.71 vs. 95.03) and IoU (89.94 vs. 90.54) but achieves a lower Hausdorff distance (2.288 vs. 2.323), suggesting the convex prior is most beneficial for structures where boundary regularity matters most. The ablation confirms the second-order condition is the strongest contributor, yielding +2.38 Dice on Swin-Unet and +6.57 Dice on DeepLabV3+ over the no-prior baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new theoretical ground by reframing convexity-constrained segmentation through the lens of quasi-concavity of the continuous mask function, rather than constraining a single binary threshold. It explicitly recovers discrete 1-0-1 line constraints (Gorelick, Royer), curvature-based level-set priors (Ukwatta, Yang), and signed-distance Laplacian conditions (Yan, Luo) as special cases of its zero-, first-, and second-order characterizations, providing a single continuous and differentiable umbrella. Methodologically, it departs from prior deep-learning shape priors (Liu's convex shape block, Han's pupil loss) by operating on the full mask function without thresholding and by integrating the constraint through an unrolled gradient-projection module (CGPM) rather than a bespoke architectural block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The threshold-free formulation removes a practical pain point in prior convexity methods that required committing to a specific level-set threshold, and the O(|Ω|) second-order penalty makes the prior scalable to dense, per-pixel application in modern networks. The unification clarifies why certain discrete or PDE-based convexity checks succeed or fail, which should guide future shape-prior design. Open questions include whether the quasi-concavity assumption generalizes beyond approximately convex anatomical structures (the tested scope), how the prior behaves under heavy occlusion or multi-instance overlap, and whether the sufficient-but-not-necessary second-order condition can be tightened without losing differentiability.
