## Problem

Reinforcement learning for humanoid whole-body control suffers from severe sample inefficiency due to complex dynamics, partial observability, and composite reward structures. Existing state representation learning (SRL) methods either rely on reconstruction objectives that preserve irrelevant details or use single-modality contrastive learning that ignores privileged simulator information, limiting their ability to extract task-relevant features. The paper asks whether the intrinsic complementarity between proprioceptive and privileged states can be exploited directly as a contrastive signal to accelerate and stabilize policy learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263581/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At deployment, the policy consumes only the proprioceptive state (joint positions, joint velocities, base angular velocity, and gravity estimate in the base frame) together with a velocity command or motion reference, and outputs angular position offsets for k actuated joints that are added to nominal targets and tracked by a fixed-gain PD controller. During training, the privileged state (root pose/velocity, per-link poses/velocities, contact indicators, terrain features) is available to the critic and to the SRL module but is never accessed by the deployed policy. The key structural constraint is that the proprioceptive state is a strict subset of the privileged state (o ⊂ s), which is what enables the zero-masking trick to form natural contrastive pairs.

## Method

PvP constructs a contrastive pair by taking the full privileged state s and a zero-masked variant s̃ in which all privileged-only components (e.g., root linear velocity, contact flags) are set to zero while proprioceptive observations are retained, so s̃ ≈ o. The policy encoder f_θ and a predictor h_ψ are trained with a SimSiam-style negative cosine similarity loss with stop-gradient, pulling the embedding of s toward the predictor output of s̃ and vice versa, without any hand-crafted data augmentations. This SRL objective is combined with PPO (the backbone RL algorithm) via a weighted total loss L_RL + λ·L_SRL, and an interval-update mechanism gates the SRL loss every T steps to prevent premature convergence on low-quality early rollouts. The entire pipeline is implemented in SRL4Humanoid, a modular framework that fully decouples the SRL and RL training loops and supports applying the SRL loss to either the policy encoder or the value encoder, with the paper showing the policy encoder yields more stable and faster convergence.

## Results

The paper evaluates PvP against three SRL baselines (SimSiam, SPR, VAE) and vanilla PPO on two LimX Oli 31-DoF tasks: velocity tracking and motion imitation. Results are reported as training-curve comparisons (mean ± std) rather than single final scores. On velocity tracking, PvP significantly accelerates convergence of overall reward and action smoothness, while SimSiam, SPR, and VAE yield only marginal improvements over vanilla PPO. On motion imitation, PvP achieves the highest final performance across all three tracking KPIs (position alignment metrics), with VAE actually degrading below vanilla PPO, indicating that naive reconstruction is insufficient for this task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PvP outperforms all four baselines on both tasks. Against vanilla PPO, PvP shows the largest gains: on velocity tracking it 'significantly accelerates the learning process' in overall reward and action smoothness convergence, while on motion imitation it achieves the highest values across all three tracking KPIs. Against SimSiam (the closest contrastive-learning baseline), PvP wins on both tasks by incorporating privileged-state information as a natural pseudo-augmentation, whereas SimSiam relies solely on the proprioceptive modality. Against SPR (self-predictive representations), PvP again leads on both tasks, with the gap more pronounced on motion imitation. Against VAE (reconstruction-based), PvP clearly wins; VAE actually degrades below vanilla PPO on motion imitation, demonstrating that simply reconstructing sensory data is insufficient. The paper also shows that applying SRL to the value encoder (rather than the policy encoder) causes training collapse on velocity tracking, confirming that the policy encoder is the appropriate target for the contrastive objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SRL-for-RL literature (contrastive methods like SimSiam/CURL, reconstruction-based VAE, and dynamics-modeling SPR) and prior attempts to apply SRL to humanoid WBC (e.g., PIM's perceptive internal model, elevation-based internal maps, world-model reconstruction). It departs from these by exploiting the intrinsic complementarity between proprioceptive and privileged states as a natural contrastive pair via zero-masking, eliminating the need for hand-crafted data augmentations that prior contrastive methods (CURL, ATC) require. The SRL4Humanoid framework further distinguishes itself as the first unified, modular benchmark specifically for SRL in humanoid robot learning, decoupling SRL and RL training in a plug-and-play manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PvP offers a simple, general recipe for data-efficient humanoid WBC that avoids task-specific augmentation design, and SRL4Humanoid provides the community with a reproducible baseline suite for systematic SRL comparison in this domain. Practical findings on interval updates, data proportions, and encoder choice (policy vs. value) give actionable guidance for integrating SRL with massively parallel RL. Limitations include evaluation on a single robot platform (LimX Oli) with only two task categories, and the absence of perception-based (RGB/depth) inputs, which the authors identify as a key direction for extending the framework to more complex, visually grounded environments.
