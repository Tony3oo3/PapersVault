## Problem

Extending the autoregressive next-token prediction paradigm from language to vision requires causal 1D visual tokens, but existing approaches fail: 2D tokenizers flattened into sequences lack true causality, and diffusion autoencoders either condition on all tokens (eliminating causal structure) or use nested dropout that disproportionately favors early tokens, introducing imbalance that degrades AR generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260224/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 256×256 RGB image concatenated with K learnable registers and produces 16-dimensional continuous (non-quantized) 1D tokens, which are normalized before conditioning the decoder. The decoder operates in the latent space of a frozen KL-16 MAR-VAE and supports both one-step sampling (z₀ = ε − u_θ(ε, 0, 1, V_K)) and multi-step Euler integration. For AR generation, a learned class token serves as the sole prior, and the AR model predicts the full token sequence before the MeanFlow decoder renders the image in a single step.

## Method

CATOK pairs a causal ViT-B/8 encoder (with registers and a causal attention mask where 1D tokens attend to all image features but only preceding tokens) with a MeanFlow DiT decoder. The key mechanism is selecting 1D tokens within a sampled time interval [r·K, t·K] and binding them to the MeanFlow objective, so each token segment models the average velocity field along the corresponding subpath of the noise-to-image flow, inherently maintaining both causality and balance. Training jointly optimizes four losses: the MeanFlow objective (25% of samples with r < t), the Rectified Flow objective (75% with r = t for stability), REPA aligning decoder hidden states with DINOv2 features, and the proposed REPA-A aligning encoder image features with DINOv2 to produce more discriminative representations. An adaptive L₂ loss (with c=10⁻³, w=1.0) is used for both flow objectives, and the entire system is trained from scratch on ImageNet-1K for 80–160 epochs depending on model size.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 256×256 reconstruction, CATOK-L-256 achieves state-of-the-art PSNR of 22.53 and SSIM of 0.674, along with a competitive rFID of 0.75, using only 160 training epochs (vs. 400 for Semanticist-L-256). The smaller CATOK-B-256 variant matches OpenMagViT-V2's rFID (1.17) in just 80 epochs with 224M parameters. For class-conditional generation, CATOK-L-128 attains a gFID of 2.95 and IS of 269.2, comparable to leading 1D autoregressive approaches like Semanticist-L-256 (gFID 2.57). CATOK-L-256† also achieves the best PSNR (20.99) and SSIM (0.630) among one-step 1D tokenizers, demonstrating its dual capability for fast one-step and high-quality multi-step sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CATOK-L-256 outperforms the strongest diffusion autoencoder baseline Semanticist-L-256 on PSNR (22.53 vs. 21.61) and SSIM (0.674 vs. 0.626) while achieving a slightly better rFID (0.75 vs. 0.78), all with fewer than half the training epochs (160 vs. 400). Against FlowMo-Lo-256 (945M params, 130 epochs), CATOK-L-256 wins on all three metrics (rFID 0.75 vs. 0.95, PSNR 22.53 vs. 22.07, SSIM 0.674 vs. 0.649) with fewer parameters (552M). The smaller CATOK-B-256 (224M, 80 epochs) matches OpenMagViT-V2's rFID (1.17) and surpasses it on PSNR (22.10 vs. 21.63) and SSIM (0.666 vs. 0.640). For generation, CATOK-L-128 (gFID 2.95) is competitive with Semanticist-L-256 (gFID 2.57) and SpectralAR-64 (gFID 3.02), though it trails the masked-prediction TiTok-S-128 (gFID 1.97) which uses a different generation paradigm. Among one-step 1D tokenizers, CATOK-L-256† achieves the best PSNR (20.99) and SSIM (0.630), substantially outperforming One-D-Piece-B-256 (PSNR 18.77) and TiTok variants (PSNR ≤17.52), confirming that the MeanFlow decoder enables high-quality single-step reconstruction without sacrificing multi-step fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CATOK sits at the intersection of diffusion autoencoders (Preechakul et al., 2022), MeanFlow one-step generative modeling (Geng et al., 2025), and the growing line of 1D causal visual tokenizers (FlexTok, Semanticist, SelfTok, DDT-Llama). Its key departure is from prior causal tokenizers that enforce ordering via nested dropout—conditioning on the first k tokens—which the authors show introduces a systematic imbalance favoring early tokens. Instead, CATOK repurposes MeanFlow's time-interval conditioning: by selecting tokens within [r·K, t·K] and binding them to the corresponding flow subpath, causality emerges naturally from the generative process without any dropout heuristic. The REPA-A regularization extends REPA (Yu et al., 2024) from decoder-side to encoder-side alignment, a modification specific to conditional diffusion autoencoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CATOK demonstrates that a single MeanFlow objective can simultaneously provide causal structure for AR modeling and one-step sampling capability, eliminating the need for separate mechanisms. The SOTA reconstruction (0.75 rFID, 22.53 PSNR) achieved in 160 epochs (vs. 300–640 for competitors) suggests the MeanFlow + REPA-A recipe is a more efficient training paradigm for diffusion autoencoders. However, the evaluation is confined to ImageNet-1K at 256×256, the AR generation results are 'comparable' rather than SOTA, and the one-step rFID (4.63) still lags behind multi-step baselines—open questions include whether the causal structure generalizes to higher resolutions, larger datasets, and text-conditioned generation.
