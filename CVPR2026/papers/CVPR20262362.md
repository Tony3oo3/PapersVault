## Problem

Existing multi-modal cancer survival prediction methods suffer from four key limitations: (1) the hierarchical structure of gigapixel WSIs is underutilized, (2) Mamba-based scanning is restricted to a single horizontal direction, limiting long-range dependency capture, (3) genomic profiles are grouped into broad categories with functional overlaps that obscure gene-gene relationships, and (4) rigid feature fusion between heterogeneous modalities introduces redundancy that degrades discriminative power.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262362/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSIs at 10× magnification, pre-split into 4096×4096 regions and 512×512 patches, along with genomic profiles (RNA-seq, CNV, SNV) compressed into six gene-group embeddings via a fully connected layer. A pre-trained ResNet-50 extracts 512-dim features from each region and patch. The model outputs a continuous survival risk score per patient, from which the survival function is estimated by cumulating the negated hazard function over discrete time points (months), evaluated via the concordance index under 5-fold cross-validation.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-directional composite scanning (MDCS) strategy that serializes WSI features in five directions (horizontal, vertical, left-oblique, right-oblique, loopback) at both region and patch levels, and genomic features via forward and interval scanning with a defined stride Δ, thereby enlarging the receptive field beyond single-direction Mamba. Each scanning sequence is processed by a Mamba2 encoder with linear complexity, and the resulting multi-directional features are aggregated, passed through a PPEG spatial-correlation module, and distilled via attention pooling into a single intra-modal representation. For inter-modal interaction, a Mixture of Attention and Mamba Experts (MoAME) module uses a gating network to sparsely select one of three experts—Cross-Attention, Cross-Mamba, or Stacked Cross-Mamba—per input pair, enabling flexible and less redundant fusion in a progressive two-stage scheme (image→genome, then genome→fused). Finally, L1-based alignment losses penalize redundancy between inter-modal and intra-modal representations (L_cro) and amplify the difference between region-level and patch-level features (L_intra), with the total objective combining negative log-likelihood survival loss plus weighted alignment terms.

## Results

MDCS-MoAME achieves the best average c-index of 0.7383±0.0377 across five TCGA cancer datasets (BLCA, BRCA, GBMLGG, LUAD, UCEC), outperforming all 16 baselines. The strongest individual results are 0.8633 on GBMLGG, 0.7263 on UCEC, and 0.7079 on LUAD. Compared to the closest multi-modal competitor PIBD (0.7149 average), MDCS-MoAME gains 2.34 percentage points, and over the best Mamba-based multi-modal method SurvMamba (0.6985), it gains 3.98 percentage points. The method also uses fewer parameters (8.93M) and lower FLOPs (5.67G) than competing MoE-based methods PAMoE (11.17M, 11.04G) and MoME (12.25M, 6.95G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDCS-MoAME wins on all five datasets against all 16 baselines. Against gene-modal methods (MLP, SNN, SNNTrans), it improves average c-index by 7.81%, 11.61%, and 6.48% respectively, confirming the value of multi-modal fusion. Against pathology-modal methods (ABMIL, CLAM_MB, TransMIL, MambaMIL, PAM, PAMoE), gains range from 11.12% to 16.88%, with the largest margin over PAMoE (+16.34%) and PAM (+14.61%). Among multi-modal competitors, the closest rival is PIBD (0.7149 avg), which MDCS-MoAME surpasses by 2.34 points; over SurvMamba (0.6985) the gain is 5.70%, and over MoME (0.6693) it is 10.31%. Notably, MDCS-MoAME also achieves the best per-dataset c-index on all five cancers, with the largest absolute gains on UCEC (+4.09 over PIBD) and BRCA (+2.23 over PIBD). In terms of efficiency, it uses 20% fewer parameters and 49% fewer FLOPs than PAMoE while delivering a 10.37-point c-index improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba-based WSI processing line (PAM, SurvMamba, MambaMIL) by replacing their single horizontal scanning with a five-directional composite scanning strategy (horizontal, vertical, left/right oblique, loopback) at both region and patch levels, and introduces an interval scanning scheme for sparse genomic profiles. The inter-modal fusion module (MoAME) is directly inspired by MoME's gating architecture but departs from it by mixing attention and Mamba experts rather than using homogeneous expert types, and by adding L1 alignment constraints to suppress the redundancy that MoME's over-fusion design introduces. Genomic feature extraction follows CMTA's six-group scheme, and the backbone relies on Mamba2 for linear-complexity sequence modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-directional scanning idea is broadly transferable to any WSI-based pipeline that currently relies on a single scan direction, and the MoAME framework offers a practical template for combining heterogeneous sequence models (attention vs. SSM) in a sparsely-gated manner. Limitations include reliance on a pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone, evaluation only on five TCGA cancer types, and a fixed interval length Δ=5 that was not sensitivity-analyzed. Open questions include whether the five scanning directions are all necessary (the ablation shows straight+oblique already captures most gains), whether the approach generalizes to non-TCGA cohorts or other modalities (e.g., proteomics), and whether the alignment constraints remain beneficial at larger scale.
