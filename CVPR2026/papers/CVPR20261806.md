## Problem

Current parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods are largely confined to single-task adaptation, and existing PEFT-MTL frameworks (e.g., MTLoRA, DiTASK, TADFormer) share structure or feature representations through separate adapter paths rather than truly reusing the same parameter set across tasks. This prevents full exploitation of common knowledge across tasks and introduces redundant computation and memory overhead that scales linearly with the number of tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (PASCAL-Context) or RGB-D images (NYUDv2) by prepending learnable task tokens to image patch tokens before feeding them into a Swin Transformer Tiny encoder. A single shared encoder-decoder pipeline simultaneously produces all dense prediction outputs—semantic segmentation masks, human part masks, saliency maps, surface normal maps, depth maps, and edge maps—without maintaining independent per-task decoder parameter sets. The key structural detail is that task-specificity is injected solely through frequency modulation of shared weights, not through separate network branches.

## Method

Free Sinewich achieves true parameter reuse via a frequency-switching mechanism built on a Sine-AWB layer: low-rank factors A and B are fused with an intermediate convolutional kernel W into a single base matrix M_AWB, which is then element-wise modulated by sin(ω_t · M_AWB) to yield task-specific weights M_t. A Lightweight Clock Net (LCN), a single-layer MLP with tanh bounding, maps each task token to a scalar frequency ω_t that controls the sine transformation. The 'fuse-then-sine' ordering is critical because the sine function is not multiplicative-homomorphic, so applying it separately to A, W, and B would not preserve the rank-enrichment property. A 7×7 Gaussian low-pass filter smooths high-frequency artifacts in the sine-transformed kernel. In the encoder, task-agnostic blocks use standard LoRA while the final block employs the TS-Module with frequency switching; in the decoder, per-task convolution weights are replaced by the same shared M_AWB modulated at task-specific frequencies, eliminating the linear parameter growth with task count.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (4 dense prediction tasks), Free Sinewich (r=64) achieves mIoU of 71.25 (SemSeg), 61.38 (Human Parts), 66.24 (Saliency), and RMSE of 16.14° (Normals), yielding a +5.39% average relative improvement (Δm) over single-task fine-tuning with only 6.53M trainable parameters. On NYUDv2, Free Sinewich (r=64) reaches a near-zero Δm of −0.52% with 6.55M parameters, effectively matching full fine-tuning performance. Even at r=32, Free Sinewich (+4.51% Δm, 4.04M params) surpasses TADFormer at r=64 (+4.24% Δm, 7.38M params), demonstrating that the frequency-switching mechanism captures task-specific variation more efficiently than simply increasing rank.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Free Sinewich outperforms all PEFT-MTL baselines on PASCAL-Context in both Δm and parameter efficiency. Against the closest competitor TADFormer (r=64), Free Sinewich (r=64) improves Δm from +4.24% to +5.39% while using fewer parameters (6.53M vs. 7.38M); notably, Free Sinewich at r=32 (+4.51%, 4.04M) already exceeds TADFormer at r=64. Compared to MTLoRA (r=64, +2.55%, 8.34M) and DiTASK-MTL (+1.47%, 3.55M), Free Sinewich achieves substantially larger gains with comparable or fewer parameters. Single-task PEFT methods (LoRA, VL-Adapter, BitFit, Compactor, VPT) all suffer negative transfer (Δm < 0), confirming that naive parameter-efficient fine-tuning does not transfer to multi-task settings. On NYUDv2, Free Sinewich (r=64) achieves Δm of −0.52%, far outperforming TADFormer (−2.68%), MTLoRA (−3.45%), and DiTASK (−14.27%), while using fewer parameters than all of them. The shared-decoder design further reduces decoder parameters from 1.94M (TADFormer/HRNet) to 1.07M (Free Sinewich/HRNet) at r=32, a 45% reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three prior threads—LoRA's low-rank factorization, Sine-LoRA's element-wise sine transformation for rank enrichment, and ConvLoRA/TADFormer's convolution-in-the-middle design—into a unified Sine-AWB module. The key departure from prior PEFT-MTL methods (MTLoRA, DiTASK, TADFormer) is conceptual: rather than routing task-specific information through separate adapter branches (pseudo-sharing), Free Sinewich applies a task-dependent sinusoidal frequency to a single shared base matrix, achieving genuine parameter reuse. The biological motivation from thalamocortical oscillatory multiplexing provides a framing that prior PEFT-MTL work has not explored, positioning frequency as the axis of task specialization rather than separate weight sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-switching mechanism offers a principled way to mitigate task conflict in MTL without inflating parameter counts, and the shared-decoder formulation (Sec. 3.5) further reduces redundancy that plagues per-task decoder groups. However, the method is validated only on Swin-Tiny backbones for dense prediction tasks on two benchmarks, and the LCN's contribution is explicitly described as minor (primarily stabilizing training), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger architectures, non-dense tasks, or settings where spatially varying frequencies might be needed. The Gaussian low-pass filter, while effective, introduces an additional design choice whose optimal parameters may be task-dependent.
