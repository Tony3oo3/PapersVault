## Problem

MLLMs generate pixel coordinates in natural language for grounding, but conventional parameter and data scaling yields disproportionately weak gains on grounding compared to standard QA. The paper identifies the root cause as a Semantics-Dominated Scaling Gap: across model scales, the visual perception stage already captures sufficient regional information, while the semantic refinement stage (where representations are mapped to coordinate text) remains the true bottleneck and is under-scaled by uniform enlargement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a free-form referring expression as input and outputs a bounding box in the model's native coordinate-text format. At inference, the multimodal MCTS runs with a fixed tree depth of 4 and 16 rollouts per node; only vision-dominated leaf nodes produce valid bounding boxes, which are then merged via NMS weighted by CLIP-based regional feature alignment scores between each candidate region and both the original and the refined textual description.

## Method

R-Ground builds a self-evolved reasoning tree via a multimodal Monte Carlo Tree Search that explicitly allocates more computation to the semantic refinement stage. The action set is asymmetric: two vision-dominated actions (global and local grounding under the grounding prompt) are paired with three semantic-dominated actions (target existence judgment with and without positional cues, and description reconstruction under the REG prompt), so that each reasoning path contains more SRS steps than VPS steps. Node selection uses a modified UCT (UCT*) that adds a CLIP-based multimodal feature alignment score σ(S); when the cross-modal alignment turns negative, the branch is truncated, reducing wasted computation. Final solution selection replaces majority voting with a regional feature weighted voting scheme that scores each candidate bounding box by its CLIP alignment to both the original and the refined description, then feeds the weighted scores into NMS to produce the final box.

## Results

R-Ground achieves an average AP@0.5 of 93.16 on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg using Qwen2.5-VL-7B, surpassing the 72B Qwen2.5-VL (90.25) by 2.91 points and the 241B InternVL3.5 (92.39) by 0.77 points. With Qwen3-VL-8B, R-Ground further improves to 94.47 average AP@0.5, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. The largest gains appear on RefCOCO+ (the most challenging split without spatial cues), where R-Ground reaches 91.67/94.14/89.97 (Val/TestA/TestB) versus 84.2/89.1/76.9 for the unmodified 7B base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R-Ground (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) outperforms all specialist methods (best: ONE-PEACE at 89.85 avg) by 3.31 points, all MLLM-based methods including the 241B InternVL3.5 (92.39) by 0.77 points, and all reasoning-based baselines (best: UniVG-R1 at 88.20) by 4.96 points. Most strikingly, the 7B R-Ground model surpasses the 10× larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (90.25) by 2.91 points, demonstrating that targeted reasoning scaling is more effective than parameter scaling for grounding. On the hardest split (RefCOCO+ TestB, which removes spatial cues), R-Ground achieves 89.97 versus 86.9 for InternVL3.5-241B and 83.7 for Qwen2.5-VL-72B. The Qwen3-VL-8B variant further extends the lead to 94.47 average, confirming transferability across model families. R-Ground also shows the largest absolute improvement over its own base model on RefCOCO+ TestB (+13.07 points for 7B), indicating the framework is most beneficial where semantic refinement is most challenged.

## Relation to Prior Work

R-Ground extends the unimodal MCTS-based self-evolved reasoning paradigm (rstar-math) to the multimodal grounding setting, replacing unimodal consistency-based scoring with CLIP-based cross-modal alignment. It departs from two dominant grounding paradigms: regression-decoder methods (LLaVA-Grounding, GLaMM, Groma) that break end-to-end generative design, and pure parameter/data scaling of MLLMs (Qwen-VL, InternVL, Ferret) that the authors show fails to close the semantic refinement gap. Unlike fixed-template CoT reasoning frameworks (Rex-Thinker, Argus, UniVG-R1), R-Ground uses a self-evolved search tree with heterogeneous vision- and semantic-dominated actions to dynamically allocate computation toward the semantic refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSR/SDSG diagnostic reveals that MLLMs of vastly different scales encode nearly identical regional information in the visual perception stage, yet diverge sharply in the semantic refinement stage—reframing the scaling problem from 'bigger model' to 'targeted reasoning at the right stage.' The practical implication is significant: a 7B model with R-Ground (93.16 avg AP@50) surpasses a 72B model without it (90.25), suggesting that test-time reasoning scaling can substitute for parameter scaling in perception-heavy tasks. Open questions include whether the MCTS overhead (depth 4, 16 rollouts, CLIP scoring) is acceptable for real-time deployment, whether the approach generalizes beyond referring expression grounding to segmentation or detection, and whether the CLIP-based alignment score is the optimal cross-modal consistency measure or whether task-specific scorers would further improve results.
