## Problem

Existing RL-based post-training methods for video understanding VLMs (e.g., Video-R1, GRPO-CARE) rely on external, human-defined constraints—such as enforcing temporal logic or aligning with prescribed reasoning steps—to guide the model's chain-of-thought. The authors argue this forced alignment to human cognitive patterns can constrain the model's potential and even degrade performance, and that the model's own intrinsic confidence in its reasoning is an underexploited, more authentic signal for optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263147/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip sampled at 16, 32, or 64 frames together with a text question as input. For each input, the policy model samples G candidate responses, each structured as a chain-of-thought reasoning path enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer in answer tags. The reward is not computed from the generated text directly but from the answer-token probabilities extracted by a frozen reference model, converting a sparse binary correctness signal into a continuous, fine-grained scalar per candidate.

## Method

Video-RAISE replaces the binary 0/1 reward of standard GRPO with a continuous reward derived from the model's intrinsic answer-token confidence, computed by a reference model. Two complementary reward strategies handle different problem types: SCRE (Sequential Confidence Rigorous Evaluation) targets strict-logic, exact-match tasks by computing a position-weighted product of answer token probabilities (with exponential decay favoring earlier tokens) and multiplying by a variance penalty to discourage erratic confidence; IGSR (Intragroup Score Re-ranking) targets open-ended tasks by first clustering candidates into semantically similar groups via ROUGE-L scores, then re-ranking within each group by average negative log-probability, with a cross-group reward gap constraint and a group-size-dependent penalty to maintain stable inter-group separation. The overall training loop follows the GRPO framework—group sampling, reward computation, advantage estimation, and policy update—but the reward signal is entirely self-generated rather than handcrafted, eliminating the need for task-specific rule engineering.

## Results

Video-RAISE-7B achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across all six video understanding benchmarks. At the 32-frame setting, it reaches 53.0% on VideoMMMU (vs. 50.4% for CARE-7B), 75.1% on TempCompass, 65.9% on MVBench, and 60.7% on VideoMME. The advantage is most pronounced at the more challenging 16-frame setting, where Video-RAISE outperforms Video-R1-7B by +3.0 points on VideoMMMU (52.8 vs. 49.8) and +2.1 points on TempCompass (74.7 vs. 72.6). At 64 frames, it further extends the lead to 53.6% on VideoMMMU and 62.6% on VideoMME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest RL-based competitor Video-R1-7B, Video-RAISE wins on every benchmark at all three frame settings (16/32/64), with the largest margins on VideoMMMU (+3.0 at 16 frames, +0.7 at 32, +1.2 at 64) and MMVU (+2.2 at 16, +2.1 at 32, +3.1 at 64); the only tie is VideoMME at 16 frames (57.4 vs. 57.4). Against CARE-7B (32 frames), Video-RAISE leads on all six benchmarks, most notably VideoMMMU (+2.6) and TempCompass (+1.6). At 64 frames, Video-RAISE also surpasses Video-RTS-7B on VideoMMMU (+0.9) and MMVU (+0.6). Compared to SFT/agent-based models, the gains are substantially larger: e.g., +19.0 points over Kangaroo-8B on VideoMMMU (16-frame) and +15.0 points over Video-UTR-7B on TempCompass. However, Video-RAISE (7B) still trails the much larger proprietary models GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro on VideoMMMU (53.0 vs. 61.2/53.4) and VideoMME (60.7 vs. 71.9/75.0), though it matches or exceeds them on VSI-Bench (36.6 vs. 34.0 for GPT-4o at 32 frames).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework introduced by DeepSeek-R1 for LLM post-training, which has been rapidly extended to multimodal video understanding by methods like Video-R1 (temporal logic rewards) and GRPO-CARE (consistency-aware bonuses). The key departure is rejecting the prevailing paradigm of handcrafted, human-defined reward constraints in favor of the model's own intrinsic confidence (answer-token probabilities) as the optimization signal. The two proposed reward strategies—SCRE for exact-match tasks and IGSR for open-ended tasks—represent a new-loss contribution that replaces sparse 0/1 correctness rewards with continuous, fine-grained signals derived from the reference model's token-level probability distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in the video RL post-training community—that aligning model reasoning with human cognitive patterns via external constraints is the optimal path—arguing instead that such alignment can constrain the model and degrade performance. The 90% reasoning path consistency (nearly double that of GRPO baselines) and the finding that token-level confidence exhibits a polarization pattern (correct answers sharply at 1.0, incorrect answers split between guessing at 0.0 and confidently-wrong at 1.0) provide a useful diagnostic for reward design. However, the performance gains over strong RL baselines (Video-R1, CARE) are modest (1–3 points), and the method is validated only on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, leaving open whether the intrinsic-confidence signal generalizes across architectures, scales, or modalities beyond video.
