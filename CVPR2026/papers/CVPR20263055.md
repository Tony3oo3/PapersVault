## Problem

Existing federated unlearning methods fail under Non-IID data because conflicting client updates drive the global model into sharp loss basins, where even small parameter perturbations during unlearning cause catastrophic forgetting of retained knowledge. Additionally, misaligned task vectors generated under heterogeneous distributions cannot reliably isolate target client contributions. No prior method addresses the loss landscape geometry as a root cause of unlearning instability in federated settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263055/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained global FL model (e.g., ResNet-18/34 or CNN), a forgetting dataset held by the requesting client, and three hyperparameters: perturbation radius ρ (controls exploration distance into flat regions), number of perturbed models K (controls directional consensus granularity), and scaling factor λ (controls negation magnitude). It outputs a single unlearned global model in the same parameter space, requiring no access to raw client data—clients only upload task vectors computed from local fine-tuning on the forgetting set.

## Method

GDFA operates in three stages. First, K flattened models are generated by applying layer-wise perturbations to the global model using random ±1 signature vectors scaled by gradient information and radius ρ, with a constraint ensuring their average equals the original model, thereby guiding it toward a flat loss basin. Second, each flattened model is fine-tuned on the forgetting dataset to produce a task vector; a sign-consensus merging rule then retains only parameter components whose direction agrees with the dominant sign across all K vectors, zeroing out conflicting entries to isolate shared knowledge attributes. Third, the merged aligned vector is subtracted from the original global model (reverse aggregation) to erase target knowledge while preserving retained performance. A PAC-Bayesian bound (Theorem 1) formally links flat-minima sharpness to the performance gap under data heterogeneity, providing theoretical grounding for the geometry-driven design.

## Results

On the Non-IID Dir(0.5) setting with 100 clients, GDFA achieves the highest retained accuracy (RA) on MNIST (95.89%), Fashion-MNIST (89.95%), and CIFAR-10 (69.33%), surpassing all baselines including the retraining gold standard. Forgetting accuracy (FA) reaches 1.53%, 1.55%, 1.57%, and 1.05% on MNIST, FMNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 respectively, approaching the retraining baseline (0.11–0.51%). JSD values are lowest for GDFA on MNIST (0.0012) and CIFAR-100 (0.0014), and MIA scores hover near the 50% random-guessing threshold across all four datasets, confirming strong privacy protection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDFA achieves the best retained accuracy (RA) on three of four datasets: MNIST (95.89% vs. 95.73% for Recovery), Fashion-MNIST (89.95% vs. 89.82% for Retrain), and CIFAR-10 (69.33% vs. 68.88% for Retrain), but trails Retrain on CIFAR-100 (46.08% vs. 47.82%). On forgetting accuracy (FA), Retrain remains the gold standard (0.11–0.51%), while GDFA (1.05–1.57%) is competitive with lightweight methods like SGA (1.70–3.41%) and SIFU (1.06–3.92%) but does not match full retraining. For JSD, GDFA achieves the lowest divergence on MNIST (0.0012 vs. 0.0018 for SGA) and CIFAR-100 (0.0014 vs. 0.0015 for MoDe), while Eraser and Recovery tie at 0.0005 on CIFAR-10. MIA scores for GDFA stay within 0.5% of the 50% random-guessing baseline across all datasets, outperforming Editor (49.76–57.49%) and SIFU (50.59–54.41%). In terms of efficiency, GDFA completes unlearning in 11.28 s (MNIST) to 67.88 s (CIFAR-100), roughly 2× faster than SGA and Recovery, and 30–50× faster than full retraining (223.96–1623.14 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

GDFA extends the task arithmetic framework (Ilharco et al., 2022) from centralized model merging to federated unlearning by introducing reverse task vector aggregation with a directional alignment filter. It also adapts the sharpness-aware minimization principle (Foret et al., 2020) to the federated setting, using controlled layer-wise perturbations to migrate the global model into flat loss regions before unlearning. This departs from prior federated unlearning methods (FedEraser, FedRecovery, MoDe, PGD, OSD) that rely on historical update storage, gradient correction, or degraded-model subtraction, by instead grounding the unlearning operation in loss landscape geometry and multi-model vector consensus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled explanation for why existing federated unlearning methods degrade under Non-IID data—sharp minima amplify the damage of parameter perturbations—and offers a geometry-aware remedy that avoids costly retraining while preserving retained knowledge. The PAC-Bayesian bound linking flatness to performance gaps under distribution shift gives theoretical grounding, though the bound's tightness in practice remains unverified. Open questions include scalability to transformer architectures and larger-scale deployments, handling of multiple concurrent unlearning requests, and whether the perturbation-based flat-region migration generalizes beyond the relatively small CNN models and image datasets evaluated here.
