## Problem

Monocular human motion recovery produces trajectories that appear jittery or overly smoothed despite low joint-position error, because existing pipelines lack reliable high-order temporal cues (velocity and acceleration) needed for realistic momentum timing and high-frequency detail. Implicit smoothness priors suppress legitimate rapid motion, while generative priors struggle to balance global consistency with frame-level 2D evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a monocular RGB video and an initial global motion estimate from any existing HMR pipeline (e.g., TRAM, GVHMR) as input, and outputs refined world-space SMPL/SMPL-X parameters (per-frame pose, root translation, root orientation with fixed shape). PVA-Net predicts camera-space 3D per-joint velocities and accelerations rather than world-space quantities, deliberately avoiding entanglement with global scale ambiguity and camera egomotion. A lightweight calibration step aligns the magnitude of induced 3D joint velocity with predicted velocity before optimization begins.

## Method

PVA-Net is a lightweight temporal transformer built on a frozen ViTPose-L backbone, adding eight trainable transformer blocks with rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) to capture high-order motion patterns such as reversals and rhythmic cycles; three small heads decode per-joint 2D keypoints, camera-space 3D velocity, and 3D acceleration, trained with a multi-task loss including a temporal gradient-matching term for keypoint stability. HTD-Refine then performs differentiable optimization over SMPL parameters, minimizing a weighted energy that enforces velocity consistency, acceleration consistency, 2D keypoint reprojection, jerk smoothness, and parameter regularization against the initial estimate. The key insight is that second-order differences (acceleration) attenuate slowly varying camera drift and are less sensitive to global scale than first-order velocity, yielding a cleaner supervisory signal for temporal refinement. A final velocity-thresholded post-processing step applies inverse kinematics to lock feet and hands during contact phases, reducing foot sliding at a small cost to overall accuracy.

## Results

On the EMDB-2 moving-camera benchmark, HTD-Refine reduces jitter by 58.1%–75.0% across all baselines (e.g., TRAM: 25.1→6.6 m/s³; Human3R: 529.6→132.5 m/s³) and cuts mean per-joint acceleration error (MPJAE) by up to 72.5% (Human3R: 143.3→39.4 m/s²). World-aligned MPJPE (WA-MPJPE) improves by 7.6%–41.7%, with GVHMR+HTD-Refine achieving the best overall score of 69.2 mm versus GVHMR's 118.7 mm. On the static-camera RICH test set, jitter drops by 72.3%–77.5% and WA-MPJPE by 2.8%–12.9%, while root translation error (RTE) remains stable or improves by up to 8.0%, confirming that enforcing high-order dynamics does not sacrifice global trajectory accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TRAM [39], HTD-Refine reduces jitter from 25.1 to 6.6 m/s³ (73.7%↓), foot sliding from 12.0 to 7.5 mm (37.5%↓), and WA-MPJPE from 78.8 to 71.7 mm, while keeping RTE unchanged at 1.5 m. Against GVHMR [29], the gains are even more pronounced: jitter drops from 17.2 to 7.2 m/s³, WA-MPJPE from 118.7 to 69.2 mm (41.7%↓), and RTE from 2.1 to 1.5 m (28.6%↓), though foot sliding marginally increases from 4.0 to 5.7 mm due to GVHMR's occasional near-stationary predictions on complex scenes. Against Human3R [7], HTD-Refine dramatically cuts jitter (529.6→132.5 m/s³, 75.0%↓) and MPJAE (143.3→39.4 m/s², 72.5%↓), though W-MPJPE slightly worsens (367.1→391.4 mm). In the refinement-method comparison (Table 4), HTD-Refine outperforms both the Gaussian trajectory filter (jitter 6.6 vs 25.1) and RoHM [50] (jitter 6.6 vs 28.4, FS 7.5 vs 9.9), with RoHM actually degrading jitter relative to the simple filter due to noisy per-clip corrections. On RICH (Table 5), GVHMR+HTD-Refine dominates NeMF [12] on every metric (jitter 3.6 vs 11.6, FS 3.3 vs 17.1, WA-MPJPE 75.2 vs 245.5 mm) and PACE [19] (WA-MPJPE 75.2 vs 197.2 mm, W-MPJPE 123.8 vs 380.0 mm, PA-MPJPE 38.0 vs 49.3 mm).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRAM's camera-space-to-world-space initialization pipeline and extends it with an explicit high-order temporal refinement stage. It departs from the dominant paradigm of implicit temporal regularization (smoothness priors in TRAM/GVHMR, autoregressive rollouts in WHAM) and generative motion priors (diffusion in RoHM, VAE in HuMoR) by treating per-joint velocity and acceleration as first-class, explicitly estimated supervision signals. The PVA-Net architecture repurposes a frozen ViTPose backbone with a lightweight temporal transformer (using RoPE) to jointly predict 2D keypoints, 3D velocities, and 3D accelerations, positioning itself as a general post-processing module rather than an end-to-end HMR model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a recognized gap between low joint-position error and perceptually natural motion in monocular HMR, and its plug-and-play design means it can enhance any existing pipeline (TRAM, GVHMR, Human3R) without retraining the base model. The introduction of MPJVE and MPJAE as evaluation metrics shifts the community's attention toward dynamic fidelity beyond MPJPE. Open questions include whether explicit velocity/acceleration estimation from monocular video is fundamentally limited by depth and scale ambiguity, how the approach behaves on very long sequences or with occlusions, and whether the foot-locking post-processing trade-off (slight accuracy loss for visual quality) can be resolved more elegantly.
