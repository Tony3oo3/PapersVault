## Problem

Existing lifelong learning methods assume homogeneous task sequences (e.g., only classification or only segmentation), overlooking real-world scenarios where tasks with fundamentally different output structures—discrete class labels, continuous depth values, surface normal vectors—arrive sequentially. This paper formalizes the broader setting as lifelong heterogeneous learning (LHL) and demonstrates that vanilla sequential training on heterogeneous dense prediction tasks suffers severe catastrophic forgetting regardless of task order.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (3 × H × W) as input and produces pixel-wise predictions whose output channel dimension C_t^out varies per task: 13 channels for semantic segmentation (discrete class labels), 1 channel for depth estimation (continuous values), 3 channels for surface normal estimation (continuous unit vectors), and analogous per-pixel outputs for edge, keypoint, and reshading tasks. All tasks share the same input domain but have heterogeneous output spaces, and the method is exemplar-free—no historical data or stored predictions from previous tasks are retained.

## Method

HAD employs a task-shared encoder (ResNet-18/50 with dilated convolutions) and task-specific ASPP decoders, trained via self-distillation where frozen teacher models from prior phases generate pseudo-labels on the current task's data. Two complementary distillation losses address distinct failure modes: (1) the DB-HAD loss partitions pixels into semantic groups (per-class for segmentation, foreground/background for regression) and computes the geometric mean of per-pixel losses within each group before averaging arithmetically across groups, thereby equalizing group contributions and suppressing pseudo-label noise; (2) the SG-HAD loss applies the Sobel operator to the pixel-wise teacher-student loss map to extract salient edge pixels, then restricts the distillation objective to those high-gradient regions where informative spatial transitions reside. The total training loss combines these per-task distillation terms (weighted by α) with the standard supervised loss for the new task.

## Results

On the NYUv2 3-task benchmark (ResNet-18), HAD achieves 35.12 mIoU on segmentation, 0.7410 Abs Err on depth, and 35.32° mean angle distance on surface normals, yielding a +32.74% average relative improvement over vanilla training and a Mean Rank of 3.67. On the more challenging 10-task Taskonomy benchmark, HAD attains +10.90% average relative improvement and MR 3.50, outperforming all baselines. Joint training (the upper bound) reaches +40.83% on NYUv2 and +44.57% on Taskonomy, indicating HAD closes roughly 80% of the gap to the oracle on NYUv2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAD outperforms all six lifelong learning baselines on both the 3-task NYUv2 and 10-task Taskonomy benchmarks. On NYUv2 (ResNet-18), HAD achieves +32.74% average relative improvement over vanilla training, surpassing the strongest baseline LwF (+24.74%) by 8 percentage points and EWC (+24.39%) by 8.35 points; notably, replay-based methods iCaRL (−4.82%) and DER (−3.31%) actually degrade below vanilla training, indicating that naive exemplar replay is counterproductive in the heterogeneous dense-prediction setting. On the 10-task Taskonomy benchmark, HAD (+10.90%) beats the best baseline iCaRL (+8.47%) and EWC (+6.41%), while LwF (+0.68%), SPG (+0.11%), and SGP (+0.79%) offer negligible gains over vanilla. In terms of Mean Rank, HAD achieves 3.67 (NYUv2) and 3.50 (Taskonomy), the best among all methods, whereas baselines range from 4.20 to 6.80. Joint training remains the upper bound (+40.83% / +44.57%), confirming that HAD recovers a substantial fraction of the performance achievable with full data access.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation framework for continual learning (Pham et al., 2022) by introducing two task-specific loss components—distribution-balanced and salience-guided distillation—that address the unique challenges of heterogeneous output spaces. It departs from the dominant lifelong learning paradigm, which assumes homogeneous task streams (e.g., classification-only or segmentation-only), by formalizing a new setting where sequentially arriving tasks have fundamentally different output structures (discrete class labels vs. continuous depth maps vs. surface normal vectors). The architectural choice of a task-shared encoder with task-specific decoders builds on multi-task dense prediction literature, but the sequential, exemplar-free training protocol distinguishes it from joint multi-task training approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LHL4DP scenario is practically motivated by autonomous driving and intelligent agent systems that must accumulate diverse perception capabilities over time without retraining from scratch, and the exemplar-free design addresses real privacy constraints. The finding that sequential heterogeneous learning can outperform separate task-specific models (by up to ~10% on surface normals) suggests positive transfer across heterogeneous DP tasks, which is a non-trivial result. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond dense prediction to truly heterogeneous task families (e.g., mixing DP with classification or detection), how it scales to longer task sequences, and whether the Sobel-based salience extraction remains effective for tasks without clear spatial boundaries.
