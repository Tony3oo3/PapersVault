## Problem

Existing federated learning methods for medical report generation assume each client draws i.i.d. samples from a stationary distribution, ignoring the temporal evolution of disease progression across sequential visits. This time-agnostic treatment causes conventional aggregation to average out progression-related signals, leading to unstable optimization and temporally incoherent reports under the pronounced cross-site and temporal drift observed in longitudinal imaging cohorts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D chest CT volumes (processed by a ConvViT encoder warmed up with ImageNet 21K) alongside minimal patient demographics—a pseudonymized identifier, age, and sex—encoded as a normalized 3-dimensional vector. Each patient contributes exactly five time-ordered examinations, and the model outputs a free-form radiology report text generated by a DistilGPT2 decoder. Demographics never leave the client; only lightweight LoRA parameters and their gradients are communicated to the server.

## Method

FedTAR couples two sequentially linked modules. On the client side, patient demographics are soft-clustered via a 16-component GMM, projected into an embedding space, and fed into a lightweight hypernetwork that generates patient-specific LoRA adapters (rank r=4) on top of a shared client adapter, enabling fine-grained personalization without exchanging raw demographics. On the server side, temporal residual aggregation computes a weighted residual at each visit: w_g^(t) = w_g^(t-1) + α_t · (w̄_g^(t) − w_g^(t-1)), where the coefficients {α_t} are parameterized by a softmax over time-embedding MLP scores and meta-learned via first-order MAML (hypergradient descent on a held-out validation loss). Theorems 1 and 2 guarantee that the global model always remains a convex combination of past snapshots and that per-step update magnitude is bounded by α_t·G, providing convergence stability regardless of the learned schedule.

## Results

On the J-MID chest CT report generation task (5 institutions, ~58K patient cases), FedTAR achieves BLEU-1 of 40.08, BLEU-4 of 12.40, ROUGE-L of 29.54, and CIDEr of 42.80, outperforming the strongest FL baseline (FedAdam) by +1.82 BLEU-1, +1.47 BLEU-4, +0.93 ROUGE-L, and +12.18 CIDEr. On MIMIC-CXR (370K images, 4 clients), FedTAR attains CE-F1 of 33.21, BLEU-4 of 19.08, ROUGE-L of 44.42, and CIDEr of 120.58, yielding relative gains of +4.4% CE-F1 and +11.5% CIDEr over the best baseline. Against the centralized CT2RepLong model on J-MID, FedTAR achieves a 66.7% CIDEr improvement (42.80 vs. 25.68) and higher CE-F1 (14.47 vs. 13.24) while preserving privacy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedTAR outperforms all six FL baselines on every reported metric (BLEU-1 through BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, CIDEr) on both J-MID and MIMIC-CXR. The closest competitor is FedAdam, which FedTAR surpasses by +1.82 BLEU-1 and +12.18 CIDEr on J-MID; on MIMIC-CXR, FedAdam leads on BLEU-1 (39.17 vs. 36.59) but FedTAR wins on CE-F1 (33.21 vs. 31.80), BLEU-4 (19.08 vs. 18.39), ROUGE-L (44.42 vs. 43.08), and CIDEr (120.58 vs. 97.07). SCAFFOLD and FedYogi trail substantially, with CIDEr scores of 24.82 and 24.79 respectively on J-MID, roughly 18 points below FedTAR. DRFA achieves the second-best BLEU-3 (16.63) and BLEU-4 (11.60) among baselines on J-MID but still falls short of FedTAR by 1.17 and 0.80 points. Against the centralized CT2RepLong model [15], FedTAR trades some early n-gram precision (BLEU-1: 40.08 vs. 47.20) for substantially better semantic coherence (CIDEr: 42.80 vs. 25.68, a 66.7% gain) and higher clinical efficacy F1 (14.47 vs. 13.24), demonstrating that federated temporal adaptation can match or exceed centralized performance on key clinical metrics while preserving data privacy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing Federated Temporal Adaptation (FTA) as a distinct FL problem setting where client distributions are explicitly time-varying, departing from the stationary i.i.d. assumption underlying FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, and prior federated report-generation methods (FedMRG, FedMME). Methodologically, it repurposes three established ideas—GMM-based soft clustering for demographic conditioning, hypernetwork-generated LoRA adapters for parameter-efficient personalization, and first-order MAML for meta-learned aggregation weights—into a unified pipeline that had not previously been combined for longitudinal medical imaging. The temporal residual aggregation with convex-hull guarantees draws on ideas from online learning and adaptive optimization, but the specific bilevel formulation for visit-weighted federated aggregation is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making temporal non-stationarity a first-class component of the federated objective, FTA provides a principled framework for the growing class of longitudinal, multi-institutional clinical tasks where patient states evolve across visits. The main limitation is that the primary evaluation relies on a proprietary five-site dataset with exactly five equally-spaced visits per patient, leaving open how the method handles irregular visit intervals, variable numbers of time points, or larger-scale deployments. The MIMIC-CXR results are mixed (FedAdam outperforms FedTAR on BLEU-1/2), suggesting the demographic-conditioned personalization may not transfer cleanly to X-ray report generation. Future work should address irregular temporal sampling, scalability beyond five clients, and whether the GMM demographic bottleneck is truly necessary versus simpler conditioning.
