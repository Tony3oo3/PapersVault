## Problem

Existing ST-based anomalous region detection methods rely solely on local molecular features from individual tissue spots, overlooking patient-level bulk RNA-seq data that carries crucial diagnostic information (cancer vs. normal). Additionally, current cross-domain genomic alignment strategies match individual gene expression values rather than gene co-expression patterns, and graph-based ST models construct edges based only on spatial proximity, missing non-local biological relationships between distant spots with similar molecular profiles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262511/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three aligned data sources per patient: spatial transcriptomics (spot-level gene expression vectors restricted to 5000 shared HVGs, batch-corrected via SpaCross), H&E pathological images cropped into patches matched to spot spatial coordinates (encoded by the CONCH vision-language model), and bulk RNA-seq profiles from TCGA for the same cancer subtype. Output is a per-spot binary classification (cancerous vs. normal) that, when aggregated spatially, yields an anomalous region mask overlaid on the tissue section.

## Method

BRGMAR introduces two key modules. First, a Dynamic Multi-Relational Graph Learning (DMRGL) module builds an initial KNN spatial graph and then iteratively refines edges by selecting the top-k most gene-expression-similar neighbors in the learned embedding space, with a Graph Attention Network propagating information across both relational types. Second, an Optimal Transportation-based Gene Module Alignment (OTGMA) module partitions the shared gene set into latent functional modules per domain, computes a Topological Overlap Matrix for each module, and aligns bulk-to-ST modules by minimizing a combined cost of squared Wasserstein-2 distance between module Laplacians (compositional) and distance between module centroids in a pre-trained gene functional embedding space (functional), solved via the Sinkhorn algorithm. ST and image representations are fused through a cross-attention mechanism where gene features serve as queries and image features as keys/values. The full model is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining cross-entropy classification on both bulk and ST domains plus the entropic-regularized OT alignment loss.

## Results

On the three ST datasets, BRGMAR achieves the best AUC and F1 across all settings: 0.801/0.775 on BRCA, 0.897/0.863 on HCC, and 0.724/0.732 on ccRCC. The largest margin is on HCC, where BRGMAR outperforms the next-best multi-modal method (MEATRD, AUC 0.861) by 3.6 AUC points and the best transfer-learning method (STEM, AUC 0.836) by 6.1 points. Even the ST-only variant BRGMAR-G (AUC 0.806 on HCC) surpasses all image-based baselines (best: f-AnoGAN at 0.673) and most ST-only baselines, confirming the contribution of the DMRGL module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BRGMAR wins on every dataset and metric against all 10 multi-modal and transfer-learning baselines. Among multi-modal ST+image methods, MEATRD is the closest competitor (AUC gap: 0.037 on BRCA, 0.036 on HCC, 0.043 on ccRCC), while STANDS and STAIG trail by 0.05–0.07 AUC. Among transfer-learning methods, Bulk2Space and STEM are the strongest (AUC 0.770 and 0.721 on BRCA; 0.831 and 0.836 on HCC), yet BRGMAR still leads by 0.03–0.06 AUC, indicating that gene-module-level alignment via OTGMA captures co-expression disruptions better than gene-level or instance-level domain adaptation. Against uni-modal baselines (Table 1), the ST-only variant BRGMAR-G already outperforms all image-based methods (SimpleNet, f-AnoGAN, THOR) by 0.10–0.20 AUC, and the full multi-modal BRGMAR further improves over BRGMAR-G by 0.075 AUC on BRCA and 0.091 on HCC, confirming the added value of both the bulk RNA-seq guidance and the image fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

BRGMAR extends the spatial transcriptomics (ST) anomalous region detection line (SEDR, FICT, SpaGIC, STANDS, MEATRD) by introducing a third modality—patient-level bulk RNA-seq—as a diagnostic guide, rather than relying solely on spot-level molecular features or ST+image pairs. Its OTGMA module departs from prior cross-domain genomic alignment methods (Bulk2Space, STEM, SpaGTL) that match individual gene expression values, instead aligning latent gene co-expression modules via optimal transport, building on the graph-comparison framework of Petric Maretic et al. (GOT, NeurIPS 2019) for computing Wasserstein distances between Laplacian matrices. The DMRGL module also moves beyond the standard KNN spatial graph assumption by dynamically incorporating gene-expression-similarity edges, addressing the limitation that distant spots can share molecular patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The integration of patient-level bulk RNA-seq diagnostic labels into spot-level anomaly detection offers a practical pathway for leveraging the abundant TCGA cohort data to improve AR detection on smaller ST slices, which is particularly relevant for early or heterogeneous tumors where local molecular signals are subtle. However, the approach requires matched bulk RNA-seq from the same cancer subtype and a common gene panel, limiting immediate applicability to tissue types without well-characterized bulk references. Open questions include whether the module-level OT alignment generalizes to single-cell or multi-omic settings, how the method scales to the thousands of spots in newer high-resolution ST platforms, and whether the diagnostic signal from bulk RNA-seq remains informative when the ST sample is from a different disease stage than the bulk cohort.
