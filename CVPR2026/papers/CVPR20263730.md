## Problem

Most IR-VIS image fusion methods assume both modalities are available at inference, but in realistic deployment the infrared sensor may be absent. When IR is missing, existing work resorts to pixel-space generative models (GANs, diffusion) to synthesize a substitute IR image before fusing, which is hard to control, physically inconsistent, and uninterpretable. No prior framework performs interpretable, coefficient-domain inference and fusion under missing-IR conditions.

## Input / Output

At inference the method requires only a single visible (RGB) image; no infrared input is needed. During training, paired IR-VIS images are randomly cropped to 128×128 patches, and the shared dictionary is learned on MSRS then transferred to FLIR and KAIST without retraining. A frozen LLM additionally consumes a task-description prompt to produce text features that modulate coefficients, but no free-form user text is required at test time. The output is a single fused image that simultaneously preserves visible structural detail and inferred thermal saliency.

## Method

The framework operates entirely in a shared convolutional-dictionary coefficient space through three modules. JSRL learns a cross-modal dictionary via an unfolding network of IV-DLB blocks that alternate Fourier-domain data-consistency steps (solved with the Sherman-Morrison formula) and learnable proximal updates (CoeNet U-Net for coefficients, DicNet for dictionary atoms), with HypNet predicting stage-wise hyperparameters. VGII encodes the visible image into frozen coefficient space, then an attention-based encoder-decoder (RIN) maps VIS coefficients to pseudo-IR coefficients; a frozen LLM extracts text features that drive a single FiLM-style affine modulation (γ, β) on the coefficients before a second RIN pass, providing a weak semantic prior without generative noise. AFRI fuses the VIS and refined pseudo-IR coefficients through two Convolution-Attention Fusion blocks (window self-attention + convolutional mixing) with an atom-wise gating mechanism, and reconstructs the final image via the shared dictionary and TailNet, closing the encode→transfer→fuse→reconstruct loop.

## Results

On the KAIST dataset, the proposed method (using only visible input) achieves the best AG (4.414), EI (37.115), and SF (11.031) among all 10 baselines that require both IR and VIS inputs. On MSRS, it similarly leads in AG (5.037), EI (53.168), and SF (14.898), while on FLIR it attains the lowest CE (0.596). For downstream tasks, the fused images yield an mIoU of 62.94 on FMB (nearly matching the best baseline LRRNet at 62.94) and an mAP of 0.948 on M3FD, competitive with full-modal fusion methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The critical distinction is that the proposed method requires only a visible image at inference, while all 10 baselines (U2Fusion, TarDAL, CDDFuse, LRRNet, IVFWSR, CoCoNet, EMMA, TIMFusion, DCEvo, SAGE) require both IR and VIS inputs. On KAIST, Ours outperforms every baseline on AG (4.414 vs. next-best DCEvo 3.229), EI (37.115 vs. DCEvo 34.459), and SF (11.031 vs. DCEvo 9.879), but trails on Qcb (0.405 vs. SAGE 0.520) and CE (2.227 vs. EMMA 0.859). On MSRS, Ours leads in AG (5.037 vs. EMMA 4.913), EI (53.168 vs. EMMA 52.642), and SF (14.898 vs. DCEvo 14.262), yet lags on Qcb (0.454 vs. DCEvo 0.571) and CE (3.142 vs. CoCoNet 0.758). On FLIR, Ours achieves the best CE (0.596 vs. TarDAL 0.643) but is outperformed by CDDFuse on AG (5.079), EI (52.633), and SF (13.346). For downstream tasks, Ours attains mAP 0.948 on M3FD (vs. SAGE 0.956, TIMFusion 0.954) and mIoU 62.94 on FMB (essentially tied with LRRNet at 62.94, ahead of TIMFusion 62.84), confirming that the VIS-only pipeline preserves sufficient thermal and structural information for detection and segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-driven unfolding paradigm (data-consistency + proximal updates, as in DKSVD, Learned-CSC, and Deep M²CDL) from single-modality restoration tasks to cross-modal IR-VIS fusion, replacing hand-tuned priors with learnable CoeNet/DicNet subnetworks. It departs from the dominant pixel-space generative approaches (GANs, diffusion models) for IR-VIS fusion by performing all inference and fusion operations in a shared dictionary-coefficient domain, and introduces a frozen LLM as a lightweight semantic prior that modulates coefficients via Feature-wise Linear Modulation rather than generating pixels. The shared convolutional dictionary with atom-level correspondences draws on sparse coding theory, while the window-attention fusion block borrows from Transformer-based fusion architectures like SwinFusion and CoCoNet.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap—IR sensor failure or absence in deployed systems—by showing that a single visible image can be enhanced to near full-modal fusion quality through interpretable coefficient-domain reasoning. The coefficient-domain formulation offers a principled alternative to black-box pixel generation, with the potential to extend to other missing-modality scenarios (e.g., missing VIS, missing hyperspectral bands). Open questions include whether the shared dictionary generalizes across domains without retraining, whether the LLM modulation provides meaningful gains over simpler heuristics at scale, and how the approach performs under severe domain shift (e.g., nighttime, adverse weather) where VIS-to-IR transfer becomes fundamentally harder.
