## Problem

Existing learning-based MVS methods rely heavily on photometric consistency for multi-view matching, leading to degraded reconstruction in weakly textured regions and non-Lambertian surfaces where appearance-based similarity is unreliable. While monocular depth priors have been explored (e.g., MonoMVSNet), prior work applies them only to the reference view, leaving the potential of per-pixel 3D spatial information across all source views largely untapped.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262969/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N RGB images (5 for DTU at 1152×1600, 21 for Tanks & Temples at 1056×1920), their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, COLMAP-derived sparse depth maps, and metric monocular depth priors from Prior Depth Anything. The monocular depth priors are converted into a 3-channel normalized 3D spatial position encoding (SPE) per pixel in the reference view's coordinate system, which is fed alongside the RGB images as a parallel input stream. The output is a multi-scale depth probability map refined through two enhancement stages, ultimately yielding a dense depth map and 3D point cloud after geometric-consistency post-processing.

## Method

SPE-MVS constructs a Spatial Position Encoding (SPE) for every input view by back-projecting monocular depth priors into the reference view's 3D coordinate system and normalizing by scene extents, yielding a per-pixel 3D coordinate tensor. A dual-branch fused FPN (PSHF) processes both the RGB images and SPEs in parallel encoder branches, aggregating them in a shared decoder to produce multi-scale photometric-spatial hybrid features. Cost volume construction simultaneously computes a feature-based volume (group-wise correlation) and an SPE-based volume (squared Euclidean distance between normalized 3D positions at each depth hypothesis), which are fused via 3D CNNs. A two-stage Monocular Depth-guided Enhancement (MDGE) module then progressively refines the depth probability map: MFE injects frozen monocular features from Depth Anything V2, and MDE further incorporates the monocular depth map and a soft-argmax depth estimate to enforce geometric continuity. Training uses a two-phase protocol (15 epochs on DTU, 10 epochs on BlendedMVS) with cross-entropy supervision on all intermediate and final probability maps.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, SPE-MVS achieves an Overall score of 0.272 mm and Completeness of 0.220 mm, setting new SOTA on both metrics and improving over the previous best (MonoMVSNet) by 0.006 mm in Overall and 0.023 mm in Completeness. On Tanks & Temples, the method attains a mean F1-score of 69.13 on the Intermediate set and 44.72 on the Advanced set, outperforming all compared methods. The accuracy metric (0.324 mm) is slightly higher than MVSFormer++ (0.309 mm) and RRT-MVS (0.309 mm), indicating a minor trade-off in precision for substantially better completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPE-MVS outperforms all 14 baselines on the DTU Overall and Completeness metrics. The closest competitor, MonoMVSNet [8] (which also uses monocular depth priors but only for the reference view), is beaten by 0.006 mm in Overall (0.272 vs. 0.278) and 0.023 mm in Completeness (0.220 vs. 0.243), confirming that extending monocular priors to all views via SPE yields meaningful gains. On Tanks & Temples, SPE-MVS achieves the highest mean F1-score on both Intermediate (69.13 vs. 68.63 for MonoMVSNet) and Advanced (44.72 vs. 43.58) sets, with the largest per-scene margins on Pantheon (65.87 vs. 68.54 for MonoMVSNet on Intermediate) and Ballroom (49.96 vs. 46.76 on Advanced). In terms of accuracy on DTU, SPE-MVS (0.324 mm) is slightly worse than MVSFormer++ (0.309 mm) and RRT-MVS (0.309 mm), but this is more than offset by its superior completeness, yielding the best Overall score. The gap over Transformer-based methods (MVSFormer, MVSFormer++) is consistent at ~0.009–0.010 mm in Overall, while the gap over CNN-based baselines (ET-MVSNet, GeoMVSNet, DS-PMNet) is larger at ~0.018–0.023 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPE-MVS extends the standard MVSNet-style cost volume pipeline (feature extraction → cost volume construction → regularization → depth regression) by introducing Spatial Position Encoding (SPE) as an additional input modality derived from metric monocular depth priors. It builds directly on ET-MVSNet's architecture as its baseline skeleton and departs from the closest prior work, MonoMVSNet, which applies monocular depth priors only to the reference view, by systematically encoding per-pixel 3D positions for all input views and integrating them into both feature extraction (via the PSHF dual-branch FPN) and cost volume construction (via the SPEC module). The reliance on Prior Depth Anything and COLMAP for generating reliable metric depth maps positions this work at the intersection of monocular depth estimation and multi-view stereo, rather than treating them as separate problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness of learning-based MVS—poor reconstruction in weakly textured and non-Lambertian regions due to over-reliance on photometric matching—by injecting explicit 3D geometric priors into the matching process. The completeness gains (0.243→0.220 on DTU) suggest the approach is particularly valuable for applications requiring dense, gap-free reconstructions. However, the method introduces non-trivial computational overhead (0.27s→0.58s, 4.2→5.9 GB) and depends on the quality of COLMAP sparse reconstruction and monocular depth priors, which may degrade in scenes with large viewpoint changes, dynamic content, or poor texture. Open questions include how the approach generalizes to real-world uncontrolled captures where COLMAP may fail, and whether the SPE mechanism can be made lighter or learned end-to-end rather than relying on a frozen pretrained depth model.
