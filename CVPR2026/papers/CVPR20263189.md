## Problem

Existing model merging methods assume test-time data are clean and distributionally aligned with training and auxiliary sources, but real-world deployment involves test-time bias (sensor noise, domain heterogeneity) and generalization to unseen tasks, both of which cause substantial performance degradation. No prior merging strategy explicitly models sample-level distributional discrepancies to adaptively adjust merging behavior under such shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from eight classification benchmarks (SUN397, Cars, RESISC45, EuroSAT, SVHN, GTSRB, MNIST, DTD) through a pretrained CLIP ViT backbone, along with unannotated auxiliary data (no labels required) for training the router. It produces a class prediction over a unified label space spanning all K tasks, accompanied by per-class Dirichlet evidence and a scalar uncertainty value. The debiased router additionally outputs per-sample task-specific or layer-specific weight vectors that dynamically reconfigure the merged parameters at inference time.

## Method

BD-Merging introduces a joint evidential head over a unified label space that outputs Dirichlet concentration parameters, augmented with an inter-class evidential contrast (IEC) metric and an inverse correlation loss to capture cross-task semantic dependencies beyond standard total-evidence uncertainty. From these evidential outputs, an Adjacency Discrepancy Score (ADS) is computed per sample-neighbor pair by multiplying three factors: prediction sharpness (local evidence concentration), semantic divergence (L1 distance between normalized Dirichlet parameters), and opinion conflicts (belief disagreement weighted by mutual confidence). Guided by ADS, a discrepancy-aware contrastive loss partitions each sample's radius-r neighborhood into positive (low-discrepancy) and negative (high-discrepancy) sets, training a two-layer MLP debiased router that assigns per-sample task- or layer-specific merging weights via softmax. The router is jointly optimized with an unsupervised entropy objective, and the final merged model is θ* = θ₀ + Σ wₖ·τₖ, where weights are dynamically determined by input features rather than fixed at merge time.

## Results

On eight image classification tasks with ViT-B/32, BD-Merging (Layer-wise) achieves 87.15% average accuracy on clean data and 78.78% under moderate corruption (L2), outperforming the strongest baseline Twin-Merging (84.10% clean, 74.17% L2) by 3.05% and 4.61% respectively. Under severe corruption (L3), BD-Merging retains 75.36% average accuracy versus 70.88% for Twin-Merging and 70.97% for AdaMerging w/Surgery. On unseen-task generalization, BD-Merging reaches 55.01% average accuracy (vs. 53.03% for Twin-Merging and 49.83% for AdaMerging) while also achieving the best seen-task accuracy of 94.53%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BD-Merging (Layer-wise) outperforms all six baselines on both clean and corrupted settings. Against the strongest competitor Twin-Merging [26], BD-Merging gains +3.05% clean accuracy (87.15 vs. 84.10) and +4.61% under L2 corruption (78.78 vs. 74.17). Against AdaMerging w/Surgery [45,47], the closest baseline under corruption, BD-Merging leads by +4.45% (78.78 vs. 74.33 at L2). Simpler methods like Task Arithmetic [20] and Ties-Merging [44] trail by 18.5% and 14.4% respectively under L2 corruption. On unseen-task generalization (Table 2), BD-Merging achieves 55.01% average accuracy, surpassing Twin-Merging (53.03%) and AdaMerging (49.83%), though it falls slightly short of the unmerged pretrained CLIP baseline (56.99%) on unseen tasks while dramatically exceeding it on seen tasks (94.53% vs. 39.27%). The ablation confirms the debiased router is the single most critical component, with its removal causing a 10.9% drop under corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

BD-Merging sits at the intersection of three research threads: Dirichlet-based Evidential Deep Learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) for fine-grained uncertainty quantification, Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2022) as the canonical parameter-space merging operation, and AdaMerging (Yang et al., 2023) for learning task-adaptive merging coefficients. The key departure is that prior MM methods (Ties-Merging, Twin-Merging, Surgery) operate under the implicit assumption of clean, distributionally aligned test data, whereas BD-Merging explicitly models test-time distribution shift by introducing a joint evidential head over a unified label space and an Adjacency Discrepancy Score that drives a per-sample debiased router via contrastive learning. This reframes merging from a static weight-combination problem into a dynamic, uncertainty-guided allocation process that adapts at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: deployed multi-task systems inevitably encounter test-time corruptions and unseen tasks, yet most MM methods degrade sharply under such shifts. The debiased router offers a lightweight mechanism for per-sample adaptivity without retraining, and the time-cost analysis suggests a favorable efficiency-accuracy tradeoff compared to Surgery-based approaches. However, the seen-vs-unseen accuracy gap remains substantial (94.53% vs. 55.01%), indicating that cross-task generalization is far from solved. Open questions include whether the evidential-contrastive framework transfers to LLM or multimodal merging, whether the controlled corruption protocol (20% of samples, up to 8 corruptions) captures the full spectrum of real-world distribution shift, and how the method scales when the number of merged tasks grows beyond eight.
