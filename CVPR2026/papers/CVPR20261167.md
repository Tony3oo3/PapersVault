## Problem

Current single-image 3D scene generation methods produce photorealistic, explorable worlds that are entirely silent, leaving immersion perceptually incomplete. No prior framework jointly generates a navigable 3D visual scene and a spatially coherent sound field—semantically grounded to scene content and geometrically aligned to 3D source locations—from a single RGB image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261167/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (no camera intrinsics, no audio, no text prompt required) and produces a real-time navigable 3D Gaussian splat scene together with a first-order ambisonics (FOA) spatial sound field parameterized as a point-cloud-based representation. The audio supports heterogeneous source types (point, areal/clustered, and ambient) and can be decoded to binaural audio at any 6-DoF listener pose, enabling free-viewpoint audio-visual exploration with sub-millisecond audio callback latency.

## Method

SONOWORLD is a training-free, four-stage pipeline. First, GeoCalib estimates camera elevation and FoV, the image is warped to equirectangular and outpainted via WorldGen into a full 360° panorama, which is lifted into a 3D Gaussian splat scene. Second, a VLM (GPT-5 or LLaVA-Next) proposes sounding categories with source-type labels and synthesis prompts; X-Decoder performs open-vocabulary instance segmentation on overlapping FoV tiles, and SAM2 refines these into globally consistent panoramic masks that are unprojected to 3D using scene depth. Third, MMAudio synthesizes per-source and scene-level ambient waveforms from text prompts, which are equalized by VLM-predicted energy levels and spatialized into ambisonics coefficients via analytic point-source, clustered-source (averaged over point clouds), and omnidirectional ambient models with exponential distance attenuation. Fourth, an HRTF-based decoder converts ambisonics to binaural audio synchronized with Gaussian splat rendering. A key design choice is that the entire spatial audio encoding is differentiable with respect to source waveforms and scene parameters, enabling downstream optimization tasks such as one-shot room acoustic learning and audio-visual spatial source separation.

## Results

On SONOSCENE360, SONOWORLD (Proprietary) achieves the best spatial accuracy with a geodesic angular error (Δ_Angular) of 0.728°, reducing DoA error by 47% over the best spatial-audio baseline (SEE-2-SOUND at 1.397°), and a spherical energy correlation coefficient (CC) of 0.658, a >239% improvement over baselines (0.142–0.194). On semantic alignment, it reaches D-CLAP_T of 0.457 and D-CLAP_R of 67.6%, exceeding the monaural MMAudio baseline (0.322, 33.8%) by >39% and the spatial-audio baselines by >117%. Even the fully open-source variant (Δ_Angular = 0.975°, CC = 0.491, D-CLAP_T = 0.413) outperforms all baselines that rely on proprietary models. In the one-shot room acoustic learning extension (Table 2), the differentiable renderer achieves Δ_Angular = 0.22° and ENV = 1.22, substantially outperforming NAF (1.76°, 3.60), INRAS (1.64°, 5.97), and AV-NeRF (1.58°, 1.89).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONOWORLD wins decisively across all spatial and semantic metrics against every baseline. Against the three spatial-audio generation methods (SEE-2-SOUND, ViSAGe, OmniAudio), it reduces Δ_Angular by 47–56% (0.728° vs. 1.397–1.649°) and improves CC by 239–365% (0.658 vs. 0.142–0.194), indicating far more accurate source direction and energy distribution. Against the monaural MMAudio baseline (adapted with ground-truth panning), SONOWORLD still leads on D-CLAP_T by 42% (0.457 vs. 0.322) and D-CLAP_R by 100% (67.6% vs. 33.8%), showing that semantic grounding from the 3D scene provides a substantial advantage over video-conditioned audio synthesis alone. Notably, the open-source configuration (HunyuanWorld + LLaVA-Next) already surpasses all baselines that benefit from proprietary model outputs, suggesting the pipeline's gains come from the joint audio-visual grounding design rather than model scale. In the one-shot room acoustic learning task, the differentiable renderer outperforms NAF, INRAS, and AV-NeRF on all three metrics (Δ_Angular, MAG, ENV), with the largest margin on ENV (1.22 vs. 1.89–5.97), demonstrating data-efficient acoustic modeling from a single microphone pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper composes three previously separate research threads—panoramic 3D scene generation (WorldGen, HunyuanWorld, Marble), spatial audio synthesis (MMAudio, SEE-2-SOUND, ViSAGe, OmniAudio), and audio-visual grounding (VLMs, SAM2, X-Decoder)—into a unified training-free pipeline. Prior 3D scene generation methods produce silent worlds, while prior spatial audio methods require video or panorama inputs rather than a single image; no existing work jointly generates a navigable 3D scene and a semantically grounded spatial sound field from one photograph. The key architectural departure is the differentiable ambisonics encoder that treats source waveforms and 3D positions as optimizable variables, enabling downstream tasks (room acoustic learning, source separation) that prior spatial audio pipelines, which treat rendering as a fixed black box, cannot support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing IMAGE2AVSCENE and SONOSCENE360, the paper establishes a benchmark and evaluation protocol for a task that was previously undefined, giving the community a concrete target for future work. The training-free design makes the approach immediately deployable without large-scale audio-visual training data, and the differentiable rendering pipeline opens a path toward 3D audio-visual inverse problems (acoustic learning, separation) that were previously intractable without purpose-built neural fields. Key limitations include the static-scene assumption (no moving sources), reliance on proprietary models (GPT-5, Marble) for best results, and the single-image input that precludes temporal audio cues; extending to dynamic scenes with moving emitters and higher-order ambisonics remains open.
