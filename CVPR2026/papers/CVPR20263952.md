## Problem

Visual geometry foundation models (e.g., DepthAnything, VGGT) predict only visible surface points, yet occupancy prediction requires reasoning about volumetric interiors. Existing indoor occupancy methods either lift 2D features into dense 3D volumes or initialize random Gaussian anchors, making limited use of 3D geometric cues and incurring substantial redundancy by representing vast empty regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263952/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 518 px on the longer side) and camera intrinsics as input, producing a voxel-wise semantic occupancy map over a 60×60×36 grid (4.8 m × 4.8 m × 2.88 m) with 12 semantic classes. Depth is regressed at 1/4 spatial resolution via a lightweight MLP, and K=16 points are sampled per pixel along the corresponding camera ray. For streaming video, sequential frames with known camera poses are fused into a global scene-level occupancy at 0.08 m voxel resolution.

## Method

GPOcc extracts 3D-aware features and surface depth from a pretrained geometry prior (VGGT or DepthAnything), then extends each surface point inward along its camera ray by K=16 offsets with a dynamically predicted scale to approximate object interiors. Each sampled point's feature (combined with a learnable per-depth embedding) is passed through an MLP to predict Gaussian attributes (scale, rotation, opacity, semantic feature). An opacity-based pruning step (τ=0.01) discards negligible primitives, and the remaining sparse Gaussians are splatted into occupancy via a probabilistic superposition formulation from GaussianFormer2, where regions far from any primitive are naturally empty. For streaming input, a training-free incremental update fuses per-frame Gaussians into a global memory bank using confidence-weighted averaging with temporal weighting (γ<0.5 favors newer frames). Training uses focal loss, LovászSoftmax, scene-class affinity losses, and a Huber depth loss for end-to-end geometric consistency.

## Results

On Occ-ScanNet (monocular), GPOcc with VGGT priors achieves 63.14 IoU and 56.19 mIoU, surpassing the previous best (EmbodiedOcc++ at 46.20 mIoU) by +9.99 mIoU. With the same DepthAnything prior as EmbodiedOcc, GPOcc-DPT reaches 51.88 mIoU (+6.73 over EmbodiedOcc) while running 2.65× faster (28.22 vs. 10.66 FPS) with fewer parameters (97.95M vs. 231.45M). On the streaming EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet benchmark, GPOcc-VGGT achieves 61.41 IoU and 55.39 mIoU, improving over EmbodiedOcc++ by +9.21 IoU and +11.79 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPOcc outperforms all seven baselines across both monocular and streaming settings. Against the closest competitor EmbodiedOcc++ [24], GPOcc-VGGT gains +9.99 mIoU (56.19 vs. 46.20) on Occ-ScanNet and +11.79 mIoU (55.39 vs. 43.60) on EmbodiedOcc-ScanNet. With the identical DepthAnything prior used by EmbodiedOcc [33], GPOcc-DPT still improves mIoU by +6.73 (51.88 vs. 45.15) while running 2.65× faster (28.22 vs. 10.66 FPS) and using less than half the parameters (97.95M vs. 231.45M). Earlier methods—TPVFormer [4], MonoScene [2], ISO [39], SurroundOcc [31], and GaussianFormer [6]—all fall in the 24.6–30.8 mIoU range on Occ-ScanNet, trailing GPOcc-VGGT by over 25 mIoU. The consistent per-class gains (e.g., +11.54 on ceiling, +14.97 on floor, +10.19 on objects over EmbodiedOcc++) indicate that the ray-based volumetric sampling and sparse Gaussian formulation benefit all semantic categories rather than a subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPOcc builds directly on the Gaussian-based occupancy formulation of GaussianFormer2 [5] (probabilistic Gaussian superposition for voxel inference) and the training objective of EmbodiedOcc [33] (focal + LovászSoftmax + scene-class affinity losses), while departing from both in a key way: instead of initializing dense 3D anchors or lifting 2D features into full volumetric grids, it uses ray-based volumetric sampling to extend surface-level geometry priors (from VGGT or DepthAnything) into object interiors. This positions the work at the intersection of the visual geometry foundation model line (DUSt3R → VGGT → StreamVGGT) and the sparse Gaussian occupancy line (GaussianFormer → GaussianFormer2 → EmbodiedOcc), repurposing increasingly powerful 3D priors that were previously underutilized for volumetric reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the surface-to-volume gap in geometry priors can be bridged with a simple ray-extension strategy, yielding a compact sparse representation that is both more accurate and substantially faster than dense-anchor or dense-grid alternatives. The training-free incremental update for streaming inputs is practically important for embodied agents that must build scene representations online without retraining. Open questions include how well the fixed ray-based sampling handles heavily occluded or concave interiors, whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether the gains over prior SOTA are primarily attributable to the stronger VGGT prior versus the architectural changes.
