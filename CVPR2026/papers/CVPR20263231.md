## Problem

Data-free model merging must combine multiple task-specific experts into a single model without access to training data, yet existing methods rely on parameter-space heuristics (sign alignment, pruning, SVD subspaces) that do not explicitly model task statistics. The paper identifies that under a local linear approximation, fine-tuning updates encode second-order structure related to input covariance, but no prior method exploits this connection in a principled, closed-form manner that also handles cross-task scale imbalance and spectral concentration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the pretrained base weights W_0 and the fine-tuned expert weights {W_t} for T tasks, computing layer-wise task vectors ΔW_t = W_t − W_0 as its sole input. It produces a single set of merged layer weights W̄ per layer, with no additional parameters, adapters, or routing modules at inference time. The procedure is fully closed-form per layer (matrix multiplications, one matrix inversion, and optionally one SVD), requiring no access to any task data, labels, or validation sets.

## Method

ACE-Merging derives a closed-form optimal merge by showing that the centered column Gram matrix of each task vector, (ΔW_t − 1μ_tᵀ)ᵀ(ΔW_t − 1μ_tᵀ), serves as a data-free proxy for the task input second-moment matrix Σ_t under local linearization. Three components address practical challenges: (1) an adaptive covariance normalization that computes a heterogeneity statistic γ from log-Frobenius norms of task vectors and, when γ exceeds a threshold τ, trace-normalizes each covariance proxy before applying Tikhonov regularization to balance task scales; (2) a Collective Structural Prior (CSP) that constructs a rank-one consensus matrix from the mean row of the aggregated covariance and adds it to the system matrix, injecting shared geometric structure beyond isotropic ridge; and (3) a spectral refinement step (applied only under high heterogeneity) that fuses a structural residual with the preliminary solution, then rescales the top-k singular values to their mean via SVD, flattening the spectrum while preserving the dominant subspace. The entire pipeline is closed-form per layer with no iterative optimization, distinguishing it from gradient-based methods like WUDI-Merging.

## Results

On GPT-2 (GLUE), ACE-Merging achieves an average score of 74.1, surpassing the strongest data-free baselines (TSV-M at 70.2, Task Arithmetic and Ties-Merging at 70.0) by more than 4 absolute points. On RoBERTa, it improves the normalized GLUE average from 85.3 (WUDI-Merging) to 90.4 on RoBERTa-Base and from 88.8 to 91.7 on RoBERTa-Large. On vision benchmarks, ACE-Merging attains the top result on all three ViT-B/32 settings (87.9 / 82.3 / 78.8 for 8/14/20 tasks) and on the 14-task ViT-L/14 benchmark (91.1), while remaining within 0.5 points of the best method (Iso-CTS) on the remaining configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE-Merging outperforms all 11 baselines on the GPT-2 GLUE benchmark, with the largest margins over Weight Averaging (+18.0), Fisher Merging (+15.4), and RegMean (+5.3), and a consistent +3.9 to +4.1 point gain over the strongest data-free competitors (TSV-M, Task Arithmetic, Ties-Merging). On RoBERTa, it beats WUDI-Merging by 5.1 points (Base) and 2.9 points (Large), and surpasses all other baselines by 15+ points. On vision, ACE-Merging wins on all ViT-B/32 settings and the 14-task ViT-L/14 setting, but Iso-CTS edges it out by 0.3–2.3 points on several ViT-B/16 and 20-task configurations, while TSV-M and CART remain within 1–2 points. The data-dependent baselines (Fisher Merging, RegMean) are substantially weaker on GPT-2, confirming that data-free methods with proper second-order modeling can match or exceed data-dependent approaches. Overall, ACE-Merging's advantage is most pronounced under high task heterogeneity (GPT-2, RoBERTa), consistent with its adaptive normalization and spectral refinement design.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACE-Merging builds directly on the task vector formulation from Task Arithmetic [4] and reinterprets WUDI-Merging [23] through a unified second-order proxy lens, showing that WUDI's iterative gradient descent can be understood as approximately using a norm-normalized covariance proxy. It departs from the SVD-based decomposition line (TSV-M, Iso-C, Iso-CTS) by instead estimating task-specific input covariance from centered task vectors under a local linearization, and from heuristic data-free methods (Ties-Merging, sign alignment) by grounding the merge in a closed-form statistical objective. The key conceptual departure is treating fine-tuning updates as carriers of second-order data geometry rather than unstructured parameter offsets, yielding a principled bridge between weight-space operations and input-space statistics without requiring any data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The closed-form, data-free formulation offers a practical and stable alternative to iterative or SVD-based merging, with the heterogeneity-adaptive design (γ threshold) making it robust across architectures with very different scale profiles. The >4-point average gain on GPT-2 and consistent improvements across vision benchmarks suggest the covariance-aware perspective captures structure that purely geometric or heuristic methods miss. Open questions include whether the local linearization assumption holds for deeper or more nonlinear layers, whether the fixed regularization strength ε can be made adaptive (acknowledged by the authors as future work), and whether the covariance proxy remains informative when fine-tuning involves large architectural changes rather than small perturbations.
