## Problem

Existing diffusion-based acceleration methods for portrait animation (cache-based and distillation-based) fail to maintain identity consistency beyond roughly 20 seconds, suffering from body distortions, color drift, and ID inconsistency when facial motions are large and complex. The paper addresses how to achieve substantial inference speedup (6×) while preserving identity stability in infinite-length portrait animation with intricate, high-amplitude facial expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262769/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image (for identity) and a driven video (for facial motion) as input. The driven video is processed by PD-FGC to extract identity-agnostic facial embeddings (head pose, eyes, emotion, mouth), while the reference image is encoded by a CLIP image encoder and a frozen 3D VAE. The output is an ID-preserving portrait animation video at 480×832 resolution, demonstrated to remain stable beyond 1,800 frames (up to 5,400+ frames at 30 FPS) without significant quality degradation.

## Method

FlashPortrait builds on Wan2.1 I2V-14B and introduces three key components. First, a Normalized Facial Expression Block replaces each Image Cross-attention layer in the DiT: facial embeddings from PD-FGC are refined via self-attention and FFN, then normalized using the mean and standard deviation of both the facial features and diffusion latents to align their distribution centers, reducing ID drift. Second, a Weighted Sliding-Window Strategy generates long videos by fusing overlapping latents (v=5 frames) between adjacent context windows using arithmetic weights based on relative frame indices, ensuring smooth clip transitions. Third, an Adaptive Latent Prediction Acceleration Mechanism uses Taylor series expansion with finite differences to predict future latents and skip denoising steps; two dynamic functions—s(t) scaling the timestep interval K based on latent variation rate, and w(t,l,i) rescaling per-layer finite differences by their derivative magnitude ratio—adaptively correct prediction errors caused by large-amplitude facial motion, achieving 6× speedup with negligible quality loss. Training is lightweight: only DiT attention modules are updated with a face/lip-masked reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the Hard100 benchmark (1-minute videos), FlashPortrait achieves AED 29.68, APD 24.40, and MAE 12.54, outperforming the strongest competitor Wan-Animate (AED 42.98, APD 35.06, MAE 20.08) by 30.9%, 37.5%, and 37.5% respectively. On VoxCeleb2&VFHQ (10-second videos), FlashPortrait attains the best FID (65.18), FVD (320.47), and LMD (4.90) among all methods. Inference speed is 720 s for a 20-second 480×832 video, representing a 6× speedup over the unaccelerated baseline (4328 s) and 3.2× faster than Wan-Animate (2298 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlashPortrait outperforms all seven portrait-animation baselines (LivePortrait, Skyreels-A1, FollowYE, X-Portrait, HunyuanPortrait, FantasyPortrait, Wan-Animate) on every metric for both short (10 s) and long (1 min) videos. The gap is most pronounced on Hard100: FlashPortrait reduces AED from 42.98 (Wan-Animate) to 29.68 and FID from 143.61 to 62.33, indicating substantially better identity preservation and video fidelity over extended sequences. Among DiT-based competitors, FlashPortrait is 3.2× faster than Wan-Animate (720 s vs 2298 s) and 6× faster than FantasyPortrait (720 s vs 4339 s) while simultaneously achieving lower AED, APD, and MAE. Against acceleration-specific baselines (TeaCache, FoCa, Self-Forcing), FlashPortrait's adaptive latent prediction delivers a 6× speedup with negligible quality loss, whereas Self-Forcing (266 s) and FoCa (862 s) suffer severe identity inconsistency and artifacts on large-amplitude facial motions. The only metric where FlashPortrait is not the absolute best on VoxCeleb2&VFHQ is PSNR (32.36 vs Wan-Animate's 32.54) and SSIM (0.79 vs 0.82), but it leads on all motion-accuracy and long-video metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashPortrait builds on the Wan2.1 video diffusion transformer as its backbone and extends the portrait animation line of work (Wan-Animate, FantasyPortrait, HunyuanPortrait, Skyreels-A1) by targeting the specific challenge of infinite-length, ID-preserving synthesis. It departs from prior acceleration strategies—cache-based methods (TeaCache, FORA, TaylorSeer, FoCa) and distillation-based methods (Self-Forcing)—by proposing a training-free, adaptive Taylor-series latent prediction that dynamically adjusts prediction order and per-layer scaling based on latent variation rates, rather than relying on fixed-order extrapolation or expensive student-model training. The Normalized Facial Expression Block introduces a distribution-alignment mechanism between diffusion latents and facial embeddings that is absent from prior DiT-based portrait animators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× inference speedup with negligible quality degradation over 1,800+ frames addresses a critical practical bottleneck for real-time or near-real-time portrait animation applications (virtual assistants, film production). The finding that aggressive acceleration methods (Self-Forcing at 5–20×) actually degrade identity consistency more than a moderate 6× adaptive approach challenges the assumption that higher speedup ratios are always achievable without quality loss in high-motion domains. Open questions include whether the adaptive Taylor prediction generalizes to other high-motion video generation tasks beyond portraits, and whether the 14B-parameter backbone requirement limits deployment to resource-constrained settings.
