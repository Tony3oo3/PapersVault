## Problem

Pre-trained text-to-image (T2I) generative models have been widely adapted for monocular depth and normal estimation, but dense prediction is inherently an image-to-image task, making image editing models a more natural foundation. The paper systematically demonstrates that editing models possess structural priors already aligned with geometric cues, converge more stably during fine-tuning, and ultimately outperform their generative counterparts, yet no prior work has successfully adapted a state-of-the-art DiT-based editor for dense geometry prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (H×W×3) as input and jointly produces a depth map and a surface normal map in a single forward pass. During training, both the input image and the geometric annotations are encoded into VAE latent space, where the DiT operates; the VAE decoder is applied only at inference to recover pixel-space predictions. Depth supervision uses logarithmic quantization (log(D+1e-6)) normalized to [-1,1] to remain compatible with the model's native BF16 precision, avoiding the severe quantization artifacts that uniform or inverse schemes introduce at near or far ranges.

## Method

FE2E adapts the Step1X-Edit DiT-based editor for dense geometry prediction through three key modifications. First, it reformulates the standard flow matching objective into a 'consistent velocity' loss where the predicted velocity is constant (independent of timestep t) and the starting point is fixed at zero, reducing inference to a single forward pass that directly outputs the target latent without ODE integration. Second, it applies logarithmic depth quantization to resolve the precision conflict between BF16 training (sufficient for RGB) and the high numerical precision demanded by depth values spanning 0–80 m. Third, it repurposes the DiT's horizontally concatenated output: the left half (normally discarded) supervises depth while the right half supervises normals, enabling cost-free joint estimation that leverages global attention for implicit cross-task information exchange. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 64) on ~71K synthetic images from Hypersim and Virtual KITTI, updating only the DiT module while freezing the VAE and other components.

## Results

FE2E, trained on only 71K images (0.1% of DepthAnything's 62.6M), achieves the best average rank (1.4) across five zero-shot depth benchmarks. On ETH3D, it reduces AbsRel to 3.8 (a 35% improvement over the second-best Lotus-D at 6.1) and δ1 to 98.7%. On KITTI, it achieves AbsRel 6.6 and δ1 96.0%, outperforming all prior methods. For surface normal estimation (Table 3), FE2E also attains the best average rank (1.6), with MeanErr of 13.8° on ScanNet and 15.1° on iBims-1, surpassing all baselines including those trained on comparable or larger data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FE2E outperforms all diffusion-based baselines (Marigold, Lotus-G/D, Diffusion-E2E-FT, GeoWizard, GenPercept) on every depth benchmark, with the largest margin on ETH3D where it achieves AbsRel 3.8 vs. 6.1 for Lotus-D (37% reduction) and 6.5 for Marigold (42% reduction). Against the data-scaled DepthAnything series (62.6M images, ~880× more data), FE2E wins on ETH3D (3.8 vs. 12.7/13.1), KITTI (6.6 vs. 7.4/7.6), NYUv2 (4.1 vs. 4.3/4.5), and DIODE (22.8 vs. 26.0/26.5), but narrowly loses on ScanNet (4.4 vs. 4.3 for DepthAnything v1). For surface normal estimation, FE2E beats Marigold v1.1 (the strongest normal baseline) on ScanNet (13.8° vs. 14.5° MeanErr), iBims-1 (15.1° vs. 16.3°), and Sintel (31.2° vs. N/A), while matching it closely on NYUv2 (16.2° vs. 16.1°). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the editing-model foundation (Step1X-Edit) is the single largest contributor: even with identical improvements, the FLUX-based variant (ID7) trails the Step1X-Edit variant (ID6) by 0.6 AbsRel on ETH3D, validating the core hypothesis that editing priors are inherently better suited for dense geometry tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of repurposing T2I generative models (Stable Diffusion, FLUX) for dense geometry estimation, as established by Marigold, GeoWizard, and Lotus. Instead, it positions I2I editing models—specifically Step1X-Edit, a DiT-based editor fine-tuned from FLUX—as a more natural foundation, arguing that their inherent image-conditioning and structural priors align better with the deterministic, image-to-image nature of depth/normal prediction. Technically, it extends Lotus's deterministic prediction idea to the flow-matching setting (consistent velocity with fixed start) and adapts Marigold's normalization strategy into a logarithmic quantization scheme compatible with BF16 training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that editing models converge more stably and achieve lower loss floors than their generative counterparts challenges the field's default assumption that T2I models are the go-to prior for dense prediction, and the consistent-velocity formulation reduces inference to a single forward pass—a meaningful efficiency gain over iterative ODE solvers. The 35% AbsRel improvement on ETH3D using only 71K training images (vs. 62.6M for DepthAnything) highlights a data-efficiency angle that could reduce the barrier to entry for geometry estimation. Open questions include whether the editor-over-generator advantage generalizes to non-DiT architectures, other dense prediction tasks (segmentation, optical flow), and whether the single-step inference quality degrades at higher resolutions or more complex scenes.
