## Problem

The thinking-with-frames paradigm for long video understanding alternates between global temporal reasoning and local frame examination, but suffers from a severe efficiency bottleneck: the multi-modal context grows progressively with each iteration, accumulating highly redundant visual tokens (over 90% receive attention scores below 10⁻³). This redundancy substantially inflates inference latency without contributing meaningfully to reasoning, creating a fundamental tension between the iterative zoom-in mechanism's accuracy gains and its computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long video (ranging from under 1 minute to over 10 minutes) and a textual query. The video is initially consumed as 10 uniformly sampled frames (short-form) or 32 frames (long-form); the target model then predicts temporal regions of interest, within which the draft model performs dense sampling at 1 fps and returns only 2 representative frames per iteration. The structured output includes a reasoning trace (<think>), predicted temporal evidence segments (<segment>), draft-selected salient frames (<frame>), and a final text answer (<answer>), with up to 3–4 iterative speculation-verification rounds before convergence.

## Method

SpecTemp decouples temporal perception from reasoning via a cooperative dual-model architecture: a 7B target MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) handles high-level temporal reasoning and verification, while a 3B draft MLLM performs rapid dense exploration and selects sparse representative frames from predicted temporal regions. The iterative loop works as follows: the target model reasons over initial frames and predicts temporal regions needing further examination; the draft model densely samples those regions and proposes 2 salient frames; the target model verifies the proposal and either outputs an answer or triggers another round. Training uses a two-stage protocol—cold-start SFT to establish output structure and basic capabilities, followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with distinct reward signals (format + answer correctness + IoU for the target; format + CLIP-based visual information gain for the draft). The approach is supported by the SpecTemp-80K dataset, which provides synchronized dual-level annotations (coarse evidence spans for the target, fine-grained frame evidence for the draft) generated via GPT-4o with manual validation.

## Results

On long-form video benchmarks, SpecTemp with 58.1 average frames achieves 47.8% on Video-Holmes, 61.4% on LongVideoBench, 50.9% on MLVU, and 64.1% on Video-MME, while reducing inference latency to 8.9s (vs. 11.5s for VideoChat-R1.5, a 23% speedup). With only 14.5 frames, SpecTemp already surpasses the 16-frame Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by +12.0% on Video-Holmes and +8.0% on MLVU. On short-form benchmarks, SpecTemp (47.6 frames) matches or exceeds VideoChat-R1.5 (64 frames) on MMVU (70.4 vs. 70.1) and VSI-Bench (39.7 vs. 39.4) while being 19% faster (4.7s vs. 5.8s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary thinking-with-frames baseline VideoChat-R1.5 (64 frames), SpecTemp (58.1 frames) wins on Video-Holmes (+2.7), LongVideoBench (+0.8), and Video-MME (+0.7) but trails on MLVU (50.9 vs. 52.3), while cutting latency by 23% (8.9s vs. 11.5s). Compared to the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B at matched frame budgets, SpecTemp gains +12.0 on Video-Holmes and +8.0 on MLVU with only 14.5 frames (vs. 16), and +10.0 / +2.7 at the 64-frame budget. Against proprietary models, SpecTemp (58.1 frames) outperforms GPT-4o on all four long-form benchmarks (e.g., 61.4 vs. 50.6 on LongVideoBench) and beats Gemini 2.5 Pro on three of four, losing only on Video-MME (64.1 vs. 65.6). Among open-source 64-frame baselines, SpecTemp surpasses InternVL3-8B, VideoChat-Flash, and Video-R1 across all long-form benchmarks, with the largest margin over Video-R1 (+8.2 on Video-Holmes). The efficiency advantage is consistent: SpecTemp's 3.7s latency at 14.5 frames is faster than every 16-frame baseline except InternVL3-8B (3.9s), and its 8.9s at 58.1 frames is faster than all 64-frame competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpecTemp extends the speculative decoding paradigm (Chen et al. 2023; Leviathan et al. 2023) from token-level LLM acceleration to frame-level visual perception in video understanding. It builds directly on the thinking-with-frames paradigm (VideoChat-R1.5, Yan et al. 2025), which iteratively zooms into temporal regions, but departs by decoupling the dense-sampling/frame-selection step into a separate lightweight draft model rather than keeping all visual tokens in the target model's context. The RL training follows GRPO (Shao et al. 2024) and draws conceptual inspiration from Speculative Thinking (Yang et al. 2025), which delegates reasoning segments between models of different capacities, here applied to the visual perception channel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and growing bottleneck in long video MLLMs: the quadratic cost of accumulating redundant visual tokens across iterative zoom-in rounds. The 19–23% latency reduction while matching or exceeding VideoChat-R1.5 accuracy is practically meaningful for deployment, and the SpecTemp-80K dataset with dual-level annotations provides a reusable training resource. However, the evaluation is limited to the Qwen2.5-VL family (7B/3B), videos up to ~17 minutes, and a maximum of 3–4 iterations; whether the draft-verify gap widens or narrows at larger model scales or for hour-long videos remains open. The reliance on GPT-4o for trajectory generation also raises questions about annotation quality at scale.
