## Problem

Long video understanding requires processing extremely long token sequences (e.g., ~2.7M tokens for a 1-hour video at 1 fps), which is infeasible for Transformer-based MLLMs due to quadratic attention cost and limited context windows. While hybrid Mamba-Transformer backbones offer linear-time efficiency, no prior work has explored how to compress redundant vision tokens *within* such hybrid LLMs, and it remains unclear how information flows between vision and text tokens in these architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263302/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Videos are sampled at 1 fps with each frame resized to 384×384 and encoded by SigLIP into 768 tokens, then compressed to 16 tokens per frame via ToMe in the projector. During training, videos longer than 256 frames are uniformly subsampled; during evaluation, at most the first 256 frames are used. With TransV enabled, the model extends its capacity from ~5K to over 10K frames (i.e., ~1.7 hours of video), producing multiple-choice answers, temporal boundary pairs, or free-form detailed captions depending on the task.

## Method

TimeViper builds on the Nanov2-9B hybrid backbone (27 Mamba-2 layers, 4 self-attention layers, 25 MLP layers) and introduces TransV, a lightweight in-LLM compression module that explicitly transfers vision token information into instruction tokens via gated cross-attention with a learnable scalar gate (initialized to zero). The key empirical finding motivating TransV is a vision-to-text aggregation phenomenon: attention analysis shows visual information progressively converges into instruction tokens across depth, rendering vision tokens nearly 100% redundant in deep layers. TransV is applied at two points—layer 7 with uniform 50% token dropping and layer 39 with attention-guided 90% dropping—progressively shortening the sequence while preserving task performance. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) image-text alignment on 3M pairs with compression disabled, then (2) joint fine-tuning of projector, LLM, and TransV on 4.8M multimodal instruction pairs spanning video QA, captioning, and temporal grounding.

## Results

TimeViper (with TransV) achieves 56.9 overall accuracy on VideoMME, 35.6 on LVBench, 37.9 mIoU on Charades-STA, and 39.1 on VDC, while processing over 10K frames. Without TransV, the base model scores 58.8 on VideoMME and 40.5 mIoU on Charades. The model generates 40.1% more tokens per second than Qwen3 when processing 32K input tokens (~2K frames) with batch size 32. At 4,096 frames, TransV reduces GPU memory consumption by 54.8% compared to the base TimeViper model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TimeViper with TransV outperforms Video-XL by +1.4 points on VideoMME (56.9 vs. 55.5) and exceeds the task-specific AuroraCap by +0.7 on VDC (39.7 vs. 39.0). On temporal video grounding, TimeViper achieves 40.5 mIoU on Charades, surpassing VTimeLLM-13B by +5.9 points (40.5 vs. 34.6) despite using only SigLIP positional embeddings without explicit MRoPE timestamp modeling. Against proprietary models, TimeViper beats Gemini-1.5-Pro on LVBench (35.5 vs. 33.1) but trails on VideoMME (58.8 vs. 75.0) and VDC (39.7 vs. 43.1). Compared to strong Transformer-based baselines like Qwen2.5-VL (65.1 VideoMME) and VideoChat-Flash (64.0 VideoMME), TimeViper lags by 6–8 points, though it matches or exceeds them on Charades (40.5 vs. 43.6/48.4) and VDC (39.7 vs. 41.6/–). The concurrent Nanov2-VL, trained on 6× more data (46.7M vs. 7.8M samples), serves as an upper bound, achieving 66.0 on VideoMME vs. TimeViper's 58.8, confirming the scaling potential of hybrid architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeViper builds on the emerging family of hybrid Mamba-Transformer LLMs (specifically Nanov2/Nemotron Nano 2) as its backbone, combining the O(n) efficiency of Mamba-2 state-space layers with the expressivity of self-attention. For vision token compression, it extends the projector-level token merging (ToMe) approach used by VideoChat-Flash and Apollo, but departs from prior work by introducing TransV, the first in-LLM token compression module designed for hybrid architectures. Prior internal compression methods (PDrop, Video-XL, VoCoLLaMA) were developed exclusively for Transformer backbones and rely on attention-score-guided dropping; TransV instead uses gated cross-attention (borrowed from Flamingo) to explicitly transfer vision information into instruction tokens, a mechanism that the authors show is more compatible with hybrid architectures than with pure Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The discovery of vision-to-text information aggregation in hybrid MLLMs provides a new interpretability lens for understanding how state-space and attention layers divide labor, and the finding that vision tokens become nearly 100% redundant in deep layers opens the door to aggressive compression strategies for hybrid models. Practically, TransV enables a 7B model to process 10K+ frames with 54.8% memory reduction, making hour-long video understanding feasible on consumer hardware. Limitations include the lack of ViT fine-tuning (cited as a computational constraint), reliance on only 7.8M training samples versus 46.7M for the concurrent Nanov2-VL, and the modest (0.6–1.3 point) performance cost of compression. Open questions include whether the vision-to-text aggregation phenomenon generalizes to larger hybrid models, whether TransV can be made adaptive per-frame rather than uniform, and how these findings transfer to other SSM variants beyond Mamba-2.
