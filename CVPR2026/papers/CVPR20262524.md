## Problem

Existing pose-driven character animation methods require spatially-aligned reference-pose pairs with matched skeletal structures, and fail catastrophically when the reference image and driving video differ in scale, body-part coverage, or facial geometry. This misalignment causes identity loss, distorted body shapes, and pose overfitting, limiting practical use to carefully curated aligned inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262524/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-layout reference image (which may be partially visible or at a different scale than the driving content) and a driving video's 2D pose keypoint sequence. At inference, the reference is pose-guided translated—resized via inter-shoulder/inter-ear distance estimation and zero-padded to the driving resolution—producing a masked input triplet (adjusted reference, padding mask, pose sequence). The output is an animated video at the driving resolution that completes occluded regions and follows the driving motion, and the same framework also produces pose-transferred images at up to 944×624 resolution.

## Method

The core insight is reformulating training as a self-supervised outpainting task: face-centered random masking simulates spatial mismatches, forcing the model to hallucinate occluded regions and learn generation from diverse layouts. A dedicated Reference Extractor (M text-free DiT blocks operating in parallel with the denoising backbone) extracts multi-level identity features from occluded references, injected via Hybrid Reference Fusion Attention—a cross-attention layer using 3D RoPE with frame index fixed to zero for the reference, preventing absolute frame-position coupling and enabling both image and video generation. Identity-Robust Pose Control decouples facial appearance from skeletal structure by perturbing only facial keypoints during training (breaking identity-skeleton coupling) and concatenating the unmodified reference pose with the driving pose through a self-attention block to stabilize training. For long video, a Token Replace strategy encodes the last five frames of each segment as context tokens that replace the first two latent frames of the next segment during denoising, ensuring seamless cross-segment transitions. The full system is built on the Wan2.1 DiT backbone, trained with Rectified Flow in three progressive stages (appearance-only → pose-conditioned → token-replace).

## Results

On the TikTok benchmark, One-to-All-14B achieves the best LPIPS (0.254), FID (50.49), and FID-VID (13.93) among all ~14B models, while One-to-All-1.3B leads on PSNR (17.75), SSIM (0.788), and LPIPS (0.269) among ~1.3B models. On the Cartoon dataset, One-to-All-14B achieves the lowest FID (50.07), FID-VID (15.07), and FVD (403.47) across all compared methods. On DeepFashion image pose transfer, One-to-All-1.3B attains the best FID (6.85 at 512×352; 6.92 at 944×624) and LPIPS (0.249; 0.285), outperforming CFLD and MCLD on perceptual quality metrics at both resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among ~1.3B models, One-to-All-1.3B substantially outperforms MimicMotion, StableAnimator, and Animate-X on PSNR (+2.5 dB over the best baseline), SSIM, and LPIPS on both TikTok and Cartoon, though Animate-X achieves a lower FID (52.96 vs. 74.96 on TikTok), suggesting better distributional realism at the cost of pixel-level fidelity. Among ~14B models, One-to-All-14B beats UniAnimate-DiT on LPIPS (0.254 vs. 0.265), FID (50.49 vs. 55.32), and FID-VID (13.93 vs. 17.42) on TikTok, and dominates on Cartoon across FID, FID-VID, and FVD; however, UniAnimate-DiT retains a slight edge on PSNR (19.07 vs. 18.07) and SSIM (0.816 vs. 0.812) on TikTok. Wan-Animate achieves the lowest FVD on TikTok (282.86 vs. 297.94) but is clearly outperformed by One-to-All-14B on LPIPS, FID, and FID-VID. On DeepFashion image pose transfer, One-to-All-1.3B achieves the best FID and LPIPS at both 512×352 and 944×624 resolutions, while CFLD and MCLD marginally lead on PSNR/SSIM—though the paper notes that One-to-All produces visibly sharper facial details despite slightly lower pixel-level scores. A user study against Wan-Animate on 100 misaligned cases confirms a strong preference for One-to-All in identity preservation (72.4% vs. 16.1% for seen-region fidelity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiT-based video generation lineage (Wan2.1 backbone, Rectified Flow training) and the pose-driven character animation line (Animate Anyone, MimicMotion, StableAnimator, UniAnimate, Wan-Animate), all of which assume spatially aligned reference-driving pairs. Its key conceptual departure is reformulating training as a self-supervised outpainting task with face-centered random masking, which forces the model to learn generation from occluded/misaligned references rather than relying on perfect alignment. It also unifies two previously separate tasks—aligned image-to-video animation and unaligned image-to-image pose transfer—into a single framework, whereas prior methods (e.g., CFLD, MCLD for images; UniAnimate, Wan-Animate for video) address only one setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By solving the misalignment problem, this work removes a major practical barrier to deploying pose-driven animation in real-world pipelines where reference images and driving videos rarely share identical framing or scale. The outpainting-based training reformulation is a clean, reusable idea that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks with partial inputs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D pose or non-human characters, whether the 2D keypoint representation remains a bottleneck for complex motions, and whether the multi-stage training protocol can be simplified. The method still depends on accurate pose extraction and retargeting for body-level control, leaving a residual dependency on upstream tools.
