## Problem

Existing EEG-to-video reconstruction methods produce inconsistent results across subjects due to inter-subject EEG distribution shifts caused by physiological differences and acquisition variability. Prior approaches either assign subject-specific encoders (incurring high storage and compute costs), lack explicit modeling of brain-region functional specialization and multi-scale neural dynamics, or fail to generalize to entirely new subjects without costly retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260807/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw EEG signals (B×C×T: batch × electrode channels × time steps) as input and produces a reconstructed video as output. During training, EEG is aligned to CLIP text embeddings (77×768) derived from BLIP-generated descriptions and to VAE video latents (H/8 × W/8). At inference, the EEG latent sequence substitutes for random noise in the SparseCtrl T2V model, while EEG text embeddings and a keyframe (generated via an I2I model from the first-frame EEG latent) jointly condition video generation.

## Method

SAM-Net comprises four components: (1) an HRT Encoder that partitions EEG into five anatomical brain regions (Frontal, Parietal, Central, Temporal, Occipital) with region-specific 1D CNN extractors, then applies multi-scale temporal attention (1D convolutions at kernel sizes 5, 11, 21 followed by stacked transformer layers with learnable queries) to capture motion events at different timescales; (2) a Centered-progressive Subject Adversarial (C-SA) mechanism that identifies the most representative 'central' subject, then iteratively adds the nearest remaining subject while training a subject classifier through a Gradient Reversal Layer to force subject-invariant representations; (3) a New2Source Mapper that first trains a Source2New model to simulate new-subject EEG from known subjects, then trains New2Source to map the combined simulated+real new-subject data back to the known-subject distribution; (4) a keyframe-guided generation pipeline where the first-frame EEG latent is decoded to a blurry structural anchor, refined by an I2I model with text embeddings into a keyframe, and the full EEG latent sequence plus embeddings drive SparseCtrl to produce the final video.

## Results

On the full 40-class SEED-DV dataset, SAM-Net achieves a cross-subject (CS) video-based 40-way semantic accuracy of 0.291 and frame-based 40-way accuracy of 0.301, with SSIM of 0.279 (best subject). In the single-subject (SS) 40-class setting, the method reaches 0.320 video-based and 0.307 frame-based 40-way accuracy with 0.284 SSIM, outperforming DynaMind/EEG2Video (0.284 / 0.241 / 0.280). For new-subject generalization (last 5 unseen subjects), the method attains 0.142 video-based and 0.137 frame-based 40-way accuracy with 0.254 SSIM under the most challenging 40-class setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two EEG-to-Video baselines: DynaMind [16] and EEG2Video [17], both of which only report single-subject (SS) results. In the 40-class SS setting, SAM-Net's best cross-subject configuration (CS Best) surpasses both baselines on all four semantic metrics (0.291 vs. 0.284 video 40-way; 0.301 vs. 0.241 frame 40-way) and matches SSIM (0.279 vs. 0.280). The critical distinction is that DynaMind and EEG2Video do not evaluate cross-subject generalization at all, whereas SAM-Net's CS results demonstrate that the C-SA mechanism and HRT Encoder enable meaningful reconstruction when test subjects differ from training subjects. In the 10-class CS setting, SAM-Net (Best) reaches 0.312 video 40-way and 0.388 frame 40-way, exceeding DynaMind's SS 40-way scores (0.394 video, 0.308 frame), indicating that cross-subject performance with fewer classes can rival or exceed single-subject performance. For new-subject generalization (Table 2), no baseline is directly compared, but SAM-Net achieves 0.142/0.137 40-way accuracy with only 15% of new-subject EEG data, demonstrating the New2Source Mapper's effectiveness in a data-scarce regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEG-to-video reconstruction line (EEG2Video, DynaMind) by explicitly addressing cross-subject and new-subject generalization, a dimension those prior methods overlook. For video generation, it repurposes SparseCtrl's keyframe-guided sparse control architecture, substituting EEG latents for random noise and EEG-CLIP embeddings for text prompts. The C-SA mechanism draws on progressive domain adaptation and gradient reversal from cross-subject EEG emotion recognition, while the HRT Encoder introduces brain-region-segmented spatial perception and multi-scale temporal attention that prior EEG-to-video methods (which use generic CNN or masked-reconstruction encoders) lack. The New2Source Mapper departs from fine-tuning-based adaptation by performing distributional alignment at the encoding stage only, avoiding perturbation of known-subject representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-subject EEG-to-video is a practical bottleneck for real-world brain-computer interfaces, where retraining per subject is infeasible; this paper provides a unified framework that handles both known cross-subject and unseen new-subject scenarios without per-subject fine-tuning. However, evaluation is confined to a single dataset (SEED-DV, 20 subjects, 40 categories), and new-subject performance drops substantially (40-way video-based accuracy falls from ~0.29 cross-subject to ~0.14 new-subject), suggesting the mapping approach still struggles with large distribution shifts. Open questions include scalability to larger subject pools, applicability to other EEG paradigms (e.g., naturalistic viewing without discrete categories), and whether the keyframe-guided generation paradigm generalizes to longer or more complex video sequences.
