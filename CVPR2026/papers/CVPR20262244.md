## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models are constrained to fixed resolutions because conventional tokenizers produce token counts that grow linearly with image size, causing inference FLOPs to scale quadratically via self-attention. While 1D Transformer-based tokenizers offer stronger compression, they still assume fixed training grids and fail to generalize across arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios, leaving AR models far behind diffusion models in production flexibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262244/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, VibeToken-Gen takes a class label and a target (H, W) resolution as conditioning and produces a full RGB image at that arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio using only 64 discrete tokens. The VibeToken tokenizer itself accepts any-resolution RGB images and encodes them into a user-controllable sequence of 32–256 discrete tokens (via MVQ with 8 codebooks of size 4096), and can decode back to a different target resolution than the input, enabling native 4× super-resolution without a separate upscaler.

## Method

VibeToken is a 1D Transformer tokenizer made resolution-agnostic through four mechanisms: (1) a learned 32×32 grid of positional embeddings that is bilinearly resized to match any input patch lattice, (2) adaptive patch embedding where a single k_max=32 kernel is differentially resized to support variable patch sizes k∈{8,12,16,32}, (3) an adaptive decoder that predicts pixels at a fixed 4× intermediate resolution then applies a learned 2D-Conv downscaling layer to reach the target (H,W), and (4) dynamic-length training where both encoder and decoder are trained on uniformly sampled latent lengths L∈[32,256] with no padding. VibeToken-Gen is a LlamaGen-style class-conditioned AR model that additionally conditions on the target resolution via an MLP, trained on mixed resolutions (256²–512²) and aspect ratios with L∈{64,128,256}. Because the token count is decoupled from pixel dimensions, inference FLOPs remain constant at 179 GFLOPs regardless of output resolution, versus ~11 TFLOPs for LlamaGen at 1024×1024.

## Results

VibeToken-Gen(XXL) achieves 3.54 gFID at 1024×1024 using only 64 tokens with a constant 179 GFLOPs inference cost, outperforming the diffusion SOTA NiT-XL (5.87 gFID, 1024 tokens) by 33% in gFID while being 2.35× faster (0.46 s vs 1.08 s). The VibeToken-LL tokenizer attains 0.40 rFID at 256², 0.51 at 512², and 2.40 at 1024², matching or surpassing prior 1D tokenizers at 256² while being the only method to natively support arbitrary resolutions and dynamic token lengths (32–256). Across all tested aspect ratios and resolutions from 256×256 to 1024×1024, VibeToken-Gen(XXL) maintains an average gFID of 5.53, compared to 6.05 for NiT-XL and 23.23 for EDM2-L.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion baselines, VibeToken-Gen(XXL) decisively outperforms EDM2-L (avg gFID 5.53 vs 23.23) and FiT-v2-XL (5.53 vs 124.32) across all high-resolution settings, and beats NiT-XL on 6 of 8 aspect-ratio/resolution combinations (e.g., 3.54 vs 5.87 at 1024², 4.01 vs 4.47 at 768×512), though NiT retains a slight edge at 512² (1.80 vs 3.69) and 512×1024 (12.23 vs 11.91 is actually VibeToken-Gen winning). Among AR baselines, VibeToken-Gen is the only model supporting arbitrary resolutions; at fixed 256² it trails specialist models like RAR-XXL (1.48 FID) and LlamaGen-XXL (2.34 FID) due to shared generalist training budget, but at 1024² it achieves 3.54 gFID with 64 tokens versus LlamaGen's ~11T FLOPs requirement. For tokenizer reconstruction, VibeToken-LL (0.40 rFID at 256²) is competitive with UniTok (0.33) and IBQ (0.97) while uniquely supporting both dynamic resolution and dynamic token length (32–256), whereas TiTok, VAR, ImageFolder, DetailFlow, and UniTok are fixed-resolution, and FlexTok/One-D-Piece support dynamic tokens but not dynamic resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1D Transformer tokenizer paradigm established by TiTok (Yu et al., 2024) by making it resolution-agnostic through dynamic grid positional embeddings (inspired by NaViT and ViTAR), adaptive patch embedding (inspired by FlexiViT), and dynamic-length training. The AR generator (VibeToken-Gen) directly adapts the LlamaGen architecture (Li et al., 2024) but decouples inference compute from image resolution by conditioning on a fixed-length token sequence. The key departure from prior 1D tokenizers (TiTok, One-D-Piece, DetailFlow) is that they assume fixed training grids and cannot generalize to out-of-distribution resolutions, whereas VibeToken natively supports arbitrary resolutions and aspect ratios with a user-controllable token budget of 32–256.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 63× reduction in inference FLOPs (179 GFLOPs constant vs. ~11 TFLOPs for LlamaGen at 1024²) makes AR image generation practically viable for production workloads where compute budgets are fixed, addressing the primary reason AR models have lagged diffusion in deployment. The finding that 64 tokens suffice for 1024×1024 generation (gFID 3.94, beating NiT's 5.87) challenges the assumption that high-resolution synthesis requires proportionally more tokens. Limitations include class-conditional-only evaluation on ImageNet-1k, a small generalist-training degradation versus single-resolution specialists, and untested scaling to text-to-image or video settings. Open questions include whether the resolution-agnostic tokenization strategy transfers to continuous-token AR models and whether the constant-compute property holds under stronger conditioning signals.
