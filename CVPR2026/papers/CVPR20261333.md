## Problem

Reconstructing well-lit dynamic scenes from low-light video inputs remains largely underexplored. Existing low-light 3D reconstruction methods are restricted to static scenes, while dynamic scene reconstruction methods assume well-lit inputs. The combination introduces compounding ambiguities from motion-induced shadows, self-occlusion/disocclusion, and view-dependent brightness shifts that make the problem highly ill-posed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261333/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N low-light sRGB video frames at 450×800 resolution, corresponding camera poses (rotation, translation, intrinsics), and a sparse 3D point cloud as input, requiring no exposure metadata, motion masks, or well-lit reference images. It outputs a 3D dynamic Gaussian scene that can render well-lit novel spatiotemporal views and depth maps in real time (>30 fps). The Brightness Attenuation Features and BAFE-Net are used exclusively during training for self-supervision and are discarded at inference, keeping the rendering pipeline lightweight.

## Method

L²DGS decomposes each 3D Gaussian into a view- and time-dependent illumination component (encoded via Spherical Harmonics) and a view-time-invariant reflectance component, with the rendered color being their Hadamard product. An Occlusion-Disocclusion Network (OCD-Net) predicts per-Gaussian time-dependent scale factors a_j(t) that modulate the SH illumination coefficients to capture intensity changes from occlusion and disocclusion caused by object motion. For self-supervision, per-Gaussian Brightness Attenuation Features (BAFs) are rasterized and enhanced by a BAFE-Net to synthesize a low-light image from the estimated well-lit image via element-wise exponentiation, which is then matched to the input. Training is fully self-supervised with a composite loss combining photometric fidelity (L1 + gamma-decayed SSIM), an exposure regularizer on reflectance, a Signal Amplification Regularizer that maximizes the illumination map, a Scene-Adaptive Structural Regularizer aligning BAF gradients with reflectance and illumination gradients, and an edge-aware depth smoothness term. The dynamic motion field follows the Hexplane 6-plane representation with MLP heads for position, rotation, and scale, trained in two stages (coarse then fine) over ~23,000 iterations.

## Results

On synthetic low-light dynamic scenes (Mochi, Handwavy, Apple, Creeper), L²DGS achieves Overall PSNR of 17.61, 11.72, 13.31, and 13.96 respectively, with corresponding SSIM of 0.77, 0.58, 0.63, and 0.57. In dynamic regions specifically, L²DGS reaches 21.00 PSNR on Mochi and 15.49 PSNR on Creeper, substantially outperforming the next-best method (Luminance-GS at 14.06 and 11.48). In the user study on 12 real L²DyV scenes with 62 participants, L²DGS received the highest binary-choice preference (72–94%) and highest average ratings (4.05–4.36 out of 5) across all five perceptual criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L²DGS outperforms all six compared baselines across all four synthetic scenes and all three evaluation regions (Dynamic, Static, Overall). The largest margins are against 4DGS [39], the dynamic GS baseline without low-light handling, where L²DGS gains over 10 dB PSNR (e.g., 17.61 vs. 6.65 on Mochi Overall), confirming that standard dynamic GS fails catastrophically under low light. Among low-light-specific methods, L²DGS beats the closest competitor Luminance-GS [4] by 3.1 dB PSNR on Mochi (17.61 vs. 14.51) and by 0.8–1.0 dB on the remaining scenes, while also achieving substantially lower LPIPS (0.16 vs. 0.32 on Mochi). The NeRF-based baselines AlethNeRF [3] and LLNeRF [34] trail by 2.7–7.4 dB PSNR and show particularly poor dynamic-region performance (e.g., AlethNeRF drops to 4.89 PSNR on Apple Dynamic). Lo-Gaussian [47] produced entirely black images and was excluded from quantitative comparison. In the user study, L²DGS received 75–94% binary-choice preference and 4.05–4.36/5 average ratings, compared to the next-best method LLNeRF at 23–26% preference and 2.83–3.05/5, indicating a clear perceptual advantage in brightness, structure preservation, and artifact minimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 4DGS (Wu et al., CVPR 2024) and Hexplane (Cao & Johnson, CVPR 2023) for dynamic Gaussian motion modeling, but departs from the standard GS paradigm of assigning view-dependent color to each Gaussian by instead decomposing per-Gaussian appearance into a view-time-dependent illumination channel and a view-time-invariant reflectance channel. The closest prior work, Lighting up NeRF (Wang et al., ICCV 2023), performs a similar view-dependent/independent decomposition but is restricted to static scenes and lacks any temporal modeling. The self-supervision strategy of forward-modeling a low-light image from the estimated well-lit scene (via BAFs and BAFE-Net) contrasts with prior low-light GS methods that either require camera metadata (Gaussian-in-Dark, DarkGS), well-lit reference frames (LL-Gaussian), or operate on RAW images (NeRF-in-Dark, Bright-NeRF).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to jointly perform dynamic 3D scene reconstruction and low-light enhancement in a single self-supervised pipeline, and it introduces the L²DyV dataset—the first real-world low-light dynamic video benchmark—which fills a notable gap for the community. The forward-modeling self-supervision design (synthesizing low-light from well-lit rather than enhancing low-light to well-lit) is a clean architectural choice that avoids noise amplification and enables real-time inference (>30 FPS) by discarding BAFs at test time. Key limitations include the requirement for accurate camera poses and a 3D point cloud (challenging in extreme darkness), the absence of ground-truth well-lit references for real data (relying on user studies), and the synthetic evaluation data being generated by Led-Net, which itself produces artifacts. Open questions include whether the illumination-reflectance decomposition generalizes to scenes with multiple light sources or rapidly changing illumination, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object dynamics.
