## Problem

2D vision foundation models like DINOv3, while powerful for semantic point characterisation, lack true 3D geometric understanding and exhibit systematic failures on partial observations such as left-right mismatches and boundary artefacts. Existing partial shape matching descriptors (HKS, WKS, SHOT) are fragile to missing geometry or biased by extrinsic spatial position, and no prior work has injected 3D geometry awareness into 2D foundation features for partial, non-rigid, untextured shape matching.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D triangle meshes (full or partial) and renders them into 512×512×3 RGB images from multiple camera viewpoints (9 views for full shapes, 4 for partial during training; 49 at inference). Pixel-wise features from the frozen DINOv3+LoRA encoder are backprojected onto the mesh by averaging all observing pixels per vertex, yielding 768-dimensional vertex-wise descriptors. These descriptors support nearest-neighbour correspondence search or plug directly into downstream matching pipelines (DPFM, ULRSSM, EchoMatch) that expect per-vertex feature vectors.

## Method

GeoLoRA freezes a DINOv3 ViT-B/16 backbone and attaches low-rank adaptation modules (rank 16, α=32) to all attention layers, trained in a siamese fashion so the same LoRA weights process both full and partial shape renderings. Partial shapes are generated from full templates via three synthetic procedures—plane cuts, random holes, and ray-based camera partiality—providing ground-truth correspondences without any labelled data. The key loss is a Geodesic-Aware PointInfoNCE: the standard contrastive denominator is reweighted by the geodesic distance matrix on the full shape (shifted by 0.5), so that vertices far apart on the surface are penalised more heavily than nearby ones, giving the network an explicit geometric cue to disambiguate symmetric regions. Training uses only synthetic full shapes from the training split, and the resulting features transfer to unseen partial observations, non-isometric deformations, and real-world scans without further fine-tuning.

## Results

GeoLoRA achieves a mean geodesic error of 5.57 on BECOS (partial-to-full), a 3.6× improvement over DINOv3 (19.95) and a 10.5× improvement over WKS (58.35). Across all seven benchmark datasets, GeoLoRA consistently outperforms every baseline, with particularly large gains on PFAUST-M (2.88 vs. 11.36 for DINOv3) and PFAUST-H (3.33 vs. 13.82). When integrated into SOTA matching pipelines (ULRSSM, DPFM, EchoMatch), GeoLoRA features reduce geodesic error by 20–56% compared to DINOv3 features, and improve overlap prediction IoU by up to 6.2 points on PSMAL (85.41 vs. 79.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoLoRA decisively outperforms all handcrafted descriptors (WKS, HKS) by 5–10× in geodesic error across every dataset, confirming that spectral signatures are fragile under partiality. Among learning-based baselines, GeoLoRA beats Diff3f by 2–4× (e.g., 5.57 vs. 25.61 on BECOS P2F) and DINOv2 by 2–4×, while surpassing DINOv3 by 2–4× on all datasets (e.g., 5.57 vs. 19.95 on BECOS, 2.88 vs. 11.36 on PFAUST-M). In the matching-pipeline integration experiments (Tables 2–3), GeoLoRA features reduce geodesic error in ULRSSM from 26.97 to 11.62 on BECOS and from 5.19 to 2.29 on PFAUST-H, and in DPFM from 23.16 to 13.38 on BECOS. For partial-to-partial matching, GeoLoRA improves EchoMatch's geodesic error from 9.74 to 5.55 and IoU from 67.07 to 71.27 on BECOS, and DPFM's error from 11.25 to 8.91 on PSMAL. The one notable exception is CP2P24, where GeoLoRA (12.46) is slightly worse than DINOv3 (18.55) in raw feature quality but comparable in the supervised DPFM pipeline (3.48 vs. 3.89), suggesting the supervised overlap predictor masks the feature advantage on this near-isometric dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DINOv3 vision transformer as a frozen backbone and the multi-view rendering/backprojection pipeline from Diff3f to extract vertex-wise features from 3D meshes. It departs from prior partial shape matching work (which relies on handcrafted descriptors like HKS/WKS or extrinsic spatial coordinates) by instead adapting 2D foundation features through a lightweight LoRA module trained with a novel geodesic-aware contrastive loss derived from PointInfoNCE. The key conceptual departure is that no prior work injects explicit 3D geometric supervision (geodesic distances on the surface) into a frozen 2D foundation model for partial, non-rigid, untextured shape matching; earlier attempts at 3D grounding of 2D features were limited to textured rigid objects or 2D-only augmentations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Partial shape matching is a critical real-world problem (occlusions, incomplete scans, reconstruction artefacts) where existing descriptors and even advanced foundation features fail, and this paper provides the first self-supervised, geometry-aware solution that substantially closes the gap. The geodesic-aware loss is a simple but effective mechanism that also improves chirality (left-right) disambiguation, suggesting partial-to-full matching can serve as a useful auxiliary task for enhancing 3D understanding in foundation models more broadly. Limitations include inheriting DINOv3's upright-orientation bias and the current focus on humanoid/animal shapes; extending to external clutter, man-made objects, and upside-down poses remains open. The LoRA-on-frozen-backbone paradigm demonstrated here is a lightweight, broadly applicable recipe for injecting domain-specific 3D knowledge into large pretrained 2D models.
