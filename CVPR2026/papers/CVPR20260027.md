## Problem

LiDAR-based 3D human motion capture suffers from sparse, noisy, and heavily occluded point clouds that cause existing methods to produce jittery or completely failed pose predictions, especially under long-term occlusion of critical joints. Prior approaches primarily exploit action priors from specific point cloud patterns, limiting their ability to handle missing frames; this paper instead leverages the intrinsic kinematic properties of motion itself to bridge observation gaps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T LiDAR point cloud frames (each with N 3D points) as input and produces per-frame 3D joint positions (K joints × 3 coordinates) and SMPL pose parameters. Per-frame point features are extracted via a PointNet++ encoder into a fixed-dimensional observation vector, which is then processed jointly with multi-level motion token embeddings in a single Transformer forward pass. The predicted joint locations are converted to SMPL parameters through an STGCN-based inverse kinematic solver, and forward kinematics yields the final mesh-level joint positions.

## Method

BMLiCap represents human motion as C¹-continuous cubic Bézier chains fitted to joint trajectories, then applies a Trajectory-Aware Degradation (TAD) strategy that progressively reduces control points at multiple temporal scales while solving for optimal control-point lengths to preserve motion dynamics. A Time-scale Motion Transformer (TMT) with a block-wise causal mask predicts motion curves at all temporal scales simultaneously in one forward pass, allowing coarse-level trends to guide finer-scale refinement. A Multi-level Motion Aggregator (MMA) then progressively fuses the multi-scale predictions by upsampling coarser curves via their Bézier parameters and blending with finer predictions through MLPs. Training uses multi-level Bézier representation loss plus IK parameter and forward-kinematics losses, enabling the model to reconstruct temporally coherent motion even when up to 50% of input frames are missing.

## Results

BMLiCap achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four LiDAR-based motion capture benchmarks. On FreeMotion, the 32-frame variant (BMLiCap†) reduces JPE to 47.2 mm, VPE to 59.0 mm, and Acceleration Error to 22.5 cm/s², improving over the previous best (LiveHPS++) by 14.7, 16.3, and 31.7 respectively. On SLOPER4D, BMLiCap† achieves an AE of 13.6 cm/s² versus 43.4 for LiveHPS++, a 3.2× reduction in temporal jitter. On NoiseMotion, BMLiCap matches LiveHPS++ on JPE/VPE (34.0/42.8 mm) while cutting AE from 34.8 to 24.1 cm/s².

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMLiCap outperforms all seven baselines on every metric where they overlap. Against the strongest prior method LiveHPS++ [55], BMLiCap wins decisively on FreeMotion (49.6 vs 61.9 JPE; 27.1 vs 54.2 AE) and SLOPER4D (39.7 vs 42.7 JPE; 22.3 vs 43.4 AE), while matching it on NoiseMotion JPE/VPE (34.0/42.8) but halving the AE (24.1 vs 34.8). Against LiveHPS [56], gains are more modest on LiDARHuman26M (70.1 vs 71.9 JPE) but large on FreeMotion (49.6 vs 69.4 JPE) and SLOPER4D (39.7 vs 53.4 JPE). The older LiDARCap [34] baseline is surpassed by 9.2–36.7 JPE and 17.8–56.4 AE across datasets. Notably, the 32-frame variant BMLiCap† further extends the lead, achieving 13.6 AE on SLOPER4D (vs 43.4 for LiveHPS++), though on NoiseMotion the shorter default window is preferred due to viewport jumps in that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LiDAR-based human motion capture (LiDARCap, LiveHPS, LiveHPS++) and progressive/coarse-to-fine reconstruction (CARP, visual autoregressive modeling). It departs from prior LiDAR motion capture methods that learn directly from incomplete point-cloud features by instead modeling motion through a Bezier-curve parameterization with a trajectory-aware degradation strategy, drawing on the kinematic smoothness properties of Bezier curves explored in motion inbetweening. Unlike iterative progressive methods that are slow at inference, BMLiCap achieves coarse-to-fine reconstruction in a single forward pass via a block-wise causal-masked Transformer, and unlike frequency-decomposition or VQ-VAE-based representations, it preserves C¹ continuity and explicit velocity/acceleration structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically critical gap in outdoor LiDAR motion capture: severe and prolonged occlusions that cause jittery or failed pose predictions in autonomous driving and robotics. The Bezier hierarchical representation offers an interpretable, kinematically grounded mechanism for bridging observation gaps, and the 50% frame-dropout robustness test demonstrates real-world applicability. Open questions include whether the Bezier degradation generalizes to richer skeletal topologies (hands, faces), whether the approach extends to other sparse sensing modalities, and whether the single-pass Transformer can be further scaled to longer sequences without the window-length trade-offs observed on NoiseMotion.
