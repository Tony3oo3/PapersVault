## Problem

Open-vocabulary change detection (OVCD) in remote sensing requires recognizing arbitrary land-cover changes without a fixed class set, but existing methods (e.g., DynamicEarth's M-C-I) rely on CLIP-style image-text matching for category identification, which fails on fine-grained remote sensing categories due to the domain gap and the inherent ambiguity of textual features for complex land-cover semantics. The paper identifies through controlled oracle experiments that category identification errors, not change localization errors, are the primary bottleneck degrading OVCD performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260781/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a bi-temporal RGB remote sensing image pair (I1, I2) and produces per-instance change proposals, each annotated with a semantic category label. For the training-free OpenDPR, no training data is needed at inference; for OpenDPR-W, only image-level binary change labels (one bit per image pair) are used during a 10,000-iteration pre-training phase at 256×256 resolution. The prototype set is constructed entirely offline using LLM-generated prompts and DiffusionSat-synthesized satellite images, so the online pipeline requires only the test image pair and the precomputed prototype bank.

## Method

OpenDPR reformulates OVCD as a two-stage pipeline: (1) SAM generates class-agnostic masks on both temporal images, NMS removes duplicates, and DINOv2 feature differences (negative cosine similarity) score change proposals; (2) each proposal's DINOv2 feature is region-pooled and matched against a precomputed prototype bank via cosine similarity to assign a category. The prototype bank is built offline by prompting GPT-4 for diverse category descriptions, synthesizing images with DiffusionSat, localizing class regions with APE, extracting DINOv2 features, applying masked average pooling, and clustering with K-means to obtain K visual prototypes per class. This entirely visual-space retrieval avoids CLIP's image-text matching limitations. The optional S2C module adds a Siamese MiT-b1 encoder trained with image-level labels, where CAM-based pseudo labels are refined by intersecting them with VFM-derived change proposals (overlap ratio > γ1), and at inference S2C's binary change map filters OpenDPR's proposals to suppress false positives.

## Results

On building change detection, training-free OpenDPR achieves 44.8 IoU / 61.9 F1 on LEVIR-CD and 54.3 IoU / 70.4 F1 on WHU-CD, improving over the previous best M-C-I by 8.2% and 13.7% IoU respectively. On semantic change detection, OpenDPR (multi-class) reaches 17.5 mIoU / 27.8 mF1 on Hi-UCD mini and 28.6 mIoU / 43.7 mF1 on SECOND, surpassing M-C-I(MP) by 3.6 and 6.4 mIoU. The weakly supervised variant OpenDPR-W further lifts IoU to 48.1 (LEVIR-CD) and 68.3 (WHU-CD), outperforming the best weakly supervised baseline ACWCD by 5.9% and 4.6% IoU while using fewer trainable parameters (13.4M vs. 18.1M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenDPR decisively outperforms the closest OVCD baseline M-C-I [21] across all four datasets: +8.2% IoU on LEVIR-CD, +13.7% on WHU-CD, +3.6 mIoU on Hi-UCD mini, and +6.4 mIoU on SECOND, confirming that vision-space prototype retrieval resolves the category-identification bottleneck that limits CLIP-based identification. Among unsupervised building CD baselines, OpenDPR surpasses SCM [34] (18.8/18.6 IoU) and AnyChange-H [50] (23.0 F1) by a wide margin, and also exceeds the older classical methods (CVA, PCA-KM, CNN-CD, DSFA, DCVA, GMCD) by 20–40 IoU points. In the weakly supervised setting, S2C (13.4M params) beats ACWCD [24] (18.1M params) by 2.5% IoU on LEVIR-CD and 1.1% on WHU-CD, and OpenDPR-W extends this lead to 5.9% and 4.6% IoU over ACWCD. On semantic CD, OpenDPR also outperforms I-M-C [21] on Hi-UCD mini (14.4 vs. 9.1 mIoU per-class) and on SECOND (26.1 vs. 12.0 mIoU per-class), though both methods fail on rare categories such as pedestrian bridge. The multi-class inference mode of OpenDPR further narrows the gap with per-class inference while reducing inference time, a practical advantage not shared by M-C-I or I-M-C.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DynamicEarth [21], which introduced the OVCD task and the M-C-I pipeline (mask generation → comparison → identification) using off-the-shelf VFMs and CLIP for category recognition. The key departure is replacing CLIP's image-text matching paradigm with a vision-centric prototype retrieval mechanism: diffusion-generated images (via DiffusionSat) are used offline to construct diverse visual prototypes per category, and inference-time identification becomes a nearest-prototype search in DINOv2 feature space. The S2C module extends the standard weakly supervised CD recipe (CAM pseudo-labels + Siamese encoder) by incorporating VFM-based spatial priors (SAM proposals) to refine noisy pseudo-labels, drawing on the broader trend of leveraging foundation models for dense prediction tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that category identification—not change localization—is the primary OVCD bottleneck reframes where effort should be directed in this task, and the training-free prototype retrieval offers a practical, annotation-free solution for remote sensing scenarios where dense labels are scarce. However, absolute performance remains low (mIoU ~14–17% on semantic CD benchmarks), and the method fails entirely on rare categories (e.g., pedestrian bridge), revealing that diffusion-generated prototypes inherit semantic biases and cannot cover the full diversity of real land covers. Open questions include whether scaling diffusion models or incorporating real-image prototypes would close the gap for rare classes, and whether the visual-space retrieval paradigm generalizes beyond the specific VFM choices (DINOv2 + SAM) used here.
