## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal fusion methods struggle to recover dynamic land-cover changes due to large scale discrepancies between coarse and fine satellite images. Purely data-driven deep learning approaches produce artifacts, fail to model temporal spectral inconsistencies, and cannot exploit high-frequency reference details to capture semantic transitions driven by phenology or land-cover change.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263783/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a coarse (MODIS, ~250–1000 m) image at the target time, paired fine (Landsat, 30 m) and coarse images at two reference times, plus scalar noise-variance and residual-control coefficients. The coarse target image is not used as a direct spatial prior but is embedded as a residual term in the forward diffusion process, constraining the solution space. Inference uses a DDIM sampler with 50 steps to iteratively reconstruct the 30 m fine-resolution multispectral image at the target timestamp.

## Method

SA-STF reformulates residual denoising diffusion (RDDM) into a single-network architecture by introducing an intermediate variable F₁ᵗ that links noise and residual estimation through a first-order Taylor expansion at the feature level. A Taylor-inspired decoder explicitly decouples the residual component η_res·x_res from the noisy feature f_θ(x_t) via a deep residual removal module, then a denoising decoder suppresses the remaining noise. A Temporal Feature Alignment (TFA) module projects multi-temporal fine-resolution features into a unified latent space using pixel-level linear regression with cross-attention adaptation to bridge the coarse-to-fine scale gap. A Semantic-Adaptive Fusion (SAF) module computes Euclidean similarity masks to distinguish invariant from changed regions, then adaptively selects the most semantically matched reference for high-frequency detail transfer in dynamic areas. Training combines L1 reconstruction loss, VGG-16 perceptual loss, and a cosine warm-up-scheduled temporal alignment loss over 200 epochs.

## Results

SA-STF achieves the best performance on all five metrics (CC, SSIM, ERGAS, RMSE, SAM) across all three benchmark datasets. On the dynamic LGC flooding scenario, it reaches CC 0.9360, SSIM 0.9440, ERGAS 0.6751, RMSE 0.0169, and SAM 0.0531. On the most challenging AHB dataset with diverse land-cover types and seasonal phenology, SA-STF attains CC 0.8729, SSIM 0.8962, ERGAS 0.8620, RMSE 0.0283, and SAM 0.0709, outperforming the next-best method (SwinSTFM) by 0.7% in CC and 0.0096 in RMSE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SA-STF outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest margins on the AHB dataset where dynamic land-cover transitions are most severe. On CIA (short time span, limited changes), the traditional ESTARFM is surprisingly competitive (CC 0.8823 vs. SA-STF's 0.8950), while deep learning methods like STFMamba degrade noticeably (CC 0.8172) due to the resolution gap. On LGC, STFMamba is the closest competitor (CC 0.9284 vs. 0.9360), but SA-STF still leads by 0.76% in CC and 0.0034 in RMSE. On AHB, the advantage widens substantially: SA-STF surpasses SwinSTFM (the next-best) by 0.71% in CC and 0.0043 in RMSE, while STFMamba collapses (CC 0.8612, RMSE 0.0435) and ESTARFM fails to capture semantic changes (CC 0.7654). Generative baselines (STFDiff, MLFF-GAN) produce plausible textures but suffer from spectral distortion in change regions, reflected in their higher SAM values (e.g., STFDiff SAM 0.0708 on LGC vs. SA-STF's 0.0531).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Residual Denoising Diffusion Model (RDDM, CVPR 2024), which introduced a dual-network architecture to independently estimate noise and residual components; SA-STF reformulates this into a single-network pipeline by introducing an intermediate variable F₁ᵗ and using a first-order Taylor expansion at the feature level to decouple noise from residual. It also extends the diffusion-based STF line (STFDiff) by adding explicit physical constraints—temporal feature alignment via linear regression and semantic-adaptive fusion via similarity-based masking—that go beyond purely data-driven generative approaches. The broader STF lineage (ESTARFM, SwinSTFM, STFMamba, MLFF-GAN) is positioned as insufficient for dynamic scenes due to artifacts, spectral distortion, and insensitivity to land-cover transitions, motivating the constrained diffusion framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SA-STF addresses a practical bottleneck in remote sensing monitoring (agriculture, disaster response, urban planning) where high temporal and spatial resolution cannot be simultaneously achieved by any single satellite. The ablation revealing that TFA+SAF modules remain competitive even without the diffusion process suggests these components could serve as plug-and-play modules for other STF or image restoration pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation: for rare abrupt changes with no semantically matched reference image, the model's ability to reconstruct fine spatial details is constrained, leaving open the question of how to incorporate additional priors (e.g., external change maps or generative priors) for such edge cases.
