## Problem

Existing activation-aware pruning methods for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) treat textual and visual calibration tokens uniformly, overlooking that the two modalities exhibit fundamentally different sensitivity to pruning. The paper identifies that the textual pathway is highly sensitive to calibration source while the visual pathway is remarkably redundant, yet prior work (e.g., TAMP) still mixes both modalities in a shared, heuristic selection process without explicitly accounting for this asymmetry.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LVLM (LLaVA-NeXT 8B or Qwen2-VL 7B), a calibration set of 128 image-text pairs sampled from ShareGPT4V, and a target unstructured sparsity ratio (50% or 60%). It produces sparsified weight matrices for all linear layers in the LLM backbone's Attention and MLP blocks. The calibration set is used solely to estimate per-channel activation norms (‖X_j‖₂) for importance scoring; no weight updates or gradient computation are performed, making the procedure a single-pass post-training operation.

## Method

ATV-Pruning builds on Wanda's importance score (|W_ij|·‖X_j‖₂) but replaces the modality-agnostic calibration pool with an asymmetric one: all text tokens are retained, while only a compact, block-adaptive subset of visual tokens is included. Visual token saliency is measured by 'visual drift'—the cosine distance between a token's input and output representations within each Transformer block—and the per-block visual-token budget K is set as ⌊α·s̄·n_text⌋, where s̄ is the block-average drift and α is a global scaling factor (default 1.0). This means blocks that process visual information more intensively (mid-to-late layers) automatically receive more visual tokens for calibration. The resulting text-anchored, visually selective activation statistics yield importance scores that protect linguistic capability while tolerating high sparsity in the redundant visual pathway, achieving 94.00% average retention at 50% sparsity on LLaVA-NeXT with only 1.35× the pruning time of vanilla Wanda.

## Results

On LLaVA-NeXT 8B at 50% unstructured sparsity, ATV-Pruning achieves 94.00% average performance retention across nine multimodal benchmarks, outperforming SparseGPT (91.74%), Wanda (88.36%), and TAMP (92.67%). At the more aggressive 60% sparsity, ATV-Pruning retains 77.01% on LLaVA-NeXT and 85.65% on Qwen2-VL 7B, exceeding the next-best baselines by +0.77 and +1.86 points respectively. The method also achieves the best individual scores on GQA, MME, MMMU, OK-VQA, and VizWiz at 50% sparsity, and on MMMU, OK-VQA, SQA_img, and VizWiz at 60% sparsity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATV-Pruning consistently outperforms all three baselines across every model and sparsity setting evaluated. Against Wanda, the gains are most dramatic: +5.64 points at 50% sparsity on LLaVA-NeXT and +12.56 points at 60% sparsity, reflecting Wanda's inability to handle modality heterogeneity in its calibration pool. Against TAMP, ATV-Pruning leads by +1.33 points at 50% and +3.26 points at 60% on LLaVA-NeXT, and by +1.86 points at 60% on Qwen2-VL; TAMP's heuristic multimodal token selection is less effective than ATV-Pruning's principled text-anchored, block-adaptive strategy. Against SparseGPT, the advantage is narrower (+2.26 at 50%, +0.77 at 60% on LLaVA-NeXT, +0.35 on Qwen2-VL), but ATV-Pruning achieves these gains at only 1.35× the pruning time of Wanda versus SparseGPT's 9.04×, making it substantially more efficient. Notably, at 60% sparsity on LLaVA-NeXT, ATV-Pruning is the only method that maintains above 55% on SQA_img and above 55% on VizWiz, while Wanda collapses to 38.67 and 49.85 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATV-Pruning is built directly on Wanda's activation-aware importance scoring (|W_ij| · ||X_j||₂), but rethinks the calibration pool construction for multimodal settings. Prior LVLM pruning work (TAMP, EfficientLLaVA) either allocates sparsity budgets across blocks or selects tokens via attention/MMD heuristics while still treating text and visual tokens within a shared selection process. This paper departs by first decoupling text and visual pathways via a Mixture-of-Transformers probe to empirically demonstrate asymmetric sensitivity, then designing an asymmetric calibration strategy (all text tokens + a small block-adaptive visual subset) rather than a unified modality-agnostic pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the visual pathway tolerates 60% unstructured sparsity with near-zero degradation while the text pathway collapses under non-text calibration is practically important for anyone deploying LVLMs on resource-constrained hardware, as it suggests aggressive visual-pathway sparsification is safe. The method's 1.35× overhead over Wanda makes it a drop-in improvement for existing pruning pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only two model families (LLaVA-NeXT 8B, Qwen2-VL 7B), a single calibration set of 128 samples, and unstructured sparsity only; the authors note extension to broader LMMs (audio, depth) as future work. Whether the block-adaptive visual drift signal generalizes to video or multi-image inputs remains open.
