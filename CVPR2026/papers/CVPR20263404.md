## Problem

Existing VLM-as-a-Judge methods for image aesthetics regress a single global score, lacking the ability to reason compositionally over multiple attributes (composition, lighting, color, geometry) and to produce calibrated pairwise win probabilities. This limits discriminative reliability and prevents the model from explaining *why* one image is preferred over another through explicit attribute-level reasoning under a unified probabilistic framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263404/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text prompt specifying the evaluation criteria. For each image it generates k=6 candidate completions, each consisting of a d-dimensional attribute score vector (composition, color, DoF, lighting, content) bounded in [-1, 1], a scalar global aesthetic score in [0, 1], and a natural-language rationale. The structured output is consumed by the JoPPO reward function, which computes conditional pairwise win probabilities across all candidate pairs within a batch of B images.

## Method

JoPPO is a two-stage training recipe on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 applies LoRA SFT on a synthetic composition dataset (augmented via ControlNet conditioned on depth and Canny maps from PICD) to inject compositional priors. Stage 2 performs reinforcement learning with a novel joint conditional probabilistic objective: the model's attribute scores A and global score S are modeled as a joint multivariate Gaussian, from which the conditional distribution S|a is derived in closed form. Pairwise win probabilities between any two candidates are then computed via a Thurstone-style formula using the conditional means and variances, and the reward for each candidate is the expected agreement with ground-truth MOS-based binary preferences across the batch. This reward is standardized within each candidate group and optimized through the standard GRPO clipped policy-gradient objective with KL regularization, unifying attribute-conditioned reasoning and holistic scoring in a single probabilistic space.

## Results

On the in-domain PICD composition classification benchmark, JoPPO achieves 72.0% top-1 accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (39.3%) by +32.7 points and the strongest open-source baseline InternVL3-38B (31.8%) by +40.2 points. On CADB, the method reaches 0.629 SRCC / 0.612 PLCC for aesthetic score regression, outperforming GPT-4o (0.538/0.517) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (0.586/0.527). On out-of-distribution PARA, JoPPO attains 0.789 SRCC / 0.822 PLCC, exceeding GPT-4o by +0.128 SRCC and +0.084 PLCC, while on AVA it reaches 0.764/0.804 versus GPT-4o's 0.678/0.738.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JoPPO decisively outperforms all open-source VLM baselines (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA variants) across every in-domain and OOD benchmark, with the largest margins on PICD (+40.2% ACC over InternVL3-38B) and CADB (+15.5% ACC over Qwen2.5-VL-72B). Against GPT-4o, JoPPO wins on 10 of 12 reported metrics, including PICD ACC (0.720 vs 0.393), CADB SRCC (0.629 vs 0.538), PARA SRCC (0.789 vs 0.661), and AVA PLCC (0.804 vs 0.738). The sole clear loss is on TAD66K SRCC, where GPT-4o achieves 0.501 versus JoPPO's 0.427, though JoPPO edges ahead on TAD66K PLCC (0.434 vs 0.428). Among RL-based aesthetic judges, JoPPO surpasses Q-Insight by +0.190 SRCC on CADB and +0.107 SRCC on PARA, and outperforms ArtiMuse by +0.334 SRCC on CADB, indicating that the joint conditional probabilistic objective provides substantially stronger discriminative power than prior RL approaches that optimize a single global score. On the attribute-to-overall evaluation (Table 2), JoPPO leads every individual attribute (composition, color, DoF, lighting, content) in both SRCC and PLCC, confirming that the gains are not merely in holistic scoring but in faithful multi-attribute reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) VLM-as-a-Judge systems (PrometheusVision, JudgeLM) that use SFT to imitate score labels, (2) RL-based judges (GRPO, J1, VisualQuality-R1, Aes-R1, Q-Insight) that sharpen comparative discrimination but still regress a single global score, and (3) image aesthetics assessment (CADB, PARA, ArtiMuse) that provides multi-attribute annotations but lacks a unified probabilistic comparison framework. JoPPO departs from prior RL judges by replacing the single-score Thurstone objective with a joint conditional Gaussian over attribute vectors and the global score, yielding closed-form attribute-conditioned pairwise win probabilities optimized via a GRPO-style contrastive group objective. The two-stage recipe (SFT for compositional priors via synthetic ControlNet data, then JoPPO RL) is a practical departure from prior single-stage RL judges that assume the base VLM already has adequate perceptual grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling the dependency structure between fine-grained attributes and holistic quality—rather than treating them as independent regressions—improves both ranking accuracy and the faithfulness of attribute-level rationales, which is important for any application requiring interpretable visual judgment. The strong OOD generalization (outperforming GPT-4o on PARA, TAD66K, and ShotBench-Composition) suggests the attribute-conditioned probabilistic objective transfers beyond the training distribution. Key limitations include reliance on high-quality, well-covered attribute annotations and restriction to still images; the authors identify extension to technical image quality (sharpness, noise) and video-level temporal attributes as natural next steps. An open question is whether the joint Gaussian assumption holds for highly non-linear attribute interactions, and whether the approach scales to open-ended attribute sets beyond the fixed d dimensions used here.
