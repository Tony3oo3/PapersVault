## Problem

Multimodal contrastive geo-foundation models (e.g., SatCLIP, GeoCLIP) align satellite imagery with geo-coordinates under the multi-view redundancy assumption, implicitly discarding modality-specific (unique) information that may be critical for diverse downstream tasks. Prior approaches to retain unique information introduce extra training objectives or external feature databases, increasing complexity. The paper asks whether unique information can be recovered without any additional training components.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261373/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained contrastive encoders that take 256×256 multi-spectral Sentinel-2 satellite images and geographic coordinates (longitude, latitude) as paired inputs. Rather than using only the final-layer embedding (as standard CLIP-style models do), BWS extracts intermediate-layer representations from both encoders and produces a fused geo-embedding via weighted concatenation. This fused embedding is then used with linear probing for seven downstream tasks (three classification, four regression), requiring no retraining of the base model.

## Method

The paper formalizes multimodal contrastive learning through an information bottleneck lens, showing via the Data Processing Inequality that input-specific information monotonically decreases with depth while cross-modal alignment increases, driven by the InfoNCE loss applied only at the final layer. This hypothesis is validated through a trifecta layerwise analysis on SatCLIP: relative modality gap (geometric), CKA similarity (correlational), and KSG-estimated mutual information (information-theoretic) all confirm that intermediate layers retain more modality-specific structure. The proposed Beyond What's Shared (BWS) method fuses a subset of layer representations via weighted concatenation with softmax depth-dependent weights parameterized by α, where α=0 (uniform weighting) yields the best results. BWS introduces no new parameters, no auxiliary losses, and no external models, making it a zero-cost post-hoc augmentation applicable to any pretrained contrastive geo-foundation model.

## Results

Using linear probing with α=0 (uniform layer weighting), BWS consistently improves all four geo-foundation models across seven downstream tasks (three classification, four regression). The largest absolute gains appear on CSP, where average performance rises from ~0.665 to ~0.793 (e.g., Elevation R² jumps from 0.390 to 0.659). On the strongest baseline, RANGE+BWS achieves the best overall scores: 0.945 Biome accuracy, 0.959 Country accuracy, and 0.869 Elevation R². Gains are most pronounced on regression tasks (Elevation, Population) where modality-specific spatial structure is critical, while classification tasks see more modest but still consistent improvements of 5–10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BWS is model-agnostic and improves every baseline it is applied to. Against CSP, BWS yields the largest relative gains (e.g., +0.269 on Elevation R², +0.101 on Biome accuracy), suggesting CSP's final-layer embeddings discard the most modality-specific structure. Against GeoCLIP, BWS adds roughly 0.10 average improvement, with the strongest lift on Elevation (0.608→0.809) and Ecoregions (0.739→0.870). Against SatCLIP, BWS improves average performance from ~0.718 to ~0.790, with notable gains on Elevation (+0.120) and Population (+0.081), though the Housing task sees only a small increase (0.400→0.421). Against RANGE (the strongest baseline, which already augments SatCLIP with external SatMAE features), BWS still adds consistent gains across all seven tasks (e.g., Biome 0.931→0.945, Elevation 0.842→0.869), confirming that layerwise fusion captures complementary unique information beyond what retrieval-based augmentation provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal contrastive learning lineage (CLIP, GeoCLIP, SatCLIP, CSP) by reframing the problem of retaining modality-specific information through the lens of the information bottleneck rather than through auxiliary training objectives or external retrieval models. Prior approaches to preserving unique information—factorized contrastive learning, intra-modality regularization, and RANGE's retrieval-augmented strategy—all add computational or architectural complexity during training. BWS departs from this paradigm by showing that the unique information is already present in intermediate layers of the pretrained encoder and can be recovered post-hoc via a simple weighted concatenation, requiring no retraining, no extra parameters, and no external models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a zero-cost layerwise fusion yields consistent 5–10 point gains across seven geospatial tasks challenges the practical sufficiency of the multi-view redundancy assumption in CLIP-style models and offers an immediately adoptable technique for any practitioner using contrastive geo-foundation models. The trifecta analysis (modality gap, CKA, mutual information) provides a reusable diagnostic framework for understanding where information lives in multimodal encoders. Key open questions include whether the layerwise hierarchy generalizes to three or more modalities, to non-geospatial domains, and whether the optimal fusion strategy (uniform vs. depth-weighted) changes with task type or encoder architecture.
