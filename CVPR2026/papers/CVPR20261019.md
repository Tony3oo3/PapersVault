## Problem

SAM2-based segmentation methods applied to large-scale 3D electron microscopy volumes suffer from two critical limitations: their streaming memory, built from past 2D predictions, cannot enforce strict spatial continuity across slices, and they still demand extensive manual annotation. Existing semi-supervised approaches for 3D EM, while reducing label needs, typically operate slice-by-slice and fail to preserve volumetric morphology, producing inter-slice discontinuities and fragmented structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261019/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D EM volume (resized to near-isotropic resolution and cropped to 1024³) along with sparse 2D slice annotations (1/64 of slices, used as conditional frames for SAM2's few-shot propagation). The 3D U-Net operates on a 1/2-resolution downsampled volume to predict the SDF grid, which is upsampled to full resolution before slicing. The final output is a per-slice binary segmentation mask that composes into a complete 3D volume, with the SDF serving as an intermediate geometric representation rather than a final deliverable.

## Method

Spatial-SAM replaces SAM2's logit-based streaming memory with a precomputed 3D signed distance field (SDF) generated by a lightweight 4-layer 3D U-Net, providing direction-agnostic, spatially smooth geometric guidance that avoids error accumulation since it is computed once rather than written from online predictions. During inference, SAM2 conditions on bidirectional SDF memory from K=6 neighboring slices (excluding the current slice to prevent self-coupling), enabling fully automatic segmentation without manual prompting. Training follows a dual-track semi-supervised scheme: SAM2's few-shot capability bootstraps high-quality pseudo-labels from minimal annotations, then the system alternates between SDF regression (MSE + Eikonal loss on the 3D U-Net) and mask prediction (Dice + IoU + Focal loss on SAM2), with each cycle refining both modules through mask-to-SDF and SDF-to-mask conversions. This design couples geometric and semantic supervision intrinsically, allowing 1/64 slice annotations to generalize to full-volume segmentation with spatial consistency comparable to fully supervised baselines.

## Results

With only 1/64 of slices annotated, Spatial-SAM achieves 96.51% Dice / 93.25% mIoU on OOMLM, 98.14% / 96.34% on OOMLN, 94.45% / 89.51% on MitoEM-R, and 90.10% / 82.02% on MitoEM-H. This outperforms the strongest semi-supervised baseline (CPS U-Net) by +8.07% Dice and +11.49% mIoU on average across the four datasets, while matching the strongest fully supervised method (SAM4EM) within 0.1% on both metrics. On the challenging MitoEM-H (human mitochondria), Spatial-SAM degrades far less than any semi-supervised baseline, retaining 90.10% Dice versus 88.77% (CPS) and 73.50% (GCT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three semi-supervised U-Net baselines (CCT, GCT, CPS) at the same 1/64 label budget, Spatial-SAM wins on every dataset and metric: it gains +8.07% Dice and +11.49% mIoU on average over the best semi-supervised method (CPS U-Net), with the largest margin on OOMLN where CPS collapses to 69.03% Dice while Spatial-SAM reaches 98.14%. Against fully supervised 3D networks, Spatial-SAM surpasses 3D U-Net by +2.37% Dice on OOMLN (98.14 vs. 92.91) and by +1.55% on MitoEM-R, while Swin UNETR and Cellpose-SAM remain slightly ahead on OOMLM/OOMLN but degrade sharply on MitoEM-H (81.10% and 85.25% Dice respectively vs. Spatial-SAM's 90.10%). Against the strongest SAM-based fully supervised baseline SAM4EM, Spatial-SAM is essentially tied on the four-dataset average (−0.09% Dice, −0.10% mIoU) while using only 1/64 of the labels, and it edges out SAM4EM on MitoEM-H (90.10 vs. 91.10 Dice is a slight loss, but the gap is far smaller than for any semi-supervised method). On the harder MitoEM-H benchmark, all semi-supervised baselines drop 10–30+ points in Dice relative to MitoEM-R, whereas Spatial-SAM loses only ~4.4 points, confirming the spatial-consistency benefit of the SDF memory under appearance variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM2's streaming memory mechanism by replacing its logit-based memory entries with precomputed 3D signed distance field (SDF) slices from a lightweight 3D U-Net, shifting the memory from probabilistic to geometric. The dual-track semi-supervised training draws conceptual inspiration from Dual-task Consistency (DTC) but instantiates the two tracks as the intrinsic SDF-regression and mask-decoding modules of the architecture itself, coupled through SDF memory and pseudo-label conversions. It departs from prior semi-supervised EM methods (CCT, GCT, CPS) that operate on 2D slices with consistency regularization, and from SAM4EM's momentum-updated 3D memory that still relies on online 2D predictions, by making the volumetric guidance direction-agnostic and error-resilient through precomputation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for large-scale EM workflows: matching fully supervised SAM4EM accuracy with only 1/64 annotated slices directly reduces the bottleneck of expert annotation in connectomics and organelle morphometry. The SDF memory concept—using a precomputed geometric field rather than online predictions as cross-slice context—is a clean architectural idea that could transfer to other sequential or volumetric segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other organelle types or tissue scales, how it behaves under severe acquisition artifacts (which the authors acknowledge as a limitation), and whether the dual-track training overhead can be reduced for even larger volumes.
