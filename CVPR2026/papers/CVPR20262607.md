## Problem

3D vision-language models are severely constrained by the scarcity of paired 3D-text data, and existing methods that rely solely on next-token prediction loss provide only indirect supervision to point cloud features. This causes fine-grained geometric information to degrade and be lost in intermediate LLM representations, as structural cues orthogonal to immediate language tasks are discarded during training. The paper asks how to explicitly preserve 3D geometric-semantic information throughout the language modeling process with minimal additional cost.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D point cloud of 8192 points (each with 6-dimensional features encoding spatial coordinates and attributes) and a text prompt as input, producing free-form text outputs for classification, captioning, and question-answering tasks. The point cloud is encoded by PointBERT, projected through an MLP and Q-Former into the LLM's token space, then concatenated with text tokens for autoregressive generation. A lightweight alignment projector (3 linear layers, 8.39M parameters) is used exclusively during training to compute the alignment loss and is discarded at inference, introducing zero additional inference overhead.

## Method

PointAlign introduces a two-stage training strategy built on MiniGPT-3D: Stage 1 performs standard pre-training with three recipes, while Stage 2 freezes all visual front-end modules (PointBERT encoder, MLP, Q-Former, modality projector) and trains only LoRA adapters on the LLM plus a new 3-layer alignment projector with SiLU activations. The core mechanism aligns point cloud tokens extracted from the 16th LLM layer with the frozen Q-Former output via a cosine similarity loss, mapping LLM hidden states into Q-Former feature space through the projector. The Q-Former output is chosen as the alignment target because it encodes both geometric and semantic information learned from point cloud-text pairs, whereas the point cloud encoder captures only geometry and deep LLM layers have already lost 3D structure. The total loss combines next-token prediction with a weighted alignment term (λ=0.1), and the Q-Former output is detached from the computation graph to preserve Stage-1 alignment knowledge while the LoRA and projector parameters are updated.

## Results

On generative 3D object classification, PointAlign achieves an average accuracy of 66.08% across ModelNet40 and Objaverse, outperforming the strongest 3D baseline MiniGPT-3D (64.00%) by 2.08 pp. The most striking gain is on open-vocabulary Objaverse classification under the instruction-based prompt, where PointAlign reaches 72.50% versus MiniGPT-3D's 65.00% (+7.50 pp). For 3D object captioning on Objaverse, PointAlign scores 53.05 on Qwen2-72B-Instruct evaluation (vs. 48.17 for MiniGPT-3D, +4.88 pp) and 51.44 average across all three metrics, surpassing all 3D and 2D baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointAlign (2.7B, LoRA + alignment projector) outperforms the closest 3D baseline MiniGPT-3D (2.7B) by 2.08 pp on average classification accuracy, with the largest margin on open-vocabulary Objaverse instruction-based classification (+7.50 pp: 72.50 vs. 65.00). Against the larger PointLLM-13B (13B), PointAlign gains 8.86 pp on average (66.08 vs. 57.22) despite using a 5× smaller LLM, and leads by 8.75 pp on Objaverse average (71.00 vs. 62.25). On the captioning task (Table 2), PointAlign surpasses MiniGPT-3D by 4.88 pp on Qwen2-72B-Instruct scoring (53.05 vs. 48.17) and by 1.74 pp on the three-metric average (51.44 vs. 49.70), while also exceeding PointLLM-7B by 4.90 pp on the average. All 2D-image baselines (InstructBLIP, LLaVA, GPT-4o mini) are substantially outperformed on both tasks, with the largest gap being 33.44 pp on overall classification average versus InstructBLIP-7B. PointAlign slightly trails MiniGPT-3D on ModelNet40 instruction-based accuracy (61.55 vs. 61.99, −0.44 pp), but this is more than offset by its large Objaverse gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MiniGPT-3D, which leverages 2D VLM priors (Q-Former) to bridge 3D point cloud encoders and LLMs, and departs from the standard next-token-prediction-only training paradigm used by PointLLM, ShapeLLM, and MiniGPT-3D itself. It draws on 2D VLM research showing that visual representations degrade across network depth without explicit supervision, and transplants this insight to the 3D point cloud setting by introducing a cosine-similarity alignment loss between intermediate LLM hidden states and frozen Q-Former outputs. The method is positioned as a lightweight, parameter-efficient regularizer (LoRA + 3-layer projector, ~8.4M trainable parameters) rather than a full architectural redesign, distinguishing it from full fine-tuning approaches like PointLLM-13B that demand over 200 GPU-hours.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in 3D VLM development—data scarcity—by showing that explicit geometric supervision at intermediate layers enables more effective use of limited 3D-text pairs, with the most striking evidence being that the baseline model actually degrades (−4.55 pp) when trained on 100% vs. 50% data while the aligned model improves monotonically. This suggests standard NTP training is unstable on diverse 3D data, a finding with broader implications for multimodal LLM training. However, the approach is constrained to a single fixed alignment layer and a simple cosine loss, and its quality is bounded by the Q-Former's representational capacity; open questions include whether multi-layer or contrastive alignment strategies, learnable distance metrics, or application to larger LLM backbones would yield further gains.
