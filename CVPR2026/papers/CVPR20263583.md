## Problem

Existing CNN-based infrared small target detection and segmentation (IRSTDS) methods focus on enhancing high-frequency feature representation to improve target localization, but this emphasis on high-frequency components simultaneously increases false alarm rates due to embedded noise. No prior work has addressed this trade-off from a noise suppression perspective, leaving a gap between improved detection accuracy and uncontrolled false alarms in practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes single-frame infrared images as input (512×512 for IRSTD-1k, various sizes for NUAA-SIRST; resized to 224×224 for segmentation and 640×640 for detection) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks and detection bounding boxes. NS-FPN is designed as a plug-in module that replaces the 1×1 convolutions and upsampling operations in a standard FPN, so it can be integrated into any existing IRSTDS backbone (demonstrated with MSHNet for segmentation and YOLOv8n-p2 for detection) without modifying the backbone or head architecture.

## Method

NS-FPN integrates two noise-suppression modules into a standard FPN. The LFP module performs a 2D discrete wavelet transform to split each scale feature into low- and high-frequency components, then uses the low-frequency component to generate a spatial attention map (via average/max pooling + convolution + sigmoid) that modulates the high-frequency features, followed by a gated Gaussian filter that applies smoothing only to low-confidence (below-threshold) high-frequency responses before inverse DWT reconstruction. The SFS module replaces FPN upsampling with spiral-pattern deformable sampling: a fixed spiral offset (motivated by the Gaussian intensity profile of IRST) plus shared learnable biases constrains where upper-layer features are sampled, and cross-attention between the purified lower-layer query and sampled upper-layer key/value produces the fused output. The design adds only 0.26M parameters and 1.16G FLOPs over the baseline FPN, making it a lightweight drop-in replacement that reduces false alarms while improving IoU and mAP.

## Results

On IRSTD-1k, MSHNet+NS-FPN achieves 69.29% IoU, 95.24% Pd, and 8.58×10⁻⁶ Fa for segmentation, and 86.3% mAP50, 36.9% mAP75, and 42.1% mAP for detection. On NUAA-SIRST, it reaches 78.75% IoU, 100.0% Pd, and 1.60×10⁻⁶ Fa for segmentation, and 97.5% mAP50, 61.6% mAP75, and 58.0% mAP for detection. The method adds only +0.26M parameters and +1.16G FLOPs over the base FPN, making it a lightweight plug-in. The most notable gain is in false alarm reduction: Fa drops from 15.03 to 8.58 on IRSTD-1k and from 17.21 to 1.60 on NUAA-SIRST relative to the MSHNet baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NS-FPN outperforms all 17 compared segmentation methods on every metric across both datasets. The closest competitor on IRSTD-1k is SCTransNet (68.64% IoU, 11.92 Fa), which NS-FPN surpasses by 0.65% IoU and 3.34 Fa; on NUAA-SIRST, SFCANet (78.46% IoU, 8.02 Fa) is nearest, with NS-FPN leading by 0.29% IoU and 6.42 Fa. The most dramatic improvement is in false alarm suppression: relative to the MSHNet backbone, Fa drops from 15.03 to 8.58 (−43%) on IRSTD-1k and from 17.21 to 1.60 (−91%) on NUAA-SIRST, while Pd simultaneously increases. For detection, YOLOv8n+NS-FPN beats PConv (the strongest detection baseline) by 0.2% mAP50, 2.4% mAP75, and 1.3% mAP on IRSTD-1k, and by 1.1% mAP50, 11.7% mAP75, and 3.1% mAP on NUAA-SIRST. Among FPN variants (Table 4), NS-FPN also outperforms PANet, BiFPN, and HS-FPN on both IoU and mAP while adding fewer parameters than PANet and BiFPN.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the standard FPN architecture (Lin et al., 2017) and the deformable attention mechanism from Deformable DETR (Zhu et al., 2020), but departs from the dominant IRSTDS paradigm of designing complex feature-enhancement structures (DNANet, MSHNet, IRPruneDet, IRSAM). The key conceptual departure is reframing the problem from 'enhance target features' to 'suppress noise in features,' motivated by a frequency-domain analysis showing that high-frequency components critical for localization simultaneously carry the noise responsible for false alarms. The SFS module specifically reinterprets deformable attention's random sampling as a structured spiral pattern grounded in the Gaussian intensity distribution of infrared small targets, and the LFP module repurposes low-frequency wavelet components—typically discarded—as a guidance signal for purifying high-frequency features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The false alarm problem is practically critical in defense applications (bird warning, sea rescue, aerial surveillance) where even a small Fa rate leads to operational failures, and this paper provides a principled frequency-domain explanation for why feature-enhancement approaches inadvertently worsen it. The plug-and-play design (replacing 1×1 convolutions and upsampling in any FPN) makes the technique broadly applicable to existing IRSTDS pipelines without architectural redesign. Limitations include reliance on an empirically set threshold τ in the gated Gaussian filter, the spiral pattern being somewhat ad-hoc and specific to the Gaussian intensity assumption of IRST, and evaluation on only two datasets. Open questions include whether the noise-suppression-via-low-frequency-guidance principle generalizes to other small-object or low-SNR detection tasks beyond infrared, and whether the spiral sampling can be replaced by learned structured patterns without the inductive bias.
