## Problem

Most multimodal active learning methods rely on fixed selection rules that assume modality importance is stable across rounds, causing batches to be biased toward whichever modality dominates while underusing complementary cues. As the model and labeled pool evolve, the relative value of each modality and the difficulty of remaining samples shift, but static strategies cannot adapt, wasting limited labeling budget and degrading fusion quality.

## Input / Output

For image-text tasks (Food101), the method consumes RGB images (processed with ResNet-101) paired with concise textual descriptions (processed with BERT-base); for video-audio tasks (KineticsSound, VGGSound), it takes video clips sampled at 10 FPS (ResNet2P1D-18) alongside audio spectrograms computed with a 512-sample window and 353-sample overlap (modified ResNet-18). At each active learning round, the method outputs a query batch of b unlabeled samples selected from the pool for annotation, and the retrained backbone produces class predictions over C categories. A fixed stratified validation split is maintained across all rounds to compute modality contributions and calibration statistics.

## Method

RL-MBA formulates multimodal sample selection as a Markov Decision Process where a lightweight MLP policy selects b samples from a Top-K candidate set, optimized via REINFORCE with a reward defined as relative Top-1 improvement over precomputed baseline learning curves. Two components drive adaptivity: (1) Adaptive Modality Contribution Balancing (AMCB) computes per-modality Top-1 gaps on the validation split each round and maps them to a simplex of weights via temperature-controlled softmax, injecting the same weights into fusion, scoring, and the policy state so emphasis shifts coherently; (2) Evidential Fusion for Difficulty-Aware Adjustment (EFDA) fuses per-modality Dirichlet evidence additively weighted by AMCB weights, then computes Dirichlet predictive variance as a calibrated difficulty proxy that prioritizes challenging-but-informative samples over merely the hardest ones. A unified score combining modality-weighted uncertainty and k-means++ centroid distance forms the candidate set, and the policy performs sequential without-replacement sampling to produce the final batch, with per-round retraining and policy updates closing the feedback loop.

## Results

With a fixed labeling budget of 3,000 samples, RL-MBA achieves top-1 accuracy of 0.8650 on Food101 (image+text), 0.4841 on KineticsSound (video+audio, 31 classes), and 0.2223 on VGGSound (video+audio, 309 classes), outperforming all seven baselines on every dataset. The largest margin over the strongest baseline BMMAL appears on VGGSound (+1.70 pp), where the higher class count and greater modality heterogeneity amplify the benefit of adaptive reweighting. RL-MBA also attains the lowest per-round wall-clock time (884 s vs. 1,151–2,232 s for other methods), driven by a 33 s selection step versus 310–1,273 s for gradient- or graph-based alternatives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RL-MBA outperforms all seven baselines on all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline BMMAL (Shen et al., ACM MM 2023), RL-MBA gains +0.41 pp on Food101, +0.96 pp on KineticsSound, and +1.70 pp on VGGSound, with the advantage growing as task complexity increases. Among uncertainty-based methods, Entropy (Settles) and BALD (Gal) trail RL-MBA by 1.7–2.3 pp across datasets, while DeepFool (Ducoffe) is competitive on KineticsSound (0.4680 vs. 0.4841) but lags on VGGSound (0.1973 vs. 0.2223). Diversity-oriented CoreSet (Sener) and gradient-embedding BADGE (Sener) are the weakest on Food101 (0.8422 and 0.8420) and do not recover on the audio-visual benchmarks. GCNAL (Caramalau) sits mid-pack on Food101 (0.8510) but drops to 0.4600 on KineticsSound, below even Random. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that both AMCB and EFDA contribute independently, with EFDA providing the larger single-component gain on the video-audio datasets (+1.24 pp on VGGSound over BMMAL).

## Relation to Prior Work

RL-MBA sits at the intersection of two active learning threads: RL-driven sample selection (RAL, DRAL, Policy-based AL) and multimodal active learning (BMMAL, BADGE). It builds directly on evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018) for calibrated uncertainty via Dirichlet evidence, repurposing the predictive variance as a difficulty signal rather than a pure acquisition criterion. The key departure from BMMAL is replacing its static modality-balancing heuristic with a feedback-updated simplex (AMCB) and wrapping the entire selection loop in a lightweight MDP whose policy is optimized via REINFORCE, so that modality weights and difficulty emphasis co-adapt across rounds rather than being fixed at design time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck—multimodal annotation is expensive and fixed selection rules waste budget on redundant or uninformative samples—by showing that a lightweight RL policy (0.23 s per round) can outperform stronger baselines while also reducing per-round wall-clock time. However, the gains over the strongest baseline (BMMAL) are modest (0.4–1.7 percentage points), the RL component is a simple MLP over a small candidate set, and the reward depends on precomputed baseline curves, raising questions about how well the policy generalizes to unseen modality combinations or larger-scale settings. Open questions include whether the adaptive modality reweighting yields compounding benefits over very long AL horizons, and whether the evidential fusion step is truly necessary compared to simpler weighted-averaging of per-modality uncertainties.
