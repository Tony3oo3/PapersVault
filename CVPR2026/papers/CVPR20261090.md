## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction degrades severely under sparse inputs, and existing remedies—feed-forward Gaussian predictors, generative novel-view synthesis, and single-step diffusion fixers like DIFIX—either fail at extreme sparsity, lack 3D consistency, or only handle mild artifacts. The paper addresses the gap of achieving photorealistic, 3D-consistent 3DGS scenes from as few as one input image while supporting large view extrapolation (e.g., 180°+ range from <10 views).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261090/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts any number of sparse RGB input views (tested at 1–6 images), a set of novel camera poses (interpolated or extrapolated), and point cloud renderings generated by a vision foundation model (π³) as structural guidance. It outputs a fully optimized 3DGS scene renderable from the expanded view range, with the intermediate artifact-free novel-view images serving as dense supervision during optimization. The method is density-agnostic and can be plugged into any baseline 3DGS optimizer.

## Method

S2D combines a one-step diffusion artifact fixer with a tailored 3DGS optimization strategy. The fixer, built on pix2pix-turbo with SD-Turbo-initialized UNet weights, uses dual guidance: a nearby reference view for texture and a point cloud rendering for structure, fused through a mixing module that extracts DINO and image features to produce a balanced mixed input before VAE encoding and single-step denoising. For reconstruction, the paper introduces random sample drop—a probabilistic interleaving strategy that maintains a fixed ratio (α=0.7) of input-view to novel-view supervision samples—and a pixel-level weighted gradient that scales backpropagation by a point-cloud confidence mask (β=0.4), so regions lacking structural support contribute less to Gaussian parameter updates, preventing overfitting to incorrectly fixed artifacts.

## Results

On the most extreme setting (3DOVS with a single input image), S2D achieves 21.41 PSNR and 0.27 LPIPS, substantially outperforming the closest competitor DIFIX (14.10 PSNR, 0.56 LPIPS) by over 7 dB in PSNR. Across all four in-the-wild benchmarks (3DOVS@1, RE10K@2, MIP360@6, DL3DV@6), S2D consistently leads on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID. On the Waymo driving benchmark, S2D achieves 31.44 PSNR / 0.07 LPIPS for interpolation and the lowest lane-shift FID (41.3–53.9) among all methods, beating DIFIX (60.3–71.6) and StreetCrafter (57.4–66.4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2D outperforms all 12 baselines across every reported metric. Against traditional 3DGS methods (3DGS, Mip-Splatting, ScaffoldGS), S2D gains 7–11 dB PSNR on the single-image 3DOVS setting, where these methods collapse to ~10–11 PSNR. Among feed-forward methods, DepthSplat is the strongest baseline (25.79 PSNR on RE10K@2), yet S2D still leads by 1.8 dB and 0.09 LPIPS; AnySplat and NoPoSplat trail further behind. The closest overall competitor is DIFIX, which S2D beats by 7.3 dB PSNR on 3DOVS@1, 1.5 dB on RE10K@2, 1.5 dB on MIP360@6, and 2.8 dB on DL3DV@6, with consistent LPIPS improvements of 0.04–0.29. Generative methods (ViewCrafter, SEVA) achieve competitive LPIPS (0.29–0.36) but lack structural metrics (PSNR/SSIM) because they cannot support precise camera control over long sequences, making them unsuitable for explicit 3D scene reconstruction. On the Waymo driving benchmark, S2D surpasses StreetCrafter (the strongest generative baseline) by 11–19 FID points on lane-shift extrapolation and DIFIX by 14–18 FID points, while also achieving the best interpolation PSNR (31.44 vs. 30.26 for DIFIX).

## Relation to Prior Work

S2D sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3D Gaussian Splatting and its sparse-view variants (feed-forward methods like AnySplat, DepthSplat), (2) diffusion-based artifact fixing for 3D reconstruction, most directly DIFIX3D+ which uses a single-step diffusion model to repair mild novel-view artifacts, and (3) vision foundation models (VGGT, MapAnything, π³) that produce structurally consistent point clouds from few images. The key departure from DIFIX is the introduction of dual guidance—combining point cloud rendering with a reference view through a learned mixing module—and a reconstruction strategy (random sample drop + weighted gradient) that addresses the gap between sparse input supervision and dense novel-view guidance, which DIFIX overlooks. The one-step diffusion backbone is inherited from pix2pix-turbo/SD-Turbo, repurposed here with LoRA adapters and DINO feature loss rather than CLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling stable 3DGS reconstruction from as few as 1–6 input images with a 30°–180°+ view range, S2D addresses a practical bottleneck that has limited 3DGS deployment in autonomous driving, embodied AI, and real-world scanning where dense capture is infeasible. The pipeline is modular (VFM for structure, one-step diffusion for appearance, standard 3DGS optimization) and can be grafted onto existing 3DGS backbones, lowering the barrier for adoption. Open questions include whether the VFM dependency can be removed or replaced with lighter geometric priors, how the method behaves on dynamic or very large-scale scenes, and whether the artifact fixer generalizes beyond the DL3DV training distribution to truly in-the-wild conditions without retraining.
