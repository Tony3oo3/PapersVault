## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods struggle to simultaneously produce high-quality geometry and accurate poses under severe occlusion and open-set settings because they lack sufficient open-set priors for de-occlusion and pose estimation. Scene-native methods are confined to indoor domains, while object-native methods still suffer from geometry collapse on small objects and pose shifting due to coupled geometry-pose representations and limited training data diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262532/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB scene image as input and internally derives segmentation masks (via Grounded-SAM), a depth map (via MoGe), and per-object point clouds. It outputs a complete 3D scene consisting of textured mesh geometry for each detected object plus a 6D pose (rotation in 6D representation, 3D translation, and uniform scale) per object, with all objects normalized to a unified scene space. The framework handles an arbitrary number of objects per scene and generalizes beyond the 2–5 objects seen during training thanks to RoPE-based positional encoding.

## Method

SceneMaker decouples 3D scene generation into three independently trained modules: (1) a de-occlusion model fine-tuned from Flux Kontext on a 10K curated dataset with three masking strategies (background-free cutouts, right-angle border cropping, random brush strokes) to learn diverse open-set occlusion priors from image-scale data; (2) an off-the-shelf image-to-3D generator (Step1x-3D) applied to the de-occluded images; and (3) a unified flow-matching diffusion model (DiT architecture) that jointly predicts rotation, translation, and size conditioned on scene images, point clouds, and canonical object geometry. The pose estimator's key architectural innovation is a decoupled attention scheme: local self-attention operates within each object's token quadruple (rotation, translation, size, geometry), global self-attention enables inter-object interaction, local cross-attention lets rotation tokens attend only to canonical-space object conditions, and global cross-attention lets translation/size tokens attend to scene-level conditions. Open-set generalization is achieved by training on a 200K synthetic scene dataset built from a curated 90K-model subset of Objaverse rendered in Blender with random environment maps, ground planes, and per-object rotations.

## Results

On the MIDI test set (1K scenes), SceneMaker achieves the best overall performance across all five metrics: scene-level CD of 0.051, scene-level F-Score of 0.5642, object-level CD of 0.0963, object-level F-Score of 0.6544, and bounding-box IoU of 0.671. On the more challenging indoor test set with severe occlusions, SceneMaker reduces scene CD to 0.0470 and achieves IoU-B of 0.7693, compared to MIDI's 0.1672 and 0.3855. On the open-set test set, SceneMaker further improves to CD-S of 0.0285 and IoU-B of 0.7549, demonstrating strong generalization beyond indoor domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneMaker outperforms all 12 baselines across every evaluated metric. On the MIDI test set, it surpasses the strongest prior method MIDI [22] by a large margin: scene CD drops from 0.080 to 0.051 (−36%), scene F-Score rises from 0.5019 to 0.5642, and IoU-B jumps from 0.518 to 0.671 (+29.5%). Against other scene generation methods (PanoRecon, Total3D, InstPIFu, SSR, DiffCAD, Gen3DSR, REPARO), SceneMaker leads on all five metrics, with the largest gaps over Total3D (CD-S 0.270→0.051) and PanoRecon (IoU-B 0.240→0.671). On the harder indoor and open-set test sets with severe occlusions, SceneMaker beats PartCrafter [29] (CD-S 0.1846→0.0470 indoor; 0.2171→0.0285 open-set) and MIDI [22] (IoU-B 0.3855→0.7693 indoor; 0.5079→0.7549 open-set). For the de-occlusion sub-task, the fine-tuned model outperforms both BrushNet [23] (PSNR 11.07→15.03) and Flux Kontext [25] (PSNR 13.91→15.03), and for occluded object generation it beats Amodal3R [53] on all three metrics (CD 0.0443→0.0409, F-Score 0.7124→0.7454, Volume IoU 0.5279→0.5985).

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneMaker positions itself against two families of 3D scene generation methods: scene-native approaches (MIDI, PartCrafter, DiffuScene) that learn geometry and pose jointly from limited scene datasets, and object-native approaches (Amodal3R, CAST3D) that leverage large-scale 3D object datasets but still lack sufficient open-set de-occlusion and pose priors. The key departure is a decoupled architecture that separates de-occlusion, 3D object generation, and pose estimation into independently trained modules, each exploiting the dataset best suited to its sub-task. For de-occlusion, it repurposes the Flux Kontext image editing model and fine-tunes it on a purpose-built 10K dataset, rather than relying on 3D-native completion. For pose estimation, it introduces a unified diffusion model with decoupled attention (local cross-attention for rotation attending to object-level conditions, global cross-attention for translation/size attending to scene-level conditions), extending prior pose estimators like FoundationPose and CAST3D that lacked size prediction or inter-object interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled framework addresses a fundamental bottleneck in open-set 3D scene generation: the mismatch between the data needed for occlusion reasoning (abundant in 2D image datasets) and the data needed for 3D geometry (scarce in 3D datasets). The 200K synthetic scene dataset built from curated Objaverse objects is a practical resource that the community can reuse for open-set pose estimation. However, the method remains a single-image pipeline, and the upper-bound analysis showing a ~4× improvement in scene Chamfer Distance with complete point clouds suggests that video or multi-view inputs could unlock substantially better performance. Open questions include whether the decoupled attention design generalizes to scenes with more than five objects in practice (the RoPE-based extrapolation is demonstrated but not stress-tested), and whether the reliance on multiple frozen pretrained encoders (Grounded-SAM, MoGe, DINOv2, 3D VAE) introduces compounding error in real-world deployment.
