## Problem

Text-to-image models cannot reliably follow precise geometric instructions such as '30° left-side view' or '45° top-down perspective,' often hallucinating incorrect poses or collapsing to biased canonical angles. Prior viewpoint-control methods either require additional inputs beyond text (reference images, depth maps) or support only limited control (e.g., azimuth alone) while overfitting to training-object appearance correlations rather than learning generalizable geometric representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt plus a 5-parameter camera specification (azimuth, elevation, radius in object-diameter units, pitch, yaw) defined in an object-centric coordinate system where the object's front faces the positive x-axis. The camera parameters are encoded into a single viewpoint token via a lightweight MLP and concatenated with the text token sequence; the system assumes zero roll and a fixed ~55° field of view. The output is a single viewpoint-conditioned RGB image generated by the fine-tuned text-to-image backbone.

## Method

A 5-parameter camera representation is first transformed into a 6D vector (azimuth via sin/cos for periodicity, radius normalized to [0,1], elevation/pitch/yaw as radians) and then mapped through a 3-layer ReLU MLP to a token embedding in the same space as text tokens, which is inserted adjacent to the object description in the prompt. The backbone (Harmon, SD2.1, or SD3.5) is fine-tuned jointly with this viewpoint MLP using separate learning rates (2×10⁻⁴ for the MLP, 2×10⁻⁵ for the backbone) over 7,500 iterations. Training data combines ~373K canonically-aligned 3D renderings from TexVerse (3,111 objects × 120 viewpoints) for strong geometric supervision with ~6.6K photorealistic augmented images (generated via Nano Banana editing) to preserve appearance diversity and prevent the model from forgetting complex scene generation. The key design choice is a factorized, object-centric camera parameterization that decouples viewpoint from object identity, enabling transfer to unseen categories without the overfitting observed in prior token-based methods like Compass Control.

## Results

On the main test set of 5,550 samples, the proposed method (Harmon backbone) achieves a mean azimuth error of 18.11° and median of 7.63°, the best among all non-oracle methods, while also attaining the highest CLIP prompt-image similarity (0.3555). ControlNet with oracle depth access is slightly better on elevation (5.77° vs. 7.62°), radius (0.09 vs. 0.11), yaw (0.80° vs. 1.25°), and pitch (0.94° vs. 1.38°), but requires explicit depth maps at inference. On the GenEval benchmark, the method scores 94.38 on Single Object and 76.60 on Colors, substantially outperforming Compass Control (85.62 and 58.78) while remaining close to the unmodified Harmon backbone (99.90 and 92.60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Compass Control [27], the proposed method wins on every camera parameter: azimuth error drops from 31.07° to 18.11° (mean), elevation from 14.49° to 7.62°, and CLIP similarity rises from 0.3433 to 0.3555. The generalization gap is stark: Compass degrades from 18.62° azimuth error on 'easy' objects to 37.29° on 'diverse' objects, whereas the proposed method stays within 16.22°–19.06°, and Compass overfits to training categories 94.2% of the time on three test objects. Against Stable-Virtual-Camera [52], the proposed method is superior on all metrics (e.g., azimuth 18.11° vs. 54.89°, CLIP 0.3555 vs. 0.2596) and does not require a reference image input. Against ControlNet-Depth [50], the proposed method achieves a lower azimuth error (18.11° vs. 25.65°) and higher CLIP score (0.3555 vs. 0.3307) without any oracle geometry, though ControlNet retains a small edge on elevation, radius, yaw, and pitch thanks to its access to ground-truth depth maps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the viewpoint-conditioned text-to-image generation line initiated by Compass Control (azimuth-only tokens) and View-NeTI (object-specific multi-view tokens) by introducing a factorized 5-parameter camera representation encoded through a lightweight MLP and appended to text embeddings. It departs from novel-view-synthesis methods (Zero-123, Stable Virtual Camera) that require reference images, and from structural-conditioning methods (ControlNet) that need oracle geometry, by achieving full camera control from text alone. The two-part dataset design—large-scale 3D renderings for geometric supervision plus photorealistic augmentations for appearance diversity—is a novel training recipe not present in prior viewpoint-control work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D camera structure can be embedded into text-vision latent spaces via simple parametric tokens, opening a pathway toward geometrically-aware prompting without requiring additional geometric inputs at inference. Key limitations remain: elevation is restricted to [0°, 45°], roll and intrinsics are excluded, and the T2I backbone's prior toward eye-level, centered views persists for well-known landmarks. Open questions include whether this approach scales to scene-level (multi-object, multi-camera) control, whether the two-part dataset recipe generalizes to other controllable attributes, and whether the factorized encoding advantage over Plücker rays or full camera matrices holds at larger scale or with more complex scenes.
