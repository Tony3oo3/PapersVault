## Problem

Existing pose-guided human image animation methods are limited to single-character scenarios, and naively extending them to multiple characters causes identity confusion and implausible occlusions. Moreover, prior multi-character approaches are trained with a fixed number of participants and cannot generalize to unseen character counts, making them impractical for scalable deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260937/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a reference image (3×H×W), a T-frame driving pose sequence (T×3×H×W), and n per-person binary tracking masks (each T×1×H×W) to produce a T-frame RGB video (T×3×H×W). A key design constraint is that the model is trained exclusively on two-character data yet must produce identity-consistent videos for up to n characters (n=3 or n=7) at inference, with the number of characters determined solely by how many tracking masks are supplied.

## Method

Built on the Wan2.1 DiT I2V backbone, the pipeline fuses a reference stream (VAE-encoded image + 2D-conv pose features) with a motion stream (3D-conv pose features + mask features) via element-wise addition of latent tokens. The Identifier Assigner unifies all per-person tracking masks into a single label map and one-hot encodes it into a 3-channel binary tensor, while the Identifier Adapter (stacked 3D convolutions) converts this into the DiT feature space, explicitly encoding per-person spatial occupancy and inter-person relationships such as proximity and occlusion. A scalable training strategy randomly samples identity labels from a learnable Identity Label Bank of size n at every iteration, activating only the corresponding channels in an Identifier Weight Bank; this ensures all n channels are trained to be mutually distinguishable despite only two characters appearing per sample, resolving the symmetry problem where identity would otherwise bind to a fixed channel rather than to the tracking mask.

## Results

On the Swing Dance dataset, MultiAnimate (Stage 1) achieves PSNR 19.40, SSIM 0.687, LPIPS 0.335, FVD 648.84, and FID-VID 22.50, outperforming the strongest baseline UniAnimate-DiT (PSNR 16.15, FVD 891.89, FID-VID 27.71) across all metrics. On unseen dance videos with 3–7 characters, the Extended model reaches PSNR 23.24, SSIM 0.857, LPIPS 0.185, FVD 358.74, and FID-VID 43.12, substantially surpassing UniAnimate-DiT (PSNR 17.94, FVD 624.45, FID-VID 71.24). On the Gen-dataset, the model achieves the best FVD (454.92) and PSNR (19.05) but is slightly behind UniAnimate-DiT on FID-VID (33.57 vs. 32.10) and VACE on LPIPS (0.251 vs. 0.231).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiAnimate outperforms all four baselines on the Swing Dance dataset across every metric, with the largest gains over UniAnimate-DiT (the strongest single-character baseline) in FVD (648.84 vs. 891.89, −27%) and FID-VID (22.50 vs. 27.71, −19%). On the Gen-dataset, MultiAnimate leads in FVD (454.92 vs. 565.50 for UniAnimate-DiT) and PSNR (19.05 vs. 18.10), but trails UniAnimate-DiT slightly on FID-VID (33.57 vs. 32.10) and VACE on LPIPS (0.251 vs. 0.231), suggesting minor weakness in highly diverse scene adaptation. On unseen dance videos with 3–7 characters, the Extended model dominates all baselines by wide margins, achieving FVD 358.74 versus 624.45 for UniAnimate-DiT (−43%) and FID-VID 43.12 versus 71.24 (−40%), confirming strong generalization from two-character training to multi-character inference. MimicMotion and DisPose consistently rank last across all datasets, while VACE shows competitive FID-VID on Swing Dance but poor frame-level quality (PSNR 11.15). On the single-person TiKToK evaluation (Table 2), MultiAnimate also surpasses UniAnimate-DiT and DisPose on all metrics (PSNR 23.68 vs. 17.76/17.17; FVD 342.48 vs. 649.30/615.27), confirming that the multi-character design does not degrade single-character performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the UniAnimate-DiT framework (Wang et al., 2025) and the Wan2.1 DiT-based video generation architecture, extending them from single-character to multi-character pose-guided animation. It departs from the concurrent DanceTogether approach, which aggregates per-character features via summation, by instead introducing an explicit mask-driven identifier mechanism (Identifier Assigner + Identifier Adapter) that preserves per-person spatial structure. The scalable training strategy—randomly sampling identity labels from a learnable bank at each iteration—repurposes the idea of data augmentation to solve a symmetry/generalization problem specific to multi-character identity binding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The extensibility property (training on 2-character data, generating 3–7 character videos) is practically significant because it eliminates the need to collect and retrain on increasingly large multi-person datasets, a major bottleneck in the field. However, the approach still depends on high-quality per-person tracking masks (via Sa2VA) and is limited to human characters; generalization to non-human subjects, real-time inference, or very large character counts (>7) remains untested. An open question is whether the identifier-bank mechanism degrades gracefully as the number of characters approaches the bank size, and whether the quality gap between 2-character and 7-character generation widens substantially.
