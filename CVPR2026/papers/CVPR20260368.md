## Problem

Existing benchmarks for emerging sensors (event cameras, 4D radar, thermal cameras) are modality-specific and primarily compare against conventional RGB and LiDAR, leaving direct cross-sensor comparisons and systematic fusion studies underexplored. No prior dataset simultaneously captures all these modalities in the same driving scenes with precise 3D annotations, making it impossible to fairly evaluate each sensor's strengths and design principled multi-sensor fusion strategies under diverse weather and illumination conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260368/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes five sensor streams: long-range LiDAR point clouds (Livox HAP, 150 m range, 120°×25° FoV, 10 FPS), 4D radar point clouds with Doppler (RETINA-4FN, 100 m range, 100°×24° FoV, 20 FPS), stereo RGB images (2448×2048, 10 FPS), stereo thermal images (640×512, 30 FPS), and stereo event camera data (1280×720, >10k events/s). Only the left camera from each stereo pair is used, and evaluation is restricted to the front-camera FoV (X-axis 0–70 m). The output is 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation, class) with track IDs, evaluated via AP at 3D IoU 0.5; a 2D detection benchmark additionally reports 2D boxes at IoU 0.5.

## Method

The proposed fusion framework operates in two stages. First, LiDAR and 4D radar point clouds are voxelized, collapsed to BEV features, channel-concatenated, and passed through a detector (CenterPoint) to produce initial 3D box proposals. Second, RGB, thermal, and event images are encoded by a Swin Transformer backbone, and their features are injected into 3D space via a voxel-centric sampling strategy: non-empty voxel indices from the fused range-sensor features are projected onto each camera plane, and nearby image features are aggregated using learnable offsets and weights (deformable cross-attention with the voxel feature as query). A confidence-based gating mechanism then computes per-camera scalar weights from a global feature summary (sigmoid of the mean), reweighting each camera branch before concatenation with the original voxel features through a feed-forward network. Finally, initial boxes are refined by ROI pooling over the fused voxel features followed by an MLP regression head, all trained end-to-end with RPN, confidence, and box-regression losses.

## Results

On the DSERT-RoLL dataset (Vehicle class, AP at 3D IoU 0.5), the proposed R+E+T+4R+L fusion method achieves 90.30 AP in clear conditions, 71.42 in fog, 95.10 in light rain, 80.26 in heavy rain, 85.59 in light snow, and 72.94 in heavy snow. Under lighting conditions, it reaches 82.93 (normal), 92.65 (low light), 85.47 (overexposed), and 86.33 (HDR). These results consistently outperform the strongest single-modality baseline (VoxelNeXt on LiDAR: 86.06 clear, 59.51 fog, 54.75 heavy snow) and the best prior multi-modal fusion method (SAMFusion R+T+4R+L: 87.03 clear, 65.13 fog, 70.59 heavy snow) across all ten weather/lighting conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (R+E+T+4R+L) outperforms all 17 baselines across every weather and lighting condition. Against the strongest LiDAR-only baseline (VoxelNeXt, 86.06 AP clear), the gain is +4.24 AP in clear conditions but expands to +11.91 AP in fog and +18.19 AP in heavy snow, where LiDAR degrades most severely. Among multi-modal fusion baselines, SAMFusion (R+T+4R+L, the closest competitor) is outperformed by +3.27 AP in clear, +6.29 in fog, and +2.35 in heavy snow; the addition of the event modality provides the largest marginal gain in fog and overexposed conditions. LiDAR+4R fusion methods (InterFusion, RL3DOD) improve over LiDAR alone in adverse weather but remain 3–6 AP below the full five-modality fusion. Stereo-only methods (DSGN, LIGA) and 4D-radar-only methods (RTNH, VoxelNeXt-4R) are substantially weaker (20–44 AP range), confirming that neither camera-only nor radar-only sensing is sufficient for robust all-weather 3D detection. In the 2D detection benchmark (Table 5), the multi-modal GM-DETR (R+T+E) similarly dominates single-modality detectors, reaching 90.36 AP in clear and 93.52 in HDR versus 76–82 AP for the best single-modality methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by being the first dataset to simultaneously capture stereo event, RGB, and thermal cameras alongside 4D radar and dual LiDAR with unified 3D annotations, whereas prior novel-sensor datasets (K-Radar, DSEC, KAIST) are modality-specific and cannot support fair cross-sensor comparison. Methodologically, it repurposes well-established components—VoxelNet-style voxel backbones, Swin Transformer for 2D features, deformable cross-attention for camera-to-3D lifting, and CenterPoint-style detection—into a unified pipeline whose novel architectural contribution is a voxel-centric sampling strategy with confidence-based camera gating. The fusion design is conceptually closest to SAMFusion's sensor-adaptive philosophy but extends it to five modalities and introduces per-voxel sparse fusion between LiDAR and 4D radar before camera integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSERT-RoLL fills a critical gap: no prior benchmark allows like-for-like evaluation of event, thermal, 4D radar, and LiDAR under identical weather and lighting conditions, which is essential for understanding true sensor complementarity rather than confounded comparisons. The ablation reveals that 4D radar contributes the largest single-modality gain in heavy snow (+15.8 AP over LiDAR alone), while event and thermal cameras provide targeted improvements in HDR and low-light respectively, validating the hypothesis that synergy—not substitution—is the right framing. Open questions include whether the confidence-based gating generalizes to unseen sensor combinations, whether the 22K-frame scale is sufficient for training robust models, and how the fusion strategy performs under domain shift (e.g., different cities or vehicle platforms).
