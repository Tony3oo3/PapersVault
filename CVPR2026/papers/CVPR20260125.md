## Problem

State-of-the-art low-light image enhancement (LLIE) methods rely on large neural networks (tens of millions of parameters) and operate in a single color space, limiting real-time edge deployment and introducing instability with visible exposure or color artifacts. Drastically reducing model size below 1M parameters typically causes significant quality degradation, and existing lightweight approaches still suffer from partial luminance-color coupling, red discontinuity, and training instability across challenging scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-light RGB image (H×W×3, normalized to [0,1]) and produces an enhanced RGB image of identical dimensions. Rather than outputting a full reconstruction, the network predicts an additive enhancement delta Δ_I = Δ_L ⊙ Δ_R that is added to the input, where Δ_L is a single-channel luminance correction and Δ_R is a three-channel reflectance correction. Two model variants are provided: Multinex (44.7K parameters, 2.50 GFLOPs) and Multinex-Nano (0.7K parameters, 0.04 GFLOPs), both sharing the same analytic guidance stacks and enhancement formulation.

## Method

Multinex reformulates Retinex decomposition as an additive residual (enhancement delta) rather than a multiplicative reconstruction, predicting only the correction needed to fix exposure while preserving the input's inherent structure. To reduce representation learning burden under tight parameter budgets, it analytically computes two multi-prior guidance stacks from the input: a 4-channel luminance stack (BT.709 Y, Vmax, HSL lightness, L2 norm) and a 5-channel reflectance stack (Cb, Cr, chromaticity ratios r and g, and saturation), providing diverse physical and perceptual cues without learning a color-space transformation. A dual-branch lightweight fusion network then processes each stack independently using Fusion Blocks (sequential MSEF channel recalibration and depthwise separable convolution with ReLU) and a Component-wise Attention mechanism (depthwise 7×7 conv + 1×1 projection) that produces per-descriptor attention maps for selective weighting. The two branches output Δ_L (1 channel) and Δ_R (3 channels), which are combined via Hadamard product and added to the input. Training uses a hybrid loss combining pixel-wise MSE, multi-scale SSIM, and perceptual loss, with the full architecture requiring only 45K parameters (T=3 FB layers per stage) or 0.7K parameters in the nano variant (T=1, simplified path).

## Results

On the reference benchmarks, Multinex (45K params) achieves PSNR 23.19 / SSIM 0.843 / LPIPS 0.129 on LOLv1, 23.04 / 0.860 / 0.178 on LOLv2-real, and 25.04 / 0.930 / 0.068 on LOLv2-syn, outperforming all other lightweight (<1M) methods. Multinex-Nano (0.7K params) reaches PSNR 19.42 on LOLv1 and 21.05 on LOLv2-syn, the best in the micro (<10K) category. On no-reference datasets, Multinex attains mean NIQE 3.64 and BRISQUE 14.33, surpassing the best mid-size method CIDNet (NIQE 3.81, BRISQUE 17.60) while using <2.5% of its parameters. For low-light object detection on ExDark, Multinex-Nano achieves the highest mAP50 of 80.7% with only 0.7K parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the lightweight (<1M) group, Multinex (45K params) outperforms LYT-Net (45K) by +0.81 dB PSNR on LOLv1 and +1.26 dB on LOLv2-syn, and beats ZeroDCE (79K) by +8.33 dB on LOLv1, PairLIE (330K) by +4.72 dB, and RetinexNet (840K) by +6.42 dB. In the micro (<10K) group, Multinex-Nano (0.7K) surpasses RSFNet (2.1K) by +0.03 dB on LOLv1 and +3.87 dB on LOLv2-syn, and outperforms RUAS (3.4K) and SCI (0.26K) by large margins. Against mid-size methods, Multinex approaches CIDNet (1.88M) within 0.62 dB on LOLv1 and 0.67 dB on LOLv2-syn while using only 2.4% of the parameters, and is within 1.96 dB of RetinexFormer (1.53M) on LOLv1. On no-reference benchmarks, Multinex achieves the best mean NIQE (3.64) and BRISQUE (14.33), improving over CIDNet by 0.17 NIQE and 3.27 BRISQUE. For ExDark detection, Multinex-Nano attains the highest mAP50 (80.7%), edging out EMV-YOLO (79.7%) and PE-YOLO (78.0%), while Multinex ties EMV-YOLO at 79.7% mAP50.

## Relation to Prior Work

Multinex sits at the intersection of Retinex-based LLIE and lightweight network design, departing from prior Retinex methods (Retinex-Net, KinD, RetinexFormer) by reformulating the illumination-reflectance decomposition as an additive residual (enhancement delta) rather than a multiplicative reconstruction. It also diverges from single-color-space approaches (YUV in LYT-Net, HVI in CIDNet) by analytically extracting multiple complementary luminance and chrominance priors (BT.709, L2-norm, saturation, chromaticity ratios) simultaneously, reducing the burden on the learnable network. The component-wise attention mechanism is a novel lightweight alternative to standard attention modules, and the overall design philosophy—using analytic physics to substitute for learned representations under extreme parameter budgets—distinguishes it from prior lightweight methods like ZeroDCE and PairLIE that rely on handcrafted losses or simpler architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 45K-parameter model can match or exceed 1.5–2M-parameter SOTA methods on reference benchmarks, and that a 0.7K-parameter nano variant remains competitive for extreme edge deployment (surveillance, phones, drones). The analytic-prior design principle—replacing learned color-space transformations with fixed physical descriptors—offers a reusable strategy for other low-resource vision tasks. Open questions include whether the additive residual formulation generalizes to high-dynamic-range or mixed-lighting scenes, whether the approach extends to video or multi-frame settings, and whether the analytic priors remain effective under severe sensor noise or spectral distortion.
