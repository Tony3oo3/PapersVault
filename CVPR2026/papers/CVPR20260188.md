## Problem

Vision Foundation Models produce spatially downsampled features that limit pixel-level tasks, and existing upsampling methods face a fundamental trade-off: classical filters are fast and VFM-agnostic but use fixed kernel forms, while modern learnable upsamplers achieve higher accuracy but must be retrained for each specific VFM. No prior method achieves zero-shot, VFM-agnostic upsampling that outperforms VFM-specific approaches across diverse tasks and model scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260188/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NAF takes a high-resolution RGB image (serving as the sole guidance signal) and a low-resolution feature map from any VFM (arbitrary channel dimension d) as input, and produces a high-resolution feature map at an arbitrary upsampling factor (up to 72×, scaling to 2K feature maps at 18 FPS). The guidance is extracted exclusively from the input image via a lightweight dual-branch CNN encoder, completely decoupling the upsampling from the VFM's feature distribution. In the image restoration extension, the same architecture takes a noisy RGB image as both guidance and input (with pooling reduced to identity) and outputs a denoised image.

## Method

NAF formulates upsampling as Cross-Scale Neighborhood Attention: each high-resolution query (from a RoPE-encoded dual-branch image encoder) attends only to a compact local neighborhood of low-resolution keys (obtained by average-pooling the same RoPE-encoded features to the LR grid), with attention values being the VFM features themselves. The dual-branch encoder combines 1×1 pixel-wise convolutions and 3×3 contextual convolutions to capture both fine-grained and local structural information. RoPE encodes relative spatial offsets directly into the attention computation, and the paper shows this design implicitly learns an Inverse Discrete Fourier Transform of the aggregation kernel, bridging classical filtering interpretability with learned adaptability. Training uses a simple L2 reconstruction loss against VFM features extracted at full resolution, with no additional regularization. The local neighborhood constraint reduces key-query interactions by ~40% compared to full attention (JAFAR), enabling higher upsampling ratios and 7B-model compatibility within a single A100 40GB GPU.

## Results

On Pascal VOC semantic segmentation, NAF achieves an average +5.58 mIoU over the Nearest baseline across four VFMs (DINOv2-R, RADIOv2.5, Franca, DINOv3), reaching 87.85 mIoU with DINOv3-B and 88.84 mIoU with DINOv3-7B. On NYUv2 depth estimation, NAF gains +3.16 δ₁ over Nearest and +0.57 over the best VFM-agnostic baseline AnyUp, with a striking +12.69 δ₁ improvement on DINOv3-7B. NAF is the first VFM-agnostic upsampler to surpass VFM-specific methods (JAFAR, FeatUp) while operating at 18 FPS with only 0.66M parameters, and it scales to 2K feature maps and 7B-parameter VFMs where prior methods run out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAF outperforms all VFM-specific upsamplers (JAFAR, FeatUp) and the VFM-agnostic AnyUp on both segmentation and depth estimation across every tested VFM. On Pascal VOC segmentation, NAF leads JAFAR by +0.15 to +0.75 mIoU per VFM (e.g., 87.85 vs 87.10 on DINOv3) and AnyUp by +0.84 to +1.17 mIoU, while being roughly 4× faster (265 vs 329 GFLOPs) with 25% fewer parameters. On NYUv2 depth, NAF surpasses AnyUp by +0.57 δ₁ on average and JAFAR by +0.37 δ₁. A critical practical advantage is that NAF remains applicable to DINOv3-7B (88.84 mIoU, 91.74 δ₁) where JAFAR and FeatUp fail with out-of-memory errors even at batch size 1 on an A100 40GB. On Cityscapes (Table 3), NAF achieves 64.98 mIoU, beating bicubic (63.62), JBF (63.85), and JAFAR (62.46) by 1.36, 1.13, and 2.52 mIoU respectively. For image denoising (Table 8), NAF with only 0.66M parameters approaches Restormer (26.13M params) on Gaussian noise (27.11 vs 27.77 PSNR at σ∈[0.1,0.5]) and even surpasses it on salt-and-pepper noise (39.62 vs 40.41 PSNR), while substantially outperforming DnCNN, IRCNN, and REDNet.

## Relation to Prior Work

NAF sits at the intersection of classical joint bilateral filtering (JBF, JBU) and modern attention-based feature upsamplers (JAFAR, AnyUp, LoftUp). It departs from VFM-specific upsamplers by deriving all guidance solely from a lightweight dual-branch CNN encoder applied to the input image, eliminating any dependency on the target VFM's low-resolution features. The Cross-Scale Neighborhood Attention mechanism with RoPE is a novel reformulation that the authors show is mathematically equivalent to predicting spectral coefficients whose IDFT yields an adaptive spatial filter, bridging the interpretability of classical kernels with the expressiveness of learned attention. Unlike AnyUp, which still entangles guidance with VFM features, NAF achieves full VFM-agnosticism through this image-only guidance design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAF provides the first zero-shot, VFM-agnostic upsampler that consistently outperforms VFM-specific methods across segmentation, depth estimation, open-vocabulary segmentation, and video propagation, while scaling to 7B-parameter models where prior methods hit out-of-memory limits. Its practical efficiency (18 FPS at 2K, 0.66M parameters) makes it a viable drop-in replacement in production VFM pipelines. The extension to image denoising with the same architecture suggests the neighborhood attention filtering paradigm may generalize to broader restoration tasks. Open questions include whether the IDFT interpretation can be exploited for further architectural improvements, whether the approach extends to video or 3D feature maps, and whether the local neighborhood constraint becomes limiting at very high upsampling ratios.
