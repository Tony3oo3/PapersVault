## Problem

Existing 6D object pose estimation methods require extensive dataset-specific training, CAD models, or both, limiting generalization to novel objects. Even recent approaches that leverage vision foundation models (e.g., Oryon, Horyon, FoundationPose) still depend on a trainable correspondence network or neural object field, preventing true training-free deployment. This paper asks whether language-driven semantic reasoning via vision-language models can replace learned geometric features entirely for zero-shot relative pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single anchor-query RGB-D image pair of the same object instance from different viewpoints, a category name string (e.g., "cup"), ground truth object masks, and camera intrinsics. No CAD model, training data, or ground-truth poses are required at inference. The output is a 6DoF camera-to-camera relative transformation (R_rel ∈ SO(3), t_rel ∈ R³), optionally converted to absolute object coordinates by composing with the ground-truth anchor pose for benchmarking. Intermediate 3D concept vectors are L-dimensional (default L=20) softmax-normalized probability distributions attached to each backprojected point.

## Method

ConceptPose is a fully training-free, model-free pipeline that repurposes VLM explainability for pose estimation. An LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro) generates L semantically orthogonal concept labels from the category name, covering parts, geometry, affordances, and attributes. For each concept, Grad-CAM on the SigLIP2-giant vision encoder produces a spatial saliency map, which is backprojected into 3D using depth to form dense point clouds where every point carries an L-dimensional concept vector. Correspondences between anchor and query points are established by minimizing forward KL divergence between their concept vectors, yielding putative 3D-3D pairs. A RANSAC loop (100k iterations, 0.01 m threshold) with Umeyama's closed-form solution estimates the relative transformation, followed by ICP refinement for local optimization. An optional voxelization step (64³ grid with mean-pooled concept vectors) reduces runtime by ~11% with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

On zero-shot relative pose estimation, ConceptPose achieves 71.5% ADD(-S) on REAL275, 55.0% on Toyota-Light, 41.2% on YCB-Video, and 38.6% on LINEMOD, yielding a 51.6% average ADD(-S) that surpasses the strongest trained baseline (Horyon, 31.7%) by 62.8% relative improvement. On BOP Average Recall, ConceptPose scores 60.4% (REAL275), 51.6% (TYOL), 32.8% (YCB-V), and 31.0% (LINEMOD), with a 44.0% average. In the few-shot pose tracking extension on YCB-Video, ConceptPose reaches 90.1% ADD-AUC and 95.4% ADD-S-AUC using only 2 reference frames, outperforming the trained FoundationPose (87.4%/94.3%) without any training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptPose decisively outperforms all eight baselines on the primary ADD(-S) metric. Against the strongest trained method Horyon [8] (31.7% avg ADD(-S)), ConceptPose gains +19.9 points (62.8% relative); against Any6D [22] (42.9%†) and One2Any [29] (37.8%†), it leads by +8.7 and +13.8 points respectively on the two-dataset average. On BOP AR, ConceptPose wins on REAL275 (60.4 vs. 57.9 Horyon), TYOL (51.6 vs. 43.3 Any6D), and YCB-Video (32.8 vs. 28.6 Horyon), but trails Horyon by 3.4 points on LINEMOD (31.0 vs. 34.4), attributed to heavy occlusion in that dataset. Classical training-free baselines SIFT [33] (15.7%) and ObjectMatch [14] (8.4%) are far behind, confirming that semantic concept vectors provide substantially richer correspondence cues than geometric features alone. In the few-shot tracking extension, ConceptPose (90.1% ADD-AUC) surpasses the trained FoundationPose [51] (87.4%) by 2.7 points while remaining training-free, though it falls short of UA-Pose [25] (92.8%) which benefits from online object completion during testing.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptPose departs from the dominant paradigm in zero-shot pose estimation where VLMs (DINO, DINOv2) serve as frozen feature extractors feeding a separately trained correspondence or pose head (Oryon, Horyon, FoundationPose). Instead, it repurposes VLM explainability—specifically Grad-CAM on SigLIP2's vision encoder—as a spatial concept localization mechanism, generating dense per-point concept vectors from LLM-generated language labels. This positions the work at the intersection of VLM interpretability, language-guided segmentation, and classical geometric estimation (RANSAC + Umeyama + ICP), but the key conceptual leap is treating Grad-CAM outputs not as diagnostic heatmaps but as the primary correspondence signal, eliminating all learned matching components. It also contrasts with Any6D, which still relies on image-to-3D generation and a pretrained neural network (FoundationPose) for final pose refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a fully training-free pipeline can outperform trained methods by over 62% in ADD(-S) across four real-world datasets, the paper challenges the assumption that learned geometric features are necessary for competitive pose estimation and opens a path toward pose estimation that adapts to novel objects purely through language queries. Practical limitations remain: ~7 seconds per image pair, degradation under severe occlusion and extreme viewpoint changes, and dependence on a large VLM (SigLIP2-giant) and a frontier LLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro). A key open direction the authors identify is extending the concept-vector framework to category-level pose estimation requiring no reference view at all, which would eliminate even the one-shot requirement and make the system fully open-vocabulary.
