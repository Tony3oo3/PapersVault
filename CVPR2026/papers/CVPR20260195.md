## Problem

Existing diffusion-native watermarking methods for latent diffusion models are fundamentally capacity-limited: even the strongest baseline (Gaussian Shading) suffers a 15% accuracy drop when scaling from 256 to 8,192 bits, and most methods become infeasible beyond 4,096 bits. This bottleneck arises because directly perturbing the latent noise disrupts its Gaussian distribution, degrading image quality and extraction reliability as payload grows. The paper asks whether high-capacity watermarking (up to the full 16,384-bit latent space) can be achieved without sacrificing image fidelity or robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a binary secret key of up to 16,384 bits (matching the 4×64×64 latent space of Stable Diffusion) and a text prompt, producing a 512×512 watermarked image. Extraction recovers the secret key by encoding the image back to latent space, applying DDIM inversion, running the inverse INN, and decoding the Reed-Solomon-enhanced sign bits. The framework generalizes to any payload length up to the trained maximum without per-message retraining, and extends to video (ModelScope) and audio (AudioLDM2) modalities by embedding across the full spatiotemporal latent span.

## Method

MaxMark combines two cooperating modules. The robust watermark embedding module enhances the secret key with Reed-Solomon error-correcting codes (hyperparameters auto-searched via a closed-form block-failure probability under an i.i.d. error model) and embeds the enhanced bits into the sign bits of the Gaussian latent noise, pushing modified values away from zero by a margin β to improve separability. The distribution transformation module then maps the watermarked latent back to N(0,I) using a 12-block RealNVP-style invertible neural network with multiplicative coupling, ensuring the diffusion model receives a valid Gaussian input. Training requires only an MLE loss plus a KL divergence term—no reconstruction loss—because the INN's exact invertibility guarantees lossless recovery. Extraction reverses the pipeline: DDIM inversion recovers an approximate transformed latent, the inverse INN reconstructs the watermarked latent, and RS decoding corrects residual bit errors to recover the original secret key.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5, MaxMark achieves 95.4% bit accuracy at the maximum 16,384-bit payload, compared to 49.9% for Gaussian Shading and 43.6% for PRC Watermark — a 46% absolute improvement over the strongest diffusion-native baseline. Under adversarial attacks (blur, JPEG, resize, etc.), MaxMark retains 86.9% bit accuracy at 16,384 bits, while all baselines drop below 60%. MaxMark also maintains a perfect TPR@1%FPR of 1.00 across all payload sizes, and image quality metrics (CLIP Score 0.33, FID ≈ 41.8–42.2) remain comparable to unwatermarked generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaxMark decisively outperforms all six baselines at high payload sizes. Against the strongest diffusion-native baseline, Gaussian Shading [Yang et al., CVPR 2024], MaxMark gains 11.6 points at 8,192 bits (96.0% vs 84.4%) and 45.5 points at 16,384 bits (95.4% vs 49.9%), where Gaussian Shading essentially fails. PRC Watermark [Gunn et al., 2025] collapses to near-random accuracy (~50%) beyond 4,096 bits, while MaxMark maintains 95.4% at 16,384 bits. The post-processing baselines DwtDct [Al-Haj, 2007] and DwtDctSvd [Navas et al., 2008] degrade sharply at high capacity (59.1% and 64.1% at 16,384 bits) and are further vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Stable Signature [Fernandez et al., ICCV 2023] and DiffuseTrace [Lei et al., 2024] are infeasible or perform at chance level (~50%) beyond 4,096 bits due to per-message retraining costs or encoder-decoder asymmetry. Under adversarial conditions, MaxMark's 86.9% accuracy at 16,384 bits exceeds the next-best method (DwtDctSvd at 57.6%) by nearly 30 points, and it is the only method achieving TPR@1%FPR = 1.00 at the maximum payload.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaxMark builds on the RealNVP invertible neural network architecture (coupling blocks with affine transformations) and repurposes it for a new role: mapping watermarked latents back to the standard Gaussian prior required by LDMs. It departs from prior latent-based watermarking methods (Gaussian Shading, PRC Watermark, Tree-Ring) that directly perturb the initial noise and are inherently capacity-limited because perturbations must remain small to preserve image quality. It also contrasts with encoder-decoder approaches (DiffuseTrace) that accumulate distortion through asymmetric forward/inverse paths, and with model-modification methods (Stable Signature) that require per-payload retraining. The key conceptual departure is treating the distribution mismatch caused by watermark embedding as a learnable bijective transformation problem rather than a perturbation-minimization problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MaxMark addresses the central practical bottleneck in diffusion-native watermarking—capacity—by enabling reliable extraction at up to 16,384 bits, which is necessary for C2PA-style provenance metadata that prior methods cannot carry. The extension to video and audio modalities suggests the INN-based distribution transformation is a general-purpose tool for any latent diffusion pipeline. Open questions include robustness against adaptive or model-aware attacks (the evaluated attacks are relatively mild), whether the approach transfers to newer architectures like flow-based or rectified-flow models, and how performance scales with larger latent spaces in next-generation LDMs. The reliance on DDIM inversion for extraction also raises questions about compatibility with faster samplers that do not support exact inversion.
