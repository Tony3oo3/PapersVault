## Problem

Prevailing 3D texture generation methods rely on either multi-view fusion (introducing inter-view inconsistencies, seams, and incomplete occluded-region coverage) or 2D UV-space generation (fragmenting continuous 3D surfaces into disjointed islands that break geodesic neighborhoods). No existing approach generates textures as a truly native 3D representation that simultaneously avoids both fusion artifacts and UV-seam discontinuities while remaining extensible to other 3D attribute tasks like segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263615/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image from an arbitrary viewpoint and a 3D mesh as input. The image is first canonicalized to a front view via a finetuned diffusion model, then projected onto a 1024³ sparse voxel grid to form the conditioning signal. Output is a sparse 3D attribute grid (compressed to a 128³ latent with 16-dim embeddings by the VAE) that stores color, semantic labels, and PBR material channels per voxel; querying this grid via a pre-computed UV position map with trilinear interpolation yields 2048×2048 texture or segmentation maps.

## Method

TEXTRIX introduces a native 3D attribute grid—a sparse voxel field storing raw color, semantic, and PBR channels adjacent to the object surface—replacing DINOv2 feature projections or UV maps as the generative target. An end-to-end attribute VAE with a hybrid sparse-convolution + sparse-transformer architecture compresses the 1024³ grid to a 128³ latent (16-dim), trained with online rendering supervision, LPIPS, GAN, and KL losses. A Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with spatial sparse attention (following Direct3D-S2) generates latents under a rectified-flow objective, conditioned via a novel sparse latent conditioning strategy: the front-view image is projected into a 3D voxel grid, encoded by the VAE encoder, and injected through cross-attention alongside global DINOv3 features, bridging the domain gap between image conditions and the latent space. The same architecture extends to 3D part segmentation by training the DiT to predict semantic-label attributes instead of color, using 2D SAM masks merged by DINO feature similarity as the conditional input, producing sharp boundaries even on high-polycount meshes.

## Results

On texture generation (Table 1), TEXTRIX achieves SSIM 0.9421, PSNR 30.10, and LPIPS 0.0627 for front-view reconstruction, and CLIP Score 0.8545 / CLIP FID 19.85 for novel-view generation, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +4.56 PSNR over TRELLIS). On class-agnostic part segmentation (Table 3), the method reaches 60.82 mIoU on the Objaverse (Complex) subset, surpassing PartField (51.79) by 9.03 points, though it trails PartField slightly on the Objaverse (Random) subset (72.26 vs. 74.63).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEXTRIX decisively outperforms all three texture-generation baselines (Paint3D, TexGen, TRELLIS) on every metric in Table 1, with the largest gap over TRELLIS (+4.56 PSNR, +0.027 SSIM) and the smallest over TexGen on CLIP FID (19.85 vs. 22.15). For multi-view texturing (Table 2), combining TEXTRIX with MVAdapter yields the best CLIP score (0.8357) and lowest CLIP FID (21.76), marginally beating MVAdapter alone. On part segmentation, TEXTRIX wins by a large margin on the complex-mesh subset (60.82 vs. PartField's 51.79 mIoU) but trails PartField by 2.37 points on the random subset (72.26 vs. 74.63), which the authors attribute to minor inconsistencies on large, simple faces spanning multiple voxel cells. SAMesh and SAMPart3D are substantially outperformed on both segmentation subsets (by 25–30 mIoU points). Overall, the method is strongest on high-frequency geometric detail and front-view fidelity, with a small trade-off on simple, low-complexity meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in 3D texturing: multi-view fusion (TRELLIS, Hunyuan3D) that projects 2D diffusion outputs onto meshes, and UV-space generation (TexGen, Paint3D) that operates in fragmented 2D parameterization. It builds on the native 3D generation line (XCube, Direct3D-S2) by adopting a sparse voxel representation and spatial sparse attention, but replaces the DINOv2 feature-based appearance encoding with a raw attribute grid storing color, semantics, and PBR channels directly. The sparse latent conditioning strategy—projecting the input image into the same 3D voxel space and encoding it with the attribute VAE—repurposes the VAE encoder as a condition encoder, bridging the domain gap that plagues CLIP/DINO-based conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified sparse attribute grid offers a practical path toward a single architecture handling texture, segmentation, and PBR generation, reducing the need for task-specific pipelines in 3D content creation. However, the approach still requires a pre-existing mesh and a UV position map for querying, limiting its applicability to mesh-free or implicit representations. The segmentation advantage is concentrated on complex, high-polycount meshes (60.82 vs. 51.79 mIoU), while on simpler geometry it trails PartField slightly, suggesting the voxel-grid query introduces minor inconsistencies on large flat faces. Open questions include whether the 1024³ resolution and 32-A100 training cost are sustainable for broader adoption, and whether the unified representation truly generalizes to all attribute types beyond the demonstrated color and segmentation cases.
