## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models like VGGT have shown competitive accuracy with optimization-based SfM, but their scaling behavior with model capacity and data volume remains unexplored. VGGT's architecture is memory-intensive (high-resolution DPT heads, full global attention, multiple dense prediction heads), limiting how much supervised data and model size can be practically trained. Additionally, most training data is restricted to static scenes, ignoring the vast majority of Internet videos that contain dynamic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263011/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N RGB frames (batch size drawn uniformly from 1–24, longer side 512 px, aspect ratio in [0.33, 1.33]) and outputs per-frame camera parameters (rotation quaternion, translation vector, and 2-DoF field of view, with principal point fixed at image center) plus a depth map with a confidence channel. It also emits 16 learnable scene tokens per frame that aggregate global spatial context and can be consumed by downstream models (e.g., VLA policies). Point maps and tracking features are not explicit outputs but are supervised through auxiliary losses computed from the depth and camera predictions.

## Method

VGGT-Ω replaces 25% of VGGT's global attention layers with scene-token attention (self-attention restricted to 16 register tokens per frame), cutting backbone FLOPs substantially while preserving scene-level context. High-resolution DPT convolutional blocks are swapped for a single MLP followed by a pixel-shuffle upsampler, and all redundant dense heads (point maps, tracks) are removed in favor of a single depth head whose predictions drive multi-task losses (camera L1, depth with aleatoric uncertainty and gradient terms, unprojected point L1, and token-level matching BCE). Training data is expanded to ~4M sequences via a multi-stage annotation pipeline (VLM pre-filtering, Grounding DINO dynamic masking, ensemble feature matching, COLMAP bundle adjustment, multi-view consistency, and a handcrafted-feature classifier) applied to 40M internal videos, yielding 200K dynamic and 600K static annotated scenes. A DINO-inspired teacher-student self-supervised stage on 18M unlabeled videos further improves generalization. Scaling from 0.5B to 10B parameters and from 2K to 2M sequences yields consistent power-law-like reductions in 3D point error, and the 10B model achieves 77% relative improvement in AUC@3° over the prior best on Sintel while being 50× faster than MegaSaM.

## Results

On the dynamic Sintel benchmark, VGGT-Ω (10B) achieves AUC@3° of 40.0 for camera pose estimation, a 77% relative improvement over the previous best (MegaSaM at 22.5), and AUC@30° of 79.1 vs. 58.3 (35% improvement). For depth estimation on Sintel, it reaches δ₁.₂₅ of 93.5 and AbsRel of 0.081, surpassing the strongest feed-forward baseline DA3 (86.1 / 0.118). Across all six static and dynamic benchmarks, the 10B variant consistently outperforms the 1B variant, confirming that both model capacity and data scale yield monotonic gains in reconstruction accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-Ω (10B) outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark and both metrics (AUC@3° and AUC@30°). The most competitive feed-forward baseline, DA3 (Depth Anything 3), is surpassed by 19.1 points on Sintel AUC@3° (40.0 vs. 16.2) and by 7.6 points on ETH3D AUC@3° (56.3 vs. 46.1). The optimization-based dynamic method MegaSaM, which was the previous state of the art on Sintel, is beaten by 17.5 points on AUC@3° (40.0 vs. 22.5) while VGGT-Ω is 50× faster. The direct predecessor VGGT is outperformed by 25 points on Sintel AUC@3° (40.0 vs. 15.0) and by 12.6 points on 7 Scenes AUC@3° (36.4 vs. 10.9). Optimization-based methods MonST3R and MapAnything degrade severely on wide-baseline or low-texture scenes (e.g., MonST3R scores only 1.7 on ETH3D AUC@3°), whereas VGGT-Ω maintains strong performance uniformly. On the LIBERO VLA benchmark, frozen scene tokens from VGGT-Ω added to OpenVLA-OFT improve average success rate from 97.1% to 98.5%, validating their utility beyond reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGGT-Ω is a direct architectural and training-protocol revision of VGGT (Wang et al., CVPR 2025), the first feed-forward transformer to surpass optimization-based SfM pipelines. It departs from VGGT by replacing global attention with a sparse scene-token mechanism, swapping high-resolution DPT convolutional blocks for MLP-plus-pixel-shuffle decoders, and collapsing multiple dense prediction heads into a single depth head supervised by multi-task losses. The self-supervised teacher-student protocol draws on DINOv2/DINOv3, and the data annotation pipeline integrates COLMAP, VGGT, Grounding DINO, and modern feature matchers (SuperGlue, LightGlue) into a unified quality-filtering cascade. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of the DUSt3R/MASt3R feed-forward reconstruction lineage and the scaling-law literature from NLP, asking whether 3D reconstruction exhibits similar predictable gains from capacity and data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that feed-forward 3D reconstruction follows a clean power-law with both model size (0.5B→10B) and data volume (2K→2M sequences), providing the field with a clear scaling roadmap and demonstrating that data is not yet saturated. The 77% relative improvement in camera AUC@3° on Sintel over the best prior method (MegaSaM), while being 50× faster, shifts the practical default for dynamic scene reconstruction from iterative optimization to a single forward pass. The scene-token mechanism and single-head multi-loss design offer a practical template for making transformer-based 3D models trainable at scale on commodity GPU clusters. Open questions include whether the power-law holds beyond 10B parameters, whether the self-supervised protocol (which yielded only a modest 0.073→0.070 point-error gain) can be made more impactful, and how well the model generalizes to truly unstructured, long-horizon real-world sequences beyond the 24-frame training window.
