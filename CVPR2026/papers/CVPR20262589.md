## Problem

Chemical reaction diagrams in scientific literature exist as non-machine-readable images, blocking their use for training ML models. Existing parsing approaches (RxnScribe, RxnIM) force large vision-language models to predict bounding box coordinates via a 'BBox and Role in One Step' strategy, which falls outside LVLMs' core competencies and yields poor performance. Additionally, available datasets are either too small (RxnScribe: ~1,300 real samples) or artificially synthesized (RxnIM), limiting real-world generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a chemical reaction diagram image that has been pre-annotated with molecular bounding boxes and index numbers drawn directly onto the pixel canvas by the MolYOLO detector, producing a 'pre-annotated image' as visual input. The LVLM outputs a structured JSON listing every reaction with its reactants, conditions, and products, where molecular components are referenced by their pre-assigned index numbers and text components (e.g., reagent names, temperatures) are extracted as strings. During training, ground-truth boxes are used for molecules inside reaction regions while MolYOLO-detected boxes cover the rest; at inference, only MolYOLO predictions are available.

## Method

The core contribution is the BIVP (BBox and Index as Visual Prompt) strategy, which reformulates reaction diagram parsing as an image captioning task: a YOLOv10-based molecular detector (MolYOLO, trained on the newly constructed MolDet-33k dataset of 33k images with 219,721 annotated boxes) pre-draws bounding boxes and index labels onto the input image, and a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B then describes the reactions in natural language by referencing those indices. This two-stage pipeline bypasses the coordinate-prediction bottleneck that cripples LVLMs under the prior BROS paradigm. The model is trained on the newly released U-RxnDiagram-15k dataset (15,128 real-paper images, 45,426 reactions, balanced across single-line, multi-line, tree, and cyclic layouts) combined with an expanded RxnScribe set, using AdamW with cosine decay over 5 epochs on 8 A100 GPUs with DeepSpeed ZeRO-2. An ablation shows that swapping MolYOLO for weaker detectors (YoDe, MolDetect) drops Hybrid-F1 by up to 18.9 points, confirming the detector's critical role, while an error-attribution analysis reveals that the LVLM's reaction-extraction reasoning—not the detector—is the dominant remaining bottleneck.

## Results

RxnCaption-VL (BIVP strategy) achieves state-of-the-art performance on both test sets: HybridMatch F1 of 72.2 and SoftMatch F1 of 86.2 on RxnScribe-test, and HybridMatch F1 of 59.8 and SoftMatch F1 of 70.4 on U-RxnDiagram-15k-test. On the more challenging U-RxnDiagram-15k-test, it outperforms the strongest general VLM baseline (Gemini-2.5-Pro with BIVP) by 19.4 points in HybridMatch F1 and 3.8 points in SoftMatch F1. The BIVP strategy consistently outperforms the BROS strategy across all tested LVLMs, with the trained RxnCaption-VL gaining +3.0 Hybrid-F1 and +10.0 Soft-F1 over its BROS counterpart on RxnScribe-test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RxnCaption-VL (BIVP) outperforms all specialized RxnDP baselines: it beats RxnScribe_official by +3.1 Hybrid-F1 and +6.2 Soft-F1 on RxnScribe-test, and by a dramatic +24.9 Hybrid-F1 and +24.5 Soft-F1 on U-RxnDiagram-15k-test. Against RxnIM, the gap widens further on U-RxnDiagram-15k-test (+22.4 Hybrid-F1, +29.9 Soft-F1), confirming that synthetic training data suffers from domain shift. Among general LVLMs tested zero-shot with BIVP, RxnCaption-VL surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro by +22.4 Hybrid-F1 on RxnScribe-test and +19.4 on U-RxnDiagram-15k-test, and outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-72B by +22.1 and +32.9 Hybrid-F1 respectively. GPT-4o and Qwen-VL-Max trail by even larger margins (20+ points in Hybrid-F1 on both test sets). In the detector ablation, MolYOLO (built on YOLOv10) outperforms YoDe by +18.9 Hybrid-F1 and MolDetect by +1.4 Hybrid-F1 on RxnScribe-test when used as the BIVP visual prompt source, validating the detector as a critical pipeline component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the BROS (Bbox and Role in One Step) strategy used by RxnScribe and RxnIM, which forces LVLMs to jointly predict molecular bounding boxes and reaction roles in a single sequence-generation step. Instead, it draws on the visual prompting literature to introduce the BIVP strategy, where a dedicated detector (MolYOLO, built on YOLOv10) pre-annotates the image with boxes and indices, reducing the LVLM's job to natural-language captioning. The base LVLM is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, fine-tuned with DeepSpeed, and the evaluation protocol follows RxnScribe's SoftMatch/HybridMatch framework. The U-RxnDiagram-15k dataset extends the small real-paper RxnScribe benchmark by an order of magnitude while maintaining the same annotation schema.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BIVP reformulation offers a broadly applicable recipe for any LVLM-based structured extraction task where coordinate prediction is a bottleneck: offload spatial grounding to a lightweight detector and let the LVLM reason in language. The finding that RxnIM's 48k synthetic images underperform RxnScribe's 1.2k real images on out-of-distribution tests underscores that data synthesis for chemical diagrams has not yet closed the domain gap, a limitation that will constrain future scaling efforts. Cyclic and tree-layout reactions remain a clear performance bottleneck, and the error-attribution analysis shows the LVLM's reaction-assembly reasoning—not the detector—is the dominant ceiling, pointing to future work on stronger multimodal reasoning rather than better detection.
