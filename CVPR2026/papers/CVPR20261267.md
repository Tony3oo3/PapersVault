## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised dense video captioning methods (e.g., ILCACM) generate Gaussian masks that merely enforce non-overlap across temporal regions without ensuring semantic alignment between masked features and their corresponding event captions, resulting in simplistic, uniformly distributed masks. Additionally, the inherent sparsity of ground-truth annotations (often only 1–3 events per video) provides insufficient supervision for the model to learn fine-grained event localization.

## Input / Output

The method takes untrimmed video clips (ActivityNet ~120 s, YouCook2 ~320 s) with only caption annotations and no temporal boundary labels. Video frames are encoded by a CLIP spatial encoder into frame-level embeddings, which are then masked and passed through a video encoder; a Distilled-GPT2 decoder auto-regressively generates captions for both the positive (target event) and negative (remaining events) masked features. The model outputs per-event captions along with implicit temporal boundaries derived from the predicted Gaussian mask centers and widths.

## Method

SAIL introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of the ILCACM complementary-masking framework. First, a similarity-aware mask guide replaces the non-overlap-only constraint with a margin ranking loss (L_sim) that maximizes CLIP cross-modal cosine similarity between average-pooled masked video features and their corresponding caption embeddings while penalizing similarity to other event captions, forcing each Gaussian mask to highlight semantically salient regions. Second, an LLM-based augmentation strategy (Qwen3-8B) generates synthetic transitional captions between consecutive ground-truth annotations; these are incorporated through a separate inter-mask mechanism—new Gaussian masks centered at the midpoint of adjacent event centers with a fixed width—trained with an auxiliary alignment loss (L_aug) that softly guides the model toward denser event boundaries without contaminating the primary contrastive objective. The final training objective combines positive and negative captioning losses, the cross-modal similarity loss, and the weighted auxiliary augmentation loss.

## Results

On ActivityNet Captions, SAIL achieves a CIDEr of 35.38 and SODA_c of 6.29 for captioning, surpassing the previous weakly-supervised state-of-the-art ILCACM (CIDEr 33.42, SODA_c 6.08) and also outperforming fully-supervised methods like E2DVC (CIDEr 33.63) and CM2 (CIDEr 33.01). For event localization, SAIL attains an F1 of 57.00 (R@Avg 54.39, P@Avg 59.87), exceeding ILCACM (F1 56.20) and the best fully-supervised method PDVC (F1 56.71). On YouCook2, SAIL achieves CIDEr 14.61 and localization F1 20.94, improving over ILCACM (CIDEr 13.49, F1 17.88) by +1.12 and +3.06 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary weakly-supervised baseline ILCACM [11], SAIL wins on every reported metric: CIDEr +1.96 (35.38 vs 33.42), SODA_c +0.21, METEOR +0.15, ROUGE-L +0.52, and localization F1 +0.80 (57.00 vs 56.20). Remarkably, SAIL's weakly-supervised CIDEr of 35.38 surpasses all fully-supervised competitors including E2DVC (33.63), CM2 (33.01), and Vid2Seq (30.10), though E2DVC retains a slight edge on BLEU-4 (2.43 vs 2.30). On localization, SAIL's F1 of 57.00 exceeds the best fully-supervised method PDVC (56.71) and E2DVC (56.14). Older weakly-supervised baselines (WSDEC, ECG, EC-SL, PWS-DVC) are decisively outperformed, with CIDEr gaps of 12–21 points. On YouCook2, SAIL improves over ILCACM by +1.12 CIDEr and +3.06 F1, confirming consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAIL extends ILCACM (Ge et al., AAAAI 2025), which introduced differentiable Gaussian masks for implicit event localization via complementary captioning in weakly-supervised dense video captioning. The key departure is that ILCACM's mask learning only enforces temporal non-overlap through a diversity loss, whereas SAIL adds a CLIP-based cross-modal similarity objective that guides each mask to emphasize video regions semantically aligned with its corresponding caption. Additionally, SAIL introduces an LLM-based (Qwen3-8B) caption augmentation pipeline that generates synthetic transitional captions between consecutive ground-truth annotations, addressing the annotation sparsity problem that prior WSDVC methods (WSDEC, EC-SL, PWS-DVC) did not explicitly tackle. The broader methodological lineage traces through DETR-based fully-supervised DVC architectures (Vid2Seq, CM2, E2DVC) that SAIL inherits for its decoder and feature backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that a fixed, non-trainable Gaussian mask baseline performs comparably to ILCACM's learned masks is a useful revelation: it suggests the complementary masking framework's prior gains came primarily from the captioning objective rather than from meaningful mask learning, reframing what 'implicit localization' actually achieves in WSDVC. The LLM-based augmentation strategy is a practical, one-time preprocessing step that could be adapted to other weakly-supervised multimodal tasks suffering from sparse annotations, and the authors explicitly note this as future direction. However, the overall performance gains are modest (CIDEr +1.96, F1 +0.80 over ILCACM), the method is validated on only two datasets, and the quality of synthetic captions depends entirely on the LLM's world knowledge, which may not generalize to domains with highly specialized or atypical event sequences.
