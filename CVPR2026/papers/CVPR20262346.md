## Problem

EDM provides a unified design space for diffusion models but is restricted to pixel-wise independent Gaussian noise, preventing it from encompassing flow-based methods that diffuse arbitrary noise patterns. Moreover, EDM's forced injection of Gaussian noise into degraded images during restoration corrupts task-specific information, artificially extends the restoration distance, and increases task complexity. The paper asks whether a unified SDE-based framework can support arbitrary noise patterns without sacrificing EDM's structural flexibility or incurring extra computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded image as input (MRI slice with bias field, CT slice with metal streak artifacts, or natural RGB image with shadow) and produces the corresponding restored image. Critically, the reverse sampling process initiates directly from the known degraded image without adding extra Gaussian corruption, shortening the restoration path. The task-specific basis set H_{x0} encodes the noise structure: fixed low-frequency templates for smooth MRI bias, sample-dependent residual (B−A) for CT metal artifacts, and boundary-aware patterns for shadows. Only 5 Euler sampling steps are needed at inference, compared to 100 steps for Gaussian-diffusion baselines.

## Method

EDA replaces EDM's diagonal Gaussian covariance σ²(t)I with a multivariate Gaussian whose covariance Σ_{x0} = H_{x0}H_{x0}ᵀ is parameterized by an arbitrary basis set H_{x0} and a stochasticity mediator η, yielding a forward SDE driven by M independent Wiener processes. The key theoretical contribution is a rigorous proof (Proposition 1) that this framework can diffuse arbitrary noise patterns through three configurations: fixed basis sets, sample-dependent basis functions, and discrete non-Gaussian noise. Remarkably, Proposition 2 shows that all additional terms introduced by the structured covariance analytically cancel in the PFODE, so the deterministic sampling rule and denoiser training objective are identical to EDM's, incurring zero extra computational overhead. The denoiser D_θ(x;σ) is trained with the standard DDPM/DDIM noise-prediction objective, and sampling uses Euler's first-order method with the same update formula as EDM.

## Results

On MRI bias field correction (HCP), EDA achieves PSNR 38.02 dB, SSIM 0.99, and COCO 0.99 with only 5 sampling steps, outperforming the 100-step Refusion (PSNR 34.67) by ~3.4 dB and achieving the lowest CV for both GM (12.36) and WM (7.40). On natural image shadow removal (ISTD), EDA attains the best overall PSNR of 32.01 dB, SSIM 0.968, and RMSE 4.14, surpassing ShadowFormer (31.81/0.967/4.27). On CT metal artifact reduction (DeepLesion), EDA achieves an average PSNR/SSIM of 38.67/0.9823 using only image-domain input, competitive with dual-domain SOTA methods (DICDNet: 41.83/0.9923) while being ~53× faster than Refusion (0.182 vs 9.665 sec/slice).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MRI bias field correction, EDA decisively outperforms all baselines: it surpasses the strongest diffusion baseline Refusion by 3.35 dB PSNR (38.02 vs 34.67) and the best task-specific method ABCNet by 8.44 dB (38.02 vs 29.58), while also achieving the lowest tissue heterogeneity (CV). On CT metal artifact reduction, EDA (38.67/0.9823 average PSNR/SSIM) trails the dual-domain SOTA methods DICDNet (41.83/0.9923) and InDuDoNet+ (41.50/0.9891) by ~3 dB, but notably outperforms Refusion (38.15/0.9793) and MeanFlow (33.86/0.9276) using only image-domain input. On shadow removal, EDA achieves the best overall PSNR (32.01) and RMSE (4.14), narrowly beating ShadowFormer (31.81/4.27) and substantially outperforming Refusion (27.23/7.24) and MeanFlow (25.77/9.77). The ODE-based MeanFlow consistently underperforms across all three tasks, with the largest gap on shadow removal (9.77 vs 4.14 RMSE), attributed to its averaged trajectory failing to capture high-frequency texture in ill-posed restoration. EDA's 5-step sampling matches or exceeds Refusion's 100-step results on all three tasks, demonstrating a ~53× speedup in inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

EDA directly extends EDM (Karras et al., NeurIPS 2022), which unified diffusion model design spaces but was restricted to pixel-wise independent Gaussian noise. By replacing EDM's diagonal covariance with an unconstrained multivariate Gaussian parameterized by arbitrary basis functions and a stochasticity mediator η, EDA retains EDM's structural flexibility (noise schedules, training objectives) while enabling arbitrary noise patterns. The SDE/PFODE machinery is inherited from the score-based framework (Song et al.), and the training objective follows DDPM/DDIM. The paper positions itself against ODE-based alternatives (Flow Matching, MeanFlow) and operator-based methods (Cold Diffusion) that sacrifice SDE stochasticity, arguing that a unified SDE design space is essential for high-fidelity restoration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical insight is that starting the reverse process directly from the known degraded image (rather than from a Gaussian-corrupted version) shortens the restoration distance, enabling competitive results in under 5 sampling steps versus 100 for EDM-based methods like Refusion. The theoretical proof that arbitrary noise complexity introduces no additional sampling overhead is elegant and removes a perceived barrier to adopting structured noise in restoration pipelines. However, the paper acknowledges an inherent trade-off: the most general cases (sample-dependent basis functions) reduce stochasticity toward deterministic behavior, partially eroding the SDE advantage over ODE methods. Open questions include whether the SDE-over-ODE gap persists at scale, how to optimally choose basis functions for novel corruption types, and whether the framework extends naturally to 3D volumetric or video restoration.
