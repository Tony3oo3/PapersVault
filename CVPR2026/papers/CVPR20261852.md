## Problem

Existing noisy correspondence learning methods attribute error accumulation primarily to misclassified noisy pairs, but overlook a second source: anchor correlation discrepancy, where even clean anchor pairs exhibit inconsistent cross-modal geometric structures due to modality-specific local detail features. This discrepancy distorts the angular consistency used for soft label correction, causing re-estimated labels to progressively deviate from true semantics and amplifying error accumulation during training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (encoded to 2048-dim features) and text captions (encoded to 1024-dim features), both projected into a shared 2048-dim joint embedding space. Beyond standard retrieval rankings (R@1/5/10 for image-to-text and text-to-image), the pipeline produces re-estimated soft correspondence labels for noisy pairs, computed as the average of bidirectional angular ratios in the frequency domain. A Beta Mixture Model partitions the training set into clean and noisy subsets at each iteration, with a confidence threshold of 0.5.

## Method

GSL comprises three coupled components operating in the frequency domain. First, FoA² applies a discrete Fourier transform to spatial-domain features, concatenates amplitude and phase components into a joint frequency representation, and performs mean-centering to attenuate local detail features that cause anchor correlation discrepancy. Second, GALC selects the two most similar clean anchors for each noisy query, computes intra-modal similarity ratios (R_I, R_T) in the frequency domain, and derives the corrected soft label as the mean of bidirectional angular consistency scores (S_I2T and S_T2I). Third, the Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss (SCT) augments a standard hard-negative triplet loss with two regularization terms: an intra-modal semantic consistency penalty (MSE between image and text similarity matrices) and an inter-modal semantic asymmetry penalty (negative MSE between the cross-modal similarity matrix and its transpose), encouraging the model to rely on semantic rather than local-detail signals during optimization. Training proceeds in two phases: a 10-epoch warmup using only the triplet loss, followed by 40 epochs of full L_SCT optimization.

## Results

On Flickr30K, GSL achieves RSum of 507.5, 498.6, and 484.1 at 20%, 40%, and 60% noise levels respectively, outperforming the best baselines by 3.0, 4.8, and 7.0 points. On MS-COCO, GSL reaches RSum of 525.1, 520.9, and 513.2 across the same noise levels, with margins of 0.4, 2.0, and 1.9 over the strongest competitors. On the real-world noisy CC152K dataset, GSL attains an RSum of 379.6, surpassing the best baseline (L2RM at 374.2) by 5.4 points. GSL achieves either the best or second-best score across all individual R@1, R@5, and R@10 metrics in nearly every setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSL outperforms all 11 baselines on RSum across every dataset and noise level tested. The closest competitors vary by setting: BiCro (CVPR 2023) is the strongest on Flickr30K at 20% noise (504.7 vs. 507.5), PC2 (ACM MM 2024) leads among baselines at 40% noise on Flickr30K (493.8 vs. 498.6), and ESC (CVPR 2024) is the runner-up at 60% noise (477.1 vs. 484.1). On MS-COCO, L2RM (CVPR 2024) is the nearest competitor at 20% noise (524.7 vs. 525.1), while SPS (IJCAI 2025) edges out other baselines at 40% and 60% noise (518.9 and 511.3 vs. GSL's 520.9 and 513.2). On the real-world CC152K benchmark, L2RM achieves the best baseline RSum of 374.2, with GSL extending the lead to 379.6. Notably, NCR (NeurIPS 2021) degrades severely at 60% noise on Flickr30K (RSum drops to 316.9), highlighting the error accumulation problem that GSL specifically targets, while GSL maintains a much more graceful degradation (484.1). GSC (CVPR 2024), the method most directly extended by GSL's GALC module, is consistently outperformed by 3–10 RSum points across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noisy correspondence learning line initiated by NCR [7] and the geometric-structure-consistency label correction of GSC [12], but departs from them by identifying a previously overlooked error source: anchor correlation discrepancy arising from clean (anchor) pairs rather than noisy ones. The Fourier-based anchor alignment (FoA²) is a novel application of frequency-domain decomposition to cross-modal feature spaces, drawing on frequency-domain techniques from person re-identification [31, 32] rather than from the cross-modal retrieval literature. The Semantic-Constrained Triplet Loss builds on asymmetric similarity regularization ideas from [12, 14] but reframes them as explicit intra-modal consistency and inter-modal asymmetry constraints within a triplet framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of anchor correlation discrepancy as a distinct error-accumulation channel from clean pairs reframes how the community thinks about noisy correspondence learning, suggesting that even well-aligned anchor sets can propagate geometric distortion into label correction. However, the empirical gains are modest (3–7 RSum points on Flickr30K, 0.4–2 on MS-COCO), and the dramatic robustness gap at 60% noise on Flickr30K is partly driven by NCR's apparent collapse rather than GSL's absolute strength. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain alignment generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., audio-visual), whether the anchor discrepancy analysis holds under different backbone architectures, and whether the Beta Mixture Model partitioning remains reliable at very high noise rates where clean/noisy loss distributions overlap heavily.
