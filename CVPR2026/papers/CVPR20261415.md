## Problem

Existing visual token compression methods for LVLMs operate at the individual-token level without high-level semantic understanding, causing semantically distinct but texturally similar objects to be erroneously merged, intra-object redundancy to persist, or scene context to be lost. This paper introduces an object-centric paradigm that uses segmentation masks as a semantic prior to guide token merging, ensuring each distinct object is consolidated into a single compact representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261415/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (resized to 1024×1024) and a text prompt as input. The image is processed by a shared ConvNeXt-L backbone producing a multi-scale feature pyramid (1/4 to 1/32 resolution), from which Mask2Former generates object masks. The output is a variable-length sequence of compact object-centric visual tokens (typically 1–179 tokens in adaptive mode, or fixed at 160/320/640) that are spatially ordered by centroid position, followed by an auto-regressive text response from the LLM.

## Method

CORE routes features from a shared ConvNeXt-L backbone to a Mask2Former segmentation head that produces per-object probability masks via 300 learnable queries and a pixel-wise competitive filtering strategy. Each valid mask guides the merging of the corresponding visual tokens into a single compact representation via weighted average (soft masks) or arithmetic mean (hard masks), with the centroid of each mask used to sort the merged tokens in spatial order before feeding them to the LLM. The shared-encoder design means the segmentation pathway adds only ~0.30T FLOPs and 37.3M parameters on top of the backbone, keeping total visual-module cost below that of LLaVA-NeXT's CLIP ViT-L/14. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: first, only the MLP projector is trained on 558K image-caption pairs with all other components frozen; second, the projector and LoRA adapters (rank 512) on InternLM2-7B are fine-tuned on LLaVA-NeXT instruction data.

## Results

On fixed-rate compression at 640 tokens, CORE achieves 86.9 (POPE), 1521.6 (MME), 60.0 (MMB-CN), 69.2 (SQA-I), 67.6 (SEED-I), and 38.3 (MMMU), outperforming all competing methods on every benchmark. At the extreme 160-token setting, CORE retains 97.4% of its full-token baseline performance (e.g., 86.0 vs. 86.4 on POPE, 1405.3 vs. 1626.7 on MME). In adaptive-rate mode, CORE compresses 2880 tokens down to an average of 63.1 tokens, reducing FLOPs by 16.0×, KV Cache by 182.3×, and GPU memory by 3.0× (with 4-bit quantization) while maintaining 85.9/86.8 POPE score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORE dominates all 12 baselines across all six benchmarks at every tested compression rate (640, 320, and 160 tokens). At 640 tokens, the closest competitor DivPrune trails CORE by 51.9 points on MME (1469.7 vs. 1521.6), 2.7 on MMB-CN (57.3 vs. 60.0), 1.4 on SQA-I (67.8 vs. 69.2), and 1.4 on MMMU (36.9 vs. 38.3), while tying on POPE (86.9) and SEED-I (67.6). The gap widens at lower token budgets: at 160 tokens, CORE leads DivPrune by 6.0 on POPE (86.0 vs. 80.0), 48.7 on MME (1405.3 vs. 1356.6), and 3.0 on MMB-CN (56.7 vs. 53.7). In the dynamic adaptive setting (Table 2), CORE's hard-mask variant outperforms Slot-MLLM (the strongest object-centric baseline) by 5.8 on POPE (85.6 vs. 79.8), 194.1 on MME (1396.7 vs. 1202.6), and 10.7 on SEED-I (63.1 vs. 47.4). On efficiency, CORE at 160 tokens runs 2.04× faster than LLaVA-NeXT-7B (1122s vs. 2293s on POPE) while scoring 86.0 vs. 86.8, and 1.6× faster than SparseVLM at the same token budget. Notably, CORE's compressed model sometimes exceeds its own full-token baseline (e.g., 101.3% on SQA-I, 104.1% on MMMU at 640 tokens), which the authors attribute to the regularization effect of object-centric merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

CORE departs from the dominant token-centric compression paradigm (similarity-based merging, attention-based pruning, query-guided selection) by introducing an object-centric merging strategy that uses segmentation masks as a high-level semantic prior. Architecturally, it builds on LLaVA-NeXT's two-stage training pipeline and LLM decoder but replaces the single-scale CLIP ViT with a ConvNeXt-L backbone to natively provide the multi-scale feature pyramid required by the Mask2Former segmentation head. The shared-encoder design, where one ConvNeXt-L feeds both the segmentation pathway and the language pathway, is a key efficiency departure from multi-backbone object-centric VLMs like Slot-MLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× FLOPs and 182× KV Cache reduction while retaining 97.4% of baseline performance at 2.2% token retention offers a practically compelling efficiency path for deploying LVLMs at high resolution. The finding that compressed CORE sometimes outperforms the full-token baseline on certain benchmarks (POPE, SQA, MMMU) suggests object-centric merging acts as a beneficial regularizer, an observation with broader implications for how we think about token redundancy. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video (temporal object tracking), how it scales to open-vocabulary segmentation beyond the 134 predefined categories, and whether the acknowledged memory-bandwidth bottleneck can be resolved through systems-level optimization to realize the full theoretical speedup.
