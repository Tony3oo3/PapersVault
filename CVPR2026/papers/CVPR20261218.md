## Problem

ReFlow-based distillation methods like PeRFlow have been overlooked in practice despite their theoretical consistency with flow matching, because they suffer from a fundamental training-inference distribution mismatch: stage starting points are constructed via linear interpolation between data and noise during training, but during sequential inference each stage receives the output of the previous stage. This causes both a teacher trajectory mismatch (the teacher is evaluated off its own authentic path) and an inter-stage distribution mismatch (training and inference distributions diverge), leading to error accumulation that degrades few-step generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a single RGB image in 4 NFE (one forward pass per step, guidance-free). It is applied to Stable Diffusion 3 Medium and SD3.5 Large (MMDiT architecture) via LoRA fine-tuning. The teacher model uses classifier-free guidance with scale sampled uniformly from [7,13], while the distilled student operates at guidance scale ω=0, eliminating the dual forward pass. Training prompts are drawn from the FluxReason-6M dataset, and generation trajectories are created online during training.

## Method

FlowSteer introduces three components. First, Online Trajectory Alignment (OTA) replaces PeRFlow's off-trajectory linear interpolation with on-policy ODE solving: the starting state z_{t_k} for each stage is obtained by integrating the teacher's velocity field from pure noise down to time t_k, ensuring the teacher operates on its authentic trajectory and training distributions match inference. Second, an adversarial distillation loss is applied directly on the ODE trajectory: a discriminator built from a pretrained DiT backbone (12 blocks, fully fine-tuned) with a single head on the final block, trained with hinge loss plus feature matching, forces the student's 4-step trajectory to be perceptually indistinguishable from the teacher's multi-step trajectory. Third, the paper identifies and fixes a flaw in the standard FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler where the final step to σ=0 is disproportionately large in few-step regimes; the fix appends σ=0 to the 1000-step schedule before uniformly sampling N+1 points, ensuring proportional step sizes.

## Results

On SD3-Medium at 4 NFE, FlowSteer achieves PickScore 22.39, HPSv2 28.60, CLIP Score 32.81, and GenEval Overall 0.6859, outperforming all few-step baselines. On SD3.5-Large, it reaches PickScore 22.61, HPSv2 28.47, and GenEval Overall 0.6780, surpassing PeRFlow by 0.32 PickScore and 1.33 HPSv2. Notably, FlowSteer operates at 4×1 NFE (single forward pass per step), while PCM and Hyper-SD require 4×2 NFE due to classifier-free guidance, effectively doubling their inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowSteer outperforms PeRFlow (the closest ReFlow-based baseline) on every metric: +0.20 PickScore, +2.24 HPSv2, and +0.050 GenEval Overall on SD3-Medium, with similar margins on SD3.5-Large (+0.32 PickScore, +1.33 HPSv2). Against Flash Diffusion (DMD-based), FlowSteer wins on HPSv2 (28.60 vs. 27.35) and GenEval Overall (0.6859 vs. 0.6672) while matching PickScore (22.39 vs. 22.37), all at the same 4×1 NFE. Compared to PCM and Hyper-SD (consistency-based methods), FlowSteer leads on PickScore, HPSv2, and GenEval Overall, and does so with half the NFE (4×1 vs. 4×2) since it does not require the dual forward pass of classifier-free guidance. The pre-trained SD3-Medium at 20×2 NFE remains slightly ahead on PickScore (22.41 vs. 22.39), confirming that FlowSteer nearly matches full-quality generation at 5× fewer function evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits squarely within the ReFlow/Rectified Flow lineage for few-step image generation, directly extending PeRFlow's piecewise straightening strategy. It departs from prior ReFlow work by identifying that PeRFlow's offline linear interpolation creates a fundamental training-inference distribution mismatch for both teacher and student, and replaces it with on-policy ODE solving (OTA). The adversarial distillation component adapts the LADD-style discriminator to operate on intermediate ODE trajectory states rather than only final outputs, a novel application within the ReFlow framework. The CFG-distillation setup follows Meng et al. to produce guidance-free students, and the scheduler fix corrects a structural flaw in the widely-used FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler that disproportionately harms few-step inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The scheduler fix is broadly applicable: any practitioner using FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler for few-step flow matching inference (distilled or not) benefits from a few lines of code change, making this a practical contribution beyond the specific distillation method. The OTA principle—that training data should lie on the teacher's authentic trajectory rather than artificial interpolations—provides a clean diagnostic that may generalize to other piecewise or staged distillation schemes. Limitations include the doubled training cost from online ODE solving, evaluation restricted to the SD3/SD3.5 family at 4 steps, and modest absolute gains over the strongest baselines (Flash Diffusion). Open questions include whether OTA and trajectory-level adversarial loss transfer to video generation or to consistency-distillation frameworks, and whether the scheduler fix interacts differently with other shift values or model architectures.
