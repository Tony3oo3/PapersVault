## Problem

Casual photographers struggle to capture well-composed images, yet existing composition guidance is either limited to post-processing cropping or restricted to narrow camera-pose adjustments (e.g., yaw/pitch only). No prior framework simultaneously provides actionable textual instructions and a visual example image during the capturing process, leaving users without an intuitive, multi-modal composition assistant.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263532/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a poorly composed RGB image (resized to 512 shorter side, aspect ratio in [0.45, 2.2]) and a text task prompt (shift, zoom-in, view-change, or an auto prompt) as input. It produces two outputs jointly: a natural-language composition guidance describing specific adjustments with justifications, and a well-composed example image generated via 30-step flow-matching denoising from pure noise. The generated image attends to the predicted text guidance through the attention mechanism, making the textual instruction a conditioning signal for image synthesis rather than a separate post-hoc description.

## Method

PhotoFramer fine-tunes Bagel, a unified understanding-generation model, using a 207K-triplet dataset organized into three hierarchical sub-tasks (shift, zoom-in, view-change) that mirror how humans frame photos. The architecture consumes visual input through two token streams—FLUX VAE tokens for pixel-level generation and SigLIP2 ViT tokens for semantic understanding—and is trained with a combined cross-entropy loss on text tokens and a flow-matching loss on image latents. A key design choice is the two-stage inference: the model first predicts text guidance (understanding), then conditions image generation on that guidance, enabling instruction-driven synthesis. Auto prompts allow the model to adaptively fuse multiple operations (e.g., shift + zoom-in) rather than committing to a single task type. The view-change training pairs are synthesized via a two-stage degradation pipeline: a model trained on multi-view 3D data learns to produce poor compositions from good ones, then is applied to expert photos to create additional training pairs.

## Results

On the shift task, PhotoFramer achieves 80.4%/35.6% win rate (vs. original / vs. ground-truth) under GPT-5 evaluation and 88.1%/43.8% under human evaluation, substantially outperforming all open-source baselines. On the view-change task, PhotoFramer reaches 82.1%/50.5% (GPT-5) and 85.9%/47.3% (human), matching or surpassing gpt-image-1 (84.6%/51.7% GPT-5) while preserving higher image fidelity. For text-guidance consistency, PhotoFramer improves the average score from 83.10% (original Bagel) to 92.02% across all three sub-tasks. PhotoFramer also attains the highest DeQA quality score (4.07) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhotoFramer decisively outperforms all open-source editing baselines: on the shift task it achieves 80.4% vs. original win rate (GPT-5) compared to 46.0% for Qwen-Image-Edit, 39.9% for Kontext, and 27.6% for the original Bagel. Against the proprietary gpt-image-1, PhotoFramer wins on shift (80.4% vs. 69.9% GPT-5; 88.1% vs. 68.5% human) and zoom-in (67.2% vs. 52.7% GPT-5), while gpt-image-1 retains a narrow edge on view-change (84.6% vs. 82.1% GPT-5). However, the paper notes that gpt-image-1 frequently alters semantic details of the original image, whereas PhotoFramer preserves fidelity. For the composition assessment sub-model (Table 2), the 7B GRPO-trained model outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-32B (0.420/0.426 vs. 0.420/0.426 SRCC/PLCC on CADB) and all prior assessment methods (VFN, VEN, AutoPhoto, Q-Align). On text-guidance consistency (Table 4), PhotoFramer improves the average from 83.10% to 92.02% over the unfine-tuned Bagel, with gains of 14.95, 7.77, and 4.05 points on shift, zoom-in, and view-change respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhotoFramer builds on the unified understanding-generation architecture of Bagel [9], fine-tuning it to jointly produce textual composition guidance and a well-composed example image. It departs from prior composition guidance work—retrieval-based systems [12,13,76] and CPAM [31] (which predicts only yaw/pitch adjustments)—by supporting a broader set of operations (shift, zoom-in, view-change) and by generating both text and image in a single model. The dataset construction repurposes existing cropping datasets (GAIC, CPC, SACD, etc.) for shift/zoom-in pairs and introduces a degradation model trained on multi-view 3D data (DL3DV-10K) to synthesize view-change pairs from expert photos, a pipeline not seen in prior composition literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhotoFramer offers a practical, pre-capture composition assistant that makes expert photographic priors accessible to casual users, moving composition help from post-processing to the moment of shooting. The 207K triplet dataset and the demonstration that textual guidance meaningfully steers image generation (ablation in Fig. 11) provide a useful resource and validation for unified text-image models in aesthetic tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video composition, whether the synthetic view-change pairs (via the degradation model) introduce biases that limit real-world transfer, and whether the 512px resolution constraint is sufficient for practical deployment on mobile devices.
