## Problem

Real-world image restoration datasets suffer from suboptimal ground truth images due to practical acquisition constraints—deblurring GTs come from video frames with residual camera shake, and denoising GTs are constructed by averaging noisy captures, introducing blur. Models trained on these imperfect references inherit their limitations, capping achievable perceptual quality regardless of architectural sophistication. The paper identifies this supervision bottleneck as the key unaddressed constraint in real-world restoration.

## Input / Output

The supervision enhancement framework takes an original ground truth image, upsamples it at three scale factors (2×, 3×, 4×) via bicubic interpolation, applies OSEDiff super-resolution to each, and downsamples back to original resolution to produce N=3 enhanced variants. The frequency-domain mixup then fuses the original GT with these variants using adaptive masks to yield a single enhanced GT image at the original resolution. The downstream ORNet takes the output of any frozen pre-trained restoration model (e.g., FFTformer, NAFNet) along with a scalar parameter λ∈[0,1] controlling enhancement strength, and produces a refined restored image at the same resolution as the input (512×512 in experiments).

## Method

The core mechanism is a conditional frequency mask generator that produces N+1 adaptive masks by combining 25 predefined ring-shaped Gaussian basis masks with coefficients predicted by a small network g (4 NAFBlocks + 2 FC layers) that jointly consumes the RGB image and its FFT representation. The enhanced GT is constructed as F⁻¹(Σᵢ Mᵢ ⊙ F(Iᵢ^GT)), where softmax over the masks ensures they sum to one at every frequency location, preserving low-frequency semantics from the original GT while selectively incorporating high-frequency perceptual details from super-resolved variants. Training balances an L2 reconstruction loss against the original GT with a no-reference IQA perceptual loss (MUSIQ, MANIQA, TOPIQ), weighted by λ. The lightweight ORNet (4.5M params, 20G MACs, a 4-encoder/1-middle/4-decoder U-Net of NAFBlocks) is trained to map the original GT toward the enhanced GT, making it a plug-and-play module that can be attached to any restoration backbone without architectural changes or retraining.

## Results

On GoPro deblurring, ORNet (applied to AdaRevD) achieves MUSIQ 64.25, MANIQA 0.5916, TOPIQ 0.4880, and LIQE 2.4291, substantially outperforming the best baseline FFTformer (MUSIQ 46.47, MANIQA 0.5420, TOPIQ 0.3456, LIQE 1.6130). On SIDD denoising, ORNet (applied to NAFNet) reaches MUSIQ 35.87, MANIQA 0.4380, TOPIQ 0.3776, and LIQE 1.9591, versus the best baseline NAFNet alone (MUSIQ 22.73, MANIQA 0.3937, TOPIQ 0.2458, LIQE 1.2189). The A-FINE fidelity metric (lower is better) drops from 36–45 for baselines to 14.38 with ORNet on GoPro, confirming that the gains are not at the expense of semantic fidelity. ORNet adds only 4.5M parameters and 20G MACs, negligible compared to the 15–116M parameters of the base restoration models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORNet wins decisively across all 10 baselines on every perceptual quality metric for both GoPro deblurring and SIDD denoising. On GoPro, the strongest baseline FFTformer [17] scores MUSIQ 46.47 and TOPIQ 0.3456, while ORNet (on AdaRevD) reaches MUSIQ 64.25 and TOPIQ 0.4880 — a gap of ~18 MUSIQ points and ~0.14 TOPIQ. Diffusion-based baselines (IR-SDE [28], DiffIR [44], HI-Diff [6]) achieve slightly higher MUSIQ (45.86–46.13) than CNN/Transformer baselines but still fall ~18 points short of ORNet. On SIDD, the best baseline NAFNet [5] scores MUSIQ 22.73, whereas ORNet (on NAFNet) reaches 35.87, a 13-point improvement; Xformer [6] and MIRNet-v2 [50] are similarly outperformed by 13+ MUSIQ points. The A-FINE fidelity metric (lower is better) confirms that ORNet's perceptual gains do not come from hallucination: it drops from 36–45 (baselines) to 14.38 on GoPro and from 52–58 to ~41 on SIDD. No baseline matches or exceeds ORNet on any reported metric in either task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of improving restoration through better architectures or generative training objectives (e.g., diffusion-based methods like DiffIR, IR-SDE, HI-Diff) by instead identifying the ground truth itself as the performance ceiling. It builds on OSEDiff (one-step diffusion SR) as a component to generate perceptually enhanced GT variants, but the core novelty lies in the conditional frequency-domain mixup with ring-shaped Gaussian basis masks, which selectively fuses frequency components to avoid the hallucination artifacts that plague purely generative approaches. The lightweight ORNet refinement module is positioned as a model-agnostic alternative to the costly retraining or finetuning that prior perceptual-quality methods (e.g., DiffIR, ResShift) require, drawing on NAFNet-style blocks for its architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck that many restoration practitioners encounter but rarely formalize: suboptimal GT from real-world datasets (video-frame deblurring, multi-frame denoising) fundamentally caps achievable quality regardless of model sophistication. The plug-and-play ORNet (4.5M params, 20G MACs) offers a low-cost path to substantially better perceptual quality without modifying or retraining existing restoration backbones, which has immediate practical value. Open questions include whether the frequency-domain mixup strategy generalizes to video restoration or multi-degradation scenarios, whether the approach can be extended to self-supervised or zero-shot settings, and whether the dependence on a strong external SR model (OSEDiff) limits applicability when no suitable SR prior exists for a given domain.
