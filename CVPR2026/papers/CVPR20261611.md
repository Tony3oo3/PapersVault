## Problem

Existing procedural error-detection methods assume a single-view setting, yet in practice the reference demonstration is often a third-person (exo) video while the execution to be assessed is first-person (ego). This Ego→Exo cross-view regime introduces three coupled difficulties—temporal misalignment between asynchronously recorded streams, heavy redundancy that dilutes attention, and a pronounced appearance/motion domain gap between ego and exo footage—that prior single-view or naive-concatenation approaches fail to address.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of asynchronously captured, length-mismatched RGB videos (an exocentric demonstration V^exo of length T_x and an egocentric imitation V^ego of length T_y) and produces a variable-size set of predictions on the ego timeline, each consisting of normalized start/end times in [0,1] and a binary imitation-correctness label. A frozen TSP encoder (d=512) extracts per-frame features from both streams; the ego-side adaptive sampler conditions on the already-resampled exo features, creating an asymmetric information flow that encodes demonstration cues into the ego selection process.

## Method

SAVA-X is an Align-Fuse-Detect pipeline built on a frozen TSP backbone and a PDVC-style deformable Transformer encoder-decoder. (1) Gated adaptive sampling uses Gumbel-TopK hard indices with a residual soft-gating path: the exo stream is scored by self-attention, while the ego stream is scored by cross-attention against the resampled exo summary, so that ego selection is explicitly conditioned on demonstration key points. (2) Scene-adaptive view embeddings are generated by cross-attending each stream's features against a shared M×d dictionary of view sub-factors, then injected both pre-fusion and at multiple encoder levels; attention-entropy and dictionary-orthogonality regularizers prevent over-sharpening and prototype collapse. (3) Bidirectional cross-attention lets each stream retrieve complementary evidence from the other (exo→ego for boundary/ordering cues, ego→exo for hand-object details), followed by learnable sigmoid-gated residual mixing that preserves view-specific representations in redundant regions. Training jointly optimizes a Hungarian-matched DVC localization loss and a binary imitation-correctness classification loss (λ=0.5), with auxiliary selection-entropy and VICReg-style decorrelation terms.

## Results

On the EgoMe validation set, SAVA-X achieves a Mean AUPRC of 22.36 and tIoU of 59.31, outperforming the strongest baseline Exo2EgoDVC (Mean AUPRC 19.69, tIoU 59.06) by +2.67 absolute (+13.56% relative) in AUPRC. On the test set, SAVA-X reaches Mean AUPRC 18.50 and tIoU 58.32, again leading all baselines. The gains are consistent across all tIoU thresholds (0.3, 0.5, 0.7), with the largest relative improvement at the strict 0.7 threshold where SAVA-X scores 9.48 vs. 7.49 for Exo2EgoDVC on validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVA-X outperforms all four baselines on every tIoU threshold and both validation and test splits. Among DVC baselines, Exo2EgoDVC is the strongest (Mean AUPRC 19.69 val / 15.99 test), while PDVC trails slightly (18.88 / 16.20); SAVA-X improves over Exo2EgoDVC by +2.67 (val) and +2.51 (test) in Mean AUPRC. TAL baselines (ActionFormer, TriDet) are substantially weaker, particularly at stricter thresholds: ActionFormer drops to 2.63 AUPRC@0.7 on validation versus SAVA-X's 9.48, and its tIoU (48.89) is roughly 10 points below SAVA-X's 59.31, indicating poor boundary localization. A single-view ablation (PDVC with Ego input only) yields Mean AUPRC of just 12.79, confirming that the exocentric demonstration is essential for step localization and error discrimination. Overall, SAVA-X's advantage is most pronounced at the 0.5 and 0.7 tIoU thresholds, where precise boundary alignment and cross-view evidence fusion matter most.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVA-X sits at the intersection of dense video captioning (PDVC, Exo2EgoDVC) and temporal action localization (ActionFormer, TriDet), repurposing the set-prediction decoding paradigm of DVC for a cross-view error-detection objective. It departs from prior ego-exo transfer work (Ego-Exo, Viewpoint Rosetta Stone, synchronization-based alignment) by treating the view-domain gap as a learnable, scene-conditioned embedding problem rather than an adversarial or unsupervised alignment problem, and from adaptive frame-selection literature by coupling Gumbel-TopK hard selection with residual gating to preserve gradient flow. The frozen TSP backbone and deformable Transformer decoder follow the standard 'frozen encoder + lightweight task head' recipe, while the scene-aware dictionary view embeddings and bidirectional gated cross-attention are the paper's principal architectural departures from single-view or one-way-fusion baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formalization of cross-view (ego→exo) imitation error detection, a setting with clear practical demand in industrial training, medical protocol adherence, and robot learning from demonstration, yet previously unaddressed because no suitable paired dataset existed. The EgoMe benchmark and the unified evaluation protocol (AUPRC at multiple tIoU thresholds plus standalone tIoU) give the community a reproducible starting point. However, absolute performance remains modest (mean AUPRC ≈ 22 on test), the method is validated on a single dataset with a frozen backbone, and open questions include whether the scene-adaptive dictionary generalizes to unseen procedural domains, whether the framework extends to streaming/online settings, and whether the bidirectional fusion can be made more efficient for long videos.
