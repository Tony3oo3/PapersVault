## Problem

Personalizing unified autoregressive (AR) models for subject-driven image generation faces a fundamental dilemma: adaptation-based methods (LoRA, DreamBooth) overfit severely on 3–5 reference images and require unbounded per-concept weight storage, while existing concept-injection methods confine learnable tokens to the input layer, causing fine-grained identity cues to attenuate through dozens of transformer blocks and resulting in poor visual fidelity and impaired re-contextualization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263071/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For subject personalization, the method takes 3–5 reference images of a target subject plus a text prompt template ('a photo of [V] [Class]') and generates the subject in novel scenes described by 25 test prompts. For stylization, a single style reference image suffices, and subject–style composition at inference simply concatenates the pre-trained subject and style context tokens with an Identity Mask to prevent cross-attention interference. All generation operates on discrete VQ-VAE tokens via the frozen Lumina-mGPT-7B backbone, producing standard RGB images.

## Method

DCoAR introduces Layer-wise Multimodal Context Learning (LMCL), which injects one learnable text token and one learnable image token into each of the first 9 transformer layers (0.073M total parameters) rather than only at the input, enabling persistent concept reinforcement through depth. Training is stabilized by two regularizers: a Dual Prior Preservation (DPP) loss that computes NTP loss on class images plus KL divergence between zero-shot and concept-conditioned output distributions to prevent semantic drift, and a Context-Aware Self-Regularization (CASR) loss that initializes image tokens with the frozen model's average subject embeddings and constrains them via L2 distance to prevent over-specialization. The backbone remains entirely frozen, and at inference, arbitrary subject–style combinations are achieved training-free by concatenating their respective context tokens under an Identity Mask that restricts attention flow between token types.

## Results

On DreamBench subject-driven personalization, DCoAR achieves the best CLIP-I (0.815) and CLIP-T (0.318) scores among all methods, while ranking second on DINO (0.723) behind Proxy-Tuning (0.752). DCoAR requires only 0.073M trainable parameters, compared to 142.6M for Proxy-Tuning and 1610.6M for PersonalAR. On StyleDrop style personalization, DCoAR achieves the highest text-alignment (0.308) and style-alignment (0.605), trailing ZipLoRA only on subject-alignment (0.604 vs. 0.655).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCoAR outperforms all concept-injection baselines (Yo'Chameleon, UniCTokens) by a wide margin on every metric, e.g., CLIP-I 0.815 vs. 0.795/0.782 and CLIP-T 0.318 vs. 0.225/0.304, while using fewer parameters (0.073M vs. 0.13M). Against the strongest adaptation-based method Proxy-Tuning, DCoAR wins on CLIP-I (0.815 vs. 0.809) and CLIP-T (0.318 vs. 0.312) but trails on DINO (0.723 vs. 0.752), yet requires ~1950× fewer trainable parameters (0.073M vs. 142.6M). DCoAR also surpasses PersonalAR on all three metrics (DINO 0.723 vs. 0.671, CLIP-I 0.815 vs. 0.805, CLIP-T 0.318 vs. 0.302) with 22,000× fewer parameters. Among diffusion-based baselines, DCoAR beats DreamBooth(SD) and DreamBooth(Flux) on CLIP-I and CLIP-T, and matches or exceeds DreamBooth(Imagen) on CLIP-I (0.815 vs. 0.812). For style personalization, DCoAR dominates B-LoRA on all three metrics (0.604/0.605/0.308 vs. 0.579/0.505/0.258) and beats ZipLoRA on style-alignment and text-alignment, though it lags ZipLoRA on subject-alignment (0.604 vs. 0.655).

## Relation to Prior Work

DCoAR extends the concept-injection paradigm for unified AR models (Yo'Chameleon, UniCTokens) by replacing their shallow, input-layer-only token injection with a Layer-wise Multimodal Context Learning strategy that inserts learnable tokens across multiple transformer layers. It adapts DreamBooth's prior-preservation idea to the discrete token setting via a Dual Prior Preservation loss, and introduces Context-Aware Self-Regularization to combat overfitting from limited reference images. The paper positions itself explicitly against the adaptation-based paradigm (LoRA, Proxy-Tuning, PersonalAR), arguing that deep injection can close the fidelity gap while retaining the scalability advantage of a frozen backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCoAR demonstrates that fewer than 0.1M trainable parameters can match or exceed methods requiring 142M+ parameters on key fidelity and alignment metrics, making large-scale personalization in AR models practically feasible. The training-free subject-style composition via token concatenation is a practical contribution that avoids per-style optimization. However, evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Lumina-mGPT-7B) and a single benchmark (DreamBench), and the DINO gap with Proxy-Tuning (0.723 vs. 0.752) suggests the fidelity ceiling of concept injection may not be fully closed. Open questions include generalization to other AR architectures, multi-subject composition, and whether deeper injection benefits emerge with more reference images.
