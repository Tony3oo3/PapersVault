## Problem

Prior IMU+UWB pose estimators treat inter-sensor distances as auxiliary scalar features, ignoring the geometric constraints they impose on sensor positions. This prevents recovery of the full 3D sensor layout and allows predictions to directly contradict measured distances (e.g., estimating wrists 70 cm apart when UWB reports 90 cm). The paper asks whether explicitly modeling these distances as spatial constraints during both conditioning and sampling can improve pose accuracy and physical plausibility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260898/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of N frames from 6 body-worn IMUs (orientations R ∈ R^{6×3×3} and accelerations A ∈ R^{6×3} in a common global frame) plus pairwise UWB distance matrices D ∈ R^{6×6}, along with a fixed SMPL shape vector β. It outputs SMPL local joint rotations in 6D representation (Θ ∈ R^{24×3}) and global body translation (T ∈ R^3) per frame, forming a 147-dimensional motion vector. For temporal coherence, the model operates autoregressively by prepending N_H previously predicted frames as history, and the root joint is canonically normalized to start at (0, root_height, 0).

## Method

UDP is a fully learnable autoregressive diffusion-inpainting model (DDPM with an LSTM denoiser) that integrates UWB geometric constraints at two levels. First, a Spatial Layout Module applies metric MDS in closed form to the pairwise distance matrix to recover 3D sensor positions, then a learnable 3-layer LSTM Rotation Estimator orients both the MDS layout and its mirror into a consistent frame, adding per-sensor residuals to absorb noise; this oriented layout serves as a strong geometric prior in the conditioning signal. Second, UWB-Diffusion Guidance applies classifier guidance during sampling: at each denoising step, in-the-loop forward kinematics maps the predicted SMPL pose to sensor positions, and the gradient of the squared distance mismatch between predicted and measured UWB distances shifts the sampling mean, steering predictions toward distance-consistent poses without per-frame post-optimization. Training uses the standard DDPM loss plus auxiliary translation, SMPL joint-angle, and velocity losses, avoiding the expensive FK-based losses used by DiffusionPoser.

## Results

UDP achieves the lowest joint position error (JPE) across nearly all evaluation settings, with up to 22% improvement over the best IMU+UWB baseline and up to 35% over IMU-only methods. On DIP-IMU, UDP reaches 3.42 cm JPE and 10.39° SIP, outperforming the next-best IMU+UWB method UMotion (4.38 cm JPE, 15.05° SIP). On the real-world GIP-DB dataset with modern UWB sensors, UDP achieves 8.86 cm JPE zero-shot and 6.68 cm after fine-tuning, beating GIP (9.45 cm) and UMotion (9.52 cm). UDP also produces the lowest jitter (0.125 m/s³ on DIP-IMU) without requiring a physics optimizer, though it underperforms UIP on the noisier UIP-DB in the zero-shot setting (11.72 vs 10.65 cm JPE).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IMU-only baselines (TIP, PIP, PNP, GlobalPose, DynaIP), UDP consistently wins on JPE across all five datasets, with the largest gap on DanceDB (4.67 vs 7.18–8.87 cm) and TotalCapture (3.76 vs 4.25–5.61 cm), confirming that explicit UWB geometric modeling provides a substantial advantage over inertial-only approaches. Among IMU+UWB methods, UDP outperforms UIP and UMotion on JPE in every setting except UIP-DB zero-shot, where UIP's lighter reliance on UWB (10.65 cm) edges out UDP (11.72 cm) due to >17 cm UWB noise; however, after fine-tuning UDP recovers to 9.04 cm, beating UIP's 10.65 cm. On the modern GIP-DB dataset with lower UWB noise, UDP achieves SOTA in both zero-shot (8.86 cm JPE) and fine-tuned (6.68 cm) settings, surpassing GIP (9.45 / 8.70 cm) and UMotion (9.52 / 9.34 cm). UDP also produces the lowest jitter in every dataset (e.g., 0.125 m/s³ on DIP-IMU vs 0.240 for UIP), demonstrating that in-loop UWB-Diffusion Guidance yields smoother motion than post-hoc physics optimization used by PIP, PNP, and UIP. The one clear loss is on UIP-DB zero-shot SIP (24.95° vs UIP's 24.12°), which the authors attribute to UDP's tighter integration of noisy UWB signals into the architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the IMU+UWB motion capture line (UIP, UMotion, GIP) by departing from the standard practice of treating inter-sensor UWB distances as auxiliary scalar features. Instead, it explicitly models the geometric constraints they impose: a Spatial Layout Module reconstructs 3D sensor positions via closed-form MDS, and a UWB-Diffusion Guidance mechanism steers the denoising process using in-the-loop forward kinematics. The autoregressive diffusion inpainting backbone follows DiffusionPoser, while the classifier-guidance formulation draws on Dhariwal & Nichol's diffusion guidance framework, repurposed here for distance-consistency rather than class-conditional generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 22% JPE reduction and consistently lowest jitter across five datasets demonstrate that explicitly enforcing on-body geometric constraints during generation yields more physically plausible poses than post-hoc optimization or feature-level fusion. However, the tight integration makes UDP sensitive to very noisy UWB (underperforming UIP zero-shot on UIP-DB with 17.5 cm errors), and the fixed six-sensor setup limits real-world flexibility. Open questions include extending to arbitrary sensor layouts, inter-person UWB constraints, environment-fixed beacons, and whether physics-based guidance terms can be integrated into the diffusion loop to address foot-skating and ground-penetration artifacts.
