## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (e.g., VGGT, DUSt3R) recover geometry only for regions visible in input views, leaving unseen object geometry unmodeled. While unified reconstruction-and-generation frameworks like Matrix3D address this gap, their diffusion-based design incurs prohibitive inference costs (~27 s per view), making real-time interactive use infeasible. The paper asks whether a single feed-forward transformer can both reconstruct observed geometry and generate plausible unseen structure and appearance in under a second.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263168/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 4 unposed 256×256 RGB source images (tokenized by a DINO ViT) and a target camera pose encoded as a Plücker ray map. The first source view is pinned to a canonical pose [I | 0,0,−1] to define the world coordinate frame. Outputs include per-source-view camera poses, a novel-view RGB image, and a pixel-aligned 3D point map with an uncertainty channel; accumulating point maps across multiple queried viewpoints yields a complete 3D point cloud. The entire pipeline runs in ~0.2 s for reconstruction plus <0.1 s per novel view on an A800 GPU.

## Method

RnG inherits VGGT's 24-layer interleaved global/frame attention architecture and pretrained weights, then introduces a reconstruction-guided causal attention mask that forbids source-view query tokens from attending to target-view keys while allowing the reverse. This single binary mask decouples reconstruction (source tokens estimate poses and geometry) from generation (target tokens synthesize appearance and depth) at the attention level while sharing all parameters. The causal structure makes the source-view K/V tokens in each global attention block a viewpoint-independent implicit 3D representation: at inference, these are cached once (Stage 1, ~0.2 s), and arbitrary target Plücker rays then query the cache directly without recomputing source-view attention (Stage 2, <0.1 s per view). Two DPT heads decode the target-view tokens into an RGB image and a point map, supervised jointly with MSE+perceptual loss, aleatoric-uncertainty point-map loss, and Huber camera-pose loss. The design is 100× faster than diffusion-based Matrix3D while matching or exceeding its reconstruction and generation quality.

## Results

On the Google Scanned Objects (GSO) benchmark with 4 unposed input views, RnG achieves 85.15 RA@5 and 86.02 RT@5 for camera pose estimation, 0.584 relative error for source-view depth, and 0.717 relative error for novel-view depth. For novel view synthesis, RnG reaches 26.28 PSNR, 0.891 SSIM, and 0.0975 LPIPS. The complete 3D reconstruction (Chamfer Distance) is 0.0067, an order of magnitude better than the next best unposed method (VGGT at 0.0260). RnG runs in ~0.2 s for reconstruction and <0.1 s per novel view on an A800 GPU, over 100× faster than diffusion-based Matrix3D (27 s per view).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unposed reconstruction baseline VGGT, RnG improves pose estimation by ~11 points in RA@5 (85.15 vs 74.24) and reduces source-view depth relative error by 10× (0.584 vs 5.960), while additionally providing novel-view depth and RGB synthesis that VGGT cannot produce. Compared to the unified unposed model Matrix3D, RnG dominates across every metric: pose RA@5 (85.15 vs 43.77), source depth Rel (0.584 vs 9.430), novel-view depth Rel (0.717 vs 9.964, an order-of-magnitude gap), novel-view PSNR (26.28 vs 18.74), and complete 3D Chamfer Distance (0.0067 vs 0.0670, a 10× improvement). Against the best posed novel-view synthesis method LVSM, RnG achieves a slightly lower PSNR (26.28 vs 27.52) but does so without requiring accurate camera poses as input, and matches LVSM's SSIM (0.891 vs 0.902). RnG also substantially outperforms the posed Gaussian-splatting method LGM on novel-view synthesis PSNR (26.28 vs 13.70) and avoids the layering artifacts that plague multi-view Gaussian stacking. In terms of efficiency, RnG with KV-Cache renders a novel view in 85 ms versus 27 s for Matrix3D, a >300× speedup, while using less peak memory (7.10 GB vs 11.31 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

RnG inherits the architecture and pretrained weights of VGGT, a feed-forward 3D reconstruction foundation model, and extends it with a reconstruction-guided causal attention mechanism that decouples source-view reconstruction from target-view generation at the attention level. Conceptually, it inverts the prevailing direction of knowledge transfer in the field: whereas prior unified models like Matrix3D repurpose diffusion-based generative priors to aid 3D perception, RnG demonstrates that reconstruction priors can drive novel view generation. The KV-Cache reinterpretation as an implicit, viewpoint-independent 3D representation is a novel architectural insight that enables a two-stage inference pipeline (reconstruct once, then query at arbitrary poses), drawing on causal attention patterns from language and streaming vision models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RnG achieves real-time complete 3D modeling (reconstruction + novel view RGBD generation) from a few unposed images in under a second, a 100× speedup over diffusion-based unified models like Matrix3D, making interactive applications in robotics and AR practically feasible. The finding that reconstruction priors outperform generative priors for novel view synthesis challenges the assumption that diffusion backbones are necessary for high-fidelity generation. Key limitations include reduced fine detail compared to diffusion models, a world-origin bias from the training pipeline, and noise in multi-view point cloud accumulation; extending to dynamic and non-rigid scenes remains open.
