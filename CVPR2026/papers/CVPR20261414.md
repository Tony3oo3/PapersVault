## Problem

Active grasping in clutter requires selecting informative views before grasp prediction, but prior methods estimate information gain from visibility or affordance maps (biasing toward scene completion) or project grasp distributions onto 2D/3D grids (ignoring SE(3) manifold structure). Additionally, uncalibrated grasp models produce biased entropy estimates, making information gain unreliable. No existing approach simultaneously computes task-specific information gain from a calibrated grasp distribution on the full SE(3) manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multiple RGBD views (800×800 in simulation) with a 2D target mask to initialize a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene representation with a semantic channel, plus 128 candidate view poses sampled via Spherical Fibonacci Sampling on a sphere of radius 0.3–0.5 m around the target. The 3DGS is downsampled to 512 foreground and 512 background points (fused with one-hot semantic vectors) as input to the energy-based model. Outputs include the selected next-best-view pose, SE(3) grasp poses sampled via Annealed Langevin Dynamics, and calibrated success/failure probabilities per grasp.

## Method

ActiveGrasp defines information gain as the entropy reduction of the conditional grasp-success distribution H[S|G,W], where each grasp is a Bernoulli variable, and computes this entropy via the Gaussian Approximation of Posterior (GAP) over the 3DGS scene parameters combined with a calibrated energy-based model on SE(3). The EBM is trained with dual denoised score matching (on both successful and failed grasps) plus a differentiable Average Precision loss and a learnable temperature, replacing the fixed T=1 of standard EBMs to stabilize energy levels and align predicted probabilities with true success rates. The Hessian of the entropy function is approximated as ∇η∇ηᵀ + λI, enabling efficient computation of information gain for each candidate view without explicit second-order backpropagation. The network architecture fuses 3DGS means with semantic one-hot vectors via Vector Neurons, concatenates with gripper anchor-point features from an MLP, and processes the combined set through PointNet to output success/failure logits.

## Results

In simulation (400 trials, 2 initial + 2 active views), ActiveGrasp achieves a 79.00% success rate with an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of 0.02, outperforming the best same-budget baseline (Se3diffCalib+Random at 74.25%) and all prior methods (best prior: GSNet+ActiveNGF at 62.00%). On the ACRONYM dataset, the full calibrated model reaches 87.84 AP and 0.03 ECE, compared to 34.69 AP and 0.17 ECE for the uncalibrated SE(3)-Diffusion baseline. In real-world experiments across three object setups, ActiveGrasp achieves 9/10, 8/10, and 9/10 successes, consistently outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper decouples grasp model quality from view selection quality by fixing the calibrated SE(3)-Diffusion model (SE3Diff-Calib) and swapping only the NBV strategy. With this controlled setup, ActiveGrasp's information-gain-based view selection (79.00% SR) beats Breyer [5] (73.75%), FisherRF [30] (73.50%), ActiveNGF [45] (74.00%), and ACE [71] (74.00%) by 4.25–5.25 percentage points. Against prior end-to-end systems, the gap is much larger: GSNet+ActiveNGF [65] reaches only 62.00%, SE3Diff+ActiveNGF [64] reaches 58.25%, Contact GraspNet+ActiveNGF [61] reaches 32.50%, and ACE+ACE [71] reaches 22.50%. Even a random-view baseline with 8 extra views (Random†, 76.50%) falls short of ActiveGrasp's 79.00% with only 2 active views, confirming the value of task-aware information gain over brute-force observation. In real-world trials, ActiveGrasp achieves 9/10, 8/10, 9/10 across three setups, outperforming ACE [71] (8/10, 4/10, 8/10), Breyer [5] (6/10, 6/10, 8/10), and ActiveNGF [45] (7/10, 6/10, 8/10). The calibrated model also reduces ECE from 0.28–0.40 (uncalibrated or prior methods) to 0.02, indicating that the predicted success probability closely matches the true execution outcome.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SE(3)-DiffusionFields [64] as its base energy-based model for grasp generation, extending it with scene conditioning via 3D Gaussian Splatting and a calibration mechanism inspired by JEM [21]. It departs from prior active grasping methods (Breyer, ACE, ActiveNGF, FisherRF) that estimate information gain through visibility, affordance scores, or 3D grid entropy, instead formulating it as entropy reduction of the grasp distribution on the SE(3) manifold using the Gaussian Approximation of Posterior [37]. The key conceptual departure is treating grasp success as a Bernoulli variable whose entropy—rather than the Shannon entropy of the pose distribution itself—drives view selection, and calibrating the EBM's energy level to match real success rates, which the authors claim is the first such calibration for grasp pose detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, information-theoretic framework that eliminates heuristic or surrogate-based view selection for grasping, and the calibrated EBM yields an ECE of 0.02 versus 0.28–0.40 for uncalibrated baselines, suggesting that reliable success-rate prediction is achievable in practice. The released PyBullet benchmark with YCB objects addresses a reproducibility gap in active grasping research. Open questions include whether the second-order Hessian approximation (Eq. 16) holds under larger domain shifts, whether the calibration requirement generalizes beyond the ACRONYM training distribution, and whether the approach scales to dexterous or multi-fingered grasping where the success model is no longer a simple Bernoulli.
