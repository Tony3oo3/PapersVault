## Problem

Self-supervised feed-forward scene flow methods are limited by two-frame supervision, which produces temporally inconsistent and unreliable signals under occlusions, sensor noise, and sparse observations. While multi-frame architectures exist, they remain constrained by two-frame training objectives because naive multi-frame extensions yield abruptly varying point correspondences that conflict across frames. The paper addresses how to derive stable, temporally consistent supervisory signals from multiple frames to unlock the full potential of multi-frame feed-forward models.

## Input / Output

The network takes a temporal window of 5 LiDAR point clouds (past frames aligned to the target frame via odometry) and produces per-point 3D residual flow vectors representing dynamic object motion after ego-motion compensation. The backbone (DeltaFlow) processes a 76.8×76.8×6 m region as a 512×512×40 voxel grid at 0.15 m resolution, using a lightweight Δ-scheme that computes inter-frame voxel differences to keep input size constant regardless of frame count. Static/dynamic segmentation is provided externally by DUFOMap, and dynamic clusters are pre-computed via HDBSCAN.

## Method

TeFlow introduces a temporal ensembling strategy that, for each dynamic cluster, constructs a pool of motion candidates combining an internal anchor (the network's current mean prediction over the cluster) with external geometric hypotheses (top-K nearest-neighbor displacements to dynamic points in each other frame, normalized by temporal gap). A consensus voting scheme then selects the most reliable target: a directional consistency matrix (cosine similarity > 0.7071) identifies agreeing candidates, each weighted by a temporal decay factor (γ=0.9) and displacement magnitude, and the final supervision is a weighted average of the consensus winner and all directionally consistent candidates. Training uses a dynamic cluster loss with both point-level and cluster-level L2 terms (the latter preventing large objects from dominating), plus static and multi-frame geometric consistency losses. Built on the DeltaFlow multi-frame backbone, the approach achieves optimization-level accuracy at 150× speedup by replacing unstable two-frame signals with temporally stable multi-frame consensus targets.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 test set, TeFlow achieves a Three-way EPE of 3.57 cm and a Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE of 0.205, matching the best optimization-based method (Floxels, 3.57 cm) while running 150× faster (8 s vs. 24 min per sequence). On nuScenes validation, TeFlow reaches 4.64 cm Three-way EPE and 0.395 Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE, a 22.4% improvement over the previous best feed-forward method (SeFlow++). The most notable gain is a 33.8% error reduction for the pedestrian class on nuScenes, demonstrating the benefit of temporally consistent multi-frame supervision for small, occluded objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TeFlow outperforms all feed-forward baselines on both Argoverse 2 and nuScenes. On Argoverse 2, it reduces the Dynamic Bucket-Normalized EPE by 22.3% over SeFlow++ (0.205 vs. 0.264) and by 33.3% over SeFlow (0.205 vs. 0.309), with the largest per-class gain on pedestrians (31% reduction vs. SeFlow++). On nuScenes, the improvement over SeFlow++ is 22.4% in mean dynamic normalized EPE (0.395 vs. 0.509) and 33.8% for the pedestrian class (0.474 vs. 0.716). Against optimization-based methods, TeFlow matches Floxels on Three-way EPE (3.57 cm) while being 150× faster, and approaches EulerFlow's dynamic normalized score (0.205 vs. 0.130) at a fraction of the runtime (8 s vs. 1440 min). The gap to the best optimization-based methods remains on the Dynamic Bucket-Normalized metric, but TeFlow narrows it substantially compared to prior feed-forward approaches, effectively closing the accuracy–efficiency trade-off that previously separated the two paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeFlow builds directly on the DeltaFlow multi-frame backbone and the static-dynamic segmentation paradigm from SeFlow, but departs fundamentally from the two-frame supervision that has constrained all prior self-supervised feed-forward methods. The key conceptual shift is replacing point-wise two-frame correspondences with a temporal ensembling strategy that mines consensus motion across a multi-frame candidate pool, effectively treating supervision signal generation as a voting problem rather than a direct regression target. This positions TeFlow as the first method to make multi-frame architectures (previously useful only under supervised or optimization-based training) viable in a self-supervised, real-time setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving optimization-level accuracy (matching Floxels on Argoverse 2) at 150× the speed, TeFlow makes high-quality scene flow practical for real-time autonomous driving pipelines where per-scene optimization is infeasible. The temporal ensembling principle—aggregating temporally consistent cues via consensus voting—could generalize to other self-supervised motion estimation tasks beyond LiDAR. Open questions include whether the hand-designed voting thresholds (cosine similarity, temporal decay) can be learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to camera-based or multi-sensor settings, and whether the reliance on external tools (DUFOMap for segmentation, HDBSCAN for clustering) limits robustness in unstructured environments.
