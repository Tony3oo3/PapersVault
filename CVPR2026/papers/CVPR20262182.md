## Problem

Existing RL-based video reasoning methods rely on text-only chains that produce ungrounded or hallucinated conclusions, while frame-retrieval approaches introduce visual grounding but suffer from inaccurate evidence localization. No prior framework simultaneously performs multi-scale frame identification (evidence vs. context vs. distractor), cross-frame deductive reasoning, and adaptive action decisions (when to retrieve more frames vs. when to answer) in a unified, evidence-grounded manner.

## Input / Output

The model receives 16 uniformly sampled video frames (resolution 448×28×28) and a text question as initial input. During multi-step reasoning (up to 3 rounds), it can retrieve up to 8 additional frames per step via either random sampling or targeted retrieval around specific timestamps. The output is a structured reasoning trace where each round contains frame identification labels, textual evidence reasoning, and an action tag (random frames sampling / specific frames retrieval / confident question answering), culminating in a final answer. For long video understanding evaluation, videos are expanded to 32 frames.

## Method

Conan builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with a two-phase curriculum. Phase 1 is a multi-stage progressive cold-start (SFT on Conan-CoT-60k) that incrementally activates reasoning: first on textual frame descriptions (temporal/causal reasoning), then with interleaved visual frames and timestamps (multimodal alignment), and finally on full vision-centric multi-step traces. Phase 2 applies AIR RLVR (Identification-Reasoning-Action) using GRPO on Conan-RLVR-31k, with a joint reward that combines format validity, answer accuracy (exact match or ROUGE), identification accuracy of evidence/context frames, and retrieval quality (ratio of relevant frames retrieved). The Conan-91k dataset is constructed from GenS-Video-150K via automated reasoning trace generation with Kimi K2, using multi-scale frame categorization and an Evidence Difficulty Index (EDI = (1−P)·Var) to stratify samples from simple to complex for progressive training.

## Results

Conan (7B) achieves an average accuracy of 57.4% across six multi-step video reasoning benchmarks (MMR-V, Video-Holmes, VRBench, VCRBench, Human-P&C, LongVideoReason), surpassing its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (46.9%) by 10.5 points and GPT-4o (54.7%) by 2.7 points. On four long video understanding benchmarks (LongVideoBench, MLVU, LVBench, Video-MME), Conan reaches 54.9% average, a 6.9-point gain over the base model. The largest individual improvements are on Video-Holmes (+16.1 over base) and VRBench (+14.6 over base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Conan (57.4% Avg. VR) outperforms all compared baselines on the average video reasoning score, beating the strongest open-source competitor Rewatch-R1 (55.7%) by 1.7 points and the 72B Qwen2.5-VL (55.1%) by 2.3 points. Against the closed-source GPT-4o (54.7%), Conan gains 2.7 points on average, with particularly large margins on Video-Holmes (44.6 vs. 42.0) and VRBench (81.0 vs. 76.7), though GPT-4o retains an edge on MMR-V (44.0 vs. 42.7) and VCRBench (54.0 vs. 51.0). Among text-CoT methods, Video-R1 (51.7%) and VideoChat-R1 (49.8%) trail Conan by 5.7 and 7.6 points respectively, underscoring the benefit of explicit visual evidence grounding. Among video-CoT methods, Video-MTR (49.1%) and Rewatch-R1 (55.7%) are both surpassed, with Conan leading on 5 of 6 individual VR benchmarks. On long video understanding (Avg. LU), Conan (54.9%) also leads all baselines, exceeding Video-R1 (53.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (53.4%), while Rewatch-R1 (50.5%) lags significantly, suggesting its gains on VR partly stem from in-domain training on LongVideoReason.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (pioneered by DeepSeek-R1 for text) to video reasoning, building on the line of work that applied RL to MLLMs (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1) but departs from their text-only chain-of-thought by introducing explicit multi-scale frame identification (evidence/context/distractor) and action decision. It also builds on concurrent video-CoT approaches (Video-MTR, Rewatch-R1, FrameThinker) that introduced frame retrieval, but addresses their imprecise evidence localization through a structured Identification-Reasoning-Action (AIR) reward and a progressive cold-start curriculum. The data pipeline repurposes GenS-Video-150K's frame relevance scores and leverages Kimi K2 as a teacher to auto-generate interleaved video-text reasoning traces, carving out a middle ground between ungrounded text CoT and loosely-grounded video CoT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Conan-91K dataset and the progressive cold-start → AIR RLVR training recipe provide a practical, reproducible blueprint for training evidence-grounded video reasoning in 7B-scale MLLMs, and the 10%+ average gain over the base model (while outperforming GPT-4o on most benchmarks) demonstrates that careful curriculum design and reward shaping can close the gap to much larger models. Limitations include dependence on a single teacher LLM (Kimi K2) for trace quality, a cap of three reasoning rounds and 16 initial frames, and training data derived from one source dataset (GenS-Video-150K), raising questions about robustness to out-of-distribution video domains. The authors note future work toward chain-of-frame reasoning with dynamic frame generation, and the modest 6.9% transfer to long-video understanding (vs. 10.5% on reasoning) suggests the multi-step retrieval mechanism may not yet fully exploit very long temporal spans.
