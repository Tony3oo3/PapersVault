## Problem

Multi-image diffusion models (for multi-view synthesis and video generation) still exhibit persistent texture and structure drift across generated images, undermining spatiotemporal consistency. Existing remedies rely on external geometric priors (depth maps, epipolar constraints) or optical flow, which are unavailable during pure generative inference from Gaussian noise, making it difficult to identify cross-image correspondences and enforce alignment without auxiliary inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CorrAdapter operates as a plug-and-play bypass branch injected into the highest-resolution transformer blocks of an existing multi-image diffusion model. It consumes only the model's own intermediate latent features (no external depth, segmentation, or flow inputs) and produces updated feature representations that are blended back into the denoising stream via a weighted sum. For image-conditioned tasks it processes all timesteps, while for text-conditioned tasks it is applied only to the first 10–15 timesteps to preserve generation diversity.

## Method

The key insight is that multi-image diffusion models implicitly encode reliable spatial correspondences in their intermediate noisy features before images are fully synthesized. CorrAdapter exploits this with two components: a native correspondence constructor that reuses the self-attention weights already computed by the host transformer to build per-pixel correspondences between image pairs via nearest-neighbor matching (with a 0.05 similarity threshold), and an aligned area aggregator that crops a (2r+1)×(2r+1) window around each matched region and computes a local attention-weighted feature sum restricted to those corresponding areas. The aggregated features are blended with the original transformer output using a hyperparameter η (default 0.1), then passed through a feed-forward network before feeding the next block. All adapter parameters are initialized from the host transformer and frozen, making the method training-free; an optional LoRA fine-tuning scheme with a consistency loss (Eq. 14) can further improve correspondence quality when ground-truth correspondences are available.

## Results

On image-conditioned multi-view generation (GSO dataset), CorrAdapter improves SyncDreamer's PSNR from 19.24 to 19.72 and cPSNR from 26.28 to 27.20, while reducing depth error from 14.15 to 12.60 and MEt3R from 0.1656 to 0.1529. On MVAdapter, the training-free version lifts PSNR from 23.15 to 23.82 and cPSNR from 18.75 to 19.68; the trained variant (CorrAdapter*) further reaches PSNR 24.05 and cPSNR 20.47. On text-conditioned video generation (VBench), CorrAdapter boosts Wan2.1's subject consistency from 0.9536 to 0.9715 and scene consistency from 0.2202 to 0.2878, at the cost of a slight drop in dynamic degree (0.5556→0.5139).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SyncDreamer, CorrAdapter consistently wins on all quality and consistency metrics: PSNR improves by +0.48 dB, cPSNR by +0.92 dB, depth error drops by 1.55, and MEt3R decreases by 0.0127, confirming that the adapter corrects structural drift without degrading single-image fidelity. Against MVAdapter (image-conditioned), the training-free CorrAdapter gains +0.67 dB PSNR and +0.93 dB cPSNR, while the trained variant (CorrAdapter*) pushes PSNR to 24.05 and cPSNR to 20.47, a +1.72 dB improvement over the baseline. On text-conditioned multi-view generation, CorrAdapter* improves MVAdapter's cPSNR from 14.09 to 15.27 and MEt3R from 0.3017 to 0.2701, though the training-free version shows a slight FID increase (24.20→23.42 is actually an improvement) while cSSIM marginally drops. On video generation, Wan2.1+CorrAdapter wins on subject consistency (+0.018), background consistency (+0.007), scene consistency (+0.068), and overall consistency (+0.005), but loses on dynamic degree (−0.042) and imaging quality (−0.010), reflecting the expected trade-off between consistency and motion amplitude. Overall, CorrAdapter is a strict improvement on geometric consistency across all three baselines, with the only notable regression being reduced dynamic range in video.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the discovery of emergent correspondences in single-image diffusion models (Tang et al., NeurIPS 2023) to the multi-image setting, where no reference image is available as input. Prior approaches to multi-image consistency either rely on data-driven cross-image transformers with weak implicit guidance, hard geometric constraints (epipolar geometry, depth maps) limited to static scenes, or optical-flow-based matching that requires known video/keyframes. CorrAdapter departs from all of these by mining correspondences directly from the diffusion model's own intermediate attention features at inference time, making it training-free, input-agnostic, and applicable to both static multi-view and dynamic video generation without any auxiliary priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CorrAdapter offers a broadly applicable, drop-in consistency booster that removes the dependency on external geometric or semantic inputs, which has been a persistent limitation of prior multi-image generation methods. The practical plug-and-play design (reusing existing attention weights, frozen parameters) lowers the adoption barrier significantly. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (e.g., ~0.5 dB PSNR, ~1 dB cPSNR), and the method has only been validated on a limited set of backbones (SyncDreamer, MVAdapter, Wan2.1). Open questions include how well the native correspondences hold under very large viewpoint changes or long video sequences, and whether the optional LoRA-based training scheme generalizes beyond architectures with identical topology.
