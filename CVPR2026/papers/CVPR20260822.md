## Problem

Existing virtual try-on methods perform a direct replacement of the original clothing with the target, rigidly replicating the same dressing pattern and producing fixed, monotonous outputs. In reality, individuals wear the same garment in multiple ways (tucked vs. untucked, different hemlines), yet current approaches offer no mechanism for users to control how clothing adapts to the body, limiting the diversity of try-on results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260822/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes four inputs: a person image, an in-shop clothing image, a DensePose map of the person, and a user-drawn curve sketch that specifies the desired clothing boundary (e.g., where the hem should fall). The curve sketch is the key interactive element — a simple 2D curve drawn by the user that gets converted into a spatial layout constraint. The output is a single try-on image where the target clothing is rendered with its spatial arrangement (hem position, tuck state, region interaction) adjusted to match the drawn curve, rather than being locked to the original garment's layout.

## Method

MOFA-VTON builds on a latent diffusion UNet (Adapt-Net) with two key innovations. First, a mask construction strategy converts the user-drawn curve into a dual-region mask by intersecting the curve with a DensePose-derived torso silhouette, producing separate upper and lower region masks with distinct combination weights (β=1, γ=0.5) that provide fine-grained layout guidance. Second, layout adjustment blocks are inserted into intermediate and decoder layers of Adapt-Net; each block uses cross-attention where the backbone feature map serves as query and region-specific features (extracted by a symmetric region encoder from the clothing image and lower-body parsing) serve as key/value, independently learning spatial correspondences for upper and lower regions. Transposed convolutions expand region feature influence, and mask filtering with the dual-region mask constrains propagated features to their intended regions, ensuring the generated clothing boundary precisely follows the user's sketch.

## Results

On VITON-HD, MOFA-VTON achieves the best FID (5.97 paired, 8.61 unpaired), KID (0.92 paired, 1.17 unpaired), and SSIM (0.8870 paired) among all 10 baselines, with LPIPS at 0.0632 (second-best behind StableVITON's 0.0605). On DressCode upper-body, MOFA-VTON leads in FID (6.41 paired, 9.17 unpaired), KID (0.72 paired, 1.06 unpaired), and SSIM (0.9452 paired), while LPIPS (0.0316) trails IDM-VTON (0.0291) by a narrow margin. The method also uniquely supports user-controlled layout diversity via curve sketches, a capability absent in all compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOFA-VTON outperforms all 10 baselines on VITON-HD in FID, KID, and SSIM, with the closest competitor being StableVITON (FID 6.05 vs. 5.97; KID 1.09 vs. 0.92). On LPIPS, StableVITON edges out MOFA-VTON (0.0605 vs. 0.0632), the only metric where MOFA-VTON is not best. On DressCode upper-body, MOFA-VTON leads all five baselines in FID (6.41 vs. next-best IDM-VTON at 7.36), KID (0.72 vs. 1.09), and SSIM (0.9452 vs. 0.9362), while IDM-VTON achieves a slightly lower LPIPS (0.0291 vs. 0.0316). The diversity-oriented baseline COTTON, which uses sparse point control, shows substantially worse quality (FID 16.54 paired) and only minor layout variation compared to MOFA-VTON's fine-grained curve-based control. Overall, MOFA-VTON achieves state-of-the-art generation quality while uniquely offering user-interactive layout diversity, a capability none of the compared methods provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOFA-VTON builds on the latent diffusion framework (Stable Diffusion) and the ControlNet-style injection mechanism (coarse and detail injection blocks) as its generative backbone, while replacing the standard clothing-agnostic mask with a dual-region mask derived from user-drawn curve sketches. It departs from prior diversity-oriented try-on methods (COTTON's sparse points, Wear-Any-Way's point alignment, PromptDresser's text prompts) by introducing pixel-level curve-based control and a dedicated layout adjustment block with cross-attention that independently refines upper and lower region spatial arrangements. The region encoder, which extracts texture features from clothing and lower-body regions separately, is a novel architectural addition not present in prior diffusion-based try-on pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap in e-commerce virtual try-on: the inability to explore different wearing styles (tucked vs. untucked, varied hemlines) without re-photographing. The dual-region mask and layout adjustment blocks provide a reusable mechanism for fine-grained spatial control that could extend to other garment categories or full-body try-on. Limitations include the requirement for users to draw curves (a usability barrier), evaluation primarily on upper-body try-on, and modest quantitative gains over strong baselines like StableVITON. Open questions include whether the sketch-based interface scales to complex multi-garment scenarios and whether the approach generalizes to 3D-consistent try-on.
