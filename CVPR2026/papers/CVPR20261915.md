## Problem

Generating creative 3D creatures that combine parts from different source objects (e.g., a tiger body with dragon wings and a robot's legs) remains an open problem. Existing approaches either rely on part-level optimization that struggles with granularity control and interface blending, or on 2D creative image generation followed by 3D lifting that is highly sensitive to image quality and fails on out-of-distribution compositions. No prior method achieves automatic, training-free, feed-forward generation of coherent 3D creatures from arbitrary multi-source prompts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language prompt specifying which body parts to source from which creatures (e.g., 'a creature with a body of a cat and wings of a butterfly and a tail of a tailmon and a head of a sprigatito'). Internally, it first generates 3D assets and auto-rigged skeletons for each mentioned creature via Trellis and Puppeteer, then composes them. The output is a textured 3D mesh in SLAT format decoded at 64³ resolution, produced in under one minute on a single RTX A6000 GPU. The pipeline is entirely feed-forward and training-free, requiring no per-instance optimization.

## Method

Muses is a three-stage, training-free pipeline driven by 3D skeletons as the structural backbone. Stage 1 (skeleton-guided concept design) classifies each source skeleton into semantic regions (body, legs, wings, tail, head) via graph-based heuristic rules, then uses an LLM (Qwen-Plus) to reason about spatial layout, scale, and orientation, emitting a sequence of rotation/translation/scale operators that assemble a coherent target skeleton. Stage 2 (SLAT-based content composition) maps skeletal regions to structured latent voxels using skinning-weight-derived region assignments, then performs linear interpolation in the compact 16³ voxel space to fuse geometry and texture from different source objects, avoiding the gaps that arise from stitching sparse 64³ voxels directly. Stage 3 (style-consistent texture generation) renders the coarse composed creature, applies geometrically invariant texture editing via FLUX.1 Kontext to produce a styled reference image, and feeds it back through Trellis's second-stage transformer to generate a harmonious, style-consistent texture while preserving the composed geometry.

## Results

Muses achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four evaluation metrics in Table 1. On CLIPScore, Muses scores 0.2878 vs. the next-best OmniPart (0.2690); on VQAScore, 0.9254 vs. 0.8151. The most dramatic gains appear in the user-study metrics: visual fidelity of 66.67 (vs. 12.62 for OmniPart, the closest competitor) and text alignment of 85.40 (vs. 9.84 for OmniPart), indicating substantially better perceptual quality and prompt adherence. All experiments run on a single RTX A6000 GPU with generation time under one minute per instance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Muses outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the SDS-based methods (DreamBeast and GaussianDreamer), Muses achieves roughly 17–20% higher CLIPScore and 84–86% higher VQAScore, while also eliminating the per-instance optimization bottleneck. Compared to the 2D-to-3D pipeline (UNO + Trellis), Muses scores 20% higher on CLIPScore and 82% higher on VQAScore, and dramatically higher on user-study metrics (66.67 vs. 1.94 visual fidelity), confirming that lifting creative 2D images to 3D degrades severely for out-of-distribution compositions. Against direct feed-forward Trellis-Text-to-3D, Muses gains 18% on CLIPScore and 22% on VQAScore, with a 6.4× improvement in visual fidelity (66.67 vs. 10.36), showing that skeleton-guided composition is essential for complex multi-concept prompts. The closest competitor, OmniPart (part-level generation with manual assembly), is surpassed by Muses on all metrics, with the largest margins in visual fidelity (66.67 vs. 12.62, a 5.3× gap) and text alignment (85.40 vs. 9.84, an 8.7× gap), highlighting that automatic skeleton-driven assembly produces far more coherent and prompt-faithful creatures than manual part stitching.

## Relation to Prior Work

Muses builds on the Trellis SLAT-based 3D generation backbone and Puppeteer's auto-rigging for skeleton extraction, but departs fundamentally from both part-aware 3D generation (DreamBeast, OmniPart) and 2D-creative-image-to-3D pipelines (UNO + Trellis). Rather than treating composition as a part-stitching or image-synthesis problem, it reframes creature creation as a skeleton-guided design-compose-generate pipeline, using graph-constrained LLM reasoning to lay out sub-skeletons and voxel-space interpolation in the compact 16³ latent to fuse regions. The style-consistent texture stage repurposes FLUX.1 Kontext for geometry-invariant appearance editing, a use case not explored in prior 3D generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Muses demonstrates that a training-free, feed-forward pipeline can produce coherent multi-concept 3D creatures in under a minute, opening a practical path for interactive 3D content creation in gaming and animation. However, the method is limited to skeletonizable categories (animals, humanoids, robots) and inherits failures from Trellis and Puppeteer; non-biological or abstract objects remain out of scope. Open questions include whether the skeleton-based composition generalizes to more than five semantic regions, how it scales to interactive real-time editing, and whether the LLM-guided layout reasoning can be replaced by learned policies for greater reliability.
