## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) and continual TTA methods for medical image segmentation rely on two impractical assumptions: that the source model possesses strong generalization priors and that the target distribution remains stable during testing. In practice, source models overfit limited labeled data, undermining regularization strategies, while continuously evolving domain shifts amplify error accumulation and class forgetting, degrading continual adaptation performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261874/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts single-channel or RGB medical images—fundus images cropped to 800×800 and resized to 256×256, or polyp images resized to 352×352 with ImageNet normalization—and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks (binary for OD/OC, multi-class for polyp). At test time, each incoming image is first passed through a lightweight learnable decorator that generates an instance-specific augmented counterpart before being fed to the segmentation network, so the model effectively consumes a style-calibrated version of the raw input rather than the original.

## Method

TanGo is a two-stage framework. During training, Distribution-wise Consistency Learning (DSCL) extracts low-frequency amplitude components from a frozen SAM ViT-B encoder (fine-tuned with adapters) via 2D FFT, injects them into the specialist CNN's intermediate features using a variance-scaled, noise-perturbed fusion, and enforces bidirectional KL-divergence consistency between the original and foundation-informed prediction branches to build a more generalizable source feature space. At test time, Instance-wise Style Adaptive Calibration (ISAC) employs a per-sample learnable decorator (LID) that shifts each test image toward the enhanced source distribution, constrained by Source Knowledge Anchored Recalibration (SKAR): a Gram-matrix-based distributional loss aligns intermediate feature styles between the adapted and source models, a semantic loss preserves output consistency before and after decoration, and an entropy-minimization loss stabilizes batch-normalization updates—jointly suppressing error accumulation and catastrophic forgetting under continuously shifting test distributions.

## Results

On the fundus OD/OC segmentation task under CTTA, TanGo achieves an average DSC of 85.23%, surpassing the second-best method GraTa (74.32%) by 10.91% and the No-Adapt baseline (65.86%) by 19.37%. On the polyp segmentation CTTA task, TanGo reaches 83.70% average DSC, outperforming VPTTA (80.46%) by 3.24%. Under static TTA on polyp segmentation, TanGo attains 84.46% average DSC, exceeding the second-best MGMDG (81.48%) by 2.98%, with particularly strong gains in the A→rest, B→rest, and D→rest long-term adaptation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TanGo outperforms all 14 compared baselines across every evaluation setting. On the fundus CTTA task, the closest competitor is GraTa (AAAI 2025) at 74.32% average DSC, which TanGo exceeds by 10.91%; VPTTA (CVPR 2024) and CertainTTA (IF 2025) trail further at 71.93% and 71.84% respectively. On the polyp CTTA task, VPTTA is the strongest baseline at 80.46% DSC, with TanGo ahead by 3.24%; entropy-based methods (TENT, SAR) and BN-alignment methods (DUA, DomainAdaptor) all fall 5–15 points behind. Under static TTA on polyp segmentation, MGMDG (CVPR 2025) is the nearest competitor at 81.48%, while DIGA (CVPR 2023) and DomainAdaptor (ICCV 2023) achieve 77.69% and 77.96% respectively. In the distillation ablation (Table 5), TanGo's DSCL module outperforms channel-wise (CWD, 78.69%), attention-based (AttnKD, 79.02%), and standard (SpaKD, 76.73%) knowledge distillation on the polyp task, confirming that distribution-level consistency learning provides stronger generalization transfer than local feature imitation.

## Relation to Prior Work

TanGo positions itself at the intersection of continual test-time adaptation (CTTA) and vision foundation model (VFM) distillation for medical segmentation. It departs from prior CTTA methods (CoTTA, VPTTA, DomainAdaptor) that assume a well-generalized source model, by explicitly addressing source overfitting through a training-time frequency-domain consistency loss that injects low-frequency priors from frozen VFMs (SAM, DINOv2) into a lightweight specialist network. The test-time component extends visual prompt learning (VPTTA) with an instance-specific decorator and Gram-matrix-based style alignment, while the overall 'training-to-adapt' paradigm bridges the gap between offline domain generalization (FADA, TinyMIG) and online adaptation, treating the two as complementary rather than alternative strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified training-plus-testing framework offers a practical recipe for medical imaging deployments where source data is limited and target distributions shift continuously, and the ~11% DSC gain on fundus segmentation over the prior best method is substantial for a clinical task. However, the approach is validated only on 2D fundus and polyp images with a single VFM (SAM ViT-B), leaving open whether the frequency-domain consistency mechanism transfers to 3D volumetric data, other modalities (CT, MRI, ultrasound), or different foundation model architectures. The reliance on a frozen VFM with adapters during training also introduces non-trivial computational overhead that may limit adoption in resource-constrained clinical settings.
