## Problem

Convolutional neural networks aggregate features based on fixed spatial positions, fundamentally limiting their ability to model long-range, content-dependent interactions that self-attention captures through feature similarity. This paper asks whether the convolution operation itself can be reinterpreted and modified so that its aggregation is driven by content similarity rather than purely by spatial proximity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264052/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images at 224×224 for classification, 512×512 random crops for segmentation, and 800×1280 multi-scale inputs for detection. A distinctive aspect of the internal computation is that after pointwise convolutions produce K and V tensors, each channel is independently sorted in ascending order of K values, converting 2D spatial feature maps into 1D sequences where positional adjacency reflects content similarity before any convolution is applied.

## Method

Ego's token mixer (SpatialEgo) consists of two parallel branches sharing the same K and V from a gated convolution. The first branch performs standard spatial gated convolution; the second branch sorts K channel-wise (Sort_dw), permutes V to match (SortBy_dw), applies a 1D depth-wise convolution with a window of 24⌊ln(N)⌋+1 using logspace-decaying weights that are shared across all positions and channels, then restores original positions (SortBack_dw). The key insight is that channel-wise sorting maps feature similarity into positional proximity, so a local 1D convolution over the sorted sequence implicitly aggregates globally similar tokens while maintaining O(N log N) complexity. The architecture follows a four-stage meta-architecture similar to ConvNeXt and Swin Transformer, with three variants (Ego-T/S/B) differing in channel widths and block counts, and requires no additional learnable parameters beyond the standard gated convolution weights.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (224×224), Ego-T (27M params, 3.8G FLOPS) achieves 84.0% top-1 accuracy, outperforming MogaNet-S (83.4%) and Conv2Former-T (83.2%) at a similar parameter scale. Ego-S (39M) reaches 84.8% and Ego-B (57M) reaches 85.1%, surpassing OverLock-S (84.8%) and MogaNet-L (84.7%) respectively. On ADE20K semantic segmentation, Ego-B achieves 52.3% mIoU with UperNet, and on COCO object detection, Ego-B attains 53.3 AP^b / 45.8 AP^m with Cascade Mask R-CNN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the ~27M parameter scale, Ego-T (84.0%) outperforms all listed CNN baselines including MogaNet-S (83.4%), Conv2Former-T (83.2%), and ConvNeXt-T (82.1%), as well as the attention-based Swin-T (81.5%). At the ~40–57M scale, Ego-S (84.8%) matches OverLock-S (84.8%) with fewer parameters (39M vs. 56M) and beats MogaNet-B (84.3%) and ConvFormer-S36 (84.1%); Ego-B (85.1%) surpasses OverLock-S (84.8%) and MogaNet-L (84.7%) by 0.3–0.4%. On ADE20K, Ego-B (52.3% mIoU) edges out VAN-B4 (52.2%) and OverLock-S (51.9%), while Ego-T (49.0%) leads its parameter class over ConvNeXt-T (46.7%) and Swin-T (45.8%). On COCO detection, Ego-B (53.3 AP^b) beats MogaNet-B (52.6) and HorNet-S (52.7), though Ego-T (50.7 AP^b) trails MogaNet-S (51.6) and HorNet-T (51.7) by ~1%, which the authors attribute to a reduced batch size (8 vs. 16) necessitated by memory constraints on RTX 3090s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the gated convolution formulation from Conv2Former [18], reinterpreting it through a self-attention lens where convolution parameters act as position-dependent attention weights. It departs from the broader MetaFormer/VAN/MogaNet/HorNet line of CNN improvements (which rely on large kernels, recursive convolutions, or gating) by introducing a channel-wise sorting operation that converts feature similarity into positional proximity, thereby making local 1D depth-wise convolutions implicitly content-driven. Unlike self-attention or GNNs, Ego avoids explicit pairwise interaction computation or graph construction, instead achieving content-aware aggregation through a simple reordering step followed by standard convolutions with logspace-decaying, non-learnable weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ego demonstrates that CNNs can recover Transformer-like content-driven global aggregation at O(N log N) complexity with zero additional learnable parameters, offering a lightweight alternative for practitioners who prefer convolutional backbones. The main open question is the theoretical understanding of why and when the sorting mechanism effectively approximates content-based attention; the paper acknowledges this gap. Extending the approach to video or sequential data is non-trivial because sorting disrupts temporal order and risks information leakage from future frames, a limitation the authors flag but do not resolve. The modest but consistent gains over strong CNN baselines (e.g., +0.6% over MogaNet-S, +0.7% over MogaNet-B) suggest the mechanism is useful but not yet a paradigm shift.
