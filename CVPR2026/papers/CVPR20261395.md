## Problem

Existing online dynamic scene reconstruction methods based on 3D Gaussian Splatting rely solely on photometric loss to update both appearance and motion, causing per-Gaussian motion to chase pixel residuals rather than recover true 3D dynamics. This mismatch leads static Gaussians to acquire redundant motion while truly dynamic ones are underestimated, degrading temporal consistency and rendering quality in streaming settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential multi-view video stream (e.g., 20 views at 30 FPS on N3DV, 13 views on Meet Room) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, processing frames causally without access to future observations. Optical flow is computed only on 4 designated key views per scene using SEA-RAFT, and motion masks are sampled every 5 keyframes, keeping per-frame overhead minimal. The output is a 3D Gaussian scene representation with per-Gaussian 3D motion displacements, a learned motion confidence scalar per Gaussian, and real-time rendered novel views at ~200–300 FPS.

## Method

MoRGS introduces three coupled components for explicit per-Gaussian motion reasoning in an online 3DGS pipeline. First, sparse optical flow on key views provides directional supervision: each Gaussian's 3D displacement is projected to the image plane and aligned with observed flow via an endpoint-error loss, constraining motion to follow scene geometry rather than photometric residuals. Second, a learnable per-Gaussian 3D motion offset field refines the flow-guided base displacement by aggregating multi-view gradients, correcting view-dependent flow errors while being L1-regularized to remain small. Third, a per-Gaussian motion confidence in [0,1] is learned from SAM2-refined flow masks on keyframes and applied as an element-wise weight on attribute residual updates, suppressing redundant motion in static regions and accelerating convergence on large dynamic motions. The full training objective combines reconstruction (L1 + D-SSIM), mask, flow, and offset losses, optimized sequentially per frame with 3DGS densification at each timestep.

## Results

On N3DV, MoRGS-l achieves the best PSNR of 32.53 dB among all online methods, surpassing the previous best online method QUEEN-l (32.19 dB) by +0.34 dB and even exceeding the best offline method Swift4D (32.23 dB). The lightweight variant MoRGS-s reaches 32.42 dB with only 3.4 s per-frame training time. On Meet Room, MoRGS achieves 31.79 dB PSNR and 0.957 SSIM, outperforming the strongest online baseline 3DGStream (30.79 dB) by +1.0 dB while training 3× faster (2.3 s vs. 7.2 s per frame). MoRGS also achieves the lowest masked Total Variation (mTV) in static regions, confirming superior temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRGS outperforms all online baselines on both datasets. On N3DV, it beats QUEEN-l (the closest online competitor) by +0.34 dB PSNR and +0.004 SSIM while training only 1.1 s slower per frame; it surpasses 3DGStream by +0.86 dB with 3.25× less storage and 3.25× faster training. On Meet Room, MoRGS leads 3DGStream by +1.0 dB PSNR and +0.011 SSIM while training 3.1× faster (2.3 s vs. 7.2 s). Against QUEEN-l on Meet Room, MoRGS gains +2.32 dB PSNR at the cost of 5.3× more storage (2.01 MB vs. 0.38 MB). MoRGS also achieves the lowest mTV in static regions (0.671 and 0.587 on two N3DV scenes) compared to 3DGStream (0.892, 0.668) and QUEEN-l (1.51, 1.30), confirming stronger temporal consistency. Notably, MoRGS-l's PSNR (32.53 dB) exceeds all offline methods (best: Swift4D at 32.23 dB) while operating in a causal, streaming setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the online 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction line (3DGStream, QUEEN, HiCoM, 4DGC, IGS) by introducing explicit per-Gaussian motion reasoning, where prior methods update Gaussian attributes solely under photometric supervision. It borrows tools from adjacent areas—SEA-RAFT for sparse optical flow and SAM2 for view-consistent segmentation—and repurposes them as lightweight motion priors within the differentiable Gaussian rendering pipeline. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between offline 4DGS methods that use learned deformation fields over full sequences and online methods that treat inter-frame changes as unstructured residuals, by adding a structured, motion-aware update mechanism that remains causal and streamable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For AR/VR/XR streaming applications where frames arrive sequentially and latency is critical, this work shows that explicit motion cues (even sparse ones) can substantially improve both rendering quality and temporal stability without sacrificing streamability. Limitations include the static-camera assumption, reliance on pretrained flow and segmentation networks, and evaluation only on indoor multi-view scenes with ≤20 views; generalization to outdoor scenes, moving cameras, or higher view counts remains open. A key open question is whether the per-Gaussian scalar confidence (shared across all views) is sufficient for scenes with view-dependent dynamics, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences without accumulating drift in the offset field.
