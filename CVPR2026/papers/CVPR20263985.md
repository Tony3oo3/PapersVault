## Problem

Existing reward models for RL-based text-to-image generation focus on global semantics and coarse visual quality, neglecting fine-grained spatial relationships such as object positioning, orientation, depth ordering, and rendered-text placement. This gap produces images that appear plausible overall yet contain spatial inaccuracies, and current approaches suffer from prompt-side rigidity (structured evaluators cannot handle free-form prompts) and vision-side overlooking (holistic scorers cannot detect positional errors).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263985/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and a generated 512×512 RGB image as input. The prompt is first decomposed into a structured constraint set (evaluation tag, inclusion constraints, exclusion constraints) specifying object categories, quantities, colors, orientations, spatial relations, and textual inscriptions. The final output is a scalar spatial reward score computed as the sum of verified attribute sub-rewards (presence, count, color, orientation, depth, text) plus a CoT-reasoned spatial relation score, with penalties for satisfied exclusion constraints.

## Method

SpatialReward is a three-stage verifiable reward pipeline: (1) a Prompt Decomposer (fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B on ~100k metadata-prompt pairs) parses arbitrary free-form prompts into structured inclusion/exclusion constraints; (2) expert detectors (Grounding DINO, YOLO-World, PaddleOCR, monocular depth estimation, orientation models) verify each constraint against the generated image, producing objective sub-rewards for presence, count, color, orientation, depth ordering, and text content/localization; (3) a vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL) performs chain-of-thought reasoning over the verified bounding boxes and attribute scores to assess complex inter-object spatial relations that rule-based geometry cannot resolve. The key design principle is grounding VLM reasoning in verifiable detection signals to reduce hallucination while retaining flexibility for nuanced spatial semantics. The reward is integrated into the Flow-GRPO RL framework with LoRA (rank 32, α=64) to fine-tune Stable Diffusion 3.5-M and FLUX1-dev, using a group size of 24 and KL coefficient 0.04.

## Results

On GenEval(80-Obj) with SD3.5-M, SpatialReward achieves an overall score of 0.94, outperforming the strongest baseline UnifiedReward (0.89) and the base model (0.67) by +0.05 and +0.28 respectively. On SpatRelBench(1k-Obj), SpatialReward reaches 0.42 overall versus 0.33 for UnifiedReward and 0.23 for the base model. With FLUX1-dev, SpatialReward lifts GenEval overall from 0.76 to 0.97 (+0.21) and SpatRelBench overall from 0.28 to 0.46 (+0.18). In human alignment (Table 3), SpatialReward achieves the highest Spearman ρ (0.63) and classification accuracy (0.79) among all compared reward models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialReward outperforms all five open-source reward model baselines (TextOCR, PickScore, QwenVL, ImageReward, UnifiedReward) on both GenEval and SpatRelBench across both SD3.5-M and FLUX backbones. The largest gains appear on spatially demanding metrics: on GenEval Pos (position), SpatialReward scores 0.97 vs. 0.81 for UnifiedReward and 0.28 for the base SD3.5-M; on SpatRelBench Ori (orientation), it reaches 0.26 vs. 0.12 for UnifiedReward and 0.07 for the base model. Against proprietary models, SpatialReward-optimized SD3.5-M surpasses GPT Image 1 and Qwen-Image on SpatRelBench overall (0.42 vs. 0.37 and 0.23) and on Ori (0.26 vs. 0.15 and 0.07), though GPT Image 1 retains an edge on P-Text (0.50 vs. 0.51 is a near-tie) and Cpx (0.53 vs. 0.43). In the human-alignment study (Table 3), SpatialReward achieves Spearman ρ = 0.63 and accuracy 0.79, exceeding VisionReward (0.55 / 0.74), UnifiedReward (0.51 / 0.72), ImageReward (0.48 / 0.70), and CLIPScore (0.42 / 0.68). On general-purpose metrics (Table 2), SpatialReward maintains or slightly improves Wise, DPG, and PickScore scores while showing a minor drop in Aesthetic (5.39→5.23 for SD3.5-M), confirming that spatial optimization does not degrade overall visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning framework for T2I optimization but departs from both existing reward model paradigms: structured evaluators (GenEval, T2I-CompBench) that are rigid to prompt format, and holistic scorers (CLIPScore, ImageReward, VLM-based RMs) that miss fine-grained spatial errors. Its key conceptual move is transplanting the 'verifiable reward' idea from logical reasoning (rule-based, checkable rewards à la DeepSeek-R1) into visual spatial evaluation, combining prompt decomposition, open-set expert detectors, and VLM chain-of-thought reasoning into a single pipeline. The SpatRelBench benchmark extends prior spatial evaluation (GenEval, T2I-CompBench) by adding orientation, 3D depth ordering, and rendered-text placement dimensions that those benchmarks ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial inconsistency is a major, previously under-addressed failure mode in T2I generation, and that verifiable, detector-grounded rewards can close this gap substantially without sacrificing general image quality. SpatRelBench provides the field with a more discriminative evaluation tool for spatial reasoning. Limitations include testing only at 512×512 resolution on two backbones, a slight aesthetic score drop suggesting a quality trade-off, and continued dependence on a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) for the CoT stage that could introduce its own errors. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher resolutions, video generation, and whether the CoT component can be replaced by more efficient geometric reasoning.
