## Problem

Panoramic depth estimation lags behind perspective depth due to the high cost of collecting and annotating 360° data, resulting in limited-scale, domain-specific training sets. Existing methods—whether scale-invariant (PanDA, DA²) or metric (DAC, UniK3D)—struggle to generalize across diverse real-world scenes, particularly outdoors, and fail to produce robust, metrically consistent depth over varying distances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261723/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single equirectangular (ERP) panoramic image at 512×1024 resolution as input and produces two outputs: a dense metric depth map in meters and a binary range mask that selects valid spatial regions under a user-chosen distance threshold (10 m, 20 m, 50 m, or 100 m). The final depth is the element-wise product of the mask and the raw depth prediction, making the range mask a plug-and-play mechanism for adapting to different spatial scales without retraining.

## Method

DAP uses DINOv3-Large as a frozen-then-fine-tuned ViT backbone with a distortion-aware depth decoder and a lightweight range mask head. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) a Scene-Invariant Labeler is trained on 110K synthetic indoor/outdoor panoramas with ground-truth depth; (2) a PatchGAN-based discriminator filters the top 600K highest-confidence pseudo-labeled samples from 1.9M unlabeled real panoramas, which are used to train a Realism-Invariant Labeler; (3) DAP is jointly trained on all labeled and refined pseudo-labeled data (~2M samples). The loss combines SILog, a DF-Gram term computed over 12 icosahedron-projected perspective patches for sharpness, a Sobel-gradient edge loss, surface-normal and point-cloud geometry losses, and a weighted BCE+Dice mask loss, all modulated by a distortion map that compensates for ERP pole over-representation.

## Results

On the three standard zero-shot benchmarks, DAP achieves AbsRel of 0.0921 / 0.1186 / 0.0659 and δ₁ of 0.9135 / 0.8518 / 0.9525 on Stanford2D3D, Matterport3D, and Deep360 respectively, setting new state-of-the-art among metric panoramic depth methods. On the proposed DAP-Test outdoor benchmark (1,343 images), DAP reduces AbsRel to 0.0781 and raises δ₁ to 0.9370, substantially outperforming the previous best (Unik3D: AbsRel 0.2517, δ₁ 0.6086). The model predicts absolute metric scale directly without any post-hoc scale alignment, unlike scale-invariant baselines that require ground-truth depth at test time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among metric panoramic depth methods, DAP dominates all three benchmarks. On Stanford2D3D, DAP reduces AbsRel by 48% over Unik3D (0.0921 vs. 0.1795) and by 33% over DAC (0.0921 vs. 0.1366), while raising δ₁ from 0.7823/0.8393 to 0.9135. The gap is most dramatic on Deep360 (outdoor), where DAP's AbsRel of 0.0659 is 4× lower than DAC (0.2611) and 25% lower than Unik3D (0.0885), and δ₁ jumps from 0.6311 (DAC) to 0.9525. On the proposed DAP-Test benchmark, DAP achieves AbsRel 0.0781 vs. 0.2517 (Unik3D) and 0.3197 (DAC), a 3–4× improvement. Against scale-invariant references, DA² still reports lower AbsRel on indoor sets (0.0723 vs. 0.0921 on Stanford2D3D), but requires ground-truth depth for scale alignment at test time, whereas DAP predicts metric depth directly. PanDA and DepthAnythingv2 are clearly outperformed by DAP across all shared metrics, confirming the advantage of the large-scale data engine and three-stage pseudo-label pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the panoramic depth estimation line from PanDA, DA², DAC, and UniK3D by reframing the problem as a data-scaled foundation model rather than an in-domain or limited zero-shot task. It repurposes the DINOv3-Large backbone (analogous to how DepthAnything repurposed DINOv2 for perspective depth) and introduces a three-stage pseudo-label curation pipeline that bridges synthetic-real and indoor-outdoor domain gaps, a strategy not previously applied at this scale to panoramic depth. The plug-and-play range mask head and the icosahedron-decomposed DF-Gram loss are novel architectural and optimization components that depart from prior distortion-aware designs which relied on learned convolutional filters or projection-based fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2M-panorama dataset and the DAP-Test benchmark fill a critical gap in panoramic depth supervision, particularly for outdoor scenes where ground-truth data has been scarce. The three-stage pipeline demonstrates that large-scale semi-supervised learning with quality-filtered pseudo labels can close the synthetic-real domain gap for metric depth, a recipe likely transferable to other panoramic perception tasks. Open questions include whether the three-stage pipeline is strictly necessary versus a simpler two-stage approach, how the method behaves at higher resolutions beyond 512×1024, and whether the DINOv3 backbone's pre-training distribution introduces biases that limit generalization to truly novel scene types (e.g., underwater, aerial).
