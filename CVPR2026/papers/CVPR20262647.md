## Problem

Existing large-scale PDE neural operators rely on a single network architecture, which cannot simultaneously capture the macroscopic diversity across different PDE types (varying dynamics, boundary conditions, and parameter regimes) and the microscopic spatial heterogeneity within a single PDE's physical field. This architectural uniformity becomes a bottleneck for unified pre-training across heterogeneous PDE systems, limiting generalization and transferability to downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262647/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes T preceding frames of 2D PDE solution fields (e.g., velocity, pressure, density on a spatial grid) with small-scale noise injected for robustness, and predicts the next-frame solution field. Inputs are divided into non-overlapping patches of size P_H × P_W, embedded into a D-dimensional space with positional encoding, then compressed along the time axis via a learnable weighted sum. Pre-training spans 12 PDE datasets (FNO, PDEBench, PDEArena, CFDBench) with varying parameters, and downstream evaluation includes 512×512 high-resolution turbulence prediction.

## Method

NESTOR introduces a two-level nested Mixture-of-Experts architecture within a neural operator. At the image level, a global-average-pooled representation is routed via top-k selection to activate 2 of 6 non-shared experts (Flash Attention for fine-grained spatiotemporal features) plus 1 shared expert (AFNO for global low-frequency Fourier features), enabling adaptive specialization across PDE types. Within each activated image-level expert, a token-level Sub-MoE routes individual spatial tokens to MLP-based experts, capturing local regional correlations within the physical field. The spatio-temporal encoder compresses T input frames into a fixed-dimensional representation before feeding into the nested MoE blocks. Training uses L2RE as the primary loss plus two load-balancing auxiliary losses (one per MoE level) to prevent expert collapse, and the model is pre-trained on 12 mixed PDE datasets before fine-tuning on specific downstream tasks.

## Results

On the 12-dataset pre-training benchmark, NESTOR (13M activated params) achieves the best L2RE on 6 of 12 tasks, including all five PDEBench sub-tasks (CNS avg 0.0270, DR avg 0.0161, SWE 0.0308) and FNO-ν at ν=1e-3 (0.0093). After 500 epochs of fine-tuning, NESTOR reaches SOTA on 11 of 12 tasks, with particularly large gains on FNO-ν (ν=1e-4: 0.0217 vs. DPOT's 0.0367) and PDEBench DR (avg 0.0103 vs. 0.0156). On the 2D high-resolution turbulence downstream task, fine-tuned NESTOR achieves a 47.3% improvement in prediction accuracy over training from scratch.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPOT-T (the strongest pre-training baseline, 7M params), NESTOR (13M activated) wins on all five PDEBench sub-tasks in pre-training (e.g., CNS avg 0.0270 vs. 0.0285; DR avg 0.0161 vs. 0.0176; SWE 0.0308 vs. 0.0321) but trails on FNO-ν=1e-5 (0.1195 vs. 0.0976), PDEArena NS (0.132 vs. 0.125), and CFDBench (0.0112 vs. 0.0095). After 500-epoch fine-tuning, NESTOR reverses these gaps and achieves the best L2RE on 11 of 12 tasks, with the largest margins on FNO-ν=1e-4 (0.0217 vs. 0.0367, a 41% relative reduction) and PDEBench DR (avg 0.0103 vs. 0.0156). Compared to single-architecture operators (FNO, GK-T, GNOT, Oformer), NESTOR dominates across nearly all PDEBench and PDEArena tasks, confirming the benefit of the nested MoE routing. In the efficiency comparison (Table 6), NESTOR activates only 16.67% of its 83M total parameters per forward pass, versus 56.67% for MoE-POT-T (30M total) and 100% for DPOT-T (7.5M total), achieving higher capacity at lower per-token compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

NESTOR extends the autoregressive denoising pre-training paradigm of DPOT [11] by replacing its single-architecture operator backbone with a nested Mixture-of-Experts structure. The image-level MoE draws on the heterogeneous-expert idea (AFNO for low-frequency global features, Flash Attention for fine-grained spatiotemporal features), departing from the homogeneous-expert MoE used in MoE-POT-T [32]. The token-level Sub-MoE introduces a second routing granularity within each image-level expert, a design not present in prior PDE operator work. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of large-scale PDE pre-training (MPP, DPOT, Poseidon) and the MoE scaling literature (Switch Transformer, V-MoE), arguing that PDE heterogeneity demands multi-level specialization rather than a monolithic network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The nested MoE design offers a practical path to scaling PDE neural operators: 83M total parameters with only 13M activated (16.67% ratio) suggests the architecture can grow capacity without proportional compute cost, which is important as PDE datasets and spatial resolutions increase. However, the current evaluation is limited to 2D tasks and 12 datasets, and several PDEArena results (NS, NS-cond) remain behind DPOT even after fine-tuning, leaving open whether the nested structure provides consistent gains across all equation families. A key open question is whether the two-level routing generalizes to 3D or higher-dimensional PDEs, and whether the heterogeneous expert pairing (AFNO + Flash Attention) is optimal or whether other expert combinations could further improve performance.
