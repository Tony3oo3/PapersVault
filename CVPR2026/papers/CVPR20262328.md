## Problem

Most MLLMs rely on uniform frame sampling to fit limited context windows, which risks missing sparse, critical visual evidence in long videos. Existing alternatives either incur high inference cost (MLLM-based scoring) or optimize proxy objectives like query-frame relevance that can be misleading and fail to align with actual downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262328/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The selector consumes N=128 uniformly sampled video frames (encoded as 768-d CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings) and a text query, and outputs a deterministic top-K (K=8) subset of frames passed to a frozen downstream MLLM for answer generation. The selector itself is a 3-layer Transformer decoder with a query-key scoring head, adding only ~0.425B parameters and ~3.4 s of selection latency per video, making it a plug-and-play module.

## Method

The selector is trained in two stages. Stage 1 distills a sharpened relevance prior from a frozen CLIP teacher by minimizing KL divergence between temperature-scaled cosine similarities (τ=0.03) and the selector's output distribution. Stage 2 fine-tunes the policy with a modified GRPO objective using a hierarchical reward: a frame-level signal (per-frame helpfulness scores 0–5 from Qwen2-VL, within-set centered) and a combination-level signal (binary task correctness, within-group standardized across G parallel rollouts), aggregated with learned weights. An adaptive KL monitor with hard rollback replaces the conventional KL penalty, immediately reverting updates when divergence exceeds a threshold to prevent policy collapse. At inference, the selector performs a single forward pass to score all 128 frames and deterministically selects the top-K.

## Results

Across six MLLM–benchmark pairs, the proposed frame selector yields an average accuracy gain of +3.28% over uniform sampling, with the largest improvements on longer videos: +6.16% on MLVU (12 min) and +3.87% on LongVideoBench (8 min). On LongVideoBench with Qwen2-VL-7B, the selector reaches 56.5% accuracy (vs. 51.1% uniform, 55.7% CLIP-TopK, 55.9% AKS) while matching CLIP-TopK's selection latency (3.36 s vs. 3.34 s) and running 2.3× faster than AKS (7.84 s). Larger model variants realize larger gains on long videos (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video: +4.3% at 7B vs. +7.6% at 34B on MLVU), confirming that frame selection is most critical when evidence is temporally sparse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AKS [21], the proposed selector achieves 56.5% vs. 55.9% accuracy on LongVideoBench (+0.6%) while cutting selection time from 7.84 s to 3.36 s (2.3× speedup). Against the MLLM-based selector [7], the method delivers substantially larger gains: +3.0% vs. +0.8% on VideoMME (Qwen2-VL) and +1.5%/+2.0% vs. +1.1%/+1.3% on EgoSchema (Qwen2-VL / Idefics2), while using a 0.425 B selector versus a 1.5 B+7 B MLLM scorer. Against FrameVoyager [28] on the VILA-1.5-8B backbone, the method wins on all three benchmarks: +6.1% vs. N/A on LongVideoBench, +3.7% vs. +3.0% on VideoMME, and +7.5% vs. +3.5% on MLVU. The one comparison where the method trails is Q-Frame [32] on MLVU (+7.5% vs. +8.1%), though it leads on LongVideoBench (+6.1% vs. +4.5%) and VideoMME (+3.7% vs. +3.2%). Overall, the selector outperforms or matches all baselines on the majority of benchmarks while maintaining the lowest parameter count (0.425 B) and competitive inference latency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frame-selection-for-video-MLLMs line of work (AKS, FRAG, FrameVoyager, Q-Frame) by replacing proxy objectives—query-frame cosine similarity or predicted loss—with direct end-task utility optimized via reinforcement learning. It builds on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) from the LLM reasoning literature, repurposing the group-relative advantage mechanism for a combinatorial subset-selection action space where each 'token' is a frame. The key conceptual departure from prior MLLM-as-scorer approaches (FRAG, M-LLM Based Selection) is that the selector is a lightweight CLIP-based module (~0.425B) trained with a hierarchical reward (per-frame helpfulness + combination-level task success) rather than relying on expensive MLLM inference at selection time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent gains across six MLLM backbones and four benchmarks make this a practical drop-in improvement for long-video pipelines, particularly where evidence is temporally sparse. A notable open question is the training-time dependency on a frozen Qwen2-VL as both reward oracle and frame-helpfulness judge, which couples the selector's ceiling to that model's capabilities and raises questions about transferability to substantially different downstream MLLMs. The paper does not explore streaming or multi-turn settings, and the selector is trained on 2–3-minute clips while evaluated on videos up to 17 minutes, leaving generalization across duration regimes partially untested.
