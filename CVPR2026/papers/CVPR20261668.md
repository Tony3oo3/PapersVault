## Problem

Existing VLM debiasing methods typically require training auxiliary networks, sensitive-attribute-annotated datasets, or operate on only one modality, and none provide theoretical guarantees that downstream utility is preserved. This paper addresses the gap by seeking a unified, training-free, data-free debiasing approach that simultaneously neutralizes bias in both image and text embeddings across multiple downstream tasks while offering provable bounds on utility loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261668/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen VLM (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14), an input image and text prompt, and a set of sensitive attribute group definitions (e.g., gender, race, or their intersections). An LLM is queried to generate multiple group-specific prompt variants per group, whose text-encoder embeddings are combined via spherical mean to form group prototypes. The output is a pair of debiased unit-norm embeddings (image and text) in the shared cross-modal space, each orthogonal to the attribute subspace while retaining maximal semantic alignment with the original embeddings.

## Method

The method constructs an attribute subspace A spanned by pairwise differences between LLM-guided group prototypes, then decomposes each original embedding into components parallel and orthogonal to A. By Lemma 1, the optimal debiased embedding lies in the 2D span of these two components, reducing the problem to a 1D scalar optimization over a single parameter α. To obtain a task-agnostic solution robust to any fairness-utility trade-off preference, the authors apply Chebyshev (minimax) scalarization over the weight simplex, yielding a closed-form expression for α* (Theorem 1). The resulting debiased embedding achieves Pareto-optimal fairness with a self-utility loss strictly below the worst-case orthogonal projection, and Proposition 1 provides an upper bound on cross-utility loss in terms of the per-modality self-utility losses. The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no annotated data, operating purely through embedding arithmetic in the VLM's cross-modal space.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification (CelebA, intersectional G×A), the method achieves 56.5% F1 (vs. 54.0% baseline) while reducing ΔMaxEO from 45.0 to 40.1 and ΔAvgEO from 25.1 to 23.6. On text-to-image retrieval (Flickr30K), it reduces MS@1000 from 20.3 to 11.8 while maintaining R@5 at 90.4% (vs. 91.0% baseline). For text-to-image generation (SD v2.1), it lowers Statistical Parity from 47.9 to 39.7 (BLIP-2) and from 58.6 to 28.8 (human-annotated) while preserving generation accuracy at 74.6% (vs. 75.4% baseline), substantially outperforming Orth-Proj (53.4%) and Orth-Cali (56.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on both fairness and utility simultaneously, which no other method achieves. On CelebA (intersectional G×A), it improves F1 by 2.5 points over the baseline while reducing ΔMaxEO by 4.9 points; RoboShot [1] achieves the best ΔMaxEO (40.0) but at a 1.7-point F1 cost, while PRISM [31] and SFID [19] show similar utility drops (1.1–3.6 points) with only marginal fairness gains. On FACET, the method matches the baseline Macro F1 (70.7 vs. 70.8) while reducing ΔMaxEO from 49.8 to 47.5, whereas RoboShot achieves a slightly lower ΔMaxEO (47.3) but drops F1 to 69.3. Text-only methods (Orth-Proj, Orth-Cali [5]) consistently underperform on both fairness and utility across all tasks, confirming the necessity of joint modality debiasing. On text-to-image retrieval (Flickr30K), the method reduces MS@1000 from 20.3 to 11.8 (a 42% reduction) while retaining R@5 at 90.4%, compared to CLIP-clip [45] which achieves 11.7 but drops R@5 to 87.7, and PromptArray [2] which reaches 12.9 with R@5 at 87.2. For text-to-image generation, the method achieves the best CLIP score (24.2%) and generation accuracy (74.6%) among all debiasing methods, while SFID [19] is the closest competitor on utility (22.6%, 67.2%) but trails on fairness (SP=41.1 vs. 39.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior VLM debiasing methods (PRISM, BiasedPrompt, SANER) that project embeddings orthogonally onto the subspace spanned by group prototypes, which inadvertently removes semantic content alongside bias. Instead, it reformulates debiasing as projection onto the *attribute subspace* (the span of differences between prototypes), yielding a closed-form solution with provably bounded utility loss — a theoretical guarantee no prior method provides. The LLM-guided prototype construction extends PRISM's idea by generating multiple linguistic variants per group and taking their spherical mean, addressing the observation that attribute groups are not linguistically monolithic. Conceptually, the work unifies the geometric debiasing line (PRISM-mini, Orth-Proj) with the multi-modal, multi-task setting of SFID and JointV-L, while eliminating their need for training or annotated data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being simultaneously training-free, data-free, multi-modal, multi-task, and equipped with a closed-form utility bound, this method offers a practical, plug-and-play debiasing tool that avoids the computational and annotation costs of prior approaches. The extension to intersectional fairness (e.g., gender × age, gender × skin tone) addresses a gap where prior VLM debiasing work has focused almost exclusively on single-attribute group fairness, making the evaluation more realistic. A key limitation is that the utility guarantee is geometric (cosine similarity in embedding space) rather than directly on task metrics like F1 or Recall@K, and the framework does not extend to image/text decoders for generative tasks. Open questions include whether the closed-form solution generalizes to non-contrastive VLMs and whether the attribute subspace construction remains stable with very large numbers of intersectional groups.
