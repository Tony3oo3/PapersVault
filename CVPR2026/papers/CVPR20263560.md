## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) methods neglect the interference of view-exclusive artifacts during consensus representation learning, which corrupts the resulting similarity measure. Additionally, inconsistent anchor ordering across views distorts the bipartite graph structure, and reliance on carefully tuned regularization hyper-parameters hinders practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263560/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view feature matrices {D_v ∈ R^{d_v×n}} along with missing-entry index vectors {m_v} that specify which samples are observed per view, and produces cluster labels via spectral partitioning on a learned m×n consensus bipartite affinity matrix H (where m ≪ n anchors are sampled from the data). The entire pipeline is non-parametric—no regularization hyper-parameters are required—and the anchor-based bipartite formulation avoids constructing full n×n self-expression matrices, keeping both time and space complexity linear in n.

## Method

PJFTH introduces a Janus-faced affinity learning mechanism that explicitly models view-exclusive artifacts through per-view artifact matrices H_v, then adaptively suppresses them in the consensus reconstruction D_v E_v ≈ θ_v C_v Q_v H E_v + (θ_v − 1) C_v H_v E_v via a balancing factor θ_v ∈ [0,1]. A topology harmonization module applies a permutation matrix Q_v with unary encoding constraints (binary, row/column sums equal to 1) to synchronize anchor ordering across views while preserving the originally learned anchor values. View-specific weights β_v are learned dynamically to calibrate each view's contribution, and all variables (C_v, Q_v, H, H_v, θ_v, β_v) are jointly optimized through a six-step alternating minimization scheme. The algorithm achieves provable linear complexity in the sample size n by exploiting the diagonal structure of E_v E_v^⊤ and the sparsity of the indicator matrices, and it requires zero hyper-parameters, making it a plug-and-play module.

## Results

At 60% missing ratio, PJFTH achieves the best ACC on 5 of 6 datasets: WASHING (52.91%), ORLR (52.31%), NUSCENE (13.87%), NUJECTEN (24.33%), and YOUBESEL (18.67%), with the only loss on NUSWDBJ (12.53% vs. PIMVC's 13.80%). On the FSC metric, PJFTH attains the top score across all six datasets, e.g., 49.31 on WASHING, 35.84 on ORLR, and 23.66 on NUJECTEN. Notably, PJFTH requires zero hyper-parameters (NH=0) whereas all baselines require 1–3, and it runs on all datasets including the large-scale NUSWDBJ (30K samples) and YOUBESEL (101K samples) where several baselines (HCMSC, LSIVC, LRIVC, etc.) fail to execute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PJFTH outperforms the strongest baseline OSIMC by +4.65 ACC on WASHING (52.91 vs. 48.26) and by +5.33 ACC on YOUBESEL (18.67 vs. 13.34), while also leading on FSC by +9.30 and +3.94 respectively. On ORLR, PJFTH surpasses the next-best IVCBG by a substantial +8.61 ACC (52.31 vs. 43.70) and +1.17 FSC. On the large-scale NUJECTEN dataset, PJFTH edges out LRIVC by +0.50 ACC and leads by a wide +4.03 FSC (23.66 vs. 19.63). The sole ACC loss occurs on NUSWDBJ where PIMVC scores 13.80 vs. PJFTH's 12.53, though PJFTH still wins on FSC (9.85 vs. 9.30). Against subspace/kernel-based methods (LSIVC, AGIMC, GRIMC), the anchor-based PJFTH consistently achieves higher ACC and FSC, confirming the advantage of the bipartite graph formulation. All baselines require 1–3 hyper-parameters, whereas PJFTH operates with none, and several baselines (HCMSC, LSIVC, LRIVC, AGIMC, GRIMC, UIMC, USETL, HCLGL) cannot run on NUSWDBJ or YOUBESEL at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

PJFTH builds on the anchor-based bipartite graph paradigm for IMVC (which reduces the cubic cost of full self-expression matrices to linear) and the classic self-expression affinity formulation. Its key departure is the 'Janus-faced' decomposition that explicitly learns a view-exclusive artifact matrix H_v alongside the consensus affinity H, thereby isolating and suppressing view-specific interference (e.g., incompleteness-induced bias) during consensus generation. The topology harmonization component, which applies a permutation matrix with unary encoding constraints to synchronize anchor ordering across views, is a novel mechanism not present in prior anchor-based IMVC methods. The paper also distinguishes itself from most competitors by eliminating all regularization hyper-parameters, making it a true plug-and-play module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hyper-parameter-free, linear-complexity design makes PJFTH practically deployable on large-scale datasets (tested up to ~100K samples) without manual tuning, addressing a real barrier to adoption in prior IMVC methods. However, the authors acknowledge that the orthogonal constraint on anchors may cause them to deviate from the original data distribution, and the two-stage pipeline (affinity construction followed by spectral partitioning) introduces additional clustering variance compared to direct discrete label derivation. Open questions include whether direct end-to-end cluster indicator learning could further stabilize results, and whether the Janus-faced artifact separation generalizes beyond the anchor-based setting to other IMVC formulations.
