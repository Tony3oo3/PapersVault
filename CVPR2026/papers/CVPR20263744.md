## Problem

Existing single-view scene reconstruction methods rely on SDF-based representations requiring marching cubes for surface extraction, producing overly smooth, high-face-count meshes unsuitable for downstream editing. Meanwhile, autoregressive mesh generators that produce compact, artist-ready meshes are limited to object-level outputs, and no pipeline unifies native mesh generation with scene-level layout prediction. Prior compositional approaches further depend on post-hoc optimization-based layout estimation that is prone to local minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263744/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and internally extracts depth (via Depth Pro), instance segmentation masks (via GroundedSAM), and image features (via DINOv2 with register tokens) using off-the-shelf models. The output is a complete 3D scene composed of native artist-ready meshes (a few thousand faces per instance) placed at predicted gravity-aligned 7-DoF bounding-box poses, all in a unified scene coordinate frame. Unlike SDF-based methods, no iso-surface extraction step is needed; meshes are directly generated as discrete token sequences.

## Method

PixARMesh repurposes pre-trained object-level autoregressive mesh generators (EdgeRunner and BPT) for scene-level reconstruction through three key modifications. First, the point-cloud encoder is augmented with pixel-aligned image features: each 3D point is projected to its corresponding pixel on the DINOv2 feature map, and the geometric and appearance features are fused via a Transformer-based cross-attention block with learnable queries. Second, global scene context is injected by normalizing all point clouds into a shared scene frame and applying cross-attention from each instance latent to a scene-level latent, compensating for occluded geometry. Third, object poses are tokenized as 8 bounding-box corners using the same vertex vocabulary as mesh tokens, so pose and mesh are predicted jointly in a single autoregressive sequence (<bos>, pose_seq, <sep>, mesh_seq, <eos>) under a unified next-token prediction loss, eliminating the need for separate layout optimization.

## Results

On the 3D-FRONT test set, PixARMesh-EdgeRunner achieves state-of-the-art scene-level performance with CD of 98.8×10⁻³, CD-S of 49.1×10⁻³, and F-Score of 33.55%, substantially outperforming the strongest SDF-based baseline DepR (CD 153.2×10⁻³, F-Score 25.00%). At the object level, PixARMesh achieves CD of 4.04×10⁻³ and F-Score of 82.27%, placing it second only to DepR (CD 2.57×10⁻³, F-Score 89.66%) while producing compact, artist-ready meshes without requiring Marching Cubes surface extraction. The BPT-based variant is slightly weaker (scene F-Score 32.26%, object F-Score 80.30%) but still surpasses all SDF-based methods at the scene level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixARMesh decisively outperforms all six SDF-based baselines on every scene-level metric. Against the strongest prior method DepR, PixARMesh-EdgeRunner reduces scene CD from 153.2 to 98.8 (−35%), CD-S from 56.4 to 49.1 (−13%), and raises F-Score from 25.00% to 33.55% (+8.5 pp). Against MIDI, the scene-level gains are similar (CD 156.3→98.8, F-Score 24.83%→33.55%), and against earlier compositional methods like DeepPriorAssembly and Gen3DSR the margins are even larger (scene F-Score 16.72%→33.55% and 13.52%→33.55%, respectively). On object-level metrics, PixARMesh is second-best: DepR leads with CD 2.57 and F-Score 89.66%, while PixARMesh-EdgeRunner achieves CD 4.04 and F-Score 82.27%, a gap of roughly 1.5 in CD and 7.4 pp in F-Score. The paper attributes the scene-level advantage to the unified autoregressive formulation that jointly predicts pose and geometry, avoiding the local-minima issues of post-hoc layout optimization used by DepR, MIDI, and DeepPriorAssembly.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixARMesh sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: object-level autoregressive mesh generators (EdgeRunner, BPT) and compositional single-view scene reconstruction (DepR, MIDI, DeepPriorAssembly). Prior scene reconstruction methods rely on SDF representations with marching-cubes extraction and post-hoc optimization-based layout assembly, while mesh generators have been confined to single objects. This paper repurposes the autoregressive mesh decoder by augmenting its point-cloud encoder with pixel-aligned image features and cross-attention to a global scene point cloud, then extends the token vocabulary to jointly encode object poses and mesh geometry in a single autoregressive sequence—eliminating both SDF decoding and separate layout optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing compact, artist-ready meshes directly (rather than dense iso-surfaces from SDFs), PixARMesh opens a path toward scene reconstructions that are immediately editable and compatible with downstream graphics pipelines, a practical gap that SDF-based methods leave unaddressed. The joint pose-mesh autoregressive formulation is a clean architectural simplification, but the method remains dependent on a chain of upstream perception models (monocular depth, instance segmentation, image features) and is trained only on synthetic 3D-FRONT, with scene-level F-Score still moderate (~34%). Open questions include whether the autoregressive approach scales to scenes with many more objects, whether real-world training data can close the remaining gap, and whether the object-level fidelity trade-off (slightly worse than DepR) is acceptable for the scene-level gains.
