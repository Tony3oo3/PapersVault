## Problem

Coarse-to-fine point cloud completion methods produce unconstrained local guesses when key structures are absent from the input, while diffusion-based approaches risk geometric inconsistencies. Existing auxiliary-signal methods (multi-view images, text, symmetry) either require additional sensors or fail to provide the high-fidelity local 3D shape information needed for fine-grained detail recovery. The paper asks whether imagined tactile sensing—generated without physical contact—can supply the missing local geometric guidance for detail-aware completion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262996/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial point cloud (typically from a single LiDAR or depth scan) and produces a completed, dense point cloud. Internally, a pre-trained LSTNet first generates a coarse point cloud, from which 50 touch poses are sampled on a spherical surface around the object centroid; only poses whose approach rays intersect the convex hull are retained. The final touch point cloud is formed by merging local charts from all valid poses and randomly sampling 256 points, which then guide the refinement network. Objects are normalized to unit scale (divided by 3.1) during tactile generation and rescaled afterward.

## Method

TouchDream generates tactile latent vectors via a conditional diffusion model (built on DiffusionSDF with DALLE-2-style cross-attention blocks) conditioned on coarse point features (extracted via FPS+KNN patching and max-pooled MLPs) and per-pose embeddings. A touch encoder maps rendered tactile images to compact latent codes, and a local chart predictor (a base 25-vertex triangular mesh with learned vertex deformations) decodes latents into local 3D geometry; a separate MLP predicts rotation/translation to align coarse-sampled poses with ground-truth surfaces during training. At inference, the diffusion model denoises from Gaussian noise to produce latents for each valid pose, which are decoded, transformed to world coordinates, merged, and sampled into a scattered touch point cloud. This touch cloud is then fused with the coarse point cloud through cross-attention and self-attention layers (with point transformers) inside a SymmCompletion-style refinement network that iteratively upsamples and predicts per-point offsets, trained end-to-end with Chamfer Distance loss.

## Results

On the PCN dataset, TouchDream achieves the best average Chamfer Distance of 6.05 (×10³) and the highest F1-Score of 0.87, outperforming the previous best SymmCompletion (CD 6.28, F1 0.85) by 3.7% in CD. On ShapeNet55/34, it attains the lowest L2 CD of 0.65 (×10³) across 55 categories and a particularly strong 0.66 on 21 unseen categories (vs. 0.97 for SymmCompletion), demonstrating superior generalization. On the real-world KITTI dataset, TouchDream achieves the best FD (1.43) and MMD (0.71) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TouchDream outperforms all 12 baselines on every evaluated dataset and metric. On PCN, it beats the closest competitor SymmCompletion [34] by 0.23 in average CD (6.05 vs. 6.28) and by 0.02 in F1 (0.87 vs. 0.85), while the gap widens substantially against older methods like PCN [40] (CD 9.64) and PoinTr [37] (CD 8.38). On ShapeNet55/34, the advantage is most pronounced on the 21 unseen categories, where TouchDream achieves CD 0.66 versus SymmCompletion's 0.97 and PCDreamer's 1.09, indicating stronger generalization. On the real-world KITTI dataset, TouchDream attains the best FD (1.43) and MMD (0.71), surpassing SymmCompletion (FD 2.54, MMD 1.72) and SVDFormer (FD 11.3, MMD 0.97). The ablation in Table 4 further confirms that generated touch guidance (CD 6.05) is nearly on par with ground-truth touch (CD 5.93) and clearly superior to skeleton-point guidance (CD 6.04), validating the core design choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

TouchDream builds directly on SymmCompletion's coarse-to-fine pipeline (using its LSTNet for initial coarse generation and its refinement architecture) and on the tactile chart-prediction framework from Smith et al. and Wang et al. (Touch2Shape), which established pose sampling and local mesh deformation for touch-based shape recovery. The key departure is replacing physical or simulated tactile sensing with a diffusion model that 'dreams' tactile latent codes conditioned on coarse points and sampled poses, drawing its generative backbone from DiffusionSDF/DALLE-2. This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion-based 3D generation and touch-based reconstruction, but repurposes tactile data as a purely internal generative signal rather than an external sensor input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that imagined local tactile geometry can serve as an effective refinement signal for point cloud completion, offering a principled alternative to multi-view visual cues that avoids the geometric consistency issues of rendered-image-based methods. However, the approach inherits the dependency on a high-quality initial coarse prediction (a poor LSTNet output degrades the entire pipeline), requires object meshes for training supervision, and is computationally expensive (6.7 hours for 1,200 PCN shapes on a single GPU). Open questions include whether learnable pose sampling (via RL) can reduce the number of required touch queries, whether the framework extends to truly real-world settings without mesh access, and whether the imagined-touch signal can be integrated into unified multimodal (vision + touch) generative pipelines.
