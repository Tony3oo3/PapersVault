## Problem

Existing YOLO architectures apply uniform dense computation to every input regardless of scene complexity, wasting resources on trivial scenes while under-serving complex dense ones. This static one-size-fits-all paradigm prevents adaptive capacity allocation and caps the accuracy-efficiency trade-off at a fixed point determined at design time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261746/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB image at 640×640 resolution and produces standard YOLO detection outputs: bounding boxes, class labels, and confidence scores across 80 COCO categories (or task-specific class sets). A key design detail is that routing is image-level rather than spatial: a single Global Average Pooling compresses the entire feature map into one descriptor, so all spatial positions within a given feature map share the same expert selection, making the computation truly input-adaptive at the scene level.

## Method

YOLO-Master inserts an Efficient Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (ES-MoE) block into the YOLO backbone, where E=4 experts are depthwise-separable convolutions with distinct kernel sizes (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) to capture multi-scale patterns. A lightweight gating network (GAP → 1×1 conv with 8× channel reduction → SiLU → 1×1 conv) produces per-expert logits, and a phased routing strategy uses Soft Top-K during training for differentiable gradient flow while switching to Hard Top-K (K=2) at inference for genuine sparsity. A load-balancing loss (MSE between per-expert utilization and uniform 1/E) prevents expert collapse, and ablations show that removing the DFL regression loss in favor of a stronger MoE loss (λ=1.5) avoids gradient conflicts and yields the best convergence. Backbone-only placement is critical: adding ES-MoE to the neck or both stages causes gradient interference and degrades performance by up to 5.9%.

## Results

On MS COCO, YOLO-Master-N achieves 42.4% mAP at 1.62 ms latency, outperforming YOLOv13-N by +0.8% mAP while being 17.8% faster. Across the five benchmarks, YOLO-Master-N leads on every dataset: +1.4% mAP on PASCAL VOC, +2.1% on VisDrone, +1.5% on KITTI, and +0.7% on SKU-110K. The largest relative gains appear on dense/small-object scenes (VisDrone, KITTI), confirming the adaptive expert routing benefits most where static architectures under-allocate capacity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOLO-Master-N outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark. Against YOLOv13-N (the strongest baseline), it gains +0.8% mAP on COCO, +1.4% on VOC, +2.1% on VisDrone, +1.5% on KITTI, and +0.7% on SKU-110K, while also reducing latency from 1.97 ms to 1.62 ms (17.8% faster). Compared to YOLOv12-N, the improvement is larger at +1.8% mAP on COCO, and YOLO-Master-N is marginally faster (1.62 vs 1.64 ms). Against YOLOv11-N, YOLO-Master-N wins on accuracy across all five datasets (+3.0% mAP on COCO) but is slightly slower (1.62 vs 1.50 ms, ~8% overhead). Versus YOLOv10-N, the gap is widest at +3.9% mAP on COCO, with YOLO-Master-N also being 12% faster. The consistent wins on VisDrone and KITTI—datasets dominated by small, densely packed objects—highlight that the MoE routing provides the most benefit precisely where static YOLO variants under-allocate representational capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLO-Master extends the YOLOv12 architecture by inserting an Efficient Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (ES-MoE) module into the backbone, adapting the conditional-computation paradigm well established in NLP (Switch Transformers, sparsely-gated MoE) to lightweight CNN-based detection. It departs from all prior YOLO variants (v5–v13) and RTDETR, which rely on static dense computation, by routing each image through a subset of depthwise-separable-convolution experts with diverse receptive fields (3×3, 5×5, 7×7) selected by a GAP-based gating network. The phased routing strategy—soft Top-K during training for gradient continuity and hard Top-K at inference for genuine sparsity—is directly inherited from the Switch Transformer literature, while the load-balancing MSE loss is a detection-specific adaptation of the auxiliary balancing objectives used in large-scale MoE language models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that input-adaptive capacity allocation can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce latency in real-time detection, offering a practical recipe (backbone-only placement, 4 experts, Top-2 routing, MoE-only loss) that YOLO practitioners can adopt with minimal architectural change. Two ablation findings carry broader design lessons: cascading MoE modules in both backbone and neck causes gradient interference that degrades performance by ~6%, and the standard DFL regression loss conflicts with MoE load-balancing gradients, suggesting that mixture-of-experts architectures may require rethinking of auxiliary losses. Open questions include whether spatially-resolved (per-pixel) routing rather than image-level GAP routing could further improve dense-scene detection, whether the approach scales to medium/large YOLO variants, and how the method behaves under domain shift or adversarial inputs that might exploit the routing mechanism.
