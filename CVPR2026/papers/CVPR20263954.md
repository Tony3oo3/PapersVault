## Problem

RL-based GUI agents largely underexploit historical context: most prior methods either omit past screenshots entirely (losing visual cues needed for disambiguation and temporal consistency) or naively include full history (incurring quadratic attention cost and distraction from irrelevant tokens). No existing framework jointly addresses how to adaptively select which history to use during sampling and how to compress history during policy updates without degrading decision quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263954/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language task instruction, the current high-resolution screenshot, and a variable-length history of (screenshot, action) pairs. During training rollouts, the history length τ is dynamically sampled per response variant (τ ∈ {0, 1, 2}), while at inference the full history is always provided. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a structured GUI action consisting of a discrete type (e.g., CLICK, SCROLL, TYPE, FINISH) and a type-specific value (coordinates, text, direction).

## Method

HCPO augments GRPO-based RL training with two complementary mechanisms. Dynamic Context Sampling (DCS) replaces fixed-length history with G sampled variants drawn from an exponential-biased distribution ExpBias(u) whose bias λ(u) grows linearly over training steps, shifting from uniform exploration toward full-context usage and preventing the degeneration observed under naive uniform sampling. Anchor-guided History Compression (AHC) introduces a dual-branch update: the uncompressed branch is optimized with standard GRPO, while a compressed branch—where all history vision tokens are pruned after an early fusion layer k (default k=6) but history action tokens are retained as information-flow anchors—is trained with the same group advantages plus a KL alignment loss to the detached uncompressed branch. This design is grounded in a layer-wise token-drop analysis showing that action tokens, not raw visual tokens, are the primary carriers of historical semantics in later layers, so compressing visuals after early fusion preserves decision quality while reducing FLOPs by ~60% and yielding 2.47× speedup.

## Results

On GUI-Odyssey, HiconAgent-3B achieves 52.10% grounding accuracy and 50.11% step successful rate (SR), outperforming the larger GUI-R1-7B by +8.46% and +11.32% respectively under identical training data and settings. On AndroidControl-High, HiconAgent-3B reaches 65.51% grounding and 52.40% SR, matching or slightly exceeding GUI-R1-7B (65.56% / 51.67%). Across all three benchmarks (AC-High, AITW, GUI-Odyssey), HiconAgent-3B attains the highest average SR of 51.47% among all compared models, while achieving 2.47× computational speedup and 60% FLOPs reduction relative to the uncompressed 3B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GUI-R1 (the primary RL baseline), HiconAgent-3B surpasses GUI-R1-7B by +8.46% grounding and +11.32% SR on GUI-Odyssey, and by +0.73% SR on AC-High, while matching grounding within 0.05%. Versus GPT-4o (zero-shot), HiconAgent-3B leads by a wide margin on all metrics (e.g., 52.40% vs 21.17% SR on AC-High). In the broader OOD comparison (Table 2), HiconAgent-3B outperforms OS-Atlas-7B (32.72% avg SR), UI-shift-7B (46.43%), and UI-AGILE (45.53%) on average SR, despite using only 3K unfiltered training samples versus 13M, 2K-filtered, and 9K-filtered respectively. Against infiGUI-R1-3B, HiconAgent-3B loses substantially on AC-High (52.40% vs 71.10% SR) but wins on AITW (51.91% vs 46.51%) and GUI-Odyssey (50.11% vs 33.15%), yielding a higher average SR (51.47% vs 50.25%) and demonstrating stronger cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm (from DeepSeekMath) and the GUI-R1 agent framework by introducing history-aware modifications to both the sampling and policy-update phases. Prior GUI RL works (GUI-R1, UI-AGILE, UI-Shift) either omit history observations entirely or naively include full visual history, leaving the trade-off between decision quality and computational cost unaddressed. HiconAgent departs from this by proposing Dynamic Context Sampling (exponential-biased variable-length history during rollouts) and Anchor-guided History Compression (dual-branch optimization with action tokens as information-flow anchors), grounded in a novel layer-wise token-drop analysis specific to the GUI navigation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 3B model outperforms a 7B baseline by +11.32% step successful rate on GUI-Odyssey while achieving 2.47× speedup and 60% FLOPs reduction, making history-aware GUI agents viable under tight compute budgets. The empirical finding that different decision steps prefer different history lengths, and that action tokens serve as critical anchors for visual information flow, provides a principled basis for future compression strategies in sequential multimodal agents. Open questions include whether the compression strategy generalizes to longer horizons (τ > 2), whether the exponential-biased schedule is optimal, and whether the dual-branch alignment loss scales to larger models or more complex action spaces.
