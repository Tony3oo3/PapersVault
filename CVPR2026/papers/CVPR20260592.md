## Problem

Existing video watermarking methods fail to simultaneously achieve imperceptibility, robustness, and computational efficiency: noise-space approaches require expensive DDIM inversion, model fine-tuning methods apply uniform modulation that limits per-frame control, and post-hoc schemes add latency while remaining decoupled from generative priors. No prior in-generation scheme supports arbitrary multi-key watermarking with per-frame messages and temporal modification localization without per-key retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning input (text prompt at 256×256 for ModelScope or a 576×1024 image for SVD-XT), a video-level base key K_base, and latent noise z, and produces a watermarked video at the same resolution and frame count as the base model. The base key is expanded via HMAC-SHA256 into per-frame 28-bit messages (one per frame), and the watermark extractor (a frame-wise ResNet-50) outputs 28 logits per frame. Verification additionally produces a set of valid frame assignments that localizes temporal modifications (drops, insertions, swaps, trims) through maximum bipartite matching and binomial hypothesis testing.

## Method

SPDMark embeds watermarks by selectively displacing parameters in the frozen latent decoder of a video diffusion model, leaving the encoder and denoiser untouched. The displacement is decomposed into L=14 layer-wise additive basis shifts, each implemented as a LoRA module (rank r=32) with P=4 parallel adapters per layer, yielding a 28-bit per-frame payload (log₂4 × 14). A simple key-mapping procedure partitions the watermark key into L chunks of log₂P bits, where each chunk selects exactly one basis shift per layer, so a single trained dictionary supports arbitrary keys without retraining. During training, the basis shifts and a ResNet-50 extractor are jointly optimized by minimizing a weighted combination of binary cross-entropy (message recovery), LPIPS (perceptual similarity), and a luminance-difference temporal consistency loss, all in expectation over random keys, prompts, and noise. At verification time, per-frame extracted messages are aligned to reference messages via the Hungarian algorithm on a bipartite similarity graph, and each assignment is validated with a binomial tail test to control frame-level and video-level false positive rates.

## Results

On SVD-XT, SPDMark achieves 99.5% bit accuracy with 28 bits/frame × 25 frames payload, while attaining the highest Subject Consistency (0.966) and Motion Smoothness (0.975) among all compared methods. On ModelScope, SPDMark reaches 98.8% bit accuracy (28 bits/frame × 16 frames) and leads in Background Consistency (0.968), Motion Smoothness (0.972), and Imaging Quality (0.623). Across 16 photometric, temporal, and post-processing attacks, SPDMark achieves the best average robustness of 93.5% (SVD-XT) and 93.9% (ModelScope), outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPDMark clearly outperforms VidSig across nearly all metrics: on SVD-XT, bit accuracy is 99.5% vs. 95.8%, and average robustness is 93.5% vs. 68.5%; on ModelScope the gap widens to 93.9% vs. 81.2% robustness, with VidSig particularly fragile under blur (0.420) and geometric transforms. Against VideoSeal, SPDMark trades a marginal bit-accuracy gap (99.5% vs. 99.9% on SVD-XT) for substantially better robustness (93.5% vs. 91.2% on SVD-XT; 93.9% vs. 90.6% on ModelScope) and superior temporal quality (MS 0.975 vs. 0.961). Against VideoShield, SPDMark wins on robustness (93.5% vs. 83.3% on SVD-XT) and all quality metrics, while VideoShield retains a higher per-video payload (512 bits vs. 28×25) but suffers severe degradation under cropping (0.521) and rotation (0.507). Overall, SPDMark is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-perfect bit recovery, top-tier video quality, and the strongest average robustness across both diffusion backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPDMark extends the in-generation video watermarking line (LVMark, VidSig) by replacing uniform layer modulation or fixed signatures with a key-conditioned composition of learned low-rank basis shifts, borrowing the LoRA parameterization to keep the displacement parameter-efficient. It departs from noise-space methods (VideoShield, VideoMark) by avoiding DDIM inversion entirely, and from post-hoc methods (VideoSeal) by embedding the watermark during generation. The per-frame key derivation via HMAC-SHA256 combined with Hungarian-algorithm bipartite matching for temporal alignment is a novel verification pipeline not present in prior video watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPDMark offers a practical deployment path for video provenance tracking: multi-key support without per-key retraining, negligible inference overhead, and built-in temporal forensics that can localize frame drops, swaps, and insertions. Limitations include evaluation on only two architectures (SVD-XT, ModelScope), a modest 28-bit per-frame payload, and noticeable degradation under strong blur and rescaling. Open questions include whether the basis-shift dictionary generalizes to longer videos, DiT-based decoders, and adversarial attacks specifically designed to erase the watermark while preserving visual quality.
