## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance on plane geometry but fail on solid geometry due to 3D spatial reasoning demands, complex occluded structures, and the need for rigorous algebraic computation. Existing solid geometry benchmarks are scattered, contain LLM-generated answers prone to errors, and lack formal geometric annotations. No unified formal language framework existed for representing and reasoning about solid geometric entities and their spatial relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural language problem description paired with a 2D diagram image depicting a 3D geometric configuration. The MLLM parser (using 15–45 in-context examples covering all 120 predicates) produces a formal Condition Description Language (CDL) string encoding entities, spatial relations, and metric constraints. The reasoning engine then outputs an ordered theorem sequence (typically ~57–175 steps) and a final numerical or descriptive answer, with each step being individually verifiable and human-readable.

## Method

Hilbert-Geo is a two-stage neural-symbolic pipeline. The Multimodal Formalization Parser (M2FP) uses in-context learning with an MLLM (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro) to jointly parse text and diagram into CDL, a formal language of 120 predicates (35 fundamental, 20 entity types, 35 relations, 30 attribute descriptors) that eliminates cross-modal ambiguity. The Solid Geometry Reasoning Engine (SGRE) then performs search-based inference over a node-based reasoning tree, iteratively matching expandable nodes against a 220-theorem bank (extending the plane FormalGeo framework) to derive solutions through logical substitution and algebraic computation. The key design insight is decoupling perception (neural, error-prone) from deduction (symbolic, exact), so that the MLLM only handles the ill-posed task of grounding multimodal inputs into formal predicates, while all geometric reasoning is delegated to a deterministic theorem prover that guarantees no calculation or logical errors. The framework is NP-hard in the search phase, but over 80% of solvable problems resolve within 1.2 seconds.

## Results

On SolidFGeo2k, Hilbert-Geo (using Gemini-2.5-Pro as the parsing MLLM with 45 in-context samples) achieves 77.3% overall accuracy, substantially outperforming the best standalone MLLM (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 54.2%) by 23.1 percentage points. With ground-truth CDL annotations, the reasoning engine reaches 78.7%, approaching the human baseline of 81.8%. On MathVerse-Solid, Hilbert-Geo attains 84.1% accuracy (vs. GPT-5's 62.9%), and on PlaneFGeo3k it achieves 80.2%, demonstrating generality across solid and plane geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hilbert-Geo (77.3%) outperforms all seven compared MLLMs on SolidFGeo2k by a wide margin. The closest competitor, Gemini 2.5 Pro (54.2%), is surpassed by 23.1 points; GPT-5 (50.6%) and Claude 3.7 Sonnet (47.1%) trail by 26.7 and 30.2 points respectively. Among open-source models, Llama 3.3 70B (33.6%) and DeepSeek-V3 67B (30.1%) are the strongest but still fall 43+ points behind Hilbert-Geo. On MathVerse-Solid, Hilbert-Geo (84.1%) beats GPT-5 (62.9%) by 21.2 points. Notably, even the weakest MLLM used as a parser (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 30.7% in Table 2) still exceeds several standalone MLLMs' direct-answering accuracy, indicating that the formal reasoning pipeline provides substantial gains regardless of the underlying perceptual model. The gap between Hilbert-Geo and the human baseline (81.8%) is only 4.5 points, whereas the best MLLM lags humans by 27.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hilbert-Geo directly extends the FormalGeo plane geometry formalization framework [38] to the three-dimensional domain, augmenting its predicate library (from plane to 120 solid-geometry predicates) and theorem bank (220 theorems) with 3D-specific primitives for polyhedra, spherical surfaces, and spatial topological relations. The neural-symbolic pipeline—MLLM-based parsing followed by formal theorem search—follows the established pattern of GNS [25] and Inter-GPS [19], but is the first to apply it to solid geometry, where prior formalization work (Wu's Method, Gröbner bases, Geometry3K, GeoQA) was confined to 2D. The paper also positions itself against the broader MLLM literature, arguing that stereometric knowledge's heterogeneity across text, graphics, and intuition demands a unified formal paradigm that current models lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a clear gap: solid geometry has been largely neglected by both formal reasoning systems and MLLM benchmarks, and the 23-point accuracy gap between the best MLLM (54.2%) and Hilbert-Geo (77.3%) on SolidFGeo2k underscores how poorly current models handle 3D spatial reasoning. The curated datasets (SolidFGeo2k, PlaneFGeo3k) with expert-annotated CDL formalizations provide a much-needed benchmark for the community. However, the framework still trails human performance (81.8%), and the authors acknowledge limitations in vector-related tasks and problems requiring sophisticated auxiliary line constructions. Open questions include whether the combinatorial explosion in the reasoning engine can be mitigated for more complex 3D configurations, and whether the parsing bottleneck (the 1.4-point gap between ground-truth CDL and MLLM-parsed CDL) can be closed with better multimodal models.
