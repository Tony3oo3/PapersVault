## Problem

State-of-the-art B-rep learning methods encode face geometry using absolute 3D coordinates and normals in the global frame, making them inherently sensitive to model orientation. The paper demonstrates that models achieving over 95% accuracy on aligned benchmarks can collapse to as low as 10% under arbitrary SO(3) rotations, a critical limitation for manufacturing pipelines where CAD models arrive in diverse orientations. No existing B-rep descriptor captures both local surface geometry and global structural context in a rotation-invariant manner.

## Input / Output

Each B-rep face is encoded as three grid tensors: a 10×10×7 LRF UV-grid (relative coordinates, normals, trim mask in a per-face local frame), and two 6×12×3 Field-of-View grids (Outward and Inward Vision) generated by ray casting from the face center over hemispheres, recording hit flag, distance, and incidence angle per ray. These are concatenated with a 7D per-face attribute vector (one-hot surface type + area) and processed through the B-rep adjacency graph. For classification, node embeddings are max-pooled and classified; for segmentation, per-face embeddings are passed to a prediction head.

## Method

FoV-Net introduces a two-part rotation-invariant face descriptor. First, LRF UV-grids express each face's UV-sampled points and normals in a per-face orthonormal basis (tangent U, binormal V, normal N) anchored at the face center, so identical faces in different poses yield identical descriptors. Second, Field-of-View grids are constructed by casting rays from the face center over the outward hemisphere (along N) and inward hemisphere (along −N), discretized into elevation×azimuth bins aligned with the LRF, recording first-hit distance and incidence angle; because ray origins and directions co-rotate with the face, the resulting grids are inherently rotation-invariant. Dedicated lightweight 2D CNNs (with circular padding along azimuth for FoV grids) encode each tensor to 64-D vectors, which are fused with face attributes via a two-layer MLP. A 3-layer graph attention network (4 heads, hidden dim 64) then propagates information across the B-rep face-adjacency graph for task-specific prediction. The approach requires no rotation augmentation and achieves identical performance under arbitrary SO(3) rotations while also improving data efficiency in low-supervision segmentation regimes.

## Results

On SolidLetters classification, FoV-Net achieves 96.35% accuracy identically on both rotated and original test sets, while UV-Net collapses from 97.10% to 8.94% (−87.41 pp) and AAGNet from 96.68% to 14.03% (−82.65 pp) under arbitrary SO(3) rotations. On MFCAD++ segmentation, FoV-Net maintains 99.33% accuracy and 97.81% IoU regardless of orientation, whereas UV-Net's accuracy drops by 63.51 pp (98.95% → 35.44%) and IoU by 77.86 pp. On Fusion360 segmentation, FoV-Net achieves 91.72% accuracy / 73.81% IoU (rotation-invariant), outperforming UV-Net (69.13% / 37.07% rotated) and AAGNet (79.85% / 53.42% rotated). Rotation-augmented variants (UV-Net(aug), AAGNet(aug)) recover partial robustness but still trail FoV-Net and sacrifice overall accuracy on original data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoV-Net is compared against UV-Net [21] and AAGNet [55], the two leading B-rep learning methods, along with their rotation-augmented variants. Under arbitrary SO(3) rotations, FoV-Net dominates decisively: on SolidLetters it retains 96.35% accuracy while UV-Net drops to 8.94% and AAGNet to 14.03%; on MFCAD++ segmentation, FoV-Net holds 99.33% accuracy versus UV-Net's 35.44% and AAGNet's 80.13%. On original (aligned) test sets, UV-Net edges out FoV-Net on SolidLetters (97.10% vs. 96.35%) by exploiting pose cues (e.g., distinguishing b from d), but FoV-Net remains within 0.75 pp. AAGNet is competitive on aligned Fusion360 (90.60% vs. 91.72% for FoV-Net) and MFCAD++ (99.33% vs. 99.33%), but its rotation-invariant attributes (surface type, area) provide only partial protection under rotation. Rotation augmentation recovers much of the lost accuracy (UV-Net(aug): 94.97% on rotated SolidLetters) but at the cost of reduced full-dataset performance (95.34% vs. 97.10% original), a trade-off FoV-Net avoids entirely by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

FoV-Net builds directly on UV-Net's UV-grid paradigm for B-rep face descriptors, but re-expresses them in per-face Local Reference Frames (LRFs) to decouple local geometry from global pose, a technique borrowed from rotation-invariant point cloud learning. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of Field-of-View ray-casting grids (outward and inward) as a rotation-invariant mechanism for capturing global structural context between faces—something no prior B-rep descriptor explicitly provided. The GAT backbone and overall graph-based B-rep formulation follow the standard lineage from UV-Net and AAGNet, while the ray-casting idea is adapted from visibility analysis in computer graphics and point cloud processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art B-rep models collapse from >95% to ~10% accuracy under arbitrary rotations exposes a critical vulnerability for industrial CAD pipelines where models arrive in uncontrolled orientations. The data-efficiency results (80% accuracy with 50 samples vs. ~30% for UV-Net) further demonstrate that rotation invariance is not merely a robustness nicety but a generalization property that matters even on well-aligned data in low-data regimes. Open questions include scalability to large assemblies, whether spherical CNNs can replace the polar-distorted equiangular FoV mapping, and whether the framework extends to B-rep generation where edge features are essential.
