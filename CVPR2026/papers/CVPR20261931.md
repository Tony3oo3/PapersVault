## Problem

Dense prediction tasks on small datasets face a difficult trade-off: parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA require backpropagation through large backbones (high memory and compute), while linear readouts are fast but produce only low-resolution outputs due to the coarse spatial resolution of transformer backbones. No existing method combines the training speed of linear probing with the fine-grained spatial detail needed for tasks like contour prediction, keypoint localization, and small-object detection, all while remaining parameter-light enough to avoid overfitting on a few hundred samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 512×512 px (518×518 for DINOv2) and extracts multi-level features from a frozen backbone (layers 4, 8, 11, and last for ViT-B; 8, 16, 22, 24 for ViT-L), which are L2-normalized per layer and concatenated. The output is a dense prediction map at 256×256 resolution (64×64 query grid upsampled 4× by the CNN), whose channel dimension is task-specific: class logits for segmentation, keypoint heatmaps for pose, a triple of center/size/class-embedding maps for CenterNet-style detection, or a binary contour map. For contour evaluation on BSDS500, the image is additionally tiled into 180×180 patches and predictions are composed from tile outputs.

## Method

LiDeRe consists of three stages: (1) a linear projection that maps concatenated multi-layer backbone features to a compact 64-dimensional space; (2) a feature interpolation module that combines a content-agnostic learnable interpolation prior with content-guided attention; and (3) a two-block CNN upscaler (conv + transposed conv, 2× each) that raises resolution to 256×256. The interpolation prior is an implicit MLP with sine activations that takes 4-D coordinate-difference vectors (axis-wise and squared differences between feature-volume and target positions) and outputs an interpolation matrix, learning a task-dependent geometric upsampling kernel. This matrix is added as a bias to a standard multi-head (8-head) cross-attention whose queries are position-encoded target coordinates and whose keys/values come from the projected features, so after softmax the spatial prior and content attention effectively multiply. Because the backbone is fully frozen, no gradients flow through it, yielding training speeds 2.5–20× faster than Conv-LoRA; optionally, LoRA adapters can be inserted into the backbone for further accuracy gains at modest parameter cost.

## Results

On semantic segmentation with small datasets (Table 1), LiDeRe with DINOv3 ViT-L achieves Fβω=87.6 on CAMO and mIoU=84.2 on Trans10K-hard, matching or exceeding Conv-LoRA (84.0 and 84.4) while using only 0.34M trainable parameters versus 4.0M. On the very small Leaf dataset (399 samples), LiDeRe achieves FG-IoU=77.5, a 3-point gain over Conv-LoRA (74.5). With LoRA complement, the method reaches mIoU=90.5 on Pascal VOC (vs. 79.6 for SAM-PARSER), ODS=85.3 on BSDS500 contour segmentation (vs. 82.9 for UAED), and mAP=73.8 on iRodent pose estimation (vs. 73.0 for SuperAnimal with task-specific pretraining).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary PEFT baselines (VPT, LoRA, Conv-LoRA) on SAM ViT-B, LiDeRe with DINOv3 ViT-L matches or exceeds Conv-LoRA on CAMO Fβω (87.6 vs. 84.0) and Trans10K-hard mIoU (84.2 vs. 84.4) while using only 8.5% of the trainable parameters (0.34M vs. 4.0M); the gap widens substantially on the 399-sample Leaf dataset where LiDeRe gains 3 FG-IoU points. On Pascal VOC, LiDeRe+LoRA (mIoU=90.5) outperforms all SAM-based PEFT methods (SAM-PARSER 79.6, LoRA 79.5) by over 10 points, and also beats DINOv2 linear+multi-scale baselines (86.0). For pose estimation, LiDeRe+LoRA surpasses SuperAnimal (which uses task-specific pretraining on AP-10K) on both iRodent (mAP 73.8 vs. 73.0) and Horse10 (mAP 96.3 vs. 95.2) despite having no pose-specific pretraining. On BSDS500 contour segmentation, LiDeRe+LoRA (ODS 85.3) outperforms task-specific detectors UAED (82.9) and EDTER (82.4) while using 0.73M vs. 72M/469M parameters. On PlantDoc object detection, LiDeRe (DINOv3 ViT-L) achieves mAP=50.9, slightly below Cascade-DN-Def-DETR with COCO pretraining (52.7) but beating all methods without task-specific pretraining, and notably achieving mAP@50=73.6 vs. 66.5 for the same baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and dense prediction by repurposing the implicit neural representation idea (coordinate-conditioned MLPs with sine activations) as a learnable feature interpolation prior, combined with content-guided cross-attention. It departs from the dominant SAM-based PEFT paradigm (Conv-LoRA, SAM-PARSER, VPT) by showing that a frozen self-supervised backbone (DINOv2/DINOv3) with a sub-400K-parameter readout can match or exceed those methods, eliminating the need for backbones natively designed for dense outputs or large task-specific heads. The method also builds on the broader PEFT taxonomy (additive, selective, reparameterized) but occupies a distinct niche: a lightweight, backbone-agnostic readout that requires no backpropagation through the backbone, placing it closer to linear probing in efficiency but far exceeding it in resolution and expressiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for applied settings where labeled data is scarce and compute is limited: training in under five minutes on a single GPU with a tiny parameter count makes dense prediction accessible to users without large clusters. The broader implication—that increasingly powerful self-supervised backbones reduce the need for complex task-specific architectures—is supported here but remains an open question as backbone quality continues to improve. A key limitation is that the readout must be trained from scratch, precluding zero-shot or few-shot use, which constrains its applicability relative to SAM-based methods. Future work could explore whether the interpolation prior generalizes across tasks without retraining or whether it can be combined with other PEFT strategies beyond LoRA.
