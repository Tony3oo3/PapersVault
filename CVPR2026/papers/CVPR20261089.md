## Problem

ViT-based sparse multi-view 3D object detectors achieve strong accuracy but suffer from high inference latency due to dense token processing. Existing token compression strategies—pruning, merging, and uniform patch size enlargement—each introduce distinct failure modes: discarding informative background cues needed for hard negatives, disrupting contextual consistency, or losing fine-grained semantics critical for detection. The paper revisits these strategies and identifies that adaptive patch granularity with targeted detail recovery is the missing piece for an effective accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (6 cameras at 320×800 or 640×1600 on nuScenes, 640×960 on Argoverse 2) along with 8 frames of historical object queries from the StreamPETR memory queue. The depth values of these historical queries in ego-centric coordinates drive the dynamic patch size decision, while motion-aligned query embeddings provide temporal context for patch selection. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via NDS/mAP (nuScenes) or CDS/mAP (Argoverse 2), with the primary metric being inference latency reduction at comparable accuracy.

## Method

SEPatch3D introduces a dynamic dual patch embedding pipeline with three coupled modules. First, Spatiotemporal-aware Patch Size Selection (SPSS) computes the mean depth of historical object queries and fits a linear regression over the last 8 frames' depth trends; based on the depth level and its temporal slope, it switches between a small patch size (preserving fine details for nearby objects) and a large patch size (reducing computation for distant scenes), using FlexiViT-style flexible patch embedding. Second, Informative Patch Selection (IPS) scores vanilla (fine) patches by the entropy of their L2-normalized shallow features after cross-attention with motion-aligned queries, then adaptively selects patches exceeding the scene-mean entropy rather than using a fixed top-K. Third, Cross-Granularity Feature Enhancement (CGFE) applies cross-attention where selected coarse patches act as queries to retrieve fine-grained details from their corresponding vanilla patches, with a residual connection preserving global structure. The entire framework is plug-and-play, requiring only ~3.6% additional parameters over the baseline StreamPETR.

## Results

On nuScenes (320×800), SEPatch3D-fast matches StreamPETR's accuracy (61.2% NDS, 52.1% mAP) while reducing inference time by 21.1% (317.0→250.2 ms); SEPatch3D-faster achieves 60.3% NDS with a 38.7% speedup (194.3 ms). At higher resolution (640×1600), SEPatch3D-faster delivers a 57.7% inference speedup (1309.9→554.4 ms) with only 0.3% NDS drop (62.7→62.4%). On Argoverse 2 (640×960), SEPatch3D-faster reduces inference time by 40.7% (683.1→405.1 ms) with less than 1% CDS degradation (19.0→18.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline StreamPETR [37], SEPatch3D-fast matches accuracy exactly (61.2% NDS, 52.1% mAP at 320×800; 62.7% NDS at 640×1600) while cutting inference time by 21–48%. Against the closest competitor ToC3D-faster [49], SEPatch3D-faster is 17.6% faster in total inference (194.3 vs. 237.2 ms at 320×800) with comparable NDS (60.3 vs. 60.5%), and at 640×1600 the gap widens to 37% faster (554.4 vs. 878.5 ms) with 0.5% higher NDS. Against tgGBC [42] (decoder-only pruning), SEPatch3D-faster at 640×1600 is 32% faster (554.4 vs. 817.7 ms) and achieves 1.3% higher NDS (62.4 vs. 61.1%). For generalization, SEPatch3D applied to DETR3D [38] and Sparse4Dv2 [24] reduces latency by 22–37% with ≤0.5% NDS drop, confirming the method is detector-agnostic. On Argoverse 2, SEPatch3D-faster outperforms all VovNet-based detectors (BEVStereo [19], SOLOFusion [29], PETR [28], Sparse4Dv2 [24]) in both CDS and mAP while being the only ViT-based entry, demonstrating that the efficiency gains do not come at the cost of representational quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEPatch3D builds directly on StreamPETR's sparse query-based multi-view 3D detection framework and FlexiViT's flexible patch embedding mechanism, but departs from prior token compression strategies (pruning, merging, fixed patch enlargement) by introducing spatiotemporal-aware dynamic patch size selection. The most closely related work, ToC3D, also targets ViT-based sparse 3D detectors via token compression but relies on importance-scored pruning that discards background tokens; SEPatch3D instead preserves all spatial coverage while adaptively varying granularity and selectively enhancing informative coarse patches. The paper's empirical revisit of pruning, merging, and patch enlargement specifically in the 3D detection context—highlighting that background regions serve as critical hard negatives—provides the motivation for this design shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 57% inference speedup with less than one point NDS degradation is practically significant for real-time autonomous driving deployment, and the method's demonstrated generalization across multiple sparse detectors (StreamPETR, DETR3D, Sparse4Dv2) and ViT backbones (ViT-B, ViT-L, ToC3DViT-L) suggests broad applicability. The insight that entropy-based patch selection outperforms learned scores or attention weights at shallow feature stages is a useful practical finding for any ViT acceleration pipeline. Open questions include whether the spatiotemporal patch size switching introduces temporal flickering in edge cases, whether the polynomial budget-aware fitting generalizes to substantially different architectures, and whether the approach extends to other ViT-heavy tasks beyond 3D detection.
