## Problem

Learned perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS) used to train image compression networks are calibrated on fixed human-judgment datasets, can be gamed via null-space exploitation, and may not generalize to semantically diverse content. The paper asks whether off-the-shelf vision-language models can serve as zero-shot perceptual judges that replicate human 2AFC similarity judgments, and how to exploit such non-differentiable VLM preferences to improve compression without distilling them into a separate loss network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262426/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image (trained at 256×256 on ImageNet; evaluated on images up to 4 MP via tiled inference) and produces a compressed bitstream consisting of a 1-D discrete latent code (FSQ-quantized) further compressed by an autoregressive transformer entropy coder and arithmetic coding, plus a stochastically decoded reconstruction from a diffusion decoder. Two operating points are provided at 0.07 and 0.21 bpp (prior to entropy coding). The decoder is stochastic, so multiple reconstructions are possible for the same latent code, which is essential for the preference-based post-training loop.

## Method

VLIC builds on the FlowMo diffusion autoencoder architecture (rectified flow, FSQ quantization) and adds a two-stage training procedure: (1) standard flow-matching pretraining with an LPIPS loss on the one-step denoised prediction, and (2) post-training via Diffusion DPO where two stochastic decodings of the same latent code are ranked by Gemini 2.5-Flash prompted to reason about artifacts and rate which reconstruction is closer to the original. To mitigate VLM hallucination and order bias, the reward is computed by reversing image order, self-ensembling over n=3 random seeds, and requiring unanimous agreement with LPIPS before a preference pair is used. The DPO objective is co-trained with the original flow-matching loss to prevent divergence, and VLM queries are issued asynchronously so that inference latency overlaps with gradient updates. A secondary autoregressive transformer entropy coder is trained separately to compress the discrete FSQ tokens, yielding the final bitstream.

## Results

On MS-COCO at 0.21 bpp, VLIC achieves a Human Elo of 1112, FD-DINO of 16.83, FID of 1.35, LPIPS of 0.168, and PSNR of 26.50 dB; at 0.07 bpp it reaches Elo 858, FD-DINO 62.25, FID 2.20, LPIPS 0.274, and PSNR 21.78 dB. VLIC generally outperforms HiFiC, PerCo, and HFD on perceptual metrics (Elo, FD-DINO, LPIPS) across MS-COCO, CLIC 2020, and CLIC 2022, with particularly strong gains on MS-COCO due to its high proportion of human-relevant content (faces, text). However, VLIC tends to sacrifice PSNR relative to these baselines, consistent with the known rate-distortion-perception tradeoff. On CLIC 2022, VLIC underperforms PO-ELIC, though that dataset contains only 30 images and PO-ELIC's code is not publicly available.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HiFiC (Mentzer et al., NeurIPS 2020), VLIC achieves superior perceptual metrics (Elo, FD-DINO, LPIPS) on MS-COCO and CLIC 2020 while typically reporting lower PSNR, reflecting a deliberate tradeoff toward human-aligned quality. Against PerCo (Careil et al., ICLR 2023), VLIC outperforms on most perceptual metrics; PerCo cannot handle high-resolution CLIC 2020/2022 data, limiting the comparison to MS-COCO. Against HFD (Hoogeboom et al., 2023), VLIC achieves stronger perceptual scores (Elo, FD-DINO) but worse PSNR, which the authors argue is appropriate given the goal of maximizing human-perceived quality. Against PO-ELIC (He et al., CVPR 2022), VLIC underperforms on CLIC 2022, but PO-ELIC has no released code and its performance on other datasets or resolutions is unknown, making this the only dataset where VLIC is clearly behind. Overall, VLIC achieves competitive or state-of-the-art human-aligned compression performance depending on the dataset, with its strongest advantage on MS-COCO where human-relevant features (faces, text) are prevalent.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLIC builds directly on FlowMo's diffusion autoencoder architecture and rectified-flow training, replacing lookup-free quantization with FSQ and adding an autoregressive entropy coder. Its key departure from the dominant paradigm in perceptual image compression is replacing learned differentiable perceptual losses (LPIPS, DISTS, DreamSim) with zero-shot VLM binary judgments, delivered through Diffusion DPO post-training rather than distillation into a separate metric network. This connects two previously separate literatures—diffusion model preference alignment (DDPO, DPO) and learned image compression—by treating the VLM as a non-differentiable reward signal that the diffusion decoder can be optimized against without gradient access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central implication is that as VLMs improve through ongoing industry investment, human-aligned compression quality could improve automatically without collecting new human judgment data or retraining perceptual metric networks, addressing a known generalization limitation of calibrated perceptual losses. However, practical adoption is constrained by the high cost of VLM queries during training and the added latency of the diffusion decoder relative to GAN-based methods. Open questions include whether cheaper or smaller VLMs can serve as adequate perceptual judges, whether the approach extends to video compression, and how much of the VLM's perceptual signal is genuinely semantic versus low-level texture matching.
