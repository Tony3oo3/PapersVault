## Problem

Existing MLLM-based FAS methods (e.g., I-FAS) reformulate spoof detection as generating brief textual descriptions, which biases the model toward coarse semantic cues like mask contours or screen borders while remaining blind to fine-grained visual patterns such as texture irregularities and frequency anomalies. This limits cross-domain generalization, especially against high-quality spoofs with subtle visual evidence. The paper asks how to guide MLLMs beyond intuitive classification toward deeper, tool-assisted investigation of hidden spoof traces.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 224×224 aligned and cropped RGB face image as input. It produces a two-stage output: a fast intuitive classification in the first turn, followed by a multi-turn reasoning chain (up to 6 turns) in which the model adaptively invokes external visual tools (Zoom-In, LBP, FFT, Wavelet, HOG, Laplacian Edge Detection), interprets their outputs, and arrives at a final binary real/spoof decision. The original classification logits are preserved throughout generation for HTER/AUC computation.

## Method

TAR-FAS reformulates FAS as a Chain-of-Thought with Visual Tools (CoT-VT) paradigm on top of InternVL-3-8B, where the MLLM first gives an intuitive answer and then adaptively calls low-level visual operators to investigate subtle spoof cues. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) FAS Knowledge Transfer via LoRA fine-tuning on I-FAS-format data to establish basic classification; (2) Tool-call Format Injection on the newly constructed ToolFAS-16K dataset (16,172 multi-turn tool-use trajectories annotated by Gemini-2.5 Pro with an expert-model-guided verification mechanism), using a loss-scale factor α=0.9 on the first turn to preserve classification ability; (3) Diverse-Tool Group Relative Policy Optimization (DT-GRPO), a full-parameter RL stage that samples G=8 rollouts per query and optimizes a composite reward combining fast-answer accuracy, final reasoning correctness, and a tool-diversity score that encourages the model to invoke multiple complementary tools, all gated by final classification correctness. This enables the model to autonomously learn when and which tools to use from query-label pairs alone, without explicit tool-use supervision in the RL stage.

## Results

Under the challenging one-to-eleven cross-domain protocol (train on CelebA-Spoof, test on 11 unseen datasets), TAR-FAS achieves an average HTER of 7.54% and AUC of 96.67, outperforming the previous SOTA I-FAS (11.30% HTER, 93.71 AUC) by 3.76 percentage points in HTER. The advantage is most pronounced on datasets with attack modalities absent from the source domain: on CASIA-SURF-3DMask (3D masks), TAR-FAS reaches 2.09% HTER vs. 6.18% for I-FAS, and on HKBU-MARs-V1+ (novel material attacks), it achieves 3.48% HTER vs. 18.64% for I-FAS. On the most difficult target (Rose-Youtu), TAR-FAS records 3.61% HTER compared to 5.52% for I-FAS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAR-FAS outperforms all four baselines across the 11-dataset average, with the largest margin over ViTAF (16.31% HTER reduction) and ViT-L (13.54% HTER reduction). Against the closest competitor I-FAS (the prior SOTA MLLM-based FAS method), TAR-FAS improves average HTER from 11.30% to 7.54% and AUC from 93.71 to 96.67. The gap widens substantially on datasets containing attack types unseen in the training domain: on CASIA-SURF-3DMask, TAR-FAS achieves 2.09% HTER vs. 6.18% for I-FAS (a 4.09-point gap), and on HKBU-MARs-V1+ it reaches 3.48% vs. 18.64% (a 15.16-point gap). FLIP, a CLIP-based method, is consistently outperformed by 11+ HTER points on average, while the non-MLLM baselines ViTAF and ViT-L show the weakest cross-domain generalization overall. Notably, TAR-FAS is the only method to achieve sub-5% HTER on both CASIA-SURF-3DMask and HKBU-MARs-V1+, confirming that tool-augmented fine-grained investigation provides a meaningful advantage over purely intuitive MLLM classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends I-FAS (Zhang et al., 2025), which first framed FAS as an MLLM task generating brief textual explanations, by addressing the known limitation that MLLMs are blind to fine-grained low-level visual patterns. The key conceptual departure is repurposing classical FAS operators (LBP, FFT, HOG, Wavelet, Laplacian Edge Detection) as external 'visual tools' that an MLLM can adaptively invoke during a Chain-of-Thought with Visual Tools (CoT-VT) reasoning process, drawing on the broader tool-use MLLM agent literature (ReAct, Chameleon, DeepEyes). The training pipeline combines supervised format injection on a purpose-built multi-turn dataset (ToolFAS-16K) with a reinforcement-learning stage (DT-GRPO) that uses a tool-diversity reward to encourage autonomous tool selection, moving beyond the single-shot description generation of prior MLLM-based FAS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that coupling MLLMs with classical signal-processing tools can close a meaningful gap in cross-domain FAS generalization (≈3.7% HTER reduction over I-FAS on the one-to-eleven protocol), while producing interpretable reasoning chains that show *which* visual evidence drove the decision. This is practically relevant because it offers a path to more trustworthy and auditable spoof detection in deployment. Open questions include whether the CoT-VT paradigm generalizes to other fine-grained visual tasks beyond FAS, how the approach scales to a larger tool library or higher-resolution inputs, and whether the multi-turn inference cost is acceptable for real-time biometric systems. The ToolFAS-16K dataset, while useful, is modest in scale and built on a single source domain, limiting its broader applicability.
