## Problem

VGGT and π³ achieve strong multi-view 3D reconstruction but rely heavily on global self-attention with O(N²) cost over N frames, making dense multi-view inference prohibitively expensive. Existing acceleration methods (FastVGGT, FasterVGGT) apply generic sparse-attention or token-merging tricks without a systematic understanding of what global attention actually does in the alternating global-frame architecture, leaving the core question of how to reduce its cost while preserving multi-view alignment unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a training-free inference-time modification on VGGT or π³, accepting N RGB images (evaluated from 10 to 800 frames) that are encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone into per-frame patch token grids plus one camera token and four register tokens per frame. Outputs are camera poses (rotation/translation), dense depth maps, 3D point maps, and tracking results, produced by the original model heads unchanged. A tunable subsampling factor σ ∈ {2, 4, 6, 9} controls the trade-off between speed and accuracy, with the first frame kept uncompressed in VGGT (as reference view) but all frames uniformly subsampled in π³.

## Method

The paper proposes a training-free two-step acceleration grounded in a layer-wise analysis showing that early global layers lack 3D signal, middle layers perform cross-view alignment, and last layers only refine. Step 1 (Global-to-Frame conversion): the first t_early global attention blocks (9 for VGGT, 10 for π³) are converted to per-frame attention by simply skipping the tensor reshaping that pools all frames, reducing their cost from O((NL)²) to O(NL²). Step 2 (Subsampling Global Attention, SGA): in the remaining global layers, patch tokens are subsampled as Keys/Values on a uniform 2D grid (one token per s_h×s_w window, e.g., σ=4 → 2×2) while all Queries and special tokens are retained; the attention is computed over three disjoint components — the retained K/V subset, the diagonal self-term for each query, and a single mean K/V pair approximating all dropped columns — sharing one softmax normalization. The design is motivated by a point-cloud alignment analogy: aligning two point clouds under a rigid transform requires only a few anchor points, making dense token-to-token matching redundant.

## Results

On the dense 7-Scenes setting (333 frames), AVGGT(4) achieves 78.29 AUC@30 for camera pose (vs. 77.76 for VGGT) and 0.054 mean accuracy for point maps, while reducing inference time from 79.9 s to 20.6 s (~3.9× speedup). Under the extreme 800-frame setting, AVGGT(9) reaches 77.38 AUC@30 (exceeding VGGT's 74.16) in 50.0 s versus VGGT's 397.1 s (8× speedup), while FasterVGGT and Fasterπ³ both run out of memory. On the sparse 10-frame RealEstate10K setting, AVGGT(2) matches VGGT's AUC@30 (87.76 vs. 88.13) with a small but consistent speedup (0.298 s vs. 0.307 s). Overall, the method delivers 2× speedup at 100 frames, 4–5× at 300 frames, and 8–10× at 800 frames while matching or slightly improving accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original VGGT, AVGGT(4) slightly improves AUC@30 (78.29 vs. 77.76) while cutting inference time by ~3.9× (20.6 s vs. 79.9 s) on the 333-frame 7-Scenes benchmark; at 800 frames the gap widens to 8× speedup with a 3.2-point AUC@30 gain. Against π³, Aπ³(4) matches AUC@30 within 0.4 points (80.27 vs. 80.67) at 4.7× lower runtime (12.6 s vs. 59.2 s), and at 800 frames Aπ³(9) retains 79.41 AUC@30 (vs. 80.57 original) in 30.3 s (10× speedup). Compared to FastVGGT, AVGGT(4) is both faster (20.6 s vs. 28.0 s) and more accurate (78.29 vs. 76.90 AUC@30) on the dense setting, and on the sparse 10-frame RealEstate10K setting AVGGT(2) avoids the accuracy drop that FastVGGT suffers (87.76 vs. 85.24 AUC@30). Against FasterVGGT, AVGGT(4) substantially outperforms both FasterVGGT_25 (73.23) and FasterVGGT_75 (70.07) in AUC@30 while also being faster (20.6 s vs. 70.0 s and 32.5 s respectively); critically, at 800 frames both FasterVGGT variants OOM whereas AVGGT(9) completes in 50.0 s. The consistent pattern across all baselines is that AVGGT/Aπ³ achieves the best or near-best accuracy at the lowest runtime, with the advantage growing as sequence length increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT and π³, which established alternating global and frame self-attention as the backbone for feed-forward multi-view 3D reconstruction. It departs from prior acceleration efforts (FastVGGT's token merging, FasterVGGT's block-sparse SpargeAttention) by first conducting a systematic layer-wise analysis of global attention to understand *why* it works, then designing a training-free two-step scheme (early-layer global-to-frame conversion plus grid-based K/V subsampling with diagonal preservation) grounded in a point-cloud alignment perspective. Rather than importing sparse-attention heuristics from other domains, the method is derived from the observed division of roles across early, middle, and last global layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of the acceleration means it can be applied immediately to existing VGGT/π³ checkpoints without retraining, and the 8–10× speedup at 800 frames while matching or slightly improving accuracy makes dense multi-view 3D reconstruction practically feasible on a single GPU. The layer-wise analysis (early layers are ineffective, middle layers perform alignment, last layers refine) provides a conceptual framework that could guide architecture design and training objectives for future 3D transformers. Limitations include evaluation restricted to VGGT and π³, and the subsampling strategy's behavior on architectures without the alternating global/frame structure remains untested. An open question is whether the point-cloud alignment lens generalizes to other multi-view tasks (e.g., tracking, novel view synthesis) or to models with different attention topologies.
