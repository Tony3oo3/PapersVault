## Problem

Model merging has been extensively studied for in-distribution and multi-task settings, but its behavior under domain generalization—where models are fine-tuned on distinct distribution shifts and must generalize to unseen domains—remains largely unexplored. The paper shows that domain shifts induce significantly stronger subspace conflicts (measured by Subspace Alignment Ratio) than task shifts, because models solving the same classification problem under different distributions align along similar singular directions, creating destructive interference during merging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260205/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only the fine-tuned parameter checkpoints from D source domains and the shared pre-trained backbone (CLIP image encoders: ViT-B/32, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14); no source training data, validation sets, or gradient steps are needed. It operates layer-wise on the delta weight matrices (θ_d − θ_pre) and outputs a single merged model with the same architecture, evaluated under a leave-one-domain-out protocol across 49 domains spanning 8 benchmarks. The scaling factor λ is fixed at 1 since out-of-distribution validation data is prohibited.

## Method

SCORE (Subspace COnflict-Resolving mErging) constructs a shared orthogonal basis by concatenating the top singular vectors of all domain-specific delta matrices, then computing the SVD of the concatenated matrices to obtain U_⊥ and V_⊥ that best represent all domain subspaces simultaneously. Each domain matrix is projected into this shared basis, where diagonal entries capture agreement (shared dominant directions) and off-diagonal entries expose inter-domain conflicts. A trim operation retains all diagonal elements and off-diagonal entries within 1.96 standard deviations of the off-diagonal mean, pruning outlier couplings that represent destructive singular-direction competition. The trimmed matrices are summed and the merged weight is reconstructed as U_⊥ Σ_score V_⊥^T, which is added to the pre-trained weights. This data-free, optimization-free procedure consistently outperforms seven baselines (Task Arithmetic, TIES, MagMax, PCB, TSV, ISO-C, ISO-CTS) by 0.58–0.74 p.p. on average across three model scales and eight domain generalization benchmarks.

## Results

SCORE achieves the highest average accuracy across all model merging methods on the leave-one-domain-out protocol: 65.69% on ViT-B/32 (beating the next-best TSV by 0.74 p.p.) and 73.04% on ViT-L/14 (beating TIES by 0.58 p.p.) across eight domain generalization benchmarks. On the larger ViT-L/14, SCORE leads on PACS (98.62%), DomainNet (68.26%), NICO++ (86.54%), OfficeHome (91.28%), and TerraIncognita (61.19%), with gains of up to 1.18 p.p. over the best competitor. All merging methods substantially outperform the zero-shot CLIP baseline (55.63% / 64.53%), confirming that model merging is a viable compositional mechanism for domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCORE outperforms all seven baselines on average across both model scales. On ViT-B/32, the closest competitor is TSV (64.95% avg), which SCORE beats by 0.74 p.p.; TIES follows at 64.84%. On ViT-L/14, TIES is the nearest rival at 72.46%, with SCORE ahead by 0.58 p.p. Per-dataset, SCORE leads on DomainNet (58.68% vs. PCB's 58.53% on ViT-B/32; 68.26% tied with PCB on ViT-L/14), NICO++ (80.59% vs. PCB's 80.19%), and OfficeHome (84.23% vs. PCB's 83.59%) on the smaller model, and on TerraIncognita (61.19% vs. TSV's 60.01%) and NICO++ (86.54% vs. PCB's 86.44%) on the larger model. Task Arithmetic performs worst among merging methods (42.47% / 53.89% avg), confirming its sensitivity to hyperparameter choice without validation data. The SVD-based baselines TSV and Iso-C/Iso-CTS are the strongest competitors, validating the paper's motivation that subspace conflicts are the key bottleneck in domain-generalization merging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SVD-based model merging line initiated by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al.) and refined by TSV (Gargiulo et al.), which decomposes task matrices and merges within singular subspaces. The key conceptual departure is reframing model merging from the multi-task setting (diverse label spaces) to domain generalization (same task, shifted distributions), where the authors show that subspace overlap between fine-tuned models is substantially higher, creating stronger singular-direction conflicts that existing SVD-based methods do not adequately handle. SCORE builds directly on TSV's orthogonalization step but adds a trim operation that selectively prunes off-diagonal outliers in the shared-basis representation to resolve these domain-specific conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of model merging under a leave-one-domain-out domain generalization protocol, providing a practical, data-free, training-free recipe for combining domain-specific checkpoints into a single generalizing model. The subspace overlap analysis (SAR metric) offers a useful diagnostic for understanding when SVD-based merging will struggle, and the finding that full off-diagonal retention catastrophically degrades performance highlights a previously unexamined failure mode. Limitations include the requirement for identical architectures and a common pre-trained backbone, the restriction to image classification, and the fixed λ=1 scaling; open questions include whether the approach transfers to NLP, generative models, or can be combined with domain-generalization training objectives.
