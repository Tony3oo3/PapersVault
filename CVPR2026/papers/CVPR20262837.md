## Problem

Standard Classifier-Free Guidance in flow-matching diffusion models relies on linear extrapolation between conditional and unconditional velocity predictions, which becomes unstable at high guidance scales—producing oversaturated colors, warped structures, and oscillatory divergence. Existing improvements (weight schedulers, APG, CFG-Zero*, Rectified-CFG++) still operate within linear control laws that cannot handle the inherent nonlinearity of large-capacity generative dynamics, leaving a gap in principled, stability-guaranteed guidance.

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play inference-time modification applied to pretrained flow-matching T2I models (SD3.5, Flux-dev, Qwen-Image). It consumes a text prompt and an initial Gaussian noise sample, then modifies the velocity field at each ODE integration step using only two additional hyperparameters (sliding surface shape λ and switching gain k) alongside the standard guidance scale w. No retraining or architectural changes are required; the output is a standard RGB image at the model's native resolution.

## Method

The paper first introduces CFG-Ctrl, a unified control-theoretic framework that reinterprets CFG as a proportional state-feedback controller acting on the semantic error e(t) = v_θ(x_t, t, c) − v_θ(x_t, t, ∅), with the guidance scale as the proportional gain and existing variants (weight schedulers, APG, CFG-Zero*, Rectified-CFG++) as alternative gain-schedule or projection-based control laws. Building on this, SMC-CFG defines an exponential sliding mode surface s(t) = ė(t) + λe(t) over the semantic error and introduces a switching control term Δe(t) = −k·sign(s(t)) that enforces nonlinear, feedback-driven correction toward the manifold. The guided velocity becomes v̂ = v_θ(∅) + w·(e(t) + Δe(t)), where the switching term adaptively shapes the error dynamics rather than merely scaling the model prediction. A Lyapunov analysis with V = ½‖s‖² proves finite-time convergence to the sliding manifold under the condition k·σ_min(Γ_s) > ‖Φ_s‖, guaranteeing stable guidance even at large scales.

## Results

On MS-COCO (5,000 image-text pairs), SMC-CFG achieves the best FID across all three flow-matching backbones: 20.044 on SD3.5, 26.398 on Flux-dev, and 33.371 on Qwen-Image, outperforming CFG-Zero* (20.317/26.901/35.391) and Rectified-CFG++ (20.550/27.219/34.371). It also leads on CLIP score (0.3694/0.3743/0.3856), ImageReward (0.9486/1.0558/1.2035), HPSv2.1 (0.2875/0.3021/0.3105), and MPS (7.5719/8.2307/8.4320), indicating superior semantic alignment and human-preference scores. The method is model-agnostic, requiring only two fixed hyperparameters (λ and k) shared across all models and prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMC-CFG consistently outperforms all three baselines on FID across every backbone, with the largest margin on Qwen-Image (33.371 vs. 35.391 for CFG-Zero*, a 2.02-point improvement) and SD3.5 (20.044 vs. 20.317). Against CFG-Zero*, SMC-CFG wins on FID, CLIP, ImageReward, HPSv2.1, and MPS, but trails slightly on Aesthetic score (e.g., 5.5790 vs. 5.6124 on SD3.5) and PickScore on Qwen-Image (0.2275 vs. 0.2279). Against Rectified-CFG++, SMC-CFG dominates on nearly all metrics, with particularly large gaps on ImageReward (e.g., 0.9486 vs. 0.7097 on SD3.5) and MPS (7.5719 vs. 6.7854). Against vanilla CFG, SMC-CFG improves FID by 1.4–2.1 points and CLIP by 0.001–0.004 while boosting human-preference metrics (ImageReward, HPSv2.1, MPS) by 5–10% relative. The only metric where SMC-CFG does not achieve the top score is Aesthetic on SD3.5 and Qwen-Image, where CFG-Zero* edges it by ~0.03–0.04 points, suggesting a minor trade-off between perceptual sharpness and overall generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) through the lens of control theory, showing that standard CFG is mathematically equivalent to a proportional (P) controller acting on the conditional-unconditional velocity discrepancy. It unifies a range of existing guidance variants—weight schedulers, Adaptive Projected Guidance, CFG-Zero*, and Rectified-CFG++—as specific instances of a general state-feedback law parameterized by a gain schedule and a direction operator. Departing from the linear feedback assumption shared by all prior methods, the paper introduces Sliding Mode Control (SMC) from robust control theory, adding a nonlinear switching term that enforces convergence onto a predefined sliding manifold, with a Lyapunov-based finite-time convergence guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CFG-Ctrl framework provides a principled, unified vocabulary for understanding and designing guidance strategies, which could accelerate future method development by making the role of each component (gain, direction, nonlinearity) explicit. The SMC-CFG mechanism offers a drop-in improvement that is model-agnostic and robust across guidance scales, addressing a persistent practical pain point (oversaturation and instability at high w). However, the empirical gains over strong baselines like CFG-Zero* are modest (e.g., ~0.3 FID on SD3.5), the evaluation is limited to a 5K MS-COCO subset and T2I only, and the SMC hyperparameters (λ, k) are fixed per model rather than adaptive. Open questions include whether the control-theoretic perspective generalizes to video, 3D, or other generative modalities, and whether the sliding surface can be made data-adaptive to reduce the need for per-model tuning.
