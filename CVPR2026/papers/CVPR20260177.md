## Problem

Current multi-view indoor 3D object detectors depend on sensor-derived geometric inputs—precisely calibrated multi-view camera poses or depth maps—that are expensive to obtain and often inaccessible in real-world deployment. This paper introduces the Sensor-Geometry-Free (SG-Free) setting, which removes all such geometric inputs, and presents the first transformer-based framework explicitly designed for this challenging regime.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260177/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of unposed multi-view RGB images (typically 40–80 frames per scene) with no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or depth maps provided. It outputs K=256 object queries, each yielding a 7-parameter 3D bounding box (center, dimensions, orientation) and a class label over 18 (ScanNet) or 17 (ARKitScenes) categories. The VGGT encoder is frozen throughout training, so all learnable parameters reside in the decoder and the two proposed modules.

## Method

VGGT-Det builds an encoder–decoder transformer on top of a frozen VGGT encoder that extracts 3D-aware tokens from all views, concatenated into a unified token sequence. Two key modules mine VGGT's internal priors: (i) Attention-Guided Query Generation (AG) replaces plain farthest-point sampling with a fused priority that combines normalized VGGT attention weights (semantic prior) and normalized minimum-distance to already-sampled points (spatial dispersion), steering object queries toward object regions while preserving global coverage; (ii) Query-Driven Feature Aggregation (QD) introduces a learnable See-Query token that is concatenated with object queries, interacts with them via self-attention to 'see' their needs, and then produces softmax weights over four VGGT intermediate layers (4th, 11th, 17th, 23rd) to form a weighted-sum aggregated feature used as the cross-attention key/value. The decoder iterates L layers of query self-attention followed by cross-attention to the aggregated features, with the See-Query updated each layer to adaptively re-weight the multi-level geometric features. Training is supervised with standard 3DETR-style losses (classification, L1, GIoU) using AdamW and cosine annealing on 8×H800 GPUs for approximately two days.

## Results

On ScanNet, VGGT-Det achieves 46.9 mAP@0.25, surpassing the strongest baseline MVSDet (42.5) by 4.4 points and the point-cloud-based FCAF3D (40.6) by 6.3 points. On ARKitScenes, VGGT-Det reaches 28.0 mAP@0.25, outperforming MVSDet (19.4) by a substantial 8.6 points. The method also demonstrates strong efficiency: with 40 input frames it uses only 3.57 GB of memory (vs. 13.81 GB for MVSDet) while achieving 5.6 points higher mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-Det consistently outperforms all four baselines on both datasets. On ScanNet, it beats MVSDet (42.5) by 4.4 mAP@0.25, NeRF-Det (41.2) by 5.7, FCAF3D (40.6) by 6.3, and ImVoxelNet (35.2) by 11.7. On ARKitScenes, the margin over MVSDet widens to 8.6 points (28.0 vs. 19.4), and VGGT-Det surpasses NeRF-Det by 9.9 and ImVoxelNet by 15.6. Notably, the gains are most pronounced on smaller or harder categories (e.g., chair: 61.9 vs. 55.5 for MVSDet; table: 50.3 vs. 43.2), suggesting the attention-guided query generation and query-driven feature aggregation particularly benefit objects that are harder to localize without explicit sensor geometry. All baselines were retrained with VGGT-predicted poses or point clouds to ensure a fair SG-Free comparison, yet VGGT-Det's integrated use of VGGT's internal priors still yields clear advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VGGT feed-forward 3D reconstruction model and the DETR/3DETR transformer detection paradigm, but departs from prior multi-view indoor 3D detectors (ImVoxelNet, NeRF-Det, MVSDet) that require precisely calibrated camera poses or depth maps. Rather than treating VGGT as a black-box geometry provider, VGGT-Det integrates the VGGT encoder directly into a detection pipeline and mines its internal representations—attention maps for semantic query initialization (AG) and multi-level intermediate features for geometric aggregation (QD). The approach is conceptually closest to 3DETR's non-parametric query sampling but replaces uniform FPS with attention-guided sampling and sequential feature querying with a learnable See-Query mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for sensor-derived poses and depth, VGGT-Det targets a more deployable setting for indoor 3D detection where calibration is costly or infeasible (handheld cameras, AR applications). The observation that VGGT's attention maps encode semantic structure despite the model being trained only for geometry opens a broader question about what other internal transformer representations can be repurposed for downstream tasks. Limitations include reliance on a frozen VGGT encoder (no joint fine-tuning), evaluation limited to two indoor datasets, and the fact that baselines are retrained with VGGT-predicted poses, which may understate their true capability. Open questions include whether the SG-Free formulation extends to outdoor or dynamic scenes, and whether unfreezing VGGT layers would yield further gains.
