## Problem

Existing video tokenizers assign a fixed number of tokens to every temporal block regardless of content complexity, wasting budget on static or repetitive segments while underserving dynamic or complex ones. Prior adaptive tokenizers (ElasticTok, AdapTok) rely on heuristic threshold searches or mini-batch ILP to select variable-length assignments, but neither approach identifies the per-video optimal assignment that maximizes the quality-cost trade-off. The paper addresses the absence of a principled, per-sample optimal assignment estimation method for video tokenization.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 16×128×128 video clips, which are spatio-temporally patchified (8× spatial, 4× temporal downsample) into 4×16×16 3D embeddings, then divided into T=4 causal temporal blocks. Each block is assigned a variable number of 1D tokens from the candidate set {32, 64, 128, 256, 512}, yielding 625 possible assignments per video. For downstream AR generation, the model takes a class label (UCF-101) or 8 conditioning frames padded from 5 (K600) and autoregressively produces a variable-length token sequence (averaging ~756 tokens vs. 1024 for fixed-length baselines) that is decoded back into video frames.

## Method

EVATok is a four-stage framework centered on a novel proxy reward metric R = w_q·Q − w_l·L that quantifies the quality-cost trade-off for a given token assignment. Stage 1 trains a Q-Former-style 1D proxy tokenizer to reconstruct videos under randomly sampled assignments; Stage 2 brute-forces all 625 candidate assignments per video using this proxy reward to curate 100k (video, optimal-assignment) pairs; Stage 3 trains a lightweight 19.9M ViT-S router to predict the optimal assignment in a single forward pass via classification; Stage 4 trains the final adaptive tokenizer from scratch conditioned on router-predicted assignments, closing the training-inference gap of prior adaptive tokenizers. The final tokenizer is further enhanced with V-JEPA2 representation alignment (cosine similarity between decoder features and frozen semantic features) and a VideoMAE-based semantic discriminator, both of which improve reconstruction FVD and downstream generation quality beyond what the adaptive assignment alone provides.

## Results

On UCF-101 class-to-video generation, EVATok (633M generator) achieves a gFVD of 4.0 with 756 tokens (−26.2%), outperforming LARP (gFVD 5.7, 1024 tokens) and AdapTok (gFVD 67, 1024 tokens). On UCF-101 reconstruction, EVATok achieves rFVD 9.7 with 774 tokens (−24.4%), compared to LARP's rFVD 20 and AdapTok's rFVD 36 at 1024 tokens. On K600 frame prediction, EVATok reaches gFVD 4.0 with 862 generated tokens (−15.8%), beating LARP (gFVD 5.1) and AdapTok (gFVD 11) at 1024 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among AR video generative models, EVATok dominates all direct competitors: it beats LARP (the prior SOTA) by 16% on UCF class-to-video gFVD (48 vs. 57) while using 26.2% fewer tokens, and by 20% on K600 frame prediction gFVD (4.0 vs. 5.1) with 15.8% fewer tokens. Against AdapTok, EVATok improves UCF gFVD from 67 to 48 and K600 gFVD from 11 to 4.0, while also reducing reconstruction rFVD from 36 to 9.7. Compared to the larger MAGVIT-v2-AR (840M generator, 1280 tokens), EVATok achieves better UCF gFVD (48 vs. 109) and K600 gFVD (4.0 vs. 58) with a smaller model and fewer tokens. Against diffusion-based baselines (VideoFusion gFVD 173, HPDM gFVD 66 on K600), EVATok's AR approach is substantially more efficient, though W.A.L.T-L (diffusion, gFVD 3.3 on K600) remains competitive on that specific benchmark. The key differentiator is that EVATok is the only method achieving SOTA quality *and* the lowest token count simultaneously across all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

EVATok extends the adaptive video tokenization line initiated by ElasticTok (threshold-based tail-token-dropping) and AdapTok (ILP-based minibatch assignment), both of which use heuristic or constrained-optimization methods to select token budgets. The key departure is formulating optimal assignment as a maximum proxy reward problem and replacing expensive search with a lightweight learned router, eliminating the training-inference gap that plagues prior variable-length tokenizers. The training recipe also extends image-domain representation alignment (V-JEPA2) and semantic discriminators (VideoMAE) to video tokenizers, building on recent work showing these techniques benefit image tokenization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that adaptive-length AR video generation can simultaneously improve generation quality and reduce token cost, challenging the conventional quality-efficiency trade-off assumption. The router-based approach is practical and generalizes across datasets, making it a viable component for future video generation pipelines. However, the work is limited to 16-frame clips and class-to-video generation; open questions include whether the proxy reward formulation and router generalize to longer videos, text-conditioned generation, and whether the gains persist at larger scales. The identification of the training-inference gap in prior adaptive tokenizers is a useful diagnostic that may inform other variable-length compression settings.
