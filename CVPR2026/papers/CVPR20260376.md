## Problem

Document images contain large non-informative backgrounds and only sparse, localized supporting evidence, yet existing token-reduction methods designed for natural images or videos fail to exploit this structural sparsity and rely on fixed heuristic pruning layers that ignore how model comprehension evolves across depths. This leads to substantial wasted computation on long documents without a principled way to determine when and where to prune.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260376/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within a two-stage retrieval-augmented pipeline: ColPali retrieves top-K document pages (1, 2, or 4) from a large collection given a question, and Qwen2-VL (7B) generates a free-form answer from the retrieved pages. Document pages are split into non-overlapping 32×32 pixel patches before encoding, and the pruning stages progressively reduce the token count from the full set (e.g., 2,508 tokens per page) down to as few as 7% before the LLM decoder processes them.

## Method

DOCPRUNE is a training-free, three-stage progressive token pruning framework. Background Token Pruning (BTP) detects the dominant pixel intensity per page and discards patches where a high fraction of pixels match that background value, removing ~36–60% of tokens before encoding. Question-aware Token Pruning (QTP) computes cosine similarity between pre-computed document token embeddings and question token embeddings from the retrieval stage, applies Gaussian smoothing to broaden relevant regions, and retains only tokens exceeding a relevance threshold. Comprehension-aware Token Pruning (CTP) monitors the L2 norm of the last token's hidden state at each decoder layer; once this norm exceeds a comprehension threshold, tokens with attention weights below a second threshold are dropped, adaptively selecting the pruning layer based on the model's actual understanding rather than a fixed heuristic.

## Results

On M3DocRAG (M3DocVQA) with 4 retrieved pages, DocPrune reduces encoder TFLOPs from 59.28 to 16.36 (−72%) and decoder TFLOPs from 86.27 to 25.45 (−70%), achieving 3.0× and 3.3× throughput gains in the encoder and decoder respectively. Despite this aggressive compression, DocPrune improves EM from 31.5 to 33.0 (+1.5) and F1 from 36.3 to 37.3 (+1.0) over the unpruned Qwen2-VL (7B) baseline. On MMLongBench-Doc (4 pages, Qwen2.5-VL), DocPrune similarly cuts TFLOPs by ~60% while improving ACC by +0.7 and F1 by +0.3. On VDocRAG benchmarks (ChartQA, SlideVQA, InfoVQA, DUDE), DocPrune yields an average 2.4× speedup with competitive or slightly improved accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocPrune outperforms all three baselines on both accuracy and efficiency across every page setting. Against FastV (ECCV 2024), which only prunes decoder tokens at a fixed layer, DocPrune achieves +3.9 F1 at 4 pages (37.3 vs. 33.4) while also reducing encoder TFLOPs by 72% (16.36 vs. 59.28) — something FastV does not address. Against DivPrune (CVPR 2025), DocPrune gains +1.8 F1 at 4 pages (37.3 vs. 35.5) and dramatically improves decoder throughput (2.0 vs. 0.4 samples/s), since DivPrune's iterative diversity-based selection introduces overhead that actually degrades speed at high token counts. Against VTW (AAAI 2025), which withdraws all visual tokens at a fixed layer, DocPrune achieves a large +12.6 F1 margin at 4 pages (37.3 vs. 24.7), showing that VTW's aggressive one-shot withdrawal severely damages fine-grained document understanding. Notably, DocPrune is the only method that simultaneously reduces computation in both the encoder and decoder while surpassing the unpruned baseline on accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the general token-pruning paradigm for VLMs (FastV, DivPrune, VTW) by identifying document-specific structural sparsity that prior methods ignore: large non-informative backgrounds and the extreme sparsity of question-relevant content. It builds on the retrieval-augmented document understanding pipeline (M3DocRAG, VDocRAG) and leverages pre-computed retrieval embeddings for question-aware filtering, but departs from prior work by introducing a comprehension-aware criterion (L2 norm of the output token) to adaptively select pruning layers rather than relying on fixed heuristics. The three-stage progressive design (background → question → comprehension) is novel in its exploitation of both document layout structure and the model's internal state simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that training-free token pruning can simultaneously improve accuracy (+1.0 F1) and throughput (3×) challenges the common assumption that pruning necessarily degrades performance, suggesting that removing distracting background and irrelevant tokens sharpens attention on true evidence. This has direct practical impact for deploying document QA systems where long multi-page documents make inference prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the L2-norm comprehension criterion generalizes to substantially different architectures or much longer contexts, whether the Gaussian smoothing and threshold hyperparameters remain stable across document domains, and whether the approach extends to tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning over the full page layout.
