## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (VGGT, DUSt3R, π³) are built on pinhole projection assumptions and generalize poorly to panoramic imagery, where spherical distortions, non-uniform sampling density, and the absence of a canonical reference frame break standard geometric reasoning. End-to-end panoramic reconstruction that jointly infers pose, depth, and coherent 3D structure in a single forward pass remains largely unexplored, compounded by the scarcity of large-scale panoramic datasets with complete multi-view pose and depth annotations.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an unordered set of N equirectangular RGB panoramas (N ∈ [2, 24] during training; 4096×2048 native resolution in PanoCity, resized to 336×672 for training and 512×1024 for evaluation) and jointly outputs per-view SE(3) camera-to-world poses, per-pixel Euclidean depth maps, and both camera-frame (local) and world-frame (global) 3D point clouds. The architecture is strictly permutation-equivariant: reordering the input set permutes the outputs without changing their content, and the global coordinate frame is resolved at inference via a randomly selected anchor view.

## Method

PanoVGGT extends the encode–aggregate–decode Transformer paradigm to the spherical domain. A DINOv2 backbone extracts patch tokens, which are augmented with spherical-aware positional embeddings (sin/cos of azimuth and elevation) that preserve wrap-around continuity at the panorama seam. A Geometry Aggregator of L alternating-attention blocks (intra-view self-attention followed by global cross-view self-attention) reasons over local distortion and cross-view correspondence without any view-ordering cue. Three parallel heads decode camera poses, local point clouds (via spherical ray model on predicted depth), and global point clouds, with the explicit global-point regression providing over-complete supervision. Training employs a panorama-specific three-axis SO(3) rotation augmentation applied jointly to RGB, depth, and poses—decoupling fixed grid positions from dynamically rotated content to force the network to learn distortion-invariant geometry—and a stochastic anchoring strategy that randomly re-centers the coordinate frame each iteration to eliminate ordering bias while preserving permutation equivariance. The multi-task loss combines scale-consistent L1 geometry losses (local and global points), a normal-consistency regularizer, and relative pose supervision (angular rotation error plus scale-aligned L1 translation error).

## Results

On camera pose estimation (Table 2), PanoVGGT achieves AUC@30 of 0.459 on Matterport3D, 0.556 on Stanford2D3D, and 0.949 on PanoCity, with mean rotation errors of 21.4°, 18.8°, and 0.87° respectively—dramatically outperforming perspective-based VGGT (AUC 0.034/0.047/0.205) and π³ (AUC 0.047/0.076/0.571). For monocular depth (Table 3), PanoVGGT attains Abs Rel of 0.0884 (Matterport3D), 0.0711 (Stanford2D3D), and 0.0312 (PanoCity), with multi-view inputs further improving to 0.0840, 0.0778, and 0.0196. On zero-shot Pano3D (Table 4), PanoVGGT achieves Abs Rel 0.0869 and δ₁ 0.9223 under scale-only alignment, surpassing all pinhole-based and panoramic baselines. For point cloud reconstruction on PanoCity (Table 5), PanoVGGT reduces overall Chamfer distance to 0.737 (mean) versus 1.074 for the retrained π³* and 6.570 for original π³.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoVGGT decisively outperforms all perspective-based methods (VGGT, π³, MoGe, MoGe-2) on camera pose estimation, with AUC@30 improvements of 10–100× over their direct panoramic application, confirming that pinhole assumptions severely limit 360° geometry recovery. Against the retrained panoramic variant π³*, PanoVGGT still leads on AUC@30 (0.459 vs. 0.305 on Matterport3D; 0.949 vs. 0.682 on PanoCity) and on translation error (18.9° vs. 31.6° on Matterport3D), though π³* achieves a slightly lower rotation error on PanoCity (0.53° vs. 0.87°). For monocular depth, PanoVGGT beats all panoramic-specific baselines (UniFuse, HoHoNet, EGFormer, Elite360D, PanoFormer, OmniFusion, BiFuse++) on Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D in both Abs Rel and δ<1.25, but on PanoCity it trades a small δ<1.25 deficit (0.9812 vs. 0.9885 for HoHoNet) for the best Abs Rel (0.0196 vs. 0.0200 for BiFuse++). In zero-shot Pano3D evaluation, PanoVGGT achieves the lowest Abs Rel (0.0869 scale-only; 0.0833 scale+shift) and highest δ₁/δ₂, outperforming both pinhole methods (VGGT†, π³†, MoGe†, MoGe-2†) and panoramic methods (PanDA, Unik3D), though Unik3D and π³† achieve lower RMSE under scale+shift alignment. For point cloud reconstruction, PanoVGGT reduces overall Chamfer distance by ~31% over π³* (0.737 vs. 1.074) and by ~89% over original π³ (0.737 vs. 6.570), with the largest gap in completion (0.705 vs. 11.737 for π³), indicating far fewer missing geometric structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanoVGGT directly extends the feed-forward Transformer paradigm established by VGGT and π³—both of which jointly predict pose, depth, and 3D structure from unordered perspective images in a single forward pass—to the fundamentally different geometry of equirectangular panoramas. The key architectural departures are spherical-aware sin-cos positional embeddings (replacing standard ViT embeddings that break at the panorama seam) and a three-axis SO(3) rotation augmentation that exploits the natural rotational symmetry of spherical imagery. Unlike prior panoramic methods (BiFuse, PanoFormer, EGFormer, etc.) that address isolated sub-tasks such as monocular depth or relative pose, PanoVGGT unifies all three outputs under a single permutation-equivariant model, and introduces stochastic anchoring to resolve the global-frame ambiguity inherent in the unordered-input setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first unified feed-forward pipeline that jointly recovers camera poses, dense metric depth, and globally consistent point clouds from unordered panoramas, removing the need for multi-stage SfM/MVS optimization or tiling panoramas into pinhole crops. The PanoCity dataset (120K+ outdoor frames with synchronized 6DoF pose and 16-bit depth) addresses a critical data bottleneck that has limited progress in panoramic 3D reconstruction. Limitations include reliance on synthetic UE5 data, restriction to equirectangular projections, and acknowledged sensitivity of pose estimation to extreme domain shifts; open questions include whether the SO(3) augmentation and spherical embeddings transfer to other projection types (cubemap, fisheye) and whether real-world deployment on consumer 360° cameras closes the synthetic-to-real gap.
