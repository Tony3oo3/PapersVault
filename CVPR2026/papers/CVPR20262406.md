## Problem

The checkerboard pattern, the gold standard for frame-based camera calibration, has never been applied to event cameras because two fundamental issues prevent direct use: asynchronous events accumulate into blurred edges on a static image plane, and events are theoretically absent at corner intersections due to locally symmetric intensity gradients. Existing event-camera calibration work either relies on lossy grayscale reconstruction, specialized blinking-LED hardware, or circle-grid patterns that are sensitive to lens distortion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw asynchronous event stream (pixel location, timestamp, polarity) from event cameras such as DAVIS346 (346×260), Prophesee Gen 3, and Samsung Gen 3, segmented into coherent motion windows. It produces per-window sub-pixel 2D corner coordinates and, for AprilTag, a unique tag ID with orientation; these are jointly fed into a batch reprojection-error minimization to yield the intrinsic matrix K and distortion coefficients d. No grayscale frames or learned reconstruction are required at any stage.

## Method

The paper first derives a closed-form Event Rate (ER) model showing that ER vanishes exactly at checkerboard corners (Eq. 11) while remaining strictly positive along edges (Eq. 12), providing the theoretical basis for the entire pipeline. Events are warped to a common reference time using a constant motion vector estimated by maximizing IWE variance, producing a sharp Image of Warped Events (IWE) and a polarity-aware variant (pIWE). Corner initialization exploits the edge–corner ER contrast: seed pixels on high-density edges are propagated inward until four clusters merge, two lines are fitted to odd/even clusters, and their intersection gives the initial corner. Sub-pixel refinement then runs gradient descent on the IWE surface toward the local minimum of event density, constrained to narrow bands around the two fitted lines. The framework extends to AprilTag by fitting quads from detected corners, binarizing the warped pIWE patch via gradient projection and Otsu thresholding, and decoding the tag ID through Hamming-distance matching against the AprilTag codebook.

## Results

On three checkerboard patterns (5×6, 6×8, 7×10), the proposed method achieves calibration RMSE of 0.487, 0.516, and 0.503 pixels respectively, consistently outperforming E2Calib (0.747, 0.647, 0.559) while remaining below the frame-based pseudo ground-truth (0.199, 0.192, 0.153). Corner localization RMSE is 0.85, 0.84, and 0.78 pixels for the three boards, compared to 1.40, 1.04, and 0.78 for E2Calib. The proposed method also exhibits markedly lower standard deviations on all intrinsic and distortion parameters, indicating more stable and repeatable calibration across 50 random sampling trials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is E2Calib [35], which reconstructs grayscale images via E2VID and then applies conventional frame-based corner detection. The proposed method wins on calibration RMSE across all three checkerboard sizes: 0.487 vs. 0.747 (5×6), 0.516 vs. 0.647 (6×8), and 0.503 vs. 0.559 (7×10) pixels. On corner localization RMSE, the advantage is most pronounced on the 5×6 board (0.85 vs. 1.40 px, a ~40% reduction) and 6×8 board (0.84 vs. 1.04 px), while the two methods essentially tie on the 7×10 board (0.78 vs. 0.78 px). The proposed method also shows substantially lower standard deviations on every intrinsic and distortion parameter (e.g., f_x std of 1.60 vs. 3.00 on 5×6), indicating more repeatable calibration. Both event-based methods remain above the frame-based pseudo ground-truth (RMSE ≈ 0.15–0.20 px), which is expected since the DAVIS grayscale frames provide direct pixel-level access to the checkerboard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Image of Warped Events (IWE) representation from Gallego et al. [11,12] for motion-compensated event alignment, and on the event-rate-to-gradient relationship established by Lin et al. [32], combining them with the classical checkerboard corner intensity model of Krüger et al. [29] to derive a first-principles analysis of event generation at corner intersections. It departs from the dominant prior approach of converting event streams to grayscale images via learned reconstruction (E2VID/E2Calib [35,39]) and from circle-grid calibration methods [6,23,41] that avoid corners altogether, instead showing that the checkerboard's geometrically well-defined corners can be localized directly in the event domain by exploiting the theoretical absence of events at the corner point itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reintroducing the standard checkerboard pattern for event cameras without reconstruction artifacts, this work removes a practical barrier to deploying event cameras in calibration-sensitive pipelines (robotics, AR/VR, multi-sensor fusion) where checkerboard-based calibration is the de facto standard. The extension to AprilTag detection under partial visibility broadens applicability to fiducial-marker-based tracking. A key limitation is the assumption of a single dominant global motion vector per window, which restricts the method to controlled calibration scenarios; incorporating pixel-wise or more general motion models remains open. The framework's reliance on OpenCV's calibrateCamera for the final optimization also means it inherits that solver's assumptions about distortion models and pose distribution.
