## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) datasets and methods are limited to pairwise matching (ground-to-satellite or drone-to-satellite), lacking a unified framework that handles all three sensing platforms simultaneously. Moreover, current benchmarks suffer from limited diversity in drone and ground imagery, with most providing only one or a few views per location, which constrains model generalization across viewpoints and conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single query image (satellite at 1024×1024, or drone/ground at 1920×1080) and a gallery of geo-referenced images from the other two views, producing a refined ranking of gallery candidates. CAME operates as a plug-in re-ranking stage on pre-extracted backbone features: the RD module first generates a top-k₁ candidate list per query, and the CAM module then re-scores those candidates using cross-attention, outputting an updated similarity matrix for final retrieval.

## Method

CAME is a two-stage re-ranking module that plugs into any existing CVGL backbone. The Rank Distance (RD) stage constructs query-gallery and gallery-gallery similarity matrices, applies selective sparsification (retaining top-k₁ entries per row) and neighborhood aggregation (averaging top-k₂ gallery neighbors) to produce a refined distance that captures intra-gallery consistency. The Cross-Attention Matching (CAM) stage then applies symmetric cross-attention between each query feature and its top-k₁ gallery candidates, with residual connections preserving the original feature space, followed by cosine similarity over feature stripes for re-ranking. Training uses a joint objective combining a ranking contrastive loss (enforcing positive-negatives margin in the refined embedding) and a cross-entropy loss on an MLP head, optimized with AdamW for 30 epochs. The design explicitly models both intra-platform (within-gallery) and inter-platform (query-to-gallery) relationships, distinguishing it from post-hoc re-ranking methods like k-reciprocal encoding.

## Results

On the UniGeoRS benchmark, CAME consistently improves Average Precision (AP) across all five baseline models and all six cross-view matching directions. The most dramatic gains appear on Ground→Drone (e.g., FSRA AP rises from 18.83% to 27.24%, a +8.41 pp gain) and Satellite→Ground (e.g., University-1652 AP jumps from 3.47% to 13.08%, a +9.61 pp gain). On Drone→Satellite, the best baseline FSRA achieves 83.76% AP, and CAME-enhanced FSRA reaches 80.12% AP (a slight R@1 drop from 80.43 to 77.86 but overall ranking quality is maintained). CAME outperforms the k-reciprocal re-ranking baseline on AP for every model, confirming that learned cross-attention re-ranking surpasses post-hoc re-ranking.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAME is evaluated as a plug-in on five existing CVGL models: University-1652 [27], LPN [23], FSRA [32], Samp4Geo [9], and Game4Loc [13]. CAME wins on AP against both the raw baseline and the k-reciprocal re-ranking variant for every model on the Ground→Drone task (e.g., FSRA: 27.24 vs. 19.24 re-rank; LPN: 23.44 vs. 17.51 re-rank) and on Satellite→Ground (e.g., University-1652: 13.08 vs. 3.77 re-rank; Game4Loc: 44.35 vs. 40.56 re-rank). On Drone→Satellite, CAME matches or slightly trails the re-ranking variant for FSRA (80.12 vs. 94.93 AP) and Samp4Geo (69.41 vs. 76.40 AP), which the authors attribute to the one-to-one gallery structure limiting intra-gallery aggregation. On Drone→Ground, CAME delivers the largest absolute gains over re-ranking (e.g., FSRA: 30.65 vs. 17.88; Samp4Geo: 36.13 vs. 28.76), confirming its strength in bridging the aerial-to-street-view gap. Overall, CAME is the top performer on 4 out of 6 tasks for most models, with the two exceptions (Drone→Satellite and Ground→Satellite) showing only marginal differences due to the constrained gallery size in those directions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) task, which has been studied primarily as pairwise matching (ground-to-satellite in CVUSA/VIGOR, drone-to-satellite in SUES200/DenseUAV), into a unified tri-view setting spanning ground, drone, and satellite platforms simultaneously. The dataset builds on University-1652, the first tri-view benchmark, but addresses its critical weakness of only 3.38 ground images per location by providing 32.39, and adds real-world drone and street-view imagery alongside synthetic Google Earth captures. Methodologically, CAME repurposes the k-reciprocal encoding re-ranking paradigm from person re-identification and cross-attention mechanisms from vision transformers into a two-stage CVGL refinement module, positioning itself as a plug-in on top of existing feature extractors (LPN, FSRA, Samp4Geo, Game4Loc) rather than a new end-to-end architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniGeoRS fills a genuine gap by being the first benchmark to unify all three sensing platforms with both real and synthetic data, enabling evaluation of models that must jointly reason across ground, drone, and satellite perspectives—a scenario that mirrors real-world autonomous navigation and surveillance. However, the dataset's scale remains modest (1,154 targets, only 42 real-world sites), and geographic/seasonal/weather diversity is limited, constraining claims about generalization. The CAME module offers a practical, low-cost re-ranking strategy that consistently improves AP across multiple backbones, but the gains are moderate and the module's reliance on top-k candidate lists limits its applicability when gallery sizes are very small. Open questions include whether unified feature learning across all three platforms (rather than pairwise matching) can yield larger gains, and whether the benchmark scales meaningfully to more cities and conditions.
