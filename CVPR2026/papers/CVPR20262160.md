## Problem

CLIP's contrastive pretraining optimizes for global image-text alignment, producing attention maps whose patch-level representations are insufficient for pixel-wise segmentation, especially on remote sensing imagery with extreme scale variation and heterogeneous landscapes. Existing training-free methods treat the attention map as a monolithic entity (e.g., re-computing QK or QK^T), failing to decompose and independently control the distinct information flows between patch tokens and the [CLS] token.

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing RGB image (resized to 448×448, processed via 224×224 sliding windows) and a natural-language category prompt (averaged over 80 OpenAI-style templates) as input, and produces a pixel-wise segmentation mask at the original resolution after SimFeatUp upsampling. No gradient updates or task-specific training are required; all modifications operate on the frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP encoder at inference time.

## Method

ReAttnCLIP decomposes the final-layer attention matrix into three interpretable sub-blocks and redefines each: (1) the patch-to-patch block is replaced by a rotation-augmented raw embedding similarity S = α·S_rot + Σ β_l·A^(l), where S_rot aggregates XX^T computed from intermediate layers (9–11) across four 90° rotations, and A^(l) are intermediate Query-Key attention maps; (2) the [CLS]→patch row is reconstructed by averaging the first-row attention vectors across selected intermediate layers (6–9), yielding a richer global representation than the final-layer [CLS] alone; (3) the patch→[CLS] column is zeroed to eliminate residual global bias on local features. The resulting redefined matrix A_refine = [[A̅_00, A̅_0,1:N], [0, S]] is applied to the value matrix in the last transformer block, after which standard CLIP text-similarity scoring and SimFeatUp upsampling produce the final mask.

## Results

On open-vocabulary remote sensing segmentation, ReAttnCLIP achieves a mean mIoU of 40.9 across eight datasets, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art SegEarth-OV (39.2) by 1.7 points on average. The largest per-dataset gains are on UDD5 (53.7 vs. 50.6, +3.1 mIoU) and VDD (49.7 vs. 45.3, +4.4 mIoU). On building and road extraction, the method reaches 21.1 average mIoU versus 20.0 for SegEarth-OV. As a plug-and-play module, it boosts MaskCLIP by +9.5 mIoU and ClearCLIP by +9.0 mIoU on remote sensing benchmarks, and by up to +9.7 on natural image datasets (COCO-Stuff, ADE20K, Cityscapes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAttnCLIP outperforms all seven baselines on every one of the eight open-vocabulary segmentation datasets. Against the closest competitor SegEarth-OV (CVPR'25), it gains +1.7 mIoU on average, with the most substantial improvements on UDD5 (+3.1) and VDD (+4.4), while the gain on LoveDA is marginal (+0.1) due to blurry imagery and small objects. Earlier methods show a clear performance hierarchy: ClearCLIP (33.4) and ResCLIP (32.6) lead among ECCV/CVPR 2024–25 methods, followed by GEM (32.3) and SCLIP (31.1), with MaskCLIP (27.2) and raw CLIP (11.4) trailing significantly. On building/road extraction (Table 2), ReAttnCLIP leads SegEarth-OV by +1.3 mIoU on WHU^SAT.II and +0.9 on Massachusetts. The plug-and-play experiments (Table 7) confirm that the attention-refinement module transfers effectively: it adds +9.5 mIoU to MaskCLIP, +4.5 to SCLIP, and +9.0 to ClearCLIP on remote sensing data, and up to +9.7 on natural image benchmarks (Table 8), indicating the gains are not domain-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free CLIP adaptation lineage for dense prediction (MaskCLIP → SCLIP → ClearCLIP → ResCLIP → SegEarth-OV) by introducing a systematic three-way decomposition of the final-layer attention map into patch-to-patch, CLS-to-patch, and patch-to-CLS components, each modified independently. It builds directly on SegEarth-OV's [CLS] subtraction and SimFeatUp upsampling, but replaces the single-layer [CLS] subtraction with a multi-layer reconstructed global representation and substitutes learned QK projections with raw patch-embedding similarity (XX^T) augmented by rotational invariance. The conceptual departure from prior work is the component-wise analysis rather than treating the attention map as a monolithic entity, and the use of intermediate-layer features for both patch similarity and CLS reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration (up to 9.5% mIoU gain when bolted onto MaskCLIP, SCLIP, or ClearCLIP) suggests the attention-decomposition strategy is a broadly applicable refinement rather than a monolithic pipeline, which could accelerate adoption across the training-free segmentation community. However, the method's 60.2 GFLOPs and 52.3 ms latency are roughly 2× those of SegEarth-OV, and the modest 1.7% average gain over the strongest baseline limits its practical urgency. Open questions include whether the rotation augmentation and multi-layer fusion generalize to other ViT backbones or non-remote-sensing domains, and whether the domain gap (evident in the limited LoveDA improvement) can be addressed without task-specific fine-tuning.
