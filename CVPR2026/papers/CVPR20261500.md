## Problem

Egocentric 3D body pose estimation for AR/VR remains challenging due to limited body coverage, frequent self-occlusions, and scarce labeled data. Prior transformer-based methods (e.g., EgoPoseFormer) use per-joint query tokens that scale linearly with the number of keypoints, lack explicit temporal modeling, and are not fully end-to-end differentiable, resulting in jittery and inaccurate motion estimates that generalize poorly to in-the-wild scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261500/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view egocentric images (4 monochrome global-shutter cameras on a VR headset) together with the 6DoF headset pose at each timestamp. It outputs 3D body keypoints in world space, parametric body representations (joint rotations, body scales, and head-to-headset 6-DoF transform via the Momentum body model), and per-joint 3D covariance uncertainty. The architecture supports both keypoint and parametric output heads under a constant compute budget, decoupling model cost from the choice of body representation.

## Method

EPFv2 replaces EgoPoseFormer's per-joint query tokens with a single holistic pose query initialized from auxiliary metadata (headset pose, user identity), making computation independent of the number of predicted joints. Two architecturally identical transformer decoder blocks perform coarse-to-fine estimation with full gradient flow: the first predicts an initial 3D pose, and the second refines it by projecting coarse 3D keypoints onto image planes and using those 2D positions as spatial conditioning for standard multi-head cross-attention (replacing hardware-unfriendly deformable attention). Causal temporal attention with RoPE positional encoding enables each frame to attend to its temporal history, producing smooth motion even for occluded body parts, with KV-Cache at inference for efficiency. Per-keypoint uncertainty is modeled via a 3D Student-t distribution (extending LUVLi), and an auto-labeling system uses a DINOv3-initialized teacher to generate pseudo-labels on 70M unlabeled in-the-wild frames, training the student with uncertainty distillation to down-weight unreliable pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the EgoBody3M benchmark, EPFv2 with the auto-labeling system achieves an overall MPJPE of 4.02 cm and MPJVE of 0.42 cm, outperforming EgoPoseFormer (4.75 cm / 0.87 cm) by 15.4% and 51.7% respectively, and EgoBody3M (5.18 cm / 0.54 cm) by 22.4% and 22.2%. For the particularly challenging wrist joints, EPFv2 reduces MPJPE to 4.99 cm versus 6.01 cm (EgoPoseFormer) and 6.14 cm (EgoBody3M). The full model runs at 0.8 ms GPU latency, making it suitable for real-time VR deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EPFv2 outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against EgoPoseFormer (the closest prior method), EPFv2 reduces overall MPJPE by 15.4% (4.02 vs 4.75 cm) and dramatically cuts temporal jitter (MPJVE) by 51.7% (0.42 vs 0.87 cm), demonstrating the benefit of causal temporal attention. Against EgoBody3M's LSTM-based model, the gains are 22.4% in MPJPE and 22.2% in MPJVE. The largest relative improvement is over UnrealEgo, which lacks any temporal modeling and uses single-frame heatmap regression, yielding an 84.3% higher MPJPE and 202.4% higher MPJVE. On the wrist-specific metric—where occlusions and rapid hand motion are most problematic—EPFv2 achieves 4.99 cm, beating EgoPoseFormer by 1.02 cm and EgoBody3M by 1.15 cm. The auto-labeling system contributes an additional 13.1% wrist MPJPE reduction on top of the architectural improvements alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EPFv2 directly extends EgoPoseFormer (EPFv1) by replacing its per-joint query tokens and deformable stereo attention with a single identity-conditioned holistic query and standard projection-conditioned cross-attention, while adding causal temporal attention and full end-to-end differentiability. The auto-labeling system adapts the teacher-student pseudo-labeling paradigm (well-established in detection and segmentation) to egocentric motion estimation for the first time at scale (70M unlabeled frames), and the per-keypoint uncertainty head extends LUVLi's 2D Laplacian likelihood to 3D with a Student-t distribution. The method also inherits the pose jerk loss and headset-space regression formulation from EgoBody3M, positioning itself as a practical, deployable successor rather than a new problem formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.8 ms GPU latency and single-query design that decouples computation from body representation make EPFv2 one of the first transformer-based egocentric pose estimators genuinely suitable for real-time VR headsets, addressing a long-standing deployment gap. The auto-labeling pipeline offers a practical path to scaling egocentric pose estimation beyond the labor-intensive EgoBody3M collection process, though its generalization to substantially different capture hardware or multi-person scenes remains untested. Open questions include whether the uncertainty estimates are well-calibrated for downstream rejection or fusion, whether the approach extends to full-body tracking with hands and feet in extreme occlusion, and whether the semi-supervised gains persist when the labeled set is even smaller.
