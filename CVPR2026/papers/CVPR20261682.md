## Problem

Existing unified VLA models inherit their base model's generation paradigm—either fully autoregressive or hybrid (AR text + parallel image/action)—which introduces architectural complexity, objective misalignment between pretraining and fine-tuning, and slow action inference. Meanwhile, VLM-based VLAs lack explicit physical dynamics modeling, and world-model-based approaches lack instruction understanding and sub-task planning. The paper asks whether a single, architecturally unified parallel-decoding framework can jointly handle task planning, future image prediction, and action generation without modality-specific decoders or mixed objectives.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view robot images (padded to square, downsampled to 256×256, encoded into 256 tokens via a Show-o quantizer with an 8,192-entry codebook), task instruction text, and optionally robot proprioceptive states (normalized to [−1, 1] and quantized via a 2,048-bin tokenizer). A modal token prepended to the shared context selects the generation target: task-planning text (≤256 tokens), a 256×256 future prediction image (256 tokens), or an action chunk of size d_action × N_chunk (N_chunk=8) decoded back to continuous values. At deployment only the action modality is invoked, using a single forward pass over a fully masked block to sustain a 40 Hz control rate.

## Method

MM-ACT is an 8B-parameter bidirectional-attention Transformer (built on MMaDA) that represents text, image, and action as discrete tokens in a shared vocabulary and predicts them with a single masked-token cross-entropy objective—no modality-specific decoders or attention variants. The key architectural choice is a fully parallel decoding strategy: action generation uses one-step decoding (t=1, all tokens masked, one forward pass), while text and image use multi-step re-mask decoding with linear and cosine mask schedules respectively. Context-Shared Multimodal Learning trains all three generation tasks from an identical multimodal context (robot views + instruction + states), with a two-stage schedule: Stage 1 optimizes text and image losses to convergence, then Stage 2 introduces action supervision while retaining small text/image loss weights (≈0.05–0.1) to preserve cross-modal generative capability. This shared-context multi-task training yields a +9.25% action success-rate gain over action-only training on out-of-domain RoboTwin2.0 tasks, demonstrating that cross-modal generation acts as a regularizer for action quality.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, MM-ACT achieves an average success rate of 96.3% (with text planning co-training on Long), surpassing the strongest baseline UniVLA (95.5%) and π₀ (94.2%). On the out-of-domain RoboTwin2.0 benchmark (8 bimanual tasks), MM-ACT with joint text-image-action training reaches 52.38%, outperforming π₀ (48.13%) by 4.25% and OpenVLA-OFT (23.13%) by 29.25%. On Franka real-world tasks, MM-ACT achieves 72.0% average success rate, beating π₀ (70.0%) and OpenVLA-OFT (58.6%). The context-shared multimodal learning pipeline yields a +9.25% gain over action-only training on RoboTwin2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-ACT outperforms all nine baselines across all three evaluation settings. On LIBERO, it beats the closest competitor UniVLA by 0.8% (95.5% → 96.3%) and π₀ by 2.1%, while the gap widens substantially against VLM-based VLAs (OpenVLA by 19.8%) and visual-prediction VLAs (TraceVLA by 21.5%, CoT-VLA by 15.2%). On the out-of-domain RoboTwin2.0 benchmark, the advantage is most pronounced: MM-ACT with joint text-image-action training (52.38%) surpasses π₀ (48.13%) by 4.25% and OpenVLA-OFT (23.13%) by 29.25%, with the multimodal co-training contributing +9.25% over its own action-only variant (43.13%). On Franka real-world tasks, MM-ACT (72.0%) edges out π₀ (70.0%) by 2.0% and leads OpenVLA-OFT (58.6%) by 13.4%. Notably, the unified VLA baselines (UniVLA, WorldVLA) are the strongest competitors on LIBERO, but neither is evaluated on RoboTwin2.0 or Franka, where MM-ACT's parallel decoding architecture and context-shared training provide the clearest separation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MM-ACT builds directly on MMaDA, a multimodal discrete diffusion language model with bidirectional attention, and inherits its LLaDA text tokenizer and Show-o image quantizer. It departs from prior unified VLA approaches (e.g., UniVLA, WorldVLA) that either use fully autoregressive generation or hybrid AR-text-plus-diffusion-image/action pipelines, by applying a single masked-token parallel decoding objective uniformly across text, image, and action modalities. The Context-Shared Multimodal Learning training paradigm—jointly supervising task planning, future image prediction, and action chunk generation from an identical multimodal context—is the paper's key conceptual departure from prior VLA work that typically trains action heads in isolation or with modality-specific objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that co-training text planning and image prediction alongside action generation yields a 9.25% absolute gain on out-of-domain RoboTwin tasks suggests that cross-modal generative supervision is a practical and underexploited lever for improving robotic policies, potentially reducing the need for large amounts of action-only data. The unified single-objective training pipeline simplifies what was previously a multi-stage, multi-objective setup, and the 40Hz one-step action decoding makes the approach deployable on real hardware. Open questions include whether the cross-modal gains persist at larger scale or on more diverse task distributions, whether the one-step fully-masked action decoding is always sufficient versus iterative re-masking, and how the approach extends to longer-horizon or multi-robot settings.
