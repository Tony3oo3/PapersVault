## Problem

Current VLA models map 2D observations to actions but suffer from two critical gaps: 3D spatial understanding either requires additional sensors (point clouds, depth maps) incurring substantial computational overhead, or relies on 2D-to-3D projection that introduces geometric inconsistencies and occlusion errors; and 4D spatiotemporal reasoning is limited to future-frame prediction without alignment to instruction-grounded scenes, undermining consistency as actions unfold and the scene evolves.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three synchronized RGB views (Main, Left, Right) plus a natural-language instruction, and outputs a parallel-decoded action chunk. A key design constraint is aggressive token sparsification: CV-Aligner retains only Top-K=32 instruction-relevant tokens per view (~1/8 of original), CO-Fuser compresses geometric relations to 1/12–1/8 of original tokens, and the 4D reasoning tokens (dynamic objects + global depth) together account for less than 10% of the observation-instruction sequence at inference, enabling 2.3× speedup over OpenVLA.

## Method

ConsisVLA-4D is a three-stage pipeline. (1) CV-Aligner applies FiLM modulation of SigLIP visual tokens by the instruction embedding, selects Top-K tokens by cosine similarity, then fuses them with VGGT 3D features via N-layer cross-attention (Single-Fusion) to produce instruction-relevant object representations with cross-view identity consistency. (2) CO-Fuser performs block-by-block element-wise fusion of DINOv2 geometric features with VGGT 3D features using a cosine-decayed weight schedule, followed by Implicit Geometric Relation Aggregation (block-wise causal self-attention over concatenated geo-3D and learnable aggregation tokens) to resolve single-view spatial ambiguities. (3) CS-Thinker introduces Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention (SC-Attn), a targeted attention pattern that jointly decodes dynamic-object tokens (CoTracker-supervised) and global-depth tokens (Depth-Anything-supervised) alongside action tokens in a single parallel window; during training these auxiliary losses (L_dyn-4D + L_dep-4D) teach implicit spatiotemporal knowledge, while at inference the model skips explicit prediction and relies on the learned representations for stable action generation.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, ConsisVLA-4D achieves a 98.1% average success rate across four suites (Spatial 98.8%, Object 99.8%, Goal 98.0%, Long 95.6%), surpassing the strongest baselines OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and π₀.₅ (96.9%). On ManiSkill2, it reaches 94.3% average (vs. OpenVLA-OFT 88.7%), and on real-world bimanual tasks it attains 70.0% on Galaxea R1 Lite and 68.3% on AgileX Cobot Magic (vs. OpenVLA-OFT 51.8% and 50.3%, respectively). In terms of efficiency, ConsisVLA-4D achieves 2.3× inference speedup over OpenVLA (0.110s vs. 0.254s latency) and 1.25× over OpenVLA-OFT, while using only ~1/8 of the original visual tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsisVLA-4D outperforms all 17 compared baselines on the primary LIBERO benchmark, with the largest margins over OpenVLA (+21.6%), SpatialVLA (+20.0%), and CoT-VLA (+14.2%), and a narrower but consistent lead over the strongest competitors OpenVLA-OFT (+1.0%) and π₀.₅ (+1.2%). On ManiSkill2, it surpasses OpenVLA-OFT by 5.6% (94.3% vs. 88.7%) and CogACT by 1.8% (94.3% vs. 92.5%), while achieving a dramatic 27.0% improvement over OpenVLA (94.3% vs. 67.3%). In real-world bimanual tasks, the advantage is most pronounced: ConsisVLA-4D leads OpenVLA-OFT by 18.2% on Galaxea R1 Lite (70.0% vs. 51.8%) and by 18.0% on AgileX Cobot Magic (68.3% vs. 50.3%), with OpenVLA trailing by over 40 percentage points. On efficiency, ConsisVLA-4D (without E3D) achieves 72.7 Hz throughput vs. 3.9 Hz for OpenVLA and 58.4 Hz for OpenVLA-OFT in simulation, with 4.59T FLOPs vs. 8.48T for OpenVLA, confirming that the 3D/4D reasoning overhead is offset by the 8× token sparsification. The sparsification comparison in Table 7 further shows that at the same 1/8 compression ratio, ConsisVLA-4D's instruction-guided selection retains 98.1% LIBERO accuracy versus 88.8% (FastV) and 85.6% (SliME), demonstrating that semantic-aware filtering is critical for preserving performance under aggressive token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying 3D spatial perception and 4D spatiotemporal reasoning within a single VLA framework, departing from prior work that either relies on explicit 3D sensors (PointVLA, GeoVLA, 3D-VLA) or performs 2D-to-3D projection with inherent geometric bias (SpatialVLA, BridgeVLA, Evo-0). It repurposes three pre-trained encoders—SigLIP for semantics, DINOv2 for geometric consistency, and VGGT for 3D spatial structure—into a novel pipeline where instruction-guided FiLM filtering and cross-attention fusion reduce visual tokens to 1/8 of the original, while a custom Spatiotemporal Consistency Attention (SC-Attn) jointly decodes future dynamic objects, global depth, and action chunks in parallel. Unlike world-model-based VLAs (WorldVLA, V-JEPA 2) that predict future frames, ConsisVLA-4D reasons about spatial scene evolution through compact implicit knowledge tokens rather than explicit image generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's ability to achieve SOTA LIBERO performance (98.1%) while running 2.3× faster than OpenVLA addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for VLA models, making large-scale 3D-aware manipulation feasible on consumer-grade hardware (single RTX 5090). The consistent real-world results across two different robot platforms (±1.7%) and the strong sim-to-real transfer from RoboTwin 2.0 suggest practical viability. Open questions include whether the 4D reasoning generalizes to highly dynamic or occluded scenes beyond the tested manipulation tasks, whether the 1/8 sparsification ratio remains optimal for more complex multi-object interactions, and how the approach scales to longer-horizon tasks with more substantial scene changes.
