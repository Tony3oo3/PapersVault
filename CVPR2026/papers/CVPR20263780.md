## Problem

Existing Open-Vocabulary Camouflaged Object Segmentation (OVCOS) methods rely on unidirectional text-to-vision guidance, where textual features unilaterally enhance visual representations. This design neglects the semantic gap between CLIP's image-level textual descriptions and the pixel-level precision required for segmentation, causing severe semantic confusion in camouflage scenarios where visually similar background regions are misidentified as the target object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 384×384) and a natural-language class description as input. The text prompt is first expanded via CamoPrompts and encoded by CLIP's frozen text encoder, while the image is processed by CLIP's frozen ConvNeXt-L visual encoder. The output is a high-resolution binary segmentation mask (generated by SAM's Mask Decoder) paired with a predicted class label (obtained via CLIP's zero-shot classification on the segmented region).

## Method

BaCLIP introduces a bidirectional semantic alignment framework centered on the Mutual Refinement and Enhancement Module (MREM), which performs multi-head cross-attention in both directions: visual features attend to textual keys/values (Q_v, K_t, V_t) and textual features attend to visual keys/values (Q_t, K_v, V_v), enabling mutual semantic calibration. A Camo Clue Extractor (CCE) then refines the aligned visual features using three cascaded Multi-Frequency Multi-Scale Attention (MFMSA) blocks, each combining DCT-based frequency decomposition with channel attention (MFCA) and foreground/background differential attention (MSDA). The MREM-refined textual embeddings are further processed by a Self-Attention layer and projected into SAM's frozen Prompt Encoder to form adaptive semantic-aware prompts, which are fused with the CCE-enhanced visual features in SAM's Mask Decoder to produce the final mask. Training uses a combined BCE + Dice loss with CLIP parameters frozen, and the entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on the OVCamo base categories for 30 epochs.

## Results

On the OVCamo benchmark (train on OVCamo setting), BaCLIP achieves cSm=0.589, cFβω=0.540, cMAE=0.327, cFβ=0.559, cEm=0.640, and cIoU=0.488, establishing a new state-of-the-art for open-vocabulary camouflaged object segmentation. Compared to the strongest OVCOS baseline OVCoser, BaCLIP improves cIoU by 4.5% (0.443→0.488) and cFβω by 5.0% (0.490→0.540). On the hardest 25% of categories (N=15), the advantage widens to +6.3% cIoU and +7.1% cFβω, confirming that bidirectional alignment is most effective where semantic confusion is most severe.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BaCLIP outperforms all eight compared methods across every metric in the train-on-OVCamo setting. Against the direct OVCOS baseline OVCoser [16], BaCLIP gains +4.5% cIoU (0.443→0.488), +5.0% cFβω, and +3.9% cFβ, while also reducing cMAE by 0.9%. Against SuCLIP [19], the second OVCOS-specific method, the margin is even larger: +23.5% cIoU (0.395→0.488) and +20.0% cFβω. Among general OVSS methods, SAN [24] is the strongest (cIoU=0.189) but still trails BaCLIP by 30 points, highlighting that generic open-vocabulary segmentation strategies are poorly suited to camouflage. Notably, BaCLIP achieves these gains without any additional visual backbone (e.g., ResNet, Swin), relying solely on the frozen CLIP-ConvNeXt-L encoder plus SAM's prompt/mask decoders, making it substantially more parameter-efficient than methods like SAN or CAT-Seg that require separate backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

BaCLIP builds directly on the OVCOS task and OVCamo benchmark introduced by Pang et al. (ECCV 2024), reusing their CamoPrompts text mechanism and CLIP-ConvNeXt-L backbone, and integrates SAM's frozen prompt encoder and mask decoder for mask generation. Its key conceptual departure from prior CLIP-based OVSS and OVCOS methods (OVCoser, SuCLIP, OVSeg, ODISE) is replacing the standard unidirectional text-to-vision attention with a bidirectional cross-attention module (MREM) that lets visual features reciprocally refine textual embeddings. The Camo Clue Extractor with DCT-based multi-frequency and multi-scale attention extends prior camouflage-specific feature extraction (edges, depth cues in OVCoser) into a learned frequency-domain formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional alignment framing offers a principled explanation for why unidirectional CLIP-based methods fail on camouflage: image-level text semantics cannot resolve pixel-level ambiguity without visual feedback, and the hard-categories analysis (+6.3% cIoU vs. +4.5% overall) confirms the mechanism targets exactly the failure mode. However, the absolute gains over OVCoser remain modest (≤5% per metric), the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark (OVCamo, 61 novel classes), and it is unclear whether the bidirectional cross-attention advantage persists in less ambiguous open-vocabulary segmentation settings or with larger-scale training. Open questions include whether the MREM mechanism generalizes to other dense prediction tasks (panoptic segmentation, detection) and whether the DCT-based frequency decomposition in CCE is essential or merely a convenient inductive bias.
