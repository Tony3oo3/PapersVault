## Problem

Existing RL-based video reasoning methods (GRPO, DAPO) rely on sparse outcome rewards that provide insufficient fine-grained guidance for perception, while fine-grained perception annotations are prohibitively expensive. The paper demonstrates empirically that enhancing perception ability yields greater performance gains than enhancing reasoning in complex video tasks, yet no existing algorithm effectively targets perception during RL training without additional reward models or costly annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (processed at 224×224 resolution, up to 30 frames for 3B and 16 for 7B during training; 60 frames at 1 fps for evaluation) alongside a text question, and produces a free-form reasoning trajectory ending in a final answer. During training, the model's internal attention weights from response tokens to visual tokens across multiple transformer layers are extracted and used to identify which frames each response focuses on, enabling token-level reward computation without any external annotation or reward model.

## Method

APPO extends GRPO with two core mechanisms. First, attention-guided frame selection: a group of G sampled responses is split into high-reward (S₁) and low-reward (S₂) sets; attention weights from response tokens to visual tokens are averaged across layers to identify the top-K₂ frames each response focuses on, and target frames are selected via set operations (Hard/Soft/All) between S₁ and S₂ focused frames. Second, intra-group perception tokens re-weighting: for each selected frame, the top-K₃ tokens from every response that attend most to that frame form a group; KL divergence between each token's probability distribution and the group average measures token-level discrepancy, which is min-max normalized and averaged across all K frame groups to produce a per-token weight W = 1 + α·(1/K)ΣD⁽ᵏ⁾. The final APPO loss multiplies the standard GRPO advantage by these token weights, so tokens from high-reward paths that focus on crucial frames receive stronger learning signals while corresponding tokens from low-reward paths are suppressed, effectively converting sparse outcome rewards into dense, frame-level perception guidance without any additional reward model.

## Results

On SEED-Bench-R1 (the primary video reasoning benchmark), APPO achieves 37.2% average accuracy on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 50.5% on 7B, outperforming DAPO by 1.9% and 0.5% respectively, and GRPO by 3.2% and 1.6%. On fine-grained spatiotemporal grounding (NExT-GQA mIoU), APPO improves over DAPO by 0.5% on both 3B (11.1 vs 10.6) and 7B (32.9 vs 32.4) models. In cross-model comparison (Table 2), APPO trained on only 34K samples surpasses methods trained on up to 310K samples, achieving the best scores on SEED-Bench-R1 (36.1%), Perception Test (64.7%), VSI-Bench (32.7%), MVBench (64.6%), and NExT-QA (79.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APPO consistently outperforms GRPO [11] and DAPO [33] across nearly all benchmarks, with the largest gains on SEED-Bench-R1 (up to +3.2% on 3B) and VSI-Bench (+1.5% on 3B), while showing a slight loss on MVBench (−0.4% on 3B) and NExT-QA (−0.3% on 7B) compared to DAPO. Against TW-GRPO [6] (trained on only 1K samples), APPO leads by 7.8% on SEED-Bench-R1 Avg and 9.8% on Perception Test. Compared to VideoChat-R1 [15] (18K training data), APPO achieves +4.3% on SEED-Bench-R1 Avg and +7.0% on Perception Test, though VideoChat-R1 retains an edge on NExT-GQA mIoU (22.0 vs 16.9) due to its dedicated temporal grounding training data. Against the larger-scale baselines GRPO-CARE [10] (260K), Video-R1 [8] (260K), and VideoRFT [21] (310K), APPO trained on just 34K samples still achieves the best SEED-Bench-R1 Avg (36.1%), Perception Test (64.7%), VSI-Bench (32.7%), MVBench (64.6%), and NExT-QA (79.2%), demonstrating that perception-aware token-level optimization is more data-efficient than scaling training data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

APPO extends the GRPO/DAPO policy optimization lineage by introducing attention-guided, token-level dense rewards specifically for video perception. It departs from prior video RL work (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, TW-GRPO) that primarily designs task-specific sparse rewards (IoU, timestamps) or improves data quality, by instead targeting the perception bottleneck directly through intra-group perception token re-weighting. Unlike perception-aware RL methods (Visionary-R1, Perception-R1) that require additional neural reward models or rigid perception-reasoning separation, APPO derives fine-grained signals purely from attention weights and KL divergence over existing responses, avoiding extra model overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that perception scale (7B→32B, +1.4%) yields larger gains than reasoning scale (Qwen3-8B→o3, +0.7%) provides a useful diagnostic for practitioners allocating compute to video MLLMs. The method's practical advantage—converting sparse outcome rewards into dense frame-level signals without additional reward models—makes it attractive for low-cost perception enhancement. However, improvements are modest (0.5–4%), the 7B gains are notably smaller than 3B gains (suggesting diminishing returns as base perception improves), and the approach has only been validated on Qwen2.5-VL at two scales, leaving open whether the attention-guided mechanism transfers to other architectures or longer videos.
