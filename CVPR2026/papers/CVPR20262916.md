## Problem

Existing GUI agent benchmarks suffer from three critical limitations: they evaluate isolated skills without capturing how perception, grounding, and planning interact; their metrics prioritize raw success rate while ignoring action efficiency; and their platform coverage is narrow, omitting major operating systems like macOS. This fragmentation prevents systematic, cross-level diagnosis of where and why GUI agents fail.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262916/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For offline levels (L1/L2), the agent receives a single GUI screenshot (varying from single-window to fullscreen) plus a text question or instruction, and outputs a multiple-choice answer or a 2D (x,y) coordinate point. For online levels (L3/L4), the agent iteratively observes the current UI state and interaction history, producing action-parameter pairs (click, type, scroll) until a terminal action or step limit is reached. Notably, all evaluations deliberately exclude auxiliary artifacts such as accessibility trees or Set-of-Marks overlays, aligning with real-world deployment where only the rendered screen is available.

## Method

MMBench-GUI organizes evaluation into four ascending capability levels—Content Understanding (MCQA on screenshots), Element Grounding (instruction-to-coordinate mapping), Task Automation (single-app multi-step execution), and Task Collaboration (cross-app workflows)—all built on shared GUI scenes across six platforms with 8,000+ tasks. The key novel contribution is the Efficiency-Quality-Aware (EQA) metric, which computes the normalized area under the success-rate-vs-step-budget curve, penalizing agents that succeed only through redundant action sequences. Data construction employs a multi-model annotation pipeline (Claude 3.7 generation → GPT-o4-mini verification → GPT-o3 refinement → human review) for L1/L2, while L3/L4 tasks are sourced from and extended beyond existing benchmarks (OSWorld, AndroidWorld, WebArenaLite-v2, WindowsAgentArena) plus a newly curated 70-task MacOSArena suite. All platforms are standardized into a unified action interface and evaluated with programmatic final-state success checks, enabling cross-level failure attribution and fair cross-platform comparison.

## Results

On L1 (GUI Content Understanding), InternVL3-72B achieves the highest overall accuracy at 79.15% (Easy), 77.89% (Medium), and 75.70% (Hard), outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-72B (66.98/67.45/64.56%) and GPT-4o (60.16/57.24/53.49%). On L2 (Element Grounding), UI-TARS-72B-DPO leads with 74.3% average point-in-box accuracy, while GPT-4o and Claude-3.7 exhibit near-zero grounding ability (2.9% and 4.7% respectively). On L3 (Task Automation, 50 steps), the best agent (GPT-4o + UI-TARS-1.5-7B) reaches only 27.2% SR and 18.7% EQA, and on L4 (Task Collaboration, 50 steps), the top system achieves just 9.8% SR and 7.4% EQA, revealing a severe gap in cross-application automation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 11+ agent configurations across four hierarchical levels. On L2 grounding, UI-TARS-72B-DPO (74.3%) and InternVL3-72B (72.2%) dominate, while general-purpose VLMs like GPT-4o (2.9%) and Claude-3.7 (4.7%) are essentially non-functional, highlighting that grounding is a specialized skill. On L3 automation (50 steps), UI-TARS-72B-DPO achieves the highest SR (27.9%) and EQA (21.6%), but the hybrid GPT-4o + UI-TARS-1.5-7B (27.2% SR) is competitive and outperforms GPT-4o alone (9.4% SR) by nearly 3×, confirming that coupling a strong planner with a dedicated grounder is critical. On L4 cross-application tasks, all agents collapse: the best SR is only 9.8% (shared by UI-TARS-72B-DPO and GPT-4o + UI-TARS-1.5-7B), with EQA at 6.9–7.4%, exposing a fundamental gap in multi-app coordination. Qwen2.5-VL-72B and Qwen-Max-VL show strong L1 understanding (64–79%) but underperform on L2 grounding (41.8% and 58.0% respectively), indicating that visual comprehension does not automatically translate to precise localization. The EQA metric further reveals that even successful agents incur substantial step redundancy, with EQA consistently 30–50% lower than SR across all configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends a fragmented landscape of GUI agent benchmarks—ScreenSpot for element grounding, OSWorld for Linux task automation, AndroidWorld, WebArena, and WindowsAgentArena—into a single hierarchical framework (L1–L4) spanning six platforms. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing a curated macOS task suite (MacOSArena) as a first-class component, filling a notable gap in cross-platform coverage, and (2) proposing the EQA metric to jointly evaluate success and step efficiency, whereas prior benchmarks report only success rate. The L2 grounding protocol directly inherits ScreenSpot's point-in-box accuracy and Basic/Advanced instruction split, while L3/L4 success checks follow OSWorld's programmatic final-state verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMBench-GUI is likely to become a standard evaluation resource for GUI agent research given its breadth (6 platforms, 8000+ tasks, 4 capability levels) and the practical EQA metric that exposes step redundancy invisible to success-rate-only evaluation. The finding that frontier LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7) score near-zero on visual grounding despite strong content understanding, and that cross-application (L4) automation remains essentially unsolved (best SR ≈ 8.8%), sharply delineates where current agents fail and where modular grounding modules are indispensable. Open questions include whether persistent memory and adaptive state tracking can close the L4 gap, and whether EQA-aware training objectives can reduce the pervasive action redundancy observed across all agents.
