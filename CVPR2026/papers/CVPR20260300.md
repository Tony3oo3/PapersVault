## Problem

Diffusion-based style transfer models treat style as a global feature and lack explicit spatial grounding, making it impossible to restrict style application to a specific object or region without handcrafted masks or multi-stage post-processing that introduce boundary artifacts. No existing diffusion model can perform true mask-free, single-object localized style transfer directly from a text prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260300/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB context image (processed at 1024×1024) and a natural-language regional style prompt (e.g., 'Make the cat in expressionism style, keep other area unchanged') as input, and produces a single edited image where only the referenced object is restyled while all other regions remain unchanged. No segmentation mask, bounding box, or other spatial control signal is required at inference; the model internally localizes the target region from the prompt alone.

## Method

RegionRoute fine-tunes the Flux.1-Kontext DiT backbone by explicitly supervising the text-to-image attention maps of style tokens to match binary object masks during training. Two complementary losses enforce this alignment: a Focus Loss (KL divergence between the softmax-normalized attention map and the normalized mask) concentrates attention mass on the target region, while a Cover Loss (per-token binary cross-entropy) ensures dense, uniform coverage within the object to prevent attention collapse. A LoRA-MoE module assigns each style a lightweight LoRA expert (rank 4) on the shared frozen backbone, so the backbone learns spatial grounding while each expert learns style-specific rendering, enabling plug-and-play multi-style support. The full training objective combines standard flow-matching reconstruction with the two attention losses, and the model is trained on 600 pseudo-GT samples (150 per style across four styles) generated by compositing global style transfer outputs with original images.

## Results

On COCO, Pascal VOC, and BIG, RegionRoute achieves RSM of 0.6128, 0.6147, and 0.6159 respectively, competitive with the strongest global-style baselines (Flux.1-Kontext: 0.6126–0.6169; Qwen-Image-Edit: 0.6235–0.6329) while substantially reducing background distortion. Specifically, RegionRoute lowers LPIPS_bg to 0.2103/0.1331/0.1593 and MSE_bg to 0.0729/0.0269/0.0474, compared to 0.43–0.48 LPIPS_bg and 0.15–0.20 MSE_bg for Flux.1-Kontext. In VLM-based controllability evaluation (Table 2), RegionRoute achieves 73–76% target-style detection (Q1) with only 7–9% background style leakage (Q2), outperforming all baselines on the joint RSM + background-preservation trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionRoute achieves the best balance between regional style fidelity (RSM) and background preservation among all eight baselines. Against the strongest global-style editors—Flux.1-Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit—RegionRoute matches or slightly trails on RSM (e.g., 0.6128 vs. 0.6235 on COCO) but reduces LPIPS_bg by 55–72% (0.2103 vs. 0.4546/0.7530), confirming that these baselines apply style globally rather than locally. Against mask-based methods (SD2-Inpainting, Instruct-Pix2Pix), RegionRoute achieves higher RSM (0.6128 vs. 0.6028/0.5978 on COCO) at the cost of somewhat higher background distortion, reflecting its stronger stylization capability. Compared to object-centric editors (Style-Editor, Text2LIVE), RegionRoute delivers comparable or better RSM while maintaining similar background fidelity. In VLM-based evaluation (Table 2), RegionRoute achieves 73–76% target-style confirmation (Q1) with only 7–9% background leakage (Q2), whereas Qwen-Image-Edit leaks style to 78–90% of the background and AnyEdit produces semantically chaotic outputs (Q3–Q4 up to 58%). No single baseline dominates both RSM and background preservation simultaneously; RegionRoute is the only method that remains competitive on both axes across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention-modulation lineage (Attend-and-Excite, Prompt-to-Prompt, TokenCompose) by repurposing cross-attention supervision not for general text-to-image alignment but specifically for binding style tokens to object regions during fine-tuning. It builds directly on Flux.1-Kontext's DiT backbone and its joint text-image self-attention, adding two dedicated losses (KL-divergence focus loss and BCE cover loss) that have no direct analogue in prior style transfer work. The LoRA-MoE design borrows the mixture-of-experts parameter-efficiency idea from the MoE literature and applies it to style-specific adapters, departing from the single-LoRA fine-tuning paradigm common in diffusion editing. Unlike prior localized style methods (Style-Editor, D²Styler) that rely on external masks or attention injection at inference, RegionRoute internalizes spatial grounding during training to achieve mask-free inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing diffusion editors either apply style globally or require handcrafted masks, making true single-object regional stylization inaccessible in a single forward pass. The RSE-Score metric (RSM + masked LPIPS/MSE) provides the first standardized evaluation protocol for localized style transfer, which the field has lacked. Limitations include a small training set (150 images, 4 styles), reliance on pseudo-GT generation, and acknowledged difficulty with small or occluded objects. Extending the framework to example-based (image-reference) style transfer and to multi-object simultaneous editing are natural next steps the authors identify.
