## Problem

Existing RVOS pipelines couple a VLM-based keyframe selector with a separate propagation module, treating reasoning and segmentation as loosely coupled steps. This design suffers from three compounding failures: fixed keyframes cause drift under rapid motion and occlusion, external modules (e.g., CLIP) prevent end-to-end optimization, and CLIP-based semantic encoders misalign with the hierarchical spatial features that segmentation decoders require. As a result, methods that score ~70 J&F on static benchmarks drop 10–20 points on motion-intensive or reasoning-oriented datasets.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video uniformly sampled into T_seg RGB frames together with a free-form natural-language referring expression, and produces a per-frame binary mask sequence (aggregated via pixelwise OR when multiple objects are referred). Unlike frame-wise methods that require N separate LLM forward passes, VIRST performs a single VLM forward pass over the entire video, with the resulting spatiotemporal prompt tokens sliced per frame to drive the SAM2-based mask decoder. The TDAU module additionally conditions non-anchor frames on stored memory features from temporally nearest anchors and a sliding FIFO window of P recent frames.

## Method

VIRST integrates a segmentation-aware vision encoder, a VideoChat-Flash-7B VLM backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA), and a SAM2 mask decoder into one end-to-end architecture. The Spatio-Temporal Fusion (STF) module bridges the two encoders in two stages: an Initial ST-Fusion cross-attends learnable [ST] tokens against downsampled segmentation features before the VLM, and a Second ST-Fusion cross-attends temporally expanded [ST] tokens (enriched with temporal RoPE) against 3D RoPE-augmented video tokens, yielding frame-specific segmentation prompts. The Temporal Dynamic Anchor Updater (TDAU) replaces fixed keyframes with a set of α=3 dynamically updated anchor frames; non-anchor frames are segmented by conditioning the decoder on anchor-frame memory and a FIFO memory of recent predictions, fused via cross-attention. Training follows a three-stage progressive curriculum—alignment (STF + LoRA only), few-image prediction (unfreezing projection and decoder), and propagation (introducing anchor-based temporal propagation with memory-efficient random sampling of 10–20 target frames per clip)—optimized with a composite loss combining BCE, Dice, token cross-entropy, occlusion, and IoU terms.

## Results

On the three standard RVOS validation benchmarks, VIRST achieves J&F of 62.9 on MeViS, 74.2 on Ref-YT-VOS, and 79.5 on Ref-DAVIS17, setting new state-of-the-art on all three. On the reasoning-oriented ReVOS benchmark, VIRST reaches an overall J&F of 68.4, surpassing the previous best (VRS-HQ-13B, 60.0) by 8.4 points, with the largest margin on the reasoning split (+9.3 points). On image segmentation, VIRST leads on RefCOCO val/testA (86.4/90.7 IoU) and RefCOCOg val/test (82.1/83.1 IoU), though it trails InstructSeg on RefCOCO testB and ReasonSeg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRST outperforms all 18 compared baselines on every RVOS benchmark. The largest gains are on MeViS (+12.0 J&F over VRS-HQ-13B) and ReVOS reasoning (+9.3 J&F over VRS-HQ-13B), where motion-intensive and reasoning-oriented queries expose the weaknesses of fixed-keyframe propagation. On Ref-YT-VOS and Ref-DAVIS17, VIRST leads the previous best (VRS-HQ-13B) by 3.2 and 5.1 J&F respectively, and surpasses the non-MLLM baseline MPG-SAM2 by 0.3 and 7.1 J&F. Among MLLM-based methods, VIRST beats VRS-HQ-7B/13B, InstructSeg, ViLLa-6B, VideoLISA, and VideoGLaMM across all video benchmarks. On image segmentation (Table 3), VIRST wins on RefCOCO val/testA, RefCOCO+ testA, and RefCOCOg val/test, but trails InstructSeg on RefCOCO testB (81.2 vs 84.0), RefCOCO+ val/testB (78.2/68.8 vs 80.1/75.6), and ReasonSeg gIoU/cIoU (60.8/65.0 vs 61.9/65.2), indicating that the spatiotemporal fusion mechanism transfers well to spatial grounding but does not fully close the gap on the hardest image-level reasoning segmentation tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIRST builds directly on SAM2's memory-based propagation architecture and the LISA-style learnable [ST] token as a vision-language bridge, but departs from the dominant keyframe-plus-propagation paradigm (VISA, VRS-HQ, RGA3) by unifying global video reasoning and pixel-level mask prediction within a single VLM forward pass. The Spatio-Temporal Fusion module addresses the encoder-discrepancy problem identified in prior work—where CLIP-based semantic features misalign with hierarchical segmentation features—by fusing segmentation-aware video tokens into the VLM backbone via cross-attention rather than concatenating them externally. The Temporal Dynamic Anchor Updater replaces fixed or CLIP-guided keyframe selection with a dynamic, temporally-local anchor update scheme, drawing on SAM2's dual memory (anchor + FIFO) but making the anchor set itself a learned, time-varying quantity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8–10 point J&F gains on reasoning-intensive benchmarks (ReVOS, MeViS) over prior SOTA address a concrete and previously unaddressed failure mode: the sharp performance drop when queries require multi-step temporal reasoning rather than simple spatial grounding. The unified single-pass design also eliminates the semantic misalignment and brittleness inherent in loosely coupled VLM + propagation pipelines, which is practically important for robotics and embodied AI where end-to-end differentiability matters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hour-long videos (the current T_seg sampling is bounded), how it handles multi-object reasoning with interacting instances, and whether the progressive three-stage training is necessary or whether a single-stage curriculum could suffice. The image-segmentation generalization is competitive but not dominant (InstructSeg still leads on RefCOCO+ testB and ReasonSeg gIoU), suggesting the spatiotemporal priors help but do not fully substitute for image-specific optimization.
