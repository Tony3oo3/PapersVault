## Problem

Existing diffusion-based perception models are either task-specific (incurring proportional training costs per task) or unified but structurally rigid, relying on textual prompts to distinguish tasks and thus unable to generalize to novel perceptual objectives without full retraining. The paper addresses the lack of an efficient, scalable mechanism to adapt a single diffusion model to entirely new perception tasks with minimal data and parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263891/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (resized to ~1024 px while preserving aspect ratio) and produces one of 14 dense prediction outputs, all unified into a consistent 3-channel RGB latent space: single-channel maps (depth, roughness, metallicity) are replicated across channels, while discrete annotations (segmentation, line art, skeleton) are rendered as color-coded RGB images. Inference uses a 2-step Flow-DPM-Solver schedule with 6 ensemble predictions from different noise initializations, achieving ~2.7 s/image at 1024×1024 on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

UniPercept adopts a foundation-adapter paradigm built on the Sana DiT backbone, reformulated as a dual-branch Rectified Flow model where the image and annotation branches are encoded by DC-AE and aligned via domain-invariant positional embeddings, with DINOv3 features augmenting the image branch. Task-specific bottleneck adapters (d/r=64, <1% of total parameters) are inserted into both linear-attention and Mix-FFN layers and dynamically activated per task during foundation training over 7 base perceptual domains. Task conditioning is further enforced through AdaLN-Zero modulation that combines timestep and task-branch embeddings to predict per-block scale, shift, and gate parameters. For novel tasks, the backbone is frozen and only a new adapter plus its embedding is trained (as few as 1K samples on a single GPU), while a modified half logit-normal timestep schedule concentrates training on high-noise regions to support fast few-step inference. Textual cross-attention is removed entirely, as visual conditioning alone suffices for perceptual tasks.

## Results

On normal estimation, UniPercept achieves 17.2° mean angular error on NYUv2, 16.8° on ScanNet, and 17.9° on iBims, ranking first among all unified diffusion frameworks and approaching specialist methods like DSINE (16.4°) and Lotus-G (16.5°). For depth estimation, it attains 6.7 AbsRel on NYUv2 and 25.1 on DIODE, outperforming all unified baselines (OmniGen: 9.8/30.6, PixWizard: 7.0/25.4, Jodi: 10.1/25.9). With only 1,000 training samples, UniPercept surpasses the specialist discriminative method PiDiNet on edge detection (0.810 ODS vs. 0.807), and achieves 2.7 s/image inference at 1024×1024 resolution—faster than Diception (4.6 s at 768×768), RGB2X (15.4 s), and PixWizard (43.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among unified diffusion frameworks, UniPercept clearly outperforms all competitors: on normal estimation it reduces mean angular error by 3.1° over Diception (18.3°→17.2° on NYUv2), 3.9° over Jodi (21.1°→17.2°), and 11.7° over OmniGen (28.9°→17.2°). On depth estimation, UniPercept achieves 6.7 AbsRel on NYUv2 versus 7.2 for Diception, 7.0 for PixWizard, and 10.1 for Jodi, while on DIODE it reaches 25.1 AbsRel, beating PixWizard (25.4) and Jodi (25.9). Against specialist models, UniPercept is competitive but slightly behind: on NYUv2 normal it trails DSINE by 0.8° and Lotus-G by 0.7°, and on depth it lags DepthAnythingV2 (4.5) and Lotus-G (5.4) by 1.3–2.2 AbsRel. A notable strength is data efficiency: with only 1,000 samples, UniPercept surpasses the specialist PiDiNet on edge detection (0.810 vs. 0.807 ODS) and approaches full-data performance on roughness/metallicity. In inference speed, UniPercept at 2.7 s/image (1024×1024) is 1.7× faster than Diception (4.6 s at 768×768), 5.7× faster than RGB2X (15.4 s), and 16× faster than PixWizard (43.3 s), while using only 12.7M trainable parameters versus >800M for full fine-tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPercept builds directly on the Sana DiT backbone (linear attention + Mix-FFN) and the Rectified Flow / Flow Matching formulation, repurposing a generative image-synthesis architecture for dense perception. It departs from prior unified diffusion perception models (RGB2X, Diception, OmniGen, PixWizard, Jodi) that rely on textual prompts to switch tasks, instead introducing a foundation-adapter paradigm borrowed from PEFT literature (adapters, LoRA) where a shared backbone learns common perceptual representations and lightweight bottleneck adapters capture task-specific behavior. The multi-objective training over seven heterogeneous base tasks (depth, normal, albedo, irradiance, segmentation, line art, skeleton) follows the spirit of Lotus and Diception but adds the critical mechanism of dynamic adapter activation to disentangle shared from task-specific knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The foundation-adapter design offers a practical path to deploying perception systems that must accommodate new tasks without retraining the full model—adapting to a novel task requires <1% trainable parameters and as few as 1K image pairs on a single GPU, which is orders of magnitude cheaper than prior unified approaches. The 2.7 s inference at 1024×1024 (vs. 15–43 s for RGB2X/PixWizard) makes high-resolution perception feasible in latency-sensitive settings. Open questions remain: whether the seven-task foundation generalizes to truly out-of-distribution modalities (e.g., 3D point clouds, video), how performance scales if the base task set is expanded, and whether the current gap to specialist models on tasks like dichotomous segmentation (Table 8) can be closed without sacrificing the unified architecture.
