## Problem

VR gaze estimation is hindered by two intertwined gaps: the absence of large-scale datasets captured with the off-axis camera geometry standard in modern headsets (existing datasets like OpenEDS2020 use on-axis cameras, and NVGaze contains only ~260K off-axis frames from 14 subjects), and the lack of unsupervised methods adapted to the near-eye infrared modality, where prior gaze-redirection approaches rely on geometric priors, multi-view consistency, or full-face RGB inputs that do not transfer to headset-mounted sensors.

## Input / Output

The model consumes pairs of near-eye infrared eye images (400×400 px) from the same eye at different timestamps during training, or single face crops (64×32 px) in remote-camera settings. At inference, only the lightweight gaze encoder (342K parameters, 55 MFLOPs) processes a single eye image to produce a gaze embedding, which is then mapped to 2D Point-of-Regard coordinates (yaw, pitch) via a per-person linear regressor (VR) or a shared 100-shot MLP (remote). The appearance encoder and decoder are discarded at test time, keeping the deployed model to a 5 ms forward pass on a mobile GPU.

## Method

GazeShift learns gaze representations through an unsupervised gaze-redirection pretext task: a source eye frame is reconstructed to match a target frame, conditioned on a gaze embedding extracted from the target. Two architecturally distinct encoders separate the signals—a deep MobileNetV2-based gaze encoder (inverted bottleneck blocks) producing a compact non-spatial embedding, and a shallower appearance encoder preserving 2D spatial structure. The gaze embedding is projected to a single global query that cross-attends over self-attended appearance tokens, and the resulting context vector is added residually to modulate appearance features toward the target gaze without disrupting spatial layout. The key loss innovation is a gaze-focused reconstruction term that reweights per-pixel MSE using the model's own self-attention maps raised to a sharpening power γ, creating a self-reinforcing loop where attention localizes gaze-relevant regions (iris, glints) and the loss concentrates supervision there, eliminating the need for external gaze masks or geometric priors.

## Results

On the VRGaze off-axis dataset, GazeShift achieves a 1.84° mean gaze error under per-person few-shot calibration, outperforming the supervised appearance-based baseline (1.54°) by only 0.30° while requiring no labeled training data. On the remote-camera MPIIGaze benchmark, the lightweight MobileNetV2 variant of GazeShift reaches 7.15° person-agnostic error with just 1M parameters and 2M FLOPs, surpassing Cross-Encoder (7.20°) which uses 11M parameters and 75M FLOPs. Deployed on a VR headset's Exynos 2200 GPU, the gaze encoder completes inference in 5 ms for both eyes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GazeShift consistently outperforms Cross-Encoder [30], the primary unsupervised baseline, across all three evaluation settings: 1.84° vs 2.15° on VRGaze (per-person), 3.43° vs 3.69° on OpenEDS2020 (per-person), and 7.15° vs 7.20° on MPIIGaze (person-agnostic, 100-shot). On MPIIGaze, GazeShift with the lightweight MobileNetV2 encoder (1M params, 2M FLOPs) also beats the supervised ResNet-18 baseline (8.35°) and Cross-Encoder (8.32°) while using 10× fewer parameters and 35× fewer FLOPs. Against general-purpose self-supervised methods, GazeShift substantially outperforms SimCLR [10] (7.15° vs 10.00°) and BYOL [9] (7.15° vs 11.10°) on MPIIGaze fine-tuning, confirming that the gaze-redirection pretext task is far more effective than generic contrastive or distillation objectives for this domain. The only method that beats GazeShift is the fully supervised appearance-based model on VRGaze (1.54° vs 1.84°), a gap of just 0.30° achieved without any labeled training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

GazeShift extends the unsupervised gaze-redirection paradigm introduced by Yu & Odobez (CVPR 2020) and refined by Cross-Encoder (Sun et al., ICCV 2021), but departs from both in two key ways: it replaces geometric warping fields and shared encoders with a pair of dedicated encoders (gaze via MobileNetV2, appearance via a spatial CNN) linked by cross-attention, and it introduces a self-supervised gaze-focused loss derived from the model's own attention maps rather than external masks. The paper also contributes VRGaze, the first large-scale off-axis near-eye infrared gaze dataset, addressing a data gap that prior on-axis datasets (OpenEDS2020, NVGaze) leave unaddressed for modern headset geometries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VRGaze dataset (2.1M off-axis images, 68 subjects) fills a critical gap for training and benchmarking gaze estimators on the oblique camera geometry used by commercial headsets, and the demonstrated 5 ms on-device inference with 10× fewer parameters and 35× fewer FLOPs than Cross-Encoder makes unsupervised gaze tracking practically deployable on resource-constrained VR hardware. A key open question is whether the core assumption—that gaze is the dominant source of inter-frame appearance variation—holds in AR or mixed-reality settings where uncontrolled lighting and reflections introduce substantial non-gaze variation. The cross-modality generalization (VR off-axis → remote MPIIGaze) suggests the framework is broadly applicable, but the gap between on-axis-trained and off-axis-trained models (5.2° vs. 1.84°) underscores that dedicated data remains necessary for each camera geometry.
