## Problem

Modern vision models must capture image-level context while preserving fine spatial detail, but existing architectures entangle global reasoning and local representation in a single feature stream, forcing width, resolution, and interaction range to compete and inflating compute. The paper argues that this coupling, not global modeling itself, is the main source of cost, and that explicitly decoupling the two roles is the key to achieving subquadratic scaling without sacrificing locality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The backbone consumes an intermediate feature map X ∈ R^{C×H×W} and produces a modulated output of the same spatial shape, making it a drop-in replacement for any downstream head. For classification it takes 224×224 RGB crops and outputs a class label; for detection it processes 800×1333 images through Cascade Mask R-CNN to yield bounding boxes and instance masks; for segmentation it handles 512×2048 inputs through Semantic FPN or UperNet to produce per-pixel class maps. The global branch internally operates on a bottlenecked dimension C_m < C with fixed chunk size L (196 for classification, 4096 for detection), so its cost grows roughly linearly with spatial size regardless of input resolution.

## Method

ConvNeur is a two-branch architecture that separates global and local computation structurally. The locality-preserving branch applies depthwise convolution with a large kernel followed by channel mixing to capture edges and textures while retaining CNN inductive priors. In parallel, the global branch projects features to a compressed memory space (C_m channels), flattens them into a spatial sequence, partitions into fixed-size chunks, and for each chunk performs a retrieval-update cycle: a query reads from the current memory state to produce chunk-level context, while a key-value pair computes a reconstruction (surprise) loss that drives an adaptive memory update via learnable step size, momentum, and decay. Chunkwise readouts are reassembled to the original spatial layout, lifted back to full channel width, and passed through a sigmoid to form a spatial gate that multiplicatively modulates the local features (F_out = X + DropPath(A ⊗ F_loc)), so global cues guide rather than overwrite local evidence. This design yields subquadratic scaling, preserves translation-friendly priors, and allows the global branch to be budgeted and compressed independently of the local one.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ConvNeur-M1 achieves 75.4% Top-1 accuracy with only 0.7 GFLOPs and 4.3M parameters, surpassing PVT-T (75.1% at 1.9 GFLOPs) with less than half the compute. At the mid scale, ConvNeur-M3 reaches 80.0% Top-1 at 1.8 GFLOPs, matching DeiT-S (79.9% at 4.6 GFLOPs) and PVT-S (79.8% at 3.8 GFLOPs) at roughly one-third the FLOPs. At the high end, ConvNeur-M4 delivers 81.5% Top-1 at 3.1 GFLOPs, exceeding CrossViT-S (81.0% at 5.6 GFLOPs) and PVT-M (81.2% at 6.7 GFLOPs). On COCO 2017 detection, ConvNeur-M3 achieves 42.4 AP, surpassing ResNeXt101-32x4d (41.9 AP) and PVTv2-B1 (41.8 AP); on ADE20K segmentation with UperNet, ConvNeur-M4 reaches 45.72 mIoU at 908 GFLOPs, outperforming Swin-T (44.51 at 945 GFLOPs) and Flatten-Swin-T (44.82 at 946 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvNeur consistently outperforms baselines at every compute tier on ImageNet-1K: M1 (75.4%) beats all sub-1.5 GFLOPs methods including PVT-T (75.1%), VRWKV-T (75.1%), and ConViT-Ti (73.1%) while using the fewest FLOPs (0.7G) and parameters (4.3M) in the comparison. M2 (77.6%) surpasses EdgeViT-XS (77.5%), MobileOne-S2 (77.4%), and PoolFormer-S12 (77.2%) at 1.0 GFLOPs. M3 (80.0%) matches or exceeds DeiT-S (79.9%) and PVT-S (79.8%) at roughly one-third their FLOPs (1.8G vs. 4.6G/3.8G). M4 (81.5%) edges out CrossViT-S (81.0%), PVT-M (81.2%), KAT-S (81.2%), and HgVT-S (81.2%) at 3.1 GFLOPs versus 4.4–6.7 GFLOPs for those baselines. On COCO detection, ConvNeur-M3 (42.4 AP) beats ResNeXt101-32x4d (41.9 AP) and PVTv2-B1 (41.8 AP), and on ADE20K segmentation, ConvNeur-M4 (45.72 mIoU) exceeds Swin-T (44.51), Flatten-Swin-T (44.82), and DeiT-S (43.92) at lower or comparable FLOPs. The ablation further confirms that the neural memory global branch outperforms self-attention (79.8%), non-local (79.1%), CBAM (78.4%), and Titans (79.6%) at matched scaffolding, while gated fusion (80.0%) beats addition (79.6%) and concatenation (79.5%).

## Relation to Prior Work

ConvNeur sits at the intersection of two well-trodden lines: modern convolutional backbones (ConvNeXt-style depthwise convolutions for local structure) and neural memory mechanisms (specifically the surprise-driven, chunked retrieval-update cycle from Titans). Its key departure from prior hybrid designs (CoAtNet, LSNet, and similar conv+attention architectures) is structural rather than operational: instead of running global and local computation in the same feature space at the same resolution, ConvNeur places them on separate branches with independent width and resolution budgets, connected only through a learned sigmoid gate. This turns the global branch into a lightweight, compressed, chunked memory reader whose sole role is to produce a spatial modulation map, rather than a full-width attention or token-mixing layer that competes with the local path for representational capacity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle offers a practical template for high-resolution and dense-prediction settings where quadratic global attention is prohibitive: by bottlenecking the global path and processing it in fixed-size chunks, the cost grows roughly linearly with spatial size while the local path retains convolutional inductive priors. The consistent gains across classification, detection, and segmentation at matched budgets suggest the design is not task-specific. Open questions the authors flag include extending to multi-scale and dynamic chunking, adapting the memory to video/streaming, and developing a formal theory of the inner fast-weight update dynamics. A limitation is that the chunk size and memory dimension are fixed hyperparameters per variant, and the per-stage (rather than per-layer) insertion, while efficient, may underutilize global context in very deep or multi-scale architectures.
