## Problem

Existing end-to-end driving frameworks evaluate safety at a coarse, scene-level granularity—scoring entire trajectories against global representations—without identifying which specific surrounding agents pose a collision risk or at which future timestep a violation occurs. Dense world models (occupancy or BEV-based) further limit safety reasoning by encoding the environment in a grid-centric manner that lacks explicit relational dynamics between the ego vehicle and individual agents. This paper addresses the gap by introducing instance-level, temporally-grounded safety reasoning into the E2E planning loop.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-modal sensor input: 360° camera images (8 views at 256×512 for NAVSIM, 4 views at 576×1024 for Bench2Drive) and LiDAR point clouds (5 sensors, voxel-encoded via SECOND), processed at 2 Hz. It outputs a single ego driving trajectory over a 4-second (8-step) horizon on NAVSIM or 3-second (6-step) horizon on Bench2Drive, selected from 256 K-means-initialized trajectory anchors. Intermediate outputs include per-agent collision-free probabilities at each future timestep (PwNC) and per-timestep drivable-area compliance scores (TwDAC), providing interpretable safety diagnostics alongside the final plan.

## Method

SafeDrive comprises three modules: ProposalNet encodes camera and LiDAR inputs into spatio-temporal BEV features (BEVFormer-M with ResNet-34 + SECOND), detects surrounding instances via deformable attention, and filters 256 trajectory anchors down to 128 safety-aware candidates using scene-level scoring. SWNet then constructs a trajectory-conditioned Sparse World for each candidate by replicating instance queries and applying intra-world self-attention plus trajectory-guided deformable attention to jointly refine ego and agent future motions, capturing pair-wise interaction dynamics that dense representations cannot express. FRNet performs fine-grained safety reasoning over these sparse worlds: PwNC concatenates each planning query with every instance query and predicts a sigmoid collision-free probability per agent per timestep (product-aggregated across all agents and steps), while TwDAC combines a learned compliance score with drivable-area probability sampled at 9 key points of the ego box at each timestep. The final trajectory is selected by a weighted log-sum of PwNC, TwDAC, and scene-level metrics (NC, DAC, TTC, comfort, ego progress) in log-probability space. The key architectural insight is that a sparse, instance-centric world representation—rather than a dense grid—enables the model to localize risk to specific agents and timesteps, yielding substantially better collision avoidance (99.5 NC, 0.5% collision rate) and drivable-area compliance (99.0 DAC) on NAVSIM.

## Results

On the NAVSIM open-loop benchmark, SafeDrive achieves a PDMS of 91.6 and an EPDMS of 87.5, with a No-Collision score of 99.5 (only 61 collisions across 12,146 test scenarios, i.e., 0.5%) and a Drivable-Area Compliance score of 99.0. On the closed-loop Bench2Drive benchmark, SafeDrive attains a 66.77% Driving Score and 42.40% Success Rate, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. The method uses Camera + LiDAR inputs with a ResNet-34 backbone and BEVFormer-M encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeDrive outperforms all 18 compared baselines on both benchmarks. On NAVSIM PDMS, it leads the closest competitor GoalFlow (90.3) by 1.3 points and SeerDrive (88.9) by 2.7 points, with the largest margin over UniAD (83.4) at 8.2 points. On the extended EPDMS metric, SafeDrive (87.5) surpasses GaussianFusion (85.0) by 2.5 points and DiffusionDrive (84.8) by 2.7 points. On Bench2Drive, SafeDrive (66.77 DS) beats the previous SOTA DriveTransformer (63.46) by 3.31 points and DriveDPO (62.02) by 4.75 points, while also achieving the highest Success Rate (42.40% vs. 35.01% for DriveTransformer). The NC score of 99.5 (61 collisions in 12,146 scenes) is the most notable safety improvement, exceeding the next-best SeerDrive (98.4) by 1.1 points and translating to roughly 13 fewer collisions than the runner-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeDrive sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: trajectory-evaluation-based E2E planning (Hydra-MDP, DriveSuprim, GoalFlow) and world-model-driven scene simulation (OccWorld, WoTE, LAW, DriveWorld). It departs from both in a specific way: prior world models use dense grid-based representations (occupancy, BEV) that the authors argue cannot capture instance-level relational dynamics, while prior safety evaluators score entire trajectories against holistic scene encodings without identifying which agent or timestep is at risk. The paper repurposes deformable attention and query-based detection (from BEVFormer/UniAD) into a 'sparse world' that tracks individual agents, then introduces pair-wise, timestep-resolved collision and drivability scoring (PwNC, TwDAC) that no prior E2E planner provides at that granularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fine-grained, interpretable safety reasoning (identifying exactly which surrounding agent poses risk at which future timestep) addresses a practical gap in E2E driving where scene-level scores can miss localized collision risks, as demonstrated in the qualitative PwNC analysis. The SOTA results on both open-loop (NAVSIM PDMS 91.6, EPDMS 87.5) and closed-loop (Bench2Drive DS 66.8%) benchmarks validate the approach across evaluation regimes. However, the authors acknowledge that PwNC and TwDAC remain learned proxy scores rather than formal safety guarantees, leaving open the question of whether sparse-world reasoning can be combined with certified or constraint-based safety layers. The sparse-vs-dense world model distinction may also influence how future work structures scene representations for safety-critical reasoning.
