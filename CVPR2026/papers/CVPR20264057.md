## Problem

Sketch editing requires simultaneously handling high-level semantic modifications (e.g., changing hairstyle or outfit via text) and precise local redrawing (e.g., redrawing a masked region following hand-drawn guidance lines), a combination that is particularly difficult for sparse, style-sensitive line art. Existing image editing and inpainting models lack controllable paired training data for sketches and cannot unify global semantic control with fine-grained structural guidance within a single architecture while preserving overall composition and artistic style.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source sketch image and, depending on the editing mode, either a natural-language instruction (for global semantic edits) or a binary mask plus user-provided guidance lines (for local redrawing). All spatial conditions are packed into a standard 3-channel RGB composite: the R channel carries the source sketch (with the masked region erased in line mode), the G channel encodes the editable mask, and the B channel carries structural guidance lines (empty in instruction mode). The output is a full edited or redrawn sketch at the same resolution, with the model expected to preserve unmodified regions and overall style.

## Method

SketchAssist is built on the FLUX.1-Kontext DiT backbone and introduces two key designs. First, a unified 3-channel RGB input representation encodes the source sketch, target mask, and guidance lines in a single standard image, avoiding extra input channels or separate control branches. Second, a Task-guided Mixture-of-Experts (T-MoE) module is integrated into LoRA layers: a Shared LoRA captures task-agnostic priors while multiple Expert LoRA modules learn task-specific patterns, with a TopK softmax router over concatenated text-visual features selecting active experts per forward pass. Training data is generated via a three-stage pipeline: (1) atomic addition sequences from attribute-free bases using fixed-seed ControlNet generation, (2) cross-sequence sampling that pairs sketches across different sequences and time steps to synthesize multi-attribute edits with controlled edit distance D=1–5, and (3) style-preserving removal applied to diverse real-world sketches to expand stylistic coverage. The model is trained entirely on natural-language instructions rather than tag-based prompts for user-friendly interaction.

## Results

On instruction-guided sketch editing (200 test samples), SketchAssist achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics: CLIP-T 0.305, CLIP-I 0.931, DINO 0.879, and VIEScore Overall (Q_O) 8.04, outperforming the strongest baseline FLUX.1-Kontext (Q_O 6.42) by a wide margin. On line-guided region redrawing (200 test samples), SketchAssist achieves LPIPS 0.0972, CLIP-I 0.961, and DINO 0.949, substantially surpassing the best baseline MagicQuill (LPIPS 0.1472, DINO 0.887). A 50-rater user study confirms a markedly high winning rate against all competing methods on both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FLUX.1-Kontext (the base architecture), SketchAssist improves CLIP-I from 0.882 to 0.931 and DINO from 0.791 to 0.879, with VIEScore Overall jumping from 6.42 to 8.04, confirming that the T-MoE and unified input design add substantial editing capability beyond the pretrained model. Step1X-Edit is the closest competitor on semantic consistency (Q_SC 7.60 vs. 8.74) but still trails by over 1 point; Qwen-Image-Edit achieves the highest perceptual quality (Q_PQ 7.66 vs. 7.49) yet lags significantly on overall score (Q_O 6.79 vs. 8.04) and Gemini-based overall (G_O 7.89 vs. 8.52), indicating it over-smooths at the expense of instruction adherence. ICEdit, the earliest baseline, is outperformed by a large margin on every metric (e.g., DINO 0.705 vs. 0.879). For line-guided redrawing, SketchAssist reduces LPIPS from 0.1472 (MagicQuill) to 0.0972 and raises DINO from 0.887 to 0.949, while SketchEdit and BrushNet both exceed 0.18 LPIPS, showing that existing inpainting-style methods struggle to follow guidance lines in sparse line art.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—instruction-based image editing (InstructPix2Pix, FLUX.1-Kontext, Step1X-Edit, Qwen-Image-Edit) and local mask/line-guided redrawing (SketchEdit, BrushNet, MagicQuill)—into a single DiT-based framework tailored for sparse line art. It departs from prior approaches that either expand input channels or add separate control branches (e.g., OminiControl) by exploiting the monochrome nature of sketches to pack source, mask, and guidance into a standard 3-channel RGB composite, and introduces a Task-guided Mixture-of-Experts within LoRA layers to decouple task-specific behaviors without inflating model size. The data side builds on ControlNet-conditioned generation and WD14 tag vocabularies but introduces a novel cross-sequence sampling strategy to synthesize structurally aligned multi-attribute edit pairs, addressing a gap the authors identify in existing sketch datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, end-to-end tool for the second phase of professional sketching (detail refinement), a workflow step where artists spend most of their time. The controllable data generation pipeline (atomic sequences → cross-sequence sampling → style-preserving removal) is a reusable recipe for constructing paired editing data in other sparse or line-art domains. Limitations include a focus on anime/character sketches (Danbooru tags, WD14 vocabulary), reliance on a single generator for base sketches, and evaluation on pipeline-generated test data rather than real user interactions, leaving open questions about generalization to diverse art styles, multi-object scenes, and real-time interactive use.
