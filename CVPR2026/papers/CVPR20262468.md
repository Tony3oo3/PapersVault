## Problem

Existing event-based tracking methods either rely on ANNs that ignore the sparse nature of event data and incur high inference costs, or use hybrid ANN-SNN architectures that fail to fully exploit SNN energy efficiency. Additionally, current event aggregation methods discard multi-directional motion and global trajectory information, limiting tracking accuracy. No fully spike-driven transformer-based tracking pipeline existed prior to this work.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262468/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes asynchronous template and search event streams (each event: x, y, t, p) from event cameras, which are aggregated into 3-channel event frames at 128×128 (template) and 256×256 (search) resolution via the GTP method. The two frames are diagonally concatenated with zero padding into a unified matrix before processing. The output is a predicted target bounding box (center position and scale) produced by an SNN center prediction head, with no data augmentation, dynamic template updates, or post-processing (e.g., Hanning window) applied during inference.

## Method

SDTrack comprises two key components: (1) Global Trajectory Prompt (GTP), a 3-channel event aggregation where channels 1–2 accumulate positive and negative polarity counts within each time window, and channel 3 captures global trajectory via a decayed accumulation of newly-appearing polarity changes across consecutive frames; and (2) a fully spike-driven tracker that uses Intrinsic Position Learning (IPL) to diagonally concatenate template and search frames (enabling position learning without explicit positional encoding), processes them through SNN Conv Blocks (depthwise + pointwise convolutions with LIF/I-LIF neurons), then through SNN Transformer Blocks (SNN Self-Attention + SNN MLP) for template-search interaction, and finally predicts the target via an SNN center head. The entire pipeline replaces MAC operations with AC operations, achieving 8.16 mJ energy consumption for the Tiny variant. Training uses ImageNet-1K pre-training followed by frame-pair fine-tuning with weighted focal loss, L1 loss, and GIoU loss.

## Results

SDTrack-Base (I-LIF, 1×4) achieves state-of-the-art AUC on all three event-based tracking benchmarks: 59.9% on FE108, 40.0% on FELT, and 37.4% on VisEvent, with corresponding PR of 91.5%, 51.4%, and 51.5%. SDTrack-Tiny (I-LIF, 1×4) achieves 59.0% AUC on FE108 with only 19.61M parameters and 8.16 mJ energy consumption — the lowest among all compared methods. The Tiny variant outperforms the previous best SNN-based tracker SNNTrack (35.4% AUC on VisEvent) by 0.2% AUC while using fewer parameters (19.61M vs. 31.40M) and comparable energy (8.16 mJ vs. 8.25 mJ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDTrack-Base achieves the highest AUC on all three datasets, outperforming the strongest ANN-based tracker ARTrack256 (39.5% AUC on FELT) by 0.5% and the best SNN-based tracker SNNTrack (35.4% on VisEvent) by 2.0%. On FE108, SDTrack-Tiny (I-LIF 1×4) leads all methods with 59.0% AUC, surpassing STARK (57.4%), GRM (56.8%), and SimTrack (56.7%) by 1.6–2.6% AUC. In the efficiency dimension, SDTrack-Tiny uses only 19.61M parameters and 8.16 mJ, less than half the parameters of HiT-B (42.22M) and one-seventh of its energy (19.78 mJ), while delivering 3.1% higher AUC on FE108. On VisEvent, SDTrack-Base (37.4% AUC) beats the previous SNN-based best SNNTrack (35.4%) by 2.0% and the ANN-based best SimTrack (34.6%) by 2.8%. The LIF-based SDTrack-Tiny (4×1) achieves 56.7% AUC on FE108, slightly below the I-LIF variant (59.0%), indicating that the I-LIF neuron with temporal resolution D=4 provides a meaningful accuracy gain over standard LIF at comparable energy cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDTrack builds on the OSTrack one-stream tracking framework (Ye et al., ECCV 2022) for its cross-correlation-based center prediction head, and on the Spike-Driven Transformer V3 architecture (Yao et al., 2024) for its I-LIF-based SNN Transformer backbone. It departs from prior SNN-based event trackers (STNet, SNNTrack) that relied on hybrid ANN+SNN architectures by constructing a fully spike-driven pipeline, and introduces two novel components: the Global Trajectory Prompt (GTP) event aggregation method that replaces prior polarity-only or time-surface approaches, and Intrinsic Position Learning (IPL) that substitutes explicit positional encoding with diagonal template-search concatenation. The paper positions itself as the first Transformer-based, end-to-end SNN tracking pipeline for event cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SDTrack establishes the first fully SNN-based event tracking baseline, demonstrating that spike-driven inference can match or exceed hybrid ANN+SNN trackers while reducing energy consumption by roughly an order of magnitude (8.16 mJ vs. 103.53 mJ for STNet), which is critical for real neuromorphic chip deployment. The GTP method's demonstrated generalizability—improving performance of five unrelated mainstream trackers—suggests it could become a standard preprocessing step for event-based vision tasks beyond tracking. Key open questions include whether the pipeline transfers to multimodal Event+RGB settings, whether actual neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Loihi, TrueNorth) can execute the full Transformer-based SNN at the reported energy levels, and whether the modest accuracy gains over hybrid approaches will widen as SNN training techniques mature.
