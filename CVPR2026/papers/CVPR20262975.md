## Problem

Existing subject-driven image generation methods focus on combining multiple subjects from reference images but neglect distinction — the ability to correctly identify and generate the target subject when a reference image contains multiple candidate subjects. This leads to subject omissions, misidentification, and interference from irrelevant content in complex, realistic visual settings, limiting practical performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262975/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one to four reference images (each potentially containing multiple candidate subjects) and a text instruction that specifies the target subject(s) by index or distinctive attribute (e.g., 'the woman on the left of the image'). Output is a single composed RGB image at 1024×1024 resolution. The instruction can reference subjects across multiple images for multi-subject composition, and in distinction tasks must disambiguate among candidates within a single reference image.

## Method

Scone builds on BAGEL, a Mixture-of-Transformer Experts unified understanding-generation model, and introduces an understanding bridge strategy with a two-stage training scheme. Stage I fine-tunes both experts on single-candidate data to learn basic composition. Stage II first trains the understanding expert to compute cosine similarity between early-layer visual and textual tokens, producing a binary semantic mask that sets attention logits to −∞ for instruction-irrelevant reference tokens, thereby filtering out non-target subjects; then both experts are jointly trained so the generation expert aligns with the bridge's semantic guidance. No additional parameters are introduced — the mechanism operates entirely through attention masking derived from the understanding expert's early-layer representations, enabling end-to-end collaboration that suppresses irrelevant interference while preserving target subject identity.

## Results

On the proposed SconeEval benchmark, Scone achieves an overall score of 8.50 (COM 8.21, DIS 8.79), the best among all open-source models, with particularly strong distinction scores of 9.73 (cross-category) and 8.44 (intra-category) on the Distinction & Composition task. On the OmniContext benchmark, Scone attains an average score of 8.01, also ranking first among open-source methods and surpassing the next-best open-source model Echo-4o (7.95) by 0.06. Closed-source models GPT-4o (8.94 on SconeEval, 8.78 on OmniContext) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (8.70, 8.07) remain ahead, but Scone narrows the gap to within 0.2–0.4 points on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scone outperforms all open-source baselines on both benchmarks. On SconeEval, it leads Echo-4o (8.09) by 0.41, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.70) by 0.80, and its base model BAGEL (6.97) by 1.53 in overall score; the distinction advantage is especially pronounced, with Scone scoring 9.73 vs. 8.33 (Echo-4o) and 7.95 (BAGEL) on cross-category distinction. On OmniContext, Scone (8.01) edges out Echo-4o (7.95) by 0.06 and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.60) by 0.41. Against closed-source models, Scone trails GPT-4o by 0.44 on SconeEval and 0.77 on OmniContext, and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image by 0.20 and 0.06 respectively. Notably, unified models with weaker composition (e.g., OmniGen2, COM 7.39) still beat dedicated generation models (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit, COM 7.76) on distinction, confirming the paper's thesis that understanding capability aids subject discrimination. The ablation further shows the understanding bridge strategy adds +0.07 overall over a two-step training without the bridge (8.50 vs. 8.43), with the largest gain in distinction (+0.09).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on BAGEL, a Mixture-of-Transformer Experts unified understanding-generation model, and extends the subject-driven image generation literature by introducing 'distinction'—the ability to identify and generate the correct target subject when a reference image contains multiple candidates—as a first-class objective alongside composition. Prior unified models (OmniGen2, Echo-4o) leverage understanding for generation stability but lack explicit mechanisms to suppress irrelevant subjects in complex references; Scone departs from them by formalizing the understanding expert as a 'semantic bridge' that produces an attention-based mask from early-layer visual-textual alignment, filtering instruction-irrelevant regions before the generation expert attends to them. The SconeEval benchmark also departs from existing evaluations (OmniContext, DreamBench++) that assume single-subject references and rely on similarity metrics, instead requiring cross-modal reasoning over multi-candidate images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The distinction problem is practically important for real-world applications where reference images are cluttered and instructions must disambiguate among multiple visible subjects, yet it has been largely unaddressed in the subject-driven generation literature. The understanding bridge strategy is attractive because it adds no parameters and operates entirely within the unified architecture, making it a lightweight technique potentially transferable to other MoE-based unified models. However, the approach is validated only on BAGEL, the threshold τ requires manual tuning, and evaluation relies on GPT-4.1 scoring, which introduces its own biases. Open questions include whether the bridge generalizes to non-MoE unified architectures, whether it scales beyond four reference images, and whether the distinction gains persist in video or 3D generation settings.
