## Problem

Existing zero-shot image denoising methods either under-exploit global non-local self-similarity priors or fail to preserve fine-grained local details, and they struggle to balance denoising performance against computational overhead. Methods like Self2Self achieve strong results but incur prohibitive cost, while faster alternatives like ZS-N2N suffer significant performance degradation, leaving a gap for an approach that jointly leverages both local and global image priors efficiently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy RGB image (no clean reference or external training data) and outputs a denoised image of the same spatial resolution. It is trained per-image for 1500 iterations using an 8-layer fully convolutional network (3×3 kernels, 48 channels, ~126K parameters). Evaluation uses 256×256 central crops across Gaussian (σ=10/25/50), Poisson (λ=50/25/10), real-world camera, and fluorescence microscopy noise, with peak memory of ~780 MB and inference latency of ~28 s per image.

## Method

ZS-HPD generates pseudo training pairs from the noisy image itself via two complementary samplers. The local-prior down-sampler S_d computes Sobel gradients, aggregates them over 5×5 windows, sorts within 2×2 tiles, and groups by gradient magnitude (low-low and high-high pairs) to produce down-sampled input-target pairs that preserve spatial locality. The global-prior random sampler S_r builds a pixel bank by searching for K=10 most similar 7×7 patches (in the Y channel) across the entire image, with candidate positions drawn from a Gaussian distribution (σ_G=10) that prioritizes proximal pixels while permitting long-range exploration, yielding full-resolution pseudo pairs with reduced noise spatial correlation. Training uses a spectral weighted loss (SWL) computed in the Fourier domain, which applies higher weight (β=1.0) to the high-frequency band than the low-frequency band (α=0.5) to counteract the observation that noise predominantly corrupts HF components.

## Results

On Kodak24 and McMaster18 with Gaussian noise (σ=10, 25, 50), ZS-HPD achieves state-of-the-art PSNR among zero-shot methods on 5 of 6 settings, e.g., 29.88 dB / 0.8376 SSIM on Kodak24 at σ=25 and 25.10 dB / 0.6959 SSIM on McMaster18 at σ=50. For Poisson noise (λ=50, 25, 10), ZS-HPD leads on all 6 settings, reaching 30.12 dB / 0.8506 on Kodak24 at λ=50 and 31.64 dB / 0.8954 on McMaster18 at λ=50. On real-world noise, ZS-HPD attains 36.24 dB / 0.9504 on PolyU, 35.57 dB / 0.8705 on SIDD, and 32.54 dB / 0.8301 on FMD, outperforming all zero-shot baselines. Inference latency is 28 s on 256×256 images with 780 MB peak memory, comparable to Pixel2Pixel (26 s, 3902 MB) and far below Self2Self (38 min, 966 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZS-HPD outperforms the strongest zero-shot baseline Pixel2Pixel [28] on 11 of 12 synthetic noise settings (Gaussian and Poisson on Kodak24 and McMaster18), with margins of 0.3–0.6 dB PSNR at moderate-to-high noise levels (e.g., +0.57 dB on Kodak24 σ=25, +0.57 dB on McMaster18 σ=50 for Poisson λ=50). The sole exception is Kodak24 Gaussian σ=10, where ZS-N2N [29] edges ahead by 0.12 dB (33.69 vs 33.57). Against ZS-N2N [29], ZS-HPD gains up to 1.55 dB at high noise (Kodak24 σ=50: 26.36 vs 24.81) and consistently leads on McMaster18. On real-world noise (Table 3), ZS-HPD surpasses Pixel2Pixel by 0.13 dB on PolyU, 1.23 dB on SIDD, and 0.20 dB on FMD, while also outperforming MASH [5] by 1.29–4.29 dB and Self2Self [33] by 0.27–2.98 dB. Compared to supervised baselines DnCNN [40] and NB2NB [14], ZS-HPD trails by 1.7–3.3 dB on low-noise settings but narrows the gap to <1 dB at σ=50, all without requiring any paired training data. In terms of efficiency, ZS-HPD uses 126K parameters (vs 150K for Pixel2Pixel, 1000K for Self2Self) and 780 MB peak memory (vs 3902 MB for Pixel2Pixel), with inference latency of 28 s comparable to Pixel2Pixel's 26 s but far below Self2Self's 38 min.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot denoising lineage that began with Noise2Noise's self-supervision principle and was specialized to single-image settings by DIP, Self2Self, ZS-N2N, and Pixel2Pixel. Prior methods implicitly exploit either spatial locality (P1, via fixed-pattern down-sampling in ZS-N2N/Noise2Fast) or non-local self-similarity (P2, via local-window pixel banks in Pixel2Pixel), but none systematically combine both priors in a single pseudo-sample generation pipeline. ZS-HPD departs from this by introducing a gradient-merging down-sampler (Sd) that better preserves P1 and a Gaussian-conditioned global random sampler (Sr) that captures P2 across the full image, plus a Fourier-domain spectral weighted loss that explicitly accounts for noise's disproportionate impact on high-frequency content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hybrid prior framework offers a principled way to balance denoising fidelity against computational cost in zero-shot settings, which is critical for deployment in domains like medical imaging and astronomy where paired training data is unavailable. The ablation showing that Sd and Sr are individually weaker than existing baselines but synergistically stronger highlights that complementary prior exploitation matters more than any single sampling trick. Open questions include whether the fixed Gaussian σ_G and 8-layer CNN architecture scale to high-resolution images or more complex real-world noise, and whether the spectral weighting strategy generalizes to other restoration tasks beyond denoising.
