## Problem

Few-shot multi-class anomaly detection requires a single model to judge normality across many object categories using only a handful of normal samples each, yet existing VLM-based methods depend on handcrafted, category-specific anomaly descriptions or heavy auxiliary modules (e.g., Q-Former, VV-CLIP) that limit scalability and inflate inference cost. The core difficulty is the asymmetry between shared normality and category-dependent abnormality: forcing heterogeneous defect semantics into a unified textual space causes inter-class confusion, while removing descriptions entirely loses the class-level structure needed to organize multi-class representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 240×240 for the global view at inference; during training, 480×480 images are split into four non-overlapping 240×240 crops) and a class name string as the sole textual input—no defect-type descriptions are required. It produces an image-level anomaly score computed as the harmonic mean of a contrastive normality probability and the maximum fused pixel score, plus a pixel-level anomaly map obtained by harmonically fusing a lightweight decoder's output with a memory-bank nearest-neighbor similarity map. At test time only the global scale prefix is active, so inference runs at a single resolution without multi-scale overhead.

## Method

AnoPLe builds on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone and introduces bidirectional deep prompting: learnable textual context vectors (conditioned on the class name and a unified 'abnormal' token) and learnable visual context vectors are injected at every encoder layer, and at each layer the two prompt streams are cross-projected via learnable linear maps and concatenated back, so class-level semantics and instance-level cues iteratively refine one another. For localization, a scale-aware prefix (a learnable token encoding resolution identity) is prepended to the visual prompts during training on both global and local crops, while a lightweight decoder upsamples patch embeddings to produce pixel logits; at inference only the global prefix is used, preserving single-resolution speed. Training is self-supervised on normal-only data, generating pseudo-anomalies via pixel-space perturbations and latent-space Gaussian noise, and is optimized with a combined loss of Dice+Focal pixel supervision, contrastive cross-entropy image-level scoring, and a cosine-alignment term that propagates anomaly-weighted local decoder outputs back to the global [CLS] representation. At inference, a memory bank of stored normal patch features provides a similarity-based anomaly map that is fused with the decoder output via harmonic mean to yield the final pixel-level prediction.

## Results

Under the 1-shot multi-class setting, AnoPLe achieves 94.5% image-level AUROC and 90.8% PRO on MVTec-AD, 86.0% AUROC and 87.5% PRO on VisA, and 81.2% AUROC and 88.0% PRO on Real-IAD. These results surpass IIPAD on VisA and Real-IAD while remaining competitive on MVTec-AD (within 0.4 AUROC of INP-Former). The method also runs at 27.92–29.64 FPS across the three benchmarks, substantially faster than methods requiring sliding-window inference or auxiliary Q-Former/VV-CLIP forward passes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnoPLe outperforms description-based methods by large margins: over PromptAD by +3.3 I-AUROC on MVTec-AD, +3.6 on VisA, and +29.0 on Real-IAD (81.2 vs 52.2), where PromptAD's reliance on handcrafted anomaly descriptions causes severe degradation. Against WinCLIP, AnoPLe gains +17.0 I-AUROC on MVTec-AD and +16.0 on VisA, reflecting the advantage of learned bidirectional prompts over fixed handcrafted templates. Compared to IIPAD, AnoPLe wins on VisA (+0.6 I-AUROC, +0.2 PRO) and is the only method reporting Real-IAD results in this comparison, while IIPAD retains a slight edge on MVTec-AD pixel AUROC (96.4 vs 96.0). Against the vision-only INP-Former, AnoPLe trails by 0.2 I-AUROC on MVTec-AD but leads by +2.0 on VisA and +3.5 PRO on MVTec-AD; however, INP-Former is stronger on Real-IAD (84.4 vs 81.2 I-AUROC). In the medical domain (BMAD, 4-shot), AnoPLe achieves the best P-AUROC on Brain MRI (97.1) and Retinal OCT (97.0), and the best I-AUROC on Liver CT (74.8), outperforming both MedCLIP and UniVAD on most metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

AnoPLe builds on the CLIP-based anomaly detection lineage initiated by WinCLIP (memory-bank scoring, text-embedding averaging) and the lightweight decoder from 'Extract Free Dense Labels from CLIP,' while deliberately departing from the description-driven prompt paradigm of PromptAD and the heavy Q-Former-based instance-induced prompts of IIPAD. Its core conceptual shift is replacing class-specific defect descriptions with class names as lightweight textual anchors, coupled bidirectionally with visual prompts to form class-aware yet defect-agnostic representations. The scale-aware prefix mechanism also departs from VV-CLIP-style multi-scale inference by encoding scale identity directly into the prompt sequence, avoiding additional forward passes at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for expert-crafted anomaly descriptions and heavy auxiliary modules, AnoPLe addresses a practical bottleneck in industrial deployment where defect taxonomies are incomplete, category-specific, and costly to maintain. The demonstrated generalization to unseen anomaly types and the medical domain (BMAD) suggests the learned normality priors are genuinely transferable rather than overfit to specific defect vocabularies. Open questions include whether the bidirectional coupling remains beneficial at very large category counts (beyond the ~15–30 tested), whether the pseudo-anomaly simulation strategy (pixel + latent perturbations) is sufficient for highly structured defects, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or 3D industrial inspection.
