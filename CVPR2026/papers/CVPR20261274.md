## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for LiDAR semantic segmentation—relying on pseudolabels or batch normalization updates—degrade significantly under severe corruptions such as adverse weather and sensor failures, because inaccurate predictions or unstable statistics provide poor adaptation signals. The paper identifies the lack of a reliable self-supervised objective that can guide test-time training without depending on the model's own (potentially wrong) semantic predictions in corrupted environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261274/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw LiDAR point cloud where each point is a 4D vector (3D spatial coordinates + intensity). During both source training and test-time adaptation, the input is augmented with synthetic off-manifold points generated by displacing each point along a randomly chosen axis by a uniform offset in [−δ, δ], with intensity copied from the nearest real point. The segmentation head outputs per-point class probabilities over 19 (SemanticKITTI) or 16 (nuScenes) classes, while the GeoID head outputs a per-point inlier score in [0,1] indicating geometric plausibility.

## Method

The core contribution is Geometric Inlier Discrimination (GeoID), a self-supervised binary classification task that trains a shared encoder to separate geometry-consistent inliers from synthetically displaced off-manifold outliers, using axis-aligned spatial displacements to construct the outlier distribution. During source training, the encoder is jointly optimized with a segmentation cross-entropy loss (on raw points only) and a GeoID binary cross-entropy loss (on the augmented point cloud). At test time, adaptation uses only the GeoID loss on unlabeled target data, updating the shared encoder and GeoID decoder. To stabilize this self-supervision under real corruptions, Bidirectional Unreliable Point Filtering (BiUPF) computes inlier scores from a frozen source-trained model and discards low-confidence points on both the original (score < τ_r) and synthetic (score > 1−τ_r) sides, restricting gradient updates to high-confidence inliers and outliers that clearly exhibit or violate scene geometry.

## Results

On SemanticKITTI-C, GeoID achieves a mean mIoU of 46.96%, outperforming the strongest baseline TTT (43.58%) by 3.38 points and the source-only model (42.33%) by 4.63 points across eight corruption types. The largest gains appear under crosstalk (+16.36% over source-only) and snow (+8.59% over source-only). On the Cylinder3D-based benchmark (Table 3), GeoID reaches 55.46% mIoU, edging out NormFusion (55.25%) and CoTTA (54.11%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoID wins against all six baselines on SemanticKITTI-C mean mIoU, with the largest margins over GIPSO (+6.24 pts) and HGL (+5.56 pts), both of which rely on pseudo-label self-training and degrade under severe corruption. The closest competitor is TTT (rotation prediction), which achieves 43.58% mean mIoU; GeoID's 46.96% represents a 3.38-point gap, indicating that geometric inlier discrimination is a more suitable self-supervised signal than rotation prediction for LiDAR. 2D-domain methods TENT and DUA actually underperform the source-only model (41.58% and 41.57% vs. 42.33%), confirming that entropy minimization and BN-statistics adaptation transfer poorly to 3D point clouds. On the Cylinder3D backbone (Table 3), GeoID (55.46%) narrowly beats NormFusion (55.25%) and CoTTA (54.11%), demonstrating architecture-agnostic generalization. On nuScenes-C, GeoID similarly outperforms all baselines on most corruption types, with comparable results on the remaining cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time training (TTT) paradigm—originally developed for 2D images via rotation prediction, entropy minimization, and batch-norm adaptation—into the LiDAR semantic segmentation domain under corruption. It departs from existing LiDAR-specific TTA methods (GIPSO, HGL) that rely on pseudo-labels, and from normalization-based approaches (TENT, DUA, NormFusion), by introducing a geometry-aware self-supervised objective (GeoID) that discriminates real inliers from synthetically displaced off-manifold points. The bidirectional unreliable point filtering (BiUPF) mechanism further distinguishes this work by explicitly gating the self-supervision signal to prevent harmful updates from geometrically ambiguous points, a concern not addressed in prior LiDAR TTA methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that purpose-built LiDAR TTA methods (GIPSO, HGL) can underperform the source-only model under severe corruption highlights a critical gap in the field: pseudo-label-based adaptation is unreliable precisely when it is most needed. GeoID offers a geometry-grounded alternative that is robust across diverse corruption types and backbones, making it a practical tool for safety-critical autonomous driving deployments. Open questions remain around the simplicity of axis-aligned displacement as a proxy for off-manifold geometry, the assumption of shared scene structure across domains, and the inability to explicitly detect or model real corruption artifacts—directions the authors identify for future work.
