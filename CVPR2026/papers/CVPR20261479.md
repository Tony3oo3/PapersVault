## Problem

Existing eyeglasses removal methods target opaque or fully transparent lenses, but semi-transparent sunglasses present a unique challenge: binary masks cannot capture partial lens transmittance, causing visible eye-region information to be discarded or distorted during removal. This degrades identity preservation and fine-detail fidelity, which in turn harms downstream tasks such as face recognition and expression analysis. The core difficulty is not generative capacity but dynamically balancing generative freedom with faithful use of the residual visible information beneath the lenses.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 RGB facial image with semi-transparent eyeglasses and a binary eyeglass mask as input, and produces a high-fidelity eyeglass-free facial image. Internally, the pipeline also consumes a predicted transparency soft mask (from the TPM) and a generative prior image (from GPDM), both of which are derived from the input rather than provided externally. Training uses 25,000 paired samples synthesized via a radial-gradient alpha compositing pipeline built on FFHQ, with 1,000 additional pairs held out for evaluation.

## Method

Diff-SemiER decouples the task into two independently trained diffusion branches. The Generative Prior Diffusion Model (GPDM) is trained with masked regions filled by Gaussian noise rather than the semi-transparent image, forcing it to learn an unconstrained generative distribution that produces structurally plausible eyeglass-free priors. The Transparency-Aware Adaptive Fusion Diffusion Model (TAFDM) is a U-Net denoiser that simultaneously ingests the noisy input, the generative prior, and the original semi-transparent image through a separate restore encoder. At each denoising step, the Soft Mask-Aware Adaptive Fusion (SMAF) module modulates restorative features by the TPM-predicted transparency soft mask, then computes coarse-to-fine spatial and channel attention weights (via 7×7 convolutions, global pooling, channel shuffle, and group convolution) to produce a dynamic per-pixel, per-channel fusion weight that adaptively blends generative and restorative features—preserving true eye details under low occlusion while relying on the generative prior under heavy occlusion.

## Results

On the synthetic FFHQ-Test set, Diff-SemiER achieves the best scores across all five metrics: FID 47.45, PSNR 36.16 dB, SSIM 0.9802, LPIPS 0.0094, and IDD 0.534, approaching the ground-truth FID of 46.44. On real-world datasets (FFHQ, CelebA-HQ, CelebA), it attains the best or near-best FID (66.13, 99.80, 61.60 respectively), with only a marginal gap to RDDM on CelebA-HQ (99.53) and CelebA (61.82). In a 50-participant user study, Diff-SemiER is preferred over the strongest baselines (RDDM, Resfusion) by 65–69% of raters, and over GAN-based methods by over 87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Diff-SemiER outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in the synthetic FFHQ-Test evaluation. The largest margins are in identity preservation (IDD): Diff-SemiER scores 0.534 versus 0.576 for RDDM and 0.582 for Resfusion, the two closest diffusion-based competitors, and 0.752 for Pix2Pix. Among GAN-based methods, ERGAN [10] is the weakest overall (FID 56.83, IDD 0.831), while Pix2Pix [13] is slightly better on FID but still far behind. The Transformer-based MAT [17] improves structural consistency (SSIM 0.9576) but lags in perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.0176) and identity (IDD 0.734). Among diffusion baselines, Palette [28], IR-SDE [24], and Refusion [25] cluster around FID 47.6–49.8 but show notably higher IDD (0.651–0.699), indicating identity drift. On real-world datasets, Diff-SemiER achieves the best FID on FFHQ (66.13 vs. 66.21 for RDDM) and CelebA (61.60 vs. 61.82), while RDDM edges it out by 0.27 on CelebA-HQ (99.53 vs. 99.80), a negligible difference. In the user study, Diff-SemiER is preferred over RDDM by 65–69% and over Resfusion by 68–69% of participants, confirming the quantitative advantage in perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based inpainting for eyeglasses removal (Palette, IR-SDE, RDDM, Resfusion) by replacing binary occlusion masks with a transparency-aware soft mask that models partial lens transmittance. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing a decoupled two-branch architecture where a Generative Prior Diffusion Model (GPDM) trained without the semi-transparent image as condition provides global structural guidance, and (2) a Soft Mask-Aware Adaptive Fusion (SMAF) module that dynamically reweights generative vs. restorative features across channel and spatial dimensions. The transmittance-based data synthesis pipeline, which constructs paired semi-transparent eyeglass images via radial-gradient alpha compositing, is also a new contribution absent from prior eyeglasses-removal datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Semi-transparent sunglasses are a common real-world occlusion that prior binary-mask methods handle poorly, often destroying visible eye details and degrading downstream tasks like face recognition. The 25K-pair synthetic dataset and the soft-mask formulation provide a reusable resource for the community. However, the method is evaluated only at 128×128 resolution, and the authors acknowledge failure under extreme head poses, leaving open questions about scalability to higher resolutions and robustness to pose variation. The modest margins over the strongest baselines (RDDM, Resfusion) suggest the soft-mask fusion, while principled, may not be the only viable path to this improvement.
