## Problem

Existing collaborative perception systems rely on LiDAR or LiDAR-camera fusion, both of which degrade under adverse weather, and their shared vulnerability can cause simultaneous failure. While 4D radar offers weather-robust sensing, naively fusing it with degraded LiDAR in a multi-agent setting introduces cross-modal corruption and information loss, and treating sparse radar identically to dense LiDAR during transmission doubles communication overhead without proportional benefit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263400/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-agent LiDAR point clouds (dense, high-resolution) and 4D radar point clouds (sparse, low data volume) from agents within a 70 m communication range. LiDAR features are encoded via a shared PointPillar backbone (0.4 m voxel resolution) and transmitted as intermediate BEV features, while raw 4D radar point clouds are spatially warped and merged before encoding, exploiting their inherently small data volume. The output is a set of 3D bounding boxes predicted from a fused multi-agent multi-modal BEV representation via a classification and regression head.

## Method

HRCP introduces a hybrid collaboration strategy that transmits LiDAR as intermediate features but 4D radar as raw point clouds (early collaboration), reducing additional communication overhead while preserving fine-grained radar information. The modality fusion is formulated as joint reconstruction of a latent ideal feature rather than simple aggregation, decomposed into two sequential stages: a Bidirectional Cross-Modal Gating (BCMG) module that generates soft attention gates from each modality to validate and suppress unreliable regions in the other via element-wise multiplication, followed by an Adaptive Feature Enhancement (AFE) module that applies sparse channel-wise attention over a concatenated embedding of both gated features and the original radar feature, producing a residual correction term added to the radar-verified LiDAR feature. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with focal loss for classification and smooth L1 loss for regression, without explicit supervision on the latent ideal feature.

## Results

On the V2X-R simulated dataset, HRCP achieves mAP@0.7 of 66.04% under fog and 76.63% under snow, outperforming the strongest single-modal baseline CoAlign (61.62% and 67.54%) by 4.42% and 9.09% respectively. Under clear conditions, HRCP reaches 84.07% mAP@0.7, also surpassing all baselines. On the real-world V2X-Radar-C dataset, HRCP achieves 33.19% mAP@0.7, exceeding the second-best method (L4DR at 30.71%) by 2.48%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRCP outperforms all seven baselines across every weather condition and both datasets. Against the strongest single-modal collaborative method CoAlign, HRCP gains 4.42% and 9.09% in mAP@0.7 under fog and snow respectively, attributed to the LiDAR-4D radar fusion and the BCMG/AFE modules. Compared to the best multi-modal fusion baseline L4DR, HRCP improves mAP@0.7 by 5.10% (fog) and 8.70% (snow), demonstrating that the joint reconstruction formulation and hybrid collaboration strategy are more effective than L4DR's multi-scale gated fusion. The denoising-diffusion-based MDD baseline (the implementation base) is surpassed by 6.52% and 13.32% in mAP@0.7 under fog and snow, confirming that the proposed BCMG+AFE fusion is superior to diffusion-based denoising. On the real-world V2X-Radar-C dataset, HRCP leads the second-best method (L4DR) by 1.53%, 3.68%, and 2.48% on mAP@0.3/0.5/0.7, validating generalizability beyond simulation. InterFusion, the earliest 4D radar-LiDAR fusion method, lags significantly (e.g., 36.49% vs. 66.04% mAP@0.7 under fog), highlighting the gap between naive cross-modal attention and the proposed reliability-aware fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the collaborative 3D object detection line (AttFuse, V2X-ViT, CoBEVT, CoAlign) by introducing 4D radar as a weather-robust complement to LiDAR, building directly on the MDD/V2X-R implementation [11] for the multi-agent fusion backbone. It departs from prior single-agent LiDAR-4D radar fusion methods (InterFusion, L4DR, 3D-LRF) by formulating the fusion problem as joint reconstruction of a latent ideal feature rather than simple feature aggregation, and by adopting a hybrid collaboration strategy—early collaboration for sparse 4D radar and intermediate collaboration for dense LiDAR—that prior multi-agent works have not explored. The BCMG and AFE modules represent a new architectural contribution within this extended setting, combining bidirectional cross-modal gating with sparse channel-wise residual refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: collaborative perception systems that rely on LiDAR or LiDAR-camera fusion degrade simultaneously under adverse weather, creating a single point of failure. The hybrid collaboration strategy offers a concrete communication-efficiency insight (transmitting raw sparse radar points is cheaper than encoded features), and the reconstruction-based fusion formulation provides a principled alternative to naive concatenation. However, the approach still incurs additional communication overhead from 4D radar, and the authors acknowledge the need for a joint representation to better balance accuracy and bandwidth. Open questions include whether the reconstruction formulation generalizes to other sensor pairs or degradation types beyond fog and snow, and whether the gains hold with different backbones or at larger scale.
