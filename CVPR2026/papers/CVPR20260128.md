## Problem

Latent action learning from large-scale videos assumes all inter-frame changes are agent-caused, but real-world videos contain action-irrelevant distractors (moving backgrounds, stochastic dynamics) that corrupt learned representations. Existing remedies rely on action supervision, which becomes unstable and prone to overfitting when labels are extremely scarce relative to available video data, causing the model to drift toward spurious visual correlations rather than physically meaningful motion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive RGB observations (o_t, o_{t+1}) encoded into a shared feature space via DINOv2, along with language instructions for the downstream policy. Optical flow pseudo-labels are generated by RAFT and converted from (u,v) vectors into RGB format via HSV color mapping (direction→Hue, magnitude→Saturation/Value) so they can be processed by the same visual encoder as images; in dynamic-distractor settings, LangSAM masks restrict flow to the agent. The output is a 128-dimensional continuous latent action vector that approximates the physical action space, ultimately decoded into a physical action via a lightweight MLP for deployment.

## Method

LAOF extends the LAPO autoencoding framework (IDM + FDM) with a dedicated flow decoder that directly maps latent actions to optical flow features in the DINOv2 state space, imposing a pixel-level physical motion constraint via an L2 loss against RAFT-generated pseudo-labels. The training follows a three-stage pipeline: pre-training jointly minimizes next-state reconstruction and flow reconstruction losses; distillation transfers IDM representations into a deployable policy via behavior cloning on latent-labeled data; and fine-tuning trains an action decoder on a small labeled set. For sparse-label settings, LAOF-Action adds an action decoder with alternating updates (unlabeled data → flow decoder, labeled data → action decoder) weighted by λ = M/(N+M). Ablations show that a dedicated flow decoder attached directly to latent actions outperforms integrating it into the FDM or conditioning on the current state, because the latter allows state-to-flow shortcuts that weaken the motion constraint on latent actions.

## Results

On LIBERO (continuous actions, imitation learning), LAOF reduces average MSE by 0.069 and improves average success rate by +4.2% over the LAPO baseline without any action labels. With 1% action labels, LAOF-Action further reduces MSE by 0.096 and boosts success rate by +11.5% over LAPO, outperforming the action-supervised LAOM-Action on all four task suites. On PROCGEN (discrete actions, reinforcement learning), LAOF improves average action accuracy by +16.20% and normalized episodic return by +0.16, while LAOF-Action achieves +20.73% accuracy and +0.22 return over LAPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LAPO (the original reconstruction-only baseline), LAOF wins on every LIBERO task suite, reducing MSE by 0.03–0.10 and improving success rate by 2.3–7.3 percentage points, while LAOF-Action extends these gains to 0.07–0.10 MSE reduction and 3.5–19.0 pp success rate improvement. Against CoMo (temporal-difference IDM), LAOF dominates on all four suites; notably, CoMo actually degrades SPATIAL and GOAL performance relative to LAPO, whereas LAOF improves them. Against LAOM-Action (the strongest action-supervised baseline at 1% action ratio), LAOF-Action achieves lower MSE (e.g., 0.076 vs. 0.108 on SPATIAL) and higher success rate (88.2% vs. 86.0%) across all suites. A particularly striking finding is that LAOF without any action supervision matches or surpasses LAOM-Action trained with 1% action labels on most tasks, demonstrating that optical flow pseudo-supervision can substitute for scarce action annotations. On PROCGEN, LAOF-Action similarly outperforms LAOM-Action on all four environments (e.g., 62.75% vs. 52.95% accuracy on CHASER), confirming the advantage in the discrete-action RL setting as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAOF extends the LAPO autoencoding framework (IDM + FDM with reconstruction loss) by adding a dedicated flow decoder that maps latent actions to optical flow, providing a physical motion constraint absent from the original formulation. It also builds on the action-supervised paradigm of LAOM and its successors, which showed that even sparse action labels improve latent action quality, but departs from their alternating labeled/unlabeled training by using optical flow as a continuous pseudo-supervision signal available on all frames. The visual encoder choice (DINOv2) and the object-centric flow extraction via LangSAM follow recent trends in feature-space dynamics modeling and distractor mitigation, while the overall architecture remains a spatial-temporal transformer autoencoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in showing that optical flow—a cheap, label-free signal—can substitute for scarce action annotations, with LAOF matching or exceeding LAOM-Action at 1% action ratio without any action labels. This is directly relevant to the embodied AI community scaling latent action pre-training on large video corpora where action labels are the bottleneck. Key limitations include restriction to 'eye-to-hand' (static-camera) scenarios, dependence on LangSAM's imprecise text-driven segmentation for dynamic-distractor settings, and the observation that optical flow constraints can hurt performance at 100% action ratio due to pseudo-label noise. Extending to 'eye-in-hand' egocentric data, cross-embodiment, and multi-view settings remains open.
