## Problem

Existing sample generation strategies for cross-modal hashing face a fundamental trade-off: interpolation-based methods are deterministic and class-independent, confining synthetic samples to a narrow region around original data and limiting intra-class semantic diversity, while generative network-based methods introduce substantial model complexity and training instability. This paper addresses how to adaptively generate semantically rich synthetic samples that capture intra-class variation without the overhead of a separate generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262641/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB images and text descriptions as input, encoded by 12-block vision and text transformers into continuous embeddings, and produces binary hash codes of configurable length (16–128 bits) via tanh relaxation and sign thresholding. Synthetic samples are generated entirely in the embedding space by sampling from per-class Gaussian distributions (not in pixel or token space), with M=3 synthetic copies per original sample. The framework is plug-and-play, compatible with contrastive, triplet, multi-similarity, and proxy-NCA loss functions without architectural modification.

## Method

IDGH comprises three components operating in the learned embedding space. First, Intra-class Distribution Estimation (IDE) uses maximum likelihood to compute per-class mean vectors and diagonal covariance matrices from the continuous hash embeddings. Second, Neighbor-guided Distribution Refinement (NDR) corrects unreliable covariance estimates for small classes by aggregating weighted covariances from K=20 nearest-neighbor classes (ranked by mean distance), blended with a global covariance via a dynamic coefficient α that decays as per-class sample count increases. Third, Distribution-aware Adaptive Generation (DAG) synthesizes M new samples per original by sampling from N(b̃_i, ηΣ_{l_i}), shifting features along class-specific distribution-guided directions rather than fixed interpolation axes. The covariance matrices are updated every five epochs, and the generation strength η is annealed from 1.0 to 0.6 over training, yielding semantically diverse synthetic positives and negatives that enrich triplet construction without any additional generative network.

## Results

IDGH achieves mAP@ALL of 0.8621 (I→T) and 0.8379 (T→I) at 64 bits on MIRFLICKR-25K, 0.7451/0.7403 on NUS-WIDE, 0.6991/0.6781 on IAPR TC-12, and 0.6591/0.5521 on XMediaNet. Performance improves consistently with bit length, reaching 0.8687/0.8411 at 128 bits on MIRFLICKR-25K. The method also demonstrates plug-and-play compatibility, improving mAP by 1.5–4.0 points when integrated with contrastive, triplet, multi-similarity, and proxy-NCA loss functions. Computational overhead is minimal: IDGH adds only 0.04 h training time and 2.40 s generation time per 100 epochs compared to baselines sharing the same backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDGH achieves the best mAP across nearly all dataset–task–bit-length combinations, with the most substantial gains on XMediaNet (e.g., +9.2 points over DECH at 64-bit I→T) and IAPR TC-12 (e.g., +2.7 points over DECH at 64-bit I→T). On MIRFLICKR-25K, IDGH leads the closest competitor DSPH by 0.8 points (I→T, 64-bit) and DPBE by 0.2 points (T→I, 64-bit). On NUS-WIDE, IDGH surpasses BiLGSEH by 2.3 points (I→T) and 2.0 points (T→I) at 64 bits. Notably, DPBE outperforms IDGH on IAPR TC-12 T→I at 64 and 128 bits (0.7175 vs. 0.6781 and 0.7493 vs. 0.7106), suggesting that the probabilistic binary embedding approach retains an edge in that specific direction. Among generation-based baselines, IDGH consistently outperforms HashGAN, Mixup, and HDML on MIRFLICKR-25K (Table 4), with the largest margin over HashGAN at 128-bit T→I (0.8411 vs. 0.8004). The plug-and-play experiments (Table 3) confirm that IDGH adds 1.5–4.0 mAP points regardless of the underlying loss function, indicating the gains stem from the generation strategy itself rather than a particular loss interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of cross-modal hashing and latent-space sample generation, departing from both interpolation-based methods (CMDA, MixGen) that are deterministic and class-independent, and generative network-based methods (HashGAN, diffusion models) that introduce substantial model complexity. Rather than learning a generative model or performing algebraic interpolation, IDGH repurposes classical statistical tools—maximum likelihood estimation of per-class Gaussian distributions and k-nearest-neighbor covariance aggregation—as a lightweight, plug-and-play augmentation mechanism. The neighbor-guided refinement step is motivated by an empirical observation (validated via Spearman correlation) that classes with similar mean embeddings also share similar covariance structures, a connection not previously exploited in the hashing literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (compatible with contrastive, triplet, multi-similarity, and proxy-NCA losses) makes IDGH a practical drop-in module for existing cross-modal hashing pipelines, and its generation cost (2.4 s per batch) is an order of magnitude lower than GAN-based alternatives while yielding better mAP. The strong few-shot results suggest the distribution-based augmentation is particularly valuable when per-class data is scarce, a common real-world constraint. Open questions include whether the Gaussian MLE assumption holds for highly multimodal or long-tailed class distributions, whether the neighbor-refinement strategy degrades when class boundaries are ambiguous, and whether the approach transfers to other retrieval settings (e.g., video-text or 3D-text) without re-deriving the distribution statistics.
