## Problem

Unsupervised multi-modal anomaly detection under few-shot conditions suffers from the identity shortcut problem, where powerful networks trivially reconstruct anomalous inputs. Recent methods address this with discrete prototypes as an information bottleneck, but hard quantization and isotropic distance metrics fail to capture the continuous, anisotropic structure of normal feature distributions—over-penalizing normal variation along high-variance directions while under-penalizing anomalies along low-variance directions.

## Input / Output

GPFlow takes paired RGB images and surface normal maps (resized to 224×224) as input, processed by two frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 encoders whose multi-scale patch tokens (layers 2–9, class tokens discarded, layers averaged) form the feature space. At inference, it produces a pixel-level anomaly map by computing cosine-similarity discrepancies between encoder features and PMP-constrained coupled reconstructions, smoothed with a 5×5 Gaussian kernel; the image-level score is the maximum value of the merged RGB+shape anomaly map. The method is designed for extreme few-shot regimes (5, 10, or 50 normal samples per class) with per-category training.

## Method

GPFlow models normality per modality as a learnable Gaussian Mixture Model (K=30 prototypes with learnable means, diagonal covariances, and mixture weights) and reconstructs features via an analytical Posterior-Mean Path (PMP) router derived from Tweedie's formula. The PMP router iteratively applies the closed-form GMM posterior mean update (8 steps, τ²=10⁻²), which acts as a covariance-aware information bottleneck: the gain matrix A_k = Σ_k(Σ_k+τ²I)⁻¹ performs anisotropic shrinkage, preserving feature variation along high-variance prototype directions while strongly suppressing deviations along low-variance directions. To exploit complementary cues, GPFlow routes features through both intra-modal and cross-modal pathways under the same PMP router (coupled reconstruction), and applies Instance-Aware Prior Calibration (IPC) at test time—using Mutual Scoring to select the most confident 60% of patches and updating prototype means/covariances via EMA to cover unseen normal variations. Training uses a cosine-similarity soft-mining loss between raw encoder features and the coupled reconstructions, with the PMP router and prototypes as the only learnable parameters.

## Results

Under the challenging 5-shot setting, GPFlow achieves an I-AUROC of 0.912 on MVTec-3D-AD and 0.913 on Eyecandies, outperforming the strongest baseline FIND (0.899 and 0.840 respectively). In the 10-shot setting, GPFlow reaches 0.935 I-AUROC on MVTec-3D-AD and 0.923 on Eyecandies, maintaining a clear lead over all baselines. In the full-data (ALL) setting, GPFlow achieves 0.970 I-AUROC on MVTec-3D-AD, slightly trailing FIND (0.978), while on Eyecandies it reaches 0.942 versus FIND's 0.955. GPFlow also requires only 129 GFLOPs per forward pass (18% of FIND's 728 GFLOPs) with a latency of 69 ms at batch size 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPFlow consistently outperforms all six baselines in the main comparison table (Table 1) under few-shot settings (5, 10, 50 shots) on both MVTec-3D-AD and Eyecandies. The closest competitor is FIND [19], which GPFlow surpasses by 1.3 I-AUROC points in the 5-shot MVTec-3D-AD setting (0.912 vs. 0.899) and by a substantial 7.3 points on Eyecandies (0.913 vs. 0.840); however, in the full-data ALL setting, FIND edges out GPFlow on MVTec-3D-AD (0.978 vs. 0.970) and Eyecandies (0.955 vs. 0.942). Memory-based M3DM [41] and alignment-based CFM [6] trail GPFlow by 8–13 I-AUROC points in the 5-shot regime, while reconstruction-based 3D-ADNAS [22] and the older methods BTF [10] and AST [32] are significantly weaker (0.67–0.83 I-AUROC in 5-shot). In the ablation comparison (Table 3), GPFlow's Gaussian+PMP router also outperforms the supervised DPDL [40] (Gaussian+Schrödinger Bridge) by 2.7 I-AUROC points (0.935 vs. 0.908) and discrete VQ-codebook variants by 5–18 points, confirming the advantage of continuous Gaussian prototypes with covariance-aware routing over both discrete and diffusion-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPFlow extends the prototype-based reconstruction paradigm for multimodal anomaly detection (HVQ-Trans, INP-Former, PIRN) by replacing discrete codebook quantization with learnable Gaussian mixture prototypes, and draws on score-matching / Tweedie's formula literature to derive an analytical Posterior-Mean Path router that performs covariance-aware anisotropic shrinkage. It departs from the Schrödinger Bridge diffusion approach of DPDL by using a fixed-noise, closed-form GMM posterior mean instead of a learned stochastic process, and builds on the coupled reconstruction framework of FIND by routing both intra-modal and cross-modal features through the same PMP bottleneck. The instance-aware prior calibration is inspired by INP-Former's on-the-fly prototype derivation but operates in a probabilistic (GMM) rather than discrete space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple analytical GMM posterior-mean update can serve as a strong, interpretable information bottleneck for anomaly detection, outperforming both discrete prototype routing and a learned Schrödinger Bridge diffusion process while using only 18% of FIND's FLOPs. This is practically significant for industrial inspection where few normal samples are available and computational budgets are tight. However, GPFlow trails FIND in the full-data regime (0.970 vs. 0.978 I-AUROC on MVTec-3D-AD), suggesting the Gaussian inductive bias may cap performance when data is abundant. Open questions include whether full (non-diagonal) covariance matrices, more modalities, or adaptive noise schedules could close this gap, and whether the approach transfers to 2D-only or video anomaly detection settings.
