## Problem

Discriminative vision foundation models and generative models learn fundamentally misaligned semantic representations, and prior unified approaches treat this gap as a byproduct of distillation or weight initialization rather than addressing it directly. No existing self-supervised framework explicitly reconciles both semantic paradigms within a single latent space without relying on frozen teacher models, data augmentations, or online tokenizers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260550/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes precomputed discrete token sequences rather than raw pixels: each image is encoded once into a generative token sequence (VQGAN indices) and a position-aligned discriminative token sequence (k-means cluster IDs from DINOv2 patch features). During training, a variable fraction (50–100%, mean 69%) of generative tokens are masked and dropped, and the encoder processes only the surviving tokens. The unified latent representation supports linear probing for classification, unconditional/conditional image generation (via the decoder), and downstream transfer; for conditional generation a CLIP class embedding is appended to the decoder input.

## Method

LEASE pairs a generative codebook (unsupervised VQGAN) with a discriminative codebook (k-means centroids over DINOv2 patch features) and trains a single ViT-Base encoder–decoder on two jointly optimized objectives. The generative objective is a masked-token reconstruction loss: masked tokens are predicted by a canvas-based decoder that interleaves visible latent embeddings with a [CLS] template. The discriminative objective is a novel codebook contrast loss that, for each unmasked token, retrieves its corresponding discriminative centroid plus K_sel nearest-neighbor centroids as adaptively weighted positives (weights from a softmax over cosine similarities with τ=0.1) and treats all remaining codebook centroids as negatives, eliminating batch-dependent sampling noise. Because both objectives operate entirely on precomputed integer tokens, LEASE requires no online tokenizer, no data augmentations, no dual-encoder architecture, and no frozen VFM during training, yielding a 48.7% speedup over MAGE and 8.75% over Sorcen.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, LEASE (ViT-B) achieves 76.7% linear probing accuracy, FID 9.62, and IS 91.78 for unconditional generation, establishing a new Pareto-front for unified SSL models. It outperforms the prior unified SoTA methods MAGE (74.7% LP, FID 11.1, IS 81.17) and Sorcen (75.1% LP, FID 9.61, IS 90.96) on both discriminative and generative metrics. In few-shot settings, LEASE averages 59.65% top-1 accuracy (5/10/13/25 shots), surpassing MAGE by 2.32% and Sorcen by 0.56%. LEASE also trains 48.7% faster than MAGE and 8.75% faster than Sorcen under identical hardware.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified SSL baselines, LEASE beats MAGE on all three metrics (LP +2.0%, FID −1.5, IS +10.6) and Sorcen on LP (+1.6%) and IS (+0.82) while matching FID (9.62 vs 9.61). MAGE-C achieves higher LP (78.2%) but sacrifices generation quality severely (FID 31.8, IS 37.40), confirming that its contrastive objective prioritizes discrimination at the expense of unification. Among specialized generative models, ADDP achieves a lower FID (8.9) and higher IS (95.32) but its LP is only 11.5%, far below LEASE's 76.7%; DiT and l-DAE trail LEASE on LP (62.5% and 69.6% respectively). Among MIM methods, LEASE outperforms the strongest (Ge2-AE at 75.3% LP) by 1.4% while also providing competitive generation. In the DINOv2-based comparison (Table 6), LEASE surpasses REPA (68.2% LP), VFMTok (69.4% LP), and DiGIT (71.7% LP, IS 73.85) on both discriminative and generative metrics, and outperforms MergeVQ (77.9% LP) in fine-tuning (82.7% vs 82.0%) while matching its FID in conditional generation (3.72 vs 3.25) with fewer parameters (196M vs 343M).

## Relation to Prior Work

LEASE builds on the VQGAN-based unified SSL paradigm established by MAGE (for the masked-token reconstruction canvas and ViT-Base architecture) and DiGIT (for constructing a discriminative codebook via k-means clustering over DINOv2 patch features). Its key departure is replacing the VFM-distillation or dual-encoder strategies used by REPA, SVG, Sorcen, and MergeVQ with a single-encoder design where a paired generative-discriminative codebook provides both reconstruction targets and contrastive supervision. The novel codebook contrast objective eliminates batch-dependent negatives by using all non-selected centroids as negatives, drawing conceptually from nearest-neighbor contrastive learning (NNCLR) but operating entirely in a precomputed discrete semantic space rather than in continuous feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing the generative-discriminative gap as a semantic misalignment addressable through paired codebooks, LEASE offers a clean, efficient recipe (no online tokenizer, no augmentations, no frozen teacher, single forward pass) that achieves new SOTA unified SSL performance with ~49% training speedup over MAGE. The notably stronger robustness results (e.g., 21.6% vs. 13.5% on ObjectNet) suggest the codebook contrast produces more stable features than reconstruction alone. Open questions include whether the framework extends to text-conditioned or video settings, whether the tokenizer can be bypassed at inference, and whether the codebook-contrast idea generalizes beyond ImageNet-scale datasets where k-means centroids remain semantically meaningful.
