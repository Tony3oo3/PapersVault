## Problem

Existing SDS-based text-to-3D methods produce severe view-inconsistencies (Janus artifacts) and anatomically incorrect structures when applied to hands, because the probability landscape defined by a text prompt contains many plausible modes due to large variations in hand articulations and camera poses. No prior method enables zero-shot 3D hand model generation directly from text prompts, leaving a gap for customizable hand avatars in VR and gaming applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262398/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing the desired hand appearance and a set of MANO hand pose parameters that define the target articulation. The MANO initialization stage (~15 min) is prompt-agnostic and reusable across prompts, while the SDS generation stage (~45 min on an RTX A6000, ~30 GB VRAM) produces a NeRF-based 3D hand model. The NeRF can be exported to a ~300k-vertex mesh with a rigged hand skeleton for downstream articulation and animation via Threestudio.

## Method

HandDreamer is a two-stage pipeline: (1) NeRF volumetric density is initialized by minimizing L2 distance between rendered opacity maps and MANO silhouette masks, providing a low-score geometric prior that ensures all views start near the correct mode; (2) SDS optimization is conditioned on 2D hand skeleton projections via a ControlNet, which embeds both viewpoint and hand pose to reduce the number of probable modes in the diffusion model's probability landscape. A novel Corrective Hand Shape (CHS) loss is applied throughout SDS, computing the L2 distance between NeRF opacity and MANO silhouette with a timestep-annealed weight (λ_max=15000 at high noise, λ_min=1000 at low noise) to prevent geometric degradation from finger self-occlusion, particularly in side views. The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) showing that minimizing the expected absolute score requires the initial model to be semantically close to the view-consistent ground truth, motivating the MANO initialization over standard spherical initialization.

## Results

On a test set of 5,400 rendered images from 45 generated 3D hand models, HandDreamer achieves a CLIP L14 score of 28.63, FID of 254.62, and HPSv2 of 0.241, outperforming all 9 baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, CFD (CLIP 26.62, FID 262.83, HPSv2 0.223), trails by 2.01 CLIP points, 8.21 FID, and 0.018 HPSv2. A user study with 50 raters across 30 prompts confirms HandDreamer is preferred on geometry, texture, and consistency over all 8 compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HandDreamer outperforms all 11 baselines on all three metrics (CLIP L14, FID, HPSv2). Among general text-to-3D methods, CFD [55] is the strongest competitor (CLIP 26.62, FID 262.83, HPSv2 0.223) but still produces anatomically incorrect hands with wrong finger counts; ProlificDreamer [49] and ESD [48] suffer from severe Janus artifacts with fingers protruding at unnatural locations. Text-to-human methods (DreamWaltz [16], DreamAvatar [2], HumanNorm [14]) generate hand regions with very little detail, scoring 4–9 CLIP points below HandDreamer. The one-shot image-based method OHTA [61] performs worst overall (FID 467.51) and fails to generalize to fictional or non-realistic hand prompts, producing painted-looking avatars. DreamFusion [34] and LatentNerf [28] lag by 3.5 and 4.3 CLIP points respectively, reflecting their lack of hand-specific geometric priors. The user study (50 raters, 30 prompts) further confirms HandDreamer's superiority, scoring highest on geometry, texture, and view consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

HandDreamer builds directly on the Score Distillation Sampling framework from DreamFusion, adopting its SDS gradient formulation and the HiFA timestep annealing schedule, while introducing a ControlNet-based skeleton conditioning to reduce the number of probable modes in the diffusion model's probability landscape. It departs from prior text-to-3D work by providing a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) that reframes view-inconsistencies as a mode-ambiguity problem rather than the previously assumed mode-collapse problem, and by leveraging the MANO parametric hand model both as a geometric initialization and as a continuous shape prior through the novel Corrective Hand Shape loss. The method also distinguishes itself from text-to-3D human avatar approaches (DreamWaltz, DreamAvatar, HumanNorm), which treat hands as a minor region of the full body, and from image-to-3D hand methods (OHTA), which require a reference image and fail on fictional hands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first zero-shot text-to-3D hand generation method, filling a gap that is practically important for VR, gaming, and digital avatar creation where users need customizable hand models without expensive multi-view capture. The theoretical characterization of why SDS fails for highly articulatable objects (mode ambiguity in the probability landscape) provides a principled design guideline that could transfer to other complex, pose-variant 3D generation tasks. However, the method inherits biases from the pretrained Stable Diffusion model, and articulation of generated hands still requires manual mesh export and rigging rather than being natively supported, leaving automated articulation as an open problem.
