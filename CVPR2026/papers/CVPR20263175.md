## Problem

Standard attention in vision and diffusion transformers scales quadratically with sequence length, creating a major computational bottleneck. Existing quantization approaches for attention typically stop at 8-bit or 4-bit precision, as reducing to 1-bit (binary) was considered infeasible due to extreme information loss and optimization instability. This paper asks whether the essential similarity relationships in attention can be preserved under 1-bit quantization of queries and keys, and if so, how to make it practically effective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BinaryAttention is a drop-in replacement for the standard softmax attention module inside ViT and DiT/SiT architectures, so the model's input and output interfaces remain unchanged: RGB images at task-specific resolutions (224×224 or 384×384 for classification, 1024×1024 for detection, 512×2048 for segmentation, 256×256 for generation) produce the corresponding task outputs. The only architectural modification is within the attention computation itself—queries and keys are projected to 1-bit representations before the QK^T dot product, while the rest of the transformer block (MLP, layer norm, residual connections) operates in full precision. A learnable bias matrix of shape [N×N] (or position/context-dependent variants) is the sole added parameter.

## Method

BinaryAttention replaces the floating-point QK^T dot product with a 1-bit binary computation: queries and keys are quantized via a scaled sign function (s_i' = μ_q·sign(q_i), t_j' = μ_k·sign(k_j)), and the similarity is computed as μ_q·μ_k·s_i^T·t_j using hardware XNOR and popcount instructions. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) shows that the outer product of binary queries and keys is a consistent estimator of the original covariance structure, guaranteeing that Hamming-space distance mirrors Euclidean distance and cosine similarity is preserved. To counteract the flattened attention distribution caused by discarding magnitude information, a learnable bias term b_ij is added to the attention scores, which can be dense, position-sensitive, or context-aware. The attention coefficients P and values V are further quantized to 8-bit (unsigned for P, channel-wise for V) to enable end-to-end integer arithmetic. The full kernel is implemented on A100 Tensor Cores using mma.s32.b1.b1.s32 for QK^T and mma.s32.u8.s8.s32 for PV, building on FlashAttention2's tiling strategy, and the model is trained with quantization-aware training plus self-distillation from a full-precision teacher to align binary similarity with full-precision similarity.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, BinaryAttention-B achieves 82.04% top-1 accuracy at 224×224 and 83.64% at 384×384, surpassing full-precision DeiT-B (81.8% / 83.1%) and SageAttention-B (81.83% / 82.89%) while using fewer OPs (17.0G / 50.2G vs. 17.6G / 55.4G). On COCO detection (Mask R-CNN), BinaryAttention-B reaches 48.28 box mAP and 43.24 mask mAP, exceeding DeiT-B (47.99 / 42.98) with 10% fewer OPs. On ADE20K segmentation, BinaryAttention-B achieves 47.76 single-scale mIoU, outperforming DeiT-B by 0.90 mIoU. For image generation (DiT-XL/2, cfg=1.50), BinaryAttention attains the lowest FID of 2.19, matching or exceeding FlashAttention2 (2.27) with fewer training steps (4000K vs. 7000K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against full-precision DeiT (FlashAttention2), BinaryAttention wins on all model sizes: +0.68% top-1 at T, +0.44% at S, +0.24% at B (224²), and +0.54% at B (384²), while also reducing OPs. Against SageAttention (INT8/INT4 attention quantization), BinaryAttention consistently outperforms by 0.77% (T), 0.42% (S), 0.21% (B at 224²), and 0.75% (B at 384²) top-1 accuracy with fewer OPs. Against W8A8 quantization methods (PTQ4ViT, I-ViT), BinaryAttention achieves higher accuracy (e.g., 82.04% vs. 81.5%/81.7% at B) but at higher OPs; however, combining BinaryAttention with PTQ4ViT yields 83.55% at 384² with only 13.5G OPs, beating SageAttention+PTQ4ViT (82.98%) by 0.57% with 3G fewer OPs. Against linear attention and SSM baselines (Flatten-T, InLine-T, Vim-T), some methods achieve comparable or slightly higher accuracy at the tiny scale, but BinaryAttention scales robustly to S and B sizes without architectural changes, whereas InLine-S/B requires larger inputs and Vim-S/B demands significantly more parameters. For image generation, BinaryAttention matches or exceeds FlashAttention2 and SageAttention on FID for DiT-XL/2 at cfg=1.50 (2.19 vs. 2.27) while using fewer training steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the attention quantization line initiated by SageAttention (INT8→INT4→FP4) by pushing QK computation to the extreme of 1-bit binary representations, a regime prior work considered infeasible due to 'abrupt performance degradation.' It builds directly on FlashAttention2's IO-aware tiling and parallelism strategy for the hardware kernel, while introducing a learnable bias term and a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1, linking binary sign products to the original covariance structure) that has no direct analogue in prior quantization literature. The method is conceptually distinct from architecture-level alternatives (linear attention, sparse attention, SSMs) because it preserves the standard softmax attention formulation and requires no architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2× inference speedup over FlashAttention2 with matching or slightly better accuracy across classification, detection, segmentation, and diffusion-based generation is a practically significant efficiency win that could reshape how attention is deployed at scale. The theoretical grounding (covariance preservation under sign quantization) gives practitioners confidence beyond empirical validation. Key limitations remain: the PV multiplication still uses conservative 8-bit quantization, MLP layers are untouched, and the 'exceeds full-precision' claims are often marginal (≤0.6% top-1) and task-dependent. Open questions include whether the binary QK formulation transfers to language models, whether sub-8-bit PV is viable, and whether the learnable bias can be made more parameter-efficient for very long sequences.
