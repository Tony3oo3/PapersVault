## Problem

Estimating the 3D pose of a known rigid object from its 2D silhouette alone (Pose-from-Silhouette, PfS) has no globally optimal solution for general shapes. Existing approaches are either local methods requiring an initial pose, stochastic optimizers without optimality guarantees, or restricted to special shape classes (ellipsoids, surfaces of revolution, cylinders). This paper provides the first globally optimal PfS solution valid for arbitrary shapes irrespective of convexity or genus.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a 2D unoccluded silhouette (an ordered sequence of boundary points in the image plane) and a 3D object template (a triangulated mesh or semi-dense point cloud of ~29k surface points). For the perspective variant, a coarse depth prior (e.g., from RGB-D or monocular estimation) is additionally required. The output is a full 6-DoF pose: a rotation matrix in SO(3) and a 2D translation (orthographic) or 3D translation (perspective), with the guarantee that the solution is within ε_o of the global optimum of the Hausdorff-distance objective, where ε_o → 0 as sampling thresholds are refined.

## Method

The key insight is that the Area-of-Silhouette (AoS) is Lipschitz-continuous with respect to rotation (Theorem 1), enabling a pre-computed Projected Area Response Surface (PARS) over the Postel disc — a 2D cross-section of the SO(3) rotation space that eliminates Z-axis redundancy. At query time, the input silhouette's area is intersected with the PARS via marching squares to extract candidate rotation directions, and a second signature (the aspect ratio of an algebraically fitted ellipse, PEARS) further branches the search. A resolution-guided candidate search then filters candidates by 1D projection lengths along Z-axis rotations, yielding a small feasible set over which exhaustive Hausdorff-distance evaluation recovers the globally optimal pose (Theorem 2: ε_o-global optimality). A final non-linear refinement on the SE(3) manifold via tangent-plane steps and retraction polishes the discrete solution to sub-degree accuracy.

## Results

On orthographic silhouettes (Table 1), GlOptiPoS+ achieves mean orientation error (OE) of 0.32°, 0.61°, and 0.50° on Stanford Bunny, Phlegmatic Dragon, and Pelvic Bone respectively, with mean RMSE below 1% of bounding-box diagonal in all cases. The closest baseline STI-Pose_Π_O yields mean OE of 3.12°, 4.29°, and 3.47° on the same shapes, with RMSE up to 101.55% for PD. On the 20-object BcOT perspective benchmark (Table 2), GlOptiPoS_Π+ achieves mean RMSE as low as 0.71 mm (Stitch) and 0.72 mm (Cat), while STI-Pose-A and STI-Pose-B produce mean RMSE of 29–111 mm and 10–38 mm respectively. The paper reports 89.74%, 85.78%, and 85.59% improvement in mean OE over STI-Pose_Π_O on SB, PD, and PB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: NlR (Levenberg-Marquardt local refinement), Nl-PaR (iterative project-and-refine), Ms-GO (multi-start global optimization), and STI-Pose_Π_O (orthographic adaptation of the closest recent silhouette-based method [Cui et al., ICCVM 2024]). GlOptiPoS+ dominates all four on every metric and every shape: mean OE is 0.32–0.61° versus 3.12–4.29° for STI-Pose_Π_O and 33–56° for the local/multi-start methods. STI-Pose_Π_O is the second-best method overall but suffers from stochastic failures with maximum OE exceeding 110° (practically unusable), whereas GlOptiPoS+ worst-case OE is ~8.6° (PB, attributed to numerical artefacts). On the perspective BcOT benchmark, GlOptiPoS_Π+ is the best method across all 20 objects on RMSE, OE, and TE, with STI-Pose-B and GlOptiPoS_Π±8 splitting second place; STI-Pose-A (using the original authors' wide depth bounds of [5,100] cm) lags significantly, confirming its sensitivity to inaccurate depth priors. The local methods (NlR, Nl-PaR) and multi-start method (Ms-GO) are consistently 30–50× worse in OE, underscoring the value of the globally optimal branching strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Hartley and Kahl's [17] framework for global optimization through rotation space search, repurposing it for the silhouette-only setting by exploiting the Lipschitz continuity of the Area-of-Silhouette as a branching function over SO(3). It departs from the broader silhouette-based pose estimation literature (contour generators [25], texture cues [7,10,32], silhouette-lookup [20,21]) by discarding all correspondence and appearance assumptions, and from the closest prior method STI-Pose [10] by replacing stochastic PSO with a deterministic, resolution-guided candidate search that carries a formal ε-global optimality guarantee. The Postel projection reparametrisation and the two-stage PARS/PEARS intersection strategy are novel algorithmic contributions that make the branching tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first globally optimal solution to the correspondence-free Pose-from-Silhouette problem for arbitrary shapes, filling a gap that has persisted despite silhouettes being a primary visual cue in robotics, AR, medical imaging, and autonomous driving. The practical limitation is that the method requires a known 3D template and unoccluded silhouettes, and for symmetric objects the solution is inherently ambiguous (a geometric truism the paper acknowledges). Open questions include whether the approach extends to partially occluded or multi-object scenes, whether the depth-prior requirement for perspective can be eliminated, and whether the BnB-style runtime can be made competitive with real-time deep-learning pipelines for deployment.
