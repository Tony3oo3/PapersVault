## Problem

Lightweight few-shot CLIP adapters (e.g., Tip-Adapter) achieve fast inference but rely on a single spatially-averaged global descriptor, leaving fine-grained patch-level evidence and its structural alignment with class text under-exploited. Patch-level adapters that reason over visual tokens improve detail sensitivity but introduce permanent GNN or extra-token computation at test time and are often unimodal. The paper targets the gap of injecting fine-grained, cross-modal relational reasoning into a lightweight adapter without any additional inference cost.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes K-shot support images (C classes, N=K×C samples) and class text prompts; the teacher additionally extracts 18 multi-scale patches per image (global view, 3×3 grid, 2×2 grid, vertical halves, horizontal halves), each resized to 224×224 and encoded by frozen CLIP. At test time, only a single global CLIP feature per query image is used, and the final prediction is L_ZS + α·L_Cache — architecturally identical to Tip-Adapter-F with zero extra latency or memory. The output is a class label over C categories via cosine similarity between the adapted feature and text prompt embeddings.

## Method

TOGA introduces an asymmetric, training-only distillation framework where a high-capacity Heterogeneous Image-Patch-Text Graph Teacher supervises a lightweight Tip-Adapter student (key-value cache) and is entirely discarded at inference. The teacher builds a unified graph whose nodes are 18 multi-scale visual patches and C text prompts, connected by three edge types (patch↔patch/image, patch↔text, text↔patch); a Modality-aware Graph Transformer (MGT) performs type-specific, relation-aware multi-head attention with learned relation-level scaling coefficients to capture cross-modal fine-grained correlations. A Discriminative Node Filtering step (learned Top-N scoring) selects the most informative patches to form the teacher's visual embedding, avoiding dilution from background noise. Training uses a dual-objective loss: cross-entropy on the combined ZS+Cache+Graph logits (updating both student and teacher) plus a Focal Loss applied to the teacher logits alone, which down-weights easy examples and forces the teacher to focus on hard, fine-grained cases. At deployment, the entire teacher stack is removed and inference follows the standard Tip-Adapter path, preserving its parameter count and latency while benefiting from the relational knowledge distilled into the cache.

## Results

TOGA establishes a new state-of-the-art across all 11 few-shot benchmarks (1–16 shots), achieving an average accuracy of 72.2% (1-shot), 75.0% (2-shot), 77.9% (4-shot), 80.0% (8-shot), and 82.3% (16-shot). The largest gains appear in the extreme low-shot regime, where TOGA outperforms the nearest competitor CCA by +5.9% (1-shot) and +6.1% (2-shot), and on fine-grained FGVC-Aircraft by +8.5–9.8% over CCA. On OOD robustness (ImageNet + 4 shifted variants), TOGA achieves the best average of 63.1%, improving zero-shot CLIP by +4.0% and outperforming the strongest prompt-learning baseline MaPLe (62.4%) by +0.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOGA outperforms all 13 baselines across every shot setting and dataset. Against the closest few-shot adapter competitor CCA (ICCV'25), TOGA leads by +5.9% average at 1-shot (72.2 vs 66.3), +6.1% at 2-shot (75.0 vs 68.9), and +4.7% at 16-shot (82.3 vs 77.6), with the gap widening on fine-grained FGVC-Aircraft (+8.5% at 1-shot, +9.8% at 2-shot). Compared to the patch-level GraphAdapter (NeurIPS'23), which requires permanent GNN computation at test time, TOGA achieves +7.4% average at 1-shot (72.2 vs 64.8) and +6.1% at 16-shot (82.3 vs 76.2) while retaining zero extra inference latency. The lightweight TIP-Adapter-F (ECCV'22) and TaskRes (CVPR'23) baselines trail by 7.6% and 7.9% respectively at 1-shot, confirming that global-feature-only adapters leave substantial fine-grained signal unexploited. On OOD robustness (Table 2), TOGA achieves the best average of 63.1% across ImageNet and four shifted variants, edging out MaPLe (62.4%), KgCoOp (62.3%), and CoCoOp (62.1%), while improving over zero-shot CLIP by +4.0%. The only dataset where TOGA does not lead by a wide margin is ImageNet-A (49.8%), where it is competitive with MaPLe (50.9) and KgCoOp (50.7) but slightly behind, suggesting adversarial-style shifts remain challenging even with graph-based supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two PEFT strands for CLIP: lightweight cache-based adapters (Tip-Adapter, CLIP-Adapter, TaskRes) that are fast but operate on a single global feature, and patch/graph-level adapters (GraphAdapter, VPT) that reason over fine-grained structure but incur permanent inference overhead. It departs from both by introducing an asymmetric training-only distillation scheme: a high-capacity heterogeneous image-patch-text graph teacher (using a Modality-aware Graph Transformer) supervises the Tip-Adapter's key-value cache during training, then is entirely discarded at test time. Unlike prior knowledge distillation in VLMs that typically transfers soft logits or uses offline teachers, this work directly supervises the cache keys and values with a Focal Loss regularizer, making the distillation target the prototype memory itself rather than a logit distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'train heavy, test light' paradigm offers a practical resolution to the performance-latency tradeoff that has constrained PEFT for VLMs: practitioners can now benefit from graph-level cross-modal reasoning without carrying GNN or extra-token computation at deployment. The consistent 5–6% average gains over the strongest baseline (CCA) across 11 datasets and all shot settings, with particularly large margins on fine-grained categories (Aircraft: +8.5–9.8%), suggest that relational patch-text knowledge is genuinely underexploited by global-feature adapters. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP ViT-B/16 to larger or non-CLIP backbones, whether the ad-hoc multi-scale patch set (five fixed views) can be made adaptive, and whether the training-only teacher paradigm extends to open-vocabulary or retrieval tasks where the cache mechanism is less natural.
