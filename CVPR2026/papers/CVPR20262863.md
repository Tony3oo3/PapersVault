## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces ghosting artifacts and degraded geometry when training views contain transient objects that violate multi-view consistency. Existing transient-suppression methods face a circular dependency: reliable transient detection requires a well-reconstructed static scene to expose mismatches, yet clean reconstruction itself depends on accurate transient masks, causing errors to reinforce each other when both are optimized jointly from poor initialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262863/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (downsampled 8×, or 4× for the Patio scene), calibrated camera poses, and an initial point cloud, and additionally leverages SAM2 instance proposals, FiT3D view-consistent features, DINOv2 features, and Depth Anything v2 monocular depth predictions as auxiliary signals. It outputs a clean static 3D Gaussian scene, per-pixel transient probability masks from the learned MLP, and photorealistic novel-view renderings. The pipeline is inherently two-pass: the first pass produces a deliberately imperfect reconstruction whose failure patterns are mined before the final optimization begins.

## Method

DualSplat introduces a Failure-to-Prior paradigm that decouples transient discovery from clean scene optimization into two sequential stages. Stage I performs a conservative 3DGS reconstruction (10k iterations) to expose incomplete fragments caused by transients, then converts these failures into object-level pseudo-masks by jointly thresholding instance-averaged photometric residuals (with an adaptive, time-decaying threshold), FiT3D feature cosine similarity, and SAM2 instance boundaries. Stage II (30k iterations) reconstructs the scene under these high-recall pseudo-masks while a lightweight per-pixel MLP refines them online, taking cached DINOv2 features and Depth Anything v2 depth residuals as input. The MLP is trained with a decaying prior loss that anchors it to the pseudo-masks early, then transitions to self-consistency losses (feature cosine similarity between ground-truth and rendered views, plus residual-bound constraints) as geometry stabilizes, effectively converting the temporary prior into an adaptive estimator.

## Results

On the NeRF On-the-go dataset, DualSplat achieves a mean PSNR of 23.42 dB, SSIM of 0.820, and LPIPS of 0.088, outperforming all six baselines across all three metrics. The closest competitor, RobustSplat, scores 23.16 dB PSNR and 0.819 SSIM, while vanilla 3DGS lags far behind at 19.04 dB PSNR. On RobustNeRF, DualSplat also achieves the best average performance, slightly surpassing all baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the paper noting the margins are modest but consistent across all five scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualSplat wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against every baseline on NeRF On-the-go. The largest gap is over vanilla 3DGS (+4.38 dB PSNR), confirming that transient-aware masking is essential. Among the strongest competitors, DualSplat edges out RobustSplat by +0.26 dB PSNR (23.42 vs. 23.16) and +0.001 SSIM, while also slightly improving LPIPS (0.088 vs. 0.089). DeGauss is the second-closest on PSNR (23.26) but trails DualSplat by 0.16 dB and 0.016 SSIM. On RobustNeRF, the margins are smaller—the paper notes DualSplat 'slightly surpasses' all baselines on all three mean metrics—indicating the advantage is most pronounced in transient-heavy scenes (high-occlusion settings) rather than uniformly across all difficulty levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualSplat builds directly on 3DGS as its scene representation and on RobustSplat as its implementation base, inheriting the delayed-densification schedule and progressive MLP training. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant online-suppression paradigm (WildGaussians, DeSplat, SpotLessSplats, RobustSplat) by introducing a two-stage Failure-to-Prior pipeline: a conservative first-pass reconstruction exposes transient-induced failures, which are then consolidated via SAM2 instance boundaries, FiT3D view-consistent features, and photometric residuals into object-level pseudo-masks that guide a clean second-pass optimization. This sequential decoupling contrasts with prior methods that detect and suppress transients within a single coupled optimization loop, and the specific combination of FiT3D for feature consistency (rather than DINOv2 or diffusion features) is a notable design choice validated by ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The circular dependency between transient detection and scene reconstruction is a fundamental bottleneck in in-the-wild 3DGS, and the Failure-to-Prior framing offers a clean conceptual resolution that could transfer to other reconstruction tasks where observation quality and model quality are mutually dependent. However, the approach doubles training cost (two 3DGS passes plus SAM2 inference) and the per-scene MLP limits generalization; the paper also acknowledges a failure mode when transients persist across a large fraction of views. Open questions include whether the pseudo-mask construction can be made more lightweight, whether the two-stage design can be partially parallelized, and whether the paradigm extends to dynamic (rather than merely transient) scenes.
