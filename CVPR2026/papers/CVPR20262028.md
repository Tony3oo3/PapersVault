## Problem

Vision-language models have achieved strong open-ended 2D perception but lack native multi-object 3D detection from monocular images. Existing monocular 3D detectors rely on task-specific heads, closed label spaces, and calibrated cameras, failing to inherit the versatility and instruction-following behavior of VLMs. The paper asks: what is the most VLM-native recipe that makes multi-object monocular 3D detection work as a next-token prediction problem?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262028/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (decomposed into up to 12 adaptive 448px tiles plus a global thumbnail), a free-form text query, and optional visual prompts (2D boxes or click points) as input. It outputs a variable-length autoregressive token sequence where each detected instance is represented as a 2D box immediately followed by its 3D box (center in camera coordinates, dimensions, rotation), serialized from near to far and terminated by an end-of-sequence token. All 3D geometry is expressed in the camera frame rather than a world frame to improve cross-domain generalization.

## Method

The core contribution is Chain-of-Sight (CoS), a decoding scheme that interleaves 2D and 3D predictions per instance so that 2D localization serves as an explicit visual chain-of-thought anchoring subsequent 3D tokens to visible evidence. Across objects, detections are serialized in near-to-far depth order (an ego-centric curriculum that places high-evidence instances first and constrains later geometry via relative scale and occlusion); within each object, the 3D box is factorized as center → size → rotation, ranking parameters by monocular observability. The architecture couples a SigLIP vision encoder to a Qwen2-8B autoregressive decoder via a lightweight MLP projector, trained end-to-end with standard cross-entropy over the token sequence on ~1.74M unified camera-centric examples from six datasets. This design preserves open-vocabulary and visual-prompting capabilities within a single interface without specialized 3D heads, achieving 38.90 AP3D on Omni3D—surpassing the prior best by +13.98 even when the baseline receives ground-truth 2D boxes.

## Results

On the full Omni3D benchmark, LocateAnything3D achieves a state-of-the-art 38.90 AP₃D, surpassing the previous best (DetAny3D with ground-truth 2D boxes at 34.38) by +4.52 absolute points and the prior best without oracle 2D (DetAny3D at 24.92) by +13.98. On the outdoor-only Omni3D_OUT track, it reaches 36.1 AP₃D_out, outperforming DetAny3D (32.2) and even DetAny3D aided by ground-truth 2D boxes (35.9). For zero-shot novel categories, LocateAnything3D leads on all three held-out splits: 25.87 on KITTI, 26.33 on SUN-RGBD, and 29.06 on ARKitScenes, without relying on any external 2D detector.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocateAnything3D outperforms all six baselines on the full Omni3D AP₃D metric. Against the strongest open-vocabulary baseline DetAny3D (24.92 AP₃D), it gains +13.98 points; against DetAny3D augmented with ground-truth 2D boxes (34.38), it still leads by +4.52, demonstrating that jointly learning 2D and 3D in a single autoregressive decoder is more effective than bolting a 3D head onto externally supplied 2D proposals. On the outdoor-only track, LocateAnything3D (36.1) beats DetAny3D (32.2) by +3.9 and DetAny3D w/ GT 2D (35.9) by +0.2. For zero-shot novel categories, LocateAnything3D surpasses DetAny3D+Grounding-DINO by +0.14 (KITTI), +5.26 (SUN-RGBD), and +4.50 (ARKitScenes), and dramatically outperforms OV-Mono3D+Grounding-DINO (e.g., 25.87 vs. 4.71 on KITTI). The closed-vocabulary baselines (Cube R-CNN, ImVoxelNet, SMOKE) are substantially weaker, with AP₃D scores ranging from 9.4 to 23.26, underscoring the advantage of the open-vocabulary, prompt-driven formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of coupling a specialized 3D head to an external open-vocabulary 2D detector (OV-Mono3D, DetAny3D) by recasting multi-object monocular 3D detection as a native next-token prediction problem within a single VLM decoder. The Chain-of-Sight formulation draws a direct analogy to textual chain-of-thought in LLMs, repurposing 2D bounding boxes as an explicit visual intermediate that conditions subsequent 3D tokens, rather than treating 2D as a separate upstream module. The near-to-far inter-object curriculum and center→size→rotation intra-object ordering are novel serialization choices tailored to autoregressive decoding, contrasting with the scanline or arbitrary orderings used in prior VLM-based 2D detectors (Kosmos-2, QwenVL). Conceptually, the work bridges the VLM-for-perception line (unified token-based interfaces) with the monocular 3D detection line (Omni3D, Cube R-CNN) without introducing task-specific heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a VLM can jointly predict 2D and 3D boxes end-to-end and outperform baselines given ground-truth 2D inputs, the paper validates a unified perception interface that simplifies system design for embodied agents and makes 3D supervision scalable with data. The zero-shot generalization to held-out categories (outperforming DetAny3D+Grounding DINO by up to +5.26 AP) suggests the CoS curriculum transfers better than bolting a 3D head onto frozen 2D proposals. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video (temporal consistency), multi-view or stereo settings, and whether the camera-frame parameterization limits deployment in world-frame planning pipelines. The paper is limited to monocular RGB and a single 8B backbone, leaving open whether the CoS principle scales to larger models or benefits from additional modalities (depth, LiDAR).
