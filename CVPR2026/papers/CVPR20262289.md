## Problem

Consistent image generation—preserving identity, style, and logic across multiple images—remains difficult because supervised training lacks large-scale consistency datasets, and existing reward models evaluate aesthetics or prompt alignment but not visual consistency. Applying RL to this task is further hindered by the absence of a dedicated consistency reward model and by the high computational cost and multi-reward instability of current RL algorithms for multi-image generation.

## Input / Output

For Text-to-ImageSet, a single text prompt produces a 2×2 grid of coherent sub-images; for Image Editing, a reference image plus a text instruction yields an edited image that preserves the reference's identity. The reward model consumes two images and a shared prompt, outputting a scalar consistency score derived from the probability of the 'Yes' token in a VLM's autoregressive output. During RL training, the generator operates at reduced resolution (512×512 per sub-image) for reward computation, while full-resolution (1024×1024) generation is reserved for inference and evaluation.

## Method

PaCo-Reward reformulates pairwise consistency evaluation as a generative next-token prediction task on Qwen2.5-VL-7B: the model outputs a binary 'Yes'/'No' answer followed by a CoT reasoning sequence, with the 'Yes' token probability serving as the consistency score. Training uses a weighted likelihood objective (α=0.1) that emphasizes the decision token over reasoning tokens, and is supervised on 54,624 human-annotated pairs constructed via sub-figure combinatorial pairing of 2×2 FLUX.1-dev grids. PaCo-GRPO extends Flow-GRPO's SDE-based GRPO with two efficiency/stability strategies: (1) resolution-decoupled training, which samples low-resolution images during RL to cut quadratic Transformer cost while preserving full-resolution inference, and (2) log-tamed multi-reward aggregation, which applies a log(1+R) transformation to any reward whose coefficient of variation exceeds a threshold δ, adaptively compressing high-variance signals to prevent reward domination and keep the consistency-to-prompt-alignment ratio below 1.8.

## Results

On T2IS-Bench, FLUX.1-dev+PaCo-Reward-7B achieves an average score of 0.576/0.757 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B/Gemma-3-4B evaluators), outperforming the strongest open-source baseline AutoT2IS (0.515/0.686) by 0.117/0.103 in visual consistency. On ConsistencyRank, PaCo-Reward-7B reaches 0.449 Accuracy and 0.288 Spearman's ρ, surpassing the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.344/0.138) by 10.5% and 0.150 respectively. On EditReward-Bench, PaCo-Reward-7B attains 0.751 Overall accuracy, matching GPT-5 (0.755) while outperforming all open-source baselines including EditScore-72B (0.703). On GEdit-Bench, integrating PaCo-Reward-7B improves Qwen-Image-Edit's Overall score from 7.307 to 7.451 (EN-I) and FLUX.1-Kontext's from 6.143 to 6.636.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On T2IS-Bench, FLUX.1-dev+PaCo-Reward-7B (0.576/0.757 Avg) substantially outperforms all open-source baselines, beating AutoT2IS (0.515/0.686) by 0.117/0.103 and Gemini&FLUX.1-dev (0.490/0.659) by 0.086/0.098 in visual consistency, while approaching closed-source GPT-4o (0.501/0.697) and Seedream 4.0 (0.589/0.758). On EditReward-Bench, PaCo-Reward-7B (0.751 Overall) matches GPT-5 (0.755) within 0.4% and clearly surpasses GPT-4.1 (0.705), Gemini2.5-Pro (0.722), and all Qwen2.5-VL variants (0.432–0.621) and EditScore models (0.659–0.703). On ConsistencyRank, PaCo-Reward-7B (0.449 Acc, 0.288 ρ) outperforms CLIP-I (0.394/0.206), DreamSim (0.403/0.214), InternVL3.5-8B (0.359/0.176), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.344/0.138) by 8.2–10.5% in accuracy. On GEdit-Bench, PaCo-Reward-7B consistently improves both SC and PQ over its base models (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit Overall 7.307→7.451, FLUX.1-Kontext 6.143→6.636), whereas EditReward [77] improves SC but degrades PQ for Step1X-Edit (PQ 6.962→6.946). The only notable loss is on T2IS-Bench prompt alignment metrics, where Seedream 4.0 (0.813 Entity) and AutoT2IS+GPT-4o (0.763 Relation) still lead, reflecting PaCo-RL's focus on consistency over raw prompt fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm for image generation established by Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO, which introduced SDE sampling to flow-matching models, by adding two targeted strategies: resolution-decoupled training (inspired by FlowGRPO's observation that low-quality images still yield useful reward signals) and log-tamed multi-reward aggregation to address reward domination in multi-objective optimization. On the reward side, it departs from existing VLM-based reward models (EditScore, EditReward, HPSv3) that focus on aesthetics and prompt alignment, instead formulating consistency evaluation as a generative pairwise comparison task aligned with the VLM's next-token prediction. The paper also unifies two previously separate task families—Text-to-ImageSet generation and Image Editing—under a single 'Consistent Image Generation' framework, enabling a shared reward model and RL pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3.5-8B) perform worse than simple similarity metrics (CLIP-I, DreamSim) on consistency ranking challenges the assumption that more capable vision-language models automatically better capture nuanced human perceptual preferences, highlighting the need for task-specific reward modeling. The resolution-decoupled training strategy is practically significant for making RL feasible on multi-image, high-resolution generation tasks where computational cost is the primary bottleneck, and it composes with existing efficiency techniques like MixGRPO. Open questions include whether the pairwise formulation generalizes to larger image sets beyond 2×2 grids, whether the approach transfers to video consistency, and whether the log-tamed aggregation threshold requires re-tuning across different reward model pairs.
