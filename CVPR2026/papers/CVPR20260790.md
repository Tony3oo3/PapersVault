## Problem

Existing multimodal flow estimation methods fuse image, LiDAR, and event features in separate heterogeneous spaces, requiring complex pairwise alignment modules at every stage. This design causes information erosion from delayed fusion, high architectural complexity, and fragility under sensor degradation (extreme exposure, LiDAR sparsity). The paper identifies the absence of a shared homogeneous latent space where all modalities can be aligned before interaction as the core structural gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260790/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized RGB image frames, consecutive LiDAR point clouds (augmented with geometric and depth descriptors), and asynchronous event streams (voxelized in spacetime and processed by a sparse 3D CNN into a multi-scale feature pyramid). It produces dense per-pixel 2D optical flow and per-point 3D scene flow simultaneously, using PWC-style coarse-to-fine supervision where each level's estimate is upsampled and warped to initialize the next. The LiDAR features are projected onto the 2D image plane for the optical flow branch while remaining in the 3D point domain for the scene flow branch.

## Method

The core contribution is the Event Edge Space, a homogeneous latent space where a self-supervisedly pretrained (then frozen) event edge encoder provides fixed edge prototypes that anchor all modalities. Image and LiDAR features are projected into this shared dimension and symmetrically regularized toward the frozen event prototypes via edge-strength-weighted L1 alignment losses, transferring motion-edge semantics to both 2D and 3D branches. Within this aligned space, a reliability-aware adaptive fusion module estimates per-modality global reliability (via spatiotemporal decomposition) and local spatial attention, then computes a weighted combination followed by a cross-attention transformer for final feature refinement. A cross-dimension contrastive learning objective further couples the two tasks: a temporal pull loss enforces inter-frame motion coherence between 2D and 3D features, while a variational push loss minimizes mutual information to encourage complementary intra-frame representations.

## Results

On EKubric (standard), x²-Fusion achieves EPE_2D = 0.430, ACC_1px = 96.86%, and EPE_3D = 0.024, outperforming RPEFlow (0.439 / 95.99% / 0.027) and VisMoFlow (0.026 EPE_3D). On DSEC (standard), it reaches EPE_2D = 0.322, ACC_1px = 95.80%, EPE_3D = 0.094, and ACC_.05 = 64.39%, surpassing RPEFlow (0.326 / 95.28% / 0.103 / 60.80%). Under degraded conditions the gains are dramatic: on EKubric under-exposure, EPE_2D drops from 3.663 (RPEFlow) to 1.143, and on drifting LiDAR, EPE_3D improves from 0.457 to 0.285.

## Baselines & Comparisons

x²-Fusion outperforms the closest tri-modal competitor RPEFlow [8] on every reported metric under standard conditions: on EKubric it reduces EPE_2D by 0.009 (0.430 vs 0.439) and EPE_3D by 0.003 (0.024 vs 0.027), while on DSEC it improves EPE_3D from 0.103 to 0.094 and ACC_.05 from 60.80% to 64.39%. Against VisMoFlow [10], which reports only 3D metrics, x²-Fusion achieves lower EPE_3D on both datasets (0.024 vs 0.026 on EKubric; 0.094 vs 0.100 on DSEC) and higher ACC_.05 (96.78% vs 95.98%; 64.39% vs 61.78%). The advantage widens substantially under degradation: on EKubric under-exposure, x²-Fusion cuts EPE_2D from 3.663 to 1.143 (−69%), and on drifting LiDAR it reduces EPE_3D from 0.457 to 0.285 (−38%). Compared to bi-modal baselines, the tri-modal x²-Fusion dominates all single- and dual-modality methods (RAFT, FlowFormer, PV-RAFT, E-RAFT, RAFT-3D, CamLiFlow, DCEIFlow, STFlow) by large margins on both 2D and 3D metrics, confirming that the event modality contributes critical motion cues absent from image or LiDAR alone. The model also uses fewer parameters (8.2M) than RPEFlow (9.8M) while achieving better accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal flow estimation (RPEFlow, VisMoFlow, CMX) where each modality is processed in its native heterogeneous space and fused via pairwise or stage-wise interaction modules. Instead, it introduces the Event Edge Space—a frozen, edge-centric latent domain derived from pre-trained event features—into which image and LiDAR features are explicitly aligned before any fusion occurs, reframing multimodal fusion as representation unification rather than cross-space interaction. The cross-dimension contrastive learning component extends prior 2D-3D coupling (e.g., CamLiFlow's bidirectional interpolation) by adding explicit inter-frame coherence and intra-frame complementarity constraints between optical and scene flow branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Event Edge Space offers a principled, modality-agnostic alternative to the increasingly complex stack of modality-specific fusion blocks that has characterized recent RGB-LiDAR-event systems, potentially simplifying architecture design and easing extension to additional sensors. The robustness gains under synthetic degradations (e.g., ~3× smaller EPE degradation under under-exposure vs. RPEFlow) are practically relevant for all-day autonomous driving, though the degradation models are synthetic and validated only against one strong baseline. Open questions include whether the frozen event prototype generalizes to unseen edge statistics, whether the approach scales beyond the image-LiDAR-event triad as the authors claim, and whether the modest standard-condition improvements (0.439→0.430 EPE2D) justify the added pretraining stage in deployment-critical settings.
