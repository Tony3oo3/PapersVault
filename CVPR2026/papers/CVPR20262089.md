## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting produces blurring and floaters in complex scenes because small Gaussians—needed for high-frequency detail—exhibit positional gradients far larger than other attribute gradients, causing abrupt positional drift during optimization. Existing remedies such as gradient clipping, normalization, and weight decay are heuristic and inevitably discard gradient information, leaving the fundamental instability unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262089/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

PDEO is a plug-and-play optimizer that takes the same inputs as any 3DGS-based pipeline: multi-view RGB images and a COLMAP-initialized sparse point cloud. It outputs rendered novel views and extractable 3D surfaces, and can be dropped into existing methods (MipGS, 2DGS, RaDeGS, MCMC, SpecGS, etc.) without altering their rendering or loss formulations. Internally it maintains a 64x64x64 voxel grid (with adaptive cell merging) that stores per-voxel velocity fields used to regulate Gaussian motion during training.

## Method

The paper first derives that the 3DGS gradient-descent update is a discretization of a PDE whose right-hand side is a sum of squared attribute gradients (Eq. 5), then augments this PDE with a viscous term borrowed from fluid dynamics to dampen abrupt positional changes while preserving the original gradient signal. The resulting PDE is solved numerically via the Material Point Method: a Particle-to-Grid (P2G) step attenuates each Gaussian's velocity and deposits the excess into a voxel grid, and a Grid-to-Particle (G2P) step blends the attenuated particle velocity with the local voxel velocity to produce a stabilized update. Two auxiliary losses enforce the particle hypothesis—a scale loss that penalizes Gaussians exceeding a size threshold and a confidence loss that pushes opacity toward 0 or 1—and a velocity-guided densification criterion (cosine similarity between particle and voxel velocity) replaces the original viewspace-gradient threshold for cloning and splitting.

## Results

On novel view synthesis, PDEO consistently improves PSNR across all base methods: SpecGS+PDEO achieves the best Mip-NeRF360 PSNR of 28.81 (vs. 27.96 for SpecGS alone), MCMC+PDEO reaches 22.77 PSNR on Tanks&Temples (vs. 21.03), and MILO+PDEO attains 28.25 PSNR on ScanNet++ (vs. 28.02). Memory usage drops substantially, e.g., SpecGS from 1147 MB to 99.6 MB and MCMC from 714 MB to 198 MB. For surface reconstruction on DTU, RaDeGS+PDEO achieves the best Chamfer Distance of 0.68 (vs. 0.70 for RaDeGS), and on Tanks&Temples, MILO+PDEO reaches 0.475 F1-score (vs. 0.397 for MILO alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDEO is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to eight existing 3DGS-based methods. The most dramatic improvement is with SpecGS, where PSNR jumps from 27.96 to 28.81 on Mip-NeRF360 (+0.85 dB) while memory drops from 1147 MB to 99.6 MB. MCMC+PDEO achieves the best Tanks&Temples PSNR (22.77 vs. 21.03 for MCMC alone, +1.74 dB) and the best ScanNet++ PSNR (28.23 vs. 28.01). RaDeGS+PDEO improves Mip-NeRF360 PSNR from 28.03 to 28.16 and Tanks&Temples from 20.80 to 22.61. For surface reconstruction, RaDeGS+PDEO reduces DTU Chamfer Distance from 0.70 to 0.68, and MILO+PDEO improves Tanks&Temples F1-score from 0.397 to 0.475. Notably, 2DGS+PDEO shows minimal PSNR gain on Mip-NeRF360 (27.42→27.42) but still reduces memory from 476 MB to 63.8 MB, suggesting the benefit is more pronounced in scenes with larger geometric gaps. Overall, PDEO wins or ties on PSNR in 13 of 14 base-method/dataset combinations, with the sole exception being 2DGS on Mip-NeRF360 where PSNR is unchanged.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) as the scene representation and repurposes the Material Point Method (MPM) from continuum mechanics as a numerical solver for the optimization problem. It departs from prior gradient-stabilization techniques (clipping, batch normalization, weight decay) by arguing they are heuristic and cause information loss, instead formulating the 3DGS update rule as a discretized PDE and adding a viscous term borrowed from fluid dynamics to suppress abrupt positional changes in small Gaussians. The plug-and-play positioning distinguishes it from methods that modify the Gaussian representation itself (e.g., 2DGS, RaDeGS, GES), as PDEO is an orthogonal optimization layer that can be stacked on top of any existing 3DGS-based pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means PDEO can immediately benefit a wide range of 3DGS-based methods without architectural changes, and the consistent (if modest) gains across 10+ baselines on three datasets suggest broad applicability. The theoretical reframing of 3DGS optimization as a PDE provides a principled lens for understanding and controlling optimization dynamics, potentially guiding future work on convergence guarantees. However, the authors acknowledge that the method does not handle particle rotation and cannot reliably relocate Gaussians into regions with large point-cloud gaps, limiting its utility for sparse or incomplete initializations. The memory reduction (e.g., ~37% less for 3DGS+PDEO vs. 3DGS) is a practical bonus but not the primary contribution.
