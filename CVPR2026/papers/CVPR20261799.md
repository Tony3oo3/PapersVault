## Problem

Single-image HDR reconstruction is highly ill-posed because over-exposed regions lose all detail (requiring hallucination) while under-exposed regions suffer severe noise amplification (requiring denoising), yet existing diffusion-based methods treat all pixels uniformly and demand hundreds of iterative sampling steps. This paper addresses both the spatially heterogeneous degradation and the computational cost simultaneously by formulating HDR reconstruction as a one-step, exposure-aware consistency problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261799/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 LDR RGB image and produces the corresponding HDR image in one forward pass (0.33 s on an NVIDIA 3090). The LDR input is first processed by a lightweight exposure mask module that computes percentile-based luminance statistics to produce three soft weighting maps (over-, under-, well-exposed), and the original LDR image is concatenated with a noisy latent state along the channel dimension as the U-Net input. Training uses 256×256 random crops from paired LDR-HDR datasets.

## Method

ExpoCM replaces the uniform PF-ODE consistency trajectory with an Exposure-Aware Consistency Trajectory (EACT) that spatially blends three region-specific flows: over-exposed regions use a pure-noise trajectory (no LDR guidance, since information is irrecoverable), under-exposed regions inject a low-pass-filtered version of the LDR as coarse structural guidance, and well-exposed regions follow the standard LDR-conditioned trajectory. The blending is performed via element-wise multiplication with the soft exposure masks, yielding a single unified trajectory that is trained from scratch via consistency training (no distillation from a pre-trained diffusion model). A second key contribution is the Exposure-guided Luminance-Chromaticity (ELC) loss in CIE L*a*b* space, which applies spatially adaptive weights: luminance supervision is amplified in shadows while chromaticity supervision is amplified in highlights, using differentiable gating functions and a near-white saturation indicator. The U-Net backbone (3 down/up stages with residual blocks and sinusoidal time embeddings) is trained in two stages—first with the consistency loss for stable one-step generation, then fine-tuned with the ELC loss for perceptual color and brightness fidelity.

## Results

On HDR-REAL, ExpoCM achieves 28.66 dB PSNR-μ, 0.8684 SSIM-μ, 0.1919 LPIPS, and 4.02 ΔE2000, outperforming all baselines including DDPM (25.45 dB PSNR-μ) and Reti-Diff (27.64 dB). On AIM2025, it reaches 29.02 dB PSNR-μ, 0.8922 SSIM-μ, 0.1511 LPIPS, and 3.90 ΔE2000, again leading all competitors. Inference takes only 0.33 s per 512×512 image, yielding >400× speedup over DDPM (174.10 s, 1000 steps) and ~20× over DDIM (7.85 s, 50 steps) while matching or exceeding their fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpoCM outperforms all CNN-based baselines (HDRCNN, SingleHDR, ExpandNet, HDRUNet, HDR-Trans.) by large margins on every dataset and metric; for example, on HDR-REAL it gains +10.7 dB PSNR-μ over ExpandNet (28.66 vs 18.07) and +13.0 dB over HDRCNN. Against the strongest diffusion baseline DDPM (1000 steps), ExpoCM achieves higher PSNR-μ on HDR-REAL (+3.21 dB) and AIM2025 (+5.99 dB) while running 400× faster; DDPM retains a slight LPIPS edge on AIM2025 (0.1286 vs 0.1511) but is far slower. DDIM (50 steps) degrades substantially relative to DDPM, confirming its reliance on iterative sampling, and ExpoCM surpasses it on all fidelity metrics across all three datasets. The closest competitor, Reti-Diff (ICLR 2025), is outperformed by ExpoCM on PSNR-μ (+1.02 dB on HDR-REAL, +5.71 dB on AIM2025), SSIM-μ, LPIPS, and ΔE2000, though Reti-Diff retains a small PSNR-l advantage on HDR-REAL (35.93 vs 36.22 is actually ExpoCM winning; Reti-Diff wins on SSIM-l 0.9397 vs 0.9521 — actually ExpoCM wins here too). On HDR-EYE, ExpoCM leads all methods on PSNR-μ and SSIM-μ but trails DDPM/DDIM on LPIPS (0.2353 vs 0.2005), suggesting the small 46-image benchmark stresses perceptual diversity where multi-step diffusion still has an edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Consistency Models (CMs) [47], which learn a direct mapping from any point on a PF-ODE trajectory to the clean image, enabling one-step generation without iterative sampling. It departs from standard CMs in two key ways: (1) replacing the spatially uniform consistency trajectory with an exposure-aware, region-conditioned trajectory that blends three distinct PF-ODE flows (pure-noise generation for saturated regions, low-pass-guided denoising for dark regions, and standard conditional flow for well-exposed regions), and (2) introducing a distillation-free training procedure, whereas most CM implementations require costly distillation from a pre-trained multi-step diffusion model. The exposure-guided Luminance-Chromaticity loss in CIE L*a*b* space is a new perceptual supervision signal not present in prior HDR or diffusion-based restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400× speedup over DDPM and 20× over DDIM while matching or exceeding their fidelity makes one-step HDR reconstruction practically viable for real-time or resource-constrained applications, a regime previously inaccessible to diffusion-based methods. The exposure-aware trajectory design offers a general template for handling spatially heterogeneous degradations in other ill-posed imaging tasks (e.g., low-light enhancement, deblurring with spatially varying noise). Open questions include whether the percentile-based exposure mask generalizes to extreme scenes (e.g., near-uniform lighting), whether the U-Net backbone is optimal compared to transformer-based alternatives, and whether the distillation-free training strategy scales to higher-resolution or video HDR reconstruction.
