## Problem

Existing masked skeleton reconstruction methods must predict a very large number of spatiotemporal patches (e.g., 750), making pre-training computationally expensive, and they apply uniform reconstruction targets to all masked regions regardless of semantic importance. Simply increasing patch size to reduce the number of targets degrades performance because larger patches contain more complex and redundant information that is harder to reconstruct accurately.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262632/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D skeleton sequence X ∈ R^{T×V×C} (T frames, V joints, C channels per joint) and patchifies it into spatiotemporal patches of l consecutive frames per joint. During pre-training, the model reconstructs masked patches scaled to r times the standard size (e.g., 125 target patches instead of 750), with a motion-energy-weighted focal loss. During downstream evaluation, the frozen or fine-tuned encoder produces action class labels via a linear probe or end-to-end fine-tuning on NTU-60, NTU-120, and PKU-MMD II.

## Method

AMR introduces two key mechanisms. First, a decoupled cross-attention decoder replaces the standard self-attention Transformer decoder: a set of learnable query vectors (N_t = N/r) attend to visible patch features as keys and values, eliminating the redundant self-attention among mask tokens and enabling flexible patch-size prediction by simply adjusting the query count without modifying the encoder. Second, a focal reconstruction loss adaptively weights the MSE by local motion energy: per-joint motion energy is computed as the mean squared displacement within temporal windows (multi-scale: n = 4, 8, 12), mapped through a smooth weighting function w(e_s) = 1/(1 + k·e_t/(e_s+ε)) that approaches 1 for high-motion regions and 0 for static ones, then applied element-wise to the reconstruction error. This guides the model to concentrate capacity on discriminative motion-critical regions, mitigating the performance drop from larger patches while achieving 8.1× training speedup over prior methods.

## Results

Under the linear evaluation protocol, AMR achieves 87.4% top-1 accuracy on NTU-60 x-sub, 92.3% on NTU-60 x-view, and 60.3% on PKU-II x-sub, while requiring only 3.7 hours of pre-training (8.1× speedup over prior methods) with 3.8G FLOPs. On NTU-120, AMR reaches 81.1% (x-sub) and 81.9% (x-setup), outperforming the previous best S-JEPA by 1.5 and 2.0 points respectively. In the semi-supervised setting with only 1% labeled data on NTU-60 x-sub, AMR achieves 72.2%, surpassing the next-best GFP (71.8%) and MAMP (66.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMR outperforms all 19 compared baselines on the primary NTU-60 x-sub linear evaluation, with the largest margins over SkeletonMAE (+12.6%) and MAMP (+2.5%). Against the closest competitor NAT w/ Con [9] (86.9%), AMR gains 0.5 points while training 12.6× faster (3.7h vs. 46.6h). On the more challenging PKU-II dataset, AMR's advantage is most pronounced: 60.3% vs. 56.2% for GFP and 53.8% for MAMP, a 4.1–6.5 point gap that the authors attribute to the focal reconstruction mechanism. On NTU-120, AMR leads S-JEPA [1] by 1.5 points (x-sub) and 2.0 points (x-setup). In the transfer learning setting (Table 4), AMR is essentially tied with MAMP and NAT on NTU-120→PKU-II (73.0 vs. 73.2) but edges them out on NTU-60→PKU-II (70.9 vs. 70.6/70.7), confirming comparable generalizability. The only scenario where AMR does not clearly dominate is the NTU-120 x-setup transfer, where it trails MAMP and NAT by 0.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the masked autoencoder paradigm (He et al., CVPR 2022) to skeleton-based action recognition, building most directly on MAMP (Mao et al., ICCV 2023) and its successors (GFP, NAT, S-JEPA) that use self-attention Transformer decoders to reconstruct masked spatiotemporal patches. The key architectural departure is replacing the standard self-attention decoder with a cross-attention-only decoder that uses learnable queries, thereby decoupling the decoder's sequence length from the encoder's patch granularity. The focal reconstruction loss, which weights reconstruction targets by local motion energy, is positioned as distinct from prior saliency-guided methods (ActCLR for contrastive learning, MAMP for masking strategy) by embedding motion guidance directly within the reconstruction objective rather than in the masking or contrastive pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.1× pre-training speedup while simultaneously improving downstream accuracy (e.g., 87.4% vs. 85.9% on NTU-60 x-sub) makes large-patch masked reconstruction practically viable for skeleton action recognition, removing a key barrier to scaling these methods. The empirical finding that self-attention among mask tokens is redundant in the decoder offers a general architectural simplification that could transfer to other masked reconstruction settings. Open questions include whether the motion-energy weighting generalizes to other modalities (e.g., video, point clouds), whether the multi-scale window fusion is optimal, and whether the approach extends to longer sequences or higher-dimensional skeleton representations beyond the three benchmarks tested.
