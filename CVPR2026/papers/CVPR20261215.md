## Problem

Reference-based face restoration (Ref-FR) methods recover high-quality facial details but ignore degradation in the surrounding body and background, while full-scene restorers either condition only on the degraded input (making the problem underdetermined) or rely on coarse LQ-only degradation cues (e.g., two global scalars) that misinterpret texture or lighting as corruption. No existing approach leverages the strong, identity-conditioned signal available from a restored face to guide scene-wide restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded full-scene RGB image (tested at 1024×1024) and one or more clean reference photos of the same identity. The face is detected, cropped, and rigidly aligned to a 512×512 canonical frame for the Ref-FR stage; the output is a single restored full-scene image at the original resolution and location, covering face, body, clothing, and background in one diffusion step (plus N Ref-FR inference steps).

## Method

Face2Scene is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 runs an off-the-shelf Ref-FR model (FaceMe) on the aligned face crop to produce a spatially aligned LQ–HQ face pair, whose residual isolates corruption from content. Stage 2 feeds this pair into FaDeX, a lightweight CNN encoder trained with a contrastive (InfoNCE) objective that attracts embeddings sharing the same degradation operator and repels others, yielding a content-disentangled degradation embedding. A MapNet module then converts the spatial FaDeX features into 21 multi-scale conditioning tokens (via patch embedding, residual attention, and grid pooling at 4×4, 2×2, 1×1) that are injected into a one-step SD-Turbo diffusion model through cross-attention. The diffusion model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16 in VAE, rank 32 in UNet) under a combined L2 + LPIPS + DINO-based GAN loss, requiring no diffusion teacher or multi-step supervision.

## Results

On the InScene synthetic validation set, Face2Scene achieves the best DISTS (0.1007), LPIPS (0.2421), FID (15.26), MUSIQ (74.76), CLIP-IQA (0.7640), MANIQA (0.4347), LIQE (4.7157), and TOPIQ (0.6515) among all 9 methods, while trailing only on PSNR (22.90 vs. S3Diff's 23.60) and SSIM (0.5574 vs. 0.5916). On the more challenging real validation set, Face2Scene leads on 9 of 10 metrics including DISTS (0.1178), LPIPS (0.2502), PSNR (22.90), SSIM (0.6197), and MUSIQ (75.37), with the sole exception being FID (42.21 vs. S3Diff's 38.64). The method operates in a single diffusion step plus N Ref-FR steps (1+N), matching the inference budget of other one-step methods like OSEDiff and S3Diff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Face2Scene outperforms all 8 baselines on the majority of perceptual and no-reference quality metrics (DISTS, LPIPS, MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, MANIQA, LIQE, TOPIQ) on both synthetic and real validation sets. Against the closest competitor S3Diff (also one-step and degradation-aware), Face2Scene improves DISTS by 11% (0.1007 vs. 0.1131) and LPIPS by 5% (0.2421 vs. 0.2557) on synthetic data, and by 47% and 51% respectively on real data, while also achieving substantially better FID (15.26 vs. 18.06 synthetic; 42.21 vs. 38.64 real, where S3Diff is slightly better). The trade-off is in pixel-level fidelity: Face2Scene scores lower on PSNR and SSIM on the synthetic set (22.90/0.5574 vs. S3Diff's 23.60/0.5916), though it reverses this on the real set (22.90/0.6197 vs. 17.14/0.4894). Compared to multi-step methods (SUPIR 50-step, DiffBIR 50-step, PASD 20-step, ResShift 4-step), Face2Scene achieves better perceptual quality and FID while running in a single diffusion step (3.3 s total including Ref-FR, vs. 34.6 s for DiffBIR and 19.9 s for SUPIR). Among one-step methods (OSEDiff, SinSR, InvSR), Face2Scene consistently leads on DISTS, LPIPS, FID, and all NR-IQA scores, confirming that the face-derived degradation oracle provides a meaningful advantage over LQ-only or scalar-based degradation estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate threads: reference-based face restoration (Ref-FR, e.g., FaceMe, Ref-LDM) and degradation-aware one-step diffusion restoration (S3Diff, DeeDSR). Rather than estimating degradation from the LQ image alone—as S3Diff does with two global scalars or DeeDSR does via unsupervised contrastive learning—it uses a Ref-FR model to produce an HQ face reconstruction, then extracts degradation from the HQ-LQ face residual via a contrastive encoder (FaDeX). The restoration backbone builds directly on S3Diff's codebase and SD-Turbo, with LoRA fine-tuning, but the conditioning signal is replaced by multi-scale degradation tokens produced by MapNet from the face-derived code.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a face is a reliable, identity-anchored probe for scene-wide degradation—offers a practical path to degradation-aware restoration without requiring a separate degradation estimator trained on LQ images alone. The constructed InScene dataset (with identity references and paired degradations) fills a gap in the literature for scene-level restoration benchmarks. The main limitation is the assumption of spatially homogeneous degradation, which breaks down for depth-of-field or motion blur; the authors acknowledge this and suggest pixel/depth-dependent blur estimation as future work. Broader applicability beyond human-centric scenes and validation on larger, more diverse real-world test sets remain open.
