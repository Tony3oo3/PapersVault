## Problem

State-of-the-art animatable face avatars based on 3D Gaussian Splatting still struggle with high-frequency details such as specular reflections on eyes and glasses, thin structures like teeth and hair, and nonlinear deformations like eyelid closing. Most prior work focuses on predicting better Gaussian parameter offsets from expression codes, but the underlying 3D Gaussian representation itself lacks the expressivity to capture these effects. This paper rethinks the Gaussian primitive itself rather than the prediction network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262455/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video at 512×512 (25 FPS, ~2000 training frames) or multi-view video at 2048×2048 from 16 cameras, along with FLAME/3DMM expression and pose coefficients obtained via MICA or BFM tracking. Instead of predicting per-Gaussian offsets directly, the deformation MLP outputs a per-Gaussian latent code (n=8 dimensions) that conditions the HyperGaussians. The output is a set of conditioned 3D Gaussian primitives (position, scale, rotation, color, opacity) that are splatted via differentiable rasterization to produce rendered novel-view face images at 300 FPS.

## Method

HyperGaussians extend each 3D Gaussian from m=3 attribute dimensions to (m+n) dimensions by coupling 3D Gaussian attributes (position, scale, rotation) with a learnable latent embedding z through a joint multivariate Gaussian. Conditioning on z yields a closed-form conditional distribution whose mean recovers a standard 3D Gaussian for splatting, while the conditional covariance provides uncertainty estimates. The key efficiency contribution is the inverse covariance trick: by reparameterizing in terms of the precision matrix Λ=Σ⁻¹, conditioning only requires inverting the small Λ_aa block (m×m) rather than the large Σ_bb block (n×n), reducing cost from O(n³+mn²) to O(m³+m²n). This makes high-dimensional conditioning practical at 300 FPS. Integration into FlashAvatar and GaussianHeadAvatar requires only replacing the offset-prediction head with a latent-prediction head; all other architecture, losses, and hyperparameters remain unchanged.

## Results

On the monocular setting (19 subjects across 5 datasets), HyperGaussians plugged into FlashAvatar achieve PSNR 29.99, SSIM 0.9510, and LPIPS 0.04978, outperforming the FlashAvatar baseline (PSNR 29.43, SSIM 0.9466, LPIPS 0.05107) and MonoGaussianAvatar (PSNR 29.94, SSIM 0.9456, LPIPS 0.06545). On the multi-view NeRSemble benchmark (10 subjects), HyperGaussians plugged into GaussianHeadAvatar improve PSNR from 24.10 to 24.38 and LPIPS from 0.20273 to 0.19768. The gains are achieved with zero architectural or hyperparameter changes—only the Gaussian representation is swapped—while maintaining real-time rendering at 300 FPS thanks to the inverse covariance trick.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGaussians (Ours FA) beat FlashAvatar on all three metrics: +0.56 PSNR, +0.0044 SSIM, and −0.00129 LPIPS, with the only modification being the replacement of 3D Gaussians with 8-dimensional HyperGaussians. Against MonoGaussianAvatar, Ours (FA) wins on SSIM (+0.0054) and LPIPS (−0.01567) while matching PSNR (29.99 vs 29.94), and does so with ~15k Gaussians and <20 min training versus MonoGaussianAvatar's >100k Gaussians and >12 h training. Against SplattingAvatar, the gains are substantial across all metrics (+1.41 PSNR, +0.114 SSIM, −4.043 LPIPS), reflecting SplattingAvatar's inability to model expression-dependent appearance. In the multi-view setting, Ours (GHA) improves over GaussianHeadAvatar by +0.28 PSNR and −0.00505 LPIPS while tying on SSIM, with only a 30 min (1%) increase in training time. The paper also shows that scaling the deformation MLP (deeper or wider) to match HyperGaussians' parameter count degrades LPIPS by 3–15% and reduces rendering speed by 41–47%, confirming that the gains come from the representation itself rather than added capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to higher dimensions by introducing HyperGaussians, drawing conceptual inspiration from HyperNeRF's use of higher-dimensional spaces sliced by learnable embeddings and from NDGS's N-dimensional Gaussian formulation. However, it departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it provides a unified Bayesian interpretation showing that high-dimensional Gaussians perform MAP estimation over conditional 3DGS scenes, and (2) it introduces the inverse covariance trick, which reparameterizes conditioning via the precision matrix to reduce computational cost from cubic to linear in the latent dimension. Unlike most face avatar methods that improve the neural network predicting Gaussian offsets, HyperGaussians modify the representation itself, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing architectures like FlashAvatar and GaussianHeadAvatar.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of HyperGaussians—requiring only a swap of the Gaussian primitive with no architectural or hyperparameter changes—makes this a low-friction upgrade for any 3DGS-based avatar pipeline, and the inverse covariance trick (300 FPS at n=8) removes the primary barrier that previously limited NDGS-style methods to low dimensions. The Bayesian framing also clarifies *why* high-dimensional conditioning helps: the implicit Gaussian prior acts as a regularizer that preserves detail during inference. Limitations include the still-increased memory and compute over vanilla 3DGS (mitigated but not eliminated), and the evaluation is confined to face avatars; whether the approach generalizes to full-body or non-rigid scenes with higher latent dimensions remains open.
