## Problem

Collecting diverse robotic demonstrations for spatial generalization is prohibitively expensive, and existing augmentation methods either rely on simulation engines (introducing Sim2Real gaps), require digital assets for manipulated objects, or are incompatible with the dominant multi-view RGB paradigm used by current VLA policies. No prior method can rapidly scale real-world 2D multi-view manipulation videos with novel object placements and trajectories while preserving both visual realism and interaction fidelity.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single source demonstration (1–5 episodes) comprising synchronized multi-view RGB video from head, left-wrist, and right-wrist cameras, joint angles, and end-effector actions, along with the robot's URDF kinematic model and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs 200 augmented demonstrations as 20-second, 30-FPS multi-view manipulation videos at the same three camera viewpoints, accompanied by kinematically consistent depth sequences, corrected joint states, action trajectories, and updated camera poses. The depth maps produced during editing serve as the primary 3D control signal for the downstream video generator, making the pipeline end-to-end from raw RGB to policy-ready video.

## Method

Real2Edit2Real bridges 3D editability with 2D visual data through a three-stage pipeline. First, a hybrid training paradigm co-trains VGGT on simulated data (for precise camera poses and noise-free depth) and real robot data (for metric-scale geometry), yielding a feed-forward model that predicts metric depth and camera poses from sparse three-view inputs in seconds. Second, depth-reliable spatial editing performs point-cloud manipulation: objects are relocated via random transforms, motion segments are replanned with Curobo, and skill segments are transformed to preserve robot-object interaction; critically, a robot pose correction step segments arm links via URDF and re-renders arm depth using forward kinematics on synthesized joint states, eliminating rigid-body artifacts and producing kinematically consistent depth maps. Third, a Transformer-based video generator (fine-tuned from GE-Sim/Cosmos-Predict2B) synthesizes multi-view video from the first frame, conditioned on depth concatenated with image latents plus auxiliary Canny edges, action maps, and ray maps, using a dual-attention mechanism (intra-view self-attention for spatial detail, cross-view self-attention for multi-view consistency); smooth object relocation interpolates translation and rotation before manipulation starts, converting what would be a first-frame editing problem into a unified video generation task.

## Results

On four real-world manipulation tasks (Mug to Basket, Pour Water, Lift Box, Scan Barcode), policies trained on 200 demonstrations generated from just 1 source demo achieve 65.0% (Go-1) and 57.5% (π0.5) average success rate, already matching or exceeding the 61.3% achieved by 50 real demos for Go-1. Scaling to 5 source demos yields 78.8% (Go-1) and 81.3% (π0.5), surpassing the 50-real-demo baseline by 17.5 and 20 percentage points respectively. This corresponds to a 10–50× improvement in data efficiency. The generation pipeline produces a 20-second, 30-FPS multi-view episode in 48.6 seconds on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other demonstration-generation methods (e.g., MimicGen, DemoGen, Real2Render2Real) in a head-to-head table; instead, it benchmarks against real-data baselines of 10, 20, and 50 teleoperated demonstrations. The key finding is that 200 generated demos from a single real source (Real 1 Gen 200) already match the 50-real-demo baseline for Go-1 (65.0% vs. 61.3%) and come close for π0.5 (57.5% vs. 61.3%). With 5 source demos, both policies clearly surpass the 50-real baseline: Go-1 improves from 61.3% to 78.8% (+17.5 pp) and π0.5 from 61.3% to 81.3% (+20 pp). Per-task, the largest gains appear on Pour Water (Go-1: 8/20 → 12/20) and Scan Barcode (Go-1: 12/20 → 18/20), indicating that spatial generalization is the primary bottleneck addressed. The authors attribute the improvement over 50 real demos to the generated data covering more diverse spatial configurations and robot-object interaction patterns than the fixed 50 real episodes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VGGT for metric-scale geometry reconstruction and GE-Sim/Cosmos-Predict2B for video generation, but its core contribution is the architectural bridge between 3D point-cloud editing and 2D video synthesis via depth as a control interface. It departs from the MimicGen family (which segments and interpolates trajectories) and DemoGen (which edits point clouds but cannot produce RGB videos) by closing the loop: edited 3D geometry is rendered into depth maps that condition a video diffusion model, yielding photorealistic multi-view demonstrations without simulation engines or digital assets. The hybrid real+sim training paradigm for the reconstruction model and the robot pose correction step are novel components not present in prior demo-generation pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10–50× data efficiency gain (1–5 source demos matching or exceeding 50 real demos) directly addresses the cost bottleneck in scaling VLA training, and the framework's extensibility to height and texture editing suggests a unified data-augmentation engine for manipulation. Limitations include dependence on depth sensors for reconstruction training, restriction to a single 3-camera humanoid platform and tabletop-scale tasks, and a ~49-second generation time per 20-second episode that may constrain large-scale data production. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to long-horizon multi-step tasks, more complex scenes with deformable objects, and whether the depth-conditioned video model can be distilled for faster inference.
