## Problem

Dynamic Point Maps (DPMs) extend the DUSt3R point-map representation to capture scene motion, but they are limited to image pairs and require test-time optimisation to fuse more than two views. This paper asks how to extend DPMs to multi-view/video settings so that a single feed-forward pass can reconstruct full 4D scene geometry and motion from an entire video snippet, without post-processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262618/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes N video frames (5, 9, or 19 during training; generalises to ~50 at test time) plus a scalar target timestamp t_j, and outputs two sets of point maps in a common reference frame π_0: time-variant maps P_i(t_i, π_0) (one per frame at its own timestamp) and time-invariant maps P_i(t_j, π_0) (all frames aligned to the reference time). Camera intrinsics and extrinsics are predicted from dedicated camera tokens. For sequences longer than the training window, a sliding-window scheme with bundle adjustment fuses overlapping predictions.

## Method

V-DPM builds on the pretrained VGGT Alternating Attention Transformer backbone and introduces a two-stage decoding scheme. First, the standard DPT head predicts time-variant point maps P_i(t_i, π_0) directly from backbone features, requiring only minimal fine-tuning from the static VGGT model. Second, a time-conditioned transformer decoder with alternating frame-level and global attention blocks takes the same backbone features and, conditioned on a target-time token via adaptive LayerNorm (adaLN), predicts time-invariant point maps P_i(t_j, π_0) that align all frames to a common timestamp, implicitly recovering dynamic correspondences and scene flow. The target timestamp is injected as an additional input token processed by the backbone, so the backbone runs once and only the lightweight decoder is re-evaluated for different t_j values. Training fine-tunes VGGT on a mixture of static (ScanNet++, Blended-MVS) and synthetic dynamic (Kubric-F, Kubric-G, PointOdyssey, Waymo) data with a confidence-calibrated point-map loss plus camera pose regression, demonstrating that modest 4D synthetic data suffices to adapt a static reconstructor to state-of-the-art dynamic reconstruction.

## Results

On 4D reconstruction (2-view EPE, margin 2), V-DPM achieves 0.031 on PointOdyssey, 0.018 on Kubric-F, 0.023 on Kubric-G, and 0.064 on Waymo, reducing error by roughly 3–5× over the strongest prior feed-forward methods (DPM, St4RTrack, TraceAnything). On video depth (Sintel), V-DPM reports 0.247 Abs Rel and 69.4% δ<1.25, competitive with its VGGT backbone (0.242) and trailing only π³ (0.210). On camera pose (Sintel ATE), V-DPM achieves 0.105, improving over VGGT (0.167) but remaining behind π³ (0.074).

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-DPM decisively outperforms all prior feed-forward 4D reconstructors: it achieves ~5× lower EPE than St4RTrack (e.g., 0.031 vs 0.148 on PointOdyssey) and TraceAnything (0.031 vs 0.161), and ~3–4× lower than DPM (0.031 vs 0.115). On video depth (Sintel), V-DPM (0.247 Abs Rel) substantially beats MonST3R (0.335), CUT3R (0.466), and DUSt3R (not directly listed but implied via MonST3R's 0.335), and is nearly on par with its VGGT backbone (0.242). On camera pose (Sintel ATE), V-DPM (0.105) improves over VGGT (0.167), MonST3R (0.108), and CUT3R (0.217), but is outperformed by π³ (0.074), which the authors attribute to π³'s larger training scale (14 datasets vs. 6) and stronger backbone. The only consistent loss is to π³ on both depth and pose metrics, a gap the authors expect to close with a stronger backbone or more training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

V-DPM extends Dynamic Point Maps (DPMs) from their original pairwise formulation to a multi-view/video setting, and builds its implementation on VGGT, a static 3D reconstruction transformer. The key architectural departure is a two-phase design: the pretrained VGGT backbone predicts time-variant point maps (one per input frame), and a novel time-conditioned decoder with adaptive LayerNorm produces time-invariant point maps at an arbitrary reference timestamp. This contrasts with prior dynamic reconstructors (MonST3R, DPM, St4RTrack) that either operate on pairs only or require auxiliary 2D trackers to recover scene motion, and with static multi-view methods (VGGT, π³) that assume no scene motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that a model trained exclusively on static scenes can be adapted to state-of-the-art 4D reconstruction with only modest synthetic dynamic data, dramatically reducing the data and compute barrier for 4D reconstruction. This opens a path for the broader community to leverage large-scale static 3D pretraining (which is easy to obtain and auto-annotate) for dynamic tasks. Limitations include constrained training scale (6 datasets vs. 14 for π³), which explains the remaining gap on depth and pose benchmarks, and the current 20-frame training window (generalising to ~50 at test time). Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, whether integrating with stronger backbones like π³ closes the remaining gap, and how well it handles real-world uncontrolled video beyond synthetic data.
