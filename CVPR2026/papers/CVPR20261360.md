## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., Gaussian Splatting, NeRF) entangle illumination with appearance, producing textures that are not physically meaningful for relighting. While 2D diffusion models can predict high-quality PBR material maps (albedo, roughness, metallic), transferring these per-view 2D estimates onto 3D geometry while maintaining multi-view consistency and suppressing baked-in lighting remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261360/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images of a single object (100 training + 200 evaluation views for synthetic ARIA objects; ~27 views for real-world objects) and produces a fully relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting asset with per-Gaussian spatially varying PBR parameters (3-channel base color, 1-channel roughness, 1-channel metallic). The 2D material predictor is swappable—any diffusion model that outputs PBR maps can be used—making the pipeline plug-and-play. For highly specular objects, DiffusionRenderer normals are additionally fed as RGB to guide the Gaussian Splatting geometry optimization.

## Method

MatSpray first reconstructs scene geometry via R3DGS, then uses a 2D diffusion model (DiffusionRenderer) to predict per-view PBR material maps. These 2D maps are lifted to 3D by projecting material values onto Gaussians via Gaussian ray tracing (using the opacity-based intersection formulation of Moenne-Loccoz et al.), with per-Gaussian median aggregation across pixel footprints to reduce outliers. The key architectural contribution is the Neural Merger: a lightweight MLP (3 hidden layers, 128 neurons) that takes each Gaussian's position (positional encoding) and its per-view material arrays as input, and outputs softmax-normalized view weights that interpolate among the projected values rather than generating new ones. This softmax constraint is critical—it prevents the merger from converging faster than the environment map and producing physically implausible values, while also allowing it to collapse onto a subset of views to exclude outlier predictions. Refinement is supervised by two losses: an L1 loss between rendered material maps and the 2D diffusion predictions (optimizing only the Neural Merger), and a PBR photometric loss (0.8·L1 + 0.2·SSIM) from deferred shading against ground-truth images (jointly optimizing the Neural Merger and an optimizable environment map).

## Results

On 17 synthetic ARIA objects, MatSpray achieves 27.28 PSNR / 0.897 SSIM / 0.080 LPIPS on relighting, outperforming extended R3DGS (25.48 / 0.875 / 0.094) and IRGS (24.41 / 0.850 / 0.166). For material estimation, it reaches 21.34 PSNR on base color and 15.33 PSNR on roughness, while its metallic maps achieve 27.20 PSNR (excluding fully non-metallic objects where PSNR is infinite) versus 10.07 for extended R3DGS and N/A for IRGS. Across the broader Table 2 evaluation (ARIA, Stanford Orb, NeRF Synthetic), MatSpray leads all baselines on every dataset, e.g. 31.2 PSNR on Stanford Orb vs. 30.2 for IRGS and 29.4 for extended R3DGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against extended R3DGS, MatSpray wins on every metric: +1.80 PSNR on relighting, +2.98 PSNR on base color, +1.24 PSNR on roughness, and a dramatic +17.13 PSNR on metallic (27.20 vs. 10.07), reflecting R3DGS's inability to model specular metallic surfaces well. Against IRGS, MatSpray leads on relighting (+2.87 PSNR), base color (+2.14 PSNR), and metallic (IRGS does not predict metallic at all); IRGS narrowly edges out on roughness PSNR (16.18 vs. 15.33) but MatSpray recovers the advantage on SSIM (0.820 vs. 0.744) and LPIPS (0.181 vs. 0.192). In the broader Table 2 comparison, MatSpray outperforms the diffusion-based relighting methods Lightswitch and Neural Gaffer by large margins on all three datasets (e.g. 27.3 vs. 16.3 PSNR on ARIA for Lightswitch; 25.4 vs. 22.0 on NeRF Synthetic for Neural Gaffer), confirming that explicit 3D material reconstruction substantially exceeds image-to-image relighting approaches. The only metric where a baseline wins is roughness PSNR vs. IRGS, a gap the authors attribute to roughness estimation remaining an open challenge across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatSpray sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) relightable Gaussian Splatting pipelines (R3DGS, IRGS) that optimize per-scene BRDF parameters, (2) 2D diffusion-based material predictors (DiffusionRenderer, Marigold, RGB-to-X) that produce PBR maps from images, and (3) Gaussian ray tracing (Mai et al., Moenne-Loccoz et al.) for differentiable ray-Gaussian intersection. The key departure from R3DGS and IRGS is that instead of learning materials purely from photometric supervision per scene, MatSpray lifts pre-computed 2D diffusion predictions into 3D via ray-traced projection and then refines them with a lightweight softmax-weighted Neural Merger MLP. This reframes the problem from 'optimize materials from scratch' to 'fuse world-knowledge priors into a 3D representation,' making the pipeline plug-and-play across diffusion backends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for content production: a 3.5× reduction in per-scene optimization time versus IRGS, combined with plug-and-play compatibility with any future diffusion material model, lowers the barrier to producing relightable 3D assets. However, material quality remains bottlenecked by the underlying diffusion predictor, and the method inherits geometric limitations from R3DGS (inconsistent normals, missed small/flat Gaussians during ray tracing). Open questions include whether the Neural Merger's softmax interpolation generalizes to more than a handful of views, whether the approach extends to dynamic or non-rigid objects, and whether the proposed 3D part-segmentation and language-conditioned manipulation directions can be realized without additional 3D supervision.
