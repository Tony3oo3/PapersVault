## Problem

VLA models face a fundamental mismatch: low-dimensional action signals are too sparse to supervise large backbones processing high-dimensional sensory inputs. Existing remedies—directly predicting future frames, compressing visual states into tracks, or using latent action quantizers—either distribute model capacity with prohibitive training cost, create information bottlenecks that lose fine-grained motion cues, or neglect language supervision, causing the backbone to lose its semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities after action fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model receives a language instruction and two RGB frames: a primary camera image at higher resolution and a wrist camera image at lower resolution (exploiting Qwen2.5-VL's flexible input resolution). The action head outputs an n-step continuous action trajectory by denoising Gaussian noise via a 10-step diffusion process, while the DVF head (used only during training) generates future frames at multiple time-step gaps (1–6) via a 30-step diffusion process. During inference the DVF head is entirely omitted to reduce computational overhead, and the Adaptive Temporal Ensemble can skip full model calls by up to 50% when motion stability is not needed.

## Method

Mantis decouples visual foresight from the VLA backbone by routing 9 learnable latent-action queries through a 12-layer connector into a Sana-based DiT head, which predicts future frames conditioned on the current frame via a residual connection; this residual design lets the queries capture inter-frame dynamics (latent actions) rather than reconstructing full images. Six action queries then aggregate information from both the backbone output and the latent-action queries via causal attention, and a DiT-based action head denoises them into the n-step trajectory. Training follows a three-stage progressive recipe: (1) multi-gap future-frame prediction on 220K human manipulation videos with the backbone frozen, (2) joint vision-action training on 76K DROID robot episodes, and (3) language-supervised mixed training on 38 multimodal datasets to preserve reasoning. At inference, the proposed Adaptive Temporal Ensemble dynamically activates or deactivates temporal ensembling based on the overlap between instruction-relevant target patches and visually dynamic patches, cutting inference calls by ~50% while maintaining success rates.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, Mantis achieves a 96.7% average success rate across four task suites (Spatial: 98.8%, Object: 99.2%, Goal: 94.4%, Long: 94.2%), surpassing the previous best vision-augmented method F₁ (95.7%) and the strongest non-vision-augmented baseline π₀ (94.2%). Mantis also demonstrates faster convergence than entangled visual foresight methods like UnifiedVLA, which maintains 0% success rate for the first 10 fine-tuning epochs. The Mantis-ATE variant reduces inference calls by approximately 50% while maintaining comparable success rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mantis outperforms the strongest vision-augmented baseline F₁ [32] by 1.0% in average SR (96.7 vs. 95.7), winning on Spatial (+0.6), Object (+1.4), and Long (+2.9) but losing on Goal (−1.0). Against UnifiedVLA [43], which entangles foresight prediction with the backbone, Mantis gains +1.2% average SR and converges significantly faster (UnifiedVLA remains at 0% SR for the first 10 fine-tuning epochs). Compared to the non-vision-augmented π₀ [6], Mantis achieves +2.5% average SR, with a particularly large margin on the Long suite (+9.0). In real-world experiments on the Agilex platform, Mantis consistently outperforms π₀.₅ [21] on both in-domain and out-of-domain instructions across three scenarios, demonstrating superior instruction-following and generalization. The ATE ablation shows that adaptive temporal ensemble reduces inference count by ~50% with negligible performance loss compared to standard temporal ensemble.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mantis builds on the meta-queries mechanism (Pan et al., 2025) and the DiT-based action head from DreamVLA (Wu et al., 2025), but departs from prior visual-foresight VLAs (UnifiedVLA, CoT-VLA, WorldVLA) by decoupling future-frame prediction from the VLM backbone via a separate DiT head with a residual connection to the current frame. This contrasts with entangled approaches where the backbone itself must produce dense visual tokens, and with track-guidance or latent-action-quantization methods that introduce information bottlenecks or extra training stages. The Adaptive Temporal Ensemble extends the standard Temporal Ensemble (Zhao et al., 2023) with a dynamic on/off mechanism based on instruction-relevance and motion overlap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The disentanglement design addresses a practical pain point: entangled visual foresight slows convergence (UnifiedVLA stays at 0% for 10 epochs) and burdens the backbone, while the progressive training recipe and language supervision preserve instruction-following and reasoning that robot-specific fine-tuning typically erodes. The ATE variant's 50% inference reduction is directly relevant to real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D inputs or multi-arm settings, and whether the modest LIBERO margin over F1 (96.7 vs 95.7) holds on harder, more diverse benchmarks; the paper itself notes minor motion rollbacks from missing proprioceptive state.
