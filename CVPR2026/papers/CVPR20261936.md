## Problem

Optical flow-based image alignment is fundamentally limited by the many-to-one mapping in occlusion regions (causing ghosting artifacts), sensitivity to illumination changes that violate brightness constancy, and warping distortions from inaccurate motion fields. No existing large-scale dataset provides ground-truth aligned images under combined camera motion, foreground dynamics, and varying illumination, making it difficult to train and evaluate alignment methods that go beyond simple warping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261936/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only two consecutive RGB images (I1, I2) at 960×540 resolution as input, requiring no auxiliary depth maps, camera intrinsics, foreground masks, or pose estimates. It outputs a single aligned image representing the scene at time t2 rendered from the camera viewpoint of I1, effectively performing alignment-oriented view synthesis. Training uses random 480×256 crops with flipping, while inference supports larger inputs on a single GPU.

## Method

DMAligner replaces the discriminative flow-estimation-and-warping paradigm with a generative Latent Diffusion Model that directly synthesizes the aligned image in VQ-VAE latent space. The key architectural component is the Dynamics-aware Mask Producing (DMP) module, which computes a normalized dot-product cost volume between the encoded latents of I1 and I2, predicts a motion mask via a small network, dilates it, and fuses the static background of V1 with the dynamic foreground of V2 to form a mixed conditioning latent V_M. During training, the diffusion UNet (initialized from scratch) learns to denoise V_gt conditioned on the channel-concatenated [V1, V2, V_M] plus timestep embedding, using a 'predict x0' objective with a mask-weighted L2 denoising loss and a cross-entropy mask loss. Inference employs a non-Markovian DDIM-style sampling schedule (T=1000, step Δ=20) to iteratively refine the aligned latent. The authors also introduce the DSIA dataset (1,033 Blender-rendered scenes, 30K+ image pairs) that provides ground-truth aligned images under controlled camera motion, foreground dynamics, and illumination variation.

## Results

On the proposed DSIA benchmark, DMAligner achieves an average PSNR of 26.67 and SSIM of 0.81, outperforming the best flow-warping method (DPFlow†, 25.29 PSNR / 0.81 SSIM) by +1.38 dB and the best view-synthesis method (GenWarp, 24.23 PSNR / 0.78 SSIM) by +2.44 dB. On the cross-domain Sintel and DAVIS evaluations (no ground truth), DMAligner attains the lowest average LPIPS (0.211) and DreamSim (0.108), surpassing DPFlow (0.214 / 0.109) and GenWarp (0.250 / 0.130). The method requires only two input images without depth, masks, or camera intrinsics, unlike all geometry-dependent baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DMAligner outperforms all nine baselines on every evaluated dataset. Among flow-warping methods, DPFlow† is the strongest competitor (25.29 avg PSNR on DSIA), yet DMAligner still leads by +1.38 dB PSNR while matching its SSIM (0.81); the gap widens to +2.44 dB PSNR and +0.03 SSIM against the best view-synthesis baseline GenWarp (24.23 / 0.78). The older flow methods (PWCNet, RAFT) trail DMAligner by 6–8 dB PSNR, and the 3DGS-based COGS and AccidentalGS underperform dramatically (17.10 and 19.58 avg PSNR), likely due to their reliance on estimated depth and camera priors. On the cross-domain Sintel/DAVIS evaluation, DMAligner achieves the best average LPIPS (0.211) and DreamSim (0.108), edging out DPFlow (0.214 / 0.109) by a narrow margin while clearly surpassing GenWarp (0.250 / 0.130) and SD-Inpaint (0.260 / 0.140). Notably, DMAligner requires only two RGB images as input, whereas COGS, AccidentalGS, and GenWarp additionally need depth maps, foreground masks, or camera intrinsics, making the comparison favorable in terms of input simplicity as well as output quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes image alignment—a task traditionally solved via optical flow estimation followed by image warping—as a conditional generative view synthesis problem built on the Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) framework. It departs from both the flow-warping lineage (PWCNet, RAFT, FlowFormer++, DPFlow) and the 3DGS-based view synthesis approaches (COGS, AccidentalGS, GenWarp) that require auxiliary depth maps, camera intrinsics, or pose estimation. The key architectural novelty is the Dynamics-aware Mask Producing (DMP) module, which computes a latent-space cost volume to generate motion masks that condition the diffusion UNet, and the introduction of the DSIA dataset, the first large-scale benchmark purpose-built for image alignment with rendered ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a persistent practical limitation of flow-based alignment—ghosting and tearing in occluded or dynamically changing regions—by replacing warping with direct generation, and it does so with a simpler input interface (two RGB images only, no depth or intrinsics). The DSIA dataset fills a notable gap in the literature by providing 30K+ paired samples with ground truth alignment targets, enabling rigorous evaluation that was previously impossible. Open questions include the computational cost of iterative diffusion inference (50 steps at 960×540) for real-time applications, the synthetic-to-real generalization gap (evaluated only qualitatively on DAVIS), and whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or longer temporal sequences without additional conditioning.
