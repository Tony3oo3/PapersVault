## Problem

Existing few-shot anomaly detectors score each query patch by independent similarity to retrieved normal neighbors, which fails when a patch is locally plausible to individual normals yet globally inconsistent with the collective structure they form. Graph-based alternatives that enforce smoothness or message passing are counterproductive because they blur the very deviations that signal anomaly. The paper asks: how difficult is it to make a query patch conform to the normal manifold as a structured set, rather than merely how similar it is to individual exemplars?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262449/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single query image and K ≤ 4 normal reference images (few-shot regime), extracting patch-level features from a frozen DINOv3-L/16 backbone (18th transformer layer) arranged on a 2-D grid. It produces a dense per-patch anomaly map and an image-level score obtained by max-pooling patch energies. No learnable parameters are introduced; the entire pipeline is training-free and the anomaly score is the magnitude of the optimization-induced feature drift, not the optimized features themselves.

## Method

ANoCo repurposes the graph Laplacian from a smoothing prior into a non-conformity operator that measures the cost of reconciling a query with a frozen normal manifold. For each query patch, an anchor-driven retrieval first identifies its most similar normal patch, then retains only other normals whose similarity to the anchor exceeds the query-anchor similarity, yielding a coherent local manifold. A sparse bipartite graph is built with edges only between query and reference patches, weighted by cosine similarity times a norm-compatibility factor, explicitly excluding query-query and reference-reference edges to prevent evidence dilution. The anomaly score is obtained by solving a strictly convex anchored Laplacian energy minimization (Laplacian conformity + L2 identity regularization) in closed form—each query patch updates independently via element-wise division because the query block of the Laplacian is diagonal. The per-patch anomaly energy is the squared L2 drift times (1 − cosine similarity) between the original and optimized features, so the score directly quantifies how far the patch must move to satisfy normality constraints.

## Results

On MVTec-AD (1-shot), ANoCo achieves 97.9% image-level AUROC and 97.7% pixel-level AUROC, outperforming the strongest baseline INP-Former (96.6% / 97.0%) by 1.3 and 0.7 points respectively. On VisA (1-shot), ANoCo reaches 92.7% image AUROC and 98.7% pixel AUROC, surpassing INP-Former (91.4% / 96.3%) by 1.3 and 2.4 points. Gains are consistent across 2-shot and 4-shot settings, with pixel-level PRO on MVTec-AD reaching 95.4% (1-shot) and 96.2% (4-shot), the highest among all compared methods. The method also achieves 99.4% image AUROC on MVTec-AD in the full-shot setting, approaching the ceiling of the benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANoCo outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric across both MVTec-AD and VisA in all few-shot settings. The closest competitor is INP-Former (CVPR 2025), which ANoCo surpasses by 1.3 points in image AUROC and 0.7–2.4 points in pixel AUROC on MVTec-AD and VisA respectively (1-shot). KAG-Prompt (AAAI 2025) trails by 2.1 points in MVTec-AD image AUROC and 1.1 points in VisA image AUROC, while also lacking pixel-level F1-MAX and PRO scores. WinCLIP and PromptAD, both CLIP-based, are further behind by 4–9 points in image AUROC, confirming that the gains are not solely attributable to backbone quality (verified in the backbone-matched ablation of Table 3). Against the memory-bank baselines SPADE and PatchCore, ANoCo leads by 14.5 and 14.5 points in MVTec-AD image AUROC (1-shot), demonstrating that the graph-energy formulation captures structural non-conformity that independent nearest-neighbor similarity cannot. On the medical OCT2017 benchmark, ANoCo (WRN backbone) matches or slightly exceeds InCTRL and WinCLIP in 2/4/8-shot settings, confirming generalizability beyond industrial domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ANoCo departs from the dominant memory-based anomaly detection paradigm (SPADE, PatchCore, WinCLIP) that scores each query patch by independent nearest-neighbor similarity, arguing that local plausibility does not guarantee global consistency with the normal manifold. It also explicitly rejects the graph-based approaches (GNN message passing, Laplacian smoothing, label propagation) that enforce homophily and would blur the very deviations anomaly detection needs to preserve. The key conceptual move is repurposing the graph Laplacian from a smoothing prior into a non-conformity operator: the anomaly score is the magnitude of the closed-form feature update required to satisfy anchored normal-manifold constraints, rather than a distance or density measure. The bipartite query-reference structure with anchor-driven retrieval is a deliberate architectural choice to prevent anomalous patches from self-reinforcing through neighborhood consensus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, closed-form formulation with O(N_q·d) complexity makes ANoCo practically attractive for industrial deployment where per-product retraining is infeasible and inference latency matters (the paper reports ~100× speedup over WinCLIP). The physically interpretable anomaly score (energy of deformation to normality) offers a clearer diagnostic than raw similarity distances. However, the method still depends entirely on the quality of the frozen backbone (DINOv3), operates only in the few-shot regime (K ≤ 4 references), and the bipartite structure precludes any inter-query reasoning that might help with context-dependent anomalies. Open questions include whether the anchor-driven retrieval threshold generalizes across domains with very different normal-mode diversity, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D industrial inspection where temporal or volumetric consistency matters.
