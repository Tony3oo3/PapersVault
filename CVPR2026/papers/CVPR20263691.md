## Problem

Transferring visual grounding from natural images to remote sensing remains difficult because targets occupy only a tiny fraction of kilometer-scale scenes (extremely low effective resolution) and queries encode complex geospatial relations across distant objects. Existing one-step MLLM approaches lack hierarchical spatial modeling and cannot progressively refine localization, while RL-based multi-step search methods require costly large-scale reward datasets impractical for remote sensing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263691/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a remote sensing RGB image (resolutions ranging from 512×512 to 1024×1024 across benchmarks) and a free-form textual query as input. The query is first parsed into a structured geospatial context triplet {target object, spatial attributes, relational references}. The output is a single bounding box in global image coordinates, but the pipeline internally produces a sequence of candidate subregions (via zoom-in/zoom-out operations) that serve as intermediate visual cues before the final localization.

## Method

GeoViS reformulates remote sensing visual grounding as a two-stage process: (1) a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) over candidate regions using zoom-in (3×3 grid partition) and zoom-out (fixed scaling) actions, guided by a geospatial reward combining QA-based semantic verification and IoU-based geometric consistency; and (2) conditional grounding that uses the best-searched subregion as an additional visual input alongside the global image to refine the final bounding box. A unified VisualRAG model (initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B, fully fine-tuned) jointly performs all three roles—reward evaluation, action selection, and grounding prediction—trained via four supervised atomic operations (QA reward, IoU reward, zoom-in action, conditional grounding). This design avoids external detectors and RL rollouts, instead using the MLLM itself as both the search policy and the reward signal, enabling efficient data construction and interpretable step-wise reasoning.

## Results

GeoViS achieves 79.8% Pr@0.5 and 72.6% meanIoU on DIOR-RSVG, 68.5% Pr@0.5 and 59.2% meanIoU on VRSBench, 70.3% Pr@0.5 and 61.5% meanIoU on OPT-RSVG, 51.5% Pr@0.5 on RSVG-HR, and 23.7% Pr@0.5 on GeoChat. These results represent 5–15% improvements over the strongest general-purpose and remote-sensing-specific MLLMs across all five benchmarks. GeoViS approaches specialist grounding models (e.g., LQVG at 83.4% Pr@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG) while substantially outperforming all MLLM-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoViS outperforms all general-purpose MLLMs by a wide margin: on DIOR-RSVG it achieves 79.8% Pr@0.5 versus 51.1% for Qwen3-VL-4B and 33.4% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, and on VRSBench it reaches 68.5% versus 56.3% for Qwen3-VL-4B. Among remote-sensing-specific MLLMs, GeoViS surpasses EarthGPT (76.7% Pr@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG) by 3.1 points and SkySenseGPT (63.5% on VRSBench) by 5.0 points, while also leading on GeoChat (23.7% vs. 22.7% for the GeoChat model). On OPT-RSVG, GeoViS (70.3% Pr@0.5) approaches the specialist LPVA (78.0%) but far exceeds the best general MLLM GLM-4.1V-9B (43.4%). The main gap remains against dedicated specialist models on RSVG-HR, where LQVG achieves 87.4% Pr@0.5 versus GeoViS's 51.5%, suggesting that the MCTS search depth and reward design still have room to close the gap on extremely small targets in high-resolution imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoViS sits at the intersection of two research threads: remote sensing visual grounding (two-tower encoders, RS-specific MLLMs like EarthGPT and GeoChat) and visual search mechanisms for MLLMs (V*, DyFo, DeepEye, Mini-o3). It departs from prior visual search work by avoiding both external detectors and RL-based training, instead using a unified VisualRAG model that jointly performs reward evaluation, zoom-in/zoom-out action selection, and conditional grounding within a single MCTS loop. The effective-resolution framing—arguing that simply upscaling or tiling images does not help because the target's pixel proportion remains unchanged—provides a principled motivation for the progressive search design, distinguishing GeoViS from resolution-inflation approaches like LQVG and LPVA.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, interpretable recipe for handling the extreme scale disparity in remote sensing grounding without requiring large-scale RL rollouts or domain-specific detectors, which are costly and hard to generalize. The cross-dataset generalization results (training on DIOR-RSVG, transferring to VRSBench and OPT-RSVG) suggest the learned atomic operations capture transferable spatial reasoning patterns. Open questions include how the fixed MCTS budget (10 simulations, depth 5) scales to even larger scenes or multi-target queries, whether the structured (o, p, r) query decomposition handles free-form or adversarial phrasing, and whether the QA+IoU reward design generalizes beyond single-object grounding to relational or counting tasks.
