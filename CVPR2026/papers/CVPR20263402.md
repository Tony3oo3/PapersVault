## Problem

Existing self-supervised pre-training methods for dense visual representations either rely on small, semantically curated datasets (e.g., MAE on ImageNet-1K) or employ excessive benchmark-centric data curation (e.g., DINOv2/v3 injecting Mapillary and ImageNet images with up to 100× repetitive sampling). This leaves a gap: no method has demonstrated that large-scale, spatially structured web data with minimal curation bias can learn strong dense representations for spatial intelligence tasks like depth estimation, 3D reconstruction, and segmentation.

## Input / Output

The pre-training model consumes RGB images at 256×256 resolution, which are split into 16×16 patches and partially masked at 4×4 patch-block granularity. The frozen ViT encoder (e.g., Pixio-H with 631M parameters) outputs dense patch-token features plus 8 global class tokens, which are then fed into lightweight trainable heads (DPT for depth/segmentation, linear regression for metric depth, or a CNN+MLP policy network for robot learning) to produce depth maps, 3D point clouds, segmentation masks, or action predictions.

## Method

Pixio is an enhanced MAE trained on 2B web-crawled images curated via MetaCLIP-S, a self-curation strategy that assigns each image a sampling probability P(i) = min(l_i, 1) based on its reconstruction loss (downsampling easy product/text images) combined with histogram-entropy filtering to remove low-light-interaction content. Three algorithmic modifications to MAE are introduced: (1) a deeper 32-block decoder (vs. the original 8) so the encoder need not sacrifice semantic capacity for pixel regression; (2) larger 4×4 patch-block masking (vs. single-patch) to eliminate spatial reconstruction shortcuts and provide richer local context; and (3) 8 class tokens (vs. 1) to capture diverse global properties like scene type, camera pose, and image style. The largest model (ViT-5.4B/16) is trained for 20B seen samples over 1.3M iterations with batch size 16384, and smaller variants (H, L, B) are obtained via distillation.

## Results

On domain-specific monocular depth estimation (Table 1), Pixio (ViT-H/16, 631M params) reduces NYUv2 RMSE from 0.320 to 0.268 and improves δ₁ from 93.2 to 95.5 over DINOv3-H+ (841M params) with a DPT head, and achieves 0.366 RMSE / 90.8 δ₁ on NYUv2 with a linear head versus DINOv3's 0.559 / 76.3. On feed-forward 3D reconstruction (MapAnything), Pixio improves ScanNet++v2 pose AUC@5 from 68.5 to 80.5 and ETH3D τ from 39.8 to 51.3 over DINOv3. On robot learning (CortexBench), Pixio achieves 78.4 average success rate, outperforming DINOv3 (75.3) and R3M (77.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixio substantially outperforms the original MAE [22] across all evaluated tasks (e.g., NYUv2 depth RMSE 0.465→0.268, ADE20K mIoU 37.2→46.8), demonstrating the necessity of both the data curation and algorithmic changes. Against DINOv2 [34], Pixio wins on nearly all dense prediction benchmarks, with the largest gaps on linear-head depth estimation (NYUv2 RMSE 0.366 vs. 0.560 for DINOv2-g) and 3D reconstruction (ScanNet++v2 pose AUC@5 80.5 vs. 73.2). Against the strongest baseline DINOv3 [42], Pixio achieves better or comparable results on most tasks: it wins on depth estimation (both DPT and linear heads), 3D reconstruction (all three scene types), SAM2 segmentation (Cityscapes 52.1 vs. 50.5, YouTube-VOS 88.6 vs. 87.9), and robot learning (avg 78.4 vs. 75.3), while slightly trailing on KITTI zero-shot depth (rel 0.083 vs. 0.081) and Sintel (rel 0.535 vs. 0.393), which the authors attribute to DINOv3's explicit injection of 1M+ Mapillary driving images. On SAM2 segmentation, Pixio (61.4 LVIS, 63.5 ADE20K) outperforms the originally used Hiera [38] encoder (58.2 LVIS, 59.3 ADE20K) by 3+ points. In robot learning, Pixio surpasses all specialized baselines—VC1 [31] (72.3 avg), R3M [32] (77.2 avg), and Theia [40] (73.4 avg)—achieving 78.4 average success rate, with the largest margin on Adroit (70.7 vs. 74.7 for R3M) and Trifinger (72.8 vs. 67.4 for R3M).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revisits MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) as its core pre-training paradigm, building on MetaCLIP (Xu et al.) for large-scale web image collection. It departs from the DINOv2/DINOv3 line of contrastive learning with benchmark-centric data curation (e.g., 100× repetitive sampling of Mapillary or ImageNet images) by advocating for minimally-curated, spatially-structured web data with a loss-based self-curation strategy. Algorithmically, the changes are deliberately minimal—deeper decoder, larger mask blocks, and multiple class tokens—positioning the work as a data-driven improvement rather than a new learning objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that spatial data curation (via reconstruction-loss-based sampling and histogram-entropy filtering) can close or close the gap between MAE-based and DINOv3-based pre-training on dense prediction tasks, suggesting that the choice of data strategy may matter as much as the learning paradigm. A key open question is whether this approach generalizes beyond single-view 2D images to video or 3D settings, and whether the self-curation signal remains effective as model scale increases further. The paper also acknowledges that domain-specific performance (e.g., KITTI driving) still benefits from targeted data injection, leaving a tension between general-purpose curation and domain adaptation.
