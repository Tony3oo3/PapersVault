## Problem

Two-stage Referring-by-Tracking (RBT) for Referring Multi-Object Tracking offers irreplaceable advantages in training efficiency and incremental deployment, yet has lagged far behind one-stage methods (e.g., iKUN achieves only 10.32 HOTA on Refer-KITTI-v2). The paper identifies two root causes: overly heuristic feature construction that unnecessarily duplicates backbone computation, and fragile correspondence modeling that depends on CLIP's pretrained alignment space, imposing an upper bound on achievable performance.

## Input / Output

FlexHook consumes video frames at 224×672 resolution, natural language expressions (length 25), and pre-computed bounding-box trajectories from an off-the-shelf detector-tracker pair. For each p-frame trajectory segment (p=4), it samples N̂=36 expressions per forward pass and outputs N̂ binary matching scores representing the correspondence between that trajectory segment and each expression. The global image is encoded only once (no separate cropped-patch encoding), and multi-scale features at 4 pyramid levels are decoded and averaged to produce the final score.

## Method

FlexHook redefines two-stage RBT through a hook-like design that preserves the original backbone gradient flow. The Conditioning Hook (C-Hook) replaces dual-encoding with Neighboring Grid Sampling—directly sampling target features from the backbone's raw feature maps via differentiable grid sampling with stochastic augmentations (fragmented segments, Gaussian noise, identity-switch recombination) to bridge the train/inference gap—and Conditioning Enhancement, which decodes M=10 language-conditioned reference points via cross-attention from learnable queries to inject expression-specific visual focus. Temporal Integration concatenates inter-frame grid displacements (serving as feature optical flow) with multi-frame target features, compressed by an MLP. The Pairwise Correspondence Decoder (PCD) eliminates CLIP dependence by performing masked cross-attention between learnable queries and concatenated trajectory/reference/linguistic features, decoded layer-by-layer through a feature pyramid with Focal Loss supervision and a boundary-regularization term to prevent reference-point collapse.

## Results

On Refer-KITTI-V2, FlexHook-best (RoBERTa + ROPE Swin-T) achieves 42.53 HOTA, surpassing the best one-stage method HFF-Tracker (36.18) by 6.35 points and the prior two-stage iKUN (10.32) by 32.21 points. On Refer-KITTI, FlexHook-best reaches 53.83 HOTA, outperforming the strongest one-stage baseline HFF-Tracker (52.41) by 1.42 points. On LaMOT, FlexHook-best achieves 56.77 HOTA, beating LaMOTer (48.45) by 8.32 points. On Refer-Dance, FlexHook-best scores 32.17 HOTA versus iKUN's 29.06.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexHook outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset. Against one-stage RBT methods, FlexHook-best leads HFF-Tracker (52.41) by 1.42 HOTA on Refer-KITTI and by 6.35 HOTA on Refer-KITTI-V2 (42.53 vs. 36.18), and similarly surpasses DKGTracker, SKTracker, CGATracker, MLS-Track, and TempRMOT. The most dramatic gain is over the prior two-stage method iKUN: on Refer-KITTI-V2, FlexHook-best achieves 42.53 HOTA versus iKUN's 10.32, a 32-point improvement that the authors attribute to C-Hook's sampling-based feature construction and PCD's active correspondence modeling. On LaMOT, FlexHook-best (56.77 HOTA) beats the Tracking-by-Referring method LaMOTer (48.45) by 8.32 points and the non-task-specific baselines BYTETrack (46.13) and MOTRv2 (45.19) by over 10 points. On Refer-Dance, FlexHook-best (32.17) improves over iKUN (29.06) by 3.11 HOTA. Notably, FlexHook also maintains competitive performance with a weaker detector-tracker (D-DETR + StrongSORT: 40.73 HOTA on V2), demonstrating robustness to upstream tracking quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the two-stage Referring-by-Tracking (RBT) paradigm introduced by iKUN (CVPR 2024), which decouples tracking from referring by leveraging off-the-shelf trackers and constructing visual features from tracked bounding boxes. It departs from iKUN in two fundamental ways: replacing the dual-encoding strategy (full image + cropped patches through a shared encoder) with a sampling-based Conditioning Hook that directly hooks into the visual backbone's feature stream, and replacing CLIP-based cosine similarity with a learnable Pairwise Correspondence Decoder. The paper positions itself against the now-dominant one-stage RBT methods (TransRMOT, TempRMOT, HFF-Tracker, DKGTracker) that require end-to-end joint optimization, arguing that the two-stage paradigm's advantages in training efficiency and incremental deployment have been overlooked due to poor implementation choices rather than inherent limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling incremental deployment: practitioners can upgrade the referring module without retraining the entire detection-tracking pipeline, and can swap in future tracker improvements without re-optimizing the referring head. The finding that a 30+ HOTA point gap (iKUN 10.32 → FlexHook 42.53 on Refer-KITTI-V2) stems from feature construction and correspondence modeling choices rather than the two-stage paradigm itself challenges the field's assumption that one-stage methods are inherently superior. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary scenarios (LaMOT's TAO subset still shows lower DetA), whether the sampling-based C-Hook generalizes to 3D tracking or longer video sequences, and whether the PCD's pairwise formulation remains efficient as the number of candidate trajectories grows in crowded scenes.
