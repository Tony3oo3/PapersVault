## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily evaluate low-level geometric perception and factual recall (where things are), leaving higher-order cognitive abilities—structured spatial map construction, affordance inference, and knowledge-grounded functional reasoning (what things are for)—largely unexamined. This gap prevents systematic diagnosis of whether multimodal models can integrate perception, memory, and external knowledge to support goal-directed agentic behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263687/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an egocentric indoor video clip (sourced from ARKitScenes and ScanNet++, covering residential, professional, and industrial scenes) with a four-option multiple-choice question. For the four spatial/functional tasks, the model receives only the video and question; for the two knowledge-grounded tasks (Operation Planning, Causal Troubleshooting), models equipped with web-search tools may retrieve device manuals or online instructions before answering. Output is a single selected option, scored by accuracy (25% random baseline).

## Method

SFI-Bench is constructed via a three-stage pipeline: (1) automatic question generation using Gemini-2.5-Pro for multi-pass metadata extraction (objects, attributes, spatial relations, functional roles) followed by task-specific templates and few-shot prompts; (2) human verification where annotators watch each video, validate questions, and provide ground-truth answers (or derive answers from expert-retrieved manuals for knowledge-grounded tasks); and (3) multi-turn human-AI post-hoc filtering that removes questions solvable without visual grounding and refines ambiguous items. The 1,555 questions span six tasks in two cognitive dimensions—Structured Spatial Reasoning (Conditional Counting, Cross-View Multi-hop Path Reasoning, Layout Inference) and Functional Reasoning (Functional Association, Operation Planning, Causal Hypothesis & Troubleshooting)—designed to require compositional logical inference, multi-view spatial memory, and integration of visual evidence with external knowledge rather than frame-level perception. Evaluation is zero-shot with uniform prompt templates across 15+ proprietary and open-source MLLMs, supplemented by controlled ablations (frame-order shuffling, caption-only vs. visual input, reasoning-length analysis) to isolate specific cognitive bottlenecks.

## Results

On SFI-Bench (1,555 multiple-choice questions across 6 tasks), the best proprietary model Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves 73.8% macro-average accuracy, while the best open-source model LLaVA-Video-72B reaches 64.9%. Global Conditional Counting is the hardest task (best: 59.1%), while Layout Inference is the easiest (best: 86.8%). GPT-5 equipped with web search gains up to 8% over its offline counterpart on the two knowledge-grounded functional tasks (Operation Planning and Troubleshooting), underscoring the critical role of external knowledge retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-3.1-Pro leads with 73.8% average, outperforming GPT-5.4-High (72.1%) by 1.7 points and GPT-5 (69.4%) by 4.4 points; the gap widens on Global Conditional Counting (59.1% vs. 58.4% vs. 58.4%) and Layout Inference (86.8% vs. 81.1% vs. 81.5%). Among open-source models, LLaVA-Video-72B (64.9%) surpasses Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (60.7%) by 4.2 points and even beats Gemini-2.5-Flash (55.3%) on several spatial tasks, though it trails all proprietary models by at least 12 points overall. A striking finding is that open-source reasoning (Thinking) variants trained with RLVR show minimal or negative gains over their instruct counterparts—e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (57.9%) actually underperforms the Instruct version (60.7%)—indicating poor transfer of RLVR-trained reasoning to spatial-functional tasks. GPT-5 with web search achieves up to 8% higher accuracy than without search on Operation Planning and Troubleshooting, but weaker models (GPT-5 low-reasoning, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Qwen3-VL-Plus) degrade when given the tool, revealing that strong reasoning is a prerequisite for effective tool use.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a deliberate departure from prior spatial-reasoning benchmarks like VSI-Bench, which the authors characterize as probing only 'geometric perception and factual recall'—the first step in a developmental hierarchy. Rather than introducing a new architecture or training method, SFI-Bench repurposes existing egocentric video datasets (ARKitScenes, ScanNet++) and reformulates familiar task labels (counting, spatial relations) as cognition-level challenges requiring compositional inference, multi-hop memory, and external knowledge retrieval. It also extends the evaluation scope beyond spatial reasoning to include functional/affordance reasoning and knowledge-grounded troubleshooting, dimensions the authors argue have been 'largely unexamined' in prior MLLM benchmarks. The work connects to the broader VLA (vision-language-action) literature by framing these capabilities as prerequisites for grounded agentic behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SFI-Bench fills a clear gap by providing a systematic diagnostic for the spatial-functional reasoning bottleneck that limits current MLLMs from serving as grounded agents, and its 1,555 expert-annotated questions across six tasks will likely become a standard reference for evaluating this capability. The finding that RLVR-trained open-source models fail to transfer reasoning gains from mathematical domains to spatial-functional tasks is practically important for the community investing in reasoning-enhanced MLLMs. Open questions remain: how to build models that maintain temporally coherent cognitive maps (rather than static abstractions), how to integrate web-searched knowledge without introducing noise for weaker reasoners, and whether the observed reasoning-length saturation reflects a fundamental limitation or an artifact of current training objectives.
