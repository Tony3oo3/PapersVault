## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses for vehicle-to-vehicle collaborative perception either assume the ego vehicle is inherently trustworthy or rely on additional binary classifiers, making them impractical in fully untrusted-vehicle settings where any vehicle (including the ego) may be compromised. Moreover, verification costs in sampling-based methods scale linearly with fleet size, and no prior method achieves both constant per-frame detection cost and complete attacker identification without prior knowledge of the attack.

## Input / Output

The method consumes LiDAR point clouds from the ego vehicle, encoded feature maps (W×H×C) broadcast by n−1 collaborators, and the previous frame's perception output as a self-referential trusted signal. It produces a set of identified malicious vehicles, defended perception bounding boxes after filtering adversarial features, and an estimated attacker count m. Notably, the previous frame's perception replaces any trusted-vehicle assumption, and the pseudo-random grouping ensures only 2 verifications per frame regardless of fleet size n.

## Method

PRBI exploits the observation that benign inter-frame Jaccard similarity stays around 0.8 while adversarial frames drop below 0.3, using the prior frame's output as a frame-wise self-supervision reference. Each frame, vehicles are split into two pseudo-random groups (the ⌊m⌋ most suspicious vs. the rest), and each group's fused perception is validated against the reference via Jaccard similarity, yielding only two verifications per frame. The attacker count is estimated as m = log₂(n·N / Σβᵢ) from the empirical all-benign group ratio, and per-vehicle benign probabilities are computed via Bayes' theorem combining short-term and long-term detection statistics. A T-test on a sliding window of m values determines convergence, at which point the ⌊m⌋ vehicles with lowest benign probability are flagged as attackers. Theoretical proofs establish that floor rounding on m guarantees monotonic convergence to the true attacker count k, while ceiling or nearest-integer rounding introduce systematic bias.

## Results

On V2X-Sim with n=5 vehicles and k=2 attackers under the V2VNet backbone, PRBI recovers 79.4%, 81.0%, and 86.9% of the AP loss caused by PGD, BIM, and C&W attacks respectively, restoring AP@0.5 from 10.68–17.02 (attacked) to 68.76–71.87 (defended), approaching the benign upper bound of 80.73. PRBI requires only 2.5 verifications per frame on average (range 2–3), compared to 7.1 for ROBOSAC and 6.7 for PASAC. The method maintains 100% malicious vehicle identification rate and converges within ~4 frames on average, with at most 6% benign misclassification at 40% attacker ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRBI outperforms both SOTA baselines across all attack types and fusion strategies. Against ROBOSAC (ICCV 2023), PRBI achieves 1.3–3.5% higher AP@0.5 (e.g., 69.85 vs. 64.13 under PGD on V2VNet) while requiring only 2.5 verifications per frame versus ROBOSAC's 7.1 average (and up to 30.3 max). Against PASAC (AAAI 2025), PRBI gains 1.0–4.0% AP@0.5 (e.g., 69.85 vs. 68.39 under PGD) with roughly half the verification cost (2.5 vs. 6.7 average). The efficiency advantage is most pronounced at higher attacker ratios: at 80% attackers, PRBI averages 2.86 verifications while ROBOSAC peaks at 17 and PASAC at 8. PRBI also exceeds the single-vehicle lower bound (56.35 AP@0.5) by 13.5 points, confirming that defended collaborative perception still provides substantial gains over isolated sensing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both sampling-based defenses (ROBOSAC, PASAC) and classifier-based defenses (CP-Guard, MADE) in collaborative perception, which all assume a trusted ego vehicle as a reference signal. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it repurposes classical statistical tools—Bayesian posterior estimation, T-test hypothesis testing, and random-sampling-without-replacement probability—into a unified defense framework for the 'fully untrusted-vehicle' setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing the ego-trust assumption with a temporal self-referential signal (inter-frame Jaccard similarity), and replacing linear-cost verification with a constant-cost pseudo-random binary grouping strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the ego-trust assumption, PRBI addresses a gap that limits all prior CP defenses in realistic deployments where any vehicle (including the ego) can be compromised via LiDAR injection or data interception. The constant 2-verification-per-frame cost is practically significant for large-scale V2V networks where linear verification becomes infeasible. However, convergence can be slow for larger fleets (up to ~500 frames for n=20 at 50% attacker ratio), evaluation is limited to a single dataset (V2X-Sim) with n≤5 in most experiments, and the method assumes all vehicles share the same perception model—open questions include scalability to heterogeneous fleets, robustness to adaptive attacks that exploit the temporal reference, and whether the 6% benign misclassification at 40% attacker ratio is acceptable in safety-critical driving.
