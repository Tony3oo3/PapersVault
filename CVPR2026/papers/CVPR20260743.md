## Problem

State-of-the-art video generation relies almost exclusively on diffusion-based models, which are inherently non-causal (parallel denoising of all frames), non-end-to-end in training (per-noise-level supervision), and suffer from compounding exposure bias in autoregressive rollouts. Normalizing flows offer exact likelihood, non-iterative sampling, and native invertibility, but no prior work has demonstrated that they can produce high-quality autoregressive video at practical resolutions, leaving open whether NFs are a viable alternative backbone for video.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a text prompt (or a conditioning first-frame image for I2V, or a full source clip for V2V) and produces a 480p, 16 fps video of 81 frames (~5 s) in pixel space. All generation occurs in the 48-channel latent space of a pretrained 3D causal VAE (spatial ×16, temporal ×4 compression); the VAE decoder is applied only at the final step. Because the flow is invertible, the same backbone serves as both encoder and decoder across T2V, I2V, and V2V without any task-specific modules—conditioning frames are simply flow-encoded to populate the KV cache before autoregressive rollout.

## Method

STARFlow-V factorizes the video density via a global-local autoregressive flow: a deep causal Transformer (f_D) models inter-frame dependencies in a compact latent sequence u, while a shallow stack of alternating-mask flow blocks (f_S) captures within-frame local structure, yielding p(x) = ∏_n p_D(u_n | u_{<n}) |det J_{f_S}(x_n)|. Training is end-to-end exact MLE on σ-smoothed data (noise-augmented NF training), and a jointly trained lightweight causal denoiser (flow-score matching) removes the residual noise while preserving one-frame look-ahead causality. For fast inference, the paper introduces block-wise Jacobi iteration that parallelizes the fixed-point inversion within contiguous token blocks (sequential across blocks with KV caching) and a video-aware initialization from the previous frame, achieving ~15× lower latency than strict autoregressive decoding. A pipelined schedule overlaps f_D and f_S execution, and a sliding-window KV-cache rebuild enables streaming generation beyond the 5-second training horizon.

## Results

On VBench text-to-video evaluation, STARFlow-V achieves a total score of 78.67 (80.24 quality, 72.37 semantic), and 79.70 with GPT-augmented Rewriter prompts (80.76 quality, 75.43 semantic). This makes it the first normalizing-flow-based video model to reach the same performance range as recent causal diffusion baselines, though it trails the strongest diffusion models (Wan2.1-T2V at 83.69, HunyuanVideo at 83.24) and closed-source systems (Veo3 at 85.06). The non-causal variant scores 79.22, confirming that enforcing causal structure incurs negligible perceptual quality loss. Qualitatively, STARFlow-V sustains coherent 30-second generations well beyond its 5-second training horizon, where NOVA and Self-Forcing exhibit blurring and color drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among autoregressive diffusion baselines, STARFlow-V (78.67) outperforms MAGI-1-distill (77.92) and CogVideo (67.01) on total VBench score, but trails NOVA (80.12), CausVid (81.20), and Self-Forcing (84.31) by 1.5–5.6 points. Against non-autoregressive diffusion models, STARFlow-V beats OpenSora-v1.1 (75.66) by 3.0 points but falls short of CogVideoX (80.91), HunyuanVideo (83.24), and Wan2.1-T2V (83.69). The gap to closed-source systems (Gen-3 at 82.32, Veo3 at 85.06) is 2.7–6.4 points. Notably, the non-causal variant (79.22) scores nearly identically to the causal version, indicating that the causal constraint does not sacrifice perceptual quality. Qualitatively, STARFlow-V demonstrates superior long-horizon stability at 30 seconds compared to NOVA (gradual blurring, identity loss) and WAN-2.1-Causal-FT (strong artifacts, color distortion), and maintains coherence where Self-Forcing degrades structurally.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARFlow-V extends STARFlow (Gu et al., NeurIPS 2025), which demonstrated that autoregressive normalizing flows with causal Transformers can scale competitively for high-resolution image synthesis, into the substantially harder video domain. It adopts the noise-augmented training strategy from TARFlow (Zhai et al., ICML 2024) but replaces both the decoder-fine-tuning and score-based denoising post-processing steps with a novel flow-score matching objective that trains a lightweight causal denoiser jointly with the flow. The key architectural departure from STARFlow is the global-local restructuring: the shallow flow blocks are restricted to within-frame operations while only the deep block propagates inter-frame causal context, a design motivated by video's spatiotemporal structure that has no direct analogue in the image setting. The paper positions itself explicitly against the dominant diffusion-based paradigm for video, arguing that NFs offer end-to-end training, exact likelihood, and native invertibility as practical advantages for autoregressive and streaming generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration, to the authors' knowledge, that normalizing flows can produce high-quality autoregressive video generation at 480p/81f, challenging the near-universal reliance on diffusion backbones in the video domain. The invertible, likelihood-based formulation is particularly relevant for world-model applications (gaming, robotics, embodied AI) where exact density evaluation and causal, streamable rollouts are desirable properties that diffusion models lack by construction. However, STARFlow-V still trails the strongest closed-source diffusion systems (Veo 3, Gen-3) on VBench, inference remains far from real-time despite the ~15× Jacobi speedup, and the authors report no clean scaling law under current data curation—leaving open whether NFs can close the quality gap at scale. Future work on distillation, pruning, and data selection will determine whether this paradigm shift is durable or remains a proof-of-concept.
