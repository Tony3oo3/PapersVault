## Problem

Deep learning-based online mapping models exhibit significant performance drops under geographically disjoint evaluation splits, but existing benchmarks conflate two distinct failure modes: memorization of location-specific sensor features and overfitting to known map geometries. No prior evaluation protocol systematically disentangles these effects, and dataset-level geometric biases (beyond the well-studied geographic overlap) remain unquantified, leading to inflated and unreliable generalization assessments.

## Input / Output

The evaluation protocol consumes standard multimodal sensor inputs (lidar point clouds, camera images, radar) paired with vectorized HD map ground truth, but additionally requires per-sample geographic distance to the nearest training sample and a class-wise geometric similarity cost computed via discrete Fréchet distance with minimum-cost bipartite matching over all point orderings (original, reversed, and cyclic permutations for polygons). Beyond the models' standard vectorized map element predictions, the framework produces diagnostic scores: a Fréchet-distance-based reconstruction statistic (median M ± IQR of matched costs), a localization overfitting score O_loc, a geometric overfitting score O_geom, MST-based geometric diversity for each split, and a symmetric coverage-based inter-split similarity. The sparsification module outputs a pruned training set with reduced redundancy.

## Method

The paper introduces a Fréchet distance-based performance measure (median of class-wise bipartite-matched costs) that captures per-element shape fidelity with point-order awareness, avoiding the threshold calibration and permutation invariance limitations of Chamfer-distance mAP. To disentangle failure modes, validation samples are stratified by geographic distance d(v) to the nearest training sample and by geometric similarity s(v), with the close/far subsets further aligned on s(v) distributions to isolate localization overfitting (O_loc = relative M drop between aligned close and far sets). Geometric overfitting (O_geom) is quantified as the negative slope of a weighted linear fit of M across similarity bins within the geographically distant subset. At the dataset level, a minimum spanning tree over pairwise Fréchet similarity costs yields a geometric diversity score (sum of MST edge weights), and a symmetric nearest-neighbor coverage measure quantifies inter-split geometric similarity. An MST-based sparsification strategy removes samples connected by edges below a similarity threshold, retaining one representative per cluster, which reduces training size while improving geometric balance and model performance.

## Results

Across four state-of-the-art online mapping models on nuScenes and Argoverse 2, all exhibit significant failure modes: localization overfitting scores (O_loc) range from 7.29% (MapTRv2 on Argoverse 2 original) to 57.07% (MapQR on nuScenes original), while geometric overfitting scores (O_geom) range from 7.98% to 24.47%. On the original nuScenes split, MapTRv2 achieves mAP of 60.95 and Fréchet-based measure M of 1.94±3.05, dropping to mAP 24.96–28.53 and M 3.24–4.07 under geographically disjoint splits. The proposed geometric splits yield the highest geomsim(T,V) (21.08 m for nuScenes, 26.51 m for Argoverse 2) and further reduce performance, confirming that map geometry similarity drives generalization. MST-based sparsification at thresholds 0.1–1 reduces training samples substantially while maintaining geomdiv(T) and improving mAP, outperforming random sampling at equivalent sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four online mapping architectures—MapTR, MapTRv2, MapQR, and MGMap—using the proposed failure-mode scores rather than proposing a new model. MapQR exhibits the most severe localization overfitting on nuScenes (O_loc = 57.07%), more than double that of MapTR (24.42%) and MapTRv2 (24.73%), indicating its query-refinement decoder heavily memorizes location-specific features. Conversely, MapTRv2 generalizes best on Argoverse 2 with the lowest O_loc (7.29%), suggesting its end-to-end design is less prone to geographic memorization on that dataset. For geometric overfitting, MGMap shows the highest O_geom on Argoverse 2 geo.[37] (24.47%) and nuScenes original (24.12%), while MapQR achieves the lowest O_geom on Argoverse 2 geo.[37] (9.08%) and MapTRv2 on nuScenes geo.[19] (9.75%). No single model dominates across all failure modes and datasets, confirming that architectural choices (query design, auxiliary masking, end-to-end training) differentially affect localization versus geometric overfitting. The key takeaway is that all four models show consistent performance degradation as geometric novelty increases, validating the need for the proposed disentangled evaluation protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the geographic bias analysis of Lilja et al. (CVPR 2024), which showed that standard online mapping splits leak location-specific information, by introducing a second, orthogonal axis of bias: geometric similarity of map structures between training and validation scenes. Rather than proposing a new mapping architecture, it repurposes existing DETR-based BEV models (MapTR, MapTRv2, MapQR, MGMap) as testbeds to validate a new evaluation protocol built on discrete Fréchet distance and minimum spanning tree analysis. The key conceptual departure is the explicit disentangling of two previously conflated failure modes—localization overfitting (memorizing sensor features at specific locations) and geometric overfitting (memorizing road topology shapes)—and showing they must be measured independently because d(v) and s(v) are strongly correlated (r=0.724).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the online mapping community with a more honest evaluation protocol: the Fréchet-based measure M avoids threshold tuning and works on small subsets, while the O_loc and O_geom scores give practitioners a diagnostic for whether a model's apparent performance is driven by memorization. The MST-based sparsification strategy is immediately practical—removing redundant training samples at low similarity thresholds improves performance while shrinking dataset size, offering a concrete recipe for dataset curation. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to temporal/multi-frame models, whether learned similarity approximations can scale the MST construction to larger datasets, and whether map-geometry-aware training objectives can directly reduce geometric overfitting rather than only exposing it.
