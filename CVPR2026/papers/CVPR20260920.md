## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for remote sensing suffer from two critical limitations: semantic poverty, where simple class-name templates cannot capture the diverse visual appearances of the same category across regions, seasons, and sensor types; and visual rigidity, where frozen visual encoders fail to adapt to RS-specific features such as oblique views and large spatial context. This paper tackles both gaps simultaneously through a knowledge distillation framework that enriches textual supervision and unlocks visual adaptability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes top-down remote sensing images (various spatial resolutions across six classification benchmarks) paired with class labels, and at inference produces either a class probability distribution (for few-shot or base-to-novel classification) or a cosine-similarity ranking over a frozen gallery (for cross-modal retrieval). The text side is not limited to bare class names: during training, up to 50 LLM-generated aerial-view descriptions per class are verified and aggregated into a single textual prototype, but at inference the student operates independently using only its learned prompts and the standard class-name prompt. All experiments use a ViT-B/32 student backbone (GeoRSCLIP) with a frozen ViT-H/14 teacher, and the student requires no gallery recomputation for retrieval.

## Method

AVION is a teacher-to-student distillation framework in which a frozen GeoRSCLIP (ViT-H/14) teacher constructs semantically rich textual prototypes offline: Gemini 2.5 Flash generates up to 50 RS-aware descriptions per class, an RS-Flag filter removes non-aerial or off-topic candidates, and a selective prototype aggregation step scores each candidate against the class visual prototype (cosine similarity), prunes outliers via a median/MAD threshold, and combines the survivors with softmax-normalized weights into a single prototype vector. The student (GeoRSCLIP ViT-B/32) receives learnable deep prompts in both its vision and text encoders and is trained with a tri-aspect alignment objective: (i) visual alignment minimizes cosine distance between student and teacher visual embeddings, (ii) textual alignment aligns student text embeddings with the teacher's verified prototypes, and (iii) similarity logit alignment matches the full teacher probability distribution over all classes via temperature-scaled KL divergence (τ=2), supplemented by a standard cross-entropy task loss. The teacher is used only during training; at inference the student operates standalone with its learned prompts, requiring well under 1% of backbone parameters to be updated.

## Results

On six optical remote sensing benchmarks (AID, RESISC-45, EuroSAT, WHURS19, PatternNet, UCMerced), AVION achieves 74.27% (1-shot), 88.31% (4-shot), and 93.69% (16-shot) average few-shot classification accuracy, outperforming all PEFT baselines at every shot level. In the base-to-novel generalization setting, AVION reaches 95.64% base accuracy and 79.94% novel accuracy (HM 87.05%), the only method to exceed the GeoRSCLIP zero-shot novel-class score (79.75%) while also achieving the highest base accuracy. For cross-modal retrieval, AVION improves mean recall by +1.11 pp on RSITMD (52.92% vs. 51.81%) and +0.93 pp on RSICD (39.80% vs. 38.87%) over full fine-tuning of GeoRSCLIP, using fewer than 1% trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVION ties APPLeNet at 1-shot (74.27%) but pulls ahead progressively with more samples, reaching +2.65 pp at 8-shot (91.85% vs. 89.20%) and +2.08 pp at 16-shot (93.69% vs. 91.61%). Against text-only prompt methods (CoOp, CoCoOp, PromptKD), AVION leads by 4.3–13.4 pp across shot levels, confirming that visual-side prompting and LLM-enriched textual prototypes are critical for RS imagery. In base-to-novel generalization, AVION (HM 87.05%) surpasses the next-best APPLeNet (HM 83.84%) by 3.21 pp and is the sole method to exceed GeoRSCLIP's zero-shot novel-class accuracy (79.94% vs. 79.75%), whereas all other PEFT baselines degrade novel-class performance by 4–10 pp. For cross-modal retrieval, AVION outperforms full fine-tuning of GeoRSCLIP (GeoRSCLIP-FT) on both RSITMD and RSICD in every R@K metric, demonstrating that its lightweight prompt-based adaptation with LLM-guided distillation is more effective than opening all backbone parameters. Compared to zero-shot VLMs (CLIP, RemoteCLIP, GeoRSCLIP), AVION gains 15.7–20.7 pp at 4-shot, showing that even a strong domain-pretrained initialization benefits substantially from the proposed tri-aspect distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVION extends the prompt-learning lineage for VLMs (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe) and the distillation-to-prompts paradigm (PromptKD) by introducing two targeted mechanisms for remote sensing: LLM-generated, vision-verified textual prototypes to combat 'semantic poverty,' and learnable visual prompts to address 'visual rigidity.' Unlike prior PEFT methods that typically update only the text encoder or use simple class-name templates, AVION jointly tunes both encoders under a tri-aspect distillation objective (visual, textual, and logit alignment) guided by an offline teacher. The selective prototype aggregation step, which uses teacher visual embeddings to score and weight LLM candidates, is a novel component not present in prior prompt-learning or distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, lightweight path to adapting large RS foundation models (GeoRSCLIP) to new scenarios with minimal trainable parameters and no gallery recomputation, which is important for deployment-constrained settings. The finding that AVION is the only PEFT method to simultaneously improve base-class accuracy and avoid degrading novel-class generalization (surpassing the GeoRSCLIP zero-shot baseline on novel classes) addresses a persistent trade-off in prompt learning. Limitations include dependence on an external LLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash) for prototype generation, evaluation restricted to optical RS benchmarks, and modest retrieval gains (+1 pp) over full fine-tuning, leaving open whether the approach transfers to SAR, multispectral, or non-RS domains.
