## Problem

GUI agents built on multimodal LLMs are highly vulnerable to pop-up-based environmental injection attacks, where a single malicious pop-up can divert the agent's attention and trigger unsafe actions. Existing defenses either require costly retraining (e.g., DPO, RFT) or rely on prompt-level alerts that fail against inductive pop-ups whose text is semantically aligned with the user request. Crucially, prior work treats the model as a black box and leaves the internal mechanism of vulnerability unexplained.

## Input / Output

LaSM operates as a post-hoc, plug-and-play module on top of a frozen MLLM-based GUI agent. It takes the same inputs as the underlying agent (an RGB GUI screenshot and a natural-language user instruction) and produces the same output (a GUI action such as clicking <icon-cross> or <button-confirm>). The only modification is that during the forward pass, the projection weight matrices of attention (W_Q, W_K, W_V, W_O) and MLP (W_up, W_gate, W_down) in a narrow layer range are pre-multiplied by a scalar α before computation, requiring no architectural change or retraining.

## Method

LaSM first performs a progressive range-narrowing search: starting from all layers, it iteratively fixes the lower bound and decreases the upper bound (or vice versa), measuring the Defense Success Rate at each step to identify the narrow layer interval where scaling most improves correct pop-up-closing decisions. Within this interval, it jointly scales both attention and MLP weights by a small factor α (typically 1.1–1.2), which amplifies task-relevant saliency in mid-level semantic layers while leaving high-level aggregation layers untouched. The key empirical finding is that mid-level layers (e.g., L7–18 for Qwen2-VL-7B) encode the vision-language alignment critical for distinguishing legitimate UI elements from deceptive pop-ups, whereas scaling the deepest layers disrupts high-level semantic aggregation and harms performance. Ablations confirm that joint scaling of both attention and MLP is necessary—scaling either component alone drops DSR below the no-defense baseline—because the two modules jointly regulate the nonlinear amplification and suppression of token representations at decision-critical depths.

## Results

On Qwen2-vl-7B, LaSM (Layers 7–18, α=1.1) raises the average Defense Success Rate (DSR) from 18.9% to 66.4% under overlay injection and from 14.8% to 68.3% under inductive injection when applied to the no-defense baseline. When composed with CoT alerts, LaSM pushes DSR to 100.0% (overlay) and 99.8% (inductive) on Qwen2-vl-7B. On LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-13B (Layers 12–28, α=1.2), LaSM improves average DSR from 68.6% to 81.2% (overlay) and 60.8% to 78.0% (inductive). In multi-step AndroidControl episodes, LaSM increases Task Success Rate from 18.75% to 30.36% with negligible impact on action-type and grounding accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaSM is evaluated as a plug-in on top of three baseline defenses: DPO fine-tuning [19], Direct Alert prompting [38], and CoT Alert prompting [38]. Against DPO, LaSM yields the largest absolute gains: on Qwen2-vl-7B, DPO alone achieves only 15.9% (overlay) and 10.5% (inductive) average DSR, while DPO+LaSM reaches 63.9% and 65.2% respectively (+48.0 and +54.7 points). Against Direct Alert, LaSM also provides substantial improvements, e.g., raising Qwen2-vl-7B inductive DSR from 42.8% to 79.8% (+37.0) and LLaVA-v1.6-13B overlay DSR from 28.7% to 78.3% (+49.6). Against CoT Alert, which is already strong (96.3% overlay on Qwen2-vl-7B), LaSM closes the remaining gap to 100.0% overlay and 99.8% inductive. Notably, LaSM never degrades performance relative to any baseline (all Δ values are positive or near-zero), confirming its plug-and-play safety. The paper also reports that DPO alone performs poorly under inductive injection (near-zero on LLaVA), whereas LaSM consistently recovers robustness across all injection types and pop-up sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the environmental injection attack literature (Ma et al. [19], Zhang et al. [38]) that established pop-up windows as a critical vulnerability for MLLM-based GUI agents, and on the attention-visualization methodology of Zhang et al. [37] for probing MLLM perception. It departs from prior defenses—retraining-based approaches (DPO, SecAlign) and prompt-level alerts—by treating the model as a white box: rather than modifying inputs or retraining weights, LaSM selectively rescales attention and MLP projection matrices in a narrow layer range identified through a progressive range-narrowing search. Conceptually, it repurposes the transformer layer-scaling idea (analogous to LayerScale) as a post-hoc, training-free intervention, positioning itself between black-box prompt engineering and costly fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GUI agents are deployed on consumer devices, the ability to defend against environmental injection without retraining or architectural changes is practically important; LaSM's plug-and-play design lowers the deployment barrier significantly. The finding that mid-level semantic layers (not the deep layers where attention divergence is most visible) are the safety-critical ones, and that scaling either attention or MLP alone *worsens* performance below the no-defense baseline, provides a useful architectural insight for future interpretability work. Open questions include whether the per-model layer ranges and α coefficients transfer across model updates or fine-tuning, whether the approach generalizes to other environmental attack types (e.g., fine-print injections, background content manipulation), and whether the modest multi-step TSR gain (18.75% → 30.36%) is sufficient for real-world deployment where compounding errors across steps remain a challenge.
