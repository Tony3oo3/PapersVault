## Problem

Existing incremental learning methods are evaluated under single-domain class-incremental or multi-domain task-incremental settings that assume known task identifiers and abundant data, assumptions that fail in real-world deployments where task boundaries are ambiguous, domains are heterogeneous, and only a few labeled examples are available. This paper introduces Heterogeneous Incremental Learning (HIL) as a more realistic benchmark and proposes a model-editing-based approach to address the resulting stability-plasticity tradeoff under limited supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images paired with text prompts (5-shot per class) through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, and produces a class label by cosine similarity matching over a global label space spanning all 11 domains without access to task or domain identifiers at inference time. The key-value statistics are accumulated as second-order covariance matrices (KᵀK and KᵀV) across all batches, yielding a constant-size representation regardless of dataset size, and the final edited projection is a closed-form matrix update to the FFN output layers.

## Method

SAME extends the FFN key-value editing paradigm from LLMs to VLMs by sparsely fine-tuning only the FFN output projection matrices (W_out) with a binary mask that freezes 30% of parameters per task, then extracting task-specific key-value pairs as covariance statistics from the adapted model. A double-anchor constraint regularizes the update: a knowledge anchor (λ_k‖K₀W−V₀‖²) aligns the edited projection with the original pre-trained model's feature mappings, while a parameter anchor (λ_p‖W−W_out‖²) constrains weight drift. Aggregating key-value statistics across all tasks yields a symmetric positive definite linear system whose closed-form solution W* = H⁻¹(K₁ᵀV₁ + λ_k K₀ᵀV₀ + λ_p W_out) provides gradient-free, order-agnostic knowledge injection with constant memory cost and no additional learnable parameters.

## Results

Under the Split-2, 5-shot HIL setting on 11 visual domains (22 sequential tasks), SAME achieves 66.0% average accuracy, outperforming the best continual learning baseline (MoE-Adapter, 59.2%) by 6.8% and the best model fusion method (TIES-Merging, 61.7%) by 4.3%. SAME retains 95.8% of the Oracle (FFN) upper bound of 68.9%. Under the harder Split-3 setting (33 tasks), SAME maintains 61.4% average accuracy versus 56.9% for the best IL method (MoE-Adapter), demonstrating scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAME (66.0% avg) decisively outperforms all five IL baselines: MoE-Adapter (59.2%), GNSP (58.7%), ZSCL (56.1%), DIKI (53.9%), and Wise-FT (50.8%), with the largest gap over MoE-Adapter at +6.8%. Against the three model fusion baselines, SAME also leads: TIES-Merging (61.7%, +4.3%), Task Arithmetic (60.2%, +5.8%), and Consensus TA (58.6%, +7.4%). SAME is the only method that approaches the Oracle upper bound (68.9%), retaining 95.8% of its performance, whereas the best IL method (MoE-Adapter) retains only 85.9%. Notably, SAME achieves the highest per-domain accuracy on EuroSAT (80.9 vs. 67.7 for GNSP), Food-101 (88.4), MNIST (89.5), and OxfordPet (88.1), while trailing slightly on Aircraft (26.1 vs. 28.6 for MoE-Adapter) and Caltech101 (57.9 vs. 73.1 for DIKI), suggesting the method excels on domains with larger class counts or more uniform distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the FFN key-value editing paradigm from LLMs (ROME, MEMIT) to vision-language models, specifically CLIP, where prior model-editing work has been largely unexplored. It departs from conventional incremental learning (CIL/TIL) by introducing HIL, a multi-domain, task-ID-free, few-shot evaluation setting that jointly stresses intra-domain continuity and cross-domain heterogeneity. The double-anchor constraint (knowledge anchor + parameter anchor) and sparse masking are novel regularizers not present in prior model-editing or continual-learning methods, and the resulting closed-form solution avoids iterative optimization entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HIL provides a more deployment-realistic benchmark for incremental VLM adaptation, and the order-agnostic, constant-memory, closed-form nature of SAME makes it attractive for practical sequential deployment where task order is unpredictable and storage is constrained. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP ViT-B/16 at 5-shot across 11 fixed datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger VLMs, more aggressive few-shot regimes, or truly open-ended domain streams. A key open question is whether the covariance-based key-value summarization remains sufficient as the number of accumulated tasks grows well beyond 22.
