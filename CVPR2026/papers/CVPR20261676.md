## Problem

Purely text-driven instruction-based video editing struggles in complex scenes with multiple similar objects, physical constraints, and dynamic interactions, because ambiguous linguistic signals force the model to simultaneously handle cross-frame semantics, spatial localization, and physical plausibility. Existing methods lack an explicit mechanism to decouple *what* to edit from *where* to edit, resulting in localization drift, erroneous target selection, and physically implausible edits such as violated motion trajectories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (sampled as keyframes) and a natural-language editing instruction as input, and produces a temporally coherent edited video at 720×1280 resolution. Internally, the planner emits a temporally aligned sequence of normalized bounding boxes [t, x₁, y₁, x₂, y₂] plus an enriched instruction string; the Guide branch converts these into per-frame binary masks at the same spatial resolution as the input frames; and the Editor composites the final output by applying edits only within mask regions while preserving the original video outside them.

## Method

CoT-Edit introduces a three-stage Plan-Guide-Edit pipeline built on the Wan2.2 5B diffusion backbone. The Planner is a CoT-enhanced MLLM (Qwen3-VL-32B) that performs a five-step structured reasoning process—task parsing, cross-frame perception, physics/temporal consistency modeling, enriched instruction synthesis, and bounding-box output—explicitly resolving spatial and physical ambiguities before any pixel-level generation. The Guide branch uses the bounding boxes as hard spatial constraints and video features plus the enriched instruction as soft constraints to generate spatiotemporally consistent masks, with a bidirectional coupling to the Editor via a Reverse-Connector (editor features → mask branch) and a Mask-Connector (mask features → editor layers) that iteratively refine both. The Editor injects the masks, Qwen-VL visual-language features (first 1024 tokens, projected from 3584 to 3072 channels via MLP), and the original video latent (channel-concatenated with noise) into the diffusion backbone through dedicated cross-attention modules. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the Editor and Mask branches are trained separately on segmentation and editing datasets; then both are jointly fine-tuned on ~100k high-quality video editing pairs with precise mask annotations, with the connectors initialized to zero to preserve the pretrained diffusion distribution.

## Results

On the Koala-36M evaluation set (100 sampled videos with diverse editing instructions), CoT-Edit achieves the best FVD of 1015.67 (vs. 1076.44 for the next-best OmniVideo), the highest CLIPScore of 0.445, and the top Gemini-rated scores for Physical Rule (0.741), Spatial Relation (0.841), Instruction Following (0.629), and Overall Editing Quality (0.648). It also leads on Background Consistency (0.974), Motion Smoothness (1.18), and Aesthetics (0.62), while being marginally lower on Temporal Consistency (0.945 vs. 0.961 for Lucy-1.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoT-Edit outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics, with the most pronounced gains in physical plausibility and spatial reasoning. Against the strongest competitor OmniVideo (which combines MLLMs with diffusion), CoT-Edit improves FVD from 1076.44 to 1015.67, Physical Rule from 0.590 to 0.741, Spatial Relation from 0.641 to 0.841, and Overall Editing Quality from 0.604 to 0.648, while OmniVideo retains a slight edge on Temporal Consistency (0.954 vs. 0.945). Lucy-1.1, the best end-to-end diffusion-based editor, is surpassed on FVD (1488.12 → 1015.67), CLIPScore (0.376 → 0.445), Physical Rule (0.488 → 0.741), and Instruction Following (0.562 → 0.629), though it leads on TC (0.961 vs. 0.945). The remaining baselines—InsV2V, StableV2V, InsViE, and AnyV2V—are clearly outperformed across nearly all dimensions, with FVD gaps exceeding 1000–3000 points and Physical Rule scores below 0.40, highlighting the substantial advantage of explicit CoT-guided spatial planning over purely text-driven or implicit-attention approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the instruction-based video editing line (InstructPix2Pix → InstructVid2Vid → InsViE/Lucy-1.1) by introducing an explicit Plan-Guide-Edit decomposition: a CoT-enhanced MLLM planner generates bounding-box sequences and enriched instructions that condition a separate mask branch and a diffusion editor, rather than relying on end-to-end text-to-video editing. It departs from InstructX and OmniVideo, which use MLLMs for implicit semantic guidance, by making the MLLM's spatial and physical reasoning explicit and verifiable (normalized bounding boxes per keyframe). The bidirectional connector between the mask and editor branches is a novel architectural coupling not seen in prior mask-conditioned video editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that explicit structured reasoning (CoT) about spatial localization and physical constraints before diffusion-based generation can substantially close the gap in physical plausibility and spatial accuracy that purely text-driven methods leave open, while requiring far less aligned training data (~100k pairs vs. millions). Open questions include whether the CoT planner generalizes to longer videos or highly dynamic multi-object scenes, whether the Gemini-based evaluation protocol introduces systematic bias, and whether the two-stage modular training can be replaced by a more efficient end-to-end alternative. The approach also raises the broader question of how much of the 'planning' burden in generative video tasks can be offloaded to an MLLM's world knowledge versus learned in the diffusion backbone.
