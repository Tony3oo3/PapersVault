## Problem

Existing video correspondence methods are either sparse and query-conditioned (TAP-style point trackers) or restricted to adjacent-frame pairs (optical flow), leaving the dense, all-pairs structure of a video implicit. This paper formalizes All-Pairs Tracking (APT): predicting dense displacement and visibility for every ordered frame pair in a sequence, so that per-pixel trajectories can be read out uniformly and sequence-wide temporal consistency becomes a first-class modeling objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263097/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video as a sequence of RGB frames (trained on 384×512 clips of 30–60 frames; evaluated on up to 1920×1080 at 120 frames) and produces, for every ordered frame pair (s,t), a full-resolution dense displacement field, a per-pixel visibility probability, and a confidence score. Unlike TAP methods that propagate from a single source frame, the all-pairs output means trajectories for any pixel in any frame are directly available without chaining, and long-video inference uses a query-centric sliding window of length L=16 with boundary-state reuse across overlapping windows.

## Method

PairFormer is a feed-forward transformer guided by the principle of first constructing pairwise correspondences, then enforcing global sequence-level consistency. A ConvNeXt-based spatio-temporal patch encoder produces per-frame patch features; CorrBank replaces explicit 4D cost volumes with K=64 shared learnable correlation tokens queried via cross-attention to yield pair-conditioned features, which are then used as values in a bank-augmented matching step (source features as queries, target features as keys) to produce pairwise motion tokens. A Broadcast Motion Mixer performs trajectory-wise self-attention over the temporal sequence of motion tokens for each patch, collects context tokens across all patches within a source frame, and broadcasts the refined context back via cross-attention to condition each pairwise token on its full trajectory summary. A trajectory field decoder first predicts coarse displacement, visibility, and confidence on the patch grid, then iteratively refines them using local source-target evidence (in the spirit of AllTracker) with learned upsampling to full resolution. Training combines per-pair L1 displacement loss, a confidence-weighted residual with log regularization, visibility BCE, and a correspondence regularizer that enforces agreement between direct and multi-hop trajectory descriptors for pixels on the same physical path.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid protocol across 8 datasets, PairFormer achieves the highest average δ_avg of 68.1, surpassing CoTracker3 (67.2) and LocoTrack (63.1), with particularly strong gains on RGB-Stacking (89.1 vs. 84.2) and RoboTAP (83.8 vs. 81.6). On the APT-specific benchmarks, PairFormer attains the best δ_avg, EPE, AJ, and strided-EPE across CVO, CVO-Extended, and APT-Bench; on APT-Bench it reaches δ_avg 77.5, EPE 3.26, and Δ₂₅^epe 5.39, outperforming DOT (76.7, 3.68, 6.02) and CoTracker3_Offline (75.2, 4.25, 6.48). PairFormer also leads on TAP-Vid AJ (65.9 vs. 63.1) and Occlusion Accuracy (91.1 vs. 89.3) over CoTracker3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PairFormer outperforms all 10 baselines on average δ_avg across the 8 TAP datasets. The closest competitor is CoTracker3 (67.2), which PairFormer edges out by 0.9 points; the gap widens to 5.0 over LocoTrack (63.1), 5.7 over BootsTAPIR (62.4), and 6.2 over DELTA (61.9). Optical-flow-based methods (RAFT 50.8, SEA-RAFT 50.7, AccFlow 30.8) and the dataset-centric PIPs++ (52.7) trail by 15–37 points, reflecting the difficulty of chaining adjacent-frame flow for long-range tracking. On the APT-specific APT-Bench, PairFormer beats DOT by 0.8 δ_avg and 0.42 EPE, and CoTracker3_Offline by 2.3 δ_avg and 0.99 EPE, with the advantage growing at larger temporal strides (Δ₂₅^epe: 5.39 vs. 6.02 for DOT, 6.48 for CoTracker3_Offline). On TAP-Vid AJ and OA, PairFormer improves over CoTracker3 by 2.8 and 1.8 points respectively, indicating better joint localization-visibility and occlusion reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

PairFormer carves new conceptual ground by unifying two previously separate threads—pairwise optical flow (RAFT, SEA-RAFT) and query-conditioned point tracking (CoTracker, TAPIR)—under a single all-pairs dense correspondence formulation. Architecturally, it repurposes the correlation-volume idea from flow networks into a learnable token-based CorrBank, borrows the iterative refinement decoder from AllTracker, and introduces a Broadcast Motion Mixer for trajectory-wise context aggregation that has no direct predecessor in either the flow or tracking literature. The data platform PAIRender follows the synthetic-scene-generation paradigm of Kubric and PointOdyssey but scales to 10K scenes with per-pixel all-pairs annotations, filling a gap neither prior generator addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the all-pairs trajectory field a first-class output rather than a byproduct of propagation from a single source frame, PairFormer enables applications like multi-keyframe video editing and 4D reconstruction that previously suffered from inconsistent correspondences when running a tracker per source frame. The all-to-all evaluation protocol in APT-Bench is a meaningful departure from TAP's first-frame-only sampling and may become a standard for assessing sequence-wide temporal consistency. Key open questions include whether the quadratic cost in sequence length can be reduced for very long videos, and whether the synthetic-to-real domain gap (the paper acknowledges this limitation) limits transfer to in-the-wild footage.
