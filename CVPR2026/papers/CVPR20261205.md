## Problem

Most efficient single object trackers are restricted to RGB-only inputs, while existing multi-modal tracking methods rely on complex architectures and large model structures that are too slow for resource-constrained deployment. This paper asks whether a single lightweight framework can efficiently handle multiple modalities (RGB, Depth, Thermal, Event, Language) while maintaining real-time inference speed on edge hardware.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261205/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a 6-channel composite image formed by channel-concatenating the RGB frame with an auxiliary modality (depth, thermal, or event) at 224×224 for the template and 112×112 for the search region; for RGB-only or language tasks the RGB is simply replicated to fill six channels. For language tracking, a frozen CLIP text encoder produces token embeddings that are linearly projected to match the image token dimension and appended to the sequence. The output is a center probability map plus bounding-box regression (focal, GIoU, L1) from a center head, with a Hanning window penalty applied at inference.

## Method

UETrack builds on the Fast-iTPN-T backbone and replaces selected FFN layers with a Token-Pooling-based Mixture-of-Experts (TP-MoE) module that performs local average-pooling aggregation, projects pooled tokens into compact expert tokens, and routes input tokens to experts via a similarity-driven soft assignment (softmax over dot-product scores) rather than a discrete gating network, enabling fully parallel expert computation without token sorting or inter-expert communication. A Target-aware Adaptive Distillation (TAD) strategy uses a small Adaptive Net (global average pooling + MLP + Gumbel-Softmax) that takes both student and teacher search-region features and outputs a per-sample binary decision on whether to apply KL-divergence and MSE feature distillation from a frozen SUTrack-B teacher, filtering out unreliable supervision on occluded or deformed samples. Three model variants (B/S/T) differ in backbone depth, TP-MoE insertion layer, and expert count (8/4/2), yielding 13M/9M/6M parameters and 163/183/221 FPS on GPU while supporting all five modalities with a single trained model.

## Results

UETrack-B achieves 69.2% AUC on LaSOT, 48.4% on LaSOT_ext, 82.7% on TrackingNet, and 72.6% AO on GOT-10k, setting new real-time state-of-the-art across all four RGB benchmarks. It runs at 163/56/60 FPS on GPU/CPU/Jetson AGX Xavier, outperforming the previous best real-time tracker AsymTrack-B by 4.5%, 3.8%, 2.7%, and 4.9% on the respective benchmarks. On multi-modal tasks, UETrack-B achieves 68.3% EAO on VOT-RGBD22 (depth), 55.5% AUC on LasHeR (thermal), 59.2% AUC on VisEvent (event), and 58.0% AUC on TNL2K (language), all at real-time speeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UETrack-B decisively outperforms the previous best real-time tracker AsymTrack-B [103] on all four RGB benchmarks (LaSOT AUC 69.2 vs 64.7, TrackingNet 82.7 vs 80.0, GOT-10k AO 72.6 vs 67.7) while running faster on CPU (56 vs 38 FPS) and AGX (60 vs 64 FPS). Against other real-time trackers, UETrack-B leads HiT-Base [41] by 4.6% AUC on LaSOT and MixFormerV2-S [19] by 8.6% AUC, with UETrack-T still gaining 2.8% over MixFormerV2-S. On multi-modal benchmarks, UETrack-B surpasses the unified SUTrack-T [14] on depth (68.3 vs 68.1 EAO), thermal (55.5 vs 53.9 AUC on LasHeR), and event (59.2 vs 58.8 AUC on VisEvent) while running 1.6–2.2× faster across platforms. However, UETrack-B trails SUTrack-T on the language benchmark TNL2K (58.0 vs 60.9 AUC), and all real-time variants remain below non-real-time methods like MCITrack-B224 (75.3% LaSOT AUC) and SeqTrackv2 (74.4% VOT-RGBD22 EAO) in absolute accuracy, reflecting the inherent speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

UETrack builds directly on SUTrack's unified token-embedding pipeline for multi-modal tracking (concatenating auxiliary modalities with RGB into 6-channel inputs) and on Fast-iTPN-T as a lightweight backbone. Its two key departures are architectural and training-related: TP-MoE replaces the discrete gating mechanism in standard Mixture-of-Experts with a similarity-driven soft assignment via token pooling, and TAD introduces a Gumbel-Softmax-based Adaptive Net that selectively gates distillation signals per sample. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of two previously disjoint lines of work—efficient RGB-only trackers (HiT, MixFormerV2, AsymTrack) and heavy multi-modal trackers (SUTrack, ViPT, SDSTrack)—arguing that neither family alone addresses the practical need for fast, multi-modal deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single trained model can be deployed across five modalities (RGB, depth, thermal, event, language) on edge hardware (Jetson AGX at 60+ FPS), which prior multi-modal trackers could not achieve without sacrificing real-time performance. This opens the door to unified tracking pipelines in resource-constrained settings such as drones, robotics, and surveillance. Limitations include a dependency on a frozen teacher (SUTrack-B) during training, and the language-modality results still trail specialized vision-language trackers (e.g., UVLTrack-B) by a noticeable margin, suggesting the unified approach has not yet fully closed the gap on text-guided tracking. An open question is whether the TP-MoE soft-assignment strategy generalizes beyond tracking to other vision tasks where modality heterogeneity is present.
