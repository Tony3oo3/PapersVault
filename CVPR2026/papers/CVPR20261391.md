## Problem

Existing dense feature matchers operate in a pairwise (1-to-1) fashion, and chaining their predictions across views yields fragmented, geometrically inconsistent tracks that degrade 3D reconstruction quality. Post-hoc refinement strategies that enforce multi-view consistency are computationally prohibitive for dense matchers and remain heavily dependent on the quality of initial pairwise matches. MV-RoMa addresses this by being the first model to jointly estimate dense correspondences from a source image to multiple co-visible targets in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261391/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes one source image and a set of co-visible target images (default: 1 source + 4 targets at 672×672 base resolution) along with initial pairwise matching results from an off-the-shelf matcher (e.g., UFM), which are clustered into T=512 track tokens serving as sparse geometric priors. It outputs dense source-to-target warp fields and per-pixel confidence maps for all target views simultaneously, which are then post-processed via confidence-based selection, bidirectional consistency filtering, and NMS to produce multi-view tracks directly consumable by an SfM pipeline such as COLMAP.

## Method

The architecture has two key components. First, a track-guided multi-view encoder inserts a three-stage module (attentional sampling onto track tokens, a view-order-invariant track transformer, and attentional splatting back to image grids) into the second half of a DINOv2 backbone, enabling efficient multi-view feature exchange along the sparse track prior without full cross-attention. Second, a multi-view matching refiner performs coarse-to-fine refinement using VGG19 features, where at each pyramid level target features are warped to the source grid and a stack of pixel-aligned multi-view attention blocks (per-pixel attention across aligned views followed by ConvNeXt spatial propagation) fuses multi-view context before predicting residual warp updates. The model is trained supervised on MegaDepth and ScanNet with RoMa's robust loss, and a post-processing strategy converts the jointly predicted correspondences into robust SfM tracks via confidence maximization, cycle-error filtering, and NMS-based keypoint selection.

## Results

On HPatches homography estimation, MV-RoMa achieves 46.1/71.9/80.1 AUC-DLT @1/3/5px, outperforming the closest baseline RoMa (41.0/67.9/76.9) by 5.1 points at the strict 1px threshold. On ETH3D 3D triangulation, MV-RoMa reaches 81.91% accuracy and 7.52% completeness at 1cm, surpassing RoMa (75.58%/5.64%) and RoMa+Dense-SfM (84.79%/7.38% accuracy but lower completeness). On multi-view camera pose estimation, MV-RoMa attains 51.79/66.77/81.74 AUC@3°/5°/10° on Texture-Poor SfM and 51.31/62.92/75.92 on IMC PhotoTourism, consistently leading all dense matching and SfM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MV-RoMa outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Among dense matchers, the closest competitor is RoMa+Dense-SfM, which MV-RoMa surpasses by 1.85 AUC points on Texture-Poor SfM @3° (51.79 vs 49.94) and by 2.83 points on IMC @3° (51.31 vs 48.48); the gap widens at stricter thresholds. LoFTR-based pipelines (LoFTR+PixSfM, LoFTR+DF-SfM) trail by 25–31 AUC points on Texture-Poor SfM @3°, highlighting the advantage of multi-view joint reasoning over pairwise chaining. Among detector-based methods, even the strongest (SP+SG+PixSfM at 14.00 AUC@3° on Texture-Poor SfM) is outperformed by a factor of ~3.7×. Deep feedforward SfM methods (VGGSfM, VGGT, Pi3, Map-Anything, Depth-Anything v3) all fall well below MV-RoMa, with the best (Depth-Anything v3 at 44.15 on IMC @3°) still 7.16 points behind. On HPatches, MV-RoMa beats RoMa by 5.1 AUC-DLT points at 1px and all other sparse/dense baselines by 15+ points, confirming that the multi-view encoder and refiner yield substantially more geometrically consistent correspondences.

## Relation to Prior Work

MV-RoMa extends the RoMa coarse-to-fine dense matching framework by replacing its single-view feature processing with a multi-view encoder and refiner. The encoder borrows the three-stage attentional sampling–propagation–splatting mechanism from Tracktention, adapting it for view-order invariance and visibility masking to handle partial tracks. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant pairwise-matching-then-chain paradigm (and its post-hoc refinement fixes like DF-SfM and Dense-SfM) by jointly estimating correspondences from one source to multiple targets in a single forward pass, using sparse track tokens as a geometric prior to avoid the quadratic cost of full multi-view cross-attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first dense matcher to produce geometrically consistent multi-view correspondences in one pass, directly addressing the error accumulation and track fragmentation that plague chained pairwise matching in SfM pipelines. The practical impact is substantial for structure-from-motion and 3D reconstruction, where track quality is the bottleneck. However, the method still depends on an off-the-shelf pairwise matcher to construct the initial track tokens, so its performance is bounded by the quality of that prior; scalability to very large view counts (beyond the 5-view groups tested) and the computational cost of the multi-view refiner at high resolutions remain open questions for broader deployment.
