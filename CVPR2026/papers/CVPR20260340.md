## Problem

Collaborative perception for autonomous driving is bottlenecked by tight communication budgets, yet existing bandwidth-reduction methods address only half the problem: spatial selection methods optimize *where* to communicate but still transmit all redundant channels, while compression methods use fixed-rate encoders that cannot adapt to dynamic scene complexity. No prior work jointly optimizes both the spatial and channel dimensions under a globally coordinated, receiver-centric allocation scheme.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260340/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's raw point cloud is encoded by a PointPillars backbone into a feature map X_j ∈ R^{H×W×C}; the communication module then exchanges lightweight importance maps (a spatial map M_S,j ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} and a channel saliency map M_C,j) before transmitting only the selected feature subsets under a per-agent bandwidth budget B. The system produces 3D object detections (evaluated at IoU 0.5 and 0.7) and BEV semantic segmentation (mIoU over Road, Lane, Vehicle) within a 70 m communication range, with the full intermediate-fusion 16× compression serving as the 1.0 communication-rate reference.

## Method

WhisperNet introduces a receiver-centric, two-stage communication protocol with three modules. (1) The Sender-Side Importance Estimation Module generates a spatial importance map via a two-layer convolutional head and a channel saliency map by scoring patch-wise Laplacian magnitudes, classifying each channel into primary/secondary/marginal groups via a 1×1 conv + softmax, and computing a group-weighted maximum score per patch. (2) The Receiver-Side Confidence-Aware Module acts as a global coordinator: a Channel Merit Distributor refines raw channel maps across all agents with a conv + Gaussian filter to compute per-agent collaborative shares, while a Spatial Focus Engine redistributes the total budget B across patches via a softmax over the element-wise-max global saliency map, yielding a per-agent, per-patch allocation matrix b_{j,k} that is broadcast to all senders. (3) The Collaborative Feature Routing Module aligns the sparse received features by routing each channel to one of m=4 specialized experts (mixture-of-experts) via a soft MLP assignment, refining within each expert with parallel 3×3/5×5/7×7 convolutions, applying attention-based inter-agent recalibration, and combining with a residual Squeeze-and-Excitation branch. The design is plug-and-play, integrating into existing fusion backbones (V2VNet, V2X-ViT, F-Cooper) to cut communication by ~95% with negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

On OPV2V, WhisperNet achieves 93.34% AP@0.5 and 87.64% AP@0.7 with only 7.28M parameters, outperforming the previous best (DSRC, 85.26% AP@0.7) by 2.4% while using fewer parameters. On DAIR-V2X, it reaches 79.15% AP@0.5 and 64.80% AP@0.7, surpassing the runner-up DSRC (63.60%) by 1.2%. Under extreme bandwidth constraints (1% of full communication), WhisperNet outperforms the second-best method by 10.6% on OPV2V and 11.7% on DAIR-V2X, and maintains baseline-level accuracy at just 0.5% communication cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WhisperNet achieves state-of-the-art AP@0.7 on both benchmarks, winning against all 10 directly compared methods in Table 1. The closest competitors are DSRC (85.26% vs. 87.64% on OPV2V; 63.60% vs. 64.80% on DAIR-V2X) and ERMVP (84.04% vs. 87.64%; 63.50% vs. 64.80%), with WhisperNet leading by 2.4% and 1.2% respectively. Against spatial-selection methods like Where2comm (78.89% OPV2V) and CoSDH (83.73%), the gap widens to 8.8% and 3.9% on OPV2V, confirming the benefit of joint spatial-channel selection over spatial-only strategies. WhisperNet also uses substantially fewer parameters (7.28M) than V2VNet (16.08M), How2Comm (35.79M), and MRCNet (18.79M) while outperforming them by 5.4%, 16.2%, and 10.7% AP@0.7 on OPV2V. As a plug-and-play module, WhisperNet boosts F-Cooper's AP from 0.704 to 0.723 at 5% bandwidth on DAIR-V2X and enables V2X-ViT to surpass its full-bandwidth baseline at 10% communication on OPV2V. In robustness tests under pose noise (σ=0.6), WhisperNet degrades by only 16% versus over 75% for ERMVP, and when integrated into V2X-ViT at 5% bandwidth, the combined model loses less than 1% accuracy under extreme noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

WhisperNet departs from the two dominant strategies in bandwidth-efficient collaborative perception—fixed-rate feature compression (CORE, AttFuse) and spatial-only selection (Where2comm, CoSDH, How2comm)—by introducing a receiver-centric, globally-coordinated paradigm that jointly allocates budget across both spatial regions and feature channels. The key conceptual departure is the identification of channel-dimension redundancy as a previously overlooked axis: prior spatial-selection methods transmit all channels within selected regions, while WhisperNet's preliminary experiment shows that nearly half of channels can be pruned without performance loss. Architecturally, it repurposes Laplacian-based channel scoring, a lightweight MoE-style routing mechanism, and Squeeze-and-Excitation recalibration into a unified sender-estimates / receiver-coordinates / router-aligns pipeline, rather than building on any single prior framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint spatial-channel allocation with global (rather than pairwise or ego-centric) budget coordination offers a principled path toward near-zero-bandwidth collaborative perception, and the plug-and-play design (95% bandwidth reduction with negligible accuracy loss when inserted into V2X-ViT, V2VNet, F-Cooper) makes the technique broadly adoptable. However, evaluation is confined to point-cloud inputs on two datasets (OPV2V, DAIR-V2X), and scalability to larger agent counts, camera-based modalities, or real-time vehicular networks remains untested. An open question is whether the channel-redundancy finding generalizes across backbones and scene types, and whether the receiver-centric protocol's additional round-trip latency is acceptable in safety-critical driving loops.
