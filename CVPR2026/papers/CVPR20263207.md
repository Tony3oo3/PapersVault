## Problem

Vision-language models excel at common VQA and abstract logical reasoning but fail at tasks requiring the integration of spatial understanding with multi-step causal reasoning in real-world scenes. No existing benchmark systematically evaluates this combined capability—spatial logical reasoning—where models must deduce a logically coherent, spatially grounded sequence of dependent operations from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263207/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a single RGB indoor scene image and a natural-language task instruction (e.g., 'Pick up the yellow box') as input, and expects an open-vocabulary, JSON-structured answer where each step specifies an action ('content') and an array of prerequisite step IDs ('precondition'). The RSGAR method additionally consumes a depth map (from Depth Anything V2) and a segmentation map (from SAM) as auxiliary visual inputs during scene graph construction. Answers range from 2 to 10 steps, with preconditions forming a directed acyclic graph over the step set.

## Method

The paper introduces SpatiaLQA (9,605 QA pairs from 241 indoor scenes) built via three-stage data collection: manual annotation, subgraph extraction augmentation (deriving sub-tasks from logical dependency subgraphs), and graph expansion augmentation (appending 'place on' steps to 'pick up' tasks). For improving model performance, the authors propose Recursive Scene Graph Assisted Reasoning (RSGAR), which first extracts depth and segmentation maps, then iteratively (T=5 rounds) uses the VLM to identify objects in direct contact with a source object and their spatial relationships, building a scene graph where each round's target objects become the next round's sources. The accumulated scene graph is then concatenated with the task prompt and fed back to the VLM to produce the final multi-step answer, effectively decomposing the complex visual scene into a task-relevant hierarchical representation that focuses the model's attention on spatially relevant object interactions.

## Results

On SpatiaLQA, the best-performing VLM (GPT-5) achieves F1 scores of 76.0 for content and 47.0 for preconditions, while a human evaluator reaches 97.6 and 92.5 respectively, revealing a substantial gap in spatial logical reasoning. The proposed RSGAR method, built on GPT-4o, achieves Fc=69.8 and Fp=28.1, outperforming all baselines including CoT (67.6/27.0) and PhysAgent (64.7/22.2). Notably, simply adding depth or segmentation maps to vanilla GPT-4o reasoning degrades performance (e.g., +seg drops Fc to 50.3), indicating that raw visual cues without structured decomposition are counterproductive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSGAR outperforms CoT [76] by +2.2 on Fc (69.8 vs. 67.6) and +1.1 on Fp (28.1 vs. 27.0), confirming that explicit scene graph decomposition provides a more structured reasoning scaffold than free-form chain-of-thought prompting. Against PhysAgent [18], RSGAR gains +5.1 on Fc (69.8 vs. 64.7) and +5.9 on Fp (28.1 vs. 22.2), showing that physical commonsense priors alone are insufficient for multi-step spatial logical reasoning. Both baselines also underperform vanilla GPT-4o on Fp (CoT: 27.0, PhysAgent: 22.2 vs. 25.1), suggesting that without proper spatial decomposition, additional reasoning strategies can introduce noise. The ablation further reveals that RSGAR's advantage is concentrated on complex tasks (4+ steps), where it improves Fc by +4.5 over vanilla reasoning, while on simpler 2-step tasks it slightly underperforms (−1.3), indicating the method's value scales with task complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'spatial logical reasoning' as a distinct task that sits at the intersection of VQA, logical reasoning, and embodied question answering, yet is explicitly separated from all three: unlike VQA it requires multi-step dependency-aware reasoning, unlike abstract logical reasoning it is grounded in real spatial scenes, and unlike EQA it operates in an open-vocabulary space without motor primitives. Methodologically, RSGAR repurposes existing visual foundation models (Depth Anything V2, SAM) and the scene graph formalism into a recursive, task-conditioned decomposition pipeline, but no single prior method directly precedes this recursive multi-round graph construction. The evaluation protocol (GPT-4o semantic matching + Hungarian algorithm for optimal one-to-one step alignment) is a novel combination adapted from assignment problems to open-vocabulary multi-step answer scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and consistent gap between human performance (>90% F1) and even the strongest proprietary VLMs (GPT-5 at ~76/47), with precondition reasoning dramatically weaker than step-content generation across all 41 models, suggesting that causal/dependency understanding remains a fundamental bottleneck. The finding that naively adding depth or segmentation maps actually degrades performance (GPT-4o drops from 67.4 to 50.5 Fc) challenges the assumption that more visual cues always help, and RSGAR's modest gains (~2-3 points) indicate that the problem is not merely perceptual but deeply tied to multi-step compositional reasoning. Open questions include whether the benchmark generalizes beyond static indoor scenes to dynamic or outdoor environments, whether the recursive graph approach scales to longer horizons, and whether training on such structured spatial-logical data could close the human-model gap.
