## Problem

Processing hour-scale videos with Video-LLMs is computationally prohibitive due to quadratic attention and large FFN activation costs. Existing efficiency methods either irreversibly discard spatio-temporal tokens (compromising fine-grained perception) or impose rigid, predefined sparse patterns (inhibiting long-range temporal modeling). The paper addresses the lack of a unified, adaptive sparsity mechanism that preserves both fine-grained detail and global temporal dependencies while substantially reducing computation.

## Input / Output

The method ingests long-form videos sampled at 4 fps at native spatial resolution, producing up to 48k visual tokens within a 64k-token context window (double the backbone's native limit). Visual tokens are partitioned into 8×8×4 spatiotemporal cubes before entering the LLM. For extra-long video evaluation, the system processes 4,096 frames at 224 resolution, yielding a 300k-token context. Outputs include free-form text responses for understanding tasks and temporal boundary predictions for video grounding (Charades-STA).

## Method

AdaSpark introduces a unified adaptive sparsity framework operating at two granularities within 3D spatiotemporal cubes. Adaptive Cube-Selective Attention (AdaS-Attn) computes each query's similarity to the mean key vector of every preceding cube, then uses entropy-based Top-p (nucleus) sampling to adaptively select which cubes to attend to—selecting more cubes for flat (high-entropy) distributions and fewer for sharp ones—while always retaining full attention within the local cube. Adaptive Token-Selective FFN (AdaS-FFN) ranks tokens within each cube by L2-norm importance, applies Top-p selection to identify salient tokens for full FFN processing, and estimates the transformation for bypassed tokens via mean compensation from the activated set. Text tokens always pass densely through both modules. The entire framework is trained via supervised fine-tuning on Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B backbones with a frozen visual encoder, achieving up to 57% FLOPs reduction while matching or exceeding dense-model performance across long-video benchmarks.

## Results

AdaSpark achieves up to 57% FLOPs reduction (299.5→128.5 TFLOPs) while outperforming the dense Qwen2.5-VL backbone on all eight benchmarks. The 7B model attains top scores across all four long-form video benchmarks: MLVU 69.8, VideoMME 66.2, LongVideoBench 62.1, and LVBench 47.9, surpassing the dense 7B backbone (68.3, 65.1, 60.7, 45.3). On the extra-long VideoNIAH benchmark, AdaSpark 7B reaches 97.5 retrieval accuracy versus 83.0 for the dense backbone and 92.5 for the best prior sparse method (FrameFusion). The 3B model similarly leads on three of four long-form benchmarks and achieves 45.0 mIoU on Charades-STA video grounding, the highest among all encoder-free models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaSpark consistently outperforms all four integrated sparse methods (FastV, ToMe, MoBA, FrameFusion) on both 3B and 7B backbones across nearly every benchmark. Against the strongest sparse competitor FrameFusion, AdaSpark 7B gains +5.0 on VideoNIAH (97.5 vs 92.5), +3.5 on MLVU (69.8 vs 66.3), +1.0 on LongVideoBench (62.1 vs 61.1), +5.1 on LVBench (47.9 vs 42.8), and +4.0 on CharadesSTA (55.3 vs 51.3). MoBA performs worst among all sparse baselines (e.g., 65.5 VideoNIAH, 39.4 LVBench at 3B), which the authors attribute to its lack of meaningful visual sequence partitioning. Notably, AdaSpark also surpasses the dense Qwen2.5-VL backbone on all eight benchmarks at both scales, indicating that the adaptive sparse design not only saves computation but also improves quality—likely because the entropy-based Top-p selection preserves information that dense attention dilutes. The only benchmark where a non-AdaSpark method leads is MVBench (short video) at 3B, where VideoChat-Flash-2B scores 70.0 vs AdaSpark's 66.5, though this is a different model family and scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSpark departs from two dominant paradigms in efficient video-LLM inference: (1) irreversible information disposal (frame sampling, token pruning, token merging) that permanently discards spatio-temporal content, and (2) rigid, predefined sparse attention patterns (local windows, fixed top-k) that break long-range temporal connectivity. Conceptually, it draws on the Native Sparse Modeling line (NSA, MoBA) for LLMs but extends it to video by introducing 3D spatiotemporal cube partitioning as the atomic unit and by applying entropy-based (Top-p) adaptive sparsity to both attention and FFN in a unified, co-designed manner. The mean-compensation strategy for bypassed FFN tokens is a novel lightweight mechanism not present in prior sparse-FFN or MoE-style video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving a 57% FLOPs reduction while simultaneously improving over the dense Qwen2.5-VL baseline on most benchmarks (especially the 9–14 point VideoNIAH gains) is practically significant for deploying video-LLMs on hour-scale inputs where compute is the binding constraint. The entropy-based adaptive selection offers a principled alternative to hand-tuned fixed sparsity ratios, and the 3D cube structure is a natural fit for video that prior 1D token-level methods lack. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL family, whether it can be integrated into pre-training rather than post-training, and how it behaves at substantially larger scales (e.g., 70B+ models or >1M token contexts).
