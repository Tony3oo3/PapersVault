## Problem

Existing LVLM adaptation methods (projector tuning, LoRA, full fine-tuning) couple the vision encoder to the language model, so any encoder change forces costly re-alignment and often causes catastrophic forgetting of linguistic capabilities. The paper asks whether a vision encoder can be adapted to a new domain while keeping the LLM entirely frozen and portable across different LLM backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263072/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed at 256×256 by a SigLIP-Large ViT-L/16 encoder) and a text prompt, producing a text response (short answer capped at 5 tokens for accuracy evaluation, up to 1024 tokens for reasoning). Unusually, the visual representation is a sequence of discrete codebook indices rather than continuous embeddings, and at inference a rarity-weighted pruning step reduces the token count to ~80% of the original grid before feeding the LLM, yielding a compact visual summary.

## Method

CRAFT fine-tunes only the discrete vision encoder against a frozen codebook using a composite objective: a surrogate alignment loss (next-token prediction through a smaller surrogate LLM that backpropagates domain-relevant supervision), a commitment loss (keeping encoder outputs close to their assigned codebook entries), and a contrastive loss (preserving pretraining semantic structure via image-text alignment). Gradients flow through the non-differentiable quantization step via the straight-through estimator. At test time, a rarity-weighted token pruning scheme allocates a per-codebook-ID keep quota based on training-set frequency, then selects within each ID by quantization residual and spatial isolation, removing redundant background tokens. Because the codebook is fixed and shared, the adapted encoder can be plugged into any LLM backbone that uses the same codebook without retraining or re-alignment, decoupling vision adaptation from language-model updates.

## Results

CRAFT achieves an average accuracy of 68.58% across 10 domain-specific benchmarks (VQARAD, PlantVillage, Dogs, Cars, etc.) when trained and evaluated with the VILA-U-7B backbone, a 13.51-point gain over the zero-shot VILA-U-7B baseline (55.07%). Even with a small 0.5B surrogate (Qwen2-0.5B), CRAFT reaches 61.25% average, surpassing the continuous VILA-7B zero-shot baseline (59.89%). On reasoning quality (Table 2), CRAFT attains the highest Overall score on VQARAD (3.21) and Dogs (1.19), while all continuous fine-tuning baselines (LDIFS, Projector FT, LLM-LoRA) produce negative Overall scores, indicating collapsed instruction-following.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT (68.58% avg) outperforms all four continuous fine-tuning baselines by substantial margins: Vision FT (61.76%, +6.82 pts), Projector FT (56.60%, +11.98 pts), and LDIFS (54.98%, +13.60 pts). On reasoning quality (Table 2), CRAFT achieves the best Overall score on VQARAD (3.21 vs. 3.17 for VILA-7B) and substantially outperforms all continuous methods, which suffer catastrophic forgetting of instruction-following (e.g., LLM-LoRA drops to −0.98 Overall on VQARAD, −0.84 on Flowers, −0.89 on Cars, −0.75 on Dogs). The key advantage is that CRAFT's discrete codebook interface preserves the LLM's linguistic capabilities: while LLM-LoRA raises correctness on Cars to 94.18%, its explanation presence collapses to 0.01, whereas CRAFT maintains 41.98% presence with 92.68% correctness. CRAFT also demonstrates cross-LLM transferability (Table 3): an encoder trained with a 0.5B surrogate boosts Qwen2.5-3B accuracy from 46.74% to 59.98% without any re-alignment, a capability none of the continuous baselines offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT builds directly on the discrete LVLM architecture of VILA-U, which introduced a hierarchical codebook for unifying visual understanding and generation, and on the vector-quantization mechanism from VQVAE. It departs from the dominant paradigm of continuous-space adaptation (projector tuning, LoRA on the LLM, LDIFS regularization) by treating the codebook as a frozen, shared interface: the vision encoder is fine-tuned to select and arrange existing codebook entries rather than shifting a continuous feature distribution. The surrogate-model training strategy—using a much smaller LLM to provide end-to-end supervision to the encoder—is a novel training recipe not seen in prior encoder-adaptation work, and the rarity-weighted test-time token pruning is a new inference-time regularization specific to discrete visual tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for resource-constrained and multi-backbone deployments: a single adapted encoder can be swapped across LLM families (Llama2, Qwen2, Qwen2.5) without any re-alignment, and training with a 0.5B surrogate cuts memory by ~62% and time by ~74% compared to full VILA-7B fine-tuning. A key open question is whether this decoupling advantage extends to continuous LVLMs or to generation tasks, since the method is inherently tied to the discrete codebook interface. The codebook-size ablation (32% at 10% capacity vs. 77% at full) also highlights a fragility: the approach's expressiveness is bounded by codebook capacity, and scaling to very fine-grained or high-resolution domains may require substantially larger codebooks.
