## Problem

Prior residual approaches for physics-based motion adaptation suffer from two key limitations: base policies are trained without anticipating the state distribution shifts that occur when a residual controller later modifies specific body parts, leading to instability; and existing composition methods restrict adaptation to fixed body regions or lack an integrated mechanism for semantic (e.g., text-conditioned) control.

## Input / Output

The system operates on a 63-DOF SMPL humanoid simulated in IsaacGym at 60 Hz with 30 Hz PD control. At each step it consumes the full simulated state, a binary mask over five coarse body-part groups (Trunk, Left/Right Arm, Left/Right Leg; at most three masked simultaneously), and optionally a free-form text prompt. The output is a 63-dimensional joint-position action vector; in the text-driven setting, a pre-trained DART autoregressive diffusion model converts the text prompt into short-horizon kinematic trajectories from which localized per-joint goals are extracted for the residual policy.

## Method

MaskAdapt is a two-stage residual framework. In stage 1, a base policy is trained under stochastic body-part masking (zeroing state components for randomly sampled joint groups with probability ρ) together with a mask-invariant (MI) loss that minimizes the KL divergence between the policy's action distribution under the full state and under the masked state, yielding a motion prior that remains stable when observations are missing. In stage 2, a residual policy ψ is trained atop the frozen base policy, conditioned on the mask, to produce a residual action Δa_t that modifies only the masked body parts; the executed action is a gain-weighted sum of the base and residual outputs. Adversarial training uses a single conditional discriminator D^ψ(s,s′|m) that conditions on the mask itself (rather than an ensemble of part-specific discriminators), with real samples constructed by kinematically blending reference and new-motion sequences. For text-driven partial tracking, a pre-trained DART diffusion model generates future trajectories online from text prompts, and the residual policy learns to track the extracted localized goals while the base policy preserves the underlying locomotion.

## Results

On motion composition, MaskAdapt achieves H_norm of 0.8580/0.8935 (Case a), 0.9268/0.8710 (Case b), and 0.8980/0.9214 (Case c) for adapted/preserved regions, consistently outperforming CML across all six sub-metrics. On text-driven partial goal tracking, the method reduces tracking error to 0.055±0.007 m and pose error to 0.047±0.001 m, beating CML (0.069/0.079 m) and a from-scratch model (0.076/0.057 m). On goal-driven tasks, MaskAdapt achieves 64.2% success on the Strike task versus 46.5% for CML and 1.0% for AdaptNet, while matching or exceeding baselines on Location (99.5%) and Heading (76.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AMP (the unmasked baseline), MaskAdapt with the MI loss (λ=1.0) achieves H_norm of 0.9025 under masking, nearly matching AMP's 0.9124, whereas removing the MI loss causes a sharp drop to 0.7448, confirming the regularization's critical role. Against CML (the closest prior work on motion composition), MaskAdapt wins on all six H_norm sub-metrics in Table 2, with the largest gap in Case (b) adapted arms (0.8710 vs 0.7478, +12.3 points), and also outperforms CML on the Strike goal-driven task (64.2% vs 46.5%) and partial tracking error (0.055 m vs 0.069 m). Against AdaptNet, MaskAdapt dramatically outperforms on Strike (64.2% vs 1.0%) since AdaptNet cannot introduce new motions, while being comparable on Location (99.5% vs 94.3%) and slightly behind on Heading (76.9% vs 82.1%). Against MaskedMimic on arm-level tracking, MaskAdapt achieves lower tracking error (0.0423 m vs 0.0849 m) and 100% success rate, whereas MaskedMimic struggles with part-constrained generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MaskAdapt extends the residual RL paradigm for physics-based character control established by CML [33] and AdaptNet [34], which layer a lightweight adapter on a frozen base policy. The key departure is the mask-invariant training of the base policy: prior residual methods never exposed the frozen controller to the state distribution shifts that arise when the residual policy modifies specific body parts, leading to instability. By introducing stochastic body-part masking with a KL-divergence consistency loss during base policy training, the paper makes the prior robust to exactly the perturbations the residual stage will introduce. The conditional discriminator design borrows from CALM [25], and the text-driven tracking task integrates DART [35]'s autoregressive diffusion model as a kinematic goal provider, bridging semantic motion generation with physics-based execution in a way that prior hybrid systems (CLoSD, DART) did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mask-invariant prior offers a practical, low-cost solution to a well-identified failure mode in residual RL: the frozen base policy degrades when the residual controller alters its input distribution. This makes flexible, on-demand motion composition (multiple body parts, dynamic mask switching) feasible without retraining, and the integration of text-conditioned kinematic guidance into the residual loop enables interactive, semantically driven partial control. Limitations include failure under conflicting motions on closely related body parts and dependence on the quality and semantic coverage of the pre-trained diffusion model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-body simultaneous adaptation, whether the mask-invariant prior can be learned more efficiently, and how well the framework transfers to more complex or contact-rich tasks beyond the demonstrated scenarios.
