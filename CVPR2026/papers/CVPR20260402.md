## Problem

Existing conversational avatar systems are one-way (speak-only or listen-only), and the sole prior joint speak-listen method (DualTalk) is not end-to-end because it requires the speaker's precomputed facial motion to drive the listener. Direct end-to-end training of a unified model fails because of a fundamental audio-motion correlation imbalance: the speaker's audio strongly drives their own motion but only weakly correlates with the listener's, causing the listening branch to collapse into stiff, static expressions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260402/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes dual-track audio (speaker-A's and speaker-B's speech, encoded by a frozen wav2vec 2.0 encoder) and a learned per-speaker style embedding, and autoregressively generates FLAME 3D facial motion parameters (expression ψ, head/jaw pose, and eye gaze θ) for both speakers in 100-frame (4-second) chunks. The discrete motion representation uses a temporal multi-scale VQ codec with 256 codebook entries of dimension 64 across five resolution levels [1, 5, 25, 50, 100], and each new chunk is conditioned on all previously generated motion chunks.

## Method

UniLS introduces a two-stage training paradigm to decouple the learning of internal motion dynamics from audio-driven modulation. Stage 1 trains an audio-free autoregressive transformer on unpaired multi-scenario video data (news, interviews, casual talking) to build an internal motion prior capturing spontaneous behaviors like blinks, nods, and micro-expressions. Stage 2 fine-tunes this generator on paired conversational data by adding two cross-attention layers per transformer block—one attending to speaker-A's audio (driving speaking) and one to speaker-B's audio (driving listening)—while applying LoRA to the inherited backbone weights to preserve the learned motion prior without overwriting it. The autoregressive model predicts the next motion chunk conditioned on past motions, both audio tracks, and the style embedding, trained with a chunk-wise reconstruction loss.

## Results

On the Seamless Interaction test set, UniLS achieves the best speaking accuracy across all metrics: LVE 5.83, MHD 1.89, FDD 18.41, PDD 4.67, and JDD 0.71, outperforming the next-best DualTalk (LVE 6.35, MHD 1.95) and ARTalk* (LVE 6.79, MHD 2.02). For listening, UniLS delivers up to 44.1% improvement in FDD over ARTalk* (17.12 vs. 30.62) and 60.7% over DualTalk (17.12 vs. 43.58), with F-FID of 4.304 versus 10.779 (ARTalk*) and 13.143 (DualTalk). In a user study with 25 participants, over 90% prefer UniLS's listening reactions over DualTalk, and 75–77% prefer it over ARTalk* across sync, expression, and pose categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniLS outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the speaking-only baselines DiffPoseTalk and ARTalk, UniLS improves LVE by 38.4% and 22.1% respectively, and FDD by 43.7% and 41.8%, confirming superior lip-sync and upper-face dynamics. Against the adapted ARTalk* (which adds a second audio track to the original ARTalk), UniLS reduces listening FDD from 30.62 to 17.12 (44.1% improvement) and F-FID from 10.779 to 4.304 (60.0% improvement), demonstrating that simply adding audio input without the two-stage prior-learning paradigm is insufficient. Against DualTalk, the only prior end-to-end speak-listen method, UniLS achieves substantially better listening quality (FDD 17.12 vs. 43.58; F-FID 4.304 vs. 13.143) while also improving speaking accuracy (LVE 5.83 vs. 6.35), and unlike DualTalk it runs in real time at 560.6 FPS on a single RTX 5090 GPU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior work ARTalk [5], reusing its autoregressive transformer backbone, multi-scale VQ codec, style embedding, and chunk-based generation paradigm. It departs from DualTalk [26], the first unified speak-listen method, by eliminating the dependency on the interlocutor's precomputed facial sequence, making the pipeline fully audio-driven and end-to-end. Conceptually, the key departure from the broader speech-driven animation literature (FaceFormer, CodeTalker, SelfTalk, etc.) is the two-stage training paradigm: first learning an audio-free internal motion prior, then fine-tuning with dual-track audio via cross-attention and LoRA, rather than jointly optimizing audio-to-motion from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniLS provides the first practical, real-time (560.6 FPS) end-to-end solution for generating both speaking and listening facial motions from audio alone, removing the extra motion-acquisition pipeline that blocked real-time deployment in prior speak-listen systems. The 44.1% improvement in listening distributional metrics over DualTalk addresses a long-standing stiffness problem that has kept most methods focused on speak-only generation. However, the model lacks semantic understanding—listener reactions are driven by acoustic cues (rhythm, emphasis) rather than conversational meaning—so it cannot yet produce grounded reactions like nodding in agreement. The chunk-based formulation also introduces subtle temporal discontinuities, pointing toward the need for continuous long-horizon generation as a future direction.
