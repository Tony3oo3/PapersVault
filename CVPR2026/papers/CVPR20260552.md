## Problem

Existing training-free methods for MLLM hallucination rely on disjoint strategies—either enhancing visual attention or suppressing text inertia via contrastive decoding at the logit level—that face critical trade-offs: boosting vision alone cannot overcome strong language prior, while distorting images for negative samples introduces unstable, out-of-distribution noise. The paper demonstrates that naively combining these two paradigms actually degrades performance, revealing a need for a principled unified framework that operates cohesively at the same representational level.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text query as input, producing an autoregressive text response. The image is encoded into vision tokens (e.g., 576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5), and an augmented copy (horizontal flip, Gaussian blur, salt-and-pepper noise) is also encoded. All interventions occur on intermediate hidden states within the Transformer decoder—SVC at a single middle layer (layer 16) and CRC across shallow layers (1 through 16)—without modifying the input image or the final logits, incurring only a 1.06× inference latency overhead over greedy decoding.

## Method

The framework repurposes vision tokens for two complementary roles at the latent representation level. The Synergistic Visual Calibration (SVC) module concatenates original and augmented vision tokens into a synergistic memory bank, then injects this enriched visual context into a critical middle layer via scaled dot-product attention with interpolation, counteracting the progressive decay of visual attention during generation. The Causal Representation Calibration (CRC) module creates latent-space negative samples by randomly pruning the vision token sequence to retain only 5 tokens (an information-gap rather than a modality-gap), runs parallel forward passes to compute a stable hallucination direction vector via averaging the differential representations, and subtracts this direction from the main computational stream in normalized space across shallow layers. The CRC design is grounded in Structural Causal Models, where the differential vector isolates the causal effect of missing visual information by canceling shared query and bias terms. Both modules are training-free, parameter-free (aside from two hyperparameters λ_s and λ_c), and operate entirely on intermediate representations, achieving an average 2% absolute POPE accuracy improvement on LLaVA-1.5 across multiple benchmarks.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (averaged across random, popular, and adversarial splits), the proposed method achieves 86.79% accuracy and 87.04% F1 with LLaVA-1.5 on MSCOCO, 82.23% accuracy on AOKVQA, and 81.54% accuracy on GQA, outperforming all baselines by 0.6–2.0% absolute accuracy. On CHAIR (64 tokens), it achieves the best sentence-level score of 18.1 (LLaVA-1.5) and 16.7 (Shikra), and the best instance-level score of 5.9 (Shikra). On MME, it reaches Perception 1456.28 and Cognition 332.86 on LLaVA-1.5, surpassing all baselines. The method incurs only 1.06× inference latency overhead (32.1 ms/token) and 14,924 MB peak GPU memory, making it faster and lighter than VCD (2.4×) and VISTA (1.1×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary model LLaVA-1.5, the proposed method consistently outperforms all four baselines across all three POPE benchmarks (MSCOCO, AOKVQA, GQA) on both accuracy and F1, with the largest margin over VCD (+2.0% on MSCOCO accuracy) and a clear lead over the strongest competitor VISTA (+0.64% on MSCOCO, +1.0% on AOKVQA, +0.65% on GQA). On Shikra, the method also wins across all benchmarks, achieving 83.84% accuracy on MSCOCO vs. ONLY's 82.75% and VISTA's 82.44%. However, on MiniGPT-4, ONLY slightly edges out the proposed method on MSCOCO (76.98% vs. 76.78% accuracy) and AOKVQA (72.54% vs. 73.13% — actually Ours wins here), while on InstructBLIP, ONLY and VISTA marginally surpass the proposed method on MSCOCO accuracy (84.90% and 84.87% vs. 84.59%). In terms of efficiency, the proposed method (32.1 ms/token, 1.06×) is substantially faster than VCD (72.72 ms/token, 2.4×) and slightly faster than VISTA (33.35 ms/token, 1.1×), while using less peak memory than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant training-free paradigms for MLLM hallucination mitigation—visual attention enhancement (PAI, VISTA) and logit-level contrastive decoding (VCD, ONLY)—by arguing they operate at incompatible levels (attention stage vs. final logits) and cannot be naively combined. It repurposes the vision token as a single source for both enhancement (SVC, drawing on augmented-image complementarity explored in [8,14,39]) and suppression (CRC, replacing VCD's image-level modality-gap with a latent-space information-gap via token pruning). The SCM-based theoretical justification for CRC is novel in this context, grounding the differential-vector calibration in a causal decomposition of visual signal versus shared linguistic bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified latent-level framework offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc patchwork combinations, and the information-gap vs. modality-gap distinction provides a reusable design principle for constructing negative samples in any representation-space intervention. However, the SCM justification relies on a local linear approximation that is not rigorously validated, the augmentation strategy (fixed flip/blur/noise) is not adaptive, and evaluation is limited to four MLLMs and text-only outputs. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or multi-image MLLMs, whether the specific middle-layer intervention point generalizes across model depths, and whether the 2% POPE gain translates to meaningful improvements in downstream agentic or safety-critical applications.
