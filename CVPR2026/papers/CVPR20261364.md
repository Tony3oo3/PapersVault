## Problem

Existing prototype-based federated learning methods construct a single global prototype per class by averaging local prototypes across all clients, which dilutes domain-specific semantics and forces domain-agnostic alignment that ignores feature distribution differences across domains. This leads to semantic conflict when visually distinct representations of the same class (e.g., a photo vs. a sketch of a dog) are forced to match one blended prototype, degrading generalization under domain shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client provides RGB images from a known domain (e.g., Clipart, Painting, Real for DomainNet; Caltech, Amazon, Webcam, DSLR for Office-10; Photo, Art, Cartoon, Sketch for PACS) along with class labels and a domain identifier. The server produces a domain-specific global prototype tensor of shape D×C×I (domains × classes × feature dimension), which is broadcast back to clients for local training. The final model outputs class predictions, and the prototype tensor serves as structured semantic supervision during local updates.

## Method

FedDAP replaces the single per-class prototype with a set of domain-specific prototypes, one per (class, domain) pair, aggregated on the server via a cosine similarity-weighted fusion mechanism that up-weights semantically consistent local prototypes and down-weights outliers. During local training, a dual alignment strategy is applied: (1) Domain-Consistent Prototype Alignment (DPA) minimizes the cosine distance between local features and the intra-domain prototype for the same class, enforcing domain-specific semantic consistency; (2) Cross-Domain Prototype Contrastive Learning (CPCL) applies a softmax contrastive loss that pulls features toward same-class prototypes from other domains while pushing them away from different-class cross-domain prototypes, encouraging domain-invariant class discrimination. The total local loss combines cross-entropy classification with weighted DPA and CPCL terms (λ₁·L_DPA + λ₂·L_CPCL), and the global model is updated by standard weighted FedAvg of client parameters each round.

## Results

FedDAP achieves the highest average top-1 accuracy across all three multi-domain benchmarks: 65.20% on DomainNet (+5.61% over FedAvg), 72.53% on Office-10 (+15.06% over FedAvg), and 84.63% on PACS (+7.56% over FedAvg). The largest absolute gain is on Office-10, where domain shift between Caltech, Amazon, Webcam, and DSLR is most pronounced. FedDAP also converges faster than all baselines, reaching higher accuracy in fewer communication rounds as shown in the training curves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedDAP outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset. Against the standard FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, MOON), the gains are substantial: +5.61%, +4.63%, and +5.05% on DomainNet respectively, confirming that domain-aware prototypes address a failure mode these methods do not target. Among prototype-based methods, FedDAP surpasses FedProto by +4.69% (DomainNet) and +7.84% (Office-10), and FPL by +3.03% and +5.01%, showing that domain-specific prototype construction and dual alignment are critical beyond generic prototype aggregation. The closest competitors are FedPLVM (NeurIPS'24) and FedRDN (CVPR'25): FedDAP leads FedPLVM by +2.98% on DomainNet and +3.76% on Office-10, and leads FedRDN by +4.19% on DomainNet but only +1.46% on PACS, where FedRDN's domain-normalization approach is more competitive. On PACS, FedDAP still achieves the best result (84.63%) but the margin over FedRDN (83.17%) is the smallest across all dataset-method pairs, suggesting that for fewer, more visually distinct domains the advantage of explicit domain-specific prototypes narrows.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedDAP extends the prototype-based federated learning paradigm introduced by FedProto (Tan et al., AAAI 2022) and the domain-shift-aware prototype view of FPL (Huang et al., CVPR 2023) by replacing the single domain-agnostic global prototype per class with a set of domain-specific prototypes for each (class, domain) pair. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) a cosine similarity-weighted fusion mechanism for aggregating local prototypes within the same domain, and (2) a dual alignment strategy that treats intra-domain prototypes (pulling local features toward them) and cross-domain prototypes (contrastive separation) asymmetrically, rather than applying uniform alignment as in prior prototype-based FL methods. This positions FedDAP between the broader federated domain generalization line (COPA, FedGA) and the prototype-learning line (FedProto, FedPLVM), explicitly modeling domain-conditioned semantics rather than seeking domain-invariant representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The domain-specific prototype formulation offers a principled way to preserve domain semantics during federated aggregation, addressing a realistic deployment scenario where clients naturally operate in distinct visual domains (e.g., different sensors, styles, or collection modalities). However, the approach assumes each client has a known, fixed domain identifier, which may not hold in dynamic or open-world federated settings where new domains emerge over time. The evaluation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (10–345 classes, ResNet-10/18 backbones) and a leave-one-domain-out protocol, leaving open whether the gains transfer to larger-scale or more heterogeneous domain distributions. Future work could explore adaptive domain discovery, handling clients with mixed-domain data, and scaling to high-resolution or video modalities.
