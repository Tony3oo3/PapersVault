## Problem

Pretraining noise in large-scale visual models causes catastrophic inheritance, degrading downstream task performance. Prior work attributes this to spectral energy degradation (weakening of dominant singular values) and proposes amplifying those components, implicitly assuming principal feature directions remain fixed. This paper reveals an overlooked mechanism: even mild noise induces significant rotation of the principal feature subspace, which disrupts semantic alignment during fine-tuning independent of spectral magnitude changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263790/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates as a fine-tuning adapter on top of frozen pretrained backbones (ResNet, ViT, Swin Transformer, ConvNeXt, EfficientNet), taking standard RGB images and producing class labels. A lightweight learnable projection module is inserted between the backbone and task classifier, requiring no backbone gradients and making the approach applicable to black-box or closed-source pretrained models. Evaluation spans 8 in-domain classification benchmarks (CIFAR-10/100, Flowers102, Food101, OxfordPet, StanfordCars, DTD, Caltech101) and 4 out-of-domain DomainNet transfer settings.

## Method

The Feature Geometry Stabilization (FGS) framework introduces three coordinated regularization losses applied during fine-tuning through a lightweight projection module. Feature Perturbation Consistency (FPC) injects controlled noise (e.g., salt-and-pepper) into backbone features and enforces cosine-similarity alignment between original and perturbed representations, anchoring local directional stability. Variance-Activation Regularization (VAR) penalizes feature dimensions that simultaneously exhibit high batch variance and high activation magnitude, suppressing noise-aligned shortcut directions from dominating the spectral structure. Feature Consistency Distillation (FCD) minimizes KL divergence between softmax-projected and pretrained features to preserve inherited semantic structure during subspace correction. The total objective combines cross-entropy with these three terms, and the paper introduces the Principal Directional Angle (PDA) metric—computed via SVD of feature matrices—to quantitatively measure and validate the subspace rotation that FGS counteracts.

## Results

On in-domain (ID) tasks across 8 datasets, FGS achieves average accuracies of 0.8448 (EfficientNet-B3), 0.8665 (ResNetv2-152x2), 0.9074 (Swin-L), 0.9417 (ConvNeXt-L), and 0.9354 (ViT-L), consistently outperforming MLP and NMTune by 1.4–2.0% and 0.7–1.5% respectively. On out-of-domain (OOD) DomainNet tasks, FGS improves over MLP and NMTune by up to 3.97% and 3.56% (ResNetv2-152x2), 3.03% and 2.59% (ViT-L), and 3.97% and 1.53% (Swin-L). Under synthetic noise (ResNet-50/ImageNet-1K), FGS gains 1.8–2.3% over MLP and 1.1–1.4% over NMTune on ID tasks, with comparable OOD improvements. The overall average gain over the strongest prior method (NMTune) is at least +1.53% across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGS consistently outperforms all three baselines (LP, MLP, NMTune) across every model architecture and both ID and OOD settings. Against LP, the weakest baseline, FGS gains 2.5–5.5% in average accuracy depending on the backbone, confirming that simple linear probing is insufficient for noisy pretrained features. Against MLP (Chen et al., ICLR 2024), which adds nonlinear adaptation capacity, FGS still leads by 1.4–3.0% on ID and 2.8–4.0% on OOD tasks, indicating that nonlinear capacity alone does not address the geometric misalignment caused by pretraining noise. Against NMTune (Chen et al., TPAMI 2025), the strongest spectral-based baseline that amplifies leading singular values, FGS achieves a more consistent and larger margin: 0.7–1.5% on ID and 1.5–3.6% on OOD, with the largest gaps appearing on OOD tasks (e.g., +3.56% on ResNetv2-152x2, +2.59% on ViT-L). This pattern supports the paper's central claim that spectral energy recovery alone is insufficient when the principal subspace has rotated, and that geometric stabilization provides complementary benefits beyond what NMTune's spectral regularization captures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant spectral perspective on catastrophic inheritance (Chen et al. 2024, NMTune), which attributes noise-induced degradation to weakening of dominant singular values and remedies it by amplifying leading components. The authors argue this view implicitly assumes principal directions remain fixed, and instead show that even mild noise causes measurable rotation of the dominant feature subspace while spectral energy stays relatively stable. Their FGS framework repurposes standard regularization ideas (perturbation consistency, variance penalties, distillation) but organizes them around the new geometric objective of stabilizing subspace direction rather than recovering spectral magnitude, and introduces PDA as a diagnostic metric absent from prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from spectral energy to subspace geometry offers a more complete mechanistic account of why noisy pretraining hurts downstream tasks, and explains why purely spectral corrections (e.g., NMTune) can be insufficient or even counterproductive when noise-aligned directions have shifted. The FGS framework is lightweight (a projection module requiring no backbone gradients), making it applicable to black-box or frozen-backbone settings common in practice. Open questions include whether PDA-based diagnostics generalize to very large or multi-modal foundation models where SVD is intractable, whether the synthetic random-label noise protocol captures the structured biases of real web-scale data, and whether the moderate gains (1.5–4%) scale further with stronger noise or larger architectures.
