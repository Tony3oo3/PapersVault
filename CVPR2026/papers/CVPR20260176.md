## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language encoders struggle to simultaneously achieve strong global image-text alignment and precise dense patch-text alignment, with larger models paradoxically underperforming smaller ones on patch-level grounding tasks. The paper identifies that standard masked image modeling objectives (iBOT) leave visible token representations unsupervised, causing local semantics to be lost in the final transformer layers, which limits zero-shot segmentation and other dense multimodal tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260176/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (processed at 224×224 or 448×448 for global crops, 98×98 or 140×140 for local crops) alongside text captions drawn from three sources: noisy web alt-text, PaliGemma synthetic captions, and Gemini Flash captions. The ViT image encoder outputs a global CLS embedding plus N patch-level embeddings, while a standard transformer text encoder produces a single text embedding; at inference the frozen encoder supports zero-shot segmentation via cosine similarity between patch features and class-name text features, retrieval, classification, depth, and normal estimation.

## Method

TIPSv2 introduces three modifications to the TIPS pretraining recipe. First, iBOT++ extends the iBOT masked-image-modeling loss by removing the mask indicator from the summation, so the student is supervised to match the teacher's patch representations on both masked and unmasked tokens, anchoring visible-patch semantics that standard iBOT leaves free. Second, a head-only EMA strategy sets the teacher vision encoder equal to the student (f_t = f_s) and applies EMA updates only to the projection heads, exploiting the fact that the CLIP contrastive loss already prevents encoder collapse; this reduces trainable parameters by ~42% on ViT-B. Third, multi-granularity caption sampling randomly alternates between detailed Gemini captions and simpler PaliGemma captions to supervise the second CLS token, balancing contrastive difficulty and detail extraction. The full training objective is L_CLIP + L_DINO + L_iBOT++, trained on 116M WebLI images at ViT-g scale for 2 days on 512 TPUv5 chips.

## Results

TIPSv2 achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot segmentation, reaching 44.4 mIoU on VOC21 and 24.7 mIoU on ADE150 (ViT-L), surpassing all prior vision-language encoders. On image-only tasks (ViT-g), TIPSv2 attains 85.1 mIoU on PASCAL segmentation, 0.334 RMSE on NYUv2 depth, and 86.8% top-1 linear probe on ImageNet, achieving best or second-best in 7 of 9 evaluations. On global image-text retrieval, TIPSv2 leads on all COCO and DOCCI benchmarks (e.g., 75.7 I→T COCO, 72.8 T→I DOCCI), outperforming even larger ViT-G models. Against the very recent DINOv3 (trained with 6× more parameters and 15× more images), TIPSv2 wins 4 of 6 metrics at the comparable ViT-L scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TIPS (the direct predecessor), TIPSv2 improves zero-shot segmentation from 20.8 to 24.7 mIoU on ADE150 and PASCAL segmentation from 83.6 to 85.1 mIoU, with the iBOT++ change alone contributing +14.1 mIoU on ADE150 zero-shot segmentation. Versus SigLIP2, TIPSv2 dominates on dense alignment (44.4 vs 26.8 VOC21, 24.7 vs 15.6 ADE150) and image-only tasks (85.1 vs 78.1 PASCAL seg, 0.334 vs 0.466 NYUv2 depth), while SigLIP2 has no reported UnED or ImageNet numbers. Against DINOv2 (g/14), TIPSv2 wins PASCAL seg (+2.0), ADE20k seg (+2.1), NYUv2 depth (−0.038), and UnED retrieval (+4.3), but DINOv2 retains a small edge on ImageNet linear probe (87.3 vs 86.8). Compared to PE-core G/14 (which has 56% more parameters and 47× more training pairs), TIPSv2 wins COCO I→T retrieval (75.7 vs 75.4) and T→I (60.7 vs 58.1) but trails on ImageNet zero-shot (80.7 vs 85.4). Against DINOv3 L/16 (trained with 6× more parameters and 15× more images), TIPSv2 L/14 wins on depth (0.339 vs 0.352), COCO I→T (73.5 vs 63.7), T→I (57.4 vs 45.6), and zero-shot seg (25.1 vs 24.7), while DINOv3 leads on ADE20k seg (54.9 vs 51.4) and ImageNet (82.3 vs 79.7). TIPSv2 also outperforms SILC on zero-shot segmentation (37.1 vs 31.6 PC59, 24.7 vs 19.3 ADE150) and COCO retrieval (75.7 vs 73.2), and surpasses FRANCA on PASCAL seg (85.1 vs 81.3) and ADE20k seg (51.6 vs 42.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

TIPSv2 directly extends TIPS (ICLR 2025), which unified CLIP-style contrastive image-text learning with DINO/iBOT self-supervised objectives in a single ViT pretraining recipe. The key methodological departure is iBOT++, which modifies the iBOT masked image modeling loss to supervise all patch tokens (both masked and unmasked) rather than only masked ones, inspired by the empirical finding that full-token distillation dramatically improves patch-text alignment. The paper also simplifies the standard EMA teacher setup to a 'head-only EMA' (EMA on projection heads only, sharing the encoder), justified by the contrastive loss already preventing encoder collapse, and introduces multi-granularity caption sampling from PaliGemma and Gemini to balance learning difficulty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a smaller distilled student can surpass its larger teacher in dense patch-text alignment challenges the assumption that scale monotonically improves all capabilities, and suggests that the pretraining recipe itself (specifically, which tokens receive supervision) is a critical lever for spatial grounding. Practically, TIPSv2 sets new SOTA in zero-shot semantic segmentation while matching or exceeding larger models on global image-text tasks, and the head-only EMA reduces training parameters by ~42% on ViT-B, making the recipe more accessible. Open questions include whether the iBOT++ benefit persists at even larger scales (the paper's largest model is ViT-g with 1.1B params vs. DINOv3's 7B), whether the ImageNet classification gap (86.8 vs. 89.5 for PE-core) can be closed without sacrificing dense alignment, and whether the multi-granularity caption strategy generalizes to multilingual or non-English settings.
