## Problem

Existing unified large vision models achieve functional integration across image, video, and 3D modalities but fail to enable true cross-vision synergy—bidirectional, synesthetic reasoning that transfers complementary priors (e.g., temporal motion cues to static images, or 3D geometric structure to video). Two critical gaps remain: (i) no architectural mechanism explicitly facilitates inter-modal feature interaction beyond shared encoders or concatenation, and (ii) no training strategy jointly aligns modality-specific representations at both instance and object/relation levels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263099/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

PolyV accepts a single visual modality at inference—either one RGB image, a video (processed as a temporally ordered frame sequence with dynamic key-frame sampling and temporal positional embeddings), or 3D data (multiple rendered views augmented with per-pixel 3D coordinate encodings)—alongside a free-form text prompt. The universal ViT-based encoder projects all modalities into a shared token space, which is concatenated with text embeddings and fed into the LLM. The model outputs a natural-language text response, but critically, it can reason about unobserved modalities: e.g., given only a static image, it implicitly leverages temporal and spatial priors learned by other experts to infer motion or 3D structure.

## Method

PolyV builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and replaces FFN layers at 4-layer intervals with sparse MoE blocks (4 experts, top-2 activated per token), coordinated by a dynamic router that adaptively allocates tokens to modality-specialized experts. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 performs modality-specific pretraining where each expert independently learns image, video, or 3D representations via cross-entropy on captioning and instruction data. Stage 2 introduces synergy-aware fine-tuning with two components: (a) coarse-grained tuning that distills temporal priors from V-JEPA 2 and spatial priors from VGGT into dedicated <synergy> tokens via MSE loss, and (b) fine-grained tuning on a 20K Cross-Vision Synergy QA dataset (CSQA) constructed from universal scene graphs, aligning object- and relation-level correspondences across modalities. A differentiable load-balancing loss ensures balanced expert utilization throughout training.

## Results

On image understanding, PolyV achieves 71.4 on MMStar, 63.4 on 3DSR-Bench, and 31.7 on MMSI-Bench, surpassing its backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 8.9, 15.0, and 7.0 points respectively. On video understanding, PolyV scores 52.7 on VSI-Bench (+19.7 over backbone), 59.1 on CVBench (+7.8), and 69.6 on VideoMME (+4.5). On 3D understanding (Table 2), PolyV reaches 50.2 BLEU-1 and 105.6 CIDEr on ScanQA, 64.8 EM on SQA3D, and 63.4 on Open-EQA, improving over the backbone by 22.4, 51.7, 18.3, and 6.8 points respectively. Overall, PolyV delivers an average improvement of over 10% across all ten benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PolyV outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark in Table 1, with the largest gains on spatially grounded tasks: +19.7 on VSI-Bench over the backbone and +15.0 on 3DSR-Bench. Against the strongest open-source competitor InternVL3-8B, PolyV leads by 3.2 on MMStar, 5.3 on 3DSR-Bench, and 2.0 on VSI-Bench. Compared to GPT-4o, PolyV wins on MMStar (71.4 vs 64.7), 3DSR-Bench (63.4 vs 44.2), MMSI-Bench (31.7 vs 30.3), and VSI-Bench (52.7 vs 34.0), but trails on CVBench (59.1 vs 69.1) and VideoMME (69.6 vs 71.9), indicating that GPT-4o retains an edge on general video comprehension. On 3D benchmarks (Table 2), PolyV surpasses the strongest 3D-specialized baselines ROSS3D (BLEU-1: 50.2 vs –) and 3DRS (BLEU-1: 50.2 vs 48.4), and leads on SQA3D EM (64.8 vs 63.0 for ROSS3D) and Open-EQA (63.4 vs 53.2 for LLaVA-3D). Spatially focused models like Spatial-MLLM-4B and SpaceR are substantially outperformed on both spatial and general image tasks, confirming that cross-vision synergy transfers effectively to static spatial reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in unified LVMs, which achieves functional integration via shared encoders or feature concatenation (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OV, InternVL) but leaves modality-specific features isolated. It also moves beyond one-directional knowledge transfer works (video→3D in Video-3D-LLM, ROSS3D) by explicitly targeting bidirectional, interactive cross-vision synergy. Architecturally, it repurposes the MoE structure—previously applied to unimodal or task-specific settings (Uni3D-MoE, MoE-LLaVA, UniMoE)—as a mechanism for modality-specialized experts coordinated by a dynamic router, combined with synergy tokens and knowledge distillation from single-modality foundation models (V-JEPA 2, VGGT) to inject temporal and geometric priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolyV provides the first unified LVM framework that explicitly trains for bidirectional cross-vision synergy rather than mere functional integration, offering a general recipe (MoE experts + synergy tokens + coarse-to-fine distillation) that could be adopted by other unified vision models. However, several open questions remain: whether the synergy is truly bidirectional at inference time or still biased toward the training-time distillation direction, whether the approach scales beyond 7B parameters and 4 experts, and whether the 20K CSQA pairs suffice for robust fine-grained alignment. The reliance on GPT-4o-generated QA pairs and external teacher models also raises questions about data quality and the ceiling of distilled knowledge.
