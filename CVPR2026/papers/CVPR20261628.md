## Problem

Masked Discrete Diffusion Models (MDMs) process all tokens—including redundant masked ones—at every sampling step and use full attention that prevents KV-cache acceleration, making inference slow. Existing efficiency methods like Block Diffusion impose a left-to-right block-causal order that destroys the bidirectional context essential for image generation, editing, and inpainting. The paper asks whether MDMs can be made efficient via token truncation and KV caching without sacrificing bidirectional context or generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts, full images, or partial images (for inpainting/editing) and produces 1024×1024 generated images, edited images, text responses, or inpainted images. Images are tokenized into 4096 discrete tokens; during inference, only a small subset of these is actively decoded at each step while the rest are represented compactly by 64 register tokens. For text generation and understanding tasks, the model uses semi-autoregressive block sampling (block size 32) with confidence-based unmasking within each block.

## Method

Sparse-LaViDa introduces a sparse parameterization of MDMs that avoids materializing all masked tokens: at each sampling step, the model input consists only of prompt tokens, previously decoded tokens (stored in KV cache), the current decode subset, and 64 special register tokens that serve as compressed representations of all truncated masked positions. A step-causal attention mask is designed for training that simulates the incremental KV-cache behavior of inference—clean tokens attend to earlier blocks, masked tokens attend only to prompt/clean tokens and their own block, and register tokens follow the same rules as their associated masked block—closing the training-inference gap without distillation. The model is initialized from LaViDa-O (10.4B) and fine-tuned for 100k steps on 64 H100 GPUs using the standard MDM objective, with the only change being the sparse parameterization and step-causal masking. This design preserves full bidirectional context and supports arbitrary unmasking orders (e.g., pre-generated 2D spatial orders for images), unlike block-causal alternatives.

## Results

On GenEval, Sparse-LaViDa achieves 0.78 overall score (vs. 0.77 for LaViDa-O) while reducing text-to-image latency from 21.27s to 10.86s (1.95× speedup). On ImgEdit, it reaches 3.79 overall (vs. 3.71 for LaViDa-O) with a 2.83× speedup (22.55s vs. 63.98s). On MathVista visual math reasoning, it matches the baseline accuracy (56.7 vs. 56.9) while achieving a 2.80× speedup (3.72s vs. 10.41s). On DPG-Bench, Sparse-LaViDa scores 82.4 vs. 81.8 for LaViDa-O, and on MJHQ-30K it achieves HPSv3 of 8.89 vs. 8.81.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct baseline LaViDa-O, Sparse-LaViDa matches or slightly exceeds quality (GenEval 0.78 vs. 0.77; DPG 82.4 vs. 81.8; ImgEdit 3.79 vs. 3.71) while delivering 1.95×–2.83× speedups across tasks. Compared to Flux-Dev (12B), Sparse-LaViDa achieves higher GenEval overall (0.78 vs. 0.68) at roughly 3× lower latency (10.86s vs. 31.6s). Against BAGEL (14B), Sparse-LaViDa trades a small GenEval gap (0.78 vs. 0.82) for a 4.2× latency reduction (10.86s vs. 45.1s). On image editing, Sparse-LaViDa (3.79) outperforms all unified models including BAGEL (3.20), OmniGen2 (3.44), and UniWorld-V1 (3.26), though it remains below GPT-4o (4.20) by 0.41 points while being 5× faster (22.55s vs. 111.4s). On MathVista, Sparse-LaViDa (56.7) slightly trails LaViDa-O (56.9) but substantially outperforms the training-free Fast-dLLM baseline (56.1) in both accuracy and latency (3.72s vs. 5.57s). On image understanding benchmarks (MME, MMMU, ChartQA, DocVQA, MathVerse), Sparse-LaViDa is competitive with LaViDa-O, with small drops on MME (450 vs. 488) and MMMU (43.6 vs. 45.1) but gains on ChartQA (82.0 vs. 80.0), DocVQA (75.7 vs. 73.7), and MathVerse (37.9 vs. 36.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

Sparse-LaViDa builds directly on the LaViDa-O unified masked discrete diffusion model and the standard MDM formulation of Sahoo et al. (NeurIPS 2024), proposing a sparse parameterization that avoids materializing all masked tokens at each sampling step. It departs from Block Diffusion and D2F, which achieve efficiency through left-to-right block-causal attention masks that sacrifice bidirectional context, and from training-free KV-caching heuristics (Fast-dLLM, dKV-Cache, Sparse-dLLM) that assume semi-autoregressive decoding. The key conceptual departure is that Sparse-LaViDa treats token truncation as a parameterization choice rather than a decoding-order constraint, enabling arbitrary-position truncation while preserving the full bidirectional attention structure of vanilla MDMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first MDM acceleration method that simultaneously supports KV caching, arbitrary-position token truncation, and bidirectional context, making it applicable to tasks like inpainting and text infilling that block-causal methods cannot handle. The ~2× speedup across generation, editing, and reasoning tasks—achieved on top of LaViDa-O's existing optimizations—has direct practical value for deployment. Key limitations include the requirement for supervised fine-tuning (not training-free) and the fact that experiments are conducted in a post-training setting rather than pre-training from scratch, leaving open whether the sparse parameterization scales to large-scale pre-training. The finding that register tokens primarily improve low-level visual fidelity (FID, HPS) rather than structural coherence (GenEval) suggests an interesting separation of concerns for future design.
