## Problem

Diffusion model inference is too slow for interactive use, and existing acceleration methods (training-free ODE solvers and distillation) fail to produce previews that are both high-quality and consistent with the final full-step output. Training-free solvers yield low-fidelity previews at low step counts, while distillation methods disrupt the deterministic PF-ODE mapping between noise and data space, breaking the guarantee that refining a satisfactory preview reproduces the expected result.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262904/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and a fixed random noise vector z~N(0,I) and produces a few-step preview (5–12 NFE) that closely approximates the 40-step DPM-Solver reference; for image editing (FLUX.1-Kontext), it takes a reference image plus an editing instruction. The same (prompt, noise) pair is reused in the refinement stage, so the preview and final image are deterministically linked via the PF-ODE. The solver operates in the normalized y-space (x_t/α_t) and only requires a lightweight MLP (~order-4 coefficients) on top of the frozen diffusion model's ε-predictions.

## Method

ConsistencySolver is a learnable, explicit, order-m linear multistep ODE solver whose integration coefficients w_j(t_i, t_{i+1}) are produced by a small MLP policy network conditioned on the current and next timesteps, rather than fixed by Taylor-expansion or polynomial-extrapolation assumptions. The update rule is y_{t_{i+1}} = y_{t_i} + (n_{t_{i+1}} − n_{t_i}) · Σ_j w_j(t_i,t_{i+1})·ε_{i+1−j}, with w_0=0 (explicit) and μ_0=1 (anchor to current state). The policy is trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) against an offline dataset of (prompt, noise, full-step reference) triples, using a perceptual similarity reward (default: Depth Anything V2 PSNR) between the K-step preview and the 40-step target. This RL formulation avoids backpropagating through the diffusion trajectory, supports non-differentiable rewards, and requires only the compact MLP in the loss computation, yielding lower memory and better generalization than trajectory-distillation alternatives.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion (COCO 2017), ConsistencySolver achieves FID 20.39 at 5 steps, outperforming Multistep DPM-Solver (25.87) and UniPC (23.15) at the same step count, and reaches FID 18.53 at 12 steps with peak consistency scores of 97.9 (CLIP) and 95.1 (Inception). On FLUX.1-Kontext (KontextBench), it attains the best Edit Reward (0.86) and Edit Score (6.02) at 5 steps, surpassing Multistep DPM (0.83 / 5.92). In the user study, the preview-and-refine workflow reduces average end-to-end inference time by up to 55% (e.g., 6.35 s → 2.87 s on LAION) while maintaining 94.2% prompt satisfaction versus 57.0% for DMD2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free ODE solvers, ConsistencySolver consistently wins on FID and all six consistency metrics at every step count: at 5 steps it improves FID by 2.8 over UniPC (20.39 vs 23.15) and 5.5 over Multistep DPM (20.39 vs 25.87); at 8 steps it beats Multistep DPM by 0.71 FID (18.82 vs 19.53). Versus distillation-based methods (DMD2, LCM, PCM, Rectified Diffusion), ConsistencySolver at 4–8 steps achieves lower FID and substantially higher consistency scores (e.g., DINO 91.2 vs LCM's 75.1 at 4 steps), while preserving the deterministic PF-ODE mapping that distillation disrupts. Against trajectory-distillation solvers (AMED, Ours-Distill), the RL-trained ConsistencySolver yields better FID (18.82 vs 19.22 at 8 steps) and higher consistency across CLIP, DINO, and Depth. Cross-model transfer shows the solver trained on SD1.5 generalizes to SDXL (FID 23.32 vs 26.32 for Multistep DPM at 10 steps) and SD1.4 (FID 20.22 vs 25.22 at 5 steps) without retraining. In the user-satisfaction study, ConsistencySolver (8-step preview) retains 94.2% of base-model prompt satisfaction on GenEval, whereas DMD2 drops to 57.0% (with GAN) or 47.1% (without), confirming that consistency—not just FID—matters for interactive workflows.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsistencySolver is derived from classical Linear Multistep Methods (LMMs) for ODE integration, adapted to the PF-ODE formulation of diffusion models by making the multistep coefficients timestep-conditioned and learnable. It positions itself between two existing acceleration paradigms: training-free ODE solvers (DPM-Solver, DEIS, UniPC) that use fixed, theoretically-derived coefficients, and distillation-based methods (DMD2, LCM, PCM, AMED) that bake acceleration into model weights. The key departure is treating solver coefficients as a policy optimized via PPO against a perceptual similarity reward, rather than deriving them analytically or regressing them via trajectory matching. The 'Diffusion Preview' two-stage workflow (fast preview → full-step refinement) is framed as a new paradigm, though the underlying task of few-step diffusion sampling is well-studied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that distillation-based methods like DMD2, despite competitive FID scores, satisfy dramatically fewer user prompts (57% vs. 100% on GenEval) because they break the deterministic noise-to-image mapping that preview-and-refine workflows depend on — a failure mode invisible to distribution-level metrics. This suggests the field needs consistency-aware evaluation beyond FID for interactive generation settings. Limitations include reliance on an offline reference dataset for RL training, evaluation limited to Stable Diffusion and FLUX.1-Kontext, and the preview quality still degrading at very low step counts (1–2 steps). Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion, flow-matching models, or settings where the base model itself is being fine-tuned.
