## Problem

Deformable image registration (DIR) requires optimizing over million-dimensional dense displacement fields, making direct reinforcement learning infeasible, while the scarcity of voxel-level annotations limits supervised approaches for large non-rigid deformations. Existing RL-based registration methods are confined to low-dimensional rigid transforms, and single-shot inference architectures struggle to recover fine local boundaries under complex deformations without expensive multi-stage cascades.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263731/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of 3D medical volumes (moving image I_m and fixed image I_f, both in R^{H×W×D}) concatenated as input, and produces a dense per-voxel displacement field Φ ∈ R^{3×H×W×D} that warps the moving image to align with the fixed image. Unusually, the policy action is not the deformation field itself but a stochastic latent vector z sampled from a Gaussian distribution parameterized at the top encoder level; the decoder then maps z (along with skip-connection features) to the single-step deformation. The final deformation is accumulated over T refinement steps, with J trajectories sampled at each step and the highest-reward trajectory greedily selected to update the state.

## Method

MorphSeek decouples the encoder and decoder of any U-Net-style registration backbone and appends two 1×1×1 convolutional heads (mean and log-std) at the top encoder level, turning the encoder output into a stochastic Gaussian policy over latent features. Training proceeds in two phases: an unsupervised warm-up with deterministic latent (τ=0) that shapes a stable latent manifold via image-similarity, deformation-regularization, and KL losses, followed by weakly supervised fine-tuning using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with J trajectories per step over T refinement steps, where each labeled pair yields T×J relative supervision events. To make GRPO numerically stable in latent spaces of tens of thousands of dimensions, the paper introduces Latent-Dimension Variance Normalization (LDVN), which rescales the log-likelihood by s=√N to control within-group variance while preserving policy-gradient direction. The framework is backbone-agnostic (validated on VoxelMorph-L, TransMorph, and NICE-Trans) and adds less than 3% parameters, with the warm-up objective retained as an anatomy-preserving regularizer during GRPO to prevent reward hacking and posterior collapse.

## Results

Across three 3D registration benchmarks (OASIS brain MRI, LiTS liver CT, Abdomen MR←CT) and three backbones, MorphSeek consistently improves Mean Dice by 2–4% and reduces NJD by 30–60% over the corresponding baselines. The strongest configuration (NICE-Trans + MorphSeek) achieves 89.02% Dice / 0.02% NJD on OASIS, 90.47% / 0.16% on LiTS, and 86.51% / 0.32% on Abdomen MR←CT. On the most challenging cross-modality Abdomen task, TransMorph + MorphSeek yields a >4% Dice gain and nearly halves NJD relative to the TransMorph baseline. Most improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05, Wilcoxon signed-rank test).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MorphSeek is applied as a plug-in on top of three backbone architectures (VoxelMorph-L, TransMorph, NICE-Trans) and consistently outperforms each backbone's own baseline on all three datasets: e.g., TransMorph + MorphSeek reaches 88.89% Dice on OASIS vs. 85.89% for TransMorph alone (+3.0%), and 86.49% vs. 82.37% on Abdomen MR←CT (+4.1%). Against the strongest standalone multi-stage baseline, CorrMLP (88.35/89.22/86.82 Dice), NICE-Trans + MorphSeek wins on OASIS (89.02) and LiTS (90.47) but is marginally behind on Abdomen (86.51 vs. 86.82), a near-tie within one standard deviation. MorphSeek clearly surpasses the RL-based SPAC (78.92/75.38/69.29 Dice) by 10–17 Dice points, confirming that operating in a high-dimensional latent space rather than a 64-D bottleneck preserves spatial detail. Compared to the iterative RIIR (12 steps) and the semi-supervised WarpDDF+RegCut, MorphSeek achieves higher Dice and substantially lower NJD (e.g., 0.06% vs. 0.12% on OASIS; 0.32% vs. 1.11% on Abdomen), indicating better deformation regularity with fewer refinement steps (3 vs. 12). All reported gains are statistically significant (p < 0.05) under the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

## Relation to Prior Work

MorphSeek sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) deep-learning-based deformable registration (VoxelMorph, TransMorph, NICE-Trans), which it refactors by inserting a stochastic Gaussian policy head at the encoder's top-level latent features, and (2) RL-based registration (SPAC, Krebs et al.), which was previously confined to low-dimensional rigid transforms or required infeasible dense DVF supervision. The key conceptual departure is treating the encoder's high-resolution latent representation as the policy action space rather than the deformation field itself, combined with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) borrowed from LLM reasoning. The proposed Latent-Dimension Variance Normalization (LDVN) is a new technical ingredient that makes GRPO numerically stable in the tens-of-thousands-dimensional latent spaces typical of 3D U-Nets, a regime where standard GRPO formulations break down.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making RL-based step-wise optimization practical for 3D deformable registration, MorphSeek opens a path toward adaptive, data-driven coarse-to-fine refinement that goes beyond the fixed cascaded schedules of LapIRN or RIIR. The label-efficiency result—reaching 98.5% of full-label performance with only 60% of labeled pairs—is practically significant in medical imaging where voxel-level annotations are extremely scarce. Open questions include whether the fixed T-step refinement schedule can be made adaptive, whether stronger physical priors (beyond NJD) can further constrain the latent policy, and whether the latent-space policy optimization paradigm generalizes to other dense correspondence problems beyond registration. A limitation is that multi-step inference scales linearly with refinement steps and more than 8 trajectories per step triggers out-of-memory errors, constraining the exploration budget.
