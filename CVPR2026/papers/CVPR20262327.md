## Problem

Machine unlearning lacks a fast, lightweight diagnostic baseline: existing methods require hours of retraining, auxiliary retain data, or complex optimization pipelines, leaving practitioners with no quick way to assess whether selective forgetting is even feasible for a given model before committing to expensive procedures. This gap is particularly acute in safety-critical settings where harmful model behaviour must be evaluated and addressed within minutes of discovery.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model's weight matrices and a small number of feature vectors extracted from forget-set images at a chosen layer (no retain data required for the base variant; retain features are optional for the entangled-feature variant). It produces updated weight matrices in closed form, completing the entire unlearning process in under one second for classification models and roughly 0.7 seconds for Stable Diffusion. The operation is applied independently to each linear, attention, and convolutional layer, with convolutional filters temporarily unfolded into patch-wise matrix form before projection and reshaped back afterward.

## Method

MUpHT exploits the Neural Collapse property that well-trained networks concentrate same-class features in low-dimensional, near-orthogonal subspaces. It extracts the forgetting subspace via SVD of the forget-set feature matrix (top d_f left singular vectors) and removes the model's sensitivity to that subspace by the closed-form projection W* = W − W U_f U_f^T, which zeroes out activations aligned with the forgotten concept while leaving orthogonal (retain) directions essentially unchanged. For highly entangled features where the orthogonality assumption breaks down, a retain-aware variant (MUpHT†) solves a generalized Rayleigh quotient u^T C_ff u / u^T C_rr u to identify directions critical for forgetting yet unimportant for retention, then applies a weighted projection W* = W − W U_s diag(η) U_s^T with per-direction weights η_i = min(1, R(u_i)). The same projection is applied to query/key/value matrices in attention blocks, to unfolded convolutional patches, and to text-conditioned attention projections in diffusion models, making the method architecture-agnostic and training-free.

## Results

On class-wise forgetting with ResNet18 on CIFAR-100, MUpHT achieves 99.24% Unlearning Accuracy (UA), 97.42% Remaining Accuracy (RA), 75.20% Testing Accuracy (TA), and 100% MIA, yielding an average gap of only 0.91 from the retrain-from-scratch gold standard. The entire unlearning process completes in 0.004 minutes (~0.24 seconds), making it roughly 600× faster than SalUn (2.56 min) and ~10,000× faster than retraining (41.45 min). On Stable Diffusion class-wise forgetting (Imagenette), MUpHT achieves an average UA of 98.09% and FID of 0.98 in ~0.6 seconds, compared to SalUn's 99.82% UA and 1.22 FID requiring over 2 hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUpHT outperforms SalUn [8] on UA (99.24% vs. 90.53%) and average gap (0.91 vs. 2.82) while being ~600× faster (0.004 min vs. 2.56 min), and also surpasses it on Stable Diffusion FID (0.98 vs. 1.22) in 0.6 s versus >2 hours. Against the closest training-free competitor SSD [9], MUpHT achieves comparable TA (75.20% vs. 75.48%) and UA (99.24% vs. 98.67%) but is the only method that is also retain-set-free, eliminating the need for D_r access. MUpHT dominates GF [20] (the other gradient-free method) on every metric: UA 99.24% vs. 94.89%, RA 97.42% vs. 94.52%, TA 75.20% vs. 69.10%, and average gap 0.91 vs. 4.21. Compared to optimization-based methods like GA [43] and SCRUB [23], MUpHT achieves higher RA (97.42% vs. 94.15% and 96.27%) and TA (75.20% vs. 69.09% and 71.64%) without any iterative training. On the highly entangled CIFAR-20 subclass task, the retain-aware variant MUpHT† achieves 99.89% UA and 91.65% RA, outperforming all other training-free methods (SSD: 84.64% RA; GF: 85.56% RA) and even surpassing SalUn (92.13% RA) while running in 0.02 min versus 2.60 min.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUpHT repurposes the well-documented Neural Collapse and Tunnel Effect geometry—where distinct concepts occupy nearly orthogonal low-dimensional subspaces in deep networks—into a closed-form unlearning operation via SVD-based subspace projection. The closest prior work is Kodge et al. (GF, TMLR 2024), which similarly removes a 'forget' subspace from model weights but fundamentally requires retain data and two grid-searched hyperparameters; MUpHT is retain-set-free and hyperparameter-free in its base form. The retain-aware variant (MUpHT†) draws on generalized Rayleigh quotient optimization to handle entangled features, extending the projection idea beyond the strict orthogonality assumption. Conceptually, the method also aligns with training-free steering-vector approaches in LLMs and vision-language models, but applies the principle at the weight level rather than the activation level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing unlearning from hours of iterative optimization to a sub-second closed-form operation, MUpHT provides the field with a practical 'litmus test' for quickly assessing whether a given MU problem is tractable before committing to expensive pipelines—a gap the authors argue is critical in real-world deployment (e.g., the Grok-2 incident). The method's broad applicability across discriminative (CNN, ViT), generative (Stable Diffusion), and multimodal (CLIP) architectures makes it a versatile diagnostic tool. Key limitations include reliance on the orthogonality assumption (which may degrade for highly entangled or fine-grained concepts without the retain-aware variant) and the fact that it is explicitly positioned as a blunt baseline rather than a replacement for SOTA methods. Open questions include whether the subspace-separation property holds in very large foundation models and whether the approach extends to sequential or continual unlearning settings.
