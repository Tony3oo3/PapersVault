## Problem

Bundle adjustment jointly optimizes camera translations and 3D point coordinates from 2D observations, but the literature has not addressed whether the resulting solution is unique. The bipartite BA graph can contain subgraphs whose edge scales are independent of each other, leading to misplaced cameras and 3D points in reconstructions. This paper examines this unique-solvability question through the lens of parallel rigidity theory, which had not previously been applied to the BA setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point tracks (each a subgraph of cam-pt edges for one point) and the viewgraph (camera-camera edges with matched 2D observations) from which the tracks are constructed. It operates on the viewgraph rather than the full bipartite graph for efficiency, since |E_VG| ≪ |V_P| ≪ |E_BA| in practice. The output is a set of generically parallel rigid (GPR) subgraphs of the bipartite BA graph plus a filtered track set with hanging observations removed, which together yield a cleaner 3D reconstruction with fewer misplaced cameras and points.

## Method

The GPRBA algorithm extracts GPR subgraphs of the bipartite BA graph by identifying 4-length loops (the smallest possible in a bipartite graph, and provably GPR in R³) and merging them via three interaction types: shared camera+point (Type-1), two shared cameras (Type-2), and two shared 3D points (Type-3). To avoid O(|V_P|²) track-pair checks, the algorithm works implicitly through the viewgraph: all matched observations within a single cam-cam edge form a GPR subgraph (Type-2), adjacent cam-cam edges sharing a track are merged (Type-1), and the resulting subgraphs are further merged if they share ≥2 tracks (Type-3), using a union-find data structure on cam-cam edges. A complementary Hanging Observation Filter (HOF) removes cam-pt edges that are no longer supported by any cam-cam edge after track modifications. The full pipeline is invoked at every stage where tracks are constructed, subset-selected, or filtered, retaining only the largest GPR subgraph each time for efficiency.

## Results

On IMC 2022, applying HOF alone dramatically increases reconstructed 3D points without retriangulation (e.g., Brandenburg Gate: 0.5k → 16k; Colosseum: 1.6k → 56k) while keeping camera counts nearly identical. Adding GPRBA on top of HOF slightly reduces camera and point counts (removing misplaced components) but substantially improves median camera translation errors (e.g., Buckingham Palace: 0.69 m → 0.17 m; Temple Nara: 0.74 m → 0.28 m) and reduces the number of cameras with errors > 5 m (e.g., Buckingham Palace: 13 → 9; Colosseum: 26 → 24). On 1DSfM, HOF+GPRBA removes a large fraction of cameras and 3D points that cannot form a GPR graph (e.g., Ellis Island: 1302 → 321 cameras; Yorkminster: 2014 → 585 cameras), confirming widespread independent-scaling issues in global SfM. Computationally, five invocations of HOF+GPRBA take < 2% of GLOMAP's reconstruction time (e.g., Trafalgar: 4.07 min vs. 651.54 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is GLOMAP [64], a recent global SfM pipeline. HOF alone wins on 3D point count: it increases reconstructed points by 10–100× without retriangulation (e.g., 0.5k → 16k on Brandenburg Gate) while keeping camera counts essentially unchanged, indicating that hanging observations were suppressing valid triangulations. HOF+GPRBA trades a small reduction in camera and point counts for substantially lower median camera translation errors on most IMC 2022 scenes (e.g., 0.69 → 0.17 m on Buckingham Palace, 0.74 → 0.28 m on Temple Nara, 0.60 → 0.40 m on Colosseum) and fewer cameras with errors > 5 m (e.g., 13 → 9 on Buckingham Palace, 26 → 24 on Colosseum). The cameras removed by GPRBA have very high mean errors in GLOMAP (e.g., 1081 m on Brandenburg Gate, 329 m on Buckingham Palace, 184 m on St Pauls), confirming they were misplaced due to independent scaling. On 1DSfM, where ground truth is unavailable for all cameras, HOF+GPRBA removes a large fraction of the reconstruction (e.g., 76% of cameras on Ellis Island, 71% on Yorkminster), demonstrating that a significant portion of global SfM output is not generically parallel rigid and cannot be reliably merged. The method is computationally negligible, adding < 2% overhead to GLOMAP's reconstruction time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper connects parallel rigidity theory—developed primarily in sensor network localization and robotics (Arrigoni & Fusiello 2018; Whiteley 1986, 1989; Katz et al. 2007)—to the bundle adjustment problem in structure-from-motion, a connection the authors argue has been absent from the BA literature. It builds on the GLOMAP global SfM pipeline (Pan et al. 2024) as the experimental platform and leverages the viewgraph structure (camera-camera matched observations) that underlies track construction in standard SfM pipelines. The algorithmic contribution draws on the GPR merging property for bipartite graphs (Katz et al. 2007) and the union-find data structure for efficient subgraph extraction, but the specific application to 4-length loops in the camera-point bipartite graph and the Hanging Observation Filter are novel to this work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental uniqueness question in BA that has gone unexamined: when the bipartite camera-point graph is not generically parallel rigid, subgraphs can be independently scaled, producing misplaced cameras and 3D points that standard reprojection-error optimization cannot detect or correct. The practical impact is demonstrated through significantly cleaner reconstructions on IMC 2022 and 1DSfM, with the HOF alone yielding large gains in reconstructed 3D points by removing unsupported observations. Limitations include the restriction to 4-length loops (higher-order interactions may be missed, so extracted GPR subgraphs may not be maximal), the lack of outlier handling, and the observation that incremental pipelines are less affected since they naturally maintain GPR connectivity. Open questions include whether the analysis extends to non-pinhole camera models, whether it can be integrated into incremental SfM/SLAM systems, and whether the GPR subgraph extraction can be made provably maximal.
