## Problem

Zero- and few-shot object detection (ZSOD/FSOD) is hampered by data scarcity for novel classes and catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new categories. Existing vision-language models achieve zero-shot performance but rely on large-scale visual pretraining incompatible with standard ZS/FSOD protocols, while traditional few-shot methods struggle to integrate external supervision or generalize robustly. Moreover, most prior works treat zero-shot and few-shot settings separately, lacking a unified framework that smoothly transitions between them.

## Input / Output

The system consumes an RGB query image, a persistent Knowledge Base of patch-level entries (each storing a visual embedding, bounding-box coordinates, and matched semantic attributes), and class-name text prompts. At inference, the query image is over-segmented into patches without any object annotations; at training, ground-truth crops are over-segmented for higher-purity KB entries. The output is a set of bounding boxes with confidence scores computed as the product of token-level sequence probabilities from the LLM, filtered by a threshold β.

## Method

AgentDet decomposes detection into four cooperating agents—Scout, Pinner, Curator, and Judge—that jointly maintain a transient Shared Blackboard and a persistent Knowledge Base. Agent-Scout applies multi-scale uniform cropping and CLIP-based visual-semantic alignment to produce holistic textual cues (target category set with scores); Agent-Pinner retrieves fragment-level visual references from the KB via DINOv2 dot-product similarity and projects them into a reference sequence; Agent-Curator performs pseudo-incremental KB maintenance (crop-then-oversegment with labels at training, full-image over-segmentation with consistency gating at inference) to densify the KB without new-class box supervision; Agent-Judge encodes the query with EVA-CLIP + Q-Former, feeds visual tokens and a language prompt containing the retrieved references and target categories into a LoRA-adapted LLM, and applies a self-refinement prompting loop to produce final boxes. Only Agent-Judge's image encoder and LLM detection head are updated during training, making the recipe lightweight and encouraging generalization to unseen categories while the pseudo-incremental schedule unifies ZSOD and FSOD within a single architecture and execution loop.

## Results

On PASCAL VOC few-shot detection, AgentDet-Qwen3-8b achieves 69.6% mAP at 10-shot on Novel Split 1, 59.5% on Split 2, and 63.9% on Split 3, setting new state-of-the-art across all three splits. On COCO, the same model reaches 37.5% mAP at 30-shot and 31.6% at 10-shot, outperforming all prior LLM-based methods in the low-shot regime (1–10 shots). In the zero-shot setting on PASCAL VOC, AgentDet-Qwen3-8b attains 35.7/29.2/33.5 mAP across the three novel splits, substantially exceeding the best VLM baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7b at 26.2/23.6/23.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentDet-Qwen3-8b outperforms all 15 baselines on PASCAL VOC at 10-shot across all three novel splits: it beats the strongest traditional method ICPE (66.3/52.5/60.1) by +3.3/+7.0/+3.8 mAP, and surpasses the best LLM-based competitors LLaFS (60.9/59.1/61.3) and FM-FSOD (61.2/58.7/62.6) by +8.7/+0.4/+2.6 and +8.4/+0.8/+1.3 mAP respectively. On COCO, AgentDet dominates in the low-shot regime (1–10 shots), e.g., at 10-shot it reaches 31.6% mAP versus 27.8% for LLMdet and 27.7% for FM-FSOD, a gap of roughly 4 mAP. However, at the 30-shot setting on COCO, LLMdet (37.8%) narrowly edges out AgentDet-Qwen3-8b (37.5%) by 0.3 mAP, while AgentDet still leads FM-FSOD (37.0%) and all non-LLM baselines (≤31.0%). In the zero-shot PASCAL VOC setting, AgentDet-Qwen3-8b (35.7/29.2/33.5) substantially outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-7b (26.2/23.6/23.6) and all other VLM baselines, with the gap reaching up to 9.9 mAP on Split 1. The Llama3.1-8b variant of AgentDet is consistently weaker than the Qwen variants, suggesting that the LLM backbone's language prior quality matters for detection grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentDet carves new ground by repurposing the shared-blackboard multi-agent paradigm from LLM agent literature (AutoGen, CAMEL-style role division) into a structured detection pipeline, rather than building on any single prior detector. It departs from traditional FSOD methods (transfer learning, meta-learning, prototype learning) that suffer from catastrophic forgetting, and from VLM-based zero-shot detectors (Grounding DINO, Qwen2.5-VL) whose large-scale visual pretraining is incompatible with standard ZS/FSOD protocols. The pseudo-incremental learning schedule—where the Knowledge Base is maintained from unlabeled streams via crop-then-oversegment at training and full-image over-segmentation at inference—unifies ZSOD and FSOD within one architecture, a framing absent from prior work that typically treats the two settings separately.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of zero-shot and few-shot detection under a single execution loop, with a lightweight LoRA-based training recipe that updates only the Judge's encoder and detection head, offers a practical path for deploying detectors that must generalize to genuinely novel categories without retraining the full model. However, evaluation is limited to PASCAL VOC (20 classes) and COCO (80 classes), leaving open whether the KB-based retrieval and multi-agent coordination scale to open-vocabulary or long-tail settings with hundreds or thousands of categories. The computational overhead of the four-agent pipeline at inference (CLIP scoring, DINOv2 retrieval, LLM generation with self-refinement) is not quantified, and the modest margins over strong baselines (e.g., +3.3 mAP over ICPE on VOC 10-shot) suggest the gains may narrow on harder benchmarks.
