## Problem

CLIP-based audio-visual localization methods like ACL-SSL replace the [CLS] token with an audio-embedded token [V_A] inside a fixed prompt "a photo of a [V_A]", but this static prompt has no semantic connection to the audio token and fails to establish meaningful cross-modal alignment. The result is degraded generalization, particularly on unseen objects and open-set categories, because the handcrafted context tokens cannot adapt to the specific audio-visual input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262138/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 10-second audio clip sampled at 16 kHz and a single central video frame resized to 352×352 pixels as input, producing pixel-level segmentation masks that highlight sounding objects. Notably, the pipeline is entirely textless and label-free: no text queries or ground-truth class annotations are required at inference or training time. The frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder, BEATs audio encoder, and CLIPSeg mask decoder are used as fixed backbones, with only ~2.38M trainable parameters (under 1% of the ~242M total model capacity).

## Method

SOUPLE replaces the fixed prompt "a photo of a [V_A]" with a sequence of M learnable context tokens [V_1]…[V_M] followed by the audio-embedded token [V_A]. A Meta-net (two-layer Linear-ReLU-Linear with a 16× bottleneck) maps the CLIP image features into these context tokens, making them instance-conditional on the visual input. The audio signal is converted into [V_A] via an Audio Tokenizer (MLP + attention pooling), and the concatenated token sequence is fed through the frozen CLIP text encoder to produce audio-text features that drive the CLIPSeg mask decoder. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive objective computed at both the image level and the spatial-feature level, plus an area regularization loss, with all CLIP encoders frozen. Ablations show that placing [V_A] at the end of the prompt (after the context tokens) is optimal, because CLIP's causal left-to-right attention lets the transformer layers process instance-conditional visual cues first, priming the semantic space before the audio-driven query aggregates richer context.

## Results

On VGG-SS, SOUPLE achieves 53.21 cIoU and 48.15 AUC (without OGL), surpassing the closest CLIP-based baseline ACL-SSL (49.46 cIoU, 46.32 AUC) by +3.75 cIoU and +1.83 AUC. On SoundNet-Flickr, SOUPLE reaches 84.80 cIoU and 67.64 AUC, improving over ACL-SSL by +4.00 cIoU and +3.02 AUC. In the open-set evaluation (110 unseen categories), SOUPLE attains 48.40 cIoU and 46.24 AUC, outperforming ACL-SSL by +6.42 cIoU and +4.69 AUC. On AVSBench S4 (single-source segmentation), SOUPLE achieves 62.89 mIoU and 71.47 F-Score, beating ACL-SSL by +3.13 mIoU and +2.44 F-Score, though it trails ACL-SSL on the multi-source MS3 subset (38.96 vs. 41.08 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SOUPLE outperforms all 11 compared methods on both VGG-SS and SoundNet-Flickr without using object-guided localization (OGL), a post-processing step that several competitors rely on. Against the closest baseline ACL-SSL (the CLIP-based method SOUPLE builds upon), SOUPLE gains +3.75 cIoU on VGG-SS and +4.00 cIoU on SoundNet-Flickr, with the gap widening to +6.42 cIoU in the open-set (unseen-category) setting. Compared to non-CLIP methods, SOUPLE leads FNAC (w/ OGL) by +1.36 cIoU on VGG-SS and Alignment (w/ OGL) by +0.57 cIoU, while surpassing MarginNCE (w/ OGL) by +3.43 cIoU. The CLIP representation baselines WAV2CLIP and AudioCLIP trail SOUPLE by a wide margin (15.5 and 9.1 cIoU on VGG-SS, respectively), confirming that the prompt-learning reformulation—rather than merely using CLIP features—is the key driver of improvement. The only setting where SOUPLE underperforms is AVSBench MS3 (multi-source), where the absence of class-label supervision causes it to segment multiple plausible sounding regions, yielding 38.96 mIoU versus ACL-SSL's 41.08.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOUPLE extends ACL-SSL (Park et al., WACV 2024), the first CLIP-based textless framework for audio-visual sound source localization, by replacing its fixed handcrafted prompt ('a photo of a [V_A]') with instance-conditional learnable context tokens generated from visual features via a meta-net. The meta-net architecture and conditioning mechanism are directly inherited from the CoCoOp prompt learning lineage (Zhou et al., CVPR 2022), which conditions prompts on image features to improve generalization to unseen categories. The paper also distinguishes itself from GAVS, which applies prompt learning to the segmentation decoder, by performing soft prompt learning within the CLIP text-encoder pathway of a grounding pipeline, making the contribution a cross-paradigm adaptation rather than a direct comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent 3–6 cIoU gains over ACL-SSL across five benchmarks, particularly the 6+ cIoU improvement on unseen (Unheard) categories, demonstrate that instance-conditional prompts meaningfully improve open-set generalization in audio-visual grounding without any class labels or text supervision. A notable limitation is the underperformance on multi-source (MS3) scenarios, where the absence of class-specific supervision causes the model to segment multiple plausible regions, highlighting an open question about how to handle multi-source disambiguation in a fully label-free setting. The finding that only four context tokens are optimal (more degrades performance) and that [V_A] must be placed at the end of the prompt for causal-attention priming provide practical design guidance for future prompt-based audio-visual methods.
