## Problem

Leveraging pretrained image diffusion priors for PBR texture generation is hindered by two issues: (1) naively feeding roughness and metallic channels into a frozen RGB-trained VAE encoder produces out-of-distribution latents that degrade downstream diffusion fine-tuning, and (2) correspondence-aware attention for multi-view consistency fails in pixel space unless the latent-to-image mapping preserves spatial locality, which breaks when the encoder is adapted to new channels. Large-scale PBR datasets remain orders of magnitude smaller than image datasets, making effective prior transfer essential.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh and a text prompt as input. During generation, six canonical views (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) are rendered and encoded by MATVAE into a single latent code per view; the diffusion model generates multi-view PBR latents that are decoded into five-channel material images (3-channel albedo, 1-channel roughness, 1-channel metallic) and projected onto the mesh surface. Training uses 26 rendered views per mesh from Objaverse-XL (40,723 assets) with captions from Bootstrap3D and Cap3D; evaluation uses 128 held-out meshes with 20 camera views and randomly sampled HDR environment maps for shaded rendering.

## Method

MATLAT is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (MATVAE) fine-tunes the pretrained VAE encoder via residual prediction: a frozen pretrained encoder encodes albedo into a base latent, while a learnable residual encoder (zero-initialized) predicts additive mean and multiplicative std adjustments that inject roughness/metallic information, keeping the latent dimensionality unchanged. A KL regularizer constrains the learned latent distribution to stay close to the pretrained one, and a locality regularizer enforces spatial alignment by cropping latent patches, decoding them, and matching against the corresponding image regions with an ℓ₂ loss. Stage 2 fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion 3.5-Medium velocity model on multi-view PBR latents using Conditional Flow Matching, with correspondence-aware attention (CAA) modules inserted into attention blocks to restrict cross-view information exchange to geometrically corresponding tokens. The synergy between CAA and locality regularization is critical: CAA provides geometrically valid attention targets, while locality regularization ensures those targets correspond to the correct pixel regions in the decoded output.

## Results

On 128 held-out meshes from Objaverse-XL, MATLAT achieves a shaded FID of 3.083 and albedo FID of 4.599, substantially outperforming the best SDS-based method DreamMat (5.422 / 9.621) and the best multi-view baseline MaterialMVP (6.309 / 9.630). MATLAT also attains the lowest shaded KID (1.327) and albedo KID (1.574) among all baselines, and generates textures in 34 seconds—comparable to MaterialMVP (35 s) but 70× faster than DreamMat (2400 s). The only marginal losses are albedo CLIP score (0.314 vs. MaterialAnything's 0.317) and metallic RMSE (0.134 vs. MaterialMVP's 0.133).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATLAT dominates all seven baselines on the primary fidelity metrics: it reduces shaded FID by 43% over the best SDS method DreamMat (3.083 vs. 5.422) and by 51% over the closest multi-view competitor MaterialMVP (3.083 vs. 6.309), with similar margins on albedo FID (4.599 vs. 9.621 / 9.630). Among scratch-trained models, MeshGen and TexGaussian lag far behind on both FID and KID, confirming that limited PBR supervision constrains quality. SDS-based methods (Paint-it, DreamMat, FlashTex) improve over scratch models but still trail MATLAT by 2–4 FID points on shaded images and incur 4×–70× longer runtimes. The closest multi-view competitor, MaterialMVP, is competitive on metallic RMSE (0.133 vs. 0.134) and runtime (35 s vs. 34 s) but is outperformed on every FID/KID metric. MaterialAnything achieves a marginally higher albedo CLIP score (0.317 vs. 0.314) but is substantially worse on all other metrics and 15× slower (500 s). Overall, MATLAT sets a new state-of-the-art on the majority of quantitative metrics while maintaining the fastest inference among multi-view methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATLAT extends the multi-view PBR texture generation line (MaterialMVP, MaterialAnything, MeshGen) by replacing the widely-used Frozen VAE approach—where roughness and metallic channels are zero-padded and fed to a frozen RGB encoder—with a fine-tuned Material VAE (MATVAE) that uses stochastic residual prediction and KL regularization to keep the adapted latent distribution close to the pretrained one. This draws on latent-space adaptation ideas from LayerDiffuse (transparency) and Orchid (depth/normals), but departs from both by predicting full Gaussian parameters (mean and std) rather than a deterministic mean or direct full-latent output, and by adding a locality regularization that has no direct analogue in those works. The multi-view consistency mechanism builds on correspondence-aware attention from MVDiffusion, but the paper identifies and addresses a previously unacknowledged prerequisite: that CAA only works when the latent-to-image mapping preserves spatial locality, which breaks after VAE fine-tuning for new channels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PBR texture generation remains bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale material datasets, making effective reuse of pretrained image diffusion priors essential; MATLAT's MATVAE provides a principled recipe for extending any pretrained VAE to new material channels while preserving the diffusion prior, a technique likely transferable to other multi-channel modalities (e.g., clearcoat, sheen, subsurface). The finding that CAA without locality enforcement actually degrades multi-view consistency (c-PSNR drops from 21.9 to 19.4 in ablation) is a practical warning for anyone combining geometric attention with fine-tuned encoders. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex PBR models beyond albedo/roughness/metallic, whether the locality regularization interacts well with higher-resolution or larger-scale diffusion backbones, and whether the single-latent formulation (vs. separate albedo and material latents) remains advantageous as the number of material channels grows.
