## Problem

Full-body motion estimation from sparse VR observations (HMD + two hand controllers) is fundamentally under-constrained, producing a severe one-to-many ambiguity where infinitely many kinematically plausible poses explain the same head-hand trajectories. Existing approaches either collapse this ambiguity via deterministic regression, rely on generative priors without calibrated per-joint uncertainty, or require additional sensors, leaving the core challenge of principled uncertainty quantification on the rotation manifold largely unresolved.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 45-dimensional per-frame vector aggregating positions, linear velocities, and 3×3 rotation matrices of the HMD and both hand controllers, concatenated with autoregressive pose history (198-dim SMPL rotations from prior frames) fed through a causal Transformer. The output is a full-body SMPL pose of 22 joints (198-dim) represented not as a single rotation but as a per-joint Matrix-Fisher distribution on SO(3), parameterized by a 3×3 Fisher matrix whose SVD yields a mode rotation and a concentration vector that geometrically encodes directional uncertainty for each joint.

## Method

FisherPoser combines three components: (1) a causal Transformer encoder that fuses sparse VR observations with pose history into a global context vector; (2) a region-wise token module that partitions the body into five semantic regions (torso, L/R arms, L/R legs), constructs region-specific queries from semantic VR anchors (head-centric relative transforms for arms, HMD forward-direction priors for legs) and cross-attends over local joint history features to produce compact tokens driving per-region Fisher regression; and (3) a hierarchical limb refinement stage that recursively propagates each parent joint's Fisher parameters and concentration to its children along kinematic chains, blending direct and propagated predictions with a learned mixing weight. Training uses the Matrix-Fisher negative log-likelihood on SO(3) plus a geodesic mode alignment loss, with a learnable data-dependent scalar stabilizing the wide dynamic range of Fisher singular values during optimization.

## Results

On Protocol 1 (CMU/BMLrub/HDM05 test split), FisherPoser achieves the best MPJRE of 2.04° and MPJPE of 29.7 mm, outperforming the previous best HMDPoser (2.28°, 31.9 mm) by 10.5% and 6.9% respectively. On the harder Protocol 2 (Transitions/HumanEva), it reaches 3.89° MPJRE and 53.4 mm MPJPE, while also achieving the lowest Jitter of 3.18 (vs. 5.62 for HMDPoser). The method trades a modest MPJVE increase (205.2 mm/s vs. RPM-Reactive's 174.1) for substantially better spatial accuracy, and achieves the best Jitter on Protocol 2 (3.18 vs. 4.99 for RPM-Reactive).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FisherPoser outperforms all six baselines on MPJRE and MPJPE across both protocols. Against the strongest prior method HMDPoser, it improves MPJRE by 10.5% (2.04° vs. 2.28°) and MPJPE by 6.9% (29.7 vs. 31.9 mm) on Protocol 1, and by 8.9% and 1.8% on Protocol 2. The main trade-off is with RPM-Reactive, which achieves lower MPJVE (174.1 vs. 205.2 mm/s) and lower Jitter on Protocol 1 (4.21 vs. 5.33) but at a severe accuracy cost: 37.2% worse MPJRE and 21.8% worse MPJPE. On Protocol 2, FisherPoser reverses this trade-off, achieving both the best accuracy and the best Jitter (3.18 vs. 4.99). AGRoL (diffusion-based) remains competitive on MPJVE (190.8 mm/s on P1) but lags on spatial accuracy (2.70° vs. 2.04° MPJRE). AvatarPoser and AvatarJLM, the earlier deterministic/autoregressive baselines, are consistently outperformed on all four metrics by 10–30%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Matrix-Fisher distribution framework from single-rotation regression (Mohlin et al., NeurIPS 2020) to the multi-joint full-body pose estimation setting, and builds on the probabilistic inertial poser paradigm (Kim et al., ICCV 2025) that models per-joint uncertainty from sparse sensors. It departs from the dominant deterministic regression (AvatarPoser, HMDPoser) and generative sampling (AGRoL, SAGE) approaches by formulating pose prediction as maximum-likelihood inference on SO(3), and introduces two architectural contributions absent in prior work: region-wise attention-pooled tokens that match model capacity to heterogeneous observability, and a hierarchical parent-to-child Fisher parameter propagation that enforces kinematic chain dependencies. The causal Transformer temporal encoder follows the autoregressive pattern established by AvatarPoser and HMDPoser, but the probabilistic output head and refinement decoder represent a distinct methodological lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibrated per-joint uncertainty is practically important for VR applications where downstream systems (avatar rendering, physics interaction, collision detection) need to know when the lower-body estimate is unreliable rather than silently committing to a potentially wrong pose. The finding that a naive Gaussian uncertainty head actively degrades temporal stability (jitter nearly triples) compared to the deterministic baseline, while the MF head restores smoothness, provides a concrete argument for manifold-aware uncertainty in rotation estimation. Open questions include whether the autoregressive architecture suffers drift in long-horizon real-world sessions, whether the approach extends to object interaction or terrain-aware estimation, and whether the region-wise + hierarchical decomposition generalizes to other sparse-sensor configurations beyond HMD-plus-controllers.
