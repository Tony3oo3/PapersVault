## Problem

Personalized text-to-image generation requires disentangling the target concept from irrelevant residual information in reference images, but existing methods rely on manual guidance (linguistic cues, segmentation masks, auxiliary image sets) that often fails to precisely capture all visual details and limits scalability. Without such guidance, the reconstruction objective forces the target token to absorb both the desired concept and undesired attributes, degrading the trade-off between concept fidelity and text alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261235/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a small set of 4–6 reference images at 512×512 resolution and, at inference, a free-form text prompt containing the learned target token. Unlike baselines, it does not require a class noun to identify the target concept. The residual tokens are initialized from VLM-generated scene descriptions (8–32 words) encoded via CLIP, and the final output is a set of personalized images generated with DDIM (25 steps, CFG scale 7.5) using the optimized tokens and LoRA-adapted cross-attention layers.

## Method

ConceptPrism introduces a two-stage pipeline. In the token optimization stage, a single target token and N image-wise residual tokens are jointly trained with two complementary objectives: a reconstruction loss that ensures the target–residual pair faithfully represents each reference image, and an exclusion loss that penalizes the residual token for being informative about the target concept by matching its noise prediction to the unconditional prediction on *other* images in the set (j ≠ i). This cross-image exclusion creates an information vacuum that forces the target token to absorb the shared concept without any external supervision. In the subsequent fine-tuning stage, the learned tokens condition LoRA-adapted cross-attention layers for 120 steps, allowing the model to specialize on the disentangled concept while the residual tokens prevent re-entanglement of image-specific details.

## Results

On DreamBench, ConceptPrism achieves the highest concept fidelity scores (DINO 0.210, CLIP-I 0.353) while maintaining competitive text alignment (CLIP-T 0.357, tied with Custom Diffusion). The DINO score of 0.210 represents a 15.4% relative improvement over the next-best method (DreamBooth-LoRA at 0.182), while CLIP-I surpasses all baselines by at least 1.2 points. Training time is 477 s, comparable to DreamBooth-LoRA (434 s) and Custom Diffusion (460 s), and roughly 2× faster than DisenBooth (929 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConceptPrism outperforms all six baselines on concept fidelity (DINO and CLIP-I) while matching the best text alignment. Against DreamBooth-LoRA, it gains +0.028 DINO and +0.022 CLIP-I while recovering +0.044 CLIP-T, directly resolving the fidelity–alignment trade-off that causes DreamBooth to overfit reference compositions. Compared to Custom Diffusion (the closest CLIP-T competitor at 0.357), ConceptPrism improves DINO by +0.032 and CLIP-I by +0.012, showing that linguistic-cue-based disentanglement cannot capture the same visual detail. Against the disentanglement-focused baselines DisenBooth and DisEnvisioner, ConceptPrism leads by +0.040 and +0.039 DINO respectively, while also surpassing them on CLIP-T by +0.002 and +0.048. The tuning-free encoder-based methods ELITE and DisEnvisioner trail on all three metrics, confirming the limited precision of encoder-only approaches. In terms of efficiency, ConceptPrism's 477 s training time is comparable to DreamBooth and Custom Diffusion but roughly 2× faster than DisenBooth (929 s), with the 200-step token optimization stage reducing fine-tuning from 400 steps (DreamBooth) to 120 steps.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConceptPrism sits within the optimization-based personalized T2I generation lineage (DreamBooth, Custom Diffusion, Textual Inversion), adopting the same LoRA-based fine-tuning of cross-attention layers. It departs from prior disentanglement methods (DisenBooth, DisEnvisioner, ELITE) that rely on manual guidance—linguistic cues, segmentation masks, or auxiliary image sets—by instead using a cross-image exclusion loss to automatically isolate shared features. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of image-wise residual tokens paired with a KL-divergence-based exclusion loss that creates an 'information vacuum,' forcing the target token to absorb only the common concept without any external supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exclusion loss offers a lightweight, guidance-free mechanism for resolving the fidelity-alignment trade-off that plagues personalized generation, and its simplicity (a single additional loss term on cross-image latents) makes it easy to integrate into existing pipelines. However, the method still requires a reference set of 4–6 images and can capture unintended commonalities if all references share spurious features, though a single counterexample mitigates this. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-concept or video personalization, and whether the token-optimization stage generalizes cleanly to DiT-based backbones beyond the Stable Diffusion v2.1 experiments shown.
