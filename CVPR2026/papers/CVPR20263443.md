## Problem

Blind deconvolution requires jointly estimating the sharp image and the unknown blur kernel from a single degraded observation, but existing approaches suffer from severe convergence instability, kernel drift, and pronounced sensitivity to hyperparameters such as kernel size. Pretrained generative priors (diffusion models, GANs) cannot estimate the kernel, while training-free implicit-prior methods like SelfDeblur collapse to trivial solutions when the kernel size is mismatched. The paper addresses this by asking whether the noise-regulated spectral bias inherent in self-diffusion can be extended to stabilize the otherwise ill-posed joint image-kernel optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB blurry image (e.g., 255×255 to 1006×665 depending on dataset) degraded by either a Zernike-modeled optical aberration PSF or a motion-blur kernel, with no additional calibration data, reference images, or known kernel size required. It produces two outputs: the reconstructed sharp image at the same spatial resolution and a 2D blur kernel (PSF) of size K×K, where K can range from 15 to 33 without re-tuning. The entire pipeline is zero-shot and training-free—both networks are randomly initialized per image and optimized only against the single observation.

## Method

DeblurSDI extends the self-diffusion framework to the blind setting by introducing two randomly initialized, jointly optimized networks: a U-Net image denoiser D_θ (five encoder-decoder levels with NonLocal blocks) and a fully-connected PSF generator G_φ (multi-layer ReLU network with softmax output enforcing non-negativity and unit sum). Starting from pure Gaussian noise for both the image estimate x_T and the PSF latent z_T, the method runs T=30 outer reverse-diffusion steps; at each step, scheduled noise (σ_t for the image, μσ_t for the PSF) is injected, and S=200 inner Adam iterations minimize a data-fidelity loss ‖D_θ(x̂_t) * G_φ(ẑ_t) − y‖² plus an L1 sparsity penalty on the generated kernel. The key insight is that the hierarchical noise schedule acts as an implicit regularizer that enforces a coarse-to-fine reconstruction order, preventing the joint optimization from collapsing to Dirac kernels or reproducing the blurred input. The PSF generator's ReLU layers and separate (scaled) noise schedule further stabilize kernel estimation, which is the primary source of instability in blind deconvolution.

## Results

On the four benchmark datasets for blind motion deblurring, DeblurSDI achieves PSNR/SSIM of 31.85/0.7911 (Levin), 28.73/0.8859 (Cho), 29.17/0.7653 (Köhler), and 33.90/0.9064 (FFHQ), consistently outperforming all four baselines on PSNR across every dataset. The largest margins appear on Levin (+6.8 dB over the next-best SelfDeblur) and FFHQ (+8.1 dB over Phase-Only). For optical aberration correction (Table 4), DeblurSDI similarly leads on PSNR in all six aberration scenarios, reaching up to 33.00/0.9343 on FFHQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeblurSDI wins on PSNR against all four baselines on every dataset, with margins ranging from +0.9 dB (Köhler vs. Phase-Only) to +18.3 dB (FFHQ vs. FastDiffusionEM). The closest competitor is SelfDeblur [32], which is the strongest baseline on Levin (25.06 PSNR) but still trails DeblurSDI by 6.8 dB; on Cho and FFHQ, SelfDeblur is actually the weakest of the four baselines. Phase-Only [29] is competitive on Köhler (28.23 PSNR, 0.8092 SSIM) and even edges out DeblurSDI on SSIM there (0.8092 vs. 0.7653), but loses on PSNR and is far behind on the other three datasets. FastDiffusionEM [21] performs worst overall, with kernels degenerating into trivial point- or line-like structures, and FFT-ReLU Deblur [2] shows high variance across datasets. The paper also highlights that DeblurSDI is robust to kernel-size choice (tested 15–33), whereas SelfDeblur requires per-image kernel-size selection and still suffers from reconstruction shifting and color distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeblurSDI extends the self-diffusion framework of Luo et al. [24], originally designed for non-blind linear inverse problems with a known forward operator, to the blind setting where the degradation kernel is also unknown. It departs from the classical alternating-optimization lineage (Fergus et al., Levin et al.) and from implicit-prior methods like SelfDeblur [32] by replacing handcrafted or learned priors with a noise-regulated spectral-bias mechanism that simultaneously stabilizes both image and PSF estimation. Unlike pretrained diffusion solvers (DPS, FastDiffusionEM) that rely on large-scale training and cannot estimate the kernel, DeblurSDI is entirely training-free, using two randomly initialized networks (a U-Net denoiser and an FCN kernel generator) optimized within a shared reverse-diffusion loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing practical pain point in blind deconvolution: the extreme sensitivity of joint image-kernel optimization to initialization, kernel size, and hyperparameters, which often causes collapse to trivial solutions. By showing that a hierarchical noise schedule can regularize both variables simultaneously, DeblurSDI offers a calibration-free, training-free alternative that could simplify deployment in consumer imaging pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to 128 synthetic image-kernel pairs across four small datasets with no real-world validation, and the method's longer runtime (30 outer steps × 200 inner iterations) may limit practical adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-uniform or spatially varying blur, whether the kernel-size robustness claim holds at much larger scales, and whether the noise schedule can be adapted for other blind inverse problems (e.g., blind super-resolution).
