## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods predict pixel-aligned primitives per view, producing an excessive and uncontrollable number of Gaussians that scales linearly with view count and resolution. This makes them impractical for resource-constrained end-user devices and prevents predictable latency, memory, and bandwidth in real-time or streamable rendering pipelines, since no mechanism exists to enforce a prescribed primitive count.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260130/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-view RGB images (256×256) and a user-specified target primitive count K as input, and outputs exactly K 3D Gaussian primitives (3D centers, opacities, 3D covariances, and colors) that can be rasterized to render novel views. At inference, K is not split uniformly across views; instead, a DFT-based high-frequency score allocates more primitives to views with richer scene detail, ensuring the total across all views sums to K while maximizing rendering quality.

## Method

EcoSplat uses a two-stage training process. Stage 1 (PGT) trains a ViT encoder–decoder with cross-attention to predict pixel-aligned Gaussians per view, supervised by MSE+LPIPS rendering loss. Stage 2 (IGF) freezes the backbone and finetunes only the Gaussian parameter head, injecting a learnable importance embedding derived from the preservation ratio ρ=K/(NHW) so the model adaptively suppresses opacities of less important primitives. A pseudo-GT importance mask is constructed from photometric and geometric variation maps (with K-means clustering in low-variation regions), and an importance-aware opacity loss (BCE) drives the model to match it. A Progressive Learning on Gaussian Compaction (PLGC) schedule gradually anneals the minimum sampled K from 85% to 5% of total pixels for stable training. At inference, the top-K Gaussians by predicted opacity are retained, with per-view allocation weighted by view importance.

## Results

On RE10K with 24 input views, EcoSplat achieves PSNR 24.72 / SSIM 0.822 / LPIPS 0.183 at just 5% of pixel-aligned primitives (78K Gaussians), and PSNR 25.11 / SSIM 0.835 / LPIPS 0.164 at 40% (629K Gaussians). At 40% primitives, EcoSplat matches or exceeds the full-budget SPFSplat (24.74 PSNR at 1573K Gaussians) while using 2.5× fewer primitives. On the cross-domain ACID dataset, EcoSplat at 40% achieves 24.02 PSNR with 629K Gaussians, trailing SPFSplat (24.40 PSNR, 1573K Gaussians) by only 0.38 dB while using 60% fewer primitives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EcoSplat dominates all baselines by a wide margin across every primitive-count setting. At 5% primitives, EcoSplat (24.72 PSNR) outperforms the next-best method, AnySplat (8.08 PSNR), by 16.6 dB, while GGN cannot operate below 15% at all. At 40% primitives, EcoSplat (25.11 PSNR) exceeds AnySplat/WorldMirror (19.66 PSNR) by 5.5 dB and SPFSplat+PUP 3D-GS (12.01 PSNR) by 13.1 dB. In the multi-view comparison (Table 2), EcoSplat at 40% (25.11–25.27 PSNR, 419–629K Gaussians) matches or surpasses full-budget SPFSplat (24.74–25.09 PSNR, 1048–1573K Gaussians) with 2.5–3× fewer primitives, and beats GGN (15.80–15.82 PSNR) by ~9 dB while using 7× fewer Gaussians. On cross-domain ACID (Table 3), EcoSplat at 40% (24.02 PSNR) trails SPFSplat (24.40 PSNR) by 0.38 dB but uses only 40% of the primitives; it outperforms all efficient baselines (AnySplat 21.96, WorldMirror 22.34, GGN 18.06) by 1.7–6.0 dB. Post-pruning cascades (MVSplat/SPFSplat + LightGaussian or PUP 3D-GS) degrade catastrophically under aggressive pruning (5–10%), dropping to 5.8–8.2 PSNR, confirming that per-pixel pruning is fundamentally incompatible with pixel-aligned feed-forward representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

EcoSplat extends the feed-forward 3DGS line (MVSplat, SPFSplat, NoPoSplat, DepthSplat) that predicts pixel-aligned Gaussians from multi-view images, and the efficient feed-forward methods (GGN, AnySplat, WorldMirror) that attempt to reduce redundancy via voxelization or graph-based aggregation. It departs from both by introducing explicit, user-specified control over the output primitive count through a two-stage training process: a standard pixel-aligned Gaussian Training stage followed by an Importance-aware Gaussian Finetuning stage that learns to rank and suppress less important primitives via an importance-aware opacity loss and progressive compaction scheduling. The view-importance allocation at inference borrows the DFT-based high-frequency scoring from MoBGS to distribute the primitive budget non-uniformly across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first feed-forward 3DGS framework that guarantees a user-specified primitive count at inference, which is critical for deployment on resource-constrained devices (mobile, AR/VR headsets) where memory and bandwidth budgets are fixed. The enormous performance gap over threshold-based baselines at aggressive compression (e.g., ~17 dB PSNR at 5% primitives) demonstrates that learned importance ranking fundamentally outperforms heuristic pruning for pixel-aligned representations. Limitations include evaluation only at 256×256 resolution and dense-view settings (16–24 views); open questions include scalability to very large or sparse-view scenes, dynamic content, and whether the importance ranking generalizes to downstream tasks like editing or relighting.
