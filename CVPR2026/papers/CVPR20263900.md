## Problem

Machine unlearning for parametric deep networks is difficult because individual training sample contributions are implicitly encoded in model weights, making it nontrivial to remove specific samples without costly retraining or approximate fine-tuning. While non-parametric models like KNN allow trivial unlearning by sample removal, they lack competitive task performance. The paper asks whether one can design neural network architectures that are inherently easy to unlearn while maintaining strong predictive or generative quality.

## Input / Output

At test time, the SPM receives both the query (an image for classification or a class label for generation) and an explicit inputted set of training samples, which is the key departure from standard parametric inference. The inputted set can be the full training data or a reduced subset obtained via nearest-neighbor retrieval or class-level clustering, keeping the set size constant for efficiency. Unlearning is performed by simply removing the target samples from this inputted set before the forward pass, with no modification to learned parameters.

## Method

The proposed SPM uses a two-branch architecture with three module types: a parametric module f (standard deep-net layers, e.g., ResNet or UNet blocks), a non-parametric module h (permutation-equivariant, e.g., shared instance-wise transformations that encode each training sample into an instance embedding), and a fusion module g (attention-like weighted combination that connects the two branches). The fusion explicitly excludes the query sample's own embedding to force the model to learn features relative to the rest of the dataset, and a label-permutation augmentation during training prevents the model from collapsing the non-parametric branch into a parametric bias. For classification, cross-attention over training-set embeddings produces the final class prediction; for generation, patch-level Bahdanau attention fuses instance embeddings into the UNet mid-block of a diffusion model. Unlearning reduces to deleting samples from the inputted set at test time, achieving sub-second unlearning time while closely matching a fully retrained oracle.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 classification, SPM-C (clustering, 100% training set) achieves 94.5% top-1 accuracy, matching ResNet18 (94.9%) while running in 0.7s vs. 26.1s on ImageNet. For unlearning, SPM achieves the lowest soft prediction gap (PG_S = 0.27 on CIFAR-10, 0.12 on ImageNet) and near-zero ΔUA/ΔRA, closely matching the retrained oracle. Critically, SPM's unlearning time is <1s (test-time deletion) compared to 8.9–369.1s for parametric MU methods on CIFAR-10 and 24.8–225,519s on ImageNet, yielding over 10× speedup. On CIFAR-10 generation, SPM with |T|=50 achieves FID 7.17, slightly beating DDPM (7.28), and unlearning via deletion achieves FID_0 ≈ 1.9 vs. 6.1–477.6 for gradient-based baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPM outperforms all eight parametric MU algorithms (GA, FT, IU, BE, BS, ℓ₁-sparse, SalUn, MUNBa) on every unlearning metric: it achieves the lowest PG_S (0.27 vs. 0.32–3.51), near-zero ΔUA and ΔRA, and the smallest ΔTA (0.52 vs. 0.14–50.70), while completing unlearning in <1s versus 8.9–369.1s. Against the non-parametric KNN baseline, SPM achieves a lower PG_S (0.27 vs. 0.55) and ΔTA (0.52 vs. 1.77), confirming that the parametric backbone adds meaningful predictive power over pure nearest-neighbor lookup. On ImageNet, the advantage is even more pronounced: SPM's PG_S of 0.12 and ΔTA of 0.00 dwarf the best parametric method (SalUn: PG_S 0.42, ΔTA 0.39), and the unlearning time gap widens to <1s vs. up to 225,519s. For generation, SPM with |T|=50 achieves FID 7.17, marginally beating DDPM (7.28), while GMM (FID 198.42) fails to capture the data distribution. In generation unlearning, SPM's deletion achieves FID_0 ≈ 1.9 and ΔUA < 0.1 across all five tested classes, substantially outperforming SA (FID_0 31–477), SalUn (FID_0 3.6–6.9), and SFR-on (FID_0 25.7–91.5), with unlearning time <1s vs. 23.8–18,711s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant algorithm-centric framing of machine unlearning (gradient ascent, influence functions, boundary shifting, fine-tuning) by asking a design question instead: can architectures be built that are inherently easy to unlearn? It repurposes the semi-parametric modeling tradition (kernel methods, deep kernel learning, NPT) and the trivial unlearning property of non-parametric models like KNN, wrapping them in a two-branch deep architecture with attention-based fusion modules. The generative SPM builds directly on the DDPM/UNet pipeline, replacing the mid-block with a patch-level Bahdanau-attention fusion over training-set instance embeddings, while the classification SPM adapts ResNet18 with a cross-attention head over clustered or retrieved training features. A novel label-permutation augmentation is introduced to prevent the model from collapsing the non-parametric branch into a parametric bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core contribution is a paradigm shift: unlearning becomes a test-time indexing operation (<1 s) rather than an iterative parameter-update procedure (minutes to hours), while maintaining near-oracle accuracy gaps. However, the approach fundamentally requires the training set to be available at inference time, which is a significant practical constraint that limits applicability to settings where data retention is already assumed. Open questions include scalability to very large or streaming training sets, whether the non-parametric dependency itself introduces new privacy or memorization risks, and whether the design principles transfer to other modalities (text, audio) or tasks (detection, segmentation). The ~2% accuracy gap on ImageNet (67.1% vs 68.93%) also suggests the performance cost of the semi-parametric design is non-trivial at scale.
