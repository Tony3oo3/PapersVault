## Problem

Current physically plausible video generation methods collapse complex physical phenomena into a single static moment defined by a prompt, failing to model the causal progression of events over time. This stems from two gaps: causal ambiguity, where a semantic tag cannot capture the dynamic, multi-stage nature of physical processes, and insufficient physics-consistent constraints, where language alone cannot convey the continuous causal transitions between events.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262842/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a free-form text prompt describing a physical phenomenon (e.g., 'hydrochloric acid poured into purple litmus solution') and produces a 161-frame video at 1360×768 resolution using CogVideoX-5B as the base generator. The text prompt is consumed by an LLM (GPT-OSS20B) for multi-step reasoning rather than being fed directly to the diffusion model; the output video is conditioned by both a causally condensed semantic prompt and a sequence of VAE-encoded keyframe features that replace the initial Gaussian noise.

## Method

The framework consists of two modules. The Physics-driven Event Chain Reasoning (PECR) module decomposes the described phenomenon into an ordered sequence of elementary events by retrieving relevant physics formulas from a knowledge base, computing physical parameter vectors at each stage, and using a variation threshold to determine event boundaries; scene graphs are then updated per event to track object attributes and inter-object relations. The Transition-aware Cross-modal Prompting (TCP) module bridges these events to generation via dual conditions: a progressive narrative revision that merges event descriptions into a single causally connected semantic prompt, and an interactive keyframe synthesis that uses Qwen-Image-Edit to iteratively generate visually consistent keyframes (with editing operators and magnitudes bounded by physical parameter changes), which are linearly interpolated and VAE-encoded to serve as structured priors replacing random noise in the diffusion denoising process.

## Results

On PhyGenBench, the proposed framework achieves an average Physical Commonsense Alignment (PCA) score of 0.66 across four physical domains (Mechanics 0.67, Optics 0.72, Thermal 0.65, Material 0.60), surpassing the previous SOTA PhysHPO (0.61) by 8.19%. On VideoPhy, the method attains 49.3% joint Semantic Adherence and Physical Commonsense (SA=1, PC=1), outperforming the best physics-aware baseline (Vanilla DPO at 41.3%) by 8 percentage points. The largest gains appear in Optics (0.72) and Mechanics (0.67), while Material remains the weakest domain at 0.60.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper outperforms all 18 baselines on both benchmarks. On PhyGenBench, the closest competitor is PhysHPO (0.61 avg), which the method surpasses by 0.05; among video foundation models, Gen-3 (0.51) and Kling (0.49) lead, but both trail the proposed method by 0.15 and 0.17 respectively. DiffPhy (0.54) is the strongest standalone physics-aware baseline, yet still falls 0.12 short. On VideoPhy, the method achieves 49.3% joint SA+PC, beating the best physics-aware baseline Vanilla DPO (41.3%) by 8 points and the raw CogVideoX-5B backbone (39.6%) by nearly 10 points. Notably, the advantage is most pronounced in Solid-Fluid interactions (60.0% vs. 53.1% for CogVideoX-5B) and Solid-Solid interactions (40.6% vs. 24.4%), suggesting the event-chain decomposition particularly benefits scenarios with multi-step causal transitions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in physically plausible video generation (PPVG), where LLM-assisted prompt augmentation (PhyT2V, DiffPhy, PAG-SAD) treats a physical phenomenon as a single static moment described by a semantic tag. It repurposes chain-of-thought reasoning from the visual generation literature (LayerCraft, GoT) but redirects it toward deterministic causal decomposition grounded in retrieved physics formulas, rather than spatial or semantic reasoning. The dual-conditioning strategy (text + image) builds on reference-image video generation (STiV, TI2V-Zero), but introduces a key novelty: conditions are not fixed but evolve progressively across events via interactive image editing and linear interpolation, creating a temporally structured conditioning signal absent in prior dual-prompt approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underexplored gap—modeling causal *progression* rather than a single snapshot of physical phenomena—which is critical for applications like embodied AI and autonomous driving where temporal dynamics matter. The ablation revealing that interactive keyframe synthesis (IKS) contributes ~17% while progressive narrative revision (PNR) contributes only ~3% suggests that visual priors, not linguistic coherence, are the primary driver of physical plausibility in current diffusion models, a finding that could redirect future effort. Open questions include scalability to longer event chains (beyond the 4–6 events implied by 161 frames), coverage of phenomena lacking closed-form physics formulas, and whether the pipeline's multiple LLM and image-editing calls can be made more efficient for practical deployment.
