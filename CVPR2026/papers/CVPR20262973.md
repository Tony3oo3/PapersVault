## Problem

Prior work largely attributes LVLM object hallucination to insufficient visual attention, but this paper shows that real and hallucinated objects receive comparable attention magnitudes in the model's mid-to-late layers. The unresolved question is what actually distinguishes hallucinated from real objects, and whether a single mitigation strategy can address all hallucination causes. The paper identifies semantic inconsistency between attended visual regions and generated tokens as the true signal, and further reveals two distinct mechanisms—visual uncertainty and contextual prior—that demand different remedies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt (e.g., 'Describe this image') and produces a corrected image description. Internally, it operates on the LVLM's per-layer attention weights and hidden states during autoregressive generation, specifically within the Image-Attention Stage (layers 20–27 for LLaVA-1.5-7B). The detection stage outputs a binary hallucination label and a type classification (visual uncertainty vs. contextual prior) for each object token; the mitigation stage either replaces specific image patches with a constant value (HARM) or blends Logit-Lens-derived visual logits into the decoding distribution (VEED) before producing the final text.

## Method

The paper proposes a training-free Detect-Mitigate framework built on Logit Lens applied to image-token hidden states. For detection, the Logit-Lens Consistency Check (LLCC) first flags object tokens by their average image attention exceeding a threshold, then decodes the top-k attended image tokens via the model's output head and checks semantic consistency (via WordNet similarity) with the generated token. For classification, it masks the high-attention regions and regenerates: if the hallucination disappears it is visual uncertainty, otherwise it is contextual prior. Mitigation is type-specific: HARM simply masks the unreliable high-attention patches and regenerates, while VEED injects the Logit-Lens logits of the most-attended visual region into the decoding distribution as p_t = softmax((1−α)·W_U·h(ω_max) + α·logits_θ(y_t|y_<t, I^mask)), thereby restoring correct visual evidence that strong contextual priors had suppressed.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (500 COCO-2014 images), the proposed Detect-Mitigate framework achieves the lowest hallucination rates across all four LVLMs: CHAIR_S=26.8 / CHAIR_I=10.0 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, 31.3 / 12.4 on LLaVA-1.5-13B, 31.4 / 12.7 on Shikra-7B, and 24.0 / 8.3 on Qwen2-VL-7B. On AMBER, it attains the best CHAIR (2.8/4.0/5.3), Hal (14.7/24.1/30.3), and Cog (1.2/2.5/2.0) scores while maintaining or slightly improving object coverage (51.2/52.0/52.3). For hallucination detection, the Logit-Lens Consistency Check (LLCC) reaches F1=0.7932, substantially outperforming the best baseline SVAR (F1=0.6842).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all seven baselines on every model and benchmark. Against the strongest prior mitigation methods, it reduces CHAIR_I by 3.7 points over Devils (10.0 vs 13.7 on LLaVA-1.5-7B) and by 3.2 points over PAI (10.0 vs 13.2), with similar margins on the other three models. On AMBER, it achieves the lowest hallucination rate (CHAIR=2.8) while preserving coverage (51.2) that is comparable to or slightly above the original greedy baseline (51.0), whereas most baselines sacrifice coverage (e.g., OPERA drops to 47.3, DeCo to 46.4). For detection, LLCC's F1 of 0.7932 exceeds SVAR (0.6842), InterConf (0.6811), and Uncertainty Score (0.6182) by 11, 11, and 17 points respectively, attributed to its explicit semantic-consistency check on attended regions rather than relying on attention magnitude or cross-layer probability aggregation. Notably, DeCo and Devils do not report results on Qwen2-VL, so the comparison there is limited to PAI and OPERA, where the proposed method still leads by 0.7 and 4.0 CHAIR_S points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Logit Lens interpretability technique (nostalgebraist, 2020), repurposing it from LLM layer analysis to decode visual semantics from image-token hidden states in LVLMs. It explicitly departs from the dominant 'insufficient visual attention' narrative that underpins methods like VCD, OPERA, DeCo, Devils, and PAI, arguing instead that attention magnitude is not the differentiator between real and hallucinated objects. The two-type taxonomy (visual uncertainty vs. contextual prior) and the corresponding targeted remedies (HARM and VEED) represent a new causal decomposition that prior attention-based or contrastive-decoding methods do not provide, positioning the work as a diagnostic reframing rather than another attention-amplification strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that hallucinated and real objects receive comparable visual attention challenges a widely-held assumption in the LVLM community and redirects the research question from 'how much' to 'what and why.' The training-free, inference-time nature of the Detect-Mitigate framework makes it immediately deployable without retraining, and the cause-aware routing (mask vs. logit injection) avoids the one-size-fits-all trade-off that plagues prior methods. Limitations include reliance on WordNet for semantic similarity (which may miss novel or compound objects), the core attention analysis being demonstrated primarily on LLaVA-1.5-7B, and the masking remedy being somewhat crude for complex scenes. Open questions include whether the 2:1 visual-uncertainty-to-contextual-prior ratio generalizes across architectures and whether the framework extends to attribute or relational hallucination beyond object-level.
