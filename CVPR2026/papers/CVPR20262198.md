## Problem

Existing multi-subject video generation methods struggle to resolve the inherent tension between preserving the distinct identity of each subject and producing a temporally coherent, semantically aligned scene. Prior approaches that inject multi-subject information into pretrained video diffusion models suffer from identity leakage, semantic conflicts between subjects and prompts, and degraded identity fidelity over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262198/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt and N reference images (each depicting a unique subject) as input and produces a 480p subject-consistent video. The VLM encoder (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) jointly processes the text and all reference images to produce semantically enriched conditioning tokens, which are concatenated with per-subject visual feature tokens to form the full conditioning context. Inference uses an Euler sampler with 50 steps and classifier-free guidance (scale 2.5), taking approximately one minute per video on a single GPU.

## Method

ID-Crafter extends the Wan-Video-1.3B latent diffusion transformer (Rectified Flow) with three components. First, a hierarchical identity-preserving attention mechanism operates in three cascaded stages— intra-subject attention for fine-grained per-identity features, inter-subject attention to model cross-subject interactions and prevent identity leakage, and cross-modal attention to fuse subject features with text and video tokens. Second, a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) replaces the conventional text encoder, providing granular cross-modal semantic guidance that actively shapes the hierarchical attention rather than serving as a static encoder. Third, an online RL phase using GRPO-style groupwise advantage estimation (avoiding a fragile value network) optimizes a composite reward combining ArcFace-based facial fidelity, holistic subject consistency (NexusScore), aesthetic quality, and a VLM-based naturalness score to mitigate reward hacking. The model is trained for 30k iterations on a curated multi-source dataset (real subject-video pairs, synthetic cross-subject compositions, and professionally shot videos) at 480p on 16 H20 GPUs.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Nexus benchmark (180 subject-text pairs), ID-Crafter-1.3B (Base) achieves a Total Score of 54.33% with FaceSim of 58.12%, already surpassing all open-source baselines. After online RL fine-tuning, the 1.3B model reaches 55.16% Total Score and 66.10% FaceSim, outperforming the strongest proprietary system Kling 1.6 (54.46% Total, 40.10% FaceSim). The 14B variant achieves the best overall score of 57.05% with 60.71% FaceSim and 40.34% Motion quality, establishing new state-of-the-art across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ID-Crafter-1.3B (Base) at 54.33% Total Score already exceeds the best open-source baseline VACE-14B (52.87%) and Phantom-14B (52.32%) by 1.5–2.0 points, and matches the proprietary Kling 1.6 (54.46%). After online RL, the 1.3B model reaches 55.16% Total Score with a striking 66.10% FaceSim, surpassing Phantom-14B (51.48%) by 14.6 points and VACE-14B (55.09%) by 11.0 points on identity preservation. The 14B variant (57.05%) leads all methods on Total Score, NexusScore (45.11%), and NaturalScore (73.23%), while Kling 1.6 retains a slight edge on Motion (41.60% vs. 40.34%) and NaturalScore (79.06%). Notably, ID-Crafter-1.3B outperforms 14B-parameter competitors (Phantom-14B, VACE-14B, SkyReels-A2-P14B) on Total Score, demonstrating that the architectural innovations (hierarchical attention + VLM guidance) compensate for model scale. The largest margin over baselines appears on FaceSim, where the hierarchical identity-preserving attention and VLM semantic grounding directly target multi-subject identity disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

ID-Crafter builds directly on the Wan-Video latent diffusion transformer (rectified flow) as its generative backbone and adapts the Flow-GRPO online RL algorithm for post-training, extending it from single-subject or text-to-video settings to the multi-subject conditioning regime. Architecturally, it departs from prior subject-consistent video methods (Phantom, SkyReels-A2, VACE) that inject identity via simple cross-attention or adapter concatenation, instead proposing a three-level hierarchical attention (intra-subject → inter-subject → cross-modal) and repurposing a pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as a dynamic semantic guide rather than a static text encoder. The data curation pipeline also diverges from prior work by synthesizing cross-subject compositions with an image editing model to explicitly counter 'copy-paste' artifacts, a gap not addressed by existing subject-to-video datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Multi-subject video generation is a practical bottleneck for content creation, advertising, and virtual storytelling, and this paper provides a complete, open-source recipe (hierarchical attention + VLM grounding + online RL) that practitioners can adopt. The ablation revealing that removing the VLM encoder drops alignment by 18% and that omitting the naturalness reward triggers reward hacking (boosting FaceSim while degrading perceptual quality) offers useful design guidance for future reward-engineering in generative models. However, the authors acknowledge limitations in modeling complex inter-subject interactions and fine-grained dynamics, and the reliance on a 1.3B/14B DiT at 480p constrains real-world applicability; open questions include whether the online RL strategy scales to longer videos, more subjects, or higher resolutions without additional stability mechanisms.
