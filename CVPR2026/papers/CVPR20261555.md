## Problem

End-to-end text-image machine translation (TIMT) lacks comprehensive evaluation resources: existing benchmarks cover only a handful of languages and single-domain scenarios, leaving robustness across diverse visual scenes and low-resource languages unexplored. Additionally, effective reasoning-oriented data paradigms for TIMT remain immature—existing designs either cascade OCR and translation sequentially or apply language-only CoT that overlooks the visual cognition central to vision-language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261555/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image containing naturally occurring text in one of 14 non-English, non-Chinese languages (German, Spanish, Turkish, Vietnamese, Korean, Malay, Portuguese, Russian, French, Indonesian, Thai, Italian, Japanese) and produces translated text in both English and Chinese. Under the CPR-Trans paradigm, the output is further structured as a three-stage reasoning trace—scene cognition, text perception, and translation reasoning—enclosed in think tags, followed by the final translation in answer tags. The benchmark comprises 1,400 human-verified images averaging 160 words per image across documents, web, and street scenarios.

## Method

CPR-Trans is a cognitively inspired data construction paradigm that decomposes the translation reasoning process into three explicit stages: (1) Scene Cognition, where the model describes global visual context (objects, layout, background) while explicitly excluding text recognition; (2) Text Perception, where it reasons about spatial arrangement, reading order, and structure of textual regions given human OCR annotations; and (3) Translation Reasoning, where it explains the linguistic transformation integrating visual and textual understanding. Training data is generated via a VLLM-driven multi-stage pipeline using Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct with structured prompts, producing 165,200 aligned multimodal samples across 14 languages. Models (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) are fine-tuned with full-parameter optimization for 3 epochs. The key finding is that this structured, interpretable reasoning supervision substantially outperforms direct translation, simple OCR+translation concatenation, and uncontrolled thinking-model distillation, yielding average gains of +11.2 (Gemini judge) and +8.2 (Qwen3-VL judge) over the OCR+translation baseline, and even a 7B model fine-tuned with CPR-Trans surpasses much larger proprietary VLLMs on the benchmark.

## Results

On MMTIT-Bench, the 7B Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned with CPR-Trans achieves 83.98 (other→En) and 82.84 (other→Zh) under the Gemini-2.5-Flash judge, 82.99/82.23 under the Qwen3-VL judge, and 81.07/81.04 COMET scores. This 7B model outperforms all larger baselines, including the 235B Qwen3-VL-Thinking (73.81/77.90 Gemini-flash) and the 671B dots.vlm1 (79.23/76.73 Gemini-flash), and even surpasses the proprietary Gemini 2.5-Flash on 5 of 6 evaluation metrics. The ablation (Table 4) shows the full CPR-Trans configuration yields average gains of +11.2 (Gemini) and +8.2 (Qwen3-VL) over the OCR+translation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The 7B CPR-Trans model decisively outperforms both cascade baselines: MinerU2.5+Qwen3 scores only 48.32/49.70 (Gemini-flash o2e/o2z) and dots.ocr+Qwen3 reaches 66.24/65.27, both far below the proposed 83.98/82.84, confirming that OCR-dependent pipelines suffer severe error propagation on multilingual scenes. Against the 671B dots.vlm1 (79.23/76.73) and the 235B Qwen3-VL-Thinking (73.81/77.90), the 7B CPR-Trans model leads by 4.75 and 10.17 points respectively on Gemini-flash o2e, demonstrating that structured reasoning supervision compensates for a 30–95× parameter gap. The proprietary Gemini 2.5-Flash is the closest competitor, winning only on Gemini-flash o2z (85.00 vs. 82.84) while the CPR-Trans model leads on the other five metrics (e.g., 82.99 vs. 74.76 on Qwen3VL o2e, 81.07 vs. 79.26 on COMET o2e). Seed1.6-Thinking (74.09/75.82) trails the proposed method by roughly 10 points on Gemini-flash scores. Overall, CPR-Trans on a 7B backbone achieves the best or near-best score across all six evaluation columns, with the sole exception being the Gemini-flash o2z metric where Gemini 2.5-Flash retains a 2.16-point edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TIMT literature in two complementary directions: benchmark construction and reasoning-oriented data design. On the benchmark side, it departs from prior TIMT datasets (DITrans, DoTA, OCRMT, PATIMT, MTIT6) that are largely confined to English-Chinese pairs or at most four languages, by introducing fourteen non-English/non-Chinese languages across web, document, and street scenarios. On the method side, it builds on the growing body of Long-CoT work in LLMs and VLLMs but departs from existing TIMT reasoning paradigms—cascaded OCR-then-translate pipelines and language-only CoT—by proposing CPR-Trans, which explicitly structures a unified cognition-perception-reasoning chain grounded in the visual input. The data generation pipeline leverages VLLM-assisted annotation (Gemini 2.5 Flash, Qwen3-VL) and SynthDog for scaling, positioning the work at the intersection of multimodal data curation and structured reasoning supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMTIT-Bench fills a clear gap: no prior TIMT benchmark covers this breadth of low-resource languages and real-world visual diversity, making it a necessary resource for evaluating VLLM translation robustness beyond the English-Chinese comfort zone. The CPR-Trans paradigm offers a practical, interpretable recipe for structuring reasoning data in multimodal translation, and the finding that a 7B model fine-tuned with CPR-Trans approaches or exceeds 235B-scale models suggests that data design can partially substitute for parameter scale in this task. Open questions include whether the paradigm generalizes to video-level or document-level translation (acknowledged by the authors), whether the 1,400-sample benchmark is sufficient for reliable cross-language comparisons, and whether the VLLM-based judging protocol introduces systematic biases relative to human evaluation at scale.
