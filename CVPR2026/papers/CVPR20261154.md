## Problem

Existing polygonization methods are class-specific, so producing a complete vector basemap requires per-class inference followed by post-hoc stitching, which introduces gaps, overlaps, and duplicated edges. No prior framework generates a single, globally topologically consistent planar partition covering all land-cover classes in one run, and no public benchmark or standardized metrics exist for evaluating such all-class polygonal vectorization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261154/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 aerial RGB patch (0.3 m GSD) as input and produces a complete vector basemap: a topologically consistent planar partition of the image domain into per-class polygons (buildings, roads, vegetation, water, unvegetated area) where adjacent polygons share exactly one common boundary with zero gaps or overlaps. Internally, geometry is represented as a Gaussian-mixture vertex heatmap (peaks at polygon corners) at 1/4 spatial resolution in latent space, combined with a multi-class semantic mask to drive deterministic polygon reconstruction.

## Method

ACPV-Net couples two components: (1) a Semantically Supervised Conditioning (SSC) mechanism where a latent diffusion model generates vertex heatmaps in VAE latent space, and the semantic conditioning encoder is explicitly trained with a segmentation loss so that the conditioning itself learns task-coupled semantics that guide vertex placement along class-consistent boundaries; and (2) a proposition-driven topological reconstruction that first builds an overdense planar straight-line graph (PSLG) from all label-transition loci in the predicted mask, then applies vertex-guided subset selection using the predicted vertex peaks to simplify the graph while preserving geometric keypoints. A formal proposition proves that this two-step construction satisfies all ACPV topological constraints (disjoint interiors, shared boundaries, zero gap/overlap, linear geometry, semantic consistency, minimal vertex redundancy) by design, eliminating the need for heuristic post-processing.

## Results

On Deventer-512, ACPV-Net achieves 0% gap rate, 0% inter-class overlap, 0% intra-class overlap, and 100% shared-edge consistency (Table 2), the only method to satisfy all topological constraints. Per-class, it attains the best IoU across all five land-cover classes (Road 76.01, Building 82.08, Unvegetated 66.89, Vegetation 80.05, Water 67.96) and the best C-IoU (geometric accuracy) in every class except water (59.53 vs. HiSup's 51.79). On WHU-Building, ACPV-Net reaches 88.50 IoU, 81.45 C-IoU, and 34.85 MTA, outperforming the strongest baseline HiSup (87.63 IoU, 67.15 C-IoU, 35.27 MTA) while using fewer vertices (N-ratio 1.07 vs. 1.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACPV-Net outperforms all five baselines (DeepSnake, FFL, TopDiG, HiSup, GCP) on nearly every metric across all five land-cover classes on Deventer-512. The strongest baseline is HiSup, which ACPV-Net surpasses by 2.09 IoU on Road, 0.86 on Building, 5.43 on Unvegetated, 4.35 on Vegetation, and 3.12 on Water; the sole exception is MTA on water (42.63 vs. 42.22), attributed to ACPV-Net's more compact vertex representation (N-ratio 0.92 vs. 3.26). On WHU-Building, ACPV-Net achieves the best IoU (88.50 vs. HiSup 87.63), C-IoU (81.45 vs. 67.15), MTA (34.85 vs. 35.27), and topological errors (χ-Err 1.60, β-Err 1.81 vs. 1.95, 2.28), while using fewer vertices (N-ratio 1.07 vs. 1.93). GCP exhibits a severe intra-class overlap rate of 41.91% and FFL produces extreme topological errors (χ-Err 39.71 on Building), highlighting the topological fragility of per-class stitching. TopDiG is competitive on vertex efficiency (N-ratio 0.36 on Water) but lags substantially on semantic and geometric fidelity, confirming that vertex sparsity alone does not guarantee polygon quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion framework of Rombach et al. (CVPR 2022) for vertex generation, but departs from standard conditional diffusion pipelines by introducing Semantically Supervised Conditioning (SSC), which explicitly supervises the conditioning branch with a segmentation loss rather than treating it as an untrained external signal. It also departs from the dominant single-class polygonization paradigm (DeepSnake, FFL, TopDiG, HiSup, GCP) by formulating all-class vectorization as a unified task with a deterministic PSLG-based topological reconstruction, replacing the common post-hoc stitching of per-class outputs that introduces gaps and overlaps. The vertex-guided subset selection step replaces heuristic simplification (e.g., Douglas-Peucker) with learned keypoint preservation, bridging generative geometry extraction and cartographic conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to produce a seamless, topologically consistent multi-class vector basemap in a single run, addressing a long-standing practical bottleneck in automated cartography where per-class stitching breaks topology. The Deventer-512 benchmark fills a clear gap as the first public dataset with multi-class vector annotations, shared-edge consistency, and global topology metrics, enabling standardized evaluation of future ACPV methods. Open questions include scalability beyond 512×512 patches, generalization to regions with different cartographic conventions, and whether the diffusion-based vertex generation can be made faster for large-scale production mapping. The cross-region robustness test (mentioned in supplementary) is a promising but underdeveloped direction.
