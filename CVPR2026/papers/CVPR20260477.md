## Problem

Existing rank-based OOD detection methods like CRAFT require fine-tuning pretrained models and model class rankings as 2D probability matrices, ignoring the relative ordering of class ranks. The paper asks whether one can directly enforce a canonical class-ranking structure on raw logits using a listwise objective, without architectural changes, fine-tuning, or auxiliary outlier data, to achieve stronger OOD separation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260477/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) through a ResNet-18 backbone and produces both a class label (argmax logit) and a scalar OOD score (RankOOD-S). The OOD score is a weighted sum of log-softmax values computed from logit deviations against class-specific 95th-percentile reference thresholds, with multiplicative penalties (γ^r) that accumulate for each rank-position violation relative to the canonical ranking of the predicted class.

## Method

RankOOD operates in three stages. First, a pretrained CE classifier's correctly-classified samples yield a Rank Probability Matrix per class, from which a canonical rank permutation is extracted by solving a 0-1 integer linear program that maximizes total assignment probability. Second, a new classifier is trained with a hybrid loss L = L_CE + α·L_ListMLE, where ListMLE (derived from the Plackett-Luce model) maximizes the likelihood of the canonical ranking over the full logit vector, enforcing relative ordering across all supervised rank positions rather than only the top-1 decision. Third, at inference the RankOOD-S score measures weighted log-softmax deviations of penalized logits from class-specific reference thresholds, where the penalty δ_i = γ^r grows with the number of rank-order violations from position i onward, making the score sensitive to any disruption of the learned sequential logit hierarchy that OOD samples typically exhibit.

## Results

On the TinyImageNet near-OOD benchmark, RankOOD achieves SOTA performance with 85.30% AUROC and 50.05% FPR95, improving AUROC by 0.50% and reducing FPR95 by 4.3% over the strongest baseline. Across all three ID datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet), RankOOD achieves an average near-OOD FPR95 of 44.79% (second-best overall, behind only OE which uses outlier data) and ranks third-best in far-OOD detection. Among methods that do not use auxiliary outliers, RankOOD obtains the best FPR95 on CIFAR-100 (52.59%), outperforming GEN by 3.36%. The method outperforms both prior rank-based approaches (CRAFT and ExCeL) by an average FPR95 reduction of 4.21% (near-OOD) and 7.51% (far-OOD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankOOD outperforms both prior rank-based methods: CRAFT (46.76% avg FPR95) and ExCeL (59.89% avg FPR95), achieving 44.79% avg FPR95 in near-OOD — a 4.21% reduction over CRAFT and 15.1% over ExCeL. Among post-hoc methods, RankOOD surpasses the best (GEN at 54.43% avg FPR95) by 9.64% in FPR95, and also beats LogitNorm (49.56%) by 4.77%. The only method that outperforms RankOOD in near-OOD is Outlier Exposure (OE, 30.17% avg FPR95), which relies on auxiliary outlier data during training — an assumption RankOOD explicitly avoids. In far-OOD, RankOOD (83.63% AUROC, 47.44% FPR95 on CIFAR-100) ranks second behind G-ODIN, but outperforms all outlier-exposure-based methods (OE, MCD, UDG, MixOE) on both CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet. On TinyImageNet near-OOD specifically, RankOOD achieves SOTA, beating the previous best by 0.50% AUROC and 4.3% FPR95, highlighting its advantage in high-cardinality label spaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankOOD extends the class-ranking line of OOD detection initiated by ExCeL and CRAFT, which showed that in-distribution samples exhibit consistent inter-class rank orderings while OOD samples disrupt them. Where CRAFT models each class's ranking as a 2D C×C probability mass function requiring fine-tuning and architectural modifications, RankOOD instead derives canonical per-class rankings via an integer linear program over a rank probability matrix, then retrains the classifier with a ListMLE loss (from the information-retrieval literature) grounded in the Plackett-Luce model. This shifts the problem from learning class-specific PMFs to enforcing a global listwise ordering constraint on raw logits, eliminating the need for auxiliary outlier data or network modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is that RankOOD achieves competitive OOD detection (top-2 near-OOD, top-3 far-OOD across 34 baselines) using only standard ID training data and a ResNet-18 backbone, with no architectural changes or outlier exposure. The finding that ListMLE training improves even the vanilla MSP score suggests the loss reshapes the logit geometry in a broadly beneficial way, not just for the custom RankOOD-S score. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT, deeper ResNets) and higher-cardinality datasets (full ImageNet-1K), whether the ILP step remains tractable at scale, and whether the rank-structure signal degrades under domain shift beyond the near/far-OOD splits tested here.
