## Problem

Existing post-training quantization methods for Vision-Language Models rely on static identification and global compensation of sensitive channels, overlooking the fact that the positions and occurrence frequencies of important channels vary significantly across modalities and among tokens within the same modality. This token-level dynamism means that a single fixed compensation strategy cannot adequately address both globally persistent and locally specific quantization errors, leading to unsatisfactory accuracy under aggressive low-bit settings.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained VLM (Qwen2VL 2B/7B/72B or InternVL2 2B/8B) and a small calibration set of 128 image-caption pairs from COCO Caption (ShareGPT4V-enhanced). It produces quantized weights at W4A6, W4A8, or W3A16 bit-widths, augmented with lightweight low-rank adapters (total SVD rank 64, split evenly between one shared expert and N_r=8 routed experts) plus a per-layer router matrix. At inference, the quantized model accepts standard image + text prompt inputs and generates text responses, with the router dynamically selecting one of the routed experts per token.

## Method

QE first computes per-token important channels via top-k selection on |x_t| ⊙ w and aggregates their occurrence frequencies over calibration data to partition channels into token-independent (top-k most frequent) and token-dependent (next N_r·k channels) groups. A shared expert, implemented as a low-rank adapter trained via whitening SVD on the residual error, reconstructs global quantization error from token-independent channels while applying channel-wise scaling to suppress activation outliers. Token-dependent channels are clustered into N_r sub-groups using NPMI-based spectral clustering on their co-occurrence matrix, and each cluster is assigned a routed expert (another low-rank adapter) trained on the weighted residual error specific to that cluster. At inference, a lightweight router (initialized from the per-expert residual error magnitudes) scores each routed expert against the current input token and selects the one with minimal predicted error, enabling adaptive local compensation alongside the fixed global correction. An optional layer-wise refinement step fine-tunes only the routed experts and router with frozen shared expert and base weights.

## Results

On Qwen2VL-2B under the challenging W4A6 setting, QE achieves 58.74 average accuracy across 11 multimodal benchmarks, improving over MBQ (54.73) by 4.01% and LQER (55.92) by 2.82%, with only a 4.23% drop from full precision (62.97). Under W4A8, QE reaches 61.14 average, narrowing the gap to full precision to within 2%. On the larger Qwen2VL-72B model, QE delivers a remarkable 5.09% average accuracy improvement over MBQ under W4A6, nearly matching full-precision performance (e.g., 76.60 vs. 78.70 on OCRBench, 90.33 vs. 91.22 on ScienceQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QE consistently outperforms all four baselines across every quantization setting and model scale tested. Against the strongest baseline MBQ (modality-aware channel scaling), QE gains 4.01% average accuracy on Qwen2VL-2B W4A6 and 5.09% on Qwen2VL-72B W4A6, demonstrating that token-level adaptivity provides substantial benefits beyond modality-level balancing. Compared to LQER (static global low-rank error reconstruction), QE improves by 2.82% on Qwen2VL-2B W4A6 and 3.13% on InternVL2-2B W4A6, confirming that splitting error compensation into shared and routed experts captures local token-dependent variations that a single global adapter misses. SmoothQuant, which relies on fixed channel-wise scaling, lags significantly behind (50.27 vs. 58.74 on Qwen2VL-2B W4A6), highlighting the inadequacy of static scaling for dynamic channel importance. For weight-only W3A16 quantization, QE surpasses AWQ by 3.65% on Qwen2VL-2B, showing that the token-aware MoE framework generalizes beyond weight-activation quantization to weight-only settings as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of low-rank quantization error reconstruction (LQER, ASER) by decomposing the global error into token-independent and token-dependent components, each handled by a separate low-rank adapter within a Mixture-of-Experts framework. It builds on SmoothQuant's channel-wise scaling for the shared expert and on MBQ's modality-aware observation, but departs from both by introducing token-level granularity: rather than a single static set of important channels, QE identifies per-token channel importance and clusters token-dependent channels via NPMI-based spectral clustering. The key conceptual departure from prior PTQ methods (GPTQ, SpQR, AWQ, LQER) is the shift from fixed, globally-calibrated compensation to adaptive, input-conditioned expert selection at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs scale to 70B+ parameters and are deployed in latency-sensitive applications, the gap between full-precision and low-bit performance under aggressive settings like W4A6 remains a practical bottleneck; QE's 5.09% recovery on Qwen2VL-72B with minimal overhead (3.5–4.5× kernel speedup) makes it a strong candidate for production quantization pipelines. The empirical finding that most important channels are token-dependent rather than globally fixed challenges the assumption underlying many static PTQ methods and suggests that future quantization research should account for input-conditioned channel dynamics. Open questions include whether the co-occurrence clustering structure persists under different calibration data or model families, whether the approach extends to even lower bit-widths (W2A4), and whether the optional refinement step can be made fully data-free.
