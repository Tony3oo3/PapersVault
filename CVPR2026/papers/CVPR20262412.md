## Problem

While LLMs benefit from externalized reasoning workspaces (scratchpads, chain-of-thought), visual language models and text-to-image generators lack an analogous spatial reasoning substrate. Existing layout- or region-conditioned approaches restrict spatial reasoning to 2D hints (bounding boxes, masks, segmentation maps), which fundamentally limits the ability to handle complex 3D geometric relations, object orientations, and compositional intent in generated images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262412/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a complex compositional scene and optionally a user edit instruction (text-based or manual 3D manipulation). It produces a spatially coherent, identity-preserving RGB image along with an editable 3D scene where subjects are represented as texture-less meshes with fixed colors on a ground plane with fixed X/Y/Z bounds. The 3D scene serves as a persistent intermediate representation that can be re-rendered from any viewpoint after edits, enabling iterative refinement without regenerating from scratch.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free agentic pipeline that uses 3D space as a reasoning scratchpad, decomposing generation into four LLM-agent sub-tasks: (1) subject identification and instantiation as 3D meshes via text-to-image followed by image-to-3D (Hunyuan-3D), (2) 3D bounding box placement planning conditioned on global prompt, subject descriptions, and aspect ratios, (3) orientation and transformation adjustment where a separate OrientationEstimator agent reads subject crops from a depth-conditioned render and a TransformPlanner agent uses multi-view scene renders to propose rotations/translations, and (4) camera viewpoint selection from five pre-rendered proposals. The final image is synthesized by SIGMA-Gen using depth maps from the 3D scene, per-subject identity images, and an enhanced prompt in a single denoising loop, preserving multi-subject identities while enforcing spatial structure. A SubjectEditor agent further enables 3D-aware editing by converting user instructions into world-coordinate transformations, with ObjectClear for subject removal and latent blending for background-preserving reinsertion.

## Results

On GenAI-Bench (870 prompts), the 3D spatial scratchpad achieves 0.83 VQAScore text alignment and 4.81 Q-Align image quality, outperforming the no-reasoning Flux baseline (0.63 / 4.75) by 32% in text alignment while matching image quality. On CompoundPrompts (540 prompts), the method reaches 0.91 text alignment and 4.84 image quality, surpassing the strongest text-reasoning baseline Idea2Img (0.87 / 4.76). The method is training-free, relying solely on GPT-5 for agentic 3D planning, Flux.1-dev for subject image generation, Hunyuan-3D 2.5 for mesh creation, and SIGMA-Gen for identity-preserving depth-conditioned image synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the no-reasoning Flux.1-dev baseline, the 3D scratchpad improves GenAI-Bench text alignment from 0.63 to 0.83 (+32% relative) and CompoundPrompts from 0.85 to 0.91, while maintaining or slightly improving image quality. Versus Idea2Img (text-only iterative prompt refinement with GPT-5), the method wins on both benchmarks (0.83 vs. 0.80 on GenAI-Bench; 0.91 vs. 0.87 on CompoundPrompts) in a single iteration, whereas Idea2Img requires three refinement passes; combining the two (Ours + Idea2Img full) yields a further boost to 0.85 on GenAI-Bench. Against the 2D-reasoning RPG baseline, the 3D approach dominates: RPG-Flux scores 0.71 on GenAI-Bench and 0.84 on CompoundPrompts, while RPG-SDXL drops to 0.60 and 0.71 respectively, and the 3D method exceeds both on text alignment with equal or better image quality. The paper also reports improvements over DALL-E 3 across all five GenAI-Bench reasoning sub-categories (counting, comparison, differentiation, negation, universal), with the largest relative gain on negation prompts where diffusion models are weakest. Notably, none of the baselines support consistent 3D-aware editing of generated images, a capability unique to the proposed spatial scratchpad.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper draws a direct analogy between linguistic scratchpads in LLM reasoning (chain-of-thought, ReAct, PAL) and proposes a spatial analogue: a 3D scene as an intermediate reasoning workspace for text-to-image generation. It departs from 2D layout-based methods (LayoutGPT, RPG, SLD, MUSES) by lifting planning into full 3D with explicit geometry, orientation, and camera control, and from 3D scene generation work (OptiScene, SceneCraft, Scenethesis) by treating 3D not as the output artifact but as a disposable reasoning substrate that is rendered back to 2D. The final image synthesis step builds on SIGMA-Gen for multi-subject identity and depth control, while the agentic decomposition (subject extraction, bounding-box planning, orientation estimation, camera selection) follows the multi-agent LLM planning paradigm established by ReAct and MM-ReAct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit 3D spatial reasoning can improve compositional text-to-image fidelity (32% over raw Flux on GenAI-Bench) without any additional training, and uniquely enables 3D-consistent, identity-preserving image editing that 2D layout methods cannot support. However, the gains over the strongest text-reasoning baseline (Idea2Img) are modest (~3%), and the ablation shows that the orientation and camera agents contribute only marginal improvements, suggesting the core benefit comes from structured 3D placement rather than fine-grained 3D manipulation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes, whether the 3D representation is truly necessary versus a well-designed 2D layout with depth, and how the pipeline's reliance on multiple sequential LLM calls affects latency and cost in practice.
