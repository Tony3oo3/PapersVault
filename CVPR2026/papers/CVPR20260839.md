## Problem

Dentistry remains largely unexplored in multimodal large language models due to limited domain-specific data, scarce expert annotations, heterogeneous imaging modalities, and the absence of reliable, explainable diagnostic reasoning. Existing general-purpose and medical MLLMs produce inconsistent, hallucinated, or opaque outputs on dental imaging, and no unified benchmark exists to systematically evaluate MLLMs across the full range of dental modalities and clinical tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260839/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts eight dental imaging modalities (intraoral photographs, panoramic/periapical/cephalometric radiographs, pathology slides, 3D model scans, intraoral videos, and interleaved image-text sequences) paired with free-form text prompts. For abnormality diagnosis, the output is not a single label but a structured five-step TRACE-CoT reasoning chain (image inspection → hypothesis generation → medical expertise reference → feature-based verification → evidence-informed conclusion) followed by the final diagnosis; other tasks yield CVM stage predictions, treatment plans, bounding-box-based tooth localization with counts, and video comprehension responses.

## Method

OralGPT-Omni is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and trained via a four-stage paradigm: (1) dental knowledge injection on 16 textbooks (LM-only), (2) dental concept alignment on 6,318 image-caption pairs (projector-only), (3) supervised fine-tuning on 52,725 instruction pairs including 31,777 TRACE-CoT reasoning chains generated by a pipeline that combines GPT-5-mini visual descriptions, sparse annotations as hypotheses, and retrieved clinical-guideline/Wikipedia knowledge, and (4) reinforcement learning tuning under GRPO with a difficulty-aware data selection strategy (retaining only medium-difficulty cases where 0.2 ≤ S_avg ≤ 0.8 and Max(S)−Min(S) ≥ 0.4 across N=5 rollouts) and a composite reward R_total = α·R_answer + β·𝕀[R_answer>0]·R_trace + γ·R_format, where R_trace is an LLM-judged score over factual soundness, logical coherence, and answer consistency. The TRACE-CoT pattern is the key differentiator: it forces the model to externalize radiologist-style diagnostic reasoning rather than producing black-box predictions, and the RLT stage specifically incentivizes this reasoning on cases the SFT model finds moderately difficult.

## Results

On the MMOral-Uni benchmark (2,809 open-ended QA pairs across 5 modalities and 5 tasks), OralGPT-Omni achieves an overall score of 51.84, substantially outperforming the strongest proprietary model o3 (38.70) and GPT-5 (36.42). On the MMOral-OPG benchmark for panoramic X-ray analysis, OralGPT-Omni scores 45.31. The model's strongest per-modality scores are on intraoral image localization (66.80), cephalometric radiograph CVM staging (65.90), and intraoral video comprehension (65.00), while its weakest is treatment planning (47.33), where proprietary models lead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OralGPT-Omni (51.84 overall) outperforms all 27 baselines on the MMOral-Uni benchmark, beating the strongest proprietary model o3 (38.70) by 13.14 points and GPT-5 (36.42) by 15.42 points. Among open-source models, the gap is even larger: the best general-purpose model MiMo-VL-7B scores 29.62, a 22.22-point deficit. All eight medical-specific MLLMs (ranging from LLaVA-Med at 10.16 to Lingshu-7B at 27.08) are substantially outperformed, with the best medical model Lingshu-7B trailing by 24.76 points. The sole task where OralGPT-Omni underperforms is treatment planning (TP), where GPT-5 (80.67), Claude-Sonnet-4-5 (79.33), and o3 (75.33) all exceed OralGPT-Omni's 47.33, which the authors attribute to treatment planning comprising only 0.006% of the training data. On the MMOral-OPG panoramic X-ray benchmark, OralGPT-Omni achieves 45.31, also surpassing GPT-5. A clinical validity assessment by a radiologist with 10+ years of experience confirmed OralGPT-Omni's superior accuracy and potential clinical utility over GPT-5 and Lingshu-7B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the medical MLLM line of work (dermatology, ophthalmology, chest radiology, pathology) into dentistry, a domain the authors argue has been underexplored due to data scarcity and modality heterogeneity. It builds directly on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the backbone and departs from prior dental MLLM efforts (DentVLM, OralGPT) by introducing a clinically grounded TRACE-CoT reasoning pattern that mirrors radiologists' five-step diagnostic workflow, rather than relying on generic CoT prompting or human-verified annotation. The four-stage training paradigm (knowledge injection → vision-language alignment → SFT → GRPO-based RLT with a TRACE-specific reward) is a structured adaptation of standard VLM fine-tuning pipelines to the dental domain, and the MMOral-Uni benchmark generalizes the prior single-modality MMOral-OPG benchmark to eight imaging modalities and five clinical tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MMOral-Uni (2,809 QA pairs across five modalities and five tasks) fills a critical evaluation gap in dental AI, providing the first comprehensive suite for benchmarking MLLMs in digital dentistry. The TRACE-CoT pattern addresses a key clinical adoption barrier—explainability—by making diagnostic reasoning transparent, which is essential for clinician trust in high-stakes settings. However, the model's weak treatment-planning performance (47.33 vs. GPT-5's 80.67, attributed to only 0.006% of training data) highlights that comprehensive clinical decision support remains out of reach, and the moderate absolute scores (~52/100) suggest substantial room for improvement. Open questions include whether the TRACE-CoT reasoning framework transfers to other medical imaging specialties and whether the difficulty-aware RLT data selection strategy generalizes beyond dental diagnostics.
