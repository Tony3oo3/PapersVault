## Problem

Contrastive vision-language models like CLIP remain vulnerable to spurious correlations, yet existing interpretability methods produce noisy, pixel-level attributions misaligned with the model's internal representation, and existing benchmarks like CounterAnimals rely solely on accuracy drops to diagnose background reliance—conflating background-driven errors with fine-grained confusions, viewpoint shifts, and scale variations. The paper shows that accuracy-based partitioning is an unreliable proxy for disentangling object versus background-driven failures.

## Input / Output

CCI takes an RGB image processed by CLIP's ViT encoder (yielding N patch embeddings, e.g., 14×14 for ViT-B/16) and a text prompt, requiring no model modification or retraining. It outputs a spatial importance map S ∈ R^{W×H} computed as a weighted sum of cluster masks, per-cluster importance scores w_k, and—when combined with GroundedSAM—binary foreground/background attribution labels for each prediction. The patch-level map is upsampled to 224×224 for pixel-level deletion/insertion evaluation.

## Method

CCI is a training-free interpretability method that operates entirely at inference time. It first extracts CLIP's patch embeddings and applies K-means clustering to group them into K semantically coherent clusters. For each cluster, it constructs a binary mask and performs attention attenuation by setting attention logits to −∞ for all masked patch positions at every transformer layer and head, thereby preventing the CLS token from aggregating information from those patches. The importance of each cluster is quantified by the cosine similarity drop Δs_k between the original and modified CLS-text scores, normalized across clusters to yield weights w_k. The final spatial importance map is a weighted sum of cluster masks, producing coherent, representation-aligned region-level attributions rather than the fragmented pixel-level saliency typical of gradient-based or perturbation-based methods.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K faithfulness evaluation, CCI achieves a deletion AUC of 0.1809 (Top-1, ground-truth label) and 0.3276 (Top-5), outperforming the strongest baseline Grad-ECLIP (0.2464 / 0.4272) by 27% and 23% respectively. On MS COCO cross-modal retrieval, CCI delivers more than a twofold improvement in deletion AUC for Top-5 image retrieval (0.1056 vs. Grad-ECLIP's 0.2670) and a 48% reduction for Top-1 image retrieval (0.0650 vs. 0.1246). For insertion, CCI also leads across all settings, reaching 0.4175 (Top-1, GT) on ImageNet and 0.1812 (Top-1, IR) on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCI outperforms all seven baselines (Attention Rollout, Grad-CAM, GAME, MaskCLIP, CLIP-Surgery, M2iB, and Grad-ECLIP) on every deletion and insertion metric across both ImageNet and MS COCO. The closest competitor is Grad-ECLIP, which leads among baselines on most settings; CCI improves over it by 27% on ImageNet deletion Top-1 (0.1809 vs. 0.2464) and by 60% on COCO deletion Top-5 image retrieval (0.1056 vs. 0.2670), the paper's headline twofold gain. MaskCLIP is the second-best baseline on ImageNet deletion (0.2848 Top-1) but still trails CCI by 36%. On insertion, CCI's advantage is more moderate but consistent: it surpasses Grad-ECLIP by 8.8% on ImageNet Top-1 (0.4175 vs. 0.3838) and by 15% on COCO Top-1 image retrieval (0.1812 vs. 0.1576). Gradient-free methods (Rollout, Raw Attention) are uniformly the weakest, confirming that CCI's cluster-level attention attenuation captures decision-relevant regions far more faithfully than raw or rolled-out attention weights.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCI departs from prior CLIP interpretability methods (Grad-ECLIP, MaskCLIP, M2iB, RISE) by operating at the representation level rather than pixel space: it clusters CLIP's own patch embeddings via k-means and attenuates attention logits to those clusters, yielding semantically coherent region-level attributions without pixel-level artifacts. COVAR extends the CounterAnimals benchmark by replacing its coarse two-background design with controlled, multi-factor perturbations (background, viewpoint, scale, flip, rotation, crop, translation) across 33 diverse ImageNet classes, synthesized via Emu2. Methodologically, the paper challenges CA's core assumption that accuracy-based easy/hard partitioning isolates background sensitivity, using CCI to show that fine-grained confusion, not background reliance, dominates errors in both sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that background-driven errors constitute only 6–9% of total errors (nearly identical across CA's easy and hard sets) while fine-grained confusion accounts for 46–60% reframes how the community should diagnose CLIP failures and design robustness benchmarks. The observation that scale perturbation nearly doubles BG-Er while viewpoint changes do not, and that larger models trained on web-scale data (LAION) show more background reliance than smaller models on curated data (DataComp), suggests that data curation and explicit scale handling matter more than model scaling for spurious-correlation robustness. Open questions include whether CCI's clustering-based attribution generalizes to non-ViT backbones or multi-image inputs, whether the GPT-4o-based fine-grained confusion classifier introduces systematic bias, and whether COVAR's 33-class scope captures the full diversity of real-world spurious correlations.
