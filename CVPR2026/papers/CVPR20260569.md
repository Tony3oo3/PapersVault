## Problem

MLLMs pretrained on static image-text pairs produce temporally inconsistent segmentation prompts when applied per-frame to video, causing severe jitter in SAM-generated masks. Existing remedies either demand costly video pretraining of the MLLM or introduce complex temporal fusion modules, both of which compromise the zero-shot generalization and deployment efficiency of foundation models. The paper asks whether temporal consistency can be achieved solely by constraining the MLLM's output behavior with physical motion priors, without any architectural modification or video-specific training data.

## Input / Output

The system receives a video sequence of T frames and a natural language query, and produces a per-frame binary segmentation mask sequence. Internally, the MLLM (Qwen-VL-7B/13B) operates in a two-round dialogue: the first round outputs a bounding box for the referred object, and the second round samples a 5×5 grid of points within that box, assigning each a foreground/background label. These prompts are fed to a frozen EfficientViT-XL1-SAM instance per frame, and points below a confidence threshold are filtered before mask generation. No ground-truth labels or test-time optimization are accessed at inference.

## Method

SPOT co-optimizes the spatiotemporal generation behavior of MLLM prompt points via a triple-constraint loss applied during LoRA fine-tuning. The temporal constraint is a Brownian Bridge Loss that models the target centroid trajectory as an endpoint-constrained Gaussian process, using ground-truth mask centroids from the first and last frames of a segment as priors; a variance-adaptive weighting term (1/α(1−α)) enforces precise localization near endpoints while permitting motion tolerance in intermediate frames. The spatial constraint is a Prompt Quality-aware Loss combining SmoothL1 bounding-box regression with a geometric-consistency hard supervision (binary cross-entropy on whether each prompt point falls inside the ground-truth mask), directly aligned with SAM's sensitivity to positive prompt placement. A standard autoregressive text-alignment loss preserves the MLLM's semantic reasoning capability. The key insight is that the non-differentiable SAM acts as a fixed black-box, so the entire optimization reduces to searching a LoRA parameter subspace whose output prompt trajectory approaches the set of prompts maximizing IoU, with the Brownian bridge providing the physical smoothness prior that static-image pretraining never learned.

## Results

On Ref-YouTubeVOS, SPOT-13B achieves 71.8 J&F, surpassing the strongest LLM-based baseline DTOS-9B (68.5) by 3.3 points and the best non-LLM method SAMWISE (69.2) by 2.6 points. On Ref-DAVIS-2017, SPOT-13B reaches 77.2 J&F, outperforming DTOS-9B (74.9) by 2.3 points. On the ReVOS reasoning benchmark, SPOT-13B attains 54.8 overall J&F and 18.0 stability score R, exceeding VISA-13B (50.8 J&F, 15.1 R) by 4.0 and 2.9 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPOT-13B outperforms all 17 baselines across every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest LLM-based competitor DTOS-9B, SPOT-13B gains +3.3 J&F on Ref-YouTubeVOS (71.8 vs. 68.5), +2.3 on Ref-DAVIS-2017 (77.2 vs. 74.9), and +2.3 on MeViS (51.2 vs. 48.9). Against the best non-LLM method SAMWISE, SPOT-7B already surpasses it by +1.3 J&F on Ref-YouTubeVOS and +5.4 on Ref-DAVIS-2017, while SPOT-13B extends the lead to +2.6 and +6.6 respectively. On the reasoning-focused ReVOS benchmark, SPOT-13B achieves 54.8 overall J&F and 18.0 stability (R), beating VISA-13B (50.8, 15.1) by 4.0 and 2.9 points, and TrackGPT-13B (45.0, 12.8) by 9.8 and 5.2 points. The ablation in Table 5 further confirms that the Brownian Bridge Loss contributes +5.3 J&F and the Prompt Quality Loss contributes +7.8 J&F over a no-constraint MLLM+SAM2 baseline (66.9), validating that both spatiotemporal constraints are necessary for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of addressing temporal inconsistency in MLLM-guided video segmentation through video pretraining (Video-LLaVA, Elysium) or complex temporal fusion modules (GLUS, SAM 2's streaming memory). Instead, it repurposes the Brownian bridge stochastic process—a well-known endpoint-constrained Gaussian process from probability theory—as a loss function that constrains the MLLM's prompt-point output trajectories, treating the problem as output-space optimization rather than model modification. The framework builds on the established MLLM+SAM cascaded architecture (as in SAMWISE, VISA, ViLLa) and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning, but introduces the key conceptual shift that physical motion priors, not learned temporal representations, are sufficient to enforce temporal smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core claim—that temporal consistency in video segmentation can be achieved by constraining prompt trajectories with a physical motion model, without video pretraining or architectural changes—challenges the field's working assumption that temporal understanding must be baked into the model. If this generalizes beyond the MLLM+SAM pipeline, it could significantly lower the barrier to deploying video understanding with existing image-pretrained foundation models. Open questions include whether the Brownian bridge's smoothness assumption holds under occlusions, abrupt direction changes, or multi-object scenes, and whether the approach scales to longer video sequences where endpoint constraints become less informative. The reliance on a single MLLM backbone (Qwen-VL) and a single SAM variant (EfficientViT-XL1) limits the generality of the evidence.
