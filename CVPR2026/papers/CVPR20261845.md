## Problem

Conventional vision-language models depend on high-quality RGB images and degrade sharply under adverse conditions such as low light, high dynamic range, or fast motion. Event cameras capture complementary per-pixel brightness changes with microsecond latency and wide dynamic range, but event-only VLMs lack the color and texture information needed for full scene understanding. No prior work has fused both modalities in a single VLM, and large-scale RGB-Event-Text supervision for training such a model does not exist.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a synchronized RGB frame and a temporally aligned event stream (rendered into N_w=3 two-channel event images for fair comparison with frame-based baselines) plus a text instruction prompt. At inference it supports three input configurations—RGB-only, event-only, or joint RGB+Event—and produces free-form scene captions or attribute-level VQA answers. Evaluation follows an LLM-as-a-judge protocol (GPT-3.5-Turbo, cross-validated with Qwen3-Omni-30B) scoring captions on Correctness, Detail Orientation, and Contextual Understanding (0–5 Likert) and VQA on average quality and attribute-level accuracy.

## Method

RE-VLM is a dual-stream architecture built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B: a frozen RGB ViT encoder and a trainable event ViT encoder each produce feature maps, which are projected into LLM space via lightweight modality adapters. The event branch applies multi-scale depthwise 1D convolutions along the temporal slice axis followed by SE-style temporal weighting to capture motion at multiple time scales. A training-only Spatio-Temporal Alignment Module (STAM) resamples both feature maps to a shared lattice, computes dual self-attention to derive per-token importance maps, and imposes a weighted cross-modal discrepancy loss (L_CA-WTD) that pulls RGB and event representations closer in salient regions. Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) event-language alignment on 1.3M Event-Text pairs with the LLM and RGB branch frozen, (2) event-RGB alignment on 600K paired samples optimizing the event encoder and STAM, and (3) LoRA-based instruction tuning on 120K multimodal dialogues. To address data scarcity, a graph-driven pipeline converts synchronized RGB-Event streams into modality-specific scene graphs, performs degradation-aware fusion (anchoring motion/structure to events and color/text to RGB), and synthesizes verifiable captions and VQA pairs, yielding the PEOD-Chat (11k) and RGBE-Chat (113.7k) datasets.

## Results

On the illumination-challenged PEOD-Chat benchmark, RE-VLM (4B) achieves the highest caption quality (Ave = 3.82) and VQA accuracy (Acc = 0.63), surpassing the best RGB-only baseline Qwen2.5-VL* (Ave = 3.61, Acc = 0.55) and the event-only EventGPT (Ave = 3.04, Acc = 0.40). On the more diverse RGBE-Chat benchmark, RE-VLM attains Ave = 4.20 and Acc = 0.75, again outperforming all RGB-only and event-only baselines. The gains are most pronounced under adverse lighting, where RE-VLM's caption CI score jumps from 3.23 (best RGB-only) to 3.68, a 14% relative improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE-VLM (4B) outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Against the strongest RGB-only baseline, fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL* (3B), RE-VLM gains +0.21 in caption Ave and +0.08 in VQA Acc on PEOD-Chat, and +0.34 / +0.10 on RGBE-Chat. The larger RGB-only models (InternVL2 4B, DeepSeek-VL 7B, LLaVA-1.5 7B) score lower than even the 3B Qwen2.5-VL* on PEOD-Chat, confirming that parameter count alone does not compensate for the lack of event cues under adverse lighting. The event-only EventGPT (7B) trails RE-VLM by a wide margin (Ave 3.04 vs. 3.82 on PEOD-Chat; Acc 0.40 vs. 0.63), illustrating that events alone cannot recover color and texture information. Notably, RE-VLM achieves these gains with only 4B parameters, fewer than the 7B RGB-only and event-only baselines, indicating that the dual-stream fusion is more parameter-efficient than simply scaling a single-modality model.

## Relation to Prior Work

RE-VLM extends the event-based vision-language line (EventGPT, EventCLIP, EventBind) by introducing RGB as a complementary second stream, positioning itself as the first dual-modality VLM that jointly encodes static appearance and dynamic event cues. Architecturally, it builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as the LLM backbone and adds a ViT-based event encoder with multi-scale temporal depthwise convolutions, a training-only Spatio-Temporal Alignment Module (STAM) for cross-modal feature alignment, and LoRA-based instruction tuning. The graph-driven data generation pipeline—converting synchronized RGB-Event streams into verifiable scene graphs before synthesizing captions and QA pairs—is a departure from prior event-text generation approaches that relied on RGB-trained VLMs and degraded precisely when RGB quality was poor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: current VLMs fail under low light, HDR transitions, and fast motion, conditions common in autonomous driving and surveillance. By contributing two new RGB-Event-Text datasets (PEOD-Chat, RGBE-Chat) and a reproducible graph-driven annotation pipeline, it lowers the barrier for future event-augmented VLM research. Open questions include whether the graph-driven pipeline scales to truly large corpora without manual screening, whether the STAM alignment loss remains beneficial at larger model scales, and whether the gains transfer to real-time deployment where event streams must be processed asynchronously rather than rendered into fixed slices.
