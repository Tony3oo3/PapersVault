## Problem

Whether a pre-trained diffusion model holds a single 'mental average' of a concept—one as sharp and realistic as a typical sample—remains unanswered. Prior averaging techniques (pixel-space congealing, dataset distillation) produce either blurry or inconsistent prototypes, and diffusion models lack an explicit semantic bottleneck with a decoder, making straightforward feature-space averaging ill-defined.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., Stable Diffusion variants), initializing K=1000 Gaussian noise latents from N(0,I). It outputs a single sharp prototype image representing the model's mental average, or, for multimodal concepts, a set of mode-specific prototype images after clustering in CLIP or BLIP-VQA embedding space and applying per-cluster LoRA or Textual Inversion conditioning. The optimization runs for 300 Adam steps per latent per timestep with a cutoff at t_stop=10 out of 20 DDIM steps, taking roughly 10 hours on an RTX 4080.

## Method

The core contribution recasts concept averaging as trajectory alignment within the diffusion model's denoising process. At each timestep t, the h-space (U-Net bottleneck) activations of all K noise latents are averaged to form a semantic target, and each latent is optimized via gradient descent to match this target before DDIM denoising to the next step, progressively aligning coarse-to-fine semantics from global layout to local detail. For multimodal concepts, samples are clustered in CLIP embedding space (or BLIP-VQA for user-grounded criteria), and a lightweight rank-1 LoRA or Textual Inversion embedding is trained per cluster to bridge the semantic gap between the clustering space and the diffusion h-space, yielding faithful mode-specific prototypes. The approach generalizes across SD variants and to DiT architectures by substituting the final transformer block output for the h-space.

## Results

Across 12 concepts in four categories (Animal, Person, Object, Abstract), DMA achieves the best ImageReward score of 1.002, substantially outperforming D⁴M (0.823) and MGD³ (0.755). On consistency (CLIP cosine distance), DMA scores 0.031, roughly 5× lower than D⁴M (0.168) and MGD³ (0.180), indicating far more stable prototypes across random initializations. DMA also attains the best representativeness on CLIP (0.179) and DreamSim (0.341), confirming that its prototypes sit closest to the semantic center of the concept distribution. GANgealing and Avg VAE achieve zero consistency by construction but produce blurry, unrealistic averages with negative ImageReward scores (−0.684 and −2.262 respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GANgealing [70], DMA trades perfect (but trivial) consistency for dramatically better realism and representativeness: GANgealing's pixel-space averaging yields blurry prototypes with an ImageReward of −0.684 and CLIP representativeness of 0.386, while DMA scores 1.002 and 0.179 respectively; GANgealing also cannot handle abstract concepts at all. Against D⁴M [89], DMA wins on every metric: consistency is 5.4× lower on CLIP (0.031 vs 0.168), representativeness is better on all three distance functions, and ImageReward is higher by 0.179 (1.002 vs 0.823). Against MGD³ [13], the pattern is similar: DMA's consistency is 5.8× lower on CLIP (0.031 vs 0.180), representativeness is marginally better across all metrics, and ImageReward is higher by 0.247 (1.002 vs 0.755). In summary, DMA is the only method that simultaneously achieves high consistency, strong representativeness, and positive human-preference scores, whereas the baselines are either consistent but blurry (GANgealing, Avg VAE) or realistic but inconsistent (D⁴M, MGD³).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Kwon et al.'s discovery that U-Net diffusion models possess a semantically meaningful h-space with approximately linear structure, repurposing it as the averaging substrate where prior work used it only for segmentation or correspondence. It departs from the established prototype-image literature (patch-based discovery, sample selection, dataset distillation) by formulating averaging as trajectory alignment across denoising steps rather than as a one-shot spatial or feature-space operation. The multimodal extension draws on CLIP for stable semantic clustering and on Textual Inversion/LoRA to bridge the gap between the clustering space and the diffusion model's internal h-space, a combination not previously explored for concept summarization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DMA provides the first practical tool for extracting a single, realistic 'mental average' from a pre-trained diffusion model, opening a window into how these models encode and bias concept representations (e.g., gender bias in 'soldier,' geographic bias in 'Italy'). Key limitations include high computational cost (~10 hours per concept with 1,000 latents), dependence on external encoders (CLIP/BLIP) whose own biases propagate into clustering, and the need to identify an architecture-specific semantic layer (h-space for UNet, final transformer block for DiT). Open questions include reducing the sample and compute requirements, extending the approach to video or 3D diffusion models, and developing objective, embedding-space-agnostic metrics for representativeness.
