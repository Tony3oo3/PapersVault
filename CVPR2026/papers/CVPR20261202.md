limitations: The paper does not include an explicit limitations section. The evaluation is confined to standard closed-set detection benchmarks with fixed pattern counts (50 or 150) and two backbone families (ResNet-50, Swin-L), leaving open whether the shared-pattern formulation scales to open-vocabulary, long-tail, or extremely high-diversity settings. The quality-aware assignment relies on a fixed IoU–confidence scoring formula whose generalizability to detection tasks with fundamentally different matching structures (e.g., 3D detection, tracking) is not explored.

surprise_factor: The Gini coefficient of 0.97 in DINO is a striking quantification of what practitioners anecdotally observed (most queries are effectively dead), and the fact that 50 shared patterns still yield +0.7 mAP over the baseline is a compactness result that challenges the assumption that each query needs independent parameters. ## Problem

DETR-based detectors rely on fixed learnable queries and one-to-one Hungarian matching, which together cause severe query activation imbalance: a small subset of 'winning' queries dominates gradient updates while the majority remain weakly optimized, leaving model capacity underused. Prior work addresses either query adaptivity or supervision density in isolation, but does not treat the representation-side and supervision-side imbalance as a unified optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The model takes a standard RGB image and produces per-object bounding boxes with class labels (detection) or additionally segmentation masks (instance segmentation). During training, the quality-aware one-to-many assignment is applied only in intermediate decoder layers to enrich supervision, while the final layer retains standard one-to-one Hungarian matching so that inference remains unchanged and NMS-free. Queries are not fixed parameters but are dynamically composed per image from a compact set of shared patterns (50 or 150 patterns generating 300 or 900 queries).

## Method

PaQ-DETR introduces two synergistic components. First, a pattern-based dynamic query module represents each of n object queries as a convex combination of m shared base patterns, with combination weights produced by a content-aware generator that fuses multi-scale encoder features (via dilated convolutions, top-down fusion with channel/spatial attention, and global average pooling) through a two-layer MLP and softmax; this shared-basis formulation enables gradient flow across queries and stabilizes semantics. Second, a quality-aware one-to-many assignment dynamically determines the number of positive samples per ground-truth instance using a quality score that balances IoU and classification confidence (s = IoU − γ·conf), selecting top-k predictions adaptively and applying Varifocal Loss in intermediate decoder layers. A cosine-similarity diversity regularizer prevents pattern redundancy, and the total loss combines the one-to-many assignment loss, auxiliary Hungarian losses, and the diversity term.

## Results

On COCO val2017 with ResNet-50 and 900 queries (12 epochs), PaQ-DINO achieves 51.9 mAP, surpassing DINO++ (50.3) by 1.6 mAP and DDQ-DETR (50.7) by 1.2 mAP. With 24-epoch training, PaQ-DINO reaches 52.6 mAP, outperforming DDQ-DETR (52.0) and Stable-DINO (51.5). On the Swin-L backbone (12 epochs, 900 queries), PaQ-DINO attains 57.8 mAP, matching the best prior method (Relation-DETR, 57.8). On specialized datasets, PaQ-DINO improves over DINO by 0.8 mAP on CSD (54.2 vs 53.4) and 4.2 mAP on MSSD (55.2 vs 51.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaQ-DETR consistently outperforms all 18 compared baselines on COCO val2017. Against the strongest 12-epoch baselines, PaQ-DINO (51.9 mAP) surpasses Ease-DETR (50.8) by 1.1, DDQ-DETR (50.7) by 1.2, and DINO++ (50.3) by 1.6 mAP. At 24 epochs, PaQ-DINO (52.6) leads DDQ-DETR (52.0) by 0.6 and Stable-DINO (51.5) by 1.1 mAP. In the one-to-many assignment comparison (Table 3), PaQ-DETR (52.4 mAP) edges out Co-DETR (52.1) by 0.3 and MS-DETR (50.0) by 2.4 mAP. On the Swin-L backbone, PaQ-DINO (57.8) ties Relation-DETR (57.8) and matches Stable-DINO (57.7) while achieving the best AP50 (76.2). On specialized datasets, the gains are most pronounced on MSSD where PaQ-DINO improves over DINO by 4.2 mAP (55.2 vs 51.0), driven largely by a 7.2 mAP gain on small objects (AP_S: 27.2 vs 20.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

PaQ-DETR sits at the intersection of two DETR research threads: dynamic query design (DDQ-DETR, Dy-DETR, EASE-DETR) and one-to-many assignment (Group-DETR, MS-DETR, Co-DETR). It departs from prior dynamic-query methods by reframing the problem as a structural optimization imbalance—quantified via Gini coefficients—rather than merely a representational flexibility issue, and addresses it jointly through shared-pattern query composition and quality-aware positive selection. The framework is built directly on DINO's denoising pipeline and Deformable-DETR's multi-scale attention, extending them without auxiliary decoders or extra inference cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Gini-coefficient analysis (0.97 in DINO) provides the first clear quantitative evidence that one-to-one Hungarian matching causes severe, structural query underutilization, reframing a long-suspected training pathology as a measurable optimization problem. The finding that as few as 50 shared patterns suffice to generate competitive 300-query detectors, combined with the t-SNE evidence of semantic clustering in dynamic weights, suggests a compact, interpretable alternative to fully independent learnable queries. Open questions include whether the pattern count must scale with dataset diversity (e.g., open-vocabulary or long-tail settings), whether the quality-aware assignment generalizes beyond IoU-classification scoring, and whether the approach transfers to detection tasks with fundamentally different matching structures.
