## Problem

Despite ARC puzzles being inherently visual (involving concepts like reflection, symmetry, and gravity), existing research has almost exclusively framed the problem through language-oriented approaches (LLMs, recurrent reasoning models), leaving the vision-centric perspective largely unexplored. This paper asks whether standard vision architectures and visual inductive biases (spatial locality, translation invariance, scale invariance) can be leveraged directly to solve ARC, without any linguistic intermediates or internet-scale pre-training.

## Input / Output

Each ARC task provides 2–4 demonstration pairs of 2D grids (max 30×30, with C=10 discrete color indices) plus one inference input grid. The raw grids are randomly scaled (integer ratio) and translated onto a fixed 64×64 canvas with an extra background color, then processed as a natural image. The model outputs a per-pixel categorical distribution over colors, aggregated via average pooling across canvas pixels that map to the same raw location; final predictions are consolidated over 510 random views by majority voting under a pass@2 protocol.

## Method

VARC formulates each ARC task as an image-to-image translation problem solved by a standard ViT (or U-Net) with separable 2D positional embeddings (RoPE) and 2×2 patchification, which expands the effective token vocabulary from 10 colors to an exponentially large set of local patch configurations. A key design is the 'canvas' representation: inputs are randomly scaled and translated onto a 64×64 canvas, enabling translation and scale augmentations that inject critical visual inductive biases absent from the raw grid. Training is two-stage: offline supervised training on 400 ARC-1 tasks (augmented with RE-ARC to ~400k pairs) with a shared task-conditional token per task, followed by test-time training on the 2–4 demo pairs of each unseen task (with flip/rotation/color-permutation augmentations treated as auxiliary tasks). At inference, the model performs fully feedforward multi-view prediction (510 views) with majority voting, requiring no recurrence. The 18M-parameter ViT achieves 54.5% on ARC-1, and ensembling a ViT with a U-Net (each TTT'd four times) reaches 60.4%, matching average human performance.

## Results

VARC (18M ViT, trained from scratch on ARC data) achieves 54.5% pass@2 accuracy on ARC-1 and 8.3% on ARC-2. Ensembling an 18M ViT with a 55M U-Net (each with 4 TTT runs) boosts ARC-1 accuracy to 60.4%, matching the reported average human performance of 60.2%. On ARC-2, the ensemble reaches 11.1%. The single-model result is reported as 54.5 ± 0.7 (ARC-1) and 8.3 ± 0.4 (ARC-2) over four runs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the controlled setting of training from scratch on ARC data only, VARC (54.5%) substantially outperforms the best recurrent baselines: TRM (44.6%, +9.9 pts) and HRM (40.3%, +14.2 pts) on ARC-1. VARC also surpasses several leading LLMs reported at the time, including GPT-5 (44.0%), o3-mini-high (34.5%), Claude 3.7 (21.2%), and Deepseek R1 (15.8%), despite using a model orders of magnitude smaller (18M vs. 671B+ parameters) and no internet-scale pre-training. The VARC ensemble (60.4%) matches the average human performance (60.2%) on ARC-1. However, VARC trails the strongest LLM-based systems—Grok-4-thinking (66.7%) and Bespoke/Grok-4 (79.6%)—by 12.2 and 19.2 points respectively on ARC-1, and by 7.7 and 18.3 points on ARC-2. On ARC-2, VARC (8.3%) edges out TRM (7.8%) and HRM (5.0%) but remains well below the top LLM entries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant language-centric framing of ARC (LLM-based inductive/transductive reasoning and recurrent reasoning models like HRM and TRM) by recasting the problem as image-to-image translation, analogous to semantic segmentation. It builds on the test-time training paradigm (Akyürek et al., ICML 2025) for few-shot adaptation and adopts separable 2D positional embeddings (Chen et al., ICCV 2021) for the ViT backbone. The key conceptual departure is the 'canvas' formulation with scale and translation augmentations, which injects standard visual inductive biases (locality, translation invariance, scale invariance) that prior ARC methods, operating in discrete token space, did not exploit. Unlike the earlier ViT-ARC attempt [34], which could only fit training tasks, this framework is explicitly designed for few-shot cross-task generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Matching average human performance (60.4% vs. 60.2%) with an 18M-parameter model trained from scratch on ARC data alone demonstrates that substantial abstract reasoning can emerge from visual learning without internet-scale pretraining or linguistic intermediates. This opens the ARC problem to the broader vision community and suggests that visual priors (scale invariance, 2D structure) are underexploited in current ARC research. Open questions include whether larger-scale image pretraining (e.g., on natural images) could push performance toward best-human levels, whether the overfitting observed at 66M parameters can be addressed with better regularization, and how this vision-centric approach might integrate with language-based methods for multimodal reasoning.
