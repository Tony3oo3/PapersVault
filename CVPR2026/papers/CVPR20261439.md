## Problem

Existing 3D motion reconstruction methods either depend on 3D MoCap supervision (limiting generalization to underrepresented motions like gymnastics and martial arts) or assume static cameras with uniform viewpoint coverage (as in MVLift). No prior framework can reconstruct globally consistent, world-coordinated 3D human motion and human-object interactions from in-the-wild Internet videos captured by dynamic cameras with limited view coverage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261439/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes single-view 2D human keypoint sequences (T×K×2, extracted via ViTPose), camera extrinsic trajectories (sequences of 4×3 matrices normalized to the initial frame, estimated via MegaSaM), and for HOI, object 2D keypoints (T×M×2, tracked via DELTA). It outputs SMPL parameters (root translation, global orientation, body pose) in world coordinates, and for HOI additionally a 6D object rotation, translation, and global scale per frame. Notably, no 3D supervision is used at any stage; all 3D structure is recovered purely from multi-view 2D consistency.

## Method

AnyLift is a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1, a conditional single-view 2D motion diffusion model (DDPM with a Transformer backbone) is trained on camera trajectories and epipolar lines as conditioning signals, using a hybrid data-source strategy that combines video-extracted global 2D poses with locally reprojected poses from GVHMR (with root translation decomposed out) to compensate for limited viewpoint coverage in Internet videos. Multi-view training data are then synthesized via score distillation sampling with epipolar line-matching losses between adjacent views. In Stage 2, a multi-view 2D motion diffusion model with cross-view attention layers is trained on the synthesized data to predict consistent multi-view 2D motions from a single-view input; 3D joints are recovered by minimizing multi-view reprojection error, and SMPL/object poses are fitted via VPoser and 6D rotation estimation respectively.

## Results

On the collected Internet gymnastics videos, AnyLift achieves J2D of 21.6 and J2D^C of 11.4, substantially outperforming the closest competitor MVLift (33.1, 17.0) and 3D-supervised methods like GVHMR (71.5, 18.8). On martial arts videos, AnyLift reaches J2D of 15.1 and J2D^C of 9.8, again the best across all baselines. For HOI reconstruction on BEHAVE under static cameras, AnyLift achieves T_root of 24.61 and O-MPJPE of 32.98 for boxes, outperforming VisTracker (143.59, 359.50) by a wide margin, and maintains similar accuracy under dynamic cameras (29.99, 33.96).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyLift outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both the gymnastics and martial arts Internet video datasets. Against the closest weakly-supervised competitor MVLift [18], AnyLift reduces J2D by 35% on gymnastics (21.6 vs. 33.1) and by 39% on martial arts (15.1 vs. 24.6), with similar gains on J2D^C and FID. The 3D-supervised methods WHAM [36] and GVHMR [34], trained on AMASS, perform poorly on these underrepresented motion categories (J2D of 88.6 and 71.5 on gymnastics respectively), confirming the generalization gap that motivates AnyLift's 2D-prior approach. SMPLify [2], which requires no training, is consistently the weakest baseline due to depth ambiguity in its optimization-based fitting. For HOI reconstruction on BEHAVE, AnyLift dominates VisTracker [45] by a large margin on object metrics (O-MPJPE of 32.98 vs. 359.50 for boxes), while also improving human motion accuracy (T_root of 24.61 vs. 51.72), demonstrating the benefit of jointly modeling human and object dynamics in a unified diffusion framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MVLift [18], which reformulated 3D motion reconstruction as multi-view 2D motion generation via diffusion, eliminating the need for 3D supervision. AnyLift extends this formulation in three key ways: (1) conditioning the diffusion model on camera trajectories and epipolar lines to handle dynamic cameras, (2) introducing a hybrid data-source training strategy that combines video-extracted 2D keypoints with reprojected local poses to overcome limited viewpoint coverage in Internet videos, and (3) unifying human motion and human-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction under the same two-stage pipeline. The approach also draws on the broader weakly-supervised lifting literature (MAS, ElePose) and multi-view generation ideas from 3D object synthesis, but departs from prior work by operating entirely in 2D keypoint space without requiring paired 3D training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: MoCap datasets underrepresent dynamic motions (gymnastics, martial arts) and HOI, while Internet videos contain such motions but feature dynamic cameras and limited views that break existing pipelines. The unified framework for both human motion and HOI under a single 2D diffusion formulation is a useful architectural contribution that could be extended to new motion categories or object types. Limitations include reliance on off-the-shelf 2D pose and camera estimators (ViTPose, MegaSaM) as separate preprocessing steps, HOI evaluation restricted to a few object categories, and the hybrid training strategy's dependence on a third-party 3D estimator (GVHMR) for local pose supervision. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more diverse object categories, and whether the camera-conditioning mechanism generalizes to highly non-rigid camera motions.
