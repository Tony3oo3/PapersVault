## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods applied to multimodal LLMs cause catastrophic alignment drift: the projector network's Jacobian becomes severely ill-conditioned (condition numbers exceeding 10^6), leading to indiscriminate refusal of both harmful and benign queries (SARR > 100% for gradient-based methods on LLaVA-7B). Prior work modifies modality-specific encoders or the language backbone but overlooks the projection layers that mediate cross-modal alignment, leaving the geometric coupling between vision and language representations unaddressed during forgetting.

## Input / Output

The method operates on LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B models that consume RGB images (processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder producing 1024-d visual embeddings) and text prompts, producing text responses. Unlearning is performed on forget-retain pairs (image, text, target output) from SafeEraser (28.8k safety-sensitive VQA/captioning/dialog samples) and MLLMU-Bench (celebrity entity forgetting at 5%, 10%, 15% deletion ratios). The projector maps 1024-d vision embeddings to 4096-d language input space through a two-layer MLP with 4096-d hidden dimension, and only this projector plus LoRA adapters (rank 32) on the language backbone are updated during unlearning.

## Method

SINEPROJECT replaces direct optimization of the projector weights with a bounded sinusoidal reparameterization: the pretrained weights W are frozen, and new trainable parameters ΔW are introduced such that the effective projector weights become W + sin(ΔW), constraining all perturbations to [−1, 1]. Theorem 3.1 proves that this reparameterization yields a uniformly bounded Jacobian (all partial derivatives contain multiplicative sin/cos factors in [−1,1]), in contrast to the standard MLP projector whose Jacobian columns grow unboundedly with weight magnitudes. During unlearning, the sine projector is trained alongside the chosen forgetting objective (PO+PD, GD+PD, or KL+PD) while the vision encoder remains frozen, requiring less than 1% additional parameters. Empirically, this reduces the second-layer Jacobian condition number from >10^6 to <10^3 (a 3–4 order-of-magnitude improvement) and keeps the Modality Integration Rate within the optimal [2.5, 3.0] range, preventing the modality decoupling that causes over-forgetting.

## Results

On the SafeEraser benchmark (safety unlearning), SINEPROJECT achieves 100% Refusal Rate (perfect forgetting) while reducing the Safe Answer Refusal Rate (SARR, over-forgetting) to 25.8% on LLaVA-7B and 25.1% on LLaVA-13B, compared to 30.3% and 27.3% for the SafeEraser (PO+PD) baseline, respectively. On MLLMU-Bench (privacy unlearning), SINEPROJECT(NPO) attains the best average score across all three deletion ratios: 62.1 (5%), 68.4 (10%), and 66.2 (15%), substantially outperforming NPO (51.8, 44.5, 53.5) and MMUnlearner (53.9, 52.4, 51.8). Geometrically, SINEPROJECT maintains Jacobian condition numbers below 10³ versus >10⁶ for SafeEraser, and keeps the Modality Integration Rate within the optimal [2.5, 3.0] range.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SafeEraser (PO+PD) on the safety benchmark, SINEPROJECT wins on every utility metric while matching or exceeding forgetting quality: on LLaVA-7B it reduces SARR from 30.3% to 25.8% (−4.5 pp) while improving ROUGE (+0.4) and GPT-Eval (+0.1); on LLaVA-13B it cuts SARR from 27.3% to 25.1% (−2.2 pp) and gains +1.2 ROUGE and +1.2 GPT-Eval. On the privacy benchmark (MLLMU-Bench), SINEPROJECT(NPO) dominates both NPO and MMUnlearner at all deletion ratios: at 10% deletion it achieves an average score of 68.4 versus 44.5 (NPO) and 52.4 (MMUnlearner), with the largest gap in Retain Cls (46.16 vs. 44.81 and 43.18) and Real-Celebrity Cls (56.41 vs. 47.89 and 47.26). The advantage grows with deletion ratio, indicating that geometric stabilization becomes increasingly critical as more knowledge is removed. Unregularized baselines (GD, KL, PO without Prompt Decoupling) suffer catastrophic over-forgetting with SARR ≈ 100%, a failure mode that SINEPROJECT entirely avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends machine unlearning from text-only LLMs to multimodal LLMs, building directly on SafeEraser's unlearning framework (PO+PD) and benchmark protocol. It departs from prior unlearning methods that modify language backbones or vision encoders by identifying the projection MLP as the critical bottleneck: the paper's theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) shows that standard projector Jacobians become ill-conditioned during unlearning, while sinusoidal reparameterization keeps them bounded. The sinusoidal modulation connects to the authors' prior work on weight conditioning for smooth optimization and to periodic activation functions in implicit neural representations, but the specific application to cross-modal projector stabilization during unlearning is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a diagnostic framework (Jacobian conditioning + MIR) and a practical, low-overhead fix (<1% parameters) for a failure mode—alignment drift causing indiscriminate refusal—that was previously observed empirically but not mechanistically explained. Key limitations include scope restricted to MLP projectors (not deeply integrated architectures like Flamingo's interleaved cross-attention), inability to resolve semantic entanglement beyond ~25% forgetting, and absence of certified unlearning guarantees against adversarial fine-tuning. Open questions include extending bounded modulation to attention-based fusion, composing geometric stabilization with certified defenses, and whether the projector-centric diagnosis generalizes to architectures where cross-modal interaction is distributed across many layers.
