## Problem

Synthetic datasets are essential for training stereo matching networks, yet it remains unclear which design choices—floating object density, background realism, material diversity, lighting, camera baseline—actually drive zero-shot generalization. Existing datasets change many factors simultaneously and do not open-source their generation code, making it impossible to isolate the contribution of individual parameters.

## Input / Output

The trained stereo networks consume a pair of RGB images (left and right views) rendered from a 3D scene and output a per-pixel disparity map, from which depth is derived. The synthetic training data consists of stereo pairs with ground-truth disparity extracted directly from the Blender rendering pipeline's depth buffer. Evaluation is performed in a strict zero-shot setting (no fine-tuning on real data) across benchmarks with varying disparity ranges, from small-baseline indoor scenes (Middlebury, ETH3D) to large-baseline driving and specular scenes (KITTI, Booster).

## Method

The paper builds a procedural stereo dataset generator on top of Infinigen's object/scene generators and the Blender Python API, adding a custom floating-object placement interface (raycast or bounding-box based) and a random lighting augmentation module. A systematic ablation varies one parameter at a time—floating object count (0–30), background furniture presence, object category, material set, lighting, stereo baseline range, and scene-type mix—generating 5,000 stereo pairs per setting and training RAFT-Stereo for 75k steps to measure zero-shot EPE. Key findings: realistic indoor backgrounds plus dense floating objects outperform either alone; diverse materials and a wide baseline range (0.04–0.4 m) are critical; and fully transparent/reflective materials should be excluded because current architectures degrade on diffuse regions. The best settings are combined into WMGStereo-150k (163k pairs across indoor-floating, dense-floating, and nature scenes), with generation cost reduced ~6× via greedy solver steps, OptiX denoising at 1024 samples, and 20 stereo rigs per scene for amortization.

## Results

Training DLNR on WMGStereo-150k alone achieves 3.76% 2px error on Middlebury 2014 (H), 6.72% on Middlebury 2021, 2.50% on ETH3D, 3.30% on KITTI-12, 4.54% on KITTI-15, and 9.09% on Booster(Q), outperforming the same model trained on a 600k-pair mixture of SceneFlow, CREStereo, TartanAir, and IRS by 28% on Middlebury and 25% on Booster. The dataset is highly sample-efficient: just 500 WMGStereo-150k examples achieve lower EPE on Middlebury than 100,000 CREStereo samples. On held-out benchmarks (DrivingStereo, Middlebury train), WMGStereo-150k still outperforms FSD by 27% in 3px error on DrivingStereo, confirming the findings generalize beyond the tuning set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the mixed-dataset baseline (DLNR-Mixed on SceneFlow+CREStereo+TartanAir+IRS), DLNR-WMGStereo-150k wins on every benchmark except ETH3D (tie at 2.50%), with the largest gains on Middlebury 2014 (H) (3.76 vs 5.21, −28%) and Booster (9.09 vs 12.17, −25%). Against FoundationStereo (the SOTA zero-shot method), WMGStereo-150k loses on all six benchmarks (e.g., 3.76 vs 1.10 on Middlebury 2014 H), though the gap narrows on KITTI-12 (3.30 vs 2.30) and the paper notes that combining WMGStereo-150k with FSD further improves FSD's own numbers. Against StereoAnywhere, DLNR-WMGStereo-150k wins on Middlebury 2014 (H) (3.76 vs 4.75) and KITTI-12 (3.30 vs 3.52) but loses on Middlebury 2021 (6.72 vs 5.71), ETH3D (2.50 vs 1.43), KITTI-15 (4.54 vs 3.79), and Booster (9.09 vs 9.01). Against individual prior datasets (SceneFlow, CREStereo, TartanAir, IRS), WMGStereo-150k dominates across all benchmarks with the largest margin over CREStereo (e.g., 3.76 vs 11.53 on Middlebury 2014 H). The same pattern holds for RAFT-Stereo and Selective-IGEV architectures, confirming the dataset benefit is architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Infinigen procedural generation system (Raistrick et al., 2023/2024) and the floating-object recipe established by SceneFlow and CREStereo, extending them with new placement generators, material controls, and lighting augmentation. It departs from FoundationStereo (Wen et al., 2025), which introduced many dataset features simultaneously without ablation, by systematically isolating each parameter's contribution to zero-shot performance. It also extends the earlier parameter study by Mayer et al. (2018) from 2D warped FlyingChairs-style data to full 3D ray-traced scenes, addressing the gap in understanding what makes modern procedural stereo datasets effective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic, open-source ablation of procedural stereo dataset design parameters, giving practitioners concrete guidance (e.g., realistic backgrounds + floating objects > either alone; wide baseline range is critical; denoised low-sample rendering is cost-effective). The WMGStereo-150k dataset and its generation code enable further research and co-design of data with architectures, addressing a key limitation of prior static datasets. Open questions remain around non-Lambertian surfaces (glass/metal still cause significant errors), the need for architecture-data co-design, and whether the findings transfer to video-consistent or multi-view stereo settings.
