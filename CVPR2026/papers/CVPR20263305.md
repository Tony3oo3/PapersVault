## Problem

Existing LiDAR scene generation frameworks treat all spatial regions uniformly during synthesis, ignoring the non-uniform semantic difficulty inherent in real-world scenes. This uniform assumption produces geometric artifacts and temporal instability in challenging regions such as distant surfaces, thin structures, and semantically ambiguous areas, limiting the reliability of generated 4D worlds for autonomous perception.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real LiDAR point clouds projected into range-view images of size H×W×2 (normalized depth and reflectance in [-1,1]) with a binary validity mask, at resolutions of 32×1024 (nuScenes) or 64×1024 (SemanticKITTI). A pretrained segmentation network is applied to the input to produce per-point Shannon entropy scores, from which the top-K highest-entropy points form a sparse uncertainty point cloud that drives generation. The output is a complete, temporally coherent LiDAR scene sequence (multiple frames generated simultaneously) along with the intermediate uncertainty range image used as a structural prior.

## Method

U4D performs two-stage 'hard-to-easy' diffusion generation: first, an unconditional diffusion model reconstructs high-entropy uncertainty regions in range-view format with an auxiliary binary mask loss to preserve spatial validity; second, a conditional diffusion model completes the full scene by concatenating the noisy full-frame input with the reconstructed uncertainty range image along the feature dimension. Spatial uncertainty is quantified via Shannon Entropy over the softmax outputs of a pretrained LiDAR segmentation model, selecting the top-K most ambiguous points as structural anchors. For temporal coherence, a Mixture of Spatio-Temporal (MoST) block decomposes intermediate features into parallel spatial and temporal convolution branches, then adaptively fuses them through a mixture-of-experts-style gating mechanism with stochastic noise perturbation during training, weighted by a variance-to-mean-squared regularization that prevents overemphasis on either modality. The entire pipeline uses a Curve U-Net backbone trained for 500K steps with AdamW and cosine annealing, performing 256 denoising steps at inference.

## Results

On nuScenes, U4D achieves the best FRD (223.96) and FPD (12.90) among all compared methods, surpassing the strongest baseline R2DM by ~12% in FRD and ~10% in FPD. On SemanticKITTI, U4D similarly leads with FRD 245.73 and FPD 10.92. For temporal coherence, U4D attains the lowest TTCE (2.63 at interval 3) and competitive CTC scores, and for downstream segmentation it reaches 76.4% mIoU (voxel, 50% labels) and 76.2% (fusion, 50% labels), outperforming all baselines across label budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U4D outperforms all six scene-generation baselines (LiDARGen, LiDM, R2DM, Text2LiDAR, UniScene, OpenDWM) on the primary geometric fidelity metrics FRD and FPD on both nuScenes and SemanticKITTI, with the closest competitor being R2DM (FRD 253.80 vs. 223.96 on nuScenes). The sole exception is MMD on nuScenes, where R2DM edges out U4D (0.48 vs. 0.53), and JSD on SemanticKITTI where R2DM is marginally better (0.03 vs. 0.04). For temporal consistency, U4D achieves the lowest TTCE across all frame intervals and beats LiDARCrafter on CTC at every interval (e.g., 4.41 vs. 4.81 at interval 4), though UniScene has a slightly lower CTC at interval 1 (0.90 vs. 0.97). In downstream semantic segmentation, U4D-generated data improves mIoU over both the R2DM augmentation baseline (e.g., 76.4% vs. 75.9% at 50% labels, voxel) and the semi-supervised baselines LaserMix and Mean Teacher across all label budgets and both voxel and fusion representations. For calibration (ECE), U4D reduces the error of RangeNet++ from 4.57% to 2.72% on nuScenes and from 4.01% to 3.31% on SemanticKITTI, confirming that uncertainty-aware generation yields better-calibrated perception models.

## Relation to Prior Work

U4D builds directly on the R2DM diffusion framework (Nakashima & Kurazume, ICRA 2024), adopting its Curve U-Net architecture and range-view representation for LiDAR generation, and draws on mixture-of-experts gating (LiMoE, Xu et al., CVPR 2025) for its MoST block's adaptive fusion mechanism. Conceptually, it departs from the uniform-generation assumption shared by LiDARGen, LiDM, R2DM, and Text2LiDAR by introducing a two-stage 'hard-to-easy' paradigm that first reconstructs high-entropy regions before completing the full scene. It also bridges two previously separate literatures—uncertainty quantification in 3D perception (SalsaNext, Calib3D) and generative LiDAR modeling—by using estimated semantic uncertainty as an explicit structural prior for diffusion-based synthesis, a combination the authors claim is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The uncertainty-aware generation paradigm offers a principled way to allocate generative capacity where perception is most unreliable, with demonstrated downstream benefits in segmentation mIoU and calibration ECE that go beyond pure generation fidelity. However, the inference cost (8.9 s/frame) remains higher than single-frame baselines, and the paper itself flags latent diffusion as a path toward greater efficiency. Open questions include whether the entropy-based uncertainty signal generalizes to other sensor modalities or to open-vocabulary settings, and whether the two-stage pipeline can be unified into a single forward pass without sacrificing the 'hard-to-easy' inductive bias.
