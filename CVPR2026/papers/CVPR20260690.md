## Problem

GRPO for generative models requires large sampling groups for reliable advantage estimation, but this creates a prohibitive computational bottleneck. The paper identifies a 'reward clustering phenomenon' where most trajectories collapse near the group-mean reward, yielding near-zero normalized advantages and negligible gradient contributions while consuming the same compute as informative samples. The central question is how to maximize trajectory diversity and optimization signal without paying the full cost of a large group.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as conditioning input and produces an RGB image via a pre-trained diffusion (SD v1.4) or rectified flow (SD 3.5-M) backbone. During training, the prompt conditions a group of latent trajectories that are denoised over T SDE/ODE steps; reward models (PickScore, HPSv2.1, CLIP) score the decoded images to compute group-relative advantages. The framework operates entirely in the model's latent space for pruning decisions, only decoding to pixel space for reward computation at checkpoints and at the terminal step.

## Method

Pro-GRPO introduces a dynamic 'Expand-and-Prune' framework that decouples exploration breadth from optimization cost. At pre-specified denoising checkpoints, each active latent is projected to its terminal state via a single-step ODE update, decoded through the VAE, and scored by the reward model to obtain a proxy reward. Optimal Variance Filtering (OVF) then selects a high-variance survivor subset by maximizing within-set reward variance, early-terminating the remaining trajectories so they incur no further denoising steps. The initial group is expanded to G_max (e.g., 48) to maximize diversity, then progressively pruned across checkpoints down to K survivors (e.g., 12), so the backpropagation cost scales with K rather than G_max. This in-process pruning replaces post-sampling filtering, eliminating the wasted computation on reward-clustered trajectories while preserving the 'less-is-more' benefit of a small, high-variance training set.

## Results

On flow-based SD 3.5-M (DrawBench), Pro-GRPO achieves a PickScore of 24.008 (+0.686 over Flow-GRPO's 23.322), Aesthetic 6.046, and ImageReward 1.397, while delivering a 1.26× training speedup. On diffusion-based SD-v1.4 (HPSv2), Pro-GRPO reaches HPSv2.1 of 0.391 vs. DanceGRPO's 0.369 and ImageReward of 1.114 vs. 1.079. The lightweight Pro-GRPO-Flash variant achieves a 1.41× speedup while still surpassing the baseline (PickScore 23.722 vs. 23.322). On the unseen GenEval benchmark, Pro-GRPO improves the Overall score to 0.726 (vs. 0.719) with the largest gain on Position (0.323 vs. 0.288).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Flow-GRPO on SD 3.5-M, Pro-GRPO wins on every reported metric across both DrawBench and HPSv2: PickScore improves by +0.686 (24.008 vs. 23.322), ImageReward by +0.045 (1.397 vs. 1.298 on DrawBench; 1.509 vs. 1.463 on HPSv2), and OOD HPSv2.1 by +0.006 (0.322 vs. 0.316), all while achieving a 1.26× training speedup. The Flash variant (G_max=24→12) narrows the gap slightly (PickScore 23.722) but still beats Flow-GRPO and delivers a 1.41× speedup, isolating the benefit of in-process pruning. Against DanceGRPO on SD-v1.4, Pro-GRPO consistently outperforms under both single-objective (HPSv2.1: 0.391 vs. 0.369) and multi-objective (HPSv2.1+CLIP: 0.596 vs. 0.575) settings, with OOD ImageReward gains of +0.035 (1.114 vs. 1.079) and +0.083 (1.140 vs. 1.057) respectively. On the held-out GenEval benchmark, Pro-GRPO surpasses Flow-GRPO on all fine-grained categories, with the largest margin on Position (+0.035), indicating improved spatial compositional reasoning. Pro-GRPO does not lose to any baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO lineage from DeepSeekMath [32] into visual generative models, building directly on Flow-GRPO [21] (which reformulates flow matching as an SDE for stochastic policy exploration) and DanceGRPO [46] (which adapts GRPO to diffusion denoising). The key departure is identifying a previously overlooked inefficiency—reward clustering within GRPO sampling groups—and proposing Optimal Variance Filtering (OVF) as a selection criterion, then elevating it to Pro-GRPO, a dynamic in-process pruning framework that decouples exploration breadth from optimization cost via an expand-and-prune schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a smaller, high-variance trajectory subset outperforms a larger unfiltered group challenges the implicit assumption in GRPO that more samples always yield better advantage estimation, offering a principled way to reduce compute by 26–41% while improving alignment quality. The expand-and-prune strategy is broadly applicable to any SDE/ODE-based generative model trained with group-relative RL, making it a practical tool for the growing T2I alignment literature. Open questions include whether OVF's greedy variance-maximizing heuristic can be replaced by learned or adaptive criteria, whether the fixed pruning checkpoint schedule generalizes across model scales, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where trajectory costs are even higher.
