## Problem

Deterministic point embeddings in text-video retrieval implicitly assume one-to-one semantic correspondence, failing to capture the pervasive many-to-many ambiguity where a single video can be validly described by multiple distinct captions. Existing probabilistic embedding methods address this but suffer from two critical flaws: they impose rigid uncertainty priors (e.g., KL divergence regularizers or geometric inclusion constraints) that bias representations, and they decompose similarity into independent additive terms that ignore the interaction between distance and uncertainty, treating all pairs with the same mean separation identically regardless of their confidence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12 video frames (224×224) for MSR-VTT/DiDeMo or 32 frames for ActivityNet, plus text queries up to 50 tokens, processed by a CLIP-ViP backbone (ViT-B/32 or ViT-B/16). Rather than producing a single point embedding per modality, lightweight uncertainty heads attached to each encoder output a Gaussian distribution—mean μ ∈ ℝ^D and diagonal covariance Σ—per sample. The final output is a scalar alignment score S_align = A + B + C that combines semantic attraction, centroid misalignment penalty, and joint-uncertainty penalty, used directly for ranking in both text-to-video and video-to-text retrieval.

## Method

GraviAlign reformulates cross-modal alignment through a physics-inspired Semantic Gravitational Interaction (SGI) integral that is analytically intractable due to its inverse-distance kernel. The key contribution is decoupling this integral into three closed-form, O(D)-cost components: Term A (Semantic Attraction) computes gravitational pull between Gaussian centroids weighted by an uncertainty-derived 'semantic mass' m = exp(−λ·H_norm/(1+H_norm)) and Mahalanobis distance; Term B penalizes centroid misalignment via the squared Mahalanobis distance (short-range 'docking'); and Term C penalizes the log-determinant of the joint covariance (rejecting 'fuzzy-fuzzy' false positives). Each factor carries independent veto power—a low score from either attraction or overlap suffices to reject a mismatch. Training combines standard InfoNCE contrastive loss with an SGI structural loss applied only to positive pairs, requiring no external KL regularizers since the gradient formulation naturally prevents variance collapse.

## Results

On MSR-VTT, GraviAlign achieves 55.8% R@1 (ViT-B/16) and 52.4% R@1 (ViT-B/32) for Text-to-Video retrieval, improving over the CLIP-ViP baseline by 1.6% and 2.3% respectively. On DiDeMo, it reaches 52.3% R@1 (ViT-B/16), a 1.8% gain over baseline. On ActivityNet Captions, GraviAlign sets a new SOTA at 56.0% R@1 (ViT-B/16), a 2.6% absolute improvement. The method also delivers consistent gains on R@5, R@10, and Video-to-Text retrieval across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraviAlign consistently outperforms the deterministic CLIP-ViP baseline [53] by 1.6–2.6% R@1 across all three datasets and both backbone sizes, confirming that the probabilistic alignment score provides a fundamental improvement over point embeddings. Against the strongest probabilistic baseline UATVR [12], GraviAlign achieves a substantial 5.0% R@1 gain on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16: 55.8% vs. 50.8%), attributed to its closed-form geometric overlap terms that exploit full covariance structure rather than stochastic sampling. Compared to the latest deterministic method NarVid [18], GraviAlign wins by 3.1% R@1 on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16) but is slightly behind by 1.1% on DiDeMo (ViT-B/16: 52.3% vs. 53.4%), suggesting NarVid's frame-level caption augmentation provides a complementary advantage on that dataset. Against NeighborRetr [28], which addresses hub centrality bias, GraviAlign leads by 6.3% R@1 on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/32), demonstrating that uncertainty-aware alignment is more effective than hub-balancing alone. The method also surpasses KDProR [61] by 7.1% R@1 on MSR-VTT (ViT-B/16), further validating the physics-inspired decomposition over knowledge-decoupling approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

GraviAlign extends the probabilistic embedding line initiated by PCME [9] and UATVR [12], which model each modality as a Gaussian distribution to capture semantic uncertainty. However, it departs sharply from prior probabilistic methods by rejecting their rigid uncertainty priors (e.g., ProLIP's inclusion hierarchy) and their failure to couple distance with uncertainty in the similarity metric. The framework builds on the CLIP-ViP [53] backbone and UATVR's lightweight uncertainty heads, but replaces sampling-based or additively-decomposed alignment scores with a closed-form, physics-inspired decomposition into semantic attraction (mass-weighted Mahalanobis distance) and geometric overlap (log Gaussian intersection).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, interpretable, and self-regularizing alternative to ad-hoc probabilistic alignment scores, showing that explicitly modeling the distance-uncertainty interaction yields consistent 1.3–2.6% R@1 gains across three benchmarks without external regularizers or sampling. The gravitational analogy, while creative, ultimately reduces to standard Gaussian overlap terms, so the broader impact depends on whether this framing generalizes beyond TVR to other cross-modal or multimodal alignment tasks. The authors note future exploration in multimodal large models, but the current scope is limited to fixed-backbone retrieval with diagonal covariances, leaving open questions about full-covariance extensions, scalability to very large corpora, and whether the physics-inspired decomposition offers advantages over simpler learned scoring functions.
