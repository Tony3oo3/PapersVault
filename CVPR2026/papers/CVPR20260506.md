## Problem

End-to-end joint training of a feature extractor and MIL network on gigapixel WSIs (100,000×100,000 pixels at 20×) is infeasible on a single 24 GB commodity GPU, forcing existing methods to freeze the extractor and train MIL on static features. This introduces a persistent semantic gap between frozen features and downstream classification objectives that limits performance regardless of pretraining strategy. No prior method achieves genuine end-to-end MIL training on 20× WSIs within single-GPU memory constraints.

## Input / Output

Input consists of WSIs at 20× magnification partitioned into 256×256 non-overlapping patches, from which a pretrained extractor (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/32) produces d-dimensional frozen instance features. During training, a small stratified subset of patches (C clusters × M samples, where CM ≪ N_i) is additionally fed through a trainable copy of the extractor to produce end-to-end features, while a multi-head MLP translator maps the full frozen feature set into the end-to-end space. At inference, only the cached frozen features pass through the lightweight translator (27 s per WSI vs. 120 min for re-extraction), and the EMA MIL network outputs a single slide-level class label (binary for NSCLC, 3-class for STAD).

## Method

FBTA constructs three complementary feature-bag views per WSI: an end-to-end pseudo-bag (from a KMeans-stratified patch subset processed by a trainable extractor), a frozen bag (all patches through a frozen pretrained extractor), and a translated bag (a K-head MLP with residual connections mapping frozen features to approximate the end-to-end space). All three bags are fed into a shared MIL network, and training minimizes a hybrid loss combining cross-entropy classification on all views, an EMA-teacher cosine-similarity consistency loss (curriculum-weighted by epoch fraction), and an L2 regression loss that trains each translator head against the EMA extractor's output on the sampled patches. The three networks (trainable extractor, MIL, translator) are jointly updated each step, while EMA copies are refreshed per epoch. At inference, the translator converts cached frozen features into the end-to-end feature space, yielding a 200× speedup over re-extraction while preserving the benefits of joint optimization; a Lipschitz-continuity argument in the supplement justifies using translated features as a surrogate for true end-to-end features.

## Results

On the TCGA-STAD three-class task with ResNet-50, FBTA boosts ABMIL accuracy from 42.5% to 58.3% (+15.8%) and F1 from 34.0% to 57.1% (+23.1%), and improves MambaMIL accuracy from 55.0% to 64.2% (+9.2%). On NSCLC-Shot50 with ResNet-50, FBTA raises ABMIL accuracy by 13.1% (68.4%→81.5%) and MambaMIL accuracy by 4.1% (79.0%→83.1%). Across all four datasets and two extractors (ResNet-50, ViT-B/32), FBTA delivers consistent gains of 1–16% in accuracy over frozen-feature baselines, with the largest improvements on the most challenging fine-grained STAD task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FBTA is evaluated as a plug-and-play enhancement on top of three MIL architectures (ABMIL, TransMIL, MambaMIL) using frozen ImageNet-pretrained features as the vanilla baseline. On the most challenging STAD dataset, FBTA improves ABMIL accuracy by 15.8% and MambaMIL by 9.2%, effectively closing the gap between the classical ABMIL and the state-of-the-art MambaMIL. Against Local Learning (Zhang et al., MICCAI 2022), the closest algorithmic alternative for end-to-end WSI training, FBTA achieves 10% higher AUC and 4.7% higher accuracy on NSCLC-Shot20 while running 11× faster (27 s vs. 306 s per epoch) and using 6× less GPU memory (4 GB vs. 24 GB). Direct fine-tuning, Adapter, and LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning all fail with out-of-memory errors even on 5× downsampled WSIs with ResNet-34, whereas FBTA completes training comfortably on a single 24 GB GPU. When combined with the DTFD-AFS data augmentation method, FBTA adds a further 4.6% AUC on NSCLC-Shot20 and 13.3% on NSCLC-Shot50 (ABMIL), demonstrating that its gains are complementary to existing augmentation strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard frozen-extractor MIL paradigm (CLAM, ABMIL, TransMIL, MambaMIL) by introducing a three-view bag strategy that enables joint optimization of the feature extractor and MIL network on a single 24 GB GPU. It builds on pseudo-bag sampling from DBPAug [10] and the locally supervised training idea of Local Learning [38], but departs from both by achieving true end-to-end gradient flow through a trainable extractor while using a lightweight MLP-based feature translator to bridge frozen and end-to-end feature spaces at inference. The EMA-based consistency losses draw on self-supervised learning techniques (SimSiam, DINO) to stabilize training when the feature distribution shifts at every iteration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FBTA addresses a practical bottleneck that has constrained the entire WSI-MIL community: the inability to jointly optimize the feature extractor and MIL head on commodity hardware, which forces a semantic gap between frozen features and downstream objectives. The plug-and-play design (compatible with any MIL architecture, any extractor, and existing pretraining/augmentation pipelines) makes it broadly adoptable, and the 100× training speedup plus 200× inference speedup over full-WSI end-to-end training are practically significant. However, validation is limited to two TCGA datasets in few-shot settings with only two extractors, leaving open whether the gains persist at larger scale, on more heterogeneous tissue types, or for downstream tasks like survival prediction and tumor microenvironment analysis that the authors themselves flag as future work.
