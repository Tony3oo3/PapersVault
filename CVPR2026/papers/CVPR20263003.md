## Problem

LLMs possess rich implicit semantic knowledge about human activities from natural language pretraining, yet sEMG signals are continuous, non-linguistic time series that cannot be directly interpreted by them. Existing approaches either fine-tune LLMs on limited sEMG data (risking catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge) or remain confined to signal-level modeling without leveraging LLM reasoning. The paper addresses how to translate sEMG signals into a language-like representation that an LLM can process while keeping its pre-trained weights entirely unchanged.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-channel sEMG signals (T×C) divided into overlapping segments of fixed length t, which are discretized into sequences of K=512 codebook tokens with dimension D=5120 (matching LLaMA-13B's embedding space). A residual-based adaptive allocation mechanism assigns more tokens to high-information-density transient regions and fewer to steady-state phases, producing variable-length token sequences per segment. The output is a single activity class label (17 classes on GRABMyo, 50 on NinaPro DB2), predicted by the LLM from an instruction prompt containing the token sequence.

## Method

The LLM-sEMG framework operates in two stages. Stage 1 trains an sEMG-oriented VQ-VAE under an iterated-learning protocol inspired by the Lewis Signaling Game: the encoder and codebook are preserved across generations as 'knowledge carriers' while the decoder is periodically reinitialized to simulate new learners, with a warm-up step to stabilize the new decoder before joint training resumes. Human linguistic biases—Zipf's law (via Jensen-Shannon divergence between empirical and theoretical token frequency distributions) and context sensitivity (via token co-occurrence correlation loss and inter-generational Frobenius-norm preservation)—are explicitly regularized to accelerate convergence toward natural-language-like structure. Stage 2 applies LoRA to LLaMA-13B, keeping all pre-trained weights frozen, so the LLM learns to map the emergent 'sEMG language' token sequences to activity labels via cross-entropy loss without degrading its prior semantic knowledge.

## Results

On the GRABMyo dataset (17 activities), LLM-sEMG achieves 95.14% overall accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline STET (90.76%) by 4.38 percentage points. On the NinaPro DB2 dataset (50 hand/wrist activities), LLM-sEMG reaches 93.17% overall accuracy, surpassing STET (89.13%) by 4.04 points. The method consistently leads across all sub-categories, with the largest gains on dynamic gestures (e.g., 94.50% multi-finger on GRABMyo vs. 89.93% for STET). Ablation studies confirm that iterated learning (+15.55 pp), human-linguistic biases (+7.79 pp), and residual-based adaptive tokenization (+2.54 pp) each contribute substantially to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-sEMG outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets across every activity sub-category. The largest margin is against the strongest baseline STET [30]: +4.38 pp on GRABMyo overall (95.14% vs. 90.76%) and +4.04 pp on NinaPro DB2 (93.17% vs. 89.13%). Among the remaining baselines, Informer [103] and LST-EMG-Net [101] are the next strongest on GRABMyo (87.71% and 85.31% overall), while JASNN [29] (spiking neural network) and BeyondVision [85] (dynamic segmentation) trail by roughly 9–10 pp. On NinaPro DB2, the gap to the runner-up STET is most pronounced on Exercise B (+5.31 pp) and Exercise A (+4.77 pp), indicating the LLM-based approach benefits particularly from the higher discriminative demand of dynamic hand exercises. Notably, LLM-sEMG also achieves the lowest standard deviation (0.01) across all categories on both datasets, suggesting more consistent per-subject performance than the baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on using LLMs as recognizers for non-linguistic signals (Qu et al., CVPR 2024, for video action recognition) to the sEMG domain, but departs from the common strategy of fine-tuning LLMs to accept raw non-linguistic inputs. Instead, it draws on the Lewis Signaling Game and iterated learning literature (Rita et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2024) to evolve a discrete token vocabulary that mimics natural language structure, guided by Zipf's law and context-sensitivity priors. The VQ-VAE discretization builds directly on van den Oord et al. (2017), while the residual-based adaptive token allocation borrows from anomaly-detection literature on reconstruction error as an information-density proxy. The overall positioning is that sEMG activity recognition is an existing task, but the method of routing it through an LLM's linguistic prior—without altering pre-trained weights—is the novel contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a frozen 13B LLM can outperform purpose-built sEMG classifiers by ~4% when signals are translated into a linguistically structured token sequence, suggesting that LLMs' implicit semantic knowledge of actions is genuinely transferable to physiological signal interpretation. However, practical deployment is constrained by the compute cost of LLaMA-13B inference, the user-specific evaluation protocol (no cross-subject generalization is tested), and the limited dataset coverage (two benchmarks, 17 and 50 classes). Open questions include whether smaller LLMs retain the benefit, whether the emergent 'sEMG language' is truly interpretable or merely a learned code, and whether the iterated-learning mechanism scales to larger activity vocabularies or multi-day recordings without the 15% accuracy collapse seen when it is removed.
