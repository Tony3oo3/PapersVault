## Problem

Bi-temporal remote sensing change detection is fundamentally limited to two observations, yet mainstream approaches either perform static feature differencing (Siamese CNNs/Transformers) or reconstruct dense pseudo-video sequences that introduce redundant processing of unchanged pixels. Neither paradigm explicitly models the temporal ordering or sparsity of surface changes, leaving the 'when' dimension of change unaddressed under constrained temporal cues.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261629/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pair of co-registered bi-temporal remote sensing images (same geographic location, different acquisition times) at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The ANN backbone (ViT-SAM Large with LoRA adapters on qkv modules) extracts dense semantic features, while the SNN branch processes a sparse binary spike tensor of shape B×T×2C×H×W (T pseudo-temporal steps, 2C channels for on/off polarity). The final output is a pixel-wise binary change detection map produced by the ANN decoder from the fused feature representation.

## Method

SpikeAdapter introduces a lightweight adapter that converts bi-temporal radiometric differences into sparse, geographically-aware spike sequences via the GSI-P module, which maps change magnitude to time-to-first-spike delays (fast changes fire early, slow changes fire later) while modulating delays with a spectral-structural consistency map and suppressing pseudo-changes through a learnable correction network. Polarity modeling separates enhancement (on) and attenuation (off) events, and a triangular temporal smoothing kernel preserves sparsity while bridging isolated spikes. The STSpikeFuse module then bridges the spiking and semantic domains: Soft-TfS produces a differentiable first-spike timing map, TIMR accumulates membrane potentials with per-channel learnable exponential decay to capture change duration, and a cross-domain attention mechanism (with temporal bias from Soft-TfS) plus a learnable gating convolution adaptively injects temporal saliency into the ViT-SAM semantic features before the decoder produces the change map.

## Results

On LEVIR-CD, SpikeAdapter at 512 resolution achieves the best F1 of 93.08% and IoU of 87.05%, surpassing the previous best (TTP, F1=92.1%) by ~1%. On CLCD, SpikeAdapter achieves F1=82.96% and IoU=70.88%, outperforming the strongest prior method (Change3D, F1=78.03%) by nearly 5 points. On SYSU-CD, SpikeAdapter reaches F1=84.11% and IoU=72.58%, exceeding the next-best CD-Lamba (F1=82.66%) by 1.45 points. Across all three benchmarks, SpikeAdapter consistently achieves the highest F1 and IoU among all 28 compared baselines spanning CNN, Transformer, Mamba, and video-interpolation paradigms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeAdapter wins on all three benchmarks against all 28 baselines. On LEVIR-CD, it outperforms the strongest Transformer baseline TTP (F1 92.1%) by ~1% and the best video-interpolation method DWTCD-Net (F1 92.06%) by ~1%, while also surpassing Mamba-based CDMamba (90.75%) by over 2.3%. On CLCD, the margin is largest: SpikeAdapter (F1 82.96%) exceeds the previous best Change3D (78.03%) by nearly 5 points and the strongest CNN STMTNet (73.92%) by over 9 points, indicating that sparse spiking temporal encoding is particularly effective for the texturally complex cropland domain. On SYSU-CD, SpikeAdapter (F1 84.11%) beats the top Mamba method CD-Lamba (82.66%) by 1.45% and the best CNN AFCF3D-Net (82.15%) by ~2%, confirming consistent gains in multi-class change scenarios. No baseline matches or exceeds SpikeAdapter on any of the three datasets for either F1 or IoU.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Siamese differencing paradigm (CNN, Transformer, and Mamba-based) and the pseudo-video interpolation line (P2V-CD, Change3D) by repurposing spiking neural network concepts—specifically LIF dynamics and time-to-first-spike coding—from neuromorphic computing into the remote sensing change detection setting. Rather than building on a prior SNN-for-change-detection lineage (none exists in the cited literature), it introduces GSI-P as a differentiable bridge that converts radiometric differences into sparse spike sequences, and STSpikeFuse as a decoupled ANN–SNN fusion mechanism. The ANN backbone (ViT-SAM with LoRA) follows the recent trend of leveraging foundation-model pretraining for remote sensing, but the temporal encoding via SNNs is the paper's distinctive architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural gap: bi-temporal change detection lacks explicit temporal ordering, and dense interpolation methods introduce redundant computation over unchanged regions. The sparse spike representation offers an interpretable 'when did the change occur' signal that complements the ANN's 'what changed' semantics, which could inform downstream tasks like change classification or temporal reasoning. However, the approach is evaluated only on three datasets with a single backbone, and the SNN branch adds architectural complexity whose marginal benefit over a well-tuned ANN temporal module (e.g., a learned positional encoding or a small temporal MLP) is not fully isolated. Open questions include whether the SNN formulation generalizes to multi-temporal sequences, whether the sparsity assumption holds for gradual land-cover transitions, and whether the computational overhead of the SNN encoder is justified at scale.
