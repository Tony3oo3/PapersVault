## Problem

Sequential unlearning in large vision-language models distorts shared multimodal representations, causing spurious associations that lead to two failure modes: irrelevant refusals where new forget tasks overwrite prior refusal patterns, and over-refusal where retain queries are mistakenly rejected. Existing continual unlearning methods (EWC, LwF, SCRUB, etc.) degrade general utility and cannot distinguish valid refusal contexts as the number of accumulated forget categories grows, making precise, context-aware refusal generation across sequential tasks an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text instruction as input, processed through a frozen ViT-g/14 visual encoder and a Vicuna-7B or LLaMA-2-7B language model. For forget categories, the output is a context-aware refusal response semantically aligned with the specific visual-linguistic concept combination (e.g., refusing to describe weapons rather than a generic 'I cannot answer'); for retain data, the model produces its original text response. The framework operates over a sequence of 16 unlearning tasks (12 question-answering + 4 classification), each introducing new forget categories with 20 visual and 20 textual concept descriptions per category.

## Method

CORE decomposes each forget category into granular visual and textual concepts (generated via GPT-4) and grounds refusal behavior in these concepts through three mechanisms. First, concept modules produce interpretable activations supervised by cosine similarity to a pretrained EVA encoder, and a concept modulator reweights them via cross-entropy classification to suppress irrelevant concepts and emphasize category-specific combinations. Second, a mixture of 20 specialized refusers (connection modules initialized from MiniGPT) generates concept-aware refusals, with a router whose activation is guided by a contrastive loss that aligns router outputs with conceptually similar previous tasks and differentiates from unrelated ones, enabling refuser reuse and adaptation. Third, at inference time, the refuser mixture contribution is scaled by a relevance score β ∈ [0,1] computed against all previously unlearned tasks, suppressing over-refusal on retain queries. Training follows a two-stage strategy: concept modules and modulator are trained first (L_con + L_mod), then the router and refusers are trained (L_ce + L_ref), with prototypes from earlier tasks replayed to prevent catastrophic forgetting of prior categories.

## Results

On the Vicuna-based LVLM after 16 sequential unlearning tasks (Last), CORE achieves 96.54% Specificity (general capability preservation), 88.02% Answer Rate on retain data, 90.67% Context-aware Refusal Rate on forget data, and only 3.74% Refusal Gap (ΔRR), indicating highly contextually appropriate refusals. On the LLaMA-2-based LVLM, CORE similarly leads with 97.26% S, 84.41% AR, 84.54% CRR, and 6.95% ΔRR. Conventional continual learning methods (EWC, LwF, GMM, EProj, SCRUB) collapse dramatically, with AR dropping to 8.84–43.12% and CRR to 41.01–59.87%, while also exhibiting high ΔRR (26.08–37.43), confirming indiscriminate refusal behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORE outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric for both the Vicuna and LLaMA-2 LVLMs. Against the strongest competitor O³ [14], CORE gains +5.26% AR, +17.64% CRR, and reduces ΔRR by 5.29 points (Last, Vicuna), and +17.68% AR, +7.80% CRR (Last, LLaMA-2), demonstrating that concept-grounded refusal generation is substantially more precise than O³'s parameter-subspace approach. MoEAdapter [54], while preserving general capability (94.46% S), suffers a 33.77% AR gap and 37.85% CRR gap versus CORE, with a ΔRR of 31.98 indicating widespread irrelevant refusals. The five conventional continual learning/unlearning methods (EWC, LwF, GMM, EProj, SCRUB) are uniformly inferior: their AR ranges from 8.84% to 43.12% and CRR from 41.01% to 59.87% (Last), with ΔRR between 26.08 and 37.43, confirming that direct parameter updates cause catastrophic distortion of shared vision-language representations and produce contextually misaligned or over-refusal responses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the concept bottleneck model literature (Sun et al., ICLR 2025) by repurposing concept modules not for interpretability of predictions but for grounding refusal behavior in unlearning. It extends the continual unlearning line initiated by O3 (Gao et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced small parameter subsets for LLM unlearning, to the multimodal setting with a mixture-of-experts refuser architecture inspired by MoEAdapter (Yu et al., CVPR 2024). The key departure from prior continual unlearning methods (EWC, LwF, SCRUB) is avoiding direct parameter updates that distort shared representations; instead, CORE decomposes vision-language pairs into interpretable visual and textual concepts and routes refusal generation through specialized experts, with a novel concept modulator to handle semantic overlap across growing concept sets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The concept-grounded framing of unlearning offers a more interpretable and controllable alternative to black-box parameter editing, which is increasingly important as AI regulations (GDPR, CCPA) drive sequential deletion requests in deployed LVLMs. The inference-time refusal calibration mechanism addresses a practically critical failure mode—over-refusal on retain queries—that prior methods handle poorly, and the routing scheme for reusing vs. adapting refusers provides a scalable strategy for long unlearning sequences. Limitations include dependence on GPT-4 for concept description generation, evaluation on only 16 unlearning tasks, and the added architectural complexity (concept modules, modulator, 20 refusers, router) that may be difficult to deploy in production. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of sequential tasks, whether concept descriptions can be generated without a powerful external LLM, and whether the framework extends to audio or video modalities.
