## Problem

Existing medical AI systems predominantly rely on single-modal data (ECG, CXR, or laboratory results in isolation), limiting their ability to capture the cross-modal complementarity essential for diagnosing complex comorbidities. While multimodal LLMs have advanced in general domains, their application to medicine remains confined to single-modality physiological description rather than interpretable, multi-disease diagnostic reasoning that integrates heterogeneous clinical evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263724/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes three patient-aligned modalities from the same hospitalization encounter: raw ECG waveforms (N×L time series), chest X-ray images (C×H×W), and structured laboratory panels (50 indicators grouped into 7 physiological categories), alongside a natural-language prompt. Output is a structured long-text response (≥300 words) containing a Chain of Evidence reasoning block that explicitly links cross-modal findings to specific diseases, followed by a final <answer> tag listing the diagnosed conditions from a fixed set of 7 diseases (Coronary Artery Disease, Acute Renal Failure, Hypertension, Atrial Fibrillation, Pneumonia, Diabetes Mellitus, Sepsis) with ICD-10 subtypes.

## Method

MedTVT-R1 pairs modality-specific encoders (ECGFM-KED, ViT-B/16, Symile) with Dense projectors that map all features to a shared 2048-dim space, then introduces a Modality Perception Layer (MPL) comprising a Cyclic Multi-Head Attention mechanism—where each modality feature cyclically serves as Query, Key, and Value to capture cross-modal dependencies—and a Contribution-Aware Operator that applies sigmoid-gated, context-dependent weights to adaptively scale each modality's contribution per diagnostic scenario. The fused features replace placeholder tokens in a text prompt fed to LLaMA 3.2-1B (LoRA rank 8). Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) pre-training on physiological-level QA pairs to establish single-modality comprehension, (2) supervised fine-tuning on disease-level QA pairs with Chain-of-Evidence supervision to learn cross-modal diagnostic reasoning, and (3) reinforcement fine-tuning via GRPO with a novel Jaccard Reward that scores set-overlap between predicted and ground-truth disease labels, combined with a Format Reward for structural compliance—eliminating the need for a separate critic model while directly optimizing multi-disease prediction accuracy.

## Results

On the MedTVT-QA test set (375 samples), MedTVT-R1 achieves an F1 score of 0.5190 and AUC of 0.6554 for multi-disease diagnosis, substantially outperforming the best medical-specific baseline LLaVA-Med (F1 0.2075, AUC 0.5318) and the best general-purpose baseline Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (F1 0.1995, AUC 0.5000). In natural language generation, MedTVT-R1 scores 0.3536 METEOR and 0.2295 ROUGE, roughly 1.5× the best medical baseline (LLaVA-Med: 0.2358 METEOR, 0.1637 ROUGE). Ablations confirm that removing physiological-level pre-training drops F1 to 0.4672 and removing GRPO-based RFT drops it to 0.4992.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedTVT-R1 outperforms all 10 baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest medical-specific baseline LLaVA-Med, MedTVT-R1 improves F1 from 0.2075 to 0.5190 (+150%), AUC from 0.5318 to 0.6554 (+23%), and METEOR from 0.2358 to 0.3536 (+50%). Against the best general-purpose model Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, the F1 gap is even larger (0.1995 → 0.5190, +160%), and recall jumps from 0.1397 to 0.5908, indicating that general-purpose MLLMs largely fail to identify the correct disease set. Notably, Molmo-7B-O-0924 collapses to near-chance on clinical efficacy (F1 0.0398, AUC 0.5001), suggesting that scale alone without medical-domain alignment is insufficient. The ablation variants (w/o PT: F1 0.4672; w/o RFT: F1 0.4992) confirm that both the physiological pre-training stage and the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning contribute meaningfully to the final performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-for-medical-diagnosis line of work, which has largely been confined to single-modal settings (ECG-only, CXR-only, or text-only) or to simple multi-modal classification without interpretable reasoning. It departs from QoQ-Med (which aggregates heterogeneous datasets without patient-level tri-modal alignment) and DrVD-Bench (which excludes ECG and structured lab data) by constructing the first patient-/encounter-aligned ECG+CXR+LAB instruction dataset with Chain-of-Evidence supervision. The training strategy builds on DeepSeek-R1's GRPO framework, which has been applied to medical image analysis (Med-R1, MedVLM-R1) but not previously to multi-disease diagnosis requiring joint time-series, visual, and tabular reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedTVT-R1 and MedTVT-QA establish a practical template for tri-modal medical MLLMs, and the Jaccard Reward offers a simple, verifiable signal for multi-label disease prediction that could transfer to other multi-condition diagnostic settings. However, the evaluation is limited to seven diseases on a single hospital dataset (MIMIC-IV) with a 1B-parameter LLM, and the qualitative failure analysis reveals that the model still struggles with complex multi-disease interference (e.g., missing sepsis when cardiac findings dominate). Open questions include whether the framework scales to broader disease taxonomies, larger models, external validation cohorts, and additional modalities (e.g., imaging beyond CXR, continuous vitals), and whether the Chain-of-Evidence supervision generalizes beyond the GPT-4o-generated annotations.
