## Problem

Existing zero-shot camouflaged object segmentation methods rely on a two-stage MLLM-then-SAM pipeline where the MLLM generates localization prompts. This approach suffers from inaccurate or missed localizations because MLLMs prioritize high-level semantics over fine-grained visual cues, and performance degrades sharply in multi-instance scenes where spatially distinct camouflaged objects coexist. The paper addresses this by redefining the discovery process with visual feature clustering and introducing a final selection stage to correct imperfect proposals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263369/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a binary segmentation mask of the camouflaged object (or merged mask for multiple instances). Internally, it operates on DINOv2 patch-level features reduced to 16 dimensions via PCA, generates multiple candidate masks through SAM2 prompted with bounding boxes, and requires no training data or task-specific supervision. The entire pipeline runs on a single 24 GB GPU with a 7B MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL), consuming approximately 42 seconds per image.

## Method

DSS is a three-stage training-free pipeline. In the Discover stage, DINOv2 patch features are clustered via the Leiden algorithm, then a Part Composition (PC) module iteratively refines cluster assignments by minimizing a feature-coherence energy that enforces intra-cluster compactness and inter-cluster separability; a Similarity-based Box Generation (SBG) module then computes self-similarity maps between each refined foreground region and all patches, deduplicates highly correlated maps via Pearson correlation, and extracts robust bounding boxes that are more complete than those from binary masks. In the Segment stage, these boxes prompt SAM2 to produce multiple candidate masks. In the Select stage, a heuristic scoring function (Pearson correlation with the similarity map plus inverse boundary-contact rate) ranks candidates, and the top-K are resolved through iterative pairwise comparison where Qwen2.5-VL judges which masked region best matches the camouflaged object in the original image.

## Results

On the four standard COD benchmarks, DSS achieves F_β^w of 0.766 (CAMO), 0.870 (NC4K), 0.849 (COD10K), and 0.848 (CHAMELEON), with corresponding M scores of 0.078, 0.031, 0.022, and 0.034. These results set a new state-of-the-art in the zero-shot setting, surpassing the previous best zero-shot method RDVP-MSD by up to 7.5 points in F_β^w (NC4K: 0.870 vs. 0.795) and 7.4 points (COD10K: 0.849 vs. 0.775). DSS also outperforms all unsupervised methods without any training and narrows the gap with fully-supervised approaches (e.g., CGCOD achieves 0.824 F_β^w on COD10K vs. DSS's 0.849). In multi-instance scenarios (3+ objects), DSS shows the smallest performance degradation among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSS outperforms all 19 compared baselines in the zero-shot setting on NC4K, COD10K, and CHAMELEON across all four metrics. On CAMO, DSS achieves the best M (0.078), S_α (0.808), and E_φ (0.870), though RDVP-MSD [43] edges ahead on F_β^w (0.785 vs. 0.766). Against the strongest zero-shot competitor RDVP-MSD, DSS gains +7.5 F_β^w on NC4K (0.870 vs. 0.795), +7.4 on COD10K (0.849 vs. 0.775), and +3.4 on CHAMELEON (0.848 vs. 0.814). Compared to the MLLM-only baseline QWen+SAM2, DSS improves F_β^w by +2.5 on CAMO, +2.4 on NC4K, +2.2 on COD10K, and +6.3 on CHAMELEON, confirming that the FOD and SMS stages add substantial value over direct MLLM localization. In multi-instance (3+) scenarios, DSS shows the smallest relative performance drop, while methods like IAPF and RDVP-MSD degrade more sharply. DSS also requires only 17.90 GB GPU memory versus 31–33 GB for ProMaC and RDVP-MSD, at the cost of 2.3× inference time (41.96 s vs. 18.05 s for RDVP-MSD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper restructures the dominant two-stage MLLM-SAM pipeline for zero-shot camouflaged object segmentation (exemplified by GenSAM, ProMaC, MMCPF, RDVP-MSD, and IAPF) into a three-stage Discover-Segment-Select framework. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the MLLM from a localizer (generating bounding boxes or points) to a post-hoc evaluator that selects among multiple SAM-generated candidate masks, while shifting the primary discovery burden to a feature-coherent clustering module (DINOv2 + Leiden + Part Composition refinement). This inverts the assumed division of labor: visual feature geometry drives candidate generation, and language-model reasoning is reserved for the final disambiguation step, a role prior zero-shot COS methods had not assigned to MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for task-specific training and large annotated datasets, DSS offers a practical path to deploying COS in diverse real-world settings (military, medical, agricultural) where annotation is infeasible. The paper honestly identifies the MLLM-based selection stage as both the accuracy bottleneck (a visible gap to the 'Ideal Seg.' upper bound) and the computational bottleneck (72.9% of inference time), pointing to a clear open question: can more reliable or cheaper mask-evaluation strategies close this gap? Additionally, the authors note that multi-scale feature aggregation remains unexplored for detecting very small camouflaged objects, and the reliance on a 7B MLLM for pairwise comparisons limits real-time applicability despite the modest GPU footprint.
