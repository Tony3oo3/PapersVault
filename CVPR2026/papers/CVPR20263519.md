## Problem

Existing safety benchmarks for embodied VLM agents cover only narrow hazard categories (typically environment harm) and evaluate safety solely via final task success, obscuring where and why failures occur across the perception-planning-execution pipeline. They also lack a unified risk taxonomy spanning human, environmental, and agent-centric harms, and lack a universal adapter to ground high-level VLM reasoning into low-level simulator actions, making systematic multi-stage safety diagnosis infeasible.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a first-person RGB frame from the AI2-THOR simulator, a natural language instruction (one of 9,900 spanning normal, baseline hazardous, and adversarially-enhanced categories), and the full interaction history. The system outputs the agent's explicit thought trace and action plan at each timestep, which are then scored by SAFE-DIAGNOSE into five stage-specific metrics (Grounding Recall, Hallucination Rate, Planning Rejection Rate, Planning Success Rate, Task Success Rate). The SAFE-AUDIT module additionally takes the initial thought, instruction, and scene context to produce a refined thought that replaces the original before plan generation.

## Method

AGENTSAFE comprises three tightly coupled components: (1) SAFE-THOR, an AI2-THOR-based sandbox with a universal adapter that decouples perception grounding (mapping VLM object references to simulator IDs) from action grounding (translating natural-language plans into atomic primitives like Navigate, Pickup, Toggle), enabling plug-and-play integration of any agent workflow; (2) SAFE-VERSE, a risk-aware task suite of 45 adversarial scenes, 1,350 tasks, and 9,900 instructions organized into three harm categories (Human, Environment, Self) with 6 jailbreak transformations (JailBroken, DeepInception, PAP, MultiLingual, Cipher, ReNeLLM) applied to each risky instruction; and (3) SAFE-DIAGNOSE, a three-stage protocol using LLM-as-a-Judge to score perception (GR/HR), planning (PRR/PSR), and execution (TSR) independently. The key empirical finding is that planning is the critical failure point: agents with high perception accuracy still generate unsafe plans (high PSR, low PRR) on hazardous instructions, and jailbreak methods that bypass LLM safety filters often degrade embodied execution because they impair instruction clarity. SAFE-AUDIT addresses this by inserting a zero-shot GPT-4o auditor at the thought level, triaging the initial thought for danger and either refusing, enriching, or passing it through before plan decomposition.

## Results

On normal instructions, GPT-5-mini and Step-v1-8k lead with the highest Grounding Recall (81.51% and 82.79%) and Task Success Rate (75.33% and 75.72%), while all models achieve near-zero Planning Rejection Rate as expected. On hazardous instructions, planning is the critical failure stage: Claude-sonnet-3.5 achieves the highest PRR (90.11% for Human-Harm) with correspondingly low TSR (1.11%), whereas Step-v1-8k and Gemini-2.5-flash show 0% PRR and high PSR (>70%), translating into dangerous execution (TSR up to 61.56% for Gemini on Env-Harm). SAFE-AUDIT, the proposed thought-level safety module, achieves the lowest PSR (3.52%) and TSR (0.48%) on hazardous instructions while simultaneously improving average TSR by 2.22% on normal instructions, outperforming both AgentSpec and ThinkSafe defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares SAFE-AUDIT against two defense baselines: AgentSpec (rule-based action vetting) and ThinkSafe (LLM-based pre-execution risk assessment). On normal instructions, both baselines incur a significant utility cost—ThinkSafe degrades TSR by up to 14.96% and AgentSpec also reduces TSR—whereas SAFE-AUDIT's thought-level refinement actually improves average TSR by 2.22%, preserving agent utility. On hazardous instructions, SAFE-AUDIT achieves the lowest PSR (3.52%) and TSR (0.48%), substantially outperforming both AgentSpec and ThinkSafe in suppressing harmful plan generation and execution. The key advantage of SAFE-AUDIT is its proactive intervention at the initial thought stage, preventing unsafe intentions from propagating into multi-step plans, rather than blocking actions only at execution time as the baselines do.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of embodied safety benchmarks (EARBench, EIRAD, SafeAgentBench, IS-Bench, Safe-BeAI) by addressing three gaps the authors identify: prior work covers only narrow hazard taxonomies, evaluates solely on final task outcomes, and lacks a universal adapter to ground high-level VLM plans into low-level simulator actions. Conceptually, it repurposes the jailbreak literature (JailBroken, DeepInception, PAP, etc.) from pure LLM safety into the embodied setting, and draws on Asimov's Three Laws to structure a unified human/environment/agent risk taxonomy. The multi-stage SAFE-DIAGNOSE protocol (perception → planning → execution) departs from the outcome-only evaluation paradigm of all cited prior benchmarks, and the SAFE-AUDIT module introduces thought-level intervention as distinct from execution-layer defenses like AgentSpec or ThinkSafe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for embodied agent safety evaluation given its scale (9,900 instructions, 45 scenes, 9 VLMs, 2 workflows) and its full-pipeline diagnostic coverage, which prior benchmarks lack. The finding that planning—not perception or execution—is the critical safety failure point, and that LLM-targeted jailbreaks often *degrade* embodied execution rather than increase it, reframes how the community should think about transferring safety attacks to physical agents. Key limitations include the simulation-only setting (AI2-THOR, 4 indoor scene types, 104 objects), the reliance on a single LLM-as-a-Judge for planning evaluation, and the absence of sim-to-real validation, all of which the authors flag as open directions.
