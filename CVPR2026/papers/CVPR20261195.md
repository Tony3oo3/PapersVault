## Problem

Converting exocentric (third-person) videos into egocentric (first-person) videos requires handling extreme camera pose translations with minimal view overlap, a regime where existing camera-control diffusion models fail. Prior exo-to-ego methods either demand additional inputs (multiple exocentric views or a first egocentric frame) or avoid generating the full sequence from scratch, limiting practical applicability to a single exocentric video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261195/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single exocentric RGB video and a target egocentric camera pose trajectory as input, and produces a temporally coherent egocentric video of the same scene. The exocentric video is first lifted into a 3D point cloud via aligned monocular and video depth estimates, then rendered from the egocentric viewpoint to form a noisy prior video that serves as one of two conditioning signals. Training uses 4,000 clips from Ego-Exo4D (3,600 train / 400 test) plus 100 held-out unseen clips for generalization evaluation.

## Method

EgoX fine-tunes the pretrained Wan 2.1 (14B) inpainting video diffusion model with lightweight LoRA (rank 256), preserving its spatio-temporal priors. A unified conditioning strategy concatenates the clean exocentric latent width-wise (encouraging implicit cross-view spatial warping) and the rendered egocentric prior latent channel-wise (providing viewpoint-aligned pixel guidance), with the exocentric latent kept clean throughout all denoising steps rather than being noised. A Geometry-Guided Self-Attention (GGA) module injects 3D geometric cues into the attention mechanism by computing per-frame cosine similarity between direction vectors from the ego camera center to each query and key token, adding this as a log-scaled bias to the attention logits so that spatially aligned exocentric regions receive higher attention while irrelevant background is suppressed. The full pipeline requires only a single exocentric video and a camera pose, with no additional reference frames or multi-view inputs.

## Results

On seen scenes, EgoX achieves PSNR 16.05, SSIM 0.556, LPIPS 0.498, and FVD 184.47, outperforming all baselines by a large margin (e.g., FVD 184.47 vs. 508.69–622.47 for competitors). On unseen scenes, EgoX maintains strong generalization with PSNR 14.38 and FVD 440.64, compared to 821.71–1283.50 for baselines. Object-level metrics show the largest gaps: EgoX achieves IoU 0.363 and Contour Accuracy 0.546 on seen scenes, versus 0.074–0.128 IoU for all baselines, indicating substantially better geometric and object consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoX outperforms all four baselines on every image-level metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP-I) and every object-level metric (IoU, Contour Accuracy, Location Error) in both seen and unseen settings. The most striking gap is in object IoU: EgoX reaches 0.363 on seen scenes versus 0.074 (Exo2Ego-V), 0.128 (TrajectoryCrafter), 0.076 (Wan Fun Control), and 0.114 (Wan VACE), reflecting far superior geometric and object consistency. On FVD, EgoX achieves 184.47 (seen) and 440.64 (unseen), roughly 2–3× lower than the next-best baseline (Wan VACE at 508.69 and 1045.45 respectively). The sole exception is Wan VACE's higher Temporal Flickering (0.989 vs. 0.977) and Motion Smoothness (0.994 vs. 0.990) on seen scenes, which the authors attribute to Wan VACE generating overly static videos (Dynamic Degree 0.673 vs. EgoX's 0.974). On unseen scenes, EgoX also leads in Dynamic Degree (0.989), confirming it produces temporally coherent and visually dynamic sequences where Wan VACE collapses to near-static output (0.427).

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoX extends the exo-to-ego video generation line of work (Exo2Ego-V, EgoExo-Gen, 4Diff) by removing the need for multiple exocentric views or a first egocentric frame, requiring only a single exocentric video. Methodologically, it builds on the Wan 2.1 video diffusion model via lightweight LoRA adaptation and borrows the point-cloud rendering pipeline from ViPE for depth alignment, but departs from prior camera-control approaches (Trajectory Crafter, Gen3C) that assume modest viewpoint changes. The key architectural departure is the unified conditioning strategy—width-wise concatenation for cross-view context and channel-wise concatenation for viewpoint-aligned priors—combined with a geometry-guided self-attention that injects 3D direction cues directly into attention logits, a mechanism not present in prior exo-to-ego or camera-control methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single LoRA fine-tune of a large pretrained video diffusion model can handle extreme exocentric-to-egocentric viewpoint translation with strong in-the-wild generalization, EgoX opens a practical path toward immersive first-person content generation for film, AR/VR, and robotics without requiring multi-camera rigs or auxiliary inputs. The main limitation is the dependence on a known egocentric camera pose, which the authors acknowledge as a barrier to fully autonomous deployment; integrating automatic head-pose estimation is identified as a natural next step. An open question is whether the geometry-guided self-attention generalizes to even more extreme viewpoint changes (e.g., aerial-to-ground) or to dynamic scenes with significant object motion, where the static-background assumption in point-cloud rendering may break down.
