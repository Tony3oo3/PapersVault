## Problem

Current video avatar generation methods excel at identity preservation and motion alignment but lack genuine agency—they cannot autonomously pursue long-term goals through multi-step planning and adaptive environmental interaction. This gap is compounded by two unique challenges in generative settings: maintaining accurate internal state under the stochasticity of I2V models (where identical actions yield diverse visual outcomes), and planning in an open-domain, semantic action space without predefined primitives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fixed-viewpoint, single-room initial scene image (containing annotated interactive objects) and a high-level natural language intention (e.g., 'host a product demo') as input. It produces a sequential video sequence of 3–8 chunk-level autoregressive clips, where each new clip is generated by the I2V model (Wanx2.2 with distilled LoRA) conditioned on the final frames of the previous clip and a detailed action caption. The L-IVA benchmark constrains tasks to single-room settings to avoid spatial inconsistencies in current I2V models, and each task involves more than three objects.

## Method

ORCA is a training-free framework that embodies Internal World Model capabilities through two mechanisms. First, a closed-loop OTAR (Observe-Think-Act-Reflect) cycle continuously verifies predicted outcomes against actual I2V generations: the Reflect stage compares sampled frames against the predicted next state, accepting or rejecting the generation and triggering retries (up to N_retry) or adaptive re-planning on mismatch, preventing belief corruption from stochastic generation. Second, a hierarchical dual-system architecture separates strategic reasoning (System 2, which maintains the belief state, decomposes the intention into sub-goals, and predicts next states) from execution grounding (System 1, which translates abstract sub-goals into detailed, I2V-model-specific action captions). Both systems are implemented via structured prompting of a pre-trained VLM (Gemini-2.5-Flash), requiring no task-specific training. The overall task is formalized as a POMDP where the agent maintains a belief state updated through observation and verified through reflection.

## Results

On the L-IVA benchmark (100 tasks, 5 scenarios), ORCA achieves the highest average Task Success Rate of 71.0%, outperforming Open-Loop Planner (62.3%), VAGEN (61.2%), and Reactive Agent (50.9%). ORCA also leads on Physical Plausibility (3.72/5 vs. 3.11–3.22 for baselines), Action Fidelity (0.64 vs. 0.55–0.62), and Subject Consistency (0.93 vs. 0.90–0.92). In human preference via Best-Worst Scaling, ORCA scores +28.7% while all baselines are negative (Reactive: −18.0%, Open-Loop: −7.52%, VAGEN: −4.12%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORCA outperforms all three baselines on every primary metric. Against the Reactive Agent (ReAct-style observe-act loop without belief state), ORCA gains +20.1 points in TSR and +0.61 in PPS, as the reactive baseline suffers from repetitive actions and object permanence failures due to lacking world-state tracking. Against the Open-Loop Planner (full sequence planned upfront, no feedback), ORCA leads by +8.7 TSR on average, with the gap widening dramatically in high-dependency scenarios: Garden (81.5% vs. 46.2%) and Workshop (80.4% vs. 72.7%), where undetected early errors cascade. Against VAGEN (world-model CoT assuming deterministic outcomes), ORCA gains +9.8 TSR and +0.50 PPS, benefiting from its Reflect stage that catches I2V hallucinations before they corrupt the belief state. Notably, Open-Loop remains competitive in low-dependency scenarios (Kitchen: 72.3% vs. 73.8%; Livestream: 72.3% vs. 58.4%), where ORCA's retry mechanism can exhaust the step budget, but this advantage reverses sharply as task complexity increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing concepts from control theory (POMDP, internal world models) and cognitive science (dual-process theory) into the video avatar generation domain, where prior work has treated avatars as passive signal processors (audio-driven, pose-driven) or as single-clip quality optimization targets. It departs from agent-based video generation frameworks (DreamFactory, StoryAgent, VISTA, GENMAC) that focus on narrative coherence or per-clip refinement, and from world-model-based VLM agents (VAGEN) that assume deterministic environments, by explicitly addressing the stochasticity of I2V generation through closed-loop outcome verification. The architecture is training-free, composing off-the-shelf VLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash) and I2V models (Wanx2.2) via structured prompting rather than learning task-specific policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The L-IVA benchmark is the first to evaluate long-horizon, goal-directed task completion in generative video environments, filling a gap where existing benchmarks only assess per-clip aesthetics or single-step alignment. The finding that open-loop planning is competitive in low-dependency scenarios but fails catastrophically in high-dependency ones (Garden: 46% vs. 81% TSR) provides a useful diagnostic for when closed-loop verification is truly necessary. Key limitations include fixed-viewpoint single-room settings, a single I2V model, and only 100 tasks, leaving open whether the OTAR framework generalizes to multi-room, multi-agent, or higher-fidelity generative environments. The training-free design is both a strength (accessibility) and a ceiling (prompting may not scale to truly open-world scenarios).
