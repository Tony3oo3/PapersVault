## Problem

Existing deep learning models for diffusion MRI cannot jointly capture the spatial structure, diffusion-weighting strength, and directional dependencies inherent in dMRI signals, and they fail to generalize across varying acquisition protocols (different numbers of directions and b-values). As a result, no general-purpose, transferable representation has been learned directly from raw dMRI volumes that handles arbitrary diffusion sampling schemes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261337/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a set of 3D dMRI volumes (one per diffusion direction/b-value pair) along with their b-value and b-vector metadata, patchified into 3D patches of size (8,8,4). It handles an arbitrary number of diffusion directions without resampling to a fixed protocol. The primary output is a 384-dimensional CLS token latent representation; during pretraining it also reconstructs masked volumes, and downstream it produces age, sex, MCI label, or ADAS-Cog score predictions via lightweight heads.

## Method

The architecture is a modified ViT with 10 encoder layers that alternates attention between the spatial and diffusion dimensions (inspired by video transformers), with absolute positional encodings combining 3D sinusoidal spatial coordinates and a learnable mapping of spherical diffusion-space coordinates. The key contribution, D-RoPE, generalizes Rotary Positional Embedding by replacing the linear sequence distance (m−n) with a diffusion-space distance D(m,n) = √(γ(b_m−b_n)² + arccos²(|v_m·v_n|)), which encodes both the b-value gap and the angular separation between b-vectors on the unit sphere (with 180° symmetry for opposite gradients). Pretraining uses a masked autoencoding objective with an alternating strategy that randomly masks either 75% of spatial patches or 50% of entire diffusion directions per epoch, forcing the model to learn cross-domain dependencies. The resulting encoder is then frozen or partially fine-tuned for downstream tasks, demonstrating that the learned latents encode microstructural information predictive of cognitive and demographic attributes.

## Results

On the ADNI clinical tasks, the pretrained D-RoPE encoder with frozen latents and an MLP head achieves the best balanced accuracy of 64.70% for CN vs. MCI classification (vs. 61.90% for handcrafted tract FA/MD features and 58.40% for a fully trained 3D ResNet) and the best ADAS-Cog prediction with ρ=0.38 and MSE=0.77. On HCP age prediction, the finetuned variant reaches ρ=0.94 (second only to the fully supervised 3D ResNet at ρ=0.96), and on sex classification the frozen-latent linear probe achieves 74.60% accuracy. In the MAE reconstruction ablation, D-RoPE with alternating masking reduces FID by 37–40% compared to spatial-only masking without D-RoPE (e.g., 8.05 vs. 13.49 at b=2000).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the fully supervised 3D ResNet (Gao et al., 2024) trained on FA/MD maps, D-RoPE wins on both ADNI clinical tasks: MCI balanced accuracy improves from 58.40% to 64.70% (+6.3 pp) and ADAS-Cog correlation from ρ=0.33 to ρ=0.38, while the 3D ResNet retains a slight edge on HCP age prediction (ρ=0.96 vs. 0.94) and sex classification (ACC 76.0% vs. 74.6%). Compared to the ViT with standard RoPE adapted from Zheng et al. (2023), D-RoPE substantially outperforms on raw dMRI input (MCI BA 64.70% vs. 47.40%; ADAS ρ 0.38 vs. −0.05) and also beats the ViT fed with FA/MD maps (MCI BA 64.70% vs. 58.10%; ADAS ρ 0.38 vs. 0.25), indicating that joint spatial–diffusion modeling is necessary when protocols vary. Against handcrafted tract-based features (Wen et al., 2021), D-RoPE's frozen latents with an MLP head improve MCI balanced accuracy from 61.90% to 64.70% and ADAS-Cog correlation from ρ=0.33 to ρ=0.38, while matching or exceeding them on age prediction (ρ=0.90 for both). All metric differences between the best D-RoPE variant and the ViT-with-raw-dMRI baseline are statistically significant after Bonferroni correction (paired t-test), except MCI-AUROC and age-MSE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPE) from 1D linear sequences to the spherical-plus-linear geometry of diffusion MRI space, replacing the simple (m−n) distance with a weighted combination of b-value difference and b-vector angular separation. The overall architecture follows the ViT patch-tokenization paradigm and the MAE self-supervised pretraining recipe, while the alternating spatial/diffusion attention blocks are inspired by video transformer designs (e.g., Bertasius et al.). It departs from prior dMRI deep learning work, which has been largely task-specific (super-resolution, microstructure estimation, tractography), by framing the problem as general-purpose representation learning from raw multi-direction dMRI volumes that must handle arbitrary acquisition protocols without resampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to learn general, transferable representations directly from raw dMRI that are agnostic to the number of diffusion directions and b-values, addressing a key practical barrier to multi-cohort and multi-protocol analysis. The finding that frozen self-supervised features outperform fully supervised baselines on MCI classification and ADAS-Cog prediction suggests the encoder internalizes microstructural patterns without task-specific supervision, which could accelerate clinical biomarker discovery. Limitations include the O(lh²) computational cost of D-RoPE (vs. O(lh) for standard RoPE), evaluation on relatively small cohorts (ADNI n=276), and the fact that the pretraining data (HCP-YA, n=1065) is still modest for a foundation model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger and more diverse dMRI datasets, whether it extends to non-brain organs, and how it integrates into multi-modal MRI frameworks.
