## Problem

Multimodal person ReID combining RGB images with low-frequency RF signals is severely underexplored due to the fundamental heterogeneity between the two modalities and the absence of large-scale paired datasets. Conventional cross-modal alignment via distribution matching or triplet loss over-constrains optimization, erasing modality-specific discriminative features that are critical for complementary learning in the RGB-RF setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally synchronized RGB images (processed by a CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder) and complex-valued RF signal frames (amplitude and phase, processed by a 4.94M-parameter Mamba encoder with a quantum mapping module that converts complex signals to real-valued representations). Beyond the person identity match, the model also regresses 2D pedestrian position coordinates from RF features via a lightweight MLP supervised by MSE, enabling simultaneous ReID and sub-meter localization (MAE 0.205 m).

## Method

VRCLIP employs a three-stage training strategy. Stage 1 fine-tunes the CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder with lightweight residual adapters in shallow layers and a Lighting-Aware Margin Loss that maximizes centroid distance between ideal and degraded lighting domains while preserving intra-class compactness. Stage 2 introduces a Mamba-based RF encoder with a quantum mapping module (computing signal amplitude and a learnable inner product for phase) and aligns RF features to the frozen CLIP visual space by minimizing the negative sum of squared canonical correlation coefficients (k=5 directions capturing >99% of cross-modal variance), rather than enforcing point-to-point or distribution-level matching. Stage 3 applies an RF-anchored reliability gate where the Euclidean distance between batch-level RGB and RF feature centroids modulates the RGB fusion weight via W_d = 2·exp(−βd), dynamically down-weighting unreliable visual features under occlusion or low light.

## Results

On the VRR dataset, VRCLIP achieves a state-of-theart overall mAP of 93.9%, Precision of 87.5%, F1 of 81.5%, and CMC-1 of 80.3%, substantially outperforming all unimodal and multimodal baselines. On the THP dataset, VRCLIP reaches 98.7% mAP and 100% CMC-5, surpassing the strongest RF-only baseline (RFMamba, 97.6% mAP) and both multimodal fusion methods. The gains are most pronounced under challenging conditions: in nighttime outdoor scenes VRCLIP scores 94.1% mAP versus 76.5% for the RGB-only CAL baseline, and in occluded indoor scenes it achieves 94.1% mAP versus 8.4% for CAL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRCLIP outperforms all four baselines across every scenario and metric on both VRR and THP. Against the RGB-only baseline CAL, VRCLIP gains 23.0 percentage points in overall mAP (93.9 vs. 70.9), with the gap widening to 85.7 points in occluded indoor scenes (94.1 vs. 8.4). Against the RF-only baseline RFMamba, VRCLIP achieves a modest but consistent mAP improvement of 0.9 points overall (93.9 vs. 93.0) and 1.1 points on THP (98.7 vs. 97.6), confirming that CLIP-derived visual semantics add value even when RF is already robust. Against the two multimodal fusion baselines, the gains are large: VRCLIP surpasses DeMo by 15.1 mAP points (93.9 vs. 78.8, a 19.1% relative improvement) and MambaPro by 18.0 points (93.9 vs. 75.9, a 23.7% relative improvement), with F1 gains of 12.7 and 15.0 points respectively. On THP, VRCLIP leads DeMo by 3.3 mAP points and MambaPro by 11.6 points, while matching RFMamba's perfect CMC-5 score of 100%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRCLIP builds on two distinct lineages: CLIP's vision-language pretraining (fine-tuned via LoRA adapters on ViT-B/16 for illumination-disentangled features) and RFMamba's state-space-model RF encoder (modified with a quantum mapping module to replace the dual-branch amplitude/phase design). Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant cross-modal ReID paradigm—visible-infrared methods that rely on distribution matching (triplet loss, InfoNCE)—by reframing alignment as canonical correlation maximization in a shared subspace, arguing that hard distribution matching over-constrains and erases modality-specific discriminative information. The intermittent modality missing scenario and the RGB+RF modality pair itself are novel; prior multimodal ReID work has focused on RGB+NIR/TIR or RGB+depth, none of which can penetrate physical occlusions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VRR dataset (651K paired samples, 31 participants, 2D position labels) fills a genuine gap as the first large-scale vision-radio ReID benchmark, and its public release will enable the community to develop and compare methods for RF-augmented surveillance. The CCA-based alignment strategy offers a principled alternative to distribution matching that may generalize to other highly heterogeneous modality pairs beyond RGB-RF. However, the evaluation is constrained to only 31 identities, which inflates accuracy metrics and limits confidence in scalability to realistic open-world identity spaces; the baselines (DeMo, MambaPro) were designed for NIR/TIR pairing, making the large performance gaps partly attributable to modality mismatch rather than pure method superiority. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds or thousands of identities, whether real-time deployment is feasible with the Mamba-based RF encoder, and how performance degrades with increasing RF signal-to-noise ratio in outdoor long-range settings.
