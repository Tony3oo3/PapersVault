## Problem

Pretrained multimodal LLMs exhibit a systematic cross-attention misalignment: object nouns produce diffuse, scattered activations over visual tokens while attribute words yield sharp, localized patterns, causing models to fail at grounding user-specified objects from pure text. Existing fine-grained object understanding methods work around this by requiring explicit visual prompts (masks, points, boxes) at inference, which adds architectural complexity and diverges from natural user interaction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, SWIM takes a video clip and a natural language prompt containing a specific object reference (e.g., 'the red car on the left') and produces object-level descriptions or multiple-choice answers about that object, with no visual prompt input required. During training, the model additionally consumes ground-truth pixel-level instance masks solely for attention supervision, which are discarded at test time. The text prompt must contain exactly one tagged object noun (marked with <ins> delimiters in the training data) that is deterministically linked to its corresponding mask.

## Method

SWIM is a training-only regularization strategy applied on top of Qwen2.5-VL-7B that explicitly aligns cross-attention from object noun tokens to visual regions. During supervised fine-tuning, it extracts cross-attention maps from the tagged noun token at six evenly-spaced decoder layers, averages them into a single spatial map, and supervises this map against the ground-truth object mask using pixel-wise binary cross-entropy loss. To enable this supervision, the authors construct NL-Refer (125K videos) from VideoRefer by using GPT-4o to replace <region> placeholders with concise natural language referring expressions and tag the key object noun with <ins> markers. The approach requires no architectural modifications and no visual prompts at inference; the mask supervision is purely a training signal that teaches the model to concentrate its cross-modal attention on the correct object region when processing the corresponding noun token.

## Results

On VideoRefer-Bench-Q, SWIM achieves 78.3% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest generalist model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (71.8%) by +6.5% and GPT-4o (71.3%) by +7.0%, as well as the specialist VideoRefer-7B (71.9%) by +6.4%. On VideoRefer-Bench-D, SWIM scores 3.78 average, outperforming the best specialist baseline DAM-8B (3.68) by +0.10 and GPT-4o (3.25) by +0.53. On general video benchmarks, SWIM attains 62.1 on MVBench, 55.9 on VideoMME, and 55.6 on ActivityNet-QA, remaining competitive with prior methods while requiring no visual prompts at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWIM, using only text prompts, outperforms all 20 baselines on both fine-grained benchmarks. Against generalist models, SWIM leads Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +6.5% on VideoRefer-Q and +0.54 on VideoRefer-D, and surpasses GPT-4o by +7.0% and +0.53 respectively. Among specialist models that require visual prompts (masks, points, or boxes), SWIM exceeds the strongest baseline DAM-8B by +0.10 on VideoRefer-D average and +0.48 on Subject Correspondence (4.92 vs. 4.69), while also beating VideoRefer-7B (which uses mask prompts) by +6.4% on VideoRefer-Q and +0.32 on VideoRefer-D. On general video understanding, SWIM improves over VideoRefer-7B on MVBench (62.1 vs. 59.6) and ActivityNet-QA (55.6 vs. 55.2 for INST-IT), while matching VideoRefer on VideoMME (55.9), confirming that the attention regularization does not degrade holistic video comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

SWIM builds directly on the Qwen2.5-VL-7B architecture and the VideoRefer dataset's mask annotations, but departs fundamentally from the dominant paradigm in fine-grained video object understanding that requires explicit visual prompts (masks, points, boxes) at inference time. While prior specialist methods (VideoRefer, PixelRefer, Ferret, PAM, DAM) introduce additional region-level encoders and visual prompt inputs, SWIM instead uses mask supervision solely as a training-time cross-attention regularization signal, eliminating all visual prompt dependencies at inference. The empirical observation that attribute words yield localized cross-attention while object nouns produce diffuse patterns provides a mechanistic motivation absent from prior work, which typically treats vision-language misalignment as a holistic problem rather than a token-type-specific one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the need for visual prompts at inference, SWIM aligns fine-grained object understanding with the natural interaction pattern of users who specify objects through language alone, reducing both architectural complexity and inference cost. The scalability analysis showing monotonic improvement up to 125K mask-annotated samples without plateau suggests substantial untapped potential if larger annotated corpora become available. However, the approach is validated only on a single 7B architecture (Qwen2.5-VL) and a single dataset family (VideoRefer-derived), leaving open whether the cross-attention regularization transfers to other MLLM backbones, longer videos, or multi-object scenes. The modest gains on general video benchmarks (MVBench, VideoMME) confirm no catastrophic forgetting but also indicate the training signal is tightly scoped to object grounding.
