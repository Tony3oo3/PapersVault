## Problem

Generating multi-frame, action-rich visual narratives without fine-tuning faces a threefold tension: faithful action rendering, subject identity preservation, and cross-frame background continuity. Existing fine-tuning methods are costly and brittle, adapter methods remain largely single-frame, and in-context pipelines require cluster-scale compute while struggling to disentangle overlapping subjects or realize natural interactions like hugging and nestling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260951/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a long narrative prompt spanning 2–20 scenes, per-subject reference images, and per-scene grounding boxes as input, and produces a sequential 1024×1024 image sequence at 30 denoising steps. All generation runs zero-shot on a single RTX 4090 (24 GB) with no per-identity training; grounding boxes are reconstructed for each scene from the prompt, and the system must handle close-proximity and overlapping subject layouts without identity entanglement.

## Method

StoryTailor builds on SDXL with MS-Diffusion's Resampler for multi-subject conditioning and introduces three synergistic inference-time modules. Gaussian-Centered Attention (GCA) replaces hard box masks with a two-stage, Voronoi-partitioned anisotropic Gaussian decay: an inner slow-decay region protects the identity core while an outer fast-decay ring decouples subjects from backgrounds, with radii dynamically scaled by per-subject attention influence strength. Action-Boost Singular Value Reweighting (AB-SVR) performs a thin SVD on the current frame's text tokens, retains a principal subspace by cumulative spectral energy (τ = 0.85), projects the current frame onto that trunk to amplify verb semantics, and notch-projects all other frames' tokens away from it to suppress cross-frame action leakage. Selective Forgetting Cache (SFC) maintains a capacity-capped (512-token) per-layer KV cache that accumulates top-k historical keys/values with a negative logit bias (δ_h = −0.1) for gradual forgetting, and additionally blends the previous frame's attention output into the current one at background positions (α = 0.6) to build cross-scene semantic ties without freezing subject motion.

## Results

On the multi-frame visual narrative task (Table 3), StoryTailor achieves the highest CLIP-T across both single-subject (0.431) and multi-subject (0.406) settings, outperforming the strongest in-context baselines (FluxKontext 0.381/0.373, Qwen-Edit 0.392/0.386) by 4–10%. DreamSim is competitive at 0.171 (single) and 0.189 (multi), below FluxKontext (0.187/0.212) and Nano-Banana (0.183/0.195) but slightly above Qwen-Edit (0.169/0.206). CLIP-I is modestly lower than most baselines (0.869 single, 0.671 multi), which the authors attribute to richer action rendering and reduced background carry-over. Inference runs at 7.02 s/frame with 20.01 GB peak VRAM on a single RTX 4090, roughly 4.6× faster than FluxKontext (32.31 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StoryTailor decisively wins on CLIP-T (text–image alignment) against every baseline: it leads FluxKontext by 5.0 pts (single) and 3.3 pts (multi), Qwen-Edit by 3.9/2.0 pts, Nano-Banana by 4.2/1.5 pts, MS-Diffusion by 11.6/8.8 pts, and the 1Prompt1Story combinations by 19.9/17.4 pts. On DreamSim (perceptual coherence, lower is better), StoryTailor beats FluxKontext, Nano-Banana, MS-Diffusion, and both 1Prompt1Story variants in both settings, but trails Qwen-Edit by a narrow 0.002 (single) and 0.017 (multi). On CLIP-I (identity fidelity), StoryTailor is the lowest or near-lowest among all methods (0.869 single, 0.671 multi), losing to Qwen-Edit (0.922/0.718), Nano-Banana (0.911/0.737), and FluxKontext (0.913/0.732); the authors attribute this gap to richer action rendering and reduced background carry-over rather than identity degradation, noting DINOv2 scores remain strong. In terms of efficiency, StoryTailor at 7.02 s/frame and 20.01 GB VRAM is 4.6× faster than FluxKontext (32.31 s, 22.99 GB) and comparable to MS-Diffusion (5.36 s) while delivering substantially higher CLIP-T. Against the adapter-only baselines (IP-Adapter, MS-Diffusion), StoryTailor wins on CLIP-T and DreamSim but concedes a small CLIP-I margin, reflecting the trade-off between action expressiveness and pixel-level identity matching.

## Relation to Prior Work

StoryTailor sits at the intersection of two prior lines: multi-subject zero-shot personalization (MS-Diffusion's Resampler + grounding boxes for conditioning) and inference-time text-embedding manipulation (SVR from One-Prompt-One-Story for suppressing or amplifying semantic directions). It departs from fine-tuning-based identity methods (DreamBooth, LoRA, Textual Inversion) by requiring no per-subject optimization, and from in-context editing pipelines (FluxKontext, Qwen-Edit) by avoiding cluster-scale orchestration. The three proposed modules—GCA, AB-SVR, and SFC—are each incremental modifications of known mechanisms (Gaussian masking, SVD-based reweighting, KV caching), but their specific combination into a single zero-shot narrative pipeline on a 24 GB GPU is the paper's architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that action-rich, multi-subject, multi-frame narrative generation becomes accessible on a single consumer GPU without any fine-tuning, lowering the barrier for creative and interactive-media applications. Limitations include a modest CLIP-I drop (attributed to richer motion and reduced background carry-over), semantic degradation beyond ~16 frames, and evaluation confined to MSBench with rewritten prompts. Open questions the authors flag include finer micro-attribute control and adaptive KV inheritance under large scene transitions; whether the SFC capacity cap and top-k selection generalize to longer sequences or more than two subjects remains untested.
