## Problem

Text-driven 3D human motion synthesis in realistic scenes requires simultaneously satisfying semantic intent (e.g., 'sit on the couch') and physical feasibility (avoiding collisions), yet current methods entangle global planning and local contact reasoning within a single generative framework and rely on computationally expensive 3D scene representations such as voxel grids or point clouds. No prior work has demonstrated that structured 2D scene projections can provide sufficient geometric and semantic cues for physically grounded motion synthesis at a fraction of the encoding cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260709/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and a 3D scene, consuming the scene as two lightweight 2D projections: a bird's-eye-view RGB image rendered from an elevated corner (encoded into 49 DINOv2 patch features of dimension 768) for global spatial reasoning, and a 32×32 local heightmap (±0.6 m around the character's root joint, oriented to body forward direction) for fine-grained contact. The output is a sequence of 80 frames of 3D human motion in SMPLX parameterization, comprising root translation, 6D joint orientations, root-invariant local joint offsets, joint velocities, and binary foot-ground contact indicators.

## Method

SceMoS disentangles motion synthesis into two stages: (1) an 8-layer causal transformer planner that autoregressively predicts a sequence of 20 discrete motion tokens from concatenated DINOv2 BEV features and T5 text embeddings, and (2) a conditional VQ-VAE (codebook K=1024, latent dim 512, 4× downsampling) whose interaction decoder is explicitly conditioned on the local 2D heightmap, forcing the learned codebook to encode geometry-specific motion behavior (e.g., 'bend knees to contact a surface at height h') rather than generic kinematics. A lightweight 1D-convolutional trajectory refinement module then corrects residual foot-sliding by regressing smoothed root velocities from local joint features. The key architectural insight is that the 2D factorization—BEV semantics for global layout and heightmaps for near-surface physics—replaces dense 3D volumetric reasoning, reducing trainable scene-encoding parameters from ~50 M to ~4 M while matching or exceeding the contact accuracy and motion realism of voxel- and point-cloud-based baselines on TRUMANS.

## Results

On the TRUMANS benchmark, SceMoS achieves the best FID (0.31) and the lowest mean penetration (1.81 mm) and max penetration (11.12 mm) among all compared methods, while tying with TRUMANS on contact score (0.98). It also attains the best physical foot contact score (PFC 0.61) and near-ground-truth diversity (2.67 vs. 2.75). Crucially, SceMoS requires only ~4M trainable scene-encoding parameters, an order of magnitude fewer than TRUMANS (~86M), Humanise (~55M), SceneDiffuser (~55M), and TeSMo (~35M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceMoS outperforms all four baselines on FID (0.31 vs. 0.34–0.82), PFC (0.61 vs. 0.58–1.21), and max penetration (11.12 vs. 11.75–17.49 mm). Against the strongest baseline TRUMANS, SceMoS matches the contact score (0.98) while improving FID by 0.03 and reducing max penetration by 0.63 mm, all with ~21× fewer trainable scene parameters (4M vs. 86M). Compared to TeSMo, SceMoS improves FID by 0.17 and contact by 0.06 while using roughly one-ninth the scene-encoding parameters. Against Humanise and SceneDiffuser, SceMoS achieves substantially lower FID (0.31 vs. 0.75–0.82) and PFC (0.61 vs. 1.09–1.21), though SceneDiffuser reports a marginally lower mean penetration (1.71 vs. 1.81 mm) at the cost of a much higher max penetration (17.49 vs. 11.12 mm). Overall, SceMoS demonstrates that lightweight 2D scene cues (a single BEV image plus local heightmaps) can match or exceed dense 3D representations in motion quality while drastically reducing model complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceMoS extends the tokenized text-to-motion paradigm established by TM2T and T2M-GPT (VQ-VAE + autoregressive transformer) into the scene-aware HSI setting, where prior methods (TRUMANS, TeSMo, Humanise, SceneDiffuser) rely on dense 3D scene encodings such as voxel grids or point clouds. The key departure is a two-stage disentanglement: a global planner conditioned on a single BEV image encoded by frozen DINOv2 features, and a local geometry-grounded VQ-VAE whose decoder is explicitly conditioned on 2D heightmaps around the character's root. This replaces the heavy 3D volumetric backbones (Point Transformer, ViT-B on voxels) used by baselines with a frozen vision foundation model plus a small linear projection, reducing trainable scene parameters from ~50–86M to ~4M.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that carefully chosen 2D projections (a BEV semantic image and local heightmaps) can match or exceed dense 3D representations for physically grounded HSI—challenges the prevailing assumption that volumetric scene geometry is necessary for contact-aware motion synthesis, offering a substantially cheaper and more scalable alternative. However, the method is limited to static indoor scenes and macro-scale full-body interactions; fine-grained object manipulation, dynamic scenes, and outdoor terrain remain unaddressed. The ~8-second inference latency for 80 frames and the single-dataset evaluation (TRUMANS) temper the generality claims, and whether the 2D factorization holds under more complex or multi-person settings is an open question.
