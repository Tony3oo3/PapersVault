## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models for video-based spatial reasoning consume a fixed set of uniformly sampled frames and have no mechanism to request additional viewpoints once reasoning begins, often missing the geometric parallax needed for reliable 3D judgments. Moreover, initial frame selection typically ignores the trade-off between semantic relevance to the query and geometric diversity of camera viewpoints, and the model cannot iteratively verify spatial hypotheses against specific views in a shared coordinate system.

## Input / Output

The system takes a video clip and a textual spatial query as input. An offline SLAM pass recovers per-frame camera poses and depth maps to build a reusable BEV map and pose database; at inference, the VLM receives a compact set of SPF-DPP-selected keyframes, the BEV image, and the query under a fixed token budget. During reasoning, the model can emit BEV pose queries (2D coordinates plus orientation angle) to retrieve the nearest real frame, with a hard cap of six tool-call steps per trajectory; the final output is a spatial reasoning answer (numerical or multiple-choice).

## Method

EagleVision decouples spatial reasoning into two stages. In macro perception, a Semantics-Perspective-Fusion DPP (SPF-DPP) selects k keyframes by constructing a sparse SE(3) pose graph, applying heat-kernel diffusion to obtain a global viewpoint-diversity kernel, and combining it with FG-CLIP semantic scores in an L-ensemble kernel (L = Q K_view Q) solved via greedy MAP. In micro verification, the model performs iterative Spatial MCoT: at each step it either generates text, predicts a BEV pose to retrieve the nearest real frame via a scale-aware distance metric, or terminates with an answer, forming a closed-loop hypothesize-look-verify cycle. The querying policy is trained purely via GRPO with a spatial grounding reward that penalizes queries whose best retrieval similarity falls below a coverage threshold, requiring no human-annotated reasoning traces. The base model is Qwen3-VL-8B fine-tuned for 80 GRPO cycles with 16 rollouts per prompt and a KL coefficient of 0.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, EagleVision achieves an overall score of 63.5, the best among all open-source VLMs, improving over the base Qwen3-VL-8B (59.4) by +4.1 points and over VLM-3R-7B (60.9) by +2.6 points without modifying the visual input pipeline. On SQA3D, EagleVision scores 60.3% (EM@1), outperforming Struct2D (58.5), Spatial-MLLM (55.9), and SpatialMind (46.3). The largest per-task gains on VSI-Bench appear in Object Count (74.9 vs. 70.2 for VLM-3R) and Relative Distance (84.7 vs. 80.5), while the model also leads on all multiple-choice sub-tasks (Route Plan 46.3, Approximate Order 49.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EagleVision outperforms all open-source spatial-reasoning baselines on both benchmarks. On VSI-Bench, it leads VLM-3R-7B (60.9) by +2.6 points, Spatial-MLLM (48.4) by +15.1, SpatialMind (47.1) by +16.4, and Struct2D (43.6) by +19.9, while also surpassing the proprietary Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and GPT-4o (34.0) by wide margins. On SQA3D, EagleVision (60.3) beats Struct2D (58.5) by +1.8, Spatial-MLLM (55.9) by +4.4, and SpatialMind (46.3) by +14.0, confirming cross-benchmark generalization of the RL-trained BEV-grounded querying paradigm. Notably, EagleVision achieves these gains without adding a 3D reconstruction encoder to the model architecture (unlike VLM-3R) or modifying the visual input pipeline, relying instead on geometry-aware frame selection (SPF-DPP) and active BEV pose querying trained purely via GRPO. The base Qwen3-VL-8B (59.4) is surpassed by +4.1 points, and ablations confirm each component (MCoT, BEV grounding, SPF-DPP) contributes complementary gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

EagleVision extends the interleaved-modal Chain-of-Thought paradigm introduced by DeepEyes [46]—which demonstrated that RL-trained models can learn to crop and zoom on single images—from single-image tools to multi-view video spatial reasoning with a shared 3D coordinate system. It departs from prior spatial MLLMs (Spatial-MLLM, Struct2D, SpatialMind, VLM-3R) that inject 3D features or reconstruction outputs as static preprocessing, by enabling the model to actively request new frames during inference via BEV-grounded pose queries. The macro-perception stage repurposes Determinantal Point Processes for keyframe selection, combining semantic relevance (via FG-CLIP) with SE(3) viewpoint diversity in a way not previously applied to video spatial tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to close the loop between spatial hypothesis formation and active viewpoint acquisition in video-based MLLMs, addressing a gap where existing models are locked into their initial frame set once reasoning begins. The purely RL-trained querying policy (no SFT cold start, no CoT annotations) is practically attractive for scaling to new spatial tasks. However, the framework's dependence on SLAM-quality poses, its evaluation limited to indoor ScanNet-style scenes, and the moderate per-component ablation gains (+0.5 to +2.5 points each) suggest that broader applicability to outdoor, long-horizon, or dynamic scenes remains an open question.
