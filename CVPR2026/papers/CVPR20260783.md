## Problem

Generating realistic 3D urban scenes remains challenging: pure 3D diffusion methods suffer from appearance degradation due to limited 3D resolution, while pure 2D video diffusion methods compromise camera controllability and multi-view consistency. The paper asks how much of the generation process should occur in 3D space versus being delegated to 2D, and proposes a cascaded answer that leverages both.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260783/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method optionally accepts 3D bounding boxes, road maps, or text prompts as content conditions, plus a camera trajectory for viewpoint control. Internally, the 3D stage consumes a colored voxel grid (32×128×192, covering ~50m×100m×60m) constructed by unprojecting metric depth estimates from posed input images, while the 2D stage consumes rendered RGB frames at 640×960 from the generated 3DGS. The final output is a high-resolution urban video with accurate camera poses and a renderable 3D Gaussian Splatting scene within the foreground volume.

## Method

ScenDi cascades a 3D latent diffusion model with a 2D video diffusion model. The 3D stage comprises a novel Voxel-to-3DGS VQ-VAE (trained from 2D supervision and pseudo-3D ground truth from a metric depth estimator, using Asymmetrical Residual Blocks from Cylinder3D) that maps colored voxel grids to 3D Gaussian primitives, followed by a 3D DiT diffusion model trained with v-prediction on the VQ-VAE latent space, optionally conditioned on downsampled 3D semantic layout volumes or CLIP text embeddings via cross-attention. The 2D stage fine-tunes a pretrained video diffusion backbone (SVD or Wan2.1-1.3B) by channel-concatenating the rendered 3DGS RGB as a condition, refining foreground details and synthesizing distant background regions. A key design choice is that the 3D stage produces coarse RGB appearance rather than only geometric cues, which improves 2D training efficiency and loop consistency. Temporal consistency across video clips is maintained via a Diffusion Forcing strategy that conditions later frames on previously generated ones with independently sampled noise levels.

## Results

On KITTI-360, ScenDi with the Wan2.1-1.3B backbone achieves the best FID (22.9), KID (0.016), FVD (262.6), and Met3R (0.1247) among all 3D generation methods, substantially outperforming the strongest 3D GAN baseline UrbanGen (FID 33.0, FVD 300.1, Met3R 0.2196). The SVD-based variant also surpasses UrbanGen on Met3R (0.2141 vs. 0.2196) and RotErr (0.13 vs. 0.25) while remaining competitive on FID (36.9 vs. 33.0). For camera controllability, both ScenDi variants achieve TransErr below 0.23, far exceeding the I2V baselines Vista (2.33) and Gen3C (0.04), confirming that the explicit 3DGS backbone preserves accurate camera trajectories even under out-of-distribution viewpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three 3D GAN baselines, ScenDi (Wan2.1) dominates across all quality metrics: FID drops from 135.3 (DiscoScene) and 90.8 (CC3D) to 22.9, and FVD improves from 2025.9 and 706.1 to 262.6. Compared to the strongest 3D GAN, UrbanGen, ScenDi (Wan2.1) achieves a 31% lower FID (22.9 vs. 33.0), 12% lower FVD (262.6 vs. 300.1), and 43% lower Met3R (0.1247 vs. 0.2196), while the SVD variant trades a slightly higher FID (36.9 vs. 33.0) for better RotErr (0.13 vs. 0.25). Against the I2V baseline Vista, ScenDi (Wan2.1) achieves comparable FID (22.9 vs. 25.6) and KID (0.016 vs. 0.016) but a much lower TransErr (0.06 vs. 2.33), demonstrating superior camera controllability; Vista's lower FVD (234.0 vs. 262.6) and Met3R (0.0907 vs. 0.1247) come at the cost of losing explicit 3D geometry. Against Gen3C, ScenDi (Wan2.1) achieves a lower FVD (262.6 vs. 426.1) and Met3R (0.1247 vs. 0.1476), indicating better temporal consistency and 3D coherence, though Gen3C edges ahead on FID (24.1 vs. 22.9 is actually ScenDi winning) and KID (0.012 vs. 0.016), with the paper noting Gen3C suffers significant quality degradation when input and current viewpoints have minimal overlap.

## Relation to Prior Work

ScenDi positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of work: direct 3D scene generation (DiscoScene, CC3D, UrbanGen, GaussianCity) which provides explicit geometry but limited appearance fidelity, and 2D video diffusion (Vista, Gen3C) which produces high-quality images but lacks precise camera control. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D-to-2D cascades (StreetScapes, UniScene, InfiniCube, ConsistentCity) is that the 3D stage outputs coarse RGB appearance alongside geometry, rather than only geometric cues like depth or semantic maps, thereby reducing the burden on the 2D model. Architecturally, it builds on 3DGS as the output representation, DiT-3D for the 3D diffusion backbone, Cylinder3D asymmetrical convolutions for efficient voxel processing, and Diffusion Forcing for temporal consistency in the 2D refinement stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated recipe for combining 3D and 2D diffusion models, directly answering the open question of how much generation should occur in each modality. The ablation showing RGB conditioning dramatically outperforms depth conditioning (FID 36.9 vs 78.6) validates the design choice and suggests that prior methods relying solely on geometric buffers for 2D refinement are suboptimal. A key limitation is that 2D refinement quality is bounded by the preceding 3D LDM, and the authors acknowledge that scaling training data and model size is needed to improve native 3D generation. Open questions include whether this cascaded paradigm generalizes beyond forward-driving trajectories to fully free-viewpoint or indoor scenes, and whether the 3D stage can be made strong enough to reduce reliance on 2D hallucination.
