## Problem

Existing discretization strategies for diffusion ODE sampling enforce a single, globally optimized timestep schedule shared across all initial noise samples, ignoring instance-specific trajectory complexity. The paper demonstrates through controlled synthetic experiments that this global treatment is suboptimal, with instance-specific schedules achieving up to 50% lower endpoint error than globally optimized ones, motivating a framework that adapts timesteps per sample.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263575/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial Gaussian noise sample x_T (dimension d) and optional conditional information c (class label index or pooled text-prompt embedding) as input, and outputs a per-instance discretization set {τ_n, Δτ_n, γ_n} for n=1..N that parameterizes the ODE solver's timesteps, temporal shifts, and output scales. At inference, this requires only a single forward pass of the lightweight network φ before the standard N-step solver loop, adding roughly 2.3–2.5% overhead to total sampling time. The final output is a generated image (32×32 to 256×256 pixel/latent space, or FLUX.1-dev resolution) or video (LTX-Video), produced by integrating the probability flow ODE with the tailored schedule.

## Method

INDIS learns a lightweight conditioning network φ: R^d × R^e → R^{3×N} that maps the initial noise and condition to instance-specific timesteps (via softmax parameterization ensuring monotonicity), time-shift factors Δτ, and output-scale factors γ. Training follows a gradient-based distillation objective: a precomputed teacher dataset of (x_T, c, x_0*) tuples is generated with a high-NFE iPNDM solver, and the student solver with φ-predicted discretizations is optimized to minimize LPIPS distance to the teacher endpoint. The key design choice is conditioning the discretization on x_T and c jointly, which the paper shows is the most critical component (ablation in Fig. 5), while the shift/scale factors address exposure bias between training-time noise and sampling-time isotropic Gaussian. The framework is solver-agnostic (compatible with iPNDM, DPM-Solver++, UniPC) and requires no modification to the base diffusion model, only a short tuning phase on a fixed teacher dataset.

## Results

On pixel-space DPMs, INDIS achieves FID of 9.26 (NFE=3), 3.31 (NFE=5), and 2.60 (NFE=7) on CIFAR-10, outperforming the strongest baseline (LD3) by 35.33%, 31.50%, and 15.62% average FID improvement across CIFAR-10, FFHQ, AFHQv2, and ImageNet-64 respectively. On latent-space LSUN-Bedroom 256×256, INDIS reaches FID 12.44/4.99/3.81 at NFE=3/5/7, a 14.12% average improvement over the best baseline. On FLUX.1-dev (text-to-image), INDIS achieves FID 44.35 and CLIP 26.33 at NFE=3, surpassing both RDS (64.50 FID) and globally optimized discretization (56.82 FID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

INDIS consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and NFE setting. Against the strongest global-optimization baseline LD3 [18], INDIS achieves 35.33% average FID reduction at NFE=3 (e.g., 9.26 vs. 16.52 on CIFAR-10) and 15.62% at NFE=7. DMN [22] and GITS [21] perform poorly on FFHQ and AFHQv2 (FID >178 and >24 respectively at NFE=3), while INDIS keeps FID below 18 and 11. AMED [33] is competitive at higher NFEs but still trails INDIS by 2–4 FID points at NFE=5. Against solver distillation methods EPD [36] and AdaSDE [37], INDIS achieves lower FID at all NFEs (e.g., 9.26 vs. 10.40 and 12.62 on CIFAR-10 at NFE=3) without requiring distillation training. On FLUX.1-dev, INDIS beats both the RDS heuristic [6] (FID 44.35 vs. 64.50 at NFE=3) and globally optimized discretization (FID 44.35 vs. 56.82), with the gap narrowing at NFE=7 due to FLUX's inherent robustness to timestep choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based discretization search paradigm introduced by LD3 [18] from a globally shared timestep schedule to an instance-aware one, conditioning the discretization on the initial noise prior and optional guidance (class labels or text embeddings). It departs from prior optimization-based approaches (DMN, GITS, AYS) that all enforce a single schedule across all samples, and from solver fine-tuning methods (AMED, bespoke solvers) that learn fixed solver parameters rather than per-instance timesteps. The framework also incorporates learnable time-shift and scale factors to address exposure bias, generalizing the approach of [44] from heuristic corrections to instance-dependent ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving substantial few-step sampling improvements (up to 35% FID reduction at NFE=3) with negligible inference overhead (~2.5% of total sampling time) and minimal tuning cost relative to training the base model. This positions INDIS as a lightweight, architecture-agnostic plugin applicable to any pretrained diffusion or flow-matching model. Open questions include whether adjoint-based optimization can eliminate the gradient-checkpointing overhead on large models like FLUX, and whether the instance-aware paradigm extends to other solver hyperparameters beyond timesteps. The finding that benefits are most pronounced at very low NFEs (3–6) suggests the approach is particularly relevant for the increasingly important real-time generation regime.
