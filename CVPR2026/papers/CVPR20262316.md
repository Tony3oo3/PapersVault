## Problem

In multi-modal 3D human pose estimation, dominant modalities (e.g., RGB, LiDAR) suppress the optimization of weaker ones (e.g., mmWave, WiFi) during end-to-end training, causing modality imbalance. Existing balancing techniques are tailored to classification tasks that rely on cross-entropy loss or explicit class attribution, making them unsuitable for regression-based pose estimation, and often require auxiliary uni-modal heads or additional learnable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four synchronized sensor streams per frame: an RGB 2D joint sequence (N frames × j joints × 2 coordinates), a LiDAR point cloud (N_L 3D points), an mmWave radar point cloud (N_M points each carrying x, y, z, Doppler velocity, and signal intensity), and WiFi CSI data shaped as a 3D tensor (antennas × subcarriers × time samples). The model outputs 3D coordinates for j human joints (j×3 matrix), evaluated via MPJPE and PA-MPJPE in millimeters.

## Method

The framework pairs two parameter-free mechanisms. First, a Shapley value-based contribution algorithm quantifies each modality's utility using Pearson correlation between ground-truth and predicted joint coordinates (rather than MSE/MAE, which would bias toward high-magnitude modalities in regression), computed across all modality subsets with zero-padding for absent modalities. Second, an Adaptive Weight Constraint (AWC) loss regularizes encoder parameters by penalizing their deviation from epoch-initial values, weighted by the diagonal of the Fisher Information Matrix; K-Means clustering on Shapley scores partitions modalities into superior and inferior groups, assigning stronger regularization (α_S) to dominant modalities and milder constraints (α_I) to weaker ones. The AWC loss is applied only during a tunable learning window (first K epochs), after which standard MPJPE training resumes. The overall architecture uses modality-specific encoders (VideoPose3D, Point Transformer, MetaFi++) feeding into a fusion module (concatenation, MLP, or self-attention) and a linear pose regression head, with no auxiliary parameters introduced.

## Results

On the MM-Fi dataset (Protocol 1, Concat fusion), the proposed AWC method achieves 51.16 mm MPJPE and 34.46 mm PA-MPJPE, reducing MPJPE by 2.71 mm and PA-MPJPE by 0.63 mm over the naive concatenation baseline (53.87 / 35.09). Across all three protocols, the method consistently outperforms the best competing balancing technique (Modality-level resampling) by 2.08 mm in MPJPE on Protocol 1 and 3.16 mm on Protocol 3. The computational overhead of the Shapley-based scoring module is under 1% for Concat/MLP fusion and under 5.4% for Attention fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed AWC method outperforms all seven baselines across all three MM-Fi protocols under both Concat and Attention fusion. Against the naive Concat baseline, it reduces MPJPE by 2.71 mm (Protocol 1) to 0.62 mm (Protocol 3) and PA-MPJPE by 0.63 mm to 0.39 mm. Among existing balancing methods, G-Blending [49] and AGM [21] actually degrade performance relative to the Concat baseline (e.g., G-Blending worsens MPJPE from 53.87 to 58.40 on Protocol 1), while OGM-GE [31] and Modality-level resampling [52] offer modest gains; AWC surpasses the best of these (Modality-level) by 2.08 mm MPJPE on Protocol 1 and 3.16 mm on Protocol 3. The joint-training MM-Fi baseline [56] and the single-modality HPE-Li [8] are far inferior (72.90 and 152.71 mm MPJPE respectively), confirming that balanced multi-modal fusion is essential. X-Fi [6] reports only Protocol 3 results (88.60 mm MPJPE), still well above AWC's 47.55 mm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Shapley value-based modality contribution assessment—previously applied to classification tasks (Hu et al., IJCAI 2022; Li et al., ICCV 2023; Wei et al., CVPR 2024)—to the regression setting of 3D human pose estimation by replacing cross-entropy with Pearson correlation as the profit function. It departs from existing gradient-modulation balancing methods (OGM-GE, AGM, G-Blending) that suppress dominant modalities by instead proposing a Fisher Information Matrix–weighted parameter regularization (AWC loss) that constrains update magnitude and direction for all modalities simultaneously. Unlike approaches that introduce auxiliary uni-modal heads or extra network branches, the method is fully parameter-free and operates within standard multi-modal fusion architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, drop-in regularization for multi-modal regression that requires no architectural changes or extra learnable parameters, making it broadly applicable beyond 3D HPE. A notable empirical finding is that fusing all four modalities (RGB+LiDAR+mmWave+WiFi) actually degrades performance relative to RGB+LiDAR alone, confirming that modality competition is a first-order problem rather than a minor training artifact. Limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (MM-Fi), reliance on K-Means for the superior/inferior partition (sensitive to score distributions), and the learning window being a fixed hyperparameter. Open questions include whether the FIM-based regularization generalizes to other regression tasks (e.g., depth estimation, motion prediction) and whether the approach scales gracefully beyond four modalities.
