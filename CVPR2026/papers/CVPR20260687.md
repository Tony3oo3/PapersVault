## Problem

Omnidirectional (360°) aerial data remains scarce because 360° cameras are rarely used in daily life and exhaustive human labeling is impractical, leaving panoramic methods constrained to small datasets with unexplored data scaling. Existing UAV simulators support only perspective views with limited geometric and semantic annotations, and none systematically model the 4D real world—combining high-quality static environments with movable, interactive pedestrians—under a full omnidirectional setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260687/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The platform consumes UE5 3D scenes, sparse flight waypoints, and camera configurations, and produces equirectangular panoramic images stitched from six simultaneously captured 90° cube-face views, slant-range depth maps (distance along the viewing ray rather than orthogonal z-axis), full-coverage semantic and entity segmentation masks, 3D human keypoints in world coordinates, and Minimum Snap–smoothed flight trajectories. A key structural detail is that entity segmentation labels every static mesh, skeletal mesh, and landscape element in the scene (not just a subset), and all sensor outputs are synchronized via a unified Event Dispatcher trigger to avoid temporal misalignment during UAV motion.

## Method

AirSim360 builds on Unreal Engine 4.27–5.6 and integrates three core modules. For render-aligned data generation, six pinhole cameras (front, back, left, right, up, down) are rendered in a single GPU pass, and their cube-face textures are stitched into an ERP image via a custom Render Hardware Interface (RHI) texture-copying pipeline, avoiding secondary stitching in material nodes; depth is re-projected to slant range using known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, semantic labels are extracted through the Stencil Buffer with a post-process material, and entity segmentation uses a dedicated method that bypasses the 256-category Stencil Buffer limit to label all scene actors. The Interactive Pedestrian-Aware System (IPAS) combines NPC Behavior Trees with State Machines and a multi-actor message-dispatch mechanism so pedestrians autonomously transition between walking, chatting, and phone-call states, while 3D keypoints are extracted in real time from skeletal mesh sockets. For trajectory generation, Minimum Snap polynomial planning converts user-specified sparse waypoints into smooth, dynamically feasible flight paths parameterized by maximum velocity and acceleration, directly applicable to real quadrotors.

## Results

For panoramic depth estimation (Table 7), models trained on Omni360 outperform those trained on Deep360 in both out-of-domain (SphereCraft test: AbsRel 5.44 vs. 8.26, δ₁ 0.399 vs. 0.349) and cross-domain settings (Omni360→Deep360: AbsRel 0.176, RMSE 0.023, δ₁ 0.667). For monocular pedestrian distance estimation (Table 6), adding Omni360-Human to nuScenes training reduces average angular error from 21.21° to 17.02° and mean distance error from 0.484 m to 0.458 m across public benchmarks, with the largest gain on FreeMan (angular error 17°→11.6°). For panoramic segmentation (Table 8), incorporating Omni360-Scene data boosts semantic mIoU from 58.0 to 67.4 and entity mAP from 24.6 to 38.9 on WildPASS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole named baseline is Deep360 (MODE, ECCV 2022), a prior omnidirectional depth dataset. On the out-of-domain test (SphereCraft), Omni360-trained UniK3D reduces AbsRel by 34% (5.44 vs. 8.26) and improves δ₁ by 5 percentage points (0.399 vs. 0.349), indicating substantially better generalization to unseen environments. In the cross-domain setting, the advantage is even more pronounced: a model trained on Omni360 and tested on Deep360 achieves AbsRel 0.176 and δ₁ 0.667, nearly halving the error and gaining 18 percentage points in δ₁ over the reverse direction (Deep360→Omni360: AbsRel 0.360, δ₁ 0.490). This asymmetry suggests Omni360's greater scene diversity and scale (60K+ frames across four UE5 cities) produce more transferable depth representations. No head-to-head comparison is provided for segmentation or pedestrian tasks against a named prior method; those results are framed as ablations showing the benefit of adding Omni360 data to existing training sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

AirSim360 extends the lineage of UE-based UAV simulators (AirSim, UnrealZoo, OpenFly) by adding native omnidirectional rendering via six cube-face cameras stitched into equirectangular projection, a capability absent from all prior platforms. It departs from existing simulators' partial instance segmentation by introducing full entity-level segmentation across the entire panoramic image, and from static-scene simulators by incorporating an interactive pedestrian-aware system with behavior trees and state machines. The automated trajectory generation builds directly on Minimum Snap planning, while the GPU-side RHI texture-copying stitching mechanism is a novel engineering contribution that avoids the latency of blueprint-based secondary compositing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The platform addresses a genuine bottleneck in panoramic vision research: the scarcity of large-scale, pixel-level annotated omnidirectional data. By providing 60K+ frames with depth, semantic, entity, and 3D keypoint annotations across diverse urban scenes, it enables systematic study of data scaling for 360° tasks that was previously infeasible. Open questions include how well the simulation-to-real gap closes for more complex or dynamic scenes, whether the entity segmentation approach scales beyond UE's 256-stencil-buffer limitation in truly open-world settings, and whether the preliminary VLN results generalize to longer-horizon, multi-step navigation. The platform's backward compatibility (UE 4.27–5.6) and open-source commitment increase its potential as a community standard.
