## Problem

Existing feed-forward text-guided motion generation methods rely on fixed-length, discrete frame-wise inputs, forcing them to either crop variable-length motion sequences into short fragments (losing semantic completeness) or uniformly downsample them (producing jittery, non-smooth results). This prevents the model from capturing rich, semantically complete motions spanning tens to hundreds of frames that are present in real-world datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial 3D mesh (vertex positions and shared triangular faces) and a free-form text prompt as input, and produces a dynamic mesh sequence of arbitrary length T via B-spline reprojection from 16 learned control points. During training, the mesh is converted to vertex-wise displacements from the initial shape, uniformly pre-sampled to 200K points with normals, then randomly downsampled to 4096 points (further compressed to 512 tokens via FPS) per iteration. The B-spline basis matrix can be constructed for any target frame count at inference, decoupling output length from the fixed 16-control-point representation.

## Method

BiMotion represents variable-length motion as continuous cubic B-spline curves with a fixed set of 16 control points per vertex, obtained via a closed-form Laplacian-regularized least-squares solver that compresses sequences of 5–200 frames into this compact representation. A B-spline VAE encodes these control points using a novel multi-level hierarchical embedding (inspired by wavelet packet decomposition) that captures both coarse and fine motion details, combined with a normal-fusion strategy for local geometric disambiguation and FPS-based spatial compression with cross-attention for long-range correspondences. The VAE is trained with Charbonnier fit loss, a correspondence loss aligning B-spline-reprojected trajectories to ground-truth displacements, and a local rigidity loss preserving inter-neighbor distances across frames. For generation, a DiT-based velocity network trained with rectified flow-matching predicts the motion latent conditioned on the initial shape latent and CLIP text embeddings, and inference samples a clean latent via ODE integration with classifier-free guidance before decoding control points and reprojecting to arbitrary-length mesh sequences.

## Results

On the BIMO validation set, BiMotion achieves the highest VBench scores in overall consistency (OC=0.187), temporal flickering (TF=0.995), aesthetic quality (AQ=0.529), and dynamic degree (DD=0.800), while slightly trailing AnimateAnyMesh on subject consistency (0.948 vs. 0.951). In the user study, BiMotion dominates all three perceptual metrics—text-to-motion agreement (4.095/5), motion plausibility (4.062/5), and motion expressiveness (4.048/5)—surpassing the next-best method (V2M4) by over 1 point on each. BiMotion is also the fastest and most memory-efficient method, generating a 16-frame animation in 4.4 s with only 1.25 GB peak GPU memory, compared to 16.8 s / 3.1 GB for AnimateAnyMesh and 1.67 h / 48.4 GB for V2M4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiMotion outperforms all three baselines on the majority of VBench metrics and every user-study metric. Against AnimateAnyMesh [96] (the closest feed-forward competitor), BiMotion gains +0.032 in OC, +0.015 in AQ, and a dramatic +0.700 in DD (0.800 vs. 0.100), reflecting that AnimateAnyMesh's fixed-frame cropping produces near-static outputs; the only metric where AnimateAnyMesh edges ahead is SC (0.951 vs. 0.948), which the authors note is inflated for near-static videos. Against GVFDiffusion [111], BiMotion leads on OC (+0.020), AQ (+0.024), and DD (+0.150), while also being ~29× faster (4.4 s vs. 2.1 min) and using ~11× less peak memory. Against the optimization-based V2M4 [11], BiMotion wins on OC (+0.012), SC (+0.072), TF (+0.009), AQ (+0.051), and DD (+0.050), and is roughly 136× faster (4.4 s vs. 1.67 h) with ~39× less memory. In the user study, BiMotion's scores (≈4.1/5) exceed V2M4 (≈2.9/5) by more than 1 point on all three perceptual axes, indicating substantially better prompt alignment and motion expressiveness.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiMotion extends the VAE-latent diffusion paradigm established by AnimateAnyMesh [96] and GVFDiffusion [111] for text-driven mesh animation, but departs fundamentally in its motion representation: instead of discrete frame-wise tokens (which force cropping or downsampling of variable-length sequences), it encodes motion as continuous B-spline control points via a closed-form Laplacian-regularized solver. The generative backbone follows the rectified-flow DiT architecture from [54] and [87], while the dense-point sampling strategy is inherited from DNF [117]. The novel multi-level control-point embedding, inspired by wavelet packet decomposition, and the normal-fusion strategy represent the paper's key architectural departures from prior feed-forward motion generation methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BIMO dataset (~39K variable-length sequences with high-quality text annotations) fills a gap in publicly available data for text-guided mesh animation, and the B-spline representation offers a clean, general solution to the variable-length-to-fixed-capacity bottleneck that plagues current VAE-latent diffusion approaches. The significant efficiency gains (4.4s vs. 16.8s generation time, 1.25GB vs. 3.1GB peak memory) make the method more practical for interactive applications. However, the fixed-mesh assumption precludes topology-changing motion, and the representation may struggle with very high-frequency details unless more control points are used—open questions for whether the approach scales to more complex, articulated characters or longer semantic motions.
