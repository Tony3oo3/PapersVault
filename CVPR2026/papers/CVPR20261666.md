## Problem

WiFi-based human activity recognition degrades sharply under domain shifts caused by changes in environment, subjects, or hardware, yet real-world deployments often lack access to source data for retraining. While test-time adaptation (TTA) offers a source-free self-calibration path, the field lacks a unified benchmark to systematically evaluate TTA methods under the distinct physics-induced shifts unique to WiFi sensing.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes WiFi Channel State Information (CSI) streams from two heterogeneous NICs (Intel 5300: 30 subcarriers × 3 antennas; Atheros AR9580: 56 subcarriers × 1 antenna) and produces activity class labels. Adaptation operates in two modes: OTTA processes unlabeled target samples in a streaming, per-batch fashion with no memory buffer, while TTDA fine-tunes on a full unlabeled target dataset offline before freezing the model. Evaluation metrics include accuracy, per-sample latency (OTTA), total adaptation time (TTDA), GFLOPs, and number of trainable parameters.

## Method

WiTTA-Bench standardizes a shared CNN backbone (four Conv2d-BN-ReLU-MaxPool blocks with [16,32,64,128] channels plus a two-layer MLP classifier) and benchmarks 20 TTA methods across two protocols (OTTA and TTDA) and three physics-induced shifts (cross-environment, cross-subject, cross-device). The paper introduces WiHAR-Dual, a new dataset with paired recordings from two heterogeneous NICs under identical conditions, enabling the first controlled cross-device evaluation. Key empirical findings reveal a physics-induced hierarchy of shift severity (CE < CS < CD), demonstrate that OTTA and TTDA occupy complementary deployment regions (lightweight statistical calibration vs. structural manifold realignment), and show that OTTA is robust to hyperparameters while TTDA is sensitive due to recursive self-training feedback.

## Results

Under OTTA, lightweight normalization/entropy methods (TENT, EATA, DUA) sustain ~34–35% cross-domain accuracy within <10 ms latency and ≤1 M trainable parameters, offering the best accuracy–efficiency trade-off. Under TTDA, clustering-based ASFA and self-supervised SHOT++ reach 45–60% accuracy (e.g., ASFA recovers up to +30% absolute accuracy on cross-device), but require 80–500 s offline adaptation. The difficulty hierarchy is CE < CS < CD: cross-environment is most TTA-friendly (global envelope drift), while cross-device causes near-complete manifold divergence where OTTA yields only marginal gains. OTTA methods show broad, flat hyperparameter sensitivity, whereas TTDA methods (e.g., ASFA) exhibit sharp optima with accuracy dropping from ~63% to ~35% when learning rate or batch size deviates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among OTTA baselines, normalization-based DUA [19] and EATA [20] consistently outperform entropy-minimization TENT [28] and SAR [21] by 0.3–0.5% on CE/CS, while all four cluster near the upper-left Pareto front (<10 ms, ≤1 M params). Pseudo-labeling OTTA methods T3A [8] and TAST [9] trail by 1–7% (e.g., T3A drops to 32.28% on CS vs. EATA's 39.52%), and consistency-based CoTTA [32] provides limited or unstable gains at substantially higher computational cost. Among TTDA baselines, clustering-based ASFA [34] achieves the highest cross-device accuracy (recovering up to +30% over base), while self-supervised SHOT++ [15] attains the overall peak (~57%) at greater resource cost. Pseudo-labeling TTDA methods SHOT [14], ASL [36], and BMD [23] yield moderate but less stable improvements, and source-estimation methods BAIT [39] and DaC [41] show only moderate gains. Notably, unlike vision TTA benchmarks where CoTTA dominates, here lightweight statistical calibration (TENT, DUA, EATA) suffices for CE/CS, while only structural TTDA methods (ASFA, SHOT++) can address the hard cross-device shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the test-time adaptation (TTA) paradigm—well established in computer vision (TENT, CoTTA, SHOT, EATA, etc.)—into WiFi-based human activity recognition, where prior domain adaptation work (DATTA, MDTA, CARING) either required source data, domain supervision, or labeled target samples, violating the strict source-free TTA assumption. It carves new ground by identifying that WiFi domain shifts are physics-induced (multipath geometry, Doppler scattering, hardware calibration) rather than semantic or appearance-based, which fundamentally changes which TTA strategies are effective. The newly introduced WiHAR-Dual dataset fills a long-standing gap by providing paired recordings from heterogeneous NICs (Intel 5300 vs. Atheros AR9580) under controlled conditions, enabling the first isolated cross-device TTA evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first systematic benchmark for TTA in WiFi sensing, WiTTA-Bench provides the field with standardized protocols, 20 baselines, and a clear deployment guideline: lightweight OTTA for mild statistical drift (cross-environment, cross-subject) and heavier TTDA for structural hardware shifts (cross-device). The finding that the vision TTA hierarchy (consistency > normalization) reverses in WiFi challenges a core assumption and redirects method design toward physics-aware, statistic-level recalibration. Open questions include whether the CE/CS/CD hierarchy generalizes beyond HAR to other WiFi sensing tasks (gesture, vital signs), whether purpose-built physics-aware TTA objectives can close the cross-device gap, and how adaptation behaves under compound shifts (e.g., simultaneous environment + device change).
