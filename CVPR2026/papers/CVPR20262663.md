## Problem

Existing 4D generation pipelines that animate static 3D models via multi-view video diffusion suffer from persistent appearance drift and temporal inconsistency across frames. The authors attribute this to supervision limited to pixel- or latent-space video-diffusion losses, which lack explicit temporally-aware, feature-level tracking guidance to enforce point correspondences over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262663/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static 3D mesh and a text prompt describing the desired animation as input. The mesh is rendered into multi-view static images that condition the diffusion model, while the text prompt guides motion semantics. Stage One outputs temporally coherent multi-view videos; Stage Two outputs a dynamic 4D Gaussian Splatting representation (deformable Gaussians with time-varying position, rotation, scale, and 4D-SH color) that is animatable and text-editable. CoTracker3 tracking is used only as a training-time auxiliary signal and is not required at inference.

## Method

Track4DGen is a two-stage framework that injects foundation-tracker motion priors into both video generation and 4D reconstruction. In Stage One, CoTracker3 produces dense keypoint tracks on ground-truth multi-view videos; these tracks are localized in the U-Net's second spatiotemporal upsampling block (identified via ablation as the feature layer with strongest long-range temporal correspondence), and a correspondence loss (cosine similarity between adjacent-frame descriptors) plus a position loss (Huber on soft-argmax predicted locations) are added to the standard diffusion loss to enforce feature-level temporal consistency. In Stage Two, the 4D-GS motion field is driven by a hybrid representation that concatenates Hex-plane features with co-located diffusion features (projected via camera intrinsics/extrinsics from the Stage-One U-Net), so that tracking-aware priors directly inform per-Gaussian deformation. A 4D Spherical Harmonics model replaces static SH coefficients with truncated cosine (Fourier) basis expansions over time for higher-fidelity color dynamics. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with the tracking signal serving purely as an auxiliary constraint, and the 4D-GS is further refined with 4D-SDS and ARAP losses.

## Results

On the Sketchfab28 dataset, Track4DGen achieves CLIP-O(img) 0.8884, CLIP-O(text) 0.2664, CLIP-F(img) 0.8955, CLIP-F(text) 0.2664, and CLIP-C 0.9819, outperforming Animate3D (0.8812, 0.2653, 0.8906, 0.2634, 0.9801) and DG4D (0.8619, 0.2578, 0.8708, 0.2592, 0.9700) across all five CLIP-based metrics. On the Animate3D dataset, the method reaches CLIP-O(img) 0.9428 and CLIP-C 0.9844, again leading both baselines. In the user study (20 participants, 1–5 scale), Track4DGen is preferred on all four criteria: Align. Text 3.57, Align. 3D 3.87, Motion 3.73, and Appearance 3.44, versus Animate3D's 3.50/3.78/3.63/3.41.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Track4DGen is compared against two baselines: Animate3D (the closest prior work, which it directly builds upon) and DG4D (DreamGaussian4D). Against Animate3D, Track4DGen wins on all five CLIP-based 4D generation metrics on both Sketchfab28 and Animate3D datasets, with the largest margin on CLIP-O(img) (+0.0072 on Sketchfab28, +0.0044 on Animate3D) and CLIP-C (+0.0018, +0.0036). Against DG4D, the gains are substantially larger across every metric (e.g., +0.0265 CLIP-O(img) on Sketchfab28). In the multi-view video generation stage, Track4DGen also leads Animate3D on 4/5 VBench metrics on both datasets, though it narrowly trails on Dynamic Score on the Animate3D dataset (0.778 vs. 0.787). The user study confirms consistent perceptual preference for Track4DGen over both baselines on all four evaluation criteria.

## Relation to Prior Work

Track4DGen extends the Animate3D pipeline (MV-VDM backbone, 4D-SDS refinement, ARAP regularization) by injecting foundation-tracker motion priors at the feature level, drawing on CoTracker3 for dense point correspondences and MVDream/AnimateDiff for multi-view and temporal attention. The key conceptual departure from prior 3D-to-4D methods is treating a pretrained point tracker as a motion expert whose supervision shapes diffusion features during training, rather than relying solely on pixel/latent-space video-diffusion losses. On the reconstruction side, the hybrid motion encoding (Hex-plane features concatenated with co-located diffusion features) and the 4D Spherical Harmonics color model represent architectural extensions over classic 4D-GS, which learns motion exclusively from Hex-planes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode in 4D generation—appearance drift and temporal inconsistency under sparse inputs—by providing explicit feature-level temporal supervision that is absent from standard video-diffusion training. The Sketchfab28 dataset fills a gap in object-centric 4D benchmarking. However, the quantitative gains over Animate3D are modest (e.g., +0.014 I2V, +0.008 Dynamic Score on video generation; +0.007 CLIP-O(img) on 4D generation), and the approach inherits Animate3D's reliance on a static 3D mesh input, limiting applicability to text-to-4D or image-to-4D settings. Open questions include whether the tracking supervision generalizes to longer sequences, more complex non-rigid motions, or multi-object scenes, and whether the feature-level injection strategy transfers to other diffusion backbones beyond the U-Net architecture used here.
