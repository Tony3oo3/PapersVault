## Problem

Vision-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation (VisRAG) systems degrade significantly when document images suffer from real-world visual distortions such as blur, noise, low light, or shadow, because semantic and degradation factors become entangled within pretrained visual encoders, corrupting both retrieval and generation. Existing mitigations—two-stage image restoration, parameter-efficient or full fine-tuning, and adversarial robustness training—fail to explicitly disentangle these factors and thus provide limited or inconsistent gains under complex degradations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query and a corpus of document images (potentially degraded) spanning seven domains (scientific papers, charts, forms, slides, infographics, handwritten notes, reports) and produces top-k retrieved document images plus a generated answer. At inference time, only the causal (semantic) branch output Z_sem is fed to downstream retrieval and generation modules, so the architecture and computational cost remain identical to the standard VisRAG pipeline. The evaluation benchmark (Distortion-VisRAG) contains 367K question-document pairs with 12 synthetic and 5 real degradation types at multiple severity levels.

## Method

RobustVisRAG augments the vision encoder with a causality-guided dual-path design grounded in a structural causal model. A non-causal path uses a single learnable token with strictly unidirectional attention (the token attends to all patch tokens, but patch tokens are masked from attending to it) to aggregate degradation cues into Z_deg, trained via a Non-Causal Distortion Modeling (NCDM) contrastive objective that clusters same-type degradations. In parallel, a causal path performs standard bidirectional attention among patch tokens (excluding the non-causal token) to produce degradation-invariant semantic representations Z_sem, trained via a Causal Semantic Alignment (CSA) objective that aligns degraded semantics with clean counterparts while enforcing independence from Z_deg. The unidirectional attention constraint structurally prevents degradation information from flowing back into semantic tokens, while the two objectives jointly enforce functional specialization within a single forward pass. Built on MiniCPM-V 2.0 (retriever) and MiniCPM-V 2.6 (generator), the framework is trained end-to-end with a combined contrastive + CSA + NCDM loss for retrieval and CSA + NCDM for generation, achieving 7.35% retrieval, 6.35% generation, and 12.40% end-to-end improvements on real-world degradations without additional inference cost.

## Results

On the real-world degradation subset of DVisRAG, RobustVisRAG achieves 63.82 MRR@10 for retrieval and 55.39% top-1 accuracy for generation, improving over the vanilla VisRAG baseline (56.47 / 42.99) by 7.35% and 12.40% respectively. On clean VisRAG data, RobustVisRAG reaches 80.11 MRR@10 and 58.22% top-1 accuracy, surpassing VisRAG (77.57 / 50.40) while maintaining competitive performance. Under synthetic degradations, the method scores 73.21 MRR@10 and 48.02% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best fine-tuned VisRAG variant (VisRAG-FT) by 4.10% and 3.06% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustVisRAG outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluation setting. Against text-based retrieval baselines (BM25, BGE-large, NV-Embed-v2, MiniCPM-FV/FM), RobustVisRAG leads by 25–31 MRR@10 points on real degradations, confirming that OCR-dependent pipelines are severely degraded by visual distortions. Among vision-based retrievers, SigLIP (15.07) and ColPali-FV (41.70) on real data are far below RobustVisRAG (63.82), while the direct VisRAG-Ret backbone (56.47) is surpassed by 7.35 points. For generation, RobustVisRAG (55.39% top-1, Real) beats GPT-4o (41.49%) by 13.9 points and the FARE-adversarially-tuned VisRAG-Gen (50.95%) by 4.4 points, showing that explicit semantic-degradation disentanglement outperforms both general-purpose VLMs and adversarial robustness training. The Two-Stage PromptIR restoration pipeline actually underperforms vanilla VisRAG on real degradations (40.42% vs 42.99%), demonstrating that perceptual restoration does not translate to downstream RAG gains. PEFT (LoRA) fine-tuning of the generator (47.22% top-1, Real) also trails RobustVisRAG by 8.2 points, confirming that parameter-efficient adaptation alone is insufficient for degradation robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VisRAG (ICLR 2025), which established vision-based retrieval-augmented generation using VLMs to jointly retrieve and generate from document images. It departs from prior robustness strategies—two-stage image restoration, adversarial fine-tuning (FARE), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning—by reframing visual degradation as a causal nuisance factor to be explicitly disentangled from semantics via a structural causal model. The causal representation learning literature (SCM, independent causal mechanism principle) provides the theoretical grounding, but the specific application to the degradation→representation pathway in VLM encoders for RAG pipelines is new. The unidirectional attention mechanism for the non-causal path is a lightweight architectural addition that avoids the computational overhead of full fine-tuning while enforcing structural separation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VisRAG is an emerging paradigm for document understanding, and real-world deployment inevitably encounters degraded scans, photos, and captures; this paper provides both a principled method and the first dedicated benchmark (Distortion-VisRAG, 367K pairs, 17 degradation types) for evaluating robustness in this setting. The finding that intuitive two-stage restoration pipelines can actually hurt performance on real degradations challenges a common engineering assumption and motivates learned disentanglement over perceptual quality. Open questions include whether the single non-causal token suffices for complex multi-degradation interactions, whether the S⊥D independence assumption holds for natural degradations, and whether the framework generalizes beyond the MiniCPM-V backbone to other VLM architectures.
