## Problem

Single-image human relighting is profoundly ill-posed because a 2D image entangles 3D geometry, intrinsic appearance, and illumination. Existing approaches either use end-to-end pixel translators that lack geometric modeling (producing physically implausible shadows) or sequential pipelines that estimate intrinsics then render, suffering from error accumulation where geometry inaccuracies are baked into the final image. No prior method has jointly solved relighting and 3D geometry reconstruction in a single generative model, despite the two tasks being mutually beneficial.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image of a person (832×1280 px) and an HDR environment map as input. The HDR map is decomposed into three LDR-compatible representations (Reinhard tonemapped panorama, normalized log-intensity, and directional map) because the pretrained Cosmos VAE operates in LDR space. The model jointly outputs five modalities: a relit image under the target illumination, an albedo map, a surface normal map, a 3D geometry map encoded as iNOD (a distortion-free orthographic depth in [-1,1] that can be unprojected to a metric point cloud without camera intrinsics), and a segmentation mask. Inference requires 17.5 GB VRAM on a single A100 and takes ~35 seconds per image.

## Method

GeoRelight is a 7B-parameter Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer initialized from DiffusionRenderer-Cosmos7B, with the key architectural insight of repurposing the video DiT's temporal dimension as a modality dimension, so all five target modalities are stacked and denoised in parallel as if they were video frames. A learnable modality type embedding and a shared 2D RoPE (replacing the original 3D temporal RoPE) ensure spatial pixel-to-pixel correlation across modalities in self-attention, while a binary modality switch mask (c_switch) flexibly designates each modality as either a clear conditioning input or a noisy target to generate. For 3D geometry, the paper introduces iNOD (isotropic Normalized Orthographic Depth): pixels are unprojected to a metric 3D point cloud, the entire shape is isotropically scaled to fit a [-1,1] bounding box, and then orthographically projected back to a 2D z-value map, preserving relative geometry without the anisotropic distortion of standard depth normalization and remaining robust to VAE compression. Training uses a strategic mixed-data procedure: 30K steps on fully-labeled synthetic data to learn physical disentanglement, followed by 10K steps on a hybrid of synthetic, light-stage (Dome), and in-the-wild (ITW) data where the synthetic-trained model auto-labels intrinsics for real images, with different training modes (Default, Rendering, Intrinsic→Relit, etc.) tailored to each data source's strengths.

## Results

On the primary relighting task, GeoRelight achieves PSNR of 27.22 (Synthetic), 25.87 (LightStage), and 21.17 (HumanOLAT), substantially outperforming the best open-source baselines (NeuralGaffer: 18.84/18.88/20.77; DiffusionRenderer: 19.28/21.09/17.58) across all three datasets. For 3D geometry reconstruction, GeoRelight achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.766 and F-Score of 81.56, dramatically surpassing specialized geometry estimators VGGT (CD 3.37, F-Score 21.05) and MoGe2 (CD 3.54, F-Score 23.96). For intrinsic decomposition, GeoRelight reaches albedo PSNR of 28.07 and normal angular error of 8.64°, outperforming DiffusionRenderer (PSNR 19.46, Ang. 20.44°) and RGB-X (PSNR 15.45, Ang. 21.12°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoRelight decisively outperforms all four open-source relighting baselines (IC-Light, NeuralGaffer, DiLightNet, DiffusionRenderer) on every dataset and metric in Table 3, with the largest margins on synthetic data (e.g., +7.94 PSNR over DiffusionRenderer) and LightStage (+4.78 PSNR over DiffusionRenderer). On HumanOLAT, the gap narrows slightly (NeuralGaffer scores 20.77 vs. GeoRelight's 21.17 PSNR), which the authors attribute to artifacts in the ground-truth relit images. For geometry reconstruction (Table 4), GeoRelight's Chamfer Distance of 0.766 is roughly 4.4× lower than VGGT (3.37) and 4.6× lower than MoGe2 (3.54), with F-Score of 81.56 vs. 21.05 and 23.96 respectively. For intrinsic decomposition (Table 5), GeoRelight's normal angular error of 8.64° beats Sapiens (13.99°), MoGe2 (16.33°), DiffusionRenderer (20.44°), and RGB-X (21.12°), while its albedo PSNR of 28.07 exceeds DiffusionRenderer (19.46) and RGB-X (15.45) by large margins. The only comparison where GeoRelight does not clearly win is HumanOLAT relighting PSNR vs. NeuralGaffer (21.17 vs. 20.77), a narrow 0.4 dB gap the authors note may reflect ground-truth quality issues.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the joint relighting line initiated by UniRelight, which jointly denoises albedo and relit video, by adding explicit 3D geometry (normals, point clouds) as a co-generated modality within a single Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer. It departs from the dominant sequential pipeline paradigm (Total Relighting, RGB-X, DiffusionRenderer) where intrinsic estimation errors are irreversibly baked into a separate renderer. The iNOD representation addresses a gap identified in latent-diffusion geometry work (Marigold's anisotropic normalization, point-map VAE noise) by providing a distortion-free, VAE-compatible depth encoding that requires no camera intrinsics at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that relighting and 3D reconstruction are synergistic rather than sequential, with ablations showing joint modeling slightly outperforms even ground-truth geometry conditioning—suggesting the mutual information flow during generation is itself valuable. The iNOD representation and the 'modality-as-time' DiT design are practical, reusable components for any task requiring 3D geometry in a latent diffusion pipeline. Key limitations include single-image operation (no temporal consistency despite the video-DiT backbone), human-centric evaluation, and reliance on pseudo-labels from a synthetic-pretrained model for real data, which may propagate systematic errors. Extending to video and broader object-centric domains are natural next steps the authors identify.
