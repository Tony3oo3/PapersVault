## Problem

Existing multi-exposure fusion methods optimize statistical or pixel-level reconstruction losses that fail to capture human perceptual preferences such as visual comfort and artifact tolerance. While EEG provides direct physiological evidence of perception, no dataset pairs EEG signals with multi-exposure stimuli, and EEG is unavailable at inference time, creating a fundamental train-test modality gap for cognition-aware fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262470/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of LDR images (the most underexposed and overexposed from a multi-exposure group) as visual input. During training, the Teacher additionally consumes 64-channel EEG recordings (1 kHz sampling, 1000 ms per stimulus) captured while participants viewed the corresponding exposure images; these are projected into compact latent cognitive tokens via a 1D-CNN-Transformer encoder. At inference, the Student operates on images alone, deriving pseudo-cognitive priors from the input LDR images to replace the unavailable EEG signals, and outputs a single fused HDR image.

## Method

The core contribution is a Bi-level Optimization (BLO) framework coupling a cognition-guided Teacher (Mental Integrated Transformer) with an image-only Student. The Teacher is a multi-modal U-Net where each encoder block performs cross-attention with visual features as Query and EEG-derived cognitive tokens as Key/Value, while the decoder attends to a target cognitive state token to align reconstruction with human perceptual preferences. The Student mirrors this architecture but substitutes image-derived pseudo-cognitive priors for the EEG tokens. Crucially, the BLO formulation makes the Teacher's lower-level objective explicitly dependent on the Student's parameters, so the Teacher dynamically learns representations that remain distillable rather than converging to a fixed, potentially intractable solution. Training proceeds via Alternating Gradient Descent with a reconstruction loss on both networks and a pixel-plus-feature distillation loss (with stop-gradient) on the Student, all optimized end-to-end over 300K iterations.

## Results

On the SICE benchmark (with reference), the proposed method achieves PSNR 23.764 dB, SSIM 0.6065, and MS-SSIM 0.8203, surpassing the second-best HSDS-MEF (20.568 dB, 0.5593) by 3.2 dB in PSNR. On MEF-LUT, it reaches PSNR 22.793 dB and SSIM 0.6369, outperforming all baselines. On the no-reference MEFB benchmark, it attains the best BRISQUE (19.492, lower is better), MUSIQ (67.310), DBCNN (0.6208), and QABF (0.6645), confirming superior perceptual quality aligned with human visual comfort.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best score on every metric across all three benchmarks (SICE, MEF-LUT, MEFB), winning against all nine baselines. The closest competitor is HSDS-MEF, which the method outperforms by 3.2 dB in PSNR on SICE (23.764 vs. 20.568) and by 0.034 in SSIM on MEF-LUT (0.6369 vs. 0.6033). On the no-reference MEFB benchmark, the gap over HSDS-MEF narrows slightly (BRISQUE 19.492 vs. 20.112; MUSIQ 67.310 vs. 66.454), but the method still leads on all five no-reference metrics. Older methods such as GIFNet (13.417 dB) and DeFusion (13.493 dB) on SICE are substantially outperformed, while more recent Transformer-based approaches like U2Fusion (18.740 dB) and HoLoCo (19.800 dB) trail by 4–5 dB in PSNR. On MEF-LUT, the method edges out HSDS-MEF by only 0.17 dB in PSNR but leads by a wider margin in SSIM (+0.034) and MS-SSIM (+0.015), suggesting better structural fidelity in cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: deep multi-exposure fusion (building on Transformer-based MEF methods like TransMEF and HoLoCo), EEG-based visual decoding (Brain2Image, Neuro-3D), and knowledge distillation. It departs from prior MEF work by introducing physiological EEG signals as a training-time cognitive prior rather than relying solely on pixel-level or perceptual losses, and from prior EEG-vision work by eliminating the need for brain signals at inference through a bi-level teacher-student distillation. The bi-level optimization formulation, where the Teacher's objective is explicitly conditioned on the Student's parameters, is a departure from standard two-stage or joint distillation, aiming to make the Teacher's representations inherently distillable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cog-Expo is the first dataset pairing multi-exposure stimuli with human EEG responses, opening a pathway for cognition-aware low-level vision research beyond MEF. The bi-level distillation framework offers a general recipe for transferring privileged training-time signals (EEG, gaze, fMRI) into deployable image-only models. However, practical adoption is constrained by the high cost of EEG collection (10 participants, 64-channel caps, multi-session recording), and the cognitive prior's contribution, while validated by ablation, remains tied to a small and demographically narrow subject pool. Open questions include whether the cognitive guidance generalizes across subjects without retraining, whether cheaper proxies (e.g., predicted EEG from images) could substitute for real recordings, and whether the framework extends to other exposure-invariant tasks like HDR reconstruction or tone mapping.
