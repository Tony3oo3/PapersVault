## Problem

Advertising aesthetic assessment currently relies on subjective human scoring that lacks scalability and consistent criteria, or on automated systems that act as opaque threshold filters without diagnostic feedback. While MLLMs can produce holistic scores, they neglect the progressive cognitive stages of human aesthetic processing and generate unstable, prompt-sensitive rationales that frequently misalign with final outputs. The paper addresses this by introducing the first theory-driven, multi-stage framework that operationalizes advertising aesthetic evaluation into an executable hierarchy for annotation, training, and evaluation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single advertising image (RGB) paired with a text instruction specifying which of the 12 sub-rules to evaluate, and produces a structured response containing a Chain-of-Thought rationale followed by a rule-specific answer: a binary suitable/unsuitable judgment for 8 classification rules, bounding-box coordinates for promotional icon localization, or a continuous 1–5 score for aesthetic and advertising attributes. For three rules (Hue Adaptability, Color Harmonization, Copywriting Tone), the model may invoke lightweight analytical tools (hue-cluster statistics, Hasler colorfulness index, OCR) whose outputs are integrated as auxiliary evidence within the CoT rather than serving as deterministic answers.

## Method

A³-Align is built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct and trained in two phases. First, supervised fine-tuning on 120K instruction-response pairs from 30K advertising images teaches the model the A³-Law rules, structured output format, tool-calling protocol, and CoT reasoning via token-level cross-entropy. Second, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) refines the model with a normalized multi-signal reward combining general terms (format validity, non-repetition via sentence and n-gram overlap) and rule-specific terms (binary accuracy, IoU-based detection reward with Hungarian matching, tool-utilization indicator, and a Gaussian-shaped continuous score reward R = exp(−(s−ŝ)²/2σ²) for subjective ratings). The three-stage A³-Law paradigm—Perceptual Attention (fidelity, realism, polish), Formal Interest (hue adaptability, color harmonization, layout adaptability), and Desire Impact (copywriting tone, promotional iconography, aesthetic/advertising attributes)—provides the theoretical scaffold that structures both the dataset annotations and the reward design, ensuring the model learns progressive, rule-grounded aesthetic judgment rather than holistic scoring.

## Results

A³-Align (built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) achieves 0.870/0.824/0.917 accuracy on the three Perceptual Attention subtasks, 0.824/0.880/0.870 on Formal Interest, and 0.991/0.926/0.701 (Acc/Acc/mAP@0.5) on Desire Impact rules, surpassing the strongest closed-source baseline (Gemini 2.5 pro) by margins of 0.092–0.130. On continuous subjective scoring, A³-Align reaches SRCC 0.880 for Aesthetic Attribute and 0.838 for Advertising Attribute, exceeding the best prior model (Claude Opus 4.1, SRCC 0.772) by over 0.10. Most strikingly, on Promotional Iconography localization, A³-Align achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.701, more than double the best closed-source result (ChatGPT 5 high, 0.283).

## Baselines & Comparisons

A³-Align outperforms all 30+ evaluated baselines (open-source, closed-source, and their thinking-mode variants) across every metric in Table 1. Against the strongest closed-source model Gemini 2.5 pro, A³-Align gains +0.092 to +0.111 on Perceptual Attention accuracy, +0.130 on Color Harmonization, and +0.120 on Layout Adaptability. On the hardest subtask—Promotional Iconography localization—A³-Align achieves mAP@0.5 of 0.701 versus 0.283 for ChatGPT 5 high (the best prior model) and 0.027 for Gemini 2.5 pro, representing a 2.5× improvement over the strongest baseline. For continuous subjective scoring, A³-Align's Aesthetic Attribute SRCC of 0.880 exceeds Claude Opus 4.1 (0.772) by 0.108 and Gemini 2.5 pro-thinking (0.759) by 0.121. Notably, enabling 'thinking' mode helps closed-source models modestly (e.g., Gemini 2.5 pro SRCC 0.743→0.759) but is unstable or harmful for open-source models, and neither mode closes the gap to A³-Align, which benefits from its dedicated SFT+GRPO training with rule-specific rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by operationalizing the classic AIDA marketing model (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action) into an executable three-stage evaluation hierarchy (A³-Law) grounded in signal detection theory, Gestalt psychology, semiotics, and affective design—none of which had been jointly formalized for automated advertising assessment. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes a standard MLLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) and aligns it via SFT plus GRPO with a multi-signal reward structure, departing from prior MLLM-based aesthetic evaluation that relies on one-step holistic scoring without progressive cognitive decomposition. The dataset and benchmark are constructed from scratch, filling a gap the authors identify in existing advertising image research that focuses on CTR prediction or single-dimension attractiveness scores without interpretable, rule-based diagnostics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in advertising: replacing subjective, non-scalable human scoring with a structured, interpretable, and progressively diagnostic automated pipeline that can both select high-quality ads and provide prescriptive critique. The 3× improvement in Action Intent over other models in the selection task and the >2× mAP gap in icon localization suggest the multi-stage, tool-augmented training paradigm transfers meaningfully to downstream commercial decisions. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes across cultural contexts (explicitly deferred), how it handles spatially dense or minimalist-premium designs (identified failure modes), and whether the AIDA-derived hierarchy is the optimal decomposition or whether alternative cognitive taxonomies would yield better alignment.
