## Problem

Existing event-image monocular depth estimation methods primarily improve contextual feature fusion but still directly regress the full depth distribution, which is inherently ill-posed and highly nonlinear. No prior work has applied the cost-volume-based iterative refinement paradigm (successful in optical flow via RAFT) to explicitly constrain and refine depth predictions in the event-image setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a synchronous event stream as input; events are binned into a 3-channel voxel grid (B=3 temporal bins) over a 50 ms (MVSEC) or 20 ms (DSEC) window before feeding a Swin-T encoder. The output is a dense depth map in real-world metric units, produced by iteratively refining normalized log-depth residuals from 1/16 up to 1/4 resolution and applying convex upsampling to full resolution.

## Method

HypoDepth introduces a discrete Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV) that samples D candidate normalized log-depth values within prior bounds, converting the ill-posed regression into a constrained search over a structured hypothesis set. A 3D cost volume is built by computing dot-product similarity between DHV embeddings (after bidirectional cross-attention with fused contextual features) and the contextual features, yielding a per-pixel matching confidence along the depth axis. At each GRU iteration, a local correlation lookup of radius r around the previous depth estimate retrieves multi-scale correlation cues (from a cost-volume pyramid), which a geometry encoder fuses with contextual features to predict a residual depth update. The entire pipeline runs coarse-to-fine across 1/16, 1/8, and 1/4 resolutions with increasing D (32→64→96), and cross-modal fusion uses cross-attention at low resolutions and convolution at high resolution to balance global alignment with local detail.

## Results

On DSEC, HypoDepth (Ours-B, event+image) achieves the best Abs Rel of 0.099, RMSE of 3.583, and RMSE log of 0.135, outperforming PCDepth (0.103 / 3.591 / 0.137) with fewer FLOPs (155G vs. 162G) and parameters (60.2M vs. 66.5M). On MVSEC, Ours-B attains the lowest Abs Rel of 0.212 (day) and 0.268 (night), surpassing PCDepth (0.228 / 0.271). The event-only tiny variant (Ours-E-T) reaches 0.168 Abs Rel on DSEC with only 1.4M parameters and 11.8 ms inference, demonstrating a strong accuracy–efficiency trade-off for resource-constrained deployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest fusion-based baseline PCDepth [21], HypoDepth (Ours-B) wins on every DSEC metric (Abs Rel 0.099 vs. 0.103; RMSE log 0.135 vs. 0.137) while using fewer FLOPs and parameters, and also leads on MVSEC (Abs Rel 0.212 day / 0.268 night vs. 0.228 / 0.271). Cascading the proposed DHV-based refinement onto PCDepth (PCDepth-Ours) further reduces Abs Rel to 0.098, confirming that the iterative depth-search mechanism is complementary to feature-level fusion. Among event-only methods, Ours-E-B improves Abs Rel by 49% over DepthAnyEvent-R (0.129 vs. 0.252) and by 68% over EReFormer-C (0.129 vs. 0.399), attributing the gain to multi-resolution coarse-to-fine refinement and the constrained DHV search space. On MVSEC, Ours-B also outperforms HMNet [11] (Abs Rel 0.212 vs. 0.230 day; 0.268 vs. 0.349 night) and SRFNet [26] (0.212 vs. 0.268 day), demonstrating that explicit depth-space iterative refinement surpasses purely feature-level multimodal fusion strategies. The tiny event-only model (Ours-E-T, 1.4M params, 11.8 ms) achieves 0.168 Abs Rel, substantially outperforming the much larger EReFormer (41.8M params, 50.1 ms, Abs Rel 0.387) in both accuracy and efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts RAFT's cost-volume-based iterative refinement paradigm (originally for optical flow) to event-image monocular depth estimation by introducing a Depth Hypothesis Volume (DHV) that discretizes the continuous depth space into candidate hypotheses, converting ill-posed full-depth regression into a constrained local search. It departs from prior event-image fusion methods (RAMNet, PCDepth, SRFNet, UniCT) that focus on improving contextual feature representations, instead performing explicit depth-level refinement via a 3D cost volume between DHV embeddings and fused features. The multi-resolution coarse-to-fine strategy and GRU-based iterative unit follow the RAFT/PCDepth lineage, while the DHV construction and multi-scale correlation lookup are novel adaptations specific to the depth domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DHV formulation provides a principled way to mitigate the ill-posedness of monocular depth by constraining the solution space, and the resulting lightweight 3D cost volume enables efficient multi-resolution refinement that prior single-resolution fusion methods cannot match. The tiny model (1.4M params, 5G FLOPs, 11.8 ms) achieving competitive accuracy opens the door to real-time event-image depth on embedded platforms. Open questions include whether the discrete DHV granularity (D=32–96) is optimal across all depth ranges, whether the approach extends to other ill-posed inverse problems (e.g., normal estimation), and how the method behaves under extreme motion or very sparse event conditions beyond the tested datasets.
