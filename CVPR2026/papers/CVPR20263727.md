## Problem

Existing VLM-based autonomous driving methods face a fundamental space misalignment: text-based Chain-of-Thought reasoning operates in semantic space while trajectory planning requires physical-space predictions, and image-only reasoning lacks planning-oriented guidance to focus on relevant objects. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing a progressive multimodal reasoning framework that smoothly transitions from semantic understanding through visual imagination to physical trajectory prediction, mimicking the human perception-imagination-action mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263727/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests six surround-view RGB camera images plus four recent front-view history frames (at 0.5s intervals) to capture scene dynamics, along with high-level driving commands, ego-vehicle velocity/acceleration, and a structured text instruction. Outputs are produced sequentially: a textual chain-of-thought (scene analysis, risk assessment, behavior reasoning, action decision), a dreamed future front-view scene image at 128×192 resolution (generated as discrete VQ-VAE tokens decoded by MoVQGAN), and a future trajectory over 3 seconds. The three output stages are demarcated by special tokens (think, dream, answer) within a single autoregressive generation pass.

## Method

MindDriver unifies text reasoning and visual generation in a single Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone by expanding the MoVQGAN visual codebook into the LLM vocabulary, so both text and image tokens are predicted with a shared language-modeling head. The core reasoning pipeline follows a T2I order—textual CoT first provides planning-oriented semantic guidance, which then conditions the generation of a future scene image, which in turn grounds the final trajectory prediction—ablations confirm this order outperforms image-first (I2T) reasoning. Training uses a feedback-guided auto-annotation pipeline that generates CoT with Qwen2.5-VL-72B, applies three filters (format, decision correctness vs. GT, and logic validated by Qwen3-235B), and iteratively re-annotates failures with error feedback. Post-training employs a two-stage progressive reinforcement fine-tuning with GRPO: Stage 1 optimizes CLIP-based semantic similarity between dreamed and GT images, then Stage 2 optimizes L2 trajectory distance, ensuring each reasoning transition is aligned before the next is optimized.

## Results

On nuScenes open-loop (without ego status), MindDriver achieves 0.53 m average L2 error and 0.17% collision rate under ST-P3 metrics, and 0.93 m L2 / 0.38% collision under UniAD metrics, outperforming all prior VLM-based and non-VLM baselines. With ego status, it further improves to 0.33 m L2 and 0.12% collision (ST-P3). For future frame generation, MindDriver achieves the best FID of 9.4, surpassing diffusion-based methods (GEM: 10.5, Drive-WM: 15.8) and the image-only CoT method FSDrive (10.1). On closed-loop Bench2Drive, MindDriver scores 65.48 Driving Score with 39.55% Success Rate, competitive with methods trained on substantially larger datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest VLM-based competitor FSDrive (image-only CoT, NeurIPS 2025), MindDriver reduces average L2 from 0.55 m to 0.53 m and collision from 0.21% to 0.17% (ST-P3, no ego status), confirming that prefixing textual reasoning before image generation improves both accuracy and safety. Against AutoVLA (text-only CoT, NeurIPS 2025), MindDriver achieves 0.53 m vs. 0.48 m L2 and 0.17% vs. 0.13% collision (ST-P3, with ego status), showing that the dreamed future image further reduces collisions. On closed-loop Bench2Drive, MindDriver (DS 65.48, SR 39.55%) outperforms DriveAdapter (DS 64.22, SR 33.08%) and ReasonPlan (DS 64.01, SR 34.55%), but trails AutoVLA (DS 78.84, SR 57.73%), which is trained on a substantially larger multi-dataset corpus (nuPlan, CARLA-Garage, etc.). For future frame generation, MindDriver achieves FID 9.4, beating GEM (10.5), FSDrive (10.1), and all diffusion-based baselines (Drive-WM 15.8, GenAD 15.4). Against classical end-to-end methods (VAD, UniAD, OccWorld, BEV-Planner), MindDriver consistently achieves the lowest L2 and collision rates across both ST-P3 and UniAD evaluation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

MindDriver extends the VLM-based autonomous driving line initiated by FSDrive [55], which first proposed generating future scene images as a visual CoT intermediary to bridge semantic and physical spaces. However, FSDrive relies solely on image reasoning without explicit planning-oriented textual guidance, while text-only CoT methods (e.g., AutoVLA [64]) suffer from a fundamental misalignment between linguistic semantics and trajectory geometry. MindDriver departs from both by introducing a *progressive* three-stage pipeline—textual semantic reasoning, guided visual imagination, then physical trajectory prediction—unified within a single autoregressive LLM via VQ-VAE visual tokens. The training strategy also departs from standard SFT by introducing a feedback-guided auto-annotation pipeline and a two-stage progressive GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning that separately optimizes image-semantic alignment and trajectory accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive multimodal reasoning framework offers a principled way to close the semantic-to-physical gap that plagues both text-only and image-only CoT approaches in driving VLMs, and the feedback-guided annotation pipeline provides a reusable recipe for generating high-quality multimodal reasoning data without manual labeling. However, the closed-loop results remain behind AutoVLA (which trains on substantially more data), and the system is limited to front-view image generation at 128×192 resolution with 1 Hz inference speed on a single GPU, constraining real-world deployment. Open questions include whether richer multi-view or higher-resolution visual imagination would yield further gains, whether the progressive RFT strategy transfers to other VLM backbones, and how the approach scales to longer-horizon or more complex interactive scenarios.
