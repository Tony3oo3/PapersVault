## Problem

Ultra-high-resolution remote sensing images (4000–5000 px) contain rich fine-grained detail but overwhelm existing vision-language models that rely on a single-pass, passive perception paradigm. Dynamic-resolution and token-pruning approaches force the model to process increasingly redundant visual tokens when seeking finer detail, and no prior benchmark explicitly supervises the active search-and-zoom process needed to overcome this bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261891/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a UHR RS image (4000–5000 px) and a text question, but initially consumes only a 512-px downsampled version for global context. For region- or object-level questions, the model outputs a bounding box, which is used to crop the corresponding region from the original full-resolution image and re-feed it as a second visual input. The final output is a structured chain-of-thought containing the predicted ROI BBox, the cropped high-resolution patch, and a natural-language answer, evaluated on both answer accuracy (WordNet similarity > 0.8) and APO IoU between predicted and ground-truth ROIs.

## Method

ZoomEarth is built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B and implements an adaptive cropping-zooming loop: the model first reasons over a coarse global view, then actively predicts a BBox for the relevant ROI, crops it from the original UHR image, and performs detailed perception on the zoomed patch. Training proceeds in two stages—SFT on 1,000 CoT-annotated samples teaches the localization-cropping-answering format, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 2,500 samples to build a robust tool-use policy. The key reward innovation is a Region-Guided term r_{R-G} = sigmoid(α/(distance+ε)) that rewards predicted BBoxes by their Euclidean distance to the ground-truth center, providing dense gradient signal where standard IoU would be zero due to the large spatial scale of UHR scenes. The accompanying LRS-GRO benchmark (1,224 images, 13,245 questions across 17 types at global/region/object levels) is annotated via a semi-automatic pipeline combining manual BBox labeling with GPT-4o question generation, explicitly distinguishing region-level (multi-object planned areas) from object-level (single homogeneous objects) ROIs.

## Results

On the LRS-GRO benchmark, ZoomEarth (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, 512×512 input) achieves 53.76% average accuracy and 34.39% APO IoU, outperforming all baselines including the larger InternVL3-8B (53.67% avg. acc) and VLM-R3-7B (50.17% avg. acc, 19.93% APO IoU). In zero-shot evaluation on three external UHR RS benchmarks, ZoomEarth attains 40.97% average accuracy, surpassing VLM-R3 (37.88%) and InternVL3 (38.43%). The cropping-zooming tool contributes an average +4.39% accuracy gain across four datasets, and the Region-Guided reward proves more impactful than the standard IoU reward for UHR RS localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZoomEarth (3B, 512×512) beats the closest competitor InternVL3-8B (53.67% avg.) by a narrow 0.09% margin overall, but gains a decisive +4.00% on object-level tasks (51.80 vs. 47.80) and +1.53% on region-level tasks (46.11 vs. 44.58), while using a far smaller model and lower input resolution. Against VLM-R³-7B, the only other tool-calling baseline, ZoomEarth improves average accuracy by 3.59% (53.76 vs. 50.17) and more than doubles the APO IoU (34.39% vs. 19.93%), demonstrating superior active localization. In zero-shot transfer, ZoomEarth leads all baselines on MME-RealWorld-RS (44.10 vs. 41.00 for InternVL3), XLRS-Bench (40.20 vs. 39.60 for LLaVA-OV-1.5), and GeoLLava-8k (38.61 vs. 37.60 for InternVL3). The reproduced Geochat* baseline (fine-tuned on the same LRS-GRO data) scores 46.09%, confirming that ZoomEarth's advantage stems from the active perception framework rather than additional RS pre-training data. Notably, ZoomEarth trails larger models on global-level questions (63.09 vs. 71.60 for InternVL3), which is expected since global context benefits from higher-resolution holistic input rather than cropping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the passive perception paradigm (dynamic resolution and token pruning) that dominates UHR RS VQA, instead adopting an active cropping-zooming strategy inspired by tool-use VLMs like VLM-R3 and Groundlight AI. However, it explicitly rejects the OCR/object-detection-based supervision those methods rely on, which is inapplicable to RS imagery lacking textual cues and featuring region-level rather than discrete-object targets. The training pipeline combines SFT with GRPO (following the DeepSeekMath lineage), but introduces a Region-Guided reward motivated by the spatial co-occurrence structure of geographic scenes, addressing the sparsity of IoU rewards at UHR scales. The LRS-GRO benchmark extends prior RS VQA datasets (RSVQA, XLRS-Bench, MME-RealWorld) by adding hierarchical spatial levels and explicit ROI annotations for evaluating the perception process itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The APO IoU metric and the LRS-GRO benchmark fill a genuine gap: no prior RS dataset evaluates whether a model actually localizes the relevant region, not just whether it answers correctly. The toolkit integration (cloud removal, segmentation, editing) positions ZoomEarth as a potential foundation for autonomous RS agents, though the training-free setting and 3B scale limit immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the active perception paradigm scales to even larger scenes (beyond 5000×5000), whether the Region-Guided reward generalizes to non-RS domains, and whether the semi-automatic annotation pipeline can be further automated to reduce the manual filtering bottleneck. The finding that SFT alone makes cropping *harmful* (Table 5) underscores that RL is not optional for tool-use reasoning, a point that may influence broader VLM training strategies.
