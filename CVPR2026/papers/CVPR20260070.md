## Problem

Existing topology reasoning methods for driving scenes rely on sequential cascade pipelines where centerline detection and topology reasoning are optimized independently, leading to inconsistent feature representations. Their topology reasoning modules typically employ simplified MLP heads that fail to capture complex spatial dependencies in urban road networks, and they largely neglect fine-grained point-to-instance (P2I) relationships that are critical for accurate connectivity prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260070/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB camera images (7 views for subset_A, 6 for subset_B) resized to 1024×775, projected into a 200×100 BEV grid via BEVFormer-style view transformation. It outputs vectorized 3D centerlines (11×3 3D offsets per centerline from 200 or 300 hierarchical queries), a lane-to-lane topology matrix, lane-to-traffic topology, and traffic element detections. Each centerline is represented by 11 point-level queries plus 1 instance-level query, and the DDT mask is initialized to zeros before the first decoder layer to avoid restricting early interactions.

## Method

TopoHR introduces a cyclic interaction architecture where the hierarchical centerline decoder and topology reasoning module iteratively exchange information: P2I and I2I attention weights flow forward from the decoder to the topology module, while inferred topological relations flow backward as attention masks into the decoder. The hierarchical centerline decoder jointly models point-level and instance-level queries through three specialized modules: an Instance-Aware Module (masked-attention with a discrete distance transform mask plus topo-aware attention), a Point-Aware Module (line-aware attention with P2P constraints plus cross-attention), and a Hierarchical Integrator (cross-attention between point and instance queries followed by learnable aggregation). The topology reasoning module predicts both I2I relations (dual-MLP similarity plus attention-weight MLP) and P2I relations (point-to-instance similarity plus attention-weight MLP), fusing them for final topology output. Training uses an adaptive topological loss with exponential negative-sample weighting to penalize high-confidence false positives, combined with focal loss for detection and dice+CE loss for DDT segmentation supervision.

## Results

On OpenLane-V2 subset_A, TopoHR-L (300 queries, 48 epochs) achieves DET_l=37.6, TOP_ll=34.6, TOP_lt=35.6, and OLS=50.8, setting new state-of-the-art records. On the more challenging subset_B, TopoHR-L reaches DET_l=43.6, TOP_ll=39.7, TOP_lt=28.0, and OLS=53.4. The headline improvements over the previous best (RelTopo) are +3.8 in DET_l and +5.4 in TOP_ll on subset_A, and +11.0 in DET_l and +7.9 in TOP_ll on subset_B. The lighter TopoHR variant (200 queries, 24 epochs) already surpasses all prior methods on subset_A with OLS=49.9 while running at 12.6 FPS on a single RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoHR outperforms all seven baselines on both subset_A and subset_B across DET_l, TOP_ll, and TOP_lt. Against the closest competitor RelTopo (300 queries, no SD Map), TopoHR-L gains +3.8 DET_l and +5.4 TOP_ll on subset_A, and a substantial +11.0 DET_l and +7.9 TOP_ll on subset_B, where the larger query count and extended training yield the most dramatic gains. TopoHR (200 queries) already surpasses RelTopo on subset_A in DET_l (36.1 vs 33.8) and TOP_lt (34.6 vs 32.2) despite using fewer queries. Methods leveraging SD Map priors (SMERF, SEPT, TopoLogic+SD) are also outperformed by TopoHR without any external map input, confirming that the cyclic architecture and hierarchical representation compensate for the absence of SD Map. TopoFormer, which uses geometric distance guidance, is surpassed by +1.4 DET_l and +7.5 TOP_ll (TopoHR-L vs TopoFormer on subset_A). The DET_t metric remains nearly identical to TopoLogic since the same traffic decoder is shared, indicating that all gains stem from the centerline and topology components rather than traffic detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoHR directly extends TopoLogic's sequential 'detect-then-reason' pipeline by replacing it with a cyclic architecture where centerline detection and topology reasoning iteratively exchange attention weights and topological predictions. It departs from the instance-level-only query representation used in TopoNet, TopoMLP, and TopoLogic by introducing a hierarchical representation that jointly models point-level and instance-level queries, and further introduces point-to-instance (P2I) topological relations that prior work has not explicitly modeled. The discrete distance transform mask for centerline segmentation supervision draws on Mask2Former's masked-attention mechanism but repurposes it for the virtual-object nature of centerlines, and the adaptive topological loss builds on reparameterized cross-entropy ideas to address class imbalance in topology prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cyclic co-evolution of detection and topology reasoning challenges the long-standing assumption in this line of work that the detector must be solved before the reasoning module can be optimized, offering a principled alternative to cascaded architectures. The P2I relation provides a new inductive bias for topology: connectivity is not purely an instance-level property but is also encoded in how individual points of one centerline relate to the global representation of another. However, all results are on a single benchmark (OpenLane-V2) with one backbone (ResNet-50), and the paper does not explore whether the cyclic mechanism generalizes to other map element types (e.g., crosswalks, stop lines) or to multi-modal inputs. Open questions include whether the hierarchical representation and cyclic loop remain beneficial at larger scales or with more complex urban topologies, and whether the 12.6 FPS throughput is sufficient for real-time deployment in production autonomous driving stacks.
