## Problem

Composed Video Retrieval requires modeling fine-grained compositional transformations across entity, action, and scene dimensions when a user specifies a textual modification to a reference video. Existing supervised methods overfit to noisy web-scale triplets and fail to generalize to unseen domains or fine-grained scenarios, while training-free re-ranking approaches are bottlenecked by static first-stage candidate filtering that prematurely discards relevant targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video (7–17 seconds) and a short modification text (4–5 words on EgoCVR/WebVid-CoVR, up to 31 words on Dense-WebVid-CoVR) as a composed query, and outputs a ranked list of target videos from a database of thousands of clips. Because the modification text is typically very brief and ambiguous, the pipeline first expands it into a structured entity-action-scene description via an MLLM before using it for similarity scoring and reasoning. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no task-specific training.

## Method

MoRe is a two-stage training-free framework. Stage 1 (Recall-Oriented Video Candidate Selection) formulates candidate retrieval as a three-objective optimization problem—visual similarity, textual relevance, and multimodal consistency—and iteratively applies non-dominated sorting with crowding-distance diversity to extract a Pareto-optimal candidate set of size K=15, replacing the single-objective visual filtering used by prior methods. A fine-grained semantic prediction sub-module uses Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct to decompose the reference video into entity, action, and scene tokens, then predicts how the modification text transforms each dimension, producing a fused target description that replaces the raw modification text in the similarity objectives. Stage 2 (Pairwise Transformation Reasoning) avoids absolute relevance scoring (where MLLMs are unreliable) by asking the MLLM to compare two candidates at a time and judge which better embodies the intended transformation, aggregating pairwise win/loss votes into a global ranking. Feature extraction uses LanguageBind encoders for visual, textual, and fused multimodal embeddings.

## Results

On EgoCVR, MoRe achieves R@1 = 20.4 (Global) and R@1 = 45.0 (Local), surpassing the best prior zero-shot method Dense-CoVR (14.6 R@1) by +5.8 points. On WebVid-CoVR, MoRe reaches R@1 = 63.0, exceeding the strongest zero-shot baseline FDCA-BLIP (52.2) by +10.8 points and even outperforming transfer-learning methods like ECDE (60.1). On Dense-WebVid-CoVR, MoRe attains R@1 = 49.6, edging out Dense-CoVR (48.1) by +1.5 points. The method also demonstrates strong efficiency: with K=10 candidates, runtime drops to 10.4s while maintaining R@1 = 17.2, still above the TFR-CVR baseline (14.1 at 8.2s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRe consistently outperforms all baselines across all three benchmarks under the zero-shot setting. On EgoCVR, it surpasses the strongest prior method Dense-CoVR by +5.8 R@1 (20.4 vs. 14.6) and the training-free TFR-CVR by +6.3 R@1 (20.4 vs. 14.1), with particularly large gains at R@5 (+12.6) and R@10 (+17.2) over Dense-CoVR. On WebVid-CoVR, MoRe achieves 63.0 R@1, beating the best zero-shot method FDCA-BLIP (52.2) by +10.8 and even exceeding transfer-learning methods such as ECDE (60.1) and CoVR-BLIP (53.1) without any fine-tuning. On Dense-WebVid-CoVR, the margin is smaller (+1.5 R@1 over Dense-CoVR), suggesting that when modification texts are already very detailed (avg. 31 words), the advantage of explicit entity-action-scene decomposition is less pronounced. The recall-oriented candidate selection module contributes to a 10-point increase in target-video recall within the candidate pool (74.5% vs. 64.5% on EgoCVR) compared to the visual-similarity-only filtering used by TFR-CVR, which is critical for downstream MLLM reasoning accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of composed video retrieval (CoVR) work: supervised triplet-based methods (CoVR-BLIP, ECDE, FDCA, Dense-CoVR) that learn from large-scale video-text-video data, and the training-free re-ranking paradigm of TFR-CVR that uses visual similarity filtering followed by text-based re-ranking. MoRe departs from both by replacing single-objective candidate filtering with a Pareto-based multi-objective selection (drawing on nondominated sorting from multi-objective optimization) and by substituting absolute relevance scoring with pairwise MLLM comparison over entity-action-scene decomposed semantics. The conceptual lineage is thus: supervised contrastive CoVR → training-free two-stage re-ranking → multi-objective candidate selection + MLLM pairwise reasoning, with the key novelty being the explicit multi-objective formulation and the decomposition of transformation semantics into three interpretable dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a zero-shot MLLM-based pipeline can outperform supervised methods trained on millions of triplets (by +5.8 R@1 on EgoCVR and +10.8 on WebVid-CoVR) suggests that compositional video retrieval may not require task-specific training data, reducing the barrier to deploying CoVR in new domains. However, the 23.9-second per-query runtime and the O(K²) pairwise comparison complexity raise practical scalability concerns for large databases. Open questions include whether the entity-action-scene decomposition generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks, whether the multi-objective selection can be made more efficient for very large candidate pools, and whether the approach extends to longer videos or more complex multi-step transformations.
