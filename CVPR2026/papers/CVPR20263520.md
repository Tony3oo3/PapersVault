## Problem

Existing single-image human mesh recovery systems inherit a fixed intact-limb topology from parametric body models like SMPL-X, causing them to hallucinate missing distal segments and produce unstable optimization around amputation sites for individuals with limb loss. No prior method explicitly reconstructs residual-limb surfaces or adapts the kinematic optimization to limb-loss anatomy, leaving a gap for clinically meaningful 3D representations needed in prosthetics, rehabilitation, and gait analysis.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and 2D keypoints in LDPose format (135 OpenPose WholeBody keypoints for body, hands, and face plus 8 residual-limb endpoints) and produces a watertight 3D SMPL-X mesh with an explicit convex stump surface, along with full SMPL-X parameters (pose, shape, expression, camera) and a per-limb residual factor λ_r ∈ [0,1] encoding proportional termination along the kinematic chain. The framework is entirely optimization-based with no learned components, and operates as a plug-in module on any HMR backbone that outputs SMPL-X parameters, so it can be layered atop both optimization-based (SMPLify-X) and regression-based (HSMR) pipelines.

## Method

ResiHMR introduces two components. First, a Residual Anchor-Factor Optimization stage jointly optimizes, per residual limb, an anchor joint position J_a and a scalar residual length factor λ_r via L-BFGS, where the 3D stump endpoint is defined as a linear interpolation R_r = J_a + λ_r(J_t − J_a) along the kinematic segment; the loss combines 2D reprojection error against the residual keypoint, anchor displacement regularization, and a length-preservation term encoding anthropometric priors. Second, a Residual-Limb Reconstruction stage performs explicit mesh editing: segmentation-guided coarse pruning removes distal part labels, a signed-distance cut plane at the optimized endpoint trims the remaining geometry, and boundary cleanup with concentric vertex rings generates a smooth, convex, watertight stump surface. The key design choice is to keep the full SMPL-X kinematic chain intact while parameterizing where each limb terminates, rather than removing joints from a fixed topology, which preserves global skeleton consistency and enables the module to be applied as a post-processing step on any existing HMR output.

## Results

On the LDPose-LimbLoss Evaluation Dataset (255 images), ResiHMR with the HSMR backbone achieves the best Body Kpts 2D MPJPE (24.75 px) and a dramatic reduction in Res-Limb 2D MPJPE from 73.61 px (HSMR alone) to 23.19 px, while maintaining competitive Intact Kpts accuracy (24.87 px vs. 24.56 px). Under the SMPLify-X backbone, ResiHMR reduces Intact Kpts MPJPE from 41.32 to 37.40 px and mIoU from 0.662 to 0.703. CameraHMR attains the highest mIoU (0.752), slightly ahead of ResiHMR (HSMR) at 0.741, but ResiHMR dominates on all keypoint-based metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResiHMR (HSMR) outperforms all five baselines on Body Kpts MPJPE (24.75 px vs. 28.27–51.07 px) and Res-Limb MPJPE (23.19 px vs. 73.61–129.59 px), with the largest margin over HSMR alone (68% reduction in residual-limb error). On Intact Kpts MPJPE, ResiHMR (HSMR) is nearly tied with HSMR (24.87 vs. 24.56 px) and clearly beats SMPLify-X (37.40 vs. 41.32 px), TokenHMR (31.73 px), and PromptHMR (46.88 px). The only metric where ResiHMR does not lead is mIoU: CameraHMR achieves 0.752 versus ResiHMR (HSMR)'s 0.741, a 0.011 gap, while PromptHMR is close at 0.751. Under the SMPLify-X backbone, ResiHMR improves every metric over the SMPLify-X baseline (e.g., Res-Limb MPJPE 98.36 vs. 129.59 px; mIoU 0.703 vs. 0.662), confirming the plug-in nature of the framework. Overall, ResiHMR is the only method that explicitly localizes residual-limb endpoints rather than relying on a midpoint proxy, which explains its dominant advantage on the Res-Limb metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResiHMR extends the SMPLify-X optimization pipeline and the LDPose 2D keypoint schema by introducing topology-adaptive kinematic constraints and explicit residual-limb surface reconstruction, departing from the fixed intact-limb topology that all prior parametric body models (SMPL, SMPL-X, SKEL) assume. The closest prior work, AJAHR, predicts joint existence but still terminates limbs at intact SMPL joints rather than at anatomically meaningful stump locations, while LDPose provides 2D residual-limb endpoints without any 3D parametric representation. ResiHMR's key conceptual departure is treating the residual limb as a first-class geometric entity with its own endpoint parameter (λ_r) and watertight stump surface, rather than a shortened or masked segment within a fixed skeleton.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a large underserved population (tens of millions with traumatic limb loss) and provides 3D representations directly usable for prosthetic socket assessment, gait analysis, and rehabilitation monitoring—applications where residual-limb geometry is clinically critical. Key limitations include the absence of ground-truth 3D annotations (evaluation is 2D reprojection only), a small 255-image dataset, and reliance on per-instance L-BFGS optimization that limits throughput. Open questions include whether learned residual-limb priors (e.g., diffusion models of stump geometry) can replace or augment the optimization, and whether calibrated multi-view capture can provide the 3D supervision needed for stronger evaluation.
