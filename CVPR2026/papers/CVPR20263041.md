## Problem

Existing T2V evaluation benchmarks rely on a no-reference, prompt-only paradigm, which makes it difficult to concretely attribute sample-level failures (e.g., object hallucination, motion artifacts, temporal inconsistency) and to produce accountable, interpretable judgments. The growing dependence on opaque MLLM judge pipelines further introduces biases, hallucinations, and inconsistent scoring, limiting reliability as evaluation authorities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a reference video (real-world clip ≤20 s, 1–3 coherent shots, from 9 thematic categories) and a generated video (resized to 448 px short side) as paired inputs, along with a tightly evidence-bounded text prompt. It outputs four dimension scores on a [0, 100] scale (via standard-normal CDF mapping of z-scores) plus structured per-sample diagnostic evidence: entity-attribute matchings, event-graph alignments, foreground/background motion trajectories, and per-item VQA answers with signal-bound consistency scores.

## Method

Ref4D-VideoBench constructs a 600-video reference dataset with structured spatio-temporal annotations (entity-attribute lists, event boundaries via DDM-Net, semantic descriptions via VideoLLaMA3) and a 12-metric evaluation suite across four dimensions. For semantic alignment, it performs Hungarian bipartite matching on MLLM-extracted entity-attribute sets to compute CatCov, AIC, and Hallucination Penalty; for motion, it tracks sparse foreground/background points to derive relative-motion discrepancies (direction, magnitude, smoothness) plus degeneration indicators (repeated-frame and low-speed ratios); for event temporal consistency, it builds event graphs and scores alignment (EGA), Allen-interval relation consistency (ERel), and coverage/redundancy (ECR); for world knowledge, it distills per-video physical/causal/safety rules into a targeted VQA question bank scored by an MLLM with importance-weighted aggregation. For the first three dimensions, a dimension-specific linear aggregator is fitted via least squares on z-scored human MOS over a sample-ID split, replacing ad-hoc weighting while preserving interpretability of the atomic feature space.

## Results

On Ref4D-VideoBench, the proposed reference-based framework achieves SRCC of 0.822 (Semantic), 0.659 (Motion), 0.755 (Event), and 0.847 (World Knowledge) with human MOS, substantially outperforming all no-reference baselines. The best baseline (Q-Align) reaches only 0.358 SRCC on Motion, while UMTScore peaks at 0.304 on World Knowledge; no baseline exceeds 0.42 on any correlation metric. At the model level, the framework preserves near-monotonic rankings across eight T2V models, with closed-source models (ViduQ2, JiMeng, Sora2) outperforming open-source ones (CogVideoX, Wan2.1, VideoCrafter2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against nine no-reference baselines spanning generic similarity metrics (CLIPScore, BLIPScore), quality scorers (Q-Align, VideoScore), multi-dimensional benchmarks (VBench, UMTScore), and specialized probes (NeuS-V, T2V-CompBench, VideoPhy). The proposed method wins decisively on all four dimensions: on Semantic, it reaches 0.822 SRCC versus the best baseline Q-Align at 0.317; on Motion, 0.659 versus Q-Align's 0.358; on Event, 0.755 versus UMTScore's 0.220; and on World Knowledge, 0.847 versus Q-Align's 0.391. Generic metrics (CLIPScore, BLIPScore) are weakest overall, with SRCC below 0.24 on most dimensions, confirming that dimension-agnostic similarity scores cannot capture fine-grained scene-grounded quality. Specialized metrics like T2V-CompBench motion_binding (0.053 SRCC) and NeuS-V (0.197) are narrowly scoped and underperform even on their target dimensions. The gap is most dramatic on Event (0.755 vs. 0.220 best baseline) and World Knowledge (0.847 vs. 0.391), where reference-grounded evidence provides the largest advantage over prompt-only evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant no-reference evaluation paradigm (VBench, EvalCrafter, T2VScore, CLIPScore/BLIPScore) by introducing reference videos as structured spatio-temporal evidence for fine-grained T2V assessment. It repurposes MLLMs (MiniCPM-V-4.5, VideoLLaMA3-7B) not as opaque judges but as evidence extractors within a transparent, rule-based pipeline, and borrows formal tools—Hungarian bipartite matching, Allen interval relations, point-tracking motion analysis—from compositional evaluation (T2V-CompBench) and temporal reasoning literature. The key conceptual shift is from prompt-conditioned scoring to reference-grounded, per-sample diagnosis across four orthogonal dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a real accountability gap: no-reference metrics cannot concretely attribute failures like object hallucination or temporal inconsistency at the sample level, which limits their utility for reward design, safety auditing, and model debugging. The 600-video dataset with tightly bounded prompts and structured JSON annotations is a reusable resource for the T2V community. However, the framework is limited to reference-available, controlled scenarios and inherits assumptions from its underlying MLLMs and video-analysis modules; the authors themselves note that combining reference-based and no-reference evaluation remains open, as does generalization to open-domain generation without a ground-truth exemplar.
