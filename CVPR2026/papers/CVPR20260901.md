## Problem

Transforming perspective-view (PV) image features into bird's-eye-view (BEV) representations for semantic segmentation remains difficult due to depth ambiguity and occlusion. Existing cycle-consistency approaches (CVTM, FocusBEV) only partially exploit the reverse mapping—enforcing consistency in semantically vague feature spaces or applying projections without an explicit cycle-consistency loss—and they integrate the inverse module into the inference architecture, increasing model size and computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260901/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surround-view RGB camera images (scaled to 224×448) and produces a 200×200 BEV semantic segmentation map covering a 100 m × 100 m area around the ego-vehicle, with three classes (drivable area, vehicle, pedestrian). During training, the VT model additionally predicts a normalized height map (values in [0,1]) that is discarded at inference. The inverse view transformation (IVT) network, which maps BEV maps back to PV segmentation maps, is used exclusively during training, so inference complexity and model size remain unchanged from the base VT model.

## Method

CycleBEV introduces a training-only IVT network with a dual-branch architecture: a CNN encoder produces multi-resolution BEV feature maps, and per-camera PV query maps attend to these features via Transformer cross-attention with perspective-projection-based positional embeddings, ultimately decoding PV segmentation maps. The VT model is regularized by a cycle-consistency BCE loss that compares the IVT's reconstructed PV maps against pseudo-labels (generated by a Mask2Former trained on nuScenes PV annotations). Two additional objectives extend the regularization: (1) height-aware geometric regularization, where the VT model predicts a height map that the IVT uses to recover vertical cues absent in the 2D BEV grid, and (2) cross-view latent consistency, which aligns the VT model's BEV features with the IVT's multi-resolution BEV features via a smooth-ℓ₁ loss. The IVT is first pre-trained on GT BEV–PV pairs, then jointly fine-tuned with the VT model under a combined loss; at inference the IVT and height decoder are simply discarded.

## Results

On nuScenes, CycleBEV consistently improves all four VT baselines across drivable area, vehicle, and pedestrian classes. The largest average mIoU gains are +2.96 for LSS (28.59→31.55), +2.30 for PETRv2 (39.54→41.84), +2.08 for CVT (39.70→41.78), and +1.02 for BEVFormer (41.00→42.02). Per-class gains reach up to 0.74 mIoU (drivable area), 4.86 mIoU (vehicle), and 3.74 mIoU (pedestrian). The IVT network is used only during training, so inference cost and model size remain unchanged.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CycleBEV outperforms both competing cycle-consistency methods (CVTM [32] and FocusBEV [34]) across all four baselines and all three semantic classes. CVTM yields only marginal average gains (≤0.41 mIoU) and even slightly degrades BEVFormer (−0.02), while FocusBEV consistently hurts performance—dropping LSS by 3.37 mIoU and PETRv2 by 23.28 mIoU—because it applies feature-space projections without an explicit cycle-consistency loss. Against the BEV auto-encoder supervision [35], CycleBEV's IVT-derived alignment loss produces substantially larger gains (e.g., +0.77 vs. +0.18 mIoU average for CVT), confirming that the reverse-mapping representations encode richer 3D cues than a conventional auto-encoder. Compared to semantic-aware backbone training [33], CycleBEV achieves larger and more consistent improvements; in particular, the feature-exclusion variant (SA-II) degrades performance, whereas CycleBEV always improves. The gains are most pronounced for occluded objects (visibility <40%), where CycleBEV adds +0.54 mIoU for vehicles and +0.36 mIoU for pedestrians on CVT, while CVTM fails to improve or slightly degrades these categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the cycle consistency principle from unpaired image-to-image translation (CycleGAN) for BEV semantic segmentation, building on CVTM [32] and FocusBEV [34], which were the first to introduce view cycle consistency to this task. The key departure is architectural: rather than integrating the inverse view transformation (IVT) module into the inference pipeline (as both prior methods do), CycleBEV uses it exclusively as a training-time regularizer, and replaces the ill-posed task of reconstructing PV images with the well-posed task of reconstructing PV segmentation maps. Two additional regularization objectives—height-aware geometric regularization and cross-view latent consistency—extend the cycle-consistency signal into geometric and representation spaces, going beyond the feature-space alignment used by CVTM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is plug-and-play and adds zero inference cost, making it immediately applicable to any existing VT model across all three major paradigms (LSS, cross-attention, deformable cross-attention). The consistent gains across four diverse architectures (up to 4.86 mIoU on vehicle) suggest the regularization captures a genuine information bottleneck in PV-to-BEV mapping rather than an architecture-specific artifact. Open questions include whether the approach extends to temporal VT models (the authors note this as future work), whether it transfers to other BEV tasks like 3D detection or occupancy, and whether the IVT network's reliance on pseudo-labeled PV segmentation maps (generated by Mask2Former) limits its ceiling in scenes with heavy occlusion or novel object categories.
