## Problem

Existing chain-of-thought approaches for visual generation apply textual reasoning either before synthesis (as a fixed pre-plan) or after synthesis (as costly post-refinement), but never during the generation process itself. This leaves a gap: there is no mechanism for on-the-fly, co-evolving textual reasoning that can guide upcoming local regions and reflect on previously synthesized content within a single generative trajectory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260787/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt and produces one RGB image, but the image is synthesized progressively in K spatial regions (default K=3: upper background, central content, lower background) within a single autoregressive token sequence. Between each region's generation, the model emits a textual reasoning thought and an optional reflection score, all as part of the same forward pass—no separate image-to-image call or additional inference round is required. The visual context from prior regions is carried forward as raw visual tokens at the end of the sequence rather than as an image input.

## Method

TwIG interleaves textual reasoning with autoregressive visual generation through three coupled components: (i) a scheduling step that partitions the canvas into K regions, (ii) a reasoning step where the understanding pass (ULM_u) produces a localized sub-caption conditioned on the prompt, all prior thoughts, and all prior visual tokens, and (iii) a reflection step where ULM_u scores the just-generated region and optionally triggers a local re-generation guided by a revised sub-caption. The key architectural insight is that both the reasoning insertion and the local correction preserve a single text-to-image trajectory: the textual pre-context is simply extended (or a thought is replaced) while previously generated visual tokens remain untouched at the end of the sequence, eliminating the need for image-to-image capability. The authors explore three training routes—zero-shot prompting, SFT on a curated TwIG-50K dataset (nine subtasks spanning thinking, generation, and reflection), and RL via a customized TwIG-GRPO that jointly optimizes all understanding and generation passes with a shared reward from an ensemble of four reward models (HPS v2, GroundingDINO, GIT, and a fine-tuned ORM).

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, TwIG-RL (the full pipeline: zero-shot prompting → SFT on TwIG-50K → TwIG-GRPO) achieves the best scores across all eight evaluation categories, reaching 82.49 on Color, 61.28 on Shape, 73.19 on Texture, 34.06 on 2D-Spatial, 38.87 on 3D-Spatial, 31.99 on Non-Spatial, 61.93 on Numeracy, and 40.31 on Complex. Compared to the Janus-Pro-7B baseline, TwIG-RL improves by +18.90 (Color), +26.00 (Shape), +23.83 (Texture), +13.45 (2D-Spatial), +5.93 (3D-Spatial), +1.14 (Non-Spatial), +20.61 (Numeracy), and +4.72 (Complex). Even the zero-shot variant (TwIG-ZS) already surpasses the baseline on most categories without any parameter updates, and SFT provides further consistent gains with reduced variance across random seeds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TwIG-RL outperforms all ten compared baselines on T2I-CompBench++ across every metric where both methods report scores. Against the strongest competitor T2I-R1 [23], TwIG-RL leads by +1.19 (Color), +2.76 (Shape), +0.76 (Texture), +0.28 (2D-Spatial), +0.96 (Numeracy), and +0.38 (Complex), while also reporting 3D-Spatial (38.87) and Non-Spatial (31.99) where T2I-R1 does not. Against FLUX.1 [25], a strong closed-source diffusion model, TwIG-RL wins on all eight categories, with the largest margins on Color (+8.42), Shape (+4.10), and 2D-Spatial (+5.43). Compared to the Janus-Pro-7B baseline that serves as the shared backbone, the full TwIG pipeline yields gains of +18.9 to +26.0 points on attribute binding and +13.45 on 2D-Spatial, demonstrating that interleaved reasoning is the primary driver of improvement rather than model scale. The zero-shot variant already surpasses GoT [11], Show-o+PARM [17], and PixArt-α [6] on most categories without any training, while SFT narrows the gap to FLUX.1 and T2I-R1, and RL closes it entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established paradigms for incorporating reasoning into visual generation: think-before-generation (e.g., GoT, ImageGen-CoT, T2I-R1) which produces a fixed plan prior to synthesis, and think-after-generation (e.g., PARM, Reflect-DiT) which critiques and revises a completed image. TwIG inverts the modality flow inspired by image-text interleaved reasoning in visual understanding LMMs, weaving textual thoughts *into* the unfolding autoregressive generation trajectory rather than bracketing it. It builds on Janus-Pro as a unified understanding-generation backbone and adapts GRPO for joint reinforcement of thinking, generation, and reflection passes, while distinguishing itself from concurrent works (IRG, Uni-CoT) that still treat visual synthesis as a monolithic block.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TwIG demonstrates that interleaved reasoning during generation is feasible and beneficial even zero-shot, suggesting current ULMs possess latent capacity for on-the-fly multimodal coordination that prior think-before/after pipelines leave untapped. The progressive gains from SFT (stability) and RL (substantial quality jumps, e.g., +7.91 on Color, +7.04 on Spatial over SFT) outline a clear training recipe for future interleaved generators. Key open questions remain: adaptive scheduling is unreliable with current ULMs, the framework is validated only on a single autoregressive model (Janus-Pro-7B) for T2I, and extension to diffusion-based generators, video, or 3D is left as future work. The authors explicitly frame this as a preliminary study, so the practical impact hinges on whether the paradigm generalizes beyond the tested setting.
