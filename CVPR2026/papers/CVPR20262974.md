## Problem

Existing video datasets are split between 3D geometric perception (providing depth, camera poses, and point tracks) and video generation (providing semantic captions), leaving no unified large-scale resource that supports both domains simultaneously. This gap fundamentally constrains the development of World Foundation Models that must both perceive dynamic 3D scenes and generate controllable, physically grounded video content.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests in-the-wild video clips filtered to >1080p resolution, ≥10 FPS, and 5-second-to-1-minute duration, sourced from HD-VILA-100M, Panda-70M, Koala-36M, and Pexels (668k additional clips). For each of the ~1M resulting clips (156.7M frames, 4,000+ hours), the output is a structured scene description (settings, subjects, actions), per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics, temporally consistent high-resolution depth maps, binary dynamic motion masks, and long-term 3D point tracks (also projected to 2D for compatibility). A curated subset, SceneScribe-MVS, further constrains dynamic object inclusion via geometric re-projection scoring while preserving camera motion diversity.

## Method

The annotation pipeline integrates three specialized models in sequence: Qwen2.5-VL-72B generates structured semantic descriptions using dynamic resolution processing and absolute temporal encoding; MegaSaM (built on DROID-SLAM) produces motion masks, camera parameters (via bundle adjustment augmented with mono-depth priors from Depth Anything and UniDepth), and temporally consistent depth maps; and TAPIP3D leverages MegaSaM's depth and poses to project 2D features into 3D world space for robust long-term 3D point tracking. Curation employs Qwen2.5-VL-72B as an automated content evaluator across six quality dimensions, TransNetV2 for shot-boundary segmentation of non-continuous videos, and a multi-view re-projection strategy (Algorithm 1) that computes 2D/3D/photometric consistency errors to score and filter scenes by object motion intensity. The entire pipeline is parallelized across 1,000+ NVIDIA H20 GPUs (~150k GPU-hours), and downstream validation on depth estimation (MoGe), 3D/4D reconstruction (VGGT, MonST3R), point tracking (CoTracker3, SpatialTrackerV2), and pose-controlled video generation (AC3D) confirms annotation quality.

## Results

Across monocular depth estimation (MoGe), adding SceneScribe-1M to TartanAir training data improves average Rel from 6.17 to 6.14 and δ₁ from 93.8% to 94.0% (scale-invariant), with the largest gains on iBims-1 (Rel 3.46→3.26) and DDAD (Rel 9.17→8.95). For text/pose-to-video generation (AC3D), SceneScribe-1M yields the most dramatic improvements: TransErr drops from 0.374 to 0.318, RotErr from 0.039 to 0.026, FVD from 38.20 to 35.15, and CLIP score rises from 28.62 to 29.98. In 3D reconstruction (VGGT), AUC₃₀ improves from 89.5 to 89.9, and in 4D reconstruction (MonST3R), ATE decreases from 0.108 to 0.099. Dynamic point tracking (CoTracker3) shows a mean δᵢₛ improvement from 76.6 to 77.4 across TAP-Vid benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Since SceneScribe-1M is a dataset rather than a model, all comparisons are between each downstream model trained with vs. without SceneScribe-1M data. The most substantial gains appear in text/pose-to-video generation (AC3D), where SceneScribe-1M reduces TransErr by 15% (0.374→0.318) and RotErr by 33% (0.039→0.026), attributed to the dataset's superior camera pose annotation precision and content diversity over RealEstate10K. For monocular depth estimation (MoGe), improvements are modest but consistent across most benchmarks (e.g., iBims-1 Rel 3.46→3.26, DDAD Rel 9.17→8.95), though Sintel δ₁ slightly decreases (73.4→72.0). In 3D reconstruction (VGGT), camera pose estimation improves (AUC₃₀ 89.5→89.9) while point map estimation slightly degrades (ACC 0.873→0.890, Comp 0.482→0.504). For 4D reconstruction (MonST3R), pose estimation improves (ATE 0.108→0.099, RPE trans 0.042→0.038) while depth estimation remains comparable (Rel 0.335→0.320). Dynamic point tracking (CoTracker3, SpatialTrackerV2) shows small but uniform gains across all TAP-Vid and TAPVid-3D sub-benchmarks, confirming annotation quality comparable to purpose-built tracking datasets like Kubric and PointOdyssey.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneScribe-1M sits at the intersection of two previously separate dataset lineages: 3D perception datasets (RealEstate10K, CO3Dv2, PointOdyssey) that provide geometric annotations like depth and camera poses but lack rich semantics, and video generation datasets (HD-VILA-100M, Panda-70M, Koala-36M) that offer detailed captions but no geometric structure. The paper builds its annotation pipeline on MegaSaM (for depth, camera poses, and motion masks) and TAPIP3D (for 3D point tracks), leveraging Qwen2.5-VL-72B for structured semantic descriptions. Concurrent WFM-oriented datasets like Sekai-Real (~0.4M clips) and SpatialVID (~2M clips) move in the same direction but are either smaller in scale or lack consistent 3D point tracks, which SceneScribe-1M provides across all ~1M clips.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying structured captions, dense video depth, camera poses, motion masks, and 3D point tracks at a scale of 4,000+ hours, SceneScribe-1M addresses a concrete bottleneck for world foundation model research: the absence of a single resource that supports both 3D perception and controllable video generation. The multi-view subset (SceneScribe-MVS) with its camera/object motion disentanglement is a practical contribution for multi-view reconstruction tasks. However, annotation quality is entirely dependent on the underlying automated models (MegaSaM, TAPIP3D, Qwen2.5-VL-72B), and the modest downstream improvements suggest the annotations, while useful, are not yet a drop-in replacement for purpose-built ground truth. Open questions include whether the annotation noise floor limits ceiling performance on tasks like metric depth or whether the pipeline can be extended to longer, more complex scenes.
