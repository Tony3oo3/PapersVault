## Problem

Unified 3D anomaly detection models that jointly train across multiple categories suffer from Inter-Category Entanglement (ICE), where latent features from different object classes overlap in the embedding space. This causes the model to reconstruct objects under incorrect semantic priors (e.g., a chair partially reconstructed with table-like geometry), producing unreliable anomaly scores and mislocalized defects. The core gap is that existing unified frameworks attempt reconstruction before establishing the object's semantic identity, rather than conditioning reconstruction on correctly disentangled category-level semantics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud of N×3 coordinates as input, from which g centers are selected via Farthest Point Sampling and multi-resolution kNN neighborhoods (at k/2, k, 2k) are encoded by a pretrained PointMAE local encoder. Training uses only normal samples (4 per category in both benchmarks); Real3D-AD provides single-view test scans while Anomaly-ShapeNet uses complete 360° point clouds. At inference, the model outputs per-point anomaly scores (reconstruction errors normalized per-instance and smoothed with a 1D Gaussian filter) and an object-level score defined as the maximum per-point score, alongside the reconstructed feature tokens used to compute these errors.

## Method

SeDiR reformulates unified 3D-AD as semantically conditioned reconstruction through three components. Coarse-to-Fine Global Tokenization (CFGT) encodes multi-resolution neighborhoods around FPS centers with a pretrained PointMAE encoder, prepends a learnable Adaptive Context Token to capture instance-level semantics, and fuses pooled features across all resolutions with the encoded ACT into a category-aware global token, supervised by an auxiliary classification head and cross-scale cosine alignment loss. Category-Conditioned Contrastive Learning (C3L) uses a dynamic buffer of 64 embeddings to enforce intra-category compactness and inter-category separation via supervised contrastive loss, directly mitigating ICE. The Geometry-Guided Decoder (GGD) performs reconstruction where the global semantic token acts as the attention query over encoded feature tokens, with a geometry-derived bias (encoding surface normal and curvature variations) added to attention logits to enforce category-consistent geometric reconstruction. The total training objective combines the C3L loss (contrastive + classification + cosine alignment) with an MSE reconstruction loss, trained exclusively on normal data.

## Results

On Real3D-AD (12 categories), SeDiR achieves 81.0% object-level AUROC as a unified model, outperforming the best category-specific method ISMP (76.7%) by 4.3% and the prior unified method MC3D-AD (78.2%) by 2.8%. On Anomaly-ShapeNet (40 categories), SeDiR reaches 93.3% O-AUROC, surpassing MC3D-AD (84.2%) by 9.1% and the best category-specific baseline PO3AD (83.9%) by 9.4%. For point-level localization, SeDiR attains 80.6% P-AUROC on Real3D-AD and 81.0% on Anomaly-ShapeNet, ranking second-best on both datasets behind ISMP (83.6%) and PO3AD (89.8%) respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeDiR outperforms all 11 baselines on object-level AUROC in both the unified and category-specific settings. Against the closest unified competitor MC3D-AD, SeDiR gains +2.8% on Real3D-AD (81.0% vs. 78.2%) and a substantial +9.1% on Anomaly-ShapeNet (93.3% vs. 84.2%), with the largest per-category margins on Gemstone (+6.7%) and Starfish (+8.8%). Among category-specific methods, ISMP (76.7%) and PO3AD (76.5%) are the strongest on Real3D-AD, both trailing SeDiR by 4+ points; on Anomaly-ShapeNet, PO3AD (83.9%) leads the category-specific group but is still 9.4% below SeDiR. For point-level localization, SeDiR (80.6% / 81.0%) ranks second on both datasets, behind ISMP on Real3D-AD (83.6%) and PO3AD on Anomaly-ShapeNet (89.8%), indicating that while semantic disentanglement strongly benefits object-level detection, fine-grained localization still benefits from memory-based or pseudo-anomaly supervision strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MC3D-AD, the first unified multi-category 3D anomaly detection framework, by diagnosing its core failure mode—Inter-Category Entanglement (ICE)—where latent features from different categories overlap and cause reconstruction under incorrect semantic priors. Conceptually, it draws on the 2D unified anomaly detection literature (e.g., DINO, MambaAD) that uses contrastive learning to enforce inter-class separation, but extends this to 3D by coupling semantic disentanglement with geometric consistency through a geometry-guided decoder. The key departure from prior unified 3D-AD is the reframing of the problem as 'semantically conditioned reconstruction': the model must first establish category identity before attempting to reconstruct, rather than decoding blindly from entangled latent features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ICE diagnosis provides a useful and actionable explanation for why unified 3D-AD models fail, shifting the focus from reconstruction quality to semantic identity establishment. The framework's three-component design (CFGT, C3L, GGD) is modular and could inform future unified 3D perception tasks beyond anomaly detection. However, the method still requires category labels during training, limiting its applicability to open-set or zero-shot scenarios, and evaluation is confined to two datasets with relatively few categories (12 and 40). Open questions include whether the approach scales to hundreds of categories, whether it generalizes to other 3D representations (meshes, voxel grids), and whether the contrastive disentanglement strategy remains effective when category boundaries are ambiguous.
