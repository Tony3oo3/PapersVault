## Problem

CNN-based domain adaptive object detection methods are limited by local receptive fields, restricting feature alignment to local regions and failing to capture global domain-invariant attributes such as inter-region relationships and spatial/semantic dependencies among objects. Transformer-based alternatives address this with global attention but incur quadratic computational cost that hinders practical deployment, leaving a gap for an efficient mechanism to model long-range domain dependencies in detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261468/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method jointly processes labeled source-domain images and unlabeled target-domain images in alternating batches (batch size 2 per domain) to compute supervised, adversarial, and pseudo-label losses. At inference, a single RGB target-domain image is fed through the YOLO-World-based detector to produce per-instance class labels and bounding boxes. The text modality enters only at initialization: CLIP text encoder outputs for prompts like 'A photo of [Class]' generate fixed category prototype vectors used by the OA-SSM module.

## Method

DA-Mamba inserts two novel modules into a YOLO-World CNN detector: an Image-Aware SSM (IA-SSM) in the FPN backbone and an Object-Aware SSM (OA-SSM) in the detection head. Each module uses a dual pipeline—a convolution branch for local features and a Mamba (selective SSM) branch for global long-range dependencies—concatenated and projected back via an up-sample convolution. IA-SSM conditions the pipeline with a learnable image-level visual prompt broadcast across spatial locations, while OA-SSM generates pixel-wise instance-level prompts by projecting features through a meta-net into a category similarity matrix and multiplying with CLIP-derived category prototypes, thereby encoding both spatial co-occurrence and semantic category relationships. Training combines image-level and instance-level adversarial losses (with Gradient Reverse Layer and instance masks), source-domain supervised loss, and target-domain pseudo-label semi-supervised loss, achieving global-local domain alignment at both image and instance granularity with only linear-time SSM computation.

## Results

DA-Mamba achieves 58.1% mAP on Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes, 48.7% mAP on Cityscapes→BDD100K, 52.5% mAP on Pascal VOC→Clipart, and 43.8% mAP on Pascal VOC→Comic, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. The headline improvement is +2.2% mAP over the previous SOTA (DA-Pro, 55.9%) on C→F and +5.4% over DATR (43.3%) on C→B. Notably, DA-Mamba achieves these gains with only 53.1% of the FLOPs of Transformer-based DAOD methods (148G vs. 279G for DATR) and 51.2% inference memory of DA-Pro.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA-Mamba outperforms all 26 compared baselines across all four cross-domain benchmarks. On C→F, it surpasses the strongest VLM-based method DA-Pro [38] (55.9%) by 2.2% and the Transformer-based DATR [5] (53.4%) by 4.7%, while also beating the recent large self-supervised model DT [36] (55.4%) by 2.7%. On C→B, the margin over the previous SOTA DATR [5] (43.3%) widens to 5.4%, indicating stronger gains under larger domain shift. On P→Clp, DA-Mamba (52.5%) exceeds CAT [35] (49.1%) by 3.4% and DA-Ada [40] (48.0%) by 4.5%; on P→Cmc it beats D-adapt [31] (40.5%) by 3.3%. The method also improves over its own CNN-only baseline (UDA) by 5.8–6.8% mAP, confirming that the SSM modules contribute the majority of the gain rather than architectural changes alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) CNN-based DAOD methods that use adversarial feature alignment (DA-Faster, SIGMA++, CAT) but are limited to local receptive fields; (2) Transformer-based DAOD methods (DATR, MTM) that capture global dependencies at quadratic computational cost; and (3) the recent Mamba/SSM literature demonstrating linear-time long-range sequence modeling. DA-Mamba departs from both prior lines by proposing a hybrid CNN-SSM architecture where SSMs replace the quadratic attention mechanism, and by introducing domain-aware visual prompts (image-level for the backbone, CLIP-prototype-conditioned instance-level for the head) that go beyond vanilla Mamba's domain-agnostic state updates. The GRL-based adversarial training framework is inherited directly from DA-Faster, while the base detector is YOLO-World.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in DAOD: Transformer-based global alignment methods are too expensive for real-time deployment, while CNN-only methods cannot model cross-region dependencies. By showing that SSMs can substitute for attention with linear complexity while maintaining (or exceeding) accuracy, DA-Mamba offers a deployable path to global domain-invariant feature learning. The ablation revealing that standard Mamba without domain-aware prompts yields only marginal gains (1.0–2.9%) versus the full DA-Mamba (5.7–6.8%) suggests that the prompt mechanism is critical, not merely the SSM backbone. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond YOLO-World to two-stage detectors or DETR-style architectures, whether the CLIP text-encoder dependency for category prototypes is necessary or could be replaced by learned embeddings, and how the method scales to open-vocabulary or multi-domain settings.
