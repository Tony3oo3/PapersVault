## Problem

Generating 3D medical volumes with anatomically consistent structures under user-controllable multimodal conditions remains unresolved: 2D diffusion methods lack inter-slice coherence, full 3D diffusion is computationally prohibitive, and existing conditional models rely on predefined segmentation masks that limit diversity and controllability. No prior framework combines intuitive structural inputs (sketches) with semantic descriptions (text) to guide 3D medical volume synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260038/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D binary edge-map sketch of the target organ (extracted via Canny edge detection from 3D Slicer projections, or hand-drawn) and a structured text description of 3D geometry (auto-generated by GPT-4o-mini from multi-view snapshots plus geometric metrics like volume, sphericity, and principal-axis lengths, or freely edited by users). All volumes are resampled to 128×128×128. The output is a 3D segmentation mask followed by a corresponding 3D CT/MRI volume, both in voxel space after latent-space diffusion and autoencoder decoding.

## Method

Sketch2CT is a two-stage latent diffusion pipeline. Stage 1 generates 3D segmentation masks conditioned on fused sketch-text features: a capsule-attention encoder extracts structural features from the sketch, a sentence transformer embeds the text, and two fusion modules—TSFE (FiLM-based text-guided channel modulation of sketch features) and CGFM (two-level cross-attention followed by self-attention for global semantic alignment)—produce a joint multimodal embedding injected via cross-attention into a 3D UNet denoiser operating in AutoencoderKL latent space. Stage 2 synthesizes the medical volume by concatenating the segmentation latent with the noisy image latent at each diffusion timestep, guiding a second latent diffusion model to produce anatomically aligned tissue appearance. Both stages use v-prediction parameterization with a 1000-step DDPM scheduler, trained with Adam at lr 1e-4 on an NVIDIA H200 GPU.

## Results

On the four evaluation datasets, Sketch2CT achieves the best FID on CHAOS liver (33.7), AVT aorta (36.9), and Decathlon heart (65.1), and the best LPIPS on CHAOS liver (0.332), AVT aorta (0.321), and Decathlon heart (0.269). On Decathlon liver, Seg-Diff edges out Sketch2CT slightly (FID 34.8 vs. 36.5; LPIPS 0.335 vs. 0.328), likely benefiting from its larger training set. In downstream segmentation (Table 3), a model trained on Sketch2CT-synthesized images reaches Dice scores within 0.008–0.112 of the real-data baseline across all four datasets, substantially outperforming Med-DDPM (gap up to 0.411) and MedGen3D (gap up to 0.142).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sketch2CT outperforms Med-DDPM by a wide margin on all four datasets (e.g., FID 33.7 vs. 114.4 on CHAOS liver; LPIPS 0.332 vs. 0.220), confirming that 2D slice-level diffusion without structural conditioning produces poor volumetric coherence. Against MedGen3D, Sketch2CT improves FID by 9.9–31.7 points and LPIPS by 0.021–0.084 across datasets, demonstrating that sketch-text multimodal conditioning yields more anatomically faithful volumes than MedGen3D's random-mask two-stage pipeline. The closest competitor is Seg-Diff: Sketch2CT wins on CHAOS liver (FID 33.7 vs. 37.8), AVT aorta (36.9 vs. 38.9), and Decathlon heart (65.1 vs. 68.4), but narrowly loses on Decathlon liver (FID 36.5 vs. 34.8; LPIPS 0.328 vs. 0.335), which the authors attribute to Seg-Diff benefiting from the larger 131-scan training set in a 2D setting. In the faithfulness evaluation (Table 2), Sketch2CT achieves the highest Dice against input masks on all four datasets (e.g., 0.912 on Decathlon liver vs. 0.892 for Seg-Diff), and in downstream segmentation (Table 3) it produces the smallest gap to the real-data baseline, confirming superior structural preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sketch2CT extends the two-stage 3D medical generation paradigm established by MedGen3D (mask generation followed by volume synthesis) by replacing the random or predefined mask condition with a user-controllable multimodal input: a 2D sketch and a geometric text description. The sketch encoder draws on capsule networks (Sabour et al.) for structure-aware feature extraction, while the text-guided modulation borrows the FiLM conditioning mechanism (Perez et al.) to adaptively reweight sketch channels. The latent diffusion backbone and v-prediction parameterization follow the standard Stable Diffusion / MONAI pipeline, and the segmentation-conditioned volume synthesis is analogous to ControlNet-style channel concatenation. The key conceptual departure is the first use of sketch-and-text as joint structural-semantic conditions for 3D medical volume generation, bridging the sketch-based 3D shape literature (Wu et al., Guillard et al.) with the medical diffusion literature (Seg-Diff, MedGen3D).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, low-cost data augmentation pipeline where a clinician or researcher can draw a simple sketch and write a short geometric description to produce anatomically coherent 3D CT volumes with paired segmentation masks—addressing the persistent data scarcity problem in medical imaging without requiring large annotated datasets. However, the current scope is limited to single-organ generation on small datasets (20–131 volumes), and the text descriptions are auto-generated from ground-truth geometry during training, raising questions about how well the model generalizes to truly novel user-authored descriptions at inference. Open questions include extending to multi-organ and disease-specific generation, validating with broader expert panels, and determining whether the capsule-attention sketch encoder is essential or whether simpler convolutional encoders would suffice.
