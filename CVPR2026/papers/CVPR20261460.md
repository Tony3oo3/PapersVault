## Problem

Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) relies on expensive triplet annotations that inevitably contain Noisy Triplet Correspondence (NTC), where the modification text mismatches the reference-target image pair. Existing noise-robust methods depend on the 'small-loss hypothesis' to identify noise, but this breaks down for 'hard noise'—samples where the reference and target images are visually similar yet the text is wrong—causing them to be misclassified as clean. The paper identifies three overlooked challenges in this setting: Modality Suppression (visual similarity drowns out the text mismatch signal), Negative Anchor Deficiency (no structured semantic opposite exists to push noisy samples toward), and Unlearning Backlash (naive push-away operations damage nearby clean representations).

## Input / Output

At inference, the method accepts a reference RGB image and a short modification text as a composed query, and outputs a ranked list of target images from a gallery in a shared embedding space. During training, the model consumes triplets (reference image, modification text, target image) that may be corrupted by NTC noise at configurable rates (0%–80%). Features are extracted via BLIP-2's Q-Former into Q×D dimensional vectors, and the retrieval metric is cosine similarity in the resulting cone-structured embedding space.

## Method

ConeSep is a three-stage framework built on BLIP-2's Q-Former. (1) Geometric Fidelity Quantization (GFQ) estimates a noise boundary B by averaging cosine similarities over K random Gaussian-sampled image pairs with shuffled text, then applies a nonlinear fidelity function F(ReLU(s−B))²·(ReLU(s−B)−1) to partition each batch into clean and noisy sets, circumventing modality suppression. (2) Negative Boundary Learning (NBL) introduces a learnable negative prompt P_neg to construct a 'Diagonal Negative Composition' F_neg for each query, trained via a dual-path objective: a target-oriented sigmoid loss that makes F_neg the semantic opposite of the corresponding target while aligning with non-matching targets, and a query-oriented hinge loss that constrains cos(F_c, F_neg) near zero for orthogonal separation. (3) Boundary-based Targeted Unlearning (BTU) formulates the correction of noisy samples as a B×2B entropy-regularized Optimal Transport problem (solved by Sinkhorn-Knopp), where a mask matrix blocks noisy samples from transporting to their positive targets and protects clean samples from being pulled toward negative boundaries; the resulting transport plan is blended with hard labels into a soft target Y, and a KL-divergence loss drives the model to forget noisy correspondences while preserving clean alignment.

## Results

On FashionIQ (average across Dress/Shirt/Toptee), ConeSep achieves 55.58% R@10 and 75.88% R@50 at 0% noise, and maintains 49.25% R@10 / 70.30% R@50 even at 80% noise, outperforming the best robust baseline (HABIT) by 0.89% R@10 at 80% noise. On CIRR, ConeSep reaches 82.34 Avg(R@5, Rsub@1) at 0% noise and 76.38 at 80% noise, surpassing INTENT (75.97) and HABIT (75.86) at the highest noise level. The performance gap over SOTA robust baselines widens with increasing noise: on FashionIQ, the R@10 advantage over HABIT grows from +0.20% (0% noise) to +0.89% (80% noise).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConeSep consistently outperforms all six baselines on FashionIQ across every noise level (0%–80%), with the largest margins appearing at high noise: at 80% noise it beats TME by +2.58% R@10 and +1.70% R@50, and beats the closest competitor INTENT by +0.74% R@10. On CIRR, ConeSep leads at 0% (82.34 vs. TME 82.01), 20% (80.43 vs. TME 79.74), and 80% (76.38 vs. INTENT 75.97) noise on the Avg(R@5, Rsub@1) metric, though at 50% noise HABIT narrowly edges it (78.87 vs. 78.75). Traditional CIR methods (SSN, CALA, SPRC) degrade catastrophically under noise—SSN drops from 51.40 to 22.29 AVG on FashionIQ—confirming their unsuitability for the NTC setting. Among robust NTC methods, the performance gap between ConeSep and the best baseline widens monotonically with noise on FashionIQ (from +0.44% AVG at 0% to +0.83% at 80%), indicating that ConeSep's targeted unlearning mechanism becomes increasingly valuable as the noise ratio grows.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Noisy Triplet Correspondence (NTC) problem first formalized by TME (Li et al., CVPR 2025) for composed image retrieval, departing from the prevailing 'identification-correction/suppression' paradigm used by both TME and broader noisy-correspondence-learning methods (e.g., RCL [42]). Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: noisy-label robustness in cross-modal retrieval and machine unlearning, repurposing optimal transport (Sinkhorn-Knopp) as a mechanism for targeted feature-space correction rather than the gradient-ascent 'push-away' operations common in unlearning. The robust contrastive loss is inherited from RCL [42], and the reverse-matching target matrix draws on the Sigmoid Loss formulation [100], but the cone-space geometric framing and the diagonal negative composition as a structured anchor are novel architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NTC problem is practically critical because real-world CIR datasets (FashionIQ, CIRR) inevitably contain annotation errors from human subjectivity or LVLM hallucination, and the paper's articulation of three specific failure modes (modality suppression, negative anchor deficiency, unlearning backlash) provides a useful diagnostic framework for the community. However, the empirical gains over strong SOTA baselines (HABIT, INTENT, TME) are modest—roughly 0.5–1.5% in average recall—and the method is tightly coupled to the BLIP-2/Q-Former backbone, leaving open whether the cone-geometry and OT-unlearning principles transfer to other architectures or to more complex noise structures (e.g., multi-modal corruption). A key open question is whether the fidelity threshold ω and the OT regularization ε can be made adaptive per-dataset rather than fixed hyperparameters.
