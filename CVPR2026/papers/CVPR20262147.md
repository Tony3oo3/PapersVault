## Problem

Existing MLLM personalization methods are limited to static, single-turn settings: input-augmentation approaches (e.g., Yo'LLaVA, RAP) recognize fixed user concepts but cannot track preference shifts, while alignment techniques (e.g., ALIGNXPERT, PAS) assume static user traits and fail to adapt as personality evolves over long-term interactions. No prior framework simultaneously handles dynamic multimodal memory management, multi-turn retrieval reasoning, and evolving personality alignment in a unified, self-contained system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262147/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each dialogue turn, the agent receives a text instruction, an optional RGB image, and a timestamp, alongside a sliding-window dialogue context (bounded by a time threshold t_s) and the full personalized memory database. The response stage outputs a personality-aligned text response after potentially multiple rounds of agentic retrieval across semantic, episodic, and procedural memories. The update stage then produces a revised 5-dimensional Big Five personality vector (converted to a textual summary) and performs CRUD operations on all four memory types, with core and procedural memories retaining only their latest versions while episodic and semantic memories accumulate chronologically.

## Method

PersonaVLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and operates as a two-stage agent: a Response stage where the model iteratively reasons and issues structured retrieval queries (specifying time period and keywords) against a four-type memory database (core, semantic, episodic, procedural), and an Update stage that evolves the user's personality via a Personality Evolving Mechanism (PEM) using exponential moving average with cosine-decay smoothing over Big Five scores, while proactively extracting and updating memories. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on 78k synthetic multi-turn samples (generated via a pipeline over 500 personas from PersonaHub) to establish memory-management and reasoning skills, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining LLM-judged accuracy, reasoning-answer consistency, and format adherence, capping retrieval at three attempts per trajectory. The key architectural insight is decoupling personality tracking (a continuous 5-d vector updated per-turn) from knowledge storage (discrete, typed, timestamped memories), enabling the model to adapt tone and content independently as both the user's traits and factual context evolve.

## Results

Under the 128k-context RAG setting, PersonaVLM_RL achieves 71.05% overall accuracy on Persona-MME and 47.28% on PERSONAMEM, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by 12.04% and 9.4% respectively. On the alignment sub-task (Table 2), PersonaVLM scores 92.22% on Persona-MME (128k) and 49.60 overall on P-SOUPS, leading the next-best model (Qwen3-30B-A3B) by 9.16% and 2.46% respectively. In open-ended generation evaluated by Gemini-2.5-Pro, PersonaVLM achieves a 79% win rate against GPT-4o (vs. 16% loss). The two-stage training (SFT → RL) yields an additional 5.35% average gain on Persona-MME over SFT alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPT-4o (128k-Full), PersonaVLM_RL wins on overall Persona-MME (71.05 vs. 69.23, +1.82 pts) and PERSONAMEM (47.28 vs. 45.32, +1.96 pts), with particularly large margins in Growth (+11.33) and Behavior (+4.61), though GPT-4o retains a lead on Memory (84.44 vs. 69.28). Against the open-source baselines at 128k, PersonaVLM_RL outperforms InternVL3-38B by 3.87% on Persona-MME and 0.72% on PERSONAMEM, InternVL3-8B by 8.62% and 10.66%, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B by 14.39% and 33.0%, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 16.57% and 44.2%. On the alignment benchmark (Table 2), PersonaVLM leads Qwen3-30B-A3B by 9.16% on Persona-MME (128k: 92.22 vs. 83.06) and by 2.46% on P-SOUPS overall (49.60 vs. 47.14), while also surpassing InternVL3-38B by 29.21% on Persona-MME alignment. Notably, a straightforward RAG augmentation of Qwen2.5-VL-7B actually degrades preference understanding by up to 9.33% in the 32k setting, underscoring that naive retrieval is insufficient without the structured memory architecture and multi-step reasoning that PersonaVLM provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

PersonaVLM unifies three previously siloed personalization streams—adaptation-based (Yo'LLaVA, MyVLM), augmentation-based (RAP, Memory OS), and alignment-based (ALIGNXPERT, PAS)—into a single agentic framework that addresses their shared limitation of static, single-turn personalization. Conceptually, it draws on MemGPT's memory architecture for structured user profiles, GRPO for multi-turn reasoning training, and the Big Five personality model with an exponential moving average for dynamic trait tracking. The key departure is from treating personalization as a fixed input-augmentation or output-alignment problem to framing it as a continuous, two-stage (response + update) process where both memory and personality evolve over long-term interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Persona-MME (2,000+ cases, 14 fine-grained tasks, 7 dimensions) fills a notable gap: no prior benchmark holistically evaluates long-term, dynamic, multimodal personalization, and the paper benchmarks over 10 models on it. The framework's self-contained design (no proprietary model dependencies, local operation) addresses real privacy concerns that limit adoption of prior augmentation-based approaches. Open questions include whether the Big Five + EMA mechanism captures the full complexity of evolving user traits, how well the framework generalizes beyond the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, and whether synthetic training data from PersonaHub adequately represents real user diversity. The authors acknowledge that memory recall still lags GPT-4o with full context, suggesting the retrieval mechanism has room for improvement.
