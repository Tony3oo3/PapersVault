## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval methods are trained on semantically aligned image-text pairs using consistency-driven objectives, yet at inference they must compose unaligned reference images with modification texts that describe new or conflicting visual concepts. This training-inference distribution shift means models never learn to perceive or reconcile cross-modal discrepancies, which are the core signal for compositional reasoning in CIR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263782/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language modification text, extracts super-patch-level visual features and phrase-level textual features, and produces a single compositional query embedding via adaptive Slerp interpolation. The query is matched against pre-encoded gallery embeddings by cosine similarity. Notably, the phrase-guided masking module used during training is discarded at inference; only the super-patch representation and difference-aware composition modules operate on the unmodified reference image.

## Method

DiffComp follows a Differentiate-then-Compose paradigm with three modules. (1) Contextual Semantic Super-patch (CSS) groups S×S adjacent CLIP patches into spatially coherent super-patches, each independently encoded through the visual encoder to yield localized [CLS] representations. (2) Phrase-guided Selective Masking (PSM) extracts noun/verb/prepositional phrases via Stanford CoreNLP, computes phrase–super-patch cosine similarities, and uses Gumbel-Softmax sampling with a straight-through estimator to differentially mask super-patches most aligned with the most salient phrase, injecting the binary mask into attention via a log(1−M) bias to produce a semantically pruned image. (3) Difference-aware Composition (DAC) performs dual-level spherical linear interpolation: a global Slerp between the differentiated image and text features, and a per-super-patch Slerp whose weight α_j is inversely proportional to that patch's text similarity, so text-aligned regions retain more visual content while divergent regions are pulled toward the text. Training uses a three-term loss (unidirectional contrastive alignment, difference perception encouraging removal of salient-phrase-aligned regions, and masking-ratio regularization) on 10% of CC3M, with no triplet annotations required.

## Results

On FashionIQ (CLIP ViT-L/14), DiffComp achieves 32.62 R@10 and 53.85 R@50 (average across Dress, TopTee, Shirt), surpassing the previous best HIT (30.30 / 51.00) by +2.32 and +2.85 points respectively. On CIRR (CLIP ViT-L/14), DiffComp reaches 32.36 R@1, 62.90 R@5, 74.88 R@10, and 93.23 R@50, outperforming HIT (27.90 / 57.60 / 70.50 / 90.40) by up to +4.46 points at R@1. Under the BLIP ViT-L/16 backbone, DiffComp attains 39.72 R@1 and 95.43 R@50 on CIRR, and 18.35 mAP@5 on CIRCO, setting new state-of-the-art on both benchmarks. On GeneCIS, DiffComp leads on Focus (17.95 R@1) and Object (14.89 R@1) dimensions while remaining competitive on Change subtasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiffComp outperforms all ten baselines on FashionIQ and CIRR under both CLIP and BLIP backbones. Against the strongest pseudo-word method HIT [20], DiffComp gains +2.32 R@10 on FashionIQ (CLIP) and +4.46 R@1 on CIRR (CLIP), with similar margins under BLIP (+0.21 R@10 FIQ, +2.82 R@1 CIRR). Against the feature-composition baseline SlerpTAT [13], DiffComp improves CIRR R@1 by +1.42 (CLIP) and +5.74 (BLIP), while also surpassing it on CIRCO mAP@5 under BLIP (18.35 vs. 17.84) despite using one-eighth the training data. On CIRCO under CLIP, SlerpTAT retains a slight edge (16.98 vs. 16.19 mAP@5), indicating that global Slerp fusion remains competitive when paired with a larger training set. On GeneCIS, DiffComp leads on Focus (17.95 vs. 15.45 R@1 over PrediCIR) and Object (14.89 vs. 14.80 R@1) but trails PrediCIR on Change (15.95 vs. 17.80 R@1), suggesting the difference-driven paradigm excels at attribute/object identification but is less effective for explicit change reasoning. Pseudo-word methods (Pic2Word, SEARLE-XL, Context-I2W, LinCIR) and knowledge-enhanced KEDs are consistently outperformed by 5–8 points on CIRR R@1, confirming the advantage of explicit cross-modal difference modeling over token-concatenation or alignment-only strategies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant consistency-driven paradigm in ZS-CIR (exemplified by pseudo-word methods like Pic2Word, SEARLE, Context-I2W, and feature-composition approaches like SlerpTAT) by arguing that enforcing cross-modal alignment during training is fundamentally misaligned with the inference task, which requires reasoning over unaligned reference images and modification texts. It builds on CLIP/BLIP vision-language backbones and borrows tools from masked image modeling (Gumbel-Softmax differentiable masking) and spherical linear interpolation, but repurposes them within a 'differentiate-then-compose' loop: first inducing controlled semantic discrepancies via phrase-guided selective masking, then adaptively fusing features based on the degree of discrepancy. The super-patch representation bridges the gap between CLIP's global [CLS] semantics and unstructured patch tokens, a design choice informed by prior observations that CLIP patch hidden states encode holistic rather than local semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled reframing of ZS-CIR as a discrepancy-reconciliation problem rather than an alignment problem, which could influence how future methods design their training objectives. The practical efficiency (4-hour training on a single A100 with 10% CC3M, lightweight modules adding negligible parameters) makes the approach accessible. However, the reliance on Stanford CoreNLP for phrase extraction introduces a non-differentiable external dependency, and the masking module is only active during training, leaving open whether the learned discrepancy sensitivity transfers robustly to out-of-distribution modification texts. Whether the difference-driven paradigm generalizes beyond the tested CLIP/BLIP backbones and to more complex multi-attribute modifications remains to be validated.
