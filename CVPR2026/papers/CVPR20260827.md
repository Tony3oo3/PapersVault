## Problem

Video generation models struggle with complex outputs that require significant semantic branching or repeated high-level reasoning about what should happen next. Existing approaches either treat text as static conditioning (T2V) or generate a complete text plan before any video (Think2V), failing to leverage the model's language reasoning capabilities dynamically during generation. TV2TV addresses this by decomposing video generation into an interleaved text-and-video process where the model 'thinks in words' about upcoming content before 'acting in pixels' to produce frames.

## Input / Output

The model takes a meta-prompt, an initial conditioning frame, and optionally intermediate text interventions at arbitrary timesteps, producing video at 320×192 resolution and 16 FPS in atomic chunks of 4 frames (0.25 s each in latent space, 240 tokens per chunk). The output is not only video but also interleaved text plans describing upcoming content, which users can inspect and edit mid-generation to steer the trajectory. The model dynamically decides when to switch between text and video modes via a special beginning-of-frame (BOF) token, so the generation schedule is emergent rather than fixed.

## Method

TV2TV adopts a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture with dedicated text and video towers (3B or 8B parameters each) that share global self-attention over the full interleaved sequence while using modality-specific attention and FFN parameters. It jointly optimizes a language-modeling cross-entropy loss and a rectified-flow matching loss, with the text tower initialized from a pretrained Llama model. At inference, the model autoregressively generates text tokens until it emits a BOF token, triggering a switch to flow-matching denoising of one video frame chunk via an ODE solver (with optional classifier-free guidance), after which text generation resumes. Within each frame chunk, bidirectional attention is used; across the sequence, strict causal attention enforces temporal ordering, and both noisy and clean copies of each chunk are maintained in the input to support teacher-forced training. For real-world data lacking temporally aligned captions, a VLM-based pipeline generates interleaved action descriptions, yielding 8K hours of sports video for training.

## Results

On CS:GO controlled experiments, TV2TV is preferred over the T2V baseline 91% of the time in pairwise human evaluation (vs. 1% for T2V) and over Think2V 48% vs. 38%, with gains more pronounced for 64-second long-form videos. For fine-grained controllability, TV2TV achieves a 19-point improvement in intervention correctness over Think2V across four action types (left-click, jump, reload, backward) at both 1s and 3s intervention timestamps. On the 8K-hour sports dataset, TV2TV wins 54.0% vs. 34.7% against a T2V baseline and 53.3% vs. 41.3% against Think2V, and outperforms all external models (Cosmos2, MAGI-1, WAN 2.2) on prompt alignment, real-world fidelity, and overall preference, though it trails WAN 2.2 5B on raw visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the T2V baseline (no interleaved text, conditioned only on meta-prompt and initial frame), TV2TV dominates overwhelmingly: 91% preference on CS:GO short videos and 54.0% on sports data, confirming that interleaved textual planning substantially improves generation quality. Against Think2V (which generates a full text plan before any video), TV2TV still wins on visual quality (48% vs. 38% on CS:GO, 53.3% vs. 41.3% on sports) and shows a clear 19-point advantage in fine-grained controllability, demonstrating that interleaved (rather than sequential) text-video generation is critical for both quality and user steering. Against external video generation models on the sports domain, TV2TV outperforms Cosmos-Predict2 (2B and 14B) and MAGI-1 (4.5B and 24B) on prompt alignment, real-world fidelity, and overall preference, and matches MAGI-1 on visual quality. The one loss is against WAN 2.2 5B on raw visual quality, which the authors attribute to WAN's stronger general-domain pretraining; however, TV2TV still wins on prompt alignment and overall preference, suggesting the interleaved text mechanism provides a meaningful advantage in semantic coherence even when pixel-level quality is slightly lower.

## Relation to Prior Work

TV2TV extends the Transfusion paradigm [63]—which jointly learns next-token prediction and image diffusion—into the video domain by replacing image diffusion with rectified-flow video generation, and adopts the Mixture-of-Transformers architecture [29] with modality-specific towers initialized from a pretrained LLM (following LMFusion [40]). Its autoregressive chunked video generation design is closely related to MAGI-1 [44], but departs from it by maintaining separate clean and noisy latent copies per frame to support interleaved text conditioning. The key conceptual departure from prior omni models (Chameleon [12], BAGEL [19], Janus [52]) and interactive video models (Genie [10]) is the dynamic, model-driven switching between text and video generation modes, rather than fixed modality schedules or explicit per-step user input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By offloading high-level semantic planning to the language tower, TV2TV reduces the effective entropy of the video generation process and enables mid-generation text interventions that no prior T2V system supports at this granularity. The VLM-based data augmentation pipeline (8K hours of sports video with interleaved captions) offers a practical recipe for creating interleaved text-video corpora from existing video datasets, which could be extended to other domains. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon reasoning (beyond ~6-second windows), whether the 8B model's visual quality gap versus WAN 2.2 5B closes with more compute, and whether the controllability gains transfer to domains with less structured action spaces than sports or video games.
