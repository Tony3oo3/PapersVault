## Problem

Text-conditioned 3D hand motion generation lacks sufficient training data: existing hand-focused datasets (BOTH57M, ~1.8k motions) are prohibitively small due to manual annotation, while larger sign language video datasets contain no hand descriptions. The paper asks whether open-vocabulary language descriptions can drive a generative hand motion model, and how to scale motion-text pairs to make this feasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260092/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form English text description of hand motion (e.g., handshapes, finger configurations, locations, and movements) encoded as a CLIP text embedding, and produces a 274-dimensional SMPL-X upper-body motion sequence comprising 15 6D joint rotations per hand, 13 upper-body 6D joint rotations, and 16 face parameters. Generation uses 100 diffusion steps with classifier-free guidance (5% condition dropout during training). The text vocabulary spans ~1,500 words drawn from sign language descriptions, and the model is evaluated via motion-to-motion or motion-to-text retrieval using a dedicated THMR encoder.

## Method

The core contribution is a data pipeline that automatically constructs 1.3M motion-text pairs from BOBSL sign language videos: pseudo-glosses are matched to SignBank phonological attributes and rule-based HandMotionScript pose features, which are then converted into natural language descriptions by prompting Gemini 2.5 Pro (avoiding gloss names to prevent LLM hallucination). A THMR contrastive retrieval model performs k-medoids-based sign variant assignment to reduce label noise. The generative model, HandMDM, extends MDM-SMPL to the SMPL-X expressive body model by replacing the body-only parameterization with the full upper-body SMPL-X vector, using the same Transformer encoder architecture (CLIP text token, diffusion timestep, two register tokens, noisy motion) trained with MSE loss. The key design choice is operating in a high-scale, high-noise regime—accepting imperfect pseudo-labels, imprecise monocular reconstructions (HAMER + SMPLer-X stitched), and LLM-generated text—rather than requiring clean manual annotations.

## Results

On the ASL-Text test set, the BOBSL3DT-trained model with HandMotionScript (Phon.+HMS) achieves R@1=17.09 and R@3=35.43, substantially outperforming the ASL-Text-trained baseline (R@1=5.98, R@3=16.21) by ~2.9x on R@1. On MS-ZSSLR-W, the same model reaches R@1=15.39 and R@3=31.50 versus the MS-ZSSLR-W baseline (R@1=5.01, R@3=13.08). On the non-sign BOTH57M dataset, the zero-shot transfer model (R@1=18.61, R@3=40.70) also surpasses the BOTH57M-trained baseline (R@1=15.70, R@3=35.47). On the in-domain BOBSL3DT-Test (seen signs), the best model achieves R@1=21.68 and R@3=34.18 with FID=0.16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its BOBSL3DT-trained HandMDM against baselines trained from scratch on each target dataset's own training split. On ASL-Text, the zero-shot BOBSL3DT model (Phon.+HMS) achieves R@1=17.09 vs. 5.98 for the ASL-Text-trained baseline — a 2.9× improvement — and R@3=35.43 vs. 16.21. On MS-ZSSLR-W, the gap is even wider: R@1=15.39 vs. 5.01 (3.1×) and R@3=31.50 vs. 13.08. On the non-sign BOTH57M dataset, the BOBSL3DT model wins on R@1 (18.61 vs. 15.70) but the Phon.-only variant actually achieves higher R@3 (44.19 vs. 35.47), which the authors attribute to BOTH57M descriptions being more handshape-focused and less motion-focused. FID values are consistently higher for the zero-shot model on cross-domain datasets (e.g., 0.47 vs. 0.36 on ASL-Text), reflecting the domain gap in motion style, though the retrieval metrics confirm superior text-motif alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MDM/MDM-SMPL diffusion architecture for text-conditioned motion generation to the SMPL-X expressive body model, enabling hand articulations, and adapts the TMR contrastive retrieval framework (as THMR) for both data curation and evaluation. The core methodological departure is in data construction: rather than relying on small manually-annotated hand datasets (BOTH57M, Motion-X) or hand motions conditioned on speech/objects/body dynamics, the authors build a 1.3M-pair dataset by combining sign language video pseudo-labels, a phonological dictionary (SignBank), rule-based pose scripts (HandMotionScript, adapted from PoseScript), and LLM-generated free-form descriptions. This positions the work at the intersection of sign language production, text-to-motion generation, and large-scale noisy data pipelines, with the explicit framing that text-only conditioning for hand motions is a new setting not addressed by prior gesture, hand-object, or body-to-hand generation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BOBSL3DT is the first dataset providing over 1M motion-text pairs focused on hand articulations, and the demonstrated viability of a high-scale/high-noise training regime (pseudo-labels, variant assignment errors, monocular 3D reconstruction noise, LLM imperfections) challenges the assumption that hand motion generation requires prohibitively expensive manual annotation. The strong cross-domain transfer (BSL-trained model outperforming ASL-trained baselines by ~10 R@1 points) suggests the learned representations capture generalizable hand motion semantics. Key limitations include imprecise contact/touch modeling due to monocular reconstruction depth errors, and the dependence on sign language dictionary coverage, which constrains the vocabulary to ~1,500 words. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other sign languages with available dictionaries, whether explicit contact detection can improve fidelity, and whether the noisy pipeline can be further refined to reduce the gap to clean manual annotations.
