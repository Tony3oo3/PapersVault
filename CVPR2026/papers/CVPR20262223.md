## Problem

In federated post-deployment settings, each client experiences client-specific, time-varying distribution shifts (label or covariate) without access to ground-truth labels, making fixed learning rates inadequate—too small causes underfitting, too large causes divergence. The learning rate selection problem is particularly underexplored in this federated, non-stationary, unsupervised regime where hundreds of heterogeneous clients undergo distinct shift dynamics simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262223/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a pre-trained model split into shared layers (ψ_c) and personalized layers (φ_c), along with a confusion matrix M computed server-side from pre-training data. At each timestep, the client consumes an unlabeled batch (image or text) and produces an updated model plus a scalar adaptive learning rate η_c^t bounded in [η_min, η_max]. Only the shared layers are communicated to the server for aggregation; personalized layers and all shift-estimation statistics remain local, requiring O(|I|) + O(d) memory per client for the two summary vectors.

## Method

Fed-ADE constructs a per-client, per-timestep adaptive learning rate from two lightweight, label-free estimators: (i) uncertainty dynamics, computed as the cosine distance between consecutive batch-mean softmax vectors, and (ii) representation dynamics, computed as the cosine distance between consecutive batch-mean ℓ2-normalized feature vectors from the shared encoder. These are averaged into a unified shift signal S_c^t ∈ [0,1], which linearly maps to η_c^t = η_min + (η_max − η_min)·S_c^t. The unsupervised risk is estimated via BBSE (confusion-matrix correction of pseudo-labels), and updates follow a two-step personalized FL schedule: a joint gradient step on both layer groups, followed by a personalized-only step with shared layers frozen at the server-aggregated value. The paper proves that the cumulative shift surrogate approximates true distribution path length up to calibration error, and that the resulting adaptive schedule achieves the min-max optimal dynamic regret rate O(S̄_c^{1/3} T^{2/3}).

## Results

Fed-ADE achieves the highest accuracy across all 24 dataset-shift combinations in Table 1. On CIFAR-10 under linear label shift, Fed-ADE reaches 73.8% accuracy, outperforming the best fixed-rate variant (FixLR Mid, 70.8%) and the strongest federated baseline Fed-POE (71.3%). On the text benchmark LAMA under linear label shift, Fed-ADE attains 95.8% versus 95.6% for FedCCFA and 85.4% for Fed-POE. In covariate shift (CIFAR-10-C, linear), Fed-ADE scores 64.4% versus 63.9% for FixLR Mid and 44.5% for Fed-POE. Fed-ADE also runs in ~109.5 s average wall time, 17–24× faster than localized methods (1242–2631 s) and ~2× faster than FedCCFA (210.8 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-ADE wins against all six baselines in every dataset-shift combination. Against localized methods (FTH, ATLAS, UNIDA, UDA), the gap is large: on CIFAR-10 under linear label shift, Fed-ADE scores 73.8% while FTH, ATLAS, UNIDA, and UDA achieve only 31.4%, 36.5%, 23.0%, and 33.3% respectively, reflecting their inability to leverage cross-client collaboration. Among federated baselines, Fed-POE is the closest competitor (71.3% on CIFAR-10 Lin.), but Fed-ADE still leads by ~2.5 points; FedCCFA trails further at 65.8%. The fixed-rate variants of Fed-ADE (FixLR Low/Mid/High) serve as the most informative ablation: FixLR Mid is within 1–3 points of Fed-ADE on most settings, confirming that the adaptive mechanism provides a consistent but moderate gain over a well-chosen constant rate. On the LAMA text benchmark, FedCCFA is surprisingly competitive (95.6% Lin.), yet Fed-ADE still edges it out (95.8%) and pulls ahead more clearly under non-linear schedules (96.4% Squ. vs. 92.0%). In covariate shift, the advantage over federated baselines widens to 6+ points (e.g., 64.4% vs. 44.5% for Fed-POE on CIFAR-10-C Lin.), indicating that distribution-shift-aware learning rates are especially valuable when feature-level drift dominates.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fed-ADE builds on two foundational threads: the Black-box Shift Estimation (BBSE) framework of Lipton et al. for unsupervised label distribution correction, and the partial-model-sharing personalized FL architecture of Collins et al. that decouples shared and client-specific layers. It departs from prior federated adaptation methods (Fed-POE, FedCCFA, ATP) that rely on fixed learning rates or ensemble-based strategies, and from centralized online label-shift methods (ATLAS, FTH) that cannot operate in a federated, multi-client setting. The key conceptual departure is treating learning rate selection as a first-class, shift-driven decision variable rather than a fixed hyperparameter, using lightweight cosine-distance-based dynamics estimators that require no extra communication or supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: deployed federated models face client-specific, time-varying distribution shifts, yet prior methods either assume stationarity or use fixed adaptation rates that underfit or diverge. The min-max optimal dynamic regret bound O(S_c^{1/3} T^{2/3}) provides rare theoretical grounding for federated post-adaptation, and the O(|I|)+O(d) memory overhead makes the approach deployable on resource-constrained edge devices. Open questions include whether the linear interpolation model for shift (Eq. 1) captures more complex non-monotonic or multi-modal drift patterns, how the method scales to thousands of clients with highly heterogeneous shift magnitudes, and whether the cosine-similarity-based shift signal remains reliable under severe class imbalance or open-world (new-class) scenarios.
