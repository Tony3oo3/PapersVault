## Problem

Existing implicit motion transfer methods for image-to-video generation are limited to single-object or single-motion scenarios, making it impossible to assign distinct motion patterns to different objects within the same image. The core technical obstacle is cross-object motion entanglement: when multiple motion tokens interact globally in attention layers, motions bleed across objects and cannot be independently controlled.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261546/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static multi-object image (720×480) containing K objects, N reference videos (49 frames each) with corresponding text captions describing the desired motions, and produces a 49-frame target video where each object independently follows its assigned reference motion while the first frame preserves the input image's appearance. At inference, Grounded SAM segments the first frame to obtain per-object masks, and a local temporal window (W=2) governs mask propagation across frames. The base model is CogVideoX-5B I2V with MM-DiT architecture, and motion tokens are trained for 2,000 steps at 720×480 resolution.

## Method

FlexiMMT injects trainable motion tokens into the MM-DiT attention layers alongside text and video tokens, then applies a Motion Decoupled Mask Attention (MDMA) mechanism that constructs an object-specific binary mask matrix partitioned into M2X (Motion-to-Video, Video-to-Motion, Motion-to-Motion) and T2X (Text-to-Video, Video-to-Text, Text-to-Motion, Motion-to-Text) sub-masks, ensuring each object's motion and text tokens attend only to their own video tokens. For mask extraction, a Differentiated Mask Extraction Mechanism (DMEM) uses a lightweight QK-multiplication threshold during training (single-object videos) and a Regressive Mask Propagation Mechanism (RMPM) at inference that recursively propagates Grounded SAM first-frame masks to subsequent frames via L2-normalized feature correlation within a sliding temporal window. A Dynamic RMPM variant terminates mask updates early when inter-step mask differences fall below a 5% threshold, exploiting the observation that motion transfer is largely determined in early denoising steps. Training uses single-object reference videos with the full MDMA mask active only on M2M, M2V, and T2M blocks, while inference activates the complete mask for multi-object disentanglement.

## Results

On the 200 video-image pair benchmark, FlexiMMT achieves the best Trajectory Fidelity (TF = 0.577) and Flow Fidelity (FF = 0.723), surpassing the strongest baseline Go-with-the-Flow (TF = 0.488, FF = 0.648) by 18% and 12% respectively. In human evaluation, FlexiMMT dominates all four criteria, with Motion Fidelity at 89.475% compared to the next-best FlexiAct at 6.5%. On appearance and temporal consistency (AC = 0.904, TC = 0.932), FlexiMMT is slightly below CogVideoX-5B-I2V (AC = 0.948, TC = 0.956), which the authors attribute to these CLIP-based metrics favoring near-static outputs that fail to transfer motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexiMMT decisively outperforms all five baselines on the two motion-specific metrics: TF (0.577 vs. 0.488 for Go-with-the-Flow, the best baseline) and FF (0.723 vs. 0.648), confirming superior multi-object motion transfer. In human evaluation, the gap is even more dramatic — FlexiMMT wins 76.75% of Motion Fidelity votes versus 6.5% for FlexiAct and 3.5% for CogVideoX-5B-I2V, while all other baselines score near zero, indicating they essentially fail at multi-object motion assignment. On appearance and temporal consistency, FlexiMMT trails CogVideoX-5B-I2V (AC 0.904 vs. 0.948; TC 0.932 vs. 0.956) and FlexiAct (AC 0.904 vs. 0.939), but the authors argue these CLIP-based metrics reward minimal motion and thus penalize successful animation. Go-with-the-Flow, the strongest explicit-motion baseline, achieves the second-best TF and FF but still lags FlexiMMT by 0.089 and 0.075 respectively, and scores 0% on all human-evaluation criteria. AnyV2V and I2VEdit, both tuning-free or LoRA-based implicit methods, perform worst on motion metrics (TF ≤ 0.395, FF ≤ 0.562) and receive negligible human preference, underscoring the difficulty of multi-object motion transfer without explicit decoupling mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit motion transfer line (FlexiAct, I2VEdit, AnyV2V, MotionDirector) from single-object to multi-object I2V settings, building on the CogVideoX-5B MM-DiT architecture and the motion-token paradigm introduced by recent works. The key conceptual departure is the Motion Decoupled Mask Attention mechanism, which imposes object-specific attention masks to prevent cross-object motion entanglement—a problem that prior single-object methods never needed to address. For mask extraction, it departs from external segmentation at training time (using lightweight QK attention instead) while still relying on Grounded SAM at inference, and introduces a regressive propagation scheme to extend first-frame masks across subsequent frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to explicitly handle multi-object, multi-motion I2V transfer, opening a practically important scenario (e.g., animating multiple characters in a scene with distinct motions) that prior implicit methods could not address. The mask-attention disentanglement strategy is a clean, generalizable principle that could inform other multi-entity control tasks in diffusion models. However, evaluation is limited to 200 video-image pairs at 720×480 with 49 frames, baselines were adapted rather than purpose-built for multi-object, and the reliance on an external segmentation model at inference introduces a practical dependency. Open questions include scalability to more than two objects, longer/higher-resolution videos, and whether the attention-based training mask generalizes beyond the single-object training regime.
