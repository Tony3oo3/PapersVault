## Problem

Existing hallucination benchmarks (POPE, DASH, AMBER) probe only coarse, single-element mismatches, leaving unexplored whether MLLMs can detect fine-grained errors involving multiple objects, attributes, and relations simultaneously. The paper shows that as negative queries grow in granularity, model accuracy drops sharply (e.g., from ~80% to ~20% for InternVL3.5-14B), revealing a systematic brittleness to fine-grained negations that current evaluation suites fail to capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263179/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a text query that embeds multiple semantic elements (objects, attributes, or relations) drawn from a scene graph, with exactly one element replaced by a semantically plausible but incorrect counterpart. Outputs are structured as multiple-choice answers (one correct option plus four distractors drawn from the other generated negatives) or free-form text responses. Evaluation uses a paired-accuracy metric requiring the model to answer both the positive and negative variant of each question correctly, preventing success through simple 'No' memorization or label-bias exploitation.

## Method

FINER-Tuning applies Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to frontier MLLMs using preference tuples constructed from Pixmo long captions: Phi-4-14B extracts fine-grained positive phrases across four settings (Multi-obj, Multi-attr, Multi-rel, Wh), generates negative counterparts by replacing one element, and composes query–answer pairs where the accepted response correctly affirms or denies the query while the rejected response does the opposite. A key design choice is training on both positive and negative queries simultaneously, which ablations show is critical—DPO with only negative queries underperforms, and SFT degrades performance substantially. The approach targets fine-grained negative *input* queries rather than response-side hallucinations, and uses a small open LLM (Phi-4-14B) for data generation to avoid costly closed-source models. Fine-tuning is performed with LoRA for one epoch on up to 160k preference tuples, and the method is model-agnostic, applied to LLaVA-1.6, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL-3.5 at multiple scales.

## Results

On FINER-CompreCap, FINER-Tuning yields up to 24.2% paired accuracy gains (InternVL-3.5-14B on Multi-rel: 47.0→71.2), and LLaVA-1.6-7B improves by 23.1% (Multi-obj), 25.4% (Multi-attr), and 16.6% (Multi-rel). On FINER-DOCCI, FINER-Tuned InternVL-3.5-14B reaches 65.9/65.0/57.0/23.0 across the four settings, matching Gemini-2.5-Flash in 3 of 4 settings. Wh-questions remain the hardest: even InternVL-3.5-38B and Gemini-2.5-Flash achieve only 36.6% and 49.6% paired accuracy on FINER-DOCCI. On eight external hallucination benchmarks, FINER-Tuning improves all four base models (e.g., +6.2% DASH for InternVL-3.5-8B, +19.3% HaloQuest for LLaVA-1.6), while also improving general capabilities on six benchmarks (e.g., +1.4% avg. for InternVL-3.5-14B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINER-Tuning substantially outperforms all prior hallucination-aware fine-tuning methods on the FINER benchmarks: RLAIF-V-12B (the strongest prior method) scores 62.2/39.6/19.2/20.5 on FINER-CompreCap, while FINER-Tuned InternVL-3.5-14B reaches 80.0/78.9/71.2/30.1, a gap of up to 52 points on Multi-rel. OPA-DPO, RLHF-V, LLaVA-RLHF, and LRV-V2 all score below 14% on most FINER settings, confirming that coarse hallucination-reduction training does not transfer to fine-grained negative queries. Against the stronger Gemini-2.5-Flash, FINER-Tuned InternVL-3.5-14B is competitive on FINER-DOCCI (matching or exceeding it in 3 of 4 settings) but trails on Wh-questions (23.0 vs. 49.6). Notably, FINER-Tuned InternVL-3.5-14B surpasses the 38B variant on Multi-rel (71.2 vs. 66.8), demonstrating that targeted fine-grained training can exceed raw model scale. On external hallucination benchmarks, FINER-Tuning improves all four base models across DASH, POPE, RePOPE, HallusionBench, AMBER, CRPE_R, MMHal-Bench, and HaloQuest, whereas RLAIF-V degrades its base OmniLMM on DASH (−2.7), POPE (−0.3), RePOPE (−0.4), and HallusionBench (−1.2). On six general-purpose benchmarks, FINER-Tuning avoids the 'alignment tax' and slightly improves average scores (e.g., +1.4% for InternVL-3.5-14B), unlike RLAIF-V which drops OmniLMM's average from 46.3 to 45.7.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hallucination-evaluation line (POPE, DASH, AMBER, CRPE) from coarse single-element yes/no probes to fine-grained multi-element negative queries composed from scene graphs, and extends hallucination-aware fine-tuning (RLAIF-V, OPA-DPO, RLHF-V) by targeting input-side query errors rather than response-side generation errors. Conceptually, it repurposes DPO—previously applied to correct the model's own hallucinated outputs—toward teaching the model to *detect* hallucinations planted in the user's question, a shift from 'don't hallucinate' to 'spot when someone else hallucinated.' The scene-graph-based construction draws on CompreCap and DOCCI, while the training data pipeline borrows from Pixmo captions and uses a small LLM (Phi-4-14B) to avoid dependence on closed-source annotators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmarks expose a clear and quantified vulnerability—accuracy drops from ~80% to ~20% as query granularity increases—that prior coarse-grained benchmarks mask, making FINER a likely standard for evaluating fine-grained negation robustness. FINER-Tuning's 'no alignment tax' result (improving hallucination metrics while also gaining on six general-purpose benchmarks) is practically important, as it shows targeted DPO on fine-grained negatives need not sacrifice general capability. Open questions remain: Wh-questions still challenge even 38B models and Gemini-2.5-Flash (36.6% and 49.6% paired accuracy), the benchmarks are not fully human-curated, and the Multi-rel setting is capped at three relations, leaving room for harder future probes.
