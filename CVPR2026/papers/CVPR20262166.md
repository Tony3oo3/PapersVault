## Problem

State-of-the-art self-supervised image encoders like DINOv3 produce semantic rather than geometric features, while the closest 3D-oriented SSL method, CroCo, relies on fragile data sampling that requires substantial overlap between view pairs. This paper asks whether a simple, symmetric multi-view masked autoencoding objective can learn geometric features for 3D vision without CroCo's sampling constraints or DINOv3's heavy compute requirements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary number (2–24) of 256×256 RGB images from the same scene, each uniformly masked at a 75% patch ratio, and produces per-patch visual feature representations from a ViT-L encoder for downstream 3D tasks. During pretraining, the output is a reconstruction of the normalized RGB values for masked patches, computed via a linear head on decoder token embeddings. The symmetric formulation means every view is both a source and target of information, with no designated reference frame.

## Method

MuM extends MAE to multi-view by uniformly masking all views at 75% and jointly decoding them through a lightweight ViT-B decoder with 6 alternating attention blocks that interleave frame-wise attention (within each view) and global attention (across all views). This symmetric, reference-free design contrasts with CroCo's asymmetric 90%/0% masking and unmasked reference view, making the objective robust to arbitrary frame sampling and reducing to standard MAE when only one view is present. The encoder (ViT-L with axial RoPE) processes each masked view independently, while the decoder enables inter-frame communication before a linear head regresses normalized pixel values. Training uses 500K steps on ~20M frames from 12 diverse 3D datasets plus 10% ImageNet-1K, on 64×A100 GPUs for roughly three days, with a global batch size of 6144 and cosine LR schedule.

## Results

On dense feature matching via linear probing (Table 4), MuM (64×A100) achieves EPE of 10.2 and robustness of 94.2% on MegaDepth, and EPE of 27.9 and robustness of 78.9% on ScanNet, outperforming DINOv3 (EPE 19.0 / 28.7) and CroCo v2 (EPE 22.0 / 29.0). On multi-view camera pose estimation (AUC@30), MuM's frozen encoder reaches 71.5 on CO3Dv2, 50.8 on Re10K, and 73.0 on MegaDepth, versus 66.9/36.7/59.3 for DINOv3. On relative pose estimation, MuM achieves 26.7% AUC@5° on MegaDepth, nearly doubling DINOv3 (15.6%) and CroCo v2 (13.9%). On single-view semantic tasks, MuM trails DINOv3 (e.g., 70.8% vs 86.9% ImageNet-1K classification) but surpasses CroCo v2 and MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MuM decisively outperforms DINOv3 on all multi-view 3D tasks: camera pose AUC@30 improves from 66.9→71.5 (CO3Dv2) and 36.7→50.8 (Re10K), point cloud accuracy on DTU drops from 6.4→3.7, and relative pose AUC@5° on MegaDepth jumps from 15.6→26.7, all while using ~30× less training compute. Against CroCo v2, MuM wins on every 3D benchmark (e.g., EPE 10.2 vs 22.0 on MegaDepth matching; AUC@30 73.0 vs 60.7 on MegaDepth pose) but loses on semantic tasks (ImageNet-1K: 70.8% vs 57.4% for CroCo v2, though DINOv3 leads at 86.9%). Compared to MAE, MuM's multi-view extension yields a large matching gain (EPE 10.2 vs 29.7 on MegaDepth) while MAE retains a slight edge on ImageNet-1K classification (76.6% vs 70.8%). Among other SSL baselines in Table 4, MuM substantially outperforms iBOT (EPE 28.5), CAPI (21.4), I-JEPA (42.6), and V-JEPA2 (60.6) on MegaDepth matching, and also beats the weakly-supervised SigLIP2 (62.1) and PEcore (75.8). In the full RoMa pipeline (Table 5), MuM as the coarse encoder achieves 100-PCK@1px of 4.0 on MegaDepth, improving over RoMa(DINOv2) (4.6), CroCo v2 (4.6), and DINOv3 (5.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

MuM extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) objective from single-view to an arbitrary number of views of the same scene, departing from CroCo's asymmetric two-view cross-view completion (one fully masked, one unmasked reference) in favor of uniform 75% masking across all views. The decoder's alternating frame-wise and global attention mechanism is borrowed from VGGT, but applied in a self-supervised context with a symmetric architecture that avoids designating any reference frame. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as a simplification: by removing the reference-view constraint and the need for co-visibility-aware sampling, it recovers the robustness of standard MAE while gaining multi-view geometric signal, and it contrasts sharply with the DINO family's self-distillation + instance-discrimination paradigm, arguing that a plain pixel-reconstruction target is more effective for geometric features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: MuM matches or exceeds DINOv3 on 3D geometric tasks (matching, pose, reconstruction) while requiring roughly 30× less training compute and no billion-image-scale dataset, making strong 3D-vision pretraining accessible to academic groups. The symmetric, sampling-agnostic design also removes the fragile co-visibility requirement that plagued CroCo, enabling straightforward mixing of multi-view and single-view data. Open questions include whether self-distillation objectives can be fruitfully combined with multi-view data to recover DINOv3's semantic strength without sacrificing geometric performance, and whether the approach scales further with more compute and data. The paper acknowledges it underperforms DINOv3 on purely semantic tasks (classification, segmentation), suggesting a genuine trade-off between geometric and semantic feature quality.
