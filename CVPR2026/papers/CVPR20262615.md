## Problem

mmWave human point clouds are inherently sparse and noisy, and large-scale annotated datasets remain scarce, hindering practical human sensing. Existing enhancement and generation methods are typically designed for scene-level autonomous driving rather than fine-grained human sensing, and they treat enhancement and generation as separate tasks. Furthermore, condition-free flow matching between real (non-Gaussian) distributions suffers from latent path crossings that corrupt velocity field supervision, a problem overlooked in prior cross-modal flow matching work.

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired sparse mmWave point clouds and dense point clouds (from RGB-D, LiDAR, or SMPL-X simulation), each subsampled or zero-padded to 512 points. Both are encoded into 32 latent tokens of dimension 128 via a VecSet VAE, then projected through 16 shared cross-attention queries into a unified 16×128 latent space. A single trained model produces either a dense point cloud from sparse mmWave input (M2D enhancement) or a realistic mmWave point cloud from dense input (D2M generation), with the direction determined at inference time.

## Method

mmWaveFlow unifies enhancement and generation as a single invertible transport between two latent distributions learned by a shared VAE backbone. A Cross-modal Latent Alignment module uses shared learnable queries with cross-attention to project modality-specific tokens into a common semantic space, further stabilized by a token-level InfoNCE contrastive loss that enforces correspondence between paired tokens. The core transport is a 30-layer DiT trained with Origin-Aware Flow Matching (OA-Flow), which conditions the velocity field on the path origin (randomly z₀ or z₁) to disambiguate crossing trajectories in latent space—a key departure from condition-free flow matching. Training proceeds in two stages: joint optimization of VAE and flow with gradient surgery for multi-task stability, followed by freezing the VAE and fine-tuning only the flow module. The total loss combines Chamfer Distance, Earth Mover's Distance, KL regularization, flow-matching MSE, and the alignment contrastive term.

## Results

mmWaveFlow achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD) and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) on all six task–dataset combinations (D2M and M2D on mmBody, MM-Fi, and mRI). Headline numbers include CD of 1.17 cm / EMD of 4.83 cm on mmBody D2M, CD of 0.75 cm / EMD of 3.69 cm on MM-Fi M2D, and CD of 0.72 cm / EMD of 2.69 cm on mRI M2D. Its Mean Rank (MR) across all 12 metrics is 1.11, far ahead of the next-best method Tiger (MR = 2.11). Notably, mmWaveFlow is trained once to handle both enhancement and generation, whereas all baselines require separate training for each direction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

mmWaveFlow outperforms all five baselines on every CD and EMD metric across the three datasets and both directions. The closest competitor is Tiger [34], a general-purpose 3D point cloud diffusion model; mmWaveFlow improves CD by 0.15 cm on mmBody D2M (1.17 vs 1.32) and by 0.19 cm on mRI M2D (0.72 vs 0.81), while reducing EMD by up to 3.03 cm on mRI M2D (2.69 vs 4.92). RadarDiff [52] and mmPoint [42] are moderate performers, with mmWaveFlow cutting EMD by roughly 30–50% relative to them on most settings. LiDiff [28] is the only baseline that beats mmWaveFlow on a single metric (MM-Fi M2D CD: 0.66 vs 0.75), but it degrades severely on D2M tasks (CD up to 9.12 on MM-Fi) because it is designed for LiDAR completion rather than mmWave generation. RadarHD [33] is the weakest baseline overall (MR = 5.44), reflecting its design for outdoor autonomous-driving scenes rather than fine-grained human sensing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) from its standard Gaussian-noise source to a setting where both source and target are real data distributions (sparse mmWave and dense point clouds), following the cross-modal evolution paradigm of CrossFlow (Liu et al., CVPR 2025). It departs from prior cross-modal flow matching work (CrossFlow, FlowTok) in a critical way: those methods rely on a powerful pretrained encoder in one modality as a semantic anchor, whereas here neither modality has such a privilege, necessitating a from-scratch shared latent alignment via cross-attention and InfoNCE. The paper also diverges from existing mmWave enhancement methods (RadarHD, RadarDiff) that target autonomous-driving scene-level tasks and use BEV images as supervision, instead operating directly in 3D point cloud space for fine-grained human sensing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying mmWave enhancement and generation into a single bidirectional transport, the framework addresses the core data-scarcity bottleneck in mmWave human sensing with one model rather than two, and the downstream task results (9% MPJPE reduction, matching full-data accuracy with 50% real data) demonstrate practical utility for pose estimation and action recognition. The path-crossing diagnosis and the origin-aware conditioning fix are broadly applicable to any flow matching setup with non-Gaussian paired sources, making the insight relevant beyond mmWave. Open questions include whether the approach scales to scene-level point clouds without paired data, how sensitive it is to the quality of the dense reference modality, and whether the VAE bottleneck (32 tokens) limits fidelity for complex multi-person or articulated scenes.
