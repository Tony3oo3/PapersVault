## Problem

Existing 3DGS watermarking methods rely on handcrafted thresholds or globally fixed hyperparameters to balance invisibility and robustness, making their embedding behavior static and scene-agnostic. Decisions about where and how much to write are predetermined by heuristics rather than learned adaptively from decoding feedback, limiting the ability to allocate watermark energy based on each scene's specific characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261667/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained Scaffold-GS model (with N learnable anchors, each encoding a local Gaussian cluster via a differentiable mapping F) and a binary copyright message M ∈ {0,1}^B (B ∈ {32, 48, 64}) as input, and produces watermarked 3DGS parameters Θ^(K) from which the message can be extracted. Extraction uses a frozen pretrained HiDDeN decoder applied to rendered views that may have undergone image-space distortions (noise, rotation, scaling, blur, crop, JPEG) or model-space perturbations (Gaussian removal, cloning, parameter noise). The policy operates on the compact anchor feature matrix F ∈ R^{N×d} rather than individual Gaussians, reducing the action space from hundreds of thousands to a manageable number of anchor-level decisions.

## Method

W2M formulates watermark embedding as a Markov Decision Process solved with on-policy Actor-Critic (A2C). At each RL step, a two-layer MLP policy observes the current anchor feature matrix and outputs a per-anchor Writing Guidance vector W ∈ [0,1]^N that scales the gradient of an Imprint Objective (combining BCE decoding loss, L1+SSIM invisibility loss, and a Gaussian scaling regularizer) for each anchor, performing a directed one-step update. A key efficiency insight is that anchor-wise gradient scaling on the Scaffold-GS backbone avoids the intractable per-Gaussian action space while preserving spatially coherent control over where and how much to embed. Each step samples a view-distortion pair, applies the weighted update, then evaluates a reward on the post-update render passed through the same cached distortion and decoded by the pretrained HiDDeN decoder, closing the loop between embedding decisions and decoding feedback. The critic predicts state value to compute TD errors that drive policy gradient updates, and the entire process runs for 1–5 epochs of 1000 steps per scene.

## Results

W2M achieves the highest bit accuracy across all tested message lengths: 98.34% (32-bit), 97.80% (48-bit), and 91.17% (64-bit), while simultaneously delivering the best or near-best rendering fidelity (PSNR 36.16 / 35.93 / 32.79 dB, SSIM up to 0.979, LPIPS as low as 0.023). Under image-space attacks, W2M maintains 94.49% bit accuracy on the combined distortion pipeline, outperforming the next-best method by over 4 points. Under model-space attacks (20% Gaussian removal/cloning, parameter noise), W2M sustains 97.89–98.07% accuracy, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

W2M outperforms all three baselines on bit accuracy at every bit length tested. At 48 bits the gap is most pronounced: W2M reaches 97.80% versus 93.72% for 3D-GSW and 93.53% for GuardSplat, a margin of over 4 percentage points, while GaussianMarker (97.78%) is close on accuracy but lags significantly on rendering quality (PSNR 31.47 vs. 35.93 dB). On rendering fidelity, W2M matches or exceeds GuardSplat (the prior best on PSNR/LPIPS) at 32 and 48 bits and leads at 64 bits. Under image-space robustness (Table 2), W2M's advantage is largest on geometric distortions—Rotation (91.86% vs. 79.76% for GaussianMarker) and the combined pipeline (94.49% vs. 90.06%)—while photometric-only attacks show smaller but consistent gains. Under model-space attacks (Table 3), W2M leads on all three operations (noise, 20% removal, 20% cloning), with the clearest margin on parameter noise (91.57% vs. 87.12% for GaussianMarker). Overall, W2M is the only method that simultaneously achieves top bit accuracy and top rendering quality, whereas each baseline trades off one objective for the other.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Scaffold-GS's anchor-organized 3DGS representation and departs from existing 3DGS watermarking methods (GaussianMarker, GuardSplat, 3D-GSW) that rely on handcrafted thresholds, globally fixed hyperparameters, or pretrained saliency/frequency mappings to decide where and how much to embed. The key conceptual shift is formulating watermark embedding as a Markov Decision Process solved with an Actor-Critic policy, drawing on the broader RL-for-watermarking line of work in LLM text and code token selection, but adapting it to the high-dimensional, scene-dependent action space of 3DGS. The anchor-wise gradient scaling mechanism is the practical bridge that makes per-region policy control tractable at the scale of hundreds of thousands to millions of Gaussians.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the de facto standard for photorealistic 3D asset exchange, adaptive, scene-aware watermarking is increasingly critical for copyright protection, and W2M demonstrates that learned policies can outperform static heuristics on both robustness and fidelity simultaneously. The main limitation is reliance on a frozen pretrained HiDDeN decoder and a fixed distortion collection, which constrains end-to-end adaptivity; the authors identify integrating the decoder into the RL loop for co-evolution as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the policy generalizes across vastly different scene scales without retraining and how the approach behaves under adaptive or unknown attack distributions.
