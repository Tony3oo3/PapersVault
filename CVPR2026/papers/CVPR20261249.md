## Problem

Existing face obfuscation methods that balance human indecipherability (HI) and machine decipherability (MD) operate primarily in the spatial domain, inadvertently leaving structural information that reconstruction attacks can exploit to recover hidden identity. This paper addresses the gap by moving obfuscation into the frequency domain, where identity cues, task-relevant information, and tamper-resistant components can be independently controlled.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image as input and produces an obfuscated RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. The input is partitioned into non-overlapping 8×8 pixel blocks for block-wise DCT processing, and the output is generated via inverse DCT back to the spatial domain. The method is entirely training-free: a pretrained face analysis network (e.g., IResNet50 with ArcFace) is frozen and used only as a feature extractor during per-image optimization, so no obfuscation network needs to be trained or retrained when the underlying analysis model changes.

## Method

FreM decomposes each 8×8 DCT block into four frequency subbands (LL, LH, HL, HH) and applies three dedicated modules: a Neutralization module replaces the LL subband with the DCT coefficients of a dataset-average face plus learnable Gaussian perturbations to erase identity; a Perturbation module element-wise scales LH and HL coefficients with learnable parameters (initialized to 1) to enhance machine-decipherable discriminative cues; and a Suppression module replaces the HH subband with learnable Gaussian noise to disrupt reconstruction attacks. The learnable parameters across all three modules are then refined per-image via the Backpropagating Refinement Scheme, minimizing a combined objective of feature-similarity loss (MD) in a frozen face analysis network and a coefficient energy constraint loss that prevents the optimizer from amplifying low-frequency coefficients back into human-perceptible identity cues. This subband-adaptive design is the key distinction from prior spatial-domain methods: each frequency band is manipulated according to its specific contribution to HI, MD, and reconstruction robustness rather than treating the image as a uniform spatial field.

## Results

On face recognition, FreM achieves the best XDR/ODR accuracy in 8 out of 10 dataset-protocol combinations across LFW (99.53/98.67), AgeDB (95.95/92.45), CALFW (94.95/92.86), CPLFW (90.91/86.88), and CFP-FP (94.41/90.74). FreM also achieves the lowest reconstruction PSNR (13.59 dB), indicating the strongest robustness against reconstruction attacks, while running at ~67 ms per image. On age estimation (MORPH II), FreM attains 2.41 MAE and 91.8% CS versus Forbes's 3.38 MAE and 77.4% CS; on expression recognition (RAF-DB), FreM reaches 83.02% accuracy versus Forbes's 75.23%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Forbes (the closest training-free baseline using BRS in the spatial domain), FreM substantially outperforms on every metric: LFW XDR improves from 95.72% to 99.53% and ODR from 82.77% to 98.67%, while runtime drops from ~740 ms to ~67 ms (over 10× faster). Against IdentityHider (a training-based GAN approach), FreM edges ahead on most datasets (e.g., LFW XDR 99.53 vs. 99.08, CFP-FP ODR 90.74 vs. 87.14) and achieves a lower reconstruction PSNR (13.59 vs. 15.33), indicating stronger resistance to reconstruction attacks. Against all four PRO-Face variants (Blur, Pixelate, FaceShifter, SimSwap), FreM dominates on both recognition accuracy and reconstruction robustness; for instance, the best PRO-Face variant (FaceShifter) reaches only 96.48% XDR on LFW with a PSNR of 36.12, whereas FreM achieves 99.53% XDR with PSNR 13.59. On secondary tasks, FreM consistently surpasses Forbes: age estimation MAE drops from 3.38 to 2.41 (MORPH II), expression accuracy rises from 75.23% to 83.02% (RAF-DB), and attribute mAcc. improves from 88.11% to 88.80% (CelebA), all while maintaining a ~15× speed advantage over Forbes.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreM builds directly on the Backpropagating Refinement Scheme (BRS) [18] used by Forbes [20] for training-free, per-image face obfuscation, but shifts the manipulation from the spatial domain to the frequency domain via block-wise DCT. It departs from prior DCT-based obfuscation methods [19, 34–36, 52] that apply uniform operations (shuffling, masking, channel selection) across all coefficients, instead introducing a subband-adaptive strategy with three dedicated modules (Neutralization, Perturbation, Suppression) that independently control low-, mid-, and high-frequency components for distinct objectives (HI, MD, reconstruction robustness). The design is also inspired by subband-wise processing in image watermarking [3, 23, 26], where frequency-selective manipulation achieves a better balance between imperceptibility and tamper resistance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete vulnerability of spatial-domain obfuscation methods—reconstruction attacks that recover identity from residual structural cues—by showing that frequency-domain manipulation, particularly high-frequency suppression, substantially degrades reconstruction quality (lowest PSNR across all baselines). As a training-free method running in ~67 ms, FreM avoids the retraining burden of task-specific obfuscation networks and scales naturally to new face analysis tasks, as demonstrated by the multi-task experiment. Open questions include whether the block-wise DCT formulation generalizes to video or multi-person scenes, whether the reliance on a frozen pretrained face analysis network for the MD criterion limits long-term applicability as such models evolve, and whether the approach extends to other biometric modalities (e.g., gait, iris).
