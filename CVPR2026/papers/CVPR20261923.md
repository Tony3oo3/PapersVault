## Problem

Adapting SAM to medical image segmentation faces two bottlenecks: (i) the absence of modality- and task-aware adaptation mechanisms, causing SAM's output tokens to homogenize during training on heterogeneous medical data and weakening cross-modal discriminability; and (ii) existing methods rely on large, undifferentiated datasets (e.g., 4.6M images in SAM-Med2D, 361M in IMed-361M) that introduce supervision noise and distribution shift, degrading the model's original capabilities. The paper asks whether a small, curated dataset with a lightweight adaptive module can match or exceed these large-scale approaches.

## Input / Output

The model accepts medical images (CT, MRI, X-ray, dermoscopy) resampled to 512×512, along with standard SAM-style prompts (point clicks or bounding boxes). For binary-classification datasets (ISIC, SZ-CXR), Progressive Prompt Tokenization generates prompts automatically from randomly sampled mask and text cues, eliminating all user interaction at inference. The output is a per-pixel segmentation mask produced by the selected expert token interacting with frozen image embeddings via token-to-image attention and element-wise multiplication.

## Method

SegMoTE freezes the entire SAM encoder and decoder and introduces only 17M learnable parameters split between a Mixture of Token Experts (MoTE, 10M) and Progressive Prompt Tokenization (PPT, 7M). MoTE appends N learnable expert tokens (N×256) to SAM's original 4 output tokens; a noisy top-k router computes per-token expert scores, selects the highest-confidence token as the routing target, and applies a confidence-weighted gating to amplify high-reliability representations while suppressing low-confidence ones. A load-balancing loss based on the squared coefficient of variation of expert importance and load prevents routing collapse. PPT uses randomly sampled mask and text prompts to guide learnable queries through multi-head attention over normalized image features, progressively transforming latent features into semantically aligned prompt tokens for interaction-free inference on binary tasks. Training uses Dice loss plus a small-weight (λ=0.01) balancing term on MedSeg-HQ (154,569 curated masks from 12 public datasets covering 6 modalities and 100+ categories), requiring only 8 RTX 4090 GPUs with batch size 10.

## Results

On out-of-domain evaluation with bounding-box prompts, SegMoTE achieves 77.30 Dice on ISLES (ischemic stroke lesion), 83.39 average Dice on SegThor (thoracic organs), and 71.48 average Dice on TotalSegmentator (MRI, abdominal organs), surpassing the strongest baseline IMIS by 6.1%, 2.9%, and 0.9% respectively. With full decoder fine-tuning (Table 2), SegMoTE further improves to 78.05 on ISLES, 83.91 on SegThor, and 73.67 on TotalSegmentator (MRI), while also leading on all in-domain datasets (e.g., 93.02 on ISIC2018, 95.04 on SZ-CXR, 89.00 on CHAOS-T1). The model achieves these results using only 17M trainable parameters (1.4% of SAM's total) trained on 0.15M masks from MedSeg-HQ, compared to IMIS's 29M parameters trained on 361M annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegMoTE outperforms all six baselines on the out-of-domain ISLES dataset, achieving 77.30 Dice vs. IMIS (71.24), SAM-Med2D (67.93), SAM2 (59.17), MedSAM (58.02), Huang et al. (56.54), and SAM (55.00) — a 6.1% margin over the closest competitor. On SegThor, SegMoTE leads by 2.9% over IMIS (83.39 vs. 80.52) and by a large margin over MedSAM (54.09), though it trails IMIS slightly on the Aorta sub-category (87.40 vs. 88.67). On TotalSegmentator (MRI), SegMoTE edges out IMIS by 0.86% on average (71.48 vs. 70.62) and wins on 10 of 12 individual organs. With full decoder fine-tuning (Table 2), the gap widens: SegMoTE beats IMIS by 6.85% on ISLES (78.05 vs. 71.20), 4.09% on ISIC2018 (93.02 vs. 88.93), and 3.01% on SZ-CXR (95.04 vs. 92.03). Notably, MedSAM and SAM-Med2D, which rely on much larger training datasets (COSMOS 1050K, SAM-Med2D 4.6M images), are consistently outperformed, validating the paper's claim that data quality and modality-aware routing matter more than raw data scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegMoTE extends SAM by freezing its encoder and introducing a token-level Mixture of Experts (MoTE) module within the mask decoder, drawing on the sparsely-gated MoE paradigm (Shazeer et al., ICLR 2017) but repurposing it for modality-aware token routing in segmentation. It departs from prior medical SAM adaptation methods (MedSAM, SAM-Med2D, IMIS) that rely on full or partial decoder fine-tuning over large heterogeneous datasets, instead arguing that a small curated dataset (MedSeg-HQ, ~0.15M masks) with a lightweight 17M-parameter adapter suffices. The Progressive Prompt Tokenization mechanism is a novel addition that converts mask and text prompts into learnable query tokens for fully automatic binary segmentation, a direction not explored in prior interactive medical segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that data curation and architectural adaptivity (MoTE routing) can substitute for brute-force dataset scaling in medical SAM adaptation, offering a practical path to deploying foundation segmentation models in resource-constrained clinical settings. However, the comparison is confounded by a resolution mismatch (512×512 vs. 1024×1024 for most baselines), and PPT is restricted to binary tasks, leaving multi-class automatic prompting unresolved. Open questions include whether the MoTE mechanism generalizes to 3D volumes and medical video, and whether the 4-expert configuration remains sufficient as modality and task diversity grow beyond the seven tested here.
