## Problem

Spherical compound eye cameras capture distortion-free omnidirectional images, but their spherical data are poorly suited to conventional planar neural networks, introducing projection distortions that degrade depth estimation. The joint co-design of compound eye hardware with task-optimized spherical learning algorithms remains largely unexplored, and no native spherical compound eye dataset existed for training and benchmarking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264026/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a grayscale spherical compound eye image (20,609 ommatidia, each integrating light over its FOV into a single intensity value) resampled onto a HEALPix grid with n_side=128 (12 base pixels × 128² quadrilateral sub-pixels ≈ 196,608 pixels) and produces a dense spherical depth map on the same grid. The framework also generalizes to fisheye (162×162 after 10×10 pooling) and panoramic inputs (e.g., 1280×966 for SynWoodScape) by resampling to the HEALPix representation.

## Method

SCE-Depth uses HealSwin as its spherical backbone on the HEALPix grid and introduces two key modules that exploit the depth-sensitive gradient cues arising from overlapping ommatidial FOVs. The Spherical Gradient Feature Extractor (SGFE) applies Sobel operators within each HEALPix base pixel's local Cartesian neighborhood, then fuses local gradients into global spherical coordinates (θ, φ) via a coordinate-aware operator that maintains continuity across base-pixel boundaries; the resulting gradient map is concatenated with the raw input before the network. The Spherical Gradient Loss (SGL) enforces consistency between predicted and ground-truth depth gradients using the BerHu penalty on both θ and φ components, adding geometric regularization to the standard MSE loss (total loss = MSE + λ·SGL, λ=1.0). Training uses Adam (lr=1e-4, batch 4, 1000 epochs, gradient clipping norm 1.0) on two RTX 4090 GPUs, and the optimal ommatidial FOV α=3° balances depth sensitivity against image clarity.

## Results

On the CompoundDepth dataset (α=3°), SCE-Depth achieves RMSE 0.256, AbsRel 0.050, δ₁ 95.74%, and δ₂ 98.48%, outperforming the strongest spherical baseline HealSwin (RMSE 0.350) by 26.9% in RMSE and the strongest planar baseline DepthAnyCamera (RMSE 0.266) by 3.8%. On FisheyeDepth, SCE-Depth attains RMSE 0.287 and δ₁ 94.68%, surpassing all planar and spherical baselines. Generalization to SynWoodScape and Stanford2D3D confirms consistent RMSE improvements over both HealSwin and SUFormer across diverse panoramic scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCE-Depth outperforms all seven baselines on both CompoundDepth and FisheyeDepth across RMSE, δ₁, and δ₂. Against the closest planar competitor DepthAnyCamera (208.9M params), SCE-Depth (41.3M params) achieves lower RMSE (0.256 vs. 0.266) and higher δ₁ (95.74 vs. 94.89) on CompoundDepth while using only ~20% of the parameters. Among spherical baselines, HealSwin is the strongest (RMSE 0.350 on CompoundDepth), yet SCE-Depth reduces RMSE by 26.9% and improves δ₁ by 2.98 points. SUFormer lags further behind (RMSE 0.429), and UGSCNN performs poorly (RMSE 0.866), confirming that existing spherical architectures remain underdeveloped relative to planar counterparts. On generalization datasets, SCE-Depth beats HealSwin on SynWoodScape (RMSE 7.606 vs. 7.896) and Stanford2D3D (RMSE 0.418 vs. 0.497), though it trails SUFormer slightly on Stanford2D3D AbsRel (0.114 vs. 0.071), which the authors attribute to the absence of a relative-error term in the loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on HealSwin (CVPR 2024) as its spherical backbone, extending the HEALPix-grid vision transformer to a new application: dense depth estimation from spherical compound eye (SCE) imagery. It departs from prior compound eye depth work, which relied on planar sub-image stitching, inter-ommatidial disparity, or sparse point-source ranging, by processing the full spherical image natively and exploiting the depth-sensitive gradient cues arising from overlapping ommatidial FOVs. The Spherical Gradient Feature Extractor (SGFE) and Spherical Gradient Loss (SGL) are novel contributions that adapt classical Sobel-based gradient regularization to the spherical domain, filling a gap the authors identify between compound eye hardware design and task-optimized spherical learning algorithms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of CompoundDepth, the first publicly available spherical compound eye dataset with paired depth, provides a much-needed benchmark for the bionic-vision community and enables systematic study of the clarity-depth trade-off governed by ommatidial FOV α. The demonstrated 26.9% RMSE reduction over HealSwin and consistent omnidirectional consistency (vs. fisheye barrel-distortion errors) make a concrete case for hardware-software co-design in wide-FOV perception. However, practical impact is currently limited by the low-resolution prototype (37 ommatidia) and the fact that spherical network baselines remain underdeveloped relative to planar counterparts; scaling to high-resolution compound eyes and validating on real-world dynamic scenes remain open challenges.
