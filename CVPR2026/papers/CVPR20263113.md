## Problem

Existing video editing methods for autonomous driving scenes rely on text prompts or 2D structural priors (depth, sketches) that cannot adequately constrain the spatial variations of dynamic 3D objects, leading to geometric instability and structural drift. Methods that incorporate 3D structural priors typically depend on a single fixed viewpoint per frame, which causes geometric distortions and temporal inconsistencies in edited results. The paper addresses how to achieve precise, controllable editing of dynamic foreground objects while preserving both 3D geometric structure and spatiotemporal coherence across video frames.

## Input / Output

The method takes an N-frame masked video (576×1024 resolution, 10 frames per clip in experiments), a binary mask sequence defining target editing regions, a single reference foreground image, and per-frame 3D bounding box positions (8×3 vertex coordinates). It produces a fully edited video supporting four operations: deletion, replacement, insertion, and repositioning of foreground objects. The reference image is encoded by a pretrained VAE and fed into SV3D to generate 21 surrounding-view latent features, which are then warped to arbitrary target viewpoints per frame rather than being used directly.

## Method

RecEdit-Drive builds on Stable Video Diffusion and integrates two novel modules. The Spatial Feature Warping (SFW) module uses SV3D to generate 21-view latent features from the reference image, selects the two views nearest in azimuth to each target frame, computes per-face homography matrices via DLT from visible 3D bounding box faces, warps and aggregates these features into a target-view representation, refines it with angularly-weighted cross-view attention, and injects it into the video latent through a zero-convolution layer. The Spatiotemporal Collaborative Modeling (SCM) module applies Gaussian-blurred foreground masks as soft attention weights in a cross-frame attention mechanism between the current frame and its immediate neighbors, preventing abrupt boundary transitions and propagating contextual information across frames. Additionally, a noise replacement strategy at early denoising stages (t > T/2) substitutes the background latent with the corresponding forward-diffusion noise to lock in correct background structure before foreground editing proceeds.

## Results

On the nuScenes dataset, RecEdit-Drive achieves the best FVD and FID across all four editing tasks (deletion, replacement, insertion, repositioning). For deletion, it reaches FVD 170.98 and FID 26.97, substantially outperforming the next-best DriveEditor (FVD 208.79, FID 29.30). For replacement, it achieves FVD 38.59 and FID 9.88, edging out DriveEditor (FVD 40.97, FID 10.24) while dramatically surpassing TAV (FVD 848.75) and T2V (FVD 168.27). In insertion and repositioning, RecEdit-Drive improves over DriveEditor by 3.95 and 1.87 FVD points respectively, with corresponding FID gains of 0.41 and 0.41.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RecEdit-Drive outperforms all four baselines on every task it is evaluated against. Against DriveEditor (the strongest competitor, compared on all four tasks), RecEdit-Drive wins by 37.81 FVD on deletion (170.98 vs. 208.79), 2.38 FVD on replacement (38.59 vs. 40.97), 3.95 FVD on insertion (42.01 vs. 45.96), and 1.87 FVD on repositioning (32.27 vs. 34.14), with consistent FID improvements of 2.33, 0.36, 0.41, and 0.41 respectively. Against ProPainter on deletion, the margin is large: 163.81 FVD and 7.17 FID. On replacement, TAV performs worst (FVD 848.75, FID 63.28) due to style drift, while T2V (FVD 168.27, FID 15.28) is also far behind RecEdit-Drive by 129.68 FVD and 5.40 FID. The paper also reports that downstream 3D object detection (StreamPETR) improves when trained on RecEdit-Drive-augmented data, with NDS rising from 0.5617 (real data only) to 0.5905 (real + both augmentation types).

## Relation to Prior Work

RecEdit-Drive builds on Stable Video Diffusion as its generative backbone and SV3D as its 3D structural prior source, but departs from prior generation-reconstruction fusion work in two key ways. Where methods like I2V3D and ReconX rely on a single fixed viewpoint per frame for 3D guidance, RecEdit-Drive's Spatial Feature Warping module selects multiple relevant novel views and warps them via homography to construct arbitrary target-view features, improving geometric fidelity. The Spatiotemporal Collaborative Modeling module with Gaussian cross-view attention addresses a gap left by prior driving-scene editors (DriveEditor, Tune-A-Video) that model temporal consistency only through binary masks or text conditioning, lacking explicit cross-frame structural propagation for dynamic objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D reconstruction-guided editing can produce driving videos with sufficient geometric and temporal fidelity to serve as effective data augmentation for 3D object detection (StreamPETR), closing a practical loop between generative editing and downstream perception. However, the evaluation is confined to nuScenes with short 10-frame clips and requires 3D bounding boxes as input, leaving open whether the approach scales to longer sequences, more complex multi-object interactions, or settings without explicit 3D annotations. The noise-replacement inference trick, while simple, highlights an underexplored direction: leveraging forward-diffusion intermediates to anchor background structure during early denoising.
