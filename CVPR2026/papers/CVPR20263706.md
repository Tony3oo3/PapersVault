## Problem

Existing spatial transcriptomics prediction methods operate primarily at the spot level in Euclidean space, neglecting the multi-level hierarchical structure inherent in both pathology images and gene expression profiles. Additionally, a fundamental information asymmetry exists: gene expression profiles encode fine-grained molecular details that often lack salient visual correlates in H&E images, making standard image-to-gene alignment incomplete. The paper asks whether modeling these hierarchies in hyperbolic space—a geometry naturally suited to tree-like structures—can bridge this modality gap and improve cross-modal prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two scales of H&E pathology image patches per spot: a spot-level patch centered on the target spot and a niche-level patch encompassing the central spot plus its six immediate neighbors in Visium's hexagonal layout, all resized to 224×224 pixels. Corresponding gene expression profiles are provided at both scales (spot-level raw counts and niche-level as the mean of the central spot and K−1 neighbors), restricted to the top 200 highly mean, highly variant genes with log-transformation. The output is a predicted spot-level gene expression vector of dimension 200, produced by an MLP decoder that takes the concatenated aligned spot- and niche-level image representations.

## Method

HyperST extracts multi-level features via a LoRA-fine-tuned UNI pathology foundation model (for images) and a trainable fully connected network (for gene expression), yielding spot- and niche-level embeddings in each modality. These are projected into the Lorentz model of hyperbolic space via the exponential map at the origin, where a Hierarchical Contrastive Alignment module performs cross-modal alignment using a modified infoNCE loss with Lorentzian distance, carefully restricting niche-to-spot direction to avoid false negatives from one-to-many mappings. A Hierarchical Entailment Alignment module then imposes a partial-order structure using hyperbolic entailment cones, encoding two relationships: spot-level features entail their more information-rich niche-level counterparts, and image features entail their corresponding gene expression profiles (since genes are semantically more specific). The final gene decoder is a simple MLP fed by concatenated aligned image representations, trained end-to-end with a combined objective of MSE prediction loss plus weighted contrastive and entailment alignment losses.

## Results

HyperST achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four spatial transcriptomics datasets (Kidney, Colorectum, Skin, Lung), consistently leading on PCC@10, PCC@50, PCC@200, MSE, and MAE. On the headline PCC@200 metric, HyperST reaches 0.390 (Kidney), 0.477 (Colorectum), 0.758 (Skin), and 0.459 (Lung), surpassing the strongest baseline TRIPLEX by approximately 11%, 3%, 2.5%, and 17% respectively. In a zero-shot clinical validation on TCGA-COADREAD, HyperST's predicted gene profiles yield MSI-H AUROC of 0.719 and MSS AUROC of 0.601, outperforming TRIPLEX (0.630/0.567) by ~14% and ~6%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperST outperforms all four main baselines (TRIPLEX, StNet, BLEEP, Stem) on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor is TRIPLEX, which also uses multi-scale features but lacks explicit hierarchical constraints; HyperST improves PCC@200 by 10.95% (Kidney), 3.24% (Colorectum), 2.52% (Skin), and 16.7% (Lung) over TRIPLEX. StNet (direct spot-level regression) and BLEEP (Euclidean contrastive alignment) trail further behind, with BLEEP showing the weakest PCC@200 on Kidney (0.310) and Lung (0.311). Stem (diffusion-based generative model) performs worst on Kidney PCC@200 (0.271) and has the highest MSE on Kidney (1.380). In the zero-shot MSI classification task, HyperST achieves 0.719/0.601 AUROC for MSI-H/MSS, beating TRIPLEX (0.630/0.567) by ~14%/6%, while StNet, BLEEP, and Stem all fall below 0.60 on both classes. Ablation comparisons against MERU (hyperbolic without multi-level) and CLIP (Euclidean contrastive) confirm that both the hierarchical structure and hyperbolic geometry contribute meaningfully to the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperST extends the contrastive image-gene alignment paradigm established by BLEEP (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2023) and TRIPLEX (Chung et al., CVPR 2024) by moving the alignment from Euclidean to hyperbolic space, directly building on the entailment-cone machinery of MERU (Desai et al., ICML 2023). Where prior ST prediction methods treat the problem as flat spot-level matching or multi-scale image fusion, HyperST introduces a two-axis hierarchy (spot→niche on both modalities, and image→gene across modalities) grounded in an information-specificity principle. The pathology backbone leverages the UNI foundation model (Chen et al., Nature Medicine 2024) with LoRA fine-tuning, positioning the work at the intersection of foundation-model adaptation and geometric deep learning for spatial omics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By encoding the intrinsic parent-child structure of ST data in hyperbolic geometry, HyperST offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc multi-scale feature fusion, and the zero-shot MSI classification validation suggests the learned representations carry clinically actionable signal beyond per-spot PCC. However, the evaluation is confined to four HEST-1K tissues with 55 μm Visium spots and top-200 genes, leaving open whether the hierarchical hyperbolic formulation transfers to higher-resolution platforms (MERFISH, Xenium), larger gene panels, or non-Visium geometries. A key open question is whether the information-specificity hierarchy (image entails gene) is universally valid or tissue-dependent, and whether the curvature parameter c should be learned per-dataset or shared.
