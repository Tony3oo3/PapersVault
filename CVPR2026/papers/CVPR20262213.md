## Problem

Generating structurally aligned sample pairs (images, videos, 3D shapes) that share spatial correspondence while depicting different objects remains challenging. Existing approaches either rely on editing one sample to match another (biasing toward one description), use Score Distillation Sampling (slow, prone to mode collapse, cartoonish results), or perform joint generation without principled constraints on transition smoothness. The paper seeks a fast, training-free, multi-modal method that produces high-quality aligned pairs without these limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262213/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two text prompts as input and produces a pair of structurally aligned samples in the target modality (image, video, or 3D shape). For the Trellis.2 3D pipeline, it can alternatively accept an aligned image pair as conditioning instead of text. The method operates exclusively on structured latents (dense voxels for 3D, pixel-space for images, frame sequences for video) and requires only a modification to the inference loop of a pre-trained rectified flow model—no weight changes or additional training. Inference is orders of magnitude faster than SDS-based methods (e.g., ~1–2 min vs. ~2 h for 3D).

## Method

The core mechanism is joint transport of a line segment [x^a, x^b] in latent space along the rectified flow velocity field while preserving its linear structure. At each step, k intermediate points on the segment are individually updated using the model's velocity field with interpolated text embeddings, then a closed-form linear regression (Eq. 4–5) re-fits the segment endpoints to the updated points, yielding an analytical joint transport velocity for each endpoint. A smoothness regularization pulls both endpoint velocities toward an anchor velocity predicted by the flow model at the segment midpoint (Eq. 9), preventing rapid divergence that causes misalignment. The sampling density p(α) over the segment starts concentrated at the midpoint and gradually shifts toward uniform, providing flexible co-guidance during early inference. The entire procedure is inference-time only, requiring no retraining and working atop any rectified flow model with structured latents.

## Results

On the image generation task (Table 1), the method achieves the best DIFT structural alignment score (6.44% of object size) and best Depth L1 distance (26.83), while tying Qwen-Image-Edit on MLLM score (89.60) and running fastest (35 s vs. 37 s for RF-Inversion and 77 s for Qwen). For 3D generation (Tables 2–3), the Trellis.2-based variant achieves the best DIFT alignment (4.44%) and best CLIP score (28.29 with Trellis.1), while completing inference in 1–2 minutes versus 40 minutes (MVEdit) or 2 hours (A3D, LucidDreamer). On video (Table 4), the method attains the highest VLM quality score (7.66), best DINO temporal consistency (0.98), and lowest Depth MAE (0.89) among all competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On images, the method decisively outperforms both editing-based baselines on structural alignment: DIFT distance drops from 11.33 (Qwen) and 8.62 (RF-Inversion) to 6.44, and Depth L1 improves from 37.64/28.50 to 26.83, while matching Qwen's MLLM prompt-alignment score (89.60) at nearly half the inference time. For 3D, the Trellis.2-based variant achieves the best DIFT alignment (4.44% vs. 5.45 for MVEdit, 5.82 for A3D, 11.29 for LucidDreamer) and is 20–60× faster than all competitors (2 min vs. 40 min–2 h). On GPTEval, the method is preferred over MVEdit (77.35%), LucidDreamer (76.81%), A3D (61.00%), and the Trellis.1 variant (67.46%) in overall quality. For video, the method beats MatchDiffusion on all three metrics (VLM 7.66 vs. 7.64, DINO 0.98 vs. 0.96, Depth MAE 0.89 vs. 1.12), dominates VACE (Depth MAE 0.89 vs. 2.60), and outperforms LucyEdit on VLM (7.66 vs. 4.77) and DINO (0.98 vs. 0.97), though LucyEdit shows a nominally lower Depth MAE (0.84) because it largely fails to perform the requested edit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on A3D's observation that smooth transitions between samples are necessary for structural alignment, and on MatchDiffusion's training-free joint-generation idea of merging diffusion trajectories. However, it departs from both: unlike A3D, it avoids SDS entirely in favor of a single-pass inference-time transport of a line segment in latent space; unlike MatchDiffusion, it enforces plausibility of intermediate points via a weighted linear regression with a closed-form solution rather than a hard trajectory merge at a threshold timestep. The method is framed as a drop-in modification of the inference loop for any rectified flow model operating on structured latents, positioning it as a general-purpose layer atop FLUX, Trellis, and WAN rather than a domain-specific architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude speedup over SDS-based 3D alignment (1–2 min vs. ~2 h) makes structurally aligned 3D asset generation practically viable for iterative design workflows, and the modality-agnostic formulation (image, video, 3D) with only inference-loop changes lowers the barrier to adoption for teams already using rectified flow backbones. Open questions include whether the two-sample segment formulation extends naturally to N-sample alignment, whether the structured-latent requirement limits applicability to newer unstructured 3D representations, and how the method behaves under very dissimilar prompt pairs where the linear interpolation may leave the data manifold. The paper also notes potential extension to 4D video and keypoint sequences as future directions.
