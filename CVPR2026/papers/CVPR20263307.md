## Problem

Multi-frame feed-forward visual geometry estimation methods achieve better cross-frame consistency than per-frame methods but consistently underperform on single-frame accuracy, and the reasons for this gap remain unclear. This paper systematically investigates the critical factors—training data composition, loss design, and alignment strategies—through rigorous ablation studies on a representative method (VGGT) to identify what drives the performance difference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263307/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of T monocular RGB images and processes them at both low resolution (518×518) and high resolution (1036×1036), fusing the two feature streams before the transformer. It outputs per-frame depth maps, world-coordinate 3D point maps, and camera parameters (quaternion rotation, translation vector, and horizontal/vertical field-of-view angles) for each frame in the sequence. The high-resolution input is handled without the 4× token blowup of direct upsampling, keeping the main transformer branch at low-resolution token count.

## Method

CARVE builds on the VGGT architecture (DINOv2 encoder + transformer blocks + depth/point/camera heads) and introduces three key changes. First, a consistency loss enforces geometric coherence by penalizing the discrepancy between the predicted point cloud and the point cloud obtained by unprojecting the predicted depth through the predicted camera intrinsics and extrinsics, integrating perspective-projection constraints directly into training. Second, an efficient high-resolution module extracts features separately from low- and high-resolution inputs and fuses them via frame-wise cross-attention (low-res as query, high-res as key/value) with zero-initialized learnable gating parameters, preserving pretrained weights while injecting high-resolution detail. Third, the training objective replaces the original spatial-gradient and confidence-aware losses with a simpler inverse-depth-weighted regression plus per-frame scale-shift alignment, which ablations show outperforms the more complex alternatives. The model is trained on a scaled-up, diverse dataset (Data3) with 30K iterations and dynamic batch sizes up to 24 frames.

## Results

CARVE achieves the best overall average rank (1.42) on point cloud reconstruction across KITTI, 7-Scenes, and TUM, with Chamfer L1 distances of 0.238, 0.043, and 0.029 respectively, outperforming VGGT (0.296, 0.049, 0.051) and Pi3 (0.273, 0.049, 0.032). On video depth estimation across seven datasets, CARVE attains the best rank (1.50) with relative errors as low as 0.020 on HAMMER and 0.040 on TUM. For camera pose estimation, CARVE achieves the best rank (1.69) on KITTI/7-Scenes/TUM/HO3D with ATE of 0.664 on KITTI and 0.052 on 7-Scenes. The efficient high-resolution architecture processes 1036×1036 inputs at 15.26 FPS with only 16.05 GiB peak memory for 32 frames, compared to 2.54 FPS and 47.89 GiB for VGGT at the same resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARVE outperforms VGGT (the direct baseline it builds upon) on nearly all metrics across all seven evaluation datasets, with the most notable gains on KITTI point cloud C-L1 (0.238 vs. 0.296, a 19.6% reduction) and TUM F@5 (0.861 vs. 0.712). Against the strongest competitor Pi3, CARVE wins on KITTI (C-L1 0.238 vs. 0.273) and TUM (C-L1 0.029 vs. 0.032) for point clouds, and on video depth for KITTI (Rel 0.082 vs. 0.078 — actually Pi3 slightly wins here) and HAMMER (Rel 0.020 vs. 0.033), but Pi3 retains an edge on Bonn point cloud (C-L1 0.031 vs. 0.043) and ETH3D (C-L1 0.106 vs. 0.236). CARVE decisively surpasses MoGe v2+LoFTR, Spann3R, and Fast3R across all tasks, with Fast3R showing particularly large gaps (e.g., KITTI C-L1 of 4.974 vs. CARVE's 0.238). On monocular depth estimation (Table 10), CARVE (rank 1.86) outperforms both MoGe (2.43) and Pi3 (2.93) despite not being explicitly optimized for this task. For camera pose, CARVE achieves the best rank on KITTI/7-Scenes/TUM/HO3D (1.69) and ties Pi3 on HAMMER/Bonn/ETH3D (1.92), with particularly strong FoV Rel of 0.024 on 7-Scenes and 0.018 on ETH3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT's feed-forward transformer architecture (DINOv2 encoder, camera tokens, multi-head decoders) and systematically ablates its training objectives to understand why multi-frame methods underperform per-frame methods on single-frame accuracy. It incorporates MoGe's inverse-depth weighting and per-frame scale-shift alignment, while departing from both VGGT and MoGe by removing spatial gradient and confidence-aware loss terms that the ablations show to be counterproductive. The consistency loss bridges optimization-based geometric constraints (reprojection consistency) into the learning-based training loop, and the cross-attention high-resolution fusion module is a novel architectural addition not present in either VGGT or prior multi-frame methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ablation findings challenge standard practice in feed-forward 3D geometry estimation: commonly adopted spatial gradient losses and learnable confidence weight maps degrade rather than improve performance, and a simple inverse-depth weight map suffices. The efficient high-resolution architecture (6× FPS over direct upsampling at 1036×1036) offers a practical path to higher-resolution inference without prohibitive compute costs. Open questions include whether these loss-design insights generalize beyond the VGGT architecture family, whether continued data scaling yields diminishing returns, and whether the consistency loss can be extended to handle dynamic scenes or longer sequences where geometric assumptions may break down.
