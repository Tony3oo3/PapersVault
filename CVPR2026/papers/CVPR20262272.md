## Problem

Existing road segmentation datasets cannot simultaneously provide global geographic diversity, pixel-level annotation quality, and structural continuity, limiting model generalization across terrains. Additionally, mainstream interactive segmentation models rely on point- or box-based prompts that fail to encode the elongated, topologically connected geometry of road networks, leading to fragmented predictions and ambiguous user intent in remote sensing scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 512×512 aerial satellite image (0.8–1.1 m/pixel resolution), a set of connectivity-aware prompts (positive/negative clicks or scribbles of varying geometry: center, line, or Bézier), and the previous iteration's prediction mask. It outputs a binary road segmentation mask at the same spatial resolution. The process is iterative: each round appends new prompts to a cumulative prompt map and refines the mask, typically converging in 5–7 interactions with a 3.7M-parameter network running at ~40 ms per prediction on a single GPU.

## Method

RoadGIE builds on a lightweight UNet backbone augmented with a Directional Aggregation Module that applies learnable 1D convolutions along four dominant directions (vertical, horizontal, diagonal, anti-diagonal) to enforce directional consistency in road-like structures. The key training innovations are: (1) an expert-guided prompting strategy that computes inter-model disagreement to target high-uncertainty regions, simulating realistic user corrections; (2) a topo-semantic coupled road instantiation pipeline that extracts centerlines, groups segments by attributes, and uses a prompt-conditioned scorer to resolve ambiguity about which road class the user intends; and (3) a prompt-excluded skeleton recall loss that applies topology-preserving supervision only to non-prompt pixels, preventing overfitting on already-constrained regions while preserving connectivity in under-annotated areas. The framework is trained on the newly introduced WorldRoadSeg-360K dataset (366,947 images across 38 countries and 223 cities) with five interaction rounds per batch using 1–3 simulated prompts per round.

## Results

On the WorldRoadSeg-360K test set, RoadGIE achieves a Dice of 0.835 and APLS of 0.620, surpassing the strongest baseline ScribblePrompt (0.809 Dice, 0.592 APLS) by 2.6 and 2.8 points respectively. On the combined baseline dataset, RoadGIE reaches 0.807 Dice and 0.593 APLS, again leading all competitors. When evaluated on the OOD LSRV test set with six topology-aware metrics, training on WorldRoadSeg-360K yields 0.835 Dice, 0.905 clDice, and 0.620 APLS, with the lowest β0 (5.823) and β1 (2.752) among all pretraining configurations, confirming superior topological consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoadGIE outperforms all six compared methods on both the baseline dataset and WorldRoadSeg-360K across Dice and APLS. Against the closest competitor ScribblePrompt [42], RoadGIE gains +1.6 Dice on the baseline dataset (0.807 vs. 0.791) and +2.6 Dice on WorldRoadSeg-360K (0.835 vs. 0.809), with corresponding APLS improvements of +0.9 and +2.8 points. The SAM family [18] (ViT-b and ViT-h) trails by 6.9–11.6 Dice points, confirming that point/box-based prompting is poorly suited to elongated road structures. ScribbleSeg [4] (B0/B3) achieves mid-range performance (0.761–0.788 Dice) but lacks the topological awareness of RoadGIE's skeleton-based loss. PRISM-2D [20] and its Lite variant [21] are the weakest baselines (0.622–0.669 Dice), likely due to their medical-imaging design not transferring well to aerial road scenes. In terms of inference speed, RoadGIE (39.52 ms) is comparable to ScribblePrompt (30.76 ms) and substantially faster than SAM (283.65 ms), PRISM (305.36 ms), and EISeg (270.28 ms), while using only 3.7M parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoadGIE sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: interactive segmentation (SAM, ScribblePrompt) and connectivity-aware road extraction (strip convolutions, clDice). It adapts the skeleton-based recall loss from Kirchhoff et al. [17] into an interactive setting with a prompt-excluded variant, and repurposes directional 1D convolutions from strip pooling [12] as a Directional Aggregation Module at the decoder. The key conceptual departure is from point/box-based prompting paradigms: by introducing scribble-based, topology-aligned prompts and a topo-semantic instantiation module, the paper argues that prompt geometry should match the elongated, connected morphology of roads rather than relying on generic spatial cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WorldRoadSeg-360K dataset (366,947 images, 38 countries, 223 cities) fills a genuine gap between vector-based global datasets (Global-Scale) and small pixel-level benchmarks (LSRV), and is likely to become a standard reference for road segmentation evaluation. The 79% annotation time reduction and 3.7M-parameter real-time inference make interactive road extraction practically deployable for map-update pipelines. Open questions include generalization beyond 0.8–1.1 m resolution, handling scenes requiring more than the six training interaction steps, and whether the topo-semantic instantiation generalizes to other linear-structure tasks (rivers, power lines) beyond roads.
