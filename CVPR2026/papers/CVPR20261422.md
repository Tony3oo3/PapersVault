## Problem

Few-shot industrial anomaly detection methods have grown increasingly complex, relying on large memory banks, multi-stage training, prompt tuning, or auxiliary datasets, even as vision foundation models like DINOv2 produce dense, transferable features that capture both semantic and structural properties. The paper asks whether such complexity is still necessary: can a simple statistical model over foundation-model features match or exceed these pipelines in the few-shot regime?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261422/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes k (1, 2, or 4) normal RGB images per category and a test RGB image, all processed at a fixed 672×672 resolution. Each normal image is augmented with 30 random rotations (0°–345°) to build a representative covariance estimate. The output is a patch-level anomaly map (h×w grid) that is bilinearly upsampled and Gaussian-smoothed (σ=4) to pixel resolution for segmentation, plus a single image-level score computed as the mean of the top 1% of patch scores (empirical TVaR).

## Method

SubspaceAD is a two-stage, training-free pipeline. First, patch-level features are extracted from the k normal images (and their 30 rotated augmentations) using a frozen DINOv2-G backbone, with tokens mean-pooled across intermediate layers 22–28 to balance semantic and structural detail. Second, a deterministic PCA model is fitted to the aggregated feature set, retaining the top r eigenvectors that explain ≥99% of variance, yielding a compact subspace basis C and mean μ. At inference, each test patch feature is projected onto this subspace and the squared reconstruction residual ‖x_p − (μ + CCᵀ(x_p − μ))‖² serves as the anomaly score, grounded in the probabilistic PCA negative log-likelihood. The entire per-category model is just μ and C (<1 MB), and inference on a 672×672 image takes ~300 ms on a single H100 GPU, with the DINOv2 forward pass dominating the cost.

## Results

In the 1-shot setting, SubspaceAD achieves 97.1% image-level AUROC and 97.5% pixel-level AUROC on MVTec-AD, and 93.4% image-level AUROC and 98.2% pixel-level AUROC on VisA, surpassing all prior methods. In the 4-shot setting, it reaches 97.9% I-AUROC and 94.3% PRO on MVTec-AD, and 94.7% I-AUROC and 98.4% P-AUROC on VisA. The method is training-free, requires only a frozen DINOv2-G backbone and a PCA fit, and adds negligible inference overhead (~74 ms for subspace projection on an H100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SubspaceAD outperforms the strongest baseline, AnomalyDINO [11], across nearly all settings: in 1-shot it leads by 0.5% I-AUROC on MVTec-AD (97.1 vs 96.6) and by a substantial 6.0% on VisA (93.4 vs 87.4). In 4-shot, the gap on VisA widens to 2.1% I-AUROC (94.7 vs 92.6), though AnomalyDINO retains a slight 0.3% edge on VisA PRO (94.1 vs 93.8). Memory-bank methods (SPADE, PatchCore) trail by 10–16% I-AUROC in the 1-shot setting, and VLM-based methods (WinCLIP, PromptAD, IIPAD) fall 2–6% behind on VisA I-AUROC. FastRecon [15] reports only partial results (no 1-shot, no VisA), limiting direct comparison. Overall, SubspaceAD achieves the best or near-best result in every reported cell of Table 1 while being training-free and requiring no memory bank, prompt tuning, or auxiliary data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SubspaceAD departs from the three dominant paradigms in few-shot anomaly detection—reconstruction-based models, memory-bank retrieval, and VLM prompt tuning—by replacing all learned components with a frozen DINOv2-G encoder and a classical PCA subspace fit to normal patch features. Conceptually, it repurposes the well-established statistical principle that anomalies are outliers from the dominant PCA subspace of normal data, but applies it to the rich feature space of a modern vision foundation model rather than raw pixels or hand-crafted descriptors. The TVaR image-level aggregation is borrowed from AnomalyDINO, and the multi-layer feature averaging draws on the observation that intermediate DINOv2 layers balance semantics and spatial detail, but the core contribution is the argument that no training, memory bank, or prompt engineering is needed once features are sufficiently expressive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a <1 MB per-category model, zero training, and a single forward pass make SubspaceAD far easier to deploy and maintain in industrial settings than memory-bank or multi-stage pipelines. The 6-point VisA gap over AnomalyDINO in the 1-shot setting suggests that prior methods were over-parameterizing the problem, and the finding that DINOv3 performs slightly worse than DINOv2-G hints that feature quality is not monotonically tied to model scale. Open questions include whether the PCA subspace assumption holds for highly textured or multi-material categories, how the method behaves when the test distribution shifts substantially from the few normal references, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D industrial inspection.
