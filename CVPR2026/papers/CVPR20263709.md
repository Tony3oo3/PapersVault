## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting-based inverse rendering methods fail under high-illumination scenes because (1) the unstructured point-based representation lacks explicit surfaces for reliable visibility and shadow reasoning, (2) simultaneous material-illumination estimation from sparse views is ill-posed, and (3) extreme radiance disparities in specular highlights destabilize photometric gradients, causing shadows and highlights to be baked into material maps. No prior 3DGS method combines physically-grounded visibility, generative illumination priors, and radiance-aware optimization to address all three failure modes jointly.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 100 multi-view RGB images per scene (rendered under one of 6 high-luminance HDR environments) and produces a 2D Gaussian surfel scene representation with per-surfel PBR attributes (albedo, roughness, metallic), a learned HDR environment map, and a scene-level exposure parameter. The output supports real-time novel view synthesis (~92 FPS) and relighting under arbitrary unseen HDR environments. Training runs for 30k iterations on a single RTX 4090, with the proxy mesh re-extracted every 5k iterations to amortize ray-tracing cost.

## Method

IR-HGP decomposes the scene into 2D Gaussian surfels (constrained to flat disks via zero z-scaling) and a periodically extracted TSDF mesh; the mesh provides binary ray-traced visibility V(p, d_in) that gates direct illumination in the PBR shading equation, while indirect light is approximated by trainable spherical harmonics. A pre-trained conditional HDR diffusion model (Bolduc et al.) is distilled into the learnable environment map via Score Distillation Sampling, conditioned on a lightweight encoder feature extracted from the input views, steering the map toward the manifold of physically plausible HDR lighting. A single learnable exposure parameter β modulates an ACES tone-mapping curve applied to both rendered and target images before computing the L1 photometric loss, stabilizing gradients across orders-of-magnitude radiance ranges without introducing per-pixel black-box corrections. The total loss combines this corrected photometric term, the SDS generative loss, a normal consistency loss, and an edge-aware normal smoothing loss, all optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

On the Shiny Blender high-illumination benchmark, IR-HGP achieves a mean PSNR of 33.61, SSIM of 0.9761, and LPIPS of 0.0369, outperforming the strongest baseline DSDF (32.12 / 0.9700 / 0.0453) by +1.49 dB in PSNR and −0.0084 in LPIPS. On NeRF Synthetic, IR-HGP wins on 4 of 6 objects (e.g., Ficus: 36.86 vs. 36.37 PSNR; Hotdog: 37.73 vs. 35.63) but trails DSDF on Chair (35.10 vs. 37.54) and Lego (34.03 vs. 36.68). On the real-world Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, IR-HGP achieves the best LPIPS (0.196) but trails 2DGS (PSNR 27.68) and GS-IR (SSIM 0.805) on pixel-level metrics. Training time is 1.5 h per scene on a single RTX 4090, with 92 FPS rendering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TensoIR [17] (NeRF-based tensor decomposition), IR-HGP wins on every Shiny Blender object by 3–9 dB in PSNR and on 4 of 6 NeRF Synthetic objects, confirming that the 3DGS representation with PBR shading substantially outperforms volumetric NeRF for relighting. Against GS-IR [23] (view-space Gaussian shading), IR-HGP dominates across all 10 objects in both datasets, with the largest gaps on highly specular scenes (e.g., Helmet: 35.00 vs. 26.56 PSNR), attributable to the explicit mesh-based visibility and diffusion-prior illumination. Against R3DG [10] (ray-traced relightable Gaussians), IR-HGP wins on 8 of 10 objects; the advantage is most pronounced on Shiny Blender (mean +3.8 dB PSNR), where R3DG's simplified visibility and unconstrained SH indirect lighting break down under intense highlights. Against DSDF [55] (discretized SDF + Gaussians), the closest competitor, IR-HGP wins on the Shiny Blender mean (+1.49 dB PSNR, −0.0084 LPIPS) and on 4 of 6 NeRF Synthetic objects, but loses on Chair (−2.44 dB) and Lego (−2.65 dB), suggesting DSDF's explicit SDF geometry still has an edge on certain diffuse-dominant shapes. On the real-world Mip-NeRF 360 benchmark, IR-HGP achieves the best perceptual quality (LPIPS 0.196 vs. 0.267–0.297) but trails 2DGS [15] (PSNR 27.68) and GS-IR (SSIM 0.805) on pixel-level fidelity, indicating that the PBR decomposition introduces a small accuracy-perception trade-off under sensor noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS-based inverse rendering lineage (GS-IR, R3DG, DSDF) by addressing three specific failure modes under high illumination: unreliable visibility from unstructured Gaussians, ill-posed illumination-material coupling, and gradient instability from extreme radiance ranges. It draws on 2D Gaussian surfels for geometrically accurate normals, a pre-trained HDR diffusion model (Bolduc et al.) as a conditional generative prior via Score Distillation Sampling (DreamFusion), and the ACES tone-mapping curve as a learnable radiance correction. Conceptually, it bridges the gap between the NeRF-based inverse rendering tradition (TensoIR, NeRF-Relight) and the real-time 3DGS paradigm, while being the first to introduce a diffusion-based environment map prior into the 3DGS inverse rendering pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical, real-time-capable pipeline for producing relightable 3D assets under challenging lighting, which is directly relevant to graphics, AR/VR, and content creation workflows. The modular design (HVD, GIFP, PARC) is ablated cleanly, making individual components reusable in other 3DGS-based rendering tasks. However, the approach still relies on a pre-trained diffusion model for illumination, limiting its applicability to scenes whose lighting falls within the prior's training distribution, and the authors acknowledge the scarcity of diffuse-only evaluation data. Open questions include whether the diffusion prior can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives for on-device use, and whether the periodic mesh extraction (every 5k iterations) can be made adaptive to scene complexity.
