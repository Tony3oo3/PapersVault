## Problem

Flicker artifacts from AC-powered lighting combined with rolling-shutter exposure produce structured, periodic, and directional brightness patterns that degrade short-exposure images. Existing deep restoration methods treat flicker removal as a generic image restoration task, ignoring these physical priors, which leads to incomplete flicker suppression and ghosting artifacts, especially under severe or subtle flicker conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262361/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network receives a burst of three spatially aligned flickering RGB frames (one base frame and two reference frames) at resolution H×W×3, concatenated along the channel dimension and processed by a 3×3 group convolution to extract per-frame features. The output is a single restored flicker-free RGB image of the base frame, produced via residual learning (Î₁ = I₁ + R) after upsampling the decoder features back to full resolution.

## Method

Flickerformer is an asymmetric U-shaped encoder-decoder transformer (3 levels, 2 blocks per level, channels [32,64,96]) that embeds two flicker priors into its architecture. For periodicity, a Phase-based Fusion Module (PFM) computes inter-frame phase correlation in the FFT domain to generate frequency-domain weight maps that adaptively fuse reference-frame features, while an Autocorrelation Feed-Forward Network (AFFN) applies the Wiener-Khinchin theorem to extract intra-frame spatial autocorrelation and reinforces recurring structural patterns via a dual-domain (frequency + spatial) modulation with learnable scaling. For directionality, a Wavelet-based Directional Attention Module (WDAM) performs Haar DWT to split features into LL/LH/HL/HH subbands, then uses the horizontal and vertical high-frequency subbands to generate a directional modulation map that weights the value vectors in window-based multi-head attention over the low-frequency branch, reducing attention cost by ~75% while improving flicker localization. The model is trained end-to-end with Adam (lr=1e-4) using a combined L1 + VGG-19 perceptual loss on the BurstDeflicker dataset.

## Results

On the BurstDeflicker benchmark, Flickerformer achieves a PSNR of 31.226 dB, SSIM of 0.920, and LPIPS of 0.045, outperforming the second-best method (AST, 30.646 dB) by +0.580 dB in PSNR while using only 3.92M parameters (19.70% of AST's 19.90M) and 128.76G FLOPs. It also achieves the best LPIPS score (0.045) among all 16 evaluated methods, indicating superior perceptual quality. The model's efficiency is notable: it matches or exceeds methods with 5–7× more parameters (e.g., Restormer at 26.10M, FPro at 22.38M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flickerformer outperforms all 15 baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS). The closest competitor is AST [75] (30.646 dB PSNR, 0.918 SSIM, 0.050 LPIPS), which Flickerformer surpasses by +0.580 dB in PSNR and +0.002 in SSIM while using only 20% of the parameters (3.92M vs. 19.90M). Among general-purpose restoration transformers, Restormer [71] (30.630 dB) and Uformer [63] (30.544 dB) trail by +0.596 and +0.682 dB respectively, despite having 4–7× more parameters. For burst-specific methods, Burstormer [14] (29.439 dB) and FBANet [66] (29.459 dB) lag by roughly 1.8 dB, highlighting the benefit of explicitly modeling flicker periodicity and directionality. In the low-light and HDR categories, Retinexformer [5] (29.598 dB), HDRTransformer [44] (30.031 dB), and AFUNet [37] (28.922 dB) all fall well behind, confirming that generic restoration priors are insufficient for structured flicker degradation. Notably, Flickerformer also achieves the lowest FLOPs (128.76G) among all methods with PSNR above 30 dB, demonstrating that the wavelet-based directional attention reduces computational cost by ~75% relative to standard window-based attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of treating flicker removal as a generic image restoration problem (as done by Burstormer, Restormer, AST, and others) by explicitly embedding two physical priors of AC flicker—periodicity and directionality—into a transformer architecture. The phase-based fusion module repurposes classical phase correlation from signal processing for inter-frame feature alignment, while the wavelet-based directional attention module adapts Haar decomposition to inject orientation-specific high-frequency cues into window-based attention. The work is built directly on the BurstDeflicker benchmark established by the same authors [54], which first formalized multi-frame flicker removal as a distinct task, and extends the U-shaped encoder-decoder transformer lineage (SwinIR, Restormer, Uformer) with flicker-specific inductive biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that structured degradations like flicker benefit substantially from architecture-level prior embedding rather than generic restoration, this paper provides a template for tackling other physically-structured artifacts (e.g., moiré, sensor-specific banding) with domain-aware transformer designs. The 75% computational reduction in the attention module via wavelet decomposition is practically relevant for deployment on mobile devices where burst photography is most common. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is failure under large-scale light extinction where no clean reference region exists across the burst, leaving open the question of how to handle flicker in scenes with globally varying illumination. Generalization beyond the specific AC-frequency flicker patterns in BurstDeflicker to other flicker sources (e.g., PWM screens, fluorescent lighting) remains untested.
