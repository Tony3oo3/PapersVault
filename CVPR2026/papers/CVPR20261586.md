## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal prediction methods for ocean forecasting fail to simultaneously address three critical gaps: they treat coupled ocean variables (temperature, salinity, velocity) as independent despite equation-of-state constraints, they rely on planar projections that introduce severe distortions at high latitudes and cannot handle longitude wraparound, and they lack mechanisms to represent multi-scale temporal dynamics from daily internal waves to seasonal-interannual variability while preserving conservation laws.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261586/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes two consecutive historical ocean states (t−1 and t−2) comprising 145 variables across 36 depth levels (surface to 1062 m) plus sea surface height at 1° resolution (resampled from native 1/12° GLORYS12V1), augmented with external forcing terms and intra-annual temporal position encodings. It outputs a probabilistic forecast of the full ocean state at time t as an ensemble of 10 members, generated by iteratively denoising the residual r_t = X_t − X_{t−1} under a second-order Markov assumption, with the loss weighted by depth-dependent oceanographic priors and latitude-dependent grid area corrections.

## Method

PhyOceanCast is a physics-informed diffusion model built on two complementary modules. SGAN-MOC encodes each of the 145 variables through a dedicated convolutional encoder, maps features onto a multi-resolution icosahedral mesh via geodesic-proximity aggregation, and performs k-hop-constrained spherical attention to enable cross-variable interactions while preserving anisotropic dynamics. PWTC decomposes temporal features via 3D discrete wavelet transform (Daubechies-4) into multi-scale subbands, applies depth-coupled multi-head attention across 36 vertical levels with learnable depth embeddings, injects Coriolis-force encoding for velocity components, and enforces advection-diffusion physics through learnable convolutional operators within ConvGRU blocks. The denoiser is trained with a noise-level-weighted MSE loss on the residual, using depth-dependent and area-dependent weighting to respect oceanographic structure, and generates ensemble forecasts through iterative reverse diffusion.

## Results

On the GLORYS12V1 dataset (145 ocean variables, 36 depth levels, 1° resolution), PhyOceanCast achieves a 13.7% RMSE improvement over the best deterministic baseline (Video Swin Transformer) at the 30-day lead time, reaching RMSE 1.1109 vs. 1.2632. At 7-day lead time, PhyOceanCast attains RMSE 0.7205 and ACC_lat 0.9647, outperforming all 11 baselines across every metric (RMSE, MAE, ACC_lat, Pearson, SSIM) at all four lead times (3, 7, 15, 30 days). The improvement is most dramatic against the diffusion baseline DiffCast, which degrades to RMSE 4.8514 at 30 days versus PhyOceanCast's 1.1109 (a 77% reduction).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyOceanCast outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric at every lead time. Against the strongest deterministic baseline, Video Swin Transformer, PhyOceanCast reduces 30-day RMSE from 1.2632 to 1.1109 (13.7% improvement) and lifts ACC_lat from 0.8713 to 0.9023. The spherical-graph baseline GraphCast degrades substantially at longer lead times (RMSE 1.7577 at 30 days), while PhyOceanCast maintains 36.8% lower RMSE, attributed to anisotropic dynamics handling and physics-informed constraints. The diffusion baseline DiffCast (10 ensemble members) suffers catastrophic error accumulation at 30 days (RMSE 4.8514), whereas PhyOceanCast's physics-guided denoising keeps RMSE at 1.1109—a 77% reduction—demonstrating that physics constraints are essential for stable long-range probabilistic forecasting. Among the remaining baselines, 3D-Geoformer (RMSE 1.3228) and SimVP (1.2787) are the closest deterministic competitors at 30 days, both still trailing PhyOceanCast by 16–19% in RMSE. The MambaOut variants and SVQ show consistent underperformance, with SVQ degrading most severely (RMSE 2.5322 at 30 days), suggesting that vector-quantization-based approaches struggle with the multi-scale temporal dynamics of ocean variables.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyOceanCast sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: GraphCast's icosahedral-mesh spherical graph architecture for global weather forecasting, and DiffCast's diffusion-based residual forecasting paradigm for probabilistic prediction. The paper departs from both by introducing physics-informed constraints (advection-diffusion operators, Coriolis encoding) and multi-scale wavelet temporal decomposition that neither predecessor addresses, while also adding heterogeneous variable-specific encoders and depth-coupled attention to model the equation-of-state coupling among temperature, salinity, and velocity fields. Unlike prior ocean-specific models (WenHai, 3D-Geoformer) that treat variables semi-independently or use planar convolutions, PhyOceanCast explicitly couples cross-variable and cross-depth interactions within a spherical, probabilistic framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.7% RMSE reduction at 30-day lead time over the best deterministic baseline, and the large gap over DiffCast (which shares the diffusion backbone but lacks physics constraints), suggest that embedding conservation-law operators is not merely a regularizer but a structural necessity for long-horizon ocean forecasting. The paper's main limitation is evaluation on a single 1°-resolution dataset (GLORYS12V1) with 145 variables, leaving open whether the physics-informed modules generalize to higher-resolution eddy-resolving grids or to real-time operational settings where 700–1500 GPU-hours of training is prohibitive. Land-sea discontinuities reduce effective training samples, and the authors acknowledge this as a key future direction. Broader impact: if the SGAN-MOC + PWTC recipe transfers to other geophysical domains (atmosphere, cryosphere), it could become a template for physics-informed probabilistic forecasting on the sphere.
