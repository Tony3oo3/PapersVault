## Problem

Despite rapid advances in text-to-image model fidelity and prompt adherence, it remains unclear whether these improvements translate into better synthetic training data for downstream vision tasks. This paper reveals a surprising regression: newer T2I models (2022–2025) consistently produce synthetic data that yields *lower* classification accuracy on real test images, challenging the assumption that generative realism implies data realism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes text prompts (either bare ImageNet class names or detailed 2–3 sentence GPT-4.1-nano captions of real training images) and a T2I model checkpoint, generating 500 images per class across 200 ImageNet-1k classes at 512×512 (1024×1024 for SDXL), all downsampled to 256×256. Outputs include Synth→Real classification accuracy, Real→Synth accuracy, density/coverage metrics in CLIP-ViT-L feature space, and per-domain (texture, structure, low-frequency, high-frequency) performance gaps. A key design choice is the two-tier prompting: class names represent the realistic deployment scenario, while detailed captions serve as an upper bound that is impractical at scale.

## Method

The paper benchmarks thirteen open-source T2I diffusion models (SD 1.5 through Lumina 2.0, including distilled turbo variants) by training ResNet-50 (plus ViT-Ti, ConvNeXt-Ti, Swin-Ti) exclusively on synthetic images and evaluating on the real ImageNet validation set. To diagnose *why* newer models underperform, three controlled probes are applied: (1) structure–texture separation via depth-map classification (Depth Anything V2) and 9×9-patch BagNet texture classification; (2) spectral decomposition via high-pass (f ≥ 0.8 f_N) and low-pass (f ≤ 0.2 f_N) filtering; and (3) distributional analysis using Naeem et al.'s density and coverage metrics in CLIP feature space, complemented by the Real→Synth vs. Synth→Real transfer asymmetry. The central finding is that newer models collapse into narrow, high-density, low-coverage clusters in feature space, degrading texture and high-frequency detail while preserving global structure and low-frequency content, and that this collapse is exacerbated by strong prompt-following on simple class-name inputs.

## Results

Across 13 open-source T2I models (2022–2025) evaluated on a 200-class ImageNet-1k subset (500 images/class), Synth→Real top-1 accuracy for ResNet-50 trained solely on synthetic data shows a consistent downward trend with model recency, despite monotonic improvements in GenEval and CLIPScore. The real-data baseline (trained on actual ImageNet images) sits above all synthetic-trained classifiers, and the gap widens for the newest generators (Flux-Dev, Qwen-Image, Lumina 2.0). Using detailed GPT-4.1-nano captions instead of bare class names partially recovers accuracy and increases coverage, but the high-frequency/texture gap persists. Density–coverage analysis reveals that newer models produce high-density, low-coverage feature distributions (collapsed manifolds), while older models like SD 2.1 and SDXL show lower density with broader coverage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 13 T2I generators as synthetic data sources rather than comparing a single proposed method against baselines. Older models (Stable Diffusion 1.5, SD 2.1) achieve the highest Synth→Real accuracy, while the newest models (Flux-Dev, Qwen-Image, Lumina 2.0) produce the lowest transfer accuracy despite scoring highest on GenEval and CLIPScore—revealing an inverse relationship between prompt-following quality and training-data utility. SDXL and Sana occupy a middle ground with moderate accuracy but notably low density and low coverage, indicating a domain shift rather than a simple collapse. Distilled/turbo variants (SDXL-Turbo, SD 3.5-Large-Turbo, Flux-Schnell) generally underperform their full counterparts, consistent with reduced diversity from fewer denoising steps. In the texture/structure decomposition, all models show a smaller gap in depth (structure) space than in RGB or BagNet (texture) space, and the high-frequency gap exceeds the low-frequency gap for every generator. The Real→Synth transfer is consistently much higher than Synth→Real across all models, confirming that synthetic images remain individually classifiable but collectively fail to span the real data manifold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-data-for-training literature (Geng et al. 2024, Hammoud et al. 2024, Tian et al.) by systematically benchmarking thirteen T2I models spanning 2022–2025 under a controlled ImageNet-1k protocol, whereas prior studies typically evaluated one or two generators. It departs from the prompt-engineering line (CiP, Singh et al.) by showing that richer captions only partially recover performance and cannot fix the core texture/high-frequency deficit. The diagnostic framework repurposes established tools—depth-based structure classifiers (Depth Anything V2), local BagNet texture classifiers, frequency-band filtering, and Naeem et al.'s density-coverage metrics—into a unified probe for separating sample realism from distribution realism, a distinction prior work had not explicitly operationalized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely held assumption in vision research that progress in generative fidelity implies progress in data realism, with direct practical consequences for anyone planning to substitute real training sets with T2I-generated data. The finding that newer, higher-scoring models are progressively worse as data generators suggests that current T2I training objectives (aesthetic quality, prompt adherence) are misaligned with the diversity and frequency statistics that discriminative models need. Open questions include whether the trend holds for detection, segmentation, or larger-scale datasets, whether proprietary models exhibit the same collapse, and whether diversity-optimized generation or learnability-aware distillation can close the gap without sacrificing visual quality.
