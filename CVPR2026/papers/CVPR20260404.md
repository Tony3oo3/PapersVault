## Problem

Pretrained models lack reliable uncertainty estimates, and existing methods like deep ensembles and MC dropout are too computationally expensive for large-scale deployment. Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) offers efficient single-pass uncertainty estimation but requires training from scratch with an evidential objective, which is incompatible with the pretrain-then-finetune paradigm. The paper addresses how to convert an arbitrary pretrained model into an evidential model post-hoc, using only a small adaptation dataset, without degrading the original predictive accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input the last hidden state (or logit vector) of a frozen pretrained model—either a VGG-16/ResNet-50 image classifier or a Llama-3.1-8B LLM whose output space is restricted to four answer tokens (A, B, C, D). The ETN module produces a sample-dependent scalar transformation parameter A drawn from a learned Gamma variational distribution, which rescales the logits before they are mapped through softplus plus a learnable prior to form Dirichlet parameters. The final output is a Dirichlet distribution over class probabilities, from which uncertainty scores (total concentration α₀, mutual information, differential entropy) are computed via Monte-Carlo marginalization over A.

## Method

ETN is a lightweight MLP that takes the pretrained model's last hidden state and outputs the parameters of a Gamma variational distribution over a scalar logit-scaling factor A. The transformed logits z′ = A·z are passed through softplus plus a learnable prior vector b to yield Dirichlet parameters α′ = softplus(Az) + b, preserving logit ordering and thus accuracy. Training minimizes an ELBO-derived objective: the negative log-likelihood of a label-conditioned target Dirichlet under the transformed model Dirichlet (Monte-Carlo approximated over M samples of A), plus a KL regularization term pulling the variational distribution toward a Gamma prior with mode > 1 to enlarge inter-class margins. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 1) is that cross-entropy training imposes no constraint on logit magnitude, so Dirichlet concentration is uncalibrated; a sample-dependent A is therefore necessary to recover meaningful per-sample uncertainty. At inference, M Monte-Carlo samples of A are drawn and the predictive probability is averaged, adding negligible latency while enabling full evidential uncertainty quantification.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, ETN achieves 90.70% accuracy (matching the best baseline DeepEns at 90.69%) while attaining the highest AUPR for confidence estimation (MP: 98.99, UM: 98.41) and OOD detection (MP: 85.19, MI: 85.22, DE: 85.60 on SVHN). On ImageNet, ETN preserves the pretrained ResNet50 accuracy at 79.61% (vs. 46.03% for MAP_CE and 2.53% for DMM) and leads all OOD detection metrics (e.g., 95.78 MP on ImageNet-A). On LLM QA (RACE/OBQA with Llama 3.1-8B), ETN maintains accuracy (89.69%/88.80%) and achieves the best AUPR across ID and OOD settings (e.g., 96.80 MP on RACE→MMLU), with only ~10 MC samples needed at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETN outperforms all eight baselines on CIFAR-10 OOD detection: it exceeds the next-best method (DMM, 81.31 MP on SVHN) by 3.9 points and IB-EDL (62.37 MP) by 22.8 points, while also leading on MI and DE. On ImageNet, the advantage is dramatic—ETN retains 79.61% accuracy versus 46.03% (MAP_CE), 26.98% (DeepEns), and 2.53% (DMM), and achieves 95.78 MP on ImageNet-A versus 92.50 (MAP_CE) and 83.78 (DMM). Among LLM baselines, MC-Dropout is the closest competitor (96.94 MP on RACE ID vs. ETN's 97.60), but requires multiple stochastic forward passes at inference; Laplace-LoRA collapses to 64.98 MP, and MAP_EDL drops accuracy to 84.94%. For transformation-strategy comparisons, ETN (sample-dependent variational scaling) beats static Platt scaling and AdaTS by 17–25 points on OOD MI, confirming that modeling a distribution over the transformation parameter is essential. The key differentiator is that ETN is the only method that simultaneously preserves pretrained accuracy, provides all three uncertainty metrics (MP, MI, DE), and adds negligible inference cost (single forward pass + ~10 MC samples of a lightweight MLP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Evidential Deep Learning (EDL) [35] from a training-time framework to a post-hoc adaptation setting, addressing the gap between EDL's requirement for end-to-end evidential training and the reality that most practitioners work with pretrained models trained under cross-entropy. Conceptually, it bridges calibration methods (Platt scaling [12], AdaTS [20]) and full EDL by learning a sample-dependent logit-space transformation whose output is interpreted as Dirichlet parameters, rather than merely rescaling probabilities. The variational formulation (ELBO over a Gamma-distributed transformation parameter) departs from both deterministic calibration and from heavier post-hoc EDL approaches like DMM [36] or IB-EDL [26], which require access to training data or large meta-models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETN offers a practically deployable path to uncertainty estimation for pretrained models: it adds negligible inference cost, preserves accuracy, and requires only a small adaptation set—addressing the core deployment barrier that limits ensembles and MC dropout. The finding that a scalar (not vector or matrix) transformation suffices is both a simplification and a limitation, suggesting the method may not capture richer per-class uncertainty structure that higher-dimensional EDL models can express. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to generative or open-ended tasks (beyond fixed-choice QA), whether the variational distribution over A is truly necessary versus a well-tuned deterministic network, and how performance holds as base models grow to hundreds of billions of parameters where even small per-sample overheads compound.
