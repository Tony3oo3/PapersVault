## Problem

Controllable generation methods (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, GLIGEN, etc.) are typically trained with the same supervision loss as the base T2I model (ε-prediction for diffusion, u-prediction for flow matching), which leads to very long training times before convergence. The paper identifies that this choice implicitly weights the learning signal by the signal-to-noise ratio, severely down-weighting early denoising steps where the global layout is determined—precisely the steps most critical for spatial control.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen pre-trained T2I backbone (Stable Diffusion 1.4/1.5 with UNet, or FLUX.1 with DiT) and trains only a lightweight adapter that ingests a novel control signal alongside the text prompt. Control signals span two families: spatially-aligned image-form inputs (segmentation masks, depth maps, Canny edge maps, pose skeletons) and non-spatially-aligned inputs (bounding box coordinates paired with text or image tokens). The output is a single generated image that simultaneously satisfies the textual description and the spatial/structural constraint.

## Method

The core contribution is a simple re-parameterization: any predictor (ε, v, or u) is converted into an equivalent x₀-predictor via a linear transformation using time-dependent coefficients c_skip(t)=1/α_t and c_out(t)=−σ_t/α_t, and the network is then supervised directly against the clean image x₀. This removes the implicit SNR weighting (α_t²/σ_t²) that the original ε-loss applies, giving the model a much stronger learning signal at early denoising steps where layout is established. The paper proves this is mathematically equivalent to re-weighting the original ε-loss by the inverse SNR (σ_t²/α_t²), and validates the equivalence empirically. A new convergence-speed metric, mAUCC (mean Area Under the Convergence Curve), is introduced to quantify training efficiency across multiple horizons. The approach generalizes across both diffusion and flow-matching paradigms and across UNet and DiT architectures, yielding up to 2× faster convergence and improved final control fidelity.

## Results

On spatially-aligned controls (Table 1), x0-supervision consistently outperforms ε- and v-supervision across ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, and OminiControl. For ControlNet, mAUCC improves by 25% on semantic segmentation (31.52 vs. 25.19) and 17.65% on pose (42.19 vs. 35.86), while FID drops from 17.68 to 17.50 on depth. For T2I-Adapter, the gain is most dramatic on pose control with a 65.25% mAUCC improvement (21.45 vs. 12.98) and 27.97% mAP gain. On non-spatially-aligned controls (GLIGEN, Table 2), x0-supervision boosts mAUCC by 121.98% for box+text grounding (18.38 vs. 8.28) and 31.22% for box+text+image (8.07 vs. 6.15), while also improving mAPbox by 8.47%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ControlNet (ε-supervision baseline), x0-supervision wins on every metric across all four spatially-aligned tasks: FID drops by 0.18 on depth, mIoU rises from 35.84 to 39.54 on segmentation, and mAPpose increases from 58.00 to 59.18 on pose, with mAUCC gains of 25% (seg) and 17.65% (pose). Against T2I-Adapter (ε-supervision), the advantage is even larger—mAUCC on pose jumps from 12.98 to 21.45 (+65.25%) and mAPpose from 29.50 to 37.75 (+27.97%). Against OminiControl (u-supervision, flow matching), x0-supervision matches or slightly trails on depth and Canny edge mAUCC (13.74 vs. 13.47; 14.41 vs. 14.40) but clearly wins on pose mAUCC (42.07 vs. 34.54, +21.80%) and semantic mIoU (45.60 vs. 42.09, +8.34%). Against GLIGEN (ε-supervision, non-spatially-aligned), x0-supervision achieves the largest relative gains: mAUCC for box+text nearly doubles (18.38 vs. 8.28, +121.98%) and mAPbox improves from 30.70 to 33.30 (+8.47%), while v-supervision collapses on both tasks (mAPbox drops to 8.39 and 2.18). Overall, x0-supervision never loses to the original supervision on any task, and the v-supervision variant consistently underperforms both ε and x0 across all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the well-known equivalence between ε-prediction and x₀-prediction parameterizations (Salimans & Ho, 2022) and the network preconditioning framework of Karras et al. (2022), but applies them in a context where they had been overlooked: controllable generation adapter training. Prior controllable generation work (ControlNet, T2I-Adapter, GLIGEN, OminiControl) straightforwardly continues training with the base model's original loss (ε or u-supervision), treating the adapter as a plug-in that inherits the training objective. The paper's key departure is recognizing that the implicit SNR weighting in ε-supervision suppresses learning precisely at the early denoising steps where spatial layout is determined, which is the critical phase for controllable generation. The approach also connects to the consistency training literature, which uses similar preconditioning to convert between parameterizations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone training controllable generation adapters: up to 2× faster convergence and halved memory requirements for non-spatially-aligned controls, with consistent (if sometimes modest) gains across diffusion and flow-matching paradigms and UNet/DiT architectures. The mAUCC metric provides a useful, general-purpose tool for comparing convergence speed across training runs. Limitations include the fact that gains are smaller for already-fast-converging tasks (e.g., OminiControl on depth/segmentation with the strong FLUX.1 backbone), and the paper does not explore whether the same principle applies to video, 3D, or audio generation. An open question is whether further re-weighting schemes (beyond uniform x₀-supervision) could yield additional gains, or whether the benefit diminishes as base models become stronger.
