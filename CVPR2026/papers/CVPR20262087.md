## Problem

During diffusion model inference, all samples are generated independently: patches within a single image interact via attention, but patches across different images in a batch are processed in isolation. This paper asks whether a group of samples sharing the same conditioning can be jointly denoised, unlocking cross-sample attention as a previously unexplored signal for improving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262087/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a class label (or text prompt) as conditioning and produces a group of N jointly denoised 256×256 RGB images, encoded as 32×32×4 latents via a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE. During training, each group is constructed by retrieving N−1 semantically similar images from the dataset using cosine similarity on CLIP or DINOv2 embeddings (threshold 0.7); at inference, N images are generated together in a single batch under the same condition. The group size N is a tunable hyperparameter (tested from 1 to 16), and a learnable sample embedding is added to all patches of each image so the model can distinguish individual samples within the group.

## Method

GroupDiff modifies the standard DiT/SiT attention by reshaping hidden states from R^{N×L×C} to R^{1×(NL)×C} before the attention operation, so that every patch attends to patches from all N images in the group, then reshapes back afterward. Two variants are introduced: GroupDiff-f applies group attention to both conditional and unconditional score predictions, while GroupDiff-l (the default) applies it only to the unconditional branch, keeping 90% of training identical to standard diffusion for computational efficiency. A key design choice is allowing per-sample noise-level variation (σ_tv) within each group, which encourages noisier samples to attend to cleaner ones and strengthens cross-sample interaction. The paper shows that cross-sample attention is concentrated in early denoising steps and shallow layers, and defines a neighbor-focused attention score S_cross = (P_cross-max − P_cross-mean)/P_cross-max that correlates at r=0.94 with FID, revealing that generation quality is governed by how strongly each image attends to its most similar counterpart in the group.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, GroupDiff-4* (resumed from pre-trained SiT-XL/2 for 100 additional epochs) achieves an FID of 1.40, a 32.2% improvement over the SiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 2.06). With DiT-XL/2, GroupDiff-4* reaches FID 1.55 (from 2.27, a 32.2% reduction). When combined with REPA semantic feature distillation, GroupDiff-4* further improves to FID 1.14 (from 1.42, a 20.9% gain). Training from scratch, GroupDiff-4 matches or exceeds baselines using only 57% of the original training iterations (e.g., FID 1.63 vs. SiT-XL/2's 2.06 at 800 epochs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupDiff-4* with SiT-XL/2 (FID 1.40) outperforms all non-distillation baselines including MDTv2-XL/2 (1.58), SRA (1.58), VAR-d30 (1.92), Dispersive Loss (1.97), SiT-XL/2 (2.06), and DiT-XL/2 (2.27). With DiT-XL/2, GroupDiff-4* (FID 1.55) beats DiT-XL/2 (2.27) by 32% and also surpasses MDTv2-XL/2 (1.58) and MaskDiT (2.28). When combined with REPA distillation, GroupDiff-4* achieves FID 1.14, outperforming SiT-XL/2+REPA (1.42) by 20.9% and matching or exceeding DDT-XL and SiT-XL/2+REPA-E (both 1.26). Training from scratch, GroupDiff-4 at 800 epochs already achieves FID 1.63 (SiT) and 1.66 (DiT), beating the full 1400-epoch baselines (2.06 and 2.27) with only 57% of the training compute. The method does not outperform the strongest distillation-based methods (DDT-XL, REPA-E at FID 1.26) in the non-distillation setting, but it is the only approach that improves upon them when stacked on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

GroupDiff builds directly on the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) and SiT architectures, modifying their self-attention mechanism to operate across a group of images rather than within a single image. Conceptually, it departs from the recent line of work (REPA, REPA-E, SRA, Dispersive Loss) that improves diffusion models by explicitly aligning their internal representations with pretrained vision encoders; instead, GroupDiff argues that cross-sample attention implicitly learns stronger representations through inter-image correspondence without any external alignment loss. The paper also positions itself against prior multi-image generation work (multi-view, style-controlled group generation) by showing that the cross-sample interaction need not be learned from individual samples but can be directly exploited during joint denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32.2% FID improvement over SiT-XL/2 with only 100 additional training epochs, achieved through a minimal architectural change (reshaping tokens for group attention), suggests a broadly applicable and low-cost technique for improving diffusion-based generation. The strong correlation (r=0.95) between the cross-sample attention score and FID provides a useful diagnostic for understanding and tuning group-based generation. However, evaluation is limited to ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation; open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image, video, or higher-resolution settings, and whether the scaling benefit of larger groups persists beyond the tested range of 16. The finding that group attention is only needed in early denoising steps (t < 0.4) offers a practical efficiency gain but has not been validated across architectures or datasets.
