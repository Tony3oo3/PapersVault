## Problem

Existing model immunization methods either simulate only a short attacker horizon (K inner-loop steps) or modify local geometry near the initialization, but both are eventually overcome when a real attacker trains for thousands to tens of thousands of additional steps. No prior method provides a mechanism to constrain the attacker's cumulative progress over the full long-horizon fine-tuning trajectory, leaving a gap between the simulated defense window and the actual attack duration.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained foundation model's weights, a primary task dataset (e.g., ImageNet for classification, benign sub-concept images for diffusion, or benign Q&A pairs for LLMs), a harmful task dataset representing the target of immunization, and the attacker's optimizer configuration (learning rate, step rule). It outputs immunized model weights that retain primary-task performance and benign fine-tunability while resisting harmful adaptation. The formulation is Hessian-free: contraction factors are estimated via directional spectral-norm probes (power-iteration-style finite differences) around the simulated attacker state, avoiding explicit second-order tensor storage and making the method tractable for multi-layer deep networks and large LLMs.

## Method

CLAMP formulates immunization as a bi-level optimization where the inner loop simulates the attacker's fine-tuning for K steps and the outer loop updates base weights to minimize a composite immunization loss. The central mechanism is enforcing contractivity of the attacker's update map: by pushing the spectral norm of the update Jacobian below 1 (via a Hessian-free directional probe and a softplus penalty), successive attacker weight updates shrink geometrically, yielding a closed-form bound on total future parameter movement (a geometric series) and, via the descent lemma, a closed-form upper bound on all remaining harmful loss reduction to infinity. The long-horizon loss minimizes the sum of the observed K-step loss drop and this tail bound. A complementary directional curvature penalty (second-order finite difference along the attacker's step direction) creates adversarial ridges that make the limited remaining updates less productive, and an inverse harmful loss term prevents the optimizer from inadvertently lowering the initial harmful loss. All four loss components are backpropagated through the simulated inner loop to update the base model weights.

## Results

Across classification (ResNet18), generative (Stable Diffusion V1-4), and autoregressive (LLaMA 3.2 1B, Gemma 3 1B, Mistral 7B) settings, CLAMP consistently achieves the highest Similarity Gap Ratio (SGR) on harmful tasks while preserving benign fine-tunability. On the LLM setting (Table 3), CLAMP records SGR_C of 26.4, 6.57, and 31.75 on LLaMA 3.2 1B, Gemma 3 1B, and Mistral 7B respectively, outperforming both IMMA and Booster on all three models. On the diffusion model (Table 2), CLAMP achieves an average SGR_G of 26.70 on harmful concepts versus 4.55 for IMMA and −2.87 for CN. Crucially, CLAMP's harmful SGR_C remains stable across training epochs (e.g., 29.38→24.46 on Cars) while CN degrades sharply (7.91→1.58), demonstrating long-horizon robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAMP outperforms all three baselines across every experimental setting. Against CN [43] (condition-number-based immunization), CLAMP achieves a 23-point average SGR_C improvement on the Cars classification task (26.37 vs. 3.35) and a 29.6-point lead on the diffusion model (26.70 vs. −2.87); CN's protection also collapses over longer horizons (SGR_C drops from 7.91 to 1.58 on Cars across epochs), while CLAMP remains stable. Against IMMA [41], CLAMP leads by 27.2 points on Cars (26.37 vs. −0.84), 22.2 points on diffusion (26.70 vs. 4.55), and by 15.8, 3.2, and 27.6 points on LLaMA, Gemma, and Mistral respectively; IMMA also degrades benign fine-tuning more severely (e.g., 13.94 avg benign SGR_G vs. CLAMP's 7.66 on diffusion). Against Booster [20] (compared only on LLMs), CLAMP wins on all three models: 26.4 vs. 25.4 (LLaMA), 6.57 vs. 4.86 (Gemma), and 31.75 vs. 29.52 (Mistral), while also better preserving ARC-C and MMLU utility (e.g., 51.9 vs. 49.9 on ARC-C for LLaMA).

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAMP builds directly on the bi-level meta-learning immunization framework of Zheng et al. [43] (condition-number perspective) and the SGR evaluation protocol of IMMA [41], but departs from both in a key conceptual way: rather than shaping the initial loss landscape (inflating curvature/condition number) or penalizing observed loss drops over a short simulated inner loop, CLAMP targets the attacker's optimization dynamics themselves by enforcing contractivity of the update map. This shifts the defense from 'make the first K steps unhelpful' to 'bound all future progress to infinity via a geometric series on update magnitudes.' The Hessian-free spectral-norm probe for estimating the contraction factor and the directional curvature trap are new practical components that avoid the memory cost of explicit second-order quantities, extending the meta-learning immunization recipe to settings where full Hessians are infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and practically important gap: existing immunization defenses (IMMA, SOPHON, condition-number methods) are shown to degrade as the attacker simply trains longer, which is the realistic threat model for open-source foundation models. The closed-form long-horizon bound provides theoretical grounding that prior empirical defenses lack, and the Hessian-free formulation makes the approach tractable for multi-layer deep models. Open questions include whether the local contractivity assumption holds under adaptive optimizers (Adam, AdamW) with momentum, how the method scales to 70B+ parameter models, and whether the bound remains tight when the attacker uses non-gradient-descent update rules. The evaluation on 1B-parameter LLMs and ResNet-18 leaves the scalability story partially open.
