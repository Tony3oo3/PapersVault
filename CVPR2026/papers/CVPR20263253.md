## Problem

Multi-view roadside perception systems that fuse camera and LiDAR data in a shared BEV space are critically brittle to two real-world issues: (1) even small calibration or depth-estimation errors cause catastrophic feature misalignment under deterministic Lift-Splat-Shoot projections, and (2) naive multimodal fusion lets dense camera features dominate sparse but geometrically precise LiDAR cues, yielding redundant rather than complementary representations. No prior framework simultaneously models spatial uncertainty in the lifting step and enforces cross-modal complementarity during fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (512×512 on RCooper, 864×1536 on DAIR-V2X-I), a LiDAR point cloud, and known camera extrinsics (pitch angle, focal length, principal point). It produces a fused BEV feature map (200×200 grid covering 100 m × 100 m on RCooper; 128×128 covering 102.4 m × 102.4 m on DAIR-V2X-I) from which a detection head regresses 3D bounding boxes. Notably, the image-to-BEV lifting uses per-pixel 3D Gaussians rendered via alpha-blending rather than the standard BEV-pooling, and the LiDAR branch uses PointPillars with 0.5 m × 0.5 m × 1 m voxels.

## Method

GSV2X replaces the deterministic per-pixel projection of LSS with a probabilistic 3D Gaussian representation: each pixel's lifted feature is a N(μ, Σ) whose mean comes from a predicted depth distribution over D bins and whose covariance is scaled by a learnable, view-dependent perturbation factor λ (predicted by a lightweight MLP from camera extrinsics) to absorb calibration noise. A Geometry-guided Depth Estimation (GDE) module stabilizes the depth prediction by regressing ground-plane distance d and object height H instead of raw depth z, then recovering z through a closed-form geometric relation involving pitch and focal length. For fusion, an Orthogonal Fusion Module (OFM) treats the LiDAR BEV as a geometric backbone and the camera BEV as a residual enhancement modulated by an attention-derived spatial weight map A, while an auxiliary orthogonality loss L_orth = E_p[(F_LiDAR(p)ᵀ F_img(p))²] penalizes feature correlation between modalities, encouraging each sensor to contribute complementary information. The full pipeline uses ResNet-50 + FPN for the image branch, PointPillars for LiDAR, and is trained end-to-end with AdamW under a cosine schedule (50 epochs on RCooper, 100 on DAIR-V2X-I).

## Results

On the RCooper intersection scene, GSV2X achieves AP@0.3/0.5/0.7 of 74.7/63.4/40.3, surpassing the strongest multimodal baseline BEVFusion (58.6/43.7/24.1) by +16.1/+19.7/+16.2 points. On the corridor scene, GSV2X reaches 77.6/65.3/41.2, improving over BEVFusion (73.1/60.8/35.5) by +4.5/+4.5/+5.7. On DAIR-V2X-I, GSV2X attains 83.8/70.2/70.2 AP₃D|R40 for vehicles (Easy/Middle/Hard), outperforming BEVFusion by up to +1.7, and leads on pedestrians (58.3/56.2/56.3) and cyclists (65.5/63.6/64.0) by +6.8–7.1 and +4.3–5.5 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSV2X decisively outperforms BEVFusion [28], the strongest prior multimodal method, by +16.1 AP@0.3 and +19.7 AP@0.5 on the RCooper intersection scene, where calibration noise is most severe; the gap narrows to +4.5 AP@0.3 on the corridor scene with cleaner geometry. Against the best LiDAR-only baseline (Late Fusion, 65.1 AP@0.3) and best camera-only baseline (BEVHeight++, 35.0 AP@0.3), GSV2X gains +9.6 and +39.7 AP@0.3 respectively on intersections, confirming that the fusion of both modalities is essential. On DAIR-V2X-I (high-quality calibration), GSV2X still leads BEVFusion by +1.7 AP₃D|R40 for vehicles and by +6.8 to +7.1 for pedestrians, indicating the orthogonal fusion module contributes beyond mere calibration robustness. The calibration-perturbation ablation (Table 4) is particularly telling: under perturbed calibration, BEVFusion drops 16.4 AP@0.5 while GSV2X^GS degrades by only 3.2, directly attributing the robustness gain to the Gaussian uncertainty representation. Among LiDAR-only cooperative baselines (AttFuse, F-Cooper, Where2Comm, CoBEVT), GSV2X surpasses all by at least +14 AP@0.3 on intersections, and the gap widens to +21 AP@0.5 over the weakest (F-Cooper, 32.0). On DAIR-V2X-I, GSV2X also beats LiDAR-only methods (PointPillars, SECOND) and camera-only methods (BEVHeight, BEVSpread, BEVHeight++) across all three object classes and difficulty levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSV2X extends the Lift-Splat-Shoot paradigm by replacing the deterministic point-wise projection with a probabilistic 3D Gaussian representation, building directly on GaussianLSS [29] for uncertainty-aware depth lifting and PointPillars [20] for the LiDAR branch. Its fusion strategy departs from the dominant attention-based or concatenation-based approaches (BEVFusion, FUTR3D, SparseFusion3D) by introducing an Orthogonal Fusion Module that explicitly regularizes camera and LiDAR features to occupy complementary directions in feature space. The Geometry-guided Depth Estimation module further differentiates the work from prior LSS variants (BEVHeight, BEVSpread) by disentangling depth into ground-plane distance and object height, making the lifting step more robust to varying camera extrinsics in roadside deployments.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: real-world roadside systems inevitably suffer from calibration drift, and the controlled perturbation experiment (16.4 vs. 3.2 AP@0.5 degradation) provides strong evidence that explicit uncertainty modeling is a viable remedy. However, the approach still assumes LiDAR availability and reasonably good initial calibration, and the orthogonal fusion adds computational overhead that may limit edge deployment. Open questions include whether the Gaussian representation can handle more extreme calibration failures (e.g., complete sensor misalignment), whether the orthogonal regularizer generalizes to more than two modalities, and whether the geometry-guided depth strategy transfers to on-vehicle settings with different camera geometries.
