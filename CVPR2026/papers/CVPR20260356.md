## Problem

Existing image-to-point cloud registration methods face a fundamental trade-off: detect-then-match approaches yield high-quality but sparse correspondences vulnerable to keypoint detection errors, while detection-free coarse-to-fine methods produce dense matches but are degraded by non-overlapping regions and low-quality matches in textureless or repetitive areas. Additionally, even when suitable matching regions are identified, the inherent representation gap between texture-driven image features and structure-driven point cloud features causes inconsistent region encodings that increase the likelihood of incorrect cross-modal matches.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image I ∈ R^{H×W×3} and a point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} from the same scene as input, and produces a rigid transformation [R, t] (R ∈ SO(3), t ∈ R³) along with pixel-to-point correspondences. The pipeline first generates a coarse patch-level matching set M_c via cosine similarity, then refines it into a dense pixel-to-point matching set M_f using high-resolution features, before applying PnP+RANSAC for final pose estimation. The HZRS module additionally outputs binary selection masks identifying high-value regions in both modalities, which are used to discard low-quality feature patches before dense matching.

## Method

R²³Net combines a ResNet+FPN 2D backbone with a KPFCNN 3D backbone, followed by self- and cross-attention transformers for cross-modal feature alignment. The High-Value Zone Reinforced Selection Module (HZRS) addresses the non-differentiable region selection problem via a REINFORCE-based policy network whose reward combines inverse circle loss (matching discriminability) and bidirectional projection correctness terms, dynamically selecting high-value regions for dense matching. The Zone Representation Consistency Alignment Module (ZRCA) enforces cross-modal consistency through three units: (A) Zone Consistency Mask Reconstruction aligns selected region boundaries using Dice+KL divergence between the image mask and top-k similar image regions; (B) Similarity Distribution Consistency Validation constrains the Frobenius norm difference between intra-patch self-similarity matrices of matched image and point cloud features; (C) Cross-Modal Feature Cluster Alignment uses learnable cluster prototypes with Gumbel-Softmax stochastic gates, Sinkhorn-normalized soft correspondences, and NCE contrastive loss to accelerate cluster-level alignment before point-wise convergence. The total loss combines circle loss for matching, the RL selection loss, mask reconstruction loss, similarity consistency loss, and cluster alignment loss with learned warm-up schedules.

## Results

On RGB-D Scenes v2, R²³Net achieves a mean Inlier Ratio of 43.4%, Feature Matching Recall of 93.6%, and Registration Recall of 77.0%, outperforming the previous best (Flow-I2P at 40.1% IR and 68.4% RR) by 3.3 pp and 8.6 pp respectively. On 7-Scenes, it reaches 54.9% IR, 93.2% FMR, and 83.8% RR, surpassing Diff²I2P (53.2% IR, 83.0% RR) and CA-I2P (51.6% IR, 79.5% RR). For pose estimation, R²³Net attains the lowest mean RRE (2.087° on RGB-D Scenes v2, 2.738° on 7-Scenes) and RTE (0.055 m, 0.068 m), and on the outdoor KITTI dataset it achieves 0.17±0.20 m RTE and 1.15±2.18° RRE, beating ICL-I2P (0.20 m, 1.24°) and GraphI2P (0.68 m, 1.91°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²³Net outperforms all 17 baselines on the primary Registration Recall metric across both indoor datasets: +8.6 pp over Flow-I2P (68.4→77.0) on RGB-D Scenes v2 and +0.8 pp over Diff²I2P (83.0→83.8) on 7-Scenes. On Inlier Ratio, it leads by 3.3 pp over Flow-I2P (40.1→43.4) on RGB-D Scenes v2 and by 1.7 pp over Diff²I2P (53.2→54.9) on 7-Scenes. For Feature Matching Recall, R²³Net (93.6/93.2) is competitive with B2-3Dnet (94.4/93.1) and CA-I2P (93.6/92.4), trailing B2-3Dnet by 0.8 pp on RGB-D Scenes v2 but matching or exceeding all others on 7-Scenes. On pose estimation (Table 2), R²³Net achieves the lowest mean RRE and RTE on both datasets, beating CA-I2P by 0.47° RRE and 0.006 m RTE on RGB-D Scenes v2, and by 0.46° RRE and 0.008 m RTE on 7-Scenes. On the outdoor KITTI benchmark (Table 3), R²³Net (0.17 m RTE, 1.15° RRE) surpasses ICL-I2P (0.20 m, 1.24°) and GraphI2P (0.68 m, 1.91°), confirming strong generalization beyond indoor scenes. The detect-then-match baselines (FCGF-2D3D, P2-Net, Predator-2D3D) are substantially outperformed, with R²³Net achieving 2–4× higher Registration Recall, validating the benefit of the detection-free coarse-to-fine paradigm augmented by HZRS and ZRCA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 2D3D-MATR coarse-to-fine detection-free pipeline (patch-level matching → pixel-to-point dense matching → PnP-RANSAC) and augments it with two new modules. Conceptually, it bridges the two dominant paradigms in image-to-point cloud registration—detect-then-match (high-quality sparse correspondences) and detection-free dense matching—by using reinforcement learning to select high-value regions (HZRS) and then applying dense matching only within those regions. The ZRCA module introduces a three-stage cross-modal alignment strategy (mask reconstruction, similarity structure consistency, and Gumbel-Softmax feature clustering) that departs from prior channel-adaptive or diffusion-based alignment approaches (CA-I2P, Diff²I2P) by operating at the zone and cluster level rather than the feature-channel or generative level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and fundamental challenge in cross-modal registration: the representation gap between texture-driven image features and geometry-driven point cloud features. The RL-based non-differentiable region selection is a practical and elegant solution to a problem that prior methods handled heuristically or ignored. However, the method adds considerable architectural complexity (RL policy network, three alignment sub-units, cluster gating) for moderate gains (~8 pp in Registration Recall), and the added inference time and memory overhead (Table 5) may limit deployment in real-time SLAM or localization pipelines. Open questions include whether the RL reward design generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes beyond KITTI, whether the cluster alignment unit can be replaced by simpler domain-adaptation losses, and whether the approach extends to multi-view or video-based registration.
