## Problem

Existing visual grounding methods for autonomous driving lack 3D spatial awareness and forward-looking reasoning, causing them to fail on ambiguous, context-dependent commands in real-world scenes with motion blur, multi-agent traffic, and occlusions. While world models have been applied to end-to-end driving and scene simulation, their potential for grounding—reasoning about how a scene will evolve before localizing a target—remains entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261704/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a frontal-view RGB image (1600×900) and a natural language command (averaging 14.72 words), augmented at inference by a monocular depth map from ZoeDepth and top-N object vectors from CenterNet. It outputs a grounding probability distribution over visual nodes, from which the target bounding box is extracted; evaluation uses IoU_0.5 per the C4AV protocol. The DrivePilot dataset additionally provides paired BEV images (1200×800) and 14-dimensional LLM-generated scene annotations for training and analysis.

## Method

ThinkDeeper comprises three stages: (1) multimodal backbones (ViT + BERT + ZoeDepth + CenterNet) encode the image, command, depth, and object detections; (2) a Spatial-Aware World Model (SA-WM) first distills the scene into a command-aware latent state z₀ via cross-modal attention and depth-biased saliency scoring, then iteratively rolls out N future latent states {z₁,…,z_N} through a gated residual MLP conditioned on the text, providing forward-looking cues without generating pixels; (3) a cross-modal hypergraph decoder builds a bipartite hypergraph over the N visual latent nodes and L text tokens, forms hyperedges by top-k affinity, and performs multi-layer hypergraph convolution to fuse higher-order visual-textual relations before a multi-layer dynamic attention produces the final grounding distribution. The depth-derived prior in the saliency computation (Eq. 4) biases attention toward physically plausible near-field regions, and the hypergraph's multi-node edges capture relations beyond pairwise graph updates. Training is supervised with AdamW (lr 1e-4, batch 32) over two stages (25+45 epochs) on 4× A100 GPUs, achieving 39 ms inference with 135.81 M parameters.

## Results

On the primary autonomous driving grounding benchmarks, ThinkDeeper achieves 76.64 IoU₀.₅ on Talk2Car, 75.20 on MoCAD, and 79.52 on DrivePilot, setting new state-of-the-art across all three. On the challenging corner-case subsets, it scores 73.53 (visual constraints), 74.19 (multi-agent), and 75.26 (ambiguous commands), and reaches 74.08 on the long-text set. On the standard RefCOCO/+/g benchmarks, ThinkDeeper attains 85.74/87.78/80.64 (val/testA/testB), 77.72/82.10/66.71, and 72.73/80.90/77.72 respectively, surpassing prior SOTA by at least 2.9–3.5 points per split. The model also runs at 39 ms per sample with 135.81 M parameters, outperforming CAVG (69 ms, 172.78 M) in both speed and accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThinkDeeper outperforms all 22 baselines across every test set. Against the strongest dedicated VG baselines, it leads CAVG [30] by +2.02 on Talk2Car (76.64 vs 74.62), +2.76 on MoCAD (75.20 vs 72.44), and +2.51 on DrivePilot (75.76 vs 73.25), and surpasses UNINEXT [50] by +5.77 on Talk2Car and +4.42 on DrivePilot. On the corner-case subsets the gap widens substantially: ThinkDeeper beats UNINEXT by +7.2 on visual constraints and +4.9 on multi-agent scenes, and exceeds VLTVG [52] by +5.28 on the long-text set (74.08 vs 68.80). General-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, LLaVA-NeXT, MiniGPT-v2) trail the best dedicated baselines by 15–25 IoU₀.₅ points, confirming that their lack of 3D spatial inductive biases makes them unsuitable for precise grounding. On RefCOCO/+/g, ThinkDeeper edges out TransCP [45] by +1.49 on RefCOCO val (85.74 vs 84.25), VILLA [16] by +1.67 on RefCOCO+ val (77.72 vs 76.05), and VILLA by +5.0 on RefCOCO-g testA (80.90 vs 75.90). In the efficiency comparison (Table 4), ThinkDeeper achieves 78.93 IoU₀.₅ at 39 ms with 135.81 M parameters, beating CAVG (74.50, 69 ms, 172.78 M) and RSD-LayerAttn [3] (74.12, 54 ms, 112.28 M) on both accuracy and speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the world model paradigm—previously applied in AD for end-to-end driving (Drive-WM, LAW), scenario simulation (DriveDreamer-2, Vista), and 3D/4D representation learning (DriveDreamer4D, FSDrive)—for the first time as an intermediate reasoning mechanism in visual grounding. Rather than building on a single prior VG architecture, ThinkDeeper synthesizes cross-modal attention (in the VLTVG/CAVG lineage), monocular depth priors (ZoeDepth), and a hypergraph decoder into a unified pipeline where the SA-WM's latent-state rollout serves as a forward-looking disambiguation step before grounding. The DrivePilot dataset extends the annotation pipeline of prior AD grounding benchmarks (Talk2Car, MoCAD) by introducing RAG-augmented, CoT-guided LLM annotation with manual expert validation, a departure from purely human-annotated or rule-based labeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 135M-parameter, 39ms-inference model can outperform 7B–72B VLMs by 20+ IoU points on grounding, suggesting that task-specific spatial reasoning (depth priors, latent rollout) can substitute for brute-force language-model scale in safety-critical, latency-constrained AD deployment. The DrivePilot dataset, with its 14 semantic dimensions and multi-source (front-view + BEV) format, provides a more realistic benchmark than prior single-view grounding datasets. Open questions include whether the latent-state rollout genuinely encodes meaningful temporal dynamics or functions primarily as a learned nonlinear transformation, how the approach scales to multi-camera or 4D occupancy settings, and whether the depth prior's contribution (6.3% ablation drop) holds in depth-estimation-degraded conditions like heavy rain or night driving.
