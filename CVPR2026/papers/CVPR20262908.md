## Problem

Text-supervised video pretraining captures strong semantics but relies on noisy synthetic captions that miss implicit world knowledge (3D geometry, causal relations, fine-grained motion), while self-supervised masked video modeling captures spatiotemporal structure yet lags on semantic benchmarks. The authors identify this gap as stemming from architectural issues: linear pixel decoders force the predictor's latent to be linearly separable to pixels (conflicting with semantic abstraction), and latent-prediction methods encourage shortcut learning rather than genuine world modeling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips of 8–32 frames at 224×224 spatial resolution, with 80% masking in Stage 1 and multi-block spatiotemporal masking in Stage 2. The predictor is the last 5 layers of a ModernBert-Large transformer initialized from a pretrained text decoder, and the semantic teacher is a frozen SigLIP2-1B encoder. The final deliverable is a frozen ViT encoder whose representations are evaluated via lightweight probing heads (single-layer attention pooling, cross-attention with learnable queries, or a VDA depth head) across recognition, depth, tracking, and retrieval tasks.

## Method

InternVideo-Next disentangles the conventional encoder-decoder MVM into an Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) framework where the predictor acts as a latent world model sharing a semantically coherent space with the encoder. Stage 1 jointly optimizes pixel reconstruction through a lightweight conditional diffusion decoder (6-layer MLP, width 1536, 1000-step cosine noise schedule) and a cosine semantic alignment loss against a frozen SigLIP2 teacher, with the predictor initialized from a pretrained text decoder to bridge high-level semantics. Stage 2 performs latent-space prediction with a frozen teacher (initialized from Stage 1) under multi-block masking, forcing the student to learn genuine spatiotemporal dynamics and causal relations within the already detail-preserving latent space rather than exploiting shortcut correlations. The diffusion decoder is critical: replacing it with a linear head causes the pixel and semantic objectives to conflict, while the diffusion formulation allows the predictor's latent to remain semantically rich while still supporting pixel-level reconstruction.

## Results

On the primary video action recognition benchmarks (attentive probing, frozen encoder), InternVideo-Next-s2 (Large) achieves 88.4% top-1 on Kinetics-400, 73.0% on SSv2, and 93.6% on COIN, using only 1.1M public unlabeled videos and 9.7K A100 GPU-hours. This surpasses the previous video-only SOTA V-JEPA2-Large (83.3/72.0/85.9) by +5.1/+1.0/+7.7 points respectively, and outperforms the video-text pretrained InternVideo2s2-1B (87.9/67.3/91.7) on SSv2 and COIN while using ~3x less compute. On depth estimation (ScanNet δ1), InternVideo-Next-L reaches 92.2% with a simple probing head, nearly matching the purpose-built Video Depth Anything (92.6%), and achieves 72.4 mean IoU on Waymo object tracking, the best among all compared video models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest video-only baseline V-JEPA2-Large, InternVideo-Next-s2-Large wins on all three action recognition benchmarks (K400 88.4 vs 83.3, SSv2 73.0 vs 72.0, COIN 93.6 vs 85.9) while using comparable compute (9.7K vs 10K GPU-hrs). Against the video-text pretrained InternVideo2s2-1B (30K GPU-hrs), InternVideo-Next-s2-Large surpasses it on SSv2 (73.0 vs 67.3) and COIN (93.6 vs 91.7) with roughly 3× less compute, though it trails slightly on K400 (88.4 vs 87.9). The 6B InternVideo2s2 (200K GPU-hrs) remains marginally ahead on K400 (88.8 vs 88.4) but is outperformed on SSv2 (67.7 vs 73.0) and COIN (92.6 vs 93.6). On depth estimation, InternVideo-Next-L (ScanNet δ1 92.2, KITTI δ1 94.6) nearly matches the purpose-trained Video Depth Anything (92.6/94.6) using only a frozen backbone and probing head. On zero-shot retrieval, InternVideo-Next-L leads V-JEPA2-L by +8.8 R@1 on MSRVTT (43.2 vs 34.4) and +5.6 on MSVD (46.1 vs 40.1), and edges out InternVideo2s2-L on MSRVTT and MSVD. On chat-centric benchmarks, InternVideo-Next-L achieves 50.6 on MVBench and 49.2 on Perception Test, outperforming InternVideo2-L (47.0/46.7) and V-JEPA2-L (44.3/44.2) by 3.6 and 5.0 points respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets the standard Encoder-Decoder paradigm of masked video modeling (MAE, VideoMAE) by explicitly decoupling it into an Encoder-Predictor-Decoder (EPD) structure, arguing that the conventional linear decoder forces the predictor's latent to be linearly separable in pixel space, which conflicts with semantic abstraction. Stage 1 builds on VideoMAE-style pixel reconstruction but replaces the linear head with a conditional diffusion decoder and injects image-level semantic priors from a frozen SigLIP encoder; Stage 2 adapts V-JEPA's latent prediction by freezing the teacher at Stage 1 weights to prevent shortcut learning. The work also positions itself against the InternVideo series, which fused video and text priors at the weight or embedding level, by instead resolving the detail-vs-semantics tension within a unified video-only reconstruction framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InternVideo-Next demonstrates that a model trained solely on 1.1M public, unlabeled videos can match or exceed video-text pretrained models on both semantic (K400) and motion-centric (SSv2) benchmarks, suggesting that expensive video-text annotation pipelines may be less necessary than previously assumed for strong general video representations. The EPD reframing provides a concrete architectural diagnosis for why pixel MVM and latent prediction each underperform, and the two-stage recipe is a practical, reproducible path toward scalable video pretraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger datasets and model sizes (the paper acknowledges this as future work), whether the reliance on a frozen image-text teacher (SigLIP) limits the ceiling of learned semantics, and how the framework extends to interactive or multi-modal world modeling.
