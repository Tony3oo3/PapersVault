## Problem

Reconstruction-trained VAEs (e.g., SD-VAE) produce latents that preserve local geometric structure but lack semantic discriminability, while semantic-encoder-based VAEs (using DINO, SigLIP, etc.) yield semantically rich latents that lose fine-grained spatial detail, making accurate reconstruction difficult. This fundamental tradeoff between semantic abstraction and geometric fidelity has been underexplored, particularly regarding how it affects downstream diffusion model training, and no prior method achieves strong performance on both reconstruction quality and classification accuracy simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 RGB images as input. The DINOv3-ViT-H/16+ encoder produces patch embeddings on a 16×16 spatial grid, which are optionally further downsampled via convolutional layers to support compression ratios of 16×, 32×, or 64×. The output is a diagonal Gaussian latent (channel dimension C, spatial size H/f × W/f) that serves dual purposes: it is decoded back to a 256×256 reconstructed image, and it is consumed by a downstream Diffusion Transformer for generative modeling. The latent retains both semantic structure (measured by linear probing accuracy on ImageNet) and geometric detail (measured by PSNR, SSIM, rFID).

## Method

S-AE uses a pretrained DINOv3-ViT-H/16+ as the encoder backbone, followed by optional convolutional downsampling layers, a VAE bottleneck (diagonal Gaussian), and a progressive upsampling decoder. Training follows a two-stage progressive strategy: Stage 1 freezes the encoder and trains only the decoder, downsampling layers, and bottleneck with L1 + LPIPS + PatchGAN losses to establish a reconstruction baseline; Stage 2 performs end-to-end fine-tuning of the encoder while introducing a semantic regularization loss (MSE between the trainable student encoder's patch features and a frozen teacher DINOv3 copy) to prevent catastrophic degradation of discriminative capability. Without this regularization, end-to-end training drops ImageNet linear probing accuracy from 88.4% to 12.4%; with λ_reg=200, the model retains 86.1% accuracy while achieving PSNR=32.21, SSIM=0.9458, and rFID=0.10. The key insight is that the semantic regularization acts as a distillation constraint that allows the encoder to adapt its features for reconstruction without collapsing the semantic structure, yielding a unified latent space that simultaneously supports high-fidelity image synthesis and strong visual understanding.

## Results

On ImageNet, S-AE achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality with PSNR of 33.84, SSIM of 0.96, and rFID of 0.06, substantially outperforming all baselines on reconstruction metrics. Simultaneously, S-AE retains strong semantic discriminability with 81.9% linear probing accuracy, approaching the best semantic encoder (R-AE at 84.5%) while achieving 1.7× higher PSNR and 8× lower rFID. The frozen-encoder baseline (Freeze) retains 88.4% accuracy but suffers severe reconstruction artifacts (PSNR=19.39, rFID=1.17), while naive end-to-end training (E2E) achieves near-perfect reconstruction (PSNR=41.35) but collapses accuracy to 12.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-AE dominates all reconstruction metrics: it achieves PSNR 33.84 vs. 27.96 (VA-VAE), 25.83 (Align.VF), 25.29 (VAE), and 19.21 (R-AE), and rFID 0.06 vs. 0.28 (VA-VAE), 0.26 (Align.VF), 0.62 (VAE), and 0.49 (R-AE). On semantic accuracy, S-AE (81.9%) is competitive with R-AE (84.5%) and SVG (81.8%), but vastly outperforms VAE (8.0%), DC-AE (12.7%), and Align.VF (35.1%). The key tradeoff is that R-AE achieves 2.6% higher accuracy but at the cost of 14.6 dB lower PSNR and 8× higher rFID, making it far inferior for reconstruction. Among non-semantic baselines (VAE, DC-AE, VQ-GAN, MaskGit, LFQ-Tokenizer), S-AE improves rFID by 10–38× while also providing strong semantic structure. VA-VAE is the closest competitor on reconstruction (rFID 0.28) but still trails S-AE by 4.7× in rFID and 5.9 dB in PSNR, and does not report accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of semantic-encoder-based autoencoders initiated by R-AE (DINOv2) and SVG (SigLIP), which replaced reconstruction-trained VAEs with pretrained vision encoders to enrich latent spaces with visual semantics. It departs from these prior works by identifying a fundamental tradeoff—semantic abstraction degrades fine-grained geometric structure—and by building on DINOv3's joint global-local feature design to address it. The progressive training strategy (frozen-encoder decoder pretraining followed by end-to-end fine-tuning with a distillation-based semantic regularization loss) is a practical departure from both the frozen-encoder baselines (which lose reconstruction quality) and naive end-to-end training (which catastrophically destroys discriminability).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-ablated recipe for constructing autoencoder latent spaces that simultaneously support high-fidelity reconstruction (rFID 0.06) and strong semantic classification (81.9% ImageNet), which is directly relevant to the growing unified understanding-and-generation paradigm. The finding that latent-space diffusion loss does not reliably track RGB-space visual quality is a useful diagnostic insight for practitioners training DiT models. However, the evaluation is limited to 256×256 ImageNet, the method depends on the very recent DINOv3 backbone, and it remains open whether the tradeoff can be further narrowed or whether the approach generalizes to video, higher resolutions, or other semantic encoders.
