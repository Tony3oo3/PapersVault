## Problem

Video matting is constrained by the limited scale and realism of existing datasets, and leveraging segmentation data to compensate often degrades boundary fidelity, producing segmentation-like mattes. The lack of ground-truth alpha mattes at scale makes it impossible to directly supervise boundary regions or curate large real-world training sets, leaving a persistent gap between semantic accuracy and fine detail preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The matting model takes a video clip and a single segmentation mask from the first frame (not per-frame masks) and outputs per-frame alpha mattes; training clips are cropped to 480×480 with 8-frame local windows. The MQE sub-network takes a tuple of (RGB frame, predicted alpha matte, segmentation mask) and produces a pixel-wise binary evaluation map (1 = reliable, 0 = erroneous). The reference-frame strategy additionally injects long-range frames beyond the 8-frame window into memory without increasing sequence length.

## Method

The core contribution is a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) built on a DINOv3 encoder and DPT decoder, trained as a binary segmentation task on P3M-10k with focal loss to handle class imbalance. The MQE is deployed in two roles: (1) online, it generates a probability map whose L1 norm serves as a penalty loss (L_eval) that suppresses erroneous regions during matting training without requiring ground-truth alpha; (2) offline, it acts as a quality arbiter in a dual-branch annotation pipeline that fuses temporally stable video-matting predictions (MatAnyone) with detail-rich image-matting predictions (MattePro) via a pixel-wise fusion mask, yielding the VMReal dataset (28K clips, 2.4M frames). A reference-frame training strategy with random dropout augmentation extends temporal context to handle large appearance variations in long videos without additional memory cost.

## Results

MatAnyone 2 achieves state-of-the-art results across all four synthetic benchmarks (VideoMatte and YouTubeMatte at both 512×288 and 1920×1080) and the real-world CRGNN dataset. On YouTubeMatte (1920×1080), it attains MAD 1.61, MSE 0.50, Grad 7.14, and dtSSD 1.53, reducing Grad and Conn by 27.1% and 22.4% over the prior best mask-guided method MatAnyone. On the real-world CRGNN benchmark, it achieves MAD 4.24 and Grad 11.74, outperforming the diffusion-based GVM (MAD 5.03, Grad 14.28) and MatAnyone (MAD 5.76, Grad 15.55) across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatAnyone 2 outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on all four synthetic benchmarks and the real-world CRGNN dataset. Against the closest competitor MatAnyone [39], it reduces Grad by 27.1% and Conn by 22.4% on VideoMatte, and improves MAD from 1.99 to 1.61 on YouTubeMatte (1080p), demonstrating that MQE-based supervision and VMReal training yield substantially sharper boundaries. The diffusion-based GVM [5], which leverages Stable Video Diffusion priors, is outperformed on all metrics (e.g., MAD 2.68 vs. 1.61 on YouTubeMatte 1080p), showing that the CNN-based approach with learned quality guidance surpasses the generative prior. MaGGIe [8], which requires per-frame instance masks, is also beaten despite its richer input (MAD 2.37 vs. 1.61), confirming that first-frame-only guidance suffices with the proposed framework. Among auxiliary-free methods, RVM-Large [19] is the strongest but still trails by a wide margin (MAD 3.58 vs. 1.61 on YouTubeMatte 1080p), highlighting the benefit of mask-guided training. On the real-world CRGNN benchmark, the gap over MatAnyone widens (MAD 4.24 vs. 5.76, Grad 11.74 vs. 15.55), indicating improved generalization to unseen real-world conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to MatAnyone [39], which introduced joint segmentation-matting training with memory propagation for video matting. It departs from that paradigm by replacing weak unsupervised boundary losses with a learned Matting Quality Evaluator (MQE) that provides pixel-wise quality feedback without ground-truth mattes, using a DINOv3 encoder and DPT decoder (following the Depth Anything architecture). The data curation pipeline repurposes existing models—MatAnyone for temporal stability, MattePro for boundary detail, and SAM 2 for segmentation—fused by the MQE as a quality arbiter, while the reference-frame strategy borrows the long-range frame mechanism from ProPainter [48].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VMReal dataset (28K clips, 2.4M frames, ~35× larger than VM800) is the first large-scale real-world video matting dataset, addressing a long-standing data bottleneck that has constrained the field. The MQE's dual role as both an online training signal and an offline data-curation arbiter offers a reusable recipe for scaling other matting or segmentation tasks where ground-truth annotations are scarce. Open questions include whether the MQE generalizes beyond human-centric matting to arbitrary objects, whether the automated annotation quality degrades on highly occluded or transparent subjects, and whether the approach extends to real-time or on-device deployment given the DINOv3 backbone cost.
