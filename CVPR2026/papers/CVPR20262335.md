## Problem

Existing AIGI detectors suffer a 'Benefit then Conflict' paradox: aggregating data from more generators initially helps but eventually degrades performance due to (1) increasing feature-distribution overlap from data-level heterogeneity and (2) a model-level bottleneck where fixed pretrained encoders (e.g., frozen CLIP) cannot adapt to the rising complexity of diverse generative artifacts. No prior work simultaneously addresses both the data and model challenges in the scaling-up regime.

## Input / Output

The detector takes a single RGB image at arbitrary resolution, encodes it via CLIP-ViT-L (projected to 128 dimensions), and outputs a binary real/fake logit from a linear classifier. The image embedding is re-expressed as a weighted linear combination of 64 learned prototypes (32 real, 32 generated) via cross-attention before classification, effectively constraining the decision to a low-variance prototype-defined subspace rather than the full encoder space.

## Method

GAPL follows a 'Turn Thousands into a Few' philosophy with a two-stage training scheme. In Stage 1, a small MLP is trained on a prototype set (2,000 images each from ProGAN, Stable Diffusion v1.4, and Midjourney plus matched real images) to create a forgery-aware subspace; PCA is then applied to the MLP's intermediate embeddings to extract N=64 generator-aware prototypes (top N/2 principal components from real and generated subsets). In Stage 2, LoRA fine-tunes the CLIP encoder while a cross-attention Prototype Mapping module uses image embeddings as queries and the fixed prototypes as keys/values, projecting each image into a compact, low-variance prototype space before a linear classifier produces the final binary prediction. This design decouples the encoder's semantic priors from the forgery-specific decision boundary, allowing the model to absorb knowledge from thousands of generators without the embedding overlap that plagues frozen-encoder approaches.

## Results

GAPL achieves 90.4% mean accuracy and 94.9% mean AP across 55 test subsets in 6 benchmarks, outperforming the previous best scaling-up detector (CommForen, 86.9% Acc / 93.4% AP) by 3.5% in accuracy while using only 550k training images versus CommForen's 5M. On individual benchmarks, GAPL reaches 97.2% accuracy on ForenSynths and UniversalFakeDetect, 96.7% on GenImage, and 89.4% on Community Forensics. In the cross-architecture comparison (Table 3), GAPL achieves 95.5% mean accuracy, surpassing even strong general vision backbones like Swin-T (89.7%) and ConvNeXt (86.2%) when all are trained on the same scaling-up data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAPL outperforms all 16 baselines in mean accuracy across the 6 benchmarks. Against the strongest scaling-up competitor CommForen (4.7K generators, 5M images), GAPL gains +3.5% mean accuracy (90.4% vs 86.9%) and +1.5% mean AP (94.9% vs 93.4%) while using only 11% of the training data (550k vs 5M). D3 (9 generators, 2M images) trails by 7.2% in mean accuracy (83.2%), and the scaled AIDE (8 generators, 1.3M) falls further behind at 75.4%. Among single-source detectors, B-Free is the closest competitor at 85.0% mean accuracy, but it still lags GAPL by 5.4%; frequency-based methods like SAFE and NPR achieve >95% on GAN benchmarks yet collapse to ~45–50% on diffusion-based benchmarks, exposing their narrow generalization. In the controlled cross-architecture experiment (Table 3), where all methods are retrained on identical data, GAPL (95.5% mean) beats AIDE (85.2%), Effort (77.5%), D3 (65.9%), and even general vision backbones Swin-T (89.7%) and ConvNeXt (86.2%), confirming that the prototype-learning architecture—not merely data scale—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AIGI detection line that relies on frozen pretrained encoders (CLIP-based detectors like UniFD, AIDE, D3) and the emerging scaling-up efforts (Community Forensics, D3) that aggregate data from thousands of generators. It departs from prior work by identifying that this scaling strategy is fundamentally flawed—introducing the 'Benefit then Conflict' paradox—and by repurposing prototype learning (PCA-based canonical forgery patterns) combined with LoRA and cross-attention to create a 'Turn Thousands into a Few' framework. Unlike prior detectors that either freeze the encoder entirely or fine-tune end-to-end, GAPL's two-stage scheme (MLP for forgery-aware projection, then LoRA for low-rank adaptation) is positioned as the first to simultaneously address both data-level heterogeneity and model-level bottleneck in AIGI detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'Benefit then Conflict' finding challenges the field's default assumption that aggregating more diverse generators monotonically improves detector generalization, suggesting that current architectures hit a ceiling that no amount of additional data can overcome without architectural changes. GAPL's 3.5% accuracy gain over the previous SOTA (Community Forensics) while using only 550k training images versus 5M is practically significant for resource-constrained settings. However, the authors acknowledge a key open question: whether future generative models built on fundamentally new techniques will still leave detectable artifacts, or whether the detection problem itself may become intractable as generators approach photorealism.
