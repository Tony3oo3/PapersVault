## Problem

When diffusion-based super-resolution models perform patch-wise inference on ultra-high-resolution (≥4K) images, a semantic mismatch arises: the global text prompt derived from the full LR image conflicts with the limited semantic content of individual patches, causing over-generation artifacts. Conversely, relying solely on local patch prompts under-activates the pre-trained generative prior, leading to under-generation of fine textures. Existing methods have not adequately resolved this dual failure mode in the context of Diffusion Transformer backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260505/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a low-resolution RGB image (tested at 128×128 up to 720-pixel short edge, with ×4 upscaling), a global text prompt, and per-patch text prompts—all generated by LLaVA at inference time. The LR image is encoded to latent space, partitioned into overlapping patches, and processed through a two-stage pipeline (1-step degradation removal + 16-step texture generation) before patch outputs are aggregated via Multi-Diffusion into a single high-resolution reconstruction.

## Method

DreamSR builds on FLUX.1-dev (a DiT-based diffusion model) and introduces a dual-branch Patch Context aware MM-ControlNet: a ControlNet branch conditioned on local patch prompts extracts patch-level image and text features, while the frozen DiT backbone receives the global prompt; a Context Cross-Attention module dynamically fuses global and local text features per block, suppressing over-generation while preserving semantic consistency. A Restoration Acceleration LoRA (rank 256) performs one-step degradation removal, and its output serves as an intermediate noisy state (t=0.8) for the subsequent 16-step texture generation, bypassing redundant early denoising. Training employs stage-specific degradation pipelines—Real-ESRGAN for the removal stage and a novel i2i pipeline (FLUX-based partial denoising at strength 0.3–0.5) for the texture stage—plus a Receptive-Field Enhancement strategy that crops 512×512 patches directly from native ~2K images to maintain sufficient divergence between local and global prompts during training.

## Results

DreamSR achieves the best no-reference image quality scores (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA+) on 4 out of 5 real-world datasets. On RealSR (128×128 input), it reaches MUSIQ 70.56, MANIQA 0.5731, and CLIPIQA+ 0.7318, outperforming the next-best DiffBIR (69.28/0.5582/0.7233). On the high-resolution RealDeg dataset (720px short edge), DreamSR scores MUSIQ 45.62, MANIQA 0.4574, and CLIPIQA+ 0.6647, leading all competitors. Inference time is 86 s for a 2560×1440 image using a 1+16 step scheme, faster than StableSR (156 s), DiffBIR (127 s), SeeSR (119 s), SUPIR (124 s), DreamClear (185 s), and DiT4SR (228 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamSR outperforms all 10 baselines on MUSIQ and MANIQA across every dataset, with the largest margins over GAN-based methods (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN) and DreamClear. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitors, DreamSR leads DiffBIR by ~1.3 MUSIQ on RealSR and ~1.5 on DRealSR, and surpasses SeeSR by ~1.0 MUSIQ on RealLR200. The closest competitor is DiT4SR, which edges out DreamSR on RealLQ250 MUSIQ (71.83 vs. 71.65) and RealDeg CLIPIQA+ (0.6841 vs. 0.6647), but DreamSR wins on MANIQA for both datasets (0.5319 vs. 0.4607; 0.4574 vs. 0.4437). On reference-based metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), DreamSR trails BSRGAN and Real-ESRGAN, consistent with the known perception-distortion tradeoff of generative diffusion models. In inference speed, DreamSR (86 s at 2K) is faster than StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, SUPIR, DreamClear, and DiT4SR, and only slower than the lightweight FaithDiff (30 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamSR extends the diffusion-based real-world SR line (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, SUPIR, OSEDiff, FaithDiff, DreamClear, DiT4SR) by shifting the backbone from U-Net-based latent diffusion to the FLUX DiT architecture, and by introducing a dual-branch MM-ControlNet that injects both visual and textual features from local patches into the frozen DiT. It builds on Multi-Diffusion for patch aggregation and on the ControlNet paradigm, but departs from prior ControlNet-based SR methods that only inject visual features by also routing patch-level text embeddings through a Context Cross-Attention module. The two-stage inference (one-step LoRA degradation removal followed by multi-step texture generation) draws on one-step diffusion SR ideas (OSEDiff, TSD-SR) but integrates them into a DiT pipeline rather than a U-Net one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically important gap: existing diffusion-based SR methods degrade noticeably at ultra-high resolutions (≥4K) where patch-wise inference is mandatory, producing over-generated or semantically inconsistent local textures. The dual-branch prompt design and Receptive-Field Enhancement training directly address this, and the 1+16-step inference scheme offers a meaningful efficiency gain over 50–200-step competitors. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other DiT backbones (SD3, Sana), how sensitive results are to the LLaVA-generated prompts, and whether the i2i degradation pipeline can be replaced by cheaper synthetic alternatives. The authors also note that reference-based metrics (PSNR/SSIM) remain poorly suited to evaluating generative SR, leaving the field without a fully reliable fidelity benchmark.
