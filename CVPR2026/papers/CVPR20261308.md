## Problem

Applying speculative decoding to Video-LLMs faces two critical bottlenecks: (1) sequential execution of draft and target models creates significant idling time that scales with the massive visual token prefix, and (2) pruning visual tokens from the draft model to improve the speed ratio introduces positional bias (via attention-score guidance) that degrades draft-target alignment and acceptance rates. Existing methods like SpecVLM address pruning but not the parallelism gap, leaving up to 50% of decoding time idle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128 sampled video frames (yielding ~25K visual tokens for LLaVA-OV, or equivalent for Qwen2.5-VL) concatenated with a text prompt, and produces up to 512 generated text tokens (video captions or descriptions). The draft model operates on a pruned subset of visual tokens (retaining 10% at α=0.9) while the target model retains the full visual context for lossless verification, meaning the output distribution is provably identical to vanilla autoregressive decoding.

## Method

ParallelVLM is a training-free, lossless framework with two parallel stages: Parallel Prefilling (PP) runs the draft model's pruned prefilling concurrently with the target's full prefilling, hiding draft startup latency; Parallel Decoding (PD) overlaps draft token generation with target verification in coordinated windows. The key pruning mechanism, UV-Prune, selects draft visual tokens by measuring vision-text cosine similarity variation across early target-model layers (tokens whose cross-modal alignment increases through layers are retained), avoiding the positional bias inherent in attention-score-based selection. The draft window size γ is set to the pruned speed ratio c* = T_p/T_q(α), expanding from 5 to 9 for LLaVA-OV-7B/72B at α=0.9. A theoretical speedup theorem shows the overall acceleration equals τ̂(P,α)·c*(α), where the unbiased pruning keeps acceptance rate τ̂ high even at aggressive pruning ratios.

## Results

ParallelVLM achieves up to 3.36× average speedup on LLaVA-OneVision-72B (7B draft) and 2.42× on Qwen2.5-VL-32B (7B draft) across five video understanding benchmarks (VideoDetailCaption, VideoMME, MVBench, MVLU, LongVideoBench), compared to 1.00× autoregressive decoding. The mean accepted length M reaches 6.83 for LLaVA-OV-7B/72B and 4.28 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B, substantially exceeding the ~4.2–4.5 range of all baselines. In the Self-SD setting (7B/7B), ParallelVLM achieves M > 14 with 1.51–1.55× speedup, outperforming SpecVLM (1.21–1.22×) and STD (1.23–1.24×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParallelVLM outperforms all nine baselines across every model pairing and benchmark. Against the closest lossless SD competitor SpecVLM [17], ParallelVLM gains 0.30–0.64× in speedup (e.g., 3.36× vs 2.74× on LLaVA-OV-7B/72B), driven by the parallel pipeline eliminating sequential idling and UV-Prune expanding the draft window from γ=5 to γ=9. Compared to Vanilla SD [18], OnSD [13], and SD-Tree [23], the advantage is even larger (up to 2.3× over Vanilla SD on LLaVA-OV-7B/72B), as these methods suffer from the sequential execution bottleneck with massive video token prefixes. In the Self-SD setting (7B/7B), ParallelVLM surpasses STD [50] by ~0.30× (1.55× vs 1.24×) by overlapping draft generation with verification rather than waiting sequentially. Against lossy visual token pruning methods (FastV [6], SparseVLM [51], P-Drop [47], DyCoke [40]), ParallelVLM delivers 2.1–2.4× higher speedup (3.36× vs 1.44–1.63× on LLaVA-OV-72B) while maintaining near-lossless token-wise acceptance ratios of 98.2–99.7%, whereas pruning methods degrade to 83–92%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the speculative decoding paradigm (Leviathan et al., 2023) to Video-LLMs by co-designing two parallel stages (prefilling and decoding) with a novel token pruning strategy. It directly departs from SpecVLM (Ji et al., 2025), the closest prior work, by replacing attention-score-guided pruning—which suffers from positional bias toward attention sinks and query-adjacent tokens—with UV-Prune, which uses vision-text similarity variation across early target-model layers as a bias-free alignment signal. The parallel decoding pipeline with adaptive draft length and pre-rollback restart is inherited from PEARL (Liu et al., 2025), but is here specifically adapted to hide the expensive draft prefilling under the target's long video-token prefilling, a bottleneck unique to Video-LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.36× lossless speedup for 72B Video-LLMs addresses a critical deployment bottleneck as video understanding moves into production (content auditing, autonomous driving, long-form video QA). The UV-Prune insight—that cross-modal similarity *variation* across layers is a better pruning signal than raw attention scores—offers a generalizable diagnostic for any attention-guided token selection method and is fully compatible with FlashAttention, which attention-score methods are not. Open questions include whether the parallelism overhead (two concurrent model processes) remains negligible at even larger scales (100B+ targets, million-token videos), and whether the similarity-variation signal degrades when the draft and target models are architecturally very different rather than same-family different sizes.
