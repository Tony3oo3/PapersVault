## Problem

Existing medical vision-language pre-training models (CLIP and its variants) are designed for CT, MRI, and pathology, and fail on ultrasound due to its heterogeneous anatomical structures, operator-dependent imaging planes, and ultrasound-specific diagnostic vocabulary (echogenicity, posterior acoustic phenomena, vascularity). Ultrasound data constitutes less than 5% of existing medical VLP datasets, and standard binary contrastive objectives cannot handle the semantic ambiguity of diverse ultrasound report phrasings or model the structured relationships among diagnostic attributes.

## Input / Output

The model takes a static ultrasound frame (decomposed from video at 0.5-second intervals) and a paired text caption (typically ≤25 words) as input. The text is first parsed via a UDAF-guided LLM pipeline into a structured set of labels across nine diagnostic dimensions (body system, organ, diagnosis, shape, margins, echogenicity, internal characteristics, posterior acoustic phenomena, vascularity), which are then converted into a sample-specific bipartite graph. The output is a pair of L2-normalized D-dimensional embeddings (image and graph-enhanced text) used for retrieval, multi-label classification across the nine UDAF dimensions, and zero-shot/linear-probe/fine-tuning transfer to downstream lesion classification.

## Method

Ultrasound-CLIP uses a dual-encoder (ViT image backbone + BioClinical-BERT text encoder) augmented with two key components. First, a UDAF-guided heterogeneous graph encoder converts each sample's extracted diagnostic and attribute labels into a bipartite graph, processes it with a heterogeneous graph transformer, pools node embeddings via attention, and fuses the resulting graph summary with the raw text embedding through multi-head attention and a gated residual connection (α_max=0.2). Second, a UDAF-based semantic prior constructs a B×B soft similarity matrix from precomputed per-dimension label similarity matrices, providing continuous (non-binary) supervision. Training optimizes a dual objective: standard symmetric CLIP contrastive loss plus a semantic loss combining MSE and KL-divergence (weighted α_s=0.6) between the predicted similarity matrix and the soft prior, with λ=0.2 balancing the two terms.

## Results

On the US-365K classification benchmark (9 diagnostic attributes), Ultrasound-CLIP achieves an average accuracy of 59.61% and average recall of 61.08%, outperforming the best medical baseline BiomedCLIP (33.81% AvgAcc) by over 25 percentage points. On image-text retrieval, the full model reaches I2T R@10 of 0.3745 and T2I R@50 of 0.8022, surpassing the strongest baseline PMC-CLIP (0.3011 / 0.7312). On four downstream ultrasound classification datasets, Ultrasound-CLIP attains average zero-shot accuracy of 54.42%, linear-probe accuracy of 75.40%, and fine-tuning accuracy of 84.23%, with a peak of 92.13% on the Breast dataset under fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ultrasound-CLIP decisively outperforms all seven baselines on the primary classification task, with a 25.8-point AvgAcc margin over the strongest medical baseline BiomedCLIP (59.61% vs. 33.81%) and a 46.3-point margin over general CLIP (13.29%). The gap is especially pronounced on fine-grained attributes: Diagnosis accuracy jumps from 39.40% (BiomedCLIP) to 64.05%, and Margins from 64.13% to 84.44%. On retrieval, Ultrasound-CLIP leads all baselines by a clear margin (I2T R@10: 0.3745 vs. 0.3011 for PMC-CLIP; T2I R@50: 0.8022 vs. 0.7312). In zero-shot downstream transfer, Ultrasound-CLIP (54.42% avg) surpasses UniMed-CLIP (49.44%) and BiomedCLIP (50.38%); in linear probing it edges out BiomedCLIP (75.40% vs. 74.14%). Under full fine-tuning, results are more competitive: Ultrasound-CLIP (84.23% avg) is within 0.1 point of UniMed-CLIP (84.33%), winning on BUS-BRA and Breast but trailing slightly on GIST514-DB and BreastMNIST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-style contrastive pre-training paradigm to the ultrasound modality, building directly on the original CLIP architecture (Radford et al., 2021) and its medical adaptations such as BiomedCLIP, PMC-CLIP, and UniMed-CLIP. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the standard binary contrastive objective with a dual loss that incorporates UDAF-derived semantic soft labels to handle the inherent ambiguity of ultrasound diagnostic language, and (2) introducing a heterogeneous graph transformer encoder that models structured relationships among diagnostic attributes within each text annotation. The work also carves new ground on the data side by constructing the first pan-anatomy, large-scale ultrasound VLP dataset (US-365K) grounded in a novel two-level taxonomy (UHAT + UDAF), addressing a gap the authors document as ultrasound comprising less than 5% of existing medical VLP corpora.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The US-365K dataset and UDT taxonomy fill a genuine resource gap for ultrasound vision-language research, providing a standardized, semantically rich benchmark that prior domain-specific datasets (Fetal-CLIP, RemUD, EchoPrime) did not cover across all anatomical systems. The semantic soft label + graph encoder formulation offers a principled way to inject structured clinical knowledge into contrastive pre-training, a strategy that could transfer to other modalities with similarly attribute-rich reports. Open questions include whether the heterogeneous graph encoder's added complexity is justified over simpler attribute-embedding approaches, whether the semantic loss generalizes beyond ultrasound to other report-heavy modalities, and whether the 6:2:2 patient-level split adequately prevents leakage given the multi-image nature of many cases.
