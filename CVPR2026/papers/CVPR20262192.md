## Problem

The small-loss trick, a dominant paradigm in learning with noisy labels, suffers from two overlooked confirmation biases: class-level bias (easy-to-learn classes are over-selected because they naturally yield lower losses) and instance-level bias (mislabeled samples with spuriously low loss are retained, forcing the model to memorize wrong labels). Both biases accumulate over training and degrade generalization, yet existing sample-selection methods do not explicitly address them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262192/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images with their (potentially incorrect) class labels and operates entirely on the model's predicted class probabilities and per-class confidence trajectories over training epochs. It produces two outputs: a per-sample selection mask (which samples to include in training) and a modified per-sample loss that restricts the softmax denominator to a subset of classes, effectively reducing the classification task from k classes to k−|I_i| classes for each sample. No architectural changes or additional modalities are required; the modules are plug-and-play on top of any backbone (ResNet, Inception-ResNetV2, or ViT-B/16 from CLIP).

## Method

The paper proposes two lightweight, plug-and-play debiasing modules. Marginal Distribution Adjustment (MDA) estimates the model's predicted class marginal via an exponential moving average across batches, then reweights the posterior p(y|x)/p'(y) to push the effective class distribution toward uniformity, ensuring fairer sample selection across easy and hard classes. Candidate Class Selection (CCS) tracks per-class confidence trajectories using the Mann-Kendall non-parametric trend test to identify classes whose confidence is consistently rising (likely true labels), then excludes those classes from the softmax denominator in the cross-entropy loss for that sample. This prevents the model from being forced to rank the annotated (possibly wrong) label above the true label, while still leveraging weakly related incorrect labels as valid supervision. Together, MDA and CCS form Debiased Sample Selection (DSS), which can be extended to DSS+ by adding cross-selection between two networks and weak-strong consistency regularization, achieving state-of-the-art results on both synthetic and real-world noisy benchmarks.

## Results

On real-world noisy benchmarks, DSS+ achieves 94.74% top-1 on CIFAR-10N-Worst, 74.67% on CIFAR-100N-Noisy, 75.13% on Clothing1M, 82.40% top-1 on WebVision, and 78.48% top-1 on ILSVRC12, setting new state-of-the-art across all five datasets. In the sample-selection-only setting (Table 1), DSS reaches 90.1% on CIFAR-10 with 50% instance-dependent noise (vs. 81.5% for the prior best DIST+CT) and 70.0% on CIFAR-100 with 40% asymmetric noise (vs. 69.1% for DIST+CT). When paired with a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, DSS+ further lifts CIFAR-100N-Noisy accuracy to 83.24% and ILSVRC12 to 86.12%, surpassing the CLIP-specific DeFT method (79.04% and 84.92% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSS+ outperforms all 15 compared baselines on every reported benchmark. Against the closest competitors, DSS+ leads LSL (the prior best on C10N at 94.57%) by 0.17 points on CIFAR-10N-Worst and by 0.21 points on CIFAR-100N-Noisy (74.67 vs. 74.46), while also surpassing LSL on WebVision top-1 (82.40 vs. 81.40) and ILSVRC12 top-1 (78.48 vs. 77.00). DSS+ beats DULC (the strongest Clothing1M baseline at 75.09%) by 0.04 points and leads on all other metrics. In the sample-selection-only setting (Table 1), DSS achieves a dramatic 8.6-point gain over DIST+CT on CIFAR-10 with 50% instance-dependent noise (90.1% vs. 81.5%) and a 0.9-point gain on CIFAR-100 asymmetric noise (70.0% vs. 69.1%), while also improving over GMM, AUM, FINE, L2D, DIST, and HMW across all noise types. The CLIP-based comparison (Table 6) shows DSS+ (83.24% on C100N, 86.12% on ILSVRC12) surpasses the CLIP-specific DeFT method (79.04%, 84.92%) by over 4 points, confirming architecture-agnostic applicability. Ablation (Table 5) confirms both MDA and CCS are indispensable: removing CCS drops C10N accuracy by 2.07 points and C100N by 1.83 points, while removing MDA costs 2.08 points on C100N.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the small-loss trick paradigm for learning with noisy labels by identifying two specific confirmation biases (class-level and instance-level) that prior sample-selection methods have overlooked. It builds directly on Co-teaching's cross-selection mechanism and ReMixMatch's weak-strong consistency regularization for its advanced pipeline (DSS+), and on the authors' own prior work CT [27] for the Mann-Kendall trend test used in CCS. Conceptually, MDA draws an analogy to logit adjustment in long-tail learning but applies it dynamically to the model's evolving predictions rather than to a static class-frequency prior, while CCS departs from the relabeling tradition by temporarily excluding likely-true classes from the loss denominator instead of correcting labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design of MDA and CCS makes them immediately applicable to a wide range of existing LNL pipelines without architectural changes, and the demonstrated compatibility with CLIP-based vision-language models suggests the approach remains relevant in the foundation-model era. A key open question is whether the Mann-Kendall trend test is the optimal mechanism for identifying likely-true classes, and whether the approach scales gracefully to very large class spaces (e.g., 1000+ classes) where per-class confidence tracking becomes more expensive. The paper also leaves open whether the bias-mitigation framing generalizes beyond sample selection to other LNL paradigms such as loss correction or contrastive learning.
