## Problem

Reasoning LMMs produce overly generic predictions in fine-grained open-world image classification (e.g., 'flower' instead of 'daisy'), and naively encouraging specificity via prompting, SFT, or standard RFT degrades correctness. The paper identifies that models do possess the fine-grained knowledge (demonstrated via Best-of-64 analysis) but fail to reliably express it in a single inference, making the balance between specificity and correctness a non-trivial, underexplored challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a text prompt instructing the model to reason about the main object and provide a final prediction in <answer> tags. The output is an open-ended fine-grained class label (not drawn from a fixed vocabulary) preceded by a chain-of-thought reasoning trace. Predictions are then evaluated by an external LLM judge (Llama3-72B) that assigns each prediction to one of six ordered specificity categories (Wrong, Abstain, Generic, Less Specific, Specific, More Specific) relative to the ground-truth label.

## Method

SpeciaRL fine-tunes a reasoning LMM (Qwen2.5VL-7B) using GRPO with a novel specificity-aware dynamic reward. For each training sample, N rollouts are generated and an LLM judge categorizes each prediction's specificity level; the best category c_best within the group defines an adaptive reference threshold c*. A prediction receives reward 1 if its category meets or exceeds c*, and 0 otherwise, making the reward sample-specific and dynamically calibrated to the model's current per-sample capability. This design prevents the model from being pushed beyond its actual knowledge (which would increase wrong predictions) while still incentivizing more specific outputs. The BoN computation is performed online using the same N rollouts as GRPO, requiring no additional generations. Training uses only 3000 CUB bird samples, and the method generalizes out-of-domain to flowers, food, pets, cars, and aircraft.

## Results

On the fine-grained set (Flowers102, Food101, OxfordPets), SpeciaRL-7B achieves specificity 0.920, correctness 0.848, and harmonic mean (HM) 0.883, improving over the base Qwen2.5VL-7B (HM 0.790) by +0.093. On the very fine-grained set (StanfordCars, FGVCAircraft), SpeciaRL-7B reaches specificity 0.818, correctness 0.855, and HM 0.830, a +0.138 gain over the base model (HM 0.692). SpeciaRL attains the highest HM across both test groups among all training-based and zero-shot methods, approaching the BoN-64 upper bound (HM 0.933 / 0.868) while maintaining competitive correctness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeciaRL outperforms all zero-shot baselines: it surpasses CaSED (HM 0.797→0.883 fine-grained), Qwen2.5VL-7B base (0.790→0.883), and InternVL2.5-8B (0.624→0.883) on the fine-grained set, with even larger margins on very fine-grained (0.692→0.830). Against the prompting baseline ("Be specific"), SpeciaRL gains +0.061 HM on fine-grained and +0.079 on very fine-grained, while also maintaining higher correctness (0.848 vs 0.832). Compared to SFT, SpeciaRL trades a small specificity drop (0.920 vs 0.935) for a substantial correctness gain (0.848 vs 0.807), yielding a higher HM (0.883 vs 0.866). Against RFT (static binary reward), SpeciaRL improves both specificity (0.920 vs 0.875) and correctness (0.848 vs 0.785) on fine-grained, and achieves higher HM on very fine-grained (0.830 vs 0.821). SpeciaRL approaches but does not reach the BoN-64 upper bound, confirming that the dynamic reward successfully steers the model toward its best achievable specificity without exceeding its capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeciaRL builds directly on the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath [37] and extends the RLVR paradigm beyond structured-solution tasks by incorporating an LLM-based verifier for reward computation, following the direction set by Gunjal et al. [14] and Su et al. [42]. It departs from standard RLVR's binary exact-match reward by introducing a dynamic, sample-wise specificity-aware reward anchored to the model's own best prediction within online rollouts. The evaluation framework and the observation that reasoning LMMs are overly generic on fine-grained concepts are inherited from Conti et al. [9], which this paper takes as its starting point and addresses with a training-based solution rather than prompting or retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the genericness of reasoning LMMs on fine-grained classification is not a knowledge deficit but a sampling/reasoning inefficiency, which reframes how practitioners should approach improving LMM classification: through RL that sharpens existing capabilities rather than SFT that injects new knowledge. The dynamic reward design is a practical and elegant mechanism that could generalize to other open-ended generation tasks where partial credit and capability-awareness matter. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even finer-grained or multi-object settings, how sensitive performance is to the quality of the LLM judge, and whether the out-of-domain generalization (trained only on birds) holds when the training domain is more distant from the test domain.
