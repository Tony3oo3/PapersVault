## Problem

Existing vision-language foundation models for satellite imagery face two persistent limitations: multi-spectral inputs introduce band redundancy and inter-band misalignment that yield inconsistent or even degrading performance gains, and CLIP-style text encoders constrain semantic expressiveness, weakening fine-grained cross-modal alignment. This paper asks whether an RGB-only model can retain spectral knowledge learned during training and achieve stronger alignment by replacing the lightweight text encoder with an instruction-augmented LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model consumes only RGB satellite patches (no multi-spectral bands required) paired with instruction-augmented text prompts (a caption plus a task instruction). The visual descriptor is a 2d_v-dimensional vector formed by concatenating the ViT class token with the mean of all patch tokens; the text side is a mean-pooled LLM token embedding projected to the same 2d_v-dimensional space. Because the LLM text encoder is frozen, all label embeddings can be precomputed and cached, so per-query cost is a single forward pass through the RGB encoder and two lightweight projectors.

## Method

SATtxt is pre-trained in two stages, both with frozen backbone encoders. Stage 1 (Spectral Representation Distillation) distills spectral priors from a frozen multi-spectral teacher (SpectralGPT) into a frozen RGB student (DINOv3 ViT-L) by training only a lightweight two-block transformer projector and a linear head under a DINO-style centering-and-temperature-sharpening objective, using multi-crop RGB views paired with global MS views. Stage 2 (Spectrally Grounded Alignment with Instruction-Augmented LLMs) freezes both the spectrally distilled RGB encoder and a Llama-3.1-8B text encoder (initialized via LLM2Vec), then trains only two lightweight linear projectors under a symmetric InfoNCE loss to align the visual descriptor with mean-pooled, instruction-augmented LLM embeddings. The key design choice is that both encoders remain frozen throughout, concentrating all learnable capacity in the projectors, which reduces pre-training to roughly 7 hours on 8×H200 GPUs while preserving pretrained capabilities and enabling RGB-only inference.

## Results

SATtxt achieves the strongest zero-shot classification across all three benchmarks: 73.40% on EuroSAT, 60.18% on BigEarthNet, and 17.61% on ForestNet, using only RGB inputs at inference. For text-to-image retrieval (mAP@100), it reaches 78.97 on EuroSAT, 53.10 on BigEarthNet, and 22.59 on ForestNet. On open-vocabulary segmentation (DFC2020), SATtxt attains 31.23 mIoU, surpassing the best MS-input baseline Llama3-MS-CLIP (28.58 mIoU). In linear probing, SATtxt scores 98.04 on EuroSAT, 84.80 on BigEarthNet (100%), and 53.27 on ForestNet, outperforming all contrastive and MIM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SATtxt outperforms the strongest MS-input baseline Llama3-MS-CLIP by 5.54 points on EuroSAT zero-shot classification (73.40 vs. 67.86) and by 3.71 points on EuroSAT retrieval (78.97 vs. 75.26), while using only RGB inputs. Against DOFA-CLIP (RGB), SATtxt gains 5.76 points on EuroSAT CLS and 0.61 on EuroSAT retrieval, and substantially leads on ForestNet retrieval (22.59 vs. 12.56). Compared to FT-DINOv3txt (fine-tuned on the same SSL4EO-S12 data), SATtxt improves EuroSAT CLS by 14.82 points and ForestNet CLS by 0.87 points. On open-vocabulary segmentation, SATtxt (31.23 mIoU) surpasses Llama3-MS-CLIP (28.58 mIoU) and FT-DINOv3txt (26.81 mIoU) by 2.65 and 4.42 points respectively. In linear probing, SATtxt leads all baselines on every dataset, with the largest margin on ForestNet (53.27 vs. 49.65 for DOFA-CLIP RGB) and BigEarthNet 10% (80.73 vs. 78.90 for Llama3-MS-CLIP). Generative baselines (GeoChat, EarthDial) show limited transfer to remote-sensing categories, with EarthDial reaching only 8.22% on ForestNet CLS versus SATtxt's 17.61%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SATtxt sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) DINO-style self-distillation, whose centering and temperature-sharpening mechanism is repurposed here for cross-modal spectral-to-RGB knowledge transfer rather than intra-modal view matching; (2) LiT/DINOtxt-style frozen-backbone alignment, extended by freezing *both* the vision and text encoders and training only lightweight projectors, in contrast to prior RS VLFMs (RemoteCLIP, SkyCLIP, Llama3-MS-CLIP, DOFA-CLIP) that fine-tune at least one encoder; and (3) LLM2Vec's demonstration that decoder-only LLMs serve as competitive text encoders, which the paper adopts to replace the CLIP text encoder with an instruction-augmented Llama-3.1-8B. The key conceptual departure from Llama3-MS-CLIP and DOFA-CLIP is that spectral knowledge is injected once during a distillation stage and then discarded, enabling RGB-only inference while retaining MS-informed representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for operational Earth-observation systems: RGB-only inference removes the dependency on full multispectral coverage (often unavailable due to atmospheric conditions or sensor degradation), and the frozen-encoder design reduces pre-training to roughly 7 GPU-hours on 8×H200s, making the pipeline accessible to groups without large compute budgets. The finding that a distilled RGB model matches or exceeds dedicated MS VLFMs across four benchmarks and two sensor families (Sentinel-2, Landsat-8) challenges the assumption that explicit spectral inputs are necessary for strong zero-shot transfer. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-optical modalities (SAR, thermal), how the LLM text encoder's memory footprint scales with prompt length in production settings, and whether the spectral distillation stage can be replaced by cheaper proxies (e.g., band-reconstruction losses) without sacrificing the gains.
