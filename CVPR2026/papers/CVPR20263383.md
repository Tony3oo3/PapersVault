## Problem

Vision Transformer self-attention inherently acts as a low-pass filter, causing affinities used for CAM refinement to smooth out high-frequency signals such as object boundaries. Existing WSSS methods that rely on these low-frequency-dominated affinities can expand activation into object interiors but fail to recover precise boundary regions, leaving a gap in producing both complete and accurate CAMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263383/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 320×320 RGB images and class-name text prompts, processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-Base-16 encoder, and produces pixel-wise segmentation masks along with refined class activation maps. The pipeline is single-stage, requiring only image-level labels during training, and the final CAM is derived from an Optimal Transport plan that directly allocates patch tokens to semantic classes without iterative refinement or post-processing.

## Method

The Dual Frequency-Aware (DFA) framework exploits the spectral duality between low-pass and high-pass filtering of the self-attention matrix: the normal attention yields a smoothed affinity capturing interior semantics, while its Fourier-domain inverse yields an inverse affinity encoding boundary relations. The LFAA module aligns the smoothed affinity with dense low-frequency feature relations from the decoder via a selective KL-divergence loss, and the HFAR module rectifies the inverse affinity by reinforcing positive boundary relations and suppressing negative noise relations using high-frequency features and pseudo-label cross-conditions. A novel Frequency-Guided CAM Generation module then unifies both refined affinities into a score matrix and solves an Optimal Transport problem (via Sinkhorn iterations) to allocate token features to target classes in a single step, eliminating the traditional multi-stage CAM refinement pipeline.

## Results

The proposed DFA framework achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 79.3% on PASCAL VOC 2012 val, 79.8% on VOC 2012 test, and 51.5% on MS COCO 2014 val, all using a single-stage pipeline with a frozen ViT-B/16 CLIP backbone. In terms of CAM quality, DFA produces 79.1% mIoU on CAM seeds and 80.8% on pseudo labels on the VOC train set, surpassing the previous best (ExCEL) by 1.1% in CAM performance. The method outperforms the strongest multi-stage baseline (PSDPM) by 5.2% on VOC val and 4.3% on COCO val, while also beating the best single-stage CLIP-based method (ExCEL) by 0.9% on VOC val and 1.2% on COCO val.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFA outperforms all 24 compared baselines across both datasets. Among single-stage methods, it surpasses the previous SOTA ExCEL [50] by +0.9% on VOC val (79.3 vs 78.4), +1.3% on VOC test (79.8 vs 78.5), and +1.2% on COCO val (51.5 vs 50.3). Against other CLIP-based single-stage methods, DFA leads WeCLIP [56] by +2.9% on VOC val and FFR [52] by +3.3% on VOC val. Among multi-stage methods, DFA exceeds the strongest baseline S2C [21] by +1.1% on VOC val and +2.3% on COCO val, and outperforms PSDPM [62] by a large margin of +5.2% on VOC val and +4.3% on COCO val. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that the FG CAM generation module contributes +1.2% over traditional element-wise refinement, and the HFAR module adds +2.8% over using only the low-frequency-aware affinity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three WSSS research threads: CLIP-based CAM generation (CLIP-ES, ExCEL, WeCLIP), affinity learning from self-attention (AFA, AffinityNet), and frequency-domain feature analysis (FFR, PCSS). Its key departure is reframing ViT self-attention as a low-pass filter and explicitly constructing its spectral inverse as a high-pass filter, then treating the two resulting affinities as complementary signals for interior consistency and boundary precision, respectively. The Frequency-Guided CAM generation module further departs from the standard generate-then-refine pipeline by unifying both steps into a single Optimal Transport allocation, building on the OT formulations in DHR and POT but introducing a novel score matrix that encodes both frequency-aware affinities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-frequency perspective offers a principled explanation for why prior affinity-based refinement methods struggle at boundaries: the inherent low-pass smoothing of self-attention suppresses exactly the high-frequency signals that encode edge structure. By rectifying the high-pass affinity with supervised positive/negative relation masks, the paper provides a reusable recipe for boundary-aware affinity that could transfer to other ViT-based dense prediction tasks. However, the method still depends on pseudo labels for the HFAR rectification masks, and the frozen CLIP backbone limits how much the encoder can adapt; whether the OT-based single-step generation generalizes to open-vocabulary or video segmentation settings remains open.
