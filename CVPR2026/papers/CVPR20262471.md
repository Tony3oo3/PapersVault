## Problem

Existing 3D visual grounding methods rely on point-based backbones (e.g., PointNet++) that require aggressive downsampling from ~50,000 to ~2,048 points, creating a severe information bottleneck that degrades localization of small or occluded objects. Voxel-based backbones preserve fine-grained spatial details but produce exponentially many voxels during upsampling, making dense cross-attention with text features computationally infeasible. No prior architecture efficiently unites voxel-level fidelity with keypoint-level interaction efficiency for text-conditioned 3D grounding.

## Input / Output

The input is a raw 3D point cloud with XYZ+RGB coordinates (N×6), clamped to an 16m×16m×4m bounding box and voxelized at 0.02m resolution, paired with a free-form natural language description encoded by a pre-trained RoBERTa. The model outputs both a 3D bounding box (grounding) and a per-point segmentation mask (referring expression segmentation) through a shared multi-task prediction head inherited from MCLN. Internally, FPS selects 1024 uniform keypoints as aggregation anchors, which are then reduced to 256 text-guided target-aware keypoints before the final interaction and decoding stages.

## Method

PV-Ground replaces the standard PointNet++ backbone with a sparse voxel convolution network that progressively downsamples the voxelized scene (stride-2 blocks) and appends a BEV feature map from the 8× downsampled level, forming a multi-scale voxel feature pyramid. A Voxel Set Abstraction (VSA) module then distills this pyramid into compact keypoint features: each of 1024 FPS-sampled keypoints queries spherical neighborhoods (radii 0.2–1.6m) across all pyramid levels, and the gathered voxel features are processed by a PointNet and fused with raw point and BEV features via an MLP. The key novelty is the Text-Guided Keypoint Sampling (TGS) module, which first enhances visual features via cross-attention with text, then uses self-attention and a fully connected layer to predict per-keypoint sampling weights, and finally applies Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization to perform differentiable soft assignment, concentrating 256 output keypoints around text-relevant objects rather than uniformly across the scene. These target-aware keypoint features are fed into MCLN's multi-task decoder for joint bounding-box regression and segmentation prediction, trained end-to-end with supervised multi-task losses.

## Results

On ScanRefer (single-stage), PV-Ground achieves 59.31% Acc@0.25 and 47.77% Acc@0.5, surpassing the point-based baseline MCLN by +5.0% and +5.1% respectively. In the two-stage pipeline, it reaches 59.87% Acc@0.25 and 47.56% Acc@0.5 (+2.7% and +2.0% over MCLN). On ReferIt3D, PV-Ground sets a new SOTA of 51.3% on Nr3D (single-stage, +5.6% over MCLN) and 62.1% on Nr3D (two-stage, +2.3%). For segmentation on ScanRefer, it achieves 47.9% mIOU, a +3.2% gain over MCLN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PV-Ground outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every evaluated setting. Against the primary point-based baseline MCLN [32], it gains +5.0%/+5.1% (single-stage) and +2.7%/+2.0% (two-stage) on ScanRefer Overall Acc@0.25/0.5, and +5.6%/+3.1% on ReferIt3D Nr3D/Sr3D (single-stage). The closest voxel-based competitor TSP3D [12] (CVPR'25) achieves 56.45%/46.71% on ScanRefer single-stage, still trailing PV-Ground by 2.86%/1.06%. Among point-based methods, EDA [54] (CVPR'23) is the strongest prior single-stage baseline at 53.83%/41.70%, with PV-Ground leading by 5.48%/6.07%. In the two-stage pipeline, CORE-3DVG [62] (NeurIPS'23) and VPP-Net [45] (CVPR'24) are the strongest prior methods at 56.77%/43.83% and 55.65%/43.29% respectively, both clearly outperformed. For segmentation, PV-Ground surpasses MCLN [32] by +3.5% Acc@0.25, +4.1% Acc@0.5, and +3.2% mIOU, and leads RG-SAN [52] (NeurIPS'24) by +5.2% Acc@0.25 and +7.1% mIOU.

## Relation to Prior Work

PV-Ground departs from the dominant point-based backbone paradigm (PointNet++) in 3D visual grounding by replacing it with a sparse voxel convolution encoder, while retaining keypoint-based multi-modal interaction for computational tractability. It adapts the Voxel Set Abstraction module from PV-RCNN/PV-RCNN++ to bridge the voxel feature pyramid into compact keypoint features, and draws on the differentiable soft-assignment idea from MICAS to implement text-guided keypoint sampling via Gumbel-Softmax. The multi-task decoder and prediction head are inherited from MCLN, positioning PV-Ground as a backbone-level upgrade rather than a full pipeline redesign. Conceptually, it contrasts with TSP3D's hard voxel pruning by using soft, differentiable aggregation that avoids irreversible information loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in 3D VG: aggressive point downsampling (50k→2k) discards fine-grained geometry critical for localizing small or occluded objects, a problem that has been overlooked while the field focused on interaction modules. The point-voxel interaction framework is a practical architectural pattern that could transfer to other 3D perception tasks requiring both high-resolution features and efficient cross-modal attention. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large outdoor scenes, whether the Gumbel-Softmax temperature and keypoint count (n=256) require per-scene tuning, and whether the gains persist when the backbone is swapped to a different voxel architecture. The consistent improvements in both grounding and segmentation suggest the representation benefit is task-agnostic, but the paper does not explore computational cost trade-offs in detail.
