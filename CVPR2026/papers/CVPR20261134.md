## Problem

Existing training-free zero-shot temporal action localization methods perform test-time adaptation independently on each video, resetting all learned information before processing the next one. This per-instance adaptation discards valuable cross-video knowledge (e.g., shared actions like 'running' across basketball and soccer clips), preventing the system from accumulating discriminative representations that could improve action-background separation on subsequent videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed RGB videos sampled at their original frame rate and a fixed set of text prompts (one per action class, templated as 'a video of action [CLS]'). It produces per-frame activation scores that are thresholded to yield continuous action segments with start/end boundaries, along with a video-level pseudo-label. Crucially, the system operates in a streaming fashion: the lookup table and text prototypes persist and evolve across the test sequence, so the output for video n depends on all previously processed videos.

## Method

The LLT framework maintains a per-class buffer (capacity B) of high-confidence lookup candidates extracted from top-k activation frames of previously seen videos, updated via a confidence-aware replacement policy that evicts the least confident entry when the buffer is full. For each new video, a learnable residual vector r_v is optimized (via a separation loss on top-K/bottom-K frames plus an alignment loss) to adapt the aggregated lookup item to the current video's distribution, while a second residual r_y refines the text prototype and is committed via a momentum update gated by a confidence threshold. The VLM encoder (CoCa ViT-L/14) remains frozen throughout; only the two lightweight residual vectors and the lookup table are updated, eliminating backpropagation through the backbone. This design imposes an implicit bottleneck that aggregates diverse action-positive evidence across videos, mitigating background interference from fixed text prompts while requiring far less storage than caching per-video frame features.

## Results

On THUMOS'14 (75% seen / 25% unseen, OOD setting), LLT achieves an average mAP of 12.8 at IoU 0.3–0.7, outperforming the closest training-free baseline T3AL (9.2) by 40% relatively. On the harder 50%–50% split, LLT reaches 12.6 avg mAP versus T3AL's 10.4. On ActivityNet v1.3 (75%–25%), LLT attains 17.1 avg mAP at IoU 0.5–0.95, surpassing T3AL (15.4) and T3AL without TTA (14.3). Training-based zero-shot methods (EffPrompt, STALE) collapse to near-zero mAP (<5) under the same OOD protocol, highlighting the advantage of the training-free design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLT consistently outperforms T3AL [19] (CVPR 2024), the most directly comparable training-free ZS-TAL method, by 40% relative average mAP on THUMOS'14 (12.8 vs 9.2, 75%–25% split) and by 11% on ActivityNet v1.3 (17.1 vs 15.4). Even without any test-time optimization steps (T=0), LLT already surpasses T3AL's full TTA variant on THUMOS'14 (12.1 vs 9.2), indicating that the lookup table alone provides substantial cross-video knowledge. Training-based zero-shot baselines EffPrompt [13] and STALE [23] (both ECCV 2022) degrade catastrophically under the OOD protocol, dropping to 4.6 and 0.3 avg mAP respectively on THUMOS'14, confirming that fine-tuning on seen classes harms generalization to unseen actions. DeTAL [18] (TPAMI 2024) is reported only in the in-domain setting (25.3 avg mAP) and is not evaluated under the OOD protocol, making a direct comparison inapplicable. ActionFormer [43] (ECCV 2022) serves as a fully-supervised upper bound (66.8 avg mAP) and is not directly comparable due to its reliance on frame-level annotations for all classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends T3AL (Liberatori et al., CVPR 2024), which introduced training-free ZS-TAL by adapting a frozen VLM's projection layer per video, by replacing the per-instance reset with a persistent, confidence-aware lookup table that accumulates action-positive visual candidates across the streaming test set. The residual learning module draws on entropy-minimization TTA (Tent, DMN) but repurposes it for TAL-specific action-background separation rather than general OOD robustness. Conceptually, the work bridges the test-time adaptation literature (which typically optimizes model weights on unlabeled data) and the TAL literature (which relies on temporal structure) by introducing a compact memory mechanism that neither backpropagates through the VLM encoder nor requires any source training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lookup table mechanism offers a practical, low-storage solution to the knowledge-loss problem in streaming zero-shot video understanding, and the oracle analysis (9.6% gain with perfect samples) reveals that the framework's architecture is sound but pseudo-label quality and frame selection remain the dominant bottlenecks. Absolute performance is still modest (~12.8 mAP on THUMOS'14, ~17.1 on ActivityNet), leaving substantial headroom and suggesting that better pseudo-labeling strategies or richer temporal modeling could yield large gains. Open questions include whether the fixed buffer size and confidence threshold generalize to open-vocabulary or long-tail action distributions, and whether the streaming assumption (sequential test videos) holds in real deployment where videos may arrive in arbitrary order.
