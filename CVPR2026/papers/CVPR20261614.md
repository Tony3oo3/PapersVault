## Problem

VLMs applied to autonomous driving lack structured spatial-temporal representations: they cannot perceive 3D geometric layout, selectively attend to safety-critical agents, or forecast how the scene will evolve. Existing VLA driving methods either generate trajectories in text form or use diffusion decoders without explicit world-state modeling, leaving the model without the anticipatory understanding needed for safe planning in complex, long-tail scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-frame front-view camera images (processed by a 300M InternViT encoder), a natural language driving instruction, ego-vehicle state, and historical trajectory. It produces a continuous driving trajectory via a DiT diffusion planner, alongside auxiliary outputs: a 3D occupancy grid (current and future), 3D bounding-box states for safety-critical agents (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) at both current and future timesteps, and a short-term ego-pose goal approximately 4 seconds ahead. The <world> queries are initialized by a dedicated encoder that fuses ego state, historical trajectory, and visual features before being processed by the VLM.

## Method

SGDrive introduces a scene-agent-goal hierarchy of special <world> tokens appended to the VLM's multimodal embeddings, each supervised by a dedicated head: a VAE decoder reconstructs occupancy geometry, a DETR-style set-prediction head detects safety-critical agents, and an MLP head regresses the short-term driving goal. A blockwise structured attention mask prevents cross-category information leakage while allowing temporal attention within each knowledge type, preserving specialized representations. The VLM backbone (InternVL3-2B with Qwen2.5 LLM) is trained in two stages: first, supervised fine-tuning jointly optimizes VQA, occupancy, agent detection, and goal prediction; second, the frozen VLM serves as a world model conditioning a DiT diffusion planner that denoises waypoints from a prior grounded in the <world> query embeddings. An optional reinforcement fine-tuning stage further improves collision-avoidance metrics.

## Results

On the NAVSIM v1 navtest benchmark, SGDrive-2B (camera-only, SFT) achieves a state-of-the-art PDMS of 87.4, surpassing the previous best VLM-based method ReCogDrive-8B (86.8) by 0.6 points and larger general-purpose VLMs (InternVL3-8B, QwenVL2.5-8B) by 4.1 points. With reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT), SGDrive-2B reaches 91.1 PDMS, the best overall score in the table. The method also achieves the highest NC (98.6) and TTC (95.4) among all camera-only methods, validating its collision-avoidance capability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among VLM-based camera-only methods (SFT), SGDrive-2B (87.4 PDMS) outperforms ReCogDrive-8B (86.8) by 0.6, ReCogDrive-2B (86.5) by 0.9, ImagiDrive-A-7B (86.0) by 1.4, and the larger general-purpose VLMs InternVL3-8B and QwenVL2.5-8B (both 83.3) by 4.1 points. Against non-VLM end-to-end baselines that use both camera and LiDAR, SGDrive-2B trails WoTE (88.3) and DiffusionDrive (88.1) by 0.9 and 0.7 PDMS respectively, but surpasses ARTEMIS (87.0) by 0.4 and Epona (86.2) by 1.2, all while using only camera input. Under RFT, SGDrive-2B achieves 91.1 PDMS, beating ReCogDrive-2B (90.8) by 0.3, ReCogDrive-8B (90.5) by 0.6, and AutoVLA-3B (89.1) by 2.0. Notably, SGDrive achieves the best NC (98.6) and TTC (95.4) scores among all camera-only methods in both SFT and RFT settings, confirming its superior collision-avoidance capability. The 2B parameter model outperforming 8B general-purpose VLMs by 4.1 PDMS highlights the effectiveness of the hierarchical world-knowledge structuring over simply scaling model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGDrive extends the VLA-for-driving line initiated by ReCogDrive [29] and EMMA [16], which map camera inputs through a VLM to driving actions, but departs from them by explicitly decomposing the VLM's internal representation into a scene-agent-goal hierarchy rather than relying on the model's generalist features. It borrows occupancy supervision from OccVLA [37] and Occ3D [51] for geometric scene layout, DETR-style set prediction [4] for safety-critical agents, and DiT [41] for trajectory generation, but the key architectural departure is the blockwise structured attention mask that prevents cross-level information leakage between the three knowledge channels. Compared to prior VLM-based planners (DriveLM, ORION, ReCogDrive) that produce trajectories directly from holistic VLM outputs, SGDrive argues that the missing ingredient is an explicit, disentangled spatial-temporal world model that the VLM must learn to forecast before planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 2B-parameter model outperforms 8B generalist VLMs by over 4 PDMS, suggesting that structured driving-specific knowledge extraction is more effective than raw model scale for safe planning — a useful signal for deployment-constrained settings. The hierarchical scene-agent-goal decomposition also provides interpretability: each subquery can be inspected to diagnose failure modes (e.g., missed agent vs. wrong goal). Open questions include whether the occupancy-based geometric supervision is the optimal proxy for 3D spatial understanding (vs. explicit depth or BEV features), whether the non-reactive NAVSIM setting masks limitations in reactive multi-agent interactions, and whether the structured attention mask generalizes to more than three knowledge levels or longer prediction horizons.
