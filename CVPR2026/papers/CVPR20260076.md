## Problem

The principles underlying the construction of high-quality vision-language (VL) reasoning training datasets remain poorly understood. Specifically, it is unclear how the choice of context (image-question) sources affects downstream reasoning, which targeted data interventions actually improve performance beyond visual perturbations and difficulty filtering, and whether VL reasoning data should be scaled along specific axes such as questions per image or CoTs per image-question pair.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input and produces a structured chain-of-thought trace followed by a final answer. Each CoT is composed of two parts: an image caption (understanding component) and a step-by-step solution (problem-solving component), averaging ~600 words (780 tokens) in length. The HONEYBEE dataset contains 2.5M training instances spanning 28K unique images and 350K unique questions, with 1.5M VL instances and 1M text-only reasoning instances.

## Method

The paper introduces a three-stage data curation pipeline: (1) context curation, where six source datasets are ranked by downstream accuracy and found that mixing sources degrades performance relative to the best single source (ViRL); (2) data interventions, where perception-enhancing strategies (caption-and-solve, visual perturbation, perceptual redundancy, shallow perception, text-rich images) and problem-solving strategies (text-only reasoning, increased distractors, length/difficulty filtering) are tested, revealing that caption-and-solve (+3.3pp) and text-only reasoning augmentation (+7.5pp) yield the largest gains; (3) multi-axis scaling, where images, synthetic questions per image, and CoTs per image-question pair are independently scaled, all showing consistent improvements. The final HONEYBEE dataset is built on ViRL by generating 16 CoTs per real pair (filtered for correctness), 14 new questions per image with 4 CoTs each (majority-voted answers), prepending image captions, and merging with 1M text-only reasoning instances. Models (PLM 1B–8B) are trained via supervised fine-tuning using Llama4-Scout (109B) as the CoT generator, and a shared-caption decoding strategy is proposed for test-time scaling that reuses the image caption across N solution attempts, reducing inference tokens by 73% without accuracy loss.

## Results

PLM-HONEYBEE models achieve the highest average accuracy across all three model size categories (1B, 3B–4B, 7B–8B) on ten VL reasoning and perception benchmarks. At the 3B scale, PLM-HONEYBEE-3B reaches 46.2% average accuracy, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (42.6%) by 8.4 pp and the base PLM-3B (33.8%) by 24.8 pp. On MathVerse specifically, PLM-HONEYBEE-3B scores 42.8% versus 35.0% for Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (a 7.8 pp gain cited in the abstract). At the 8B scale, PLM-HONEYBEE-8B achieves 49.8% average, edging out Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (48.5%) by 2.7 pp and InternVL-3-8B-Instruct (45.1%) by 4.7 pp.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against InternVL-2.5 (Chen et al., 2024), PLM-HONEYBEE wins decisively at every scale: +8.6 pp at 1B (36.2 vs 27.6), +4.7 pp at 4B (46.2 vs 41.5), and +8.4 pp at 8B (49.8 vs 41.4). Against InternVL-3 (Zhu et al., 2025), the gap is largest at 1B (+7.9 pp, 36.2 vs 28.3) and moderate at 8B (+4.7 pp, 49.8 vs 45.1). Against Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025), PLM-HONEYBEE-3B leads by 8.4 pp on average (46.2 vs 42.6), with the biggest single-benchmark margin on MathVerse (+7.8 pp); at 8B the lead narrows to 2.7 pp (49.8 vs 48.5), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B still edges out on MMMU-Pro (37.1 vs 33.8). In the RL comparison (Table 5), PLM-HONEYBEE-3B-GRPO achieves 46.2% average, surpassing OpenVLThinker-v1.2-3B (Deng et al., 2025) at 42.3% by 9.2 pp, confirming that HONEYBEE SFT provides a stronger warm-start for subsequent RL training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic CoT data curation paradigm established for text-only LLM reasoning (e.g., OpenThoughts) to the vision-language setting, where prior datasets like Math-LLaVA, LLaVA-CoT, and R1-OneVision each made ad hoc choices about context sources and generation strategies without systematic comparison. It departs from prior VL reasoning data work by fixing training and evaluation protocols to isolate the effect of individual design decisions (context sourcing, targeted interventions, scaling axes), and by building on the self-consistency test-time scaling framework with a novel shared-caption decoding strategy that reduces inference cost. The generator model (Llama4-Scout) and student models (PLMs) follow the standard knowledge-distillation setup common in the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HONEYBEE provides the first large-scale, systematically curated VL reasoning dataset (2.5M examples, 350K unique image-question pairs) with clear, reproducible recipes, and the controlled ablations yield actionable guidance: most intuitively-motivated interventions (visual perturbation, text-rich images, perceptual filtering, difficulty balancing) actually degrade performance, while the simple additions of image captions and text-only reasoning data drive the largest gains. The 73% token reduction in test-time scaling via shared captions is a practical efficiency win for deployment. Open questions include whether these curation principles transfer to multi-image reasoning, non-mathematical VL tasks (e.g., general VQA), and whether the observed scaling trends (not yet saturated at 2.5M) continue at larger scales.
