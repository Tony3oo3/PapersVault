## Problem

State-of-the-art vision-language models for earth observation produce language-only reasoning that cannot ground spatial analysis in pixel-accurate visual evidence, leading to failures on tasks like area quantification and boundary detection. Additionally, existing multi-modal reasoning methods rely on coarse-grained representations (bounding boxes, crops) that are inadequate for the continuous spatial distributions in EO imagery, and no unified framework handles both multi-sensor (optical+SAR) and multi-temporal reasoning with pixel-level grounding.

## Input / Output

TerraScope accepts one or more EO images (optical, SAR, or temporal sequences) alongside a natural-language question, processed through InternVL3's dynamic tiling pipeline that splits images into sub-tiles of s×s=16 tokens per patch. At each reasoning step, the model outputs a segmentation mask at the token-grid resolution, from which masked visual tokens (those with >50% mask coverage) are extracted and re-injected as visual context for subsequent text generation. The final output is an interleaved sequence of textual reasoning steps, segmentation masks, and a final answer, with the mask decoder operating independently per temporal frame when multi-temporal indicators ("Image: t_i") are generated.

## Method

TerraScope builds on InternVL3 with an added pixel-level segmentation decoder, creating a dual-decoder system where the language model autoregressively generates text and [SEG] trigger tokens, and the mask decoder predicts segmentation masks upon detecting [SEG]. The predicted mask is resized to the vision encoder's token grid, and visual tokens under the mask are projected into the LLM's embedding space and fed back via the KV cache to condition subsequent reasoning. For optical-SAR pairs, a text-guided cross-attention mechanism computes per-token relevance scores for each modality and selects the higher-scoring token at each spatial position, enabling adaptive modality switching (e.g., SAR for cloud-covered regions). Training proceeds in two stages: grounding pretraining on 2M referring-expression segmentation pairs (mask decoder only, lr=2e-5), followed by instruction tuning on 1M Terra-CoT samples with LoRA on the LLM (lr=1e-5) and a combined objective of cross-entropy language loss plus Dice and pixel-wise segmentation loss (λ=0.5).

## Results

On TerraScope-Bench (optical-only), TerraScope (8B) achieves an average score of 68.9%, substantially outperforming the best general VLM (Qwen3-VL-Think at 43.3%) and the best EO-specific VLM (EarthMind at 42.1%). On Landsat30AU, TerraScope reaches 73.9% average (vs. 65.0% for Qwen3-VL-Think), and on DisasterM3 it achieves 46.5% (vs. 32.5% for Qwen3-VL-Think). Fine-tuning general VLMs on Terra-CoT alone yields intermediate gains (InternVL3: 54.9%, GLM-4.1V-Think: 59.6% on TerraScope-Bench), confirming that both the pixel-grounded architecture and the dataset contribute to the final performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TerraScope outperforms all 10 baselines across all three benchmarks. On TerraScope-Bench, it leads the strongest general VLM (Qwen3-VL-Think, 43.3%) by 25.6 points and the best EO-specific model (EarthMind, 42.1%) by 26.8 points, with the largest margins on Distance Measurement (65.9 vs. 25.6) and Building Change Estimation (52.1 vs. 20.8). On Landsat30AU, TerraScope gains 8.9 points over Qwen3-VL-Think (73.9 vs. 65.0) and 5.9 over the fine-tuned GLM-4.1V-Think (73.9 vs. 68.0). On DisasterM3, the advantage is even more pronounced: 46.5% vs. 32.5% for Qwen3-VL-Think and 7.7 points over fine-tuned GLM-4.1V-Think (46.5 vs. 38.8). Notably, EO-specific models (GeoChat, TeoChat, LHRS-Bot, EarthDial) do not significantly outperform general VLMs, suggesting that domain-adapted training alone is insufficient without pixel-grounded reasoning architecture. Fine-tuning on Terra-CoT alone (without the dual-decoder architecture) recovers part of the gap but still trails TerraScope by 9.3–14.0 points on TerraScope-Bench, confirming the architectural contribution is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

TerraScope extends the 'thinking with images' paradigm (GRIT, DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus) from natural images to earth observation by replacing coarse-grained spatial representations (bounding boxes, crops) with pixel-level segmentation masks interleaved into the reasoning chain. It builds on InternVL3 as its base VLM and departs from prior EO-specific VLMs (GeoChat, EarthDial, SkySenseGPT) that focus on perception tasks without grounding reasoning in precise visual evidence. The text-guided cross-attention mechanism for adaptive optical-SAR modality selection is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior multi-sensor EO models, which typically concatenate or average features without spatially selective fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first benchmark and training dataset specifically targeting pixel-grounded geospatial reasoning, filling a clear gap where existing EO benchmarks only assess coarse perception. The finding that even GPT-4o and EO-specific models achieve near-random performance on fine-grained spatial tasks underscores that current VLMs lack genuine pixel-level understanding for EO. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution imagery (<5 m), whether the 1M Terra-CoT dataset suffices for truly complex multi-step reasoning, and whether the dual-decoder architecture can be extended to video or 3D geospatial data. The dual evaluation protocol (answer accuracy + mask IoU) sets a methodological standard for future pixel-grounded reasoning work.
