## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models effectively remove narrow, specific concepts (e.g., Pikachu, Elon Musk) but degrade significantly on broad, multi-faceted concepts like 'sexual' or 'violent', which manifest through diverse visual forms and textual expressions. The root cause is that prior approaches implicitly model all concepts with a single or unified embedding direction, an assumption that breaks down for high-variance concepts spanning multiple semantic modes.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a user text prompt and a pre-computed bank of textual concept prototypes (soft prompts of L learnable token embeddings) for the target concept. It selects the single most relevant prototype via cosine similarity with the prompt embedding (subject to a threshold τ) and injects it as a negative signal into the denoising loop. The output is a standard-resolution image (512×512 for SD v1.4) generated via DDIM sampling (30 steps, guidance scale 7.5) with the target concept suppressed while preserving all unrelated prompt content. No model weights are modified at any point.

## Method

The method constructs concept prototypes in two stages. First, it generates paired images with and without the target concept from N prompts, computes all pairwise CLIP image-embedding differences, and applies k-means clustering to yield K image-space prototypes, each capturing a distinct semantic mode of the concept. Second, it transfers each image prototype into text space by optimizing a soft prompt (L learnable token embeddings) to maximize cosine similarity with its image counterpart through the frozen CLIP text encoder (2,000 iterations, lr=5e-2). During inference, the most relevant textual prototype is selected by cosine similarity with the user prompt and inserted as an additional negative term in classifier-free guidance: ε̃ = ε(z_t) + α(ε(z_t,c) − ε(z_t)) − β(ε(z_t, p_T^(k*)) − ε(z_t)), effectively steering the denoising trajectory away from the target concept's semantic region without touching model parameters.

## Results

On the I2P broad-concept erasure benchmark (Q16 detector), the proposed method achieves the best overall inappropriate-content rate of 5.2%, outperforming the strongest baseline TRCE (5.7%) and substantially beating RECE (8.5%), Safree (8.8%), AdaVD (10.0%), and ESD (12.2%). Under adversarial attacks (Ring-a-Bell, P4D, UnlearnDiff), the method achieves ASR of 6.7%, 14.5%, and 13.3% respectively, with a FID of 45.1 for knowledge preservation. On narrow-concept erasure (style + IP), the method attains the highest Aesthetic Score (5.89) and the lowest LPIPS on unerased concepts (0.18), indicating superior preservation of unrelated semantics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best overall Q16 detection rate (5.2%) on I2P, beating TRCE (5.7%) by 0.5 pp and RECE (8.5%) by 3.3 pp; it ties TRCE on Sexual (1.7%) but loses on Illegal Activity (5.5% vs. 3.4%). Against training-free baselines, the method dominates: Safree (8.8%) and AdaVD (10.0%) are outperformed by 3.6 and 4.8 pp respectively, while SLD (35.6%) shows virtually no erasure benefit. Under adversarial attacks, the method ties TRCE on Ring-a-Bell (6.7%) but trails on P4D (14.5% vs. 2.0%) and UnlearnDiff (13.3% vs. 7.7%), though it maintains a lower FID (45.1 vs. 48.7), indicating better knowledge preservation; it substantially outperforms Safree (22.4%/38.0%/28.2%) and RECE (9.8%/35.1%/15.5%) on all three attack suites. On narrow-concept erasure (style + IP), the method achieves the highest Aesthetic Score (5.89) and the lowest LPIPS on unerased concepts (0.18 vs. 0.33–0.74 for baselines), confirming superior preservation of unrelated semantics while ESD, RECE, and AdaVD all show higher LPIPS_u values indicating collateral damage to non-target content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance framework (Dhariwal & Nichol) and latent diffusion model architecture (Rombach et al.) by introducing a multi-prototype negative guidance mechanism. It departs from prior concept erasure methods (ESD, RECE, TRCE, SLD, Safree, AdaVD) that implicitly model all concepts—broad or narrow—as a single directional signal in embedding space. The key conceptual departure is the hypothesis that broad, multi-faceted concepts (e.g., 'sexual', 'violent') occupy multiple compact regions in the embedding space, requiring a set of k-means-clustered prototypes rather than one unified direction. The cross-modal transfer from image-space to text-space prototypes via CLIP alignment is a practical bridge that keeps the method fully training-free and compatible with standard LDM conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: existing erasure methods reliably remove narrow concepts (specific IPs, named individuals) but fail on broad safety-critical categories, leaving a significant content-safety hole in deployed T2I systems. The training-free design and demonstrated compatibility across SD v1.4, SDXL, and SD3.5 make the approach deployable without retraining, which is critical for production pipelines. Open questions remain around how to handle truly novel semantic modes not captured by the finite prototype set, the sensitivity of erasure quality to the prototype count hyperparameter, and whether the method's reasonable (but not designed-for) adversarial robustness can be made principled. The modest but consistent gains over strong baselines like TRCE and Safree suggest the prototype formulation is a useful inductive bias, though the field may still need fundamentally different mechanisms for the most adversarially robust erasure.
