## Problem

Tracking 3D human motion from egocentric multi-camera headsets is challenged by rapid 6-DoF egomotion, partial visibility across multiple cameras with frequent hand-offs, and the scarcity of 3D-annotated training data for specific device configurations. Existing monocular or static-rig methods assume slow or fixed cameras, cannot efficiently fuse temporally asynchronous multi-view observations, and inherit scale ambiguity, making them brittle on dynamic egocentric captures.

## Input / Output

LAMP consumes a posed multi-camera video clip (K cameras over a 4-second / 120-frame window at 30 Hz) together with known 6-DoF camera poses and intrinsics/extrinsics. Internally, per-camera 2D keypoints (17 MSCOCO joints from ViTPose) are back-projected into 6-D Plücker rays plus a confidence score, forming a tensor Φ ∈ R^{T×K×J×7} in a gravity-aligned local world frame. The output is per-person, per-timestamp SMPL motion parameters (69-D pose, 10-D shape, SO(3) root rotation, R³ root translation) expressed in metric 3D world coordinates, enabling direct evaluation without SIM(3) alignment.

## Method

LAMP employs an early world-space ray-lifting paradigm that factors out headset egomotion before any spatio-temporal reasoning: 2D keypoints from all cameras are unprojected and posed into 3D rays in a gravity-aligned frame, so the network only needs to learn a human-motion prior in world space. A spatio-temporal transformer (LAMP-Net, 3 encoder-decoder blocks, 512-dim) performs self-attention jointly over joint and frame dimensions on the ray-cloud sequence, with a multi-level cross-attention decoder that queries each encoder block for improved temporal stability. Training is fully supervised on the Nymeria dataset using simulated 2D keypoint projections with extensive augmentation (noisy detections, occlusions, varied headset motion), enabling sim-to-real transfer and re-training for arbitrary camera layouts without new video data. At inference, a sliding-window strategy averages overlapping predictions to reduce jitter, trading up to T−1 frames of latency for smoother output.

## Results

On the Nymeria dataset (egocentric, dynamic head motion), multi-camera LAMP achieves 54.8 mm MPJPE, 113.3 mm W-MPJPE, and 0.05% RTE, substantially outperforming PromptHMR (109.2 mm MPJPE, 246.0 mm W-MPJPE, 0.11% RTE). Even in monocular mode, LAMP beats PromptHMR on every metric (92.3 vs. 109.2 mm MPJPE; 203.4 vs. 246.0 mm W-MPJPE; 23.8 vs. 114.1 jitter). On EMDB (slowly moving cameras), LAMP-mono achieves the best world-space localization (0.2% RTE, 165.1 mm W-MPJPE, 4.6 jitter) but trails PromptHMR on local pose metrics (82.3 vs. 68.1 mm MPJPE; 77.8 vs. 63.9 mm WA-MPJPE₁₀₀), which the authors attribute to the ray-based representation sacrificing pixel-level detail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PromptHMR [79] on Nymeria (the paper's target egocentric setting), LAMP-mv wins decisively on every metric: 54.8 vs. 109.2 mm MPJPE (49% reduction), 113.3 vs. 246.0 mm W-MPJPE, and 21.8 vs. 114.1 jitter; even LAMP-mono outperforms PromptHMR on all metrics. On EMDB, the picture is mixed: LAMP-mono achieves superior world-space accuracy (0.2% vs. 0.4% RTE; 165.1 vs. 278.1 mm W-MPJPE; 4.6 vs. 16.3 jitter) but lags PromptHMR on local pose (82.3 vs. 68.1 mm MPJPE; 77.8 vs. 63.9 mm WA-MPJPE₁₀₀), which the authors attribute to the ray-based input discarding pixel-level appearance cues. Against WHAM [68] and GVHMR [67] on EMDB, LAMP-mono dominates on RTE (0.2% vs. 4.1% and 1.9%), jitter (4.6 vs. 21.0 and 16.5), and foot skating (3.2 vs. 4.4 and 3.5), while trailing on MPJPE and PA-MPJPE. Against GEM [36], LAMP-mono wins on RTE (0.2% vs. 0.9%) and jitter (4.6 vs. 17.7) but loses on MPJPE (82.3 vs. 73.0) and WA-MPJPE₁₀₀ (77.8 vs. 69.5). The multi-view advantage is most pronounced on Nymeria, where adding cameras drops MPJPE from 92.3 to 54.8 mm and W-MPJPE from 203.4 to 113.3 mm, confirming that the early ray-lifting formulation effectively exploits multi-view geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMP departs from prior world-coordinate 3D human pose methods (WHAM, GVHMR, TRAM, PromptHMR) that either jointly learn camera motion with human motion, perform late composition from camera-space predictions, or rely on non-real-time optimization. Its core conceptual departure is an 'early ray lifting' paradigm: 2D keypoints are back-projected into 3D Plücker rays in a gravity-aligned world frame *before* spatio-temporal reasoning, explicitly factoring out the known 6-DoF headset motion. This builds on the ray-based representation idea from Ray3D (static rigs) and the spatio-temporal transformer architecture from Humans in 4D, but repurposes them for a fundamentally different setting—moving, multi-camera, egocentric input with known poses—enabling a clean separation between 2D detection and 3D motion fitting that prior methods do not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LAMP is the first method to deliver real-time, metric-scale multi-person tracking from egocentric multi-camera headsets, a capability directly relevant to AR/smart-glasses social understanding. The early-lifting formulation enables training entirely from simulated 2D projections of existing motion datasets, sidestepping the data-scarcity bottleneck that has kept most research in the monocular, un-posed regime; the demonstrated cross-device generalization (Gen1-trained model handling Gen2 hardware) is practically significant. Key limitations include dependence on accurate 6-DoF tracking, the requirement for multi-view input (ruling out phone captures), and the observed trade-off where local pose accuracy (MPJPE) lags pixel-based methods on static-camera benchmarks while global/world-space metrics improve substantially—suggesting the ray abstraction discards fine-grained appearance cues that could be recovered with additional pixel-derived features.
