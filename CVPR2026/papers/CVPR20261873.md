## Problem

Existing knowledge distillation methods for dense prediction apply uniform spatial weighting, treating all pixel locations equally during feature alignment. This is inadequate for tiny-dense object segmentation (e.g., thousands of touching rock fragments), where geometric precision at boundaries and junctions is critical but homogeneous interiors are not. The result is that distilled students learn coarse semantic alignment while losing the boundary fidelity needed to separate densely packed instances.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (512×512 for ADE20K, 1024×1024 for RockFrag) and optional prompts for promptable architectures, producing per-pixel segmentation masks in [0,1]^{H×W}. During training, a frozen teacher network provides intermediate C-channel feature maps from which the structure-importance map is computed; at inference, only the student network is executed with no additional modules, so the output is a standard segmentation mask with zero extra overhead.

## Method

SARD computes a structure-importance map W(i) from frozen teacher features using three cues: boundary salience E(i) (structure-tensor eigenvalue difference), geometric complexity C(i) (eigenvalue geometric mean), and local feature dispersion D(i) (mean L2 deviation within a 15×15 window), combined as S(i)=β_e·E+β_c·C+β_d·D and normalized over the spatial domain. The representation loss then weights both a feature-consistency term (L2 between 1×1-conv-projected teacher and student features) and a denoising score-matching term (a lightweight head predicts Gaussian noise injected into teacher features, encouraging the student to match the score of the noisy teacher distribution) by W(i), so gradients concentrate at boundaries, junctions, and crowded regions. A standard Dice+BCE segmentation loss is added when ground-truth masks are available, and the entire objective is optimized in a single joint stage with only student-side parameters updated.

## Results

On the most challenging tiny-dense benchmark (RockFrag), SARD with a Swin-L→Swin-T pair achieves 60.2% mIoU and 47.3% bIoU, improving over the strongest baseline CWD by +4.3 mIoU and +6.7 bIoU. On standard benchmarks, SARD reaches 80.3% mIoU / 76.1% bIoU on Cityscapes and 46.9% / 40.8% on ADE20K, surpassing CWD by +1.4/+2.3 and +2.1/+2.2 respectively. A ResNet-50 student distilled from Swin-L achieves 58.6% mIoU on RockFrag with 7.7× fewer parameters and 9× higher throughput than the teacher, recovering ~70% of the teacher–student gap without any additional inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARD outperforms all four distillation baselines (Vanilla KD, CWD, DKD, MGD) on every dataset and both teacher–student configurations. The largest margin is against CWD on RockFrag (+4.3 mIoU, +6.7 bIoU for Swin-L→Swin-T), where boundary-critical tiny fragments amplify the benefit of structure-aware weighting; on Cityscapes and ADE20K the gains over CWD are more modest (+1.4/+2.1 mIoU) but bIoU consistently exceeds mIoU gains, confirming the boundary-focused design. Against DKD and MGD, SARD leads by 1.7–3.9 mIoU on RockFrag and 1.2–1.8 mIoU on Cityscapes. In the efficiency comparison (Table 4), SARD-distilled ViT-T (5.7M params) reaches 59.4% mIoU / 46.8% bIoU on RockFrag, beating FastSAM-s (53.8/39.5), MobileSAM (52.4/38.2), EfficientSAM-Ti (54.6/40.8), and SegFormer-B1 (55.1/41.5) by 4.3–7.0 mIoU at equal or lower parameter counts, demonstrating that structure-aware distillation yields better tiny-dense accuracy than architecture-specific compression alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARD extends the knowledge distillation lineage for dense prediction (Hinton et al. → FitNets → Structured KD → CWD) by replacing uniform spatial weighting with a structure-importance map derived from structure tensor analysis and local feature dispersion. The structure tensor component borrows directly from the edge-detection literature (HED, BiDCN), while the denoising score-matching term repurposes a diffusion-model objective for feature distribution alignment. The key conceptual departure from CWD, DKD, and MGD is the explicit argument that tiny-dense scenes have spatially non-uniform information density, so distillation losses should be modulated by local geometric complexity rather than applied uniformly across all pixels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 7.7× parameter reduction and 9× throughput gain over the teacher while recovering ~70% of the performance gap, with zero additional inference cost, makes this directly deployable for real-time industrial segmentation. The RockFrag benchmark, while small (1,600 images), addresses a genuine gap in tiny-dense segmentation evaluation that existing benchmarks (Cityscapes, ADE20K) do not stress-test. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted structure map (Sobel gradients + fixed window radius) can be replaced by a learned module, whether the approach transfers to 3D or video segmentation, and whether the gains persist when the teacher and student have very different inductive biases (e.g., CNN teacher → ViT student). The reliance on a frozen, pre-trained teacher also limits applicability in settings where no large model is available.
