## Problem

Existing video-to-4D methods are limited to specific input modalities and object categories, rely on slow per-scene optimization loops (30–45 minutes), and produce outputs that lack the topology consistency and rig-free properties needed for production pipelines like texturing and retargeting. No prior feed-forward model generates animated 3D meshes with constant topology from diverse inputs at practical speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262409/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a monocular video (16 frames), but the masked generative design also accepts a 3D mesh plus a video, a 3D mesh plus a text prompt, an image plus a text prompt, or text alone. The output is an animated 3D mesh expressed as a fixed reference mesh (V, F) together with per-frame vertex displacement fields δ_k, so that (V + δ_k, F) approximates the object at each timestep. Because topology is constant and no rig is required, textures and other per-vertex attributes transfer automatically across the animation.

## Method

ActionMesh is a two-stage feed-forward model built on the TripoSG rectified-flow 3D latent diffusion backbone. Stage I (temporal 3D diffusion) inflates the self-attention layers so that tokens from all N frames attend jointly in a single attention pass, and adds rotary positional embeddings encoding relative frame offsets to suppress inter-frame jitter; a masked-generation mechanism keeps a subset of latents noise-free (flow step 0) so the model can condition on a known reference mesh. Stage II (temporal 3D autoencoder) reuses the frozen 3D encoder to map each independent mesh to a shape latent, then a modified decoder ingests the full latent sequence and predicts, via cross-attention over query points augmented with normals, the per-vertex deformation of the reference mesh for every frame. The entire pipeline runs in ~2 minutes on a single H100 GPU, roughly 10× faster than prior optimization-based methods, while achieving state-of-the-art chamfer distances on the newly introduced ActionBench (128 Objaverse scenes) and the Consistent4D benchmark.

## Results

On the newly introduced ActionBench (128 animated Objaverse scenes), ActionMesh achieves CD-3D of 0.053, CD-4D of 0.081, and CD-M of 0.148, outperforming all baselines by 5%, 35%, and 39% respectively over the best prior method on each metric. Inference takes only 2 minutes on a single H100 GPU, roughly 10× faster than the next-fastest methods (15 min) and ~17× faster than optimization-based approaches (35 min). Qualitative evaluation on Consistent4D confirms superior geometric fidelity and temporal coherence, and the model generalizes to real-world DAVIS videos despite being trained solely on synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActionMesh wins on all three metrics against every baseline. Against the closest per-frame image-to-3D baselines (TripoSG and TRELLIS), it improves CD-3D marginally (0.053 vs 0.056/0.065) but dramatically on CD-4D (0.081 vs 0.184/0.181), confirming that temporal consistency is the key differentiator. Against the strongest video-to-4D baseline LIM, ActionMesh reduces CD-4D from 0.126 to 0.081 (35% improvement) and CD-M from 0.243 to 0.148 (39% improvement) while running 7.5× faster. DreamMesh4D and V2M4, both optimization-based methods requiring 35 minutes, are outperformed on every metric by wide margins (e.g., CD-M: 0.148 vs 0.265 and 0.616). The concurrent feed-forward method ShapeGen4D is the closest competitor on CD-3D (0.056 vs 0.053) but lags substantially on temporal metrics (CD-4D 0.170 vs 0.081; CD-M 0.348 vs 0.148), attributed to its lack of explicit topology enforcement. Qualitatively on Consistent4D, LIM and DreamMesh4D produce coarse geometries, V2M4 and ShapeGen4D show artifacts and temporal drift, while ActionMesh maintains the highest geometric fidelity with strong temporal coherence across all frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActionMesh extends the 3DShape2VecSet latent diffusion paradigm by adding a temporal axis to a pretrained 3D diffusion backbone (TripoSG), drawing the inflated-attention mechanism from MVDream's multi-view generation and the masked-generation strategy from CAT3D. It departs from prior video-to-4D methods (DreamMesh4D, LIM, V2M4) that rely on per-scene optimization loops, and from concurrent feed-forward approaches (L4GM, 4DGT, ShapeGen4D) that produce Gaussians or per-frame independent meshes without enforcing a single globally consistent topology. The two-stage design—temporal 3D diffusion for synchronized shape latents followed by a temporal 3D autoencoder for topology-consistent deformation—repurposes pretrained 3D autoencoders rather than training a new 4D representation from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× speedup (2 min vs. 15–45 min) and topology-consistent, rig-free output make animated mesh generation practical for production pipelines (texturing, retargeting, game assets) where per-scene optimization is prohibitive. The introduced ActionBench benchmark (128 Objaverse-based scenes) fills a gap in reproducible quantitative evaluation for video-to-4D. Open questions remain around topological changes (the fixed-connectivity assumption) and strong occlusions, and the paper suggests topology-aware latent updates as a promising direction. The emergent motion-transfer capability without explicit training hints at broader geometric motion priors learnable from video corpora.
