## Problem

Existing embodied AI benchmarks and floorplan generators are confined to single-floor, residential-scale environments, lacking vertical structural consistency (aligned stair/elevator cores, cross-floor portals) and open-vocabulary building types. This prevents the development and evaluation of realistic long-horizon, cross-floor tasks such as multi-story delivery or hospital supply transport, where agents must reason over vertical connectivity, structural interactions, and extended spatial memory.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260210/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language building description (e.g., 'a six-story office building with a hospital wing') and an outer footprint polygon as input, and produces a set of vertically aligned, semantically grounded vector floorplans (orthogonal polygon partitions per floor) that are directly instantiable in AI2-THOR. The output 3D scene includes architectural elements, doors, placed objects, multi-flight stairwells, and functional elevators, and can be exported to Blender or NVIDIA Isaac Sim. A post-hoc Task-Semantic Scene Editing Agent accepts open-vocabulary natural-language commands to modify object placement and scene contents without regenerating the building structure.

## Method

MANSION is a hierarchical multi-agent pipeline orchestrated by LangGraph that decouples MLLM-driven semantic planning from a deterministic geometric solver. Floorplan generation is formalized as a verifiable constrained search over candidate partitions (L* = argmax Score(L;w) s.t. Topo(L,G)=true), where a building-level MLLM node produces cross-floor functional zones and per-floor bubble diagrams, and a cutting MLLM node proposes growth seeds on a rasterized free region. A topology-aware single-cut solver (a variant of Lopes-style constrained growth) then iteratively partitions rooms along the topological front, enforcing vertical-core alignment as a first-class hard constraint across all floors. Scene instantiation follows a two-level progressive design: a building-level 'chief designer' fixes global visual style, while per-floor room cards drive material, opening, and object placement under reachability and quality-first pruning. The ecosystem extends AI2-THOR with stair/elevator assets and cross-floor skill APIs (UseStairs, CallElevator, UseElevator) that handle scene-graph transitions, enabling seamless multi-floor agent navigation.

## Results

On the ResPlan-1K benchmark (complex, multi-room layouts), MANSION's constrained growth solver achieves 76.74% micro-IoU and 76.64% macro-IoU under the manual-annotation (MA) setting, substantially outperforming ChatHouseDiffusion-MA (33.49%/25.39%) by over 40 points. On the standard T2D residential benchmark, MANSION-MA (81.67%/80.66%) is comparable to CHD-MA (82.81%/79.04%), confirming parity on in-distribution data. For object placement, MANSION achieves 100% floor-object reachability and zero collision pairs across all four room types, while earning the highest user-study preference scores for realism (up to 96.1%) and layout quality (up to 80.4%). On embodied cross-floor tasks, all tested agents (COME, BUMBLE, BUMBLE+object-type) drop to 0% success on the four-floor setting, with the best two-floor result at 60% (BUMBLE with object-type information).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary floorplan-generation competitor is ChatHouseDiffusion (CHD) [33]. On T2D (residential, ≤8 rooms), MANSION-MA (81.67% micro-IoU) is within 1.1 points of CHD-MA (82.81%), a near-tie. However, on the harder ResPlan-1K benchmark with >8 rooms and richer topology, MANSION-MA (76.74%) nearly doubles CHD-MA (33.49%), and even the end-to-end MLLM-driven variant (63.56% with Gemini-2.5-Pro) more than doubles CHD's zero-shot score (29.36%). The older image-based baselines (Obj-GAN, CogView, Imagen, original T2D) [27,11,36,25] are far behind on T2D (10–54% micro-IoU) and are not evaluated on ResPlan. For object placement, MANSION outperforms both LayoutGPT [13] and Holodeck [47] on all four room types: it achieves 100% reachability (vs. 65–99% for baselines), zero out-of-boundary objects and zero collision pairs, and the highest user-study layout scores (52.9–80.4% vs. 5.9–39.2% for Holodeck and 7.8–19.6% for LayoutGPT). For embodied cross-floor tasks, COME [50] and standard BUMBLE [39] achieve 20–40% success on single/double-floor settings but 0% on four floors; adding object-type information to BUMBLE improves two-floor success to 60%, yet all methods still fail completely on the four-floor benchmark, underscoring the difficulty of the proposed setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

MANSION sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) floorplan generation, where it departs from data-driven approaches (HouseDiffusion, ChatHouseDiffusion, Graph2Plan) that are confined to single-story residential layouts by introducing vertical structural alignment as a hard geometric constraint; (2) LLM-driven 3D scene synthesis (Holodeck, SceneCraft, SceneWeaver), where it adopts the LLM+rule-based placement paradigm but shifts from quantity-first to usability-first object placement with reachability guarantees; and (3) the AI2-THOR simulation platform, which it extends with cross-floor assets (stairs, elevators) and skill APIs that no prior version supported. The geometric core is a topology-aware variant of Lopes-style constrained growth, while the semantic layer is a multi-agent MLLM pipeline orchestrated via LangGraph, decoupling high-level planning from low-level geometry in a way that prior single-MLLM or single-diffusion-model approaches did not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MansionWorld fills a clear and previously unaddressed gap: no existing embodied AI benchmark supports multi-floor, building-scale tasks with vertical transit, yet real-world robotic applications (hospital supply transport, office package delivery) are inherently cross-floor. The complete failure of state-of-the-art agents (BUMBLE, COME) on four-floor tasks underscores that current planning and memory architectures are not ready for this setting, making the benchmark a useful stress test. Open questions include whether the MLLM-dependent pipeline scales to buildings beyond 10 stories, whether the constrained growth solver handles highly irregular footprints, and whether the Task-Semantic Scene Editing Agent can generalize to task types far from its training distribution. The practical reusability of a single building across many tasks via semantic editing is a promising direction that could reduce the cost of benchmark construction.
