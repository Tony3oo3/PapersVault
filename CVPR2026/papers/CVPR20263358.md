## Problem

Fine-tuning a text-to-image model with a few real examples to generate synthetic training data for fine-grained classification introduces a critical trade-off: the model overfits to the limited examples, losing its strong generative prior and producing low-diversity samples that reinforce spurious class-context associations. Prior methods either fine-tune for accuracy without preserving intra-class visual diversity or generate diverse samples without addressing unintended inter-class correlations, leaving a performance gap especially on datasets where T2I models have weak prior knowledge (e.g., aircraft subtypes).

## Input / Output

The method takes 5 or 10 real images per class along with their class labels and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model (v1.5 or v2.1-base) as input. A vision-language model (Qwen 2.5VL-7B) is used to extract per-image background and pose descriptions, which are stored in a caption bank. The output is 100 synthetic images per class, generated by the fine-tuned T2I model, which are combined with the real images (replicated to a 50/50 ratio) to train a downstream classifier (CLIP ViT-B/16, ResNet-50, or MAE ViT-B/16).

## Method

BOB operates in two stages. In Context Preservation, each training image is paired with a unique caption of the form 'a [descriptor] photo of a [classname] in the [background] background with the [pose] pose,' where background and pose are extracted by a captioning model; the T2I model's U-Net and CLIP text encoder attention layers are then fine-tuned via LoRA on these enriched image-text pairs, recovering the intra-class visual range lost by generic class-level prompts. In Context Marginalization, synthetic images are generated by randomly sampling background-pose pairs from the full caption bank independent of the class label, which the authors formalize as sampling from the intervention distribution P(X|do(Y)) via back-door adjustment P(X|do(Y)) = Σ_Z P(X|Y,Z)P(Z). This decouples class-relevant visual features from incidental context, preventing the model from reinforcing dataset-specific spurious correlations while preserving the T2I model's generative diversity.

## Results

On the 5-shot Aircraft dataset with a CLIP backbone, BOB (SD v2.1) achieves 57.37% accuracy, outperforming the best prior method DataDream (50.04%) by 7.33 percentage points. Across all three backbones (CLIP, ResNet-50, MAE) and four datasets, BOB outperforms the best existing method in 18 of 24 experimental settings, with gains up to 24.53% (MAE, 5-shot average). Notably, 5 real images augmented with BOB-generated synthetic data surpasses 10 real images alone on Aircraft, Car, and CUB (e.g., 60.31% vs. 55.48% on Aircraft with ResNet-50), effectively doubling the value of each real training example.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BOB consistently outperforms DataDream (Kim et al., ECCV 2024), the strongest prior method, by 5.73–7.33% on Aircraft, 2.26–2.67% on Car, and 4.70–4.14% on CUB in the 5-shot setting across backbones, while matching it within 1% on Pets where the baseline is already near saturation. Against Diff-II (Wang et al., CVPR 2025), the second-strongest baseline, BOB leads by 6.35–11.03% on Aircraft, 5.94–6.39% on Car, and 5.02–6.68% on CUB. The older methods RealGuidance (He et al., ICLR 2023) and Da-fusion (Trabucco et al., ICLR 2023) are substantially outperformed, with BOB exceeding them by 14–24% on Aircraft and 10–15% on CUB. Diff-Aug, Diff-Gen, and Diff-Mix (Wang et al., CVPR 2024) show the weakest gains over real-only baselines (0.5–2.6% average), while BOB achieves 7–24% average improvements. The only dataset where BOB does not clearly win is Pets, where all methods cluster within 1% due to strong pre-training overlap with the domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOB builds directly on DataDream's framework of fine-tuning a T2I model (via LoRA on U-Net and text encoder) with a few real examples to generate synthetic training data, but departs by enriching the text conditioning with class-agnostic attributes (background, pose) extracted via a vision-language captioning model. Unlike personalization methods (DreamBooth, Textual Inversion) that prioritize concept fidelity over diversity, or prior synthetic-data methods (Diff-Aug, Diff-Gen, Diff-Mix, Diff-II) that modify different pipeline components, BOB introduces a two-stage strategy: context preservation during fine-tuning and context marginalization during generation, formalized via a causal back-door adjustment. This positions the work at the intersection of diffusion-based data augmentation and causal inference for debiasing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: in three of four fine-grained benchmarks, 5 real images augmented with BOB-generated synthetic data outperforms 10 real images alone, effectively doubling the value of each scarce training example. However, the method is explicitly scoped to fine-grained recognition where class-agnostic attributes (background, pose) are well-defined and reliably extractable; the authors note that scaling to coarse-grained settings like ImageNet is challenging because classes become context-dependent and spurious correlations may actually simplify the task. The open problem of automatically discovering which attributes to preserve and marginalize, rather than manually specifying background and pose, remains unresolved and limits broader applicability.
