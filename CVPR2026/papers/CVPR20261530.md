## Problem

Standard multi-dimensional RoPE applied to image generation suffers from three structural limitations: fixed coordinate-indexed rotation planes misaligned with learned semantic subspaces, block-diagonal axis-wise independence that suppresses cross-axis interactions (e.g., diagonal or rotational couplings), and uniform positional mapping across all attention heads that prevents head-level specialization. These constraints degrade fine-grained spatial relation modeling, color fidelity, and object counting in generated images.

## Input / Output

HARoPE is a drop-in positional encoding module that replaces RoPE or APE inside existing transformer architectures without altering their input/output interfaces. For text-to-image generation (Flux, SD3-medium), it consumes text prompts and produces RGB images at 512×512, 1024×1024, or 2048×2048; for class-conditional generation (DiT-B/2), it takes class labels and produces images at 128–512 resolution; for image understanding (DeiT), it takes 192×192 or 224×224 RGB patches and outputs class logits. The only architectural change is the insertion of a per-head d×d linear transform before the rotary rotation, adding ~2.7–4.1% parameters.

## Method

HARoPE inserts a head-specific learnable linear transform A_h = U_h Σ_h V_h^T (SVD parameterization) immediately before the rotary mapping, so that queries become q'_h = R_m A_h^T q_h and keys become k'_h = R_n A_h^{-1} k_h. Applying the same A_h to both queries and keys rigorously preserves RoPE's relative-offset equivariance: attention scores depend on positions only through the relative offset n−m. The SVD factors have distinct roles—V_h rotates rotary planes to align with semantically meaningful directions and enables cross-axis mixing, Σ_h redistributes effective frequency capacity across subspaces, and U_h maps the enriched signal back to the model's native basis. Initialization sets A_h = I (U_h = V_h = I, Σ_h = I) for seamless compatibility with pretrained checkpoints; orthogonality of U_h and V_h is maintained via matrix exponentials of skew-symmetric matrices, and singular values are kept positive and near 1 via softplus with regularization to prevent norm explosion.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet generation with DiT-B/2 (1M steps), HARoPE achieves the best FID-50K of 8.90 and highest Inception Score of 127.01, with Precision 0.74 and Recall 0.55. On FLUX.1-dev text-to-image at 1024×1024, HARoPE improves GenEval Overall from 0.757 to 0.771 and DPG-Bench from 83.26 to 83.77 over the RoPE baseline. On SD3-medium (MS-COCO, 256×256), HARoPE reduces FID from 5.35 (RoPE) to 5.22. The method adds only ~2.7–4.1% parameters and FLOPs relative to the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HARoPE outperforms all four RoPE-based baselines on ImageNet generation. Against 2D-RoPE (Axial) [13], HARoPE improves FID-50K from 9.49 to 8.90 (−0.59) and IS from 124.78 to 127.01, while matching Precision (0.74) and improving Recall (0.54→0.55). Against STRING/Rethinking RoPE [28, 20], HARoPE achieves a 0.41 FID reduction (9.31→8.90) and a 1.92 IS gain (125.09→127.01). Against Vanilla RoPE [31], the gap is larger: FID drops from 9.81 to 8.90 and IS rises from 121.75 to 127.01. VideoRoPE [35] performs worst among RoPE variants (FID 10.86, IS 118.84), and HARoPE surpasses it by 1.96 FID and 8.17 IS. On FLUX text-to-image, HARoPE consistently beats RoPE on GenEval Overall (0.771 vs 0.757 at 1024px; 0.756 vs 0.735 at 2048px) and DPG-Bench (83.77 vs 83.26 at 1024px; 84.46 vs 84.12 at 2048px), with the largest gains in spatial-relation and attribute-binding sub-metrics at higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HARoPE extends the RoPE lineage (Su et al.) by inserting a head-wise, SVD-parameterized linear transformation immediately before the rotary mapping, addressing three specific limitations of standard multi-dimensional RoPE: fixed coordinate-indexed rotation planes misaligned with learned semantics, enforced axis-wise independence via block-diagonal structure, and uniform positional mapping across all attention heads. Conceptually, it builds on STRING's (Schenck et al.) demonstration that learnable matrices can generalize positional encodings, but narrows the design to a lightweight, per-head SVD that provably preserves RoPE's relative-offset equivariance. Unlike prior multi-dimensional extensions (RoPE-ViT, MRoPE, VideoRoPE) that modify frequency allocation or axis partitioning, HARoPE keeps the rotary spectrum fixed and instead learns a change of basis, making it a true drop-in replacement rather than a reparameterization of the rotation itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, low-overhead (~3–4% parameters) upgrade path for any RoPE-based transformer in image generation, validated across DiT, FLUX, and SD3 at multiple resolutions. The consistent gains at higher resolutions (e.g., Position score 0.370→0.455 at 2048px on FLUX) suggest the head-wise frequency rebalancing partially mitigates the known degradation of fixed-spectrum RoPE under resolution extrapolation. Key open questions remain: whether the static, post-training-fixed SVD matrices generalize to video, audio, or 3D content, and whether input-conditional or dynamically evolving transformations could further close the gap. The evaluation is confined to the image domain, and the 512px FLUX results show marginal or slightly negative deltas on some GenEval sub-metrics, tempering the universality of the gains.
