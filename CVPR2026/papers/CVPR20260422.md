## Problem

Existing caching-based diffusion acceleration methods rely on fixed or locally adaptive schedules that make short-sighted, greedy decisions about which timesteps to skip, neglecting the global structure of the denoising trajectory. This leads to error accumulation, irreversible drift, and visual artifacts, particularly in critical transition regions where feature dynamics change rapidly. No prior method formulates the selection of computation vs. caching steps as a holistic, trajectory-wide optimization problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260422/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

DPCache is a training-free wrapper around a pre-trained diffusion model (FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo, or DiT-XL/2) that takes the same inputs as the base model—text prompts for image/video generation or class labels for class-conditional generation—and produces outputs at the same resolutions (1024×1024 images, 640×480×65 videos, 256×256 images). The only additional requirement is a one-time calibration on approximately 10 samples to determine the optimal key-timestep schedule, after which the schedule is content-agnostic and reusable for arbitrary inputs without per-sample overhead.

## Method

DPCache formulates diffusion sampling acceleration as a global path planning problem over the denoising trajectory. During a calibration stage, it runs the full T-step denoising process on a small sample set and constructs a 3D Path-Aware Cost Tensor (PACT), where entry C[i,j,k] encodes the cumulative L1 prediction error of skipping from timestep j to k, conditioned on the preceding key timestep i—capturing the path-dependent nature of feature prediction. Dynamic programming then selects an optimal sequence of K key timesteps (K < T) that minimizes the total path cost, with the first M timesteps fixed to preserve early-stage denoising dynamics. At inference, the model performs full forward passes only at these precomputed key timesteps, while intermediate features are predicted via Taylor-series expansion from cached final-layer features, achieving up to 4.87× speedup with negligible memory overhead (+0.36 GB) since only the last layer's features are cached.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), DPCache(K=13) at 3.54× speedup achieves an ImageReward of 1.007, surpassing the full-step baseline (0.979) by +0.028, while DPCache(K=9) at 4.87× speedup reaches 0.958 ImageReward, outperforming the strongest prior method (SpeCa, 0.927) by +0.031. On HunyuanVideo (VBench), DPCache(K=9) at 4.75× speedup scores 80.23%, exceeding the best baseline (TaylorSeer, 79.99%) by +0.24%. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), DPCache(K=12) at 3.02× speedup achieves FID 3.285, beating the second-best method (SpeCa, 4.705) by −1.420 FID.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPCache consistently outperforms all three caching-based baselines (TeaCache, TaylorSeer, SpeCa) across every evaluated model and metric. On FLUX at 4.87× speedup, DPCache leads SpeCa (the strongest prior method) by +0.031 ImageReward and +0.10 CLIP Score, while TaylorSeer and TeaCache trail by larger margins (−0.165 and −0.094 ImageReward respectively). At the more moderate 3.54× speedup, DPCache even exceeds the unaccelerated 50-step baseline in ImageReward (+0.028), a result none of the baselines achieve. On HunyuanVideo at 4.75× speedup, DPCache scores 80.23% VBench versus 78.50% (SpeCa), 79.99% (TaylorSeer), and 78.17% (TeaCache), and achieves substantially higher fidelity (PSNR 21.04 vs. 17.83–18.23 for baselines). On DiT-XL/2, DPCache attains FID 3.285 at 3.02× speedup, beating SpeCa (4.705) and TaylorSeer (6.163) by wide margins, while also using less GPU memory (3.19 GB vs. 3.28 GB) because it caches only final-layer features rather than all transformer layers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPCache extends the feature-caching acceleration line initiated by DeepCache and advanced by TaylorSeer, TeaCache, and SpeCa, which reuse or predict intermediate features across timesteps to avoid redundant forward passes. The key departure is in the scheduling strategy: rather than fixed intervals or locally adaptive (greedy) decisions, DPCache formulates the selection of which timesteps to compute fully as a global path-planning problem solved via dynamic programming over a Path-Aware Cost Tensor (PACT). This tensor captures the path-dependent prediction error conditioned on the preceding key step, a dependency that 2D pairwise cost matrices cannot represent. The framework is predictor-agnostic, building on TaylorSeer's Taylor-series prediction as a default but remaining compatible with any future predictor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the denoising trajectory shape is largely content-agnostic—requiring only ~10 calibration samples (or even 1) to determine an optimal schedule—makes DPCache practically deployable without per-input overhead. The observation that a globally optimized sparse schedule can match or even slightly exceed the full-step baseline in quality (e.g., +0.028 ImageReward at 3.54× on FLUX) suggests that the full-step trajectory is not strictly necessary and that well-placed computation steps with good prediction suffice. Open questions include whether the schedule generalizes across different guidance scales, classifier-free guidance configurations, or fine-tuned model variants, and whether input-adaptive scheduling (the authors' stated future work) could further close the gap at very aggressive compression ratios.
