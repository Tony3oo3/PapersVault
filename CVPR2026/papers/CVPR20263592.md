## Problem

Existing RGB-to-RAW conversion methods treat all pixels uniformly, ignoring that reconstruction difficulty is inherently signal-dependent: low-intensity regions have small, stable residuals enabling high-fidelity recovery, while high-intensity and over-exposed regions suffer from large, uncertain residuals due to non-linear tone mapping and value clipping. Additionally, multi-camera conversion via a single unified model conflates different ISP characteristics, causing performance degradation that camera-specific adaptation could prevent.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (cropped to 512×512 during training) and a discrete camera label as input, and produces a RAW image in four-channel RGGB format (256×256 patches during training, full resolution at inference). RAW images are normalized by their sensor-specific black and white levels and linearly scaled to [-1,1]. The model operates directly in pixel space without a VQGAN latent, since the latent representation is ill-suited for RAW data. The camera label acts as a discrete control signal that selects which LoRA branch modulates the denoising network.

## Method

SpiralDiff builds on ResShift's 4-step residual-shifting diffusion framework but replaces isotropic Gaussian noise with a signal-dependent, time-varying weight map w_t = x_0 + η_t·e_0 that modulates the noise variance spatially at each diffusion step. Low-intensity pixels receive smaller perturbations to preserve detail, while high-intensity and over-exposed pixels receive stronger noise to accommodate reconstruction uncertainty from ISP nonlinearity and clipping; the weight map evolves with the diffusion schedule so noise injection aligns with the progressive RAW-to-RGB transition. The backbone is a U-Net with Swin Transformer blocks, and CamLoRA augments each Q, K, V, O projection matrix in every Swin layer with a camera-specific low-rank update (rank 8, ~2.7% extra parameters), selected by the input camera label. This design isolates camera-specific ISP adaptations in lightweight LoRA branches while the shared backbone learns universal features, enabling both unified multi-camera training and few-shot adaptation to new cameras by fine-tuning only one LoRA branch.

## Results

In the separate (per-camera) setting, SpiralDiff achieves 42.82/0.9936 PSNR/SSIM on FiveK Canon, 41.72/0.9925 on FiveK Nikon, 53.64/0.9990 on NOD Nikon, and 50.46/0.9980 on NOD Sony, outperforming the strongest baseline RAW-Diffusion by 2.9–3.1 dB PSNR. In the combined (unified multi-camera) setting with CamLoRA, SpiralDiff reaches 42.46/43.82/52.62/50.08 PSNR, approaching separate-model performance while using a single model. On the over-exposed test set, SpiralDiff scores 31.10 PSNR on FiveK Canon and 40.79 on NOD Nikon, exceeding RAW-Diffusion by >0.5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpiralDiff outperforms all four baselines (CycleISP, InvISP, ReRAW, RAW-Diffusion) across every camera test set and both training settings. The closest competitor, RAW-Diffusion, is surpassed by 2.9–3.1 dB PSNR in the separate setting (e.g., 42.82 vs. 39.96 on FiveK Canon) and by 1.8–3.3 dB in the combined+CamLoRA setting. CycleISP and InvISP trail by 3–9 dB PSNR, with InvISP showing the largest gap on FiveK Nikon (34.30 vs. 41.72). ReRAW is the weakest baseline overall, losing 7–10 dB PSNR to SpiralDiff. In the combined setting without CamLoRA, all methods degrade relative to their separate counterparts; SpiralDiff+CamLoRA recovers most of this gap (e.g., 42.46 vs. 42.82 on FiveK Canon), whereas RAW-Diffusion drops from 39.96 to 40.63 only marginally, confirming that camera-specific adaptation is critical for multi-sensor generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpiralDiff builds directly on ResShift's residual-shifting diffusion framework for image restoration, replacing its isotropic Gaussian noise with a signal-dependent, time-varying weight map that modulates perturbation strength by local pixel intensity. For multi-camera generalization, it extends the LoRA parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm into a camera-conditioned variant (CamLoRA) applied to Swin Transformer layers, departing from prior RGB-to-RAW methods (CycleISP, InvISP, ReRAW, RAW-Diffusion) that either train per-camera models or naively merge multi-camera data without explicit device-specific adaptation. The conceptual departure from prior diffusion-based restoration work is the observation that reconstruction difficulty is inherently non-uniform across intensity levels, motivating spatially heterogeneous noise injection rather than the uniform schedule assumed by standard diffusion formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RGB-to-RAW conversion unlocks the use of abundant RGB datasets for RAW-domain tasks (denoising, low-light enhancement, object detection) without the high cost of collecting new sensor-specific RAW data, and the demonstrated downstream detection gains on NOD validate this pipeline. The signal-dependent noise principle is task-agnostic in spirit and could inform other restoration problems where degradation severity varies spatially, though the paper does not test this generalization. Open questions include whether CamLoRA scales gracefully to dozens of camera models, whether the four-step ResShift sampling remains sufficient for the more complex spatially-varying noise schedule at higher resolutions, and how the method performs on cameras with fundamentally different sensor architectures (e.g., stacked sensors, global shutter).
