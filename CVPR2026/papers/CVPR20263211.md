## Problem

Virtual IHC staining from H&E images suffers from inherent spatial dislocation between paired images (obtained from adjacent tissue sections), which weakens pixel-level supervision. Existing auxiliary-task approaches mitigate this by enforcing consistency on a single task applied only to virtual IHC outputs, but they overlook the rich pathology-relevant semantic features learned by auxiliary task models and fail to address the task gap between those features and the virtual staining objective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single H&E histopathology image as input and produces a corresponding virtual IHC image as output. During training, it additionally requires the adjacent real IHC image and four frozen auxiliary task models (H&E and IHC classification and reconstruction) whose multi-layer features are extracted and aligned. At inference, only the VIS generator is used, introducing no additional computational cost beyond the base CUT-based translation network.

## Method

The framework leverages multi-task features from four frozen auxiliary models (classification and reconstruction on both H&E and IHC) to guide a CUT-based VIS generator through a two-stage alignment strategy. For spatial alignment, a structure-stain feature modulator is trained via structure-enhanced learning (contrastive loss with structure-retained positive and stain-retained negative samples), then active-passive matching clusters features into three semantic groups (background, positive, negative) and establishes bijection by actively selecting top-2 most similar cluster pairs and passively assigning the remainder, producing an alignment matrix that rearranges real IHC features to virtual IHC positions. For task gap alignment, a task-gap alignment model is trained by freezing the VIS generator and optimizing the model to minimize the virtual staining loss, using performance improvement as an implicit signal that the semantic gap has been bridged. The resulting dual-aligned features are then added to a duplicate of the VIS generator, whose intermediate features serve as stable guidance targets for the actual generator through an L2 guidance loss, combined with adversarial, PatchNCE, Gaussian pyramid, and standard-deviation losses.

## Results

On the MIST dataset (4 biomarkers: HER2, ER, PR, Ki67), the proposed method achieves the best FID on 3 of 4 biomarkers (40.34 on HER2, 35.40 on PR, 28.51 on Ki67) and the best KID on 3 of 4 (6.56 on HER2, 5.05 on PR, 4.03 on Ki67). On the BCI-HER2 dataset, it attains the best FID (45.57), KID (12.57), and LPIPS (0.5689) among all compared methods. The method consistently outperforms the strongest baselines (SIM-GAN, PSPStain) by 2–12 FID points and 1–4 KID points across biomarkers, while SSIM is competitive but not always the highest due to its sensitivity to pixel-level misalignment inherent in adjacent-section pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on FID and KID for MIST-HER2, MIST-PR, MIST-Ki67, and BCI-HER2. The closest competitors are SIM-GAN (OT-based matching) and PSPStain (semantics-preserving framework): on MIST-HER2, the proposed method improves FID from 44.83 (SIM-GAN) / 46.39 (PSPStain) to 40.34, and KID from 7.22 / 9.34 to 6.56. On MIST-Ki67, the gap is largest, with FID dropping from 31.03 (SIM-GAN) to 28.51 and KID from 4.09 to 4.03. On MIST-ER, the method achieves the best FID (35.90) and LPIPS (0.5955) but SIM-GAN retains a slight KID edge (6.38 vs. 7.06). On BCI-HER2, the advantage over PSPStain is FID 45.57 vs. 47.86 and KID 12.57 vs. 13.46, while LPIPS shows the largest margin (0.5689 vs. 0.5827). SSIM is not consistently the highest (e.g., PSPStain scores 0.2306 on MIST-ER vs. 0.2062 for the proposed method), which the authors attribute to SSIM's sensitivity to pixel-level misalignment in adjacent-section pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CUT as the underlying unpaired image-translation generator for virtual IHC staining, extending the line of work that incorporates auxiliary tasks to compensate for spatial misalignment in H&E-IHC pairs. It departs from prior auxiliary-task methods (TDKStain, Zeng et al.) that apply a single task only to virtual IHC outputs at the loss level, instead extracting features from multiple frozen auxiliary models (classification and reconstruction on both H&E and IHC) and injecting them at the feature level. It also contrasts with OT-based alignment approaches (Guan et al.) that perform global cost-minimizing matching, arguing that uniform cost minimization is unreliable in positive-staining regions where appearance shifts are large; the proposed active-passive matching restricts similarity computation to semantically consistent clusters instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-alignment framework (spatial via SEL+APM, task-gap via M_ta) offers a principled recipe for leveraging auxiliary task features in any paired-but-misaligned image translation setting, not just virtual staining. A notable practical advantage is that all auxiliary models are used only during training, adding zero inference cost. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets and four biomarkers, the complexity of the multi-stage training pipeline (SEL, APM, M_ta, alternating updates), and the reliance on K=3 clustering informed by histological priors that may not generalize to all IHC patterns. Open questions include whether the framework extends to multiplexed IHC or other staining modalities, and whether the task-gap alignment objective can be simplified or made more interpretable.
