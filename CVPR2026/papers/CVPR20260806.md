## Problem

Existing text-based 3D motion editing methods, particularly SimMotionEdit, aggregate motion features along the joint dimension per frame, which limits the model to understanding *when* an edit should occur temporally but not *which specific joints* must be modified versus preserved. This under-specification of joint-level control constrains semantic alignment between the text instruction and the resulting edits, as the encoder lacks explicit supervision on per-joint motion differences.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source motion sequence of N frames, each represented as a 207-dimensional SMPL parameter vector (126 six-dim rotation parameters for 21 joints, 66 position parameters for 22 joints, 3 global translation, and 12 global orientation), together with a free-form text edit instruction encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 text encoder. It produces an edited motion sequence in the same 207-dim SMPL parameter format, generated via a 300-step DDPM reverse process with a cosine noise schedule and two-way classifier-free guidance (scale 2.0) on both text and source motion conditions.

## Method

The architecture employs two axis-anchored transformers (4 layers, 8 heads, 512-dim) that separately model the joint axis (aggregating each joint's trajectory across all frames into h_joint ∈ R^{K×D}) and the time axis (capturing full-body pose per frame into h_time ∈ R^{N×D}). A cross-axis fusion block then performs multi-head attention with h_time as Query and h_joint as Key/Value, producing a fused conditioning feature that is concatenated with the noised motion input to an 8-layer DiT. A novel joint-wise auxiliary task trains a regression head on the 126 rotation channels of h_joint to predict per-channel Soft-DTW distances between source and target rotation trajectories, providing timing-robust, joint-specific supervision that teaches the model which joints to modify and by how much. The total loss combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction MSE with the auxiliary Soft-DTW regression MSE, and ablations confirm that Soft-DTW substantially outperforms L2 distance for this auxiliary objective due to its invariance to local temporal shifts.

## Results

On the MotionFix dataset, the proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all retrieval and fidelity metrics. In the batch evaluation, it reaches R@1=74.38%, R@2=88.54%, R@3=92.08%, and AvgR=1.92 for generated-to-target retrieval, while on the full test set it attains R@1=29.45%, R@2=45.26%, R@3=54.55%, and AvgR=16.42. The method also achieves the lowest FID of 0.097, confirming superior motion fidelity and diversity compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four direct baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor SimMotionEdit (CVPR 2025), it improves batch R@1 from 70.62% to 74.38% (+3.76 pp), test-set R@1 from 25.49% to 29.45% (+3.96 pp), and reduces FID from 0.110 to 0.097. Over TMED (the MotionFix baseline), gains are substantially larger: batch R@1 rises from 62.90% to 74.38% (+11.48 pp) and FID drops from 0.167 to 0.097. MotionReFit (CVPR 2025) reports only batch metrics (R@1=66.33%), still trailing the proposed method by 8.05 pp. MDM and MDM-BP, which rely on text-to-motion generation or GPT-based body-part extraction rather than direct editing supervision, are far behind (batch R@1 of 4.03% and 39.10% respectively), underscoring the advantage of the supervised triplet setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-based 3D motion editing line initiated by MotionFix/TMED [3] and advanced by SimMotionEdit [26], which introduced a condition transformer with frame-wise motion similarity prediction. The key conceptual departure is from SimMotionEdit's frame-level aggregation: rather than collapsing joint information per timestep, this work introduces two axis-anchored transformers (joint-anchored and time-anchored) with a cross-axis fusion block to separately model per-joint trajectory characteristics and per-frame pose characteristics. The joint-wise Soft-DTW auxiliary loss replaces SimMotionEdit's frame-wise similarity target, providing timing-robust, joint-specific supervision that explicitly teaches the encoder which joints to modify versus preserve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint-wise supervision addresses a genuine gap in prior editing methods: knowing when to edit is insufficient without knowing which body parts to change, and the Soft-DTW formulation elegantly handles the timing-shift sensitivity that plagues naive L2-based alternatives. However, evaluation is confined to the 6,730-sample MotionFix dataset with a single SMPL parameterization, and the absolute improvements over SimMotionEdit, while consistent, are moderate (e.g., R@1: 70.62→74.38 batch). Open questions include whether the axis-anchored decomposition and joint-wise supervision generalize to other motion representations (e.g., joint angles, end-effector trajectories), longer sequences, or multi-instruction editing, and whether the approach transfers to generation-from-scratch settings.
