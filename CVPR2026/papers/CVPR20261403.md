## Problem

State-of-the-art single image-to-3D generation models fail to produce voluminous 3D meshes when the input is a flat image (cartoons, line drawings, flat-shaded art) that lacks 3D cues such as shading, depth gradients, or relative positional information. These flat images are out-of-distribution for models trained predominantly on natural photos or rendered views, causing failures in both novel view synthesis and direct 3D generation. Existing workarounds—silhouette inflation, 2D-guided pipelines, and parametric regression—remain silhouette-driven, restricted to 2D-space deformations, or constrained by a predefined model space.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single flat 2D RGB image (cartoon, line drawing, or flat-shaded art) as input and produces a voluminous, detailed 3D mesh as output. It is designed as a plug-and-play pipeline that wraps around any pretrained conditional 3D latent generative backbone (demonstrated with Hunyuan3D-2.1 and Direct3D), requiring no retraining of the backbone. Inference takes approximately 3 minutes per image on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU, and the framework also supports image-conditioned 3D editing of the generated mesh.

## Method

REVIVE 3D is a two-stage pipeline. In Stage 1 (Inflated Prior Generation), the foreground silhouette is extracted and inflated into a 3D height field by solving a discrete Poisson equation (adapted from Monster Mash), producing a convex Base 3D with global volume; SAM 2 segmentation masks are then individually inflated and superimposed onto the base to add part-aware local structure, yielding the Inflated Prior. In Stage 2 (3D Latent Refinement), the Inflated Prior is encoded into the latent space of a pretrained 3D autoencoder, Gaussian noise is injected at a tunable initial noise level t₀ (default 0.8), and a conditional diffusion transformer denoises the noisy latent using the input image as conditioning. The key insight is that the Inflated Prior supplies direct 3D volumetric and part-aware spatial cues—rather than restrictive 2D guidance—while the stochastic noise injection allows the backbone's pretrained 3D knowledge to correct the prior's inherent convex-only geometry (e.g., turning a flat mouth bump into a concavity or a flat tail bump into a back-facing structure) without losing the injected volume.

## Results

On a 2,232-image flat-image test set, REVIVE 3D (Direct3D backbone) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: Uni3D 0.3097, ULIP 0.1375, Compactness 0.2178, and Normal Anisotropy 0.0908. The Hunyuan3D-2.1 backbone variant achieves the lowest NA (0.0767) and competitive C (0.2179). A user study with 51 participants confirms that REVIVE 3D is the most preferred method on Quality, Volume, and Details ratings on a 5-point Likert scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVIVE 3D outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against the strongest structured 3D generator Trellis, it improves Uni3D by +0.036 (0.3097 vs 0.2736) and reduces NA by 0.037 (0.0908 vs 0.1282). Against the character-specialized DrawingSpinUp, gains are largest: Uni3D +0.076 and C +0.057, reflecting DrawingSpinUp's high variability on non-character inputs. Against Hunyuan3D-Omni (bounding-box conditioned), REVIVE 3D wins on C (+0.047) and NA (−0.021), showing that bounding-box conditioning produces uniform volumetric expansion that breaks image consistency. Most notably, REVIVE 3D improves over its own backbones: over Hunyuan3D-2.1, C jumps from 0.1408 to 0.2179 (+55%) and NA drops from 0.1347 to 0.0767 (−43%); over Direct3D, C rises from 0.2012 to 0.2178 and NA falls from 0.1019 to 0.0908, confirming that the Inflated Prior + latent refinement pipeline adds meaningful volumetric and geometric detail beyond the backbone alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVIVE 3D builds directly on the geometric inflation technique from Monster Mash [7], adapting its discrete Poisson equation height-field solver to construct a volumetric prior from a foreground silhouette. It departs from the three prior lines of work on flat-image 3D generation—silhouette inflation (which yields convex-only geometry), 2D-guided pipelines (which supply only depth/pose/edge cues that cannot produce back-facing or concave structure), and parametric regression (which is constrained to a predefined model space)—by instead providing explicit 3D volumetric and part-aware cues that are refined in the latent space of a pretrained conditional 3D diffusion backbone (Hunyuan3D-2.1 or Direct3D). The method is plug-and-play: it requires no retraining of the backbone and operates as a pre-processing (Inflated Prior) plus stochastic latent refinement (noise injection at t₀ followed by conditional denoising).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visually obvious failure mode—flat images producing flat 3D meshes—that persists across current SOTA single-image-to-3D systems, and provides a practical, backbone-agnostic fix. The introduction of Compactness and Normal Anisotropy as reference-free metrics for volume and surface flatness fills a gap in evaluation, since standard metrics like Chamfer Distance require ground-truth geometry that is unavailable for stylized inputs. A key limitation is the reliance on a photorealism-biased pretrained backbone, which can shift cartoon-style outputs toward realism; the authors flag better texture/style alignment as future work. Open questions include whether the inflation-then-refine paradigm generalizes to other out-of-distribution inputs (e.g., extreme viewpoints, multi-object scenes) and whether the noise-level trade-off (fidelity vs. plausibility) can be made input-adaptive.
