## Problem

Existing adverse-weather LiDAR generation methods jointly denoise geometry and reflectance in a single network, causing geometric cues to dominate optimization while reflectance signals are underfitted—especially under the severe scarcity of real adverse-weather training data. Additionally, prior simulation-based augmentation relies on simplified physics that fails to capture the statistical complexity of real degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262141/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a discrete weather condition label (fog, rain, or snow) as input and produces a 2-channel range-view image of shape H×W, where one channel encodes geometric structure (depth) and the other encodes reflectance intensity. At generation time, a weather-specific embedding is retrieved from a precomputed weather bank rather than requiring runtime simulation, and the output is a full adverse-weather LiDAR scan in the same range-view representation used for training.

## Method

SiD factorizes the joint distribution as P(S,I|c) = P(S|c)·P(I|S,c), exploiting the asymmetric causal dependency where reflectance is conditioned on geometry but not vice versa. At each reverse diffusion timestep, UNet_S denoises the noisy structure map to produce an estimated clean geometry Ŝ₀, which is encoded into a compact embedding φ_S by a structure encoder; UNet_I then denoises the intensity map conditioned on both the weather embedding φ_W and φ_S via AdaGN modulation. To address data scarcity, RPWS synthesizes adverse-weather scans from clear ones by transferring depth-wise decay profiles and spatial occlusion masks from real adverse scans (geometric degradation) and applying an optimal-transport map between clean and adverse reflectance histograms (radiometric degradation). Training uses Min-SNR weighting, gradient detachment to enforce the S→I dependency direction, and a two-stage strategy: pretraining on RPWS-augmented data followed by fine-tuning on real STF scans.

## Results

On the STF adverse-weather benchmark, SiD achieves the best scores across all five metrics (FMD, FPD, FRD, MMD, JSD) for fog, rain, and snow. The most striking gains are in fog, where FPD drops from 314.14 (WeatherGen) to 119.29 and FRD from 1968.90 to 1370.00, and in rain, where FMD improves from 4.65 to 0.42. On clear-weather KITTI-360, SiD also leads in FMD (1.75 vs. 1.86 for WeatherGen) and FRD (135.13 vs. 138.62), confirming the structure-to-intensity factorization generalizes beyond adverse conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SiD outperforms the primary state-of-the-art WeatherGen [45] on every metric across all three weather conditions. In fog, the FPD improvement is the largest (119.29 vs. 314.14, a 62% reduction), and MMD drops from 8.08 to 3.62, indicating much tighter BEV occupancy alignment. For rain, SiD achieves an FMD of 0.42 versus WeatherGen's 4.65 (an 11× improvement), while matching WeatherGen's JSD of 0.93. Under snow, FPD improves from 59.28 to 32.38 and FRD from 1241.70 to 1080.68. The physics-based simulators (FSRL, LISA, LSS) are substantially weaker, with FPD values 2–5× higher than SiD, confirming that learned generation with real-prior augmentation surpasses handcrafted atmospheric models. On clear-weather KITTI-360, SiD edges out WeatherGen (FMD 1.75 vs. 1.86; FRD 135.13 vs. 138.62) and clearly beats earlier diffusion baselines like LiDM (FPD 34.36) and LiDARGen (FPD 90.29), showing the factorized design is not limited to adverse conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DDPM-based LiDAR generation line (LiDARGen, R2DM, LiDM) from clear-weather to adverse-weather settings, and directly departs from WeatherGen [45] by replacing its joint structure-and-intensity denoising with a causal factorization: geometry is denoised first, then reflectance is conditioned on the recovered structure. The RPWS augmentation module builds on physics-based simulation pipelines (FSRL, LSS, LISA) but replaces their handcrafted atmospheric models with empirical degradation statistics extracted from real adverse-weather scans, including an optimal-transport map for reflectance. The FMD evaluation metric extends the Fréchet-distance paradigm (FPD, FRD) into a voxel feature space grounded in adverse-weather data via SLidR pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structure-to-intensity factorization offers a principled, physics-motivated decomposition that could generalize to other multi-channel sensor generation tasks where channels have asymmetric causal dependencies. The data-efficiency result (strong performance with only 10% real adverse-weather data) is practically significant given the cost and difficulty of collecting such scans. Open questions include whether the factorization extends to 4D LiDAR or multi-sensor fusion settings, whether the optimal-transport reflectance mapping generalizes to unseen weather severities, and whether the FMD metric transfers reliably across sensor types and resolutions.
