## Problem

Vision-Language Transformers suffer sharp performance drops when an input modality is missing, and while existing missing-aware prompt methods (MAP, DCP, MaPLe) adapt the representation extraction, their prediction heads remain a single uniform Fully-Connected layer that computes class scores identically regardless of which modality is present. This architectural mismatch between adaptive representations and a static decision layer prevents the model from fully exploiting the missing-modality cues captured by the prompts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261909/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 224×224) and a text description (max 77 tokens) as input; when a modality is missing, it is replaced by a zero-filled tensor of the same shape. The frozen pretrained encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViLT) produces per-modality feature vectors, which are L2-normalized before scoring. Output is a K-dimensional logit vector computed via cosine similarity between the available modality's representation and the corresponding modality-specific prototype component, yielding either a single class label (multi-class) or a set of labels above a 0.5 sigmoid threshold (multi-label).

## Method

DPL replaces the conventional FC prediction head with a set of class-wise prototypes that are decoupled by missing-modality case (complete, image-missing, text-missing) and further decomposed into image-specific and text-specific components, so that only the components corresponding to the observed modality contribute to the logit. Training optimizes these prototypes with two complementary losses: an ArcFace-style angular margin loss with adaptive scale and margin parameters assigned per prototype type to balance decision boundaries across missing cases, and a Prototype Relational Contrastive Loss that pulls same-class prototypes across different missing patterns together while pushing different-class prototypes apart. The pretrained backbone remains entirely frozen, making DPL a parameter-efficient drop-in replacement for the FC head that can be used standalone or stacked on top of existing prompt-tuning methods (MAP, DCP, MaPLe) without modifying their prompt design.

## Results

On MM-IMDb (F1-Macro), DPL with DCP achieves 56.94% under 50% text-missing, improving over the FC baseline (53.62%) by +3.32 points. On UPMC Food-101 (Top-1 Accuracy), DPL with DCP reaches 85.14% under 50% text-missing vs. 82.18% for FC (+2.96). On Hateful Memes (AUROC), DPL with DCP scores 70.87% under 50% text-missing vs. 69.84% for FC (+1.03). Gains are consistent across all three missing rates (50%, 70%, 90%) and all missing cases (image-missing, text-missing, mixed-missing), with the largest improvements observed under severe missingness (e.g., +4.06 F1 on MM-IMDb at 90% image-missing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPL is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the FC prediction head across four prompt-tuning baselines: MaPLe, MAP, DCP, and DePT. Against MaPLe, MAP, and DCP, DPL consistently improves performance on all three datasets and all missing rates; for example, at 50% text-missing on MM-IMDb, DPL lifts MaPLe from 54.31→56.36 (+2.05), MAP from 53.32→56.03 (+2.71), and DCP from 53.62→56.94 (+3.32). Against DePT (CVPR'24), which targets the Base-New tradeoff rather than missing-modality robustness, DPL wins decisively: on UPMC Food-101 at 50% both-missing, DePT degrades MAP from 85.13→84.02 (−1.11) while DPL improves it to 87.08 (+1.95); at 90% mixed-missing, DePT yields only +0.43 over FC whereas DPL achieves +2.44. DPL also outperforms DePT in the 10-seed robustness evaluation, showing a narrower inter-quartile range and no outliers, whereas DePT exhibits occasional performance drops. Overall, DPL never degrades any baseline and provides the largest absolute gains under the most severe missingness (90%), confirming its role as a complementary prediction-head improvement orthogonal to prompt design.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPL extends the missing-aware prompt learning line (MAP, DCP, MaPLe) by identifying and addressing an architectural gap: while prior methods adapt representations via prompts, their prediction heads remain a single static FC layer insensitive to which modality is present. The method repurposes prototype learning from face recognition (ArcFace) and few-shot classification, adapting the margin-based loss with per-missing-case scale/margin parameters and adding a Prototype Relational Contrastive Loss to maintain coherence across the decoupled prototype sets. Conceptually, DPL mirrors MAP's missing-aware selection mechanism but applies it at the prediction level rather than the representation level, making it a complementary rather than competing contribution to prompt-based frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular design—replacing only the prediction head while keeping the backbone and any existing prompts frozen—makes DPL a low-cost, drop-in upgrade for deployed multimodal classifiers facing real-world modality dropout (sensor failures, privacy constraints, data collection gaps). Consistent 2–3% gains across three datasets, three missing rates, two backbones (CLIP, ViLT), and four prompt frameworks suggest broad practical applicability. Open questions include scalability beyond two modalities (the formulation generalizes but is only validated for image+text), whether the approach transfers to generative or retrieval tasks, and whether the per-case prototype sets introduce meaningful parameter overhead at very large class counts.
