## Problem

Existing model merging methods often suffer performance degradation, especially across heterogeneous tasks, because they fail to preserve the directional geometry of each task's knowledge components in the merged vector. The paper identifies two root causes: an imbalanced long-tailed energy distribution that causes weaker but semantically important singular directions to be neglected, and geometric misalignment of per-task low-rank subspaces that distorts directions during direct parameter-space aggregation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of task vectors (ΔW_i = W_i − W_0 for full fine-tuning, or ΔW_i = B_i A_i for LoRA) from T fine-tuned models plus the pre-trained base weights W_0. Each task vector is decomposed via truncated SVD to rank r, and the method operates entirely on these low-rank factorizations (U_i, Σ_i, V_i). The output is a single merged multi-task vector ΔW̃ that, when added to W_0, yields a unified model; no additional data or forward passes are required at inference time.

## Method

DC-Merge consists of two complementary modules applied to the SVD factorizations of each task vector. First, energy smoothing replaces the original singular values of each task vector with a smoothed version (e.g., their mean or a linear interpolation), equalizing the contribution of all retained knowledge components so that weaker directions are not suppressed during merging. Second, cover space merging constructs a shared orthogonal basis by concatenating all tasks' left and right singular vectors and applying whitening, then projects every smoothed task vector into this common subspace, aggregates them via Task Arithmetic or TIES-Merging, and projects the result back to parameter space using a block-diagonal structural mask that prevents cross-task directional interference. The key insight is that preserving the directional consistency (quantified by the proposed DirSim metric) between the merged vector and each original task vector is what actually retains task capability, rather than matching energy magnitudes.

## Results

In the LoRA setting (Table 1), DC-Merge achieves 64.17% absolute / 73.90% normalized accuracy on ViT-B-32 with 8 tasks, outperforming the prior best Iso-CTS (63.01 / 72.71) and TSV-M (58.91 / 68.25). Under full fine-tuning (Table 2), DC-Merge reaches 87.05% / 93.55% on ViT-B-32 with 8 tasks, surpassing Iso-CTS (86.20 / 91.78) and TSV-M (85.86 / 92.31), with the gap widening as task count grows (e.g., 80.58 / 88.18 vs. 78.05 / 85.48 at 20 tasks). On the vision-language MM-MergeBench (Table 3), DC-Merge attains 59.63% on seen tasks and 39.84% on unseen tasks, exceeding RobustMerge (57.33 / 37.99) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DC-Merge consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every backbone and task count in both LoRA and FFT settings. In the LoRA setting, it beats the strongest prior method Iso-CTS by 1.16% absolute accuracy (64.17 vs. 63.01) on ViT-B-32/8 tasks and by 2.03% (68.40 vs. 66.28) at 12 tasks, with the margin growing to 1.66% at 16 tasks. Under FFT, DC-Merge surpasses Iso-CTS by 0.85% at 8 tasks and 2.53% at 20 tasks (80.58 vs. 78.05), while TSV-M trails by 1.19% and 2.51% respectively. On the vision-language MM-MergeBench, DC-Merge leads RobustMerge by 2.30% on seen tasks (59.63 vs. 57.33) and 1.85% on unseen tasks (39.84 vs. 37.99), and exceeds the multi-task fine-tuned upper bound on unseen tasks (39.84 vs. 36.06). The gap over simpler baselines like Task Arithmetic and TIES-Merging is substantially larger (e.g., 11.37% over TA in LoRA/8 tasks), confirming that directional-consistency-aware merging is critical for multi-task knowledge retention.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Task Arithmetic's task-vector formulation and the growing family of SVD-based merging methods (TSV-M, Iso-CTS, KnOTS) that operate on low-rank decompositions of task vectors. It departs from these prior approaches by identifying directional consistency of knowledge components—rather than subspace orthogonality or isotropic singular-value scaling—as the critical property for knowledge retention, and by introducing a two-stage pipeline (energy smoothing + cover-space projection via whitening) that explicitly preserves this geometry. The DirSim metric further distinguishes the work by decoupling directional alignment from energy magnitude, a separation not made by prior cosine-similarity-based analyses of task vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of directional consistency as the governing factor for post-merge task retention provides a principled diagnostic (DirSim) and a design principle for future merging methods, shifting focus from magnitude-matching to geometry-preserving aggregation. The method's consistent SOTA across FFT and LoRA settings on both vision and vision-language benchmarks makes it a practical drop-in improvement for multi-task model consolidation. A notable open question highlighted by the authors is the persistent performance gap between LoRA and FFT merging, which they attribute to the scarcity of knowledge components in low-rank updates; promoting balanced energy distributions during fine-tuning itself is suggested as a promising but unexplored direction.
