## Problem

Current fMRI visual decoding models cannot generalize across subjects: inter-individual variability in neural representations forces per-subject training or gradient-based fine-tuning, making population-wide decoding impractical. No prior method achieves cross-subject and cross-scanner generalization without retraining, anatomical alignment, or stimulus overlap.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of context image-brain activation pairs (as few as 20–200 images) from a novel subject, along with the fMRI voxel activations for a target stimulus, and image embeddings from a pretrained backbone (CLIP, DINOv2, or SigLIP). It outputs a decoded image embedding in the same space as the backbone, evaluated via nearest-neighbor retrieval over a test image pool. Notably, the model accepts variable-length voxel contexts (no fixed voxel count) and requires no anatomical alignment or shared stimuli between training and test subjects.

## Method

BrainCoDec performs hierarchical in-context functional inversion in two stages. Stage 1 uses the pretrained BrainCoRL meta-optimized transformer to infer per-voxel encoder parameters from a context of stimulus-response pairs, repeated independently for each voxel in higher visual cortex. Stage 2 concatenates each voxel's inferred encoder parameters with its measured activation into a single context token, then feeds the variable-length set of voxel tokens into a second transformer with a [CLS] output token to jointly invert the aggregated encoding functions and recover the image embedding. To handle variable voxel counts and order invariance, the model omits positional embeddings and applies logit scaling (multiplying attention logits by log of context length). Training follows a three-stage pipeline—synthetic-data pretraining, variable-length context extension, and real-fMRI fine-tuning—optimized with a hybrid cosine-embedding plus InfoNCE contrastive loss that simultaneously enforces reconstruction fidelity and instance-level discriminability.

## Results

On unseen NSD subjects (S1, S2, S5, S7), BrainCoDec-200 achieves a mean Top-1 retrieval accuracy of 22.7% and Top-5 accuracy of 54.0%, using only 200 in-context images and no fine-tuning. On the BOLD5000 cross-scanner evaluation (3T, different voxel size and protocol), BrainCoDec with CLIP backbone achieves 31.45% Top-1 accuracy, 81.67% Top-5 accuracy, and 0.72 cosine similarity using just 20 in-context images. Performance scales positively with both the number of in-context images (Stage 1) and the number of voxels (Stage 2), with 200 images and 4,000 voxels approaching full-context performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainCoDec-200 substantially outperforms both baselines across all four unseen NSD subjects. Against MindEye2 (which requires anatomical alignment via MNI space and subject-specific fine-tuning), BrainCoDec-200 achieves 22.7% vs. 3.90% mean Top-1 accuracy (~5.8× improvement) and 54.0% vs. 9.81% mean Top-5 accuracy (~5.5× improvement). Against TGBD (which also requires retraining per subject), the gap is even larger: 22.7% vs. 0.82% Top-1 (~28× improvement) and 54.0% vs. 3.09% Top-5 (~17× improvement). Critically, BrainCoDec achieves these gains without any fine-tuning, anatomical alignment, or stimulus overlap, whereas both baselines require at least one of these. On the BOLD5000 cross-scanner test, BrainCoDec with CLIP reaches 31.45% Top-1 accuracy using only 20 in-context images, demonstrating that the meta-learned inversion transfers across scanner field strengths (7T→3T), voxel sizes, and acquisition protocols without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

BrainCoDec builds directly on BrainCoRL [114], which meta-optimizes a transformer to predict per-voxel fMRI responses in-context, and repurposes it as the Stage-1 encoder parameter estimator. It extends the classical functional-inversion paradigm (Brouwer & Heeger; Naselaris et al.)—previously applied to simple stimuli like colors and orientations—into a learned, hierarchical transformer that inverts a variable number of higher-visual-cortex voxels simultaneously. Unlike prior cross-subject approaches (MindEye2, TGBD, MindBridge) that rely on anatomical alignment, 1-D pooling, or subject-specific fine-tuning, BrainCoDec frames decoding as a two-stage in-context learning problem requiring neither shared anatomy nor stimulus overlap, drawing on the broader in-context-learning-as-implicit-meta-learning literature for its architectural choices (logit scaling, order-invariant attention).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first training-free, cross-scanner brain decoding framework that generalizes to novel subjects without anatomical alignment or stimulus overlap, opening a concrete path toward population-level brain foundation models and practical BCI deployment where per-subject retraining is infeasible. Key limitations include decoding only image embeddings (not full images) and evaluation on a small number of subjects (4 NSD, 3 BOLD5000), leaving open whether performance holds across more diverse populations, pathologies, or acquisition protocols. The authors note the approach could extend to EEG, MEG, and other modalities, but the current evidence is confined to fMRI. A practical open question is whether the BrainCoRL dependency in Stage 1 can be replaced or simplified for settings where a pretrained encoder is unavailable.
