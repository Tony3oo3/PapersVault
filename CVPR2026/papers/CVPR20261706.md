## Problem

Autoregressive visual generators are optimized solely for token-level likelihood, while tokenizers are trained to reconstruct clean images from ground-truth tokens. This objective mismatch causes the AR model to produce high-likelihood token sequences that decode into visually degraded, off-manifold images, with no direct pixel-space supervision during generation. Prior remedies (noisy-context regularization, tokenizer post-training) improve robustness but do not directly align the generator's output distribution with the pixel-space image manifold.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen visual tokenizer (encoder–quantizer–decoder) and a pretrained AR generator. During post-training, it consumes a reference image (or class/text conditioning), encodes it to a discrete token sequence of length N, and the AR model samples target tokens under teacher forcing with contextual noise. The sampled tokens are decoded back to a 384×384 image (resized to 256×256 for FID/IS evaluation), and the reconstruction quality against the reference image serves as the reward signal. No external reward model or additional input modality is required beyond the conditioning signal already used by the base AR model.

## Method

VA-π frames the AR generator as a variational posterior over discrete tokens and derives an ELBO whose reconstruction term enforces pixel-space fidelity while a prior regularization term preserves the pretrained token distribution. Because the reconstruction term is non-differentiable through discrete quantization and sampling, the paper optimizes it via reinforcement learning (GRPO), using the negative reconstruction loss (MSE + LPIPS) as an intrinsic reward computed under teacher forcing rather than expensive free-running rollouts. The prior regularization is implemented as a cross-entropy next-token prediction loss with stochastic contextual noise (following reAR), which simultaneously mitigates exposure bias and acts as the KL penalty in the GRPO objective. This design eliminates the need for a reference model, external reward models, or tokenizer retraining, enabling convergence in ~25 minutes on 8×A100 GPUs with only 1% of the pretraining data.

## Results

On class-conditional ImageNet-1K generation, VA-π reduces FID from 14.36 to 7.65 and increases IS from 86.55 to 116.70 on LlamaGen-XXL (1.4B) without classifier-free guidance, using only 25 minutes of post-training on 1% of the pretraining data. With CFG (scale=1.75), it achieves FID 2.28 and IS 273.53. On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, VA-π improves LlamaGen-XL's overall score from 0.306 to 0.339 and Janus-Pro 1B's score from 0.725 to 0.744, without any external reward model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AR-GRPO [1], VA-π wins on every reported metric: on LlamaGen-XL with CFG, VA-π achieves FID 2.94 vs. 3.63 and IS 299.63 vs. 293.07, while training 7.5× faster (20 min vs. 149 min) and without any external reward model. On text-image alignment (Table 1), VA-π surpasses AR-GRPO on both CLIP (0.291 vs. 0.274) and HPS v2 (0.211 vs. 0.208) despite not being optimized for those rewards. On GenEval, VA-π improves the overall score from 0.324 (AR-GRPO) to 0.339, with the largest gain on two-object composition (+0.065 over LlamaGen-XL baseline). Compared to the LlamaGen-XXL baseline, VA-π cuts FID by ~47% (14.36→7.65) and boosts IS by 35% (86.55→116.70) in just 25 minutes, outperforming both tokenizer post-training (FID 14.26) and STE-based AR post-training (FID 11.46, 381 min). Applied to the unified multimodal model Janus-Pro 1B, VA-π raises the GenEval overall score from 0.725 to 0.744, with notable gains in attribute binding (+0.045) and two-object relations (+0.034).

## Relation to Prior Work

VA-π sits at the intersection of three research threads: the VAE/ELBO framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013), which provides the variational objective structure; GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which supplies the RL optimization machinery; and reAR (He et al., 2025), whose contextual-noise regularization is adopted for the prior term. It departs from prior tokenizer-generator alignment work (noisy-context regularization, tokenizer post-training, STE-based gradient flow) by reframing the alignment problem as a variational policy optimization where pixel-space reconstruction serves as an intrinsic reward, eliminating the need for external reward models. The key conceptual departure from AR-GRPO is that the reward is derived from the tokenizer's own decoder rather than from a separately trained scorer, and the KL penalty is replaced by a lightweight cross-entropy regularization that avoids maintaining a reference model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 25-minute, 1%-data post-training procedure that requires no external reward model or tokenizer retraining can halve FID on LlamaGen-XXL, offering a low-cost alignment step for any existing AR visual generator. The ELBO formulation provides a principled theoretical bridge between token-level likelihood and pixel-space distribution, which prior heuristic approaches lacked. Open questions include whether the teacher-forcing-based reward generalizes well to longer free-running sequences (the exposure bias gap remains), whether the approach scales to much larger AR models or alternative tokenizer architectures (e.g., continuous or hierarchical tokenizers), and whether the moderate-noise sweet spot (ξ=0.5) is architecture-dependent. The method's reliance on a frozen tokenizer also means it cannot correct fundamental tokenizer limitations, only the generator's token selection.
