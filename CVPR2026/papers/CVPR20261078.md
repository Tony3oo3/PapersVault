## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) suffer from quadratic computational cost in the number of spatial tokens, making high-resolution generation prohibitively slow. While temporal acceleration (reducing denoising steps) has been well studied, spatial acceleration via latent upsampling remains underexplored for training-free settings. Naive latent upsampling introduces two distinct artifact types—aliasing in high-frequency edge regions and distribution mismatching from noise-timestep discrepancies—that prior methods like Bottleneck Sampling fail to address.

## Input / Output

RALU takes a standard text prompt and produces a high-resolution RGB image (1024×1024) using pretrained DiT backbones (FLUX.1-dev, SD3) without any retraining. Internally, the method operates on latent tokens at three resolution levels: 1/4 resolution for early denoising, a mixed resolution where only edge-region patches are upsampled, and full resolution for final refinement. The method is also composable with temporal acceleration (caching, forecasting) and timestep-distilled models (FLUX.1-schnell, SD3.5L-Turbo) for compounded speedups.

## Method

RALU is a three-stage training-free spatial acceleration framework. Stage 1 denoises at 1/4 latent resolution to reduce token count quadratically. Stage 2 performs region-adaptive early upsampling: it estimates the clean latent via Tweedie's formula, decodes with the VAE, applies Canny edge detection, and upsamples only the top-r (20–30%) edge-region patches to prevent aliasing while keeping most tokens at low resolution. Stage 3 upsamples all remaining latents to full resolution for final refinement. To prevent mismatching artifacts, RALU introduces Noise and Timestep Matching (NT-Matching): it injects correlated noise with a block-diagonal covariance to restore isotropic variance after nearest-neighbor upsampling, then minimizes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the induced timestep sampling distribution and the original flow-matching schedule via numerical search over per-stage shifting parameters.

## Results

On FLUX-1.dev, RALU achieves a 7.02× speedup (426 TFLOPs vs. 2991 TFLOPs) with an ImageReward of 0.999, CLIP-IQA of 0.681, and NIQE of 6.87, closely matching the unaccelerated 50-step baseline (ImageReward 1.095, NIQE 6.75). At a 5.53× speedup, RALU scores ImageReward 1.022 and NIQE 6.43, surpassing the base model on both metrics. On Stable Diffusion 3, RALU reaches a 3.02× speedup with ImageReward 0.798 and NIQE 5.44, outperforming all temporal and spatial baselines at comparable compute budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RALU consistently outperforms the spatial baseline Bottleneck Sampling [50] on ImageReward and NIQE at every speedup level: at 7× on FLUX, RALU scores ImageReward 0.999 vs. 0.792 and NIQE 6.87 vs. 8.71, indicating substantially fewer artifacts. Against temporal methods, RALU dominates at aggressive speedups: at 7× on FLUX, TaylorSeer [29] drops to ImageReward 0.660 and NIQE 9.43, while RALU maintains 0.999 and 6.87. At the 5× level, Δ-DiT [9] and ToCa [61] suffer severe quality collapse (ImageReward 0.102 and −1.827 respectively), whereas RALU achieves 1.022. On SD3 at 3× speedup, RALU (ImageReward 0.798, NIQE 5.44) beats TaylorSeer [29] (0.059, 7.44) and Bottleneck [50] (0.628, 5.58), though the gap over RAS [32] (0.423, 5.70) is narrower. Overall, RALU is the only method that maintains near-baseline quality (within ~0.1 ImageReward of the unaccelerated model) at 7× speedup on FLUX.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of flow-matching-based DiT acceleration and multi-resolution generation. It builds conceptually on Pyramidal Flow Matching [22], which introduced learned noise injection for resolution transitions, but adapts that idea to a training-free setting by analytically deriving the noise and timestep parameters rather than learning them. The key departure is from Bottleneck Sampling [50], the only prior training-free spatial acceleration method, which the paper shows produces substantial artifacts due to uncorrected trajectory skew after upsampling. RALU further distinguishes itself from cascaded diffusion frameworks (Relay Diffusion, Pyramidal Flow) by requiring no additional training and by selectively upsampled only edge-region latents early rather than all latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 7× speedup on FLUX-1.dev with near-identical quality metrics, and composability with temporal caching/forecasting methods to reach 15.9×, makes this directly relevant for real-time or on-device DiT deployment. The paper itself flags an open question about the precise mechanism by which DiTs handle simultaneous multi-resolution tokens in self-attention, suggesting the robustness is not yet theoretically understood. Limitations include evaluation on only two base models (FLUX, SD3) and a fixed 2× upsampling factor, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger resolution gaps or video DiTs.
