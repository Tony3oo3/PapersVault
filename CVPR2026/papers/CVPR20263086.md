## Problem

Existing MLLMs can produce correct answers to visual questions while attending to irrelevant image regions, relying on language bias rather than genuine visual-spatial perception. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether an MLLM's answer is actually grounded in the visual evidence it perceives, making it impossible to diagnose this form of visual hallucination or assess reliability for embodied applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263086/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a compositional text question as input and produces a multi-step reasoning chain where each step names a relevant entity and outputs its normalized bounding box [x, y, w, h], followed by a final answer. The MM-GCoT benchmark contains 1200 examples across three task types (Attribute, Judgement, Object), and evaluation is conducted under three prompt settings: answer-first, grounding-first, and grounding-CoT (multi-turn dialogue where the model grounds each step sequentially).

## Method

The paper formulates GCoT as a multi-step decision process P(A|I,T) = ∏ P(R_t, G_t | I,T, G_<t, R_<t), where each step produces a text thought R_t and spatial coordinates G_t, distinguishing it from prior visual CoT work that focuses on knowledge reasoning without grounding. The MM-GCoT benchmark is constructed via a four-stage pipeline: (1) IoU-based alignment of Visual Genome region descriptions with object annotations, (2) construction of a spatial-semantic relationship graph and iterative path sampling to generate multi-step reasoning chains, (3) structured template filling with bounding boxes and attributes, and (4) LLM-based translation into natural language questions with manual validation. A novel answer-grounding consistency metric (Con.) is introduced, defined as |S_ca,cb| / (|S_ca,cb| + |S_ca,wb| + |S_wa,cb|), which quantifies visual hallucination by measuring whether correct answers are supported by correct grounding. Experiments on 12 MLLMs (LLaVA, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL2.5, Qwen2.5-VL) reveal that consistency is low across all models, uncorrelated with parameter scale, and degrades substantially under multi-step grounding-CoT prompting.

## Results

On the MM-GCoT benchmark (1200 samples, 3 task types), the best overall performer under the Answer-First setting is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieving 71.1% answer accuracy (A-Acc), 63.5% grounding accuracy (G-Acc), and 56.3% answer-grounding consistency (Consist.). However, even the best model shows a large gap between answer accuracy and consistency, indicating persistent visual hallucination. The largest models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 38.8% Consist., InternVL2.5-78B at 36.6% Consist.) perform worse on consistency than their smaller counterparts, and LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B scores 0% on both G-Acc and Consist. across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the four model families evaluated, Qwen2.5-VL-7B dominates on the Answer-First setting with the best G-Acc (63.5%) and Consist. (56.3%), outperforming LLaVA-13B (13.2% G-Acc, 13.3% Consist.) by over 50 points on grounding and InternVL2.5-38B (44.4% G-Acc, 39.2% Consist.) by ~17 points on consistency. Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the highest A-Acc (73.2%) but drops sharply to 39.7% G-Acc and 38.8% Consist., falling below its own 7B variant by 24.3% in G-Acc and 17.5% in Consist. InternVL2.5-38B is the strongest in the InternVL family (44.4% G-Acc, 39.2% Consist.), but the 78B variant degrades to 42.9% G-Acc and 36.6% Consist., confirming that scaling does not improve grounding reliability. LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B is unable to produce any valid bounding boxes (0% G-Acc and Consist. across all settings), while LLaVA-OneVision-72B reaches only 16.4% G-Acc and 15.3% Consist., far behind Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Under the Grounding-CoT setting, all models degrade substantially, with InternVL2.5-38B dropping from 39.2% to 17.8% Consist., indicating that multi-step grounded reasoning remains a major unsolved challenge for current MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three existing threads—visual chain-of-thought (LLaVA-CoT, Visual CoT), grounded question answering (Visual7W), and hallucination evaluation (HallusionBench)—into a unified diagnostic task that requires per-step spatial grounding alongside multi-step reasoning. It departs from visual CoT by shifting focus from knowledge-based reasoning to spatial-visual perception, and from grounded QA by inserting annotated intermediate reasoning steps with bounding boxes at each step. The closest prior work, VoCoT, also uses grounded reasoning steps but differs in dataset construction and does not introduce the answer-grounding consistency metric or the hallucination-diagnosis framing that anchors this paper.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The answer-grounding consistency metric offers a concrete, quantitative way to measure whether an MLLM's answer is actually supported by what it sees, addressing a gap that existing hallucination benchmarks (which test answer correctness in isolation) cannot fill. The finding that consistency does not improve—and can even degrade—with model scale challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling resolves perception failures, suggesting a data or training-objective bottleneck rather than a capacity one. Open questions include whether the 1200-sample MM-GCoT benchmark is sufficiently diverse to generalize beyond Visual Genome-derived scenes, whether the three prompt settings capture the full range of deployment scenarios, and how one might actually train models to produce reliable GCoT (the authors gesture at RL/GRPO but do not pursue it).
