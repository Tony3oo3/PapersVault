## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from memorization, where generated samples can exactly replicate training data, yet in practice neural networks produce novel samples despite theory suggesting perfect score learning leads to pure replication. This paper asks how neural networks partially resolve memorization and provides a theoretical framework showing that the empirical score function's sharp softmax weights cause individual training points to dominate, while neural networks implicitly smooth these weights to enable local manifold exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263984/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes random noise vectors and iteratively denoises them to produce synthetic RGB images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 256×256 (CelebA-HQ). A key architectural difference is that the unconditioning variant removes the noise-level input from the score network, so the network s_θ(x) is evaluated without knowing the current noise scale. For temperature smoothing, KNN is computed in a 512-dimensional ResNet-18 feature space (rather than pixel space) to identify the top-K nearest training samples whose explicit score contributions are used during training.

## Method

The paper proves that the empirical score function is a weighted sum of per-sample Gaussian scores with sharp softmax weights, and that in high dimensions a single nearest training point dominates at low noise levels, causing sampling collapse (memorization). Two smoothing methods are proposed: (1) Noise Unconditioning removes the noise-level conditioning from the score network, unifying all marginal distributions into a single M×N Gaussian mixture p_MN so that sampling becomes gradient ascent on log p_MN, allowing each training point to adaptively select its optimal noise shell; (2) Temperature Smoothing introduces a temperature vector T_i into the softmax weights, with T_i = max(σ_collapse/σ_i, 1), so that at small noise levels the top-K nearest training samples contribute with softened weights rather than a single point dominating. The complete training loss adaptively switches between the unconditioning objective (for σ > σ_collapse) and the temperature-based explicit score matching (for σ ≤ σ_collapse), and the approach is validated across CIFAR-10, CelebA, ImageNet, and CelebA-HQ with FID metrics and a custom cat-caracal dataset for qualitative generalization analysis.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (32×32, SDE-PC, 1K NFE), the standard conditioning baseline achieves FID 6.49 (vs. train) / 6.56 (vs. test), while unconditioning yields 7.33 / 7.34 and the best temperature-smoothing variant (T_i=7/σ_i, K=100, feature-space KNN) reaches 7.96 / 7.98. On CelebA (64×64, SDE-PC, 1K NFE), conditioning achieves FID 8.78 / 8.54 (feature-space KNN), while temperature smoothing with T_i=10/σ_i, K=30 attains 8.40 / 8.19. The paper reports that FID slightly degrades under stronger smoothing, but this is accompanied by visible generalization (e.g., novel cat-caracal hybrids), and feature-space KNN consistently outperforms pixel-space KNN across all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard VE-SDE with noise conditioning [Song et al., ICLR 2021]. On CIFAR-10, the conditioning baseline achieves the lowest FID (6.49/6.56), while unconditioning (7.33/7.34) and temperature smoothing (7.96–8.35/7.89–8.30 with feature-space KNN) trade a modest FID increase (≈0.5–1.5) for visible generalization. On CelebA, the gap narrows: the best temperature-smoothing variant (T_i=10/σ_i, K=30, feature-space KNN) actually achieves a slightly lower FID (8.40/8.19) than conditioning (8.78/8.54). A consistent finding across both datasets is that feature-space KNN (via a pre-trained ResNet-18) outperforms pixel-space KNN by 0.2–1.0 FID in every setting, and enables much higher temperatures (e.g., T_i=7/σ_i) without quality collapse. The paper's central claim is not FID superiority but that the smoothing methods produce novel, non-memorized samples (demonstrated on the cat-caracal dataset) while keeping FID within a small margin of the baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VE-SDE framework of Song et al. (ICLR 2021) and the NCSN score-matching loss of Song & Ermon (NeurIPS 2019), modifying both by removing noise conditioning from the network input and introducing temperature-scaled softmax weights. Conceptually, it departs from prior memorization analyses (Carlini et al. 2023, Somepalli et al. 2023, Yoon et al. 2023) that identified the phenomenon empirically or via Fokker-Planck arguments, by providing an explicit geometric decomposition of the empirical score into sharp Gaussian-shell weights and showing that neural networks implicitly smooth these weights. The denoising score matching framework of Vincent (2011) underpins the loss derivation, while the manifold hypothesis provides the geometric intuition for why local-manifold exploration (rather than point collapse) is the right generalization mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean theoretical resolution to a long-standing theory-practice inconsistency: if perfect score learning leads to memorization, why do neural networks produce novel samples? The answer—that capacity limits and regularization cause implicit smoothing of softmax weights—unifies several previously puzzling observations (interpolation experiments, isolated-point memorization, scale-dependent behavior). The two proposed methods (Noise Unconditioning and Temperature Smoothing) are practical and plug-and-play, though empirical FID gains are modest and sometimes slightly negative, suggesting the primary value is conceptual clarity rather than a new SOTA. Open questions include whether these smoothing strategies transfer to latent diffusion models (the feature-space KNN result hints they should), how they interact with classifier-free guidance, and whether the temperature parameter can be learned adaptively rather than set heuristically.
