## Problem

Existing face anonymization methods either fail to maintain a stable virtual identity across multiple images of the same person (making them unsuitable for identity-related applications) or irreversibly discard the original identity, leaving no mechanism to verify ownership or trace provenance when misuse occurs. No prior work simultaneously guarantees anonymity, cross-instance virtual identity consistency, and a verifiable link back to the originating user.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263601/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an aligned 512×512 RGB face image and a user-specific key (a stored random seed, latent code, or condition vector) as input, and produces a photorealistic 512×512 virtual face image with a different identity but preserved head pose and facial expression. The output image carries an imperceptible 128-bit watermark derived from a privacy-preserving transformation of the user's biometric token, which can be decoded to authenticate ownership without revealing the real face. The virtual identity embedding is sampled in the ℓ₂-normalized ArcFace feature space (S^{d-1}), ensuring the generated identity is angularly separated from the original by a configurable margin τ_a.

## Method

The framework builds on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and integrates three modules: (1) a Virtual Identity Sampler that produces a virtual identity embedding distinct from the original while maintaining cross-instance consistency, instantiated as a training-free von Mises-Fisher rejection sampler, a hyperspherical autoencoder (HS-AE) with KL-free tangent-noise training, or a lightweight MLP-based ID-Mixer trained with a multi-task loss balancing anonymity, diversity, and intra-identity consistency; (2) a 3D geometric/expression conditioning pipeline using EMOCA to extract pose and coarse expression (rendered as a normal map for a ControlNet branch) and SMIRK to extract fine-grained FLAME expression codes (injected via an IP-Adapter-style cross-attention module); and (3) a watermark-aware VAE decoder that embeds a 128-bit identity signature during latent-to-pixel decoding, inspired by GenPTW but simplified to remove tamper-localization components. The virtual identity embedding is projected into CLIP text-encoder space and fed through cross-attention as a pseudo-text prompt, while pose and expression signals are injected through separate conditioning branches, cleanly separating identity from geometry and affect.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ, the vMF and HS-AE samplers achieve a virtual identity consistency EER of 0.002±0.001 (AUC 1.000) under ArcFace, substantially outperforming the best baseline RiDDLE (EER 0.046) and even surpassing the original face consistency (EER 0.050). All three samplers achieve 100% Identity Anonymous Rate (IAR) with cosine similarity as low as 0.023 (AdaFace), and the highest diversity scores (Div 0.997–0.999, ODV up to 86.77°). For pose and expression preservation on FFHQ, the method attains the lowest angular offsets (Pitch 2.66°, Roll 1.52°, Yaw 2.30°) and the highest emotion cosine similarity (0.863). The embedded 128-bit watermark is recovered with 0.998 bit accuracy while maintaining PSNR > 39.6 dB and SSIM > 0.974.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines on virtual identity consistency: the vMF/HS-AE samplers achieve EER 0.002 vs. the best baseline RiDDLE (0.046) and IVFG (0.161) under ArcFace, while the ID-Mixer (EER 0.021) still beats every baseline by a wide margin. On anonymity, all three samplers reach 100% IAR with cosine similarity ≤ 0.062, matching or exceeding IVFG (Sim 0.034) and RiDDLE (Sim 0.061), and substantially outperforming CIAGAN (Sim 0.172), FIT (Sim 0.254), and FAS (Sim 0.169). For diversity, the vMF sampler achieves Div 0.997 and ODV 84.06° (ArcFace), surpassing IVFG (Div 0.913, ODV 79.20°) and RiDDLE (Div 0.765, ODV 75.13°). On pose and expression preservation (Table 5), the method attains the lowest angular offsets (e.g., Yaw 2.30° on FFHQ vs. G2Face 3.04° and RiDDLE 4.26°) and the highest emotion similarity (0.863 vs. G2Face 0.793). The only area where the method is not the best is FID (38.04 on FFHQ vs. G2Face 7.75) and bounding-box/landmark distances, which the authors attribute to the full-identity regeneration design rather than local editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of face anonymization and image watermarking, departing from the two dominant prior paradigms: reversible identity transformation (FIT, RiDDLE, G2Face) which limits anonymity by keeping the original face recoverable, and one-way identity mapping (IVFG) which discards all provenance. It builds directly on IVFG's multi-task training objective for the ID-Mixer sampler and on GenPTW's latent watermarking strategy for the diffusion decoder, but repurposes the watermark not for forensic tamper localization but as a lightweight 128-bit identity binding signal. The 3DMM-based pose/expression preservation (EMOCA + SMIRK) is an engineering integration of existing 3D face analysis tools into a diffusion generation pipeline, rather than a new reconstruction method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in virtual identity systems: prior methods force a binary choice between anonymity (irreversible, untraceable) and accountability (reversible, less private), and this work offers a third option via embedded watermarking that enables ownership verification without exposing biometric identity. The near-perfect virtual identity consistency (EER 0.002, exceeding even the original face consistency of 0.050) and the negligible visual cost of watermarking (PSNR > 39.6 dB) make the approach deployable. Open questions include watermark robustness under adversarial editing or re-photographing, whether the 128-bit signature is sufficient for large-scale user populations, and how the framework extends to video or multi-modal identity verification.
