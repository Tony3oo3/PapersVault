## Problem

Existing mmWave datasets for human pose estimation are scarce and lack diversity in both point cloud attributes (noise, density, motion sensitivity) and human poses, severely limiting model generalization to unseen environments. While unlabeled mmWave data and diverse LiDAR datasets are readily available, leveraging them for mmWave training is challenging due to the fundamentally different sensing physics between the two modalities and the absence of co-labeled multi-modal pairs.

## Input / Output

The HPE model takes a temporal sequence of 5 consecutive mmWave point clouds, each uniformly processed to 256 points via truncation or padding, with only the point's height used as a per-point feature to ensure hardware independence. The output is a 15-keypoint 3D skeleton (joint coordinates in Cartesian space) at the current timestep. The pseudo-label estimator and the inference model share the same network architecture (P4T or SPiKE) and input format.

## Method

EMDUL expands an mmWave dataset through two independent modules. First, a closed-form PC converter translates LiDAR point clouds into mmWave counterparts via a sequential pipeline of Noisy Point Addition, Flow-based Point Filtering (FPF), Random Sampling, and Noise Injection; FPF interpolates per-point 3D flow from skeleton flow using inverse-distance weighting over an extended skeleton (joints plus 8 bounding-cube vertices) and then stochastically discards low-flow points to simulate mmWave's Doppler-based motion detection. Second, a pseudo-label estimator is trained jointly with a supervised MSE loss on labeled data and a novel Unsupervised Temporal Consistency Loss (UTCL) on unlabeled data, where UTCL enforces the physical prior that joints near detected points should be dynamic (Dynamic Consistency Loss) while joints far from points should be static (Static Consistency Loss). During training, the pseudo-label estimator is iteratively refined alongside the inference model, regenerating pseudo-labels each epoch to progressively extract information from unlabeled data.

## Results

On the in-domain F→F setting (10% labeled MM-Fi), EMDUL with P4T achieves 10.06 cm MPJPE and 7.01 cm PA-MPJPE, while EMDUL with SPiKE achieves 10.40 cm MPJPE and 7.23 cm PA-MPJPE. On the out-of-domain B→F setting (trained on mmBody, tested on MM-Fi), EMDUL with SPiKE achieves 22.80 cm MPJPE and 14.09 cm PA-MPJPE, representing an 18.9% MPJPE reduction over the best 10%-labeled baseline. The paper reports a 15.1% MPJPE reduction in-domain and 18.9% out-of-domain, with the largest gains appearing in cross-dataset generalization scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMDUL outperforms all five baselines in the majority of settings. Against the strongest 10%-labeled baselines (P4T and SPiKE), EMDUL reduces MPJPE by 15.1% on F→F (10.06 vs. 11.85 cm) and by up to 32% on B→F (22.80 vs. 33.62 cm for SPiKE vs. P4T). Compared to the Mean Teacher (MT) semi-supervised baseline under the same full-data setting, EMDUL wins in 7 out of 8 configurations; with SPiKE as the backbone, EMDUL surpasses MT by 17.5% MPJPE on F→F (10.40 vs. 12.45 cm) and 30.3% on B→F (22.80 vs. 32.71 cm). PT [39] and mmDiff [12] are clearly weaker baselines, with mmDiff showing particularly poor cross-dataset transfer (44.00 cm MPJPE on F→B). The PC conversion component (EMDUL-PCC) alone already beats the raw LiDAR-augmented P4T/SPiKE baselines on out-of-domain F→B (15.22/15.41 vs. 16.08/17.29 cm), confirming that the flow-based filtering is critical for realistic mmWave simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of semi-supervised learning and cross-modal data augmentation for mmWave HPE. Prior data expansion efforts either synthesize mmWave PCs from video skeletons (MIDAS, Video2mmPoint) without diversifying PC attributes, or require co-labeled mmWave-LiDAR pairs for augmentation (DenserRadar, diffusion-based super-resolution). EMDUL departs from both by leveraging an *independent* LiDAR dataset (no pairing) and unlabeled mmWave data, introducing a closed-form PC conversion pipeline (with flow-based point filtering to simulate Doppler-based motion detection) and a novel Unsupervised Temporal Consistency Loss that encodes the physical prior that joints near detected points are likely moving while distant joints are static. The pseudo-labeling framework draws on mean-teacher-style semi-supervised learning but is specifically adapted to the point-cloud HPE setting with the UTCL as a domain-specific regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in mmWave HPE: labeled datasets are small, homogeneous in PC attributes, and limited in pose diversity, directly capping model generalization. The 15.1% in-domain and 18.9% out-of-domain MPJPE reductions are practically meaningful, and the approach is deployable because it requires only readily available data sources (unlabeled mmWave captures and any annotated LiDAR HPE dataset) without expensive multi-modal co-labeling. Limitations include reliance on a LiDAR dataset with ground-truth skeletons, evaluation on only two mmWave datasets, and the somewhat artificial 10%-labeled-data protocol; whether the conversion fidelity holds for radically different LiDAR hardware or for multi-person scenes remains open. The finding that pseudo-labeling alone can *hurt* out-of-domain performance (due to distributional overlap with the labeled set) while PC conversion is the primary driver of generalization is a useful caution for practitioners combining semi-supervised and cross-modal strategies.
