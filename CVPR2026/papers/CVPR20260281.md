## Problem

Existing LLM-based text-to-motion models suffer from three interrelated limitations: (1) standard VQ-VAE quantization loses fine-grained motion details, (2) the non-causal global encoding of VQ-VAE fundamentally mismatches the causal autoregressive nature of LLMs, and (3) no prior method incorporates human preference alignment, leading to common errors like mirrored or incomplete motions. These issues collectively cap the quality and semantic fidelity of generated motions.

## Input / Output

The primary input is a natural language text description (optionally augmented with an initial pose), and the output is a variable-length 3D human motion sequence parameterized by SMPL body keypoints and rotation parameters. The motion is internally represented as V=4 levels of causal motion tokens produced by the Causal RVQ-VAE, where the base level encodes global motion and residual levels capture progressively finer details. The framework also supports motion captioning (pose sequence → text) and conditional generation (text + initial pose → motion), all within a single LLM.

## Method

MoTiGA builds on Llama 7B (fine-tuned via LoRA, rank 64) and introduces three key components. First, a Causal RVQ-VAE replaces standard VQ-VAE by inserting 1D causal convolutions into the encoder and decoder, ensuring each time step's encoding depends only on current and past frames while retaining iterative residual quantization across V=4 levels to minimize quantization error. Second, a time-lagged causal prediction strategy enables parallel decoding across all V levels by temporally staggering predictions: coarser tokens are generated for future time steps while finer tokens are generated for earlier steps, preserving the causal dependency structure without the sequential overhead of step-by-step decoding. Third, Multi-level Hybrid-weighted Preference Optimization (MHPO) extends GRPO by replacing the uniform binary reward with a dynamic per-level reward that blends the overall preference signal with continuous semantic similarity scores (measured by TMR) at each quantization level, weighted by α=0.6, and applies stronger penalties to non-preferred samples with low top-level similarity. Training uses the newly released HumanML3D-R dataset of 101,490 human-annotated preference pairs.

## Results

On HumanML3D, MoTiGA (Llama) achieves an FID of 0.041, R-Precision Top-1 of 52.3%, and MM-Dist of 2.991, representing an 82.3% FID improvement over the best prior LLM-based method (MotionGPT FLAN-T5, FID 0.232). On KIT-ML, MoTiGA reaches FID 0.180 and R-Precision Top-1 of 44.3%, a 64.7% FID improvement over MotionGPT (FLAN-T5, FID 0.510). Notably, MoTiGA's FID of 0.041 on HumanML3D is competitive with the best task-specific method MoMask (FID 0.045) while surpassing it on R-Precision Top-1 (52.3% vs. 52.1%). For conditional generation with initial pose, MoTiGA generates each sample in 3.684 seconds versus 14.494 seconds for MotionGPT (Llama), a 74.5% speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LLM-based baselines, MoTiGA dominates on all metrics: it reduces FID from 0.232 (MotionGPT FLAN-T5) to 0.041 on HumanML3D and from 0.510 to 0.180 on KIT-ML, while also improving R-Precision Top-1 by 3.1 and 7.7 points respectively. Against the strongest task-specific method MoMask (CVPR'24), MoTiGA achieves a slightly lower FID (0.041 vs. 0.045 on HumanML3D; 0.180 vs. 0.204 on KIT-ML) and higher R-Precision Top-1 (52.3% vs. 52.1%; 44.3% vs. 43.3%), though MoMask retains a marginal edge on MM-Dist (2.958 vs. 2.991 on HumanML3D). Compared to T2M-GPT (CVPR'23), MoTiGA improves FID by 64.7% (0.041 vs. 0.116) and R-Precision Top-1 by 3.2 points. Against MotionLLM (Gemma, TPAMI'25), MoTiGA achieves a 91.6% FID reduction on HumanML3D (0.041 vs. 0.491) and a 76.9% reduction on KIT-ML (0.180 vs. 0.781). For the conditional task (text + initial pose), MoTiGA achieves FID 0.040 in 3.684 s per sample, outperforming MotionGPT (Llama) which reports FID 0.493 in 14.494 s, demonstrating both superior quality and a 74.5% inference speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-based text-to-motion generation paradigm established by MotionGPT (Zhang et al., AAAI 2024) and Jiang et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which treat motion as a foreign language for decoder-only LLMs. It departs from prior work in three specific ways: (1) replacing the non-causal VQ-VAE tokenizer with a Causal RVQ-VAE that inherits the iterative residual quantization from MoMask (Guo et al., CVPR 2024) but adds causal convolutions to match the autoregressive nature of LLMs; (2) introducing a time-lagged causal prediction strategy that resolves the tension between parallel multi-level decoding efficiency and strict temporal causality; and (3) adapting the GRPO preference optimization framework (Shao et al., 2024) into MHPO with multi-level semantic similarity weighting, a task-specific reward shaping not present in the original formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of HumanML3D-R (101,490 human preference pairs) is the first large-scale preference dataset for text-to-motion generation, providing a critical resource that was previously absent from the field. The causal alignment insight—that non-causal VQ-VAE encoding fundamentally mismatches causal LLMs and that this mismatch measurably degrades generation quality—offers a design principle likely applicable to other LLM-based generation tasks with discrete tokenizers. Open questions include whether the preference alignment transfers across datasets without re-annotation, whether the time-lagged strategy generalizes to more than 4 quantization levels, and whether the approach scales to longer or more complex motions (e.g., multi-person or object-interaction scenarios).
