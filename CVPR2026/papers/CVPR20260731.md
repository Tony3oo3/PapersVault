## Problem

Existing driver attention datasets and prediction models rely exclusively on narrow, forward-facing cameras, capturing only a fraction of the driver's visual field and missing critical gaze behaviors during lane changes, turns, and interactions with peripheral or rear objects. This limitation prevents holistic modeling of omnidirectional driver awareness and degrades attention prediction on wide panoramic inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260731/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 consecutive RGB frames (≈0.5 s) from five synchronized cameras (three front + two rear-view at 72° each) that are horizontally concatenated and resized to 1120×224, yielding a 16×3×224×1120 tensor. It produces two outputs: a dense 360° gaze probability map and a per-pixel semantic segmentation map restricted to attended road users (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists, traffic signs, traffic lights). The underlying dataset provides ~1 M frames at 30 Hz with 1280×720 resolution per camera, with instance segmentation masks extracted directly from the CARLA simulator.

## Method

DriverGaze360-Net uses a Video Swin Transformer backbone (pretrained on Kinetics-400) to extract multi-scale spatio-temporal features, which are fused through a shared Conv3D decoder feeding two parallel heads: an attention decoder that outputs the 360° gaze map and an attended-object segmentation decoder that predicts per-pixel semantic labels. The key architectural insight is the attended-object supervision: rather than using full-scene segmentation as input (as in prior work), the authors generate masks retaining only objects the driver is actually fixating on (via binarizing the gaze map and intersecting with road-user instances), forcing the model to focus on attention-relevant entities in inherently sparse panoramic scenes. Training jointly optimizes a combined loss of KL divergence and negative correlation coefficient for attention, plus negative Dice, negative IoU, and cross-entropy for segmentation, with equal weighting (λ=1). The attended-object head provides auxiliary spatial guidance that yields consistent gains across all saliency metrics, particularly on wide panoramic inputs where attention signals are sparse and localized.

## Results

On the DriverGaze360 dataset, DriverGaze360-Net achieves KLD 1.067, SIM 0.515, CC 0.667, and NSS 6.309, outperforming the second-best method (FBLNet) by 12.18%, 4.24%, 4.51%, and 4.94% respectively. On the real-world DADA-2000 dataset, the method achieves KLD 1.654, SIM 0.396, CC 0.506, and NSS 3.478, improving over the second-best baseline by 7.32%, 4.55%, and 3.36% on SIM, CC, and NSS, while trailing by only 0.48% on KLD. The attended-object segmentation head further boosts attention metrics by 5.32% on KLD and 2.45% on NSS over the attention-only baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriverGaze360-Net outperforms all five baselines on every metric (KLD, SIM, CC, NSS) on the panoramic DriverGaze360 dataset. The closest competitor, FBLNet [6], is surpassed by 12.18% on KLD (1.067 vs. 1.215) and 4.94% on NSS (6.309 vs. 6.012), with consistent 4–5% gains on SIM and CC. DADANet [12] and ViNet++ [14] trail by larger margins, while the older Dr(eye)VE [32] and BDDA [36] baselines show substantially weaker performance, particularly BDDA with KLD 2.566. On the real-world DADA-2000 dataset, DriverGaze360-Net wins on SIM, CC, and NSS against all baselines but is marginally outperformed on KLD by DADANet (1.646 vs. 1.654, a 0.48% gap), confirming strong cross-dataset generalization from simulated panoramic data to narrow-FoV real footage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the driver attention prediction line of work (DR(eye)VE, LBW, BDD-A, DADA-2000) by moving from narrow frontal field-of-view to full 360° panoramic inputs, a setting absent from all prior datasets. Methodologically, it repurposes the Video Swin Transformer backbone for panoramic video saliency and introduces a joint attention-plus-attended-object-segmentation formulation, departing from prior approaches that either ignore object semantics or use full-scene segmentation as input without distinguishing attended from unattended objects. The dataset itself is collected via real human drivers in the CARLA simulator, occupying a middle ground between real-world driving datasets and video-watching paradigms like BDD-A and DADA-2000.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DriverGaze360 fills a critical gap by providing the first large-scale omnidirectional gaze dataset, enabling research on attention during lane changes, turns, and rear-view monitoring that was previously unmodelable. The attended-object segmentation as an auxiliary supervision signal is a practical and interpretable mechanism for improving sparse panoramic saliency, and the demonstrated generalization to real-world DADA-2000 data suggests the approach is not purely simulation-bound. Key open questions include the sim-to-real transfer gap (all training data is synthetic), whether the 360° formulation generalizes to real panoramic hardware, and whether the object-level guidance benefit persists at larger scale or with more diverse driving populations.
