## Problem

Existing video generation models condition on text prompts, camera trajectories, or 2D skeletal poses, but none provide explicit, fine-grained control over the camera wearer's articulated 3D body in egocentric video synthesis. This gap is critical for embodied AI agents that must simulate the visual consequences of planned full-body actions (reaching, turning, hand-object manipulation) from a first-person perspective, where camera motion is driven by head pose and local body parts produce occlusions and interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes 13 past egocentric frames at 480×480 resolution (16 FPS) as visual context and a sequence of 32 target 3D full-body poses (23 joints, each encoded as a 6D relative transformation) as motion control, producing 32 future egocentric frames (2 seconds) at the same resolution. The pose input is structured as an M×23×6 tensor combining relative head motion, relative pelvis motion, and pelvis-relative joint positions, capturing both global camera dynamics and articulated body state in a unified egocentric coordinate frame.

## Method

EgoControl builds on the Cosmos 2B latent video diffusion model (DiT architecture, EDM formulation) and introduces a dual-pathway pose control mechanism. The first pathway flattens the entire pose tensor and maps it through two-layer MLPs to produce AdaLN modulation vectors (shift, scale, gate) that conditionally modulate self-attention, cross-attention, and MLP blocks in every DiT layer, with the diffusion timestep integrated additively into the same modulation. The second pathway preserves temporal structure by projecting each frame's 23-joint pose into a token sequence with sinusoidal positional encoding, which serves as cross-attention context for frame-level alignment. The pose representation uses differential (frame-to-frame) relative transformations for head and pelvis motion, and pelvis-centric coordinates for the remaining 21 joints, which ablations show outperforms cumulative or per-joint differential encodings. Training is supervised on the Nymeria dataset (186 videos, ~50 hours), fine-tuning the pretrained Cosmos backbone with 13 conditioning frames and 32 target frames.

## Results

On the Nymeria dataset, EgoControl with full-body pose control achieves SSIM 58.60, LPIPS 26.71, DreamSim 8.54, and FVD 20.18, substantially outperforming the base Cosmos backbone (SSIM 42.29, FVD 71.00) and the head-only control variant (SSIM 56.94, FVD 22.68). For body alignment, the full-body model reaches mIoU 52.13 and 96.33% arm-visibility accuracy, a ~55% relative improvement in mIoU over head-only control (33.70). Global motion errors drop to TransError 4.90 and RotError 2.96, compared to 16.53 and 15.60 for the unconditioned Cosmos baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Cosmos backbone, EgoControl improves every metric: FVD drops from 71.00 to 20.18, mIoU rises from 20.03 to 52.13, and TransError falls from 16.53 to 4.90. Compared to PEVA (which generates a single 224×224 frame 2 s in the future from 15 past frames), EgoControl achieves lower LPIPS (24.3 vs. 29.8), lower DreamSim (11.3 vs. 18.6), and lower FID (50.68 vs. 61.10) despite using a shorter context window (13 vs. 15 frames). Against CAMI2V, a camera-control model fine-tuned on Nymeria, EgoControl wins on all four reported metrics: SSIM 58.87 vs. 43.28, TransError 2.22 vs. 4.60, RotError 1.33 vs. 3.79, and mIoU 56.85 vs. 24.72, demonstrating that full-body pose conditioning provides substantially richer control than camera-trajectory conditioning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoControl builds directly on the Cosmos latent video diffusion model (DiT-based, EDM formulation) as its backbone, extending it with a dedicated pose-conditioning pathway. It departs from prior pose-controllable video generation—which has operated exclusively in the exocentric regime with 2D skeletal poses (via cross-attention, concatenation, or ControlNet-style mechanisms)—by formulating the problem in the egocentric setting with 3D full-body poses where the camera is mounted on the subject. The control mechanism itself synthesizes ideas from adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN, used in DiT/Cosmos for timestep conditioning) and cross-attention (used in ControlNet-style architectures), combining both to handle global camera dynamics and fine-grained articulated motion respectively. The closest concurrent work, PEVA, generates a single low-resolution frame per step conditioned on 3D upper-body pose, whereas EgoControl produces temporally coherent multi-frame video at higher resolution with full-body control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first model to enable explicit 3D full-body pose control over egocentric video generation, a capability that is a prerequisite for embodied AI agents to internally simulate the visual consequences of planned actions (navigation, manipulation, hand-object interaction). The paper identifies two key open limitations: the absence of explicit hand-pose annotations in the training data (Nymeria) prevents fine-grained hand articulation control, and the training distribution is biased toward fisheye-mounted cameras and mocap-suited subjects, raising generalization concerns for everyday viewpoints and clothing. Future work will need to incorporate explicit hand representations and broaden training data diversity to close the gap between controllable egocentric synthesis and realistic embodied simulation for downstream planning tasks.
