## Problem

Existing evaluations of MLLMs on spatial intelligence are fragmented, focusing on superficial queries (e.g., positional relationships) with narrow scopes (single modality, single question type, or isolated skills), while neglecting rigorous visual geometry perception such as camera pose estimation and 3D dynamics. This paper asks: to what extent do current MLLMs actually achieve in holistic spatial intelligence, and what complementary strategies can close the remaining gap to human-level performance?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263125/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark accepts a textual question paired with visual input in three forms: single RGB images, multi-frame image sequences, or video clips (uniformly sampled at 32 frames). Outputs are free-text spatial answers scored in three formats—Yes/No judgment, multi-choice, and open-ended QA—with open-ended numerical answers evaluated via Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) and a dual extraction protocol (parsing functions plus GPT-OSS-20B). For SpatialAgent, the output additionally includes structured tool invocation plans and intermediate tool outputs (depth maps, camera extrinsics, optical flow fields, 3D bounding boxes) before the final summarized answer.

## Method

The paper contributes three complementary components. First, SpatialScore is constructed by repurposing precise 3D annotations (bounding boxes, camera poses) from five datasets (ScanNet++, Omni3D, WildRGB-D, PointOdyssey, CA-1M) into QA pairs via template-based generation and LLM rewriting, then integrating and curating samples from 23 existing spatial benchmarks down to 5,025 manually verified samples across 30 tasks. Second, SpatialCorpus (331K QA pairs) is built with the same repurposing pipeline plus 2D simulator rendering for mental animation, used to supervised fine-tune Qwen3-VL with standard cross-entropy loss. Third, SpatialAgent is a training-free multi-agent system with 12 open-source expert tools (VGGT for camera pose, Depth-Anything-V2 for metric depth, RAFT for optical flow, Rex-Omni+SAM2 for detection/segmentation, DetAny3D for 3D detection, OrientAnything for orientation, MapAnything for 3D reconstruction, SIFT for homography) orchestrated under two paradigms: Plan-Execute (planner generates a k-step tool sequence, executor runs it, summarizer produces the answer) and ReAct (observer iteratively selects tools based on accumulated memory, executor processes each action, until a Terminate signal triggers summarization).

## Results

On SpatialScore (5,025 samples, 30 tasks), Gemini-3-Pro achieves the best overall accuracy of 60.12%, followed by GPT-5 (58.13%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (56.63%, best open-source). A 26.48-point gap remains between the best model and human-level performance (86.60%). The authors' data-driven approach (SFT on SpatialCorpus) improves Qwen3-VL-4B from 42.52% to 52.99% (+10.47) and Qwen3-VL-8B from 45.48% to 54.71% (+9.23), while the training-free SpatialAgent yields +6.41 (PE) and +7.78 (ReAct) for the 4B model and +7.27 (PE) and +8.33 (ReAct) for the 8B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-3-Pro (60.12) outperforms GPT-5 (58.13) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (56.37) by 1.99 and 3.75 points respectively, while Claude-4.5-Sonnet (45.68) trails significantly. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (56.63) leads, surpassing InternVL3-78B (50.67) by 5.96 points and Qwen3-VL-32B (54.11) by 2.52 points. Notably, spatial-specific fine-tuned models show limited gains: SpaceR-7B (43.36) only marginally beats its base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (40.53), while SpaceLLaVA-13B (23.97) actually underperforms LLaVA-1.5-13B (26.67), and SpaceThinker-3B (34.47) trails Qwen2.5-VL-3B (36.92). The authors' SFT on SpatialCorpus yields the largest single improvement (+10.47 for 4B, +9.23 for 8B), while SpatialAgent provides smaller but more task-uniform gains without any training. The blind text-only GPT-5 baseline (30.62) confirms that visual input is essential, as it sits only slightly above chance level (28.29).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a unifying and comprehensive successor to a fragmented landscape of spatial intelligence benchmarks (SpatialEval, VSI-Bench, RoboSpatial, 3DSR-Bench, SPAR-Bench, OmniSpatial, etc.), each of which covers only a narrow slice of spatial reasoning with limited modalities or QA formats. Methodologically, SpatialAgent directly adopts the ReAct paradigm (Yao et al., ICLR 2023) and the broader multi-agent orchestration literature (AutoGen, MetaGPT, Reflexion), repurposing them for spatial perception by integrating 12 specialized open-source tools (VGGT, Depth-Anything-V2, RAFT, SAM2, Rex-Omni, DetAny3D, OrientAnything, MapAnything, SIFT). The data-driven pathway builds on the 3D annotation repurposing strategy from datasets like ScanNet++, Omni3D, and WildRGB-D, extending it into a scalable QA generation pipeline that prior spatial benchmarks had not systematically exploited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 26.48-point gap between the best model (Gemini-3-Pro, 60.12) and human-level performance (86.60) underscores that spatial intelligence remains a fundamental unsolved capability for MLLMs, with critical implications for embodied AI and autonomous navigation. The finding that spatial-specific fine-tuning (SpaceThinker, SpaceR) yields only marginal gains or even degrades performance relative to base models challenges the assumption that domain-specific data automatically transfers, suggesting current spatial training data is too narrow or noisy. Open questions include whether the agent-based approach can scale to more complex multi-step spatial tasks, whether the SFT gains on specific categories (mental animation, camera pose) generalize as model scale increases, and whether the 30-task taxonomy captures the full breadth of spatial intelligence needed for real-world deployment.
