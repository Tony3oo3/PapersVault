## Problem

Existing PBR material generation methods are constrained by small, low-diversity annotated datasets (e.g., MatSynth, Deschaintre et al.) that fail to capture the breadth of materials seen in large-scale image corpora. While fine-tuning or distillation from pretrained image models is straightforward within the same domain, bridging the gap between single-image texture generation and multi-map SVBRDF material synthesis remains an open challenge, limiting both realism and diversity of generated materials.

## Input / Output

The model accepts either a text prompt or an image prompt (an ambient-lit rendering of a target material), encoded via a pretrained CLIP model, and produces five spatially varying PBR maps—base color, normal, height, roughness, and metallic—representing a Cook-Torrance microfacet model with GGX distribution. Base generation occurs at 512×512, then a SDEdit-based refiner upscales to target resolutions up to 4096×4096 using patched diffusion. Inference with the distilled Latent Consistency Model requires only 4 denoising steps plus 2 refinement steps, completing in 0.6 s at 512×512 and 18.6 s at 4096×4096 on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

StableMaterials builds on MatFuse's latent diffusion backbone but replaces the multi-encoder VAE (271M params) with a single-encoder model (101M params) that compresses concatenated material maps into the same disentangled latent space, and additionally fine-tunes a texture encoder to create a shared latent representation between single textures and multi-map materials. The key training innovation is a semi-supervised adversarial distillation scheme: SDXL generates unannotated texture samples from text prompts, and a latent discriminator (initialized from the U-Net encoder, trained with hinge loss in a time-conditional fashion) forces the generator to map both annotated material latents and unannotated texture latents into a common realistic distribution, combining a supervised noise-prediction loss with an adversarial loss on denoised latents. For fast inference, a Latent Consistency Model is distilled to enable 4-step generation, and a novel 'features rolling' technique achieves tileability by shifting feature maps within each convolutional and attention layer of the U-Net rather than rolling noise, avoiding the seams that plague noise-rolling at low step counts. A two-stage pipeline (512×512 base generation followed by SDEdit refinement with strength 0.5 and patched diffusion) produces high-resolution outputs while preserving global consistency.

## Results

On 80 text-conditioned generations, StableMaterials achieves the highest CLIP Score (29.6) among all compared methods, outperforming MatGen (28.8), MatFuse (26.2), and Substance 3D Sampler (24.9). On CLIP-IQA, StableMaterials scores 0.70, matching Substance 3D Sampler (0.71) and surpassing MatGen (0.66) and MatFuse (0.52). In a user study with 20 domain experts, StableMaterials was preferred 105 times versus 66 (Sampler), 25 (MatGen), and 4 (MatFuse), with the highest average rating of 3.50/5. Inference with the distilled LCM (4+2 steps) runs in 4.9 s at 2048×2048 and 18.6 s at 4096×4096, roughly 4× faster than a standard DDIM pipeline (20.6 s and 65.4 s respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

StableMaterials outperforms MatFuse [61] on both metrics (CLIP Score 29.6 vs 26.2; CLIP-IQA 0.70 vs 0.52), consistent with the qualitative finding that MatFuse's 256×256 resolution produces blurry, simplistic outputs. Against MatGen [60], StableMaterials wins on CLIP Score (29.6 vs 28.8) and CLIP-IQA (0.70 vs 0.66), while MatGen exhibits over-sharpening artifacts and struggles with complex prompts. Compared to Substance 3D Sampler [1], StableMaterials achieves a substantially higher CLIP Score (29.6 vs 24.9) but trails by a narrow margin on CLIP-IQA (0.70 vs 0.71), likely because Sampler benefits from a large-scale image model for texture synthesis before SVBRDF estimation. Material Palette [34] is compared qualitatively in Figures 6–7; it requires per-prompt LoRA fine-tuning and suffers from SVBRDF estimation biases, whereas StableMaterials directly outputs PBR maps. In the user study, StableMaterials dominates all baselines (105 vs 66/25/4 preferences; ANOVA F=60.5, p<0.05; χ²=120.44, p<0.001), confirming that the semi-supervised adversarial distillation yields perceptually superior materials.

## Relation to Prior Work

StableMaterials builds directly on the author's prior MatFuse framework, which adapted Latent Diffusion Models to SVBRDF material synthesis, and on the broader LDM/SDXL ecosystem for image generation. It departs from prior material generation methods (MatGen, MaterialPalette, Substance 3D Sampler) that either rely on private data, require per-prompt LoRA fine-tuning, or use a two-step texture-then-estimate pipeline, by introducing a semi-supervised adversarial distillation strategy that bridges the domain gap between unannotated SDXL textures and annotated PBR materials within a shared latent space. The features rolling technique for tileability is a novel departure from the noise rolling approach used in ControlMat/MatGen, shifting the rolling operation from the diffusion input to internal U-Net feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in CG pipelines: material datasets are inherently limited in diversity compared to large-scale image corpora, constraining the generative range of learned material models. By distilling SDXL's distributional knowledge through adversarial training in a shared latent space, StableMaterials demonstrates a template for how domain-specific generators can leverage general-purpose pretrained models without requiring domain-annotated data at scale. Remaining limitations include struggles with spatially complex prompts, occasional hallucination of reflectance properties for unannotated classes, and continued dependence on the coverage of the unannotated texture set. Open questions include whether the semi-supervised distillation strategy generalizes to other multi-map generation tasks (e.g., full 3D asset synthesis) and whether the features rolling approach can be extended to non-tileable but spatially coherent generation.
