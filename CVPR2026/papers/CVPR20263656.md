## Problem

CLIP's pre-training objective emphasizes global image-text semantic alignment, making the model inherently insensitive to the fine-grained local details that anomaly detection requires. Existing CLIP-based ZSAD methods either rely on static, pre-designed text prompts that cannot adapt to diverse anomaly morphologies, or inject global CLS features that further suppress local anomaly signals. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a mechanism to dynamically extract local visual cues as prompts and jointly encode them with text for precise cross-modal alignment.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 518×518 RGB image and predefined normal/abnormal text prompts as input, processed through a ViT-L/14 backbone. It produces two outputs: an image-level anomaly score (softmax over normal/abnormal text embeddings) and a pixel-level anomaly map of size h×w, computed by averaging softmax similarity maps across four intermediate layers (6, 12, 18, 24). The image-level score fuses the global CLS token with multi-scale local patch features aggregated via global average pooling and linear projection.

## Method

DLVP-CLIP introduces three components. First, the Semantic-Aware Local Feature Selector (SLFS) reads the CLS token's attention vector from the final ViT layer, selects the top-K (K=4) highest-attention image patches, and extracts their features as dynamic visual prompt tokens. Second, the Multi-modal Local Prompt (MLoP) module injects these prompt tokens into both the visual and text encoder Transformer layers: in the visual encoder they are concatenated with input tokens (M≪N), while in the text encoder they are projected and combined with learnable vectors, enabling fine-grained cross-modal semantic anchoring; prompts remain learnable for the first J=9 layers and are frozen thereafter. Third, the High-Low Frequency Decomposition (HFD) module applies Discrete Wavelet Transform to split each layer's features into low-frequency (global structure) and high-frequency (local texture) branches, processes them independently via linear projections, and reconstructs via inverse DWT with a residual connection to preserve subtle anomaly textures that ViT's self-attention would otherwise smooth. The model is fine-tuned on MVTec-AD (or VisA for MVTec-AD evaluation) with binary cross-entropy for classification and Dice+Focal loss for segmentation.

## Results

DLVP-CLIP achieves the best image-level AUROC on all 9 evaluated datasets, with standout scores of 99.3% on DAGM, 98.1% on Br35H, and 94.2% on MVTec-AD. At the pixel level, it attains the top AUROC on 8 out of 10 datasets, reaching 98.9% on DTD-Synthetic and 98.8% on SDD2, though it trails Bayes-PFL (91.8%) and AnomalyCLIP (91.1%) on MVTec-AD pixel-level segmentation. Across the 13 industrial and medical datasets, the method demonstrates consistent superiority in both anomaly classification and localization, particularly on texture-based and medical imaging tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLVP-CLIP outperforms all five baselines on every image-level dataset. The closest competitor is Bayes-PFL, which ranks second on most industrial datasets (e.g., 92.3 vs. 94.2 on MVTec-AD, 97.4 vs. 97.9 on SDD2) but drops sharply on medical data (88.2% on HeadCT vs. 95.0% for DLVP-CLIP). AdaCLIP is competitive on industrial object datasets (90.5% on BTAD, 96.0% on SDD2) yet lags by 1–3 points on texture and medical benchmarks. AnomalyCLIP and TPS show similar mid-range performance on industrial tasks but degrade noticeably on medical datasets (e.g., AnomalyCLIP scores 90.3% on BrainMRI vs. 96.2% for DLVP-CLIP). WinCLIP is consistently the weakest baseline, particularly on medical images where it scores below 87% on all three brain datasets. At the pixel level, DLVP-CLIP is slightly behind Bayes-PFL (91.8%) and AnomalyCLIP (91.1%) on MVTec-AD (90.4%) and behind TPS/AnomalyCLIP (94.2%) on BTAD (93.4%), but leads on all other pixel-level benchmarks including the medical datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection lineage (WinCLIP → AnomalyCLIP → AdaCLIP/VCP-CLIP → TPS/Bayes-PFL) by shifting the prompt source from text-space or global visual features to dynamically extracted local visual patches. Prior methods either rely on predefined text prompts to guide local perception (AnomalyCLIP, TPS) or inject global CLS features into the text encoder (AdaCLIP, VCP-CLIP); DLVP-CLIP instead uses the CLS attention map to select semantically critical image patches as visual prompt tokens, then jointly encodes them in both visual and text Transformer layers. The wavelet-based high-low frequency decomposition (HFD) module is conceptually related to FE-CLIP's frequency adapters but operates at the feature level via DWT rather than through separate adapter branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-identified tension in CLIP-based ZSAD—global semantic alignment versus local detail sensitivity—and provides a principled mechanism (attention-guided local patch selection + cross-modal prompt injection) to resolve it. Extending evaluation to seven medical datasets (brain CT/MRI, colon, skin, retina) broadens the practical relevance beyond industrial inspection, where most prior ZSAD work concentrates. Open questions include whether the Top-K local patch selection generalizes to very high-resolution or multi-scale anomaly scenarios, whether the approach transfers to other VLM backbones beyond ViT-L/14, and whether the wavelet decomposition adds value at deeper network layers where frequency mixing is already substantial.
