## Problem

Existing unified video creation models (e.g., VACE, UNIC) require 50–100 denoising steps and complex multi-adapter architectures, making them impractical for real-time deployment. Moreover, standard distribution-matching distillation (DMD) via reverse KL divergence suffers from zero-forcing and gradient collapse in the few-step regime, where the initial gap between real and fake score distributions is large and the mode-seeking nature of KL prevents the student from covering the full teacher distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263009/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

All conditioning signals are unified into a Video Condition Unit V = [T; F; M] (text prompt, context frames, binary masks), where frames and masks are reconfigured per task (e.g., zero-filled frames with all-ones masks for T2V, reference images prepended for R2V, source video with binary masks for MV2V). The model produces a generated video at 832×480 resolution in only 4 denoising steps, compared to 50–100 steps for baselines, supporting 18 single- and composite-condition tasks including pose, depth, optical flow, scribble, greyscale, outpainting, temporal extension, and reference-to-video generation.

## Method

VDOT distills the VACE-Wan2.1-14B teacher into a 4-step generator by replacing the reverse-KL objective in DMD with an Entropic Optimal Transport (EOT) discrepancy computed over flattened spatial latent dimensions via the Sinkhorn algorithm. The resulting OT plan T* provides an explicit frame-level geometric alignment between real and fake score distributions, whose gradient (∂W₂^ε/∂D = T*) regularizes transport direction and prevents the zero-forcing and gradient collapse that plague KL-based few-step distillation. A GAN discriminator—built from three cross-attention blocks (23, 31, 39) of the fake score network with learnable registration tokens—incorporates real video data to correct score-approximation errors and suppress artifacts inherited from the teacher. Training alternates between distribution-matching steps (OTD + λ·DMD loss on the generator; denoising loss on the fake critic) and adversarial steps (GAN loss on both generator and discriminator), with the real score network kept frozen throughout. A two-stage curriculum first distills Wan2.1-T2V-14B via Self-Forcing, then fine-tunes on multi-task data using an AccWanInit strategy that seeds the VACE Wan blocks with the few-step Wan weights for faster convergence.

## Results

VDOT achieves competitive or superior video quality using only 4 denoising steps (NFE), compared to 50–100 steps for all baselines. On the UVCBench normalized average (across six VBench dimensions), VDOT scores 78.50% (Depth), 80.54% (Pose), 80.18% (Flow), 76.77% (Scribble), 72.13% (Grey), 76.54% (Outpaint), 80.53% (Extension), and 81.32% (R2V), matching or exceeding VACE-Wan2.1-14B (100 NFE) on 6 of 8 tasks. In the user study (1–5 Likert scale), VDOT attains an average of 4.46–4.76 across tasks, on par with or slightly above VACE-Wan-14B (4.43–4.75) while being 25× faster in inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against task-specific baselines, VDOT (4 NFE) substantially outperforms all of them: on Depth it beats Control-A-Video (78.50% vs 71.16%) and ControlVideo (78.50% vs 72.46%); on Pose it exceeds Follow-Your-Pose by over 11 points (80.54% vs 69.55%); on Flow it surpasses FLATTEN (80.18% vs 77.26%); and on Outpaint it leads Follow-Your-Canvas (76.54% vs 72.59%). Against the unified VACE-Wan2.1-14B (100 NFE), VDOT matches or slightly exceeds on Depth (+1.16%), Pose (+0.98%), Scribble (+0.14%), Outpaint (+1.05%), and Extension (+3.43%), while being marginally lower on Flow (−0.17%), Grey (−0.31%), and R2V (−1.22%). Among commercial R2V systems, VDOT slightly trails Keling-1.6 (81.32% vs 83.50%) but outperforms Vidu-2.0 (81.32% vs 79.71%). In the user study, VDOT's average scores (4.36–4.76) are on par with VACE-Wan-14B (4.43–4.75) and far exceed all task-specific baselines (1.37–4.04), confirming that the 25× speedup comes at negligible quality cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDOT builds directly on VACE's unified video creation architecture (which reformulates heterogeneous conditioning into a Video Condition Unit) and the Self-Forcing distillation paradigm that extends Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) to video. The key conceptual departure is replacing the pure reverse-KL objective in DMD with an Entropic Optimal Transport (EOT) discrepancy, which the authors claim is the first application of OT within distribution-matching distillation. This OT regularizer provides a geometrically meaningful transport plan between real and fake score distributions, addressing the zero-forcing and gradient-collapse pathologies that plague few-step KL-based distillation. An adversarial discriminator is additionally introduced to incorporate real video data, complementing the score-matching objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4-step generator matching 50–100-step baselines on unified video creation opens the door to near-real-time interactive video editing and generation in production settings. UVCBench, covering 18 tasks with composite conditions, fills a notable gap in standardized evaluation for all-in-one video creation. However, the approach is validated only on a single base architecture (VACE-Wan2.1-14B) at 832×480 resolution, and the OT computation operates at frame-level spatial granularity, raising open questions about scalability to higher resolutions or longer video sequences. Whether the OT-regularized distillation generalizes beyond the VACE/Wan family to other DiT or non-DiT video architectures remains untested.
