## Problem

Action-conditioned robot video generation suffers from a representational gap between sparse 7-DoF control inputs and dense pixel outputs, leading to limited visual fidelity, poor temporal consistency, misaligned future predictions, and restriction to single-view settings. Existing methods that add fine-grained cues (optical flow, masks) do not provide a geometrically robust bridge that also supports multi-view coherence and simulation-to-real transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263425/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference first frame, a sequence of 7-DoF end-effector poses (chunked into groups of r for temporal alignment with 3D-VAE compressed latents), and 2D renderings of 4D semantic occupancy (depth + semantic maps) produced by projecting non-learnable Gaussian splats with adaptive depth-dependent scaling (σ = k₂·ẑ^k₁) to mitigate perspective distortion. Outputs are temporally consistent robot manipulation videos in single-view, multi-view (with cross-view consistency), or sim-to-real settings, generated via a bidirectional diffusion process conditioned on both action and occupancy priors.

## Method

ORV builds on CogVideoX-2B (a DiT-based video diffusion model) with a two-stage supervised fine-tuning that injects two complementary conditioning signals. For actions, a chunk-level Action-Expert AdaLN maps every r consecutive 7-DoF actions into a single token via a shallow MLP and modulates video latents within each DiT block, reusing the pretrained Vision Expert AdaLN parameters to avoid extra computation. For visual priors, 4D occupancy grids are assigned non-learnable Gaussian splatting and rendered to 2D maps; these are encoded by an MLP and added to the input noise through a zero-initialized projector (rather than ControlNet-style layer-wise injection), which avoids corrupting latents when conditions are soft. ORV-MV adds a view-attention module (2D over pixels, 1D over views) before the temporal attention to enforce cross-view consistency, while ORV-S2R exploits the domain-invariant nature of occupancy representations to transfer from simulator-derived conditions to real-world generation. The training data (ORV-Data) is curated from BridgeV2, DROID, and RT-1 via MonST3R+NKSR 4D reconstruction, VLM+K-means semantic labeling, and Grounding DINO+SAM2 instance tracking.

## Results

On conditional video generation, ORV (full occupancy+action conditioning) achieves FVD of 16.525 on BridgeV2, 34.603 on DROID, and 19.931 on RT-1, outperforming the best action-conditioned baseline IRASim by 20.9%, 15.7%, and 22.1% respectively in FVD. ORV also attains the highest PSNR (28.258/22.310/28.214) and lowest FID (3.418/3.222/4.013) across all three datasets. On visual planning (VP²), ORV achieves 74.7% average success rate, surpassing iVideoGPT (72.2%) and MCVD (67.2%). For policy learning on SimplerEnv-WidowX, ORV-augmented data yields +4.1% average success for RoboVLM (29.8%→33.9%) and +2.3% for SpatialVLA (36.2%→38.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORV (full) dominates all action-conditioned baselines across every dataset and metric. Against IRASim (the strongest action-only baseline), ORV reduces FVD by 20.9% on BridgeV2 (16.525 vs 20.910), 15.7% on DROID (34.603 vs 41.031), and 22.1% on RT-1 (19.931 vs 25.580), while also improving PSNR by 1.0–2.2 dB. Against HMA, ORV achieves substantially lower FVD (16.5 vs 67.1 on BridgeV2; 34.6 vs 47.4 on DROID) and higher PSNR, though HMA has a slight FID edge on DROID (3.108 vs 3.222). The text-conditioned CogVideoX baseline is far behind on all metrics (e.g., FVD 83.6 vs 16.5 on BridgeV2), confirming the necessity of action and occupancy conditioning. When the same occupancy+action conditions are added to IRASim (IRASim†), ORV still wins on FVD across all three datasets (16.5 vs 22.5, 34.6 vs 44.3, 19.9 vs 42.1), demonstrating that the occupancy injection mechanism (noise-level vs. layer-wise ControlNet) matters. On visual planning (VP²), ORV achieves 74.7% average success, beating iVideoGPT (72.2%), FitVid (67.3%), MCVD (67.2%), and MaskViT (55.0%). For policy learning, ORV-augmented data consistently improves both RoboVLM and SpatialVLA over their finetuned-only counterparts by 2–5% per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORV builds on the CogVideoX-2B diffusion transformer backbone and extends action-conditioned video generation (AVID, HMA, IRASim) by introducing 4D semantic occupancy as a complementary visual prior, rendered via non-learnable Gaussian splatting into 2D soft conditions injected into initial noise. This departs from prior geometric-conditioning approaches (Cosmos-Transfer, RoboTransfer) that use pixel-aligned depth/normal maps via ControlNet-style layer-wise injection, and from autonomous-driving occupancy generation (UniScene, OccSora) by adapting the occupancy-centric paradigm to robot manipulation with 7-DoF action coupling. The multiview extension draws on SyncCamMaster/MVGenMaster view-attention mechanisms, while the sim-to-real transfer leverages the domain-invariance of occupancy representations relative to appearance-specific cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORV addresses a core bottleneck in embodied AI—data scarcity—by providing a controllable, high-fidelity video generation engine that simultaneously improves visual planning (+3.5%) and policy learning (+6.4–13.7%) across multiple robot embodiments. The occupancy-centric design's robustness to geometric noise (coarse vs. fine conditions) opens a practical path for sim-to-real transfer without requiring pixel-perfect alignment between simulator and real-world condition maps, a constraint that limits prior geometry-conditioned methods. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-robot scenarios, whether the 4D occupancy pipeline (MonST3R + NKSR + voxelization) can be made real-time, and whether the gains persist on more diverse or cluttered manipulation tasks beyond the three evaluated datasets.
