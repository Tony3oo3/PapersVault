## Problem

Inverse tone mapping becomes significantly harder when the SDR input is produced by local tone mapping, which jointly applies global radiometric compression and spatially varying adaptations that distort dynamic range, contrast, and channel-wise color ratios. Existing methods either regress HDR values directly (costly and inefficient at 4K) or rely on single-channel gain maps that scale luminance only and cannot restore the compressed wide color gamut, leaving the structured degradation of local TMO unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260751/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution SDR image (4K) as input, downsampled to a 256×256 thumbnail for all neural computation, and produces a three-channel color gain map in log domain normalized to [-1,1] along with a global illuminance scalar Q_max. The final HDR reconstruction is computed in the log domain via I_HDR^log = log2(I_HR^2.2 + 1/64) + Q_max · GM_norm^log, then exponentiated back to linear. Because all high-resolution operations (grid slicing, LUT lookup, affine modulation) are network-free, the pipeline is resolution-agnostic and can process 4K in 6.2 ms.

## Method

FastGaMer explicitly mirrors the forward local tone-mapping degradation by decoupling its inversion into spatially varying local adaptation and global radiometric mapping. A small encoder on the 256×256 thumbnail generates three image-adaptive operators in parallel: (1) K=3 dynamic bilateral grids (8×8×8) that undo spatially varying local distortions via grid slicing, (2) a learnable 3D LUT (17×17×17) that performs fast SDR-to-log-GM translation through tri-linear sampling, and (3) a lightweight MLP that predicts channel-wise affine (α, β) parameters for global GM refinement. The operators are applied sequentially to the full-resolution SDR image with no per-pixel neural computation, keeping the entire high-resolution path network-free. Training uses supervised L1 losses on both the normalized and unnormalized log-GM plus LUT smoothness and monotonicity regularizers, on a newly constructed dataset of 8,150 synthetic 4K SDR-color-GM pairs derived from RAISE RAW imagery and 82 real-captured pairs.

## Results

On the synthetic 4K test set, FastGaMer achieves 32.06 dB PQ-PSNR and 13.89 ΔE_ITP, surpassing the best LUT-based method ITMLUT by +1.4 dB PQ-PSNR and the best network-based method GMNet by +0.27 dB PQ-PSNR while also attaining the lowest ΔE_ITP. On the real-captured test set, FastGaMer reaches 30.02 dB PQ-PSNR and 24.61 ΔE_ITP, again leading all baselines. In terms of efficiency, FastGaMer processes a 4K image in 6.20 ms with only 0.64M parameters and 0.48G FLOPs, making it 70% faster than ITMLUT (18.5 ms) and over two orders of magnitude faster than network-based baselines (455–941 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest LUT-based competitor ITMLUT [11], FastGaMer gains +1.4 dB PQ-PSNR (32.06 vs. 30.66) on the synthetic set and +0.43 dB on the real set, while running 3× faster (6.20 ms vs. 18.5 ms at 4K). Against the strongest network-based baseline GMNet [24], FastGaMer achieves +0.27 dB PQ-PSNR and a lower ΔE_ITP (13.89 vs. 14.08), demonstrating that the LUT-based efficiency does not sacrifice accuracy. Compared to other network baselines (FMNet, KUNet, HDRUNet), FastGaMer leads by 0.8–6.1 dB PQ-PSNR, with the largest gap over HDRUNet (25.94 dB). The two adapted image-enhancement LUT methods (LUTwithGrid, SVDLUT) lag substantially in PQ-PSNR (25.5–25.7 dB) and ΔE_ITP (20.6–21.4), confirming that generic LUT enhancement is insufficient for structured local tone-mapping inversion. On the real-captured test set, the same ranking holds: FastGaMer achieves the best PQ-PSNR (30.02) and lowest ΔE_ITP (24.61), edging out GMNet (29.93 / 24.73) and ITMLUT (29.59 / 25.84).

## Relation to Prior Work

FastGaMer extends the gain-map learning paradigm introduced by GMNet [24] from a single luminance channel to a three-channel color gain map, enabling wide-color-gamut restoration that single-channel GMs provably cannot achieve. It repurposes dynamic bilateral grids (originally from real-time image enhancement [8,17]) as a network-free spatial operator for inverting local tone-mapping degradations, and combines them with learnable 3D LUTs and a lightweight MLP modulator for global radiometric recovery. The key architectural departure from prior LUT-based ITM methods like ITMLUT [11] is the resolution-agnostic design: all neural computation is confined to a 256×256 thumbnail, while high-resolution operations are purely table-lookup and grid-slicing, eliminating the per-pixel network cost that limits prior approaches at 4K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.2 ms 4K runtime with network-level PQ-PSNR makes real-time HDR/WCG up-conversion feasible on consumer-grade hardware (smart TVs, set-top boxes), a deployment target that prior network-based ITM methods (455–941 ms) cannot meet. The color-GM formulation and the 8,000+ 4K SDR-GM dataset address a genuine gap: no public resource previously supported per-channel gain supervision under realistic local tone-mapping degradations. Open questions include generalization to video sequences (temporal coherence), robustness to TMO implementations beyond Adobe Camera Raw, and whether the 256×256 thumbnail feature is sufficient for very large or highly structured scenes where local adaptation varies at scales below the thumbnail resolution.
