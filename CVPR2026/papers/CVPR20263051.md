## Problem

Existing pansharpening methods underperform in spectrally mixed regions (land-cover boundaries, complex textures) because conventional optimization treats all image regions equally, causing structurally complex but pixel-sparse spectral mixing patterns to be overlooked during training. Rigid architectural enhancements or fixed physical constraints can improve specific features but fail to generalize across diverse mixing patterns and limit the model's capacity to learn unseen spectral combinations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263051/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution multispectral image (C channels at h×w) and a high-resolution panchromatic image (1 channel at H×W) as input, producing a high-resolution multispectral fused image (C channels at H×W). Experiments span 8-band (WorldView-3) and 4-band (QuickBird, GaoFen-2) configurations, with evaluation on both reduced-resolution (ground-truth available) and full-resolution (no ground truth) test splits.

## Method

The paper proposes a flexible regularization framework with two components: (1) MixShuffle, a data-level transformation that performs random convex combinations across training samples (sample-level mixing with Beta-distributed weights) and across spectral channels (random permutation-based channel shuffle), generating augmented data with richer spatial structures and stronger spectral mixing; and (2) HAL (Hierarchical Attention Loss), which applies a polynomial weighting function W(x)=x(1+x)^γ to MAE at pixel, channel, and sample levels, then combines the three weighted losses via convex combination to adaptively amplify gradients in challenging regions during early training while reducing to standard L1 as errors diminish. These strategies are integrated into DANet, a dual-scale attention network that uses cascaded Dual-scale Attention Interaction Modules (combining Swin Transformer self-attention blocks with a parameter-efficient Shared Cross Transformer for cross-modal interaction) to establish direct spatial-spectral attention at different scales, mitigating boundary blurring from scale misalignment. The regularization strategies are architecture-agnostic, incurring negligible training overhead with no additional inference cost.

## Results

On the WV3 dataset (8-band), the proposed DANet achieves SAM 2.69±0.42, ERGAS 1.91±0.39, Q2n 0.921, and SCC 0.991, outperforming the next-best ARNet (SAM 2.89, ERGAS 2.14) by 4.6% and 3.5% respectively. On the GF2 dataset (4-band), it reaches SAM 0.58±0.13 and ERGAS 0.53±0.10, surpassing the closest competitor PanMamba (SAM 0.68, ERGAS 0.64) by 14.7% and 5.4%. On the QB dataset, integrating the regularization strategies (MixShuffle + HAL) into existing baselines consistently improves boundary ERGAS by 4.21%–19.03% across seven architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed DANet outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric across both WV3 and GF2. On WV3, it beats the strongest CNN-based competitor ARNet (SAM 2.89, ERGAS 2.14) by 4.6% on SAM and 3.5% on ERGAS, and surpasses the best Transformer-based method FusionMamba (SAM 2.82, ERGAS 2.11) by 4.6% and 9.5% respectively. On GF2, the margin widens substantially: DANet achieves SAM 0.58 vs. PanMamba's 0.68 (14.7% improvement) and ERGAS 0.53 vs. PanMamba's 0.64 (5.4% improvement), while also leading on Q2n (0.987 vs. 0.982) and SCC (0.998 vs. 0.985). The generality experiments on QB (Table 3) show that adding MixShuffle+HAL to any of seven baselines (FusionNet, LAGNet, Panformer, Invformer, PanMamba, FusionMamba, ADWM) consistently improves SAM and ERGAS, with the largest gains on FusionNet (SAM 4.96→4.77, ERGAS 4.19→4.06) and LAGNet (SAM 4.58→4.46, ERGAS 3.87→3.69). Boundary-specific ERGAS (Table 4) confirms the advantage is concentrated in spectrally mixed regions: FusionNet's boundary ERGAS drops from 10.78 to 8.76 on WV3 (18.74% improvement) and from 14.03 to 11.36 on QB (19.03% improvement). No baseline matches or exceeds the proposed method on any primary metric in any dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in deep pansharpening—architecture enhancement (PanNet, Panformer, FusionMamba) and physical-constraint embedding (GPPNN, FAFNet, CDFInet)—by arguing that both rigid inductive biases and fixed priors limit generalization in spectrally mixed regions. Instead, it introduces a regularization-first perspective: MixShuffle adapts the Mixup and Channel Shuffle ideas from general CV to the spatial-spectral structure of pansharpening, while HAL is a novel polynomial-reweighted L1 loss operating hierarchically at pixel, channel, and sample levels. The DANet backbone combines Swin Transformer self-attention with a parameter-efficient Shared Cross Transformer (SCT) that uses shared query-key matrices, distinguishing it from standard cross-attention designs in prior fusion networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The architecture-agnostic nature of MixShuffle and HAL—demonstrated across seven distinct backbones (CNN, Transformer, Mamba)—makes them practical drop-in improvements for existing pansharpening pipelines without retraining from scratch or adding inference cost. The boundary-specific ERGAS gains of up to 19% directly address the most visually and quantitatively problematic regions in fused imagery. However, the approach is validated only on three standard benchmarks with limited sensor diversity, and the hyperparameters (γ, θ₁, θ₂) may require per-dataset tuning; whether the regularization transfers to other remote-sensing fusion tasks (e.g., hyperspectral-panchromatic) or to non-remote-sensing spectral reconstruction remains open.
