## Problem

Existing cloud-edge Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) frameworks assume architectural homogeneity between cloud and edge models, which is impractical as cloud models increasingly adopt Transformers while edge devices are constrained to lightweight CNNs. This architectural gap makes high-fidelity knowledge transfer notoriously difficult under the strict communication and compute budgets of edge deployment, and no prior method effectively bridges it for continual adaptation across shifting domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The edge ResNet-18 consumes streaming RGB test images at their original resolution for low-latency local inference, while the cloud ViT-B processes uploaded samples resized to 224×224. The edge model outputs a class prediction in real time; a bounded buffer (capacity 32) of selected samples is asynchronously uploaded to the cloud, which returns refined predictions and intermediate representations used to update the edge model. The overall system produces a continuously adapted class label per incoming image with minimal edge-to-cloud bandwidth (335.1K samples at severity 5, ~44.7% of the stream).

## Method

CAA deploys a ViT-B teacher in the cloud (stabilized by progressive-masking contrastive learning) and a ResNet-18 student on the edge, connected by two modules. Multi-criteria Dynamic Cross-domain Sampling (MDCS) scores each sample by a weighted combination of prediction uncertainty (symmetric KL under stochastic TTAug) and representativeness (distance to online k-means centroids in the student's layer-4 feature space), maintaining a min-heap buffer under a fixed upload budget; a dual CUSUM + statistical-anomaly detector triggers buffer and centroid resets upon cross-domain shifts. Multi-level Adaptive Heterogeneous Distillation (MAHD) transfers knowledge across the CNN–ViT gap via (i) semantic-consistency alignment (weighted cross-entropy at layers {1,2,4} using teacher soft labels plus a one-hot pseudo-label) and (ii) attention-feature structural alignment (Frobenius-norm matching of QK and VV similarity matrices, with bilinear interpolation to reconcile token counts), all coupled by a difficulty-aware adaptive gate that down-weights structural losses when classification loss is high and up-weights them once predictions stabilize, updated via exponential moving average.

## Results

On ImageNet-C at severity level 5, CAA achieves a state-of-the-art average top-1 accuracy of 41.2%, surpassing the previous best method CoLA (38.6%) by 2.6% and CEMA (38.1%) by 3.1%. CAA outperforms all baselines on 13 out of 15 corruption types, with the largest margins on Motion blur (+6.0% over CoLA) and Glass blur (+3.5% over CoLA). In terms of communication efficiency, CAA uploads only 335.1K samples (44.7% of the test stream) at severity 5, fewer than both CEMA (369.0K) and EATA (344.2K), while simultaneously achieving the highest accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAA outperforms all eight baselines on average accuracy, with the closest competitor being CoLA (38.6%) and CEMA (38.1%), both trailing by over 2.5%. CAA wins on 13 of 15 individual corruption types; the two exceptions are Defocus (26.2% vs. CoLA's 26.3%, a negligible 0.1% gap) and Contrast (33.0% vs. CoLA's 35.0% and CEMA's 35.4%), where the cloud-edge heterogeneous distillation appears less effective. Among the edge-only TTA methods (BN Adaptation, ONDA, LAME, Tent, CoTTA), CAA leads by a wide margin of 6–27% in average accuracy, confirming the substantial benefit of cloud-side ViT guidance. In communication cost, CAA uploads 335.1K samples at severity 5, roughly 9% fewer than CEMA (369.0K) and 3% fewer than EATA (344.2K), while simultaneously achieving the highest accuracy—demonstrating that the MDCS sampling strategy is both more selective and more effective than the upload policies used by competing cloud-edge methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) paradigm—established by CoTTA, ETA, and CoLA—into a heterogeneous cloud-edge setting where the cloud teacher (ViT-B) and edge student (ResNet-18) have fundamentally different architectures. It departs from prior cloud-edge CTTA methods (e.g., CEMA) that assume architectural homogeneity, and from static heterogeneous distillation approaches (e.g., HeteroAKD) by introducing a difficulty-aware, multi-level distillation that jointly aligns attention structure and semantic features. The sampling strategy draws on core-set selection and CUSUM-based change detection, repurposing them for the dynamic, communication-constrained CTTA context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As edge deployments increasingly pair lightweight CNNs with large Transformer backbones in the cloud, this work provides a practical recipe for cross-architecture knowledge transfer under strict bandwidth budgets, which is a gap most prior CTTA work ignores. The communication-efficiency result (335K uploads vs. 369K for CEMA at comparable or better accuracy) is practically relevant for real edge systems. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more extreme architectural gaps (e.g., Mamba vs. CNN), whether the progressive masking on the cloud side is necessary or a conservative choice, and how the method behaves under truly non-stationary (rather than sequential) domain shifts.
