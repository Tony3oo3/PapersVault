## Problem

Physical contaminants adhering to camera lens protectors—mud, water droplets, and condensation—produce severe, spatially irregular artifacts that degrade semantic segmentation, yet this scenario remains largely unexplored. Existing robustness datasets lack realistic physical contamination with pixel-aligned clean/degraded pairs, dense semantic labels, and systematic control over the lens-to-protector distance, which itself induces markedly different refraction and degradation patterns.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides aligned triplets per scene: an original (protector-free) reference, a clean (protector-only) image, and a degraded image with one of three contamination types, each captured at four lens-to-protector distances (0, 1, 3, 5 cm) at 1000×750 resolution. Segmentation outputs are dense pixel-wise masks over 125 semantic classes (19,203 instances total), while restoration outputs are full-resolution RGB images evaluated against the original reference. The 600 scenes are split 480/120 for train/test, with every class appearing in the test set.

## Method

The core contribution is a real-world data collection framework using a custom 3D-printed smartphone holder that mounts replaceable glass plates at precisely controlled distances, enabling reproducible captures of paired clean-contaminated images across mud, water droplets, and condensation. Semantic annotations are created on the original (protector-free) images and transferred to degraded counterparts via the controlled alignment, avoiding unreliable labeling on heavily occluded views. The paper then benchmarks a broad suite of segmentation paradigms—supervised (Mask2Former, VMamba), domain generalization (SoMA, Rein), domain adaptation (DAFormer, MIC), foundation models (DINOv2, SAM), robustness methods (RobustSAM, FIFO), and task-aware restoration (UniRestore, URIE)—alongside restoration baselines (NAFNet, Restormer, MambaIR, DiffUIR). Key findings include that domain generalization trained only on clean images outperforms domain adaptation with access to unlabeled contaminated data, DINOv2 achieves the best overall mIoU (47.2%) among foundation models, and UniRestore is the only restoration method that consistently improves downstream segmentation (+1.6 mIoU overall), suggesting task-aware restoration is a promising direction for perception under real contamination.

## Results

On the CLP test set (600 scenes, 125 semantic classes), the best semantic segmentation mIoU is 48.0% (Rein, domain generalization) and 47.2% (DINOv2 foundation model), both substantially outperforming the supervised Mask2Former baseline (37.6%) and domain adaptation methods (DAFormer: 32.2%). For image restoration, the diffusion-based DiffUIR achieves the highest PSNR of 27.92 dB, followed by UniRestore (26.85 dB), compared to supervised baselines NAFNet (25.76 dB) and Restormer (25.22 dB). In the joint restoration-segmentation pipeline, UniRestore is the only method that consistently improves segmentation over the direct baseline (+1.6 mIoU overall), with the largest gain under condensation (+3.1 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Domain Generalization methods dominate: Rein (48.0% mIoU) and SoMA (46.9%) outperform the best Domain Adaptation baseline (DAFormer, 32.2%) by +15.8 and +14.7 mIoU respectively, despite DA having access to unlabeled contaminated images. Among foundation models, DINOv2 (47.2%) substantially outperforms SAM (39.2%), confirming that prompt-driven architectures generalize poorly under physical lens distortion. Robustness-specific methods (RobustSAM, FIFO) fail to improve over the supervised Mask2Former baseline (37.6%), both scoring only 32.2%, indicating that fog-oriented or object-centric priors do not transfer to real lens contamination. For restoration, DiffUIR (27.92 dB PSNR) leads all baselines, beating NAFNet (25.76 dB) and Restormer (25.22 dB) by 2.2 and 2.7 dB. In the joint pipeline, UniRestore is the sole method that improves segmentation over direct inference (+1.6 mIoU overall), while URIE actually degrades performance (−4.2 mIoU), showing that generic enhancement priors are counterproductive under severe physical artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of robust semantic segmentation and image restoration by introducing the first real-world dataset with physically controlled lens-protector contamination. It departs from prior contamination datasets (SIDL [13], [67]) that captured only fixed smartphone configurations, by adding a systematic lens-to-protector distance dimension (0–5 cm) and providing aligned clean/degraded pairs with dense 125-class semantic labels. Rather than proposing a new architecture, the paper repurposes existing segmentation (Mask2Former, DINOv2, SAM, SoMA, Rein, DAFormer) and restoration (NAFNet, Restormer, DiffUIR, UniRestore) methods as baselines to systematically evaluate how domain generalization, domain adaptation, foundation models, and task-aware restoration strategies perform under this specific physical degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CLP fills a concrete gap: no prior benchmark combined real physical contamination, pixel-aligned clean/degraded pairs, dense semantic annotations, and controlled distance variation, making it a valuable resource for evaluating robust perception in autonomous systems and robotics. The finding that domain generalization methods (trained only on clean images) consistently outperform domain adaptation methods (which have access to unlabeled contaminated data) challenges the assumption that target-domain data is always beneficial, and the observation that higher restoration PSNR does not guarantee better downstream segmentation highlights the need for task-aware restoration. Open questions include whether these findings generalize beyond smartphone-proxy captures to automotive or robotic camera systems, whether larger-scale data collection can close the remaining performance gap under severe mud contamination, and whether the distance-dependent degradation patterns observed here transfer to other optical geometries.
