## Problem

Existing autoregressive font generation methods rely on patch-level tokenization that fragments global stylistic cues, leading to style inconsistencies across a generated glyph set. Additionally, all prior Few-shot Font Generation (FFG) methods operate in a vision-only paradigm, ignoring textual descriptions that convey high-level design intent and could complement limited visual references.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260136/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a content glyph image (defining structure), 2–8 style reference glyph images (defining target appearance), and optionally a free-text style description, all at 64×64 resolution. It outputs generated glyph images at the same resolution, encoded internally as 64 discrete tokens from a 2048-entry codebook. In the multimodal setting, as few as 2 visual references plus one text description can match the quality of 8 visual references alone.

## Method

GAR-Font introduces a Global-aware Tokenizer (G-Tok) with a hybrid CNN-ViT encoder that fuses local convolutional stroke features with global Transformer attention, followed by vector quantization and a causal ViT-CNN decoder, capturing both fine-grained geometry and font-level style. The autoregressive generator is trained in two stages: first on visual inputs with a content-style aggregator and soft decoding (softmax projection onto the codebook for gradient flow), then augmented with a lightweight language-style adapter that aligns Flan-T5 text embeddings to visual style features via iterative cross-attention, avoiding costly joint multimodal pretraining. A post-refinement pipeline applies LoRA-based novel font adaptation (NFA) for few-shot style generalization, followed by GRPO-based structural enhancement (SE) that uses OCR confidence and style-discriminator rewards to sharpen glyph legibility.

## Results

On the Large (L) dataset, GAR-Font(I₈) with NFA-8+SE achieves RMSE 0.2503 / SSIM 0.6411 / FID 8.99 on UFSC and RMSE 0.2540 / SSIM 0.6356 / FID 8.66 on UFUC, substantially outperforming all seven baselines in reconstruction and perceptual quality. Style accuracy (Acc(S)) reaches 0.3518 (UFSC) and 0.3735 (UFUC), far exceeding the next-best CF-Font (0.1549 / 0.1570). In the multimodal setting, GAR-Font(M₄) with only 4 visual references plus one text description surpasses the 8-reference vision-only model, achieving FID 7.49 vs. 7.72 on UFSC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAR-Font(I₈)+NFA-8+SE dominates all seven baselines on reconstruction (RMSE, SSIM) and perceptual (LPIPS, FID) metrics across both UFSC and UFUC splits. Against the strongest diffusion baseline Font-Diffuser, GAR-Font reduces RMSE from 0.2645 to 0.2503 and FID from 21.42 to 8.99 on UFSC, while also achieving far higher style accuracy (0.3518 vs. 0.0527). The AR-based IF-Font achieves the highest content accuracy (0.9387) but suffers severe reconstruction degradation (RMSE 0.3969, SSIM 0.3374), indicating structural collapse. Among GAN/VAE methods, CF-Font is the closest competitor on style accuracy (0.1549) yet still trails GAR-Font by over 2×. Diff-Font reports a competitive FID (9.71) but has the lowest content accuracy (0.4029) and omits UFUC results entirely. In the multimodal setting (Table 2), GAR-Font(M₄) with 4 images + 1 text description outperforms the 8-image vision-only GAR-Font(I₈) on RMSE, SSIM, LPIPS, and FID, confirming that textual style cues complement and reduce reliance on visual references.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAR-Font extends the autoregressive image generation paradigm (VQ-VAE tokenizer + Transformer decoder) to few-shot font generation, departing from the dominant GAN and diffusion formulations in prior FFG work. It directly adopts the content-style aggregator architecture from LF-Font [46] and Tang et al. [54], and repurposes GRPO [50] (originally for mathematical reasoning) as a structural enhancement mechanism for glyph refinement. The key architectural departure is the hybrid CNN-ViT global-aware tokenizer (G-Tok), which replaces the patch-level tokenization used in VQ-Font and IF-Font, and the decoupled visual-pretraining-then-language-adapter paradigm that avoids costly joint multimodal pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 4 visual references plus one textual description can match the quantitative performance of 8 visual references offers a practical path toward more flexible, human-in-the-loop font design with fewer hand-drawn examples. The global-aware tokenizer addresses a genuine limitation of patch-based approaches in capturing font-level stylistic coherence, which is critical for logographic systems with complex stroke geometries. However, the work is constrained to 64×64 resolution and Chinese characters, and the text-image fusion remains a late-stage adapter rather than a deeply integrated mechanism, leaving open whether earlier multimodal fusion could further reduce reference requirements or enable finer-grained stylistic control (stroke thickness, slant, width).
