## Problem

Zero-shot image-to-image generation methods (e.g., IP-Adapter, InstantID) can replicate facial identities or artistic styles from a single reference image without modifying any model weights, creating urgent intellectual property risks. Existing defenses are either irreversible (IDProtector), limited to a single task (identity only, ignoring style plagiarism), or designed for fine-tuning scenarios rather than the more accessible zero-shot setting. No universal, authentication-integrated solution exists that simultaneously blocks unauthorized generation and permits authorized users to recover the original content.

## Input / Output

The method takes an original RGB image (normalized to 112×112 for faces, or painting resolution for artworks) and a password vector whose dimensionality matches the target image encoder's embedding space (e.g., 512-dim for ArcFace, 1024-dim for CLIP, or 257×1280 for CLIP hidden states). It produces a protected image (original plus imperceptible perturbations bounded by ε=11/255 or 21/255), encrypted embeddings that replace the original ones in the generation pipeline, and—upon providing the correct password—decrypted embeddings that faithfully recover the original for authorized generation. A separate encryptor/decryptor pair is trained per image encoder, so the same protected image carries defenses against multiple encoders simultaneously.

## Method

Adapter Shield operates in two stages. Stage 1 trains a pair of lightweight transformer modules (self-attention + cross-attention + FC) as an encryptor and decryptor, where the cross-attention layer makes the encrypted output explicitly dependent on the password; they are jointly optimized with five loss terms (encryption, decryption, wrong-password, cross-password diversity, and same-password diversity) to ensure encrypted embeddings are maximally dissimilar to originals while correct-password decryption recovers them faithfully. Stage 2 applies a robust multi-targeted FGSM-based adversarial attack that iteratively updates pixel-level perturbations δ to push the protected image's embeddings—across m different image encoders simultaneously—toward the corresponding encrypted targets, while injecting differentiable distortions (noise, blur, JPEG) during optimization for robustness against post-processing. The key insight is that since all zero-shot I2I methods funnel reference-image information through encoder embeddings into UNet cross-attention, disrupting these embeddings at the source universally blocks generation regardless of the specific downstream diffusion model, while the reversible encryption provides a clean authentication mechanism for authorized users.

## Results

On facial identity protection, Adapter Shield (general) reduces identity similarity (ISM) to 0.1422 (IP-Adapter FaceID) and 0.0685 (InstantID), compared to 0.87–0.96 for all four baselines, while maintaining PSNR of 32.10 dB. On artwork anti-plagiarism, the method drops embedding similarity (ESM) to 0.1175 (IP-Adapter) and 0.2713 (IP-Adapter Plus), versus 0.72–0.85 for baselines, with LPIPS of 0.0980 (best among all methods). The method also achieves the highest abnormal face rate (AFR) of 0.07–0.19, indicating that unauthorized generation produces visibly distorted faces rather than subtle degradations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Adapter Shield decisively outperforms all four baselines on every protection metric across both tasks. On facial identity protection, ISM drops from 0.87–0.96 (baselines) to 0.07–0.14, a reduction of over 85%, while AFR increases from 0.01–0.05 to 0.07–0.19. On artwork anti-plagiarism, ESM falls from 0.72–0.85 to 0.12–0.27, again a >60% improvement. The baselines (Pretender, Mist, ACE, CAAT) were designed for fine-tuning defense and show limited transferability to zero-shot generation, with their ISM/ESM values remaining close to 1.0 (i.e., near-identical embeddings). In terms of image quality, Adapter Shield matches or exceeds baselines on PSNR (32.10 dB for faces, 30.81 dB for artworks) and achieves the best LPIPS (0.0980), indicating the protective perturbations remain visually imperceptible. CAAT is the closest competitor on PSNR for artworks (32.55 dB) but offers negligible protection (ESM 0.73–0.85).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of image protection research that targets fine-tuning-based diffusion model customization (Mist, CAAT, Pretender, ACE, Glaze, AntiDreamBooth), which the authors show is largely ineffective against zero-shot image-to-image generation. The closest prior work, IDProtector, addresses zero-shot protection but is irreversible and limited to facial identity. Adapter Shield repurposes the reversible encryption/decryption paradigm from face de-identification (RIDDLE, password-conditioned anonymization) and combines it with a multi-target FGSM-based adversarial perturbation, creating a unified framework that simultaneously handles identity and style protection with password-based access control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The authentication-integrated design is the key practical contribution: image owners can grant selective access to authorized users while blocking unauthorized generation, a capability absent from all prior protection methods. However, the weak robustness to JPEG compression (embedding similarity rising to 0.37–0.69) is a significant practical limitation, since JPEG is ubiquitous in real-world image sharing. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to an ever-growing set of zero-shot methods, whether the adversarial perturbation budget (11–21/255) remains imperceptible at higher resolutions, and how the framework handles combined attacks (post-processing plus password guessing).
