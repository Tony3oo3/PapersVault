## Problem

Computing spectral decompositions on manifolds traditionally requires selecting a discrete operator (e.g., cotangent Laplacian), constructing mass and stiffness matrices from mesh connectivity, and running a numerical eigensolver. This pipeline is designed for intrinsically 2D surfaces with well-triangulated meshes and does not scale to unstructured point clouds or high-dimensional data manifolds, where reliable operator discretization is intractable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262789/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unstructured point cloud of n points in R^d (no mesh or connectivity required) and produces K orthonormal spectral basis vectors, their associated eigenvalues, and a diagonal mass matrix encoding the local sampling density. The network outputs an n×K matrix that is QR-decomposed to enforce orthonormality; the first eigenvector's squared entries directly yield the mass matrix diagonal, and eigenvalues are recovered a posteriori from worst-case reconstruction errors rather than learned as parameters. This works identically whether the input is a 3D surface point cloud or a 768-dimensional DINOv2 image embedding.

## Method

A neural network (MLP for 1D, Transformer for 3D/high-dim) predicts K per-point feature vectors, which are QR-decomposed to yield an orthonormal basis. Training is unsupervised: random probe functions are generated by smoothing Gaussian-kernel-filtered signals on the k-nearest-neighbor graph, and the loss is the average reconstruction error when projecting these probes onto progressively truncated bases (k=1..K), grounded in the PCA formulation of optimal-approximation theory (Theorem 3.2). The key insight is that the optimal basis for approximating a constrained signal class is the eigenvectors of the constraint operator, so minimizing reconstruction error over smooth probes implicitly recovers a Laplacian-like spectral decomposition without ever constructing the operator. The first eigenvector encodes the metric (mass matrix), and eigenvalues are estimated at inference via the min-max relation λ_{k+1} = 1/α_k, where α_k is the worst-case reconstruction error at resolution k.

## Results

In the overfitting setting on 3D surface point clouds, the learned spectral basis achieves cosine similarity with the oracle cotangent Laplacian eigenfunctions of 0.823–0.993 at k≤10, 0.696–0.988 at k≤20, and 0.544–0.981 at k≤50, with mean relative eigenvalue discrepancy of 0.066–0.200 across nine shapes. In the generalization setting, the model trained on diverse surface point clouds transfers to unseen shapes and even to 3D volumetric point clouds (a manifold dimensionality never seen in training). On high-dimensional image manifolds (STL10, Imagenette, CIFAR-100, Caltech-256 with DINOv2/CLIP embeddings), the proposed Optimal-Approximation Eigenmaps consistently provide competitive or superior clustering (NMI/ARI) and 2D visualizations compared to PCA, Isomap, Laplacian Eigenmaps, t-SNE, and UMAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the cotangent Laplacian oracle [87] on 3D surfaces, the method matches or slightly exceeds the reference in low-frequency components (cosine similarity >0.96 for most shapes at k≤10) and even captures additional geometric detail in top-k spectral reconstructions that the oracle misses (e.g., on Pegaso). The eigenvalue estimates from worst-case reconstruction errors approximate the oracle spectrum with relative discrepancies under 0.20. On high-dimensional image manifolds, the proposed Optimal-Approximation Eigenmaps outperform the most directly comparable method, Laplacian Eigenmaps [11], in clustering quality (NMI/ARI), suggesting the learned implicit operator is superior to the standard graph Laplacian used in that method. The method also matches or exceeds PCA [58], Isomap [126], t-SNE [83], and UMAP [85] in both quantitative clustering metrics and qualitative 2D visualizations across STL10, Imagenette, CIFAR-100, and Caltech-256. The key advantage over all baselines is that no explicit operator construction, meshing, or graph connectivity is required, enabling direct application to unstructured point clouds of arbitrary dimensionality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the optimal-approximation theory of Aflalo, Brezis, and Kimmel [3,4,24], which establishes that the eigenvectors of an SPD operator are the unique optimal basis for progressive k-term approximation of signals bounded in that operator's norm. It departs from the standard geometry processing pipeline (choose operator → discretize via mass/stiffness matrices → solve generalized eigenvalue problem) by eliminating all three steps: the network learns a spectral basis end-to-end from an approximation loss on probe functions, with the operator and its eigenvalues recovered a posteriori. Unlike prior neural approaches to eigenproblems that require explicit domain parameterization and assume flat Euclidean geometry [111,56], or learning-Laplacian methods that still assemble discrete operators and call eigensolvers [95,141], this framework operates purely on unstructured point clouds of arbitrary dimensionality with no mesh, no operator matrix, and no numerical solver.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By replacing the entire operator-selection-and-discretization pipeline with a single learned basis, this framework removes the primary bottleneck for spectral analysis on unstructured and high-dimensional data, where meshing is infeasible and graph Laplacians are unreliable. The elegant recovery of the Riemannian metric from the first eigenvector unifies spectral decomposition and metric estimation in one output, which could simplify downstream geometry processing pipelines. Key limitations include the computational cost of training, the need to manually tune probe distribution hyperparameters per shape (especially in the overfitting setting), and the fact that generalization across shapes yields noticeably lower precision than per-shape overfitting. Open questions include whether probe distributions can be learned jointly, whether the framework extends to non-Laplacian operators (Finsler, Hamiltonian) in a principled way, and whether a true foundation model for spectral analysis across arbitrary dimensionalities is achievable.
