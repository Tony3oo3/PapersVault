## Problem

Autoregressive 3D mesh generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: the token-by-token sequential process accumulates errors over long sequences and forces the model to over-smooth high-frequency geometric details in order to maintain global topological consistency. No existing framework can simultaneously produce artist-level global structure and rich local surface detail without this conflict.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263430/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud as input, which is semantically segmented (via PartField) and encoded by a pre-trained Michelangelo encoder into a hierarchical conditioning representation: one global shape vector plus N part-specific local geometry vectors. The output is a 3D triangle mesh serialized as discrete tokens using Blocked and Patchified Tokenization (BPT), organized into part-level blocks of up to 1024 tokens with a maximum total sequence length of 4096 tokens. The point cloud's segmentation must align with the mesh's semantic parts, and the conditioning is dynamically reconfigured per part during generation.

## Method

PartDiffuser is a semi-autoregressive discrete diffusion framework built on a DiT architecture that decouples global structure from local detail: autoregression operates at the part level (each part attends only to previously generated parts via a composite self-attention mask), while within each part all tokens are generated in parallel via masked discrete diffusion. A key architectural addition is a part-aware cross-attention layer that dynamically injects hierarchical geometric conditioning—for part i, the Key/Value is the concatenation of the global feature C_global and the part-specific feature C_part_i—ensuring precise local geometric guidance. During training the model operates in parallel mode with all blocks visible; during inference it reconstructs one semantic part at a time over N strides, applying a full denoising schedule (iterative predict-and-remask) per part before advancing to the next. The mesh is first segmented by PartField with a random cluster count, and parts are ordered via BFS on the adjacency graph to preserve spatial locality in the 1D token sequence.

## Results

On the complex Objaverse dataset, PartDiffuser achieves the best score across all four metrics: CD 17.813×10⁻³ (27% lower than MeshAnythingV2's 24.402), HD 0.238, EMD 0.115, and F1 0.343 (~20% higher than the second-best 0.285). On the more structured HSSD dataset, it secures the best EMD (0.059) and F1 (0.471), while on 3DFront it leads in CD (6.461), HD (0.147), and EMD (0.068), with F1 (0.453) narrowly behind TreeMeshGPT (0.462). The model uses 0.3B parameters with a 4096-token context window and 1024-token block size, trained on ~81K samples from Objaverse and 3DFront.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PartDiffuser is compared against three autoregressive mesh generation baselines: MeshAnythingV2, BPT, and TreeMeshGPT. On the diverse Objaverse dataset, PartDiffuser decisively outperforms all three on every metric, with a 27% CD improvement over MeshAnythingV2 and a 52% improvement over BPT. On HSSD, PartDiffuser wins EMD (0.059 vs. 0.061) and F1 (0.471 vs. 0.441) over TreeMeshGPT, though TreeMeshGPT retains a slight edge in CD (4.522 vs. 5.364) and HD (0.135 vs. 0.138). On 3DFront, PartDiffuser leads in CD, HD, and EMD but trails TreeMeshGPT by 0.009 on F1 (0.453 vs. 0.462). BPT consistently performs worst across all datasets, with CD values 2–5× higher than PartDiffuser, indicating it struggles with the complexity of these benchmarks. The paper attributes PartDiffuser's advantage on complex shapes to its part-wise decoupling of global structure (autoregression between parts) from local detail (parallel diffusion within parts), which avoids the error accumulation and over-smoothing inherent in purely token-level autoregressive generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PartDiffuser sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) autoregressive mesh generation (MeshGPT, MeshAnything, TreeMeshGPT, BPT), which treats meshes as 1D token sequences but suffers from error accumulation and a global-vs-local fidelity trade-off; (2) block-wise semi-autoregressive diffusion (BD-LM), which interpolates between AR and parallel diffusion at the block level; and (3) part-level 3D generation (PartGen, PartCrafter, PartPacker), which decomposes objects into semantic units. The paper's key departure is applying the BD-LM paradigm at the *semantic part* level rather than at an arbitrary token-block level, and coupling it with a part-aware cross-attention mechanism that injects hierarchical point-cloud features (global + per-part) from a pre-trained encoder (Michelangelo) to guide denoising. This makes the AR dependency structure semantically meaningful rather than purely positional.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The part-wise semi-autoregressive strategy offers a principled way to decouple global topological consistency from local geometric fidelity in mesh generation, a trade-off that has constrained purely autoregressive methods. The 27% CD improvement on the diverse Objaverse dataset and the ablation confirming that both global and part-specific conditioning are essential (with 'parts only' performing substantially worse) validate the design. However, the method's reliance on upstream PartField segmentation, its 0.3B parameter scale with a 4096-token context window, and the steep quality drop under accelerated sampling (k=4) all point to open questions around scalability, end-to-end joint training, and more efficient sampling schedules that the authors themselves flag as future work.
