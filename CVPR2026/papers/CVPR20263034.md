## Problem

Multi-Image Composition (MICo)—combining multiple reference images into a coherent target while preserving identity consistency—lacks both high-quality training data and a dedicated evaluation benchmark. Existing MICo datasets suffer from stylistic homogeneity (relying on a few fixed T2I models), person-centric bias, and semantically ambiguous samples from segmentation-based pipelines, creating a significant gap between open-source and proprietary model capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263034/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes 2–5+ source images (drawn from categories: objects, humans, clothing, scenes) along with a natural-language composition prompt, and produces a single composite RGB image in which all source entities appear with faithful identity preservation. The De&Re subset uniquely pairs each set of decomposed components with both a real-world original and a synthetically recomposed target, enabling controlled comparison. Source images are curated at varying resolutions (scenes filtered to ≥512px on the shorter side; input images resized to 512×512 for some models), and prompts include explicit token-to-source mappings for future latent-space alignment research.

## Method

MICo-150K is constructed via a multi-stage pipeline: (1) high-quality source images are collected from seven public datasets and filtered/annotated using Qwen2.5-VL-72B, with DINO-v3 + SigLIP2 feature clustering (DBSCAN) to remove visual redundancy; (2) a Compose-by-Retrieval strategy uses GPT-4o to select semantically compatible source combinations and generate diverse, natural composition prompts; (3) Nano-Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) synthesizes composite targets, which are then verified by Qwen2.5-VL-72B (object/scene presence) and ArcFace (face ID via Hungarian-algorithm matching) in a human-in-the-loop loop. The De&Re track decomposes 11K real portraits into components, has annotators verify and rewrite prompts, then recomposes them—yielding paired real/synthetic targets. For evaluation, the proposed Weighted-Ref-VIEScore replaces joint multi-image VLM scoring with per-source binary presence checks (yielding weights) plus a reference-image comparison via GPT-4o, avoiding cross-image attention overload. Five open-source models (BAGEL, BLIP3-o, Lumina-DiMOO, OmniGen2, Qwen-Image-Edit) are fine-tuned on the dataset and evaluated on the 1,000-case MICo-Bench.

## Results

On MICo-Bench (Weighted-Ref-VIEScore), fine-tuning on MICo-150K yields substantial gains across all five open-source models. The best open-source result is Qwen-MICo (fine-tuned Qwen-Image-Edit with real De&Re data), achieving 58.2 overall, up from 38.5 base — a 20-point improvement that approaches closed-source GPT-4o (59.6) and Nano-Banana (60.3). Models that originally lacked MICo capability (BLIP3-o: 2.2→43.2; DiMOO: 4.3→34.2) gain the most in absolute terms, while OmniGen2 (41.0→51.2) shows the smallest relative gain due to its strong pretraining. Synthetic De&Re targets perform nearly identically to real ones (e.g., Qwen-Image: 58.1 vs. 58.2 overall), confirming synthetic data as a viable substitute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against two closed-source proprietary models: GPT-4o (Overall 59.6) and Nano-Banana (Overall 60.3). The best open-source model, Qwen-MICo (58.2 overall), trails Nano-Banana by 2.1 points and GPT-4o by 1.4 points on the overall score, but narrows the gap substantially on De&Re (60.7 vs. 60.4 and 61.3 respectively). On the Person sub-task, Qwen-MICo (56.6) lags both closed models (53.8 for GPT-4o, 59.4 for Nano-Banana), while on HOI it is closer (56.9 vs. 60.1 and 62.1). The paper highlights that Qwen-MICo matches Qwen-Image-2509 on 3-image composition despite being trained on a dataset hundreds of times smaller, and uniquely supports arbitrary multi-image inputs beyond the latter's 3-image limit. Nano-Banana scores slightly higher overall but is noted to occasionally produce incomplete bodies or copy-paste artifacts, whereas GPT-4o shows superior robustness across input configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant segmentation-based paradigm for MICo data construction (Subject Diffusion, MS-Diffusion, UNO, DreamO), which relies on GroundingDINO + SAM to extract sources from full images and suffers from incomplete masks and limited diversity. Instead, it curates high-quality source images independently and uses a proprietary model (Nano-Banana) with a Compose-by-Retrieval strategy to synthesize targets, addressing the stylistic homogeneity of prior synthetic pipelines. For evaluation, it extends VIEScore by introducing source-specific weighting and a reference-image comparison mechanism to overcome the cross-image attention bottleneck of VLMs when scoring multi-source compositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MICo-150K is the first large-scale, taxonomy-structured dataset purpose-built for multi-image composition with identity consistency, and the accompanying MICo-Bench and Weighted-Ref-VIEScore fill a clear evaluation gap that previously forced researchers to rely on ad-hoc or single-reference metrics. The finding that carefully curated synthetic De&Re targets yield nearly identical performance to real photographs suggests a scalable path to generating unlimited training pairs without manual curation. Open questions remain around ultra-complex scenes (5+ individuals, rare objects, highly dynamic actions), and whether the emergent MICo capability observed in untrained models reflects a general property of large MMDiT architectures or is specific to the scale and diversity of the pretraining corpus.
