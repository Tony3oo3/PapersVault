## Problem

Existing HOI video generation methods are fragmented across three disjoint tracks: pose-only synthesis that produces no pixels, single-image appearance generation that lacks temporal dynamics, and video generation that requires both a complete pose sequence and a ground-truth first frame. This makes true sim-to-real deployment impossible, since simulators like GraspXL provide pose trajectories and object geometry but no photorealistic first frame. The paper asks whether a unified engine can generate high-fidelity HOI videos from only sparse pose keyframes and object geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260669/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial MANO hand pose (51×3), a target MANO hand pose (51×3), an untextured object mesh, and an initial 6-DoF object pose as the sole user-provided inputs—no ground-truth first frame or full pose sequence is required. It outputs a photo-realistic HOI RGB video at 480×720 resolution depicting a temporally coherent grasp-to-place motion. Intermediate conditions (depth maps, instance segmentation masks, 2D hand keypoint images) are rendered from the generated pose trajectory rather than supplied externally, and depth for real-data training is estimated via DepthCrafter.

## Method

PAM decomposes generation into three decoupled stages: (1) GraspXL synthesizes the full intermediate HOI pose trajectory from the initial/target poses and object mesh; (2) a Flux image diffusion model fine-tuned with a ControlNet fork generates the first frame, conditioned on channel-concatenated VAE latents of depth, segmentation, and hand keypoints injected into two DiT layers via zero-convolution; (3) a CogVideoX video diffusion model animates the first frame into a full clip, with the same three conditions rendered per-frame and injected through 12 duplicate DiT blocks, using random per-cue masking (p=0.2) to prevent over-reliance on any single modality. Only ControlNet parameters are trained while the backbone remains frozen. The key architectural insight is that decoupling appearance from motion eliminates the first-frame bottleneck, and combining geometric (depth), semantic (segmentation), and structural (keypoints) conditions is necessary to preserve fine hand details that no single modality captures alone.

## Results

On DexYCB, PAM achieves an FVD of 29.13 (vs. 38.83 for InterDyn and 58.51 for CosHand), MPJPE of 19.37 mm (vs. 30.05 mm for CosHand and 57.30 mm for ManiVideo), and LPIPS of 0.069 (vs. 0.079 for ManiVideo), while generating 480×720 videos compared to 256×256 or 256×384 for baselines. On OAKINK2, the full multi-condition model reduces FVD from 68.76 (CosHand) to 46.31 and MPJPE from 14.49 mm to 7.01 mm. The method also achieves the best Motion Fidelity (0.712) and SSIM (0.914) on DexYCB across all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAM outperforms all three baselines on every metric where they are directly comparable. Against InterDyn (the strongest FVD baseline at 38.83), PAM improves FVD by 25% to 29.13 and Motion Fidelity from 0.680 to 0.712, while also producing videos at nearly 3× the resolution (480×720 vs. 256×384). Against CosHand, PAM reduces MPJPE from 30.05 mm to 19.37 mm (a 36% improvement) and FVD from 58.51 to 29.13, with substantially better SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.767) and PSNR (30.17 vs. 23.20). Against ManiVideo, PAM achieves lower LPIPS (0.069 vs. 0.079), marginally higher SSIM (0.914 vs. 0.913) and PSNR (30.17 vs. 30.10), and dramatically lower MPJPE (19.37 vs. 57.30 mm), indicating far more accurate hand pose reconstruction. On OAKINK2, PAM with all conditions improves FVD by 33% (68.76→46.31) and MPJPE by 52% (14.49→7.01 mm) over the fine-tuned CosHand baseline, confirming consistent gains across datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously disjoint tracks in HOI generation—pose-only synthesis (GraspXL), single-image appearance generation (HOIDiffusion-style methods), and video motion generation (InterDyn, ManiVideo, CosHand)—into a single decoupled three-stage pipeline. The key architectural departure from prior video generation methods is the elimination of the ground-truth first-frame requirement: instead of conditioning on a real initial frame, PAM synthesizes it via a Flux+ControlNet image model conditioned on rendered depth, segmentation, and hand keypoint maps, then animates it with a CogVideoX+ControlNet video model. The ControlNet zero-convolution injection mechanism is reused across both stages, and the multi-condition design (depth + segmentation + keypoints) is motivated by the observation that geometric and semantic cues alone cannot capture the high degrees of freedom in hand articulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling true sim-to-real HOI video generation from only sparse pose keyframes and object geometry, removing the first-frame bottleneck that has limited prior methods to image-to-video settings. The downstream validation—showing that 3,400 synthetic videos (207k frames) can substitute for 50% of real training data in hand pose estimation—demonstrates concrete utility as a data augmentation tool for embodied AI. Limitations include the reliance on a separate pretrained pose generator (GraspXL) that is not jointly optimized, the single-hand focus of the main experiments, and the qualitative-only zero-shot evaluation on bimanual OAKINK2. Future work could unify the three stages end-to-end and extend to more complex multi-object interactions.
