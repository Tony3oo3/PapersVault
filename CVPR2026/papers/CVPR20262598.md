## Problem

Existing 3D semantic instance segmentation methods process each indoor scan independently, ignoring temporal identity continuity, while 4D LiDAR panoptic segmentation methods assume dense, high-frequency observations with minimal per-frame change. No unified formulation exists for jointly segmenting, identifying, and temporally associating object instances across sparsely captured 3D scans of evolving indoor scenes observed over days, months, or years, where objects move, appear, or disappear between captures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262598/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T registered 3D point cloud scans of the same indoor scene and represents them as a unified 4D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×4}, where the fourth coordinate encodes temporal stage. Critically, points from different temporal observations are kept separate during voxelization (no temporal pooling or superposition), and the encoder processes each stage independently within a batch. The output is a set of K instance masks, each spanning the entire sequence (m^k ∈ {0,1}^N over all N points across all stages), with a single semantic class label per instance, maintaining consistent identity across all temporal observations.

## Method

ReScene4D adapts the Mask3D mask-transformer architecture to 4DSIS by replacing per-stage queries with shared spatio-temporal queries that jointly refine masks across all temporal stages via masked cross-attention, eliminating post-hoc matching steps. Queries use 4D Fourier positional encodings over (x,y,z,t) coordinates, and the number of queries remains fixed regardless of sequence length to keep memory bounded. Three temporal information-sharing mechanisms are introduced: (1) a supervised InfoNCE contrastive loss on pooled superpoint features that enforces instance-level semantic consistency and propagates temporal cues via positive/negative pairs sampled across all frames; (2) spatio-temporal masking that logically ORs predicted masks across stages at each hierarchical resolution to guide cross-attention toward shared spatio-temporal locations; and (3) spatio-temporal decoder serialization that randomly shuffles 3D and 4D space-filling curve patterns in the PTv3 decoder to attend to both spatial and temporal neighbors. The method is evaluated with three frozen backbones (Minkowski sparse conv U-Net, Sonata, and Concerto) and trained on a mixed set of 3RScan two-stage sequences and ScanNet single scans.

## Results

On the 3RScan dataset, ReScene4D (Concerto backbone) achieves a t-mAP of 34.8 and standard mAP of 43.3, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best baseline, Mask3D with geometric matching, reaches only t-mAP 20.7 and mAP 29.7, while the 4D LiDAR baselines Mask4Former (t-mAP 17.0) and Mask4D (t-mAP 1.3) perform considerably worse due to their reliance on dense temporal sampling and smooth motion assumptions. Notably, ReScene4D (C) also improves per-stage 3DSIS mAP to 47.8/48.3 across two temporal stages, surpassing Mask3D's 46.4 mAP when trained specifically for single-scan segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReScene4D (Concerto) outperforms all three baselines by a wide margin on the primary t-mAP metric: 34.8 vs. 20.7 (Mask3D+geo), 20.1 (Mask3D+sem), 17.0 (Mask4Former), and 1.3 (Mask4D). The 4D LiDAR baselines Mask4D and Mask4Former underperform even the simpler Mask3D+matching approach because they assume dense frame-to-frame observations and smooth motion, which do not hold in sparsely captured indoor scenes; Mask4D is further hampered by its LiDAR-specific backbone trained from scratch on limited 3RScan data. Among the three ReScene4D backbone variants, the Concerto (2D-3D self-supervised) encoder yields the best results, followed by Sonata (3D-only self-supervised) and Minkowski (trained from scratch), confirming the value of strong pretrained semantic features. On per-stage 3DSIS mAP (Table 2), ReScene4D (C) achieves 47.8 and 48.3 on stages 1 and 2 respectively, exceeding Mask3D's 46.4, demonstrating that temporal information sharing also improves single-scan segmentation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Mask3D mask-transformer architecture (sparse-UNet backbone + masked-attention query decoder) to a 4D spatio-temporal setting, while drawing on Mask4Former's joint-query formulation to eliminate post-hoc matching steps. It deliberately departs from dense 4D LiDAR panoptic segmentation methods (Mask4D, Mask4Former, SP2Mask4D), which assume superimposed scans, smooth per-frame motion, and high-frequency temporal sampling—assumptions that break down for sparsely captured indoor scenes. The key architectural departure is preserving temporal distinction in the voxel grid (4D rather than 3D pooling) and introducing three temporal information-sharing mechanisms (cross-time contrastive loss, spatio-temporal mask pooling, and spatio-temporal serialization) that allow the model to leverage geometric and semantic priors across observations without enforcing point-level correspondence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes a practically important but previously undefined task—jointly segmenting, identifying, and temporally associating instances across intermittently observed indoor scans—and provides the first dedicated metric (t-mAP) that handles ambiguous instance groups, a common indoor scenario. The finding that temporal information sharing improves even per-stage 3DSIS (ReScene4D(C) mAP 47.8–48.3 vs. Mask3D's 46.4) suggests multi-observation fusion acts as a form of data augmentation, a useful insight for practitioners. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (3RScan, 478 scenes) with only T=2 sequences and 18 classes, and the authors acknowledge that inconsistent temporal annotations and limited instance-change diversity (17% in validation) constrain the conclusions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences (T>2), more diverse indoor environments, and whether the t-mAP metric generalizes to other sparse-observation settings.
