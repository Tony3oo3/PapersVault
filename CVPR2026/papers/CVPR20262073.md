## Problem

Iterative adversarial optimization tends to over-specialize perturbations to the local gradient characteristics of the surrogate model, causing the perturbation to fall into sharp, high-curvature regions of the surrogate's loss landscape and degrading transferability to other models. Existing mitigation strategies (neighborhood sampling, input transformations, ensembling) still operate in the global perturbation space and remain constrained by the surrogate model's gradients, making it difficult to escape local optima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262073/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (C×H×W) with its ground-truth label and a surrogate model (CNN or ViT) as input, and produces an adversarial example x_adv = x + δ satisfying the L∞ constraint ||δ||∞ ≤ ε (ε = 16/255). The attack runs for T = 10 iterations with step size α = 1.6/255, and the generated adversarial examples are evaluated for transferability across 11 target models (5 CNNs, 6 ViTs) plus 3 adversarially trained ensembles and 3 defense methods on 1,000 ILSVRC 2012 validation images.

## Method

LPAA constructs multiple augmented local subspaces at each iteration via stochastic binary masking (masking ratio p = 0.95), constraining each gradient evaluation to a randomly sampled subspace of the perturbation rather than the full vector. Gradients from N = 20 independently sampled subspaces are averaged to form a more comprehensive and stable update direction, while an augmentation coefficient β = 35 scales the perturbation to enlarge the effective search range and help the optimization approach the decision boundary more effectively. A dedicated perturbation initialization strategy (PI) combines GMI_PI—converting the Global Momentum Initialization direction into a projected initial perturbation—with LPA_PI, which refines this initialization by applying one round of the LPA operation, providing a highly transferable starting direction that mitigates sensitivity to the initial perturbation. The full framework incorporates a momentum mechanism (decay factor μ) for stable iterative updates, and the approach is compatible with input transformation-based methods (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix) and can replace the neighborhood sampling component in existing gradient-based attacks (GRA, PGN, GAA, VMI-FGSM) to further boost their transferability.

## Results

Using ResNeXt-50 as the surrogate model, LPAA achieves an average transfer attack success rate of 86.5% across 10 target models (5 CNNs + 5 ViTs), outperforming the second-best method PGN (82.2%) by 4.3%. With ViT-B as surrogate, LPAA reaches 91.5% average, surpassing SIA (85.8%) by 5.7%. Against adversarially trained models and defenses (NRP, HGD, Bit-Red), LPAA with RN-50 surrogate achieves 76.6% average vs. PGN's 70.9%, and with ViT-B surrogate achieves 73.1% vs. PGN's 66.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPAA consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across all three surrogate model settings. Against the strongest gradient-based competitor PGN, LPAA gains 3.1% (RN-50 surrogate), 4.3% (RNX-50), and 7.9% (ViT-B) in average transfer attack success rate. Against input-transformation-based SIA, the margins are 7.9%, 9.9%, and 5.7% respectively. The gap is largest against ANDA (15–22% improvement) and GRA (9.5–10.7%), indicating that methods relying on single-gradient or neighborhood-sampling strategies are most vulnerable to LPAA's multi-subspace exploration. When combined with input transformations (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix), LPAA boosts their average transferability by nearly 50% (e.g., Admix goes from 41.8% to 90.0%), demonstrating strong compatibility. Against adversarially trained ensembles (Inc-v3_ens4, IncRes-v2_ens) and defenses (NRP, HGD, Bit-Red), LPAA maintains a 5–6% lead over PGN, confirming robustness against model-level defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPAA extends the iterative gradient-based adversarial attack lineage (I-FGSM → MI-FGSM → GRA/PGN) by reframing the neighborhood sampling step: rather than adding random noise τ around the current point (as in GRA, PGN, VMI-FGSM, GAA), it applies stochastic binary masks to the perturbation vector itself, constructing multiple augmented subspaces whose gradients are averaged. The perturbation initialization strategy (PI) repurposes the momentum direction from GMI [38] as a transferable starting perturbation, and the augmentation coefficient β expands the effective search range beyond what random neighborhood sampling covers. Conceptually, LPAA positions itself as a 'perturbation-based neighborhood sampling strategy' that exploits both the direction and magnitude of accumulated perturbations, in contrast to the isotropic random sampling used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in LPAA's modularity: it can be dropped into existing neighborhood-sampling methods (GRA, PGN, GAA) for consistent ~3–8% gains, and when combined with input-transformation attacks (DIM, TIM, SIM, Admix) it yields roughly 50% average improvements, making it a broadly applicable plug-in component. The finding that random-noise initialization actively degrades performance (Table 5) underscores that initialization direction matters more than magnitude for transfer-based attacks, a point not previously isolated. Open questions include whether the masking ratio p and augmentation coefficient β generalize to other modalities (video, audio) or to stronger adaptive defenses, and whether the subspace-exploration idea can be formalized with theoretical guarantees on loss-landscape flatness.
