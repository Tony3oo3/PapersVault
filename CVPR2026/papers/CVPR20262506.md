## Problem

Existing Chinese historical document benchmarks are restricted to a single carrier type and small document counts, and they evaluate only recognition while ignoring the critical post-correction step that experts perform using domain knowledge and external references. No prior benchmark systematically probes whether large multimodal or language models can generalize across materially and temporally heterogeneous carriers spanning over 3,000 years of Chinese history.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262506/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes high-resolution RGB images whose dimensions range from 77×135 px (Oracle Bone fragments) to 2,327×2,039 px (AncientBook pages), with character counts per image varying from ~14 (JianDu, Inscription) to ~577 (AncientBook). For the post-correction tasks, the input is a recognized text string optionally augmented with retrieved passages from the Daizhige knowledge base (1.7B+ characters, hierarchically chunked), and the output is a corrected text string. Character-level evaluation uses individually cropped glyphs sampled from pages whose page-level 1-NED falls below 15%, isolating visual decoding from long-range language modeling.

## Method

MCHDoc assembles 15,724 annotated documents across six carriers (AncientBook, JianDu, Calligraphy, Inscription, Silk, Oracle Bone), with the Inscription subset manually transcribed by epigraphy-trained annotators and verified through peer review and third-party adjudication. The evaluation protocol defines four tasks that mirror the expert reading workflow: page-level OCR, character-level OCR, LLM post-correction using only internal parametric knowledge, and RAG-based post-correction that retrieves relevant passages from the Daizhige corpus via an adaptive hierarchical chunking strategy. Over 20 open-source and closed-source MLLMs and LLMs are evaluated under a unified protocol using AR, CR, and 1-NED metrics, with missing API predictions counted as failures. The benchmark further includes fine-tuned variants (Qwen2.5-VL-3B with full-parameter SFT, InternVL3.5-1B with LoRA) to isolate the contribution of domain adaptation from raw model scale.

## Results

On the page-level recognition task (Table 3), Doubao-Seed-1.6-VL achieves the best overall 1-NED scores across four carriers: 56.52% (AncientBook), 18.82% (JianDu), 49.81% (Calligraphy), and 65.90% (Inscription). The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B (SFT) closely follows with 52.53%, 17.09%, 54.26%, and 48.44%, while GLM-4.5V-106B reaches 47.59%, 17.68%, 45.55%, and 53.71%. Notably, GPT-4o and GPT-5 score only 3.92% and 6.87% 1-NED on AncientBook, and all models remain below 20% on JianDu, indicating severe cross-carrier generalization challenges. For knowledge-based post-correction (Table 6), Gemini-2.5-Flash-Think achieves the highest 1-NED on AncientBook (65.64%) and Calligraphy (62.16%), surpassing the raw recognition baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Doubao-Seed-1.6-VL leads on AncientBook (56.52% 1-NED) and Inscription (65.90%), outperforming the next-best open-source model InternVL3.5-38B (54.03% and 53.79%) by 2.5 and 12.1 points respectively. GPT-4o and GPT-5 are dramatically weaker on AncientBook (3.92% and 6.87% 1-NED) compared to all other models, though GPT-5 recovers to 50.86% on Inscription. The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B (SFT) surpasses all open-source models on Calligraphy (54.26%) and nearly matches Doubao on AncientBook (52.53% vs. 56.52%), demonstrating that domain adaptation compensates for parameter scale. GLM-4.5V-106B is the strongest open-source model on Inscription (53.71%) but trails Doubao by 12.2 points. On JianDu, no model exceeds 19% 1-NED, with Doubao (18.82%) and GLM-4.5V-106B (17.68%) marginally ahead; InternVL3.5 variants (1B–8B) collapse to 3.7–4.1%, far below their performance on other carriers. For LLM post-correction with internal knowledge (Table 5), all LLMs degrade the original recognition output, with GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro causing the largest drops on Inscription (50.86%→50.86% and 45.78%→45.13%), while Deepseek-V3.1-671B preserves the most on Inscription (63.80% 1-NED). With external knowledge (Table 6), Gemini-2.5-Flash-Think and Deepseek-V3.1-Think-671B achieve the largest gains on Calligraphy (62.16% and 72.92% 1-NED), surpassing the raw recognition baseline by 12–18 points, whereas JianDu remains nearly uncorrectable for all models due to extremely low input quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends several existing single-carrier datasets (M5HisDoc for ancient books, DeepJianDu for bamboo slips, CalliBench for calligraphy, OBI-Bench for oracle bone, and Wa-net for silk) into a single multi-carrier benchmark, while adding a manually annotated inscription corpus that no prior benchmark covers. It departs from prior Chinese historical document benchmarks, which are restricted to one or two carriers and focus solely on recognition, by introducing post-correction tasks (both internal-knowledge and RAG-based) that mimic the full expert workflow. The RAG-based correction pipeline draws on the Daizhige knowledge base (1.7B characters) and adapts retrieval-augmented generation to the epigraphic domain, a setting not previously explored in the document-reading literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MCHDoc provides the first standardized, large-scale (15,724 documents, six carriers, 3,000+ years of history) evaluation framework for multi-carrier Chinese historical document reading, filling a critical gap for digital humanities and document analysis research. The finding that even top-tier MLLMs achieve only moderate accuracy on the easiest carrier and near-failure on degraded ones (JianDu, Oracle Bone) underscores that cross-carrier generalization remains an open problem. The demonstration that naive LLM post-correction with internal knowledge consistently degrades outputs, while RAG-based correction with an external knowledge base yields substantial gains, provides actionable guidance for building reliable historical text digitization pipelines. Open questions include how to improve retrieval for fragmented or severely degraded inputs (JianDu, Inscription) and whether domain-adapted small models can close the remaining gap to closed-source systems.
