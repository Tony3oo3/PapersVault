## Problem

Despite strong general-purpose segmentation models like SAM 2, robot segmentation remains challenging due to embodiment diversity, appearance ambiguity with cluttered backgrounds, complex articulated structures, and rapid shape changes during manipulation. Existing methods also suffer from three method-level deficiencies: no mechanism for modeling articulated robot structures, reliance on manual prompts that undermine autonomy, and costly per-frame mask annotation requirements for video training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262059/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RobotSeg accepts a video clip or single RGB image and optionally user-provided prompts (single click, three clicks, or bounding box on the first frame) for refinement. It outputs pixel-level segmentation masks at three granularity levels—robot arm, robot gripper, and whole robot—enabling part-aware downstream tasks. During training, only the first-frame ground-truth mask per video is required, making the annotation cost scale linearly with the number of videos rather than frames.

## Method

Built on SAM 2, RobotSeg introduces three components: (1) a Structure-Enhanced Memory Associator (SEMA) that encodes previous-frame features and masks into memory, extracts Canny edge maps to modulate current features, and uses a multi-scale structure perceiver with cross-attention to produce a structure map that enhances temporally propagated features; (2) a Robot Prompt Generator (RPG) that produces class tokens from a learnable token bank (selected by target category) and object tokens via hierarchical KMeans clustering of historical features within previously segmented regions, initialized by Farthest Point Sampling; and (3) a label-efficient training strategy that supervises with only the first-frame mask using three hierarchical losses—video-level cycle consistency (forward-backward propagation), object-level semantic consistency (cosine alignment of spatially-averaged feature embeddings), and patch-level consistency (IoU loss against DINOv3 patch-similarity pseudo-labels).

## Results

On the VRS video benchmark, RobotSeg (41.3M params) achieves 85.1 J&F in automatic mode for whole-robot segmentation, outperforming the finetuned SAM 2.1 baseline (73.6 J&F) by 11.5 points and RoboEngine (80.2 J&F) by 4.9 points. It also delivers fine-grained arm (75.6 J&F) and gripper (76.0 J&F) masks in automatic mode—capabilities no prior method supports. On the RoboEngine image benchmark, RobotSeg reaches 87.9 J&F (AU) and 93.5 J&F (3-click), surpassing finetuned SAM 2.1 (90.2 J&F at 3-click) and RoboEngine (86.6 J&F AU). The model is 15–340× smaller than competing methods (41.3M vs. 638.5M–13,992.9M parameters).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobotSeg outperforms all six baselines across every setting it supports. Against the strongest promptable baseline, finetuned SAM 2.1, RobotSeg gains 11.5 J&F in automatic whole-robot mode (85.1 vs. 73.6) and 1.6 J&F in online-interactive mode (86.7 vs. 85.1), while also being the only method that segments arm and gripper simultaneously. Against the dedicated robot-segmentation baselines, RobotSeg beats finetuned RoboEngine by 4.9 J&F (85.1 vs. 80.2) and RoVi-Aug by 46.2 J&F (85.1 vs. 38.9) in automatic mode. Language-conditioned methods (CLIPSeg, LISA, EVF-SAM) trail by 21–59 J&F in automatic mode and do not support click/box prompting or video propagation. Notably, RobotSeg achieves these gains with only 41.3M parameters—15× smaller than RoboEngine/EVF-SAM (898.4M) and 340× smaller than LISA (13,992.9M)—making it the most efficient method in the comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

RobotSeg builds directly on SAM 2, the state-of-the-art promptable video segmentation foundation model, but departs from it in three robot-specific ways: adding a structure-enhanced memory associator (SEMA) that injects edge-based structural priors into temporal memory, a robot prompt generator (RPG) that replaces manual clicks/boxes with learned class and object tokens, and a label-efficient training strategy that requires only first-frame masks. It extends the image-level robot segmentation line (RoboEngine, RoVi-Aug) into the video domain, addressing temporal coherence and articulated-structure challenges that static-image methods ignore. Compared to language-conditioned segmentation (CLIPSeg, LISA, EVF-SAM), RobotSeg avoids the computational overhead of large LLMs while achieving stronger robot-specific accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VRS dataset (2,812 videos, 138k frames) is the first video-level robot segmentation benchmark, filling a critical gap for evaluating temporal consistency in robot perception. The label-efficient training strategy (first-frame-only supervision) makes large-scale robot video annotation practical, which is essential for autonomous deployment where per-frame labeling is infeasible. Open questions include generalization to entirely unseen robot embodiments, real-time inference on embedded hardware, and whether the structural priors (Canny edges) transfer to non-rigid or deformable robot parts. The compact 41.3M parameter model also raises the question of whether robot-specific inductive biases can consistently outperform much larger general-purpose models across diverse manipulation tasks.
