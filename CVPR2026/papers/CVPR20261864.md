## Problem

Existing Text2Pose (T2P) methods for sign language production struggle to simultaneously achieve semantic consistency, fine-grained motion accuracy, and temporal coherence. Frame-level VQ-VAE models compress whole-body poses into single tokens, limiting motion detail capacity, while region-level models preserve local fidelity but lose global semantic alignment. Additionally, both autoregressive decoding (exposure bias) and fully parallel discrete diffusion (no causal structure, inter-frame jitter) fail to produce temporally smooth pose sequences.

## Input / Output

The method takes spoken-language text (without gloss annotations) as input and produces a sequence of 2D sign poses, where each frame contains 121 keypoints (42 hand, 68 facial, 11 upper-body) extracted via HRNet. A lightweight two-layer Transformer first predicts the target sequence length S, and generation operates entirely in a discrete latent space: one semantic codebook plus four region-specific codebooks (body, right hand, left hand, head), each with 1024 entries of dimension 768. The final continuous pose sequence is reconstructed by the S-VQVAE decoder from the concatenated semantic and regional latent embeddings.

## Method

SignPR is a two-stage progressive framework. Stage 1 (S-VQVAE) uses a GCN + Transformer encoder to quantize each frame into one semantic token (whole-body structure) and four regional tokens (per body part), with a structural consistency loss that trains the semantic latent to predict the regional IDs of the same frame, enforcing cross-level alignment. Stage 2 (S-Diffusion) performs two sequential discrete diffusion processes: a semantic U-Net (temporal self-attention + text cross-attention) first denoises the semantic ID sequence, then a regional U-Net (adding region attention and cross-attention to the generated semantic IDs) refines the four regional ID sequences conditioned on both text and semantic poses. At inference, a block-wise causal refinement strategy (InferRef) partitions the token sequence into blocks of K frames; each block attends causally to all prior blocks while simultaneously refining them, enforcing temporal order without any retraining and reducing inter-frame jitter.

## Results

On Phoenix-14T (test), SignPR achieves ROUGE 32.86, BLEU-1 31.91, BLEU-4 9.41, FID 2.15, MPJPE 23.04, and DTW-MJE 0.111, outperforming the strongest T2G2P baseline Sign-IDD (ROUGE 27.11, BLEU-1 26.45, MPJPE 47.19) and the best T2P baseline MoMP (ROUGE 17.02, BLEU-1 16.87, MPJPE 45.71) across all metrics. On CSL-Daily, SignPR improves BLEU-4 from 2.14 (MoMP) to 3.01 and reduces MPJPE from 48.24 to 44.22. On USTC-CSL Split-I, SignPR achieves ROUGE 95.48 and MPJPE 51.40 versus PT-GN's 25.09 and 183.14, demonstrating consistent gains across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignPR, a T2P method (no gloss supervision), outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three datasets. Against the SOTA T2G2P model Sign-IDD [37], SignPR improves BLEU-1 from 26.45 to 31.91 (+5.46) and nearly halves MPJPE from 47.19 to 23.04, while also achieving a lower FID (2.15 vs. 2.46) and DTW-MJE (0.111 vs. 0.118). Compared to G2P-DDM [42], SignPR gains +12.49 ROUGE and +12.51 BLEU-1, with MPJPE reduced from 40.02 to 23.04. Against the T2P baselines, SignPR surpasses MoMP [32] by +15.84 ROUGE and +15.04 BLEU-1 on Phoenix-14T, and by +2.76 ROUGE on CSL-Daily. The earliest T2P baselines PT and PT-GN [30] are far behind, with SignPR achieving roughly 4× their ROUGE scores and ~2× lower MPJPE, highlighting the benefit of the progressive VQ-diffusion architecture over simple progressive transformer decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

SignPR builds directly on the VQ-Diffusion paradigm (Gu et al., 2022) and the discrete diffusion formulation for sign pose generation introduced by G2P-DDM (Xie et al., 2024), extending both from a single-level latent space to a two-level structural representation (semantic whole-body tokens plus region-specific tokens). It departs from prior T2P methods like T2S-GPT, MS2SL, and SOKE, which either compress each frame into a single token (sacrificing motion detail) or model regions independently (sacrificing global coherence), by introducing a structural consistency constraint that couples the two levels. The block-wise causal inference strategy is a novel inference-time mechanism that borrows the causal ordering idea from autoregressive decoding but applies it to discrete diffusion without any retraining, distinguishing it from prior causal diffusion approaches like SS-DLM that modify the training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core tension in sign language production—balancing semantic fidelity with fine-grained motion accuracy—through a clean architectural decomposition, and the training-free block-wise causal inference offers a practical, drop-in improvement for any discrete diffusion model that suffers from temporal jitter. The structural consistency constraint (semantic latent predicting regional IDs) is a simple but effective inductive bias that could transfer to other multi-scale generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D pose or full video generation, whether the block size K=8 generalizes across sequence lengths and datasets, and whether the very large USTC-CSL gains (ROUGE 95.48 vs. 25.09) reflect genuine method superiority or dataset-specific factors. The reliance on HRNet for 2D keypoint extraction and the absence of 3D evaluation limit the practical impact for downstream video synthesis.
