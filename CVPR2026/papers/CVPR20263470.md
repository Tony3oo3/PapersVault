## Problem

Existing intention-driven 3D affordance grounding methods either generate explicit text descriptions via MLLMs (requiring manual templates and two-stage training) or rely on off-the-shelf 2D segmenters with back-projection (suffering from detail loss and geometric inconsistency). Neither approach fully exploits the rich multimodal understanding of MLLMs to directly extract interaction intentions and align them with 3D geometry for accurate affordance localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263470/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and a paired RGB interaction image I ∈ R^{H×W×3} showing a human-object interaction. The image is consumed by a pre-trained MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with an object-centric text prompt containing the object category, while the point cloud is encoded by PointNet++. The output is a per-point affordance probability map p ∈ [0,1]^N, assigning each point a continuous score indicating its likelihood of being an actionable region.

## Method

HAMMER introduces three key mechanisms: (1) an affordance-guided intention embedding that inserts a special [CONT] token into the MLLM to aggregate interaction cues into a single vector, with auxiliary supervision for textual affordance label prediction to sharpen semantic focus; (2) a hierarchical cross-modal integration that fuses MLLM hidden states into point features at two levels—cross-attention at the PointNet++ bottleneck (points as queries, MLLM tokens as keys/values) and a gated global descriptor concatenated at full resolution; (3) a multi-granular geometry lifting module that progressively attends from the intention embedding to multi-scale decoder features (coarse-to-fine) with residual connections, injecting spatial awareness without requiring camera parameters or intermediate 2D masks. Training is end-to-end with LoRA (rank 16) on the MLLM language component, using a combined focal+dice affordance loss and a language modeling loss for the auxiliary text task.

## Results

On PIADv2, HAMMER achieves 40.06% aIOU and 94.19% AUC on the Seen split, 24.28% aIOU and 84.78% AUC on Unseen Object, and 13.28% aIOU and 72.21% AUC on Unseen Affordance, outperforming the strongest baseline GREAT by 2.45%, 5.12%, and 0.50% in aIOU respectively. On the smaller PIAD benchmark, HAMMER reaches 22.20% aIOU (Seen) and 13.71% aIOU (Unseen), surpassing GREAT by 2.59% and 5.39% in aIOU. On the corrupted benchmark, HAMMER maintains a 5.69–9.31% aIOU advantage over GREAT under jitter, drop-out, and add-local noise corruptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAMMER outperforms all ten baselines across every split of both PIAD and PIADv2. Against the closest intention-driven competitor GREAT [32], HAMMER gains +2.45% aIOU (Seen), +5.12% (Unseen Object), and +0.50% (Unseen Affordance) on PIADv2, and +2.59% / +5.39% on PIAD's Seen / Unseen splits. Against IAGNet [42], the original intention-driven baseline, HAMMER improves aIOU by +5.77% (Seen) and +7.50% (Unseen Object) on PIADv2. Language-driven methods LASO [18] and GEAL [24] trail HAMMER by 5.18% and 6.15% aIOU on PIADv2 Seen, and by 8.23% and 6.88% on Unseen Object. Older multi-sensor fusion baselines (PMF [50], ILN [4], PFusion [39], XMFNet [1]) and the remote-sensing FRCNN [40] are far behind, scoring below 13% aIOU on PIAD Seen versus HAMMER's 22.20%. On the corrupted benchmark, HAMMER's advantage over GREAT widens to 5.69–9.31% aIOU under jitter, drop-out, and add-local noise, indicating substantially better robustness to point-cloud degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAMMER departs from two dominant paradigms in intention-driven 3D affordance grounding: GREAT's text-generation-then-fusion pipeline and InteractVLM's 2D-segmentation-then-back-projection approach. Instead of producing intermediate language descriptions or 2D contact maps, it directly repurposes MLLM hidden states as cross-modal features via a hierarchical cross-attention mechanism and a multi-granular geometry lifting module that progressively injects 3D spatial cues into the 2D-derived intention embedding. The task itself was established by IAGNet (PIAD) and scaled by GREAT (PIADv2), while the broader MLLM-for-3D line (PAVLM, 3D-ADLLM, SeqAfford) informs the use of large multimodal models, though HAMMER is the first to exploit MLLM internal representations rather than their generated outputs for affordance grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a cleaner, more direct route to leveraging MLLM understanding for 3D tasks without the error accumulation of 2D-to-3D lifting or the manual template design of text-generation pipelines, and the newly constructed corrupted benchmark provides a much-needed robustness evaluation standard for the field. The substantial unseen-object gains (+5.1–5.4% aIOU) suggest the cross-modal integration genuinely improves generalization to novel geometries, though the more modest unseen-affordance improvement highlights a remaining gap in generalizing to entirely new interaction types. The authors note future extension to scene-level affordance grounding as an open direction, and the reliance on a single MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) with LoRA fine-tuning leaves open how the approach scales to larger or differently-architected multimodal models.
