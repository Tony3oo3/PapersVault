## Problem

Existing arbitrary-scale super-resolution methods degrade sharply when the inference scale exceeds their training range due to cross-scale distribution shift, producing blur, artifacts, and detail loss. Cascading multiple specialized networks mitigates this but introduces parameter redundancy and lacks flexibility for dynamically varying scales. The paper identifies two compounding bottlenecks in any cyclic approach: progressive distribution drift across iterations and cross-patch texture inconsistencies from patch-wise diffusion processing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image and an arbitrary target scaling factor s, which is decomposed into a sequence of sub-scales each bounded by s_max=4. Auxiliary inputs include depth maps from a pretrained DepthAnything model and text prompts dynamically extracted via RAM at each upsampling stage. For high-resolution inference, the progressively enlarged image is processed in 512×512 patches with 64-pixel overlap, and the final output is a full-resolution HR image assembled via MultiDiffusion blending.

## Method

CASR reformulates ultra-magnification as a sequence of in-distribution scale transitions by repeatedly applying a single SD-Turbo backbone (fine-tuned with LoRA and guided by ControlNet) at bounded sub-scales, so each step stays within the learned distribution. The Superpixel-based Structural Alignment Module (SSAM) stabilizes cross-iteration distributions by decomposing each intermediate image into a superpixel representation (via a pruned SuperPixel-FCN) and a depth map, suppressing accumulated noise and edge misalignment before the next upsampling step. The Self-similarity Aware Refinement Module (SARM) restores high-frequency textures by computing global self-attention over cached bottleneck features across patches, injecting LR global semantics via cross-attention from a SAM encoder, and enforcing a correlation-guided loss that aligns pairwise similarity matrices between the reconstruction and ground truth. Training proceeds in two stages: first fine-tuning the diffusion backbone with reconstruction and depth-consistency losses, then freezing it and training the SARM with an additional correlation term.

## Results

On the RealSR dataset at ×30 magnification, CASR achieves MUSIQ 37.84, NIQE 7.81, and PI 6.73, outperforming the second-best method IDM (MUSIQ 28.22, NIQE 8.35, PI 7.44) by 34.1%, 6.5%, and 9.5% respectively. On the DIV8K synthetic dataset at ×30, CASR outperforms LIIF+Diff by 16.9% in LPIPS and surpasses IDM by 75.2%, 12.3%, and 15.8% in MUSIQ, NIQE, and PI. On CelebA-HQ at ×12, CASR achieves MUSIQ 71.71 and NIQE 4.77, substantially outperforming IDM (MUSIQ 41.47, NIQE 9.66) and Kim (MUSIQ 31.66, NIQE 11.10). Performance remains robust across all tested scales from ×4 to ×30, while baselines exhibit significant degradation beyond ×8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASR decisively outperforms all six baselines across every scale and dataset tested. Against the strongest diffusion-based competitor IDM [9], CASR leads by 34.1% in MUSIQ at ×30 on RealSR (37.84 vs. 28.22) and by 75.2% in MUSIQ at ×30 on DIV8K. Kim [17] is the second-strongest baseline but still trails CASR by 10.41 MUSIQ points at ×30 on RealSR and by 40.05 points on DIV8K. The implicit-representation baselines (LINF [32], BFSR [27], LIIF+Diff [8], CiaoSR+Diff [5]) all degrade severely beyond ×8, with MUSIQ scores dropping below 20 at ×30 on RealSR, whereas CASR maintains 37.84. Notably, the progressive upsampling experiment (×4×3×1.5) shows that without distribution alignment, even in-range cascading fails to improve over direct ×18 upsampling for all baselines, confirming that CASR's SSAM module is critical for making cyclic SR effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASR departs from the dominant INR-based ASISR paradigm (LIIF, LINF, BFSR) that attempts to extrapolate a single learned mapping beyond its training scale range, and from cascaded multi-network pipelines (SR3, CDM) that stack specialized SR models. Instead, it reframes ultra-magnification as repeated in-distribution transitions using a single diffusion backbone (SD-Turbo), borrowing superpixel segmentation from SuperPixel-FCN for structural alignment and MultiDiffusion for patch fusion. The correlation-guided loss and cross-attention mechanism in SARM draw on self-similarity concepts from non-local methods but apply them specifically to enforce cross-patch consistency in a cyclic diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cyclic in-distribution framing offers a principled, single-model alternative to both extrapolation-based ASISR and multi-network cascades, with the practical advantage of unbounded scale without retraining. However, the pipeline's reliance on multiple pretrained models (DepthAnything, SAM, SD-Turbo, ControlNet) and its two-stage training with LoRA adaptation raise questions about deployment simplicity and computational cost at extreme scales. Open questions include whether adaptive superpixel sizing can resolve the detail-vs-artifact trade-off observed in the ablation, and whether the cyclic framework extends naturally to video, 3D, or cross-modal reconstruction as the authors suggest.
