## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods struggle with thin semi-transparent specular surfaces (e.g., car windows, plastic films) where sharp reflections and clear transmission coexist. A single opacity parameter conflates geometric presence with optical transmissivity, forcing a trade-off: high opacity yields sharp reflections but occludes the background, while low opacity preserves transmission but produces blurry reflections and spurious floaters. Prior deferred-shading approaches fail because G-buffers store only the nearest surface, making transparency fundamentally difficult to handle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 220–240 multi-view RGB images per scene plus a binary transparent mask from a pre-trained SAM2 model, which serves solely as a regularization signal rather than a segmentation cue for multi-stage pipelines. It produces a real-time novel-view renderer (~33 FPS) that outputs a decomposed scene: a specular reflection layer from the deferred pass, a subsurface-transport layer (transmitted background plus intrinsic scattering), and an editable Gaussian representation where roughness, transmissivity, specular intensity, and material tint can be independently manipulated post-training.

## Method

RT-Splatting factorizes each 2D Gaussian's opacity into a geometric occupancy σ (probability of surface interaction) and an optical opacity α (conditional absorption probability), yielding a unified surface-volume representation from a single set of primitives. A hybrid deferred-forward pipeline then uses σ to aggregate first-hit surface attributes into G-buffers for per-pixel specular shading, while the product α_eff = σ·α drives a concurrent volumetric forward pass that accumulates transmitted background radiance without occlusion. To resolve the optimization ambiguity where residual specular errors leak misleading gradients into the transmission branch, a Specular-Aware Gradient Gating mechanism computes per-pixel gating weights from the local variance of the specular component and scales the backpropagated image-loss gradients accordingly, suppressing floater formation in high-specular regions. A learnable attenuation factor β modulates the subsurface-transport component to model the perceptual suppression of transmitted details under strong highlights, and a BCE loss against the SAM2 mask regularizes optical opacity to prevent unconstrained ghost geometries from accumulating in diffuse regions.

## Results

On six public benchmark scenes (Ref-Real, NeRF-Casting, EnvGS, T&T), RT-Splatting achieves 27.49 PSNR and 0.831 SSIM on entire images, and 39.77 PSNR / 0.992 SSIM / 0.010 LPIPS on transparent regions, outperforming the strongest baseline EnvGS (27.14 PSNR; 37.95 PSNR on transparent regions) by +0.35 dB overall and +1.81 dB in transparent areas. On two self-captured scenes, the advantage widens to +1.27 dB (entire) and +2.92 dB (transparent) over the best baseline. RT-Splatting renders at 33.3 FPS with 0.9 h training time, faster than EnvGS (18.3 FPS, 2.9 h) while delivering substantially higher quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RT-Splatting outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both the public-benchmark and self-captured evaluations. The largest gains appear in transparent regions: +1.81 dB PSNR over EnvGS (39.77 vs 37.95) and +2.92 dB over 3DGS (35.49 vs 32.57) on self-captured scenes, confirming that the occupancy-opacity factorization and gradient gating specifically address the reflection-transmission ambiguity. Among reflective-surface methods, EnvGS is the closest competitor on entire-image PSNR (27.14 vs 27.49) but trails by a wider margin in transparent regions and is nearly 2× slower at inference (18.3 vs 33.3 FPS) with 3.2× longer training. The deferred-shading baselines 3DGS-DR and Ref-GS achieve similar entire-image quality (~26.6 PSNR) but cannot model transmission at all, capping their transparent-region PSNR around 37.8. The foundational 3DGS and 2DGS are fastest (200+ FPS) but produce the weakest transparent-region results (37.3–37.7 PSNR) because they lack any explicit surface or reflection modeling. GShader, which uses per-Gaussian shading with a learnable environment map, performs worst overall (25.78 PSNR), indicating that per-primitive shading is insufficient for complex view-dependent effects on semi-transparent surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

RT-Splatting builds directly on the 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) framework and the deferred shading pipeline popularized by Ref-GS and 3DGSDR, but departs from both reflective-scene methods (which treat surfaces as opaque) and transparent-object methods (which rely on multi-stage pipelines or simplified backgrounds). The key conceptual departure is the occupancy-opacity factorization, which reinterprets the single alpha-blending parameter as two physically distinct quantities—geometric occupancy for surface aggregation and optical opacity for volumetric attenuation—thereby unifying surface and volume roles within one set of Gaussians. The Specular-Aware Gradient Gating mechanism is a novel optimization technique with no direct analogue in prior 3DGS work, addressing a specific failure mode (gradient leakage from specular residuals into the transmission branch) that prior methods sidestep by decoupling training stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a long-standing tension in Gaussian Splatting: a single opacity parameter cannot simultaneously support sharp specular reflections (requiring high geometric presence) and clear transmission (requiring low optical attenuation). The factorization is a clean, minimal change that enables a hybrid deferred-forward pipeline and naturally supports scene editing (adjusting roughness, transparency, or tint independently). However, the method is explicitly limited to thin semi-transparent surfaces and does not model refraction or multi-bounce light transport, leaving thicker refractive media (solid glass, water) as an open problem. The reliance on SAM2 for transparent mask supervision also introduces a dependency on segmentation quality, and the approach has not been validated on scenes with multiple overlapping transparent layers.
