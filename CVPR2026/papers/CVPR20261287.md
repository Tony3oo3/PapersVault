key_contribution: DrivoR demonstrates that repurposing ViT register tokens as camera-aware scene descriptors (16 per camera, 64 total) achieves near-lossless 250× token compression for end-to-end driving, and that a disentangled scoring decoder with stop-gradient enables interpretable, behavior-conditioned trajectory selection, yielding SOTA results on NAVSIM-v1/v2 and HUGSIM with 3× throughput over ViT-L baselines.
limitations: The model uses only 4 static RGB cameras with no temporal/historical frames, LiDAR, or map information, limiting its applicability to more sensor-rich or dynamic driving scenarios. The SOTA margins on NAVSIM-v1 are narrow (93.7 vs. 93.5 PDMS), and the NAVSIM-v2 evaluation relies on a small 7-scene validation set, leaving open questions about robustness under stricter closed-loop conditions at scale.

surprise_factor: The most striking result is the 250× token compression (16k → 64 scene tokens) with only ~0.3 PDMS loss, achieved by a mechanism (register tokens) that was designed for a completely different purpose (fixing attention sinks), and the ablation showing that a transformer decoder with the same parameter budget performs noticeably worse than registers. The back-camera register collapse (tokens converging to a single representation) is a visually intuitive but previously unobserved emergent behavior. However, the overall SOTA margins are small and the efficiency gains, while practical, are not order-of-magnitude.

## Problem

End-to-end driving architectures built on Vision Transformers produce thousands of spatial tokens per camera that must be processed for hundreds of trajectory candidates, creating a severe computational bottleneck. The standard remedy—uniform spatial pooling—treats all cameras identically and enforces rigid resolution constraints, discarding planning-relevant structure. The paper asks how few tokens suffice to represent a driving scene and whether ViT register tokens can serve as a learned, camera-aware compression mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261287/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 4 RGB camera views (front, front-left, front-right, back) plus ego state (pose, velocity, acceleration) and a driving command. It outputs 64 candidate trajectories, each a sequence of (x, y, θ) poses in the ego-local frame at uniform time intervals, along with 6 interpretable PDMS sub-scores (no-collision, drivable-area, time-to-collision, comfort, progress, and one additional component) per trajectory. At inference, the final trajectory is selected by maximizing a weighted combination of sub-scores, where the weights encode a desired driving behavior profile.

## Method

DrivoR uses a DINOv2 ViT-S encoder per camera, LoRA-finetuned (rank 32), with 16 additional per-camera register tokens appended to the patch sequence; only these 16 registers are retrieved at the final layer, compressing ~16k patch tokens per camera into 16 camera-aware scene tokens (64 total across 4 cameras). Two 4-layer transformer decoders follow: a trajectory decoder that decodes 64 learnable queries (augmented with ego state) into candidate trajectories via cross-attention to scene tokens, and a scoring decoder that re-embeds each decoded trajectory through a separate MLP into a new feature space before scoring, with a stop-gradient preventing scoring losses from backpropagating into the trajectory decoder. Training uses a winner-takes-all L1 regression loss for trajectories and binary cross-entropy against oracle PDMS sub-scores for the scoring head. At inference, the learned sub-scores are combined with adjustable weights (borrowed from offline-RL reward shaping) to select trajectories matching a desired behavior, enabling a single trained model to produce safety-oriented or progress-oriented driving without retraining.

## Results

On NAVSIM-v1, DrivoR achieves a PDMS of 93.7, outperforming all prior camera-only methods and approaching human-level performance (94.8). On NAVSIM-v2 (navhard-two-stage), DrivoR scores 48.3 EPDMS, surpassing ZTRS (48.1) and GTRS variants (45.0–45.4). On the closed-loop HUGSIM benchmark, DrivoR achieves 49.8% Road Completion and 35.7 HD-Score zero-shot, beating UniAD (45.9/32.7) and LTF (38.9/23.7). The model also delivers >3× throughput improvement over a ViT-L baseline (110 ms vs. 400 ms per forward pass on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM-v1, DrivoR (PDMS 93.7) edges out DriveSuprim (93.5) by 0.2 points and Centaur (92.6) by 1.1 points, while maintaining a 100% comfort score. It surpasses the trajectory-proposal methods iPad (91.7) and Hydra-MDP++ (91.0) by 2.0 and 2.7 PDMS respectively, and the VLA-based DriveVLA-W0 (90.2) by 3.5 points. On NAVSIM-v2, DrivoR (48.3 EPDMS) narrowly beats ZTRS (48.1) and substantially outperforms GTRS-A (45.4) and GTRS-D (45.0); notably, replacing GTRS's V2-99 backbone with DrivoR's ViT-S + registers (GTRS-DrivoR, 45.8) already improves over the original GTRS-D, confirming the compression benefit. On HUGSIM closed-loop, DrivoR achieves the highest RC (49.8) and HD-Score (35.7) among all reported baselines, beating UniAD by 3.9 RC and 3.0 HD-Score. The only method scoring above DrivoR on NAVSIM-v1 is RAP-DINO (93.8), which is trained on a 10× larger dataset, making the comparison non-standard.

## Relation to Prior Work

DrivoR repurposes ViT register tokens (originally introduced by Darcet et al. to fix attention sinks) as a scene-compression mechanism for end-to-end driving, a use the authors claim is novel in this domain. It sits within the trajectory-proposal planning lineage (Hydra-MDP, GTRS, iPad) but departs from prior E2E methods that rely on CNN backbones, large ViT variants with BEV projections, or Perceiver-style cross-attention for token reduction. The behavior-conditioned scoring draws conceptually from offline RL (CtRL Sim), where reward weights are adjusted at inference to shift driving style, but is implemented here as a supervised sub-score prediction head rather than an RL objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that 64 register tokens (16 per camera) nearly match 16k-token performance (250× compression) with only 0.6M extra parameters offers a practical, low-overhead alternative to pooling for real-time E2E driving, and the 3× throughput gain over a ViT-L baseline makes ViT backbones more deployable. The disentangled scoring architecture (stop-gradient between trajectory and scoring decoders) is a clean architectural insight that yields measurable gains and enables interpretable, tunable driving behavior without retraining. Open questions include whether register-based compression generalizes to more cameras, LiDAR, or temporal inputs, and whether the modest SOTA margins (93.7 vs. 93.5 PDMS on NAVSIM-v1) hold under stricter closed-loop evaluation at scale.
