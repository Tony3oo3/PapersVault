## Problem

Classical VQA methods output only a single numerical score, failing to explain the complex, interrelated quality dimensions (e.g., flicker, motion blur) that determine perceived quality. While adapting video LMMs via instruction tuning could provide human-readable justifications, prior efforts relied heavily on costly human annotations and proprietary APIs (e.g., GPT-4), were mostly confined to the image domain, and could not simultaneously achieve precise scoring and rich justification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263205/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (16 frames uniformly sampled) as input and produces three types of output: a numerical quality score (derived via softmax pooling over discrete text-defined levels), a hierarchical quality justification with reasoning across 14 dimensions, and per-dimension ratings mapped to five ITU BT.500 tiers (bad, poor, fair, good, excellent). The resulting S2I dataset contains 320K instruction-response pairs: 104K detailed justifications (avg. 129.4 words) and 216K QA pairs in What/How and Yes/No formats with balanced answer distributions.

## Method

The SIG pipeline automates instruction generation in three stages: (1) collecting 104K videos from both labeled VQA databases (Maxwell, 4,543 videos with 13 annotated dimensions) and unlabeled general video databases using a lightweight quality assessor for balanced quality distribution; (2) scoring 14 quality dimensions via a deployed video processing platform's expert models and discretizing scores to five ITU text levels (validated at SRCC/PLCC > 0.95); (3) aggregating dimension ratings through a hierarchical CoT that groups dimensions into distortion-related and aesthetic-related categories, then uses Vicuna-7B for rephrasing, high-level caption integration via ShareCaptioner-Video, and expansion into diverse QA formats. Training follows a progressive two-stage strategy: Stage I pretrains only the visual encoder and projector (LLM frozen) on 100K dimension-description pairs to establish initial quality perception, while Stage II applies LoRA (r=16) to the LLM alongside the connector and visual encoder on 220K justifications and QA pairs for full instruction-following capability.

## Results

On the proposed S2I-Bench, S2I-tuned models achieve an average VCG sum improvement of +1.39 over their untuned baselines, with the largest gains in Temporal Understanding (TU, +0.45) and Correctness of Information (CI, +0.40). The best-performing model, LLaVA-OneVision (Vicuna-v1.1-7B), improves its VCG sum from 7.96 to 9.88 (+1.92). On standard VQA scoring benchmarks, average SRCC/PLCC improvements range from +0.207/+0.211 (LIVE-VQC) to +0.314/+0.340 (Maxwell), with LLaVA-OneVision reaching 0.795/0.812 SRCC/PLCC on Maxwell (up from 0.474/0.428).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All six video LMMs (LLaVA-OneVision, LLaVA-Next-Video, InternVL-Chat, Video-LLaVA, LLaVA-Video, VideoLLaMA3) show consistent improvements after S2I tuning on both justification (S2I-Bench VCG) and scoring (SRCC/PLCC) metrics. The largest VCG sum gain is LLaVA-OneVision (+1.92, from 7.96 to 9.88), followed by InternVL-Chat (+1.70) and LLaVA-Next-Video (+1.33); the smallest is LLaVA-Video (+0.91). On scoring, LLaVA-OneVision achieves the highest absolute SRCC of 0.795 on Maxwell (vs. 0.474 untuned), while VideoLLaMA3 reaches 0.793 SRCC on LSVQ-test. Cross-dataset generalization is notable: LLaVA-OneVision gains +0.299 SRCC on LSVQ-1080p despite distribution shift. The paper also reports zero-shot results for GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, and Gemini-1.5 Pro in the supplement, with S2I-tuned open-source models surpassing these closed-source baselines on both scoring and justification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on quality-centric instruction tuning for LMMs (Q-Instruct, Q-Align, VQA²) by replacing human-written quality descriptions and proprietary LLM APIs with an automated pipeline (SIG) that scores 14 quality dimensions and aggregates them via hierarchical Chain-of-Thought. It builds on Q-Align's discrete text-defined rating levels for scoring and adapts Video-ChatGPT's VCG evaluation metrics to video quality justification. The key departure from prior work is the elimination of subjective human studies and the use of unlabeled general video databases (not just VQA databases) as data sources, enabling 320K instruction pairs at a scale and diversity unattainable through manual annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIG pipeline provides a reproducible, scalable recipe for generating video quality instruction data without the bottleneck of expert annotation, which is critical as UGC and AIGC video volumes grow. The progressive tuning strategy (dimension-description pretraining followed by full instruction tuning) offers a practical two-stage recipe that practitioners can adopt for other quality-related tasks. Open questions include whether incorporating non-visual dimensions (e.g., audio quality) in pretraining would further improve scoring, whether the approach generalizes to other modalities, and whether the current 14-dimension coverage is sufficient for emerging content types. The S2I-Bench, with its video-specific temporal understanding metric, addresses a gap in existing benchmarks that primarily measure text-level accuracy and completeness.
