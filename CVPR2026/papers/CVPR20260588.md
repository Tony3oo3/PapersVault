## Problem

Text-to-image models consistently produce images with exaggerated saturation and contrast even when prompted for realistic-style outputs, a bias reinforced by existing evaluation metrics (PickScore, ImageReward, HPSv3, MPS) that favor visually vivid images over photometrically faithful ones. No dedicated benchmark or objective metric exists to quantify color fidelity specifically, leaving a gap between semantic/aesthetic evaluation and the perceptual dimension of natural color distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260588/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CFM takes a generated RGB image (longer side ≤ 1024 px) paired with its text prompt and outputs a single scalar color fidelity score via the <|Reward|> token logit of a Qwen2-VL backbone. CFR is a training-free, plug-and-play module that takes only a text prompt and a base guidance scale s₀, then produces a refined image by replacing the uniform CFG scale with a spatially varying, temporally decaying field s_t(u,v) computed from CFM's cross-modal attention maps, which are reshaped to 2D and upsampled to the latent resolution.

## Method

The framework has three components. CFD constructs 1.33M images in ordered 7-image groups (one real photo + six synthetic variants at increasing CFG scales) across 12 categories, providing explicit ordinal supervision of color realism. CFM uses Qwen2-VL to jointly encode image and text tokens into a unified embedding, then an MLP head predicts per-image scores trained with a differentiable softrank loss that enforces the correct ordering within each group via pairwise sigmoid probabilities and MSE against ground-truth ranks. CFR exploits CFM's text-to-image attention matrix to identify spatial regions with high color-semantic discrepancy, then adaptively reduces the local guidance scale in those regions with a linear temporal decay α(t)=1−t/T, suppressing over-saturation without altering any model parameters.

## Results

On the CFD-Test benchmark, CFM achieves 83.6% pairwise discrimination accuracy on synthetic pairs (CFD-SynPairs) and 80.1% on real-vs-synthetic pairs (CFD-Real&Syn), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline HPSv3 (57.5% / 58.3%). On the CFD-Human dataset, CFM attains Spearman ρ = 84.9, Pearson r = 85.4, and Kendall τ = 71.4 correlation with human ratings, exceeding the best baseline HPSv3 (74.4 / 76.0 / 62.8) by 10+ points on all three coefficients. The CFR refinement pipeline further improves color fidelity: on SD3.5, CFM-driven CFR raises CFM from 4.9 to 6.9 while reducing saturation deviation (ΔSat.) from 0.15 to 0.07, with negligible impact on FID (13.3→13.1) and CLIPScore (28.2→28.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFM decisively outperforms all eight baselines on both discrimination settings. The strongest baseline, HPSv3 (57.5% / 58.3%), is surpassed by CFM by approximately 26 and 22 percentage points on CFD-SynPairs and CFD-Real&Syn respectively. Traditional no-reference IQA methods (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA, LIQE) perform near-randomly on synthetic pairs (44.7–54.5%) and collapse to 21.5–24.0% on real-vs-synthetic pairs, confirming they cannot generalize from real-image degradation to generative color distortion. Among generative aesthetic metrics, PickScore (51.4% / 48.5%) and MPS (44.4% / 46.2%) trail HPSv3, while ImageReward (44.3% / 42.7%) is the weakest overall. On human correlation (Table 2), CFM leads HPSv3 by 10.5 Spearman points (84.9 vs. 74.4) and 8.6 Kendall points (71.4 vs. 62.8), with PickScore and MPS falling further behind at 71.2 and 66.7 Spearman respectively. The consistent margin across all baselines and both evaluation protocols underscores that existing metrics' bias toward vivid, high-contrast imagery systematically misranks color realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework [12] by replacing the fixed global guidance scale with a spatially varying, temporally decaying field guided by cross-modal attention from a vision-language model. For evaluation, it departs from the dominant line of preference-trained T2I metrics (PickScore, ImageReward, HPSv3, MPS) that the authors show systematically favor over-saturated imagery, and from traditional no-reference IQA methods (MUSIQ, MANIQA, CLIPIQA) that operate on real-image degradations and fail to generalize to generative color distortions. The CFM backbone follows the Qwen2-VL multimodal encoder paradigm used by HPSv3, but re-purposes it with an ordinal softrank objective over guidance-scale-ordered image groups rather than pairwise human preference labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely observed but previously unquantified failure mode in T2I generation—excessive saturation and contrast that makes outputs look 'too vivid to be real'—and provides both a diagnostic (CFM) and a training-free corrective (CFR) that can be plugged into any CFG-based diffusion model. The 1.3M-image ordered dataset and the >80% discrimination accuracy (vs. ~50–58% for existing metrics) give the community a concrete benchmark for color realism. Open questions include whether the CFG-scale-as-proxy construction captures all relevant color distortions (e.g., hue shifts, white-balance errors), whether the approach extends to video generation, and whether the narrow saturation/contrast focus generalizes to other photometric dimensions like dynamic range or color temperature.
