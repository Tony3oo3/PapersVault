## Problem

VLA models report success rates exceeding 95% on benchmarks like LIBERO, yet real-world deployment reveals stark fragility under environmental variation. Existing simulation-based robustness evaluations suffer from narrow perturbation coverage, reliance on manually designed scenarios, and coarse aggregate reporting that obscures when and how models actually fail. This paper asks: can we systematically characterize the boundaries of VLA model generalization across a broad spectrum of controlled perturbations with fine-grained, reproducible analysis?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260595/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance provides multi-view RGB observations (third-person and optional wrist camera), a natural language instruction, and robot proprioceptive state to a VLA model, which outputs a continuous action sequence executed in the LIBERO simulator. The benchmark produces per-dimension, per-difficulty-level success rates (L1–L5) rather than a single aggregate score, enabling identification of the exact perturbation intensity at which a model degrades. Over 56K automatically generated scenarios span 7 perturbation dimensions and 21 sub-dimensions, with 10,030 task instances in the core test set.

## Method

LIBERO-Plus extends the LIBERO benchmark through three methodological axes: (1) comprehensive perturbation coverage across seven dimensions—object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state, language instruction, lighting, background texture, and sensor noise—each parameterized to produce diverse sub-settings; (2) fully automated, scalable generation via parameterized methods that construct both training and test datasets without manual design; and (3) progressive difficulty stratification (L1–L5) calibrated empirically by evaluating tasks with four representative VLA models, creating a graduated robustness profile. The paper further introduces a statistical framework defining the compositional generalization gap Δ_ij as the conditional covariance between two perturbation indicators given task success, quantifying whether combined perturbations introduce super-additive difficulty beyond their independent effects. Evaluation of ten VLA models (spanning autoregressive, diffusion-based, and RL-trained architectures) reveals that performance drops from ~95% to below 30% under modest perturbations, with camera viewpoint and robot state changes being the most damaging, while language perturbations have minimal impact—suggesting models largely ignore linguistic inputs.

## Results

On LIBERO-Plus, the best-performing model (OpenVLA-OFT) achieves 97.1% success on unperturbed tasks but drops to 69.6% average across all seven perturbation dimensions, with camera viewpoint changes causing the largest single-dimension drop (−40.8 pts). The most fragile model (OpenVLA) collapses from 76.5% to 15.6% total, and even the second-best (RIPT-VLA, 97.5% original) falls to 68.4%. Across all ten models, camera and robot initial state perturbations are the most damaging (average drops of ~55–65 pts), while language perturbations produce the smallest average drop (−25.3 pts), revealing that models largely ignore linguistic input.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the ten evaluated VLA models, OpenVLA-OFT (97.1% → 69.6% total) and RIPT-VLA (97.5% → 68.4%) are the most robust, both benefiting from wrist-camera inputs and diverse co-training. OpenVLA-OFT_w, which removes the wrist view, drops to 55.8% total, confirming that the third-person-only variant is far more vulnerable to camera and lighting shifts (−84.9 and −18.5 pts respectively). π₀-fast (61.6% total) outperforms π₀ (53.6%) by 8 pts, attributed to its more diverse training data. At the bottom, OpenVLA (15.6%) and WorldVLA (25.0%) are catastrophically fragile, with camera perturbation alone reducing success to 0.8% and 0.1% respectively. A critical cross-model finding is that language perturbations cause only a −25.3 pt average drop, and ablation experiments show that removing instructions entirely has minimal impact on short-horizon tasks, indicating that most models function as Vision-Action rather than true Vision-Language-Action systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LIBERO benchmark and the broader line of simulation-based robustness evaluations for VLA models (AGNOSTOS, RL4VLA, INT-ACT, Gembench, VLATest, COLOSEUM) by unifying seven perturbation dimensions under a single automated, difficulty-stratified framework. Prior efforts addressed individual perturbation axes with manually designed scenarios and reported only aggregate success rates; LIBERO-Plus departs by providing comprehensive multi-dimensional coverage, parameterized generation of 56K+ scenarios, and progressive L1–L5 difficulty levels that expose *when* and *how* models fail. The evaluation targets the same family of VLA architectures (autoregressive token-based, diffusion-based, and RL-augmented) that dominate recent manipulation research, positioning the benchmark as a diagnostic tool for the field's current generation of models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a stark gap between near-saturated LIBERO scores (>95%) and actual robustness (dropping below 30% under modest perturbations), challenging the community's assumption that high benchmark performance reflects genuine competency. The finding that VLA models largely ignore language instructions—degenerating into Vision-Action policies—and that they rely on memorized object positions rather than semantic understanding, points to fundamental architectural limitations that current training paradigms do not address. Open questions include whether these findings transfer to real-world deployment, whether the compositional (negative) interaction gap can be mitigated by architectural changes, and whether the proposed augmented training set (20K+ trajectories) can be scaled to close the robustness gap without sacrificing in-distribution performance.
