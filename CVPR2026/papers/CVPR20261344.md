## Problem

Modern diffusion models have converged on Transformer backbones (e.g., DiT), driven by the belief that full attention is the only scalable path to high-quality generation. This shift has made training increasingly dependent on massive GPU infrastructure, while the locality bias, parameter efficiency, and hardware friendliness of modern ConvNets remain largely unexplored in generative modeling. The paper asks whether a well-designed fully convolutional architecture can match or exceed Transformer-based diffusion models in both quality and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261344/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in a compressed latent space (VAE-encoded) at 256×256 or 512×512 pixel resolutions. At each denoising step it consumes a noisy latent tensor, a class label embedding, and a scalar timestep, all fused into the network via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN). The output is a predicted noise (or velocity) at the same spatial resolution as the input latent, which is iteratively denoised over 250 DDPM steps and decoded by the VAE to produce a final RGB image.

## Method

FCDM reassembles the ConvNeXt block for conditional diffusion: a 7×7 depthwise convolution is followed by two 1×1 pointwise convolutions in an inverted-bottleneck arrangement (channel expansion applied *after* the depthwise conv so its cost is unchanged), with Global Response Normalization (GRN) between them to promote diverse channel activations without extra parameters. LayerNorm is replaced by AdaLN to inject class and timestep conditioning, with zero-initialized final scale for stable optimization. The blocks are organized in a U-shaped hierarchy with skip connections, parameterized by only two hyperparameters—number of blocks L and hidden channels C—both doubled at each 2× downsampling stage, yielding an easy scaling law. Compared to the prior convolutional diffusion model DiCo, FCDM eliminates the separate feedforward module and replaces compact channel attention (CCA) with GRN, reducing FLOPs to roughly 75% of DiCo while achieving lower FID. Trained with standard DiT-style settings (AdamW, EMA, 256 batch size) on ImageNet, FCDM-XL reaches FID 2.03 at 256×256 using only 64.6 GFLOPs (~50% of DiT-XL/2) and 7× fewer training steps.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, FCDM-XL achieves FID 10.72 at 400K iterations using only 64.6 GFLOPs (≈50% of DiT-XL/2's 118.6 GFLOPs) and 272.7 it/s throughput (3.4× faster than DiT-XL/2's 80.5 it/s). With extended training to 1M iterations, FCDM-XL reaches FID 7.91, surpassing DiT-XL/2 trained for 7M iterations (FID 9.62) with 7× fewer training steps. At 512×512, FCDM-XL achieves FID 7.46 at 1M iterations, outperforming DiT-XL/2 at 3M iterations (FID 12.03) with 7.5× fewer steps, while maintaining the best FLOPs (257.7G) and throughput (129.6 it/s) among all compared models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCDM outperforms DiT across all four model scales (S, B, L, XL) in both FID and FLOPs at 400K iterations: at XL scale, FCDM-XL achieves FID 10.72 vs. DiT-XL/2's 19.47 while using only 54.5% of the FLOPs. Against the strongest convolutional baseline DiCo, FCDM-XL achieves FID 10.72 vs. DiCo-XL's 11.67 at 400K with 26% fewer FLOPs (64.6G vs. 87.3G) and 1.6× higher throughput (272.7 vs. 174.2 it/s). With extended training, FCDM-XL at 1M iterations (FID 7.91) surpasses DiT-XL/2 at 7M (FID 9.62) and DiG-XL/2 at 1.2M (FID 8.60), while DiC-H at 800K (FID 8.96) requires 3.2× more FLOPs (204.4G). At 512×512, FCDM-XL at 1M (FID 7.46) beats DiT-XL/2 at 3M (FID 12.03) and DiCo-XL at 1.3M (FID 8.10), with the best FLOPs (257.7G vs. 524.7G for DiT) and throughput (129.6 vs. 18.6 it/s). The only baseline that outperforms FCDM-XL at 400K is DiC-H (FID 11.36 vs. 10.72 — actually FCDM wins here), and at the 256×256 benchmark with guidance (Table 4), FCDM-XL achieves the best FID (2.03) and IS (285.7) among all latent diffusion models, slightly edging out DiCo-XL (FID 2.05) and SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the ConvNeXt/ConvNeXt-V2 classification backbone and adapts it for conditional latent diffusion by replacing LayerNorm with AdaLN (following DiT's conditioning mechanism) and organizing blocks into a U-shaped hierarchy with skip connections. It positions itself against the transformer-dominant trajectory established by DiT and its successors (SiT, MM-DiT, FLUX), and specifically against the closest prior convolutional diffusion models DiC and DiCo, arguing that ConvNeXt's 7×7 depthwise convolutions, inverted bottleneck, and GRN provide a more efficient alternative to DiCo's 3×3 separable convolutions and compact channel attention. The training protocol and diffusion parameterization are inherited from ADM, and the scaling philosophy (two hyperparameters: blocks L and channels C) mirrors DiT's design space but simplified.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that transformer backbones are necessary for scalable diffusion models, showing that a fully convolutional architecture can match or exceed DiT at the same parameter count while using ~50% fewer FLOPs and converging 7× faster in training steps. The practical implication is significant: FCDM-XL trains on a 4-GPU system, making competitive diffusion model training accessible to smaller labs. However, the authors acknowledge that FCDM-XL does not yet surpass the absolute SOTA of EDM-2 or Simpler Diffusion, and the text-to-image extension (mentioned in supplementary materials) remains to be validated at production scale. Open questions include whether the efficiency advantage persists at much larger scales or with more advanced training frameworks (e.g., flow matching, REPA-style alignment).
