## Problem

The 7-point fundamental matrix estimation problem is known to have a unique solution under a special V-Umlaut configuration (five points on two lines), but this uniqueness was only observed algebraically in prior work without an elementary geometric explanation or a practical solver. The standard 7-point algorithm still returns two spurious solutions in this configuration, and no linear minimal solver existed for the associated 5-point problem where the dependent correspondences are virtual midpoints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The solver takes five independent 2D point correspondences between two views plus camera intrinsics for K⁻¹ normalization. It internally constructs two virtual (dependent) correspondences as midpoints along lines through the first point in each view, yielding seven total correspondences that satisfy collinearity constraints. The output is a unique rank-2 fundamental matrix (3×3, up to scale) from which relative pose (R, t) is decomposed; unlike the standard 7-point solver which returns up to three candidates, this method returns exactly one solution.

## Method

The method applies per-view projective normalization via homographies that map four points to canonical positions, reducing the fundamental matrix to a 5-unknown form with diagonal zeros and gauge f₃₂=1. The key insight is that the collinearity constraints on the dependent points (lying on lines through p₁ and q₁) allow expressing f₂₁ linearly in f₁₂ and f₃₁ linearly in f₁₃, so the rank-2 determinantal constraint det(F)=0 factors as f₁₂·f₁₃·(c₁(s)·f₃₁ + c₂(s))=0. Under non-degeneracy assumptions (camera centers not coplanar with scene points), the factor f₁₂·f₁₃ ≠ 0, leaving a single linear equation that, together with the four epipolar constraints, yields a unique solution to a 5×5 linear system. The two spurious solutions of the standard 7-point algorithm are shown to correspond to degenerate affine fundamental matrices where the epipoles lie on the line at infinity, contradicting the non-coplanarity assumption. For the practical 5-point heuristic, virtual midpoints of line segments between correspondences serve as the dependent points, making the entire pipeline a linear 5-point solver suitable for RANSAC.

## Results

On PhotoTourism (13 scenes, 1000 RANSAC iterations), V-Umlaut+ENM achieves a median pose error of 1.25° and mAA@10° of 79.62, matching the SOTA 5pE+ENM (1.26°, 79.65) at 2.6× lower runtime (44.12 ms vs. 116.17 ms). On Aachen Day-Night v1.1, V-Umlaut+ENM attains the best median error (2.69°) and competitive mAA@10° (64.07) at 36.93 ms, versus 98.99 ms for 5pE+ENM. Without ENM, the raw V-Umlaut solver is the fastest method (3.52 ms on PhotoTourism, 3.16 ms on Aachen) but sacrifices accuracy (mAA 49.79 vs. 79.58 for 5pE). The Fast ENM variant further reduces runtime to 36.37 ms while retaining mAA@10° of 79.44 on PhotoTourism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SOTA 5-point essential matrix solver (5pE, Nistér 2003), V-Umlaut without ENM is ~7× faster (3.52 ms vs. 24.68 ms on PhotoTourism) but substantially less accurate (mAA 49.79 vs. 79.58); however, V-Umlaut+ENM matches 5pE+ENM in accuracy (mAA 79.62 vs. 79.65, MED 1.25° vs. 1.26°) while running 2.6× faster (44.12 ms vs. 116.17 ms). Against the virtual-correspondence 4-point solver 4pE(M) (Tzamos et al. 2025), V-Umlaut+ENM again matches accuracy (79.62 vs. 79.58 mAA) at lower runtime (44.12 ms vs. 108.11 ms). Against the classical 7-point F-matrix solver (7pF, Hartley et al. 1992), V-Umlaut is both faster (3.52 ms vs. 8.07 ms) and more accurate without ENM (mAA 49.79 vs. 46.44), and with ENM both reach comparable accuracy (79.62 vs. 79.58) but V-Umlaut remains faster (44.12 ms vs. 61.44 ms). On Aachen Day-Night, V-Umlaut+ENM achieves the best median pose error (2.69°) among all methods, slightly edging out 5pE+ENM (2.71°) and 4pE(M)+ENM (2.71°). Overall, the paper's key claim is that V-Umlaut+ENM is the best accuracy-to-runtime trade-off, matching SOTA accuracy at roughly 40% of the runtime of 5pE+ENM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PLMP classification of Kiehn et al. [29], which identified the V-Umlaut configuration (5 points on two lines) as having a unique fundamental matrix solution, and on Tzamos et al. [47], whose virtual midpoint strategy for generating dependent correspondences and Early Non-Minimal Refitting (ENM) framework are adopted here. The authors' prior work on minimal point-line problems [9] provides the methodological template. Conceptually, the paper departs from the standard 7-point algorithm by showing that two of its three solutions are provably spurious under nondegeneracy assumptions, and from Nistér's nonlinear 5-point solver by deriving a purely linear system through projective normalization and barycentric parameterization of the dependent points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first linear, minimal solver for the V-Umlaut configuration, which is both theoretically clean (elementary analysis of spurious solutions) and practically fast (3.5 ms vs. 24.7 ms for the SOTA 5-point solver without ENM). The finding that a naive virtual-midpoint heuristic for dependent correspondences, combined with ENM, matches the accuracy of Nistér's 5-point solver at roughly 40% of the runtime suggests that simple geometric priors can substitute for more complex nonlinear solvers in RANSAC pipelines. Open questions include whether the midpoint strategy can be replaced by learned matching for improved robustness, whether the approach extends to configurations with more virtual correspondences (e.g., three virtual points from room-corner motifs), and whether the linear solver's sensitivity to noise in dependent points (visible in Figure 4b) limits its applicability in low-texture or wide-baseline scenarios.
