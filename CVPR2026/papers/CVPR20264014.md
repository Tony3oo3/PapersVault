## Problem

Articulated tractor-trailer systems have a time-varying 6-DoF transform between tractor-mounted and trailer-mounted cameras due to the fifth-wheel joint, suspension flex, and load shifts, which breaks the fixed-baseline assumption underlying most multi-view perception and calibration pipelines. Existing geometry-based methods (COLMAP, VGGT, DUSt3R) fail under rapid articulation, weak parallax, and texture-poor highway scenes, while static calibration becomes inaccurate within milliseconds of a maneuver. No public benchmark with dynamic inter-rig geometry existed to evaluate these challenges systematically.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six synchronized 1600×900 RGB images (110° FOV; three on the tractor cabin, three on the trailer rear) plus the ego incremental motion Δp_t = (Δx, Δy, Δψ) between consecutive frames. It outputs the 6-DoF pose of one trailer rear camera as a quaternion and translation vector, which is then propagated to the other two trailer cameras via known intra-rig extrinsics. For downstream detection, these predicted extrinsics replace static calibration in BEVFormer to produce 3D bounding boxes in the nuScenes evaluation protocol. All experiments run on the CARLA-based STT4AT benchmark (87 scenes, 8 towns, 4,533 annotated frames at 10 Hz).

## Method

dCAP uses a frozen VGGT backbone to encode the six views into camera-specific tokens, then a lightweight trainable decoder with three key components: (1) a Camera Cross-Attention (CCA) module where a learnable rear-camera query attends to all six encoder tokens to aggregate spatial cues for the trailer region; (2) a Camera Temporal Self-Attention (CTA) module that linearly projects the ego incremental motion into feature space to align the previous frame's rear-camera token before temporal self-attention, stabilizing translation under rapid articulation; and (3) an AdaLN-modulated refinement stack (L=3) where pose-dependent shift/scale/gate parameters adapt intermediate features across iterative steps before a lightweight MLP head regresses the 6-DoF pose in quaternion form. Training is supervised with an L1 translation + L1 rotation loss (equal weight), 24 epochs, Adam at 1e-4, batch size 4 on a single RTX A6000, with only the decoder optimized. The key design insight is exploiting the rigid-within-rig structure—only the inter-rig transform changes—so that cross-view spatial attention constrains orientation while pose-aware temporal fusion smooths translation, yielding complementary gains that neither module achieves alone.

## Results

On the STT4AT benchmark, dCAP (with both CCA and CTA) achieves a translation error of 0.452 m and a Relative Rotation Accuracy (RRA) of 0.042 rad for trailer rear-camera pose estimation, substantially outperforming static calibration (ΔT=1.284 m, RRA=0.148) and geometry-based methods (VGGT: ΔT=6.040 m; DUSt3R: ΔT=8.625 m). For downstream 3D object detection with BEVFormer, dCAP attains AP=0.103 and NDS=0.036, compared to 0.058/0.033 for static calibration and 0.033/0.031 for VGGT, approaching the ground-truth upper bound of AP=0.129. COLMAP fails entirely due to the absence of a valid static initialization pair under articulated motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

dCAP decisively outperforms all three geometry-based baselines on trailer pose estimation. VGGT [30] produces a translation error of 6.040 m and RRA of 0.309 rad, while DUSt3R [31] is even worse at 8.625 m and 0.578 rad, both roughly 13–19× worse than dCAP's 0.452 m / 0.042 rad. COLMAP [23] fails entirely, unable to establish a valid reconstruction from the articulated multi-view sequence. Against the GNSS-IMU Kalman Filter (ΔT=1.379 m, RRA=0.129), dCAP reduces translation error by ~67% and rotation error by ~67%. In the downstream 3D detection task, dCAP (AP=0.103) more than doubles the AP of VGGT (0.033) and DUSt3R (0.034), and nearly doubles static calibration (0.058), closing the gap to the ground-truth upper bound (0.129) to within 20%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the VGGT backbone as a frozen multi-view encoder and wraps it with a lightweight transformer decoder (cross-attention + temporal self-attention + AdaLN modulation) to regress the time-varying inter-rig pose between tractor and trailer cameras. It departs from static calibration methods (UniCal, CaLiV) that assume fixed inter-sensor geometry, and from geometry-first approaches (COLMAP, DUSt3R) that fail under articulation and weak parallax, by explicitly modeling the fifth-wheel joint as a 6-DoF dynamic transform between two rigid sub-rigs. The articulated perception component repurposes BEVFormer by swapping its static extrinsics for dCAP's predicted ones, rather than redesigning the detector for articulated geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STT4AT is the first public benchmark with time-varying inter-rig geometry for semi-trailer trucks, filling a gap left by MAN TruckScenes (rigid-body assumption) and proprietary truck datasets. However, all evaluation is CARLA-simulated, absolute detection scores remain low (AP 0.103 vs. 0.129 GT upper bound), and BEVFormer was never designed for articulated rigs, so the practical transfer to real-world trucking is unproven. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real sensor noise, rolling-shutter effects, and the operational reality of mismatched tractor-trailer pairs, and whether the low AP reflects a fundamental limitation of BEV-based detection under articulation or simply insufficient training data.
