## Problem

Flow-based Transformer models like FLUX achieve state-of-the-art image generation quality but suffer from high inference latency due to their large parameter sizes, as all sampling stages use the full model at full resolution. Current efficiency methods (token reduction, linear attention, VAE compression) trade off quality, and existing multi-resolution approaches (Pyramid Flow, MatryoshkaDM) do not address parameter redundancy in DiT architectures. The paper identifies that early generation stages only need coarse layout and concept contours, yet no prior work jointly segments the temporal flow and rationally allocates model capacity across resolution stages.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multilingual text prompt (encoded via mT5 and mCLIP) and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image in latent space. Generation proceeds through three progressive resolution stages (256→512→1024), each using 10 Flow-Euler-discretization steps (30 total), with upsampling and BridgeFlow transformations at stage boundaries. A 0.1 probability of prompt dropping during training enables classifier-free guidance at inference.

## Method

NAMI decomposes the rectified flow into K=3 time windows aligned with resolution stages, where each stage k uses a progressively larger subset of MM-DiT blocks (9:7:6 layer ratio for the 2B model) to match the increasing detail requirements. A BridgeFlow module—a learnable linear transformation W·Up(x)+B—replaces the non-parametric renoising of Pyramid Flow to align probability distributions at stage boundaries in a data-driven manner, pretrained with MSE loss. Training employs a multi-resolution joint strategy where all stages are optimized simultaneously with dynamically weighted losses, sampling data at resolutions ≥ the current stage's resolution to enable knowledge sharing and prevent catastrophic forgetting. This architecture reduces 1024×1024 inference time by 64% (53% from flow piecewise, 11% from model partitioning) while maintaining competitive generation quality on GenEval, DPG-Benchmark, and the newly proposed NAMI-1K human-preference benchmark.

## Results

NAMI-2B achieves an overall human preference score of 70.69 on the NAMI-1K benchmark, outperforming all comparable-size models (SD3-medium: 69.97, Infinity: 69.77, Hunyuan-DiT: 68.95, SANA: 67.80) while trailing the much larger FLUX-dev (12B) at 85.05. On GenEval, NAMI-2B scores 0.65 overall, competitive with Sana (0.66) and SD3-medium (0.62). On DPG-Benchmark, NAMI-2B achieves 83.8 overall, close to Sana (84.8) and SD3-medium (84.1). Critically, NAMI-2B reduces inference time for 1024×1024 images from 8.47s to 2.98s (64.82% reduction) while maintaining these quality levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-scale diffusion models, NAMI-2B consistently wins on the NAMI-1K human evaluation: it beats SD3-medium (70.69 vs 69.97), Infinity (70.69 vs 69.77), Hunyuan-DiT (70.69 vs 68.95), and SANA (70.69 vs 67.80), with the largest margin over SANA (+2.89). On GenEval, NAMI-2B (0.65) is competitive with Sana (0.66) and SD3-medium (0.62) but trails Sana by a narrow 0.01; it clearly outperforms LUMINA-Next (0.46) and SDXL (0.55). On DPG-Benchmark, NAMI-2B (83.8) is slightly behind Sana (84.8) and SD3-medium (84.1) but substantially ahead of Hunyuan-DiT (78.9), LUMINA-Next (74.6), and SDXL (74.7). Against the 12B FLUX-dev, NAMI-2B loses on all three benchmarks (70.69 vs 85.05 on NAMI-1K; 0.65 vs 0.67 on GenEval; 83.8 vs 84.0 on DPG), but achieves this at 64.82% lower inference cost. The BridgeFlow module outperforms Pyramid Flow's renoising approach (FID 8.93 vs 9.82, CLIP 25.57 vs 24.21) while being 2.4× faster at the jump point (0.05s vs 0.12s).

## Relation to Prior Work

NAMI sits at the intersection of rectified flow training (Liu et al., 2022; Esser et al., 2024), the MM-DiT architecture from FLUX (Black Forest Labs, 2024), and multi-resolution progressive generation strategies (Pyramid Flow Matching, Jin et al., 2024; MatryoshkaDM, Gu et al., 2023). Its key departure is the joint decomposition along both temporal (piecewise flow across resolution stages, following PerFlow, Yan et al., 2024) and architectural (progressively adding transformer layers) dimensions, connected by a learnable BridgeFlow module that replaces the non-parametric rescaling-and-renoising transitions used in Pyramid Flow. The multi-resolution joint training strategy also departs from the conventional sequential low-to-high fine-tuning paradigm, enabling simultaneous optimization across resolutions to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 64% inference time reduction at 1024×1024 resolution is practically significant for deployment of flow-based T2I models, and the approach is explicitly orthogonal to other efficiency techniques (linear attention, token reduction), enabling compositional gains. The NAMI-1K benchmark addresses a real gap in evaluation by combining open benchmarks, AI-generated, and human-authored prompts to mitigate the distribution biases and limited prompt diversity of GenEval and DPG-Benchmark. However, NAMI-2B still trails the 12B FLUX-dev in absolute quality, and the paper does not explore whether the progressive architecture extends to video generation or scales beyond three stages, leaving open questions about the generality of the temporal-spatial decomposition strategy.
