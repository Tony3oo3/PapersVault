## Problem

Current compositional video generation approaches focus on binding instance semantics but neglect the diversity of motion categories specified in prompts, producing overly similar motions even for markedly different user descriptions. Two specific gaps drive this: (1) motion semantics in natural language are ambiguous, making direct bounding-box inference unreliable, and (2) uniform diffusion guidance conflates distinct motion types (static, rigid, non-rigid) into a single undifferentiated signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a multi-instance scene and a frozen pre-trained video diffusion model (VideoCrafter-v2.0 with 3D U-Net or CogVideoX-2B with DiT architecture). An LLM (LLaMA-3.3-70B) parses the prompt into a structured motion graph at inference time; no model weights are updated. The output is a compositional video in which each instance is assigned exactly one of three motion categories (motionless, rigid, non-rigid), with per-instance bounding box sequences and motion-specific attention masks guiding the denoising process.

## Method

The framework decomposes scene dynamics into three canonical motion categories and applies two training-free modules. First, Structured Motion Reasoning (SMR) converts the ambiguous prompt into a motion graph (nodes = instances with motion labels, directed edges = spatial/dynamic relationships), then reasons per-instance bounding box sequences: fixed boxes for static instances, velocity/acceleration updates for rigid motion, and boundarywise displacement vectors for non-rigid deformation. Second, Disentangled Motion Guidance (DMG) injects three specialized masks into the diffusion attention maps during sampling: a reference-frame anchor for static regions, a k-means-derived shape template warped per-frame for rigid bodies, and a pixel-wise deformation penalty (comparing perceptual correspondences in diffusion features against box-induced bilinear interpolation) for non-rigid instances. The guidance is architecture-agnostic, applied via gradient-based latent updates for 3D U-Net models and direct score modification for DiT models.

## Results

On CVGBench-m with VideoCrafter-v2.0 (3D U-Net), the method achieves 98.40% Subject Consistency, 98.11% Background Consistency, 97.39% Temporal Flickering, 98.63% Motion Smoothness, and 82.21% Dynamic Degree, substantially outperforming the best compositional baseline Vico (40.00% Dynamic Degree). On CogVideoX-2B (DiT), the method reaches 98.27% Subject Consistency and 96.00% Dynamic Degree on CVGBench-m, improving over R&P by +7.27% and +4.98% respectively. The largest relative gains appear in Dynamic Degree, where the method nearly doubles the score of the strongest compositional baseline on the 3D U-Net architecture (82.21% vs. 40.00%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 baselines across every metric on both benchmarks. Against traditional T2V models (LVDM, modelScope, ZeroScope, LATTE, Show-1, LaVie, VideoCrafter-v1.0), the gains are most dramatic in Dynamic Degree: e.g., ZeroScope scores only 30.77% on CVGBench-m while the method reaches 82.21%. Among compositional baselines on VideoCrafter-v2.0, Vico is the strongest (40.00% Dynamic Degree), yet the method more than doubles this to 82.21% while also improving Subject Consistency from 97.72% to 98.40%. On CogVideoX-2B, R&P achieves 91.02% Dynamic Degree and 91.00% Subject Consistency on CVGBench-m; the method improves these to 96.00% and 98.27% respectively, with a particularly large Subject Consistency gain (+7.27%) attributed to cross-frame consistency enforcement that R&P lacks. The method also surpasses the raw baselines (VideoCrafter-v2.0, CogVideoX-2B) on all five metrics, confirming that the motion factorization framework adds value beyond the underlying diffusion model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional video generation (CVG) line of work that uses LLMs to plan bounding-box trajectories (LVD, VideoDirectorGPT, FlowZero) and attention-guided synthesis (BoxDiff, A&R, R&P). It departs from prior motion guidance approaches (TrailBlazer, FreeTraj, motion_prompting) that apply a single uniform guidance signal across all instances, instead factorizing scene dynamics into three canonical categories—motionlessness, rigid, and non-rigid motion—and assigning each instance a category-specific guidance branch. The structured motion graph as an intermediate LLM reasoning representation is a novel bridge between ambiguous natural-language prompts and unambiguous per-instance motion representations, building on the LLM-as-planner paradigm but adding explicit pairwise interaction edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic design (validated on both 3D U-Net and DiT backbones) makes the motion factorization framework immediately deployable on existing video diffusion models without retraining, which is practically valuable for the CVG community. The newly introduced CVGBench-m and CVGBench-p benchmarks, derived from real-world video descriptions with four compositional linguistic modes, fill a gap in evaluation for diverse motion scenarios. Key limitations include the assumption of stable camera viewpoints (no global scene motion) and failure on small objects where bounding boxes provide insufficient spatial coverage for guidance. Open questions include whether the three-category factorization is sufficient for articulated or multi-body interactions, and how the approach extends to camera-controlled generation.
