## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) exhibit significant performance disparities across demographic groups (age, sex, race) in clinical tasks like radiology report generation, yet fairness mitigation in MLLMs remains largely underexplored. Standard debiasing techniques—frequency-based resampling/reweighting and adversarial classifiers—either treat disparities as mere count imbalances or cause catastrophic forgetting of clinical knowledge, and word-level fairness probes are inapplicable because radiology reports rarely contain explicit demographic tokens. The core challenge is that medical images encode latent demographic signals that models exploit as shortcuts, requiring representation-level intervention rather than surface-level text corrections.

## Input / Output

The method consumes medical images (grayscale chest X-rays at standard radiology resolution or RGB dermoscopy images) paired with a fixed text instruction, and generates free-text clinical narratives—structured radiology reports (Indication/Findings/Impression sections) or diagnostic descriptions. Demographic metadata (age group, race, gender) is provided per sample solely to compute the mutual-information regularizer during training; it is never part of the inference-time input. Evaluation uses both surface-text metrics (BLEU-1/4, ROUGE-L) and clinically grounded metrics (RadGraph-F1, CheXpert-F1, GREEN) computed over the full generated text rather than a single label.

## Method

FairLLaVA is a two-stage, parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy built on LLaVA (BioMedCLIP image encoder + Vicuna-7B LM). Stage 1 aligns the frozen image encoder with the LM by training only the multimodal projector under standard cross-entropy loss. Stage 2 introduces a mutual-information regularizer: a lightweight variational demographic attribute classifier (DAC, a small MLP of ~57K parameters) is first trained with cross-entropy to predict each demographic attribute from pooled hidden states of tapped LM layers, then frozen; the model's LoRA adapters and projector are updated to minimize the CLUB-style contrastive log-ratio upper bound on MI between those hidden states and the demographic labels, while simultaneously optimizing the language-model loss. This forces the representations to discard demographic shortcuts, promoting diagnostic-invariant feature learning. The approach is architecture-agnostic, requires no pretrained discriminator, no preference data curation, and adds only modest computational overhead (~57K extra parameters) on top of standard LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On MIMIC-CXR, FairLLaVA-All (jointly debiasing Race, Age, and Gender) achieves 7 of 12 best equity-scaled (ES) scores across BLEU-1, BLEU-4, RadGraph-F1, and GREEN, with headline values of ES-BLEU-1 = 13.36 (Race), 21.89 (Age), and 24.89 (Gender), and ES-RadGraph-F1 = 6.34 (Race), 4.06 (Age), and 19.40 (Gender). On PadChest, FairLLaVA-All attains the best overall BLEU-1 (25.11), BLEU-4 (12.03), RadGraph-F1 (15.66), and GREEN (40.03) while also topping 5 of 8 ES cells. On HAM10000, FairLLaVA-All leads 5 of 6 ES metrics, including ES-Acc = 19.56 (Gender) and ES-BLEU4 = 12.17 (Age). On the CheXpert-F1 comparison with Chen et al. [8], FairLLaVA scores 69.21 (Race), 68.70 (Age), and 69.38 (Gender) versus 24.06, 23.85, and 24.13 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairLLaVA-All outperforms all general-purpose MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen2.5-7B, DeepSeek-VL2) and medical MLLMs (LLaVA-Rad, MedGemma-4B/27B, CheXagent) on the majority of ES metrics; for example, it beats LLaVA-Rad by 7.07 on Race ES-BLEU-1 (13.36 vs 5.29) and by 13.61 on Age ES-BLEU-1 (21.89 vs 8.28). Against the only prior MLLM-specific fairness method (Chen et al. [8]), FairLLaVA achieves a ~3× margin on equity-scaled CheXpert-F1 (69.21 vs 24.06 for Race; 69.38 vs 24.13 for Gender). Classical reweighting and resampling baselines are inconsistent: Resampling-All edges out FairLLaVA on Gender ES-BLEU-1 (30.38 vs 24.89) and Gender ES-GREEN (16.11 vs 12.27), but FairLLaVA dominates on Race and Age ES metrics and on RadGraph-F1 across all three attributes. The adversarial MLP classifier (DEAR-style) underperforms FairLLaVA on nearly every ES cell, confirming the paper's observation that it triggers catastrophic forgetting of clinical knowledge. On PadChest and HAM10000, FairLLaVA-All similarly leads or ties on most ES cells while also achieving the highest overall (non-ES) scores, demonstrating that fairness gains do not come at the cost of utility.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairLLaVA extends the LLaVA visual instruction tuning architecture by inserting a mutual-information regularizer (based on the CLUB contrastive log-ratio estimator) between the LM's hidden states and demographic attributes, replacing the need for adversarial discriminators or data-level resampling. It departs from the dominant LLM-fairness literature, which operates at the word/lexical level (polarizing-term probes, content-conditional embeddings), by targeting performance parity in generative outputs where demographic labels are absent from the text. The method also extends equity-scaled metrics from discriminative classification to open-ended text generation, filling a gap between fairness evaluation in vision-only models and the multi-modal generative setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fairness in MLLM-based medical report generation is a largely unaddressed risk: the paper documents that even the majority demographic group (White patients on MIMIC-CXR) can be under-served, and that standard word-level fairness probes are inapplicable when reports contain no explicit demographic tokens. The plug-and-play, LoRA-compatible design (~57K extra parameters) makes the approach practical for clinical deployment, but the method still requires reliable demographic labels at training and evaluation time, which many real-world clinical datasets lack. Open questions include whether the MI-based debiasing generalizes to individual-level (cross-sectional) fairness, to settings where demographic metadata is entirely unavailable, and to non-medical MLLM applications where the shortcut structure may differ.
