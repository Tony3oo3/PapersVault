## Problem

Proprietary systems like GPT-Image and NanoBanana have begun to answer visual questions with generated images (Visual Question-Visual Answering), but open-source models consistently fail at this task because they are trained on standard image-editing datasets that emphasize predefined pixel-level operations rather than free-form generation requiring world knowledge and multi-step reasoning. The primary bottleneck is the absence of large-scale, knowledge-centric training data where the answer image is a semantically related but visually distinct counterpart to the question image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260730/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a question image (a real photograph or illustration) and a free-form text question, and must generate an entirely new answer image that is semantically connected to the question image through knowledge or reasoning (e.g., a broken window → shards of glass on the ground), rather than performing a local edit. During training, the data format is a quadruplet <Question Image, Question Text, Reasoning Trace, Answer Image>, where the reasoning trace is a chain-of-thought explanation of the transformation. Evaluation uses GPT-4o as an automatic judge scoring generated images on a 0–1–2 scale against a human-curated reference answer image.

## Method

The core contribution is VQ-VA World, a data-centric framework built around a five-module agentic pipeline (Retriever, Instruction Generator, Filter, Rewriter, Reasoner) that mines web-interleaved documents to construct ~1.8M high-quality VQ-VA training samples spanning world knowledge, design knowledge, and reasoning. Each module is powered by advanced VLMs (GPT-4o, Seed1.5VL-Thinking) with carefully designed system prompts and chain-of-thought reasoning, operating independently without memory sharing; the Filter uses a multi-score strategy (Question Score + Answer Score + Context Dependence Score, all must be 2/2) to remove low-quality triplets. For training, LightFusion (Qwen2.5-VL-7B understanding branch + Wan2.2-TI2V-5B generation branch with double fusion) is fine-tuned in two stages: 30k steps of continued pretraining on the full 1.8M dataset mixed at 25% sampling ratio, followed by 15k steps of SFT on a stricter ~500k high-quality subset plus ~100k temporally related samples generated via the Seedance video model. The pipeline also introduces IntelligentBench, a 360-example human-curated benchmark with expert cross-review, to systematically evaluate VQ-VA along world knowledge, design knowledge, and reasoning dimensions.

## Results

On IntelligentBench (the paper's primary VQ-VA benchmark), LightFusion-World achieves 53.06 overall, a 6.8× improvement over the vanilla LightFusion baseline (7.78) and a 27× improvement over UniWorld-V1 (1.94). The model also posts strong gains on reasoning-centric editing: 15.3 on RISEBench (vs. 4.2 for LightFusion) and 61.85 on KRIS-Bench (vs. 52.52). On standard image editing (GEdit-Bench-EN / ImgEdit-Bench), LightFusion-World scores 6.58 / 3.85, a modest improvement over LightFusion's 6.06 / 3.77, confirming that VQ-VA training primarily benefits knowledge-driven tasks rather than routine pixel-level edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightFusion-World (53.06) decisively outperforms all fully open-source baselines on IntelligentBench: Qwen-Image (34.31), FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev (21.11), Step1X-Edit (12.36), OmniGen2 (10.69), LightFusion (7.78), and UniWorld-V1 (1.94). It also surpasses Qwen-Image and FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev on RISEBench (15.3 vs. 8.9 and 5.8) and KRIS-Bench (61.85 vs. not reported), and beats BAGELThink on RISEBench (15.3 vs. 11.9) and KRIS-Bench (61.85 vs. 53.36). However, it trails BAGELThink on IntelligentBench (53.06 vs. 60.42) and remains well behind the proprietary leaders NanoBanana (81.67) and GPT-Image-1 (82.64) on the same benchmark. On standard editing (GEdit-Bench-EN), LightFusion-World (6.58) is competitive with open-weight models like BAGEL (6.52) and Ovis-U1 (6.42) but still below UniPic 2.0 (7.10) and Step1X-Edit (6.70), indicating that VQ-VA training does not substantially alter routine editing performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-editing paradigm (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix, SEED-Data-Edit, UltraEdit, AnyEdit) where the target image is a direct pixel-level modification of the source, and instead defines VQ-VA as a distinct task requiring world knowledge and multi-step reasoning to synthesize an entirely new image. It builds on LightFusion (LightBagel) as the base unified multimodal model, but the core contribution is a data-centric agentic pipeline that mines web-interleaved documents—borrowing the data-source philosophy from LLM pretraining—to construct knowledge-rich training triples. The benchmark (IntelligentBench) is positioned as complementary to RISEBench and KRIS-Bench, which still primarily reward pixel-level edits, whereas IntelligentBench deliberately probes high-level semantic reasoning beyond what is visible in the source image.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing a 1.8M-sample dataset, a human-curated benchmark, and full model weights, this work provides the open-source community with the first systematic resource for VQ-VA, a capability previously exclusive to proprietary systems like GPT-Image and NanoBanana. The remaining gap (53.06 vs. ~82 for proprietary leaders) suggests that data alone may not fully close the divide, raising open questions about whether architectural changes, larger-scale reasoning traces, or additional training signals are needed. The finding that VQ-VA training also yields consistent gains on standard editing benchmarks hints at a broader transfer benefit, but this is demonstrated on a single base model (LightFusion) and would benefit from validation across architectures.
