## Problem

Existing Ego-Exo adaptation methods for action anticipation require access to target-view data (labeled or unlabeled) during training, creating a bottleneck in computational and data collection costs. This paper makes the first exploration of Test-time Ego-Exo Adaptation for Action Anticipation (TE²A³), where a source-view-trained model must adapt online during test time to anticipate target-view actions without any re-training. The task is particularly challenging due to multiple simultaneous action candidates per event and a significant temporal-spatial inter-view gap between Ego and Exo perspectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder paired with a TA3N anticipation network (trained on source-view data) and processes a stream of unlabeled target-view observation clips, each consisting of 5 uniformly sampled frames from a 2-second window with a 1-second interval before the action onset. At test time, the model also consumes a frozen GRU-based narrator that generates textual descriptions from the extracted frame features, and an on-the-shelf CLIP model for cross-modal logit inference. The output is a set of Top-5 noun class predictions and Top-5 verb class predictions for the upcoming actions, evaluated via class-mean Top-5 recall under Exo2Ego and Ego2Exo settings.

## Method

DCPGN comprises two modules operating during online test-time adaptation. The Multi-Label Prototype Growing Module (ML-PGM) assigns Top-K pseudo labels to each video-level representation, stores them in per-class memory banks updated via an entropy priority queue (retaining the N lowest-entropy samples), and computes class prototypes through confidence-based reweighting to mitigate interference from incorrectly assigned negative classes. The Dual-Clue Consistency Module (DCCM) extracts a visual clue (final observation frame) and a textual clue (narrator-generated description of action progression), encodes both via CLIP along with learnable prompts prepended to action class embeddings, and constrains the resulting visual and textual logits through a symmetric KL divergence loss to explicitly bridge the Ego-Exo gap in spatial and temporal dimensions. The final anticipation logits are a weighted sum of the prototype logits, visual clue logits, and textual clue logits, with learnable prompts optimized online via SGD at a small learning rate.

## Results

On the EgoMe-anti benchmark, DCPGN achieves 79.03% noun and 43.84% verb Top-5 recall under Exo2Ego, and 72.01% / 40.10% under Ego2Exo. On EgoExoLearn, it reaches 46.26% / 42.98% (Exo2Ego) and 48.48% / 46.51% (Ego2Exo). The evaluation metric is class-mean Top-5 recall for both noun and verb anticipation. DCPGN outperforms the strongest baseline (ML-TTA) by up to 9.91% in noun recall on EgoExoLearn Exo2Ego.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCPGN outperforms all seven baselines across every setting and metric. The closest competitor is ML-TTA [70], which uses bound entropy minimization for multi-label image-level TTA; DCPGN surpasses it by 1.92% / 6.92% (noun/verb) on EgoMe-anti Exo2Ego and by 9.91% / 5.31% on EgoExoLearn Exo2Ego, with similar margins on Ego2Exo (5.52% / 6.08%). Among single-label TTA methods, TCA [69] is the strongest on EgoMe-anti Exo2Ego noun (77.23%), but DCPGN still leads by 1.80% in noun and 7.51% in verb. ZERO [11] and TDA [26] trail DCPGN by 3.84–4.92% in noun and 7.18–7.81% in verb on EgoMe-anti. The older baselines Tent [63], TPT [57], and VITTA [34] are consistently 3–13 points behind DCPGN, confirming that entropy-minimization or prompt-tuning strategies alone are insufficient for the multi-action, cross-view anticipation setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of Ego-Exo cross-view adaptation and Test-Time Adaptation (TTA). Prior Ego-Exo methods (Exo2EgoDVC, AE2, Sync, GCEAN) all require target-view data during training—either labeled for fine-tuning or unlabeled for unsupervised domain adaptation—whereas this work eliminates that requirement entirely by adapting purely at test time. On the TTA side, existing methods (Tent, TPT, TDA, ZERO, ML-TTA) assume single-label image classification or simple video classification; this paper repurposes prototype-growing and prompt-tuning ideas for the fundamentally different setting of multi-label, temporally asynchronous Ego-Exo video anticipation, adding a dual-modality (visual + textual) consistency mechanism absent from prior TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TE²A³ formulation is practically important for embodied AI and human-robot cooperation, where a model trained on one camera perspective must adapt online to a new perspective without any retraining data—a realistic deployment constraint. The newly constructed EgoMe-anti benchmark provides a needed evaluation resource for paired Ego-Exo anticipation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond two views, whether the frozen-CLIP assumption limits ceiling performance, and whether the lightweight narrator can be replaced by more scalable temporal models. The reliance on a pre-trained CLIP and a separately trained narrator also raises questions about robustness in truly open-world scenarios where action vocabularies shift.
