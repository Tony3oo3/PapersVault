## Problem

Existing concept customization methods (DreamBooth, LoRA, etc.) disrupt the original model's behavior by altering unrelated image elements (background, lighting, style) when inserting a new concept, and degrade the model's prompt-adherence and image-quality capabilities due to distribution drift on scarce reference data. No prior work has explicitly addressed or studied these two failure modes in personalized T2I generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261702/cropped_page0_idx9.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–5 reference images of a target concept and a text prompt containing a [V] identifier token (e.g., 'A [V] dog in a large steaming pot'), where the prompt is decomposed into a Base text (without [V]) and a Target text (the [V] token plus its noun). It produces a single generated image at the resolution supported by the SD 3.5-M flow backbone. During training, the prompt is consumed in three forms simultaneously: the complete text (for the standard CC loss), the base text (for the original conditional prediction branch), and the target text with layer-wise tunable embeddings (for the frozen extractor's implicit guidance).

## Method

PureCC introduces a decoupled learning objective v_t^PureCC = v_t^original + λ·v_t^target, directly inspired by the implicit-guidance decomposition in Classifier-Free Guidance, so that the target concept is added as a perturbation on top of the original model's prediction rather than replacing it. A dual-branch pipeline realizes this: a frozen representation extractor (a flow model fine-tuned with LoRA plus layer-wise tunable concept embeddings that replace the [V] embedding at every transformer layer) provides the target-concept guidance R(y_tar) = v_t^θ1(x_t|y_tar) − v_t^θ1(x_t|∅), while a trainable flow model initialized from a separate pretrained copy produces the original conditional prediction v_t^θ2(x_t|y_base). An adaptive scale λ* is computed in closed form as the projection coefficient of the trainable model's learned concept representation onto the frozen extractor's guidance representation, dynamically reducing guidance when the trainable model has not yet aligned with the target direction and increasing it once alignment is achieved. The overall training loss combines the standard flow-matching CC loss with the PureCC decoupled loss, enabling pure additive concept insertion without contaminating the original generative prior.

## Results

On DreamBenchPCC, PureCC achieves near-zero degradation in all preservation metrics: ΔCLIP-T(base) = −0.31 (instance) and −0.26 (style), ΔHPSv2.1 = +0.10 (instance, actually improving quality), and Seg-Cons = 69.37, compared to DreamBooth's −4.81 and 18.38 respectively. For concept responsiveness, PureCC attains CLIP-I(target) = 0.81 and DINO = 0.73 on instance concepts, surpassing the best tuning-based baseline CIFC (0.78 / 0.65) and tuning-free DreamO (0.71 / 0.67). On style concepts, PureCC scores CSD = 0.63, competitive with CIFC (0.64) and DreamO (0.65), while maintaining far superior preservation (ΔCLIP-T = −0.26 vs. −4.70 for CIFC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PureCC dominates all tuning-based baselines on preservation metrics by a wide margin: Seg-Cons of 69.37 vs. 13–26 for DreamBooth, Mix-of-Show, and CIFC, and ΔCLIP-T(base) of −0.31 vs. −1.93 to −4.81. On instance concept fidelity, PureCC (CLIP-I = 0.81, DINO = 0.73) outperforms the strongest tuning-based baseline CIFC (0.78, 0.65) and tuning-free DreamO (0.71, 0.67). For style concepts, PureCC (CSD = 0.63) is marginally below DreamO (0.65) and CIFC (0.64), but achieves these scores with dramatically less degradation in prompt adherence (ΔCLIP-T = −0.26 vs. −4.70 for CIFC). DreamBooth+EWC, which adds EWC regularization, still degrades substantially (ΔCLIP-T = −4.17, Seg-Cons = 26.37), confirming that standard regularization is insufficient. UNO, a tuning-free in-context method, shows the weakest style responsiveness (CSD = 0.34) and moderate instance fidelity, highlighting the trade-off of avoiding fine-tuning entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

PureCC builds directly on the concept customization lineage (DreamBooth, LoRA, Mix-of-Show, CIFC) that fine-tunes pre-trained T2I models on a few reference images, but departs fundamentally in its learning formulation. Rather than treating the entire custom language-vision set as the training signal, it reformulates customization as adding an implicit concept guidance term to the original conditional prediction—an idea borrowed from Classifier-Free Guidance but transplanted into the training objective. The dual-branch architecture (a frozen LoRA-tuned representation extractor providing purified concept representations, plus a trainable flow model producing the original prediction) has no direct precedent in prior customization work, and the adaptive scale λ* derived from cross-branch representation alignment extends the static guidance-scale concept of CFG into a dynamic, self-calibrating mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies two failure modes—disruption of the original model's behavior (unrelated image elements changing) and degradation of prompt-adherence/quality capabilities—that prior customization work largely ignored, providing a useful diagnostic lens for the field. The large preservation margins (e.g., Seg-Cons 69.37 vs. 26.37 for the next best method) suggest that the decoupled objective is not merely a marginal improvement but a qualitatively different training regime. Open questions include whether the two-stage dual-branch pipeline generalizes beyond SD 3.5-M to other architectures (e.g., DiT variants, video diffusion models), whether the frozen extractor can be replaced by a lighter-weight module to reduce training cost, and how the approach scales to dozens of simultaneous concepts without cross-interference.
