## Problem

Existing GUI benchmarks evaluate comprehensive grounding and navigation capabilities but largely overlook hallucination phenomena, where models confidently output incorrect or fabricated element coordinates. No prior work systematically categorizes GUI grounding hallucinations into distinct failure modes (confusion vs. fabricated) or investigates how parsing deficiencies propagate into grounding errors, leaving the root causes of unreliable GUI interaction unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263677/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes GUI screenshots (mobile, desktop, web) paired with a text functional description as input and expects either a bounding box [x1,y1,x2,y2] for valid grounding targets or an explicit rejection response when the queried element does not exist. The parsing subset requires the model to enumerate all visible icons and texts with their semantics and coordinates in a structured format, while the hallucination subset tests robustness under confusion (visually similar distractors) and fabrication (nonexistent elements) conditions. The benchmark is bilingual (1,000 English + 1,000 Chinese instances), with ~40% confusion and ~60% fabricated hallucination cases reflecting real-world prevalence.

## Method

The paper introduces GUI-HalluBench, a 2,000-instance bilingual benchmark built via a pipeline combining Grounding DINO detection, PaddleOCR text recognition, LMM-based semantic verification, and multi-annotator human review, with balanced sampling guided by redundancy and information density principles. Two complementary easing strategies are proposed: (1) Parsing-guided Prompt (PGP), a training-free approach that reformulates the grounding instruction into a single structured prompt enforcing an explicit parse-then-ground reasoning sequence within one inference round, and (2) Hallucination-aware Fine-Tuning (HFT), which applies LoRA fine-tuning (rank 8, α=32, 3 epochs) on 10K hallucination-annotated interfaces plus 20K parsing interfaces and public GUI datasets to improve robustness. Correlation analysis (SRCC) confirms that parsing precision and recall strongly predict hallucination robustness, while function similarity shows weaker correlation, indicating that detection and contextual reasoning—not semantic labeling—are the primary drivers of hallucination susceptibility.

## Results

On GUI-HalluBench, the best open-source baseline Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 58.1% average parsing accuracy and 66.0% average hallucination robustness, outperforming the best closed-source model Gemini-2.0 (Project Mariner) at 41.6% and 60.5% respectively. The proposed training-free PGP approach yields modest gains (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B hallucination avg improves from 66.0% to 67.7%), while the training-based HFT approach delivers up to 7% absolute improvement, with Qwen3-VL-8B(HFT) reaching 72.3% parsing and 73.0% hallucination. GPT-4o, despite strong general reasoning, exhibits extremely low element precision (4.3% EN), revealing limited grounding robustness under cluttered GUI conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 14 baselines (3 closed-source, 11 open-source) on GUI-HalluBench. Open-source models with domain-specific GUI training (Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B, GUI-Owl-7B) consistently outperform all closed-source models on both parsing and hallucination metrics; Qwen3-VL-8B leads with 66.0% avg hallucination vs. Gemini-2.0's 60.5%. GPT-4o, despite its general reasoning strength, shows the weakest element precision (4.3% EN, 3.4% CN), indicating that general multimodal capability does not transfer to structured GUI grounding. Among open-source baselines, GUI-specialized models like SeeClick-9.6B (30.3%) and CogAgent-9B (40.4%) underperform significantly, while general-purpose LMMs (Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B) achieve the best results. The PGP approach provides modest but consistent improvements across all tested models (e.g., +1.7% hallucination avg for Qwen3-VL-8B, +2.5% for Claude), while HFT delivers the largest gains, improving Qwen3-VL-8B by 7.0% on hallucination and 14.2% on parsing over its base version.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: GUI grounding benchmarks (ScreenSpot, ScreenSpot-Pro, MMBench-GUI-L2) that evaluate element localization, and hallucination benchmarks (POPE, HallusionBench, MMHal-Bench) that evaluate perception reliability in natural image settings. It departs from both by introducing hallucination-specific evaluation into the structured GUI domain, where prior benchmarks either ignore reliability or focus on natural-image hallucination. On the method side, the Parsing-guided Prompt (PGP) repurposes chain-of-thought-style prompting for GUI parsing-before-grounding, while Hallucination-aware Fine-Tuning (HFT) extends standard LoRA fine-tuning with automatically generated hallucination-labeled data, building on the data aggregation strategies seen in SeeClick, OS-Atlas, and UI-TARS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As GUI agents move toward production deployment (OpenAI Operator, Anthropic Computer Use, Gemini Project Mariner), the reliability of grounding under adversarial or ambiguous conditions becomes a safety-critical concern that comprehensive-competence benchmarks do not capture. The empirical finding that parsing deficiencies are the primary driver of both confusion and fabricated hallucinations provides a concrete diagnostic pathway: improving structural perception should reduce hallucination rates, which is validated by the PGP and HFT results. Key limitations include the absence of specialized high-stakes domains (medical, financial), the forward-generalization risk as UI paradigms evolve, and the moderate benchmark scale (2,000 instances). Open questions include whether the parsing-hallucination link persists in multi-step navigation tasks and whether hallucination-aware training transfers to unseen interface styles.
