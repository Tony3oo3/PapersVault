## Problem

Current vision-language models can visually ground target objects in cluttered scenes but lack the multi-step spatial reasoning needed to identify which obstructing objects must be removed and in what order before a robot can grasp the target. No prior benchmark or training recipe specifically targets this obstruction-reasoning capability, leaving a gap between object detection and actionable grasp planning in dense, physically interdependent scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261156/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB-D image (RGB used for reasoning and action planning; depth used downstream for 3D grasp-point estimation) and a free-form natural-language instruction (e.g., 'grasp the white iPhone box') that uniquely identifies the target among clutter. It produces a two-part structured output: a <think> block containing a step-by-step obstruction-path reasoning chain where each step is anchored to a physically contacting neighbor with coordinates and occlusion cues, followed by an <answer> block listing the minimal set of top-level (accessible) obstructors to remove first. Two evaluation settings exist: an Oracle mode with Set-of-Mark numeric IDs for pure reasoning assessment, and a Natural Language Prompting mode where the model must also perform spatial grounding from free-form language.

## Method

UNOGrasp (built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) formulates obstruction reasoning as a target-centric directed graph where nodes are objects relevant to the target's accessibility and edges encode 'obstructed → obstructing' relations, rather than a scene-level graph over all pairwise relations. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) supervised fine-tuning on UNOBench (100k+ human-annotated obstruction paths with contact points, occlusion ratios, and degree words) to learn visually-grounded reasoning chains that traverse valid obstruction paths; (2) reinforcement fine-tuning via GRPO with a composite reward combining a binary format reward (valid <think>/<answer> structure) and a set-level IoU task reward between predicted and ground-truth top-level obstructors, which provides a smoother optimization signal than binary correctness and indirectly improves internal reasoning-path fidelity. The key design choice is anchoring each reasoning step to physically adjacent (contacting) neighbors and quantifying obstruction severity as auxiliary signals, which incentivizes the model to reconstruct complete obstruction paths before identifying the accessible top-level set F(o_t) that constitutes the next-step action.

## Results

On the UNOBench synthetic test set (Oracle/SoM split), UNOGrasp achieves 83.3% SR-F1 on Easy, 69.1% on Medium, and 54.5% on Hard difficulty levels, with MP_NED of 0.11, 0.37, and 0.51 respectively. On the Natural Language Prompting split, it reaches 74.9%, 59.7%, and 37.2% SR-F1 across the same levels. In real-robot experiments on a UR5e platform (30 scenarios, 25 objects), UNOGrasp achieves a 50% average success rate, compared to 40% for Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 and 3% for Qwen2.5-VL (ICL), with the largest gap on Hard cases (40% vs 10% vs 0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNOGrasp is compared against two baselines: Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 (proprietary, in base and ICL variants) and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (open-source, in ICL and SFT variants). UNOGrasp outperforms Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 across nearly all difficulty levels in both Oracle and Natural Language Prompting settings; the gap is most dramatic on Hard cases, where UNOGrasp scores 54.5% SR-F1 vs. 5.4% (base) or 13.8% (ICL) for Gemini. Against Qwen2.5-VL (SFT), which shares the same backbone, UNOGrasp gains +13.5% SR-F1 on Easy and +20.2% on Hard (synthetic), confirming the value of the RFT stage with obstruction-aware rewards. On the real-robot evaluation, UNOGrasp matches Gemini on Easy (80%) and Medium (30%) but significantly outperforms it on Hard (40% vs. 10%), while Qwen2.5-VL (ICL) fails almost entirely (3% average). A notable weakness of Gemini is hallucination in No-Obstructions cases (68.7% SR in Oracle, 50.2% in NL), where it invents obstructions for fully accessible targets, whereas UNOGrasp reaches 94.8% and 92.5% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Qwen2.5-VL-3B as its base VLM and MetaGraspNetV2 as its data foundation, but departs from prior cluttered-grasping work by reframing obstruction as a VLM spatial-reasoning problem rather than a low-level control or affordance-prediction task. Unlike scene-level graph approaches (VMRN, REGRAD, Rabino & Tommasi) that model all pairwise relations, UNOGrasp constructs a compact target-centric obstruction graph focused solely on the accessibility of the requested object. The two-stage SFT + GRPO-based RFT training pipeline with obstruction-aware IoU rewards extends recent grounded-reinforcement-learning-for-VLMs work (Sarch et al., RoboRefer) to the specific domain of multi-step obstruction path inference, while the Set-of-Mark prompting and free-form language grounding follow established visual-prompting conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNOBench (100k+ annotated obstruction paths with language descriptions) is the first large-scale benchmark specifically targeting multi-step obstruction reasoning, filling a gap where prior datasets lacked both language annotations and reasoning-oriented evaluation. The results reveal that the dominant bottleneck for current VLMs is not obstruction reasoning per se but spatial grounding under free-form language: even Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 drops from ~60 F1 (Oracle/SoM) to <10 F1 (Natural Language Prompting) on object-level metrics, suggesting that linguistic-to-spatial alignment remains the critical unsolved sub-problem. Limitations include single-view evaluation, a synthetic-to-real gap (UNOGrasp trained only on synthetic data), and restriction to bin-picking-style scenes; future work on multi-view perception, larger object diversity, and integration with real-time control loops would be needed for deployment.
