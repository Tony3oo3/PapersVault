## Problem

Long video generation faces a fundamental tension: bidirectional models capture long-range temporal coherence but incur quadratic computational cost, while autoregressive models scale efficiently but accumulate prediction errors that cause appearance drift and motion inconsistency over extended horizons. The paper asks whether these two paradigms can be integrated to achieve both global temporal consistency and efficient inference without the error accumulation inherent in sequential frame-by-frame generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262003/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt, an optional conditioning image (for I2V), or trailing video frames (for continuation), all injected through a unified Multi-Mask mechanism that zero-pads condition frames to the target sequence length and concatenates their latents with a frame-wise mask along the channel dimension. Output is a 24 fps video at 480p resolution (16:9 aspect ratio), with the generation process internally producing intermediate 6 fps and 12 fps sequences before the final refinement. For long-form generation, the model extends beyond its native 500-frame window by using the last 5 seconds of each segment as conditioning for the next, producing videos exceeding 1500 frames.

## Method

TempoMaster reformulates video generation as next-frame-rate prediction: it first generates a sparse 6 fps blueprint capturing global temporal dynamics, then progressively refines to 12 fps and 24 fps, with each stage conditioning on all previously generated frames as anchors. The architecture is a Diffusion Transformer (built on Wan2.2's MoE design) trained with flow matching loss in two stages—single-frame-rate Multi-Mask conditioning, then multi-frame-rate generation with frame rate encoded via modified RoPE that scales temporal position index spacing by 2^i, augmented with random start indices for temporal extrapolation. At inference, a W-way tree structure enables parallel generation within each frame-rate level since sibling segments share a predetermined parent, reducing overall complexity from O(N²) to O(N²/4^K) when W≥2, with the default configuration using W=2 across three sequential stages at 6, 12, and 24 fps.

## Results

On VBench long-video evaluation (500 frames, 480p), TempoMaster achieves a total score of 80.30, outperforming the strongest baseline FramePack (79.52) by 0.78 points, with particular strength in imaging quality (70.20) and aesthetic quality (59.62). On short-video evaluation (121 frames), TempoMaster scores 80.76, edging out MMPL (80.55) and SkyReels-V2 (80.54). In a human study on 500-frame videos, TempoMaster achieves the highest overall score of 3.69/5, leading in semantic alignment (3.92) and content consistency (3.68) over all baselines including the short-video model LongCat (3.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TempoMaster (14B) outperforms all four long-video baselines on VBench total score for both long (500-frame) and short (121-frame) videos. Against MAGI-1 (24B), TempoMaster gains +1.80 on long-video total and +1.71 on short-video total, despite having fewer parameters. FramePack (13B) is the closest competitor on long videos (79.52 vs 80.30) but TempoMaster leads by +0.86 on short videos; notably, FramePack scores highest on motion smoothness (99.54) but lowest on dynamic degree (16.82), indicating overly static outputs. SkyReels-V2 (14B) and MMPL (14B) trail TempoMaster by 1.13 and 1.50 points respectively on long videos, with both showing significant performance drops from short to long video (e.g., SkyReels-V2: 80.54→79.17), a pattern the authors attribute to error accumulation in autoregressive generation. In the human study, TempoMaster (3.69) beats LongCat (3.58), a short-video model used as a strong aesthetic baseline, by +0.11 overall, with the largest margin in semantic alignment (3.92 vs 3.83) and content consistency (3.68 vs 3.34).

## Relation to Prior Work

TempoMaster builds on the Wan2.2 MoE DiT architecture as its base model and positions itself as a synthesis of two competing paradigms: bidirectional diffusion models (which capture long-range coherence but scale quadratically) and autoregressive models (which are sequential but accumulate errors). Its core departure is the 'next-frame-rate prediction' formulation, which replaces sequential frame-by-frame conditioning with hierarchical refinement across frame rates (e.g., 6→12→24 fps), distinguishing it from anchor-based methods like NUWA-XL that rely on fixed-scale sparse keyframes. The Multi-Mask conditioning mechanism is a new unified interface that handles T2V, I2V, FLF2V, and video continuation as special cases without extra parameters or context-length growth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical frame-rate prediction paradigm offers a principled way to decouple global temporal planning from local detail synthesis, directly addressing the error-accumulation problem that plagues autoregressive long-video generation. The parallel inference strategy with O(N²/4^K) complexity is a concrete efficiency win that makes minute-long video generation tractable without sacrificing quality, and the robustness across parallel configurations (Table 3) gives practitioners flexible compute-quality tradeoffs. However, the paper acknowledges it does not optimize Time-To-First-Frame latency, limiting applicability to real-time streaming, and the Vbench margins over strong baselines (e.g., 80.30 vs. 79.52) are modest, suggesting the gains are partly attributable to the base model's strength rather than the hierarchical formulation alone.
