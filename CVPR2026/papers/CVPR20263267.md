## Problem

Autoregressive transformer-based mesh generation methods are bottlenecked by prohibitively long token sequences and constrained global quantization resolution, limiting them to roughly 8K faces. Production-quality artist meshes in games and film routinely exceed 100K triangles, creating a substantial gap between what current generative models can produce and what real-world applications demand.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a 3D reference shape as a point cloud and a semantic segmentation from PartField to define patch boundaries; at training, random Voronoi segmentation replaces PartField for diversity. Each patch is independently scaled to [0,1] and quantized at 512³ resolution with 16,384 farthest-point-sampled points as conditioning, yielding a higher effective merged resolution than global quantization. The output is a single assembled artist triangle mesh of 100K+ faces, with patches glued via displacement correction to ensure seamless boundaries.

## Method

MeshMosaic decomposes the mesh into semantically meaningful patches ordered by BFS from the spatially lowest patch, then generates each patch autoregressively using a 0.5B hourglass transformer (fine-tuned from DeepMesh). For each patch, the 512 spatially nearest triangles from previously generated patches are tokenized and encoded by a GRU to form a boundary condition, which is concatenated to the front of the target patch's token sequence so self-attention enforces local continuity. A frozen Michelangelo encoder extracts both local (patch) and global (full-shape) point cloud features, concatenated with the GRU boundary embedding as the final conditioning input. Crucially, each patch is quantized independently at 512³ rather than applying a single global quantization, enabling finer geometric detail per patch. After generation, a displacement-correction step translates each new patch to align its boundary faces exactly with the already-assembled mesh, guaranteeing seamless splicing.

## Results

On ShapeNet, MeshMosaic achieves the best F1 (0.929), NC (0.973), and EF1 (0.211), tying with DeepMesh on HD (0.037) and matching BPT on CD_L1 (0.003). On Thingi10K and Objaverse, it wins on every metric, with particularly large margins on HD (0.051 vs. 0.157–0.233 for baselines on Thingi10K; 0.072 vs. 0.111–0.237 on Objaverse) and EF1 (0.271 and 0.348 respectively). In the user study with 27 professional raters, MeshMosaic scores 2.78–2.91 across all four criteria (Neatness, Artistry, Similarity to GT, Detail Recovery), roughly 2.5× higher than the second-best method (BPT at 0.93–1.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshMosaic outperforms all four baselines on nearly every metric across all three datasets. On ShapeNet, it ties BPT on CD_L1 (0.003) and DeepMesh on HD (0.037), but leads in F1 (0.929 vs. 0.875 for BPT, 0.791 for DeepMesh) and EF1 (0.211 vs. 0.159 for BPT, 0.177 for DeepMesh). On the more complex Thingi10K and Objaverse datasets, the advantage widens dramatically: HD drops to 0.051 (Thingi10K) and 0.072 (Objaverse) versus 0.157–0.233 and 0.111–0.237 for baselines, and EF1 reaches 0.271 and 0.348, roughly 1.5–2× the next-best. BPT is the strongest single baseline on ShapeNet (best HD at 0.017, best CD_L2 at 0.012) but degrades sharply on complex shapes, while TreeMeshGPT is consistently the weakest across all datasets. In the user study, MeshMosaic dominates every category by a wide margin (2.78–2.91 vs. 0.93–1.08 for BPT, the runner-up), confirming that the geometric gains translate into perceived artistry and detail quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshMosaic builds directly on the DeepMesh 0.5B autoregressive transformer (hourglass architecture, KV-caching, tokenization scheme) as its base model, but fundamentally departs from the single-pass, full-mesh token sequence paradigm shared by MeshGPT, MeshAnything, BPT, TreeMeshGPT, and DeepMesh. Instead of serializing an entire mesh into one long sequence, it introduces a local-to-global decomposition: the mesh is segmented into patches (via PartField at inference, random Voronoi partitioning at training), each generated autoregressively with boundary-conditioned context from previously assembled neighbors. The key technical departures are per-patch local quantization (512³ per patch rather than globally), a GRU-encoded boundary token sequence injected as prefix context, and dual local-to-global point cloud conditioning via a frozen Michelangelo encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: production-quality game and film assets routinely require 100K+ triangles, a regime where all prior autoregressive mesh generators (capped around 8K faces) simply fail. MeshMosaic's patch-based paradigm is a general recipe for scaling autoregressive generation of any structured 3D data, not just meshes. However, boundary conditioning remains purely local, leaving distant symmetric parts weakly coupled (e.g., asymmetric arms in Fig. 10), and the authors acknowledge that global perception mechanisms, multi-node synchronous generation, and adaptive quantization are needed to close remaining gaps. The approach also inherits dependence on PartField for inference-time segmentation, adding a non-trivial pipeline dependency.
