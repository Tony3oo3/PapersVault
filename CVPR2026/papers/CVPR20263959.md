## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot image interpretation methods that fine-tune pre-trained visual backbones with limited target-domain labels tend to latch onto superficial, non-transferable shortcut patterns rather than robust domain-invariant semantics. Existing fine-tuning and prompt-based approaches rely exclusively on visual cues, lacking explicit semantic guidance to bridge the gap between source and target domain characteristics under extreme data scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263959/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For each target-domain image, the framework consumes the raw RGB image, an image-level caption generated by a frozen captioning model (BLIP or CoCa), and a domain-level description generated by an LLM (ChatGPT or LLaMA) from a structured prompt template. These two text sources are concatenated and encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into a single linguistic vector. The refined visual features are then passed to a task-specific head producing class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or object bounding boxes, depending on the downstream task.

## Method

The framework introduces two plug-and-play modules into the standard few-shot fine-tuning pipeline. The Attribute Generator (AG) extracts image-level attributes via a pre-trained captioning model and domain-level attributes via an LLM, fuses them into a single text string, and encodes it with a frozen CLIP text encoder to obtain a linguistic representation. The Feature Refiner (FR) embeds this linguistic representation into the visual feature map through a lightweight residual cross-attention mechanism: visual features act as queries while the linguistic vector provides keys and values, and a residual projection of the original visual features is added to preserve low-level information. Only four fully connected projection layers are added to the backbone, keeping the overhead minimal. The authors further provide an information-bottleneck analysis showing that the linguistic conditioning reduces mutual information between input and representation (suppressing shortcut cues) while increasing task-relevant information, yielding tighter generalization bounds.

## Results

On cross-domain few-shot classification (5-way K-shot, averaged over Chest, ISIC, EuroSAT, CropDisease), the proposed method integrated with CD-CLS achieves 57.98% (1-shot) and 69.88% (5-shot) average accuracy, surpassing the strongest prior baseline REAP (56.04%/68.03%) by +1.94%/+1.85%. On cross-domain few-shot segmentation (averaged over ISIC, Chest X-Ray, FSS-1000, DeepGlobe), IFA+Ours reaches 71.9% (1-shot) and 75.0% (5-shot) mIoU, improving over GPRN (71.7%/75.3%) and IFA (67.8%/71.4%). On cross-domain few-shot object detection (averaged over six target domains), CD-ViTO+Ours achieves 18.3/31.5/36.9 mAP under 1-/5-/10-shot settings, outperforming CD-ViTO by +4.4/+5.4/+7.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is plug-and-play and is evaluated by integrating into three representative baselines per task. In classification, CD-CLS+Ours (57.98%/69.88% avg) outperforms all 10 prior methods including REAP (56.04%/68.03%), ReCIT (55.87%/68.06%), and AttuTemp (55.39%/67.61%), with the largest gains over PMF (+4.83%/+3.33%) and StyleAdv (+2.50%/+1.90%). In segmentation, IFA+Ours (71.9%/75.0% avg mIoU) surpasses GPRN (71.7%/75.3%) by +2.9/+2.4 points and IFA (67.8%/71.4%) by +4.1/+3.6 points, while also outperforming DFN-ViT (63.99%/69.77%), SDRC (63.22%/67.30%), and LLF-ViT (65.01%/70.43%). In detection, CD-ViTO+Ours (18.3/31.5/36.9 mAP) beats DE-ViT-FT (10.1/22.3/25.2), Detic-FT (6.6/13.3/16.5), and ViTDeT-FT (5.4/16.9/19.4) by wide margins, with the most notable improvement over CD-ViTO itself (+4.4/+5.4/+7.3 mAP). Against prompt-tuning baselines (VPT: 51.68%/64.45%, MetaPrompt: 52.58%/64.86%) and multimodal fusion (SEVPro: 52.14%/64.79%), the method achieves 55.74%/67.41%, confirming that cross-modal linguistic embedding outperforms both purely visual prompting and naive multimodal fusion. The method wins on every single comparison across all three tasks and all few-shot settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of cross-domain few-shot fine-tuning (StyleAdv, PMF, CD-CLS) and multimodal feature enhancement (SEVPro, class-level text prototypes from Han et al. and Shangguan et al.), but departs from both in a key way: rather than building class-level text prototypes or learning visual prompts, it generates per-image and per-domain linguistic descriptions (via a captioning model and an LLM) and embeds them into visual features through a lightweight residual cross-attention module. The cross-attention mechanism itself is standard, but its application here—as a plug-and-play feature refiner that operates on spatial feature maps (h×w×d) with a single-token linguistic key/value—is a novel repurposing. The information bottleneck analysis connects the approach to the broader literature on representation compression for generalization, though the theoretical treatment remains informal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, task-agnostic design (only four additional FC layers) makes this a practical drop-in improvement for any existing CD-FSII pipeline, and the consistent gains across classification, segmentation, and detection validate its generality. However, the approach depends on the quality of external captioning and LLM outputs, and the domain-level attribute generation relies on a manually designed prompt template, limiting full automation. Open questions include whether the method extends to video or 3D domains, how it behaves when the source and target domains share minimal visual overlap, and whether the IB-based justification can be made into a rigorous generalization bound. The reliance on a frozen CLIP text encoder also constrains the semantic expressiveness of the linguistic channel.
