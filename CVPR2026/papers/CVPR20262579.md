## Problem

Existing learnable motion estimators for multi-object tracking suffer from two persistent issues: probabilistic predictions introduce noise and instability in linear-motion scenarios, and trajectories fragment under occlusion when visual evidence is lost. Both Kalman filters and data-driven predictors estimate motion independently per target, missing the opportunity to leverage correlated motion states across objects for mutual stabilization and continuity inference.

## Input / Output

The motion estimation module consumes 5-frame trajectory segments of multi-object positions (P_L^N ∈ R^{N×L×4}) rather than raw images, encoding them into trajectory embeddings that guide each cascaded layer. The overall pipeline takes video frames processed by a YOLOX detector, and the HyperSSM module predicts future bounding-box positions for the next sliding window at one-frame intervals, which are then matched to detections via a multi-stage adaptive association strategy to produce tracked boxes with consistent identities.

## Method

HyperSSM embeds hypergraph convolution directly into a State Space Model's state transition: the hidden state update becomes h_t = Āh_{t-1} + B̄·HConv(X_t), and the output adds a residual HConv term (Y_t = X_t + Ch_t + HConv(X_t)), removing the conventional D parameter to avoid over-parameterization. The hypergraph is constructed dynamically by computing EMA-weighted velocity features for each object and forming e-ball hyperedges around objects whose motion features fall within a similarity threshold θ, enabling gathering (vertex-to-edge averaging) and scattering (edge-to-vertex redistribution) that suppress individual noise through group consensus. Eight cascaded HyperSSM layers progressively refine trajectories, with trajectory embeddings injected at each layer and hidden states acting as messengers between layers. Training is supervised with smooth L1 and GIoU losses on predicted versus ground-truth positions across the sliding window, using only trajectory segments (no images) as input to the motion module.

## Results

On MOT17 (linear motion), HyperSSM achieves 66.9 HOTA, 83.1 IDF1, and 81.5 MOTA, outperforming all competing methods by a clear margin. On MOT20, it reaches 65.2 HOTA and 80.0 MOTA. On the non-linear motion benchmarks, HyperSSM attains 65.7 HOTA on DanceTrack (best among motion-based methods) and 78.5 HOTA on SportsMOT (best overall, surpassing the previous best DiffMOT by 2.3 HOTA). The method consistently leads on AssA (association accuracy) across all four benchmarks, confirming its strength in identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MOT17, HyperSSM (66.9 HOTA) surpasses the strongest Kalman-based tracker SparseTrack (65.1) by 1.8 HOTA and the best learnable-motion tracker DiffMOT (64.5) by 2.4 HOTA, while also outperforming the top query-based method MOTRv2 (62.0) by 4.9 HOTA. On MOT20, it leads HybridSORT (63.9) and SparseTrack (63.4) by 1.3 and 1.8 HOTA respectively. On DanceTrack, HyperSSM (65.7 HOTA) ties with HybridSORT on HOTA but clearly wins on IDF1 (68.0 vs 67.4) and MOTA (92.7 vs 91.8); however, query-based methods DualMOT (76.2) and SAM2MOT (75.8) still outperform it on HOTA, reflecting the known advantage of query-based approaches in fixed-count, highly non-linear scenarios. On SportsMOT, HyperSSM achieves the best overall HOTA of 78.5, beating DiffMOT (76.2) by 2.3 and TrackSSM (74.4) by 4.1. The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the hypergraph component is critical: replacing HyperSSM with a plain SSM drops MOT17 HOTA from 65.5 to 63.4 and DanceTrack HOTA from 59.7 to 53.7, while the Kalman filter, though competitive on linear MOT17 (64.5), collapses on DanceTrack (47.0).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: learnable motion estimation for tracking-by-detection (following TrackSSM and DiffMOT, which replace Kalman Filters with data-driven predictors) and hypergraph-based relational modeling (building on HGNN+ for high-order spatial interactions). It departs from prior graph-based MOT methods, which use GNNs to link the same target across frames for identity association, by instead modeling within-frame motion correlations among *different* targets via hyperedges. The SSM component inherits the S4 formulation for temporal state propagation, but the key architectural departure is embedding hypergraph convolution directly into the SSM's state transition and output equations, creating a unified spatial-temporal reasoning block rather than treating spatial and temporal modeling as separate stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The collaborative reasoning principle—that objects with similar motion states can mutually constrain and refine each other's trajectories—offers a principled mechanism for addressing two persistent MOT failure modes (noise-induced instability and occlusion-induced fragmentation) without requiring additional visual cues. The consistent SOTA across both linear (MOT17/20) and highly nonlinear (DanceTrack/SportsMOT) benchmarks suggests the approach generalizes well beyond the linear-motion regime where Kalman Filters traditionally excel. Open questions include scalability to scenes with hundreds of objects (hyperedge construction cost), whether the heuristic distance-threshold hyperedge construction can be learned end-to-end, and whether integrating appearance features into the collaborative motion model would further improve identity robustness in appearance-similar scenarios.
