## Problem

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) can comprehend complex, implicit user intent but frequently fail to manifest that understanding in generated imagery, particularly when prompts demand genuine multi-step reasoning or specialized domain knowledge. Existing reasoning-focused T2I datasets conflate verbose paraphrasing with authentic reasoning, offering no intermediate reasoning chain to bridge the understanding-generation gap. This paper identifies the absence of a large-scale, logically rigorous dataset that pairs implicit prompts with explicit step-by-step reasoning chains and corresponding images.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single implicit text prompt (e.g., a constraint-satisfaction puzzle or a knowledge-implicit description) and must produce a two-stage textual output—a step-by-step reasoning chain followed by an explicit visual description—before synthesizing the final RGB image. The 120k-sample dataset is partitioned into a 65k Reasoning Subset (arithmetic, spatial-attribute constraint, deductive, abductive) and a 55k Knowledge Subset (disciplinary, entity, spatio-temporal), with token-length profiles varying by category: implicit prompts dominate in spatial-attribute tasks while explicit prompts are longest in knowledge tasks. Images are rendered by state-of-the-art T2I models (Nano-Banana, HunyuanImage-3.0) and verified via MLLM recaptioning for semantic alignment.

## Method

The core contribution is a CoT-Injected Generation Process that jointly trains Bagel's understanding and generation modules with two parallel objectives: (1) a Next Token Prediction loss that teaches the understanding module to produce the reasoning chain concatenated with the explicit prompt from the implicit prompt, and (2) an MSE loss that conditions the generation module on the derived CoT to reconstruct the ground-truth image. The total loss is a weighted combination (λ_NTP=0.1, λ_IMG=1), and comprehensive data shuffling across batches is critical for stable convergence, with warm-up set to ~20% of total steps. The dataset itself is constructed via two distinct pipelines: an LLM-driven iterative inflation strategy for the Reasoning Subset (seed prompts → exemplar-guided generation) and a template-based approach for the Knowledge Subset (structured templates grounded in verified knowledge, then LLM-refined for linguistic diversity), followed by a two-tiered MLLM verification and salvage filtering stage.

## Results

On the standard evaluation (thinking mode off), BAGEL_w/CoT achieves 0.56 overall on WISE and 0.44 overall on R2I-Bench, outperforming the unmodified BAGEL baseline (0.52 / 0.36) and the w/o CoT variant (0.55 / 0.40). With thinking mode enabled, the gains widen: BAGEL_w/CoT reaches 0.77 on WISE and 0.54 on R2I-Bench, versus 0.70/0.48 for the baseline. The most dramatic improvement is on R2I-Bench Causal (CA) reasoning, where BAGEL_w/CoT scores 0.69 compared to 0.35 for the baseline and 0.32 for w/o CoT, more than doubling the baseline score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three configurations of the Bagel unified multimodal model: the unmodified baseline, a variant fine-tuned on UniVerse without CoT injection (w/o CoT), and the proposed CoT-injected training (w/ CoT). BAGEL_w/CoT consistently outperforms both alternatives on both WISE and R2I-Bench in both standard and thinking-mode evaluations. The advantage is modest on WISE (0.56 vs. 0.55 vs. 0.52 standard; 0.77 vs. 0.74 vs. 0.70 thinking) but becomes substantial on R2I-Bench, where the CoT-injected model gains +0.08 over baseline and +0.04 over w/o CoT in standard mode, and +0.06 / +0.03 in thinking mode. The single most striking comparison is on R2I-Bench Causal reasoning: BAGEL_w/CoT scores 0.69 versus 0.35 (baseline) and 0.32 (w/o CoT), indicating that CoT-injected training specifically activates causal reasoning capability that neither the base model nor simple prompt-conditioned fine-tuning can unlock. Notably, the w/o CoT variant actually underperforms the baseline on CA (0.32 vs. 0.35), suggesting that training on implicit prompts without an intermediate reasoning chain can be mildly detrimental for causal tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Bagel (Deng et al., 2025), a unified multimodal model with dual thinking/non-thinking modes, and positions its contribution as a data-centric complement to prior architectural and RL-based approaches for reasoning in generation. It explicitly departs from earlier reasoning-focused T2I datasets (e.g., those underlying Commonsense-T2I, PhyBench) by arguing they conflate linguistic elaboration with genuine logical inference, and from CoT-guided generation methods (MINT, GOT) by providing a large-scale, structured training corpus rather than a new inference-time prompting strategy. The evaluation framework draws directly on WISE and R2I-Bench, situating the work within the emerging benchmarking landscape for reasoning-driven generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 120k-sample UniVerse dataset and the CoT-injected training recipe offer a concrete, reproducible recipe for closing the understanding–generation gap in UMMs, and the consistent gains across WISE and R2I-Bench (especially the near-doubling on Causal reasoning, 0.35→0.69) suggest that intermediate reasoning chains are a genuinely useful supervisory signal rather than mere verbosity. Limitations include validation on a single base model (Bagel), reliance on LLM-generated reasoning chains that may inherit hallucination biases, and the fact that the 'salvaged' filtering step, while shown to be non-harmful, introduces a circular dependency between image quality and text alignment. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other UMMs (Janus, BLIP-3-o, Show-o), whether the reasoning chains can be generated without expensive LLM calls at scale, and whether the benefits extend to video or 3D generation.
