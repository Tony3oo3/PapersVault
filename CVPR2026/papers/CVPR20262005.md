## Problem

Relative depth foundation models (e.g., MiDaS, DepthAnything) achieve strong cross-scene generalization but produce scale-ambiguous outputs, while existing scale-recovery approaches either apply a single global rescaling that fails under local scale inconsistencies or require domain-specific training that limits generalization. The paper addresses how to convert relative depth into accurate metric depth using only extremely sparse 3D cues, without fine-tuning the depth model or requiring large-scale depth completion datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262005/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three inputs: a relative depth map from any depth foundation model, a color image for superpixel segmentation, and a set of extremely sparse 3D seeds (as few as 1–1.5% of available LiDAR points, or derived from disparity/correspondence matches) projected onto the image plane. It outputs a dense metric depth map at the same resolution as the input image. The 3D seeds need only provide a scalar proxy equivalent to depth via a monotonic bijection, making the method agnostic to the specific 3D sensor or estimation source.

## Method

MTD is a nonparametric, two-stage coarse-to-fine pipeline. First, segment-wise recovery partitions the image into superpixels, fits per-segment calibration functions (in inverse-depth domain) on segments containing projected 3D seeds, then propagates calibration parameters to unseeded segments by solving a graph-regularized quadratic optimization over a sparse superpixel graph. Second, pixel-wise refinement formulates depth propagation as a discontinuity-aware geodesic problem: the path cost is derived from a Proposition bounding the second-order remainder of depth by an integral of φ = √(z_uu² + z_vv²), and the geodesic distance is computed via dynamic programming with a path-integral update rule using a harmonic step-size sequence. The entire conversion is training-free and plug-and-play; for deployment efficiency, the front-end depth model is optionally distilled into lightweight TinyViT or EfficientViT backbones, adding only ~1.9 ms of back-end overhead at 480×640 resolution.

## Results

On the depth completion benchmark (Table 1), MTD achieves the best RMSE and MAE across all 10 evaluated datasets. On the challenging outdoor nuScenes dataset (sparse LiDAR, high depth range), MTD reduces MAE to 2.177 and RMSE to 4.387, outperforming the previous SoTA Marigold-DC by 0.418 MAE and 0.537 RMSE. As a plug-and-play module for relative depth estimation (Table 2), MTD with DepthAnythingV2 achieves AbsRel of 0.022 and δ₁ of 0.987 on KITTI, and AbsRel of 0.013 and δ₁ of 0.995 on NYUv2, substantially improving over the uncalibrated backbone (AbsRel 0.080 / 0.043). The back-end computation adds only 1.9 ms and <1.8 GB memory on an RTX 3090 at 480×640 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MTD outperforms all 25 compared baselines on every dataset in both the depth completion (Table 1) and depth estimation (Table 2) evaluations. The closest competitor is Marigold-DC (ICCV'25), a diffusion-based depth completion method; MTD beats it by 0.418 MAE on nuScenes, 0.535 MAE on DDAD, and 0.013 MAE on VOID1500. Among learned depth completion methods, DMD³C (CVPR'25) and PromptDA (CVPR'25) are the strongest neural baselines, but MTD still leads by 1.17–1.35 MAE on outdoor datasets (nuScenes, DDAD) and by 0.011–0.013 MAE on indoor datasets (VOID, ScanNet). In the plug-and-play depth estimation setting (Table 2), MTD consistently reduces AbsRel by 3–7× across all relative depth backbones (MiDaS, DPT, DepthAnythingV2, Marigold, etc.) and metric depth models (Metric3Dv2, UniDepthV2), with the largest gains on outdoor KITTI (e.g., DPT AbsRel drops from 0.111 to 0.032). MTD also surpasses the SoTA zero-shot MVS method MVSAnywhere in multi-view stereo (RMSE 0.079 vs. 0.088) and reduces SLAM trajectory error by 2–5× over Droid-SLAM with raw LiDAR on KITTI Odometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

MTD carves a distinct niche between two established lines of work: learned depth completion (BPNet, DMD³C, Marigold-DC) that fuses sparse 3D data with images via trained networks, and direct metric depth estimation (Metric3D, UniDepth) that sacrifices cross-domain generalization for absolute scale. Rather than training a new model or fine-tuning a depth foundation model, MTD repurposes the strong generalization of relative depth estimators (DepthAnythingV2, MiDaS) and applies a nonparametric, mathematically grounded post-processing pipeline—segment-wise sparse graph optimization for local scale recovery followed by a discontinuity-aware geodesic dynamic-programming refinement. This positions the work as a plug-and-play bridge that inherits the generalization of foundation models while resolving their inherent scale ambiguity, without the domain-specific training costs that limit prior depth completion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.9 ms, <1.8 GB back-end that converts any relative depth output to metric depth enables real-time deployment on handheld edge devices (Jetson AGX Orin) and serves as a drop-in module for SLAM, multi-view stereo, and occupancy prediction pipelines. The segment-wise formulation addresses a fundamental limitation of global scale recovery—local scale inconsistencies across segments—that prior least-squares or single-network approaches cannot handle, and the ablations confirm this is the dominant source of error. Open questions remain: the method still requires at least some 3D seed data (even if extremely sparse), and the authors note future work on eliminating this requirement via continual learning. Additionally, the reliance on superpixel segmentation as a spatial prior introduces a hyperparameter (segment scale) that must be tuned per sparsity level, limiting full automation.
