## Problem

Existing storyboard synthesis methods built on image diffusion models struggle to maintain long-range temporal coherence, consistent character identities, and narrative flow across multiple shots, while video-based models that capture temporal dynamics are computationally expensive and produce dense, redundant frames unsuitable for editable storyboard creation. This creates a fundamental trade-off: image models offer flexibility but lack coherence, while video models offer consistency but lack efficiency and controllability for story-level generation.

## Input / Output

DreamShot accepts three types of input depending on the generation mode: text prompts with shot-level annotations (perspective, environment, action, style), one or more reference character images (encoded independently via the video VAE), and optionally preceding storyboard shots as noise-free conditions for continuation. The output is a sequence of S key storyboard shots at 480p resolution, where each shot is a single representative frame capturing cinematic composition rather than a dense video clip. Each storyboard group is structured as K reference roles paired with S annotated shots, and the model supports all three modes (Text-to-Shot, Reference-to-Shot, Shot-to-Shot) within a single unified framework.

## Method

DreamShot repurposes a video diffusion backbone (Wan2.1-14B with spatio-temporal VAE and DiT) for storyboard generation by repeating each shot T times to match the VAE's temporal compression, converting independent images into a causally ordered latent stream that the DiT interprets as a coherent temporal narrative. Reference image tokens are concatenated before shot tokens in the sequence, exploiting 3D RoPE to propagate role identity forward in time, while a shot-wise cross-attention layer provides fine-grained per-shot text alignment. The key training innovation is the Role-Attention Consistency Loss (RACL), which uses ArcFace and a VLM to establish one-to-one role correspondences, then applies BCE supervision on DiT self-attention maps to enforce that each reference role attends only to its spatial counterpart in generated shots, directly mitigating multi-character feature entanglement. Training uses a Flow Matching objective with a two-phase schedule (192p with RACL for 10k steps, then 480p without for 10k steps) via LoRA (rank 256), and inference employs a reference-free CFG scheme with separate guidance weights for reference-image and text conditions.

## Results

On the DreamShot Test Set (Reference-to-Shot mode), DreamShot achieves CIDS-Cross of 51.6, CSD-Self of 64.5, and Alignment Score of 3.39, outperforming the strongest baselines (DreamO: 40.0 CIDS-Cross, 58.3 CSD-Self) by 11.6 and 6.2 points respectively. In Text-to-Shot mode, DreamShot leads with CSD-Self of 65.4 (vs. 62.6 for Story2Board) and Alignment of 3.22. On VistoryBench, DreamShot attains the best CIDS-Self (65.6) and CIDS-Cross (60.2), surpassing OmniGen2 by 0.9 and 5.4 points on character identity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamShot consistently outperforms all image-diffusion-based baselines (Story-Adapter, StoryDiffusion, Story2Board, UNO) across all three generation modes, with the largest margins in CIDS-Cross (51.6 vs. 40.0 for DreamO, the strongest reference-based competitor) and CSD-Self (64.5 vs. 58.3 for DreamO in Reference-to-Shot; 65.4 vs. 62.6 for Story2Board in Text-to-Shot). On VistoryBench, DreamShot leads in character identity (CIDS-Self 65.6, CIDS-Cross 60.2) over OmniGen2 (64.7, 54.8) and UNO (62.0, 48.5), though it trails OmniGen2 slightly on CSD-Cross (43.9 vs. 45.4), suggesting a minor trade-off in cross-shot style transfer. DreamShot also achieves the highest Alignment Score in Reference-to-Shot (3.39) and Shot-to-Shot (3.31) modes, indicating better text-to-visual correspondence. The only setting where DreamShot does not lead on every metric is Shot-to-Shot CIDS-Self (40.4 vs. 42.2 for Story-Adapter), where the absence of reference images limits identity anchoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

DreamShot departs from the dominant image-diffusion paradigm for storyboard generation (StoryDiffusion, Story-Adapter, UNO, AnyStory) by repurposing a video diffusion backbone (Wan2.1-14B) whose spatio-temporal VAE and 3D RoPE naturally encode sequential structure. The key conceptual move is treating discrete storyboard shots as a temporally ordered latent stream—repeating each shot T times to match the VAE's causal compression—so that the DiT propagates identity forward in time rather than relying on cross-frame attention hacks. The Role-Attention Consistency Loss extends prior identity-preservation mechanisms (IP-Adapter, ControlNet, ArcFace-based matching) by explicitly supervising attention maps between reference and generated roles, addressing multi-character feature entanglement that image-based methods leave unresolved.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that video diffusion priors can substitute for dense video generation while retaining image-level editability, DreamShot opens a practical path toward long-form, multi-character cinematic storytelling that was previously infeasible with either pure image or pure video models. The RACL loss and the reference-free CFG scheme are modular techniques likely transferable to other multi-subject generation settings. However, the authors acknowledge that performance is bounded by the base model's quality and the limited length of available storyboard training data, leaving extremely long shot sequences and fine-grained camera-language control as open challenges.
