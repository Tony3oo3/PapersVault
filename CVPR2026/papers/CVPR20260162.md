## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for remote sensing each target a single functional pathway—spatial (LoRA), semantic (Adapter), or frequency (Earth-Adapter)—and fail when confronted with domain shifts outside their specialty. Because RS domain gaps are multifaceted and unpredictable (simultaneously involving spatial, semantic, and frequency shifts), no static single-pathway PEFT can comprehensively handle cross-domain adaptation across diverse climate zones, spectral bands, and geographic regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-band RS images (3-channel RGB, IR-R-G, R-G-B, or R-GB-IR) along with frozen pretrained foundation model weights (DINOv2, SatMAE, Scale-MAE, or SAM backbones) and produces pixel-level semantic segmentation masks with task-specific class labels (e.g., road/building/water/forest for CASID; impervious surface/building/tree/car/clutter for Potsdam and RescueNet). All modules are initially inserted at every transformer block of the backbone, but only a dynamically selected subset is active at any given training phase, keeping trainable parameters in the 0.7–4.9 M range versus 304 M for full tuning.

## Method

CrossEarth-Gate introduces a structured RS module toolbox that simultaneously inserts three module types at every transformer block: a Spatial Module (LoRA low-rank matrices on MSA query/value projections), a Semantic Module (parallel Adapter with down-projection, GELU, and up-projection alongside the MLP), and a Frequency Module (Earth-Adapter performing Fourier decomposition with a mixture-of-adapters router). The key innovation is a Fisher-guided adaptive selection mechanism: every N training iterations, all modules are temporarily activated, and the empirical diagonal Fisher Information Matrix is computed over a small batch to score each module's contribution to task-specific gradient flow. Module-level scores are aggregated by summing constituent parameter scores, then category-normalized across all blocks to make different module types comparable. Only the Top-k highest-scoring modules are activated for the next N iterations, effectively gating gradient flow to the most impactful pathways. This periodic re-evaluation enables a multi-stage learning process where the active module configuration evolves as the model adapts, achieving SOTA on 16 of 18 cross-domain benchmarks with 3.0–4.4 M trainable parameters.

## Results

CrossEarth-Gate achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on 16 out of 18 cross-domain RS semantic segmentation benchmarks. On the CASID dataset (12 DG scenarios across four climate zones), it secures the highest average mIoU for every source domain: 60.6% (Sub), 65.1% (Tem), 59.3% (Tms), and 62.4% (Trf), using only 3.0–4.4M trainable parameters. On the disaster-scenario DG tasks, it reaches 60.1% mIoU on P(r)2Res and 58.6% on P(i)2Res. On the four DA benchmarks (P2V, V2P, R2U, U2R), it achieves a 59.1% average mIoU, outperforming the best PEFT baseline (Earth-Adapter, 58.1%) by 1.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossEarth-Gate outperforms all PEFT baselines on the CASID averages: it beats Earth-Adapter (the strongest single-pathway PEFT) by 1.6% (Sub source), 0.6% (Tem), 1.3% (Tms), and 0.6% (Trf) mIoU, while using roughly half the parameters (3.0–4.4M vs. 9.6M). It also surpasses AdaptFormer by up to 2.0% on individual scenarios (e.g., Sub2Tms: 66.6 vs. 58.4) and LoRA by up to 2.0% (Sub2Tms: 66.6 vs. 60.5). On the disaster DG tasks, it leads AdaptFormer by 0.9% (P(r)2Res: 60.1 vs. 59.2) and 1.2% (P(i)2Res: 58.6 vs. 57.4). On DA benchmarks, it edges out Earth-Adapter by 1.0% average (59.1 vs. 58.1) and AdaptFormer by 1.4% (59.1 vs. 57.7). The only losses are marginal: CrossEarth-Gate trails Earth-Adapter by 0.8% on Tem2Sub and Adaptformer by 0.8% on Trf2Sub, while tying LoRA on V2P (62.4%). Full-Tuning consistently underperforms the Frozen backbone on CASID (e.g., 53.0 vs. 55.2 for Sub source), confirming that unconstrained fine-tuning overfits to the source domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three established PEFT paradigms—LoRA for spatial (MSA) adaptation, AdaptFormer/Adapter for semantic (MLP) adaptation, and Earth-Adapter for frequency-domain artifact mitigation—into a unified module toolbox, then introduces a Fisher Information–guided gating mechanism to dynamically select which modules to activate at which transformer blocks. The Fisher-based importance scoring draws on the sparse fine-tuning literature (Sung et al., 2021) that uses diagonal FIM approximations to rank parameter relevance, but here it is repurposed at the module level rather than the individual-parameter level, and applied periodically during training rather than once at initialization. The key conceptual departure from prior RS-specific PEFT work (Earth-Adapter, SLR, CrossEarth) is the rejection of a single static functional pathway in favor of a data-driven, task-adaptive combination of multiple pathways.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine practical pain point in RS adaptation: no single PEFT method reliably handles the intertwined spatial, semantic, and frequency shifts that co-occur in real cross-domain scenarios, and the Fisher-guided selection offers a principled, interpretable answer to 'which adapter where and when.' However, the margins over the best single PEFT baseline are modest (≈1–2 mIoU), and the two losing cases (Tem2Sub, Trf2Sub) where a static method coincidentally aligns better suggest the dynamic selection is not yet a strict superset of the best static choice. Open questions include sensitivity to the re-evaluation interval N, scalability to additional module types or deeper backbones, and whether the Fisher criterion remains informative under the noisy pseudo-labels typical of DA settings.
