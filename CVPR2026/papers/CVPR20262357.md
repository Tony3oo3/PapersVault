## Problem

Existing LMM-based segmentation methods (e.g., LISA) fail to correctly localize anomalous regions in industrial and medical zero-shot settings because anomaly concepts are inherently abstract and context-dependent—lacking stable visual prototypes like concrete objects—and because high-level semantic embeddings are poorly aligned with pixel-level spatial features. CLIP-based ZSAS approaches have reached a performance bottleneck and do not naturally produce binary masks, while prior LMM-based anomaly works focus on image-level classification rather than precise pixel-level segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image and a free-form text instruction (e.g., 'Please segment the anomalies in this image') and directly outputs a binarized segmentation mask (thresholded at 0.5) along with optional textual anomaly descriptions. It supports four inference modes—Direct Segmentation, Describe-then-Segment, Describe-then-Segment-Plus, and Segment-then-Explain—that differ in whether reasoning is externalized before or after mask prediction. The pixel encoder operates at high resolution (SAM-ViT-H) while the semantic encoder uses siglip-patch14-384, and the final mask is a per-pixel binary map at the original image resolution.

## Method

AG-VAS extends the LMM vocabulary with three learnable anchor tokens: an absolute anchor [SEG] that encodes world knowledge about defect appearance, shape, and location, and relative anchors [NOR]/[ANO] that model the contextual contrast between normal and abnormal patterns. A Semantic-Pixel Alignment Module (SPAM) bridges the LLM's embedding space and the pixel encoder via multi-head cross-attention (semantic features as queries attending to pixel features as keys/values), and the concatenated features are fed into the LLM alongside the text instruction. An Anchor-Guided Mask Decoder (AGMD) then performs L layers of bidirectional cross-attention between anchor-conditioned learnable queries and pixel embeddings, producing an absolute sigmoid probability map from [SEG] and a relative softmax contrastive map from [NOR]/[ANO], which are fused with α=0.5 to yield the final mask. Training jointly optimizes autoregressive cross-entropy over all tokens (including anchors) and a combined BCE+Dice segmentation loss, using LoRA (rank 16) fine-tuning on LLaVA-OneVision-7B with a curated Anomaly-Instruct20K instruction dataset that injects structured five-field anomaly knowledge (Expectation, Observation, Diagnosis, Summary, Explanation) generated by Intern-S1-241B.

## Results

AG-VAS (LLaVA-OneVision-7B) achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot anomaly segmentation across all six benchmarks, with AP scores of 51.0 (MVTec-AD), 76.6 (KSDD2), 56.2 (RSDD), 92.7 (ISIC), 70.7 (ColonDB), and 86.6 (ClinicDB). On the most challenging industrial dataset MVTec-AD, it reaches IoU_ano of 44.8 and IoU_nor of 87.7, substantially outperforming the best LMM baseline LISA* (IoU_ano 32.3, IoU_nor 80.9) and the best CLIP-based method Bayes-PFL* (IoU_ano 29.9). On medical datasets, AG-VAS leads by large margins, e.g., 92.7 AP on ISIC vs. 84.9 for LISA* and 81.9 for Bayes-PFL*, with no medical images included in training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AG-VAS outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across all six datasets. Against the strongest CLIP-based method Bayes-PFL*, AG-VAS gains +10.7 AP and +14.9 IoU_ano on MVTec-AD, and +10.8 AP and +12.9 IoU_ano on ISIC, indicating that CLIP-based approaches produce ambiguous anomaly scores that degrade sharply after binarization. Against the strongest LMM baseline LISA* (LLaVA-OneVision-7B), AG-VAS improves IoU_ano by +12.5 on MVTec-AD (44.8 vs. 32.3), +5.4 on KSDD2 (53.8 vs. 48.4), and +14.9 on ISIC (65.0 vs. 50.1), while also achieving substantially better normal-sample rejection (IoU_nor 87.7 vs. 80.9). On medical datasets, the gap widens further: AG-VAS reaches 70.7 AP on ColonDB vs. 67.2 for LISA* and only 30.5 for Bayes-PFL*, and 86.6 AP on ClinicDB vs. 81.0 for LISA* and 47.6 for Bayes-PFL*. The LMM-based baselines PixelLM and PaDT, designed for general referring segmentation, perform poorly on industrial anomaly tasks (e.g., PaDT achieves only 6.8 AP on MVTec-AD), confirming that general-purpose segmentation LMMs do not transfer well to anomaly-specific semantics without dedicated anchor mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

AG-VAS extends the embedding-as-mask paradigm introduced by LISA and the bidirectional cross-attention decoder design from SAM, but departs from both by introducing learnable semantic anchor tokens ([SEG], [NOR], [ANO]) specifically tailored to the abstract, prototype-free nature of anomaly concepts. Unlike CLIP-based ZSAS methods (AnomalyCLIP, Bayes-PFL) that rely on prompt learning over a fixed vision-language space, AG-VAS leverages the richer world knowledge and instruction-following capacity of LMMs (LLaVA-OneVision) to ground anomaly semantics. The Semantic-Pixel Alignment Module (SPAM) addresses a gap that prior LMM segmentation works (PixelLM, PaDT) leave unaddressed: the misalignment between high-level LLM embeddings and fine-grained pixel features, which is particularly acute when the target concept (anomaly) lacks a stable visual referent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AG-VAS is the first LMM-based framework to directly output binarized segmentation masks for ZSAS, closing a practical gap where prior LMM approaches only produced image-level descriptions or classification scores. The strong rejection performance (IoU_nor = 87.7% vs. <10% for most LMM baselines) is practically critical for deployment, as false positives on normal samples are costly in industrial and medical settings. Open questions include whether the anchor-token approach generalizes to anomaly types beyond surface defects (e.g., structural or functional anomalies), whether the 20K instruction dataset is sufficient for truly diverse industrial scenarios, and how the method scales to higher-resolution or video-based anomaly detection.
