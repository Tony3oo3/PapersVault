## Problem

Applying distribution matching distillation (DMD) to video diffusion models produces two severe artifacts absent or milder in image distillation: color oversaturation (driven by the teacher's overemphasis on fine-grained detail) and temporal mode collapse (reduced or static motion). These issues are amplified in autoregressive video generation where errors accumulate across frames, yet no prior distillation framework was specifically designed to address both simultaneously for video.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and generates a 5-second, 16 fps video at 832×480 resolution using only 4 denoising steps (vs. 100 steps for the teacher). The student model is distilled from Wan 2.1 teacher models (1.3B or 14B parameters) and operates in the VAE latent space. During inference, the first two denoising steps run at half frame rate, after which a lightweight UNet interpolates the sequence back to full frame rate before the remaining low-noise steps refine details.

## Method

The framework builds on DMD2's distribution-matching objective and introduces two additional losses. First, an Adaptive Regression Loss directly supervises the student on real video data but dynamically down-weights samples whose loss deviates from a per-timestep EMA cache, preventing tearing and object-fusion artifacts caused by inconsistent supervision. Second, a Temporal Regularization Loss penalizes low temporal variance in generated frames (via a log-variance term) to explicitly restore motion dynamics lost during distillation. At inference, a decoupled frame-interpolation strategy halves the sequence length during high-noise steps (where inter-frame similarity is high) and restores full frame rate before low-noise refinement, yielding ~30% speedup with negligible quality loss. The total training cost adds only one extra forward pass per generator update beyond standard DMD2.

## Results

On the 1.3B Wan2.1 model, the proposed method achieves a VBench2 Total score of 55.08 and VBench1 Total of 81.35 in 4 steps (7.8 s inference), outperforming the DMD* baseline (53.63 / 80.66) and all consistency-based methods (LCM 40.12/72.12, PCM 47.40/73.70, DCM 51.80/73.92, rCM 54.03/80.15). On the 14B model, it reaches VBench2 Total 59.06 and VBench1 Total 82.57 in 22.2 s, surpassing DMD* (56.87 / 79.63) by over 2 points on both benchmarks. The method also attains the highest Human Fidelity (88.26 / 89.00) and Semantic Score (65.65 / 74.06) across all compared methods, while reducing inference time by ~30% relative to the 4-step DMD* baseline through frame-interpolation acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five distillation baselines (DMD*, LCM, PCM, DCM, rCM) on the overall VBench2 and VBench1 Total scores for both the 1.3B and 14B models. Against the closest competitor DMD*, it gains +1.45 on VBench2 Total (55.08 vs 53.63) and +0.69 on VBench1 Total (81.35 vs 80.66) at 1.3B, and +2.19 / +2.94 at 14B, while also being ~30% faster in inference (7.8 s vs 10.8 s; 22.2 s vs 28 s). The largest margins are over consistency-based methods: LCM trails by 14.96 points on VBench2 Total (40.12 vs 55.08) and 9.23 on VBench1 Total (72.12 vs 81.35) at 1.3B. The method achieves the highest Human Fidelity (88.26 at 1.3B, 89.00 at 14B) and Semantic Score (65.65 / 74.06), indicating superior motion realism and prompt alignment. The one metric where it trails is Creativity (48.19 vs DMD*'s 55.90 at 1.3B; 52.89 vs DMD*'s 54.92 at 14B), suggesting the adaptive regression loss slightly constrains out-of-distribution creative variation in exchange for more stable, physically plausible outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD/DMD2) from image to video generation, explicitly targeting two failure modes that are amplified in the temporal dimension: color oversaturation and motion collapse. Rather than introducing a new distillation paradigm, it augments the DMD objective with an adaptive regression loss (EMA-weighted to suppress inconsistent real-data supervision) and a temporal variance regularizer, while retaining DMD2's two-timescale update rule for the online score model. The inference-time frame-interpolation strategy draws on the observation that high-noise denoising steps encode coarse semantics with low inter-frame variance, a property also noted in consistency-model literature but not previously exploited for compute reduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Few-step video distillation is a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment, yet prior work has largely ported image distillation recipes without addressing video-specific pathologies; this paper provides a principled, ablated fix that practitioners can adopt directly on top of existing DMD pipelines. The dual-purpose adaptive regression loss (simultaneously enabling distillation and domain fine-tuning) is a practical advantage over sequential fine-tune-then-distill workflows. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-autoregressive or longer-horizon video models, whether the temporal regularizer's truncation threshold transfers across architectures, and whether the frame-interpolation trick remains effective at higher resolutions or frame rates where inter-frame variance in high-noise steps may be less negligible.
