## Problem

Cross-Domain Few-Shot Segmentation requires adapting a source-trained model to data-scarce target domains using only a few support images, but two critical obstacles limit performance: the extremely limited quantity and diversity of accessible target samples, and the substantial domain gap that leaves the source-trained model with weak few-shot capability in the target domain. This weakness prevents effective utilization of augmented views during early adaptation, as heavily perturbed views are too challenging for a model that has not yet established target-domain competence.

## Input / Output

During adaptation, the method receives a single support image with its ground-truth mask (1-shot) or K such pairs (K-shot), all resized to 400×400. From this single support, N augmented query views are generated via progressive data augmentation operations (flips, rotations, brightness/hue variation, grid shuffle). The output is a per-pixel binary segmentation mask for each query view, predicted via cosine similarity between query features and a support prototype derived through masked average pooling and the Self-Support Prototype mechanism.

## Method

MPA introduces two complementary designs. Hybrid Progressive Augmentation (HPA) progressively increases task difficulty by (i) cumulatively stacking stronger augmentation operations across views (e.g., view 1 uses only flipping, view 6 combines all prior operations plus grid shuffle) and (ii) adaptively increasing the number of augmented views N when performance stagnates for three consecutive epochs. Dual-chain Multi-view Prediction (DMP) exploits these views through two parallel learning paths: a sequential chain where predictions propagate from support through q1→q2→…→qN (errors accumulate and are regularized by dense supervision at each step), and a parallel chain where each augmented view is independently segmented from the support prototype, directly mimicking inference-time support-to-query prediction. Both chains incorporate bi-directional (reverse) predictions and the Self-Support Prototype for enhanced accuracy. The total loss combines BCE terms for support base, sequential (from index 2 onward), parallel support, and parallel query predictions with fixed weights, and the framework can operate entirely without source-domain meta-training, reducing total training time by ~80% while matching or exceeding two-stage baselines.

## Results

On the four primary CD-FSS benchmarks (Deepglobe, ISIC, Chest X-Ray, FSS-1000), MPA without source training achieves 73.4% average mIoU (1-shot) and 75.5% (5-shot), surpassing the strongest baseline IFA by 7.0% and 5.5% respectively. On SUIM (underwater imagery), MPA reaches 54.2% (1-shot) and 61.1% (5-shot), setting a new state-of-the-art. Notably, MPA without any source-domain training still outperforms IFA (which requires source training) by 5.6% (1-shot) and 4.1% (5-shot), while reducing total training time by over 80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPA outperforms all 21 compared baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The closest competitor is IFA [40], which MPA surpasses by 7.0% (1-shot) and 5.5% (5-shot) in average mIoU; the gap is largest on Chest X-Ray (+15.0% 1-shot) and ISIC (+4.8% 1-shot). Among source-free methods, ABCDFSS [20] is the strongest, yet MPA (w/o source) still leads by 12.7% (1-shot) and 10.5% (5-shot) on average. MPA also beats SAM-based methods (APSeg, TAVP, PerSAM) by 8.1% average mIoU while using a much smaller model. On SUIM, MPA improves over the previous best (ABCDFSS at 35.1% 1-shot) by 19.1 points. Even without source-domain training, MPA matches or exceeds methods that rely on it (e.g., DR-Adapter, SSP), confirming that the progressive adaptation strategy is the primary driver of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CD-FSS paradigm established by PATNet (two-stage meta-training + adaptation) and builds directly on IFA's iterative support-query correspondence mining and SSP's self-support prototype mechanism as core prediction components. Its key departure is reframing the adaptation stage as a progressive curriculum problem: rather than treating target-domain fine-tuning as a single uniform optimization, MPA introduces Hybrid Progressive Augmentation (cumulatively stronger augmentations and increasing view count) and a Dual-chain Multi-view Prediction strategy that jointly exploits sequential error accumulation and parallel diverse predictions. This positions the work at the intersection of curriculum learning and few-shot segmentation adaptation, departing from prior CD-FSS methods that apply fixed augmentation or single-view fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that source-domain meta-training can be entirely removed (saving ~80% training time) while maintaining near-comparable performance challenges the foundational two-stage assumption in CD-FSS and shifts emphasis toward designing better adaptation strategies with limited target data. The +7.0% mIoU gain over the prior SOTA (IFA) across five diverse domains (satellite, medical, chest X-ray, natural objects, underwater) demonstrates broad robustness. Open questions include whether the progressive multi-view strategy transfers to other cross-domain tasks (detection, classification), whether it interacts favorably with foundation-model-based approaches (SAM), and whether the adaptive criterion for increasing complexity (3-epoch stagnation) is optimal or dataset-dependent.
