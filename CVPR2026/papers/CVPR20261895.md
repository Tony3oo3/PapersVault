## Problem

Existing incremental unified anomaly detection methods overlook how spurious inter-object features and redundant information in fused multimodal features exacerbate catastrophic forgetting during sequential learning. This paper demonstrates empirically that the multimodal setting is particularly vulnerable—cross-modal fusion amplifies the capture of spurious and redundant features, causing more severe performance degradation and forgetting than unimodal baselines under identical incremental protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261895/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB and depth images of industrial objects (10 categories on MVTec 3D-AD and Eyecandies), which are first encoded by EfficientNet backbones and then perturbed via feature jitters to synthesize abnormal inputs for the reconstruction network. The model outputs per-pixel anomaly localization maps and scalar anomaly scores (reported as I-AUROC, P-AUROC, and AUPRO) for each object, evaluated across all previously seen objects at every incremental step to also compute a forgetting metric.

## Method

IB-IUMAD introduces two complementary denoising components into a unified multimodal reconstruction pipeline. The Mamba decoder (stacked ESSM blocks with ES2D scanning, deep separable convolution, and attention) is integrated into the multimodal reconstruction network alongside a label classifier, using object identity to disentangle inter-object feature coupling and suppress spurious cross-object interference during reconstruction. The Information Bottleneck Fusion Module (IBFM) fuses multi-scale RGB and depth features via cascade concatenation and cross-attention, then applies a bottleneck projection (linear–dropout–ReLU–linear) whose output is regularized by a KL-divergence loss between P(Y|F_fu) and P(Y|F_fu^g), provably minimizing redundant mutual information I(F_fu; F_fu^g | Y) while preserving task-relevant information I(F_fu^g; Y). The overall training objective combines MSE reconstruction, cross-entropy classification on both modalities, and the IB KL loss, all weighted equally.

## Results

On MVTec 3D-AD under the most challenging incremental setting (6-1 with 4 steps, RGB+3D), IB-IUMAD achieves 78.6 I-AUROC and 82.4 AUPRO, surpassing IUF (75.1/79.5) by 3.5%/2.9% and CDAD (69.5/75.7) by 9.1%/6.7%, while reducing the forgetting metric (FM) from 15.1/8.4 (IUF) to 9.3/6.9. On Eyecandies under the same setting, IB-IUMAD reaches 69.4/75.1 I-AUROC/AUPRO, outperforming IUF (65.8/71.5) and CDAD (68.2/73.8). In terms of efficiency, IB-IUMAD achieves a 21.4 FPS frame rate and 1483.7 MB memory usage, representing a 41× speedup and 44× memory reduction over M3DM while maintaining comparable I-AUROC (91.0 vs. 94.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IB-IUMAD consistently outperforms both incremental unified baselines (IUF and CDAD) across all four incremental settings and both datasets in the multimodal (RGB+3D) setting, with the largest gains appearing under the most challenging 6-1/4-step protocol where forgetting is most severe. Against unified MAD baselines evaluated in the 10-0/0-step setting on MVTec 3D-AD (RGB), IB-IUMAD achieves 88.9 I-AUROC, beating MambaAD (86.2), DiAD (84.6), DeSTSeg (79.6), UniAD (78.9), and SimpleNet (72.5). In the multimodal unified setting, IB-IUMAD improves I-AUROC by 4.3% over DiAD (91.0 vs. 86.7) and by 2.3% over IUF (91.0 vs. 88.7) on MVTec 3D-AD. For computing efficiency, IB-IUMAD achieves 21.4 FPS and 1483.7 MB memory, compared to M3DM's 0.514 FPS and 65261.2 MB, while maintaining a competitive I-AUROC of 91.0 vs. 94.5. The only case where IB-IUMAD does not lead is the 3D-only modality on Eyecandies under setting 3, where CDAD achieves a slightly lower FM (−0.4 vs. 1.4), though IB-IUMAD still wins on I-AUROC (52.9 vs. 53.1 is a near-tie, with IB-IUMAD actually trailing by 0.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incremental unified anomaly detection line (IUF, CDAD) from unimodal RGB to the multimodal RGB+depth setting, and is the first to frame the combined problem of incremental + unified + multimodal anomaly detection (IUMAD). Methodologically, it departs from prior incremental AD work by explicitly identifying spurious and redundant features as amplifiers of catastrophic forgetting, then addressing them through two complementary mechanisms: a Mamba-based decoder (borrowing state-space models from the MambaAD lineage) for disentangling inter-object feature coupling, and an information bottleneck fusion module (drawing on multimodal IB literature) for filtering redundant fused features. The foundational architecture follows IUF's reconstruction-based unified pipeline, with EfficientNet backbones and cross-attention fusion, but the denoising modules represent the core departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single model replacing 10 per-object models yields 44× memory reduction and 41× faster inference while maintaining competitive accuracy, which is directly relevant to industrial deployment where new product categories appear continuously. The empirical demonstration that cross-modal fusion specifically amplifies catastrophic forgetting (causing near-collapse under spurious feature injection, unlike the unimodal case) provides a useful diagnostic for the field. Open questions include whether the information bottleneck regularization generalizes beyond RGB+depth to other modality combinations (thermal, point clouds, CT), whether the approach scales beyond 10 objects, and whether the Mamba decoder's benefit over simpler attention-based decoders holds at larger scale. The evaluation is limited to two 10-object datasets, leaving robustness to more diverse industrial scenarios untested.
