## Problem

Existing Person De-ReID methods learn forgetting and retaining objectives within a single unified feature space, causing conflicting optimization goals that degrade recognition performance on retained or novel identities. Additionally, when a novel query appears at test time, it remains unsolved how to determine whether it belongs to a forgotten or retained identity (the 'test-time routing problem'), since a trained classifier suffers from overfitting on limited forgotten identities and a training-testing gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB person image resized to 256×128 as query input. At test time, the frozen pretrained ViT-B backbone first extracts the original feature, which is then compared via cosine similarity against precomputed per-identity prototypes of forgotten persons to produce a binary routing decision (forget vs. retain). The final output is a feature embedding drawn from either the forgetting subspace (identity-suppressed) or the retaining subspace (discriminative), used for gallery retrieval.

## Method

SANER decouples the pretrained ViT-B feature space into two task-specific subspaces using a Switchable Adapter (SA) composed of two independent LoRA modules: an F-Adapter that maps inputs to a forgetting space suppressing identity-specific semantics, and an R-Adapter that maps inputs to a retaining space preserving discriminative cues. During training, forgetting and retaining samples are routed to their respective adapters and optimized with separate losses (L_f and L_r), avoiding the contradictory gradient signals of a unified space. At test time, a Non-parametric Enhanced Routing (NER) algorithm compares the query's pretrained feature against precomputed prototypes of all forgotten identities using cosine similarity; if the maximum similarity exceeds a threshold τ (set to the maximum inter-prototype similarity), the query is routed to the F-Adapter, otherwise to the R-Adapter. This routing is entirely non-parametric and operates in the frozen pretrained feature space, eliminating the training-testing gap that plagues classifier-based detectors, especially when the number of forgotten identities is small.

## Results

On Market-1501, SANER achieves H-Mean of 88.3% (M_T=25) and 91.1% (M_T=50), with R-1_T as low as 7.3% and 6.7% while maintaining R-1_O of 95.7% and 95.3% for accessible persons. On MSMT17, SANER sustains H-Mean above 78% across all forgetting scales (M_T=25 to 100), with R-1_T consistently below 5.5% and R-1_O above 82%. These results represent new state-of-the-art De-ReID performance, outperforming the previous best method VIS by 3.9–7.9 H-Mean points on Market-1501 and 1.7–11.9 points on MSMT17.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SANER outperforms all six baselines on both datasets across every M_T setting. Against the strongest De-ReID-specific baseline VIS [27], SANER improves H-Mean by 3.9 points (88.3 vs 84.4) at M_T=25 and 7.9 points (91.1 vs 83.2) at M_T=50 on Market-1501, while also reducing R-1_T from 10.7% to 7.3% and boosting R-1_O from 91.1% to 95.7%. On MSMT17, the gap widens as M_T grows: at M_T=100, SANER achieves H-Mean 78.6% versus VIS's 66.7%, a 11.9-point margin, indicating that the decoupled adapter design scales better with more forgotten identities. Among general unlearning methods, LIRF [37] is the closest competitor on forgetting quality (R-1_T=25.3% vs SANER's 7.3% at M_T=25 on Market-1501) but sacrifices retention (R-1_O=84.8% vs 95.7%), yielding H-Mean 72.9% vs 88.3%. GS-LoRA [40] and NoMUS [5] achieve high R-1_O (~91.7%) but fail to forget effectively (R-1_T≈56%), resulting in H-Mean below 50%. SCRUB [16] and BS [4] perform worst overall, with H-Mean in the 44–51% range, confirming that classification-oriented unlearning strategies transfer poorly to the open-set retrieval setting of De-ReID.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the De-ReID task formalized by Peng et al. (VIS, CVPR 2025), adopting its problem setup, loss functions, and evaluation protocol, but departs fundamentally from the unified-feature-space paradigm by decoupling forgetting and retaining into two independent LoRA-based adapter branches. The LoRA mechanism (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) is repurposed from single-task parameter-efficient fine-tuning to a dual-adapter architecture where each adapter learns a task-specific residual mapping. The Non-parametric Enhanced Routing (NER) algorithm contrasts with both prior De-ReID methods (which have no explicit routing) and with parametric classifier-based detectors, operating entirely in the frozen pretrained feature space via prototype comparison.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupled feature space design addresses a core tension in De-ReID—conflicting optimization objectives that degrade retained-identity representations—offering a cleaner architectural solution than the variation-guided identity shift of VIS. The non-parametric routing avoids the training-testing gap that plagues learned classifiers, making the approach more robust when the number of forgotten identities is small. However, the evaluation is limited to two datasets and a single ViT-B backbone, and the method's reliance on a strong pretrained model and a training-time-computed threshold τ raises open questions about cross-domain transfer, scalability to very large forgetting sets, and deployment in streaming surveillance settings where prototypes must be updated incrementally.
