## Problem

Existing 6D object pose estimation methods and benchmarks assume objects are perfectly rigid and match idealized canonical meshes, yet real-world nominally rigid items (boxes, bottles, cans) frequently deform through wear, impact, or handling. No prior dataset captures multiple deformation states of the same instance with accurate 3D registration to the canonical form and corresponding pose annotations, leaving the impact of deformation on pose estimation unquantified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 133K RGB-D frames at 1920×1080 resolution, each containing five object instances (mix of canonical and deformed) with inter-object occlusions, alongside 26 canonical 3D meshes and their three deformed counterparts per category. The annotation pipeline consumes RGB-D frames, SAM2-generated binary instance masks, and the canonical mesh, producing 665K 6D pose annotations (rotation + translation) per instance per frame. Deformed meshes are registered to canonical form via a flow-driven alignment, yielding precise 3D-3D correspondences across deformation states.

## Method

The dataset creation pipeline has three stages: (1) high-precision 3D scanning of 104 objects (26 categories × 4 deformation levels) with a Go!SCAN SPARK scanner; (2) flow-driven 3D registration where SCFlow2's pre-trained optical flow network computes dense 2D correspondences between multi-view renderings of canonical and deformed meshes, which are lifted to 3D and refined via RANSAC + Umeyama similarity transformation; (3) semi-automatic pose annotation that chains SAM2 segmentation, FoundationPose initial pose estimation with consistency voting, and a Co-SLAM-based global bundle adjustment that jointly optimizes camera poses and a neural implicit scene representation (color + TSDF) using mask-constrained ray sampling and an instance mask alignment loss. The evaluation benchmarks three SOTA methods (SCFlow2, FoundationPose, GenPose) under BOP metrics, revealing that Average Recall drops from ~0.8 (canonical) to ~0.2–0.3 (severe deformation) in static scenes, with further degradation in dynamic scenes due to hand occlusion and motion blur.

## Results

On the DeSOPE dataset, all three benchmarked pose estimation methods show sharp performance degradation as deformation severity increases. SCFlow2 achieves the highest Average Recall (AR) on canonical objects (0.82 static, 0.77 dynamic) but drops to 0.23 (static) and 0.20 (dynamic) at severe deformation (Deformed 3), representing a 72% relative drop. GenPose, a category-level diffusion-based method, shows relative robustness at severe deformation in static scenes (AR 0.31 vs. 0.23 for SCFlow2 and 0.24 for FoundationPose), while dynamic scenes consistently underperform static scenes by 5–10 AR points across all methods due to hand occlusions and motion blur.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three representative pose estimation methods on DeSOPE: SCFlow2 (unseen-object, flow-based), FoundationPose (unseen-object, 2D-3D matching), and GenPose (category-level, diffusion-based). SCFlow2 leads on canonical and mild deformation in both static and dynamic scenes (e.g., 0.82 vs. 0.78 vs. 0.67 AR on canonical static), but its advantage erodes at severe deformation where GenPose overtakes it (0.31 vs. 0.23 static, 0.28 vs. 0.20 dynamic). FoundationPose consistently ranks in the middle, never leading on any deformation level. The key finding is that no method maintains more than 30% AR at severe deformation, and the gap between canonical and Deformed 3 performance reaches 60–75% relative degradation for all three methods, confirming that rigid-object assumptions are a fundamental limitation. GenPose's category-level generalization provides a modest buffer against deformation (smaller static-to-dynamic gap), while geometry-matching methods like SCFlow2 and FoundationPose suffer more from the mesh-image mismatch.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of instance-level 6D pose datasets (LINEMOD, YCB-V, T-LESS), which assume perfect rigidity, and category-level datasets (REAL275, HouseCat6D), which collapse all instances into a single canonical mesh. It departs from both by capturing multiple deformation states of the *same* instance with precise 3D registration between states, a formulation absent from prior benchmarks. The annotation pipeline repurposes Co-SLAM's neural implicit representation and bundle adjustment framework, modified with mask-constrained ray sampling and FoundationPose initialization, while the mesh alignment step leverages SCFlow2's pre-trained optical flow for flow-driven registration between canonical and deformed meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DeSOPE is the first dataset to explicitly benchmark 6D pose estimation under realistic deformation of nominally rigid objects, revealing that all evaluated SOTA methods suffer severe degradation (AR dropping from ~0.8 to ~0.2–0.3) as deformation increases—a gap that has been invisible in prior rigid-object benchmarks. This motivates future work on deformation-aware representations, temporal modeling, and robust pose estimators that do not assume input images match a fixed canonical mesh. Limitations include the relatively small scale (26 categories, 104 instances, 5 scenes), manually induced rather than naturally accumulated deformation, and the absence of a proposed method that actually solves the deformation problem, leaving the benchmark without a strong baseline to beat.
