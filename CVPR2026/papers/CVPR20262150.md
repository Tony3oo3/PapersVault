## Problem

Most Robotic Foundation Models are built by fine-tuning internet-pretrained 2D Vision-Language Models that lack the 3D spatial reasoning inherently required for embodied control, forcing them to learn geometric structure implicitly from large-scale, embodiment-specific robot demonstrations. This dependence on costly robotic data limits scalability and zero-shot generalization to unseen environments. The paper asks whether 3D understanding can be injected into the VLM backbone using cheap, non-robotic 2D images enriched with 3D annotations, thereby reducing the need for robot demonstrations by an order of magnitude.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two RGB camera views (external at 280×210, wrist at 112×112, resized by central crop or padding to preserve intrinsics), the robot's end-effector pose and gripper state, and a natural-language instruction. It outputs a sequence of H=5 actions at 5 Hz, each decomposed into a 3D translation vector, a unit quaternion rotation, and a gripper command, generated by integrating a learned flow-matching vector field from τ=0 to τ=1. When the wrist camera is unavailable, a black image is fed in its place.

## Method

SPEAR-1 uses a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 builds SPEAR-VLM by augmenting PaliGemma (SigLIP encoder + Gemma LLM) with a MoGe monocular depth encoder whose last-4-layer ViT features are projected and averaged with SigLIP features, plus 1024 new 3D tokens in the tokenizer; it is trained on 3D VQA tasks (oriented 3D bounding box prediction, object-to-object distance estimation) over 200k non-robotic 2D images annotated via a Gemini→SAM2→MoGe pipeline. Stage 2 appends a flow-matching action expert that attends to the VLM's key-value pairs and predicts action sequences, trained on ~45M frames from 24 Open X-Embodiment datasets. A key design choice is performing flow matching on the S³ manifold of unit quaternions (using slerp interpolation and a combined cosine + geodesic loss) for rotations while using linear-space flow matching for translations, which proves more stable than linear R⁴→S³ formulations. During VLA training the MoGe encoder is frozen to preserve its dense depth representations while SigLIP remains trainable, a configuration ablated to be optimal over alternatives.

## Results

On the SIMPLER WidowX simulation benchmark, SPEAR-1 achieves 57.3% average success rate, outperforming SpatialVLA (42.7%) and OpenVLA (1.0%) by more than 10 and 56 percentage points respectively. On the challenging Franka (DROID) zero-shot evaluation in unseen environments, SPEAR-1 reaches ~5× higher performance than π₀-FAST and matches π₀.5, while being trained on only ~45M frames versus over 900M frames used by both baselines (a 20× reduction in robot demonstrations). On real-world WidowX tasks, SPEAR-1 achieves 10% higher average task progress than OpenVLA. The 3D VLM pretraining ablation shows SPEAR-VLM improves average success rate from 20.8% (PaliGemma baseline) to 35.4% on SIMPLER, and from 34% to 46% average task progress on Franka (DROID).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPEAR-1 decisively outperforms OpenVLA on both SIMPLER simulation (57.3% vs 1.0% avg success rate) and real-world WidowX tasks (10% higher average task progress), confirming that 3D-aware VLM pretraining provides substantial gains over a standard 2D VLM backbone. Against SpatialVLA, which also integrates a monocular depth encoder but without VLM alignment or 3D pretraining, SPEAR-1 achieves 57.3% vs 42.7% on SIMPLER, demonstrating that explicit 3D VQA pretraining on non-robotic data is more effective than learning 3D cues solely from robot demonstrations. On the Franka (DROID) zero-shot benchmark, SPEAR-1 reaches approximately 5× the performance of π₀-FAST without any environment-specific fine-tuning, despite using 20× fewer robot demonstration frames. SPEAR-1 matches π₀.5 on the same Franka zero-shot evaluation, suggesting that 3D VLM pretraining on diverse non-robotic 2D images is a more scalable path to environment generalization than the large-scale diverse robotic data mixture used by π₀.5. Notably, the 'Carrot on Plate' task is absent from the DROID training set, yet π₀-SPEAR-VLM (DROID) completes it in some trials while π₀-PaliGemma (DROID) fails every time, highlighting improved zero-shot generalization from 3D pretraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPEAR-1 builds directly on the π₀ architecture (PaliGemma VLM + flow-matching action expert) but replaces the standard 2D VLM backbone with SPEAR-VLM, a 3D-aware variant that integrates the MoGe monocular depth encoder and is pretrained on object-level 3D VQA tasks (bounding boxes, inter-object distances) derived from non-robotic 2D images. This departs from prior spatial VLA work (SpatialVLA, MolmoAct) that either adds depth encoders without VLM alignment or performs spatial reasoning at inference time, and from Gemini Robotics 1.0's similar 3D pretraining strategy, by demonstrating the approach at open-source scale and explicitly quantifying the robot-data reduction it enables. The flow-matching formulation is extended from linear space to the S³ manifold of unit quaternions for rotation, a design choice ablated against Euler angles and rotation matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that 200k non-robotic 3D-annotated images can substitute for hundreds of millions of robot demonstration frames, dramatically reducing the cost and logistical barrier to training generalist VLA policies. This reframes the data bottleneck in robotics from 'collect more robot demos' to 'annotate cheap 2D images with 3D geometry,' a shift with broad implications for how the field scales. Open questions remain: whether the affine-invariant (non-metric) depth from MoGe limits downstream precision, how the approach handles deformable or complex-shaped objects, whether embodiment-specific fine-tuning can be eliminated, and what the scaling laws are between 3D pretraining data volume/quality and VLA performance.
