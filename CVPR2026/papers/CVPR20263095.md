## Problem

Existing multi-view inverse rendering methods face a fundamental speed-accuracy tradeoff: optimization-based approaches achieve high fidelity but require minutes to hours per scene, while feed-forward single-view methods are fast but produce inconsistent material predictions across viewpoints. Additionally, models trained on synthetic data suffer from temporal flickering and poor generalization when applied to real-world videos due to the scarcity of annotated real-world material data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263095/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N RGB images (H×W×3) as input and, in a single forward pass, produces per-view albedo (H×W×3), metallic (H×W), roughness (H×W), camera-space surface normal (H×W×3), and diffuse shading (H×W×3) maps at the input resolution. The final diffuse image is computed as the element-wise product of albedo and diffuse shading. Unlike optimization-based methods that iterate per scene, all views are processed simultaneously in one feed-forward inference taking only seconds.

## Method

MVInverse uses DINOv2 to encode each frame into patch tokens, then processes them through a VGGT-style transformer backbone that alternates between frame-wise self-attention (capturing intra-view long-range lighting interactions) and global self-attention (associating corresponding 3D regions across views without explicit pose supervision). Five DPT-style prediction heads decode the multi-view features into intrinsic maps, augmented with an auxiliary multi-resolution ResNeXt encoder whose skip connections preserve high-frequency spatial details that pure DPT heads tend to blur. Training follows a two-stage strategy: supervised pretraining on seven synthetic datasets (Hypersim, Interiorverse, Structured3D, etc.) with MSE, multi-scale gradient, and cosine-similarity losses, followed by self-supervised consistency finetuning on unlabeled real videos where optical-flow-warped adjacent-frame predictions are penalized for disagreement, with an anchor loss to a frozen pretrained model preventing solution collapse.

## Results

On Interiorverse single-view inverse rendering, MVInverse achieves PSNR 23.0, SSIM 0.92, and LPIPS 0.09 for albedo, with metallic RMSE 0.14 and roughness RMSE 0.17, outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. For multi-view material consistency on Hypersim, it achieves the lowest RMSE across all channels (albedo 0.0660, metallic 0.0634, roughness 0.0259). On zero-shot surface normal estimation, it attains the best mean angular error on 4 of 5 benchmarks (e.g., 14.2° on ScanNet, 31.3° on Sintel) and the highest 30° accuracy on 4 of 5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVInverse outperforms all five single-view baselines on Interiorverse across every reported metric; the closest competitor, DiffusionRenderer [60], trails by 1.1 dB in albedo PSNR (21.9 vs 23.0) and by 0.14 in metallic RMSE (0.28 vs 0.14). For multi-view consistency on Hypersim, MVInverse reduces albedo RMSE from 0.0816 (ColorfulDiffuse [16]) and 0.0878 (IntrinsicImageDiffusion [48]) to 0.0660, and cuts roughness RMSE from 0.1055 (DiffusionRenderer) to 0.0259, indicating substantially tighter cross-view agreement. On zero-shot normal estimation, MVInverse achieves the lowest mean angular error on NYUv2 (16.1°), ScanNet (14.2°), iBims-1 (16.0°), and Sintel (31.3°), narrowly beating Lotus-D [39] and Diffusion-E2E-FT [35]; it is slightly behind Lotus-D on OASIS (23.1° vs 22.3°) but still within the top three. The older baselines IIW [7] and RGB↔X [111] are substantially outperformed, with IIW lagging by over 13 dB in albedo PSNR. Overall, the feed-forward approach matches or exceeds diffusion-based methods (RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer, IntrinsicImageDiffusion) while running in seconds rather than requiring iterative sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MVInverse transplants the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm—specifically the VGGT-style alternating global and frame-wise attention backbone—into multi-view inverse rendering, a task that has historically relied on per-scene differentiable optimization (NeRF/NeuS-based, 3DGS-based) or iterative diffusion sampling. It departs from single-view intrinsic decomposition methods by explicitly modeling cross-view material consistency through global attention, and from prior multi-view learning-based approaches like MAIR/MAIR++ by handling large camera motions in a single forward pass rather than constrained viewpoints. The auxiliary multi-resolution ResNeXt encoder fused into DPT-style heads addresses a gap specific to inverse rendering: preserving high-frequency albedo texture that pure transformer decoders tend to smooth away.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing multi-view inverse rendering from minutes-to-hours of optimization to a few seconds of inference, this work makes physically-based material recovery practical for real-time applications like video relighting and view-consistent material editing. The consistency finetuning strategy—using optical-flow-warped temporal losses with an anchor to prevent collapse—offers a general recipe for closing the synthetic-to-real gap in dense prediction tasks lacking ground truth. Key limitations remain: training data is predominantly indoor and object-centric, material annotation diversity is limited, and the method has not been validated on highly reflective or subsurface-scattering materials. Open questions include whether the feed-forward paradigm can extend to outdoor scenes with complex global illumination and whether the approach scales to very long video sequences.
