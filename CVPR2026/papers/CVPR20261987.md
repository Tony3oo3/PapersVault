## Problem

Video LLMs suffer from high computational cost due to dense frame processing, and existing keyframe selection methods rely on greedy, decoupled evaluation of query relevance and content diversity. This combination leads to local optima, error propagation from early suboptimal choices, and the erroneous inclusion of irrelevant noise frames that distract model attention from critical information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261987/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

GIFT takes 128 uniformly sampled candidate frames from an input video and a text query as input. Visual and text embeddings are extracted using a pretrained SigLIP model to compute frame-query similarity and inter-frame distances. The output is a set of K frame indices (K ∈ {4, 8, 16, 32} in experiments) that are fed to a downstream VLM for video question answering, with a fixed batch size B=9 governing the iterative refinement granularity.

## Method

GIFT reformulates keyframe selection as maximizing a unified irreplaceability score rather than balancing decoupled relevance and diversity objectives. The key mechanism is Directed Diversity: for each frame, it measures the minimum L2 distance only to frames with strictly higher query relevance (its 'potential substitutes'), so a frame is penalized for redundancy only when a more relevant and visually similar alternative exists. The final irreplaceability score is the product of normalized relevance and directed diversity (s_i = r_i × d_i). A Budget-Aware Refinement strategy then iteratively selects batches of B highest-scoring frames, removes them from the candidate pool, and recomputes directed diversity for all remaining frames, progressively releasing the suppressive effect on temporally adjacent neighbors to build coherent event context as the budget expands.

## Results

On LLaVA-Video-7B, GIFT achieves an average score of 62.9 at 32 frames, 62.0 at 16 frames, 60.8 at 8 frames, and 58.7 at 4 frames across MVBench, LongVideoBench, MLVU, and VideoMME, consistently outperforming all baselines at every budget. The maximum average improvement over uniform sampling reaches 12.5% on long-form video benchmarks. When applied as a plug-and-play module to four diverse VLMs (VILA-V1.5, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, VideoLLaMA3), GIFT yields gains of 3.0–11.6 points on MLVU and 1.8–6.3 points on LongVideoBench at 8 frames, with the largest single gain being +19.6% on VideoLLaMA3's MLVU score (59.1→70.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFT outperforms both BOLT and AKS on the average score at every frame budget tested (32, 16, 8, 4) on LLaVA-Video-7B. At 32 frames, GIFT scores 62.9 vs. BOLT's 62.5 and AKS's 61.8; at the tightest 4-frame budget, the gap widens to 58.7 vs. 56.0 (BOLT) and 57.3 (AKS), reflecting GIFT's superior robustness under severe budget constraints. On individual benchmarks, GIFT leads on LongVideoBench and MLVU at all budgets, while BOLT occasionally matches or slightly edges GIFT on MVBench (e.g., 59.7 vs. 59.1 at 32 frames). Compared to uniform sampling, GIFT's advantage grows as the budget shrinks: +2.6 points at 32 frames but +5.2 points at 4 frames (58.7 vs. 53.5), a relative improvement of ~9.7%. The cross-model experiments (Table 2) confirm that GIFT's gains are model-agnostic, with consistent improvements of 1.8–11.6 points across VILA-V1.5, LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen2.5-VL, and VideoLLaMA3, indicating the method's effectiveness is not tied to a specific VLM architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant greedy, decoupled paradigm in keyframe selection (BOLT, AKS, MDP3, MaxInfo) that treats query relevance and content diversity as separate objectives balanced by hand-tuned hyperparameters. Rather than incrementally selecting the next best frame, GIFT reframes the problem as a global assessment of each frame's irreplaceability, introducing Directed Diversity that measures dissimilarity only against frames with higher relevance. The Budget-Aware Refinement stage then adds an iterative select-remove-re-evaluate loop that dynamically releases temporal context as the budget grows, addressing a limitation shared by all prior static-scoring methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, model-agnostic module, GIFT offers a low-friction path to improving any existing video LLM without retraining, which is practically significant given the rapid proliferation of new VLM architectures. The consistent gains across four diverse models and four frame budgets strengthen confidence in the approach's generalizability. Open questions include whether the reliance on SigLIP embeddings limits performance in domains where visual similarity is poorly captured by a single encoder, how the method scales beyond the 128-candidate-frame assumption for hour-scale videos, and whether the batch-size heuristic (B=9) transfers across different video lengths and task types.
