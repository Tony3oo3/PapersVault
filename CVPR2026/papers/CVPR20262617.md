## Problem

SAM generalizes poorly to remote sensing imagery due to severe domain shift, and existing point-supervised adaptation methods (e.g., PointSAM) rely on large prototype banks for feature alignment that are memory-intensive and unstable on large or heterogeneous datasets. Additionally, SAM's independent per-mask predictions produce overlapping or fragmented masks in densely packed RSI scenes, a problem that prior self-training frameworks do not explicitly resolve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-resolution remote sensing images (ranging from 0.075 m/px on WHU to multi-scale VHR optical and SAR imagery) together with 1-3 sparse point prompts per instance (both positive and negative points sampled from ground truth). It outputs per-instance binary segmentation masks. A key operational detail is the weak-strong dual-view pipeline: weakly augmented views generate self-prompted pseudo-masks that supervise strongly augmented views during training, and the model internally converts point prompts into bounding-box prompts before the final mask query.

## Method

ReSAM introduces a closed-loop Refine-Requery-Reinforce (R3) strategy. In Refine, initial SAM masks from point prompts are filtered by a Shannon-entropy confidence threshold and overlapping pixels are suppressed so each pixel is assigned to a single instance. In Requery, minimal bounding boxes are extracted from the refined masks and fed back as box prompts to re-query SAM, converting noisy point supervision into structured region queries. In Reinforce, a Soft Semantic Alignment (SSA) loss aligns L2-normalized instance embeddings across weak and strong augmentations using a FIFO queue of size 128 and a soft cosine-similarity objective (no negatives or margins required), inspired by MoCo but far lighter than prototype banks. The entire framework is trained with LoRA (rank 4) on SAM's ViT-B transformer blocks and a composite loss of focal, Dice, IoU, and beta*SSAL terms, reducing peak GPU memory by 84% compared to PointSAM while closing the gap to fully supervised performance to 1.3-8.5 mIoU across three benchmarks.

## Results

On the 1-point SAM-based setting, ReSAM achieves 70.25 mIoU / 79.80 F1 on NWPU VHR-10, 75.86 mIoU / 83.80 F1 on WHU, and 58.40 mIoU / 70.11 F1 on HRSID-Inshore. These results narrow the gap to the fully supervised upper bound to 8.5% (NWPU), 1.3% (WHU), and 4.9% (HRSID) in mIoU, respectively. The ablation (Table 4) shows the full R³ pipeline contributes +14.8 mIoU over direct SAM on WHU, with Requery adding +4.5 over Refine-only and SSA adding +4.7 over Requery-only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReSAM outperforms the closest point-supervised competitor, PointSAM [16], by +3.59 mIoU on NWPU (70.25 vs 66.66), +3.23 mIoU on WHU (75.86 vs 72.63), and +2.34 mIoU on HRSID 1-point (58.40 vs 56.06). Against source-free domain adaptation baselines (DePT, Tribe, WeSAM), ReSAM leads by 5–10 mIoU on NWPU and 4–10 mIoU on WHU, with DePT being the strongest non-point-supervised method on WHU (71.31 mIoU) yet still trailing ReSAM by 4.55 points. On HRSID-Inshore, ReSAM wins on 1-point and 3-point settings but underperforms PointSAM on the 2-point setting (54.69 vs 57.79 mIoU), which the authors attribute to cluttered backgrounds and small objects. The fully supervised LoRA upper bound remains 1.3–8.5 mIoU above ReSAM depending on dataset, confirming that the self-prompting loop recovers most of the performance lost by removing dense mask annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReSAM extends the point-supervised SAM adaptation line initiated by PointSAM, PENet, and SAPNet, which demonstrated that sparse point prompts can drive pseudo-label self-training for remote sensing instance segmentation. Its key conceptual departure is from prototype-based feature alignment (PointSAM's FINCH clustering with fixed-size prototype banks) to a lightweight Soft Semantic Alignment (SSA) strategy using a rolling FIFO queue and cosine-similarity loss, drawing inspiration from MoCo-style contrastive learning. The self-prompting Refine-Requery-Reinforce loop—converting uncertain point-derived masks into box prompts to re-query SAM—repurposes SAM's own promptable architecture as an iterative refinement mechanism rather than relying on external prompt generators or dense supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the annotation burden to a single point per instance while closing the gap to fully supervised LoRA fine-tuning to as little as 1.3% mIoU (WHU), ReSAM offers a practical path toward scalable segmentation of large-scale RSI archives where pixel-wise labeling is prohibitive. The 84% memory reduction over PointSAM makes the approach viable on standard hardware for large datasets. However, performance on HRSID-Inshore is inconsistent (ReSAM underperforms PointSAM in 2-point and 3-point SAM2 settings), and the authors acknowledge that highly dense scenes remain challenging due to residual pseudo-label noise. Open questions include whether the dual weak/strong view architecture can be simplified, whether richer prompt strategies (e.g., multi-point or trajectory prompts) further close the supervised gap, and whether SSA generalizes beyond remote sensing to other domain-shift settings.
