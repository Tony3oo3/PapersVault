## Problem

Anchor-based ensemble clustering methods produce biased anchors when base clustering results are contaminated with outliers, because minimizing reconstruction loss on distorted partitions pulls anchor directions toward outlier-influenced samples. Existing approaches either fix anchors via k-means or update them through reconstruction, but neither explicitly separates clean consensus structure from outlier contamination in the anchor space, and none simultaneously supports downstream outlier detection from the learned representation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes m base clustering results (decision-level partitions, no raw features required) as an s×n binary bipartite graph indicator matrix, along with two hyperparameters: the number of anchors k (set as c+k′ where c is the true cluster count and k′∈{−1,…,3}) and a similarity threshold τ∈[0.9,1]. It outputs a consensus partition obtained by running k-means on the clean anchor-sample similarity matrix Z_c, and a residual outlier structure Z_n (k×n, non-negative) that encodes per-sample abnormality at the decision level and can be directly fed to standard detectors (IForest, LOF) for outlier scoring.

## Method

RANGE first enhances the initial bipartite graph via a High-order Fuzzy Enhancement Strategy (HFES): it computes Jaccard similarity between base clusters, performs a random walk to capture high-order cluster relationships, partitions base clusters into granules via connected components, and fuses the resulting fuzzy sample–cluster memberships back into the incidence matrix. For robust reconstruction, RANGE decomposes the anchor similarity matrix as Z = Z_c + Z_n and imposes two decoupling mechanisms: a global cross-correlation penalty ‖Z_c^T Z_n‖_F² that encourages approximate orthogonality between clean and residual subspaces, and a row-wise ℓ_{2,1}-norm on Z_n that activates only a small number of residual anchor directions, confining outlier effects to a compact noise subspace. The resulting non-convex problem is solved by alternating SVD-based updates for the projection matrix P and anchor matrix A, an Augmented Lagrangian Method for Z_c (enforcing the simplex constraint Z_c^T 1_k = 1_n), and an iterative reweighting scheme for Z_n, yielding O(n) time complexity in the number of samples.

## Results

On the 10 clustering datasets (ranging from 1,440 to 2,000,000 samples), RANGE achieves the best ACC on all 10 datasets, with headline values of 74.6% on SF-2M (2M samples), 74.1% on COIL20, 73.2% on ALOI100, and 58.5% on FashionMNIST. In ARI, RANGE leads on 9 of 10 datasets, with 69.1% on COIL20 and 63.4% on ALOI100. For outlier detection on 14 AD-Bench datasets, RANGE-D with IForest achieves a mean AUC of 74.91%, surpassing the standalone IForest (66.67%), LOF (60.40%), and the decision-level baseline COR (62.19%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RANGE outperforms the strongest dynamic-anchor baseline YACHT on all 10 datasets in ACC, with the largest margins on COIL20 (+5.5 pp), FashionMNIST (+3.4 pp), and MNIST (+0.4 pp), and the smallest on SF-2M (+0.9 pp). Against the fixed-anchor method FSEC, RANGE gains 11.4 pp on SF-2M (74.6 vs 63.2) and 3.4 pp on COIL20 (74.1 vs 70.7), confirming that dynamic anchors with outlier decoupling substantially outperform static k-means-selected anchors. Among SOTA non-anchor methods, RANGE beats GPEC by 4.4 pp on COIL20 and 6.5 pp on COIL100, and surpasses ECCMS by 9.1 pp on COIL20 and 10.7 pp on COIL100. For outlier detection, RANGE-D(IForest) achieves a mean AUC of 74.91% across 14 AD-Bench datasets, exceeding the standalone IForest (66.67%) by 8.2 pp and the only decision-level baseline COR (62.19%) by 12.7 pp, though it underperforms COR on Vertebral (49.02 vs 60.71) and Optdigits (98.51 vs 83.74 in favor of RANGE-D). RANGE is not the fastest method—FSEC and U-SENC are faster due to fixed anchors—but its accuracy advantage (3–11 pp over the next-best scalable method) makes it the most competitive overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

RANGE extends the anchor-based ensemble clustering line initiated by YACHT [39], which introduced dynamic anchor updates via reconstruction, and the reparameterization technique of Feng et al. [7] for efficient matrix factorization. The key departure is the explicit treatment of outlier-contaminated base clusterings: prior anchor-based methods (FSEC, YACHT) assume base results are sufficiently reliable, whereas RANGE introduces a high-order fuzzy enhancement strategy (HFES) to stabilize the bipartite graph and a decoupling mechanism (cross-correlation penalty + row-wise ℓ₂,₁-norm) to separate clean consensus structure from residual outlier structure in the anchor space. This positions RANGE as the first anchor-based ensemble method to simultaneously address both the reliability of base clusterings and the robustness of anchor learning under contamination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RANGE demonstrates that O(n)-scalable ensemble clustering can be made robust to outliers without sacrificing accuracy, reaching 2M samples (SF-2M) while outperforming all compared SOTA methods. The cross-task extension to outlier detection via the residual structure Zₙ is a practical bonus, though the outlier detection results are mixed—RANGE-D wins on average but underperforms IForest on several individual datasets, suggesting the decision-level outlier signal is not always sufficient. An open question is whether the decoupling formulation generalizes beyond k-means-generated base clusterings to heterogeneous ensemble members, and whether the fixed random-walk order (d=20) and threshold τ can be adapted automatically per dataset.
