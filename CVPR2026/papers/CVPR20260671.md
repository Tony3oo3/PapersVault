## Problem

MLP-based 3DGS methods such as Octree-GS and Scaffold-GS decode Gaussian attributes from structured anchors but do not account for occlusion, causing a large fraction of anchors to correspond to heavily occluded regions that never contribute to the final image. Existing LOD and pruning strategies reduce distant redundancy but leave occluded content unaddressed, resulting in wasted decoding overhead and inconsistent anchor–Gaussian binding in spatially complex scenes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images with camera poses (from COLMAP for outdoor scenes or MapAnything for indoor scenes) plus a lightweight proxy mesh derived from a dense point cloud and simplified to coarse geometry. At inference it produces a rendered novel-view image and a 1000×1000 hardware-rasterized depth map that is retained on the GPU to avoid CPU round-trips; the depth map is consumed directly by a fused CUDA kernel that performs both frustum and occlusion culling in a single pass.

## Method

Proxy-GS builds on Octree-GS's MLP decoder and octree LOD structure, adding a proxy-guided filter that leverages GPU hardware rasterization on a simplified proxy mesh to generate an occlusion depth map in under 1 ms. At inference, a single CUDA kernel fuses frustum culling with a per-pixel depth test (converting hardware NDC depth to linear camera-space depth with a safety margin) to discard occluded anchors before MLP decoding. At training, the same proxy depth is used for proxy-guided densification: patches whose rendering error exceeds 3× the frame mean are identified, their center pixels are back-projected through the proxy depth to 3D, and new anchors are placed on the proxy surface subject to a proxy-grid occupancy limit to prevent spatial redundancy. This unified use of the proxy during both training and inference keeps anchor–Gaussian binding consistent, yielding up to 2.5× speedup over Octree-GS on occlusion-rich scenes while simultaneously improving PSNR.

## Results

On the MatrixCity dataset (highly occluded urban streets), Proxy-GS achieves PSNR 22.11 / SSIM 0.751 / LPIPS 0.330 / 126 FPS on Block 1&2, outperforming Octree-GS (21.94 / 0.737 / 0.347 / 32 FPS) by ~0.17 dB PSNR and ~3.9× in speed. Across all three MatrixCity blocks, Proxy-GS consistently leads in both quality and throughput, reaching up to 151 FPS (Block 5) versus 48 FPS for Octree-GS. On real-world scenes with weaker occlusion (Berlin, CUHK-LOWER), Proxy-GS matches or slightly exceeds Octree-GS in quality (e.g., 27.85 vs 27.83 PSNR on Berlin) while adding negligible overhead (275 vs 263 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proxy-GS outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all datasets. Against the strongest MLP-based baseline Octree-GS, Proxy-GS gains 0.11–0.27 dB PSNR and 0.009–0.013 SSIM while delivering a 2.7×–4.5× FPS speedup (e.g., 126 vs 32 FPS on MatrixCity Block 1&2; 139 vs 51 FPS on Small City). Against explicit 3DGS, Proxy-GS improves PSNR by 0.56 dB (22.11 vs 21.55 on Block 1&2) and more than doubles the frame rate (126 vs 115 FPS), while also surpassing Scaffold-GS (21.44 PSNR, 81 FPS) and Hierarchical-GS (20.50 PSNR, 61 FPS) by a wider margin. On low-occlusion scenes (Berlin, CUHK-LOWER), the quality gap over Octree-GS narrows to ≤0.02 dB PSNR and the speed advantage shrinks to ~5%, confirming that the proxy overhead is negligible when occlusion is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

Proxy-GS builds directly on Octree-GS's MLP-based LOD-structured 3D Gaussian framework, adding a lightweight proxy mesh layer that was absent from all prior structured Gaussian methods. It departs from distance-based LOD strategies (Hierarchical-GS, Octree-GS) by introducing per-pixel occlusion culling via hardware rasterization, and from cluster-based occlusion reasoning (OccluGaussian) by operating at pixel granularity. The proxy-guided densification mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from Mvg-Splatting's multi-view depth-guided anchor placement, but repurposes it with pre-computed proxy depth maps rather than multi-view consistency losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for MLP-based 3DGS in VR/AR deployment: the decoding overhead of anchors in occluded regions that contribute nothing to the final image. The 2.5–3× speedup on occlusion-rich urban scenes, combined with a modest quality gain, makes structured Gaussian methods more viable for real-time walkthroughs. However, the approach depends on the availability of a reasonable proxy mesh (from COLMAP or MapAnything), and its gains diminish in scenes with minimal occlusion (aerial, sparse indoor), limiting its universality. An open question is how the method behaves in dynamic or partially observed scenes where a static proxy mesh may become stale.
