## Problem

Current zero-shot 3D object perception methods rely on ensembles of frozen foundation models (SAM, GroundingDINO, DINOv2) for detection via appearance-based similarity matching, which limits deep geometric understanding and cross-domain generalization—especially when test objects diverge from training distributions (e.g., industrial objects vs. web images). Additionally, state-of-the-art pose refinement depends on depth data, introducing errors from occlusions, harsh lighting, and reflective surfaces. 3PT addresses both limitations by unifying detection, segmentation, and 6DoF pose estimation in a single framework trained directly for 3D-object perception without depth input.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more calibrated RGB images and a 3D CAD model of the target object as input. The detection stage (3PT-D) tiles images into overlapping 512×512 patches and conditions on 12 pre-rendered CAD views for object prior embeddings plus 5,140 densely sampled rotation renders for orientation matching; a scale prior constrains target objects to 128–350 px (or S=3 scale passes for unknown depth). The refinement stage (3PT-R) receives cropped image regions around detected objects and K initial pose hypotheses, producing per-view segmentation masks, a single refined 6DoF pose, and a confidence score per hypothesis after 3 iterative render-and-compare steps.

## Method

3PT comprises two large-scale trained transformers. The detection stage (3PT-D) is a two-tower ViT where the Object Encoder processes rendered CAD views to produce prior embeddings (early-fused into the Image Encoder) and 5,140 query template vectors for orientation matching via a single matrix-multiply cosine distance; it is trained contrastively on ~1B image-render pairs with a soft-scaled matching loss that down-weights near-miss hypotheses by IoU. The refinement stage (3PT-R) performs an iterative render-and-compare loop: DINOv2 encodes both real crops and rendered views, cross-attention registers hypotheses to visual evidence, and a DPT decoder predicts dense feature maps, segmentation masks, and confidence scores in one forward pass. Reciprocal edge-based matching yields sparse 2D-3D correspondences fed to multi-view PnP for pose updates, while per-iteration confidence scoring progressively filters the bottom 50% of hypotheses. Both networks are trained on 900K synthetic Blender scenes from 110K+ mesh files, and the key architectural insight is that native 3D-conditioned early-fusion detection at scale outperforms late-fusion similarity matching with frozen foundation models, while multi-view RGB refinement achieves mm-level accuracy without any depth sensor.

## Results

3PT achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot 6DoF pose estimation across all BOP benchmarks. On BOP-Industrial (mm-level pose), 3PT reaches 80.6 AP-mm, surpassing the best zero-shot RGB-D method FreeZeV2.2 (51.5) by +29.1 and the non-zero-shot FRTPose ensemble (68.3) by +12.3. On BOP-H3 single-view RGB pose, 3PT achieves 58.7 AP (+12.3 over Co-Op), and on BOP-Industrial detection, 3PT-D reaches 57.9 AP (+23.6 over SAM-6D). On T-LESS multi-view RGB, 3PT's zero-shot method (90.0 AR) outperforms supervised per-object-trained methods by +4.2 AR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3PT outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every benchmark. In detection (Table 1a), 3PT-D surpasses MUSE by +13.3 AP on BOP-H3 and +23.6 AP on BOP-Industrial, and beats SAM-6D by +24.3 AP on BOP-Industrial (57.9 vs 33.6). For single-view RGB 6DoF pose (Table 1b), 3PT exceeds Co-Op by +12.3 AP on BOP-H3 (58.7 vs 46.4) and +5.4 AP on BOP-Classic (66.0 vs 60.6), and GigaPose by +27.5 AP on BOP-H3. On mm-level industrial pose (Table 1c), 3PT's RGB-only multi-view approach beats the strongest zero-shot RGB-D baseline FreeZeV2.2 by +29.1 AP-mm and the non-zero-shot FRTPose ensemble (which uses 4 detectors + 3 refiners with high-end depth sensors) by +12.3 AP-mm. On T-LESS multi-view RGB (Table 2), 3PT's zero-shot result (90.0 AR) exceeds the best supervised per-object-trained method Haugaard et al. (85.8 AR, MV-RGB-D) by +4.2, and CenDerNet (71.3) and DPODv2 (74.2) by even larger margins. Notably, 3PT is the only method that is simultaneously zero-shot, depth-free, and outperforms methods that train per-object or rely on depth sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

3PT departs from the dominant paradigm of zero-shot 3D object perception, which chains frozen foundation models (SAM, GroundingDINO, DINOv2) in multi-stage propose-and-match pipelines. The detection stage (3PT-D) adapts the two-tower ViT architecture from open-vocabulary detection (OwL-ViT, Scene-graph ViT) but replaces text conditioning with 3D CAD model conditioning, enabling early-fusion of geometric and visual information rather than late-fusion similarity matching. The refinement stage (3PT-R) follows the render-and-compare lineage of MegaPose and Co-Op's cross-attention design, but extends it to multi-view RGB without depth, using reciprocal edge matching and MV-PnP for iterative pose updates. The use of DINOv2 as a frozen encoder and DPT for dense decoding connects to the broader foundation-model ecosystem, while the KDE-based orientation fusion over SO(3) with von Mises-Fisher kernels is a novel geometric component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that zero-shot multi-view RGB can outperform supervised RGB-D methods by 29+ AP-mm on industrial benchmarks challenges the field's assumption that depth is necessary for mm-level pose accuracy, with direct implications for reducing sensor cost and complexity in industrial robotics. Practical validation through 100+ successful bin-picking attempts and 96/96 DIMM insertions at 0.5mm tolerance suggests the approach is deployment-ready rather than purely academic. Key limitations include the 30.5s per-scene runtime (bottlenecked by per-object forward passes through the detection tower) and the reliance on a defined depth range for scale prior in industrial settings. Open questions include whether the per-object inference bottleneck can be amortized for scenes with many diverse objects, and whether the 110k-CAD training vocabulary is sufficient for truly open-world industrial environments.
