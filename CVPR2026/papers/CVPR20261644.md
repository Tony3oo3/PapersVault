## Problem

Traditional VLN methods assume a closed-set setting where training and test data share the same environment styles and instruction formats, failing to generalize to unseen non-residential scenes or diverse user language styles. The GSA-VLN task exposes this limitation by evaluating agents across 150 scenes spanning 20 building types and three instruction categories (basic, scene-specific, user-personalized), but existing adaptation methods either treat fast and slow reasoning as independent parallel systems or focus solely on visual scene adaptation while ignoring instruction style variation.

## Input / Output

The agent receives K single-view panoramic images at each timestep, a navigation instruction (which may be in basic, scene-specific, or user-personalized style), GPS coordinates, neighbor node topology, and accumulated historical navigation records. Visual observations are converted to textual descriptions via Llama3.2-vision for each visited node. The output is a discrete navigation action selected from navigable views plus a stop token, accompanied by an action probability distribution and a navigation confidence score. The slow-reasoning module additionally produces structured experience vectors (scene type, spatial context, spatial rules, navigation strategy, historical success rate, occurrence frequency) stored in a finite-capacity experience library.

## Method

The slow4fast-VLN framework establishes a dynamic interactive loop between a fast-reasoning policy network (DUET architecture with dual-scale graph encoding) and a slow-reasoning LLM-based reflection module. The fast module executes real-time navigation and accumulates step-level historical records (timestamp, viewpoint, local topology, selected action, visual description, step metrics) as memory. The slow module uses a structured Chain-of-Thought prompt to reflect on these records, extracting generalized experiences that are stored in a capacity-K experience library. At each decision step, relevant experiences are retrieved via similarity matching on scene type, spatial context, and navigation strategy, encoded into vectors, and fused with the policy network's visual features through multi-head attention followed by a linear projection, producing experience-enhanced navigation decisions. An LLM-based instruction style conversion module (using CoT prompt engineering) transforms scene and user-style instructions into the model's familiar basic style in real time, with a confidence threshold to retain the original instruction when conversion quality is low.

## Results

On the GSA-R2R benchmark with basic instructions, the proposed slow4fast-VLN achieves 70.8% SR and 65.0% SPL on in-distribution residential scenes (Test-R-Basic), and 58.4% SR and 52.9% SPL on out-of-distribution non-residential scenes (Test-N-Basic). Compared to the SOTA GR-DUET, the method improves SR by 1.5 points (70.8 vs. 69.3) on residential and 1.8 points (58.4 vs. 56.6) on non-residential scenes, while also reducing Navigation Error from 3.1 m to 2.9 m (R) and 4.4 m to 4.2 m (N). Under scene-style instructions (Test-N-Scene), the method reaches 50.7% SR and 46.6% SPL, outperforming GR-DUET (48.1% SR, 42.8% SPL) by 2.6 and 3.8 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms the SOTA GR-DUET [7] on all headline metrics in both residential and non-residential settings: SR improves by 1.5 points (70.8 vs. 69.3) on Test-R-Basic and 1.8 points (58.4 vs. 56.6) on Test-N-Basic, with corresponding SPL gains of 0.7 and 1.4 points. Against the strongest optimization-based baseline (+BT [19]), the method leads by 9.5 SR points on residential (70.8 vs. 61.3) and 8.9 points on non-residential (58.4 vs. 49.5), indicating that test-time optimization alone is insufficient for OOD generalization. Memory-based baselines TourHAMT [9] and OVER-NAV [27] perform substantially worse (SR 14.9 and 22.3 on R; 11.0 and 16.6 on N), confirming that naive memory retrieval without structured experience extraction is inadequate. Under scene-style instructions (Table 3), the method achieves 50.7% SR vs. GR-DUET's 48.1%, a 2.6-point gap attributable to the LLM-based instruction style conversion module. Under user-style instructions (Table 2), the method leads GR-DUET by 0.3–1.6 SR points across five character personas, with the largest gain on Keith (68.3 vs. 66.7) and Sheldon (65.5 vs. 63.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DUET dual-scale graph transformer as its fast-reasoning backbone and positions itself against GR-DUET, the current SOTA for the GSA-VLN task. Its key conceptual departure is from prior fast-slow reasoning approaches (e.g., FSR-VLN [28], DynaThink [14]) that treat the two systems as independent parallel modules; here, slow reasoning (LLM-based reflection) explicitly feeds structured experiences back into the fast policy network via attention-based feature fusion, creating a closed-loop adaptation mechanism. The instruction style conversion component via CoT prompting extends beyond GR-DUET's purely visual scene adaptation to also handle linguistic style variation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The interactive fast-slow framework offers a practical path toward continual adaptation in open-world navigation without full retraining, which is relevant as embodied agents move beyond curated benchmarks. However, the approach's reliance on an LLM (Llama3.2-vision) for the slow-reasoning loop introduces significant latency and compute costs that may limit real-time deployment, and the experience library's fixed capacity (K=50–100) raises open questions about scalability to truly open-ended environments. The evaluation is confined to a single dataset (GSA-R2R) with indoor scenes, leaving unclear whether the experience distillation mechanism transfers to outdoor or multi-episode settings.
