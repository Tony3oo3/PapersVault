## Problem

Existing Federated Prompt Learning (FPL) and Personalized FPL methods produce static models after training that cannot adapt to unseen domain shifts at deployment time, since they rely solely on historical training data. This paper introduces Test-Time Federated Prompt Learning (TTFPL), a new paradigm that enables federated VLM-based classifiers to adapt to distribution shifts using only unlabeled target-domain data, without access to training samples, labels, or cross-client communication.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263999/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, each client receives a stream of unlabeled RGB images (no labels, no training data, no inter-client communication) processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. The method outputs per-sample class predictions and a test-time adapted prompt vector (16 learnable context tokens) that dynamically blends the global federated prompt, the client-local personalized prompt, and the original CLIP prompt. The adaptation is fully self-supervised, relying on pseudo-label confidence and entropy signals computed on-the-fly from the unlabeled test batch.

## Method

COTE introduces a tri-prompt architecture where three complementary prompts (global, local, and frozen CLIP) are dynamically selected and refined at test time based on a novel Model-Data Alignment (MoDA) metric. MoDA integrates macro-level statistics (normalized Gini index and entropy over the full label space) with micro-level statistics (local regularity over observed classes), weighted by an adaptive factor φ that shifts emphasis from global to local evidence as more classes are activated. An alignment signal ψ = M − λM^csi compares the client's MoDA score against a client-specific ideal reference, guiding prompt selection: for high-confidence samples (c(x) > τc), the global prompt is refined via pseudo-label cross-entropy when ψ > 0, or the local prompt when ψ < 0; for low-confidence samples, a TPT-style entropy minimization over augmented views is applied, initialized from the CLIP prompt (ψ > 0) or local prompt (ψ < 0). This confidence-partitioned, alignment-guided mechanism enables each client to balance global generalization and local specialization without any labeled data.

## Results

COTE achieves the highest average test accuracy across all five benchmarks: 71.08% on CIFAR-100 and 81.44% on ImageNet, surpassing the strongest baseline (TPT) by 6.91 and 6.23 percentage points respectively. The most dramatic gains appear under the out-of-client (OoC) setting, where COTE lifts accuracy from 19.24% to 42.34% on CIFAR-100 and from 6.87% to 36.60% on ImageNet. On fine-grained datasets, COTE attains 95.14% (Caltech-101), 86.49% (Flowers-102), and 81.74% (Food-101) average accuracy, with the largest margin (+23.4%) on Food-101 OoC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

COTE outperforms all seven baselines on average across every dataset. Against the closest TTA competitor TPT [42], COTE gains +6.91 pp on CIFAR-100 (71.08 vs 64.17) and +6.23 pp on ImageNet (81.44 vs 75.21), with the gap widening to +23.10 pp on CIFAR-100 OoC and +29.73 pp on ImageNet OoC. Among PFPL baselines, PromptFL+FT [14] (65.04/74.23 avg) and pFedMoAP [32] (59.32/69.48 avg) suffer severe OoC collapse (22.07% and 11.07% on CIFAR-100), while COTE maintains 42.34%, confirming that training-time personalization alone cannot handle unseen domain shifts. The non-personalized FPL baseline PromptFL [14] (52.12/47.28 avg) is the weakest overall, lacking both personalization and test-time adaptation. TENT [46] degrades sharply under corruption (50.47% on CIFAR-100 Corr vs COTE's 79.13%), indicating its entropy-minimization objective is fragile to distribution shifts. On fine-grained datasets (Table 2), COTE leads PL [21] by +5.65 pp on Flowers-102 and +5.65 pp on Food-101, while matching or slightly exceeding TENT [46] on Caltech-101 (95.14 vs 95.05).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of federated prompt learning (PromptFL, FedOTP, pFedMoAP), personalized federated prompt learning (pFedPrompt, pFedPG), and test-time adaptation (TENT, TPT, CoTTA), but departs from all three by formalizing a new deployment-time problem: adapting federated prompt learners to unseen domain shifts using only unlabeled test data. It builds on the tri-prompt structure implicit in PFPL (global + local prompts) by adding the frozen CLIP prompt as a third, domain-neutral anchor, and on TTA's entropy/pseudo-label mechanisms by gating them through a novel Model-Data Alignment (MoDA) metric. The closest prior work, FedTHE, performs test-time personalization in FL but modifies classifier heads, making it incompatible with frozen-backbone VLMs like CLIP that the paper targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing TTFPL as a formal problem and showing that a lightweight tri-prompt selection guided by a distributional alignment score can recover 20–30 points of accuracy under out-of-client shift, the paper opens a practical path for deploying federated VLMs in IoT and edge settings where post-deployment labeling is infeasible. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to a single VLM (CLIP ViT-B/16) with 16 context tokens, reliance on pseudo-label quality that may degrade under severe shift, and the absence of multi-round or streaming test-time scenarios. Open questions include whether the MoDA-guided prompt selection generalizes to other VLM architectures, larger prompt budgets, or multi-modal tasks, and whether the approach scales to hundreds of clients with highly heterogeneous label spaces.
