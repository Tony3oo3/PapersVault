## Problem

Existing sparse multi-view human reconstruction methods either rely on parametric templates (SMPL/SMPL-X) that perform poorly on dressed bodies, or fuse multi-view features with simple operations like concatenation and averaging that cannot adaptively weight views. There is no end-to-end, template-free implicit 3D reconstruction framework that employs learned, attention-based fusion strategies to optimally integrate sparse multi-view inputs for high-fidelity human modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260568/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N sparse multi-view RGB images (2, 4, or 8 cameras evenly distributed around the subject) along with camera intrinsic and extrinsic matrices. Depth and normal maps are estimated via a pretrained Sapiens model, and Plücker rays are computed from calibrations; all four modalities are stacked as multi-channel input to a modified ResNet. The output is a continuous occupancy field defined over a 3D grid (64³ during training, 256³ at inference, spanning [-1.0m, 1.0m]), from which a watertight mesh is extracted via Marching Cubes at threshold 0.5.

## Method

SMVRT introduces three attention-based fusion modules at distinct stages of the pipeline. First, the Alternating Fusion Module (AFM) tokenizes last-level 2D features and alternates between global (cross-view) and local (per-view) transformer or Mamba blocks to produce globally regularized 2D features. Second, the Multi-view Multi-level Fusion Transformer (MMFT) bilinearly warps multi-level 2D features onto 3D grid centers, then applies scaled dot-product attention across all views at each center to selectively integrate the most relevant features, followed by a 3D U-Net for volumetric regularization. Third, the Query Point Fusion Transformer (QFT) trilinearly samples multi-level 3D volume features and warps low-level 2D features at each query point, fusing them via attention to produce enhanced embeddings that preserve fine details. A coordinate-based MLP decoder then predicts per-point occupancy, trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy loss on Gaussian-perturbed surface samples.

## Results

On THUman 2.1 (4 cameras), SMVRT achieves a mean CD l1 of 3.58×10⁻³, IOU of 0.958, and Normal Consistency of 0.940, outperforming MV-PIFu (CD l1 10.24, IOU 0.844) and Zins et al. (CD l1 3.65, IOU 0.908). On the THUman 2.0 benchmark, SMVRT achieves CD l2 of 2.39×10⁻⁵ and P2S l2 of 2.14×10⁻⁵, roughly a 2× improvement over the best template-free baseline (3DFG-PIFu at 5.80/5.81). Zero-shot evaluation on the unseen MultiGarment dataset yields CD l1 of 3.62×10⁻³ and IOU of 0.962, confirming strong generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMVRT outperforms all baselines on the primary THUman 2.1 evaluation. Against MV-PIFu [36], the improvement is dramatic: CD l1 drops from 10.24 to 3.58 (≈3× reduction) and IOU rises from 0.844 to 0.958, while F0.5 jumps from 0.410 to 0.847, indicating far fewer missing surface regions. Against Zins et al. [50], the closest competitor, SMVRT achieves a modest but consistent edge in CD l1 (3.58 vs. 3.65) and a substantial gain in IOU (0.958 vs. 0.908) and NC (0.940 vs. 0.900), reflecting better surface detail. On the broader THUman 2.0 comparison (Table 2), SMVRT (CD l2 2.39, P2S 2.14) roughly doubles the accuracy of the best template-free method 3DFG-PIFu (5.80/5.81) and substantially outperforms template-based methods like SeSDF (6.30/9.18) and DeepMultiCap (8.21/7.51). On MultiHuman, SMVRT (3.66/3.36) again leads all non-template baselines, with the closest being 3DFG-PIFu (6.32/5.74). The only metric where SMVRT does not lead is F0.5 on THUman 2.1 (0.847 vs. Zins et al.'s 0.861), a narrow gap at the strictest 0.5% threshold, while SMVRT wins at the more standard 1.0% and 1.5% thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

SMVRT sits at the intersection of implicit 3D human reconstruction (PIFu, PIFuHD, occupancy networks) and multi-view feature fusion, but departs from prior sparse-view methods like 3DFG-PIFu and Zins et al. that rely on naive fusion strategies (concatenation, average pooling) by introducing three distinct transformer-based attentional fusion stages (AFM, MMFT, QFT) at different network depths. The use of a shared 3D volume centered on the body, regularized by a 3D U-Net, follows the volumetric reconstruction paradigm of MF-PIFu and 3DFG-PIFu, while the multi-level feature warping and query-point decoding extend the pixel-aligned implicit function framework. The auxiliary inputs (Sapiens-predicted depth, normals, Plücker rays) follow the multi-modal conditioning trend in recent human reconstruction work, but the paper's core novelty is the staged, geometry-aware attentional fusion rather than any single architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~2× improvement over prior SOTA on THUman 2.0 and strong zero-shot transfer to the MultiGarment dressed-human dataset demonstrate that attentional multi-view fusion meaningfully outperforms the concatenation/averaging strategies that have dominated sparse-view human reconstruction since PIFu. The modular three-stage design (AFM for global 2D context, MMFT for view selection at grid centers, QFT for detail-preserving query decoding) offers a reusable template for other sparse-view 3D reconstruction tasks beyond humans. Open questions include whether the approach scales to real-time or near-real-time inference, how it performs with highly variable camera configurations beyond evenly-distributed rigs, and whether the reliance on Sapiens for depth/normal estimation limits robustness in the wild.
