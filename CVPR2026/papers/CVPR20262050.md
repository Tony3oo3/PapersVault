## Problem

Existing diffeomorphic image registration methods rely on scaling-and-squaring integration schemes coupled with multiple handcrafted regularizers (Jacobian penalties, smoothness constraints, explicit inverse/cycle consistency terms) to enforce invertibility. The paper asks whether diffeomorphism can be derived from a single, principled constraint—the semigroup property—eliminating both the integration machinery and all auxiliary penalties, while also making inverse and cycle consistency inherent rather than explicitly imposed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262050/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of fixed and moving medical images (2D or 3D, MRI or CT) and produces a time-continuous deformation field φ_t(x) = x + t·F(x, t; I_f, I_m, θ) defined over t ∈ [−1, 1], where F is a time-embedded network (UNet or DiT) conditioned on both images. Unlike prior methods that output a single displacement at t = 1, SGDIR can be queried at any arbitrary time point to obtain intermediate warps, and the same field simultaneously provides forward and backward deformations via the semigroup structure (φ_{t−1} acts as the inverse of φ_{1−t}).

## Method

SGDIR replaces scaling-and-squaring integration with a partial semigroup regularization L_sg^t = ‖φ_{2t−1} − φ_t ∘ φ_{t−1}‖₂ + ‖φ_{2t−1} − φ_{t−1} ∘ φ_t‖₂, enforced at randomly sampled continuous time points during training. The paper proves (Theorem 1) that satisfying this composition rule together with φ_0 = Id compels the deformation to be an exponential map solving an autonomous ODE, thereby guaranteeing invertibility, cycle consistency, and topology preservation without any Jacobian penalty or explicit inverse modeling. The full training objective combines a time-continuous NCC similarity loss (warping both images toward a shared intermediate time) with the semigroup term weighted by λ; strict enforcement (λ = 10⁵) yields near-zero folding and diffeomorphic behavior, while relaxed enforcement (λ = 10⁴) permits larger deformations and outperforms state-of-the-art non-diffeomorphic methods. The framework is architecture-agnostic, instantiated with both time-embedded UNets and Diffusion Transformer backbones, and eliminates all integration steps at inference, achieving roughly 2× faster inference and half the memory of integration-based baselines.

## Results

On OASIS (atlas-based), diffeomorphic SGDIR DiT (λ=10⁵) achieves 86.53±0.82 Dice with 0.0% topology violations, outperforming the best diffeomorphic baseline (TransMorph-diff, 84.63±1.12) by ~1.9 Dice points while matching or exceeding top deformable methods (HViT, 85.38±0.93). In the deformable setting (λ=10⁴), SGDIR DiT reaches 88.09±0.91 Dice, surpassing all deformable baselines by 2.7+ points. On AbdomenCTCT, SGDIR UNet (λ=10⁵) achieves 53.64±2.01 Dice with zero folding, a >5-point improvement over the best diffeomorphic competitor (NePhi, 45.32±2.24). On LungCT, SGDIR UNet (λ=10⁵) attains 2.37±0.15 TRE with 0.0% negative Jacobians, reducing TRE by ~10% relative to the best diffeomorphic method (GradICON, 2.64±0.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the diffeomorphic setting (λ=10⁵), SGDIR consistently outperforms all diffeomorphic baselines (CycleMorph, GradICON, TransMorph-diff, R2Net, NODEO, NePhi, PULPo) on Dice across all eight datasets, with the largest margin on AbdomenCTCT (+8.3 Dice over NePhi) and Mindboggle101 (+1.1 over GradICON). SGDIR achieves 0.0% or near-zero negative Jacobian voxels, whereas competitors range from 0.0008% (NePhi) to 0.0224% (CycleMorph). In the deformable setting (λ=10⁴), SGDIR surpasses all deformable baselines (TransMorph, TransMatch, HViT, CorrMLP, SACB-Net, DiffuseReg, DiffuseMorph, VoxelMorph, AdaCS) on Dice by 2.5–5.5 points, with the largest gains on AbdomenCTCT (+3.2 over SACB-Net) and IXI (+3.2 over HViT). SGDIR's diffeomorphic variant matches or slightly trails the best deformable methods on raw Dice (e.g., 86.53 vs. HViT's 85.38 on OASIS atlas-based, where SGDIR actually leads) while maintaining zero topology violations, whereas deformable baselines exhibit 0.36–2.0% folding. On LungCT, SGDIR UNet (λ=10⁵) achieves the lowest TRE (2.37±0.15) among all methods, beating GradICON (2.64) and CorrMLP (2.48). The ablation on λ confirms that the semigroup weight acts as a continuous dial: at λ=10⁵ the model is strictly diffeomorphic, while at λ=10⁴ it becomes a deformable model that outperforms dedicated deformable architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGDIR departs from the dominant paradigm in diffeomorphic registration where velocity fields are estimated and deformations reconstructed via scaling-and-squaring integration coupled with multiple handcrafted regularizers (Jacobian penalties, smoothness terms, explicit inverse/cycle consistency). Rather than introducing a new architecture, the paper repurposes time-embedded backbones already common in diffusion models (temporal UNets, Diffusion Transformers) and shows that a single partial semigroup constraint—enforcing φ_{2t−1} = φ_t ∘ φ_{t−1}—is mathematically sufficient to recover an ODE flow, thereby inheriting invertibility, cycle consistency, and topology preservation without any auxiliary penalties. This contrasts with prior continuous-time approaches (DiffuseMorph, DiffuseReg, NODEO) that still rely on iterative sampling or explicit consistency losses, and with learning-based diffeomorphic methods (SYMNet, TransMorph-diff, GradICON) that embed scaling-and-squaring in the forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is twofold: strict semigroup enforcement (λ=10⁵) yields near-zero folding while matching or exceeding the accuracy of leading deformable models, and relaxed enforcement (λ=10⁴) produces a deformable variant that surpasses state-of-the-art non-diffeomorphic methods—suggesting the semigroup inductive bias is useful even when not strictly satisfied. Eliminating scaling-and-squaring gives roughly 2× faster inference and half the memory of integration-based competitors, which matters for clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the partial semigroup guarantee extends to non-autonomous (time-dependent) velocity fields, how the approach behaves under large inter-subject deformations where the ODE-flow assumption may be strained, and whether the architecture-agnostic claim holds for backbones beyond UNets and DiTs.
