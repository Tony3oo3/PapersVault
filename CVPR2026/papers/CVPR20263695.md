## Problem

Soft boundaries—thin hairs, semi-transparent structures—produce ambiguous foreground-background mixing that makes pixel-wise 3D estimation ill-posed. State-of-the-art depth estimators yield broken or detached depth in these regions, while generative stereo and view synthesis methods suffer from texture hallucination and detail compression, leading to geometrically inconsistent results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263695/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The depth fixer consumes an RGB image, its estimated depth map, and Sobel-derived edge guidance, and outputs a refined depth map in a plug-and-play fashion compatible with any base depth model (Depth Anything V2, Depth Pro, UniDepthV2). For view synthesis, the fixed depth drives forward warping at 448×448 patches, the scene painter operates at 480×832 resolution to inpaint disoccluded regions, and the color fuser fuses warped and inpainted results to produce final novel views or stereo video frames.

## Method

HairGuard comprises three components trained with a novel data curation pipeline that synthesizes depth and view-synthesis training pairs from image matting datasets (alpha mattes + foreground/background images) without manual trimaps. The depth fixer pairs a DINOv2+DPT feature branch with a U-Net pixel branch and introduces a gated residual module: a predicted gate map G∈[0,1] selects between the original depth and a learned residual, enabling precise soft-boundary correction while preserving global depth quality; training uses a two-stage strategy (local matting loss on soft regions, then global matting loss) to avoid gate collapse. The scene painter is a fine-tuned VACE/Wan2.1-1.3B video diffusion model that fills disoccluded regions, and the color fuser is a pretrained SDXL VAE decoder augmented with a dual skip module that concatenates multi-scale features from both warped and inpainted images, fine-tuned with L1+LPIPS loss to eliminate redundant background colors and hallucinated textures.

## Results

On the Marvel-10K stereo image conversion benchmark, HairGuard achieves PSNR 36.59, SSIM 0.8953, LPIPS 0.0909, DISTS 0.0331, and SIoU 0.3337, outperforming the strongest baseline SplatDiff (PSNR 36.23, LPIPS 0.1116, SIoU 0.3259) across all metrics. For depth boundary accuracy on AIM-500, the depth fixer plugged into Depth Anything V2 improves edge precision from 19.90% to 34.56% and edge recall from 6.50% to 13.08%. On novel view synthesis (AIM-500), HairGuard achieves FID 18.82 and CLIP-F 99.38, marginally surpassing SplatDiff (FID 19.26, CLIP-F 99.36). A user study with 27 participants (1332 votes) confirms perceptual superiority over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HairGuard outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in stereo conversion. Against the closest competitor SplatDiff (splatting-guided diffusion), HairGuard gains +0.36 dB PSNR, −0.021 LPIPS, and +0.008 SIoU, indicating better texture fidelity and stereo consistency. The gap widens dramatically against pure generative methods: ReCamMaster (video diffusion) lags by 6.15 dB PSNR and 0.317 LPIPS, while StereoCrafter (tiled video diffusion) trails by 4.07 dB PSNR, reflecting texture degradation from latent-space compression. For depth estimation, the plug-and-play depth fixer consistently improves boundary accuracy across all three base models (Depth Anything V2, Depth Pro, UniDepthV2), e.g., boosting edge precision from 19.90% to 34.56% on AIM-500 with DAv2. In novel view synthesis on matting datasets, HairGuard edges out SplatDiff by 0.44 FID on AIM-500 and 0.23 FID on P3M-10K, while substantially outperforming diffusion-only methods like ReCamMaster (FID 57.19 vs. 18.82) and NVS-Solver (FID 51.71 vs. 18.82). The zero-shot depth evaluation (Table 2) confirms the depth fixer preserves the base model's generalization without degradation on standard benchmarks (NYUv2, KITTI, ETH3D, ScanNet, DIODE).

## Relation to Prior Work

HairGuard positions itself as a targeted refinement layer on top of existing depth estimation models (Depth Anything V2, Depth Pro, UniDepthV2) and view synthesis pipelines (SplatDiff, ReCamMaster, StereoCrafter), rather than replacing them. Its conceptual departure is the repurposing of image matting datasets and the alpha-composition formulation to synthesize 3D training data with fine-grained soft-boundary depth labels, a data curation strategy not previously applied to depth or view synthesis. The gated residual mechanism for depth correction and the dual-skip VAE-based color fuser are novel architectural contributions that decouple soft-boundary fixing from global depth estimation and texture preservation from generative inpainting, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Soft boundaries (hair, thin structures, semi-transparent objects) remain a persistent and visually prominent failure mode across 3D vision tasks, and this paper provides a unified, modular solution that can be plugged into current and future depth models without retraining them. The Marvel-10K dataset and the matting-to-3D data curation pipeline offer practical tools for the film production and AR/VR communities where hair and fine structures are common. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the specific soft-boundary characteristics captured by matting datasets (e.g., volumetric fog, motion blur), whether the three-stage pipeline (depth fixer → scene painter → color fuser) can be unified or accelerated for real-time use, and how performance scales to very long video sequences with complex temporal soft-boundary dynamics.
