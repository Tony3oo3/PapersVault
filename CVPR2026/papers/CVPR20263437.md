## Problem

Large multimodal models perform video reasoning in a single pass, returning an answer without verifying whether the reasoning is grounded in explicit visual evidence or follows a valid path. Even when multiple reasoning traces are sampled at test time, prior work rarely checks cross-candidate evidence consistency or performs principled arbitration, leaving conclusions fragile under minor perturbations and long temporal horizons.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263437/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip (16 frames uniformly sampled during training; 32 frames for standard tasks, 1 fps subsampling for long videos) and a text question. The model is trained to emit a structured output with three mandatory fields: a <think> reasoning trace, an <answer> string, and <keyframes> indices. At inference, multiple diversified candidates (via temperature grid, spatial crops, and brightness perturbations) are generated and parsed into canonical answers, cited keyframe sets, reasoning segments, and a token-probability confidence proxy before aggregation.

## Method

RLER is a dual paradigm that decouples evidence production from evidence-based decision. RLER-Training fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B with GRPO and five weighted rewards: a Frame-sensitive reward that scores valid keyframe citations against invalid ones, a Think-transparency reward using a sin² length bonus to prefer moderate reasoning length, an Anti-repetition reward penalizing n-gram redundancy, plus standard format and accuracy rewards. RLER-Inference is a train-free orchestrator that generates K diversified candidates, parses each into answer/keyframes/reasoning/confidence, computes a composite evidence score (frame grounding + transparency + non-redundancy + confidence), performs evidence-weighted election with outlier trimming and Jaccard-based frame consensus, applies early stopping when the margin and confidence thresholds are met, and runs a single referee-style self-check that can trigger one additional resample if counter-evidence is found. The same three evidence notions shaped during training become the quantitative scoring levers at inference, creating a closed loop between learning and decision.

## Results

RLER achieves state-of-the-art open-source results across all 8 benchmarks, with headline numbers of 43.3% on VSIBench, 54.2% on VideoMMMU, 68.5% on VideoMME (w/o sub), 76.2% on TempCompass, 72.9% on MVBench, 57.5% on WildVideo, 50.7% on LVBench, and 63.0% on LongVideoBench. This represents an average improvement of 6.3% over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model while using only 3.1 candidates per question on average (vs. a cap of 8). On VideoMME, RLER (68.5%) surpasses the proprietary GPT-4o entry (67.9%), and on long-video benchmarks it outperforms all reported open-source and RL-based baselines by 8–12 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, RLER wins on all 8 benchmarks with gains ranging from +5.5 (VideoMMMU: 54.2 vs 47.4) to +8.7 (LVBench: 50.7 vs 42.0). Among RL-based competitors, RLER outperforms Video-R1 by 7.5 points on VSIBench (43.3 vs 35.8) and 12.3 points on MVBench (72.9 vs 63.9); it beats VideoChat-R1.5 by 2.1 points on VideoMME (68.5 vs 67.1) and 2.3 points on MVBench (72.9 vs 70.6); and it surpasses VideoRFT by 6.5 points on VSIBench (43.3 vs 36.8). Against the proprietary GPT-4o, RLER wins on VSIBench (43.3 vs 34.0), VideoMME (68.5 vs 67.9), MVBench (72.9 vs 57.5), and LVBench (50.7 vs 30.8), but trails on VideoMMMU (54.2 vs 61.2), WildVideo (57.5 vs 62.1), and LongVideoBench (63.0 vs 66.7). Among open-source LMMs, RLER sets new bests on every benchmark where it is reported, exceeding InternVL2.5-8B by 1.7 points on MVBench (72.9 vs 70.5) and LLaVA-Video-7B by 14.8 points on VideoMMMU (54.2 vs 36.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1) as its RL optimization backbone and Qwen2.5-VL as the base multimodal model, but departs from prior video-reasoning work by unifying two previously isolated lines—reward-shaped RL training (Video-R1, STAR-R1, VideoRFT, MOSS-ChatV) and test-time scaling (best-of-N, beam search, MCTS)—under a single evidence-centric principle. The key conceptual departure is making evidence (keyframe citations, transparency, non-redundancy) the shared currency between training rewards and inference-time scoring, so that the same signals that shape the policy during learning become the quantitative levers for candidate election at inference. Prior RL-based video models optimize for accuracy or format but do not explicitly train the model to emit machine-parsable evidence that a downstream orchestrator can consume.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual paradigm offers a clean, modular recipe for improving video-reasoning reliability without enlarging the model: shape evidence in training, elect by evidence at inference. The 6.3% average gain with only ~3.1 candidates per question is practically attractive for deployment settings where compute is constrained. However, the framework is validated on a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL) with LoRA, leaving open whether the evidence-centric reward design and election mechanism transfer to larger or architecturally different LMMs. The referee self-check is a single-pass heuristic, and the evidence-weighted election relies on hand-tuned thresholds (δ=0.08, γ=0.4) that may not generalize across domains; scaling the approach to longer videos, multi-turn dialogue, or non-video modalities remains unexplored.
