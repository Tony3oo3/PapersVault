## Problem

Existing radar data generation methods produce only partial representations (2D Range-Doppler maps or 3D spatial cubes), losing critical information needed for downstream tasks. No prior work synthesizes complete dense 4D radar tensors—spanning Doppler, range, azimuth, and elevation in native polar coordinates—guided by LiDAR, despite the substantial modality gap (sparse 3D vs. dense 4D, missing Doppler dimension) and the extreme dynamic range of radar signal power.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse 3D LiDAR tensor in native polar coordinates (R×A×E), where each voxel stores range and intensity, rasterized onto the same grid as the radar. It produces a dense 4D radar tensor (D×R×A×E) in polar coordinates, reconstructed from a compact three-part representation: a per-voxel Doppler-mean matrix, a binary key-voxel mask (~1.5% of voxels identified by CFAR), and full Doppler vectors at key object voxels, with background noise synthesized via Gaussian-softmax sampling. All processing preserves the native polar grid to avoid coordinate-transform distortion.

## Method

L2RLDB is a three-stage latent diffusion bridge. First, a key-voxel-aware 4D Radar VAE (3D ResNet with self-attention, treating Doppler as a feature channel) compresses the re-represented radar into a low-dimensional latent code, trained with BCE for the key-voxel mask, L2 for normalized reconstructions, and L1 for denormalized absolute power values. Second, a LiDAR VAE with identical architecture is trained with a patch-wise contrastive loss (cosine similarity at matching spatial locations) to align its latent space with the frozen radar VAE latent space. Third, a Brownian bridge diffusion process is formulated between the two aligned latents, with a 3D U-Net (AdaLN time embedding) predicting the SDE drift; inference uses a DDIM-style deterministic sampler to generate the radar latent from the LiDAR latent in S steps.

## Results

On the K-Radar dataset, L2RLDB achieves the best 4D radar synthesis quality across all metric groups: RAE-space PSNR of 29.21 dB and SSIM of 0.7324, XYZ-space PSNR of 33.01 dB and SSIM of 0.9092, and key-voxel IoU of 0.2885. In downstream 3D object detection (RTNH backbone), synthetic-only training with L2RLDB-generated radar reaches 91.9% of real-data AP_BEV (57.37 vs. 62.50 at IoU 0.3), while data augmentation with L2RLDB surpasses real-data-only training, achieving 77.14 AP_BEV and 74.18 AP_3D (IoU 0.3) versus 71.98/68.71 for real data alone (107–116% relative achievement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2RLDB outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against Latent Pix2PixHD (the strongest adversarial baseline), L2RLDB improves RAE-space PSNR by 0.98 dB (29.21 vs. 28.23), SSIM by 0.036 (0.7324 vs. 0.6963), and key-voxel IoU by 0.093 (0.2885 vs. 0.1960). Against Latent Diffusion (the strongest diffusion baseline), the gains are larger: RAE PSNR +2.14 dB, SSIM +0.059, and key-voxel IoU +0.154 (0.2885 vs. 0.1350), confirming that the Brownian bridge formulation with dual boundary conditions is substantially more effective than conditional cross-attention diffusion. L2RDaS, which operates in Cartesian XYZ space without Doppler, is not directly comparable on RAE or key-voxel metrics but is surpassed on XYZ PSNR (33.01 vs. 31.01). In the downstream detection task (Table 2), L2RLDB's synthetic data achieves 57.37 AP_BEV (IoU 0.3) versus 19.62 for Latent Pix2PixHD and 7.31 for Latent Diffusion, and its augmentation setting exceeds real-data-only training by 5.16 AP_BEV points, whereas other baselines yield only marginal or negative gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Brownian bridge diffusion framework (BBDM, Li et al., CVPR 2023) from 2D image-to-image translation to the synthesis of dense 4D radar tensors, and adapts DDIM-style non-Markovian sampling for accelerated inference. It departs from prior LiDAR-to-radar synthesis work (L2RDaS, Pix2PixHD-based methods) that targets only 2D or 3D partial representations in Cartesian or polar coordinates, by formulating the first method to generate complete 4D radar tensors (Doppler × Range × Azimuth × Elevation) in native polar coordinates. The key-voxel-aware VAE with CFAR-based re-representation and the patch-wise contrastive alignment module are novel architectural contributions not present in prior radar or LiDAR VAE literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Generating full 4D radar tensors from cheaper LiDAR inputs addresses a critical data bottleneck in radar-based autonomous perception, where large-scale annotated radar datasets are expensive to collect. The augmentation results showing >100% of real-data detection performance suggest synthetic radar can meaningfully improve downstream models, though this is demonstrated only on the K-Radar dataset with a single detection backbone (RTNH). Open questions include generalization to other radar hardware configurations, real-time inference feasibility, and whether the CFAR-based key-voxel decomposition transfers to radar systems with different noise characteristics. Performance degrades under fog and sleet where LiDAR itself is compromised, highlighting a fundamental limitation of LiDAR-guided generation in the very conditions where radar is most valuable.
