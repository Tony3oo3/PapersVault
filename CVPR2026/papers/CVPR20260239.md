## Problem

Stealthy MLLM agents can bypass existing CAPTCHAs (image-based, reasoning-based, and even contour-illusion-based) by mimicking human visual perception, yet no prior method actively forces such agents to expose their non-human identity. The paper addresses this gap by asking whether a CAPTCHA can simultaneously block agents and convert their failure into a verifiable identity disclosure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260239/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes an RGB image, a luminance-derived foreground mask, grating parameters (orientation θ, spatial period T, per-channel color vectors A and B), a target response string (e.g., a model version name), and an ensemble of surrogate CLIP encoders. It outputs a single dual-illusory CAPTCHA image where the adversarial perturbation δ is constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε (ε = 8/255 in experiments), making the perturbation imperceptible to humans while the underlying contour illusion preserves the original image's semantic outline for human solvers.

## Method

DualMirage is a two-stage generation pipeline. Stage 1 (Colored Abutting Grating Illusion) generalizes the binary abutting grating distortion to full-color images by generating two phase-shifted (π) square-wave gratings with independently parameterized R, G, B channels, then compositing them via foreground/background masks so that an illusory contour forms at the boundary. Stage 2 (Privacy-induced Adversarial Illusion Injection) optimizes a bounded perturbation δ on top of the contour-illusory image: in white-box mode, PGD maximizes the log-likelihood of a target token sequence (e.g., the model's own name); in black-box mode, it maximizes the average cosine similarity between the adversarial image's embedding and a target text embedding across N surrogate CLIP models. The key design insight is that the contour illusion destabilizes the MLLM's visual feature representation, which amplifies the transferability and potency of the adversarial perturbation, turning a simple blocking mechanism into an active identity-hunting trap.

## Results

DualMirage achieves a 95.8% average human success rate (HSR) across all challenge configurations, with near-perfect 99.1% first-attempt accuracy on Illusion-Selfie2Anime. On the Agent Blocking Rate (ABR), DualMirage achieves 100% against both white-box models (LLaVA-v1.5-7b, Qwen2.5-vl-7b) and 97.25–98.25% against black-box models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-pro, Claude-3-Sonnet). The Attack Success Rate (ASR) for identity disclosure reaches 62.25% (LLaVA) and 55.25% (Qwen) in white-box settings, and 20.00–25.00% in black-box settings, a capability entirely absent in all baseline CAPTCHAs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against image-based CAPTCHAs (reCAPTCHA, hCAPTCHA), DualMirage dominates on ABR, achieving 100% blocking on white-box models versus 53–86% for baselines, and 97–98% on black-box models versus 49–77%. Against reasoning-based CAPTCHAs (Angular, GoBang, IconCrush, Space, Space Reasoning), DualMirage matches or exceeds the best baselines (e.g., GoBang at 99.6% ABR on Qwen) while adding the unique ASR dimension that no reasoning CAPTCHA provides. Against IllusionCAPTCHA, DualMirage ties on ABR (both reach 100% on white-box models) but introduces a critical new capability: active identity disclosure with 55–62% ASR in white-box and 20–25% ASR in black-box settings, whereas IllusionCAPTCHA offers no mechanism to confirm or exploit an agent's failure. The ablation (Table 4) further confirms that the synergy between contour and adversarial illusions is essential: contour alone achieves 92–95% ABR but 0% ASR, while adversarial alone achieves 100% ABR on LLaVA but drops to 36% ASR on GPT-4o, whereas the combined DualMirage reaches 100% ABR and 44–100% ASR across models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research threads into a unified CAPTCHA framework. The contour illusion component generalizes Fan & Zeng's (2023) abutting grating distortion from binary MNIST to arbitrary color images, while the adversarial component builds on Bagdasaryan et al.'s (2024) multi-modal adversarial illusions but redirects the objective from generic misclassification to targeted text-output hijacking (forcing model-name disclosure). The paper also positions itself as a direct successor to IllusionCAPTCHA (Ding et al., 2025), which used contour illusions as a passive barrier, by adding the active 'hunting' dimension that converts a failed attempt into a verifiable identity signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a timely and practically important gap: as MLLM agents become increasingly capable of solving traditional CAPTCHAs, defenders need mechanisms that not only block but positively identify automated agents. However, the practical impact is tempered by significant robustness vulnerabilities—the adversarial component's ASR drops from 45.6% to 5.5% under simple 5-pixel cropping, and black-box ASR remains modest at ~22%. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to next-generation MLLM architectures, whether the contour illusion advantage persists as visual encoders improve, and whether the 'hunting' signal can be made robust enough for real-world deployment where agents may apply preprocessing.
