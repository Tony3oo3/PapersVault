## Problem

Diffusion-based real-world image super-resolution models under-reconstruct fine high-frequency details because the standard noise-prediction loss is inherently low-frequency-biased and misaligned with the network's depth-wise 'low-first, high-later' processing hierarchy. Existing frequency-aware or self-distillation approaches remain frequency-agnostic, applying uniform supervision across all layers and bands, which starves the later HF-refining layers of targeted optimization signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-resolution image (generated via Real-ESRGAN degradation pipeline) and a text caption (produced by LLaVA) as conditioning inputs, and outputs a 512×512 high-resolution image (×4 upscaling). FRAMER is strictly a training-time scheme: at inference, the original diffusion backbone (U-Net or DiT) runs unmodified with no additional heads, modules, or computational overhead, making it fully plug-and-play across architectures.

## Method

FRAMER decomposes intermediate-layer (student) and final-layer (teacher) feature maps into low- and high-frequency bands via 2D FFT masks, then applies two frequency-specific contrastive losses: IntraCL on the LF band (pulling students toward the teacher while repelling a random same-image layer, with no in-batch negatives to avoid false negatives on shared structure) and InterCL on the HF band (pulling toward the teacher while repelling both a random-layer negative and in-batch negatives from other images to enforce instance discrimination). Two adaptive modulators further refine supervision: FAW computes per-layer, per-band weights inversely proportional to the student-teacher frequency-magnitude discrepancy, and FAM gates these weights by the current cosine-alignment score (with detached gradients) to suppress distillation when early layers are still far from the teacher. The total training objective is the standard noise-prediction loss plus the sum of all layer-wise, frequency-gated distillation terms, and all auxiliary components are removed at test time.

## Results

On DrealSR, FRAMER_U (U-Net/PiSA-SR backbone) achieves PSNR 26.96, SSIM 0.786, LPIPS 0.333, NIQE 5.386, MANIQA 0.595, and MUSIQ 74.53, improving over PiSA-SR by +3.0% to +21.4% across metrics. FRAMER_D (DiT/DiT4SR backbone) reaches PSNR 24.73, SSIM 0.687, LPIPS 0.412, NIQE 5.959, MANIQA 0.514, and MUSIQ 68.47 on DrealSR, with gains of +4.6% to +16.6% over DiT4SR. On the more challenging RealLR200 and RealLQ250 benchmarks, both FRAMER variants rank first on all perceptual quality metrics (NIQE, MANIQA, MUSIQ). The largest relative improvements appear in perceptual metrics: MANIQA improves by up to +22.9% (FRAMER_D on RealSR) and NIQE by up to +18.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRAMER is evaluated as a plug-and-play training add-on on two backbones: FRAMER_U on PiSA-SR (U-Net) and FRAMER_D on DiT4SR (DiT). FRAMER_U outperforms its base PiSA-SR on every metric across all four datasets, with the largest gains in MANIQA (+21.4% on DrealSR, +17.5% on RealSR) and NIQE (+12.2%, +6.6%). FRAMER_D similarly beats DiT4SR on all metrics, with particularly strong perceptual improvements: MANIQA +22.9% and NIQE +18.8% on RealSR. Against the strongest non-base baselines, FRAMER_U surpasses SeeSR (CVPR'24) on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and all perceptual metrics on DrealSR and RealSR, while FRAMER_D outperforms DreamClear (NeurIPS'24) on every reported metric. Both FRAMER variants also clearly dominate the Swin-based methods (SwinIR, ResShift) across all benchmarks. On the harder RealLR200 and RealLQ250 sets, FRAMER ranks first on all three perceptual metrics, confirming consistent gains under more severe real-world degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FRAMER extends the self-distillation paradigm (Hinton et al.) into the frequency domain by decomposing teacher/student feature maps via FFT masks and applying separate contrastive objectives to LF and HF bands, rather than the frequency-agnostic alignment used in prior diffusion self-distillation work. It departs from existing frequency-aware diffusion methods (which rely on fixed noising schedules or auxiliary wavelet modules) by making supervision adaptive to each layer's actual spectral change rate (FAW) and student-teacher alignment (FAM). Architecturally, it is instantiated as a training-only add-on to PiSA-SR (U-Net) and DiT4SR (DiT), inheriting their pre-trained T2I priors without any inference-time modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic design means practitioners can drop FRAMER into any diffusion-based SR pipeline (U-Net or DiT) with minimal engineering, and the consistent gains across four real-world benchmarks and six metrics make it a practical improvement for the field. The empirical validation that regression-based frequency distillation (MSE in Fourier space) actively harms perceptual quality while contrastive distillation in the same feature space helps provides useful guidance for future auxiliary-loss design. Open questions include whether the simple binary FFT ring masks are optimal versus learned or multi-scale decompositions, whether the approach transfers to video SR or other restoration tasks, and whether the gains persist at higher upscaling factors (×8, ×16) where HF content is even scarcer.
