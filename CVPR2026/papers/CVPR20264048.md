## Problem

Existing open-set active learning (OSAL) methods rely on separately trained out-of-distribution detectors, incurring substantial training overhead, and they overlook the supervisory value of labeled unknowns for improving known-class learning. This paper asks whether the latent class structure within labeled unknowns can be directly exploited—without any external detector—to simultaneously strengthen model training and guide more reliable query selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264048/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) from an unlabeled pool that may contain previously unseen classes, alongside a growing labeled set where known-class samples receive true labels and unknown-class samples receive a single 'unknown' label. Each AL round outputs a batch of |B| selected samples for annotation and updates a dual-head ResNet-50 classifier: a primary head over known classes and an auxiliary head over known plus estimated unknown pseudo-classes. The per-sample outputs include a logit-margin purity score and a JS-divergence informativeness score used jointly for selection.

## Method

E²OAL is a unified, detector-free OSAL framework operating in two stages per AL round. In stage 1, it performs label-guided K-Means clustering on frozen CLIP features of all labeled samples, using a ternary search over candidate cluster counts and a structure-aware F1-product objective (with Hungarian-algorithm alignment to known labels) to estimate the number and composition of unknown classes; these pseudo-clusters feed a Dirichlet-calibrated auxiliary head (Evidential Deep Learning) trained with NLL + KL losses alongside a standard CE primary head, improving known-class discrimination and yielding translation-aware confidence. In stage 2, a logit-margin purity score (max known logit minus max unknown logit from the auxiliary head) ranks samples by known-class likelihood, and a three-component GMM fit to the purity distribution adaptively sizes a high-purity candidate pool to meet a target query precision via an online calibration update; within the pool, a Jensen-Shannon divergence product metric (divergence from uniform × divergence from one-hot) selects samples with moderate uncertainty, suppressing both overconfident and overly ambiguous predictions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (30% mismatch), E²OAL achieves 95.94% final-round test accuracy, surpassing the closest competitor EAOA (95.88%) and BUAL (95.04%). On CIFAR-100 (30% mismatch), E²OAL reaches 67.54% vs. EAOA's 67.14% and BUAL's 63.73%. On the more challenging Tiny-ImageNet (15% mismatch), the gap widens substantially: E²OAL attains 60.44% compared to EAOA (57.31%), BUAL (56.09%), and EOAL (56.13%). Under intermediate mismatch ratios, the full E²OAL pipeline (with class expansion) pushes accuracy to 97.52% (CIFAR-10), 72.10% (CIFAR-100), and 64.02% (Tiny-ImageNet), while maintaining query precision close to the target p*=0.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E²OAL outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and mismatch ratio tested. Against the strongest hybrid competitor EAOA (CVPR 2025, same group), E²OAL gains +0.06 pp on CIFAR-10, +0.40 pp on CIFAR-100, and a substantial +3.13 pp on Tiny-ImageNet, with the advantage growing as the open-set difficulty increases. BUAL (ECCV 2024) and EOAL (AAAI 2024) trail by 0.9–3.8 pp and 2.3–4.4 pp respectively, reflecting their reliance on separately trained OOD detectors and limited exploitation of labeled unknowns. Informativeness-only methods (Uncertainty, Coreset, BADGE) consistently oversample unknown-class samples, degrading accuracy, while purity-only methods (MSP, LfOSA) query redundant known-class instances with low learning value. MQNet (NeurIPS 2022) combines both factors but suffers from inaccurate purity estimation. E²OAL also maintains query precision closer to the target p*=0.6 than EAOA, whose fixed-step adaptive strategy exhibits notable fluctuations in early rounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-set active learning (OSAL) line initiated by LfOSA, MQNet, EOAL, BUAL, and EAOA, all of which combine uncertainty/diversity with separately trained OOD detectors to balance purity and informativeness. E²OAL departs from this detector-based paradigm by eliminating auxiliary OOD networks entirely, instead using a Dirichlet-calibrated auxiliary head and a logit-margin purity score derived from the same model. It also repurposes labeled unknowns—previously discarded or collapsed into a single 'unknown' class—as structured supervision via label-guided clustering in a frozen CLIP feature space, a signal that prior OSAL methods overlook.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing separate detectors and exploiting the latent structure of labeled unknowns, E²OAL offers a more practical and computationally lighter OSAL pipeline that practitioners can deploy without training and maintaining additional OOD models. The finding that preserving unknown-class structure (rather than collapsing it) consistently benefits known-class learning challenges the common practice of treating all unknowns as a monolithic category. Open questions include scalability to very large unknown-class spaces, sensitivity to the quality of the frozen contrastive backbone, and whether the two-stage GMM-based pool construction generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities or higher-dimensional feature spaces.
