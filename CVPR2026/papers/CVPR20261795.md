## Problem

Edge-based wireframe reconstruction methods rely on Hungarian matching to establish one-to-one correspondences between predicted and ground-truth edges, resulting in inherently sparse supervision where only a small subset of matched edges receive informative gradients. Unmatched proposals are uniformly penalized as negatives regardless of their geometric quality, leaving most edge proposals under-optimized and preventing the model from learning discriminative confidence rankings across the full proposal distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261795/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes aerial LiDAR point clouds as input and produces compact 3D wireframes represented as parameterized edges (each encoding midpoint, direction, and length). GREO operates exclusively as a training-time strategy layered on top of existing edge-based architectures (PBWR, EdgeDiff), generating no additional parameters or inference-time computation. At inference, standard confidence-based NMS and DBSCAN clustering merge adjacent endpoints to form continuous wireframe structures.

## Method

GREO is a plug-and-play, reinforcement learning-style training strategy inspired by GRPO that provides dense supervision across all edge proposals. For each predicted edge, it computes a continuous reward from a composite geometric cost (Hausdorff distance, cosine orientation mismatch, and midpoint L1 distance) against the nearest ground-truth edge, then applies group-wise Z-score normalization within each sample to convert absolute rewards into relative advantages. These normalized advantages are transformed into a target confidence distribution via temperature-scaled softmax, and the network's predicted confidence scores are aligned to this target through cross-entropy minimization. An entropy regularization term prevents over-confidence and maintains distributional stability during training. The entire mechanism introduces no architectural changes or inference overhead, functioning purely as an auxiliary loss alongside the standard geometric and confidence losses of the base edge-based method.

## Results

On the Building3D Entry-Level subset, EdgeDiff+GREO achieves the best WED of 0.108 (vs. 0.115 for EdgeDiff and 0.117 for BWFormer), with CF1 of 0.93 and EF1 of 0.89. On the more challenging Tallinn subset, EdgeDiff+GREO reduces WED to 0.225 (vs. 0.255 for EdgeDiff and 0.238 for BWFormer), improving edge recall from 0.70 to 0.75 and EF1 from 0.79 to 0.82. PBWR+GREO similarly improves WED from 0.271 to 0.250 on Tallinn, with corner and edge recall each rising by 4.0 percentage points. GREO adds zero inference overhead, with FLOPs and memory unchanged from the base models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the edge-based baseline PBWR, GREO consistently improves WED (0.145→0.130 on Entry, 0.271→0.250 on Tallinn) and raises both corner and edge recall by 1–4 percentage points, with EF1 gains of 1% (Entry) and 2% (Tallinn). Against the stronger edge-based baseline EdgeDiff, GREO reduces WED from 0.115 to 0.108 (Entry) and 0.255 to 0.225 (Tallinn), while boosting edge recall by 3% and EF1 by 1–3% respectively. Compared to the vertex-based BWFormer, EdgeDiff+GREO achieves lower WED (0.108 vs. 0.117 on Entry; 0.225 vs. 0.238 on Tallinn) and higher EF1 (0.89 vs. 0.84; 0.82 vs. 0.79), though BWFormer retains a slight CF1 edge on Tallinn (0.88 vs. 0.86) due to its explicit vertex detection. The older vertex-based methods Point2Roof and PC2WF are substantially outperformed across all metrics, with WED gaps exceeding 0.1 on both subsets. Overall, GREO wins on every metric against both edge-based baselines and matches or exceeds the best vertex-based method on the primary reconstruction fidelity metrics (WED, EF1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) paradigm from DeepSeek-R1 [7]—originally designed for stochastic token-level policy gradients in LLMs—to the deterministic, structured prediction setting of edge-based wireframe reconstruction. It builds directly on PBWR [12] and EdgeDiff [25] as base pipelines, targeting their shared limitation of sparse supervision from Hungarian matching. The key conceptual departure is replacing GRPO's clipped policy gradient and KL penalty with a simpler reward-weighted cross-entropy objective over a softmax-normalized confidence distribution, making the adaptation tractable for regression rather than generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (zero inference overhead, no architectural change) makes GREO immediately adoptable by practitioners working on edge-based reconstruction, and the consistent gains across two base methods and two dataset subsets validate its generality within that niche. However, the 'reinforcement learning' framing is somewhat loose—there is no policy gradient, exploration, or environment interaction; the core mechanism is Z-score-normalized reward weighting with cross-entropy, which is closer to a reweighted supervised loss. Open questions include whether the group-relative formulation transfers to other structured prediction tasks (e.g., line segment detection, mesh edge extraction) and whether the modest Tallinn-subset gains (WED 0.255→0.225) scale to more complex or out-of-domain scenes beyond Building3D.
