## Problem

State-of-the-art online video segmentation models rely on complex pipelines combining a per-frame segmenter (ViT-Adapter + Mask2Former decoder) with specialized tracking modules (context-aware features, re-identification layers, multi-block Transformer trackers), incurring significant architectural complexity and computational overhead. The paper asks whether a sufficiently large, well-pretrained ViT encoder can absorb the functionality of all these specialized components and perform both segmentation and temporal tracking within a single encoder-only architecture.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of RGB video frames in an online fashion, where predictions at timestep t may only depend on the current frame and all prior frames. During training, a temporal window of 5 frames is used with batch size 8; at inference, frames are processed sequentially with batch size 1. For each frame, the model outputs a variable number of predictions, each consisting of a binary segmentation mask at full spatial resolution (H×W) and a semantic class label, with query indices maintaining consistent object identity across frames to provide temporal track association.

## Method

VidEoMT is an encoder-only architecture built on a DINOv2-pretrained ViT that unifies segmentation and tracking by injecting learnable queries into the last L₂ Transformer blocks, following the EoMT design. The key temporal mechanism is query propagation: at t=0, standard learnable queries are used, but at subsequent frames the previous frame's output track queries are fed back as input to the last L₂ blocks, carrying object-level information across time at zero additional per-frame cost. To prevent the model from losing the ability to detect newly appearing objects as the influence of the original learnable queries diminishes, a lightweight query fusion step computes Q_t^F = Linear(Q_{t-1}^S) + Q^lrn, combining propagated temporal context with temporally-agnostic learned queries via element-wise addition. The model is trained end-to-end with Mask2Former losses (cross-entropy for classification, binary cross-entropy and Dice for masks) and a DVIS++-style ground-truth matching strategy that assigns each object to a fixed query index from its first appearance, enforcing temporal consistency in supervision.

## Results

On YouTube-VIS 2019 val, VidEoMT (ViT-L, DINOv2) achieves 68.6 AP with 566 GFLOPs at 160 FPS, compared to CAVIS's 68.9 AP at 15 FPS (838 GFLOPs). On YouTube-VIS 2022 val, VidEoMT reaches 42.6 AP^L, surpassing CAVIS (39.5) and DVIS-DAQ (42.0). On VSPW, VidEoMT attains 64.9 mIoU and 95.0 mVC₁₆, outperforming DVIS++ (62.8 mIoU) while running at 73 FPS versus 13 FPS. Overall, VidEoMT delivers a 5×–10× speedup over state-of-the-art methods with at most ~1.5 AP degradation on the primary benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidEoMT nearly matches CAVIS [22] on YouTube-VIS 2019 (68.6 vs 68.9 AP, −0.3) and YouTube-VIS 2021 (63.1 vs 64.6 AP, −1.5) while running 10× faster (160 vs 15 FPS). It clearly outperforms DVIS++ [40] on both YouTube-VIS 2019 (+0.9 AP) and 2021 (+0.8 AP), and beats DVIS-DAQ [41] by +0.3 and +0.7 AP respectively, all at 14–16× higher speed. On YouTube-VIS 2022, VidEoMT surpasses CAVIS by +3.1 AP^L and DVIS-DAQ by +0.6 AP^L. The only losses are on OVIS (−0.7 AP vs CAVIS, −1.8 vs DVIS-DAQ) and VIPSeg (−1.7 VPQ vs CAVIS), where the accuracy gap remains small relative to the 7–19× speed advantage. On VSPW, VidEoMT wins outright, improving mIoU by +2.1 over DVIS++ while being 5.6× faster. Compared to the efficiency-focused MinVIS [18], VidEoMT gains +7.0 AP on YouTube-VIS 2019 at 5.5× the speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidEoMT directly extends EoMT (Kerssies et al., CVPR 2025), which demonstrated that a large pretrained ViT can perform image segmentation in an encoder-only fashion by injecting learnable queries into the final transformer layers. It departs from the dominant video segmentation paradigm (CAVIS, DVIS, DVIS++, DVIS-DAQ) that decouples a complex segmenter (ViT-Adapter + Mask2Former decoder) from a dedicated tracker (multi-layer cross-attention with context-aware features and re-identification layers), instead unifying both segmentation and temporal association within a single ViT encoder via lightweight query propagation and query fusion. The work is also informed by the observation that DINOv2-style self-supervised pre-training produces cross-view consistent features, which are naturally suited for temporal tracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–10× speedup (up to 160 FPS with ViT-L) while maintaining within ~1 AP of state-of-the-art methods makes online video segmentation practical for real-time applications that were previously infeasible with tracker-heavy architectures. The well-evidenced finding that specialized tracking modules (context-aware features, re-identification layers, dedicated tracker) are largely redundant given strong VFM pre-training challenges the field's assumption that explicit temporal association requires dedicated architectural components. Limitations include a persistent ~1–2 AP gap on some benchmarks (YouTube-VIS 2021), dependence on large-scale pre-training (performance degrades notably with ImageNet-1K), and the fact that query propagation only carries one frame of temporal context, which may limit performance on very long videos with complex object re-appearances.
