## Problem

When Vision Foundation Models are adapted to domain generalization semantic segmentation via parameter-efficient fine-tuning, adapters overfit to source-domain statistics and seen-class boundaries, causing domain bias and semantic rigidity. Existing perturbation-based regularization strategies inject random noise that disrupts the pretrained manifold geometry of VFMs, leading to semantic drift and unstable generalization. The paper addresses how to expand representational diversity for downstream tasks while respecting the intrinsic geometric structure established during large-scale pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263596/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at varying resolutions (e.g., 1914×1052 for GTA5, 1280×720 for BDD-100K, 1920×1080 for Mapillary) which are patchified into N tokens of dimension d by a frozen VFM encoder (DINOv2, EVA02, or CLIP ViT-Large). It produces per-pixel segmentation masks of shape C×H×W for known classes, and for open-set evaluation, additionally outputs an unknown-region response map where higher values indicate greater probability of an unseen class. The perturbation and regularization operate at the token level within the encoder's feature space before the decoder produces final predictions.

## Method

GeCo introduces two complementary mechanisms applied to LoRA adapters (4.7M trainable params) inserted into ViT blocks. First, curvature-guided perturbation estimates local manifold curvature at each token via PCA-based tangent space approximation: it measures how strongly neighborhood points deviate from the tangent plane, then modulates perturbation magnitude inversely to curvature (larger in flat regions, smaller in semantically complex regions) and constrains perturbation direction to the tangent space for high-curvature tokens while allowing normal-space exploration for low-curvature tokens. Second, geodesic-based regularization replaces standard Euclidean or KL consistency losses with a squared geodesic distance computed on a hyperspherical surrogate of the probability simplex (via square-root mapping), which adaptively scales gradients based on angular separation between original and perturbed predictions and per-class confidence shifts. Together, these components preserve the pretrained VFM manifold geometry while enabling structure-consistent exploration for improved cross-domain and open-set generalization.

## Results

On the primary closed-set DGSS benchmark (GTA5 → Cityscapes + BDD-100K + Mapillary), GeCo with DINOv2-L achieves 67.7% average mIoU using only 4.7M trainable parameters, outperforming the strongest baselines FisherTune (66.5%, 15.2M params) and FADA (66.1%, 11.7M params). In the multi-source setting (GTA5+SYNTHIA → same targets), GeCo reaches 71.1% average mIoU, surpassing SoMA (70.8%) with fewer parameters. For open-set DGSS, GeCo+RPL attains 93.5 AP and 64.7 mIoU on ACDC-POC, and 58.6 AP / 41.5 mIoU on MUAD, exceeding Rein+RPL by +0.9 AP and +3.1 mIoU on ACDC-POC, and by +6.8 AP and +3.8 mIoU on MUAD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeCo consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every backbone and evaluation setting. Against the closest competitors on DINOv2-L (single-source), GeCo gains +1.2 mIoU over FisherTune (67.7 vs. 66.5) and +1.6 over FADA (67.7 vs. 66.1), while using roughly one-third of FisherTune's parameters (4.7M vs. 15.2M). Compared to the lightweight adapter Rein (2.99M params), GeCo improves average mIoU by +3.4 points (67.7 vs. 64.3), with the largest gap on BDD (+4.2). In the multi-source setting, GeCo surpasses SoMA by +0.3 mIoU average (71.1 vs. 70.8) with fewer parameters (4.7M vs. 6.6M). For open-set DGSS, GeCo+RPL beats Rein+RPL by +6.8 AP and +3.8 mIoU on MUAD, and by +0.9 AP / +3.1 mIoU on ACDC-POC, while also achieving the lowest FPR95 (15.1 vs. 20.7 on MUAD). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that both curvature-guided perturbation and geodesic regularization contribute independently: replacing either with naive random perturbation + MSE drops average mIoU by 1.0–2.0 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeCo sits at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) of Vision Foundation Models and domain generalized semantic segmentation, departing from prior VFM adaptation methods (Rein, FADA, SET, FisherTune) that treat pretrained token spaces as flat Euclidean regions. Its conceptual departure is the explicit modeling of VFM representations as curved manifolds: curvature-guided perturbation replaces the random-noise perturbations used in consistency-regularization approaches (PACE, adversarial style augmentation), and geodesic-based regularization replaces standard MSE/KL consistency losses that implicitly assume a flat prediction space. The manifold hypothesis and PCA-based tangent estimation are repurposed from classical differential geometry into a lightweight, per-token computational framework, while the prototype bank extends local neighborhoods to compensate for limited batch diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and increasingly relevant problem—PEFT of large VFMs for dense prediction—by showing that respecting pretrained geometric structure yields consistent gains (1.8–2.0% mIoU) with only 4.7M trainable parameters, and that the approach is model-agnostic (pluggable into Rein, FADA). The open-set results (6.8% AP gain on MUAD) suggest the geometric regularization implicitly improves OOD separability without explicit anomaly modeling, which could reduce the need for auxiliary OOD data. Open questions include whether the local curvature proxy scales meaningfully to larger models or higher-dimensional token spaces, whether the hyperspherical surrogate for the prediction manifold is the right choice versus other Riemannian metrics, and whether the gains persist under more aggressive domain shifts or larger class vocabularies.
