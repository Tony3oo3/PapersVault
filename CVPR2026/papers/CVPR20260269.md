## Problem

Existing DiT-native layout controllers either require training (introducing data bias and quality degradation) or operate as training-free regional prompting methods that cannot manage object occlusion order, often producing concept blending where semantics from different regions erroneously fuse. No prior method achieves robust simultaneous control over both spatial layout and occlusion while preserving the base model's generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260269/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured specification: a background prompt for initialization, a full scene prompt for the nursing phase, and N layered regional inputs each pairing a regional text prompt with a spatial cue (bounding box or mask) whose token indices are mapped into the DiT sequence. The layer index i (1=farthest to N=nearest) explicitly encodes occlusion order. Output is a single 1024×1024 RGB image respecting all spatial cues and layer ordering; the region-branching design additionally supports post-hoc edits such as swapping per-region instances or reordering visible layers without regenerating unaffected regions.

## Method

LayerBind is a training-free, two-phase controller exploiting the observation that spatial layout and occlusion are rigidly established at very early denoising steps, so rearranging the early latent structure deterministically propagates through the ODE trajectory. Phase 1 (Layer-wise Instance Initialization, first η₁ fraction of steps) copies per-region token subsets into independent branches that update via Contextual Attention—each branch queries against its own region plus the shared background and its regional text—while a Hard Binding mechanism in text-dominant DiT layers severs the background link to prevent modality competition on small objects; at step t₁ the branches are fused sequentially by layer order with optional foreground alpha compositing. Phase 2 (Layer-wise Semantic Nursing, steps t₁ to t₂) runs standard global joint attention in parallel with a sequential layered path where each region's local attention enhancement is composited onto the global embedding via a layer-transparency scheduler (α_o = β·M), ensuring top-layer semantics robustly overwrite bottom layers in overlapping regions while progressively reinforcing regional details.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench-3D, LayerBind (FLUX) achieves the best depth-occlusion score (UniDet 44.97) and highest occlusion perception (O_VQA 59.49), while LayerBind (SD3.5) leads on O_VQA (65.78). On the more challenging BindBench (3–5 object occlusions), LayerBind (FLUX) attains L_Acc 92.18 and O_VQA 52.55, substantially outperforming the next-best methods (HybridLayout: 78.30 / 29.20). LayerBind also preserves image quality (HPS 29.66 on FLUX) and runs at 30.11 s (+30% overhead), far faster than region-partitioned baselines like HybridLayout (78.75 s, +240%) and RAGD (62.14 s, +168%). On T2I alignment (Table 2), LayerBind+FLUX improves Spatial (70.63 vs. 39.09 base) and Numeracy (70.93 vs. 59.81 base) over the unmodified FLUX model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayerBind (FLUX) outperforms all training-based baselines on the depth-occlusion metric UniDet (44.97 vs. InstanceDiffusion 41.53, HybridLayout 41.33, CreatiLayout 39.37) and on layout accuracy L_Acc on BindBench (92.18 vs. CreatiLayout 85.18, HybridLayout 78.30). Among training-free methods, LayerBind dominates RAGD (UniDet 44.97 vs. 30.13; L_Acc 92.18 vs. 36.61) and LaRender (UniDet 44.97 vs. 39.41/37.52; O_VQA on BindBench 64.81 vs. 38.53/42.62), confirming that the contextual-sharing mechanism avoids the instance-neglect and concept-blending failures of prior occlusion methods. On image quality (HPS), LayerBind (FLUX, 29.66) matches or slightly exceeds CreatiDesign (29.79) and HybridLayout (29.20) while being 2.6× faster than HybridLayout (30.11 s vs. 78.75 s). On T2I alignment (Table 2), LayerBind+FLUX surpasses HybridLayout on Spatial (70.63 vs. 63.39) and Numeracy (70.93 vs. 64.57) but trails slightly on Shape (66.48 vs. 68.82) and Texture (75.69 vs. 77.31), indicating a small trade-off in fine-grained attribute binding. Overall, LayerBind is the only method that simultaneously achieves top occlusion control, competitive image quality, and low inference overhead without any training.

## Relation to Prior Work

LayerBind extends the training-free regional prompting paradigm for DiTs (RAGD, regional prompting) by adding explicit occlusion-order control, a capability previously addressed only by LaRender on UNet-based models (GLIGEN, IterComp). Its core design repurposes the contextual sharing mechanism inherent to multimodal joint attention in MM-DiT architectures, splitting early denoising into per-instance branches that attend to shared background context before being fused in layer order. The two-phase decomposition (early instance initialization followed by semantic nursing) is motivated by prior observations that spatial layout crystallizes at very early denoising steps, but the paper is the first to exploit this for structured, multi-object occlusion control in a training-free DiT setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LayerBind fills a practical gap: no prior training-free method for DiTs could simultaneously enforce regional layout, per-instance semantics, and explicit occlusion order without quality degradation, and the plug-and-play nature across FLUX and SD3.5 makes it immediately deployable. The inherent editability (changing instances, reordering layers, composited editing) opens a path toward interactive, layer-based image creation workflows. Limitations remain—occasional incomplete object generation, instance-background over-decoupling at high η₁, and poor handling of physically unreasonable layouts—suggesting that robustness to adversarial or ambiguous spatial instructions is still an open problem.
