## Problem

Submodular-based attribution methods like VPS achieve state-of-the-art faithfulness for object-level interpretation, but their core greedy selection requires evaluating every remaining candidate at each step, incurring O(m²) forward passes that are prohibitive for real-world deployment. The paper asks whether the faithfulness of greedy submodular attribution can be preserved while breaking this quadratic computational barrier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260742/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image partitioned into m disjoint subregions (50 or 100), a black-box detection model (Grounding DINO or Florence-2), and a target detection tuple (bounding box, class, score). It produces an ordered subset of regions whose sequential insertion most efficiently recovers the model's confidence on the target, scored by the VPS objective combining a clue score and a collaboration score. The model is queried only via forward passes on masked inputs, with no gradient access required.

## Method

PhaseWin replaces the quadratic greedy loop with a phased coarse-to-fine search. Each phase performs one full evaluation to select an anchor region, then uses the anchor's marginal gain to define fixed-ratio selection and deletion thresholds that prune the majority of low-potential candidates. A WindowSelection subroutine then performs fine-grained search over the surviving high-potential pool using a sliding window and a window policy (top-1 or batch-above-cutoff), augmented by a stage-exit rule that terminates unproductive phases early and an annealed deferral mechanism that avoids poor local choices. Under monotone submodular assumptions, PhaseWin retains a (1 − 1/e − o(1)) approximation guarantee while reducing the expected number of scoring calls from O(mk) to O(m), achieving 5–10× empirical speedup with over 95% of greedy faithfulness.

## Results

On MS COCO (50-region, Grounding DINO), PhaseWin achieves an Insertion AUC of 0.4785 vs. 0.5195 for VPS (Greedy), while reducing model evaluations from 2548.8 to 536.8 (4.7× fewer) and raising the A-C ratio from 2.04 to 8.92. On RefCOCO, PhaseWin reaches 0.7013 Insertion vs. 0.7278 for VPS (Greedy) with only 630.1 evaluations (vs. 2290.6), yielding an A-C ratio of 11.13. On LVIS rare-class detection, PhaseWin-50 requires just 465.9 evaluations (vs. 2544.6 for VPS Greedy) while maintaining 90% of the greedy Insertion score. Across all settings, PhaseWin retains over 95% of greedy faithfulness at roughly 20% of the computational budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhaseWin substantially outperforms all gradient-based baselines (Grad-CAM, SSGrad-CAM++, ODAM) on every faithfulness metric across all three datasets, with Insertion AUC gaps of 0.15–0.35 points. Against perturbation-based methods, PhaseWin-50 achieves higher Insertion AUC than D-RISE (0.4785 vs. 0.4412 on COCO) while requiring only 536.8 model evaluations versus D-RISE's fixed 5000, and also surpasses D-HSIC in faithfulness (0.4785 vs. 0.3776) at comparable cost. The key comparison is against VPS (Greedy): PhaseWin-50 retains 92–96% of greedy Insertion AUC (e.g., 0.4785/0.5195 = 92% on COCO, 0.7013/0.7278 = 96% on RefCOCO) while using only 21–28% of the model evaluations, translating to a 4–11× improvement in A-C ratio. On Florence-2 (Table 3), PhaseWin-50 actually matches or slightly exceeds VPS (Greedy) on RefCOCO Insertion (0.8312 vs. 0.8301) while using fewer evaluations (2349.1 vs. 2547.8). In failure-case settings (Tables 4–6), PhaseWin-100 on RefCOCO REC failures slightly surpasses VPS (Greedy)-100 in Insertion (0.5047 vs. 0.4981) and class score (0.6023 vs. 0.5990) with 3× fewer evaluations, and on undetected COCO samples PhaseWin-100 exceeds VPS (Greedy) in both Insertion (0.2156 vs. 0.2102) and ESR (44.44% vs. 41.33%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VPS (Visual Precision Search), which formulated object-level attribution as maximizing a near-submodular scoring function via greedy region selection. Rather than modifying the scoring function, PhaseWin retains VPS's objective unchanged and replaces the quadratic-cost greedy loop with a phased coarse-to-fine search combining adaptive threshold pruning, windowed local exploration, and annealed deferral. Conceptually, it extends the submodular optimization literature (lazy greedy, fast multi-stage submodular maximization) into the attribution setting, where prior acceleration attempts either ignored submodularity or optimized a proxy rather than the insertion-AUC objective itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic evaluation cost of greedy submodular search was the primary barrier to deploying high-faithfulness attribution in real-world or large-scale settings; PhaseWin's 5–10× speedup with near-greedy quality makes submodular-based interpretation practically viable for safety-critical and open-world perception pipelines. A notable limitation is that the speedup is substantially smaller on Florence-2, whose scoring function exhibits near-globally supermodular behavior, suggesting the method's effectiveness is tied to the local submodularity structure of the objective. Open questions include whether the phased search generalizes to other attribution formulations (e.g., multi-object or video-level) and whether tighter theoretical bounds can be established beyond the o(1) term.
