## Problem

MLLMs are trained on 2D image-text data and lack the 3D spatial structure understanding required for 3D Visual Grounding. Existing MLLM-based 3DVG methods compensate by explicitly reconstructing point clouds and rendering viewpoint-dependent 2D images (multi-view or BEV) at inference, which is computationally expensive and inherently limited by occlusion and viewpoint selection. The paper asks whether a model can be trained to implicitly internalize 3D structure so that spatial reasoning occurs in latent space without any reconstruction at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263289/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 or 24 sampled RGB-D frames from a 3D scene (treated as a video sequence), their camera intrinsics and extrinsics, a set of object-proposal bounding boxes (from Mask3D at inference), and a natural-language referring expression. It outputs the index of the best-matching proposal (via InfoNCE contrastive scoring against a <ground> token hidden state) and a generated text response naming the object category. During training, ground-truth bounding boxes serve as proposals; during inference, predicted boxes from Mask3D are used, and the reconstruction branch is entirely disabled.

## Method

S²-MLLM fine-tunes LLaVA-Video-7B with LoRA and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a Spatial Guidance (SG) strategy attaches a reconstruction branch—comprising the MLLM's visual encoder, a projection layer, and Fast3R's fusion transformer plus decoding heads—that predicts local and global pointmaps with a confidence-weighted regression loss; this branch is active only during training, forcing the shared encoder to develop structure-aware latent representations that enable implicit 3D reasoning at inference. Second, a Structure-Enhanced (SE) module applies divided attention (intra-view attention over patches within each frame and inter-view attention across frames at the same patch index) to capture local context and cross-view semantic consistency, combined with a multi-level position encoding that adds sinusoidal embeddings of per-patch 3D world coordinates and a learnable MLP encoding of the camera-ray viewing direction to each visual token. The overall training objective jointly optimizes an InfoNCE grounding loss, the reconstruction pointmap loss, and a cross-entropy language loss for category generation.

## Results

On ScanRefer, S²-MLLM achieves Overall Acc@0.25 of 59.2% and Acc@0.5 of 52.7%, surpassing the previous SOTA Video-3D-LLM (full fine-tuning) by 1.1% and 1.0% respectively, and outperforming its LoRA-tuned variant by over 5.1% on each metric. On ReferIt3D (Pred setting), it reaches 53.9% on Sr3D and 50.6% on Nr3D, matching or exceeding the best traditional method MCLN. Out-of-distribution evaluation on MultiScan and ARKitScenes yields Acc@0.25 of 59.13% and 43.26% respectively, outperforming both the supervised MCLN and zero-shot SeeGround baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²-MLLM outperforms the strongest MLLM-based baseline Video-3D-LLM (full fine-tuning) by 1.1% on Overall Acc@0.25 and 1.0% on Acc@0.5 on ScanRefer, while requiring only 25% of the trainable parameters (1,767 MB vs. 8,079 MB) and 72 vs. 256 GPU hours. Against the LoRA-tuned Video-3D-LLM, the improvement is more substantial at +5.1% on both metrics, attributed to the spatial guidance and structure-enhanced module. Among traditional supervised methods, S²-MLLM surpasses MCLN (57.2/45.7) and TSP3D (56.5/46.7) on Overall accuracy, and notably achieves a 10.0% gain over Video-3D-LLM on the Multiple subset (Acc@0.5: 46.6 vs. 45.3), where complex spatial reasoning among same-class distractors is critical. On ReferIt3D under the realistic Pred setting, S²-MLLM matches MCLN on Sr3D (53.9%) and leads on Nr3D (50.6% vs. 46.1%), while under the idealized GT setting, traditional methods like MCLN (68.4% Sr3D) still hold an edge due to template-based query exploitation. For out-of-distribution generalization, S²-MLLM beats SeeGround by 5.7% on MultiScan Acc@0.25 and 4.4% on ARKitScenes Acc@0.25, while MCLN drops to single-digit accuracy, confirming that spatial guidance enables dataset-agnostic spatial reasoning rather than memorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the video-sequence representation of 3D scenes from Video-3D-LLM and the LLaVA-Video MLLM backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior MLLM-based 3DVG methods (SeeGround, GPT4Scene) that require explicit point-cloud reconstruction and multi-view rendering at inference time. Instead, it repurposes the feed-forward reconstruction model Fast3R as a training-time auxiliary loss (spatial guidance), allowing the MLLM to internalize 3D structure in latent space without any reconstruction at test time. The structure-enhanced module with divided intra-view/inter-view attention and multi-level position encoding (3D coordinates + camera ray direction) is a novel architectural contribution that addresses the cross-view semantic consistency and fine-grained spatial relation gaps identified in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gain is significant: no point-cloud reconstruction or multi-view rendering at inference, only 25% trainable parameters versus full fine-tuning, and training on a single A100 GPU, making 3DVG more deployable in embodied and robotics settings. The OOD generalization to MultiScan and ARKitScenes without fine-tuning suggests the spatial guidance teaches transferable structure awareness rather than dataset-specific patterns. However, the method still depends on Mask3D proposals at inference and requires RGB-D input, limiting applicability to pure-RGB or real-time streaming scenarios. An open question is whether the implicit spatial reasoning learned through the reconstruction auxiliary loss can be further strengthened or made more interpretable, and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond grounding (e.g., 3D navigation, manipulation).
