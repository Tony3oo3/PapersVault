## Problem

Existing audio-driven digital human animation methods struggle to simultaneously achieve fine-grained text/audio semantic control, robust identity preservation over long durations, and practical 1080P generation efficiency. Training datasets are biased toward narrow scenarios (e.g., portraits only) and lack fine-grained annotations, while long-term inference suffers from latent feature drift that degrades quality and breaks identity consistency. No prior open-source system combines all these capabilities at commercial-grade resolution and speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263784/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single-frame portrait image (supplying subject identity, background, and style), a free-form text prompt (specifying actions, gestures, scene details, shot scale, and camera motion), and an audio track (talking or singing) as joint inputs. It produces 1080P (1088×1920) human animation video, with each generation clip containing 109 pixel-space frames (28 latent frames); long-term videos are assembled by chaining clips with overlap. The framework generalizes beyond single-person portraits to upper-body, full-body, multi-person, anime, and animal scenarios, all scaled to an equivalent 1088×1920 resolution while preserving aspect ratio.

## Method

Soul builds on the Wan2.2-5B DiT backbone and injects Whisper-extracted audio features via a dedicated Audio-Attention layer in each DiT block, initialized from the original text-attention weights to accelerate convergence. For long-term generation, it employs three complementary strategies: a pivotal reference frame duplicated at the start of each clip for identity anchoring, a 2-frame intra-clip latent overlap for inter-clip coherence, and a threshold-aware codebook replacement (K-Means over 40K clusters from Soul-1M latents) that clips out-of-distribution conditioning features toward their nearest centroid to prevent progressive latent feature drift. Training uses 1M finely annotated samples (8.5K hours) with 20% hybrid general-video mixing and negative-prompt augmentation for temporal consistency. For deployment, DMD2-inspired step/CFG distillation (removing the GAN loss) and a lightweight 35M-parameter eVAE decoder (vs. 555M original) jointly yield an 11.4× end-to-end speedup with negligible quality loss (LPIPS 0.0324→0.052).

## Results

On Soul-Bench, Soul achieves the best scores on four of six metrics: Video-Text Consistence (4.85), LSE-D lip-sync error distance (0.130, vs. next-best 0.419), Identity Consistence (0.763), and Video Quality (72.60). Soul trails InfiniteTalk on LSE-C (6.82 vs. 8.48) and Wan-S2V on Audio-Video Alignment (0.255 vs. 0.330), though the paper notes these metrics approach a ceiling where differences become less distinguishable. In a human study against commercial products, Soul outperforms HeyGen and Kling-Avatar on overall naturalness (4.17 vs. 4.07/3.93), ID consistency (4.00 vs. 3.54/3.86), and text consistency (4.11 vs. 3.82/3.90), while tying HeyGen on audio-visual synchronization (4.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Soul dominates on lip-sync accuracy (LSE-D: 0.130 vs. 0.419 for HunyuanVideo-Avatar, 0.663 for Sonic, and 5.455 for Wan-S2V) and identity preservation (0.763 vs. 0.750 for Wan-S2V, 0.733 for StableAvatar, and 0.497 for OmniAvatar), while also leading in video-text alignment (4.85) and video quality (72.60). The closest competitor is Wan-S2V, which edges out Soul on Audio-Video Alignment (0.330 vs. 0.255) but is far behind on LSE-D (5.455 vs. 0.130) and video quality (71.22 vs. 72.60). InfiniteTalk achieves the highest LSE-C (8.48 vs. Soul's 6.82) but trails Soul on every other metric, particularly LSE-D (2.313) and identity consistency (0.609). OmniAvatar is the weakest baseline overall, scoring last on identity consistency (0.497) and video quality (67.24). In the human study, Soul beats both commercial products (HeyGen, Kling-Avatar) on three of four dimensions, with the only tie being audio-visual synchronization (4.20 vs. HeyGen's 4.20).

## Relation to Prior Work

Soul extends the DiT-based audio-driven human animation lineage (Hallo3, AlignHuman, Wan-S2V, OmniAvatar) by building on the Wan2.2-5B backbone and adding Whisper-extracted audio features via a dedicated Audio-Attention layer initialized from text-attention weights. Its key conceptual departures are threefold: (1) a 1M-sample, multi-scenario training set (Soul-1M) that addresses the data scarcity limiting prior methods to narrow portrait-only settings; (2) a long-term generation pipeline combining pivotal-frame conditioning, intra-clip latent overlap, and a threshold-aware K-Means codebook replacement to counteract the train/inference distribution mismatch that causes identity drift; and (3) a deployment stack (step/CFG distillation via DMD2-style training plus a 35M-parameter eVAE decoder) that targets 1080P real-time inference rather than offline quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.4× end-to-end speedup at 1080P with only marginal metric drops makes high-fidelity digital-human animation practically deployable for virtual anchors and film production, a gap that prior open-source models could not close. Soul-1M (8.5K hours across portrait/upper-body/full-body/multi-person) and Soul-Bench (226 curated samples with multi-metric evaluation) fill real resource gaps that have hindered fair comparison in this area. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge artifacts on complex full-body motions, the absence of cross-lingual audio robustness, and the lack of 3D geometric priors—directions that could further close the gap between generated and real human video.
