## Problem

Metric-based few-shot classification methods, particularly Prototypical Networks, are highly sensitive to label noise in the support set: a single mislabeled sample shifts the class prototype and distorts the decision boundary. Existing robust methods designed for large-scale datasets fail under the extreme sample scarcity of few-shot settings, and current graph-based FSL approaches rely on nominal labels or fixed k-NN rules that are easily misled by noisy annotations, producing spurious edges that corrupt intra-class and inter-class relationships.

## Input / Output

The method operates in an N-way K-shot episodic protocol (5-way 5-shot with 15 query samples per class), taking RGB images resized to 84×84 pixels as input. Support images carry potentially noisy labels (20% or 40% corruption across three noise types: in-episode, out-of-episode, and out-of-distribution), while query images are unlabeled at inference. The output is a class label per query sample, obtained by applying softmax to the negative Euclidean distances between the query's graph-refined feature and each dynamically generated class prototype.

## Method

GAPNet combines three modules: (1) a feature extractor that fuses local CNN-4+SE features with global features from a Group Attention Broad Learning (GABL) module—a flat network with randomly initialized and partially fixed transformation weights plus group-level attention to suppress noisy patterns; (2) a Pseudo-Label Guided Graph Constructor (PLGC) that builds separate intra-class and inter-class adjacency matrices using top-k feature similarity (with pseudo-labels assigned to queries via initial prototypes) and an edge-attention module that scores each edge's reliability, followed by an Edge-Aware Graph Attention (EAGAT) layer that processes the two graph types in parallel branches, weighting message passing by edge reliability; (3) an Adaptive Noise-Robust Prototype Generator (ANRPG) that assigns exponential distance-based weights to down-weight outliers, refines each support feature by aggregating its intra-class neighbors via softmax attention, and computes the final prototype as the mean of refined features. Training uses cross-entropy on query predictions plus a prototype contrastive regularization loss that enforces inter-prototype separation, all optimized via episodic meta-training with AdamW.

## Results

On the 5-way 5-shot clean benchmark, GAPNet achieves 75.71% on CIFAR-FS, 65.16% on miniImageNet, 69.22% on tieredImageNet, and 80.63% on CUB-200-2011, outperforming the best prior method by 2.84%, 2.01%, 1.64%, and 0.61% respectively. Under 40% in-episode label noise, GAPNet retains 58.04% on CIFAR-FS and 47.33% on miniImageNet, while ProtoNet drops to 44.76% and 40.57% respectively. Across all three noise types (IE, OOE, OOD) at 20% and 40% ratios, GAPNet consistently leads by 3–13 percentage points over the strongest competitor (APPN or BiFRN), with the largest margin observed on miniImageNet under 40% IE noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAPNet outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and noise condition. On clean data, the closest competitor is BiFRN [40], which leads on CUB-200-2011 (80.02% vs. 80.63%) and miniImageNet (63.15% vs. 65.16%), while MatchingNet [37] is strongest on CIFAR-FS (72.87% vs. 75.71%). Under label noise, the gap widens substantially: on miniImageNet with 40% IE noise, GAPNet (47.33%) surpasses ProtoNet [33] (40.57%) by 6.76 points and APPN [7] (43.73%) by 3.60 points. APPN [7], the most recent noise-robust method, is the strongest baseline under OOE and OOD noise but still trails GAPNet by 3–8 points across all settings. RapNets [24], RNNP [25], and RelationNet [35] show the largest degradation under 40% IE noise (dropping 15–20 points from clean), whereas GAPNet's drop is comparatively smaller, confirming its noise-robust prototype generation. HGNN [45] and tSF [19], which use graph/transformer structures without explicit noise handling, perform mid-pack on clean data but degrade sharply under IE noise, validating the paper's claim that standard graph construction is easily misled by label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

GAPNet extends the Prototypical Network framework (Snell et al., 2017) by replacing its simple mean-prototype aggregation with a multi-stage pipeline that incorporates graph-based relation modeling and adaptive prototype refinement. It departs from prior robust FSL methods (RapNets, RNNP, APPN, TraNFS) that address label noise through feature-level weighting or distance-metric modification, instead explicitly modeling intra-class and inter-class sample relationships via a pseudo-label guided graph constructor and edge-aware graph attention. The integration of a Broad Learning System with group attention for global feature extraction is a novel architectural choice not seen in prior few-shot or robust FSL work, complementing the standard ConvNet-4 backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label noise in few-shot support sets is a practical concern in domains like medical imaging and remote sensing where annotation budgets are tight, yet prior robust FSL methods have shown limited effectiveness in this regime. GAPNet's consistent 3–8% accuracy gains across four benchmarks and three noise types demonstrate that explicit topological relation modeling is a more principled noise-mitigation strategy than feature-level reweighting alone. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-way/higher-shot settings, whether the graph construction generalizes beyond 5-way tasks, and whether the Broad Learning component's benefit persists with stronger pretrained backbones (e.g., ResNet-12, ViT).
