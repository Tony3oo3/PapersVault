## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) exhibit a persistent capability gap: they excel at visual understanding but produce comparatively weak text-to-image generation, largely because the two processes are trained independently with sparse cross-task integration. Joint optimization of understanding and generation further causes negative transfer, where gains in one task hinder the other. The paper asks whether a UMM's own understanding branch can be repurposed as an internal evaluator to guide and improve its generation without any external supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text-only prompts (50,000 samples describing objects with positional, quantitative, and fine-grained attribute relationships) as the sole training input, requiring no external image datasets. The generation branch autoregressively produces discrete image tokens that are decoded into full-resolution RGB images via a diffusion head. During reward computation, the understanding branch re-processes each generated image together with the original prompt to yield token-level alignment probabilities, forming a closed-loop generation-evaluation cycle entirely within the model.

## Method

GvU (Generate via Understanding) computes a token-level intrinsic reward by evaluating the autoregressive probability of each ground-truth prompt token conditioned on the generated image, then taking the geometric mean across all tokens to mitigate length bias. This dense, semantically aligned signal replaces coarse image-level rewards (e.g., CLIPScore) and provides fine-grained per-token feedback on text-image correspondence. The reward drives a self-supervised GRPO reinforcement learning loop: for each prompt, a group of G images is generated, their intrinsic rewards are normalized within the group to compute advantages, and the policy is updated with clipped probability ratios plus a KL penalty against a reference model. No external reward model, value function, or additional image supervision is needed—the understanding branch serves simultaneously as teacher and the generation branch as student, enabling iterative self-improvement via LoRA fine-tuning on the X-Omni AR+diffusion architecture.

## Results

On GenEval, GvU improves the base X-Omni model from 0.68 to 0.81 overall (19.1% relative gain), reaching 0.84 with LLM prompt rewriting to match GPT-4o and BLIP3-o 8B. On DPG-Bench, GvU attains 85.68 overall, surpassing BAGEL (85.07) and Janus-Pro (84.19). On the more challenging GenEval++ benchmark, GvU achieves 0.404 overall, a 43.3% improvement over the base model (0.282→0.404) and outperforming all compared UMMs including BAGEL (0.371) and OmniGen2 (0.325). On fine-grained visual understanding (MMT-Bench), GvU shows consistent gains across subtasks (e.g., Visual Illusion +5.06, Visual Commonsense Reasoning +5.0) without degrading standard understanding benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, GvU (0.81) substantially outperforms its base model XOmni (0.68) and other UMMs like Show-o (0.53), Emu3 (0.54), and Janus (0.61), but trails Janus-Pro (0.80), OmniGen2 (0.80), and BAGEL (0.81) by a narrow margin; with LLM prompt rewriting, GvU† reaches 0.84, tying GPT-4o and BLIP3-o 8B. On DPG-Bench, GvU (85.68) achieves the best overall score among all compared models, edging out BAGEL (85.07) and Janus-Pro (84.19), with particularly strong Entity (91.38) and Relation (93.88) sub-scores. On GenEval++, GvU (0.404) clearly leads all baselines, surpassing BAGEL (0.371) by 3.3 points and OmniGen2 (0.325) by 7.9 points, with the largest gains in Pos/Size (0.675 vs. 0.475 for BAGEL) and Pos/Count (0.525 vs. 0.250 for BAGEL). On standard visual understanding benchmarks (POPE, GQA, MMBench, SEED, DocVQA, OCRBench), GvU remains essentially on par with the X-Omni base (e.g., POPE 86.3 vs. 86.1, DocVQA 88.4 vs. 89.1), confirming that generation-focused RL does not degrade comprehension. The ablation on the weak base model further shows that GvU yields a +138.1% GenEval improvement when the understanding-generation gap is wider, versus +19.1% on the regular base, validating the self-teaching mechanism's dependence on this asymmetry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the X-Omni unified multimodal architecture (autoregressive backbone with a diffusion head) and adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm for self-supervised training of UMMs. It departs from prior RL-for-UMM work that relies on external reward models (CLIPScore, ImageReward, HPS) or reconstruction losses (RecA) by proposing a token-level intrinsic reward computed entirely from the model's own understanding branch, creating a closed-loop self-teaching signal. The conceptual framing—using the understanding branch as an internal 'teacher' to guide the generation branch—extends the self-rewarding paradigm from LLMs to the multimodal generation setting, but with the key distinction that the reward is derived from the model's visual comprehension rather than from a separate scoring network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway to closing the understanding-generation gap in UMMs without external supervision, which is significant given that joint training of both capabilities typically causes negative transfer. The bidirectional finding—that improved generation also strengthens fine-grained visual understanding (e.g., +5.06 on Visual Illusion, +5.0 on Visual Commonsense Reasoning in MMT-Bench)—suggests a genuine synergy rather than a one-directional transfer, though the absolute understanding gains remain modest. A key open question is whether this self-teaching dynamic scales beyond the single X-Omni architecture tested here, and whether the modest understanding improvements can be amplified to fully realize the mutual-enhancement vision the authors outline.
