## Problem

Existing interactive world generation models suffer from three critical limitations: they are trained on game datasets creating a domain gap for realistic scenes, their diffusion-based inference is too slow for real-time continuous exploration, and they lack text-controlled event generation, supporting only keyboard/mouse camera control. These bottlenecks collectively prevent the creation of realistic, explorable, and dynamically editable virtual worlds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262042/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (decomposed into an event description specifying the scene and an action description encoding discrete keyboard/mouse controls), an optional conditioning image for I2V mode, and continuous keyboard inputs during inference. It produces autoregressive video chunks at 544×960 or 704×1280 resolution and 16 FPS, where each new chunk is conditioned on the model's own previously generated frames compressed via TSCM. The action description is precomputed and cached since the set of possible keyboard commands is finite, while the event description is processed only at the initial generation step, substantially reducing T5 overhead during subsequent inference.

## Method

Yume1.5 introduces Joint Temporal-Spatial-Channel Modeling (TSCM), which compresses historical frames along two complementary axes: temporal-spatial compression (random 1/32 frame sampling plus variable-rate Patchify from (1,2,2) to (1,8,8) depending on temporal distance) fed through standard DiT attention, and channel-wise compression (Patchify at (8,4,4) reducing channels to 96) fused with prediction frames via a linear attention layer using ReLU kernel. For real-time acceleration, the paper combines Self-Forcing (training the generator on its own outputs to mitigate error accumulation) with TSCM replacing the KV cache, then applies DMD distillation to reduce inference to 4 steps. Text-controlled event generation is achieved by decomposing captions into event and action descriptions processed separately through T5, combined with an alternating T2V/I2V training strategy on a mixed dataset (Sekai-Real-HQ, synthetic Openvid videos, and a curated 4,000-sample Event Dataset) built on the Wan-2.2-5B DiT backbone.

## Results

On Yume-Bench (544×960, 16 FPS, 96 frames, 4 inference steps), Yume1.5 achieves the highest Instruction Following score of 0.836, substantially outperforming Yume (0.657), MatrixGame (0.271), and Wan-2.2-5B (0.243). It also attains the best Background Consistency (0.945) and matches Yume on Subject Consistency (0.932) and Motion Smoothness (0.985). Most strikingly, Yume1.5 reduces per-chunk inference time from 572 s (Yume) and 971 s (MatrixGame) to just 8 s, achieving an average generation speed of 12 fps at 540p on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Yume (the direct predecessor), Yume1.5 improves Instruction Following by 0.179 (0.836 vs 0.657) and Background Consistency by 0.004, while matching Subject Consistency (0.932) and trailing by only 0.001 on Motion Smoothness and 0.012 on Aesthetic Quality. The dramatic speedup—71× faster per chunk (8 s vs 572 s)—comes from the TSCM + Self-Forcing distillation pipeline with only 4 sampling steps, versus Yume's 50 steps. Compared to MatrixGame, Yume1.5 wins decisively on Instruction Following (0.836 vs 0.271) and Aesthetic Quality (0.506 vs 0.435), but concedes Imaging Quality (0.728 vs 0.750) and is 121× faster. Against the general-purpose Wan-2.2-5B, Yume1.5 dominates on Instruction Following (0.836 vs 0.243) and all consistency metrics, confirming that the interactive-world training paradigm yields far superior camera-control fidelity than text-only conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

Yume1.5 extends the interactive world generation line initiated by Yume and Matrix-Game, which rely on game-domain data and spatial-only context compression, by introducing Joint Temporal-Spatial-Channel Modeling (TSCM) that replaces the KV cache mechanism inherited from CausVid and Self-Forcing. It builds on the Wan-2.2-5B diffusion transformer backbone and the Self-Forcing training paradigm (which forces the model to condition on its own generated frames to reduce train-inference discrepancy), but departs from Self-Forcing by substituting TSCM for the KV cache, enabling substantially longer context without truncation. The text-controlled event generation capability is a novel addition absent from prior interactive world models (Yume, Matrix-Game, WORLDMEM), which support only keyboard/mouse control, and is enabled by a decomposed caption architecture (Event vs. Action descriptions) and a mixed-dataset training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70× speedup over Yume (8 s vs. 572 s per video, 12 fps on a single A100) makes real-time interactive world exploration practically feasible for the first time in this model family, opening the door to deployment in interactive entertainment and virtual embodiment. The text-controlled event generation (e.g., triggering a UFO or sudden rainfall via prompt) adds a layer of user agency beyond camera navigation that prior systems lack. Limitations include the 540p resolution ceiling, reliance on a single A100, and evaluation on a relatively narrow Yume-Bench; open questions include whether TSCM's compression strategy generalizes to more complex multi-agent interactions, higher resolutions, or non-walking camera motions, and whether the Self-Forcing + TSCM distillation paradigm transfers to other video diffusion backbones.
