## Problem

Reconstructing HDR video from alternately exposed LDR frames is ill-posed due to large inter-frame motion, exposure-induced photometric inconsistency, and information loss in saturated or underexposed regions. Prior pipelines follow an alignment-reconstruction paradigm that explicitly registers frames to a reference, but this is limited by alignment precision and propagates ghosting artifacts into the final reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261698/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a three-frame sliding window of LDR video captured in alternating exposure modes (e.g., EV-3/EV+0 for two-exposure or EV-2/EV+0/EV+2 for three-exposure), where a pretrained FiLM interpolator first synthesizes a non-reference intermediate frame so the core network operates on a reference frame plus one interpolated frame. The output is a single HDR frame in the calibrated linear radiance domain (L^γ/e with γ=2.2), and the final fusion is a normalized convex combination of six linear-HDR candidates per pixel.

## Method

LRHDR replaces explicit cross-exposure alignment with two components. The ACCR network first encodes each exposure stream with an Exposure-aware Interleaved Context encoder (a large-field convolutional branch plus a pixel-unshuffle high-fidelity branch, gated by a learned scalar exposure gate), then a Representation Mapper applies per-pixel, per-channel affine modulation (K⊙F+B) guided by signed and squared cross-exposure cues computed in the linear-radiance domain, projecting all exposures onto a shared representation that is decoded into linear HDR. The APSWF module then performs pixel-wise candidate selection over six linear-HDR candidates using a U-Net with triplet-attention blocks and multi-scale heads, projecting logits onto the 6-simplex via α-entmax (α=1.75) to yield sparse winner-takes-most masks, and fusing candidates as a normalized weighted sum in the linear HDR domain. Training is supervised end-to-end with an ACCR loss (L1 plus multi-scale gradient in a μ-law tonemapped domain), a Vote Loss (entmax cross-entropy against oracle per-pixel candidate selection), and a frame reconstruction L1 loss.

## Results

On the Cinematic Video dataset under 2-exposure, LRHDR achieves 41.11 PSNR_T, 0.9274 SSIM_T, and 75.23 HDR-VDP-2, outperforming the strongest baseline NECHDR (40.59 / 0.9241 / 73.31) by +0.52 dB, +0.0033, and +1.92 respectively. Under 3-exposure on the same dataset, the HDR-VDP-2 gap widens to +2.65 over NECHDR (71.01 vs 68.36). On DeepHDRVideo (2-exposure), LRHDR reaches 43.49 PSNR_T and 80.68 HDR-VDP-2, edging out NECHDR (43.44 / 79.20). The only metric where a baseline prevails is 3-exposure HDR-VDP-2 on DeepHDRVideo, where NECHDR scores 76.98 vs LRHDR's 76.01.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LRHDR outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR_T and SSIM_T across every dataset and exposure setting. The strongest competitor is NECHDR [6], which is the closest on DeepHDRVideo 2-exposure (PSNR_T 43.44 vs 43.49) but is clearly surpassed on Cinematic Video (e.g., +0.52 dB PSNR_T and +1.92 HDR-VDP-2 under 2-exposure). HDRFlow [39] is competitive on 3-exposure DeepHDRVideo PSNR_T (40.56 vs 40.93) and is the second-best on 2-exposure DeepHDRVideo HDR-VDP-2 (77.29 vs 80.68). Chen et al. [2] and LAN-HDR [4] trail by 1–3 dB in PSNR_T depending on the setting. Kalantari [14], Yan [40], and Prabhakar [30] are consistently the weakest baselines, with Yan [40] scoring the lowest PSNR_T (31.65) on Cinematic Video 2-exposure. The sole exception to LRHDR's dominance is 3-exposure HDR-VDP-2 on DeepHDRVideo, where NECHDR edges ahead (76.98 vs 76.01), though LRHDR still leads on PSNR_T and SSIM_T in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant alignment-reconstruction paradigm in HDR video reconstruction (optical-flow warping, deformable convolution, attention-based registration, state-space propagation) by eliminating explicit cross-exposure alignment entirely. Instead, it maps features from different exposure modes into a unified, exposure-independent representation via per-pixel, per-channel affine modulation (drawing on FiLM-style conditioning and adaptive instance normalization), then performs fusion as sparse pixel-wise candidate selection using α-entmax in the linear HDR domain. The pretrained FiLM interpolator supplies a reference-free intermediate frame, and the U-Net backbone with triplet attention provides multi-scale context for the fusion masks, but the core novelty is the representation-mapping-plus-sparse-voting pipeline that replaces alignment-then-fuse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a well-designed representation mapper can substitute for explicit alignment, this work offers a simpler and more motion-robust alternative for HDR video pipelines, particularly under large displacements where optical flow degrades. The sparse fusion formulation (winner-takes-most masks via α-entmax) is a clean, interpretable mechanism for handling per-pixel reliability across exposures. Limitations include reliance on a pretrained interpolator, evaluation only on 2- and 3-exposure alternating patterns, and a slight HDR-VDP-2 regression on the 3-exposure DeepHDRVideo setting versus NECHDR. Open questions include scalability to more exposure levels, non-alternating or irregular exposure schedules, and whether the representation-mapping idea transfers to other multi-view or multi-sensor fusion tasks.
