## Problem

Most existing video tokenizers assign a fixed number of tokens to every frame, ignoring the inherent redundancy in video content. While adaptive tokenization has been explored for images, extending it to video remains challenging: prior attempts like ElasticTok rely on 2D spatial priors and fixed thresholds, leading to imbalanced allocation across samples and regions. The paper asks how to build a video tokenizer that is simultaneously temporally causal (supporting streaming), operates in a 1D latent space decoupled from spatial structure, and globally optimizes token allocation under a controllable budget.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes 16×128×128 video clips, which are patchified at 4×8×8 into 1024 tokens organized into K=4 causal blocks of M=512 tokens each. At inference, a global token budget (average tokens per block N_b) is specified, and the ILP solver assigns each block a variable token count between 32 and 512. The output is a variable-length discrete token sequence (with an end-of-block token per block) that can be decoded back to video or fed to a Llama-style AR transformer for generation.

## Method

AdapTok uses a block-causal transformer encoder that projects 3D video patches into a 1D latent sequence via learnable latent tokens, with block-causal attention ensuring each block only attends to itself and prior blocks. During training, a block-wise tail-drop sampler randomly discards trailing tokens per block (sampled from a truncated Gaussian), forcing the model to encode information hierarchically from head to tail. A separate block-causal scorer, trained with perceptual-loss ground-truth scores, predicts reconstruction quality for every candidate token length in a single forward pass. At inference, an Integer Linear Programming solver (IPAL) jointly optimizes per-block token counts across a mini-batch to minimize total predicted perceptual loss subject to a global token budget. The full system is trained with L1, VQ, perceptual, adversarial, and autoregressive-prior losses, and for generation the adaptive tokens are decoded by a Llama-style AR transformer with end-of-block delimiters.

## Results

On UCF-101 video reconstruction, AdapTok achieves rFVD=28 with 2048 tokens and rFVD=36 with 1024 tokens, outperforming all causal tokenizer baselines. With only 512 tokens, AdapTok still achieves rFVD=60, surpassing CausalTok (rFVD=37 at 1024 tokens) with 1.8× fewer tokens. For video generation, AdapTok-AR achieves gFVD=11 on Kinetics-600 frame prediction and gFVD=67 on UCF-101 class-conditional generation with 633M parameters, substantially outperforming CausalTok (gFVD=80) and MAGVIT-v2-AR (gFVD=109) on UCF-101.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdapTok consistently outperforms all compared causal video tokenizers on UCF-101 reconstruction. Against the closest non-adaptive baseline CausalTok (same architecture, fixed tokens), AdapTok achieves rFVD 36 vs. 37 at 1024 tokens and rFVD 60 vs. 37 at 512 vs. 1024 tokens, demonstrating the value of adaptive allocation. Compared to ElasticTok (the prior adaptive video tokenizer), AdapTok improves rFVD from 230 to 36 at comparable token budgets and is 11× faster at inference (50.9 ms vs. 571.7 ms per video). Against OmniTokenizer and Cosmos-DV, AdapTok achieves rFVD 36 vs. 94 and 140 respectively at similar or fewer tokens. For generation, AdapTok-AR (gFVD 67 on UCF, 11 on K600) surpasses CausalTok (80), MAGVIT-v2-AR (109), OmniTokenizer (191), and Phenaki (36.4 on K600), confirming that adaptive tokenization benefits downstream autoregressive generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdapTok unifies three previously separate design axes in video tokenization: the 1D latent token space from TiTok and LARP (learnable latent tokens decoupling representation from spatial layout), temporal causality from CosmosTokenizer and OmniTokenizer (block-causal attention for streaming), and adaptive variable-length encoding from ElasticTok, OneD-Piece, and FlexTok (tail-drop regularization). The key departure from the closest prior work, ElasticTok, is twofold: (1) the 1D transformer architecture removes the 2D spatial prior that limited ElasticTok's tail-drop effectiveness, and (2) a learned causal scorer combined with integer linear programming (IPAL) replaces ElasticTok's fixed-threshold heuristic with a globally optimal, budget-constrained allocation across a mini-batch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AdapTok demonstrates that adaptive token allocation can yield Pareto-optimal trade-offs between reconstruction quality and token budget, making autoregressive video generation more scalable and token-efficient without sacrificing quality. The 11× inference speedup over ElasticTok and the robustness of the 1D space to aggressive token reduction (rFVD 94 vs. 850 at 512 tokens) are practically significant for deployment. However, the model is trained on fewer than 0.5M videos and limited to 16-frame clips, leaving open questions about scaling to longer sequences, continuous (non-VQ) latent spaces, and broader domain generalization that the authors explicitly flag as future work.
