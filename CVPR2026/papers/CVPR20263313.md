## Problem

Existing event-based motion deblurring methods rely on handcrafted fixed-weight kernels (e.g., Voxel Grid, SCER) to aggregate sparse event streams into frames, which cannot adapt to the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of real-world motion where different regions exhibit varying speeds and directions. This lack of sample adaptivity leads to poor event representations in both dense (fast-motion) and sparse (slow-motion) regions, limiting the effective exploitation of motion cues for deblurring.

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurred RGB image (256×256 patches during training; 1280×720 for GoPro) and a corresponding asynchronous event stream with discrete (x, y, t, polarity) tuples normalized to [0,255]³. Positive and negative polarity events are processed separately, and the event stream is synchronously segmented to match the image patch. The output is a single deblurred RGB image at the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

The core contribution is a learnable 3D Gaussian event representation module that adaptively selects K spatiotemporal kernel centers and covariance matrices via a multi-branch MLP sampler (inspired by SampleNet), conditioned on a voxelized event histogram augmented with a blur-map prior from the input image. Each 3D Gaussian kernel applies a full covariance weighting to aggregate events into 2D frames, enabling the attention range to shrink for fast motion and expand for slow motion. A two-stage fusion strategy then exploits these frames: the first stage integrates local multi-scale motion features for fine detail restoration, while the second stage introduces a 1D Gaussian-weighted global event frame processed through a Bidirectional Attention Fusion (BAF) module (SE-based channel attention + FFN) to correct spatial misalignment. The overall architecture is built on EFNet with depthwise separable 3D convolutions, trained end-to-end on GoPro-ESIM and fine-tuned on HS-ERGB and REBlur.

## Results

On the GoPro dataset, the proposed method achieves 36.86 dB PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, surpassing the previous best (SepNet, 36.70 dB) by 0.16 dB. On HS-ERGB, it reaches 28.55 dB PSNR and 0.813 SSIM, a 0.62 dB improvement over MAENet (27.93 dB). On the real-world REBlur dataset, it attains 38.68 dB PSNR and 0.977 SSIM, outperforming SepNet (38.53 dB) by 0.15 dB. The model uses 16.7M parameters and 172.6G FLOPs at 224×224 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best PSNR on all three benchmarks, outperforming the strongest event-based competitor SepNet [43] by 0.16 dB on GoPro (36.86 vs. 36.70) and 0.15 dB on REBlur (38.68 vs. 38.53), while also matching its SSIM of 0.977. On HS-ERGB, the gain is most pronounced: 28.55 dB versus MAENet [26]'s 27.93 dB (+0.62 dB), indicating the adaptive 3D Gaussian representation handles the semi-synthetic blur distribution particularly well. Among image-only baselines, UFPNet [6] (34.06 dB GoPro) and NAFNet [3] (33.71 dB) trail by over 2.8 dB, underscoring the value of event cues. The closest event-based competitors—MAENet [26] (36.07 dB GoPro, 38.47 dB REBlur) and EFNet [22] (35.46 dB GoPro, 38.12 dB REBlur)—are surpassed by 0.79–0.82 dB on GoPro and 0.21–0.56 dB on REBlur. Notably, the method uses 16.7M parameters, comparable to MAENet (13.9M) and EFNet (8.5M), while image-only methods like HINet (88.7M) and UFPNet (80.3M) require 5× more parameters yet underperform.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based motion deblurring line initiated by EFNet [22], whose two-stage cross-modal fusion architecture serves as the backbone for the proposed network. The key departure is replacing handcrafted event representations (Voxel Grid, SCER, SBT, DA) with a learnable 3D Gaussian event representation module that adaptively selects spatiotemporal sampling locations and adjusts kernel covariance per sample, drawing on the differentiable sampling idea from SampleNet [15]. The bidirectional attention fusion module for global alignment repurposes Squeeze-and-Excitation [10] blocks in a cross-modal setting, and the depthwise-separable 3D CNN encoder follows standard hierarchical feature extraction patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in event-based deblurring: fixed-kernel event representations cannot handle the wide range of motion speeds and directions present in real scenes, leading to quality inconsistencies across regions. The adaptive 3D Gaussian formulation is a natural and well-motivated solution, and the consistent (if modest) gains across synthetic, semi-synthetic, and real-world datasets validate the approach. However, the added complexity of the 3D CNN encoder, multi-head MLP sampler, and BAF module raises questions about computational efficiency at higher resolutions and real-time deployment. An open question is whether the full 3D covariance (with off-diagonal coupling terms) is necessary, or whether simpler adaptive temporal windows could capture most of the benefit at lower cost.
