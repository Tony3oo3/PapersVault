## Problem

Embodied navigation research is fragmented into narrowly defined tasks (ObjNav, PointNav, ImgNav, VLN), each with separate benchmarks and methods, so agents trained on one task cannot handle others. No existing benchmark supports free-form instructions that simultaneously combine multiple modalities (text, reference images, coordinates) and multiple navigation capabilities within a single command, leaving a gap toward truly generalist navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260667/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multi-modal instruction (free-form text with embedded reference images and 3D coordinate placeholders), a sequence of historical egocentric frames (video), and the current egocentric image. In standard mode it outputs a single low-level action (move forward / turn left / turn right / stop) paired with a magnitude (distance in cm or angle in degrees); in TBA mode it first emits a <Think> reasoning block before the <Action> block, and the mode is switched at inference time purely via the prompt, allowing flexible control over thinking frequency (e.g., every 10, 20, or 40 steps).

## Method

OctoNav-R1 is built on LLaMA-VID and trained via a three-stage Hybrid Training Paradigm (HTP). Stage I performs Action-SFT on 45k+ instruction-trajectory pairs, followed by TBA-SFT on a 10k+ Think-Before-Action Chain-of-Thought dataset (constructed by prompting Qwen-VL for scene descriptions and DeepSeek-R1 for reasoning traces conditioned on ground-truth actions). Stage II applies Nav-GRPO, a GRPO variant with a stepped reward (1.0 for exact action+magnitude match, 0.5 for correct action with wrong magnitude, 0 otherwise) and diverse prompts to further sharpen reasoning quality. Stage III runs online A2C reinforcement learning in continuous Habitat environments, using a distance-to-goal reward and a linear critic on the last Transformer hidden states, with a warm-up phase that freezes the policy while training the critic. The key design insight is the Think-Before-Action paradigm: by interleaving explicit reasoning with action generation, the model gains the deliberative capacity needed to track multi-step, multi-capability instructions that direct observation-to-action mapping cannot handle.

## Results

On OctoNav-Bench, OctoNav-R1 achieves 19.40% SR, 13.77% SPL, and 29.40% OSR overall, more than doubling the best prior method (Uni-NaVid† at 9.20% SR). Per-capability, OctoNav-R1 leads on all five navigation tasks: ObjNav (49.18% SR), VLN (37.14% SR), Ins-ImgNav (30.24% SR), ImgNav (23.97% SR), and PointNav (23.51% SR). The ablation study shows progressive gains from each HTP stage: Base (5.80%) → +Action-SFT (8.80%) → +TBA-SFT (14.40%) → +Nav-GRPO (17.00%) → +Online RL (19.40%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctoNav-R1 (19.40% SR) more than doubles the strongest baseline Uni-NaVid† (9.20% SR) and NaVid† (8.80% SR), both of which were fine-tuned on OctoNav-Bench. The gap is even larger against discrete-environment methods adapted to continuous settings: NavGPT-2* achieves only 2.00% SR and NaviLLM* only 1.20% SR, confirming the substantial difficulty of multi-modal, multi-capability instructions in continuous environments. Off-the-shelf MLLMs used as zero-shot agents perform worst: Video-LLaVA reaches 0.80% SR, LLaVA-NeXT 0.20%, and Qwen-VL 0.00%, indicating that general-purpose vision-language models cannot handle compound navigation instructions without task-specific training. Per-capability, OctoNav-R1 leads on all five tasks, with the largest margins on ImgNav (+13.64% SR over Uni-NaVid†) and PointNav (+11.96% SR), while the smallest margin is on ObjNav (+6.56% SR over Uni-NaVid†), suggesting ObjNav is the most transferable capability from prior multi-task training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of embodied navigation (ObjNav, PointNav, ImgNav, Ins-ImgNav, VLN) into a single free-form, multi-modal, multi-capability instruction-following setting, departing from prior multi-task benchmarks like GOAT-Bench and LHPR-VLN that keep tasks independent. Architecturally, it builds on LLaMA-VID as a video-based VLA backbone and adapts the DeepSeek-R1 'think-before-answer' paradigm to embodied navigation as 'think-before-action' (TBA), a conceptual transfer not previously attempted in this domain. The Hybrid Training Paradigm (Action-SFT → TBA-SFT → Nav-GRPO → Online RL) extends the GRPO recipe from text-only LLMs to continuous-environment navigation with a linear critic and A2C, combining imitation learning and active RL in a single pipeline for the first time in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OctoNav-Bench is likely to become a standard reference for generalist navigation research, as it is the first benchmark to jointly require multi-modal (visual, linguistic, spatial) and multi-capability instruction following in continuous environments with 45k+ pairs across 400+ scenes. The TBA-CoT dataset and Nav-GRPO provide a reusable recipe for injecting deliberative reasoning into VLA models, potentially transferable to other embodied tasks. Key limitations include still-low absolute success rates (~19% SR), preliminary (not rigorous) sim2real evaluation, and an unoptimized thinking frequency; open questions include whether the TBA paradigm scales to longer horizons, more diverse scenes, and real-world deployment without task-specific fine-tuning.
