## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models systematically fail at generating scenes with multiple distinct humans, producing merged identities, duplicated faces, or incorrect person counts. The root cause is that existing methods conflate spatial layout reasoning and identity rendering in a single generation pass, causing the model to lose track of where each person should appear and which face belongs where. This failure persists across regional conditioning, identity-preserving, and unified multi-ID architectures on recent benchmarks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (e.g., 'three people playing basketball') and N reference identity images (1–5 people) as input, and produces a single 1024×1024 photorealistic image with all referenced individuals correctly placed and identifiable. An intermediate canvas representation—comprising bounding boxes and optionally pose skeletons—is constructed from the Architect's layout prediction and fed to the Artist as spatial conditioning. The system is trained primarily on synthetic multi-person scenes paired with real reference faces, requiring no real multi-human training images.

## Method

Ar2Can decomposes multi-human generation into two stages: an Architect that predicts structured spatial layouts (bounding boxes and optionally pose) and an Artist that renders the final image conditioned on that layout. Two Architect variants are provided—Architect-A (Qwen-2.5-0.5B with coordinate regression heads trained via L1+GIoU loss) for strong count accuracy, and Architect-B (Flux-Schnell fine-tuned with GRPO) for richer 2D spatial priors and pose. The Artist (Flux-Kontext) is trained via GRPO with a compositional reward combining count accuracy, HPSv3 prompt alignment, a spatially-grounded face matching reward (Hungarian centroid matching between planned and detected face locations, followed by ArcFace identity similarity), and a frontal pose reward. A token dropping and sharing strategy reduces inference cost by 2× while enabling the model to learn natural occlusion through shared RoPE positional encodings on overlapping regions. Curriculum learning over person count (starting with 2–3 people, expanding to 7) stabilizes early training and prevents identity collapse on complex scenes.

## Results

On MultiHuman-Testbench, Ar2Can(Arch-A) achieves the highest count accuracy (90.2%), while Ar2Can(Arch-B) achieves the best Multi-ID identity similarity (68.2), surpassing the previous best open-source method MH-OmniGen (54.5) by 13.7 points and GPT-Image-1 (28.8) by 39.4 points. Ar2Can(Arch-B) also attains the highest HPSv3 quality score (30.8) and the best Unified metric (72.4), outperforming all proprietary and open-source baselines. On MultiID-Test, Ar2Can leads in Multi-ID(Ref) (54.3 vs. 50.1 for WithAnyone) and Multi-ID(GT) (36.5 vs. 33.8), confirming view-invariant identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ar2Can decisively outperforms all open-source multi-human methods on identity preservation: Multi-ID scores of 68.2 (Arch-B) and 67.6 (Arch-A) exceed MH-OmniGen (54.5), WithAnyone (44.3), XVerse (30.6), and DreamO (34.7) by 13–38 points. Against proprietary systems, Ar2Can achieves 68.2 Multi-ID versus GPT-Image-1's 28.8 and Nanobanana's 20.6, a gap the authors attribute to identity hallucination in those models; however, GPT-Image-1 and Nanobanana retain a lead on Action-S (97.0/98.9 vs. 86.2) and Action-C (91.1/95.5 vs. 82.0), indicating Ar2Can trades some action fidelity for identity accuracy. On count accuracy, Ar2Can(Arch-A) leads at 90.2%, narrowly beating WithAnyone(Arch-B) at 89.8% and GPT-Image-1 at 87.9%. The Unified metric (balancing identity, prompt alignment, and quality) shows Ar2Can(Arch-B) at 72.4, surpassing the next-best MH-OmniGen (61.6) by 10.8 points and GPT-Image-1 (55.8) by 16.6 points. On the MultiID-Test benchmark (biased toward 1–2 person scenes), Ar2Can leads WithAnyone by 4.2 points on Multi-ID(Ref) and 2.7 on Multi-ID(GT), with a smaller margin reflecting the simpler compositional difficulty of that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

Ar2Can builds on the Flux-Kontext and Flux-Schnell diffusion models as its rendering and layout bases, respectively, and adopts GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as the RL training paradigm for both its Architect-B and Artist components. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-human generation methods (XVerse, UMO, DreamO, WithAnyone) that attempt to jointly reason about spatial layout and identity in a single generation pass, instead decomposing the problem into a dedicated spatial-planning stage (Architect) and an identity-rendering stage (Artist). The spatially-grounded face matching reward—combining Hungarian centroid alignment with ArcFace identity similarity—is a novel RL reward design not present in prior diffusion-RL work like DisCo or DDPO, which used count or diversity rewards without explicit spatial-identity coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a two-stage decomposition with RL-trained components can substantially outperform both proprietary systems (GPT-Image-1) and specialized multi-ID open-source methods on identity preservation (68.2 vs. 54.5 Multi-ID), while doing so with primarily synthetic training data rather than scarce real multi-human images. The modular architecture—where Architects can be swapped without retraining the Artist—offers practical deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 5 people, how well it generalizes to non-portrait or heavily occluded scenes, and whether the synthetic-data training strategy (relying on DisCo-generated scenes) introduces distributional biases that limit real-world robustness.
