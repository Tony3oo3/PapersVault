## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods are fundamentally interpolative, producing degraded or frozen trajectories when extrapolated beyond observed timestamps, while 2D video prediction approaches yield geometrically inconsistent results in complex scenes. The core difficulty is that individual Gaussian primitives move independently, causing spatially incoherent motion that accumulates over time, and prior extrapolation models lack object-level structure to maintain physically plausible long-term dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261406/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casually captured dynamic video (e.g., iPhone dataset with hundreds of frames from multiple handheld cameras) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, using only a subset of frames (80% or 60%) as observations. It outputs rendered novel-view frames at future timesteps beyond the observed range, evaluated from held-out test viewpoints, alongside a structured 4DGS representation where Gaussians are partitioned into object-level motion groups with explicit rigid/non-rigid labels.

## Method

MoGaF operates in three stages on a Shape-of-Motion (SoM) 4DGS backbone. First, motion-aware Gaussian grouping uses Grounding DINO for 2D segmentation and rigidity labeling, then alternates keyframe-based front-Gaussian seeding with feature-space region growing (using canonical-space means and PCA-reduced motion coefficients) to build spatiotemporally coherent object groups. Second, group-wise constrained optimization applies a shared SE(3) transformation to rigid groups (rigid motion anchoring loss) and spatial smoothness regularization on motion coefficients for non-rigid groups, producing a physically structured 4D representation. Third, a lightweight single-layer Transformer encoder (8 heads, 32-dim embedding) is trained per group with masked motion modeling (contiguous-span masking annealed during training) and acceleration regularization to autoregressively extrapolate future SE(3) trajectories, decoupling heterogeneous motion dynamics across objects for stable long-horizon forecasting.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset (real-world multi-camera dynamic scenes), MoGaF achieves average mPSNR of 15.58 and mLPIPS of 0.4227 at the 80% observation setting, and 15.51 mPSNR / 0.4245 mLPIPS at the more challenging 60% setting, outperforming all baselines. On the synthetic D-NeRF dataset (60% observed / 40% forecasted), MoGaF reaches 23.37 PSNR and 0.0746 LPIPS, surpassing GSPred (21.78 PSNR, 0.0919 LPIPS) by a notable margin, with the largest gain on the Lego scene (21.61 vs. 12.65 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoGaF consistently outperforms GSPred [50] across all metrics on both datasets: on iPhone it gains +1.82 mPSNR (15.58 vs. 13.76) and reduces mLPIPS by 0.053 at 80% observation, and the gap widens at 60% (+1.71 mPSNR, −0.095 mLPIPS). Against the SoM-adapted baselines (GSPred-SoM and ODE-GS-SoM), MoGaF achieves the best mPSNR and mLPIPS at both observation ratios, though its mSSIM is marginally below GSPred-SoM at 80% (0.6395 vs. 0.6405) while exceeding it at 60% (0.6143 vs. 0.6107). On D-NeRF, MoGaF surpasses GSPred by +1.59 PSNR on average, with the most dramatic improvement on the Lego scene (+8.96 PSNR), where GSPred's per-Gaussian motion model fails to preserve object structure. The only per-scene loss is on Mutant (SSIM 0.9246 vs. 0.9375), where GSPred's simpler motion model happens to better capture the specific deformation pattern. Overall, MoGaF's group-wise formulation provides a clear and consistent advantage, particularly as the forecasting horizon increases from 20% to 40% of frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoGaF builds directly on Shape of Motion (SoM) [37] for its 4DGS canonical-space and motion-basis parameterization, and extends the Gaga [26] 3D-aware Gaussian grouping pipeline to dynamic scenes via spatiotemporal region growing and keyframe-based seeding. It departs from prior 4DGS forecasting methods (GSPred [50], ODE-GS [36]) that treat Gaussians as independent primitives or model motion via a single global network, instead introducing explicit object-level motion groups (rigid vs. non-rigid) that receive type-specific optimization constraints and dedicated lightweight forecasters. The masked motion modeling training strategy borrows from NLP masked language modeling [6] but is applied here to SE(3) motion sequences rather than token sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Long-term, physically consistent scene extrapolation is a prerequisite for downstream applications like robotic planning and autonomous driving, where anticipating unobserved future motion is critical. The paper demonstrates that object-level structural priors (rigid/non-rigid grouping) are essential for preventing the trajectory collapse and geometric drift that plague per-Gaussian forecasting over extended horizons. Open questions include scalability to scenes with many interacting objects, handling of objects that enter or leave the scene, and whether the reliance on 2D segmentation priors (Grounding DINO) limits robustness in cluttered or occluded real-world settings. The forecasting horizon, while improved, still covers only 20–40% of the video, leaving truly long-horizon prediction unresolved.
