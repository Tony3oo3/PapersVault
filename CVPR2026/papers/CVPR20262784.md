## Problem

Training-free concept erasure in diffusion models faces a core trade-off: existing guard-railing methods are either rigid (failing to generalize to paraphrased, aliased, or contextually shifted prompts) or coarse (distorting unrelated content and degrading fidelity). Methods like AdaVD and SAFREE can mitigate paraphrase leakage only when all rephrasings are explicitly enumerated, which is infeasible in real-time, user-specific deployment scenarios. The paper tackles the gap between precise erasure, paraphrase-level generalization, and faithful preservation of non-target semantics simultaneously.

## Input / Output

GenErase takes a text prompt, a target concept to erase, a set of preserve concepts (semantics to protect), and a neutral anchor concept as input, all processed through the text encoder of Stable Diffusion v1.4. At inference time, it intervenes per-token and per-layer in the cross-attention value space during the 30-step denoising process, requiring no gradient updates or weight modification. The output is a standard 512×512 RGB image where the target concept is suppressed while preserve concepts remain intact; the method adds only ~1.8 s per batch over vanilla sampling with a smaller peak memory footprint.

## Method

GenErase is a training-free, geometry-grounded framework that enforces semantic orthogonality in the cross-attention value (CA-V) space through three complementary modules. First, a per-token Preserve Projector (S³) constructs an orthonormal basis from preserve-concept value vectors, decomposing each token into a protected component and an editable complement, so edits never affect non-target semantics. Second, a Hard Geometric Gate (HGG) computes the normalized directional alignment between each token's editable component and the target direction, triggering an edit only when this ratio exceeds a fixed threshold τ, making the decision invariant to layer-wise magnitude changes and robust to paraphrasing. Third, an Orthogonal Erase-and-Replace (OER) update removes the target-aligned component and redistributes its energy along a neutral anchor direction that is made orthogonal to both the preserve subspace and the erasure direction, preventing feature collapse and residual semantic leakage. The multi-concept extension orthonormalizes multiple target directions and uses a matrix of anchors, maintaining mutual orthogonality across all preserved, erased, and replacement subspaces by construction.

## Results

On single-celebrity erasure (Donald Trump, Mark Zuckerberg, Dwayne Johnson), GenErase achieves the best average Erase Success Rate (ESR) of 81.83% and harmonic mean (HM) of 40.22, outperforming AdaVD (ESR 78.33%, HM 39.79%) while maintaining a non-target FID of 11.85 (vs. AdaVD's 8.09). On the novel GenBench-40 paraphrase-generalization benchmark, GenErase achieves the lowest average failure rate of 22.70%, compared to 29.88% for AdaVD and 27.17% for NP. On object erasure, GenErase attains the highest average HM of 36.49, and on nudity suppression it reaches 87.35% success, exceeding the ~83% of prior SOTA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenErase outperforms all four baselines on Erase Success Rate and harmonic mean across identity, object, and paraphrase benchmarks. Against the closest competitor AdaVD, GenErase gains ~3.5 points in ESR (81.83 vs. 78.33) and ~0.4 in HM on single-celebrity erasure, at the cost of a modest FID increase (11.85 vs. 8.09) that the authors attribute to minor perceptual variation rather than semantic drift. On the GenBench-40 paraphrase-generalization benchmark, GenErase reduces the average failure rate to 22.70%, a ~7-point improvement over AdaVD (29.88%) and ~4.5 points over NP (27.17%), confirming its core advantage in handling unseen rephrasings. SAFREE and NP suffer severe non-target FID degradation (68+), indicating they distort unrelated content substantially, whereas GenErase and AdaVD both maintain FID below 15. On nudity suppression, GenErase achieves 87.35% success versus ~83% for AdaVD, and scales to 50 simultaneous identities with only marginal ESR/PSR decline, a regime where NP and SLD fail due to memory constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenErase extends the value-space projection lineage initiated by AdaVD, which first demonstrated that erasing target semantics in the cross-attention value (CA-V) space is effective but fragile under paraphrased prompts. The key conceptual departure is replacing AdaVD's soft, adaptive token-wise scaling with a hard geometric gate (a discrete threshold on normalized directional alignment) and adding an explicit per-token preserve projector that orthogonally shields non-target semantics before any edit is applied. The orthogonal erase-and-replace update further distinguishes GenErase from both AdaVD (which nulls target components) and SAFREE (which operates in text-embedding space rather than CA-V space), by redistributing erased energy along a neutral anchor direction to prevent feature collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paraphrase-robustness gap identified here is practically critical: real-world moderation cannot rely on exhaustively enumerating every alias of a target concept, and the GenBench-40 benchmark (~3,000 cases across 40 entities) provides a principled, concept-normalized protocol to measure this. GenErase's training-free design with only ~1.8 s overhead per 30-step batch makes it deployable in real-time pipelines, and its scalability to 50 simultaneous targets on a 24 GB GPU addresses a practical bottleneck for platform-level filtering. Open questions include whether the hard geometric gate's fixed threshold (τ=0.1) generalizes across model architectures beyond SD v1.4, whether the approach extends to video diffusion (acknowledged as future work), and how it performs under adversarial or obfuscated prompts, which the paper explicitly does not test.
