## Problem

As autonomous driving datasets grow to hundreds of thousands of frames, the critical challenge shifts from data collection to efficiently locating rare, safety-critical objects and scenes that may appear fewer than 50 times across the entire corpus. No large-scale benchmark existed for semantic image retrieval of such rare classes in the AD domain, and existing corner-case datasets (e.g., CODA, OpenAD) rely on pre-selection that biases class distributions and makes them unsuitable for evaluating retrieval methods on naturally long-tailed data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260544/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image retrieval, the query is a pair of precise keywords plus extended descriptions derived from labeling guidelines; for image-to-image retrieval, the query is a vision support set of 5 reference images selected for size variance and low occlusion. Dataset images are processed as square crops at model-specific resolutions (up to 1024×1024 for RADIO/NARADIO, 512×512 for SigLIP2), and images containing relevant objects smaller than 50 pixels are selectively excluded per class to ensure fair evaluation. The output is a full ranking of all test-set images by cosine similarity, evaluated with R-Precision (recall within the top K_c relevant images per class) and MAP averaged over all 90 classes.

## Method

SearchAD is constructed by exhaustively annotating 423,798 frames from 11 established AD datasets with 513k+ bounding boxes across 90 rare categories, using an eight-month multi-stage manual labeling pipeline with 100% human quality control and >98% accuracy verification. The retrieval benchmark evaluates zero-shot VLMs (NARADIO, RADIO, SigLIP2, MetaCLIP2, BLIP2, OpenCLIP, NACLIP) and grounded detectors (GroundingDINO) via a unified framework: query features (averaged over the support set or text-encoded) are compared against offline-computed dataset embeddings using cosine similarity, with the image score taken as the maximum similarity across patch or summary features. A fine-tuning baseline adapts BLIP2 with standard image-text matching and contrastive losses on the SearchAD training annotations without box-level supervision. The key finding is that NARADIO—whose neighbor-aware gaussian kernel preserves spatial patch localization and whose lightweight language adapters align spatial features to the text space—achieves the best zero-shot MAP of 14.27%, while fine-tuned BLIP2 reaches 12.06% MAP, both revealing substantial room for improvement in semantic precision for rare-class retrieval.

## Results

On the SearchAD test set, NARADIO achieves the best zero-shot text-to-image retrieval with 14.27% MAP and 17.91% MRP, substantially outperforming the next-best method NACLIP (9.59% MAP). For image-to-image retrieval, NARADIO also leads with 8.31% MAP, ahead of BLIP2 (7.95%) and GroundingDINO (7.62%). A fine-tuned BLIP2 baseline improves text-to-image MAP to 12.06% and image-to-image MAP to 11.13%, but still falls short of NARADIO's zero-shot text-to-image score. Overall, absolute retrieval performance remains low across all methods, with the best MAP not exceeding 14.27%, highlighting the extreme difficulty of rare-object semantic retrieval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NARADIO (neighbor-aware RADIO) dominates both retrieval modalities in zero-shot settings, achieving 14.27% MAP for text-to-image—nearly 5 points above the runner-up NACLIP (9.59%)—and 8.31% MAP for image-to-image, edging out BLIP2 (7.95%) and GroundingDINO (7.62%). Text-based methods consistently outperform image-based ones by roughly 5–6 MAP points, confirming that language grounding provides stronger semantic discrimination for rare classes. GroundingDINO, despite its high input resolution (1333×600), trails all VLM-based text-to-image methods except in the Object and Animal categories, where its spatial precision helps with small instances like hydrants and strollers. The fine-tuned BLIP2 baseline (12.06% text-to-image MAP) improves over its zero-shot self (9.14%) but still lags NARADIO's zero-shot score, suggesting that spatially aligned VLM features remain more effective than image-level contrastive fine-tuning without box supervision. No single method excels across all nine categories: NACLIP leads in Scene (25.95% text-to-image), while NARADIO is best in 7 of 9 categories, with its weakest performance in Sign (4.12%) and Marking (13.23%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing eleven established AD datasets (KITTI, nuScenes, BDD100K, Cityscapes, Mapillary Vistas, etc.) into a unified semantic image retrieval benchmark for rare objects, rather than introducing a new model architecture. It deliberately departs from prior rare-class datasets like CODA and OpenAD, which rely on pre-selection and auto-labeling that distort the natural long-tail distribution, by exhaustively hand-annotating unfiltered data to preserve realistic rarity. On the retrieval side, it extends the VLM-based image retrieval literature (CLIP, BLIP2, RADIO, SigLIP2, MetaCLIP2) and grounded detectors (GroundingDINO) from general-purpose or instance-level benchmarks (RParis6K, MSCOCO) to the underexplored setting of semantic retrieval for extremely rare AD categories, introducing R-Precision as a more appropriate metric than Recall@1 for multi-relevant-image queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SearchAD is the first large-scale, naturally long-tailed benchmark for retrieval-driven data curation in autonomous driving, addressing a practical bottleneck: as AD datasets grow beyond 100k frames, efficiently locating safety-critical rare events (animals, road debris, invalid signs) becomes infeasible without effective retrieval. The extremely low absolute performance (best zero-shot MAP of 14.27%, best fine-tuned MAP of 12.06%) reveals that current VLMs are far from capable of reliable rare-object retrieval, opening substantial room for research in spatially grounded retrieval, few-shot adaptation, and box-level fine-tuning. Key open questions include whether incorporating bounding-box supervision or multi-modal query strategies can close the gap, and whether the benchmark's 90-class taxonomy generalizes to truly open-world scenarios beyond the predefined categories.
