## Problem

Current video generation methods excel at single-shot clips but cannot produce narrative multi-shot videos with flexible shot arrangement, coherent inter-shot consistency, and controllability beyond text prompts. Existing multi-shot approaches either rely on keyframe-based pipelines with limited conditional coverage or end-to-end generation constrained to fixed shot durations and counts, and none support reference-image-driven subject/scene customization or motion control in a multi-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262927/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts a hierarchical prompt (global caption describing subjects and environment, plus per-shot captions for actions, backgrounds, and camera), optional subject reference images paired with per-frame bounding box sequences for spatiotemporal grounding, optional background reference images per shot, and user-configurable shot count (1–5) and per-shot duration (5–20 s at 15 fps). Each shot is independently encoded through a 3D VAE and concatenated in latent space; reference images are similarly encoded and appended as tokens. The output is a single multi-shot video at 384×672 resolution with inter-shot subject and scene consistency, where shot boundaries are explicitly controlled by the RoPE phase-shift parameter rather than learned implicitly.

## Method

MultiShotMaster extends a pretrained ~1B-parameter single-shot DiT (Wan2.1-T2V) via two RoPE modifications that require no additional adapters. First, Multi-Shot Narrative RoPE inserts a fixed angular phase shift (i·φ) into the temporal RoPE index at each shot boundary, breaking RoPE continuity so the model can distinguish intra-shot consecutive frames from inter-shot frames while preserving narrative order. Second, Spatiotemporal Position-Aware RoPE samples 3D-RoPE coordinates from user-specified bounding box regions and applies them to reference tokens, creating strong attention correlations that inject subject or background appearance into precise spatiotemporal locations; multiple copies of subject tokens with different RoPE assignments enable motion trajectory control. A Multi-Shot & Multi-Reference Attention Mask restricts each shot to attend only to its own reference tokens while maintaining full inter-shot attention for global consistency. Training proceeds in two stages—300k single-shot samples for reference injection, then multi-shot & multi-reference data with random subject/background dropping—followed by a cross-shot subject-focused post-training that doubles the loss weight on subject regions.

## Results

On the multi-shot text-to-video generation task, MultiShotMaster (w/o Ref) achieves the best text alignment (0.196), inter-shot consistency (0.697 holistic / 0.491 subject / 0.447 scene), transition deviation (1.72 frames), and narrative coherence (0.695) among all multi-shot baselines. With reference injection enabled (w/ Ref), the method further improves to 0.227 TA, 0.702/0.495/0.472 inter-shot consistency, 1.41 transition deviation, 0.825 narrative coherence, and adds reference consistency scores of 0.493 (subject), 0.456 (scene), and 0.594 (grounding mIoU). The framework is built on a ~1B-parameter Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B backbone at 384×672 resolution, generating 77–308 frame videos (5–20 s at 15 fps) with 1–5 shots.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two dedicated multi-shot methods, MultiShotMaster dominates across every metric: it outperforms CineTrans by +0.022 in text alignment, +0.019 in holistic inter-shot consistency, and reduces transition deviation from 5.27 to 1.41 frames, while narrative coherence jumps from 0.496 to 0.825. EchoShot, designed for portrait consistency rather than narrative, trails significantly on narrative coherence (0.213 vs. 0.825) and scene consistency (0.346 vs. 0.472). Against the single-shot reference-to-video methods VACE and Phantom (run independently per shot), MultiShotMaster achieves substantially higher inter-shot subject consistency (0.495 vs. 0.468/0.462) and scene consistency (0.472 vs. 0.273/0.279), and is the only method that supports spatiotemporal-grounded reference injection (0.594 mIoU), which neither VACE nor Phantom can provide. The key advantage stems from full temporal attention across shots plus the proposed RoPE mechanisms, whereas baselines either weaken inter-shot attention (CineTrans) or generate shots independently (VACE, Phantom).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pretrained Wan2.1 single-shot T2V model by modifying its RoPE positional encoding in two ways: Multi-Shot Narrative RoPE inserts angular phase shifts at shot boundaries (departing from ShotAdapter's learnable transition tokens and CineTrans's attention masking), and Spatiotemporal Position-Aware RoPE assigns region-specific RoPE indices to reference tokens for grounded injection. Unlike prior controllable video generation methods (VACE, Phantom, Tora, Motion Prompting) that operate in single-shot settings with separate adapters, this work unifies reference injection and motion control within the multi-shot DiT architecture without additional modules. The data curation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (TransNet V2, YOLOv11, ByteTrack, SAM, Gemini-2.5, OmniEraser) into an automated multi-shot annotation pipeline, addressing the data scarcity that has limited prior multi-shot generation research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly support text-driven inter-shot consistency, subject reference with motion control, and background-driven scene customization in multi-shot video generation with flexible shot counts and durations, addressing a practical gap between single-shot generation and real filmmaking workflows. The RoPE-based approach is architecturally lightweight (no extra adapters, no learnable parameters for shot transitions), making it a potentially adoptable technique for other DiT-based video models. Key limitations include the camera-object motion coupling (explicitly acknowledged in Fig. 5), the restriction to ~1B parameter models at 384×672 resolution, and the reliance on Gemini-2.5 for captioning and subject grouping, which introduces external model dependencies. Open questions include whether the RoPE phase-shift mechanism generalizes to longer narratives (beyond 5 shots), how to decouple camera and subject motion, and whether the approach scales to larger models and higher resolutions.
