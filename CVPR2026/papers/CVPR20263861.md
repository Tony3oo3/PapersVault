## Problem

Existing agent-based MLLMs that use image cropping tools exhibit an 'answer first, crop later' behavior: they derive answers from the global image and invoke the crop merely to confirm a pre-existing conclusion, with performance showing negligible sensitivity to the actual content of the cropped region. This stems from a training-time information redundancy where the full image and the crop source share the same resolution, so the crop provides no additional signal for the model to learn from.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263861/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution image (processed at a selected lower resolution of up to 1,024 or 16,384 visual tokens) and a text question. It can autonomously invoke a cropping tool that extracts regions from the original full-resolution image, creating a resolution asymmetry between the global view and the local crop. The output is a text answer accompanied by bounding box coordinates for the cropped region, with a maximum of 12 dialogue turns per question.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage pure RL framework (GRPO-based, no trajectory supervision) that forces the model to genuinely rely on cropped regions. In Stage 1 (Learn to Focus), an Information Gap Mechanism downsamples the global image to the lowest resolution at which the model's answer still diverges from the original, while crops are extracted from the full-resolution source—making the crop's fine-grained details indispensable for a correct answer. The reward combines accuracy, format compliance, and a tool-usage bonus granted only when the model both answers correctly and invokes at least one crop. In Stage 2 (Learn to Crop Precisely), hierarchical bounding box annotations (nested from minimal to contextual regions) provide a grounding reward that combines a conditional IoU term (gated by a 0.9 overlap threshold on the smallest GT box to prevent under-coverage) with an L1 distance term for denser gradient signal, encouraging precise yet sufficient crops without redundant context.

## Results

With only 1,024 visual tokens for the full input image (vs. 16,384 for baselines), the method achieves 72.1% overall on HR-Bench 8K, 75.3% on HR-Bench 4K, and 80.6% on V*, outperforming all compared methods by 4–13 points. At the 16,384-token budget, it reaches 75.4% (HR-Bench 8K), 76.4% (HR-Bench 4K), and 89.5% (V*), matching or exceeding the best baselines. The efficiency analysis further shows that the 1,024-token setting reduces per-question inference time to 2.6–2.8 s, roughly 4–10× faster than baselines using 16,384 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CoF-sft (SFT-only, 7B), the method wins by a wide margin on every benchmark at both token budgets (e.g., +13.2 pts on HR-Bench 8K Overall at 1,024 tokens). Pixel Reasoner (SFT+RL, 7B) is also clearly outperformed, with gaps of 12.2 pts (HR-Bench 8K) and 6.5 pts (V*) at the 1,024-token setting. Mini-o3 (SFT+RL, 7B) is the closest competitor: at 1,024 tokens the method leads by 6.1 pts on HR-Bench 8K and 4.6 pts on HR-Bench 4K, while V* is nearly tied (80.6 vs. 80.1); at 16,384 tokens, Mini-o3† (32-run average) edges out the method on HR-Bench 4K (77.5 vs. 76.4) but the method still wins on HR-Bench 8K (75.4 vs. 73.3) and V* (89.5 vs. 88.2). DeepEyes (pure RL, 7B) is beaten on all benchmarks at 1,024 tokens (by 10.1 pts on HR-Bench 8K) and at 16,384 tokens on HR-Bench 8K (75.4 vs. 72.6) and HR-Bench 4K (76.4 vs. 75.1), though DeepEyes‡ (no option shuffling on V*) slightly leads on V* (90.1 vs. 89.5). Notably, the method is trajectory-free (no SFT trajectories required), matching or exceeding methods that depend on expensive teacher-generated trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the agent-based MLLM line of work (DeepEyes, Mini-o3, Chain-of-Focus, Pixel Reasoner) where models autonomously invoke an image-cropping tool for high-resolution VQA. It departs from prior work on two axes: (1) it identifies and diagnoses a previously overlooked failure mode—models trained with SFT+RL or pure RL perform 'answer first, crop later,' treating the crop as a perfunctory confirmation rather than an information source—and (2) it introduces a two-stage pure-RL framework that eliminates the need for teacher-generated trajectories entirely, using an information-gap mechanism (downsampled global image vs. full-resolution crop) in stage one and a hierarchical grounding reward in stage two. Conceptually, it reframes the cropping tool from a 'zoom-in convenience' to an information bottleneck that the model must actively exploit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that existing agentic MLLMs barely attend to their own crops (Table 1a: near-zero performance delta when replacing crops with noise) is a useful caution for the field, suggesting that reported gains from cropping agents may partly reflect the full image rather than the tool. The information-gap mechanism is a simple, trajectory-free training trick that practitioners can adopt without expensive teacher models, and the efficiency result (1,024 visual tokens outperforming 16,384-token baselines) points toward a practical path for deploying fine-grained perception under tight compute budgets. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 7B models and VQA to other agentic tool-use settings (e.g., multi-step spatial reasoning, video), and whether the offline resolution-selection procedure scales to open-ended training distributions without per-sample model queries.
