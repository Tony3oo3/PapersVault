## Problem

Existing RL-based video reasoning methods (e.g., GRPO) suffer from thinking drift and weak temporal comprehension because outcome-only rewards leave intermediate reasoning unconstrained. Prior fixes rely on costly SFT+RL pipelines with expensive CoT annotations that enforce rigid reasoning paths and limit generalization. The paper asks whether a single-stage RL framework can provide structural supervision on the reasoning process without any SFT.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262958/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B) receives 16 uniformly sampled video frames at 128x28x28 resolution together with a text question. It produces a three-segment structured CoT — a Summary of salient ordered events, a Thinking block, and a final Answer — where the Summary segment is triggered by a dedicated <summary> tag prepended to the prompt. During training, G=8 sampled outputs per input are generated and segmented at token-level boundaries into Summary, Thinking, and Answer spans for token-wise weighting.

## Method

SDRL extends GRPO by embedding a Structured CoT (Summarize → Think → Answer) directly into the policy objective and adding two self-supervised token-wise weighting mechanisms. Consistency of Vision Knowledge (CVK) enforces factual grounding by computing a position-wise KL divergence between each sampled summary and a group-level anchor (derived from correct-answer summaries in self-supervised mode, or from GT summaries with sBERT/BLEU similarity in supervised mode), then down-weighting inconsistent token positions. Dynamic Variety of Reasoning (DVR) encourages exploration in the Thinking segment by weighting tokens proportionally to their predictive entropy, dynamically scaled by (1 − group accuracy) so that diversity pressure is strongest for uncertain groups and suppressed for confident ones. Both weights are multiplied into the standard GRPO clipped-ratio objective at the token level, yielding a single-stage RL training loop that requires no SFT or human-annotated CoT.

## Results

SDRL (Ours), trained with RL-only on Video-R1-260K, achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across seven VideoQA benchmarks: 79.3% on NExT-GQA, 68.6% on MMVU, 51.3% on VideoMMMU, 64.2% on MVBench, 73.4% on TempCompass, and 54.7% on VideoMME. A variant trained on the smaller EventFlowQA dataset (20% the size of Video-R1-260K) reaches 74.4% on TempCompass and 36.1% on VSI-Bench, demonstrating strong data efficiency. Ablations on EventFlowQA show that the full self-supervised CVK+DVR pipeline lifts accuracy from 42.37% (base) to 56.10%, with self-supervision (+11.91%) outperforming GT supervision (+6.19%) on the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest SFT+RL baseline VideoRFT*, SDRL (Ours) wins on NExT-GQA (79.3 vs 75.1, +4.2 pp), MMVU (68.6 vs 67.3, +1.3 pp), VideoMMMU (51.3 vs 50.6, +0.7 pp), MVBench (64.2 vs 61.4, +2.8 pp), and TempCompass (73.4 vs 73.1, +0.3 pp), but trails on VSI-Bench (32.9 vs 35.7) and VideoMME (54.7 vs 58.1). Among RL-only methods, SDRL consistently outperforms VideoChat-R1 and TW-GRPO on all shared benchmarks, e.g., 79.3 vs 76.1 on NExT-GQA and 68.6 vs 65.8 on MMVU versus TW-GRPO. Compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SDRL gains up to +6.3 pp (VSI-Bench, Ours†) and +5.7 pp (NExT-GQA, Ours). The EventFlowQA-trained variant (Ours†) notably surpasses the full-data variant on VSI-Bench (36.1 vs 32.9) and TempCompass (74.4 vs 73.4), indicating that targeted temporal-action data can be more effective than larger general-purpose RL data for temporal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based RL paradigm for video reasoning (Video-R1, VideoRFT, VideoChat-R1, TW-GRPO) by replacing the standard two-stage SFT+RL pipeline with a single-stage RL-only framework that embeds a Structured CoT (Summarize → Think → Answer) directly into the policy objective. It departs from prior process-supervision work (PRMs, PSPO*, ThinkPRM) by deriving self-supervised signals—group-level KL consistency for summaries and entropy-based diversity for thinking—without requiring annotated intermediate reasoning steps. The EventFlowQA dataset is introduced to provide fine-grained temporal action-sequence annotations that prior video QA benchmarks lack, enabling controlled ablation of the consistency and diversity mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the SFT stage and its associated CoT annotation cost, SDRL offers a more data-efficient and simpler training recipe for video MLLMs, which is practically significant given the expense of multi-stage pipelines. The finding that self-supervised consistency outperforms GT supervision for the 7B model (+11.91% vs. +6.19%) challenges the intuitive assumption that human-annotated anchors are always superior, suggesting that pre-trained semantic priors can be better preserved through group-level self-consistency. Open questions include whether the structured CoT format generalizes beyond VideoQA to other temporal tasks (e.g., video generation, embodied planning), whether the approach scales to larger model families, and whether the 16-frame input setting limits temporal fidelity for long videos.
