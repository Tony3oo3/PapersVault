## Problem

Applying Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to flow matching models creates a fundamental conflict: GRPO requires stochastic exploration, while flow matching's core advantage is deterministic ODE sampling. Existing SDE-based solutions (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) resolve this by converting ODEs to SDEs, but this sacrifices compatibility with high-order solvers and introduces inefficient credit assignment between terminal rewards and single-step noise injections.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260867/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes text prompts (from the HPDv2 dataset, 103,700 prompts total) and generates 1024×1024 RGB images using FLUX.1-dev as the base flow matching model. During training, a group of G=12 initial noise vectors is constructed by perturbing a base noise with controlled strength σ, and all trajectories are rolled out deterministically via ODE solvers (DDIM or DPM++). At inference, standard deterministic ODE sampling is used without any stochastic leaping, preserving the efficiency of few-step generation (8 or 16 NFEs).

## Method

The paper reinterprets SDE-based GRPO as distance-based contrastive learning: the ODE sample acts as an anchor pulled toward high-reward candidates and pushed away from low-reward ones. Building on this, Neighbor GRPO constructs a neighborhood of G candidate trajectories by perturbing initial noise conditions (ε⁽ⁱ⁾ = √(1−σ²)ε* + σδ⁽ⁱ⁾), then defines a softmax-distance surrogate leaping policy over the group that assigns probability proportional to exp(−‖x_t⁽ⁱ⁾ − x_t⁽ᶿ⁾‖²). This surrogate provides the stochastic policy needed for GRPO's clipped ratio objective while keeping inference fully deterministic. Three practical enhancements are introduced: symmetric anchor sampling (exploiting near-equidistance of perturbed noises to reduce backward passes from G to B per iteration), group-wise quasi-norm advantage reweighting with p<2 to suppress reward flattening, and direct use of high-order DPM++ solvers for rollout with one-step DDIM transitions for policy updates.

## Results

On the multi-reward HPDv2 benchmark, Neighbor GRPO with DPM++ (16-step) achieves the best out-of-domain scores: CLIP 0.386 and UnifiedReward 3.324, surpassing DanceGRPO (0.364, 3.156) and MixGRPO (0.382, 3.257). The 8-step DPM++ variant further improves CLIP to 0.391 and UnifiedReward to 3.334 while reducing training time to 45 s/iteration (vs. 238 s for DanceGRPO), a 5.3× speedup. In human evaluation, Neighbor GRPO achieves 72% and 61% win rates against the two baselines. Single-reward (HPSv2.1) training shows competitive in-domain scores (0.379) with the best out-of-domain UnifiedReward (3.166) at p=0.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceGRPO (pure SDE), Neighbor GRPO wins decisively on out-of-domain metrics: CLIP improves from 0.364 to 0.386–0.391 and UnifiedReward from 3.156 to 3.324–3.334, while DanceGRPO retains a slight edge on in-domain HPSv2.1 (0.371 vs. 0.359–0.369). Training cost drops from 238 s/iter to 45–142 s/iter (5.3× faster at 8-step). Against MixGRPO (mixed ODE-SDE), Neighbor GRPO matches or exceeds in-domain scores and clearly wins on CLIP (0.386 vs. 0.382) and UnifiedReward (3.324 vs. 3.257); the MixGRPO-Flash variant is outperformed on nearly all metrics. Against BranchGRPO (single-reward setting), Neighbor GRPO (p=2) ties on HPSv2.1 (0.379) but wins on CLIP (0.368 vs. 0.362) and UnifiedReward (3.132 vs. 3.138 is a near-tie); with p=0.8 reweighting it leads on CLIP (0.371), UnifiedReward (3.166), and Aesthetic (6.626). All methods substantially outperform the unaligned FLUX.1-dev base model across every metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SDE-based GRPO line (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) that converts deterministic flow-matching ODEs into equivalent SDEs to enable stochastic policy exploration. Its key departure is a conceptual reframing: by showing that SDE-based GRPO optimization is mathematically equivalent to advantage-weighted contrastive learning in sample space, the authors eliminate the SDE entirely and instead construct a neighborhood of ODE trajectories via initial-noise perturbation, optimizing a softmax-distance surrogate policy. This positions Neighbor GRPO as a fundamentally different mechanism (discrete leaping over a deterministic neighborhood) rather than an incremental fix to the SDE framework, while remaining fully compatible with the GRPO objective and its trust-region clipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: eliminating SDEs enables high-order solvers (DPM++) for few-step sampling and reduces policy-update cost by up to 12×, bringing a full 300-iteration GRPO training run to ~4 hours on 32 H800s — making flow-model RLHF far more accessible. Open questions include whether the neighborhood-perturbation approach generalizes to video generation (where trajectory neighborhoods may be less well-behaved), whether the surrogate policy's marginal-preservation approximation degrades at larger perturbation strengths, and whether the quasi-norm reweighting transfers to multi-reward settings beyond the three tested. The method is currently validated on a single base model (FLUX.1-dev) and one prompt dataset (HPDv2), so broader architectural and task generalization remains to be established.
