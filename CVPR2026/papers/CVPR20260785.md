## Problem

Vision-LLMs struggle with video temporal grounding because existing temporal encoding strategies each introduce trade-offs: positional encoding fails to capture absolute temporal positions, per-frame text-based timestamps cause rapidly growing token counts and sparse visual attention, and visual frame numbering degrades the spatial detail that these models rely on. No prior approach effectively repurposes the strong 2D spatial attention of Vision Transformers for fine-grained temporal reasoning without sacrificing spatial fidelity or incurring prohibitive computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260785/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video as a sequence of frames at their original resolution (no downsampling) along with a natural language query, and outputs a temporal segment as start and end frame indices (formatted as 'From x to y'). Frames are grouped into sliding windows of configurable size k (e.g., 12 frames in a 4×3 grid) with a stride s that controls overlap; for short videos s < k to preserve action continuity, while for long videos s = k to limit redundancy. The method operates in a training-free mode or with lightweight LoRA fine-tuning (rank 64) on a base Vision-LLM such as Qwen2-VL-7B.

## Method

T2SGrid reformulates video temporal understanding as a spatial reasoning problem by 'gridifying' frames from each sliding temporal window into a single composite 2D image arranged in row-major order, which is then processed by the standard 2D Vision Transformer without any task-specific temporal module. The row-major layout implicitly encodes temporal order through the model's existing 2D positional embeddings, while the spatial neighborhood structure strengthens local attention to capture inter-frame motion cues that sequential processing misses. To recover absolute global time lost by the local windowing, a single composite text timestamp (e.g., 'from 0 to 11') is prepended to each grid image, forming a continuous temporal chain across the video. Overlapping windows (stride < window size) further preserve temporal continuity for short videos. The approach is validated as both a training-free plug-in and a fine-tuning strategy, with the latter using LoRA on Qwen2-VL-7B trained on 18K videos with 50K QA pairs.

## Results

T2SGrid-FT (Qwen2-VL-7B fine-tuned with T2SGrid) achieves the best mIoU of 53.2 on Charades-STA and 46.7 on ActivityNet, surpassing all prior VTG-tuned Vision-LLMs. In the training-free setting, T2SGrid applied to Qwen2-VL-7B reaches 44.3 mIoU on Charades-STA and 33.3 on ActivityNet, already exceeding several dedicated VTG methods. GPT-4o benefits from T2SGrid with gains of +1.5 mIoU on Charades-STA and +11.9 on ActivityNet, while LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B (an image-only model) sees the largest absolute improvement, jumping from 14.5 to 28.8 mIoU on Charades-STA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2SGrid-FT (Qwen2-VL-7B) achieves the highest mIoU on both benchmarks (53.2 Charades-STA, 46.7 ActivityNet), outperforming the strongest VTG-tuned baselines NumPro (41.4/38.8) and TRACE (–/39.0) by 11.8 and 7.9 mIoU respectively. In the training-free setting, T2SGrid on Qwen2-VL-7B (44.3 mIoU) surpasses HawkEye (33.7), VTimeLLM (31.2), and Momentor (29.3) on Charades-STA without any task-specific fine-tuning. GPT-4o + T2SGrid improves over GPT-4o alone by +1.5 mIoU on Charades-STA and a substantial +11.9 on ActivityNet, confirming the method's portability to strong closed-source models. Qwen3-VL-8B + T2SGrid yields only modest gains (+1.8 mIoU on Charades-STA) due to a slight conflict between T2SGrid's local encoding and Qwen3-VL's native text-timestamp scheme. The most dramatic improvement is on LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B, an image-only model, where T2SGrid lifts mIoU from 14.5 to 28.8 on Charades-STA (+14.3) and from 7.5 to 23.6 on ActivityNet (+16.1), demonstrating that spatial gridification effectively converts static-image models into capable temporal reasoners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant temporal-encoding strategies in Vision-LLMs—positional encoding (Qwen2.5-VL), per-frame text timestamps (Qwen3-VL), and visual frame numbering (NumPro, VIP-LLaVA)—by proposing a fundamentally different input representation: rearranging frames from a sliding temporal window into a composite 2D grid image. While prior grid-based video representations (IG-VLM, DynImg) concatenated keyframes for VQA, T2SGrid is the first to systematically exploit the implicit temporal ordering encoded in a row-major spatial layout for video temporal grounding, and to combine it with composite text timestamps for global temporal awareness. The method repurposes the pre-existing spatial attention of 2D Vision Transformers as a temporal reasoning mechanism, eliminating the need for task-specific temporal modules or additional encoding layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

T2SGrid offers a training-free, plug-and-play temporal encoding that works across heterogeneous base models (GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL, Qwen3-VL, LLaVA-OneVision), making it broadly applicable without model-specific architectural changes. The dramatic improvement on Qwen2-VL-7B (8.7→70.1 R@0.3) demonstrates that the absence of explicit temporal encoding in a Vision-LLM can be largely compensated by spatial reorganization alone, which has implications for how future video-capable LLMs are designed. Open questions include scalability to very long videos (the overlap setting nearly doubles token count), whether the gridification benefit persists at higher resolutions or with 3D positional encodings, and whether the approach generalizes to tasks requiring sub-frame temporal precision.
