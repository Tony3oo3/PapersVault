## Problem

RGB-only motion deblurring is fundamentally ill-posed under extreme motion because the sensor integrates multiple scene moments into a single frame, losing structural and temporal cues. Existing brain-inspired alternatives (event cameras) suffer from event-rate saturation, entangled edge/motion representations, and complex hardware alignment. The Complementary Vision Sensor (CVS, Tianmouc) offers hardware-level decoupling of spatial and temporal difference signals within a single exposure, but no effective deblurring framework had been developed to exploit these complementary modalities.

## Input / Output

The method takes a motion-blurred RGB frame (10-bit, 3-channel), a single central spatial difference frame (2-channel, ±7-bit, encoding ±45° gradients), and a variable-length sequence of N−1 temporal difference frames (1-channel, ±7-bit) all captured within the same RGB exposure window. The number of TD frames scales with exposure duration (N=5,7,9,11 in experiments), and the recurrent architecture adapts to any sequence length without architectural changes. The output is a single deblurred sharp RGB image, recovered via a residual connection from the blurred input.

## Method

STGDNet is a recurrent multi-branch encoder-decoder that sequentially processes each TD frame in temporal order while maintaining a persistent SD structural prior. At each recurrent step, the Temporal Recurrent Refinement Module (TRRM) fuses the current TD feature and the SD feature into the encoder via Cross-modal Complementary Fusion (CCF), which applies two cascaded attention operations—first integrating motion cues from TD, then reinforcing structural edges from SD. A Supervised Attention Module (SAM) modulates the intermediate residual using a spatial attention map aligned with the blurred input, and the refined residual is fed back for the next iteration. The final deblurred image is produced by adding a learned residual to the blurred input. Training uses a PSNR-based loss with AdamW, and the dataset is generated by projecting high-frame-rate SportsSloMo frames through a DMD-based optical setup onto the CVS sensor to produce realistic, pixel-aligned multi-exposure training pairs.

## Results

On the SportsSloMo-CVS dataset, STGDNet achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM across all four exposure durations (N=5, 7, 9, 11 averaged blur frames). At the most severe setting (N=11), it reaches 40.12 dB PSNR and 0.9874 SSIM, outperforming the strongest event-based baseline EFNet (39.37 dB / 0.9847) by 0.75 dB. The method uses only 13.9M parameters, comparable to EFNet (8.5M) and ELEDNet (12.8M), while the diffusion-based CVS method CBRDM requires 166.2M parameters and 538.5 TFLOPs yet achieves only 30.70 dB PSNR at N=11.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STGDNet outperforms all seven baselines across every exposure setting. Against the strongest event-based method EFNet [34], it gains +0.59 dB PSNR at N=5 and +0.75 dB at N=11, with the gap widening as blur severity increases. The CVS-based diffusion method CBRDM [26] is dramatically outperformed by 10.4 dB (N=5) to 9.4 dB (N=11) in PSNR, while also being ~40× more computationally expensive (538.5 vs. 0.674 TFLOPs). Among RGB-only baselines augmented with SD/TD channels, Restormer* [45] and Turtle* [10] trail STGDNet by 2.37 dB and 2.51 dB respectively at N=5, confirming that simple channel concatenation is insufficient compared to the proposed recurrent cross-modal fusion. Event-based methods STCNet [41] and ELEDNet [19] show consistent degradation under heavier blur (N=9, 11), losing 2.28 dB and 1.76 dB to STGDNet at N=11, while MAENet [35] is only compared qualitatively in real-world settings where it exhibits incomplete deblurring and color blending.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Complementary Vision Sensor (CVS, Tianmouc) introduced in a 2024 Nature paper [42], which provides hardware-level separation of spatial (SD) and temporal (TD) difference signals alongside RGB frames. It departs from the event-camera deblurring line (EFNet, STCNet, ELEDNet) by arguing that events entangle edge and motion cues and saturate under rapid motion, whereas CVS's fixed-rate multi-bit SD/TD signals inherently decouple structure from dynamics at the sensing level. Architecturally, it incorporates the Supervised Attention Module from Zamir et al. [44] and replaces the group's own prior diffusion-based CVS reconstruction (CBRDM [26]) with a lightweight recurrent encoder-decoder, motivated by CBRDM's high compute cost and color distortion. The DMD-based data generation pipeline [27] is repurposed to synthesize realistic CVS training data from existing high-frame-rate RGB sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first comprehensive benchmark and dataset for CVS-based motion deblurring, including a DMD-based hardware data-generation pipeline that produces pixel-aligned, multi-exposure training pairs without requiring a physical CVS in the loop. The performance boundary analysis (rotating-disk benchmark) provides a practical characterization of where CVS deblurring breaks down, which is valuable for sensor deployment decisions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to higher resolutions or longer exposures beyond the tested range, whether the recurrent architecture can be made real-time on embedded hardware, and how the method generalizes to CVS configurations with different frame-rate/precision trade-offs. The strong ablation result—that simple channel concatenation of SD/TD to a standard network already yields large gains—suggests the sensor data itself is the dominant factor, leaving room for further architectural innovation.
