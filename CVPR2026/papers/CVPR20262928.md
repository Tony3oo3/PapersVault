## Problem

Existing enzyme kinetic prediction pipelines fuse enzyme and substrate representations through shallow operations (concatenation, single cross-attention layer) and regress a single score, implicitly treating catalysis as a static compatibility problem. This overlooks the staged nature of enzymatic turnover—substrate recognition followed by conformational adaptation—and fails to explicitly condition pretrained Protein Language Models on the concrete substrate and pocket geometry, risking erosion of biochemical priors when 3D evidence is naively injected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262928/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities: an enzyme amino acid sequence (truncated/padded to 1024 tokens, encoded by a PLM with D=1024 latent dimension), a substrate SMILES string (encoded by an MPNN guided by SMILES fingerprint priors), and 3D active-site pocket geometry (encoded by an E-GNN over pocket residues). Outputs are three kinetic constants (k_cat, K_m, K_i) predicted in log10 space via a heteroscedastic Gaussian head that outputs both a mean and log-variance per sample, capturing the positivity and multiplicative noise structure of kinetic parameters.

## Method

ERBA (Enzyme-Reaction Bridging Adapter) implements mechanism-aligned staged conditioning on a frozen-then-fine-tuned PLM backbone. Stage 1, Molecular Recognition Cross-Attention (MRCA), performs single-layer cross-attention where enzyme residue tokens query substrate tokens to inject substrate semantics into the PLM latent space, followed by residual connection and LayerNorm. Stage 2, Geometry-aware Mixture-of-Experts (G-MoE), constructs a joint routing vector from pooled MRCA pocket features and E-GNN geometry descriptors, activates top-k=2 of n=4 experts via sparse gating, and each expert applies a pocket-local low-rank adaptation modulated by a geometry summary, capturing induced-fit conformational changes. Enzyme-Substrate Distribution Alignment (ESDA) regularizes the process by minimizing RBF-kernel Maximum Mean Discrepancy in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space between the sequence-only, sequence-substrate, and sequence-substrate-structure representation distributions and the original PLM manifold, preventing feature flooding and semantic drift. The full objective combines a heteroscedastic Gaussian NLL task loss with a G-MoE expert-balancing term and the ESDA alignment loss, and the PLM is adapted via LoRA (rank 8) on top layers.

## Results

On the BRENDA/SABIO-RK benchmark, ERBA (ESM2-3B backbone) achieves R²=0.54, PCC=0.74, RMSE=1.13, MAE=0.74 for k_cat; R²=0.61, PCC=0.79, RMSE=0.70, MAE=0.61 for K_m; and R²=0.61, PCC=0.78, RMSE=1.26, MAE=0.91 for K_i, all in log₁₀ space. These represent consistent improvements of 0.10–0.23 in R² over the strongest prior methods (CatPred, CataPro) across all three kinetic endpoints. On the EITLEM out-of-distribution test sets, ERBA further achieves R²=0.50 (k_cat) and R²=0.55 (K_m), roughly doubling the R² of the next-best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERBA outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across all three kinetic endpoints. Against the strongest prior method CatPred [5] (which also uses 3D structure), ERBA improves k_cat R² from 0.40 to 0.54 (+0.14), K_m R² from 0.49 to 0.61 (+0.12), and K_i R² from 0.45 to 0.61 (+0.16), while reducing RMSE by 13–23%. CataPro [53], the next-best on k_cat and K_m, is surpassed by ERBA by +0.13 R² on k_cat and +0.20 R² on K_m. EITLEM [43], the only other method reporting all three endpoints, trails ERBA by +0.20 R² on K_i (0.38→0.61) and +0.24 R² on k_cat. The older sequence-only baselines (DLKcat [28], TurNup [26], UniKP [61], MPEK [48]) are substantially weaker, with R² values below 0.40 on k_cat and below 0.36 on K_m, confirming that shallow fusion of sequence and substrate underuses both PLM priors and structural context. On the EITLEM OOD test sets, ERBA's advantage widens further (R²=0.50 vs. 0.27 for EITLEM on k_cat; R²=0.55 vs. 0.30 for CatPred on K_m), indicating that mechanism-aligned staged conditioning generalizes better to unseen enzyme–substrate pairs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in enzyme kinetics prediction (DLKcat, UniKP, CataPro, CatPred) that treats catalysis as a static enzyme–substrate compatibility problem solved by shallow fusion (concatenation or a single cross-attention layer) followed by regression. It reframes the same prediction targets as a staged conditional process—substrate recognition then conformational adaptation—mirroring the actual enzymology, and realizes this through two adapter modules (MRCA and G-MoE) that fine-tune a PLM rather than consuming its embeddings as fixed features. The ESDA regularizer, which enforces distributional alignment via MMD in an RKHS, is a novel loss design that addresses the well-known risk of pretrained biochemical priors being eroded during multimodal fine-tuning, a concern raised but not formally resolved in prior PLM-based kinetics pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mechanism-aligned staged conditioning offers a principled, extensible interface for incorporating additional modalities (cofactors, point mutations, time-resolved structures) into enzyme kinetics prediction without re-architecting the pipeline, which is practically valuable for high-throughput enzyme design and synthetic biology. The consistent gains across ESM2 (8 M–3 B), ProtT5-3B, and Ankh3 backbones, together with the strongest OOD transfer to the EITLEM test set, suggest the approach generalizes beyond the training distribution rather than overfitting dataset-specific correlations. Open questions remain: performance is still weakest for EC-6 Ligases and for k_cat (the most variable endpoint), the 3D structures for OOD testing are model-predicted (OpenFold/ESMFold) rather than experimental, and the framework has not yet been validated on cofactor-dependent or allosteric enzymes where the two-stage assumption may break down.
