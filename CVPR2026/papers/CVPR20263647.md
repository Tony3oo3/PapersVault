## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting rendering degrades severely in large-scale scenes due to tile-based rasterization overhead, yet existing acceleration methods either require costly scene re-training or focus solely on intra-frame rasterization redundancy, overlooking inter-frame redundancy in preprocessing and sorting stages as well as tile-level load imbalance that causes low GPU utilization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263647/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CaT-GS takes a pre-trained 3D Gaussian model (no re-training required) and a continuous, interactive camera pose trajectory at 120 FPS and 1920×1080 resolution, processing frames in groups of a key frame followed by up to 4 sub-frames. It outputs rendered RGB images in real-time, with the key distinction that sub-frames reuse cached preprocessing and sorting results from the key frame rather than recomputing them, and the camera trajectory encompasses arbitrary panning, zooming, and rotation at varying speeds.

## Method

CaT-GS introduces three mechanisms: (1) speculative multi-frame preprocessing that predicts camera motion (translation and rotation) to compute a 'Gaussian trail'—the swept area of each Gaussian across a batch of frames—then performs fine-grained intersection detection on this trail using ellipse and tangent-line boundary tests, with a motion-adaptive scheduler that shrinks the speculation window when displacement exceeds tile size; (2) inter-frame caching that stores frustum-culled Gaussian indices and depth-sorted render lists from the key frame, allowing sub-frames to skip frustum culling and sorting entirely; (3) load-aware task splitting that partitions heavy tiles (containing >2× average Gaussians) into k subdivisions, reformulating alpha blending as a batched computation (Eq. 7–8) so sub-tasks can be distributed across multiple SMs via a dedicated CUDA kernel, eliminating single-SM stalls.

## Results

CaT-GS achieves up to 10× speedup over vanilla 3DGS and up to 83% speedup over the previous SOTA (Flash-GS) in average FPS across 10 test scenes. On large-scale UAV city scenes (7M+ Gaussians), CaT-GS reaches 202–378 FPS, while Flash-GS manages only 113–241 FPS and vanilla 3DGS drops to 23–54 FPS. Rendering quality is preserved: key-frame PSNR/SSIM exactly matches Flash-GS, and sub-frame degradation is negligible (≤0.05 PSNR drop). The advantage grows with scene complexity, reaching 60–83% improvement over Flash-GS on the largest UAV models versus 45–54% on standard 1–4M Gaussian scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaT-GS outperforms all three baselines on every scene in both speed and quality. Against vanilla 3DGS, CaT-GS achieves 4–10× higher FPS (e.g., 736.5 vs. 63.4 on Truck; 241.5 vs. 25.2 on UAV-1) while maintaining comparable PSNR/SSIM. Against ADR-GS, CaT-GS wins on FPS by 73–100% (e.g., 736.5 vs. 425.2 on Truck) and also surpasses it in rendering quality, since ADR-GS requires model retraining that degrades PSNR by 1.7–2.1 dB (e.g., 26.50 vs. 28.21 on Truck). Against Flash-GS (the previous SOTA), CaT-GS achieves 45–83% higher FPS, with the largest gains on the largest UAV scenes (83% on UAV-1, 78% on UAV-2). In terms of quality, CaT-GS key frames exactly match Flash-GS (identical PSNR/SSIM values), while sub-frames show a negligible ≤0.05 PSNR drop attributable to speculative Gaussian positioning. The ablation confirms that inter-frame caching contributes up to 80% of the speedup, while load-aware task splitting adds a further ~10%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CaT-GS extends the 3DGS rendering pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023) by introducing temporal exploitation across consecutive frames, departing from prior acceleration work (FlashGS, ADR-GS) that focused exclusively on intra-frame redundancy reduction through tighter Gaussian-tile intersection tests and opacity pruning. While FlashGS improved compute-memory balance and ADR-GS used adaptive bounding boxes, both evaluated on fixed camera poses from reconstruction datasets; CaT-GS reframes the problem around continuous interactive trajectories where inter-frame coherence in frustum culling and depth sorting becomes exploitable. The tile load-balancing contribution via alpha-blending-aware task splitting is orthogonal to prior intersection-based optimizations and addresses a GPU scheduling bottleneck that neither FlashGS nor ADR-GS tackled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By pushing large-scale UAV city scenes (7–8M Gaussians) from ~25–55 FPS to 200+ FPS, CaT-GS makes real-time interactive rendering of city-scale 3DGS models practically viable, which was previously infeasible. The finding that inter-frame caching (eliminating sorting and frustum culling for sub-frames) contributes up to 80% of the speedup while tile task splitting contributes only ~10% suggests the field has under-invested in temporal redundancy relative to spatial optimization. Limitations include the assumption of static scenes (no dynamic objects tested), the ~10% render-list redundancy from speculative preprocessing, and the dependence on smooth camera trajectories; open questions include how the approach degrades under rapid viewpoint changes, dynamic scene content, and whether the speculative window can be further adapted per-region rather than globally.
