## Problem

Existing VLM-based FSCIL methods struggle with the stability-plasticity dilemma: they cannot simultaneously retain base-class knowledge and effectively learn new classes from only a handful of samples, leading to catastrophic forgetting and few-shot overfitting. Prior prompt-learning approaches for FSCIL only fine-tune shallow input prompts and fail to fully exploit the rich base-class data available during the initial session, limiting their representational capacity for both old and new classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (processed at CLIP's 224×224 resolution) and outputs a class label over all classes encountered so far. At inference, the final prediction combines three scoring channels: global vision-text cosine logits, visual prototype classifier logits, and local Optimal Transport-based logits, weighted by learned coefficients. The text side consumes category-specific learnable prompts, local attribute prompts (e.g., color, shape), and LLM-generated semantic descriptions that serve as distillation targets during training but are not needed at test time.

## Method

SGLC builds on a frozen CLIP ViT-B-16 backbone and introduces a dual-alignment prompt learning architecture. At the global level, it injects learnable deep-layer prompts into both vision and language branches plus a bottleneck visual-adaptive adapter, then aligns visual features with category-specific text prototypes; during incremental sessions, only the current session's text prompts are updated while old-class prompts remain frozen, directly preventing forgetting. At the local level, it generates multiple visual feature views via distinct visual prompts and multiple textual attribute features via LLM-identified inter-class attributes (e.g., color, shape), then aligns them through an Optimal Transport formulation solved with the Sinkhorn algorithm rather than naive pairwise cosine matching, which better handles overlapping attribute information. A semantic-guided distillation loss uses LLM-generated descriptions to regularize both global and local text prompts, mitigating overfitting to the few novel-class samples, while a visual diversity loss preserves heterogeneity among local feature views.

## Results

SGLC achieves the best average accuracy (A_avg) across all three FSCIL benchmarks: 95.12% on miniImageNet, 80.18% on CUB200, and 84.57% on CIFAR-100, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art by 1.60%, 1.06%, and 3.19% respectively. The method also leads on base-session accuracy (A_base: 97.52%, 87.05%, 89.25%) and incremental-session average accuracy (A_navg: 94.82%, 79.49%, 83.99%). Notably, SGLC achieves these results with only 0.71M trainable parameters (0.47% of the ViT-B-CLIP backbone), compared to 5.84M for IVFL and 86.68M for Approxima.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGLC outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. On miniImageNet, it surpasses the closest competitor IVFL* (93.62% A_avg) by 1.50% and BiMC (93.60%) by 1.52%, while also achieving the highest base accuracy (97.52% vs. 96.37% for IVFL*). On CUB200, SGLC leads Approxima (79.12% A_avg) by 1.06% and FDR (77.70%) by 2.48%, with a particularly large gap in incremental accuracy (79.49% vs. 78.38% for Approxima). On CIFAR-100, the margin is largest: SGLC exceeds FDR (79.30% A_avg) by 5.27% and IVFL* (78.89%) by 5.68%, indicating that the semantic-guided global-local alignment is especially effective on fine-grained or diverse-class datasets. Among ResNet-18-based methods, SGLC's ViT-B-CLIP backbone provides a substantial advantage, but even among VLM-based methods, SGLC consistently wins, suggesting the dual-alignment and LLM-guided distillation contribute beyond backbone capacity. The method also demonstrates superior parameter efficiency, using only 0.71M trainable parameters versus 5.84M (IVFL) and 86.68M (Approxima).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CoOp prompt-learning paradigm for CLIP by introducing deep-layer prompts in both vision and language branches, a visual-adaptive adapter, and category-specific text prompts that can be selectively frozen during incremental sessions. It departs from prior VLM-based FSCIL methods (Approxima, BiMC, IVFL) that perform only global image-to-text alignment, by adding a local multi-view alignment via Optimal Transport between attribute-specific text features and multiple visual feature views. The use of LLMs to generate semantic descriptions that are distilled into learnable prompts (rather than used as direct classification features) is a distinct design choice compared to prior text-augmented FSCIL approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework achieves consistent SOTA across all three standard FSCIL benchmarks while training only 0.47% of parameters (0.71M vs. 86.68M for full fine-tuning), offering a practical efficiency advantage for deployment in resource-constrained incremental settings. The clean separation—freezing global prototypes and prompts of old classes while updating only current-class prompts—provides a simple, interpretable mechanism for mitigating catastrophic forgetting without data replay. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to many incremental sessions (beyond the 10 tested), whether the LLM-generated descriptions remain beneficial when the base session is very small, and how the method performs on larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet-1K.
