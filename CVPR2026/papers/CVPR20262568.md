## Problem

Existing computational lithography methods, including FNO-based frameworks like DOINN and CFNO, implicitly model diffraction in the frequency domain but do not explicitly incorporate the physical principles of optical diffraction theory that govern light propagation through masks. This lack of explicit physics integration limits their ability to capture the full complexity of lithographic behavior and, critically, their generalization to out-of-distribution mask designs encountered in real manufacturing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262568/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For mask optimization, the model takes a 2048×2048 grayscale target wafer pattern (1 nm² per pixel) and produces an optimized binary mask; for lithography simulation, it takes an optimized mask and outputs both aerial and resist images at the same resolution. Internally, the network operates in the complex domain, decomposing optical fields into real and imaginary components at each convolution step. A Total Variation loss is applied during training to suppress grainy artifacts and improve mask manufacturability, with a weighting parameter λ_tv controlling the regularization strength.

## Method

OptiCo is a CNN built on a MetaNeXt backbone that explicitly embeds optical diffraction physics through an Optical Phase (OP) kernel derived from the Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction integral. The OP kernel Q(x,y) = exp(jk/2z [x²+y²]) (Fresnel form) encodes the parabolic phase variation of light propagation and is combined with a learnable complex weight via element-wise multiplication (or a stricter scalar-scaled variant) to form an effective kernel. The core operation is a complex convolution: input and kernel are decomposed into real/imaginary parts, and four real convolutions reconstruct the complex output, so each convolution step inherently reflects physical wave propagation. The OP-convolved feature is phase-modulated with the backbone output via a Hadamard product and added back as a residual, injecting diffraction effects into the mask representation. The framework is formulation-flexible, supporting Fresnel, Helmholtz-Kirchhoff, Green's function, and Hopkins TCC kernels, and is trained end-to-end on LithoBench with an MSE + TV loss objective.

## Results

On the primary mask optimization task (LithoBench), OptiCo achieves the best average MSE (14,535), PVB (29,373), and EPE (0.4) across all four subtasks, outperforming the closest competitor ILILT (MSE 22,143; EPE 2.6). The advantage is most dramatic on OOD sets: on StdContact, OptiCo records an EPE of 0.1 versus 7.1 (ILILT), 55.8 (DOINN), and 55.1 (CFNO). On the secondary lithography simulation task, OptiCo also leads with the lowest average MSE_aerial (5.01e-6) and highest average IoU (0.96), surpassing DOINN (1.31e-5 / 0.95) and Nitho (1.54e-5 / 0.93).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiCo outperforms all five baselines on the primary mask optimization task. Against the closest competitor ILILT (which embeds Inverse Lithography Technology), OptiCo reduces average MSE from 22,143 to 14,535 and average EPE from 2.6 to 0.4; the gap widens sharply on OOD StdContact where OptiCo achieves EPE 0.1 versus ILILT's 7.1. Against the FNO-based methods DOINN and CFNO, which implicitly model diffraction in Fourier space, OptiCo achieves roughly 2.4× lower average MSE (14,535 vs. 34,691 and 38,578) and dramatically lower OOD EPE (0.1 vs. 55.8 and 55.1 on StdContact), demonstrating the benefit of explicit over implicit physics encoding. Against the purely data-driven DAMO (UNet++ backbone), OptiCo cuts average MSE by 44% (14,535 vs. 26,056) and reduces StdContact EPE from 26.7 to 0.1. On the secondary lithography simulation task, OptiCo also leads all baselines including Nitho (complex-valued neural fields), achieving the lowest average MSE_aerial (5.01e-6 vs. 1.31e-5 for DOINN) and highest IoU (0.96 vs. 0.95).

## Relation to Prior Work

OptiCo carves new ground by reinterpreting the Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction integral as a convolution and using the resulting optical phase (OP) kernel as a fixed, physics-grounded filter within a CNN, rather than relying on data-driven learning or implicit Fourier-space modeling. It departs from the FNO-based lineage (DOINN, CFNO) that captures diffraction indirectly through learned spectral embeddings, and from ILILT/Nitho which embed physics through iterative ILT or complex MLPs, by making the diffraction kernel an explicit architectural primitive. The MetaNeXt backbone and TV loss are borrowed from general CNN and regularization literature, but the core contribution is the conceptual identification of the diffraction integral as a convolution kernel that can be directly instantiated in a network layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large OOD generalization gap (EPE 0.079 vs. 22.6–55.8 for competitors on StdContact) is practically significant because real manufacturing mask patterns routinely deviate from training distributions, and the formulation-flexibility (Fresnel, Green's, Helmholtz, Hopkins TCC) suggests the OP kernel can be swapped to match different optical regimes without retraining. Open questions include whether the approach scales to full-chip layouts, multi-wavelength or 3D mask effects, and whether the fixed-kernel assumption holds under process variation. The ablation showing that complex-domain modeling alone (without the specific diffraction kernel) already recovers much of the OOD gain raises the question of how much of the benefit is truly physics-specific versus a general advantage of complex-valued feature spaces.
