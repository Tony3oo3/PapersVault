## Problem

Simplified optical systems (single-lens, metalens) suffer from compound degradation where spatial-varying aberration blur and diffuse veiling glare coexist, yet existing methods address them independently and fail under this coupling. A critical bottleneck is the absence of realistic paired training data: physically-accurate veiling glare simulation requires computationally prohibitive non-sequential ray-tracing, and conventional scattering models (e.g., atmospheric haze) are physically incompatible with glare's depth-independent, spatial-varying nature.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (1280×1920) captured by a simplified optical system, containing both aberration blur and veiling glare, and produces a restored clean image. Internally, the pipeline also estimates latent optical transmission and glare maps that parameterize the spatial distribution of contrast attenuation and additive light. Training data consists of paired clean/degraded images generated offline by VeilGen from 500 clean Flickr2K images, combined with source-domain screen-capture pairs from DIV2K.

## Method

The framework is a three-stage physics-informed pipeline. Stage I (VeilGen) is a Stable Diffusion-based generator that embeds a Latent Optical Transmission and Glare Map Predictor (LOTGMP) to estimate physical maps during denoising, which are injected via a Veiling Glare Imposition Module (VGIM) to modulate features and synthesize realistic compound degradations. Stage II distills VeilGen into a lightweight Distilled Degradation Network (DDN) that preserves the forward degradation behavior for efficient supervision. Stage III (DeVeiler) is a restoration network whose Veiling Glare Compensation Module (VGCM) applies an inverse modulation using the same latent maps, trained with a reversibility loss that enforces cycle consistency through the frozen DDN—forcing the latent maps to parameterize two mutually inverse operations and thereby acquire physical interpretability rather than remaining diffusion-optimized black-box features.

## Results

On the Screen-Compound domain (paired GT), DeVeiler achieves PSNR 22.38 / SSIM 0.729 / LPIPS 0.261 on the single-lens (SL) system and PSNR 21.57 / SSIM 0.746 / LPIPS 0.301 on the metasurface-refractive hybrid lens (MRL) system, outperforming all 10 baselines across every metric. On the Realworld-Compound domain (no GT), DeVeiler attains the best CLIPIQA (0.607 SL, 0.440 MRL) and NIQE (4.448 SL, 5.296 MRL) scores, confirming generalizability to uncontrolled real-world captures. Inference latency is 0.387 s per 1280×1920 image on an A100 GPU, comparable to the fastest baselines (0.208–0.428 s) and far below diffusion-based methods (66–92 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeVeiler wins on every metric in both the Screen-Compound and Realworld-Compound evaluations. On Screen-SL, it surpasses the strongest single-degradation baseline (SwinIR, PSNR 18.18) by 4.2 dB and the best cascaded pipeline (SwinIR + Flare7K++, PSNR 21.67) by 0.71 dB, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.261 vs. 0.297). On Screen-MRL, the gap over QDMR (the best DA baseline, PSNR 20.67) is 0.90 dB, and over SwinIR + Flare7K++ (PSNR 20.74) it is 0.83 dB. Cascaded dehazing pipelines (SwinIR + RIDCP, SwinIR + DiffDehaze) consistently underperform, confirming that atmospheric-scattering models are physically incompatible with depth-independent veiling glare. On the no-reference Realworld-Compound domain, DeVeiler leads in CLIPIQA by 0.034 (SL) and 0.012 (MRL) over the next-best method, and in NIQE by 1.69 (SL) and 0.93 (MRL), demonstrating that the gains are not artifacts of paired-GT evaluation. All baselines fall into three categories—single-degradation, cascaded, and domain-adaptation—and none jointly model the coupled aberration-plus-glare degradation that DeVeiler addresses through its reversible VGIM/VGCM structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DiffBIR/Stable Diffusion pipeline for generative image restoration and the domain-adaptation paradigm from DiffDehaze (CVPR 2025), but departs from both by embedding explicit optical physics (transmission and glare maps) into the diffusion process via the LOTGMP module, rather than treating degradation as a black-box statistical mapping. It extends the computational aberration correction (CAC) literature, which has focused on spatially-varying blur, by jointly modeling the co-occurring veiling glare that prior CAC methods cannot address. The reversibility constraint coupling VGIM (forward) and VGCM (inverse) is a novel training paradigm not seen in prior restoration or domain-adaptation work, and the distillation of a multi-step diffusion model into a lightweight forward model (DDN) for efficient supervision is a practical departure from invoking the full generator during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical bottleneck in compact imaging systems (AR/VR displays, metalens cameras) where veiling glare from non-ideal coatings is pervasive yet unaddressed by existing CAC or dehazing pipelines. The physics-informed latent maps provide interpretability and physical fidelity that black-box generative approaches lack, and the reversibility constraint offers a general recipe for training restoration networks that explicitly invert a learned forward degradation. Limitations include evaluation on only two lens prototypes via screen-capture, the reliance on a frozen Stable Diffusion backbone, and the assumption that aberration can be sufficiently handled by source-domain pre-training before glare removal. Open questions include whether the latent-map formulation generalizes to more complex multi-element optical assemblies and whether the approach can be made real-time for embedded deployment.
