## Problem

Video Novel View Synthesis from a single monocular video is ill-posed, and existing generative approaches face a fundamental data bottleneck: synthetic multi-view data is diversity-limited, while reformulating the task as inpainting on real monocular videos introduces a training-inference gap that causes visual artifacts (e.g., incorrect occlusion handling). The paper asks whether large-scale real monocular videos can be used for VNVS training without this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260153/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 49-frame monocular source video at 480×480 resolution and a target camera trajectory (sequence of 4×4 poses) as input, and produces a novel-view video at the same resolution. The control signal is a 9-channel tensor combining the source video, a 2D correspondence field, the warped source video, and a render mask. During training, correspondence is obtained via optical flow (RAFT) between two arbitrary clips; during inference, it is derived from depth-based point-map warping (GeometryCrafter), creating a deliberate train/inference asymmetry that the architecture is designed to absorb.

## Method

The core insight is to reformulate VNVS from an inpainting problem into a correspondence-guided generation task: the composite operation Φ⁻¹ ∘ T^r ∘ Φ defines a 2D pixel correspondence C^r that serves as the control signal, and any two clips from a monocular video with optical flow between them form a valid training pair, enabling self-supervised scaling on ~3M real videos. Architecturally, a Correspondence Projector (convolutional layers + patchifying) encodes the 9-channel control into tokens spatially aligned with the video latent, and VNVS Blocks—inserted between pretrained Wan MMDiT blocks—perform joint attention over video and control tokens while explicitly decoupling from text features. Each VNVS Block omits the FFN layer, treating controllability as transient contextual memory handled by attention alone. A Houdini-based synthetic pipeline (10K samples with exact geometric correspondence) supplements real data to improve pose precision, and the full system is trained with flow matching loss on 64 A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the single-view dataset (400 videos from Panda-70M), Scaling4D achieves the best scores across all seven metrics: FID 62.83, FVD 411.17, CLIP-V 91.81, RotErr 6.48°, and TransErr 286.77, outperforming the next-best method (Voyager) by 5.58 in FID and 60.8 in TransErr. On the multi-view iPhone dataset, Scaling4D leads with PSNR 14.85 dB, SSIM 0.336, and LPIPS 0.468, surpassing the closest baseline (TrajectoryCrafter, PSNR 14.13) by 0.72 dB in PSNR. The method also demonstrates clear scalability: performance improves monotonically as training data grows from 1k to 3M samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scaling4D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both the single-view and multi-view evaluations. Against the closest competitors (Voyager and TrajectoryCrafter, both inpainting-based), the gains are most pronounced in camera control accuracy: TransErr drops from 347.57 (Voyager) and 320.38 (TrajectoryCrafter) to 286.77, and RotErr improves from 7.04/6.65 to 6.48. ReCamMaster-Wan, which relies on implicit camera-matrix control, is substantially weaker (FID 83.71, TransErr 737.71) and fails entirely on the multi-view iPhone dataset due to its requirement that the target trajectory's first frame align with the source. The inpainting-based methods (GEN3C, TrajectoryCrafter, Voyager) exhibit characteristic artifacts—missing foreground objects, holes beneath objects, and incomplete back-facing surfaces—attributed to their training-inference gap, which Scaling4D's correspondence-guided formulation explicitly eliminates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan MMDiT video generation model as its foundation, inserting lightweight VNVS Blocks between pretrained DiT layers to inject correspondence control. It departs conceptually from prior generative VNVS methods (GEN3C, TrajectoryCrafter, Voyager) that frame the problem as point-map-guided inpainting, and from ReCamMaster which relies on synthetic multi-view data with implicit geometry control. The key reframing—recognizing that the composite unproject-project operation defines a 2D correspondence, and that optical flow in any monocular video provides such correspondences—converts VNVS into a self-supervised correspondence-guided generation task, eliminating the training-inference gap inherent in inpainting formulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling scalable training on ~3M real monocular videos (SpatialVID) rather than being limited to small synthetic datasets, this work addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in VNVS and demonstrates a clear positive correlation between data volume and performance. The correspondence-guided formulation is architecturally clean (no FFN layers, text-decoupled attention) and could be adopted by other controllable video generation tasks. Open questions remain: pose accuracy plateaus at high data volumes suggesting an architectural ceiling, visual quality metrics have not yet saturated, and the reliance on a separate depth estimator (GeometryCrafter) at inference adds a potential failure point. The Houdini-based synthetic pipeline, while useful for precision, still cannot match real-data diversity, leaving room for improved procedural generation.
