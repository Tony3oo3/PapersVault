## Problem

Evaluating hallucinations in image restoration is blocked by a circular dependency: reliable hallucination analysis requires labeled data, yet hallucinations are ambiguous, costly to annotate (inter-annotator κ=0.30), and existing quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) systematically favor perceptual sharpness over semantic correctness. No prior work provides controllable, taxonomically grounded hallucination synthesis with spatial annotations for systematic benchmarking in safety-critical restoration tasks.

## Input / Output

HalluGen takes a degraded measurement y (produced by a composite forward operator A = Blur(DS_k(Γ_γ(·))) on 256×256 MRI slices), the corresponding ground-truth image x_gt, and a binary mask m (patches of 16–24 px selected via entropy-based sampling) to produce a hallucinated reconstruction x̂ with known patch-level annotations. The resulting dataset entry is the tuple (x_gt, y, x̂, m), with 4,350 images balanced across intrinsic/extrinsic types and 1–3 hallucinated patches per image. Downstream, the SHAFE metric consumes a prediction–ground-truth pair and outputs a scalar hallucination score plus a spatial heatmap, while the reference-free detector takes only x̂ and y (no ground truth) and outputs a binary hallucination-presence label.

## Method

HalluGen extends Diffusion Posterior Sampling by replacing the uniform data-consistency gradient with a masked, sign-flipped update: within hallucination patches it performs gradient ascent on the measurement-consistency loss (intrinsic) or on pixel- and feature-space divergence from ground truth (extrinsic), while outside patches it retains standard gradient descent. For extrinsic hallucinations, a pretrained feature extractor (DINOv3, SAM, or MedSAM) is incorporated to drive semantic deviation in feature space while preserving measurement consistency, addressing the null-space ambiguity of nonlinear forward operators. A Hallucination Verification Module enforces taxonomy compliance via Cohen's d thresholds on measurement- and image-space deviations, re-sampling patches that fail. Patch locations are selected by an annotation-free Shannon-entropy heuristic targeting non-homogeneous regions, and the diffusion prior acts as a soft manifold regularizer—gradient ascent is gradually suppressed in late timesteps so the denoising network re-anchors samples to the data manifold, preserving perceptual realism (FID ≈ 0.36–0.43) while segmentation IoU within hallucinated regions drops from 0.86 to 0.36.

## Results

On the HalluGen hallucination benchmark, SHAFE-DINOv3 achieves AUC-ROC of 0.76 (intrinsic) and 0.74 (extrinsic), with Cohen's |d| of 1.12 and 0.92 respectively, substantially outperforming the best traditional metric (MS-SSIM: AUC 0.79/0.21, Cohen's |d| 0.69/0.16). On real restoration outputs (SwinIR, EDSR, ESRGAN), SHAFE-DINOv3 reaches a Real AUC of 0.79, improving detection by 0.25 over the best baseline. For hallucination generation, HalluGen+SAM achieves FID 0.36 (comparable to DPS's 0.32) while dropping segmentation IoU from 0.861 to 0.367, confirming realistic yet semantically incorrect outputs. The reference-free CNN detector trained on HalluGen data achieves AUC 0.91 on intrinsic and 0.77 on extrinsic synthetic hallucinations, generalizing to 0.73 AUC on real predictions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHAFE variants dominate all traditional metrics on the hallucination benchmark: SHAFE-ResNet50 achieves AUC 0.82/0.72 (intrinsic/extrinsic) versus MS-SSIM's 0.79/0.21, and LPIPS/DISTS perform near chance (AUC ≈ 0.50–0.54), confirming that standard perceptual metrics are essentially blind to hallucinations. On real restoration outputs, SHAFE-DINOv3 (Real AUC 0.79) outperforms LPIPS (0.42) and DISTS (0.44) by over 0.3 AUC, with the paper reporting a 0.25 AUC improvement and 24-percentage-point reduction in false negatives over the best baseline. For hallucination generation (Table 1), HalluGen+SAM matches DPS's FID (0.36 vs. 0.32) while reducing segmentation IoU from 0.861 to 0.367, whereas Random Rotation achieves the same FID but only works for intrinsic hallucinations and introduces visible patch artifacts, and Measurement Perturbation yields higher FID (0.48) with weaker semantic deviation (IoU 0.721). The reference-free detector trained solely on HalluGen data generalizes to real SwinIR/EDSR/ESRGAN outputs (AUC 0.73), though detection is notably harder for extrinsic hallucinations (AUC 0.77) than intrinsic ones (AUC 0.91) due to the latter's measurement-consistency violation.

## Relation to Prior Work

HalluGen extends Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) by replacing the uniform data-consistency gradient with masked, direction-controlled gradient ascent/descent, enabling spatially targeted hallucination injection while preserving the diffusion prior's manifold regularization. It adopts the intrinsic/extrinsic hallucination taxonomy from prior tomographic and medical imaging work [6, 23] but is the first to operationalize it through controllable synthesis in a general inverse-problem setting. The SHAFE metric repurposes pretrained vision encoders (DINOv3, SAM, MedSAM) with a soft-attention aggregation scheme borrowed from NLP sentence-pair modeling, departing from the uniform spatial averaging that characterizes LPIPS and DISTS. The reference-free detection CNN is deliberately simple, positioned as a proof-of-concept that synthetic hallucination data suffices for training detectors that transfer to real model failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper breaks a circular dependency that has stalled hallucination research in image restoration: you need labeled hallucinations to build detectors and metrics, but labeling is subjective (κ=0.30) and unscalable. By providing 4,350 annotated images with known hallucination type, location, and severity, it enables the first systematic benchmark of metric sensitivity and the first reference-free detector that generalizes to real SwinIR/EDSR/ESRGAN outputs. Key limitations include failure in homogeneous regions where the diffusion prior dominates, restriction to healthy brain MRIs, and lack of explicit semantic control over *what* is hallucinated. Open questions include extending to pathological scans, multi-organ imaging, and whether SHAFE's soft-attention mechanism can be integrated into restoration training loops for hallucination-aware optimization.
