## Problem

MeanFlow enables principled one-step image generation but has only been demonstrated with fixed class labels (e.g., ImageNet). Extending it to flexible text conditioning is non-trivial: naively integrating powerful LLM-based text encoders with standard diffusion training losses yields unsatisfactory results, and the underlying cause—why some text representations fail under extremely limited refinement steps—remains unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263846/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input, which is encoded by the BLIP3o-NEXT LLM-based text encoder into a sequence of token embeddings that condition the velocity network. It outputs a single RGB image generated in as few as one denoising step (or 2, 4, or 8 steps for higher quality), operating in the latent space of the BLIP3o-NEXT flow-matching backbone. Training uses approximately 170k image-text pairs from BLIP3o-60k, ShareGPT-4o, and Echo-4o at a batch size of 128.

## Method

The paper identifies two critical properties that text representations must possess for effective few-step generation: semantic discriminability (capturing subtle differences among similar texts) and semantic disentanglement (preserving distinct semantic components within a prompt). Through retrieval and subsequence-similarity experiments, it validates that BLIP3o-NEXT's LLM-based text encoder excels on both properties compared to CLIP, T5, and SANA-1.5's encoder. Architecturally, the standard single temporal embedding is replaced with two separate layers—φ_interval(t−r) and φ_end(t)—that jointly encode the time interval and endpoint, enabling the network to predict the average velocity u_θ(z_t, t, r, ψ_text) over an arbitrary segment. Training minimizes the MeanFlow objective with a stop-gradient target computed via Jacobian-vector products, using adaptively sampled (t, r) pairs from uniform or logit-normal distributions whose parameters shift during training to balance short- and long-range segments.

## Results

On GenEval, EMF achieves an overall score of 0.90 with only 4 sampling steps, nearly matching the BLIP3o-NEXT baseline (0.91) that requires 30 steps, and substantially outperforming all distilled models (best: SANA-Sprint at 0.77). In the extreme few-step regime, EMF at 1 step scores 0.74 versus BLIP3o-NEXT's 0.46, and at 2 steps scores 0.85 versus 0.63. On DPG-Bench, EMF at 4 steps reaches 81.20 overall (vs. 82.05 for BLIP3o-NEXT at 30 steps), and on HPS-v2.1 it attains 29.25 average human preference score, closely matching the 30-step baseline (29.42).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMF at 4 steps (GenEval 0.90) decisively outperforms every distilled model tested: SANA-Sprint (0.77), rCM (0.75), FLUX.1-schnell (0.69), SD3.5-L-Turbo (0.68), and all SDXL-based distillations (0.50–0.58), with the largest gap over SANA-Sprint at +0.13. Against the BLIP3o-NEXT backbone itself, EMF at 4 steps (0.90) surpasses the 4-step baseline (0.86) and nearly matches the 30-step reference (0.91), while at 1 step EMF (0.74) more than doubles the 1-step baseline (0.46) and at 2 steps (0.85) exceeds the 2-step baseline (0.63) by +0.22. On DPG-Bench, EMF at 4 steps (81.20) trails BLIP3o-NEXT at 30 steps (82.05) by only 0.85 points but exceeds the 4-step baseline (78.15) by +3.05. On HPS-v2.1, EMF at 4 steps (29.25) is within 0.17 of the 30-step baseline (29.42) while the 4-step baseline scores only 26.96. Notably, EMF also scales gracefully beyond 4 steps (DPG-Bench rises to 81.94 at 8 steps), unlike consistency-distilled models that saturate or degrade at higher step counts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MeanFlow (Geng et al., 2025), a principled one-step generation framework built on average-velocity flow maps, from its original class-label-conditioned ImageNet setting to flexible text-conditioned generation. It builds on BLIP3o-NEXT (Chen et al., 2025) as the diffusion backbone and LLM-based text encoder, adapting MeanFlow's bidirectional time conditioning and JVP-based training objective to handle text features. The work departs from prior few-step methods (consistency models, SANA-Sprint, rCM) by avoiding distillation from a teacher model and instead fine-tuning a single pretrained checkpoint, while also distinguishing itself from representation-learning-for-generation work by identifying specific text-encoder properties (discriminability, disentanglement) as the critical enabler rather than visual representation quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that text-encoder discriminability and disentanglement are the bottleneck for MeanFlow in T2I settings provides a concrete, testable criterion for selecting or designing text encoders in few-step generation pipelines, which is practically useful as the field moves toward LLM-based conditioning. The SANA-1.5 failure—even after domain-matched encoder fine-tuning—suggests that not all LLM encoders are interchangeable and that vision-language alignment during pretraining is non-negotiable for one-step generation, a cautionary result for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether the JVP stability bottleneck can be further mitigated, and whether the discriminability/disentanglement criteria generalize beyond the two encoders that were validated.
