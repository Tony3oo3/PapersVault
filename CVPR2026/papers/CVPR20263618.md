## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models exhibit a failure mode the authors term Visual Inertia: they rigidly commit to initial visual interpretations and rarely revise them even when inconsistencies emerge, leading to wrong answers despite flawless textual logic. Existing structured CoT patterns and RLVR methods refine textual reasoning chains but lack mechanisms to trigger re-evaluation of the underlying visual evidence, leaving this cross-domain gap unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image paired with a free-form text question (multiple-choice, math, or short open-ended). It produces a free-form reasoning chain in which referenced visual cues are explicitly tagged with <vcues_*> markers, optionally followed by a self-triggered cue-rethinking segment where the model revisits and revises those cues, and concludes with a concise answer in a standardized format (exact option letter, verified math expression, or short phrase). The rethinking step is adaptive: the model decides when to invoke it rather than performing it on every sample.

## Method

GThinker introduces the Cue-Rethinking Pattern, a three-stage free-form reasoning mechanism (initial cue-grounded reasoning, self-triggered rethinking prompt, cue-based re-examination and revision) that avoids rigid CoT templates while making visual re-evaluation an integral, adaptive part of the reasoning flow. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Pattern-Guided Cold Start on 7K annotated samples, where a Judge-Guided Selective Training strategy uses GPT-4o to diagnose which base-model failures stem from flawed visual cues and selectively applies rethinking annotations only to those cases, teaching the model both how and when to rethink; (2) Incentive Reinforcement Learning using DAPO with a hybrid reward (exact match for MC, Math-Verify for math, edit-distance for open-ended) and offline balanced sampling over 4K curated samples. The GThinker-11K dataset is built via an iterative multimodal annotation pipeline that feeds image+question+answer to successive models for refinement, plus a flawed-sampling-contrast-generation step where o3 contrasts high-temperature incorrect rollouts against correct annotations to produce rethinking examples.

## Results

On the M³CoT benchmark, GThinker-7B achieves a leading 81.5% overall accuracy among open-source models, surpassing o4-mini (80.9%) by 0.6 points and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by a substantial +19.1 points. On the broader suite of reasoning benchmarks, GThinker-7B delivers consistent gains over its base model: +2.5 on MMStar (66.4 vs. 63.9), +4.5 on MathVista (72.7 vs. 68.2), +2.4 on MMMU-Pro (40.7 vs. 38.3), and +2.2 on HallusionBench (55.1 vs. 52.9), yielding an average improvement of approximately 2.1% across general reasoning benchmarks. The model also outperforms the previous open-source SOTA InternVL2.5-MPO-8B by +8.2 points on M³CoT (81.5 vs. 73.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GThinker-7B (81.5%) surpasses o4-mini (80.9%) on M³CoT by 0.6 points, making it the strongest open-source model on this benchmark, though it trails the larger closed-source o3 (83.8%) and Gemini-2.5 Pro (85.9%). Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (62.4%), GThinker gains +19.1 points, and against the structured reasoner LLaVA-CoT-11B (56.0%), the margin widens to +25.5 points. Among RL-trained open-source competitors, GThinker outperforms InternVL2.5-MPO-8B (73.3%) by +8.2, VLAA-Thinker-7B (68.0%) by +13.5, MM-Eureka-7B (67.4%) by +14.1, and R1-OneVision-7B (61.8%) by +19.7 on M³CoT. On the secondary benchmarks, GThinker consistently leads all open-source baselines: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +2.5 on MMStar, +4.5 on MathVista, and +2.2 on HallusionBench, while surpassing LLaVA-CoT-11B by +8.8 on MMStar and +17.9 on MathVista. Notably, GThinker avoids the domain-specific degradation seen in VLAA-Thinker-7B and MM-Eureka-7B (which underperform their base models on MMStar), achieving balanced gains across science, commonsense, and mathematics sub-domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two main lines of multimodal reasoning work: structured CoT methods (LLaVA-CoT, Virgo, Mulberry) that impose rigid reasoning templates, and RLVR-based approaches (MM-Eureka, R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker) that refine textual reasoning chains via verified rewards. The key departure is the identification of 'Visual Inertia'—a failure mode where models commit to initial visual interpretations and never revise them—which neither structured patterns nor standard RLVR address. Methodologically, it builds on DAPO for the RL stage but introduces a novel two-stage pipeline (judge-guided selective cold start followed by incentive RL) specifically designed to teach both *how* and *when* to re-examine visual cues, rather than relying on RL alone to discover this behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, adoptable mechanism (Cue-Rethinking with explicit visual cue markers) that MLLM developers can integrate to mitigate a previously under-addressed failure mode. The finding that RLVR alone cannot reliably induce visual rethinking—requiring explicit pattern-guided supervision first—is a useful practical insight for the community building reasoning MLLMs. Open questions include whether this approach scales to larger models or video inputs, whether multiple rethinking passes are needed for more complex visual scenes, and whether the GThinker-11K dataset (11K samples) is sufficient for broader generalization or whether the gains plateau with more data.
