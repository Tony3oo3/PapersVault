## Problem

Models trained on monocular portrait videos produce incomplete 3D head reconstructions because the driving expression signal becomes entangled with the target viewpoint, causing the network to predict only the visible portion of the head. Multi-view data resolves this entanglement but is too scarce for broad identity coverage, and simply mixing both data types does not prevent the model from exploiting the monocular shortcut.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261288/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB portrait image (or 4 images in few-shot mode, or ~900 frames from a monocular video) and produces a set of articulated 3D Gaussians anchored to a template head mesh UV space, controllable via FLAME expression codes and an arbitrary camera pose. The avatar code is a 2D latent tensor in UV space that is agnostic to viewpoint and expression, and the final rendering uses the tile-based differentiable rasterizer from 3DGS. In fitting mode, only the avatar code is optimized while the entire decoder remains frozen.

## Method

FlexAvatar uses a transformer encoder-decoder where the encoder extracts DINOv2+ViT image features and cross-attends them to UV-anchored sinusoidal queries to produce a compressed avatar code, while the decoder cross-attends with sequenced expression codes and upsamples via a StyleGAN2-PixelShuffle block before decoding 3D Gaussian attributes. The key architectural contribution is a pair of learnable bias-sink tokens (z_2D, z_3D) concatenated to the expression sequence: during training, monocular samples receive z_2D and multi-view samples receive z_3D, explicitly absorbing dataset-specific biases so the model learns to produce partial heads only under the monocular token. At inference, z_3D is always used, yielding complete 3D heads while retaining the generalization from monocular training. Training combines L1, SSIM, DINOv2, and SAM perceptual losses over 1M steps on mixed monocular (CelebV-Text, Hello3) and multi-view (NeRSemble, Ava256, Cafca) data, and the resulting smooth latent space enables fast fitting to additional observations by optimizing only the avatar code.

## Results

On the single-image 3D head avatar creation task (Ava256, π_target ≠ π_drive), FlexAvatar achieves PSNR 16.9, SSIM 0.762, LPIPS 0.265, and CSIM 0.695, substantially outperforming the best baseline LAM (PSNR 13.1, LPIPS 0.399, CSIM 0.411). In the few-shot setting (4 images), FlexAvatar reaches PSNR 21.1, LPIPS 0.218, and CSIM 0.755, surpassing Avat3r (PSNR 20.8, LPIPS 0.310, CSIM 0.616). On the NeRSemble monocular benchmark, FlexAvatar attains PSNR 20.9, LPIPS 0.156, and CSIM 0.827, outperforming all 7 baselines including RGBAvatar (PSNR 20.6, LPIPS 0.181) and CAP4D (PSNR 19.8, LPIPS 0.185).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexAvatar wins on nearly all metrics across all four evaluation tasks. In single-image avatar creation (Ava256), it outperforms LAM by +3.8 PSNR and −0.134 LPIPS, GAGAvatar by +4.2 PSNR, and Portrait4D-v2 by +5.0 PSNR, with the largest gap in identity preservation (CSIM 0.695 vs. 0.411 for LAM). In few-shot creation, FlexAvatar beats Avat3r (the closest competitor) by +0.3 PSNR, −0.092 LPIPS, and +0.139 CSIM, while InvertAvatar and GPAvatar are far behind. On the NeRSemble monocular benchmark, FlexAvatar surpasses RGBAvatar (the strongest baseline) by +0.3 PSNR and −0.025 LPIPS, and outperforms CAP4D by +1.1 PSNR while fitting in ~10 minutes vs. CAP4D's ~4 hours. The only metric where FlexAvatar does not lead is AKD in the 3D Portrait Animation task (VFHQ), where LAM achieves 2.059 vs. FlexAvatar's 2.965, though FlexAvatar still wins on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CSIM, AED, and APD in that setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexAvatar builds on the articulated 3D Gaussian representation from 3DGS [21] and the cross-attention animation modeling from Avat3r [25], but departs from the dominant paradigm of rigging Gaussians to a FLAME mesh by learning facial expressions directly from data. Its central conceptual contribution—learnable bias sinks that explicitly separate monocular and multi-view training behaviors—addresses a failure mode (entanglement of driving signal and target viewpoint) that prior single-image avatar methods implicitly suffered from without diagnosing. The encoder-decoder transformer architecture with DINOv2 features and a StyleGAN2-PixelShuffle upsampler positions the work at the intersection of recent transformer-based portrait animation and high-fidelity 3D Gaussian rendering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bias sinks mechanism offers a general recipe for combining scarce multi-view supervision with abundant monocular data, a problem that extends well beyond head avatars to any 3D reconstruction task with partial supervision. The smooth latent avatar space enables practical flexibility (few-shot fitting in ~7 minutes, monocular video fitting in ~10 minutes) without per-identity optimization of the full network. Key limitations remain: lighting is baked from the input image, and despite the model-agnostic design, all experiments still rely on FLAME expression codes, capping fine-grained expressiveness (e.g., tongue). Whether the bias-sinks idea transfers to other modalities (full body, dynamic scenes) or other data-mixing regimes is an open question the authors flag as promising future work.
