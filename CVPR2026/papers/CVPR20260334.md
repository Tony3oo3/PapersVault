## Problem

Existing depth-preserving normal integration methods operate on regular grids, making them unable to handle scattered oriented points—an increasingly common representation in data-rich 3D vision. Depth discontinuities introduce spatial irregularities that grid-based numerical differentiation cannot capture, and no unified formulation exists that jointly parameterizes surface geometry and pairwise point correlations on unstructured domains while explicitly modeling discontinuities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260334/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of oriented 3D points (position + unit surface normal) sampled from a semi-differentiable surface, which may lie on a regular grid or be scattered (e.g., via a Halton quasi-random sampler retaining ~20% of grid points). Graph connectivity is established offline via K-nearest neighbors on x-y coordinates. The output is per-point depth (z in orthographic or log-depth d in perspective projection) along with estimated per-point surface normals, recovered by minimizing projected point-to-plane distances across the graph. The same formulation handles both projection types without mode switching.

## Method

The paper formulates normal integration as a weighted least-squares problem on a directed graph, where each edge encodes a projected point-to-plane distance between two connected points and edge weights capture depth discontinuities. Edge weights are inferred via a two-component Gaussian mixture model over per-point triplets (two edges sharing a center vertex), where the center point is treated as a sample jointly generated by its two neighbors; the posterior probability of each component yields the edge weight through a sigmoid of the difference in squared distances. Optimization proceeds via variational inference with alternating steps: (1) depth estimation via conjugate gradient on the weighted incidence system, and (2) shape inference by estimating the gradient field to update normals and recompute weights. An importance sampling scheme selects triplets to ensure at least one neighbor is compatible (avoiding fully detached configurations), and a location-adaptive precision variable k, driven by cosine distance between ground-truth and estimated normals, modulates sensitivity to discontinuities. This generalizes BiNI by removing axis-alignment constraints, operating on geometry-based rather than depth-based distances, and adapting precision per-point.

## Results

On DiLiGenT-MV grid data (front view), GNI achieves MADE errors of 0.60 (bear), 1.91 (buddha), 5.62 (cow), 0.59 (pot), and 2.42 (reading), outperforming BiNI on 4 of 5 models. On side-view grid data, GNI reduces errors to 1.24, 4.04, 0.36, 7.2, and 2.87 respectively, again beating BiNI on 4 of 5 models. On the perspective DiLiGenT dataset, GNI matches or beats BiNI on 8 of 9 models (e.g., 0.47 vs 0.49 on bear, 2.83 vs 3.34 on harvest). The method also generalizes to scattered oriented points, where triplet sampling reduces MADE by up to 48% (e.g., cow front: 18.72 with triplets vs 35.70 without).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BiNI (the closest competitor and state-of-the-art), GNI wins on 4 of 5 models in both front and side grid views, with the largest margin on 'reading' (2.42 vs 14.73 MADE, front) and 'cow' (0.36 vs 9.84, side). GNI loses to BiNI on 'cow' in front view (5.62 vs 0.65) and on 'buddha' in side view (4.04 vs 3.81), which the authors attribute to self-occlusion ambiguities outside the problem scope. Against IPF, GNI is dramatically superior on all models (e.g., 0.60 vs 24.29 on bear front; 0.36 vs 38.66 on cow side). Against MD (mesh decimation baseline), GNI dominates especially in side view where MD errors exceed 13–42 MADE while GNI stays below 7.2. On the perspective DiLiGenT dataset (Table 5), GNI and BiNI are closely matched, with GNI winning on 8 of 9 models and the sole loss on 'goblet' (9.75 vs 8.51) attributed to prominent self-occlusions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Bilateral Normal Integration (BiNI, Cao et al., ECCV 2022) from its axis-aligned, grid-based formulation to a unified graph-based framework that operates on scattered oriented points. The lineage traces back to classical PDE-based normal integration on regular grids, through dynamic weighting schemes, to BiNI's semi-differentiable connectivity pattern; the key departure is replacing per-pixel axis-aligned comparators with a directed weighted graph where edges encode geometric compatibility via projected point-to-plane distance. The variational inference framework with two-component Gaussian mixtures over sampled triplets is the novel architectural element, subsuming BiNI as a special case with fixed priors, axis-aligned triplets, and non-adaptive precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform depth-preserving normal integration on unstructured, scattered oriented points addresses a practical gap as point-based representations become increasingly common in data-rich 3D vision pipelines. The unified graph formulation means a single codebase handles both grid and scattered inputs without mode switching, which simplifies deployment. However, the method's reliance on uniform sampling (demonstrated to fail on non-uniformly distributed points like 'horse' and 'torso') and its inability to resolve self-occlusion-induced structural ambiguities limit its applicability to real-world data. Open questions include adaptive triplet sampling for non-uniform distributions and whether the variational inference framework can be extended to multi-view or learned normal inputs.
