## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models lack an efficient mechanism for early quality assessment, forcing workflows like prompt optimization, seed selection, and RL-based training to complete full denoising before evaluating output quality. Existing approaches either rely on expensive external VLMs (e.g., ICEdit's 72B decoder) or require human interpretation of attention maps (e.g., PromptCharm), leaving no lightweight, automated, model-agnostic probe that can predict final image quality from early-stage internal signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260968/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The probe consumes per-token cross-attention spatial maps extracted at a single early denoising step (default t=5 out of 25) from a fixed set of middle-stage blocks (final 10 encoder blocks for UNet models like SDXL; 10 consecutive middle DiT blocks for FLUX/Qwen-Image), concatenated with a learned timestep embedding. It outputs a single scalar quality score trained to match a chosen ground-truth metric (ImageReward by default, but generalizable to aesthetic score, CLIP score, etc.). The probe operates at 1024×1024 or 512×512 resolution and requires no modification to the base diffusion model, functioning as a plug-and-play module.

## Method

The core insight is that cross-attention maps at early denoising steps (t≈5) already encode a strong signal about final image quality: focused, stable attention on semantically salient tokens predicts successful generation, while diffuse, fragmented attention predicts object omission or semantic failure. The Diffusion Probe is a lightweight CNN composed of stacked DownBlocks with residual connections followed by an OutputLayer (normalization, global pooling, convolutions) that maps the concatenated attention maps and timestep embedding to a scalar prediction. It is trained end-to-end via supervised MSE regression against a pre-trained reward model's score (e.g., ImageReward) on 15,000 MSCOCO prompts, with oversampling of low-score instances to handle class imbalance. The architecture is deliberately model-agnostic—targeting the same conceptual 'middle encoding stage' across UNet and DiT backbones—and achieves SRCC > 0.7 and AUC-ROC > 0.9 across SDXL, FLUX.1, and Qwen-Image without any fine-tuning of the base model.

## Results

On the core probe accuracy task (Table 1), Diffusion Probe achieves peak SRCC of 0.79, AUC-ROC of 0.91, and PCC of 0.78 on FLUX at step 10, with comparable performance on SDXL (SRCC 0.76, AUC 0.89) and Qwen-Image (SRCC 0.72, AUC 0.87). In downstream Prompt Optimization on SDXL, the probe lifts CLIP Score from 28.31 (baseline) to 30.24 and Aesthetic Score from 5.13 to 5.29, approaching the heavier LLM-based method (30.80 CLIP, 5.34 Aesthetic). For Seed Selection on FLUX, the probe improves ImageReward from 1.02 to 1.06 and Aesthetic Score from 5.67 to 5.79 over random selection, while in Flow-GRPO training it enriches batches with 2.5× more high-quality samples for faster convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Diffusion Probe against two baselines: a no-optimization baseline (random seed or unmodified prompt) and an LLM-based prompt rewriting method. In Prompt Optimization on SDXL, Diffusion Probe (CLIP 30.24, Aesthetic 5.29) substantially outperforms the baseline (28.31, 5.13) and reaches within ~2% of the LLM-based method (30.80, 5.34) while being far lighter in computation. In Seed Selection on FLUX, the probe (ImageReward 1.06, Aesthetic 5.79) clearly beats random selection (1.02, 5.67) with a modest but consistent gain across all three metrics. On FLUX Prompt Optimization, the LLM baseline (33.11 CLIP) edges out the probe slightly, but the probe's advantage lies in its plug-and-play nature and negligible overhead compared to invoking an external LLM. Overall, Diffusion Probe matches or approaches LLM-based quality gains while eliminating the need for full image generation or expensive external model calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM probing paradigm (Alain & Bengio, Hewitt & Manning) to text-to-image diffusion models, repurposing the idea of training a lightweight classifier on internal model states to predict a downstream property. It departs from prior cross-attention work in diffusion models (DAAM, Attend-and-Excite, SAG), which are interventional—actively modifying attention during generation—by instead treating attention as a read-only diagnostic signal. It also contrasts with ICEDIT, which predicts quality by decoding an early latent and evaluating it with a 72B VLM, and PromptCharm, which relies on human interpretation of attention visualizations; Diffusion Probe replaces both with a small CNN trained on raw attention statistics, eliminating the need for expensive external models or manual analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by requiring only 5 of 25 denoising steps, the probe can gate or rank candidates at roughly 20% of the full generation cost, directly accelerating prompt optimization, seed selection, and RL-based training loops that currently demand full rollouts. The cross-architecture generalization (UNet-based SDXL and DiT-based FLUX/Qwen-Image) suggests the signal is a property of the diffusion process rather than a specific backbone. Open questions include whether the probe transfers to video generation, whether it can provide per-object or per-attribute quality signals rather than a single scalar, and how performance degrades on highly complex multi-object prompts beyond MSCOCO captions.
