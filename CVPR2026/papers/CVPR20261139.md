## Problem

Existing agentic text-to-image methods rely on expensive handcrafted priors or costly human-annotated datasets to acquire tool-usage knowledge, employ rigid single-path decision mechanisms that invoke only one T2I model per generation, and largely ignore inference efficiency. This paper reformulates agentic T2I as a joint optimization of generation quality and inference efficiency, seeking a router that adaptively selects the cheapest tool meeting a user-specified quality threshold.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261139/cropped_page0_idx7.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a single generated image as output, selected from up to R=4 rounds of generation across a library of five T2I models (Flow-GRPO, SDXL-Turbo, SD Turbo, SANA-1.5, SANA-Sprint). At each round, an MLLM-based evaluator (NVILA-Lite-2B-Verifier) scores the candidate image against the prompt via softmaxed yes/no logits, and the loop terminates when the score exceeds a configurable quality threshold θ (default 0.8) or the round limit is reached, returning the highest-scoring image.

## Method

OctoT2I implements a stateful, multi-round router where an LLM-based decision policy (Qwen2-0.5B, distilled from GPT-4.1) performs a 'filter-then-select' reasoning step: it estimates quality and cost for each tool using a long-term Knowledge Module and a short-term Memory Module recording prior-round outcomes, filters tools whose estimated quality meets θ, and selects the minimum-cost candidate. The Knowledge Module is built entirely from scratch via a Self-Evolving Mechanism: the agent first autonomously defines N_D=7 orthogonal conceptual dimensions (e.g., color, position, counting), then iteratively executes a Propose-Solve-Evaluate-Learn (PSEL) loop over dimension combinations, generating prompts, running them through each tool, scoring results with Pass@1, and consolidating findings into hierarchical tool profiles and prompt-level exploration records. An Exploration Space Pruning strategy enforces a recursive prerequisite principle—complex dimension combinations are explored only after all simpler sub-combinations are mastered—reducing explored prompts by 70.9% while preserving identical performance. This self-interaction-based knowledge acquisition eliminates the need for handcrafted priors or supervised fine-tuning, and the resulting evidence-based routing achieves 0.96 on GenEval with a 90.3% inference speedup over Flow-GRPO.

## Results

On GenEval, OctoT2I achieves an overall score of 0.96, surpassing the strongest non-agentic baseline Flow-GRPO (0.93) and all prior agentic methods (Idea2Img 0.67, GenArtist 0.49, ChatGen 0.44). On T2I-CompBench++, it reaches an average of 0.6618, leading Flow-GRPO (0.6332) and SANA-1.5 (0.5874). In terms of efficiency, OctoT2I delivers a 90.3% inference speedup (10.02s vs. 19.07s) and a 56.6% energy-efficiency gain (1.29 kWh·PUE vs. 2.02) over Flow-GRPO, while also achieving the lowest CO₂e (559.50 g) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctoT2I outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest non-agentic method Flow-GRPO, it gains +0.03 on GenEval (0.96 vs. 0.93) and +0.0286 on T2I-CompBench++ (0.6618 vs. 0.6332), while running nearly 2× faster (10.02s vs. 19.07s). Among agentic baselines, the margin is even larger: OctoT2I beats Idea2Img by +0.29 on GenEval (0.96 vs. 0.67), GenArtist by +0.47 (0.96 vs. 0.49), and ChatGen by +0.52 (0.96 vs. 0.44). On T2I-CompBench++, it leads SANA-1.5 (0.6618 vs. 0.5874), DALLE 3 (0.6618 vs. 0.5734), and SD3.5-M (0.6618 vs. 0.5499) by 0.07–0.11 points. On the WISE generalization benchmark, OctoT2I (0.54) edges out BAGEL (0.52) and SD3.5-L (0.46). In the user study with 30 real-world prompts, OctoT2I receives a 70.4% voting rate versus ChatGen's 29.6%, while being ~3× faster (18.45s vs. 53.34s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established T2I lines—large-scale high-quality models (Flow-GRPO, FLUX, SD3) and lightweight fast models (SD-Turbo, SANA)—and the emerging agentic T2I paradigm (GenArtist, ChatGen, DiffAgent, Idea2Img). It departs from prior agentic methods on three axes: replacing handcrafted tool descriptions or costly human-annotated SFT with a self-evolving PSEL loop that builds knowledge autonomously; replacing static single-path tool selection with a stateful multi-round 'reason-act-reflect' router; and explicitly co-optimizing generation quality against inference cost rather than treating efficiency as an afterthought. Conceptually, it repurposes the LLM-as-controller idea from general tool-use agents (ToolLLM, GPT4Tools) into a domain-specific routing problem with a novel knowledge-acquisition mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The co-optimization framing (quality threshold + minimum cost) is a practical formulation that directly addresses deployment concerns—90%+ speedup while matching or exceeding the best single-model quality is compelling for interactive and resource-constrained settings. The self-evolving mechanism's ability to integrate new tools with only ~60 exploration prompts (Table 8) suggests a scalable path as the T2I model landscape continues to fragment. Open questions include: how the approach behaves with much larger toolsets (beyond 5–7 models), whether the VQA-style binary evaluation adequately captures all quality dimensions for routing decisions, and whether the self-evolved knowledge transfers across fundamentally different model architectures (e.g., autoregressive vs. diffusion). The paper also notes future extension to image editing and 3D generation.
