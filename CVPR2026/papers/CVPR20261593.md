## Problem

In PTM-based class-incremental learning, task-specific feature subspaces become increasingly entangled as tasks accumulate, yet existing prompt-based and adapter-based methods lack an explicit, learnable mechanism to quantify sample-to-task relevance and route knowledge accordingly. Both families rely on shallow similarity or hand-crafted heuristics for routing and fusion, which degrade under overlapping subspaces and task-agnostic inference, leading to catastrophic forgetting and biased predictions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a frozen ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone with the first-task adapter (bottleneck dimension 64) to produce a high-level feature vector. Task representations are derived by stacking per-task adapter parameters and applying truncated SVD (rank 12) to obtain compact task-level vectors. The output is a single class label from a unified classifier over all classes seen so far, with the quantum gate producing per-task relevance coefficients that weight adapter fusion at inference time.

## Method

QKD introduces a Quantum-Gated Task Modulation (QGTM) module that encodes the normalized sample feature and each task's SVD-reduced representation into a q-qubit Hilbert space via a parameterized quantum circuit composed of angle encoding, learnable variational rotations, and CNOT entangling chains. Fidelity between the sample quantum state and each task state yields raw relevance scores, which are sharpened by a temperature-controlled softmax into sample-to-task coefficients. These coefficients drive a Task-Interaction Knowledge Distillation (TIKD) loss that weights KL-divergence between historical adapter logits and the new adapter's logits, enabling selective knowledge absorption from relevant tasks while suppressing interference from unrelated ones. The same quantum-derived coefficients are reused at inference for adaptive adapter fusion under task-agnostic evaluation, and an L1 sparsity regularizer encourages the gate to focus on the most relevant historical adapters. Only the current adapter and quantum gating network are updated per task, while all previously trained adapters remain frozen.

## Results

QKD achieves state-of-the-art average and final accuracy across all five exemplar-free CIL benchmarks using a ViT-B/16-IN21K backbone. On CIFAR-100 (B0-Inc10), QKD reaches 94.08% average and 90.20% final accuracy, surpassing the previous best MoTE (93.06%/88.98%) by +1.02/+1.22. The largest gains appear on ImageNet-A (B0-Inc10) with +2.77/+6.56 over MoTE, and on VTAB (B0-Inc10) with +2.25/+6.16, indicating that quantum-gated task interaction is most beneficial when task subspaces overlap heavily. QKD also outperforms exemplar-based methods (iCaRL, DER, FOSTER, MEMO) by 1.9–11.7% final accuracy while storing zero exemplars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QKD outperforms all 10 compared baselines on every dataset and metric. Among prompt-based methods, CODA-Prompt [28] is the strongest (91.44/86.70 on CIFAR), yet QKD exceeds it by +2.64/+3.50; L2P [37] and DualPrompt [36] trail further behind, confirming that local-similarity-driven prompt retrieval is insufficient under subspace overlap. Among adapter-based PTM-CIL methods, MoTE [16] is the closest competitor, with QKD improving final accuracy by +1.22 (CIFAR), +3.35 (CUB), +6.56 (IN-A), +2.37 (IN-R), and +6.16 (VTAB) over it. EASE [46] is competitive on IN-R (81.76 vs. 81.79 average) but falls behind on all other datasets. The SimpleCIL/Aper family [44] (Finetune, VPT-S, VPT-D, Adapter, SSF variants) is consistently outperformed, with the largest gaps on IN-A where QKD gains +6.56 final accuracy over the best Aper variant. In the exemplar-based comparison (Table 2), QKD (0 exemplars) surpasses MEMO [43] (20/class) by +1.92 on CIFAR and +1.05 on IN-R final accuracy, and beats iCaRL [26], DER [40], and FOSTER [32] by 1.9–11.7% final accuracy, demonstrating that quantum-gated task interaction eliminates the need for stored exemplars entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PTM-based CIL line of work that uses per-task adapters (MoTE, EASE, SimpleCIL) by replacing their classical routing/fusion heuristics with a parameterized quantum circuit that encodes sample and task representations into a Hilbert space and measures fidelity as a relevance score. The quantum encoding draws on fidelity quantum kernels [Schuld & Killoran, 2019] and projected quantum kernel measurement strategies [Huang et al., 2021] to avoid exponential concentration, while the distillation objective adapts standard KL-based knowledge transfer with quantum-derived adaptive weights. Conceptually, it departs from prompt-based CIL (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) and classical adapter fusion by framing task interaction as a geometric mutual-information problem in a high-dimensional quantum state space rather than a similarity search in the original feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QKD offers the first quantum-circuit-based routing mechanism for CIL, providing a principled, learnable alternative to heuristic prompt retrieval or naive adapter averaging that could generalize as task counts grow and subspaces overlap more heavily. However, the quantum circuit is entirely simulated on classical hardware, so the practical advantage over a well-tuned classical parameterized network (e.g., a small MLP or attention module) remains an open empirical question; the paper's ablation shows only modest margins (≈1–1.5 points) over the best classical controller. Open questions include whether the quantum formulation provides genuine scaling benefits at 50+ tasks, whether real quantum hardware would change the expressivity landscape, and whether the SVD-based task embedding bottleneck limits fidelity estimation for highly overlapping subspaces.
