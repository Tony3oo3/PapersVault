## Problem

Despite widespread adoption of test-time 'thinking' modes in large vision-language models, there is no systematic analysis of when explicit reasoning chains actually improve visual reasoning versus when they produce long, weakly grounded trajectories that underperform standard instruct decoding. The paper identifies that thinking benefits depend strongly on model capacity, sampling budget, and task category, with recognition-centric domains often suffering from over-long, image-ignoring chains.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a standard RGB image paired with a text question, producing a text answer preceded by a reasoning chain. It is applied during streaming autoregressive generation with generous token budgets (16,384 for Instruct, 32,768 for Thinking modes) and 10 sampled passes per item. The controller intervenes mid-generation by injecting short lookback prompts into the ongoing chain, so the output structure is a reasoning trace with embedded visual re-consultation segments rather than a monolithic chain.

## Method

The paper proposes uncertainty-guided lookback, a training-free decoding strategy built on a token-level visual sensitivity probe. Offline, the probe computes per-step perplexity contrasts under three visual contexts (real image, Gaussian noise, no image) to derive Δ_content (image-specific helpfulness) and Δ_presence (generic image-presence sensitivity); from these, it mines a pause-phrase vocabulary P (steps with high presence sensitivity but low content sensitivity) and a lookback template set L (phrases co-occurring with strongly negative Δ_content in correct traces). At inference time, a lightweight controller performs n-gram matching over the streamed tokens: whenever a pause phrase from P appears during the thinking phase, a lookback template from L is appended to force explicit re-examination of the image before reasoning continues, with at most one trigger per L-token window. An optional parallel lookback sampling step branches M short continuations after each trigger and selects the branch with the most negative aggregate Δ_content, adding only modest overhead since lookback events are rare and localized.

## Results

On MMMU_val (Pass@1), the uncertainty-guided lookback method improves overall accuracy from 67.0% to 69.7% (4B), 70.3% to 73.0% (8B), and 75.3% to 81.7% (32B) over the original Qwen3-VL Thinking models, while reducing token usage by 34–43%. The parallel-sampling variant pushes 4B to 73.0% (+6.0) and 32B to 79.2% (+3.9) with comparable token savings. Gains are largest in specialist categories: DLM improves by +5.0 to +6.3 points and EP by +4.5 to +9.3 points at 4B. Cross-benchmark results (Table 4) show consistent improvements on MMBench (+1.2 to +3.1), MMStar (+1.8 to +4.3), MathVista (+1.8 to +5.5), and MathVision (+1.8 to +5.6) across all model sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three text-only adaptive-CoT baselines (DEER, DeepConf, REFRAIN) are outperformed by the proposed uncertainty-guided lookback on overall MMMU_val Pass@1 at every model size. DEER is the weakest baseline, dropping 10–15 points below the Original Thinking model (e.g., 53.3% vs. 67.0% at 4B) because its confidence-based early exit truncates reasoning before visual grounding is established. DeepConf and REFRAIN stay within 1–4 points of the Original while saving roughly 20–27% of tokens, but neither recovers the accuracy lost to early stopping. The proposed lookback method beats the Original by +2.7 to +6.4 points on overall accuracy while using only 57–66% of the original tokens, and the parallel-sampling variant adds a further +1.2 to +3.3 points at a modest extra token cost. The largest category-specific gains appear in DLM (+4.6 to +6.3) and EP (+2.3 to +9.3), where the baselines show the smallest improvements over the Original, confirming that the lookback mechanism specifically targets the visual-grounding failures that generic early-exit signals miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-only adaptive chain-of-thought literature (DEER, DeepConf, REFRAIN) to the vision-language setting, where the core challenge is not merely when to stop reasoning but when to re-engage with the image. It builds directly on REFRAIN's step-decomposition protocol and DeepConf's uncertainty markers to construct a token-level visual sensitivity probe, but departs from all three baselines by introducing a vision-specific signal (per-token perplexity contrast between real and noise images) rather than relying on language-side confidence alone. The empirical analysis of thinking vs. instruct modes across InternVL3.5 and Qwen3-VL families is positioned as the first systematic, controlled comparison of test-time thinking in LVLMs, filling a gap the authors identify in the rapidly growing but under-analyzed 'thinking mode' literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that extended thinking chains frequently produce 'long-wrong' trajectories that ignore the image challenges the prevailing assumption that more test-time compute monotonically improves LVLM performance, and the practical implication is that adaptive, uncertainty-triggered lookback can improve accuracy while reducing token usage by 35–45%. A key limitation is the dependence on token-level log-probabilities for probe construction, which restricts applicability to closed-source models without such access. Open questions include whether the mined lookback phrases transfer across model families without re-mining, whether the approach composes with other test-time strategies (e.g., self-consistency voting), and whether the gains persist as LVLMs continue to improve their native visual grounding.
