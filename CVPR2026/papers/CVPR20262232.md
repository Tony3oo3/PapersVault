limitations: The method is evaluated on only 30 subjects from the DreamBooth dataset and two base models (FLUX, SANA), and the block selection strategy requires precomputing features across multiple LoRA-fine-tuned models, adding setup overhead. surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that naive block skipping (without residual features) causes complete personalization failure, yet adding a simple precomputed residual term restores near-LoRA quality even at 50% skip ratio—a clean demonstration that the skipped blocks' contribution is largely a fixed offset rather than a dynamic transformation. ## Problem

Fine-tuning large DiT-based text-to-image models (e.g., FLUX) for subject personalization demands substantial GPU memory due to full backpropagation through deep transformer stacks, making on-device deployment impractical. Existing memory-efficient fine-tuning methods either still require full forward/backward passes (LoRA, LISA) or are architecturally tied to U-Net backbones (HollowedNet), leaving DiT-based architectures largely unaddressed for efficient personalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262232/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4–6 reference images of a target subject plus a text prompt as input. During training, high-resolution reference images are dynamically cropped at timestep-dependent patch sizes and resized to a fixed low resolution (256×256 for FLUX, 512×512 for SANA), while inference generates full 1024×1024 personalized images. The training resolution is thus substantially lower than the inference resolution, and the effective receptive field of each training sample varies with the diffusion timestep.

## Method

DiT-BlockSkip combines two memory-reduction mechanisms. First, dynamic patch sampling linearly interpolates the crop size between s_min and s_max as a function of the diffusion timestep t/T, so that noisy (high-t) steps emphasize global structure via larger crops while clean (low-t) steps emphasize fine detail via smaller crops; all crops are then resized to s_min×s_min before entering the DiT. Second, block skipping with residual feature precomputation identifies mid-level transformer blocks as critical for subject encoding via a cross-attention masking analysis (masking mid-level blocks causes the largest DINO semantic distance), then skips the first n* and last m* blocks (n*+m*=k) by precomputing their residual features Δf = f_{i+l} − f_i. During fine-tuning, only LoRA adapters on the unskipped mid-level blocks are updated, while the precomputed residuals are added to the updated activations to bypass the skipped blocks, offloading their parameters from GPU and reducing both parameter and activation memory.

## Results

On FLUX (256×256 training resolution), DiT-BlockSkip at 30% skip ratio achieves DINO 0.7194, CLIP-I 0.8036, and CLIP-T 0.3199, closely matching LoRA (0.7324 / 0.8146 / 0.3173) while using only ~58% of LoRA's training memory. On SANA (512×512), the 30% skip variant reaches DINO 0.7388 and CLIP-I 0.8043, slightly exceeding LoRA's DINO (0.7374) while remaining within 0.7% on CLIP-I. The method substantially outperforms HollowedNet on FLUX (DINO 0.7194 vs. 0.4899 at 30% skip) and maintains competitive scores at 50% skip (DINO 0.6963 on FLUX, 0.7277 on SANA), demonstrating robustness to aggressive block removal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LoRA (the strongest full-resolution baseline), DiT-BlockSkip at 30% skip matches or slightly exceeds LoRA on SANA (DINO 0.7388 vs. 0.7374) while trailing by ~1.3% on FLUX DINO (0.7194 vs. 0.7324); CLIP-T is actually higher for the proposed method on FLUX (0.3199 vs. 0.3173). Against HollowedNet, the gap is dramatic on FLUX: DiT-BlockSkip achieves DINO 0.7194 at 30% skip versus HollowedNet's 0.4899, a 47% relative improvement, because HollowedNet's empirically-driven U-Net skipping strategy removes critical mid-level DiT blocks. On SANA, both methods perform comparably at 30% skip (DINO 0.7388 vs. 0.7208), but DiT-BlockSkip degrades more gracefully at 50% (0.7277 vs. 0.6301). LISA and LoRA-FA, designed for LLMs, show severe degradation on SANA (DINO 0.5226 and 0.4692 respectively) while performing competitively on the larger FLUX model, confirming their architecture-specific limitations. DreamBooth (full fine-tuning) achieves slightly lower CLIP-T than LoRA on FLUX (0.3073 vs. 0.3173), indicating text-adherence loss from full parameter updates, whereas DiT-BlockSkip preserves text fidelity at 0.3199. A user study (Table 3) further confirms that 45.6% of raters prefer the proposed method's text fidelity over LoRA, while subject fidelity is a near-tie (37.7% vs. 40.0%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the memory-efficient fine-tuning line of work (HollowedNet, LISA, LoRA-FA, ZOODiP) from U-Net-based diffusion models to DiT-based architectures (FLUX, SANA), where prior methods either do not apply or degrade significantly. It repurposes inference-time layer-skipping and feature-caching ideas (DeepCache, Delta-DiT) into a training-time block-skipping mechanism, and introduces a novel spatial strategy—dynamic patch sampling tied to diffusion timesteps—that has no direct analogue in prior PEFT literature. The cross-attention masking analysis to identify critical blocks for personalization is a new diagnostic tool for DiTs, filling a gap left by the less interpretable constant-resolution architecture compared to U-Net's hierarchical downsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is enabling on-device personalization of large DiT models (e.g., FLUX) by reducing training memory to ~58% of LoRA while preserving subject and text fidelity, a step toward smartphone/IOT deployment of generative AI. The finding that mid-level transformer blocks encode subject-specific information while early and late blocks are largely dispensable for personalization provides a useful architectural insight for future DiT design and pruning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video DiTs, whether larger skip ratios (>50%) remain viable, and how the method interacts with emerging quantization or distillation strategies for further compression.
