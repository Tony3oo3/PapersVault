## Problem

Monocular 3D human motion capture under occlusion remains unsolved: image-based methods lack temporal constraints, while video-based methods relying on time-domain motion priors produce oversmoothed motions under long-term occlusions. The paper identifies that occluded body parts retain periodic patterns and consistent momentum, but existing frequency-domain approaches (DCT, phase-based) either discard local detail or fail on non-stationary signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an occluded RGB video and 2D keypoints with confidence scores from ViTPose as input. Keypoints are partitioned into reliable (high-confidence, used directly) and unreliable (low-confidence, modeled as Gaussian distributions N(p, I)) before entering the diffusion pipeline. The output is a full SMPL parameter sequence (pose θ∈R^144, translation τ∈R^3, shape β∈R^10) per frame, from which 3D joints and mesh vertices are derived; the 3D joints are reprojected to 2D to close the diffusion loop.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-branch frequency-domain denoising prior built on Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) along both spatial and temporal axes, which decomposes the 2D keypoint sequence into four subbands (LL, HL, LH, HH). A transformer-based network predicts a per-subband coefficient map that acts as a learned selection mask (Hadamard product), removing occlusion-induced noise while preserving local periodicity and momentum. A modified diffusion process diffuses only the unreliable keypoints toward their initial Gaussian distributions rather than toward standard Gaussian, so the model learns to refine uncertainty using the reliable visible keypoints as anchors. After training the 2D prior, its encoder is frozen and a 3D transformer decoder lifts the wavelet subbands to 3D motion conditioned on ViT image features, trained with the same diffusion schedule via a joint reprojection loss. The approach is further supported by OcMotion, the first video-based 3D occluded motion dataset (43 sequences, 6 views, 300K+ frames with SMPL annotations).

## Results

On the proposed OcMotion dataset, the method (trained without OcMotion data) achieves 79.2 MPJPE, 51.7 PA-MPJPE, and 20.1 Accel, outperforming the strongest baselines GVHMR (80.6/51.6/20.5) and ScoreHMR (81.1/53.1/24.6). On 3DPW-OC, it reaches 63.1 MPJPE and 9.1 Accel, beating ScoreHMR (65.9/10.3) and HMR2.0 (71.0/15.8). On Hi4D with severe inter-person occlusions, the method achieves 61.5 MPJPE and 15.6 Accel, surpassing CloseInt (63.1/19.9) and Human4D (72.1/21.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all video-based baselines on OcMotion: it beats GVHMR by 1.4 MPJPE and 0.4 Accel, ScoreHMR by 1.9 MPJPE and 4.5 Accel, and PhaseMP by 18.6 MPJPE. On 3DPW-OC, it surpasses ScoreHMR (65.9→63.1 MPJPE, 10.3→9.1 Accel) and HMR2.0 (71.0→63.1 MPJPE), with the largest gap over TCMR (90.3→63.1 MPJPE). On Hi4D, it achieves the best MPJPE (61.5) and Accel (15.6), beating CloseInt by 1.6 MPJPE and 4.3 Accel, and Human4D by 10.6 MPJPE. Notably, the method is evaluated without training on OcMotion, yet still outperforms methods that do, suggesting strong generalization. Image-based occlusion-aware methods (OOH, JOTR, DPMesh) are consistently outperformed on 3DPW-OC, confirming the advantage of temporal frequency-domain modeling over single-frame approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three research threads: occlusion-aware 3D pose estimation (OOH, JOTR, DPMesh), frequency-domain motion representations (DCT-based PoseFormerV2, phase-based PhaseMP/DeepPhase, wavelet-based MotionWavelet), and diffusion models for human recovery (ScoreHMR, DiffPose). Its key departure is formulating occluded motion capture as a wavelet coefficient *selection* process in the frequency domain rather than direct regression or time-domain temporal smoothing, motivated by the observation that occlusion is a local phenomenon better captured by DWT's multi-scale analysis than by DCT's global decomposition or phase-based stationary assumptions. The two-stage design—first learning a 2D frequency-domain denoising prior, then using its frozen encoder to guide a 3D decoder—repurposes the diffusion framework to bridge 2D observations and 3D lifting in a way prior methods (which either regress directly or optimize per-frame) do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of OcMotion, the first video-based 3D occluded motion dataset with complete SMPL annotations across 43 sequences and 300K frames, fills a critical gap that has limited progress on occlusion-robust video methods. The frequency-domain denoising prior offers a principled alternative to time-domain temporal consistency, which the paper shows leads to oversmoothing under long-term occlusion. However, the method remains dependent on 2D keypoint detection quality (high-confidence but incorrect predictions still propagate errors), and the training data lack challenging poses, limiting generalization. Open questions include whether the wavelet selection formulation extends to multi-person occlusion, whether the 2D prior can be replaced by end-to-end learning, and whether the approach scales to higher-fidelity motion (e.g., hand or facial articulation).
