## Problem

Online data curation—dynamically selecting training samples based on the model's evolving state—has proven effective in classification and multimodal learning but remains largely unexplored for object detection. Two fundamental barriers prevent direct transfer: (1) a single detection image contains a variable number of instances with heterogeneous quality, making per-image scoring ill-defined, and (2) detection losses are fragmented across classification, localization, and centerness terms and destabilized by stochastic proposal sampling and assignment rules, rendering loss-based learnability signals unreliable.

## Input / Output

At each training iteration, the method consumes a super-batch of 80 annotated RGB images (COCO-format bounding boxes with class labels) and runs forward passes through both a frozen pretrained teacher and the current student detector. From the per-image predicted boxes (scores, classes, IoUs with ground truth), it computes a scalar DetGain score for each model, then selects the top 20% of images by the teacher-student DetGain gap to form the 16-image sub-batch for backpropagation. The output is thus a filtered mini-batch plus per-image marginal-mAP contribution scores; no model weights, loss functions, or training schedules are modified.

## Method

DetGain estimates each image's marginal perturbation to dataset-level mAP by modeling how inserting its true/false-positive detections deforms the global precision-recall curve. Rather than relying on unstable per-sample losses, it derives analytic closed-form expressions for the AP change of a single TP or FP insertion under a uniform (Beta(1,1)) prior on confidence scores, yielding O(1) cost per detection and O(m) per image. Learnability is defined as the teacher-minus-student DetGain gap: images where the teacher contributes more to global mAP than the student are prioritized, capturing residual knowledge in a metric-aligned, architecture-agnostic way. To prevent overfitting from repeatedly sampling high-learnability images, the method pairs DetGain scoring with strong online data augmentation (color jitter, affine transforms, copy-paste), which expands the sampling space before subsampling and allows the selector to filter low-quality augmentations. The entire pipeline is plug-and-play, modifying only the data-loading stage without altering model architecture, loss functions, optimizers, or schedules.

## Results

On COCO val2017, DetGain-based online curation yields an average improvement of ~+2.0 mAP across six representative detectors (Faster R-CNN, ATSS, FCOS, GFL, VFNet, Deformable DETR), with the largest gain of +2.8 mAP on FCOS (38.2 → 41.0) and +2.5 mAP on Faster R-CNN (37.5 → 40.0). In the head-to-head comparison against seven alternative sampling metrics (Table 4), DetGain achieves 40.0 / 40.9 / 41.6 AP on Faster R-CNN / FCOS / ATSS respectively, outperforming the strongest baseline (loss-learnability) by +1.1 to +1.2 AP. Under noisy annotations, DetGain gains up to +6.9 mAP over the uniform baseline, and combining DetGain with CrossKD pushes FCOS-Res50 to 42.2 AP (+3.7 over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetGain consistently outperforms all seven compared sampling metrics across all three detectors. Against the strongest competitor, loss-learnability (RHO-LOSS / JEST [13, 36]), DetGain gains +1.1 AP on Faster R-CNN (40.0 vs 38.9), +2.8 AP on FCOS (40.9 vs 38.1), and +1.2 AP on ATSS (41.6 vs 40.4). The image-wise AP metric [58] is the second-best baseline, trailing DetGain by 1.7–1.6 AP. Loss-based hard mining [33] and entropy-based criteria [35] perform substantially worse, with hard mining actually degrading FCOS by 3.7 AP below the uniform baseline (34.5 vs 38.2), illustrating the instability of loss signals in detection. GradNorm [21] is nearly indistinguishable from the uniform baseline, confirming that gradient-magnitude signals carry little useful information for image-level selection in detection. DetGain's advantage is most pronounced on FCOS, where its AP-aligned scoring avoids the architecture-specific loss weighting issues that plague loss-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student learnability framework from RHO-LOSS (Mindermann et al., 2022) and JEST (Evans et al., 2024) to object detection by replacing the unstable loss-gap signal with a metric-aware DetGain score aligned to dataset-level mAP. It also generalizes the offline, image-level AP-based selection of Zhou et al. (2024) into an online, global-mAP marginal contribution estimator with a closed-form uniform-prior approximation. The key conceptual departure is decoupling the data-selection signal from architecture-specific loss internals (RPN sampling, Hungarian matching, multi-task weighting), making the method plug-and-play across one-stage, two-stage, and transformer-based detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DetGain provides the first consistently effective online data curation strategy for object detection, achieving ~+2.0 mAP across six diverse architectures without modifying model code, loss functions, or training schedules. Its robustness under noisy annotations (up to +6.9 mAP) and complementarity with knowledge distillation (+3.7 mAP combined) suggest it can serve as a general, low-cost layer atop existing detection pipelines. Open questions include whether the uniform-prior approximation degrades on datasets with highly skewed score distributions, whether the added forward-pass overhead scales to very large super-batches, and whether the approach transfers to other structured-prediction tasks (segmentation, keypoint detection) with analogous ranking-based metrics.
