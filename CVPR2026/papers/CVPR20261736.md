## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generators serialize faces into sequences and train on truncated windows, but their tokenization schemes (coordinate-ordered, depth-first, or patch-based) do not guarantee that structurally relevant neighboring faces fall within the truncated context. This topology-context mismatch forces the model to predict connectivity without seeing the correct local neighborhood, producing broken surfaces, holes, and fragmented components that rolling KV caches at inference cannot fix.

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (16,384 points randomly sampled from 40,960) as geometric conditioning, encoded by a Michelangelo encoder and injected via cross-attention; for image-conditioned generation, a two-stage pipeline first synthesizes a mesh via TRELLIS and extracts a point cloud. Vertex coordinates are quantized into 256 bins per axis, and the autoregressive input window is 1,024 faces while the full generated mesh can reach up to 20,000 faces. The output is a sequence of quantized triangle-face tokens that directly form an editable, artist-style 3D triangle mesh.

## Method

MeshRipple introduces Ripple Tokenization, a half-edge BFS traversal that maintains an explicit FIFO frontier queue and records a root pointer for each new face, ensuring the structurally relevant context is concentrated at the tail of the sequence so truncated windows reliably capture it. The autoregressive transformer uses an hourglass backbone to compress coordinate tokens into face-level embeddings, then applies a Frontier Attention mask that restricts each query to attend only to active frontier faces, and a Native Sparse Contextual Attention (NSCA) module that causally retrieves long-range cues from the full mesh history via compressed block summaries and top-k block selection. An Expansive Prediction scheme jointly predicts the next face attached to the current root and the offset to the next root along the frontier, enforcing connected surface growth. Training uses standard next-token cross-entropy plus a root-offset pointer loss over 1,024-face windows, with the model trained on ~300k meshes from ObjaverseXL, G-Objaverse, ShapeNet, Toys4K, and 3D-FUTURE.

## Results

On the Artist Mesh benchmark, MeshRipple achieves the best scores across all three metrics: CD 46.68×10⁻³, HD 0.0938, and NC 0.5166, outperforming the strongest baselines (FastMesh at CD 47.26, BPT at NC 0.5084). On the Dense Mesh benchmark, MeshRipple attains the best CD (48.73×10⁻³) and NC (0.6280), while DeepMesh retains a slight edge on HD (0.0893 vs. 0.1057). Notably, MeshRipple achieves these results with substantially lower compute: 16 A800 GPUs for 16 days versus DeepMesh's 128 A100 GPUs for 4 days plus DPO post-training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Artist Mesh benchmark, MeshRipple wins on all three metrics: it beats FastMesh (the closest competitor) by 0.58 in CD and 0.0034 in HD, and surpasses BPT by 0.0082 in NC. On Dense Mesh, MeshRipple leads in CD (48.73 vs. DeepMesh's 50.27) and NC (0.6280 vs. BPT's 0.6066), but DeepMesh retains a narrow HD advantage (0.0893 vs. 0.1057). MeshAnythingV2 and Mesh Silksong trail significantly on both benchmarks, with CD values 10–60 points higher. FastMesh shows competitive CD on Artist Mesh (47.26) but has near-zero NC (0.0106), indicating poor normal consistency. The authors note that DeepMesh's HD edge comes at the cost of 8× more GPU resources (128 A100s for 4 days + DPO) compared to MeshRipple's 16 A800s for 16 days, making MeshRipple the more compute-efficient option overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshRipple extends the autoregressive mesh generation line (MeshGPT, MeshTron, DeepMesh, Mesh Silksong) by identifying and fixing a fundamental misalignment: prior tokenization schemes (coordinate-ordered, depth-first, patch-based) do not guarantee that structurally relevant faces fall within the truncated training window, causing broken surfaces. The paper's Ripple Tokenization (BFS with an explicit frontier queue) directly addresses this by concentrating the active growth boundary at the tail of the sequence, while the NSCA module adapts DeepSeek's Native Sparse Attention [76] to provide bounded-memory access to full mesh history. The hourglass backbone and truncated training regime are inherited from MeshTron [14] and DeepMesh [82], but the expansive prediction strategy (jointly predicting root offset and attached face) is a new decoding constraint not present in prior AR mesh generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frontier-aligned tokenization offers a principled fix for the topology-context mismatch that has plagued AR mesh generation, and the NSCA module makes 50K–100K face generation feasible on a single GPU (vs. OOM without it), which is practically important for industrial-scale asset creation. However, the quantitative gains over the strongest baseline (DeepMesh) are moderate (CD 48.73 vs. 50.27 on dense meshes), and the ablation reveals that NSCA is not strictly necessary for quality at 5K faces—it is a scaling enabler rather than a quality driver. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to quadrilateral meshes, whether RL-based preference tuning (as in DeepMesh/MeshRFT) can be combined with the frontier constraint, and how the method behaves under substantial label noise or extreme topologies, which the authors acknowledge as limitations.
