## Problem

No dedicated 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) registration framework exists, and existing point-cloud methods (ICP, FGR, REGTR) fail on 3DGS models due to their non-uniform point distributions, arbitrary SfM-derived scales, and sensitivity to initialization. The harder cross-instance case—aligning two different objects of the same category (e.g., two different cars)—remains entirely unsolved, as prior 3DGS registration work (e.g., GaussReg) only handles same-object pairs and typically requires the true scale as input.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two independently constructed feature-augmented 3DGS models, each built from SAM-masked images with COLMAP-estimated poses and 3-channel viewpoint-guided spherical-map features (from Mariotti et al. [20]) lifted into the 3D Gaussian parameters. During alignment both models are held fixed; the only optimized quantity is a single global Sim(3) transformation (scale, rotation, translation) mapping one model into the other's coordinate frame. No ground-truth scale or pose is required at test time.

## Method

GSA uses a two-step coarse-to-fine pipeline. The coarse step is an iterative feature-guided absolute-orientation solver that extends ICP in three ways: it prunes candidate correspondences using semantic-geometric feature similarity (Eq. 5) before selecting spatially closest points, it solves a hybrid closed-form Sim(3) (Kabsch-Umeyama for rotation/translation + Horn's symmetric formulation for scale) so that scale is estimated jointly rather than fixed, and this combination makes it robust to 180° misalignment and up to 10× scale gaps. The fine step generalizes the inverse radiance-field formulation from single-view camera-pose estimation over SE(3) to multi-view field-to-field registration over Sim(3): it differentiably renders the feature field of the transformed source model and the target model from N diverse target cameras and minimizes the summed L2 distance between the rendered feature maps (Eq. 8), with gradients flowing through the fixed 3DGS representation to update only the transformation. The choice of geometry-aware spherical-map features over standard DINOv2 features is critical, as the former encode spatial distinctions (left/right, front/back) that disambiguate correspondences across instances.

## Results

On same-object 3DGS registration (Objaverse, 15 objects), GSA Coarse+Fine achieves 0.36° RRE and 0.001 ATE, an order-of-magnitude improvement over the best prior method DReg-NeRF (10.37° RRE, 0.04 ATE) and GaussReg (18.60° RRE, 0.07 ATE). In the harder cross-instance setting (ShapeNet, 6 categories, 60 pairs with up to 180° rotation and 10× scale variation), GSA Coarse+Fine achieves 1.71–22.68° RRE versus 82–155° for all baselines. On real-world data (3D Real Car, CO3Dv2), GSA reduces mean RRE from ~110–126° (GaussReg) to 1.82–44.56°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSA decisively outperforms all four baselines on every metric and setting. On same-object registration, GSA Coarse+Fine (0.36° RRE) beats DReg-NeRF (10.37°) by ~29×, GaussReg (18.60°) by ~52×, FGR (51.32°) by ~142×, and REGTR (125.34°) by ~348×; even GSA's coarse stage alone (3.13°) already surpasses all prior methods. In cross-instance registration (Table 2), the gap widens dramatically: GaussReg degrades to 120–155° RRE (worse than random), FGR and REGTR remain at 77–97°, while GSA Coarse+Fine achieves 1.71–22.68° across six ShapeNet categories. On real-world data (Table 3), GaussReg's mean RRE is 109–126° while GSA Coarse+Fine achieves 1.82° (3D Real Car) to 44.56° (CO3Dv2 chair), confirming the advantage generalizes beyond synthetic settings. Notably, prior methods assume unit scale (s=1), whereas GSA estimates scale jointly, giving it an additional advantage when SfM-derived scales are arbitrary.

## Relation to Prior Work

GSA extends the ICP-based registration paradigm (Besl & McKay) by introducing feature-guided correspondences and a hybrid Kabsch-Umeyama/Horn closed-form solver that handles unknown scale, overcoming three fundamental limitations of classical ICP in the 3DGS setting. For the fine-alignment stage, the paper generalizes the inverse radiance-field formulation from iNeRF/iComMa (single-view camera pose estimation over SE(3)) to a multi-view, field-to-field registration problem over Sim(3), replacing color rendering with geometry-aware feature rendering. The feature-augmented 3DGS construction follows the lifting pipeline of Feature3DGS but substitutes DINOv2 with viewpoint-guided spherical-map features (Mariotti et al.) specifically for their superior spatial disambiguation, which the authors show is critical for alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GSA provides the first effective solution for category-level 3DGS registration, unlocking practical applications such as geometrically consistent object replacement and synchronized novel-view synthesis across instances—tasks that were previously infeasible. The main limitation is dependence on the quality of geometry-aware features; if those degrade, alignment accuracy suffers, though the authors note this is a rapidly improving subfield. An open question is how well the approach generalizes to highly diverse categories (e.g., furniture vs. vehicles) where intra-class geometric variation is extreme, and whether the multi-view feature-consistency loss can be extended to handle partial observations or occluded views more gracefully.
