## Problem

Existing VLM-based lifelong person re-identification methods rely on global-aware learning with coarse supervision, which misguides the model to attend to background regions rather than person-specific semantics. This limits fine-grained attribute knowledge acquisition and accelerates catastrophic forgetting across sequential domains, as the model lacks a mechanism to explicitly transfer and reinforce human-relevant attribute knowledge over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263309/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes person images resized to 256×128 with identity labels from a sequential stream of domains in an exemplar-free setting (previous domain data is inaccessible). During training, BLIP-2 generates part-wise local text descriptions (head, upper body, lower body, foot) and CLIP with learnable prompts produces global ReID-aware text attributes. At inference, the output is a concatenated feature vector combining the ViT [CLS] global feature and N_a local visual attribute features decoded via cross-attention, used for cosine-similarity-based person retrieval.

## Method

VLADR comprises two stages: Multi-grain Text Attribute Disentanglement (MTAD) and Inter-domain Cross-modal Attribute Reinforcement (ICAR). MTAD uses BLIP-2 with predefined region-specific queries to extract four local text attributes per image, while CLIP with per-identity learnable text prompts (optimized via contrastive loss with frozen encoders) yields a global ReID-aware text attribute. ICAR then performs three learning branches: (1) standard ReID loss plus cross-modal global visual-text alignment; (2) fine-grained local attribute mining where N_a learnable attribute queries attend over ViT image tokens via a cross-attention decoder, and the resulting visual local features are aligned with CLIP-encoded text local features through a symmetric contrastive loss; (3) inter-domain attribute knowledge transfer that computes row-normalized cross-instance relation matrices from both the current model and a frozen previous-domain model, then enforces KL-divergence-based relation distillation per attribute to preserve and transfer historical attribute structure. The overall loss combines all four terms with equal weighting (α=β=1.0), and the frozen CLIP encoders are retained throughout all training steps to preserve pretrained knowledge.

## Results

On the standard LReID benchmark (Training Order-1, CLIP backbone), VLADR achieves 77.8% Seen-Avg mAP and 72.0% Seen-Avg R@1, and 72.0% Unseen-Avg mAP and 72.0% Unseen-Avg R@1. Compared to the strongest CLIP-based baseline DASK†, VLADR improves Seen-Avg mAP/R@1 by 2.2%/1.9% (Order-1) and 2.1%/1.9% (Order-2), and Unseen-Avg mAP/R@1 by 2.1%/2.5% (Order-2). Against ResNet- and ViT-based methods, the gains are substantially larger (15.9%/15.5% in Seen-Avg mAP/R@1 over the best ResNet method). Ablation confirms that the multi-grain attribute alignment loss (L_MAlign) contributes +9.2% Seen-Avg mAP and the inter-domain alignment loss (L_DAlign) adds a further +4.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLADR is compared against 8 baselines spanning three backbone families (ResNet, ViT, CLIP). Among CLIP-based methods, VLADR outperforms the strongest baseline DASK† by 2.2%/1.9% in Seen-Avg mAP/R@1 (Order-1) and 2.1%/1.9% (Order-2), and by 2.1%/2.5% in Unseen-Avg mAP/R@1 (Order-2). Against PAEMA† (the next-best CLIP method), VLADR gains approximately 10.2% in Seen-Avg mAP. Compared to ResNet-based methods (LwF, DKP, LSTKC, DKP++, LSTKC++, DASK), VLADR achieves 15.9%/15.5% improvements in Seen-Avg mAP/R@1, and over ViT-based methods (PAEMA, DRE) the margin is 10.1%/9.1%. The largest individual-domain gains appear on MSMT17 (45.5 vs. 42.1 mAP over DASK†) and LPW (67.8 vs. 64.7 mAP), indicating that attribute disentanglement is particularly effective on challenging, low-resolution domains. VLADR also demonstrates superior unseen-domain generalization, surpassing all baselines by at least 2.2% in Unseen-Avg mAP across both training orders.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLADR builds directly on CLIP-ReID [18] as its architectural foundation, inheriting the learnable text prompt mechanism and frozen CLIP encoders, while adopting the KL-divergence-based relation distillation from DASK [43] for inter-domain knowledge transfer. The key conceptual departure is from prior VLM-based ReID methods that rely solely on global alignment supervision: VLADR introduces explicit multi-grain attribute disentanglement (via BLIP-2 for local part descriptions and CLIP prompts for global semantics) to replace coarse global-only learning. This positions the work at the intersection of VLM-driven ReID and exemplar-free lifelong learning, where prior methods either lacked human-centric priors (ResNet/ViT backbones) or underutilized VLM knowledge through global-only constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicitly modeling fine-grained human attributes within a VLM framework meaningfully improves both knowledge acquisition and anti-forgetting in lifelong ReID, offering a practical path toward privacy-preserving (exemplar-free) deployment. The lightweight additions (learnable attribute queries, a single cross-attention decoder) make the approach efficient compared to data-generation-based alternatives like DASK. Open questions include whether the fixed set of four body-part attributes is optimal across all domains, how the method scales to more granular attribute taxonomies, and whether the attribute-disentanglement paradigm transfers to other continual vision tasks beyond ReID.
