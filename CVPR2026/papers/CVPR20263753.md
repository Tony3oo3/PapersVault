## Problem

Existing neural field approaches for radar-based novel view synthesis are restricted to static scenes and fail to recover moving objects in dynamic outdoor driving environments. Additionally, prior radar NF methods exhibit an occupancy-reflectance contradiction where high-occupancy regions paradoxically show suppressed reflectance, violating the physical expectation that occupied targets produce stronger radar returns. Radar sensing, despite its robustness to adverse weather and low light, remains largely underexplored in the NF literature compared to RGB and LiDAR modalities.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of 2D range-azimuth maps (400 azimuth beams × 1150 range bins after discarding the innermost 50 bins corresponding to ~3 m ego-vehicle reflections), SE(3) radar poses, and normalized timestamps from a mechanically spinning FMCW mmWave radar performing full 360° horizontal sweeps. It produces a novel radar range-azimuth measurement at any query pose and time, along with per-point 3D occupancy (a soft binary mask via Gumbel-Sigmoid) and view-dependent radar cross-section (RCS) values. Scene coordinates are uniformly scaled to [−1,1]³ and timestamps to [0,1] before encoding.

## Method

RF4D represents the scene as a spatiotemporal neural field where position is encoded via a multi-resolution hash grid and time via a learnable embedding, concatenated and passed through an MLP to yield a latent feature χ. From χ, two radar-specific quantities are predicted: occupancy α (through a Gumbel-Sigmoid MLP encouraging near-binary outputs) and RCS σ (through an MLP that also receives the view direction encoded with spherical harmonics to capture angle-dependent reflectivity). A dedicated scene flow module predicts 6D motion offsets (3D to the previous frame, 3D to the next) from χ, warping query points to adjacent frames to enforce temporal occupancy coherence. The key rendering insight is a radar-specific power formulation P̂r = α · log₁₀(σ/δ²) that treats occupancy as a soft gating mask activating radar responses only where targets are physically present, resolving the occupancy-reflectance contradiction of prior work and eliminating the need for external occupancy supervision. Training combines MSE on rendered power, temporal occupancy consistency, global occupancy sparsity, and motion-offset magnitude regularization.

## Results

On the Oxford Radar RobotCar dataset, RF4D achieves SSIM of 0.4971–0.6258 and CD of 3.29–18.76 across four driving scenes, substantially outperforming Radar Fields (SSIM 0.2829–0.3498, CD 9.54–20.98) in structural fidelity and occupancy accuracy. On the Boreas dataset, RF4D reaches PSNR 26.21–27.98 and SSIM 0.6635–0.7946 across sun, snow, rain, and static scenes, with CD as low as 5.45 (sun) and RCD as low as 0.0033. The largest gains over Radar Fields appear in SSIM (roughly 2× improvement) and occupancy metrics (CD reduced by 30–60%), while PSNR is competitive but occasionally marginally lower (e.g., 22.16 vs. 22.24 in Scene 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Radar Fields [4], the closest radar-specific baseline, RF4D achieves dramatically higher SSIM (e.g., 0.6103 vs. 0.3372 in Scene 1; 0.7946 vs. 0.4823 in Boreas snow) and lower CD/RCD across all scenes, while PSNR is competitive but occasionally marginally lower (22.16 vs. 22.24 in Scene 2; 23.76 vs. 24.02 in Scene 3). Against D-NeRF [34] and Hexplane [7], which are RGB-based dynamic NVS methods adapted to radar, RF4D wins on every metric by large margins (e.g., SSIM 0.61 vs. 0.13–0.27; CD 7.3 vs. 79–81 in Scene 1), confirming that radar-specific modeling is essential. For occupancy estimation, RF4D outperforms both CFAR [36] and Bayesian filtering [49] on CD and RCD across all seven evaluated scenes (e.g., CD 7.34 vs. 19.53/13.28 in RobotCar Scene 1; CD 5.45 vs. 8.76/6.07 in Boreas sun), demonstrating that learning occupancy directly from radar measurements avoids the multipath-induced false positives inherent in traditional detectors.

## Relation to Prior Work

RF4D builds directly on Radar Fields (Borts et al., SIGGRAPH 2024), the first neural field formulation for FMCW radar that replaced optical volume rendering with a physics-driven power model. The key departures are twofold: (1) introducing a temporal dimension with a scene flow module to handle dynamic objects, and (2) replacing Radar Fields' disentangled occupancy-reflectance rendering with a soft-gating formulation where occupancy multiplies the log-RCS term, which the authors argue resolves a physical inconsistency (high occupancy paired with suppressed reflectance) observed in the prior work. The temporal modeling draws on dynamic NVS techniques from the RGB literature (D-NeRF, Hexplane, NeRFies), but the paper explicitly positions itself as the first to apply neural fields to dynamic radar scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Radar's inherent robustness to rain, snow, fog, and darkness makes it a critical sensor for autonomous driving, yet radar-based scene reconstruction has lagged far behind RGB and LiDAR. This paper demonstrates that neural fields can synthesize novel radar views in dynamic outdoor scenes without relying on external occupancy estimators, opening a path toward weather-robust 4D scene understanding. Key limitations include the per-scene training paradigm (no cross-scene generalization), the radar's narrow ~2° elevation FoV that constrains 3D reconstruction, and the inherently low spatial resolution of FMCW radar that caps achievable fidelity. Open questions include whether the scene flow module scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object dynamics, and whether the approach can be made feed-forward rather than per-scene optimized.
