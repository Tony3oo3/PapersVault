## Problem

High-fidelity 4D shape generation from video remains elusive due to temporal artifacts, severe 4D training data scarcity, and the quadratic computational cost of full spatiotemporal attention O((T×P)²) that makes scaling to long or high-resolution sequences prohibitive. Existing approaches either rely on slow per-instance optimization (SDS), suffer error accumulation in two-stage video-then-reconstruct pipelines, or treat pretrained 3D models as frozen black boxes that cannot natively capture temporal dependencies.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video as an image sequence (24 multi-view images per frame at 512×512, rendered via Hammersley-sampled camera poses) and produces a temporally coherent 4D mesh sequence represented as SDFs, trained on 16-frame sequences. Image features are extracted with DINOv2 at 518×518 after background removal and global alignment. Optionally, textures are propagated to the generated geometry via topology-consistent ARAP optimization, yielding textured 4D mesh sequences.

## Method

Sculpt4D extends the pretrained Hunyuan3D 2.1 DiT by inserting a temporal self-attention module (with 1D RoPE along the frame axis) into each block, creating a 4D-DiT block that separates spatial and temporal modeling. The key contribution is a Block Sparse Attention mechanism operating on 128-token blocks: all tokens attend to the first frame (an 'attention anchor' for identity preservation), while for other frames a time-decaying stride schedule S=[1,1,2,4,8,16] defines a relative diagonal mask where block u attends to block v only if (u mod s(d))=(v mod s(d)), preserving 1-to-1 spatial correspondence while pruning uncorrelated pairs. This reduces total network computation by 56% versus full attention. A temporally consistent VAE encoding is achieved by propagating barycentric coordinates from a rest-pose mesh through the animation and sharing a single noise vector across all frames during reparameterization, eliminating stochastic jitter in the latent space.

## Results

On a 50-model holdout test set from Objaverse (16-frame sequences), Sculpt4D achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1052, IoU of 0.3381, and F-Score of 0.3137, outperforming all baselines across every reported metric. The closest competitor, V2M4, records CD=0.1268, IoU=0.3071, and F-Score=0.2909, while the per-frame Hunyuan3D baseline (with shared noise) reaches CD=0.1231, IoU=0.3176, and F-Score=0.2883. Qualitative evaluation on in-the-wild DAVIS videos further demonstrates robust generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sculpt4D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest per-frame baseline (Hunyuan3D with shared noise), Sculpt4D reduces Chamfer Distance by 14.6% (0.1052 vs. 0.1231) and improves IoU by 6.5% (0.3381 vs. 0.3176). Against V2M4, the closest 4D-specific competitor, Sculpt4D achieves a 17.1% lower Chamfer Distance (0.1052 vs. 0.1268) and a 7.8% higher F-Score (0.3137 vs. 0.2909). L4GM, a feed-forward Gaussian-splatting method, trails substantially with CD=0.1655 and F-Score=0.2033, and GVFD (deformation-field approach) performs worst overall (CD=0.4235, F-Score=0.0717), confirming that single-reference-frame deformation strategies struggle with complex motion. IoU is unavailable for L4GM and GVFD due to difficulties converting their Gaussian outputs to watertight meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Sculpt4D extends the pretrained Hunyuan3D 2.1 Diffusion Transformer and its 3DShape2VecSet VAE by inserting spatiotemporal attention blocks, moving beyond prior approaches that treat the 3D model as a frozen per-frame generator (V2M4) or a single-reference deformation predictor (GVFD). The Block Sparse Attention mechanism synthesizes the 'attention sink' concept from StreamingLLM/Radial Attention with a time-decaying diagonal mask inspired by FramePack's computational-density principle, but applies them specifically to 4D shape tokens rather than video pixels. Concurrent work ShapeGen4D pursues the same end-to-end 3D-DiT-to-4D direction but relies on full spatiotemporal attention with O((T×P)²) cost, which this paper explicitly targets as the key bottleneck to overcome.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 56% reduction in total network computation (186.3 vs 425.7 PFLOPs) while achieving near-full-attention quality (CD 0.0972 vs 0.0958) makes long-sequence, high-fidelity 4D generation practically tractable on current hardware, and the sparse mask design is a clean, reusable pattern for any token-based spatiotemporal generative model. Limitations include training on only 13k Objaverse objects with 16-frame sequences, evaluation on a small 50-model test set, and the fact that the attention-sink ablation shows only a marginal gain (CD 0.0986→0.0972), leaving open whether the anchor is truly necessary or merely a regularization effect. Broader open questions include scaling to longer sequences, more diverse 4D content (multi-object, scene-level), and whether the fixed stride schedule generalizes across motion types without per-domain tuning.
