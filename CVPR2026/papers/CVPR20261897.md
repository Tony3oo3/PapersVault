## Problem

Most scene graph generation research prioritizes graph quality over computational efficiency, leaving a critical gap for real-time and resource-constrained deployment. In particular, no prior work addresses low-latency models specifically for panoptic scene graph generation (PSGG), and existing fast SGG methods like REACT only classify a subset of relations rather than producing comprehensive scene graphs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

DSFlash takes a single RGB image (640×640) as input and produces a comprehensive panoptic scene graph: segmentation masks with instance class labels for all detected objects, plus subject-predicate-object triplets for every ordered pair of instances (56 predicate classes). During training, ground truth segmentation masks are supplied to the relation predictor to provide stronger supervision and skip the segmentation head, while at inference the frozen EoMT backbone generates both masks and feature patches in one forward pass. The model is designed for batch-size-1 video stream processing, with latency measured as a single forward pass.

## Method

DSFlash is a two-stage architecture built on a frozen EoMT (Encoder-only Mask Transformer) backbone that simultaneously produces panoptic segmentation masks and multi-scale feature patches, eliminating the dual-backbone inefficiency of DSFormer. The key architectural contribution is a bidirectional relation predictor: a gating mechanism splits the enriched feature tensor into forward and backward intermediate representations, which a shared MLP then maps to relation logits for both (S₀→S₁) and (S₁→S₀) in a single forward pass, halving the number of neck passes needed for a comprehensive graph. A feature consistency loss during training enforces that swapping mask orderings yields symmetric predictions, preventing the model from exploiting dataset annotation biases. Additional speedups come from mask-based dynamic patch pruning (dropping patches with zero overlap to either mask before the transformer neck) and ToMe-SD token merging within the backbone's attention layers, both of which reduce token counts with minimal accuracy loss.

## Results

On the PSG dataset (SGDet protocol, batch size 1, RTX 3090), DSFlash-L achieves the best mR@50 of 30.90 with a latency of 50 ms, outperforming DSFormer (30.70 mR@50, 458 ms) by a small margin while being ~9× faster. The smaller DSFlash-S* variant reaches 25.05 mR@50 at just 18 ms latency (≈56 FPS) with only 40 M parameters, surpassing all other methods except DSFormer in accuracy. DSFlash-B* achieves 28.50 mR@50 at 23 ms with 116 M parameters, offering a strong speed–accuracy trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSFlash dominates all five compared baselines on mR@50. Against DSFormer (the closest competitor), DSFlash-L edges out with 30.90 vs. 30.70 mR@50 while reducing latency from 458 ms to 50 ms (~9× speedup). Against REACT, the only other low-latency method, DSFlash-S* achieves 25.05 mR@50 at 18 ms versus REACT's 19.00 mR@50 at 19 ms—a 6-point accuracy gain at essentially the same speed. Against HiLo-L (19.08 mR@50, 427 ms), DSFlash-L improves accuracy by 11.8 points while being 8.5× faster. Against VCTree-MD (17.58 mR@50, 520 ms) and MotifNet-MD (16.32 mR@50, 504 ms), DSFlash-S* alone surpasses both in accuracy (25.05) while running 28× and 28× faster respectively. No baseline beats DSFlash on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSFlash builds directly on DSFormer (Lorenz et al., ECCV 2024), adopting its mask-embedding mechanism and two-stage PSGG formulation while replacing the dual-network design (separate MaskDINO segmentation + ResNet relation backbone) with a single frozen EoMT backbone that jointly produces segmentation masks and feature tokens. It also draws on the broader PSGG task established by Yang et al. (ECCV 2022) and the efficiency-oriented SGG work of REACT (Neau et al., 2025), which demonstrated that YOLO-based backbones can dramatically reduce SGG latency but only briefly addressed the panoptic setting. The key architectural departures—bidirectional relation prediction, mask-based dynamic patch pruning, and ToMe-SD token merging—are novel contributions layered onto the DSFormer foundation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSFlash is, to the authors' knowledge, the first model specifically targeting low-latency panoptic scene graph generation, filling a gap that limits real-world deployment of PSGG in autonomous systems and edge devices. The practical accessibility of training on a single nine-year-old GPU in under 24 hours lowers the barrier for researchers to adapt PSGG to specialized domains. Open questions include whether the frozen-backbone assumption holds when fine-tuning for out-of-domain scenes, how the method scales to very dense scenes with many instances, and whether the bidirectional prediction and patch-pruning techniques transfer to other transformer-based relation or segmentation architectures beyond PSGG.
