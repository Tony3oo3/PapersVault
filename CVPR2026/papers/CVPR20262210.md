## Problem

Existing Video LLMs handle global, pixel-level, and temporal-level understanding in isolation, with each model specialized to a single granularity and lacking generalization across others. This fragmentation prevents the mutual enhancement that arises when fine-grained temporal knowledge, pixel-level object perception, and holistic video semantics are jointly leveraged. Additionally, no benchmark exists to evaluate multi-grained video cooperative understanding where a model must simultaneously reason across these scales.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video (N frames), a text question, and optionally single-frame object mask prompts (N_s masks over K_I frames) as input. Video duration is normalized to a fixed temporal token length of 100 via relative temporal tokens <Temp-τ>. Outputs are task-dependent: a textual response (language generation), a temporal localization (start/end times scaled back from the 100-token grid), and pixel-level segmentation masks for N_s objects across K_O subsequent frames, with the mask decoder producing masks from LLM hidden states at <Seg> token positions.

## Method

UFVideo unifies multi-grained video understanding through a single LLM backbone (VideoRefer 7B) with a SigLIP vision encoder and a SAM2 mask decoder, connected via three special token types: <Temp-τ> for relative temporal grounding, <Ref> for injecting object visual prompt features, and <Seg> for extracting segmentation-specific language embeddings. Video frames and object masks are separately encoded and projected into the LLM's token space, while the SAM2 Hiera-L encoder processes K random frames to feed the mask decoder alongside the <Seg>-position embeddings. Training follows a two-phase strategy: stage 1 aligns temporal and segmentation heads on 58K+70K samples, and stage 2 jointly trains on ~3M samples across all tasks. The combined loss is a weighted sum of next-token prediction (γ=1.0) and mask supervision (BCE α=2.0 + DICE β=0.5), enabling a single model to flexibly produce text, temporal intervals, or segmentation masks depending on the task prompt.

## Results

On MVBench, UFVideo achieves 67.3 average accuracy, surpassing LLaVA-ST (64.2), UniPixel (62.5), and GPT-4V (43.5) by 3.1, 4.8, and 23.8 points respectively. On Charades-STA temporal grounding, UFVideo reaches tIoU 44.7 and R@0.3 71.2, outperforming the previous best LLaVA-ST (tIoU 42.4, R@0.3 63.1) by 2.3 and 8.1 points. On the challenging ReVOS reasoning segmentation benchmark, UFVideo achieves 64.8 J&F, exceeding UniPixel (64.0) and RGA3 (58.0). On the novel UFVideo-Bench cooperative understanding tasks, UFVideo outperforms GPT-4o across all three sub-tasks (PixRQA SAvg 3.35 vs. 2.58; PixHQA SAvg.wT 4.22 vs. 4.14; PixTRQA SAvg 4.13 vs. 3.87) and is competitive with the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UFVideo consistently outperforms all compared Video LLMs across the nine evaluated benchmarks. On MVBench general understanding, UFVideo (67.3) beats LLaVA-ST (64.2) and UniPixel (62.5), and substantially exceeds GPT-4V (43.5) by 23.8 points. On video object referring (VideoRefer-Bench-Q, multi-frame), UFVideo achieves 77.9 average, surpassing UniPixel (76.3), RGA3 (74.0), and its base model VideoRefer (72.1). On referring/reasoning video segmentation, UFVideo leads on all five datasets (MeViS val^u J&F 62.8, Ref-YouTube-VOS 73.4, Ref-DAVIS17 74.6, ReVOS 64.8), exceeding the closest competitor UniPixel by 2.9–6.8 J&F points and RGA3 by 3.1–11.7 points. On Charades-STA temporal grounding, UFVideo (tIoU 44.7) improves over LLaVA-ST (42.4) and Grounded-VideoLLM (36.8). On the proposed UFVideo-Bench, UFVideo outperforms GPT-4o on all three cooperative tasks and matches or exceeds Qwen3-VL-32B on PixRQA and PixTRQA while being slightly behind on PixHQA (4.22 vs. 4.28 SAvg.wT), demonstrating that a 7B unified model can rival 32B general-purpose MLLMs on multi-grained video understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

UFVideo builds directly on VideoRefer for its base 7B architecture and object visual prompt encoding, and incorporates SAM2's Hiera-L encoder and mask decoder for pixel-level segmentation. It departs from prior unified approaches—RGA3 and UniPixel (pixel-level only), LLaVA-ST (spatial-temporal with bounding boxes)—by introducing a single model that simultaneously handles global video QA, pixel-level referring/segmentation, and temporal grounding through a unified visual-language guided alignment with task-specific special tokens (<Temp-τ>, <Ref>, <Seg>). The key conceptual departure is treating these three granularities as complementary rather than isolated, enabling cooperative multi-grained understanding within one forward pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of UFVideo-Bench with its three cooperative tasks (PixRQA, PixHQA, PixTRQA) fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark required a model to jointly produce language, temporal localization, and segmentation masks in response to a single query. The ablation confirming mutual enhancement across granularities (e.g., adding temporal grounding data improves segmentation J&F from 52.26 to 62.80) provides evidence that multi-grained joint training is not merely task-stuffing but yields genuine cross-task transfer. Open questions include whether this unification scales to longer videos or more objects, how the approach generalizes to audio-visual inputs, and whether the cooperative benchmark's difficulty (even GPT-4o scores only 3.87/5 on PixTRQA) reflects a fundamental limitation of current MLLMs or simply insufficient training signal.
