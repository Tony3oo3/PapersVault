## Problem

Multimodal continual instruction tuning (MCIT) suffers from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially adapting MLLMs to new tasks, and existing solutions either expand the model via Mixture-of-Experts architectures or rely on static regularization terms that cannot adapt to the evolving optimization landscape. This paper identifies the root cause as the absence of old-task gradients during new-task optimization, which prevents convergence to the joint-optimal parameters achievable through multi-task training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261571/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on LLaVA-7B with LoRA adapters, consuming RGB images paired with instructional text queries and producing autoregressive text responses. Training proceeds over a sequential stream of multimodal instruction-tuning tasks (e.g., 10 VQAv2 subtasks or 6 heterogeneous UCIT datasets), where each task provides (image, query, response) triplets. A small replay buffer (0.5k–2k samples per task) is maintained alongside the current task data, and the method requires no additional model parameters beyond the base LoRA weights.

## Method

The core insight reformulates catastrophic forgetting as a gradient approximation problem: the missing old-task gradient ∇L(θ;T₁) is approximated by the directional vector θ − θ* (current parameters minus previously optimal parameters), scaled to match the current task gradient norm to avoid excessive magnitude. This geometric gradient guidance is fused with real gradients computed from a limited replay buffer to form a more accurate estimate of the old-task gradient. A Bernoulli sampling mechanism with probability α stochastically decides at each optimization step whether to incorporate the approximated old-task gradient, dynamically balancing model plasticity (new task learning) against stability (old task retention). The entire procedure operates within the same parameter space without any model expansion, requiring only storage of the previously optimal parameter snapshot and a small replay buffer.

## Results

On the VQAv2 benchmark (10 subtasks, 0.5k replay per task), the method achieves 65.17% FAA, outperforming the strongest baseline SEFE (63.57%) by 1.60% and reaching within 1.09% of the MultiTask upper bound (66.26%). On the more challenging UCIT benchmark (6 tasks with significant distribution shifts, 2k replay per task), it achieves 73.82% FAA, exceeding the best baseline DISCO (69.66%) by 4.16% and falling only 0.96% short of the MultiTask upper bound (74.78%). Notably, the method attains these results without any model expansion (no MoE or additional task-specific parameters), using only LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning on LLaVA-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four baselines on both datasets in terms of FAA. On VQAv2, it beats SEFE (63.57%) by 1.60%, DISCO (63.09%) by 2.08%, HiDE (59.44%) by 5.73%, and CL-MoE (57.27%) by 7.90%. On UCIT, the margins are substantially larger: +7.28% over SEFE (66.54%), +4.16% over DISCO (69.66%), +11.53% over HiDE (62.29%), and +15.33% over CL-MoE (58.49%). The advantage is most pronounced on UCIT, where distribution shifts between tasks are severe and MoE-based baselines (CL-MoE, HiDE) struggle to retain knowledge across heterogeneous domains. A key differentiator is that all compared baselines rely on model expansion (MoE experts, task-specific prompts, or expanded LoRA modules), whereas this method operates entirely within the original parameter space using dynamic gradient guidance, achieving near-MultiTask performance without any architectural overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MCIT paradigm that relies on Mixture-of-Experts or prompt-based model expansion (CL-MoE, HiDE, DISCO, ModalPrompt) by operating entirely at the optimization level within a fixed parameter space. Conceptually, it extends the regularization-based line of work (EWC, SEFE/RegLoRA) but replaces static weight-penalty terms with a dynamic, geometry-driven gradient approximation: the direction from current parameters to previously optimal parameters serves as a surrogate for missing old-task gradients. The Bernoulli sampling mechanism for stochastically gating these approximated gradients is a novel control strategy not seen in prior continual learning methods, drawing an analogy to the stochasticity inherent in mini-batch SGD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving near-MultiTask performance (within ~1% FAA) without any model expansion is practically significant for deployment scenarios where parameter storage and inference cost are constrained, as it avoids the overhead of MoE routing and expert storage. However, the method's sensitivity to the Bernoulli parameter α under large distribution shifts (up to 3.81% FAA variation on UCIT) and its increased reliance on replay buffers in such settings remain open challenges. Future work on adaptive gradient approximation that accounts for distributional distance between tasks, or on learning the optimal α schedule, could close the remaining gap to the joint-training upper bound.
