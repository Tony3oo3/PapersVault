## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models like VGGT suffer from quadratic attention complexity that prevents scaling to kilometer-scale sequences with thousands of frames. Existing workarounds—chunk-based divide-and-conquer (VGGT-Long) or aggressive token merging (FastVGGT)—either discard global context or lose fine-grained spatial cues, leading to degraded accuracy and consistency on long sequences. The paper asks how a 3D foundation model can efficiently retain and leverage long-range contextual cues, analogous to human global scene understanding, to maintain both local accuracy and global consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long RGB video sequence (ranging from ~200 to ~4,600 frames spanning 50–5,000+ meters) and divides it into overlapping chunks of M frames with overlap O, distributed across multiple GPUs for parallel processing. Per-frame outputs include 9-D camera parameters (intrinsic + extrinsic), a depth map, a 3-D point cloud, and a feature grid for point tracking; these are then aligned via similarity transforms on overlapping regions and fused into a single kilometer-scale 3D reconstruction, with optional retrieval-based loop closure for trajectories with revisits.

## Method

Scal3R embeds a Global Context Memory (GCM) module into the VGGT backbone after selected global attention layers. Each GCM consists of lightweight neural sub-networks (Adaptive Memory Units, ~4M parameters) that store long-range context as fast weights, updated per-chunk via a self-supervised dot-product loss over all tokens in the chunk treated as a single batch (inspired by LaCT for GPU-efficient parallelism). A Global Context Synchronization (GCS) mechanism then all-reduces the AMU gradients across all GPUs so every chunk's sub-networks encode sequence-wide context, effectively turning multi-GPU chunk processing into context parallelism. A learnable gate vector balances the GCM output against the original tokens, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with a multi-task loss (camera L1, confidence-weighted depth, point-cloud) on 18 diverse datasets, using random GPU-group partitioning during training to improve length generalization.

## Results

On Virtual KITTI, Scal3R achieves RRE 0.31°/100m, RTE 0.43 m/100m, and ATE 0.26 m, outperforming the strongest feed-forward baseline VGGT-Long (RRE 0.71, RTE 2.01, ATE 1.03) by 2–4×. On KITTI Odometry (avg. 758 frames), Scal3R reaches RRE 0.97, RTE 4.61, ATE 14.55, halving the ATE of VGGT-Long (25.94) and reducing it by ~14× compared to TTT3R (177.73). On Oxford Spires, Scal3R attains ATE 4.45 m vs. 15.46 m for VGGT-Long and 31.57 m for TTT3R. For 3D geometry (Table 2), Scal3R achieves F1 0.91 on ETH3D, 0.96 on Oxford Spires, and 0.91 on Virtual KITTI, with the lowest Chamfer Distance across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scal3R decisively outperforms all feed-forward and streaming baselines: on KITTI Odometry it reduces ATE from 25.94 m (VGGT-Long) to 14.55 m, and from 177.73 m (TTT3R) to 14.55 m, a 12× improvement over the closest TTT-based method. FastVGGT and CUT3R suffer large errors on long sequences (KITTI ATE 206.69 and 209.78 m respectively), while Scal3R maintains sub-15 m ATE. Against the classical SfM baseline COLMAP, Scal3R wins on Virtual KITTI (ATE 0.26 vs 9.09 m) and KITTI RTE/ATE (4.61 vs 15.88, 14.55 vs 37.79), but COLMAP remains stronger on Oxford Spires (ATE 0.15 vs 4.45 m) where its feature matching and global optimization are well-conditioned with known intrinsics. DPVO++ is competitive on short Virtual KITTI sequences (RRE 0.07 vs 0.31) but degrades sharply on longer KITTI and Oxford trajectories (ATE 52.69 and 29.17 m vs. 14.55 and 4.45 m for Scal3R). Among SLAM-based methods, MASt3R-SLAM and VGGT-SLAM experience tracking failures on long sequences, yielding ATE values above 190 m on KITTI, whereas Scal3R avoids such failures entirely through its global context mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

Scal3R builds directly on VGGT's unified Transformer backbone for joint camera pose, depth, and point-cloud prediction, and on the Test-Time Training (TTT) paradigm where fast weights serve as an online-adapted memory. It departs from VGGT-Long's chunk-and-align strategy by introducing a neural Global Context Memory (GCM) module—implemented as lightweight sub-networks (Adaptive Memory Units)—that retains long-range context across chunks, and from TTT3R's fixed-size token memory by using a larger-capacity non-linear memory updated in chunk-wise batches. The Global Context Synchronization (GCS) mechanism, framed as context parallelism across GPUs, is a novel aggregation step that propagates sequence-wide context to every local chunk during both training and inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Scal3R demonstrates that feed-forward 3D reconstruction can approach classical SfM accuracy (e.g., COLMAP) on kilometer-scale sequences without requiring known intrinsics or auxiliary sensors, which is practically significant for autonomous driving and robotics mapping. The inference cost (~300 s for 758 frames on a single GPU) remains substantially higher than streaming SLAM methods, and the approach's scalability beyond the tested 32-GPU training regime is not fully characterized. Open questions include whether the GCS mechanism can be made more communication-efficient for very large GPU counts, whether the chunk-based TTT update can be further accelerated toward real-time operation, and how the method behaves on scenes with extreme viewpoint changes or dynamic content.
