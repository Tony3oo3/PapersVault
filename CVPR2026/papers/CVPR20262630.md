## Problem

Existing 3D part generation methods fail to simultaneously achieve structural coherence between parts, geometric plausibility, fine-grained geometric fidelity, and computational efficiency. Methods that embed all parts in shared latent codes (BANG, PartPacker) sacrifice detail; those generating parts independently (HoloPart) lose inter-part coherence; fixed-length token approaches (PartCrafter, X-Part) waste capacity on small parts while under-representing large ones; and voxel-based methods (OmniPart) are constrained by bounding boxes that cause overlapping voxels and prevent expansion beyond initial boundaries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monolithic 3D mesh (GLB format, filtered to ≤20 connected sub-meshes) and renders normal maps and canonical coordinate maps from six orthographic views as conditioning signals. It outputs a set of K individual 3D parts, each represented as sparse voxels with fine-grained surface geometry, where parts are structurally coherent with one another and geometrically complete (not merely the visible fragments from segmentation). The frontal normal map (n₁) serves as the primary cross-modal conditioning throughout both generation stages.

## Method

EI-Part employs a three-stage pipeline: (1) a multi-view diffusion segmentation model (MVSegNet) combined with a 3D inpainting network (InSegNet) produces globally consistent part labels; (2) an Explode stage radially disperses incomplete part voxels to eliminate inter-part overlap and expand available spatial resolution, then performs conditional rectified-flow diffusion (DiT + CFM) with self-attention over concatenated part/noise tokens and cross-attention with DINOv2 normal-map features to complete part geometry; (3) an Implode stage iteratively pulls parts back toward the center (stopping at collision) to maximize local voxel resolution, then refines fine geometric details using a higher-capacity Sparc3D VAE in a second conditional diffusion pass. The key insight is that spatial resolution is the bottleneck at different stages—exploding frees up space for meaningful completion, while imploding concentrates resolution for surface detail—so the same parts are processed at two different spatial scales with self-attention maintaining structural coherence throughout.

## Results

On the PartVerse benchmark, EI-Part achieves the best performance across all six evaluation metrics. The headline numbers are Voxel IoU of 0.7981 (vs. 0.7478 for the next-best X-Part), Chamfer Distance of 0.0194 (vs. 0.0431 for HoloPart), and F-Score@0.1 of 0.9910 (vs. 0.9557 for HoloPart). The method also leads on fine-grained metrics: F-Score@0.05 of 0.9742 and F-Score@0.01 of 0.8129, confirming superior geometric fidelity at tight tolerances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EI-Part outperforms all five quantitatively evaluated baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor X-Part, EI-Part improves Voxel IoU by 5.0 points (0.7981 vs. 0.7478), reduces CD by 67% (0.0194 vs. 0.0599), and raises F-Score@0.1 by 6.5 points (0.9910 vs. 0.9256), with the gap widening at tighter tolerances (F-Score@0.01: 0.8129 vs. 0.7923). HoloPart, which generates each part independently, is beaten on Voxel IoU (0.7981 vs. 0.6106) and CD (0.0194 vs. 0.0431), confirming that the self-attention-based structural coherence mechanism is critical. OmniPart and PartPacker, both limited by their voxel/bounding-box or dual-latent representations, trail by large margins (Voxel IoU ≤ 0.29). BANG, a commercial product without open-source code, is compared only qualitatively and is noted to struggle with fine geometric details due to its single-set latent encoding. For segmentation quality, EI-Part's MVSegNet+InSegNet pipeline produces more accurate and meaningful part boundaries than PartField and P3-SAM in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the line of 3D part generation methods (BANG, PartPacker, HoloPart, PartCrafter, X-Part, OmniPart) and 3D part segmentation approaches (SAM-Part3D, PartField, P3-SAM), while leveraging structured latent representations from TRELLIS (SS-VAE) and Sparc3D for high-fidelity geometry. Its key departure is the Explode-Implode strategy: prior methods either embed all parts in a shared latent space (BANG, PartPacker), generate parts independently (HoloPart), use fixed-length tokens (PartCrafter, X-Part), or restrict completion to bounding-box-confined voxels (OmniPart). EI-Part instead uses a dispersed voxel state to maximize spatial resolution for meaningful completion, then a compact state to maximize resolution for fine detail refinement, with self-attention in both stages to maintain inter-part structural coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Part-level 3D generation is a critical bottleneck for downstream applications in gaming, film, and industrial design where individual components must be independently manipulable. EI-Part addresses the long-standing tension between structural coherence, geometric plausibility, fidelity, and efficiency simultaneously, which prior methods have only partially resolved. Limitations include the requirement for a 3D input shape (no text-to-parts or image-to-parts capability), the geometric (non-learned) nature of the explosion/implosion vectors, and the absence of physical constraints in the generated parts. The authors note future work on integrating physical principles, and the broader open question of whether this two-stage resolution-allocation strategy generalizes to other coarse-to-fine 3D generation tasks remains unexplored.
