## Problem

Existing 3D medical vision SSL methods face a fundamental trade-off: invariance-based approaches struggle with medical-specific augmentations, while masked autoencoders rely on high masking ratios that enforce holistic structure at the cost of fine-grained tissue texture. The generative-semantic mutual benefit paradigm demonstrated in 2D natural vision—where reconstruction and denoising objectives complement each other—remains unexplored for 3D medical volumes, where diagnostically critical regions are often small, spatially localized, and distinct from surrounding anatomy.

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D brain MRI volumes (160³ voxels, z-score normalized, single or multi-channel) and produces a clean volume prediction from a doubly-corrupted input. Corruption is applied jointly: patch-based spatial masking (16³ voxel blocks, variable ratio sampled from U(0.01, 0.99)) zeros out masked regions, while variance-exploding Gaussian noise (σ_t = t·σ_max) is added to visible regions before masking. The encoder features extracted from this process serve as the learned representation for downstream classification and segmentation tasks.

## Method

MDAE applies simultaneous spatial masking and diffusion noise corruption to create two complementary learning objectives within a single time-conditioned framework: masked-region reconstruction enforces holistic anatomical reasoning, while visible-region denoising preserves fine-grained tissue texture. The architecture is a ResNet U-Net with FiLM conditioning that injects a sinusoidal time embedding at every encoder and decoder stage, allowing the network to adapt its reconstruction strategy to the corruption severity. Training minimizes a weighted sum of masked-region MSE (normalized by |Ω_M|) and visible-region MSE (normalized by |Ω_V| with noise-level weighting w(σ_t)), both targeting the clean volume X_0. Variable masking ratio is key: the diffusion noise on visible regions prevents trivial pattern copying even at low masking ratios, enabling the model to learn both low-level texture and global structure in one pretraining objective. The framework reduces to standard MAE as σ_max→0 and to denoising score matching as p_mask→0, unifying both paradigms.

## Results

On in-distribution T1/T2 brain MRI tasks (tumor type classification, MGMT methylation, IDH1 mutation detection), MDAE achieves 73.6% mean AUROC and 71.6% mean AP, outperforming the strongest SSL baseline MAE (69.5% AUROC) by 4.1% and the best foundation model BrainIAC (67.4%) by 6.2%. On cross-modal generalization to rare sequences (FLAIR, T1-Gd, ASL, SWI), MDAE reaches 78.6% mean AUROC, improving 8.6% over MAE and 10.7% over BrainIAC. On multi-modality integration, MDAE achieves 92.1% AUROC on BraTS18 (4 modalities) and 85.2% Dice on UCSF-PDGM (6 modalities) segmentation, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MDAE outperforms all 11 baselines on aggregate in-distribution AUROC (73.6%), with the largest margins over SwinUNETR (+10.1%), MRI-Core (+15.5%), and DinoV2 (+13.5%). Among SSL methods, MDAE leads MAE by 4.1% AUROC and VoCo by 6.6%; among foundation models, it surpasses BrainIAC by 6.2% and BrainMVP by 6.7%. The advantage is most pronounced in cross-modal generalization (Table 2), where MDAE gains 8.6% over MAE and 10.7% over BrainIAC, and even exceeds the general-domain DinoV2 by 6.5%—demonstrating that medical-specific dual corruption pretraining adds value beyond generic visual features. On multi-modality tasks (Table 3), MDAE leads by 2.0% AUROC over MAE on BraTS18 and by 0.7% over Models Genesis on UCSF-PDGM, while also achieving the best segmentation Dice (85.2% vs. 84.8% for MAE). The only near-ties are BrainMVP on MGMT AP (59.6% vs. 57.6%) and SwinUNETR on T2 IDH1 AP (60.0% vs. 56.4%), where individual sub-metrics are competitive but MDAE still leads decisively on aggregate.

## Relation to Prior Work

MDAE unifies two previously separate corruption paradigms—spatial masking from MAE (He et al., 2022) and Gaussian noise corruption from Denoising Score Matching (Vincent, 2011)—into a single dual-corruption objective applied simultaneously to 3D medical volumes. This follows the broader 2D-vision trend (RAE, REPA, STELLAR) showing that generative and semantic objectives can mutually reinforce each other, but is the first to operationalize this for 3D medical imaging with a time-conditioned CNN decoder. The method departs from standard MAE by replacing fixed high masking ratios with a variable distribution and by adding diffusion noise on visible regions, which maintains task difficulty at lower masking ratios and exposes the network to fine-grained texture that pure masking discards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing tension in medical SSL: high masking ratios capture holistic anatomy but discard diagnostically critical fine texture, while low ratios preserve texture but enable trivial reconstruction. MDAE's dual corruption resolves this trade-off within one objective, and the 8.6% cross-modal AUROC gain over MAE suggests the learned representations transfer better to unseen sequences—a practically important property given the scarcity of annotated rare MRI contrasts. Limitations include evaluation restricted to brain MRI (no CT, ultrasound, or histopathology), reliance on a single large pretraining corpus (OpenMind), and the finding that ResNet substantially outperforms ViT, which may limit adoption in transformer-centric pipelines. Whether the dual-corruption principle generalizes to other anatomies and modalities remains an open question.
