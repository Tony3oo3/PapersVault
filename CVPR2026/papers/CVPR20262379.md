## Problem

Unsupervised scene understanding has made significant progress in image segmentation (semantic, instance, panoptic), but the video domain remains largely unexplored. No prior work has performed video panoptic segmentation without any human supervision, leaving a gap in understanding dynamic scenes in a fully annotation-free setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262379/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular video clips (no stereo or multi-view input required) and produces a full panoptic video segmentation: a per-pixel semantic class map over c_p pseudo-classes plus binary tracked instance masks for 'thing' objects with consistent IDs across frames. Pseudo-labels are generated at 27 pseudo-classes following CUPS, and the model is trained sparsely on only moving 'thing' instances to generalize to static objects at inference time.

## Method

VideoCUPS generates temporally consistent panoptic video pseudo-labels via a bottom-up pipeline: (1) instance masks are extracted per-frame using motion-based region growing that merges pixels sharing consistent relative depth (from DynamoDepth) and optical flow (from SMURF), capturing non-rigid motion; (2) semantic labels come from k-means clustering of distilled DINO features with depth-guided sliding-window inference and dense CRF regularization; (3) temporal consistency is enforced via optical-flow-based mask propagation with Hungarian IoU matching for instance tracking and three-frame majority voting for semantic smoothing. Training uses a novel Video DropLoss that pseudo-supervises only 'thing' detections and their tracking representations exceeding an IoU threshold, while allowing the model to freely predict objects not covered by pseudo-labels, combined with a self-enhanced video copy-paste augmentation that pastes the model's own confident predictions back into training clips with random trajectories.

## Results

On Cityscapes-VPS val, VideoCUPS achieves 22.2% STQ (15.3% AQ, 32.3% SQ), outperforming the strongest unsupervised baseline CUPS+SORT (stereo) by 1.6% STQ and the monocular CUPS†+SORT by 4.4% STQ. Cross-domain generalization is consistent: VideoCUPS leads on KITTI-STEP (37.3% vs. 34.2% STQ), Waymo (18.4% vs. 17.5%), and OOD MOTS (18.6% vs. 16.7%). In the label-efficient setting, VideoCUPS fine-tuned on only 10% of Cityscapes-VPS labels matches the STQ of a randomly initialized model trained on 100% of labels, and with full labels nearly closes the gap to the supervised upper bound (38.1% vs. 40.4% STQ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoCUPS outperforms all four unsupervised baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against DepthG+VideoCutLER (STQ 9.9%) and U2Seg+SORT (STQ 11.4%), VideoCUPS gains 12.3 and 10.8 STQ points respectively, which the authors attribute to those baselines' reliance on object-centric training data rather than scene-centric video. The closest competitor is CUPS+SORT using stereo video (20.6% STQ); VideoCUPS surpasses it by 1.6% STQ while requiring only monocular input. The monocular variant CUPS†+SORT (17.8% STQ) is outperformed by 4.4% STQ, demonstrating that VideoCUPS's motion-based region growing and temporal tracking extract more useful structure from monocular cues than CUPS's stereo-dependent pipeline. SORT [9] serves as the tracking backbone for three baselines but does not match VideoCUPS's integrated flow-based propagation and Hungarian matching. The supervised upper bound (Panoptic FPN + MaskTrack R-CNN, 42.0% STQ) remains substantially ahead, but VideoCUPS with 10% fine-tuning labels already reaches the STQ of a model trained from scratch on 100% of labels, highlighting its value as a strong unsupervised initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoCUPS extends the unsupervised panoptic segmentation line from CUPS (image, stereo) and U2Seg (image, object-centric) into the video domain, while replacing stereo scene-flow-based rigid instance grouping with monocular optical-flow-and-depth region growing that captures non-rigid motion. The training pipeline builds on the DropLoss formulation from CutLER and the self-enhanced copy-paste idea from CUPS, both adapted to the temporal setting via a new Video DropLoss and video copy-paste augmentation. For evaluation, the paper extends the supervised STQ metric from Weber et al. with a pseudo-semantic matching step, filling a gap since no prior unsupervised VPS evaluation protocol existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first unsupervised VPS approach, VideoCUPS establishes a task definition, evaluation protocol, and four competitive baselines that give the community a concrete starting point for future work. The label-efficient results (10% VPS labels with VideoCUPS initialization matching 100% labels from random init) suggest a practical path toward reducing annotation costs for video panoptic tasks. Key limitations remain: pseudo-labels only capture moving 'thing' instances, monocular depth quality still lags stereo (visible in SQ), and the absolute gap to supervised VPS (22.2% vs. 42% STQ) is substantial, leaving open whether fully unsupervised approaches can close this gap or whether semi-supervised fine-tuning is the more viable route.
