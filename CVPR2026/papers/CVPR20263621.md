## Problem

Existing MLLM–World Model integrations for embodied agents either treat the MLLM as an external planner/reward tool or rely on unidirectional, task-agnostic connectors that map MLLM embeddings into the WM's latent space. This leaves two critical gaps: no gradient feedback from environmental dynamics back into the MLLM's semantic space, and a uniform alignment strategy that cannot adapt to task-specific semantics, limiting multi-task stability and cross-environment generalization.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a textual task instruction, a video observation sequence rendered at 64×64 pixels (sequence length 32, batch size 64), and previous actions, all processed through a shared InternVideo2 video-language backbone. It produces an action sequence for control, imagined latent trajectories over a horizon H in the fused semantic-dynamic space, and dense per-step rewards computed as cosine similarity between the WM rollout and a task-conditioned imagination reference. The task instruction is encoded into a global task embedding that acts as a continuous routing signal for every fusion layer rather than being consumed only at the input stage.

## Method

ModularAgent couples an RSSM-based world model with an MLLM through three synergistic modules. The Task-Aware Modular Fusion (TAMF) module stacks L layers, each containing a semantic expert adapter and a dynamics expert adapter; a lightweight gating network maps the task embedding to a per-layer scalar p ∈ (0,1) via a two-layer MLP with GELU, and each layer computes z^(ℓ) = z^(ℓ−1) + (1−p)·A_sem(z^(ℓ−1)) + p·A_dyn(z^(ℓ−1)), enabling task-conditioned routing that mitigates cross-task gradient conflicts. In the forward path, MLLM visual embeddings are injected into the WM latent space through TAMF for semantically guided imagination; in the backward path, a text-imagination module predicts task-aligned latent transitions, and the KL divergence between the WM rollout and this reference yields dense differentiable rewards that backpropagate through the joint loss to reshape the MLLM's semantic representations. The unified training objective L_total = λ_WM·L_WM + λ_MLLM·L_MLLM + λ_JBO·L_JBO combines RSSM prior–posterior KL and reconstruction (L_WM), visual reconstruction plus cross-modal alignment (L_MLLM), and a discounted negative-similarity joint behavior optimization loss (L_JBO) that aligns imagined trajectories with task semantics over a horizon H.

## Results

On the DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) with 10 locomotion tasks across four embodiments, ModularAgent achieves an overall normalized episodic reward of 0.91±0.02, outperforming the strongest model-based baseline FOUNDER (0.87±0.03) and GenRL (0.82±0.01). The method wins 9 out of 10 individual tasks, with the largest gains on challenging tasks such as walker run (0.87 vs. 0.78 for FOUNDER) and stickman stand (0.95 vs. 0.91 for FOUNDER). All scores are min-max normalized where a random policy corresponds to 0 and an expert policy to 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three model-free baselines, ModularAgent dominates across all tasks: IQL (0.39 overall), TD3+BC (0.34), and TD3 (0.45) all fall far below, confirming that explicit dynamics modeling is critical for long-horizon locomotion. Among model-based MLLM-WM methods, ModularAgent (0.91) outperforms FOUNDER (0.87) by 0.04 and GenRL (0.82) by 0.09 in overall score, winning 9 of 10 individual tasks. The sole exception is cheetah run, where FOUNDER edges ahead (0.81 vs. 0.79), likely due to the high-speed dynamics of that task. WM-CLIP, which learns a reversed (WM-to-MLLM) connector, trails significantly at 0.69 overall, underscoring that unidirectional mapping in either direction is insufficient. The largest absolute gains over FOUNDER appear on walker run (+0.09) and stickman stand (+0.04), tasks where semantic-dynamic alignment is most challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unidirectional MLLM-to-WM connector paradigm established by GenRL (Mazzaglia et al., NeurIPS 2024) and FOUNDER (Wang et al., ICML 2025) by making the coupling bidirectional and task-aware. Where prior work projects MLLM embeddings into the WM's latent space via a static connector, ModularAgent introduces a Task-Aware Modular Fusion (TAMF) module with layer-wise dual experts (semantic and dynamics adapters) gated by a task embedding, and closes the loop by backpropagating dense text-conditioned rewards from WM rollouts back into the MLLM. The architectural design draws on adapter-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Houlsby et al., VL-Adapter) and the RSSM formulation from Dreamer, while the joint optimization objective unifies semantic reconstruction, dynamic prediction, and behavior alignment under a single loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bidirectional gradient coupling between MLLMs and world models is a conceptually important step toward unified embodied agents that can both reason about task semantics and predict physical dynamics within a single trainable system. However, the experimental validation is confined to four DMC locomotion environments with relatively simple tasks (stand/walk/run), leaving open whether the task-aware routing and dense semantic rewards generalize to more complex settings such as manipulation, navigation, or long-horizon multi-stage tasks. The modest but consistent gains over GenRL and FOUNDER (0.82→0.91 overall) suggest the framework is sound but the practical ceiling in richer embodied scenarios remains untested.
