## Problem

4D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction from real-world monocular video is severely degraded by motion blur, yet existing deblurring 3DGS methods only handle static backgrounds and current 4D deblurring approaches (BARD-GS, Deblur4DGS) rely on extensive prior data such as depth maps, trajectories, and optical flow. No prior method can simultaneously address both camera motion blur and non-rigid object motion blur in dynamic scenes while remaining practical with minimal supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261699/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of motion-blurred monocular video frames (1280×720 on Stereo Blur, 960×540 on the real-world blurry dataset) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and additionally uses BootsTAPIR to extract 2D dynamic masks for static/dynamic Gaussian separation. It outputs a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene model that renders deblurred images and synthesizes novel views at 121 FPS, with the video divided into short subsets (each with N=3 virtual exposure samples) that are reconstructed gradually and concatenated chronologically.

## Method

MSCD-GS separates scene Gaussians into static and dynamic sets using motion distance (top 1.5%) and BootsTAPIR-derived masks, then models their motion differently: static Gaussians follow a linear trajectory with an MLP predicting rigid rotation and translation at N sampled exposure times, while dynamic Gaussians use Catmull-Rom splines for position, Slerp for rotation, a dual Gaussian decay for opacity, and a second MLP predicting per-Gaussian deformations (Δμ, ΔR, ΔS). At each sampled time, static and dynamic Gaussians are merged and rendered into virtual sharp images, which are averaged to produce a virtual blurred image. Training uses a cooperative loss that balances supervision from a pre-trained NAFNet deblurring output (providing sharp priors) against the real blurred input (preventing overfitting to imperfect deblurring), weighted by λ=0.4, enabling the model to recover detail that neither component alone can achieve.

## Results

On the Stereo Blur dataset, MSCD-GS achieves a deblurring PSNR of 33.21 dB (SSIM 0.957, LPIPS 0.043) and a novel view synthesis PSNR of 29.49 dB (SSIM 0.941, LPIPS 0.065), outperforming the strongest dynamic deblurring baselines Deblur4DGS (30.32/27.84 dB) and BARD-GS (30.21/27.02 dB) by 2.9 dB and 2.5 dB respectively on deblurring and NVS. On the real-world blurry dataset, MSCD-GS reaches 28.13 dB PSNR for novel view synthesis, surpassing BARD-GS (25.13 dB) by 3.0 dB. MSCD-GS also achieves the highest rendering speed (121 FPS) and shortest training time (0.72 h) among all dynamic deblurring methods, compared to 96 FPS / 6.10 h for Deblur4DGS and 0.2 FPS / 51.1 h for DyBluRF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSCD-GS outperforms all 10 baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest dynamic deblurring competitors, it beats Deblur4DGS [38] by 2.89 dB PSNR on deblurring (33.21 vs 30.32) and 1.65 dB on NVS (29.49 vs 27.84), while also reducing LPIPS from 0.085 to 0.043 and cutting training time from 6.10 h to 0.72 h. BARD-GS [19] trails by 3.0 dB on deblurring PSNR and 2.47 dB on NVS PSNR, with a 4.62 h training cost. DyBluRF [29] is the weakest dynamic deblurring method (28.67 dB deblurring, 25.90 dB NVS) and runs at only 0.2 FPS with 51.1 h training. The naive combination of 4D reconstruction with NAFNet (SoM+NAFNet at 29.01 dB deblurring, 27.76 dB NVS) confirms that simply feeding deblurred images into a 4DGS pipeline is insufficient—MSCD-GS's cooperative supervision adds 4.2 dB over this baseline on deblurring. Static-only methods (Deblurring-3DGS, BAD-GS) and dynamic methods without deblurring (4D Gaussians, E-D3DGS, SoM, SplineGS) all fall 5–13 dB behind MSCD-GS on deblurring PSNR, underscoring the difficulty of handling motion blur in dynamic scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework to 4D dynamic scenes and inherits the exposure-time virtual camera pose sampling strategy from Deblur-GS and BAD-Gaussians, which previously only addressed static scene deblurring. It departs from concurrent 4D deblurring methods (BARD-GS, Deblur4DGS) by eliminating their reliance on heavy prior data (depth maps, optical flow, trajectories) and instead introducing a motion-separation strategy that models static Gaussians with linear motion and dynamic Gaussians with Catmull-Rom spline interpolation. The key conceptual departure is the cooperative deblurring formulation: a pre-trained 2D deblurring network (NAFNet) provides sharp-image supervision while motion-synthesized blurred images provide a complementary constraint, preventing overfitting to the deblurring network's imperfect outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Motion blur is an unavoidable artifact of real-world monocular capture, making this a practically critical problem for dynamic scene reconstruction in robotics, autonomous driving, and AR/VR. The method's 5× training speedup and 1.5× rendering speedup over the closest 4D deblurring competitors (BARD-GS, Deblur4DGS) alongside a ~3 dB PSNR margin make it a strong practical candidate for deployment. A key open limitation is the inability to reconstruct 'missing content' from fast-moving objects that vanish during exposure; the authors suggest diffusion-based inpainting as a future direction. Whether the cooperative deblurring loss (balancing sharp prior vs. synthetic blur) generalizes to more extreme blur conditions or multi-camera setups remains untested.
