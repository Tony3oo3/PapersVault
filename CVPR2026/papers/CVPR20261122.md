## Problem

Conventional multiview point cloud registration relies on a pairwise-then-global paradigm: exhaustive pairwise alignment builds a pose graph, followed by synchronization to recover absolute poses. This approach is computationally expensive (quadratic in scan count), inherently ill-posed without holistic geometric constraints, sensitive to pairwise outliers that propagate globally, and burdened by handcrafted inductive biases. No prior method directly predicts globally consistent poses from all scans in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261122/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N unordered, partially overlapping point cloud scans (N ranging from 2 to 200 in experiments) and outputs a global SE(3) rigid pose (rotation + translation) for each scan, aligning them into a common coordinate frame. Scans are consumed as permutation-equivariant sets—reordering inputs does not change outputs—and are first downsampled into compact superpoint tokens (M_i' << M_i) via a five-layer sparse 3D CNN before entering the transformer. FUSER-DF additionally takes FUSER's pose estimates as priors and outputs refined poses after 10 denoising steps in the joint SE(3)^N space.

## Method

FUSER is a feed-forward transformer with three stages: (1) absolute geometric encoding via a MinkowskiEngine sparse 3D CNN that preserves translation cues (unlike relative encodings), producing low-resolution superpoint features; (2) Geometric Alternating Attention with 32 layers (16 intra-scan + 16 cross-scan) that alternates local and global message passing, initialized with 2D attention priors from the π³ VGGT variant to transfer view-grouping and alignment-consistency knowledge across modalities; (3) a global pose predictor that regresses per-scan SE(3) poses via MLP heads with SVD-based rotation projection, supervised by a reference-free relative pose loss (geodesic rotation + Huber translation + point-wise geometric loss). FUSER-DF extends this with an SE(3)^N diffusion refinement: it formulates pose correction as denoising from FUSER's prior estimates toward ground truth in the joint SE(3)^N manifold, uses FUSER itself as the multiview surrogate registration model within the reverse process, and is trained via a prior-conditioned variational lower bound that introduces a third denoising coefficient λ₂ controlling the prior's influence across timesteps.

## Results

On ScanNet (30 scans), FUSER achieves 6.7°/2.1° mean/median rotation error and 0.15/0.07 m mean/median translation error, substantially outperforming the best pairwise-then-global baseline MDGD (17.4°/19.0° and 0.37/0.31 m). On 3DMatch (60 scans), FUSER reaches 90.3% Registration Recall with 3.2° RE and 0.14 m TE, versus 61.9% RR, 31.2° RE, and 0.68 m TE for the strongest baseline (Full+PARENet). On ArkitScenes (200 scans), FUSER attains 92.1% RR, 5.4° RE, and 0.13 m TE, compared to 26.7% RR, 91.2° RE, and 1.48 m TE for the best baseline (SGHR+GeoTrans). Inference time drops from hundreds to thousands of seconds (two-stage pipelines) to 0.31 s (3DMatch) and 0.61 s (ArkitScenes) for FUSER, and 2.91 s / 6.50 s for FUSER-DF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSER and FUSER-DF outperform all 13 baselines across every dataset and metric. On ScanNet, FUSER reduces mean rotation error from 17.4° (MDGD, the strongest prior method) to 6.7° and mean translation error from 0.37 m to 0.15 m, while requiring zero pairwise registrations versus 13,920 pairs for full-graph baselines. On 3DMatch, FUSER achieves 90.3% RR compared to 61.9% for the best baseline (Full+PARENet) and 3.2° RE versus 26.8° for Full+Predator, a roughly 8× reduction in rotation error. On the large-scale ArkitScenes benchmark (200 scans), the gap widens dramatically: FUSER reaches 92.1% RR and 5.4° RE while the best baseline (SGHR+GeoTrans) manages only 26.7% RR and 91.2° RE. In terms of efficiency, FUSER completes inference in 0.31 s on 3DMatch and 0.61 s on ArkitScenes, compared to 155–763 s for the fastest two-stage baselines on 3DMatch and 583–2455 s on ArkitScenes, representing a 500×–4000× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSER departs fundamentally from the dominant pairwise-then-global paradigm in multiview registration, which has relied on exhaustive pairwise alignment followed by pose-graph synchronization. It builds on two conceptual threads: (1) the SE(3) diffusion registration framework of Jiang et al. (NeurIPS 2023), which is extended from a single pairwise SE(3) transformation to the joint SE(3)^N manifold with a prior-conditioned variational bound; and (2) VGGT's alternating intra/cross attention mechanism (CVPR 2025), which is adapted from 2D multi-image reasoning to 3D point cloud matching with permutation-equivariant design. A distinctive departure is the transfer of pretrained 2D attention weights from pi3 (a VGGT variant) to initialize the 3D alternating attention layers, a cross-modal initialization strategy not previously explored for point cloud tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating pairwise estimation entirely, FUSER removes the quadratic computational bottleneck and error accumulation that have constrained multiview registration, cutting inference from minutes to under a second while achieving substantially higher accuracy. The 2D-to-3D attention prior transfer opens a broader question about whether pretrained 2D foundation model knowledge can systematically benefit 3D geometric tasks, potentially reducing the data and compute needed for 3D-specific training. Open questions include scalability beyond 200 scans, generalization to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether the feed-forward paradigm can handle degenerate cases (e.g., highly symmetric or near-zero-overlap scans) where global geometric constraints are weakest.
