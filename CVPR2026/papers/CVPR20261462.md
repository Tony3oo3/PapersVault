## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary and described object detection models produce highly variable features for the same object when the surrounding background changes, revealing an intra-modal consistency gap that prior cross-modal alignment work has overlooked. This inconsistency causes significant performance drops under environmental variation, as demonstrated by the authors' D³_BC benchmark where baseline models degrade substantially after background replacement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261462/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives an RGB image and a free-form text query (ranging from single category words to multi-word descriptions) and outputs bounding boxes with category labels and confidence scores. During training, each original image is expanded into K background-replaced variants sharing identical foreground objects, and the model is fine-tuned for one epoch on this augmented joint dataset (0.25M images) rather than the full 0.8M-image set used by baselines. The text modality consistency loss is only active when the backbone supports cross-modal fusion (FIBER), while the image modality loss applies to both GLIP and FIBER.

## Method

CCL comprises two complementary components: Contextual Bootstrapped Data Generation (CBDG) and Contextual Consistency Loss (CCLoss). CBDG first performs categorical augmentation via SAM-based object extraction and cross-image object insertion to increase per-image category diversity, then generates diverse backgrounds using ChatGPT prompts fed to Stable Diffusion (seasonal, sky, natural landscape), and finally composites the original foreground onto each generated background to produce identity-preserving image pairs. CCLoss is a within-modality contrastive objective that, for each batch of K images sharing the same C object categories, computes Context-Aware Aggregated Features (CAAF) by pooling regional features and applies a temperature-scaled cosine-similarity contrastive loss pulling same-category features toward their category centroid while pushing cross-category features apart. The total training loss combines standard localization and classification losses with CCLoss, weighted separately for image (λ_I) and text (λ_T) modalities. The framework is model-agnostic and requires no architectural modification or additional inference overhead, simply fine-tuning pre-trained GLIP or FIBER checkpoints for one epoch on the CBDG-augmented dataset.

## Results

On OmniLabel, FIBER-B + CCL achieves 42.0 AP (vs. 25.7 for FIBER-B alone, +16.3 AP) and GLIP-T + CCL reaches 32.2 AP (vs. 19.3, +12.9 AP). On D³, the corresponding gains are +14.9 AP (37.6 vs. 22.7 FULL) for FIBER and +10.9 AP (30.0 vs. 19.1 FULL) for GLIP. Under background-replacement robustness testing (D³_BC), FIBER-B + CCL retains 33.1 FULL (only −4.5 drop) while FIBER-B alone drops to 20.1 (−2.6 drop from its clean score), and under COCO-C-style corruptions (D³_C) the CCL-enhanced model achieves 27.5 mFULL versus 16.7 for the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCL (with FIBER-B) outperforms every compared method on both benchmarks: it surpasses the strongest prior method Real-Model [5] by +5.5 AP on OmniLabel (42.0 vs. 36.5 AP-d) and +3.5 FULL on D³ (37.6 vs. 34.1). Against the GN-FIBER variant [64], the gain is +13.9 AP on OmniLabel and +11.6 FULL on D³. On the robustness benchmark D³_BC, FIBER-B + CCL scores 33.1 FULL versus 20.1 for FIBER-B alone, a 13-point gap that highlights the robustness advantage. Under COCO-C corruptions (D³_C), CCL achieves 27.5 mFULL (rFULL 73.1%) compared to 16.7 mFULL (rFULL 73.6%) for FIBER-B, showing comparable relative degradation but substantially higher absolute performance. The GLIP-T + CCL variant also beats all baselines, reaching 32.2 AP on OmniLabel and 30.0 FULL on D³, confirming the method's model-agnostic nature.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GLIP and FIBER as language-based object detection backbones, but departs from the dominant paradigm of cross-modal (vision-language) alignment by introducing intra-modal consistency as the primary training signal. The CBDG pipeline repurposes SAM for foreground extraction and Stable Diffusion for background synthesis, drawing on the diffusion-model-for-editing literature (GLIDE, GLIGEN, IAM) but applying it to paired data generation rather than direct image editing. The CCLoss is a contrastive objective applied within each modality separately, which contrasts with the cross-modal contrastive losses (e.g., CLIP-style) that have dominated prior OVOD training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underexplored robustness gap—object features drifting across backgrounds—and shows that a simple consistency loss plus generated paired data closes it substantially, with no inference overhead and model-agnostic integration. The dual robustness evaluation (D³_BC for background shifts, D³_C for standard corruptions) strengthens the claim that gains are not merely overfitting to the generated backgrounds. Limitations include dependence on SAM segmentation quality and the fact that backgrounds are generated from three relatively simple prompt families (seasonal, sky, natural landscape), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex or adversarial scene changes.
