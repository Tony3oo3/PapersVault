## Problem

Existing text-conditioned motion in-betweening methods fail to align textual semantics with the implicit spatio-temporal cues encoded in keyframes, producing motions that deviate from the artist's intended low-level timing and pose characteristics. Additionally, they treat keyframes as soft constraints, yielding noticeable spatial deviations that break critical physical relationships such as foot-ground contacts and object grasps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263134/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (tokenized via CLIP and compressed to a fixed-length feature), a sparse set of keyframes represented as a feature matrix K ∈ R^{N×D} with a binary mask m_K indicating which joints are constrained per frame, and optionally an original motion sequence for editing. The output is a 22-joint SMPL-X motion sequence at 30 FPS (60–300 frames) comprising global root translation/rotation, local joint poses with foot-contact labels, and body shape parameters, where keyframe-constrained frames are matched exactly (0 cm error). For editing, the original motion is inverted via DDIM and re-synthesized under new keyframe constraints while preserving source semantics.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-level diffusion framework on a Conv1D U-Net backbone that separates guidance into two complementary levels: local guidance concatenates per-frame keyframe embeddings with noisy motion features to guide transitions around keyframes, while global guidance uses a Keyframe Transformer Encoder to produce a compact sequence-level keyframe feature that is concatenated with the text feature and injected via cross-attention to modulate overall motion dynamics. During inference, a novel trajectory refinement strategy corrects root positions autoregressively between keyframes by distributing position errors proportionally to per-frame root speed (preserving the speed trend that governs foot-contact patterns), followed by diffusion imputation that enforces exact keyframe poses via Hadamard masking at each denoising step. For semantics-preserving editing, the original motion is inverted to latent noise via DDIM and re-denoised under new keyframe constraints using fixed-point iteration, which keeps the denoising path close to the inversion trajectory to retain original action semantics.

## Results

On the HumanML3D test set for keyframe in-betweening, the proposed method achieves 0.000 cm Keyframe Error (perfect spatial adherence), 0.804 SS Similarity, 0.803 R-Precision (Top-3), and 0.023 FID, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, MaskControl, achieves 3.255 cm Keyframe Error, 0.567 SS Similarity, and 0.155 FID, while CondMDI and OmniControl show substantially larger spatial deviations (10.128 and 12.883 cm) and much worse motion quality (FID 0.667 and 7.115). In the partial joint control setting, the method again achieves 0.000 cm Keyframe Error with the best R-Precision (0.813) and MM Dist (2.426). For motion editing, the method attains 0.000 cm Keyframe Error and superior FID (0.284 for pose editing, 0.418 for trajectory editing) compared to DNO (FID 1.798 and 1.812).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines for motion in-betweening (OmniControl, CondMDI, MaskControl, SFControl) and one for motion editing (DNO). The proposed method decisively outperforms all in-betweening baselines on Keyframe Error (0.000 vs. 1.911–12.883 cm), SS Similarity (0.804 vs. 0.449–0.726), and FID (0.023 vs. 0.155–7.115), with the largest gaps over OmniControl and CondMDI. MaskControl is the closest competitor on R-Precision (0.801 vs. 0.803) and Skating Ratio (0.046 vs. 0.043), but still lags significantly on spatial accuracy and semantic alignment. In the partial joint control task, the method beats SFControl (the strongest baseline there) on Keyframe Error (0.000 vs. 6.578), R-Precision (0.813 vs. 0.789), and MM Dist (2.426 vs. 2.606), while matching its Skating Ratio (0.043) and slightly trailing on FID (0.036 vs. 0.032). For motion editing, the method dominates DNO on both pose and trajectory editing: achieving 0.000 cm Keyframe Error (vs. 6.983 and 3.130), higher Source Similarity (0.756 vs. 0.739; 0.886 vs. 0.784), and substantially lower FID (0.284 vs. 1.798; 0.418 vs. 1.812).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-conditioned motion in-betweening line (CondMDI, MaskControl, OmniControl) by addressing two specific failures: treating keyframes as soft rather than hard constraints, and capturing only high-level text semantics while ignoring implicit temporal/spatial cues from keyframe sequences. Architecturally, it builds on the Conv1D U-Net backbone from Guided Motion Diffusion [20] and the x̂₀-prediction formulation from [47], but introduces a novel multi-level guidance mechanism (local keyframe conditioning + global text-and-keyframe-sequence cross-attention) that has no direct precedent in prior controllable motion generation. The editing pipeline repurposes DDIM inversion [39] and diffusion imputation [26] in a training-free invert-and-sample process, drawing on fixed-point iteration ideas from [10, 29].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 0 cm keyframe error while maintaining competitive semantic alignment (SS Similarity 0.804, FID 0.023) addresses a genuine production bottleneck: artists need physically plausible foot-ground contacts and object grasps that prior methods could not guarantee without post-processing. The training-free editing capability lowers the barrier for interactive motion authoring, though it inherits the known limitation that DDIM inversion can drift on long sequences. An open question is how the method behaves when text semantics and keyframe constraints are in genuine conflict (e.g., 'crawl' with large displacement), which the authors acknowledge but do not systematically evaluate. The trajectory refinement's reliance on preserving root speed proportionality is a practical insight that could transfer to other constrained motion synthesis settings.
