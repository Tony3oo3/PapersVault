## Problem

Existing egocentric social perception tasks (e.g., Ego4D's 'Talking to me') identify only momentary speaker-listener relationships over short windows, failing to capture the broader, more stable communication context—i.e., who belongs to the camera wearer's conversation group even when silent or out of direct view. No prior dataset or model addresses this long-term, multi-party social reasoning challenge from first-person video, particularly in scenarios with simultaneous subgroups and varying group sizes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a variable-length tensor of shape (P, T, 68), where P is the number of tracked individuals (zero-padded when out of view) and T is the number of frames; the 68 features per face comprise bounding box coordinates, detection/recognition confidence scores, 20 3D face landmarks, and head pose (yaw, pitch). Output is a per-face, per-frame sigmoid probability of conversation-group membership, shaped (P, T), enabling online inference as new frames arrive. The architecture is permutation-invariant over the person dimension, so individuals can enter or leave the scene without architectural changes.

## Method

CoCoNet factorizes temporal and relational reasoning into two complementary pathways: a per-person causal LSTM (64 hidden units, shared across individuals) preceded by a short 1D temporal convolution (kernel 5) captures each individual's behavioral history, while three stacked self-attention layers (4 heads, skip connections) applied across the person dimension at each timestep model inter-person interaction dynamics. This factorization avoids the quadratic cost of full spatiotemporal attention while preserving temporal continuity per individual. Training employs variable-length input segments (1–4096 frames) and a randomly sampled person dimension (1–9) to encourage generalization across temporal scales and group sizes. With only 431k parameters, the model achieves 96% balanced accuracy and runs in under 0.5 s on CPU for a 5-minute clip, making it suitable for real-time deployment in assistive hearing applications.

## Results

CoCoNet achieves 96% balanced accuracy (bAcc) and 99% mAP on the CCI test set, with 87% temporal bAcc (TbAcc) summarizing performance across segment lengths from 1 frame to ~5 minutes. Performance is robust across group sizes, ranging from 99% bAcc for dyads to 95% for groups of 4–5 and 97% for large groups (6–10). The model also performs well with limited temporal context, reaching 80% bAcc on single-frame inputs, and generalizes to unseen participants (97% bAcc on the unseen test set vs. 95% on matched). With only 431k parameters, CoCoNet runs in under 0.5 s on CPU for a 5-minute clip.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is the LSTM-based affinity predictor from Tan et al. [39], adapted from exocentric group detection to the egocentric CCI setting. CoCoNet outperforms it by 6 points in bAcc (96% vs. 90%), 3 points in mAP (99% vs. 96%), and 6 points in TbAcc (87% vs. 81%). The gap is especially pronounced without temporal context: the affinity predictor drops to 65% bAcc on single frames while CoCoNet maintains 80%, attributed to CoCoNet's self-attention-based inter-person interaction modeling. Against internal baselines, simple spatial cues (center distance, face box size) achieve only 59–61% bAcc, a feature MLP reaches 73%, and ResNet-based visual encoders (face crops or binary masks) reach 64–69%, all substantially below CoCoNet. The paper also shows that training CoCoNet on restricted group-size subsets (only 2–3 or only 4+) generalizes poorly to unseen group sizes (e.g., 27% bAcc when a small-group model is tested on large groups), underscoring that dataset diversity in group configuration is critical for robust CCI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining Communication Context Identification (CCI) as a distinct task from prior egocentric social perception work. It departs from Ego4D's 'Talking to me' and SAAL, which target momentary active-speaker detection over short (sub-second) windows with audio, by formulating a purely visual, long-term (minutes-scale) problem of identifying all conversation group members including silent participants. It also diverges from exocentric F-formation and social group detection literature, which relies on spatial proximity of all individuals in a third-person frame, by being user-centric and leveraging egocentric cues like the wearer's implicit head orientation encoded in face position. The method (CoCoNet) repurposes standard LSTM and self-attention components but applies them in a permutation-invariant person dimension that is novel for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 68.9-hour multi-conversation egocentric dataset with ground-truth labels from experimental design fills a critical gap: prior datasets either contain only a single conversation group, lack non-partner annotations, or are far smaller (5–50 h). The finding that a model trained exclusively on small groups collapses to 27% bAcc on large groups underscores that group-size diversity is essential for generalization, a practical consideration for deploying such systems in real-world settings. The work opens a path toward context-aware hearing aids that enhance all socially relevant voices without requiring the user to orient toward each speaker. Open questions include whether the controlled seated-around-a-table setting generalizes to standing, mobile, or outdoor conversations, and whether audio-visual fusion could further improve robustness in noisy environments.
