## Problem

Current gloss-free sign language translation (GFSLT) models still depend on text annotations during backbone pretraining (e.g., contrastive image-text alignment, CTC with pseudo-glosses), limiting scalability and generalization. The paper asks whether text supervision is truly necessary and whether existing self-supervised learning (SSL) methods can be directly applied to sign videos to produce effective sign features without any textual or gloss supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260645/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes only global sign language video frames (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) as input and produces a spoken-language text sequence via an mBART decoder. During pretraining, the student model additionally receives masked local views—cropped face, hands, or face+hands regions that retain their original spatial resolution with all other pixels zeroed out—while the teacher sees only the full global frame. No skeletons, pose inputs, or text are required at any inference stage.

## Method

SignDINO adapts the DINO self-distillation framework with a sign-aware augmentation scheme: the teacher encoder (DINOv3-Base) processes global sign frames, while the student encoder processes masked local views (face, hands, or both) that preserve original spatial scale rather than being resized to match global views. The student is trained to match the teacher's softmax output distributions (K=65536) via cross-entropy, with teacher parameters updated via EMA (cosine schedule 0.996→1). The key insight is that sign language discriminative cues are concentrated in small local regions (hands, face) while global frames share similar backgrounds, so forcing the student to recover global semantics from these local cues teaches the teacher to attend to sign-relevant features. After pretraining, the teacher weights serve as the frozen SL tokenizer, paired with an mBART translation model fine-tuned under standard cross-entropy loss. The approach is backbone-agnostic (validated on PoolFormer, SwinTransformer, DINOv2, DINOv3) and requires no external sign-language datasets or text annotations during pretraining.

## Results

On Phoenix14T (the primary benchmark), SignDINO achieves ROUGE-L 53.79, BLEU-1 54.15, BLEU-3 33.44, and BLEU-4 27.17 on the test set, outperforming all text-based GFSLT baselines on most metrics. On CSL-Daily, it reaches ROUGE 52.75 and BLEU-4 25.46; on OpenASL, ROUGE 38.65 and BLEU-4 17.46; and on How2Sign, ROUGE 36.14 and BLEU-4 15.47. Notably, SignDINO requires no text annotations during backbone pretraining, no pose input, and no multi-stage pretraining, yet still surpasses methods that rely on all three. The only metric where it trails is BLEU-2 on Phoenix14T (38.40 vs. 40.55 for PGG-SLT and 40.20 for C2RL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SignDINO outperforms all 10 text-based GFSLT baselines on Phoenix14T in ROUGE-L (53.79 vs. best 51.85 for PGG-SLT), BLEU-1 (54.15 vs. 53.45), BLEU-3 (33.44 vs. 32.65), and BLEU-4 (27.17 vs. 26.85), while trailing only on BLEU-2 (38.40 vs. 40.55 for PGG-SLT and 40.20 for C2RL). On CSL-Daily, SignDINO leads in BLEU-2 (39.64), BLEU-3 (33.73), and BLEU-4 (25.46) over MixSignGraph (36.91/27.54/20.78) and LLaVA-SLT (36.24/26.47/20.42), though PGG-SLT edges it slightly on ROUGE (53.29 vs. 52.75). On OpenASL, SignDINO achieves a large margin over C2RL (ROUGE 38.65 vs. 31.36; BLEU-4 17.46 vs. 13.21) and MixSignGraph (ROUGE 38.65 vs. 25.71; BLEU-4 17.46 vs. 8.69). On How2Sign, SignDINO surpasses the previous SOTA PGG-SLT by +4.64 ROUGE (36.14 vs. 31.50) and +2.37 BLEU-4 (15.47 vs. 13.10), and outperforms MixSignGraph by +8.13 ROUGE and +5.06 BLEU-4. The key differentiator is that SignDINO achieves these results without any text supervision during pretraining, without pose/skeleton inputs, and without multi-stage training, whereas all competing methods rely on at least one of these auxiliary signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO self-distillation framework (Caron et al., ICCV 2021) to sign language by introducing a sign-aware data augmentation scheme where the student model receives masked local views (hands, face) while the teacher receives global frames. It departs from the dominant GFSLT paradigm that relies on text-based pretraining (contrastive image-text alignment, pseudo-gloss CTC, multi-stage text conditioning) by eliminating text supervision entirely from the pretraining stage. It also addresses a key limitation of prior DINO-based sign language work (SHuBERT, SignMusketeers), which restricted inference to localized hand/face crops rather than full global frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing text dependency from pretraining improves scalability for sign language, where collecting text annotations is costly and limits dataset coverage. The simplicity of the approach (global frames at inference, no pose/skeleton inputs) makes it practical for deployment. However, cross-dataset transfer shows a notable performance drop (ROUGE 41.83 vs. 53.79 in-domain), suggesting the learned representations may still be sensitive to signer and dataset distribution, and the LoRA constraint on base-sized models limits the generality of the architectural findings. An open question is whether the sign-aware local-to-global distillation generalizes to truly large-scale unlabeled sign video corpora beyond the four benchmark datasets tested.
