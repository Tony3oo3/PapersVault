## Problem

Conventional WSI analysis pipelines use a two-stage process (offline tile encoding followed by MIL aggregation) that decouples the tile encoder from slide-level context, discards original receptive-field and spatial-continuity information, and produces task-dependent slide representations. Training a true end-to-end WSI model from scratch is infeasible due to prohibitive computational cost at gigapixel resolution and insufficient WSI-level pre-training data. The paper asks whether pre-trained tile-level ViTs can be directly repurposed as end-to-end WSI models without retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

The method ingests a WSI at a chosen magnification, applies tissue masking (OTSU or GrandQC) to remove background, then crops non-overlapping H×H tiles (H must be a multiple of the ViT kernel size P) and concatenates them into a single long-strip image of shape 3×H×(H·N). The output is a slide-level embedding derived from the transformer backbone's CLS token or patch-merged token sequence, which serves either as a survival risk score (via a downstream classifier) or as a task-agnostic representation for linear probing. The patch-merger allows flexible control over output sequence length to match downstream task or hardware constraints.

## Method

E2E-ViT converts a vanilla pre-trained ViT into an end-to-end WSI model with three parameter-free modifications. First, the WSI is fed as a continuous long-strip image directly into the ViT's non-overlapping convolutional patch-embedding layer, preserving the original spatial organization and enabling the transformer to model cross-tile interactions from the raw image. Second, a patch-merger module compresses the extremely long token sequence (N×(H/P)² tokens) via mean pooling over groups of tokens, reducing inference cost while maintaining consistency with the pre-trained patch-token feature space. Third, learnable absolute positional embeddings are replaced with ALiBi, a parameter-free relative positional encoding that applies distance-based attention biases, improving extrapolation to the much longer sequences encountered at WSI resolution. The key insight is that pre-trained ViTs have already learned cross-patch interaction priors that remain effective at higher resolutions (validated via attention heatmap consistency across resolutions), so the original weights transfer directly to slide-level inputs without any additional trainable parameters.

## Results

Under full-parameter fine-tuning, E2E-ViT achieves overall C-index of 0.6667 (ViT-Small), 0.6978 (CONCH), and 0.7158 (H0-mini) across five survival prediction datasets, consistently outperforming all two-stage MIL baselines. Under linear probing (frozen backbone), E2E H0-mini reaches 0.6685 overall C-index, surpassing all vision-only SFMs (best: CHIEF at 0.6386) and both vision-language SFMs (TITAN 0.6582, PRISM 0.6458). E2E H0-mini also achieves the fastest inference, generating slide-level features in under one second—2.64× faster than CHIEF and 7.49× faster than FEATHER at 10,000 tiles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2E-ViT outperforms all seven two-stage MIL baselines (ABMIL, CLAM, DSMIL, TransMIL, WiKG, RRTMIL, 2DMamba) across every backbone and dataset under fine-tuning. The margin is largest for H0-mini, where E2E H0-mini (0.7158) beats the best MIL (2DMamba, 0.6810) by 0.0348 overall C-index, and for ViT-Small, where E2E ViT-Small (0.6667) surpasses the best MIL (TransMIL, 0.6386) by 0.0281. Under linear probing against seven SFMs, E2E H0-mini (0.6685) outperforms all vision-only SFMs (CHIEF 0.6386, GigaPath 0.6229, MADELEINE 0.6378, HIPT 0.5905, FEATHER 0.6152) and both vision-language SFMs (TITAN 0.6582, PRISM 0.6458). E2E CONCH (0.6534) beats all vision-only SFMs but trails TITAN by 0.0048. E2E ViT-Small (0.5959) underperforms most SFMs overall but still surpasses HIPT (0.5905) and FEATHER (0.6152) on individual datasets, demonstrating that end-to-end slide-level learning can partially compensate for weaker tile-level pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage WSI analysis paradigm (offline tile encoding followed by MIL aggregation) and from Slide Foundation Models (SFM) that pre-train a slide encoder on top of frozen tile features. It builds on the ViT architecture and the ALiBi relative positional encoding, but its core contribution is a parameter-free conversion strategy that exploits the sequence extrapolation capability of pre-trained ViTs to directly process entire tissue regions as long-strip inputs. Conceptually, it repurposes tile-level pre-training priors for slide-level modeling without any architectural additions, positioning itself as a bridge between the tile-encoder world and true end-to-end WSI representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E2E-ViT offers a practical, drop-in conversion that eliminates the need for task-specific MIL training while producing task-independent slide representations, and it does so with faster inference than existing SFMs. However, the performance gains over the strongest MIL baselines (e.g., CONCH+2DMamba) are modest, and the approach has only been validated on five survival prediction datasets with relatively small sample sizes. Open questions include whether the strategy scales gracefully to the largest pathology ViTs (the authors note sensitivity to perturbations in large models), whether multi-scale mechanisms can be integrated, and whether the end-to-end paradigm generalizes beyond survival prediction to broader clinical tasks such as classification and segmentation.
