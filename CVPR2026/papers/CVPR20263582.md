## Problem

Existing video generation and understanding methods model dynamics directly from 2D pixel observations, which loses critical 3D geometric structure and entangles camera motion with scene dynamics. This leads to physically implausible motion, poor handling of occlusions and viewpoint changes, and degraded performance in out-of-distribution scenarios. The paper asks whether explicitly reconstructing and learning continuous dynamics in the native 4D world (3D space + time) before re-projecting to 2D can yield more physically consistent and geometrically faithful visual generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263582/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of monocular video frames (5–20 frames at 960×540 resolution) as input, optionally augmented with a text prompt for editing tasks. It produces temporally unseen frames (past, interpolated, or future), spatially unseen novel-viewpoint videos under canonical camera motions (dolly, tilt, pan), and edited videos (object removal, replacement, time-lapse). The 4D re-projection yields a geometric scaffold that may contain holes or low-confidence regions, which are then completed by the video generator.

## Method

SeeU follows a three-stage 2D→4D→2D pipeline. Stage 1 (2D→4D) reconstructs a dynamic scene as 80k canonical 3D Gaussians with per-frame rigid transforms, using MegaSaM for camera/depth, Track-Anything for foreground segmentation, and TAPIR for 2D point tracking, building on Shape-of-Motion's low-rank motion parameterization. Stage 2 (Discrete 4D→Continuous 4D) introduces the C4DD model, which represents both camera and foreground motion as cubic B-spline curves with 8 shared control points (K≪N basis functions), trained with a hybrid loss combining data fidelity to discrete observations and a physics-informed penalty on second-order temporal derivatives (acceleration) that is weighted more heavily in extrapolated time intervals. Stage 3 (4D→2D) evolves the 4D scene to target timestamps, re-projects to 2D as a video scaffold encoding geometry and occlusion structure, and feeds it alongside VLM-derived captions and per-frame inpainting masks into a fine-tuned VACE video diffusion model that fills in unseen or uncertain regions with temporally coherent appearance.

## Results

On the SEEU45 benchmark, SeeU achieves the best performance across all three temporal generation sub-tasks: past frame inference (PSNR 20.47, SSIM 0.550, LPIPS 0.248), dynamic frame interpolation (PSNR 21.07, SSIM 0.572, LPIPS 0.227), and future frame prediction (PSNR 20.54, SSIM 0.558, LPIPS 0.243). For spatially unseen generation across five canonical camera motions, SeeU achieves the lowest epipolar error (EE ≈ 0.19–0.21) and highest epipolar inlier ratio (EIR ≈ 0.78–0.82) and CLIP-V scene consistency (≈ 0.95–0.97), outperforming both GCD and ReCamMaster. The C-LPIPS cross-frame consistency metric (where values closer to the reference ~0.036–0.039 are better) confirms SeeU's temporal coherence, with values of 0.033–0.036 across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeeU outperforms all baselines on every reported metric across both temporal and spatial generation tasks. Against the closest 4D-reconstruction baseline SoM [94], SeeU gains +4.9 dB PSNR on past inference, +4.7 dB on interpolation, and +5.1 dB on future prediction, with substantially lower LPIPS (0.248 vs 0.388 for past). Against the video-generation baseline VACE [38], SeeU improves PSNR by 3.3–2.9 dB and reduces LPIPS by ~0.11–0.12, while also achieving far better cross-frame consistency (C-LPIPS 0.033–0.036 vs 0.092–0.182). For future prediction specifically, SeeU beats Cosmos [70] by +0.47 dB PSNR and −0.046 LPIPS, and beats Wan2.2 [88] by +2.24 dB PSNR and −0.013 LPIPS. On spatial generation (Table 2), SeeU achieves the lowest epipolar error (0.190–0.208) and highest EIR (0.784–0.823) across all five camera motions, surpassing ReCamMaster [1] by ~0.02–0.04 in EE and ~0.08–0.11 in EIR, and outperforming GCD [86] (which only supports dolly-out) by a similar margin. The one near-tie is C-LPIPS on future prediction, where Cosmos achieves 0.0355 vs SeeU's 0.0358, both essentially at the reference level.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeeU departs from the dominant paradigm of modeling video dynamics directly in 2D pixel or latent space by introducing a 2D→4D→2D pipeline: it first reconstructs a 4D scene using 3D Gaussian Splatting with Shape-of-Motion's low-rank motion parameterization, then learns continuous dynamics via B-spline curves with physics-informed regularization (C4DD), and finally re-projects to 2D for a VACE-based in-context video generator to fill in appearance. This positions the work at the intersection of dynamic 3D reconstruction (NeRF/3DGS lineage), physics-informed learning, and video diffusion, but the key conceptual departure is treating 4D dynamics as an explicit intermediate representation rather than an implicit property of a 2D sequence model. Unlike prior physics-aware generation methods that either simulate-then-generate or distill physics priors into a 2D generator, SeeU infers deterministic 4D trajectories first and uses them as a geometric scaffold for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2D→4D→2D framing offers a principled path toward physically grounded video generation that can extrapolate (not merely interpolate) in time and generalize to novel viewpoints, addressing a known failure mode of pure 2D sequence models. However, the approach inherits fragility from its upstream modules (MegaSaM camera estimation, TAPIR tracking, 3DGS reconstruction), and the authors explicitly limit scope to scenes with smooth, temporally stable foreground motion, leaving non-rigid deformation, large occlusions, and long-horizon prediction open. The SEEU45 benchmark and the C4DD module (B-spline + physics loss) are reusable components that could inform future 4D-aware generation systems, but broader applicability will depend on improvements in the underlying tracking and reconstruction pipelines.
