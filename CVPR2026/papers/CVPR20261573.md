## Problem

Existing LiDAR OOD detection methods rely on static scoring functions that assume roughly uniform class distributions, yet real LiDAR scenes exhibit per-class point counts varying by several orders of magnitude, causing scores to overfit dominant classes and fail on tail categories. Additionally, generating diverse auxiliary OOD samples for LiDAR is non-trivial because point clouds are sparse and irregular, and void-class points from existing datasets are too limited and repetitive to serve as reliable OOD supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single LiDAR frame as a set of 3D points with associated labels, processed through a multi-scale sparse UNet encoder and a Mask4Former-3D transformer decoder. It produces two parallel outputs: a per-point OOD confidence score (derived from reweighted energy/entropy over 2K logit channels for the Extended Energy variant) that is thresholded into binary ID/OOD labels, and a closed-set panoptic segmentation mask for in-distribution classes. The OOD score is a scalar per point, while the segmentation output follows standard panoptic format with instance and stuff masks.

## Method

The Neural Distribution Prior (NDP) projects each point's logit vector into a latent embedding and performs cross-attention against a learnable prior matrix ψ ∈ R^{K×d}, producing a scalar reweighting term W(f_Θ, ψ) that multiplicatively calibrates any base OOD score (entropy, energy, or the proposed Extended Energy) according to alignment with the learned class-distribution manifold. For OOD training data, a Perlin Raise algorithm samples flat road patches, applies spatially coherent fractal noise to elevate a subset of points, and labels the largest DBSCAN cluster as synthetic OOD, providing diverse geometric perturbations without external datasets. A Soft Outlier Exposure (SOE) loss assigns a soft target β=0.9 to void-region points rather than hard OOD labels, preventing overfitting to repetitive background structures. The total training objective combines standard cross-entropy for ID points, a binary classification loss on Perlin-generated OOD samples, and the SOE soft-regression term, all operating on the NDP-reweighted score with a trainable bias.

## Results

On the STU test set, NDP-EE achieves a point-level AP of 61.31% and AUROC of 99.26%, representing a >10× improvement over the previous best AP of 5.17% (Deep Ensemble) and reducing FPR@95 from 58.05% to 3.30%. At the object level, NDP-EE attains a Panoptic Quality (PQ) of 24.99%, nearly 2× the previous best of 8.81%. On SemanticKITTI outlier classes, NDP-EE reaches 70.12% AP and 98.01% AUROC, outperforming all prior LiDAR OOD methods. In-distribution panoptic segmentation is preserved, with PQ of 52.37 on STU and 59.38 on SemanticKITTI, closely matching the closed-set Mask4Former baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDP-EE dominates all STU test-set baselines by large margins: it surpasses Deep Ensemble (AP 5.17→61.31, FPR@95 58.05→3.30), MaxLogit (AP 0.95→61.31), Void Classifier (AP 3.92→61.31), RbA (AP 0.81→61.31), and MC Dropout (AP 0.11→61.31). On SemanticKITTI outlier classes (Table 3), NDP-EE (AP 70.12) outperforms UEM (61.73), RbA (55.23), MaxLogit (53.86), LiON (44.68), APF (36.10), and REAL (20.08). Against the statistical class-prior reweighting method [29] (AP 23.87) and Balanced Energy [16] (AP 57.05) under the same energy-based scoring protocol, NDP-Energy achieves 66.54% AP, confirming that a learnable prior substantially outperforms fixed statistical corrections under severe LiDAR class imbalance. For in-distribution segmentation, Mask4Former-NDP (PQ 52.37 on STU, 59.38 on SemanticKITTI) closely matches the closed-set Mask4Former (52.73, 60.72), showing that OOD training does not degrade standard segmentation, while the void-classification variant [4] drops to PQ 26.96/47.97.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Mask4Former-3D architecture for 3D panoptic segmentation and extends the energy-based OOD scoring framework by introducing a learnable Neural Distribution Prior (NDP) that adaptively reweights static OOD scores through cross-attention with a class-level prior matrix. It departs from prior Outlier Exposure training by proposing Soft Outlier Exposure (SOE), which assigns soft labels to unreliable void regions rather than treating them as hard OOD samples, and replaces external OOD datasets with Perlin noise-based geometric perturbation of inlier scans. The Extended Energy formulation extends the dual-logit energy score from recent work by explicitly modeling both ID and OOD logit channels within a single scoring function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a critical safety gap in autonomous driving where closed-set LiDAR models silently misclassify road hazards as known classes, and the modular design (compatible with entropy, energy, and extended-energy scores) makes it broadly applicable to existing OOD pipelines. The 10× AP improvement over prior SOTA on STU is striking, though partly attributable to the weakness of earlier baselines on this new benchmark. Open questions include whether the learnable prior generalizes to multimodal (camera+LiDAR) settings, whether temporal context can further refine boundary precision, and whether the Perlin synthesis strategy transfers to other 3D sensing modalities.
