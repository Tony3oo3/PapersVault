## Problem

VLA models trained on mixed-quality human demonstrations inherit the full behavioral distribution, producing variable execution quality where tasks may succeed but with imprecise placement, premature releases, or unintended collisions. Existing refinement approaches either reshape the static dataset or require invasive fine-tuning, leaving no lightweight mechanism to selectively improve how actions are performed at inference time without retraining the base policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262031/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes RGB-D visual observations, a natural-language task instruction, and robot proprioceptive state at each timestep. The base flow-matching VLA policy generates stochastic candidate actions, and the Elegance Critic (built on a frozen VLM backbone with a lightweight refinement head) scores them. Output is a single refined action per timestep: most timesteps pass through the default policy action unchanged, while at detected critical moments the system returns the highest-Q-value action among N sampled candidates.

## Method

The framework decouples execution from evaluation in three stages: (1) a flow-matching generative VLA policy is trained on mixed-quality demonstrations to capture the full behavioral distribution; (2) an Elegance Critic is trained via Calibrated Q-Learning (Cal-QL) on the LIBERO-Elegant benchmark, where binary rewards are assigned over 25-frame windows encoding Implicit Task Constraint satisfaction, with the Cal-QL regularizer preventing value overestimation on out-of-distribution actions; (3) at inference, the Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) mechanism monitors the critic's Q-value fluctuation Δq_t = |q_t − q̄_t| over a sliding window, and only when Δq_t exceeds a threshold τ does it trigger multi-sample evaluation—sampling N candidate actions from the policy and executing the one with the highest predicted elegance. This event-driven design reduces critic interventions by over 60% compared to full-guidance while achieving higher Elegant Success Rate, and the critic generalizes zero-shot to unseen semantically similar tasks.

## Results

On the LIBERO-Elegant benchmark (8 manipulation tasks, 50 rollouts each), the JITI-guided Elegance Critic achieves an average Elegant Success Rate (ESR) of 67.2% with both SmolVLA and GR00T N1.5 base policies, substantially outperforming all baselines. The largest per-task gains appear on T-7 (92% vs. 72% for SmolVLA base) and T-0 (86% vs. 70%). In real-world validation on a SO-100 arm across six tasks, ESR improves from 34.3% (base) to 58.0% (+23.7 pts).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The JITI-guided Elegance Critic outperforms all three baseline VLA models on average ESR across the eight LIBERO-Elegant tasks. Against π₀.5 (44.2% avg ESR), the method achieves 67.2%, a +23.0-point gain; against Isaac GR00T N1 (40.2%), the gain is +27.0 points. Compared to the two base policies the critic is directly paired with—SmolVLA (49.8%) and GR00T N1.5 (46.0%)—the JITI mechanism adds +17.4 and +21.2 points respectively, confirming its plug-and-play, model-agnostic nature. Notably, the method wins on 7 out of 8 tasks with SmolVLA and 6 out of 8 with GR00T N1.5, with the sole losses being marginal (e.g., T-2: 42% vs. 42% tie for SmolVLA; T-1: 36% vs. 22% for GR00T N1.5 where the base already underperforms). In the real-world setting, the same framework lifts ESR from 34.3% to 58.0% on the SO-100 arm, validating that the gains are not simulation-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the decoupled inference-time guidance paradigm for VLA models, building directly on Cal-QL (Nakamoto et al., NeurIPS 2023) for offline critic training and extending the broader offline RL literature (CQL, IQL) to a quality-aware objective. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing the LIBERO-Elegant benchmark with explicit Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs) that make execution quality measurable beyond binary success, and (2) proposing the JITI mechanism, an event-driven intervention triggered by Q-value fluctuation rather than continuous multi-sample guidance. The flow-matching generative policy (SmolVLA) provides the stochastic action diversity that makes value-guided selection possible, while the critic architecture reuses frozen VLM backbones (SmolVLM2, Eagle2) in a modular, non-invasive fashion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LIBERO-Elegant benchmark and the Elegant Success Rate metric offer the community a standardized way to evaluate manipulation quality beyond pass/fail, addressing a gap where all LIBERO demonstrations are labeled 'successful' yet vary substantially in execution quality. The JITI mechanism's 60% reduction in interventions relative to full guidance, combined with its plug-and-play compatibility across architectures (SmolVLA 450M and GR00T N1.5 3B), makes it a practical tool for deploying VLA policies with quality guarantees. Open questions include whether the elegance concept generalizes beyond the 8 curated tasks and 327-episode dataset, how the method scales to longer-horizon or multi-object manipulation, and whether the Q-value fluctuation trigger remains reliable under the compounding distribution shift of real-world deployment.
