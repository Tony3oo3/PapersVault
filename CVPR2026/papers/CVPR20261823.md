## Problem

GRPO-based post-training for visual generative models treats all reward signals as equally reliable, but textual-visual correspondence is inherently many-to-many: a single prompt admits diverse valid outputs and a single image or video supports multiple equally correct descriptions. This ambiguity causes reward models to emit uncertain, weakly discriminative signals, leading GRPO to underutilize reliable feedback and overfit noisy ones, resulting in semantically mismatched generations despite visually plausible outputs.

## Input / Output

For text-to-video and text-to-image tasks, the method consumes a text prompt and generates a video or image respectively; for image-to-video, it additionally takes a reference conditioning frame. During training, G candidate outputs are sampled per prompt (G=8 for video, G=12 for image), scored by task-specific reward models (VideoCLIP-XL for video, HPSv2 for image), and a semantic prior R_prior is maintained per task—derived from SFT-model generations for T2V, the conditioning frame's text-alignment for I2V, or a running mean of group rewards for T2I. Evaluation uses VideoAlign and Qwen3-VL Embedding for video alignment, and PickScore and ImageReward for image quality.

## Method

BPGO extends GRPO with two complementary modules under a unified Bayesian framework. Reliability-Adaptive Scaling (RAS) computes a smooth, differentiable trust weight w_i = 1 + α[2σ(k(R̄_i − R_prior)) − 1] per prompt group, amplifying gradients from groups whose mean reward exceeds the semantic prior and softly down-weighting ambiguous groups, thereby reallocating learning capacity toward trustworthy semantic regions. Contrastive Reward Transformation (CRT) reshapes individual rewards within each group via R̃_i^j = [λ(R_i^j − R_prior) + 1_{R_i^j > R_prior}] exp(R_i^j), geometrically stretching confident deviations from the prior while compressing uncertain ones, and feeds the transformed rewards into an auxiliary GRPO loss. The final objective L_BPGO = (1/N) Σ w_i · L_GRPO({R_i^j}) + β · L_CRT^(i) combines macro-level group trust modulation with micro-level intra-group discriminability enhancement, implementing a hierarchical Bayesian adjustment that corrects location bias at the group level and refines signal contrast at the sample level without introducing a separate uncertainty estimator.

## Results

On text-to-video (Wan2.1), BPGO improves VideoAlign-TA from 0.8984 (GRPO) to 1.1193 (+24.6%) and VideoAlign-overall from −0.5411 to −0.0478, while also raising VideoCLIP-XL from 2.6714 to 2.6788. On image-to-video (Wan2.2), BPGO recovers the severe degradation caused by GRPO (VideoCLIP-XL 2.0713 → 2.6855; VA-TA 0.2307 → 1.0589). On text-to-image (FLUX), BPGO achieves the best PickScore (0.2288 vs. 0.2242) and ImageReward (1.2136 vs. 1.0607), though it trails GRPO slightly on HPSv2 (0.2434 vs. 0.2564).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is DanceGRPO (re-implemented as GRPO†). On text-to-video, BPGO wins on all four metrics: VideoCLIP-XL (2.6788 vs. 2.6714), VA-TA (1.1193 vs. 0.8984, +24.6%), VA-overall (−0.0478 vs. −0.5411), and Qwen3-VL embedding (0.6754 vs. 0.6722). On image-to-video, the gap is far larger—GRPO actually degrades the base model substantially (VideoCLIP-XL drops from 2.6726 to 2.0713), while BPGO restores and exceeds the base (2.6855), with VA-TA recovering from 0.2307 to 1.0589. On text-to-image, BPGO outperforms GRPO on PickScore (0.2288 vs. 0.2242) and ImageReward (1.2136 vs. 1.0607, +14.4%), but trails on HPSv2 (0.2434 vs. 0.2564), which the authors attribute to reward saturation at higher quality levels. Overall, BPGO is the clear winner on video tasks and competitive on image tasks, with the only loss being a modest HPSv2 gap on T2I.

## Relation to Prior Work

BPGO extends the GRPO framework (originally from DeepSeekMath) as adapted to visual generation by DanceGRPO, which established the first unified GRPO pipeline across diffusion and flow-matching models. The key departure is treating reward signals as uncertain rather than uniformly reliable, motivated by the many-to-many nature of textual-visual correspondence. The two proposed mechanisms—Reliability-Adaptive Scaling (a sigmoid-based group reweighting) and Contrastive Reward Transformation (prior-anchored reward stretching)—draw on empirical Bayes shrinkage and trust-region ideas from RL, but are applied here specifically to modulate GRPO's advantage estimation. Unlike prior GRPO variants that focus on architectural or sampling-level changes, BPGO operates purely at the reward-processing level, leaving the underlying diffusion/flow model untouched.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practical and underappreciated failure mode in RL post-training for visual generation: reward models produce weakly discriminative signals precisely where textual-visual alignment is most ambiguous, causing GRPO to underfit reliable feedback and overfit noise. The hierarchical prior-guided approach is lightweight (no additional networks) and task-agnostic in principle, making it a drop-in modification for existing GRPO pipelines. However, the prior must be set per task (SFT rewards, first-frame alignment, or running mean), and the added hyperparameters (α, β, k, λ) require tuning; the paper does not explore automatic prior learning. Open questions include whether the Bayesian framing offers genuine theoretical guarantees beyond the heuristic reweighting it implements, and whether the moderate gains observed here persist at larger model scales or with more sophisticated reward models.
