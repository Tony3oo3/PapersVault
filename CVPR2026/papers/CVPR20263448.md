## Problem

Existing Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) methods achieve strong results on coarse-grained benchmarks but degrade significantly on fine-grained datasets (CUB, Stanford Cars, FGVC-Aircraft). The root cause is twofold: ViT backbones exhibit attention artifacts—abnormally high-norm tokens that bias the model toward global semantics—and conventional GCD pipelines rely exclusively on the [CLS] token for classification, discarding the discriminative local cues embedded in the remaining token sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263448/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on RGB images encoded by a ViT-B/16 backbone, receiving a labeled set of known classes and an unlabeled set containing both base and novel categories. Rather than consuming only the [CLS] token as in standard GCD pipelines, TAR processes the full token sequence (196 patch tokens for 224×224 input) and outputs a refined [CLS] token that feeds into the prototypical classifier. The framework is plug-and-play, demonstrated on both a unimodal baseline (MOS) and a multimodal CLIP-based baseline (GET) that additionally leverages text embeddings.

## Method

TAR comprises three sequential modules. The Token Reweighting Module (TRM) is non-parametric: it computes z-score-normalized L2 norms of each patch token, maps them through a sigmoid to assign lower weights to high-norm artifact tokens, and retains only the top-M tokens by weight. The Consistency-Aware Token Sampling (CATS) module then computes cosine similarities between the [CLS] token and each reweighted token via learnable projection matrices, producing a softmax weight distribution π; a KL-divergence term against a uniform prior prevents π from collapsing onto few tokens, and a symmetric-KL consistency loss enforces invariance under Gaussian perturbation. Finally, the Gate Refinement Module (GRM) aggregates reweighted tokens through a weighted projection, applies a learnable sigmoid gate (with parameters α, β) to control injection strength, and adds the result residually to the [CLS] token. The total training objective augments the standard GCD loss with the KL and consistency regularization terms, requiring no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Results

On the three generic fine-grained benchmarks, GET+TAR achieves 78.4% All accuracy on CUB, 80.4% on Stanford Cars, and 60.3% on FGVC-Aircraft, improving over the GET baseline by +1.4%, +1.9%, and +1.4% respectively. The unimodal MOS+TAR variant reaches 70.6% on CUB, 65.7% on Stanford Cars, and 62.0% on FGVC-Aircraft, gaining +1.0%, +1.1%, and +0.9% over MOS. On the more challenging Herbarium 19 and ImageNet-1K datasets, GET+TAR attains 49.8% and 63.1% All accuracy, outperforming all prior methods including SimGCD (44.0%/57.1%) and GCD (35.4%/52.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAR is a plug-and-play module evaluated by augmenting two strong baselines: the unimodal MOS and the multimodal GET. GET+TAR achieves the best All accuracy across all three fine-grained benchmarks (78.4% CUB, 80.4% Stanford Cars, 60.3% FGVC-Aircraft), surpassing the previous best GET by +1.4%, +1.9%, and +1.4% respectively, and outperforming all other baselines by a wide margin (e.g., +12.6% over SPTNET on CUB, +21.4% over GCD on Stanford Cars). MOS+TAR similarly improves over MOS on every dataset, with the largest gain on Stanford Cars Base (+1.5%) and FGVC Base (+2.2%). On the harder Herbarium 19 and ImageNet-1K benchmarks, GET+TAR edges out GET by +0.1% and +0.7% All accuracy, while maintaining a clear lead over SimGCD (+5.8% and +6.0%) and GCD (+14.4% and +10.6%). Notably, TAR consistently yields larger improvements on Base classes than Novel classes (e.g., +4.3% Base vs +0.7% Novel on Stanford Cars with GET), suggesting that artifact suppression primarily benefits discrimination among already-seen categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GCD task formulation and prototypical classification pipeline established by Vaze et al. (CVPR 2022), and on the attention-artifact analysis of Darcet et al. (2023), who showed that high-norm outlier tokens distort ViT representations. However, it departs from both the register-token remedy proposed by Darcet et al. and the standard GCD practice of feeding only the [CLS] token into the classifier: TAR instead performs non-parametric token reweighting (z-score + sigmoid + Top-M selection) followed by consistency-aware sampling and gated refinement over the full token sequence. As a plug-and-play module, it is designed to slot into existing GCD backbones (MOS, GET) without altering their training objectives, positioning it as an orthogonal refinement layer rather than a competing framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that attention artifacts disproportionately harm fine-grained GCD—where discriminative cues are local and subtle—provides a concrete diagnostic for why ViT-based GCD methods plateau on datasets like CUB and FGVC-Aircraft. The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for adoption, but the per-dataset sensitivity of the Top-M ratio (0.33–0.67) and the modest absolute gains (+1–2% All accuracy) limit immediate practical impact. Open questions include whether the non-parametric TRM generalizes to deeper or larger ViT variants, whether the consistency-aware sampling principle transfers to other token-aggregation tasks (e.g., dense prediction, retrieval), and whether learned register tokens and TAR are complementary or redundant.
