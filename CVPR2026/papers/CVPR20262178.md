## Problem

Single-image 3D head reconstruction is inherently ambiguous, and existing methods either degrade severely at extreme viewpoints due to weak multi-view supervision or rely on low-level losses (L1, LPIPS, SSIM) that produce noisy signals in high-frequency regions like hair. The paper addresses both gaps: achieving robust 3D consistency across arbitrary camera angles and replacing conventional pixel-space supervision with deep perceptual signals from foundation vision models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262178/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, a single RGB image is encoded into patch-level features that drive a 3D Gaussian head (16 Gaussians per FLAME patch, anchored to 65k upsampled FLAME vertices), rendered at any camera pose via the Gaussian Splatting rasterizer and refined by a sharpening CNN. For editing, the image input is replaced by a 19-channel FaRL segmentation map (controlling geometry) and a single CLIP embedding (controlling style, from either a reference image or a zero-shot text prompt), with disentanglement emerging without additional architectural changes.

## Method

The architecture is a modified MAE ViT with a dual-branch 2D encoder that concatenates features from a trained ViT and multi-layer DINOv2 (layers 8 and 11), cross-attending into a 3D latent initialized from a FLAME template over 10 decoding layers without self-attention. The key novelty is a perceptual loss that replaces all conventional image-space losses: it compares normalized DINOv2 class and patch tokens (capturing semantics and high-frequency structure) and SAM 2.1 intermediate image encodings (capturing fine segmentation-level detail) between rendered and ground-truth views, using only the distilled small variants (67M parameters total). Training mixes multi-view data (NeRSemble, Cafca) for 3D consistency with a 3k subset of FFHQ for in-the-wild robustness. The editing model reuses the reconstruction decoder, swapping only the encoder to accept the segmentation map and CLIP embedding, then fine-tunes on the same multi-view data excluding synthetic Cafca samples.

## Results

On the unseen Ava-256 benchmark, PercHead achieves the best PSNR (16.4 dB novel, 15.9 dB extreme), LPIPS (0.269 novel, 0.291 extreme), DreamSim (0.092 novel, 0.106 extreme), and ArcFace identity distance (0.292 novel, 0.282 extreme) across all six baselines. The advantage is most pronounced under extreme-view conditions, where the best baseline (PanoHead) degrades by +11% LPIPS, +23% DreamSim, and +16% ArcFace, while PercHead remains robust. GAGAvatar achieves slightly higher SSIM (0.743 vs. 0.691 novel), but PercHead leads on every other metric, particularly those aligned with human perceptual quality and identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PercHead outperforms all five main baselines (LGM, PanoHead, SphereHead, GAGAvatar, LAM) on PSNR, LPIPS, DreamSim, and ArcFace in both novel-view and extreme-view settings. GAGAvatar is the closest competitor, achieving slightly higher SSIM (0.743 vs. 0.691 novel; 0.694 vs. 0.678 extreme) but trailing PercHead by 0.5 dB PSNR, 0.005 LPIPS, 0.022 DreamSim, and 0.056 ArcFace in novel views, with the gap widening substantially in extreme views (GAGAvatar drops to 13.5 dB PSNR vs. PercHead's 15.9). LGM, a diffusion-based multi-view Gaussian model, performs worst overall (10.5 dB PSNR novel, 9.9 dB extreme), confirming that diffusion priors struggle with the high fidelity required for head synthesis. PanoHead and SphereHead, both GAN-based 360° methods, show moderate performance but exhibit mirroring artifacts and 3D-consistency failures qualitatively. LAM, a transformer-based Gaussian avatar model, degrades most severely under extreme views (11.4 dB PSNR, 0.568 ArcFace), indicating its dual-lifting strategy limits view-independence. FaceLift is referenced for supplementary comparison only (Sec. C.1) and is not included in the main table.

## Relation to Prior Work

PercHead builds on the ViT-based 3D head generation paradigm introduced by LAM [28], which already used DINOv2 features as encoder input, and on 3D Gaussian Splatting [32] as the rendering representation. Its key departure is in the training objective: rather than relying on adversarial losses (as in GAN-based methods like PanoHead and SphereHead) or conventional perceptual losses (LPIPS, SSIM, L1), it replaces all supervision with multi-layer DINOv2 and SAM 2.1 feature comparisons, drawing on the deep visual understanding of foundation models. The architecture itself is a modification of MAE's ViT [27] with a dual-branch 2D encoder, and the editing extension repurposes the same decoder with FaRL segmentation maps and CLIP embeddings as inputs, following the disentangled-control philosophy of prior 3D editing work like ClipFace and NFE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most broadly relevant contribution is the demonstration that foundation-model-based perceptual losses can fully replace conventional image-space losses for 3D reconstruction training, challenging the near-universal assumption that some pixel-level or low-level signal is necessary. This is a drop-in replacement that practitioners in other 3D reconstruction or generative tasks could adopt. However, the method has clear limitations: no expression transfer, no temporal consistency (causing flickering in video), and baked-in lighting that prevents relighting. Open questions include whether the perceptual-loss formulation generalizes beyond heads to other 3D reconstruction domains, and whether the editing model's limited identity preservation (due to CLIP's single embedding) can be improved without sacrificing the geometry-style disentanglement.
