## Problem

Existing generative models produce high-fidelity short-form visual content but cannot maintain character identity, background, and storyline coherence across extended multi-scene sequences. Prior planning-based approaches rely on purely textual conditioning, making them incapable of delivering the fine-grained, visually-grounded control required for practical applications like filmmaking and e-commerce advertising. Additionally, no existing dataset provides the multi-modal conditioning format (text, image) → (text, {Image_i}) needed to train and evaluate such systems.

## Input / Output

The model takes a condition image (e.g., a product photo) and a text instruction (e.g., a marketing goal or narrative prompt) as joint input, and produces a textual narrative plan (shot-by-shot storyboard descriptions) interleaved with a sequence of generated keyframe images. The MLLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) consumes the image through its ViT encoder and the text through tokenization, while the diffusion backbone (Flux.1-Dev) operates in VAE latent space at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. Each generated keyframe is conditioned on the original input image, the current learnable query, and pooled VAE features from up to T preceding frames, enabling sequential autoregressive generation of arbitrarily long storyboards.

## Method

Narrative Weaver is a hybrid Autoregressive + Diffusion architecture where an MLLM simultaneously performs narrative planning (next-token prediction over storyboard text) and encodes historical multimodal context into a sequence of learnable queries, governed by a dynamic causal attention mask that grants queries full access to all prior text, images, and queries while restricting text tokens to standard causal attention. A Memory Bank caches VAE features of previously generated images and applies geometric average-pooling decay (factor λ) so that the total memory length is bounded by l·λ/(λ−1), preventing unbounded growth while preserving long-range visual anchoring. The comprehensive conditioning signal for the n-th keyframe concatenates the current learnable query, the VAE feature of the input condition image, and the decayed memory features, which is fed to a Flux.1-Dev diffusion model trained with a Flow Matching objective. Training follows a three-stage progressive strategy: (1) narrative planning via cross-entropy on ~5K samples with frozen ViT, (2) semantic alignment of queries to the diffusion space via Flow Matching on 30M low-res and 60K high-res text-image pairs, and (3) fine-grained visual consistency by fully training the diffusion model with the augmented conditioning signal including the Memory Bank.

## Results

On the consistent visual generation task (Q1), Narrative Weaver achieves the best GPT-4o scores on 4 of 5 dimensions: Image-Text Consistency (7.54), Multi-Shot Style Consistency (8.67), Multi-Shot Content Consistency (7.91), and Image Quality (7.35), outperforming the strongest baselines Qwen-Image-Edit and Flux.1-Kontext. It is surpassed only on Reference-Generated Consistency (8.86 vs. 9.41 for Flux.1-Kontext), which the authors attribute to specialized editing models being explicitly optimized for reference fidelity. On the autonomous narrative planning task (Q2, CoMM benchmark), Narrative Weaver leads on Entity consistency (6.49), Trend (6.67), Completeness (7.14), and Image Quality (6.54), while trailing SEED-Llama-8B on Style (7.10 vs. 7.35) and SEED-Llama-14B on Illustration Relevance Score (2.77 vs. 3.28).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Narrative Weaver is the only method combining both text-driven narrative planning and visual conditioning, and it outperforms all baselines on ITC, MSSC, MSCC, and IMQ in Table 1. Against the strongest editing-model baselines, it edges out Qwen-Image-Edit by +0.08 on ITC, +0.24 on MSSC, +0.10 on MSCC, and +0.06 on IMQ, while Flux.1-Kontext retains a narrow RGC advantage (9.41 vs. 8.86) due to its explicit reference-fidelity optimization. Chunk-based methods (TALC, AnimeShooter) and keyframe-based methods (StoryDiffusion, IP-Adapter) are substantially outperformed across all five dimensions, with AnimeShooter and TALC scoring below 3 on ITC and RGC. On the CoMM narrative-planning benchmark (Table 3), Narrative Weaver surpasses SEED-Llama-14B on Entity (+0.21), Trend (+0.96), CPL (+1.12), and ImgQ (+0.69), but trails SEED-Llama-8B on Style (7.10 vs. 7.35) and SEED-Llama-14B on IRS (2.77 vs. 3.28), suggesting room for improvement in illustration-text alignment. The direct ablation baseline Qwen2.5-VL + Flux.1-Dev (without the proposed training) is outperformed by a wide margin on every CoMM metric (e.g., CPL 7.14 vs. 4.79), confirming the contribution of the progressive training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: LLM-driven narrative planning (VideoDirectorGPT, Vlogger, StoryDiffusion) and long-range visual consistency via memory mechanisms (CaptainCinema's GoldenMem, FramePack's context compression). The key departure is that prior narrative methods rely on purely textual conditioning, making them unable to ground generation in a specific visual reference, while prior consistency methods (StoryDiffusion, IP-Adapter) lack autonomous planning. Narrative Weaver bridges this gap by using an MLLM as a 'director' that simultaneously plans narrative text and encodes visual context into learnable queries, with a geometrically-decaying Memory Bank (extending CaptainCinema/FramePack) to anchor each generation step to the initial visual condition. The progressive three-stage training strategy is a practical contribution that decouples narrative learning (~5K samples) from visual alignment, enabling data-efficient fine-tuning of pre-trained MLLM + diffusion backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in AI content creation (filmmaking, e-commerce advertising) where narrative coherence and visual identity must coexist over extended sequences, and the released EAVSD dataset (330K images) fills a genuine gap in multi-modal conditional generation benchmarks. A notable methodological insight is that high frame-similarity metrics (CLIP, DreamSim) can be misleading: specialized editing models like Flux.1-Kontext achieve near-perfect scores by exhibiting 'copy-paste' behavior, whereas Narrative Weaver trades some raw similarity for dynamic storytelling—a distinction only captured by LLM-based evaluation and user studies. Limitations include demonstration on keyframe images rather than full video, reliance on GPT-4o as the primary judge, and the RGC metric being surpassed by purpose-built editing models, suggesting the fine-grained reference-fidelity problem remains partially open.
