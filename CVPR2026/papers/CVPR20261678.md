## Problem

Existing VLMs lack a unified, hierarchical task design that systematically captures the full spectrum of 3D spatial intelligence from basic geometric perception to abstract reasoning, and large-scale 3D-grounded training data for in-the-wild scenes remains scarce. Prior spatial VQA datasets cover only limited task levels and fail to reveal how different spatial reasoning abilities depend on one another.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 448×448 RGB image alongside a metric-scale 3D point map of shape H×W×4 (three channels for camera-frame 3D coordinates, one binary validity mask), obtained from MoGe-2 or a depth sensor. The point map is processed through sinusoidal positional encoding and a learnable Conv2D patchify layer to match the spatial resolution of the SigLIP visual features before fusion. Outputs are autoregressive text spanning a wide range of spatial answers: pixel-level 3D coordinates, pairwise depth orderings, object positions/sizes/orientations, inter-object relative directions and distances, multi-object rankings, and multi-step spatial problem-solving solutions.

## Method

The paper organizes spatial VQA into a four-level cognitive hierarchy (geometric perception → object-level understanding → inter-object relations → abstract spatial reasoning) and builds an automated pipeline that processes ~5M images and 45M objects to generate over 2B QA pairs in free-form, multiple-choice, and true/false formats. The model architecture extends PaliGemma-2 by concatenating the patchified point-map features with RGB visual features along the channel dimension, then projecting through a linear layer to produce fused visual tokens for the Gemma-2 LLM. Training uses standard cross-entropy SFT with the visual encoder frozen, jointly fine-tuning the patchify layer, projector, and LLM, mixing spatial and general VQA data at a 1:7 ratio. Ablations reveal clear inter-level dependencies: removing lower-level training data consistently degrades higher-level performance, with Level 3 tasks most sensitive to the absence of Levels 1–2, confirming that hierarchical task composition is essential for emergent spatial reasoning.

## Results

On qualitative spatial benchmarks (Table 2), HiSpatial-3B-RGB achieves 79.78% on EmbSpatial, 83.74% on RoboSpatial, 95.58% on CV-Bench-3D, 95.08% on CV-Bench-2D Relation, and 64.34% on 3DSRBench, outperforming all proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) and open-source VLMs. With metric-scale point map input (HiSpatial-3B), scores improve to 80.71%, 86.18%, 97.58%, 95.69%, and 63.81% respectively. On quantitative benchmarks (Table 1), HiSpatial-3B with point map input reaches 79.28% average on SpatialRGPT-Quantitative and 85.16% on QSpatial-Bench, surpassing GPT-5 (40.47% and 68.45%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (26.57% and 49.92%) by large margins. On the custom benchmark (Table 3), the model achieves 92.18% on object-to-camera distance (L1), 67.21% on object direction (L2), and 47.44% on spatial problem solving (L3), substantially exceeding GPT-5 (47.19%, 59.27%, 33.33%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiSpatial-3B-RGB (RGB-only) outperforms all proprietary models across every qualitative benchmark: it beats GPT-4o by 16.4 points on EmbSpatial (79.78 vs 63.38) and 20.1 points on 3DSRBench (64.34 vs 44.20), and surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro by 3.1 points on EmbSpatial and 15.9 points on 3DSRBench. Among open-source VLMs, it leads Qwen-3-VL-8B by 1.3 points on EmbSpatial and 11.5 points on 3DSRBench, and outperforms InternVL3.5-8B by 10.0 and 12.8 points respectively. Against spatial specialist models, HiSpatial-3B-RGB dominates SpatialRGPT-8B (59.62→79.78 on EmbSpatial) and SpaceLLaVA-13B (49.40→79.78), but slightly trails RoboRefer-8B-SFT on RoboSpatial (83.74 vs 84.55), CV-Bench-3D (95.58 vs 95.92), and CV-Bench-2D Relation (95.08 vs 96.90), while still leading by 13.1 points on 3DSRBench (64.34 vs 51.24). With point map input, HiSpatial-3B further extends its lead on RoboSpatial (86.18) and CV-Bench-3D (97.58), surpassing even RoboRefer. On quantitative benchmarks, the gap is even more pronounced: HiSpatial-3B achieves 79.28% on SpatialRGPT-Quantitative vs 40.47% for GPT-5 and 26.57% for Gemini-2.5-Pro, and 85.16% on QSpatial vs 68.45% and 49.92% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on PaliGemma-2 as its base VLM and extends the spatial VLM line of work (SpatialRGPT, SpatialVLM, MM-Spatial, RoboRefer) in two key conceptual directions. First, it replaces the relative depth maps used by most prior spatial VLMs with metric-scale 3D point maps, providing richer geometric supervision. Second, and more fundamentally, it introduces a four-level hierarchical taxonomy (geometric perception → object-level understanding → inter-object relations → abstract spatial reasoning) that organizes spatial tasks into a progressive cognitive structure, whereas prior works treat spatial VQA tasks as a flat collection without explicit inter-level structure. The data pipeline also departs from prior efforts by scaling to 5M images and 2B QA pairs, far exceeding the task-level coverage of earlier spatial datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical finding that removing lower-level training data significantly degrades higher-level spatial performance (e.g., −25% on Level 2 when removing Levels 0–1) provides concrete guidance for designing training curricula for spatially intelligent VLMs, a question largely unaddressed in prior work. The 2B-pair hierarchical dataset and the metric-scale point map architecture are practical resources likely to be adopted by the community. Open questions include whether the four-level hierarchy generalizes to video and multi-view settings, whether the MoGe-2 estimation errors in the point map bottleneck further gains, and whether the observed dependencies hold at larger model scales or with reinforcement-based training paradigms.
