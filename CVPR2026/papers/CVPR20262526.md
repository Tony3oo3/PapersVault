## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based pose estimation methods rely on dense multi-view inputs and SfM initialization, which fail under sparse reference views (e.g., 8 views) and low-texture conditions, producing floating artifacts, appearance overfitting, and unstable downstream pose estimation. This paper tackles how to build a reliable object-level 3DGS from very few RGB-D views and then estimate accurate 6D pose for unseen objects without CAD models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262526/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 8 sparse RGB-D reference images of the target object (selected by viewpoint coverage ranking) and a single query RGB-D image at 640×480 resolution, with instance masks from CNOS and depth from ZoeDepth. It produces an object-level 3DGS representation (a set of Gaussian primitives with position, covariance, opacity, and spherical-harmonic color) and the object's 6D pose (translation t ∈ ℝ³ and rotation R ∈ SO(3)) in the camera coordinate frame, with the full pipeline running in ~0.67 s per query image on a single RTX 3090 Ti.

## Method

PoseGaussian is a three-stage pipeline. First, it reconstructs an object-level 3DGS by initializing Gaussian centers via depth back-projection (replacing SfM), then training with adaptive density control (pruning splats in the bottom 5% of both opacity and volumetric contribution), view-warping augmentation (synthesizing pseudo-views via small pose perturbations), and joint photometric (L1 + D-SSIM) plus depth (L1) supervision under a front-high-back-low annealing schedule. Second, a two-stage learning-guided ICP initializer extracts multi-scale features from the 3DGS point cloud and query depth point cloud via KPConv-FPN, establishes coarse-to-fine correspondences using Coarsepoint Matching, Local Superpoint Matching, and an optimal transport layer, then solves for the initial similarity transform. Third, a 3DGS-based iterative pose refiner performs differentiable render-compare-backpropagation, optimizing a combined SSIM/MS-SSIM appearance loss and a scale-normalized depth alignment loss with a translation-then-rotation decoupled update that rotates around the object centroid to avoid drift.

## Results

On LINEMOD with >200 reference views, PoseGaussian achieves a mean ADD-0.1d of 87.2, outperforming IG-6DoF (85.1) by ~2.1% but trailing SG-Pose (95.41). Under the sparse-view setting with only 8 reference views, PoseGaussian reaches 54.30 mean ADD-0.1d, surpassing Gen6D (44.62), Cas6D (47.83), and OnePose++ (40.30) which all use 16 views. On GenMOP with 200 views, PoseGaussian attains the best average ADD-0.1d (59.53) and Prj-5 (88.42) among all compared methods. The pipeline runs in ~0.67 s per 640×480 query image on a single RTX 3090 Ti.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With >200 reference views on LINEMOD, PoseGaussian (87.2) outperforms IG-6DoF (85.1) and Gen6D (73.3) but falls short of SG-Pose (95.41), which benefits from a dedicated segmentation-based 3DGS pipeline. Under the sparse-view setting (8 views), PoseGaussian (54.30) clearly beats all three model-free baselines that use 16 views: Gen6D (44.62, +9.7), Cas6D (47.83, +6.5), and OnePose++ (40.30, +14.0), demonstrating that the depth-prior initialization and sparse training strategy effectively compensate for the halved reference budget. On GenMOP with 200 views, PoseGaussian leads all baselines on both ADD-0.1d (59.53 vs. IG-6DoF 56.10, Cas6D 53.52) and Prj-5 (88.42 vs. Cas6D 87.52, IG-6DoF 86.34). In the GenMOP 8-view setting, PoseGaussian (27.56 ADD-0.1d) still trails the 16-view baselines (Cas6D 37.01, Gen6D 34.00) but surpasses OnePose++ (24.19), indicating that the gap narrows substantially when reference views are limited. Overall, the paper's strongest advantage is in the sparse-view regime, where the structural depth prior and adaptive density control provide the largest relative gains over methods relying on SfM or dense multi-view inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseGaussian sits at the intersection of model-free 6D pose estimation (Gen6D, OnePose++, Cas6D) and 3D Gaussian Splatting-based reconstruction (GS-Pose, IG-6DoF, SG-Pose), but departs from both lines in a key way: it replaces SfM-based initialization with depth back-projection from RGB-D views and introduces a sparse training strategy (adaptive density control, view-warping augmentation, joint photometric-depth loss) inspired by sparse-view NeRF regularizers (RegNeRF, DSNeR, DiNeR). For pose initialization, it adapts learning-guided ICP from GaussianReg [5] using KPConv-FPN multi-scale features and optimal transport, and for refinement it follows the differentiable render-compare-backprop paradigm of FoundationPose and GS-Pose but without any task-specific trainable parameters. The overall contribution is an integrated pipeline that makes 3DGS-based pose estimation viable under only 8 reference views without CAD models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the reference-view requirement from the typical 16–200+ down to 8 while maintaining or exceeding accuracy, which is directly relevant to robotic manipulation and AR/VR settings where capturing many views of an object is costly or infeasible. The finding that a parameter-free 3DGS differentiable refiner outperforms learned refiners (Gen6D) on unseen objects suggests that explicit geometric representations may generalize better than learned appearance-based predictors in the model-free regime. Open questions include whether the method can operate with monocular depth estimation alone (it currently relies on RGB-D sensors or ZoeDepth), how performance degrades below 8 views, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or deformable objects.
