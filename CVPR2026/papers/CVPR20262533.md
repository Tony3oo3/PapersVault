## Problem

Existing nonlinear low-rank tensor representation (LRTR) methods rely on fixed local mappings from MLPs or CNNs, which lack the flexibility to adapt to local dependencies and are constrained by network depth when modeling non-local dependencies. Moreover, most such methods lack rigorous theoretical guarantees on recoverability. The paper asks how to equip LRTR with a dynamic global mapping mechanism that jointly captures local and non-local nonlinear dependencies while providing a theoretical foundation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on tensors of arbitrary order (3rd-order MSIs at 256×256×31, 4th-order color videos at 144×176×3×100, 5th-order light fields at 256×256×3×6×6, and 4th-order video at 240×320×3×31). For inpainting, a binary mask tensor specifies observed entries at 80–95% missing rates; for denoising, the input is corrupted by Gaussian, sparse (salt-pepper), and stripe noise in varying combinations; for video frame synthesis, every other temporal frame is removed. The output is a fully recovered tensor of the same shape, optimized end-to-end via Adam with task-specific regularizers (TV, SSTV, or optical-flow consistency) added to the data-fidelity term.

## Method

SADTR reconstructs a tensor as X = H_Φ(Z) ×₁ FSR_Θ₁(E₁) ×₂ … ×ₙ FSR_Θₙ(Eₙ), where each factor is produced by a Factor Self-Representation (FSR) module and the core tensor by an implicit sparse MLP. In FSR, a learnable embedding matrix Eₙ is projected through three weight matrices into query, key, and value tensors with a nonlinear activation; attention weights Wₙᵃ = softmax(QₙKₙᵀ/√rₙ) establish a dynamic global mapping, and the factor Aₙ = WₙᵃVₙ is a nonlinear attention-weighted combination of all positions, capturing both local and non-local dependencies. The core tensor G = H_Φ(Z) is parameterized by a 2-layer MLP applied to a Gaussian random input Z, which implicitly concentrates elements near zero (proven via a Lipschitz-based sparsity theorem) without requiring an explicit ℓ₁ or TV penalty. A recoverability theorem bounds the estimation error in terms of noise, generalization gap (controlled by a covering-number bound on the solution set), and representation error, establishing a trade-off between model complexity and sample size. The framework is unified across tasks by simply swapping the data-fidelity term and adding task-specific regularizers, all optimized jointly with Adam.

## Results

On high-order data inpainting (Table 1), SADTR achieves the best PSNR across all data types and missing rates: 36.76 dB on 3D MSIs (MR=95%), 29.73 dB on 4D color videos (MR=95%), and 32.61 dB on 5D light fields (MR=95%), outperforming the strongest baseline OTLRM by 2.1, 1.5, and 1.3 dB respectively. At the easier MR=80% setting, the gap widens to 4.5 dB on MSIs and 2.3 dB on videos. On multispectral image denoising (Table 2), SADTR reaches 36.25 dB (Case 1, Gaussian noise) and 31.10 dB (Case 3, mixed Gaussian+sparse+stripe noise) on MSIs, surpassing the diffusion-based HIR-Diff by 1.25 dB and 6.0 dB respectively, with the advantage growing substantially under mixed noise corruption.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SADTR consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across the three recovery tasks. Among linear methods (FTNN, FCTN, HTNN), SADTR gains 6–11 dB in PSNR on inpainting, confirming the benefit of nonlinear modeling. Among nonlinear baselines, OTLRM [39] is the closest competitor on inpainting (e.g., 34.67 vs. 36.76 dB on MSIs at MR=95%), while HLRTF [24] and LRTFR [25] trail by 2–5 dB. On denoising, the diffusion-based HIR-Diff [28] is competitive under pure Gaussian noise (35.00 vs. 36.25 dB on MSIs, Case 1) but degrades severely under mixed noise (25.12 vs. 33.59 dB, Case 2), where SADTR's implicit sparse representation and SSTV prior provide a 6+ dB advantage. On HSIs, SADTR slightly trails HIR-Diff in Case 1 (33.68 vs. 33.90 dB) but leads by 6.8 dB in Case 2 and 5.3 dB in Case 3. The ablation (Fig. 7) confirms that both the factor self-representation (FSR) and the implicit sparse representation contribute meaningfully, with removing FSR from all factors causing the largest performance drop.

## Relation to Prior Work

SADTR extends the nonlinear low-rank tensor representation (LRTR) lineage that began with kernel-based and Gaussian-process approaches and moved to neural-network-based factor mappings (MLPs in HLRTF, LRTFR; CNNs in DeepTensor). The key departure is replacing these fixed local mappings with a self-attention mechanism (factor self-representation) that establishes dynamic global mappings across all positions in each factor, and adding an implicit sparse core via MLPs inspired by implicit neural representations. Unlike prior nonlinear LRTR methods, SADTR also provides a covering-number-based recoverability analysis, filling a theoretical gap the authors explicitly identify in the literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified SADTR framework offers a single representation applicable to inpainting, denoising, and video frame synthesis across 3D–5D tensors, reducing the need for task-specific architectural redesigns. The recoverability theorem quantifies the trade-off between model complexity (rank, embedding dimension) and sample size, giving practitioners a principled way to set hyperparameters. Open questions include whether the attention-based factor mapping scales efficiently to very high-order or very large tensors, whether the theoretical bounds are tight in practice, and how the method compares to emerging foundation-model or diffusion-based approaches for the same recovery tasks.
