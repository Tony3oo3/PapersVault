## Problem

Existing multimodal critic models are trained on general visual domains (captioning, VQA, STEM reasoning) and lack the physics awareness needed to evaluate physical AI tasks involving perception, causal reasoning, and action planning. They fail to distinguish visually coherent but physically impossible reasoning and do not ground their judgments in their own understanding of the physical scenario, leading to inconsistent or superficial verdicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263227/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a multimodal prompt (video clip from robotics or driving datasets plus a text question) together with two candidate CoT responses to be judged. Its output follows a strict self-referential structure: first a `<pred_think>` block with its own physical reasoning, a `<pred>` tag with its self-prediction answer, then a `<think>` block critiquing both responses explicitly against its self-prediction, and finally a `\boxed{}` preference verdict. This format is enforced during training via a dedicated format reward component.

## Method

PhyCritic uses a two-stage RLVR pipeline with GRPO optimization on a Qwen2.5-VL-7B base. Stage 1 (physical skill warmup, 80 steps) applies standard GRPO with accuracy reward on physical QA pairs to build foundational perception and reasoning. Stage 2 (self-referential critic finetuning, 300 steps) trains the model to simultaneously generate its own prediction and judge a response pair, with a composite reward combining self-prediction accuracy (α_sp=0.2), critic accuracy (α_crit=0.7), and format compliance (α_form=0.1). The key insight is 'solve before judging': by requiring the critic to first produce its own physically grounded answer and then explicitly reference it when comparing candidate responses, the model anchors its verdicts in internal physical understanding rather than surface-level linguistic features, yielding more stable and physically correct judgments.

## Results

On PhyCritic-Bench, PhyCritic-7B achieves 68.0% overall pairwise judgment accuracy, the best among all open-source 7B/8B models, outperforming Qwen2.5-VL-7B (51.6%), Eagle-2.5-8B (56.0%), RoboBrain2.0-7B (54.7%), and Cosmos-R1-7B (51.1%) by 16.4, 12.0, 13.3, and 16.9 points respectively. On general reward benchmarks, PhyCritic-7B scores 57.3 on VL-RewardBench and 65.9 on Multimodal RewardBench, surpassing its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base by +4.1 and +1.9. As a policy model, PhyCritic-7B achieves 63.9 on CosmosReason1-Bench (best open-source), 79.7 on CV-Bench (best 3D score of 83.9), and 42.3 on EgoPlanBench2 (second-best overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyCritic-7B decisively outperforms all open-source 7B/8B baselines on PhyCritic-Bench: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B (its own base model) by +16.4 points, Eagle-2.5-8B by +12.0, RoboBrain2.0-7B by +13.3, and Cosmos-R1-7B by +16.9. Against proprietary models, PhyCritic-7B (68.0) trails Gemini-2.5-Pro (78.2) by 10.2 points but surpasses GPT-4o (64.7) by 3.3 points on PhyCritic-Bench. On general reward benchmarks, PhyCritic-7B outperforms all open-source baselines on both VL-RewardBench (57.3 vs. 53.2 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and Multimodal RewardBench (65.9 vs. 64.4 for Eagle-2.5-8B), though it remains well below Gemini-2.5-Pro (74.9 / 85.4). As a policy model on CosmosReason1-Bench, PhyCritic-7B (63.9) edges out Cosmos-R1-7B (63.0) despite the latter being trained on millions of in-domain distilled traces, and surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B (54.3) by +9.6. On EgoPlanBench2, PhyCritic-7B (42.3) ranks second behind Eagle-2.5-8B (43.0) but ahead of all physical-reasoning-tuned models, while on CV-Bench it achieves the best 3D score (83.9) among open-source models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal critic/reward-modeling line (LLaVA-Critic, DriveCritic, VL-RewardBench) from general visual domains to physical AI, where judgments must respect causal dynamics, spatial constraints, and action sequences. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) and the RLVR paradigm popularized by DeepSeek-R1, but introduces a key departure: a two-stage pipeline where Stage 1 warms up physical perception via standard accuracy-reward RL, and Stage 2 trains a 'self-referential' critic that must first solve the problem itself before judging candidate responses. The physical reasoning foundation is drawn from Cosmos-Reason1, whose dataset and embodied-reasoning framing provide the task structure, while the critic training mechanism is novel relative to prior pairwise or scalar-head reward models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physical AI (robotics, autonomous driving, embodied agents) is scaling rapidly but lacks dedicated evaluation infrastructure for judging whether model outputs are physically correct rather than merely linguistically fluent; PhyCritic-Bench and the self-referential training recipe address this gap. The finding that critic training simultaneously improves the model's own policy performance (e.g., +0.9 over Cosmos-R1 on CosmosReason1-Bench) suggests a useful dual-use property for practitioners. A key limitation is the dependence on ground-truth answers for the self-prediction reward, which restricts applicability to fully open-ended or safety-critical scenarios where no verifiable answer exists; future work on self-verification or meta-judging could close this gap.
