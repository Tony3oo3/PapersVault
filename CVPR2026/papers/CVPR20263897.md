## Problem

Existing video editing methods operate exclusively on visual features, breaking the audio-visual synchronization that is essential for engaging content. While a few recent works attempt to incorporate audio cues, they either focus on scene-level alignment (AvED) or lack fine-grained temporal controllability (Object-AVEdit), leaving instance-level editing with precise spatial and temporal control largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263897/cropped_page0_idx6.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ~10-second 720P/24FPS video clip, a coarse instance mask (ranging from a bounding box to a precise contour, parameterized by a precision factor p∈[0,P]), a text description specifying the edit direction, and the original audio track. Optional control contexts (scribble, pose, reference image) can be injected for additional spatial guidance. The system produces an edited video where only the target instance is modified, a curated audio track that maintains synchronization with the edited content, and a refined instance mask that is iteratively sharpened during inference.

## Method

AVI-Edit builds on the pretrained Wan2.2-5B video diffusion transformer with a flow-matching objective, adding a frame-wise cross-attention layer in each DiT block to receive curated audio tokens for temporal alignment, and a mask-based latent interpolation (Eq. 2) to preserve background regions. The Granularity-Aware Mask Refiner (GAMR) mirrors the video backbone's DiT architecture but replaces text tokens with video tokens for visual semantics, injects the precision factor via AdaLN to signal mask granularity, and iteratively refines the coarse mask at each ODE step using a focal-loss training objective with Gaussian-blur-degraded masks. The Self-Feedback Audio Agent runs a separate-generate-remix-rework pipeline: a VLM plans which audio components to separate (via Spleeter/OmniSep) and generate (via ElevenLabs TTS/T2M/T2S), the components are remixed, and an MLLM judges perceptual quality, triggering iterative rework with improvement instructions until a quality threshold is met. The full framework is fine-tuned on the newly constructed AVISET dataset (71k clips) with a joint flow-matching and mask-refinement loss.

## Results

On AVISET, AVI-Edit achieves the best FVD of 312.89 (vs. 364.69 for AvED, 407.08 for Ovi, and 391.64 for VACE-Foley), along with the highest Inception Score (1.127), frame consistency (96.65%), text-video alignment (26.16%), audio-video alignment (26.93%), and lip-sync confidence (Sync-C 4.12). On AvED-Bench, AVI-Edit similarly leads with FVD 349.31, IS 1.125, FC 95.82%, TC 25.30%, and AC 21.64%. In the user preference study, AVI-Edit is preferred by 49.2% of participants for audio-visual synchronization on AVISET, compared to 36.0% for Ovi and 2.4% for AvED.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVI-Edit outperforms all three baselines on the majority of metrics across both datasets. Against AvED [40], AVI-Edit reduces FVD by 51.8 points on AVISET (312.89 vs. 364.69) and 64.5 points on AvED-Bench (349.31 vs. 413.82), while improving audio-video alignment by 3.62% and lip-sync confidence by 2.43 (Sync-C 4.12 vs. 1.69). Against Ovi [42], AVI-Edit achieves a substantially lower FVD (312.89 vs. 407.08 on AVISET; 349.31 vs. 504.14 on AvED-Bench) and higher text-video and audio-video alignment, though Ovi edges out AVI-Edit on Sync-D (9.12 vs. 9.19) by a marginal 0.07. Against the sequential VACE-Foley pipeline [28, 52], AVI-Edit improves FVD by 78.75 points on AVISET and 56.45 on AvED-Bench, and nearly doubles the lip-sync confidence (Sync-C 4.12 vs. 1.72), indicating that jointly modeling audio and video editing yields significantly better synchronization than a decoupled video-then-audio pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Wan2.2 video diffusion transformer as its generative backbone and extends the flow-matching formulation with a frame-wise cross-attention mechanism to incorporate audio tokens, bridging the gap between visual-only video editing (VACE, VideoPainter) and audio-visual editing (AvED, Object-AVEdit). It departs from prior audio-sync editing by introducing two novel components: a Granularity-Aware Mask Refiner that iteratively refines coarse user-provided masks into precise instance contours, and a Self-Feedback Audio Agent that curates accompanying audio through a separate-generate-remix-rework pipeline. The work also contributes AVISET, a dataset specifically designed for instance-centric audio-visual editing, filling a gap left by existing audio-visual datasets that lack instance-level annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper addresses a practical and increasingly important gap: as commercial video models (Sora-2, Veo3) make audio-visual synchronization a baseline expectation, editing tools that break this synchronization become inadequate. The instance-level controllability with freeform temporal specification (via the audio agent) opens applications like modifying a specific speaker's voice while preserving background audio, or adjusting event timing purely through audio cues. Key limitations include the inability to edit multiple instances simultaneously (requiring sequential processing) and reliance on a collection of off-the-shelf audio models whose quality bounds the system. Open questions include whether the self-feedback audio agent's iterative refinement loop can be replaced by end-to-end learned audio generation, and whether the granularity-aware mask refiner generalizes to more complex multi-instance scenes.
