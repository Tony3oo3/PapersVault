## Problem

Existing few-shot medical image segmentation methods rely on task-specific architectures trained from scratch on limited data, making them brittle and prone to sharp performance drops under cross-domain shifts (e.g., CT→MRI). The rich, generalizable visual priors learned by large-scale diffusion models on diverse datasets like LAION-5B remain largely unexplored for dense prediction tasks such as FSMIS, leaving a gap in data-efficient and domain-robust segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261612/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 1-way 1-shot support set (one single-channel medical image and its binary mask) and a query image, all resized to 256×256. Since the frozen VAE expects 3-channel RGB input, both images and masks are replicated across three channels and normalized to [-1,1]. At inference, a single-step DDIM pass (timestep 999) through the U-Net produces a latent mask that is decoded by the VAE and averaged across channels to yield the final single-channel binary segmentation mask.

## Method

SD-FSMIS adapts pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.5 with two targeted modules. The Support-Query Interaction (SQI) module injects class-specific information by (a) adding a cross-attention layer in each BasicTransformerBlock where the query latent attends to the concatenated support image+mask latent, and (b) a Query Enhancement step that computes a support prototype via Masked Average Pooling, finds high-similarity regions in the query, and concatenates the resulting query prototype to the query latent along the channel dimension. The Visual-to-Textual Condition Translator (VTCT) module extracts support image features with a frozen DINOv2-small encoder, aggregates them via Masked Average Pooling with the support mask into a class prototype, and projects it through a learnable MLP into the text-embedding space, providing content-aware conditioning to the U-Net's cross-attention layers. Training uses an MSE loss between the predicted and ground-truth mask latents under a 1-way 1-shot meta-learning protocol with supervoxel-based pseudo-labels, keeping the VAE frozen while training the U-Net (lr 1e-5) and MLP (lr 5e-5) for 15k iterations per fold.

## Results

On the Abd-CT dataset under Setting 1, SD-FSMIS achieves a mean Dice score of 83.66%, surpassing the previous best (DIFD at 80.19%) by 3.47%. Under the more challenging Setting 2 (zero exposure to target anatomies during training), SD-FSMIS maintains 83.25% mean Dice on Abd-CT, outperforming DIFD (79.85%) by 3.4%. In cross-domain evaluation (Table 2), SD-FSMIS achieves 81.42% mean Dice for Abd-CT→MRI and 75.90% for Abd-MRI→CT, outperforming the strongest baseline DiffewS by 4.33% and 3.94% respectively. The method shows minimal performance degradation from Setting 1 to Setting 2 (only 0.41% drop on Abd-CT), demonstrating superior robustness compared to prior methods that suffer sharp drops.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SD-FSMIS outperforms all 16 baselines on Abd-CT under both settings, with the largest margins over DIFD (+3.47% Setting 1, +3.40% Setting 2) and DiffewS (+4.86% Setting 1, +8.32% Setting 2). On Abd-MRI Setting 1, DIFD edges out SD-FSMIS by 0.96% in mean Dice (84.12 vs. 83.16), though SD-FSMIS leads on individual organs like LK (84.17 vs. 88.59 is DIFD's advantage; SD-FSMIS leads on Spleen 80.43 vs. 75.72). In the cross-domain setting (Table 2), SD-FSMIS dominates all baselines by a wide margin: 81.42% vs. 77.09% (DiffewS) for CT→MRI and 75.90% vs. 71.96% for MRI→CT, with the gap over non-diffusion methods (e.g., DIFD at 71.14% and 60.01%) exceeding 10 points. The ablation confirms that the SII module alone already reaches 80.11% mean Dice on Abd-CT, comparable to prior SOTA, while VTCT adds +3.06% and QE adds +2.16%, with the full framework achieving 83.66%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant prototype-matching and dual-branch interaction paradigm in FSMIS by instead adapting a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model with minimal architectural modifications. It builds most directly on DiffewS (NeurIPS 2024), which first demonstrated that diffusion model features can support few-shot segmentation, but extends this with two targeted innovations: the SQI module (adding support-to-query cross-attention in the U-Net's transformer blocks, inspired by DiffewS and Ke et al.) and the VTCT module (translating visual support cues into text-like embeddings, inspired by ODISE). The query enhancement strategy borrows the Masked Average Pooling prototype extraction from SSP (ECCV 2022), while the overall VAE-encoder/decoder pipeline and single-step DDIM inference follow the Latent Diffusion framework of Rombach et al. (CVPR 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a general-purpose text-to-image diffusion model, with only lightweight adaptation modules, can match or exceed purpose-built FSMIS architectures while exhibiting markedly superior cross-domain robustness (minimal performance drop from Setting 1 to Setting 2, where most baselines degrade sharply). This suggests that the visual priors learned on LAION-5B encode modality-agnostic structural knowledge that transfers to medical imaging without domain-specific training. Limitations include evaluation restricted to 2D abdominal organs (spleen, liver, kidneys) in a 1-shot setting, and the reliance on pseudo-labels from supervoxel clustering rather than true annotations. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to 3D volumetric segmentation, more diverse organ classes, and whether the single-step DDIM inference can be further accelerated without quality loss.
