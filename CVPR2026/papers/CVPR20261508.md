## Problem

Existing prototype-based few-shot industrial anomaly detection (FS-IAD) methods construct normal prototypes from limited support images but fail to systematically incorporate query image statistics to enhance prototype representativeness. Prior refinement approaches like FastRecon rely on point-to-point reconstruction, which limits characteristic transfer and, in the few-shot regime, makes anomaly reconstruction more probable—causing enhanced prototypes to be misled by defects in the query image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261508/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes k normal support images (1/2/4 shots) and a single query image, both processed through a frozen pre-trained backbone (WRN-50, CLIP ViT-B/16+, or DINOv2) to extract spatial feature maps. Support features are compressed via Coreset into a prototype set of size n = α·k·h·w, while the query yields m = h·w feature vectors. At test time, FastRef iteratively refines the prototypes per query image and outputs a pixel-level anomaly score map (upscaled via bilinear interpolation and Gaussian smoothing) plus an image-level score (max or top-1% mean, depending on the base method), requiring no gradient-based training—only a closed-form matrix update and a few Sinkhorn iterations.

## Method

FastRef formulates prototype refinement as a nested optimization balancing two objectives: (1) characteristic transfer, achieved by linearly reconstructing query features from normal prototypes via a transform matrix W (a 'composition refinement' that selects and combines prototype entries rather than operating along the feature dimension), and (2) anomaly suppression, achieved by minimizing an entropic optimal transport (Sinkhorn) distance between the distribution of enhanced prototypes and the original normal prototypes. The key insight is that in few-shot settings, anomalies in the query are more likely to be faithfully reconstructed, so the OT term forces W to down-weight anomalous regions during the next update step. The algorithm alternates between updating the transport probability T (via Sinkhorn, <10 inner loops) and updating W in closed form (Eq. 5), converging in only L=2 outer iterations. FastRef is a plug-and-play module that integrates with PatchCore, WinCLIP, and AnomalyDINO by substituting their distance functions into the shared optimization, yielding consistent AUROC gains of 0.2–7.3% across MVTec, ViSA, MPDD, and RealIAD under 1/2/4-shots.

## Results

Under the standard 2-shot setting, PatchCore+ achieves 88.8% Image AUROC on MVTec, 78.2% on MPDD, 87.1% on ViSA, and 76.9% on RealIAD, outperforming its base PatchCore by +1.7 to +7.1 points. AnomalyDINO+ reaches 97.2% on MVTec and 78.4% on MPDD, while WinCLIP+ attains 93.9% on MVTec and 76.0% on MPDD. The largest relative gains appear on the more challenging MPDD and ViSA datasets, where prototype refinement from query features is most beneficial under data scarcity. Pixel-level AUROC improvements follow the same trend, with PatchCore+ gaining up to +1.1 points over PatchCore on ViSA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PatchCore+ (FastRef integrated into PatchCore) consistently outperforms PatchCore across all four datasets and all shot settings, with the largest Image AUROC gains on ViSA (+7.1 at 2-shot) and MPDD (+6.8 at 2-shot), confirming that query-aware prototype refinement is most impactful when normal prototypes are least representative. WinCLIP+ improves over WinCLIP by up to +7.0 Image AUROC on MPDD (4-shot) and +2.4 Pixel AUROC on MVTec (2-shot), though gains on MVTec are modest (+0.2 Image) since CLIP features are already strong. AnomalyDINO+ surpasses AnomalyDINO by +4.7 Image AUROC on MPDD (1-shot) and +4.5 on RealIAD (1-shot), with smaller but consistent gains as the backbone scales up. Against FastRecon, PatchCore+ (equivalent to FastRecon+ per the paper) achieves higher Image AUROC on MVTec (88.8 vs 88.3), MPDD (78.2 vs 76.4), and RealIAD (76.9 vs 72.5) at 2-shot, while FastRecon retains a slight edge on ViSA Pixel AUROC (97.6 vs 98.0 is actually PatchCore+ winning). The paper also notes that FastRecon is a special case of FastRef under Gaussian and ordered-prototype assumptions, explaining the consistent but not overwhelming margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

FastRef extends the prototype-oriented few-shot industrial anomaly detection (FS-IAD) paradigm established by PatchCore, WinCLIP, and AnomalyDINO, which construct normal prototypes from limited support images and score queries by distance to those prototypes. It generalizes FastRecon's reconstruction-based refinement by replacing the Gaussian-distribution assumption and ordered-prototype constraint with a distribution-free optimal transport formulation, showing FastRecon is a special case (T=I, ordered prototypes). The method departs from prior test-time refinement approaches (e.g., FastRecon's point-to-point reconstruction) by formulating refinement as a nested optimization over a transform matrix and a Sinkhorn transport probability, enabling simultaneous characteristic transfer and anomaly suppression in a single closed-form update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FastRef provides a lightweight, plug-and-play refinement module that consistently improves detection and localization across three different backbone architectures (WRN-50, CLIP ViT-B/16, DINOv2) and four datasets, suggesting the prototype-representativeness bottleneck is a shared weakness across FS-IAD methods rather than a backbone-specific issue. The closed-form solution requiring only L=2 outer iterations and <10 Sinkhorn inner loops makes it practical for real-time industrial inspection. However, gains are modest on already-strong methods (WinCLIP+ improves by only 0.2–0.9% image AUROC), and the approach still depends on the quality of the pre-trained backbone and the Coreset-based prototype selection, leaving open whether the OT formulation is strictly necessary versus simpler regularization in most practical settings.
