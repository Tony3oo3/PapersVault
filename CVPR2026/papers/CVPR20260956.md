## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks provide thorough visual-quality metrics but lack convincing, multi-dimensional evaluation for synchronous audio-video generation. In particular, they do not quantify cross-modal semantic consistency (text–video, text–audio, video–audio), fine-grained temporal synchronization, lip-speech alignment, or the spatial acoustic properties of stereo audio that most current models now produce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260956/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 778 text prompts (T2AV) and 521 curated images (I2AV) spanning seven content categories (animals, human sounds, music, environmental sounds, synchronous physical sounds, complex scenes, virtual worlds), plus 116 stereo-specific prompts specifying distinct left/right sounds. Each generated sample is a 720P video paired with a 48 kHz stereo audio track; evaluation produces 15 fine-grained scores (8 expert-model metrics on a continuous scale, 7 MLLM metrics on 1–5 or 0–1 scales) and 9 normalized stereo acoustic metrics visualized as a radar chart.

## Method

VABench employs a dual-track evaluation pipeline. The expert-model track quantifies uni-modal audio quality (DNSMOS, NISQAv2, Audiobox aesthetics), cross-modal semantic alignment (ViCLIP for text–video, CLAP for text–audio, ImageBind for audio–visual), and temporal synchronization (Synchformer desync offset, LatentSync lip-sync confidence). The MLLM track uses omni-modal large language models at two granularities: macro-level 1–5 scoring for alignment, artistry, expresspressiveness, and audio/visual realism, and micro-level QA pairs (3–7 detail-oriented questions per sample) scored as average accuracy. A dedicated stereo module measures nine acoustic metrics in two dimensions—spatial imaging quality (stereo width, ITD/ILD stability, transient synchronization) and signal integrity (phase coherence, mono compatibility)—enabling the first benchmark-level assessment of spatial audio rendering in video generation.

## Results

On the T2AV task, Veo3 achieves the strongest overall performance among end-to-end AV models, leading in Audio Aesthetic (3.543), Text-Audio Alignment (0.3582), Audio-Visual Alignment (0.3164), and MLLM Alignment (4.553/5). Sora2 excels in Visual Realism (4.805) and fine-grained Audio QA (0.8082), while Wan2.5 achieves the best Lip-Sync score (3.671) and lowest Desync offset (0.4622 s). Among decoupled V+A pipelines, Kling+MMAudio is the strongest combination, yet even the best V+A model (kling_mm) fails to surpass the weakest AV model on most metrics. On I2AV, Veo3 again leads (Audio Aes 3.574, Alignment 4.906), and Seedance+MMAudio becomes the top V+A combination, with input images narrowing performance gaps across models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Veo3 (Google DeepMind) is the strongest end-to-end AV model on both T2AV and I2AV, leading in Audio Aesthetic (3.543/3.574), Text-Audio Alignment (0.3582/0.4130), and MLLM Alignment (4.553/4.906), but trails Sora2 in Visual Realism (4.773 vs. 4.805 on T2AV) and Wan2.5 in Lip-Sync (3.294 vs. 3.671) and Desync (0.5184 vs. 0.4622 s). Sora2 (OpenAI) delivers the highest Visual Realism scores (4.805 T2AV, 4.964 I2AV) and best Audio QA (0.8082/0.8287), yet lags in audio aesthetics and temporal synchronization. Wan2.5 (Alibaba) achieves the best Lip-Sync (3.671) and lowest Desync (0.4622 s) among AV models, indicating superior audio-visual temporal alignment, but its cross-modal semantic alignment scores are lower than Veo3 and Sora2. Among V+A pipelines, Kling+MMAudio is the strongest combination on T2AV (Audio QA 0.7300, Visual QA 0.7754), while Seedance+MMAudio leads on I2AV (Audio QA 0.8020, Alignment 4.918). MMAudio consistently outperforms ThinkSound across most metrics, though ThinkSound shows a distinct advantage in Lip-Sync (e.g., 2.785 vs. 1.743 for Seed+Think vs. Seed+MMA on T2AV) and in the Music category. A key finding is that even the best V+A combination (Kling+MMA) fails to surpass the weakest AV model (Wan2.5) on most metrics, confirming the advantage of end-to-end joint training for cross-modal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

VABench extends the visual-only evaluation lineage of VBench and VBench 2.0 into the joint audio-video domain, where no systematic benchmark previously existed. It departs from prior V2A benchmarks (which require reference audio ground truth) by introducing reference-free, triangular text-video-audio consistency evaluation, and from JavisBench by expanding from a handful of dimensions to 15 fine-grained metrics across 7 content categories. The evaluation methodology repurposes existing expert models (CLAP, ImageBind, ViCLIP, Synchformer, DNSMOS, NISQA, Audiobox) and MLLM-based judging into a unified dual-track framework, while the stereo audio evaluation dimension is entirely novel in the video-generation benchmarking context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As synchronous audio-video generation models (Veo3, Sora2, Wan2.5) become the dominant paradigm, VABench provides the first standardized, multi-dimensional evaluation that the field needs to track progress and identify failure modes. The finding that no current model reliably produces semantically meaningful stereo separation from text prompts, despite outputting dual-channel audio, reveals a significant gap between technical capability and perceptual intent that prior benchmarks would have missed. Open questions include how to evaluate higher-order couplings (Doppler effects, emotional cross-modal synergy, music-visual rhythm coordination) more rigorously, and whether the MLLM-based macro scores will remain stable as these models evolve. The benchmark's 778+521 sample scale and 15-metric design make it a practical reference point, though its reliance on API-accessible models limits reproducibility for closed-source systems.
