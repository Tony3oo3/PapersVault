## Problem

Medical image segmentation datasets suffer from acquisition noise, multi-center device discrepancies, and annotation ambiguity, introducing pervasive aleatoric (data) uncertainty that undermines model robustness. Existing research focuses primarily on model prediction reliability (epistemic uncertainty) and architectural improvements, while systematic quantification and exploitation of the intrinsic data uncertainty itself remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261477/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3D medical image volumes (CT or MRI) as 96×96×96 patches, optionally normalized by annotation mask regions, and feeds them through a frozen medical visual foundation model (MedSAM2) to extract per-class feature tensors Z ∈ R^{C×D×H×W}. It produces (1) standard segmentation masks, (2) a per-sample Aleatoric Uncertainty Value (AUV) in (0,1) derived from the Shannon entropy of the singular value energy spectrum, and (3) per-class semantic perception scales that quantify feature diversity without requiring any annotation. The AUV can be computed on raw images (reflecting anatomical complexity) or mask-normalized images (reflecting annotation quality).

## Method

The core mechanism reshapes each class's 3D feature map into a 2D matrix z_c ∈ R^{D×(H·W)}, performs SVD to obtain singular values, and computes the normalized squared singular value spectrum as an energy distribution p_j. The Shannon entropy of this distribution, normalized by log(r), yields the semantic perception scale S(Z_i|c); summing across classes and applying log-transform + min-max scaling produces the final AUV. Two plug-and-play applications follow: (1) quantile-based data filtering that removes the top 5–10% highest-AUV samples to eliminate noisy training data, and (2) a Dynamic Uncertainty-Aware Optimization (DUO) strategy that divides the per-class segmentation loss by (1 + α·S(·)) to down-weight uncertain classes during training, combined with a learnable label-denoising head that estimates and subtracts annotation noise under zero-mean and unit-variance constraints. The approach requires no additional model parameters beyond the noise head and no Monte Carlo sampling, making it a lightweight, annotation-free uncertainty quantification pipeline.

## Results

On the five medical segmentation benchmarks (LiTS, TotalSegmentator, WORD, FeTA 2022, KiTS23), the proposed MedSAM2-based AUV at 90% data retention achieves a mean Dice of 76.45%, improving over the 100%-data baseline (75.10%) by +1.35 points. The Dynamic Uncertainty-aware Optimization (DUO) strategy further boosts nnU-Net from 75.10% to 75.69% mean Dice, Swin-UNETR from 73.09% to 73.64%, and U-Mamba from 74.13% to 74.66%, demonstrating consistent gains across CNN, Transformer, and Mamba backbones. On the LiTS tumor class specifically, the semantic perception scale S(·) yields a Dice of 74.28% at 90% filtering (+4.97 over baseline), substantially outperforming Fisher (70.17%), Mahalanobis Distance (69.78%), and EAOA (70.41%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the five quantification strategies compared in Table 1, MedSAM2-based AUV at 90% filtering achieves the best mean Dice (76.45%), outperforming SegVol (76.11%), nnU-Net task-specific (75.17%), Variance [42] (75.39%), and CLIP-Driven (75.18%). Notably, CLIP-Driven actually degrades performance below the 100%-data baseline on LiTS (82.18 vs. 82.96) and KiTS23 (69.36 vs. 70.15), indicating that not all foundation models yield reliable uncertainty estimates. The task-specific nnU-Net as feature extractor provides only marginal gains (+0.23 mean Dice at 95%), suggesting overfitting limits its uncertainty estimation quality. For the DUO strategy (Table 2), consistent improvements of +0.5 to +0.6 mean Dice are observed across all three architectures (nnU-Net, Swin-UNETR, U-Mamba), confirming plug-and-play applicability. In the quantification method comparison (Table 3), the proposed S(·) achieves 74.28% tumor Dice at 90% filtering, beating Fisher (+4.11), MD (+4.50), and EAOA (+3.87), while also being the only method compatible with the DUO optimization strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pretrained medical vision foundation models (MedSAM2, SegVol) as fixed feature extractors for aleatoric uncertainty quantification, departing from prior approaches that rely on task-specific model features (which overfit) or simple data variance. Conceptually, it extends the line of work on learning sample difficulty from pretrained representations (Cui et al. [4,5]) by replacing Mahalanobis distance or Fisher metric with an SVD-based singular value energy distribution, framed as a 'semantic perception scale' inspired by information-theoretic entropy. The two downstream applications—quantile-based data filtering and a dynamic loss reweighting with label denoising—draw on established curriculum learning and robust optimization ideas but are novel in being driven by foundation-model-derived uncertainty rather than model confidence or prediction error.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of both the AUV-based filtering and the DUO strategy (validated across CNN, Transformer, and Mamba backbones) makes this a practical tool for medical imaging pipelines where annotation noise is pervasive but hard to audit manually. The strong correlation between the semantic perception scale and per-class predicted Dice (Pearson r=0.63, p<0.01) provides a mechanistic explanation for why foundation-model features capture sample difficulty better than task-specific features. However, the absolute gains are modest (~1–2% Dice), the approach requires running a large foundation model as a preprocessing step, and the data filtering strategy discards 5–10% of training data, which may be problematic for already-small medical datasets. Open questions include whether the method transfers to weakly supervised or federated settings and whether the SVD energy metric generalizes beyond the five tested datasets.
