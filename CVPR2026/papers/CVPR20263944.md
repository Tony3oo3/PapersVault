## Problem

Per-sample loss is the standard importance metric for dynamic data pruning, but it is often infeasible to extract from standard training pipelines that only compute mean batch loss, especially for complex multi-component objectives (e.g., object detection) or frameworks that abstract loss computation. This inaccessibility creates a critical bottleneck for deploying loss-aware pruning strategies in practice.

## Input / Output

BLS consumes only two quantities already available at every training step: the scalar mean batch loss L(B_t, t) and the set of sample indices present in the current batch. It produces a per-sample scalar score s_i(t) that is updated exclusively when sample i appears in the batch (conditional EMA), leaving the score frozen otherwise. The score is a smoothed, filtered estimate of the sample's persistent loss contribution and can be fed directly into any existing pruning framework's sampler as a drop-in replacement for the exact per-sample loss, requiring zero modifications to downstream scheduling logic.

## Method

BLS assigns each sample a score updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the mean batch loss, but only at steps where that sample is present in the sampled batch. The key theoretical insight decomposes the batch loss into a scaled signal (l_i(t)/B, the sample's own contribution) and batch composition noise (average loss of the other B−1 co-selected samples), then shows the EMA update is mathematically equivalent to a first-order IIR low-pass filter whose frequency response attenuates high-frequency components. Under a Frequency Separation Hypothesis—empirically validated via power spectral density analysis—the stochastic batch composition noise concentrates energy at higher frequencies than the slowly evolving per-sample loss signal, so the filter suppresses noise while preserving the persistent loss trend. The result is a three-line code injection that produces a robust importance proxy, enabling one-line integration into existing per-sample loss-based methods (InfoBatch, SeTa) without modifying their core logic or hyperparameters.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet18), BLS-SeTa achieves 95.6% accuracy at 30% pruning (matching the 95.6% full-training baseline) and 95.0% at 70% pruning, while BLS-InfoBatch reaches 95.6% at 30% and 94.5% at 70%. On CIFAR-100, BLS-SeTa attains 78.5% at 30% pruning (exceeding the 78.2% full baseline) and 76.7% at 70%. Across ImageNet-1K with six architectures (ResNet18/50, EfficientNet, ViT, Swin, Vim), BLS-InfoBatch prunes 22.9–32.8% of data while maintaining within 0.1–0.4% of full-training accuracy, and BLS-SeTa prunes up to 44.5% (Swin) with no accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BLS-SeTa matches or slightly exceeds SeTa across all pruning rates on CIFAR-100 (78.5% vs 78.4% at 30%; 78.1% vs 78.0% at 50%; 76.7% vs 76.7% at 70%), while BLS-InfoBatch is within 0.2% of InfoBatch at moderate pruning but drops 0.2% at 70% on CIFAR-10 (94.5% vs 94.7%). Both BLS variants substantially outperform static-selection baselines: at 70% pruning on CIFAR-10, BLS-SeTa (95.0%) beats GraNd-4 (91.2%) by 3.8 points, EL2N-20 (91.9%) by 3.1 points, and UCB (93.9%) by 1.1 points. On CIFAR-100 at 50% pruning, BLS-SeTa (78.1%) surpasses DP (73.1%) by 5.0 points and ε-greedy (74.8%) by 3.3 points. The key finding is that BLS achieves near-identical performance to the original per-sample-loss methods it proxies (InfoBatch, SeTa) while requiring only 3 lines of code injection versus 33+ lines of intrusive modification, confirming its viability as a drop-in replacement without access to individual sample losses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on dynamic data pruning methods that score samples by per-sample loss (InfoBatch, SeTa, GraNd, EL2N), identifying the practical inaccessibility of per-sample loss in complex pipelines as the key bottleneck. Rather than proposing a new pruning algorithm, BLS repurposes the already-computed mean batch loss through a sample-conditional EMA, framing the problem through a signal-processing lens (low-pass filtering of batch composition noise) that is novel in this context. The approach positions itself as a transparent, drop-in proxy that requires no modifications to downstream pruning logic, distinguishing it from prior work that either requires intrusive per-sample loss extraction or uses fundamentally different importance metrics (gradient norms, EL2N).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3-line implementation and one-line proxy integration make BLS immediately deployable in pipelines where per-sample loss is architecturally difficult to extract (multi-component detection losses, complex vision-language objectives), a practical gap that has limited adoption of loss-aware pruning. The extensive validation (14 datasets, 18 architectures, 11 tasks) with lossless 20–50% pruning is compelling, though the frequency separation assumption (Assumption 4.2) remains empirically validated only on CIFAR100/ResNet18 and may not universally hold for all loss landscapes or training regimes. Open questions include whether BLS degrades under very small batch sizes (where the noise/signal decomposition changes), how optimal α should be set adaptively rather than fixed, and whether the proxy quality holds for non-convex or highly non-stationary loss surfaces in very large-scale pretraining.
