## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion noise optimization methods (ProgMoGen, DNO) fail on highly constrained human motion generation tasks involving severe spatiotemporal restrictions (e.g., walking through a 0.4 m gap or under a 0.5 m barrier) and precise numerical controls (e.g., exactly six walking steps over four meters), producing high constraint error and motion artifacts. No prior work specifically addresses these challenging constraint regimes or offers an effective solution within the training-free optimization framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a combined constraint function F_C (constructed via motion programming, encoding spatial barriers, gap widths, step counts, and other specifications), an optional text prompt, and the HumanML3D motion dataset (14,616 samples) as a retrieval knowledge base. It outputs a 3D human motion sequence in the 284-dimensional RoHM representation per frame (root trajectory, joint positions/velocities, SMPL rotations, foot-contact labels), generated entirely training-free from a frozen MDM-RoHM base model with 50 DDIM steps. The pipeline requires approximately 500 additional optimization steps beyond standard DNO.

## Method

RG-DNO introduces a three-stage retrieval-guided noise construction pipeline. First, relational task parsing (manual or LLM-based via DeepSeek R1) identifies the most difficult constraint subset C_R for retrieval and partitions remaining constraints into C_1 (random-noise fitting) and C_2 (retrieved-noise fitting) using rules about constraint connectivity and conflict. Second, constraint-based retrieval searches the motion dataset for samples minimizing F_{C_R} (with horizontal spatial transforms and temporal resizing), then inverts the best match through the diffusion model to obtain a retrieved noise z_R. Third, masked noise optimization independently optimizes z_1 (from random noise, fitting C_1) and z_2 (from z_R, fitting C_2), then selects a downsampled binary mask M from 2^{N_T} or 2^{N_S} candidates by minimizing the full constraint error plus a quality reward (jitter, foot-skate, de-correlation, semantic alignment), producing a combined initialization z' = M·z_1 + (1−M)·z_2. A final round of standard DNO refines z' into the output motion. The key insight is that early-stage diffusion noise encodes motion structure, so seeding it with retrieved skill patterns (e.g., side-walking for narrow gaps) provides knowledge that random noise cannot capture for difficult constraints.

## Results

On the highly-constrained spatiotemporal tasks (Table 1), RG-DNO reduces constraint error by 3.2× on Task-1 (narrow gap: 0.0050 vs. 0.0162 for ProgMoGen+DNO) and 2.3× on Task-2 (low barrier: 0.000049 vs. 0.000115), while also cutting maximum scene penetration by ~2.7× and ~1.9× respectively. On the numerical constraint task (Table 2, Task-3: 6 steps over 4 m), RG-DNO achieves a constraint error of 0.0003 versus 0.282 for ProgMoGen+DNO (≈940× lower) and a success rate of 59.4% vs. 46.9%. On the joint-based Task HSI-2 (Table 3), RG-DNO matches MaskControl's zero constraint error while achieving slightly lower max joint acceleration (0.123 vs. 0.126).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unconstrained MDM-RoHM baseline, RG-DNO reduces constraint error by orders of magnitude (e.g., 14.101 → 0.0050 on Task-1), confirming that the retrieval-guided optimization is essential for satisfying hard constraints. Compared to ProgMoGen+DNO (the strongest constrained baseline), RG-DNO consistently achieves lower constraint error across all three task types: 3.2× lower on the narrow-gap task, 2.3× lower on the low-barrier task, and ~940× lower on the step-counting task, while also reducing maximum scene penetration and joint acceleration. On the joint-based Task HSI-2, RG-DNO ties MaskControl on constraint error (both 0.000) and edges it out on max joint acceleration (0.123 vs. 0.126), though MaskControl uses a different base model (MoMask). The 5-run noise search (N_S=5) further narrows the gap, with RG-DNO reaching near-zero constraint error (0.0000) on Task-1 versus 0.0019 for ProgMoGen+DNO. Foot skating is slightly higher for RG-DNO on Task-1 (0.040 vs. 0.029), suggesting a minor trade-off in temporal smoothness for the retrieval-guided approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free diffusion noise optimization (DNO) framework and the programmable motion generation (ProgMoGen) paradigm by introducing retrieval-guided noise initialization for highly constrained tasks. While prior retrieval-augmented motion generation methods (ReMoDiffuse, ReMoMask, RMD) use retrieved samples to improve general generation quality, this work repurposes retrieval specifically to craft better diffusion noise initializations when random noise is provably insufficient for difficult spatiotemporal or numerical constraints. The relational task parsing component draws on LLM reasoning (DeepSeek R1) to automatically decompose constraint sets, connecting the motion generation literature to the broader trend of LLM-guided planning in embodied agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode of current DNO-based motion generation: severe spatial obstacles (0.4m gaps, 0.5m barriers) and precise numerical controls (exact step counts) that random noise optimization simply cannot satisfy. The training-free, plug-and-play nature of the retrieval-guided initialization makes it immediately applicable to existing pipelines without retraining. Key limitations include ~300 additional optimization steps over vanilla DNO, reduced motion diversity under tight constraints, and the finding that learned reward models (MotionCritic) actively harm performance on rare motions. Open questions include whether one-step generative models could eliminate the multi-stage optimization overhead, and whether LLM-based task parsing can be made more reliable for open-ended constraint sets.
