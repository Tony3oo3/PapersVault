## Problem

Existing 3D city generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: implicit representations (NeRF, 3DGS) offer visual richness but suffer from multi-view inconsistency and incompatibility with downstream simulation, while explicit-mesh approaches are structurally reliable yet limited to a 'retrieve-and-place' paradigm constrained by fixed asset libraries. Additionally, no unified evaluation protocol exists for assessing structural correctness, material realism, and multi-view consistency in generated urban scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260996/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form natural language prompt (e.g., 'a bustling downtown with towering skyscrapers under pink sunset lighting') and produces a complete, renderable 3D city scene at 512×512 layout resolution, including semantic layout maps (5 categories: vegetation, roads, water, buildings, ground), building height maps, per-instance 3D building assets, seamlessly tilable PBR material textures, and a 360° HDR skybox. Beyond initial generation, the integrated MajutsuAgent accepts follow-up natural language commands to perform object-level Add, Delete, Edit, Move, and Replace operations on the assembled scene.

## Method

MajutsuCity operates through a four-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM parses the prompt into a structured multi-dimensional design specification covering layout, assets, materials, and skymap; (2) a two-stage cascaded diffusion model generates the semantic layout map using LongCLIP to encode long spatial descriptions, then a ControlNet-based stage synthesizes the height map conditioned on the layout as a spatial prior; (3) per-instance 3D building assets are generated via shape-constrained synthesis combining image-based refinement (isometric rendering as geometric prior with VLM self-calibration) and optional point-cloud-based constraints for strict footprint adherence, while a fine-tuned Qwen-Image model produces seamlessly tilable PBR textures and skyboxes; (4) all components are assembled into a coherent scene using Poisson disk sampling for vegetation and distance-transform sampling for roadside elements. The framework is trained on the newly constructed MajutsuDataset (13,300 OSM-derived layout/height pairs, 1,000 3D building assets across 10 architectural styles, 2,300 PBR materials, and 1,000 HDR skyboxes) using standard latent diffusion objectives with AdamW at 1×10⁻⁵ learning rate.

## Results

For layout generation, MajutsuCity achieves FID 22.7, KID 0.013, and IS 3.14, reducing FID by 83.7% over CityDreamer (139.6) and 20.1% over CityCraft (28.4). For full city scene generation, MajutsuCity ranks first across all eight AQS dimensions (e.g., SVC 8.56 GPT / 8.35 User; MTF 7.00 GPT / 8.03 User) and all eight RDR dimensions (e.g., SVC 34.13 GPT / 26.76 User; LA 32.68 GPT / 25.78 User), outperforming the next-best method by a clear margin in every category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MajutsuCity outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against CityDreamer (NeRF-based), the FID gap is dramatic (22.7 vs. 139.6, an 83.7% reduction), and CityDreamer scores lowest on SVC in both AQS (4.20 GPT) and RDR (12.39 GPT), reflecting multi-view inconsistency inherent to its implicit representation. Against CityCraft (explicit mesh with fixed asset library), MajutsuCity wins on FID (22.7 vs. 28.4) and dominates on SRC (scene richness) in RDR (28.76 vs. 21.33 GPT), while CityCraft retains a slight edge on MTF under AQS (6.64 User vs. 8.03 User for Ours — actually Ours wins here too). Against GaussianCity (3DGS-based), MajutsuCity leads on all four AQS dimensions (e.g., SVC 8.56 vs. 6.73) and all four RDR dimensions (e.g., SVC 34.13 vs. 23.56 GPT), with the paper noting that RDR reverses the AQS ranking between GaussianCity and UrbanWorld on SVC, highlighting RDR's robustness. Against UrbanWorld (coarse-primitive mesh), MajutsuCity wins decisively on all dimensions, with the largest gaps on SRC (RDR: 28.76 vs. 16.06 GPT) and MTF (RDR: 28.87 vs. 12.39 GPT). Against InfiniteGAN, the layout FID improvement is 87.4% (22.7 vs. 180.4), confirming superior topological coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

MajutsuCity sits at the intersection of three prior lines of work: (1) layout-guided city synthesis (InfiniCity, CityDreamer, GaussianCity) that produces city-scale scenes but relies on implicit neural representations with multi-view inconsistency; (2) explicit-mesh methods (CityCraft, UrbanWorld) that offer structural reliability but are constrained to fixed asset libraries, functioning more as 'retrieve-and-place' than generation; and (3) LLM-driven scene composition (SceneCraft, 3D-GPT) that provides expressive control but fails at urban scale. The paper's key architectural departure is a four-stage cascade that uses LongCLIP-conditioned diffusion for layout/height synthesis, ControlNet for spatial alignment, and shape-constrained 3D asset generation (via Hunyuan3D-omni) to produce explicit meshes, thereby combining the geometric fidelity of mesh-based methods with the generative diversity of diffusion-based approaches. The addition of MajutsuAgent (GPT-5-driven object-level editing) and the VLM-based AQS/RDR evaluation protocol are novel system-level contributions not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real gap: no prior framework simultaneously offers text-driven generation, aesthetic style adaptivity, object-level editability, and a principled evaluation protocol for 3D cities. MajutsuDataset (13,300 layout pairs, 1,000 3D assets, 2,300 PBR materials, 1,000 HDR skyboxes) is a practically useful resource that could accelerate follow-up work. However, several open questions remain: the pipeline's scalability to truly city-wide extents (beyond the 512×512 layout resolution), the latency and cost of the multi-stage generation (diffusion + 3D asset synthesis per building), whether the VLM-based evaluation generalizes beyond GPT-5 as evaluator, and how well the editing agent handles complex multi-object or global layout modifications. The 83.7% FID reduction over CityDreamer, while impressive, compares fundamentally different representations (explicit mesh vs. NeRF), limiting its interpretive value.
