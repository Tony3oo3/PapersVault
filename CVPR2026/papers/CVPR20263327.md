## Problem

Modern vision-language models are no longer evaluated on domain-specific action recognition because no sufficiently diverse and challenging dataset exists to test this capability. Existing benchmarks either use coarse-grained labels, restrict to a handful of sports, or probe fine-grained temporal attributes lacking real-world utility. This paper introduces VideoNet to revitalize domain-specific action recognition as a meaningful evaluation target for VLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263327/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a well-trimmed video clip (median 5 s, mean 12.8 s) together with an action name and its domain, and must output a binary yes/no judgment on whether the clip depicts that action. In the few-shot setting, up to 3 additional in-context video demonstrations (drawn from a separate pool) are prepended to the query. Negatives are deliberately hard: they are actions within the same domain that closely resemble the positive, differing only in subtle motion or visual details, rather than random same-domain actions.

## Method

The benchmark is built via a three-stage human-annotation pipeline (web search → 3-way clip verification by majority vote → temporal trimming) that entirely circumvents the need for domain experts, yielding 1,087 actions across 38 domains with 97.6% expert-verified accuracy. Hard negatives are generated by an LLM and then refined by a reasoning model (o3-mini) to remove ambiguous or co-occurring pairs. For training data, a fully automated pipeline crawls 8 M videos, uses Gemini 2.5 Flash as a clip localizer, extracts word-level timestamps with WhisperX, and labels clips via three filtering strategies (TRANSCRIPTLOCALIZED, TRANSCRIPTLOCALIZEDTITLEMATCH, SINGLEACTION), producing 160 K–500 K clips. Fine-tuning Molmo2-4B (ViT + LLM via MLP connector, 4 fps up to 64 frames, 8 K steps, batch 128) on the SINGLEACTION subset yields a 12-point gain, surpassing all Gemini models and GPT-4o on VideoNet. A key empirical finding is that VLMs gain only 2–3 points from 3 in-context examples versus 13 points for humans, revealing that current video models are poor few-shot learners.

## Results

On the VideoNet benchmark (binary action recognition, 50% = random chance), the fine-tuned Molmo2-4B (FT) achieves 67.36% overall accuracy, a 12.35 pp improvement over its base model (55.01%). The best proprietary model, GPT-5, reaches 71.51%, while the best prior open-weight model (LLaVA-Video-7B) attains only 55.98%. Non-expert humans score 69.1% zero-shot and 82.7% with 3 in-context examples, substantially outperforming all models in the few-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned Molmo2-4B (FT) at 67.36% decisively outperforms all three open-weight baselines: InternVL3-8B (52.16%, +15.2 pp), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (55.01%, +12.4 pp), and LLaVA-Video-7B (55.98%, +11.4 pp). It also surpasses both Gemini 2.5 Pro (65.78%) and GPT-4o (66.76%), making it the strongest fully open model on the benchmark. However, it still trails GPT-4.1 (69.02%) and GPT-5 (71.51%) by 1.7 and 4.2 pp respectively. Among the open-weight baselines, LLaVA-Video-7B leads in Beauty (58.87%) and Crafts (57.84%), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B is strongest in Food (72.16%), indicating complementary domain strengths that the fine-tuned model consolidates. The 12 pp gain from fine-tuning on 163K SINGLEACTION clips is comparable to the 13.6 pp gain humans achieve from 3 in-context examples, suggesting targeted training data can close much of the few-shot learning gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action recognition task from coarse-grained or sport-limited datasets (Kinetics, ActionAtlas, MultiSports) to a broad, fine-grained, domain-specific formulation spanning 38 domains and 1,087 actions. It builds directly on Molmo2-4B as the base VLM for fine-tuning, and draws on the LLM few-shot learning literature (Brown et al., Min et al.) to pose the question of whether in-context learning transfers to the video modality. A key methodological departure is the use of LLM-generated hard negatives refined by a reasoning model (o3-mini), and a three-stage human annotation pipeline that entirely circumvents the need for domain experts—contrasting with prior works like EgoExo4D and ActionAtlas that rely on expert verification or limited filming locations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental gap: VLMs are poor few-shot learners for visual tasks, with even GPT-5 gaining only ~1% from three in-context video examples versus a 13.6% gain for humans, suggesting current video models lack the perceptual mechanisms underlying human visual generalization. The finding that a 4B model fine-tuned on 160K automatically-labeled clips surpasses all Gemini models and GPT-4o implies that domain-specific training data, not model scale, is the bottleneck for fine-grained action understanding. Open questions include whether the few-shot deficit can be addressed through architectural changes or training objectives, and at what scale data coverage begins to matter more than data quality for long-tail domains.
