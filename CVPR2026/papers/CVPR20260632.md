## Problem

Existing functional scene understanding methods treat pairwise functional relations in isolation, failing to capture the scene-wide interdependence that humans exploit to resolve ambiguity (e.g., which knob controls which burner). Because functional relations are inherently underdetermined from static visual observations, prior approaches neither model the full distribution over plausible connections nor produce calibrated per-edge confidence scores, limiting their utility for downstream planning and decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260632/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of posed RGB-D frames (e.g., 60 uniformly sampled views per scene) and produces a part-aware 3D object map fused across views, from which a functional 3D scene graph is derived. The graph encodes both intra-object (part-to-part, part-to-object) and inter-object functional relations as edges, each annotated with a calibrated confidence score obtained from marginalization over the factor graph. The output is thresholded at 0.5 to yield the final discrete scene graph, but the full posterior distribution is retained for downstream uncertainty-aware reasoning.

## Method

FunFact operates in two stages. First, a VLM (GPT-4.1) proposes hierarchical object-part labels and coarse bounding boxes, which are grounded via GroundingDINO detection and SAM segmentation, then back-projected and fused across views following the BBQ protocol to build a consistent 3D object-part map. Second, an LLM proposes semantically plausible functional relation templates with cardinality patterns (one-to-one vs. one-to-many), which are instantiated as candidate edges over all matching part-object or part-part pairs. These candidate edges are encoded as binary variables in a dual factor graph—where scene-graph nodes become cardinality constraint factors and scene-graph edges become variables—regularized by proximity priors (exponential decay in edge length) and structural cardinality factors that penalize over- or under-connected nodes. Belief propagation is then run on disjoint connected components of this factor graph to jointly infer the posterior over all functional edges, yielding calibrated per-edge confidence scores that resolve ambiguities through scene-wide context rather than independent pairwise scoring.

## Results

On FunGraph3D, FunFact achieves 48.7% / 63.9% overall triplet recall (R@5/R@10), substantially outperforming OpenFunGraph (29.8% / 45.0%). On the newly introduced FunThor benchmark, FunFact reaches 54.1% / 54.7% (R@3/R@5) overall triplet recall versus 15.1% / 17.6% for OpenFunGraph — a 39.0 pp gain at R@3. For scene reconstruction on FunGraph3D, FunFact attains 77.9% / 86.2% overall node recall (R@3/R@10) compared to 55.5% / 65.8% for OpenFunGraph. On FunThor, FunFact also achieves markedly better calibration: ECE drops from 0.51 to 0.07 for ambiguous knob–burner and switch–lamp relations, and precision improves from 23.4% to 31.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: Open3DSG (CVPR 2024), ConceptGraphs (ICRA 2024), and OpenFunGraph (CVPR 2025). On scene reconstruction (Table 1), FunFact outperforms all three on FunGraph3D overall node recall (77.9% R@3 vs. 55.5% for OpenFunGraph, 38.9% for ConceptGraph+IED, 33.4% for Open3DSG) and on SceneFun3D objects (90.5% R@3 vs. 81.8% for OpenFunGraph). On functional triplet prediction (Table 2), FunFact decisively beats OpenFunGraph on FunGraph3D (48.7% vs. 29.8% R@5) and FunThor (54.1% vs. 15.1% R@3), but underperforms on SceneFun3D (41.0% vs. 60.4% R@5) due to systematic label-mismatch artifacts between FunFact's fine-grained open-vocabulary predictions and SceneFun3D's coarse generic labels. Open3DSG and ConceptGraphs are only compared on scene reconstruction, not on functional relations, as they do not model functional edges. The calibration advantage is most striking: FunFact reduces ECE on ambiguous relations from 0.51 (OpenFunGraph) to 0.07, an 86% relative reduction, while also improving precision from 23.4% to 31.9% on FunThor.

## Relation to Prior Work

FunFact extends the open-vocabulary functional 3D scene graph line initiated by OpenFunGraph and FunGraph, which use LLMs and vision-language models to propose pairwise functional relations but treat each edge independently. The key methodological departure is the dual factor-graph formulation: scene-graph edges become binary variables and scene-graph nodes become cardinality constraint factors, enabling joint belief-propagation inference that exploits structural priors (one-to-one mappings) and geometric proximity. For the reconstruction backbone, the pipeline follows BBQ for multi-view 3D fusion and leverages GroundingDINO + SAM for open-vocabulary object and part detection, building on the broader 3D scene graph literature (ConceptGraphs, Open3DSG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical contribution is calibrated per-edge confidence: the 7× reduction in ECE for ambiguous relations (0.51→0.07) means downstream systems (robotic verification, AR guidance) can trust which functional predictions are reliable without additional heuristics. The FunThor benchmark is valuable because its dense, rule-based annotations from AI2-THOR finally enable precision and calibration evaluation that sparse manual datasets (SceneFun3D, FunGraph3D) cannot support. Open questions include reducing LLM inference latency for real-time deployment, resolving the over/under-segmentation issues in part detection, and extending the framework to dynamic or partially observed scenes where new evidence can update beliefs incrementally.
