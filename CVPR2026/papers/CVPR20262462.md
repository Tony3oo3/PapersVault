## Problem

Existing video-based dynamical parameter estimation methods typically ignore external forcing inputs, recover only a limited subset of parameters, fail to handle implicit dynamical components (e.g., frictional drag), and assume known invariants such as initial conditions or coordinate-frame origins. In real-world settings—rover excursions, quadrotor flights—video occludes actuation commands and the absolute coordinate frame is unknown, making correct kinematic inference ill-posed without additional modalities and joint calibration.

## Input / Output

EMMA accepts any synchronized subset of RGB video frames (processed at 640×640 via YOLOv11 detection + Kalman smoothing), raw audio at 44.1 kHz (resampled to 22.05 kHz, STFT with FFT-2048/hop-512 yielding RMS, spectral centroid, and peak frequency), and chart/figure images (discretized into (x,y) time-series via PIL + OpenCV masking). All modality features are temporally interpolated to a common grid, concatenated, and spatially encoded into fixed-length vectors of dimension 100. The output is a set of K physical parameters (denormalized from a sigmoid range via an affine mapping), calibration invariants (coordinate origins, initial states) from ReLU-activated dense-layer cells, and a forward-simulated trajectory serving as a validated digital twin.

## Method

The core estimator is a Liquid Time-Constant Network (LTC-NN, 64 hidden units, 6 ODE unfolding steps) whose input-dependent time constant τ_i(t) naturally absorbs exogenous forcing inputs, while its over-parameterized hidden states (64 units vs. a few physical states) provide capacity to encode implicit dynamical components such as friction. A dense readout head with sigmoid activations maps LTC hidden outputs to physical parameters, and additional ReLU-activated cells linearly track calibration invariants (e.g., pendulum pivot, coordinate origin) that are learned jointly rather than assumed. Training is fully unsupervised with respect to parameters: a physics-informed loss combines a measurement-masked trajectory discrepancy (only observed states contribute, via a diagonal matrix M) with ReLU-based bound penalties enforcing physical validity, and the entire pipeline is optimized end-to-end through differentiable ODE simulation. The framework is trained with AdamW + cosine annealing over 16-frame windows with stride 1, batch 32, and 0.3 dropout, requiring only 53.2K parameters versus 5.7M for the Delfys baseline.

## Results

On the Delfys benchmark (75 videos, 5 physical systems), EMMA achieves near-ground-truth parameter recovery with very low variance: pendulum length L = 0.86 ± 0.073 m (GT 0.90), Torricelli drainage constant k = 0.0132 ± 0.0008 (GT 0.0128), and sliding-block acceleration a = 2.27 ± 0.029 m/s² (GT 2.300). On real-world multi-modal data, EMMA estimates rover parameters with 8.8% ± 1.7% average error and drone parameters with 15.9% ± 7.4% average error, including implicit dynamics parameters (CoM height, thrust/torque coefficients). On chart-based extraction, EMMA reduces parameter RMSE by 2–3× over PySINDY (e.g., Lorenz θ_rmse: 0.016 vs 2.3; F8 Crusader θ_rmse: 7.81 vs 21.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMMA outperforms Delfys [10] (the primary video-only baseline) on pendulum length at 90 cm (0.86 vs 1.07 m) and free-fall acceleration at medium size (9.95 vs 9.51 m/s²), while matching Delfys closely on Torricelli (0.0132 vs 0.0132). Against NIRPI [18], EMMA is substantially more accurate and stable — NIRPI shows large variance (±0.53 on pendulum) and negative acceleration estimates on the sliding block, whereas EMMA stays within 0.03 of ground truth. PAIG [21] only covers three of five systems and shows systematic bias (e.g., pendulum L = 1.01 m regardless of true length). On multi-parameter extraction (Table 4a/b), EMMA dominates PySINDy [7]: for the pendulum, EMMA recovers τ = 0.055 ± 0.026 s⁻¹ (GT 0.05) while PySINDy gives 0.73 ± 0.57; for the sliding block, EMMA estimates slope α = 24.72° (GT 25°) versus PySINDy's 27.00 ± 2.74°. On chart-based extraction (Table 5), EMMA reduces parameter RMSE by an order of magnitude over PySINDY on chaotic and biological systems (Lorenz θ_rmse 0.016 vs 2.3; HIV therapy θ_rmse 0.45 vs 4.5), with the gap widening under implicit dynamics where only one state is observed.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMMA builds on Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) networks [17] as its core continuous-time dynamics learner, repurposing their input-dependent time constants to model both forcing inputs and implicit latent dynamics within a single architecture. It departs from the video-only parameter estimation line (Delfys, PAIG, NIRPI) by introducing audio and chart-image modalities to resolve state occlusions and hidden actuation, and by jointly estimating calibration invariants (coordinate origins, initial conditions) that prior methods assume known. The physics-informed loss with differentiable simulation aligns with the broader physics-informed learning and system-identification tradition (PINNs, SINDyPI), but EMMA's key conceptual contribution is unifying explicit parameter recovery, implicit dynamics inference, and invariant calibration in one end-to-end framework rather than treating them as separate sub-problems.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EMMA addresses a practical gap in digital-twin construction for autonomous platforms: real rovers and drones operate under unobserved controller inputs and unmeasured resistive forces that video alone cannot resolve, making prior single-modality approaches ill-posed. The 107× smaller model size (53.2K vs. 5.7M parameters) relative to Delfys while matching or exceeding accuracy is practically significant for edge deployment. Open questions include whether the linear audio-speed prior generalizes beyond non-flight regimes, how the framework scales to systems with more than a dozen parameters, and whether the LTC-based ODE integration overhead becomes prohibitive for high-dimensional or long-horizon dynamics. The absence of baselines for the forced/implicit multimodal experiments limits the strength of the comparative claims in those settings.
