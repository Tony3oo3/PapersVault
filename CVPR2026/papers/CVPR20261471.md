## Problem

In AI-generated images, perceptual quality degradation and text-content misalignment frequently co-occur, making it difficult to determine whether a poor subjective rating stems from rendering artifacts or prompt noncompliance. Existing natural IQA models ignore textual information, while current CLIP-based AGIQA methods either evaluate quality and alignment in isolation or fail to model their interactive relationship, leaving a gap in jointly assessing both dimensions within a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261471/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an AI-generated image (resized to 224×224, CLIP-normalized) and its original text prompt as input. The prompt is expanded into five template-based anchor sentences of the form 'A photo where {badly/poorly/fairly/well/perfectly} matches "prompt"' to create a multi-level semantic reference space. The output is a continuous scalar score for each evaluation dimension (perceptual quality and text-image alignment), computed as the expectation over five discrete quality levels via a softmax-weighted sum of cosine similarities between fused visual features and the textual anchors.

## Method

DPGF-Net employs a dual-path architecture built on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone. The local path (TCPGA) injects spatial priors—derived from Re-IQA's distortion-aware (Q_map) or content-aware (C_map) encoders—directly into cross-modal attention as a log-bias term, so that text-conditioned patch aggregation preferentially attends to quality-sensitive or semantically relevant regions. The global path (FIM) applies FiLM-style modulation to the CLIP whole-image embedding using pooled task-specific global priors, and simultaneously predicts a per-image adaptive fusion coefficient α. The two paths are combined as s = α·s_patch + (1−α)·s_full, enabling the model to dynamically balance local distortion sensitivity against holistic semantic alignment. Training is supervised with mean absolute error against human scores, using grouped learning rates (5×10⁻⁵ for TCPGA/FIM, 1×10⁻⁵ for the prior gate) over 25 epochs on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

DPGF-Net achieves state-of-the-art performance on all three AGIQA benchmarks, ranking first in 11 out of 12 PLCC/SRCC metrics. On AGIQA-3K, it reaches SRCC 0.9010 (quality) and 0.8013 (alignment); on AIGCIQA2023, SRCC 0.8806 (quality) and 0.8278 (alignment); and on PKU-I2IQA, SRCC 0.7813 (quality) and 0.8119 (alignment). The only metric where it does not rank first is AGIQA-3K alignment PLCC (0.8698 vs. IPCE's 0.8725). In cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), DPGF-Net also leads with average SRCC of 0.7694 (quality) and 0.6387 (alignment), outperforming the next-best method IPCE (0.7415 / 0.6317).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPGF-Net outperforms the strongest prior AGIQA method, IPCE [16], on 11 of 12 metrics, with the sole exception being AGIQA-3K alignment PLCC (0.8698 vs. 0.8725, a 0.003 gap). Against QMI-Net [2], the second-closest competitor, DPGF-Net leads by 0.019–0.031 SRCC across all six dataset-dimension combinations. Traditional NR-IQA methods (DBCNN, HyperIQA, MUSIQ, TReS) lag substantially, with SRCC values typically 0.05–0.20 below DPGF-Net, confirming that natural-image distortion models are poorly suited to AI-generated content. Among CLIP-based AGIQA baselines, DPGF-Net surpasses IP-IQA [18] by up to 0.17 SRCC (PKU-I2IQA quality: 0.7813 vs. 0.6111) and TIER [35] by up to 0.13 SRCC (AIGCIQA2023 alignment: 0.8278 vs. 0.7210). In cross-dataset transfer (Table 2), DPGF-Net maintains its lead over IPCE by 0.028 SRCC on average quality and 0.007 on average alignment, demonstrating that the dual-prior mechanism generalizes better than template-only or single-branch designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPGF-Net builds directly on two prior works: Re-IQA's dual-encoder structure for extracting distortion-aware and content-aware spatial priors, and IPCE's single-prompt template that combines content descriptions with quality-level adverbs. It departs from the broader AGIQA literature by explicitly disentangling image-side distortion from content features (rather than treating the image as a monolithic input) and by coupling this with a dual-path local-global fusion (TCPGA + FIM) that models the interaction between perceptual quality and semantic alignment within a single framework. This contrasts with prior CLIP-based AGIQA methods (IP-IQA, PSCR, TIER) that evaluate the two dimensions separately, and with template-based methods (IPCE) that represent both dimensions but lack explicit task differentiation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in AGIQA: the inability to determine whether a poor subjective rating stems from visual artifacts or prompt noncompliance. By providing a unified scoring framework with interpretable local-global weighting, it offers a more diagnostic tool for generation model developers. However, the model still uses separate scoring functions for quality and alignment (τ ∈ {qual, align}) rather than truly joint prediction, which the authors acknowledge as future work. Open questions include whether the dual-prior decomposition generalizes to video generation or multi-turn editing scenarios, and whether the reliance on a frozen CLIP backbone limits ceiling performance.
