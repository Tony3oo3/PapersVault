## Problem

Existing artist-like mesh generators rely on autoregressive next-token prediction, which scales quadratically with mesh size, requires quantizing continuous vertex coordinates (causing vertex collapse and face overlap), and can terminate sequences prematurely. No prior method achieves both high-fidelity mesh generation and efficient, parallel inference without discretization artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261559/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud (or image) as conditioning input, encoded by a pre-trained point cloud encoder into feature tokens. It outputs a complete 3D triangular mesh specified by per-vertex positions, outward normals, and edge connectivity, all in continuous space. The mesh is padded to a fixed maximum vertex count, and the decoder produces a validity mask indicating which vertices are active; faces are recovered by detecting 3-edge loops and oriented via averaged vertex normals.

## Method

MeshFlow introduces MeshVAE, a VAE that compresses a mesh into N_v/4 continuous latent vectors by representing edges through per-vertex edge embeddings trained with a contrastive loss (positive pairs for existing edges, negative for non-edges), and encoding face orientation via predicted vertex normals rather than explicit winding order. A TokenMerge operator (analogous to pixel-shuffle) downsamples vertex tokens, followed by cross-attention to the full input set, yielding a compact latent code; the decoder mirrors this with TokenSplit and cross-attention. Generation uses a Rectified Flow Diffusion Transformer (427M params) trained with Conditional Flow Matching, which denoises all vertex positions, normals, and edge embeddings in parallel in ~1 second, conditioned on point-cloud features via cross-attention and using logit-normal timestep sampling to emphasize fine geometric detail. A post-processing step detects boundary edges and triangulates short (k<5) polygonal rings to repair minor topological defects from diffusion noise.

## Results

On the Toys4K dataset for point-cloud-conditioned mesh generation, MeshFlow achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 2.45 and Hausdorff Distance (HD) of 6.40 (both ×100), the best among all compared methods. It also runs in 1.2 s per batch, making it 2.8× faster than the next-best method (FastMesh-V1K at 3.41 s) and ~22× faster than MeshAnything (26.06 s). For MeshVAE reconstruction, the method achieves a CD of 1.29 with a compression ratio of 0.014, which is 16× more compact than the most efficient prior tokenizer (TreeMeshGPT at 0.22) while maintaining competitive reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshFlow outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric (CD, HD, and inference time). Against the strongest AR baseline FastMesh-V4K, MeshFlow reduces CD from 4.05 to 2.45 (−39%) and HD from 10.22 to 6.40 (−37%) while being 5.5× faster (1.2 s vs 6.60 s). Compared to the earlier AR methods (MeshAnything, MeshAnything V2, TreeMeshGPT, BPT), the gains are even larger: CD improves by 55–80% and inference time drops by 22–41×. In the MeshVAE reconstruction comparison (Table 1), MeshFlow's CD of 1.29 is slightly higher than EdgeRunner's 1.21 but far lower than TreeMeshGPT's 1.63, while its compression ratio of 0.014 is 16× better than TreeMeshGPT (0.22) and 34× better than EdgeRunner (0.47). The paper notes that AR methods frequently suffer from early stopping, producing incomplete meshes, an issue entirely avoided by the parallel diffusion-based generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshFlow departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm for mesh generation (MeshAnything, TreeMeshGPT, BPT, FastMesh) by replacing sequential token prediction with a continuous latent space and parallel flow-based denoising. Its mesh representation builds directly on SpaceMesh's idea of encoding discrete edge connectivity via continuous per-vertex embeddings, but simplifies the orientation encoding to vertex normals and drops the watertight/manifold assumption. The VAE's downsampling strategy draws on 3DShape2VecSet's vertex-subset packing and InternVL's pixel-shuffle (TokenMerge), while the generator adapts Rectified Flow and the DiT architecture with SD3's logit-normal timestep sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18× inference speedup with linear (rather than quadratic) scaling in mesh size, combined with the elimination of vertex quantization, makes high-fidelity artist-grade mesh generation practically viable for real-time and interactive applications. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to triangular faces (artists often prefer quads), minor topological holes require heuristic post-processing, and standard metrics (CD, HD) fail to capture topology quality such as flipped normals. Open directions include extending to polygonal meshes, developing topology-aware evaluation metrics, and integrating UV/texture generation.
