## Problem

UAV-based building defect segmentation suffers from mis-segmentation due to heterogeneous surface textures, illumination variations, and intertwined defect types (cracks, spalling, moss) that can mimic material patterns. Existing self-attention-based segmentation methods rely on row-major traversal for feature unfolding, which disrupts spatial continuity and weakens structural perception, while most models also overlook material-defect correlations that limit robustness in real-world inspection scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263257/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images of building surfaces (typically captured by UAVs) and produces pixel-level segmentation masks over five classes: fissures, peel off, mosses, lime, and stone. The Swin-Tiny backbone processes inputs to produce four multi-scale feature maps at 128×128, 64×64, 32×32, and 16×16 resolutions; the Hilbert curve-based flattening is applied specifically to the 16×16 feature maps during the attention computation in the decoder, where the spatial dimensionality reduction is most critical for preserving topological adjacency.

## Method

TPSegformer combines a Swin Transformer-Tiny backbone with a custom TPDecoder that operates in two stages. First, a dual-branch feature enhancement module fuses channel attention (ECANet-style 1D convolution on GAP descriptors) with a spatial branch (3×3 conv + BN + ReLU) via element-wise multiplication to strengthen intermediate representations. Second, the decoder's High-Low Feature Fusion Module concatenates upsampled low-resolution features with high-resolution features and compresses them through depthwise separable convolutions, while the Lightweight Correlation Computation Module generates Q, K, V maps from class-specific layers and applies a Hilbert curve-based 2D-to-1D indexing to preserve spatial topology during attention computation. The key insight is that Hilbert curve traversal minimizes locality loss (measured as summed squared Euclidean distances between consecutive flattened elements) compared to row-major or Z-order traversal, thereby maintaining spatial adjacency in the attention matrix and improving inter-class correlation modeling. An auxiliary supervision branch with a Joint Auxiliary Loss (Dice Loss + Sobel-gradient Edge-Aware Loss) is applied to the backbone's third-stage output to enforce boundary-aware learning at intermediate resolutions.

## Results

On the BD3 building defect segmentation dataset, TPSegformer achieves 80.77% mIoU and 90.22% pixel accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline (SegFormer) by 6.74% mIoU and 3.79% ACC. On the more challenging Dacl10k dataset with 19 defect categories, TPSegformer reaches 44.27% mIoU and 60.32% ACC, surpassing the second-best method (PIDNet) by 8.06% mIoU and 9.57% ACC. The method wins on 4 of 5 BD3 categories (Fissures, Peel Off, Mosses, Lime) but trails SegFormer on the Stone category (83.44% vs. 93.72%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TPSegformer outperforms all six baselines on overall mIoU and ACC on BD3, with the largest margins over DDRNet (+17.81% mIoU) and DSNet (+17.24% mIoU). The closest competitor is SegFormer (74.03% mIoU), which TPSegformer surpasses by 6.74% mIoU; however, SegFormer retains a notable advantage on the Stone category (93.72% vs. 83.44%), suggesting TPSegformer's topology-preserving attention is most beneficial for fine-grained defect classes (cracks, moss) rather than large homogeneous material regions. On the Dacl10k generalization benchmark, TPSegformer leads in 12 of 19 categories, with particularly strong gains on Crack (38.60% vs. 27.27% for CCNet) and Efflorescence (52.51% vs. 42.54% for CCNet), while PIDNet is the nearest rival at 36.21% mIoU. DDRNet and BiSeNetV2 struggle most with complex textures (Peel Off at 44.99% and 65.43% respectively), whereas TPSegformer reaches 91.11%, highlighting the benefit of multi-scale fusion and Hilbert-curve topology preservation for intertwined defect boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

TPSegformer extends the HilbertSCNet line of work (Zheng et al., 2024), which first embedded a Hilbert curve into self-attention for aerial image segmentation, by adding a systematic comparison against row-major and Z-order traversals, a theoretical locality-loss analysis, and integration into a full encoder-decoder pipeline. The decoder's feature enhancement module is adapted from ECA-Net (Wang et al., CVPR 2020), augmented with a spatial branch, while the backbone follows the Swin Transformer family. The paper positions itself against the broader segmentation decoder literature (U-Net, PSPNet, SegFormer, BiSeNetV2) by arguing that these methods treat multi-scale fusion, inter-class correlation, and spatial topology preservation as separate concerns, and unifies them in a single lightweight decoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important but under-resourced domain—UAV-based building defect inspection—where heterogeneous materials and illumination variations cause persistent mis-segmentation. The Hilbert curve's topology-preserving property offers a principled alternative to row-major flattening in attention, and the locality-loss analysis provides a clear quantitative justification. However, the improvement over row-major traversal is moderate (~4 mIoU points), the added computational cost of Hilbert indexing (an order of magnitude slower at 128×128) may limit real-time UAV deployment, and the Dacl10k generalization results (44.27% mIoU) remain far from deployment-ready. Open questions include whether the topology-preserving benefit persists at higher resolutions or with larger backbones, and whether the approach transfers to other dense-prediction tasks beyond building defects.
