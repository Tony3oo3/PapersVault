## Problem

Most video crowd counting (VCC) methods extract and fuse spatio-temporal features from neighboring frames via auxiliary networks, inflating model complexity, storage, and inference latency while requiring multi-frame buffering. This makes them impractical for real-time deployment under limited computational resources. The paper asks whether lightweight image-based counting (ICC) models can be adapted for VCC without adding modules or inference overhead, and provides the first systematic theoretical and empirical investigation of when single-frame inference suffices for video-level counting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262549/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes pairs of adjacent RGB frames (e.g., 1920×1080 on DRONECROWD, 238×158 on UCSD) passed independently through a lightweight ICC backbone (VGG19-based by default) to produce two predicted density maps. At inference, only a single frame is fed to the network, yielding one crowd density map in the same mode as standard image counting. The density map is constructed by convolving ground-truth dot annotations with a Gaussian kernel of bandwidth 8 pixels, and all loss computation operates on the characteristic function (ChF) of these maps over a frequency range of [−0.3, 0.3]².

## Method

The core contribution is a statistical regularizer that encodes spatio-temporal consistency directly in the frequency domain via the characteristic function. The ST information is defined as the constraint ‖C_{t+1} − C_t‖₁ ≤ ε*, where ε* is a data-driven bound computed once from ground-truth ChFs of all training frame pairs, making it model-agnostic and requiring no auxiliary networks. The regularizer activates only when the predicted ChF difference exceeds ε*, penalizing the L2 norm of the excess; a motion-tolerant variant reweights each frequency by H_t(w), derived from a theorem characterizing normal-motion-induced ChF oscillation, so expected people movement is not penalized. The motion covariance Λ governing H_t is estimated by fitting a theoretical variance function to empirical frequency-wise ChF differences across the training set, eliminating manual tuning. Theoretically, the paper proves that the gap between optimal single-frame and multi-frame estimators (Δ_{l,r}) vanishes under temporal redundancy or full single-frame observability, providing a principled criterion for when ICC models are sufficient for VCC.

## Results

On six benchmark datasets, SR achieves the best MAE and MSE on VENICE (8.2/10.5), MALL (1.43/1.82), FDST (1.27/1.61), and VSCROWD (5.4/9.5), the best MAE on DRONECROWD (14.1), and 2nd-best MAE/MSE on UCSD (0.75/0.97). Notably, SR uses single-frame inference (99.5 fps on FDST) while being trained on video frame pairs, outperforming multi-frame video-based methods by up to 3.1 MAE on VSCROWD (5.4 vs 7.1) and 1.7 MAE on DRONECROWD (14.1 vs 15.8). Training time is 85.5 s/epoch vs 435–476 s for competing video methods, and inference is 0.010 s/image vs 0.016–0.043 s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SR outperforms all image-based baselines (CSRNet, P2PNet, MAN, ChfL, PET, Gramformer) on every dataset where both report results, with the largest margins on VENICE (8.2 vs 12.2 MAE for P2PNet) and VSCROWD (5.4 vs 7.0 for P2PNet). Against video-based methods, SR achieves the best MAE on VENICE (8.2 vs 11.1 DACM), MALL (1.43 vs 1.44 DACM), FDST (1.27 vs 1.31 DACM), DRONECROWD (14.1 vs 15.8 STNNet), and VSCROWD (5.4 vs 7.1 DACM). The only losses are on UCSD where CLRNet edges ahead (0.72 vs 0.75 MAE) and on DRONECROWD MSE where STNNet is slightly better (18.7 vs 19.9). A critical practical distinction is that SR requires only a single frame at inference (99.5 fps) while all video-based competitors require buffering multiple frames, making SR uniquely suited for real-time deployment under resource constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the characteristic function (ChF) framework from Shu et al. (CVPR 2022), which used ChF as a static frequency-domain loss for image crowd counting, by analyzing its temporal evolution across consecutive frames to extract spatio-temporal (ST) information. It deliberately departs from the dominant VCC paradigm of auxiliary ST extraction and fusion modules (optical flow networks, temporal transformers, LSTM-based architectures) by instead encoding ST information as a statistical regularizer applied only during training, leaving inference as standard single-frame image counting. The theoretical contribution (Theorem 4) formalizes the information-theoretic gap between single-frame and multi-frame estimators, providing a principled criterion for when lightweight ICC models are theoretically sufficient for VCC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time deployment: achieving SOTA accuracy on six benchmarks with 99.5 fps single-frame inference (vs. 23–61 fps for video-based methods) removes the need for multi-frame buffering and heavy temporal modules, making VCC viable on resource-constrained hardware. The framework's portability across five different lightweight ICC architectures (MCNN, CSRNet, CAN, VGG19, MAN) suggests broad applicability. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the ChF representation and the specific VGG19-based backbone; whether the statistical regularizer transfers to point-based or transformer-based counting paradigms remains untested. An open question is how the method behaves under large domain shifts or very long videos where the empirically estimated ε* and Λ* may become stale.
