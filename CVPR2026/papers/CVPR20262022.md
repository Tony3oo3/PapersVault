## Problem

Softmax score-based pseudo-label filtering in semi-supervised semantic segmentation suffers from neural network overconfidence: even in the highest-confidence bin (score > 0.95), pseudo-label accuracy is only 0.81, and the Pearson correlation between softmax scores and accuracy is merely 0.79. This means a substantial fraction of incorrect pseudo-labels enters training, degrading model performance. The paper asks how to explicitly model prediction uncertainty from a distributional perspective rather than relying on unreliable point-estimate confidence scores.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262022/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images through a teacher-student pipeline: the teacher receives weakly augmented views (random crop, resize, horizontal flip) to generate pseudo-labels and uncertainty maps, while the student receives strongly augmented views (color jitter, grayscale, Gaussian blur) for consistency training. Crop sizes are 321×321 (Pascal/COCO) or 801×801 (Cityscapes) for the ResNet-101 variant, and 518×518 or 798×798 for the DINOv2-S variant. The network's final layer outputs non-negative evidence values (via ReLU) rather than logits, from which a per-pixel uncertainty scalar in [0,1) is derived to gate pseudo-label acceptance.

## Method

The core idea replaces the softmax head with a ReLU-activated evidence head and places a uniform Dirichlet prior Dir(p; 1) over class probabilities, so that Bayes' theorem yields a Dirichlet posterior Dir(p; 1+e) parameterized directly by the extracted evidence. Uncertainty is computed as u = K / Σ(1+e_i), a bounded quantity in [0,1) that correlates far more strongly with pseudo-label accuracy (Pearson 0.86) than softmax scores. To capture collective evidence shared across class subsets (e.g., visual patterns supporting both 'truck' and 'bus'), HESS introduces a Hyper-Evidence Extractor that produces hyper-evidence for arbitrary class subsets, then a learned fully-connected layer with a straight-through unit-step activation maps hyper-evidence back to per-class basic evidence. The training objective is a Dirichlet cross-entropy loss expressed via the Digamma function, and pseudo-labels are filtered by an uncertainty threshold (0.25) rather than a softmax score threshold.

## Results

On Pascal VOC 2012 (RN-101), UniMatch+HESS achieves 77.5% mIoU at the most challenging 1/16 partition (92 labeled images), surpassing the previous best (BeyondPixel, 77.3%) and the UniMatch baseline (75.2%) by 2.3 points. With the DINOv2-S backbone, UniMatch V2+HESS reaches 80.9% mIoU at 1/16 and 88.5% at full data, setting new state-of-the-art on Pascal. On Cityscapes (RN-101), the method gains +1.3% over UniMatch at 1/16 (78.0 vs. 76.7), and on COCO (XC-65) it improves by +1.9% at 1/512 (33.8 vs. 31.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HESS consistently outperforms all 16 compared baselines across all three datasets and partition protocols. On Pascal at 1/16, UniMatch+HESS (77.5%) beats the strongest prior method BeyondPixel (77.3%) by 0.2% and CSL (76.8%) by 0.7%, while the DINOv2-S variant (80.9%) leads all methods by a clear margin. On Cityscapes at 1/16, UniMatch+HESS (78.0%) surpasses BeyondPixel (78.5%) is slightly behind but UniMatch V2+HESS (81.8%) leads all methods. On COCO at 1/512, UniMatch V2+HESS (41.4%) exceeds CSL (35.1%) by 6.3% and AllSpark (34.1%) by 7.3%. The gains are largest under extreme data scarcity (1/16 or 1/512 partitions), where HESS's superior pseudo-label filtering provides 1.9–2.3% improvements over the respective UniMatch baselines, confirming that reliable uncertainty estimation is most critical when labeled data is minimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., NeurIPS 2018), which models classification uncertainty via Dirichlet posteriors over class probabilities, into the semi-supervised semantic segmentation setting where it replaces the standard softmax-score-based pseudo-label filtering used in teacher-student frameworks like UniMatch and UniMatch V2. The key conceptual departure is HESS (Hyper-ESS), which goes beyond classical evidential learning's per-class exclusive evidence by introducing a hyper-evidence layer that decouples exclusive evidence (supporting a single class) from collective evidence (simultaneously corroborating a subset of classes), mapped back via a learned fully-connected projection. The training objective is also adapted from standard cross-entropy to a Dirichlet-expected cross-entropy using the Digamma function, making the framework a drop-in replacement for the loss in existing SSS pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-recognized weakness in pseudo-label-based semi-supervised segmentation—softmax overconfidence leading to erroneous pseudo-labels entering training—by providing a principled, distributional uncertainty estimate that is more tightly correlated with pseudo-label accuracy (Pearson 0.86 vs. 0.79). The plug-and-play nature (integrated into UniMatch and UniMatch V2 with consistent 1–2.3% mIoU gains across Pascal, Cityscapes, and COCO) makes the technique immediately adoptable. Open questions include how the hyper-evidence mapping scales with the number of class subsets, whether the collective-evidence decomposition generalizes to open-vocabulary or hierarchical segmentation, and whether the modest ESS→HESS gap (≈0.6% mIoU) justifies the additional architectural complexity in practice.
