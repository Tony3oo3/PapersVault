## Problem

Vision-Language Models are increasingly vulnerable to diverse and adaptive jailbreak attacks, yet existing defenses either require prohibitively expensive fine-tuning of the backbone model or introduce excessive global visual/textual perturbations that degrade image realism and user experience. No prior method has demonstrated that a visually imperceptible, logo-sized perturbation can serve as a universal shield generalizing across multiple attack families without compromising benign utility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260534/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a text instruction as input, with a learnable perturbation δ_φ (bounded by ‖δ_φ‖_∞ ≤ ε_def, typically 64/255) injected only within a fixed binary mask M covering ≤2% of pixels, producing a safety-aligned text response. The perturbation is applied as g_φ(x) = x + M ⊙ δ_φ, making it spatially consistent and transferable across images like a watermark. During both training and evaluation, a fixed safety instruction is prepended to the text input to activate the model's intrinsic safety alignment alongside the visual logo.

## Method

SafeLogo formulates visual defense as a bi-level min-max game: the inner loop generates a pool of jailbreak candidates from multiple attack families (PAIR, GCG, PAP, AutoDAN) and selects the strongest via an LLM-as-Judge safety scorer, while the outer loop updates the localized perturbation δ_φ to maximize refusal probability under that worst-case attack. The defense loss combines a safety term (−log P_refuse) and a semantic preservation term (L2 distance between defended and undefended benign responses), weighted by λ. The perturbation is projected back into the L∞ ball after each gradient step, and the fixed spatial mask ensures the logo remains at a consistent location across all images. This is the first work to integrate min-max adversarial optimization into visual defense prompt generation, and ablations show that SafeLogo alone is insufficient—it must be combined with standard safety instructions to trigger and amplify the model's built-in safety mechanisms.

## Results

On MM-SafetyBench (SD+TYPO, in-distribution), SafeLogo reduces ASR for LLaVA-1.5-13B from 13.3%/17.1%/16.9% (No Defense) to 0.9%/4.2%/0.4% (PAIR/GCG/PAP), and for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct from 6.7%/6.3%/4.6% to 0.5%/0.0%/0.1%. On the out-of-distribution VLGuard benchmark, SafeLogo achieves 2.9%/11.4%/1.9% ASR for LLaVA-1.5-13B (vs. 33.3%/32.4%/41.0% undefended) and 1.0%/0.0%/0.0% for Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (vs. 10.5%/15.2%/13.3% undefended). On benign utility, SafeLogo preserves MM-Vet total scores of 39.2 (LLaVA) and 53.3 (Qwen3-VL) and MME total scores of 1553 and 2314 respectively, remaining close to undefended baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeLogo outperforms all four baselines on the OOD VLGuard benchmark for LLaVA-1.5-13B, where DAVSP (the strongest competitor) still yields 17.1% ASR under PAIR while SafeLogo drops to 2.9%, and MLLM-Protector/ECSO remain near undefended levels (25.7%/23.8%). On in-distribution MM-SafetyBench, DAVSP achieves marginally lower ASR than SafeLogo for LLaVA (e.g., 0.1% vs. 0.9% on PAIR/SD+TYPO), but SafeLogo uses only 2% pixel coverage with a bounded perturbation of 64/255, whereas DAVSP perturbs the entire image border. For Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, SafeLogo and DAVSP are essentially tied on both ID and OOD metrics (both reaching 0.0–1.0% ASR), while AdaShield, ECSO, and MLLM-Protector all remain 5–15 percentage points higher on VLGuard. On benign utility (MM-Vet, MME), SafeLogo preserves performance closest to the undefended model among methods that achieve comparable defense strength, whereas DAVSP and AdaShield cause substantial drops (e.g., DAVSP reduces Qwen3-VL MM-Vet total from 65.8 to 41.4, while SafeLogo keeps it at 53.3). Cross-model transfer to Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image further shows SafeLogo maintaining 0–1.0% ASR, while ECSO actually worsens ASR relative to no defense.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeLogo repurposes the classical min-max adversarial training formulation (Tramèr et al., ICLR 2018) from model-robustness training to the optimization of a fixed, spatially constrained visual perturbation, rather than updating backbone weights. It departs from prior VLM defenses—DAVSP (global border perturbations), AdaShield (text-based shield prompts), ECSO (image-to-text conversion), and MLLM-Protector (detector + detoxifier pipeline)—by restricting the defense to a logo-sized region (≤2% pixel coverage) and by introducing a dynamic inner loop that selects the strongest jailbreak from a pool of heterogeneous attack generators (PAIR, GCG, PAP) via LLM-as-Judge, rather than optimizing against a single fixed attack. The paper positions itself as the first to integrate adversarial optimization into visual defense prompt generation, framing the defense as a bi-level game between adaptive attackers and a localized defender.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is clear: a tiny, imperceptible logo patch that can be embedded like a watermark offers a lightweight, model-agnostic defense that transfers across architectures (demonstrated LLaVA → Gemini), avoiding the heavy global modifications or extra inference components of prior methods. However, several open questions remain: the ablation reveals that 9% coverage outperforms the 2% claimed in the abstract, the defense is only effective when combined with standard safety instructions (SafeLogo alone is insufficient), and training requires 5,000 steps per model with a small 600-sample set. Whether this approach generalizes to truly novel, unseen attack families beyond the training pool, and whether the LLM-as-Judge selection step introduces its own vulnerabilities or biases, are important directions for future work.
