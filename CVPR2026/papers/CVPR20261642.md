## Problem

Despite rapid advances in humanoid robot motion generation and control, no standardized, quantitative benchmark exists for evaluating how perceptually human-like robot motions are. Existing evaluations rely on task-oriented metrics (completion rate, trajectory accuracy) that do not capture the subjective impressions of naturalness and fluidity critical to human-robot interaction. This paper introduces the Motion Turing Test framework and the HHMotion dataset to fill this gap with a human-annotated, appearance-disentangled evaluation of motion human-likeness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261642/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 5-second SMPL-X motion sequence normalized into a local root coordinate frame (removing global translation and rotation) as input and outputs a continuous scalar human-likeness score in [0, 5], where 0 denotes completely robotic and 5 denotes indistinguishable from human motion. For VLM baselines, the input is a rendered SMPL-X motion video rather than raw joint parameters. All sequences are appearance-free by construction—original robot and human videos are converted to SMPL-X poses via GVHMR to ensure evaluators judge motion quality alone.

## Method

PTR-Net is a lightweight supervised regression network with three stages: a two-layer bidirectional LSTM encodes long-range temporal dependencies; a stack of spatial-temporal graph convolution blocks (ST-GCN) extracts joint coordination patterns using a parameter-free adjacency design that avoids hard-coded skeleton topology; and a temporal attention pooling module followed by a small MLP regressor produces the final score. Training uses an L2 regression loss augmented with a regularization term that penalizes excessive temporal fluctuations in predicted scores, encouraging smooth and stable outputs. The key finding is that this simple architecture (MAE 0.58, Spearman ρ 0.68) substantially outperforms state-of-the-art VLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3-VL-Plus) under multiple prompting strategies, suggesting that motion human-likeness assessment requires dedicated temporal-spatial modeling rather than general-purpose multimodal reasoning.

## Results

On the HHMotion benchmark, PTR-Net achieves the best performance across all three metrics: MAE 0.5813, RMSE 0.7926, and Spearman's ρ 0.6841. The closest trainable baseline, fine-tuned MotionBERT, reaches MAE 0.6252, RMSE 0.8465, and ρ 0.6142. VLM-based evaluators (Gemini 2.5 Pro and Qwen3-VL-Plus) perform substantially worse, with the best Gemini prompting strategy (PA-CoT) yielding MAE 1.2682 and ρ 0.2303, and Qwen3-VL-Plus producing near-constant outputs (MAE 1.7714, invalid ρ). The 0.79 RMSE indicates meaningful room for improvement in automatically matching human-likeness judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTR-Net decisively outperforms both VLM-based evaluators: compared to the best Gemini 2.5 Pro prompting (PA-CoT), PTR-Net reduces MAE from 1.2682 to 0.5813 (−54%) and improves Spearman's ρ from 0.2303 to 0.6841 (nearly 3×). Qwen3-VL-Plus is the weakest baseline, producing near-constant predictions across all prompting strategies (MAE 1.7714, no valid ρ). Among trainable baselines, fine-tuned MotionBERT is the closest competitor (MAE 0.6252, ρ 0.6142), but PTR-Net still leads by 0.044 MAE and 0.070 ρ. The lightweight Transformer baseline (MAE 0.6387, ρ 0.5728) is also outperformed, confirming that the ST-GCN + temporal LSTM + attention pooling architecture in PTR-Net captures motion coordination cues more effectively than a generic attention backbone. Overall, the results demonstrate that current multimodal LLMs remain inadequate for fine-grained motion human-likeness assessment, even with structured chain-of-thought prompting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the Motion Turing Test, a motion-only evaluation framework inspired by the classical Turing Test and prior embodied Turing Test proposals (e.g., the Embodied Turing Test for AI animals), but specifically targeting humanoid robot kinematics. It repurposes the SMPL-X body model and GVHMR pose estimation to strip appearance cues, and draws on existing motion datasets (AMASS, LAFAN1, Motion-X) and retargeting pipelines (LAFAN1 Retargeting) as data sources, but is the first to pair human and humanoid motions with explicit human-likeness Likert scores. The PTR-Net baseline composes standard components (BiLSTM, ST-GCN, attention pooling) in a straightforward regression architecture, while the VLM comparisons (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3-VL-Plus) and MotionBERT baselines position the work at the intersection of motion analysis and multimodal LLM evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HHMotion dataset and Motion Turing Test benchmark fill a clear gap: prior humanoid motion research relied on task-oriented metrics (completion rate, trajectory accuracy) that do not capture perceptual naturalness, and no standardized human-likeness scoring existed. The finding that state-of-the-art VLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro, Qwen3-VL-Plus) achieve Spearman's ρ of only ~0.16–0.23 on this task, far below a simple PTR-Net (ρ = 0.68), challenges the assumption that general-purpose multimodal models can serve as motion quality evaluators and motivates task-specific approaches. Open questions include whether the 0.80 RMSE ceiling reflects inherent ambiguity in human-likeness perception (as suggested by the human-imitating-humanoid subset), whether PTR-Net can function as a reliable reward signal in RL-based motion generation, and how the benchmark evolves as robot motion quality improves and the human-robot gap narrows.
