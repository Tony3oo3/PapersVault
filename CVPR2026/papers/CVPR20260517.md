## Problem

Large Multimodal Models encode images into hundreds or thousands of visual tokens, causing quadratic growth in LLM inference cost. Existing training-free token reduction methods are limited: importance-based approaches rely on attention or [CLS] tokens (introducing bias, poor generalization, and incompatibility with FlashAttention), while diversity-based methods treat all tokens as equally important and only capture local pairwise redundancy. No prior method jointly addresses both individual token significance and global representativeness in a single principled selection criterion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260517/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the projected visual token matrix F_V ∈ R^{N×d} (e.g., 576 tokens for LLaVA-1.5, 2880 for LLaVA-NeXT) and the mean text embedding F̄_T ∈ R^d from the text encoder. It outputs a compact subset S of K tokens (K ≪ N, e.g., 32–640 depending on budget) that replaces the full visual sequence before LLM processing. The selection is entirely training-free and model-agnostic, requiring no access to attention maps or encoder-specific [CLS] representations, and is compatible with KV caching and FlashAttention at inference time.

## Method

CoIn reformulates visual token reduction as a submodular optimal subset selection problem with a unified objective: max_S [(1−α)·Σ s_info,i + α·log det(F_S^T F_S)]. The informativeness term s_info combines intrinsic visual saliency (p-norm of each token embedding, capturing perceptual prominence) with cross-modal alignment (cosine similarity to the mean text embedding, capturing query relevance), balanced by β. The coverage term uses a volume-based criterion (log-determinant of the selected tokens' Gram matrix) to ensure the subset spans diverse directions in feature space, going beyond pairwise diversity. Because the objective is monotone submodular, a greedy procedure yields a (1−1/e) approximation; in practice, the determinant is updated incrementally via QR decomposition and Gram-Schmidt, reducing complexity to O(NK). This single-stage joint optimization avoids the sequential importance-then-diversity pipeline of prior hybrid methods, producing a subset that is both individually salient and globally representative.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-13B, CoIn achieves 96.2% average relative performance at 128 retained tokens (77.8% reduction) and 91.0% at 32 tokens (94.4% reduction), outperforming the best baselines by +1.1% and +2.4% respectively. On LLaVA-NeXT-7B (2880→160 tokens, 94.4% reduction), CoIn preserves 86.7% of full-model accuracy, beating VisionZip by 3.2%. Efficiency analysis on LLaVA-NeXT-7B shows 6.5× prefill speedup and 1.3× decoding speedup with reduced GPU memory (14.1 GB vs. 16.8 GB original), while maintaining 85.4 F1 on POPE versus 86.5 for the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoIn consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every model and token budget tested. At the most aggressive 94.4% reduction (32 tokens on LLaVA-1.5-13B), CoIn achieves 91.0% average relative performance, beating the strongest baseline DivPrune (88.6%) by 2.4 points and DART (86.8%) by 4.2 points. Among importance-based methods, PDrop and SparseVLM degrade sharply at high compression (86.3% and 88.3% at 64 tokens), while CoIn retains 94.8%, confirming the advantage of joint informativeness-coverage over single-criterion scoring. Hybrid methods (CDPruner, VisionZip, PruMerge) perform better than pure importance or diversity baselines but still trail CoIn by 1–4 points, as their sequential two-stage design cannot jointly optimize both objectives. On video understanding (LLaVA-OneVision-7B), CoIn reaches 99.6% at 75% pruning, surpassing VisionZip (98.6%) and FrameFusion (95.9%), while DyCoke (93.6%) is limited by its fixed minimum token count. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CoIn leads VisionZip by 1.0% at 80.2% reduction and 2.3% at 90.1% reduction, demonstrating model-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoIn builds on the volume-based subset selection framework of Deshpande and Rademacher (FOCS 2010), repurposing the log-determinant criterion as a global coverage measure for visual token selection. It departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm in visual token reduction—where importance scoring (attention weights, [CLS]-similarity) is applied first and diversity (pairwise similarity, clustering) is layered on second—by formulating both informativeness and coverage as a single submodular objective optimized jointly via greedy selection. The informativeness signal itself is novel in using feature-norm saliency and cross-modal cosine alignment in place of attention maps or encoder-specific [CLS] tokens, making the method compatible with FlashAttention and agnostic to the vision backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CoIn offers a practical, training-free path to 6.5× prefill speedup and substantial memory reduction while retaining 91–96% of full-model accuracy across diverse LMMs (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision), which is directly relevant to deploying LMMs on resource-constrained hardware. The ablation showing that a well-chosen 64-token subset can even slightly exceed the full 576-token model on POPE hints that redundancy removal can sharpen grounding, though this effect is modest and benchmark-specific. Open questions include whether the greedy submodular approximation remains tight at very high compression ratios, how the method behaves with dynamic or adaptive token budgets, and whether the fixed hyperparameter choices (α, β) transfer cleanly to unseen architectures or task distributions without per-model tuning.
