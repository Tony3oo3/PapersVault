## Problem

Existing targeted adversarial attacks on VLMs consistently fail under diffusion-based purification (DBP), the most widely adopted black-box image defense, because prior DBP evasion methods were designed for white-box image classifiers and suffer from prohibitive computational cost and gradient instability when adapted to VLMs. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first black-box targeted attack that effectively bypasses DBP defenses on large vision-language models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260172/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (sampled from ImageNet-1K validation set) and a target text description (sampled from MS-COCO captions) as input, and produces a single adversarial image with an ℓ∞-bounded perturbation (ε=16/255) that must survive an unknown DBP purifier while steering a black-box VLM toward the target output. A target reference image is generated via Stable Diffusion from the target text, and optimization is guided by an ensemble of three CLIP encoders (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g/14) plus a Guided Diffusion surrogate with EOT=10 to approximate the purifier's denoising behavior.

## Method

PureProof avoids backpropagating through the full diffusion denoising chain by performing only a single reverse step at a randomly sampled timestep (Stochastic Reverse Alignment, SRA), aligning the closed-form clean-image estimate with the target via CLIP cosine similarity. To handle the increasing stochasticity at larger timesteps, Adaptive Re-noising Augmentation (ARA) re-injects K Gaussian noise samples at the same noise level as the forward process and averages the resulting similarities, which the paper proves acts as a curvature-aware regularizer that adaptively smooths the loss landscape. Self-Consistency Regularization (SCR) further enforces temporal coherence by penalizing the L2 distance between the initial clean-image estimate and a second estimate obtained after one re-noise-and-denoise cycle, down-weighted by a timestep-dependent coefficient. The three losses are combined with a balancing hyperparameter α and optimized via 100-step PGD, yielding adversarial examples that are both DBP-resistant and robust to additional Gaussian noise.

## Results

Against DiffPure on LLaVA-1.5, PureProof achieves 76.8% ASR (Fool) and 12.3% ASR (Target), compared to 41.4% ASR (Fool) and 0% ASR (Target) for the strongest baseline DH-cos. Across all five open-source VLMs and three DBP defenses (DiffPure, GDMP, LM), PureProof consistently leads by 20–50+ percentage points in ASR (Fool) over the best diffusion-aware baseline, and by 50+ points over non-diffusion-aware attacks, which all achieve 0% ASR (Target). On commercial VLMs, PureProof reaches 77.0% ASR (Fool) on GPT-5 and 73.0% on Gemini-2.5-Flash against DiffPure. Computationally, PureProof requires only 1.76 s per optimization step versus 44.7 s for DA-cos, a ~25× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PureProof outperforms all seven baselines across every VLM and every DBP defense evaluated. The five non-diffusion-aware VLM attacks (MF-it, MF-ii, CoA, AnyAttack, FOA) are completely neutralized by DBP, achieving 0% ASR (Target) and typically below 20% ASR (Fool), confirming that prior targeted attacks are ineffective under purification. The two adapted diffusion-aware baselines, DA-cos (DiffAttack) and DH-cos (DiffHammer), are the strongest competitors but still trail PureProof by 25–50 percentage points in ASR (Fool); for example, on Qwen3-VL against LM, PureProof reaches 81.4% ASR (Fool) and 25.6% ASR (Target) versus 22.3% and 0% for DH-cos. On commercial VLMs, PureProof leads DH-cos by 48 points on GPT-5 (77.0% vs. 29.0% ASR Fool) and 36 points on Gemini-2.5 (73.0% vs. 37.0%). In terms of efficiency, PureProof is roughly 25× faster per step than DA-cos (1.76 s vs. 44.7 s), while DA-cos and DH-cos both require full-trajectory backpropagation through the diffusion chain. Without any defense, PureProof remains competitive (0.6245 CLIP on GPT-5) and, when combined with CoA's objective, achieves the highest scores across all four tested VLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: adversarial attacks on VLMs (AttackVLM, Chain-of-Attack, AnyAttack, FOA-Attack) and evasion attacks against diffusion-based purification (DiffAttack, DiffHammer). It departs from prior VLM attacks by explicitly incorporating DBP defense into the optimization loop, and departs from prior DBP evasion methods by replacing costly full-trajectory backpropagation with a single-step stochastic reverse prediction (SRA), while shifting the target from white-box classifiers to black-box VLMs. The DDPM formulation (Ho et al., 2020) provides the mathematical backbone, and the use of CLIP ensembles as surrogate encoders follows the transfer-attack paradigm established by AttackVLM and FOA-Attack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all prior VLM attacks achieve 0% targeted ASR against DBP defenses, while PureProof reaches 77–87% fooled ASR, exposes a significant gap in how VLM robustness has been evaluated and suggests that widely-deployed DBP purifiers (DiffPure, GDMP, LM) are far less robust than previously assumed against targeted, diffusion-aware attacks. The 25× per-step speedup over full-chain backpropagation baselines (1.76s vs. 44.7s) makes the attack practically feasible at scale. Open questions include whether stronger or ensemble DBP defenses can close this gap, whether the attack transfers to other purification paradigms (e.g., score-based or flow-matching models), and what implications this has for the safety of emerging agentic VLM deployments where user-uploaded images are automatically purified.
