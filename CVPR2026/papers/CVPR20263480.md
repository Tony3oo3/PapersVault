## Problem

Existing ray-based floorplan localization methods treat depth and semantics with separate networks and late fusion, and rely on hard semantic labels or unimodal depth distributions. This leaves them vulnerable to three forms of perceptual ambiguity in cluttered indoor scenes: environmental (furniture and occluders causing depth and semantics to latch onto different noise), geometric (multiple plausible depth values along a ray), and semantic (non-mutually-exclusive classes like glass doors that are both door and window). The paper asks whether a unified probabilistic formulation can jointly model depth and semantic uncertainty to improve robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image and a 2D floorplan (with mask) as input. The image is encoded by a frozen DINOv2 backbone into a dense feature map, while the floorplan is encoded into a global layout token that is appended to each ray's feature sequence. The final output is a camera pose (x, y, θ) selected from a discretized pose-likelihood map, with intermediate per-ray depth distributions over discrete distance bins, per-ray semantic class probability vectors, and scalar confidence scores that re-weight each ray's contribution to the pose score.

## Method

The core architecture is a floorplan-aware unified fusion network where depth and semantic branches share a DINOv2 encoder and interact through stacked cross-attention blocks, allowing geometry to regularize semantics and vice versa at the feature level. A distribution-based ray confidence estimator takes the predicted depth distribution (including its entropy) and the ray feature token, and outputs a per-ray confidence that down-weights bimodal or multimodal depth hypotheses reflecting geometric ambiguity. For semantic matching, the paper replaces hard-label equality checks with Jensen-Shannon divergence between per-ray predicted distributions and pose-conditioned floorplan target distributions, which preserves informative ambiguity and enables linear interpolation during angular downsampling. The final pose score is a weighted sum of a confidence-weighted depth discrepancy volume and a JSD-based semantic probability volume, and the model is trained with L1 depth loss plus a confidence loss that encourages low confidence on ambiguous rays.

## Results

On S3D(full), the proposed method achieves 71.4% recall at 1 m / 30°, a 13.9 percentage-point improvement over the strongest baseline SemRayLoc (57.5%), and more than doubles SemRayLoc's 0.1 m recall (12.0% vs. 5.7%). On the real-world ZInD dataset, the method reaches 50.2% R@1 m / 30° and 8.1% R@0.1 m, substantially outperforming SemRayLoc (31.9% and 3.3%) and depth-only baselines F3Loc (11.5%) and LASER (13.6%). On S3D(empty), the method attains 75.1% R@1 m / 30°, showing only a 3.7-point drop relative to S3D(full), whereas F3Loc drops by 21.5 points under the same transition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest semantic baseline SemRayLoc [12], the gain is 13.9 points at R@1 m/30° on S3D(full) (71.4% vs. 57.5%) and 18.3 points on ZInD (50.2% vs. 31.9%), with the 0.1 m recall more than doubling in both settings. Depth-only methods F3Loc [10], 3DP [8], and RSKFLoc [9] trail by 46–50 points at R@1 m/30° on S3D(full), confirming that geometry alone is insufficient in cluttered indoor scenes. LASER [21], which uses latent-space rendering rather than explicit rays, is the weakest baseline (8.7% on S3D(full), 13.6% on ZInD). Notably, even the depth-only variant of the proposed model (Ours_d) surpasses all depth-only baselines, indicating that the unified fusion architecture improves geometric predictions through cross-modal interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ray-based floorplan localization paradigm established by F3Loc and SemRayLoc, which represent images as sets of depth or semantic rays matched against a 2D floorplan. It departs from prior work in three conceptual ways: (1) replacing separate depth and semantic networks with a unified cross-attention architecture that lets the two modalities iteratively refine each other at the feature level; (2) introducing a distribution-based ray confidence estimator that explicitly down-weights geometrically ambiguous rays rather than relying solely on floorplan GT depth supervision; and (3) substituting hard-label semantic matching with Jensen-Shannon divergence over per-ray probability distributions. The floorplan is additionally used as a conditioning token in the encoder, a design choice not present in prior ray-based methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The probabilistic formulation (depth distributions, semantic distributions, and per-ray confidence) offers a principled mechanism for handling the three forms of perceptual ambiguity—environmental, geometric, and semantic—that plague indoor localization, and the JSD-based matching is a reusable technique for any setting where soft categorical predictions must be compared against structured targets. The finding that the depth-only branch already outperforms dedicated depth-only baselines suggests that cross-modal feature interaction is a broadly useful inductive bias, not merely a fusion trick. However, the floorplan conditioning shows only marginal gains on S3D due to limited structural diversity, and the authors acknowledge that moving from branch-wise label supervision to end-to-end pose-supervised training remains an open direction that could further improve the framework.
