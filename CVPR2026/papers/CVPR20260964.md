## Problem

Traditional speaker diarization systems target constrained settings like meetings and interviews with few speakers and clean acoustics, leaving open-world visual media (films, TV series) largely unexplored. This domain introduces long-form video understanding, dozens of speakers per program, audio-visual asynchrony where a character's voice does not coincide with their face being visible, and uncontrolled in-the-wild variability. No prior benchmark or unified method existed for speaker diarization in this setting.

## Input / Output

CineSRD consumes three synchronized modalities per subtitle line: a video segment v(s), a speech audio segment a(s), and the subtitle text s, all aligned via timing notes in the program's subtitles. The output is a many-to-one mapping Φ: P → R assigning each line to a registered speaker (character), producing a complete speaker diarization annotation for the entire program. The system is entirely training-free, relying on pre-trained face, timbre, and audio-language models, and operates on programs ranging from ~5 hours (English subset) to ~71 hours (Chinese subset) with up to 317 distinct speakers.

## Method

CineSRD is a training-free, three-stage multimodal framework. First, Visual Anchor Clustering uses active speaker detection (TalkNet) and face embeddings (CurricularFace) to form visual clusters that serve as reliable speaker anchors, then aligns audio timbre clusters (ERes2NetV2) to each visual cluster via majority voting to produce timbre prototype embeddings. Second, Speaker Turn Detection feeds 10 consecutive lines with their audio into Qwen2-Audio-7B to predict same/different-speaker labels for adjacent pairs, fused with normalized timbre cosine similarity via a weighted combination (w=0.45) to determine dialogue boundaries. Third, Off-Screen Speaker Supplementation groups lines by detected turn boundaries, computes a per-line confidence score σ(s) that is 1.0 when an active speaker is visually confirmed or the max timbre similarity to existing prototypes otherwise, and registers a new speaker for any group whose average score falls below threshold η=0.45, merging with existing new speakers via a timbre similarity threshold ε.

## Results

On the proposed SubtitleSD benchmark, CineSRD (AVT) achieves DER of 0.0756 / 0.0947 / 0.0893 and JER of 0.3197 / 0.4146 / 0.2909 on the Chinese, Chinese-Hard, and English subsets respectively, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline EC2P (AVT) which records DER of 0.1345 / 0.2102 / 0.1180. On the conventional AVA-AVD benchmark, CineSRD (SC) attains DER 0.1898 and SPKE 0.1616, improving over EC2P (DER 0.2032, SPKE 0.1740) and audio-only baselines AHC/SC (DER ≈ 0.213). The Chinese-Hard subset (317 speakers, dialect-heavy) shows the largest absolute gap, with CineSRD reducing DER from 0.2102 (EC2P) to 0.0947, a 55% relative reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CineSRD (AVT) decisively outperforms all four baselines across every subset of SubtitleSD. Against the strongest multimodal baseline EC2P (AVT), CineSRD reduces DER by 44% on Chinese (0.0756 vs 0.1345), 55% on Chinese-Hard (0.0947 vs 0.2102), and 24% on English (0.0893 vs 0.1180), while also improving JER by 16–26 percentage points. Audio-only baselines AHC and SC are particularly weak on the English subset (DER 0.1248 and 0.2012 respectively), highlighting their inability to handle multilingual and long-form conditions. Even in its dual-modality (AV) form without text, CineSRD already surpasses EC2P's tri-modality (AVT) results on DER for all three subsets, underscoring the value of visual anchor clustering. On the conventional AVA-AVD benchmark, CineSRD (SC) achieves the best DER (0.1898) and SPKE (0.1616), beating EC2P by 1.34 and 1.24 percentage points respectively, and outperforming audio-only AHC/SC by roughly 2.3 percentage points in DER.

## Relation to Prior Work

CineSRD extends the speaker diarization task from constrained settings (meetings, interviews) to open-world visual media (films, TV series), a domain where prior methods have not been systematically applied. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes existing off-the-shelf components—TalkNet for active speaker detection, CurricularFace for face embeddings, ERes2NetV2 for timbre embeddings, and Qwen2-Audio-7B as an audio language model—into a unified training-free pipeline. The key conceptual departure from prior multimodal diarization work (e.g., AVR-Net, EC2P) is the use of visual clusters as registration anchors with audio-visual voting, followed by ALM-based speaker turn detection and a dedicated off-screen speaker supplementation stage, addressing challenges (long-form video, 100+ speakers, audio-visual asynchrony) that prior methods were not designed to handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SubtitleSD benchmark is the first dedicated evaluation resource for visual media speaker diarization, covering bilingual (Chinese/English) and dialectal conditions with up to 317 speakers per program, filling a clear gap between meeting-oriented datasets and the in-the-wild demands of the film/TV industry. The training-free, modular design makes the approach immediately deployable without large-scale training, which is practically valuable for dubbing and subtitling pipelines. However, the framework remains a multi-stage pipeline rather than an end-to-end system, its reliance on face models limits applicability to animation and cartoon genres, and the English subset is too small for meaningful training or robust evaluation. Open questions include whether end-to-end multimodal large models can subsume the multi-stage pipeline, how to handle non-photorealistic content, and whether the visual-anchor-clustering principle generalizes to other long-form, many-speaker domains.
