## Problem

Existing event-aware vision-language models are confined to generic captioning or short QA on non-driving scenes, leaving unexplored how asynchronous event sensing contributes to the full driving reasoning loop from perception through planning. Event-based driving research remains fragmented across isolated low-level tasks (detection, segmentation, flow) without a unified framework connecting temporal cues to high-level decision-making.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261911/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates, timestamps, polarity), synchronized RGB frames, and natural-language queries, with events voxelized at three temporal resolutions (20, 50, 100 bins) to capture multi-scale motion. Outputs are task-dependent: free-text captions and QA answers for perception/understanding, bounding boxes (evaluated at IoU 0.6) for grounding, discrete speed/direction labels for prediction, and continuous ego waypoints (1/3/5-second horizons, scored by L2 error in meters) for planning. The benchmark comprises 471,543 event-frame-text samples across 17 subtasks sourced from DSEC, M3ED, and PKU-DAVIS-SOD.

## Method

EventDrive-VLM builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with a pretrained RVT backbone as the event encoder. A Dynamic Horizon Event Encoder voxelizes events at N=3 temporal resolutions, each processed by a dedicated expert network, then a Mixture-of-Experts gate (with stochastic noise for diversity) selects the top-k most informative horizon based on a global descriptor, adaptively emphasizing fine-grained motion during fast dynamics or coarse aggregation during low-motion regimes. An Event Q-Former performs cross-attention between learnable query tokens and the aggregated event feature map, extracting compact motion-aware embeddings that are projected into the LLM space alongside frame and text tokens. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: first, event-language pre-adaptation with the LLM and frame encoder frozen so gradients flow only through the event pathway; second, joint instruction tuning with LLM transformer blocks unfrozen, integrating temporal and semantic signals across all four driving task families.

## Results

On the EventDrive benchmark, the full Event+Frame EventDrive-VLM achieves the best scores across all four task families: Perception Acc@D 85.44%, Understanding mIoU 72.56% and grounding Acc@60 72.86%, Prediction Speed 42.44%, and Planning L2 Error 3.66 m. Compared to the strongest fine-tuned frame-only baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct*), the event-frame fusion model improves grounding Acc@60 by +2.92 points, mIoU by +5.37 points, Prediction Speed by +5.60 points, and reduces Planning L2 Error by 0.88 m. On the Event-Chat benchmark, EventDrive-VLM scores 4.15 on Complex Reasoning (vs. 4.09 for EventGPT-7B) and 3.43 on Detailed Captioning (vs. 3.52), showing competitive but not dominant performance on generic event-language tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the event-only baseline EventGPT-7B, EventDrive-VLM (event+frame) dominates on every metric: Perception Acc@D improves from 51.40% to 85.44%, Understanding mIoU from 8.24% to 72.56%, and Planning L2 Error drops from 11.42 m to 3.66 m, confirming that frame semantics are essential for appearance-heavy understanding. Versus the strongest fine-tuned frame-only model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct*), the event-frame fusion model gains +3.92 on Perception Acc@D, +2.92 on grounding Acc@60, +5.37 on mIoU, +5.60 on Prediction Speed, and reduces Planning L2 Error from 4.54 m to 3.66 m, demonstrating that temporal cues from events are not recoverable from RGB alone. Among zero-shot frame-based VLMs, InternVL3-8B leads on Prediction Path (91.25%) but collapses on grounding (Acc@60 = 0.24%), while LLaVA-OneVision-1.5-8B achieves the best zero-shot Perception Acc@M (86.35%) yet scores only 12.50 mIoU on Understanding, highlighting the fragility of frame-only spatial reasoning. On the Event-Chat benchmark, EventDrive-VLM slightly trails EventGPT-7B on Detailed Captioning (3.43 vs. 3.52) and VQA (3.94 vs. 4.29) but edges ahead on Complex Reasoning (4.15 vs. 4.09), suggesting the driving-specific training trades some generic captioning fluency for stronger structured reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the event-based driving perception literature (detection, segmentation, tracking) and the emerging event-VLM line (EventGPT, EventVL, LLaFEA) by unifying them into a single language-grounded benchmark spanning the full autonomy stack. Architecturally, it builds on the RVT backbone for event encoding, the Q-Former for cross-modal alignment, and Mixture-of-Experts gating, but introduces a novel dynamic-horizon multi-scale voxelization that adapts temporal resolution to scene dynamics. The key departure from prior event-VLMs is the shift from generic captioning/QA to structured driving reasoning (perception → understanding → prediction → planning) with 471k samples, and from fixed-window fusion to frequency-adaptive expert selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EventDrive fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark connected event sensing to high-level driving reasoning in a unified, language-grounded framework, and existing event-language datasets are small (<100k) or simulation-based. The hard split (low-light, motion blur) provides a targeted evaluation of where events genuinely outperform RGB, which is critical for safety-critical deployment. Open questions include whether the MoE gating generalizes to real-time inference budgets, whether the Qwen3-VL-based annotation pipeline introduces systematic biases, and whether the findings transfer to end-to-end closed-loop driving rather than per-frame QA. The benchmark is likely to become a standard reference for event-driven driving research.
