## Problem

Existing CAD sketch generation methods assume a fixed unidirectional (prefix-to-suffix) construction order, treating partial sketches as strict prefixes to be extended left-to-right. In practice, designers work from arbitrary intermediate states where missing instructions may lie on either side of the observed geometry, and each new primitive attaches to and modifies the evolving topology, making early deviations propagate downstream. This paper tackles the more general problem of completing parametric sketches from arbitrary-span partial contexts while respecting the incremental, state-dependent logic of real CAD workflows.

## Input / Output

The method takes a contiguous span of primitives (50–80% for completion, 20–50% for expansion) from a parametric sketch instruction sequence, where each instruction is a (command, parameters) pair with 6-bit quantized geometric values and special boundary tokens. It outputs the remaining modeling instructions as an ordered sequence of executable primitives (lines, arcs, circles) with full parameter specifications, directly compilable by standard CAD kernels such as Onshape. Unlike prefix-only settings, the observed segment can appear at any position in the full sequence, and the model must infer instructions on both sides simultaneously.

## Method

CADSketcher replaces the standard autoregressive decoder with a bidirectional query mechanism: two learnable query embeddings (prior and posterior) attend to a shared Transformer encoder representation of the partial sketch, enabling simultaneous recovery of instructions on both sides. A hybrid positional encoding combines global directional and relative-position embeddings with local slot-ordering and type encodings to disambiguate arbitrary-span contexts. For parallel efficiency, the instruction sequence is restructured into a right branch (original order) and a left branch (primitive-level reversal) anchored at the partial context, with dedicated attention masks. At inference, a confidence gate adaptively selects the expansion direction at each step, a validity compiler rejects geometrically infeasible parameter assignments, and an iterative context updater re-encodes the growing sketch to maintain state consistency. Training uses position-wise cross-entropy over discretized instruction tokens with masking of the observed partial context.

## Results

On the SketchGraphs dataset, CADSketcher achieves 72.8% token-level accuracy, 75.6% primitive-level accuracy, and 45.6% sketch-level accuracy on partial-to-full completion, with a 59.2% F1 score and 0% invalid rate, outperforming all baselines. On early-stage sketch expansion, it reaches 77.0% coverage, 1.12 MMD, 0.89 JSD, and 84.9% unique score while maintaining only 0.21% invalid rate. Cross-dataset evaluation on CAD as a Language confirms generalization, with CADSketcher scoring 52.5% ACCtok and 34.5% F1 versus the next-best Dual-AR at 40.7% and 28.4% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADSketcher decisively outperforms all three primary baselines on both evaluation tasks. Against Vitruvion [42] (standard autoregressive), CADSketcher more than doubles sketch-level accuracy (45.6% vs. 2.11%) and F1 (59.2% vs. 35.2%) on completion, and improves coverage by 1.1 points while halving the invalid rate on expansion (0.21% vs. 1.48%). Against DeepCAD [49] (latent-implicit paradigm), the gap is even larger: CADSketcher achieves 72.8% vs. 22.2% ACCtok and 0% vs. 17.8% IR on completion, and 77.0% vs. 57.1% COV with 0.21% vs. 35.3% IR on expansion. Against CAD-VLM [51] (cross-modal visual-symbolic fusion), the closest competitor, CADSketcher leads by 7.2 points in ACCtok, 14.8 points in ACCskt, and 12.4 points in F1 on completion, and by 1.8 points in COV with a 20× lower invalid rate (0.21% vs. 4.35%) on expansion. The two tailored baselines (Dual-AR and Dec-Only) narrow the gap somewhat but still trail CADSketcher by 5.8–7.8 points in ACCtok and 10.4–11.7 points in F1 on completion, confirming that the bidirectional query mechanism and confidence-guided pipeline provide consistent gains over simpler architectural variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant autoregressive paradigm in parametric CAD sketch generation (Vitruvion, CAD-VLM), which assumes partial context is always a strict prefix and completes left-to-right. It repurposes the query-based generation idea (analogous to DETR-style object queries) into a bidirectional sketch completion setting, where learnable prior/posterior query embeddings expand from an arbitrary-span partial context in both directions. The hybrid positional encoding and confidence-guided decoding pipeline are novel architectural contributions specific to the state-dependent, out-filling nature of CAD sketching, distinguishing it from NLP infilling or standard sequence-to-sequence generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in interactive CAD workflows where designers routinely work from partial, non-prefix geometry, and demonstrates that the generated instructions are directly executable in a commercial CAD platform (Onshape), bridging the research-to-deployment gap. Key limitations include the absence of explicit geometric constraint modeling (sketching and constraint specification remain decoupled) and limited primitive diversity due to dataset constraints. Open questions include whether the bidirectional query mechanism extends naturally to 3D solid modeling sequences, how constraint-aware decoding would interact with the confidence gate, and whether the approach scales to more complex multi-sheet or assembly-level sketches.
