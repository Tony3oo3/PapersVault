## Problem

Existing fine-tuning methods for foundation models in spectral remote sensing semantic segmentation rely on global, homogeneous feature adjustments that treat all spatial locations identically. This 'one-size-fits-all' strategy fails to handle the spatial heterogeneity of land cover, where spectrally similar but semantically distinct classes (e.g., paddy fields and ponds) are often spatially adjacent, causing inter-class confusion under spectral shifts across sensors, seasons, and regions.

## Input / Output

The method accepts spectral remote sensing images (hyperspectral, multispectral, or RGB) resized to 512×512 pixels, and additionally extracts implicit depth features from the RGB bands via a frozen Depth Foundation Model (PromptDA). It is inserted as a lightweight plugin (5.74M–10.29M parameters) into each layer of a frozen vision or remote sensing foundation model backbone, producing a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask via a Mask2Former decoder. Only the SpectralMoE adapter parameters and segmentation head are trained; all backbone weights remain frozen.

## Method

SpectralMoE employs a dual-gated Mixture-of-Experts architecture where two independent gating networks (one for visual tokens, one for depth tokens) route each spatial feature vector to top-k specialized experts using a distance-based noisy gating function (Laplacian gating with stochastic noise). Each expert performs localized feature modulation via low-rank decomposed adaptive tokens, and the top-k expert outputs are aggregated by their softmax-normalized gating weights to produce a fine-grained, spatially-adaptive adjustment. A subsequent cross-attention module then uses the visual adjustment map as query and the depth adjustment map as key/value, adaptively injecting domain-robust structural priors (height, contours, spatial relationships) into the visual stream to resolve spectral ambiguities. Training minimizes the Mask2Former segmentation loss plus a load-balancing regularizer (squared coefficient of variation of per-expert importance) to prevent expert collapse, with the optimal configuration using 6 experts per modality.

## Results

SpectralMoE achieves state-of-the-art mIoU across all seven DGSS benchmarks spanning hyperspectral, multispectral, and RGB remote sensing imagery. Headline results include 59.83% mIoU on WHU-OHS (cross-regional, hyperspectral), 66.19% on Five-Billion-Pixels (cross-sensor), 63.18% on FLAIR (cross-regional), 59.11% on LoveDA (cross-style), 64.99% on Potsdam & Vaihingen (cross-spectral-band), and 68.57% on OpenEarthMap (cross-continent). The method uses only 5.74M trainable parameters (adapter modules) on top of frozen DINOv3 or DOFA backbones, outperforming both larger-adapter methods like FADA (11.65M) and smaller ones like REIN (2.99M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectralMoE outperforms all 18 baselines on every one of the seven benchmarks. Against the strongest FM-based DGSS adapter, DepthForge [5], it gains +3.22% mIoU on WHU-OHS (59.83 vs 56.61), +7.40% on FBP cross-sensor (66.19 vs 58.79), +5.40% on FBP cross-regional (60.32 vs 54.92), +1.62% on FLAIR (63.18 vs 61.56), +1.61% on LoveDA (59.11 vs 57.50), +5.42% on Potsdam & Vaihingen (64.99 vs 59.57), and +1.72% on OpenEarthMap (68.57 vs 66.85). Compared to REIN [47], the gains are +11.12% (WHU-OHS), +7.13% (FBP-CS), +5.05% (FBP-CR), +2.72% (FLAIR), +2.15% (LoveDA), +4.45% (Potsdam&Vaih.), and +1.81% (OpenEarthMap). Against the frozen DINOv3 VFM baseline, SpectralMoE improves mIoU by +10.65% (FBP-CS), +5.88% (FBP-CR), +3.58% (FLAIR), +3.36% (LoveDA), +6.20% (Potsdam&Vaih.), and +3.09% (OpenEarthMap). The traditional DGSS method DSTC [31] and frozen RSFM baselines (DOFA, Galileo, Copernicus, SenPa-MAE) are all substantially outperformed, with DOFA lagging by 13.8% mIoU on WHU-OHS and 21.59% on FBP cross-sensor. General-purpose PEFT methods (LoRA, AdaptFormer, VPT) also trail SpectralMoE by 3.5–6.5% mIoU on the multispectral tasks (Table 3), confirming that task-specific spatially-adaptive refinement is critical for spectral RS domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing FM fine-tuning paradigm for DGSS (REIN, SET, FADA, DepthForge), which applies a single global adapter uniformly across all spatial positions, by introducing a dual-gated Mixture-of-Experts that routes each token to specialized experts for spatially-adaptive, local refinement. It also repurposes a Depth Foundation Model (PromptDA) to extract structural priors from RGB bands, a modality not previously exploited in spectral RS DGSS fine-tuning, and fuses them via cross-attention rather than simple addition. Conceptually, the work bridges the MoE literature from NLP/LLMs with the parameter-efficient fine-tuning of vision foundation models for remote sensing, while the depth-as-robust-prior idea draws on the broader observation that geometric structure is more domain-invariant than spectral appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent SOTA across seven diverse benchmarks (hyperspectral, multispectral, RGB; cross-sensor, cross-regional, cross-style, cross-continent) with a lightweight 5.74M-parameter plugin demonstrates that spatially-adaptive refinement is a practical and effective strategy for adapting FMs to spectral RS, potentially generalizable to other domain-shift-heavy segmentation tasks. A notable limitation is that depth estimation from RGB bands may be less informative for purely spectral (non-RGB) imagery or scenes with minimal 3D structure, and the modest depth contribution on some tasks (0.11% on FLAIR) suggests the structural prior is task-dependent. Open questions include whether the dual-gated MoE design scales to even larger FMs, whether the number of experts should be adaptive per layer or per domain, and whether the approach extends to video or 4D spectral data.
