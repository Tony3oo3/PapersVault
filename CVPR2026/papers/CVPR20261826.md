## Problem

Adapting SAM2 to RGB-D video salient object detection faces three gaps: vanilla LoRA lacks spatial priors and modality-specific routing for multi-modal inputs, existing SAM-based methods discard multi-scale encoder features by relying solely on the deepest level for prediction, and memory-based video segmentation requires explicit user prompts for cold-start initialization, precluding prompt-free operation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261826/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes T=4 consecutive RGB-D frames resized to 352×352, where depth maps are normalized to [0,1] and replicated to 3 channels to match the RGB input format. It produces a final salient object segmentation mask along with auxiliary coarse masks and Sobel-filtered edge maps at three intermediate decoder levels. The memory bank retains key-value pairs from the preceding T−1 frames within each clip, and at test time the memory window can be extended up to T=32 without retraining.

## Method

M⁴-SAM reformulates SAM2's Hiera encoder into a U-shaped architecture with three key innovations. First, Modality-Aware MoE-LoRA replaces linear LoRA projections with groups of convolutional experts (3×3, 5×5, and depthwise-separable) and introduces a Modality Dispatcher that routes RGB and depth streams to modality-specific expert groups (RGB, depth, shared fusion) within a single encoder, eliminating the need for dual-encoder branches. Second, a Gated Multi-Level Feature Fusion module aggregates four-level encoder features via dual spatial-channel attention and an adaptive gating convolution, then concatenates the result with the middle-level decoder feature X_D² (rather than the final level) to balance spatial detail and semantic context. Third, a Pseudo-Guided Temporal Memory bootstraps the memory bank using the first-frame coarse mask as a pseudo prior (replacing manual prompts), then performs cross-attention between current-frame fused features and stored memory keys/values to propagate temporal context, with the bank updated each frame via a prediction-guided value encoder.

## Results

M⁴-SAM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four metrics (E_ξ, S_α, F_β, MAE) on all three RGB-D VSOD benchmarks. On DViSal, it reaches E_ξ=0.925 and F_β=0.828, surpassing the second-best KAN-SAM by 4.5% and 5.7% respectively. On RDVS, it obtains E_ξ=0.927, exceeding DCTNet+ by 2.0%. On ViDSOD-100, it achieves E_ξ=0.936 and MAE=0.016, outperforming KAN-SAM by 2.6% and 0.009 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M⁴-SAM outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric on all three datasets. The closest competitors are KAN-SAM (ICME'25) and DCTNet+ (TIP'24): on DViSal, M⁴-SAM leads KAN-SAM by 4.0 points in E_ξ (0.925 vs. 0.885) and 4.5 points in F_β (0.828 vs. 0.783); on RDVS, it surpasses DCTNet+ by 1.8 points in E_ξ (0.927 vs. 0.909) and 0.8 points in F_β (0.802 vs. 0.794); on ViDSOD-100, it beats KAN-SAM by 2.4 points in E_ξ (0.936 vs. 0.912) and reduces MAE from 0.025 to 0.016. Among SAM-based baselines specifically, M⁴-SAM achieves average E_ξ improvements of 6.9% over MDSAM, 7.6% over SAM2-UNet, and 2.9% over KAN-SAM across the three datasets, confirming that the gains are not solely attributable to the SAM2 backbone. Traditional non-SAM methods (e.g., PICRNet, STDNet, HRTransNet) trail by 15–25 points in E_ξ, highlighting the advantage of foundation-model-based approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM2's streaming memory architecture and the LoRA PEFT framework into a unified prompt-free RGB-D video salient object detection pipeline. It departs from prior SAM-based SOD methods (MDSAM, SAM2-UNet, KAN-SAM) that rely on dual-encoder architectures or simple additive multi-scale fusion, instead proposing a single shared Hiera encoder with modality-specific convolutional expert routing (MoE-LoRA) and a gated hierarchical decoder. The temporal memory mechanism draws on XMem's key-value retrieval paradigm but replaces user-provided prompts with a pseudo-mask bootstrap, and the cross-modal fusion leverages the UIM and RFB modules from prior RGB-D VSOD work (Mou et al., 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prompt-free, parameter-efficient adaptation of SAM2 to RGB-D VSOD offers a practical template for deploying foundation models on small-scale multi-modal video tasks without manual annotation or prompt engineering. The modality-aware MoE-LoRA design—routing RGB and depth streams through shared and modality-specific convolutional experts within a single encoder—could generalize to other multi-modal dense prediction settings. However, the approach is validated only on three relatively small benchmarks (≤9K frames each), and the pseudo-guided initialization's robustness to very poor coarse-mask quality in extreme conditions (heavy occlusion, severe noise) remains untested. Open questions include whether the MoE-LoRA strategy transfers to other foundation backbones and whether the T=4 clip-length optimum holds for longer, more complex video sequences.
