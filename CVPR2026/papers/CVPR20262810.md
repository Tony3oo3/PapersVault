## Problem

Existing multi-task learning methods for dense scene understanding rely on 2D per-pixel supervision, producing unstructured features that lack explicit 3D geometric consistency across tasks. While prior 3D-aware MTL work (3DMTL, MuvieNeRF) introduces 3D regularization, they either fail to directly extract multi-view geometric cues into shared representations or require multi-view data and camera parameters at inference, limiting practical deployment.

## Input / Output

At inference the method requires only a single RGB image with no camera parameters; during training it optionally consumes a neighboring view (from video sequences) or duplicates the input image for single-view datasets. The shared encoder operates at full resolution while the cross-view module processes features at 1/8 resolution with 128 uniformly sampled depth candidates in inverse depth space (0.0001–10 m). Outputs are per-pixel dense predictions across up to six tasks (segmentation, depth, normals, boundaries, saliency, human parts) produced by lightweight task-specific decoder heads.

## Method

The paper introduces a lightweight Cross-view Module (CvM, ~5M parameters, ~1.5% of a typical MTL encoder) that runs in parallel to the main MTL encoder and injects geometric consistency via three sequential components: (i) a shallow ResNet-like spatial-aware CNN that extracts geometry-biased features independently of the MTL pathway to avoid interference, (ii) a multi-view Swin Transformer with stacked self- and cross-attention layers that aggregates intra- and cross-view correspondences while disambiguating occlusions and textureless regions, and (iii) a differentiable cost volume builder that warps features from neighboring views onto L depth planes using camera intrinsics and relative poses, then computes normalized dot-product matching scores to form a 3D cost volume C_i ∈ R^{H×W×L}. The cost volume and cross-view enhanced features are concatenated with the MTL encoder features before task-specific decoders, and the entire system is trained end-to-end with standard per-task losses. The design is architecture-agnostic (validated on SAK, RADIO, and DINOv3 backbones) and supports both multi-view and single-view training while requiring only one image at inference.

## Results

On NYUv2 (single-view), integrating the Cross-view Module (CvM) into DINOv3 achieves 64.98 mIoU (segmentation), 0.3909 RMSE (depth), 15.27° mErr (surface normal), and 81.58 odsF (boundary), setting a new SotA across all four tasks. On PASCAL-Context, DINOv3 + CvM reaches 84.56 mIoU (segmentation), 77.97 mIoU (part segmentation), 13.66° mErr (normal), and 79.29 odsF (boundary), again leading all compared methods. The ΔMTL score (average gain over single-task learning) improves from 16.33 to 18.66 on NYUv2 and from 2.52 to 3.71 on PASCAL-Context when CvM is added to DINOv3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv3, the proposed CvM yields consistent gains on all four NYUv2 tasks: +1.30 mIoU (64.98 vs 63.68), −0.0204 RMSE depth (0.3909 vs 0.4113), −0.26° mErr normal (15.27 vs 15.53), and +1.48 odsF boundary (81.58 vs 80.10). Against SAK, the method improves depth by 0.0269 RMSE and boundary by 1.13 odsF, with segmentation essentially tied (63.12 vs 63.18 mIoU). Against RADIO, gains are +0.94 mIoU, −0.0079 RMSE, −0.12° mErr, and +0.95 odsF. Compared to the 3D-aware regularizer 3DMTL [32], the method substantially outperforms it on NYUv2: +5.11 mIoU, −0.1170 RMSE depth, −1.21° mErr normal, and +2.26 odsF boundary, indicating that explicit cross-view cost-volume modeling is more effective than neural-rendering-based 3D regularization. On PASCAL-Context, DINOv3 + CvM leads DINOv3 by +0.49 mIoU (segmentation), +0.68 mIoU (part segmentation), and +2.99 odsF (boundary), confirming the benefit generalizes beyond indoor RGB-D data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes multi-view stereo machinery—specifically cost-volume construction from MVSNet and Xu et al., and the multi-view Swin Transformer design from MVSplat—as a geometric consistency signal for multi-task learning, rather than for 3D reconstruction. It departs from 3DMTL's NeRF-based 3D regularization and MuvieNeRF's multi-view inference requirement by introducing a lightweight, architecture-agnostic Cross-view Module (CvM) that runs in parallel to any existing MTL encoder, requiring only a single image at inference. The spatial-aware encoder is deliberately decoupled from the main MTL pathway to avoid interference between monocular task learning and cross-view matching, a design choice validated by ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CvM offers a practical, low-overhead (~5M params, 1.5% of encoder) plug-in that consistently improves multiple strong MTL backbones (SAK, RADIO, DINOv3) across two benchmarks, suggesting cross-view geometric cues are a broadly beneficial inductive bias for dense prediction. However, the method is limited to static scenes with a single neighboring view, and the single-view training strategy (duplicating the image) is acknowledged as suboptimal. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether more views or learned view selection yield further gains, and whether the cost-volume formulation can be replaced by more efficient implicit representations.
