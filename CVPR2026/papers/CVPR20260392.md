## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS, occupancy grids) recover geometry and appearance but cannot infer underlying physical properties such as friction, hardness, stiffness, and density. Prior physical-property estimation approaches are limited to one or two specific attributes, are largely confined to indoor settings, and produce deterministic point estimates without calibrated uncertainty. PhysGS addresses this by unifying dense 3D reconstruction, multi-property estimation, and uncertainty quantification in a single spatially continuous framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (for 3DGS reconstruction on ABO-500) or a single RGB image (for outdoor scenes on RUGD/RELLIS-3D). SAM generates hierarchical part-level masks, which are fed to GPT-5 as structured triplet prompts (full object, masked overlay, isolated region) alongside a textual query requesting material labels, property values, and a normalized confidence score in [0,1]. The output is a 3D Gaussian splat field where each splat carries per-point property values (friction, hardness, density, stiffness), an object-level mass estimate obtained by integrating density over volume, and pixel-wise aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty maps.

## Method

PhysGS embeds Bayesian inference directly into the 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization, treating each Gaussian primitive as a probabilistic entity whose physical properties are refined via confidence-weighted posterior updates as new multi-view observations arrive. Discrete material labels are modeled with a Dirichlet-Categorical conjugate pair, enabling closed-form recursive updates of class probabilities weighted by VLM confidence scores. Continuous properties (friction, hardness, density) are governed by a Normal-Inverse-Gamma prior, yielding a hierarchical posterior that factors into a Dirichlet-Categorical class distribution and a per-class Gaussian property distribution, whose predictive is a closed-form Gaussian mixture. The NIG posterior decomposes total predictive variance into aleatoric uncertainty (E[σ²], reflecting sensor noise and intra-class variability) and epistemic uncertainty (Var[μ], reflecting insufficient evidence that shrinks with more high-confidence observations). The pipeline runs SAM segmentation → GPT-5 VLM prompting → Bayesian fusion across views → semantic 3DGS reconstruction, producing a queryable property field without requiring access to past observations.

## Results

On the ABO-500 mass estimation benchmark, PhysGS reduces APE from 1.061 (NeRF2Physics) to 0.819, a 22.8% improvement, and ADE from 8.730 kg to 8.254 kg. On the real-world friction-hardness dataset, PhysGS achieves a 61.2% reduction in Shore hardness ADE (12.721 vs. 32.752 for GPT-4V) and an 18.1% reduction in kinetic friction ALDE (0.263 vs. 0.321 for NeRF2Physics). The method also outperforms all VLM baselines (GPT-4V, GPT-5) and CLIP on both hardness and friction metrics across all four evaluation criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysGS outperforms NeRF2Physics, the strongest 3D-based baseline, on mass ADE (8.254 vs. 8.730) and APE (0.819 vs. 1.061), but trails it on ALDE (0.999 vs. 0.771) and MnRE (0.474 vs. 0.552) for mass estimation. For dense per-point properties, PhysGS dominates NeRF2Physics on all four Shore hardness metrics (e.g., ADE 12.721 vs. 34.295) and on friction ADE, ALDE, and MnRE, though it narrowly loses on friction APE (0.365 vs. 0.360). All VLM baselines (GPT-4V, GPT-5) and the CLIP embedding baseline are substantially outperformed on both hardness and friction, with GPT-5 being the closest VLM competitor (hardness ADE 16.044 vs. PhysGS 12.721). The 2D baselines (Image2mass, 2D CNN, LLaVA) are consistently the weakest, confirming that 3D-aware reconstruction with Bayesian fusion is necessary for accurate physical property estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysGS extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction pipeline (Kerbl et al., 2023) by embedding a Dirichlet-Categorical and Normal-Inverse-Gamma Bayesian inference layer directly into the Gaussian primitives, building on the hierarchical Bayesian formulation of Ewen et al. (2024) for semantic and property prediction. The VLM prompting design follows GaussianProperty (Xu et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that language models can be queried per-segment to produce physical property estimates over 3D Gaussians. The key departure from both NeRF2Physics (Zhai et al., 2024) and GaussianProperty is the shift from deterministic, single-pass property regression to sequential, confidence-weighted posterior refinement across views, yielding explicit aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty alongside the property estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying dense per-point physical property estimation (friction, hardness, stiffness, density, mass) with calibrated uncertainty in a single 3DGS-based framework, PhysGS offers a practical path toward robots that can reason about material interactions without contact sensing. The 61.2% reduction in Shore hardness error and 22.8% reduction in mass APE over the strongest baselines are meaningful for manipulation and navigation planning. However, the method's sensitivity to SAM segmentation quality and the lack of calibrated VLM confidence scores remain open challenges; the authors note that VLM-guided mask refinement and complementary tactile/force sensing are needed to close the gap between visual inference and true physical measurement.
