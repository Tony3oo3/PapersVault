## Problem

Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) is widely used for subsurface imaging, but accurately detecting and reconstructing the fine, sparse, and weakly reflective structures of plant root systems remains a significant challenge. Existing deep learning approaches for GPR interpretation primarily target large, well-defined objects such as pipes and structural voids, leaving a gap in non-destructive 3D root reconstruction that would support agricultural research and food security.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263990/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes preprocessed GPR B-scans (2D radar images) acquired from multiple scanning directions as input, where each B-scan has undergone an 8-step pipeline including time-zero correction, dewow filtering, background removal, bandpass FIR filtering tuned to root diameter range, SEC amplitude gain, soil dielectric correction, and Kirchhoff migration. The first stage outputs per-detection bounding boxes along with regressed hyperbola parameters (vertex coordinates, curvature K=a/b², and arc length) for each root segment; the second stage consumes the sparse 3D point cloud assembled from these detections across all slices and produces a dense 3D root point cloud (8192 points for evaluation) representing the complete root architecture including taproot and lateral branches.

## Method

The framework comprises two coupled networks. The detection stage extends SE-SSD with a MobileNetV2 backbone (first six inverted residual blocks plus five additional conv layers for multi-scale context) and a four-module hybrid head that simultaneously predicts bounding boxes, class confidence, and hyperbola geometry parameters, trained with combined localization (SL1), confidence (cross-entropy with hard negative mining at 1:3 ratio), and curve-fitting (SL1 on len, vertex, curvature) losses. The 3D reconstruction stage builds a KNN graph over the sparse root points and applies a point graph neural network with residual connections and layer normalization to prevent over-smoothing, a dual-pooling attention mechanism (max ⊙ avg) to sharpen branch-intersection features, and a differential-feature attention module that emphasizes root joints. A Farthest Point Sampling–based upsampling module with affine-normalized aggregated features then densifies the point cloud, and the full reconstruction is supervised by a hierarchical loss combining Chamfer Distance (coarse alignment), Earth Mover's Distance (fine bijection), and a KNN isolation term that suppresses outlier points from soil impurities.

## Results

On simulated 2D GPR B-scans, the proposed MobileNetV2-based detection network achieves AP of 0.857, AP-50 of 0.902, and AP-75 of 0.870, outperforming all compared detectors (best baseline: DiffusionDet500 at AP 0.838). For 3D root reconstruction, the full pipeline achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 2.03 and Earth Mover's Distance of 5.03 (×100) across five test roots, substantially lower than VAPCNet (3.29/7.57), PointLLM-V2 (3.00/7.12), and Polis et al. (2.90/7.08). The model uses only 20.98M parameters, the smallest among all compared methods (vs. 63.48M for Feng et al.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In detection (Table 1), the proposed method wins on all three metrics against every baseline: it surpasses SE-SSD by +7.3 AP points (0.857 vs. 0.784), Feng et al. (ResNet-101) by +2.1 AP points, and DiffusionDet500 by +1.9 AP points, while also achieving the best AP-75 (0.870 vs. 0.859 for DiffusionDet500). In 3D reconstruction (Table 2), the method achieves the lowest mean EMD (5.03) and CD (2.03) across all five test roots, beating VAPCNet by 2.54 EMD points and 1.26 CD points, PointLLM-V2 by 2.09 EMD points, and Polis et al. by 2.05 EMD points. The method also has the fewest parameters (20.98M) compared to VAPCNet (22.15M), PointLLM-V2 (25.26M), and Feng et al. (63.48M), making it the most efficient. Notably, the detection advantage is achieved with a lightweight MobileNetV2 backbone rather than heavier ResNet or SwinTransformer architectures, and the 3D reconstruction advantage is most pronounced on roots with complex branching (e.g., root 003: EMD 5.41 vs. 7.47–8.17 for baselines).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SE-SSD single-stage detection framework by swapping in a MobileNetV2 backbone and adding a dedicated hyperbola curve-fitting head, moving beyond generic bounding-box detection to regressing geometric parameters (vertex, curvature, arc length) specific to GPR root signatures. For the 3D reconstruction phase, it adapts the point graph neural network lineage (PointNet, PointNet++) with residual connections, layer normalization, and a dual-pooling attention mechanism to handle the extreme sparsity and branching topology of root point clouds, departing from prior point cloud completion methods (VAPCNet, PointLLM) that target large uniform objects. The overall pipeline bridges two previously separate literatures—GPR-based root detection (Conyers, Wu, Molon) and learned 3D shape reconstruction—into a single end-to-end framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-destructive 3D root assessment is critical for agricultural research, soil health monitoring, and food security, yet prior GPR work has largely been limited to 2D detection, diameter estimation, or biomass inference without full geometric reconstruction. The paper's main limitation is that 3D reconstruction is validated only on synthetic gprMax data with five test roots, while real-world validation is restricted to visual detection comparisons due to the impossibility of obtaining 3D ground truth without destructive sampling. Open questions include generalization across soil types and moisture levels, scalability to dense fibrous root systems, and whether the hyperbola-fitting assumption holds for highly branched or decaying roots in the field.
