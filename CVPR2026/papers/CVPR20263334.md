## Problem

Long-term language-guided referring in fixed-view videos fails when the target is occluded or leaves the scene for extended periods, because framewise association pipelines lose semantic memory and appearance-based ReID drifts under illumination and pose changes. Existing methods lack a persistent, query-conditioned spatial reference that remains informative during invisibility, making reliable re-capture upon re-entry an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263334/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a fixed-view video sequence (static camera, average duration >120 s) and a natural-language text query, and produces per-frame bounding boxes forming an identity-consistent trajectory. Notably, it does not assume the referent is visible in the first frame and handles only intra-scene appearance variation (pose, illumination, scale), explicitly excluding cross-scene outfit changes. The pipeline runs zero-shot with all encoders frozen, requiring no task-specific training.

## Method

AR²-4FV couples a language-anchored scene memory with anchor-conditioned association. Offline, an Anchor Bank of persistent region masks, visual prototypes, and centroids is distilled from the first T₀ frames of the static background; online, the text query is aligned to this bank via cosine similarity in a shared subspace to yield a query-conditioned Anchor Map that serves as spatial memory during absence. Proposals from an open-vocabulary detector are restricted to anchor-responsive regions, and a re-entry prior (initialized from the Anchor Map, updated via EMA with Gaussian smoothing, and redirected toward the confirmed anchor) guides efficient search when no candidate is available. A lightweight ReID-Gating module then validates each candidate by jointly scoring appearance similarity (via a momentum identity queue), anchor-map evidence, and displacement in the anchor coordinate frame, accepting only candidates whose gate score exceeds a threshold. The entire system composes frozen components—GroundingDINO, SAM, a CLIP-family encoder, and a RexSeek-style refiner—without end-to-end training.

## Results

On AR²-4FV-Bench, AR²-4FV achieves an IDF1 of 64.8, Re-Capture Rate (RCR) of 0.75, and Re-Capture Latency (RCL) of 20.1 frames, outperforming the strongest baseline DUTrack (IDF1 62.3, RCR 0.69, RCL 25.8) by +2.5 IDF1, +0.06 RCR, and −5.7 RCL. In localization, AR²-4FV reaches mAP 49.2 and mIoU 66.9, surpassing DUTrack (mAP 46.5, mIoU 63.7). The paper reports a +10.3% relative RCR improvement and −24.2% relative RCL reduction over the best baseline, demonstrating substantially faster and more reliable re-capture after long disappearances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AR²-4FV outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric (IDF1, RCR, RCL, mAP, mIoU). The closest competitor is DUTrack (CVPR'25), which achieves IDF1 62.3, RCR 0.69, and RCL 25.8; AR²-4FV leads by +2.5 IDF1, +0.06 RCR, and −5.7 RCL. Among earlier R-VOS methods, MTTR and ReferFormer (both CVPR'22) rely on motion cues and score lowest on RCL (33.8 and 31.2), reflecting their inability to handle long disappearances. OnlineRefer (ICCV'23) and SOC (NeurIPS'23) accumulate errors in static scenes without stable motion updates, yielding RCL values around 30. The most recent baselines—DsHmp (CVPR'24), UVLTrack (AAAI'24), and SSA (CVPR'25)—narrow the gap but still trail AR²-4FV by 2–4 IDF1 points and 0.05–0.09 RCR, confirming that anchor-based scene memory and re-entry priors provide a distinct advantage over appearance- or motion-driven association in fixed-view long-term settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends referring video object segmentation (R-VOS) and long-term tracking into a fixed-view, long-disappearance setting that prior methods (MTTR, ReferFormer, OnlineRefer, SOC, DsHmp, SSA) do not address, as those rely on motion cues or short-window appearance that degrade when the referent is absent. It repurposes the classical fixed-view background-stability idea (MOG, ViBe) as a query-conditioned spatial memory: an offline Anchor Bank distilled from static structures is aligned online with the text query to produce a persistent Anchor Map, a concept absent from prior language-guided tracking. The ReID-Gating module borrows from person re-identification (OSNet, TransReID) but replaces pure appearance matching with a joint gate over appearance, anchor evidence, and displacement in the anchor coordinate frame.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of AR²-4FV-Bench provides the first dedicated evaluation protocol for long-term referring with explicit disappearance, occlusion, and re-entry annotations in fixed-view videos, filling a gap where prior R-VOS benchmarks assume first-frame visibility. The zero-shot, frozen-encoder design makes the approach practically deployable in surveillance and security settings where retraining is infeasible. Key limitations include the restriction to a single fixed camera (no cross-camera or outfit-change handling), evaluation on a self-created benchmark only, and reliance on a specific detector-segmentation-ReID pipeline whose individual components are not novel. Open questions include whether the Anchor Map generalizes to slowly-varying or multi-view scenes and whether the re-entry prior degrades under adversarial distractors placed near anchor regions.
