## Problem

Existing visual text rendering methods struggle with glyph accuracy for complex or out-of-domain characters: large-scale training data has limited glyph coverage and excessive stylization, while RL-based approaches rely on OCR reward models that are insensitive to fine-grained stroke-level errors. Standard DPO, which models only image-level preferences, is further ill-suited because glyph errors are typically localized to specific regions rather than distributed across the whole image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262552/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a set of text blocks each specifying a string to render and its spatial position, and corresponding glyph images (rendered with a default font and VAE-encoded) as conditioning. It produces a single RGB image at 512×512 or 1024×1024 resolution with the specified text rendered at the designated positions. A custom attention mask restricts cross-modal communication so that each image text region only attends to its own glyph feature, preventing information leakage between different text blocks.

## Method

GlyphPrinter is a two-stage pipeline built on Flux.1-Dev. Stage 1 fine-tunes the base model with flow matching loss on multilingual synthetic and realistic text images, using LoRA adapters and a structured attention mask for text localization. Stage 2 introduces Region-Grouped DPO (R-GDPO), which optimizes the model on the newly constructed GlyphCorrector dataset (7,117 images with region-level correct/incorrect glyph annotations) by constructing both inter-sample preference pairs (same region, different images in a group) and intra-sample pairs (correct vs. incorrect regions within one image), applying the DPO velocity-matching loss only within the annotated region masks. At inference, Regional Reward Guidance (RRG) blends the reference and optimized model velocity fields exclusively within text regions, yielding a controllable trade-off between glyph accuracy and background image quality without requiring an explicit reward model.

## Results

On the GlyphAcc-Multilingual benchmark, GlyphPrinter achieves the highest NED across all seven languages (e.g., 0.9851 English, 0.9728 Chinese, 0.9663 Thai) and the best sentence accuracy in most languages (e.g., 0.9362 Korean, 0.9339 Chinese). On the GlyphAcc-Complex benchmark, GlyphPrinter reaches NED 0.9013 and Sen.Acc 0.8349, substantially outperforming the next-best EasyText (NED 0.7645, Sen.Acc 0.6604) and Qwen-Image (NED 0.6189, Sen.Acc 0.3679). In the user study, GlyphPrinter scores 8.31/10 on Glyph.Acc, surpassing Qwen-Image (7.97) and EasyText (7.44), while maintaining competitive aesthetic (7.91) and text-image alignment (8.17) scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlyphPrinter outperforms all five baselines on NED and Sen.Acc across every language in GlyphAcc-Multilingual and on the Complex benchmark. The gap is most dramatic on complex Chinese characters (NED 0.9013 vs. 0.7645 for EasyText and 0.6189 for Qwen-Image) and on Thai, where prompt-guided methods collapse (Glyph-ByT5-v2 NED 0.0040, X-Omni 0.0368) while GlyphPrinter maintains 0.9663. Among glyph-image-guided methods, EasyText is the closest competitor on multilingual NED (e.g., 0.9780 vs. 0.9851 English) but falls behind by 13.7 NED points on Complex. In the user study, GlyphPrinter leads on Glyph.Acc (8.31 vs. 7.97 Qwen-Image, 7.44 EasyText) but trails Qwen-Image slightly on Aesthetic (7.91 vs. 8.07) and Text.Align (8.17 vs. 8.23), reflecting the trade-off between glyph precision and overall image quality inherent to the different base architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

GlyphPrinter sits at the intersection of two lines of work: glyph-image-guided text rendering (AnyText2, EasyText, Glyph-ByT5-v2) and preference-based alignment of diffusion/flow-matching models (DPO, DPOK). It departs from RL-based text rendering methods (X-Omni, Flow-GRPO) that rely on OCR or MLLM reward models, arguing these are fundamentally insensitive to fine-grained glyph errors such as missing or extraneous strokes. The key methodological departure is replacing image-level DPO with Region-Grouped DPO (R-GDPO), which operates on region-level preference pairs from the newly constructed GlyphCorrector dataset, and adding Regional Reward Guidance at inference time as an analogue of Classifier-Free Guidance applied to the preference-optimized model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-demonstrated failure mode of existing text rendering pipelines: recognition-based reward models assign identical scores to images with and without subtle glyph errors, causing RL to reinforce incorrect outputs. The region-level preference formulation is a natural and effective generalization that could transfer to other tasks where errors are spatially localized (e.g., fine-grained object editing, layout generation). Limitations include VAE constraints on very small characters and fixed-resolution training that restricts aspect-ratio diversity; the GlyphCorrector dataset (7,117 images) is also modest in scale, raising questions about whether the approach scales to broader glyph distributions without proportional annotation effort.
