## Problem

Music-driven 3D dance generation lacks both a large-scale, richly annotated multimodal dataset and a unified generative model capable of flexible control. Existing datasets are small (<15 hours), MoCap-based, and missing paired text/keypoint annotations, while current generative models are music-only or support only limited spatial editing, preventing arbitrary combinations of stylistic and spatial conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263737/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts music audio (Jukebox 4800-dim features), text descriptions (CLIP embeddings), 2D COCO keypoints, and 3D global trajectories, with any subset or sparse frame-level subset of these conditions permitted at inference. Output is a 3D dance sequence at 30 FPS represented as 24-joint SMPL 6-DoF rotations, 3D root translation, and binary heel/toe contact labels (per-frame vectors in R^{24×6+4+3}), with sequences of 5–10 seconds. The disentangled tokenizer design means partial or incomplete spatial inputs (e.g., only the last frame's keypoints) can be directly padded and injected without requiring full-sequence conditions.

## Method

OpenDanceNet comprises a Disentangled Dance Tokenizer (DDT) that independently quantizes joint rotations, global trajectory, and 2D keypoints into discrete tokens via separate learned codebooks, deliberately avoiding early cross-modal fusion so that sparse frame-level constraints can be directly encoded. A Multimodal-Condition Transformer (MCT) then performs masked joint prediction: it simultaneously reconstructs motion tokens, trajectory tokens, and keypoint tokens from their masked versions, which forces the model to learn fine-grained spatial constraints rather than defaulting to coarse stylistic signals. During training, random modality-level masking (probability p_mask) over music and text, combined with token-level masking over spatial streams, enables the model to handle arbitrary condition combinations. At inference, Multi-Step Logit-Ranked Re-Masking (MS-LRM) iteratively re-masks low-confidence tokens across all steps, and a per-step physics-aware refinement decodes predicted tokens via Gumbel-Softmax, computes FK, and updates logits using a foot-sliding loss to suppress skating artifacts.

## Results

On the standard AIST++ benchmark, OpenDanceNet achieves the lowest PFC (1.140, even below ground truth's 1.332), best FIDk (24.82), and highest Beat Alignment Score (0.2513), outperforming all six baselines on physical plausibility and rhythm alignment. On the authors' own OpenDanceSet, OpenDanceNet attains the best FIDk (23.19), highest BAS (0.2472), and strongest diversity (Divk=7.82, Divg=6.41); with the per-step refinement module, FIDg drops to 7.72 (best among all methods) and PFC improves to 0.2733. The method trades some diversity (Divk/Divg) on AIST++ relative to EDGE for substantially better fidelity and physical realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OpenDanceNet dominates on physical plausibility (PFC) and rhythm alignment (BAS) across both benchmarks: on AIST++ it beats FACT (PFC 1.140 vs 2.254), Bailando (1.140 vs 1.754), EDGE (1.140 vs 1.536), and MoMask (1.140 vs 1.648), and on OpenDanceSet it outperforms TM2D by a wide margin (0.3462 vs 2.8794). On motion fidelity (FIDk), OpenDanceNet leads all baselines on both datasets (24.82 on AIST++, 23.19 on OpenDanceSet), with the largest gaps over MoMask (44.92/61.11) and TM2D (69.95). The main trade-off is diversity: EDGE achieves higher Divk/Divg on AIST++ (8.73/7.18 vs 5.24/5.29), and Bailando achieves a lower FIDg (9.62 vs 12.54), suggesting OpenDanceNet's masked joint-prediction objective slightly constrains the motion manifold. On OpenDanceSet, FineDance is the closest competitor (FIDk 26.69, FIDg 9.98), but OpenDanceNet still wins on FIDk, Divk, Divg, and BAS; with the refinement module, FIDg improves to 7.72, surpassing FineDance. TM2D, the only other multimodal (music+text) baseline, is substantially outperformed on every metric, confirming the advantage of the disentangled tokenizer and joint-prediction framework over cross-attention fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the music-driven 3D dance generation line (FACT, Bailando, EDGE, MoMask) by introducing a unified masked-modeling framework that accepts arbitrary combinations of music, text, 2D keypoints, and 3D trajectories as conditions. Conceptually, it repurposes the VQ-VAE + Transformer paradigm from Bailando and the masked prediction idea from MoMask, but departs in two key ways: (1) a disentangled tokenizer with separate codebooks for motion, trajectory, and keypoints rather than a single motion codebook, and (2) a joint-prediction objective that reconstructs spatial signal tokens alongside motion tokens, addressing the observation that spatial conditions impose frame-level constraints the model otherwise ignores. The dataset construction pipeline (internet video → semi-automatic 3D motion estimation via world-grounded estimators) follows the video-based scaling trend from Motion-X and AIST++ but pushes to 100+ hours with five synchronized modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OpenDanceSet (100+ hours, 14 genres, 147 subjects, five modalities) is the largest and most richly annotated 3D dance dataset to date, and its release with source-video links provides a practical resource for the community. The unified controllability framework addresses a genuine gap: prior methods offer at most one or two control signals, while real choreography workflows require simultaneous spatial and stylistic guidance. Open questions include whether the single-CLIP-token text encoding limits fine-grained style control, whether the approach extends to hand/finger and facial expressions, and whether the internet-video annotation pipeline generalizes to other motion domains (e.g., sports, sign language). The paper does not yet benchmark video generation on the dataset, leaving a natural next step.
