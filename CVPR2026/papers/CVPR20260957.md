## Problem

Existing point-supervised nuclei segmentation methods rely on a single image representation—either RGB or Hematoxylin component—despite the two modalities exhibiting complementary strengths and weaknesses in depicting nuclear structure. RGB images capture color and texture cues that Hematoxylin components lack, while Hematoxylin images enhance nuclei-background contrast but introduce artifacts from stain separation. This paper addresses the nontrivial challenge of jointly learning from both representations within a unified weakly supervised framework, where feature alignment is complicated by differences in appearance, contrast, and structural emphasis.

## Input / Output

The method takes 250×250 pixel patches (with 80-pixel overlap) from H&E-stained histopathology slides along with sparse point annotations at nuclei centroids. The Hematoxylin component is derived on-the-fly from the RGB input via a simplified color deconvolution using a predefined stain matrix and Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, so only the RGB image and points are required as external inputs. The network produces a fused nuclei instance segmentation probability map (C×H×W) and an auxiliary Gaussian kernel prediction map; during training, Voronoi and k-means clustering pseudo labels provide additional pixel-level supervision to compensate for point sparsity.

## Method

DFGNet employs a dual-branch encoder-decoder architecture (ResNet34 backbone) that processes RGB and Hematoxylin representations in parallel. The Reciprocal Cross-scale Dynamic Fusion (RCDF) module extracts multi-scale features via parallel convolutions with varying receptive fields, applies self-enhancement through global average pooling and learnable linear mappings, then fuses cross-representation features using sequential channel-and-spatial attention (inspired by CBAM) followed by residual guided fusion. The Interleaved point-Guided Attention (IGA) mechanism is inserted at each decoder layer, enabling bidirectional cross-representation refinement where point-supervised features from one branch attend to segmentation features from the other, preserving representation-specific advantages while enabling mutual learning. The Entropy Confidence Aggregation Unit (ECAU) computes per-pixel Shannon entropy from each branch's softmax outputs and uses the reciprocal of entropy as a fusion weight, adaptively emphasizing high-confidence predictions from either representation. Training proceeds in two 120-epoch stages with Adam optimizer, combining cross-entropy losses on Voronoi and clustering pseudo labels with a weighted MSE loss on Gaussian kernel targets, using a total loss that balances all components via λ₁ and λ₂ weighting factors.

## Results

On the MoNuSeg (MO) dataset, DFGNet achieves 91.38% Accuracy, 79.03% F1-score, 74.89% Dice_obj, 54.85% AJI, 70.65% DQ, 72.54% SQ, and 51.45% PQ, setting new state-of-the-art among all point-supervised methods across every metric. On CPM17, the full model reaches 90.68% Acc and 77.18% F1, surpassing the previous best point-supervised method PUCD (90.19% Acc, 76.21% F1) by ~0.5–1.0 points. On CoNSeP, DFGNet leads in object-level metrics (Dice_obj 63.56, AJI 41.08, PQ 33.47) but trails SCNet slightly on pixel-level Acc (89.88 vs. 90.13) and F1 (71.48 vs. 71.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DFGNet outperforms all eleven baselines on the MO dataset across every metric, with the largest margins over SCNet (+8.85 Dice_obj, +12.52 AJI) and SAM (+9.18 Dice_obj, +12.46 AJI), confirming that dual-representation fusion substantially improves instance-level separation. Against the strongest point-supervised competitor MA (MixAnno), DFGNet gains +0.17 Acc, +1.07 F1, +1.87 Dice_obj, and +3.25 AJI on MO, and similar advantages on CPM17 (+0.25 Acc, +0.22 F1, +0.48 Dice_obj, +0.11 AJI). On CoNSeP, DFGNet still leads in object-level metrics (Dice_obj 63.56 vs. SCNet's 61.20; AJI 41.08 vs. 39.67; PQ 33.47 vs. 31.44) but concedes pixel-level Acc and F1 to SCNet by ~0.25 points, likely due to CoNSeP's noisier pseudo-labels. Compared to fully supervised methods, DFGNet narrows the gap considerably: it surpasses U-Net in four of seven metrics on MO (F1, AJI, DQ, PQ) and remains within ~3–6 points of Hover-Net on most metrics despite using only point annotations. The ablation (Table 4) further shows that each component—RCDF, IGA, interleaved learning, and ECAU—contributes incrementally, with the full combination yielding the best result (91.38 Acc vs. 90.13 for RGB-only baseline).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point-supervised nuclei segmentation line initiated by Qu et al. (TMI 2020) and advanced by BoNuS (TMI 2024), SCNet (MIA 2023), and PUCD (AIM 2025), all of which operate on a single image representation—either RGB or the Hematoxylin component extracted via color deconvolution (Vahadane et al., TMI 2016). DFGNet departs from this single-representation paradigm by jointly encoding both RGB and Hematoxylin inputs through a dual-branch architecture, introducing cross-scale dynamic fusion (RCDF), interleaved point-guided attention (IGA), and entropy-based confidence aggregation (ECAU) to exploit their complementary structural cues. The pseudo-label generation strategy (Voronoi diagrams and k-means clustering) and Gaussian kernel supervision are directly inherited from Qu et al., while the attention design in RCDF draws on CBAM-style channel-spatial modulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in reducing annotation cost to sparse point labels while closing part of the gap to fully supervised methods, making nuclei segmentation more deployable in clinical workflows where pixel-level annotation is prohibitive. However, a substantial performance gap to fully supervised baselines (e.g., Hover-Net) remains, and the organ-wise analysis reveals that densely clustered, morphologically heterogeneous nuclei (colon, liver) still degrade performance, indicating the dual-representation strategy does not fully resolve the hardest segmentation cases. Open questions include whether the RGB–Hematoxylin complementarity generalizes to other stain types (e.g., IHC, PAS) or imaging modalities, and whether the interleaved guidance mechanism transfers to other weakly supervised segmentation tasks beyond nuclei.
