## Problem

Standard transformer and contrastive learning recipes fail out of the box for volumetric CT due to cubic token scaling, strong geometric anisotropy (unequal voxel spacing), and weak, noisy clinical supervision that weakens contrastive signals. Prior CT foundation models are either region-specific, unimodal, or rely on hybrid CNN-transformer backbones, leaving no fully transformer-based, open-source model that jointly learns geometry-aware and clinically grounded 3D representations at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D CT volumes partitioned into anisotropic 16×16×8 voxel patches (depth halved to match typical slice-direction spacing) and paired free-text radiology reports. It produces volumetric feature embeddings of dimension 1080 from the local encoder, scan-level embeddings from the global encoder, and 512-dimensional L2-normalized image-text embeddings in a shared SigLIP space. For segmentation, the encoder outputs are upsampled from 1/4 resolution via trilinear interpolation to full H×W×D masks.

## Method

SPECTRE uses a two-stage transformer: a local ViT (ViT_ℓ) with windowed attention over 3D crops (each window gets a learnable [cls] token) and a global ViT (ViT_g) that performs full attention over the G window-level summaries, making large-scale 3D attention linear in G rather than quadratic in total token count. Geometry is encoded via anisotropic 3D patch embeddings and 3D rotary positional encodings (RoPE) with DINOv3-style stochastic box jittering to handle variable voxel spacing and FOV. Pretraining is two-stage: Stage 1 applies DINOv3-style self-distillation (DINO + iBOT + KoLeo losses, with a masked-ViT student and EMA teacher, multi-crop views, and a higher masking ratio of U(0.2, 0.7) suited to 3D); Stage 2 aligns the full model with radiology reports via SigLIP using a Qwen3-0.6B text encoder augmented with LoRA adapters, exploiting the sigmoid-based loss's robustness to noisy, many-to-many clinical pairings.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, SPECTRE achieves 86.64% Dice on KiTS23, 80.14% on LiTS, and 83.31% on WORD, outperforming all transformer-based baselines (best prior: Primus-M at 86.13/79.52/83.19) while remaining competitive with the convolutional nnU-Net ResEnc L (88.06/81.20/85.79). On CT-RATE full-report retrieval, SPECTRE reaches 17.5% Recall@5 and 59.9% Recall@100, a ~6× improvement over CT-CLIP (2.9% and 28.8%). On Merlin retrieval, SPECTRE achieves 43.2% Recall@1 on Impressions (N=32), surpassing Merlin (38.4%), though Merlin leads on Findings (77.6% vs 55.5%). On the six-benchmark biomarker classification suite, SPECTRE attains the highest frozen-embedding kNN accuracy on 4 of 6 tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPECTRE outperforms all seven transformer-based segmentation baselines (CoTr, nnFormer, SwinUNETRv2, UNETR, WaveFormer, Primus-M, Primus-L) on every reported dataset, with the closest margin being +0.51 Dice over Primus-M on KiTS23 (86.64 vs 86.13). It also edges out the original nnU-Net on all three datasets (e.g., 83.31 vs 83.11 on WORD) but trails the stronger nnU-Net ResEnc L by 1.4–2.5 Dice points, reflecting the cost of an encoder-only design without a dedicated decoder. On CT-RATE retrieval, SPECTRE dominates CT-CLIP by a wide margin (17.5% vs 2.9% Recall@5), demonstrating the benefit of the two-stage DINOv3 + SigLIP pipeline over single-stage CLIP-style training. On Merlin, SPECTRE surpasses Merlin on Impressions-to-image retrieval (43.2% vs 38.4% Recall@1 at N=32) and on the combined Full Report setting (66.4% vs no Merlin baseline reported), but Merlin retains a substantial lead on Findings (77.6% vs 55.5%), likely because Merlin was trained on a larger, more structured report corpus. Both OpenCLIP and BioMedCLIP are far behind SPECTRE on Merlin retrieval (≤4.6% vs 43–66% Recall@1), confirming that domain-specific 3D CT pretraining is essential for meaningful cross-modal alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPECTRE extends the DINOv3 self-distillation framework and SigLIP vision-language alignment to volumetric CT by introducing a fully transformer-based two-stage architecture with anisotropic 3D patch embeddings (16×16×8) that explicitly encode CT voxel geometry, and a local-global attention split that makes large-scale 3D self-attention tractable. It departs from prior CT foundation models (CT-CLIP, Merlin, CT-FM) that rely on CNN backbones or single-stage contrastive objectives, and from 3D segmentation transformers (SwinUNETR, Primus, nnFormer) that are task-specific rather than general-purpose. The encoder-only segmentation head (SEoMT) adapts the EoMT approach from 2D to 3D, and the text encoder uses Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA adapters rather than a domain-specific medical language model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SPECTRE provides the first fully open-source, fully transformer-based CT foundation model trained exclusively on public data, lowering the barrier for institutions without private imaging corpora. The two-stage SSL-then-VLA pipeline demonstrates that geometry-aware self-supervised features and clinical semantic alignment are complementary rather than competing, and the encoder-only segmentation results suggest that decoder-heavy architectures may be unnecessary for competitive dense prediction. Key limitations include a thoracic bias in the training corpus, inherent noise in free-text reports, and the smoothness of encoder-only masks that can obscure small lesions—open questions include whether the approach transfers to MRI or PET and whether joint (rather than sequential) SSL+VLA training could further improve representations.
