## Problem

Pre-training vision foundation model families incurs enormous computational costs, with the largest models dominating total expense (e.g., ViT-H/14 costs over 80× more than ViT-B/32). Existing acceleration techniques optimize individual models in isolation, ignoring that models within a family share common knowledge through identical objectives, datasets, and protocols, making traditional individual training highly redundant. The paper asks how to achieve pre-training acceleration that scales efficiently as the model family size grows, rather than treating each model independently.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260553/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a model family (e.g., ViT-T/16 through ViT-L/16) arranged in ascending size order, along with image-text pairs (CC3M, CC12M, or Merged-15M) augmented 16× via LLM-generated text variations. The output is a complete family of pre-trained VFMs where each model's weights are initialized and guided by its immediate smaller predecessor. Unlike standard pipelines that train each model from random initialization, the input is consumed sequentially along the chain, with each model's trained parameters and features serving as the knowledge source for the next.

## Method

CoM-PT organizes models into a chain ordered by ascending size, where the smallest model undergoes standard individual pre-training and each subsequent model is trained via inverse knowledge transfer from its predecessor. This transfer combines two components: inverse weight initialization (inserting the smaller teacher's parameters into the larger student's corresponding blocks, with remaining parameters randomly initialized, and duplicating layer weights for depth expansion) and inverse feature distillation (an L2 loss between teacher and transformed student features, applied to both visual and text branches). The key inversion from standard knowledge distillation is that the teacher is smaller than the student, and intermediate models serve dual roles as both student (learning from their predecessor) and teacher (guiding their successor), sustaining a continuous relay. Training epochs decrease linearly along the chain while model size grows exponentially, and the overall loss is the sum of the CLIP-style contrastive task loss and the distillation loss, with the distillation coefficient tuned so that L_IFD < L_task.

## Results

On CC3M, CoM-PT achieves lossless or superior zero-shot performance across the ViT and Swin families while reducing accumulated training cost by 4.10× (ViT) and 3.80× (Swin) in A100 GPU hours. For the largest model ViT-L/16, CoM-PT reaches 34.27% ImageNet-1K Top-1 (vs. 33.77% baseline) and 39.18% COCO R@1 (vs. 36.53%), with an individual acceleration of 7.41× in GPU hours. On Merged-15M, ViT-L/16 achieves 49.03% ImageNet-1K (vs. 48.88% baseline) with 2.37× family-level acceleration. Fine-tuning on open-vocabulary segmentation shows CoM-PT backbones outperform baselines by 5.64% (ViT-B) and 6.14% (ViT-L) average mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is CoM-PT against standard individual pre-training (the 'Baseline') using the same LaCLIP + ShareGPT4V text augmentation protocol. CoM-PT matches or exceeds baseline zero-shot accuracy on nearly all models: ViT-L/16 on CC3M improves by +0.50% ImageNet-1K and +2.65% COCO R@1, while Swin-L improves by +0.29% and +1.72% respectively, all within the 0.5% lossless threshold. The key efficiency gain is 4.10× (ViT) and 3.80× (Swin) reduction in accumulated A100 GPU hours on CC3M, with individual model acceleration reaching up to 7.41× (ViT-L) and 9.26× (Swin-L). Against prior data-efficient CLIP methods (Table 1), the paper's baseline (LaCLIP + ShareGPT4V, 128 epochs) achieves 31.80% ImageNet-1K Top-1, outperforming original CLIP (16.00%), original LaCLIP (21.50%), and MLLM-A (25.00%), establishing a strong starting point before applying CoM-PT. On fine-tuning, CoM-PT backbones consistently outperform individually-trained baselines: ViT-B/16 gains +5.64% average mIoU on segmentation and ViT-L/16 gains +6.14%, while vision-language tasks show marginal improvements (e.g., +0.49% on POPE, +0.39% on VQAv2) with only a negligible 0.05% drop on ScienceQA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of VFM training acceleration, which optimizes individual models through techniques like token reduction, data-efficient objectives (DeCLIP, ALIP, LaCLIP), or masking (FLIP). Instead, CoM-PT operates at the model family level, organizing models into a size-ascending 'model chain' where each larger model is trained via inverse knowledge transfer from its smaller predecessor. The inverse knowledge transfer itself repurposes two well-studied ideas—weight initialization (inverting Net2Net/NetExpand's large-to-small direction) and feature distillation (inverting the standard teacher-student size relationship)—but their combination into a sequential relay along the chain is the novel architectural contribution. The baseline method (LaCLIP with DreamLIP-style text augmentation) is a strong, recent data-efficient CLIP variant, ensuring the acceleration is measured against a competitive starting point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core practical impact is that VFM families can expand in size at near-zero marginal cost, directly addressing the escalating pre-training expense that currently limits deployment flexibility. The counterintuitive finding that adding more models to the chain *reduces* total training cost (up to 72% MAC reduction) challenges the standard assumption that larger model families are strictly more expensive. Key open questions include whether the acceleration holds at production data scales (LAION-2B), where the paper only extrapolates to ~2.3× rather than demonstrating it empirically, and whether the approach generalizes beyond contrastive pre-training to other paradigms like masked image modeling or autoregressive generation. The authors explicitly flag LLM pre-training as a natural extension, which would be a significant step given the even higher compute costs in that domain.
