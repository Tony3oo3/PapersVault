## Problem

Stroke-based rendering faces a fundamental dichotomy: search-based methods trace discrete strokes along local gradients but suffer from structural fragmentation, local minima, and require tens of thousands of strokes, while differentiable optimizers converge faster per stroke but lack structural priors, producing visually disorganized layouts. No existing approach bridges discrete structural exploration with continuous gradient-based refinement in a unified, differentiable framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target RGB image (1200×1200 on DIV2K, 600×800 on Im2Oil) and produces a compact set of vectorized strokes, each parameterized by Bezier control points, a uniform color, an opacity, a width, and a scalar height value. The height field is initialized from a pre-trained monocular depth estimator (Depth Anything) and refined with a spatial continuity constraint, enabling impasto-style relighting under novel lighting. The stroke count is 30–50% lower than baselines while achieving higher PSNR/SSIM, and the entire optimization converges in under 90 seconds on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual stroke representation that couples a discrete polyline (sequence of anchor points) with a continuous Bezier curve (control points) through a bidirectional differentiable mapping: chord-length parameterization plus least-squares fitting converts polylines to Bezier curves, while uniform sampling converts them back, allowing gradients to flow in both directions. An iterative search-and-optimize pipeline alternates between (1) structure-aware search, which seeds polylines in high-residual regions via NMS and traces them along smoothed gradient-flow directions, and (2) gradient-guided optimization, which refines all Bezier control points, colors, opacities, and widths via backpropagation through a differentiable polyline splatting renderer. The renderer approximates each stroke segment as a soft anisotropic kernel and composites all segments in a single GPU-parallel front-to-back alpha-blending pass, inspired by 3D Gaussian Splatting, enabling massive parallel optimization from the first iteration. An adaptive reinitialization strategy (analogous to 3DGS split/prune) resets only near-zero-opacity strokes, preserving structural coherence while preventing optimization stagnation.

## Results

On the DIV2K validation set (1200×1200, 16K strokes), the method achieves 32.16 dB PSNR, 0.93 SSIM, and 0.076 LPIPS in 87.6 s, outperforming the best baseline (CNP) by 4.25 dB in PSNR and 0.29 in SSIM while being 30% faster. On the Im2Oil gallery dataset (600×800, 8K strokes), it reaches 32.53 dB PSNR, 0.86 SSIM, and 0.192 LPIPS in 42.7 s, surpassing the strongest baseline (Learning to Paint) by 3.95 dB in PSNR and 0.13 in SSIM while running nearly 2× faster. A 100-participant user study confirms perceptual superiority, with the method scoring 4.35/5 overall versus 3.47 for the next-best method (Learning to Paint).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against Im2Oil [28] (search-based, ACM MM 2022), it gains +4.57 dB PSNR on DIV2K and +3.99 dB on Gallery while reducing runtime from 727.8 s to 87.6 s (DIV2K) and 392.8 s to 42.7 s (Gallery). Against CNP [12] (neural painting, ACM MM 2023), the closest PSNR competitor on DIV2K (27.91 dB), the gap is +4.25 dB with a 0.29 SSIM improvement, though CNP is slightly faster on Gallery (61.3 s vs 42.7 s is actually still won by Ours). Against Paint Transformer [21] (ICCV 2021), the advantage is largest in PSNR (+8.33 dB on DIV2K) and LPIPS (0.076 vs 0.378), reflecting Paint Transformer's weak structural coherence. Against Learning to Paint [14] (ICCV 2019, RL-based), the gap narrows to +3.57 dB PSNR on DIV2K but the SSIM lead (0.93 vs 0.70) and LPIPS lead (0.076 vs 0.330) remain substantial. Against SNP [32] (CVPR 2021, differentiable), the method achieves +11.53 dB PSNR on DIV2K and runs 67× faster (87.6 s vs 5837.2 s), highlighting SNP's extreme optimization cost. The user study further confirms the method's perceptual dominance, scoring 4.32/5 in structure, 4.28/5 in texture, and 4.41/5 in color versus the next-best Learning to Paint at 3.51, 3.42, and 3.47 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes two long-standing but previously separate paradigms in stroke-based rendering: discrete, gradient-following search methods (Hertzmann 1998, Turk & Banks 1996, Zeng et al. 2009) and continuous differentiable optimization (SNP 2021, CNP 2023, Learning to Paint 2019). Its core conceptual move is a bidirectional differentiable mapping between discrete polylines and Bézier control points, which had not been explicitly coupled in prior SBR work. The rendering pipeline additionally repurposes the parallel splatting paradigm from 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to 2D stroke compositing, and borrows monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) for height-field initialization—both are cross-domain repurposings rather than extensions of a single prior method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual discrete-continuous representation offers a clean, general recipe for combining structural exploration with fine-grained gradient refinement that could transfer to other vectorized or primitive-based rendering tasks beyond 2D painting. The 4–5 dB PSNR gain over the strongest baseline (Im2Oil) while using 30–50% fewer strokes and 30–40% less time is practically significant for interactive and production pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very high-resolution or 3D surface painting, whether the height-field relighting adds genuine artistic value beyond a visual demo, and how the method behaves under adversarial or highly textured inputs where gradient-driven search may still fragment.
