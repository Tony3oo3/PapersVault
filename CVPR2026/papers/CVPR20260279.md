## Problem

Large-scale urban scene reconstruction with Gaussian Splatting faces a persistent imbalance between rendering fidelity and geometric accuracy. Existing methods either rely on single-view depth constraints that lack structural consistency, use simple multi-view photometric losses that struggle with diverse scales and illumination variations, or suffer from sparse initialization in weakly textured regions that produces surface holes and artifacts. The paper tackles how to achieve both geometric precision and visual quality simultaneously in complex, large-scale environments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view image sequences (aerial views downsampled to 1600 px long side, street views at 1000×1000), SfM-derived sparse point clouds and camera poses, and monocular depth priors from a pre-trained MoGe-2 estimator. It outputs a distributed 2D Gaussian Splatting representation from which 3D meshes are extracted via median-depth TSDF fusion, along with rendered novel views and per-pixel depth maps. Training is parallelized across four GPUs using a Gaussian-wise distributed strategy with periodic load balancing.

## Method

MetroGS builds on a distributed 2DGS backbone and introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, a structured dense enhancement scheme partitions the SfM image graph via normalized cut, applies a pre-trained pointmap model in parallel per cluster to produce dense initialization, and adds a sparsity-compensation densification rule that splits Gaussians dominating large areas in low-density voxels. Second, a progressive hybrid geometric refinement strategy begins with monocular depth supervision (L1 on inverse depth plus depth-normal consistency and a scale-regularization penalty) in early training, then transitions to a PatchMatch-based multi-view depth refinement that uses multi-scale patches, filters by reprojection error, and restores filtered-out regions via local patch alignment with the monocular prior. Third, a depth-guided appearance module stores scale-adaptive 3D features in a Tri-Mip structure, queries them at geometry-aligned 3D coordinates from the rendered depth, and passes them through a lightweight MLP tone mapper conditioned on per-image appearance embeddings to decouple illumination from geometry.

## Results

On the GauU-Scene dataset, MetroGS achieves the best PSNR (24.94/24.51/27.07 dB) and F1-score (0.585/0.494/0.433) across all three scenes (Russian Building, Residence, Modern Building), improving PSNR by 0.88 dB on average and F1 by 0.033 over CityGSV2. On the synthetic MatrixCity dataset, MetroGS attains F1-scores of 0.677 (Aerial) and 0.607 (Street), surpassing CityGSV2 by an average of 0.11 in F1. Notably, CityGS-X fails with OOM on MatrixCity-Street, while MetroGS completes training on four RTX 3090 GPUs in under 25% of the training time required by CityGSV2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetroGS outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on both GauU-Scene and MatrixCity. Against the primary SOTA baseline CityGSV2, MetroGS gains +0.88 dB average PSNR and +0.033 F1 on GauU-Scene, and +0.11 average F1 on MatrixCity (0.677 vs. 0.556 Aerial; 0.607 vs. 0.503 Street). Compared to CityGS-X, MetroGS achieves higher F1 on all GauU-Scene scenes (e.g., 0.585 vs. 0.531 on Russian Building) and avoids the OOM failure that CityGS-X suffers on MatrixCity-Street. Against 2DGS (the object-level foundation), MetroGS improves F1 by 0.054–0.057 on GauU-Scene, demonstrating the benefit of the large-scale-specific geometric and appearance modules. NeRF-based methods (NeuS, Neuralangelo) fail to produce meaningful meshes on most scenes, while SuGaR and GOF trail significantly in both rendering quality and geometric accuracy (F1 < 0.42 on all scenes). The paper also reports that MetroGS reaches comparable or better quality in under 25% of CityGSV2's training time on four RTX 3090 GPUs.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetroGS is built directly on 2DGS as its geometric representation and extends the distributed training paradigm introduced by Zhao et al. [51] to a Gaussian-wise parallel strategy. It departs from prior large-scale surface reconstruction methods (CityGS, CityGS-X, CityGSV2) that rely on single-view depth supervision or simple multi-view photometric constraints, instead introducing a progressive hybrid geometric refinement that combines monocular depth priors with PatchMatch-based multi-view consistency, and a depth-guided appearance module leveraging Tri-Mip features. The structured dense enhancement (pointmap-assisted initialization plus sparsity compensation) addresses a gap in prior work where sparse-view regions in urban scenes lead to incomplete geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Large-scale geometrically accurate reconstruction is a bottleneck for autonomous driving, aerial surveying, and immersive AR/VR applications, and this paper provides a unified framework that simultaneously improves geometric fidelity, rendering quality, and training efficiency (reaching better results in under 25% of CityGSV2's training time). The depth-guided appearance decoupling addresses a practical pain point—illumination inconsistency in real-world urban datasets—that prior methods handle poorly. Open questions include whether the progressive monocular-to-multi-view strategy generalizes to dynamic or partially observed scenes, and whether the PatchMatch refinement can be further accelerated for near-real-time applications.
