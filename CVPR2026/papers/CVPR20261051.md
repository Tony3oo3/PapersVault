## Problem

Existing 3D hand motion recovery methods produce temporally inconsistent, physically implausible trajectories and offer no measure of how confidently the recovered motion satisfies the laws of dynamics. Prior physics-informed approaches enforce Euler-Lagrange residuals to zero deterministically, an assumption that breaks down under noisy image-based estimates and approximate physics models, leading to distorted motion and no interpretability about where the reconstruction is unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261051/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T=16 RGB frames, runs them through an off-the-shelf image-based pose estimator (WiLoR) to obtain per-frame MANO pose θ ∈ R^{15×3} and shape β ∈ R^{10} estimates, then uses the mean shape β_avg and per-frame poses as conditioning for the diffusion refinement. It outputs a refined 3D hand pose trajectory in MANO parameter space together with per-joint, per-time dynamic variance maps (Var(F_{1:T}^0)) that can be visualized at both joint and mesh level to flag frames where physical consistency weakens.

## Method

PAD-Hand formulates Euler-Lagrange dynamics for articulated hands (mass, Coriolis, and gravitational terms computed from MANO mesh tetrahedralization) and treats the resulting dynamic residuals as virtual observables drawn from a Gaussian distribution, integrating their negative log-likelihood into the conditional diffusion objective rather than enforcing zero residuals. The backbone is a MeshCNN-Transformer encoder-decoder (4+4 layers, 8 heads, 512-dim) that encodes per-frame mesh topology via MeshCNN and temporal dependencies via self-attention, predicting denoised motion at each of N=4 diffusion steps. For uncertainty estimation, a Last-layer Laplace Approximation (LLLA) is applied to the backbone's final layer to obtain per-joint variance predictions, which are then propagated backward through the reverse diffusion chain via a closed-form variance/covariance recursion (Algorithm 1) to yield final per-joint, per-time dynamic variances. Training uses a combined loss λ₁·L_data + λ₂·L_EL with λ₁=2000 and λ₂=500, where L_EL scores the EL residuals as virtual observables with rescaled variance σ_n = Σ_n/c.

## Results

On DexYCB, PAD-Hand (WiLoR+Ours) achieves PA-MPJPE of 4.63 mm, MPJPE of 10.56 mm, and acceleration error (ACCEL) of 3.34 mm/frame², improving over the WiLoR initialization by 5.1%, 17.2%, and 50.1% respectively. On HO3D, the method reduces PA-MPJPE from 7.50 mm to 7.43 mm and cuts the acceleration error from 4.98 to 2.71 mm/frame² (45.6% reduction). The method outperforms all video-based baselines (Deformer, VIBE, TCMR, TempCLR) on both reconstruction accuracy and physical plausibility metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAD-Hand is initialized with WiLoR [45] image-based estimates and outperforms them on every reported metric: PA-MPJPE drops from 4.88 to 4.63 mm, MPJPE from 12.75 to 10.56 mm, and ACCEL from 6.70 to 3.34 mm/frame² on DexYCB; on HO3D, PA-MPJPE improves from 7.50 to 7.43 mm and ACCEL from 4.98 to 2.71 mm/frame². Against the strongest video-based baseline Deformer [16], PAD-Hand achieves lower PA-MPJPE (4.63 vs. 5.22 mm) and dramatically lower acceleration error (3.34 vs. 6.77 mm/frame²) on DexYCB, and similarly dominates on HO3D (7.43 vs. 9.40 mm PA-MPJPE; 2.71 vs. 6.37 mm/frame² ACCEL). All other sequence-based baselines (VIBE [31], TCMR [11], TempCLR [73], S²HAND(V) [55]) are outperformed by a wide margin on reconstruction accuracy, with PA-MPJPE gaps of 1.6–2.6 mm. The image-based probabilistic baseline MaskHand [34] is also surpassed (4.63 vs. 5.00 mm PA-MPJPE). The ablation in Table 4 further shows that the probabilistic physics integration (treating EL residuals as virtual observables) consistently outperforms a deterministic L1 penalty on the same residuals, confirming the benefit of the distributional formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAD-Hand extends the diffusion-based hand motion recovery line initiated by Zhang et al. (ICCV 2025) and the Euler-Lagrange dynamics formulation from PhysPT (CVPR 2024), but departs from both by treating dynamic residuals as virtual observables drawn from a distribution rather than enforcing them as deterministic zero-residual constraints. The virtual-observable framework is borrowed from Rixner et al. and Bastek et al. for physics-informed diffusion, and the per-joint variance estimation adapts the Last-layer Laplace Approximation from BayesDiff (ICLR 2023) to the articulated-hand setting. The key conceptual departure is that prior physics-based hand methods assume observed motion can satisfy dynamics exactly, whereas PAD-Hand explicitly models the mismatch between noisy image-based estimates and approximate physics, yielding a distributional rather than point-estimate solution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The per-joint, per-time dynamic variance maps offer a physically grounded, interpretable signal for downstream consumers (e.g., embodied AI, AR/VR) to identify unreliable motion segments without requiring ground truth, which is practically valuable for safety-critical or interactive applications. A clear limitation is the absence of explicit object geometry and contact modeling, restricting the physics to free-space hand dynamics; integrating hand-object interaction remains an open direction. The paper also leaves open whether the variance estimates transfer to longer sequences or more complex multi-hand scenarios, and whether the probabilistic physics formulation scales to full-body or multi-agent dynamics.
