## Problem

Large-scale LoD 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering is bottlenecked by two inefficiencies: serial level-by-level tree traversal that consumes over 60% of rendering time, and a vast number of redundant Gaussian-tile key-value pairs that impose full sorting and α-blending overhead despite contributing negligibly to the final image. Existing solutions—fixed-threshold shrinking (FlashGS) and per-Gaussian learned pruning—either fail to reduce redundancy sufficiently in large scenes or introduce LoD-switching artifacts when applied to hierarchical structures.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained LoD Gaussian tree model (with precomputed ancestor paths for each node) and a camera pose, and outputs a rendered novel view image at 1080p resolution. A one-time pre-rendering pass over all training views computes the scene-level GTC metric to derive the shrinking threshold τ, which is then fixed for all subsequent rendering. The ancestor-path indexing adds roughly 20% memory overhead over the base tree (total model sizes range from 1.61 GB to 6.33 GB across tested scenes).

## Method

FilterGS replaces serial hierarchical traversal with a unified CUDA kernel implementing two parallel filters: the R&L Filter retains every leaf node and any internal node whose 2D pixel radius (3σ) falls below a threshold τ_R, while the Ancestor Filter uses precomputed ancestor paths to cull all descendants of retained internal nodes, ensuring each branch keeps only the coarsest qualifying level. This decouples filtering cost from tree depth L, scaling only with total in-frustum Gaussian count N. For redundancy elimination, the paper introduces the GTC metric (average per-Gaussian KPC within each tile, averaged over all tiles and views) and sets the Gaussian shrinking threshold as τ = λ_G · Ḡ⁻¹, so that scenes with severe Gaussian congestion (low GTC) automatically trigger more aggressive radius reduction, pruning over 75% of remaining redundant key-value pairs with only ~1% PSNR loss.

## Results

FilterGS achieves the highest rendering FPS across all six large-scale scenes, reaching 372 FPS on Block Small (MatrixCity), 234 FPS on Sci-Art (UrbanScene), 297 FPS on Residence (UrbanScene), 290 FPS on College (GauUScene), 212 FPS on Residence (GauUScene), and 354 FPS on Modern-Building (GauUScene). The filtering time t_f is reduced to 0.93–1.81 ms, an order-of-magnitude improvement over LoG (10.46–14.19 ms) and OctreeGS (5.13–7.59 ms). On reconstruction quality, FilterGS maintains competitive PSNR (22.13–27.04 dB) within ~0.3 dB of the best LoD baselines (LoG, OctreeGS), while delivering 2–6× higher frame rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FilterGS decisively outperforms all five baselines on rendering speed: it achieves 372 FPS on Block Small vs. FLoD's 245 FPS (1.5×), OctreeGS's 125 FPS (3.0×), and LoG's 77 FPS (4.8×), with similar margins on all other scenes. The filtering time t_f drops from 10.46–14.19 ms (LoG) and 5.13–7.59 ms (OctreeGS) to 0.93–1.81 ms, confirming the parallel filtering mechanism eliminates the serial traversal bottleneck. On reconstruction quality, FilterGS is competitive but not always the best: LoG achieves slightly higher PSNR on Block Small (26.52 vs. 26.31), Sci-Art (23.27 vs. 23.07), and Modern-Building (27.35 vs. 27.04), while OctreeGS leads on Sci-Art (23.81 vs. 23.07). FLoD, despite being the second-fastest LoD method, sacrifices the most quality (e.g., 25.06 PSNR on Block Small, 21.57 on Residence-U), indicating a more aggressive speed-quality trade-off. Vanilla 3DGS and H3DGS are consistently the slowest (38–83 FPS) and do not employ LoD, making them less relevant for large-scale real-time use. Overall, FilterGS trades at most ~0.3 dB PSNR for a 2–6× FPS gain over the strongest LoD competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

FilterGS builds directly on the hierarchical Gaussian tree structure introduced by FLoD and LoG, which organize large-scale scenes as multi-level Gaussian hierarchies for LoD selection. Its primary departure is replacing the serial, level-by-level traversal (which incurs O(L) synchronization overhead) with a single-pass parallel CUDA kernel combining an R&L radius/leaf filter and an Ancestor filter. For Gaussian shrinking, it extends FlashGS's fixed opacity threshold (τ=1/255) into a scene-adaptive strategy driven by a novel GTC metric that measures per-tile Gaussian congestion, enabling content-aware pruning aggressiveness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~5× speedup over the strongest LoD baseline (372 vs 77 FPS on Block Small) makes real-time rendering of city-scale 3DGS scenes practically viable on consumer hardware, addressing a bottleneck that has limited adoption in AR, autonomous driving, and digital-twin applications. The parallel filtering's depth decoupling (O(N) rather than O(L·N)) generalizes to arbitrarily deep hierarchies, and the GTC metric provides a principled, scene-aware alternative to ad-hoc fixed thresholds. Limitations include a ~20% memory overhead for ancestor-path indexing, visible tile-boundary artifacts in low-frequency regions at high λ_G, and evaluation restricted to six scenes at 1080p; whether the GTC pre-rendering pass scales efficiently to scenes with tens of millions of Gaussians remains open.
