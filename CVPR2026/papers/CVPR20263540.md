## Problem

Existing text-to-motion diffusion models rely on bidirectional attention over entire sequences, which breaks temporal causality and prevents real-time or streaming generation, while autoregressive motion models enforce causality but suffer from error accumulation and instability over long horizons. No prior framework unifies the generative fidelity of diffusion with the strict temporal ordering of autoregressive generation in a single, efficient architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language prompt (processed via DistilBERT into word-level embeddings) and, during training, a motion sequence of T frames with 263-dim (HumanML3D) or 296-dim (SnapMoGen) joint representations (velocities, positions, rotations). It outputs a 3D human motion sequence decoded from a 64-dim latent space at 4× temporal downsampling (T frames → T/4 latent steps), supporting both batch generation and real-time streaming via key-value caching at up to 125 fps.

## Method

CMDM comprises three components: (1) MAC-VAE, a causal VAE whose encoder/decoder use 1D causal convolutions and ResNet blocks to produce temporally causal latents, supervised by a motion-language alignment loss (marginal cosine similarity + marginal distance matrix similarity) against PartTMR features; (2) Causal-DiT, an 8-layer transformer with causal self-attention, cross-attention to text, AdaLN for per-frame timestep conditioning, and ROPE, trained via causal diffusion forcing where each frame receives an independent noise level; (3) a Frame-Wise Sampling Schedule (FSS) that assigns lower noise to past frames and higher noise to future ones, so each new frame is predicted from partially denoised preceding frames rather than fully clean ones, reducing exposure bias and cutting inference steps. Flow matching serves as the ODE sampler, and the full system (114M parameters) achieves 28 fps with standard autoregressive sampling and 125 fps with FSS on an A100 GPU.

## Results

On HumanML3D, CMDM with frame-wise sampling (FSS) achieves the best R-Precision (0.588/0.778/0.860 Top-1/2/3), the lowest FID among diffusion and autoregressive methods (0.068), and the highest CLIP-Score (0.685), outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline SALAD (FID 0.076, CLIP 0.671) and the strongest VQ baseline MoMask (FID 0.045, CLIP 0.637). On SnapMoGen, CMDM w/ FSS attains R-Precision 0.831/0.926/0.958, FID 14.45, and CLIP-Score 0.702, surpassing all VQ, diffusion, and autoregressive baselines. For long-horizon generation, CMDM achieves the best subsequence R-Top3 (0.782 on HumanML3D, 0.852 on SnapMoGen) and the lowest transition AUJ (0.42 on HumanML3D, 70.35 on SnapMoGen) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMDM w/ FSS outperforms the strongest diffusion baseline SALAD [15] on R-Precision (0.588 vs 0.581 Top-1), FID (0.068 vs 0.076), MM-Dist (2.620 vs 2.649), and CLIP-Score (0.685 vs 0.671) on HumanML3D, and similarly dominates on SnapMoGen (FID 14.45 vs 27.80 for StableMoFusion [16]). Against VQ-based methods, CMDM wins on R-Precision, MM-Dist, and CLIP-Score over MoMask [11] (0.588 vs 0.521 Top-1; CLIP 0.685 vs 0.637), though MoMask retains a lower FID (0.045 vs 0.068), indicating slightly sharper individual samples at the cost of weaker semantic alignment. Among autoregressive baselines, CMDM substantially surpasses MARDM [24] (FID 0.068 vs 0.116; CLIP 0.685 vs 0.642) and MotionStreamer [42] (FID 0.068 vs 0.201; CLIP 0.685 vs 0.610) on both datasets. On long-horizon generation (Table 3), CMDM achieves the best subsequence R-Top3 and lowest transition AUJ on both HumanML3D (AUJ 0.42 vs 0.51 for FlowMDM [3] and 0.57 for MARDM) and SnapMoGen (AUJ 70.35 vs 130.33 for MARDM), while FlowMDM's lower PJ on SnapMoGen is attributed to degenerate static outputs rather than genuine smoothness.

## Relation to Prior Work

CMDM extends Diffusion Forcing (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024) from next-token prediction to the motion domain, adapting its independent per-frame noise schedule to enforce causal temporal dependencies in a latent space. The causal VAE backbone is adapted from MotionStreamer (Xiao et al., ICCV 2025), but is augmented with a motion-language alignment loss (borrowing the marginal cosine/distance-matrix design from VAVAE, Yao et al., CVPR 2025) to ground the latent space in semantic features from PartTMR. Conceptually, the paper departs from both full-sequence diffusion models (MDM, MLD, SALAD) that break temporal causality, and from discrete-token autoregressive models (T2M-GPT, MoMask) that suffer from exposure bias, by unifying continuous diffusion denoising with strict causal attention in a single transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving 125 fps streaming motion generation (vs. 11–20 fps for prior autoregressive methods) while simultaneously improving quality metrics, making real-time text-driven motion synthesis feasible for interactive applications. The frame-wise sampling schedule's ability to improve quality over standard autoregressive sampling (by using partially denoised context) is a useful inductive bias that could transfer to other sequential generation tasks. Open questions remain around multi-character interaction, very long sequences (minutes-scale) where minor temporal artifacts accumulate, and dependence on pretrained motion-language models that may struggle with abstract or ambiguous prompts.
