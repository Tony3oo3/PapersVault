## Problem

Multimodal learning on low-quality data faces two intertwined challenges—modality imbalance and noisy corruption—that have traditionally been addressed in isolation. The paper argues both stem from a shared root cause: insufficient quantification and calibration of the model's predictive uncertainty, leading to overconfident yet unreliable predictions. No existing unified, model-agnostic framework explicitly leverages statistical uncertainty guarantees to simultaneously mitigate both issues during training.

## Input / Output

The method accepts paired multimodal inputs (audio spectrograms + video frames, RGB images + depth maps, or text + images) and outputs a single class label. During training, unimodal features are projected into a higher-dimensional space and split into n sub-components, each independently scored by a conformal predictor before selective fusion; during inference, this decomposition is bypassed and raw encoder features are fused directly. The conformal predictor is maintained on a held-out calibration set and refreshed after every epoch, so the reliability scores evolve with the model throughout training.

## Method

CPSC introduces a self-calibrating training loop built around a dynamically updated conformal predictor. The Representation Self-Calibration (RSC) module decomposes each unimodal feature into n components via a modality-specific fully-connected layer, scores each component's reliability using conformal prediction sets (rank of the true label within the prediction set), and reconstructs the calibrated feature by averaging the top-K most reliable components, with KL-divergence constraints enforcing consistency and diversity among components. The Gradient Self-Calibration (GSC) module estimates modality-wise reliability by comparing each unimodal prediction against the multimodal consensus label through the same conformal mechanism, then applies a linear re-weighting w(ρ)=a·ρ+b to modulate per-modality gradient magnitudes during backpropagation. After each parameter update, the conformal quantile is recomputed on the calibration set using the refreshed model, ensuring the uncertainty estimates remain synchronized with the evolving network. Two theoretical propositions bound the representation deviation of the calibrated feature and show that GSC reduces stochastic gradient variance when reliability is positively correlated with gradient norm.

## Results

On the imbalanced multimodal learning setting, CPSC achieves 76.08% multimodal accuracy on Kinetics Sounds, 87.83% on CREMA-D, and 77.66% on AVE, outperforming the strongest baseline (IPRM) by 1.26%, 2.48%, and 3.05% respectively. On the robust multimodal learning setting, CPSC attains 73.12% clean accuracy on NYU Depth V2, 62.12% on SUN RGB-D, and 80.07% on MVSA-Single, with the largest gains under high noise (e.g., 47.40% vs. 46.21% for EAU on NYU Depth V2 with Salt-Pepper at ε=10). The method also improves unimodal accuracy, e.g., 80.38% video accuracy on CREMA-D vs. 76.41% for IPRM, indicating that the self-calibration mechanism benefits individual modalities as well.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPSC outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and metric in both imbalanced and robust settings. The closest competitor is IPRM [49], which CPSC surpasses by 1.26% on Kinetics Sounds (76.08 vs. 74.82), 2.48% on CREMA-D (87.83 vs. 85.35), and 3.05% on AVE (77.66 vs. 74.61) in multimodal accuracy; on robust datasets, CPSC leads IPRM by 2.99% on NYU Depth V2 clean and 3.45% on SUN RGB-D clean. LFM [3] is the strongest baseline on CREMA-D (86.02%) but is surpassed by 1.81% by CPSC, while also showing a notable gap in unimodal video accuracy (75.27% vs. 80.38%). ARL [50] and DGL [51] trail CPSC by 1.7–3.3% on imbalanced datasets and by 1.3–5.8% on robust datasets. Among robust-learning baselines, EAU [8] is the strongest under clean conditions (79.15% on MVSA, 72.05% on NYU Depth V2) but CPSC extends the lead under noise, e.g., 63.32% vs. 61.78% on NYU Depth V2 with Gaussian ε=10. NLC [57] and ECML [56] are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing CPSC by 5–10% across all robust settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of multimodal learning research—modality imbalance (addressed via gradient modulation, weighted losses, and data re-sampling) and noisy corruption (addressed via robust fusion architectures and reliability modeling)—under a single predictive-uncertainty framework. It departs from prior conformal prediction applications in multimodal settings, which were either post-hoc (generating prediction sets after training) or embedded in loss functions, by integrating CP as a dynamically updated, self-calibrating mechanism that operates at both the feature-representation level (RSC) and the gradient-optimization level (GSC) throughout training. The model-agnostic, distribution-free nature of CP distinguishes this approach from Bayesian or evidential uncertainty methods that require specific architectural choices or prior assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a single training-time framework that handles both imbalance and noise without inference overhead, CPSC offers practitioners a practical, drop-in alternative to the growing zoo of specialized methods for each low-quality-data scenario. The conformal predictor's finite-sample coverage guarantees add a statistical rigor that heuristic reliability scores in prior work lack. Open questions include whether the per-iteration calibration-set recomputation scales to very large datasets or many modalities, whether the theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1–2) hold under the non-stationary conditions of ongoing training, and how the framework extends to generative or retrieval-based multimodal tasks beyond classification.
