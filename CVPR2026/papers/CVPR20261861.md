## Problem

Vision-Language Models excel at semantic and commonsense reasoning but lack grounded understanding of physical dynamics, since they are trained on static visual-language data with no causal interactions. This prevents VLMs from reliably planning fine-grained robotic manipulation tasks—such as non-toppling pushes, deformable object shaping, or precise stacking—where small variations in contact force or timing produce drastically different outcomes. The paper asks whether VLMs can acquire physical reasoning at test time, without any additional training, by coupling them with physics simulation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single calibrated RGB-D image (Intel RealSense D435i) and a natural-language task instruction specifying the goal and success/failure conditions. It outputs a sequence of continuous 6-DoF end-effector poses in SE(3) plus gripper open width, executed open-loop on a Franka Research 3 arm. Actions are generated hierarchically: the VLM first proposes symbolic action types (MOVE, GRASP, RELEASE) with continuous parameters, which a deterministic ACTION2POSE mapping converts into the full 6-DoF trajectory.

## Method

SIMPACT has two components. First, an automated simulation-construction pipeline segments objects via GroundedSAM2, reconstructs 3D meshes (Hunyuan3D) or particle volumes for deformables, estimates 6-DoF poses with FoundationPose, and prompts the VLM to infer physical parameters (mass, friction, elasticity), selecting among MuJoCo, projective dynamics, or MPM solvers. Second, a VLM-driven iterative planner samples K=10 initial action sequences, rolls each out in the simulator, and then uses the VLM as an in-context optimizer: rendered simulation frames and numerical states from all rollouts are fed back as context so the VLM reasons about which actions failed and why, producing a refined sequence. This loop repeats (up to K_max=13) until the VLM's success evaluator confirms task completion in simulation, after which the winning action sequence is executed on the real robot. The key insight is that simulation rollouts serve as physically grounded in-context examples, enabling the VLM to perform test-time physical reasoning—anticipating contact outcomes and correcting unstable strategies—without any task-specific training.

## Results

SIMPACT achieves success rates of 40–90% across seven real-world fine-grained manipulation tasks (non-toppling push: 80%, bowl stacking: 60%, pivoting: 40%, shape rope: 90%, shape dough: 80%, avoid obstacle: 80%, sweeping: 70%), evaluated over 10 trials per task. The VLA baseline π0.5 fails on all seven tasks (0%), while VLM-based baselines VoxPoser and MOKA reach at most 20% on individual tasks. SIMPACT outperforms the best baseline by at least 20 percentage points on every task, with the largest margins on shape rope (+70 pp over MOKA) and non-toppling push (+80 pp over all baselines).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIMPACT decisively outperforms all three baselines on every task. The VLA model π0.5 [2] achieves 0% success across all seven tasks, confirming that large-scale action-labeled training does not transfer to out-of-domain, physics-intensive manipulation. Among VLM-based methods, VoxPoser [25] (volumetric value maps) reaches 20% only on bowl stacking and sweeping, while MOKA [16] (keypoint affordances) reaches 20% only on bowl stacking and shape rope; both fail entirely on the remaining five tasks. SIMPACT's advantage is most pronounced on tasks requiring precise force/timing control (non-toppling push: 80% vs. 0% for all baselines) and deformable-object manipulation (shape rope: 90% vs. 20% for MOKA; shape dough: 80% vs. 0% for all baselines). The paper attributes the gap to SIMPACT's simulation-in-the-loop reasoning, which lets the VLM detect and correct physically infeasible actions (e.g., pushing too high causing toppling) before real-world execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIMPACT carves new ground at the intersection of VLM-based manipulation planning and model-based control. Prior VLM-for-robotics work (VoxPoser, MOKA) augments VLMs with static geometric representations (value maps, keypoints) but lacks temporal dynamics, while VLA models (π0.5) require large-scale action-labeled training and generalize poorly to out-of-domain physics-heavy tasks. The closest prior work, Prompting-with-the-Future, uses rigid-body simulation in an MPC loop with the VLM only as a reward signal; SIMPACT departs by using the VLM for both informed action sampling and iterative in-context optimization over simulation rollouts, and by extending to multi-physics (rigid + deformable) simulation constructed automatically from a single RGB-D image via visual foundation models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physics simulation can serve as a zero-shot, test-time reasoning substrate for VLMs, eliminating the need for task-specific training while achieving 40–90% success on fine-grained manipulation tasks where all baselines fail. This opens a path toward generalizable embodied intelligence where the VLM's commonsense knowledge is grounded in physical dynamics without retraining. Key open questions include: how well the approach scales to cluttered multi-object scenes, whether the sim-to-real gap (from single-view reconstruction and VLM-estimated physical parameters) can be closed with system identification or real-time feedback, and whether the open-loop execution limitation can be addressed with tighter perception-action loops. The finding that increasing in-context samples from 10 to 20 degrades performance (bowl stacking: 60%→20%) highlights a practical constraint on VLM context-length reasoning that will matter for any simulation-in-the-loop VLM system.
