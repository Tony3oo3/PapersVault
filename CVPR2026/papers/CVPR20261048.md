## Problem

Existing articulated object datasets lack the combination of physical grounding (metric scale, per-part material and mass), complex multi-DoF joints, interior geometry, and broad category diversity needed for realistic simulation and functional understanding. PartNet-Mobility covers 2,346 objects but only single-DoF motions without physical attributes, while ArtVIP adds physical properties to just 206 objects. This leaves a gap for large-scale, physically grounded articulated assets that support chained kinematic dependencies and plausible physics-based interaction.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests textured 3D meshes from 10 repositories (ShapeNet, ABO, 3D-Future, HSSD, Objaverse, TexVerse, 3DComPaT200, PartNeXt, SSDB) along with human-defined category-level templates specifying part labels, motion types, and dependency options. Outputs are fully annotated articulated objects with functional part labels, kinematic joint parameters (type, axis, limits, multi-DoF), per-part material and mass, metric scale, and completed interior geometry, released in GLB, USD, URDF, and JSON with standardized orientation and coordinate conventions for direct simulator integration.

## Method

The core contribution is a four-stage semi-automated annotation pipeline: (1) part segmentation via ASIA few-shot segmentation trained on category groups with a dozen manual 3D exemplars rendered to multi-view masks, projected to 3D through topological oversegmentation with label voting and union-find instance decomposition; (2) interior completion using geometry-informed extension for partially modeled parts, procedural population of missing articulated components (e.g., microwave turntables), and template-driven addition of interaction affordances (shelves, racks); (3) motion annotation inferring joint type, axis, and limits from PCA-aligned bounding boxes and contact-region point sampling, with kinematic dependencies resolved via spatial connectivity and category templates; (4) physical property estimation querying LLMs for metric scale ranges and material densities, then computing per-part volumes via tetrahedralization (solid) or inward-normal-offset shell construction (hollow). Human verification is embedded after segmentation and motion stages through a web-based 3D annotation interface, reducing manual time by 32.0% and 33.5% respectively, with 50.12% of automatic annotations requiring no correction.

## Results

For part mobility analysis (Table 3), FPNGroupMot trained on Artiverse achieves F1=50.7 and +MAO=10.8 on the Artiverse test set, substantially outperforming the same model trained on PartNet-Mobility (F1=40.6, +MAO=1.2). On the PM test set, Artiverse-trained models also improve segmentation F1 from 54.2 to 55.8. For articulated object generation (Table 4), SINGAPO retrained on Artiverse achieves AS-d_gIoU=0.822 and AOR=0.009 on the Artiverse test set, while the VLM-based Articulate-Anything baseline yields AS-d_gIoU=1.258 and AOR=0.042, confirming that structured 3D data priors outperform VLM-only knowledge for fine-grained articulation synthesis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three methods across two tasks. For part mobility analysis, FPNGroupMot (from S2O [19]) trained on Artiverse outperforms the same model trained on PartNet-Mobility by +10.1 F1 points (50.7 vs 40.6) and +9.6 +MAO points (10.8 vs 1.2) on the Artiverse test set, demonstrating that Artiverse's richer multi-DoF joints and diverse categories improve generalization. On the PM test set, the advantage narrows (F1 55.8 vs 54.2), and PM-trained models retain a slight edge on +MAO (14.3 vs 8.7), suggesting Artiverse's complexity is harder to exploit on simpler PM geometries. For generation, SINGAPO [32] retrained on Artiverse consistently beats Articulate-Anything [27] (VLM-based) on all reconstruction metrics: on the Artiverse test set, SINGAPO achieves AS-d_gIoU=0.822 vs 1.258 and AOR=0.009 vs 0.042, indicating VLM priors struggle with fine-grained articulation without direct 3D supervision. On the PM test set, SINGAPO also leads (AS-d_gIoU 0.768 vs 1.179, AOR 0.022 vs 0.024), though the gap is smaller. Overall, Artiverse-trained models win on the harder Artiverse evaluation across both tasks, while the VLM baseline is clearly outperformed by the data-driven SINGAPO approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

Artiverse positions itself as a comprehensive successor to PartNet-Mobility (the most widely used articulated object dataset) and ArtVIP (the only prior dataset with per-part physical properties), addressing their key limitations: single-DoF-only joints, limited category coverage (46 and 29 categories respectively), and absence of metric scale, material, and mass annotations. Rather than building on a single prior method, the paper aggregates objects from 10 heterogeneous repositories and introduces a semi-automated annotation pipeline that repurposes few-shot segmentation (ASIA) and geometric heuristics to initialize part boundaries and joint parameters, validated by multi-stage human verification. The annotation schema extends prior kinematic-only annotations by adding functional dependencies (e.g., button-press → door-open) and interior completion, moving beyond the part-mobility labels that dominated earlier datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Artiverse is likely to become a standard benchmark for articulated object research because it simultaneously scales category diversity (88 sub-categories), joint complexity (multi-DoF cylindrical and universal joints), and physical grounding (per-part mass, material, metric scale) in a way no prior dataset achieves. The observed performance drop of FPNGroupMot on the Artiverse test set (F1 dropping from 55.8 to 50.7, +MAO from 14.3 to 10.8) signals that current methods cannot yet handle the richer kinematic structures, establishing a clear research gap. Open questions include whether generative models can learn from the denser Artiverse distribution to close the gap with VLM-based approaches, and whether the semi-automated pipeline generalizes to categories with highly atypical articulation patterns not covered by the current templates.
