## Problem

Existing single-image 3D avatar methods animate bodies solely through rigid joint transformations, producing over-rigid motion that cannot capture realistic cloth dynamics. Personalized approaches that do model cloth deformation require dedicated multi-view video captures per subject, making them impractical for arbitrary individuals. This paper addresses the gap of reconstructing animatable 3D avatars with motion-dependent cloth dynamics from a single image in a fully zero-shot, feed-forward manner without any subject-specific optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image of a person together with a motion history of 15 time steps (one second) containing 3D poses, pose velocities, accelerations, and keypoint velocities in 6D rotation parameterization; when no history is available (e.g., the first frame), all past motions are zero-initialized and the history is normalized to a canonical world coordinate system for consistent motion semantics. The output is a canonical-space 3D Gaussian avatar whose geometry already encodes motion-dependent cloth deformations, animated via linear blend skinning with predicted per-Gaussian skinning weight offsets and rendered in real time through a 3DGS renderer.

## Method

The architecture pairs a Static Transformer pretrained on large-scale static captures (LHM) that supplies geometry and appearance priors via Multimodal Transformer Blocks with AdaLN modulation, and a Dynamic Transformer trained from scratch that ingests motion tokens to model cloth dynamics. A static-to-dynamic knowledge transfer strategy adapts the pretrained Static Transformer using lightweight LoRA adapters while the Dynamic Transformer learns motion-dependent deformations from limited dynamic supervision, preserving static knowledge without catastrophic forgetting. The proposed DynaFlow loss provides geometry-only supervision by rendering an xy coordinate map (replacing Gaussian colors with projected screen-space positions), computing optical flow via LightGlue between rendered and ground-truth images, and enforcing that sampled xy coordinates at flow source points match flow targets—decoupling geometry from color and enabling long-range correspondence under large non-rigid deformations. A single linear-layer Gaussian decoder outputs 3DGS parameters and skinning weight offsets, and the final avatar is animated with LBS combining predicted offsets with diffused skinning weights. The authors additionally reannotate SMPL-X fittings across DNA-Rendering, 4D-Dress, and Actors-HQ using a unified multi-view 2D keypoint pipeline, yielding over 11M reliable training supervisions.

## Results

DynaAvatar achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three evaluation datasets. On 4D-Dress, it reaches 23.74 PSNR / 0.960 SSIM / 0.064 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline IDOL (21.31 / 0.948 / 0.077) by 2.4 dB in PSNR. On DNA-Rendering, DynaAvatar scores 19.45 PSNR versus 17.84 for IDOL and 17.42 for LHM. On the cross-domain Actors-HQ benchmark, DynaAvatar attains 21.38 PSNR / 0.916 SSIM / 0.128 LPIPS, leading IDOL (20.93 / 0.910 / 0.138) and LHM (20.29 / 0.908 / 0.151).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynaAvatar outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against IDOL [60], the closest zero-shot competitor, DynaAvatar gains 1.6 dB PSNR on DNA-Rendering (19.45 vs. 17.84), 2.4 dB on 4D-Dress (23.74 vs. 21.31), and 0.45 dB on Actors-HQ (21.38 vs. 20.93). Against LHM [37], the static Transformer backbone that DynaAvatar builds upon, the margins are 2.0 dB (DNA-Rendering), 2.7 dB (4D-Dress), and 1.1 dB (Actors-HQ) in PSNR. PERSONA [43], a single-image method with cloth dynamics but requiring per-subject optimization, is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing DynaAvatar by 4.5 dB on DNA-Rendering and 4.7 dB on 4D-Dress. The largest absolute gains appear on 4D-Dress, where the diversity of clothing and motions most stresses dynamic deformation modeling, while the smallest gap (Actors-HQ) reflects the cross-domain generalization challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynaAvatar builds directly on LHM's pretrained multimodal Transformer as its static backbone, inheriting strong geometry and appearance priors from large-scale static captures, then extends it with a from-scratch Dynamic Transformer and LoRA adapters to inject motion-dependent cloth deformations. It departs from prior zero-shot single-image methods (IDOL, LHM, AniGS) that animate only via rigid joint transformations, and from personalized dynamic avatar methods (PERSONA, SeqAvatar, MPMAvatar) that require per-subject multi-view optimization. The DynaFlow loss introduces optical-flow-guided geometric supervision into 3DGS avatar training, a departure from the standard L1/SSIM/LPIPS image-space losses used throughout the field. The reannotation pipeline for DNA-Rendering, 4D-Dress, and Actors-HQ addresses a practical data-quality gap that had limited prior dynamic avatar training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being the first to combine zero-shot feed-forward inference, single-image input, and motion-dependent cloth dynamics, DynaAvatar removes the multi-view capture and per-subject optimization bottlenecks that have constrained realistic avatar animation. The static-to-dynamic LoRA transfer strategy is a practical recipe for leveraging abundant static data to bootstrap dynamic learning, a pattern likely transferable to other motion-dependent reconstruction tasks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to extremely loose or layered garments, whether the 1-second motion history window is sufficient for long-duration coherence, and how the method handles human-object interactions that are explicitly excluded from training. The reannotated dataset (11M+ image supervisions) also provides a valuable resource for the broader dynamic avatar community.
