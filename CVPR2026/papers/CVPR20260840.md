## Problem

Few-shot classification of B-rep CAD models remains challenging in industrial settings where annotated part data is scarce. Existing approaches either demand massive labeled samples for supervised training or rely on contrastive pre-training evaluated via linear probing, which still requires abundant downstream labels. The contrastive pre-training combined with parameter-efficient prompt tuning route has not been explored for few-shot B-rep classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260840/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a B-rep CAD model (boundary representation with faces, edges, and topological connectivity) and converts it into a hybrid graph with three topology levels and multi-semantic node/edge features (parametric attributes grouped by category plus UV-sampled non-parametric grids encoded by CNNs). It outputs a part class label under K-shot evaluation (K=1, 3, 5) on TraceParts-11 (11 classes, 943 models) and FabWave-31 (31 classes, 2,775 models), with pre-training performed on ~170,000 unlabeled DeepCAD models.

## Method

PP-Brep builds a hybrid graph representation combining an MST-based local topology graph, a constraint-based global parallel graph (connecting near-parallel non-adjacent faces), and an FPS-based regional association hypergraph (grouping geometrically clustered faces into hyperedges), with multi-semantic features from both parametric attribute groups and UV-sampled non-parametric grids. A Hierarchical HeteroGNN backbone first applies hypergraph convolution to capture regional structure, then heterogeneous TransformerConv layers jointly propagate over the local and parallel graphs with learned fusion weights. Pre-training uses an RL Actor-Critic controller that dynamically adjusts SimGRACE perturbation strength per sample, combined with an in-place perturbation mechanism that caches and restores backbone parameters to avoid full model duplication, reducing per-epoch time from ~1104s to ~19s at batch size 8192. For few-shot adaptation, a Structure-Aware Prompt module maintains three learnable token pools (local, global, regional), generates node-specific prompts via All-in-One-style cross-attention conditioned on each node's multi-graph neighborhood, and fuses them with learned weights before adding to frozen backbone inputs.

## Results

On TraceParts-11 (11-way 1-shot), PP-Brep achieves 73.35% accuracy, 73.18% F1, and 94.79% AUROC, outperforming all general-purpose graph prompting baselines. On the harder FabWave-31 (31-way 1-shot), it maintains 72.98% accuracy and 72.75% F1, showing strong robustness to increased category count. At 5-shot, PP-Brep reaches 87.55% accuracy on FabWave-31, surpassing the best baseline (MultiGprompt at 70.02%) by 17.5 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PP-Brep outperforms all five general-purpose graph prompting baselines on both datasets across all three metrics. On TraceParts-11 (1-shot), it leads the strongest competitor MultiGprompt by 4.72% in accuracy (73.35% vs. 68.63%) and by 4.90% in AUROC (94.79% vs. 89.89%). On the more challenging FabWave-31 (1-shot), the gap widens: PP-Brep achieves 72.98% accuracy versus GCoT's 64.34% and MultiGprompt's 60.09%, while the SimGRACE-based baselines (sim+EP, sim+GPF) drop sharply to ~38–39%, indicating that generic prompts fail under increased category complexity. At 5-shot, PP-Brep's advantage grows further, exceeding all baselines by over 13.7% on TraceParts-11 and 17.0% on FabWave-31, demonstrating superior scalability with additional support samples. The structure-aware prompt design is critical: ablation shows that removing the multi-graph context (Non-Context prompt) costs 2.6% accuracy, and full fine-tuning collapses to 43.82% due to catastrophic overfitting on small support sets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: B-rep-to-graph representation (UV-Net, BRepNet, BRepGAT), graph contrastive pre-training (SimGRACE, GraphCL), and graph prompt tuning (PSP, MultiGPrompt, GCoT). It departs from Jones et al.'s generative pre-training + full fine-tuning route by adopting contrastive pre-training with parameter-efficient structure-aware prompting, a combination the authors note has not been explored for few-shot B-rep classification. The hybrid graph representation (MST-based local topology, constraint-based global parallel edges, FPS-based regional hypergraph) extends prior single-graph B-rep encodings by explicitly modeling multi-scale topology from local adjacency to feature-level grouping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-place perturbation mechanism (57× speedup at batch 8192 while matching or slightly exceeding duplication-based SimGRACE) makes large-scale B-rep contrastive pre-training practically feasible on a single GPU, which is a meaningful efficiency contribution for the CAD community. The robustness gap—our method drops only 0.37% accuracy from 11 to 31 categories while generic-prompt baselines drop 8–22%—suggests structure-aware prompting is essential for complex B-rep tasks rather than a marginal gain. Open questions include whether the hybrid graph construction (particularly the hypergraph grouping) generalizes to other B-rep tasks like feature recognition or retrieval, and whether the 'Full-set K-Shot' evaluation setting overestimates real-world few-shot performance where test classes may be entirely unseen.
