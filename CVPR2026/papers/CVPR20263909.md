## Problem

Existing vision-language tracking methods rely heavily on large-scale bounding box annotations (e.g., 3.52M boxes on LaSOT) and equally fuse all visual and linguistic tokens, leading to redundant computation and poor cross-modal alignment. This paper asks whether a tracker can learn instance-level tracking from unlabeled videos using only a single natural language description as the target reference, without any per-frame bounding box supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263909/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a single natural language instruction Q_text describing an arbitrary instance and a video clip of T frames (T×H×W×3 RGB). No initial bounding box is provided; instead, a large vision-language model (APE or LISA) generates a pseudo-bounding box for only the first frame to bootstrap training. The output is a sequence of per-frame target bounding boxes B = {B}_{t=1}^{T}. At inference, the model processes a template frame (with pseudo-box) and a search clip of three unlabeled frames, running at 56 FPS on an A100 GPU.

## Method

SVLTrack employs a weak-to-strong consistency self-supervised pipeline with forward and backward tracking, where pseudo-labels from an LVLM supervise only the initial frame while unlabeled frames are learned via consistency between weakly and strongly augmented views. The core architectural contribution is a Dynamic Token Aggregation Module (DTA) inserted between MHSA and MLP layers that treats visual tokens unequally: (i) an anchor token selects top-K important template tokens via cross-attention, (ii) selected tokens are merged by attention scores and aggregated into language tokens to eliminate redundant visual noise, and (iii) the fused language tokens guide extraction of potential target tokens from the search frame, which are propagated temporally to subsequent frames. A denoising training strategy discards the top 20% of samples with the largest Euclidean distance between the strongly augmented classification map and a pseudo-Gaussian map derived from the weakly augmented prediction, improving robustness to noisy pseudo-labels. The model uses a ViT-Base (DropMAE-pretrained) visual encoder, BERT language encoder, and is trained with Focal + GIoU + L1 losses under AdamW optimization.

## Results

Under the self-supervised VL tracking setting (language-only initialization, no bounding box annotations during training), SVLTrack-L384 achieves AUC scores of 43.9% (TNL2K), 53.9% (LaSOT), and 66.7% (OTB99). In the self-supervised visual tracking setting (bounding-box-only initialization), SVLTrack-V384 reaches 55.5% (TNL2K), 66.8% (LaSOT), and 70.6% (OTB99) AUC, surpassing the best unsupervised tracker Diff-Tracker by 16.5% AUC on LaSOT. On LaSOT_ext, SVLTrack-V384 achieves 49.7% AUC, approaching fully-supervised methods like ODTrack (52.4%) and ARTrack (51.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the self-supervised VL setting, SVLTrack-L256 outperforms ATTracker [10]—the closest semi-supervised competitor that uses both language and bounding box for initialization—by 1.9% AUC on TNL2K (42.5 vs. 40.6), 3.6% on LaSOT (52.5 vs. 48.9), and 9.9% on OTB99 (65.4 vs. 55.5), despite requiring no bounding box annotations at all. However, SVLTrack-L still trails fully-supervised NL-only methods like UVLTrack [22] (55.7% vs. 43.9% TNL2K AUC) and JointNLT [47] (54.6% vs. 43.9%), reflecting the inherent cost of removing bounding box supervision. In the self-supervised visual tracking setting, SVLTrack-V384 dominates all unsupervised and self-supervised vision-only baselines: it surpasses Diff-Tracker [40] by 16.5% AUC on LaSOT (66.8 vs. 48.6) and SSTrack-256 [45] by 2.0% on LaSOT (66.8 vs. 64.8) and 3.4% on TNL2K (55.5 vs. 52.1). On LaSOT_ext, SVLTrack-V384 (49.7% AUC) narrows the gap to fully-supervised trackers like ODTrack [44] (52.4%) and ARTrack [33] (51.9%) to within 2–3 points, while SVLTrack-L384 (35.2% AUC) remains below fully-supervised VL methods such as MemVLT [9] (52.1%) and All-in-One [39] (54.5%), underscoring the difficulty of the language-only self-supervised regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVLTrack carves out a new task configuration—self-supervised VL tracking with language-only initialization—by repurposing three existing threads: (1) the weak-to-strong consistency paradigm from self-supervised representation learning, (2) LVLM-based pseudo-label generation (APE, LISA) borrowed from open-vocabulary grounding, and (3) the transformer-based tracking backbone (ViT + BERT) standard in recent VL trackers like UVLTrack and JointNLT. Its key architectural departure is the Dynamic Token Aggregation Module, which replaces the uniform multi-head attention fusion used by prior VL trackers with a selective top-K token selection-and-merge strategy, motivated by the observation that most visual tokens carry little semantic signal relative to the language description. The paper does not build on a single prior method but rather synthesizes components from unsupervised tracking (pseudo-labeling, cycle consistency), LVLM grounding, and VL tracking into a unified self-supervised pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in dramatically reducing annotation cost: a single natural language sentence replaces thousands of per-frame bounding boxes, making VL tracking feasible on unlabeled internet video. However, the performance gap to fully-supervised VL trackers remains substantial (e.g., 42.5 vs. 64.9 AUC on TNL2K), and the entire pipeline is bottlenecked by LVLM pseudo-label quality, which the authors acknowledge as a key limitation. Open questions include whether the DTA module's selective fusion generalizes to longer video sequences or more diverse language instructions, and whether the pseudo-label dependency can be replaced by purely contrastive or generative self-supervision signals that do not require an external LVLM at training time.
