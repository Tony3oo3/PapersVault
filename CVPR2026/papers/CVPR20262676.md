## Problem

Standard cross-entropy classification forces every sample into one of a fixed set of classes, offering no principled escape route for ambiguous, mislabeled, or out-of-distribution instances. Existing robust loss functions (GCE, SCE, APL, AFL, ANL) mitigate label noise but still apply a uniform penalty to uncertain samples, preventing the network from dynamically reallocating probability mass away from incorrect class predictions in high-uncertainty regions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images at dataset-specific resolutions (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 224×224 for WebVision, ILSVRC-12, and Clothing-1M) and produces a probability vector over C class logits plus one additional drainage logit. At inference, two operating modes are available: 'open drainage' exposes all C+1 probabilities (the drainage probability serving as an explicit uncertainty/rejection score), while 'closed drainage' renormalizes over the C class logits only, making the method a drop-in replacement for standard classifiers.

## Method

The Drainage framework augments the output layer with a single extra neuron (the drainage node, logit z_d) and introduces a novel loss expressed as a log-sum-exp over the target, drainage, and non-target logits: ℓ = LSE{0, z_d−z_t+log α, (z_j−z_t+log α)_{j∈J}, (z_j−z_d+log β)_{j∈J}}. This formulation imposes soft constraints that encourage the target logit to dominate, the drainage logit to surpass all non-target logits, and non-target logits to remain below the target, with hyperparameters α and β controlling the relative strength of these constraints. The loss is end-to-end differentiable, convex in the logits, and provably monotone in both target and drainage probability (Propositions 1–2). Setting α=1, β→0, and p_d=0 recovers standard cross-entropy, establishing the drainage loss as a strict generalization. For open-set recognition, the drainage logit is fixed to a constant, decoupling it from the backbone and turning it into a dedicated unknown-class score.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with 45% asymmetric label noise, Drainage achieves 82.23% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (ANL CE at 72.53%) by 9.7 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 with 45% asymmetric noise, Drainage reaches 52.69% versus GCE's 42.70% (a 10-point gap). On real-world noisy datasets, Drainage leads on WebVision (68.80% vs. 67.53% for ANL CE), Clothing-1M (70.42% vs. 68.94% for AFL), and CIFAR-100N (57.77% vs. 56.62% for ANL CE), while trailing GCE on CIFAR-10N (79.85% vs. 81.70%) and ANL CE on ILSVRC12 (64.90% vs. 65.77%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Drainage is compared against five noise-robust loss functions: GCE, SCE, AFL, APL, and ANL CE. In low-noise or clean settings (noise ≤ 0.2), all methods are within 1–2 percentage points, with CE, GCE, and ANL CE often tied at the top; Drainage is competitive but not always the best (e.g., 91.30% vs. 92.16% for CE on clean CIFAR-10). The advantage of Drainage emerges decisively at high noise: on CIFAR-10 with 45% asymmetric noise it leads by 9.7 points over ANL CE (82.23% vs. 72.53%), and on CIFAR-100 with 45% asymmetric noise it leads GCE by 10 points (52.69% vs. 42.70%). Among the five baselines, GCE is the strongest competitor in high-noise instance-dependent settings (e.g., 59.27% on CIFAR-10 at 50% noise), while SCE consistently underperforms all other robust losses. On real-world noisy datasets, Drainage wins on WebVision, Clothing-1M, and CIFAR-100N but trails GCE on CIFAR-10N (79.85% vs. 81.70%) and ANL CE on ILSVRC12 (64.90% vs. 65.77%), suggesting the gap narrows when noise is more structured or the dataset is cleaner.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by adding a single 'drainage node' to the output layer of a standard classifier and designing a cross-entropy-derived loss (expressed via log-sum-exp) that routes uncertain probability mass to this node rather than to incorrect classes. Conceptually, it repurposes the uncertainty-modeling idea from heteroskedastic regression into a classification setting, while departing from existing noise-robust losses (GCE, SCE, APL, AFL, ANL) that apply uniform penalties and offer no 'escape route' for ambiguous samples. The approach is also positioned as a lightweight alternative to Open Set Recognition methods like OpenMax, integrating uncertainty routing directly into the architecture and loss for end-to-end training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The drainage framework offers a unified, architecturally minimal solution (one extra neuron + modified loss) that handles asymmetric, instance-dependent, and real-world label noise simultaneously, achieving up to 10% accuracy gains over strong baselines in high-noise regimes. Its natural extension to Open Set Recognition and its orthogonality to input-level regularizers like MixUp/CutMix suggest broad applicability across web-scale dataset cleaning and semi-supervised settings. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class spaces (e.g., full ImageNet-1K), how it interacts with modern transformer backbones, and whether the hyperparameters α and β can be made adaptive rather than fixed per dataset.
