## Problem

The rapid proliferation of community-generated LoRAs has created a vast but unstructured ecosystem where selecting the right adapter requires extensive trial and error. Existing discovery methods rely on unreliable user-provided descriptions, names, or popularity metrics that poorly predict actual generative behavior, and no standardized, metadata-free representation exists to characterize what a LoRA does to image generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For indexing, each LoRA is applied to 280 curated prompts across 16 fixed seeds on SDXL 1.0, producing paired images (with and without the adapter) that are encoded by CLIP ViT-B/32 (d=512). For retrieval, a free-text query is processed by computing the mean CLIP text-embedding difference between 280 prompts with and without the query appended as a suffix, yielding a 512-dimensional query vector. The system outputs a three-part representation per LoRA (a 512-d Direction vector, a Strength scalar, and a Consistency scalar) and a ranked, threshold-filtered list of candidate adapters.

## Method

CARLoS characterizes each LoRA by generating paired images (base model vs. LoRA-augmented) across diverse prompts and seeds, then computing CLIP-diff vectors in 512-dimensional embedding space. These vectors are summarized into three metrics: Direction (mean CLIP-diff, capturing the semantic shift), Strength (mean L2 norm, quantifying effect magnitude), and Consistency (mean pairwise cosine similarity, measuring effect stability across prompts and seeds). Retrieval computes a query vector as the mean CLIP text-diff over prompts with the query suffixed, ranks LoRAs by cosine similarity between the query vector and each LoRA's Direction, and filters out adapters exceeding a Strength threshold (τ_s=9.8) or falling below a Consistency threshold (τ_c=0.041). The entire pipeline is training-free and requires no metadata beyond the adapter weights themselves, with indexing taking ~7 GPU-hours per LoRA and retrieval completing in under a second.

## Results

CARLoS consistently outperforms all four text-embedding baselines across all four VLM evaluators on top-3 retrieval quality (scores normalized min-max across queries and retrievers). The headline gains are most pronounced on SigLIP2 (0.350 vs. 0.307 for the best baseline Qwen3) and Qwen2.5-VL (0.532 vs. 0.495), with smaller but consistent margins on ImageReward (0.505 vs. 0.491) and HPS v2 (0.596 vs. 0.590). A subjective user study with 36 participants across ~100 A/B comparisons confirms the quantitative advantage, with CARLoS preferred on Images Quality, Relevance, and Overall Preference against every baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARLoS is compared against four strong multilingual text-embedding retrievers (Qwen3, E5, BGE-M3-reranker, GTE-mGTE-reranker) that operate on the concatenated user-provided names and descriptions of each LoRA. CARLoS wins on all four evaluation metrics against every baseline, with the closest competitor being Qwen3 across the board (margins of +0.043 on SigLIP2, +0.037 on Qwen2.5-VL, +0.014 on ImageReward, and +0.006 on HPS v2). The gap widens substantially against the weaker baselines: BGE trails CARLoS by 0.151 on SigLIP2 and 0.118 on ImageReward, reflecting its difficulty with sparse, multilingual, and often misleading LoRA descriptions. The paper also notes that textual baselines frequently latch onto irrelevant keywords in names/descriptions or fail to filter out overly strong or inconsistent LoRAs, whereas CARLoS's generation-based representation avoids these failure modes entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARLoS departs from prior LoRA retrieval work (Stylus, LoRAverse, AutoLoRA, DiffAgent) that relies on textual metadata, weight embeddings, or learned gating policies, by instead characterizing each adapter through its actual measured generative effect in CLIP space. It repurposes the CLIP-diff concept—previously used for image editing and prompt-to-prompt consistency (Prompt-to-Prompt, StyleAligned, DiffusionCLIP)—as a one-time behavioral fingerprint for adapters, requiring no training data, names, or descriptions. In the language domain, adapter retrieval methods (LoRARetriever, Arrow, LoGo) operate on task-specific embeddings or weight properties; CARLoS is the first to apply a generation-based, prompt-independent representation to the visual LoRA ecosystem at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine and growing pain point in the generative-AI community: with hundreds of thousands of community LoRAs and unreliable metadata, artists and platforms lack a principled way to discover, filter, or screen adapters. The Strength/Consistency metrics also offer a quantifiable proxy for legal notions of substantiality and volition, potentially aiding copyright screening on hosting platforms. Key limitations include dependence on CLIP ViT-B/32 (weaker on fine-grained texture and spatial composition), restriction to SDXL and popular LoRAs, and a ~7 GPU-hour indexing cost per adapter that limits scalability. Open questions include whether the representation transfers to newer backbones (FLUX, SD3), other adapter types (ControlNets, IP-Adapters), and whether the non-trivial LoRA-scale-to-Strength relationship can be exploited for adaptive generation.
