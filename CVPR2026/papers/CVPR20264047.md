## Problem

Federated active learning (FAL) degrades under realistic conditions of severe global class imbalance and extreme client heterogeneity, yet prior work treats non-IID data merely as a partitioning issue rather than a long-tailed global distribution problem. This leads to systematic bias toward head classes and inefficient annotation budgets. The paper asks: which query model (global vs. local) produces more class-balanced sampling, and how does that balance drive final performance?

## Input / Output

Each client provides its local unlabeled image pool (standard RGB images from FMNIST, CIFAR-10/100, OctMNIST, DermaMNIST), the current global model, its locally trained model, and its labeled set. The method outputs a per-client query set partitioned into class-specific subsets with uniform budget allocation, prototype-assigned pseudo-labels for the unlabeled pool, and a binary model-selection decision (global vs. local) computed per client each AL round. The global imbalance coefficient γ̄ is estimated once in the first round and held fixed, while the local-global divergence d_k is recomputed every round.

## Method

FairFAL integrates three mechanisms. First, an adaptive model-selection module estimates global class imbalance (via a class-balanced subset's predictive prior under the global model) and local-global divergence (normalized symmetric difference of predictive priors), combining them into a continuous score s_k that selects the global model when the distribution is highly imbalanced and clients are homogeneous, and the local model otherwise. Second, prototype-guided pseudo-labeling extracts ℓ₂-normalized features from the global model, computes per-class prototypes by averaging labeled features, and assigns pseudo-labels via cosine similarity, partitioning the unlabeled pool into class-specific subsets to counteract head-class bias. Third, a two-stage sampling strategy first selects the top-κ most uncertain samples per class (using the adaptively chosen query model), then applies greedy k-center selection in a gradient-embedding space (gradients of the classification loss w.r.t. global classifier parameters) to enforce diversity within each class's candidate pool, yielding query sets that are simultaneously informative, diverse, and class-balanced.

## Results

On the medical benchmarks under extreme heterogeneity (α=0.1, ρ=20), FairFAL achieves 72.80% final-round test accuracy on OctMNIST and 73.77% on DermaMNIST, outperforming the strongest FAL baselines KAFAL (70.40% / 73.27%) and LoGo (70.00% / 73.62%) by 2.4 and 0.5 percentage points respectively. On the natural-image benchmarks (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) under the same ρ=20 imbalance, FairFAL consistently leads all 11 baselines across the full AL curve, with the margin widening from FMNIST to CIFAR-100 as task difficulty increases. Under nearly homogeneous clients (α=100), several baselines (notably IFAL) degrade to near-random performance, while FairFAL maintains a clear advantage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FairFAL wins across all reported comparisons. On OctMNIST, it surpasses KAFAL by 2.4 pp, LoGo by 2.8 pp, IFAL by 4.4 pp, and Random by 4.5 pp; on DermaMNIST the margins are tighter (0.15 pp over LoGo, 0.5 pp over KAFAL, 0.8 pp over IFAL, 1.45 pp over Random), reflecting the already-high baseline accuracy on that dataset. On the natural-image benchmarks (FMNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) shown in Figure 3, FairFAL outperforms all uncertainty-based (Entropy, Margin, LC), diversity-based (Coreset), hybrid (BADGE, GCNAL, ALFA-Mix), and FAL (KAFAL, LoGo, IFAL) baselines throughout the AL curve. The gap is most pronounced under α=100 (nearly homogeneous clients), where IFAL and other methods lacking explicit class-balancing mechanisms drop to near-random levels, while FairFAL's adaptive model selection and prototype-guided sampling maintain a stable lead. Under α=0.1 (high heterogeneity), the implicit diversity from client-specific distributions helps baselines perform more competitively, but FairFAL still retains a consistent advantage, particularly on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

FairFAL builds on the two-stage uncertainty-diversity sampling paradigm of BADGE (gradient-embedding space + k-center refinement) and the Coreset diversity mechanism, but recontextualizes both within a federated setting where the query model itself must be chosen adaptively. It departs from prior FAL methods (KAFAL, LoGo, IFAL) that fix a single query model or combine global/local signals in a static manner, by showing that the optimal choice depends on the joint regime of global class imbalance and client heterogeneity. The prototype-guided pseudo-labeling component is a novel addition not present in prior FAL work, motivated by the observation that global features yield more discriminative class representations than local ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central empirical finding—that class-balanced sampling, particularly minority-class acquisition, is the dominant predictor of final FAL performance—provides a clear diagnostic for practitioners designing acquisition strategies under long-tailed federated settings. The adaptive model-selection mechanism is lightweight (a single scalar per client) and privacy-preserving, making it easy to integrate into existing FAL pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only 10 clients and relatively small datasets (≤100 classes), a fixed threshold δ=0.75 that may not transfer across domains, and reliance on the first AL round being randomly sampled for the imbalance estimate. Open questions include scalability to hundreds of clients, applicability to non-vision modalities, and whether the prototype-based approach degrades in very high-dimensional or multi-task federated settings.
