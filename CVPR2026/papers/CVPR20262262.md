## Problem

Existing 3D head avatar pipelines are constrained by multi-view captures, known camera poses, or expression labels, and large reconstruction models that relax some of these constraints still fail to capture fine-grained dynamic deformations (wrinkles, bared teeth) in real time. The paper addresses the gap of building a single feed-forward model that is simultaneously camera-pose-free, expression-free, and input-count-agnostic while producing detailed, geometrically consistent dynamic avatars.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262262/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts 1–4 RGB images at arbitrary camera viewpoints and facial expressions (no pose or expression annotations required), plus FLAME expression coefficients as a driving signal at inference time. It outputs a canonical 3D Gaussian head avatar parameterized in UV space (static attributes: position, opacity, color, scale, rotation; dynamic delta attributes for expression-dependent regions), rendered via LBS and differentiable Gaussian splatting at ~45 fps for full 360° views. An optional 10-second test-time refinement fine-tunes the reconstruction backbone against the input images to sharpen identity-specific details.

## Method

A frozen DINOv3 encoder extracts dense features from each input image; a global self-attention layer fuses them into a variable-length token sequence, making the pipeline inherently input-count-agnostic. Structured, learnable Head Query tokens then cross-attend to the fused features, collapsing them into a fixed-size UV feature map that encodes a canonical, pose-free, expression-free head representation. A lightweight UNet decoder, conditioned on the concatenation of identity features and a FLAME UV position map (constructed from driving expression coefficients via barycentric interpolation), predicts expression-dependent delta Gaussian attributes that are masked and added to the static attributes. Training employs a data distribution adjustment strategy—selecting 20 expressive anchor expressions and retrieving similar frames via FLAME cosine similarity—to rebalance rare dynamics like wrinkles and bared teeth, alongside a mouth-region LPIPS loss for dental detail. The entire system is trained end-to-end on NeRSemble and a self-captured FaceCap dataset (2150 subjects total) with photometric, perceptual, and Gaussian-attribute regularization losses.

## Results

On the NeRSemble test set, FlexAvatar (feed-forward) achieves 21.15 PSNR, 0.8335 SSIM, and 0.2193 LPIPS for self-reenactment, surpassing the best baseline (Portrait4D-v2 at 19.63 PSNR) by ~1.5 dB. After a 10-second refinement, these improve to 22.63 PSNR, 0.8491 SSIM, and 0.1833 LPIPS. For cross-identity reenactment, the feed-forward model reaches 0.8501 CSIM and 7.87 AKD, while the refined version attains 0.8549 CSIM and 7.24 AKD, outperforming all baselines on every reported metric. Inference runs at ~45 fps per driving expression on a single GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlexAvatar (feed-forward) outperforms all four open-source baselines on every metric in both self- and cross-reenactment. The largest margin is against LAM, which trails by ~3.3 dB in PSNR and 0.028 in LPIPS for self-reenactment, reflecting LAM's reliance on pure FLAME-driven deformation without a learned dynamic decoder. Against the closest competitor Portrait4D-v2, FlexAvatar gains 1.5 dB PSNR and 0.017 LPIPS, with the gap widening to 3.0 dB after refinement. GAGAvatar, the strongest baseline in identity preservation (CSIM 0.8474), is still surpassed by FlexAvatar's feed-forward output (0.8490) and further by the refined model (0.8532). In cross-identity reenactment, FlexAvatar reduces AKD from 8.06 (best baseline) to 7.87 (feed-forward) and 7.24 (refined), indicating more accurate expression transfer. Qualitative comparisons with the non-open-source Avat3r and HeadGAP (provided in the appendix) show FlexAvatar producing fewer side-view artifacts and more realistic wrinkles and teeth details.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlexAvatar sits at the intersection of large reconstruction models (LRM, LAM, Avat3r) and 3D Gaussian Splatting-based head avatars, but departs from them by removing three constraints simultaneously: fixed input count, known camera poses, and expression labels. Where prior feed-forward avatar methods (LAM, Avat3r) are limited to single images or rely on cross-attention for expression driving, FlexAvatar introduces structured Head Query tokens for pose/expression-free aggregation and a lightweight UNet decoder conditioned on FLAME UV position maps for real-time dynamic deformation. The data distribution adjustment strategy for rebalancing rare expressions (wrinkles, bared teeth) is a training-level contribution not seen in prior Gaussian avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: real-time 360° animatable avatars from as few as one image, without any pose or expression annotation, lowers the barrier for casual users and scalable deployment. The 10-second refinement step offers a useful middle ground between zero-shot quality and per-subject optimization. Key open questions remain around relightability, full-body extension, and handling rare accessories (glasses, hats) that the current training distribution underrepresents. The authors note that scaling with more 3D data in a two-stage scheme could further close the gap to per-subject methods.
