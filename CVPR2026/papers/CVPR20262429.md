## Problem

Existing dynamic NeRF methods parameterize time as discrete frames or learn sequence-specific deformation fields, which restricts them to interpolation within observed timestamps and prevents generalization to unseen initial conditions. No prior approach provides a principled mechanism for long-range temporal extrapolation or for learning a shared continuous dynamic model across multiple sequences governed by the same underlying physics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed multi-view RGB image sequences (no optical flow, depth, or 3D ground truth required) and, in the multi-sequence generalization setting, the initial pose and velocity of the dynamic object as positional encodings. It produces rendered novel views at arbitrary continuous timestamps (not just discrete frames), extrapolated future frames beyond the training horizon, a view-agnostic latent state z_t at each queried time, and auxiliary object pose and velocity estimates. The ODE solver enables querying at any real-valued time point, supporting both fine-grained interpolation and long-horizon extrapolation at constant memory cost.

## Method

Node-RF represents a dynamic scene as a latent vector z_t that evolves according to a learned Neural ODE (dh/dt = f_θ(h,t)), which is decoded by a NeRF renderer F_Θ(x, d, z_t) → (c, σ) to produce geometry and appearance. In single-sequence mode, a Latent-ODE (ODE-RNN VAE) learns the initial hidden state, after which the nODE and NeRF are jointly trained end-to-end with a photometric reconstruction loss. In multi-sequence mode, a canonical latent z_can is propagated through the nODE conditioned on initial pose and velocity (via positional encoding + MLP), and the resulting dynamic latent is added to a separately learned static background latent before NeRF rendering; auxiliary pose and velocity decoders provide L1 supervision. A Lipschitz weight-normalization regularizer on the NeRF linear layers encourages a structured latent space that reveals dynamical-system features such as bifurcation points and stable sinks. The entire pipeline is trained solely from visual observations, with the ODE solver (dopri5 or Euler) providing continuous-time state propagation that enables both long-range extrapolation and generalization to novel initial conditions.

## Results

On the Bouncing Balls 4× long-term extrapolation task, Node-RF achieves the best scores across all four metrics: X-CLIP similarity 0.1775, LLaVA-Video similarity 0.7937, VBench Motion Smoothness 0.99648, and Subject Consistency 0.97775. On the Pendulum dataset, Node-RF outperforms all baselines in both interpolation (PSNR 17.057, SSIM 0.531, LPIPS 0.0234) and extrapolation (PSNR 15.920, SSIM 0.469, LPIPS 0.0257). For multi-sequence generalization, Node-RF achieves IoU 0.3327 on Oscillating Ball and 0.485 on Bifurcating Hill, substantially exceeding D-NeRF(c) (0.0008 and 0.003) and SimVP (0.295 on Bifurcating Hill).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Node-RF outperforms all five dynamic NeRF/Gaussian baselines (D-NeRF, 4D-GS, HexPlane, TiNeuVox, Motion-GS) on the Bouncing Balls 4× extrapolation task, achieving the highest X-CLIP (0.1775 vs. 0.1773 for TiNeuVox), LLaVA-Video (0.7937 vs. 0.7883 for TiNeuVox), Motion Smoothness (0.99648 vs. 0.99617 for HexPlane), and Subject Consistency (0.97775 vs. 0.97562 for Motion-GS). On the Pendulum dataset, Node-RF surpasses D-NeRF (PSNR 17.057 vs. 13.906 interpolation) and 4D-GS (PSNR 17.057 vs. 13.391) by a wide margin, and edges out SimVP on extrapolation PSNR (15.920 vs. 15.804) while also supporting continuous-time interpolation that SimVP cannot. For multi-sequence generalization, Node-RF dramatically outperforms D-NeRF(c) on Oscillating Ball (IoU 0.3327 vs. 0.0008) and Bifurcating Hill (IoU 0.485 vs. 0.003), and beats SimVP on Bifurcating Hill (0.485 vs. 0.295) and Vid-ODE (0.485 vs. 0.000), demonstrating that the ODE-based continuous dynamics enable generalization to unseen initial conditions where frame-based and sequence-specific methods fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

Node-RF sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: dynamic NeRFs (D-NeRF, Nerfies, DyNeRF) that learn 4D scene representations, and Neural ODEs (Chen et al., Latent ODEs, VidODE) that model continuous-time dynamics. The key departure is from existing dynamic NeRFs, which parameterize time discretely or use sequence-specific deformation fields, by instead learning a shared continuous-time ODE in latent space that governs scene evolution. Unlike DONE, which uses a two-stage mesh-based pipeline with nODE, Node-RF integrates the ODE directly within a NeRF inverse-rendering loop, and unlike VidODE which is limited to 2D temporal modeling, it extends to full 3D volumetric scene representation. The Lipschitz regularization from Liu et al. is repurposed to structure the latent space for interpretability of dynamical behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental limitation of current dynamic scene representations: the inability to extrapolate beyond observed time frames or generalize to unseen initial conditions. The latent space analysis revealing bifurcation points and divergence sinks is a promising step toward interpretable dynamical system analysis from visual data, potentially enabling uncertainty estimation for ambiguous extrapolations. However, the authors explicitly frame results as 'conceptual' and 'early validation,' with all multi-sequence experiments on simple synthetic scenes (a ball in a bowl, a ball on a hill) and no real-world multi-sequence generalization demonstrated. Open questions include whether the approach scales to complex real-world dynamics, how it handles non-deterministic or stochastic scenes, and whether the continuous-time formulation provides meaningful advantages over well-tuned discrete alternatives at scale.
