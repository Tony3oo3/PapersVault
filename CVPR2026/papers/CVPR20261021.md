## Problem

Large-scale annotated datasets for detection, segmentation, and grounding are expensive to build, biased in category coverage, and difficult to scale, while existing synthetic data pipelines (simulation rendering, model-annotated images, copy-paste) each sacrifice either annotation accuracy, compositional diversity, or open-vocabulary coverage. No prior method simultaneously provides pixel-accurate masks, realistic multi-object compositions, and 40K+ category coverage at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261021/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes 46K+ object category names as prompts (fed to Qwen2.5-32B for description generation, then FLUX-1-dev for single-object image synthesis on white backgrounds) plus real background images for compositing. It produces 20M individual object segments and 2M composed images, each carrying pixel-accurate instance masks, bounding boxes, class labels, and 9+ dense referring expressions (attribute-based, spatial-based, and mixed) generated by QwQ-32B from ground-truth geometry. Because annotations are derived directly from the composition geometry rather than post-hoc labeling, they are inherently consistent with the image content.

## Method

SOC employs an object-centric composition strategy with three key mechanisms. First, 3D geometric layout augmentation models each composite as a 3D scene where object depth, position, and physical size are sampled independently of category (p(d,X,Y|c)=p(d,X,Y)), using perspective projection with category-specific size ranges to break spurious pictorial correlations; 5–20 segments per image are placed with occlusion constraints (IoU≥0.9 triggers resampling). Second, generative harmonization applies IC-Light diffusion-based inpainting and global relighting, followed by mask-area-weighted blending where smaller objects receive higher re-blend weights to preserve fine details and color fidelity, plus a soft-matting step for boundary smoothing. Third, camera configuration augmentation applies random zoom (scaling factor 1.0–4.0 with random cropping) and depth-of-field blur computed via the circle-of-confusion formula with sampled f-numbers (1.4–16), further decorrelating appearance from semantics. This design yields high-integrity annotations by construction while producing photorealistic, compositionally diverse images that outperform both real and prior synthetic data pipelines.

## Results

On open-vocabulary detection (LVIS), just 50K SOC images boost AP from 20.1 to 29.8 (+9.7) and rare-class AP from 10.1 to 23.5 (+13.4), surpassing 20M GRIT (+7.0) and matching 200K V3Det (+10.5). Scaling to 400K SOC images reaches 31.4 AP on LVIS and 22.8 AP on OdinW-35. On visual grounding, 100K SOC images raise gRefCOCO no-target accuracy from 89.3 to 97.7 (+8.4) and DoD FULL mAP from 15.6 to 19.4 (+3.8). On COCO instance segmentation (Mask2Former, 10K data from scratch), SOC achieves 12.79 AP vs. 9.32 (Copy-Paste), 9.41 (X-Paste), and 9.73 (SegGen).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRIT (20M model-annotated examples), SOC-50K achieves higher LVIS AP (29.8 vs. 27.1) and substantially higher rare-class AP (23.5 vs. 17.3) with 400× fewer examples. Against V3Det (200K human-annotated), SOC-50K matches overall LVIS AP (29.8 vs. 30.6) while exceeding it on rare-class AP (23.5 vs. 21.5). Against copy-paste baselines, SOC achieves 12.79 AP on COCO instance segmentation, outperforming Copy-Paste (9.32, +36.1%) and X-Paste (9.41, +36.0%), and on LVIS-Mini detection SOC reaches 38.6 AP vs. 35.2 (Copy-Paste) and 37.2 (X-Paste). Against diffusion-based pipelines, SOC surpasses SegGen (9.73 COCO Seg, 36.8 LVIS-Mini) and SynGround (40.1/89.2 gRefCOCO VG) with 12.79/38.6 and 41.2/93.9 respectively. Crucially, SOC is complementary to real datasets: adding 100K SOC on top of GRIT+V3Det still yields +1.3 LVIS AP and +6.2 rare-class AP, confirming that SOC introduces vocabulary and compositional diversity absent from existing corpora.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOC departs from both dominant synthetic data paradigms: simulation-based rendering (SynScapes, Virtual KITTI, Hypersim), which offers ground-truth accuracy but is limited to rigid domains with scarce 3D assets, and model-annotated pipelines (GRIT, SynGround, SegGen, MaskFactory), which offer scene diversity but inherit annotation noise from labeling models. Instead, SOC adopts an object-centric composition strategy—generating individual segments with FLUX-1-dev, placing them via category-independent 3D geometric layout augmentation, and harmonizing with IC-Light plus mask-area-weighted blending—thereby combining the annotation integrity of simulation with the compositional flexibility of model-annotated approaches. The 3D layout design explicitly breaks spurious category-to-position correlations identified in prior work on shortcut learning, and the blending step addresses a known failure mode of naive copy-paste (edge artifacts) that prior methods like X-Paste and SegGen do not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOC is the first large-scale synthetic dataset to consistently surpass real annotated datasets (V3Det, GRIT) across detection, segmentation, and grounding with multiple model architectures, and it delivers the largest gains in low-data regimes (+6.59 AP at 1% COCO), making it practically valuable for annotation-budget-constrained settings. The intra-class referring benchmark exposes a diagnostic weakness in current grounding models (relying on category rather than attribute discrimination) that existing benchmarks mask. Open questions include whether the simplified 3D layout (no physics, no inter-object interaction) limits realism for complex scenes, whether FLUX's generation quality becomes a bottleneck at scale, and whether the synthetic-ness of compositions becomes detectable and exploitable by downstream models at very large training scales.
