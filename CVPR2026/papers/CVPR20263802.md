## Problem

Self-supervised 3D occupancy prediction methods require hundreds of GPU hours of training and produce fixed-resolution, fixed-vocabulary models that cannot adapt to finer voxel grids or novel object categories without extensive retraining. The paper asks whether, given that vision foundation models now provide reliable multiview geometry and open-vocabulary semantics directly at test time, training a dedicated occupancy network is still necessary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263802/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw sensor streams at each timestep: six surrounding RGB camera images and, for the LiDAR variant, a sparse LiDAR point cloud. It produces a 3D occupancy grid with per-voxel semantic probability vectors at any user-specified voxel resolution (e.g., 0.2 m or 0.4 m) and spatial extent, with the semantic label set determined by free-text prompts to the open-vocabulary segmentation VFM rather than a fixed decoder head. The intermediate scene representation is a set of time-aware 3D Gaussians that persist and accumulate across frames.

## Method

TT-Occ follows a three-stage 'lift–track–voxelize' pipeline that operates entirely at test time with no network training. In the lift stage, geometry is obtained from a 3D VFM (VGGT or MapAnything for depth/correspondence; direct LiDAR points for the LiDAR variant) and semantics from an open-vocabulary segmentation model (REX-Omni, OpenSeed, or GroundingSAM2), both projected into 3D Gaussians whose parameters (position, covariance, opacity, color, semantic probability) are refined online via differentiable rasterization. In the track stage, dynamic Gaussians are separated from static ones using motion cues (ICP-based scene flow for LiDAR; RAFT optical flow minus ego-motion for camera) to prevent trailing artifacts, while static Gaussians accumulate across frames for temporal completeness. In the voxelize stage, the accumulated Gaussians are scattered onto a discrete grid with covariance-aware spatial weighting (Eq. 6), enabling arbitrary-resolution output; an optional Trilateral Radial Basis Function kernel periodically smooths Gaussian parameters using joint spatial, radiometric, and semantic affinities to suppress VFM noise.

## Results

On Occ3D-nuScenes (0.4 m resolution), TT-OccLiDAR achieves an mIoU of 27.41 without any pretraining, surpassing the supervised sparse-GT method RenderOcc (23.93) and approaching the dense-GT upper bound CTF-Occ (28.53). The camera-only variant TT-OccCamera reaches 16.70 mIoU, exceeding the best self-supervised LiDAR-assisted method VEON-LiDAR (15.14) and the prior self-supervised SOTA SelfOcc (10.54) by 6.2 and 11.6 points respectively. Under a controlled comparison with the same OpenSeed VFM (Table 3), TT-OccLiDAR improves RayIoU@4 by 115% over SelfOcc (25.8 vs. 12.0), and TT-OccCamera improves it by 30.8% (15.7 vs. 12.0). On the higher-resolution nuCraft benchmark (0.2 m), both variants consistently and significantly outperform the adapted SelfOcc baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TT-OccLiDAR (27.41 mIoU) outperforms all self-supervised baselines by a wide margin, beating the previous SOTA SelfOcc (10.54) by 16.9 points and VEON-LiDAR (15.14) by 12.3 points, and even surpasses the supervised sparse-GT method RenderOcc (23.93) by 3.5 points. TT-OccCamera (16.70 mIoU) exceeds every camera-only self-supervised method, most notably outperforming GaussianTR (11.70), LangOcc (11.84), and SelfOcc (10.54) by 5–6 points. Against the dense-GT supervised upper bounds, TT-OccLiDAR trails CTF-Occ (28.53) by only 1.1 points and BEVFormer (26.88) by a margin of 0.5 points, while TT-OccCamera remains below OccFlowNet (26.14) and RenderOcc (23.93) but does so without any training. In the controlled same-VFM comparison (Table 3), both variants improve RayIoU@4 substantially over SelfOcc (115% for LiDAR, 30.8% for camera), confirming gains in geometric accuracy beyond mIoU. On nuCraft at 0.2 m resolution, TT-Occ consistently outperforms the adapted SelfOcc baseline, demonstrating resolution-agnostic generalization that requires no retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

TT-Occ repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting from its traditional role in offline, per-scene photorealistic reconstruction (DrivingGaussian, Street Gaussians, OmniRe) into an online, test-time occupancy representation that requires no external priors like HD maps or bounding boxes. It departs from the self-supervised occupancy line (SelfOcc, OccNeRF, GaussianOcc, GaussianTR) by eliminating the trained occupancy decoder entirely, instead integrating off-the-shelf vision foundation models (VGGT, MapAnything for geometry; REX-Omni, OpenSeed for semantics) at inference time. The 'lift-track-voxelize' pipeline is a novel composition of these components with temporal Gaussian tracking and a trilateral smoothing kernel, but each individual ingredient (3DGS, optical flow, ICP, open-vocabulary segmentation) is well-established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that 3D occupancy prediction necessitates a dedicated, extensively trained network, showing that a modular test-time pipeline over powerful VFMs can match or exceed trained self-supervised methods while offering arbitrary resolution and open-vocabulary flexibility. Key limitations include substantial inference cost (dominated by semantic segmentation and, for the camera variant, dense optical flow), a significant accuracy gap between the LiDAR and camera variants, and reliance on the continued availability and improvement of specific VFM backends. Open questions include scalability to longer driving sequences, robustness in highly dynamic or adverse-weather scenes, and whether the approach generalizes beyond structured driving environments to unstructured embodied-agent settings.
