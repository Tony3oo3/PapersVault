## Problem

Existing information-bottleneck-based multimodal intent recognition models suffer from two structural limitations: they use a fixed compression dimension that cannot adapt to input-specific redundancy and noise levels, and they handle de-redundancy and denoising jointly in a single compression step, ignoring that redundancy (inter-modal overlap) and noise (intrinsic distortion) have fundamentally different origins and require different treatment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261618/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three modalities—text (BERT-encoded), visual/video (Faster R-CNN features), and audio/speech (wav2vec2 features)—all projected to a unified 768-dimensional space. The IDAB module dynamically adjusts its compression dimension between 64 and 768 per sample based on an input-specific influence factor, rather than using a fixed bottleneck. The output is a discrete intent class label (20 categories on MIntRec, 12 on MELD-DA) or a continuous sentiment score in [-1, +1] on CH-SIMS.

## Method

The core contribution is the IDAB module, which performs input-adaptive IB compression by (1) pretraining a linear encoder-decoder, (2) computing first-order Taylor saliency scores to rank all parameters by importance, and (3) given an influence factor α, dynamically determining the compression dimension D^c via a tempered normalization → learnable monotone SiLU projection → nonlinear power-law scaling, then retaining only the top-D^c most important parameters for compression and reconstruction. Built on IDAB, SeD-UD hierarchically decouples purification: it first applies parallel IDAB operations to each unimodal feature for de-redundancy (driven by a redundancy degree r computed via cross-modal softmax attention), then fuses the de-redundant features through text-guided modality modulation and H-head cross-attention, and finally applies a second IDAB operation for denoising on the fused feature (driven by a noise intensity γ). Training uses information distillation supervision—KL-divergence losses aligning pre- and post-IDAB predictions—alongside a cross-entropy loss on the final fused feature, with modality-specific weights that prioritize text.

## Results

On MIntRec, SeD-UD achieves 73.81% ACC, 73.55% wF1, 73.96% wP, and 71.88% Recall, with the best wP and Recall among all methods. On MELD-DA, SeD-UD attains the best performance on all four metrics: 63.72% ACC, 62.44% wF1, 62.67% wP, and 52.83% Recall. On CH-SIMS (sentiment analysis), SeD-UD achieves the best ACC-2 (82.43%) and F1 (82.11%), with MAE of 0.415 tied for best. Inference time is 21.8 ms per sample, approximately 15% faster than the closest baselines (TCL-MAP at 25.8 ms, SDIF-DA at 25.4 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MIntRec, SeD-UD achieves the best wP (73.96%) and Recall (71.88%), but trails SDIF-DA by 0.09% on ACC (73.81 vs 73.90) and 0.38% on wF1 (73.55 vs 73.93), which the authors attribute to SDIF-DA's use of ChatGPT-based data augmentation. On MELD-DA, SeD-UD dominates all baselines on every metric, outperforming the strongest IB-based competitor DIB by 1.00% ACC (63.72 vs 62.72) and 1.38% wF1 (62.44 vs 61.06), and beating the best non-IB method TCL-MAP by 1.97% ACC. Among IB-based methods specifically, SeD-UD consistently surpasses both InMu-Net and DIB across all four metrics on both MIR datasets. On CH-SIMS, SeD-UD achieves the best ACC-2 (82.43%) and F1 (82.11%), ties MUG for best MAE (0.415), but falls short of DIB on correlation (0.608 vs 0.625). Inference speed favors SeD-UD at 21.8 ms/sample, roughly 15% faster than TCL-MAP (25.8 ms), SDIF-DA (25.4 ms), and InMu-Net (25.5 ms), despite performing more IB operations due to adaptive dimension selection avoiding unnecessary computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the information bottleneck (IB) framework for multimodal learning, building directly on Tishby et al.'s IB objective and the encoder-decoder IB formulation adopted by InMu-Net for multimodal intent detection. It departs from prior IB-based multimodal methods (InMu-Net, DIB, MIB, DMIB) along two axes: replacing the fixed compression dimension with an input-adaptive mechanism (IDAB) that borrows saliency-based pruning criteria (SNIP, GraSP) for parameter selection, and decoupling the joint redundancy-noise compression into a hierarchical pipeline of separated de-redundancy followed by unified denoising. The modality-modulation fusion strategy also draws on MAG-BERT's gating mechanism and MuIT's cross-modality attention, positioning the work at the intersection of IB theory, model compression, and multimodal fusion design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle—performing de-redundancy before fusion and denoising after fusion—offers a reusable design heuristic for IB-based multimodal pipelines beyond intent recognition. The IDAB module provides a practical, lightweight mechanism for input-adaptive capacity allocation that could transfer to other IB applications (medical imaging, sentiment analysis). However, the theoretical contribution (Theorem 1) is relatively thin, the authors explicitly note IDAB is 'not a strict MI-optimal solver,' and evaluation is limited to three datasets with modest margins over the strongest baselines. Open questions include whether the decoupling hierarchy generalizes to more than three modalities, whether the saliency-based parameter selection remains effective at larger scales, and whether the adaptive dimension mechanism can be made differentiable for end-to-end optimization.
