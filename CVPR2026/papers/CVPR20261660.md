## Problem

Existing prompt-based MIL methods for few-shot WSI classification rely on static, universal pathological descriptions applied identically to every slide, ignoring the substantial morphological heterogeneity within and across individual WSIs. This one-size-fits-all knowledge injection fails to tailor semantic guidance to each slide's distinctive visual evidence, ultimately constraining diagnostic precision in data-scarce settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261660/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes WSIs preprocessed into 448×448 patches at 20× magnification, with features extracted by the TITAN (CONCH v1.5) vision encoder, alongside an offline-constructed pathology concept pool (K-means-refined from Quilt-1M) and LLM-generated class-level text prompts. It produces a slide-level class label (binary or multi-class) and, for interpretability, attention heatmaps localizing diagnostically relevant regions along with per-cluster pathological concept annotations. All experiments operate under 4-, 8-, and 16-shot per-class training budgets with 4-fold cross-validation.

## Method

DyKo's core is the WSI-Adaptive Knowledge Instantiation (WAKI) module, which performs on-the-fly K-means clustering of a WSI's patch features to obtain M slide-specific visual prototypes, uses these prototypes as queries to retrieve Top-K relevant concepts from a pre-built knowledge base via cosine similarity, and synthesizes per-instance knowledge-instantiated features through temperature-scaled attention over the retrieved concepts. A Dual-Stream Cross-Attention (DSCA) module then uses class-level prompts as queries to attend to both the original visual features and the knowledge-instantiated features in parallel, fusing the two context-aware representations by element-wise addition before a linear classifier. To prevent semantic drift where instantiated features detach from visual evidence, a Structural Consistency loss minimizes KL divergence between probability distributions produced by dual parallel cluster heads operating on visual and semantic features respectively. The full model is trained end-to-end with a composite objective of cross-entropy plus a weighted SC loss, leveraging the TITAN foundation model as the shared vision-language backbone.

## Results

DyKo achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four datasets (CAMELYON16, NSCLC, UBC-OCEAN, TCGA-RCC) under 4-, 8-, and 16-shot settings. On CAMELYON16, DyKo reaches AUC 0.871 (4-shot), 0.956 (8-shot), and 0.961 (16-shot), with corresponding F1 scores of 0.827, 0.923, and 0.943. On the multi-class UBC-OCEAN dataset (5 histotypes), DyKo attains F1 0.806 in the 16-shot setting. The advantage is most pronounced in the most data-scarce 4-shot regime, where DyKo leads the next-best prompt-based method (FOCUS) by 4.0% AUC on CAMELYON16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyKo outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The gap is largest against purely visual MIL methods: ABMIL (AUC 0.746) and TransMIL (0.718) trail DyKo by over 12% AUC in the 4-shot CAMELYON16 setting, confirming that visual-only aggregation is insufficient under extreme data scarcity. Among prompt-based VLM methods, FOCUS is the strongest competitor, yet DyKo still leads by 4.0% AUC (0.871 vs. 0.831) in 4-shot, 3.8% in 8-shot (0.956 vs. 0.918), and 2.7% in 16-shot (0.961 vs. 0.934) on CAMELYON16. On the 5-class UBC-OCEAN task, DyKo's 16-shot F1 of 0.806 exceeds the best baseline by 3.1 percentage points. The consistent margin across all settings—narrowing slightly as more training data becomes available—indicates that WSI-adaptive knowledge instantiation provides the greatest benefit precisely when labeled examples are most scarce.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt-based MIL for pathology (TOP, ViLa-MIL, FOCUS, ConcepPath) and knowledge-retrieval approaches (TQx, ConcepPath), but departs from them by replacing static, dataset-wide concept pools with per-WSI dynamic retrieval conditioned on slide-specific visual prototypes. The WAKI module repurposes K-means clustering (previously used in CLAM and MiCo for intra-class diversity) as a mechanism for adaptive knowledge querying, while the Dual-Stream Cross-Attention fusion extends the attention-based aggregation paradigm of ABMIL and TransMIL to jointly process visual and knowledge-instantiated streams. The Structural Consistency loss is a novel regularization not present in prior prompt-based or knowledge-guided MIL methods, drawing on the intuition that semantic and visual feature spaces should maintain topological alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from universal to WSI-specific knowledge guidance addresses a practical limitation in deploying VLM-based pathology tools where morphological heterogeneity across slides is substantial. The finding that the SC loss contributes more to performance than the WAKI module itself in the most data-scarce (4-shot) setting suggests that preventing semantic drift is at least as important as the knowledge enrichment mechanism, a nuance that could inform future design of knowledge-guided models. Open questions include whether the per-WSI K-means clustering scales efficiently to very large cohorts, whether the approach generalizes to tasks beyond classification (e.g., survival prediction), and whether the reliance on a single foundation model (TITAN) limits portability across different pathology VLMs.
