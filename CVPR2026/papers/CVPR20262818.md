## Problem

Prevailing identity-preserving video generation methods enforce a single facial image as the sole identity reference, which constitutes an ill-posed problem: a 2D snapshot cannot capture the 3D facial structure and dynamic expression patterns that define a person's identity, leading to severe identity drift under novel angles or expressions. Additionally, this single-reference assumption restricts creative flexibility, as real users possess diverse collections of photos and videos rather than one canonical portrait.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262818/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a set of N (1–5) heterogeneous visual references—face crops, portrait images, or short video clips—each padded to a uniform 1280×704 resolution while preserving aspect ratio, along with a differential text prompt that specifies only the changes relative to a designated primary reference across seven predefined elements (shot type, hairstyle, clothes, accessory, expression, action, background). The output is a 121-frame, 1280×704 (5-second 720p) video that preserves the person's identity while applying only the specified modifications, leaving all unmentioned attributes anchored to the primary reference.

## Method

AnyID builds on the Wan-5B DiT backbone with Rectified Flow and introduces a scalable omni-referenced architecture that encodes all heterogeneous references through the original VAE encoder (rather than external face or CLIP encoders), concatenating their latents along the temporal dimension and assigning them a static timestep of t=0 so the DiT can distinguish noise-free references from the noisy target latent. A primary-referenced generation paradigm designates one reference as the canonical anchor for all static attributes, paired with a differential prompt generated by a two-stage VLM+LLM pipeline that prunes unchanged elements via semantic similarity scoring. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised LoRA fine-tuning on 100k curated ID-groups from PortraitGala with augmented multi-format references, followed by DPO-based reinforcement learning on 1,000 human-annotated preference pairs requiring Pareto dominance on both identity fidelity and prompt controllability.

## Results

AnyID achieves the highest identity fidelity scores of 73.22% (Holi-Arc) and 67.52% (Holi-Cur), outperforming the best baseline (Phantom at 70.01% and 64.01%) by 3.2 and 3.5 points respectively. On prompt controllability, AnyID leads with 86.56% (Appearance), 60.31% (Motion), and 84.79% (Background), surpassing the strongest competitor by 13.4, 1.6, and 1.7 points respectively. For visual quality, AnyID scores 82.03% (Static) and 91.12% (Dynamic), the highest across all methods. In a user study with 20 participants, AnyID wins over 70% of matchups in identity fidelity and prompt controllability, and over 60% in element consistency and visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnyID outperforms all four baselines on 7 out of 9 metrics, with the largest margins on prompt controllability (Appearance: +13.4 over FantasyID; Motion: +1.6 over SkyReels-A2) and dynamic visual quality (+7.6 over Phantom). The only metrics where AnyID trails are Ele-CLIP (68.78 vs. SkyReels-A2's 78.14) and Ele-DINO (69.50 vs. 79.87), which the authors attribute to SkyReels-A2's 'copy-paste' artifact that inflates element similarity scores without genuine generation quality. Among the 5B-parameter baselines (ConsisID, FantasyID), AnyID leads by 3.2–3.5 points on identity fidelity and 13–26 points on appearance controllability. Against the 14B-parameter baselines (Phantom, SkyReels-A2), AnyID still wins on identity fidelity and all prompt controllability metrics despite being roughly 3× smaller, suggesting the multi-reference architecture and differential prompting contribute more to performance than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-reference identity-preserving video generation line (ConsisID, FantasyID, Phantom, SkyReels-A2) by replacing the dedicated face/CLIP encoder paradigm with a unified VAE-based omni-referenced architecture that accepts heterogeneous inputs (faces, portraits, video clips). It builds on the Rectified Flow + DiT formulation of Wan-5B and introduces a primary-referenced generation paradigm with differential prompting for attribute-level control, departing from conventional absolute scene descriptions. The RL fine-tuning stage extends Diffusion-DPO with a two-axis (identity fidelity + prompt controllability) Pareto-dominance filtering protocol for preference data construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine limitation in the field: single 2D references are underdetermined for 3D identity modeling, and the multi-reference formulation is a natural and practical solution that aligns with how users actually have their photos and videos. The differential prompt concept offers a clean mechanism for specifying what should change versus what should remain invariant, which is broadly applicable beyond identity preservation. However, the RL stage contributes only ~0.6 points on identity metrics with just 1,000 preference pairs, raising questions about its cost-effectiveness; the evaluation is limited to 50 celebrities and 5-second 720p clips, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to longer sequences, diverse demographics, and real-time deployment.
