## Problem

Existing 3D layout generation methods either treat layouts as structured language (LLM-based approaches that produce physically implausible collisions and floating objects) or rely on 2D visual supervision with iterative optimization (computationally expensive and lacking fundamental 3D spatial understanding). The paper asks whether layout generation can be learned directly from the geometric distributions encoded in pretrained 3D generative models, operating natively in 3D space rather than in bounding-box or image representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260614/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive step, the model receives the current scene state as a structured voxel-occupancy latent (H×W×L grid), a target 3D object mesh encoded into the same latent space, and a text instruction encoded by a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B model. The output is an updated scene latent from which surface points are extracted via the VAE decoder, and the original object mesh is registered to these points via ICP to recover rotation, scale, and translation. The process is sequential: each step's output scene becomes the next step's input, and the final high-fidelity scene is assembled by placing all fitted objects into the generated layout.

## Method

LaviGen repurposes the TRELLIS structured 3D latent diffusion model (retaining only the voxel-occupancy structure stage) as an autoregressive layout generator. The key architectural adaptation concatenates the scene latent, object latent, and noisy target latent into a unified token sequence processed by a 3B-parameter DiT with flow matching, while an identity-aware extension of RoPE (adding a source-identity flag to spatial coordinates) lets the model distinguish scene tokens from object tokens. To mitigate exposure bias in long autoregressive sequences, the paper introduces dual-guidance self-rollout distillation: the student model rolls out layouts from its own imperfect predictions during training, supervised simultaneously by a holistic teacher (the bidirectional base model scoring the final scene) and a step-wise teacher (the causal autoregressive model scoring each individual placement), combining scene-level and object-level corrective signals in a single distribution-matching objective.

## Results

On the LayoutVLM benchmark (8–10 object scenes), LaviGen achieves a Collision-Free score of 97.3 and In-Boundary score of 98.6, substantially outperforming all baselines on physical plausibility. It also attains the highest Physically-Grounded Semantic Alignment (PSA) score of 78.8, compared to 58.8 for the next-best method (LayoutVLM). Inference time is 24.3 s, roughly 65% faster than LayoutVLM (75.5 s) and 7.4× faster than I-Design (179.2 s), while remaining competitive with LayoutGPT (21.3 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LaviGen decisively outperforms all four baselines on physical plausibility: its CF score of 97.3 exceeds LayoutGPT (83.8), Holodeck (77.8), I-Design (76.8), and LayoutVLM (81.8), and its IB score of 98.6 is the highest overall. Against LayoutGPT, which leads on positional coherence (80.8 vs. 76.9), LaviGen trades a small semantic gap for a massive physical advantage (CF +13.5, IB +74.4) and a comparable inference time. Against LayoutVLM, the strongest prior method, LaviGen improves CF by 15.5 points, IB by 3.7 points, and PSA by 20 points, while running 3× faster (24.3 s vs. 75.5 s). Holodeck and I-Design are clearly dominated: Holodeck suffers from severe boundary violations (IB 8.1) and I-Design incurs 7.4× the inference cost of LaviGen with inferior scores on every metric. In the user study (43 participants), LaviGen is preferred for physical plausibility (52.1%) and overall quality (55.6%) over LayoutVLM (31.9% and 36.5%) and LayoutGPT (16.0% and 7.9%), while semantic consistency is comparable (33.5% vs. 38.8% for LayoutGPT).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaviGen repurposes the structured 3D latent diffusion architecture of TRELLIS (retaining only its structure-level voxel-occupancy stage) and adapts the self-rollout distillation mechanism from Self-Forcing (originally for autoregressive video) to the layout generation setting. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of treating layout as a language task (LayoutGPT, Holodeck, I-Design) or a vision-supervised optimization problem (LayoutVLM), instead formulating it as a native 3D generative process where geometric priors from large-scale 3D data directly encode spatial relationships. The identity-aware RoPE extension and dual-guidance (holistic + step-wise) distillation are novel architectural and training contributions that bridge the gap between single-object 3D generation and multi-object scene composition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating in native 3D space, LaviGen naturally supports layout completion and editing—tasks that are difficult or impossible for text-based or 2D-supervised methods—making it relevant for AR/VR, robotics, and interactive design pipelines. The 65% speed reduction over LayoutVLM (24.3s vs. 75.5s) is practically significant for iterative design workflows. However, evaluation is limited to 8–10 object indoor scenes on a single benchmark, semantic scoring relies on GPT-4o, and the ICP-based mesh fitting is a post-hoc alignment step that may not generalize to highly deformable or non-rigid objects. Open questions include scalability to larger scenes (dozens of objects), outdoor or non-furniture domains, and whether the autoregressive ordering strategy (learned vs. user-defined) can be made more robust.
