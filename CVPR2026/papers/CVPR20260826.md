## Problem

Current vision-language models remain weak at spatial grounding and metric geometry, causing VLA-based driving systems to produce unreliable localization, duplicate detections, and brittle decisions in long-tail scenarios. Existing approaches either frame spatial understanding as QA-style supervision (yielding only indirect, poorly calibrated localization) or abandon LLM backbones for diffusion decoders (losing reasoning capacity). This paper addresses the gap by embedding explicit, persistent 2D/3D world states directly within a single VLM and jointly optimizing perception with trajectory prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260826/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view high-resolution camera images (split into non-overlapping tiles via InternVL-style dynamic tiling to preserve long-range detail), optional LiDAR point clouds (encoded by a pretrained PointPillars encoder to initialize BEV tokens), and free-form text queries. Outputs are structured as discretized token sequences for detection (class, box coordinates, confidence) and as MLP-decoded waypoint trajectories; segmentation is produced via dot-product retrieval between grid tokens and 16 learned mask tokens. The PV branch uses a 10×10 grid while BEV detection uses a 40×40 grid, and the final detection score is the product of class softmax and predicted IoU confidence.

## Method

Percept-WAM initializes from InternVL2-8B and introduces two token families—World-PV (image-plane) and World-BEV (bird's-eye-view)—that encode metric coordinates with calibrated confidence, unifying 2D/3D perception in one backbone. A grid-conditioned prediction head structures dense multi-object inference via parallel autoregressive decoding, while an IoU-aware confidence objective (trained on a model-prediction-based tuning set rather than random perturbations) explicitly calibrates detection scores to reduce false positives. World-BEV tokens lift 2D evidence into 3D space through cross-attention, and can be initialized from LiDAR features when available. For trajectory prediction, four point-level query sets (Q_pv, Q_bev, Q_ego, Q_full) interact with modality-specific features under controlled attention masks and are decoded through a lightweight MLP, ensuring the action is aligned with all input modalities. Training follows a two-stage curriculum (spatial grounding then E2E VLA fine-tuning), and a streaming KV-cache mechanism with dual-recomputation reduces per-frame latency to 707 ms.

## Results

On PV perception, Percept-WAM (AD+Gen) achieves 51.7 mAP on COCO 2D detection, 49.6 mAP on nuImages 2D detection, 33.0 mAP / 38.6 NDS on nuScenes mono-3D detection, and 50.3 mIoU on COCOStuff semantic segmentation, matching or surpassing specialist detectors and segmenters. On BEV 3D detection (nuScenes), it reaches 0.589 mAP / 0.645 NDS, outperforming LiDAR-based PointPillars (0.523) and SECOND (0.526). For E2E trajectory planning, the two-stage Percept-WAM* attains 90.2 PDMS on NAVSIM (surpassing DiffusionDrive by 2.1) and 0.36 m average L2 error on nuScenes, with a streaming-inference latency of 707 ms.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Percept-WAM outperforms all VLM-based detection baselines on COCO 2D detection (51.7 mAP vs. LMM-Det 47.5, UFO 48.9, DINO 49.4) and nuImages (49.6 vs. Mask R-CNN 47.8). On mono-3D detection it edges out FCOS3D (33.0 vs. 32.1 mAP), and on instance segmentation it surpasses Mask R-CNN (41.7 vs. 38.6 mAP on nuImages) and UFO (45.9 vs. 42.6 on COCO). For BEV 3D detection, Percept-WAM (0.589 mAP) beats LiDAR-based PointPillars (0.523) and SECOND (0.526) but trails the multi-sensor BEVFusion (0.685); however, it surpasses BEVFusion on BEV map segmentation for drivable area (87.0 vs. 85.5 IoU) and pedestrian crossing (70.9 vs. 60.5 IoU). On E2E planning, Percept-WAM* achieves 90.2 PDMS on NAVSIM, exceeding DiffusionDrive (88.1), Hydra-MDP (86.5), and UniAD (83.4), while also attaining the lowest average L2 error (0.36 m) among all compared methods on nuScenes open-loop evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Percept-WAM builds directly on UFO's grid-token interpolation and segmentation-as-retrieval formulation, and on Pix2Seq's discretized token-level cross-entropy for detection, but departs from both by embedding these mechanisms inside a pretrained VLM (InternVL2-8B) rather than a standalone vision encoder. Conceptually, it positions itself against two dominant paradigms: QA-style spatial supervision (DriveVLM, DriveLM, EMMA), which the authors argue yields only indirect and poorly calibrated localization, and diffusion-decoder pipelines (DiffusionDrive, Diffe2E), which forgo LLM-level reasoning. The closest architectural relative is UniAD's planning-oriented full-stack learning, but Percept-WAM instantiates the perception-to-action pipeline within a single autoregressive VLM backbone with explicit World-PV/World-BEV token families, rather than a multi-module encoder-decoder stack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a single 8B VLM can simultaneously produce competitive 2D/3D perception (51.7 mAP COCO, 58.9 mAP nuScenes BEV) and strong closed-loop planning (90.2 PDMS NAVSIM) at 707 ms per frame, which is relevant for real-world deployment where separate perception and planning stacks introduce latency and error compounding. The IoU-aware confidence mechanism addresses a known failure mode of LLM-based detectors (overconfident duplicate boxes) with a principled, data-driven solution. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum (perception first, then E2E fine-tuning) is necessary or whether joint training from scratch would suffice, how the approach scales to higher-resolution multi-camera rigs beyond 448×796, and whether the authors' stated future direction of coupling perception accuracy with trajectory via offline/online RL can close the remaining gap to specialist planners on long-tail scenarios.
