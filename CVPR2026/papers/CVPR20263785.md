## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods adopt a pixel-aligned paradigm that generates spatially uniform, highly redundant Gaussian maps, making them impractical for resource-constrained downstream applications like SLAM, AR/VR, and robotics. The paper identifies two root causes: a distribution mismatch (rigid pixel/voxel grids vs. the content-aware non-uniform distribution that optimized 3DGS naturally produces) and a receptive field mismatch (global-context backbones paired with single-pixel prediction heads, contradicting the inherently local nature of 3DGS optimization).

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of multi-view posed RGB images (resized to 256×448 for DL3DV) and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input. A frozen backbone (adopted from DepthSplat) extracts 2D feature maps and depth maps; the method is backbone-agnostic. The output is a sparse 3D Gaussian Splatting scene whose density is controlled at inference time by a single temperature parameter τ, yielding anywhere from 10k to 150k Gaussians per scene. Each Gaussian primitive carries opacity, 3D scale, rotation quaternion, and spherical-harmonic color coefficients.

## Method

SparseSplat replaces the one-primitive-per-pixel paradigm with a three-stage pipeline. First, a frozen backbone produces per-view 2D features and depth maps. Second, an Adaptive Primitive Sampling stage computes Shannon entropy over local N×N grayscale windows to build an information-density map, normalizes it by log(L), scales by temperature τ, and performs per-pixel probabilistic sampling; selected pixels are back-projected into 3D via predicted depth to form sparse anchor points. Third, a 3D-Local Attribute Predictor queries K=20 nearest neighbors for each anchor via FAISS, concatenates projected geometric features (xyz, normals, rays) with backbone image features for the center and all neighbors, aggregates them with a geo-aware attention mechanism, and regresses all Gaussian attributes through a lightweight MLP. Training is end-to-end with RGB supervision only (MSE + LPIPS loss), with backbone weights frozen throughout.

## Results

On DL3DV, SparseSplat at 150k Gaussians achieves 24.20 PSNR / 0.817 SSIM / 0.168 LPIPS, matching the SOTA DepthSplat (24.17 PSNR) while using only 22% of its primitives (150k vs. 688k). At sparser settings, the 40k model reaches 22.65 PSNR in 111 ms and the 10k model reaches 21.29 PSNR in 105 ms—both faster than DepthSplat's 128 ms. On the unseen Replica dataset, SparseSplat-150k generalizes to 26.64 PSNR / 0.846 SSIM, surpassing DepthSplat (26.47 PSNR / 0.836 SSIM) while trailing slightly on LPIPS (0.180 vs. 0.175).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SOTA baseline DepthSplat (688k Gaussians), SparseSplat-150k matches its PSNR (24.20 vs. 24.17) and SSIM (0.817 vs. 0.816) with only 22% of the primitives, at the cost of higher LPIPS (0.168 vs. 0.152) and slower inference (398 ms vs. 128 ms due to KNN). At 40k and 10k Gaussians, SparseSplat is faster than DepthSplat (111 ms / 105 ms vs. 128 ms) while retaining 22.65 and 21.29 PSNR respectively. Against MVSplat (688k), SparseSplat-150k wins on all three metrics (24.20 vs. 22.95 PSNR). The post-processing baselines GGN (20.23 PSNR) and Long-LRM (20.92 PSNR) are substantially outperformed by SparseSplat at every sparsity level. AnySplat, the only other method supporting variable Gaussian counts, degrades severely at low counts (8.91 PSNR at 113k) compared to SparseSplat's graceful degradation (21.29 PSNR at 10k). On cross-dataset generalization (Replica), SparseSplat-150k edges out DepthSplat on PSNR (26.64 vs. 26.47) and SSIM (0.846 vs. 0.836) but is marginally worse on LPIPS (0.180 vs. 0.175).

## Relation to Prior Work

SparseSplat builds directly on the feed-forward 3DGS line of work (PixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat) but explicitly departs from their 'pixel-aligned' paradigm, which the authors diagnose as the root cause of redundant, spatially uniform Gaussian maps. It also positions itself against two partial remedies—voxel-based prediction (AnySplat, VolSplat) and post-hoc pruning (GGN, Long-LRM)—arguing that both still enforce a rigid, scene-agnostic structure. The 3D-Local Attribute Predictor draws on point cloud network literature (PointNet, DGCNN, Point Transformer) and adopts geo-aware attention for local neighborhood aggregation, while the entropy-based sampling repurposes a classical image-processing signal (Shannon entropy) as a sparsity control mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing Gaussian count to 22% of prior SOTA while matching rendering quality, SparseSplat makes feed-forward 3DGS viable for memory-constrained downstream applications (SLAM, AR/VR, robotics) where 688k-primitive maps are impractical. The temperature parameter τ offers a single, intuitive knob to trade map size against fidelity, which is practically valuable for deployment. However, the authors acknowledge that 3D KNN-based context aggregation degrades under severe depth errors (e.g., sky-boundary misprojections) and is computationally expensive, pointing toward 2D co-visibility-based aggregation as a more robust and efficient future direction. Whether the adaptive-sampling principle generalizes beyond the frozen DepthSplat backbone to other depth estimators or to unconstrained-view settings remains an open question.
