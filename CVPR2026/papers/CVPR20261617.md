## Problem

Training-free zero-shot composed image retrieval (ZS-CIR) methods that generate target captions via MLLMs suffer from two issues: (1) generated captions inherit extraneous visual details from the reference image, introducing noise that distracts from the core modification intent, and (2) point-to-point similarity between a single caption and a single image embedding fails to capture the diverse, multi-faceted compositions inherent in multimodal retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a free-form text modification instruction as input, and produces a ranked list of target images from a database. At inference, it queries Qwen2-VL-7B to generate K=5 diverse captions via top-k (k=50) and top-p (p=0.8) sampling, encodes them with CLIP, and augments each candidate target image M=10 times (random resized crop + horizontal flip) before computing the retrieval score. The entire pipeline is training-free, requiring only pretrained CLIP encoders and the MLLM, with a single hyperparameter α=0.45 controlling the transition strength.

## Method

STiTch introduces a three-module pipeline: (1) Querying generates K diverse target captions from an MLLM using stochastic sampling to form a discrete distribution P_t over the caption space; (2) Semantic Transition estimates a direction vector Δm̂ = f(m) via the CLIP text encoder on the text modification and updates each caption embedding as t̂_k = (1−α)t_k + αΔm̂, pushing the caption toward the target image while filtering out reference-image noise; (3) Set-to-set Alignment models each target image as a discrete distribution Q_y through M augmentations and computes a bidirectional conditional transport distance L_bi(P_t, Q_y) that sums forward (caption→image) and backward (image→caption) transport costs, where each direction uses a softmax-normalized transport plan weighted by cosine similarity. This replaces the standard point-to-point CLIP similarity with a distributional alignment that captures fine-grained cross-modal correspondences, all without any additional training.

## Results

On CIRCO (ViT-L/14), STiTch achieves 25.55 mAP@5 and 29.99 mAP@50, outperforming the previous best training-free method SEIZE (24.98 / 28.35) by +0.57 and +1.64 respectively. On CIRR (ViT-L/14), STiTch reaches 69.90 Recall@10 and 92.37 Recall_Subset@3, surpassing SEIZE (69.23 / 92.34) and OSrCIR (69.86 / 91.10). With the larger ViT-G/14 backbone, STiTch further extends its lead on CIRCO to 34.40 mAP@5 (+1.94 over SEIZE) and 40.02 mAP@50 (+2.47 over SEIZE). On GeneCIS, STiTch achieves the best average R@1 of 20.4 (ViT-G/14), winning 19 out of 24 sub-task settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STiTch outperforms the strongest training-free baseline SEIZE on CIRCO across all mAP thresholds (e.g., +1.94 mAP@5 with ViT-G/14) and on CIRR Recall@5/10 and Recall_Subset@2/3, though it slightly trails SEIZE on CIRR Recall@1 (28.87 vs. 28.65 with ViT-L/14; 39.23 vs. 38.87 with ViT-G/14) and Recall_Subset@1 with ViT-G/14 (73.56 vs. 74.15). Against OSrCIR, STiTch gains +2.03 mAP@5 and +3.43 mAP@50 on CIRCO (ViT-G/14), and +2.68 Recall_Subset@1 on CIRR (ViT-L/14). On FashionIQ, STiTch underperforms both SEIZE (39.12 vs. 43.05 avg R@10) and the training-dependent LinCIR (45.11 avg R@10), which the authors attribute to the simple, pure-background nature of fashion images limiting MLLM captioning. On GeneCIS, STiTch achieves the best average R@1 (20.4 with ViT-G/14) over SEIZE (19.8) and OSrCIR (19.6), winning 19 of 24 sub-task settings. Overall, STiTch is the top or second-best training-free method in the majority of settings across all four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

STiTch extends the training-free ZS-CIR paradigm established by CIReVL, OSrCIR, and SEIZE, which use MLLMs to generate target captions from a reference image and text modification. Its key departure is twofold: (1) it introduces a 'semantic transition' step that pushes generated captions toward the target image by adding a scaled CLIP text embedding of the modification, addressing the extraneous visual noise problem identified in prior LLM-based generators; and (2) it replaces point-to-point CLIP similarity with a bidirectional Conditional Transport (CT) distance between discrete distributions of captions and augmented images, building directly on the CT formulation of Zheng & Zhou (NeurIPS 2021). The method thus sits at the intersection of the MLLM-based generation pipeline and the optimal-transport alignment literature, repurposing CT from distribution comparison to cross-modal retrieval scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The transition vector is a remarkably simple yet effective mechanism—essentially a linear interpolation with the text modification embedding—that compensates for the semantic gap between generated captions and target images without any learned parameters. This suggests that the core bottleneck in LLM-based ZS-CIR may be less about generation quality and more about post-hoc refinement in embedding space. Limitations include underperformance on CIRR at k=1 (attributed to noisy annotations) and on FashionIQ, where simpler images favor text-inversion methods over MLLMs. Open questions include whether the fixed α trade-off can be made adaptive per query, whether the approach extends to video or 3D retrieval, and whether the discrete-distribution formulation scales efficiently to very large candidate sets.
