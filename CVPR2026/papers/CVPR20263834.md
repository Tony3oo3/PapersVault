## Problem

Existing Aerial-Ground Person Re-Identification methods follow a view-invariant paradigm that enforces excessive cross-view alignment, collapsing to part-level matching and suppressing view-specific discriminative cues (e.g., legs visible in ground view, shoulders in aerial view). Moreover, these methods rely predominantly on global representations, overlooking that fine-grained identity evidence resides in local patch regions that are often occluded or distorted under extreme viewpoint changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263834/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB pedestrian images from either aerial (drone) or ground (fixed camera) viewpoints, uniformly resized to 256×128 pixels, and processes them through a ViT backbone pretrained on ImageNet. It produces a single discriminative embedding vector formed by concatenating refined local features from both view-invariant and view-specific branches with the global [CLS] token, which is then used for cosine-similarity-based cross-view retrieval ranking. Training uses batches of 256 samples (64 identities × 4 instances) over 120 epochs with SGD and cosine annealing.

## Method

ViSA extends the View-Decoupled Transformer (VDT) into a dual-stream encoder that explicitly separates view-invariant identity semantics from view-specific appearance variations via independent learnable aerial/ground tokens. The Expert-driven Token Generation Module (ETGM) employs a mixture-of-experts mechanism with 8 experts per branch (view-invariant and view-specific), where a router selects top-2 experts per sample to generate adaptive semantic query tokens through cross-attention, self-attention, and FFN layers. The Dual-branch Local Fusion Module (DLFM) then uses each query to select top-k most semantically relevant local patches via cosine similarity, constructs a query-augmented fully-connected graph with pairwise similarity edge weights, and applies a GCN to capture structural relationships among selected patches. The refined queries from both branches are fused with the [CLS] token via self-attention to yield the final embedding. Training jointly optimizes cross-entropy and triplet losses (applied to both global and local features), an orthogonality constraint between invariant and view-specific features, a binary view-classification loss, and an MoE load-balancing regularization.

## Results

On the CARGO dataset, ViSA achieves 70.51% Rank-1 and 65.46% mAP under the ALL protocol, surpassing the previous best method SeCap by +1.92% Rank-1 and +5.27% mAP. The most striking gain appears in the cross-view A↔G protocol, where ViSA reaches 71.28% Rank-1 and 69.00% mAP, a 10.06% mAP improvement over SeCap. On G↔G, ViSA attains 88.39% Rank-1 and 83.90% mAP, and on A↔A it scores 82.50% Rank-1 and 67.78% mAP. The paper also reports consistent state-of-the-art mAP on AG-ReID.v2 and LAGPeR (details in supplementary material).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViSA outperforms all 12 baselines on the CARGO ALL protocol in both Rank-1 and mAP, with the largest margins over the ViT backbone (+8.97% R1, +11.92% mAP) and the view-invariant VDT (+6.41% R1, +10.26% mAP). Against the strongest recent competitor SeCap (CVPR'25), ViSA gains +1.92% R1 and +5.27% mAP overall, and a particularly large +10.06% mAP on the A↔G cross-view protocol (69.00 vs. 58.94), confirming the benefit of view-aware expert routing over prompt-based local feature capture. On G↔G, ViSA leads SeCap by +1.78% R1 and +8.48% mAP. The only metric where ViSA trails a baseline is A↔A mAP (67.78 vs. SeCap's 68.08), a 0.30% gap that is negligible relative to the cross-view gains. CLIP-based methods (CLIP-ReID, PCL-CLIP) remain 3–5% behind ViSA in mAP, indicating that pre-trained vision-language features alone are insufficient for the extreme aerial-ground viewpoint gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViSA extends the View-Decoupled Transformer (VDT, CVPR 2024) by upgrading its single-stream view-decoupling into a dual-stream encoder that explicitly preserves both view-invariant and view-specific representations, rather than suppressing viewpoint entirely. It departs from the dominant view-invariant paradigm (VDT, DTST, SeCap, VIF-AGReID) by arguing that forcing cross-view alignment collapses to part-level matching and discards identity-relevant view-specific cues. The method further introduces two architectural ingredients not previously combined in AGPReID: a Mixture-of-Experts mechanism (drawing from MoSCE-ReID and HAMoBE in general ReID) to generate view-aware semantic queries, and a query-guided sparse graph convolutional network (inspired by GPS, ADGC, RTGAT) to fuse local patch features in a structured manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely adopted assumption in AGPReID—that suppressing viewpoint variation is strictly beneficial—and provides empirical evidence that retaining view-specific identity signals alongside invariant ones yields substantially better cross-view retrieval (up to 10.06% mAP on CARGO A↔G). The view-aware semantic alignment paradigm could inform other cross-view or cross-domain recognition tasks where naive invariance discards useful discriminative structure. Open questions include whether the MoE experts learn genuinely interpretable view-specific patterns or merely additional capacity, whether the approach generalizes to more than two viewpoints (e.g., multi-drone, multi-elevation), and whether the graph-based local fusion transfers to other ReID settings such as visible-infrared or cloth-changing scenarios.
