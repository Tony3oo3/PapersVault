## Problem

Existing multi-subject personalized generation models suffer from degraded subject consistency (subjects diverge from or are omitted relative to references) and poor text-prompt adherence (attributes get swapped between subjects). The authors attribute these failures to the absence of high-quality multi-subject training data and the lack of fine-grained, subject-level post-training objectives beyond global-image SFT loss.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 2–4 reference images (each containing one subject in a realistic background, not necessarily isolated on white) and a free-form text prompt specifying the target scene, attributes, actions, or spatial layout. It outputs a single 512×512 (or 512-shortest-side) multi-subject image where all referenced subjects appear consistently and the prompt's semantics are respected. Training uses LoRA on top of FLUX.1 Kontext, with reference latents concatenated to the noise latent via a frame-wise positional offset.

## Method

The approach combines a scalable data pipeline with a two-stage training paradigm. First, a 350K-sample dataset is synthesized by prompting T2I models for multi-subject scenes, cropping individual subjects with GroundingDINO, and regenerating reference images via a single-subject personalization model (FLUX.1 Kontext), with LLM-driven recaptioning to avoid textual leakage. In the SFT stage, a virtual temporal-only positional offset (PO_i = (i, h, w)) replaces spatial h/w offsets, enabling the model to generalize across 2–4 reference images without inheriting fixed spatial-layout priors. In the RL stage, the authors introduce Pairwise Subject-Consistency Rewards (PSR): both the generated image and each reference are decoupled per-subject via GroundingDINO, and DINOv2 similarity is computed on each subject pair, yielding a fine-grained, subject-level reward. This PSR is combined with a Qwen2.5-VL semantic-alignment reward and an HPSv3 aesthetic reward in a Flow-GRPO policy-gradient update, jointly optimizing consistency, text faithfulness, and perceptual quality.

## Results

On PSRBench, PSR achieves an overall subject consistency score of 0.673, surpassing the best baselines (OmniGen2 and XVerse at 0.587) by a margin of 0.086. On aesthetic preference, PSR scores 1.124 overall, outperforming the next-best OmniGen2 (1.020) by 0.104. On semantic alignment, PSR reaches 0.783 overall, edging out Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (0.761) and OmniGen2 (0.758). The gains are most pronounced on the harder Three-subject (0.615 vs. 0.552) and Four-subject (0.571 vs. 0.508) subsets for consistency, and on the Position subset for semantic alignment (0.468 vs. 0.439 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PSR outperforms all five baselines on every subset across all three evaluation dimensions. On subject consistency (the paper's primary metric), PSR leads OmniGen2 and XVerse (tied at 0.587 overall) by 0.086, with the largest gaps on the Three-subject (+0.088) and Four-subject (+0.063) subsets where multi-reference consistency is hardest. On aesthetic preference, PSR (1.124) beats OmniGen2 (1.020) and UNO (1.009) by roughly 0.10, while Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 drops sharply to 0.533 on the Four-subject subset. On semantic alignment, PSR (0.783) narrowly surpasses Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (0.761) and OmniGen2 (0.758), with a clear advantage on the Position subset (0.468 vs. 0.439) where spatial reasoning is critical. FLUX.1 Kontext, the base model, lags significantly on consistency (0.497) and semantic alignment (0.583), confirming that multi-subject handling requires the proposed data pipeline and RL stage. The SFT-only variant (Ours-SFT) already surpasses FLUX.1 Kontext and UNO on consistency but still trails OmniGen2 and XVerse, demonstrating that the PSR reinforcement stage is essential for the full performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-subject personalization paradigm of FLUX.1 Kontext to multi-subject scenarios by introducing a scalable data generation pipeline that repurposes single-subject models to synthesize multi-subject training pairs, and a frame-wise positional encoding that replaces the spatial-offset schemes used by UNO and OmniGen2. For post-training, it adapts the Flow-GRPO reinforcement learning framework for flow matching models but introduces a novel Pairwise Subject-Consistency Reward that operates at the subject level rather than the global image level, departing from prior RL-for-generation work that relies on holistic aesthetic or text-alignment rewards. The approach is positioned against recent multi-subject methods (UNO, OmniGen2, XVerse, Qwen-Image-Edit) that the authors argue suffer from inconsistent data construction and lack of fine-grained subject-level optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PSRBench benchmark with its grounding-based subject-consistency metric and seven task subsets addresses a real gap in evaluation granularity for multi-subject personalization, and the 350K-sample data pipeline offers a practical recipe for constructing high-quality multi-subject training data without manual curation. The PSR reward mechanism demonstrates that subject-level decoupling in RL can substantially close the consistency gap that SFT alone leaves open, suggesting a broader principle for fine-grained control in generative models. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond four subjects, whether the 512-resolution training constraint can be lifted without degrading the RL gains, and whether the data pipeline generalizes to video or 3D personalization.
