## Problem

Diffusion-based dataset distillation methods inject textual semantics only during the denoising phase via cross-attention, causing prompt signals to dominate generation and over-correct samples toward label semantics at the expense of visual fidelity. Because early latent representations carry primarily visual cues, semantics introduced late act correctively rather than co-evolutionarily, producing synthetic images with unnatural shapes, text-like textures, and simplified silhouettes that drift from the encoder's visual manifold.

## Input / Output

The method consumes real RGB images and their class labels, encoding them into VAE visual latents and text-encoder class embeddings respectively. It produces a compact synthetic dataset at a user-specified images-per-class (IPC) budget, with resolutions ranging from 32×32 (CIFAR) to 256×256 (ImageNet subsets) and 224×224 (full ImageNet-1K). The cross-attention module is trained on real data, after which the fused latents serve as initial conditions for the diffusion sampler to generate the distilled samples.

## Method

EVLF inserts a lightweight cross-attention module at the encoder-backbone interface, before any diffusion steps, so that image tokens (queries) attend to text tokens (keys/values) and produce a fused latent that jointly encodes visual structure and class semantics. The module is trained with a dual objective: an MSE loss that anchors the fused latent to the original VAE encoding (preserving visual fidelity) and an InfoNCE loss that aligns a projected fused latent with same-class text embeddings (enforcing semantic consistency). Because the fusion occurs before noise injection, the denoiser starts from an initialization that already integrates both modalities, reducing prompt dominance and keeping generation closer to the visual manifold. The module is plug-and-play—it requires no changes to training schedules, loss functions, or denoiser architectures—and can optionally be paired with a lightweight denoiser fine-tuning step when the fused latent distribution differs from the pretrained prior.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 at IPC=10, D4M+EVLF achieves 45.7% accuracy, surpassing D4M (37.6%) by 8.1 points. On ImageNette at IPC=10, MGD3+EVLF reaches 66.0% vs. MGD3's 64.3%, and on ImageWoof at IPC=100 with ResNetAP-10, MGD3+EVLF attains 68.1% vs. MGD3's 64.3% (+3.8%). On full ImageNet-1K at IPC=10, MGD3+EVLF achieves 61.9% top-1 accuracy, outperforming MinimaxDiffusion (44.3%) and D4M (60.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EVLF consistently outperforms both D4M and MGD3 across all tested datasets and IPC settings, with the largest gains at low IPC (e.g., +8.1% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, +9.6% on ImageIDC IPC=10), confirming that early fusion is most beneficial when sample budgets are tight. Against non-generative baselines, D4M+EVLF surpasses SRe2L by 16.4 points and RDED by 8.6 points on CIFAR-10 IPC=10, and MGD3+EVLF exceeds MinimaxDiffusion by 17.6 points on ImageNet-1K IPC=10. On ImageWoof, MGD3+EVLF beats IDC-1 by 13.4 points (IPC=10, ResNetAP-10) and Herding by 7.3 points. In transfer learning (Table 6), MGD3+EVLF-pretrained models outperform KRR-ST by 0.9% on CIFAR-10, 1.2% on CIFAR-100, 0.7% on Dogs, and 5.6% on Flowers, indicating better preservation of discriminative features. The DiT baseline is competitive at higher IPC but is consistently surpassed by EVLF-augmented methods at IPC=10 and 20 across all datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the diffusion-based dataset distillation line that builds on LDMs and DiTs as generative backbones, with direct predecessors including D4M (prototype-driven sampling), MGD3 (multimodal guidance inside the denoiser), and MinimaxDiffusion (minimax game formulation). All of these inject textual semantics via cross-attention during the denoising phase. EVLF departs structurally by relocating the vision-language alignment to the encoder-backbone interface—before any noise is added—so that the denoiser starts from a latent that already encodes both visual structure and class semantics rather than receiving prompt signals mid-trajectory. The method is deliberately minimal: a single lightweight cross-attention module trained with MSE + InfoNCE, requiring no changes to the denoiser architecture or sampling schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means EVLF can be dropped into any existing encoder-equipped diffusion-based DD pipeline (demonstrated with both D4M and MGD3), lowering the barrier for practitioners to improve their distilled datasets without re-deriving training objectives. The consistent 2–5% gains across seven datasets, three test architectures, and IPC ranges from 10 to 100 suggest the early-fusion principle is broadly applicable rather than dataset-specific. However, the current formulation is limited to class-level conditioning; extending to instance-level or compositional prompts, and validating on non-classification downstream tasks, remain open. Whether the same early-fusion principle transfers to non-distillation generative tasks (e.g., text-to-image with class constraints) is an interesting but unexplored direction.
