## Problem

Large vision-language models lack both large-scale instruction-following training data for medical video understanding and a viable RL methodology for heterogeneous medical datasets. Standard GRPO-based RL collapses on multi-dataset medical video training because raw task metrics create fundamentally unfair reward scales—easy datasets (median mIoU ~0.5) dominate gradient updates while hard ones (median mIoU ~0.12) degrade catastrophically. Additionally, standard semantic similarity metrics fail to capture clinically critical distinctions in generated captions, such as 'tool' versus 'grasper' or 'tissue' versus 'cystic duct.'

## Input / Output

The model consumes medical video clips sampled at adaptive frame rates (0.1–3.0 FPS depending on task and procedure length, spanning 20 s–1800 s durations) alongside text instructions in conversational QA format, with each frame encoded at 8×28×28 to 48×28×28 pixel resolution. Outputs span three temporal granularities: video-level (free-text summaries, binary safety and skill labels, next-action predictions), segment-level (temporal action intervals, dense and region-specific captions), and frame-level (spatiotemporal bounding boxes for instrument localization). This multi-granular design requires a single model to simultaneously reason about when actions occur, where instruments are located, and how procedures should be performed.

## Method

MedGRPO employs a two-stage paradigm: supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-VL-7B on 532K video-instruction pairs from MedVidBench (8 medical sources, 8 tasks), followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning with two key innovations. First, cross-dataset reward normalization applies a logistic function centered on each dataset-task pair's SFT-baseline median (p50) with IQR-based scaling, so that median-level performance yields an identical normalized reward of 0.5 regardless of task difficulty, eliminating gradient bias toward easier sources while providing smooth, outlier-robust gradients. Second, a GPT-4.1-based medical LLM judge evaluates caption quality via comparative similarity scoring across five clinical dimensions (terminology precision, instrument/anatomy identification, specificity versus vagueness, procedural context, and action accuracy), combined with semantic similarity in an equal-weighted hybrid reward to balance fine-grained clinical correctness with overall coherence. The GRPO implementation adopts DAPO's asymmetric clipping (ε_low=0.2, ε_high=0.3), removes the KL penalty, and uses group size G=8 over 5000 gradient updates.

## Results

On MedVidBench (Standard version, 6,245 test samples), Qwen2.5VL-7B_MedGRPO achieves the best or near-best score across all 8 tasks: CVS accuracy 0.896, STG mIoU 0.202, TAG mIoU@0.3 0.216, TAG mIoU@0.5 0.156, DVC LLM-judge 3.797, DVC F1 0.214, VS LLM-judge 4.184, and RC LLM-judge 3.442. The SFT baseline already surpasses all off-the-shelf models (e.g., CVS 0.894 vs. GPT-4.1's 0.018; STG 0.177 vs. Gemini-2.5-Flash's 0.047), and MedGRPO further improves grounding (STG +0.025, TAG@0.3 +0.074) and captioning (VS +0.588, RC +0.685) over SFT. The only slight regression is NAP accuracy (0.442→0.405), which was excluded from the reward optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Both the SFT baseline and MedGRPO substantially outperform all five off-the-shelf baselines across nearly every task. GPT-4.1, despite being a frontier LLM, scores only 0.018 on CVS and 0.014 on STG mIoU, while Gemini-2.5-Flash reaches 0.101 and 0.047 respectively—both far below the SFT baseline's 0.894 and 0.177. VideoChat-R1.5-7B, a recent RL-trained general-domain VideoLLM, scores 0.000 on both CVS and STG, confirming that general video RL does not transfer to medical tasks. The single-dataset surgical fine-tune (Qwen2.5VL-7B_Surg-CholecT50) also fails to generalize, achieving 0.000 on CVS and STG, underscoring the necessity of multi-source training. MedGRPO further improves over the SFT baseline on 7 of 8 tasks, with the largest gains in VS LLM-judge (+0.588, from 3.596 to 4.184) and RC LLM-judge (+0.685, from 2.757 to 3.442), while NAP accuracy drops slightly (0.442→0.405) since it was not included in the reward signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (from DeepSeekMath) and DAPO's training modifications (asymmetric clipping, no KL penalty) to the medical video domain, where prior work (SurgLaVi, SurgLLM, Qwen2.5VL-7B_Surg-CholecT50) was limited to single-dataset surgical fine-tuning without multi-source RL. It also extends the LLM-as-judge evaluation paradigm from general NLP to a medical-specific comparative scoring scheme across five clinical dimensions. The key conceptual departure is identifying that standard multi-dataset RL training collapses due to reward magnitude disparity across heterogeneous sources—a problem not addressed by prior multi-task VLM work (Unified-IO, OFA) which assumed homogeneous difficulty or used architectural modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedVidBench (532K pairs, 8 sources, 8 tasks) provides the first large-scale multi-granular medical video benchmark in instruction-following format, filling a critical gap between existing surgical datasets and VideoLLM training requirements. The reward normalization technique is broadly applicable to any multi-dataset RL setting where task difficulty varies, and the ablation showing catastrophic collapse without it (CVS: 0.894→0.020) is a clear warning for practitioners. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 7B models and 4 RL-optimized tasks, whether the GPT-4.1-based judge can be replaced by cheaper alternatives for training efficiency, and whether the benchmark's dual-model generation pipeline introduces residual biases that affect downstream RL optimization.
