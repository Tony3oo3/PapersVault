## Problem

Eye images from wearable devices encode biometric iris identity, blocking safe data sharing under regulations like GDPR, yet existing anonymization methods (Rubber Sheet Model, Iris Style Transfer) either degrade gaze-estimation accuracy while still retaining recognizable iris features, or introduce severe boundary artifacts that reduce image fidelity. No prior approach simultaneously preserves gaze geometry, removes identity cues across the full eye region, and maintains photorealistic output quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260792/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single infrared eye image (cropped to 400×400, 256×256, or 480×480 depending on the source dataset) along with a fixed CLIP-encoded text prompt describing eye type. Unlike baselines that only perturb the iris patch, PrivateEyes synthesizes the entire eye region—including eyelashes, eyebrows, and periocular skin—producing a full anonymized eye image at the same resolution. Corneal glints are inpainted before synthesis and re-blended afterward to preserve illumination cues needed by model-based gaze estimators.

## Method

PrivateEyes is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a GhostNet segmenter isolates iris, pupil, and sclera regions and extracts ellipse parameters; (2) a Pose Estimation Network (PEN), a multi-layer perceptron trained on 6 million synthetic images generated by backward ray-tracing an anatomically accurate 3D eye model (Aguirre-based, with corneal refraction), maps 2D ellipse features to a 7-parameter 3D pose (azimuth, elevation, torsion, translation, depth, pupil radius); (3) ControlEye, a ControlNet-extended Stable Diffusion model, synthesizes the anonymized image conditioned on the segmentation map and a CLIP-encoded pose embedding injected via zero-convolution layers across 12 U-Net encoder blocks and one middle block at four resolutions. The key design choice is that all control signals (segmentation + pose) are identity-free by construction, so the diffusion model has no access to the original iris texture, while the pose conditioning explicitly enforces gaze-direction consistency in the output.

## Results

PrivateEyes (with PEN) achieves the lowest gaze estimation error on EV-Eye (2.89°) and LPW (2.51°), and competitive error on OpenEDS (4.43°), while simultaneously reducing iris-recognition accuracy to 14–18% (cropped) and 14–17% (full) across all three datasets — roughly a 50% reduction compared to the strongest geometry-based baseline (Rubber Sheet: 38–79% cropped). On image quality (Table 2), PrivateEyes attains the best FID (69.25 on OpenEDS, 42.98 on EV-Eye) and KID (0.083, 0.034) among all methods, confirming higher perceptual fidelity than RSM, IST, or conventional blur/downsample perturbations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PrivateEyes outperforms all three baselines on the joint privacy–utility objective. Against Rubber Sheet Model [6], PrivateEyes reduces gaze error by 22% on OpenEDS (4.43° vs 5.69°) and 18% on EV-Eye (2.89° vs 3.52°), while cutting iris-recognition accuracy from 38–79% to 16–18% (cropped) and from 87–96% to 14–17% (full). Against Iris Style Transfer [30], PrivateEyes achieves lower gaze error on OpenEDS (4.43° vs 7.65°) and EV-Eye (2.89° vs 4.80°) and dramatically lower iris recognizability (16.5% vs 78.1% on OpenEDS); however, IST retains a slight gaze-error edge on LPW (1.91° vs 2.51°) at the cost of 81% iris recognizability. Against the conventional Blur+Downsample [23] baseline, PrivateEyes improves gaze error by 43% on OpenEDS (4.43° vs 7.77°) and 61% on EV-Eye (2.89° vs 7.44°) while achieving comparable or better anonymization. The 'All Combined' perturbation (blur + noise + downsample) achieves the lowest iris recognition on OpenEDS (11.4%) but at the cost of the worst gaze error (13.42°), illustrating the privacy–utility trade-off that PrivateEyes resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant families of eye anonymization—geometry-based warping (Rubber Sheet Model) and appearance-based neural style transfer (IST)—by replacing both with a modular diffusion-based synthesis pipeline. It builds directly on ControlNet and Stable Diffusion for conditional image generation, GhostNet for semantic segmentation, and CLIP for encoding control signals, but introduces a novel anatomically accurate 3D eye model with backward ray tracing to generate 6 million synthetic training samples for a dedicated Pose Estimation Network. The key conceptual departure is treating gaze preservation as an explicit 3D pose-conditioning problem rather than an implicit byproduct of geometric or texture manipulation, and the synthetic-data pipeline for pose supervision is a new contribution not present in prior anonymization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical bottleneck in AR/VR and eye-tracking research: the inability to publicly share real eye images due to biometric privacy regulations (GDPR). By reducing iris-recognition accuracy to ~15% while maintaining or improving gaze-estimation accuracy over all baselines, it enables task-preserving data release that was previously infeasible. Key limitations include the absence of temporal coherence across video frames and sensitivity to extreme illumination or gaze angles, leaving open the question of whether the approach extends to real-time video anonymization. The modular design also raises the question of whether the pose-estimation component can be replaced by lighter-weight alternatives as gaze-estimation models improve.
