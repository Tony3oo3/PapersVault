## Problem

When foundation models like CLIP are fine-tuned in federated long-tailed learning, their inherent class balance is eroded, degrading tail-class performance below even zero-shot baselines. Moreover, conventional personalization via parameter or feature-level fusion propagates this global imbalance to client-specific models. The paper asks how to preserve the balanced knowledge of pre-trained foundation models during collaborative training while still enabling effective, unbiased client personalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone with parameter-efficient adapters (LoRA rank 8, prompt length 4, or adapter modules). In the federated setting, 20 clients hold non-IID local subsets (Dirichlet α=1) of long-tailed datasets (imbalance factors 50–500). The global model outputs a class probability distribution over C classes; the personalized model produces the same distribution via additive logit fusion of a frozen global branch and a client-specific residual branch, with the zero-shot CLIP prediction serving as a fixed reference anchor throughout training.

## Method

FedPuReL introduces two complementary mechanisms. First, gradient purification constrains local PEFT updates by computing a temperature-aligned KL divergence (TKL) between fine-tuned and zero-shot logits, yielding an alignment gradient that encodes the direction of balanced knowledge; the task gradient is then projected onto the subspace orthogonal to this alignment gradient whenever the two conflict (negative inner product), removing components that would push the model toward head-class bias. Second, personalization is reconceptualized as residual learning in the output space: a dual-branch architecture keeps the global PEFT parameters frozen and learns client-specific parameters whose logits are added to the global logits before the final softmax, so that local corrections are purely additive and cannot corrupt the balanced global representations. Training uses a composite loss balancing the fused prediction against the personal branch alone, with gradients flowing only through the client-specific parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT, FedPuReL (prompt-based) achieves 72.96% overall accuracy for the global model, surpassing the strongest baseline (PromptFolio+Fed-GraB at 69.53%) by 3.43 points, with the gap widening dramatically on few-shot classes (66.70% vs. 52.83%, a 13.87-point margin). On Places-LT, FedPuReL reaches 43.88% GM accuracy and 39.68% PM accuracy, again outperforming all baselines. The LoRA-based variant achieves 71.43% GM and 70.55% PM on ImageNet-LT, while the adapter-based variant reaches 70.51% GM and 70.19% PM, all consistently exceeding their respective category's best baseline across every shot category.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedPuReL consistently outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset, PEFT category, and shot group. Against prompt-based methods, the largest gains appear on few-shot classes: FedPuReL achieves 66.70% vs. PromptFolio's 47.02% and FedOTP's 43.74% on ImageNet-LT GM Few, a 13–23 point margin. Even the strongest combined baseline (PromptFolio+Fed-GraB, which explicitly leverages class priors) is surpassed by 3.43 points on ImageNet-LT GM All and 13.87 points on GM Few. Among LoRA-based methods, FedPuReL leads CLIPLoRA+Fed-GraB by 1.19 points on ImageNet-LT GM All but by 11.42 points on PM Few (66.98% vs. 55.56%), indicating the advantage grows with class scarcity. For adapter-based methods, FedPuReL exceeds FedClip+Fed-GraB by 1.26 points on ImageNet-LT GM All and 4.71 points on PM Few. The ablation (Table 4) further confirms that both gradient purification and residual learning contribute independently: gradient purification alone lifts ImageNet-LT GM Few from 42.32% to 64.74%, while residual learning alone improves PM Few from 48.67% to 55.89%, and their combination achieves the best overall results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three previously separate threads: federated long-tailed learning (Fed-GraB, classifier decoupling), personalized federated learning (PromptFolio, FedOTP, FedPGP), and foundation-model-based federated PEFT (PromptFL, CLIPLoRA, FedClip). Prior Fed-LT methods target a single balanced global model and ignore client heterogeneity, while PFL methods assume balanced data and propagate global bias through parameter or feature-level fusion. FedPuReL departs from both by using CLIP's zero-shot balancedness as an explicit gradient anchor (purification via orthogonal projection) and reconceptualizing personalization as additive logit-space residuals over a frozen global model, rather than fusing at the parameter or feature level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that naive fine-tuning of CLIP on long-tailed federated data degrades balancedness below the zero-shot baseline challenges the default assumption that PEFT always improves upon the pre-trained model, and provides a principled explanation for why existing PFL methods underperform on tail classes. The gradient-purification-plus-residual-learning recipe is practical and composable with any PEFT variant (prompt, LoRA, adapter), making it a drop-in improvement for federated CLIP pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond CLIP to other foundation models (e.g., SAM, LLaVA), whether it holds under more extreme client heterogeneity or fewer communication rounds, and whether the zero-shot anchor remains effective when the downstream task is substantially out-of-distribution from CLIP's pre-training.
