## Problem

Existing pixel-grounded video MLLMs rely on a static [SEG] token for frame-wise segmentation, which conveys semantics but no information about how an object's position or appearance evolves over time. This leads to spatial drift, identity switches, and fragile first-frame initialization when objects move or reappear, because errors in the initial mask propagate through subsequent frames without any temporal referential cue to correct them.

## Input / Output

The model receives a video clip and a natural-language referring expression (e.g., 'the woman in a yellow jacket') and produces per-frame segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and—on the GCG task—interleaved text descriptions with grounded masks. At inference, the LLM emits both [BOX] and [SEG] tokens; [BOX] conditions a class-agnostic Deformable-DETR proposer on frozen SAM2/Hiera multi-scale features to yield up to 300 proposals per frame, which are filtered and refined by a language-conditioned head before being passed to SAM2's pixel decoder alongside [SEG]. The design naturally supports multi-instance queries (e.g., 'both players') without explicit instance supervision, and TSF tokens are optional at test time (omitted by default for detector-free operation).

## Method

SPARROW introduces two complementary modules that plug into existing video MLLMs (VideoGLaMM, UniPixel, GLUS) via lightweight adapters without modifying backbones. First, Target-Specific Tracked Features (TSF) inject temporally aligned referent cues during training: GroundingDINO detects the referent, CLDTracker propagates it across frames, K-means clustering (K=4) selects diverse appearance representatives, and the resulting image-encoder features are projected into the LLM embedding space as additional input tokens, teaching identity persistence without requiring any tracker at inference. Second, a dual-prompt grounding mechanism decodes both [BOX] and [SEG] tokens: [BOX] drives a class-agnostic proposer (Deformable-DETR on frozen Hiera features, pretrained on COCO/Objects365/OpenImages/V3Det with all labels discarded) to produce geometric priors, while [SEG] supplies language-conditioned semantics to SAM2's prompt encoder, enabling a coarse-to-fine pipeline that stabilizes early frames and allows drift correction. Training proceeds in two stages—Stage 1 optimizes V→L adapters, L→V SEG adapters, and LoRA parameters with TSF tokens under cross-entropy plus BCE/Dice mask losses; Stage 2 fine-tunes only a lightweight filtration head and the L→V BOX adapter on task-specific grounding data while keeping all other modules frozen.

## Results

SPARROW, integrated into three video MLLM backbones, achieves consistent gains across all six benchmarks. The headline result is +8.9 J&F on MeViS val^u (48.5→57.4) when added to VideoGLaMM, demonstrating strong compositional generalization to unseen motion expressions. On Ref-DAVIS17, boundary accuracy (F) improves by up to +14.5 (65.6→80.1) for VideoGLaMM, yielding +7.3 J&F. On VidSTG visual grounding, SPARROW adds approximately +5 mIoU across all backbones (e.g., 39.66→45.06 on VideoGLaMM), and on VideoGCG it improves CLAIR by +5.4 (28.2→33.6) and mIoU by +3.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPARROW is evaluated as a plug-and-play module added to three recent video MLLMs: UniPixel (NeurIPS 2025), GLUS (CVPR 2025), and VideoGLaMM (CVPR 2025). It consistently outperforms all three baselines on every benchmark, with the largest absolute gains on the weakest backbone (VideoGLaMM): +8.9 J&F on MeViS val^u, +7.3 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17, and +5.4 CLAIR on VideoGCG. On the stronger UniPixel backbone, gains are smaller but still positive (+1.0 to +1.3 J&F on MeViS, +2.2 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17), confirming that SPARROW's benefits are not merely compensating for a weak base model. GLUS sits in between, with +1.9 to +3.4 J&F on MeViS and +2.6 J&F on Ref-DAVIS17. On VidSTG visual grounding, all three backbones receive a near-uniform ~+5 mIoU boost (41.25→46.74 UniPixel, 29.92→35.17 GLUS, 39.66→45.06 VideoGLaMM), indicating the dual-prompt design improves spatial precision regardless of the underlying architecture. No comparison shows SPARROW underperforming its respective baseline on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPARROW extends the line of pixel-grounded video MLLMs (VideoGLaMM, UniPixel, GLUS) that rely on a static [SEG] token for frame-wise segmentation, which the authors identify as insufficient for maintaining temporal referential consistency. Conceptually, it draws on two prior threads: Artemis's insight that tracking object-specific features improves temporal coherence (repurposed here as Target-Specific Tracked Features injected only during training), and Groma's localized box prompting for fine-grained image grounding (generalized to a dual [BOX]+[SEG] coarse-to-fine pipeline for video). The method is explicitly plug-and-play, attaching lightweight adapters and a class-agnostic SAM2/Hiera-based proposer to existing backbones without architectural modification, and is supported by a newly curated referential dataset (30,646 videos, 45,231 Q&A pairs) that harmonizes seven public sources into object-centric, temporally consistent annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and recurring failure mode in video MLLMs—temporal drift and identity switches under motion and occlusion—through a modular, training-time-only temporal supervision signal (TSF) that requires no external tracker at inference, making it deployable without heavy runtime dependencies. The consistent gains across three independent backbones and six benchmarks (up to +8.9 J&F on MeViS val^u, +5 mIoU on VidSTG) suggest the dual-prompt and TSF components are broadly applicable rather than backbone-specific. However, the approach remains dependent on proposal recall from the class-agnostic proposer, meaning very small or heavily occluded objects may still be missed, and TSF quality is bounded by the pseudo-tracks from GroundingDINO/CLDTracker. Open questions include whether the method extends to longer video sequences, multi-object interactive scenarios, or settings where the referent undergoes drastic appearance change.
