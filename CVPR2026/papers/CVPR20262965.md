## Problem

Existing continual adversarial training methods assume abundant adversarial examples at each attack stage, which is unrealistic when adversaries can craft effective perturbations from very few images. This paper introduces the Few-shot Continual Adversarial Training (FS-CAT) setting, where only k adversarial samples per class are available per stage, and identifies two compounding challenges: degraded robust generalization under data scarcity and severely amplified catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262965/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model is first pretrained on a large clean dataset (ImageNet-1K at 224×224 or CIFAR-100 at 32×32), then sequentially fine-tuned on k-shot adversarial sets (e.g., 10 images per class) generated by a specific attack (FGSM, PGD, CW, AutoAttack, DeepFool, etc.). During each fine-tuning stage, the model also consumes GMM-sampled pseudo-features from previously seen adversarial domains rather than raw stored images, and outputs standard class labels. Evaluation covers both clean accuracy and robust accuracy across all seen and unseen attack types.

## Method

The framework combines three components. First, an Adversarial Margin Loss (ADM) is applied during pretraining: it iteratively projects each clean sample toward its nearest decision boundary via gradient steps and penalizes the resulting margin, explicitly enlarging the safe region around clean features to reserve space for adversarial perturbations. Second, a GMM Prototype Replay mechanism fits a Gaussian Mixture Model (with λ₁ components) to the class-conditional feature distribution of each past adversarial domain; during subsequent stages, pseudo-features are sampled from these GMMs (with covariance-scaled Gaussian noise) and fed through the classifier to compute a replay loss, preserving prior knowledge without storing raw data. Third, a Multi-Domain Balanced (MDB) loss formulates a min-max objective over a virtual parameter perturbation Δθ, whose first-order Taylor expansion yields a closed-form reweighted gradient that down-weights domains with above-average loss, steering updates toward directions beneficial to the majority of past domains and stabilizing cross-domain optimization.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K under the short attack sequence [FGSM, PGD, CW, AA, Df] in a 10-shot setting, the proposed FS-CAT framework achieves 42.71% FGSM, 33.53% PGD, 36.02% CW, 28.91% AA, 32.11% Df robust accuracy, and 64.51% clean accuracy (averaged over multiple seeds). Under the longer 7-attack sequence [FGSM, BIM, PGD, SA, BS, MCG, DIM], it reaches 39.61% FGSM and 58.45% clean accuracy. The method also generalizes to unseen attacks (e.g., 38.07% on BIM, 36.03% on DIM) and natural corruptions (40.71% on fog), consistently outperforming all baselines by 3–14 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across every attack and the clean accuracy on both ImageNet-1K and CIFAR-100. Against the closest competitor SSEAT [24] (the direct CAT predecessor), FS-CAT gains +6.43% on FGSM, +3.29% on PGD, +3.94% on CW, +3.00% on AA, +3.82% on Df, and a substantial +13.86% on clean accuracy (64.51% vs. 50.65%), while also exhibiting lower standard deviation across seeds. Against AFD [30], the gains are +7.80% (FGSM) to +14.81% (Clean); against RIFT [32], +9.33% (FGSM) to +15.99% (Clean). The traditional adversarial training baselines PGD-AT [17], AWP [25], and LBGAT [7] trail by even larger margins (e.g., PGD-AT scores only 29.23% FGSM and 46.31% Clean vs. 42.71% and 64.51% for FS-CAT). Notably, FS-CAT also achieves the lowest variance across random seeds (e.g., 0.62 std on AA vs. 0.71 for SSEAT), indicating more stable continual learning under data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Continual Adversarial Training (CAT) framework introduced by SSEAT [24] by imposing a few-shot data constraint at each adversarial stage, a setting the authors explicitly frame as absent from prior continual defense work. Methodologically, it draws on three distinct threads: margin-based adversarial training (related to LBGAT [7] and AWP [25]) for the ADM loss, distributional replay (related to GMM-based pseudo-replay in continual learning) for the prototype mechanism, and multi-task gradient balancing for the MDB loss. Unlike SSEAT's exemplar-based adversarial replay, the GMM prototype approach avoids storing raw adversarial images, and unlike gradient-projection methods (Ru et al. [22]), it does not require orthogonal constraints but instead reweights domain gradients by loss deviation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FS-CAT formulation addresses a practically important gap: real-world defenders rarely have thousands of adversarial examples for each newly emerging attack, yet prior continual defense methods implicitly assume abundant data per stage. The consistent 5–10 point robust accuracy gains over SSEAT across diverse attack sequences, combined with a ~14-point clean accuracy improvement on ImageNet-1K, suggest that margin-aware pretraining plus distributional replay is a viable recipe for data-efficient adversarial defense. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ResNet-50 and CNN backbones, how it behaves under truly zero-shot (no clean pretraining) conditions, and whether the GMM replay in feature space can be extended to input-space generation for stronger cross-domain transfer.
