## Problem

The first 4D spatial-angular structured light system [43] can capture shape and reflectance simultaneously but is slow (24 min/view) because it activates only one LED at a time and uses pre-optimized, object-agnostic illumination patterns. No prior method adaptively computes high-dimensional 4D lighting conditions on-the-fly to minimize uncertainty for a specific object, leaving a gap in both acquisition efficiency and reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260493/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes 72 LDR photographs (downsampled to 127×64 during adaptive acquisition, upsampled to ~1024×1024 during fine-tuning) captured by a single 45MP camera under patterns projected by a 64×48 RGB LED array and a 1920×1080 LCD mask. The output is a depth map and multiple texture maps storing GGX BRDF parameters (albedo, roughness, anisotropy, tangent). Notably, the illumination patterns themselves are the learned variables—each pattern is a differentiable parameter optimized during acquisition rather than a fixed codebook.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. In adaptive acquisition, histogram-based probability models track per-pixel uncertainty over depth and BRDF parameters; the next batch of 3 light/mask patterns is differentiably optimized to minimize cross-entropy depth uncertainty, where candidate distinguishability is measured via ZNCC between simulated measurements under existing and new patterns. Only the top-3 peak-ZNCC candidates contribute to the loss, and mask pixels are pushed toward binary values via a large-scalar sigmoid. In fine-tuning, depth and GGX parameters (reparameterized through a 16D neural latent vector and 5 MLPs) are jointly refined by minimizing residuals between all physical and simulated measurements. A key design choice is that the loss targets depth uncertainty alone, since reflectance is shown to be recoverable as a by-product of the same adaptive illumination, and using multiple LEDs simultaneously reduces exposure time by up to 100× compared to single-LED scanning.

## Results

On 10 physical objects (9–15 cm), the method achieves a depth RMSE of ~4.94 mm with 93% inliers (inlier RMSE 0.42 mm) using 72 adaptive light/mask patterns, compared to 10.28 mm / 89% for the closest baseline [43] and 22.03 mm / 64% for Micro Phase Shifting [16]. The adaptive variant slightly outperforms a non-adaptive variant (4.78 mm, 94% inliers) in inlier rate while the non-adaptive variant has marginally lower overall RMSE. For reflectance, relighting results are visually comparable to [43] and closely match photographs taken under novel lighting conditions. Total acquisition time is ~10 minutes (15 s exposure) versus 24 minutes for [43], a 2× speedup with 100× less exposure time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline [43] (Xu et al., CVPR 2023), which uses the same 4D spatial-angular hardware but with pre-optimized single-LED patterns, the proposed method reduces depth RMSE from 10.28 mm to 4.94 mm (~52% improvement) and increases inlier percentage from 89% to 93%, while cutting per-pattern exposure from 20 s to 0.2 s (100×) by multiplexing multiple LEDs simultaneously. Against Micro Phase Shifting [16] (Gupta & Nayar, CVPR 2012), a representative spatial-only structured-light method, the improvement is even larger: RMSE drops from 22.03 mm to 4.94 mm and inlier rate rises from 64% to 93%. For reflectance, the method ties [43]: both produce GGX parameter maps whose relighting results closely match photographs under novel lighting, with no quantitative reflectance metric reported. The adaptive variant's advantage over the non-adaptive variant is modest (93% vs. 94% inliers), suggesting that the primary gain comes from the multiplexed multi-LED illumination rather than the per-object pattern optimization itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 4D spatial-angular structured light hardware proposed by Xu et al. [43], which unified spatial (geometry) and angular (reflectance) illumination in a single LED-array-plus-LCD-mask setup but captured the two modalities sequentially with pre-optimized patterns. The key departure is a differentiable, on-the-fly optimization loop that jointly adapts the next light/mask patterns to reduce per-pixel depth uncertainty, replacing the fixed schedule of [43] with an object-specific acquisition strategy. It also draws on the broader adaptive structured-light literature (information-gain pattern selection [35], auto-tuned patterns [4, 32]) and adaptive reflectance sampling [12, 23], but is the first to unify both spatial and angular adaptation in a single differentiable framework for simultaneous shape-and-reflectance capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for single-view digitization pipelines: a 100× reduction in per-pattern exposure time (via multi-LED multiplexing) and a 2× reduction in total acquisition time make high-quality shape-and-reflectance capture substantially more accessible. The finding that depth-uncertainty-minimizing patterns are sufficient to also recover high-quality GGX reflectance (without explicit reflectance-aware optimization) simplifies the acquisition objective and suggests the spatial disambiguation signal carries enough angular information as a by-product. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-view or free-form scanning, whether indirect illumination can be incorporated into the uncertainty model, and whether more expressive representations (e.g., Gaussian Splatting) can replace the depth-map-plus-parametric-BRDF output.
