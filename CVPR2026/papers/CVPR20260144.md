## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for accelerating diffusion transformers rely on either simple feature reuse or training-free polynomial forecasting, both of which assume smooth, uniform temporal feature evolution. However, the paper demonstrates that diffusion features exhibit strongly stage-dependent dynamics—drastic changes in high-noise stages, stable evolution in mid stages, and fine-grained refinement in low-noise stages—making a single fixed prediction scheme inadequate and causing quality collapse at high acceleration ratios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260144/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the LESA predictor consumes the cached feature vectors from the previous K timesteps (K=4 in the high-noise stage, K=8 in mid/low-noise stages) concatenated into a single vector, along with the relative timestep offsets Δt_i = t_pred − t_i for all L timesteps in the sequence. It outputs a predicted feature h_{t-1} for the next denoising step via a residual update, allowing the full DiT forward pass to be skipped for N−1 out of every N steps. The end-to-end system takes a text prompt and produces a generated image (evaluated on DrawBench) or video (evaluated on HunyuanVideo benchmarks).

## Method

LESA partitions the denoising trajectory into three noise-level stages and assigns a dedicated KAN-based expert predictor to each, with stage-specific temporal window lengths. Each predictor computes a linear projection z = W[h_{t+K-1}, …, h_t] + b over the cached feature sequence, and a Kolmogorov-Arnold Network f_KAN(Δt) that maps the relative timestep vector to a scalar modulation factor α, then forms the residual prediction ĥ_{t-1} = h_t + αz. Training proceeds in two stages: first, GT-Guided Training minimizes L1 loss between predicted and ground-truth features using clean cached trajectories; second, Closed-Loop Autoregressive Training conditions the predictor on its own accumulated predictions to build robustness against the drift that occurs during multi-step accelerated inference. This separation-of-variables design—linear spatial projection plus KAN-based scalar temporal modulation—yields a parameter-efficient predictor that adapts its update strength across the denoising trajectory without requiring per-step network evaluations.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, LESA achieves a 5.00× speedup (N=7) with PSNR 30.17, SSIM 0.72, and LPIPS 0.32, outperforming TaylorSeer (N=6, O=2) which reaches only 4.99× with PSNR 28.94 and LPIPS 0.40. At the highest acceleration of 6.25× (N=10), LESA maintains PSNR 29.65 and LPIPS 0.40, while TaylorSeer (N=9, O=2) degrades to PSNR 28.38 and LPIPS 0.51. On Qwen-Image at 6.25×, LESA achieves PSNR 29.23 versus TaylorSeer's 28.14 (a 20.2% relative improvement), and on HunyuanVideo at 5.00×, LESA reaches PSNR 21.43 versus TaylorSeer's 17.29 (a 24.7% improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LESA consistently outperforms all five baselines at comparable or higher acceleration ratios. Against TaylorSeer [17], the primary forecasting baseline, LESA achieves 1.23 dB higher PSNR (30.17 vs 28.94) at 5× speedup on FLUX.1-dev, and the gap widens to 1.27 dB (29.65 vs 28.38) at 6.25×, with LPIPS reduced from 0.51 to 0.40. Against FORA [31], LESA (N=5) surpasses FORA (N=3) in both speed (3.85× vs 2.94× FLOPs) and perceptual quality (PSNR 30.96 vs 30.65, LPIPS 0.24 vs 0.25). Against TeaCache [14], LESA (N=5) delivers 3.85× FLOPs speedup versus TeaCache's 2.78×, with substantially better PSNR (30.96 vs 29.03) and LPIPS (0.24 vs 0.40). On HunyuanVideo, LESA (N=7) achieves PSNR 21.43 and LPIPS 0.29, outperforming SpeCa [18] (PSNR 17.73, LPIPS 0.39) and ClusCa [45] (PSNR 17.28, LPIPS 0.42) by 21–24% in PSNR while also achieving higher wall-clock speed (5.11× vs 4.26× and 4.06× respectively). Notably, LESA is the only method that maintains PSNR above 29 on FLUX.1-dev and above 21 on HunyuanVideo at ≥5× acceleration, whereas all baselines marked with † show significant quality collapse at their highest acceleration settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching paradigm for diffusion model acceleration, departing from both 'cache-then-reuse' methods (DeepCache, FORA) that assume minimal inter-step variation and 'cache-then-forecast' methods (TaylorSeer, TeaCache, SpeCa) that assume smooth, continuous temporal evolution. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that diffusion feature dynamics are stage-dependent—drastic in high-noise, stable in mid-noise, and fine-grained in low-noise regimes—and that a single fixed prediction scheme cannot capture this heterogeneity. LESA replaces training-free polynomial extrapolation with a learnable KAN-based predictor and introduces a multi-expert architecture with dedicated predictors per noise stage, combined with a two-stage training protocol (ground-truth guided followed by closed-loop autoregressive) to handle accumulated prediction drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 5–6× acceleration on production-scale models (FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, HunyuanVideo) with only 1% quality drop, and 20–25% PSNR gains over the previous SOTA (TaylorSeer) at high acceleration ratios, making near-real-time generation more feasible. The stage-aware decomposition provides a principled explanation for why prior uniform caching strategies degrade at high speedup, and the closed-loop autoregressive training addresses a real exposure-bias problem that training-free methods cannot. Open questions include the training cost and data requirements for each new base model, whether the stage boundaries generalize across architectures without re-tuning, and whether even higher acceleration ratios (beyond 6.25×) remain tractable with this framework.
