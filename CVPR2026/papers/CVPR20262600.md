## Problem

Current approaches to motion understanding either model per-pixel video (conflating kinematics with appearance at enormous compute cost) or operate on sparse, non-generalizable representations like optical flow or individual tracks. No compact, semantically structured motion space exists that supports efficient, goal-conditioned generation of long-horizon kinematics across open-domain scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262600/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a start frame (encoded as a DINO feature map), a set of sparse tracker-derived trajectories (x,y coordinates in a normalized [-1,1] grid, 64 timesteps each), and a conditioning signal (BERT text embeddings for robotics tasks, or spatial pokes specifying target positions and timesteps). It produces a 16×16×D latent motion grid with 64× temporal compression, which can be decoded via query tokens into dense trajectories at arbitrary spatial positions, or fed to a policy head to predict robot actions. Metrics are computed with tracks scaled to [0,128].

## Method

A two-stage framework: (1) a 340M-parameter LLaMA-style transformer VAE encodes Fourier-embedded trajectory tokens (with 3D RoPE encoding time index and start position) and start-frame DINO features into a 16×16×D latent grid via global self-attention with interleaved cross-attention, trained with a β-VAE objective combining L1 autoencoding, masked reconstruction at held-out query points, and KL regularization; (2) a 530M-parameter conditional flow-matching transformer generates motion latents by learning a vector field from N(0,I) to the empirical latent distribution, conditioned on text or pokes via cross-attention. The key insight is that aggressive 64× temporal compression (aggregating 64 frames into one latent token) paradoxically improves generation quality and semantic structure (measured by kNN retrieval accuracy) while reducing token count, enabling orders-of-magnitude faster inference than video models.

## Results

On the open-domain poked motion generation task (Pexels), the proposed method achieves a mean endpoint error (EPE) of 0.5 with a single poke, compared to 429.7 for Motion-I2V and 20.9 for Track2Act (dense), while also achieving the lowest Min MSE (41.0) and Mean MSE (57.9). On the LIBERO robotics benchmark, the method reaches 79.6% average success rate, substantially outperforming ATM (60.4%) and Tra-MoE (61.4%). Against state-of-the-art video models, the method matches or exceeds Wan (Min MSE 27.08 vs. 28.67) and Veo 3 (Min MSE 27.08 vs. 36.18) in just 1 second of inference time, compared to ~1 hour for Wan, while generating >10k samples in the time-matched regime versus 1 sample for video baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Motion-I2V [34] (flow-based), the proposed method wins decisively on all metrics at every poke density: with 1 poke, EPE drops from 429.7 to 57.9 and Mean MSE from 135.7 to 41.0, a >7× improvement in endpoint adherence. Against Track2Act [6] (track-based, dense-only), the method reduces Min MSE from 138.7 to 30.4 and EPE from 20.9 to 44.1 (note: Track2Act's EPE is higher due to its end-frame conditioning paradigm). On LIBERO, the method outperforms ATM [45] by 19.2 points in average success rate (79.6% vs. 60.4%) and Tra-MoE [47] by 18.9 points (80.3% vs. 61.4%), with the largest gains on the Object task (99.0% vs. 68.0% and 91.0% vs. 73.5%). Against video models Wan [43] and Veo 3 [13], the method achieves lower Min MSE (27.08 vs. 28.67 and 36.18), lower Mean MSE (39.53 vs. 57.02 and 94.00), and lower EPE (1.17 vs. 4.68 and 6.21) in the sample-matched regime, while being ~3600× faster (1 s vs. 1 h). In the time-matched regime, the advantage widens dramatically: the method generates >10k samples achieving Min MSE 21.29, whereas Wan and Veo 3 produce only 1 sample each with Min MSE 64.20 and 65.99 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of trajectory autoencoding (WHN, An Uncertain Future), motion-conditioned video generation (Motion I2V, MOFA, iPOKE), and goal-conditioned trajectory prediction for robotics (ATM, Traj-MoE, Track2Act). Its key departure is introducing a dedicated latent motion space learned via a β-VAE with extreme 64× temporal compression, decoupling kinematics from both pixel-level video latents and sparse tracker outputs. The second stage then applies conditional flow matching directly in this motion space, borrowing the generative framework from diffusion/flow-matching literature but operating on a far lower-dimensional, semantically structured representation rather than video or flow tensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The orders-of-magnitude efficiency gain (1 s vs. ~1 h for Wan) makes goal-conditioned motion reasoning practical in settings where video generation is infeasible, and the finding that aggressive temporal compression *improves* rather than degrades motion quality challenges the assumption that fine-grained temporal resolution is necessary for capturing dynamics. Limitations include dependence on tracker quality for supervision, 2D-only formulation, and the fact that video-model comparisons require post-hoc tracking of generated frames, introducing measurement noise. Open questions include whether the motion space extends naturally to 3D, whether it can support closed-loop planning beyond the rolling-horizon setup tested here, and how it interacts with appearance generation when full video output is eventually desired.
