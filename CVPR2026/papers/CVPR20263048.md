## Problem

Stereo foundation models like FoundationStereo achieve strong zero-shot generalization but run at ~500 ms per frame, making them unusable in latency-bound systems. Conversely, real-time stereo architectures sacrifice the rich monocular and stereo priors of foundation models and require costly per-domain fine-tuning. No prior method simultaneously delivers foundation-model-level zero-shot accuracy and real-time frame rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263048/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a rectified left-right RGB stereo image pair and outputs a dense disparity map with no disparity-range constraint at inference. Internally it constructs a cost volume with a maximum disparity of 192 and performs 8 ConvGRU refinement iterations. On an NVIDIA 3090 GPU the full pipeline runs in ~49 ms (~21 ms with TensorRT), compared to ~496 ms for the FoundationStereo teacher.

## Method

Fast-FoundationStereo applies a divide-and-conquer acceleration to FoundationStereo's three stages. First, knowledge distillation compresses the hybrid DepthAnythingV2 + side-tuning CNN backbone into a single efficient student (e.g., EdgeNeXt or MobileNetV2) trained to match the teacher's multi-level feature pyramid via MSE loss. Second, the cost-filtering network is split into N local blocks; each block's candidates are trained independently via distillation, and an Integer Linear Programming combinatorial search selects the best block combination under a user-specified latency budget, reducing search complexity from O(n^N) to O(n). Third, structured pruning guided by a recurrent dependency graph of the ConvGRU refinement module removes redundant channels (ranked by first-order Taylor importance), followed by end-to-end retraining with a weighted disparity + feature-distillation loss. Training is supplemented with 1.4 M pseudo-labeled in-the-wild stereo pairs from Stereo4D, validated by a normal-map consistency check between the teacher's disparity and a monocular depth estimate.

## Results

Fast-FoundationStereo achieves 49 ms runtime (21 ms with TensorRT) on an NVIDIA 3090, running over 10× faster than FoundationStereo (496 ms) while maintaining competitive zero-shot accuracy: Middlebury-Q BP-2 of 2.12 vs. 1.30, ETH3D BP-1 of 1.22 vs. 0.50, and KITTI-2015 D1 of 3.25 vs. 2.80. Among real-time methods (26–45 ms), it outperforms the best competitor RT-IGEV by a wide margin (e.g., Middlebury-H BP-2: 2.20 vs. 12.75; ETH3D BP-1: 1.22 vs. 5.05). On the Booster-Q non-Lambertian benchmark, it achieves EPE of 1.54 px, substantially better than the best real-time baseline RT-IGEV† (4.20 px) and competitive with the slow FoundationStereo (1.13 px).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generalizable baseline FoundationStereo [68], Fast-FoundationStereo runs 10× faster (49 ms vs. 496 ms) at a modest accuracy cost (e.g., Middlebury-Q BP-2: 2.12 vs. 1.30; ETH3D BP-1: 1.22 vs. 0.50). It outperforms MonSter [7] (336 ms) on Middlebury-Q BP-2 (2.12 vs. 3.19) and ETH3D BP-1 (1.22 vs. 0.99 is a slight loss), and beats StereoAnywhere [2] (427 ms) on most metrics (Middlebury-Q BP-2: 2.12 vs. 3.25; KITTI-15 D1: 3.25 vs. 3.52). Among real-time methods, the gap is dramatic: the best real-time baseline RT-IGEV [74] (45 ms) scores Middlebury-H BP-2 of 12.75 and ETH3D BP-1 of 5.05, versus 2.20 and 1.22 for Fast-FoundationStereo — a 5–6× improvement. On the Booster-Q non-Lambertian benchmark (Table 2), Fast-FoundationStereo (EPE 1.54 px) is 2.7× better than RT-IGEV† (4.20 px) and within 0.4 px of the slow FoundationStereo (1.13 px), while also outperforming StereoAnywhere (1.21 px EPE but 427 ms runtime). The paper also shows that retraining real-time baselines on the same large-scale data (including pseudo-labels) narrows but does not close the gap, confirming the architectural advantage of inheriting foundation-model priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FoundationStereo [68], a foundation-model-based stereo matcher that combines DepthAnythingV2 monocular priors with a side-tuning CNN, a dual-branch cost filtering network (3D hourglass + Disparity Transformer), and a ConvGRU refinement module. While prior work has either pursued zero-shot generalization at the cost of prohibitive latency (MonSter, StereoAnywhere, ZeroStereo) or real-time speed at the cost of per-domain fine-tuning (LightStereo, RT-IGEV, BANet), this paper is the first to systematically accelerate a foundation stereo model to real-time frame rate via a divide-and-conquer strategy: knowledge distillation for the hybrid backbone, blockwise neural architecture search for cost filtering, and structured pruning for the refinement module. The blockwise NAS formulation, which reduces search complexity from O(n^N) to O(n) by training each block independently via distillation and then solving a combinatorial ILP, departs from both standard evolutionary NAS and direct end-to-end distillation of the full cost filtering network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving comparable zero-shot accuracy to FoundationStereo at over 10× the speed (49 ms vs. 496 ms, or 21 ms with TensorRT), this work makes foundation-level stereo matching viable for latency-bound applications such as robotics and augmented reality, where prior real-time methods required costly per-domain fine-tuning. The pseudo-labeling pipeline on 1.4M in-the-wild stereo pairs is a reusable data curation technique that benefits all stereo methods, not just the proposed architecture. Open questions include whether quantization (identified as orthogonal future work) can push inference onto resource-constrained edge devices, and whether the blockwise NAS and structured pruning strategies generalize to other multi-stage vision foundation models beyond stereo matching.
