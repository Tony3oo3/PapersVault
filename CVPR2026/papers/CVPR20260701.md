## Problem

Existing mechanistic interpretability methods for CLIP (e.g., SAEs, TextSpan) rely on model activations collected from large datasets, making them dataset-dependent, vulnerable to data bias, and limited to coarse head-level or sample-level explanations. No prior approach provides input-independent, fine-grained interpretations of individual computational directions within an attention head without requiring any data or training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260701/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SITH takes as input the Value-Output (VO) weight matrix W_VO^h = W_V^h W_O^h of each CLIP ViT attention head (a D×D matrix, D=768 for ViT-L/14) and an overcomplete textual concept pool (e.g., ConceptNet 5.5) encoded by CLIP's text encoder. The singular vectors are first projected into CLIP's shared multimodal embedding space via the learned projection W_p before concept matching. The output is a sparse, non-negative coefficient vector of length K (typically 5) over the concept pool for each singular vector, plus the option to rescale or zero out specific singular values to produce an edited model.

## Method

SITH applies Singular Value Decomposition to each attention head's VO matrix, isolating the dominant input/output directions (left/right singular vectors) that characterize what the head reads from and writes to the residual stream. Each right singular vector, projected into CLIP's multimodal space, is then decomposed via COMP (Coherent Orthogonal Matching Pursuit), a greedy sparse coding algorithm that extends Non-Negative Orthogonal Matching Pursuit by adding a coherence regularizer to the concept selection score: score(γ_i) = ⟨r_{k-1}, γ_i⟩ + (λ/|S_{k-1}|) Σ_{j∈S_{k-1}} ⟨γ_i, γ_j⟩. This coherence term encourages selecting concepts that are semantically related to those already chosen, producing interpretable concept sets (e.g., 'pink red', 'red telephone', 'scarlet reds') rather than disjointed reconstructions. The entire pipeline is data-free and training-free, requiring only the pretrained weights and a concept dictionary. This fine-grained decomposition enables precise weight-space interventions—amplifying or suppressing individual singular values—to mitigate spurious correlations, remove NSFW concepts, and improve classification accuracy without any retraining.

## Results

On Waterbirds, SITH achieves 82.7% overall and 70.6% worst-group zero-shot accuracy after suppressing background-related singular vectors, outperforming both the unmodified OpenCLIP (73.5%/47.9%) and TextSpan-based head ablation (81.8%/68.0%). On ViSU retrieval, SITH-modified CLIP reaches 74.5% R@10 for safe text-to-image queries, surpassing the training-based Safe-CLIP (69.2%), while maintaining competitive performance on unsafe queries (40.4% vs 62.3% for Safe-CLIP). For task-aware singular value amplification, SITH yields consistent gains of up to 1.0 points over OpenCLIP across Flowers 102 (77.5 vs 76.5), FGVC-Aircraft (36.9 vs 36.6), and DTD (50.9 vs 50.1), while random scaling degrades performance. In the interpretability-fidelity analysis, COMP (λ=0.3, K=5) achieves the best trade-off between reconstruction cosine similarity and LLM-rated coherence compared to NNOMP and top-k baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TextSpan [23], the primary competing interpretability method, SITH wins on Waterbirds by 0.9 points overall (82.7% vs 81.8%) and 2.6 points on worst-group accuracy (70.6% vs 68.0%), demonstrating the advantage of surgically editing individual singular vectors within a head rather than ablating entire heads. On ViSU NSFW retrieval, SITH outperforms the training-based Safe-CLIP [57] on safe queries (74.5% vs 69.2% for text-to-image, 77.3% vs 73.9% for image-to-text) but trails significantly on unsafe queries (29.5% vs 46.3% for text-to-image), indicating that weight-space suppression is less effective at fully erasing unsafe concept associations than dedicated training. In the interpretability-fidelity ablation (Fig. 3), COMP with λ=0.3 achieves higher LLM-rated coherence than NNOMP [55] at equivalent reconstruction fidelity, while top-k selection produces coherent but incomplete reconstructions that fail to improve with larger K. Random ablation of the same number of singular values consistently degrades performance across all three application settings (Waterbirds, ViSU, and classification), confirming that the gains are attributable to SITH's concept-guided selection rather than generic weight perturbation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SITH builds directly on the residual-stream decomposition framework of Elhage et al., which expresses each attention head's computation through its value-output (VO) matrix, and on TextSpan (Gandelsman et al.), which aligned head-level activations with textual concepts. The key departure is shifting from activation-based, data-dependent, head-granularity interpretation to a weight-based, data-free, intra-head (singular-vector) analysis. The COMP algorithm extends classical Non-Negative Orthogonal Matching Pursuit by adding a semantic coherence regularizer, addressing the incoherence problem that plagues naive sparse coding in embedding spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SITH provides a practical, zero-data pathway to interpret and surgically edit CLIP's internal representations, which is valuable as VLMs are deployed at scale and accountability demands grow. The finding that fine-tuning reweights a stable semantic basis rather than learning new features offers a useful lens for understanding transfer and adaptation. However, the analysis is limited to the VO matrices of the last four layers of a single model (ViT-L/14), and the QK interaction that determines attention routing remains unaddressed; extending to FFN layers, other architectures, and larger models is an open question.
