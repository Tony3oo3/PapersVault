## Problem

Existing image editing methods operate on a per-image basis, producing inconsistent results when the same edit must be applied across a set of related images with varying pose, viewpoint, and spatial layout. Optimization-based propagation approaches suffer from poor generalization and artifacts, while recent attention-based methods are limited to small image sets and fail in geometrically complex scenes (e.g., tracking a logo through rotation or identifying a specific eye across viewpoints). The core gap is the absence of a training-based framework that establishes reliable cross-image correspondences sufficient for coherent group-level editing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of T related images (treated as pseudo-video frames at 528×528 resolution), binary segmentation masks specifying the edit region in each image, and a text prompt describing the desired modification. VGGT is run over the full image set to produce explicit dense correspondence tokens that are injected alongside the latent tokens. The output is a group of T consistently edited images where the same semantic modification is applied coherently across all views, decoded from the video diffusion model's latent space.

## Method

GroupEditing reformulates a set of related images as pseudo-video frames and fine-tunes the WAN-2.1 video diffusion model to inherit its temporal coherence priors for cross-image consistency. Two complementary correspondence mechanisms are fused: (1) Ge-RoPE warps the spatial RoPE grid indices using VGGT-derived displacement fields (smoothed with a 2D Gaussian kernel) so that latent token positions align with geometric structure, and (2) Identity-RoPE normalizes spatial coordinates relative to each image's object bounding rectangle, ensuring corresponding regions share identical positional encodings regardless of absolute position. VGGT dense tokens are concatenated with latent tokens inside each transformer block for joint self-attention, then truncated back to latent dimensionality, allowing cross-view geometric constraints to influence editing without changing the output space. The model is trained end-to-end on GroupEditData (7,517 image groups with masks and captions) using AdamW at 1e-4 learning rate on 8 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On GroupEditBench (800 image groups), GroupEditing achieves the best score on every metric in both local and global editing settings. For local editing, it reaches a CLIP-Score of 0.3122, Aesthetic-Score of 5.39, DINO-Score of 0.8168, Editing Consistency of 0.9239, and PSNR of 0.7624, while also attaining the best (lowest) user-study ranks across identity consistency (1.67), aesthetic (1.46), appearance fidelity (1.50), and overall quality (1.47). For global editing, it scores 0.2987 CLIP-Score, 5.48 Aesthetic-Score, 0.9287 DINO-Score, and 0.9147 Editing Consistency, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GroupEditing outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both local and global editing. Against Anydoor [8], the gap is the largest: GroupEditing improves CLIP-Score by 0.0394 (0.3122 vs 0.2728), DINO-Score by 0.0960, and Editing Consistency by 0.0542, while Anydoor ranks last in the user study (overall rank 3.32 vs 1.47). Against OminiControl [51], GroupEditing leads on all four automatic metrics (e.g., DINO-Score 0.8168 vs 0.7326, Editing Consistency 0.9239 vs 0.8676) and on all four user-study dimensions, with the most notable margin in appearance fidelity (1.50 vs 1.96). Against Edicho [3], the closest competitor, GroupEditing still wins on every metric—CLIP-Score 0.3122 vs 0.3059, DINO-Score 0.8168 vs 0.8080, Editing Consistency 0.9239 vs 0.8988, and PSNR 0.7624 vs 0.6935—and achieves a better overall user-study rank (1.47 vs 1.95). In global editing (Table 2), GroupEditing surpasses StyleAligned [17] by 0.0077 in CLIP-Score and 0.0428 in DINO-Score, and beats Edicho [3] by 0.0288 in DINO-Score and 0.0288 in Editing Consistency, confirming consistent superiority across editing types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on WAN-2.1, a large-scale video diffusion model, by reformulating a set of related images as pseudo-video frames to inherit temporal coherence priors. It departs from prior single-image editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, Paint-by-Example) and optimization-based propagation approaches by introducing a training-based framework that fuses explicit geometric correspondences from VGGT with implicit video-model priors through a novel RoPE-based injection mechanism. The closest prior work—Edicho and Masactrl—achieves consistency via attention-based semantic matching at inference time but is limited to small image sets; GroupEditing generalizes this to arbitrary-sized groups with a dedicated training pipeline and explicit geometric alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper introduces the first dedicated dataset (GroupEditData, >7K groups) and benchmark (GroupEditBench, 800 groups) for group-level image editing, filling a gap where prior methods lacked standardized evaluation. The pseudo-video reformulation offers a reusable conceptual framework for transferring video-model temporal priors to static multi-image tasks, with demonstrated downstream benefits for 3D reconstruction and image customization. However, all training and evaluation data is synthetically generated by Gemini 2.5, leaving open how well the method generalizes to real-world image groups with uncontrolled geometric variation, occlusion, and domain shift. The reliance on VGGT for explicit correspondences also raises questions about robustness in scenes where dense matching degrades.
