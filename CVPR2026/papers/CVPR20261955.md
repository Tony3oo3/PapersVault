## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based human avatar methods treat Gaussians as free-floating entities with independently optimized geometric attributes, fundamentally lacking topological coherence with the underlying mesh. This causes Gaussian detachment and surface holes under out-of-distribution poses, and even partial constraints (e.g., fixing rotation but leaving scale unregulated) remain insufficient for robust animation. The paper argues that scale, in particular, is critical for adapting to complex unseen poses but has been excluded from topological constraints in prior work.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of a clothed person and SMPL pose parameters as input, producing a personalized deformable mesh (SMPL template + learned per-vertex offsets via multi-resolution hash encoding) and a fixed set of M+N Gaussians (one per triangular face plus one per vertex) whose only learned parameters are second-order spherical harmonic color coefficients. All geometric attributes—position, rotation, and scale—are computed analytically at render time from the posed mesh, so no per-pose Gaussian optimization or adaptive densification is needed. The output supports real-time novel view and pose synthesis via standard 3DGS alpha-blending.

## Method

Tavatar replaces free-form Gaussian optimization with an analytical binding mechanism: Face Gaussians are placed at triangle incenters, oriented along face normals, and scaled by the triangle inradius (β·r), while Vertex Gaussians are anchored at vertices, oriented by area-weighted adjacent-face normals, and scaled by the minimum one-ring edge length (γ·min_edge). The underlying mesh is a personalized SMPL deformed via Linear Blend Skinning, providing the stable topological scaffold from which all Gaussian geometry is derived. To prevent LBS-induced triangle degeneracy from corrupting the analytical scale/rotation mapping, the paper introduces an equilateral regularization that penalizes edge-length variance and angular deviation from 60° across all faces. Training jointly optimizes the shape encoder and SH color coefficients under photometric (L1+SSIM) and pseudo-normal losses, with the equilateral and mesh-smoothness regularizers, on a single RTX-3090 for 2,000 iterations.

## Results

On geometric accuracy (Table 3), Tavatar achieves the best Normal error across both datasets: 1.687 on PeopleSnapshot and 1.772 on X-Avatar, reducing normal error by 17.9% and 13.8% respectively over the best baseline (IHuman: 2.055/2.056). It also attains the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.111) and Point-to-Surface Distance (0.107) on X-Avatar. On rendering quality, Tavatar achieves the best average PSNR on X-Avatar (29.03, 25.24, 26.82, 28.11 across four subjects) and competitive scores on PeopleSnapshot, where GART slightly edges ahead on simple in-distribution rotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tavatar outperforms all four baselines on every geometric metric. Against IHuman (the closest competitor on geometry), it reduces normal error by 17.9% on PeopleSnapshot (1.687 vs. 2.055) and 13.8% on X-Avatar (1.772 vs. 2.056), while also improving CD (0.111 vs. 0.132) and P2S (0.107 vs. 0.126). GART achieves the highest PSNR on PeopleSnapshot's simple rotations (30.21 on male-3-casual) but suffers the worst geometric accuracy (Normal 4.263/3.531), confirming that free-floating Gaussians overfit to training poses. GomAvatar is competitive on rendering (PSNR 28.27 on male-3) but its lack of mesh quality regularization leads to unstable scaling under OOD poses. InstantAvatar (NeRF-based) is consistently the weakest across both photometric and geometric metrics, reflecting the computational and generalization limitations of volumetric rendering for articulated humans.

## Relation to Prior Work

Tavatar builds on the SMPL parametric body model as a topological scaffold and 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering backbone, but departs fundamentally from prior 3DGS-based avatar methods (GART, IHuman, GomAvatar) in how Gaussian geometric attributes are determined. Where existing approaches either freely optimize rotation and scale or apply only partial constraints (e.g., IHuman constrains orientation via mesh normals but leaves scale unregulated), Tavatar analytically derives all geometric attributes—position, rotation, and scale—directly from local mesh geometry (inradii for face Gaussians, edge lengths for vertex Gaussians). This shifts the paradigm from optimization-based to geometry-driven attribute determination, with an equilateral regularization term ensuring the underlying mesh remains well-conditioned for stable analytical mapping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and persistent failure mode in animatable avatar reconstruction: Gaussian detachment and surface holes under out-of-distribution poses, which degrades immersion in VR, gaming, and film applications. The topology-aware binding provides a principled mechanism for pose generalization without per-pose optimization, and the equilateral regularization offers a reusable technique for maintaining mesh quality in any mesh-Gaussian hybrid pipeline. Key limitations include dependence on SMPL fitting quality and inability to handle dynamic clothing deformation; the authors identify physics-based garment simulation as a natural next step. An open question is whether the fixed Gaussian-to-mesh correspondence (no adaptive densification) limits expressiveness for highly detailed or non-rigid surfaces.
