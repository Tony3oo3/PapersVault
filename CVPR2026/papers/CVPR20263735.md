## Problem

Standard VAEs use continuous latent variables, and their discrete-count extensions (Poisson VAEs) impose an equal mean-variance constraint that fails to capture the overdispersion prevalent in real neural spike trains. This coupling limits the flexibility of the latent space, leading to underestimated uncertainty and reduced representational expressiveness for generative modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263735/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes natural images at various resolutions (28×28 for MNIST/Fashion-MNIST, 16×16 for CIFAR, 64×64 for CelebA) and produces a 256-dimensional vector of discrete non-negative integer spike counts as the latent code, which is then decoded into a reconstructed or generated image. The encoder outputs two modulation vectors δ_r(x) and δ_p(x) that scale the prior NB parameters, while the decoder is a Gaussian with a fixed variance hyperparameter σ² that controls the reconstruction-regularization trade-off.

## Method

NegBio-VAE replaces the Poisson latent with a Negative Binomial distribution NB(z; r, p), where r is the size parameter and p is the dispersion parameter, allowing variance to exceed mean and thus modeling overdispersed spike counts. Two technical challenges are addressed: (1) the KL divergence between two NB distributions has no closed form, so the paper proposes two strategies—Monte Carlo estimation of the log-density ratio, or 'dispersion sharing' that ties the size parameter r between prior and posterior to recover an analytic KL; (2) reparameterized sampling exploits the NB-as-Gamma-Poisson mixture identity, sampling λ from a Gamma via implicit reparameterization gradients and then z from a Poisson via either Gumbel-Softmax relaxation over a truncated support or continuous-time simulation with temperature-smoothed inter-arrival times. The resulting ELBO is optimized end-to-end with convolutional encoder-decoder networks, and four variants (MC-G, MC-C, DS-G, DS-C) are evaluated across reconstruction, generation, and downstream latent-space tasks.

## Results

On MNIST, NegBio-VAE (MC-G) achieves the best FID@5k of 79.67 and KID of 0.0892, substantially outperforming P-VAE (FID 105.37, KID 0.1250) and G-VAE (FID 152.51, KID 0.1788). On CIFAR-16×16, MC-G reduces FID@5k to 40.28 (vs. 60.37 for P-VAE and 72.07 for G-VAE), while DS-C achieves the best reconstruction with MSE 0.0189 and SSIM 0.8089. On CelebA-64, DS-G attains the lowest FID@5k of 84.30 and KID of 0.0992, edging out P-VAE (FID 88.23, KID 0.0985). The MC-C variant delivers the best reconstruction on MNIST (MSE 0.0123, SSIM 0.8661) and Fashion-MNIST (MSE 0.0152, SSIM 0.7331).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against G-VAE (continuous Gaussian latent), NegBio-VAE wins decisively on all four datasets: FID@5k drops from 152.51 to 79.67 on MNIST, from 179.81 to 127.52 on Fashion-MNIST, from 72.07 to 40.28 on CIFAR-16×16, and from 195.14 to 84.30 on CelebA-64, with KID improvements of similar magnitude. Against C-VAE (categorical latent), NegBio-VAE achieves lower FID and KID on every dataset (e.g., FID 79.67 vs. 135.45 on MNIST; 40.28 vs. 89.42 on CIFAR-16×16), while C-VAE retains a slight edge on reconstruction MSE for MNIST (0.0222 vs. 0.0156 for MC-G, but MC-C matches at 0.0123). Against P-VAE (Poisson latent, the closest baseline), NegBio-VAE consistently improves FID and KID: FID@5k of 79.67 vs. 105.37 on MNIST (24% reduction), 127.52 vs. 145.98 on Fashion-MNIST, 40.28 vs. 60.37 on CIFAR-16×16 (33% reduction), and 84.30 vs. 88.23 on CelebA-64. On reconstruction, P-VAE and NegBio-VAE are closely matched (e.g., MSE 0.0125 vs. 0.0123 on MNIST), confirming that the NB dispersion parameter primarily benefits generative quality rather than pixel-level fidelity. The MC-G variant (Monte Carlo KL + Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization) is the strongest overall for generation, while DS-C (Dispersion Sharing + Continuous-time) excels at reconstruction, particularly on CIFAR-16×16 where it achieves SSIM 0.8089 versus 0.6791 for P-VAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Poisson VAE (P-VAE) of Vafaii et al. by replacing the Poisson latent distribution with a Negative Binomial distribution, introducing a dispersion parameter that decouples mean and variance to model overdispersed spike counts. It inherits the discrete-latent VAE lineage (categorical VAEs, Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization) and the brain-inspired generative modeling tradition, but departs from P-VAE's restrictive equidispersion assumption. The two technical contributions—Monte Carlo / dispersion-sharing KL estimation and Gamma-then-Poisson reparameterization via implicit gradients plus Gumbel-Softmax or continuous-time simulation—compose existing tools in a new way to make the NB latent tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Overdispersion is a well-documented property of cortical spike trains, so the NB latent is a biologically grounded correction to the Poisson assumption that has been the default in discrete VAEs. The paper's main open question is whether the dispersion-sharing constraint (tying the rate parameter between prior and posterior) is the right inductive bias or whether fully free NB posteriors with better KL estimators would yield further gains. The framework is currently single-layer; extending it to hierarchical or spatially structured latents (à la NVAE) is identified as the natural next step, and the practical impact will depend on whether the modest FID improvements over P-VAE persist at larger scales and in multi-layer settings.
