## Problem

Existing low-light pose estimation approaches face two coupled failures: (1) handcrafted or learning-based augmentations (e.g., CycleGAN, StyleID) produce unrealistic low-light images that lack the complex high-frequency noise patterns of real low-light scenes, and (2) modern DETR-based pose estimators rigidly sum image cues with pose priors via a fixed residual connection, so unreliable visual features still dominate predictions when keypoints are barely visible in darkness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260328/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the framework consumes well-lit RGB images with pose annotations (from ExLPose or CrowdPose) alongside unlabeled low-light reference images to synthesize augmented low-light training data via Stable Diffusion (SD-2.1-base, DDIM with 50 steps). At inference, a single low-light RGB image is fed to the ED-Pose backbone (Swin-T), which outputs per-person bounding boxes and 2D keypoint coordinates evaluated under the COCO AP/AR protocol at IoU thresholds 0.5:0.95.

## Method

UDAPose synthesizes low-light images by modifying the VAE decoder of Stable Diffusion: a Direct-Current-based High-Pass Filter (DHF) extracts high-frequency details from unlabeled low-light references via FFT and re-centers them to the reference's mean brightness to avoid clipping-induced information loss, while a Low-Light Characteristics Injection Module (LCIM) injects multi-scale processed high-frequency features into four convolution blocks of the decoder, trained with a composite spatial + frequency-domain reconstruction loss. For the pose estimator, a Dynamic Control of Attention (DCA) module replaces the rigid summation of pose-prior and image-cue latents in each deformable decoder layer with a learned per-keypoint softmax weighting produced by a two-layer MLP over the concatenated features, enabling the model to rely more on learned pose priors when a keypoint is occluded by darkness and more on image cues when it is visible.

## Results

On ExLPose-test, UDAPose achieves 27.0 AP (LL-A), 38.7 AP (LL-N), 28.0 AP (LL-H), and 11.7 AP (LL-E), surpassing the strongest baseline ELLA by 8.4, 6.4, 10.8, and 8.3 AP respectively. The relative gain is most dramatic on the hard subset (LL-H), where UDAPose delivers a 56.4% relative AP improvement over ELLA (28.0 vs. 17.2). In cross-dataset validation on EHPT-XC, UDAPose reaches 31.0 AP@0.5:0.95, outperforming ELLA by 7.4 AP (31.4% relative gain) without any fine-tuning on the target dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UDAPose outperforms all 10 baselines across every low-light subset. Image enhancement methods (RFormer, DarkIR, LightenDiff, QuadPrior) are the weakest, achieving only 4.5–8.9 AP on LL-A and near-zero AP on LL-H, confirming that recovering lost detail is insufficient for pose estimation. Among domain adaptation baselines, CycleGAN (19.6 AP on LL-A) and ELLA (18.6 AP) are the strongest, but both produce unrealistic low-light noise patterns that limit generalization. UDAPose surpasses ELLA by 8.4 AP on LL-A, 10.8 AP on LL-H, and 8.3 AP on LL-E (a 3.4× relative improvement on the extreme subset). On the real-camera ExLPose-OCN benchmark, UDAPose leads ELLA by 5.4 AP (51.4 vs. 46.0). In cross-dataset evaluation on EHPT-XC, UDAPose achieves 31.0 AP versus ELLA's 23.6 AP, demonstrating that the synthesized low-light characteristics transfer to unseen camera hardware and degradation types without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

UDAPose builds on the domain-adaptation line for low-light pose estimation (ELLA, UDA-HE) and the diffusion-based style-injection framework of StyleID (Chung et al.), but departs from generic image-to-image translation by explicitly extracting and injecting high-frequency low-light noise via DHF and LCIM rather than relying on global appearance transfer. On the pose-estimation side, it extends DETR-based one-stage estimators (ED-Pose, Group Pose) by replacing their rigid residual summation of image cues and pose priors with the DCA module, which learns per-keypoint adaptive fusion weights. The work thus sits at the intersection of diffusion-model-based data augmentation and Transformer architecture modification, addressing two previously separate failure modes (unrealistic synthetic degradation and over-reliance on unreliable visual features).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is practically attractive because it requires only unlabeled low-light reference images (easy to collect) plus standard well-lit pose annotations, avoiding the need for paired data or specialized hardware like event cameras. The DCA module addresses a general architectural weakness in DETR-based pose estimators—rigid fusion that cannot down-weight noisy visual cues—which could transfer to other degraded-sensing scenarios beyond low light. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D pose or video pose estimation, whether the Stable Diffusion backbone can be replaced by lighter generators for real-time deployment, and whether the DCA mechanism generalizes to non-DETR architectures (e.g., heatmap-based or top-down methods). The absolute AP on extreme low-light (11.7) remains modest, suggesting the problem is far from solved.
