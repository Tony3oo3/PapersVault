## Problem

Existing OSM-based metric cross-view geo-localization methods assume ideal sunny conditions and rely on narrow-FoV pinhole images, leaving their robustness under adverse weather, lighting, and sensor noise largely unexplored. Additionally, the richer visual information in 360° panoramas has not been leveraged for OSM-based localization, and no large-scale benchmark exists to evaluate robustness in this setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263915/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 360° equirectangular panorama (split internally into three 120° pinhole views), a rasterized OSM tile where each semantic class (areas, lines, points) is mapped to a unique 3-channel vector, and a coarse GPS prior (±50 m) that determines the map query window. The output is a 3-D score volume of shape (H, W, Angles) representing the joint probability distribution over camera position (u, v) and heading θ ∈ (−180°, 180°], from which the 3-DoF pose is extracted by argmax.

## Method

RHO employs a two-branch Pin-Pan architecture: the panoramic branch uses a Split-Undistort-Merge (SUM) module that divides the equirectangular panorama into three 120° pinhole views, undistorts each, encodes them through a shared image encoder and BEV projection, and merges the resulting BEV maps into a full 360° feature; the pinhole branch processes a single 120° front view for orientation-sensitive features. Both BEV maps are exhaustively matched against a neural OSM map to produce separate score volumes. A Position-Orientation Fusion (POF) module then performs two-stage mutual rectification: it marginalizes the panorama volume over the orientation axis via Log-SumExp to obtain a 2-D spatial prior that reweights the pinhole volume, then marginalizes the reweighted pinhole volume over spatial axes to yield a sharp orientation prior that corrects the panorama volume, with learnable scalars α and β governing each fusion step. The model is trained end-to-end with LogSoftmax loss over 64 rotation bins (256 at inference) using Adam with ReduceLROnPlateau scheduling.

## Results

On the clean CV-RHO test set, RHO achieves PR@1m/3m/5m of 24.59%/73.55%/84.36% and OR@1°/3°/5° of 43.46%/83.61%/90.44%, outperforming the best OrienterNet baseline (120°-FoV) by up to 8.72% in orientation recall. Under all noisy variations (rain, snow, fog, night, over/under-exposure, motion blur), RHO's average performance degradation is only −0.60% (PR@3m) and −0.99% (OR@3°) when trained on noisy data, compared to −3.45% and −2.72% for OrienterNet. On the cross-region Mount Vernon test set, RHO maintains PR@5m of 71–74% across all conditions, consistently exceeding OrienterNet by 5–10 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is OrienterNet [Sarlin et al., CVPR 2023], evaluated at 90°, 120°, and 360° FoV. RHO outperforms the strongest OrienterNet configuration (120°-FoV) on every metric: +2.76% PR@1m, +7.39% PR@3m, +6.33% PR@5m, +8.44% OR@1°, +8.72% OR@3°, and +4.82% OR@5°. Notably, OrienterNet at 360° FoV collapses to 28.78% PR@5m and 36.87% OR@5°, confirming that naively feeding panoramas without distortion correction is far worse than using a narrower pinhole view. Under zero-shot noisy evaluation (trained clean, tested noisy), RHO degrades by an average of −12.01% PR@3m versus −17.41% for OrienterNet; when both are trained on noisy data, RHO's degradation is nearly negligible (−0.60% PR@3m) while OrienterNet still loses 3.45%. On the cross-region Mount Vernon set, RHO leads OrienterNet by 5–10 points in PR@5m across all eight condition variations, and on the Sim2Real set RHO achieves up to 68.48% PR@5m (rain, Ogori) versus 56.36% for OrienterNet, demonstrating a clear and consistent advantage in both robustness and generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends OrienterNet's OSM-based metric cross-view geo-localization paradigm by replacing pinhole inputs with 360° panoramas and introducing a two-branch Pin-Pan architecture. The Split-Undistort-Merge module addresses the equirectangular distortion that causes naive panoramic inputs to fail catastrophically (360° OrienterNet drops to 3.79% PR@1m vs. 21.83% at 120°), while the Position-Orientation Fusion mechanism exploits the complementary strengths of panoramic (position) and pinhole (orientation) representations. The work also carves out a new robustness dimension for OSM-based MCVGL, which prior satellite-based robust CVGL studies (WELN, MCGF, CVD) have explored but no OSM-based method has addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CV-RHO (2.7M images, 8 perturbation types, 7 cities) is the first large-scale benchmark for robust OSM-based MCVGL, filling a gap between satellite-based robustness benchmarks and the more practical OSM setting that offers faster updates and lower storage. The near-zero degradation of RHO when trained on noisy data (average PR@3m drop of only 0.60%) is a strong robustness signal, but the large Sim2Real gap (PR@1m collapsing from ~24% to ~4–6% on real-world photos) and the single external baseline (OrienterNet) temper the practical claims. Open questions include whether the FLUX.1-generated perturbations adequately capture real weather diversity, whether the Pin-Pan architecture generalizes to multi-scale or multi-modal inputs, and how performance scales with more diverse OSM map styles across countries.
