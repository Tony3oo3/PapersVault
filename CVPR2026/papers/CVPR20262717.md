## Problem

Generating realistic 3D human geometry animations requires simultaneously capturing fine-grained geometric details (folds, wrinkles) and natural clothing dynamics, yet existing methods fail to satisfy both: non-generative approaches cannot generalize to unseen avatars, while generative avatar models struggle to preserve high-fidelity geometry and learn realistic pose-dependent deformations. Additionally, the scarcity of 3D human animation data makes it difficult to learn complex temporal dependencies without overfitting.

## Input / Output

The method takes a SMPL pose-and-shape sequence (encoding body articulation and morphology) plus random noise as input, and produces a sequence of 3D human geometry point clouds as output, each frame represented by a latent tensor z ∈ R^{C×H×W} that is decoded into surface points via flow matching. The generated point clouds can be directly converted to meshes via Poisson reconstruction or rendered through Gaussian splatting for depth and normal maps. The animation model operates autoregressively with a fixed short-term context window of i past latents, generating frames of arbitrary sequence length without increasing per-step computational cost.

## Method

The framework is two-stage: first, a latent space is learned by improving HuGeoDis with a low-cost uniform mapping—instead of KNN (which produces uneven point density on the SMPL mesh), a supervised network m_φ learns coarse one-to-one correspondences between SMPL and avatar surfaces, and a flow matching model u_θ then learns the residual distribution T(p) conditioned on the SMPL mesh and a per-identity latent z. Second, an autoregressive animation model v_ψ generates latents frame-by-frame using flow matching over short-term transitions (i past latents + corresponding SMPL poses), while a contrastive-learned identity condition c (via NTXent loss on a downsampled convolutional encoder) enforces long-term consistency across the full sequence. Data augmentation on SMPL body-shape and expression parameters (interpolated with a standard template using factors in (−1.0, 1.5)) prevents the model from memorizing pose-geometry correlations, and classifier-free guidance enables generation of initial frames without past latents.

## Results

On the 4d-dress reconstruction benchmark, the proposed method reduces Chamfer Distance by ~90% compared to HuGeoDis (0.15×10⁻⁵ vs. 1.86×10⁻⁵ at 1M points), while requiring only ~300K points for full geometry coverage versus 1M for HuGeoDis. For static random 3D human generation on THuman2, the method achieves a FID of 14.03 on rendered normal maps, outperforming HuGeoDis (16.16), gDNA (42.90), and all radiance-field-based baselines (60–224). For animation generation on 4d-dress, the method attains the best scores across all metrics: FID 25.01, identity consistency 0.96, and user-study scores of 4.4 (quality), 4.5 (naturalness), and 4.4 (conformance) out of 5, representing a 2.2× improvement over the next-best method in user study.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation. Against HuGeoDis [38] (the closest prior work), it achieves 90% lower Chamfer Distance in reconstruction (0.15 vs. 1.86 ×10⁻⁵ at 1M points) and a lower FID in static generation (14.03 vs. 16.16). Against radiance-field and implicit baselines—ENARF [24] (FID 223.72), GNARF [2] (166.62), EVA3D [12] (60.37), E3Gen [57] (65.32 raw / 28.12 enhanced), GetAvatar [8] (56.07 raw / 22.77 enhanced), and gDNA [45] (42.90 raw / 17.43 enhanced)—the geometry-distribution formulation yields substantially better raw geometry fidelity, with the gap most pronounced against NeRF-based methods. For animation, LHM [29] is the only direct animation baseline; the proposed method beats it on FID (25.01 vs. 33.37 in the normal-map setting, 25.01 vs. 58.19 in the geometry-extraction setting) and on all three user-study dimensions (4.4/4.5/4.4 vs. 3.3/2.7/2.0), with LHM's rigging-based deformation failing to produce realistic garment dynamics. The long-term autoregressive model (a variant of [36,37]) is competitive on FID (27.13) but lags significantly on identity consistency (0.61 vs. 0.96) and user-study naturalness (2.2 vs. 4.5), confirming that short-term transition modeling with an identity condition is more effective under limited 3D animation data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Human Geometry Distribution (HuGeoDis) framework by replacing its KNN-based SMPL-to-avatar correspondence with a learned, uniform mapping, addressing the imbalanced sampling that caused reconstruction artifacts. For animation, it departs from both data-driven garment deformation methods (TailorNet, GarNet) that are non-generative and tied to specific templates, and from generative avatar models (LHM, temporal diffusion) that struggle with fine geometric detail. The autoregressive short-term transition design draws on motion transition literature [36, 37], while the identity-conditioning via NTXent contrastive learning and the flow-matching latent space are assembled into a two-stage pipeline not previously applied to 3D geometry animation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first generative framework to simultaneously produce high-fidelity 3D human geometry (folds, wrinkles) and natural pose-dependent clothing dynamics, filling a gap where prior methods excelled at one but not the other. The 90% Chamfer Distance reduction over HuGeoDis and 2.2× user study improvement over LHM demonstrate practical value, though the results are validated primarily on the 4d-dress dataset. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to physically accurate dynamics across diverse garment materials, how it scales with larger animation datasets, and whether decomposing avatar and garment representations would enable fine-grained editing of generated sequences.
