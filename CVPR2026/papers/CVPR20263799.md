## Problem

Adversarial training has been predominantly developed and evaluated on balanced datasets, yet real-world data follows long-tail distributions where models become overconfident on head classes. The paper identifies two specific limitations of applying standard adversarial training to long-tail data: a skewed training objective caused by class imbalance, and unstable evolution of adversarial distributions across iterations. It further asks whether adversarial perturbations—which inherently alter the training distribution—can be designed to simultaneously mitigate both adversarial vulnerability and class imbalance.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-LT, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-LT) paired with class labels, producing class predictions evaluated under both natural and l∞ PGD adversarial conditions (ε=8/255, 20 steps). The key unusual aspect is that the perturbation budget is not a single fixed value but a per-class, per-iteration schedule: each class y_i receives its own intensity ε_yi determined by its imbalance ratio relative to the head class, and this intensity is further scaled by an iteration-dependent ramp factor. The framework is plug-and-play, wrapping around any existing adversarial training algorithm (AT, AWP, AT-BSL, etc.) without modifying the loss function or model architecture.

## Method

RobustLT adaptively controls perturbation intensity along two axes. Class-wise Perturbation Balancing (CPB) assigns each class y_i an intensity ε_yi = (1−α)ε + α·(√(log K_yi)/Σ_{y'} P(y')√(log K_y'))·ε, where K_yi is the class-wise imbalance ratio, so minority classes receive larger perturbations while the expected perturbation magnitude is constrained to equal the standard ε via a Wasserstein-distance-inspired normalization. Adversarial Iteration Weighting (AIW) multiplies the class-wise intensity by a linear ramp min{(t−1)/(βT), 1} over the first βT iterations, gradually increasing perturbation strength to stabilize the evolution of adversarial distributions and reduce the Wasserstein distance between adjacent iterations. The theoretical foundation (Theorems 4.1–4.4) proves that for perturbation intensities in (μ₂, μ₁), the model's weights shift toward robust features while the bias term converges to zero, simultaneously improving adversarial robustness and eliminating class overconfidence; the non-empty intersection of feasible regions for robustness and balance guarantees both objectives can be met concurrently.

## Results

On CIFAR10-LT (IR=50), RobustLT applied to AT improves robust accuracy from 13.28% to 19.50% (all classes) while maintaining natural accuracy (~58.1%). On CIFAR100-LT (IR=10), AT-BSL+RobustLT raises robust accuracy from 12.42% to 14.71%, and on TinyImageNet-LT (IR=10), TAET+RobustLT improves robust accuracy from 14.12% to 16.67%. Gains are particularly pronounced on tail classes, where RobustLT consistently yields the largest absolute improvements across all base methods and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustLT is evaluated as a plug-and-play enhancement on six base adversarial training methods (AT, AWP, RoBal, REAT, AT-BSL, TAET) and consistently improves robust accuracy over each original method across all three datasets, with the largest gains on tail classes (e.g., AT on CIFAR10-LT: 13.28%→19.50% robust accuracy). Against three alternative enhancement methods (UDR, CFA, DAFA), RobustLT clearly outperforms all of them in robustness on long-tail scenarios, as reported in Table 1. The improvement is architecture-agnostic: RobustLT remains effective on ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and DeiT-S, though gains are somewhat smaller on ResNet-18 compared to WRN-28-10. RobustLT also generalizes across attack types (PGD, CW, AutoAttack) and imbalance ratios (IR=5, 10, 50, 100), consistently improving tail-class robustness. The method is complementary to existing long-tail adversarial training approaches like RoBal and AT-BSL, which rely on Balanced Softmax Loss, whereas RobustLT operates at the perturbation level without modifying the loss function.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the adversarial training framework from an online optimization perspective (Gao et al., NeurIPS 2019) by deriving a Wasserstein-distance-based bound on final robust risk, then uses the robust/non-robust feature binary classification setup (Rice et al., ICML 2020) to characterize when class-wise perturbation intensities can simultaneously reduce robust risk and eliminate overconfidence. It departs from prior long-tail adversarial training methods (RoBal, AT-BSL, TAET) that modify the loss function via Balanced Softmax Loss, instead proposing to modify the perturbation itself as the balancing mechanism. The key conceptual departure is treating adversarial perturbations not merely as a threat to defend against but as a controllable instrument for rebalancing the training distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical characterization of why standard adversarial training degrades under long-tail distributions (skewed objective + unstable adversarial distribution evolution) and shows that a single mechanism—adaptive perturbation intensity—can address both issues, which is a clean unification. The plug-and-play design (CPB + AIW) makes it broadly applicable to existing adversarial training pipelines without architectural changes. However, the theoretical analysis is restricted to a simplified binary linear classifier with Gaussian features, and empirical validation is limited to CIFAR-scale datasets with WRN-28-10 as the primary architecture; the observed reduction in effectiveness on ResNet-18 raises open questions about scalability to ImageNet-level models and deeper non-linear networks.
