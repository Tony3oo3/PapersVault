## Problem

4D object detection (3D detection in streaming video) suffers from two fundamental gaps: existing open-set methods either predict frame-by-frame without temporal modeling (causing jitter and inconsistency) or rely on multi-stage pipelines with tracking and association modules that propagate errors across cascaded stages. Additionally, no large-scale dataset with continuous, spatiotemporally aligned 3D bounding box annotations exists, largely due to the prohibitive cost of such annotation.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video sequence (resized to 448×448, up to 10 frames in training, variable length at inference) along with text prompts as input. It produces globally consistent 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, rotation) in world coordinates, metric depth maps, camera intrinsics, and relative camera poses per frame. A key design choice is that 3D boxes are predicted in a global coordinate system anchored to the first frame of the sequence, rather than per-frame camera coordinates, enabling direct cross-frame consistency evaluation.

## Method

DetAny4D fuses frozen SAM (ViT-H) and DINOv2 (ViT-L) image encoders with a cross-attention feature aggregator from DetAny3D, plus UniDepth-V2 geometry embeddings, to produce per-frame image and geometry tokens. A Spatiotemporal Decoder of three Causal Attention Blocks (CABs) with lower-triangular causal masks aggregates tokens across frames: the first CAB integrates prompt/box tokens with image embeddings, the second with geometry embeddings to produce a geometry control signal, and the third fuses both into final implicit features. Multi-task heads (depth, camera intrinsics, camera pose, 3D b-box) provide dense supervision. Training uses random sequence cropping, object query padding for variable object counts, and interleaved single-frame samples; the loss combines SILog (depth/camera), L1 (center/depth/pose), 2D/3D IoU, permutation-invariant Chamfer distance (corners), a softmin-based dimension loss that handles geometric symmetry across viewpoints, and a dedicated consistency loss (spatial Chamfer to GT in world coords + temporal Chamfer to the sequence-average box) that enforces global spatiotemporal coherence.

## Results

On the full DA4D benchmark, DetAny4D achieves the best 3D detection AP of 27.48 and the lowest temporal variance (Var_v = 0.70, Var_c = 0.64), outperforming the strongest single-frame baseline DetAny3D (AP 27.16, Var_v 0.99, Var_c 0.90) by reducing cross-frame variance by ~30%. Against the multi-stage video-based method Kinematic3D*, DetAny4D improves AP by 2.0 points and reduces Var_v by 18%. In the open-set comparison (Table 2), DetAny4D attains F1@0.25 = 49.7 and F1@0.5 = 45.5, surpassing ConceptGraphs (45.6 / 41.9) and SpatialLM (27.1 / 12.8) on both thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetAny4D outperforms all four monocular 3D detection baselines (ImVoxelNet, Cube R-CNN, OV Mono3D, DetAny3D) on both AP_3D and temporal variance across all sub-datasets, with the largest gap over ImVoxelNet (+15.5 AP on Full DA4D) and the closest margin over DetAny3D (+0.32 AP, −29% Var_v). Against the video-based 4D baseline Kinematic3D*, DetAny4D wins on AP_3D by 2.0 points on Full DA4D and reduces Var_v from 0.85 to 0.70 (−18%), though Kinematic3D* edges ahead on Var_c for Replica (0.49 vs 0.47) and HM3D (0.66 vs 0.53). In the open-set comparison (Table 2), DetAny4D surpasses ConceptGraphs* by +4.1 F1@0.25 and +3.6 F1@0.5, and dominates SpatialLM by a wide margin (+22.6 F1@0.25, +32.7 F1@0.5), confirming that end-to-end RGB-sequence 4D detection can match or exceed multi-stage RGB-D and point-cloud-based pipelines without requiring pre-scanned geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DetAny3D feature extraction pipeline (SAM + DINOv2 encoders with a cross-attention aggregator) from single-frame monocular 3D detection to a temporal setting, adding a causal-attention-based Spatiotemporal Decoder and multi-task heads for depth, camera pose, and 3D box prediction. It departs from prior video-based 4D detection methods (Kinematic3D, ConceptGraphs, BADet) that rely on multi-stage pipelines with explicit tracking or association modules, instead formulating the problem as a single end-to-end sequence-to-sequence prediction. The camera pose loss design is adapted from StreamVGGT, and the overall architecture inherits the open-vocabulary prompt mechanism from DetAny3D, making the temporal extension a natural but non-trivial generalization of an established single-frame framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DA4D dataset (280k sequences with spatiotemporal-aligned 3D annotations, depth, and poses) addresses a critical data bottleneck that has limited progress in 4D detection, and the end-to-end formulation eliminates error propagation inherent in cascaded multi-stage pipelines. The 10–30% reduction in cross-frame variance over strong single-frame baselines demonstrates that temporal modeling can be integrated without sacrificing per-frame accuracy. However, all data is generated in the Habitat simulator with indoor scenes, raising open questions about generalization to real-world outdoor environments, scalability to much longer sequences beyond the 10-frame training limit, and real-time inference feasibility for deployment in robotics or autonomous driving.
