## Problem

Existing E2E driving benchmarks (NAVSIM, WOMD, CoVLA) predominantly feature nominal scenarios, leaving long-tail situations occurring at <0.03% frequency largely untested. Standard open-loop metrics compound this gap: ADE assumes a single ground-truth trajectory despite driving being inherently multi-modal, and PDMS requires annotated agent positions that may not exist for novel objects (e.g., flocks of birds), making both inadequate for safety-critical evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each 20-second segment provides 8-camera 360° JPEG images (concatenated to a single 768×768 image for the baseline), 4 seconds of past ego trajectory at 4Hz with aligned velocity and acceleration, and a discrete routing command (GO_STRAIGHT/GO_LEFT/GO_RIGHT). The model outputs a 5-second future trajectory as waypoints in vehicle coordinates at 4Hz. For evaluation, RFS compares the prediction against three human-rated reference trajectories (scored 0–10) using speed-scaled rectangular trust regions at t=3s and t=5s, with exponential score decay outside the region and a hard floor of 4.

## Method

The core contribution is a curated dataset of 4,021 long-tail driving segments (12 hours) mined from 6.4M miles via rule-based heuristics combined with MLLM classification into 11 scenario clusters, followed by human filtering that reduces the long-tail proportion to 0.03%. The novel Rater Feedback Score (RFS) evaluates predictions against three expert-rated trajectories using rectangular trust regions whose lateral and longitudinal thresholds are scaled by a piecewise-linear function of initial speed; scores decay exponentially with normalized distance error outside the trust region, and the final RFS is the maximum over all three references, averaged over t=3s and t=5s, floored at 4. The baseline NaiveEMMA is a simplified EMMA finetuned from Gemini Flash, consuming the concatenated 8-camera image, 3s ego history, and routing command without past frames or CoT reasoning. The paper validates RFS quantitatively (showing it rewards multi-camera inputs, long-tail training data, and test-time scaling) and qualitatively, and benchmarks 19 community submissions spanning MLP, diffusion, and MLLM architectures to demonstrate that RFS-aligned RL rewards outperform ADE-aligned ones.

## Results

On the WOD-E2E benchmark, the top leaderboard submission Poutine achieves an RFS of 7.986 and ADE of 2.741, while the paper's NaiveEMMA baseline (Gemini 1 Nano, SFT only) scores RFS 7.528 and ADE 3.018. The best diffusion-based method (UniPlan) reaches RFS 7.779 with ADE 2.986, and the best MLP-based method (Swin-Trajectory) achieves RFS 7.543 with ADE 2.814. Notably, RFS and ADE exhibit only a mild positive correlation across 19 submissions, confirming that traditional distance-based metrics fail to capture safety-critical driving quality in long-tail scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Poutine (RFS 7.986, ADE 2.741) outperforms all other submissions, leveraging SFT+RL with RFS as the reward signal and a blend of WOD-E2E and CoVLA data. It beats AutoVLA (RFS 7.556, ADE 2.958) by 0.43 RFS points despite both using Qwen2.5 with RL post-training; the key difference is that Poutine uses RFS as its RL reward while AutoVLA uses ADE, confirming reward-metric alignment is critical. HMVLM (RFS 7.736, ADE 3.071) achieves a higher RFS than Swin-Trajectory (RFS 7.543, ADE 2.814) despite a substantially worse ADE, directly demonstrating that ADE is an inadequate proxy for driving quality in long-tail scenarios. Among diffusion-based methods, UniPlan (RFS 7.779) edges out DiffusionLTF (RFS 7.717) with fewer training datasets, suggesting that data curation matters more than volume for diffusion architectures. The paper further shows that MLLM-based models benefit substantially from supplementary data sources (e.g., nuPlan, nuScenes), while diffusion-based models show only marginal gains, likely because CoT reasoning in MLLMs mitigates visual distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct response to the limitations of existing E2E driving benchmarks (NAVSIM, WOMD, CoVLA, nuScenes), which predominantly feature nominal scenarios and rely on metrics like ADE or PDMS that struggle with multi-modal, safety-critical situations. Architecturally, the baseline (NaiveEMMA) is a simplified derivative of EMMA [14], while the RFS metric's trust-region thresholds are inherited from WOMD [10]. The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) explicitly mining and curating long-tail scenarios at <0.03% real-world frequency rather than filtering nominal data, and (2) replacing single-trajectory distance metrics with human preference ratings over multiple candidate trajectories, acknowledging that driving is inherently multi-modal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WOD-E2E fills a concrete gap in E2E driving evaluation by providing a large-scale, curated benchmark for rare, safety-critical scenarios that existing datasets underrepresent, and the RFS metric offers a more human-aligned evaluation that better captures the multi-modal nature of driving. The finding that ADE and RFS exhibit only a mild positive correlation challenges the field's reliance on distance-based metrics for safety-critical assessment, and the demonstration that RFS-aligned RL (Poutine) substantially outperforms ADE-aligned RL (AutoVLA) provides actionable guidance for reward design. The primary limitation is the open-loop evaluation setup, which the authors acknowledge as a significant constraint; moving to closed-loop evaluation with realistic sensor simulation remains an open challenge. As a Waymo-hosted benchmark with a public leaderboard, WOD-E2E is likely to become a standard reference for long-tail E2E driving research.
