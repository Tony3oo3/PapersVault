## Problem

Existing 4D human datasets lack fashion-specific annotations and paired catalogue data, while 2D VTON datasets lack 3D geometric and multi-view information. No single dataset provides synchronized multi-view captures of worn garments alongside their flat catalogue images, which is essential for jointly addressing garment dynamics, size estimation, and virtual try-on.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261346/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For VTON, the model takes a person image paired with one or two garment catalogue images (frontal and/or rear) and produces a try-on result; for size estimation, it takes a garment normal image and SMPL-X body pose to regress per-garment measurements in centimeters; for NVS, it takes up to 56 synchronized RGB views to synthesize novel viewpoints. The dataset provides 72.5M frames at 15 fps from 68 cameras (60 RGB + 8 RGB-depth at 4K), with each 20-second sequence including ground-truth sizing charts, material/elasticity annotations (1–5 scale), pixel-level garment segmentations, and 3D point clouds.

## Method

The paper introduces MV-Fashion, a 68-camera multi-view capture system (60 Raspberry Pi global-shutter + 8 Orbbec Femto Bolt depth cameras) recording 80 subjects in 3–10 outfits each, with three sequence types (body, template, motion) capturing diverse styling including multi-layering and draping variations. For VTON baselines, they adapt IDM-VTON (diffusion) and InsertAnything (flow-matching) across three experimental designs: standard single-view, semantic controllability via draping-style prompt augmentation, and multi-view geometric analysis using a shared IP-Adapter to condition on both frontal and rear catalogue images. For size estimation, they adapt SPnet's encoder-decoder where network Ψ predicts a canonical garment normal from posed input and network Φ regresses normalized measurements, testing per-group, multi-task, and SwinV2+pose-conditioned variants. For NVS, they benchmark Instant-NGP, Nerfacto, and 3D Gaussian Splatting within Nerfstudio, with a view-density ablation showing reconstruction quality saturates at 56 views.

## Results

For Virtual Try-On, InsertAnything achieves the best single-view performance (SSIM 0.927, LPIPS 0.065, FID 8.192), outperforming IDM-VTON (SSIM 0.881, LPIPS 0.086, FID 10.187). Multi-view geometric analysis reveals a clear performance drop under cross-perspective constraints (SSIM drops to 0.868, FID rises to 12.907), while view-adaptive conditioning partially recovers quality (SSIM 0.873, FID 12.775). For size estimation, the Multi-Task+SwinV2 variant achieves the lowest average MAE of 4.279 cm (std 5.870). For Novel View Synthesis, splatfacto at 56 views yields the best overall quality (PSNR 29.567, SSIM 0.957, LPIPS 0.028), with performance saturating beyond 32 views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For VTON, InsertAnything [72] clearly outperforms IDM-VTON [13] on the single-view frontal subset across all three metrics (SSIM +0.046, LPIPS −0.021, FID −1.995), confirming that flow-matching-based insertion is more effective than diffusion-based approaches under standard single-view settings. When extended to multi-view, IDM-VTON's cross-view geometric test shows a notable degradation (FID jumps from 10.187 to 12.907), but the view-adaptive variant that feeds both frontal and rear catalogue images via a shared IP-Adapter partially mitigates this gap (FID 12.775), validating the dataset's multi-view pairing design. For NVS, splatfacto [36] dominates at 56 views with the best PSNR (29.567), SSIM (0.957), and LPIPS (0.028), surpassing instant-ngp [58] (PSNR 29.120, LPIPS 0.089) and nerfacto [73] (PSNR 26.535, LPIPS 0.052); however, nerfacto achieves the best SSIM at lower view counts, suggesting complementary strengths. For size estimation, the Multi-Task+SwinV2 variant outperforms both the per-group SPnet [45] baseline (4.904 cm avg MAE) and the plain Multi-Task model (4.710 cm), with the largest gains on G5 jumpsuits (12.109 → 4.889 cm) and G4 outerwear (5.092 → 4.436 cm).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two fragmented research streams: 2D virtual try-on datasets (VITON-HD, Dress Code, IGPair) that provide paired catalogue-to-worn images but lack 3D geometry, and 3D/4D human capture datasets (4D-DRESS, DNA-Rendering, MVHumanNet++) that capture rich geometry but omit the paired catalogue data essential for VTON. For baselines, it builds on IDM-VTON (a diffusion-based VTON model) by extending it with styling-augmented prompts and multi-view IP-Adapter conditioning, and adapts SPnet (a sewing-pattern predictor) for real-data size estimation. The dataset's annotation pipeline repurposes off-the-shelf tools (SAM2, YOLOv8, Qwen3-VL, SMPL-X, Sapiens) into a unified curation workflow, while the NVS benchmarks leverage standard Nerfstudio implementations (Instant-NGP, Nerfacto, splatfacto).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MV-Fashion fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides synchronized multi-view video, paired flat/catalogue garment images, and fashion-specific annotations (sizing charts, material elasticity, draping styles), making it the first resource where garment dynamics, size estimation, and VTON can be studied jointly. The baselines reveal that current VTON models degrade measurably under cross-perspective geometric mismatch and struggle with fine-grained styling control, highlighting open challenges for multi-view and semantically controllable try-on. Limitations include geographic/ethnic bias in the subject pool, reliance on off-the-shelf annotation tools without exhaustive quality benchmarking, and the fact that the 68-camera setup, while resource-friendly, still requires significant infrastructure. The public release of both data and code positions this as a potential community standard for fashion-centric 4D research.
