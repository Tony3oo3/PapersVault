## Problem

Neural asset authoring and neural rendering have evolved as disjoint paradigms: assets are generated assuming a fixed renderer, and renderers are trained on static asset distributions. This decoupling causes small errors in PBR decomposition (misidentified albedo, incorrect normals) to compound through the nonlinear rendering pipeline, producing baked-in shadows, material ambiguities, and lighting inconsistencies that no amount of per-object optimization can fully resolve.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single casually-lit RGB image (with optional geometry prior such as a mesh), a target 4K HDR environment map, and camera view parameters (position, orientation, 40° FOV) as input. It produces a relightable 3D Gaussian splatting field rendered in real-time via differentiable rasterization, along with auxiliary PBR material maps (base color, roughness, metallic) and a shadow map. The entire pipeline is feed-forward with no per-object optimization, operating at real-time frame rates (30–48 FPS depending on decoder depth).

## Method

NeAR is a two-stage coupled stack. Stage 1 lifts the input image into a Lighting-Homogenized SLAT (LH-SLAT) using a rectified flow model (Trellis backbone fine-tuned with LoRA in sparse voxel space) trained with conditional flow matching loss, transforming a shaded latent with baked-in shadows/highlights into a canonical, illumination-invariant representation. Stage 2 decodes a relightable 3DGS via two sequential transformer modules: an Intrinsic Aware Decoder (IAD) that extracts view-independent geometric and material features using self-shifted window attention with 16 learnable register tokens, and a Lighting Aware Decoder (LAD) that injects view embeddings (NeRF positional encoding of ray direction and distance) and lighting condition tokens (from a ConvNeXt-based HDRI encoder with spatial cross-attention for directional cues) via cross-attention blocks. The final Gaussian parameters (position, base color, roughness, metallic, scale, rotation, opacity, plus lighting-specific color features and shadow) are regressed from both intrinsic and lighting-aware features, with a shallow MLP predicting per-Gaussian radiance from encoded normals and color features. The key design insight is that homogenizing illumination before decoding avoids ill-posed PBR inversion, while the coupled asset-renderer contract ensures the latent space is specifically structured for the downstream renderer's needs.

## Results

NeAR achieves the highest PSNR across all four evaluation tasks and all four datasets. On the full-pipeline Novel-view Relighting task, NeAR reaches 22.87 / 25.53 / 25.97 / 22.94 PSNR on ADT / DTC / Objaverse / Glossy Synthetic, surpassing the strongest baseline Hunyuan3D-2.1 (22.30 / 24.89 / 25.47 / 22.26). On G-buffer Forward Rendering, NeAR attains 30.47 PSNR on Glossy Synthetic versus 25.46 for DiffusionRenderer, a 5 dB gap. The method also leads on LPIPS and SSIM in every sub-task, confirming consistent perceptual and structural advantages.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeAR outperforms all eight baselines on every task and dataset. Against the closest 3D-generation competitor, Hunyuan3D-2.1, NeAR gains +0.57 dB PSNR on ADT and +0.71 dB on DTC for Novel-view Relighting, while also improving LPIPS by 0.003–0.005. MeshGen and Stable-Fast-3D trail NeAR by 2.8–5.7 dB PSNR on the same task, reflecting their reliance on baked-in textures rather than a relightable neural field. In the rendering sub-tasks, DiffusionRenderer is the strongest 2D baseline but still lags NeAR by 4.7–5.1 dB PSNR on G-buffer rendering (e.g., 30.47 vs 25.46 on Glossy Synthetic). DiLightNet and RGB↔X are competitive on Random-lit Reconstruction but fall 1.2–8.5 dB behind NeAR; NeuralGaffer is substantially weaker (14.31 vs 21.95 PSNR on ADT for Unknown-lit Relighting), confirming that diffusion-based 2D relighting struggles with 3D consistency. The consistent margin across all four datasets (including out-of-domain ADT and DTC) indicates that the coupled LH-SLAT + lighting-aware decoder design generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeAR builds directly on the Trellis/SLAT representation for structured 3D latents and 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-time rendering, but fundamentally repositions both within a coupled design philosophy: rather than treating asset generation (e.g., Trellis, LRM) and neural rendering (e.g., DiffusionRenderer, RenderFormer) as independent stages, it co-designs a lighting-homogenized latent (LH-SLAT) with a lighting-aware decoder. The rectified-flow backbone with LoRA adaptation for Stage 1 follows the parameter-efficient fine-tuning paradigm from Trellis, while the feed-forward 3DGS decoder departs from per-object optimization methods (GS3, Relightable 3D Gaussians) and from 2D diffusion relighting (DiLightNet, IC-Light) by operating entirely in 3D with explicit view and lighting conditioning. The paper positions itself as a middle path between brittle PBR inversion and black-box diffusion, using illumination homogenization as the key conceptual bridge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The coupled asset-renderer framing offers a principled argument that representation and rendering should be co-designed rather than independently optimized, which could influence how future neural graphics stacks are architected. Practically, NeAR delivers real-time, feed-forward relighting without per-object optimization—a significant efficiency win over optimization-based baselines—while maintaining multi-view consistency that 2D diffusion methods cannot guarantee. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: it struggles with complex effects like transparency under standard PBR rendering, is evaluated primarily on controlled synthetic captures with moderate compute, and in-the-wild generalization and dynamic scenes remain open. The approach also depends on a geometry prior (e.g., mesh from HY3D) for the SLAT grid, which may limit applicability to open scenes.
