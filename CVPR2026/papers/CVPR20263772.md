## Problem

Foundation models like SAM2 excel at generic video segmentation but are ill-suited for scene text segmentation due to their reliance on external prompts, limited 256×256 output resolution that loses fine stroke details, and temporal instability in video sequences. Compounding this, the field suffers from a critical data gap: existing video text datasets provide only word-level bounding boxes rather than the pixel-level masks required for segmentation training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263772/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB video frames in sequences of 8 at 1024×1024 resolution, processed in a streaming fashion through SAM2's native sequential architecture. It produces pixel-level text segmentation masks simultaneously at three resolutions (256×256, 512×512, and 1024×1024) without requiring any external user prompts. The system supports both an automatic mask generation mode for full-frame segmentation and a promptable mode for interactive use, with the prompt-free setting being the primary evaluation configuration.

## Method

SAM2Text adapts SAM2 through four integrated components: (1) LoRA fine-tuning (rank 16, alpha 32) applied to QKV projections and MLP layers of the ViT image encoder for parameter-efficient text-domain adaptation while preserving streaming inference; (2) a self-prompting module that uses four-level depthwise separable convolutions to generate spatial attention maps, which are then used to compute sparse prompt tokens via weighted spatial averaging, followed by dual-path self- and cross-attention to produce text-aware prompt representations; (3) a multi-resolution decoder that extends the original 256×256 output with progressive transposed-convolution upsampling branches at 512×512 and 1024×1024, where a hypernetwork generates scale-specific dynamic convolution weights from mask tokens; and (4) a two-tier memory mechanism combining a fixed-length FIFO short-term cache with Top-K cosine-similarity retrieval from a bounded historical pool, reducing attention cost from O(T) to O(L+K) while maintaining long-range temporal context for stable mask propagation.

## Results

On the video benchmarks, SAM2Text achieves 93.25% fgIOU and 94.83% F-score on STS-SynthV, and 80.71% fgIOU and 87.45% F-score on STS-RealV, setting new state-of-the-art on both. On image benchmarks, it reaches 85.50% fgIOU on Total-Text and 89.52% fgIOU on TextSeg, outperforming the previous best (Hi-SAM) by 0.91% and 0.56% respectively. The original SAM2.1 without text-specific adaptation scores only 77.45% fgIOU on STS-RealV, underscoring the necessity of the proposed adaptations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM2Text outperforms all 11 baselines across all four benchmarks. Against the strongest image-based competitor Hi-SAM [23], it gains +1.58% fgIOU on STS-SynthV and +2.37% on STS-RealV, with the gap widening on real-world data where temporal consistency matters most. Compared to the unmodified SAM2.1 backbone [12], the improvements are +2.40% (synthetic) and +3.26% (real) in fgIOU, confirming that generic video segmentation is insufficient for fine-grained text. Image-only methods like SegFormer [20] and TexRNet [17/48] lag substantially on video (71.56% and 74.89% fgIOU on STS-RealV respectively) because they process frames independently without temporal propagation. On image benchmarks (Table 1), SAM2Text also leads: 85.50% fgIOU on Total-Text vs. Hi-SAM's 84.59%, and 89.52% on TextSeg vs. 88.96%, demonstrating that the video-oriented design does not sacrifice image-level accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SAM2, a general-purpose video segmentation foundation model, into the specialized domain of scene text segmentation by combining LoRA-based encoder adaptation, a self-prompting module, a multi-resolution decoder, and an enhanced memory mechanism. It departs from the image-level text segmentation lineage (DeepLabV3, HRNet, TexRNet, SegFormer, Hi-SAM) by leveraging SAM2's native streaming and memory architecture rather than processing frames independently. The self-prompting design eliminates the external prompt dependency that characterizes SAM2's original paradigm, while the multi-resolution decoder addresses the resolution bottleneck (256×256) that limits fine-grained text structure preservation. The contribution of two pixel-level annotated video datasets (STS-SynthV, STS-RealV) fills a notable gap, as prior video text datasets (ICDAR 2015 Video, YouTube Video Text) only provide bounding boxes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Video scene text segmentation at the pixel level was previously underexplored due to the absence of suitable training data; the released datasets (2,070 video clips, ~217K annotated frames) establish a foundation for future work in this area. The prompt-free, streaming-capable design makes the approach more practical for real-time video analysis pipelines compared to interactive or frame-independent methods. However, the video-level evaluation is limited to the authors' own datasets, and the modest per-component ablation gains (0.5–1.5% fgIOU each) suggest that the individual innovations are incremental rather than individually transformative. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to extremely long videos, diverse scripts beyond Chinese/English, and whether the synthetic-to-real domain gap can be further closed.
