## Problem

Identity-conditioned diffusion models can generate photorealistic, identity-consistent synthetic faces, but they suffer from limited intra-class variation, which is essential for training robust and generalizable face recognition systems. Existing remedies for this diversity gap rely on auxiliary labels, learned style modules, or iterative optimization over the embedding space, adding complexity and requiring model modifications. The paper asks whether a simple, purely geometric perturbation of identity embeddings can close this diversity gap without altering the underlying generative model.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained identity-conditioned latent diffusion model (IDiff-Face), a set of reference unit-norm identity embeddings extracted by a pre-trained FR model (ElasticFace/ResNet100), and random Gaussian noise latents. For each reference identity, it produces K perturbed unit-norm embeddings (default K=50) that are fed as fixed cross-attention conditioning vectors into the LDM, yielding K diverse yet identity-coherent face images per identity. The entire perturbation step adds only ~0.01 s of overhead per identity on a CPU, and no retraining or architectural change to the diffusion model is required.

## Method

IDPERTURB performs angular sampling on the unit hyper-sphere of the identity embedding space. Given a reference embedding v, it uniformly samples a target cosine similarity s ∈ [lb, 1], draws random noise n ~ N(0, I), projects n onto the hyperplane orthogonal to v to obtain a unit tangent vector u, and constructs the perturbed embedding ṽ = cos(θ)·v + sin(θ)·u where θ = arccos(s). A dynamic lower-bound adjustment (Equation 6) ensures the perturbed vector remains closer to its own identity than to any other reference identity, preventing cross-identity contamination. Each perturbed embedding is then used as a fixed conditioning vector for a fresh noise seed in the pre-trained LDM's reverse diffusion process (DDIM, 50 steps), with Classifier-Free Guidance at a moderate scale (ω=1 or 2) to balance identity fidelity against sample diversity. The approach is entirely geometry-driven, requiring no auxiliary conditions, no network modifications, and no iterative optimization.

## Results

On the primary C-WF 0.5M setting, IDPERTURB achieves 93.62% average verification accuracy across LFW, AgeDB-30, CFP-FP, CA-LFW, and CP-LFW, outperforming the closest DM-based competitor UIFace (93.27%) and narrowing the gap to authentic C-WF (94.63%) to just 1.01%. At 1.0M samples, IDPERTURB reaches 94.48% average, surpassing all SOTA synthetic-data methods. On the large-scale IJB-C benchmark, IDPERTURB (C-WF, 1.0M) achieves 85.57% / 91.19% TAR at FAR 1e-5 / 1e-4, outperforming UIFace (81.78% / 88.70%) and NegFaceDiff (77.38% / 86.11%). The optimal perturbation lower bound is lb=0.6, which balances intra-class diversity (D_intra=0.458) with identity consistency (C_intra=0.939).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDPERTURB outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the primary C-WF 0.5M setting. Among DM-based methods trained on C-WF, it beats the closest competitor UIFace by 0.35% average (93.62 vs. 93.27), CemiFace by 1.32%, NegFaceDiff by 1.39%, and ID³ by 3.82%. On IJB-C, IDPERTURB leads UIFace by 0.50% at FAR 1e-5 (82.28 vs. 81.78) and 0.79% at 1e-4 (89.49 vs. 88.70). At the 1.0M scale, IDPERTURB (94.48%) surpasses UIFace (94.06%), Arc2Face (93.14%), CemiFace (93.07%), and Vec2Face (92.47%). All GAN-based methods (SFace, SFace2+, ExFaceGAN, IDNet, SynFace, USynthFace) and digital rendering (DigiFace) trail IDPERTURB by 11–16% in average accuracy, confirming the advantage of diffusion-based generation. The only setting where IDPERTURB does not lead is the FFHQ-trained comparison, where DCFace (89.56%) slightly edges out IDPERTURB-FFHQ (88.79%), though both are well below the C-WF-trained results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on IDiff-Face (Boutros et al., ICCV 2023) as the base identity-conditioned diffusion model and on UIFace (Lin et al., ICLR 2025) for reference identity embeddings and experimental protocol. It departs from prior diversity-enhancement strategies—dual conditioning with style embeddings (DCFace), binary attribute conditioning (ID³), iterative embedding-space optimization (HyperFace), and two-phase unconditional-then-conditional sampling (UIFace)—by proposing a purely geometric perturbation of identity embeddings within a cosine-constrained spherical cap. The key conceptual distinction is that IDPERTURB requires no architectural modification, no auxiliary conditions, and no learned sampling procedure; it operates entirely in the embedding space of a frozen pre-trained model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As major real face datasets (MS-Celeb-1M, VGGFace2) are withdrawn due to privacy and legal constraints, the field needs reliable, privacy-preserving synthetic data pipelines for training robust FR systems. IDPERTURB offers a simple, plug-and-play sampling strategy that can be applied to any pre-trained identity-conditioned diffusion model without retraining, making it broadly applicable. The remaining gap to authentic C-WF (93.62% vs. 94.63% average) and the sensitivity of optimal lb to the specific embedding space and base model suggest open questions about principled selection of perturbation bounds and whether the geometric assumption (angular proximity ≈ identity similarity) holds universally across FR embedding spaces. The method's simplicity also raises the question of whether further gains require fundamentally different diversity mechanisms beyond embedding-space geometry.
