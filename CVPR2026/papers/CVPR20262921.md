## Problem

Existing dyadic interactive head generation methods are designed for offline clip-based processing, requiring the full interlocutor sequence as a prerequisite and thus unable to support real-time interaction. Even among real-time approaches, they either model only shallow audiovisual correlations without in-depth semantic understanding of the conversation, or fail to maintain consistent motion style over extended dialogues, producing unnatural and incoherent responses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262921/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes Speaker A's audio (encoded via a pretrained Whisper encoder), Speaker B's audio (MFCC features), and Speaker B's 3D head motion (extracted by Spectre) as inputs, and produces Speaker A's frame-level 3D head motion as output. Critically, Speaker B's features are processed strictly frame-by-frame with causal masks to eliminate any dependency on future context, enabling true real-time response at over 300 fps on a single GPU. An LLM-based analyzer (GPT-4o) runs in parallel on transcribed dialogue text to supply intention and topic embeddings every k conversation rounds.

## Method

MimicTalker combines three key modules within a causal transformer backbone. The MICE module fuses Speaker B's per-frame audio and motion features via cross-attention and self-attention under causal masks, then maintains a multi-scale gated memory (analogous to LSTM input/forget gates) that is updated with semantic guidance from Speaker B's intention to retain contextually relevant long-term interaction history. The SDI module encodes Speaker A's audio with Whisper, computes frame-wise intention-audio correlation via cross-attention, and injects it through adaLN blocks; it then integrates Speaker B's instantaneous and long-term features via interaction blocks, with the conversation topic appended as a global condition through causal self-attention. The MSM module maintains an external memory bank of motion styles (extracted by a contrastively-trained style encoder) keyed by Speaker A's historical intentions; at inference, the current intention queries the bank for the most similar stored intention and retrieves the corresponding style code, which is applied via adaLN to enforce long-term style consistency regardless of sequence length. The entire system is trained end-to-end with supervised loss on the DualTalk dataset, with the style encoder separately trained via contrastive loss.

## Results

On the DualTalk test set, MimicTalker achieves the best scores across all five metric groups: FD-EXP of 7.12 (vs. 11.14 for the closest dyadic baseline DualTalk), MSE-EXP of 3.09, SID-EXP of 3.63, and rPCC-EXP of 4.16. The relative improvement in FD for expression and head pose exceeds 30% over all baselines, and MSE for expression improves by over 20%. On the OOD set, MimicTalker maintains its lead with FD-EXP of 15.89 vs. 21.71 for DualTalk, confirming strong generalization. On the longer Seamless Interaction dataset (5–11 min clips), MimicTalker again achieves the best overall results, with FD-EXP of 24.52 vs. 30.13 for the DualTalk baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MimicTalker outperforms all six baselines on every metric (FD, P-FD, MSE, SID, rPCC) on both the test and OOD splits of DualTalk. The closest competitor is DualTalk [30], the only other dyadic interactive method; MimicTalker reduces FD-EXP from 11.14 to 7.12 (−36%) and rPCC-EXP from 4.73 to 4.16 (−12%), while increasing SID-EXP from 3.48 to 3.63. Among the talking-head baselines, EmoTalk [29] is the strongest (FD-EXP 29.86), yet MimicTalker still improves FD-EXP by over 4×. CodeTalker [40] and FaceFormer [11] perform worst on realism (FD-EXP 48.57 and 34.90) and have near-zero SID, indicating they produce highly repetitive motions. The listening-head baseline L2L [25] (FD-EXP 24.61) is substantially outperformed, confirming that modeling full dyadic interaction (both verbal and non-verbal) is critical. On the OOD set, the gap between MimicTalker and DualTalk narrows slightly (FD-EXP 15.89 vs. 21.71) but MimicTalker retains a clear lead across all metrics, demonstrating better generalization to unseen speakers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dyadic interactive head generation line (DualTalk, DIM, ARIG, OmniResponse) by making the entire pipeline causal and frame-by-frame, departing from the segment-level offline paradigm that dominates prior work. It repurposes LLM-based semantic analysis (GPT-4o for intentions/topics, RoBERTa for feature extraction) as a conditioning signal within a transformer-gated-memory architecture, a use of LLMs in real-time motion generation that is largely absent from prior talking/listening head literature. The Motion Style Memory mechanism draws on contrastive style encoders (StyleTalk) and external memory retrieval, but applies them in a conversation-intention-keyed manner that is novel to this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time dyadic interaction is a prerequisite for practical human–AI conversational agents in customer service, education, and entertainment, and this paper demonstrates that deep semantic understanding (via LLMs) plus long-term style memory can be integrated without sacrificing the 30 fps real-time threshold (achieving 300+ fps). Open questions include how the approach scales beyond two interlocutors, how robust the GPT-4o-based semantic analysis is to noisy or ambiguous transcripts, and whether the MSM degrades when conversations introduce genuinely novel intentions absent from the memory bank. The reliance on an external LLM call every k rounds also raises latency and cost concerns for deployment at scale.
