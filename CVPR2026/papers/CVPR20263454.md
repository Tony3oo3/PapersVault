## Problem

Video hallucination in VideoLLMs is largely under-characterized mechanistically: existing taxonomies and diagnostics are image-centric (object-level, token-level), while video errors often manifest as narrative distortion across frames. This paper identifies and formalizes Chimera Hallucination—a prevalent failure mode (34% of audited errors) where models stitch evidence from causally unrelated temporal segments into a spurious continuous story without fabricating any entities—and asks how to detect and correct this frame-level mis-assembly without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T=32 uniformly sampled video frames and a text question, processed through a standard VideoLLM in a single forward pass. Beyond the usual text answer, it outputs a CH-Risk score in [0,1] computed from mid-layer text-to-frame attention and early-middle-layer cross-frame attention centrality. Event segments are induced unsupervised per video by fusing adjacent-frame cosine similarity drops with cross-frame attention cuts (NMS with z-score thresholding), and the CH-Risk score gates whether the two-stage mitigation is activated (default τ=0.28), so low-risk samples incur virtually no extra cost.

## Method

CH-Risk combines two complementary signals in a single forward pass: SCR@α measures the minimal number of event segments needed to cover α (default 0.8) of the text-to-frame attention mass, exposing long-range stitching, while AETP computes the Spearman rank correlation between that support and the early-to-middle-layer cross-frame attention centrality, exposing stage mismatch; the product SCR@α·(1−AETP) yields the risk score. CH-M is a train-free, two-stage inference-time intervention gated by CH-Risk≥τ: first, sSAFR adds a small pre-softmax bias (λ·ĉ(f) for pathway alignment plus γ·1{f∈S*} for segment prior) at a chosen middle layer to re-route attention toward a minimal set of pathway-aligned event segments; second, RTC clips and renormalizes over-dominant per-frame visual token weights within those segments (cap s_f=ρ/N_f, default ρ=3) to suppress brittle single-token anchors while preserving frame-level mass. No parameters, architecture, or extra forward passes are modified; the total overhead is sub-5% latency, sub-2.5% memory, and ≈1% FLOPs.

## Results

CH-M consistently improves all 6 VideoLLM backbones across 9 video benchmarks, with accuracy gains ranging from +1.0% to +5.6%. The largest improvements appear on the hallucination-focused VidHalluc benchmark (+2.9% to +5.6%) and causal/temporal reasoning tasks like NExT-QA (+1.3% to +2.6%). CH-Risk drops by 0.07–0.10 and the high-risk fraction (CH-Risk ≥ 0.28) decreases by 9–14 percentage points across models. Overhead is negligible: sub-5% latency, sub-2.5% peak memory, and ≈1% FLOPs increase under the risk gate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against other hallucination-mitigation methods; instead it evaluates CH-M as a plug-in on six 7B VideoLLM backbones (Video-LLaVA, VideoLLaMA2, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-Video, VideoChat2, VideoLLaMA3). CH-M wins on every benchmark for every backbone, with no regressions observed. Weaker models benefit most: Video-LLaVA-7B gains +5.6% on VidHalluc and +2.6% on NExT-QA, while VideoChat2-7B gains +4.5% on VidHalluc. Stronger models show smaller but still consistent gains: VideoLLaMA3-7B improves by +2.9% on VidHalluc and +1.3% on NExT-QA, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +3.2% and +1.6% respectively. The risk diagnostics (Table 2) confirm that CH-Risk drops by 0.07–0.10 and the high-risk fraction falls by 9–14 pp across all six backbones, with weaker models (baseline AETP ≤ 0.43) showing the largest absolute risk reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant image-centric, token-level hallucination literature (object hallucination, relationship hallucination, decoding-time interventions) by reframing video hallucination at the frame and event-segment granularity. It builds on recent mechanistic analyses of VideoLLMs that reveal a phased temporal information flow (Kim et al. [18], Shi et al. [30]), but repurposes those observations into an operational risk measure (CH-Risk) and a train-free attention-routing intervention (CH-M) rather than a diagnostic or retraining pipeline. Within the train-free mitigation family, it is distinct in operating at segment-level routing and intra-segment token calibration rather than at the individual token or decoding-step level used by prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identifying Chimera Hallucination as a 34%-prevalent, mechanistically distinct failure mode gives the field a concrete target that prior object-centric taxonomies miss, and the frame-first formalization (event segments, causal-chain relations) provides a reusable vocabulary for future video-hallucination research. The train-free, sub-5%-overhead mitigation is immediately deployable, but the modest absolute gains (1–5% accuracy) and the reliance on a hand-calibrated threshold (τ=0.28) limit generality; open questions include whether the segment-boundary detection generalizes to very long or highly dynamic videos, whether the two-stage order (sSAFR→RTC) holds under different layer choices, and whether the approach transfers to audio-visual or embodied settings where event chains are less visually grounded.
