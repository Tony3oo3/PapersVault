## Problem

Applying standard GRPO to Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models for text-to-image generation is unstable because the number of query tokens fluctuates by orders of magnitude across coarse-to-fine resolution levels, creating asynchronous policy conflicts that degrade convergence and alignment. No prior work has systematically studied RL for next-scale VAR models, leaving their unique failure modes under heterogeneous, parallel token generation unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262668/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via the Nextflow (7B) VAR backbone, which emits a sequence of discrete token grids at progressively finer spatial resolutions (e.g., 1×1 → 2×2 → … → 32×32). During RL training, rewards are computed from the generated image using PaddleOCRv5 (for text rendering) or HPSv3 (for human preference), and the policy is optimized on-policy with group size 16 and batch size 16, without classifier-free guidance.

## Method

The framework enhances GRPO for VAR with three synergistic components. First, Value-as-Middle-Return (VMR) splits the full-horizon KL-regularized objective at a middle timestep m (default 256×256), using a risk-sensitive soft value estimate (η log-mean-exp of K=2 on-policy terminal rewards) as the prefix reward, which provably preserves the family-optimal policy while providing denser, lower-variance feedback to early steps. Second, Per-Action Normalization Weighting (PANW) scales each step's loss by (h_t × w_t)^(-α) (α≈0.6–0.8) to equalize gradient magnitudes across timesteps with vastly different token-grid sizes. Third, Mask Propagation (MP) constructs an initial spatial mask from reward-relevant regions (e.g., OCR bounding boxes) and propagates it backward through the multi-scale hierarchy, gating intermediate rewards and gradients to concentrate credit assignment on causally relevant tokens. Training alternates three prefix GRPO updates for every one suffix update, and the full pipeline is validated on CVTG-2K text rendering and HPSv3 human preference benchmarks.

## Results

On CVTG-2K (text rendering), Nextflow-RL achieves Word Accuracy 0.7841, NED 0.9081, and CLIPScore 0.8224, improving over the Nextflow base by +0.2305 (+41.6% relative) in Word Accuracy and +0.1265 (+16.2% relative) in NED. On HPSv3 (human preference), Nextflow-RL scores 10.64 on the overall 'All' metric, surpassing the strongest diffusion baselines (Kolors 10.55, Flux-dev 10.43) and improving over Nextflow (8.43) by +2.21 absolute. The method ranks first among all open-source models on CVTG-2K Word Accuracy and NED, trailing only proprietary systems (GPT Image 1, Qwen-Image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Nextflow-RL (7B) decisively outperforms its own base model Nextflow on all three CVTG-2K metrics (Word Acc. 0.7841 vs. 0.5536; NED 0.9081 vs. 0.7816; CLIPScore 0.8224 vs. 0.8068), demonstrating that the RL framework adds substantial value without architectural changes. Among open-source diffusion models, Nextflow-RL surpasses TextCrafter(SD3.5) (0.7600/0.9038/0.8023), FLUX.1 dev (0.4965/0.6879/0.7401), and SD3.5 Large (0.6548/0.8470/0.7797) on every metric, and leads all open models on Word Accuracy and NED. It trails only proprietary systems GPT Image 1 (0.8569/0.9478) and Qwen-Image (0.8288/0.9116) on CVTG-2K. On HPSv3, Nextflow-RL achieves the best 'All' score (10.64) among all listed models, edging out Kolors (10.55) and Flux-dev (10.43), and dominates in sub-categories including Architecture (11.16), Animals (10.79), Natural Scenery (10.43), Plants (10.57), and Food (11.18). The only categories where it is not first are Characters (11.72 vs. Kolors 11.79) and Design (9.77 vs. Kolors 9.87), where the gap is marginal (<0.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO-based RL alignment—previously applied to diffusion models (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) and raster-scan AR models (AR-GRPO, SimpleAR, T2I-R1)—to the next-scale prediction VAR architecture, where each step emits a parallel token grid whose spatial dimensions change across resolution levels. The theoretical foundation draws on KL-regularized RL (Ziebart et al.) and hierarchical RL (Nachum et al.) to justify a two-stage prefix/suffix decomposition (VMR) that provably preserves the family-optimal policy. The base model is Nextflow (TokenFlow), and the reward design leverages PaddleOCR for text rendering and HPSv3 for human preference, following the reward-driven RL paradigm established in LLM alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic RL framework for text-to-image VAR models, identifying and resolving the asynchronous policy conflict that makes vanilla GRPO unstable under heterogeneous, multi-scale parallel actions. The structure-preserving reward shaping (VMR) and the per-step normalization (PANW) are general principles that could transfer to other coarse-to-fine generative architectures. However, validation is limited to a single base model (Nextflow, 7B) at 1024×1024 resolution and primarily two tasks (text rendering, HPSv3); open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other VAR variants (Infinity, VAR-GPT), video generation, or substantially different reward structures, and whether the mask propagation mechanism scales to more complex compositional tasks.
