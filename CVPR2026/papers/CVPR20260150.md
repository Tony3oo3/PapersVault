## Problem

Existing unsupervised anomaly detection methods rely on matching test images against normal templates, but this matching inevitably introduces noise from intra-class variations, imperfect correspondences, and limited template availability. Most prior approaches treat retrieval and scoring as a single step, overlooking a generative reasoning stage that could suppress hallucinatory detection noise such as blurred boundaries and missed subtle defects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260150/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256 for full-shot/multi-dataset or 224×224 for few-shot evaluation) and encodes it via a frozen DINOv2-s backbone into patch tokens and a CLS token. It produces a pixel-level anomaly map at the token spatial resolution (H'×W') and an image-level anomaly score computed as the mean of the top 1% highest map responses. A pre-built hierarchical vector database of normal template tokens (80–100 templates per class for MVTec/VisA, all normals for MPDD/BTAD) is queried at inference time.

## Method

RAID reinterprets UAD as a Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline with two stages. The retrieval stage uses a three-level hierarchical vector database—class prototypes (K-means on CLS tokens), semantic prototypes (K-means on per-class patch tokens), and instance tokens (all patch tokens)—to perform coarse-to-fine retrieval that narrows each query token's search from all templates to K=150 highly relevant candidates. The generation stage constructs a 3D matching cost volume (H'×W'×K) from cosine similarities between query tokens and retrieved instance tokens, then refines it through a two-stage guided Mixture-of-Experts filter: a sparse-routing guidance-fusion MoE fuses dual guidance (retrieved semantic prototypes and query tokens), and a dense-routing denoising MoE applies cross-attention and convolutional branches under that guidance to suppress matching noise. Training is self-supervised with synthetic anomaly masks, using focal loss plus an expert-balancing regularizer, and the entire pipeline is category- and dataset-agnostic.

## Results

In the full-shot multi-class UAD setting, RAID achieves 99.4% I-AUROC / 98.6% P-AUROC on MVTec-AD, 94.9% / 99.0% on VisA, 96.3% / 98.9% on MPDD, and 95.2% / 97.6% on BTAD, setting new state-of-the-art on all four benchmarks. In the 1-shot few-shot setting on MVTec-AD, RAID reaches 95.1% I-AUROC / 96.6% P-AUROC without any text priors. Under the multi-dataset regime (36 classes jointly), RAID attains 95.4% I-AUROC / 96.7% I-AP / 98.5% P-AUROC / 57.0% P-AP, outperforming the prior best by 3.4% in I-AUROC and 8.1% in P-AP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAID outperforms all 16 baselines across every evaluation setting. In full-shot UAD, the closest competitor is CostFilter-AD [68], which RAID surpasses by +0.4% I-AUROC / +0.6% P-AUROC on MVTec-AD, +1.5% / +0.4% on VisA, +3.2% / +1.4% on MPDD, and +1.9% / +0.3% on BTAD; gains over GLAD [59] and AnomalyDINO [11] are even larger (up to +5.5% I-AUROC on MPDD). In the 1-shot few-shot setting on MVTec-AD, RAID (95.1% I-AUROC) beats DFM [57] by 7.9% and IIPAD [42] by 0.9%, while operating purely on images without the text priors used by WinCLIP [26] and PromptAD [33]. On VisA 1-shot, RAID (85.8% I-AUROC) edges out IIPAD (85.4%) and DFM (84.0%). In the multi-dataset regime (36 classes), RAID (95.4% I-AUROC / 57.0% P-AP) exceeds OneNIP [16] (92.0% / 48.9%) by 3.4% and 8.1% respectively, with the largest per-dataset gain on MPDD (+8.4% I-AUROC). Notably, RAID's pixel-level AP on MVTec-AD (71.7%) represents a substantial jump over the previous best (61.3% by AnomalyDINO), indicating markedly sharper anomaly boundary localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAID reinterprets unsupervised anomaly detection through the Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) paradigm, positioning existing reconstruction-based (GLAD, DiAD, ViTAD) and embedding-based (PatchCore, AnomalyDINO) methods as partial RAG pipelines that lack an explicit generative reasoning stage. Technically, it builds directly on CostFilter-AD's matching cost volume construction but replaces its flat global matching with a three-level hierarchical vector database (class prototype → semantic prototype → instance token) that narrows the search space via coarse-to-fine retrieval. The generation stage is recast as a guided Mixture-of-Experts filtering network that denoises the cost volume under dual guidance from retrieved semantic prototypes and input tokens, departing from prior work that treats retrieval as the final step without a subsequent refinement mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RAG framing provides a principled, extensible lens for UAD that separates retrieval quality from generation quality, making it easier to diagnose and fix matching noise—a persistent weakness in template-based detection. RAID's consistent gains across full-shot, few-shot (without language priors), and multi-dataset (36 classes) settings suggest the hierarchical retrieval + guided MoE design generalizes well beyond single-dataset benchmarks. Open questions include whether the RAG paradigm extends to cross-modal or agentic anomaly detection (as the authors envision), whether the DINOv2 backbone dependency limits transfer to non-industrial domains, and whether the MoE filtering stage can be made more interpretable for industrial deployment where explainability matters.
