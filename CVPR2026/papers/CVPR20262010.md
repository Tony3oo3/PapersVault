## Problem

While practical degeneracy tests exist for planar scenes and pure rotations (via homography estimation), no stable test was available for the general case where 3D points and camera centers lie on an arbitrary ruled quadric (hyperboloid, cone, or cylinder). The only existing test for this general critical surface (Luong & Faugeras) is highly unstable because it requires a precomputed fundamental matrix and iterative nonlinear optimization, creating a circular dependency where the very instability one wants to detect undermines the detection itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262010/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 8 normalized 2D point correspondences between two views; 7 are used to fit the quadratic transformation ψ by solving a 14×14 linear system, and the 8th serves as a held-out test point. The output is a binary degeneracy label determined by whether the reprojection error ‖y₈ − ψ(x₈)‖ falls below a threshold ε (comparable to standard RANSAC inlier thresholds), along with the explicit quadratic transformation parameters. The algorithm extends naturally to more than 8 correspondences via least-squares fitting with one point held out for error evaluation.

## Method

The key insight is a novel parametrization of the quadratic transformation ψ using the geometry of homaloidal nets of conics: ψ is expressed as a pair of bilinear equations y₀L₀ + y₁L₁ + y₂L₂ = 0 and y₀M₀ + y₁M₁ + y₂M₂ = 0, where Lᵢ and Mᵢ are linear forms. Although the general form has 18 parameters, Proposition 3 shows that the homaloidal net is invariant under 2×2 invertible row operations on the (L,M) matrix, allowing four parameters (l₀₀, l₁₀, m₀₀, m₁₀) to be fixed arbitrarily without changing ψ. This reduces the free parameters to 14, matching the 14 degrees of freedom of a quadratic transformation, so 7 correspondences (each yielding two linear constraints) exactly determine ψ via a single linear solve. No fundamental matrix estimation or nonlinear optimization is required, making the test both more numerically stable and ~200× faster than the Luong–Faugeras baseline.

## Results

On noiseless synthetic critical configurations (100 trials), the proposed method achieves a median error of 9×10⁻¹⁵ (range [1×10⁻¹⁶, 7×10⁻¹²]), compared to a median of 3×10⁻⁵ (range up to 0.96) for Luong–Faugeras [19]. In the noiseless classification experiment (500 degenerate + 500 non-degenerate points), the proposed test achieves a perfect F1 score of 1.0 across a wide range of thresholds, while [19] never reaches F1 = 1. Runtime is 8.5 ms per test versus over 1.65 s for [19], a 200× speedup. On a real-world cylindrical critical configuration, the eighth-point error is 4×10⁻⁵ pixels, correctly flagging the configuration as degenerate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Luong–Faugeras [19] method, which also fits a quadratic transformation from 7 correspondences and tests the 8th point, but requires a precomputed fundamental matrix and a non-linear iterative refinement. The proposed method dominates on every reported metric: (i) numerical stability — median noiseless error is ~12 orders of magnitude lower (9×10⁻¹⁵ vs 3×10⁻⁵); (ii) classification accuracy — achieves F1 = 1.0 over a wide threshold range in the noiseless setting, whereas [19] never reaches perfect F1; (iii) robustness to 2D Gaussian noise — maintains near-perfect F1 up to σ ≈ 10⁻² while [19] degrades sharply; (iv) speed — 8.5 ms vs >1.65 s (200× faster) because the proposed method solves a single 14×14 linear system rather than running the 7-point algorithm plus three non-linear lsqnonlin refinements. The paper does not report any metric on which [19] outperforms the proposed method.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revises the framework of Luong and Faugeras [19], who proved that critical correspondences are related by a quadratic transformation and proposed a degeneracy test requiring a precomputed fundamental matrix and nonlinear optimization. The key departure is a reparametrization of the quadratic transformation using the classical theory of homaloidal nets of conics [4], which had never been applied to degeneracy detection. By exploiting the invariance of the homaloidal net under 2×2 invertible row operations (Proposition 3), the authors fix four parameters, reducing the free parameter count from 18 to 14 and enabling a purely linear estimation from seven correspondences—eliminating the fundamental matrix dependency entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This fills a long-standing practical gap: before this work, no stable, efficient degeneracy test existed for general critical surfaces (hyperboloids, cones, cylinders), with only planar-scene and pure-rotation cases having widely adopted homography-based tests. The 200× speedup (8.5 ms vs. 1.65 s) and orders-of-magnitude improvement in numerical stability make the test viable for integration into RANSAC-based SFM pipelines. Open questions include the relationship between the quadratic transformation ψ and homography-based tests for planar scenes, whether ψ can be leveraged to extract 3D scene structure, and broader validation on real-world datasets beyond the single cylindrical-layout example shown.
