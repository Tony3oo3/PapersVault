## Problem

Text-to-LiDAR generation suffers from two critical gaps: (1) the extreme scarcity of Text-LiDAR pairs (fewer than 35K in nuScenes) leads to insufficient training priors, causing generative models to produce overly smooth scenes lacking distinct object structures and realistic geometric details; (2) existing benchmarks provide unnatural text descriptions and lack controllability evaluation metrics, limiting both generation quality and the ability to assess whether generated scenes match text semantics.

## Input / Output

The method consumes natural language text prompts (encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder into 768-dimensional features) and prior Gaussian noise, operating on LiDAR data projected into range maps of shape H×W×2 (32×1024×2 for 32-beam nuScenes, 64×1024×2 for 64-beam KITTI-360) where channels encode depth and intensity. The output is a LiDAR point cloud scene recovered via inverse spherical projection from the generated range map. For conditional variants, the input additionally includes sparse LiDAR point clouds (for upsampling), dense LiDAR (for downsampling), or semantic maps, all processed through a non-latent ControlNet-style adapter on a frozen denoising network.

## Method

T2LDM is a conditional DDPM with a U-Net denoising network that introduces two key mechanisms. First, Self-Conditioned Representation Guidance (SCRG) employs a separate Guidance Network that receives multi-level perturbation features from the Denoising Network via cross-attention, aligns them with real coordinates to reconstruct geometric details, and feeds multi-scale reconstruction features back as regularization to the Denoising Network; the Guidance Network participates in gradient backpropagation only during the first 100K training steps and is fully detached at inference, adding no inference cost. Second, a Directional Position Encoding (DPE) injects spherical geometric orientation priors by applying multi-level Fourier expansions of the horizontal and vertical field-of-view angles at each range-map pixel, with a learnable gating parameter to modulate multi-frequency contributions, thereby correcting the directional confusion inherent in flattening a spherical projection into a rectangular image. The training objective combines the standard velocity-prediction diffusion loss with a reconstruction loss and a cosine-similarity alignment loss between Guidance and Denoising Network features, weighted by an epoch-wise factor.

## Results

On KITTI-360 (64-beam), T2LDM achieves FSVD of 21.12 and FPVD of 25.39, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline R2DM (31.82 / 35.94) and Text2LiDAR (51.55 / 54.82). On nuScenes (32-beam) text-guided generation, T2LDM reaches FSVD 66.93, FPVD 65.84, JSD 0.28, MMD 3.05, and a controllability score (TBR) of 23.44%, compared to R2DM (91.15 / 88.55 / 0.45 / 5.11 / 15.45%) and Text2LiDAR (90.13 / 87.62 / 0.38 / 4.01 / 17.15%). For sparse-to-dense upsampling at 4×, T2LDM achieves CD 0.104×10⁻⁵, MSE 3.610×10⁻⁵, EMD 1.987×10⁻³, beating PUDM (0.198 / 4.275 / 2.124) and Grad-PU (0.400 / 4.169 / 2.324).

## Baselines & Comparisons

T2LDM outperforms all seven baselines on KITTI-360 across every metric. The closest competitor, R2DM [36], is beaten by 34% in FSVD (21.12 vs. 31.82) and 29% in FPVD (25.39 vs. 35.94), while the earlier diffusion-based Text2LiDAR [55] trails by 59% in FSVD (21.12 vs. 51.55). GAN-based methods (LiDARVAE, LiDARGAN, ProjectedGAN, LiDARGen) and the VAE-based LiDM [46] are all far behind, with FSVD values ranging from 187 to 346. On nuScenes text-guided generation, T2LDM improves TBR controllability from 15.45% (R2DM) and 17.15% (Text2LiDAR) to 23.44%, a relative gain of 52% and 37% respectively. For the sparse-to-dense upsampling task, T2LDM surpasses both PUDM [42] and Grad-PU [15] on all three metrics (CD, MSE, EMD) at both 4× and 8× rates, with the largest margin over Grad-PU (CD 0.104 vs. 0.400 at 4×). No baseline is competitive with T2LDM on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

T2LDM extends the conditional DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) to LiDAR scene generation, building directly on the range-map representation and text-conditioned pipeline established by R2DM [36] and Text2LiDAR [55]. Its key architectural departure is the Self-Conditioned Representation Guidance (SCRG), which adapts the idea of representation-regularized diffusion from image generation (Li et al. [23]) to an end-to-end, pretrained-prior-free setting suited to the scarce Text-LiDAR data regime. The paper also introduces a Directional Position Encoding to correct a known artifact of spherical projection, and is the first to apply non-latent ControlNet [64] to 3D diffusion for multi-task conditional generation (sparse-to-dense, dense-to-sparse, semantic-to-LiDAR).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The T2nuScenes benchmark with its 3D-box-based composable text annotation and TBR controllability metric fills a practical gap: prior LiDAR datasets offered only unnatural, non-composable captions with no way to measure whether generated scenes actually match the prompt. The counterintuitive finding that explicit spatial-location prompts degrade both quality and controllability (due to sample-distribution sparsity) provides actionable guidance for future Text-LiDAR annotation efforts. Open questions include whether SCRG's benefits persist at larger data scales where the prior-scarcity motivation weakens, and whether the range-map formulation limits generalization to non-spherical or multi-sensor LiDAR configurations.
