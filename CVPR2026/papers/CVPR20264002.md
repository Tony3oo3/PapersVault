## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary temporal action detection methods rely on global alignment between label-level semantics and visual features, which fails to capture fine-grained phase-level visual patterns that are transferable across semantically distinct actions. For example, LongJump and PoleVault share visually similar start and middle phases despite low label-level similarity, yet global alignment cannot exploit these shared temporal cues for knowledge transfer to unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264002/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes pre-extracted two-stream I3D visual features from untrimmed videos (processed through a temporal transformer) and action labels that are first decomposed by GPT-4o via CoT prompting into four phase-level textual descriptions (start, middle, end, global), each encoded by a CLIP text encoder. At inference, the model outputs per-timestep action class confidence scores and start/end boundary distances, which are post-processed with SoftNMS to yield final action proposals. The LLM decomposition is performed once per label (offline), while the visual-textual alignment is computed per video at test time.

## Method

The PDA framework introduces three modules: (1) CoT-Prompting Semantic Decomposition (CSD) uses GPT-4o's chain-of-thought reasoning to automatically decompose each action label into four coherent phase descriptions, producing richer and more transferable semantics than raw label embeddings; (2) Text-infused Foreground Filtering (TIF) computes per-phase foreground confidence by taking the max similarity across all training classes between visual features and phase-specific text embeddings, binarizes with a mean threshold, and applies the binary mask to extract phase-specific visual representations; (3) Adaptive Phase-wise Alignment (APA) performs cross-attention to infuse text into each phase's visual features, computes per-phase classification scores, and adaptively aggregates them using sigmoid-gated phase-specific weights learned by a weighting network. For localization, all phase visual features are concatenated and projected through an MLP, then fed to a foreground-aware head and a DIoU regression head. The overall loss combines cross-entropy classification, foreground-aware, and DIoU localization terms.

## Results

On THUMOS14 under the 50% Seen / 50% Unseen split, the proposed PDA framework achieves 46.9% average mAP, surpassing the previous SOTA Ti-FAD (41.2%) by 5.7 absolute points (13.8% relative lift). On ActivityNet v1.3 under the same split, it reaches 34.6% average mAP versus Ti-FAD's 32.0% (8.1% relative lift). Under the 75% Seen / 25% Unseen split, the method achieves 52.1% and 37.4% average mAP on THUMOS14 and ActivityNet v1.3 respectively, maintaining consistent superiority across all tIoU thresholds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines on both datasets and both splits. The closest competitor is Ti-FAD (NeurIPS'24), which the PDA framework surpasses by 5.7 points on THUMOS14 (46.9 vs 41.2) and 2.6 points on ActivityNet v1.3 (34.6 vs 32.0) under the 50%/50% split, and by 5.3 and 2.7 points respectively under the 75%/25% split. Among open-vocabulary methods, STOV (WACV'25) and ZEETAD (WACV'24) are the next strongest baselines, trailing by 12.9 and 16.7 points on THUMOS14 (50%/50%), while closed-set methods adapted to the OV setting (TriDet, DyFADet, DiGIT) lag far behind, achieving only 10.5–13.0% average mAP on THUMOS14. The gap over Ti-FAD is most pronounced at stricter tIoU thresholds (e.g., 24.3 vs 21.2 at tIoU 0.7 on THUMOS14), suggesting the phase-wise alignment improves boundary precision. The paper attributes the gains to the CSD module's CoT-based decomposition capturing transferable phase-level semantics, combined with TIF's adaptive foreground filtering and APA's dynamic phase weighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OV-TAD line of work (EffPrompt, STALE, DeTAL, CSP, ZEETAD, STOV, Ti-FAD) by departing from the prevailing paradigm of global label-level alignment between CLIP text embeddings and visual features. It builds directly on Ti-FAD's foreground-aware modeling and DIoU localization framework, but restructures the semantic side by leveraging LLM chain-of-thought reasoning to decompose each action label into temporally coherent phase descriptions (start, middle, end, global). This connects to prior LLM-based label expansion work (e.g., multi-attribute decomposition, multi-dimensional description generation) but distinguishes itself by using CoT specifically for temporal phase decomposition rather than merely enriching static textual semantics, and by coupling this with adaptive phase-wise visual filtering and alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The phase-wise decomposition-and-alignment strategy addresses a concrete limitation in OV-TAD: globally aligning a single label embedding with averaged visual features cannot capture the fine-grained temporal patterns that are actually transferable across semantically distinct actions. The compelling semantic similarity visualizations (e.g., LongJump and PoleVault sharing start/middle phases) provide intuitive grounding for why this works. Limitations include reliance on LLM quality for decomposition (though backbone robustness is demonstrated), the somewhat arbitrary choice of three phases plus global, and evaluation limited to two datasets with relatively small unseen class counts. Open questions include whether richer or learned phase structures could further improve transfer, and whether this decomposition-alignment paradigm generalizes to other open-vocabulary video understanding tasks beyond TAD.
