## Problem

Existing 3D-aware video MLLMs face a trilemma: explicit coordinate injection requires unavailable depth/pose inputs at inference and destroys fine-grained geometry through aggressive downsampling and voxelization ("double information loss"), while dual-encoder designs grafting external 3D foundation models increase parameters and latency while creating a representation gap between frozen 2D and 3D streams. The paper asks whether a single RGB-only encoder can internalize sufficient 3D structure for grounding and reasoning without any geometric inputs or auxiliary encoders at test time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263624/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model consumes 32 uniformly sampled RGB video frames (no depth maps, camera poses, or point clouds) plus a natural-language instruction, and produces 3D bounding boxes for grounding, region-level captions, or free-form QA answers. During training, ground-truth depth maps and known inter-frame relative poses serve as privileged supervision signals that are entirely discarded at test time, so the deployment interface is identical to a standard video-LLM. The visual encoder is SigLIP producing patch-level tokens, projected into a Qwen2-7B language backbone in a LLaVA-Next-Video-style architecture.

## Method

3D-IDE is guided by the Implicit Geometric Emergence Principle: rather than injecting 3D coordinates or fusing external 3D features, a lightweight DPT-style Auxiliary Geometric Validator (deliberately trained from scratch to remain low-capacity) is attached to the shared SigLIP encoder only during training, predicting dense depth and pixel-wise uncertainty maps. Three composite auxiliary losses drive geometry into the encoder tokens: an uncertainty-aware depth reconstruction loss with gradient consistency, a localized cross-view consistency loss that warps predicted depth between randomly sampled neighboring frames using known relative poses, and a global scene-level cosine-alignment loss against a frozen VGGT foundation model. Because the validator is weak and discarded at inference, the encoder cannot offload 3D reasoning to it and must organize geometric structure directly within its unified feature space (F_t^3D ≡ F_t). At test time, the validator and 3D teacher are removed entirely, yielding zero additional latency and a strictly RGB-only inference pipeline that achieves SOTA 3D grounding accuracy with 55% lower inference time than dual-encoder baselines.

## Results

On the RGB-only generalist setting, 3D-IDE achieves 60.9% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer, 59.8% F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer, 79.0 CIDEr on Scan2Cap, 29.8% EM on ScanQA, and 59.2% EM on SQA3D, setting new state-of-the-art on all grounding and QA metrics among methods without explicit 3D inputs. Compared to the closest RGB-only competitor VG-LLM-8B, 3D-IDE improves ScanRefer Acc@0.25 by 3.3 points while using 12.86% fewer parameters and running 55.3% faster (1.61 s vs 3.60 s mean inference time). On the geometric representation probes (NAVI), 3D-IDE improves correspondence recall@2cm from 40.15 to 42.27 and reduces surface-normal RMSE from 32.26° to 31.36° over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among RGB-only generalists, 3D-IDE clearly outperforms all competitors: it beats VG-LLM-8B by 3.3 points on ScanRefer Acc@0.25 (60.9 vs 57.6) and by 12.8 points on Multi3DRefer F1@0.25 (59.8 vs no reported score, but VG-LLM-4B reports none), and surpasses VID-LLM by 10.8 points on ScanRefer (60.9 vs 50.1) and 12.6 points on Multi3DRefer (59.8 vs 47.2). Against the RGB-only baseline Video-3D LLM*, the gains are dramatic on Scan2Cap CIDEr (79.0 vs 31.5) and moderate on ScanRefer (60.9 vs 53.7). Versus methods that use explicit 3D geometric inputs at inference, 3D-IDE matches or exceeds Video-3D LLM (60.9 vs 58.1 on ScanRefer) and LLaVA-3D (60.9 vs 54.1), but trails the strongest 3D-input methods 3DRS (62.9) and Inst3D-LLM (57.8 on ScanRefer; 58.3 on Multi3DRefer) by 1–2 points on grounding. On Scan2Cap, 3D-IDE (79.0 CIDEr) is competitive with 3D-input methods like LLaVA-3D (79.2) and Inst3D-LLM (79.7) but falls short of Video-3D LLM (83.8) and 3DRS (86.1). In terms of efficiency, 3D-IDE uses 8.06B parameters vs 9.25B for VG-LLM-8B and achieves 10.72 tokens/s vs 4.32 tokens/s, a 2.5× throughput advantage with 13% less peak GPU memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms for injecting 3D awareness into video-MLLMs: explicit per-pixel coordinate injection (Video-3D LLM, 3DRS, LLaVA-3D) and dual-encoder fusion with external 3D foundation models (Vid-LLM, VG-LLM). Building on the LLaVA-Next-Video architecture and the Video-3D LLM training pipeline, it introduces the Implicit Geometric Emergence Principle, which reframes geometry as privileged training-time supervision rather than an inference-time input. The method uses a lightweight DPT-style validator and a frozen VGGT teacher only during training, then discards both at inference, leaving a single RGB-only encoder that has internalized 3D structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating depth/pose inputs and external 3D encoders at inference reduces latency by ~55% and removes a critical deployment dependency on specialized sensors, making 3D scene understanding more broadly applicable. However, the approach still requires ground-truth depth from ScanNet during training, so generalization to environments without depth annotations (e.g., outdoor scenes, novel indoor layouts) remains untested. An open question is whether the emergent 3D representation transfers to tasks beyond the ScanNet-derived benchmarks, and whether the information-bottleneck framing can be extended to other modalities (e.g., audio-visual 3D understanding).
