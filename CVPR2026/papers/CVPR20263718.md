## Problem

Existing GUI grounding models exhibit severe coordinate prediction instability: minor visual perturbations (e.g., a 28-pixel border shift) can flip predictions between correct and incorrect, with the average coordinate shift reaching 193 pixels. This instability is particularly acute for high-resolution screenshots and small UI elements, where RoPE extrapolation limits and lossy visual projectors compound the problem, causing single-inference accuracy to fall well below the model's true capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263718/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution GUI screenshot (e.g., professional software at 2K+ resolution) and a natural language instruction as input. It internally crops multiple 1280×720 sub-regions from the screenshot, resizes each to 2560×1440, and runs independent inference on each view plus the original full image. The final output is a single (x, y) pixel coordinate in the original image space, determined by spatial clustering of all m+1 predictions.

## Method

MVP is a training-free, two-stage inference framework. First, Attention-Guided View Proposal computes cross-attention scores using the comma token from the predicted coordinate format as query against visual tokens, selects the top-k highest-scoring visual patches, crops h×w sub-regions around each, ranks candidates by how many top-k tokens they contain, and resizes the top-m regions by a factor α to enhance small-target visibility. Second, Multi-Coordinate Clustering performs independent inference on each cropped view and the original image, then applies K-means clustering (threshold τ=14 px) over all m+1 predicted coordinates and outputs the centroid of the largest cluster as the final prediction, with ties broken by the attention-based region ranking. The core insight is that correct predictions from diverse views spatially concentrate within the target bounding box while incorrect ones scatter arbitrarily, making the densest cluster a reliable signal.

## Results

On ScreenSpot-Pro, MVP delivers substantial gains across all tested models: UI-TARS-1.5-7B improves from 41.9% to 56.1% (+14.2), GTA1-7B from 49.8% to 61.7% (+11.9), Qwen3VL-8B-Instruct from 55.0% to 65.3% (+10.3), and Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct from 55.3% to 74.0% (+18.7). On UI-Vision, Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct with MVP reaches 44.1% average (from 36.4%), surpassing the 72B UI-Venus-Ground-72B (36.8%). On OS-World-G, gains are more modest (0.3–4.9 points) due to lower-resolution inputs that inherently cause less prediction instability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MVP's most striking result is Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct reaching 74.0% on ScreenSpot-Pro, surpassing all prior open- and closed-source models including UI-TARS-1.5 (61.6%), UI-Venus-72B (61.9%), and Seed1.5-VL (60.9%) by 12+ points. Notably, the 7B GTA1-7B with MVP (61.7%) outperforms the 10× larger GTA1-72B (58.4%), and Qwen3VL-8B + MVP (65.3%) exceeds UI-TARS-1.5 (61.6%) and UI-Venus-72B (61.9%). On UI-Vision, Qwen3VL-32B + MVP (44.1%) beats UI-Venus-Ground-72B (36.8%) by 7.3 points, while 7B models gain 3.4–4.7 points over their baselines. On OS-World-G, improvements are smaller (0.3–4.9 points) because the 720P/1080P resolution causes less instability; Qwen3VL-8B + MVP (72.7%) still edges past GTA1-72B (66.7%). MVP consistently wins over its own baselines across all three benchmarks and all four model architectures, with the largest gains on high-resolution, small-element scenarios (CAD, OS categories on ScreenSpot-Pro show +15–33 point improvements).

## Relation to Prior Work

MVP carves new ground by reframing the GUI grounding problem around a diagnosed failure mode—coordinate prediction instability—rather than proposing yet another training recipe or iterative zoom-in strategy. It repurposes cross-attention scores (previously used in attention-based grounding methods like V2P and GUI-Actor) not as a direct coordinate predictor but as a guide for generating diverse cropped views, then applies spatial clustering to aggregate predictions. This departs from sequential iterative zoom-in methods (RegionFocus, GUI-Spotlight) by using a parallel multi-view strategy that avoids error propagation, and from pure attention-based methods by preserving the standard text-generation output paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of MVP makes it immediately applicable to any existing grounding model without retraining, and the 10–19 point gains on ScreenSpot-Pro across four architectures (7B–32B) demonstrate broad practical utility. The diagnosis that models possess the grounding capability (pass@N improves with more views) but fail to express it consistently in a single forward pass is a useful lens for understanding LVLM limitations on high-resolution, small-target scenarios. Open questions include whether the instability diagnosis generalizes beyond GUI grounding to other spatial prediction tasks, whether the fixed view size (1280×720) and clustering threshold (14 px) transfer across domains, and whether the added inference cost (m+1 forward passes) is acceptable in latency-sensitive agent deployments.
