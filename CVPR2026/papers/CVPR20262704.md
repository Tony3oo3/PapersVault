## Problem

Planning-oriented end-to-end driving models learn statistical correlations rather than true causal relationships, making them vulnerable to causal confusion where they exploit dataset biases (e.g., ego-vehicle history shortcuts, scene co-occurrence patterns) as spurious decision shortcuts. While causal confusion is recognized in the literature, no practical, scalable mechanism exists to integrate rigorous causal intervention theory into the online training of large-scale end-to-end driving models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262704/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images and ego-vehicle kinematic history (velocity, acceleration) within a 60 m × 30 m perception range, predicting a 3-second trajectory from 2 seconds of history. Beyond the final trajectory, it produces structured intermediate outputs: sparse vectorized queries for object detection, map elements, and agent motion. A key unusual aspect is the offline pre-computation of a multi-modal confounder dictionary via K-means++ clustering on query embeddings from a frozen VAD model, which is then frozen and injected into the network during training.

## Method

CausalVAD introduces the Sparse Causal Intervention Scheme (SCIS), which operationalizes Pearl's backdoor adjustment formula P(Y|do(S)) = Σ_z P(Y|S,Z=z)P(Z=z) inside a neural network by replacing the latent confounder Z with a learnable proxy: a dictionary of prototypes extracted via K-means++ clustering on VAD's sparse query embeddings. Two plug-and-play modules implement the intervention at different pipeline stages: the Perception De-confounding Module (PDM) adjusts classification logits by subtracting a bias score derived from the confounder dictionary, and the Interaction De-confounding Module (IDM) uses cross-attention to estimate the spurious component of a query predictable from a context dictionary, then subtracts it via a learned gating unit. These modules are applied in a cascading, stage-wise fashion—first on perception classification paths, then on object/map queries before prediction, and finally on agent/map queries before planning—severing distinct backdoor paths at each information hub. The entire CausalVAD network is trained end-to-end from scratch with the original VAD loss, ensuring de-confounded causal relationships are learned from the outset rather than corrected post hoc.

## Results

On nuScenes, CausalVAD achieves the lowest average L2 displacement error (0.54 m) and a collision rate of 0.11% at 5.4 FPS, outperforming its VAD-tiny baseline by 27% in L2 and 75% in collision rate. On NAVSIM, it scores 87.2 PDMS, surpassing VAD (81.2) and approaching DiffusionDrive (88.1). On the closed-loop Bench2Drive simulator, CausalVAD attains a driving score of 49.83 and success rate of 19.42%, exceeding VAD (44.35 DS / 16.91% SR) but trailing the specialized DriveMoE (74.22 DS / 48.64% SR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalVAD outperforms all end-to-end baselines on nuScenes L2 error, achieving 0.54 m versus UniAD (0.73 m), VAD (0.62 m), PPAD (0.58 m), SparseDrive (0.61 m), and Senna (0.59 m). On collision rate, it ties or beats most competitors (0.11% avg), with only BridgeAD (0.08%) and SparseDrive (0.10%) marginally lower, while ORION achieves a lower L2 (0.34 m) but at the cost of a much higher collision rate (0.37%). On NAVSIM, CausalVAD (87.2 PDMS) surpasses VAD (81.2) and VAD-tiny (80.5) but falls slightly short of DiffusionDrive (88.1). On Bench2Drive, CausalVAD (49.83 DS, 19.42% SR) clearly beats VAD (44.35 DS, 16.91% SR) and UniAD (45.81 DS, 16.36% SR), yet remains well behind the closed-loop-optimized DriveMoE (74.22 DS, 48.64% SR), indicating that causal de-confounding improves generalization but does not fully close the gap with architectures specifically designed for closed-loop interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalVAD builds directly on the VAD vectorized-query architecture and positions itself as orthogonal to the architectural innovations (UniAD, SparseDrive, PPAD) that dominate recent end-to-end driving research. It departs from the standard supervised learning objective by operationalizing Pearl's backdoor adjustment as two plug-and-play modules (PDM and IDM) that intervene on sparse query representations using a pre-computed confounder dictionary. The paper explicitly contrasts its approach with two prior directions: heuristic de-biasing (state dropout, data augmentation) and VLM-based explanation layers, arguing that neither addresses the root cause of spurious correlation in the learning objective itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 75% collision-rate reduction and the demonstrated robustness to ego-velocity shortcuts and scenario distribution bias address a safety-critical gap that architectural improvements alone have not resolved. The plug-and-play, training-from-scratch design makes the causal intervention framework broadly applicable to other vectorized-query driving models without architectural redesign. Key open questions include whether the manually specified causal graph generalizes to parallel or iterative interaction architectures (acknowledged by the authors), whether the offline confounder dictionary remains valid under domain shift, and whether the approach extends beyond the VAD pipeline to rasterized-BEV or diffusion-based planners.
