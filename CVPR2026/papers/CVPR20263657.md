## Problem

VLM-based reward models rely on CLIP-style encoders that prioritize semantic content over perceptual qualities like aesthetics and composition, making them poor judges of visual quality. Additionally, prevailing RL alignment algorithms such as GRPO treat the multi-step denoising process as a black box, assigning a single terminal reward uniformly across all timesteps, which leads to imprecise credit assignment and unstable policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263657/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The DRM takes a noisy latent x_t at an arbitrary timestep t, a text prompt c, and the timestep value as input, and outputs a scalar preference score. During training, clean images are first encoded to latents via a VAE encoder, then corrupted with Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled timestep before being scored; images are processed at 512×512 resolution. For Step-GRPO, the method consumes k SDE-sampled candidate latents per timestep and produces a normalized per-step advantage; for Step-wise Sampling, it scores k branched candidates at each of 50 inference steps and selects the argmax to continue the trajectory.

## Method

The core insight is that a pre-trained diffusion model's generative prior encodes a deep understanding of visual aesthetics, making its DiT backbone a more perceptually sensitive reward model than CLIP-style encoders. The DRM architecture truncates the last three transformer layers of SD3.5-Medium's DiT (~2B params), feeds noisy latents through the remaining layers to obtain visual features, then passes them through a prediction head (linear projection → spatial reshape → small conv net → pooling → linear) to yield a scalar score, trained with a Bradley-Terry loss on preference triplets corrupted at random timesteps. Step-GRPO replaces GRPO's single terminal advantage with dense per-step advantages: at each timestep, k candidate next-states are sampled via SDE, scored by the DRM, and normalized locally to produce a fine-grained supervisory signal. Step-wise Sampling is a training-free inference strategy that branches into k SDE candidates at every step, scores them with the DRM, and greedily continues the highest-scoring path, preventing cascading quality degradation along fixed trajectories.

## Results

On the reward model benchmark (Table 1), DRM (pre-trained DiT, 512×512, 1 epoch) achieves 64.1% on ImageReward, 73.4% on PickScore, 82.2% on HPDv2, and 74.0% on HPDv3, with only 2B parameters. In the downstream RL alignment experiments (Table 3), DRM + Step-GRPO achieves the best scores across all three metrics: 1.17 ImageReward, 17.04 PickScore, and 10.28 HPSv3, outperforming the SD3.5-Medium baseline (1.01/16.76/8.95) and all other reward-model + GRPO combinations. Step-GRPO also converges ~2.5× faster in steps and ~3.5× faster in GPU hours compared to standard GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRM (2B params) decisively outperforms the same-sized VLM-based HPSv3-2B on all four benchmarks (e.g., 73.4% vs. 63.6% on PickScore, 74.0% vs. 66.3% on HPDv3), demonstrating that the diffusion backbone is far more parameter-efficient than scaling a VLM. Against the larger HPSv3-7B, DRM wins on PickScore (73.4% vs. 72.8%) and HPDv3 (74.0% vs. 76.9%—actually loses here), but trails on ImageReward (64.1% vs. 66.8%) and HPDv2 (82.2% vs. 85.4%), a gap the authors attribute to DRM's broader training objective of scoring noisy latents rather than only clean images. Compared to CLIP-based and aesthetic-score baselines (CLIP ViT-H/14, Aesthetic Score Predictor, ImageReward, HPS, HPSv2, MPS), DRM achieves the best PickScore accuracy and is competitive or superior on HPDv3, while trailing MPS and HPSv2 by 1–3 points on ImageReward and HPDv2. In the end-to-end RL alignment (Table 3), DRM + Step-GRPO beats HPSv3 + GRPO by 0.57 on HPSv3 (10.28 vs. 9.71) and 0.10 on ImageReward (1.17 vs. 1.15), and beats PickScore + GRPO by 0.10 on HPSv3 (10.28 vs. 9.64), confirming that the diffusion-based reward signal translates into superior downstream generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of using VLM/CLIP-based backbones for reward modeling in diffusion alignment, arguing that semantic encoders are inherently impoverished in perceptual qualities like aesthetics and composition. It builds directly on Flow-GRPO's RL framework for flow-matching models and the flow-matching generative formulation, but introduces a conceptual repurposing: a pre-trained DiT (from SD3.5-Medium) serves as the reward model backbone, inheriting a generative prior that encodes visual quality. The Step-GRPO algorithm extends GRPO by replacing the single terminal reward with dense per-step rewards computed by the DRM, while Step-wise Sampling introduces a training-free inference-time branching strategy with no direct prior in the diffusion alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The step-wise evaluative capability of the DRM addresses a genuine and underexplored gap in diffusion model alignment: the credit assignment problem in multi-step generation. Step-wise Sampling offers a practical, training-free mechanism to improve generation quality at inference time, which is immediately deployable. However, the paper is limited to a single base model (SD3.5-Medium) and a single resolution (512×512), and the slight performance trade-off on clean-image benchmarks (due to training on noisy latents) raises questions about whether the step-wise capability can be decoupled from the clean-image evaluation objective. Open questions include whether this approach scales to video generation, larger models, or more complex multi-step tasks, and whether the generative prior advantage persists as model scale increases.
