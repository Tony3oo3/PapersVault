## Problem

Multi-source manuscript restoration—where fragments from different artworks are mixed in a single pool—remains largely unaddressed. Existing methods either assume all fragments originate from one image (JigsawNet), require a known number of outlier fragments (LLMCO4MR), or incur quadratic time complexity through exhaustive pairwise enumeration, making them impractical for real-world restoration with heterogeneous, degraded fragments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260428/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pool of B shredded fragment images (RGB) drawn from an unknown number of source artworks, with no guarantee that any two fragments share a source. Each fragment is processed by extracting its contour and forming 7×7 bimodal patches (texture + contour binary map) at contour points, padded to a fixed sequence length L. The output is a set of fully spliced reconstructed images—one per detected source—along with per-pair matching confidence scores and local rigid transformation matrices used for placement.

## Method

ShreddingNet is a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline. In the coarse stage, 14-layer ResGCN encoders (on 8-NN graphs) extract separate texture and contour features, which are fused via bidirectional multi-head cross-attention; cosine similarity over fused vectors yields Top-K candidates per fragment, and label propagation clusters fragments by source—exploiting the key insight that cross-source false matches are rare (<1.1% of errors). In the fine-grained stage, a stacked cross-attention decoder jointly encodes each candidate pair to produce a contour-point matching matrix, which a lightweight three-layer CNN scores to filter final matches; RANSAC then estimates local transformations. Global assembly selects a maximum spanning tree per connected component and composes local transforms to place all fragments in a common coordinate frame, reducing model invocations to O(n) in the number of fragments.

## Results

On the art_2192 dataset (3 source images), ShreddingNet achieves a global assembly precision of 99.15% and recall of 97.59%, with an average reconstruction F1-score of 98.37%—5.72% higher than the prior state-of-the-art. Performance remains above 95% global precision across all tested configurations (3–15 source images) on both art_2192 and pex_2000. Inference time is 207.2 s per batch (3 images), substantially faster than JigsawNet (811.36 s) and PairingNet (350.84 s), thanks to the linear O(n) candidate filtering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShreddingNet outperforms all three baselines on global assembly precision and recall across both datasets. Against PairingNet [48] (the strongest baseline), ShreddingNet gains +4.34 pp GA precision and +7.10 pp GA recall on art_2192 (IC=3), and +3.37 pp GA precision and +8.07 pp GA recall on pex_2000 (IC=3). Against JigsawNet [18], the margins are even larger: +6.36 pp GA precision and +14.71 pp GA recall on art_2192 (IC=3). The rule-based method [43] is far behind, achieving only 7.48% fine-grained precision on art_2192 and producing no valid global assembly. Notably, ShreddingNet also runs ~3.9× faster than JigsawNet and ~1.7× faster than PairingNet per batch, owing to its O(n) coarse-to-fine candidate filtering that avoids exhaustive pairwise enumeration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the fragment-reassembly line from JigsawNet (single-source, O(n²) pairwise CNN matching) and PairingNet (multi-source patch-based matching with high recall but low precision) by introducing a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline that explicitly handles multi-source fragments without assuming a fixed outlier count or requiring text content. The feature extraction builds on ResGCN-based contour encoding from LLMCO4MR and PairingNet, but adds bimodal contour-texture fusion via multi-head cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is exploiting the empirical observation that cross-source false matches are rare in a Top-K coarse stage, enabling label-propagation clustering to separate sources before fine-grained verification—something neither JigsawNet nor PairingNet attempts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-complexity guarantee (O(n) model invocations via Top-K restriction) makes multi-source restoration practically scalable to large fragment pools, and the 5.72% F1 improvement over the strongest baseline (PairingNet) with robustness to stains, mold, and contour defects addresses real-world degradation that prior methods largely ignore. However, validation relies entirely on synthetic datasets with only two manually-generated real-world cases, leaving open whether performance holds on genuinely aged, irregularly torn fragments at scale. A broader open question is whether the coarse-to-fine clustering strategy generalizes to other reassembly domains (archaeological pottery, 3D object reconstruction) where the 'cross-source errors are rare' assumption may not hold.
