## Problem

Sparse-view indoor inverse rendering remains highly ill-posed because existing 3DGS-based methods target object-centric scenes and assume distant illumination sources, failing to capture near-field, high-frequency indoor lighting. Under limited supervision, Gaussian geometry becomes unreliable, cast shadows are erroneously baked into albedo, and material-illumination disentanglement degrades significantly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262161/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 12 sparse-view indoor RGB images (8 for object-level TensoIR, 24 for real-world MipNeRF/DL3DV/FIPT) along with camera poses, and initializes geometry via VGGT's dense point cloud rather than traditional SfM. It outputs a 3D Gaussian scene with per-primitive albedo and roughness, a hybrid illumination representation (scattered Spherical Gaussian point lights plus a 6×512×512×3 learnable cubemap), and supports novel-view PBR rendering at ~15 FPS on a single RTX 4090. The full two-stage training completes in approximately 40 minutes.

## Method

SGS-Intrinsic is a two-stage framework. Stage I constructs a geometry- and semantic-consistent Gaussian field by initializing with VGGT point clouds and supervising with normal priors (StableNormal), pixel-level semantic features (LSEG + CLIP + Qwen3VL for category identification), and a majority-voting semantic consistency loss between training and pseudo views to prevent overfitting. Stage II performs inverse rendering on the fixed geometry using a hybrid illumination model that combines scattered Spherical Gaussian point lights (for high-frequency local lighting) with a differentiable cubemap (for ambient illumination), a lightweight deshadowing module that uses multi-level hash encoding to predict per-point visibility to each light source, and material-consistent regularization enforcing both illumination invariance (relighting consistency via MSE and multi-scale gradient losses under perturbed lighting) and cross-view semantic consistency (matching mean albedo and semantic features across pseudo views within SAM masks).

## Results

On the Interiorverse dataset, SGS-Intrinsic achieves the best scores across all metrics: Albedo PSNR 17.7 (vs. 13.1 for GeoSplat, the strongest baseline), Normal MAE 17.0, Roughness MSE 16.1, and PBR novel-view synthesis PSNR 20.90. On real-world datasets, it leads on DL3DV (PSNR 19.31), FIPT (PSNR 20.10), and MipNeRF (PSNR 19.73), outperforming the next-best method GeoSplat by 0.33–0.65 dB in PSNR. The method also runs at 15.05 FPS, faster than all compared baselines except GSIR (40 FPS) and GIGS (25 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGS-Intrinsic outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both synthetic and real-world datasets. The largest gap is in Albedo PSNR on Interiorverse, where SGS-Intrinsic (17.7) surpasses the next-best SVGIR (16.6) by 1.1 dB and GeoSplat (13.1) by 4.6 dB, indicating substantially cleaner material recovery. On PBR novel-view synthesis, it leads GeoSplat (19.40) by 1.5 dB and R3DG (19.13) by 1.77 dB in PSNR. On real-world data (Table 2), the advantage is more modest but consistent: +0.33 dB over GeoSplat on DL3DV, +0.35 dB on FIPT, and +0.29 dB on MipNeRF. Notably, IRGS achieves the lowest FPS (0.20) due to its ray-tracing pipeline, while SGS-Intrinsic maintains 15.05 FPS, making it practical for interactive use. The method also demonstrates robustness to noisy priors and inaccurate camera poses (Table 3), with only marginal degradation compared to ground-truth camera initialization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting inverse rendering line (GS-IR, R3DG, IRGS, SVGIR, GeoSplat) from object-centric, well-viewed settings to sparse-view indoor scenes, where prior illumination models assuming distant light sources break down. It repurposes VGGT for dense point-cloud initialization in place of traditional SfM, and adapts the GS-ID hybrid illumination model (SGM point lights + cubemap environment) to capture near-field, high-frequency indoor lighting. The key departures are a two-stage training strategy that injects semantic and geometric priors (StableNormal, LSEG, CLIP, SAM2) into the Gaussian field before inverse rendering, and a lightweight hash-encoding-based deshadowing module that explicitly models point-light visibility to prevent shadows from baking into albedo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Sparse-view indoor inverse rendering is a practically important but under-addressed setting for AR/VR relighting and material editing, and this paper provides a complete, well-ablated recipe that consistently outperforms six prior 3DGS-based methods across synthetic and real-world benchmarks. The robustness analysis showing near-identical performance under perturbed geometry, normals, and semantics is encouraging for deployment where pre-trained model outputs are imperfect. Open questions include whether the multi-model pipeline (VGGT + StableNormal + LSEG + SAM2 + RGBX + CLIP) can be simplified, whether the approach scales to very large or outdoor scenes, and whether the 15 FPS rendering speed is sufficient for interactive applications.
