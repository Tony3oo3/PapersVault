## Problem

Reduced-order elastodynamic simulation for mesh-free object representations (e.g., 3D Gaussian Splatting) has been limited to approaches requiring expensive per-shape neural field optimization, as in Simplicits, which also achieves suboptimal accuracy due to the difficulty of variational optimization of elastic energy. Meanwhile, full-order particle methods like MPM and SPH are sensitive to spatial and temporal discretization and can fail under large strains. The paper addresses the gap of obtaining high-quality, material-aware reduced-order bases for arbitrary mesh-free geometries without per-shape optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263163/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts object geometry in any representation supporting volume integration (triangulated meshes, 3D Gaussian splats, or point clouds) along with volumetric material properties (Young's Modulus, Poisson ratio, density), which may be uniform or spatially varying. It first samples RKPM particles from the object volume, then produces a set of m affine transformations with associated RKPM-discretized skinning weight functions as the reduced-order basis. At simulation time, these weights drive a low-DoF implicit time-stepping solver that outputs per-frame deformed configurations, with the number of DoFs controlled by m (typically 6–32).

## Method

The core contribution is discretizing the skinning weight fields with Reproducing Kernel Particle Method (RKPM) kernels, which provides an explicit, differentiable representation that enables direct eigenanalysis rather than iterative optimization. The elastic potential is approximated by its Hessian around the rest configuration, and the optimal skinning eigenmodes are obtained by solving a generalized eigenvalue problem H_w v = λMv, where H_w is the weight-space Hessian and M is the RKPM mass matrix. The paper derives a closed-form expression for H_w under the Neo-Hookean constitutive model, showing that for homogeneous materials it reduces to a scalar multiple of the weak-form Laplace matrix, connecting the approach to classical modal analysis. This eigenanalysis formulation yields a 40× training speedup over Simplicits' gradient-based neural field optimization while achieving consistently lower simulation error (18–45% improvement in MSE) and exact orthogonality of the basis functions, which improves numerical conditioning during simulation.

## Results

On the Thingi10K dataset, the proposed method reduces normalized MSE by 34.2%, 29.8%, and 37.5% over Simplicits across Fix Side, Pull Farthest, and Pull Boundary conditions, respectively, while training in 3.19 s versus 121.44 s (~38× speedup). On the Simready dataset, MSE improvements are 18.9%, 38.1%, and 45.4% with a 3.49 s training time versus 117.45 s (~34× speedup). On the standard beam test with m=32 affine transformations, the method achieves normalized MSE of 2.93e-06 (Bend) and 6.64e-06 (Twist), surpassing both full-order MPM (1.42e-03 / 2.34e-05) and SPH (6.57e-04 / 1.33e-04) baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Simplicits (the closest prior mesh-free reduced-order method), the proposed RKPM eigenmode approach wins on every metric across both datasets and all three boundary conditions, with MSE improvements ranging from 18.9% (Simready Fix Side) to 45.4% (Simready Pull Boundary), and a ~40× reduction in training time (3.2–3.5 s vs. 117–121 s) due to solving a generalized eigenvalue problem instead of iterative neural-field optimization. On the standard beam test, with m=32 affine transformations the method surpasses both full-order particle methods: MPM (1.42e-03 / 2.34e-05) and SPH (6.57e-04 / 1.33e-04) on Bend and Twist respectively, achieving 2.93e-06 and 6.64e-06. At lower DoF counts (m=6 or 9), Simplicits and the proposed method both trail MPM/SPH, but the gap closes rapidly and the proposed method overtakes them by m=16 on Bend and m=32 on Twist. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the Hessian-based loss with grid sampling is the key accuracy driver, while the eigen-decomposition (vs. gradient descent on the same loss) provides the speed advantage without sacrificing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Skinning Eigenmode framework of Benchekroun et al. [3] from mesh-based to mesh-free settings by discretizing skinning weight fields with Reproducing Kernel Particle Method (RKPM) kernels. The closest prior work, Simplicits [31], also targets mesh-free reduced-order elastic simulation but parameterizes skinning weights with a neural field and trains them via gradient-based optimization of an elastic energy loss. The key departure here is replacing that optimization with a closed-form generalized eigenvalue solve on the RKPM mass and Hessian matrices, enabled by a derived simple expression for the Neo-Hookean weight-space Hessian (Proposition 1) that reduces to a Laplace operator for homogeneous materials.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× training speedup and consistent accuracy gains over Simplicits make reduced-order elastic simulation of mesh-free objects (including 3D Gaussian Splats) substantially more practical, with direct applications to robotics and interactive simulation. The elegant connection between the elastic Hessian and the Laplace operator for homogeneous materials provides a clean theoretical anchor linking this work to classical modal analysis. However, the method inherits standard reduced-order limitations: it cannot capture high-frequency details like wrinkles, sharp contacts, or topology changes such as fracture, and its accuracy depends on careful tuning of RKPM kernel radius and particle distribution. Open questions include extending the formulation to handle large nonlinear deformations, heterogeneous materials at scale, and coupling with contact/fracture models.
