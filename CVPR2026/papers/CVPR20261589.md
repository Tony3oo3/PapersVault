## Problem

Diffusion-based planners suffer from modality collapse, where trajectories from different noise inputs converge to nearly identical results, leaving reinforcement fine-tuning without an effective optimization signal. Anchor-based methods partially address this but rely on fixed, scenario-agnostic anchors that produce context-conflicting motions and noisy gradients. The paper tackles how to achieve both multi-modal and scenario-adaptive trajectory sampling to enable stable, sample-efficient closed-loop RL fine-tuning of diffusion planners.

## Input / Output

The planner consumes structured scene representations (surrounding agent states, vector map features, static obstacles) and a navigation command, producing a 150-frame ego-vehicle trajectory at 10 Hz. During RFT, the frozen IL-pretrained model generates a reference trajectory that serves as a prior; the fine-tuned DiT then produces K candidate trajectories under different learned guidance scales, from which one trajectory's first action is executed in the closed-loop simulator. At deployment, the Exploration Policy and reference modules are removed, so the planner retains its original single-pass diffusion inference structure.

## Method

PlannerRFT introduces a dual-branch optimization: (1) an Exploration Policy (MLP-Mixer encoder + cross-attention with scene embedding + Beta-distribution heads) learns to predict lateral and longitudinal guidance scales (η_lat, η_lon) conditioned on driving context, which modulate energy-based classifier guidance offsets injected into the DiT denoising process to produce diverse, scenario-adaptive trajectories; (2) the fine-tuned DiT is optimized via GRPO with a survival reward that accumulates non-terminal per-step rewards to avoid zero-gradient stagnation in hard scenarios. The Exploration Policy is trained with PPO and GAE over closed-loop rollouts, while the DiT is updated with GRPO over groups of K trajectories. A 5-step DDIM sampler introduces stochasticity for exploration, and a GPU-parallel simulator (nuMax, built on Waymax) achieves 10× faster rollouts than native nuPlan to support 40M-step training on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Results

On the nuPlan benchmark, PlannerRFT achieves state-of-the-art closed-loop planning scores on 3 of 4 evaluation settings: 84.46 on Val14-Reactive, 77.16 on Test14-hard-NonReactive, and 72.21 on Test14-hard-Reactive. The largest gain is on the challenging Test14-hard-Reactive setting, where PlannerRFT improves +2.99 points over its IL-pretrained Diffusion Planner baseline (69.22 → 72.21) and +1.79 over the next-best generative method Flow Planner (70.42). On Val14-NonReactive, Flow Planner edges out PlannerRFT by 0.47 points (90.43 vs. 89.96), while PlannerRFT still surpasses all learning-based baselines (PLUTO: 88.89, PlanTF: 84.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct IL-pretrained baseline (Diffusion Planner [49]), PlannerRFT improves on all four settings, with the most substantial gains in reactive traffic: +1.66 on Val14-R and +2.99 on Test14-hard-R, indicating that closed-loop RL rollouts effectively mitigate distribution shift in interactive scenarios. Compared to the closest generative competitor Flow Planner, PlannerRFT wins on three of four benchmarks (Val14-R: +1.15, Test14-hard-NR: +0.69, Test14-hard-R: +1.79) but trails by 0.47 on Val14-NR, where the non-reactive environment limits the benefit of adaptive exploration. Against learning-based baselines, PlannerRFT substantially outperforms PLUTO (+5.35 on Test14-hard-R) and PlanTF (+10.60 on Test14-hard-R), confirming the advantage of diffusion-based trajectory generation over tokenized or auto-regressive approaches. The rule-based PDM-Closed method remains competitive on Val14 (92.84 NR) but degrades sharply on Test14-hard (65.08 NR), whereas PlannerRFT maintains robustness in both regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generation-evaluation reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) paradigm for driving planners by addressing a specific bottleneck in diffusion-based planners: modality collapse during RL sampling. While prior anchor-based methods (DiffusionDrive, GoalFlow) introduce fixed, scenario-agnostic anchors to restore diversity, PlannerRFT replaces these with a learned Exploration Policy that adaptively modulates energy-based classifier guidance scales via PPO, coupled with GRPO-based trajectory optimization. The framework builds on the Diffusion Planner architecture and the DPPO/ReCogDrive formulation of denoising as an MDP, but departs from them by decoupling exploration adaptivity (PPO on guidance scales) from trajectory refinement (GRPO on denoising parameters) in a dual-branch scheme, and by introducing a survival reward to prevent gradient collapse in hard scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The policy-guided denoising mechanism is plug-and-play (removed at deployment), making it a practical drop-in for existing diffusion planners, and the 10× faster nuMax simulator lowers the barrier to closed-loop RL training at scale. However, the performance gains are modest (+1.7 to +3.0 points over the IL-pretrained baseline), and the framework is only validated on structured abstract inputs rather than raw sensory observations, leaving its applicability to end-to-end visuomotor planners unexplored. A key open question is whether the adaptive exploration advantage persists when the planner must process high-dimensional image inputs, where the guidance signal may interact more complexly with perceptual uncertainty.
