## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for MLLMs face three limitations: they operate on narrow concept sets (fewer than 20 vectors), making it difficult to calibrate steering strength without damaging general utility, and SAE-based approaches lack semantic grounding for their learned atoms, requiring costly probing or manual interpretation. DACO addresses all three by building a large, semantically grounded concept dictionary that simultaneously enables sparse coding, improves SAE training, and provides automatic annotation of SAE features.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a frozen MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, LLaVA1.6-Mistral-7B, InternVL3.5-8B-Instruct) that receives a text-image query pair, typically an adversarial jailbreak prompt with a typographic or visual trigger. At inference, the intervention is applied to the residual stream activations at selected decoder layers: the SAE encodes the last-token activation into sparse coefficients, undesirable atom contributions are zeroed out, and desirable atom contributions are amplified by a strength factor γ. The steered activation is then passed back into the autoregressive decoder to produce a safe textual response, adding only ~14.6% per-token overhead.

## Method

DACO builds a concept dictionary of ~15,000 entries from WordNet synsets, retrieves 400K+ caption-image stimuli from CC-3M using a CLIP-based geometric aggregation score (negative log of the product of text-text and text-image cosine similarities), and extracts contrastive concept vectors by differencing mean activations over positive and negative stimuli at each target layer. These vectors are partitioned into desirable and undesirable sets via an expert MLLM (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct). The dictionary serves two roles: (1) it enables Multimodal Oblique Projection (MOP), where an elastic-net sparse solver decomposes the activation and subtracts the undesirable component; and (2) it initializes the SAE decoder columns, improving reconstruction fidelity (FVE, DA) and reducing dead neurons. After SAE training, each decoder atom is automatically annotated by thresholding its cosine distance to the centroids of the desirable and undesirable concept clusters. At inference, the SAE's sparse code is edited: coefficients of undesirable atoms are negated (zeroing their contribution) and coefficients of desirable atoms are set to a fixed strength γ, yielding a compositional steering operation that detoxifies the response while preserving fluency and multimodal reasoning.

## Results

DACO achieves the best safety score across all four safety metrics (MS-R, MS-QG, JBV-R, JBV-QG) for all three target MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA1.6-Mistral-7B, InternVL3.5-8B). For example, on Qwen2.5-VL, DACO reaches 0.990/0.984/0.903/0.841 on the four safety metrics, substantially outperforming the next-best method MOP (0.771/0.840/0.835/0.752). General-purpose utility is well preserved: DACO maintains Fluency of 0.88–0.91, PPL of 2.9–3.1, and MMMU accuracy of 0.33–0.62, closely matching the unsteered base model. The per-token overhead of DACO is only +14.62% compared to no steering, far less than MOP's +49.44%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DACO outperforms all three steering baselines on every safety metric for all three target MLLMs. Against Prompting (safety-prompt prepending), DACO gains 0.28–0.38 in safety scores (e.g., 0.990 vs 0.607 on MS-R for Qwen2.5-VL) while also preserving utility better. Against ActAdd (contrastive activation addition), DACO improves safety by 0.25–0.31 (e.g., 0.903 vs 0.691 on JBV-R for Qwen) and dramatically reduces the utility degradation that ActAdd causes (Fluency 0.905 vs 0.691, MMMU 0.521 vs 0.441 on Qwen). Against MOP (Multimodal Oblique Projection, the strongest baseline), DACO still leads on most metrics by 0.05–0.22 (e.g., 0.903 vs 0.835 on JBV-R for Qwen; 0.803 vs 0.690 on JBV-QG for LLaVA), with the sole near-tie being InternVL MS-QG (0.987 vs 0.983, a gap of only 0.004). On over-refusal (MOSS-Bench), DACO's average rate of 0.11 is close to No Steering (0.09) and well below Prompting (0.22), confirming it does not over-refuse benign queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

DACO sits at the intersection of two active research threads: activation steering for MLLM safety (contrastive activation addition, representation engineering) and sparse autoencoders for interpretable feature extraction. It directly extends the first author's prior PaCE work on oblique projection over curated concept dictionaries by scaling the dictionary from synthetic text to 15K multimodal concepts grounded in real caption-image stimuli from CC-3M. The key conceptual departure is using the curated dictionary not merely as a steering basis but as a semantic initialization for SAE training, which then enables automatic annotation of SAE atoms via proximity to concept centroids—addressing the interpretability gap that plagues prior SAE-based steering methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, inference-time safety mechanism that avoids expensive retraining while achieving near-ceiling safety scores across three architecturally distinct MLLMs, with only ~15% per-token overhead. The released DACO-400K dataset and the dictionary-initialized SAE recipe provide reusable infrastructure for the MLLM safety community. Open questions include whether the static 15K-concept dictionary can adapt to novel attack categories, whether the geometric multimodal retrieval score is optimal, and whether the approach generalizes to video or audio modalities. The modest over-refusal rate (0.11 vs. 0.09 baseline) suggests the method preserves compliance, but long-term robustness against adaptive adversaries remains untested.
