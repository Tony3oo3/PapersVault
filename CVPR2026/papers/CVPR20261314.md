## Problem

The three-view Kruppa autocalibration system yields 45 bilinear equations in only 5 DIAC parameters, making it heavily overconstrained and typically inconsistent under realistic image noise. No prior work had exhaustively characterized which subsets of these constraints produce solvable, well-conditioned minimal systems, leaving constraint selection to ad hoc choices or stochastic sampling. This paper asks: among all possible equation subsets, which yield finite solution sets, and which of those are numerically stable?

## Input / Output

The method consumes three uncalibrated RGB images with point correspondences, from which fundamental matrix triplets and epipoles are estimated via 6-, 7-, or 8-point epipolar solvers; all experiments use normalized device coordinates. The output is the 5-parameter DIAC ω* = KK^T (with ω₃₃ fixed to 1 for scale), from which the full intrinsic matrix K (focal lengths f, g; principal point u, v; skew s) is recovered by Cholesky decomposition. Synthetic experiments use 640×480 images; real-world evaluation uses higher-resolution multi-view sequences from SfM benchmarks.

## Method

The paper introduces a minimal relaxation framework that selects exactly 5 equations from the 45 Kruppa constraints (15 per view-pair, each from 2×2 minors of a 6×2 matrix) to form a zero-dimensional polynomial system. Using Macaulay2 Gröbner basis computation and numerical monodromy, they exhaustively enumerate all C(45,5) ≈ 1.2M subsets, discovering that only the (1,2,2) pattern—one equation from one view-pair, two from each of the other two—yields finite solutions, with degrees of 18, 24, or 32. A field-membership test over the quotient ring Q[F₀,…,F₈]/⟨det F⟩ precisely characterizes the 9 algebraically dependent minor pairs that cause all other patterns to be underconstrained. After exploiting permutation symmetry to reduce the search to ~84K inequivalent relaxations, they score each by its Jacobian condition number κ(Jₓ) on noiseless synthetic data, finding a strong correlation with downstream accuracy. The resulting 'Global-Best' relaxation is solved online via polyhedral homotopy continuation (HomotopyContinuation.jl), tracking 32 paths, and is evaluated within an MSAC loop over 200 random 6-point samples.

## Results

On real-world multi-view datasets (SfM-eval, colmap-public, strecha08), the proposed Global-Best relaxation achieves average intrinsic errors of Δfg=0.565, Δuv=0.963, and Δs=0.312, outperforming the classical Kruppa baseline (Δfg=0.750, Δuv=1.251, Δs=0.4018) across all three metrics. The Kruppa-BnB baseline fails catastrophically with errors on the order of hundreds (e.g., Δfg≈459), because the branch-and-bound strategy frequently produces non-positive-definite DIACs that cannot be decomposed via Cholesky. On synthetic images with pixel noise σ∈[0,1], Global-Best consistently yields lower mean projection error than Kruppa across all 6-, 7-, and 8-point epipolar configurations, with the advantage growing at higher noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the classical Kruppa formulation (Luong & Faugeras [24]) solved via homotopy continuation, and the Kruppa-BnB method (Paudel & Van Gool [28]) using branch-and-bound with local refinement. Global-Best outperforms the classical Kruppa baseline on average across all three intrinsic error metrics (Δfg: 0.565 vs 0.750, Δuv: 0.963 vs 1.251, Δs: 0.312 vs 0.402), with the largest gains on SfM-eval (e.g., Herz Jesus P8: Δfg drops from 1.669 to 0.699) and consistent improvements on colmap-public and strecha08. The only scene-level exceptions are Fountain P11 (slightly worse Δuv) and South Building (slightly worse Δfg and Δuv), where Kruppa edges out Global-Best. Against Kruppa-BnB, the comparison is lopsided: the BnB method produces errors two to three orders of magnitude larger (Δfg≈459 vs 0.565), because the branch-and-bound search frequently yields non-positive-definite DIACs that violate the Cholesky decomposition requirement for recovering K. The paper notes this incompatibility is inherent to applying BnB to the Kruppa system rather than a tuning issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the minimal relaxation framework introduced by Porfiri Dal Cin et al. [6] for general-K autocalibration, but applies it to a simpler, fixed-intrinsics Kruppa formulation where the DIAC has only five parameters. It departs from the classical Kruppa approach [24], which relied on SVD-based fundamental matrix parametrization and heuristically selected five of six equations, by instead treating the fundamental matrix and epipoles algebraically and exhaustively enumerating all C(45,5) ≈ 1.2M possible five-equation subsets. The branch-and-bound autocalibration of Schaffalitzky et al. [28] is positioned as a competing global-optimization strategy, but the paper shows it is incompatible with the Kruppa system due to positive-definiteness violations. Conceptually, the paper's contribution is the first complete symbolic-numerical catalog of which constraint subsets yield zero-dimensional solution sets for this specific formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean structural result—that only the (1,2,2) relaxation pattern among all possible distributions of five equations across three view-pairs yields a finite solution set—provides a principled, non-heuristic basis for selecting constraints in overconstrained multiview geometry problems, replacing the ad-hoc equation dropping common in classical Kruppa solvers. The practical Global-Best solver is simple (tracking 32 homotopy paths) and consistently outperforms both the classical Kruppa baseline and the BnB variant on real datasets, making it a drop-in replacement for practitioners. However, the scope is limited to three views with fixed intrinsics and five DIAC parameters; extending the exhaustive enumeration to more views, unknown K, or other overconstrained systems (e.g., DAQ-based formulations) remains open. The framework's broader applicability to other overconstrained polynomial systems in vision is suggested but not demonstrated.
