## Problem

Standard one-pass VLM classifiers for AI-generated image detection compress the entire scene into a limited set of visual tokens, causing fine-grained forensic cues (tiny text artifacts, stitching seams, periodic textures) to be weakened by downsampling and global attention. This leads to unreliable decisions on high-quality synthetic images and explanations that are generic and weakly tied to specific pixel-level evidence, limiting both accuracy and forensic interpretability.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a two-stage structured output. In Query 1, the model emits a preliminary explanation, a set of bounding boxes (x1,y1,x2,y2) for suspicious regions, and an initial real/generated verdict. In Query 2, the model receives both the original full image and the cropped regions extracted from Query 1's boxes, then outputs a refined, evidence-grounded explanation and a final verdict. The dual-input mechanism in the second stage is the key structural difference from single-pass approaches.

## Method

LTE is a two-stage VLM framework built on Qwen-2.5-VL (7B/32B). Stage 1 (Locate with Global Scan) prompts the VLM to perform a holistic visual analysis and output suspicious region bounding boxes plus an initial verdict. Stage 2 (Examine with Local Evidence Check) feeds the VLM both the full image and the cropped regions simultaneously, enabling comparative magnified analysis that corrects initial misjudgments. Training follows a two-phase paradigm: supervised fine-tuning on the TRACE dataset (20,000 images with region-level annotations and forensic explanations generated by a GPT-4o + Qwen-2.5-VL pipeline with cross-model consistency checks), followed by reinforcement learning via GRPO with stage-specific rewards—format compliance and IoU for Query 1, and classification accuracy plus BLEU-2 for Query 2. The TRACE dataset is constructed by GPT-4o generating forensic explanations and Qwen-2.5-VL extracting spatial bounding boxes, with multi-stage purification (explanation-region consistency checks, IoU thresholds, area filtering) to ensure high-quality region-specific annotations.

## Results

On the TRACE test set, LTE-32B achieves 97.2% detection accuracy with 0.359 IoU for region grounding and 0.211 BLEU-2 for explanation quality, while LTE-7B reaches 94.2% accuracy. Compared to the untrained base VLM (58.7% for 32B), the full LTE pipeline yields over 30 percentage points of accuracy improvement. On out-of-distribution benchmarks, LTE-32B maintains strong performance: 89.3% on MMFR, 85.2% on SynthScars, and 90.3% on FakeClue, consistently outperforming both FakeShield and LEGION across all three external datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTE-32B substantially outperforms both external baselines on the in-domain TRACE benchmark: 97.2% accuracy vs. FakeShield's 80.1% (+17.1 pp) and LEGION's 65.4% (+31.8 pp), with similarly large gaps in grounding IoU (0.359 vs. 0.096 and 0.061) and explanation quality (BLEU-2 0.211 vs. 0.056 and 0.058). On out-of-distribution data, LTE-32B beats FakeShield on all three benchmarks (MMFR 89.3% vs. 71.0%, SynthScars 85.2% vs. 76.5%, FakeClue 90.3% vs. 73.3%) and dominates LEGION on MMFR (89.3% vs. 19.3%) and FakeClue (90.3% vs. 25.4%). The only case where a baseline edges out LTE is LEGION on SynthScars (86.1% vs. 85.2%), but the authors note LEGION was trained on that dataset, so it is not a fair OoD comparison. The two-stage LTE mechanism itself contributes an additional 3.6–5.8% accuracy over single-turn variants (E+G), confirming that the locate-then-examine refinement is the key differentiator over prior one-pass VLM approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of VLM-based image forensics (FakeShield, LEGION, FakeReasoning) and tool-augmented visual reasoning (GRIT, OpenThinkIMG, SatoriR1), departing from prior work by replacing single-pass global classification with an explicit two-stage locate-then-examine loop that treats the VLM as an active investigator rather than a passive feature extractor. The training paradigm borrows the SFT + GRPO recipe from DeepSeek-Math, adapted with forensic-specific rewards (IoU for grounding, BLEU for explanation quality). Unlike LEGION's MLP head or FakeShield's SAM-based segmentation, LTE keeps all reasoning within the VLM's language interface, using bounding boxes as a lightweight grounding mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TRACE dataset (20K images with region-level annotations and forensic explanations) fills a gap in spatially grounded training data for explainable AIGI detection, and the two-stage design offers a practical path to interpretable forensic tools that link verdicts to specific pixel evidence. However, failure cases still concentrate on highly realistic faces and animals where artifacts are imperceptible even under magnification, and the method's dependence on Query 1 region quality means a poor initial localization can cascade into a wrong final verdict. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, adversarially generated content that specifically avoids region-level artifacts, and whether the BLEU-based explanation reward remains effective as reference explanations become more diverse.
