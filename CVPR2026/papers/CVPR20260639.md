## Problem

Fusion-based open-vocabulary detectors (e.g., Grounding-DINO) achieve high accuracy but suffer from slow inference because cross-modal fusion layers make visual features query-specific, requiring separate forward passes per text chunk. Non-fusion dual-tower methods offer fast inference but were previously underperforming. This paper demonstrates that with well-curated multi-granularity training data and staged training, a retrieval-style non-fusion architecture can surpass fusion models while running at real-time speeds, and that this retrieval paradigm naturally extends to proposal generation, object retrieval, and referring expression comprehension.

## Input / Output

For detection, the model takes an RGB image (640×640 for Tiny/Base, 1280×1280 for Large) and a text prompt of class names, producing bounding boxes with confidence scores via dot-product matching between image grid features and text embeddings. For WeDetect-Uni, no text prompt is needed; it outputs class-specific box embeddings for top-scoring proposals that can be cached for fast retrieval. For WeDetect-Ref, the input is an image plus a natural-language referring expression plus a list of pre-extracted proposals; the output is a binary relevance score per proposal (not a generated bounding box), with the highest-scoring proposal identified as the target.

## Method

WeDetect uses a dual-tower architecture with an XLM-RoBERTa text encoder and a ConvNeXt-based vision encoder (CSPRepBiFPAN neck + YOLO-World contrastive head), performing classification purely via dot products without any fusion layers. Three key design choices enable strong performance: (1) initialization from a pretrained CLIP model for inherited open-vocabulary capability, (2) a ConvNeXt backbone that naturally provides multi-scale features, and (3) a 15M-image / 330M-box dataset with multi-granularity instance-specific labels generated by a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL annotator, combined with a staged training recipe (CLIP pretrain → frozen-backbone neck/head training → full end-to-end). WeDetect-Uni freezes the entire detector and trains only a single learnable objectness prompt, preserving class-specific box embeddings for retrieval. WeDetect-Ref repurposes Qwen3-VL as a parallel classifier: RoI features per proposal are compressed to a single token via a linear projector, concatenated with image and query tokens, and a binary classification head scores all proposals in one forward pass, eliminating next-token prediction.

## Results

WeDetect-Tiny achieves 37.4 AP on LVIS minival and 31.4 AP on LVIS at 62.5 fps, surpassing YOLO-World-L (35.4/26.8 AP at 54.6 fps) by 2.0 and 4.6 AP respectively. Scaling to WeDetect-Large yields 49.4 AP on LVIS and 55.0 AP on LVIS minival, outperforming the previous SOTA T-Rex2 by 3.6 AP on LVIS. For referring expression comprehension, WeDetect-Ref 4B attains 93.2 average Top-1 accuracy on RefCOCO+/g with a 13× speedup over Qwen3-VL 4B. On the new object retrieval task, WeDetect-Large-Uni achieves 83.6 F1 on COCO, exceeding the best image-level baseline (FG-CLIP2, 57.7 F1) by 25.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeDetect-Tiny outperforms YOLO-World-L by 2.0 AP on LVIS minival, 4.6 AP on LVIS, 6.1 AP on COCO-O, 8.0 AP on ODinW13, and 4.0 AP on ODinW35, while running 7.9 fps faster (62.5 vs. 54.6 fps). At the large scale, WeDetect-Large surpasses T-Rex2 (previous SOTA) by 3.6 AP on LVIS (49.4 vs. 45.8) and 0.1 AP on LVIS minival (55.0 vs. 54.9), while matching or exceeding LLMDet on COCO-OP (47.0 vs. 53.2 is a loss, but WeDetect-Large wins on LVIS by 7.4 AP). WeDetect-Base and WeDetect-Large exceed Grounding-DINO by 6× and 3× in inference speed respectively while achieving higher AP. For REC, WeDetect-Ref 4B (93.2 avg on RefCOCO+/g) beats Qwen3-VL 4B (86.6) by 6.6 points and InternVL3.5-8B (89.7) by 3.5 points, with a 13× speedup over Qwen3-VL 4B (5.3 vs. 0.4 fps). On object retrieval, WeDetect-Large-Uni (83.6 F1 on COCO) outperforms FG-CLIP2 (57.7 F1), HQ-CLIP (52.2 F1), and OpenAI CLIP (46.4 F1) by 25.9, 31.4, and 37.2 F1 points respectively, demonstrating the advantage of region-level over image-level embeddings for fine-grained local retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant fusion-based paradigm in open-vocabulary detection (Grounding-DINO, GLIP, DetCLIP series) by fully committing to a CLIP-style dual-tower architecture with no cross-modal fusion layers, arguing that recognition is fundamentally a retrieval problem. It builds on YOLO-World's contrastive head and loss design, replaces the ViT backbone with ConvNeXt for native multi-scale features, and inherits open-vocabulary priors from a pretrained CLIP model. For the REC component (WeDetect-Ref), it extends Qwen3-VL by repurposing the LLM as a parallel binary classifier over pre-extracted proposals rather than a sequential next-token generator, drawing inspiration from VideoITG's retrieval formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that with sufficiently curated multi-granularity data (15M images, 330M boxes) and staged training, a simple non-fusion architecture can match or exceed deep fusion models while running 6–13× faster, challenging the field's assumption that cross-modal fusion is necessary for fine-grained vision-language alignment. The unified retrieval framework connecting detection, proposal generation, local object retrieval, and REC offers a coherent design philosophy that could simplify future system architectures. Open questions include whether the heavy data-engineering pipeline (MLLM-based annotation, multi-granularity sampling) is reproducible at smaller scale, whether the frozen-detector approach in WeDetect-Uni limits adaptability to truly novel domains, and whether the single-token-per-object representation in WeDetect-Ref is sufficient for very fine-grained or occluded objects.
