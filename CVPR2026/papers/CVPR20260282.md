## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods improve robustness to domain shifts through algorithmic optimization but lack theoretical understanding of why pretrained models degrade on out-of-distribution data. This paper identifies that the root cause is sample-wise misalignment: under domain shifts, a test sample's feature embedding drifts away from its correct classifier weight and toward an incorrect one, a phenomenon that worsens with increasing corruption severity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260282/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained model (ResNet-50 or ViT-B/16) and processes unlabeled test images in mini-batches during inference, with no access to ground-truth labels. At each adaptation step, it computes the normalized Euclidean distance (FCA distance) between each sample's feature embedding and every classifier weight vector, then updates model parameters online. The output is both a class prediction and the adapted model weights, with the adaptation operating under constraints such as batch size 1 or continual domain shifts across multiple corruption types.

## Method

The paper first extends Neural Collapse to the sample-wise level, proving that during the terminal phase of training each sample's feature embedding converges to its corresponding classifier weight (NC3+). Building on this, NCTTA constructs hybrid alignment targets by blending geometric proximity (softmax over negative Z-score-normalized FCA distances) with predictive confidence (softmax logits), weighted by a tunable coefficient α. An InfoNCE-style contrastive loss then pulls the feature embedding toward the top-k hybrid target classifier weights while pushing it away from the remaining negatives. This alignment loss is combined with entropy minimization, entropy-based sample filtering, and a dynamic per-sample weight that down-weights high-entropy or geometrically uncertain predictions, yielding the final adaptation objective.

## Results

NCTTA achieves 78.30% average accuracy on CIFAR-10-C and 66.61% on ImageNet-C under severity level 5 corruption, outperforming the strongest baseline (DeYO) by a substantial margin. On Waterbirds, NCTTA reaches 93.13% average accuracy and 76.56% worst-group accuracy, surpassing the next-best method (DeYO) by 0.23% and 0.91% respectively. In the challenging Continual TTA setting, NCTTA attains 71.27% average accuracy across severity levels 1–5 on ImageNet-C, and under the extreme batch-size-1 constraint it achieves 59.69%, exceeding Tent by over 10 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NCTTA outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On ImageNet-C (severity 5), NCTTA (66.61%) surpasses Tent (~52.09%) by 14.52%, DeYO (~57.88%) by ~8.7%, and SAR (~53.53%) by ~13%, with the largest gaps appearing under severe corruptions like Snow and Frost where Tent even degrades below no_adapt. On CIFAR-10-C, NCTTA (78.30%) leads Tent (75.19%) by ~3.1% and DeYO by a similar margin. On Waterbirds, NCTTA (93.13% avg / 76.56% worst-group) edges out DeYO (92.90% / 75.65%) and MEMO (92.47% / 64.69%), with the worst-group gap over MEMO reaching 11.9 points. On PACS, NCTTA (77.36% avg) beats DeYO (76.67%) and Tent (76.43%). In the CTTA and batch-size-1 stress tests, NCTTA maintains its lead over DeYO (71.27% vs 69.76% on CTTA; 59.69% vs 55.79% on batch-1), confirming robustness under dynamic and resource-constrained conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Neural Collapse (NC) framework of Papyan et al. (2020) from its original class-wise formulation (requiring labeled class means) to a sample-wise property (NC3+), where individual feature embeddings align with their corresponding classifier weights during the terminal phase of training. It departs from the four established TTA strategy families (prototype-based, consistency regularization, normalization-based, entropy minimization) by grounding adaptation in NC geometry rather than heuristic objectives, and addresses the label-free constraint of TTA by replacing the infeasible ground-truth FCA distance with hybrid targets that blend geometric proximity and predictive confidence. The method is conceptually closest to entropy minimization (Tent, EATA, DeYO) but adds an explicit feature-classifier alignment loss that prior TTA methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NC3+ property provides the first principled geometric explanation for why pretrained models degrade under domain shifts—feature embeddings drift away from correct classifier weights toward incorrect ones—offering a diagnostic lens that prior TTA work lacked. The practical impact is demonstrated by consistent gains across five benchmarks including challenging CTTA and batch-size-1 settings, though the method still depends on pseudo-labels (mitigated but not eliminated by hybrid targets). Open questions highlighted by the authors include extending NC-based analysis to vision-language models and exploring whether NC properties can inform class-prototype computation in mini-batch TTA, both of which could broaden the applicability of this geometric perspective.
