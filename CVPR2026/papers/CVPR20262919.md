## Problem

Offline MARL is limited by its sensitivity to handcrafted reward functions and poor generalization to new goals, yet no existing benchmark supports goal-conditioned offline evaluation. Prior MARL environments rely on dense, task-specific rewards and single-goal evaluation, making them unsuitable for assessing whether policies can generalize across multiple goals under sparse, automatically generated reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262919/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes a local observation (proprioceptive state or a 64×64 RGB camera image) together with a local goal that is factorized from a global goal via a structured decomposition of the robot body (e.g., splitting 8 ant joints across 2 or 4 agents, or sampling per-arm visual observations for bimanual tasks). The output is a per-agent action vector (joint torques or end-effector commands) that is combined into the joint action to drive the environment. Rewards are binary (0 on goal attainment, −1 otherwise) and are either localized per agent (multi-entity tasks) or a single global scalar broadcast to all agents (joint-control tasks).

## Method

The paper introduces the first goal-conditioned offline MARL framework by extending OGCRL algorithms to multi-agent settings under both fully decentralized and CTDE paradigms. The key mechanism is a structured factorization of the global goal into per-agent local goals, enabling each agent to condition its policy π_i(a_i | o_i, g_i) on local information alone; in CTDE, a centralized critic Q_i(o, a, g) additionally leverages joint observations and actions during training while execution remains decentralized. Six baselines are instantiated—GCMBC, ICRL, IHIQL, HIQL-CTDE, GCOMIGA, and GCOMAR—by applying goal relabeling and random goal sampling to existing offline RL and offline MARL algorithms, so that rewards and goals are self-generated from raw rollouts without manual design. The accompanying MangoBench benchmark provides 3 environments (AntMaze locomotion, Ant-Soccer, and RoboFactory manipulation), 4 agent configurations, and 47 tasks with standardized offline datasets and multi-goal evaluation protocols to systematically assess generalization.

## Results

On locomotion tasks (AntMaze-navigate), IHIQL achieves the highest success rates across all maze scales: 95.1% (medium 2×4), 92.2% (large 2×4d), 57.3% (giant 2×4), and 48.7% (teleport 4×2), substantially outperforming HIQL-CTDE (74.0%, 51.2%, 1.4%, 22.4% respectively). On manipulation tasks, IHIQL outperforms Diffusion Policy by 41.4% on lift-barrier while requiring only 5% of its training time, and ICRL exceeds DP by 75% on place-food while training 93% faster. No single method dominates all 47 tasks, and GCOMIGA/GCOMAR fail in most settings due to inability to handle sparse goal-conditioned rewards.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion Policy (DP) on manipulation tasks, the goal-conditioned MARL baselines clearly win: IHIQL achieves 41.4% higher success rate than DP on lift-barrier while using only 5% of DP's training time, and ICRL exceeds DP by 75% on place-food while training 93% faster. IHIQL and GCMBC also outperform DP on place-food, confirming that goal-conditioned offline MARL can match or surpass strong imitation learning with dramatically lower computational cost. Against OMIGA (the non-goal-conditioned offline MARL baseline), the comparison in Section 5.4 is diagnostic rather than competitive: GCOMIGA (goal-conditioned OMIGA) fails under sparse goal-conditioned rewards, while OMIGA with shaped dense rewards performs significantly better, revealing that the failure stems from OMIGA's inability to handle sparse rewards rather than from goal conditioning itself. The paper also notes that multi-agent IHIQL consistently outperforms single-agent HIQL on teleport mazes, validating the benefit of decentralized joint-control factorization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-agent Offline Goal-Conditioned RL (OGCRL) to multi-agent settings by adapting five existing algorithms—HIQL, CRL, GCBC, OMIGA, and OMAR—into goal-conditioned multi-agent variants under both fully decentralized and CTDE paradigms. The key conceptual departure from prior MARL benchmarks (VMAS, SMACv2, MPE, etc.) is the shift from dense, handcrafted, task-specific rewards to sparse, self-generated goal-conditioned rewards via goal relabeling, eliminating manual reward engineering. The benchmark itself is positioned as the first to support multi-goal evaluation in a cooperative multi-agent offline setting, addressing a gap where existing environments evaluate only on a single fixed goal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MangoBench provides the first systematic evaluation suite for goal-conditioned offline MARL, and the finding that no single method dominates across all 47 tasks highlights genuine open challenges in this emerging area. Two practically important open questions remain: (1) existing offline MARL methods (OMIGA, OMAR) fail completely under sparse rewards, and no algorithm yet addresses this; (2) the CTDE paradigm, which should theoretically improve coordination, actually underperforms fully decentralized methods due to architectural instability, suggesting the coordination advantage of CTDE is unrealized in current goal-conditioned formulations. For robotics, the demonstration that goal-conditioned offline RL baselines can match or exceed Diffusion Policy on bimanual manipulation tasks while training 95% faster points toward a more scalable path for multi-agent robotic learning.
