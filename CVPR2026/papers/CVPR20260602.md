## Problem

MLLMs suffer from insufficient visual perception, often producing incorrect answers or hallucinated explanations because they lack explicit mechanisms to enhance salient visual regions. Existing post-hoc methods for focusing on important regions typically require multiple forward passes and can only handle one salient region at a time, limiting both flexibility and efficiency. The paper asks how to dynamically enhance visual perception within a single forward pass by adaptively shifting focus across multiple salient regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a vision encoder into a grid of visual tokens) and a text prompt, producing a generated text response via the LLM backbone. Unusually, the visual token sequence is dynamically modified mid-inference at selected transformer layers (default 12–18): tokens are expanded (increasing sequence length) or pruned (reverting to original length) based on per-layer saliency ratios, so the effective sequence length varies across layers within a single forward pass.

## Method

Blink comprises two synergistic modules operating within a single forward pass. Saliency-guided scanning computes per-token saliency scores from the attention map between the last text token and all visual tokens, reshapes them into a 2D spatial grid, aggregates scores over p×p patches, and derives a saliency ratio ρ that quantifies attention sharpness at each layer. Dynamic token resolution uses ρ against two thresholds: when ρ exceeds τ_exp, a trainable TokenSR module (three 2D convolutional layers with ReLU) super-resolves the salient patch tokens and inserts them between visual and text tokens; when ρ falls below τ_drop, previously expanded tokens are removed. The TokenSR modules are trained with the MLLM backbone frozen, minimizing KL divergence between enhanced tokens and reference tokens obtained from the corresponding cropped image region. The key empirical insight driving the design is that MLLMs naturally shift attention focus across layers and that replicating salient tokens at intermediate layers (12–26) measurably improves perception, which Blink exploits by dynamically allocating extra computation where and when the model is most confident.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, Blink achieves a total MME score of 1881.53 (Perception 1519.74, Cognition 361.79), improving over the vanilla baseline (1863.58) by +17.95. The fully trained Blink also leads on GQA (61.98 vs. 61.93), MMBench-CN (58.59 vs. 58.08), POPE (85.23 vs. 85.17), ScienceQA (69.66 vs. 69.46), and MM-Vet (33.40 vs. 32.20). The training-free variant Blink-interp, which replaces TokenSR with bilinear interpolation, still gains +8.36 on MME Perception and matches or exceeds the baseline on all other benchmarks except MM-Vet (31.70 vs. 32.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole baseline is the vanilla LLaVA-1.5-7B model; no external competing methods are included in the main comparison table. Blink (full) outperforms the vanilla baseline on every benchmark: MME Perception by +14.02, MME Cognition by +3.93, GQA by +0.05, MMBench-CN by +0.51, POPE by +0.06, ScienceQA by +0.20, and MM-Vet by +1.20. The training-free variant Blink-interp also surpasses the baseline on MME Perception (+8.36), MMBench (+0.09), MMBench-CN (+0.43), and ScienceQA (+0.05), but slightly underperforms on MME Cognition (−4.65) and MM-Vet (−0.50). The gap between Blink and Blink-interp is most pronounced on MME Cognition (+8.58) and MM-Vet (+1.70), confirming that the trained TokenSR module contributes meaningfully beyond simple interpolation. Ablations further show that removing the dynamic token resolution module (DTR) causes the largest single drop (−41.07 total MME for Blink), while removing saliency-guided scanning (SGS) costs −2.15, indicating that the adaptive expand/drop logic is the dominant contributor.

## Relation to Prior Work

Blink builds on the MLLM architecture (specifically LLaVA-1.5) and the growing line of visual perception enhancement methods such as V*, DyFo, and ViCrop, which identify salient regions via attention maps or post-hoc selection modules. However, those prior approaches typically require two or more forward passes or rely on fixed-layer attention, limiting flexibility when multiple salient regions coexist. Blink departs by performing saliency-guided scanning and dynamic token expansion/dropping entirely within a single forward pass, drawing conceptual inspiration from human saccadic attention and borrowing the token super-resolution module design from classical image super-resolution CNNs (SRCNN, VDSR). The empirical observation that attention naturally shifts across transformer layers and that replicating salient tokens improves perception provides the motivational grounding absent in prior post-hoc methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Blink offers a plug-and-play mechanism to improve MLLM visual perception without retraining the backbone, which is practically attractive given the cost of full fine-tuning. However, the evaluation is limited to LLaVA-1.5-7B and a single salient region per layer (one p×p patch), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger models, video inputs, or scenes requiring simultaneous multi-region focus. The modest absolute gains (e.g., +0.05 on GQA, +0.09 on MMBench) suggest the method addresses a narrow slice of the visual perception gap, and the reliance on hand-tuned layer ranges (12–18) and thresholds limits its out-of-the-box applicability. Future work could explore adaptive layer selection, multi-patch expansion, and integration with other MLLM backbones.
