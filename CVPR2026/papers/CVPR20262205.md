## Problem

VGGT, a 1.2B-parameter feed-forward 3D reconstruction transformer, is too memory-intensive for deployment on edge devices such as UAVs and mobile AR systems. Standard post-training quantization methods (SmoothQuant, GPTQ, AWQ) degrade VGGT's accuracy severely under W4A16 because the model exhibits heterogeneous per-block quantization sensitivity and outlier camera/register tokens that distort activation-based scale estimation. This paper addresses the gap by designing a geometry-aware PTQ framework tailored to 3D transformers rather than applying generic numerical stabilization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N multi-view RGB images (518×518 resolution) as input, which are patched via DINO into token sequences with one camera token and four register tokens appended per frame. It produces per-frame camera parameters (rotation quaternion, translation vector, field-of-view), a dense depth map, and a 3D point map in world coordinates. The quantization is weight-only W4A16 with per-group granularity, requiring only a small calibration set (as few as 16 images) and no retraining.

## Method

QVGGT is a three-stage post-training quantization framework. First, a per-block sensitivity analysis reveals that the 14th, 17th, and 23rd frame blocks and the 23rd global block are highly fragile, so a selective mixed-precision scheme keeps their attention projection layers at FP16 while quantizing all other layers to INT4. Second, camera and register tokens—identified as high-magnitude activation outliers that dominate per-channel scale estimation—are excluded from calibration statistics, and a PCA-derived Camera Information Compensation Token (CICT) is synthesized from the calibration set and injected into the camera head at inference to restore lost geometric cues. Third, a task-aware scale search replaces the standard layer-reconstruction objective with a composite loss combining multi-head prediction losses (camera Huber loss, uncertainty-weighted depth and point-map losses) and a geometric consistency loss that penalizes disagreement between depth-unprojected points and the direct point-map head output, thereby aligning quantization scale selection with downstream 3D reconstruction quality.

## Results

Under W4A16 quantization, QVGGT achieves near-lossless performance: 89.4 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2 (vs. 89.5 FP16 baseline) and 85.0 AUC@30 on RealEstate10K (vs. 85.3 FP16). On 3D reconstruction, QVGGT matches the FP16 baseline on 7-Scenes (Acc 0.031 vs. 0.030; NC 0.849 vs. 0.847) and NRGBD (Acc 0.029 vs. 0.024; NC 0.925 vs. 0.922). The method delivers 3–4.9× peak memory reduction and up to 2.8× real-hardware speedup over FP32, with 2.11× memory and 1.93× latency improvement over FP16 at 2 input frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QVGGT (W4A16) outperforms all four baselines on both camera pose benchmarks. Against the closest competitor QuantVGGT (W4A16), QVGGT gains +0.2 AUC@30 on CO3Dv2 (89.4 vs. 89.2) and +0.6 on RealEstate10K (85.0 vs. 84.4), while also reporting 0.23 s latency versus QuantVGGT's unreported (pseudo-quantization) timing. The gap widens dramatically against generic PTQ methods: GPTQ (W4A16) drops to 76.9/75.6 AUC@30, and AWQ (W4A16) collapses to 54.6/59.2, indicating that standard activation-aware or second-order compensation strategies fail to preserve VGGT's geometric heads. SmoothQuant (W8A8) also lags behind QVGGT (87.9 vs. 89.4 on CO3Dv2) despite using 2× more weight bits, and incurs higher latency (0.62 s vs. 0.23 s). On 3D reconstruction (7-Scenes, NRGBD), QVGGT matches or exceeds the FP16 baseline on all three metrics (Acc, Comp, NC), while GPTQ and AWQ degrade by 40–70% in accuracy, confirming that task-aware scale search and camera information compensation are critical for 3D geometry transformers.

## Relation to Prior Work

QVGGT builds directly on VGGT as its base architecture and borrows the lightweight grid-search paradigm from AWQ for scale selection, but departs from both general-purpose PTQ methods (SmoothQuant, GPTQ, AWQ) and the concurrent QuantVGGT by introducing geometry-aware design choices. The key conceptual departure is treating camera and register tokens not as mere activation outliers to be smoothed or dispersed (as standard PTQ does), but as carriers of essential geometric information that must be explicitly preserved via a PCA-derived compensation token. The task-aware scale search further distinguishes QVGGT by replacing layer-wise reconstruction objectives with multi-head geometric consistency, aligning quantization with the intrinsic 3D structure that couples camera, depth, and point-map predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving near-lossless W4A16 quantization of a 1.2B-parameter 3D reconstruction model, QVGGT makes feed-forward 3D perception practically deployable on resource-constrained platforms (UAVs, mobile AR), where the 3–4.9× memory reduction and up to 2.8× speedup are the difference between feasibility and infeasibility. Open questions include whether the camera-token compensation and task-aware scale search generalize to other 3D transformer architectures beyond VGGT, whether the approach extends to activation quantization (W4A8 or W4A4) for further compression, and whether the PCA-based global prior remains valid under significant domain shift. The paper is limited to a single base model and weight-only quantization, so the broader landscape of 3D transformer compression remains largely unexplored.
