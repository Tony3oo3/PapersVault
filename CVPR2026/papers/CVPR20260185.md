## Problem

Existing RECS pipelines for remote sensing rely on separate task-specific decoding heads (or implicit multi-head concatenations) that fragment geometric and semantic representations, causing misaligned optimization directions and poor performance on extreme-scale or non-convex targets. No prior method provides a single, constrainable geometric intermediate that simultaneously serves both visual grounding and referring segmentation while preserving scale identity across multi-scale features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260185/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a free-form referring expression as input. In the coarse stage, the full image is encoded; in the fine stage, the target region is cropped from the coarse prediction, resized back to the original resolution, and re-encoded with a template-enhanced caption. The unified output is a single autoregressively decoded polygon vertex sequence from which both the bounding box (via Rect) and the segmentation mask (via Fill) are derived, eliminating the need for separate box and mask heads.

## Method

RECS4R introduces four complementary modules operating at representation, refinement, reaggregation, and regularization levels. At the representation level, the Language-guided Unified Contour Decoding Paradigm (LUCDP) regresses a single language-conditioned polygon vertex stream that simultaneously yields the bounding box and mask, structurally enforcing geometric-semantic consistency. At the refinement level, Residual Coarse-to-Fine Encoding (RCE) injects coarse-stage global vision-language features into fine-stage encoding via channel modulation, spatial attention gating, and cross-attention, so the fine stage performs error correction rather than re-learning from scratch. At the reaggregation level, Channel Isolated Multi-scale Fusion (CIMF) projects each of four feature scales into dedicated channel subspaces and concatenates them, preserving scale identity while using learnable weights and cross-modal attention for language-guided scale selection. At the regularization level, a Gradient Consistency Loss (GCL) computes Sobel-based gradient magnitudes of predicted and ground-truth masks and penalizes their L1 difference, sharpening contours for non-convex and slender structures. The full architecture is built on PolyFormer's sequence-to-sequence decoder with Swin-Transformer, ConvNeXt, or VMamba as the image backbone and BERT for text encoding.

## Results

On the RefDIOR test set with Swin-Tiny backbone, RECS4R achieves 94.69 oIoU and 82.68 mIoU on VG, 90.01 oIoU and 74.45 mIoU on RIS, yielding a combined RECS Sum of 341.83 — surpassing the strongest baseline CCFormer (308.33) by 33.5 points. On natural-domain RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, RECS4R with Swin-Tiny reaches 94.24/94.51/92.85 oIoU on VG validation and 79.14/79.27/77.93 oIoU on RIS validation, exceeding PolyFormer-Large by up to 11.35% (VG) and 16.8% (RIS test-B). The method also demonstrates backbone generality, maintaining top performance with ConvNeXt-Tiny (Sum 346.36) and VMamba-Tiny (Sum 339.64) at only 45.37G FLOPs, well below CCFormer's 119.39G and MRLN's 310.66G.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECS4R decisively outperforms the strongest prior method CCFormer [55] on RefDIOR by 12.3% (VG oIoU), 8.59% (VG mIoU), 9.12% (RIS oIoU), and 3.49% (RIS mIoU), while using only 45.37G FLOPs versus CCFormer's 119.39G. Against the architectural baseline PolyFormer [45], the improvement is dramatic on VG mIoU (40.10 → 82.68, +66.45% relative), confirming that the unified contour decoding paradigm resolves the geometry-semantics optimization conflict. Older multi-head baselines (MCN, RefTR, SeqTR, MRLN) are all surpassed by 30+ points in RECS Sum, with MRLN [24] being the most computationally expensive (310.66G FLOPs) yet still trailing RECS4R by 137 points. On natural-domain RefCOCO/RefCOCO+/RefCOCOg, RECS4R with a Tiny backbone beats PolyFormer-Large [45] by 3.86–11.35% on VG and 5.21–16.8% on RIS, and outperforms all prior RIS methods (ReSTR, LTS, CGAN, etc.) by 4–19 points. The consistent wins across CNN (ConvNeXt), Transformer (Swin), and Mamba (VMamba) backbones demonstrate that the gains are architectural rather than backbone-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PolyFormer's sequential polygon generation for referring image segmentation, but departs from it by arguing that even a unified Transformer head (as in PolyFormer) remains an implicit multi-head when it concatenates subtask outputs. The key conceptual departure is the Language-guided Unified Contour Decoding Paradigm (LUCDP), which treats a single polygon vertex stream as the sole geometric carrier from which both the bounding box (via Rect) and the mask (via Fill) are derived, structurally eliminating the optimization conflict between geometric and semantic objectives that plagues multi-head RECS methods like MCN, RefTR, and CCFormer. The remaining three components—residual coarse-to-fine encoding, channel-isolated multi-scale fusion, and gradient consistency loss—are targeted refinements that address known weaknesses in coarse-to-fine pipelines and FPN-style feature aggregation, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LUCDP insight—that a single contour vertex sequence can serve as a truly unified decoding target for both detection and segmentation, rather than merely simplifying the head architecture—offers a reusable design principle for any multi-task referring interpretation pipeline. The validation across CNN, Transformer, and Mamba backbones and across remote-sensing and natural-image domains strengthens the claim that the gains are architectural rather than backbone-specific. Open questions include whether the coarse-to-fine two-stage design (which roughly doubles inference cost) can be collapsed into a single pass, whether the predefined fine-stage prompt template generalizes to truly open-vocabulary settings, and whether the contour-based paradigm extends naturally to 3D or video tasks as the authors note in their conclusion.
