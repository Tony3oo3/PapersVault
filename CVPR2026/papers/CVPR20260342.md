## Problem

High-resolution (4K+) video matting requires simultaneously achieving spatial fidelity, temporal consistency, and computational feasibility, yet existing methods either downsample (losing fine detail in semi-transparent regions) or process frames independently (sacrificing temporal coherence). Additionally, available training datasets suffer from imprecise alpha annotations and physically inconsistent foreground-background compositing, limiting supervised learning quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260342/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 4-frame video clip at native 4K resolution (3840×2160, 9:16 aspect ratio) and produces a per-pixel alpha matte video at the same resolution. The coarse stage operates on a 512×512 downsampled version, while the refinement stage processes only the sparse key-region pixels (~13.7% of total) at full 4K resolution, making the pipeline resolution-agnostic. The final matte is a dense T×H×W×1 tensor where non-critical regions retain the coarse prediction and key regions carry refined values.

## Method

μ Matting is a two-stage framework: (1) a Coarse Alpha Predictor (CAP) uses Sapiens-0.3B, a masked autoencoder pretrained on 300M+ human-centric images, to produce a structurally coherent coarse matte at 512×512, with key regions identified via morphological erosion/dilation on non-binary alpha areas; (2) a Fractional Alpha Refiner (FAR) applies 3D sparse convolutions (encoder-decoder) at full 4K resolution only to those key-region pixels, augmented by a Temporal Sparse Context Modulator (TSCM) that injects global spatio-temporal context by projecting the [CLS] token through a GRU and element-wise modulating sparse features. The final matte is fused as A = K×A_d + (1−K)×A_c, and training combines L1 + Laplacian pyramid losses for spatial accuracy with a temporal consistency loss enforcing smooth alpha transitions between adjacent frames in overlapping key regions.

## Results

On the real-world CRGNN benchmark, μMatting achieves MAD 4.50, MSE 1.57, Grad 13.57, and dtSSD 4.74, outperforming the best prior method RVM (MAD 6.18, MSE 2.87) by ~27% in MAD and ~45% in MSE. On the standard VM 1920×1080 benchmark, μMatting records MAD 4.21 and dtSSD 1.69, the best values across all methods. On the 4K VM-4K test set, μMatting achieves MAD 2.71, MSE 0.74, Grad 7.07, and dtSSD 1.11, roughly halving the error of RVM (MAD 5.85) and SparseMat (MAD 6.82).

## Baselines & Comparisons

μMatting outperforms all five baselines on the real-world CRGNN benchmark across every metric, with the largest margin against VMFormer (MAD 4.50 vs. 144.99), which suffers severe subject-missing artifacts due to domain insensitivity. Against the strongest frame-based competitor RVM, μMatting reduces MAD from 6.18 to 4.50 (−27%) and MSE from 2.87 to 1.57 (−45%) on CRGNN, and from 6.57 to 4.21 (−36%) on VM 1920×1080. On the standard VM 1920×1080 set, VMFormer edges out μMatting on MSE (1.52 vs. 1.62) and Grad (6.27 vs. 8.49), but μMatting leads on MAD (4.21 vs. 6.21) and dtSSD (1.69 vs. 1.78), indicating better overall pixel accuracy and temporal stability. SparseMat, the closest sparse-inference competitor, is surpassed by μMatting on all four metrics on both benchmarks (e.g., MAD 4.50 vs. 6.23 on CRGNN; MAD 4.21 vs. 7.97 on VM 1920×1080). On the 4K VM-4K test set, μMatting nearly halves the MAD of RVM (2.71 vs. 5.85) and SparseMat (2.71 vs. 6.82), while also achieving the lowest Grad (7.07 vs. 23.26 and 16.28), confirming superior boundary fidelity at ultra-high resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SparseMat's sparse refinement paradigm but departs from it in two key ways: replacing the frame-difference mechanism with 3D sparse convolutions for genuine temporal modeling, and substituting the low-resolution prior network with a Sapiens MAE backbone that injects strong human-centric priors. It also diverges from mask-guided approaches (MaGGie, MatAnyone) by eliminating the dependency on external segmentation models like SAM2, and from chunk-based transformers (VMFormer) by avoiding the quadratic cost of multi-frame self-attention at high resolution. The PBR-based dataset construction via Unreal Engine and MetaHuman represents a departure from the chroma-key and manual-annotation pipelines used by VideoMatte240K and HHM50K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The α Matte4K dataset (900 videos, 115K 4K frames with pixel-accurate PBR mattes) fills a critical gap: no prior video matting dataset offered both 4K resolution and physically consistent foreground-background compositing, and the t-SNE analysis showing it aligns more closely with real-world data than VM provides a concrete explanation for its transfer benefit. The 13.7% temporal sparsity observation offers a principled justification for the two-stage design and could inform future efficient video processing. However, the method still runs at ~12 FPS at 4K (not real-time), and all training data is synthetic, leaving open whether the PBR-accurate supervision fully captures the noise, occlusion, and lighting complexity of real footage. The gap between synthetic training and real-world evaluation (CRGNN) remains a non-trivial challenge for the field.
