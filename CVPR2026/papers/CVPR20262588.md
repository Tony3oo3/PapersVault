## Problem

Nearly all remote sensing image-text retrieval (RSITR) methods assume perfectly aligned image-text pairs during training, yet real-world datasets like RSITMD contain mismatched descriptions that mislead the learning process. This paper identifies and formalizes the previously unaddressed Noisy Correspondence (NC) problem in RSITR, where incorrect supervision signals from mismatched pairs degrade cross-modal retrieval performance, particularly under high noise rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262588/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes remote sensing images and their associated text captions, extracting both global and local (patch-level) features via a frozen CLIP ViT-B-32 backbone. At inference, it produces a fused fine-grained similarity score S^f = αS^g + (1−α)S^l that combines global cosine similarity with a double L2-norm local similarity, enabling bidirectional image-to-text and text-to-image ranking. Training operates on batches of 100 pairs with synthetic noise injected at 20%–80% rates to simulate real-world misalignment.

## Method

RRSITR first establishes fine-grained cross-modal alignment via InfoNCE contrastive losses on both global and local features. It then partitions training pairs into clean (ℓ_i < γ₁), ambiguous (γ₁ ≤ ℓ_i < γ₂), and noisy (ℓ_i ≥ γ₂) categories based on per-pair contrastive loss magnitude. For clean and ambiguous pairs, a novel multi-modal self-paced regularizer R(w_i, γ) = −(2/π)γ[w_i·arccos(w_i) − √(1−w_i²)] dynamically assigns reliability weights that increase as loss decreases, enforcing an easy-to-hard curriculum. For noisy pairs, a Robust Triplet Loss (RTL) with adaptively scaled soft margins μ̂_i = σ·(1 + max(0, S(v_i, t̂_h) − S(v_i, t_i))) prevents the model from overfitting to incorrect correspondences by widening the margin when the hardest negative is closer than the positive. The overall objective L = L_S1 + λ₁L_S2 + λ₂L_soft jointly optimizes all three components, with weights w_i and network parameters Θ updated alternately.

## Results

On RSITMD at 80% noise, RRSITR achieves mR of 42.06 (I2T R@1=22.03, T2I R@1=17.72), surpassing the strongest baseline CUP (mR=33.68) by 8.38 points. On NWPU at 80% noise, the gap widens dramatically: RRSITR reaches mR=32.13 versus CUP's 12.21, while most other baselines (MSA, KAMCL, S-CLIP) collapse to near-zero performance (mR < 5). At the lower 20% noise setting on RSITMD, RRSITR still leads with mR=45.58 over CUP's 38.99, confirming consistent gains across noise levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RRSITR outperforms all 10 baselines across every dataset and noise rate. The closest competitor is CUP (context and uncertainty-aware prompt, TNNLS 2025), which is the only method that retains meaningful performance under high noise; at 80% noise on RSITMD, RRSITR leads CUP by 8.34 mR points (35.93 vs. 27.59), and on NWPU the margin expands to 19.92 points (32.13 vs. 12.21). Transformer-based baselines such as MSA, S-CLIP, and SEMICLIP degrade catastrophically at 60–80% noise (mR dropping below 5 on NWPU and RSICD), while RRSITR maintains mR above 19 on all three datasets. Among DCNN-based methods, PIR and SWAN are the most robust but still trail RRSITR by 10–25 mR points at 80% noise. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing the self-paced learning module (#2) or the robust triplet loss (#3) each costs 3–6 mR points, and replacing the easy-to-hard strategy with hard-to-easy (#5) or random weighting (#6) causes near-total collapse (mR ≈ 1–3), validating the specific design choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes self-paced learning (SPL), originally developed for handling noisy labels and outliers in single-modality settings, to address a specific data-quality problem in remote sensing image-text retrieval: noisy correspondence (NC), where image-text pairs are mismatched. It builds on the CLIP-based contrastive learning paradigm (ViT-B-32 backbone, InfoNCE loss) that underpins recent RSITR methods like CUP and SEMICLIP, but introduces a three-category sample partitioning (clean/ambiguous/noisy) with a novel cosine-based self-paced regularizer and an adaptive-margin robust triplet loss. The authors position NC as an 'untouched problem' in RSITR, noting that while a few prior works acknowledged the existence of weakly correlated pairs, none explicitly designed training strategies to mitigate their effect on retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The NC problem is practically relevant because remote sensing annotation is expensive and the nadir viewing geometry inherently produces ambiguous scene descriptions, making perfectly aligned pairs unrealistic at scale. However, the evaluation relies entirely on synthetic noise (randomly shuffling captions), which is a strong simplification of real-world mismatches that tend to be partial or semantically close rather than fully random. Open questions include whether the fixed thresholds γ₁ and γ₂ can be learned automatically, how the method generalizes to subtler forms of correspondence noise, and whether the three-category split transfers to other cross-modal domains beyond remote sensing.
