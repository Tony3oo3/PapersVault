## Problem

Existing differentiable ray tracing pipelines for joint lens design model only geometric degradation, which is inaccurate for miniature optical systems where diffraction effects dominate. Additionally, simplifying lens systems to reduce component count traditionally requires extensive manual expertise and commercial software, with no automated mechanism to identify which component is safe to remove without collapsing the system.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263715/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a parameterized lens system (surface curvatures, conic constants, thicknesses, materials) and raw Bayer-pattern images from the FiveK dataset (3840×2160 training, 4800×2700 testing) as input. It produces a simplified lens system with one component removed, a trained DeepSN post-processing network, and restored RGB images. PSF estimation operates at three wavelengths (480/550/650 nm) with channel-specific weight ratios, sampling 129×129 rays on the entrance pupil and a 45×45 grid on the image plane at 1.6 μm pixel pitch.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, a contribution metric combining per-surface optical power and symmetry terms (summed over all sampled rays and wavelengths) identifies the least impactful component, which is then progressively flattened and thinned via a deletion loss (flat loss + residual thickness loss) until a diameter-scaled threshold triggers complete removal; the remaining system is then rapidly converged with damped least squares. Second, a joint optimization stage simulates degraded raw images by convolving demosaiced RGB with spatially variant PSFs computed via a differentiable Rayleigh-Sommerfeld diffraction integral, and co-optimizes the lens parameters and a DeepSN restoration network under a combined image-plus-optical loss. A key efficiency contribution is manually deriving PSF gradients with respect to ray coordinates and optical path length from forward-traced data, avoiding the large intermediate storage of automatic differentiation and significantly reducing GPU memory while preserving identical computation time.

## Results

On the FiveK dataset, the joint deletion pipeline (Del. Joint) outperforms the separate design baseline on both lens systems. For the USP4488788 (5-piece, 6.83 mm), the joint method achieves a recovery PSNR of 40.79 dB, SSIM of 0.9759, and LPIPS of 0.0287, compared to 40.65 / 0.9745 / 0.0345 for the separate design. For the Double Gauss (4-group, 22.50 mm), the joint method reaches 41.26 dB PSNR, 0.9797 SSIM, and 0.0166 LPIPS in recovery, versus 40.90 / 0.9785 / 0.0169 for the separate design. The simplified systems retain optical specifications close to the originals (e.g., EFFL 6.83 mm, F# 3.20 for USP4488788; EFFL 22.50 mm, F# 3.94 for Double Gauss) with only a modest increase in average spot RMS (2.5 µm vs. 3.5 µm; 1.2 µm vs. 1.0 µm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the 'separate design' approach, where lens parameters are frozen after component deletion and only the post-processing network is optimized. The joint optimization method (Del. Joint) consistently outperforms this baseline on both lens systems: on USP4488788 it gains +0.14 dB PSNR, +0.0014 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS from 0.0345 to 0.0287 in recovery; on Double Gauss it gains +0.36 dB PSNR, +0.0012 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS from 0.0169 to 0.0166. The paper also compares its Rayleigh-Sommerfeld-based PSF estimation against the geometric PSF method of Yang et al. [31] (Nature Communications 2024); the diffraction-aware PSF produces degraded images that are visually and quantitatively closer to Zemax ground truth, particularly in color fidelity and blur morphology. Ablation studies further confirm that the contribution metric avoids system failure (force-deleting C1 or C2 on USP4488788 causes complete failure) and that the deletion loss prevents convergence to poor local minima (without it, Double Gauss spot RMS degrades from 1.2 µm to 42.5 µm). Overall, the joint pipeline achieves imaging quality on par with or slightly exceeding the original multi-element lens while removing one component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the differentiable ray tracing framework for complex refractive lens systems (Sun et al. 2021; Wang et al. 2022; Teh et al. 2024/2025) by introducing a diffraction-aware PSF model based on Rayleigh-Sommerfeld theory, addressing a key limitation where prior methods only captured geometric degradation. The contribution metric for identifying removable components is adapted from Sasian and Descour's (1998) power-distribution and symmetry analysis, but generalized to all sampled rays via GPU-parallel computation rather than principal/marginal rays alone. The joint optimization pipeline integrates the DeepSN restoration network, positioning this work at the intersection of automated optical design and computational imaging, departing from prior simplified-design methods (aspherical replacements, Seidel-coefficient-based relaxation) that still required substantial manual expertise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a practical path toward automating a design process that traditionally demands weeks of expert optical engineering, with direct relevance to cost-sensitive applications like smartphone and EV cameras. The diffraction-aware PSF is critical for miniature systems where geometric approximation breaks down, and the custom gradient computation strategy makes joint optimization feasible under realistic GPU memory budgets. However, the evaluation is limited to two lens systems and a single dataset (FiveK), and the pipeline deletes only one component per run; scalability to multi-component deletion, manufacturing tolerance constraints, and broader lens families remain open questions that will determine real-world adoption.
