## Problem

Long-form video reasoning in Video LLMs suffers from information dilution when uniform frame sampling fills the context window with redundant frames, obscuring the critical evidence needed for accurate answers. Existing pixel-space video reasoning agents further fail to enforce evidence purity through their reward signals and cannot access fine-grained temporal detail beyond the initially pre-sampled frames, leaving a gap between coarse frame selection and the temporal resolution required for precise reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261621/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video uniformly sampled to at most 32 frames alongside a text question. During reasoning, it may invoke up to two frame-selection actions; each selection triggers an automatic localized re-sampling that extracts up to 16 additional frames from the temporal interval between the selected key frame and its nearest neighbor in the current context, replacing the visual context with this refined set. The output is a multimodal chain-of-thought trajectory interleaving textual reasoning steps with frame-selection tool calls, culminating in a final text answer.

## Method

The framework (EARL) trains a Qwen2.5-VL-7B model in two stages: an operation-aware SFT phase that teaches the model to execute multi-step reasoning with explicit frame-selection tool calls, followed by an evidence-aware reinforcement learning phase that refines the selection policy. The RL reward is a weighted sum of three components: a fixed action reward encouraging frame selection, an IoU-based relevance reward that directly measures overlap between selected frames and human-verified golden key frames (enforcing the 'Select Less' principle), and a correctness reward that grants full credit only when the answer is correct AND the selected-frame IoU exceeds 0.5, thereby tying answer accuracy to evidence purity. A dynamic adjustment mechanism shifts the reward weighting from exploration-heavy (high action-reward, low relevance-reward) early in training to purity-focused later, ensuring stable convergence. The key architectural innovation is the localized re-sampling step: after the model selects key frames, the system automatically re-samples the original video at fine temporal granularity around those frames, giving the model access to detail unavailable in the initial uniform sampling.

## Results

The EARL-trained 7B model (32 frames) achieves 64.9% on VideoMME (Overall), 57.8% on VideoMME (Long), 59.8% on LongVideoBench, 46.2% on LVBench, and 69.0% on MVBench, establishing new state-of-the-art among open-source Video LLMs. Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline with the same 32-frame budget, the method yields absolute gains of +11.3% on VideoMME, +16.6% on LongVideoBench, and +6.4% on MVBench. On the long-video subset (VideoMME Long, 30–60 min), the model outperforms LongVA (47.6% with 128 frames) and LongVILA (53.0% with 256 frames) despite using only 32 initial frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 32 frames), EARL delivers large absolute gains: +11.3% on VideoMME (64.9 vs 53.6), +16.6% on LongVideoBench (59.8 vs 43.2), and +6.4% on MVBench (69.0 vs 62.6). Among end-to-end agent Video LLMs, the method outperforms Pixel Reasoner by +1.2% on MVBench (69.0 vs 67.8), FrameMind by +4.8% on MVBench (69.0 vs 64.2) and +4.0% on VideoMME (64.9 vs 60.9), and VITAL by +0.8% on VideoMME (64.9 vs 64.1). Against multi-agent systems, EARL beats VideoMind on all five benchmarks (e.g., +6.7% on VideoMME, +3.5% on LongVideoBench, +4.4% on MVBench) and edges out Video-RAG on VideoMME (+2.8%) and LongVideoBench (+1.1%), though Video-RAG retains a lead on VideoMME Long (59.8 vs 57.8). On the long-video regime, the 32-frame EARL model surpasses LongVA (128 frames) by +10.2% on VideoMME Long and LongVILA (256 frames) by +4.8%, demonstrating that adaptive evidence selection is more effective than simply increasing the fixed frame budget. The proprietary GPT-4o remains ahead on VideoMME (77.2 vs 64.9) and LongVideoBench (66.7 vs 59.8), indicating a remaining gap to closed-source systems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pixel-space video reasoning line initiated by Pixel Reasoner, VITAL, and FrameMind, which use RL to enable proactive frame selection during reasoning. It departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) introducing an IoU-based multi-component reward system that explicitly enforces evidence purity (selecting only frames that overlap with annotated golden key frames), and (2) adding localized re-sampling around selected key frames to access fine-grained temporal detail beyond the initial uniform sampling. Conceptually, it also builds on the 'Thinking with Images' paradigm and the RL-for-reasoning trend (DeepEyes, Video-R1), but reframes the core reasoning step as an active evidence-interrogation policy rather than a passive CoT over fixed visual input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a 32-frame adaptive selection strategy can outperform fixed-context models using 128–256 frames on long-video benchmarks, challenging the prevailing assumption that simply increasing frame count improves reasoning. The EARL framework's evidence-purity reward mechanism is a reusable design pattern for any agentic multimodal system that must curate its own visual context. Key limitations include reliance on GPT-4o-plus-human key-frame annotation (which may not scale to open-ended or novel video domains), a hard cap of two selection operations per prompt, and evaluation on a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL), leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger or architecturally different Video LLMs.
