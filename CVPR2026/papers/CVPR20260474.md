## Problem

Existing multimodal interaction systems either handle unimodal generation or suffer from degraded reasoning and poor cross-modal alignment when jointly modeling language, speech, and motion. No prior system unifies high-level reasoning, instruction-following, temporally synchronized multimodal generation, and video synthesis within a single real-time interactive loop.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260474/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a user query in either text or speech form and produces a structured, temporally aligned output sequence: an internal chain-of-thought plan, a textual response, discrete acoustic tokens (decoded to speech audio), and discrete motion tokens (decoded to SMPL-X 6D poses). For visual output, two rendering backends are supported: a WAN-based diffusion renderer producing photorealistic 2D video conditioned on DWPose-projected 2D keypoints, and a Gaussian Splatting renderer producing 3D human video directly from pose sequences. All modalities are generated autoregressively in a single pass, with speech segmented at punctuation and pause boundaries for alignment.

## Method

U-Mind is built on the AnyGPT (LLaMA2-7B) backbone, extending it into a unified multimodal space by discretizing motion (RVQ-VAE over SMPL-X 6D joint rotations) and speech (SpeechTokenizer RVQ-VAE) into tokens that share the LLM's embedding matrix, enabling joint autoregressive next-token prediction across all modalities. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: (1) Rehearsal-Driven Pre-training mixes modality-grounding tasks (T2M, S2M, T2S) with large-scale pure-text reasoning data (OpenOrca) to prevent catastrophic forgetting, while a segment-wise alignment strategy splits inputs at prosodic boundaries and trains on randomized segment compositions to enforce fine-grained temporal synchrony; (2) Instruction Tuning introduces a text-first decoding strategy where the model must emit a CoT reasoning block (delimited by special tokens) before generating text, speech, and motion tokens, ensuring symbolic planning precedes continuous generation. At inference, the full pipeline runs in real time, with a pose-controllable video renderer closing the loop by synthesizing synchronized visual output from the generated motion and speech.

## Results

On the multimodal dialogue task (Table 1), U-Mind achieves an FGD of 7.67, substantially outperforming SOLAMI (18.43) and the LLM+TTS+LOM pipeline (17.87), while scoring 8.11 on Naturalness and 8.23 on Relevance (LLM-judged, 10-point scale). On speech-to-motion (S2M, Table 3), U-Mind attains the best FGD (11.12) and lowest Angle Error (0.188) across all baselines, with a Diversity of 11.48. On text-to-motion (T2M, Table 4), U-Mind achieves the lowest Angle Error (0.109) and highest Diversity (10.71), though SOLAMI reports a lower FGD (8.64 vs. 12.69) due to its task-specific optimization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SOLAMI [40], U-Mind wins decisively on FGD (7.67 vs. 18.43 in dialogue; 11.12 vs. N/A in S2M) and Naturalness (8.11 vs. 5.62), while SOLAMI's Relevance collapses to 1.23, indicating it fails to understand prompts and degenerates into generic gestures. Against the LLM+TTS+LOM pipeline [12], U-Mind achieves a much lower FGD (7.67 vs. 17.87) and far higher Naturalness (8.11 vs. 3.95), though the pipeline edges out on Relevance (8.72 vs. 8.23) due to its reliance on a strong standalone LLM. On S2M, U-Mind outperforms all four dedicated gesture models (CaMN, DisCo, EMAGE, LOM) on both FGD (11.12 vs. 16.47–20.37) and Angle Error (0.188 vs. 0.248–0.264), while LOM retains a slight Diversity advantage (11.96 vs. 11.48). On T2M, U-Mind achieves the best Angle Error (0.109 vs. 0.331–0.336) and Diversity (10.71), but SOLAMI's lower FGD (8.64) reflects its task-specific training without cross-modal integration overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

U-Mind extends the discrete-token multimodal LLM paradigm established by AnyGPT and SOLAMI, which map speech and motion into a shared autoregressive token space, by adding a third perceptual modality (video rendering) and explicitly addressing the reasoning-degradation problem that prior unified systems (notably SOLAMI) neglect. The speech discretization follows SpeechTokenizer's RVQ-VAE, and the motion tokenization uses a similar RVQ-VAE over SMPL-X 6D poses, but the key departure is the two-stage training recipe—rehearsal-driven pre-training mixed with pure-text reasoning data, followed by instruction tuning with a text-first CoT decoding strategy—that has no direct counterpart in prior motion-generation or dialogue systems. The video rendering backends (WAN-based diffusion and Gaussian Splatting) are borrowed from the broader video-generation literature and serve as a downstream decoder rather than a core contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first systems to close the full loop from user query through internal reasoning to synchronized text, speech, motion, and photorealistic video in a single real-time interaction, making it a practical reference architecture for embodied conversational agents. The rehearsal-driven learning and text-first decoding strategies offer a reusable recipe for preserving LLM reasoning during multimodal fine-tuning, a problem that plagues any system that extends a text LLM into perceptual modalities. Open questions remain around the empirical (rather than principled) data-mixing schedule, the expressiveness ceiling imposed by the discrete motion vocabulary (limiting fine-grained facial and hand detail), and whether the approach scales to additional modalities or longer-horizon interactions without further architectural changes.
