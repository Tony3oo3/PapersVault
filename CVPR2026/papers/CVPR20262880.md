## Problem

Existing collaborative fairness methods in federated learning assign fixed reputation proportions throughout training, under-rewarding low-contribution clients in early rounds when the global model is still weak, which degrades overall model performance. Additionally, gradient-based rewards cause inter-model inconsistency across clients, while submodel-based rewards cause unequal neuron training frequencies within the global model. FedRAC addresses both issues simultaneously to achieve high fairness without sacrificing accuracy.

## Input / Output

The server receives local client images (standard resolutions: 32×32 for CIFAR-10/SVHN/EMNIST, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet), each client's standalone-model contribution score, and maintains a neuron frequency table tracking historical allocation counts per neuron. Each round, the server outputs a binary mask and corresponding submodel per client (sized proportionally to the client's reputation), an updated global model via masked aggregation, and a refreshed neuron frequency table. The key structural detail is that submodel masks are nested—masks with more active units strictly contain those with fewer—enabling consistent evaluation and allocation.

## Method

FedRAC comprises two modules. The dynamic reputation calculation module computes per-round reputations as r_i^t = c_i + (Acc_g^t − tmp_i^t)·c_i^n/100, where tmp_i^t grows with both the training round and global model accuracy; this compresses reputation gaps in early rounds (benefiting low-contribution clients) and gradually diverges them toward contribution-proportional values. The rolling submodel allocation module maintains a neuron frequency table, sorts neurons by ascending historical allocation count each round, and constructs submodels by selecting the least-trained neurons first, ensuring uniform neuron coverage over time. Submodels are then evaluated on a 10% validation split and assigned to clients in order of reputation (higher reputation receives the higher-performing submodel). Theoretical guarantees include a fairness theorem (higher-reputation clients obtain models closer to the global optimum) and an asymptotic convergence proof under standard smoothness and strong-convexity assumptions.

## Results

FedRAC achieves the highest test accuracy across all three main datasets and four non-IID scenarios. On CIFAR-10, it reaches 54.71% (DIR(7.0)) and 49.37% (POW), outperforming the strongest baseline FedSAC (51.60% and 48.63%) by 3.1 and 0.7 percentage points respectively. On SVHN, FedRAC attains 82.71% (DIR(3.0)) versus FedSAC's 79.63%, and on EMNIST it reaches 84.00% (DIR(7.0)) versus FedSAC's 81.37%. For collaborative fairness (Pearson ρ), FedRAC scores 99.41–99.89 across scenarios, far exceeding FedSAC (96.71–98.55) and FedAvg (which is negative, e.g., −44.82 on CIFAR-10 POW).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRAC outperforms all seven baselines on both accuracy and fairness across every dataset and scenario tested. Against the closest competitor FedSAC (KDD 2024), FedRAC gains 3.1 pp accuracy on CIFAR-10 DIR(7.0) (54.71% vs 51.60%), 3.1 pp on SVHN DIR(3.0) (82.71% vs 79.63%), and 2.6 pp on EMNIST DIR(7.0) (84.00% vs 81.37%), while also improving fairness ρ from 97.95 to 99.53 (CIFAR-10 DIR(7.0)). Gradient-based methods (CFFL, CGSV, FedAVE, IAFL) trail FedRAC by 1–10 pp in accuracy and by 10–50+ points in fairness; FedAvg is the weakest, recording negative fairness scores (e.g., −44.82 on CIFAR-10 POW) and the lowest accuracy in most scenarios. The BCF rate (proportion of clients within the fairness boundary) reaches 1.00 for FedRAC in 10 of 12 scenarios, compared to at most 0.97 for FedSAC and as low as 0.10 for FedAvg. Ablation confirms both the dynamic reputation module and the rolling allocation module are necessary: removing either drops SVHN accuracy by 8–12 pp.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedRAC extends the collaborative fairness (CF) line in federated learning (CFFL, CGSV, FedAVE, IAFL, FedSAC) by addressing two specific limitations: fixed reputation proportions that under-reward low-contribution clients early in training, and static importance-based submodel allocation that causes uneven neuron training frequencies. The rolling submodel construction mechanism is directly adapted from FedRolex [1], which introduced rolling extraction for model-heterogeneous FL but without any fairness objective. The key conceptual departure is making reputation dynamic (coupled to global model performance over rounds) and coupling submodel allocation to a neuron frequency table, rather than treating reputation as a static scalar and submodel selection as a one-shot importance ranking as in FedSAC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedRAC provides the first CF framework with both a fairness guarantee (Theorem 1) and a convergence guarantee (Theorem 2) simultaneously, and empirically shows that fairness and accuracy need not be traded off. However, the experimental scope is limited to 10 clients, small models (2-hidden-layer FFN, ResNet-18), and four relatively small datasets; the authors themselves flag large-scale models as future work. Open questions include whether the dynamic reputation mechanism and neuron frequency table scale to thousands of clients or billion-parameter models, and whether the α-BCF formalization holds under more adversarial or Byzantine settings.
