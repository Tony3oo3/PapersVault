## Problem

Text-to-image person retrieval (TIPR) suffers from inherent ambiguity in fine-grained cross-modal alignment: existing models struggle to capture detailed correspondences between textual descriptions and visual pedestrian attributes, leading to mismatched retrievals. While recent methods incorporate MLLMs for text expansion or re-ranking, they only perform coarse utilization of generated content, leaving the rich fine-grained semantic correspondence between generated descriptions and visual content underexploited and the alignment unexplainable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264058/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a natural-language pedestrian description and a gallery of RGB pedestrian images (loaded without resizing) and outputs a ranked list of images scored by the sum of global and refined cross-modal similarities. During training, each image additionally passes through a 5-turn MLLM dialogue (Qwen2-VL-7B) using predefined attribute question templates covering upper clothing, hats, pants, shoes, and other cues, producing a compact summary that is encoded alongside the image and text through a CLIP-ViT/B-16 backbone.

## Method

CECA integrates three components: (1) Conversation Attribute Mining (CAM) decomposes pedestrian description into a multi-turn QA dialogue with an MLLM, then summarizes the transcript into a compact attribute summary; (2) a Bidirectional Cross-Attention Mixer (BCM) performs mutual self-attention and cross-attention between image and summary token sequences with gated residual fusion, while the text branch receives only self-attention to preserve global semantic consistency; (3) a Confidence-Aware Weighting Loss (CAWL) computes a per-sample confidence weight as the geometric mean of summary-text and summary-image InfoNCE probabilities, dynamically down-weighting noisy dialogues in a gated mixture of contrastive objectives. Training combines Triplet Alignment Loss (TAL) on both global (CLS/EOS) and refined (token-level) embeddings with CAWL, and retrieval scores average the global and refined similarities.

## Results

Without ReID-domain pre-training, CECA achieves the highest Rank-1 on all three benchmarks: 78.30% on CUHK-PEDES, 72.25% on ICFG-PEDES, and 71.40% on RSTPReid, surpassing the second-best methods by +0.39%, +3.23%, and +0.85% respectively. With ReID-domain pre-training, CECA further improves to 79.65% (CUHK-PEDES), 74.32% (ICFG-PEDES), and 73.45% (RSTPReid) Rank-1, setting new state-of-the-art on most Rank metrics. However, CECA's mAP scores (67.07, 39.56, 55.44) remain slightly below ICL^t's 70.44 on CUHK-PEDES, which the authors attribute to the attribute reasoning process amplifying inter-view variations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CECA outperforms all 15 baselines on Rank-1 across all three datasets in both settings (with and without ReID-domain pre-training). In the no-pretraining group, CECA leads ICL [23] by +0.39% on CUHK-PEDES, GAHR [19] by +3.23% on ICFG-PEDES, and ICL [23] by +0.85% on RSTPReid. With ReID pre-training, the margins widen: CECA beats ICL^t [23] by +0.59% on CUHK-PEDES, +4.27% on ICFG-PEDES, and +0.90% on RSTPReid, and surpasses GA-DMS [35] by +2.05% on CUHK-PEDES. The one notable loss is on mAP: ICL^t [23] achieves 70.44 mAP on CUHK-PEDES versus CECA's 67.07, and GAHR [19] leads on ICFG-PEDES mAP (42.10 vs. 37.57 in the no-pretraining group). The authors attribute this mAP gap to the conversational attribute mining process amplifying view-specific variations (lighting, pose, occlusion) of the same identity, which hurts ranking consistency beyond the top-1 position.

## Relation to Prior Work

CECA builds directly on the CLIP-based cross-modal retrieval paradigm and the Triplet Alignment Loss (TAL) from RDE [22] for enforcing global and local consistency. It departs from prior MLLM-augmented TIPR methods (e.g., ICL [23], NAM [27], HAM [8]) that use MLLMs only for coarse text expansion, pseudo-caption generation, or re-ranking, by instead employing a structured multi-turn QA dialogue to mine fine-grained pedestrian attributes and fusing the resulting conversation tokens with visual tokens through a novel Bidirectional Cross-Attention Mixer. The Confidence-Aware Weighting Loss further distinguishes the work by explicitly down-weighting unreliable MLLM-generated dialogues during contrastive training, a concern largely unaddressed in prior generation-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that conversational attribute mining combined with bidirectional token-level interaction can meaningfully reduce the ambiguity in fine-grained text-image alignment, yielding state-of-the-art Rank-1 on all three standard benchmarks and notably strong cross-domain transfer (Table 4). However, the authors honestly note that mAP remains slightly below some competitors because the attribute-reasoning process amplifies view-specific variations (lighting, pose, occlusion), leaving open the question of how to align shared identity features across viewpoints. The framework's MLLM-agnostic design (validated with Qwen2-VL, LLaVA, and BLIP-2) makes it a practical plug-in for existing TIPR pipelines, and the learnable question-template direction suggested for future work points toward a more adaptive, view-aware dialogue strategy.
