## Problem

No-Reference Point Cloud Quality Assessment (NR-PCQA) suffers from poor generalization due to the scarcity of annotated point cloud datasets. Existing UDA-based PCQA methods (e.g., IT-PCQA) inherit feature alignment strategies from image classification that enforce semantic consistency while ignoring quality information, causing features with similar content but different quality levels to be incorrectly aligned across domains. This quality-regardless alignment, combined with uniform alignment strength and single-domain-only feature augmentation, limits the model's ability to preserve perceptual quality ranking.

## Input / Output

Point clouds are projected onto six vertical cube faces and stitched into a single 224×224 multi-view image, which is fed into a shared ResNet-50 backbone alongside natural images (TID2013 or KADID-10k) from the source domain. The model outputs a single scalar quality score via a two-layer fully-connected regressor. During training, pseudo-labels for target-domain point clouds are generated by the predictor itself and used in the RCA alignment loss, while at test time the same predictor directly scores unseen point cloud projections.

## Method

QD-PCQA introduces two quality-aware strategies on top of a DANN-based adversarial framework. The Rank-weighted Conditional Alignment (RCA) strategy modifies the COD metric by constructing rank-weighted feature kernels that up-weight sample pairs exhibiting cross-domain ranking bias (where predicted quality ordering contradicts true/pseudo-label ordering), and conditions alignment on quality scores to enforce quality-consistent feature matching. The Quality-guided Feature Augmentation (QFA) strategy comprises three modules: a Quality-guided Style Mixup (QSM) that uses a Gaussian-kernel selection to pair source samples with similar quality before performing style interpolation; a multi-layer extension that applies QSM at different ResNet stages (shallow for high-quality, deep for low-quality samples) to exploit hierarchical feature complementarity; and a dual-domain augmentation that applies SM to target features as well, balancing the adversarial training. Training follows a two-stage schedule: DANN warm-up for 5,000 iterations without pseudo-labels, followed by joint optimization with RCA once pseudo-labels become reliable.

## Results

On TID2013→SJTU-PCQA, QD-PCQA achieves PLCC 0.842, SROCC 0.753, KROCC 0.566, and RMSE 1.358, surpassing the strongest baseline IT-PCQA (PLCC 0.693, RMSE 1.624) by 21.5% in PLCC and 16.4% in RMSE. On the more challenging TID2013→WPC transfer, QD-PCQA reaches PLCC 0.563 and SROCC 0.572, outperforming IT-PCQA by 31.2% in PLCC. With KADID-10k as source, QD-PCQA similarly leads on both targets (PLCC 0.843 on SJTU-PCQA, PLCC 0.553 on WPC), confirming consistent gains across source datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QD-PCQA outperforms all five baselines across every metric on both target datasets. Against the closest competitor IT-PCQA [38], the gains are most pronounced: +21.5% PLCC and −16.4% RMSE on SJTU-PCQA, and +31.2% PLCC on WPC (TID2013 source). The general UDA baselines DANN [7] and COD [35] trail significantly, with DANN achieving only PLCC 0.596 on SJTU-PCQA versus QD-PCQA's 0.842, indicating that naive marginal or conditional alignment without quality-awareness is insufficient. The image-to-image IQA transfer methods StyleAM [22] and Chen's [1] also underperform, confirming that quality-specific alignment strategies (RCA) and quality-guided augmentation (QFA) are critical for cross-media PCQA. The 'No Adapt' ablation (PLCC 0.548) further validates that domain adaptation itself is necessary, while the gap between No Adapt and QD-PCQA (0.842) quantifies the total benefit of the proposed framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-to-point-cloud UDA paradigm established by IT-PCQA (Yang et al., CVPR 2022), which first demonstrated that quality prediction capability can be transferred from labeled images to unlabeled point clouds via DANN-based feature alignment. It further builds on COD (Yang et al., ECCV 2024) for conditional distribution alignment and StyleAM (Lu et al., IEEE TMM 2024) for style-based feature augmentation. The key conceptual departure is that prior methods inherit alignment strategies from image classification that enforce semantic consistency while ignoring quality-level information; QD-PCQA introduces quality-conditioned alignment (RCA) and quality-guided augmentation (QFA) to ensure that features are aligned and augmented in a manner consistent with perceptual quality ranking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quality-aware alignment principle addresses a genuine gap in UDA-based quality assessment: without quality conditioning, the model can align semantically similar but quality-dissimilar samples, degrading ranking sensitivity. However, the approach remains limited to two point cloud datasets (SJTU-PCQA, WPC) and two image source datasets, and performance on WPC (PLCC ≈ 0.56) reveals that high-level semantic distortions from G-PCC compression remain a significant challenge for cross-domain transfer. Open questions include whether the quality-aware alignment principle generalizes to other media (video, 3D mesh, Gaussian splatting), whether the two-stage training protocol is strictly necessary, and whether the method scales to larger, more diverse point cloud benchmarks.
