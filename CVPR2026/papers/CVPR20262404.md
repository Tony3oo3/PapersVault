## Problem

Prior scanpath prediction work operates in 2D pixel coordinates on static images, which is fundamentally insufficient for AR/VR, robotics, and embodied AI applications that require gaze predictions grounded in a persistent 3D coordinate system. This paper introduces the novel task of forecasting 3D scanpaths from egocentric video, where the viewer continuously moves through a dynamic scene, making gaze targets potentially invisible across frames and requiring predictions in a world-anchored frame rather than per-frame pixel space.

## Input / Output

The model takes 10 observed 3D gaze fixations (each a 3D Cartesian position plus fixation duration in seconds), corresponding egocentric video frames at 224×224 resolution, and per-frame head poses (quaternion rotation + 3D translation) as input. It outputs the next 10 future fixations as 3D positions and durations in a canonical coordinate frame centered at the camera position of the last observed frame. The fixed-length formulation (10 in, 10 out) treats time as a confounding factor, focusing on the sequence of gaze locations rather than continuous temporal dynamics.

## Method

The architecture is a two-branch transformer: a visual context encoder and a 3D scanpath decoder. The encoder uses a frozen DINOv2-B backbone to extract dense patch features (16×16×768) from the canonical frame and global features from preceding frames, concatenates them with linearly embedded head pose vectors, and processes them through 2 self-attention transformer layers with sinusoidal temporal position embeddings. The decoder embeds observed fixations and learnable query vectors, then applies 2 transformer decoder layers where trajectory features self-attend bidirectionally and cross-attend to the visual context, with matching temporal position embeddings to align gaze and visual features. A linear projection decodes the query outputs into predicted 3D positions and durations. Training uses a weighted MSE multitask loss on position and duration, with the model trained for 3 epochs over 87k overlapping trajectories from the Aria Digital Twin dataset.

## Results

On the Aria Digital Twin dataset (646 test segments, N_o=10 observed fixations, N_f=10 predicted fixations), the full model (video + head pose) achieves DTW=1.377 m, EUC=1.382 m, FRE=2.173 m, EYE=1.800 m, and TDE=0.859 m, outperforming the strongest heuristic baseline (last observed point: DTW=1.533 m) and the 3D-adapted TPP-Gaze (DTW=1.972 m). In MultiMatch metrics, the full model scores Pos=1.350 and Dur=0.630, compared to Pos=1.504 for the last-observed-point baseline and Pos=1.840 for TPP-Gaze (3D modified). Ablations show that adding video frames and head pose over the trajectory-only variant reduces DTW from 1.450 to 1.377 m, with head pose most impactful on depth (Z-axis) error (0.927→0.890 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all baselines on the primary distance metrics. Against the strongest heuristic (last observed point), the full model reduces DTW from 1.533 m to 1.377 m (−10.2%), EUC from 1.533 m to 1.382 m (−9.8%), and TDE from 1.310 m to 0.859 m (−34.4%), confirming that learned prediction captures gaze dynamics beyond simple persistence. Against TPP-Gaze adapted to 3D, the full model achieves substantially lower DTW (1.377 vs. 1.972 m, −30.2%), EUC (1.382 vs. 2.102 m, −34.2%), and FRE (2.173 vs. 3.245 m, −33.0%), while also improving MultiMatch Position (1.350 vs. 1.840) and Length (0.384 vs. 0.938). The only metrics where TPP-Gaze (3D modified) edges out the full model are Direction (1.191 vs. 1.194) and Duration (0.598 vs. 0.630), both by small margins. The zero-shot TPP-Gaze with ground-truth depth performs poorly (DTW=3.278 m), highlighting the domain gap between 2D image scanpath prediction and egocentric 3D gaze forecasting. The last-observed-point heuristic is notably strong in this setting (unlike in 2D image scanpath prediction), reflecting the low inter-fixation displacement typical of continuous egocentric gaze, which the authors identify as a key challenge for evaluation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space by combining elements from 2D image scanpath prediction (TPP-Gaze, DeepGaze III), egocentric gaze estimation (In the Eye of Transformer, Gaze-LLE), and egocentric behavior forecasting (GazeMotion, FICTION), but departs from all of them by formulating gaze prediction in a persistent 3D world coordinate system rather than per-frame 2D pixel coordinates. Architecturally, it repurposes a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a standard transformer with cross-modal attention, but the key conceptual contribution is the canonical-frame formulation that grounds predictions in the wearer's 3D environment and handles cases where future gaze targets leave the camera's field of view. The paper explicitly positions itself as the first work on this task, with no direct architectural predecessor to build upon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This establishes the first baseline and evaluation protocol for 3D scanpath forecasting in egocentric video, a task with direct applications to AR/VR rendering, robot learning, and active perception. A notable open question is the evaluation methodology: unlike image scanpath datasets where multiple viewers produce diverse ground truths, egocentric gaze is unimodal, making it unclear whether a plausible but different prediction should be penalized as heavily as a wrong one. The modest gains from adding video context and head pose (DTW 1.450 → 1.377) suggest the current architecture does not yet fully exploit dynamic visual information, pointing to future work on larger-scale data and more sophisticated temporal modeling. The finding that a simple 'last observed point' baseline is competitive also raises questions about how much of the task is genuinely predictive versus inertia-driven.
