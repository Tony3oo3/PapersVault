## Problem

Transformer-based video object detectors suffer from quadratic computational complexity when modeling long-range temporal dependencies across frames, while Mamba offers linear-complexity sequence modeling but exhibits limited contextual learning capability. The application of Mamba to video object detection remains entirely unexplored, and no prior work has studied how to combine the complementary strengths of transformers and Mamba in a hybrid architecture for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262317/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 25 video frames resized to a shorter side of 600 pixels, processed through a shared backbone (ResNet-101 or Swin-B) to produce multi-scale feature maps. During training, 4 additional frames are randomly sampled from the same video clip alongside the current frame. The network outputs object class labels and bounding boxes via a shared feed-forward head, with the number of object queries set to 60 (ImageNet VID) or 300 (EPIC-KITCHENS-55) per frame, following the Deformable DETR query-based detection paradigm.

## Method

TMambaDet combines three core components: (1) a Spatial Adaptive Deformable Transformer (SADT) encoder that adaptively predicts the number of sampling points per query (K_min=2 to K_max=8) via a lightweight MLP on the query feature, allocating more points to complex objects like occluded or blurred ones; (2) a Temporal Cascaded Bidirectional Mamba (TCBM) encoder that models inter-frame dependencies with linear complexity using a novel temporal-prioritized bidirectional scan algorithm, which traverses features along the temporal dimension at each fixed spatial coordinate before moving to the next, in both forward and backward directions; (3) a Mamba Entangled Transformer (MaET) decoder that sequentially stacks multi-head self-attention, multi-scale adaptive deformable cross-attention, and cascaded bidirectional Mamba layers to refine object queries with both local spatial-temporal context and long-range temporal information. The architecture is built on Deformable DETR, trained with AdamW for 140K iterations (ImageNet VID) or 220K iterations (EPIC-KITCHENS-55) using the standard DETR loss.

## Results

On ImageNet VID, TMambaDet achieves 87.9% mAP with ResNet-101 (20.6 ms) and 92.1% mAP with Swin-B (39.0 ms), setting new state-of-the-art results on both backbone settings. On EPIC-KITCHENS-55, it reaches 45.1% mAP (ResNet-101) and 50.8% mAP (Swin-B). The method also delivers a strong efficiency-accuracy trade-off, running at 20.6 ms with ResNet-101—faster than all transformer-based competitors while achieving the highest mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With ResNet-101 on ImageNet VID, TMambaDet (87.9% mAP) outperforms the strongest transformer-based competitor TGBFormer (86.5%) by 1.4% and DGC-Net (86.3%) by 1.6%, while also being substantially faster (20.6 ms vs. 24.3 ms and 90.2 ms respectively). Against earlier transformer methods, the gains are larger: 3.2% over ClipVID (84.7%) and 7.4% over TransVOD Lite (80.5%). With Swin-B, TMambaDet (92.1%) surpasses ODND (91.3%) by 0.8% and STPN (90.6%) by 1.5%, while running at 39.0 ms compared to ODND's 55.2 ms. On EPIC-KITCHENS-55, TMambaDet leads by 3.2% over LSTFE-Net (41.9%) with ResNet-101 and by 2.5% over YOLOV++ (48.3%) with Swin-B, confirming generalization beyond ImageNet VID. Notably, TMambaDet achieves the best mAP at the lowest or near-lowest runtime among all methods in both backbone settings, demonstrating that the Mamba-based temporal encoder provides a clear efficiency advantage over quadratic-complexity transformer alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMambaDet is built directly on Deformable DETR as its base detection framework, inheriting its query-based architecture, positional encodings, and loss formulation, while introducing a hybrid encoder-decoder that replaces the standard transformer temporal modeling with Mamba's selective state space model. It departs from prior transformer-based video object detection methods (TransVOD, CETR, TGBFormer) by substituting quadratic-complexity temporal transformers with linear-complexity Mamba for inter-frame modeling, and from prior vision Mamba methods (Samba, Sigma, EAMamba) by being the first to apply Mamba to the video object detection task. The adaptive deformable attention mechanism extends standard deformable attention by making the number of sampling points query-dependent, and the temporal-prioritized bidirectional scan algorithm is a new scanning strategy tailored to the video temporal dimension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper establishes that a hybrid Transformer-Mamba architecture can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce inference time for video object detection, offering a practical path toward efficient long-sequence video understanding without sacrificing long-range modeling quality. The adaptive sampling mechanism demonstrates generalizability beyond the proposed framework (validated on Deformable DETR and TransVOD++), suggesting it could be adopted in other deformable-attention-based detectors. However, the method still struggles with very small objects due to limited temporal modeling of their dynamics, and the approach has only been validated on two datasets, leaving open whether the hybrid design generalizes to longer video sequences, higher-resolution inputs, or other video understanding tasks like tracking or action recognition.
