## Problem

Granular-ball computing (GBC) offers multi-granularity, coarse-to-fine clustering with low computational cost, but its application to image segmentation is blocked by two gaps: (1) no mechanism to explicitly represent the intrinsic uncertainty in image boundaries, noise, and low-contrast regions, and (2) no semantics-aware, justifiable quality criterion to guide GB split-merge operations in a way that preserves semantic coherence. Existing clustering-based segmentation methods (CSMs) are further limited by their single-granularity representations, which fail to capture multi-scale semantic structure.

## Input / Output

SegGBC accepts feature vectors from raw pixels or upstream deep features of natural images (BSD500, DUST at ~481×321 / 400×300), remote sensing images (LoveDA at 600×600), or 128×128 patches from COCO-Stuff. It outputs a pixel-wise segmentation mask and a hierarchical set of multi-granularity granular-ball clusters. The GB representation (SegGBs) is also designed as a plug-and-play front-end: it can replace the raw input to any existing CSM (k-means, DeepClu, PiCIE, DeepCut, etc.) without modifying their internal clustering logic, yielding consistent gains of ≥+3.25% SA and ≥+3.92% mIoU.

## Method

SegGBC introduces two core mechanisms. First, Intuitionistic Fuzzy Granular-Balls (IFS-GBs) compute per-sample membership (Gaussian decay with scale σ_m) and non-membership (Gaussian decay with σ_n < σ_m) relative to each GB center; the residual hesitation degree π = 1 − μ − ν is folded into both the radius (r_max, r_avg) and the center update, explicitly modeling intra-ball uncertainty from noise and blurred boundaries. Second, a Semantic Compactness Metric (SCM_GB) evaluates each GB by the ratio of min(ρ^avg, ρ^max)·min(ρ^sta, ρ^max) to max(ρ^avg, ρ^max)·max(ρ^sta, ρ^max), where ρ^sta is the density within a provably stable region (Theorem 1 guarantees samples there cannot be reassigned to neighboring GBs). The algorithm iteratively splits GBs whose SCM_GB falls below threshold β and merges neighboring GBs whose composite similarity—combining fuzzy membership overlap and hesitation difference—exceeds threshold ε, yielding a coarse-to-fine hierarchical partition without iterative centroid updates.

## Results

On 7 natural images (NI), SegGBC achieves a 7-mIoU of 68.76%, surpassing the second-best GB-based method Ball k-means (59.50%) by 9.26 percentage points, and the best pixel-wise method MK-SPCM (50.06%) by 18.70 points. On 3 remote sensing images (RSI), SegGBC attains a 3-mIoU of 62.10%, outperforming the next-best method by 4.41 points while also achieving the lowest mean time cost (2.06 s on NI, 4.79 s on RSI). As a plug-and-play front-end (SegGBs), the method boosts existing CSMs by ≥+3.25% SA and ≥+3.92% mIoU on COCO-Stuff, with the largest single gain being +38.22% SA for DFKM on NI 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SegGBC outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric on both natural and remote sensing images. Against the closest GB-based competitors, Ball k-means [43] and MGNR [47], SegGBC leads by an average margin exceeding 10 percentage points on SA, NMI, and F1, with the 7-mIoU gap reaching 9.26 points over Ball k-means. Among pixel-wise methods, MK-SPCM [50] is the strongest baseline (7-mIoU 50.06%), yet SegGBC still surpasses it by 18.70 points; notably, MK-SPCM and Ball k-means both degrade sharply on RSIs (SA drops to 70.37% and 69.54% respectively), while SegGBC maintains 82.91% SA on RSI 3. Among cluster-wise deep-feature methods, DeepCut [1] and IRCIS [36] achieve the highest individual-image scores (e.g., DeepCut 96.86% SA on NI 4), but their 7-mIoU (52.73%) and 3-mIoU (45.24%) remain well below SegGBC. As a plug-and-play enhancer (SegGBs), the method improves every tested CSM: DFKM gains +38.22% SA on NI 2, k-means gains +14.57% PixelAcc on COCO-Stuff-3, and IRCIS gains +13.45% PixelAcc and +3.92% mIoU on COCO-Stuff, confirming broad generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SegGBC is the first framework to apply granular-ball computing (GBC) to image segmentation, extending the Ball k-means paradigm (Xia et al., TPAMI 2020) from general data mining to the spatial, multi-scale domain of images. It departs from both pixel-wise CSMs (RLFCM, MK-SPCM) and cluster-wise CSMs (DeepClu, I²Clu, PiCIE, DeepCut) by replacing their single-granularity representations with a hierarchical coarse-to-fine GB structure. The key conceptual additions are intuitionistic fuzzy sets (Atanassov, 1989) for explicit uncertainty modeling via hesitation degrees, and a novel Semantic Compactness Metric (SCM_GB) with a 'stable region' mechanism that replaces the radius-only quality criterion of standard GBC with a semantics-aware, density-informed splitting rule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design—where SegGBs serve as a multi-granularity front-end that boosts diverse existing CSMs by ≥+3.25% SA and ≥+3.92% mIoU without modifying their internal logic—makes the contribution broadly applicable to the unsupervised segmentation community. However, the method relies on hand-tuned hyperparameters (σ_m, σ_n, β, ε), a fixed non-learned semantic criterion, and shows sensitivity to strong local variation and nontrivial overhead on high-resolution remote sensing images. Open questions include whether SCM_GB can be made differentiable and learned end-to-end within deep networks, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the relatively small single-image evaluation protocol used for most comparisons.
