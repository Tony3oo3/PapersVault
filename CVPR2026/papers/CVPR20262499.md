## Problem

Applying native MLLM paradigms to electromagnetic (EM) signals is blocked by three gaps: extreme scarcity of paired signal-text data, absence of a standardized multi-task benchmark, and a critical fragility where standard encoder-LLM architectures suffer severe performance collapse in low-SNR environments (SNR < 0 dB) due to noise-induced feature homogenization. Prior EM works rely on task-specific or pipelined architectures that limit generalization, and no end-to-end multimodal framework addresses the low-SNR robustness challenge at the feature level.

## Input / Output

The model takes raw EM IQ signals (standardized to 20 MHz sampling rate, 1024 samples, SNR ranging from −20 to 20 dB) alongside a natural-language instruction as input. For perception tasks it produces a single-choice answer selected from five options (including an 'Unable to answer' confidence option); for reasoning tasks it generates open-ended strategic text such as jamming or anti-jamming plans. All signals are encoded by a pretrained EMind encoder into high-dimensional latent vectors, projected through a 2-layer MLP into the LLM's embedding space, and concatenated with tokenized text before autoregressive generation by Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507.

## Method

MERLIN employs a two-stage training paradigm. Stage 1 performs multi-task instruction-tuning on the 134K EM-134K dataset with standard next-token prediction loss, jointly optimizing the EMind encoder, MLP projector, and Qwen3-4B LLM. Stage 2 introduces a knowledge-distillation framework where a frozen teacher (fed high-SNR signals) guides a trainable student (fed low-SNR counterparts) via a composite loss: a cross-entropy task loss, a feature-level distillation loss that uses a Denoising Subspace Module (DSM) to project the student's noisy embedding into an approximately orthogonal signal subspace before aligning it with the teacher's clean embedding, and a temperature-scaled KL-divergence logit-level loss aligning the two LLM output distributions. The key insight motivating this design is that low-SNR degradation manifests as feature collapse—embeddings become indistinguishable across classes—and that linearly interpolating noisy embeddings toward their clean counterparts recovers LLM performance, confirming that the fix must operate in the feature space rather than merely by increasing low-SNR training data.

## Results

On EM-Bench, MERLIN achieves a 78.27% average accuracy across all perception tasks, substantially outperforming the best LLM baseline (Claude-4-Sonnet at 32.35%) and the discriminative EMind encoder. On individual tasks, MERLIN reaches 44.97% on modulation classification (MOD), 87.37% on protocol identification (PI), 86.00% on communication jamming recognition (CJR), and 83.04% on radar jamming recognition (RJR). On reasoning tasks, MERLIN is the only model producing coherent strategies, achieving Rouge-L scores of 0.45 (Anti-CJ), 0.42 (RJS), 0.40 (CLS), and 0.29 (Anti-RJ), while all LLM baselines score near zero (≤0.03).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MERLIN outperforms all five LLM baselines (GPT-5, Claude-4-Sonnet, DeepSeek-v3.2-exp, Qwen3-Next-80b-A3B, Gemini-2.5-Pro) by a wide margin on perception tasks, achieving 78.27% average accuracy versus 23.20–32.35% for the best LLMs. Against the discriminative EMind encoder, MERLIN surpasses it on MOD (44.97% vs. 23.23%), PI (87.37% vs. 80.97%), CJR (86.00% vs. 34.83%), and RJR (83.04% vs. 32.84%), though EMind retains an edge on individual PE sub-tasks like NP (86.40% vs. 92.40% — actually MERLIN wins here too) and PRF (55.87% vs. 82.77%). On reasoning tasks, the gap is most dramatic: MERLIN produces meaningful strategy outputs (Rouge-L 0.29–0.45) while every LLM baseline scores at or near zero, indicating that textualized signal inputs are fundamentally insufficient for strategic reasoning. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that the two-stage distillation contributes +18.9 points over the Stage-1 baseline on low-SNR performance (59.7% → 65.1%) and +6.8 points overall (71.8% → 78.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior EM-LLM efforts (RadioLLM, Spectrum-LLM, WirelessLLM) that adopt pipelined or task-specific architectures, instead adopting the native end-to-end MLLM paradigm proven successful in vision (LLaVA, BLIP-2). It repurposes the EMind foundation model as a signal encoder and the Qwen3-4B LLM as the reasoning backbone, bridging them with a lightweight MLP projector. The low-SNR robustness stage draws conceptually from knowledge distillation in LLMs and noise-robust feature learning in image/EEG domains, but introduces the Denoising Subspace Module (DSM) as a novel mechanism to project noisy student embeddings into a signal-only subspace before alignment with a frozen teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing both EM-134K (134K signal-text pairs) and EM-Bench (4,200 expert-validated QA pairs across 14 sub-tasks), this paper provides the first standardized infrastructure for evaluating EM MLLMs, which was previously absent. The finding that low-SNR degradation is a feature-collapse problem solvable at the representation level (not the data level) offers a transferable diagnostic for other noisy-signal MLLM applications. Open questions include whether the DSM generalizes beyond the 1024-sample, 20 MHz setting, how performance scales with larger LLM backbones, and whether the framework extends to real-time or streaming EM signal processing.
