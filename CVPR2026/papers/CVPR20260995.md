## Problem

Existing zero-shot embodied navigation methods that build explicit 3D scene graphs compress rich visual observations into text-only relational edges, causing irreversible loss of visual evidence, expensive MLLM-based construction, and constrained vocabularies. Additionally, prior methods ignore the 'last-mile' problem: correctly localizing a target does not guarantee selecting a viewpoint with sufficient visibility for successful final approach.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260995/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives an RGB-D frame and its own pose, along with a target specification that can be a category label, natural language description, or reference image. The system incrementally builds a scene graph and queries a VLM (GPT-4o) using a pruned key subgraph averaging only ~4 images per query, producing an exploration decision (target or frontier) and, upon target localization, a visibility-optimized navigation viewpoint. The agent executes camera rotations or ego-motion actions until reaching within a benchmark-specific success distance (0.25 m for GOAT-Bench, 1.0 m for HM3D-ObjNav).

## Method

The core contribution is the Multi-modal 3D Scene Graph (M3DSG), which replaces traditional text-only relational edges with sets of RGB-D images in which object pairs co-occur, eliminating costly VLM queries for relation inference while preserving visual context. On top of M3DSG, MSGNav integrates four modules: (1) Key Subgraph Selection (KSS) via a Compress-Focus-Prune pipeline with a greedy set-cover algorithm that reduces VLM token cost by over 95%; (2) Adaptive Vocabulary Update (AVU) that dynamically expands the object vocabulary using VLM-generated descriptions from preserved images, enabling open-vocabulary generalization beyond preset taxonomies; (3) Closed-Loop Reasoning (CLR) that stores past exploration decisions in a decision memory to inform subsequent VLM queries; and (4) Visibility-based Viewpoint Decision (VVD) that uniformly samples candidate viewpoints around the localized target at multiple radii, computes a visibility score by ray-casting against the scene point cloud to measure occlusion, and selects the viewpoint maximizing unobstructed line-of-sight to the target.

## Results

On the GOAT-Bench (Val Unseen split), MSGNav achieves 52.0% SR and 29.6% SPL, surpassing the previous best training-based method MTU3D by 4.8% SR and 2.1% SPL, and outperforming the best training-free method TANGO by 19.9% SR and 13.1% SPL. On HM3D-ObjNav, MSGNav reaches 74.1% SR and 33.4% SPL, edging out the prior state-of-the-art WMNav by 1.9% SR while matching its SPL (33.4 vs. 33.3). The ablation shows M3DSG alone contributes +15.0% SR over the 3D-Mem baseline, and the VVD module adds a further +12.5% SR by resolving the last-mile viewpoint problem.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GOAT-Bench, MSGNav (52.0% SR) decisively outperforms all training-free baselines: TANGO (32.1%), 3D-Mem (28.8%), DyNaVLM (25.5%), and VLMnav (20.1%), with the largest gap over TANGO at +19.9% SR. It also surpasses the strongest training-based method MTU3D (47.2% SR) by 4.8% SR and 2.1% SPL, demonstrating that the multi-modal scene graph approach generalizes better than learned policies on unseen scenes. On HM3D-ObjNav, MSGNav (74.1% SR, 33.4% SPL) narrowly beats WMNav (72.2% SR, 33.3% SPL) by 1.9% SR while matching SPL, and substantially outperforms DORAEMON (66.5% SR) and VLFM (62.6% SR). The SPL parity with WMNav is attributed to the VVD module prioritizing visibility over shortest path. The ablation confirms that M3DSG's image edges contribute +3.8% SR over traditional text-edge Concept-graphs, with the largest gains on Language (+4.4%) and Image (+6.8%) goal types.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSGNav builds directly on 3D-Mem's observation that raw images are valuable for embodied reasoning, but departs fundamentally from the traditional 3D scene graph paradigm (SG-Nav, ConceptGraphs, Graph2Nav) by replacing text-only relational edges with dynamically assigned images (M3DSG). The object representation and merging pipeline follows ConceptGraphs, while the overall zero-shot navigation architecture with VLM-driven exploration is in the lineage of 3D-Mem and DyNaVLM. The paper's key conceptual departure is the claim that textual relation labels ('top', 'beside') are an irreversible loss of visual evidence, and that image edges simultaneously solve the expensive-construction, visual-deficiency, and constrained-vocabulary problems of prior scene-graph methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'last-mile' problem — that correctly localizing a target does not guarantee a usable navigation viewpoint — is a useful reframing that explains a large fraction of failures in existing zero-shot methods (33.9% → 52.0% SR from VVD alone). The M3DSG image-edge representation is a clean architectural idea that could transfer to other scene-graph-based reasoning tasks beyond navigation. However, the method's heavy reliance on GPT-4o API calls for every reasoning step limits real-time deployment, and the authors acknowledge that the last-mile problem is mitigated but not fully resolved, leaving open whether active perception or RL-based viewpoint optimization could close the remaining gap.
