## Problem

Existing registration-based infrared-visible image fusion methods rely on computationally intensive pre-alignment operations, depend on synthetic deformations for supervision, and attempt to align all information globally. The paper identifies that spatial misregistration only affects modality-shared regions while modality-specific regions remain unchanged, yet no prior method exploits this visual prior to perform targeted, post-fusion correction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260103/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FusionRegister takes a visible image, an infrared image, and their fused result from any backbone as input, operating on 260×260 patches during training and full-resolution images at test time. It outputs a registered fused image along with intermediate misregistration probability maps and hierarchical deformation fields at multiple scales. Crucially, the method is designed as a plug-and-play post-registration module that consumes the fused image as a visual prior, requiring no modification to the upstream fusion network.

## Method

The framework follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) a MIMO-UNet-inspired hierarchical encoder extracts multi-scale features from the fused, visible, and infrared inputs to estimate a misregistration probability map and a coarse-to-fine deformation field; (2) bidirectional warping applies the deformation field symmetrically (forward and reverse) only within regions flagged by the probability map, avoiding tearing and over-compensation in well-aligned areas; (3) a Modality Retainment Block (MRB) uses a correlation layer to measure local geometric correspondence, a gMLP for efficient long-range spatial modeling, and dual modality-specific attention (channel-based for visible, spatial-based for infrared) to recover texture attenuated by warping. Training is supervised with a composite loss combining DoG edge alignment, global pixel consistency, FFT frequency preservation, and Sobel-based detail loss masked by the misregistration map, all computed hierarchically across scales.

## Results

On the three evaluation datasets (MSRS, M³FD, LLVIP), FusionRegister-enhanced methods achieve the highest registration accuracy across all settings. The best FR variant (TDFusion+FR) reaches 86.7 IoU on MSRS, 81.0 on M³FD, and 82.9 on LLVIP, while MMDR+FR attains 86.5/81.6/86.0 respectively. Across all five fusion backbones, FR delivers an average ~5% IoU improvement over the unregistered versions. In terms of efficiency, FR adds only 2.94M parameters and 0.019 s inference overhead on MSRS, making it the second-fastest method tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FusionRegister outperforms all six registration-based fusion baselines by a substantial margin on every dataset. Against the strongest baselines IMF (78.7/77.2/81.3 IoU) and SemLA (78.7/75.0/81.0 IoU), the best FR variants (MMDR+FR at 86.5/81.6/86.0, TDFusion+FR at 86.7/81.0/82.9) gain roughly 8–16 IoU points depending on the dataset. The gap is even larger versus weaker baselines: MURF (58.3/61.3/67.3) and CAP (59.1/66.7/75.5) trail FR methods by 25+ IoU on MSRS. C²RF (70.5/69.4/75.4) and IVFWSR (64.6/70.7/75.6) are also clearly surpassed. Critically, the paper reports that most existing methods (SemLA, IMF, MulFS-CAP) collapse or generalize poorly on deformation-free inputs and unseen datasets, whereas FusionRegister maintains stable post-registration performance across all three test sets without requiring synthetic deformation supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'register-then-fuse' paradigm in cross-modal registration-based fusion (MURF, IVFWSR, SemLA, MulFS-CAP, IMF, C²RF), which performs global or feature-level alignment as a preprocessing step before fusion. Instead, FusionRegister inverts the pipeline to 'fuse-then-register,' using the fused output itself as a visual prior to localize and correct only the misregistered (modality-shared) regions. Architecturally, it draws on MIMO-UNet-style hierarchical processing, correlation layers from optical flow, and gMLP for efficient long-range modeling, but the core conceptual contribution is the post-registration framing and the observation that misregistration manifests only in modality-shared regions, not modality-specific ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The post-registration paradigm offers a practical, plug-and-play solution that preserves the intrinsic fusion quality of any backbone while correcting alignment, which is valuable for real-world multi-sensor systems where perfect registration is unattainable. The key limitation is the assumption of coarse pre-registration (sensors in close proximity); the authors explicitly acknowledge that significant inter-modal viewpoint discrepancy would degrade performance, leaving this as an open problem. The SAM-based segmentation evaluation is a useful but somewhat ad hoc metric for registration accuracy in the absence of ground-truth aligned pairs, and whether this evaluation protocol generalizes beyond IVIF remains to be seen.
