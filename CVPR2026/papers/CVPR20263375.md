## Problem

When extending contrastive learning beyond two modalities (M>2), in-batch negatives naturally stratify by how many non-anchor modalities they share with the positive tuple, yet existing methods either treat all negatives uniformly or only exploit fully unmatched pairs. This ignores a structured easy-to-hard hierarchy that could guide the learning process more effectively.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263375/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes M modality-specific inputs per sample (e.g., frontal CXR images, radiology report text, 50-d lab vectors; or eye-gaze, head-pose, and three IMU sensor streams) and produces a single shared embedding per modality via modality-specific encoders (ResNet, Transformer, or MLP) followed by linear projections. Evaluation is zero-shot two-to-one retrieval (e.g., Text⊗Table→Image) over a fixed 10-way candidate pool, scored by the multilinear inner product τ⟨q⁽¹⁾⊙q⁽²⁾, c⟩, with no task-specific heads or fine-tuning at test time.

## Method

Easy2Hard augments the TC/MIP contrastive objective (inherited from Symile) by partitioning in-batch negatives per anchor into partially unmatched (easy) and fully unmatched (hard) sets based on cross-modal overlap count, constructed in O(B) via axis-wise random permutations of non-anchor indices. A sigmoid-based curriculum gate w(t)=σ(k(t−t_m)) smoothly shifts the loss weight from the easy set to the hard set over training, with the generalization to M>3 levels expressed as differences of consecutive sigmoids forming a partition of unity. The encoders, MIP scoring, and batch composition remain unchanged from standard contrastive training; only the relative weighting between structured negative subsets is varied. Ablations confirm that the sigmoid schedule substantially outperforms a linear alternative, and that the slope k is more sensitive than the midpoint t_m.

## Results

On zero-shot two-to-one retrieval (10-way candidate pool, Acc@1), Easy2Hard achieves 0.503 on EH-MIMIC, 0.462 on Symile-MIMIC, 0.573 on Channel, and 0.421 on MM-IMDb, consistently outperforming both Symile and CLIP-Pairwise across all four trimodal datasets. In the 5-modal HoloAssist feasibility study, Easy2Hard reaches 0.882 Acc@1, substantially exceeding the ImageBind-like pivot baseline (0.439) and Symile (0.833). Gains over Symile range from +0.006 (Channel) to +0.028 (Symile-MIMIC), while gains over CLIP-Pairwise are larger, up to +0.067 on Symile-MIMIC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Easy2Hard outperforms Symile (the closest TC/MIP-based baseline) on all four trimodal datasets, with margins of +0.017 on MM-IMDb, +0.006 on Channel, +0.028 on Symile-MIMIC, and +0.026 on EH-MIMIC; the largest relative gains appear on the clinical datasets. Against CLIP-Pairwise, the advantage is even more pronounced, reaching +0.067 on Symile-MIMIC and +0.047 on EH-MIMIC, confirming that structured MIP scoring plus fine-grained negative partitioning substantially exceeds simple pairwise contrastive alignment. On the 5-modal HoloAssist study, Easy2Hard (0.882) beats the ImageBind-like pivot baseline (0.439) by a wide margin of +0.443, suggesting that explicit negative structuring becomes increasingly beneficial as modality count grows. All reported differences exceed the corresponding bootstrap standard errors, and the consistent ordering Easy2Hard > Symile > CLIP-Pairwise holds across every dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Symile's TC/MIP scoring framework for model-agnostic multimodal contrastive learning, retaining the same multilinear inner-product objective and encoder architecture. It departs from Symile (and from pairwise baselines like CLIP and ImageBind) by partitioning in-batch negatives into partially unmatched and fully unmatched sets based on cross-modal overlap, then reweighting their contributions via a sigmoid curriculum that shifts emphasis from easy to hard contrasts over training. The approach is positioned as complementary to the TC/MIP line: the scoring and encoders are unchanged, but the negative set gains explicit difficulty structure that prior methods treat uniformly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The structured negative partitioning with a smooth curriculum offers a lightweight, O(B)-constructible modification to multimodal contrastive training that consistently improves zero-shot retrieval across clinical, news, and movie domains, and extends to a 5-modal sensor setting. However, the absolute gains over Symile are modest (0.006–0.028 Acc@1 on trimodal datasets), the evaluation uses small candidate pools (10-way) and limited bootstrap resamples (B=10), and the curriculum hyperparameters (slope k, midpoint t_m) require per-dataset tuning. Open questions include whether the structured negative hierarchy provides larger benefits at scale (larger batches, more diverse data), whether the sigmoid schedule generalizes to more than two difficulty levels in practice, and whether the approach transfers to generative or downstream-fine-tuning settings beyond zero-shot retrieval.
