## Problem

End-to-end rPPG models suffer severe performance degradation under domain shifts in illumination, camera sensors, skin tone, and motion, yet no principled multi-source domain generalization framework exists for fully end-to-end physiological signal estimation. Existing rPPG generalization efforts either depend on handcrafted STMap preprocessing or operate in single-source settings, and lack a theoretical grounding for aligning diverse source-domain representations in a shared latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263848/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FLOW is a plug-and-play module that operates on intermediate feature tensors (of arbitrary shape, e.g., B×C×H×W or B×C×T×H×W) extracted by a shared video backbone, rather than on raw pixels. It produces aligned temporal feature sequences (B×T×C) that feed into a task head regressing heart rate or BVP signals. During training, ground-truth heart rate from an auxiliary head guides the transport cost; at inference, the learned transport plan is applied directly without any target-domain data or ground-truth signals.

## Method

FLOW interprets inter-domain variation as a structured optimal transport problem and aligns temporal features via a Prototype-based Cross-Temporal Optimal Transport (PCOT) module that softly matches each temporal step to a learnable prototype bank using entropic OT (solved with Sinkhorn iterations). The transport cost combines a learnable-weighted feature distance with a physiological coherence term penalizing heart-rate inconsistency between features and prototypes, and aligned representations are obtained by barycentric projection onto the prototype manifold. A lightweight Temporal Refinement Module (TRM) precedes PCOT, using stacked depthwise-separable 1D convolutions to unify heterogeneous spatiotemporal features into a consistent temporal form and suppress high-frequency motion artifacts. Two regularization terms—source-consistency (minimizing variance of prototype-assignment histograms across domains) and identity-preservation (bounding the L2 distance between original and aligned features)—stabilize the transport plan and prevent over-smoothing. The paper further derives a multi-source generalization bound under conditional OT discrepancy, formally linking alignment quality to prediction risk on unseen domains.

## Results

On the multi-source domain generalization benchmark (Others→U/P/B/M), FLOW achieves an average MAE of 6.84 bpm, RMSE of 10.75 bpm, and Pearson R of 0.70, outperforming the best DG baseline MMD (MAE 9.35, RMSE 13.28, R 0.57) by 26% in MAE and 0.13 in correlation. On the most challenging target BUAA-MIHR, FLOW reaches MAE 2.23 and R 0.97, surpassing MMD (MAE 2.80, R 0.95) and Coral (MAE 3.06, R 0.95). In limited-source settings (two source domains), FLOW maintains an average MAE of 8.65 on MMPD and 2.56 on BUAA-MIHR, consistently outperforming all baselines by 1.1–2.7 bpm in MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOW outperforms all 12 baselines on every target domain and every metric. Against the strongest DG baselines, FLOW reduces average MAE from 9.35 (MMD) and 9.97 (Coral) to 6.84, a 26–31% improvement, while raising average Pearson R from 0.57/0.55 to 0.70. Among traditional signal-processing methods, POS is the best (avg MAE 8.64) but still trails FLOW by 1.8 bpm; Green and CHROM are substantially worse (14.10 and 9.69 respectively). Deep-learning rPPG models without explicit DG (PhysNet, PhysFormer, EfficientPhys, RhythmFormer, NEST, Greip) show severe cross-domain instability—PhysNet even yields negative correlation on PURE (R = −0.15)—and all are outperformed by FLOW by 2–11 bpm in MAE. The shared backbone Baseline [48] (avg MAE 11.04) confirms that the gains come from FLOW's OT alignment and temporal refinement rather than architecture choice, as FLOW on the same backbone drops MAE to 6.84.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Optimal Transport framework for domain adaptation (Courty et al., 2017) from image classification to the temporal regression setting of end-to-end rPPG, a combination the authors identify as entirely unexplored. It departs from prior rPPG generalization efforts that rely on STMap preprocessing or single-source domain adaptation by formulating multi-source alignment as a prototype-guided entropic OT problem over temporal features. The Temporal Refinement Module draws on depthwise-separable convolutions for lightweight temporal filtering, while the PCOT module introduces a novel prototype-bank mechanism that couples feature similarity with physiological (heart-rate) coherence in the transport cost, distinguishing it from purely geometric OT formulations used in classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Domain generalization for end-to-end rPPG is a critical bottleneck for real-world deployment, and this paper provides the first principled OT-based solution with a derived generalization bound, offering both algorithmic and theoretical grounding. The plug-and-play design (validated across four backbones) makes it immediately applicable to existing rPPG pipelines without architectural redesign. However, the DG baselines (CORAL, MMD) are notably dated, and the theoretical bound is stated but not empirically stress-tested; open questions include scalability to more than four source domains, robustness to truly unconstrained in-the-wild conditions, and whether the prototype count and entropy regularization transfer to other physiological signals (respiration, SpO2).
