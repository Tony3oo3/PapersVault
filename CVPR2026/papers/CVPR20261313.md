## Problem

Multi-step video diffusion inference is computationally expensive, and while pyramidal models (which process higher noise at lower resolutions) can reduce this cost, existing open-source pyramidal video models were trained from scratch and underperform state-of-the-art systems in visual quality. The paper asks whether a pretrained SOTA video diffusion model can be converted into a pyramidal one via low-cost finetuning without quality degradation, and how step distillation can be adapted to the pyramidal setting for further efficiency gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261313/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as input and generates an 81-frame video at 448×832 spatial resolution in the VAE latent space of Wan2.1. Inference proceeds through three spatiotemporal stages at resolutions 81×448×832, 41×224×416, and 21×112×208, with a configurable per-stage step schedule (e.g., 2-2-1 for few-step mode). The spatial resolution is reduced from the default 480×832 to 448×832 to ensure divisibility by 64 for the patchification layer at the lowest stage.

## Method

Starting from the pretrained Wan2.1-1.3B DiT, the paper decomposes the forward and backward diffusion into three spatiotemporal stages using the PyramidalFlow framework, where nearest-neighbor upsampling and 3D average pooling transition between stages (generalized theoretically to arbitrary orthogonal-transform-based resampling, e.g., Haar wavelets). The model is finetuned for only 5K iterations on 2 H100 GPUs with a combined pyramidal flow matching loss and a distillation loss that aligns the student's stage-wise predictions with the teacher's full-resolution outputs. For few-step inference, the paper adapts both DMD (with original and pyramidal teachers) and adversarial distillation to the pyramidal setting, deriving a modified re-noising procedure and a new pyramidal DMD gradient that accounts for stage-wise boundary samples. The paper also demonstrates for the first time that Pyramidal Patchification Flow (PPF), an alternative that varies patchifier kernel size instead of resolution, can be successfully distilled for few-step video generation.

## Results

PyramidalWan (20-20-10 schedule) achieves a VBench total score of 82.83, matching or exceeding the original Wan2.1-1.3B at 50 steps (82.49) while reducing FLOPs by ~4.5×. It also attains the highest Semantic score (80.70) among all evaluated models. Among few-step pyramidal variants using the 2-2-1 schedule, PyramidalWan-DMD-OT reaches VBench total 82.86 and VBench-2.0 total 55.36, while PyramidalWan-DMD-PT* scores 82.72 and 51.75 respectively. A user study (700 responses) confirms that the 2-2-1 pyramidal model is perceived as on par with both 50-step Wan and 2-step Wan-DMD (p < 0.001 for rejecting the null hypothesis of no preference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original Wan2.1-1.3B diffusion model at 50 and 25 steps. PyramidalWan (20-20-10) matches the 50-step baseline on VBench total (82.83 vs. 82.49) and surpasses it on Semantic (80.70 vs. 78.57), while using roughly 4.5× fewer FLOPs; however, it trails on VBench-2.0 total (54.93 vs. 56.02), particularly in Creativity (44.64 vs. 48.73) and Controllability (28.39 vs. 33.96). Among few-step models, the non-pyramidal Wan-DMD at 2 steps remains the strongest on VBench-2.0 total (56.67) and Physics (55.72), but cannot perform single-step generation at the target resolution. The pyramidal few-step models (2-2-1 schedule) close this gap: PyramidalWan-DMD-OT achieves VBench total 82.86 (vs. 83.28 for Wan-DMD 2-step) and the best Physics score among pyramidal variants (56.78), while PyramidalWan-DMD-PT* is selected for the user study based on visual appeal despite a lower VBench-2.0 score (51.75). In the user study, participants showed no statistically significant preference for either the 50-step Wan or 2-step Wan-DMD over the pyramidal model (p < 0.001 for rejecting the null), confirming perceptual parity despite the quantitative VBench-2.0 gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the PyramidalFlow framework (Jin et al., ICLR 2025), which formalized multi-resolution flow matching for video generation, and on the Wan2.1-1.3B pretrained video diffusion transformer as the base architecture. The key departure from prior pyramidal video models—which were trained from scratch and underperformed SOTA systems—is demonstrating that a strong pretrained model can be converted to pyramidal form via low-cost finetuning (5K iterations on 2 GPUs) without quality loss. The paper also adapts DMD (Yin et al., NeurIPS 2024) and adversarial distillation to the pyramidal setting, and extends PyramidalFlow's stage-transition operations from simple average-pooling/nearest-neighbor to arbitrary orthogonal-transform-based upsampling (e.g., Haar wavelets).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4.5× FLOPs reduction (20-20-10 schedule) or up to ~47× reduction (1-1-1 pyramidal step distillation) while maintaining near-original visual quality, making few-step video generation viable on resource-constrained hardware. However, VBench-2.0 reveals degradation in Creativity and Controllability, and the DMD-OT variant produces oversaturated, cartoon-like outputs, indicating the quality gap is not fully closed. An open question is why the theoretically weaker simplified DMD-PT* (setting β₂=γ₂=0) consistently outperforms the full pyramidal DMD gradient, and whether PPF-based approaches can be made to work for video diffusion finetuning rather than only for distillation.
