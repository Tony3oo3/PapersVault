## Problem

Open-source joint audio-video generation models struggle with robust audio-visual synchronization, particularly for human speech and complex ambient scenes. The paper identifies three root causes: Correspondence Drift (unstable learning when both modalities are concurrently noisy), architectural conflation of fine-grained temporal alignment with global style consistency, and the inability of standard Classifier-Free Guidance to amplify cross-modal correspondence.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a reference image, a descriptive text prompt, a reference audio clip for timbre, and a speech transcript as inputs, and jointly generates a synchronized video-audio pair. The video branch is initialized from Wan2.2 5B, while the audio branch is a symmetric MM-DiT conditioned on separate speech-encoder (F5-TTS) and T5 prompt embeddings. The framework supports three generation paradigms—joint synthesis, audio-driven video, and video-driven audio—by toggling which modality's timestep is set to zero.

## Method

Harmony introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, Cross-Task Synergy training co-optimizes the joint generation loss with audio-driven video and video-driven audio losses, using clean (timestep-0) latents from one modality to provide stable alignment gradients that counteract Correspondence Drift. Second, a Global-Local Decoupled Interaction Module separates synchronization into a RoPE-Aligned Frame-wise Attention (which rescales positional indices to unify mismatched video/audio sampling rates for precise local temporal alignment) and a Global Style Alignment block (which modulates the reference audio latent with global video context via cross-attention for holistic consistency). Third, SyncCFG redefines the negative guidance anchor as a muted-audio or static-video prediction, so the guidance vector explicitly isolates and amplifies the audio-driven visual dynamics or motion-driven sound components during inference.

## Results

On Harmony-Bench (averaged across ambient sound-video, speech-video, and complex scene subsets), Harmony achieves the best audio-visual synchronization scores: Sync-C of 5.61 (vs. Ovi's 4.04 and UniVerse-1's 0.97) and Sync-D of 7.53 (vs. Ovi's 9.62). It also leads in video quality (AQ 0.59, DD 0.36, ID 0.91) and audio fidelity (PQ 6.39, PC 2.05, WER 0.15). The ablation study confirms SyncCFG provides the largest single gain, boosting Sync-C from 5.09 to 6.51 on the speech subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Harmony outperforms all four baselines on every audio-visual synchronization metric. Against the closest competitor Ovi [34], Harmony improves Sync-C from 4.04 to 5.61 (+39%) and reduces Sync-D from 9.62 to 7.53 (−22%), while also achieving lower WER (0.15 vs. 0.49) and higher PQ (6.39 vs. 6.19). UniVerse-1 [48] supports both speech and ambient sounds but exhibits severe misalignment (Sync-C 0.97, Sync-D 10.71), making it the weakest among speech-capable models. JavisDiT [32] and MM-Diffusion [44] cannot generate human speech at all and show the poorest synchronization scores (Sync-C 0.89 and N/A, respectively). Harmony also leads on video quality metrics (AQ, DD, ID) and audio perceptual quality (PQ, PC, CE), indicating that the synchronization gains do not come at the cost of unimodal fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

Harmony builds on the Wan2.2 video diffusion backbone and MMAudio's audio VAE, but departs sharply from prior joint audio-video generation methods (MM-Diffusion, JavisDiT, UniVerse-1, Ovi) by diagnosing three specific methodological failures—Correspondence Drift, monolithic cross-attention conflating local timing with global style, and CFG's blindness to cross-modal alignment. Rather than proposing yet another architectural variant, the paper introduces a multi-task training paradigm (Cross-Task Synergy) that borrows stable supervisory signals from unidirectional audio-driven and video-driven tasks, and a decoupled interaction module that separates RoPE-aligned frame-wise attention from a global style pathway. The SyncCFG inference technique repurposes the negative-condition mechanism of Classifier-Free Guidance, which prior work used only for text adherence, into an explicit cross-modal alignment amplifier.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear, mechanistic diagnosis of why open-source joint audio-video models fail at synchronization, moving the field beyond trial-and-error architecture search. The Cross-Task Synergy training strategy and SyncCFG are practical, modular techniques that other joint-generation frameworks could adopt without wholesale redesign. Open questions remain around scalability to longer sequences, broader audio categories (e.g., polyphonic music, multi-speaker dialogue), and whether the alignment gains persist under distribution shift. The introduction of Harmony-Bench with its three-tier complexity structure also gives the community a more structured evaluation protocol than prior ad-hoc comparisons.
