## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient continual learning methods for VLMs assign task-specific information to input tokens uniformly at the sample level, ignoring that different tokens require different degrees of adjustment. Moreover, the attention mechanism used in prefix-tuning inherently projects task-relevant and task-irrelevant tokens closer together in feature space, making fine-grained token-level weighting difficult and degrading knowledge transfer across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261058/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and text prompts (manually crafted class-name templates fed to the CLIP text encoder) as input, and outputs class labels. It is evaluated under two continual learning protocols: MTIL, where the task ID is available at inference for task-specific classification, and ODCL-CIL, where the model must classify over all 1201 learned classes across 11 domains without task ID information, using a likelihood-based task-identification strategy at test time.

## Method

DPW introduces three components that jointly enable token-level weighting of prefixes and adapters. First, Reparameterized Prefix Attention (RePA) replaces the conventional query-key dot product with a single learnable affine transformation (XW_G + B_G) applied directly to input tokens, bypassing the pretrained attention projections that blur task-relevant distinctions. Second, Conditional Activation (CondAct) replaces softmax with a two-step process: conditional normalization caps the total prefix weight per token at one (preserving zero-shot performance), and conditional filtering removes prefixes whose scores fall below a task-specific likelihood-derived cutoff. Third, the Residual Weighting Mechanism (RWM) computes adapter weights as the excess of prefix scores beyond the cap, so LoRA adapters activate only for tokens that demand additional adaptation beyond what prefixes provide, creating a natural aggressive/conservative synergy between the two modules.

## Results

On the MTIL benchmark (Order I), the full DPW model achieves 70.4% Transfer, 79.3% Average, and 88.3% Last accuracy, outperforming all baselines by 1.1–2.3 points on Transfer and 2.3–4.7 points on Last. The parameter-efficient variant (Ours†, 4.6M trainable params) still reaches 70.0% Transfer and 87.6% Last, surpassing full fine-tuning methods (ZSCL, GIFT) that use 149.6M parameters and require extra synthetic data. On the more challenging ODCL-CIL benchmark (no task IDs at inference), DPW achieves 70.4% Transfer, 78.6% Average, and 86.6% Last, again setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPW outperforms all six baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Against the closest PEFT competitor DIKI [32] (1.8M params), DPW gains +1.7 Transfer, +3.0 Avg, and +3.2 Last on MTIL, and +1.6 Transfer, +4.9 Avg, and +6.7 Last on ODCL-CIL. Against MoE-Adapter [44] (59.6M params), the gains are +1.5 Transfer, +2.6 Avg, and +3.3 Last on MTIL. Against full fine-tuning baselines ZSCL [48] and GIFT [39] (both 149.6M params with extra synthetic data), DPW achieves +2.3 and +1.1 Transfer respectively while using only 30.8M trainable parameters and no auxiliary data. On ODCL-CIL, DPW surpasses the prompt-based methods CoLeCLIP [18] by +1.6 Transfer and +6.9 Last, and DPeCLIP [22] by +1.3 Transfer and +2.0 Last. Notably, even the lightweight Ours† variant (4.6M params) beats DIKI (1.8M) by +1.3 Transfer and +2.5 Last on MTIL, confirming that the dynamic weighting mechanism—not raw parameter count—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prefix-tuning framework for continual learning of VLMs, building most directly on DIKI (Tang et al., ECCV 2024), which introduced cross-attention-based prefix weighting with softmax normalization. The key departure is a reparameterization insight: the authors show that the standard query-key dot product in prefix-tuning can be collapsed into a single affine transformation (RePA) that better captures token-level task relevance, and that the pretrained attention projections actually *harm* task discrimination by collapsing task-relevant and task-irrelevant tokens in feature space. The adapter component follows HydraLoRA's asymmetric LoRA structure, but the novel contribution is the Residual Weighting Mechanism (RWM), which gates adapter outputs using the residual of prefix weights rather than a separate router, creating a tight coupling between the two PEFT components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated limitation in PEFT-based CL: existing methods apply uniform sample-level weighting, ignoring that different tokens within an image require different amounts of task-specific adaptation. The empirical finding that pretrained query projections increase cosine similarity between task-relevant and task-irrelevant tokens (0.02 → 0.47) provides a mechanistic explanation for why attention-based routing in prefix-tuning is suboptimal, which could inform future prompt-based methods beyond CL. However, the evaluation is limited to CLIP-based VLMs on two benchmarks (MTIL, ODCL-CIL) with 11 datasets, and it remains open whether the RePA insight generalizes to other backbone architectures or to non-CL settings where the task-relevance signal is less structured.
