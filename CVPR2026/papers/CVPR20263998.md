## Problem

Existing long-tailed recognition methods (resampling, reweighting, decoupled learning) improve overall accuracy but leave worst-class test accuracy substantially below both overall accuracy and worst-class training accuracy, revealing a generalization failure specific to the most underrepresented categories. The spectral structure of inter-class confusion—a critical indicator of model bias—remains largely unexplored as a lever for controlling this worst-class generalization gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263998/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100-LT, 224×224 for ImageNet-LT and iNaturalist) and outputs a K-way class label, identical to any supervised classifier. What is unusual is that during training, the model additionally computes a K×K confusion matrix over each mini-batch (using a differentiable surrogate with a margin gate and soft-argmax), which is then exponentially smoothed across iterations to form the regularization signal—no changes to the inference-time I/O pipeline are required.

## Method

CAR derives a PAC-Bayesian upper bound showing that the worst-class error is controlled by the spectral norm of a frequency-weighted empirical confusion matrix plus a model-complexity term, then directly minimizes ‖C̃_{S,γ}^f Λ‖₂ as a training regularizer. To make this tractable, two components are introduced: (i) a Differentiable Confusion Matrix Surrogate that replaces the non-differentiable margin indicators with a sigmoid margin gate multiplied by a soft-argmax over non-ground-truth classes, and (ii) an EMA-based Confusion Estimator that maintains a low-variance running average of the batch-level surrogate (only the current batch term carries gradients). The final objective is standard cross-entropy plus α·‖(Ĉ_t Λ)‖₂, where Λ = diag((m_j + r₀)^{-1/2}) up-weights rare classes. This regularizer is backbone-agnostic and composes additively with data-augmentation strategies such as ConCutMix.

## Results

On ImageNet-LT (IF=100), CAR achieves 57.48% overall top-1 accuracy with ViT-Small, and CAR+ConCutMix reaches 60.07%, surpassing the previous SOTA LOS (56.20%) by 3.87%. On CIFAR100-LT, CAR+ConCutMix attains 55.68% overall (vs. LOS 50.85%, +4.83%), and on iNaturalist it reaches 73.38% (vs. LOS 71.01%, +2.37%). Tail accuracy gains are particularly pronounced: CAR+ConCutMix improves tail accuracy by 3.28%–7.98% over LOS across the three datasets (e.g., 38.07% vs. 32.73% on ImageNet-LT). Worst-class test accuracy jumps from ≤10% (best baseline) to 18–22% with CAR+ConCutMix on ImageNet-LT and CIFAR100-LT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAR outperforms all 13 baselines on overall top-1 accuracy across ImageNet-LT, CIFAR100-LT, and iNaturalist under the training-from-scratch setting. The closest competitor, LOS (ICLR 2025), is surpassed by CAR alone by 1.28% (ImageNet-LT), 1.00% (CIFAR100-LT), and 0.55% (iNaturalist) in overall accuracy; with ConCutMix, the margin widens to 3.87%, 4.83%, and 2.37% respectively. On tail accuracy, CAR alone beats the best baseline (LOS at 32.73% on ImageNet-LT) by 3.04%, and CAR+ConCutMix extends this to 5.34%. Among augmentation-based methods, SAFA and GML are the strongest standalone baselines (55.29% and 55.24% on ImageNet-LT), but both are outperformed by CAR by over 2%. In the fine-tuning setting (Table 2), CAR+ConCutMix similarly dominates, improving overall accuracy by 2.42%–4.17% over LOS on Tiny-ImageNet-LT, CIFAR100-LT, and iNaturalist. On worst-class generalization (Table 3), all baselines achieve ≤10% worst-class test accuracy, while CAR+ConCutMix reaches 22% on ImageNet-LT and 18% on CIFAR100-LT, more than doubling the best baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: PAC-Bayesian confusion-matrix generalization bounds (Morvant et al. 2012; Neyshabur et al. 2017) and confusional spectral regularization for fairness (Sun et al., ICCV 2024). It repurposes the spectral-norm-of-confusion-matrix regularizer—originally designed to suppress biased eigen-directions in group-fairness settings—by introducing a frequency-weighting matrix Λ that amplifies tail-class errors, and derives a new worst-class error bound tailored to imbalanced distributions. In the long-tailed recognition literature, this departs from the dominant paradigms of re-sampling, loss re-weighting, and decoupled learning by targeting the inter-class confusion structure directly rather than adjusting sample frequencies or classifier biases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and under-addressed failure mode: existing long-tailed methods achieve >90% worst-class training accuracy yet collapse to <10% test accuracy, revealing a severe generalization gap that overall-accuracy metrics mask. CAR's simplicity (a single spectral-norm regularizer with EMA smoothing) and its consistent gains across five backbones, three datasets, and two imbalance factors make it a practical drop-in for long-tailed pipelines. Open questions include whether the spectral norm is the optimal spectral measure (e.g., top-k singular values or trace), whether the approach extends to open-world or few-shot long-tailed settings, and whether the frequency weighting can be learned rather than fixed a priori.
