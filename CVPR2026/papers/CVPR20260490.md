## Problem

Existing multimodal reward models operate as passive, single-pass scorers that cannot retrieve, localize, or verify evidence, leading to hallucinated rationales, position/length biases, and systematic failures on complex tasks like long-document QA, fine-grained perception, and multi-step instruction following. No prior reward model supports an active think-act-verify loop with tool invocation, making it impossible to ground judgments in verifiable intermediate evidence or assign credit for tool-use decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260490/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text query, one or more images (including full-page document screenshots at high resolution), and one or more candidate responses to compare. It produces a scalar reward score accompanied by an interpretable chain-of-thought rationale and a complete tool invocation trace. Unusually, the model maintains an indexed memory map (texts_map for candidate responses, imgs_map for intermediate crops) across multi-turn reasoning, and its output is structured as a sequence of think-act-observe cycles terminated by a final answer tag.

## Method

ARM-Thinker builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and follows a ReAct-style agent loop where the model iteratively generates thoughts, invokes one of three tool categories (image crop/zoom, document page retrieval, instruction-following validators), and integrates observations before issuing a final judgment. Training proceeds in two stages: first, supervised fine-tuning on difficulty-filtered preference data with cold-start trajectories generated by a stronger LVLM to bootstrap structured tool-use behavior; second, two-stage Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) where Stage 1 rewards any valid tool call to encourage exploration, and Stage 2 shifts to a hierarchical accuracy reward that grants additional credit only when tool usage directly contributes to a correct prediction. The key insight is that adaptive, utility-conditioned reward shaping prevents both tool underuse and overuse, enabling the model to learn when and how to invoke tools rather than chasing a fixed bonus.

## Results

ARM-Thinker-7B achieves a 64.0% average on reward-modeling benchmarks, a +16.2% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (47.8%), with the largest gain on the proposed ARMBench-VL (+18.5% to 64.6%). On tool-use (think-with-images) benchmarks, it reaches 76.5% average accuracy (+9.6%), matching Mini-o3 (76.1%) while outperforming GPT-4o (57.7%). On general multimodal reasoning benchmarks, it gains +4.2% average (49.0%), with notable jumps of +10.9% on WeMath and +8.7% on LogicVista.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARM-Thinker-7B outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark category. On reward modeling, it surpasses Qwen3-VL-8B (58.5% avg) by 5.5 points and GPT-4o (64.9%) by a narrow margin, while the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B lags at 47.8%. On tool-use benchmarks, ARM-Thinker-7B (76.5%) matches Mini-o3 (76.1%) and exceeds GPT-4o (57.7%) by 18.8 points, despite learning tool use through reward-modeling supervision rather than task-answering demonstrations. On general reasoning, it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B (44.8%) by 4.2 points but remains below Gemma-3-27B (47.3%) on MMMU, though it leads on WeMath (46.1 vs. 37.9) and LogicVista (52.8 vs. 47.3). The ablation (Table 5) confirms that the base model degrades when tools are enabled (e.g., V* drops from 75.4 to 50.3), whereas ARM-Thinker gains +4.2 to +5.4 points with tools, validating that the model learns when and how to invoke them.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARM-Thinker extends the ReAct think-act-observe paradigm and the structured tool-calling format from WebWatcher into the reward-modeling setting, a combination not previously explored. It departs from existing multimodal reward models (UnifiedReward, LLaVA-Critic) that score responses in a single passive pass, and from think-with-images task-solving agents (DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner, Mini-o3) that learn tool use through direct task-answering supervision. The instruction-following check tools are adapted from MM-IFEngine's validation schema, and the training pipeline builds on GRPO for reinforcement learning, but the key conceptual move is reframing reward scoring as an active, evidence-gathering agentic process rather than a static classification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward models are the backbone of RLHF/RLAIF pipelines for multimodal systems, and the paper demonstrates that equipping them with tools yields large, consistent gains (+16.2% on reward benchmarks) that passive scoring cannot achieve, particularly on long-document and fine-grained perception tasks. The introduction of ARMBench-VL provides the first standardized evaluation for agentic reward models, filling a gap that existing benchmarks (which use static QA pairs) cannot address. Open questions include the computational overhead of multi-step tool invocation during online RL training, whether the approach scales to larger tool suites or new modalities (video, audio), and whether the cross-paradigm transfer from reward supervision to tool-use competence holds at larger model scales.
