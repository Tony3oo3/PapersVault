## Problem

Autoregressive vision-language models dominate GUI grounding research, but their sequential decoding and unidirectional attention impose inherent limitations. Discrete diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) have recently shown competitive multimodal reasoning performance with bidirectional attention and parallel token generation, yet their potential for GUI grounding remains entirely unexplored. This paper asks whether a discrete DVLM can be adapted for single-turn GUI grounding and what training strategies are needed to capture the structured geometry of bounding-box predictions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a GUI screenshot (web, desktop, or mobile) and a natural-language instruction as input, and produces a single atomic action string of the form `action_type [x1,y1,x2,y2]` (plus typed text for `type_in` actions), with all coordinates normalized to [0, 1000] relative to screen dimensions. The bounding box encodes a geometric hierarchy: (x1, y1) anchors the action location while (x2, y2) defines its spatial extent. A prediction is scored correct only when both the action type matches and the center of the predicted box falls within the ground-truth box, making the task sensitive to precise localization rather than coarse region overlap.

## Method

The paper adapts LLaDA-V (8B), a discrete DVLM with a LLaDA language tower, SigLIP-2 vision encoder, and MLP projector, for single-turn GUI grounding by framing action prediction as masked text generation conditioned on image and instruction. The key contribution is a hybrid masking schedule that decomposes the forward corruption into two phases: a linear-masking phase where the model learns to predict the action type and anchor coordinates (x1, y1) under progressive random masking, and a full deterministic-masking phase where all target tokens are masked and the model must predict the extent coordinates (x2, y2) conditioned on the visible anchor, image, and instruction. This design explicitly enforces the conditional dependency p(x2,y2 | a_type, x1, y1, I, N) that random linear masking only captures sparsely, mirroring a coarse-to-fine spatial refinement. At inference, the two phases execute sequentially—the linear phase output feeds the deterministic phase—adding mild conditional sequentiality while retaining diffusion's parallel denoising within each phase. The model is fine-tuned on 120K multi-domain GUI samples (Mind2Web, WebLinX, OS-Atlas, Rico) with OCR-guided target annotations, and inference uses 64 diffusion steps with low-confidence re-masking to balance accuracy and latency.

## Results

On the four GUI grounding benchmarks, the proposed LLaDA-V 8B with hybrid masking achieves SSR of 83.9% (Mind2Web), 63.1% (ScreenSpot-Web-Icon), 74.8% (ScreenSpot-Web-Text), and 67.5% (VisualWebArena), with action-type F1 at or near 100% across all datasets. The hybrid masking schedule improves SSR by +1.5 to +6.1 points over the linear-masking variant of LLaDA-V, with the largest gain on VisualWebArena (+6.1). Training on the 120K multi-domain dataset (vs. 7K Mind2Web-only) yields an average SSR gain of ~20 points and reduces inference latency by ~1.3 seconds. The model converges in 16–23 diffusion steps depending on the benchmark, with average latency of 4.2–6.5 seconds per prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-VL (7B), the hybrid-masked LLaDA-V 8B wins on Mind2Web (83.9% vs. 81.9% SSR) but trails substantially on ScreenSpot-Web-Icon (63.1% vs. 85.4%), ScreenSpot-Web-Text (74.8% vs. 83.0%), and VisualWebArena (67.5% vs. 87.2%), reflecting the advantage of AR models with grounding-specific pretraining. Against Qwen2.5-VL (3B), LLaDA-V wins on Mind2Web (83.9% vs. 79.3%) and ScreenSpot-Web-Icon (63.1% vs. 79.1%—actually loses here), and is competitive on ScreenSpot-Web-Text (74.8% vs. 83.0%). Against Phi-3 (3B), LLaDA-V with hybrid masking clearly outperforms on Mind2Web (83.9% vs. 56.8%) and ScreenSpot-Web-Icon (63.1% vs. 62.6%), but is slightly behind on ScreenSpot-Web-Text (74.8% vs. 77.0%) and VisualWebArena (67.5% vs. 68.5%). Overall, the diffusion-based model narrows the gap to the strongest AR baseline from ~25 points to under 20 on the hardest benchmarks, while matching or exceeding it on Mind2Web, all without any grounding-specific pretraining or reinforcement tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts LLaDA-V, a discrete diffusion vision-language model originally designed for multimodal text generation and reasoning, to the GUI grounding task previously dominated by autoregressive VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Phi-3-Vision, CogAgent, SeeClick, etc.). The key methodological departure is a hybrid masking schedule that splits the diffusion denoising into a linear-masking phase (predicting action type and anchor coordinates) and a full-deterministic-masking phase (predicting bounding-box extent conditioned on the anchor), explicitly modeling the geometric hierarchy in bounding-box coordinates. The paper positions itself as the first systematic study of discrete DVLMs for GUI grounding, filling a gap left by prior diffusion-based VLM work (LLaDA-V, MMaDA) that focused on general multimodal understanding rather than structured spatial prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes that discrete diffusion models are a viable alternative paradigm for GUI grounding, narrowing the accuracy gap to strong AR baselines from ~25 to under 15 points on SSR without any grounding-specific pretraining. However, the practical adoption is currently limited by 2–5× higher inference latency (3–6 s vs. ~1.1 s for AR models) and the restriction to single-turn, single-action grounding. Open questions include whether the bidirectional attention and parallel token generation advantages of diffusion can be better exploited to close the latency gap, whether the hybrid masking generalizes to multi-step planning and dependent actions, and whether scaling to larger diffusion models or more diverse GUI domains can match or surpass AR performance.
