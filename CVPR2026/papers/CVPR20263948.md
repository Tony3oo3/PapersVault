## Problem

Decoding visual stimuli from EEG signals is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale paired EEG-image datasets, forcing existing methods to train EEG encoders from scratch or rely on masked self-supervised pre-training. These approaches suffer from poor cross-subject generalization, overfitting, and domain shifts due to EEG's inherently low signal-to-noise ratio and strong inter-subject variability. The paper asks whether the rich visual priors embedded in large-scale pretrained Vision Transformers can be directly transferred to EEG representation learning to overcome this data limitation.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw multi-channel EEG recordings (e.g., 63 channels at 1000 Hz on THINGS-EEG, 64 channels at 250 Hz on EEG-3D) and transforms them into 16×16 EEG patches by grouping electrodes into five anatomical brain regions, linearly interpolating each region to 16 channels, resampling the temporal axis to 16 frames, and replicating the signal three times to mimic RGB channels. The visual encoder (CLIP-based) produces 1024-dimensional image features, while the EEG encoder (ViT-B/16) outputs 768-dimensional features projected to 1024 dimensions via a fully connected layer. Outputs include ranked image lists for retrieval (top-1/top-5) and discrete labels for 72-class object type and 6-class color type classification.

## Method

EEGiT reformulates EEG signals as structured patches that structurally match ViT image patches, enabling a 12-layer ViT-B/16 pretrained on ImageNet-21K to serve directly as the EEG encoder. The EEG patch construction pipeline applies z-score normalization, groups electrodes into frontal/central/temporal/parietal/occipital regions, linearly interpolates each region to P=16 channels to preserve anatomical topology, and uniformly resamples the temporal axis to P=16 frames, yielding 16×16 patches replicated across three channels. For retrieval, training uses a symmetric cross-entropy contrastive loss (temperature τ=0.07) to align EEG and image features in a shared latent space; for classification, the loss combines contrastive and MSE alignment with auxiliary shape and color cross-entropy terms following the Neuro-3D framework. The key insight is that matching the spatial-temporal structure of EEG patches to image patches allows the pretrained ViT's self-attention layers to operate natively on neural data, transferring visual priors without architectural modification beyond a single projection head.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG EEG-to-image retrieval, EEGiT achieves 24.0% top-1 and 55.6% top-5 accuracy in the inter-subject setting, roughly doubling the best baseline UBP (12.4% / 33.4%). In the intra-subject setting, EEGiT reaches 70.4% top-1 and 95.1% top-5, surpassing the strongest baseline UBP (48.0% / 80.6%) by 22.4 percentage points in top-1. On EEG-3D, EEGiT attains 5.95% top-1 for object classification and 41.32% top-1 for color classification, marginally outperforming Neuro-3D (5.91% and 39.93% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EEGiT outperforms all eight baselines on both THINGS-EEG retrieval settings. In the inter-subject (cross-subject) setting, EEGiT's 24.0% top-1 accuracy is nearly 2× the best prior method UBP (12.4%) and roughly 2× ATM-S (11.8%), indicating that pre-trained ViT weights substantially mitigate subject-specific EEG variability. In the intra-subject setting, EEGiT (70.4% top-1) leads UBP (48.0%) by 22.4 points and ATM-S (38.7%) by 31.7 points, with top-5 accuracy reaching 95.1% versus UBP's 80.6%. On EEG-3D classification, EEGiT edges out Neuro-3D by 0.04% on object top-1 (5.95% vs 5.91%) and by 1.39% on color top-1 (41.32% vs 39.93%), while also surpassing classical baselines EEGNet, DeepNet, Conformer, and TSConv by 2–4 points on object and 10–23 points on color. The consistent margin across both datasets and both retrieval/classification tasks confirms that the EEG-patch + pre-trained ViT design generalizes beyond a single benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in EEG decoding, where custom spatiotemporal encoders (ATM-S, NICE, UBP) are trained from scratch or via EEG-specific self-supervised pre-training. Instead, it repurposes a pre-trained ViT-B/16 (Dosovitskiy et al., ICLR 2021) as the EEG encoder by introducing an 'EEG patch' representation that structurally mirrors image patches through anatomical channel grouping and temporal resampling. For the classification training strategy, it adopts the Neuro-3D framework (Guo et al., CVPR 2025) with its combined contrastive and auxiliary classification losses. The key conceptual departure is treating EEG as a 'visual' signal amenable to direct transfer of large-scale image priors, rather than designing EEG-specific architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework directly addresses the central bottleneck in EEG-based BCI research—data scarcity—by leveraging visual priors from ImageNet-21K pre-training, yielding roughly 2× improvement over the best prior method in cross-subject retrieval. This suggests a broader principle: structural alignment between modalities can unlock transfer from large-scale pre-trained models even when the target modality (EEG) is low-SNR and nonstationary. However, the approach is currently limited to retrieval and classification (not reconstruction), relies on linear interpolation that may introduce artifacts with sparse electrode layouts, and has been validated on only two datasets with a single ViT variant. Open questions include whether the anatomical grouping strategy is optimal, whether the method extends to generative reconstruction tasks, and whether other pre-trained vision backbones (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2) yield comparable or superior transfer.
