## Problem

Existing Multi-modal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (MMRAG) frameworks suffer from four interrelated failures: embedding-based retrieval fetches noisy or irrelevant content, Euclidean embedding spaces misalign visual, textual, and graph-based knowledge across modalities, static retrieval strategies ignore query complexity and model knowledge boundaries, and generation struggles to balance token-level accuracy with global semantic coherence. No prior system jointly addresses all four issues within a single principled framework.

## Input / Output

The system takes a 224×224 RGB image paired with a free-form textual question, plus access to a heterogeneous knowledge base of ~10M documents spanning Wikipedia articles, a curated image database, and 1M Wikidata graph nodes. A key design choice is that the pipeline may short-circuit: the Cognitive Reflection Module first emits a binary retrieval decision (δ), and for roughly 35% of queries the system answers directly from the MLLM's parametric memory without touching the knowledge base. When retrieval is triggered, the intermediate outputs include a filtered set of relevant multi-modal documents and a query-specific subgraph (typically ~500 nodes from the full graph), which are jointly fed into the generation stage to produce the final text answer.

## Method

CogniVerse integrates three modules trained via multi-task learning. First, a Cognitive Reflection Module uses the MLLM's maximum-likelihood confidence score against a learnable threshold to decide whether retrieval is needed, then scores each candidate document's relevance with a lightweight sigmoid head trained by a contrastive loss. Second, the Multi-modal Retrieval Module maps visual, textual, and query embeddings into a 128-dimensional hyperbolic space (Lorentz model) and minimizes geodesic distance via Riemannian SGD, while simultaneously selecting a query-relevant subgraph by minimizing the Laplacian Rayleigh quotient subject to a relevance constraint, solved with the top-10 eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian. Third, the Hierarchical Generation Module produces the answer with a fine-tuned LLaVA-13B under a composite loss that combines standard token-level cross-entropy with a 2-Wasserstein distance between the generated and reference answer distributions in embedding space, weighted by a hyperparameter α=0.7. A Query Dropout Strategy (exponentially decaying mask probability) during training forces the model to rely on retrieved knowledge when query information is incomplete.

## Results

CogniVerse achieves 84.3% accuracy, 0.91 coherence, and 78.4% retrieval precision on Encyclopedic-VQA; 82.7% accuracy, 0.90 coherence, and 76.8% retrieval precision on MultiModalQA; and 79.5% accuracy, 0.89 coherence, and 74.6% retrieval precision on WebQA. It outperforms the strongest baseline (MMCoQA) by 5.8–6.9% in accuracy across all three datasets while also achieving lower end-to-end latency (0.40–0.42 s vs. 0.43–0.50 s for other MMRAG methods). The CRM identifies 35% of queries as answerable without retrieval, reducing average latency by 15% compared to MMCoQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogniVerse outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest MMRAG baseline MMCoQA, it gains +5.8% accuracy on Encyclopedic-VQA (84.3 vs. 78.5), +6.8% on MultiModalQA (82.7 vs. 75.9), and +6.9% on WebQA (79.5 vs. 72.6), while also improving retrieval precision by 7–8 points and coherence by 0.06–0.07. Compared to GraphRAG, the gains are even larger (+7.5 to +9.4% accuracy), attributed to spectral graph refinement replacing static graph retrieval. Against the non-retrieval baselines CLIP-ViT-L and BLIP-2, CogniVerse leads by 21.9–24.2% in accuracy, confirming the value of external knowledge retrieval. Notably, CogniVerse also achieves the lowest latency among all MMRAG methods (0.40–0.42 s vs. 0.43–0.50 s), thanks to the CRM skipping retrieval for 35% of queries.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogniVerse extends the MMRAG paradigm established by MuRAG, MMCoQA, and GraphRAG by repurposing three mathematical tools—hyperbolic (Lorentz-model) embeddings, spectral graph theory, and 2-Wasserstein optimal transport—into a unified retrieval-and-generation pipeline. Where prior MMRAG systems rely on static Euclidean similarity search and cross-entropy generation, this paper introduces an adaptive retrieval gate (the Cognitive Reflection Module) and a geometrically grounded alignment objective, positioning itself as a theoretically motivated upgrade rather than a new task formulation. The lineage traces from classical RAG (Lewis et al., REALM) through multi-modal extensions to this paper's cognitive-inspired, manifold-based architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a practical pain point in MMRAG—unnecessary retrieval inflating latency and noise—by showing that a lightweight confidence gate can skip retrieval for ~35% of queries while improving overall accuracy, a trade-off prior systems did not explicitly optimize. However, the theoretical guarantees (convexity of geodesic loss in hyperbolic space, convergence of the Wasserstein-based generation objective) are stated with assumptions that are difficult to verify empirically, and the practical cost of Riemannian SGD plus spectral decomposition on 1M-node graphs is not fully characterized. Open questions include whether the hyperbolic alignment advantage persists at larger knowledge-base scales, whether the optimal transport loss generalizes beyond single-reference evaluation, and whether the cognitive reflection module's binary threshold can be replaced by a softer, uncertainty-calibrated mechanism.
