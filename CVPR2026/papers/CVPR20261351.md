## Problem

Multi-view diffusion (MVD) texturing pipelines suffer from three inherent limitations tied to the 2D-to-3D baking process: occluded regions require ad-hoc inpainting, texture features misalign with fine-grained geometric details, and cross-view consistency in content and color is difficult to maintain. These errors cascade from the modality change and are largely unavoidable within the MVD paradigm. The paper asks whether 3D textures can be treated as first-class citizens and generated natively in 3D space, eliminating the projection and baking steps entirely.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D mesh (sampled as a dense point cloud of positions, normals, and optionally colors), a reference image processed at 1022 resolution through DINOv2-Giant, and optionally an initial texture for refinement or material tasks. The output is a continuous 3D color field f(x)→c that can be queried at arbitrary 3D coordinates, yielding UV texture maps, per-vertex/per-face colors, PBR material maps (roughness and metallic encoded as modified albedo channels), or part segmentation maps. The VAE operates at arbitrary resolution at inference time, and the DiT supports variable token counts (trained up to 6144×64, scalable higher) and even one-step generation without distillation.

## Method

NaTex formulates texture generation as latent diffusion over a dense color point cloud, using a dual-branch geometry-aware VAE and a multi-control DiT. The VAE encodes the point cloud (RGB + position + normal) into ordered latent vectors via cross-attention, with a parallel geometry branch that encodes only positions and normals; geometry latents serve as queries for the color encoder, achieving over 80× compression. The DiT is a rectified flow transformer conditioned on three signals: image embeddings from DINOv2-Giant, geometry control via RoPE on point-query positions plus channel-wise concatenation of the isomorphic geometry latent set, and optional color control from an initial texture. Because geometry and texture latents share the same point queries, the concatenation provides pointwise surface guidance far more precise than per-view normal or depth maps. Training uses flow matching with an illumination-invariant loss for albedo, while the VAE is trained with KL divergence, a color regression loss supervising both on-surface and near-surface queries, and a truncated UDF loss.

## Results

On the image-conditioned texture generation benchmark (following MaterialMVP's protocol), NaTex achieves the best cFID of 21.96, CMMD of 2.055, and LPIPS of 0.102, outperforming all prior methods. The closest competitors, RomanTex and MaterialMVP, score 24.78 on cFID and 2.191 on CMMD. NaTex trails MaterialMVP only on CLIP-I (0.908 vs. 0.921), suggesting slightly lower image-level semantic similarity despite superior geometric alignment and perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NaTex outperforms all five open-source baselines (Paint3D, TexGen, Hunyuan3D-2, RomanTex, MaterialMVP) on three of four metrics: cFID (21.96 vs. best 24.78), CMMD (2.055 vs. best 2.191), and LPIPS (0.102 vs. best 0.121). The only metric where NaTex does not lead is CLIP-I, where MaterialMVP scores 0.921 versus NaTex's 0.908, indicating MaterialMVP's multi-view PBR diffusion produces images with slightly higher semantic similarity to the reference. RomanTex and MaterialMVP are the strongest competitors, tied on cFID and CMMD but both clearly behind NaTex. Visually, the paper also compares against two closed-source commercial models (Rodin-Gen2 and Tripo 3.0), noting that all competing methods struggle with texture alignment along geometry boundaries while NaTex achieves near-perfect alignment, even in occlusion-free cases where others still produce misaligned features.

## Relation to Prior Work

NaTex departs fundamentally from the dominant multi-view diffusion (MVD) texturing paradigm, which generates 2D views and bakes them onto geometry, by instead formulating texture as a dense color point cloud and applying latent diffusion directly in 3D space. Architecturally, it builds on 3DShape2VecSet's VecSet-style VAE for point cloud compression, Hunyuan3D-VAE's cross-attention backbone, and FLUX's rectified-flow DiT, but introduces a novel dual-branch geometry-aware VAE and a multi-control DiT with native geometry conditioning (RoPE + channel-concatenated geometry latents) that has no direct precedent in prior texture work. Compared to earlier native 3D texture attempts (TexGaussian, TexOct, TexGarment, Trellis), NaTex avoids proxy representations like octrees, Gaussian splatting, or UV maps, operating on a continuous color field instead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the 2D-lifting pipeline, NaTex addresses three persistent failure modes of MVD texturing—occlusion inpainting, boundary misalignment, and cross-view inconsistency—that the paper argues are structurally unavoidable in any projection-based approach. The unified framework's ability to handle material generation, texture refinement, and part segmentation with the same architecture (sometimes training-free) suggests a broadly reusable paradigm for 3D appearance modeling. Open questions include whether the approach scales to extremely high-poly or open-surface meshes, how training data requirements compare to MVD methods that leverage large 2D image datasets, and whether the one-step generation capability generalizes beyond the tested 2B parameter scale.
