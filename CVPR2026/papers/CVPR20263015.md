## Problem

Existing zero-shot composed image retrieval methods commit to either a text-to-image or image-to-image retrieval paradigm, each with complementary weaknesses: T2I loses fine-grained visual details while I2I struggles with complex semantic modifications. Prior attempts to combine both paths rely on static, fixed-weight fusion that cannot adapt to varying query intents and ignores the per-candidate uncertainty of each branch, leading to unreliable rankings under diverse modification types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a short modification text (e.g., 'add a hood') as the composed query, and produces a ranked list of images from a database of up to 123K candidates. Retrieval is performed via CLIP cosine similarity with a top-K=50 candidate pool per path, and the pipeline is iterative: a confidence threshold τ=0.7 gates whether refinement is triggered, with a default of one refinement round (N=1) before final ranking is returned.

## Method

WISER is a training-free 'retrieve-verify-refine' pipeline that unifies T2I and I2I through three stages. Wider Search uses BAGEL as a unified editor to simultaneously generate an edited caption (via BLIP-2 captioning + LLM rewriting) and an edited image (via diffusion-based editing), then retrieves top-K candidates from each path and takes their union. Adaptive Fusion employs Qwen2.5-VL-7B as a verifier that scores each candidate via binary yes/no logits on whether it matches the modification intent; at the branch level, if either path's best confidence falls below τ, refinement is triggered, otherwise a lexicographical multi-level sort (fused confidence → max single-path confidence → T2I confidence) ranks the union pool. Deeper Thinking invokes GPT-4o as a refiner that performs structured self-reflection in three steps—identifying intended modifications, comparing them against the retrieved pseudo-target's caption, and generating targeted suggestions—which are concatenated with the original modification text to regenerate the edited caption or image for the next retrieval round.

## Results

On CIRCO with ViT-B/32, WISER achieves mAP@5 of 32.23, a 44.98% relative improvement over the best training-free baseline CoTMR (22.23). On CIRR with ViT-B/32, WISER reaches Recall@1 of 49.45 (56.98% relative improvement over CoTMR's 31.50) and Recall_sub@1 of 77.30%. With the largest backbone ViT-G/14, WISER attains 49.54% Recall@1 on CIRR and 36.53% mAP@5 on CIRCO, consistently outperforming all baselines including training-based methods like LinCIR and IP-CIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WISER outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and backbone configuration. Against the strongest training-free baseline CoTMR [49], WISER achieves a 44.98% relative gain in mAP@5 on CIRCO (32.23 vs. 22.23 with ViT-B/32) and a 56.98% relative gain in Recall@1 on CIRR (49.45 vs. 31.50). Against IP-CIR [33], the only other method that fuses T2I and I2I (but with a fixed weight), WISER leads by 3.78 mAP@5 on CIRCO and 10.29 Recall@1 on CIRR with ViT-G/14, confirming that adaptive fusion substantially outperforms static weighting. WISER also surpasses training-based methods: on CIRR with ViT-G/14, it exceeds LinCIR [21] by 14.29 Recall@1 (49.54 vs. 35.25) and MOA [35]/HIT [34] by over 20 points. On Fashion-IQ (Table 2), WISER with ViT-B/32 achieves 41.99% avg R@10, beating CoTMR (34.30%) by 7.69 points and OSrCIR [50] (32.34%) by 9.65 points. The consistent margin across all backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, ViT-G/14) indicates the gains stem from the framework design rather than backbone capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

WISER sits at the intersection of two established ZS-CIR paradigms: T2I methods (CIReVL, CoTMR, LDRE) that convert queries into edited captions, and I2I methods (CompoDiff, CIG, IP-CIR) that generate edited images via diffusion. The key departure is from prior dual-path approaches like IP-CIR, which fuse T2I and I2I with a fixed weight; WISER instead introduces a verification-guided adaptive fusion with an iterative self-reflection loop, making the fusion dynamic and uncertainty-aware. The framework repurposes off-the-shelf components (BAGEL as editor, Qwen2.5-VL as verifier, GPT-4o as refiner) within a novel 'retrieve-verify-refine' pipeline, requiring no task-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for ZS-CIR deployment in new domains without triplet annotation, and the 45–57% relative gains over prior training-free methods are substantial and consistent across three benchmarks and three CLIP scales. However, the pipeline's reliance on multiple expensive models (a unified multimodal editor, a 7B VLM verifier, and a GPT-4o refiner) introduces significant inference cost, and the refinement loop adds latency for uncertain queries. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D retrieval, whether lighter verifiers can match the 7B model's calibration, and whether the self-reflection mechanism generalizes to multi-turn interactive retrieval settings.
