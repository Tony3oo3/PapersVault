## Problem

Vision-language models excel at broad visual understanding but remain coarse-grained, exhibiting systematic biases and missing subtle instance-level visual details. Existing training corpora reinforce this limitation by emphasizing category-level recognition and general reasoning over fine-grained perception, providing little incentive for models to attend to nuanced, instance-specific cues. The paper asks whether targeted training on data that explicitly rewards fine-grained visual discrimination can close this perceptual gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262046/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For pair queries, the VLM receives two RGB images and must produce a chain-of-thought explanation followed by a binary yes/no judgment on whether they depict the same physical object instance. For multi queries, it receives one reference image plus three candidates and outputs a match count (0–3) with per-image reasoning. Supervision during training relies solely on the final answer correctness (binary reward), with no descriptive textual annotations required—only pairwise instance assignments.

## Method

The core contribution is TWIN, a 561K-query VQA dataset built from 1,836 household object instances (22,157 images) sourced from Amazon Reviews, combining human-verified positive pairs, human-selected hard negatives (visually similar but distinct objects identified via CLIP similarity shortlisting), and DreamBooth-generated synthetic negatives that alter fine details while preserving overall appearance. Models are post-trained using GRPO reinforcement learning with a binary outcome reward on the final yes/no answer, which the authors show preserves generalization better than SFT on out-of-distribution domains. The FGVQA benchmark suite (12K queries) repurposes recognition/retrieval datasets across art, retail, landmarks, birds, and animals into pairwise and multi-query VQA formats. Scaling analysis demonstrates that dataset size is the dominant factor: performance improves monotonically from 5K to 561K samples, and hard negatives contribute an additional ~4.5% mean accuracy gain over random negatives.

## Results

On the FGVQA benchmark suite (12,000 queries across 6 domains), Qwen2.5-VL 3B post-trained on TWIN achieves 67.0% mean accuracy, a +14.0% gain over its 53.0% base. The best open-source baseline, Gemma3 4B, scores 68.7% mean, while proprietary GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash reach 86.1% and 82.2% respectively. The largest single-dataset improvement from TWIN training is +19.3% on INQUIRE (54.4% → 73.7%) and +18.3% on ILIAS (43.5% → 61.8%). Post-training preserves general VQA performance, with only ±0.5% shifts on benchmarks like POPE, NLVR2, and MMMU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL + TWIN (67.0% mean) slightly trails Gemma3 4B (68.7% mean) overall, but decisively wins on TWIN-Eval (67.3% vs 53.8%), INQUIRE (73.7% vs 69.4%), and CUB (75.1% vs 73.2%), while losing on ILIAS (61.8% vs 67.5%), MET (66.0% vs 72.7%), and LANDMARKS (57.9% vs 73.2%)—the latter two being domains with the least overlap to TWIN's household-object training data. Against proprietary models, TWIN training narrows the gap substantially: on INQUIRE, the gap to GPT-4o shrinks from 29.5% (base) to 10.2% (+TWIN), and on TWIN-Eval from 31.6% to 14.4%. InternVL3.5 1B + TWIN shows more modest gains (+2.9% mean) but still improves on 5 of 6 datasets, confirming the benefit transfers across architectures and scales. All open-source baselines (PerceptionLM, Moondream, SmolVLM2) score below 50% mean, far behind both Gemma3 and the TWIN-trained models. On general VQA benchmarks (Table 4), TWIN-trained models retain or slightly improve performance (e.g., +1.4% on NLVR2 for Qwen2.5-VL), confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in VLM training data, which emphasizes category-level recognition, spatial reasoning, and common knowledge, by introducing a dataset (TWIN) that specifically rewards instance-level visual discrimination. It extends the line of work on fine-grained recognition (MET, ILIAS, CUB, INQUIRE, LANDMARKS), which has traditionally been framed as 1-of-N classification or retrieval, by reformulating these tasks as pairwise VQA queries suitable for VLMs. The closest prior work—SpotTheDiff and Birds-to-Words—pairs images with textual difference descriptions but is far smaller (13K and 3.3K pairs) and domain-restricted; TWIN is 40× larger, includes both positive and negative pairs, and spans diverse household objects. For post-training, the paper builds on the RL-over-SFT finding that reinforcement learning better preserves generalization, applying GRPO with a binary correctness reward rather than token-level supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is that training on 561K household-object pairs transfers to substantially improved fine-grained perception on entirely unseen domains (art, birds, plants, landmarks), suggesting the model learns a general 'attend to subtle instance-level cues' skill rather than memorizing specific object features. This positions TWIN as a drop-in addition to open-source VLM training corpora, and the scaling analysis (5K→561K) indicates further gains are available with more data. Open questions include whether structured or multi-modal rewards beyond binary correctness could yield richer perceptual improvements, whether automated hard-negative mining can reduce annotation cost, and whether 3D-aware representations could help with viewpoint-invariant instance matching. The paper's scope is limited to two small models (1B and 3B) and household objects, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse training domains.
