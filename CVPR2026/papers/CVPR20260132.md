## Problem

Existing motion control methods for video are limited to image-to-video generation (conditioning only on a single frame), human-specific pose editing, or camera trajectory manipulation, none of which support true video-to-video motion editing of arbitrary objects while preserving the full scene content. This paper identifies precise motion editing of existing videos as an under-explored paradigm and shows that standard appearance-editing techniques like DDIM inversion fail when structural correspondence between input and output frames is broken by motion changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260132/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full 49-frame input video at 480×720 resolution, sparse tracking points (up to ~20 at inference) on objects of interest rendered as colored Gaussian blobs on black backgrounds, user-specified target trajectories for those points, and a text prompt describing the scene. It produces a motion-edited video where the tracked objects follow the target trajectories while all other content is preserved. The model conditions on three video channels simultaneously: the counterfactual (input) video, the counterfactual motion tracks, and the target motion tracks, each encoded through a 3D Causal VAE into 16-channel latents.

## Method

The core contribution is a motion-conditioned video diffusion architecture that extends CogVideoX-5B with a ControlNet-style control branch duplicating the first 18 DiT transformer blocks, which processes three conditioning video channels through a patchifier handling 48 (3×16) input channels and integrates into the main branch via zero-initialized channel-wise MLPs. Training data consists of 'motion counterfactual' video pairs with identical content but different motion, generated via two strategies: frame interpolation (a video diffusion model conditioned on first and last frames with LLM-generated prompts) and temporal resampling (evenly spaced frame extraction producing speed changes and reversals), with point correspondences established using the TAPNext bidirectional tracker. The key insight is that the model successfully transfers motion patterns despite the conditioning inputs (video + motion blobs) lacking spatiotemporal synchronization with the output, suggesting the transformer blocks perform non-trivial alignment. The base model is frozen while only the control branch is trained with L2 loss on 100K synthetic video pairs over 15K iterations on 8 H100 GPUs.

## Results

In a 4-way user study with 41 participants across 20 test videos, MotionV2V achieves ~70% win rate on all three criteria (content preservation 70%, motion fidelity 71%, overall quality 69%), compared to 24–25% for ATI and <5% for ReVideo and Go-with-the-Flow. On the quantitative photometric reconstruction benchmark (100 test videos), MotionV2V achieves L2 = 0.024, SSIM = 0.098, and LPIPS = 0.031, substantially outperforming ATI (L2 = 0.038, LPIPS = 0.072) and the remaining baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionV2V decisively outperforms all three baselines on both user preference and quantitative metrics. Against ATI [37] (the strongest baseline, built on the more powerful WAN 2.1 backbone), MotionV2V wins the user study by ~45 percentage points (70% vs. 25%) and achieves 37% lower L2 error (0.024 vs. 0.038) and 57% lower LPIPS (0.031 vs. 0.072), primarily because ATI's first-frame conditioning cannot preserve content that appears mid-video or handle camera changes. ReVideo [24] and Go-with-the-Flow [3] perform markedly worse, with user win rates of only 1–5% and L2 errors of 0.096 and 0.067 respectively; ReVideo lacks text conditioning and its inpainting strategy breaks under camera motion, while Go-with-the-Flow was not designed for sparse point-trajectory control. The gap is most pronounced in scenarios involving offscreen objects, temporal reordering, and simultaneous object-plus-camera edits, where first-frame-based methods fundamentally cannot access the necessary visual context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ControlNet-style adapter paradigm (zero-initialized MLPs feeding a frozen backbone) from image generation to video, building on CogVideoX-5B as the base DiT and drawing architectural inspiration from DiffusionAsShader's multi-channel video conditioning. It departs fundamentally from prior motion-controlled video work (ReVideo, Go-with-the-Flow, ATI), which operates in an image-to-video setting and conditions only on a single first frame, by formulating motion editing as a true video-to-video task that conditions on the full input video plus rendered point trajectories. The motion counterfactual data generation pipeline—pairing real video clips with temporally resampled or diffusion-interpolated counterparts—is a novel training strategy not present in prior trajectory-conditioned generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This establishes the first practical framework for editing motion in existing user-provided videos while preserving all other content, a capability that VFX pipelines previously required extensive manual labor to achieve. Key limitations include the ~20-point tracking cap, subject drift in iterative edits, and reliance on a 5B-parameter base model trained on 8 H100s for a week. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or more complex multi-object interactions, whether newer/larger base models reduce drift, and whether the Gaussian blob trajectory representation can be replaced by more expressive encodings without the current point-count bottleneck.
