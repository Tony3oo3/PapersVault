## Problem

Prior video reasoning segmentation (VRS) methods compress video-level semantics into a single segmentation token, producing opaque inference with weak spatiotemporal reasoning and brittle behavior in long, occlusion-heavy sequences. These token-centric pipelines also rely on mixed large-scale image/video corpora for cross-modal alignment, and none explicitly cast VRS as a sequential decision process that first selects a keyframe before performing fine-grained spatial grounding with verifiable rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260723/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a uniformly sampled video sequence (T frames at H×W×3, each prefixed with its absolute video-level frame index) and a natural-language reasoning instruction that may specify targets indirectly (e.g., causal or abductive descriptions). The model outputs a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace wrapped in <think>... tags, followed by a keyframe index and a list of 2D bounding boxes serialized in <answer>...</answer> tags; pixel-wise masks for all frames are then obtained by feeding the matched boxes and keyframe timestamp into a frozen SAM2 propagator.

## Method

Veason-R1 decomposes VRS into two sequential decisions—keyframe selection and spatial grounding—and trains a single Qwen2.5-VL policy via a two-stage regimen. Stage 1 (Veason-SFT) warm-starts the policy with LoRA fine-tuning on chain-of-thought annotations generated by Seed1.5-VL, using absolute frame indexing to reduce positional ambiguity. Stage 2 applies critic-free GRPO with a four-component verifiable reward: format compliance, temporal localization (ratio of keyframe mask area to maximum), spatial alignment (normalized IoU via Hungarian one-to-one box matching), and unified consistency (average video-level mask IoU after SAM2 propagation from the selected keyframe). KL regularization to the SFT reference model prevents policy drift, and the entire pipeline is trained on ReVOS alone, avoiding the multi-source corpora required by prior token-based methods.

## Results

On ReVOS, Veason-R1-7B achieves 61.3 J&F overall (58.2 J, 64.4 F), surpassing the prior SOTA VRS-HQ-13B (60.0 J&F) by +1.3, while Veason-R1-3B (59.9 J&F) matches the 13B model. On ReasonVOS, Veason-R1-7B reaches 59.9 J&F, outperforming GLUS-7B (49.9) by +10.0. On MeViS (zero-shot, trained only on ReVOS), Veason-R1-7B attains 52.2 J&F, exceeding VRS-HQ-13B (50.9) by +0.9. Veason-R1-3B also reports a robustness score R of 28.5 versus 19.7 for VRS-HQ-7B, indicating substantially reduced hallucination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On ReVOS, Veason-R1-7B (61.3 J&F) outperforms VRS-HQ-13B (60.0) by +1.3 and Omni-R1 (58.9) by +2.4, while Veason-R1-3B (59.9) essentially matches VRS-HQ-13B despite being less than half the parameter count. On ReasonVOS, Veason-R1-7B (59.9 J&F) leads GLUS-7B (49.9) by +10.0 and VideoLISA-3.8B (47.5) by +12.4, with the largest gap on the F metric (+11.4 over GLUS), indicating sharper contour boundaries under motion and occlusion. On MeViS, Veason-R1-7B (52.2 J&F) surpasses VRS-HQ-13B (50.9) by +0.9 and GLUS-7B (51.3) by +0.9, despite being trained zero-shot on ReVOS only. In terms of robustness, Veason-R1-3B achieves R=28.5 versus 19.7 for VRS-HQ-7B and 17.9 for GLUS-7B, a +8.8 improvement over the strongest prior method, reflecting reduced hallucination from the format-compliance and consistency rewards. Notably, Veason-R1 is trained exclusively on ReVOS, whereas several baselines (e.g., Omni-R1, VRS-HQ) leverage multi-source training data, making the data-efficiency gap particularly striking.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VRS line of work (VISA, VideoLISA, VRS-HQ, HyperSeg) by replacing the dominant 'compress video semantics into a single <SEG> token' paradigm with an explicit two-step sequential decision: keyframe selection followed by spatial grounding. The RL optimization stage directly builds on GRPO (DeepSeek-R1) and its multimodal extensions (Seg-Zero, Omni-R1, Video-R1), but departs from Omni-R1's cascaded dual-system design by training a single end-to-end policy with unified temporal, spatial, and cross-frame consistency rewards. The CoT warm-start stage repurposes Seed1.5-VL as an annotation engine to bootstrap structured reasoning priors before preference optimization, a pattern borrowed from LLM reasoning literature but applied here to spatiotemporal video grounding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that reinforcement-driven, verifiable reasoning can substantially improve VRS while requiring training on a single dataset (ReVOS) rather than the multi-source corpora typical of prior methods, suggesting a more data-efficient path for video segmentation. The interpretable chain-of-thought traces and the +10 J&F gap on ReasonVOS indicate that explicit temporal decision-making (choosing *which* frame to ground in) is a meaningful bottleneck that token-based pipelines under-address. Open questions include whether the reliance on pseudo-CoT annotations and frozen SAM2 propagation limits ceiling performance, whether the keyframe-first decomposition generalizes to streaming or very long videos, and whether end-to-end mask-level rewards can replace the oracle-dependent IoU signals used here.
