## Problem

Diffusion Transformer sampling processes all image regions uniformly at every timestep, ignoring the fact that semantically important areas require more refinement while smooth backgrounds tolerate aggressive step reduction. Existing acceleration methods (step distillation, feature caching) reduce computation globally but cannot exploit this spatial heterogeneity, leaving a gap between achievable speedup and quality preservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261850/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RAS operates as a drop-in inference-time modification on existing DiT text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion 3, Lumina-Next-T2I) at 1024×1024 resolution. It takes a standard text prompt and produces a single RGB image, but internally works at the patchified latent-token level: at each diffusion step, only a user-defined fraction of spatial tokens (e.g., 12.5%–50%) are forwarded through the network while the remainder reuse cached noise predictions from the prior step.

## Method

RAS identifies fast-update regions by computing the per-patch standard deviation of the DiT's predicted noise (lower variance indicates higher semantic focus) and scaling by an exponential of each patch's historical drop count to prevent starvation. Only the selected fast-update tokens are forwarded through the transformer; slow-update tokens retain their cached noise. To preserve attention context, inactive tokens' keys and values are cached and concatenated with active tokens' fresh K/V during attention computation, so active queries still attend to the full spatial extent. A dynamic schedule runs full 100% updates for the first few steps to establish global structure, then reduces the active ratio, with periodic dense-step resets to correct accumulated drift in long-inactive regions. A kernel-fusing optimization embeds the token scatter operation into the GeMM epilogue to avoid extra memory copies.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 3 (28 timesteps), RAS at 12.5% sampling ratio achieves a 2.44× speedup (20.36 GB memory, +6% overhead) with only marginal FID degradation, while on Lumina-Next-T2I (30 timesteps) it reaches 2.70× speedup. In the Pareto comparison (Table 2), RAS with 7 steps at 25% ratio on SD3 yields FID 31.99 and CLIP 30.64 at 1.45 img/s, outperforming RFlow at 5 steps (FID 39.70, CLIP 29.84, 1.43 img/s) on all three metrics simultaneously. On Lumina, RAS at 10 steps/25% achieves FID 45.67 and CLIP 29.82 at 0.59 img/s versus RFlow at 7 steps (FID 48.19, CLIP 28.65, 0.49 img/s). Human evaluation confirms 45.21% of 1,400 votes rated RAS outputs as comparable to dense inference, with 26.50% actually preferring RAS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against rectified flow (RFlow), RAS consistently achieves Pareto improvements: at equivalent or lower throughput, RAS delivers better FID, sFID, and CLIP scores simultaneously. For example, on SD3, RAS at 7 steps/25% (1.45 img/s) beats RFlow at 5 steps (1.43 img/s) by 7.7 FID points and 0.8 CLIP points; on Lumina, RAS at 10 steps/25% (0.59 img/s) outperforms RFlow at 7 steps (0.49 img/s) by 2.5 FID and 1.2 CLIP. Against DeepCache and Δ-DiT (layer-wise caching methods adapted for DiT), RAS achieves greater speedups while also yielding improved FID and CLIP scores across all tested cache intervals (Figure 6), demonstrating that region-adaptive token selection is more effective than uniform layer-wise feature reuse for transformer-based diffusion models. The paper notes RAS is orthogonal to these methods and can be combined with them for further gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAS builds on the architectural flexibility of Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) [Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023], whose embedding-based positional encoding (e.g., RoPE) permits selective token masking without disrupting spatial structure—a property absent in U-Net backbones. It also adapts the kernel-fusing principle from PIT [Zheng et al., SOSP 2023] to embed scatter operations into GeMM epilogues for efficient partial-token updates. Conceptually, RAS departs from the dominant acceleration paradigm of uniformly reducing sampling steps (rectified flow, DPM-Solver, consistency models) or reusing layer-wise features (DeepCache, Δ-DiT) by introducing a spatially heterogeneous computation budget within each step, making it orthogonal and composable with those methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAS provides a practical, training-free path toward near-real-time DiT-based text-to-image generation, achieving 2.3–2.5× speedups with Pareto improvements over uniform rectified-flow baselines on both FID and CLIP score. The observation of strong temporal continuity in DiT attention (high NDCG across adjacent steps) is a useful empirical finding that could inform other token-pruning or sparse-computation strategies in transformer-based generative models. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video DiTs (where temporal token redundancy may interact differently with spatial selectivity), whether the noise-standard-deviation metric generalizes to non-rectified-flow schedulers, and how the method behaves under extreme compression ratios (<10% active tokens) where the key-value cache approximation may degrade.
