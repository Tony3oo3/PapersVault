## Problem

Existing LLM-based multimodal sentiment analysis methods compress audio-visual features into learnable query tokens that serve as soft prompts, but these queries are trained implicitly and lack explicit guidance on how each token encodes specific sentiment semantics. This absence of semantic grounding limits the LLM's ability to reason about fine-grained sentiment categories from non-textual modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263878/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted text, visual (video), and audio (speech) feature sequences of variable lengths, along with a fixed set of K sentiment prototype vectors (e.g., 7 for MOSEI's SN/N/WN/Neu/WP/P/SP scale). The frozen LLM backbone (ChatGLM-6B, LLaMA-2-7B, or Qwen-1.5B) receives the text features plus the learned multimodal prototypes as soft prompts and produces a sentiment polarity prediction via next-token generation, supporting both continuous scores (MAE, Corr) and discrete category labels (Acc-2, Acc-7, F1) depending on the dataset.

## Method

PaP first constructs text-conditioned prototypes by concatenating visual and acoustic prototype vectors with text features and applying self-attention, then resamples raw audio-visual features through cross-modal attention where the text-conditioned prototypes act as queries, producing compact pseudo-tokens that bridge the cross-modal gap. Three training constraints shape the prototypes: (1) sentiment-aware prototype learning maps each prototype through a classifier to predict its sentiment category via cross-entropy, explicitly binding semantics; (2) cross-modal prototype alignment minimizes Central Moment Discrepancy between visual and acoustic prototype distributions to enforce consistent sentiment encoding across modalities; (3) distance-weighted prototype diversity applies a positionally-weighted orthogonality penalty on the Gram matrix to prevent redundant prototypes. Only the prototype modules (0.09%–0.26% of total parameters) are trained while the LLM backbone remains frozen, and the final prototypes plus text features are fed into the LLM for next-token sentiment prediction.

## Results

On MOSEI, PaP with LLaMA-2-7B achieves the best binary accuracy of 87.17% and F1 of 86.91%, while PaP with ChatGLM-6B attains the highest Pearson correlation of 0.802. On SIMS-V2, PaP(C) reaches 84.75% Acc-2 and 0.264 MAE; on MELD, PaP(C) scores 67.47% accuracy; and on CHERMA, PaP(C) achieves 75.33% accuracy and 75.28% weighted F1. Across all four datasets and three LLM backbones, PaP uses only 0.09%–0.26% trainable parameters while the LLM backbone remains fully frozen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PaP consistently outperforms the closest LLM-based competitor MSE [44] across all four datasets and all three LLM backbones. On MOSEI, PaP(L) surpasses MSE(L) by 0.43% Acc-2 (87.17 vs. 86.74) and 0.67% Acc-7 (56.24 vs. 55.57); on SIMS-V2, PaP(C) leads MSE(C) by 0.98% Acc-2 (84.75 vs. 83.77) and 0.032 MAE (0.264 vs. 0.296). On MELD, PaP(C) improves over MSE(C) by 1.24% accuracy (67.47 vs. 66.23), and on CHERMA the gap widens to 2.43% accuracy (75.33 vs. 72.90). Against non-LLM baselines, PaP(L) exceeds DiffuFuse [31] by 0.47% Acc-2 and AlignMamba [20] by 0.57% Acc-2 on MOSEI, while PaP(C) outperforms EMOE [6] by 1.66% Acc-2. The Qwen-1.5B variant PaP(Q) trails MSE(C) on MOSEI Acc-2 (86.00 vs. 86.91) but still surpasses all non-LLM baselines, confirming that the prototype-as-prompt strategy is effective even with a smaller LLM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Query-as-Prompt paradigm (MSE-Adapter) for LLM-based multimodal sentiment analysis by replacing implicit learnable queries with a fixed set of sentiment prototypes that carry explicit semantic meaning. Conceptually, it repurposes the prototype learning idea from few-shot classification and domain adaptation (via CMD-based distribution alignment) into the LLM soft-prompt setting, where each prototype is bound to a specific sentiment category through supervised cross-entropy loss. The cross-modal attention resampling mechanism draws on Q-Former-style architectures, but the key departure is the explicit sentiment-aware supervision and distance-weighted diversity regularization that constrain the prototype space in ways prior query-based methods do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a frozen LLM can perform competitive multimodal sentiment analysis with under 0.3% trainable parameters, offering a practical and interpretable alternative to full fine-tuning or adapter-heavy approaches. The explicit prototype-to-sentiment mapping provides a degree of interpretability (which prototype fires for which emotion) that implicit query-based methods lack. Open questions include whether the fixed 7-prototype set generalizes to open-vocabulary or fine-grained sentiment taxonomies, whether the approach extends beyond audio-visual-text to additional modalities, and whether the CMD-based alignment remains effective when modality gaps are larger (e.g., sign language or physiological signals).
