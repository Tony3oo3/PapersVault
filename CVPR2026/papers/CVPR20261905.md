## Problem

Standard Normalizing Flows require the reverse (generative) process to be the exact analytic inverse of the forward process, constraining architectures to explicitly invertible designs with tractable Jacobian determinants. Recent NF methods like TARFlow integrate Transformers but still enforce autoregressive sequential decoding at inference (thousands of causal steps), making sampling slow and preventing the use of efficient bidirectional attention. The paper asks whether a separate, learned reverse model can replace the analytic inverse, enabling flexible architectures and single-pass generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261905/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

BiFlow operates in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE tokenizer, mapping 32×32×4 latent vectors (corresponding to 256×256 RGB images) to and from a Gaussian prior. The reverse model takes a single noise sample plus a class label and produces the full latent in one forward pass (1-NFE), which is then decoded by the VAE to a 256×256 image. Unlike the autoregressive NF baseline requiring 256×8 sequential token-level evaluations, BiFlow's bidirectional Transformer processes all 256 tokens in parallel.

## Method

BiFlow decouples the forward and reverse processes: the forward model (an improved TARFlow with in-context conditioning and norm control) is trained via standard NF maximum likelihood, then frozen. A separate bidirectional ViT reverse model is trained to approximate the inverse using a 'hidden alignment' objective, where learnable projection heads align the reverse model's intermediate hidden states with corresponding forward trajectory states without forcing repeated projections to input space. The reverse model also integrates a learned denoising block (replacing TARFlow's separate score-based denoising step) and incorporates classifier-free guidance into training by conditioning on the guidance scale, enabling 1-NFE inference with adjustable guidance at test time. The key insight is that this learned inverse, trained to reconstruct real data with perceptual losses (LPIPS + ConvNeXt V2 features), can outperform the exact analytic inverse in generation quality while being up to two orders of magnitude faster.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, BiFlow-B/2 (133M params) achieves an FID of 2.39 and IS of 303.0 using only a single function evaluation (1-NFE). This surpasses the exact analytic inverse of the same forward model (iTARFlow-XL/2, 690M params, FID 4.54) while being up to two orders of magnitude faster in wall-clock time (e.g., 0.29 ms vs. 65 ms on TPU for similar-size models). Among all NF-based methods, BiFlow sets a new state-of-the-art, matching STARFlow-XL/1 (FID 2.40) with ~10× fewer parameters and >400× faster inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among NF-based methods, BiFlow-B/2 (FID 2.39, 133M) decisively outperforms its own forward model's exact inverse iTARFlow-XL/2 (FID 4.54, 690M) and matches STARFlow-XL/1 (FID 2.40, 1.4B) while using ~10× fewer parameters and >400× faster wall-clock inference. Against 1-NFE competitors, BiFlow beats StyleGAN-XL (2.30 vs. 2.39 is a near-tie, with BiFlow slightly behind), MeanFlow-XL/2 (3.43), TiM-XL/2 (3.26), and α-Flow-XL/2+ (2.58), but trails the strongest 1-NFE distillation methods iMF-XL/2 (1.72), FACM-XL/2 (1.76), and DMF-XL/2+ (2.16). Compared to multi-NFE diffusion/flow matching models, BiFlow's FID of 2.39 is competitive with DiT-XL/2 (2.27) and SiT-XL/2 (2.06) while requiring only 1 NFE versus 500 NFEs. Against autoregressive models (VAR-d30 FID 1.92, MAR-H 1.55, RAR-XXL 1.48, xAR-H 1.24), BiFlow is slightly behind in FID but achieves its result in a single parallelized forward pass rather than thousands of sequential token-level evaluations, yielding up to 697× speedup on TPU over comparable-size iTARFlow.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiFlow builds directly on TARFlow and STARFlow, which revived Normalizing Flows by integrating Transformers into autoregressive flows, but departs from them by removing the requirement that the reverse process be the exact analytic inverse of the forward process. Instead of constraining the forward model to be explicitly invertible (which forces causal, sequential decoding), BiFlow learns a separate, bidirectional reverse model via a hidden alignment objective that supervises intermediate representations along the forward trajectory. This positions BiFlow at the intersection of NFs and the broader 1-NFE generative model family (consistency models, mean flows, GANs), while retaining the NF principle of learning forward trajectories rather than pre-scheduling them as in Flow Matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a learned approximate inverse can outperform the exact analytic inverse challenges a foundational assumption in Normalizing Flows and opens the door to using arbitrary, non-invertible architectures (e.g., bidirectional ViTs) for the reverse process. The two-orders-of-magnitude speedup while simultaneously improving FID makes NFs practically competitive with 1-NFE diffusion/flow-matching methods for the first time. Open questions include whether the scaling behavior (diminishing returns observed with ConvNeXt perceptual loss) reflects a fundamental ceiling, whether the framework extends to higher resolutions or other modalities, and how the learned-trajectory NF paradigm might synergize with continuous-time Flow Matching methods.
