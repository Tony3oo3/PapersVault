## Problem

Medical image segmentation is constrained by expensive pixel-level annotations, ambiguous anatomical boundaries, and domain shifts that cause deterministic models to fail on out-of-distribution data. While CLIP-based vision-language models offer label-efficient cross-modal representations, their dense localization capability remains weak in the medical domain, and deterministic CLIP formulations stay over-confident on OOD inputs. The paper asks how to formulate cross-modal attention to be uncertainty-aware for CLIP-based medical segmentation, enabling both data efficiency and calibrated reliability.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a medical image (RGB, partitioned into 16×16 patches by the ViT encoder) and a natural language text prompt describing the target anatomy, producing a full-resolution binary segmentation mask via bilinear upsampling of patch-text dot-product logits. At inference, 30 Monte Carlo forward passes through the probabilistic adapters yield both the mean segmentation mask and a pixel-level uncertainty map (predictive entropy across samples), with uncertainty concentrating along lesion boundaries. Text prompts for datasets lacking annotations are generated via GPT-5 with image-processing-derived visual attributes.

## Method

MedCLIPSeg introduces the Probabilistic Vision-Language (PVL) Adapter, inserted at multiple deep CLIP layers, which performs bidirectional cross-modal attention where Keys and Values are modeled as Gaussian distributions with learnable means and variances (variational formulation). Attention scores combine a mean similarity term with a variance-based confidence penalty (scaled by β=2.35), adaptively downweighting uncertain tokens to reduce over-confidence. A residual gating mechanism gradually increases reliance on attended features during training for stable optimization. Segmentation is computed by dot-product between L2-normalized visual patch tokens and the text [EOS] embedding, while a soft patch-level contrastive loss (using text-similarity-derived soft targets instead of hard one-hot labels) refines image-text alignment under limited supervision. The overall loss combines Dice+BCE segmentation loss with the soft contrastive objective, and all CLIP pretrained parameters remain frozen.

## Results

On the data-efficiency benchmark (Table 1), MedCLIPSeg achieves 88.66% DSC and 91.35% NSD at 100% training data, outperforming the strongest CLIP-based baseline CAT-Seg (85.90% DSC) by 2.76 points and the best unimodal method nnUNet (81.40% DSC) by 7.26 points. Under the most data-constrained setting (10% data), MedCLIPSeg reaches 81.10% DSC, a 2.34-point margin over CAT-Seg (78.76%) and a 7.65-point margin over nnUNet (73.45%). For domain generalization (Table 2), MedCLIPSeg attains the best DSC on every source–target pair, e.g., 75.06% on BUSI→BUSBRA vs. 70.94% for CAT-Seg, and 71.90% on Kvasir-SEG→CVC-ColonDB vs. 68.49% for CAT-Seg.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedCLIPSeg outperforms all 18 baselines on both DSC and NSD across every data fraction. Among CLIP-based methods, the closest competitor is CAT-Seg [16], which MedCLIPSeg surpasses by 2.34–3.72 DSC points (e.g., 81.10 vs. 78.76 at 10% data; 88.66 vs. 85.90 at 100%). MaPLe+Decoder [42] and CLIPSeg [51] trail by 3.7–6.4 DSC points, while prompt-based methods like MaPLe [42] (74.60 DSC at 100%) and ZegCLIP [86] (78.98) lag further behind. Among unimodal baselines, nnUNet [34] is the strongest (81.40 DSC at 100%), still 7.26 points below MedCLIPSeg; transformer-based UNets (Swin-UNet [9], TransUNet [11]) perform worst overall (65–67 DSC). Text-driven methods LViT [50] (83.35) and Ariadne's Thread [81] (70.07) are consistently outperformed, with Ariadne's Thread showing the largest gap (18.59 DSC at 100%). In domain generalization (Table 2), MedCLIPSeg leads every source–target pair, with the largest margins over CausalCLIPSeg [13] (e.g., 71.90 vs. 41.65 on Kvasir-SEG→CVC-ColonDB) and CAT-Seg [16] (e.g., 75.06 vs. 70.94 on BUSI→BUSBRA), confirming that probabilistic cross-modal fusion yields substantially better OOD robustness than deterministic or unidirectional alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedCLIPSeg extends the CLIP-based medical segmentation line (CLIPSeg, DenseCLIP, ZegCLIP, SAN, CAT-Seg, VLSM-Adapter) by replacing their deterministic, often unidirectional text-to-vision modulation with a bidirectional Probabilistic Vision-Language (PVL) Adapter that models Keys and Values as learned Gaussian distributions, drawing on Kendall & Gal's heteroscedastic uncertainty framework. It also departs from prior prompt-learning and decoder-tuning approaches by operating at the representation level across multiple deep CLIP layers while keeping all pretrained parameters frozen, and introduces a soft patch-level contrastive loss that uses text-text similarity as soft targets to handle diverse clinical descriptions. The work sits at the intersection of variational inference, vision-language pretraining, and medical segmentation, filling a gap where probabilistic CLIP adaptation had been explored for classification (CLAP4CLIP, ProbVLM) but not for dense, text-guided segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical trust gap in clinical AI: deterministic segmentation networks are systematically over-confident on out-of-distribution inputs, yet clinicians need calibrated reliability signals. By producing pixel-level uncertainty maps via Monte Carlo sampling of learned Value distributions, MedCLIPSeg offers interpretable confidence that could support human-in-the-loop review. Open questions include whether the 30-sample MC test-time cost is acceptable in real-time clinical workflows, whether the approach extends naturally to 3D volumetric segmentation, and whether the soft contrastive loss generalizes to open-vocabulary or few-shot settings beyond the supervised regime tested here.
