## Problem

Existing instruction-guided image editing models and datasets focus almost exclusively on static appearance edits (color, texture, object replacement), leaving motion-centric edits—modifying subject actions, poses, or interactions—largely unaddressed. The few datasets that include motion edits provide sparse, low-quality ground-truth targets that are unfaithful, physically implausible, or visually inconsistent, undermining both training supervision and evaluation reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262808/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes an RGB input image and a natural-language motion edit instruction (e.g., 'make the woman turn her head toward the dog') and produces a single edited image where the subject's action or interaction has changed while identity, background, and viewpoint remain consistent. The dataset is constructed from 3-second video segments (sourced from Veo-3 and KlingAI T2V models), where the first and last frames of each segment form the input-target pair, ensuring that motion transitions are grounded in real video kinematics rather than synthesized. Each of the 10,157 samples spans six motion categories: pose/posture, locomotion/distance, object state/formation, orientation/viewpoint, subject-object interaction, and inter-subject interaction.

## Method

MotionNFT extends the DiffusionNFT post-training framework by replacing the generic reward with a motion-aware optical-flow-based reward computed via UniMatch. The reward decomposes into three terms: a robust L1 motion magnitude consistency score, a cosine-based directional alignment score weighted by ground-truth flow magnitude, and a movement regularization penalty that prevents trivial near-static edits. This composite motion reward is combined 50/50 with an MLLM-based reward (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) to preserve general editing quality while providing targeted spatial guidance. Training follows the DiffusionNFT objective, which contrasts positive and negative velocity fields in the flow-matching forward process, using the motion reward to assign optimality scores that steer the model toward accurate motion transformations. Applied to FLUX.1 Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit, MotionNFT yields over 10% improvement in overall quality and over 12% in pairwise win rate on MotionEdit-Bench without degrading general editing performance on ImgEdit-Bench.

## Results

On MotionEdit-Bench, MotionNFT applied to FLUX.1 Kontext raises the Overall MLLM score from 3.84 to 4.25 (+10.7%), MAS from 53.73 to 55.45, and pairwise Win Rate from 57.71% to 64.95%. For Qwen-Image-Edit, MotionNFT improves Overall from 4.65 to 4.72, MAS from 56.46 to 57.23, and Win Rate from 72.80% to 73.67%. The MotionNFT-enhanced Qwen-Image-Edit achieves the best scores across all six metrics (Overall 4.72, Fidelity 4.79, Preservation 4.63, Coherence 4.74, MAS 57.23, Win Rate 73.67%), while the MotionNFT-enhanced FLUX.1 Kontext leads among the remaining models on Overall (4.25) and Win Rate (64.95%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine evaluated baselines, Qwen-Image-Edit is the strongest single model (Overall 4.65, MAS 56.46, Win Rate 72.80%), followed by BAGEL (Overall 4.10) and Step1X-Edit (Overall 4.02); all other baselines (Instruct-P2P, AnyEdit, MagicBrush, UltraEdit, UniWorld-V1) score substantially lower, with Overall scores between 1.30 and 2.87. MotionNFT on FLUX.1 Kontext surpasses all non-Qwen baselines on Overall (4.25 vs. BAGEL's 4.10) and Win Rate (64.95% vs. BAGEL's 61.46%), and also exceeds UniWorld-V1's MAS (55.45 vs. 55.37) despite UniWorld-V1's higher Overall generative score. MotionNFT on Qwen-Image-Edit edges out the base model on every metric (Overall +0.07, MAS +0.77, Win Rate +0.87 pp), confirming that the optical-flow reward provides incremental motion-specific guidance beyond what the MLLM reward alone captures. In the ablation against UniWorld-V2 (MLLM-only reward, Table 3), MotionNFT consistently wins on Overall, MAS, and Win Rate for both base models, e.g., FLUX.1 Kontext +MotionNFT achieves MAS 55.45 vs. 54.58 for +UniWorld-V2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DiffusionNFT (negative-aware fine-tuning) framework for flow-matching models by introducing an optical-flow-based motion alignment reward, building on UniMatch for flow estimation and on the MLLM-based reward pipeline from UniWorld-V2. It departs from prior image editing datasets (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, OmniEdit, UltraEdit) that either ignore motion edits entirely or provide low-quality, unfaithful motion ground truth, instead sourcing paired frames from T2V-generated videos (Veo-3, KlingAI) to obtain physically coherent motion transitions. The video-driven data construction pipeline with MLLM-based filtering and prompt rewriting is a methodological departure from the synthetic-edit or manual-annotation approaches used in prior editing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Motion editing is a practical bottleneck for frame-controlled video synthesis and character animation, yet no prior dataset or benchmark provided reliable supervision for it; MotionEdit fills this gap with 10K+ video-sourced pairs and a dedicated evaluation protocol (MAS + MLLM generative scores + pairwise win rate). The finding that even strong SOTA editors (FLUX.1 Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) score only ~54–57 on MAS and struggle qualitatively with basic pose changes underscores how far current models are from handling articulated motion. Open questions include whether the optical-flow reward generalizes to multi-frame or 3D-consistent motion, whether the T2V-sourced data introduces subtle distributional biases absent in real-world video, and whether the modest Qwen-Image-Edit gains (~1% overall) indicate a ceiling for flow-based supervision on already-strong models.
