## Problem

Existing robot state representations face a fundamental trade-off: motion-centric encodings (trajectories, flow fields, latent actions) are compact but semantically impoverished, while image-based state encodings are expressive yet too high-dimensional for efficient planning and action generation. Furthermore, the dominant paradigm for learning latent actions relies on video data with complex temporal models, which is computationally expensive and produces ambiguous, averaged-out motion representations due to high intra-clip variance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes single static RGB images (no video sequences required) and compresses each into just 2 tokens of 1024 dimensions (2048 total), a compression ratio orders of magnitude beyond typical feature maps (e.g., 256×1024 from DINOv2). Motion is extracted as the simple vector difference between two such state tokens from a start and goal image, yielding a latent action of the same 2×1024 shape. When integrated into VLA models, the system additionally accepts text instructions and outputs 7-DoF end-effector actions alongside predicted future state tokens, all without decoding full images at inference time.

## Method

StaMo is a Diffusion Autoencoder where a frozen DINOv2 encoder feeds a trainable transformer-based compressor that maps images to 2 compact tokens, and a DiT decoder (initialized from Stable Diffusion 3's pretrained weights) reconstructs the image via a Flow Matching objective. The critical insight is that linear interpolation between two state tokens in this compressed latent space produces smooth, physically plausible motion trajectories, and the token difference a_t = s_{t+1} − s_t serves as a meaningful latent action without any explicit motion supervision. For world modeling, a lightweight MLP head is attached to OpenVLA or OpenVLA-OFT to jointly predict next-token actions and the future StaMo state under a composite MSE+L1 loss. For policy co-training, state differences computed from consecutive video frames generate pseudo-action labels that are jointly trained with ground-truth robot actions in a single RDT-based policy, enabling scalable learning from unlabeled video sources including human egocentric data.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, OpenVLA*+StaMo state achieves 87.6% average success rate, improving over the base OpenVLA (76.5%) by 11.1 percentage points, while OpenVLA-OFT*+StaMo state reaches 96.8% average, surpassing the base OpenVLA-OFT (92.2%) by 4.6 points. In real-world deployment, OpenVLA+StaMo state achieves 0.56 average success rate across six manipulation tasks, a 31% improvement over OpenVLA (0.25). For policy co-training with only 1 real trajectory plus 4 video trajectories, StaMo pseudo-actions yield 84.6% average success, outperforming LAPA (74.2%) and ATM (73.4%) baselines by over 10 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StaMo consistently outperforms all compared world-modeling VLA baselines on LIBERO. Against the closest competitor UniVLA (96.5% avg), OpenVLA-OFT*+StaMo state edges ahead at 96.8%, while on the shorter-horizon OpenVLA backbone, StaMo (87.6–88.1%) surpasses π0-FAST (85.5%), CoT-VLA (83.9%), and WorldVLA (81.8%) by 2–6 points. The improvement over the base OpenVLA (76.5%) is +11.1% with state and +11.6% with motion, substantially larger than the +4.4% gain from simply swapping in DINOv2 features (80.9%). In policy co-training (Table 5), StaMo pseudo-actions (84.6% avg) beat LAPA (74.2%) by 10.4 points and ATM (73.4%) by 11.2 points, approaching the full-data RDT upper bound (86.2%). For zero-shot transfer to ManiSkill, OpenVLA+StaMo state (41/32/48/36%) dramatically outperforms OpenVLA (28/4/18/16%), UniVLA (32/7/22/20%), and WorldVLA (30/10/17/22%). Inference speed is preserved: OpenVLA+StaMo runs at 4.02 Hz versus 4.16 Hz for vanilla OpenVLA, while WorldVLA and UniVLA drop to 2.27 and 2.65 Hz respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

StaMo builds on the Stable Diffusion 3 / DiT architecture (Esser et al., ICML 2024) by repurposing its pretrained diffusion decoder as a generative prior for a robotics-specific autoencoder, paired with a frozen DINOv2 encoder and a lightweight transformer compressor. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant latent-action learning paradigm (LAPA, ATM, UniVLA, etc.) that extracts actions from video sequences, instead showing that a compact state representation learned from individual static images yields motion simply as the vector difference between two states. It also positions itself against prior world-modeling VLA approaches (WorldVLA, UniVLA) that require decoding full images or use high-dimensional state tokens, offering a 2-token alternative that integrates into OpenVLA/OFT with minimal inference overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that physically plausible robot motion emerges from subtracting two compact state tokens—without any video data or explicit motion supervision—challenges the field's assumption that temporal modeling is necessary for latent action learning, potentially unlocking scalable pretraining from the vast pool of static images and egocentric video. The practical impact is significant: +11.6% on LIBERO and +31% real-world success with near-zero inference cost, plus demonstrated zero-shot transfer to unseen environments and cross-embodiment generalization. Open questions include whether the linear structure of the latent motion space holds for more complex, non-rigid, or multi-agent dynamics, and how far the approach scales to long-horizon tasks requiring hierarchical planning beyond the current single-step state prediction.
