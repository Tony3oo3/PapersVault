## Problem

Existing multi-view crowd tracking methods are predominantly CNN-based and evaluated on small-scale datasets (Wildtrack, MultiviewX) with limited scene coverage (~36×12 m) and short sequences (~400 frames), making them ill-suited for real-world scenarios involving large scenes, dense crowds, and long tracking durations. No transformer-based architecture has been explored for this task, and no benchmark exists that reflects the spatial and temporal complexity of practical deployments.

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized multi-view RGB frames (7 views at 5312×2988 for MVCrowdTrack, 3 views at 2704×1520 for CityTrack) resized to 1280×720, along with calibrated intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters used for ground-plane projection. It outputs per-frame ground-plane crowd location heatmaps (1200×800 or 640×768 pixels, 1 px = 0.1 m) and per-track motion offsets (δx, δy) that are accumulated over time to form continuous trajectories. A 2D camera-view heatmap branch provides auxiliary supervision during training.

## Method

MVTrackTrans is a three-stage transformer pipeline operating in bird's-eye-view (BEV) space. A shared ResNet18 backbone extracts multi-scale features from each view, which are projected onto the ground plane via multi-height bilinear sampling and fused across views. A Deformable DETR-style encoder processes previous- and current-frame ground features; discrete track queries are sampled from the previous frame's encoded representation at known detection locations. The key novelty is a View-Ground Interaction module that uses cross-attention to let each track query attend to concatenated multi-camera view queries, thereby recovering per-person appearance cues that ground-plane sampling alone cannot capture. Decoding proceeds in two parallel branches: an MSDA-based offset decoder regresses temporal displacement, and an FPN+ConvHead heatmap decoder predicts the current-frame crowd center map. Training uses focal loss on ground and image heatmaps, L1 loss on offsets, and learnable uncertainty weights to balance the branches.

## Results

On the newly proposed MVCrowdTrack dataset, MVTrackTrans achieves MOTA 63.87, MOTP 40.59, IDF1 59.06, MT 42.85, and ML 8.16, outperforming all three baselines. On CityTrack, it reaches MOTA 55.39, MOTP 22.71, IDF1 34.41, MT 25.07, and ML 12.69, again ranking first on MOTA, IDF1, and MT. On the smaller Wildtrack benchmark, the model attains MOTA 91.2 and IDF1 94.1 (93.6/96.7 with the two-frame temporal fusion + Kalman filter variant), while on MultiviewX it scores MOTA 89.8 and IDF1 72.1 (90.2/86.3 with the variant).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the two new large-scale datasets, MVTrackTrans consistently outperforms all three directly comparable baselines. On MVCrowdTrack, it beats TrackTacular (the strongest CNN-based competitor) by +1.01 MOTA and +0.35 IDF1, and surpasses EarlyBird by +9.31 MOTA and +5.22 IDF1; MVFlow performs worst due to its weakly-supervised motion model. On CityTrack, the gap widens: MVTrackTrans leads TrackTacular by +12.02 MOTA and +1.92 IDF1, and EarlyBird by +6.54 MOTA and +2.26 IDF1. On the smaller Wildtrack benchmark, MVTrackTrans (MOTA 91.2, IDF1 94.1) beats ReST (81.6/85.7), MCBLT (87.5/93.4), EarlyBird (89.5/92.3), and REMP (88.5/–), but trails MVTrajecter (94.3/96.5) and MVTr (92.3/93.1); with the two-frame temporal fusion + Kalman filter variant it reaches 93.6/96.7, matching or exceeding all prior methods. On MultiviewX, the base model (MOTA 89.8, IDF1 72.1) lags behind MVTrajecter (92.8/85.8), TrackTacular (92.4/85.6), and MVTr (91.4/82.9), though the variant (90.2/86.3) narrows the gap substantially and surpasses EarlyBird (88.4/82.4). Overall, the transformer architecture's advantage is most pronounced on the larger, more occluded real-world scenes, while on small synthetic or short-sequence datasets the performance is competitive but not uniformly dominant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Deformable DETR encoder-decoder architecture and the multi-height bilinear sampling strategy from Simple-BEV for projecting single-view features onto a ground plane. It departs from the dominant CNN-based paradigm in multi-view crowd tracking (EarlyBird, MVFlow, TrackTacular) by introducing a Transformer-based model that operates in BEV space, and adds a View-Ground Interaction module that fuses camera-view queries with ground-plane track queries via cross-attention. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by introducing two large-scale real-world datasets (MVCrowdTrack, CityTrack) that are substantially larger in scene size, temporal length, and crowd density than the previously standard Wildtrack and MultiviewX benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two new datasets address a real gap: prior multi-view tracking research has been largely confined to small, short sequences that do not reflect the scale and occlusion complexity of practical deployments, so these benchmarks should become the default for evaluating methods under realistic conditions. A notable limitation is that MVTrackTrans does not outperform all SOTAs on the smaller Wildtrack/MultiviewX datasets (MVTrajecter remains ahead), suggesting the Transformer advantage is primarily realized at scale rather than as a universal improvement. Open questions include whether the view-ground interaction generalizes to even larger scenes with more cameras, and whether the dense heatmap supervision requirement can be relaxed for more efficient training.
