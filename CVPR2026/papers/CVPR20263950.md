## Problem

Existing flow-based GRPO methods (e.g., DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO) inject SDE stochasticity at every denoising step and uniformly broadcast the final image reward to all steps, resulting in sparse, imprecise credit assignment. Additionally, each sampled denoising direction is rigidly bound to a fixed number of subsequent ODE steps, so the group advantage reflects only a single denoising granularity and cannot robustly assess whether a direction is genuinely superior or merely favorable at one particular step count.

## Input / Output

During training, a shared Gaussian noise x_T and a text prompt c are used to generate a group of G=12 images via a 16-step denoising trajectory. At one randomly selected timestep k (from the first half of steps), SDE sampling branches the trajectory into G distinct directions; all other steps use deterministic ODE sampling. Each direction is then denoised to completion at J different granularities (Λ={1,2,3}, meaning every 1st, 2nd, or 3rd step is taken), yielding J×G final images whose reward scores are fused into a single mixed advantage per direction.

## Method

G²RPO introduces two mechanisms to produce dense and comprehensive reward signals for flow-model GRPO training. First, Singular Stochastic Sampling confines SDE noise injection to a single timestep k while using ODE sampling everywhere else, so the entire group reward variance is attributable to the one stochastic perturbation at step k, enabling step-aware advantage computation (Eq. 8) and allowing the G samples to share the same ODE velocity v_k for efficiency. Second, Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration (MGAI) denoises each of the G sampled directions to completion at multiple step-interval granularities defined by scaling factors λ∈Λ, computes intra-group advantages at each granularity, and sums them (Eq. 11) to form a mixed advantage that is robust to the arbitrary choice of denoising step count. The policy is then optimized with the standard clipped GRPO objective (Eq. 12) using the mixed advantage, jointly trained with HPS-v2.1 and CLIP Score reward models on the Flux.1-dev backbone.

## Results

Under the primary multi-reward (HPS-v2.1 + CLIP) training setting, G²RPO achieves the best scores across all five evaluation metrics: HPS 0.376, CLIP 0.406, PS 0.235, IR 1.483, and UR 3.783. In the single-reward (HPS-only) setting, G²RPO reaches HPS 0.385, a 6.52% relative improvement over DanceGRPO (0.353). The method also demonstrates robustness to inference step count, maintaining top performance at both 10 and 20 denoising steps (e.g., UR 3.805 at 10 steps vs. 3.806 at 20 steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

G²RPO outperforms both DanceGRPO and MixGRPO across all five metrics in the primary HPS+CLIP joint training setting. Against DanceGRPO, G²RPO gains +0.045 on HPS, +0.017 on CLIP, +0.008 on PS, +0.355 on IR, and +0.214 on UR. Against MixGRPO (the stronger baseline), the margins are +0.013 on HPS, +0.007 on CLIP, +0.005 on PS, +0.047 on IR, and +0.122 on UR. The largest relative gains appear on out-of-domain metrics (IR and UR), suggesting that the Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration module improves generalization beyond the training reward models. In the single-reward (HPS-only) setting, G²RPO also leads on HPS (0.385 vs. 0.378 MixGRPO, 0.353 DanceGRPO) but shows a slight drop on CLIP (0.355 vs. 0.388 base), consistent with the known model-hacking behavior of HPS-v2.1 noted by DanceGRPO. The ablation variant G²RPO w/o MGAI already surpasses both baselines, confirming that Singular Stochastic Sampling alone provides a meaningful improvement, with MGAI adding further gains particularly on out-of-domain metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based GRPO lineage initiated by Flow-GRPO and DanceGRPO, which convert deterministic ODE sampling into SDE sampling to enable stochastic exploration for RL, and builds on MixGRPO's hybrid ODE-SDE efficiency improvements. The key departure is twofold: (1) Singular Stochastic Sampling confines SDE noise injection to a single timestep rather than all steps, directly addressing the sparse-reward credit-assignment problem that plagues multi-step stochasticity; and (2) Multi-Granularity Advantage Integration evaluates each sampled direction across multiple denoising step counts, moving beyond the fixed-granularity evaluation that biases advantage estimates in prior methods. Conceptually, the work draws an analogy to sparse-reward RLHF in LLMs, where delayed terminal rewards hinder per-step credit assignment, and applies a targeted solution within the flow-matching denoising pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete weakness in current flow-based RL alignment—imprecise and incomplete reward signals—that limits the optimization ceiling of GRPO training. The multi-granularity evaluation is a practical and generalizable idea that could transfer to other iterative generative processes (e.g., video diffusion). Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (Flux.1-dev) with a fixed 16-step schedule, and the singular-stochastic-sampling strategy inherently restricts exploration to one timestep per group, which may under-explore later denoising stages. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether the granularity set Λ should be learned rather than fixed, and how the method interacts with longer-horizon or multi-turn preference signals.
