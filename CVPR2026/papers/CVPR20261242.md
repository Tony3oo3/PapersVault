## Problem

Existing burst image restoration methods rely on manually designed or fixed exposure settings (uniform across frames or predefined brackets), which cannot adapt to varying scene dynamics and lighting conditions. The problem of predicting optimal per-frame exposure times that maximize restoration quality has been largely overlooked, leaving a gap between the exposure choices and the complementary information needed for effective burst fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261242/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

BAENet takes a single preview RAW image (captured at a fixed 1/120 s exposure), its gain g_p, and a scalar motion magnitude m_p (mean optical-flow magnitude between consecutive preview frames) as input, and outputs n exposure times constrained by a bounded softmax so their sum does not exceed a predefined upper bound t_u. The gains are then derived as g_i = ke/t_i to keep all burst images at the same brightness. The restoration network (a Burstormer variant) consumes n Bayer-pattern RAW burst images and produces a single restored clean RAW image at 256×256 resolution.

## Method

DEBIR couples a lightweight MobileNetV2-based exposure predictor (BAENet) with a Burstormer-based restoration network, trained jointly via a novel differentiable burst simulator that synthesizes degraded RAW images with realistic shot/read noise and motion blur while remaining differentiable with respect to exposure times (using linear interpolation for scene-radiance integration and the reparameterization trick for heteroscedastic Gaussian noise). A three-stage alternating training strategy—pre-training the restoration network with random exposures, training BAENet first with a pseudo-ground-truth warm-up loss and then with the end-to-end restoration loss, and finally fine-tuning the restoration network—avoids the instability of joint end-to-end optimization. The bounded softmax output layer ensures physically valid exposure times, and the per-frame non-uniform exposures deliberately create complementary noise and blur levels across the burst to maximize the information available for fusion.

## Results

On the synthetic test set, DEBIR achieves 35.32 dB PSNR, 0.9519 SSIM, and 0.154 LPIPS, outperforming all five baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, Exposure Bracket [15,55], trails by 0.28 dB in PSNR (35.04) and 0.0038 in SSIM (0.9481). On a real-world dual-camera system evaluated with no-reference metrics, DEBIR also wins across the board: NIQE 6.34 vs. 6.57, BRISQUE 46.90 vs. 50.66, and TOPIQ 0.363 vs. 0.339 compared to Exposure Bracket.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEBIR outperforms all five baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. The two classical auto-exposure methods (Average AE and Gradient AE) predict a single exposure time applied uniformly to all burst frames, yielding similar noise and blur levels across frames and limiting complementary information; they score 34.69 and 34.86 dB PSNR respectively, roughly 0.5–0.6 dB below DEBIR. Exposure Bracket [15,55] uses predefined non-uniform exposures ({8,24,40,56}/1920 s) and is the strongest baseline at 35.04 dB PSNR, but its fixed schedule cannot adapt to scene dynamics, leaving a 0.28 dB gap. Among learning-based methods, Digital-Gimbal [10] (33.87 dB) is limited because its learnable exposure parameters are frozen after training regardless of the scene, while Active S-L [50] (33.89 dB) is architecturally restricted to two images and cannot scale to longer bursts. In the real-world camera evaluation, DEBIR improves over Exposure Bracket by 0.23 NIQE, 3.76 BRISQUE, and 0.024 TOPIQ, confirming that adaptive per-frame exposure prediction generalizes beyond simulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Burstormer (CVPR 2023) as the restoration backbone and extends the burst imaging literature, which has predominantly focused on alignment and fusion under uniform exposure settings. It departs from prior auto-exposure work (which targets brightness only) and from fixed exposure-bracketing approaches [15, 55] by learning per-frame exposure times conditioned on scene content, motion, and noise level. The key methodological departure is the differentiable burst simulator, which enables backpropagation of the restoration loss through the exposure prediction network—something prior learnable-exposure methods like Digital-Gimbal [10] (fixed parameters) and Active S-L [50] (classification over a discrete set) could not achieve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEBIR is the first pipeline to jointly optimize per-frame exposure prediction and burst restoration, validated on a real dual-camera system, which makes it practically deployable in consumer imaging. The differentiable burst simulator and three-stage training strategy are reusable components for any pipeline where a front-end decision (exposure, shutter, gain) must be optimized against a downstream restoration objective. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge BAENet may converge to local minima rather than truly optimal exposure sequences, and the approach is limited to low-light RAW burst scenarios; extending to joint prediction of exposure times and burst size under power constraints is identified as future work.
