## Problem

Existing LM-based trajectory prediction methods (e.g., LMTraj, GUIDE-CoT) suffer from an objective mismatch: they optimize token-level cross-entropy on serialized coordinates rather than the actual trajectory metrics (ADE/FDE) or scene compliance. Additionally, their textual prompts encode only discretized coordinates and pedestrian IDs without explicit interaction semantics, limiting the LM's ability to leverage its priors about human social behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263310/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a structured text prompt that serializes the target pedestrian's 8-frame observed 2D coordinates in a fixed template, augmented with interaction labels (companion/following/obstacle) derived from distance, heading, and bearing heuristics over the observation window. The output is a fixed-syntax text string encoding 12 future 2D coordinate pairs, deterministically parsed back via a regex-based parser with a strict one-to-one text-to-coordinate mapping. The scene semantic mask is not part of the textual input but is queried during RL to compute off-road penalties in the reward.

## Method

W2W casts trajectory prediction as sequence-to-sequence text generation with a T5-Small backbone, trained in two stages. Stage 1 performs full-parameter supervised fine-tuning with cross-entropy to teach the model to produce strictly formatted, parseable trajectory text conditioned on interaction-aware prompts. Stage 2 freezes the backbone and applies LoRA-adapted PPO, optimizing a composite programmatic reward that sums negative ADE (accuracy term) and a binary off-road penalty (scene-compliance term) computed from a semantic mask, with token-level KL clipping against the SFT reference policy. The key design choice is separating scene constraints into the optimization objective rather than the prompt, avoiding token-length bloat while directly aligning the LM with non-differentiable trajectory metrics.

## Results

On ETH/UCY (leave-one-out, K=20), W2W achieves an average minADE_K of 0.21 and minFDE_K of 0.29, placing it among the top-performing methods across both deep learning and LM-based categories. On SDD, W2W records 7.42/10.13 ADE/FDE, competitive with the strongest baselines. The RL stage further reduces the off-road rate (ORR) from 11.30% to 8.85% while maintaining near-perfect format execution rate (FER ≈ 100%), demonstrating improved scene compliance without sacrificing output parsability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among LM-based methods, W2W outperforms LMTraj-SUP (0.22/0.32 → 0.21/0.29 on ETH/UCY; 7.8/10.1 → 7.42/10.13 on SDD) and GUIDE-CoT (0.24/0.31) on both ADE and FDE, but trails VLMTRaj-SUP (0.18/0.27 on ETH/UCY; 7.4/10.3 on SDD) by a small margin on both datasets. Against deep learning baselines, W2W's ETH/UCY ADE of 0.21 ties SocialVAE and is within 0.01 of PPT, LG-Traj, and MoFlow (all 0.20), while its FDE of 0.29 is the best among all listed methods, beating SocialVAE (0.33), PPT (0.31), Fuseformer (0.32), and MoFlow (0.32). On SDD, W2W's ADE of 7.42 is slightly worse than PPT (7.03) and VLMTRaj-SUP (7.4) but better than MoFlow (7.50) and LMTraj-SUP (7.8); its FDE of 10.13 is the best overall, edging out LMTraj-SUP (10.1) and VLMTRaj-SUP (10.3). The paper also reports that the RL stage reduces the off-road rate by 21.7% relative to SFT-only, a scene-compliance metric not reported by any baseline, highlighting W2W's unique advantage in safety-critical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

W2W extends the LM-based trajectory prediction line initiated by LMTraj and continued by GUIDE-COT and VLMTraj, which all rely on cross-entropy supervised fine-tuning of a language model to generate serialized coordinates. The key departure is a two-stage training paradigm: after full-parameter SFT (which prior LM-based methods also use), W2W adds a PPO+LoRA reinforcement-learning stage that directly optimizes a programmatic reward combining ADE error and off-road penalties, explicitly addressing the objective mismatch between token-level likelihood and trajectory accuracy. Additionally, W2W introduces behavior-aware textualization that encodes three interaction types (companion, following, obstacle) into the prompt, whereas prior LM-based methods primarily serialize raw coordinates and pedestrian IDs without explicit social semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL alignment can close part of the gap between language-model training objectives and trajectory-prediction metrics while simultaneously improving scene compliance (21.7% reduction in off-road rate), offering a practical path toward interpretable, safety-aware prediction for autonomous driving. However, W2W does not clearly surpass the strongest LM-based baseline (VLMTRaj-SUP achieves 0.18/0.27 ADE/FDE on ETH/UCY vs. W2W's 0.21/0.29), and the RL stage yields only modest accuracy gains (2.8% ADE, 5.3% FDE) at roughly 4× the training time of SFT. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger LMs, whether the interaction heuristics generalize beyond the three predefined types, and whether the off-road penalty can be extended to richer scene representations beyond a binary mask.
