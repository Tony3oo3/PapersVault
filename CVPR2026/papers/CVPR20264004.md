## Problem

Curvilinear structure segmentation (e.g., vessels, roads, cracks) requires reconciling fine-grained local detail with long-range topological coherence, yet existing methods either produce fragmented segments or fail to generalize across domains without large annotated datasets. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) offers strong zero-shot generalization but is ill-suited for CSS: its discrete prompting mechanism is impractical for dense, tortuous networks, and prior adapter-based fine-tuning only refines block-internal local features while neglecting global structural modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264004/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SACM accepts a single image of any curvilinear modality (fundus, X-ray angiography, RGB, aerial, or microscopy) at arbitrary resolution and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask of the target curvilinear structure (threshold 0.5) without any user-provided point, box, or mask prompt. Training requires only 3 annotated images per dataset (18 total across six source datasets), and the in-house evaluation images are 1024×768 RGB captures annotated with pixel-level masks.

## Method

SACM builds on a frozen SAM ViT-L encoder and introduces a Dual-Level Adapter (DLAda) architecture: block-internal adapters (Adapter-I) are embedded in each Transformer block's MLP residual path for token-wise local feature refinement, while block-external adapters (Adapter-E) sit in the residual connection around each full block, injecting global, attention-mixed feature updates that propagate across layers to capture long-range dependencies. A Prompt-Free Adapter-Fusion Decoder (PFAF-D) replaces SAM's prompt mechanism by aggregating multi-layer external adapter outputs via learned softmax-weighted fusion and injecting the result into the mask decoder through a residual connection. The decoder then applies a dual-stage refinement: Stage-1 generates coarse masks and ranks prediction heads by confidence, and Stage-2 produces refined masks conditioned on the ranking, balancing boundary precision with topological connectivity. Only the adapters and decoder are trained (bottleneck ratio r=0.1) with a combined BCE + Dice loss (λ=0.4) for 50 epochs, keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

On the four Base-Seen datasets (DRIVE, CHASEDB1, DCA1, CORN), SACM achieves Dice scores of 78.89%, 79.27%, 75.67%, and 55.38% respectively, outperforming the best CNN baseline (BCUNet) by 0.81–18.15 points and the best SAM-based baseline (CWSAM) by 17.86–28.31 points. On unseen Base datasets (DSCA, CRACK, XCAD, FIVES), SACM reaches Dice of 68.43%, 63.17%, 74.29%, and 75.48%, surpassing the strongest prompt-based SAM adaptation (SAM-OCTA) by 2.78–10.87 points. On entirely novel structure classes (ROAD, LEAF, TYRE, WIRE), SACM achieves Dice of 40.43%, 36.80%, 37.43%, and 54.60%, exceeding the best competing method by 15.64–10.40 points. All results are obtained with only 18 training images (3-shot per dataset) and no prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SACM (prompt-free) decisively outperforms all ten baselines across all four Base-Seen datasets. Against the strongest CNN baseline BCUNet, SACM gains +0.81 Dice on DRIVE (78.89 vs 78.08), +1.03 on CHASEDB1 (79.27 vs 78.24), +2.32 on DCA1 (75.67 vs 73.35), and a substantial +25.62 on CORN (55.38 vs 29.76). Against the best prompt-based SAM adaptation CWSAM, SACM leads by +17.88 Dice on DRIVE, +24.42 on CHASEDB1, +11.98 on DCA1, and +28.31 on CORN, while also requiring no prompts. On unseen Base datasets, SACM surpasses SAM-OCTA (the strongest prompt-based competitor) by +2.78 Dice on DSCA, +10.87 on CRACK, +5.71 on XCAD, and +0.97 on FIVES. On entirely novel structure classes (ROAD, LEAF, TYRE, WIRE), SACM leads the best baseline by +15.64, +8.95, +4.97, and +10.40 Dice respectively, confirming that the dual-level adapter transfers curvilinear priors to structure types never seen during training. Notably, all SAM-based prompt methods (SAM-Med2D, SAM-OCTA, CWSAM, KnowSAM) suffer severe HD95 degradation (often >80 px) on Base-Seen data, whereas SACM maintains HD95 below 17 px, reflecting its superior boundary precision without prompt-induced artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SACM builds directly on the SAM ViT-L encoder as a frozen backbone, extending the line of SAM-based adaptation work (SAM-Med2D, CWSAM, SAM-OCTA) that inserts lightweight adapter modules into Transformer blocks. The key departure is the dual-level adapter design: whereas prior methods place a single bottleneck adapter inside the MLP sub-layer of each block, SACM adds a second adapter at the block boundary (external adapter) to capture cross-layer, attention-mixed global context, and an Adapter Fusion module that aggregates multi-layer external adapter outputs into the mask decoder. This positions the work at the intersection of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (adapter literature from NLP/vision) and prompt-free segmentation, explicitly arguing that SAM's prompting mechanism is ill-posed for thin, branching curvilinear structures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The prompt-free formulation removes a practical barrier for curvilinear segmentation, where dense, tortuous networks make point/box prompting infeasible, and the 18-image training budget makes the approach attractive for data-scarce scientific domains (retinal vessels, road networks, crack detection). However, absolute performance on truly novel structure classes (ROAD ~40% Dice, LEAF ~37% Dice) remains modest, and the method still inherits SAM's architectural biases. Open questions include whether the dual-level adapter design generalizes beyond curvilinear targets, whether continual adaptation can handle sequential domain shifts, and whether the prompt-free decoder can be made more efficient for real-time deployment.
