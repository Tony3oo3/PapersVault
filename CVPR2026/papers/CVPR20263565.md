## Problem

Diffusion and flow matching models learn only local velocity/score supervision, requiring dozens of sequential steps for high-quality generation. Existing few-step alternatives either depend on a pretrained teacher (distillation) or are unstable to optimize from scratch (consistency-based methods), leaving no unified framework that trains a high-quality text-to-image model from scratch while supporting arbitrary inference step counts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263565/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt as conditioning and produces a 512×512 RGB image (256×256 in ablations) via a latent transformer architecture similar to FLUX. Unusually, the network is parameterized with two timestep inputs (t, s) where s ≤ t, allowing it to predict a clean sample x̂₀ from a noisy input x_t while targeting an intermediate noise level s, which is what enables flexible any-step inference from 2 to 50+ steps within a single model.

## Method

Self-E trains a latent transformer G_θ(x_t, t, s, c) with a hybrid loss combining standard x₀-prediction (local flow matching supervision) and a self-evaluation term that enforces global distribution matching without a teacher. The self-evaluation works by re-noising the model's own generated sample x̂₀ to level s, then running the same network in stop-gradient mode twice—once with the text condition c and once with a null prompt φ—so that the difference approximates a classifier score via Tweedie's formula and CFG. This classifier score is back-propagated through the denoising path as a pseudo-target, effectively letting the model serve as its own dynamic teacher. An energy-preserving normalization of the combined regression target prevents color bias from large self-evaluation weights, and the relative weight λ_{s,t} = σ_t/α_t − σ_s/α_s naturally vanishes when s = t, reducing to pure data-driven training. At inference, the model iteratively denoises over any number of steps N by setting s_k to the next timestep, with CFG (ω=5) applied at each step.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, Self-E achieves an overall score of 0.753 at 2 steps, 0.781 at 4 steps, 0.785 at 8 steps, and 0.815 at 50 steps, improving monotonically with more inference steps. In the few-step regime, Self-E outperforms the second-best method (TiM) by +0.12 at 2 steps and +0.09 at 4 steps. At 50 steps, Self-E (0.815) surpasses SANA-1.5 (0.806) and FLUX.1-dev (0.80), demonstrating competitive quality with state-of-the-art flow-matching models despite being trained from scratch without a teacher.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-E outperforms all seven baselines on GenEval Overall at every step count tested (2, 4, 8, 50). In the critical few-step regime, the margins are substantial: at 2 steps, Self-E (0.753) beats TiM (0.634) by +0.12 and LCM (0.262) by +0.49; at 4 steps, it leads TiM (0.687) by +0.09. At 8 and 50 steps, Self-E edges out SANA-1.5 (0.779 and 0.806 respectively) by small margins (+0.006 and +0.009), while maintaining a clear lead over FLUX.1-dev (0.589 and 0.797) and TiM (0.714 and 0.780). Notably, standard flow-matching models (FLUX, SDXL, SANA) degrade severely at 2 steps (scores below 0.2), whereas Self-E retains strong performance, validating its any-step design. LCM, a distillation-based method, consistently underperforms across all step counts, confirming that the self-evaluation approach avoids the quality ceiling imposed by teacher distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

Self-E sits at the intersection of flow matching (local velocity supervision) and distribution-matching distillation (global distribution alignment), but eliminates the need for a pretrained teacher that both distillation-based methods (DMD, LCM) and large-scale consistency models (TiM, Align Your Flow) still require. The two-timestep parameterization (t, s) echoes consistency and flow-map models, but the paper explicitly argues it serves a fundamentally different purpose: rather than learning a specific flow map or average velocity, it directly predicts samples whose marginal distribution matches the data. The key conceptual departure is using the model's own in-training score estimate (via Tweedie's formula and classifier-free guidance) as a dynamic self-teacher for the global matching objective, drawing on the classifier-score distillation idea from 3D generation [63] and concurrent image work [28] but applying it natively during pretraining rather than as a post-hoc distillation step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a from-scratch model can achieve strong few-step generation (0.753 GenEval at 2 steps, +0.12 over TiM) while remaining competitive with 12B-parameter flow models at 50 steps (0.815 vs FLUX's 0.797), Self-E offers a unified, teacher-free path to any-step text-to-image generation that avoids the quality ceiling imposed by distillation. The monotonic improvement with inference steps within a single model is practically significant for deployment flexibility. Open questions include whether the self-evaluation mechanism transfers to video generation or other modalities, whether the auxiliary term can be introduced more principledly (its from-scratch inclusion is empirically harmful), and whether the approach scales beyond 2B parameters without the color-bias issues that motivated the energy-preserving normalization.
