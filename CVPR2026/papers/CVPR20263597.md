## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) remain brittle under minor environmental shifts such as small object pose changes, a fragility the paper attributes to trajectory overfitting—over-attending to spurious correlations between actions and visual entities rather than grounding in task semantics. Existing test-time adaptation methods either rely on expensive pretrained verifiers or on self-supervised confidence metrics (e.g., entropy minimization) that become unreliable when the model is miscalibrated, leaving a gap for a lightweight, verifier-free adaptation strategy that corrects overconfident decisions.

## Input / Output

At each decision step the method consumes an RGB pixel observation and a textual instruction (plus robot state in LIBERO), producing a sequence of action tokens whose softmax logits are detokenized into candidate actions; the final action is selected by dimension-wise majority voting across the original and up to N_t augmented views. The augmented views are generated by applying a set of pixel-level transformations whose count is scaled by the model's own predictive uncertainty, so the computational cost adapts per timestep. All base-model parameters (visual encoder, transformer, LM head) remain frozen; only a 9M-parameter perturbation head is updated, making the approach plug-and-play over any existing VLA.

## Method

PDF comprises two coupled mechanisms operating at test time. First, Uncertainty-Based Action Voting estimates normalized Shannon entropy over the action-token distribution to set an adaptive augmentation budget N_t = N_max · U_t; the original and augmented observations are jointly encoded, and their features are passed through both the frozen LM head and a learnable Perturbation head (P head) whose outputs are summed (z̃ = h_φ(f) + λ·h_θ(f)) before detokenization and majority voting. Second, Delayed Feedback-Guided Adaptation optimizes only the P head after each episode using a REINFORCE-style objective that increases the likelihood of actions from the perturbed policy when delayed feedback r exceeds a baseline b, combined with a KL regularizer gated by the indicator I[r > b] to stabilize updates under positive feedback while permitting exploration otherwise. This design breaks trajectory overfitting by diversifying the input distribution through augmentation and by retrospectively steering logit perturbations toward successful action directions, all without gradient access to the base VLA.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark (four 10-task suites), PDF achieves an average success rate of 0.77 with a mean rank of 2.5, the best among all compared methods. It reaches 0.90 on Spatial, 0.86 on Goal, and 0.59 on Long-horizon tasks, with the largest gain (+4.1 points) over the best prior baseline on Long. On Atari-57, PDF attains a Human-Normalized Score of 1.07 versus 0.97 for the Jat base model (+0.10), with 47 out of 57 games showing positive improvement and a mean per-game gain of 11.28%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDF outperforms the reproduced OpenVLA baseline (0.69 avg SR) by 8 points and the verifier-free TTA method MG-Select (0.71) by 6 points on LIBERO, demonstrating the advantage of uncertainty-weighted voting plus delayed-feedback perturbation over simple sample filtering. Against strong pretrained VLA variants that require fine-tuning—TraceVLA (0.75), SFT-4LIBERO (0.76), and OpenVLA-DPO (0.76)—PDF matches or exceeds them (0.77) while using only 9M trainable parameters versus their 130M and without any gradient access to the base model. On the Object suite, PDF (0.72) trails DP (0.92) and OpenVLA-DPO (0.89), indicating that object-centric manipulation remains a relative weakness. On Atari-57, PDF improves over the Jat base model from 0.97 to 1.07 HNS, with the largest per-game gains in BOXING (+60.25%), TIME PILOT (+53%), and ATLANTIS (+49%), though 10 games show degradation (worst: BATTLE ZONE, −10.72%). Overall, PDF is the only method that simultaneously achieves the best average SR and best mean rank on LIBERO while requiring no base-model fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a verifier-free test-time adaptation niche for VLAs, departing from both fine-tuning-based approaches (OpenVLA-DPO, SFT-4LIBERO) and verifier-based TTA (RoboMonkey, VGPS) that require expensive pretrained verifiers or repeated rollouts. It repurposes ideas from classical TTA (entropy-based uncertainty estimation, data augmentation for robustness) and policy gradient methods (REINFORCE-style objectives) into a lightweight 9M-parameter perturbation head that operates on frozen VLA features. The diagnostic framing of 'trajectory overfitting'—where VLAs replay memorized action traces rather than grounding in visual semantics—is a new conceptual lens that motivates the perturbation-based design, distinguishing PDF from prior self-supervised TTA that relies on entropy minimization alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDF offers a practically attractive recipe for improving VLA robustness: no base-model gradient access, only 9M trainable parameters, and no external verifier, making it deployable in settings where fine-tuning is infeasible. The cross-domain validation (LIBERO robotic manipulation and Atari-57 visual control) strengthens the claim that the approach is not domain-specific. However, the authors acknowledge that PDF alleviates rather than resolves trajectory overfitting, and the delayed-feedback mechanism is inherently episodic, limiting applicability to streaming or single-shot settings. Open questions include whether the perturbation head generalizes to continuous action spaces beyond tokenized actions, whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-task deployment, and whether the modest gains (+7.4% SR, +10.3 HNS) persist under more severe distribution shifts than those tested.
