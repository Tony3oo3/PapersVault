## Problem

Existing event-based 3D Gaussian Splatting methods render dense intensity twice per sample and compare the difference with pixel-wise accumulated events, creating a fundamental trade-off: short temporal windows miss subtle intensity changes that generate few events, while long windows produce blurry edge images that discard fine-grained temporal information. This paper addresses how to fully leverage the high temporal resolution of sparse events in 3DGS without this accuracy-versus-temporal-window dilemma.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw asynchronous event streams (pixel coordinates, timestamps, polarity) and known camera poses, targeting monochrome event cameras at VGA (640×480) or higher (e.g., 1280×720) resolution. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation (positions, covariances, colors, opacities) that supports novel view synthesis and depth rendering. Notably, the method requires no prior knowledge—no pretrained depth or video reconstruction models, no COLMAP initialization, and no additional frame data—making it applicable to the majority of modern event cameras.

## Method

GPERT decouples 3DGS rendering into two pathways connected by the Image of Warped Events (IWE): a spatially sparse, temporally dense event-by-event depth rendering via GPU-accelerated ray tracing against volumetric Gaussians, and a spatially dense, temporally sparse snapshot intensity rendering. The geometric loss applies Contrast Maximization to the IWE, using per-event depth to compute the motion field and warp events to a reference time, rewarding sharp motion-compensated edges. The photometric loss compares the IWE (with polarity) against the instantaneous brightness increment predicted by the rendered intensity and optical flow, using L2 and SSIM terms. This render-once design (one dense intensity render per optimization step, versus two in prior work) makes the method robust to the number of events processed per sample, and the IWE-based initialization places initial Gaussians near scene edges without external priors.

## Results

On the real-world EDS dataset, GPERT achieves the best average PSNR of 19.47 dB, SSIM of 0.816, and LPIPS of 0.357, outperforming the strongest baseline EventSplat (18.86 dB, 0.792, 0.362) and Robust E-NeRF (16.25 dB, 0.739, 0.543). On TUM-VIE, GPERT leads with 13.09 dB PSNR, 0.716 SSIM, and 0.411 LPIPS, surpassing Robust E-NeRF (11.79 dB, 0.573, 0.588) and IncEventGS (10.09 dB, 0.533, 0.685). On the synthetic color dataset, GPERT is competitive but trails EventSplat (28.14 dB) and Robust E-NeRF (28.19 dB) in PSNR, achieving 23.11 dB, attributed to the challenging Bayer pattern for warp-based methods. Training time is 30–45 min for EDS/synthetic and 80–130 min for TUM-VIE, significantly faster than the ~3 h reported for Robust E-NeRF and IncEventGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPERT outperforms all three baselines on both real-world datasets across all three metrics. Against EventSplat [8] (the strongest GS baseline), GPERT gains +0.61 dB PSNR and +0.024 SSIM on EDS while also being the only method evaluated on TUM-VIE among the GS baselines. Against Robust E-NeRF [14], the improvement is more substantial: +3.22 dB PSNR and +0.077 SSIM on EDS, and +1.30 dB PSNR and +0.143 SSIM on TUM-VIE, with LPIPS reduced from 0.543 to 0.357 (EDS) and 0.588 to 0.411 (TUM-VIE). Against IncEventGS [9], GPERT leads by +4.26 dB PSNR on EDS and +3.00 dB on TUM-VIE. Notably, GPERT achieves these results without any pretrained depth model, E2VID video reconstruction, or COLMAP initialization, whereas IncEventGS uses a depth-pretrained model and EventSplat relies on E2VID for initialization. On the synthetic color dataset, GPERT (23.11 dB) trails both EventSplat (28.14 dB) and Robust E-NeRF (28.19 dB) in PSNR, which the authors attribute to the Bayer pattern complicating the warp-based photometric loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPERT builds on three pillars: 3D Gaussian Splatting for scene representation, 3D Gaussian Ray Tracing (3DGRT) for GPU-accelerated per-event depth rendering, and the Contrast Maximization framework for unsupervised geometric loss. It departs fundamentally from the dominant 'render-twice' paradigm in event-based GS (EventSplat, IncEventGS, Event-3DGS), which renders dense intensity at two timestamps and compares the difference to accumulated events, creating an inherent accuracy-vs-temporal-window trade-off. Instead, GPERT decouples rendering into a spatially sparse, temporally dense depth pathway (per-event ray tracing) and a spatially dense, temporally sparse intensity pathway (single snapshot), bridged by the Image of Warped Events. Unlike most prior event-based GS methods, it requires no pretrained image reconstruction models, no COLMAP initialization, and no auxiliary data (e.g., exposure events).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of geometry and appearance rendering resolves a long-standing trade-off in event-based 3D reconstruction: short temporal windows miss subtle intensity changes while long windows blur edges. By rendering intensity only once per batch and using per-event ray-traced depth for the geometric loss, GPERT achieves robustness to the number of events processed without sacrificing accuracy, and trains 4–6× faster than competing methods. The method's ability to reach SOTA on noisy real-world data without any external priors broadens its practical applicability. Key limitations remain: the static-scene assumption precludes dynamic content, flickering events destabilize the contrast-based geometric loss, and the Bayer pattern in color event cameras complicates warping-based photometric loss, leaving dynamic (4D) event-based GS and robust color handling as open directions.
