## Problem

LiDAR place recognition degrades sharply under rain, snow, and fog because scattering and attenuation distort both geometry and reflectance, yet prior methods either learn descriptors directly in corrupted feature spaces or attempt restoration at the input level (filtering, augmentation, reconstruction) rather than intervening where retrieval representations are actually formed. This paper argues that weather compensation should be performed in a semantically anchored latent space, decoupling robustness from explicit geometric reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-sweep LiDAR point cloud (x, y, z, per-return reflectance) and projects it onto a 448×448 three-channel BEV raster encoding height, intensity, and point density. It outputs an 8448-dimensional global descriptor (a 256-d global context token concatenated with 64 Sinkhorn cluster descriptors of 128 d each), L2-normalized for cosine-similarity retrieval. Critically, the descriptor is always extracted from ODE-denoised latent tokens, enforcing training-inference consistency so retrieval operates on the same representation type used during training.

## Method

C-LaV is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a frozen DINOv2-Base ViT/14 encoder maps the BEV image to a 768×32×32 latent grid (1024 tokens of dim 768), providing a fixed semantic manifold shared across weather conditions; (2) a conditional Flow Matching model with a DiT backbone learns a velocity field that deterministically transports a Gaussian sample toward the clean-weather latent distribution, conditioned on the noisy BEV latent, and at inference the corresponding probability-flow ODE is integrated with ~50 Euler steps to obtain denoised tokens without reconstructing any image or point cloud; (3) a SALAD head performs Sinkhorn-based soft clustering (K=64 prototypes, d_c=128) plus a global context token (d_g=256) to aggregate denoised tokens into the final descriptor. Training jointly optimizes a sparsity-aware flow-matching MSE loss (up-weighting occupied BEV cells), a truncated Smooth-AP ranking loss with 10 m/50 m GPS distance thresholds, and an optional latent consistency term, all on the same denoised-latent interface.

## Results

C-LaV achieves an average Recall@1 of 75.82% and Recall@5 of 96.57% across KITTI, NCLT, and Boreas under adverse weather, substantially outperforming all baselines. The largest gains appear on the real-world Boreas dataset (79.66% R@1 in rain, 71.98% in snow) and on KITTI snow (77.60% R@1, 95.23% R@5). On NCLT, where the domain gap is larger, C-LaV still leads in most conditions, reaching 46.41% R@1 in snow versus 44.14% for the next-best method (ImLPR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-LaV outperforms all four baselines on the average Recall@1 across the three datasets (75.82% vs. 54.37% for BEVPlace++, 52.94% for ImLPR, 48.99% for MinkLoc3D v2, and 37.39% for ResLPR). The margin is largest on the real-world Boreas dataset, where C-LaV's 75.82% average R@1 exceeds BEVPlace++ (54.37%) by over 21 points and ImLPR (52.94%) by over 22 points, driven by near-saturated R@5 values (98.31% rain, 94.83% snow). On KITTI, C-LaV leads in rain (+3.6 R@1 over MinkLoc3D v2) and snow (+5.3 R@1 over ImLPR), though it trails MinkLoc3D v2 by 4.3 points in fog, consistent with fog's depth-collapse effect that voxel-based methods handle better. On NCLT, the domain gap is larger and margins are smaller (e.g., +2.3 R@1 over ImLPR in snow), but C-LaV still achieves the best R@1 and R@5 in most conditions. ResLPR, which performs input-level restoration, is consistently the weakest baseline, confirming that signal-space denoising alone is insufficient for cross-weather retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-LaV sits at the intersection of three established threads: BEV-based LiDAR place recognition (BEVPlace), flow-matching generative modeling (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) with DiT parameterization (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023), and Sinkhorn-based descriptor aggregation (SALAD, Izquierdo & Civera, CVPR 2024). Its key conceptual departure from prior weather-robust place recognition work (ResLPR, domain-adaptation approaches) is the *location* of denoising: rather than restoring corrupted point clouds or BEV images at the signal level, C-LaV performs conditional flow-matching transport in the frozen DINOv2 latent space, arguing that retrieval-relevant structure is better preserved when compensation happens after semantic encoding. The unified 3 m-spacing benchmark with shared distance thresholds across KITTI, NCLT, and Boreas also departs from prior work that evaluated weather robustness under inconsistent protocols.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central practical contribution is the demonstration that latent-space denoising (62.84% R@1 on KITTI) substantially outperforms signal-space denoising (37.00% R@1) under an identical encoder and descriptor head, suggesting a general principle for retrieval-oriented denoising: intervene where the representation is most compact and task-aligned. The unified adverse-weather benchmark with consistent sampling and labeling across three datasets fills a gap that previously made cross-dataset weather-robustness comparisons unreliable. Open questions include whether the ODE-based inference cost (~50 steps) can be reduced without quality loss, whether surrogate noisy/clean pairs (needed for Boreas) introduce systematic bias, and whether the approach extends to more extreme or compound weather conditions beyond rain, fog, and snow.
