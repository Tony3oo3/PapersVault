## Problem

Existing MLLM-based image editing agents rely on rule-based, pixel-level rewards that compare outputs to a single reference edit, but creative retouching is inherently subjective—multiple distinct edits can equally satisfy the same instruction. This makes verifiable rewards unreliable and limits the ability to train agents via reinforcement learning on open-ended, instruction-driven editing tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263114/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a free-form natural language editing instruction (e.g., 'give this photo a warm vintage feel') as input. It produces a two-part output: a natural-language reasoning trace explaining the aesthetic interpretation, followed by a structured sequence of parameterized Lightroom operations (e.g., {exposure=+0.9; contrast=-30}) that are executed in the Lightroom runtime to yield the final edited image. The editing operations are constrained to Lightroom's parameter space, ensuring explainable and reproducible edits rather than pixel-level diffusion outputs.

## Method

RETOUCHIQ is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on 190K instruction-reasoning-edit triplets curated from real user Lightroom histories, followed by reinforcement learning guided by a Generalist Reward Model (GRM). The GRM is itself an RL-fine-tuned MLLM that, given a before-after image pair and instruction, first generates a set of task-specific evaluation metrics and then assigns a scalar score, providing case-by-case feedback rather than fixed rule-based comparisons. A key training innovation is Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT), an alternating optimization scheme in which the reward model is fine-tuned on weak edits produced by the policy model itself (rather than synthetic perturbations), closing the distribution gap between training data and actual policy outputs. This co-training loop progressively sharpens both the reward signal and the editing policy.

## Results

On the RETOUCHEVAL benchmark, RETOUCHIQ-GRM achieves the best overall scores across all three retouching categories: Quality Improving (O=7.51, PQ=7.48, SC=7.57), Style Changing (O=7.31, PQ=7.34), and Local Retouching (O=6.65, PQ=6.92). On MIT-Adobe5K, RETOUCHIQ-GRM attains SSIM=0.86, LPIPS=0.16, and PSNR=23.14, outperforming all baselines. The RL stage with the generalist reward model (GRM) consistently improves over the SFT-only variant by 0.7–0.8 points on the overall score and 0.8 points on perceptual quality in the quality-improving setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETOUCHIQ-GRM decisively outperforms diffusion-based Flux-Pro across all metrics, with L1 differences roughly halved (e.g., 31.41 vs. 64.97 on Quality Improving) and perceptual quality scores improved by ~1.4 points, confirming that parameterized Lightroom edits preserve image structure far better than generative diffusion. Against general-purpose MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5), RETOUCHIQ-GRM leads on every metric, with the largest gaps in perceptual quality (7.48 vs. 6.51 for GPT-5 on Quality Improving), indicating that zero-shot prompting of large MLLMs produces over-edited or incoherent parameter configurations. Compared to the closest MLLM-agent baseline JarvisArt, RETOUCHIQ-GRM wins on perceptual quality (PQ) and overall score (O) in all three categories (e.g., PQ 7.48 vs. 6.59, O 7.51 vs. 6.90 for Quality Improving), but JarvisArt retains a slight edge on semantic consistency for Style Changing (SC 7.39 vs. 7.29) and Local Retouching (SC 6.45 vs. 6.39), suggesting its rule-based reward better preserves instruction-specific color/tonal targets at the cost of perceptual fidelity. On MIT-Adobe5K, RETOUCHIQ-GRM achieves the best SSIM (0.86), lowest LPIPS (0.16), and highest PSNR (23.14), narrowly beating MonetGPT (SSIM 0.82, LPIPS 0.17, PSNR 23.10) and clearly surpassing JarvisArt (SSIM 0.76, LPIPS 0.23, PSNR 21.03).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generalist reward modeling paradigm (Liu et al. [24], Zhou et al. [43]) from text-based LLM evaluation to multimodal image editing, where an MLLM reward model dynamically generates case-specific metrics rather than relying on fixed pixel-level comparisons. It departs from prior MLLM agent approaches for Lightroom editing (JarvisArt [22], MonetGPT [9]) that anchor RL training to a single reference edit via verifiable rewards, arguing that the inherent subjectivity of creative retouching makes such rewards unreliable. The Policy-Guided Reward Training (PGRT) mechanism is a novel training scheme that addresses distribution shift between synthetic perturbed edits and actual policy-generated outputs, a problem not explicitly tackled in prior reward-modeling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental gap in RL-based creative editing: how to provide meaningful reward signals when multiple valid edits satisfy the same instruction. The PGRT insight—that reward models trained on perturbation-based weak edits fail to generalize to the policy's own output distribution—is a practical finding likely to inform future agent-reward co-training pipelines beyond image editing. Limitations include dependence on a single editing tool (Lightroom), a 7B backbone, and the complexity of alternating policy-reward training. Open questions include whether the generalist reward approach transfers to other professional tools (Photoshop, DaVinci Resolve), whether it scales to larger models, and how well it handles truly novel or contradictory instructions outside the training distribution.
