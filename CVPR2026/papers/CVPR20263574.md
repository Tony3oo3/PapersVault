## Problem

Existing CAD generation and editing methods treat these as separate tasks with distinct pipelines, and most focus on geometry-driven modeling under external conditions while neglecting the intrinsic topological relationships among geometric elements. This fragmentation hinders coherence in real-world design workflows where designers iteratively refine initial solutions, and limits the model's ability to capture dependencies among geometric elements for both generation quality and editing consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts free-form multimodal inputs (text prompts, rendered images, or real-world photographs) and converts them via a VLM (Qwen-VL-Max) into a topological structure graph, a text redescription, and an instruction class before feeding them to the decoder. For completion tasks, a partial CAD sequence is additionally provided; for editing, a full pre-editing sequence plus a natural-language editing instruction is supplied. The output is a complete CAD construction sequence of commands and parameters, which can be parsed into a BRep solid via Open CASCADE Technology.

## Method

CAD-Refiner unifies generation, completion, and editing through three key components. First, CAD Insighter uses a one-shot Chain-of-Thought prompt to guide a VLM in extracting a hierarchical structure tree and converting it into a topological structure graph (nodes from pre-order traversal, edges from adjacency triplets), which is encoded by GraphGPS to capture structural relationships. Second, a Sequence Injection Strategy (SIS) progressively introduces [MASK] tokens during training following a sinusoidal schedule, enabling the model to learn reconstruction from arbitrarily incomplete sequences (completion) and full generation from scratch, while editing uses a fixed masking ratio of 0 to focus on input-to-output transformation. Third, CAD Checker performs step-wise BRep validation via OCCT to detect unclosed loops, self-intersections, and parameter mismatches, then adaptively reweights the command and parameter cross-entropy losses (e.g., α=2 for unclosed loops, β=2.5 for parameter mismatches) to provide geometry-aware reward shaping during optimization.

## Results

On the MMCAD benchmark, CAD-Refiner achieves 83.78% command accuracy (Acc_c) and 57.06% parameter accuracy (Acc_p) for text-to-CAD generation, outperforming the strongest non-LLM baseline FreeCAD (74.81% / 50.20%) by 8.97 and 6.86 points respectively. For multimodal input, it reaches 84.66% Acc_c and 59.48% Acc_p with a 52.91% valid ratio. On the editing task, CAD-Refiner attains 82.55% Acc_p, surpassing LLaMA3.1-8B (54.47%) and Qwen2.5-7B (56.93%) by 28.08 and 25.62 points, while running ~14× faster than LLaMA (1.13 s vs. 15.96 s inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAD-Refiner dominates all non-LLM baselines on command accuracy across every input modality: it beats FreeCAD (the strongest non-LLM competitor) by 8.97 points on text Acc_c and 9.60 on image Acc_c, and by 9.22 on multimodal Acc_c. Against LLM-based baselines (LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B), CAD-Refiner achieves substantially higher Acc_c (83.78 vs. 52.13/52.74) and Acc_p (57.06 vs. 42.09/39.47) while running 14–17× faster (1.13 s vs. 15.96/19.08 s). The one notable trade-off is with GenCAD on image input: GenCAD achieves a higher valid ratio (62.43% vs. 51.66%) and lower MMD/JSD, but at the cost of much lower parameter accuracy (29.91% vs. 57.85%), which the authors attribute to GenCAD's tendency to produce oversimplified box-shaped structures. On the editing task, CAD-Refiner's 82.55% Acc_p exceeds LLaMA by 28.08 points and Qwen2.5 by 25.62 points. On cross-dataset generalization (Fusion 360) and real-world 3D-printed images, CAD-Refiner maintains the highest Acc_c among all compared methods, confirming robustness without additional fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DeepCAD's construction-sequence representation and the line of conditional CAD generation work (text, image, point-cloud inputs) that followed it, but departs from the prevailing paradigm of treating generation and editing as separate tasks requiring separate pipelines. Rather than adopting the LLM-backbone approach of recent work (CADFusion, CAD-Editor, CAD-Assistant) that demands multi-GPU hardware and task-specific representations, CAD-Refiner uses a lightweight transformer–GNN decoder with a VLM (Qwen-VL-Max) as a front-end semantic parser that outputs topological structure graphs. The Sequence Injection Strategy, inspired by curriculum learning and diffusion masking, is a novel training mechanism for unifying generation, completion, and editing under a single model, while the CAD Checker introduces geometry-aware adaptive loss weighting via Open CASCADE Technology—a departure from the fixed-weight cross-entropy losses standard in the DeepCAD lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying generation, editing, and completion in one model with free-form multimodal input, CAD-Refiner addresses a practical gap in real design workflows where designers iteratively refine models rather than generating them once. The topological structure graph as an intermediate representation offers a domain-invariant bridge between high-level user intent and low-level geometric operations, potentially improving cross-domain generalization. However, the framework's dependence on a VLM API introduces latency and cost overhead, and the authors acknowledge that current dataset scale limits the method to coarse-grained operations unsuitable for industrial production precision. Open questions include whether the VLM dependency can be replaced with a learned encoder, whether the graph-based representation scales to more complex assemblies, and whether the adaptive loss weighting generalizes beyond the three error types currently handled by OCCT.
