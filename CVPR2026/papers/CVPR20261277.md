## Problem

Despite Vision Transformers becoming the dominant architecture in computer vision, research on their adversarial robustness remains heavily biased toward CNNs, with only 13% of robustness studies targeting ViTs. Prior work reported negative interactions between ViTs and adversarial training but provided limited mechanistic explanations, leaving unclear which internal components enable or hinder robustness and how to design AT that is architecturally compatible with ViTs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261277/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, ImageNette at 224×224) and outputs class labels, with adversarial perturbations bounded at ε=8/255 under L∞ norm. A notable detail is that the distillation signal is the pre-softmax class attention map (Q_cls·K^T/√d_h) extracted at every layer and head, rather than the final output logits, making the supervision spatially structured and hierarchical across the ViT depth.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage adversarial training framework built on two insights from a novel Gradient Path Masking (GPM) analysis: (1) the residual connection is the primary vulnerability path for adversaries, and (2) robustness-related information concentrates in the QK attention path and manifests as semantic priors in class attention maps. In Stage 1, a teacher ViT is trained with standard AT (PGD-AT, TRADES, or MART). In Stage 2, a student ViT is trained with the same AT loss plus a hierarchical CAM distillation loss that matches pre-softmax class attention maps between teacher and student across all L layers and H heads. Additionally, a learnable scalar gate r_l is inserted into each residual connection (X'_l = S_l V_l + r_l X_{l-1}), allowing the model to down-weight the residual path during optimization. The gating values learned empirically stay below 1 in early layers and increase with depth, confirming reduced residual dependence where it matters most.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, the proposed two-stage AT with residual gating (ViT + PGD-AT) achieves 82.01% clean accuracy and 47.41% AutoAttack robustness, improving over the PGD-AT baseline (79.59% / 46.37%) by +2.42% and +1.04% respectively. Hybrid architectures benefit most: ConViT under PGD-AT jumps from 69.83% to 77.21% clean accuracy (+7.38%) and from 39.23% to 43.93% AA robustness (+4.70%). On ImageNette, ConViT (PGD-AT) achieves the largest single gain of +17.2% in AA robustness (39.00% → 56.20%). Against CNN-based robust distillation methods (Table 5), the proposed method outperforms ARD (65.93%), RSLAD (73.38%), and AdaAD (20.71%) in clean accuracy while also matching or exceeding the teacher's robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three standard AT baselines (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART), the proposed method consistently improves both clean accuracy and AA robustness across all five architectures, with the largest gains on hybrid models: ConViT under PGD-AT gains +7.38% clean and +4.70% AA on CIFAR-10, while non-hybrid ViT gains only +2.42% clean and +1.04% AA. On ImageNette, the advantage for hybrids is even more pronounced, with ConViT (PGD-AT) improving AA robustness by +17.2% (39.00% → 56.20%). Against CNN-based robust distillation methods (Table 5, ViT-S on CIFAR-10), the proposed method dominates: it achieves 82.01% clean and 47.41% AA, versus ARD at 65.93%/32.37%, RSLAD at 73.38%/4.97%, and AdaAD at 20.71%/10.31%. RSLAD and AdaAD, which use the teacher to generate stronger adversarial examples, suffer severe robustness collapse when transferred to ViTs, while ARD's output-distribution matching degrades clean accuracy by 13.7% relative to the teacher. The paper attributes these failures to (1) final output logits being less informative priors than class attention maps for ViTs, and (2) teacher-induced gradient directions not meaningfully guiding the student across its decision boundary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on standard adversarial training formulations (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART) as its base optimization loop, but departs from prior ViT robustness analyses (Shao et al., Paul et al., Park et al.) by proposing a structured diagnostic tool—Gradient Path Masking (GPM)—that decomposes attention gradients into QK, V, and residual paths rather than treating the attention module monolithically. It extends Mo et al.'s Adversarial Random Dropout, which randomly drops attention gradients, into a path-specific, interpretable analysis. The two-stage training scheme with class attention map distillation is positioned as a ViT-specific alternative to CNN-based robust distillation methods (ARD, RSLAD, AdaAD), which the paper empirically shows fail to transfer effectively to ViTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the residual path as the primary adversarial bottleneck and the observation that AT shifts ViTs toward global (rather than local) patch associations in early layers provide actionable architectural insights for designing robust transformers, particularly for hybrid ViT-CNN models that impose local inductive biases. However, the practical gains from the proposed two-stage method are modest (1–2% on non-hybrid ViTs, 3–4% on hybrids), and all experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10 and ImageNette with small ViT variants, leaving open whether the path dependency phenomenon and the global-association shift persist at ImageNet scale or with larger models. The semantic segmentation result (57.7% JS on Pascal VOC) is intriguing but evaluated on a single small-scale benchmark, and the broader question of whether AT-induced semantic priors can be systematically leveraged for dense prediction tasks remains unexplored.
