## Problem

Diffusion-based video super-resolution achieves high fidelity but requires dozens of iterative sampling steps, making real-time deployment impractical. While Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) can compress multi-step diffusion into one-step generation, directly applying it to VSR suffers from training instability, degraded supervision (the frozen real score model produces spatially shifted or artifact-contaminated guidance relative to the LR anchor), and insufficient supervision (the teacher distribution remains inferior to real HR videos). Existing one-step VSR methods thus face unresolved trade-offs among stability, temporal consistency, and perceptual quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video, upscales it to the target resolution, and encodes it into latent space via a VAE; the LR latent is concatenated with noisy HR latents as joint input to a ~1.3B-parameter video DiT, which is also conditioned on text embeddings. The output is a clean high-resolution video produced in a single forward pass (one denoising step), evaluated at 1920×1080 resolution over 21-frame clips. The base model originally requires 50 sampling steps, so the distillation compresses this to one step while preserving temporal coherence.

## Method

DUO-VSR is a three-stage distillation pipeline. Stage I (Progressive Guided Distillation) first performs CFG distillation to absorb classifier-free guidance, then progressively halves the denoising steps from 64 to 1, yielding a stable one-step initialization. Stage II (Dual-Stream Distillation) is the core contribution: it jointly optimizes a DMD stream (matching the student distribution to the frozen real score model via KL-gradient) and an RFS-GAN stream (using intermediate features from both the frozen real score and the updated fake score models as a shared discriminator backbone, with hinge GAN loss and feature matching loss on diffused samples). The two streams are interleaved—student updates use L_DMD + L_G + L_FM while auxiliary updates train the fake score model and discriminator heads—so adversarial supervision from real HR videos regularizes and extends beyond the teacher distribution. Stage III (Preference-Guided Refinement) fine-tunes the student with a DPO loss on a synthetic preference dataset ranked by video quality assessment models, further aligning outputs with perceptual quality.

## Results

DUO-VSR achieves the best or near-best perceptual quality across all five evaluation datasets. On the synthetic YouHQ40 benchmark, it attains DOVER 87.28, MUSIQ 65.24, and CLIP-IQA 0.4222, surpassing the strongest one-step baselines DOVE (84.43/61.36/0.4167) and SeedVR2-7B (73.60/58.44/0.3736). On the self-constructed AIGC60 dataset, DUO-VSR leads with DOVER 88.15, MUSIQ 63.68, and CLIP-IQA 0.4886. In terms of efficiency, DUO-VSR completes a 21-frame 1920×1080 video in 11.3 s with only 1.3B parameters, roughly 8× faster than SeedVR2-7B (89.7 s, 8.2B) and ~85× faster than the 50-step MGLD (956.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DUO-VSR outperforms all eight baselines on the majority of perceptual metrics (DOVER, MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, NIQE) across all five datasets. Against the closest one-step competitors, it leads DOVE by +2.05 DOVER on SPMCS (81.47 vs 79.94) and by +2.85 MUSIQ on YouHQ40 (65.24 vs 61.36), while surpassing SeedVR2-7B by +6.08 DOVER on SPMCS (81.47 vs 75.40) and +5.38 MUSIQ on AIGC60 (63.68 vs 58.65). On fidelity metrics (PSNR, LPIPS), DUO-VSR is competitive but slightly trails SeedVR2-7B on SPMCS (PSNR 22.90 vs 23.08; LPIPS 0.315 vs 0.302) and DOVE on YouHQ40 PSNR (22.96 vs 23.12), indicating a minor trade-off between pixel-level fidelity and perceptual realism. For temporal consistency (E_warp), DUO-VSR achieves the best score on SPMCS (1.67) and YouHQ40 (1.98), but DOVE edges it out on AIGC60 (0.95 vs 1.08). In inference efficiency, DUO-VSR (11.3 s, 1.3B params) is ~8× faster than SeedVR2-7B (89.7 s, 8.2B), ~6× faster than DOVE (66.7 s, 5.6B), and ~7× faster than DLoRAL (76.6 s, 0.9B), while multi-step methods like MGLD (956.7 s) and UAV (382.1 s) are 34–85× slower.

## Relation to Prior Work

DUO-VSR builds directly on Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) for one-step generation, progressive/CFG distillation for stable initialization, and DPO for preference alignment, applied within a SeedVR-style DiT VSR backbone. Its key departure from DMD2 is the joint (rather than sequential) optimization of DMD and GAN losses, and the use of features from both the frozen real score model and the updated fake score model as discriminator backbones, rather than relying solely on the fake score model. The paper explicitly positions itself against SeedVR2 (adversarial post-training with instability from large discriminators) and DOVE (regression-based stabilization that sacrifices detail), identifying three specific failure modes of naive DMD in VSR—training instability, spatially shifted supervision from the real score model, and a hard quality ceiling at the teacher distribution—that motivate the dual-stream design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50× inference speedup over SeedVR-7B and ~90× over MGLD make high-quality video super-resolution practically deployable in real-time or near-real-time settings, which was previously infeasible with multi-step diffusion. The finding that the frozen real score model produces spatially shifted or artifact-contaminated guidance when encountering unseen student outputs is a useful diagnostic that likely applies to other conditional distillation settings beyond VSR. Open questions include whether the dual-stream strategy generalizes to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), how it scales to longer video sequences or higher resolutions, and whether the preference-guided refinement stage can be replaced by more scalable reward models as video quality assessors improve.
