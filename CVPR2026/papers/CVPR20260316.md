## Problem

Existing embodied navigation methods optimize for geometric efficiency and collision avoidance but ignore social compliance, producing trajectories that jaywalk or traverse restricted zones like lawns. Imitation learning alone cannot capture the causal structure of normative conduct, and VLM-based social reasoning remains disconnected from low-level action generation, leaving a gap between understanding social norms and executing socially compliant trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260316/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a window of n=5 recent monocular RGB frames (H×W×3) with their 2D positions and a 2D goal coordinate, and outputs m=5 future waypoints via a Diffusion Transformer action expert (12 layers, 12 heads, D=1536) that performs K=5 iterative denoising steps. The VLM brain (Qwen2.5-VL 3B) additionally produces interpretable side outputs—socially traversable region polygons, step-by-step chain-of-thought rationales, and free-form VQA answers—that are not consumed by the action expert at inference but serve as training signals and interpretability artifacts.

## Method

SocialNav adopts a hierarchical brain-action architecture: a VLM brain encodes social navigation priors and emits last-layer semantic features that condition a conditional flow-matching action expert (Diffusion Transformer) for trajectory generation, decoupling high-level reasoning from low-level control while preserving a semantic link. Training proceeds in three stages—(1) end-to-end imitation learning on 7M samples spanning internet video pseudo-trajectories, high-fidelity 3DGS/Isaac Sim scenes, real robot data, and cognitive tasks (traversability, CoT, VQA); (2) fine-tuning the action expert alone on real-robot trajectories with the VLM frozen to close the sim-to-real gap; (3) SAFE-GRPO, a flow-based RL stage that converts the deterministic flow ODE into a stochastic SDE for controlled exploration while keeping the VLM's semantic conditioning fixed, and optimizes a composite reward balancing social compliance (via a semantic occupancy map), expert-trajectory consistency, motion smoothness, and goal-directed efficiency. The key insight is that without the cognitive priors from the CAD, RL actually degrades social metrics, confirming that high-level social understanding is a prerequisite for norm-aware policy refinement rather than something RL can discover from sparse rewards alone.

## Results

On the closed-loop SocNav Benchmark, SocialNav(Full) achieves a success rate of 86.1%, route completion of 91.2%, and SPL of 77.4, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline CityWalker (SR 47.8%, RC 64.7%, SPL 44.7%). In social compliance, SocialNav attains a Distance Compliance Rate of 82.5% and Time Compliance Rate of 82.9%, more than doubling CityWalker's 36.1% and 36.6% respectively. On the open-loop CityWalker Benchmark, SocialNav reduces the MAOE to 10.2 (mean) and 7.8 (all samples), compared to 15.2 and 11.5 for CityWalker. In real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot, SocialNav achieves 85% average success rate across three environments versus 62.5% for CityWalker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SocialNav decisively outperforms all four baselines (GNM, ViNT, NoMaD, CityWalker) on every metric in both closed-loop and real-world evaluations. The largest margin is in social compliance: SocialNav's DCR of 82.5% exceeds CityWalker's 36.1% by 46.4 points and GNM's 26.5% by 56 points, reflecting the core contribution of norm-aware RL (SAFE-GRPO). In navigation performance, SocialNav gains +38.3 SR and +32.7 SPL over CityWalker, the strongest baseline, while also surpassing it by +38.3 SR in real-world deployment (85% vs. 62.5%). Among the baselines, CityWalker is consistently the strongest on navigation metrics (SR 47.8, SPL 44.7), while GNM is the weakest overall. The ablation (SocialNav* with IL only on D_real) already reaches 65.0 SR and 58.0 DCR, showing the architecture itself provides a large gap over baselines even before the full data pipeline and RL stage are applied.

## Relation to Prior Work

SocialNav extends the web-scale video navigation line (CityWalker, GNM, ViNT, NoMaD) by adding a hierarchical VLM 'brain' for social reasoning and a conditional flow-matching action expert, following the π0/π0.5 VLA architecture pattern. It departs from pure imitation learning by introducing SAFE-GRPO, the first flow-based RL framework for embodied navigation that explicitly rewards social compliance, building on GRPO and Flow-GRPO for preference alignment. The 3DGS + Isaac Sim benchmark extends prior navigation evaluation (Habitat, Matterport3D) into large-scale outdoor social scenes with physics-based collision and dynamic agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Social compliance is a critical but largely unaddressed gap for real-world robot deployment, and this paper provides both a large-scale training corpus (7M samples) and a high-fidelity benchmark to close it. The ablation revealing that RL without cognitive priors (D_cog) actually degrades social compliance is a useful cautionary finding: high-level semantic understanding is a prerequisite for effective norm-aware RL, not merely a nice-to-have. Open questions include whether hand-crafted social rewards generalize beyond traversability to richer context-dependent conventions, and whether the SPL trade-off (socially compliant paths are less geometrically direct) can be mitigated.
