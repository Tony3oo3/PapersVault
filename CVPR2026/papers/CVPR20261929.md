## Problem

Existing non-transferable learning (NTL) methods suffer from three coupled limitations: they require full backbone retraining (high training cost), force all inputs through the entire network at inference (wasteful for target-domain data that should perform poorly), and rely on a single shared backbone for both domains, creating optimization conflicts when class categories overlap. No prior NTL approach simultaneously addresses training efficiency, inference efficiency, and domain-specific optimization separation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/STL-10, 224×224 for PACS/DomainNet/Office-Home) or grayscale digit images (28×28 for MNIST/USPS/SVHN/MNIST-M) and produces a class label prediction. Unusually, the prediction is not always generated at the final layer: an entropy-based routing mechanism assigns each input to one of E intermediate exit classifiers, with source-domain samples routed to deep exits (rich semantic features) and target-domain samples routed to shallow exits (non-semantic features). The backbone is frozen throughout; only the lightweight exit classifier heads are trained, making the output a (class label, exit index) pair rather than a single fixed-depth prediction.

## Method

ENL-DEE attaches E exit classifiers at intermediate depths of a frozen backbone (VGG-11, ResNet-34, ViT, or Swin Transformer) and formulates routing as a Bayesian mixture model. Each exit has a latent variable with a standard Gaussian prior and an amortized Gaussian posterior parameterized by the frozen backbone's features; the reparameterization trick enables gradient-based optimization of only the exit heads. Routing weights η_i(x) are computed without learnable parameters via a Boltzmann transform of each exit's classifier-output Shannon entropy, so more confident (lower-entropy) exits receive higher probability. The source-domain loss maximizes the exit-mixture ELBO plus a depth-shaping entropy penalty that discourages shallow exits and encourages deep, decisive predictions; the target-domain loss minimizes the ELBO plus an inverted entropy shape that promotes shallow confident exits and flattens deep-exit distributions. A KL regularization term softly aligns the data-dependent routing distribution with domain-specific priors (π^(s) favoring deep exits, π^(t) favoring shallow exits), resolving the optimization conflict inherent in shared-backbone NTL.

## Results

On the PACS dataset with ViT, ENL-DEE achieves Performance Gain (PG) values of 20.11–30.41 across all four source-target domain pairs, substantially outperforming NTL (−0.12 to 16.99) and CUTI (0.03 to 21.12). On DomainNet with ResNet-34, ENL-DEE is the only method with positive PG across all six domains (0.49–10.82), while both NTL and CUTI yield negative PG in every setting. On CIFAR-10/STL-10 with ViT, ENL-DEE reaches PG of 50.5 (CIFAR→STL) and 40.1 (STL→CIFAR), surpassing NTL (44.9, 37.7) and CUTI (43.3, 37.9). Source-domain accuracy is preserved near supervised-learning levels (e.g., 85.10% on PACS-S vs. 85.20% SL), while target-domain accuracy drops to near-random (6.84–20.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENL-DEE consistently outperforms both NTL [Wang et al., ICLR 2022] and CUTI [Wang et al., CVPR 2023] on the multi-domain PACS benchmark (ViT), achieving PG of 20.11–30.41 versus NTL's −0.12–16.99 and CUTI's 0.03–21.12, with the largest margin on the Cartoon-as-source setting (30.41 vs. 14.45 and 21.12). On DomainNet (ResNet-34), ENL-DEE is the only method with positive PG in all six domain pairs (0.49–10.82), whereas NTL and CUTI produce negative PG in every setting (e.g., NTL: −38.75 on QuickDraw-as-source; CUTI: −30.75 on the same). On CIFAR-10/STL-10 with ViT, ENL-DEE leads both baselines by 5.6 and 2.2 PG points (CIFAR→STL) and by 2.4 and 2.2 PG points (STL→CIFAR). With ResNet-34 on CIFAR-10/STL-10, NTL slightly edges out ENL-DEE on the CIFAR→STL direction (PG 54.9 vs. 51.7), though ENL-DEE matches or exceeds CUTI. For the ownership verification task on digit datasets (VGG-11), ENL-DEE maintains the highest clean source accuracy (e.g., 98.99% on MNIST, 99.79% on USPS) while keeping watermarked accuracy uniformly low at ~10.58%, compared to NTL's 6.75–14.41% and CUTI's 6.75–14.12% watermarked accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: non-transferable learning (NTL) for model IP protection (Wang et al., ICLR 2022; CUTI, CVPR 2023) and early-exit neural networks for efficient inference (BranchyNet, 2016). It departs from prior NTL methods by freezing the backbone and training only lightweight exit classifiers, thereby eliminating the costly full retraining that NTL and CUTI require. The Bayesian ELBO formulation with entropy-based routing is a novel theoretical wrapper that had not been applied to early-exit architectures in the NTL context, though the individual components (variational inference, Boltzmann-weighted routing) are standard tools repurposed here.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency gains—training only exit heads and allowing target-domain samples to terminate at shallow layers—make NTL more deployable in settings where full retraining is prohibitive, and the domain-separated optimization resolves a genuine conflict that plagues shared-backbone NTL. However, the task itself (model IP protection via NTL) remains a niche sub-area, and the paper's own limitation section acknowledges that the static target-domain assumption restricts real-world applicability as distributions drift over time. Whether the Bayesian routing formulation provides meaningful advantages over simpler deterministic early-exit heuristics for NTL remains an open empirical question, as the ablation does not isolate the ELBO/KL terms from the entropy-shaping terms.
