## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models following the Mixture-of-Transformers paradigm (e.g., BAGEL, LMFusion) use symmetric architectures where both understanding and generation branches are initialized from the same LLM, preventing the generative expert from leveraging strong pretrained diffusion priors. Additionally, their dense layer-by-layer shared attention ignores the inherent asymmetry between understanding (high-level semantic reasoning) and generation (fine-grained low-level structure), leading to slow convergence and shallow-feature overfitting in the generative branch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262894/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model accepts a free-form text prompt and produces a photorealistic RGB image via a 4B DiT diffusion backbone operating in latent space with flow matching. For image editing, it takes a source image plus a text instruction and outputs a modified image. For understanding tasks, the frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B expert handles text and image inputs to produce text responses, preserving native VLM capabilities without any fine-tuning.

## Method

HBridge pairs a frozen pretrained LLM/VLM (understanding expert) with a pretrained 4B DiT diffusion model from OmniGen2 (generative expert) in an asymmetric H-shaped topology. A QKV-Linear alignment module projects the generative expert's query, key, and value features into the understanding expert's dimension space so that cross-modal self-attention can be computed despite differing embedding sizes, head counts, and normalization types. Only the middle layers are bridged via shared attention (skipping the first 6 and last 6 layers), reducing over 40% of attention connections and preventing the generative branch from overfitting shallow lexical features. On the generative expert's input side, 16 learnable semantic reconstruction tokens are supervised with a cosine-distance loss to reconstruct frozen ViT features of the target image, explicitly injecting relational semantic supervision into the denoising process alongside the standard flow-matching objective.

## Results

On DPG-Bench, HBridge (7B+4B) achieves an overall score of 85.23, surpassing BAGEL (7B+7B) at 85.07 and OmniGen2 (3B+4B) at 83.57, while using fewer total parameters and only ~200B T2I training tokens versus BAGEL's ~2.5T. On GenEval, HBridge reaches 0.83 overall (0.87 with an LLM re-writer), outperforming BAGEL (0.80 / 0.86) and OmniGen2 (0.80 / 0.86). On ImgEdit-Bench, HBridge attains the best overall editing score of 3.54, ahead of OmniGen2 (3.44), UniWorld-V1 (3.26), and BAGEL (3.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBridge outperforms the closest unified-model competitor BAGEL (7B+7B) on all three benchmarks: DPG-Bench (85.23 vs 85.07), GenEval (0.83 vs 0.80 without re-writer; 0.87 vs 0.86 with re-writer), and ImgEdit-Bench (3.54 vs 3.20), while using fewer total parameters (11B vs 14B) and roughly 12.5× fewer T2I training tokens (~200B vs ~2.5T). Against OmniGen2 (3B+4B), HBridge leads by 1.66 points on DPG-Bench (85.23 vs 83.57) and 0.03 on GenEval (0.83 vs 0.80), and by 0.10 on ImgEdit-Bench (3.54 vs 3.44). UniWorld-V1 (7B+12B) is surpassed by a wider margin: 3.85 points on DPG-Bench, 0.03 on GenEval, and 0.28 on ImgEdit-Bench. Among standalone diffusion models, HBridge's 85.23 on DPG-Bench exceeds SD3-medium (84.08) and FLUX.1-dev (84.0) despite having a comparable parameter budget. The pluggability experiment (Table 7) further shows that inserting HBridge's mid-layer bridge and semantic reconstruction tokens into BAGEL itself yields consistent gains (85.07→85.55 on DPG-Bench), confirming the components' generality beyond the asymmetric architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

HBridge departs from the symmetric Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) paradigm established by BAGEL and LMFusion, which mirrors one LLM-initialized expert onto another with full-layer shared self-attention. Instead, it pairs a frozen pretrained VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as the understanding expert with a pretrained diffusion DiT (from OmniGen2) as the generative expert, connected only through a selective mid-layer semantic bridge that eliminates over 40% of attention connections. The QKV-Linear alignment module resolves the architectural mismatch between the two heterogeneous backbones, and the semantic reconstruction tokens add an explicit ViT-feature supervision signal absent in prior unified models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.5× reduction in training tokens (~200B vs. BAGEL's ~2.5T) while matching or exceeding performance is a practically significant efficiency gain for anyone building unified multimodal systems. The finding that mid-layer connections dominate cross-expert information flow, while shallow and deep layers contribute minimally, provides a general architectural principle that could guide future MoT designs. Open questions include whether dynamic (content-dependent) layer selection can further improve the fixed M=N=6 coupling, and whether the asymmetric design generalizes to video generation or other modalities beyond images.
