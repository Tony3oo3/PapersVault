## Problem

One-step diffusion-based real-world image super-resolution suffers from two persistent failures: structural fidelity degradation and low-frequency inconsistency caused by VAE-based conditioning, and insufficient high-frequency detail restoration because a single denoising step cannot iteratively synthesize the fine textures that multi-step diffusion recovers. Existing one-step methods that predict a single global latent residual (e.g., PiSA-SR) further exhibit unstable HF reconstruction and residual artifacts, leaving a gap between perceptual sharpness and faithful content preservation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-quality RGB image with complex, unknown real-world degradations and produces a 512×512 super-resolved RGB image in a single forward pass. Internally, the LQ image is encoded by a frozen VAE encoder into a 4× downsampled latent, processed by a LoRA-adapted U-Net (rank 8) on Stable Diffusion 2.1-base, and decoded back to pixel space. At inference, an optional frequency-based enhancement stage (LFIM) operates on the refined latent without any model retraining, allowing flexible control over LF/HF injection strength.

## Method

FiDeSR builds on PiSA-SR's one-step latent residual formulation and introduces three components. During training, a Detail-aware Weighting (DAW) module computes a spatial difficulty map by element-wise multiplying a detail map (mean of Sobel, Laplacian, and local-variance responses on the HQ image) with a combined pixel/perceptual error map, then uses this map to spatially modulate both the MSE+LPIPS reconstruction loss and the CSD regularization loss, steering the model toward detail-rich, high-error regions. A Latent Residual Refinement Block (LRRB), built on RRDB architecture, takes the concatenation of the LQ latent and the U-Net's initial residual, predicts an adaptive correction Δr, and produces a refined residual that is subtracted from the LQ latent to yield the restored latent. At inference, a Latent Frequency Injection Module (LFIM) decomposes the refined latent into low- and high-frequency components via FFT-based Butterworth filters and selectively re-injects them using a spatial gate (from LQ detail maps) and a channel gate (from per-channel frequency energy ratios), enhancing structure in flat regions and texture in detail-rich regions without retraining.

## Results

On DRealSR, FiDeSR (1 step) achieves the best PSNR (28.90 dB), SSIM (0.7907), LPIPS (0.2836), DISTS (0.2112), MANIQA (0.6239), and FID (127.97) among all compared methods, including multi-step baselines. On RealSR, it attains the best LPIPS (0.2626), DISTS (0.1965), CLIPIQA (0.6896), NIQE (5.3194), MANIQA (0.6681), and FID (109.68). On DIV2K, FiDeSR leads in SSIM (0.6250), LPIPS (0.2678), DISTS (0.1845), MANIQA (0.6384), and FID (23.30). Across all three benchmarks, FiDeSR consistently achieves the lowest FID, indicating the closest distributional alignment with ground-truth images while using only a single diffusion step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest one-step competitor PiSA-SR, FiDeSR wins on PSNR (+0.58 dB), SSIM (+0.0103), LPIPS (−0.0124), DISTS (−0.0057), MANIQA (+0.0078), and FID (−2.51) on DRealSR, while trailing slightly on NIQE (6.20 vs 6.18) and MUSIQ (65.78 vs 66.11). Versus other one-step methods (OSEDiff, SinSR, AddSR), FiDeSR dominates on perceptual and distributional metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, MANIQA, FID) across all three datasets, with the largest FID gap over AddSR (127.97 vs 167.69 on DRealSR). Compared to multi-step methods, FiDeSR achieves the best FID on every dataset (127.97 vs 143.08 for PASD-20s on DRealSR; 109.68 vs 123.49 for OSEDiff on RealSR; 23.30 vs 25.09 for PiSA-SR on DIV2K) while matching or exceeding their PSNR/SSIM, demonstrating that a single diffusion step suffices when combined with residual refinement and frequency-aware weighting. The only consistent losses are on MUSIQ and CLIPIQA for AddSR on DRealSR (65.78 vs 65.06; 0.6974 vs 0.7121), where AddSR's adversarial distillation produces slightly higher no-reference perceptual scores at the cost of fidelity and FID.

## Relation to Prior Work

FiDeSR extends the one-step latent diffusion SR lineage initiated by OSEDiff and PiSA-SR, which distill multi-step denoising into a single U-Net forward pass operating in the VAE latent space. It departs from PiSA-SR's single global residual prediction by inserting a learned refinement stage (LRRB, built on RRDB blocks from ESRGAN) that corrects the coarse residual before latent reconstruction, and from frequency-domain SR methods (RnG, DiWa, TFDSR) by performing frequency-aware weighting and injection entirely in the spatial/latent domain rather than explicit Fourier decomposition. The DAW loss weighting and the inference-time LFIM (FFT-based Butterworth decomposition with spatial/channel gating) are novel combinations, but each individual mechanism draws on well-known building blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that FiDeSR achieves competitive or superior FID and perceptual scores with a single diffusion step, making real-world SR substantially faster than multi-step alternatives while avoiding the structural distortion and HF under-reconstruction that plague current one-step methods. The inference-time LFIM, which requires no retraining and allows flexible enhancement control, is a deployable feature for practitioners who need tunable fidelity-perception trade-offs. Limitations include reliance on SD 2.1-base (not SD3 or newer architectures), evaluation only at 4× scale, and the LFIM being a post-hoc frequency boost that may not generalize to heavily degraded or out-of-distribution inputs. Open questions include whether the residual refinement and frequency injection strategies transfer to video SR or to higher magnification factors.
