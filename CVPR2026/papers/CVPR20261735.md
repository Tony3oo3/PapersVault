## Problem

Existing mask-centric generative data augmentation methods for semantic segmentation (e.g., FreeMask, SegGen) fine-tune hundreds of millions of parameters in diffusion models, causing severe overfitting when only a few labeled samples are available. Text-centric alternatives avoid this but produce distribution-inconsistent images with misaligned masks. The paper addresses how to perform high-quality mask-to-image synthesis in the few-shot setting without the parameter overhead that breaks down under data scarcity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of binary semantic masks (one per class, H×W×C) paired with corresponding text prompts, and a pre-trained Stable Diffusion V1-4 model. It outputs K=5 synthetic RGB images per mask (50 PLMS steps, CFG scale 2) along with a filtered segmentation mask where pixels inconsistent across the K homologous images are set to background (255). In the semi-supervised MatchMask++ variant, pseudo-masks extracted from unlabeled images serve as additional conditioning inputs without requiring adapter retraining.

## Method

MatchMask first applies a Gradient Probe Method that computes epoch-level relative parameter-change scores (‖θᵢ − θᵢ′‖/‖θᵢ′‖) across all U-Net layers to identify a small set of spatially critical layers (primarily cross-attention to_v and to_out in high-resolution blocks). A LoRA-style adapter with only 0.7M trainable parameters is then attached to these layers, coupled with a Layer-adaptive Cross-attention Fusion mechanism that predicts a per-block scalar α via a linear layer to dynamically blend original attention with rectified (mask-gated) attention. A Relative Filtering Strategy generates K images per mask, runs a segmentation model on each, performs majority voting to obtain a consensus pseudo-mask, and discards pixels where individual predictions disagree with the consensus, thereby suppressing misaligned regions without relying on model confidence. The semi-supervised extension MatchMask++ reuses the frozen adapter to synthesize images from pseudo-masks on unlabeled data, avoiding any additional adapter training.

## Results

In the data-limited setting, MatchMask achieves up to +6.8% mIoU improvement on PASCAL VOC (67.5% → 72.1% at 1/4 labeled samples), +3.7% on COCO (36.0% → 39.7% at 1/128), and +2.8% on ADE20K (18.6% → 21.4% at 1/100) using only 0.7M trainable parameters. In the semi-supervised setting, MatchMask++ further boosts VOC to 74.3% mIoU and ADE20K to 24.6% mIoU. The method requires only 5 synthetic images per mask and can be trained on a single A100 GPU in 100k iterations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against text-centric GDA methods, MatchMask substantially outperforms both Dataset-Diffusion (62.5% joint mIoU) and DatasetDM (68.2%) on VOC with 366 labeled samples, reaching 72.1% while using only 1.8k synthetic images versus their 40k. Against mask-to-image baselines, MatchMask (0.7M params) consistently beats FreeMask (850M params) and SegGen (360M params) across all data budgets on VOC: at 1/16 labeled samples, MatchMask achieves 57.1% vs. 54.1% (FreeMask) and 52.5% (SegGen); at 1/4, 72.1% vs. 71.1% and 70.2% respectively. The gap is largest in extreme low-data regimes (1/16), where overfitting of the heavier baselines is most severe. In the semi-supervised setting, MatchMask++ improves upon Self-Training (72.6% → 74.3% on VOC, 22.7% → 24.6% on ADE). When integrated with Unimatch, MatchMask++ lifts mIoU from 78.3% to 79.6%, approaching the fully supervised ceiling of 79.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends mask-centric generative data augmentation (established by FreeMask/FreestyleNet and SegGen/ControlNet for fully supervised settings) into the label-scarce regime, where those methods fail due to overfitting from fine-tuning hundreds of millions of parameters. The key conceptual departure is the Gradient Probe Method, which empirically identifies that only a small subset of cross-attention layers in high-resolution U-Net blocks govern spatial alignment, enabling a 0.7M-parameter LoRA adapter to replace 360M–850M parameter fine-tuning. The Layer-adaptive Cross-attention Fusion mechanism generalizes FreestyleNet's binary rectified attention by allowing each block to dynamically weight original vs. rectified attention, while the Relative Filtering Strategy departs from confidence-based pseudo-label filtering by exploiting inter-sample consistency across homologous generations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: pixel-level annotation remains the dominant bottleneck in deploying segmentation models, and demonstrating that a handful of labeled masks can bootstrap 5× data augmentation with 4.6–6.8% mIoU gains offers a concrete, low-cost remedy. The finding that spatial control in diffusion models is concentrated in a few identifiable layers (and is dataset-agnostic) could inform parameter-efficient adaptation more broadly. Open questions include whether the critical-layer identification transfers to other dense prediction tasks (depth, normal estimation), whether the approach scales to very high-resolution or 3D segmentation, and whether the relative filtering strategy remains effective when the underlying segmentation model is extremely weak (e.g., 1/512 samples).
