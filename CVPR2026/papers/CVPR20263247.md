## Problem

Existing multimodal emotion analysis methods treat all visual and acoustic tokens uniformly, diluting the sparse, localized emotional cues (e.g., a single facial micro-expression or brief prosodic burst) in a flood of neutral data. Additionally, implicit fusion paradigms assume temporal alignment across modalities, but emotional expression is inherently asynchronous—vocal changes precede facial expressions, which precede linguistic sentiment—causing entangled representations that obscure the logical progression of complex emotions and undermine interpretability.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video clip sampled at 1 FPS, its corresponding audio track (converted to log-Mel spectrograms), and a free-form text query. It outputs both a categorical emotion label and an explicit, structured reasoning trace (Chain-of-Emotion-Thought) that walks through modality-specific evidence assessment, inter-modal comparison, conflict resolution, and final judgment. The video is decomposed at the patch level via face detection with a dilation factor λ=8, and background tokens are randomly dropped at ratio δ=0.2 before concatenation with audio tokens.

## Method

EmoThinker introduces a Structural Token Selection mechanism that splits each frame into facial focus patches (encoded directly) and background patches (conditioned on text queries via cross-attention, then stochastically pruned), ensuring the model prioritizes emotionally salient regions while retaining conditioned spatial context. A parallel Audio Evidence Extractor encodes log-Mel spectrograms and applies the same text-guided cross-attention to concentrate on paralinguistic features like tone and rhythm. The fused token sequence is fed into a frozen Qwen2.5-Omni backbone, fine-tuned via CoT-LoRA (rank 32, alpha 64) on a newly constructed Chain-of-Emotion-Thought (CoET) dataset that provides step-by-step reasoning annotations—built through uni-modal captioning, hierarchical QA generation, and multimodal thought annotation using DeepFace/OpenFace for facial Action Units—enabling the model to perform explicit, interpretable emotion reasoning rather than implicit fusion.

## Results

On DFEW (zero-shot), EmoThinker achieves UAR 51.08 and WAR 65.63, surpassing the strongest baseline Emotion-LLaMA (UAR 45.59, WAR 59.37) by 5.5 and 6.3 percentage points respectively. Under fine-tuning on DFEW, EmoThinker reaches UAR 65.23 and WAR 78.13, edging out Emotion-LLaMA (64.21, 77.06). On IEMOCAP and MELD, EmoThinker attains the best weighted-F1 scores of 72.93 and 68.97 respectively. For the emotion reasoning task on EMER, EmoThinker achieves Clue overlap 8.67 and Label overlap 7.53, outperforming Emotion-LLaMA (8.22, 6.25) by a notable margin on the label metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoThinker consistently outperforms all 23 baselines across the six evaluation benchmarks. On DFEW zero-shot, it leads Emotion-LLaMA [9] by 5.5 UAR and 6.3 WAR points, and surpasses the strongest general-purpose VLM (Qwen2.5-VL [3]) by 2.6 UAR and 7.9 WAR. Under fine-tuning, EmoThinker edges out Emotion-LLaMA by 1.0 UAR and 1.1 WAR, and exceeds VideoMAE [42] (the strongest non-LLM baseline) by 1.6 UAR and 3.5 WAR. On IEMOCAP, the w-F1 margin over Emotion-LLaMA is narrow (72.93 vs. 72.89), while on MELD the gap widens to 1.86 points (68.97 vs. 67.11). For sarcasm and humor detection, EmoThinker achieves 67.84% on MUStARD and 66.61% on UR-FUNNY, beating Emotion-LLaMA by 0.69 and 0.89 points respectively. On the EMER reasoning benchmark, EmoThinker's Label overlap score of 7.53 exceeds Emotion-LLaMA's 6.25 by 1.28 points, indicating substantially better structured emotion reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmoThinker builds directly on Qwen2.5-Omni as its multimodal backbone, extending the LLaVA-style paradigm of projecting vision/audio features into a frozen LLM's token space. It departs from the dominant implicit-fusion approach in prior MEA work (Emotion-LLaMA, Omni-Emotion, MultiEMO) by replacing monolithic cross-modal attention with an explicit, structured reasoning pipeline: a structural token selection mechanism that separates facial focus patches from text-conditioned background context, and a Chain-of-Emotion-Thought (CoET) dataset that scaffolds step-by-step, modality-aware reasoning. The audio evidence extractor with text-guided cross-attention is a new component not present in prior emotion-specific LVLMs, and the CoET dataset is, to the authors' knowledge, the first to provide structured reasoning chains for multimodal emotion analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoET dataset and the explicit reasoning formulation address a genuine gap in MEA: prior methods produce a single emotion label with no interpretable justification, limiting trust and deployment in human-centric applications. The structured token selection idea—prioritizing facial regions while pruning background noise—is a practical efficiency and accuracy win that could transfer to other fine-grained visual analysis tasks. However, the paper acknowledges persistent struggles with minority emotion classes (Sad, Fear) due to class imbalance, and the fine-tuning gains over Emotion-LLaMA are modest (1.4% WAR on DFEW), suggesting the ceiling of the current approach may be limited. Open questions include whether adaptive (rather than fixed-ratio) token selection and more sophisticated conflict-resolution mechanisms could close the remaining gap on long-tail categories.
