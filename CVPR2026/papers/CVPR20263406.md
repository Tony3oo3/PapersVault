## Problem

Existing synthetic image detectors rely on model-specific artifacts or low-level statistical cues, leading to poor generalization across unseen GAN and diffusion model generators. This paper identifies a previously unexplored distinction: real images maintain consistent semantic attention and stable feature evolution across mid-level ViT layers, whereas synthetic images exhibit abrupt inter-layer shifts, providing a model-agnostic discriminative signal.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and produces a real/fake binary label using a 0.5 decision threshold. The frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 backbone is never fine-tuned; only lightweight downstream modules are trained. Notably, the detector requires only 2 training categories (chair, tvmonitor) for UFD/GenImage evaluation and converges in 5 epochs on a single RTX 4090 GPU.

## Method

The core mechanism exploits layer transition discrepancy (LTD) in a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14: a dynamic Gumbel-Softmax-based selection strategy adaptively picks the most informative window of 5 consecutive mid-level layers (around layers 11–19) per image, then computes pairwise differences between adjacent selected CLS tokens as LTD features. A dual-branch architecture processes these in parallel—one branch stacks the raw selected features to model holistic structural consistency, while the other amplifies the LTD to capture local inter-layer variations—with learnable positional encodings preserving hierarchical context. Both branches pass through weight-shared trainable ViT transformer blocks that enforce feature alignment into a unified semantic space, and the concatenated outputs feed an MLP classification head. This design bridges global structural alignment and local transition patterns, yielding superior generalization across diverse GAN and diffusion generators.

## Results

On the UFD benchmark, LTD achieves a mean accuracy of 96.90% and mean AP of 99.51% across 15 generative models (7 GANs, DeepFake, 3 LDM variants, ADM, 3 Glide variants, and DALL-E), surpassing the strongest baseline ForgeLens by 1.34% in Acc. On DRCT-2M, LTD reaches 99.54% mean Acc and 99.99% mean AP across 13 diffusion-based test sets, exceeding ForgeLens by 1.32% and UnivFD by 10.20%. On the cross-dataset GenImage benchmark, LTD attains 91.62% mean Acc and 97.17% mean AP, outperforming ForgeLens by 2.44% in Acc. Overall, LTD exceeds the base CLIP model by 14.35% in mean Acc across the three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LTD achieves the highest mean accuracy on all three benchmarks, winning every head-to-head comparison. On UFD, it surpasses the closest competitors ForgeLens (95.56%) and FatFormer (95.98%) by 1.34% and 0.92% respectively, while leading older methods like NPR (93.49%), RINE (93.16%), and C2P-CLIP (95.55%) by 1.3–3.7%. On DRCT-2M, the gap widens dramatically: LTD (99.54%) beats ForgeLens (98.22%) by 1.32%, DRCT (96.86%) by 2.68%, and UnivFD (89.34%) by 10.20%, while low-level methods like LGrad (50.72%) and AIDE (58.52%) are essentially at chance level. On GenImage, LTD (91.62%) leads ForgeLens (89.18%) by 2.44% and FatFormer (84.34%) by 7.28%, with GAN-focused methods (CNNSpot 56.65%, LGrad 67.92%) showing severe cross-domain failure. Notably, LTD is trained on only 2 categories (chair, tvmonitor) from UFD, whereas D³ uses 2 GANs and 6 DMs for training, yet LTD still outperforms D³ by 3.65% on UFD and 11.13% on DRCT-2M, underscoring its data efficiency and generalization advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operates within the frozen CLIP-ViT detection paradigm pioneered by UnivFD, which classifies using final-layer embeddings, and extended by RINE, FatFormer, and ForgeLens to incorporate intermediate-layer features. Rather than treating each layer's features as an independent signal, LTD repurposes the ViT's hierarchical structure by modeling the *differences* between adjacent mid-level layers as the primary discriminative cue, combined with a Gumbel-Softmax-based dynamic layer selection that adaptively identifies the most informative window per image. The dual-branch design (raw features for global consistency + LTD for local transitions) with weight-shared transformer blocks is a new architectural contribution that bridges holistic and local representations within a single frozen-backbone framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical efficiency is notable: training on only 2 categories (chair, tvmonitor) for 5 epochs on a single GPU yields SOTA across three benchmarks, making this approach accessible and easy to deploy. The conceptual finding that mid-level ViT layer transitions expose a model-agnostic signature of generative pipelines—distinct from low-level artifacts or high-level semantics—offers a new lens for understanding why synthetic images remain detectable. Open questions include whether the insight generalizes beyond CLIP-ViT to other frozen backbones (e.g., DINOv2, SAM), how it performs on video or 3D content, and whether the relatively lower Midjourney accuracy (62.97% on GenImage) reflects a fundamental limitation of the CLIP feature space for certain generators.
