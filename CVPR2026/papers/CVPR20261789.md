## Problem

Most existing 3D indoor benchmarks (ScanRefer, ScanQA, Scan2Cap, Multi3DRefer) contain 40–95% direction-critical queries that use egocentric or allocentric spatial terms (e.g., 'left', 'behind'), yet they provide no ego pose, rendering these questions inherently ill-posed for 3D large multimodal models. Without a defined viewer position and orientation, directional reasoning is ambiguous and 3D LMMs cannot achieve consistent spatial understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261789/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a scene-level 3D point cloud (from ScanNet-v2), a recovered ego pose (6-DoF camera extrinsics), and a natural-language query. The point cloud is rigidly transformed into the ego camera coordinate frame (front-left-up convention) before encoding, so the model receives geometry already aligned to the viewer's perspective. Outputs are task-dependent: binary segmentation masks for referring expression segmentation, free-form text answers for QA, or dense captions for captioning, all evaluated with both traditional metrics and LLM-as-judge accuracy.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary designs. PoseRecover is a fully automatic offline pipeline that computes camera-object intersection ratios by projecting object annotations (segmentation masks, bounding boxes, or point locations) into each ScanNet RGB-D camera frustum and validating visibility with Z-buffers or Monte-Carlo sampling, producing a per-scene intersection matrix; at training/inference time, a Clip strategy (discarding top and bottom X% of scores, default X=0.3) selects a diverse yet consistent ego pose. PoseAlign then incorporates this pose into the model via three variants—Transform (rigidly rotating/translating the point cloud into the camera frame, the default and best-performing option), Embed (adding MLP-encoded pose features to the projection layer), and Prompt (serializing pose as numeric text tokens). Training requires only instruction tuning with LoRA on the LLM and projection layer while freezing the 3D point-cloud encoder, making the approach generic across architectures (LL3DA, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLAVA) and avoiding any architectural redesign.

## Results

On the PoseRecover benchmark, 3D-LLAVA + PoseAlign-Transform achieves 55.4% ScanRefer mIoU (+12.8 over its unmodified baseline of 42.6%), 54.3% Multi3DRefer mIoU (+6.2), 47.3% ScanQA LLM-as-judge accuracy (+1.6), and 31.4% Scan2Cap LLM-as-judge accuracy (+3.3, +11.7% relative). The same PoseAlign-Transform variant also outperforms all specialist models on ScanRefer mIoU (55.4% vs. 39.5% for 3D-STMN) and Multi3DRefer mIoU (54.3% vs. 32.6% for M3DRef-CLIP). Across all four 3D LMM backbones (LL3DA, LL3DA-S, Chat-Scene, 3D-LLAVA), PoseAlign yields consistent gains of 1–3% on LLM-as-judge accuracy and 0.5–1.5% on traditional QA metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Within the PoseRecover benchmark, PoseAlign consistently improves every backbone: 3D-LLAVA + PoseAlign-T gains +12.8 mIoU on ScanRefer and +6.2 mIoU on Multi3DRefer over its unmodified counterpart, while Chat-Scene + PoseAlign-E gains +0.5 A@0.5 on ScanRefer and +1.1 CiDEr on ScanQA. Against specialist models, 3D-LLAVA + PoseAlign-T substantially outperforms the best specialist on ScanRefer mIoU (55.4% vs. 39.5% for 3D-STMN) and Multi3DRefer mIoU (54.3% vs. 32.6% for M3DRef-CLIP), and matches or exceeds the best specialist on ScanQA CiDEr (99.8 vs. 85.6 for Chat-Scene). On Scan2Cap, 3D-LLAVA + PoseAlign-T achieves the highest LLM-as-judge accuracy (31.4%) among all generalist models, surpassing LEO (no L-A reported) and Grounded 3D-LLM (no L-A reported), while its CiDEr@0.5 (76.1) slightly trails LEO (72.4) and Grounded 3D-LLM (70.6) on the full set but leads on the @0.5 thresholded variant. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that PoseAlign-Transform with the Clip strategy (X=0.3) is the strongest variant, outperforming PoseAlign-Embed (43.2% vs. 55.4% ScanRefer mIoU) and PoseAlign-Prompt (44.2% vs. 55.4%), and that using random poses instead of PoseRecover-recovered poses degrades performance below the unmodified baseline, validating the pose recovery pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior attempts to address directional ambiguity in 3D LMMs (SQA3D's textual ego descriptions, View2Cap's pose regression, Scene-LLM's two-step frustum cropping) by arguing that ego poses should be treated as explicit, recoverable inputs rather than latent variables to be inferred. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it repurposes existing 3D LMM backbones (LL3DA, 3D-LLAVA, Chat-Scene) through a data-level intervention: PoseRecover recovers camera poses from ScanNet RGB-D extrinsics via frustum-object intersection, and PoseAlign simply transforms the point cloud into the ego frame. The conceptual lineage is closest to 2D egocentric vision where camera pose is a standard input, but applied here to point-cloud-based 3D LMMs where no prior work had made this explicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 40–95% of questions in widely-used 3D benchmarks are direction-critical yet ill-posed reframes a large portion of the 3D LMM literature as operating under an incomplete problem specification, and the proposed fix is lightweight enough to be adopted broadly. Limitations include dependence on SLAM accuracy, a static-scene assumption, and evaluation restricted to ScanNet-v2-derived datasets, leaving open whether the approach transfers to outdoor, dynamic, or non-ScanNet environments. A key open question is whether the recovered poses are always semantically correct for the annotator's intended viewpoint, since the pipeline selects from multiple candidate cameras rather than recovering a single ground-truth pose.
