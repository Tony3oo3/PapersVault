## Problem

Current audio-enhanced video-text retrieval methods suffer from two intertwined deficiencies: (1) the audio encoders (ResNet-18, AST) are trained on environmental sound datasets and produce poorly separated embeddings for speech content, leaving semantic dialogue information unexploited; and (2) vision and audio features are misaligned by definition, so directly fusing them via cross-attention without pre-alignment leads to suboptimal integration. The paper addresses both issues jointly rather than treating them in isolation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a short video (uniformly sampled into m frames), its sound track (converted to Mel filter bank features for AST, and transcribed to a word sequence via Whisper large-v3 for the speech branch), and a text query. The CLIP text encoder is shared between the speech branch and the query branch during training. Output is a speech-aware video representation consisting of m fused tokens and their mean, matched against the query embedding via a multi-grained similarity (cosine on the mean plus log-sum-exp over per-token scores, averaged). Missing audio or ASR transcripts are handled by zero-padding the Mel features or feeding an empty string to the tokenizer.

## Method

SAVE extends AVIGATE from a bi-branch to a tri-branch architecture by adding a dedicated speech branch that transcribes narration with Whisper and encodes the transcript using the CLIP text encoder, then applies the same gated cross-attention fusion (visual tokens as Q, speech tokens as K/V) used for the audio branch. The fused video tokens are computed parameter-free as {v_i} + ({â_i} + {ŝ_i})/2, which implicitly weights vision higher while letting the gated fusion modules learn which audio/speech signals matter. The key training innovation is soft-ALBEF: instead of enforcing hard vision-audio alignment (which fails because many video-sound pairs lack semantic correspondence), ImageBind computes a B×B soft video-audio affinity matrix that supervises the network's pre-fusion affinity matrix via a Pearson distance loss, making alignment noise-tolerant and invariant to scale/shift. The total loss combines this auxiliary Pearson loss equally with AVIGATE's adaptive margin-based contrastive loss, and the model is fine-tuned from CLIP ViT-B/32 and AST (AudioSet-pretrained) weights with a low learning rate (1e-7) on the CLIP backbone to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

SAVE achieves new state-of-the-art text-to-video retrieval across all five benchmarks, with SumR of 216.2 (MSRVTT-9k), 165.8 (MSRVTT-7k), 255.5 (VATEX), 121.4 (Charades), and 128.3 (LSMDC), yielding a mean R1 of 39.6. Compared to the prior SOTA audiovisual method AVIGATE, SAVE improves SumR by +8.5 on MSRVTT-9k, +3.1 on MSRVTT-7k, +6.2 on VATEX, +10.8 on Charades, and +2.6 on LSMDC. The largest relative gain appears on Charades, where only 13.5% of videos contain ASR transcripts, yet soft-ALBEF alignment still yields a substantial 10.8-point SumR improvement. SAVE also matches AVIGATE's inference latency (9.90 ms) while maintaining linear computational complexity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAVE outperforms all 17 vision-only baselines on every dataset where they report results, with the closest competitor PIG [23] trailing by +13.2 SumR on MSRVTT-9k and +3.4 on VATEX. Among audiovisual methods, SAVE surpasses AVIGATE [18] (the prior SOTA) by +8.5 SumR on MSRVTT-9k, +10.8 on Charades, and +6.2 on VATEX, while also beating TEFAL [17] by +7.0 and +17.0 SumR on MSRVTT-9k and Charades respectively. The ablation variant AVIGATE+ (AVIGATE augmented with soft-ALBEF but without the speech branch) reaches 211.9 SumR on MSRVTT-9k, confirming that the dedicated speech branch contributes an additional +4.3 points beyond alignment alone. On Charades, where only 13.5% of videos have ASR transcripts, SAVE still gains +10.8 SumR over AVIGATE, attributed primarily to soft-ALBEF better exploiting the sound modality. Inference latency is identical to AVIGATE (9.90 ms on MSRVTT-9k), as all video-side features (visual, audio, speech) are pre-extracted offline, preserving the linear O(n_V + n_A + n_S + n_T) complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAVE extends AVIGATE (CVPR 2025), the current SOTA for audio-enhanced video-text retrieval, by adding a dedicated speech branch that transcribes narration via Whisper and encodes it with the CLIP text encoder, addressing the observation that environmental-sound-trained encoders (AST, ResNet-18) produce poorly separated speech embeddings. The soft-ALBEF alignment strategy adapts the 'align before fuse' principle from ALBEF (NeurIPS 2021) to the vision-audio setting, replacing hard labels with ImageBind-derived soft relevance scores to handle the frequent semantic mismatch between video content and soundtrack. The speech-branch design draws on prior work showing ASR text is effective for cross-domain product retrieval, repurposing that insight within the CLIP-based VTR framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnostic (the toy experiment) showing that the audio encoders standard in the field are fundamentally unsuited for speech, and offers a practical, low-overhead fix (ASR + shared text encoder) that consistently improves retrieval across five benchmarks. The finding that directly applying ALBEF with hard labels degrades performance relative to no alignment at all highlights a non-trivial challenge in vision-audio pre-alignment that soft supervision addresses. A key open question, acknowledged by the authors, is how the approach generalizes to longer, noisier ASR transcripts (e.g., e-commerce livestreams) that exceed the CLIP text encoder's context window, which limits immediate applicability to longer-form video understanding.
