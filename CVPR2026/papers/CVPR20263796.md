## Problem

3D vision foundation models (VGGT, π³, MapAnything) are designed for offline reconstruction, but in online settings they predict geometry independently per submap, producing spatially varying inconsistencies that no single global transformation (Sim(3) or SL(4)) can correct. Existing alignment strategies assume a globally uniform error field, perform only pairwise (short-term) alignment, and are highly sensitive to geometry noise, leading to trajectory drift and catastrophic divergence in over 60% of outdoor multi-camera scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263796/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a synchronized multi-camera RGB video stream (arbitrary camera count C, from monocular to 6-camera surround-view) at 2 Hz, segmented into submaps of L new frames with O overlapping frames (O ≤ L). Each submap is independently processed by a plug-and-play 3DVFMs backbone to yield per-camera intrinsics, poses, and dense point clouds. The output is a globally consistent camera trajectory and point cloud in a unified reference frame, achieved through per-submap TPS deformation fields that warp geometry into a shared canonical space without requiring the full sequence to be available.

## Method

TALO replaces global linear warps with a higher-DOF, spatially varying Thin Plate Spline deformation (biharmonic kernel φ(r)=r) that corrects local geometric distortions while regularizing toward local rigidity. Consecutive submaps are registered via a point-agnostic strategy: the inter-submap transformation is obtained by averaging the relative poses of overlapping frames using Chordal L₂ rotation averaging, avoiding sensitivity to noisy point clouds. Sparse control points are generated via voxelization for even spatial coverage, then propagated both forward and backward across the entire sequence through overlapping frames to accumulate multi-view observations. Each control point's canonical position is estimated by MAD-filtered mean aggregation across all submaps, followed by Gaussian-weighted local smoothing of targets. The TPS parameters are solved in closed form by minimizing an energy combining interpolation error and bending-energy regularization, yielding a smooth, differentiable deformation applied to all points in each submap.

## Results

On Waymo (5 cameras) and nuScenes (6 cameras), TALO achieves the best trajectory accuracy across all three foundation models (VGGT, π³, MapAnything) with zero failure cases, maintaining average ATE around ~1 m. The most striking improvement is in rotational accuracy: on Waymo with VGGT, TALO reduces RRE from 0.71° (VGGT-Long) to 0.14°, a nearly 5× improvement. In contrast, VGGT-SLAM's SL(4) alignment diverges in over 60% of tested outdoor sequences, with average ATE reaching 12–30 m. On geometry (Chamfer Distance), TALO also leads across most scenes, e.g., 0.62 m vs. 0.75 m (VGGT-Long) and 6.95 m (VGGT-SLAM) on Waymo with VGGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TALO consistently outperforms VGGT-Long (Sim(3) alignment) across all backbones and datasets: on Waymo with VGGT, TALO reduces average ATE from 1.42 m to 1.09 m and RRE from 0.71° to 0.14° (≈5×); on nuScenes with MapAnything, ATE drops from 2.34 m to 0.91 m. VGGT-SLAM (SL(4) alignment) is catastrophically unstable in outdoor multi-camera settings, failing in over 60% of sequences with ATE values 10–30× worse than TALO (e.g., 30.50 m vs. 1.40 m average on Waymo with MapAnything). In the monocular ablation on Waymo, TALO (1.08 m avg ATE) substantially outperforms DROID-SLAM (3.59 m), MASt3R-SLAM (3.92 m), and CUT3R (9.44 m), while also matching or slightly beating VGGT-Long (1.27 m). On geometry (Chamfer Distance), TALO achieves the best scores in most scenes, e.g., 0.62 m vs. 0.75 m (VGGT-Long) and 6.95 m (VGGT-SLAM) on Waymo with VGGT, though the gap over VGGT-Long is smaller than the trajectory gap due to LiDAR coverage masking local misalignment artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

TALO builds directly on the submap-alignment paradigm introduced by VGGT-Long (Sim(3) registration) and VGGT-SLAM (SL(4) registration) for online reconstruction with feed-forward 3D vision foundation models. It departs from both by replacing their single global linear warp with a higher-DOF Thin Plate Spline deformation driven by globally propagated control points, and by substituting point-cloud-based registration with a point-agnostic strategy that averages relative camera poses from overlapping frames. The broader 3DVFMs it plugs into (VGGT, π³, MapAnything) and the streaming-reconstruction line (Stream3R, CUT3R, SLAM3R) provide the architectural context, but TALO's contribution is specifically in the alignment and consistency layer rather than the perception backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that SL(4) alignment catastrophically fails in over 60% of outdoor multi-camera scenes across three foundation models is a practical warning for the community deploying 3DVFMs in driving or robotics. TALO's plug-and-play design means any future 3DVFMs can inherit its consistency mechanism without architectural changes, and the point-agnostic registration offers a simple, robust alternative to point-cloud alignment that may generalize beyond this specific setting. Open questions include how the method scales to very long sequences (hours of driving), whether the TPS deformation model is optimal versus learned deformations, and how it interacts with explicit loop-closure or global bundle-adjustment backends. The paper also honestly notes that LiDAR-based geometry metrics understate the visual quality gains, leaving a gap in evaluation methodology for camera-only reconstruction.
