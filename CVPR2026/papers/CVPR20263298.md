## Problem

Unified image generation and editing models built on dense diffusion transformers suffer from severe task interference, as a shared parameter space must simultaneously satisfy conflicting objectives such as local content preservation and expressive subject-driven synthesis. While sparse Mixture-of-Experts architectures can expand capacity, their gating networks remain task-agnostic—routing based solely on local token features while being oblivious to the global task intent—preventing meaningful expert specialization and failing to structurally disentangle multi-task conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source image and a free-form text instruction; at inference, a VLM (Qwen-VL) rewrites the raw instruction into a richer descriptive prompt before encoding. Internally, the source image and noisy target latent are VAE-encoded and patchified into visual tokens, concatenated with text embeddings and a timestep embedding into a single sequence fed to the MM-DiT. Training additionally requires per-sample hierarchical task tags (scope, type, preservation) generated by an automated Qwen-VL pipeline, though these are not needed at inference time.

## Method

TAG-MoE replaces the FFNs in the final 10 layers of a Qwen-Image-based MM-DiT with sparse MoE layers (4 experts, top-1 routing) and introduces a Predictive Alignment Regularization that injects global task semantics into local routing decisions. A three-tier Hierarchical Task Semantic Annotation scheme (scope, type, preservation) provides structured per-sample tags, which are mapped to a fixed-dimension global semantic embedding via a learnable tag embedding matrix. An aggregated routing signature is computed by mean-pooling gating scores across all MoE layers and tokens, and a lightweight two-layer MLP prediction head is trained to reconstruct the semantic embedding from this signature using a cosine-similarity loss. Minimizing this alignment loss backpropagates gradients through the routing signature into every gating network, compelling the router to evolve from a task-agnostic executor into a semantic-aware dispatch center that routes tokens to experts whose aggregate activation pattern is predictive of the task's macro-semantics.

## Results

On ICE-Bench (the primary unified benchmark), TAG-MoE achieves the best open-source scores on aesthetic quality (5.399), CLIP-cap (0.282), and vLLM-QA (0.852), with CLIP-cap even surpassing closed-source GPT-4o (0.278) and Gemini-2.5-flash (0.281). On EmuEdit-Bench and GEdit-Bench, it leads on the vLLM-QA instruction-following metric (0.9284 and 0.8854 respectively), outperforming the next-best Qwen-Edit (0.9174 and 0.875). On subject-driven generation, it attains SOTA Face-ref (0.3678 on DreamBench++, 0.7628 on OmniContext) and Style-ref (0.5679 on DreamBench++, 0.5607 on OmniContext) scores, matching or exceeding specialized models like Kontext and DreamOmni2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source unified models, TAG-MoE clearly outperforms Qwen-Edit (the strongest open-source competitor) on aesthetic quality (5.399 vs. 5.358), CLIP-cap (0.282 vs. 0.279), and vLLM-QA (0.852 vs. 0.774), while DreamOmni2 retains a slight edge on CLIP-ref (0.739 vs. 0.732) and CLIP-src (0.866 vs. 0.857), which the authors attribute to DreamOmni2's copy-paste behavior inflating similarity metrics. On the editing-specific benchmarks (EmuEdit, GEdit), TAG-MoE leads on vLLM-QA over all baselines including Qwen-Edit (0.9284 vs. 0.9174 on EmuEdit; 0.8854 vs. 0.875 on GEdit) and BAGEL (0.7989/0.7961), though Kontext achieves marginally higher CLIP-src. For subject-driven generation, TAG-MoE surpasses Kontext on Face-ref (0.3678 vs. 0.3429) and Style-ref (0.5679 vs. 0.5655) on DreamBench++, and on OmniContext it leads on Face-ref (0.7628 vs. 0.7494) and Style-ref (0.5607 vs. 0.5596), while DreamOmni2 edges ahead on CLIP-ref (0.7733 vs. 0.7297). Against closed-source models, TAG-MoE's CLIP-cap (0.282) exceeds both GPT-4o (0.278) and Gemini-2.5-flash (0.281), but GPT-4o still leads on vLLM-QA (0.889 vs. 0.852) and aesthetic quality (5.801 vs. 5.399). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that removing the predictive alignment loss degrades vLLM-QA from 0.847 to 0.677 and Face-ref from 0.4642 to 0.3779, validating that task-aware routing—not just sparse capacity—is the key driver of the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Qwen-Image MM-DiT architecture and extends the sparse MoE paradigm (DiT-MoE, Dense2MoE, HunyuanImage-3.0) from single-task text-to-image generation to the heterogeneous unified generation-and-editing setting. The key departure is from standard task-agnostic MoE gating: prior MoE-in-DiT work routes based solely on local token features, which the authors argue is fundamentally insufficient when the shared parameter space must simultaneously satisfy conflicting objectives (e.g., local editing vs. subject-driven generation). The hierarchical task semantic annotation and predictive alignment regularization are novel mechanisms that inject global task intent into local routing decisions, positioning this work at the intersection of multi-task learning and sparse expert architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and validates a concrete architectural bottleneck (task-agnostic routing) that has likely been silently degrading unified generation models, and provides a principled fix that is relatively lightweight (a two-layer MLP prediction head plus a cosine-similarity loss). The ablation showing that MoE without the alignment loss still beats dense baselines but substantially underperforms the full model is practically important: it means the benefit is not merely from extra capacity but from semantically guided specialization. Open questions include whether the annotation scheme generalizes to entirely new task types without re-annotation, how the approach scales to larger expert counts or deeper MoE insertion, and whether the VLM-based inference-time instruction rewriting introduces latency or error propagation in production settings.
