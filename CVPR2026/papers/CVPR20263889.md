## Problem

MLLMs frequently hallucinate because their visual attention mechanism, while the primary conduit for interpreting visual information, has limited ability to accurately localize relevant image regions. Existing training-free attention-optimization methods either depend on the model's own (flawed) attention distributions or require additional forward passes, making them incompatible with FlashAttention. The paper identifies that MLLMs already encode precise visual semantics in their visual logits but fail to exploit this signal during subsequent inference.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play, training-free module on frozen MLLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, LLaVA-Next, Qwen2.5-VL). For VQA, objects are extracted from the question text via Stanza NLP parsing to construct object-specific grounding; for image captioning, grounding is object-agnostic, derived from output uncertainty over visual logits. Each token requires only a single forward pass, and the method is fully compatible with FlashAttention since it never needs to materialize explicit attention weight matrices.

## Method

VGA first constructs token-level visual grounding via Visual Semantic Confidence (VSC): the softmax probability of a target word in each visual token's logit row measures that token's semantic relevance to the object, and max-pooling across tokens yields a normalized grounding vector. This grounding is then added to the attention output as a correction term (z + β·γ·Δz), steering attention toward informative visual tokens without computing explicit attention weights. An attention-head balancing coefficient γ, derived from cosine similarity between vanilla and corrected outputs, applies weaker guidance to visually specialized heads. For image captioning, a Programmed Vision-Guidance (PVG) mechanism dynamically suppresses already-described regions at each generation step, shifting focus toward undescribed content and progressively weakening guidance as the caption grows.

## Results

On POPE (object hallucination VQA), VGA achieves the best accuracy across all four MLLMs and all three subsets (Random, Popular, Adversarial), e.g., 89.28% accuracy / 89.41% F1 on LLaVA-1.5-7B Random vs. 87.12% for vanilla. On CHAIR (image captioning), VGA reduces the hallucination rate to Cs=29.8 / Ci=8.2 on LLaVA-1.5-7B, a 43% relative reduction in Cs over vanilla (52.2). On AMBER (hybrid benchmark), VGA attains the highest AMBER score of 38.00 for LLaVA-7B (vs. 34.35 vanilla) and 43.40 for Qwen2.5-VL (vs. 43.15 vanilla), while also improving F1 to 79.5 and 90.5 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGA consistently outperforms all three baselines (PAI, VAF, TARAC) across every model and benchmark. On POPE-Adversarial (the hardest subset), VGA improves LLaVA-7B accuracy from 71.85% (vanilla) to 77.02%, surpassing PAI (70.83%), VAF (69.59%), and TARAC (71.92%) by 5–7 points. On CHAIR, VGA reduces the sentence-level hallucination rate (Cs) on LLaVA-7B from 52.2 (vanilla) to 29.8, beating PAI (33.6), VAF (52.4), and TARAC (43.0) by 3.8–22.6 points. On AMBER, VGA achieves the highest composite score for LLaVA-7B (38.00 vs. PAI 33.85, VAF 32.50, TARAC 35.00) and Qwen2.5-VL (43.40 vs. TARAC 43.20, VAF 42.90, PAI 41.75). Notably, VAF underperforms vanilla on POPE for LLaVA-7B (84.88% vs. 87.12%), while VGA improves over vanilla in every setting, indicating that blindly amplifying visual attention can be counterproductive without precise grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior training-free hallucination mitigation methods (PAI, VAF, TARAC) that directly optimize or redistribute visual attention weights, arguing that such approaches are fundamentally limited by the poor localization quality of visual attention itself (the 'attention sink' phenomenon). Instead, it introduces Visual Semantic Confidence (VSC), a novel visual prior extracted from the model's own visual logits, which provides token-level grounding without requiring explicit attention weights. This design choice makes VGA fully compatible with FlashAttention—a practical constraint that renders most prior attention-optimization methods inapplicable. The method also extends the idea of dynamic guidance (seen in TARAC's temporal accumulation) into a more principled form via Programmed Vision-Guidance for captioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MLLMs already encode accurate visual semantics in their visual logits but fail to exploit them during inference reframes hallucination as an underutilization problem rather than a perception failure, opening a new angle for mitigation. The FlashAttention compatibility and single-forward-pass design make VGA immediately deployable in production MLLM pipelines without architectural changes. Open questions include whether VSC grounding quality degrades on complex multi-object scenes or video inputs, whether the approach transfers to non-LLaVA architectures with different visual tokenization, and whether the moderate per-benchmark gains compound in downstream agentic or multi-turn visual reasoning settings.
