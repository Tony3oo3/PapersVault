## Problem

Underwater object detection is severely degraded by foreground-background ambiguity, blurred object boundaries, and reduced contrast, causing existing detectors to lose structural detail, misallocate semantic focus, and suffer cross-scale alignment drift. Prior methods rely on increasing network complexity or generic attention mechanisms that fail to explicitly model blurred regions or enforce semantic consistency across scales.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262955/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes standard underwater RGB images (no preprocessing or dehazing) and outputs per-object bounding boxes with class labels via a dual-task detection head (classification + regression). Experiments use ResNet-50 as the shared backbone across DUO (7,782 images), UTDAC (5,643 images), and COCO (118,287 images), trained for 35 epochs with SGD under the MMDetection framework.

## Method

RHCNet embeds a Residual-Guided Feature Enhancement (RGFE) module inside the ResNet-50 backbone that extracts high-frequency edge cues via depthwise separable convolutions (SCT), refines them through a dual-path channel-and-spatial attention correction unit, and injects the result back into the semantic stream via a learnable intensity coefficient λ to counteract blurring. A Hierarchical Feature Calibration Pyramid (HFCP) then performs a calibrate-then-fuse strategy: a bottom-up path accumulates global semantics through a channel branch and multi-scale convolutions, while a top-down path uses a Position-Aware Module to project high-level context downward and rectify fragmented low-level features. Crucially, a K-means clustering module (k=2) constructs foreground/background semantic prototypes from detached features, and differentiable attention maps weighted by softmax similarity to cluster centers are used to filter and align features before fusion, avoiding the noise sensitivity of pixel-to-pixel non-local operations. Training uses a Task-Adaptive Quality Focal Loss with continuous quality labels (geometric mean of IoU and centerness) for classification and GIoU loss for regression, jointly suppressing low-quality false detections from background interference.

## Results

On the DUO dataset, RHCNet achieves the highest AP of 70.53% (AP50: 87.56%, AP75: 77.29%), surpassing the next-best method CIDNet (68.83% AP) by 1.7 points. On the more challenging UTDAC dataset, RHCNet attains 53.35% AP (AP50: 86.93%, AP75: 58.97%), outperforming YOLOv11 (49.75% AP) by 3.6 points. On the general-purpose COCO dataset, RHCNet also leads with 45.68% AP (AP50: 63.51%, AP75: 49.36%), exceeding YOLOv11 (44.18% AP) by 1.5 points, demonstrating strong cross-domain generalization. The model uses 70.04M parameters and 145.16G FLOPs with a ResNet-50 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHCNet outperforms all 16 competing methods on both underwater benchmarks. On DUO, it leads CIDNet (68.83% AP) by 1.7 points and RTMD-R (68.23%) by 2.3 points, with particularly strong gains on AP75 (77.29% vs. 75.78% for CIDNet), indicating superior localization precision. On UTDAC, the margin widens: RHCNet's 53.35% AP exceeds YOLOv11 (49.75%) by 3.6 points and CIDNet (49.57%) by 3.8 points, with a notable 4.4-point advantage in AP75 (58.97% vs. 54.96% for YOLOv11), reflecting better handling of severe background interference. On COCO, RHCNet (45.68% AP) surpasses YOLOv11 (44.18%) by 1.5 points and RTMD-R (43.27%) by 2.4 points, confirming that the residual-guided calibration design generalizes beyond underwater scenes. Notably, RHCNet achieves these results with a standard ResNet-50 backbone and 70M parameters, while methods like UDMD and RFTM use 72M parameters with 234G FLOPs yet underperform by 3+ AP points on DUO. The ablation (Table 2) further confirms that replacing HFCP with BiFPN drops AP from 70.53% to 66.45%, and removing the K-means-guided calibration (CGCA) costs 4.67 AP points, validating the necessity of the proposed calibration over generic attention or fusion alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

RHCNet extends the standard FPN/BiFPN multi-scale fusion paradigm by introducing a calibrate-then-fuse strategy, where K-means clustering constructs semantic prototypes to align features by content similarity rather than spatial proximity before aggregation. The RGFE module departs from generic attention mechanisms (CBAM, SE) that perform implicit reweighting, instead adopting a signal-restoration paradigm that explicitly injects high-frequency structural cues via depthwise separable convolutions and residual guidance. The loss function adapts the quality-aware formulations from GFL and ATSS with continuous geometric quality labels tailored to underwater ambiguity. Conceptually, the paper repurposes K-means clustering (typically a standalone unsupervised tool) as a differentiable calibration mechanism within a supervised detection pipeline, which is a non-trivial architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The calibrate-then-fuse paradigm with cluster-guided semantic alignment offers a principled alternative to the direct summation assumption in FPN/BiFPN, potentially applicable to other degraded-imaging domains (fog, low-light, medical). The paper acknowledges that domain gaps across varying turbidity levels remain unresolved, and the fixed k=2 clustering for foreground/background separation is a simplification that may not generalize to scenes with multiple object classes or complex backgrounds. The strong COCO generalization (45.68% AP with ResNet-50, outperforming YOLOv11) suggests the architectural ideas are not overfit to underwater conditions, but the moderate parameter count (70M) and FLOPs (145G) limit real-time deployment compared to lightweight alternatives like YOLOv10/11.
