## Problem

Existing visual token pruning methods for MLLMs assume all attention heads contribute equally to visual interpretation, simply averaging attention scores across heads. This paper reveals through systematic ablation that different heads capture distinct visual semantics and play markedly different roles in visual processing, so equal weighting leads to retention of redundant tokens while inadvertently pruning semantically critical ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (or video standardized to 8 frames) and a text prompt as input. The image is encoded into a sequence of visual tokens (e.g., 1296 tokens at 1008×1008 fixed resolution for Qwen2.5-VL, or a variable count under native-resolution mode), while the text is tokenized into textual embeddings. Pruning is applied at the first attention layer: a top-k subset of visual tokens is selected and concatenated with the full text sequence, which is then passed through all subsequent LLM layers for autoregressive text generation. The method supports both fixed-resolution and native-resolution (variable-length) inputs.

## Method

HAWK integrates two complementary signals to score each visual token. First, a static head importance weight vector is computed offline by ablating each attention head's access to visual tokens across multiple benchmark datasets, measuring the resulting performance drop, and averaging L1-normalized drops across datasets. Second, a dynamic text-guided attention score is computed at inference time by projecting text embeddings as queries and visual embeddings as keys using the first-layer attention projections, deliberately omitting RoPE to eliminate positional bias, then averaging attention over all text tokens to yield per-head, per-token relevance. The final importance of each visual token is the weighted sum of its per-head attention scores, weighted by the precomputed head importance vector. The top-k tokens by this composite score are retained while the rest are discarded. The entire procedure is training-free, requiring only a single offline ablation pass to obtain head weights, and is plug-and-play across MLLM architectures.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-7B with fixed 1008×1008 resolution (1296 visual tokens), HAWK retains 99.0% of original average performance after pruning 60.5% of tokens, 96.0% after pruning 80.2%, and 90.0% after pruning 90.1%, consistently outperforming all baselines at every pruning ratio. On InternVL3-8B, HAWK maintains 98.2% and 94.1% relative performance at 60% and 80% pruning respectively. In efficiency analysis on MME, HAWK at 80% pruning achieves a 1.34× end-to-end speedup (20m15s → 15m04s) while reducing KV cache from 668 MB to 148 MB and GPU memory from 16.9 GB to 15.7 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAWK outperforms all three baselines across every pruning ratio and every individual benchmark in Table 1. At the headline 80.2% pruning setting on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, HAWK achieves 96.0% relative performance, beating FastV (92.6%) by 3.4 pp, CDPruner (92.5%) by 3.5 pp, and DivPrune (91.3%) by 4.7 pp. The gap widens at extreme pruning: at 90.1% pruning, HAWK reaches 90.0% vs. CDPruner's 85.9% (4.1 pp), FastV's 83.9% (6.1 pp), and DivPrune's 82.8% (7.2 pp). On InternVL3-8B (Table 2), HAWK leads DivPrune by 5.2 pp at 60% pruning and by 7.0 pp at 80% pruning, while CDPruner degrades sharply (74.8% at 80% vs. HAWK's 94.1%). On video benchmarks (Table 4), HAWK maintains 96.6% at 80% pruning versus 95.3–95.7% for the baselines, and at 90% pruning all four methods converge near 93–94%, with HAWK still marginally ahead. The consistent advantage is largest on text-heavy benchmarks (ChartQA, OCRVQA, TextVQA), where head-importance-aware pruning preserves OCR-critical tokens that uniform-attention baselines discard.

## Relation to Prior Work

HAWK extends the attention-based visual token pruning line (FastV, CDPruner) by challenging their implicit assumption that all attention heads contribute equally to visual interpretation. Rather than uniformly averaging head-level attention scores, it introduces a static head importance weight derived from a one-time offline ablation study, combined with a dynamic text-guided attention score that omits RoPE to isolate semantic relevance. The method is conceptually a repurposing of the well-known attention-head specialization phenomenon from NLP into the MLLM pruning setting, wrapped in a training-free, plug-and-play pipeline that sits alongside similarity-based (DivPrune) and hybrid (CDPruner) approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying MLLMs in latency- or memory-constrained settings, HAWK offers a practical, training-free recipe that retains ~96% accuracy after pruning 80% of visual tokens while cutting end-to-end latency by ~26% and reducing KV cache size substantially. The empirical finding that head importance is consistent across datasets is useful but raises open questions: whether the offline-computed weights transfer to unseen tasks or newly fine-tuned checkpoints, and whether the benefit persists as model scale grows. The method's reliance on a fixed set of benchmark datasets for the ablation step also limits its robustness claim, and no analysis is provided on how head importance interacts with layer depth or with the vision encoder's own attention.
