## Problem

Most RGB-X (thermal, NIR, SAR) research assumes pixel-aligned sensor pairs already exist, yet obtaining them in practice demands cumbersome calibration—measuring intrinsics, synchronizing sensors, estimating relative poses, and acquiring metric depth—with errors propagating across stages. Cross-modal image matchers can estimate a homography for warping, but this assumes planar scenes and fails on multi-depth structures. The paper tackles this fundamental data-alignment bottleneck with a scalable framework that requires no 3D priors for the X-sensor.

## Input / Output

The method takes unpaired RGB and X-modality image sequences (thermal, NIR, or SAR) with no X intrinsics, no X metric depth, and no cross-sensor relative pose; the only external dependency is standard COLMAP on RGB for camera poses (treated as no-cost). It produces dense X-images pixel-aligned to the RGB view, and optionally an RGB-X 3D Gaussian Splatting radiance field for multi-view-consistent synthesis. For texture-less regions (sky, walls, grass), GroundedSAM segmentation guides sparse area sampling from homography-warped X-images to seed the densification.

## Method

The pipeline follows a match-densify-consolidate strategy. First, a cross-modal matcher (XoFTR) extracts sparse keypoints across N frames, which are accumulated onto the RGB view to form a semi-dense X-map with per-point confidence scores. The key architectural contribution is the Confidence-Aware Densification and Fusion (CADF) module: a recurrent densifier (backbone of recurrent units + DySPN layers) is modified so that the matching-confidence map C_m is interleaved into DySPN's iterative refinement (Eq. 4), weighting down low-confidence points and concentrating propagation on reliable ones; K-level confidence thresholds produce multiple densified maps that are fused by a pretrained enhancement network F trained with self-supervised SigLIP2 cosine-similarity and self-matching losses. A self-matching filtering step then uses the matcher's patch-level similarity matrix to reject inconsistent patches, followed by fine-stage re-densification. Finally, RGB-X 3D Gaussian Splatting (with shared Gaussian parameters and added X-channels) consolidates per-frame results into a unified 3D radiance field for multi-view consistency.

## Results

On RGB-NIR-Stereo, the method achieves PSNR 21.15 dB, SSIM 0.581, and LPIPS 0.344, outperforming the strongest homography-warping baseline (MINIMA: 20.39/0.568/0.360) and the image-generation baseline PixNext (11.28/0.441/0.452). On METU-VisTIR-Cloudy (no groundtruth), it attains the best image cosine similarity (0.69) and highest 90th-percentile matcher similarity (38.72) across all six sequences. On RGBT-Scenes with groundtruth temperatures, it achieves the lowest mean RMSE of 1.70 °C (train) and 1.12 °C (novel view), and on DDHR-HK SAR it reaches PSNR 17.10 vs. 14.85 for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all four homography-warping baselines (XoFTR, LightGLUE, LoFTR, MINIMA) across every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is MINIMA (which uses a modality-invariant matcher with LightGLUE), but the proposed method still gains ~0.76 dB PSNR on RGB-NIR-Stereo and ~0.06 °C lower RMSE on RGBT-Scenes. Against the image-generation baselines, the advantage is even larger: PixNext (RGB→NIR translation) scores 11.28 dB PSNR versus 21.15 dB for the proposed method, and StyleBooth (RGB→thermal) shows worse multi-view temporal consistency (MEt3R 0.297 vs. 0.171). Notably, even without the final 3DGS stage, the method achieves 21.04 dB PSNR, still surpassing all baselines that do use 3DGS, indicating the match-densify pipeline itself is the primary source of improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in RGB-X research, which assumes pixel-aligned pairs already exist and focuses on fusion, by tackling the upstream data-alignment problem directly. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for multi-view consolidation, DySPN's recurrent spatial propagation for iterative densification, and OmniDC-style recurrent backbones, but introduces a key conceptual shift: replacing full 3D priors (metric depth, cross-sensor calibration, intrinsics) for the X-modality with a match-densify-consolidate pipeline that only requires COLMAP on RGB. Unlike prior cross-modal matching work (XoFTR, MINIMA, LoFTR) that estimates a homography and warps—constrained by the planar-scene assumption—or image-translation approaches (StyleBooth, PixNext) that discard real X-sensor information, this framework anchors densification on actual matched X-keypoints and uses confidence-aware fusion to handle matching uncertainty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck that gates the entire RGB-X research ecosystem: the substantial engineering effort in sensor calibration, synchronization, and metric depth acquisition that most prior work silently assumes away. By showing that a scalable, calibration-free pipeline can produce view-aligned X-images competitive with or superior to 3DGS-based warping baselines, it lowers the barrier to collecting large-scale real-world RGB-X data for thermal, NIR, and SAR applications. Key limitations remain: the method targets static scenes only, struggles with extremely homogeneous regions lacking effective descriptors, and depends on X-sensor quality (noisy, low-resolution thermal images degrade results). Open questions include extending to dynamic objects, handling multi-sensor rigs beyond two modalities, and whether the confidence-aware densification strategy transfers to other sparse-to-dense completion settings.
