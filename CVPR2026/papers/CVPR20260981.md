## Problem

Existing HOI reenactment methods are trained and evaluated in-domain, failing to generalize to in-the-wild scenes, while all-in-one video editing models (e.g., VACE) generalize broadly but still degrade object identity across frames. The paper targets this gap: generating physically plausible hand-object interactions with consistent object appearance in unseen, real-world environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260981/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (F×H×W×3), a user-supplied object reference image (H×W×3), and a binary mask video delineating HOI regions. Training is self-supervised: the reference video, mask, and reference image are all derived from the source video itself, while at inference the reference image is novel and the first frame is synthesized via an external image editor. The output is a full-length reenacted video (evaluated at 81 and 401 frames, ≥720p) where the target object is naturally integrated into the hand interaction.

## Method

GenHOI augments the pretrained Wan-14B-I2V DiT with a lightweight HOI Condition Unit (HCU) that reformulates generation as first-frame-conditioned video inpainting by channel-concatenating the noisy latent, reference-video latent, and pooled mask. The key novelty is a two-part attention mechanism for injecting reference-object tokens: (1) Head-Sliding RoPE assigns each attention head a different temporal offset for reference tokens, averaging their influence across all frames to counteract the temporal decay inherent in 3D RoPE; and (2) a Spatial Attention Gate combines a Hard Mask Gate (binary mask blocking reference→background attention) with a Soft Flow Gate (per-token sigmoid scaling via LayerNorm+FCN) to concentrate object conditioning on HOI regions while preserving background. The entire module adds only 157M parameters (~0.95% of the 16.5B base model) and is trained via self-supervised reconstruction on ~19K videos.

## Results

On the AnchorCrafter_HOI dataset, GenHOI achieves 31.71 PSNR, 0.952 SSIM, and 0.937 Object CLIP (OC) in short-video self-reenactment (81 frames), substantially outperforming the strongest competitor VACE (28.60 PSNR, 0.880 OC). In long-video generation (401 frames), the advantage widens: GenHOI reaches 30.69 PSNR and 42.17 FVD versus VACE's 26.32 PSNR and 195.9 FVD. In the user study, GenHOI scores 4.64/5 on Reference Fidelity compared to VACE's 2.80/5, confirming superior object-identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenHOI outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both short- and long-video settings. Against the strongest competitor VACE (all-in-one video editing), GenHOI gains +3.11 dB PSNR and reduces FVD from 211.2 to 67.95 in short-video self-reenactment; the gap widens to +4.37 dB PSNR and 195.9→42.17 FVD for long videos, and Reference Fidelity jumps from 2.80 to 4.64 in the user study. HOI-Swap, the dedicated HOI reenactment method, fails to generalize to in-the-wild scenes (24.29 PSNR, 1.20 RF), confirming its in-domain limitation. UniAnimate-DiT and MimicMotion, general human-animation models, lag further behind (22.20 and 20.13 PSNR respectively) and score poorly on object consistency (OC 0.846 and 0.777), indicating they lack mechanisms for reference-object identity preservation. The consistent margin across all metrics—especially the large FVD and OC gaps—attributes to GenHOI's temporally balanced Head-Sliding RoPE and spatially selective attention gate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two existing lines of work: domain-specific HOI reenactment methods (HOI-Swap, Re-HOLD) that achieve in-domain quality but fail to generalize to in-the-wild scenes, and all-in-one video editing models (VACE) that generalize broadly but degrade on HOI-specific challenges like object identity preservation. It builds directly on the Wan-14B-I2V DiT architecture and its 3D RoPE positional encoding, extending RoPE with a head-specific temporal offset (Head-Sliding RoPE) to counteract the natural temporal decay of reference-token attention. The Spatial Attention Gate (hard mask + soft flow) draws on gated-attention ideas from LLMs and applies them to selectively route reference-object information only into HOI regions, departing from prior work that either pastes the reference into the inpainting box or assigns it a fixed offset coordinate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in digital-human content creation (e-commerce, education) where object identity must survive across dozens or hundreds of frames while the hand pose and object orientation change. The Head-Sliding RoPE insight—that fixed-offset reference tokens suffer progressive attention decay under 3D RoPE—is a clean, generalizable diagnosis that could inform other reference-conditioned video generation tasks. Limitations include evaluation on a single 100-video subset, dependence on a specific pretrained backbone (Wan-14B), and reliance on a binary mask video that may be hard to obtain in fully open scenarios. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object interactions, 3D-consistent HOI, or longer sequences beyond 401 frames.
