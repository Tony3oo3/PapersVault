## Problem

Existing spiking self-attention mechanisms in Spiking Transformers capture spatial relationships within individual timesteps but critically lack explicit temporal dependency modeling, relying solely on implicit spiking neuron dynamics. The authors introduce the Temporal Interaction Coefficient (TIC) to quantify this deficiency, revealing that baseline mechanisms exhibit sharply peaked reliance at the current timestep with negligible historical contributions, establishing temporal modeling as a fundamental bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262535/cropped_page3_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images (ImageNet at 224×224 or 288×288) and DVS event streams (CIFAR10-DVS at 128×128, N-Caltech101 at 180×240, both resized to 64×64; UCF101-DVS and HMDB51-DVS for action recognition) as well as sequential column-wise image inputs (s-CIFAR10/100 with 32 timesteps per sample). It produces class labels via standard classification. MD-Mixer operates as a drop-in replacement for the K/V linear projections inside existing Spiking Transformer blocks, so the overall input/output interface of the host architecture remains unchanged.

## Method

MD-Mixer introduces K learnable per-channel delay branches that aggregate historical features via weighted summation (Eq. 7), applied to the Key and Value projections while keeping Queries temporally aligned, thereby converting spatial-only attention into space-time mixing. To learn discrete delays through continuous gradient descent, the authors define a triangular delay distribution whose sharpness is controlled by a temperature parameter τ that undergoes squared-cosine annealing over training (soft-to-hard optimization), progressively concentrating the distribution from broad exploration to a near one-hot peak at the optimal delay. The module reduces computational complexity from O(TD²) to O(KTD) with K≪D, making it parameter-efficient relative to standard linear layers. A Temporal Interaction Coefficient (TIC) metric, based on the entropy of gradient-based reliance vectors, is introduced to quantitatively evaluate and visualize temporal interaction patterns before and after MD-Mixer integration.

## Results

On ImageNet, MD-Mixer integrated into Spike-driven Transformer V1-8-768 achieves 78.23% top-1 accuracy (224×224), a 1.91% gain over the baseline, and into SDT-V2-8-512 reaches 80.02% (+0.53%). On neuromorphic benchmarks, MD-Mixer + QKFormer attains 85.20% on CIFAR10-DVS and 82.06% on N-Caltech101, while MD-Mixer + SDT-V2 achieves 66.3% on UCF101-DVS and 62.8% on HMDB51-DVS, setting new state-of-the-art among Spiking Transformers. On sequential classification, MD-Mixer + QKFormer reaches 85.09% on s-CIFAR10 and 64.33% on s-CIFAR100 (new SOTA), and MD-Mixer + SDT-V1 reaches 86.65% on s-CIFAR10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MD-Mixer consistently outperforms STAtten [18] across all three architectures on both neuromorphic datasets: on CIFAR10-DVS it gains +0.97% (Spikformer), +0.72% (QKFormer), and +1.30% (SDT-V1); on N-Caltech101 it gains +1.45%, +0.84%, and +1.44% respectively. Against TIM [34] on action recognition, MD-Mixer + SDT-V2 surpasses it by 2.5% on UCF101-DVS (66.3% vs 63.8%) and 4.2% on HMDB51-DVS (62.8% vs 58.6%). On sequential classification, MD-Mixer + QKFormer dramatically outperforms STAtten + QKFormer by 5.55% on s-CIFAR10 (85.09% vs 79.54%) and 8.10% on s-CIFAR100 (64.33% vs 56.23%), indicating that structured temporal dynamics benefit most from explicit multi-delay modeling. On ImageNet, MD-Mixer + SDT-V2-8-512 (80.02%) surpasses both the baseline SDT-V2 (79.49%) and SpikingReformer (78.77%), while MD-Mixer + SDT-V1-8-768 (78.23%) slightly trails SpikingReformer (78.77%) but exceeds STAtten + SDT-V1 (78.11%). Overall, MD-Mixer establishes new SOTA on CIFAR10-DVS, N-Caltech101, UCF101-DVS, HMDB51-DVS, s-CIFAR10, and s-CIFAR100 among Spiking Transformer methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Spiking Transformer lineage (Spikformer, QKFormer, Spike-driven Transformer V1/V2) by identifying and addressing a specific architectural gap: the absence of explicit temporal modeling in spiking self-attention. Prior temporal-enhancement approaches (STSA, STAtten, TIM) either add separate spatiotemporal attention branches or inject historical information into the query path, whereas MD-Mixer takes a distinct route by replacing the K/V linear projections with learnable multi-scale delay branches inspired by biological axonal conduction delays. The introduction of the Temporal Interaction Coefficient (TIC), adapted from the effective receptive field analysis in [61], provides a new diagnostic lens that prior work lacked, enabling quantitative comparison of temporal dependency patterns across attention mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TIC metric fills a notable gap in the SNN evaluation toolkit, giving researchers a principled way to diagnose temporal modeling capacity rather than relying solely on task-specific accuracy. The drop-in, parameter-efficient design (O(KTD) vs. O(TD²)) makes MD-Mixer broadly applicable across existing Spiking Transformer architectures without architectural redesign, which is practically valuable for the neuromorphic computing community. Open questions include whether the fixed triangular delay distribution is the optimal parameterization, whether the mechanism generalizes to non-visual modalities (speech, point clouds) where temporal structure differs, and whether the soft-to-hard annealing schedule is sensitive to dataset-specific temporal scales.
