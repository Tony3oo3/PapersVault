## Problem

Voting-based text-to-image leaderboards rely on model anonymity for fair evaluation, yet it remains unclear whether T2I model identities can be inferred from their generated images. While deanonymization attacks on LLM leaderboards have been studied, the T2I modality was largely unexplored, and it was unknown whether model-specific visual signatures persist strongly enough in embedding space to enable practical attribution under realistic black-box, no-prompt-control conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single leaderboard image (produced by an unknown T2I model) and the corresponding text prompt as input. The adversary additionally generates k reference images (k=10–30) per candidate model using the same prompt via public APIs. Output is a ranked list of candidate models (top-1/2/3 identification) or, in the one-vs-rest variant, a binary decision on whether the image originates from a specific target model, with a tunable false-positive rate controlled by an α-quantile threshold on in-cluster embedding distances.

## Method

The core insight is that T2I models exhibit low intra-model variation and high inter-model variation in image embedding space, causing each model's generations for a fixed prompt to form tight, well-separated clusters. The deanonymization attack is training-free: the adversary embeds k reference images per model using a pre-trained ViT image encoder (CLIP-ViT-bigG-14), computes per-model centroids, and assigns the leaderboard image to the nearest centroid via L2 distance. A one-vs-rest variant uses only the target model's embeddings and classifies via an α-quantile distance threshold, requiring no access to other models. The paper also introduces a prompt-level distinguishability metric based on nearest-neighbor clustering fractions to quantify which prompts expose maximal model-specific signal. As a countermeasure, the authors adapt a contrastive adversarial perturbation (from Hu et al.) that shifts each image's embedding away from its source cluster toward the farthest other model's cluster, reducing top-1 attack accuracy from 91% to 46% at ε=8 while preserving visual fidelity.

## Results

On the Artificial Analysis T2I leaderboard (280 prompts, 22 models, 150K+ images), the proposed training-free centroid-based embedding method (IT-Emb) achieves 90.86% top-1, 96.14% top-2, and 97.50% top-3 deanonymization accuracy, far exceeding random chance (4.55%). In the one-vs-rest setting with access to all model APIs, top-1 accuracy reaches 99.16%. Even with only 10 reference images per model, the attack retains nearly the same accuracy, and a single image per model still yields ~62% top-1 accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed embedding-based method decisively outperforms both inference-time fingerprinting baselines. Marra et al. [28] (noise-residual fingerprinting) achieves only 24.40% top-1 accuracy, while Dzanic et al. [6] (Fourier-spectrum fingerprinting) reaches just 11.79% top-1 — both far below the proposed method's 90.86%. The gap is even more pronounced at top-3: the proposed method reaches 97.50% versus 36.50% (Marra) and 28.43% (Dzanic). These fingerprinting methods, originally designed for early GANs, fail to capture the model-specific stylistic signatures that modern T2I systems exhibit in embedding space. Even the strongest supervised baseline (image-only ResNet-50 classifier at 54.86% top-1) is outperformed by a 36-point margin, and the training-free centroid approach requires no labeled data or model training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of leaderboard manipulation research from LLMs (Huang et al., Min et al., Zhao et al.) to the T2I modality, showing that deanonymization is actually easier for images than for text. It departs from prior model-attribution and fingerprinting work (Marra et al., Dzanic et al., Kim et al., Song et al.) by operating entirely at inference time with no training, no access to model weights, and no prompt control—conditions under which those methods were not designed to work. The countermeasure adapts the transferable adversarial attack formulation of Hu et al. [13] to the defense setting, using ensemble-based optimization for cross-encoder robustness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a training-free centroid classifier achieves 91% deanonymization accuracy at a cost of roughly $5 per image exposes a fundamental design flaw in all voting-based T2I leaderboards, calling into question the fairness of current model rankings. The paper demonstrates that no simple defense is sufficient: adversarial perturbation reduces but does not eliminate the attack (75% top-1 at ε=2), and such perturbations risk degrading the very visual quality that human voters are meant to judge. Open questions include whether leaderboard operators can design evaluation protocols that preserve both anonymity and meaningful human comparison, and whether the distinguishability metric can be used proactively to flag vulnerable prompts without restricting the range of visual content evaluated.
