## Problem

Always-on chain-of-thought reasoning in MLLMs incurs substantial computational overhead on simple queries, yet existing auto-thinking approaches rely on manual user toggling, complex task-specific reward functions, or labor-intensive per-example complexity annotations. The paper addresses the need for a simple, annotation-free, and token-efficient framework that lets a single MLLM autonomously decide when to reason step-by-step versus when to answer directly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263432/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image paired with a free-form text query and produces a text response structured around a shared <think> template. In thinking mode the output is <think>\n<reasoning steps>\n<answer>, while in non-thinking mode it is <think>\n\n<answer>; in auto-thinking mode the model itself selects which format to emit. Evaluation uses greedy decoding (temperature 0) with a maximum generation length of 8,192 tokens, and the auto-thinking trigger rate varies per query, yielding a variable-length output that trades off token cost against accuracy.

## Method

R-4B is trained in two stages. First, bi-mode annealing uses a heuristic-driven curation pipeline (Qwen2.5-32B-VL as teacher) to automatically partition data into reasoning-intensive and direct-answer subsets via two complementary rules—difficulty-based prompting for subjective queries and offline hard-mining (N=8 sampled responses) for objective queries—then fine-tunes a single backbone on the mixed corpus in a unified instruction format so the model becomes proficient in both modes without ever seeing explicit complexity labels. Second, Bi-mode Policy Optimization (BPO) builds on GRPO but introduces a deterministic dual-rollout mechanism: for every query the model is forced to generate g thinking responses (conditioned on <think>\n) and g non-thinking responses (conditioned on <think>\n\n), and both groups are jointly optimized under a shared clipped-surrogate objective with a simple rule-based mathematical reward that balances answer correctness against reasoning cost. This balanced exploration prevents the mode collapse (always-think or never-think) observed with vanilla GRPO, and the mathematically grounded reward generalizes to diverse non-mathematical multimodal tasks, yielding an adaptive per-query reasoning policy.

## Results

Across 25 benchmarks, R-4B-RL (auto-thinking mode) achieves state-of-the-art results among 4B-scale models, scoring 68.1 on MMMU-val, 73.1 on MMStar, 58.9 on HallusionBench, 86.2 on AI2D, 49.6 on OlympiadBench, 59.1 on LogicVista, and 39.5 on DynaMath. On reasoning-intensive tasks, R-4B-RL matches or exceeds the much larger Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking-2506 (16B total params), e.g., 49.6 vs. 33.9 on OlympiadBench and 59.1 vs. 51.0 on LogicVista, while using substantially fewer output tokens. The auto-thinking mode adaptively allocates reasoning budget: on simple OCR-style tasks it generates tokens close to non-thinking mode, while on math-heavy benchmarks it approaches full-thinking token usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Qwen2.5-VL-7B (non-thinking), R-4B-RL wins on nearly all 25 benchmarks, with particularly large margins on reasoning tasks (e.g., +29.4 on OlympiadBench, +19.0 on LogicVista, +19.4 on DynaMath). Against the larger Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking-2506 (16B total, 3B active), R-4B-RL surpasses it on OlympiadBench (49.6 vs. 33.9), LogicVista (59.1 vs. 51.0), DynaMath (39.5 vs. 27.1), and CharXiv-RQ (56.8 vs. 47.7), but trails on MathVision (47.8 vs. 56.8) and MathVista (78.0 vs. 80.1). Against the closest auto-thinking competitor Keye-VL-8B, R-4B-RL wins on MMMU (68.1 vs. 66.8), MMStar (73.1 vs. 72.8), HallusionBench (58.9 vs. 57.3), OlympiadBench (49.6 vs. 45.2), LogicVista (59.1 vs. 50.6), and DynaMath (39.5 vs. 35.3), but loses on MMBench-EN (84.9 vs. 89.7) and WeMath (52.8 vs. 58.6). Against InternVL3-8B (non-thinking), R-4B-RL dominates on reasoning benchmarks (e.g., +23.7 on OlympiadBench, +15.5 on LogicVista) while remaining competitive on perception tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) for its RL objective but introduces a bi-mode rollout mechanism that forces the model to generate both thinking and non-thinking responses per query, preventing the mode collapse observed in vanilla GRPO. It departs from prior auto-thinking approaches in two key ways: unlike Keye-VL, which requires labor-intensive explicit complexity annotations, R-4B uses a heuristic-driven curation pipeline (difficulty-based for subjective queries, performance-based hard-mining for objective queries) to construct bi-mode training data without manual labels; unlike text-only RL controllers (AdaCoT, AdaptThink) that rely on complex task-specific reward functions, BPO uses a simple rule-based mathematical reward. The bi-mode annealing stage, which unifies thinking and non-thinking supervision under a shared template, is a novel training paradigm not present in prior single-mode MLLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4B-parameter model matching or exceeding a 16B model on reasoning-intensive benchmarks while reducing token usage on simple queries addresses a core deployment bottleneck for MLLMs. The finding that a math-oriented rule-based reward generalizes to diverse non-mathematical multimodal tasks suggests that mode-selection policies may be learnable from narrow supervision, opening questions about how broadly this transfer extends. Open questions include whether the binary thinking/non-thinking formulation is sufficient for tasks of intermediate complexity, whether BPO scales to larger backbones, and whether the heuristic curation strategy (relying on a 32B teacher) can be replaced by self-supervised alternatives.
