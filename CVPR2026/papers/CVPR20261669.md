## Problem

Existing floorplan generation methods are limited to small-scale scenes (fewer than 10 rooms) with simple rectangular shapes, and fail to handle the topological complexity and irregular non-Manhattan geometries inherent in large-scale building complexes. When applied to large-scale settings, state-of-the-art methods produce semantic inconsistencies, misplaced rooms, and blurred boundaries, making them impractical for realistic architectural simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261669/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene graph (room-type nodes with adjacency edges) and a binary structural image (building envelope/walls) as primary inputs, with an optional text description that an LLM converts into a scene graph at inference time. Outputs are a 1024×1024 multi-channel floorplan image (rooms + walls), a separate door-window layout map, and an optional 3D scene. The scene graph provides room categories, counts, and connectivity, while the structural image constrains the overall building footprint.

## Method

CG-Floor explicitly decouples topology from geometry in a coarse-to-fine pipeline. A Graph Transformer jointly predicts all room centroids and sizes in a single forward pass (avoiding sequential cumulative error), which are encoded into a Size-Aware Semantic Centroid Heatmap (SASCH)—a multi-channel Gaussian heatmap where each channel corresponds to a room type and the kernel width scales with predicted room size. A VQ-VAE is pre-trained to compress floorplans with complex non-Manhattan shapes into a discrete codebook of 1024 tokens, and a discrete absorbing diffusion transformer then generates the floorplan in this latent space, conditioned on the SASCH and structural features, with graph node features injected via cross-attention skip connections every M_s blocks. A separate U-Net predicts the door-window layout, also conditioned on graph features. Training is two-stage: VQ-VAE reconstruction (1000 epochs), then frozen-codebook diffusion training (1000 epochs) with a reweighted ELBO loss that prioritizes early denoising steps.

## Results

On the MSD large-scale floorplan dataset, CG-Floor achieves FID=16.03 and KID=6.80, representing 79.8% and 89.3% reductions over the second-best method (MHD: FID=79.72, KID=63.85). It also attains the highest Shape-Similarity (0.71) and Topological Consistency (91.3) scores, surpassing MHD (0.65 and 87.1 respectively). A user study with 88 participants confirms these quantitative gains, with CG-Floor receiving over 92% preference across all three evaluation criteria (GT-Sim, Consistency, Real-Sim).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-Floor outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. The closest competitor, MHD (Modified HouseDiffusion), achieves FID=79.72 and KID=63.85 but is constrained to rectangular room shapes, yielding a lower Shape-Similarity (0.65 vs. 0.71) and Consistency (87.1 vs. 91.3). UN (Graph-informed U-Net) produces the worst Shape-Similarity (0.41) due to blurred room boundaries and artifacts, and has the highest KID (180.67) among all methods. HouseGAN++ shows moderate Shape-Similarity (0.68) but degrades significantly in visual quality (FID=160.59) on large-scale scenes. Graph2Plan performs the worst overall (FID=279.86, KID=277.49), struggling with both room count accuracy and spatial connectivity at scale. Notably, only MHD and CG-Floor report Consistency scores, as graphs cannot be reliably extracted from the pixel outputs of the other three methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-Floor extends the diffusion-based floorplan generation line (HouseDiffusion, MHD) and graph-conditioned approaches (Graph2Plan, HouseGAN++) by explicitly decoupling topological structure from geometric shape, a separation prior methods entangle in a single generative pass. It draws on graph transformers for joint centroid/size prediction, VQ-VAE for discrete shape tokenization, and discrete absorbing diffusion (Bond-Taylor et al.) for latent-space generation, combining these into a coarse-to-fine pipeline. The key departure from MHD (which forces rectangular rooms to avoid combinatorial vertex explosion) and UN (which produces blurred boundaries without global positional anchors) is the SASCH representation, which provides a structured, size-aware spatial prior that neither vector nor raw-pixel baselines offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first data-driven method to generate floorplans with dozens of rooms and non-Manhattan shapes at scale, filling a gap where prior work was limited to fewer than 10 rooms. The topology-geometry decoupling paradigm and the SASCH representation offer a reusable architectural principle for other spatial planning tasks where global structure and local detail are hard to model jointly. Limitations include reliance on a single dataset (MSD), the added complexity of the LLM-to-scene-graph pipeline, and the absence of evaluation on real-world architectural constraints (load-bearing walls, building codes). Open questions include whether the decoupling generalizes to 3D multi-story buildings or to other domains with similar topology-geometry entanglement.
