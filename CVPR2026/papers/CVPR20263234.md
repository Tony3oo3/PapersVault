## Problem

Existing bimanual manipulation policies either rely on 2D observations with limited spatial awareness or require explicit point clouds that demand careful calibration and are sensitive to noise and occlusion in real-world settings. The paper asks whether a bimanual policy can directly leverage a pre-trained 3D geometric foundation model to achieve RGB-only, 3D-aware predictive control, jointly modeling future actions and future scene geometry without any explicit point cloud pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 6-frame RGB sequence (5 uniformly sampled past frames plus the current frame) and a 14-dimensional proprioceptive vector (6 joint angles + 1 gripper state per arm). The 6-frame sequence is patchified at 14×14 and fed into the π³ encoder, while the current frame is separately patchified at 16×16 for DINOv3. The output is a bimanual action chunk of N future 14D proprioceptive states plus a future 3D pointmap at horizon N (H×W×4: x, y, z, confidence), decoded from a compact latent of shape H/14×W/14×1024.

## Method

Three parallel encoders extract heterogeneous features: π³ processes the 6-frame sequence into a 1024-d geometric latent, DINOv3 encodes the current frame into a 1024-d semantic latent, and an MLP maps proprioception to a 1024-d state embedding. These are concatenated along the token dimension and fused by a 4-layer DETR encoder into a unified Semantic-Geometric Fused Context that conditions a conditional diffusion decoder (also DETR-based). The decoder jointly denoises two heterogeneous targets—a future action chunk and a future 3D latent—under an L1 loss that supervises both the compact latent and the decoded dense pointmap. Pseudo-ground-truth 3D latents are pre-extracted from all expert demonstrations using π³ with a temporal observation window to stabilize the supervision signal. The key architectural insight is that explicitly predicting how the 3D scene will evolve under the action sequence forces the policy to internalize robust 3D spatial reasoning, which in turn yields more physically plausible and temporally coherent bimanual control from RGB inputs alone.

## Results

On the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, the method achieves the highest average success rate across all three task categories: 63.2% on Dominant-select (16 tasks), 51.3% on Sync-bimanual (8 tasks), and 50.4% on Seq-coordinate (8 tasks), outperforming all baselines. In real-world deployment on the AgileX Cobot Magic platform, the method reaches a 40% average success rate over four tasks, compared to 32.5% for the next-best method (Xu et al.) and 23.8% for ACT. The method also demonstrates superior data efficiency, achieving 24.7% success on Place Empty Cup with only 20 demonstrations where DP scores 0.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines across every task category. Against the strongest 3D-native baseline DP3 (61.2% on Dominant-select), the method gains +2.0 points while operating on RGB-only inputs, with the largest gap on spatially demanding tasks like Open Laptop (88.7% vs 77.7%) and Open Microwave (86.0% vs 70.3%). Against G3Flow, the closest competitor on Seq-coordinate tasks (46.3% vs 50.4%), the advantage is most pronounced on Hang Mug (40.0% vs 26.7%), a task requiring precise 3D geometric reasoning. Against Xu et al. [52], which also jointly predicts future frames, the method leads by 4.8 points on Sync-bimanual (51.3% vs 47.6%) and 4.9 points on Seq-coordinate (50.4% vs 45.5%), suggesting that predicting 3D geometry is more informative than predicting 2D frames for bimanual coordination. The 2D baselines ACT and DP trail significantly (34.1% and 44.4% on Dominant-select), confirming the necessity of explicit 3D structure. RDT, a 1.2B-parameter foundation model, underperforms on Sync-bimanual tasks (28.8%), likely due to its single-arm-centric design. In real-world evaluation, the method achieves 40% average success versus 32.5% for Xu et al., with particularly large margins on Hanging Mug (20% vs 5%) and Place Dual Shoes (20% vs 15%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ACT/Diffusion Policy lineage for bimanual manipulation by replacing their from-scratch ResNet encoders with pre-trained foundation models: π³ for 3D geometric features and DINOv3 for 2D semantic features. It departs from explicit point-cloud methods (DP3, G3Flow) that require calibrated depth sensors by showing that a feed-forward 3D geometric foundation model can extract equivalent spatial structure directly from RGB. The joint prediction of a future 3D pointmap latent alongside the action chunk draws on the world-model idea from Xu et al. [52] (future frame prediction) but shifts the prediction target from 2D images to 3D geometry, creating a 'geometric imagination' signal that forces the policy to reason about scene evolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is eliminating the point-cloud pipeline (calibration, depth sensors, noise sensitivity) that has been a major deployment bottleneck for 3D-aware manipulation, while retaining the spatial reasoning benefits. However, the real-world success rates remain modest (40% average across four tasks), and the method is validated on a single robot platform with only 50 demonstrations per task, leaving open questions about scalability to more complex or longer-horizon tasks. The ablation showing that geometric imagination contributes only ~1.5% over 3D perception alone (25.1 vs 23.6) raises the question of whether the future 3D prediction target is truly a planning mechanism or primarily a useful training regularizer. Broader applicability to multi-embodiment or open-world settings remains untested.
