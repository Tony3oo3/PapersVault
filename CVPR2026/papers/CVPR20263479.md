## Problem

Gaussian-Enhanced Surfels (GES) achieve sort-free, popping-free rendering but suffer from two key limitations: (1) the hard depth test applied to Gaussians truncates their color contributions at surfel boundaries, causing residual aliasing, and (2) fully opaque surfels are non-differentiable, preventing effective joint optimization with Gaussians and leading to suboptimal reconstruction with protruding or misaligned surfels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263479/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses (COLMAP) as input, using pre-downscaled JPG images at native resolution for training. It produces a novel-view rendered image at 1080p, rendered in real time at over 470 fps on an RTX 4090. The learned representation is a compact set of 2D surfels (typically <10% of total primitives) plus 3D Gaussians, stored in ~156 MB, with training taking approximately 40 minutes per scene.

## Method

DP-GES augments opaque 2D surfels with a thin semi-transparent boundary ring (opacity smoothly decays via a Gaussian kernel with fixed modulation w=30), enabling differentiable rendering. During the first rendering pass, 3-layer depth peeling extracts per-pixel depth, color, and opacity maps from surfels, which are alpha-blended to produce surfel color and per-layer transmittance maps. In the second pass, 3D Gaussians (with spherical Beta view-dependent colors) are splatted sort-free, with each Gaussian's weight multiplied by the transmittance value corresponding to its depth interval relative to the peeled layers, and culled if beyond the 3rd layer or at zero transmittance. The transmittance values create a differentiable gradient path from image loss through Gaussians back to surfel geometry parameters, enabling fully coupled joint optimization. A composite loss (RGB + surfel L1 + exponential scale penalty + transmittance regularization) drives training, where the transmittance loss counterintuitively encourages overlapping semi-transparent regions to separate, pushing T₃ toward zero to prevent background leakage.

## Results

DP-GES achieves the best or near-best PSNR across all four benchmark datasets: 28.11 on Mip-NeRF360, 30.30 on DeepBlending, 24.61 on Tanks & Temples, and 34.13 on NeRF Synthetic. It attains the lowest LPIPS on Mip-NeRF360 (0.196), DeepBlending (0.239), and Tanks & Temples (0.162), and ties for best on NeRF Synthetic (0.030). In terms of efficiency, DP-GES renders at 472 FPS at 1080p—2.5× faster than vanilla 3DGS (185 FPS) and 3× faster than DBS (156 FPS) and SSS (62 FPS)—while using only 156 MB of storage, less than half of 3DGS (734 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DP-GES outperforms all 10 baselines on Mip-NeRF360 PSNR (28.11 vs. best competitor DBS at 28.10) and LPIPS (0.196 vs. SSS at 0.203), and on DeepBlending PSNR (30.30 vs. DBS/SSS at 30.25) and LPIPS (0.239 vs. SSS at 0.242). On Tanks & Temples, SSS edges out DP-GES by 0.09 dB in PSNR (24.70 vs. 24.61), but DP-GES achieves the best LPIPS (0.162 vs. 0.166). On NeRF Synthetic, DBS leads by 0.21 dB in PSNR (34.34 vs. 34.13), though DP-GES ties for best LPIPS (0.030). Against the direct predecessor GES, DP-GES gains +0.73 dB PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 and +0.30 dB on DeepBlending while also improving LPIPS, attributable to the semi-transparent surfel boundaries and fully differentiable joint optimization. In rendering speed, DP-GES (472 FPS) is 2.5× faster than 3DGS (185 FPS), 3× faster than DBS (156 FPS) and SSS (62 FPS), though slower than GES (675 FPS) due to the additional Gaussian rendering pass that fetches peeled depth and transmittance textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DP-GES is a direct extension of Gaussian-Enhanced Surfels (GES) [42], which itself builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting [19] by introducing opaque 2D surfels for coarse geometry and sort-free Gaussian splatting for fine details. The key departure is replacing GES's fully opaque surfels with semi-transparent boundaries and employing Depth Peeling [34] to obtain accurate per-pixel surfel ordering, enabling transmittance-modulated Gaussian blending without sorting. The spherical Beta color function from Deformable Beta Splatting [25] replaces spherical harmonics for improved view-dependent effects. Conceptually, the paper bridges classic order-independent transparency (depth peeling) with modern Gaussian splatting, a combination not previously explored in this context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DP-GES demonstrates that a small number of depth-peeled surfel layers (only 3) can provide sufficient depth ordering to eliminate both popping and aliasing artifacts while maintaining real-time performance (470+ fps), offering a practical path to sort-free, view-consistent rendering that matches or exceeds quality of sorting-based methods. The fully differentiable joint optimization of surfels and Gaussians via transmittance-mediated gradients is a clean architectural insight that addresses a fundamental limitation of prior surfel-based approaches. However, the method still struggles with transparent/semi-transparent objects, surfels remain less flexible than fully translucent 2D Gaussians, and the spherical Beta function introduces overfitting risk. Open questions include whether the surfel representation can support mesh extraction, physically based rendering, and shadow casting as the authors envision.
