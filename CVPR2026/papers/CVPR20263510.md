## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit significantly degraded performance in multi-image reasoning compared to single-image tasks, due to two fundamental challenges: complex inter-image relationships (semantic, spatial, temporal) that require holistic integration, and critical information scattered across specific images within larger sets. While humans naturally employ structured cognitive processes—global overview, focused attention, error-prone hinting, internal reasoning, and final answer—existing MLLM approaches lack explicit modeling of these sequential cognitive steps for multi-image scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) takes a variable number of input images (2–32 for multi-image tasks, sequential frames for video tasks, or a single image) along with a text question, and produces a structured reasoning trajectory composed of five tagged meta-actions (<global>, <focus>, <hint>, <think>, <answer>) followed by a final answer. The Global action is explicitly disallowed for single-image inputs, and format validity of all meta-actions is enforced as a reward signal during training. Inference uses temperature 0.6, top-p 0.7, and a 1024-token generation limit.

## Method

CINEMA decomposes multi-image reasoning into five structured meta-actions (Global, Focus, Hint, Think, Answer) that explicitly model human cognitive steps. For cold-start training, a Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling strategy generates two diverse correct trajectories per instance: a student model produces an initial trajectory, GPT-4o refines it while preserving the action sequence, and a second distinct trajectory is retrieved from a pre-built tree database using BGE embedding cosine similarity. Training then proceeds in two RL stages: first, a Diversity-Preserving Strategy (DPS) built on DAPO applies a trajectory homogeneity penalty (scaling with the fraction of group members sharing identical meta-action patterns) to prevent entropy collapse and maintain exploration; second, DAPO with an annealing schedule gradually shifts the policy toward exploitation to consolidate performance. The full pipeline is trained on 56k cold-start and 58k RL instances spanning multi-image, multi-frame, and single-image tasks.

## Results

On multi-image benchmarks, CINEMA (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 71.6% on MUIR, 36.9% on MV-MATH, and 55.2% on MIRB, surpassing GPT-4o on MUIR (71.6 vs. 68.0) and MV-MATH (36.9 vs. 32.1). On video benchmarks, it reaches 66.5% on MVBench and 59.4% on VideoMME, outperforming all specialized video reasoning baselines (VideoR1, VideoRFT, TW-GRPO) on MVBench. On single-image benchmarks, the model attains 43.8% on MM-Math and 68.7% on MathVista, achieving state-of-the-art among open-source 7B models and surpassing GPT-4V on M3COT (63.5 vs. 62.6). Overall, the method improves the backbone by +6.1 points on the multi-image overall score and +4.7 on the single-image overall score.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CINEMA surpasses GPT-4o on MUIR (71.6 vs. 68.0) and MV-MATH (36.9 vs. 32.1), but trails GPT-4o on VideoMME (59.4 vs. 75.0) and VideoMMMU (49.0 vs. 61.2), reflecting the capacity gap between a 7B open-source model and a frontier closed-source system. Among open-source multi-image/video-enhanced models, CINEMA leads on MUIR by a wide margin (71.6 vs. 56.6 for VideoRFT, 44.5 for VISC) and on MV-MATH (36.9 vs. 28.2 for TW-GRPO), though VISC achieves higher MIRB (60.2 vs. 55.2) and Mantis-Eval (69.1 vs. 67.7). On video benchmarks, CINEMA outperforms VideoR1 (63.6/57.4/49.8) and TW-GRPO (63.3/55.1/40.8) on MVBench, but VideoRFT edges ahead on VideoMME (59.8 vs. 59.4) and VideoMMMU (51.1 vs. 49.0). On single-image reasoning, CINEMA (45.5 overall) outperforms all 7B reasoning baselines including VisonR1 (43.4), MixedR1 (43.0), and VLAA-Thinker (44.0), and surpasses GPT-4V on M3COT (63.5 vs. 62.6) and MM-Math (43.8 vs. 23.1). The backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B is improved by +13.7 on MUIR, +10.2 on MV-MATH, and +8.9 on EMMA, confirming that the meta-action framework and two-stage RL contribute most to multi-image and reasoning-heavy tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DAPO [69] as its RL foundation, extending it with a diversity-preserving first stage (DPS) and an annealed exploitation second stage to address entropy collapse in reasoning RL. Conceptually, it departs from prior multi-image reasoning methods (VISC, MIA-DPO, CMMCOT) that handle inter-image relationships implicitly or through sub-question decomposition, instead introducing an explicit five-step cognitive scaffold (Global, Focus, Hint, Think, Answer) grounded in human visual perception literature. The Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling strategy for cold-start data generation draws on student-teacher paradigms and retrieval-augmented generation, but applies them specifically to meta-action trajectory trees rather than free-form CoT, distinguishing it from standard SFT data pipelines used in prior MLLM reasoning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that structured, cognition-inspired reasoning can close the gap between open-source 7B models and closed-source systems like GPT-4o on multi-image tasks, and that the same meta-action scaffold generalizes to video understanding without video-specific training—a notable transfer result. However, the approach is validated on a single backbone (Qwen2.5-VL 7B), and it remains open whether the five meta-actions scale to larger models or whether the retrieval-based cold-start pipeline generalizes beyond the curated 56k/58k dataset. A key open question is whether the explicit cognitive structure adds value beyond what a sufficiently large model would learn implicitly, and whether the diversity-preserving RL stage is necessary at scale or primarily compensates for the limited capacity of 7B models.
