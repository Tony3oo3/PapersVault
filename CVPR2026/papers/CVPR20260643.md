## Problem

Online scene change detection (SCD) requires detecting relevant scene changes on the fly from unconstrained viewpoints, but existing online methods are substantially less accurate than offline approaches and often fail to sustain real-time performance. No prior method simultaneously achieves pose-agnostic, label-free, multi-view consistent, and real-time online SCD, leaving a significant accuracy gap between online and offline settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260643/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a stream of unposed RGB inference frames (1008×560) from the post-change scene and a pre-built 3DGS reference representation (constructed offline via SfM + Speedy-Splat). For each incoming frame it produces a binary change segmentation mask localizing all relevant changes while suppressing distractors (shadows, reflections, illumination shifts). After processing all frames, it outputs an updated 3DGS scene representation reflecting the current environment state. The entire pipeline operates at 11.2 FPS on an RTX 4090 without requiring depth, known poses, or human-annotated change labels.

## Method

For each incoming frame, the method estimates its pose via a lightweight PnP+RANSAC pipeline using XFeat descriptors matched against a fixed set of reference frames (O(1) complexity, no drift accumulation), then renders the aligned pre-change view from the 3DGS reference. Change cues are extracted as a combination of pixel-level differences (L1 + D-SSIM photometric error) and feature-level differences (SAM2-Tiny dense feature maps), capturing complementary low-level appearance and high-level semantic changes. A novel self-supervised fusion loss (L_SSF) optimizes a persistent 3DGS-based change representation R_change by encouraging rendered change masks to match strong cues across all observed viewpoints while regularizing against the trivial all-ones solution, replacing MV3DCD's hard-thresholded intersection heuristic. After all frames are processed, a change-guided selective reconstruction updates only changed regions (guided by refined masks) and fuses them with unchanged reference primitives, followed by a restricted global optimization that handles illumination shifts and boundary artifacts, completing the scene update in under 60 seconds.

## Results

On PASLCD, the proposed online method achieves mIoU 0.486 and F1 0.638 at 11.2 FPS, surpassing the strongest offline baseline MV3DCD (mIoU 0.478, F1 0.628) while operating in real time. In the offline setting, the method reaches mIoU 0.552 and F1 0.694 in 156 s, improving mIoU by ~15% over MV3DCD and running ~3× faster. For scene representation update on PASLCD, the change-guided strategy achieves PSNR 23.70 dB in 42 s, outperforming full 3DGS reconstruction (22.21 dB, 550 s) by 1.5 dB in 8× less time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 14 baselines across both SCD and scene-update tasks. Among offline SCD methods, it beats the closest competitor MV3DCD [13] by 7.4 mIoU points (0.552 vs 0.478) and 6.6 F1 points (0.694 vs 0.628) while running 3× faster (156 s vs 479 s); GeSCD [21] and ZeroSCD [8] trail by 7.5 and 24.6 mIoU points respectively. In the online setting, the gap widens dramatically: the method achieves 0.486 mIoU versus 0.243 for the best online baseline CS+CYWS2D [40] (a 2× improvement), and 0.638 F1 versus 0.358 for SplatPose+ [31], the strongest pose-agnostic online method. All pose-agnostic online baselines (SplatPose [23], SplatPose+ [31], OmniposeAD [55]) operate below 1 FPS, whereas the proposed method sustains 11.2 FPS. For scene representation update, the change-guided strategy achieves 23.70 dB PSNR in 42 s on PASLCD, exceeding 3DGS [19] (22.21 dB, 550 s), 3DGS-LM [16] (22.26 dB, 340 s), SpeedySplats [15] (22.25 dB, 399 s), and CLNeRF [5] (22.27 dB, 451 s) in both quality and speed (8–13× faster). No baseline is competitive on both accuracy and runtime simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MV3DCD framework (Galappaththige et al., CVPR 2025) by replacing its hard-thresholded intersection fusion with a self-supervised fusion loss that jointly integrates pixel- and feature-level change cues across viewpoints, and by moving the entire pipeline from an offline to an online, incremental setting. The 3DGS representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) serves as the backbone for both the reference scene and the change representation, while the pose estimation module draws on lightweight feature matching (XFeat) and PnP+RANSAC rather than the SfM-based registration used by prior pose-agnostic methods. The change-guided selective reconstruction strategy is positioned against continual learning approaches for scene representations (CL-Splats, CLNeRF, GaussianUpdate), departing from their full-scene or distillation-based updates in favor of mask-guided partial reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to simultaneously achieve online, pose-agnostic, label-free, and multi-view-consistent scene change detection at real-time rates, closing a significant gap for embodied and robotic systems that must detect changes during a single revisit without future observations. The practical impact is substantial: scene representation updates complete in under 60 seconds versus minutes for full reconstruction, enabling frequent repeated inspections in long-term monitoring. Limitations include reliance on a well-captured reference scene, the assumption of scene staticity within each revisit, and evaluation on a single room-scale dataset (PASLCD); open questions include whether the self-supervised fusion loss generalizes to larger outdoor scenes, dynamic objects within a revisit, and whether richer complementary cues (e.g., depth, thermal) could further close the remaining gap to oracle-level detection.
