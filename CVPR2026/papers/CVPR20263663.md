## Problem

Existing multimodal registration methods suffer from two coupled limitations: disentanglement-based shared-feature learning regularizes only the shared component, allowing modality-private cues to leak back and corrupt geometric correspondence, and multi-scale frameworks are restricted to a single transformation type (rigid or non-rigid), so they cannot simultaneously correct global misalignment and local deformation. Serial hybrid cascades (rigid stage followed by non-rigid stage) compound the problem by estimating the two transformations in separate representation spaces, causing inter-stage bias accumulation and preventing a unified deformation field.

## Input / Output

The network receives a fixed–moving image pair from any of four modality combinations (RGB-TIR, RGB-NIR, RGB-IR, RGB-SAR), all resized to 256×256 pixels, and produces a single coherent hybrid deformation field in one forward pass. The field is constructed by encoding a global rigid parameter (rotation, translation, scale) estimated at the coarsest scale into a coarse flow, then progressively adding non-rigid displacement increments at four finer scales, so the output is a dense per-pixel displacement map rather than a parametric transform. Warping the moving image with this field yields the registered output image.

## Method

HRNet couples three components in a disentangle-then-align pipeline. A shared CNN backbone with Modality-Specific Batch Normalization (MSBN) extracts five-scale features, keeping convolutional weights shared while maintaining per-modality BN statistics to absorb radiometric differences. The Cross-scale Disentanglement and Adaptive Projection (CDAP) module then decomposes each scale into shared and private branches, applies Inter-Layer Disentanglement Attention (ILDA) to compute cross-scale gating coefficients that suppress private-to-shared leakage, and projects the gated shared features into a low-dimensional subspace via input-adaptive orthonormal bases (Dynamic Shared Subspace). The Hybrid Parameter Prediction Module (HPPM) uses Mamba-based Residual State Space Blocks (RSSB) in a non-iterative coarse-to-fine loop: the coarsest scale predicts a rigid parameter vector, which is encoded as a flow and accumulated with non-rigid displacement increments at each subsequent scale, yielding a single unified deformation field. Training combines supervised registration losses (parameter L1, reprojection L1, smoothness) with four structured disentanglement losses—cross-covariance decorrelation, basis orthogonality, cross-scale directional consistency, and a triplet margin loss—scheduled via a three-phase curriculum that progressively increases the weight of non-rigid and disentanglement terms.

## Results

On the rigid registration task, HRNet achieves the lowest reprojection error (RE) across all four modality pairs: 0.785 (RGB-NIR), 0.744 (RGB-TIR), 0.578 (RGB-IR), and 3.161 (RGB-SAR), with corresponding NCC values of 0.788, 0.796, 0.917, and 0.866. On the non-rigid task, HRNet similarly leads with RE of 1.633, 2.264, 1.516, and 7.172 and NCC of 0.755, 0.701, 0.893, and 0.720. Compared to the strongest rigid baseline MMRNet, HRNet reduces RE by 75.3%, 69.9%, 86.9%, and 55.3% on the four datasets respectively; against the strongest non-rigid hybrid baseline ADRNet, the reductions are 61.2%, 62.5%, 66.9%, and 23.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HRNet outperforms all nine baselines on both rigid and non-rigid registration across all four modality pairs. On rigid registration, the closest competitor MMRNet is surpassed by a large margin: HRNet reduces RE from 3.179 to 0.785 on RGB-NIR (−75.3%) and from 4.406 to 0.578 on RGB-IR (−86.9%), while also raising NCC from 0.799 to 0.917 on RGB-IR. On non-rigid registration, HRNet beats the previous hybrid method ADRNet by 61.2%–66.9% RE reduction on RGB-NIR, RGB-TIR, and RGB-IR, and by 23.3% on the more challenging RGB-SAR (9.351→7.172). The non-hybrid baselines (SuperFusion, InMIRNet, NBRNet) perform substantially worse on non-rigid tasks, with RE values often exceeding 10–36, confirming that single-paradigm models struggle when global misalignment and local deformation coexist. Even on the hardest RGB-SAR pair, HRNet maintains a clear lead over MMRNet (RE 3.161 vs. 7.075 rigid; 7.172 vs. 8.636 non-rigid), demonstrating robustness to the large appearance gap between optical and synthetic aperture radar imagery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines in multimodal registration: multi-scale coarse-to-fine estimation (Ye, Zhu, Xu), cross-modal feature disentanglement (Shi, Deng), and hybrid rigid-plus-non-rigid registration (Xiao, Gao, Tang). It departs from prior hybrid methods, which typically cascade a rigid stage followed by a non-rigid stage in separate representation spaces, by performing both within a single shared feature space in one non-iterative pass. The disentanglement component extends prior shared-feature approaches by adding explicit cross-scale gating (ILDA) to suppress private-to-shared leakage, going beyond the common practice of regularizing only the shared component. The Mamba-based RSSB blocks (from MambaIR) are repurposed as the backbone for the hybrid parameter prediction, replacing the CNN-only or transformer-only encoders used in most registration networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified shared-space formulation for hybrid registration addresses a practical gap: real-world multimodal pairs often exhibit both large global misalignment and local structural deformation simultaneously, and serial cascades accumulate errors across stages. The explicit private-to-shared leakage suppression via cross-scale gating and the three structured regularizations (cross-covariance decorrelation, basis orthogonality, cross-scale directional consistency) offer a more principled recipe for building modality-invariant registration features than prior disentanglement attempts. Open questions include whether the Mamba-based RSSB blocks are truly necessary versus a standard CNN or transformer encoder (no dedicated ablation isolates this), whether the approach extends to 3D volumetric or multi-view registration, and how performance holds under even larger modality gaps (e.g., CT-MRI) where the current four 2D benchmarks may not be representative.
