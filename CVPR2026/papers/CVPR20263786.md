## Problem

Current vision-language models that integrate geometry encoders (e.g., DUST3R, VGGT) fuse only the final-layer geometric features with vision tokens, discarding rich hierarchical cues from intermediate layers. The paper shows that naive multi-layer fusion in the vision pathway causes feature interference rather than synergy, creating a fundamental bottleneck that limits both fine-grained spatial perception and high-level relational reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes K multi-view RGB images (arbitrary resolution, divided into p×p patches) and a text question as input. A shared vision encoder and the VGGT geometry encoder process the images in parallel; a spatial merger groups 2×2 neighboring patches to reduce token count. The output is a free-form text answer to the spatial reasoning question (e.g., relative distance, object count, route planning), generated autoregressively by the LLM decoder.

## Method

SpatialStack extracts patch-token features from three VGGT layers (11, 17, 23) and injects them as additive residuals into the first three LLM decoder layers (0, 1, 2), each through a layer-specific geometry token merger that aligns spatial resolution and embedding dimension. The key insight is a hierarchical correspondence: shallow geometry layers preserve fine-grained local structure beneficial for low-level perception, while deeper layers encode global semantic context for high-level reasoning, and mapping them to progressively deeper LLM layers avoids the feature interference observed when all levels are fused into the vision pathway. Training uses standard next-token cross-entropy loss with both the vision encoder and geometry encoder frozen, updating only the geometry token mergers and the LLM decoder. The framework is model-agnostic and is instantiated as VLM-SpatialStack on Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3.5 bases.

## Results

On VSI-Bench, SpatialStack-5B (Qwen3.5) achieves a 67.5 average score, ranking #1 among all evaluated models and surpassing the strongest proprietary model Gemini-2.5Pro (51.5) by 16 points. On CV-Bench, SpatialStack-5B reaches 85.5% average (92.2% on 3D tasks), setting a new state-of-the-art over its Qwen3.5-4B baseline (85.0%). The 4B variant (Qwen2.5) also outperforms all same-scale geometry-aware baselines, achieving 60.9 on VSI-Bench and 81.2 on CV-Bench. On general-purpose benchmarks (MMBench, Video-MME, TempCompass), SpatialStack-5B maintains performance within 0.4 points of the Qwen3.5-4B baseline, confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialStack-5B (67.5 avg) decisively outperforms all proprietary and open-source baselines on VSI-Bench, beating Gemini-2.5Pro (51.5) by 16 points and GPT-4o (34.0) by 33.5 points. Among same-scale geometry-aware models on the shared Qwen2.5 base, SpatialStack-4B (60.9) surpasses VG-LLM-4B (47.3), Spatial-MLLM-4B (47.0), and Cambrian-S-3B (57.3) by 13.6, 13.9, and 3.6 points respectively. On CV-Bench, SpatialStack-5B (85.5) edges out its Qwen3.5-4B baseline (85.0) and substantially outperforms VG-LLM-4B (79.5) and Cambrian-S-3B (76.2). The one notable loss is on BLINK-Spatial, where the Qwen3.5-4B baseline (61.12) outperforms SpatialStack-5B (55.46), suggesting the geometry injection slightly degrades fine-grained 2D visual perception. On general capabilities (MMBench, Video-MME, TempCompass), SpatialStack-5B remains within 0.4 points of the Qwen3.5-4B baseline overall, confirming that spatial specialization does not cause catastrophic forgetting of general multimodal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of geometry-aware VLMs (Spatial-MLLM, VG-LLM, VLM-3R) that integrate multi-view geometry encoders like VGGT into vision-language models, but departs from them by shifting the fusion point from the vision encoder pathway to the LLM decoder. Inspired by DeepStack's token-stacking paradigm, SpatialStack injects multi-level geometry features (from VGGT layers 11, 17, 23) as additive residuals into successive early LLM decoder layers rather than concatenating them with visual tokens. The work also builds on the DPT architecture's philosophy of multi-level feature extraction, arguing that prior methods discard intermediate geometric representations by sampling only the final encoder layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that naive multi-layer geometry-vision fusion in the visual pathway causes feature interference rather than synergy (Table 1) provides a useful negative result that redirects architectural design toward the language side. SpatialStack's model-agnostic framework could be adopted by any VLM seeking 3D spatial capabilities, and the progressive hierarchical mapping (shallow geometry → early LLM layers, deep geometry → later LLM layers) offers a principled design principle. Open questions include whether the specific layer-to-layer mapping generalizes across different LLM backbones and geometry encoders, whether the approach extends to dynamic 4D reasoning, and why BLINK-Spatial performance degrades despite gains elsewhere—suggesting fine-grained 2D perception may still be a bottleneck.
