## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-video models generate high-fidelity single-shot clips but cannot produce the coherent, multi-shot narratives that define cinematic storytelling. Existing multi-shot approaches either decouple shot generation (causing consistency drift and error accumulation) or attempt holistic generation but face prohibitive quadratic attention costs and lack precise per-shot directorial control, making minute-scale narrative synthesis impractical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262063/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a two-tier hierarchical text prompt: a global scene description (characters, environment, plot) followed by per-shot prompts (actions, camera movements, specific characters) separated by explicit [shot cut] boundary tags. It outputs a coherent multi-shot video sequence of up to 60 seconds containing up to 13 distinct shots at 480×832 resolution, generated in a single holistic diffusion pass rather than sequential chunk-by-chunk synthesis.

## Method

HoloCine builds on the Wan2.2 14B DiT-based video diffusion model and introduces two architectural mechanisms for holistic multi-shot generation. Window Cross-Attention constrains each shot's video tokens to attend only to the global prompt and its own per-shot prompt (not the full concatenated text), creating a localized alignment that enables sharp, temporally-precise shot transitions. Sparse Inter-Shot Self-Attention uses dense bidirectional attention within each shot for motion continuity, but between shots communicates only through compact summary tokens (first-frame tokens) concatenated into a shared global KV bank, reducing complexity from O((N_s·L_shot)²) to approximately O(N_s·(L_shot² + L_shot·N_s·S)). The sparse pattern is implemented via FlashAttention-3's varlen kernel for efficient block-sparse computation, and the model is trained on 400k hierarchically captioned multi-shot samples curated from films and TV series using Gemini 2.5 Flash annotations.

## Results

On the HoloCine 100-prompt multi-shot benchmark, HoloCine achieves a Shot Cut Accuracy (Transition Control) of 0.9837, dramatically outperforming CineTrans (0.5370) and the raw Wan2.2 base model (0.4843). It also leads on Inter-shot Consistency (0.7509), Intra-shot Subject Consistency (0.9448), Intra-shot Background Consistency (0.9352), and both Semantic Consistency sub-metrics (Global 0.1856, Shot 0.1837). The only metric where HoloCine trails is Aesthetic Quality (0.5598 vs. 0.5773 for StoryDiffusion+Wan2.2), a narrow gap the authors attribute to the two-stage pipeline's keyframe-based aesthetic anchoring.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloCine is compared against four baselines spanning three paradigms. Against the raw Wan2.2 14B base model (used as a single-pass pre-trained diffusion baseline), HoloCine more than doubles Transition Control (0.9837 vs. 0.4843) and improves Inter-shot Consistency by 7.2 points (0.7509 vs. 0.6772), confirming that architectural modifications are essential for multi-shot execution. Against the two-stage keyframe-to-video pipelines (StoryDiffusion+Wan2.2 and IC-LoRA+Wan2.2), HoloCine wins on every consistency and control metric; the closest competitor is StoryDiffusion+Wan2.2 on Inter-shot Consistency (0.7364 vs. 0.7509) and Aesthetic Quality (0.5773 vs. 0.5598), while IC-LoRA+Wan2.2 is competitive on Intra-shot Subject (0.9421 vs. 0.9448) but lags on Transition Control (not applicable) and Semantic Shot (0.1692 vs. 0.1837). Against the other holistic method, CineTrans, HoloCine dominates across all seven metrics, with the largest gap on Transition Control (0.9837 vs. 0.5370) and Inter-shot Consistency (0.7509 vs. 0.6152), indicating that the Window Cross-Attention and Sparse Inter-Shot Self-Attention mechanisms provide substantially better shot-level control and long-range coherence than CineTrans's approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

HoloCine builds directly on the Wan2.2 DiT-based video diffusion model and aligns with the emerging holistic generation paradigm (LCT, CineTrans) that jointly models all shots in a single diffusion pass, as opposed to the dominant decoupled paradigms (autoregressive chunk-by-chunk, keyframe-to-video pipelines). Its key departure is the introduction of two specialized attention mechanisms—Window Cross-Attention for localized text-to-shot alignment and Sparse Inter-Shot Self-Attention for near-linear scaling—that address the two practical failures of prior holistic methods: imprecise shot control and quadratic computational cost. The sparse attention design draws inspiration from efficient transformer architectures (Radial Attention, LinGen, MoC) but is structurally tailored to the intra-shot/inter-shot duality unique to multi-shot video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making minute-scale holistic multi-shot generation computationally feasible, HoloCine shifts the practical frontier from isolated 5-second clips toward directed cinematic scenes, a step the paper frames as essential for automated filmmaking. The emergent memory capability—particularly fine-grained detail persistence (e.g., a background magnet reappearing in its exact position after intervening shots)—suggests the model builds implicit scene representations beyond what the prompt specifies, raising interesting questions about whether this generalizes to truly open-world generation. Open limitations include the 13-shot/60-second training ceiling, reliance on curated cinematic data, and the fact that the most compelling emergent behaviors are demonstrated qualitatively rather than through controlled quantitative benchmarks. The Shot Cut Accuracy metric and the 100-prompt benchmark are useful but small-scale, and direct comparison with LCT (not open-sourced) remains qualitative only.
