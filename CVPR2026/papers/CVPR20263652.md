## Problem

Vision-and-language navigation suffers from partial observability, where agents must reason about unobserved landmarks and goals. Existing imagination-based methods address this by generating pixel-level or feature-level future scenes, incurring high computational cost and introducing redundant visual details. The paper argues that effective navigation hinges not on full-scene reconstruction but on identifying task-relevant key environmental semantics, which can be captured more efficiently and selectively through language.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction and, at each step, a set of RGB views (one per adjacent navigable node) with associated direction angles in a discrete graph environment (Matterport3D). The primary output is the next discrete navigation action (node selection), but the system also produces two intermediate textual outputs: a state estimation description (left brain) identifying the current navigation progress within the instruction, and imaginative descriptions of what key objects or scenes might appear at each candidate node (right brain). During inference, only the LLM encoder is invoked (decoder-free), keeping the navigation loop lightweight.

## Method

ATD employs a dual-branch, weight-sharing architecture inspired by left-right brain functional specialization: a State Estimation LLM (left brain) trained via knowledge distillation from GPT-4V to track navigation progress, and an Imagination LLM (right brain) trained on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-generated candidate-node captions to predict future key semantics. Both branches fine-tune only the Q-Former on a frozen InstructBLIP/Flan-T5 backbone. A State Grounded Cross-Attention (SGCA) layer uses state estimation embeddings as queries and imagination embeddings as keys/values, adaptively filtering irrelevant imaginative details as navigation state changes. The resulting ATD embedding is injected into a DUET-style dynamic topological graph policy via multi-head cross-attention, where a Graph-Aware Self-Attention (GASA) layer incorporates spatial distances and visual similarities before predicting the next action. Training combines cross-entropy losses for the two LLM branches with Behaviour Cloning and Pseudo Interactive Demonstrator losses for the navigation policy, all on a single GPU.

## Results

On R2R val unseen, ATD (FlanT5-XL-1.5B with PREVALENT pretraining) achieves 75% SR and 63% SPL, outperforming the same-size NavGPT2 by 5% SR and 4% SPL. On R2R test unseen, ATD reaches 74% SR and 63% SPL, surpassing the 7B-parameter NaviLLM by 6% SR and 3% SPL. Zero-shot cross-environment evaluation shows ATD outperforms DUET by 3% SR and 4% SPL on REVERIE val unseen, and by 2% SR and 2% SPL on R4R val unseen, indicating stronger generalization to semantically rich instruction formats.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ATD (1.5B) outperforms the same-size NavGPT2 by 3% SR and 3% SPL on R2R val seen, and by 5% SR and 4% SPL on val unseen, demonstrating that the dual-branch left-right brain architecture is superior to NavGPT2's single-brain latent bridging. Against the larger 7B NaviLLM, ATD gains 6% SR and 3% SPL on the test split, showing that preserving the LLM's text-generation capability via a frozen decoder is more effective than full fine-tuning. ATD matches BEVBert on val unseen (75% SR) and slightly exceeds it on test unseen (74% vs 73% SR), despite not using depth information. Compared to DUET+ScaleVLN, which leverages 4.9M synthetic instruction-trajectory pairs, ATD trained solely on MP3D achieves 75% SR vs 79% on val unseen, a 4-point gap attributable to the absence of external data. On zero-shot cross-environment transfer, ATD surpasses DUET by 3% SR and 4% SPL on REVERIE and by 2% SR and 2% SPL on R4R, indicating that language-based imagination improves generalization to high-level and compositional instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DUET's dynamic topological graph for the navigation policy and InstructBLIP's Q-Former architecture for visual-language fusion, but departs fundamentally from the dominant imagination-based VLN paradigm (PathDreamer, DreamWalker, HNR, UnitedVLN) that generates pixel-level or feature-level future observations. Instead, ATD replaces visual synthesis with language-based imagination, arguing that task-relevant semantic cues can be captured more efficiently through text than through full-scene reconstruction. The dual-branch 'left-right brain' design, where a State Estimation LLM constrains an Imagination LLM via State Grounded Cross-Attention, is a novel architectural contribution not present in prior single-LLM approaches like NavGPT2 or NaviLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the field's implicit assumption that future-scene imagination must be visual, showing that compact textual descriptions of key environmental semantics can guide navigation with fewer parameters and lower computational cost. However, the approach is limited to discrete graph-based environments, relies on GPT-4V and Qwen2.5-VL as captioning oracles for training data, and the LLM remains frozen (only Q-Former is fine-tuned), raising questions about whether the imagination quality scales to more complex or continuous scenes. Open questions include whether text-based imagination generalizes to continuous VLN, whether the SGCA mechanism transfers to other embodied tasks, and whether the gains persist when the LLM backbone is scaled or when the navigation graph is replaced by a learned spatial representation.
