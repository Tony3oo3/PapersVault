## Problem

Existing CoSOD methods rely on supervised training over closed-set datasets, limiting their generalization to unseen object categories. Although vision foundation models (SAM, DINO) demonstrate strong zero-shot segmentation and semantic understanding, no prior work has systematically combined them to identify co-salient objects across image groups without any task-specific training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a group of N related RGB images (each containing salient objects of a common category) and produces one binary co-salient segmentation mask per image. SAM (ViT-H) generates raw mask proposals at native resolution, while DINO (ViT-B/8) extracts attention maps and [CLS] prototypes after resizing masked regions to 224×224. The entire pipeline is training-free and runs on a single GPU with no gradient updates.

## Method

TF-SSD is a progressive three-stage pipeline that transforms SAM's exhaustive mask proposals into co-salient predictions through DINO-guided filtering. First, a Quality Mask Generator (QMG) removes trivial and overlapping masks via area-ratio thresholding, pairwise overlap elimination, and a balanced quality score combining SAM's predicted IoU with an area-preference penalty. Second, an Intra-image Saliency Filter (ISF) scores each surviving mask by the mean DINO [CLS] attention response within its region, retaining only the top-T masks that align with visually salient areas. Third, an Inter-image Prototype Selector (IPS) extracts a DINO [CLS] prototype for each salient mask, builds a pairwise cosine similarity matrix across all masks in the group, and for each image selects the mask whose maximum cross-image similarity scores (one per other image) sum to the highest value. The key insight is the complementary synergy: SAM provides precise boundary delineation while DINO supplies the semantic saliency and cross-image consistency signals that SAM alone cannot distinguish.

## Results

On the CoCA benchmark, TF-SSD achieves Fβmax of 0.686, Sα of 0.763, and MAE of 0.077, surpassing the previous training-free method ZS-CoSOD by 13.7% in Fβmax (0.686 vs. 0.549) and 9.6% in Sα. On CoSal2015, TF-SSD reaches Fβmax 0.899 and Sα 0.890, and on CoSOD3k it achieves Fβmax 0.860 and Sα 0.861. These results are competitive with the strongest supervised methods (CONDA, VCP) while requiring no training data or gradient updates, operating entirely through SAM mask generation and DINO feature/attention extraction on a single NVIDIA 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the only prior training-free method ZS-CoSOD [34], TF-SSD achieves substantial gains: +13.7% Fβmax on CoCA (0.686 vs. 0.549), +10.0% on CoSal2015 (0.899 vs. 0.799), and +24.3% on CoSOD3k (0.860 vs. 0.691), along with consistent improvements in MAE and Sα across all three benchmarks. Compared to the strongest supervised methods, TF-SSD is competitive but not uniformly superior: on CoCA it edges out CONDA in Fβmax (0.686 vs. 0.685) and matches it in Sα (0.763), while VCP leads in MAE (0.069 vs. 0.077) and Sα (0.774 vs. 0.763). On CoSal2015 and CoSOD3k, VCP and CONDA maintain advantages in most metrics (e.g., VCP Fβmax 0.920 vs. TF-SSD 0.899 on CoSal2015), though TF-SSD surpasses CONDA in Fβmax on CoSOD3k (0.860 vs. 0.853) and in Eξmax (0.908 vs. 0.911 is close). TF-SSD clearly outperforms all unsupervised baselines (SCoSPARC, US-CoSOD, TokenCut, DVFDVD) by large margins on every metric and dataset. The key differentiator is that TF-SSD achieves near-supervised performance without any training data, gradients, or task-specific parameters, relying solely on frozen SAM (ViT-H) and DINO (ViT-B/8) models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two foundation model lines: SAM for exhaustive mask proposal generation and DINO for self-supervised semantic understanding. It departs from the dominant training-based CoSOD paradigm (supervised methods like GCoNet+, CONDA, VCP; unsupervised methods like SCoSPARC) by requiring no task-specific training at all. The closest prior work is ZS-CoSOD, which also uses SAM in a training-free setting but relies on learned group prompts; TF-SSD instead treats SAM purely as a proposal generator and delegates all semantic discrimination to DINO's attention maps and CLS-token prototypes, introducing a three-stage progressive filtering pipeline (QMG → ISF → IPS) that has no direct analogue in prior CoSOD literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching or exceeding fully supervised methods without any CoSOD-specific training, TF-SSD demonstrates that the closed-set dataset bottleneck in CoSOD can be circumvented entirely, opening the door to open-world and long-tail co-salient scenarios. The ablation revealing that DINO's intra-image attention (ISF) contributes the single largest performance jump (16.4% F-beta) suggests that self-supervised attention maps are a surprisingly strong proxy for saliency, a finding that may transfer to other segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the pipeline's many hand-tuned hyperparameters (area thresholds, overlap ratios, top-k selections) generalize across domains, and whether upgrading to DINOv2/v3 or SAM 2 would further close the remaining gap to the best supervised methods.
