## Problem

In Source-Free Object Detection, domain shift causes the detector's feature activations to become spatially diffuse and bleed into background clutter, degrading both localization and classification. Existing SFOD methods focus on refining pseudo-labels or stabilizing teacher-student training but overlook this fundamental feature-space degradation. The paper also notes the absence of a theoretical risk-bound analysis connecting SFOD training objectives to detection error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262781/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled target images (RGB or thermal) along with pre-computed class-agnostic binary foreground masks derived from a frozen OV-SAM segmenter, which are generated once offline and never queried during training or inference. The student detector (a standard CNN backbone with classification and regression heads) outputs bounding boxes and class labels in the usual detection format. The binary masks are rescaled to match the student's channel-mean activation map resolution (H'×W') before being used in the SPAR loss.

## Method

FALCON-SFOD operates within the standard Mean-Teacher self-training framework and introduces two complementary losses. SPAR (Spatial Prior-Aware Regularization) aligns the student's channel-mean feature map with the pre-computed binary foreground mask using a combined L1 and Dice loss, directly suppressing spurious background activations and promoting compact, object-shaped responses. IRPL (Imbalance-aware Noise Robust Pseudo-Labeling) applies a peak-adjust transform (extending ε-softmax) to the student's class probabilities, which creates two regimes: when teacher and student agree, the gradient is uniformly scaled down (soft early-stopping), and when they disagree, the full corrective signal is preserved; this is further augmented with per-class foreground-background weighting and a KL-divergence entropy term to counter class dominance. A theoretical analysis decomposes detection risk into classification and localization components, showing that noisy pseudo-labels inflate classification risk by a multiplicative 1/λ factor and localization risk by additive terms η_reg and 2ζ; IRPL replaces the multiplicative inflation with a tighter additive bound, while SPAR directly shrinks the localization error terms by cleaning feature-space misalignment.

## Results

On the primary Cityscapes → Foggy Cityscapes benchmark, FALCON-SFOD achieves 46.9% mAP, outperforming the previous SFOD SOTA (DRU, 43.7%) by 3.2 points and Simple-SFOD (45.0%) by 1.9 points. On Sim10k → Cityscapes (car), it reaches 58.8% AP, surpassing Simple-SFOD (55.4%) by 3.4 points. On KITTI → Cityscapes (car), it achieves 50.1% AP, beating PETS (47.0%) by 3.1 points and DRU (45.1%) by 5.0 points. On Cityscapes → BDD100k, FALCON-SFOD attains 36.9% mAP, edging out DRU (36.6%) and Simple-SFOD (34.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among SFOD methods, FALCON-SFOD outperforms all competitors on all three primary benchmarks: it beats DRU by 3.2 mAP on C→F, 0.1 AP on S→C, and 5.0 AP on K→C; it surpasses Simple-SFOD by 1.9 mAP on C→F and 3.4 AP on S→C; and it exceeds SF-YOLO by 4.4 mAP on C→F and 0.7 AP on K→C. On the C→F benchmark, the largest per-class gains are on tail categories: train (+4.1 over Simple-SFOD), bus (+2.9), and motorcycle (+2.4). On S→C, FALCON-SFOD narrowly edges DRU (58.8 vs. 58.7 AP) while clearly beating Simple-SFOD (55.4) and SF-YOLO (57.7). On K→C, the margin over PETS (47.0) and DRU (45.1) is the widest, at 3.1 and 5.0 AP respectively. On the extreme-shift benchmarks (Pascal VOC→Clipart, FLIR Visible→Infrared, FLIR Infrared→COCO), FALCON-SFOD consistently improves over the Simple-SFOD baseline by approximately 1.5–2.0 mAP, confirming robustness under severe domain gaps.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON-SFOD is built directly on the Simple-SFOD self-training pipeline [7] and operates within the standard Mean-Teacher paradigm [27], where an EMA teacher generates pseudo-labels for student adaptation. Its key departure from prior SFOD work (IRG, PETS, DRU, SF-YOLO) is a shift in focus: rather than refining pseudo-label selection or stabilizing teacher-student dynamics, the paper argues the root cause of poor adaptation is degraded object focus in the feature space under domain shift. The IRPL loss extends the peak-adjust transform from ε-softmax [32] with foreground-background weighting and entropy regularization specifically tailored to detection's class imbalance, while SPAR introduces a novel use of class-agnostic OV-SAM masks as a one-time spatial prior for feature regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first SFOD work to formally identify and address object-focused feature representation as a distinct failure mode, complementing the well-studied pseudo-label noise problem, and provides the first theoretical risk-bound analysis for SFOD linking loss design to tighter classification and localization error bounds. The zero-inference-overhead design (masks precomputed offline) makes the approach practical for deployment. However, the performance gains are modest (+1.9 mAP over Simple-SFOD on C→F), the method relies on OV-SAM as an external foundation model for mask generation, and evaluation is concentrated on driving-scene benchmarks; whether the feature-focus argument generalizes to non-driving domains or transformer-based detectors remains open.
