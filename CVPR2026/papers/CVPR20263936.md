## Problem

Existing personalized unified multimodal models suffer from three critical gaps: (1) a single shared parameter space causes representational conflict across understanding, generation, and editing tasks; (2) learned concept embeddings remain opaque, making it impossible to verify whether the model genuinely leverages textual attribute knowledge or merely memorizes visual patterns; and (3) personalized image editing is entirely absent from both prior methods and benchmarks, despite being the most demanding test of unified personalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263936/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework is trained on approximately 10 concept images per entity paired with textual descriptions, using 32 learnable tokens per concept (16 routed to the understanding expert, 16 to the generation expert). At inference, it accepts a text prompt for generation, a source image plus editing instruction for editing, or a question for understanding, and produces text responses, personalized generated images, or personalized edited images respectively. The explicit knowledge replay mechanism operates entirely within a single unified model pass at inference time, requiring no external retrieval or additional model calls.

## Method

OmniPersona introduces structurally decoupled concept tokens that are routed to task-specialized expert subspaces within a unified Bagel (7B MoT) backbone, so that understanding and generation tokens occupy distinct parameter slots and avoid gradient interference. At inference, a three-stage explicit knowledge replay pipeline (Intent Parser → Memory Retriever → Prompt Composer) reformulates the user request into an attribute query, retrieves grounded answers from the learned token embeddings, and composes a refined prompt before generation, externalizing otherwise opaque concept knowledge. Training jointly optimizes cross-entropy loss for autoregressive understanding and rectified-flow MSE loss for both generation and editing, with the editing task providing fine-grained localization and identity-preservation constraints that regularize representations across all three tasks. The authors further construct OmniPBench, extending UnifyBench's 20 concepts with automatically generated and manually verified personalized editing triplets, enabling the first systematic cross-task evaluation of unified personalization.

## Results

On OmniPBench, OmniPersona achieves 0.852 recognition accuracy and 0.678 QA GPT score for personalized understanding, outperforming all unified baselines. For generation, it attains the highest CLIP-I (0.791) and face similarity (0.413) among all methods. In personalized attribute-reasoning generation (PARG), it scores 0.613 overall with 0.788 CLIP-I, surpassing UniCTokens by 70.8% on PARG score. Most notably, OmniPersona achieves SOTA personalized editing with 0.658 average (SEMA-C 0.711, QUAL-I 0.605), exceeding GPT-4o+IP by 17.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified personalization baselines, OmniPersona substantially outperforms UniCTokens on recognition (0.852 vs. 0.790), PARG score (0.613 vs. 0.359, +70.8%), and editing average (0.658 vs. 0.184), while UniCTokens retains an edge on DINO (0.646 vs. 0.579). Compared to Yo'Chameleon, OmniPersona leads on every metric: recognition (+11.4%), Pure Gen CLIP-I (+13.7%), face similarity (+84.8%), PARG score (+130%), and editing average (+182%). Versus the zero-shot Bagel+TP baseline (text descriptions only), OmniPersona wins on understanding (Rec. 0.852 vs. 0.788), generation identity (Face-Simi 0.413 vs. 0.309), and editing (0.658 vs. 0.432), but Bagel+TP achieves a higher PARG score (0.813 vs. 0.613) by directly injecting long-context descriptions without learned tokens. Against the 200B GPT-4o+IP upper bound, OmniPersona surpasses it on editing average (0.658 vs. 0.558, +17.9%) and recognition (0.852 vs. 0.773), while GPT-4o+IP retains advantages on Pure Gen CLIP-I (0.722 vs. 0.791 is actually won by OmniPersona) and PARG CLIP-I (0.782 vs. 0.788, marginal). Among understanding-only methods (Yo'LLaVA, MC-LLaVA, RAP), OmniPersona achieves competitive recognition (0.852) while uniquely supporting generation and editing in a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified personalization line initiated by Yo'Chameleon (dual soft prompts + self-prompting) and UniCTokens (unified concept tokens with three-stage mutual learning) by introducing structurally decoupled expert-routed tokens and an inference-time explicit knowledge replay mechanism. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) replacing a single shared latent space for concept knowledge with task-specialized token subspaces to mitigate cross-task gradient conflict, and (2) adding personalized image editing as a third training and evaluation objective, which no prior unified personalization framework has addressed. The backbone (Bagel, a hybrid autoregressive + rectified-flow architecture) and the UnifyBench concept set are inherited directly from the unified LMM and personalization literature, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of personalized editing as a first-class task and the OmniPBench benchmark fill a genuine gap: prior unified personalization work evaluated only understanding and generation, leaving the most demanding test of identity-preserving manipulation unexamined. The finding that editing supervision reciprocally improves understanding and generation (cross-task synergy) suggests a practical training recipe for future unified personalization systems. However, the evaluation is limited to 20 concepts on a single 7B backbone, the editing metric relies on LLM-as-judge (less reproducible than standard metrics), and the knowledge replay pipeline adds inference latency through multiple sequential UMM calls—open questions include whether these gains transfer to larger concept sets, different backbones, and real-time deployment settings.
