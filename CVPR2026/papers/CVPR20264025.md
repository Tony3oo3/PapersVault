## Problem

Existing contrastive multi-view clustering methods face two intertwined limitations: they require a predefined number of clusters K that is often unknown in practice, and their unweighted contrastive alignment forces high-quality views to conform to low-quality ones, causing representation degeneration. No prior work simultaneously addresses both the automatic estimation of K and the quality imbalance among views within a single unsupervised framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes M views per sample where each view may have a different input dimensionality d_v (e.g., 2-view MNIST-USPS, 3-view CIFAR-10, up to 5-view Caltech-101), and projects all views into a shared latent space of dimension d via view-specific encoder-decoder pairs. It outputs cluster assignments via K-Means, an estimated cluster number K selected from a discrete candidate set of size N_K by the RL policy, and fused global representations H ∈ R^{N×d}. The RL module operates on a state composed of the current embedding matrix and cluster centroids, with the Q-network scoring each candidate K at every training epoch.

## Method

RCNMC combines two complementary modules. The Semantics-Aware Contrastive Learning module computes the Jensen-Shannon divergence between each view's embedding and the global fused representation, then converts these discrepancies into adaptive view weights via softmax(−JSD), so that high-quality views receive larger weights in the InfoNCE-style contrastive loss while noisy views are suppressed. The Reinforcement Learning-based Cluster Number Estimation module formulates K selection as an MDP where the state is {Z_t, C_t} (embeddings and centroids), the action is a candidate K, and the reward combines negative mean intra-cluster distance with mean inter-cluster centroid distance; a Q-network is trained via experience replay with a standard TD loss to maximize this reward. The encoder is jointly optimized with a reconstruction loss (preserving view-specific information), the weighted contrastive loss, and a sharpened K-Means clustering guidance loss, while the Q-network is updated separately once the experience buffer fills, allowing K to be inferred in a single training pass without repeated retraining.

## Results

RCNMC achieves state-of-the-art performance across 12 multi-view datasets without requiring a predefined cluster number K. On MNIST-USPS it reaches 98.1% ACC / 95.5% NMI, on Fashion 99.5% ACC / 97.8% NMI, and on CIFAR-100 94.8% ACC / 98.4% NMI. Notably, these results are obtained while autonomously estimating K via reinforcement learning, whereas competing contrastive methods (ICMVC, MGBCC, DIVIDE) are given the ground-truth K as input. The estimated K values closely match the true cluster counts (e.g., K=10 for MNIST-USPS, K=5 for BDGP, K=3 for Synthetic3d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RCNMC outperforms all 8 deep contrastive baselines on the majority of datasets, and the authors note the comparison is inherently favorable to baselines since ICMVC, MGBCC, and DIVIDE are provided the ground-truth K while RCNMC estimates it automatically. Against the strongest contrastive competitor MFLVC, RCNMC wins on MNIST-USPS (98.1 vs 97.2 ACC), Fashion (99.5 vs 99.2), CIFAR-10 (99.5 vs 99.1), and CIFAR-100 (94.8 vs 82.6 ACC), though MFLVC edges ahead on BDGP NMI (0.960 vs 0.938). Against DIVIDE, RCNMC achieves substantially larger margins, e.g., 99.5 vs 61.8 ACC on CIFAR-10 and 94.8 vs 62.4 ACC on CIFAR-100. Against ICMVC, RCNMC leads on MNIST-USPS (98.1 vs 92.2 ACC), Fashion (99.5 vs 89.5), and CIFAR-100 (94.8 vs 85.2 ACC). Traditional non-parametric methods (DBSCAN, DPCA) and parametric methods (K-Means, GMM) are far behind: on MNIST-USPS, DBSCAN achieves only 43.7% ACC versus RCNMC's 98.1%, and on CIFAR-100 DBSCAN estimates K=82 (true K=100) with 52.4% ACC versus RCNMC's 94.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based cluster number estimation framework of Liu et al. (ACM MM 2023), which formulated unknown-K clustering as a Q-learning MDP in a single-view/graph setting, to the multi-view contrastive clustering domain. It simultaneously departs from the unweighted contrastive alignment paradigm of Li et al. (TKDE 2026) and Trosten et al. (CVPR 2021) by introducing JSD-divergence-based adaptive view weighting to prevent representation degeneration when view quality is imbalanced. The core contribution is the unification of these two previously separate concerns—automatic K inference and quality-aware contrastive alignment—into a single end-to-end training loop (RCNMC), rather than treating them as independent preprocessing or post-processing steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: real-world multi-view data (medical imaging, multimedia) rarely come with a known cluster count, and view quality is often heterogeneous, making the simultaneous treatment of both issues genuinely useful. However, the RL-based K estimation adds non-trivial training complexity (experience buffer, Q-network, ε-schedule) that may be hard to tune, and the JSD computation is applied to raw feature vectors rather than normalized probability distributions, raising questions about its statistical validity. Open questions include scalability beyond the 50K-sample datasets tested, whether the RL component outperforms simpler iterative heuristics (e.g., silhouette-based selection) at larger scale, and how the method behaves when the true K is genuinely ill-defined or hierarchical.
