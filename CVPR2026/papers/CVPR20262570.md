## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for object detection under adverse weather rely on either additive (lightweight adapter) or subtractive (channel pruning) strategies, each effective only within a limited range of domain shift severity. Additive methods excel under moderate shifts but fail under severe corruption, while subtractive methods handle severe shifts well but risk information loss under moderate ones. No prior method adaptively balances both strategies per-channel based on measured feature-level domain shift.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled adverse-weather driving images (fog, rain, snow, night) in batches of 16 and produces standard object detection outputs: bounding boxes with class labels evaluated via mAP@50. Adaptation is fully online, requiring only pre-computed source feature statistics (mean of image-level and RoI-aligned instance-level features at each BN layer) from the clear-weather training set, with no access to target labels or source data during test time.

## Method

CD-Buffer computes a channel-wise domain discrepancy score D_c by combining L1 distances between target and pre-computed source statistics at both image-level and instance-level (RoI alignment). This single metric drives two coordinated buffers: a subtractive buffer with learnable mask scores (initialized from BN weight magnitudes) that suppresses severely shifted channels via discrepancy-weighted regularization L_mask = (1/C)Σ‖D_c·s_c‖₁, and an additive buffer of lightweight 1×1/3×3 convolutions in residual paths whose compensation strength is inversely modulated by the same mask scores, so aggressively suppressed channels receive stronger refinement. The binary suppression mask uses a dynamic percentile threshold with a reparameterization trick for gradient flow and stochastic reactivation to prevent permanent channel loss. Optimization combines a feature alignment loss (L1 distance on per-layer mean and variance) with the mask regularization, plus layer-level gradient scaling that amplifies additive buffer updates proportionally to each layer's aggregated discrepancy.

## Results

On KITTI fog 50m (severe), CD-Buffer achieves 44.80 mAP@50, outperforming the next-best ActMAD (39.65) by +5.15 and the subtractive PruningTTA (33.97) by +10.83. On moderate shifts like KITTI rain 75mm, performance is competitive with WHW (73.03 vs. 74.33) and BufferTTA (73.43), while on ACDC night conditions CD-Buffer leads all baselines by a wide margin (8.92 vs. 6.98 for BufferTTA). The method's key advantage is consistency: it achieves the best or near-best mAP@50 across all 8 synthetic conditions and 4 real-world ACDC conditions, whereas individual baselines peak only in specific severity ranges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CD-Buffer clearly dominates under severe domain shifts: on KITTI fog 50m it beats ActMAD by +5.15, PruningTTA by +10.83, and BufferTTA by +21.59 mAP@50, confirming that the subtractive component is critical when features are heavily corrupted. Under moderate shifts (KITTI rain 100mm/75mm), WHW and BufferTTA edge out CD-Buffer by 1.75 and 0.40 mAP@50 respectively, consistent with the paper's observation that additive methods excel when features need only subtle refinement. On the real-world ACDC benchmark, CD-Buffer leads on fog (+0.29 over BufferTTA), rain (+1.19 over WHW), and night (+1.94 over BufferTTA), but trails BufferTTA (17.18) and WHW (16.99) on snow (15.41). ActMAD, which updates all parameters, shows the most inconsistent pattern: strong on severe fog but degrading sharply on moderate rain (62.94 at 100mm vs. 56.37 at 200mm), while CD-Buffer maintains a tighter performance band across all severities. Overall, CD-Buffer wins or ties on 7 of 8 synthetic conditions and 3 of 4 ACDC conditions, with its primary advantage being robustness across the full severity spectrum rather than peak performance at any single point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate TTA paradigms for object detection: additive adapter-based methods (BufferTTA, WHW, L-TTA) that inject lightweight refinement modules, and subtractive channel-pruning methods (PruningTTA) that suppress domain-sensitive channels. The key departure is the observation that these paradigms are complementary rather than competing—subtractive excels under severe shifts while additive excels under moderate shifts—and the proposal to drive both from a single channel-wise discrepancy metric in opposite directions. The feature alignment loss follows ActMAD's L1 distribution matching, and the overall framework is instantiated on Faster R-CNN with ResNet-50, consistent with prior TTA-for-detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The complementary dual-buffer design offers a principled, automatically-tuned mechanism for handling heterogeneous shift severities within a single scene, which is a realistic property of adverse weather that prior single-paradigm methods ignore. However, the evaluation is limited to one backbone (ResNet-50) and one detector (Faster R-CNN), and the fixed 5% suppression ratio and hand-tuned temperatures (λ_S, λ_A) raise questions about generalizability to other architectures or more extreme corruptions. An open question is whether the discrepancy-driven coupling generalizes beyond channel-level modulation to spatial or temporal dimensions, and whether the approach extends to tasks beyond detection (e.g., segmentation, tracking) where feature-level shift patterns may differ.
