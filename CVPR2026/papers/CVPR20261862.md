## Problem

Existing VLA models are trained on fixed near-optimal camera viewpoints, making them brittle under viewpoint shifts and unable to actively perceive cluttered or occluded scenes. Prior attempts to add camera control either discretize it into a VQA-style selection (losing continuous control) or naively extend the VLA action space, which breaks learned manipulation priors and demands scarce concurrent camera+manipulation data. No benchmark existed to evaluate active manipulation beyond fixed-view settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261862/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes an RGB image, a natural-language instruction, and optional 3D geometric inputs (depth maps, camera intrinsics/extrinsics) at each timestep. It outputs a decoupled action chunk over a horizon k: a 2-D sequence of relative pitch/yaw head-camera deltas and a 26-D sequence of joint-position deltas for the Unitree G1's dual 7-DoF arms and dual 6-DoF Inspire hands. The decoupling means camera and manipulation actions are produced by separate decoders rather than a single unified action vector.

## Method

SaPaVe uses a bottom-up, two-stage training strategy with a decoupled action space. In Stage 1, a lightweight Camera Adapter (LoRA on the VLM) is trained on the newly introduced ActiveViewPose-200K dataset (200k image-language-camera-movement pairs) to acquire semantic active perception priors without altering the base VLM weights. In Stage 2, the Camera Adapter is frozen and a Decoupled Action Head (two separate decoders for camera vs. manipulation) is fine-tuned on a mixture of ActiveViewPose-200K and real robot manipulation data. A Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection module encodes arbitrary 3D geometry via a MapAnything-based feedforward encoder, whose spatial tokens are element-wise added to VLM output tokens before the action denoising step, improving viewpoint-invariant execution. This decoupled, staged design minimizes cross-interference between the two action types and avoids the data explosion that a unified action space would require.

## Results

On the real-world active manipulation benchmark, SaPaVe achieves an average success rate of 85.00%, outperforming π₀ (45.00%) by 40 percentage points and GR00T-N1 (53.75%) by 31.25 percentage points. On the semantic active perception evaluation (ActiveViewPose-200K), SaPaVe's Stage-1 model reaches 84.3% average success rate, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.7%) by 11.6% despite using only 2B parameters. In simulation (ActiveManip-Bench), the active camera configuration achieves 74.83% average success rate versus 36.17% for a fixed camera, with the largest gap on Out-of-View tasks (72% vs. 11%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SaPaVe decisively outperforms both existing VLA baselines on all four real-world active manipulation tasks. Against π₀ (45.00% avg), SaPaVe gains +40 points, with the largest margin on Out-of-View Articulated Manipulation (80% vs. 35%). Against GR00T-N1 (53.75% avg), the gap is +31.25 points, again widest on Out-of-View tasks. On the semantic perception sub-task (Table 1), SaPaVe's 2B-parameter Stage-1 model (84.3% avg) beats the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (62.3%), Multi-SpatialMLLM (70.2%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (72.7%), demonstrating that specialized training on ActiveViewPose-200K is more effective than scaling generalist VLMs. In simulation (Table 2), SaPaVe with an active camera (74.83%) also surpasses the Active Camera + Wrist Camera combination (73.16%), indicating that additional viewpoints provide diminishing returns once robust dynamic perception and 3D spatial understanding are in place.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of active perception (traditionally formulated as discrete Next-Best-View planning or VQA-based viewpoint selection) and end-to-end Vision-Language-Action models, which have been trained exclusively on fixed near-optimal camera views. It departs from concurrent works (EgoMI, ActiveUMI) that naively extend a VLA's unified action space to include camera movement, instead proposing a bottom-up two-stage strategy with a decoupled action head and a LoRA-based camera adapter to preserve both semantic perception priors and manipulation knowledge. The Universal Spatial Knowledge Injection module repurposes a feedforward 3D geometry encoder (MapAnything) to inject arbitrary geometric configurations into the action decoder, addressing the view-invariance weakness of existing VLA models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of ActiveManip-Bench (the first simulation benchmark for active manipulation) and ActiveViewPose-200K (the first large-scale semantic camera-control dataset) fills critical evaluation and training gaps that have hindered progress in this area. The finding that a 2B-parameter model with specialized two-stage training outperforms much larger generalist VLMs and directly fine-tuned VLA models (GR00T-N1, π0) by 30–40% suggests that data-efficient, decoupled training strategies may be the key to scaling active manipulation beyond the current data bottleneck. Open questions include whether the bottom-up strategy generalizes across robot embodiments, whether the simulation-to-real gap remains manageable as tasks grow in complexity, and whether the new benchmark will become a standard testbed for the community.
