## Problem

Existing fashion editing and virtual try-on methods are limited to garment-specific images as design sources, excluding creative inputs like artwork, abstract imagery, and natural photographs, and they cannot handle complete outfits including accessories such as footwear, bags, and necklaces. Additionally, naively combining ControlNet with IP-Adapter causes content leakage from image prompts and fails to fully remove original garments, leaving residual clothing in the output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261342/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

FEAT takes four inputs: a person image, a sketch (defining garment shape/outline), an image prompt (any source providing style and/or content cues), and a text prompt (describing details). All inputs are consumed through a Stable Diffusion XL pipeline with independent scaling factors that allow users to dynamically adjust or completely exclude any modality. The output is a single try-on result image, and the training-free framework generalizes beyond human photographs to game characters, animated figures, and animals without retraining.

## Method

FEAT introduces two training-free modules on top of SDXL. Disentangled Dual Injection (DDI) separates content and style in the image prompt: a Content-Subtractive Proxy Embedding (CSPE) subtracts the CLIP embedding of a globally blurred lightness-channel proxy from the original CLIP image embedding to isolate style, which is injected only into a designated style U-Net block, while the full original embedding is routed to three content-sensitive blocks with independent scaling factors. Orthogonal-Guided Noise Fusion (OGNF) handles garment removal and synthesis: Orthogonal Fashion Removal (OFR) computes a garment-specific latent direction by subtracting the VAE encoding of a white image from the VAE encoding of the garment segmentation mask, then projects and removes this direction from the person's latent; Region-Adaptive Noise Fusion (RANF) partitions the image into three regions (new-garment synthesis, existing-garment removal, and non-garment preservation) and applies distinct noise strategies to each during the T-step denoising loop, enabling full-outfit replacement without pairwise training data.

## Results

FEAT achieves the highest GPT-4V Elo score across all three multimodal settings on both garment and accessory datasets, reaching 1172.73 (Sketch+Image, garment) and 1210.31 (Sketch+Text, accessory). On the Sketch Chamfer Distance metric (lower is better), FEAT reduces the gap to 4.83–8.30 compared to 9.04–125.37 for baselines, indicating far more faithful shape adherence and complete removal of original garments. In the user study, FEAT is preferred by 74–83% of participants versus 3.85–19.23% for the strongest baseline, a gap of over 55 percentage points in most settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEAT outperforms all four baselines on every metric in every setting. Against ControlNet+IP-Adapter, FEAT improves GPT-4V Elo by 125–285 points and reduces Sketch CD by 2–29 points, while human preference jumps from ~17–19% to 75–83%. PICTURE is the weakest baseline on accessories, with a Sketch CD of 125.37 (vs. FEAT's 8.30) and only 8.33% human preference, reflecting its inability to handle non-garment items. MGD, designed for garment-only editing, drops sharply on accessories (GPT-4V 814.02 vs. FEAT's 1210.31; human preference 6.42% vs. 80.52%), confirming its limited generalizability beyond clothing. Standalone ControlNet (Sketch+Text) is competitive on Sketch CD for garments (8.09 vs. FEAT's 5.16) but lags on GPT-4V Elo (902.70 vs. 1148.15) and human preference (7.10% vs. 74.47%), indicating that sketch fidelity alone does not guarantee holistic try-on quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEAT builds directly on the ControlNet + IP-Adapter paradigm for multimodal diffusion conditioning, extending InstantStyle's content-subtraction idea (which removes all content cues) into a selective Disentangled Dual Injection that routes style and content features to different U-Net blocks with independent scaling. The try-on component departs from prior VTON methods that rely on garment-specific paired training data by introducing a training-free Orthogonal-Guided Noise Fusion that removes residual garments via latent-space orthogonal projection and applies region-specific noise strategies. Conceptually, the paper positions itself at the intersection of style-transfer disentanglement (content vs. style in CLIP space) and inpainting-style region control (Repaint-inspired noise re-injection), repurposing both for the fashion domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free design significantly lowers the barrier to VTON for unseen fashion items and accessories, reducing dependence on expensive paired dataset curation that has limited scalability of prior methods. Extending try-on from garments to full outfits (bags, shoes, scarves, belts) and from garment-specific design sources to arbitrary imagery (artwork, natural photos) broadens practical applicability for fashion designers and e-commerce. However, the evaluation leans heavily on GPT-4V Elo scores, which may encode model-specific biases, and the authors acknowledge instability for small accessories near the face. Open questions include whether the orthogonal projection generalizes to heavily occluded or multi-layered garments, and whether the block-wise content/style assignment transfers across different base diffusion architectures.
