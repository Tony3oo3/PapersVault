## Problem

Existing GUI agents represent actions as discrete, tokenized coordinates (e.g., click(x,y), drag(start,end)), which fundamentally prevents free-form, closed-loop trajectories such as drawing, rotating elements, or solving rotation captchas—tasks that demand continuous, on-the-fly visual perception and incremental cursor adjustment. No prior work has applied flow-based generative modeling to GUI manipulation, leaving a gap between the continuous control achieved by robotic VLA models and the discrete action space of digital GUI agents.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a streaming sequence of high-resolution screenshots (60 FPS recordings, ~577 frames per task on average), a natural-language task instruction, and a previous action state vector (initialized to [-1,-1] and updated with the last predicted action). It outputs action chunks of length 20, where each step is an (x, y, m) triplet with m ∈ {down, up} encoding cursor position and mouse button state; clicks are simply two-step minimal drags at the same coordinate. During closed-loop execution, the model re-observes the environment after every 1–5 executed steps before generating the next chunk, enabling reactive trajectory correction.

## Method

ShowUI-π builds on the SmolVLA-450M architecture, pairing a SmolVLM-2 VLM backbone with a 16-layer transformer action expert that performs interleaved self- and cross-attention with the VLM at each layer. All interactions—clicks and drags—are unified as sequences of (x,y,m) triplets, eliminating task-specific action heads. The action expert is trained with flow matching to predict a conditional velocity field that transports noisy actions to clean trajectories, augmented by two key modifications: (1) temporal reweighting that assigns 10× loss weight to the first and last trajectory steps (critical for drag initiation and endpoint precision) and (2) a directional regularization term penalizing cosine misalignment between predicted and ground-truth velocity vectors. The previous action is projected back into the VLM backbone to condition subsequent predictions, and the flow is executed k times per chunk to refine the trajectory deterministically.

## Results

On the ScreenDrag benchmark's online closed-loop evaluation, ShowUI-π-450M achieves an overall task success rate of 26.98%, surpassing the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-CUA (22.18%) by 4.8 points and the best open-source model OpenCUA-7B (21.98%) by 5.0 points. In offline evaluation, ShowUI-π attains 78.55% endpoint accuracy and 159.05 px average trajectory error, dramatically outperforming all language-token baselines (best: OpenCUA-7B at 21.58% endpoint accuracy). The model achieves particularly strong results on Captcha (55.91% online) and Handwriting (34.32% online), domains requiring on-the-fly visual observation during continuous dragging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ShowUI-π outperforms all seven baseline families on overall online success rate. Against proprietary models, it beats Gemini-2.5-CUA (22.18%) by 4.8 points, Seed-1.6-Vision (19.01%) by 8.0 points, UI-TARS-1.5-7B (17.03%) by 10.0 points, and OpenAI Operator (13.27%) by 13.7 points. Against open-source models, it surpasses OpenCUA-7B (21.98%) by 5.0 points, OpenCUA-32B (20.79%) by 6.2 points, and Qwen3-VL-32B (19.01%) by 8.0 points. Notably, ShowUI-π's advantage is most pronounced on free-form drag tasks: it scores 55.91% on Captcha (where Operator scores 0% due to safety refusal) and 34.32% on Handwriting (where all baselines score ≤2.97%), while all language-token baselines score 0% on Premiere Pro. In offline evaluation, ShowUI-π's 78.55% endpoint accuracy dwarfs the best baseline (OpenCUA-7B at 21.58%), and its 159.05 px trajectory error is roughly 2–3× lower than any baseline, confirming that flow-based generation captures continuous trajectory structure that discrete tokenization cannot represent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the flow-matching action generation paradigm from robotics VLA models (π0, Diffusion Policy) into the GUI automation setting, where prior agents (ShowUI, UI-TARS, OpenCUA, Gemini-2.5-CUA) represent all actions as discrete language tokens. It builds directly on the SmolVLA-450M architecture and the flow-matching framework of Lipman et al., but introduces a unified action representation that recasts clicks as degenerate drags, eliminating the need for separate action heads. The key departure from both the robotics and GUI literatures is the explicit modeling of continuous, multi-step cursor trajectories with on-the-fly visual re-observation, rather than single-shot start-end point predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ScreenDrag benchmark (505 tasks, 5 domains, 20K training trajectories) fills a clear gap: no prior GUI benchmark captures the intermediate state changes and multi-valid-trajectory nature of continuous drags. The finding that even the strongest proprietary agents (Gemini-2.5-CUA at 22.18%, Operator at 13.27%) struggle while a 450M flow-based model reaches 26.98% suggests that discrete tokenized action spaces are a fundamental bottleneck for dexterous GUI control, not just a convenience choice. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more domains and longer-horizon tasks, whether the data-driven online evaluation (matching to nearest recorded state) faithfully approximates true closed-loop interaction, and whether the low absolute success rates (~27%) reflect task difficulty or evaluation conservatism.
