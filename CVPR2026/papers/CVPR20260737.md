## Problem

Existing VLA models directly map sensory inputs to actions and struggle with long-horizon tasks that require precisely defined goal states, such as LEGO assembly or object rearrangement. The core gap is the inability to infer the procedural 'how' from a desired 'what'—i.e., to decompose a final goal configuration into coherent intermediate steps and then execute precise manipulations grounded in those steps—without relying on human-crafted manuals or demonstration videos.

## Input / Output

The model receives a language instruction, a current scene image, a final goal-state image, and the robot's proprioceptive state. It first produces a multimodal subgoal manual consisting of a textual object description, pixel-level (U,V) centroid coordinates for target placement, and a generated subgoal image (via a VQ tokenizer with 16× downsampling and a 16384×8 codebook). The action expert then consumes a prompted image (current scene with a position mask overlaid at the predicted coordinates), the robot state, and latent features from the manual generation pass to output a diffusion-based action chunk of horizon h. Manuals are generated only at key state-change frames rather than every timestep, reducing planning overhead.

## Method

ManualVLA extends Janus-Pro (DeepSeek-LLM 1.5B) into a Mixture-of-Transformers architecture where task-specific parameter sets (FFNs, attention projections, layer norms) are duplicated for a planning expert and an action expert, while a global cross-task attention matrix allows the action expert to attend to manual-generation tokens. The key reasoning mechanism, ManualCoT, combines explicit CoT (overlaying predicted (U,V) positions as a visual mask on the current image to guide the action expert) with implicit CoT (feeding latent key-value features from the manual generation pass through a designed attention mask into the action expert's conditioning). Training proceeds in three stages: action-expert pretraining on 400K+ cross-embodiment trajectories with diffusion-policy MSE loss, manual-expert pretraining on 10K+ frames per task synthesized via a 3D Gaussian Splatting digital-twin pipeline, and joint fine-tuning on ~100 teleoperated demonstrations per downstream task using a combined cross-entropy and diffusion loss. The 3DGS toolkit reconstructs real scenes, decomposes them into individual objects, and iteratively renders photorealistic intermediate assembly states with ground-truth positions and labels, eliminating the need for large-scale manual annotation.

## Results

On three long-horizon real-world tasks (2D LEGO Assembly, 3D LEGO Assembly, Object Rearrangement), ManualVLA achieves end-to-end success rates of 85%, 65%, and 65% respectively, outperforming the strongest hierarchical baseline (VLM+π₀.₅) by 15–30 percentage points per task. The model also generates high-fidelity subgoal manuals with PSNR of 28–29 dB, FID of 24–36, and position MAE of 3.2–6.2 pixels across the three tasks. In generalization tests under unseen backgrounds, object shapes, and lighting, ManualVLA degrades by only 17–29% compared to 17–46% for the hierarchical baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ManualVLA outperforms all four baseline categories across every task. Against the direct VLA baselines (π₀, π₀.₅, FAST), the improvement is dramatic: ManualVLA's 85% S.R. on 2D LEGO exceeds π₀.₅'s 20% by 65 percentage points, and its 65% on 3D LEGO exceeds FAST's 5% by 60 points. Against CoT-VLA, which also predicts subgoal images, ManualVLA gains 55 percentage points on 2D LEGO (85% vs. 30%) and 40 points on 3D LEGO (65% vs. 25%), attributed to the richer multimodal manual (text + position + image) and the dual explicit/implicit CoT reasoning. The closest competitor is the hierarchical VLM+π₀.₅ baseline, which ManualVLA beats by 25 points on 2D LEGO (85% vs. 60%), 30 points on 3D LEGO (65% vs. 35%), and 15 points on Object Rearrangement (65% vs. 50%). Notably, ManualVLA maintains high step-wise accuracy throughout the sequence (e.g., 95%→90%→85% on 2D LEGO), whereas baselines show steep degradation after the first step, indicating that the unified MoT architecture with cross-task shared attention better sustains performance over long horizons.

## Relation to Prior Work

ManualVLA extends the VLA model lineage (π0, π0.5, FAST, OpenVLA) by addressing a specific gap: long-horizon, goal-conditioned manipulation where the final state is predefined but intermediate steps are unknown. It departs from hierarchical approaches (Vid2Robot, DexCap, CheckManual) that rely on human-provided videos or manuals by having the model autonomously generate multimodal subgoal manuals from the goal image alone. Architecturally, it adapts the Mixture-of-Transformers design (Liang et al., 2024) from multi-modal foundation models to the VLA setting, unifying a planning expert (built on Janus-Pro's generative VLM) and an action expert (diffusion-based, pretrained on DROID/Open X-Embodiment) within a single token sequence with cross-task shared attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single unified model can both plan (generate manuals) and execute (predict actions) for long-horizon goal-conditioned tasks, eliminating the need for human-crafted intermediate procedures and reducing downstream data requirements to ~100 trajectories. The 3D Gaussian Splatting digital-twin toolkit offers a practical path to scaling manual-generation training data without extensive teleoperation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond tabletop bimanual manipulation to more complex or mobile settings, how it scales to tasks with many more subgoals, and whether the MoT architecture's benefits persist at larger model scales. The evaluation is limited to a single robot platform (Franka dual-arm) and three task types, leaving broader generalization untested.
