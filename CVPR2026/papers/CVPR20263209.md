## Problem

CLIP-based open-vocabulary dense perception methods suffer from synonym-induced grounding inconsistency: semantically equivalent expressions (e.g., 'zebra' vs. 'striped horse' vs. 'hippotigris') produce markedly different spatial attention maps, causing substantial AP drops (up to 9.5 points) when category names are replaced by synonyms. This undermines robustness in real-world deployments where linguistic variation is inevitable, yet no prior method explicitly enforces attention consistency across synonymous expressions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text expression (either a simple category label or a semantically enriched string containing synonyms and definitions) as input. The frozen CLIP text encoder processes the text, while a ViT-based visual encoder (ViT-B/16 or ViT-L/14) produces dense patch-level features. The output is a refined spatial attention map per text expression, which is then plugged into a downstream detector (F-ViT) or segmentor to yield bounding boxes or segmentation masks with class labels. The key design constraint is that the attention map must remain spatially consistent whether the input text is the canonical label or any of its synonymous variants.

## Method

SynCLIP introduces two modules on top of the DeCLIP/F-ViT framework. The Semantic-consistent Spatial Attention alignment (SSA) module employs a teacher-student dual visual encoder: the student (trainable) computes attention between dense features and original label embeddings, while the frozen teacher computes attention between its own features and semantically enriched expression embeddings (synonyms + definitions); an L2 semantic alignment loss minimizes the per-patch discrepancy between the two attention maps. The Spatial Attention Refinement (SAR) module then selects the top-k highest-attention tokens from the enriched map, computes pairwise cosine-similarity spatial correlations using DINOv2 features, averages these correlations into a context-aware spatial attention map, and fuses it with the semantic attention via a weighted sum (α=β=0.5). Pretraining is supported by SEViC, a synonym-enriched corpus of 118,287 COCO2017 images paired with 1,234 categories and 11,558 LLM-verified enriched expressions, enabling the model to learn expression-invariant grounding.

## Results

On OV-COCO, F-ViT+SynCLIP achieves 43.6 and 49.8 AP₅₀^novel with ViT-B/16 and ViT-L/14 backbones, respectively, surpassing the previous best CLIP-based methods (F-ViT+DeCLIP at 41.1 and 46.2). On the more challenging OV-LVIS benchmark, SynCLIP reaches 27.8 and 37.2 mAP_r^mask, outperforming or matching all compared methods with similar backbones. Under synonym substitution, SynCLIP degrades by only 4.4 AP₅₀^novel compared to 9.5 for DeCLIP and 8.8 for CLIPSelf, demonstrating substantially improved grounding robustness to linguistic variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynCLIP outperforms all directly comparable CLIP-based baselines on OV-COCO: it beats F-ViT+DeCLIP by +2.5 (ViT-B/16) and +3.6 (ViT-L/14) AP₅₀^novel, and F-ViT+CLIPSelf by +6.0 and +5.5 respectively. On OV-LVIS, SynCLIP leads F-ViT+DeCLIP by +1.0 mAP_r^mask (ViT-B/16) and ties at 37.2 (ViT-L/14), while surpassing F-ViT+CLIPSelf by +2.5 and +2.3. Against non-CLIP baselines, SynCLIP (ViT-L/14) exceeds OV-DQUO (45.6, RN50x4) and CORA+ (43.1, RN50x4) on OV-COCO, though these use different backbone families. The most distinctive advantage appears in synonym-robustness: under synonym substitution, SynCLIP retains 39.2 AP₅₀^novel (−4.4) versus DeCLIP's 31.5 (−9.5) and CLIPSelf's 28.8 (−8.8), nearly halving the performance drop. On OV-LVIS, SynCLIP also surpasses OV-SAM (24.0, RN50x16) and F-VLM (32.8, RN50x64) with a much smaller ViT-B/16 backbone, highlighting the efficiency of the synonym-coherent pretraining approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynCLIP builds directly on the DeCLIP framework (CVPR 2025) for region-level vision-language alignment and uses F-ViT/CLIPSelf (ICLR 2024) as the downstream detection backbone, extending the CLIP-based OVDP pipeline. Its key conceptual departure is identifying synonym-induced grounding inconsistency as a previously overlooked failure mode: prior methods (CLIPSelf, DeCLIP, RegionCLIP) improve region-text alignment but do not enforce consistency across semantically equivalent expressions. The paper introduces two new modules—SSA (a teacher-student attention alignment loss) and SAR (VFM-guided spatial token selection and aggregation)—alongside a new pretraining corpus (SEViC) to address this gap, positioning itself as a robustness-oriented refinement of the existing CLIP-based OVDP paradigm rather than a new architectural family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important concern for real-world OVDP deployment: users and systems will inevitably use diverse linguistic expressions for the same object, and the demonstrated 8–10 AP drops under synonym substitution in prior methods represent a significant robustness gap. SynCLIP roughly halves this degradation (4.4 AP drop) and achieves SOTA among CLIP-based methods on both OV-COCO and OV-LVIS, while SEViC provides a reusable resource for synonym-enriched pretraining. However, the model still degrades under synonym substitution, and the OV-LVIS ViT-L/14 result merely matches DeCLIP rather than clearly surpassing it, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more extreme linguistic variation (paraphrases, multi-attribute descriptions, cross-lingual synonyms) or to larger-scale open-world settings beyond the 1,234-category vocabulary.
