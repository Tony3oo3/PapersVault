## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks primarily assess aesthetic quality and temporal consistency, while MLLM benchmarks focus on abstract reasoning tasks, leaving a critical gap in evaluating whether models can perceive and predict physically coherent dynamics in real-world domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, and industrial settings. No unified benchmark systematically tests both the prediction capability (via video generation) and perception capability (via video understanding) required for Physical AI.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263972/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PAI-Bench-G takes text prompts (curated via MLLM captioning plus manual refinement) and produces generated videos scored on 8 VBench quality metrics and an MLLM-judged Domain Score from 5,636 QA pairs. PAI-Bench-C takes control signal videos (blur, edge, depth, segmentation) combined with text prompts and produces videos evaluated via modality-specific fidelity metrics (Blur SSIM, Edge F1, Depth si-RMSE, Mask mIoU), DOVER quality, and LPIPS diversity. PAI-Bench-U takes 16 video frames (8 for InternVL3.5) plus multiple-choice questions and produces a letter answer, with a Qwen3-8B post-processor extracting the final option from model responses.

## Method

PAI-Bench introduces three complementary tracks grounded in 2,808 real-world video cases across six Physical AI domains. For physical plausibility evaluation (Domain Score), the benchmark uses Qwen3-VL-235B as a judge against curated QA pairs derived from an ontology, validated by a human arena study yielding r=0.918 Pearson correlation with ELO scores. PAI-Bench-C is the first benchmark to systematically test conditional video generation, projecting generated videos into modality-specific spaces (Blur Kernel, Canny, Video Depth Anything, GroundingDINO+SAM2) to measure fidelity against ground-truth control signals. PAI-Bench-U formalizes physical common sense into three sub-domains (Space, Time, Physical World) and embodied reasoning into two components (Predicting Action Effects, Adherence to Physical Constraints), with 1,214 manually annotated QA pairs. Bias analysis confirms the benchmark neutralizes language priors (zero-frame performance drops to random) and requires temporal context (substantial gap between 1-frame and 32-frame inputs).

## Results

On PAI-Bench-G (1,044 video-prompt pairs), the best open-source VGM Wan2.2-I2V-A14B achieves an Overall score of 82.3 (Domain 92.7, Quality 77.5), essentially matching the proprietary Veo3 (82.2; Domain 89.7, Quality 77.6), while source videos set the ceiling at 83.9 (Domain 89.8, Quality 78.0). On PAI-Bench-U (1,214 QA pairs), the best open-source MLLM Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B reaches 64.7% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-5 (61.8%) and GPT-4o (56.2%), yet all models remain far below the human baseline of 93.2%. On PAI-Bench-C (600 videos), multi-signal conditioning (All) yields the highest Quality Score (9.24) for both Cosmos-Transfer models, outperforming any single control signal by 2–3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In PAI-Bench-G, the top open-source model Wan2.2-I2V-A14B (Overall 82.3) marginally edges out the proprietary Veo3 (82.2), but both trail source videos (83.9) by ~1.6 points on Domain Score, revealing that no current VGM fully captures physical plausibility. Among open-source models, the Wan2.2 family dominates, with Wan2.2-I2V-A14B leading in Domain Score (92.7) while HunyuanVideo-I2V matches it in Quality Score (78.0). In PAI-Bench-U, the open-source Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B (64.7) outperforms all proprietary MLLMs including GPT-5 (61.8) and GPT-4o (56.2), with a 2.9-point gap over GPT-5; however, all models remain 28+ points below human performance (93.2). In PAI-Bench-C, Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B with the All-signal condition achieves the best combined fidelity (Blur SSIM 0.90, Edge F1 0.45, Depth si-RMSE 0.59, Mask mIoU 0.77) and Quality Score (9.24), while Wan2.2-Fun-A14B-Control shows the weakest depth fidelity (si-RMSE 2.10) and segmentation fidelity (mIoU 0.66). The key finding across all three tracks is that proprietary models do not uniformly dominate open-source ones, suggesting Physical AI has not been a primary optimization target for either group.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAI-Bench carves new ground by unifying three previously fragmented evaluation tracks—video generation (building on VBench/EvalCrafter for quality metrics), conditional video generation (a first for systematic controllability evaluation), and video understanding (extending EgoSchema/VideoMME with physically grounded reasoning)—under a single Physical AI domain framing. Where prior benchmarks assess either visual fidelity or physical plausibility in isolation, PAI-Bench integrates both via a dual Quality Score + Domain Score design, and where prior video understanding benchmarks target general comprehension, PAI-Bench-U introduces an ontology of Space, Time, and Physical World reasoning plus embodied action prediction. The conditional generation track repurposes standard fidelity metrics (SSIM, F1, si-RMSE, mIoU) but applies them to a novel evaluation question: whether VGMs can faithfully follow multimodal control signals in physically meaningful scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical and quantified gap: SOTA VGMs achieve near-source-video visual quality (77–78 Quality Score) yet fall 4–15 points below real videos on physical plausibility, and the best MLLM (Qwen3-VL-235B, 64.7%) trails human performance (93.2%) by nearly 30 points—suggesting Physical AI readiness is far from achieved. The finding that proprietary models do not uniformly dominate open-source ones (Qwen3-VL-235B > GPT-5) implies the domain has not been a data or optimization priority for any major lab. Open questions include whether the gap is fundamentally data-driven (insufficient Physical AI training data) or capability-driven (models failing to learn physical principles), and whether integrated visual thinking (as in GPT-5) rather than textual chain-of-thought is the key missing ingredient for embodied reasoning.
