## Problem

Streaming video vision-language models face two fundamental constraints—causality (no access to future frames) and accumulation (unbounded token growth over time)—that make both prefill computation and kv-cache memory grow without bound. Existing training-free streaming methods only regulate post-LLM kv-cache, leaving the costly pre-LLM prefill computation unchanged, while offline compression methods violate causality by leveraging future frames. No prior training-free streaming method performs strictly causal pre-LLM token reduction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming video frames sampled at 0.5 fps (clips under 30 min) or 0.2 fps (longer sequences), each encoded into N visual tokens by a vision encoder, along with a text query arriving after its end timestamp. CTR reduces each frame to exactly G=50 tokens before the LLM, and OQM stores these as 4-bit quantized groups with a total retrieval budget of 12k tokens (aligned to 64 frames × 196 tokens). The output is a text answer generated by the LLM decoder attending to at most k dequantized groups, with the full history preserved in 4-bit storage.

## Method

StreamingTOM is a training-free, two-stage framework unified by a fixed per-frame group of G tokens. Causal Temporal Reduction (CTR) operates on a strictly causal 2-frame window: it computes per-token cosine similarity between consecutive frames and attention-based saliency scores, partitions tokens into static/dynamic sets by thresholding similarity, then allocates the G-token budget proportionally—selecting top-k_d dynamic tokens by saliency and merging k_s static tokens via density-based clustering. Online Quantized Memory (OQM) stores each G-token group in 4-bit format with per-head, per-channel scale/offset and a representative key (mean of pre-quantization keys); at query time, it retrieves the top-k groups by cosine similarity between the decoder state and stored keys, dequantizing only those groups to form the active kv-cache. This design reduces prefill from O(TNLd²) to O(TGLd²) and bounds active memory to O(kGd) regardless of stream length, achieving a combined 4N/G compression ratio.

## Results

On offline long-video benchmarks (VideoMME, MLVU, EgoSchema), StreamingTOM achieves a 63.8% average, the best among all streaming systems (training-free or training-based), surpassing StreamMem (63.1%) and training-based Dispider-7B (57.9%). On the streaming RVS benchmarks under a 28 GB GPU memory constraint without CPU offloading, StreamingTOM attains 55.8% accuracy and 3.7 response quality score, outperforming StreamMem (55.2/3.6) and InfiniPot-V (54.6/3.5). In efficiency, StreamingTOM achieves a 15.7× kv-cache compression ratio, 1.2× lower peak memory, and 2× faster TTFT compared to the prior training-free SOTA LiveVLM at 256 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamingTOM outperforms all training-free streaming baselines on the offline average: it beats StreamMem (63.1→63.8), LiveVLM (60.9→63.8), and the unmodified LLaVA-OV-7B backbone (61.0→63.8), while also surpassing the training-based Dispider-7B (57.9) by 5.9 points. On the streaming RVS benchmarks (28 GB, no CPU offload), StreamingTOM (55.8 Acc / 3.7 Score) edges out StreamMem (55.2/3.6) and InfiniPot-V (54.6/3.5), and matches or exceeds Flash-VStream (55.0/3.6); ReKV with CPU offloading reports 59.0/3.8 but is excluded from the fair comparison due to its reliance on impractical CPU-GPU transfers. Against offline compression methods (HoliTom, VisionZip, DyCoke) evaluated on fixed 32-frame clips, StreamingTOM's causal 0.5/0.2 fps sampling covers longer temporal spans, yielding higher VideoMME-Overall (59.9 vs. 58.9/58.2/54.3) and EgoSchema (63.7 vs. 61.2/60.3/59.5) scores. In efficiency, StreamingTOM achieves 1.2× lower peak memory and 2× faster TTFT than LiveVLM at 256 frames, with memory remaining nearly constant (16.0–16.7 GB) from 16 to 512 frames due to CTR's fixed per-frame budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamingTOM carves new ground at the intersection of two established but previously disconnected lines of work: offline video token compression (HoliTom, DyCoke, VisionZip), which relies on global temporal context and future frames, and training-free streaming kv-cache management (LiveVLM, StreamMem, ReKV, InfiniPot-V), which only regulates post-LLM memory while leaving the full pre-LLM prefill cost intact. The paper's key departure is introducing strictly causal pre-LLM token reduction via a 2-frame window (Causal Temporal Reduction) and unifying it with 4-bit quantized group storage (Online Quantized Memory) through a fixed per-frame budget G, creating a single frame-aligned abstraction that coordinates both stages. No prior training-free streaming method had performed pre-LLM compression under strict causality, which the authors identify as the critical missing piece for true streaming efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing one-hour stream kv-cache from 18.8 GB to 1.2 GB makes long-horizon streaming video understanding feasible on a single consumer GPU, and the training-free, plug-and-play design means the technique can be dropped into any existing video LLM backbone without retraining. The conceptual contribution—that pre-LLM compression is not merely an optimization but a prerequisite for streaming viability, since post-LLM methods cannot recover already-incurred prefill cost—reframes how the field should approach streaming efficiency. Open questions include whether the fixed budget G=50 and 4-bit quantization generalize across backbones and resolutions, how the method behaves at higher frame rates or with more complex multi-turn interactions, and whether the 2-frame causal window is truly sufficient or whether slightly longer look-back windows could improve accuracy without sacrificing causality.
