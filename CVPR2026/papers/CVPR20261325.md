## Problem

Deepfake detection models increasingly produce natural-language explanations, but these rationales are frequently ungrounded in visual evidence, misattributing artifacts to lighting or color rather than geometry or texture. Existing evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) measure linguistic overlap rather than factual grounding, and no scalable framework exists to assess whether a detector's reasoning is actually faithful to the image. This paper tackles the dual gap of (1) lacking an out-of-distribution benchmark for modern generative forgeries and (2) lacking a reliable, image-grounded metric for reasoning quality.

## Input / Output

For detection, the model receives an RGB image and outputs a real/fake/edited classification. For reasoning evaluation, the judge receives the RGB image, the ground-truth authenticity label, and one or two candidate reasoning responses, and outputs either a 1–5 integer rating wrapped in <score> tags with a justification in <reasoning> tags (pointwise), or a binary A/B preference in <answer> tags (pairwise). Critically, the judge consumes the image directly alongside the text, enabling it to verify whether claimed artifacts are actually visible rather than relying on textual priors.

## Method

The core contribution is a bootstrapped generator–evaluator pipeline that scales sparse human annotations into a large graded reasoning corpus. Starting from 1,025 in-distribution samples with human-annotated bounding boxes and artifact flags (processed by GPT-4o-mini into gold rationales), a generator produces reasoning responses at each of five quality levels; an evaluator scores each response and provides feedback, and mismatches are iteratively refined until the predicted rating matches the target within a threshold α. All accepted responses are then paraphrased five times to prevent the judge from memorizing surface form. The resulting DeepfakeJudge-Meta dataset (20,625 pointwise / 41,250 pairwise tuples) is used to fine-tune Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and 3B via standard negative log-likelihood for two epochs. The trained judge achieves 96.2% pairwise accuracy and 0.96 Spearman correlation with human ratings, outperforming models 30× its size, while the OOD benchmark (DeepfakeJudge-Detect/Reason) is built from 1,000 real Open-Images samples plus 1,000 fakes generated by Gemini, SeedDream, Flux-Kontext, and Qwen-Edit to stress-test generalization.

## Results

On the DeepfakeJudge-Meta pairwise benchmark, DeepfakeJudge-7B achieves 96.2% pairwise accuracy, outperforming the 30× larger Qwen-3-VL-235B-Instruct (93.2%) and all other baselines. On the human-annotated DeepfakeJudge-Meta-Human subset, it reaches 98.9% pairwise agreement. For pointwise evaluation, DeepfakeJudge-7B attains RMSE of 0.50 and Pearson correlation of 0.95 on DeepfakeJudge-Meta-Human, substantially surpassing the next-best model (Qwen-3-VL-235B-Thinking: RMSE 0.95, p=0.86). In the user study, participants preferred DeepfakeJudge-Reason explanations 70% of the time over SIDA, GPT-4o-mini, and Qwen-3-VL-235B rationales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepfakeJudge-7B (7B parameters) outperforms all baselines on the primary DeepfakeJudge-Meta pairwise metric (96.2%), beating the 30× larger Qwen-3-VL-235B-Instruct (93.2%) by 3.0 points and Gemini-2.5-Flash (91.7%) by 4.5 points. On the human-annotated DFJMH subset, Qwen-3-VL-235B-Instruct narrowly edges ahead at 99.4% vs. 98.9%, but DeepfakeJudge-7B still surpasses all other models including Gemini-2.5-Flash (94.2%) and GPT-4o-Mini (89.8%). For pointwise evaluation, DeepfakeJudge-7B dominates across all metrics: RMSE 0.50 vs. 0.95 (Qwen-235B-Thinking) and 1.11 (Gemini) on DFJMH, with Pearson correlation 0.95 vs. 0.86 and 0.83 respectively. Specialized deepfake detectors SIDA (48.1% overall detection accuracy) and Gen-Buster++ (49.9%) fail to generalize to OOD forgeries and score lowest on reasoning quality (DFJ-3B scores of 2.32 and 2.33 vs. 3.59 for Qwen-235B). Reasoning-augmented Thinking variants (Qwen-3-VL-Thinking) show inconsistent gains—improving Fake F1 in detection but often degrading overall accuracy and pairwise judgment compared to their Instruct counterparts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm (established for text via MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena and for video via VideoJudge) to the specific domain of deepfake detection reasoning quality. It departs from prior explainable deepfake detectors (SIDA, Gen-Buster++, FakeShield) that produce textual rationales but lack a principled way to assess whether those rationales are visually grounded, and from traditional NLP metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) that measure lexical overlap rather than factual grounding. The bootstrapped generator-evaluator pipeline draws on self-consistency and self-improvement literature for LLMs, adapted here to scale sparse human annotations into structured multi-level reasoning supervision data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing reasoning fidelity as a quantifiable, measurable dimension of deepfake detection, this work provides the field with a scalable evaluation tool that does not require explicit ground-truth rationales for every new sample, which is critical as generative models evolve rapidly. The OOD benchmark incorporating recent T2I and editing models (Gemini, SeedDream, Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Edit) addresses a real gap, since prior benchmarks (FaceForensics++, DFDC) are increasingly saturated. Open questions include whether the bootstrapped judge generalizes to video deepfakes, whether performance holds as new VLM architectures emerge, and whether the small human-annotated subset (100 samples for meta-evaluation) provides sufficient statistical power for the reported correlations.
