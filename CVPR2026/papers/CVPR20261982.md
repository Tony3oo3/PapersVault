## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods treat each Gaussian as an independent primitive, enforcing only local consistency while ignoring inter-Gaussian relational dependencies. This independence causes geometric biases to accumulate in unobserved regions, leading to severe distortions and appearance degradation when extrapolating to large-deviation viewpoints such as cross-lane offsets or ground-to-aerial transitions in urban driving scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes sparse RGB images captured along a single driving trajectory, their camera poses, and an MVS-reconstructed point cloud with surface normals as initialization. It produces rendered novel view images, normal maps, and semantic maps at arbitrary test viewpoints, evaluated under lateral shifts of 3–5 m, vertical shifts of 1–2 m, and combined diagonal shifts. The 3D Gaussian scene representation uses approximately 353k Gaussians—fewer than all baselines—while achieving the best rendering quality.

## Method

CoRoGS reformulates 3DGS as a contextual representation where each Gaussian's attributes are updated as a function of its own parameters and aggregated neighborhood context. A 3D Gaussian graph is constructed via Delaunay triangulation on the MVS point cloud, with edges encoding geometric (normal cosine similarity, Euclidean distance) and semantic (PointNet++-derived) attributes. A three-stage GNN—Fourier-encoded attribute embedding, attention-based geometric/semantic message passing, and cross-modal fusion with adaptive gating—performs contextual reasoning to refine node features, which are then decoded into per-node Gaussian parameters (scale, rotation, opacity, color) via a context-aware decoder. A contextual smoothness loss, analogous to an MRF energy, enforces normal consistency within semantically homogeneous regions while relaxing constraints at boundaries. A gradient-driven graph expansion module adaptively splits boundary Gaussians with persistently high optimization gradients and prunes candidates violating Delaunay distance bounds, filling structural gaps without over-expansion.

## Results

On the supervised small-deviation setting (KITTI, 0.5 m lateral shift), CoRoGS achieves PSNR 24.96 dB, SSIM 0.849, LPIPS 0.180, and Chamfer Distance 1.32 m, outperforming the second-best DeSiRe-GS by 2.25 dB in PSNR while using fewer Gaussians (353 k vs. 482 k). Under the unsupervised large-deviation setting (averaged over KITTI and Waymo), CoRoGS attains the best FID and KID across all six deviation configurations (Left-3 m/5 m, Up-1 m/2 m, Diagonal-3 m/5 m), with FID improvements of 32.7 %, 28.4 %, and 21.0 % over DC-Gaussian on the most challenging Left-5 m, Up-2 m, and Diagonal-5 m shifts respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoRoGS outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both the supervised small-deviation and unsupervised large-deviation evaluations. In the supervised setting (Table 1), the closest competitor is DeSiRe-GS (PSNR 22.71, SSIM 0.765), which CoRoGS surpasses by 2.25 dB in PSNR and 0.084 in SSIM while also achieving the lowest Chamfer Distance (1.32 m vs. 1.88 m) with fewer Gaussians. In the large-deviation setting (Table 2), the gap widens substantially: on the most challenging Diagonal-5 m shift, CoRoGS achieves FID 0.1022 versus 0.1100 for GSDF (the best baseline), and on Left-5 m it reaches FID 0.0378 versus 0.0911 for DC-Gaussian—a 58 % relative reduction. VEGS, which relies on diffusion-based generative priors, is consistently outperformed, confirming that explicit contextual graph reasoning is more effective than generative extrapolation for large view shifts. SAGS, the only other graph-based 3DGS method, lags behind CoRoGS on all large-deviation metrics, attributed to its heuristic growth strategy that introduces redundant primitives without modeling fine-grained inter-Gaussian dependencies.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoRoGS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting [8] but departs from its core assumption of independent Gaussian primitives by reformulating the representation as a contextual graph with inter-Gaussian message passing. It positions itself against two prior lines: geometric regularization methods (GaussianPro, DC-Gaussian, GSDF, DeSiRe-GS) that enforce local depth/normal constraints without modeling relational dependencies, and generative-prior methods (VEGS) that rely on diffusion models for view extrapolation. The graph-based formulation also distinguishes from SAGS [30], which uses a heuristic growth strategy at the Gaussian level, by constructing a Delaunay-triangulated graph with explicit geometric and semantic edge attributes and a learned GNN for contextual updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap in autonomous driving and aerial perception where camera trajectories are sparse and novel views deviate substantially (cross-lane, ground-to-elevated) from training coverage. The contextual smoothness loss and progressive graph expansion offer a principled mechanism for maintaining geometric coherence in unobserved regions, which prior independent-Gaussian formulations cannot achieve. However, the method is limited to static scenes and struggles with dynamic objects and large rotations, as the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether the graph-based contextual reasoning scales to truly large-scale city reconstructions and whether the Delaunay-based initialization is the optimal topology for arbitrary scene geometries.
