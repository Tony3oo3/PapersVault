## Problem

Vision-language models for animal behavior understanding are severely constrained by data scarcity: annotated behavior videos require domain expertise, controlled observation, and are prohibitively expensive to collect at the scale needed for large-scale pretraining. Existing methods either rely on generic pretraining data with a large domain gap or remain dataset-specific without leveraging structured domain knowledge, limiting cross-species generalization and fine-grained behavioral discrimination.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261173/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes video clips (durations ranging from 0.03 s to 10.2 min, averaging 23.30 s) and behavior label texts as input. The text input is not a single label string but a structured representation: the original NBO label, its synonyms, and its definition are each encoded by a pretrained CLIP text encoder and combined via a weighted sum (with the original label receiving the highest weight). The output is a pair of d-dimensional embeddings in a shared vision-language space, from which multi-label behavior predictions are obtained via a lightweight MLP head during transfer learning.

## Method

EthoCLIP is a CLIP-style contrastive learning framework with two key ontology-driven components in the text branch. First, Ontology Semantic Embedding (OSE) computes a weighted combination of CLIP-encoded features for the label, its synonyms, and its definition (α > β, γ with α+β+γ=1), preserving core semantics while allowing moderate expansion; definitions exceeding 77 tokens are chunked and pooled via element-wise max. Second, Ontology-Aware Graph Modeling (OAGM) represents the 160-behavior NBO subset as a graph and applies a hierarchy-aware graph attention mechanism where attention scores are modulated by a sigmoid of the hierarchical level difference (g_ij = σ(−λΔl_ij)), so higher-level (broader) nodes exert stronger influence on lower-level (specific) nodes and vice versa is attenuated; a stacked multi-hop design (optimal at 2 hops) captures long-range hierarchical dependencies. The visual branch uses the X-CLIP encoder with cross-frame communication and multi-frame integration transformers. Training maximizes cosine similarity between matched video-text pairs, and the entire framework is pretrained on AnimalBand (74,671 videos from 5 datasets unified under NBO via GPT-5-based label mapping and expert verification).

## Results

On the transfer learning benchmark (Table 2), EthoCLIP pretrained on AnimalBand with a frozen backbone achieves a mean score of 78.11 across Panda (Acc@1 84.80), SheepActivity (Acc@1 85.71), and ChimpACT (mAP 63.82). With partial fine-tuning, the mean rises to 87.72 (Panda 92.40, SheepActivity 96.43, ChimpACT 74.32), a 9.24% relative improvement over ActionCLIP. In the in-domain AnimalBand classification (Table 3), EthoCLIP achieves 62.98 overall mAP versus 61.20 for the X-CLIP baseline, with a 5.78% relative gain on tail categories. Pretraining on ontology-standardized AnimalBand yields a 7.56% mean improvement over directly merging heterogeneous labels (74.26 vs. 69.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EthoCLIP with a frozen backbone (mean 78.11) trails ActionCLIP (80.30) and UniFormerV2 (79.97) by roughly 2%, but surpasses SlowFast (72.42) and X3D (73.08) by 5–6 points. With partial fine-tuning, EthoCLIP (87.72) outperforms ActionCLIP by 9.24% relative and UniFormerV2 by 9.7% relative, establishing clear superiority among traditional video backbones. All general-purpose vision-language models (VideoLLaMA3-2B, InternVL3.5-2B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) perform dramatically worse, with mean scores between 31.85 and 41.15, confirming a significant domain gap between general VLMs and animal behavior understanding. The ontology-standardized AnimalBand pretraining (X-CLIP-AnimalBand, 74.26) improves over direct label merging (X-CLIP-merge, 69.04) by 7.56% on average, isolating the contribution of semantic unification from the architectural changes in EthoCLIP. On the in-domain AnimalBand test set, EthoCLIP gains 2.91% overall mAP over the X-CLIP baseline, with the largest improvement (5.78%) on tail categories, indicating that the ontology-aware graph module particularly benefits rare behaviors.

## Relation to Prior Work

EthoCLIP builds directly on the X-CLIP video encoder (Ni et al., ECCV 2022) and the CLIP contrastive learning paradigm (Radford et al., ICML 2021), extending them with an ontology-aware graph attention module derived from standard GAT (Veličković et al., 2017) but modified with hierarchy-dependent message-passing weights. The dataset construction strategy—unifying heterogeneous labels into a shared semantic space via LLM-based mapping—follows the Pangea approach (Li et al., CVPR 2024) for human action understanding, but applies it to the animal behavior domain using the Neuro Behavior Ontology as the unifying structure. The key conceptual departure from prior animal behavior recognition work (which relies on dataset-specific labels and purely data-driven learning) is the injection of structured expert knowledge as an inductive bias to compensate for limited training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in animal behavior AI: the extreme scarcity of annotated video data compared to general-purpose vision-language pretraining corpora. By demonstrating that ontology-driven semantic enrichment can match or exceed performance with only 40–60% of the pretraining data, it offers a practical pathway for domain-specific model development where large-scale annotation is infeasible. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the NBO's coverage (160 curated behaviors), and its generalizability to other data-scarce domains with comparable ontological structure remains untested. Open questions include whether the hierarchy-aware graph module provides benefits beyond what simpler label-hierarchy priors could achieve, and whether the LLM-based label mapping introduces systematic biases that affect downstream fairness across species.
