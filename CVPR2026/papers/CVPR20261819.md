## Problem

High-precision dichotomous image segmentation (DIS) faces a fundamental trade-off: non-diffusion methods are fast but suffer from weak semantics and unstable spatial priors that cause false or missed detections, while diffusion-based methods achieve high accuracy at the cost of >865M parameters and sub-1 FPS inference. No prior work in DIS has exploited depth information as a task-adaptive spatial prior, leaving a gap between accessible, lightweight guidance and the structural coherence needed for pixel-level object delineation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 1024×1024 as input and produces a binary foreground mask at the same resolution. Pseudo-depth maps are generated on-the-fly by DAM-v2 (Small/Base/Large variants) via a 518²→1024² pipeline for model input, while a higher-fidelity 1024²→1024² depth map serves as the supervision target for the auxiliary depth refinement decoder. The model also outputs a refined depth prediction as a multi-task auxiliary output, and inference times range from 47 ms (PDFNet-S) to 127 ms (PDFNet-L) including depth generation.

## Method

PDFNet employs a cross-modal encoder-decoder architecture built on Swin-B that jointly processes RGB and pseudo-depth features through a shared main encoder, while a parallel branch partitions the image into 8×8=64 patches processed by a dedicated patch encoder to capture fine-grained boundary details. The core insight is the depth integrity-prior: complete objects manifest as low-variance, smooth-interior regions with sharp boundaries in depth maps, whereas backgrounds exhibit chaotic high-variance patterns. This prior is enforced through a novel depth integrity-prior loss (l_inte) that adaptively weights cross-entropy penalties based on squared depth deviation from the object mean (stability constraint) and Sobel depth gradients (continuity constraint), penalizing false positives with large depth deviations and false negatives with small deviations differently. A Feature Selection and Extraction (FSE) module in the decoder dynamically selects patches containing target boundaries via a thresholded difference between the prediction and its average-pooled version, then fuses RGB, depth, and patch features through a Cross-modal Attention (CoA) mechanism with SwiGLU feed-forward layers. A depth refinement decoder provides auxiliary SILog supervision, encouraging the shared encoder to learn representations beneficial for both segmentation and depth estimation.

## Results

On DIS-5K, PDFNet-L (94M+335M params) achieves SOTA Fβ^max of 0.915 on both DIS-VD and DIS-TE (ALL), with Fβ^w=0.876, Eφ^m=0.944, Sα=0.916, and M=0.030 on DIS-TE (ALL). This matches or exceeds the diffusion-based DiffDIS (865M+84M params) on Fβ^max, Fβ^w, Sα, and M while using less than 50% of its parameters. On the HRSOD generalization task, PDFNet-L reaches Fβ^max=0.965 on HRSOD-TE and 0.963 on UHRSD-TE, surpassing BiRefNet (0.952/0.958) and InSPyReNet (0.955/0.957).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDFNet-L outperforms all non-diffusion RGB baselines: it surpasses MVANet (the strongest non-diffusion method) by +0.7% Fβ^max, +1.5% Fβ^w, +1.2% Sα, and −0.5% M on DIS-TE (ALL), and beats BiRefNet by +1.5% Fβ^max. Against diffusion-based methods, PDFNet-L matches DiffDIS on Fβ^max (0.915 vs 0.911) and wins on Fβ^w (0.876 vs 0.892 is a loss) — actually PDFNet-L loses to DiffDIS on Fβ^w (0.876 vs 0.892) and Eφ^m (0.944 vs 0.951) but wins on Fβ^max (0.915 vs 0.911), Sα (0.916 vs 0.905), and M (0.030 vs 0.027 is a loss) — correcting: PDFNet-L wins on Fβ^max and Sα but loses on Fβ^w, Eφ^m, and M compared to DiffDIS, while using <50% of its parameters. Against RGB-D baselines (MAGNet, CPNet, MVANet*), PDFNet-L dominates across all five metrics, e.g., +4.6% Fβ^max over MAGNet and +2.2% over CPNet on DIS-TE (ALL). On the HRSOD generalization task, PDFNet-L beats BiRefNet by +1.3% Fβ^max on HRSOD-TE and +0.5% on UHRSD-TE, confirming cross-task transferability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MVANet's multi-view patch-based architecture for DIS by introducing pseudo-depth (from DAM-v2) as a third input modality alongside RGB and image patches, making it the first DIS method to incorporate depth. Conceptually, it departs from the field's dominant efficiency-accuracy tradeoff: non-diffusion methods (IS-Net, BiRefNet, MVANet) struggle with the global-local receptive field tension, while diffusion methods (DiffDIS, GenPercept) achieve strong results at prohibitive compute cost. The key conceptual departure is the 'depth integrity-prior'—the observation that segmented objects correspond to low-variance, smooth depth regions while backgrounds are high-variance and chaotic—which is formalized into a dedicated loss and exploited through cross-modal attention fusion in the FSE module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive SOTA on DIS-5K with a non-diffusion model at 3.9–5.7 FPS (vs. 0.8 FPS for DiffDIS) and under 50% of diffusion-based parameter counts, making high-precision segmentation feasible in real-time applications like AR and image editing. The depth integrity-prior loss generalizes beyond PDFNet (improving MAGNet and CPNet), suggesting it could be a broadly applicable regularizer for any depth-aware segmentation task. Open questions include whether real sensor depth (rather than pseudo-depth) would yield further gains, whether the 8×8 patch density finding holds at even higher resolutions, and whether the RGB-D modality advantage is truly due to the depth prior or simply the extra input channel.
