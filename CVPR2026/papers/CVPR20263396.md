## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models fail to faithfully render all concepts in complex prompts containing multiple subjects with distinct attributes and hierarchical relationships, suffering from concept omission, attribute confusion between subjects, and degraded visual quality. The root cause is the large semantic gap between long structured text and the image domain, which monolithic single-step generation cannot bridge. No prior framework systematically decomposes compositional generation into a progressive, part-by-part construction process guided by reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a complex hierarchical text prompt (4–12 subjects, each with multiple structured attributes and inter-subject relationships, averaging ~219 words as in HiCoPrompt) and produces a single RGB image. Internally, the prompt is first parsed by Qwen2.5-VL-3B into an ordered set of subject-centric sub-prompts; each sub-prompt is then fed to a FLUX-based DiT with UNO LoRA weights, where previously generated intermediate images are concatenated into the multi-modal attention input as visual context tokens rather than being re-described in text. The final output is a single assembled image covering all prompt concepts.

## Method

HiCoGen introduces a Chain of Synthesis (CoS) paradigm that replaces monolithic generation with iterative part-by-part construction: an LLM decomposes the prompt into ordered subject sub-prompts, and each is synthesized sequentially with the prior image injected as a visual context token in the DiT's concatenated input, progressively replacing entangled text with concrete visual information. To optimize this pipeline, the paper applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning guided by a three-level hierarchical reward (global CLIP+HPSv2 alignment, subject-level DINOv2 similarity + VLM attribute verification, and relationship-level VLM interaction/proportion checks). A key theoretical contribution proves that sample diversity under a fixed stochasticity budget is maximized by concentrating noise in early denoising steps (when the system is least contractive), yielding a cosine-decay Decaying Stochasticity Schedule that augments the Rectified Flow ODE and is essential for effective RL exploration. The full system is instantiated on FLUX with UNO LoRA for subject-driven in-context generation, fine-tuned with AdamW at lr 1e-4 on 8×A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the HiCoPrompt benchmark (3,000 hierarchical compositional prompts), HiCoGen achieves 0.7127 on subject existence accuracy, 0.7673 on attribute accuracy, and 0.8203 on relationship accuracy, outperforming all 10 baselines on every metric. It also leads on CLIP score (0.3192) and HPSv2 (0.3357), surpassing the strongest baseline Qwen-Image by +9% on existence accuracy and +0.044 on HPSv2. The gap widens substantially for subject-driven models: HiCoGen improves relationship accuracy by 45% over UNO (0.8203 vs 0.3697) and by 120% over MS-Diffusion (0.8203 vs 0.1960).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiCoGen wins on all five metrics against every baseline. Against the strongest end-to-end T2I model, Qwen-Image, HiCoGen gains +9% on subject existence (0.7127 vs 0.6292), +7.7% on attribute accuracy (0.7673 vs 0.6907), and +3.7% on relationship accuracy (0.8203 vs 0.7829), while also improving CLIP score by 0.05 and HPSv2 by 0.044. Against FLUX.1-Krea, the margins are larger: +19.6% existence, +16.8% attribute, and +18% relationship accuracy. Among subject-driven models, HiCoGen's advantage is most pronounced on relationship accuracy, where it scores 0.8203 versus UNO's 0.3697 (+45%) and MS-Diffusion's 0.1960 (+120%), indicating that the Chain of Synthesis pipeline and hierarchical reward specifically address the compositional binding failures that plague single-subject injection approaches. OminiGen is the closest subject-driven competitor on attribute accuracy (0.6791 vs 0.7673), but still trails by 8.8% and by 14% on relationship accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiCoGen draws a direct conceptual parallel between LLM reasoning paradigms (Chain-of-Thought, GRPO-based inference from DeepSeek-R1) and visual synthesis, proposing Chain of Synthesis as the visual analogue of step-by-step reasoning. Technically, it builds on Rectified Flow (SD3) as the base diffusion framework and adapts GRPO for diffusion model optimization, while leveraging subject-driven in-context generation models (UNO, OminiGen) for the iterative composition step. It departs from prior diffusion RL work (DDPO, DPOK, Flow-GRPO) by identifying and theoretically addressing the limited exploration problem in standard samplers, and from compositional T2I benchmarks (T2I-CompBench, DPG-Bench) by introducing explicit hierarchical multi-subject structure with per-attribute descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent and practically important failure mode of T2I models—concept omission and confusion in complex prompts—through a modular pipeline that is interpretable and debuggable at each synthesis step. The HiCoPrompt benchmark fills a genuine gap by providing systematically structured hierarchical prompts absent from prior T2I evaluation suites. Open questions include whether the multi-step LLM-parsing-plus-iterative-generation pipeline scales to very long prompts (beyond 12 subjects), whether the decaying stochasticity schedule generalizes to other diffusion architectures beyond Rectified Flow, and whether reliance on GPT-4o as an evaluation judge introduces circularity. The approach also adds significant inference latency compared to single-pass generation, a trade-off the paper does not deeply quantify.
