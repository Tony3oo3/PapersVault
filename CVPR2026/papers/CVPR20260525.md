## Problem

Existing approaches to knowledge-graph reasoning with LLMs linearize graph structures into text sequences, which loses higher-order relational and spatial cues critical for multi-hop inference. Meanwhile, no systematic benchmark exists to evaluate whether Large Multimodal Models can genuinely perceive and reason over visualized knowledge graphs, as prior multimodal benchmarks focus on natural-scene perception or unstructured visual reasoning rather than structured relational understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260525/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a rendered visual knowledge graph image (colored nodes, directed edges, spatially laid out from subgraphs of ReVerb45K, DBpedia500, or MetaQA with controlled hop depth and edge-to-node ratio) with a natural-language question. The model produces a free-form text answer, scored by exact-match accuracy in a zero-shot setting. The benchmark comprises 3,205 questions over 455 VKG images spanning 14 subtasks organized into three progressive categories: General Image Comprehension, Graph-specific Comprehension, and Graph-based Reasoning.

## Method

VKG-QA is constructed via a three-stage semi-automatic pipeline: (1) subgraph extraction using controlled random neighbor sampling (k=1,2,3 hops) with linear edge constraints |E|=w|V| to balance visual complexity, followed by interactive rendering with automated layout and manual refinement for spatial clarity; (2) hybrid question generation combining automatic attribute matching, manually designed templates, and dataset-aligned mapping (e.g., MetaQA for hop reasoning), ensuring semantic alignment between questions, answers, and the corresponding VKG image; (3) rigorous human verification for semantic consistency, visual clarity, and logical coherence. The evaluation paradigm is progressive, decoupling basic perceptual tasks (color, counting, OCR) from graph-topological comprehension (degree, direction, cycles, connectivity) and structured relational reasoning (single-hop, multi-hop, superlative, conditional). The paper evaluates 19 LMMs (closed- and open-source) under identical zero-shot prompts, and additionally compares LMM performance on visual KGs against LLM performance on textualized KGs to isolate the contribution of visual-spatial encoding to structured reasoning.

## Results

On VKG-QA (3,205 questions, 455 images, 14 subtasks), GPT-5 achieves the highest average accuracy of 85.6%, followed by Gemini-2.5-pro (84.0%) and Gemini-2.5-flash (79.0%). All open-source models fall below 65% average, with the best being Qwen2.5-VL-72B-instruct at 63.1% and the worst (Gemma-3-12B-it) at 42.3%. Graph-specific comprehension subtasks (degree, direction, connectivity) are the most challenging even for top models, while 1-hop reasoning is the easiest (GPT-5: 100%). In the LMM-vs-LLM comparison (Table 2), GPT-5 as an LMM (88.8% avg) substantially outperforms GPT-5-chat as an LLM (77.1% avg) on graph-specific tasks, with a 38.0-point gap on connectivity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Closed-source LMMs dominate across all 14 subtasks: GPT-5 (85.6%) and Gemini-2.5-pro (84.0%) lead, while the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, 63.1%) trails by over 22 points. The gap widens on graph-specific comprehension tasks—e.g., on connectivity, GPT-5 scores 94.4% versus 53.4% for Qwen2.5-VL-72B and 18.8% for Qwen2.5-omni-7B. In the LMM-vs-LLM comparison (Table 2), GPT-5 as a multimodal model (88.8% avg) beats GPT-5-chat as a text-only LLM (77.1% avg) by 11.7 points, with the largest margin on connectivity (94.6% vs. 56.6%, a 38-point gap), confirming that visual graph encoding aids structural perception. However, LLMs retain a slight edge on multi-hop (91.7% vs. 85.0%) and superlative reasoning (94.0% vs. 86.0%), suggesting text-based symbolic inference still has advantages for longer logical chains. Within the Qwen2.5-VL family, scaling from 7B to 72B yields consistent gains (51.4% → 63.1% avg), with the largest improvements in graph-specific comprehension and reasoning rather than basic image perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of knowledge graph QA and multimodal evaluation. Rather than following the dominant paradigm of linearizing KGs into text sequences for LLMs (as in KG-GPT, ARK-V1, and related work), it repurposes the Deepseek-OCR-inspired idea of visualized knowledge representation, rendering subgraphs from ReVerb45K, DBpedia500, and MetaQA as images for LMMs to perceive directly. It also extends the LMM benchmarking landscape (MMMU, MathVista, GAIA) by introducing a structured-relational-reasoning axis that prior benchmarks treat as absent, positioning VKG-QA as the first systematic evaluation of graph-topology comprehension in the visual modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and actionable bottleneck: 83% of GPT-4o errors are perceptual (edge direction, spatial orientation, node grounding) rather than reasoning failures, suggesting that improving visual-structural perception—not logical inference—is the key lever for graph-aware multimodal systems. The 38-percentage-point connectivity gap between visual and textual input for GPT-5 quantifies how much spatial topology is lost in linearization, motivating continued investment in visualized knowledge interfaces. Open questions include whether dedicated graph-structure pretraining or architectural inductive biases (e.g., spatial attention over node neighborhoods) can close the perceptual gap, and whether the closed-source advantage will narrow as open models scale.
