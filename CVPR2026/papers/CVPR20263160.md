## Problem

Existing video LLMs operate offline, requiring the entire video before producing output, making them unsuitable for real-time streaming scenarios where frames arrive sequentially and responses must be timed to evolving visual events. Prior streaming approaches either bolt on a separate decision module (creating an accuracy-efficiency trade-off) or are restricted to narrow tasks like narration, leaving a gap for a unified model that handles diverse streaming tasks with precise frame-level response timing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263160/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous video stream segmented into 1-second windows sampled at 1 fps, with text instructions (questions, grounding queries, narration prompts) that can be injected at any turn. Output is structured as a per-turn prediction: the model first emits one of three state tokens (<Silence>, <Standby>, <Response>), and only upon <Response> does it generate the full textual answer, enabling one-pass inference without a separate controller. Temporal context is encoded via explicit boundary markers (e.g., <2s-3s>) prepended to each segment.

## Method

Streamo embeds frame-level response-state prediction directly into the LLM's token sequence by appending three special state tokens to the vocabulary, so the model decides when to speak as part of its normal next-token prediction rather than relying on an external decision module. The training data is reformulated from single-turn offline format into a multi-turn dialogue where each 1-second video segment constitutes one turn, with questions and answers strategically placed across turns. To counter the severe class imbalance (Silence:Standby:Response ≈ 12:3:2), the authors design a focal loss with per-batch frequency-based alpha weights applied only to the three state tokens, dynamically emphasizing hard and rare predictions. The model is trained end-to-end on Streamo-Instruct-465K, a 465K-sample multi-task instruction dataset covering real-time narration, event/action captioning, temporal grounding, and time-sensitive QA, all annotated under a unified temporal-boundary protocol. Base models (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) are fine-tuned with the vision encoder frozen and the connector plus LLM updated at lr 1e-5 for one epoch.

## Results

On OVO-Bench, Streamo-7B (1 fps) achieves 55.61 overall average, surpassing the previous online SOTA Dispider-7B (41.78) by +13.83 points and approaching the offline Qwen2-VL-72B (56.27). When evaluated at 2 fps without retraining, Streamo-7B further improves to 57.86. On offline video benchmarks (Table 3), Streamo-7B attains a 63.9 average across MVBench, TempCompass, VideoMME, and LongVideoBench, exceeding its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (60.6) by +3.3 points. On the newly introduced Streamo-Bench, Streamo-7B scores 55.3 average, nearly doubling the best prior online model StreamingVLM-7B (24.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Streamo-7B (55.61) decisively outperforms Dispider-7B (41.78) by +13.83 on OVO-Bench overall, and also surpasses Flash-VStream-7B (33.61) and VideoLLM-online-8B (20.79 real-time only) by wide margins. However, Streamo-7B trails ViSpeak-7B (61.08) by 5.47 points on OVO-Bench overall, though it matches or exceeds ViSpeak on the Forward Active Responding sub-task (54.77 vs. 54.25). On offline benchmarks, Streamo-7B (63.9 avg) beats StreamingVLM-7B on every reported metric (e.g., MVBench 72.3 vs. 69.2, VideoMME 67.9 vs. 65.1) and remains competitive with proprietary models Gemini-1.5-Pro (66.4) and GPT-4o (66.6). On Streamo-Bench, Streamo-7B (55.3) nearly doubles StreamingVLM-7B (24.6) and Dispider-7B (14.6), with particularly large gaps on forward grounding (29.4 vs. 0) and time-sensitive QA accuracy (51.6 vs. 11.8/14.0), indicating that prior online models struggle with open-ended, multi-task instruction following.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of streaming video models that rely on a separate decision module (Dispider, StreamBridge) or a single [EOS] token for response timing (VideoLLM-Online, StreamingVLM), instead embedding frame-level state prediction (<Silence>, <Standby>, <Response>) directly into the LLM's next-token prediction for end-to-end training. It repurposes standard offline video LLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3) as base architectures and converts them to streaming assistants through a multi-turn dialogue reformulation and a focal-loss-based reweighting scheme, rather than designing a new architecture from scratch. The multi-task instruction dataset (Streamo-Instruct-465K) extends prior streaming datasets like ET-Instruct-164K by unifying annotation standards across heterogeneous sources and adding tasks (event grounding, time-sensitive QA) that were previously absent from streaming training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first large-scale, multi-task instruction-tuning dataset and a dedicated benchmark for streaming video, filling a gap where prior work was limited to narrow QA or narration tasks. A notable finding is that augmenting streaming training with offline video data (LLaVA-Video) actually degrades online streaming ability, revealing a non-trivial offline/online trade-off that practitioners should account for. Key open questions remain around scalability: the current pipeline lacks long-sequence optimizations (KV-cache management, visual token pruning, sliding-window attention), making truly unbounded streaming impractical at scale. The framework's compatibility with multiple base models (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL-3) suggests it could serve as a general recipe for converting any offline video LLM to a streaming assistant.
