## Problem

Ray-traced 3D Gaussian Splatting overcomes rasterization's limitations (inaccurate shadows, no native reflection/refraction, rigid pinhole assumption) but remains slower due to the cost of sorting all intersecting Gaussians along every ray. Moreover, existing ray-tracing methods for relightable scenes still rely on rasterization-style approximations like shadow mapping, undermining the generality that ray tracing promises. Prior stochastic ray tracing of 3DGS (Sun et al.) bypasses sorting but is not differentiable, precluding its use for scene reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with calibrated camera parameters to reconstruct a 3DGS scene; for relightable scenes it additionally requires a light source configuration (point, area, or environment map). Each standard Gaussian stores a mean, covariance, density, and view-dependent spherical-harmonic color, while each relightable Gaussian stores a 16-dimensional latent feature consumed by a shared 4-layer neural decoder. Outputs are the optimized 3DGS scene plus rendered novel views and relit renderings under arbitrary lighting, all produced via hardware-accelerated OptiX ray tracing on a single GPU.

## Method

The core contribution is an unbiased, sorting-free Monte Carlo estimator for pixel-color gradients with respect to all Gaussian parameters. In the forward pass, a single Gaussian is stochastically selected per sample with probability proportional to its alpha-blending weight (Algorithm 1), eliminating the need for depth sorting. In the backward pass (Algorithm 2), each sample draws two Gaussians: g_I for the color gradient (set to 1) and g_K behind it for the opacity gradient, which reduces to the simple color difference (c_I − c_K)/α_I — avoiding any sorted evaluation of Gaussians behind g_I. For relightable 3DGS, the same stochastic estimator drives per-Gaussian shading: the neural decoder receives shadow-ray transmittance estimated by a modified stochastic traversal (unoccluded → 1, occluded → 0), replacing shadow-mapping or shadow-prediction networks with exact ray-traced visibility. Training uses a two-pass pipeline where the forward pass renders with sorting-based 3DGRT while simultaneously collecting stochastic sample indices, and the backward pass applies Algorithm 2 with those pre-computed indices to backpropagate through both Gaussian parameters and the neural decoder.

## Results

On novel view synthesis, the method achieves 28.31 PSNR on MipNeRF360 in 33 min, matching the speed of rasterization-based 3DGS (28.69 PSNR, 24 min) while being roughly 2× faster than sorting-based 3DGRT (28.40 PSNR, 69 min). Per-iteration timing is 39.8 ms total (17.9 ms backward) versus 31.4 ms for 3DGS and 87.5 ms for 3DGRT. On the relightable NRHints benchmark, the method substantially outperforms both RNG and GS³, e.g., 30.40 PSNR on Lego (vs. 26.72 and 26.62) and 33.69 PSNR on FurBall (vs. 27.82 and 26.36).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against rasterization-based 3DGS [17], the method trades a small PSNR drop (28.31 vs. 28.69 on MipNeRF360) for the ability to handle arbitrary camera models and secondary rays, while keeping training time within ~40% (33 min vs. 24 min). Against sorting-based 3DGRT [25], the method is roughly 2× faster (33 min vs. 69 min on MipNeRF360; 39.8 ms vs. 87.5 ms per iteration) with comparable or slightly lower PSNR, and the backward pass is nearly 3× faster (17.9 ms vs. 50.5 ms). On the relightable NRHints benchmark, the method clearly beats both RNG [10] and GS³ [3] on most scenes—most dramatically on Basket (28.02 vs. 19.97 PSNR for RNG) and FurBall (33.69 vs. 27.82 for RNG)—attributed to exact ray-traced shadow rays replacing shadow-mapping or learned visibility approximations. Against StochasticSplats [19] (re-implemented as SS-Tracing), the paper reports superior reconstruction quality in visual comparisons (Figure 4), attributing the gap to StochasticSplats' high-variance gradient estimator caused by near-singular opacity terms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Sun et al.'s non-differentiable stochastic ray tracing algorithm for 3DGS rendering into a fully differentiable Monte Carlo estimator for pixel-color gradients, enabling end-to-end scene reconstruction without sorting. It departs from sorting-based ray tracing (3DGRT) by eliminating the per-ray Gaussian sort entirely, and from rasterization-based 3DGS by operating in a ray-tracing pipeline that natively supports shadows, reflections, and non-pinhole cameras. For relightable scenes, it replaces the shadow-mapping and light-space splatting approximations used by RNG and GS3 with direct stochastic shadow-ray evaluation, unifying primary and secondary ray computation under a single estimator. The work also contrasts with StochasticSplats, which achieves sorting-free rasterization but whose gradient estimator the authors show suffers from high variance due to near-singular opacity terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By matching rasterization-based 3DGS speed while retaining the generality of ray tracing, this method removes the primary performance barrier that has kept ray-traced 3DGS from displacing rasterization in practice. The unified stochastic estimator for both standard and relightable scenes is practically significant: it eliminates the need for dedicated shadow-mapping passes or learned visibility networks, and the large PSNR gains on relightable benchmarks (e.g., ~8 dB on Basket) suggest that exact shadow-ray evaluation is substantially more faithful than prior approximations. The authors note that stochastic variance interacts with densification heuristics (splitting/pruning), an open question that could affect convergence behavior at scale. Broader implications include a path toward fully path-traced 3DGS with interreflections and complex lighting, since the ray-based formulation extends naturally to multi-bounce light transport.
