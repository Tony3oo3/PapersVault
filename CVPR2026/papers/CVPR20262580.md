## Problem

Large multimodal language models (MLLMs) with 7B+ parameters dominate image captioning but are too computationally expensive for edge deployment, while existing lightweight captioners (<1B params) underperform on detailed captioning tasks. The paper asks whether factual image captioning truly requires the complex reasoning of large LLMs, and if not, how to close the remaining reliability gap in compact models that stems from visual blindness and single-pass generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262580/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (processed by a CLIP ViT-L vision encoder) and produces a two-stage textual output: an initial coarse caption followed by a refined caption. The refinement stage additionally consumes the model's own initial caption as a textual prompt and multi-layer vision encoder features (concatenated across m selected layers, yielding N×md dimensional representations). For downstream video QA, frame-level captions are aggregated and fed to a separate Qwen2.5-14B LLM for answer generation.

## Method

The architecture replaces LLaMA-7B in LLaVA-1.5 with OPT-125M (56× smaller), retaining the CLIP ViT-L vision encoder and a standard two-layer MLP connector for initial caption generation. The key contribution, MM-SeR, adds a refinement stage where a dedicated SeR-Connector (a set of Transformer blocks) fuses two inputs: token embeddings of the initial caption and multi-layer vision features from the encoder, producing enriched visual embeddings that guide the language model to correct entity/attribute errors. Training is two-stage: standard supervised captioning first, then refinement training using pseudo-initial captions generated by GPT-4o-mini (small perturbations to entities, attributes, or relations in ground-truth captions) to ensure semantic alignment between input and target, enabling targeted optimization on only the erroneous tokens. This design avoids adding auxiliary vision encoders (e.g., DINOv2) and instead maximizes the utility of the existing encoder's hierarchical features, adding only ~50M parameters for the SeR-Connector.

## Results

On MS COCO, the 450M-parameter model (OPT-125M + CLIP ViT-L) achieves CIDEr 129.6 and B@4 39.4, matching LLaVA-1.5 (7.3B) on B@4 and exceeding all prior small captioners (SmallCap: 122.7, Tag2Text: 128.7). On detailed captioning (ShareGPT4V + DCI), the model reaches CIDEr 40.5 and CAPT 45.9, surpassing Cambrian-1 (10.5B; CIDEr 38.7) and LLaVA-1.5 (CIDEr 36.1). Adding MM-SeR yields further gains of +3.9 CIDEr (MS COCO) and +3.1 CIDEr (detailed captioning), bringing the 500M model to CIDEr 133.5 and 43.6 respectively. On long-range VideoQA (LLoVi), the specialist achieves 49.3% accuracy (50.8% with MM-SeR) versus 51.1% for LLaVA-1.5, while running ~6× faster (5m10s vs. 29m20s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MS COCO, the 450M model (CIDEr 129.6) outperforms all prior small specialists—SmallCap (122.7, +6.9), Tag2Text (128.7, +0.9), LocCa (127.1, +2.5), I-Tuning (120.0, +9.6), and CapPa (125.8, +3.8)—while matching LLaVA-1.5 (7.3B) on B@4 (39.4 vs. 39.4) and trailing it by only 4.1 CIDEr points. On detailed captioning (ShareGPT4V + DCI), the model decisively beats both small specialists (SmallCap: CIDEr 20.1, Tag2Text: 32.5) and also surpasses the 7.3B LLaVA-1.5 (36.1) and 10.5B Cambrian-1 (38.7) on CIDEr, though it remains slightly below Cambrian-1 on CAPT (45.9 vs. 50.1) and GPT-judge (2.74 vs. 3.03). With MM-SeR, the 500M model closes the gap to Cambrian-1 on GPT-judge (3.02 vs. 3.03) and matches LLaVA-1.5 on MS COCO CIDEr (133.5 vs. 133.7). On long-range VideoQA, the specialist (49.3%) outperforms SmallCap (41.8%) and Tag2Text (47.1%) by 7.5 and 2.2 points respectively, and with MM-SeR (50.8%) approaches LLaVA-1.5 (51.1%) while using 14× fewer parameters and running 6× faster. The model also outperforms LLaVA-1.5 on GLaMM (CIDEr 29.1 vs. 23.4), suggesting the lightweight specialist is particularly strong on grounding-oriented captioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture by replacing its 7B LLaMA backbone with a 125M-parameter OPT model, demonstrating that the multimodal connector design transfers effectively to much smaller language models. The self-refinement stage extends the Self-Refine paradigm (Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2023) from the text-only LLM domain to the multimodal setting, where visual features from multi-layer vision encoder outputs are integrated alongside the initial caption to guide correction. The training strategy of using GPT-4o-mini-generated pseudo-initial captions with small perturbations draws on the DPO philosophy of preferred/less-preferred pairs but assigns them asymmetric roles as input and target rather than symmetric optimization objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 125M-parameter LM achieves near-parity with 7B+ MLLMs on factual captioning challenges the assumption that captioning requires large-scale language reasoning, opening a practical path for on-device and edge-deployed captioning systems (97% inference time reduction). The multimodal self-refinement framework is the first to integrate visual evidence into a self-correction loop within MLLMs, suggesting a generalizable paradigm for improving reliability without scaling model size. Open questions include whether iterative refinement can be made effective for small LMs, how to unify the initial and refinement connectors, and whether external tools (zoom, crop) can further close the remaining reliability gap with full-scale MLLMs.
