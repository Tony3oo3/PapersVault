## Problem

Existing image aesthetic assessment (IAA) models are designed for coarse-grained evaluation, where images with notable aesthetic differences are scored independently on an absolute scale. However, practical applications such as album management and photo selection require discriminating subtle aesthetic differences among visually similar images within a series—a task the paper defines as fine-grained IAA. Two fundamental challenges arise: strong semantic similarity within series interferes with extracting aesthetic distinctions, and the minor variations (slight color shifts, composition changes) demand highly discriminative representations that current models lack.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images as input; for fine-grained evaluation it processes image series (2–10 visually similar images) where a target image is tokenized relative to a reference image from the same series. During training, GPT-4o-generated comparative text descriptions are fed through a CLIP text encoder to guide alignment, but at inference only the image encoder is used. The output is a continuous aesthetic score per image (via a regression head) from which within-series rankings are derived through pairwise Bradley-Terry probabilities, enabling both absolute scoring and relative ordering.

## Method

FGAesQ builds on a CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone and introduces three modules to learn discriminative scores from relative ranks. (1) DiffToken localizes aesthetics-decisive regions by computing patch-level SSIM between target and reference images, then tokenizes those low-similarity patches at full ViT resolution while downscaling the rest, concentrating computation on perceptually significant differences. (2) CTAlign aligns the visual embedding difference between two images with the embedding of a GPT-4o-generated comparative text description via cosine similarity, orienting the visual encoder toward fine-grained aesthetic distinctions. (3) RankReg converts predicted scores into pairwise win probabilities using the Bradley-Terry model and optimizes them against ground-truth series rankings with a ListMLE loss. Training follows a two-stage strategy: pre-training on coarse-grained AVA data with EMD loss, then joint learning that alternates between coarse-grained (EMD) and fine-grained (CTAlign + RankReg) batches, preserving coarse-grained performance while refining fine-grained discrimination.

## Results

On FGAesthetics, FGAesQ (86.3M params) achieves the best performance across all three image sources and both evaluation levels. On Natural images, it reaches 0.779 pair-level accuracy and 0.729 series-level s-SRCC; on AIGC, 0.709 pair-level accuracy and 0.482 s-SRCC; on Cropping, 0.774 pair-level accuracy and 0.590 s-SRCC. On the coarse-grained AVA benchmark, FGAesQ maintains competitive performance with SRCC 0.770 and PLCC 0.781, while fine-tuned baselines suffer severe degradation (e.g., Charm FT drops from 0.777 to 0.470 SRCC). Cross-dataset evaluation confirms generalization, with FGAesQ achieving the best SRCC on ICAA17K (0.653), AADB (0.562), and TAD66K (0.423).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGAesQ (86.3M params) outperforms all 9 baselines across every source and metric on FGAesthetics. The strongest non-fine-tuned baseline, Q-Align (8.2B params), achieves 0.711 pair-level accuracy on Natural images, while FGAesQ reaches 0.779 — a 6.8-point gain with roughly 95× fewer parameters. Fine-tuned variants (Charm FT, MUSIQ FT, VILA FT) improve fine-grained scores but suffer catastrophic coarse-grained degradation: Charm FT drops from 0.777 to 0.470 SRCC on AVA, whereas FGAesQ maintains 0.770 SRCC on AVA while simultaneously achieving the best fine-grained s-SRCC of 0.729 on Natural. On AIGC, where all methods struggle most, FGAesQ's 0.482 s-SRCC exceeds the next-best (Q-Align at 0.310) by 17.2 points. Cross-dataset evaluation on ICAA17K, AADB, and TAD66K confirms that FGAesQ generalizes well, achieving the best SRCC on all three benchmarks (0.653, 0.562, 0.423 respectively), with the largest margin on AADB (+1.4 over VILA).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining fine-grained image aesthetic assessment (FG-IAA) as a distinct task from the well-studied coarse-grained IAA paradigm, where images are evaluated independently on an absolute scale. While related tasks exist (series photo selection by Chang et al., best frame selection by Ren et al.), they remain partially closed-source and narrowly scoped; FGAesthetics generalizes the problem to diverse series types (natural, AIGC, cropping) with pairwise ranking labels. Methodologically, FGAesQ repurposes standard components—CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone, Bradley-Terry pairwise probabilities, ListMLE ranking loss, and mixed-resolution tokenization—into a unified framework that alternates coarse-grained EMD regression with fine-grained rank calibration, departing from prior IAA models that treat scoring as a purely absolute prediction problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a fundamental limitation: existing SOTA IAA models (including 8B-parameter MLLMs like Q-Align) degrade substantially on fine-grained series-level discrimination, and fine-tuning them on ranking data causes severe catastrophic forgetting of coarse-grained scoring (e.g., Charm SRCC drops from 0.777 to 0.470). This has practical implications for any system that must both score individual images and rank within similar sets (album curation, photo selection, AIGC quality filtering). Open questions include whether the MLLM-generated comparative text (CTAlign) remains beneficial at scale, whether the 32K-image dataset is sufficient for robust generalization, and whether the alternating training strategy extends to other fine-grained perception tasks beyond aesthetics.
