## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for VLMs rely on entropy-based filtering to select high-confidence pseudo-labels, which implicitly inherits and amplifies the model's class-wise prediction biases from large-scale pretraining, leading to pseudo-label collapse. Additionally, cache-based TTA methods store only a narrow subset of easy, high-confidence samples, leaving low-confidence and underrepresented regions of the test distribution unexplored and reinforcing the bias in a self-reinforcing loop.

## Input / Output

The method operates in a sequential streaming setting with a single-image batch size of 1, taking unlabeled RGB test images as input and producing a class label as output. Following TPT, each test image is augmented via random resized cropping to generate up to 63 views for robustness. The cache capacity is fixed at 3 entries per class (matching DPE), and adaptation proceeds incrementally as new samples arrive, with no access to target labels or source data.

## Method

DLAE couples two components: Dynamic Logit Adjustment (DLA) and a Consistency-Guided Exploratory Cache (CGEC). DLA maintains running per-class counters and mean-confidence estimates, then applies a class-specific balancing factor B(c) = exp(−α·p̂(c)·(1−d)) to raw CLIP logits, where d measures the deviation of current prediction confidence from the class's historical mean, thereby attenuating overconfident predictions on frequent classes while preserving rare-class signals. CGEC exploits DLA's side effect of flipping predictions on boundary samples: it admits these flipped samples into the cache under a Semantic Consistency Filter (requiring cosine similarity between the text embeddings of pre- and post-DLA labels) and a Temporal Consistency Filter (requiring that each cached feature remains more aligned with the current text prototype than with the initial one), modulating sample entropy to promote reliable boundary examples in a low-entropy priority queue. The final prediction score combines the DLA-calibrated semantic logit with an affinity-modulated visual prototype computed from CGEC-stored features, optimized via a confidence-aware calibration loss and a symmetric cross-modal alignment loss.

## Results

On the cross-dataset generalization benchmark (10 diverse domains), DLAE achieves 71.88% average top-1 accuracy with the ViT-B/16 backbone, surpassing the strongest baselines SCA (70.34%) and DMN-ZS (70.30%) by 1.5–1.6 points, and the CLIP zero-shot baseline (63.58%) by 8.3 points. On the ImageNet OOD robustness benchmark, DLAE reaches 65.76% OOD average and 67.09% overall accuracy (ViT-B/16), outperforming SCA (64.77% / 66.16%) and DPE (64.43% / 65.93%). With the weaker ResNet-50 backbone, DLAE still leads with 64.81% cross-dataset average and 49.06% OOD average, a 2.9-point margin over the next-best method on the OOD benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DLAE outperforms all 10 compared baselines on both the cross-dataset and OOD benchmarks across both backbones. The closest competitors are SCA (70.34% avg, ViT-B/16) and DMN-ZS (70.30%), which DLAE surpasses by ~1.5 points on cross-dataset accuracy; on the OOD benchmark, SCA (64.77% OOD avg) and DPE (64.43%) trail DLAE (65.76%) by 1.0–1.3 points. The prompt-based methods (CoOp, TPT, DiffTPT) and prototype-shifting method (TPS) lag further behind, with DiffTPT at 65.47% and TPS at 66.96% average on ViT-B/16. Cache-based DPE, the method DLAE directly builds upon, achieves 69.40% cross-dataset and 64.43% OOD, confirming that the DLA+CGEC additions yield consistent gains over the base architecture. Notably, DLAE's advantage is largest on fine-grained and texture datasets (Aircraft: +4.08 pts over SCA; DTD: +1.77 pts; EuroSAT: +4.14 pts), suggesting the exploratory cache is most beneficial where class-wise bias is strongest.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the cache-based TTA lineage for VLMs, building directly on DPE's dual-prototype evolving framework with entropy-prioritized caching. It departs from the prevailing entropy-filtering paradigm by introducing a logit-space debiasing step (DLA) that recalibrates class-wise prediction statistics online, and by repurposing the resulting prediction flips as a signal for exploratory cache admission (CGEC) rather than discarding them as unreliable. Conceptually, it bridges distribution-based calibration methods (which adjust predictions from running statistics) with cache-based prototype learning, but does so in a coupled way where the calibration step actively generates the exploration candidates that the cache step then filters and stores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and underappreciated failure mode in sequential TTA—pseudo-label collapse driven by self-reinforcing class bias—providing both a diagnostic (Fig. 1a showing per-class variance and cache saturation in Fig. 1b) and a practical remedy that operates under the same single-image, small-cache constraints as prior work. The consistent 1–3% gains over strong baselines like DPE and SCA across both ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16 backbones, on both cross-domain and OOD benchmarks, suggest the approach is robust rather than dataset-specific. Open questions include whether the logit-adjustment + exploratory-cache coupling generalizes to open-vocabulary or non-classification VLM tasks, how it behaves under more extreme distribution shifts or with larger cache capacities, and whether the consistency filters (SCF/TCF) remain effective when the base model is very weak on the target domain.
