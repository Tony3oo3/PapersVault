## Problem

3D spatial videos used for scene understanding with video-language models contain massive visual token redundancy because the same 3D objects recur across many viewpoints. Existing text-guided and vision-guided pruning methods operate on individual or temporally adjacent frames, failing to model the global cross-frame view consistency inherent in multi-view 3D scenes, and they ignore spatial diversity of retained tokens, which is critical for object-centric tasks like 3D grounding and dense captioning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes uniformly sampled RGB frames (384×384, 16 or 32 frames per ScanNet scene) and a text query. At inference time, SigLIP extracts 2D patch features while VGGT-1B (frozen) extracts geometry features and a global cross-frame attention map; tokens are projected into a 0.1 m voxel grid via inverse camera projection. The pruned token set (reduced by 60–90%) is fed to Qwen2-7B, which outputs 3D bounding boxes, dense scene captions, or free-form text answers depending on the task.

## Method

Geo3DPruner is a training-free, two-stage pruning framework that exploits VGGT's global cross-frame attention map to model inter-view geometric relevance. In the intra-voxel stage, each 2D token is assigned to a 3D voxel via inverse camera projection; within each voxel, a contribution score is computed as the averaged incoming attention from the voxel-local attention submatrix, and only the top-α (50%) tokens are retained to collapse multi-view redundancy. In the inter-voxel stage, an iterative heuristic selects voxels by computing cross-voxel attention scores (summed over rows, averaged over columns), picking the top-K (8) most salient voxels per iteration, then recomputing attention among remaining candidates to suppress intra-object bias and enforce spatial diversity. This iterative recomputation prevents high-attention voxels belonging to the same instance from dominating the token budget, yielding a geometrically diverse subset that preserves scene completeness under strict token constraints.

## Results

On 16-frame spatial videos (3,136 total tokens), Geo3DPruner retains 98.9% of baseline performance at 60% token pruning, 96.1% at 80%, and 92.1% at 90% (retaining only 320 tokens). On 32-frame videos (6,272 tokens), it maintains 92.0% of baseline performance even at 90% pruning. The method consistently outperforms both text-guided (FastV) and vision-guided (VisPruner) pruning across all five benchmarks (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, Scan2Cap, ScanQA, SQA3D) and all pruning ratios, with the largest gaps appearing on object-centric tasks like Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 (91.3 vs. 84.7 and 88.7 at 90% pruning).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Geo3DPruner is compared against two training-free pruning baselines: FastV (text-guided, using cross-attention to final text tokens) and VisPruner (vision-guided, using visual attention and inter-token similarity). Geo3DPruner wins on all benchmarks at every pruning ratio. At the most aggressive 90% pruning (16 frames), Geo3DPruner retains 92.1% of baseline performance versus 81.0% for FastV and 84.9% for VisPruner — a 11.1-point and 7.2-point margin respectively. The gap is most pronounced on Scan2Cap CIDEr@0.5 (91.3 vs. 84.7/88.7) and ScanRefer Acc@0.5 (49.4 vs. 45.8/46.8), confirming that geometry-guided spatial diversity is critical for object-centric 3D tasks. The unpruned Video-3D LLM† baseline (with VGGT geometry features replacing 3D positional encodings) serves as the 100% reference; Geo3DPruner at 60% pruning (98.9%) nearly matches it, and even at 90% pruning the performance drop is only 7.9 points. On 32-frame videos the same pattern holds: Geo3DPruner achieves 92.0% at 90% pruning versus 82.4% (FastV) and 84.8% (VisPruner).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Video-3D LLM / VG LLM line of work, which reformulates 3D scene understanding as a video-language modeling problem and injects 3D geometry features (via VGGT) in place of hand-crafted positional encodings. It departs from existing visual token pruning methods (FastV, VisPruner) that operate on per-frame or temporally-adjacent attention signals by instead exploiting the global cross-frame attention map produced by a 3D geometry encoder, repurposing a reconstruction model's internal representations as a pruning signal. The two-stage voxel-based pruning (intra-voxel view consistency + inter-voxel spatial diversity) is a novel architectural contribution that has no direct precedent in the 2D token-pruning literature, where pruning is typically a single-pass selection over flat token sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 90% token reduction while retaining over 92% of baseline performance across five 3D scene understanding benchmarks is a practically significant efficiency gain that could make VideoLM-based 3D understanding deployable in resource-constrained settings. The observation that mild pruning (20%) can slightly *improve* performance suggests that redundant multi-view tokens act as noise, a finding with implications beyond this specific framework. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, whether the fixed voxel size (0.1 m) is optimal across scene scales, and whether the reliance on a frozen VGGT encoder limits adaptability to novel or partially observed environments.
