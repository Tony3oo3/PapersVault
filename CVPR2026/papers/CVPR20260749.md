## Problem

Existing vectorized road network extraction models, particularly the SAM-Road series, rely on a node-centric paradigm that reasons about connectivity from sparse endpoint features, making them fragile to occlusions, faint tracks, and ambiguous junctions prevalent in off-road environments. This vulnerability is compounded by the absence of any large-scale, continent-spanning vectorized dataset for off-road scenes, leaving a critical domain gap between well-structured urban roads and wild terrains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260749/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes high-resolution RGB satellite imagery (8K×4K at 0.3 m/pixel for WildRoad; 2048×2048 or 400×400 for urban benchmarks) and produces a vectorized road graph G=(V,E) where vertices are extracted from predicted keypoint and road-probability maps via non-maximum suppression, and edges are validated by a topology head. Training uses patch-based sampling (1024×1024 for WildRoad, 512×512 for urban datasets) with 256–512 source vertices sampled per patch to balance computational cost against topology coverage.

## Method

MaGRoad's core module, MaGTopoNet, shifts from node-centric to path-centric connectivity reasoning by sampling traversability values along the entire geodesic path of each candidate edge from multi-scale road probability maps (L=3 scales via average pooling with kernels {3,9,15}), computing mean, standard deviation, and softmin statistics at each scale to capture average quality, along-path consistency, and bottleneck regions. These 9-dimensional path features are concatenated with 11-dimensional geometric features (normalized offsets, Euclidean distance, and bearing angle encoded via 4-order Fourier features) to form edge tokens. An edge-biased self-attention mechanism then processes per-source candidate sets, injecting a uniform negative off-diagonal bias to encourage sparse edge selection and suppress spurious pairings. A unified single-pass NMS strategy (replacing three separate suppression stages) with a score offset for keypoint priority achieves a 2.5× inference speedup while boosting F1 to 82.22 on WildRoad.

## Results

On the WildRoad benchmark, MaGRoad achieves F1=78.85 and APLS=72.56, surpassing the previous best SAM-Road++ (F1=76.74, APLS=69.72) by +2.11 F1 and +2.84 APLS. The optimized MaGRoad-fast variant further pushes F1 to 82.22 with a 2.5× inference speedup (27.8 min vs. 74.9 min), at a slight APLS cost (69.29). On urban datasets, MaGRoad achieves the best F1 on SpaceNet (84.23) and the highest recall across all three urban benchmarks, confirming generalizability of the path-centric design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On WildRoad, MaGRoad outperforms all four baselines on both F1 and APLS: it beats Sat2Graph by +10.74 F1 and +23.83 APLS, SAM-Road by +2.24 F1 and +3.85 APLS, and SAM-Road++ by +2.11 F1 and +2.84 APLS. On SpaceNet, MaGRoad achieves the best F1 (84.23) over SAM-Road++ (81.57) and RNGDet++ (82.51), driven by its highest recall (81.01 vs. 72.23 and 75.24), though it trails SAM-Road++ on precision (87.72 vs. 93.68). On City-Scale, MaGRoad is competitive but slightly behind SAM-Road++ in F1 (78.11 vs. 80.01) while leading in APLS (71.27 vs. 68.34). On Global-Scale (in-domain), MaGRoad edges out SAM-Road++ in F1 (62.68 vs. 62.33) with higher recall (52.90 vs. 49.27) but lower precision (80.01 vs. 88.95); in the out-of-domain split, MaGRoad and SAM-Road++ are nearly tied on F1 (48.23 vs. 48.34). Overall, the path-centric design consistently improves recall and topological accuracy (APLS) at the cost of modest precision, making it particularly suited for applications prioritizing network completeness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the single-shot graph extraction lineage (Sat2Graph → SAM-Road → SAM-Road++) by replacing the node-centric TopoNet with a path-centric MaGTopoNet that aggregates multi-scale traversability evidence along the entire geodesic path of each candidate edge, rather than reasoning only from endpoint features. It also introduces WildRoad, the first large-scale vectorized off-road benchmark, constructed via a novel interactive annotation pipeline that integrates SAM-style prompts into graph labeling. The ViT-B backbone pretrained on SAM is retained from SAM-Road, but the topology head and vertex extraction strategy are redesigned to address the specific failure modes (fragmentation, spurious junctions) that node-centric reasoning exhibits under occlusion and low-contrast conditions in wild terrains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WildRoad dataset fills a critical gap: no prior vectorized benchmark covered off-road environments, leaving a substantial domain gap unaddressed for autonomous systems operating beyond paved urban roads. The path-centric paradigm offers a principled architectural alternative to node-centric topology reasoning, with the ablation showing that path features are essential (removing them drops APLS by ~10 points) and that naively combining node and path features actually degrades performance, suggesting endpoint features introduce ambiguity that path evidence cannot compensate for. Open questions include whether path-centric reasoning generalizes to other graph-structured extraction tasks (e.g., power lines, river networks), whether the urban-to-off-road domain gap can be further closed with larger off-road training sets, and whether the APLS–F1 trade-off in the fast variant can be resolved without sacrificing speed.
