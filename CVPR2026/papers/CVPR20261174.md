## Problem

Efficient stereo matching models have long been regarded as incapable of zero-shot generalization due to their limited capacity, forcing practitioners to rely on domain-specific fine-tuning. Meanwhile, accurate zero-shot methods that leverage monocular depth priors (e.g., DepthAnything) incur prohibitive computational costs, making them impractical for resource-constrained deployment. This paper challenges the assumption that ultra-light models cannot generalize, demonstrating that a compact architecture combined with a carefully designed training strategy can match or exceed non-prior-based accurate methods at less than 1% of their computational cost.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a pair of rectified stereo images (left and right) at arbitrary resolution and produces a full-resolution disparity map. Internally, all processing occurs at 1/4 resolution with a maximum disparity of 192 pixels; the low-resolution disparity is recovered to full resolution via convex upsampling. The model requires only 2.5 GB of GPU memory for 2K inputs and achieves 4K stereo depth in 21 ms on a GTX 1080, making it suitable for embedded and real-time applications.

## Method

The architecture uses a shared-weight MobileNetV2 backbone (pretrained on ImageNet, no external depth priors) to extract multi-scale features, which are upsampled to 1/4 resolution. A correlation module constructs a cost volume, which is then processed by a hybrid cost aggregation module: a small 3D convolution block (only ~4.8% of total compute, with 3×3×3 kernels) captures cross-disparity structure, followed by ConvNeXt 2D layers for efficient spatial refinement, arranged in a serial 3D-then-2D configuration. Disparity is regressed via soft-argmax over the aggregated cost volume. Training follows a three-stage strategy: (1) supervised training on 1.8M synthetic stereo pairs, (2) self-distillation where a fixed-teacher model sees clean inputs while the student sees strongly perturbed inputs, encouraging domain-invariant features via cosine feature alignment, and (3) knowledge distillation on 0.5M unlabeled real-world stereo pairs using dense pseudo-labels from the frozen FoundationStereo model. This combination effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap and is shown to be universally beneficial across different efficient architectures.

## Results

On the four standard zero-shot benchmarks (million-scale training setting), Lite Any Stereo achieves D1 of 3.04 on KITTI 2012, 3.87 on KITTI 2015, Bad-1.0 of 3.53 on ETH3D, and Bad-2.0 of 7.51 on Middlebury, using only 33G MACs. This outperforms the best efficient baselines (LightStereo-M: 4.10/4.97/5.33/10.85; BANet-2D: 3.90/4.71/5.92/10.05) by a large margin and matches or exceeds the non-prior-based accurate method Selective-IGEV (3.20/4.50/3.40/7.50 at 3619G MACs) while requiring less than 1% of its compute. On the KITTI online leaderboard, the fine-tuned model ranks 1st among all efficient methods (D1-bg 1.36 on KITTI 2015, 3-noc 1.09 on KITTI 2012). Inference time is 21 ms on a GTX 1080 for 4K input, with only 2.5 GB GPU memory for 2K inputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest efficient baselines trained on million-scale data, Lite Any Stereo wins on all four benchmarks: it beats LightStereo-M by 1.06 D1 on KITTI 2012 (3.04 vs 4.10) and 3.34 Bad-2 on Middlebury (7.51 vs 10.85), and beats BANet-2D by 0.86 D1 on KITTI 2012 (3.04 vs 3.90). Against StereoAnything-L (84G MACs), it wins on KITTI 2012 (3.04 vs 4.00), KITTI 2015 (3.87 vs 4.81), and Middlebury (7.51 vs 9.82) while using only 39% of the compute, though it trails slightly on ETH3D (3.53 vs 3.81). Against the non-prior-based accurate method Selective-IGEV (3619G MACs), Lite Any Stereo wins on KITTI 2012 (3.04 vs 3.20), KITTI 2015 (3.87 vs 4.50), and ties on Middlebury (7.51 vs 7.50), while losing narrowly on ETH3D (3.53 vs 3.40), all at <1% of the MACs. The depth-prior-based FoundationStereo (12824G MACs) still leads on every metric (e.g., 2.51 vs 3.04 D1 on KITTI 2012), confirming the remaining gap to foundation-model-augmented approaches. On the KITTI online leaderboard, Lite Any Stereo outperforms all previously published efficient methods including BANet-3D (1.27 vs 1.09 on 3-noc), HITNet (1.41 vs 1.09), and LightStereo-M (1.56 vs 1.09). On DrivingStereo weather, the student model surpasses its teacher FoundationStereo by 1.97 D1 overall (8.74 vs 10.71), demonstrating that the three-stage training strategy transfers effectively even in-domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of stereo matching research: efficiency-oriented methods (LightStereo, BANet, MobileStereoNet) that achieve fast inference but lack zero-shot generalization, and accuracy-oriented methods (FoundationStereo, Selective-IGEV, StereoAnything) that generalize well but demand orders of magnitude more compute. Rather than adopting monocular depth priors from foundation models as recent zero-shot methods do, Lite Any Stereo uses a conventional MobileNetV2 backbone and instead invests in a hybrid 3D-2D cost aggregation module and a three-stage training strategy (supervised → self-distillation → real-world pseudo-label distillation). The hybrid aggregation conceptually extends BANet's bilateral 2D aggregation by inserting a small 3D component to preserve disparity-structure awareness, while the training pipeline repurposes the underutilized pool of unlabeled real-world stereo pairs for knowledge distillation from an accurate teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that efficient stereo models are inherently incapable of zero-shot generalization, showing that a 33-MAC model can match or exceed a 3619-MAC non-prior-based method across four benchmarks. The three-stage training strategy is demonstrated to be architecture-agnostic (improving LightStereo-M and BANet-2D as well), making it a broadly applicable recipe for the field. However, the method still trails depth-prior-based approaches like FoundationStereo by a wide margin, and the authors acknowledge that limited high-quality real-world stereo data is the primary bottleneck. Open questions include whether scaling real-world unlabeled data can close the remaining gap and whether a model zoo of different sizes can be built for flexible deployment.
