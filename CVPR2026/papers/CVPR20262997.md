## Problem

Existing orbital video generation methods rely on pixel-wise attention to enforce view consistency, which breaks down for long-range extrapolation (e.g., rear-view synthesis) where pixel correspondences to the input image are sparse or absent. While 2.5D priors such as depth or normal maps have been explored, they cannot model complete object shapes, leaving unobserved and occluded parts under-constrained and producing distorted, incoherent structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 576×576 RGB object image (which also serves as the starting reference frame) and a camera trajectory specified as sequences of elevation and azimuth angles, and produces a 21-frame orbital video at 576×576 resolution. The trajectory can be a static orbit (zero elevation) or a dynamic orbit with elevation varying in [-60°, 60°], and the input image is consumed both as a VAE-encoded latent condition and as a DINOv2 feature prompt for the 3D foundation model.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-scale 3D adapter that injects shape priors from a frozen 3D foundation model (Hunyuan3D) into a Stable Video Diffusion base model via alternating cross-attention layers. Two scales of latent features are extracted: a denoised global latent vector (3072 tokens) providing overall structural guidance, and volumetric features queried on a 128³ grid and projected into 8 canonical latent images (16×16) for view-dependent fine-grained geometry. The global vector is duplicated across all N frames to share a unified shape reference, while the canonical latent images are folded into tokens and injected as key-value pairs in a separate cross-attention branch. This plug-in design preserves the base model's pretrained temporal priors and avoids explicit mesh extraction, enabling efficient inference (only ~2.5 s for 3D feature extraction on a single H200 GPU). Training is supervised on Objaverse-XL synthetic orbital videos with the 3D foundation model kept frozen, and the adapter contains 0.3B trainable parameters.

## Results

On Objaverse-XL, the proposed method (21 frames) achieves PSNR 22.78, SSIM 0.92, LPIPS 0.09, CLIP-S 94.19, and MEt3R 0.05, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On GSO, it reaches PSNR 24.67, SSIM 0.93, LPIPS 0.08, CLIP-S 91.90, and MEt3R 0.04, again leading all competitors. The method surpasses the closest video-generation baseline SV3D by +2.30 dB PSNR and −0.02 MEt3R on Objaverse-XL, and by +3.09 dB PSNR and −0.02 MEt3R on GSO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric across both Objaverse-XL and GSO. Against the closest video-generation baselines, SV3D [42] and Hi3D [47], the gains are substantial: +2.30 dB PSNR and 0.02 lower MEt3R over SV3D, and +3.46 dB PSNR and 0.04 lower MEt3R over Hi3D on Objaverse-XL. Among NVS methods, Era3D [21] is the strongest (PSNR 21.08, CLIP-S 91.06 on Objaverse-XL), yet the proposed method still leads by +1.70 dB PSNR and +3.13 CLIP-S. The 3D-generation baselines (Trellis [46], Hunyuan3D [55]) produce coherent shapes but suffer in texture fidelity, yielding lower PSNR (19.43–20.25) and LPIPS (0.11–0.14) than the proposed method. Notably, the MEt3R multi-view consistency metric (only comparable for video methods) shows the proposed method at 0.05 vs. 0.07 (SV3D) and 0.09 (Hi3D) on Objaverse-XL, confirming that the unified 3D shape prior improves temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orbital video generation line (SV3D, Hi3D, Vivid-1-to-3) by replacing pixel-wise attention and 2.5D priors (depth maps) with shape priors extracted from a 3D foundation generative model (Hunyuan3D). The key architectural departure is the multi-scale 3D adapter, which injects two scales of 3D latent features (a global shape vector and projected volumetric latent images) into a frozen video diffusion backbone via cross-attention, rather than the iterative coarse-to-fine refinement used by Im-3D or Ouroboros3D. By operating in compact latent space rather than explicit meshes, the method avoids the mesh-extraction bottleneck that limits prior 3D-guided approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that 3D foundation model latents can serve as effective, training-free shape constraints for video generation, addressing the well-known failure mode of pixel-wise attention under large viewpoint changes (e.g., rear-view synthesis). However, visual fidelity remains bounded by the base video model's resolution, and the authors explicitly note that texture/appearance synthesis from 3D priors is unexplored. Open questions include whether faster 3D foundation models (e.g., Trellis-style structured latents) can further reduce inference cost, and whether the adapter generalizes beyond orbital trajectories to arbitrary camera paths.
