## Problem

Existing VR sketch-to-shape methods treat sketches as unordered point clouds, discarding the temporal sequence of strokes and points that encodes connectivity, structure, and design intent. Additionally, the field suffers from severe data scarcity (only ~1,000 public sketch-shape pairs in a single category) and geometric misalignment between hand-drawn sketches and target shapes, making both training and evaluation unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260289/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The input is a sequential VR sketch: an ordered collection of 3D polylines (strokes), where each stroke is a temporally ordered sequence of 3D points normalized to [0,1]³. The sketch is tokenized with SEP (stroke separator) and EoS (end-of-sketch) tokens, and each point is encoded via 10-band Fourier features projected to a 256-d embedding. The output is a 3D shape represented as a 64³ signed distance field, decoded to a mesh via Marching Cubes, generated through 100 DDIM denoising steps in the latent space of a frozen 3D VQ-VAE.

## Method

The core contribution is an order-aware sketch encoder built on a 6-layer, 8-head BERT-style transformer that processes the sketch as a flat token sequence where each point token receives the sum of three embeddings: a spatial embedding (Fourier features + 2-layer MLP), a stroke-index embedding (sinusoidal positional encoding + linear projection), and a within-stroke point-index embedding (same scheme). This encoder is coupled end-to-end with SDFusion, a latent diffusion model whose U-Net is jointly trained with the sketch encoder via ℓ₂ loss on VQ-VAE latents, eliminating multi-stage modality alignment. A learning-free synthetic sketch pipeline (Sharp Edge Sampling → Bezier spline fitting via EMAP → stroke merging → depth-first traversal with 10% stochastic skipping) generates 20,838 paired samples in ~10 hours, enabling large-scale pretraining that transfers to real sketches with as few as 50 fine-tuning examples per category. Sketch-specific augmentations (15% stroke dropping, 30% point dropping, 20% stroke swapping) further improve robustness and partial-sketch generalization.

## Results

On the VRSKETCH2SHAPE dataset (all four categories), the proposed method achieves an F-score of 69.8 and a Chamfer Distance (CD×1000) of 4.8, substantially outperforming Luo et al. (F-score 48.8, CD 13.0) and LAS-Diffusion (F-score 40.2, CD 27.1). On the chair-only subset, the gap widens further: F-score 64.3 vs. 42.2 (Luo) and 37.0 (LAS-Diffusion), with CD reduced to 4.0 from 13.4 and 51.1 respectively. On the public 3DVRCHAIR benchmark, the method also leads with F-score 31.1 and CD 25.8, improving over Luo et al. by ~40% in F-score and ~27% in CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: Luo et al. [41] (ICCV 2023), which treats VR sketches as unordered point clouds and aligns them with point-cloud or 2D-rendering latents, and LAS-Diffusion [81] (ACM TOG 2023), a 2D-render-based diffusion method that converts 3D sketches into multi-view images. VRSKETCH2SHAPE wins decisively on both datasets and both metrics. On VRSKETCH2SHAPE (all categories), it improves F-score by 43% over Luo et al. (69.8 vs. 48.8) and 73% over LAS-Diffusion (69.8 vs. 40.2), while reducing CD by 63% and 82% respectively. The advantage is even more pronounced on the chair-only subset, where CD drops from 13.4 (Luo) to 4.0 — a 70% reduction. On 3DVRCHAIR, the gains are smaller but consistent (F-score 31.1 vs. 26.6; CD 25.8 vs. 35.5), suggesting the ordering-aware encoder and synthetic pre-training provide robust benefits even on the single-category public benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VR sketch-to-shape generation line (Luo et al. 2021/2023, Chen et al. 2024) by explicitly modeling the temporal sequence of strokes and points rather than treating sketches as unordered point clouds. Architecturally, it couples a BERT-style transformer encoder (adapted from the NLP architecture [15] and the 2D SketchBERT [30]) with the SDFusion latent diffusion model [11] for shape synthesis, using Fourier feature embeddings borrowed from NeRF [45] for spatial encoding. A key departure from prior deep sketch synthesis work is the use of a purely geometric, learning-free pipeline (Sharp Edge Sampling + Bezier spline fitting) to generate synthetic training sketches, rather than training a generative model on human sketches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first open, multi-category benchmark (20k synthetic + 900 real pairs across four categories) for sequential VR sketch-to-shape generation, addressing a critical data scarcity that limited prior work to a single 1,005-pair chair dataset. The practical finding that a learning-free synthetic pipeline yields strong zero-shot generalization to real sketches (and that only ~50 real sketches per category are needed for near-optimal fine-tuning) significantly lowers the barrier to building VR sketching tools. The main limitation is the frozen 64³ VQ-VAE bottleneck, which caps geometric fidelity and inference speed; lifting this constraint via end-to-end training at higher resolutions is a clear next step. Broader open questions include whether the sequential formulation scales to more complex or open-ended shapes beyond the four ShapeNet categories tested.
