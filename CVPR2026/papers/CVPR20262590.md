## Problem

Video diffusion models encode rich spatiotemporal priors but struggle with precise 6-DoF camera trajectory control, spatial-temporal consistency, and disentangling scene motion from camera motion in 3D/4D tasks. Existing remedies—per-task fine-tuning or post-hoc warping-and-repainting—either degrade pretrained priors, produce artifacts from out-of-distribution warped inputs, or incur prohibitive computational costs, leaving a gap for a general, training-free mechanism that injects fine-grained trajectory control while preserving generation quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image or a short video sequence plus a user-specified 6-DoF camera trajectory as input. A vision foundation model (e.g., VGGT) reconstructs a scene point cloud and depth maps, which are warped along the target trajectory to produce a guidance video and validity masks; the first frame is additionally converted into a textual prompt and VAE latent for the image-to-video diffusion model. Output is a trajectory-controlled video at up to 1280×720 resolution, generated on a single GPU with ≥69 GB VRAM; wider-view panoramas are assembled by concatenating per-pass segments whose length is bounded by the backbone's sequence capacity.

## Method

WorldForge is a fully training-free, inference-time guidance framework built on a warping-and-repainting pipeline, composed of three synergistic modules. (1) Intra-Step Recursive Refinement (IRR) embeds a micro predict-correct loop inside each denoising step: the one-step denoised estimate x̂₀ is fused with the VAE-encoded trajectory latent in clean space (replacing observed regions with ground-truth warped content), then re-noised and fed back, injecting trajectory constraints at every step. (2) Flow-Gated Latent Fusion (FLF) computes per-channel optical-flow similarity scores between the predicted and target trajectory latents, then selectively overwrites only motion-correlated channels (above a dynamic threshold) while leaving appearance channels untouched, decoupling motion from content. (3) Dual-Path Self-Corrective Guidance (DSG) runs a parallel unguided denoising path alongside the guided one and uses the orthogonal component of their velocity-field difference—scaled by an adaptive cosine weight βₜ—to correct the guided path toward higher perceptual fidelity, specifically addressing the large angular divergence (50°–70°) between the two paths that breaks standard CFG. The entire pipeline is model-agnostic and plug-and-play, demonstrated on Wan 2.1, SVD, and LongCat-Video without any parameter updates.

## Results

On 3D static scene generation (Table 1), WorldForge achieves the best scores across all metrics: FID 96.08, CLIPsim 0.948, ATE 0.077, RPE-T 0.086, and RPE-R 0.221, outperforming the closest training-based competitor TrajectoryCrafter (FID 111.49, ATE 0.090) by ~14% in FID and ~15% in ATE. On 4D dynamic trajectory control (Table 2), WorldForge attains FVD 93.17, CLIP-Vsim 0.938, RPE-T 0.826, and RPE-R 2.690, leading all baselines on generation quality and rotational trajectory accuracy, though TrajectoryCrafter achieves a slightly lower ATE (0.431 vs. 0.527). The method is fully training-free, matching or surpassing methods that require fine-tuning on motion-conditioned data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldForge outperforms all six baselines on 3D static scenes across every reported metric. Against the strongest training-based competitor, TrajectoryCrafter [82], it improves FID by 14.0% (96.08 vs. 111.49), ATE by 14.4% (0.077 vs. 0.090), and RPE-R by 17.2% (0.221 vs. 0.267), while also achieving the highest CLIPsim (0.948 vs. 0.910). Among training-free methods, it dominates ViewExtrapolator [38] (FID 96.08 vs. 125.50; ATE 0.077 vs. 0.183) and NVS-Solver [79] (FID 96.08 vs. 118.64; RPE-R 0.221 vs. 1.056) by wide margins. On 4D dynamic scenes, WorldForge leads on FVD (93.17 vs. 97.31 for TrajectoryCrafter), CLIP-Vsim (0.938 vs. 0.923), RPE-T (0.826 vs. 1.078), and RPE-R (2.690 vs. 8.950), though TrajectoryCrafter achieves a marginally better ATE (0.431 vs. 0.527). The training-based See3D [42] and ViewCrafter [83] are consistently outperformed on both quality and trajectory accuracy, confirming that the training-free inference-time guidance strategy is competitive with or superior to fine-tuning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldForge extends the warping-and-repainting pipeline for trajectory-controlled video generation by adding an inference-time guidance layer, departing from both fine-tuning approaches (LoRA, ControlNet adapters) and pure post-hoc inpainting. Its DSG mechanism is directly inspired by Classifier-Free Guidance [19] and the orthogonal-projection principle from APG [54], but adapts them to handle a fundamentally larger angular divergence (50°–70°) between guided and unguided velocity fields caused by trajectory-conditioned input modification. The FLF module builds on the channel-specific motion observation from Xiao et al. [73] but replaces PCA-based channel identification with a gradient-free optical-flow scoring scheme, and the IRR loop generalizes the clean-space fusion idea from inpainting work [41] into a per-step predict-corrector within the denoising schedule.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that precise 6-DoF camera control can be injected into frozen video diffusion models without any retraining, WorldForge lowers the barrier to controllable 3D/4D generation and offers a plug-and-play tool applicable across backbones (Wan 2.1, SVD, LongCat-Video) and downstream tasks (video editing, stabilization, virtual try-on). The key open limitation is computational cost: the iterative dual-path guidance with per-step flow computation precludes real-time use, and the authors acknowledge that distillation or fewer-step solvers are needed to close this gap. A broader open question is whether the channel-level motion/appearance decoupling discovered here generalizes to future, architecturally different VDMs, or whether it is an artifact of the specific VAE latent structure used.
