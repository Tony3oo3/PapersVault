## Problem

Existing partial label learning (PLL) algorithms disambiguate candidate labels by minimizing prediction loss but cannot quantify the reliability of their disambiguation results. Softmax-based models produce overconfident point estimates even when predictions are wrong, and false disambiguations can mislead training toward overfitting noisy labels. The paper asks: how to simultaneously identify the ground-truth label from an ambiguous candidate set and measure the uncertainty of that disambiguation decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260555/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a feature vector (extracted via CNN or MLP from RGB images) paired with a set of candidate labels S_i (where |S_i| ≥ 1 and only one is true) as input. Instead of outputting a single softmax probability vector, the network produces a non-negative evidence vector e = [e_1, ..., e_Q] via a ReLU activation layer, from which a Dirichlet distribution Dir(p|α) is constructed with α = 1 + e. The final output is a 'disambiguation opinion' consisting of both the predicted class (argmax of (1+e)/K) and an uncertainty mass u = Q/K, enabling the user to reject or flag unreliable predictions.

## Method

ED-PLL extends Evidential Deep Learning to the PLL setting by interpreting the ambiguous candidate label set as evidence supporting a label hypothesis, with belief masses b_c = e_c/K and uncertainty u = Q/K guiding disambiguation. The training objective combines three terms: (1) a PLL cross-entropy loss L_PLL with iteratively updated label confidence weights r_i that propagate through k-nearest neighbors to progressively refine disambiguation; (2) a non-candidate label suppression loss L_non that computes the Dirichlet expectation of log(1−p_j) for j ∉ S_i via Taylor approximation, encouraging evidence for non-candidate labels to approach zero; and (3) a conflict-aware regularization L_con that measures intra-class prediction conflict by jointly combining opinion distance (L1 divergence of predicted distributions), pairwise uncertainty products, and Jaccard distance between candidate label sets. The framework is theoretically grounded in the Expectation-Maximization perspective and is plug-and-play, compatible with any backbone network or stochastic optimizer.

## Results

On five real-world partially labeled datasets, ED-PLL achieves the best accuracy across all benchmarks: 76.78% on Lost, 59.85% on MSRCv2, 74.94% on BirdSong, 58.23% on Soccer Player, and 62.34% on Yahoo!News. On synthetic benchmark datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Kuzushiji-MNIST, CIFAR-10) with noise levels q ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5}, ED-PLL consistently outperforms loss-based baselines (PRODEN, LW, RC, CC, CAVL) by 2–10 percentage points. On CIFAR-10, ED-PLL reaches 87.95% at q=0.3, while structure-based methods with the ED-PLL loss (PiCO-ED, DIRK-ED) push accuracy to 92.90% and 93.47% respectively, demonstrating the plug-and-play nature of the proposed loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ED-PLL outperforms all seven baselines on all five real-world datasets, with the largest margins on MSRCv2 (+1.57% over RC) and BirdSong (+3.66% over CAVL). On synthetic benchmarks, ED-PLL consistently beats loss-based methods (PRODEN, LW, RC, CC, CAVL) by 2–10 points across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and Kuzushiji-MNIST at all noise levels. However, on CIFAR-10, the structure-based methods PiCO and DIRK (and their ED-PLL-loss variants PiCO-ED and DIRK-ED) surpass plain ED-PLL by 4–7 points, e.g., DIRK-ED reaches 93.47% vs. ED-PLL's 87.95% at q=0.3. The paper frames this as evidence of ED-PLL's plug-and-play compatibility: replacing the loss in PiCO and DIRK with ED-PLL's evidential loss yields PiCO-ED and DIRK-ED, which improve over their original counterparts. On MNIST at q=0.3, ED-PLL achieves 99.16%, narrowly beating PiCO-ED (99.27%) and DIRK-ED (99.07%), while on K-MNIST at q=0.5, DIRK-ED (97.50%) slightly edges out ED-PLL (91.52%), indicating that architectural inductive biases still matter on harder datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Evidential Deep Learning (EDL), which models classification uncertainty via Dirichlet distributions grounded in subjective logic and Dempster-Shafer theory, to the Partial Label Learning (PLL) setting where ground-truth labels are unavailable. Standard PLL methods (both non-deep and deep disambiguation strategies) rely on softmax-based point estimates that cannot express prediction credibility, and prior EDL work assumes fully supervised labels. The key departure is reinterpreting the candidate label set itself as the source of evidence, combined with a novel non-candidate label suppression term and a conflict-aware intra-class regularization, all unified under an EM-style iterative framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By attaching a calibrated uncertainty value to every disambiguation decision, ED-PLL enables selective prediction and risk-aware deployment in PLL scenarios where false disambiguations carry real-world consequences (e.g., medical or safety-critical labeling). The plug-and-play loss design—demonstrated by improving PiCO and DIRK when their losses are swapped—suggests broad applicability across existing PLL architectures. Open questions include scalability to very large candidate label sets (the k-NN initialization and conflict matrix computation may become costly), whether the uncertainty estimates transfer to downstream tasks like active label refinement, and how the method behaves under instance-dependent PLL where candidate sets vary per instance in more complex ways.
