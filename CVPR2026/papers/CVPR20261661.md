## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models suffer from modality imbalance, where visual information is systematically underutilized relative to text in deeper layers. This stems from the training paradigm relying exclusively on next-text-token-prediction, which provides no direct visual supervisory signal and causes progressive homogenization of visual representations. The paper identifies and addresses this gap by introducing direct visual supervision within the LLM's own latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261661/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image (encoded by a visual encoder such as SigLIP 2, CLIP, DINOv2, AIMv2, Qwen-ViT, or a raw-pixel MLP) and a text instruction, producing a text response. During training, the image is additionally processed through a separate masked-vision-token sequence where a fraction of vision tokens are replaced with a learned [MASK] embedding; the model must reconstruct these in the LLM's latent visual logit space (dimension D_v) via a 3-layer MLP head, alongside the standard text generation objective.

## Method

LaVer employs a student-teacher masked image modeling framework in the LLM's joint latent semantic space: the student receives masked vision tokens and must predict the teacher's (EMA-updated) visual output embeddings at those positions, providing direct intrinsic visual supervision absent from standard next-token training. To support spatial reasoning, the method introduces mixed attention (bidirectional full attention for vision tokens, causal for text) and 2D-RoPE that encodes patch grid coordinates. A key novelty is Clipped Gram-Anchoring (CGA), an asymmetric regularizer that penalizes only when the student's inter-token Gram matrix becomes less discriminative than the teacher's, preventing the model from collapsing all vision tokens to identical embeddings while still allowing feature improvement. The masked vision tokens are packed into an independent sequence with diagonally blocked bidirectional attention to avoid cross-image leakage, and the final objective jointly minimizes the language modeling loss, the MIM cross-entropy loss, and the CGA loss.

## Results

With SigLIP 2 and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LaVer improves the average score across 17 benchmarks from 55.72% to 57.87% (+2.15%), with the largest gains on dense visual tasks: OCR-Bench jumps from 536 to 639 (+103, 19.22%) and MMVP from 43.52% to 50.24% (+6.72%). With CLIP, the average improves from 50.58% to 53.24% (+2.66%), and MMVP sees a dramatic +12.00% gain (27.36% → 39.36%). Gains are consistent across all six encoder architectures tested, including the encoder-free MLP variant (+1.37% average).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is against ROSS (Reconstructive Visual Instruction Tuning, ICLR 2025), a reconstruction-based method that reconstructs low-level visual features. Under identical settings (Qwen2-7B-Instruct, LLaVA-558K + Cambrian-737K), LaVer outperforms ROSS on average: +2.91% vs. +2.25% with CLIP and +1.78% vs. +1.45% with SigLIP. The gap is most pronounced on reasoning-heavy benchmarks: MMMU (+4.44% vs. +0.70%) and MMBench (+4.55% vs. +1.41%) with CLIP, and OCR-Bench (+51 vs. +16) with SigLIP. However, ROSS achieves larger gains on MMVP (+12.71% vs. +10.53% with CLIP; +8.60% vs. +5.00% with SigLIP), suggesting that pixel-level reconstruction retains some fine-grained visual detail that latent-space prediction does not fully capture. Internally, the paper also shows that MIM loss alone degrades performance (53.76% vs. 55.72% baseline with SigLIP 2), confirming that the Clipped Gram-Anchoring regularizer is essential to prevent visual feature collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaVer adapts the Masked Image Modeling paradigm from vision pretraining (MAE, iBOT) to the MLLM setting, but with a critical departure: instead of reconstructing pixels or discrete visual tokens in a separate encoder space, it predicts masked vision tokens within the LLM's own latent semantic space using a student-teacher EMA framework borrowed from iBOT. The Clipped Gram-Anchoring regularizer modifies DINOv3's symmetric Gram-Anchoring into an asymmetric form that only penalizes the student for becoming less discriminative than the teacher. The three-stage training recipe follows LLaVA-OneVision 1.5, positioning LaVer as a drop-in Stage-2 objective rather than a new architecture. This contrasts with prior MLLM visual-enhancement methods (contrastive decoding, additional expert modules, low-level pixel reconstruction like ROSS) by providing direct intrinsic supervision in the model's native representation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive visual homogenization finding—where vision tokens become increasingly indistinguishable in deeper layers—gives a concrete mechanistic explanation for modality imbalance that prior work had only characterized behaviorally. The architecture-agnostic nature (consistent gains across SigLIP 2, CLIP, DINOv2, AIMv2, Qwen-ViT, and even an encoder-free MLP) suggests the latent-space MIM objective could be broadly adopted as a standard training component. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or audio modalities, whether the benefits compound at larger model scales (tested only up to 7B), and whether the masking ratio and EMA hyperparameters transfer across domains without retuning. The finding that MIM alone degrades performance without the CGA regularizer highlights that naive self-supervised objectives in MLLMs can be counterproductive, a caution for future work.
