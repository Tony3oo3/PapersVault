## Problem

Traditional video pipelines require full pixel decoding, preprocessing, and early-layer feature extraction for every inference query, creating severe computational redundancy when the same video is queried across multiple downstream tasks. Existing alternatives—Compressed Domain Inference and Video Coding for Machines—are either tightly coupled to specific codec structures or require task-specific encoders that preclude reuse of pretrained RGB backbones. No prior approach provides a single unified representation that serves arbitrary downstream tasks without re-encoding while still supporting human visualization.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a video encoded as an implicit neural representation (NeRV or HNeRV, 3–6 hierarchical decoding blocks) along with a task query specifying the target model and task type. Rather than reconstructing full RGB frames, NVP halts decoding at a task-selected intermediate layer, producing a feature map that is spatially resized and channel-transformed via a Micro Adapter before injection into a pretrained backbone. Outputs are standard task predictions (top-1 class, bounding boxes with mAP, action label, or CLIP-scored caption), and the same INR can simultaneously serve multiple tasks by sharing overlapping intermediate computations.

## Method

NVP encodes videos as INRs and, during an encoding phase, uses Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) to rank all INR-layer-to-backbone-layer pairings, selecting the top-k (e.g., top 10%) candidates per task. For each selected pairing, a lightweight Micro Adapter (1×1 convolution, adding only 0.02–0.96% of backbone parameters) is trained with a composite loss combining MSE, cosine distance, and Huber terms to align INR intermediate features with the target backbone's expected activations. At inference, NVP performs partial decoding—stopping at the task-selected INR layer—applies the adapter, and injects the resulting feature map directly into the corresponding backbone layer, bypassing pixel reconstruction and early backbone stages. The INR can be jointly optimized end-to-end with downstream task losses (weighted reconstruction + task + feature-transfer objectives), shaping the neural representation itself for task accuracy under a fixed bitrate budget. Multi-task serving exploits the hierarchical INR structure: shared intermediate layers are computed once and reused across tasks with overlapping depth requirements.

## Results

NVP reduces end-to-end inference latency by up to 89.46% (CPU) and 62.76% (GPU) compared to H.264-based pipelines, while reducing total FLOPs by up to 89.98% versus full INR decoding. Task accuracy is maintained or improved: ResNet-50 classification reaches 76.74% (vs. 74.59% for H.264), CLIP-RN50 reaches 90.86% (vs. 89.65%), and DETR mAP50 reaches 0.4395 (vs. 0.4436 for H.264). At low bitrates, NVP achieves 7.52% higher top-1 accuracy (CLIP-RN50 at 0.025 bpp) and 10.49% higher accuracy (SlowFast at 0.02 bpp) than H.264, demonstrating superior rate-accuracy trade-offs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the H.264 codec baseline, NVP wins decisively on latency (up to 89.46% reduction on CPU) and FLOPs, while matching or exceeding accuracy (e.g., ResNet-50: 76.74% vs. 74.59%; CLIP-RN50: 90.86% vs. 89.65%). Against full NeRV/HNeRV decoding, NVP achieves up to 73.39% lower latency and 89.98% fewer FLOPs by halting decoding at task-selected intermediate layers rather than reconstructing full pixels. Against machine-centric CDI methods (CoViAR at 172 FPS, MMNet at 10–41 FPS), NVP operates at 833/564 FPS with only 0.42–0.48M parameters versus 58–100M, while supporting multiple tasks from a single representation. Against DeepSVC (10 FPS, 59.3M params), NVP is ~83× faster in FPS and 125× smaller in parameters, and surpasses baseline accuracy at 0.02 bpp where DeepSVC reaches only 95.95% of baseline at 0.0316 bpp. Against VCM approaches (Omnipotent, Compressed Vision), NVP uniquely supports human visualization, end-to-end joint optimization, and task scalability via lightweight adapters (trained in <5 min) rather than full backbone retraining (days on multiple GPUs).

## Relation to Prior Work

NVP repurposes implicit neural representations (NeRV, HNeRV) from their established role as pixel-reconstruction codecs into neural-native feature substrates whose intermediate layers directly feed downstream models. This departs from both Compressed Domain Inference (which is locked to specific codec structures like H.264 motion vectors) and Video Coding for Machines (which requires task-specific encoders and precludes pretrained RGB backbones). The key conceptual leap is recognizing that INR's hierarchical decoding already produces features in the same representational space as CNN backbones, enabling lightweight 1×1-convolution micro-adapters (0.02–0.96% of backbone parameters) to bridge the gap without retraining either side.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NVP addresses the Write-Once-Read-Many workload pattern dominant in VOD, surveillance, and content moderation, where repeated multi-task queries on the same video make decoding the bottleneck. The unified representation eliminates per-task re-encoding and enables hierarchical computation sharing across concurrent tasks, a property neither CDI nor VCM provides. Key open questions include the high one-time INR fitting cost (~30 min for 10 s 1080p video), reduced accuracy on pixel-sensitive tasks like detection, and whether NeRV-style CNN decoders are optimal for transformer backbones. As faster INR variants (E-NeRV, MetaNeRV) mature, the encoding-cost barrier will shrink, potentially making this pipeline architecture broadly deployable.
