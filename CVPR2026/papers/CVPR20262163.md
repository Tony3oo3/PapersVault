## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting achieves fast and high-quality rendering but still struggles to recover accurate geometry without explicit 3D supervision. Existing strategies for improving depth accuracy—multi-view consistency, implicit field gradients, or pretrained geometric priors—either fail to impose constraints directly on individual Gaussians in a geometry-aware manner or rely on assumptions that do not generalize to complex, unseen scenes, leading to artifacts and degraded surface fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262163/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of calibrated multi-view RGB images as input and learns a set of planar 3D Gaussians (with color, opacity, and 2D planar orientation attributes) as the scene representation. From these Gaussians, the method renders per-view depth maps and RGB images via differentiable splatting; the depth maps are fused into a TSDF grid for surface extraction (via zero-level-set), while the RGB renders support novel view synthesis. No ground-truth depth, normals, or additional 3D supervision is required at training time.

## Method

The key idea is a self-constrained prior: a signed distance field f^t represented as a TSDF grid, obtained by fusing depth maps rendered from the current (still-optimizing) 3D Gaussians themselves, requiring no external data-driven model. Around the zero-level set (coarse surface), a narrow band of width controlled by a truncation threshold σ^t is defined, within which three geometry-aware constraints are applied: (1) outlier removal of Gaussians whose interpolated signed distance reaches ±1, (2) an opacity loss L_SCP that pushes opacity toward 1 for on-surface Gaussians and toward 0 for off-surface Gaussians, weighted by inverse distance, and (3) a one-step projection of each Gaussian center along the negative gradient of f^t to pull it onto the surface, treated as a position update rather than a differentiable loss for stability. The prior is periodically re-fused from the most recent depth renders (which are progressively more multi-view consistent), and the band width σ^t is shrunk over iterations in a coarse-to-fine schedule, tightening constraints as the surface estimate converges. Training also includes standard RGB rendering loss, a per-ray depth consistency term, and cross-view normal alignment via homography matrices, all combined with the self-constrained prior loss.

## Results

On the DTU dataset, the method achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.50 mm, outperforming the closest explicit baselines PGSR (0.53) and QGS (0.54), while training in 42 minutes. On NeRF-Synthetic, it attains the best CD_L1 of 1.87×10⁻² and PSNR of 34.21 dB. On Tanks and Temples, it achieves the highest mean F1-score of 0.51, and on Mip-NeRF 360 it leads in SSIM (0.754 outdoor / 0.933 indoor) and LPIPS (0.200 outdoor / 0.155 indoor) among all explicit methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 21 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among explicit 3DGS-based methods, the closest competitors are PGSR (mean CD 0.53 on DTU, F1 0.50 on TNT) and QGS (mean CD 0.54 on DTU, F1 0.50 on TNT); the proposed method improves CD by 0.03–0.04 mm on DTU and F1 by 0.01 on TNT over these. Implicit methods (NeuS, VolSDF, Neuralangelo, NeuralWarp) are consistently worse in geometry (CD 0.61–1.49 on DTU) and require >10 h of training versus ~42 min for the proposed method. On Mip-NeRF 360 rendering, the method matches or exceeds GOF (PSNR 24.81 vs 24.82 outdoor) while achieving better SSIM (0.754 vs 0.750) and LPIPS (0.200 vs 0.202) outdoors, and clearly leads indoors (PSNR 30.56, SSIM 0.933, LPIPS 0.155). Surfel-based methods (2DGS, GSurfels) and opacity-field methods (GOF, GSDF) sacrifice rendering fidelity for geometry, whereas the self-constrained prior maintains both. On TNT, the method ties PGSR on Barn (0.66) and Ignatius (0.80) but wins on Caterpillar (0.43 vs 0.41), Meetingroom (0.31 vs 0.29), and Truck (0.64 vs 0.60), yielding the best mean F1 of 0.51.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for surface reconstruction by introducing a self-constrained prior derived from the Gaussians' own rendered depth maps, fused into a TSDF grid. It departs from prior approaches like GS-Pull and GS-UDF, which jointly learn implicit distance fields alongside Gaussians, by instead extracting the geometric prior purely from rendered depth without additional network learning. Unlike PGSR's multi-view consistency constraints or SuGaR's Poisson-based regularization, the method imposes geometry-aware, per-Gaussian constraints (outlier removal, opacity scheduling, position projection) within a progressively narrowing band, creating a self-reinforcing feedback loop between rendering and geometric constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-constrained prior offers a practical, data-free mechanism to improve 3DGS geometry without the complexity of joint implicit-field optimization or reliance on pretrained depth/normal models that may not generalize to unseen scenes. The periodic update and bandwidth-shrinking strategy provide a clean coarse-to-fine schedule that stabilizes optimization. Open questions include how the method behaves under very sparse view conditions where initial TSDF fusion may be incomplete, whether the approach extends naturally to dynamic or open-surface scenes, and whether the TSDF-based prior can be replaced by lighter-weight representations for real-time applications.
