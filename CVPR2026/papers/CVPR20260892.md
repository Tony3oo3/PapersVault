## Problem

VAE latent spaces used by modern diffusion models encode high-frequency details in a fundamentally view-inconsistent manner, causing 3D Gaussian Splatting optimization in latent space to produce blurred textures and missing fine details. Existing remedies either fine-tune the VAE (degrading its reconstruction quality and breaking compatibility with pretrained diffusion models) or rely on stacked video diffusion (introducing hallucinated content). The paper asks how to recover lost high-frequency details in latent space while keeping the VAE frozen and avoiding hallucinations.

## Input / Output

The method takes N input RGB images (30 for dense, 5 for sparse settings) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, encoded at 960×540 resolution. Each image is passed through a frozen KL-VAE (compression ratio f=8) to produce a latent of shape h×w×d (where h=H/8, w=W/8, d=4). The output is a novel-view RGB image decoded from a refined latent; the intermediate refined latent itself is also a usable output. During refinement, the degraded rendered latent is tiled with V=3 reference view latents into a spatial grid that the diffusion model processes jointly.

## Method

Splatent operates in two stages: first, standard 3DGS optimization reconstructs a latent radiance field from encoded input views, which inherently loses high-frequency content due to multi-view spectral inconsistencies in the VAE; second, a single-step diffusion model (Stable Diffusion Turbo) refines the rendered latent by arranging it in a spatial grid alongside V reference view latents and applying self-attention jointly across all views, propagating high-frequency details from references to the degraded latent. The key insight is to keep the 3D representation in the low-frequency (geometrically consistent) domain and recover high-frequency details in 2D from nearby input views, rather than attempting to enforce 3D consistency on the latents themselves. The VAE remains frozen throughout, preserving its pretrained reconstruction quality. Training is supervised with an L2 latent reconstruction loss plus LPIPS and RGB losses on decoded images, and the framework can be plugged into feed-forward models like MVSplat360 with minimal modification.

## Results

On the DL3DV-10K benchmark (dense, 30 views), Splatent achieves 21.94 PSNR, 0.692 SSIM, 0.265 LPIPS, and 35.60 FID, outperforming LRF (20.19 PSNR) and Feature-3DGS (16.37 PSNR). In the sparse setting (5 views), Splatent reaches 17.44 PSNR on DL3DV-10K, 18.53 on LLFF, and 16.70 on Mip-NeRF360, consistently leading all baselines. For 3D consistency (MEt3R, lower is better), Splatent scores 0.0774 (dense) and 0.0998 (sparse), a 28–30% improvement over LRF. When integrated into the feed-forward MVSplat360 pipeline, Splatent improves PSNR from 16.69 to 17.98 and FID from 13.46 to 11.10.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Splatent outperforms both baselines (Feature-3DGS and LRF) on every metric across all three datasets in both dense and sparse settings. Against Feature-3DGS, the gains are substantial: +5.57 PSNR on DL3DV-10K dense and +2.40 PSNR in the sparse setting, with FID dropping from 263 to 36 (dense) and 308 to 86 (sparse). Against LRF, the closest competitor, Splatent leads by +1.75 PSNR (dense) and +2.10 PSNR (sparse) on DL3DV-10K, and by +2.45 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 sparse. On 3D consistency (MEt3R), Splatent improves over LRF by 28% (dense) and 22% (sparse), confirming that the multi-view attention mechanism produces more geometrically coherent outputs. In the feed-forward setting, integrating Splatent into MVSplat360 yields +1.29 PSNR and −2.37 FID, demonstrating that the refinement module reduces hallucinations while preserving the generative prior's content synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

Splatent builds directly on the latent radiance field line initiated by Feature-3DGS (which adapted 3DGS to feature space) and LRF (which first identified multi-view inconsistencies in VAE latents and addressed them by fine-tuning the VAE). The key conceptual departure is rejecting both the VAE-fine-tuning route (which degrades reconstruction quality) and the video-diffusion route (which hallucinates details): instead, Splatent keeps the VAE frozen and recovers high-frequency details in 2D from reference input views via multi-view self-attention in a single-step diffusion model. This reframes the problem from 'make the 3D representation more consistent' to 'inject 2D context into the rendered latent,' a shift that preserves the generalization capacity of pretrained autoencoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical recipe for high-quality latent-space 3D reconstruction without sacrificing VAE quality, which is important for pipelines that require latent-space compatibility with generative models (e.g., text-to-3D, video diffusion). The grid-based multi-view attention mechanism is architecturally simple and model-agnostic, making it easy to plug into future diffusion backbones. However, the approach remains bounded by the VAE's own reconstruction ceiling, and the authors acknowledge that RGB-space 3DGS is preferable when it suffices. An open question is whether the 2D detail-recovery strategy generalizes to other latent spaces (e.g., discrete VQ-VAE codes) or to video/4D settings where temporal consistency adds another axis of inconsistency.
