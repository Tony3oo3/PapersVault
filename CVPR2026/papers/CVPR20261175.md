## Problem

Existing brain-3D reconstruction methods suffer from two critical shortcomings: (1) brain-3D alignment relies on simple self-attention embeddings paired with CLIP features, failing to capture spatio-temporal representations across diverse brain signal types and multi-scale object features; and (2) multi-view reconstruction uses simple spatial embeddings for viewpoint changes, producing inconsistent poses and artifacts. The paper asks whether 3D Gaussian Splatting can be adapted to decode more natural, viewpoint-consistent 3D objects directly from noisy, sparsely represented brain signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261175/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes subject-specific fMRI volumes (no temporal dimension, single channel) or 64-channel EEG time series (downsampled to 250 Hz) alongside 192-frame video renderings of 3D objects from ShapeNet or Objaverse. It produces a deformable 3D Gaussian field (canonical + per-view offsets for position, rotation, scale, opacity, and spherical harmonics) that renders multi-view images at 155 FPS, and additionally yields object-type and color-type classification scores. The brain signals are normalized per subject, and the 3D stimuli are always presented as video sequences rather than static meshes.

## Method

BrainGS comprises three tightly coupled modules. The Fusion Time-Spatial Network (FTS-Net) encodes brain signals via patch embedding, a spatial ConvFFN stream, and a temporal stream using Riemann-Liouville fractional transforms at three scales (α ∈ {0.4, 0.6, 0.8}), fused by cross-stream attention to yield multi-scale spatio-temporal features. The Multi-Attribute Controller (MAC) decouples object features into visual-semantic (BLIP-2 + CLIP-ViT, aligned to brain features via a synchrony discriminator with cosine-similarity BCE loss), color (a Color MLP with NeRF position encoding trained by InfoNCE), and an Object-aware Enhancement stage that applies cross-channel contrastive learning followed by Hadamard-product gating with self-attended global context. The Multi-View Stabilizer (MVS) tracks viewpoint changes using Trellis 3D landmarks with iterative focal-length and pose optimization, supplements them with optical-flow-based keypoint selection via a Laplacian threshold, and feeds a View-Guided Adjustment network that adaptively modulates view features through subtraction-based discriminative encoding. The Dynamic Object Gaussian Renderer uses a multi-resolution triplane representation to predict canonical Gaussian parameters, then a deformation MLP regresses per-attribute offsets conditioned on view features; training proceeds in two stages (canonical field, then full deformation) with L1 + D-SSIM + LPIPS + cross-modal contrastive losses.

## Results

On fMRI-Shape, BrainGS achieves SOTA across all metrics: 90.8% 2-way top-1 accuracy, 2.936 FPD, 1.517 CD, 3.449 EMD, 0.202 LPIPS, 18.91 PSNR, and 0.815 SSIM, outperforming the strongest baseline MinD-3D++ by 2.4% (2-way), 2.9% (FPD), and 13.7% (LPIPS). On EEG-3D, BrainGS achieves 79.5% 2-way accuracy and 3.774 FPD, surpassing Neuro-3D by 42.4% in 2-way accuracy and 10.5% in FPD. For EEG classification, BrainGS reaches 6.48% top-1 object accuracy and 42.20% top-1 color accuracy, exceeding Neuro-3D by 9.6% and 5.6% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BrainGS outperforms all fMRI-Shape baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest fMRI competitor MinD-3D++ (TPAMI'25), BrainGS improves 2-way accuracy from 0.887 to 0.908 (+2.4%), FPD from 3.025 to 2.936 (−2.9%), and LPIPS from 0.234 to 0.202 (−13.7%), with PSNR jumping from 16.44 to 18.91 (+15.0%). The gap over earlier baselines is larger: MinD-3D (ECCV'24) is beaten by 8.4% in 10-way accuracy and 6.1% in EMD, while LEA-3D and fMRI-PTE-3D trail by over 1.0 in FPD. On EEG-3D, BrainGS dominates Neuro-3D (CVPR'25) by a wide margin: +42.4% in 2-way accuracy (0.795 vs 0.558), −21.6% in CD, and −25.0% in LPIPS. For EEG classification (Table 3), BrainGS surpasses all six EEG-specific baselines (EEGNet, Conformer, BraVL, NICE, EEGPT, Neuro-3D) on both object-type and color-type tasks, with the largest gains over Neuro-3D (+9.6% object top-1, +5.6% color top-1) and over EEGPT (+1.24% object top-1, +7.37% color top-1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain-3D reconstruction line established by Mind-3D [18], Mind-3D++ [20], and Neuro-3D [26], which relied on NeRF or multi-view diffusion for rendering. The key departure is replacing these implicit/diffusion-based renderers with explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting [30], enabling faster training and rendering while maintaining photorealistic quality. The Multi-Attribute Controller and Multi-View Stabilizer are novel architectural components that address two specific gaps in prior brain-3D work: insufficient multi-granularity feature alignment between brain signals and object representations, and inconsistent multi-view pose estimation. The FTS-Net encoder generalizes across fMRI and EEG modalities, whereas prior methods were typically modality-specific.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 3DGS can be effectively driven by noisy, sparsely represented brain signals, this work opens a practical path toward real-time brain-3D decoding (155 FPS inference) that was infeasible with NeRF-based approaches. The multi-view consistency mechanism and the biological interpretability analysis (cortical ROI mapping) strengthen the neuroscientific credibility of the decoded representations. However, OOD performance still degrades notably (FPD rises from 2.936 to 4.950 on APAC), and the method requires paired brain-3D training data, limiting immediate clinical deployment. Open questions include whether the triplane Gaussian representation generalizes to dynamic scenes or multi-object environments, and whether the FTS-Net's fractional temporal transform is truly necessary versus simpler temporal pooling.
