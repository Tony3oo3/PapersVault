## Problem

Video diffusion models process sequences of 50K+ tokens, making quadratic self-attention a prohibitive bottleneck. While linear attention reduces complexity to O(n), fully replacing quadratic attention requires costly pretraining due to a representation gap and the complexity of spatiotemporal video modeling. The paper asks whether efficient, data-free post-training can selectively replace a large fraction of quadratic attention layers with linear attention while preserving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260989/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework operates on pre-trained rectified-flow text-to-video models (Wan1.3B at 480p/16 FPS and Wan14B at 720p/16 FPS, both generating 5-second videos). Training is entirely data-free: 50K input–output pairs are collected by running the original model's own sampling trajectory from random noise, eliminating the need for curated video datasets. The final output is a decoded video produced by a VAE from the latent variable obtained through iterative denoising (50 steps, or 4 steps with optional DMD2 distillation).

## Method

LINVIDEO introduces two key techniques. First, *selective transfer* casts per-layer attention replacement as a binary classification: each layer receives a learnable scalar r that blends quadratic and linear attention (Eq. 8), with a constraint loss enforcing a target count of linear layers and a regularization term (annealed α) driving r toward 0/1 to reduce rounding error. Second, the *anytime distribution matching* (ADM) objective minimizes KL divergence between the linearized model's and original model's sample distributions at every timestep along the sampling trajectory, rather than only at t=0; the score function is estimated by the model itself under rectified-flow parameterization, avoiding the need for an auxiliary multi-step DM. The linear attention kernel uses the Hedgehog design (softmax of projected features concatenated with their negation). An optional DMD2 distillation stage further compresses inference to 4 steps, yielding 15.9–20.9× end-to-end speedup.

## Results

LINVIDEO achieves a 1.43× speedup on Wan1.3B (68.26s vs. 97.32s for FlashAttention2) and 1.71× on Wan14B (1127s vs. 1931s), while matching or slightly exceeding the dense-attention baseline on Overall Consistency (26.52 vs. 26.18 for 1.3B) and Scene Consistency (39.18 vs. 39.14). When combined with DMD2 few-step distillation, the 4-step models deliver a 15.9× speedup (6.11s) on Wan1.3B and 20.9× (92.56s) on Wan14B with only a ~1% drop in VBench scores. On VBench-2.0, LINVIDEO matches FlashAttention2's total score (56.74) for Wan1.3B and comes within 0.23 points for Wan14B (59.62 vs. 59.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LINVIDEO outperforms all four sparse-attention baselines (SVG, SVG2, DFA, XAttn) on both speedup and most VBench quality metrics. On Wan1.3B, LINVIDEO achieves 1.43× speedup versus 1.31× (SVG), 1.15× (SVG2), 1.09× (DFA), and 1.17× (XAttn), while also scoring highest on Overall Consistency (26.52) and Scene Consistency (39.18). On Wan14B, the gap widens: LINVIDEO reaches 1.71× versus 1.61× (SVG), 1.42× (SVG2), 1.51× (XAttn), and 1.40× (DFA). Against the dense-attention baseline FlashAttention2, LINVIDEO trades a small drop in Imaging Quality (66.07 vs. 66.25 for 1.3B; 66.47 vs. 67.89 for 14B) for a substantial latency reduction, while actually matching or exceeding FA2 on Overall Consistency and Scene Consistency for the 1.3B model. The 4-step distilled variant (LINVIDEO+DMD2) sacrifices roughly 1% on most VBench dimensions but delivers 15.9–20.9× end-to-end speedup, a regime no sparse-attention baseline approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work on accelerating video diffusion transformers: sparse attention (SVG, SVG2, DFA, XAttention), which skips redundant computations but retains >50% of quadratic cost, and linear attention (SANA-Video, LinGen, Matten), which achieves O(n) complexity but requires costly pretraining from scratch. LINVIDEO departs from both by proposing a data-free post-training framework that selectively replaces a subset of quadratic attention layers with the Hedgehog linear-attention kernel, using a learnable binary-classification score per layer (selective transfer) and a novel anytime distribution matching (ADM) objective that aligns sample distributions at every timestep rather than only the final output. The optional 4-step distillation stage builds on DMD2, and the score-function estimation in ADM follows the rectified-flow formulation from SIT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.43–1.71× speedup with near-lossless quality, and a 15.9–20.9× speedup after 4-step distillation, all without requiring access to the original training dataset. The data-free design and the finding that progressive, learnable layer conversion substantially outperforms even manual selection of the same layers (Table 3: 26.52 vs. 20.08 Overall Consistency) suggest that the transition dynamics matter as much as the final architecture. Open questions include whether specialized linear-attention kernels (which the authors note they do not use) could further close the gap to dense attention, whether the approach generalizes to architectures beyond Wan/DiT, and how it composes with sparse-attention methods for additional gains.
