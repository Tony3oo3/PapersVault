## Problem

Existing methods for neuronal morphology analysis treat topological structure (via persistent homology) and graph geometry in isolation: graph-based encoders capture local branching patterns but miss global topological invariants, while topology-based descriptors encode spatial arrangements but lose fine-grained structural detail. No prior work has jointly learned a shared representation that exploits the complementary strengths of both views for morphology classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes skeletonized neuronal reconstructions in SWC format (node ID, type, 3D coordinates, radius, parent ID) and internally converts each into two parallel representations: a directed tree graph with 5-dimensional node features (z-score normalized coordinates, radius, path length) and a 112×112×3 RGB persistence image where channels encode unweighted, persistence-weighted, and radius-weighted topological densities. The output is a 128-dimensional shared embedding for contrastive alignment, or a class label via a downstream classification head; for self-supervised evaluation, a frozen k-NN classifier (k=20) is applied to concatenated tree and image embeddings.

## Method

GraPHFormer adapts the CLIP contrastive framework to neuronal morphology with a dual-encoder design: a TreeLSTM performs bottom-up hierarchical aggregation over the skeleton graph, while a DINOv2-ViT-S/14 backbone processes a novel three-channel persistence image generated via radial-distance TMD filtration with a monotonicity constraint and Gaussian kernel density estimation in (birth, persistence) space. A key contribution is persistence-space augmentation—birth-death jittering, persistence scaling, and radius perturbation applied directly to topological feature vectors before image generation—preserving the semantic correspondence between pixel positions and topological features that standard image augmentations would corrupt. Both encoders project through 2-layer MLP heads into a shared 128-dim space trained with symmetric InfoNCE loss over within-batch negatives, requiring no memory queues or explicit negative mining. For supervised downstream tasks, projection heads are removed, embeddings are concatenated, and a classification head is trained via linear probing followed by end-to-end fine-tuning.

## Results

Under self-supervised learning (frozen k-NN), GraPHFormer achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on five of six benchmarks: 86.2±1% on BIL-6, 72.7±3% on JML-4, 83.8±0.6% on N7, 75.5±1.7% on M1-Cell, and 76.5±1% on M1-REG, trailing only on ACT-4 (59.1±4%). Under supervised fine-tuning, it reaches 93.51% on BIL-6, 92.3% on N7, 76.5% on JML-4, 76.5% on M1-Cell, and 79.1% on M1-REG, again with the sole exception of ACT-4 (65.5%). The image-only persistence-image baseline (PI) lags the full multimodal model by 7–13 points, confirming the necessity of graph-structure fusion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraPHFormer outperforms SGTMorph [28] by 4.6 points on BIL-6 (93.51 vs 88.9) and 4.1 points on JML-4 (76.5 vs 72.4) under supervised learning, and by 4.9 points on BIL-6 (86.2 vs 81.3) and 6.1 points on JML-4 (72.7 vs 66.6) under self-supervised learning. Against MorphRep [9], which benefits from large-scale pretraining, GraPHFormer still leads on BIL-6 (93.51 vs 88.0 SL; 86.2 vs 81.0 SS) and N7 (92.3 vs – SL; 83.8 vs – SS), but trails on ACT-4 (65.5 vs 77.0 SL; 59.1 vs 66.0 SS), where cortical-layer classification demands absolute spatial coordinates that the translation-invariant TMD encoder abstracts away. Compared to TreeMoCo [5], the closest graph-only contrastive baseline, GraPHFormer gains 7.96 points on BIL-6 (93.51 vs 85.55) and 12.0 points on N7 (92.3 vs 80.3 SS) under supervised settings. In the self-supervised regime, GraPHFormer surpasses GraphDINO [30] by 7.2 points on BIL-6 (86.2 vs 79) and 15.5 points on N7 (83.8 vs 68.3), and outperforms MACGNN [33] by 16.7 points on BIL-6 (86.2 vs 69.5). The image-only PI baseline (DINOv2 on persistence images alone) consistently underperforms the full model by 7–13 points, validating that graph-structural features are essential and that the two modalities are genuinely complementary rather than redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of research on neuronal morphology: graph-based encoders (TreeLSTM, GraphDINO, SGTMorph) that capture local branching geometry, and topological data analysis (TMD, persistence images) that captures global branching invariants. The key architectural departure is adapting the CLIP contrastive learning paradigm to align these two modalities in a shared embedding space, which to the authors' knowledge is the first such application to neuronal morphology. It also extends the original TMD persistence image by introducing a three-channel encoding (unweighted, persistence-weighted, radius-weighted) and proposes persistence-space augmentation that operates on topological feature vectors rather than pixel space, preserving the semantic correspondence between coordinates and topological meaning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides neuroscientists with a label-efficient tool for quantitative morphology analysis, achieving SOTA on five of six benchmarks in both self-supervised and supervised settings, with practical demonstrations for glial discrimination across species and cortical regions. A notable open question is why ACT-4 (cortical layer classification) remains unsolved: the translation-invariant TMD and graph encoders deliberately discard absolute spatial coordinates, which the authors identify as critical for laminar position. The modest fusion gains (+1.7–1.8%) despite very low cross-modal correlation (Pearson < 0.09) suggest that simple concatenation is insufficient and that adaptive, cross-modal attention fusion is needed to fully exploit the demonstrated complementarity. Future directions include incorporating 3D volumetric and EM-derived modalities and releasing an open multimodal benchmark.
