## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation (TTA) methods overwhelmingly focus on updating normalization layer parameters (affine weights, running statistics) to handle distribution shift, treating activation functions as fixed, immutable components. This overlooks the fact that zero-centered activations suppress informative responses and cause vanishing gradients when feature distributions shift away from the source domain, limiting the model's ability to self-correct during inference.

## Input / Output

The method operates on corrupted RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100-C, 224×224 for ImageNet-C at severity level 5) and outputs class labels. Adaptation is performed online in a streaming fashion during inference without access to source data, and the activation module is objective-agnostic: it can be plugged into any existing TTA framework (TENT, EATA, SAR, ROID, CMF, DeYO) without modifying their optimization objectives or update rules.

## Method

AcTTA reparameterizes standard activation functions (ReLU, GELU, Swish) into a learnable form g(x) = φ(x−c) + [λ_neg + (λ_pos − λ_neg)σ(β(x−c))](x−c), where c shifts the activation boundary to re-center on target-domain feature statistics, and λ_pos/λ_neg independently control the asymptotic gradient slopes in the positive and negative regions. When initialized with λ_neg = λ_pos = 0 and c = 0, the formulation exactly recovers the original pretrained activation, ensuring compatibility. During test-time adaptation, only these three per-channel activation parameters are updated (optionally alongside normalization parameters), enabling the network to adjust its nonlinear response and preserve nonzero gradients under distribution shift without modifying any network weights. The method is modular and backbone-dependent: for BN-based CNNs, freezing normalization and adapting only activation parameters yields best results, while for LN-based ViTs, jointly updating both normalization and activation parameters is more effective.

## Results

AcTTA reduces classification error across all three corruption benchmarks (severity 5) when plugged into existing TTA objectives. On CIFAR-10-C (WRN-28), AcTTA_TENT achieves 17.03% error vs. TENT's 18.51%, and AcTTA_CMF reaches 16.39% vs. CMF's 17.36%. On ImageNet-C (ViT-B/16), AcTTA_TENT drops error from 53.85% to 51.79%, and AcTTA_ROID from 52.91% to 49.89%. The largest gains appear on ImageNet-C with GN-based ResNet-50, where AcTTA_ROID cuts error from 64.39% to 55.87% (−8.5 pp).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AcTTA consistently outperforms all six normalization-based TTA baselines (TENT, ETA, SAR, ROID, CMF, DeYO) when integrated as a plug-in, with the largest margins on ImageNet-C: AcTTA_ROID reduces ResNet-50 (GN) error from 64.39% to 55.87% (−8.5 pp) and AcTTA_CMF from 64.30% to 55.46% (−8.8 pp). Against learnable activation baselines (Table 3), AcTTA beats PReLU (18.06% vs. 29.62% on WRN-28), ACON (18.06% vs. 18.46%), and PAU (18.06% vs. 34.50%) on CIFAR-10-C, and also on ViT-B/16 (52.37% vs. 53.87%, 52.92%, 99.96% respectively). In the continual adaptation setting (Table 7), AcTTA_TENT achieves a mean error of 34.89% on CIFAR-100-C corruptions, outperforming CoTTA (38.22%) and TENT-continual (40.87%), while also preserving source-domain accuracy (26.34% vs. 34.38% for CoTTA). The one exception is AcTTA_SAR on ViT-B/16 (54.17% vs. SAR's 53.90%), a marginal 0.27 pp loss, and AcTTA_ROID/CMF on ResNet-50 (BN) where the gains are smaller (0.4–0.9 pp) compared to the dramatic improvements seen with GN. Overall, AcTTA wins or ties in the vast majority of head-to-head comparisons across all 10 baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant normalization-centric paradigm in test-time adaptation (TTA), where methods like TENT, EATA, SAR, ROID, and CMF primarily update BatchNorm/LayerNorm affine parameters and running statistics. It draws conceptually from the dynamic activation function literature (ACON, PAU, PReLU), which explored learnable nonlinearities during training, but repurposes these ideas for the source-free, inference-time setting. The method is explicitly designed as a plug-in module that integrates with existing TTA objectives (entropy minimization, consistency regularization, etc.) without modifying their optimization schemes, positioning activation reparameterization as an orthogonal adaptation axis rather than a replacement for normalization updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the near-universal assumption in TTA that normalization layer updates are the primary mechanism for handling distribution shift, showing that activation-level modulation can complement or even supersede them (particularly for BN-based CNNs, where freezing BN and adapting only activations outperforms the conventional approach). This opens a new design space for TTA methods and suggests that the field's focus on affine parameter recalibration may have been overly narrow. However, the paper acknowledges that optimal design choices (which layers to adapt, which parameters to update, learning rate scaling) remain architecture-dependent and lack a principled selection strategy, limiting the method's universality. The continual forgetting analysis, while conceptually important, is supported by a single dataset and limited baselines, leaving open whether activation-level constraints genuinely mitigate long-horizon drift in more diverse deployment scenarios.
