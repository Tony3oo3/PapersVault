## Problem

Vision-Language Models for video reasoning rely on static initial frames, preventing them from actively gathering new visual evidence as reasoning unfolds. Existing frame-augmented CoT methods suffer from two compounding issues: CoT quality is often suboptimal because traces are VLM-generated without human verification, and none can synthesize hypothetical or counterfactual visual scenarios that are absent from the source video.

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of initial video frames sampled at 1 fps, a text question, and answer choices. During inference, it may trigger a retrieval call (TFVTG searching the full video at 3 fps) or a generation call (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large conditioned on a retrieved frame and a text prompt), inserting the resulting frame into the reasoning trace. The final output is a complete interleaved text-frame CoT ending with a free-form answer, produced over two sequential generation rounds.

## Method

ACT2SEE fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking via LoRA on a curated SFT dataset of interleaved video-text CoTs. The dataset is constructed by prompting Gemini 2.5 Pro with a template that instructs it to emit specialized <retrieve> or <generate> tool-call tokens with natural-language queries whenever existing visual context is insufficient; an offline retrieval model (TFVTG/BLIP-2) or conditional image-to-image generator (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large) then produces the new frame, which is spliced into the trace before a second round completes the reasoning. Quality is enforced by filtering generated CoTs against human-annotated ground-truth traces from MINERVA, CausalVQA, and Social Genome using BGE M3-Embedding similarity (>80%), yielding 3,373 samples of which 47% contain a retrieved or generated frame. At inference, the trained model autonomously decides when to invoke retrieval or generation, exhibiting an emergent active-perception capability that was not explicitly programmed but learned from the SFT traces.

## Results

ACT2SEE achieves 74.2% accuracy on Video-MME, 46.8% on VideoEspresso, 51.3% on EgoNormia, 47.1% on VCR-Bench, and 63.3% on ViTIB, all at 1 fps. It outperforms its base model Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking on all five benchmarks (e.g., 74.2 vs. 71.8 on Video-MME, 47.1 vs. 38.2 on VCR-Bench) and surpasses GPT-4o on VideoEspresso (46.8 vs. 26.4) while using only 3 frames versus GPT-4o's 1 fps sampling. On ViTIB, ACT2SEE (63.3%) also exceeds Gemini 2.5 Pro (53.9%), and on VCR-Bench it beats Gemini 2.5 Pro (47.1 vs. 61.3 is a loss, but beats GPT-4o's 46.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against same-size open-source VLMs, ACT2SEE shows large margins: 46.8 vs. 35.5 on VideoEspresso over Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 51.3 vs. 13.0 on EgoNormia over InternVL2.5-8B, and 47.1 vs. 33.0 on VCR-Bench over InternVL2.5-8B. Versus its own base model Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking, SFT with interleaved CoTs yields consistent gains of 2.4–8.9 points across all five benchmarks. Against closed-source frontier models, ACT2SEE surpasses GPT-4o on VideoEspresso (46.8 vs. 26.4) and VCR-Bench (47.1 vs. 46.9), and beats Gemini 2.5 Pro on ViTIB (63.3 vs. 53.9), though it trails Gemini on Video-MME (74.2 vs. 84.3) and EgoNormia (51.3 vs. 64.7). Among video-reasoning CoT methods, ACT2SEE (74.2% on Video-MME) outperforms Video-R1 (61.4%), Chain-of-Shot (64.4%), and FrameMind (60.9%), but is slightly behind the concurrent Chain-of-Frames (75.3%). In head-to-head comparisons with RL-based interleaving methods, ACT2SEE beats ReWatch-R1 on VCR-Bench (42.2 vs. 39.6 at matched base model) and FrameMind on Video-MME (65.8 vs. 60.9 at matched base model), and outperforms inference-only ViTCoT on both ViTIB (63.3 vs. 59.9) and Video-MME (74.2 vs. 70.2) at the same base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACT2SEE extends the active-perception line of work in video reasoning (FrameMind, ReWatch-R1, ViTCoT) by unifying frame retrieval and frame generation within a single SFT-trained CoT, rather than relying on RL or inference-only keyframe insertion. It directly follows ReWatch-R1's two-round retrieval algorithm format but departs from it in two key ways: (1) it adds a generation pathway (via Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large) to handle counterfactual/hypothetical scenarios that no prior method could visually synthesize, and (2) it uses SFT on human-annotation-filtered data rather than RL or synthetic rewatching traces. The data construction pipeline is informed by the finding that VLM-generated CoTs without human verification degrade model performance, a concern raised by ReWatch-R1 and VideoEspresso.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The emergent ability of an 8B model to autonomously decide when to retrieve or generate visual evidence during reasoning is a practical step toward agentic video understanding, and the counterfactual generation pathway opens a path to causal video reasoning that purely retrieval-based methods cannot address. However, generation quality remains imperfect (e.g., incorrect object placement in Figure 4), and the approach depends on external retrieval (TFVTG) and generation (SD 3.5) tools that add inference latency. Open questions include whether the active-perception behavior generalizes to longer videos or more complex multi-step counterfactuals, and whether RL fine-tuning on top of the SFT could further sharpen the retrieval/generation decision boundary.
