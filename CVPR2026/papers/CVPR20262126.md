## Problem

Most missing-modality learning methods assume a Shared Missing Rate (SMR) where all modalities are dropped with equal probability, but realistic systems face Imbalanced Missing Rates (IMR) where each modality is absent with different probabilities. Under IMR, representation distributions are distorted by uneven exposure and gradients are dominated by frequently observed modalities, causing biased convergence and performance instability that prior alignment- or generation-based methods do not address.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes per-sample audio, visual, and lexical feature vectors along with binary availability indicators produced by a stochastic masking operator parameterized by a per-modality missing-rate vector. Missing modalities are represented as zero vectors, and the method is designed as a plug-in that preserves the dimensionality of each unimodal feature so it can be inserted before any existing backbone's encoder without architectural changes. The output is a standard emotion class probability distribution over the label set, produced by the backbone's prediction head after fusion.

## Method

BALM comprises two complementary plug-in modules. The Feature Calibration Module (FCM) computes per-modality global descriptors by averaging available features, concatenates them through a shared FC layer to extract cross-modal context, then projects this context into modality-specific calibration weights applied via sigmoid-gated element-wise rescaling, establishing a shared representation basis across heterogeneous missing patterns. The Gradient Rebalancing Module (GRM) introduces lightweight two-layer unimodal prediction heads whose gradients are used as proxies for modality-specific optimization; it applies distribution-driven modulation by tracking KL-divergence progress between unimodal and multimodal predictions to compute per-modality attenuation coefficients, and spatial-driven modulation by measuring cosine similarity between unimodal and multimodal prediction-head gradients to softly reorient updates toward a balanced direction. The overall loss combines the task loss with a weighted spatial-alignment regularization term, and the entire framework is model-agnostic, requiring no modification to the backbone's encoder, fusion, or prediction layers.

## Results

On IEMOCAP under six IMR configurations, BALM consistently improves all backbones: MMDFN+BALM achieves up to 68.76% Acc and 68.25% w-F1 (on (0.5,0.3,0.7)), gaining +6.10% Acc over the original MMDFN under (0.5,0.7,0.3). MMGCN+BALM reaches 65.37% Acc (on (0.5,0.3,0.7)) with a peak +4.50% Acc gain, while GCNet+BALM gains up to +3.27% Acc. On CMU-MOSEI, gains are smaller but steady (up to +1.71% Acc for MMGCN+BALM), reflecting the dataset's lower sensitivity to missing-rate variation. Across all settings, BALM raises the performance lower-bound of both MER backbones above 60% for both Acc and w-F1 on IEMOCAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BALM outperforms all 10 baselines across both datasets and all six IMR configurations. Against missing-modality methods (MMIN, SDR-GNN, Mi-CGA, MoMKE, GCNet), GCNet+BALM leads by +3.0 to +6.2% Acc over the Group 1 mean, with the largest gap on (0.3,0.5,0.7) where MMIN scores only 55.97% Acc versus 61.31% for GCNet+BALM. Against the imbalance-oriented method Ada2I, BALM-equipped backbones surpass it by 2–6% Acc in most settings, confirming that gradient balancing alone is insufficient when missingness is heterogeneous. The two IMR-specific baselines (RedCore, MCE) perform worst overall (43–50% Acc on IEMOCAP), and BALM exceeds them by 10–17% Acc. Among the typical MER backbones, MMDFN+BALM achieves the strongest absolute results (up to 68.76% Acc), gaining +6.10% Acc and +7.51% w-F1 over the original MMDFN under (0.5,0.7,0.3), while MMGCN+BALM gains +4.50% Acc under the same setting. On CMU-MOSEI, the margins shrink (0.1–1.7% Acc) but remain consistently positive, with MMGCN+BALM reaching 84.84% Acc under (0.7,0.3,0.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

BALM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: incomplete multimodal learning (alignment-based methods like GCNet and SDR-GNN, generation-based methods like MMIN and MoMKE) and imbalanced multimodal learning (gradient modulation approaches like Ada2I and RedCore). Prior missing-modality methods largely assume a Shared Missing Rate where all modalities are dropped with equal probability, while imbalance-oriented methods assume all modalities are present. BALM departs from both by targeting the more realistic Imbalanced Missing Rate (IMR) setting—where each modality has a distinct missing probability—and addresses it through a combination of squeeze-and-excitation-style feature calibration (borrowed from SE-Net) and classifier-guided gradient modulation (following Guo et al.), packaged as a model-agnostic plug-in that requires no architectural changes to the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IMR setting is arguably more realistic than the widely-used SMR assumption, yet prior methods show unstable and inconsistent behavior under it, as demonstrated by the large performance swings in Fig. 2. BALM's plug-in design lowers the adoption barrier: practitioners can attach it to any existing MER backbone (early- or late-fusion) without retraining from a new architecture. However, the evaluation is confined to trimodal MER on two datasets (IEMOCAP, CMU-MOSEI), and the authors themselves note that broader multimodal tasks and large-scale pretrained backbones remain unexplored. An open question is whether the gradient-rebalancing mechanism generalizes to settings with more than three modalities or to tasks where modality roles are less asymmetric than in emotion recognition.
