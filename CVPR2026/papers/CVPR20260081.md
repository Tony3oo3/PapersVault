## Problem

Event-based stereo matching is severely constrained by the scarcity of annotated training data: existing datasets (DSEC, M3ED, MVSEC) are orders of magnitude smaller than their frame-based counterparts, and their ground truth relies on LiDAR sensors that produce sparse, noisy, and expensive annotations. No prior work has explored generating event stereo training data from the abundant, cheap color image collections already available in the community.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260081/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The trained event stereo networks consume a stereo event pair (left + right) encoded as a 3-channel Tencode tensor (or VoxelGrid for EMatch), where each event (x, y, t, p) is mapped to a spatial pixel with polarity and normalized temporal channels, and output a dense disparity map. The data generation pipeline itself takes sparse multi-view RGB images (e.g., 640×480 or 1280×720) and produces synthetic event stereo pairs at matching resolutions with proxy disparity labels, or takes calibrated RGB-event stereo pairs and produces proxy disparity labels for the event side via geometric reprojection.

## Method

EventHub combines two complementary data generation pipelines: (i) a novel-view-synthesis factory that uses SVRaster to build a radiance field from sparse RGB images, renders novel views along virtual camera trajectories (local translations or global cubic-spline paths), converts consecutive rendered frames into event streams via ESIM with motion-adaptive temporal stepping, and extracts proxy depth from the rendered radiance field with multi-regularizer optimization; and (ii) a cross-modal distillation pipeline that runs an RGB stereo foundation model (FoundationStereo or StereoAnywhere) on paired color images and transfers the predicted disparity to the event domain through back-projection, coordinate transformation, and re-projection. The key architectural insight is repurposing pre-trained RGB stereo foundation models into event stereo networks by encoding events as 3-channel Tencode tensors to match the original input channel count, then fine-tuning from the RGB pre-trained weights with a frozen monocular depth prior. This eliminates all dependence on LiDAR annotations while producing training data that yields superior cross-domain generalization compared to LiDAR-supervised baselines.

## Results

On the out-of-domain M3ED dataset, models trained with EventHub MIX 4 data reduce MAE by up to 50% compared to their LiDAR-supervised counterparts: E-FoundationStereo drops from 2.89 to 0.98 MAE (Day) and E-StereoAnywhere from 3.26 to 1.12 MAE (Day). On MVSEC, the improvement is even more dramatic, with E-FoundationStereo going from 6.04 to 1.33 MAE (Day) and E-StereoAnywhere from 7.85 to 1.96 MAE (Day). In-domain on DSEC, MIX 4 approaches the LiDAR upper bound (e.g., E-FoundationStereo: 0.87 vs. 0.60 MAE), confirming that proxy labels nearly match ground-truth supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's central comparison is not between different architectures but between training protocols applied to the same four event stereo models (SE-CFF, EMatch, E-FoundationStereo, E-StereoAnywhere). Across all four architectures, EventHub MIX 4 training consistently outperforms LiDAR ground-truth training on out-of-domain M3ED and MVSEC: for example, EMatch drops from 12.22 to 2.23 MAE on M3ED Day (an 82% reduction), and E-FoundationStereo drops from 6.04 to 1.33 MAE on MVSEC Day (a 78% reduction). The only case where LiDAR training wins is in-domain on DSEC, where it achieves slightly lower MAE (e.g., 0.60 vs. 0.87 for E-FoundationStereo), as expected since the evaluation ground truth is itself LiDAR-derived. MIX 3 (proxy labels on DSEC only, no additional NVS data) already surpasses LiDAR on most out-of-domain splits, indicating that the quality of supervision (dense proxy vs. sparse LiDAR) matters more than the quantity of training data. The paper also shows that LiDAR-free alternatives (photometric loss, EV-SceneFlow synthetic events) are far inferior, with MAE never dropping below 3 pixels in-domain, confirming that EventHub's proxy-label strategy is essential.

## Relation to Prior Work

EventHub extends the NeRF-supervised deep stereo paradigm (Tosi et al., CVPR 2023) from the RGB domain to event-based stereo, which had no equivalent data-generation pipeline. It combines SVRaster for radiance-field rendering, ESIM for frame-to-event simulation, and cross-modal distillation from RGB stereo foundation models (FoundationStereo, StereoAnywhere) into a unified data factory. The key departure from prior event stereo work (SE-CFF, EMatch) is the complete elimination of LiDAR supervision: instead of relying on sparse, noisy active-sensor annotations, the paper synthesizes proxy events and labels from monocular RGB sequences or distills them from paired RGB-event data. The repurposing strategy—encoding events into 3-channel Tencode tensors and fine-tuning pre-trained RGB stereo ViTs—directly bridges the two modalities without architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the event vision community, which has been severely constrained by the scarcity of LiDAR-annotated event stereo data (existing datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than RGB stereo ones). By showing that NVS-generated proxy data plus cross-modal distillation can match or exceed LiDAR supervision in generalization, EventHub democratizes training data generation and removes a major bottleneck. The bi-directional knowledge transfer—where event-trained models produce proxy labels that improve RGB foundation models in nighttime conditions—suggests a broader paradigm for cross-modal mutual improvement. Open questions include scalability to dynamic scenes (the NVS pipeline assumes static scenes), the computational cost of per-scene radiance-field optimization, and whether the approach extends to other event-based tasks beyond stereo.
