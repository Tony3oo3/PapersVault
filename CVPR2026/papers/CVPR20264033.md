## Problem

Establishing dense semantic correspondence between sparse line drawings and texture-rich photographs remains difficult because existing diffusion features are heavily biased toward texture and appearance, producing 'feature voids' on sketches. The paper identifies two specific gaps that prior methods fail to address simultaneously: spatial domain misalignment caused by the structural abstraction of sketches, and frequency domain inconsistencies arising from the sparse mid-frequency content of line drawings versus the broad spectral energy of textured images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean sparse line drawing and a texture-rich image (photo or stylized variant) of the same object as input, conditioned on a shared text prompt of the form 'a photo of a [class]'. It produces dense keypoint correspondence between the two images, evaluated via PCK at multiple tolerance thresholds. Internally, the pipeline consumes multi-scale feature maps from both a Unified CleanDIFT extractor and a DINOv2 backbone, and the LoFFA module operates on these at multiple spatial resolutions with channel dimensions C_l × H_l × W_l per layer.

## Method

SFA-DIFT is a two-stage spatial-frequency alignment framework. Stage 1 (Unified CleanDIFT) fine-tunes a pre-trained CleanDIFT U-Net via LoRA adapters on all linear projection layers, using a teacher-student objective where a frozen SD model processes noisy inputs at random timesteps while the LoRA-enhanced student processes clean inputs at a fixed timestep (t′=261); the loss maximizes cosine similarity between timestep-conditioned projected student features and teacher target features, yielding temporally invariant clean diffusion features that map both modalities into a shared semantic space. Stage 2 (LoFFA) takes multi-scale features from Unified CleanDIFT and DINOv2, applies AdaIN to align the image feature distribution to the sketch's, then performs a two-level Discrete Wavelet Transform to isolate the LL sub-band, enhances it via a sigmoid-gated learnable attention mask, and reconstructs via IDWT with learnable blending weights; the module is trained with a CLIP-style symmetric contrastive loss on keypoint-sampled features plus a dense matching loss using differentiable Soft-Argmax over full similarity maps.

## Results

On PSC6K, SFA-DIFT achieves 9.81% PCK@1, 72.94% PCK@5, and 92.70% PCK@10, setting a new state-of-the-art for sketch-photo dense correspondence. On the multi-style MS-PSC6K benchmark, it averages 8.70% PCK@1, 69.21% PCK@5, and 91.02% PCK@10 across five artistic styles. The method improves over the previous best supervised baseline (CleanDIFT+DINO†) by 0.37% on PCK@1, 2.63% on PCK@5, and 1.71% on PCK@10 on PSC6K, and by an average of 0.87%, 2.20%, and 0.95% respectively across all five styles on MS-PSC6K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFA-DIFT outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest zero-shot methods, it leads DINOv3-ViT-B/16 (5.98/56.11/83.05) by +3.83/+16.83/+9.65 percentage points on PCK@1/@5/@10, and surpasses CleanDIFT+DINO* (5.73/55.47/83.81) by +4.08/+17.47/+8.89. Among supervised competitors, the closest rival is CleanDIFT+DINO† (9.44/70.25/90.99), which SFA-DIFT edges out by 0.37/2.63/1.71 points; SketchFusion† (–/70.31/89.86) trails by 2.63/2.84 on PCK@5/@10; and Self-Sup† (5.33/58.02/84.87) lags by 4.48/14.92/7.83. On the stylistically diverse MS-PSC6K, SFA-DIFT maintains consistent superiority across all five styles (Abstract, Realism, Baroque, Neo-Impressionism, Post-Impressionism), while prior supervised methods like SketchFusion† and Self-Sup† degrade substantially (e.g., SketchFusion† drops to ~4.5–5.0 PCK@1 vs. SFA-DIFT's 8.5–9.0), indicating that existing approaches fail to generalize under texture and style perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CleanDIFT line of work (which extracts noise-free diffusion features for correspondence) by adding LoRA-based unsupervised fine-tuning on sketch data to close the spatial-domain gap between sparse lines and textured photos. It departs from prior cross-modal correspondence methods (SketchFusion, Self-Sup) that address the modality gap through high-level semantic modulation or self-supervised training, by introducing an explicit frequency-domain alignment stage (LoFFA with DWT) that operates on low-frequency sub-bands. The dual-domain framing—spatial alignment via unified clean features plus frequency alignment via wavelet-based aggregation—represents a conceptual departure from methods that treat the modality gap as purely a spatial/semantic problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of MS-PSC6K with five artistic style variations provides a more rigorous stress test for cross-sparsity correspondence, revealing that existing SOTA methods degrade or fail to converge when re-trained on heterogeneous style data. The finding that explicit low-frequency aggregation via DWT yields consistent (if modest) gains over purely spatial alignment strategies offers a principled recipe for handling modality gaps with different spectral profiles. However, the 0.8-second inference cost per correspondence estimation and the observation that the full SFA-DIFT actually shows slightly lower robustness ratio than Unified CleanDIFT alone raise open questions about whether diffusion backbones are the most efficient path for this task, and whether the frequency-domain gains generalize beyond the sketch-photo setting.
