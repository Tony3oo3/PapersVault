## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models for image super-resolution are locked into a rigid, size-specific design: they always start generation from a fixed 1×1 token and must memorize a unique absolute scaling schedule for each target resolution, requiring a separate model per size. Additionally, as autoregressive models trained with teacher forcing, they suffer from exposure bias that degrades inference quality. The paper argues that the 1×1 start token is a legacy constraint from image synthesis that is suboptimal for image restoration, where the LQ input already provides strong structural priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image of arbitrary size and aspect ratio (trained on LQ crops from 64×64 to 128×128, evaluated at various resolutions up to 512×512) and produces a corresponding high-quality RGB image at the target resolution. A single model handles all input sizes without resizing, unlike prior VAR-based methods that require a resize-and-resize-back procedure. The LQ image is consumed through a degradation-robust encoder that yields both conditioning prefix tokens and a dynamic multi-scale token map that serves as the generative starting point, supplemented by a text prompt derived from the LQ reconstruction.

## Method

DVAR introduces two dynamic components on top of a Switti VAR backbone fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16). First, a canonical scaling dynamic replaces fixed per-resolution schedules: a single canonical scale sequence defined at a base resolution is proportionally rescaled for any target size, and generation begins from an LQ-derived dynamic token map rather than a static 1×1 token, so early scales stabilize global structure while later scales refine details. Second, a Dynamic Sampling Scheduler mitigates exposure bias by exploiting the geometric proximity of VQ-VAE codebook tokens: it maintains an EMA 'count book' of neighbor-rank distributions per scale and samples plausible prediction errors via a zero-cost k-NN lookup, injecting realistic noise into training without an extra forward pass. A degradation-robust encoder (finetuned VAE encoder) extracts LQ features, and a size embedding is injected through cross-attention to make the Transformer explicitly size-aware.

## Results

DVAR achieves state-of-the-art no-reference IQA scores on all three benchmarks: CLIPIQA of 0.7405 (DIV2K-Val), 0.7050 (RealSR), and 0.7351 (DRealSR), and MANIQA of 0.5641, 0.5756, and 0.5653 respectively, outperforming all GAN, diffusion, and prior AR baselines. It consistently ranks second on MUSIQ (70.58, 70.54, 67.44), trailing only VARSR by a narrow margin. On reference-based perceptual metrics, DVAR surpasses VARSR on LPIPS (0.3192 vs 0.3260 on DIV2K-Val), DISTS (0.2103 vs 0.2218), and FID (30.16 vs 35.51) across all datasets, while reporting slightly lower PSNR/SSIM due to the inherent tokenization error of its VQ-based design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GAN-based methods (BSRGAN, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR-GAN), DVAR dominates on all perceptual metrics: CLIPIQA improves by 0.19–0.21 over the best GAN, MANIQA by 0.19–0.22, and MUSIQ by 7–14 points, while also achieving lower LPIPS and DISTS. Against diffusion-based methods (StableSR, SeeSR, SUPIR, OSEDiff, PiSA-SR), DVAR wins on CLIPIQA and MANIQA across all three benchmarks (e.g., CLIPIQA 0.7405 vs. 0.7279 for SUPIR on DIV2K-Val; MANIQA 0.5641 vs. 0.5598), and is competitive on MUSIQ (70.58 vs. 69.68 for PiSA-SR), though it trails on PSNR/SSIM due to VQ tokenization. Against the closest AR baseline VARSR, DVAR achieves lower LPIPS (0.3192 vs. 0.3260), lower DISTS (0.2103 vs. 0.2218), lower FID (30.16 vs. 35.51), and higher CLIPIQA (0.7405 vs. 0.7347) and MANIQA (0.5641 vs. 0.5340) on DIV2K-Val, with similar margins on RealSR and DRealSR. The one metric where DVAR trails VARSR is MUSIQ (70.58 vs. 71.27 on DIV2K-Val), a gap of less than 1 point. Overall, DVAR establishes a new perceptual-quality frontier for purely autoregressive ISR while maintaining competitive fidelity metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVAR extends the next-scale prediction paradigm introduced by VAR (Tian et al., 2024) and its ISR adaptation VARSR (Qu et al., 2025) by replacing two inherited rigidities: the fixed 1×1 start token and the memorized absolute scaling schedule. Conceptually, the paper reframes ISR as a refinement task rather than a synthesis task, arguing that the LQ input provides sufficient structural priors to eliminate the need for a minimal start token. The backbone is Switti (Voronov et al., 2024), fine-tuned via LoRA, with the degradation-robust encoder following SUPIR's (Yu et al., 2024) adaptation strategy. The dynamic sampling scheduler offers a lightweight alternative to scheduled sampling (Bengio et al., 2015) by exploiting the geometric structure of VQ-VAE codebooks rather than requiring additional forward passes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling relative scaling logic from absolute target size, DVAR removes a practical deployment barrier that made VAR-based ISR methods require a separate model per resolution — a significant step toward unified, size-agnostic restoration. The dynamic sampling scheduler's use of k-NN codebook proximity to simulate prediction errors is a clean, low-overhead technique that could transfer to other discrete autoregressive tasks. However, the VQ-based design still lags diffusion methods on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR/SSIM), and the approach inherits Switti's architectural constraints; whether the canonical scaling dynamic generalizes to substantially larger resolutions or non-square aspect ratios beyond the tested range remains to be validated at scale.
