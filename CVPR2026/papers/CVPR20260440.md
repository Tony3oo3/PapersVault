## Problem

Existing LLM-based zero-shot VLN-CE methods perform naive, unidirectional reasoning without feedback mechanisms such as reviewing historical errors or predicting future outcomes. This causes agents to suffer from decision hallucination and continuous failure on tasks where an initial navigation error occurs, leaving the full reasoning potential of LLMs unrealized for embodied navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260440/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives 12 panoramic RGB images and corresponding depth maps (sampled at 0°, 30°, …, 330°) plus a natural-language instruction for the episode; no pre-built maps are available. A standard waypoint predictor first generates candidate viewpoint images, and the LLM then outputs a selected viewpoint ID, a free-form reasoning trace, and a textual description of the chosen viewpoint, which together drive low-level actions (e.g., move forward 0.25 m, turn 15°).

## Method

EvoNav introduces a history-to-future reasoning paradigm with two complementary modules. Future Chain-of-Thought (F-CoT) parses the instruction into temporal sub-actions and spatial landmarks, predicts the next-step action and landmark, estimates completed progress, and infers a future landmark to guide direction selection. History Chain-of-Experience (H-CoE) maintains two online experience banks: a textual trajectory bank (chroma vector DB) that stores LLM-summarized lessons from completed episodes—categorized by failure, perfect success, or inefficient success—and a visual scene bank that stores CLIP appearance embeddings and LLM text descriptions of every visited viewpoint. At decision time, the agent retrieves top-K trajectory experiences via cosine similarity on landmarks and top-K scene images via text-to-description and text-to-visual retrieval using the predicted future landmark, then fuses all retrieved evidence with the current observation in a single LLM call to select the next viewpoint. The entire pipeline is train-free and zero-shot, requiring no task-specific fine-tuning.

## Results

On R2R-CE, EvoNav with Gemini-2.5-pro achieves 43% SR, 51% OSR, 37.77% SPL, and 5.04m NE, outperforming the strongest zero-shot baseline Open-Nav-Gemini-2.5-pro by +20% SR, +21% OSR, and +17.9% SPL. On the more complex NavRAG-CE benchmark, EvoNav-Gemini-2.5-pro reaches 30% SR and 51% OSR, surpassing Open-Nav-Gemini-2.5-pro by +6% SR and +7% OSR. In real-world deployment across four indoor scenes, EvoNav achieves 35–40% SR, consistently outperforming all baselines including supervised methods like BEVBert and NaVid. EvoNav also reduces inference time from 14.7 to 7.6 minutes per episode and GPU memory from 12.5 GB to 2.4 GB compared to Open-Nav.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoNav-Gemini-2.5-pro decisively outperforms the closest zero-shot train-free baseline Open-Nav-Gemini-2.5-pro [35] on R2R-CE by +20% SR (43 vs 23), +21% OSR (51 vs 30), and +17.9% SPL (37.77 vs 19.90), and on NavRAG-CE by +6% SR (30 vs 24) and +7% OSR (51 vs 44). Against the pre-trained SmartWay-GPT4o [38], EvoNav-Gemini-2.5-pro wins on NE (5.04 vs 7.01), SR (43 vs 29), and SPL (37.77 vs 22.46) despite requiring no extra training data. EvoNav-GPT4o also surpasses DiscussNav-GPT4 [29] (SR 30 vs 11), NavGPT-CE-GPT4 [57] (SR 30 vs 16.3), and MapGPT-CE-GPT4o [11] (SR 30 vs 7) by large margins. On the harder NavRAG-CE benchmark, EvoNav-Gemini-2.5-pro beats InstructNav-GPT4o [30] (SR 30 vs 13.4) and DiscussNav-GPT4o [29] (SR 30 vs 14.58). In real-world settings, EvoNav-Gemini-2.5-pro outperforms supervised methods BEVBert [4] (SR 40 vs 20 in Study Room) and NaVid [54] (SR 40 vs 20 in Study Room), as well as CMA [20] (SR 40 vs 20 in Study Room), demonstrating strong sim-to-real transfer. The only methods EvoNav does not surpass are the fully supervised BEVBert [4] and ETPNav [5] on R2R-CE (SR 43 vs 60/58), which is expected given their task-specific training on seen environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoNav departs from the dominant LLM-based zero-shot VLN-CE paradigm (Open-Nav, NavGPT, DiscussNav, MapGPT) that performs naive unidirectional reasoning from current observations to actions. It repurposes the chain-of-thought reasoning pattern into a bidirectional feedback loop: Future Chain-of-Thought (F-CoT) predicts upcoming actions and landmarks to estimate progress, while History Chain-of-Experience (H-CoE) retrieves summarized past trajectories and scene images from a dynamic vector-database experience bank. The waypoint predictor from CMA [20] is retained as a standard front-end, and CLIP embeddings are used for visual scene retrieval, but the core novelty lies in the history-future reasoning architecture rather than in any single component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical failure mode in zero-shot navigation—continuous compounding errors after an initial mistake—by introducing a feedback mechanism inspired by human navigation (review history, imagine future). The efficiency finding (7.6 min/episode and 2.4 GB GPU vs. 14.7 min/12.5 GB for Open-Nav while gaining +20% SR) is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include the cold-start problem (no experience available for the first episodes), whether the experience bank generalizes across substantially different environments, and the authors' stated direction toward multi-agent collaboration to further improve reasoning.
