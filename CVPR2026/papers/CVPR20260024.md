## Problem

Current VideoQA benchmarks often admit single-cue shortcuts, allowing models to answer correctly via language priors or a single salient frame rather than genuine multi-evidence temporal reasoning. This makes it impossible to distinguish models that truly integrate distributed visual evidence from those that exploit isolated cues, and existing metrics (e.g., Temporal Indispensability, Certificate Length) measure fundamentally different properties than the number of frames a model must actually fuse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260024/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question pairs a long-form video (395 s average, 60–2100 s range, from 336 unique sources) with a five-way multiple-choice prompt; models receive a fixed budget of 16 frames (uniformly sampled by default, or oracle-curated in ablation studies) and output a single letter (A–E). The benchmark additionally produces a per-question MRFS score—the minimum number of frames, in a question-conditioned ordering, that suffices for a reference MLLM to answer correctly—computed via adaptive bisection over the frame budget. Questions are filtered to exclude text-only-solvable items and those answerable by ≥3 of 4 blind LLMs without visual input.

## Method

HERBench is constructed through a tripartite pipeline: (1) object tracking with RF-DETR/DeepSORT generates strictly non-overlapping A-cards (appearance) and B-cards (behavior/trajectory) per entity, deliberately separating identifying cues from queried properties in temporally distant frames; (2) shot segmentation with an MLLM produces scene cards for macroscopic temporal coherence tasks; (3) human-verified narrated events from HD-EPIC anchor absence-detection and counting tasks. Questions are compiled by oriented task programming into 12 compositional subtasks across four reasoning families, each structurally enforcing k ≥ 3 distinct, non-redundant visual cues. The Minimum Required Frame-Set (MRFS) metric is defined as the smallest k such that a reference MLLM answers correctly given the top-k frames from a question-conditioned selector, computed in O(log x) calls via bisection. Evaluation disentangles two bottlenecks: a retrieval deficit (frame selectors like AKS and BOLT-ITS still trail oracle frames) and a fusion deficit (even with oracle frames, models over-concentrate importance on a single frame, as shown by leave-one-out log-probability delta analysis).

## Results

On HERBench (26,806 five-way MCQs), the mean accuracy across 13 state-of-the-art Video-LLMs is 38.2%, with the best model (Ovis-2.5-9B) reaching only 42.1% and the worst (LLaMA-4-Scout-17B) at 31.4%—a narrow band just 11–22 points above the 20% random-guess baseline. Performance is relatively strong on single-entity tracking tasks (e.g., AGBI: 74.5% mean) but collapses on multi-cue aggregation tasks (AC: 23.0%, MEGL: 23.2%) and temporal ordering (TSO: 23.1% mean, with Ovis-2.5-9B at 0.1%). Under the canonical MRFS protocol (Qwen2.5-VL + AKS, x=16), HERBench attains the highest mean MRFS (5.49) among all compared benchmarks, confirming its structurally elevated evidential demand.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the canonical MRFS protocol (Qwen2.5-VL backbone, AKS selector, x=16), HERBench achieves the highest mean MRFS (5.49) among all ten compared benchmarks, edging out Video-MME (5.31) and MINERVA (5.14), and substantially exceeding MVBench (3.52), NExT-QA (2.61), and TemporalBench (2.21); it is also the only benchmark combining language debiasing, enforced multi-frame fusion, absence checking, and oracle-frame availability. Among the 13 evaluated Video-LLMs, no model exceeds 42.1% overall accuracy (Ovis-2.5-9B), and the spread is narrow (31.4–42.1%), indicating a shared architectural limitation rather than a model-specific one. In the frame-selection ablation (Table 3), Oracle Frames yield the highest accuracy (47.8–47.9% across three models), outperforming the best learned selectors (AKS: 36.2–42.7%; BOLT-ITS: 38.4–42.1%) by 5–12 points, confirming that retrieval remains a significant bottleneck. Even with oracle frames, accuracy stays below 50%, and importance-share analysis shows that incorrect predictions concentrate ~80% of frame weight on a single frame versus ~50% for correct ones, revealing a fusion deficit independent of retrieval. The closest prior benchmark in spirit, MINERVA, pairs language debiasing with a high MRFS (5.14) but focuses on multi-step reasoning-trace auditing rather than structurally enforced multi-frame evidence aggregation, making HERBench's k≥3 design and oracle-frame diagnostics a distinct contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

HERBench extends the VideoQA benchmark lineage (MVBench, VideoMME, EgoSchema, LongVideoBench, MINERVA) by introducing a structurally enforced Evidential Requirement (k ≥ 3 non-overlapping cues) as the organizing constraint, rather than relying on video duration or question complexity as proxies for difficulty. The construction pipeline repurposes existing tools (RF-DETR, DeepSORT, BLIP embeddings) but applies them through an oriented task-programming framework that deliberately decouples appearance from behavior (A/B cards) to prevent single-frame shortcuts. The MRFS metric is a new automated, model-centric measure of evidential demand that complements but differs from prior notions like Temporal Indispensability and Certificate Length, enabling apples-to-apples cross-benchmark comparison under a fixed protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a clear dissociation: current Video-LLMs can track single described entities (74–80% on R&T tasks) but collapse to near-chance on multi-cue aggregation (23% on Action Counting, Multi-Entity Grounding), and even oracle-frame access yields <50% accuracy, indicating the fusion module itself is broken independent of retrieval. The two-bottleneck decomposition (retrieval deficit vs. fusion deficit) gives the field a concrete diagnostic framework and two distinct research directions. Open questions include whether the partially automated construction pipeline introduces residual artifacts, whether the k ≥ 3 threshold is the right granularity, and whether improvements in frame selection and multi-evidence fusion can be addressed independently or require joint architectural changes.
