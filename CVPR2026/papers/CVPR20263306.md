## Problem

Existing Graph HyperNetworks for parameter initialization, including the recent Task-Aware Learngene (TAL), leverage pre-trained models only indirectly through soft-label supervision, leaving the most direct and potent source of knowledge—the pre-trained model's internal weight parameters—entirely unexploited. This indirect signal introduces noise through backpropagation across the entire generated model and fails to capture the rich structural information encoded in the weights themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263306/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The hypernetwork takes as input the computational graph (DAG) of a target ViT architecture and the fixed, non-updatable weight matrices of a pre-trained ViT-Large model. It outputs a complete set of initialized parameters for the target network of arbitrary depth (3–12 layers) and hidden dimension (128–768), which can be deployed directly for inference without any further training—what the authors term zero-shot initialization. The pre-trained weights serve as a constant reference input during both training and inference, while the target graph is the only variable that conditions the generation.

## Method

PITH extends the LoGAH decoder into a dual-pathway structure: a projection pathway that generates size-specific low-rank projection matrices P and Q (via LoRA-style factorization) to transform pre-trained weights into target dimensions through W_proj = P·W_P·Q^T, and the original prediction pathway that generates residual weights W_pred. The final target weights are a weighted combination W_target = α·W_proj + (1−α)·W_pred, balancing inherited knowledge with architectural adaptability. A progressive dimension expansion strategy replaces the original decoder's single large-stride transformation with staged expansion through intermediate MLP layers, mitigating instability and information loss. The hypernetwork is meta-trained on ImageNet-1K followed by multi-task fine-tuning across 10 downstream tasks, with the pre-trained ViT-Large weights held fixed throughout training.

## Results

PITH achieves 53.35% zero-shot top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with a 12-layer ViT-Base, surpassing the previous SOTA (TAL) by 6.54% without any further training. After 75 epochs of fine-tuning, PITH-initialized ViT-Base reaches 67.29% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming TAL (64.28%) by 3.01%. Across the 9-task Visual Domain Decathlon, PITH-initialized untrained models achieve 50.96% average accuracy (12-layer ViT-Base), consistently exceeding TAL (50.20%), LoGAH (29.09%), and GHN-3 (25.80%). Training cost is comparable: PITH requires 95.43 hours for 200 epochs yielding 51.03% average accuracy, versus TAL's 92.37 hours for only 43.72%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PITH outperforms all three baselines across every evaluated configuration. Against GHN-3 and LoGAH, the margins are large: on the 12-layer ViT-Base Decathlon average, PITH achieves 50.96% versus 25.80% (GHN-3) and 29.09% (LoGAH), reflecting that these earlier methods lack any mechanism to exploit pre-trained knowledge. Against the closest competitor TAL (which uses soft-label distillation from a pre-trained model), PITH wins by 0.76% on the 12-Base Decathlon average (50.96% vs. 50.20%) and by a much larger 6.54% on zero-shot ImageNet-1K (53.35% vs. 46.81%). After 75 epochs of fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K, PITH leads TAL by 3.01% (67.29% vs. 64.28% for ViT-Base). On unseen tasks (Fashion-MNIST, FER2013, HAM10000), PITH consistently achieves the highest accuracy at both 20 and 100 epochs, e.g., 90.81% vs. 89.41% (TAL) on Fashion-MNIST Tiny at 100 epochs. Training cost is comparable: PITH (95.43 h, 200 epochs) matches TAL's time (92.37 h) while yielding 7.31% higher average accuracy, and even outperforms TAL trained for 300 epochs (139.25 h, 47.54%) by 3.49%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PITH extends the Graph HyperNetwork lineage (GHN → GHN-3 → LoGAH) by adding a dual-pathway decoder that directly projects pre-trained ViT-Large weights into target dimensions via learned low-rank projection matrices, rather than relying solely on the hypernetwork's own parameter prediction. This departs from TAL's approach of using pre-trained models only as indirect soft-label teachers, shifting knowledge transfer from the functional/output level to the parameter/weight level. The progressive dimension expansion strategy addresses a practical limitation in LoGAH's one-step low-rank-to-maximum-capacity transformation, and the overall framework remains within the Learngene paradigm of inheriting knowledge from ancestry models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to initialize ViT models that achieve ~53% ImageNet-1K accuracy with zero training is practically significant for resource-constrained deployment, and the 6.54% gap over TAL suggests that direct weight-level inheritance is substantially more informative than output-level distillation for initialization. However, the method is currently limited to ViT architectures with a fixed pre-trained ViT-Large source and a first-N layer alignment heuristic, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to CNNs, diffusion models, or architectures with fundamentally different layer structures. The progressive dimension expansion technique may have broader applicability to any hypernetwork decoder performing large dimensional transformations.
