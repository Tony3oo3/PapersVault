## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary affordance grounding methods predict functional regions only on visible surfaces of partial point clouds, while 3D reconstruction and generation methods recover or complete geometry without predicting affordances. In robotic settings, a robot observing an object from limited viewpoints must reason about occluded functional regions (e.g., a mug's handle) to plan manipulation, requiring both generative shape completion from depth-conditioned observations and affordance prediction on the resulting full geometry—a combination no prior method provides.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGBD images (as few as one view) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, plus a natural language affordance query (e.g., "where to grasp"). It produces a complete 3D mesh via sparse voxel generation, a per-voxel affordance probability heatmap over the full reconstructed shape, and a next-best camera pose for iterative refinement. A key design property is that the sparse voxel fusion conditioning maintains O(1) token complexity regardless of the number of input views, unlike multi-view diffusion approaches whose token count grows linearly with view count.

## Method

Affostruction extends TRELLIS's two-stage rectified flow framework with three components. First, sparse voxel fusion projects DINOv2 features from each RGBD view into 3D world coordinates using depth and camera parameters, merges overlapping voxels by feature averaging, and appends 3D sinusoidal positional encodings to form a constant-size conditioning signal fed via cross-attention to the structure flow transformer. Second, a separate sparse flow transformer trained from scratch generates affordance heatmaps conditioned on CLIP text embeddings, using a binary mask loss (BCE + Dice) instead of MSE to capture the multi-modal, binary-occupancy nature of functional regions. Third, an affordance-driven active view selection strategy renders the predicted affordance-colored mesh from K=40 candidate hemisphere poses and selects the viewpoint maximizing the sum of rendered heatmap values. Training employs stochastic multi-view sampling (1–8 views per iteration) and classifier-free guidance with 10% unconditional dropout, while the structure decoder and text encoder remain frozen.

## Results

On Toys4k 3D reconstruction, Affostruction achieves 32.67 IoU, outperforming TRELLIS (19.49, +67.6%) and MCC (21.11, +54.8%). On complete-geometry affordance grounding (Affogato), it reaches 19.1 aIoU, a 40.4% improvement over Espresso-3D (13.6). In the partial-observation setting (single RGBD view), Affostruction achieves 9.26 aIoU on full 3D geometry, nearly doubling the best two-stage pipeline (MCC+Espresso-3D at 4.74) and outperforming direct affordance methods (0.38–0.60 aIoU) by over 15×. For active view selection, one additional affordance-guided view yields 2.0× faster aIoU improvement than sequential sampling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Affostruction outperforms all baselines across every evaluation setting. On 3D reconstruction (Toys4k), it beats RGB-only generators Shap-E (6.39 IoU), InstantMesh (13.68), LGM (9.39), and TRELLIS (19.49), as well as the RGBD reconstruction model MCC (21.11), achieving 32.67 IoU. On complete-geometry affordance grounding (Affogato), it surpasses OpenAD (3.1 aIoU), PointRefer/LASO (10.5), and Espresso-3D (13.6) with 19.1 aIoU, though at a slight cost in AUC (72.0 vs. 79.0) and SIM. In the partial-observation setting, the gap widens dramatically: direct affordance methods (OpenAD 0.38, PointRefer 0.53, Espresso-3D 0.60) cannot account for occluded regions, while the best two-stage pipeline (MCC+Espresso-3D, 4.74 aIoU) is nearly doubled by Affostruction (9.26). The two-stage TRELLIS pipelines (1.49–2.23 aIoU) underperform MCC-based ones because RGB-only generation produces misaligned geometry. Affostruction's joint sparse-voxel formulation avoids the error accumulation of separate reconstruction and grounding stages, yielding the best aIoU and aCD simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TRELLIS, a single-image 3D generation foundation model, by replacing its RGB-only conditioning with multi-view sparse voxel fusion of depth-projected DINOv2 features, enabling generative reconstruction that extrapolates unseen geometry from partial RGBD observations. It departs from prior affordance grounding methods (OpenAD, PointRefer, Espresso-3D), which operate discriminatively on complete point clouds or visible surfaces only, by introducing a flow-based generative formulation that models multi-modal affordance distributions on reconstructed geometry. The key conceptual departure from two-stage pipelines (reconstruct-then-ground) is performing both tasks jointly in a shared sparse voxel space, so that affordance predictions can in turn guide active view selection in a closed loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in robotic manipulation: grounding functional regions on occluded surfaces from limited RGBD observations, which is essential for tasks like grasping a mug handle that is not visible from the current viewpoint. The closed-loop design where affordance predictions drive next-best-view selection is a natural and effective strategy, though it inherits a chicken-and-egg vulnerability (incorrect initial affordance estimates mislead view selection). Open questions include extending to multi-object scenes without relying on external segmentation (SAM3D), developing error-aware view selection to handle failure cases, and validating the pipeline in actual robotic deployment where camera poses and depth quality are less controlled.
