## Problem

Existing audio-driven human animation methods that extend videos via overlapping motion frames suffer from progressive error accumulation, causing identity drift, color shifts, and scene instability over long durations. Additionally, prior work largely neglects fine-grained hand motion modeling, resulting in visible distortions, incorrect finger counts, and misalignment between hand gestures and audio.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image, a driving audio clip, and an optional text prompt as input, and produces a high-resolution (720P) full-body talking video that can extend to 74+ seconds. The pipeline operates in two stages: a low-resolution generator produces coarse motion frames synchronized to audio, which are then refined into high-resolution output. Evaluation is conducted on EMTD (upper-body, up to 74s) and HDTF (talking-head, 512×512) benchmarks.

## Method

InfinityHuman is a coarse-to-fine framework built on a DiT backbone trained with Flow Matching. The first stage (LR-A2V) generates audio-synchronized low-resolution video using a decoupled multimodal cross-attention that separates audio and text conditioning branches. The second stage (Pose-Guided Refiner) corrects accumulated drift by conditioning on Sapiens-extracted pose sequences (8-channel pixel-level keypoints) and a prefix-latent reference strategy that keeps the initial frame noise-free to anchor identity, while simulating temporal degradation via low-pass filtering and noise augmentation on the LR latents. A hand-specific reward feedback mechanism, trained on 10,000 curated hand-structure pairs with a fine-tuned MPS evaluator, provides preference-based fine-tuning to improve anatomical plausibility of generated hands without additional annotations.

## Results

On the EMTD full-body long-duration benchmark, InfinityHuman achieves the best FID (60.71), FVD (979.88), IQA (2.48), FaceSIM (0.84), and Hand Keypoint Confidence (0.90), outperforming the previous best (OmniAvatar: FID 82.54, FVD 1104.99, FaceSIM 0.72, HKC 0.86). On the HDTF talking-head benchmark, it achieves the best FID (69.28) and FVD (239.05), surpassing Hallo3 (74.10 / 250.12), while trailing SadTalker on FaceSIM (0.89 vs. 0.93) and Sync-C (8.59 vs. 8.87). Long-form stability analysis shows minimal metric degradation from 10 s to 50 s (FID stays within 35–37, FaceSIM drops only from 0.836 to 0.806).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary EMTD full-body benchmark, InfinityHuman wins on every reported metric against all five baselines (Fantasy, HyAva, Hallo3, MultiTalk, OmniAvatar), with the largest margins over Fantasy (FID 60.71 vs. 133.73) and HyAva (FVD 979.88 vs. 2160.92). The closest competitor is OmniAvatar, which trails by 21.83 FID points, 125.11 FVD points, 0.12 FaceSIM, and 0.04 HKC. On the HDTF talking-head set, InfinityHuman achieves the best FID and FVD, beating the nearest rival Hallo3 by 4.82 FID and 11.07 FVD, but concedes FaceSIM to SadTalker (0.89 vs. 0.93) and Hallo3 (0.89 vs. 0.91), and Sync-C to SadTalker (8.59 vs. 8.87), MultiTalk (8.59 vs. 8.76), and OmniAvatar (8.59 vs. 8.81). Against the talking-head-only methods (SadTalker, AniPortrait, V-Express, EchoMimic), InfinityHuman dominates on FID/FVD/IQA/ASE while remaining competitive on lip-sync. Overall, the pose-guided refiner and hand-specific reward yield consistent gains in identity preservation and hand accuracy that no single baseline matches across both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-driven human animation line (SadTalker, Hallo, EchoMimic, OmniHuman) by tackling the long-duration, full-body, high-resolution setting where prior autoregressive continuation methods suffer from cumulative error. It builds directly on Goku-I2V as the pretrained flow-matching backbone and adopts HumanDiT's multi-resolution training strategy, but departs from the standard overlapping-motion-frame extension paradigm by introducing a coarse-to-fine two-stage pipeline: a low-resolution audio-to-video generator followed by a pose-guided refiner that uses structurally stable pose sequences and a prefix-latent reference to correct drift. The hand-specific reward feedback mechanism, trained via preference learning on a curated 10K-sample dataset, is a novel addition not seen in prior audio-driven animation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pose-guided refiner offers a practical and interpretable solution to the error-accumulation problem that plagues all autoregressive long-video generation, and the long-form stability results (Table 3) showing near-flat FID/FSIM over 50 seconds are encouraging for real-world deployment in advertising, vlogging, and film. However, the framework is limited to continuous single-person footage and cannot handle multi-person interactions, shot changes, or scene transitions, which are common in practical video content. An open question is whether the coarse-to-fine decomposition generalizes to even longer durations (minutes) or to more complex multi-character scenes, and whether the hand-specific reward can be extended to other body-part-specific quality dimensions.
