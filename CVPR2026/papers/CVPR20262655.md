## Problem

Successor representation (SR) methods achieve strong zero-shot generalization in low-dimensional state-based unsupervised RL but degrade sharply in high-dimensional visual environments. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) jointly optimizing the encoder under the SR objective yields suboptimal representations that attend to dynamics-irrelevant image regions, corrupting successor measure estimation; and (2) these flawed representations make it difficult to learn expressive, multi-modal skill-conditioned policies with reliable skill controllability.

## Input / Output

The agent receives RGB visual observations (processed through a learned encoder) and a skill vector z inferred at test time from a small reward-labeled dataset via the closed-form SR formula z_r = E[φφᵀ]⁻¹E[φr]. The encoder also produces saliency maps by thresholding gradients of the value function Q = ψ(f(o),a,z)ᵀz with respect to the raw observation, which are used to mask dynamics-irrelevant pixels. The output is a continuous action produced by a consistency policy that denoises a noisy action sample conditioned on the encoded state and skill vector, with classifier-free guidance blending conditioned and unconditioned branches.

## Method

SRCP decouples representation learning from the SR objective by introducing a saliency-guided dynamics module: saliency maps (top-gradient pixels of the value function) are used to train forward and inverse dynamics models on masked observations, pushing the encoder to capture dynamics-relevant features independent of the successor Bellman loss. For policy learning, SRCP replaces the standard actor with a skill-conditioned consistency model g_θ(s, a_t, z) ≈ a_0 that recovers clean actions from noisy ones at all noise levels, combined with classifier-free guidance a = g_θ(s,a_t,∅) + ω·(g_θ(s,a_t,z) − g_θ(s,a_t,∅)) to balance multi-modal diversity against skill controllability. The consistency policy is trained with three objectives: a skill-conditioned value loss maximizing expected successor-feature returns, a skill-conditioned behavior consistency loss regularizing outputs on noise-perturbed dataset actions, and an unconditional behavior consistency loss promoting diverse multi-modal action distributions. The framework is modular and compatible with any SR backbone (HILP, FB, etc.), and the paper provides a theoretical bound showing that successor feature approximation error directly limits zero-shot generalization quality.

## Results

SRCP achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization across 16 visual continuous control tasks in 4 domains (Walker, Quadruped, Cheetah, Jaco) on the URLB/ExORL benchmark. The headline metric is average cumulative return over 4 datasets × 4 seeds (16 runs). SRCP outperforms the strongest baseline by 13% in Walker (453 vs. FDM's 401), 33% in Quadruped (355 vs. LRA-SR's 266), and 11% in Cheetah (543 vs. HILP's 454), while also achieving the best average across all four pretraining datasets (RND, PROTO, APS, APT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRCP is compared against seven baselines spanning successor feature (SF) and forward-backward (FB) methods: AE, CL, LAP, LRA-SR, FDM, FB, and HILP. SRCP wins on all 16 tasks in Walker, Quadruped, and Cheetah domains, with the largest margins over FB (e.g., 453 vs. 115 in Walker, 355 vs. 183 in Quadruped) and over HILP (453 vs. 238 in Walker, 355 vs. 232 in Quadruped, 543 vs. 454 in Cheetah). In the Jaco domain, SRCP (41) narrowly beats FB (40) and HILP (32), with the closest competition coming from FB on individual tasks like Reach Top Left (48 vs. 45). The closest overall competitor is FDM in Walker (401 vs. 453) and HILP in Cheetah (454 vs. 543), while contrastive methods (CL, LAP) and LRA-SR consistently underperform by large margins across all domains, confirming that standard representation learning objectives are insufficient for visual URL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the successor representation (SR) paradigm for zero-shot URL—established by Barreto et al. and advanced by HILP (Hilbert-space basic features) and Forward-Backward representations—from low-dimensional state spaces to high-dimensional visual inputs. It departs from prior visual SR work by decoupling encoder training from the SR Bellman objective, instead using a saliency-guided forward/inverse dynamics task to shape representations, and by replacing standard policy networks with a consistency-model actor that employs classifier-free guidance (borrowed from diffusion models) to balance multi-modal action diversity against skill controllability. The framework is explicitly designed as a drop-in wrapper compatible with multiple SR backends (HILP, FB), positioning it as a general enhancement layer rather than a new SR formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a concrete failure mode—SR objectives bias visual encoders toward dynamics-irrelevant regions, degrading successor measures and skill-conditioned policy quality—that explains why strong state-based SR methods collapse in pixel-based settings. By showing that a relatively lightweight saliency-guided dynamics loss plus a consistency policy can recover 13–33% of the lost generalization across 16 DMC tasks and 4 datasets, it provides a practical recipe for practitioners attempting to deploy SR agents in visual domains. Key open questions include whether the approach extends to online or partially observable settings, whether the saliency maps remain informative in more complex or textured environments, and whether the consistency policy's inference cost (still multi-step denoising) is acceptable for real-time robotic deployment.
