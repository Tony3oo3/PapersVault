## Problem

Learning unified 3D representations that jointly capture geometry, appearance, and semantics from unposed multi-view images remains an open challenge. Existing self-supervised feed-forward methods suffer from three coupled failures: weak geometry induction (features overfit to local texture rather than global structure), limited appearance fidelity (coarse semantic fields cannot represent fine textures), and geometric-semantic inconsistencies (independent multi-task heads produce misaligned 3D outputs). No prior framework explicitly addresses all three issues simultaneously in a pose-free setting.

## Input / Output

The method takes V unposed RGB images at 256×256 resolution (typically 3–10 views) with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics provided. It produces a hierarchical 3D Gaussian field (256 anchor Gaussians expanded to 2,560 semantic Gaussians and further to fine-grained appearance Gaussians), per-view 3D point maps, refined camera parameters (9-DoF intrinsics + extrinsics), and rendered 2D outputs (RGB images and 64-dim semantic feature maps). All outputs are generated in a single feed-forward pass with no per-scene optimization, and the reconstruction time is 0.041 s per scene.

## Method

UniSplat builds on a ViT-L encoder–decoder with three key mechanisms. First, a dual-masking strategy applies random masking (ρ=0.5) in the encoder and a geometry-aware mask in the decoder, where the decoder mask is guided by a coarse Gaussian importance map rendered from preliminary Gaussian tokens, forcing the model to reconstruct structurally critical regions from incomplete cues. Second, a coarse-to-fine Gaussian splatting strategy hierarchically expands anchor Gaussians into semantic Gaussians (10× via learned offsets) and then into fine-grained appearance Gaussians, resolving the granularity mismatch between coarse semantic fields and dense texture representation. Third, a pose-conditioned recalibration mechanism reprojects the predicted 3D point map and 3D semantic point map into the 2D image plane using estimated camera parameters, then enforces pixel-wise alignment with the Gaussian-rendered RGB and semantic maps via L1 and cosine-similarity losses. Training combines self-supervised photometric reconstruction (L1 + LPIPS) with knowledge distillation from frozen LSeg (semantic features) and VGGT (camera poses and point maps) teachers, eliminating the need for explicit 3D annotations.

## Results

On ScanNet, UniSplat achieves 0.5625 mIoU and 0.8334 mAcc for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation on target views, surpassing the prior pose-free best (LSM) by +5.5/+6.5 points. For novel view synthesis, it reaches 25.65 PSNR, 0.8782 SSIM, and 0.1353 LPIPS without SfM or per-scene optimization, outperforming both pose-based (Feature-3DGS: 24.49 PSNR) and pose-free (LSM: 24.39 PSNR) baselines. Depth estimation improves to 3.10 Abs Rel and 69.13% inlier ratio over LSM (3.38/67.77). On embodied AI benchmarks spanning 268 tasks, the UniSplat ViT encoder achieves top scores across VC-1, RLBench (81.2%/63.3%), Meta-World (70.9%), LIBERO-90 (34.7%), and Franka Kitchen (44.5%), consistently exceeding vision-centric, multi-modal, and embodied-specific baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest pose-free competitor LSM [13], UniSplat wins on every metric: +5.3 mIoU on source-view OVSS, +5.5 mIoU on target-view OVSS, +1.26 PSNR on NVS, and −0.28 Abs Rel on depth, while also running 2.6× faster (0.041s vs 0.108s per-scene time). Pose-based methods (NeRF-DFF [26], Feature-3DGS [68], pixelSplat [6]) are substantially worse on geometry and semantics—NeRF-DFF scores only 0.4037 mIoU on target OVSS and 9.61% depth inlier ratio—confirming that SfM-dependent pipelines do not transfer well to unposed settings. On relative pose estimation (Table 3), UniSplat outperforms NoPoSplat [61] (0.607 vs 0.568 at 5° on RE10K) and SelfSplat [24] (0.607 vs 0.223), as well as DUSt3R [52] and MASt3R [27]. For cross-dataset generalization (Table 4), UniSplat leads on ACID (25.98 PSNR) but trails NoPoSplat [61] on DTU (16.85 vs 17.90 PSNR), suggesting slightly weaker object-centric transfer. On embodied AI (Table 2), the UniSplat encoder beats all 9 baselines across every benchmark: it surpasses the strongest embodied-specific method SPA [71] by +1.5 on Meta-World, +2.1 on LIBERO-90, and +3.9 on Franka Kitchen, and outperforms vision-centric MAE [18] and DINOv2 [39] by 3–20 points depending on the task, while also exceeding multi-modal CLIP [42], EVA [14], and InternViT [10] on all splits.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniSplat sits at the intersection of pose-free Gaussian splatting (NoPoSplat, SelfSplat, LEAP), self-supervised 3D representation learning (Croco, PonderV2), and feed-forward unified reconstruction (LSM, Uni3R, UniForward). It directly adopts the camera-token and Gaussian-latent-token architecture from RayZer and the anchor-to-child Gaussian expansion from Scaffold-GS, but departs from prior work by introducing three coupled mechanisms—dual-masking for geometry induction, coarse-to-fine hierarchical splatting, and pose-conditioned recalibration—that explicitly enforce cross-task consistency between geometry, appearance, and semantics within a single feed-forward pass. Unlike LSM, which treats the three modalities as parallel decoder heads, UniSplat uses estimated camera poses to reproject 3D predictions back into 2D and align them, creating a tighter geometric-semantic coupling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a single unposed-image-to-3D-representation pipeline can serve as a frozen visual backbone for 268 embodied AI tasks across 8 simulators, suggesting that spatially grounded 3D features are a stronger inductive bias for robot manipulation than 2D-only or multi-modal encoders. A notable limitation is the reliance on pseudo-ground-truth from frozen VGGT and LSeg teachers, which caps the achievable quality and introduces potential bias from those models' errors. Open questions include whether the recalibration mechanism generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the coarse-to-fine hierarchy scales to higher-resolution or larger-scene settings, and whether the heavy dependence on 2D VLM distillation for semantics (as revealed by the ablation) can be reduced through purely 3D self-supervision.
