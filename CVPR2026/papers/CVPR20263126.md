## Problem

Existing text-to-video retrieval and generation methods handle only single-step descriptions, so illustrating a multistep procedure (e.g., a recipe or gardening manual) by processing each step independently yields a visually incoherent demonstration. No prior retrieval method can assemble a sequence of clips that is simultaneously correct for every step and coherent across steps in terms of visual continuity and object state.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263126/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multistep description R = (r_1, …, r_n) of natural-language step instructions and a large collection of instructional videos C (up to 323K videos from HowTo100M, COIN, CrossTask, HT-Step), and outputs a stitched video demonstration V_R = (v_1, …, v_n) where each clip v_i is a temporal segment from any video in C. Clips may originate from different source videos, and the output must satisfy both per-step correctness and cross-step coherence (visual continuity, object state continuity). At inference, the candidate space scales as O((KN)^M) for M steps, so an adaptive set-cover reduction is applied to keep retrieval tractable.

## Method

The core component is a procedure evaluator F_R, a 4-layer transformer encoder (8 attention heads, 768-dim) that scores the probability that a full sequence of (clip, step) pairs constitutes a valid procedure, taking concatenated InternVideo2 features of each step text and its candidate clip as input and outputting a scalar via the CLS token. Training uses a large-scale weakly-supervised dataset (446K samples) created by an LLM (Llama-3.1 70B) that mixes steps from similar instructional videos into novel procedures, paired with hard negatives that specifically violate step correctness, visual continuity (unnecessary video-source switches), or object state continuity (temporal order of irreversible transformations). The evaluator is trained with binary cross-entropy against these positives and negatives, while the multimodal encoder (InternVideo2) and step-localization module (DropDTW) remain frozen. At inference, the method selects the candidate sequence maximizing F_R's probability, with a greedy set-cover algorithm reducing the search space to a manageable top-K candidate set.

## Results

On the primary cooking test sets, Stitch-a-Demo achieves MR=3 and R@50=0.84 on SaD-MC, and MR=3.5 and R@50=0.91 on SaD-VD, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline InternVideo (MR=36, R@50=0.55 and MR=8, R@50=0.71 respectively). Gains reach up to 29% in recall and 33 ranks in median rank over InternVideo across all domains. In the human preference study, the method is preferred over ShowHowTo (generative) with a 98% total win rate, and over Recipe2Video and InternVideo with 77% and 75% win rates respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stitch-a-Demo outperforms all five baselines on every metric and domain. Against the strongest retrieval baseline InternVideo [66] (which shares the same encoder and step localizer but lacks the procedure evaluator), the method improves R@50 by up to 29% (e.g., 0.84 vs. 0.55 on Cooking SaD-MC) and reduces median rank by up to 33 positions. Against Recipe2Video [61], which uses hand-crafted temporal and cross-modality metrics, the method achieves dramatically lower median ranks (3 vs. 125 on Cooking SaD-MC) and higher recall (0.84 vs. 0.21). Against CoVR [62] and VidDetours [6], which were not designed for multistep procedure retrieval, the gap is even larger (MR of 3 vs. 193 and 124 respectively on Cooking SaD-MC). In the human preference study, the method is preferred over the generative ShowHowTo [55] with a 98% total win rate, confirming that retrieval-based stitching produces more faithful and realistic demonstrations than image generation for procedural tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task at the intersection of video-text retrieval and instructional video understanding. It repurposes existing components—InternVideo2 for multimodal encoding and DropDTW for temporal step localization—into a pipeline whose key novelty is a learned procedure evaluator (a 4-layer transformer) that scores multi-clip sequences for correctness and visual coherence. Unlike prior retrieval methods (CoVR, VidDetours, Recipe2Video) that handle single-step queries or operate within one video, and unlike generative approaches (ShowHowTo, GenHowTo) that produce per-step images, this work formulates and solves the problem of stitching clips from multiple source videos into a coherent multi-step demonstration. The LLM-based data augmentation (mixing steps from different procedures via Llama-3.1) and the structured hard-negative design (violating step correctness, visual continuity, or object state continuity) are methodological contributions that distinguish it from standard contrastive video-text pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: no single instructional video covers every possible procedure combination, so personalized video demonstrations require cross-video stitching. The 29% recall gain over the strongest retrieval baseline and the 98% human preference over the generative ShowHowTo suggest that carefully constrained retrieval can outperform generation for procedural illustration, a finding with implications for how we think about retrieval-vs-generation tradeoffs. Limitations include the retrieval ceiling (the method cannot illustrate steps absent from the corpus), domain-specific procedure evaluators, and ~25% noise in LLM-generated training labels. Open questions the authors raise include hybrid retrieval-plus-generation pipelines and injecting user preferences (demonstration speed, language style) into the stitched output.
