## Problem

VLM-based visual grounding systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical applications (GUI agents, embodied AI), yet their vulnerability to backdoor attacks remains unexplored. Existing VLM backdoor methods rely on static triggers or fixed targets, which are fundamentally mismatched to grounding tasks where the object to be localized and its language description vary per image. This paper formalizes and realizes the first multi-target, input-aware backdoor attack on VLM-based visual grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The trigger generator takes a benign RGB image and a text description of the attacker-chosen target object (encoded via a frozen, clean VLM's language embedding layer) and outputs a same-resolution imperceptible perturbation that is additively composited onto the image. The backdoored VLM then receives the triggered image plus an arbitrary user query and outputs a bounding box in normalized [0, 1000] coordinate format (e.g., 'object [x0, y0, x1, y1]'), which is forced to correspond to the attacker-specified target regardless of the query content.

## Method

IAG employs a text-conditioned U-Net (three down/up blocks with cross-attention after the middle and each upsampling block) to synthesize input-aware triggers: given a benign image x and target description o, the generator produces a perturbation r such that x⊕r = G_φ(x, z_o) + x, where z_o is the frozen text embedding of o. The U-Net and the VLM are jointly trained with a combined objective: a token-level LM cross-entropy loss over both clean samples (preserving benign grounding) and poisoned samples (forcing grounding of the target), plus a reconstruction loss (L1 pixel + LPIPS perceptual) that keeps the trigger imperceptible. A theoretical margin analysis shows that the text-conditioned subspace steers r toward feature directions already exploited by the VLM's cross-attention for the named object, raising the linear gain term in the attack margin bound while keeping ‖r‖ small. This joint, content-adaptive design outperforms fixed-trigger and input-only baselines by 11–33% in ASR across five datasets and three VLMs while maintaining <3% clean-accuracy degradation.

## Results

IAG achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR@0.5) in 11 out of 12 model-dataset settings, with headline values of 58.9% (LLaVA/RefCoco), 66.9% (InternVL/RefCoco), and 53.8% (Ferret/Flickr30k Entities). Benign accuracy (BA@0.5) remains within 3% of clean accuracy (CA@0.5) across all settings, confirming stealthiness. On Flickr30k Entities, IAG outperforms the second-best baseline by 11.9%–32.8%, and on ShowUI by over 33%. Triggers remain imperceptible with PSNR values of 31–32 dB and LPIPS < 0.05.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Imperio [13] is the closest competitor, achieving 55.2% ASR on RefCoco (LLaVA) versus IAG's 58.9%, and 48.1% on Flickr30k Entities (Ferret) versus IAG's 53.8%; however, IAG surpasses Imperio in 10 of 12 settings, with the largest gaps on ShowUI (e.g., 32.3% vs. 16.0% on InternVL) and RefCocog (50.2% vs. 52.4% is the one setting where Imperio edges out IAG on InternVL). Marksman [17] is substantially weaker, typically scoring 6–16% ASR on LLaVA and InternVL, though it narrows the gap on Ferret/Flickr30k Entities (47.7% vs. 53.8%). One-to-N [70] and the Random baseline are nearly ineffective, with ASR values below 10% across all settings, confirming that static or random triggers cannot handle the multi-target, input-aware nature of visual grounding. Overall, IAG wins 11 of 12 model-dataset configurations, with margins of 11.9%–32.8% over the best baseline on Flickr30k Entities and over 33% on ShowUI.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the input-aware backdoor attack paradigm from image classification (Imperio, Marksman, One-to-N) to the substantially more complex setting of VLM-based visual grounding, where targets are open-vocabulary and vary per image. It departs from prior VLM backdoor work (TrojVLM, BadSem) that relies on static triggers with fixed targets, by introducing a text-conditioned U-Net trigger generator that dynamically produces input-specific, target-semantic perturbations. The architectural choice repurposes the cross-attention U-Net from latent diffusion models as a semantic trigger synthesizer, a novel application that bridges generative image editing and adversarial security.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete, realistic threat to VLMs deployed in GUI agents, embodied AI, and autonomous driving, where grounding errors can lead to data breaches or safety incidents. A key open question is how to defend against dynamic, input-aware triggers: all tested detection-based and adaptive defenses (Spectral Signature, Beatrix, JPEG compression, PAR, retraining) fail to meaningfully reduce ASR, suggesting current defense paradigms are fundamentally mismatched to context-adaptive attacks. The paper also leaves open whether the approach generalizes to other VLM tasks beyond grounding (though preliminary VQA transfer is shown) and whether the sub-100% ASR reflects a fundamental limit or a training-budget constraint.
