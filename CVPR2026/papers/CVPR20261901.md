## Problem

Existing visual tokenizers are fragmented along two axes: they specialize in either reconstruction (VAE-based, preserving pixel detail) or understanding (CLIP-based, extracting semantics), and they are restricted to single modalities. Prior unified attempts (VILA-U, UniTok) combine both objectives but remain limited to images, leaving video and 3D unexplored. This paper asks whether a single tokenizer can simultaneously achieve high-fidelity reconstruction and rich semantic understanding across images, videos, and 3D assets.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images at arbitrary resolutions (64–2048 px), video clips at arbitrary temporal lengths (up to 1024 px spatial), and 3D assets rendered as multi-view images from spherically sampled cameras and aggregated into 64³ voxel grids. All inputs are partitioned into non-overlapping space-time patches of size t×p×p (with temporal zero-padding for images) and encoded into a sparse 4D latent space where images occupy the (x,y) plane at t=z=0, videos extend along the temporal axis, and 3D assets fill (x,y,z) voxels. Outputs include 48-dimensional continuous latents (compression ratio 4×16×16), optional 8 discrete FSQ tokens from 4096-entry codebooks, pixel-space reconstructions, Gaussian splatting parameters for 3D rendering, and a global semantic embedding (via attention pooling) aligned to text.

## Method

ATOKEN extends the pretrained SigLIP2 vision tower (SO400M, 27 blocks, d=1152) from 2D to 4D by generalizing patch embeddings to space-time blocks and adding 4D Rotary Position Embeddings in every attention layer, while a from-scratch decoder of identical architecture reconstructs visual outputs. The key architectural insight is a sparse 4D representation where each modality naturally occupies different subspaces, enabling a single encoder to process all modalities without architectural modifications. Training replaces unstable GAN objectives with an adversarial-free loss combining L1, LPIPS, Gram matrix (second-order feature statistics), and CLIP losses for reconstruction, plus KL-divergence distillation from frozen SigLIP2 for image semantics and sigmoid loss for video/3D alignment. A four-stage progressive curriculum (image → video → 3D → FSQ quantization) incrementally expands capabilities, revealing that multimodal training improves rather than degrades single-modality reconstruction (rFID drops 19% from Stage 1 to Stage 3). KV-caching across temporal tiles eliminates redundant computation during video decoding.

## Results

ATOKEN-So/C (Stage 3) achieves 0.21 rFID with 82.2% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy for images, 3.01 rFVD with 40.2% MSR-VTT R@1 for videos, and 28.28 PSNR with 90.9% Toys4k accuracy for 3D — the first tokenizer to report competitive metrics across all three modalities simultaneously. The discrete variant ATOKEN-So/D maintains 0.38 rFID and 82.2% accuracy on images while extending to video (22.16 rFVD) and 3D (28.17 PSNR). Progressive training yields a 19% rFID reduction (0.26→0.21) from Stage 1 to Stage 3, demonstrating that adding video and 3D modalities improves rather than degrades image reconstruction quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniTok [29], the only other unified image tokenizer, ATOKEN-So/C Stage 3 improves rFID from 0.36 to 0.21 (42% reduction), PSNR from 25.34 to 29.72, and ImageNet accuracy from 78.6% to 82.2%, while uniquely extending to video and 3D. For video reconstruction, ATOKEN-So/C (36.07 PSNR, 3.01 rFVD) closely matches the specialized Wan2.2 VAE (36.39 PSNR, 3.19 rFVD) and substantially outperforms OmniTokenizer (19.89 PSNR, 202.46 rFVD) and Cosmos (34.33 PSNR, 8.34 rFVD). On 3D, ATOKEN surpasses Trellis-SLAT in PSNR (28.28 vs. 26.97) but trails slightly in LPIPS (0.062 vs. 0.054). For semantic understanding, ATOKEN is marginally below dedicated encoders: 82.2% vs. 83.4% (SigLIP2) and 83.5% (PEcoreL) on ImageNet, and 40.2% vs. 41.9% (SigLIP2) and 50.3% (PEcoreL) on MSR-VTT R@1. The discrete variant ATOKEN-So/D achieves 2.23 gFID on ImageNet generation, outperforming UniTok (2.51) but trailing TokenBridge (1.76) despite using a larger 4096-entry vocabulary per token dimension.

## Relation to Prior Work

AToken extends the pretrained SigLIP2 vision encoder from 2D images to a unified 4D space using space-time patch embeddings and 4D RoPE (from Unified-IO 2), while adapting Trellis-SLAT's multi-view rendering pipeline for 3D assets and FSQ for optional discrete tokenization. It departs from the dominant GAN-based training paradigm for reconstruction tokenizers (SD-VAE, VQGAN, GigaTok) by replacing adversarial objectives with a Gram-matrix-plus-perceptual loss, and from prior unified attempts (VILA-U, UniTok) by extending beyond images to videos and 3D within a single sparse 4D latent space. The progressive curriculum design—starting from image understanding, adding reconstruction, then video, then 3D—builds on knowledge distillation from frozen SigLIP2 while incrementally expanding modality coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that a single tokenizer can simultaneously serve reconstruction (generation) and understanding (semantic alignment) across three visual modalities, a prerequisite for truly unified multimodal foundation models analogous to how BPE unified text. The finding that cross-modal training *improves* single-modality reconstruction (19% rFID reduction from image-only to full multimodal) challenges the assumption that generality requires a quality trade-off, and the capacity threshold result (catastrophic failure below ~200M parameters) provides practical guidance for multimodal tokenizer design. Key limitations include the 48-dimensional latents being suboptimal for 3D generation compared to task-specific 8-channel approaches, the absence of a fully integrated omnimodel, and reliance on 256 H100 GPUs for training, which limits immediate reproducibility.
