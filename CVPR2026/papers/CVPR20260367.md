## Problem

Vision Language Models struggle to reason about 4D scene dynamics because motion is observed only through 2D camera projections, and existing datasets conflate object motion with camera motion. Most available training data focuses on apparent 2D motion rather than true 3D motion, leaving VLMs without the fine-grained spatio-temporal supervision needed for robust 4D understanding.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 video frames at 448×448 resolution alongside a text question and an optional visual prompt (circle annotation, pixel coordinate, or object caption). Outputs span four formats: free-form descriptive text, multiple-choice (4 options), binary Y/N, and point-tracking trajectories where coordinates are normalized to [0,1] and rounded to three decimals with per-frame occlusion flags. To prevent autoregressive shortcut learning, trajectory frame order is randomized in the output and frame indices are explicitly specified in the query.

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable QA generation pipeline that converts standardized 4D geometric data (camera poses, depth maps, 6D object poses) from five diverse sources into 400K training QA pairs and a 2.2K benchmark across 13 question types in four categories (camera motion, object motion, 3D spatial understanding, and point tracking). A key novelty is true-motion point tracking (Eq. 2), which projects 3D point tracks through a fixed reference camera pose rather than the time-varying camera, thereby disentangling object motion from camera motion and yielding an intuitive, image-aligned motion representation. The pipeline uses pre-defined templates instantiated by Gemini-2.5-Pro for diverse phrasing, heuristic-based asset sampling to ensure segments contain sufficient motion, and carefully calibrated thresholds to map continuous geometric measurements into unambiguous categorical labels. Standard VLMs (NVILA-Lite-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) are fine-tuned on the dataset with frozen visual encoders, and an optional variant integrates L4P geometry features via an MLP projector interleaved with visual tokens, trained in two stages (projector alignment then full LLM unfreezing).

## Results

On 4DP-QA-Bench, fine-tuning standard VLMs on 4DP-QA yields dramatic gains: NVILA-Lite-8B improves from 42.3% to 84.4% overall accuracy (+42.1 pts), Qwen2.5-VL-7B from 46.6% to 84.3% (+37.7), and Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 46.7% to 81.3% (+34.6). All three fine-tuned models surpass the strongest proprietary baseline (Gemini-2.5-Pro at 66.8%) by 14–18 points. On the external VLM4D benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL-7B+4DP-QA achieves 63.6% overall accuracy, outperforming Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.8%) and the best open-source baseline Qwen3-VL-32B (56.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three fine-tuned models (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B+4DP-QA, NVILA-Lite-8B+4DP-QA) outperform every proprietary and open-source baseline by a wide margin on 4DP-QA-Bench. The strongest proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-Pro (66.8% overall), is surpassed by 14–18 points; GPT-4o (53.8%) and Gemini-Robotics (62.5%) trail even further. Among open-source baselines, the best is Qwen3-VL-32B at 52.1%, while smaller models like Qwen2-VL-7B (44.2%) and NVILA-Video-8B (42.2%) perform near or below the 40.8% random baseline in several categories. On the external VLM4D benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL-7B+4DP-QA (63.6%) edges out Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.8%) and substantially outperforms Qwen3-VL-32B (56.8%) and NVILA-Video-8B (38.0%). Notably, the 3B fine-tuned model (55.5% on VLM4D) surpasses the untrained 7B and 32B open-source baselines, demonstrating that the dataset effectively transfers 4D reasoning capability to smaller architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the data-driven paradigm for VLM capability acquisition (exemplified by SpatialVLM's 2B-sample spatial QA dataset) to the 4D/motion domain, but departs from prior work in two key ways. First, it introduces true-motion point tracking as a new perceptual task that explicitly disentangles object motion from camera motion, going beyond the apparent 2D motion captured by standard visual point tracking (TAP-Vid, CoTracker). Second, it builds on L4P, a unified low-level 4D geometry encoder, by extending it with the true-motion tracking objective and integrating its features into a VLM backbone, bridging the gap between specialized 4D reconstruction methods and general-purpose VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 400K-sample 4DP-QA dataset and 2.2K-sample benchmark provide the community with the first large-scale resource specifically targeting fine-grained 4D perception (camera motion, object motion, spatial reasoning, and point tracking) in VLMs, filling a gap where prior datasets were limited in scale or lacked true-motion disentanglement. The finding that standard VLM architectures require no architectural modification to learn 4D understanding—given sufficient geometrically-grounded QA data—suggests a scalable path to 4D perception without bespoke 3D/4D modules. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly in-the-wild video without ground-truth camera poses and 6D object annotations, and whether the geometry encoder's degradation on real (longer, lower-frame-rate) videos can be resolved.
