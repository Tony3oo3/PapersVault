## Problem

Video diffusion models depend on rare 'golden data' that simultaneously exhibits high visual quality (VQ) and high motion quality (MQ), yet these two attributes are negatively correlated (r = −0.24), making dual-quality samples statistically scarce (only ~22% of a large dataset). Existing pipelines simply discard quality-imbalanced videos, wasting substantial training signal. The paper asks whether imperfect, single-strength data can be exploited effectively rather than filtered out.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video, operating within standard video diffusion or flow-matching frameworks (CogVideoX-5B, Wan-T2V-1.3B). During training, each video in the 280K-pair corpus is pre-scored with VQ and MQ via VideoAlign; these per-sample quality scores are the additional signal consumed by TQD to modulate both sample retention probability and timestep sampling distribution, without altering the model architecture or the text-conditioning pathway.

## Method

TQD (Timestep-aware Quality Decoupling) rests on a gradient-based finding: VQ-degraded samples produce gradients close to golden data at high-noise timesteps (where motion is established), while MQ-degraded samples match at low-noise timesteps (where details are refined). The method implements this via a two-level mechanism: (1) a sample-level dropout that retains each video with probability max(vq_norm, mq_norm), suppressing doubly-poor samples; and (2) a per-sample Beta(t; μκ, (1−μ)κ) timestep distribution whose center μ = 0.5 + 0.5(mq_norm − vq_norm) shifts motion-rich samples toward high timesteps and visually clean samples toward low timesteps, with concentration κ scaling with the quality disparity to sharpen specialization. When mq_norm = vq_norm the distribution degenerates to the original uniform or logit-normal sampling, preserving compatibility with existing training recipes.

## Results

On Wan-T2V-1.3B with mixed-quality data (Set-A), TQD improves VideoAlign MQ from 2.1388 to 2.2557 and VQ from 3.2537 to 3.3450, while also boosting VBench Dynamic from 0.5312 to 0.6384. Under extreme quality imbalance (Set-B, containing only high-MQ/low-VQ and high-VQ/low-MQ samples), TQD reaches MQ 2.1477 and VQ 3.2679, surpassing the baseline trained on the full mixed dataset (MQ 2.1388, VQ 3.2537). On golden data only (Set-C), TQD further lifts IQ from 0.6939 to 0.7556 and VQ from 3.3378 to 3.3743. CogVideoX-5B shows consistent but smaller gains (e.g., Set-A MQ: 2.1855→2.2345), likely due to LoRA-based fine-tuning constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TQD against each model's default timestep sampling strategy (uniform for CogVideoX, logit-normal for Wan) as the baseline, across three data configurations. On Wan-T2V-1.3B, TQD consistently outperforms the baseline in all settings: on Set-A it gains +0.117 MQ and +0.091 VQ; on Set-B (imbalanced data only) it gains +0.057 MQ and +0.034 VQ; and on Set-C (golden data only) it gains +0.028 MQ and +0.037 VQ. Most strikingly, TQD trained exclusively on separated imbalanced data (Set-B) surpasses the baseline trained on the full mixed dataset (Set-A) in both MQ (2.1477 vs. 2.1388) and VQ (3.2679 vs. 3.2537), demonstrating that timestep-aware routing of imperfect data can match or exceed conventional training on better data. On CogVideoX-5B, TQD also wins on MQ across all settings (e.g., Set-A: 2.2345 vs. 2.1855) but shows smaller VQ gains, which the authors attribute to LoRA's limited capacity for full adaptation. A 'Data Filter Only' ablation (quality-based dropout without adaptive timesteps) on VideoPhy2 confirms that the gains stem from timestep-aware routing rather than mere data selection: TQD achieves SA 3.820 and PC 3.843 versus 3.795 and 3.756 for filter-only.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on video diffusion and flow-matching architectures (CogVideoX, Wan-T2V) by modifying their timestep sampling strategy, and departs from the dominant data-curation paradigm that discards videos failing either visual or motion quality thresholds. It extends the line of timestep/loss reweighting work (cosine schedules, SNR weighting, curriculum learning) by conditioning timestep allocation on sample-specific quality metrics rather than applying a uniform schedule to all data. The gradient-based analysis connecting hierarchical denoising dynamics to quality-degraded samples provides a theoretical bridge between these two threads, positioning TQD as a data-aware generalization of existing timestep curricula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that training exclusively on quality-imbalanced data with TQD can match or exceed conventional training on mixed-quality data, the paper challenges the field's reliance on rare dual-quality 'golden' videos and opens a more data-efficient path for scaling video generation. Limitations include the relatively small fine-tuning scale (280K samples, 2000 steps), dependence on a single quality-scoring model (VideoAlign), and untested applicability to full pre-training. Open questions include whether the quality-routing principle generalizes to other quality dimensions (e.g., temporal consistency, semantic alignment), how sensitive performance is to the quality scorer, and whether the Beta-distribution formulation is optimal or merely a convenient parameterization.
