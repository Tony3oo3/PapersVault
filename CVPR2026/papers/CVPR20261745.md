## Problem

Existing video reshooting methods condition video diffusion models on per-frame depth-lifted point clouds, but they are typically trained on artifact-free renders from precise depth maps, making them brittle to the geometric errors and temporal flickering inherent in real-world 4D reconstruction of dynamic scenes. This leads to poor content preservation, imprecise camera control, and visible artifacts when target trajectories deviate from the source viewpoint.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261745/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular source video and a user-defined target camera trajectory (extrinsics and intrinsics) as input. The source video is first lifted into a world-space 4D point cloud via 4D reconstruction (STream3R or π³) and static-pixel segmentation (Grounded SAM 2), then rendered in the target cameras to produce a point cloud render and alpha mask. The output is a 49-frame reshoot video at 672×384 (or 1280×720 after fine-tuning) that preserves the source dynamics from the new viewpoint. The method also supports long-video inference by autoregressively generating chunks and registering static pixels back into the persistent point cloud as memory.

## Method

Vista4D builds a temporally-persistent 4D point cloud by making static pixels (identified via segmentation) visible across all frames, providing explicit content preservation and rich camera signals even when target and source cameras have little per-frame overlap. The key training insight is using multiview dynamic-scene data with 4D-reconstructed depths from non-frontal views, which introduces realistic spatial and temporal artifacts so the model learns to correct imperfect geometry rather than merely inpaint. The architecture fine-tunes Wan2.1-T2V-14B (a flow-matching video diffusion transformer) by concatenating patchified latent tokens of the source video and point cloud render with noisy target tokens along the frame dimension (in-context conditioning), and injects target cameras as Plücker embeddings via zero-initialized linear projections after self-attention. Training uses a mix of synthetic multiview data (MultiCamVideo) and real-world monocular data (60K subset of OpenVidHD-0.4M) for 30,000 steps at 672×384 followed by 300 steps at 1280×720, with only the patchify layers, self-attention, camera encoders, and projectors unfrozen.

## Results

On the 110 video-camera pair evaluation set, Vista4D achieves the lowest camera control errors across all metrics: translation 1.251, rotation 4.647°, intrinsics 4.927, and RE@SG 7.504, outperforming the closest baseline GEN3C (1.309, 4.751°, 5.085, 12.99). On the iphone novel-view synthesis benchmark, Vista4D attains the best PSNR (14.14), LPIPS (0.514), and optical flow EPE (1.142), indicating superior spatial reconstruction and motion preservation. In the user study (42 participants, 30 video-camera pairs), Vista4D is preferred 67.06% for source preservation, 68.17% for camera accuracy, and 77.38% for overall fidelity, far exceeding the next-best method (CamCloneMaster at 15.63%, 6.43%, 11.03% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vista4D outperforms all five baselines on every camera control metric in Table 1, with the closest competitor being GEN3C (translation 1.309 vs 1.251; rotation 4.751° vs 4.647°). The gap is most dramatic on 3D consistency (RE@SG): Vista4D scores 7.504 versus 12.99 for GEN3C, 13.11 for EX-4D, and a striking 120.5 for TrajectoryCrafter, whose double-reprojection training strategy fails to generalize to non-frontal views. On the iphone novel-view synthesis benchmark (Table 2), Vista4D leads in PSNR (14.14 vs 13.82 for TrajectoryCrafter), LPIPS (0.514 vs 0.656), and EPE (1.142 vs 2.375), though it trails TrajectoryCrafter slightly on SSIM (0.310 vs 0.320). Implicit-prior methods (ReCamMaster, CamCloneMaster) show the weakest camera control (rotation errors of 12.79° and 23.77° respectively) and consequently produce outputs with minimal camera change, which inflates their FID/FVD scores in Table 3 but does not reflect true reshooting capability. The user study confirms these quantitative findings: Vista4D is preferred by a wide margin (67–77%) over all baselines, whose preference rates never exceed 15.63%.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vista4D extends the video reshooting line of work (TrajectoryCrafter, GEN3C, EX-4D) that conditions video diffusion models on per-frame depth-lifted point clouds, but departs in two key ways: it replaces per-frame 3D point clouds with a temporally-persistent 4D point cloud (static pixels visible across all frames via segmentation), and it trains on noisy multiview 4D-reconstructed data rather than artifact-free double-reprojection pairs. For camera control, it builds on Plücker-embedding injection from ReCamMaster and in-context source-video conditioning (concatenation rather than TrajectoryCrafter's cross-attention), while leveraging the generative priors of the Wan2.1-T2V-14B flow-matching transformer as its base model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: existing reshooting methods trained on clean synthetic point clouds degrade sharply on real-world videos where 4D reconstruction is imperfect, and Vista4D's noisy-training strategy closes much of that gap (roughly 2× lower 3D reprojection error and optical-flow error vs. the next-best baseline). The temporally-persistent point cloud representation also unlocks applications beyond reshooting—dynamic scene expansion, 4D scene recomposition, and long-video inference with explicit memory—that are not available to per-frame methods. Open questions include scalability to highly dynamic scenes where static/dynamic segmentation is ambiguous, dependence on the quality of upstream 4D reconstruction (STream3R, π³), and whether the 14B-parameter base model is necessary or whether smaller models can match the robustness.
