## Problem

Existing amodal instance segmentation methods rely on shape priors to infer occluded regions, but these priors are not always compatible with the target object at test time, and how to exactly retrieve and align them remains an open problem. This paper explores interactive amodal segmentation, where a few user clicks provide direct spatial cues that can guide retrieval of more compatible shape priors and compensate for their inapplicability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260272/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 448x448 RGB image patch, a 2-channel click map (positive or negative click at the center of the largest mis-segmented region), and the previous iteration's mask as input, and produces an updated amodal mask covering both visible and occluded regions. The interaction protocol follows MFP with up to 24 rounds; at t=1 the previous mask is initialized to all-zeros and a positive click is placed at the image center. Object categories are fixed by a pre-trained model, so the network only predicts the complete mask geometry.

## Method

The framework (ClickPriorNet) has two stages. In pre-training, a shape prior codebook of 1024 slots per class is built from GT amodal masks (via K-Means clustering), and a query encoder (six conv layers mapping the concatenated previous mask + click to a 288-d vector) is trained with a triplet loss that pulls the query toward codebook slots whose stored masks have higher IoU with the target GT and pushes it away from lower-IoU slots. In formal training, the ViT-B backbone extracts image features, the pre-trained query encoder retrieves K shape priors, and a deformable attention module adaptively aligns the retrieved prior features by estimating sampling offsets that contrast the prior against the image, mask, and click features. The aligned prior features are concatenated with the image and mask-click features and fed to a segmentation head trained with normalized focal loss over all T interaction rounds.

## Results

On KINS, D2SA, and COCOA cls, ClickPriorNet achieves the lowest NoC_occ across all three target IoU thresholds (80%, 85%, 90%), e.g., 1.66 / 2.40 / 4.31 on KINS vs. 1.81 / 2.62 / 4.65 for the strongest baseline MFP+C2F. In terms of mIoU with 5 clicks, the method reaches 91.97% mIoU_full and 77.72% mIoU_occ on KINS, 95.17% / 62.67% on D2SA, and 90.48% / 58.30% on COCOA cls, outperforming MFP+C2F by +2.15% mIoU_full and +4.37% mIoU_occ on KINS. The AUC score (area under the mIoU-vs-clicks curve) on KINS is +1.678 higher than MFP+C2F, confirming consistent gains across all click budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against interactive segmentation baselines (RITM, EMC-Click, FocusCut, GPCIS, iCMFormer, SimpleClick, GraCo, MFP), ClickPriorNet consistently achieves the lowest NoC_occ on all three datasets and all three IoU thresholds, with the largest gap over MFP (e.g., −0.34 on NoC⁹⁰_occ on KINS). The strongest composite baseline MFP+C2F (which late-fuses C2F-Seg's shape prior into MFP's iterative framework) is outperformed by 0.11–0.51 in NoC_occ across datasets, with the most pronounced gain on D2SA (−0.51 on NoC⁹⁰_occ). Against non-interactive amodal methods (PCNET, AISFormer, VRSP, C2F-Seg), the method with 5 clicks surpasses C2F-Seg by +9.75% mIoU_full and +24.12% mIoU_occ on KINS, and by +10.20% mIoU_full and +30.59% mIoU_occ on COCOA cls. Even with only 1 click, ClickPriorNet already exceeds C2F-Seg's non-interactive performance on all three datasets (e.g., 83.13% vs. 82.22% mIoU_full on KINS). The ablation confirms that the click-aware triplet-loss retrieval contributes +9.69% mIoU_occ and the deformable-attention alignment adds a further +5.3% mIoU_occ over the standard shape prior baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—interactive segmentation (MFP, RITM, FocusCut) and amodal instance segmentation (VRSP, C2F-Seg)—into a single task of interactive amodal segmentation. Its core methodological departure from VRSP's shape prior retrieval (which uses natural Euclidean distance in auto-encoder feature space) is the introduction of a triplet loss that explicitly supervises retrieved priors to have higher IoU with the target's ground-truth mask, conditioned on both the previous mask and user clicks. The deformable attention alignment module addresses a practical gap left by prior shape-prior methods: the inevitable spatial mismatch between a retrieved prior and the actual target instance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that interactive clicks and shape priors are complementary rather than redundant for amodal segmentation, with the ablation showing that click-aware retrieval alone contributes ~10% mIoUocc over standard retrieval. This suggests that the quality of shape prior retrieval (not just its existence) is a critical bottleneck in amodal methods, opening a direction for better retrieval strategies. However, the interactive protocol is somewhat artificial (clicks are protocol-generated rather than human-provided), and the method is architecturally dependent on both C2F-Seg and MFP, limiting its portability. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video amodal segmentation or to settings where clicks are sparse and imprecise.
