## Problem

Existing image editing methods either rely on textual instructions (less intuitive than visual guidance) or are fragmented across narrow tasks like local appearance transfer, subject replacement, and style alignment. While multi-reference generation freely fuses features from multiple images, cross-image editing requires selective, directional attribute transfer from references to a source while preserving structural integrity—a stricter requirement for which no unified framework existed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261140/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source image, one or more reference images, and a text editing instruction, producing a single edited image that incorporates visual attributes (identity, texture, style) from the references while maintaining the source's composition. It operates as a plug-in on standard diffusion backbones (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 and Flux-Kontext), trained on 50K reference-source-synthesis triplets plus 100K single-image pairs to preserve base generation capability. The framework supports zero-shot multi-reference editing without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

OrionEdit introduces two complementary mechanisms for disentangled cross-image editing. First, symmetric orthogonal subspace disentanglement: a Gaussian matrix is QR-decomposed to yield orthogonal bases A^(i) per latent branch, and each branch receives a frozen low-rank projection ΔW^(i) = A^(i) B^(i) (A^(i))^T where only B^(i) is trainable, guaranteeing zero Frobenius inner product between branch-specific updates. Second, a reverse-causal information-flow mask (upper block-triangular) enforces unidirectional attention in the order reference → source → synthesis, preventing bidirectional contamination and ensuring the synthesis branch reads from but never writes back to source and reference tokens. The architecture combines standard LoRA blocks (rank 256) with Orion blocks in a single-stage training procedure on 8 A100 GPUs, jointly optimizing single- and multi-subject generation and editing tasks.

## Results

On OrionEditBench, OrionEdit-qwen achieves DPG 85.28, DINOv3 0.795, SigLip-I 0.791, and CLIP-T 0.285 on cross-image attribute transfer, outperforming all open-source baselines and approaching GPT-4o (DPG 88.74, SigLip-I 0.778). On style alignment, OrionEdit-qwen reaches DPG 85.19 and Style Preservation 0.687, surpassing GPT-4o (DPG 83.75, Sty-Pre 0.664) and OmniStyle (Sty-Pre 0.673). On cross-image fusion, OrionEdit-qwen attains DPG 90.60 and CLIP-T 0.311, matching or exceeding GPT-4o (DPG 89.48, CLIP-T 0.291) and Qwen-Image-Edit (DPG 90.63, CLIP-T 0.312). The Flux backbone variant (OrionEdit-flux) also consistently outperforms open-source baselines across all three task families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OrionEdit-qwen substantially outperforms its base model Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 on DPG (85.28 vs. 63.18 for attribute transfer; 85.19 vs. 44.44 for style alignment) and SigLip-I (0.791 vs. 0.698), demonstrating that the orthogonal subspace and reverse-causal mask add significant editing capability. Against GPT-4o, OrionEdit-qwen wins on SigLip-I (0.791 vs. 0.778) and CLIP-T (0.285 vs. 0.269) for attribute transfer, and on DPG (85.19 vs. 83.75), Content Preservation (0.524 vs. 0.507), and Style Preservation (0.687 vs. 0.664) for style alignment, while GPT-4o retains a slight edge on Aesthetic (6.04 vs. 5.98) and DPG (88.74 vs. 85.28) for attribute transfer. OrionEdit clearly dominates open-source multi-image models: OmniGen2 (DPG 61.16), DreamOmni2-edit (DPG 68.93), and Xverse (DPG 46.25) all trail by 15–40 DPG points. On the fusion task, OrionEdit-qwen (DPG 90.60, CLIP-T 0.311) matches Qwen-Image-Edit (DPG 90.63) and surpasses GPT-4o (DPG 89.48, CLIP-T 0.291), confirming strong multi-reference composition ability. Task-specialized editors like AnyEdit (DPG 79.83) and MagicBrush (DPG 71.57) remain below OrionEdit on attribute transfer, though AnyEdit achieves a marginally higher DINOv3 (0.800 vs. 0.795).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal subspace adaptation mechanism from multi-concept customization (Mix-of-Show, Po et al. CVPR 2024) by repurposing QR-decomposed orthogonal bases not for concept-specific LoRA branches but for branch-specific disentanglement across source, reference, and synthesis latents in a diffusion transformer. It departs from prior cross-image editing methods (Paint-by-Example, MimicBrush, AnyDoor, Cross-Image Attention), which each address a single sub-task (appearance transfer, subject replacement, style alignment) in isolation, by unifying them under a single 'one source, multiple references' formulation. The reverse-causal attention mask enforcing unidirectional information flow (reference → source → synthesis) is a new architectural element not present in prior multi-image generation or editing pipelines, which typically use symmetric or full attention across all branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing cross-image editing as a distinct paradigm from multi-image generation and providing both a unified architecture and a dedicated benchmark (OrionEditBench), this work gives the community a common vocabulary and evaluation protocol for a task family that was previously fragmented across dozens of specialized methods. The practical impact is notable: an open-source model (Qwen-Image-Edit backbone) approaches GPT-4o-level fidelity on attribute transfer and style alignment, suggesting the orthogonal-disentanglement-plus-directional-masking recipe is a viable path for competitive editing without proprietary scale. Open questions include whether the orthogonal constraint remains beneficial as the number of reference images grows beyond the tested range, whether the 50K-pair training set is sufficient for robust generalization to unseen domains, and whether the reverse-causal mask generalizes to video or 3D editing where temporal/spatial dependencies are richer.
