## Problem

Identity-preserving video generation requires maintaining a person's facial identity across generated frames, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: face-encoder-based methods lack flexibility and fail to capture fine facial details, while full-parameter fine-tuning of diffusion transformers demands enormous training resources and breaks compatibility with other AIGC tools. Achieving robust identity preservation in a lightweight, plug-and-play manner that preserves the pretrained video generation prior remains an open challenge.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference face image (cropped to 512×512 with background replaced by solid white via BiSeNet face parsing) and a free-form text prompt describing the desired scene. It produces an 81-frame video at 832×480 resolution and 25 FPS. The reference image is encoded by the pre-trained VAE into the same latent space as video tokens, then concatenated along the sequence dimension; image tokens are discarded at the final DiT layer, so the output is a standard video with no auxiliary channels.

## Method

Stand-In introduces a conditional image branch into the Wan2.1-14B DiT backbone, where the reference image is mapped into the video latent space via the model's own pre-trained VAE rather than an external face encoder. Two key mechanisms enable lightweight identity injection: (1) Restricted Self-Attention, which prevents image queries from attending to video keys (keeping the reference static) while allowing video queries to attend to both image and video keys (enabling identity reference), and (2) Conditional Position Mapping, which assigns image tokens a disjoint 3D RoPE coordinate space (temporal index −1, spatial offset beyond video dimensions) to preserve the backbone's pretrained positional prior. Only LoRA adapters (rank 128) on the QKV projections of image tokens are trained—adding ~1% parameters (153M)—on just 2,000 video pairs for 3,000 steps. At inference, the image Key and Value matrices are cached across denoising steps (KV Caching) since the reference timestep is fixed at zero, adding only 2.3% runtime overhead.

## Results

On the OpenS2V benchmark, Stand-In achieves a Face Similarity of 0.724, Naturalness of 3.922, and Prompt Following of 20.594, setting new state-of-the-art on the first two metrics across all compared methods (both open- and closed-source). In the user study, Stand-In scores 4.10/5 on facial similarity and 4.08/5 on video quality, outperforming the next-best methods (Hunyuan-Custom at 3.34 and Kling at 3.09) by a wide margin. These results are achieved with only ~1% additional trainable parameters (153M LoRA weights on the 14B Wan2.1 base) and just 2,000 training pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stand-In achieves the highest Face Similarity (0.724) among all 14 compared methods, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline VACE-14B (0.647) by 0.077 and the strongest closed-source method Hailuo (0.577) by 0.147. On Naturalness, Stand-In (3.922) also leads all methods, edging out Kling (3.900) and Phantom-14B (3.828). On Prompt Following, Stand-In (20.594) ranks third overall behind Hailuo and Pika-2.1 (both 20.649) but is the best among open-source methods, narrowly ahead of VACE-P1.3B (20.591) and ConsistID (20.552). In the subjective user study, Stand-In dominates both dimensions: facial similarity 4.10 vs. Hunyuan-Custom's 3.34, and video quality 4.08 vs. Kling's 3.09. Notably, Stand-In achieves these gains with only ~1% additional parameters and 2,000 training pairs, whereas full-parameter methods like Hunyuan-Custom and Phantom require extensive training resources.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in identity-preserving video generation: explicit face-encoder methods (ID-Animator, ConsistID) that extract identity via a separate embedding network, and full-parameter fine-tuning approaches (Phantom, HunyuanCustom) that retrain the entire diffusion transformer. Instead, Stand-In repurposes the pretrained VAE of the base video model (Wan2.1-14B) to encode the reference image into the same latent space, then injects identity through a lightweight restricted self-attention mechanism with conditional RoPE position mapping, adding only ~1% LoRA parameters. The design is conceptually closest to the 'concatenate reference tokens with video tokens' family (ConcatID, VACE) but introduces the key architectural constraint that image queries cannot attend to video keys, combined with a disjoint spatial coordinate space for reference tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the extreme efficiency: 153M trainable parameters (1% of the 14B base) trained on just 2,000 video pairs outperforms full-parameter methods in both face similarity (0.724 vs 0.647) and naturalness (3.922 vs 3.728), while KV caching adds negligible inference overhead (0.07% FLOPs). The plug-and-play design—compatible with VACE for pose guidance, stylization LoRAs, and zero-shot face swapping—suggests a reusable identity-control module rather than a monolithic pipeline. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-person scenes, longer video durations beyond 81 frames, and whether the 2,000-pair training budget is truly sufficient for robust deployment across diverse demographics or whether the OpenS2V benchmark underestimates failure modes.
