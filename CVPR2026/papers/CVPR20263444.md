## Problem

Existing fMRI-to-video reconstruction methods suffer from two persistent failures: inconsistent visual representations of salient objects across frames (appearance mismatch) and poor temporal coherence (motion misalignment or abrupt transitions). The paper argues that the brain perceives video discretely rather than frame-by-frame, suggesting that hierarchical semantic guidance—rather than exhaustive pixel-level supervision—better matches the neural encoding of dynamic visual experience.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263444/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw fMRI BOLD signals with subject-specific voxel counts (e.g., 9,114–14,828 voxels on CC2017; 5,820 on HCP 7T), temporally aligned to video frames at TR = 2 s with a 4-second hemodynamic lag correction. It produces a 12-frame reconstructed video clip. Intermediate outputs include three levels of CLIP-style semantic embeddings (77×768) and a sequence of VAE motion latents, which are progressively fused through a T2I model (for the anchor frame) and an AnimateDiff T2V model (for the full clip).

## Method

SemVideo introduces a two-stage decoding pipeline guided by hierarchical semantic cues generated by SemMiner, an MLLM-based module that decomposes each video into static anchor descriptions, motion-oriented narratives, and holistic summaries. The Semantic Alignment Decoder (SAD) maps variable-dimension fMRI signals through a subject-specific projector and a shared 4-layer MLP + Refineformer encoder to predict CLIP embeddings for all three semantic levels, trained with MSE, SoftCLIP contrastive, and diffusion-refinement losses. The Motion Adaptation Decoder (MAD) projects fMRI into a per-frame latent sequence and applies a tripartite attention fusion—spatial self-attention within each frame, temporal self-attention across frames, and cross-attention conditioned on the predicted motion-semantic embedding—to reconstruct coherent VAE motion latents. Finally, the Conditional Video Render (CVR) performs sequential generation: the motion latents pass through a VAE decoder to yield blurry motion frames, a T2I model synthesizes the anchor frame from the anchor embedding and first motion frame, and AnimateDiff generates the final video jointly conditioned on the holistic semantic embedding, the anchor frame, and the motion-frame sequence.

## Results

On CC2017, SemVideo achieves the best scores on 8 out of 10 metrics: 2-way-I (0.838), 2-way-V (0.865), 50-way-I (0.225), 50-way-V (0.264), Hue-pcc (0.849), CLIP-PCC (0.526), and EPE (4.788), while tying Mind-Animator on VIFI-score (0.608) and SSIM (0.321). It trails NEURONS only on PSNR (9.448 vs. 9.526). On the HCP 7T dataset, SemVideo leads on all semantic, hue, CLIP, and EPE metrics (2-way-I 0.815, 2-way-V 0.796, Hue-pcc 0.858, CLIP 0.523, EPE 4.05), though Mind-Animator retains a PSNR advantage (11.233 vs. 9.336).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemVideo outperforms all eight baselines on the four N-way retrieval metrics (2-way-I, 2-way-V, 50-way-I, 50-way-V), with the largest margins over NeuroClips (+0.032 on 2-way-I, +0.031 on 2-way-V) and NEURONS (+0.023 on 2-way-I, +0.012 on 2-way-V). On spatiotemporal coherence, SemVideo achieves the lowest EPE (4.788) and highest CLIP-PCC (0.526), beating the closest competitor NeuroClips by 0.045 in EPE and 0.013 in CLIP. The only metric where SemVideo does not lead is PSNR, where NEURONS scores 9.526 versus SemVideo's 9.448; however, SemVideo ties Mind-Animator on SSIM (0.321) and VIFI (0.608). On the HCP 7T generalization test, SemVideo leads on every metric except PSNR, where Mind-Animator's 11.233 substantially exceeds SemVideo's 9.336, suggesting Mind-Animator's pixel-level reconstruction remains stronger on this particular dataset. Overall, the paper claims SOTA on 8 of 10 CC2017 metrics, with the most consistent advantage in semantic retrieval and motion fidelity (EPE) over all prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemVideo extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction line that began with Nishimoto (2011) and Wen (2017) and has recently shifted toward diffusion-based generative backbones (Mind-Video, NeuroClips, Mind-Animator, NEURONS). Its key departure is the introduction of hierarchical, multi-level semantic guidance (static anchors, motion narratives, holistic summaries) mined by an MLLM, which prior methods lacked—earlier work either applied per-frame image captioners or relied on masked brain modeling without explicit semantic supervision. The tripartite attention fusion in the Motion Adaptation Decoder and the staged Conditional Video Render (VAE → T2I → T2V) represent a new architectural decomposition that explicitly separates semantic alignment, motion decoding, and video synthesis into distinct, interpretable stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By addressing the two persistent failure modes of fMRI-to-video reconstruction (appearance mismatch and motion misalignment) through structured semantic conditioning, SemVideo sets a new SOTA on 8 of 10 metrics and releases the CC2017-SE dataset extension, lowering the barrier for future semantic-guided decoding research. The ROI-wise interpretability analysis linking decoded components to known visual/motion cortices (MT, MST, V1) provides a neuroscience-grounded validation that goes beyond pixel-level metrics. Open questions remain: the method still depends on pretrained T2V priors (AnimateDiff) for final rendering, the fundamental BOLD temporal resolution (~2 s) caps achievable motion fidelity, and generalization beyond three subjects per dataset is untested. Whether the hierarchical semantic decomposition transfers to other decoding modalities (EEG, MEG) or to longer videos is an important next step.
