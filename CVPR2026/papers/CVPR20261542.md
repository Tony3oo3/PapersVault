## Problem

3D avatar modeling faces a fundamental trade-off: studio-based methods achieve high fidelity but fail to generalize to real-world inputs, while in-the-wild methods generalize broadly but produce blurry, geometrically incomplete avatars due to inherent 3D ambiguities from sparse monocular observations. This paper shows that the large-scale pre/post-training paradigm—proven effective in language and vision models—can simultaneously achieve broad generalization and high-fidelity reconstruction for animatable full-body avatars, a direction previously unexplored in the 3D avatar domain.

## Input / Output

The method takes 1–4 full-body images and corresponding face close-up crops of a subject (processed at 1024×768), extracting features via the Sapiens vision model. It produces a feedforward 3D Gaussian avatar of 65,536 Gaussians (8 per geometric token, 8192 tokens) whose attributes—position, rotation, scale, opacity, and color—are decoded by a canonical MLP head and a lightweight pose-dependent head. Animation is driven by a 138-dim body pose, 128-dim facial expression code, and 6-dim gaze vector, with the pose-dependent head running at 586 FPS on an A100 for real-time rendering via differentiable 3D Gaussian splatting.

## Method

LCA fuses two token streams—image tokens from Sapiens and geometric tokens from a template mesh—through an 8-layer, 800M-parameter transformer that alternates per-image self-attention, geometric self-attention, and masked multimodal attention (following the body-face MMDiT design of LHM) to support a variable number of input views. Two MLP decoder heads produce canonical Gaussian attributes and pose/expression-dependent deltas, which are combined and deformed via linear blend skinning before rendering. The key training insight is a two-stage paradigm: pretraining on 1M in-the-wild monocular videos builds broad appearance and geometry priors, then post-training on 2,737 multi-view studio subjects (200 cameras, 4K) with layer-wise learning-rate decay (0.65) refines fidelity and 3D completeness without erasing generalization. This modular design also enables feature extensions—loose-garment support via a two-level node-based deformation module and relighting via learnable radiance transfer—that require only post-training modifications while preserving generalization to unconstrained inputs.

## Results

In the multi-view setting, LCA achieves 27.48 PSNR on the capture-studio test set and 27.80 PSNR on the in-the-wild test set, outperforming the strongest baseline MV-LHM (26.61 / 25.80 PSNR) by ~0.9 dB in both domains. In the single-view setting, LCA reaches 26.88 PSNR (studio) and 27.69 PSNR (in-the-wild), surpassing LHM (21.90 / 18.32 PSNR) by 5.0 and 9.3 dB respectively. LCA also achieves the lowest LPIPS across all settings (0.041 studio / 0.029 in-the-wild multi-view), indicating superior perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LCA consistently outperforms all three baselines across both multi-view and single-view settings and both studio and in-the-wild domains. Against ExAvatar (optimization-based, multi-view), LCA gains 3.56 dB PSNR on studio data and 9.8 dB on in-the-wild data, with the gap driven largely by ExAvatar's poor generalization to unseen viewpoints during fitting. Against UP2You (multi-view), LCA leads by 7.9 dB (studio) and 8.7 dB (in-the-wild) PSNR. Against LHM (single-view feedforward), LCA achieves a 5.0 dB PSNR improvement on studio and a 9.3 dB improvement in-the-wild, with the largest gains observed on faces and extremities such as fingers and legs. The closest competitor is MV-LHM (the authors' own multi-view extension of LHM), which LCA surpasses by 0.88 dB (studio) and 2.01 dB (in-the-wild) PSNR, confirming that the pre/post-training strategy adds meaningful fidelity beyond simply mixing data sources.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCA builds directly on the LHM feedforward 3D Gaussian avatar paradigm (anchor-point sampling from a template mesh, body-face MMDiT attention blocks) and the VGGT hybrid attention scheme for variable-length image inputs, but departs from both by introducing a two-stage pre/post-training recipe borrowed from LLM and vision foundation models. Prior studio-based avatar work (URAvatar, Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars) achieves high fidelity but poor generalization, while in-the-wild methods (LHM, ExAvatar) generalize but suffer from 3D ambiguity; LCA's key conceptual move is to pretrain on 1M in-the-wild videos for broad priors and then post-train on 2,737 multi-view studio subjects for fidelity, explicitly framing this as the first application of the pre/post-training paradigm to 3D avatar modeling. The relighting extension reuses the learnable radiance transfer from Relightable Full-Body Gaussian Codec Avatars [65], and the loose-garment module draws on node-based deformation ideas from prior cloth simulation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that million-scale pretraining plus targeted post-training simultaneously yields broad generalization and high-fidelity 3D avatars, LCA breaks the long-standing fidelity-generalization trade-off that has constrained the field, and the emergent capabilities (relighting, loose garments, stylized characters) without direct supervision suggest the pretraining stage captures richer human priors than previously assumed. The practical impact is significant: avatars are created in seconds and animated at 586 FPS, making scalable, identity-preserving avatar services feasible. Open questions include handling secondary motion (hair, accessories), heavy occlusions, and whether the paradigm extends to other 3D reconstruction tasks beyond humans; the authors also note that fine embroidery and intricate textures remain challenging.
