## Problem

Existing style-aware image generation methods—textual prompts, reference images, and LoRA fine-tuning—cannot simultaneously achieve high stylistic consistency, the ability to create novel unseen styles, and easy reproducibility. The code-to-style generation paradigm, where a simple numerical code uniquely identifies and reproduces a style, has only been explored in closed-source industrial settings (e.g., Midjourney), leaving no open-source academic framework for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261751/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The primary input is a small integer style code that seeds a random number generator, combined with a free-form text prompt describing the desired content; an optional reference image can also be used for image-conditioned generation. The style code initializes an autoregressive process that produces 196 discrete indices (from a codebook of 1,024 entries, each 64-dimensional), which are decoded into a style embedding. The output is a stylized RGB image whose visual style is determined entirely by the code while its content follows the text prompt; training uses 392×392 reference images.

## Method

CoType comprises three components: (1) a discrete style codebook trained with a contrastive loss that maps same-style images to similar embeddings while pushing different styles apart, augmented by a reconstruction loss to prevent codebook collapse and a standard VQ loss; (2) a DiT-based T2I-DM that receives the quantized style embedding through the textual branch of a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) rather than the visual branch, trained via rectified flow matching on style-matched image pairs; and (3) an autoregressive transformer (Qwen2-0.5B, trained from scratch) that learns the joint distribution of the 196 style indices via next-token prediction, enabling novel style synthesis from a seed. At inference, the numerical code fixes the random seed, the autoregressive model generates the full index sequence, and a high-frequency index suppression strategy down-weights over-represented 'placeholder' indices to preserve stylistic diversity.

## Results

On the code-to-style task, CoType achieves a Consistency score of 0.6007, substantially outperforming Midjourney (0.4734) by 12.7 points, while also surpassing it on Aesthetics (0.7173 vs. 0.5948). CoType trails Midjourney slightly on Diversity (0.7764 vs. 0.8088), which the authors attribute to limited training data breadth. In the image-conditioned setting, CoType* achieves the best Consistency (0.5791) among all compared methods, edging out InstantStyleXL (0.5753) and significantly outperforming Flux-Kontext (0.4222) and USO (0.4395).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Midjourney (the only other code-to-style method), CoType wins decisively on Consistency (0.6007 vs. 0.4734, +12.7 pts) and Aesthetics (0.7173 vs. 0.5948, +12.3 pts), but loses on Diversity (0.7764 vs. 0.8088, −3.2 pts). Among image-conditioned baselines, CoType* achieves the highest Consistency (0.5791), narrowly beating InstantStyleXL (0.5753) and substantially outperforming CSGO (0.5540), StyleStudio (0.4711), USO (0.4395), and Flux-Kontext (0.4222). On Aesthetics, Flux-Kontext leads (0.7636) with CoType* close behind (0.7178), while on CLIP-T, USO scores highest (0.3331) with CoType* second (0.3230). Overall, CoType's primary advantage is style consistency across both code-to-style and image-conditioned settings, at a modest cost in diversity for the code-based task.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoType combines three established building blocks—vector-quantized codebooks (VQ-VAE, Taming Transformers), autoregressive next-token prediction (Qwen2-0.5B), and diffusion-transformer generation (DiT with rectified flow)—into a pipeline that no prior academic work has assembled for style control. It departs from the dominant image-conditioned style transfer paradigm (StyleStudio, CSGO, USO, Flux-Kontext, InstantStyle) and LoRA-based approaches by replacing pixel-level or weight-level style specifications with a compact discrete index sequence, and it explicitly positions itself as the first open-source counterpart to Midjourney's proprietary style-code feature. The contrastive training objective for the codebook and the high-frequency index suppression strategy are the paper's principal methodological departures from standard VQ usage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making code-to-style generation reproducible and open-source, CoType lowers the barrier for academic research on discrete stylistic representations and enables practical style sharing via a single integer rather than reference images or LoRA weights. The style-interpolation capability (linearly blending index subsets) opens a path toward composable, user-controllable style mixing that prior image-conditioned methods cannot easily support. Key limitations include a diversity gap versus Midjourney (attributed to training-data breadth) and an evaluation limited to 500 codes × 4 images, leaving open whether the approach scales to a truly vast style space or generalizes to video and 3D generation.
