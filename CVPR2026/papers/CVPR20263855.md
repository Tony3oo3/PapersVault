## Problem

Existing Gene-to-WSI synthesis methods compress RNA-Seq profiles into a single static embedding injected once at initialization, causing transcriptomic signal decay, fixed-resolution rigidity, and entanglement with non-biological confounders such as staining artifacts and tumor purity. The inverse task of generating histology directly from gene expression remains methodologically underdeveloped, with no framework that dynamically reinforces molecular guidance across scales while explicitly disentangling causal biological signals from spurious technical variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 17,655-dimensional bulk RNA-Seq expression vector as input, encoded via a β-VAE into a 200-dimensional latent prior that is injected at every autoregressive decoding stage. It produces a 256×256 H&E-stained WSI tile at 20× magnification, generated as K hierarchical token maps (via multi-scale vector quantization) decoded progressively from coarse to fine scale, requiring only 10 generation steps. Each RNA-Seq profile is paired with the set of non-overlapping tiles extracted from its corresponding gigapixel WSI.

## Method

GeneVAR reformulates Gene-to-WSI synthesis as an iterative, coarse-to-fine autoregressive process over multi-scale vector-quantized (MSVQ) token maps, with three coupled components: a β-VAE gene encoder, a Causal MeanFlow module, and RNA-guided masked autoregression. Causal MeanFlow replaces instantaneous-velocity flow matching with an average-velocity formulation conditioned on both transcriptomic embeddings and scale-aligned semantic features via adaptive layer normalization, enabling one-step generation. A counterfactual regularization loss applies three image degradations (color anomaly, contrast adjustment, sharpening) to construct negative velocity targets, pushing the learned flow to suppress non-biological variation while preserving scale-invariant morphology. Gradient-guided masking computes the ℓ₂ norm of reconstruction-error gradients with respect to scale embeddings to identify causally salient token positions, which are preferentially retained during autoregressive decoding while non-salient positions are replaced by a learnable mask token. The full model is a ViT-based transformer with a causal mask, trained with cross-entropy on token predictions plus the Causal MeanFlow velocity-matching objective.

## Results

On the pooled TCGA benchmark (ALL), GeneVAR achieves a FID of 12.95 with only 10 generation steps and 317M parameters, outperforming the strongest scale-wise autoregressive baseline VAR (16.83) by 3.88 FID and the best diffusion model U-ViT (18.55) by 5.60 FID. Per-cohort FIDs range from 11.20 (GBM) to 19.86 (COAD), with the largest margin over VAR on COAD (19.86 vs. 25.84, a 5.98 FID gap). In downstream tile classification with 75% synthetic substitution, GeneVAR yields 0.592 ACC / 0.588 F1, surpassing VAR (0.521 / 0.510) and RNA-CDM (0.492 / 0.472); with full synthetic pretraining (q=1.0), it reaches 0.767 ACC / 0.766 F1, the highest among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeneVAR wins on FID against all nine baselines across every TCGA cohort. The closest competitor is VAR [41] (16.83 ALL FID), which GeneVAR surpasses by 3.88 FID; the gap widens to 5.98 on the hardest cohort (COAD). Among diffusion-based methods, U-ViT [2] (18.55) and DiT [30] (18.11) are the strongest, yet GeneVAR still leads by 5.60 and 5.16 FID respectively while using only 10 steps versus 100–250. The flow-matching baseline SiT [28] (18.84) and token-wise autoregressive LlamaGen [39] (17.43) trail by 5.89 and 4.48 FID. The original Gene-to-WSI diffusion method RNA-CDM [4] (23.36) is outperformed by a margin of 10.41 FID, and the text-conditioned PathLDM [45] (20.29) by 7.34. In downstream classification, GeneVAR-generated tiles maintain or improve accuracy under 75% substitution (0.592 ACC vs. 0.521 for VAR and 0.492 for RNA-CDM), and synthetic pretraining yields the largest gains among all methods (0.767 ACC at q=1.0 vs. 0.708 for VAR and 0.650 for RNA-CDM), confirming that the generative fidelity translates into functional utility for WSI-level MIL classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeneVAR extends the coarse-to-fine visual autoregressive framework of VAR [41] (which introduced MSVQ and scale-wise next-scale prediction) into the molecular domain by conditioning each autoregressive step on compact β-VAE gene embeddings rather than class or text tokens. Its Causal MeanFlow module builds directly on the average-velocity formulation of MeanFlow [14], adding RNA-conditioned adaLN fusion and counterfactual regularization to suppress non-biological confounders. The paper departs from prior Gene-to-WSI methods (RNA-CDM, RNAGAN) that inject a single global transcriptomic embedding once at initialization, instead reformulating synthesis as an iterative process where molecular guidance is re-injected at every scale, and from standard flow matching by replacing instantaneous velocity with a one-step average-velocity target augmented by counterfactual degradation targets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first autoregressive Gene-to-WSI model, and the demonstration that synthetic tiles can *improve* downstream classification when substituting for real data (while baselines degrade) suggests that causal regularization captures genuine biological signal beyond visual fidelity. The 10-step generation matching or exceeding 250-step diffusion models offers a practical efficiency advantage for large-scale synthetic cohort generation. Open questions include whether the causal framing generalizes beyond TCGA bulk RNA-Seq to spatial transcriptomics or single-cell data, whether the counterfactual interventions (color/contrast/sharpening) adequately capture the full space of non-biological confounders, and whether the approach scales to gigapixel WSI-level generation rather than 256×256 tiles.
