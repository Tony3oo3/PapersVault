## Problem

Existing neural B-frame codecs exhibit limited performance gains in high-resolution videos with large motion, where optical flow estimation becomes unreliable and naive bi-directional prediction fusion introduces ghosting and temporal inconsistency. No end-to-end neural video codec had been evaluated on 4K-resolution content, leaving a critical gap in understanding how learned B-frame compression scales to modern high-resolution video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263482/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec consumes RGB frames (converted from YUV420 via BT.709) at resolutions up to 4K, organized in a hierarchical B-frame structure with GOP size 32 and intra-period 32. It produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic coding and reconstructed frames, with the motion encoder operating on multi-scale optical flows (original, half, and quarter resolution) and the decoder emitting per-pixel confidence maps alongside reconstructed flows at three resolutions.

## Method

HR-NVC reorganizes B-frame coding around three principles: (1) Spatio-Temporal Anchored Motion Estimation stabilizes SPyNet-based flow estimation by injecting a spatial anchor (coarse flow from 1/4-resolution downsampling) and a temporal virtual anchor (a synthesized intermediate frame interpolated from forward and backward references), preventing the error propagation that plagues coarse-to-fine refinement under large displacements; (2) Hierarchical Motion Representation encodes multi-scale flows in a coarse-to-fine pyramid where the decoder sequentially emits reconstructed motions and one-channel confidence maps at three resolutions, enabling compact coding of motion reliability without explicit residual compression; (3) Bi-Contextual Asymmetric Harmonization replaces naive concatenation of warped bi-directional features with confidence-weighted fusion (F_harm = λ̈_f·F_f_warp + λ̈_b·F_b_warp), adaptively suppressing unreliable references near occlusions and scene transitions before a lightweight refinement stage restores structural consistency.

## Results

On the 97-frame JCT-VC benchmark (BD-rate for PSNR, anchor HM-LDB), HR-NVC achieves an overall average of −44.27%, outperforming the strongest neural B-frame baseline DCVC-B (−39.50%) by 4.77 percentage points and the traditional VVC reference VTM-RA (−48.12%) on JCT-VC Class A (−51.87% vs. −48.50%). On full JCT-VC sequences, HR-NVC maintains a −49.53% average, surpassing DCVC-B (−44.97%) and all P-frame NVC baselines. On 4K JVET Class A1/A2, HR-NVC delivers −31.84% average BD-rate, the first end-to-end neural B-frame codec evaluated at this resolution, beating DCVC-B (−27.53%) and VVenC-Slow (−1.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest neural B-frame codec DCVC-B, HR-NVC wins on every JCT-VC class (e.g., −51.87% vs. −47.73% on Class A; −55.91% vs. −50.30% on Class D) and on UVG (−33.39% vs. −26.82%), yielding a 4.77-point overall advantage. Compared to the traditional VVC reference VTM-RA, HR-NVC surpasses it on JCT-VC Class A (−51.87% vs. −48.50%) and Class D (−55.91% vs. −46.56%), but trails on Classes B, C, and E, resulting in a slightly lower overall average (−44.27% vs. −48.12%). Against the older neural B-frame method B-CANF, HR-NVC dominates by a wide margin (−44.27% vs. −14.35% overall), with B-CANF even showing positive BD-rate on MCL-JCV. On the full-sequence evaluation (Table 2), HR-NVC (−49.53%) outperforms both uni-directional NVC baselines DCVC-DC (−38.58%) and DCVC-FM (−42.43%) by substantial margins, confirming that the bi-directional design with confidence-guided harmonization provides consistent gains over P-frame-only approaches. On 4K sequences (Table 3), HR-NVC (−31.84%) beats DCVC-B (−27.53%) and VVenC-Slow (−1.46%), establishing the first end-to-end neural B-frame result at 4K resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural B-frame coding line (DCVC-B, B-CANF, hierarchical B-frame NVC) by introducing a unifying 'Reference Information Modeling' principle that decomposes bi-directional compression into representation, translation, and harmonization. Rather than simply replacing traditional codec modules (AMC, HMVP, AMVR) with independent neural blocks as prior NVCs do, it stabilizes motion estimation via spatio-temporal anchors (low-resolution flow priors and virtual intermediate frames) and replaces naive bi-directional feature concatenation with confidence-guided asymmetric fusion. The optical flow backbone remains SPyNet, consistent with the NVC community's efficiency constraints, but the hierarchical multi-scale motion coding with per-pixel confidence maps is a departure from single-scale flow compression in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first end-to-end neural B-frame codec evaluated on 4K-resolution video, HR-NVC establishes a practical benchmark that was previously inaccessible to learned codecs, and its ~5% BD-rate gain over DCVC-B on JCT-VC plus matching VTM-RA on Class A demonstrates that principled reference organization can close the gap with traditional standards at high resolution. The confidence-guided fusion mechanism is modular and could be transplanted to other NVC architectures or motion estimation backbones. Open questions include whether the virtual anchor frame approach generalizes to even larger temporal intervals (e.g., 8K or long-GOP scenarios), whether perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, VMAF) show similar gains as PSNR, and whether the method can be made real-time given the ~1 second per-frame encoding latency on a 4090.
