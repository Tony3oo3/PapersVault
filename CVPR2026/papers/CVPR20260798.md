## Problem

Diffusion transformers lock per-step FLOPs to input resolution and allocate computation uniformly across spatial tokens, wasting resources on easy image regions. The paper demonstrates through a controlled zero-padding experiment that vanilla DiTs cannot reallocate extra compute to harder regions, making principled latency-quality trade-offs impossible without architectural changes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260798/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional image generation, the model takes a class label and Gaussian noise at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution (patchified into 16×16 or 32×32 spatial token grids); for video, it takes 29 frames at 256px; for the Qwen-Image experiment, it takes a text prompt plus noise at 1024px. The output is a generated image or video, but the key unusual aspect is that the number of retained latent tokens per group at inference is a user-controlled knob that directly sets the per-step FLOPs, yielding a smooth quality-compute continuum from full budget down to ~37% of original cost.

## Method

ELIT inserts a variable-length latent interface (K learnable tokens) between a short spatial head and tail, with most transformer blocks operating in the latent domain. Two lightweight cross-attention layers—Read (spatial→latent) and Write (latent→spatial)—transfer information between domains, using grouped cross-attention to reduce cost from O(NK) to O(NK/G). During training, a random prefix-keeping scheme drops tail latents each iteration, forcing the model to store globally important information in earlier latents and detail-refining information in later ones. At inference, retaining only the first J̃ ≤ J latents per group yields a valid, lower-cost model call. This multi-budget design also natively enables autoguidance and cheap classifier-free guidance (CCFG) by running the guidance term at a smaller latent budget, reducing inference FLOPs by ~33% without retraining.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K 512px, ELIT-MB (multi-budget) achieves FID 10.1, FDD 164.1, and IS 88.8 with DiT-XL backbone, compared to DiT-XL's FID 18.8, FDD 339.2, IS 53.0 — a 46% FID reduction and 52% FDD reduction. The gains are consistent across backbones: ELIT-MB reaches FID 7.7 on U-ViT-XL (vs. 11.6 baseline) and FID 9.6 on HDiT-XL (vs. 13.0 baseline). On Kinetics-700 256px video, ELIT-DiT improves FDD from 371.5 to 277.4 and FVD from 135.9 to 116.5 over DiT-XL. The paper reports an average 35.3% FID and 39.6% FDD improvement across architectures at 512px.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiT-XL, ELIT-MB delivers the largest relative gains: 46% FID reduction (18.8→10.1), 52% FDD reduction (339.2→164.1), and 68% IS improvement (53.0→88.8) at 512px, attributed to both the latent interface and the multi-budget tail-dropping training. Against U-ViT-XL, ELIT-MB achieves 34% FID reduction (11.6→7.7) and 33% FDD reduction (202.7→135.8), with the absolute best FID of 7.7 among all configurations. Against HDiT-XL, ELIT-MB improves FID by 26% (13.0→9.6) and FDD by 34% (260.3→171.2). On the large-scale Qwen-Image (20B MM-DiT) model, ELIT-Qwen-Image shows a modest 0.82-point average score gap on DPG-Bench (90.45 vs. 91.27 at full budget) but enables up to 63% FLOP reduction (≈2.7× speedup) while maintaining scores above 88.0 even at 12.5% token budget, demonstrating graceful degradation rather than outright superiority at matched compute.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELIT builds directly on the DiT architecture and adapts the interleaved Read/Write cross-attention mechanism from FIT [12] into a variable-length latent interface that sits between a short spatial head and tail. It departs from adaptive-generator approaches (FlexiDiT, Slimmable Networks) that still spread computation uniformly across tokens, from token-dropping methods (MaskDiT, TREAD) that accelerate training but not inference, and from RINs/FITs that use latent tokens but fix the inference budget and diverge structurally from DiTs. The key conceptual departure is making the latent interface length a user-controlled inference knob via random tail-dropping during training, which no prior DiT-compatible method achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling per-step FLOPs from input resolution with only two added cross-attention layers, ELIT offers a drop-in path to flexible compute allocation across the dominant DiT family (DiT, U-ViT, HDiT, MM-DiT) without changing the training objective. The 35–40% FID/FDD gains and 3.3–4× convergence speedup, validated across four architectures and two resolutions, suggest the technique is likely to be widely adopted in production diffusion pipelines. Open questions include whether from-scratch large-scale training (beyond fine-tuning Qwen-Image) preserves the gains, whether CCFG's tendency to saturate images limits its use in practice, and whether per-step budget schedulers (allocating fewer latents early in sampling) can push efficiency further.
