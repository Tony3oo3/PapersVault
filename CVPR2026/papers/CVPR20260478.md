## Problem

Existing medical MLLMs suffer from three persistent limitations: reliance on distilled proprietary data that lacks fine-grained clinical grounding, training pipelines that use only generative outputs without structured supervision (amplifying hallucination), and narrow focus on individual tasks or single imaging modalities rather than unified cross-modal generalization. No open-source model simultaneously achieves strong medical understanding (VQA, report generation) and precise spatial grounding (bounding-box localization) across diverse modalities like radiology, pathology, ophthalmology, and microscopy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260478/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MedMO accepts medical images across heterogeneous modalities (X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound, pathology, ophthalmology, dermatology, surgical video) at two resolution tiers—768×768 for global alignment and 1280×1280 for fine-grained spatial tasks—paired with free-form text prompts or structured clinical queries. Outputs are dual-format: natural-language responses (captions, diagnostic answers, full radiology reports) and, for grounding tasks, bounding-box coordinates in XYXY format that localize anatomical structures, lesions, or cells relative to the image canvas. The bounding-box output is resolution-invariant, normalized by the image diagonal length.

## Method

MedMO is built on Qwen3-VL (4B/8B) and trained through a four-stage progressive pipeline over 26M+ samples from 45 datasets: (1) large-scale general medical SFT on 18.5M MedTrinity pairs at 768×768; (2) high-resolution grounding SFT on 3M expert-annotated samples at 1280×1280 introducing bounding-box supervision; (3) instruction tuning on 4.3M multimodal pairs for clinical reasoning alignment; and (4) GRPO-based reinforcement learning on 300K samples with a composite verifiable reward. The key technical novelty is the Bounding Box Reward, which computes per-pair quality via a weighted combination of L1 coordinate distance (resolution-invariant, normalized by image diagonal) and GIoU, assigns predictions to ground-truth boxes through Hungarian matching, and applies FP/FN penalties—providing a spatially grounded, differentiable signal that directly optimizes localization precision during RL. The vision-language adapter uses a DeepStack fusion mechanism to project multi-level ViT features into the LLM embedding space, preserving fine-grained visual detail for both global understanding and pixel-level grounding.

## Results

On VQA benchmarks, MedMO-8B achieves an average of 60.8%, within 0.6% of the medical SOTA Fleming-VL-8B (61.4%) and +21.3% over the Qwen3VL-8B baseline (39.5%). On Text QA, MedMO-8B reaches 61.3% average, surpassing Fleming-VL-8B (45.7%) by +15.6 points and the baseline by +7.7 points. For medical report generation on MIMIC-CXR, MedMO-8B attains CIDEr 140.0 and RaTE 57.1%, outperforming Fleming-VL-8B (CIDEr 132.5, RaTE 56.7%). On grounding, MedMO-8B achieves 54.2% average IoU, a +40.4 point improvement over Qwen3VL-8B (13.8%) and +37.0 over Fleming-VL-8B (17.2%), with particularly strong results on Bacteria segmentation (54.6% vs. 8.3% for Fleming-VL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen3VL-8B baseline, MedMO-8B achieves substantial gains across all task categories: +21.3 on VQA average (60.8 vs. 39.5), +7.7 on Text QA average (61.3 vs. 53.6), and +40.4 on grounding IoU (54.2 vs. 13.8), with the largest single-benchmark jump on VQA-RAD (+33.5) and Bacteria segmentation (+45.4 IoU). Compared to the medical SOTA Fleming-VL-8B, MedMO-8B wins decisively on Text QA (+15.6 avg, e.g., MedQA 84.3 vs. 53.7, MMLU-Med 81.0 vs. 71.8) and grounding (+37.0 avg IoU, e.g., DeepLesion 38.5 vs. 0.0, MedSG multi-view 75.8 vs. 42.0), but trails by a narrow 0.6 points on VQA average (60.8 vs. 61.4), with Fleming-VL retaining an edge on SLAKE (80.0 vs. 70.0) and OMVQA (88.2 vs. 84.8). Against Lingshu-7B, MedMO-8B leads on VQA average (+12.8), Text QA average (+8.2), and grounding average (+40.3). Versus earlier open-source medical models (LLaVA-Med-7B, HuatuoGPT-V-7B, Qwen2.5VL-7B), MedMO-8B consistently outperforms by 10–25 points on VQA and 10–30 points on Text QA. On report generation, MedMO-8B surpasses Fleming-VL-8B on CIDEr (140.0 vs. 132.5 on MIMIC-CXR; 270.4 vs. 35.8 on Med-Trinity) and Semb (50.0 vs. 33.6), though Fleming-VL retains a slight ROUGE-L advantage on MIMIC-CXR (35.7 vs. 31.7). Against closed-source models (GPT-4.1, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini-2.5-Flash), MedMO-8B remains competitive on Text QA (61.3 vs. 69.9–73.1) but lags on VQA (60.8 vs. 61.5–65.1), indicating that proprietary models still hold an edge on multimodal visual reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedMO builds directly on the Qwen3-VL architecture (successor to Qwen2.5-VL) and adopts GRPO/DAPO-style reinforcement learning for its final training stage, extending the RLVR paradigm from general-domain reasoning to medical spatial grounding. It departs from prior medical MLLMs (LLaVA-Med, HuatuoGPT-Vision, Fleming-VL, Lingshu) by treating bounding-box grounding as a first-class output alongside text generation, and by introducing a verifiable GIoU-based bounding-box reward into the RL objective—a signal absent from prior medical post-training pipelines that relied solely on text-based rewards. The four-stage progressive recipe (large-scale SFT → high-resolution grounding → instruction tuning → RL) generalizes the two-stage alignment-then-tune pattern used by earlier medical VLMs into a more granular curriculum.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedMO is the first open-source medical MLLM to demonstrate strong, consistent spatial grounding (bounding-box IoU) across radiology, pathology, and microscopy alongside VQA and report generation, filling a gap where prior medical models produced only textual outputs. The +43.8 IoU gain on Bacteria segmentation and the 75–77% IoU on MedSG multi-view/tracking tasks suggest that fine-grained grounding supervision combined with high-resolution training can substantially close the localization gap in clinical imaging. Key open questions include whether the stage-wise pipeline's catastrophic forgetting (acknowledged in the Limitations section) can be mitigated, whether the RL bounding-box reward generalizes to 3D volumetric grounding, and whether the large Med-Trinity report-generation scores (270.4 CIDEr) reflect genuine capability or train/test overlap since MedTrinity serves as both the 18.5M-sample training corpus and an evaluation benchmark.
