## Problem

Existing intermediate representations for portrait generation—2D landmarks and low-rank parametric 3D models like FLAME and SMPL-X—suffer from severe signal sparsity and limited disentanglement, making them unable to capture high-frequency, subject-specific geometric details such as wrinkles and micro-expressions while preserving identity. This representational bottleneck causes identity drift and expression loss in both self- and cross-reenactment, particularly for complex, expressive motions that exceed the low-dimensional template subspace.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference portrait image (cropped to 512×512) and a driving video sequence as input. From the reference image, a personalized dense head mesh is optimized; from the driving video, SMPL-X expression coefficients (100-dim) and jaw poses (3-dim) are extracted per frame. The output is a temporally coherent portrait video at 512×512 resolution in which the reference subject faithfully reenacts the driving subject's poses and expressions, evaluated on unseen RAVDESS and NeRSemble test sets.

## Method

The core contribution is a personalized head representation built atop SMPL-X: the base mesh is upsampled via barycentric interpolation to a dense topology, and two decoupled per-vertex offset fields are jointly optimized—a static identity offset capturing expression-invariant geometry in non-facial regions, and a dynamic expression offset capturing frame-specific nonlinear skin deformations in the facial region. A temporal regularizer enforces disentanglement by forcing the static field to represent the geometric average while dynamic fields model only zero-mean expressive deviations. For cross-identity transfer, a lightweight geometry MLP predicts per-vertex dynamic offsets for the target subject by conditioning on both the target's neutral mesh and an encoded driving signal, resolving anatomical incompatibility between identities. A pretrained Wan video diffusion model (DiT in latent space) is fine-tuned with rendered normal maps from the personalized head representation as control signals, using a 3D convolutional pose encoder for driving frames and a 2D convolutional reference encoder for identity.

## Results

On self-reenactment (RAVDESS + NeRSemble, 512×512), ExpPortrait achieves PSNR 26.55, SSIM 0.859, LPIPS 0.184, and L1 0.0216, outperforming the strongest baseline Wan-Animate (PSNR 26.51, SSIM 0.844, LPIPS 0.214). For cross-reenactment, it attains CSIM 0.835, AED 0.211, and APD 0.0128, again leading all baselines. The method also achieves the best motion accuracy (AED 0.132, APD 0.0095) in self-reenactment, indicating more faithful expression and pose transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExpPortrait outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both self- and cross-reenactment. The closest competitor is Wan-Animate, which also uses a DiT-based video diffusion backbone; ExpPortrait edges it out on PSNR (26.55 vs. 26.51), SSIM (0.859 vs. 0.844), LPIPS (0.184 vs. 0.214), and cross-reenactment APD (0.0128 vs. 0.0175). Among explicit-control methods, Follow-Your-Emoji achieves the best PSNR (25.69) but lags significantly on LPIPS (0.236 vs. 0.184) and cross-reenactment identity (CSIM 0.803 vs. 0.835), reflecting the expressivity ceiling of low-rank parametric signals. Implicit-control baselines (LivePortrait, X-NeMo, Hunyuan Portrait) show weaker identity preservation in cross-reenactment (CSIM 0.729–0.830) and higher AED, confirming the identity-leakage issue the paper targets. AniPortrait and Hunyuan Portrait trail most broadly, with SSIM below 0.81 and LPIPS above 0.27, indicating noticeably lower reconstruction fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SMPL-X parametric body model as a coarse geometric prior and on the Wan video diffusion transformer as the generation backbone, but departs fundamentally from both the explicit-control lineage (2D landmarks, FLAME/3DMM-derived maps) and the implicit-control lineage (latent motion encoders in LivePortrait, X-NeMo, Hunyuan Portrait). Its core conceptual move is to replace the low-rank blendshape subspace with per-subject, decoupled static and dynamic per-vertex offset fields learned via differentiable rendering, thereby raising the information density of the intermediate signal while preserving the 3D consistency that parametric models provide. The identity-adaptive expression transfer module (a geometry MLP conditioned on target neutral geometry and encoded driving codes) is a new architectural component not present in prior reenactment pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a long-standing tension in portrait video generation: sparse or low-rank control signals limit expressiveness, while implicit latent features leak identity. By demonstrating that a personalized, high-detail 3D head proxy can serve as a conditioning signal for a DiT-based video generator, it offers a principled path toward film-grade digital humans with strong identity preservation and fine-grained expression control. However, the per-subject optimization requirement limits scalability, and the authors acknowledge that inner-mouth geometry (tongue) and fine-grained eyeball motion remain unmodeled. Open questions include whether the offset-field formulation can be made amortized (single forward pass rather than per-subject fitting), whether it extends naturally to full-body or multi-person animation, and whether the approach remains effective at higher resolutions or longer video durations.
