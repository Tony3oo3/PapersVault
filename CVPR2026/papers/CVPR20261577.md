## Problem

Partial occlusion in multi-object tracking causes inaccurate detections that destabilize Kalman Filter estimates and create ambiguous position cost matrices, leading to frequent identity switches. Existing remedies (appearance features, motion direction, detection confidence) are themselves degraded by occlusion, leaving positional cost confusion as a persistent and under-addressed failure mode in position-based association.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261577/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-frame bounding box detections from an external detector (e.g., OC-SORT's detector) along with their confidence scores, and produces per-frame tracked bounding boxes with persistent identity assignments. It operates entirely on 2D bounding box geometry—bottom-edge y-coordinates for depth ordering and pixel-level overlap for occlusion computation—requiring no appearance features, no camera intrinsics, and no additional training. The framework is training-free and plug-and-play, integrating into existing trackers' association and KF update stages without architectural changes.

## Method

OA-SORT introduces three training-free components built around an explicit occlusion severity estimate. The Occlusion-Aware Module (OAM) determines front-back relationships via bottom-edge depth ordering, computes a per-object occlusion coefficient as the ratio of occluded pixel area to total bounding box area, and refines it with a Gaussian Map that adaptively down-weights pixels near box boundaries to suppress background influence. The Occlusion-Aware Offset (OAO) blends this coefficient (computed on KF predictions) into the IoU-based spatial consistency score during first-stage association, increasing the position cost for occluded trajectories to prevent false matches. The Bias-Aware Momentum (BAM) uses the occlusion coefficient from the trajectory's latest observation, multiplied by the IoU between prediction and current low-score detection, to form a momentum weight that blends the raw detection with the KF prediction during the update step, thereby suppressing estimation drift from occlusion-corrupted detections.

## Results

On the DanceTrack test set, OA-SORT achieves 63.1% HOTA, 48.5% AssA, and 64.2% IDF1, improving over the Hybrid-SORT baseline by +0.9 HOTA, +1.1 AssA, and +1.2 IDF1. On SportsMOT, OA-SORT reaches 75.2% HOTA and 75.8% IDF1 (+0.4 HOTA, +0.7 IDF1 over Hybrid-SORT), and on MOT17-test it attains 64.2% HOTA and 79.1% IDF1 (+0.6 HOTA, +0.7 IDF1). Integrating the occlusion-aware framework into four other trackers (ByteTrack, OC-SORT, SparseTrack, PD-SORT) yields average gains of +2.08% HOTA and +3.05% IDF1, confirming plug-and-play reusability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OA-SORT consistently outperforms its direct baseline Hybrid-SORT across all three primary datasets: +0.9 HOTA / +1.2 IDF1 on DanceTrack, +0.4 HOTA / +0.7 IDF1 on SportsMOT, and +0.6 HOTA / +0.7 IDF1 on MOT17. Against other position-association trackers on DanceTrack, OA-SORT surpasses C-TWiX (62.1 HOTA) by +1.0, C-BIoU (60.6) by +2.5, and StrongSORT++ (55.6) by +7.5. The plug-and-play integration experiments show that adding OAO+BAM to ByteTrack, OC-SORT, SparseTrack, and PD-SORT improves each by 1.9–2.3 HOTA and 1.9–4.0 IDF1, even though PD-SORT and SparseTrack already exploit pseudo-depth cues. On MOT17, OA-SORT also edges out BOT-SORT-REID by +0.5 AssA and +0.3 IDF1, and improves OA-BOT (BOT-SORT without ReID) by +0.5 HOTA. The only metric where OA-SORT trails its baseline is MOTA on MOT17 (79.6 vs. 80.6), which the authors note is not the primary focus since their framework targets association accuracy rather than detection-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Hybrid-SORT's three-stage association pipeline and HMIoU metric, positioning itself within the position-association paradigm of MOT. It departs from prior occlusion-handling strategies—appearance features (DeepSORT), motion direction (OC-SORT), pseudo-depth cues (PD-SORT, SparseTrack), and confidence-based association (ByteTrack)—by explicitly quantifying occlusion severity via bottom-edge depth ordering, IoU overlap, and a Gaussian Map refinement, then injecting this coefficient into both the association cost (OAO) and the Kalman Filter update (BAM). The key conceptual shift is treating occlusion as a first-class, measurable signal rather than an implicit confound addressed indirectly through auxiliary cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes the occlusion-aware framework immediately deployable across diverse SORT-based trackers, as demonstrated by consistent gains on four additional association methods. However, the bottom-edge depth ordering assumption breaks down for airborne objects (jumping) or when lower body parts are occluded, and the lack of long-term occlusion temporal modeling limits robustness in sustained occlusion scenarios. Open questions include whether more principled depth estimation (e.g., learned or multi-view) could replace the heuristic bottom-edge rule, and whether explicit occlusion state tracking over time could further stabilize association during prolonged occlusions.
