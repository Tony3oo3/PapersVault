## Problem

Neural network weights are traditionally viewed as opaque, high-dimensional vectors whose inherent permutation symmetry makes functionally identical networks arbitrarily far apart in weight space, rendering the distribution multi-modal and difficult to learn. While implicit neural representations (INRs) naturally encode data as network parameters, it remains unclear whether these chaotic weight configurations can be transformed into structured, semantically meaningful representations suitable for reconstruction, generation, and discriminative tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263461/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each data instance (a 128×128 face image or a 3D shape) is overfit by a single neural field, and the resulting mLoRA weight vector—comprising low-rank A and B matrices across all layers—serves directly as the instance representation without any additional encoding step. These compact weight vectors (~27K parameters) can then be used for reconstruction, fed into a diffusion model to synthesize new instances, or passed to classifiers and clustering algorithms. All instances share the same frozen base model, so only the small LoRA adaptation parameters vary per instance.

## Method

The core contribution is multiplicative LoRA (mLoRA), where weight updates are applied via element-wise multiplication (W ⊙ BA) rather than the standard additive formulation (W + BA), which preserves channel structure and avoids the feature entanglement that plagues additive updates in neural fields. A pre-trained base neural field (trained via the variational autodecoder paradigm) provides transferable features, and asymmetric masking—freezing √d_out entries per row in each LoRA A matrix—breaks the internal GL(r) permutation symmetry. For generative modeling, a hierarchical diffusion transformer with a dedicated LoRA layer encoder processes the structured weights, using intra-layer multi-head attention over rank components and cross-layer attention for global relationships. The multiplicative formulation naturally aligns with modulation mechanisms in generative neural fields, and once permutation symmetry is eliminated, mLoRA weights are provably aligned with the base network's channel axes, yielding strong linear mode connectivity.

## Results

On 2D FFHQ generation, mLoRA-Asym achieves a Fréchet Distance of 0.073 and MMD-G of 0.039, substantially outperforming HyperDiffusion (FD 0.241, MMD-G 0.158). On 3D ShapeNet, mLoRA-Asym reaches FD 0.011 (Airplane) and FD 0.026 (10-category Multi), compared to HyperDiffusion's 0.027 and 0.117 respectively. For reconstruction, mLoRA-Asym attains the best PSNR of 36.91 dB on FFHQ and lowest Chamfer Distance of 2.41×10⁻² on ShapeNet Airplane. In discriminative tasks, mLoRA achieves 90.0% logistic regression accuracy and 85.1% 1-NN accuracy on ShapeNet 10-category classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is HyperDiffusion [9], a diffusion transformer over standalone neural field weights. mLoRA-Asym outperforms HyperDiffusion on every generation metric across all three settings: on FFHQ, FD drops from 0.241 to 0.073 (3.3× improvement) and MMD-G from 0.158 to 0.039 (4× improvement); on ShapeNet Airplane, FD improves from 0.027 to 0.011; and on ShapeNet Multi, FD improves from 0.117 to 0.026 (4.5× improvement). HyperDiffusion degrades substantially in the multi-category 3D setting (mMD jumps from 2.39 to 8.64), while mLoRA-Asym remains stable (1.89 to 5.52). Additive LoRA variants (LoRA, LoRA-Asym) fail catastrophically on 3D generation (mMD > 100, COV < 11%), confirming that the multiplicative formulation is essential. On FFHQ, HyperDiffusion fails to produce recognizable faces, whereas both mLoRA and mLoRA-Asym succeed, marking the first successful weight-space generation at 128×128 resolution for natural images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: LoRA-based parameter-efficient adaptation (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and weight-space diffusion for neural fields (HyperDiffusion, Erkoç et al., ICCV 2023). It departs from the broader weight-space learning literature (model merging, equivariant neural functionals, GL-equivariant LoRA processors) by not building a separate encoder over weights, but instead enforcing structure directly on the weight space through a multiplicative LoRA parameterization and asymmetric masking. The key conceptual departure from HyperDiffusion is the hypothesis that the *choice of adaptation mechanism* (multiplicative vs. additive) is what determines whether weight space is learnable, rather than the generative model architecture alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that weight-space geometry (measured via linear mode connectivity and initialization sensitivity) strongly correlates with downstream generative quality, providing a diagnostic for why some weight parameterizations are amenable to diffusion-based generation while others fail catastrophically. The first successful weight-space generation of natural images at 128×128 (FFHQ) is a concrete milestone, though the resolution remains modest relative to current image generation. Open questions include whether mLoRA's advantage generalizes beyond neural fields to other architectures, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or more diverse datasets, and the puzzling disconnect where mLoRA-Asym achieves better linear mode connectivity yet worse discriminative accuracy than plain mLoRA, suggesting connectivity and semantic separability are not the same property.
