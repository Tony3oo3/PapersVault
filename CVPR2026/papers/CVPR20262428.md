## Problem

Existing dichotomous image segmentation (DIS) methods either rely on classification backbones that lack fine-grained semantic representations for intricate foregrounds, or adopt stochastic diffusion-based denoising that is fundamentally misaligned with the deterministic nature of pixel-level segmentation, leading to slow convergence and blurred boundaries. Additionally, prior language-guided DIS approaches struggle with reliable controllability in multi-object scenes due to the scarcity of multi-foreground training examples.

## Input / Output

FlowDIS takes a 1024×1024 RGB image and an optional free-form text prompt (e.g., "bicycle" or "bicycle, tree, and white ladder") as input. The image is encoded into a VAE latent space, and the text prompt is encoded via CLIP and T5 text encoders into token embeddings. The output is a binary segmentation mask produced by decoding the final flow-matching latent back through the VAE decoder, converting the resulting RGB to grayscale, and clipping to [0, 1].

## Method

FlowDIS reformulates DIS as a deterministic flow matching problem: instead of denoising a mask from Gaussian noise, it learns a time-dependent velocity field v_θ that directly transports the image latent z^I (at t=1) to the mask latent z^M (at t=0) via linear interpolation trajectories. The velocity network is a FLUX.1-Schnell MMDiT initialized with pre-trained weights, extended with an additional image-latent conditioning channel, and trained with an MSE loss on predicted vs. ground-truth velocities using a Beta(2.5,1) timestep distribution. Inference solves the probability flow ODE with Euler integration over a Beta-CDF-derived non-uniform schedule, requiring only 1–2 steps for strong results. To improve language controllability, the Position-Aware Instance Pairing (PAIP) strategy constructs mixed training triplets by compositing foregrounds from two batch samples into a single image, then randomly selecting the target mask (one object, the other, or both) and corresponding prompt, exposing the model to diverse multi-object compositions during training.

## Results

On the full DIS-TE (1–4) test set (2,000 images), FlowDIS with 2-step inference achieves F_β^ω = 0.938, MAE = 0.016, and S_α = 0.951, outperforming the best prior method LawDIS (F_β^ω = 0.884, MAE = 0.030, S_α = 0.916) by approximately 5.5 percentage points in F_β^ω and 43% in MAE. Even with 1-step inference, FlowDIS reaches F_β^ω = 0.933 and MAE = 0.017, already surpassing all baselines. On DIS-VD (470 images), 2-step FlowDIS scores F_β^ω = 0.938 and MAE = 0.014, again leading all competitors by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowDIS outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric across all DIS5K test subsets. The closest competitor is LawDIS [34], the only other language-guided method; FlowDIS exceeds it by 5.4 percentage points in F_β^ω (0.938 vs. 0.884) and halves its MAE (0.016 vs. 0.030) on DIS-TE (1–4). Among non-language-guided methods, PDFNet [17] and MVANet [36] are the strongest, reaching F_β^ω ≈ 0.874 and 0.862 respectively, still trailing FlowDIS by 6–8 points. Diffusion-based baselines (DiffDIS [37], GenPercept [33]) lag further behind, with DiffDIS at F_β^ω = 0.848 and GenPercept at 0.816 on DIS-TE. Classical DIS architectures (IS-Net, InSPyReNet, FP-DIS, UDUN, BiRefNet) show the largest gaps, with IS-Net [26] the weakest at F_β^ω = 0.726. Notably, FlowDIS achieves its best results with only 1–2 ODE integration steps, whereas LawDIS required 36K training iterations versus FlowDIS's 10K, and FlowDIS surpasses LawDIS after just 1K iterations (Fig. 4).

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowDIS departs from the dominant diffusion-based DIS paradigm (DiffDIS, LawDIS, GenPercept), which frames segmentation as image-conditioned mask denoising from Gaussian noise, by instead formulating it as a deterministic flow-matching problem that transports the image latent directly to the mask latent. The method builds on the flow matching framework of Lipman et al. (ICLR 2023) and initializes from the pre-trained FLUX.1-Schnell MMDiT weights, repurposing a text-to-image generative backbone for a dense prediction task. The Position-Aware Instance Pairing (PAIP) strategy extends the language-guided segmentation idea introduced by LawDIS with a batch-level data augmentation that explicitly constructs multi-object training examples, addressing a gap in standard DIS datasets that lack diverse multi-foreground compositions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The large performance gap over prior methods (5.5% F-beta, 43% MAE reduction) and the dramatically faster convergence (1K vs. 36K iterations) suggest that the stochastic denoising formulation was a fundamental mismatch for deterministic dense prediction, a lesson that may transfer to other segmentation and perception tasks. However, the evaluation is confined to the DIS5K benchmark at 1024×1024 resolution with a single base architecture (FLUX.1-Schnell), leaving open whether the gains persist on larger, more diverse datasets or with different flow-matching backbones. The practical cost of multi-step ODE integration at inference, even if only 1–2 steps are needed, also warrants comparison against single-pass classification-based methods in latency-sensitive deployment settings.
