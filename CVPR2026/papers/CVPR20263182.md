## Problem

Long streaming video QA is bottlenecked by the linear growth of visual tokens that quickly exceeds LLM context windows. Existing KV-cache approaches (e.g., ReKV) store per-frame caches indiscriminately, leading to redundant memory usage and loss of both fine-grained spatial details within a frame and temporal context across frames, which degrades retrieval quality and answer accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming video sampled at 0.5 FPS, where 4 consecutive frames (8 seconds) form a segment and each frame is divided into 196 patches grouped into 4 super-patches for multi-grained representation. Text questions arrive at arbitrary timestamps and trigger online retrieval from the offline KV cache. The LLM (LLaVA-OV 0.5B or 7B) generates a text answer autoregressively using only the retrieved KV slices as context, requiring no re-encoding of past video. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic.

## Method

MuKV extracts video KV caches at three granularities per segment—patch (super-patches of the middle frame), frame (middle frame), and segment (all 4 frames)—by grouping visual tokens at different scales without repeated visual-encoder inference. A Dual-Signal KV-Cache Compression (DCP) module prunes redundant tokens by fusing last-layer self-attention scores with FFT-based frequency magnitudes, applying granularity-specific compression ratios (e.g., 10% retention for segments, 80% for patches) to balance global narrative context against local spatial detail. For online QA, a semi-hierarchical retrieval first performs grain-agnostic parallel cosine-similarity search across all three granularity levels, then reranks lower-granularity blocks using a consistency score against a global query derived from top segment-level blocks, reducing cross-granularity noise. The final top-ranked KV slices are loaded into the LLM alongside the question for answer decoding, achieving sub-linear memory growth and reduced inference tokens compared to per-frame caching.

## Results

On RVSEgo, MuKV (7B) achieves 59.5% accuracy, outperforming ReKV (7B) at 56.2% while using fewer inference tokens (8.3K vs. 12.5K) and the same memory budget (59K per 10 min). On StreamingBench (Real-Time), MuKV (7B) reaches 64.4% overall accuracy versus 62.3% for ReKV (7B) and 60.0% for LongVA (7B). The 0.5B variant also shows consistent gains: 57.9% on RVSEgo and 56.8% on StreamingBench All, both surpassing the corresponding ReKV (0.5B) baselines by 6.4 and 4.1 points respectively. Memory usage remains unchanged at 59K tokens per 10 minutes across all MuKV configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ReKV [9] (the primary KV-cache baseline), MuKV wins on all three datasets at both model scales: +3.3% on RVSEgo and +2.1% on StreamingBench All for the 7B model, and +6.4% and +4.1% for the 0.5B model, while simultaneously reducing inference tokens from 12.5K to 8.3K with identical memory. Against FVStream [54], MuKV wins decisively on StreamingBench (64.4% vs. 24.3%) and RVSEgo (59.5% vs. 58.5%), but loses on RVS-Movie (48.5% vs. 54.7%), which the authors attribute to FVStream's task-specific training overfitting. Against LongVA [55], MuKV achieves higher StreamingBench All accuracy (64.4% vs. 60.0%) with fewer inference tokens (8.3K vs. 18.4K) and a bounded memory footprint (59K vs. 0 reported, as LongVA processes all frames in-context). In the controlled compression comparison (Table 5), MuKV's DCP module outperforms InfiniPot-V [19] on RVSEgo (59.5% vs. 57.9%) while using fewer inference tokens (8.3K vs. 12.5K) and comparable memory (5.9K vs. 6K).

## Relation to Prior Work

MuKV extends the KV-cache paradigm for streaming video QA established by ReKV and InfiniPot-V, which store per-frame Key-Value caches and retrieve them at query time. It departs from this lineage in two key ways: (1) replacing single-granularity per-frame caching with a multi-grained (patch, frame, segment) representation that preserves both spatial detail and temporal context, and (2) introducing a dual-signal compression mechanism that combines self-attention importance with FFT-based frequency scores, going beyond the attention-only or value-norm pruning used by prior KV-cache methods. The semi-hierarchical retrieval strategy also distinguishes MuKV from both the parallel retrieval in ReKV and the fully hierarchical approaches in VideoTree, which suffer from error propagation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the DCP compression module alone (without multi-grained representations or semi-hierarchical retrieval) consistently improves accuracy, reduces memory, and speeds up inference over ReKV suggests that informed token pruning via frequency signals is a broadly applicable technique for any KV-cache-based streaming system. However, the method still struggles with counting tasks sensitive to fine-grained changes, and the three parallel LLM prefills per segment impose a non-trivial offline cost that may limit deployment on resource-constrained devices. Open questions include whether the multi-grained design generalizes to audio-visual streaming, whether the frequency signal remains effective at much higher frame rates or longer streams, and whether the compression ratios found optimal on 30-minute to 1-hour videos transfer to truly unbounded streams.
