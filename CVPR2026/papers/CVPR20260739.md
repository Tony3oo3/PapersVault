## Problem

Masked Auto-Regressive (MAR) diffusion models suffer from a nested inference architecture—an outer AR unmasking loop wrapping an inner multi-step diffusion chain—that makes generation extremely slow (20–31 s per image) and renders RL-based preference alignment computationally infeasible. The paper asks whether MAR's diffusion chain can be compressed into a single generation step without sacrificing quality, thereby unlocking practical RL post-training for masked auto-regressive models.

## Input / Output

For class-conditional generation, the model takes an ImageNet class embedding (256×256 output) plus Gaussian noise z ~ N(0,I); for text-to-image, it takes a natural-language prompt (512×512 output via DC-AR). Generation follows MAR's random masked auto-regressive ordering, where token indices are partitioned into K random subsets and predicted group-by-group. After distillation, each AR iteration requires only one forward pass of the student generator instead of ~100 diffusion steps, reducing total inference to 0.61–1.67 s depending on model scale.

## Method

The core contribution is Guided Score Implicit Matching (GSIM), a score-based variational distillation that minimizes KL divergence between the student's one-step distribution q_θ(x|c) and the teacher's multi-step CFG-guided distribution p(x|c) by expressing the KL as a time-integrated Fisher (score) divergence. A Gradient Equivalent Theorem makes the intractable objective optimizable: an auxiliary network S_φ approximates the student score, and the generator g_θ is updated via a Pseudo-Huber distance between the auxiliary score and the CFG-guided teacher score, with stop-gradient on the auxiliary term. A second stage, MARVAL-RL, performs post-hoc reinforcement learning where the distilled generator executes full multi-step AR inference (matching actual deployment) and is fine-tuned by backpropagating PickScore reward gradients, ensuring the reward signal reflects true multi-iteration perceptual quality rather than low-fidelity single-step intermediates.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, MARVAL-H achieves the best FID of 2.00 with an inference time of only 1.67 s per image, outperforming MAR-H (FID 2.06, 30.98 s) by both quality and ~18.5× speed. MARVAL-B reaches FID 3.06 in 0.61 s, a 32.95× speedup over MAR-B (FID 2.60, 20.10 s). After RL fine-tuning, MARVAL-RL improves CLIP score from 29.40 to 29.47 (Base) and ImageReward from −0.142 to −0.110 (Base), with larger gains at the Large and Huge scales. On the text-to-image DC-AR task, the 1-step+RL strategy boosts ImageReward from 0.5876 (20-step DDIM) to 0.6903 while compressing inference to a single step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the MAR family (same architecture), MARVAL-H slightly improves FID over MAR-H (2.00 vs 2.06) while being ~18.5× faster (1.67 s vs 30.98 s); MARVAL-B trades a small FID increase (3.06 vs 2.60) for a 32.95× speedup. Compared to the strongest continuous-token diffusion baselines, MARVAL-H achieves a lower FID than both DiT-XL/2 (2.00 vs 2.27) and U-ViT-H/2 (2.00 vs 2.29) while also being faster (1.67 s vs 6.62 s and 3.01 s respectively), though DiT-XL/2 retains a higher IS (278.2 vs 256.3). Against pixel-based ADM and latent LDM-4, MARVAL-H dominates on both FID and speed by wide margins (2.00 vs 4.59 and 3.60; 1.67 s vs 115.67 s and 37.25 s). On the text-to-image DC-AR task, the distilled+RL model surpasses the original 20-step DDIM baseline on ImageReward (0.6903 vs 0.5876) while reducing inference from 20 steps to 1 step.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Score Implicit Matching (SIM) [30] to the Masked Auto-regressive (MAR) setting by introducing Guided Score Implicit Matching (GSIM), which incorporates classifier-free guidance into the teacher score during distillation. The MAR architecture [25] itself combines autoregressive token ordering with a lightweight diffusion head, and prior one-step distillation work (SIM, Uni-instruct [60]) had not addressed this nested AR+diffusion structure. The paper further departs from prior preference-alignment work on diffusion models [28,31-33] by showing that RL post-training must be decoupled from the distillation stage for MAR, since single-step training samples are too low-fidelity to provide meaningful reward signals for the multi-step inference process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By compressing MAR's hierarchical inference into a single AR step per iteration, MARVAL makes real-time generation (≈0.6 s/image) and RL-based preference alignment practical for the first time in the MAR family, opening a path to scalable post-training for AR+Diffusion hybrids. However, the RL gains are modest (e.g., CLIP improves by only ~0.07 for Base), and evaluation is limited to ImageNet class-conditional generation and one T2I model (DC-AR), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to other AR+Diffusion architectures or higher-resolution settings. A key open question is whether the decoupled RL stage can be made more sample-efficient or whether the reward signal can be better aligned with the multi-iteration generation dynamics.
