## Problem

Classical surface PDE solvers (FEM, CPM) require explicit triangle meshes, while modern 3D assets increasingly exist as neural representations (implicit fields, point clouds, splats, neural surfaces). This mismatch forces mesh extraction or per-instance residual training (e.g., surface PINNs), breaking end-to-end differentiability and preventing a unified pipeline that solves PDEs directly where the neural data lives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263046/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts any surface representation that supports point sampling and local differential cue estimation (normals, principal curvature directions), including SDF/occupancy INRs, point clouds, meshes, Gaussian splats, and Spherical Neural Surfaces, along with a PDE initial condition and boundary conditions. The solution is computed on a narrow Cartesian band around the surface and read out by restricting the band field to the surface via Gaussian-kernel RBF interpolation, yielding a smooth surface field. The operator is representation-agnostic: geometric cues are extracted per-modality (e.g., Hessian eigenvectors for SDFs, PCA normals for point clouds, Weingarten map for SNS) but fed into the same learned update.

## Method

The core idea is to learn the closest-point extension step of the Closest Point Method as a lightweight, geometry-conditioned neural operator, eliminating the need for explicit extension or meshing. The operator is a compact MLP with an attention-like mechanism that takes a query point, local band samples with current field values, and surface features (positions, normals) expressed in a local frame defined by the normal and principal curvature directions, and outputs an updated band value enforcing normal constancy. The surface is decomposed into overlapping, surface-centered patches; local predictions are aggregated via proximity-weighted averaging, and the resulting band field is advanced with standard finite-difference time stepping. Training is supervised on a single representative shape (SPIKE) using monomial functions up to degree 5 as ground-truth extension targets, with a loss combining MSE reconstruction and a normal-consistency term computed via automatic differentiation. Once trained, the operator generalizes across unseen shapes, topologies, and input modalities without per-instance optimization, and remains fully differentiable for use as a drop-in PDE layer in learning pipelines.

## Results

On the unit sphere (Poisson, analytic ground truth), the method achieves NMAE of 1.33×10⁻² and NMaxE of 3.17×10⁻² at fine resolution, matching CPM (1.46×10⁻² / 3.49×10⁻²) while SFEM reaches 2.84×10⁻⁴ / 6.30×10⁻⁴ as the mesh-based reference. On five unseen ShapeNet shapes (chairs, tables), the spike-only model achieves NRMSE of 0.354–1.05 (×10⁻²), consistently outperforming CPM (0.600–1.92) and GINO trained on the same spike shape (6.67–13.89) by a large margin. The method also generalizes to unseen high-frequency heat-diffusion initial conditions despite being trained only on polynomial monomials.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CPM (the classical closest-point embedding solver), the proposed method achieves lower NRMSE on 3 of 5 ShapeNet shapes (e.g., 0.354 vs. 0.889 on shape D, 0.364 vs. 0.600 on shape E) and is comparable on the remaining two, while on the sphere both methods plateau at similar error levels (~1.3–1.5×10⁻² NMAE at fine resolution). Against GINO trained on the same single spike shape, the proposed method is an order of magnitude more accurate (NRMSE ~0.35–1.05 vs. 6.67–13.89), demonstrating far stronger generalization to unseen geometries without per-shape retraining. GINO with per-instance overfitting achieves the lowest errors (0.16–0.34) but requires ~8 hours of retraining per new PDE setting, whereas the proposed method is training-free at inference. SFEM (surface finite elements on a dense mesh) remains the accuracy gold standard (NMAE ~2.8×10⁻⁴ on the sphere) but requires explicit meshing, which the proposed method avoids entirely. The key takeaway is that the learned extension operator matches CPM-level accuracy while operating natively on neural representations (SDFs, point clouds, splats, SNS) without mesh extraction or per-instance optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Closest Point Method (CPM) by replacing its explicit closest-point re-extension step with a lightweight, geometry-conditioned neural operator trained on a single representative shape. It departs from mesh-based FEM (which cannot operate on neural representations without extraction) and from per-instance PINN approaches (which require optimization per shape) by learning a local, amortized update that generalizes across shapes, topologies, and modalities. The design is also distinct from global neural operators like GINO or FNO, which amortize full solution maps but assume fixed discretizations and require retraining per PDE; here, only the narrow-band extension is learned, while the volumetric PDE solve remains a standard finite-difference step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a drop-in, differentiable PDE layer that operates natively on neural shape representations (SDFs, point clouds, Gaussian splats, spherical neural surfaces) without mesh extraction or per-instance optimization, enabling end-to-end pipelines for inverse problems and PDE-constrained optimization on modern 3D assets. Key limitations include unreliable SDF gradients near self-intersections or the medial axis, the need to rebuild the narrow band for evolving surfaces, and residual grid-spacing sensitivity. Open directions include coupling the learned extension with higher-order or implicit solvers, multi-resolution training for scale invariance, and extending the operator to jointly predict band maintenance for moving surfaces.
