## Problem

Lightweight segmentation models that aggressively reduce parameters and FLOPs often fail to accurately delineate fine lesion boundaries and texture patterns critical for early skin cancer diagnosis. Existing Mamba-based segmentation architectures, while offering linear-time long-range modeling, still rely on static feature fusion and conventional skip connections that limit multi-scale representation learning and weaken boundary refinement in challenging lesion regions.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 256×256 RGB dermoscopic skin lesion images (normalized and augmented) and produces a pixel-wise binary lesion probability map via a 1×1 convolution followed by sigmoid activation. All experiments use a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 Ti GPU with 24 GB VRAM, batch size 8, and the model operates at 0.494M parameters and 0.326 GFLOPs for the 256×256 input resolution.

## Method

MambaLiteUNet integrates Vision Mamba state-space layers (SS2D with four-directional scanning) into a five-stage U-Net backbone with channel capacities {16,32,48,64,96,128}, augmented by three novel modules. Adaptive Multi-Branch Mamba Feature Fusion (AMF) splits input channels into four parallel Mamba branches with a learnable residual scalar, then applies dual-stage gating (spatial and transform) via depthwise and pointwise convolutions to adaptively weight informative channels. Local-Global Feature Mixing (LGFM) jointly extracts local texture via 3×3 depthwise convolution and global context via 8-head multi-head self-attention, fusing them through channel concatenation and projection. Cross-Gated Attention (CGA) refines skip connections by processing encoder-decoder feature pairs through Mamba blocks, computing pairwise cross-gating with sigmoid-modulated elementwise products, and generating an attention mask that suppresses background noise before decoder fusion. The model is trained for 300 epochs with AdamW (lr 0.001, cosine annealing to 0.00001) using a combined BCE + Dice loss.

## Results

MambaLiteUNet achieves an average IoU of 87.12% and average Dice of 93.09% across ISIC2017, ISIC2018, HAM10000, and PH2, with per-dataset IoU/Dice of 85.55%/92.21% (ISIC2017), 83.60%/91.07% (ISIC2018), 90.77%/95.16% (HAM10000), and 88.54%/93.92% (PH2). The model uses only 0.494M parameters and 0.326 GFLOPs at 256×256 input, reducing parameters by 93.6% and GFLOPs by 97.6% relative to U-Net while improving average IoU by 7.72 points and Dice by 4.61 points. In domain generalization on six unseen HAM10000 lesion categories, it reaches 77.61% IoU and 87.23% Dice, the best among all evaluated models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaLiteUNet outperforms all 15 baselines on average IoU and Dice across the four main datasets, with the closest competitors being LB-UNet (85.02% IoU, 91.87% DSC) and ULVM-UNet (84.89% IoU, 91.80% DSC), which it surpasses by 2.10 and 2.23 IoU points respectively. On ISIC2017, it leads ULVM-UNet by 2.50 IoU and 1.47 DSC; on ISIC2018, by 2.96 IoU and 1.78 DSC; on HAM10000, it beats LB-UNet by 1.44 IoU and ULVM-UNet by 1.99 IoU; and on PH2, it exceeds LB-UNet by 1.42 IoU and 0.80 DSC. The only metrics where it does not rank first are specificity on HAM10000 (98.33% vs. ULVM-UNet's 98.43%) and ISIC2018 (97.95% vs. UNeXt-S's 98.18%), both by less than 0.2 points. In the domain generalization setting (trained on NV only, tested on six unseen HAM10000 categories), it achieves 77.61% IoU and 87.23% DSC, outperforming the next-best LB-UNet (76.44% IoU, 86.45% DSC) and LightM-UNet (76.00% IoU, 86.82% DSC). Notably, it achieves 93.90% DSC on melanoma (MEL) and 91.13% on benign keratosis-like lesions (BKL), both clinically challenging categories with highly irregular boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

MambaLiteUNet sits at the intersection of three research threads: the U-Net encoder-decoder paradigm for dense prediction, the Vision Mamba / SS2D state-space modeling approach for linear-complexity long-range dependency capture, and the lightweight segmentation literature (UNeXt-S, MALUNet, EGE-UNet, LB-UNet) that prioritizes parameter and FLOP budgets. It departs from prior Mamba-based segmentation models (VM-UNet, VM-UNet2, LightM-UNet, ULVM-UNet) by replacing their static feature fusion and standard skip connections with three purpose-built modules—AMF for adaptive multi-branch channel partitioning with dual gating, LGFM for joint local-convolutional and global-attentional mixing, and CGA for cross-gated encoder-decoder skip refinement. The Mamba block itself is inherited directly from VMamba's SS2D formulation, so the architectural novelty is in how state-space features are routed, gated, and combined rather than in the state-space operator itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive or superior segmentation accuracy (87.12% avg IoU, 93.09% avg Dice) at a fraction of the cost of both CNN and Transformer baselines (0.494M params, 0.326 GFLOPs), making it a realistic candidate for mobile and point-of-care dermatological screening where compute is tightly constrained. The domain-generalization protocol (train on NV only, test on six unseen lesion types) adds clinical relevance by mimicking the realistic scenario where annotations for rare or malignant lesions are scarce. Open questions include whether the three-module design transfers to 3D volumetric segmentation (the authors note this as future work), whether the four-branch optimum holds at larger input resolutions or on more diverse lesion morphologies, and how the model behaves under extreme class imbalance or very small training sets beyond the limited-data ablation reported in the supplement.
