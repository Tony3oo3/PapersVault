## Problem

Standard LoRA fine-tuning of medical VLMs is highly sensitive to the training data regime: mixing heterogeneous medical sources introduces cross-dataset interference, and sequential task arrival causes catastrophic forgetting (>50% accuracy loss). No existing parameter-efficient method provides a principled mechanism to both isolate task-specific spectral components and align low-rank MoE optimization with full-rank fine-tuning dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262123/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (built on Qwen2.5-VL 7B) consumes a medical image (X-ray, ultrasound, MRI, CT, or histopathology) together with a free-form text question or instruction prompt, and produces task-appropriate outputs: open- or closed-ended text answers for VQA, structured radiology reports for generation, or discrete class labels for zero-shot classification. The same frozen backbone and LoRA-MoE adapter handle all three task types without task-specific heads, with the router dynamically selecting which spectral experts to activate per input.

## Method

MedQwen partitions the SVD of each pretrained weight matrix into N non-overlapping spectral segments, assigning one segment per LoRA expert so that each expert encodes a distinct band of singular values. A Mixtral-style top-k router then selects the k most relevant experts per input, enabling adaptive low-rank approximation without discarding pre-training structure. To stabilize training, the paper introduces a residual compensation term W_res (derived in closed form via router moment identities) that ensures the equivalent weight equals the pretrained weight at initialization, and a theoretical scaling factor s* = √(3nη/r) that aligns each expert's equivalent gradient with the corresponding full-rank MoE fine-tuning gradient. Per-expert factors are additionally damped by a coefficient ρ to reduce initialization mismatch variance, pushing the system toward the zero-initialization regime where the gradient alignment theorem holds. The entire approach requires no architectural changes to the base VLM and only trains the LoRA experts and router.

## Results

On medical VQA (Table 1), MedQwen achieves a 68.2% average across VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA, and OMVQA, surpassing the Qwen-2.5-VL base by 18.9% and the strongest medical VLM baseline HealthGPT-L14 (14B) by 5.1%. On zero-shot radiology classification (Table 4), MedQwen reaches 58.83% average accuracy—95.3% of full fine-tuning MoE (61.72%)—while using 339× fewer trainable parameters (2.24% vs. 760%). In sequential learning, MedQwen retains ~95% of its original accuracy after domain shift, whereas standard LoRA degrades by >50% and MoELoRA by >20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedQwen (7B) outperforms all medical VLM baselines on the primary VQA benchmark: it beats LLaVA-Med (45.0%) by 23.2%, Med-Flamingo (38.6%) by 29.6%, HuatuoGPT-Vision (48.1%) by 20.1%, and even the larger 14B HealthGPT-L14 (63.1%) by 5.1%. Among PEFT methods on zero-shot radiology classification, MedQwen (58.83%) surpasses LoRA rank-32 (55.45%) by 3.38 points, PiSSA (50.08%) by 8.75%, MiLoRA (49.82%) by 9.01%, MoELoRA (55.52%) by 3.31%, AdaMoLE/LLaVA-MoLE (54.61%) by 4.22%, and HydraLoRA (53.99%) by 4.84 points, while using fewer parameters than all but the base LoRA. In the catastrophic forgetting experiment, MedQwen's ~5% accuracy drop after sequential domain shift is dramatically lower than LoRA (>50% drop) and MoELoRA (>20% drop), confirming that the SVD-structured expert routing preserves prior knowledge. MedQwen also outperforms its base model Qwen-2.5-VL (49.3%) by 18.9% on average VQA accuracy, demonstrating that the spectral MoE adaptation adds substantial medical-domain capability without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedQwen unifies two previously separate lines of work: SVD-based LoRA initialization (PiSSA, MiLoRA, KaSA), which selects specific singular-value segments for adaptation, and LoRA-MoE architectures (MoELoRA, HydraLoRA, LLaVA-MoLE), which route inputs to specialized low-rank experts. The key departure is that rather than freezing all but one spectral segment (as PiSSA/MiLoRA do) or using randomly initialized experts (as MoELoRA does), MedQwen partitions the full SVD spectrum into non-overlapping segments assigned to distinct experts, letting a top-k router adaptively select the most relevant spectral prior per input. A residual compensation term and a theoretically derived scaling factor (s* = √(3nη/r)) further distinguish the method by formally aligning low-rank MoE gradient dynamics with those of a fully fine-tuned MoE, a gap not addressed in prior LoRA-MoE work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5% sequential forgetting (vs. >50% for standard LoRA and >20% for MoELoRA) on the Harvard-FairVLMed→PathVQA protocol is a practically significant result for clinical deployment, where tasks and data arrive incrementally. The 339× parameter reduction relative to full-FT MoE while approaching its accuracy makes the approach accessible on single-GPU budgets. Open questions include whether the spectral partitioning strategy generalizes to larger backbones or non-Transformer architectures, whether the router learns genuinely task-specific specialization or merely load-balances, and whether the theoretical scaling result holds under different optimizers or mixed-precision training. The ablation showing that the specific SVD segment assignment (principal vs. minor vs. random) matters little (67.3–67.7) suggests the MoE routing mechanism, not the spectral structure per se, drives most of the gain—a finding that could redirect future design effort.
