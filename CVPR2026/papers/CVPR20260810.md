## Problem

Point-level weakly-supervised temporal sentiment localization (P-WTSL) must identify fine-grained sentiment segments in untrimmed videos from only sparse point annotations, but existing methods struggle with three intertwined issues: redundant visual/audio content obscures salient sentiment cues, sparse annotations leave segment boundaries ambiguous, and abrupt sentiment transitions are hard to capture. Prior TSL approaches also largely ignore facial expressions as a direct source of sentiment stimuli and fail to model temporal cross-modal dependencies between face, audio, and visual streams.

## Input / Output

The model consumes untrimmed audible videos segmented into 16-frame windows, from which visual (I3D, 1024-d), audio (MFCC, 512-d), and facial (ResEmoteNet, 256-d) features are extracted and projected to a shared 512-d space; supervision comes only as a small set of (timestamp, one-hot sentiment category) point annotations per video. The output is a per-frame, per-category sentiment score matrix of shape T×(C+1) (C sentiment classes plus a non-sentiment class), from which segment boundaries are derived via the boundary-aware pseudo-label mechanism rather than a simple threshold.

## Method

FSENet introduces three coupled modules. First, the Face-guided Sentiment Discovery (FSD) module runs two parallel branches: a Face-Centric Interaction (FCI) branch that uses multi-head attention in two stages—facial features first attend to visual and audio features independently, then the face-conditioned audio and visual representations attend to each other—and a Global Sentiment Perception (GSP) branch that applies 1-D convolutions on concatenated modality features to produce a per-frame sentiment saliency weight in [0,1]. Second, the Point-aware Sentiment Semantics Contrast (PSSC) strategy builds class-specific prototypes from annotated point features, selects top-K most similar frames (weighted by GSP scores) as positives and other-category frames as negatives, and applies a softmax contrastive loss to tighten intra-class and widen inter-class sentiment semantics. Third, the Boundary-aware Sentiment Pseudo-label Generation (BSPG) module converts each sparse annotation into a temporally smooth pseudo-label by applying a linear decay function (controlled by window width w and floor β) around the annotation point, combined with a high-confidence non-sentiment threshold, yielding coherent segment boundaries. The full network is trained with a composite loss combining video-level alignment, frame-level focal losses on both FCI and GSP outputs against the BSPG pseudo-labels, and the PSSC contrastive term.

## Results

On TSL300 under point-level weak supervision (the primary setting), FSENet achieves an average mAP@IoU of 21.45% (29.31% at IoU 0.1, 22.49% at IoU 0.2, 13.24% at IoU 0.3) with 75.02% Recall, outperforming the previous SOTA TSL by 1.05% in average mAP and 3.88% in Recall. Under video-level weak supervision, FSENet reaches 12.53% average mAP, surpassing the best prior method (DDG-Net, 10.72%) by 16.9%. On CMU-MOSEI, FSENet generalizes to 16.54% average mAP, exceeding TSL (11.00%) and HR-pro stage2 (11.47%) by 5.54% and 5.07% respectively. FSENet also outperforms LLM baselines (Qwen3-Omni zero-shot at 5.07%, LLaMA-2-7B LoRA at 9.97%) by 16.30% and 11.48% in average mAP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under the primary point-level weak supervision setting on TSL300, FSENet wins on average mAP against all baselines: it beats TSL [55] (20.40%) by 1.05%, HR-pro stage2 [53] (19.60%) by 1.85%, SF-Net [25] (15.10%) by 6.35%, and LACP [16] (12.30%) by 9.15%. FSENet also achieves the highest Recall (75.02%) among all point-level methods, though TSL [55] retains a slight edge in F2-score (35.36 vs. 33.67). Under video-level weak supervision, FSENet (12.53% avg mAP) outperforms DDG-Net [39] (10.72%), UM [17] (10.75%), ISSF [48] (9.26%), TSL [55] (9.01%), and CoLA [51] (8.38%), with the largest margin over CoLA at 49.4%. Under full supervision, FSENet (21.87%) is slightly below AFSD [22] (22.14%) in average mAP but surpasses it in Recall (79.58% vs. 75.10%) and mAP@0.3 (14.33% vs. 11.76%), while clearly beating VAANet [58] (20.51%). Against LLM baselines, FSENet substantially outperforms Qwen3-Omni zero-shot (5.07%) by 16.30% and LLaMA-2-7B with LoRA (9.97%) by 11.48%, and even the LoRA+BSPG variant (11.75%) by 9.70%, confirming that the dedicated architecture with facial guidance and boundary-aware pseudo-labels is more effective than general-purpose LLMs for fine-grained temporal sentiment localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point-level weakly-supervised temporal sentiment localization (P-WTSL) task introduced by TSL [55], which first formulated the problem of localizing sentiment segments from sparse point annotations in untrimmed videos. The key departure is the introduction of facial features as an explicit semantic guidance signal for cross-modal interaction, a component that prior TSL methods (TSL [55], DCIN [18]) and temporal action localization baselines (HR-pro [53], LACP [16]) had overlooked. The boundary-aware pseudo-label generation (BSPG) and point-aware contrastive learning (PSSC) modules repurpose ideas from temporal action localization (threshold-based pseudo-labels, contrastive triplet losses) but adapt them specifically to the subtler, more transient nature of sentiment cues compared to action events.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified framework that achieves competitive results across full, video-level, and point-level supervision settings offers a practical path for deploying sentiment localization without expensive frame-level annotation. However, the gains over the direct predecessor TSL [55] are modest (~1% average mAP), and the facial feature pipeline (DeepFace detection + ResEmoteNet) adds deployment complexity that may limit real-world adoption. Open questions include whether the facial-guided approach generalizes across diverse populations and cultures, how it performs on longer videos beyond the ~250-second average in TSL300, and whether the modest facial feature contribution (0.59% over ΔTSL) justifies the additional inference cost in production settings.
