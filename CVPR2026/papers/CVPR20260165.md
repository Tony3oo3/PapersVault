## Problem

Supervised VQA models rely on expensive human-annotated datasets whose limited diversity causes severe performance degradation on out-of-distribution video content. This paper investigates whether weak-to-strong (W2S) learning—where a high-capacity student learns from weaker teacher models without human labels—can break the annotation barrier and achieve scalable generalization in video quality assessment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260165/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The student model consumes video clips by sampling one key frame per second for a dual-branch visual encoder and extracting SlowFast motion features across all frames within each second. It supports two input modes: single-video (direct quality score prediction) and dual-video (pairwise relative quality comparison). At inference, quality scores are derived via adaptive soft comparison against anchor videos followed by MAP estimation under Thurstone's Case V model, producing a calibrated perceptual quality score.

## Method

The framework trains a 7B LMM student (LLaVA-OneVision-Chat-7B with a motion projector) using pseudo-labels from five off-the-shelf VQA teachers and synthetic distortion simulators, unified through a learn-to-rank formulation with five-level pairwise labels (superior/better/similar/worse/inferior). A confidence loss is added to the cross-entropy objective so the student can override noisy weak labels when its own predictions are reliable. The key differentiator is an iterative W2S strategy: after each training stage, the student is recycled as the new teacher, and difficult samples are selected via a gMAD competition criterion that identifies video pairs where the student and teacher disagree most, progressively pushing the model toward challenging cases beyond any single teacher's reach.

## Results

The final iterative W2S model (Stage VI) achieves an overall in-domain SRCC of 0.865 and PLCC of 0.872, and an overall OOD SRCC of 0.745 and PLCC of 0.789, surpassing all five teacher models and two LMM-based competitors (VQA², VQAThinker) across all ten benchmarks. The OOD gains are particularly dramatic: relative SRCC improvements of 30.59%, 20.55%, and 8.27% over the best teacher are obtained on LIVE-YT-HFR, Waterloo-IVC-4K, and KVQ respectively. Notably, the method achieves these results without any human-labeled training data, whereas VQA² is trained on over 157k labeled samples and VQAThinker uses reinforcement learning with advanced LMM backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all five teacher VQA models (MinimalisticVQA VII/IX, FAST-VQA, DOVER, Q-Align) on every benchmark, with the largest margins on OOD datasets where teachers drop to SRCC 0.49–0.57 while the final model reaches 0.745 overall. Against the two strongest LMM-based competitors, VQA² (trained on 157k+ labeled samples) and VQAThinker (RL-trained), the method wins on both in-domain (0.865 vs. 0.855 SRCC) and OOD (0.745 vs. 0.615 SRCC) overall scores, with the OOD gap being especially large on challenging benchmarks like LIVE-YT-HFR (0.683 vs. 0.528 for VQAThinker) and Waterloo-IVC-4K (0.698 vs. 0.573). The progressive ablation shows that each added component—homogeneous ensembling, heterogeneous distortion simulators, confidence loss, and iterative training—contributes monotonically, with the iterative stages (V→VI) delivering the largest single jump in OOD SRCC (+0.023). Crucially, all gains are achieved without any human-annotated training labels, distinguishing this approach from both the supervised teachers and the LMM-based baselines that rely on large labeled datasets or RL reward signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the weak-to-strong (W2S) generalization paradigm from Burns et al. [1]—originally demonstrated in NLP and reward modeling—into video quality assessment, a task grounded in subjective human perception rather than deterministic semantics. Architecturally, the student model follows LMM-VQA [9] (dual-branch visual encoder with SlowFast motion features on an LLaVA-OneVision-7B backbone), while the ranking-based training and adaptive soft-comparison inference are adapted from Zhu et al. [58]. The key departure from prior weakly-supervised VQA work (which typically uses full-reference models as pseudo-labels and yields students inferior to their teachers) is the demonstration that a strong LMM student can *surpass* its weak teachers, particularly on OOD data, and the introduction of heterogeneous synthetic-distortion simulators as additional teachers alongside off-the-shelf VQA models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete, annotation-free pathway to scalable VQA that directly addresses the field's central bottleneck: the prohibitive cost of ITU-standard subjective experiments. The iterative self-teaching loop—where each student becomes the next teacher with difficulty-guided sampling—suggests a route toward VQA foundation models that generalize across content domains and distortion types without ever requiring new human labels. Open questions include whether the paradigm extends to other perceptual tasks (audio, haptics), whether the 7B LMM overhead is justified relative to lighter architectures, and how the approach behaves on truly novel distortion types (e.g., generative-AI artifacts) that fall outside both the teacher ensemble's and the synthetic simulators' coverage.
