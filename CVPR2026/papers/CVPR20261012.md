## Problem

Cross-subject EEG-to-image retrieval pipelines reduce test-time inference to bare nearest-neighbor search over frozen encoder similarities, leaving the joint effects of subject shift and hubness on top-k rankings uncharacterized and uncorrected. Existing hubness mitigations like CSLS rely on a globally fixed neighborhood size tuned on labeled data, which fails in strict zero-shot cross-subject settings where query- and class-specific local densities vary widely. No prior work provides a reusable, label-free calibration head that can be attached to arbitrary cross-subject EEG encoders to stabilize small-k shortlists.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled EEG embeddings from a held-out test subject (no labels, no weight updates) and frozen image class prototypes (200 THINGS-EEG categories) to produce a calibrated |Q|×|C| similarity matrix from which top-k class rankings are extracted. All computation operates on the precomputed similarity matrix S_new after subject-adaptive whitening and ℓ2 normalization; the encoders themselves are never modified. The structural expert is computed once from the pre-CSLS matrix and held fixed, so the full pipeline is a single-shot calibration rather than an iterative re-ranking loop.

## Method

SATTC is a label-free calibration head that fuses two experts via a Product-of-Experts rule in logit space. The geometric expert applies subject-adaptive whitening (SAW) to EEG embeddings per subject, then computes an adaptive CSLS score where each query and class uses a neighborhood size k_row(q) and k_col(c) derived from row-wise and column-wise local densities on the similarity matrix, replacing the fixed-k assumption of standard CSLS. The structural expert, built entirely from the pre-CSLS matrix, assigns a positive bias to mutual nearest-neighbor and bidirectional top-L pairs and a negative bias proportional to a normalized class-popularity hubness score for hub-like candidates. The two experts are combined as S_final = α·S_geom + β·S_struct with α fixed at 1 and β a single tunable scalar, yielding calibrated scores that reduce hubness, improve Top-1 identification, and produce more balanced per-class Recall@K without any access to test labels or encoder retraining.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG under a strict leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) protocol with 200-way retrieval, SATTC achieves 38.4% Top-5 and 14.8% Top-1 accuracy, compared to 20.0% Top-5 and 5.5% Top-1 for the original ATM baseline. The standardized inference pipeline (cosine similarity + ℓ₂ normalization + candidate whitening) alone already lifts Top-5 from 20.0% to 30.5%, and subject-adaptive whitening (SAW) further raises it to 36.4%. SATTC's structural PoE head adds a further +0.9 pp on Top-1 over adaptive CSLS (14.8% vs. 13.9%) while keeping Top-5 essentially unchanged (38.4% vs. 38.8%). Across four heterogeneous EEG encoders, SATTC as a plug-and-play calibration head yields 8–16 pp Top-5 and 4–8 pp Top-1 gains over each encoder's standardized baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the original ATM cross-subject retrieval pipeline (Song et al., ICLR 2024), which reports 20.0% Top-5 and 5.5% Top-1. SATTC outperforms ATM by 18.4 pp on Top-5 and 9.3 pp on Top-1, though a substantial portion of this gap is attributable to the standardized inference setup (cosine similarity, ℓ₂-normalized embeddings, candidate whitening) and subject-adaptive whitening rather than the calibration head itself. Against the strongest internal baselines, SATTC trades 0.4 pp of Top-5 accuracy (38.4% vs. 38.8%) for a 0.9 pp Top-1 gain (14.8% vs. 13.9%) over adaptive CSLS, and improves Top-1 by 0.7 pp over fixed-k CSLS (14.8% vs. 14.1%) while matching its Top-5 within 0.3 pp. The paper also demonstrates encoder-agnostic plug-and-play gains: when applied to EEGNetV4, EEGConformer, and ShallowFBCSPNet, SATTC consistently adds 8–16 pp Top-5 and 4–8 pp Top-1 over each encoder's own standardized baseline, confirming that the calibration operates on similarity-space geometry rather than encoder-specific features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CSLS (Lample et al., ICLR 2018) for hubness mitigation in cross-domain retrieval, extending it from a fixed-k neighborhood to query- and class-adaptive neighborhoods derived from local density estimates. It departs from the dominant EEG-to-image retrieval literature (ATM, contrastive-learning encoders) by shifting the focus from encoder design to a label-free test-time calibration layer that operates on the frozen similarity matrix. The structural expert draws on mutual nearest neighbors and bidirectional rank ideas from cross-modal re-ranking [2, 30], but repurposes them as a one-shot, label-free prior rather than an iterative graph-diffusion or pseudo-labeling scheme. The subject-adaptive whitening component connects to cross-subject EEG domain adaptation work [5, 16, 19, 29], but is applied at inference time on unlabeled test embeddings rather than during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play demonstration across four architecturally distinct EEG encoders (CSP-style, compact CNN, transformer) with consistent 8–16 point Top-5 gains suggests that similarity-space calibration is a broadly applicable layer for cross-subject neural decoding, independent of encoder choice. However, the method is validated only on THINGS-EEG under LOSO, the structural expert is hand-crafted from rank statistics, and the full SATTC gain over the SAW+Ada-CSLS baseline is modest (≈1 point Top-1, ≈0.4 point Top-5), leaving open whether the structural prior generalizes to noisier or higher-dimensional settings. Extending to retrieval-then-generation pipelines, other EEG benchmarks, and online single-trial calibration without a batch window are natural next steps.
