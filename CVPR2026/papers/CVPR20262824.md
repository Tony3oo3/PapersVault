## Problem

Existing trajectory forecasting methods for social navigation either rely on fixed-length transformer decoders with limited open-world generalization, or serialize coordinates as text tokens in LLMs, causing token inefficiency and poor spatio-temporal modeling. The paper addresses how to extend LLMs' native autoregressive generation to physical coordinate spaces while preserving their reasoning capabilities for complex human behavior understanding, without the overhead of multi-token coordinate serialization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8 frames of 160×120 RGB video at 1 Hz, 8 historical 2D waypoints, a goal location, and a structured text prompt specifying the desired trajectory length. It outputs a variable-length sequence of future 2D waypoints (typically 5–10 steps at 1 s intervals), where each waypoint is generated as a single <point> token whose hidden state is decoded by a Point Head into (x, y) coordinates. The autoregressive loop feeds each predicted waypoint back through the Point Encoder, so the trajectory length is determined at inference time by the prompt instruction rather than being architecturally fixed.

## Method

AutoTraces builds on LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B and introduces a trajectory tokenization scheme that maps each 2D waypoint to a dedicated <point> token, with its numerical value encoded via a Point Encoder (Transformer-style positional encoding + MLPs) into the LLM's embedding space and decoded back by a Point Head, preserving the autoregressive generation mechanism while extending it to physical coordinates. An automated Chain-of-Thought module uses Qwen-VL-Max to generate structured reasoning from synchronized video and trajectory data, incorporating sliding-window curvature analysis to decompose motion into discrete directional segments (straight/left/right) that guide the CoT. Training follows a two-stage QLoRA strategy: Stage 1 pre-trains on CoT rationales with cross-entropy loss over text tokens to instill visual reasoning, and Stage 2 fine-tunes for trajectory prediction with a combined objective of L1 waypoint regression loss plus cross-entropy for sequence structure, updating only LoRA layers, Text Head, Point Encoder, and Point Head while keeping the CLIP visual encoder frozen.

## Results

On the SCAND benchmark, AutoTraces achieves SOTA trajectory prediction across all horizons: L2 errors of 0.674 m (T=5), 0.923 m (T=8), and 1.089 m (T=10), with corresponding L1 errors of 0.856 m, 1.190 m, and 1.384 m. The advantage grows with horizon length, reaching a 0.318 m L2 improvement over the best baseline (CityWalker) at T=10. On unseen cross-scene datasets (GoStanford indoor, RECON outdoor), AutoTraces reduces L2 error by 30.0% (T=8) and 32.6% (T=10) relative to LLaVA-Video on RECON, and achieves 99.92% instruction execution accuracy with only 25 tokens per response versus 40.34% and 375.64 tokens for LLaVA-Video on extended 15-step horizons.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoTraces outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest non-LLM baseline CityWalker [28], AutoTraces reduces L2 error by 0.188 m at T=5 (0.674 vs. 0.862) and by 0.318 m at T=10 (1.089 vs. 1.407), with the gap widening as the prediction horizon increases. Compared to GNM [11] and ViNT [42], the improvements are consistent (0.18–0.62 m L2 reduction across horizons), while NoMad [43] is only evaluated up to T=8 and trails AutoTraces by 0.136 m (L2) at T=5. The most direct comparison is with LLaVA-Video [47], the text-only LLM baseline: AutoTraces cuts L2 error by 0.333 m at T=5, 0.625 m at T=8, and 0.874 m at T=10 on SCAND, and by 0.974 m at T=10 on the unseen RECON dataset. On extended 15-step horizons, AutoTraces achieves 99.92% instruction execution accuracy with 25 tokens per response, versus 40.34% and 375.64 tokens for LLaVA-Video, demonstrating both superior accuracy and a 15× reduction in inference token cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two prior LLM-for-trajectory lines: (1) methods that serialize coordinates as text tokens for QA-style generation [2, 24, 31, 44], which suffer from token inefficiency and weak spatio-temporal modeling, and (2) non-autoregressive LLM forecasters that use task-specialized encoders and static special tokens to produce full sequences in one pass [25, 32], which cannot model temporal dynamics or support flexible-length output. AutoTraces instead introduces a <point> token with a learned encoder-decoder that maps 2D waypoints into the LLM's latent space, preserving native autoregressive generation while extending it to physical coordinates. It also builds on the LLaVA-Video multimodal backbone [47] and adapts CoT prompting from high-level robotics planning [45, 46] to dense trajectory prediction, automating rationale generation via Qwen-VL-Max rather than manual annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The point-tokenization scheme offers a general recipe for injecting continuous spatial coordinates into autoregressive LLMs with a single token per waypoint, dramatically reducing inference cost (25 vs. 375 tokens per trajectory) and enabling flexible-length forecasting without retraining the full model. Cross-scene generalization results suggest that autoregressive, waypoint-by-waypoint generation captures temporal dependencies more robustly than single-pass decoders, a finding that could inform broader spatio-temporal forecasting architectures. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D waypoints or higher-frequency control, how it scales beyond 7B-parameter backbones, and whether the reliance on an external VLM (Qwen-VL-Max) for CoT generation can be internalized during training.
