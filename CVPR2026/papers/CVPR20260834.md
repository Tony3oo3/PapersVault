## Problem

Existing lightweight YOLO detectors excessively pursue speed at the cost of losing critical information during feature extraction and spatial transformation across network stages. Feature aggregation modules (C2f, C3k2) suffer from parameter redundancy and limited receptive fields in deep layers, downsampling modules (Conv, ADown) cause high-frequency information loss, and the standard CIoU loss exhibits training instability with overemphasis on easy samples. The paper addresses this by designing modules that are simultaneously efficient and information-preserving.

## Input / Output

Input images are scaled to 640×640 and normalized before feeding into the backbone. The detector outputs bounding boxes, confidence scores, and class labels for 80 COCO categories; redundant boxes are filtered via NMS and results are resized to original image dimensions. Five model variants (N/S/M/L/X) are derived by varying width scaling factors, with a separate Turbo line offering further reduced FLOPs and parameters for accelerated inference.

## Method

Built on YOLOv12, YOLO-ULM introduces three core components. D3C2f replaces standard bottleneck stacks with a cascaded block of 3×3 depthwise-separable convolution, 7×7 large-kernel depthwise convolution, and another 3×3 depthwise-separable convolution, using a sliced computation strategy to reduce peak memory; this cuts computational complexity by ~36% versus C2f at large channel counts. RepDown performs parallel downsampling via a 7×7 and a 3×3 depthwise convolution (both stride 2) that are reparameterized into a single equivalent 7×7 depthwise convolution at inference, reducing parameter growth from quadratic O(C²) to linear O(C). FoCIoU augments CIoU with an IoU^MF term (derived from Focaler-IoU) that applies nonlinear interval mapping to dynamically amplify gradients for hard samples while suppressing them for easy ones, achieving sample-adaptive perception without extra parameters. All models are trained from scratch on COCO with SGD, Mosaic/Mixup/copy-paste augmentation, and evaluated under TensorRT FP16.

## Results

On COCO val, YOLO-ULM achieves 41.6% mAP (N), 48.1% (S), 52.8% (M), 54.1% (L), and 55.6% (X) with 2.1M–51.0M parameters and 6.4G–213.1G FLOPs. The N variant reaches 41.6% mAP at 1.52 ms latency on a T4 GPU (TensorRT FP16), while the X variant attains 55.6% mAP at 11.47 ms. YOLO-ULM-Turbo further reduces latency (e.g., 1.48 ms for Turbo-N) and FLOPs (e.g., 5.8G for Turbo-N) with only a 0.9% mAP drop at the N scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the N scale, YOLO-ULM-N ties YOLOv13-N at 41.6% mAP while using fewer parameters (2.1M vs 2.5M) and achieving 22.8% lower latency (1.52 ms vs 1.97 ms); it outperforms YOLOv11-N by 2.2% and YOLOv12-N by 1.0% mAP. At the S scale, YOLO-ULM-S (48.1%) edges out YOLOv12-S and YOLOv13-S (both 48.0%) with 20% fewer parameters (7.4M vs 9.0–9.3M), and surpasses RT-DETR-R18 by 1.6% mAP with 64.7% fewer FLOPs and 63% fewer parameters. At the M scale, YOLO-ULM-M (52.8%) beats YOLOv12-M (52.5%) by 0.3% and YOLOv11-M (51.5%) by 1.3% while using 20% fewer parameters (16.0M vs 20.1–20.2M). At the L scale, YOLO-ULM-L (54.1%) outperforms YOLOv13-L (53.4%) by 0.7%, YOLOv12-L (53.7%) by 0.4%, and YOLOv11-L (53.3%) by 0.8%, with 17–18% fewer parameters. At the X scale, YOLO-ULM-X (55.6%) leads YOLOv12-X (55.2%) by 0.4%, YOLOv13-X (54.8%) by 0.8%, and YOLOv11-X (54.6%) by 1.0%, while using 13–20% fewer parameters than all three. YOLO-ULM-Turbo variants match or approach the base YOLO-ULM accuracy with 10–25% fewer FLOPs and lower latency across all scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

YOLO-ULM is built directly on YOLOv12's attention-centric architecture, replacing its feature aggregation and downsampling blocks with more efficient CNN-based alternatives. The D3C2f module draws on the large-kernel depthwise convolution lineage (ConvNeXt's 7×7 kernels, LSKNet's elastic kernels) and Xception's depthwise separable decomposition, while RepDown applies the reparameterization paradigm (à la RepVGG) to a parallel dual-path depthwise structure, extending ideas from ADown (YOLOv9) and SCDown (YOLOv10). The FoCIoU loss composes CIoU with Focaler-IoU's nonlinear interval mapping to address hard-easy sample imbalance. Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the 'two extremes' it identifies in the YOLO series—sacrificing speed for accuracy or vice versa—by arguing that effective lightweight design must preserve information fidelity during feature extraction and spatial transformation rather than merely compressing parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

YOLO-ULM provides a practical, well-ablated reference architecture for ultra-lightweight real-time detection, demonstrating that a carefully designed CNN backbone can match or exceed attention-based YOLOv12/v13 across all five scales (N through X) with 20–65% fewer parameters and FLOPs, all trained from scratch on COCO. The Turbo variant further shows that reparameterization and grouped convolution can shave additional latency with negligible accuracy cost, making it relevant for edge deployment. However, evaluation is limited to a single dataset (COCO), and the individual components (large-kernel DWConv, reparameterization, focal-style losses) are each known; the contribution lies in their specific combination and tuning. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other detection benchmarks (e.g., DOTA, nuScenes), whether the reparameterized inference path holds on diverse hardware beyond T4/RTX 4090, and whether the large-kernel depthwise design transfers to other tasks like segmentation or tracking.
