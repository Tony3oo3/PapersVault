## Problem

Multimodal agents for GUI interaction cannot reliably execute binary toggle control instructions, with execution accuracy below 50% for most agents including GPT-5. The core failure is a reasoning gap: agents cannot perceive the current toggle state from a screenshot, infer the desired state from the instruction, and correctly decide whether to act, leading to both false negatives (missing needed toggles) and false positives (unnecessary toggles). Prompt engineering and multi-agent annotator approaches fail to resolve this, pointing to a need for improved intrinsic reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a GUI screenshot (RGB image) paired with a natural-language toggle instruction (e.g., 'turn off Wi-Fi') and produces a three-step state-aware reasoning chain followed by a discrete action: either CLICK at normalized [0,1000] coordinates on the toggle widget, or COMPLETED (no action needed). The state control benchmark comprises 81,836 balanced samples (73,652 train / 8,184 test) where each annotated toggle quadruplet is expanded into a positive pair (state differs → CLICK) and a negative pair (state matches → COMPLETED), ensuring the model learns both action types.

## Method

StaR (State-aware Reasoning) refines the agent's reasoning chain into three explicit steps that simulate human toggle execution: (i) perceive the current toggle state σ from visual features in the screenshot, (ii) infer the desired state σ_u from the instruction text, and (iii) decide the action by comparing σ and σ_u (CLICK if σ≠σ_u, else COMPLETED). The key finding is that this structured reasoning must be internalized through supervised fine-tuning rather than prompted, as StaR-style prompting yields significantly worse results than training. Agents are fine-tuned on the state control benchmark alongside refined agentic benchmarks (AndroidControl, AITZ, GUI-Odyssey) where only toggle-related episodes receive StaR-style reasoning while other episodes retain their original reasoning, preserving general agentic capability. Training uses LLaMA-Factory with lr=5×10⁻⁶ for 3 epochs and FlashAttention, and the method is model-agnostic, applied successfully across four agents with different architectures and history-modeling strategies.

## Results

On the state control benchmark, StaR training improves Overall Action Match Rate (O-AMR) by 35.77% for OS-Atlas-7B (43.95%→79.72%), 30.41% for UI-TARS-7B (47.45%→77.86%), 14.92% for AgentCPM-GUI-8B (64.08%→79.00%), and 24.03% for GUI-Owl-7B (53.57%→77.60%). The most dramatic gain is in Negative Action Match Rate (N-AMR), where OS-Atlas-7B jumps from 35.80% to 96.48% (+60.68%) and UI-TARS-7B from 39.96% to 96.53% (+56.57%), indicating near-elimination of false-positive toggling. On the dynamic evaluation benchmark, StaR raises task success rate from 10% to 55% for OS-Atlas-7B and from 35% to 40% for UI-TARS-7B. On general agentic benchmarks (AndroidControl, AITZ, GUI-Odyssey), StaR preserves or improves performance, with up to 20.17% gains in Task Success Rate on GUI-Odyssey.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StaR training decisively outperforms all zero-shot baselines on O-AMR: the best zero-shot agent (Qwen-2-VL-72B at 66.42%) is surpassed by every StaR-trained 7–8B model (77.60–79.72%), demonstrating that StaR bridges the model-scale gap. Against prompt engineering, StaR training yields substantially larger gains (e.g., OS-Atlas-7B: 50.07% with prompting vs. 79.72% with StaR training), confirming that structured reasoning must be learned rather than prompted. StaR-style prompting (a structured prompt mimicking the three-step reasoning) also underperforms StaR training across all four agents, further validating the necessity of fine-tuning. On the dynamic evaluation benchmark, StaR consistently improves task success rates for all three tested agents (UI-TARS-7B: 35%→40%, OS-Atlas-7B: 10%→55%, AgentCPM-GUI-8B: 20%→42.5%), with the largest relative gain on the weakest-reasoning model. On general agentic benchmarks (AndroidControl, AITZ, GUI-Odyssey), StaR preserves or improves all four standard metrics (TMR, AMR, TSR, GMR), with the most notable TSR improvement of 7.14%→20.17% on GUI-Odyssey, indicating no degradation in general task performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal GUI agents (OS-Atlas, UI-TARS, AgentCPM-GUI, GUI-Owl) and structured reasoning in MLLMs (CoAT, chain-of-action-thought). It departs from two natural prior approaches—prompt engineering for state awareness and multi-agent annotator collaboration—by arguing both are fundamentally insufficient, and instead proposes training agents on an explicit three-step state-aware reasoning chain (perceive → analyze → decide). The construction of a dedicated state control benchmark (81K samples) also extends the evaluation landscape beyond the general agentic benchmarks (AndroidControl, AITZ, GUI-Odyssey) that prior GUI agent work relies on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even GPT-5 achieves below 50% accuracy on binary toggle instructions reveals a previously underappreciated bottleneck in GUI agent reliability, with real consequences in precision-critical domains (automotive, industrial control). StaR's model-agnostic training recipe offers a practical path to close this gap without architectural changes or external annotators. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-state controls (sliders, dropdowns), non-mobile platforms, and whether the structured reasoning chain can be distilled into lighter inference-time overhead. The benchmark itself, while constructed from public datasets, provides a reusable evaluation resource for the community.
