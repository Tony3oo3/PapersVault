## Problem

Existing feed-forward 4D reconstruction methods (e.g., ST4RTrack, Dynamic Point Maps) process only pairwise frames and rely on post-optimization to align results, limiting long-range motion coherence. Moreover, prior geometric diffusion models strictly align 3D attribute distributions with the original VAE latent space, despite the fundamentally different distributions of unbounded world-coordinate geometry versus bounded RGB images. This paper asks whether such strict alignment is truly necessary and whether geometry and motion can be jointly modeled in a unified representation across an entire video sequence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264017/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of N frames (H×W×3 each) and outputs, in a single feed-forward pass, a dense 3D point map (H×W×3) for every frame and a 3D scene flow vector (H×W×3) between each pair of consecutive frames, all expressed in a shared world coordinate system (the first frame's frame). Because both geometry and motion are defined in world space, static background points ideally exhibit zero flow, and no camera pose estimation or post-optimization is required at inference time.

## Method

MotionCrafter builds on Stable Video Diffusion and introduces a unified 4D VAE consisting of a Geometry VAE and a Motion VAE that jointly encode point maps and scene flows into a single latent space via channel-wise concatenation. A key insight is replacing the conventional max-normalization to [-1,1] with canonical normalization (centering by the mean of all valid 3D points and scaling by their mean distance), which preserves scale invariance and dramatically improves reconstruction quality, especially for large-scale outdoor scenes. The U-Net is conditioned on SVD's video latents (channel-wise concatenated with the 4D latent) and trained with EDM pre-conditioning in a deterministic paradigm that combines latent-space diffusion loss with direct geometry and motion reconstruction losses. Training follows a progressive two-stage pipeline: the Geometry VAE is trained first, then the Motion VAE with the Geometry VAE frozen, and finally the U-Net with both VAEs frozen. Ablations confirm that the unified concatenation fusion strategy, while not optimal at the VAE stage, yields superior downstream U-Net performance, and that relaxed distribution alignment consistently outperforms strict alignment.

## Results

On the joint geometry-and-motion benchmark (Table 1), MotionCrafter achieves an average rank of 1.0 across five datasets, outperforming all baselines by 38.64% in geometry (Rel^p) and 25.0% in motion (EPE) on average. For example, on Spring it reduces geometry Rel^p from 123.27 (ST4RTrack-S) to 29.20, and on VKITTI2 it cuts motion EPE from 156.85 (ST4RTrack-S) to 71.75. On the geometry-only benchmark (Table 2), MotionCrafter achieves the best Monkaa score (Rel^p 25.88, δ^p 74.01) but trails VGGT on Sintel (32.46 vs. 26.83 Rel^p) and DDAD (21.27 vs. 15.98 Rel^p), which the authors attribute to the absence of camera-ray and depth-map modalities.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionCrafter dominates all pairwise DUSt3R-based methods (ST4RTrack-S/P, POMATO, DELTA) by large margins, especially on Spring and VKITTI2 where pairwise approaches degrade severely (e.g., ST4RTrack-P reaches 874.94 EPE on VKITTI2 vs. 71.75 for MotionCrafter). Against Zero-MSF, the closest competitor, MotionCrafter wins on geometry across all five datasets (e.g., 29.20 vs. 142.44 Rel^p on Spring) and on motion for Spring (5.61 vs. 7.78 EPE) and VKITTI2 (71.75 vs. 112.99 EPE), but Zero-MSF+GT retains a slight edge on Dynamic Replica (0.40 vs. 0.51 EPE) and Point Odyssey (2.30 vs. 3.49 EPE) motion metrics. In the geometry-only comparison (Table 2), MotionCrafter outperforms all camera-centric methods (DepthPro, MoGe, GeoCrafter) and MonST3R, but VGGT remains stronger on Sintel (26.83 vs. 32.46 Rel^p) and DDAD (15.98 vs. 21.27 Rel^p), which the authors attribute to VGGT's multi-modal design incorporating camera rays and depth maps. Geo4D is competitive on DDAD (14.58 vs. 21.27 Rel^p) but uses post-optimization, whereas MotionCrafter reports results without any post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionCrafter extends the line of work on leveraging pretrained video diffusion models for geometric reconstruction, most directly building on Geo4D (which fine-tunes SVD for 4D point maps) and the DUSt3R/VGGT world-coordinate point map representation. It departs from pairwise methods like ST4RTrack and Dynamic Point Maps by processing long video sequences in a single feedforward pass without post-optimization, and from Geo4D by jointly encoding both geometry and scene flow into a unified 4D VAE latent rather than predicting independent per-frame point maps. A key conceptual departure is the rejection of strict distribution alignment between 3D geometric data and the VAE's original RGB latent space, replacing max-normalization with canonical (mean/scale) normalization and full VAE fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint geometry-and-motion formulation in a shared world coordinate system eliminates the need for per-scene optimization or pairwise alignment, making dense 4D reconstruction practical for long in-the-wild videos. The finding that relaxed distribution alignment outperforms strict alignment challenges a widely-held assumption in geometric diffusion and could simplify the adaptation of video diffusion priors to other structured 3D/4D modalities. Limitations include the single-modal design (no camera rays or depth supervision), limited outdoor training data, and forward-only scene flow; the authors identify multimodal integration as a promising direction for closing the gap with camera-aware methods like VGGT on certain benchmarks.
