## Problem

Existing 3D house layout generation methods operate at disconnected spatial scales: house-level models produce unfurnished room layouts, while room-level models place furniture in isolation, and multi-stage pipelines that combine them suffer from error accumulation and spatial inconsistency. Moreover, prior graph-based diffusion backbones rely on homogeneous GNNs that cannot capture the semantic and geometric disparity between rooms (high-level adjacency logic) and furniture (fine-grained geometry and diverse categories), and no prior work supports flexible masked conditional generation from a flat, house-scale heterogeneous graph in a single pass.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a heterogeneous house graph with three node types (house, room, furniture) and five typed edge relations, where users can mask arbitrary node categories with a learnable [MASK] token to specify partial layouts, or supply only topology (node/edge types and connectivity) with no attributes. A free-form natural-language description is optionally parsed by an LLM into a masked semantic graph plus a structured BERT-encoded prompt, and a house-level bounding box provides global geometric context. The output is a complete 3D furnished layout: per-node 3D bounding boxes in hierarchical local coordinates (rooms relative to house root, furniture relative to parent room), orientation classes, semantic categories for masked nodes, and DeepSDF shape codes for furniture, from which meshes are retrieved via nearest-neighbor search in shape-code space.

## Method

Nestwork represents each house as a heterogeneous graph and applies latent diffusion over node-level embeddings produced by a HetGAT autoencoder that performs type-specific message passing across three node types and five relation types. The unconditional decoder incorporates a Low-Rank Relational Field (LRF) that reconstructs edge embeddings from node latents via slot-attention over a shared dictionary of relational prototypes and a rank-32 bilinear factorization, providing relational structure without ground-truth geometric edge features at test time. A single DDPM denoiser (also HetGAT-based) is trained once with 50% random node-label masking, enabling the same model to operate under any conditioning strength from topology-only to fully annotated graphs without retraining. Multi-level conditioning combines masked node-level cross-attention (injecting semantic context post-HetGAT via residual connections) with graph-level embeddings from the house bounding box and an optional BERT-encoded text prompt, both projected and linearly combined with learned coefficients before the final residual update.

## Results

On the HG-FRONT test set (3,111 houses from 3D-FRONT), Nestwork's unified one-pass model achieves FID 7.26, KID 0.26×10⁻³, 91.91% room-graph constraint satisfaction, 88.69% walkability, and KL divergence 0.0317 under RoomGraph conditioning. The two-stage HouseDiffusion + DiffuScene baseline scores FID 41.90 and KID 2.97×10⁻³, a roughly 5.8× gap in FID. Under full semantic graph conditioning with the HetGAT backbone, Nestwork reaches FID 7.13, KID 0.29×10⁻³, 92.34% graph constraint satisfaction, and 11.21% collision rate (GT baseline: 9.80%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-stage HouseDiffusion [34] + DiffuScene [38] pipeline, Nestwork's one-pass model wins decisively on FID (7.26 vs. 41.90), KID (0.26 vs. 2.97 ×10⁻³), graph constraint satisfaction (91.91% vs. 78.60%), walkability (88.69% vs. 86.26%), and KL divergence (0.0317 vs. 0.1823), while the two-stage pipeline has a marginally lower collision rate (9.84% vs. 10.91%), though both are near the 9.80% GT baseline. In the autoencoder backbone study (Table 2), Nestwork's HetGAT outperforms TripletGCN [6] (FID 7.13 vs. 7.50; graph 92.34% vs. 88.29%) and SLN-Box [25] (FID 7.13 vs. 7.64; collision 11.21% vs. 14.80%) under identical full-semantic-graph conditioning, confirming that heterogeneous type-specific message passing yields a more expressive latent space. The paper does not directly benchmark against CHOrD [36] or other house-scale diffusion methods because none expose explicit room-furniture graph conditioning or masked-graph control, which the authors identify as the closest but non-comparable prior work.

## Relation to Prior Work

Nestwork unifies two previously separate lines of work—house-level floorplan generation (HouseDiffusion, Graph2Plan) and room-level furniture placement (DiffuScene, Graph-to-3D)—into a single heterogeneous graph diffusion framework. The architectural backbone composes GAT, edge-featured attention (EGAT), and heterogeneous type-specific transformations (HEAT) into a HetGAT that preserves type-specific message passing across room and furniture nodes, departing from the homogeneous GNNs used in prior scene-graph diffusion methods. The Low-Rank Relational Field, which reconstructs edge embeddings from node latents via slot-attention and rank-r factorization, is a novel mechanism for providing relational structure without ground-truth geometry at test time, and the random-masking training strategy for a single denoiser to handle multiple conditioning levels has no direct precedent in 3D layout generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the recursive, disconnected pipeline of two-stage systems, Nestwork demonstrates that joint reasoning over room structure and furniture placement in a single latent graph substantially reduces error accumulation and spatial inconsistency, which is critical for practical architectural design tools. The flexible masked-graph conditioning and natural-language interface lower the barrier for non-expert users to explore layout concepts. However, the model does not enforce functional plausibility (e.g., unobstructed circulation paths), limiting it to early-stage ideation rather than finalized design; extending to multimodal inputs (floor plans, sketches) and interactive subgraph inpainting are natural next steps that could broaden its applicability.
