## Problem

The text-to-image generation paradigm is shifting from diffusion models to multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which offer substantially stronger semantic understanding but may introduce new safety risks. No systematic comparison has examined how MLLMs and diffusion models differ in their propensity to generate unsafe content or their susceptibility to fake image detection, leaving it unclear whether existing safety defenses remain effective under the emerging paradigm.

## Input / Output

The evaluation feeds text prompts of varying complexity (unsafe, benign, non-English, and progressively enriched descriptive prompts) into seven generative models with all external safety filters disabled, producing 10 images per prompt for unsafe scoring and 1 image per prompt for detection testing. Outputs are scored by OpenAI's Moderation API (yielding a 0–1 unsafe score per prompt-model pair) and by four fake image detectors (yielding detection accuracy), with an additional human-annotated gender bias measurement on 500 images from gender-neutral sexual prompts.

## Method

This is a large-scale empirical study rather than a new architecture. The authors curate 1,184 unsafe prompts from four prior datasets plus two self-constructed variants (TemplateLong with expanded sentence structure, and TemplateLongChinese with full translation) to probe semantic and cross-lingual comprehension, generating 82,880 images across seven models. For detection, they produce 14,000 benign images from MSCOCO and Flickr30k prompts and evaluate four detectors (two commercial, two open-source), then fine-tune and train-from-scratch two research detectors on paradigm-inclusive balanced data to isolate distribution bias. A key ablation is the prompt-extension experiment, where three progressively richer prompt versions (v0→v2) are used to test whether MLLMs' superior language understanding produces increasingly realistic, harder-to-detect outputs—a behavior absent in diffusion models. The central finding is that MLLMs' semantic strength is a double-edged sword: it enables faithful rendering of abstract, non-English, and metaphorical unsafe prompts (raising unsafe scores) and, with richer prompts, generates images that deceive even well-trained detectors, while diffusion models' weaker comprehension yields damaged outputs that are both inherently safer and easier to flag.

## Results

On the TemplateLong unsafe prompt dataset, MLLMs achieve mean unsafe scores of 0.300–0.613 (Janus Pro highest at 0.613), while both diffusion models score only 0.200–0.280. Under non-English (Chinese) prompts (TemplateLongChinese), diffusion models produce zero unsafe images (mean 0.000) due to complete semantic failure, whereas MLLMs maintain scores of 0.013–0.407. For fake image detection, AIorNot-SigLIP2 achieves >0.810 accuracy on diffusion-model images but only 0.478–0.600 on MLLM images (Janus, VILA-U) on MSCOCO. Prompt enrichment further degrades detection: TokenFlow drops from 0.934 to 0.799 and VILA-U from 0.982 to 0.877 under the trained-from-scratch DE-FAKE detector, while diffusion models remain near 0.997.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two diffusion baselines (SD3.5 Large, SD3.5 Large Turbo), all five MLLMs produce substantially higher unsafe scores across every prompt dataset; on TemplateLong, the gap reaches 0.613 (Janus Pro) vs. 0.280 (SD3.5 Large), and on TemplateLongChinese the gap is absolute (0.407 for Bagel vs. 0.000 for both diffusion models). The diffusion models' apparent safety is partly an artifact: they produce damaged/unrecognizable images for 66–80% of complex prompts (vs. 0–4.5% for MLLMs), and 99.4% of those damaged outputs are classified as safe. Among MLLMs, Janus Pro and VILA-U consistently show the highest unsafe scores, while TokenFlow is the safest MLLM but still exceeds both diffusion models on most datasets. For detection, all four detectors (Winston.AI, Illuminarty, DE-FAKE, AIorNot-SigLIP2) show lower accuracy on MLLM images than diffusion images; the gap is largest for AIorNot-SigLIP2 (0.478 for Janus vs. >0.810 for diffusion on MSCOCO). Fine-tuning DE-FAKE on multi-source data recovers >90% accuracy across all models, but single-source fine-tuning on Janus generalizes poorly to other MLLMs, indicating weak cross-model transfer. Prompt extension (v0→v2) degrades detection accuracy for MLLMs (e.g., VILA-U: 0.982→0.877) but leaves diffusion models essentially unchanged (~0.997), confirming that MLLMs' semantic richness is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the safety evaluation methodology established by Qu et al. (CCS 2023) and Wu et al. (CCS 2024), which defined unsafe content categories and unsafe-score metrics for diffusion-based T2I models. The key departure is a systematic cross-paradigm comparison: prior work examined diffusion models in isolation, whereas this paper introduces MLLMs (Bagel, Janus, Janus Pro, TokenFlow, VILA-U) as a second paradigm and directly contrasts their safety behavior against SD3.5 Large and SD3.5 Large Turbo. For fake image detection, the paper repurposes existing detectors (DE-FAKE, AIorNot-SigLIP2, Winston.AI, Illuminarty) that were designed and validated primarily on diffusion-model outputs, applying them to MLLM-generated images for the first time in a controlled setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The findings have immediate practical implications for content moderation: MLLMs' multilingual and semantically rich prompt interpretation expands the attack surface beyond what current safety filters (tuned for diffusion models) can handle, and a simple prompt-extension strategy can evade even well-trained detectors. The methodological insight that diffusion models' high rates of damaged outputs (80% for SD3.5 Large) are classified as 'safe,' artificially inflating their safety scores, calls into question prior safety benchmarks. Open questions include how to build detectors that generalize across rapidly evolving MLLM architectures, how to address the multilingual attack surface, and whether the gender bias observed in unsafe generation persists under different prompt formulations or model versions.
