## Problem

Dynamic-resolution VLMs produce long visual token sequences that are computationally expensive to process, and existing pruning methods (FastV, SGP) rely on attention-weight aggregation or answer-driven heuristics that become unreliable when the guiding small model lacks prior knowledge for a given query. This leads to noisy token retention and substantial degradation of the large VLM's reasoning capacity, especially at aggressive pruning ratios and on visually demanding instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262639/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution image (tiled into crops and encoded by a ViT into patch embeddings) concatenated with a text prompt as input to a small VLM. A single forward pass of the small VLM produces a per-token importance score vector (KL divergences) over all visual tokens; the top-K% tokens are hard-pruned and forwarded to the large VLM, which generates the final text answer. Unlike SGP, no autoregressive decoding is required from the small model, and the importance map is computed without explicitly materializing attention weights, making it compatible with FlashAttention.

## Method

IF-Prune formulates visual token importance estimation as an amortized variational inference problem: a lightweight information bottleneck module (two MLP layers plus learnable per-channel prior mean and variance) maps each visual token embedding from the small VLM's last hidden state to a token-wise Gaussian latent variable. The posterior mean is computed via a channel-wise sigmoid gating mechanism that bounds how far the posterior can deviate from the prior, stabilizing the KL term. Training minimizes a reconstruction loss (cross-entropy on the ground-truth answer conditioned on reparameterized latent tokens) plus a token-wise KL penalty with an adaptive linear schedule on β. At inference, the KL divergence between each token's approximate posterior and the shared prior directly yields the importance score, and the top-K% tokens are retained with their original position embeddings preserved, enabling a single small-VLM forward pass to guide pruning of any same-architecture large VLM without further training.

## Results

On InternVL2-26B across eight benchmarks, IF-Prune retains 95.44% of full-model performance (score ratio) while keeping only 5% of visual tokens (K=5%, L=0), outperforming SGP (89.25%) and FastV (59.10%) by 6.2 and 36.3 percentage points respectively. At a more moderate 20% retention (K=20%, L=9), IF-Prune achieves 99.55% score ratio, slightly ahead of SGP (99.15%) and substantially ahead of FastV (93.18%) and ToMe (94.88%). In terms of efficiency, IF-Prune reduces total FLOPs to 61.2% of baseline at K=5% while SGP requires 67.9%, and IF-Prune achieves 19.5 tokens/s throughput versus SGP's 16.4 tokens/s on InternVL2-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IF-Prune consistently outperforms all three baselines at aggressive pruning (K=5%). Against SGP (the closest competitor, also using a small VLM for guidance), IF-Prune achieves 95.41% vs. 88.50% score ratio at K=5%, L=2 — a 6.9-point gap — and 95.44% vs. 89.25% at L=0. Against FastV (attention-based progressive pruning), the gap is even larger: 95.41% vs. 75.05% at K=5%, L=2, and 95.44% vs. 59.10% at L=0, where FastV collapses. Against ToMe (encoder-side token merging), IF-Prune leads by 13.1 points at K=5% (95.41% vs. 82.33%). At moderate pruning (K=20%), IF-Prune edges out SGP by 0.4 points (99.55% vs. 99.15%) and leads FastV and ToMe by 6.4 and 4.7 points respectively. Additionally, IF-Prune requires only a single forward pass of the small VLM (4.7T FLOPs) versus SGP's autoregressive generation (14.5T FLOPs), yielding 19.5 tokens/s throughput vs. SGP's 16.4 tokens/s on InternVL2-8B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the variational information bottleneck (VIB) framework from Alemi et al. (ICLR 2016) from a sequence-level compression tool to a token-level pruning signal for vision-language models. It directly inverts the small-VLM-guided pruning paradigm introduced by SGP (Wang et al., CVPR 2025): rather than aggregating attention weights from a small model's full autoregressive decoding to identify important tokens, IF-Prune trains the small model to approximate the posterior distribution of *non-informative* tokens via a lightweight latent bottleneck, using per-token KL divergence as the importance score. This shifts the mechanism from answer-driven attention heuristics (as in FastV and SGP) to a principled probabilistic formulation that is query- and answer-aware without requiring explicit attention weight extraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: a single forward pass of a 1B model replaces SGP's full autoregressive decoding, cutting small-model FLOPs by ~3× and enabling compatibility with FlashAttention, while retaining 95% of full-model accuracy at 5% token retention. The finding that one fine-tuned small VLM transfers reliably across InternVL scales (1B→8B→26B) suggests a reusable pruning infrastructure rather than per-model tuning. Open questions include whether the VIB formulation generalizes beyond the InternVL architecture family, whether it extends to video or multi-image settings, and whether the learnable per-channel prior captures truly task-relevant structure or merely learns a convenient compression code.
