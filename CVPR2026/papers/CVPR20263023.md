## Problem

Existing multi-animal tracking (MAT) datasets for wild animals are severely limited in scale (under one hour of annotated footage), species diversity (no more than 5 species), and geographic/temporal coverage, with most lacking high-quality manual segmentation masks. No single benchmark provides the scale, diversity, and annotation precision needed to train or evaluate general-purpose MAT models for real-world wildlife conservation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmarked models consume camera trap videos (320×194 to 2688×1234 resolution, 10–60 FPS, 0.5–90 s duration) paired with a text prompt (a specific species name or the generic query 'animal') and produce per-frame segmentation masks, bounding boxes (trivially derived from mask spatial limits), and persistent track IDs for each individual animal. Annotations are provided at 6 fps, and the dataset additionally supplies hard and easy negative species labels per video to support precision evaluation of open-vocabulary detectors.

## Method

The core contribution is the SA-FARI dataset: 11,609 camera trap videos from 741 independent locations across 4 continents over 10 years (2014–2024), exhaustively annotated with 16,224 masklet identities and 942,702 bounding boxes and segmentation masks spanning 99 species categories. Annotation follows a two-stage pipeline in which SAM 3 pseudo-annotates all video–species pairs at 6 fps, then human annotators verify visibility, correct masks using SAM 2 in the loop, and perform an exhaustivity check. For benchmarking, the authors evaluate SAM 3 (baseline, with SA-FARI included in training, and fine-tuned on SA-FARI), GLEE, LLMDet+SAM3TR, and vision-only pipelines (MegaDetector paired with ByteTrack, OCSort, or BoostSort++) under both species-specific and species-agnostic prompting. Fine-tuning SAM 3 on SA-FARI yields +32.9 cgF1 and +19.6 pHOTA over the untrained baseline, while the species-agnostic SAM 3 (SA-FARI) surpasses the best vision-only tracker by +23.9 IDF1. The test set is further partitioned into challenging, night, multi-masklet, large-masklet, and small-masklet subsets to enable fine-grained diagnosis of where models fail.

## Results

On the species-specific prompt evaluation (Table 3), fine-tuning SAM 3 on SA-FARI (SAM 3 FT) achieves 46.9 cgF1 and 68.1 pHOTA (Det 55.4, Ass 84.6), a +32.9 cgF1 and +19.6 pHOTA improvement over the unmodified SAM 3 baseline (14.0 cgF1, 48.5 pHOTA). In the species-agnostic setting (Table 4), SAM 3 trained on SA-FARI reaches 71.1 IDF1 and 64.4 HOTA, surpassing the best vision-only baseline (MD+BoostSort++: 47.2 IDF1, 38.3 HOTA) by +23.9 IDF1 and +18.9 HOTA. Across test subsets, small-masklet samples are the hardest (52.2 pHOTA vs. 81.4 for large masks), while multi-animal and nighttime videos perform near the overall average (~67–68 pHOTA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the species-specific setting, SAM 3 FT (SA-FARI) dominates all baselines: it outperforms GLEE [49] by +47.1 cgF1 (46.9 vs. −0.2) and +60.6 pHOTA, and LLMDet+SAM3TR [19] by +44.3 cgF1 and +26.8 pHOTA. The unmodified SAM 3 [12] baseline already leads GLEE and LLMDet, but incorporating SA-FARI data during training (SAM 3 SA-FARI) or fine-tuning yields the largest gains, confirming the value of domain-specific annotations. In the species-agnostic setting, SAM 3 (SA-FARI) [12] beats all three MegaDetector [6]-based pipelines: it exceeds MD+BoostSort++ [44] (the strongest vision-only tracker) by +23.9 IDF1 (71.1 vs. 47.2) and +26.1 HOTA (64.4 vs. 38.3), and surpasses MD+ByteTrack [52] by +32.5 IDF1 and MD+OCSort [11] by +37.2 IDF1. Notably, the vision-only baselines show a trade-off between detection and association (e.g., BoostSort++ has the best HOTA-Ass at 80.8 but the worst HOTA-Det at 18.4), whereas SAM 3 (SA-FARI) achieves strong scores on both sub-metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not introduce a new method but rather addresses a critical gap in the dataset landscape for multi-animal tracking (MAT) in the wild. Prior MAT benchmarks (AnimalTrack, GMOT, TAO/BURST) are small-scale (<4 hours), sourced from YouTube or mixed benchmarks, and lack ecological sensor footage. Wild-specific datasets (BuckTales, BaboonLand, KABR, WildLive) are confined to single protected areas, a handful of species, and often rely on unverified automated annotations. SA-FARI carves new ground by consolidating 11,609 camera trap videos from 741 sites across 4 continents with exhaustive, manually verified segmentation masks — a combination no prior dataset achieves — and benchmarks it using SAM 3 (itself built on SAM 2's memory-based tracking) alongside vision-only pipelines (MegaDetector + ByteTrack/OCSort/BoostSort++).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SA-FARI is the first dataset large and diverse enough to train and evaluate general-purpose MAT models for wild animals, directly enabling downstream conservation tasks (abundance estimation, individual re-identification, behaviour recognition) that currently bottleneck on manual annotation. The benchmarks reveal that even SOTA open-vocabulary models (SAM 3) achieve only 14.0 cgF1 on wild footage without domain data, and that fine-tuning on SA-FARI yields a ~3× improvement — underscoring that scale and annotation quality, not just model architecture, are the limiting factors. Open questions include geographic bias (four ecoregions, no Arctic or Australian representation), the absence of audio/pose/depth modalities, and whether the camera-trap-only collection strategy limits generalisation to UAV or drone-based monitoring. The dataset's long-tailed species distribution and the finding that small-masklet and occluded animals remain substantially harder to track suggest targeted data collection and model development are still needed.
