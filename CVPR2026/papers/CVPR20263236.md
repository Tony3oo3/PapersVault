## Problem

Existing VI-ReID methods rely on rigid feature alignment in a shared embedding space, which fails to capture the complex, instance-specific correlations between visible and infrared appearances and degrades under the severe data imbalance (far fewer infrared samples) typical of VI-ReID datasets. This paper asks whether a matching-based paradigm that learns adaptive, pair-specific transformations can outperform fixed global alignment for cross-modality person retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a visible-infrared image pair, each processed by a shared ViT-B/16 backbone into patch-level embeddings of shape N×C (with overlap stride 12). Rather than producing a fixed-length feature vector for cosine distance, the network outputs a single scalar similarity score in [0,1] per pair via the QA Scoring module, which is directly used for retrieval ranking. At inference, a coarse-to-fine strategy first ranks all gallery candidates by backbone features, then applies the full BCI + QA Scoring pipeline only to the top-K (K=50) candidates, yielding 14 ms per query.

## Method

BIT is a matching-based encoder-decoder architecture where the ViT backbone serves as the encoder and two modules form the decoder. The Bi-directional Cross Interaction (BCI) module stacks T=3 Transformer-style blocks in which visible and infrared patch features iteratively attend to each other via cross-attention (each modality queries the other) followed by an MLP, with residual connections preserving modality-specific cues; an Aggregation Contrastive Loss enforces identity-consistent interaction. The Query Aware Scoring module then computes bidirectional patch-wise similarity matrices, applies Top-k filtering (k=3) and Reciprocal Patch Mining to retain only mutually selected patch pairs (with a smooth-complementary fallback weighted by α=0.2 for full coverage), and feeds the resulting patch-level similarity vector into a small Confidence-Aware Scoring MLP that adaptively aggregates informative matches into the final score. Training is two-stage: the backbone is first trained with standard identity + triplet losses, then frozen while BIT is optimized with a binary pairwise matching loss plus the aggregation contrastive loss (λ=0.6).

## Results

On SYSU-MM01 (the primary benchmark), BIT achieves 80.53% Rank-1 and 79.76% mAP in All-Search Single-Shot mode without re-ranking, and 87.42% Rank-1 / 89.25% mAP in Indoor-Search Single-Shot. With re-ranking, BIT reaches 84.42% Rank-1 and 83.64% mAP (All-Search Single-Shot). On LLCM, BIT attains 73.1% Rank-1 (V2I) and 66.7% Rank-1 (I2V); on RegDB, it reaches 96.12% Rank-1 / 92.43% mAP (V2I) and 93.65% Rank-1 / 93.54% mAP (I2V), setting new state-of-the-art on all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SYSU-MM01 All-Search Single-Shot (without re-ranking), BIT (80.53% Rank-1) outperforms the strongest prior methods DiVE (79.07%), PartMix (77.78%), and WRIM-Net (77.40%) by 1.5–3.1 points in Rank-1, and leads in mAP by a wider margin (79.76% vs. 77.03% for SAAI). In the re-ranking comparison, BIT^R (84.42% Rank-1) surpasses IDKLR (81.42%) by 3.0 points and CIFTR (74.08%) by 10.3 points. On LLCM, BIT achieves a 8.2-point Rank-1 advantage over the runner-up HOS-Net (73.1% vs. 64.9% V2I) and a 11.8-point gap over DEEN. On RegDB, BIT leads CMT (96.12% vs. 95.17% V2I Rank-1) and IDKLR (96.12% vs. 94.72%) while also achieving the best mAP in both V2I (92.43%) and I2V (93.54%) directions. Notably, BIT's advantage is most pronounced in mAP, suggesting more consistent retrieval quality across the full ranking rather than just top-1 accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

BIT departs from the dominant feature-level paradigm in VI-ReID, where prior methods (PMT, CMT, SAAI, etc.) learn a fixed mapping to align visible and infrared features into a shared embedding space. Instead, it repurposes the matching-based architecture popular in single-modality Re-ID (TransMatcher, DeepReID) and adapts it to the cross-modality setting through a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism borrowed from vision-language pretraining (ALBEF). The key conceptual shift is from global, pair-agnostic feature alignment to instance-specific, pair-aware interaction: rather than asking 'where should this IR feature live in a shared space?', BIT asks 'how should this specific visible-infrared pair interact to produce a reliable match?' The backbone (ViT-B/16) and training protocol follow PMT, so the contribution is architectural rather than foundational.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The matching-based framing offers a principled answer to a persistent practical problem in VI-ReID: severe modality imbalance (far fewer IR than VIS training samples) that degrades feature-alignment methods. By focusing on relational modeling rather than global representation learning, BIT is argued to be more robust to this imbalance, though the paper's controlled imbalance experiments are deferred to supplementary material. Open questions include whether the quadratic O(N²) training cost limits scalability to larger backbones or higher-resolution inputs, whether the matching-based advantage persists in truly unsupervised or open-world settings, and whether the approach generalizes beyond pedestrian Re-ID to other cross-spectral retrieval tasks. The inference efficiency claim (O(K) with K=50) is practical but the 4ms latency overhead over PMT may matter in real-time surveillance deployments.
