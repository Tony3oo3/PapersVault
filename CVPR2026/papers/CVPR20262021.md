## Problem

Instruction-based video editing lags behind its image counterpart in visual fidelity, temporal consistency, and controllability. Existing approaches are either training-free (fragile, slow, low success rate) or trained on narrow synthetic pairs that limit generalization. The paper argues that the design space across data curation, architecture, and control has not been systematically studied, and that pretrained text-to-video models already possess latent editing capability that can be unlocked with minimal adaptation.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a source video (81 frames at 832×480 or 1280×704), a free-form text instruction, an optional binary mask video that simultaneously encodes spatial regions (which pixels to edit) and temporal scheduling (which frames the edit activates and how it evolves), and an optional reference image (e.g., from an external image editor applied to a source frame). The output is a temporally coherent edited video at the same resolution and frame count. When no mask is provided, the model defaults to a full-video, instruction-only edit; the reference image is randomly dropped during training so the model remains robust to its absence at inference.

## Method

EasyV2V builds on the Wan-2.2-TI2V-5B diffusion-transformer backbone and injects the source video via sequence concatenation of its patch-embedded tokens ahead of the noisy target tokens, rather than the more common channel-wise concatenation, which ablations show yields substantially higher edit quality. A separate zero-initialized patch-embedding layer encodes the mask video, whose tokens are added element-wise to the source tokens (exploiting the mask's low-frequency nature to avoid inflating the sequence), while an optional reference image is encoded and appended at the end of the token sequence to stay closer to the target for stronger guidance. Training uses LoRA (rank 256) on the DiT attention layers with all new parameters zero-initialized, keeping the backbone and VAE frozen to preserve generative priors and avoid catastrophic forgetting. The data pipeline composes off-the-shelf experts with fast inverses (edge↔video, depth↔video), lifts large-scale I2I edit pairs into pseudo-V2V pairs via shared 2D affine camera trajectories, mines dense-captioned T2V clips for action-centric continuation pairs, and synthesizes transition supervision to teach how edits unfold over time.

## Results

On EditVerseBench (160 videos, 16 edit types), EasyV2V without any reference image achieves a VLM editing-quality score of 7.73/9, the highest among all compared methods, surpassing EditVerse (7.64) and the commercial Runway Aleph (7.48). With a Flux-Kontext reference image, EasyV2V further improves frame-level text alignment to 28.10 and video-level alignment to 25.13, while maintaining a PickScore of 20.61. On the ImgEdit Bench (image editing as single-frame video), EasyV2V outperforms EditVerse by a margin of 0.54 despite not being specifically designed for image editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EasyV2V (w/o Ref.) achieves the best VLM editing-quality score of 7.73, edging out the closest competitor EditVerse (7.64) and the commercial Runway Aleph (7.48) by 0.09 and 0.25 points respectively. Against prior instruction-guided methods, the margin is substantial: Señorita-2M scores 6.45, InsV2V 4.95, and InsViE-1M 4.36, all well below EasyV2V. Training-free approaches (TokenFlow at 5.02, STDF at 4.20) lag further behind, confirming the benefit of supervised fine-tuning. With a Flux-Kontext reference image, EasyV2V attains the best frame-level text alignment (28.10) and PickScore (20.61), though EditVerse retains a slight edge on video-level alignment (25.37 vs. 25.13). The concurrent Lucy Edit (5.96) is outperformed by a wide margin across all four metrics, and qualitative analysis shows it suffers from motion mismatches and limited edit-type coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

EasyV2V builds on the Wan-2.2 text-to-video diffusion transformer as a frozen backbone, adding lightweight conditioning via sequence concatenation of source video tokens and LoRA fine-tuning. It departs from prior instruction-based video editors (InsV2V, Señorita-2M, InsViE-1M) primarily in its data strategy: rather than relying on a single generalist teacher or training new task-specific experts, it composes off-the-shelf experts with fast inverses and lifts large-scale I2I editing data into pseudo-V2V pairs via shared affine camera trajectories. Architecturally, it contrasts with channel-concatenation approaches (e.g., Lucy Edit) by showing sequence concatenation preserves cleaner source/target separation, and it unifies spatial and temporal edit control in a single mask video rather than relying on separate keyframe or token-schedule mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, reusable recipe—composable expert data, sequence-concat LoRA on a T2V backbone, and unified mask control—that practitioners can adopt to build high-quality video editors without training from scratch. Its data pipeline (especially the I2I-to-V2V lifting and dense-caption continuation mining) addresses a key bottleneck in the field: the scarcity of high-quality paired video editing data. Limitations include ~1-minute inference precluding real-time use, no camera-pose control, and a modest SOTA margin over the closest concurrent work (EditVerse, 7.73 vs 7.64 VLM score), suggesting the field is converging. Open questions include whether the recipe transfers cleanly to next-generation backbones and whether the mask-based temporal control generalizes to more complex multi-stage edit schedules.
