## Problem

Existing multimodal sentiment analysis methods decompose features into a binary common-vs-private structure, implicitly assuming signals are either globally shared across all modalities or entirely modality-specific. This overlooks submodally shared cues—sentiment signals expressed only by a subset of modalities (e.g., sarcasm conveyed jointly by tone and facial expression but absent from lexical content)—which get misattributed to private channels and lost, limiting the expressiveness and discriminative power of multimodal representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262069/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted linguistic features (encoded by BERT), visual features, and acoustic features (each processed by independent temporal convolutional networks) as variable-length token sequences, projected into a unified feature dimension. It outputs either a continuous sentiment score in [-3, 3] (regression on CMU-MOSI/MOSEI) or a categorical sentiment label (7-class or 2-class), and also predicts one of 20 intent categories on MIntRec. Evaluation is conducted under both aligned (perfect temporal sync) and unaligned (realistic asynchrony) settings.

## Method

TSD factorizes multimodal representations into three complementary subspaces via dedicated encoders: a shared common encoder for global modality-invariant features, pairwise shared encoders (one per modality pair) for submodally shared synergies, and per-modality private encoders for unique cues. A tri-subspace decoupling supervisor (a three-branch discriminator) plus structured regularization losses—common consistency, pairwise collaboration, private disparity via HSIC, and cross-subspace orthogonality—enforce subspace purity and suppress information leakage. On top of these disentangled representations, the Subspace-Aware Cross-Attention (SACA) module performs self-attention refinement within each subspace, constructs context sets from complementary subspaces, applies multi-head cross-attention with residual connections, and finally aggregates all nine enhanced subspace outputs through a learned gating mechanism for adaptive weighted fusion.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI (unaligned), TSD achieves 0.691 MAE, 49.0% ACC₇, 86.5% ACC₂, and 86.6% F1, outperforming the strongest baseline EMOE by 0.006 MAE and 1.2% ACC₇. On CMU-MOSEI (unaligned), TSD reaches 0.525 MAE and 54.6% ACC₇, surpassing EMOE by 0.005 MAE and 0.7% ACC₇. On the MIntRec multimodal intent recognition benchmark, TSD attains 73.67% accuracy and 72.57% F1, exceeding the best prior method CAGC (73.03% ACC, 70.62% F1) by 0.64% accuracy and 1.95% F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TSD outperforms all 17 baselines across both MSA benchmarks and the MIR benchmark. Against the strongest prior method EMOE [3], TSD improves MAE by 0.006 on CMU-MOSI (0.691 vs. 0.697) and 0.005 on CMU-MOSEI (0.525 vs. 0.530) under the unaligned setting, while also gaining 1.2% ACC₇ on MOSI and 0.7% ACC₇ on MOSEI. Compared to other recent disentanglement-based methods (FDMER, DLF, ConFEDE), TSD achieves 0.034–0.051 lower MAE on MOSI and 0.007–0.011 lower MAE on MOSEI, indicating that the tri-subspace factorization captures signals missed by binary shared-private decompositions. On MIntRec, TSD leads CAGC [21] by 0.64% accuracy and 1.95% F1, and EMOE [3] by 1.09% accuracy and 1.84% F1, confirming transferability beyond sentiment regression. Older baselines (EF-LSTM, TFN, LMF, MFN) trail TSD by 0.28–0.73 MAE on MOSI, underscoring the gains from explicit submodally-shared modeling and SACA fusion over simple or tensor-based fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the disentangled representation learning lineage initiated by MISA (binary shared-private decomposition) and refined by FDMER (contrastive modality discriminators) by introducing a third subspace type: submodally shared (pairwise) representations that capture information common to exactly two of three modalities. The decoupling supervisor generalizes FDMER's modality discriminators from a two-way to a three-way classification task, and the SACA fusion module applies structured cross-attention across the three subspace types rather than treating all features uniformly. The paper explicitly positions itself as an architectural and representation design contribution rather than a new theoretical framework, building on the observation from prior work [2,31] that intermediate pairwise-shared features are semantically meaningful but have been collapsed into private channels by existing common-vs-private methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tri-subspace decomposition addresses a genuine structural gap in multimodal representation learning: the binary common/private assumption forces pairwise-shared signals (e.g., audio-visual sarcasm cues absent from language) into private channels, where they receive limited influence on the final prediction. The framework's generalization to multimodal intent recognition (MIntRec) suggests the subspace factorization is not task-specific. However, the performance margins over strong baselines like EMOE are modest (≤0.009 MAE), and it remains open whether the gains would hold with more than three modalities, whether the pairwise subspaces capture genuinely distinct information versus a more flexible parameterization, and whether the decoupling supervisor's three-way discrimination is necessary compared to simpler orthogonality constraints alone.
