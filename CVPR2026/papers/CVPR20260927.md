## Problem

CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection suffers from foreground-background feature entanglement, where the model produces strong responses in both object and background regions, obscuring subtle fine-grained anomalies. Existing CLIP adaptation methods rely on single EOT text embeddings (coarse semantics) and treat visual tokens homogeneously, failing to explicitly separate anomaly-relevant foreground cues from irrelevant background context.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260927/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a frozen ViT-L/14@336px CLIP backbone, producing both image-level anomaly scores (for classification) and pixel-level anomaly maps (for localization). On the text side, it uses object-agnostic learnable prompts encoding generic 'normal' and 'damaged' semantics rather than class-specific descriptions. The model is fine-tuned on test data from one dataset (MVTec AD or VisA) and evaluated zero-shot on 16 industrial and medical benchmarks, with a batch size of 4 on a single RTX 3090.

## Method

FB-CLIP introduces four complementary modules: (1) Multi-Strategy Text Feature Fusion (MSTFF) fuses EOT, mean-pooled, and attention-weighted token features into a single text embedding for richer semantic guidance; (2) Multi-View Foreground-Background Enhancement (MVFBE) generates a soft foreground mask from four anomaly indicators (local saliency, center distance, CLS inconsistency, temporal variation), then applies three enhancement views—Identity, Semantic (modeling foreground information richness vs. background stability via cosine-similarity-weighted attention), and Spatial (5×5 neighborhood aggregation with foreground/background separation)—integrated through FB-Attention with LSTM-inspired adaptive gating; (3) Background Suppression (BS) constructs a prototypical background vector from multi-layer candidate tokens and subtracts it with similarity-weighted enhancement to remove residual interference; (4) Semantic Consistency Regularization (SCR) enforces confident predictions via entropy minimization and discriminative separation via a margin constraint on the normal/abnormal similarity gap, inspired by SVM principles.

## Results

FB-CLIP achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA zero-shot anomaly detection across 16 industrial and medical datasets. On the industrial domain, it attains the best pixel-level AUPRO on VisA (91.4%), MPDD (91.1%), BTAD (80.0%), and Real-IAD (88.2%, a +6.6% gain over AF-CLIP), while matching AF-CLIP on MVTec (85.7%). In the medical domain, FB-CLIP ranks among the top two in 14 out of 18 reported metrics, outperforming FAPrompt (top-two in only 10 metrics), with notable wins on ClinicDB (73.5% PRO), Kvasir (85.0% AUROC), and Endo (70.4% PRO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FB-CLIP outperforms all seven baselines on VisA (89.5/90.7 image AUROC/AP vs. AF-CLIP's 88.5/90.0) and achieves the best pixel-level PRO on Real-IAD (88.2% vs. AF-CLIP's 81.6%, a 6.6-point margin), MPDD (91.1%), and BTAD (80.0%). On MVTec, FB-CLIP ties AF-CLIP at 85.7% pixel PRO and 92.3% image AUROC, while on DAGM and DTD it is within 0.5–1.0 points of the best. In the medical domain, FAPrompt edges out FB-CLIP on HeadCT (94.0 vs. 93.4 AUROC), BrainMRI (95.8 vs. 95.1), Br35H (97.6 vs. 97.1), and TN3K (84.7 vs. 79.0 PRO), while AF-CLIP leads on ISIC (94.8 vs. 93.9 AUROC). FB-CLIP wins decisively on ClinicDB (73.5% PRO, +3.5 over AF-CLIP), Kvasir (85.0% AUROC), and Endo (70.4% PRO), and overall ranks top-two in 14 of 18 metrics versus FAPrompt's 10.

## Relation to Prior Work

FB-CLIP extends the AnomalyCLIP (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) framework, which introduced object-agnostic learnable prompts for zero-shot anomaly detection via CLIP, by addressing two specific limitations: coarse single-EOT text representations and homogeneous treatment of visual tokens. On the textual side, it departs from the prompt-learning lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, AnomalyCLIP, FAPrompt) by fusing EOT, global-pooled, and attention-weighted token features into a richer multi-strategy representation. On the visual side, it moves beyond token-level enhancement strategies (WinCLIP, AF-CLIP, AdaCLIP) by explicitly performing multi-view foreground-background soft separation along identity, semantic, and spatial dimensions, combined with a background suppression module and a semantic consistency regularization loss inspired by SVM margin constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, modular recipe for improving CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection in industrial and medical settings, with consistent gains across 16 datasets spanning both domains. However, the 'zero-shot' evaluation is qualified: the model is fine-tuned on the test set of one dataset (MVTec AD or VisA) before being evaluated on others, which introduces a semi-supervised element that limits true zero-shot generalization claims. Open questions include whether the multi-view separation and background suppression components are necessary beyond CLIP backbones, whether the approach extends to 3D medical volumes, and whether the fine-tuning-on-test-data protocol can be replaced with truly unsupervised adaptation.
