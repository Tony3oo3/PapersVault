## Problem

Existing teacher-student DAOD methods are constrained by small-scale backbones and limited internal knowledge, making them vulnerable to domain gaps. While recent VFM-based approaches (e.g., DT) inject generalized knowledge, they discard the teacher model entirely, losing domain-specific adaptation. No prior work effectively synergizes the complementary strengths of both a large VFM (generalized, domain-invariant cues) and a teacher model (domain-specific properties) to guide student learning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes labeled source-domain images (Pascal VOC or Cityscapes) for initial training and unlabeled target-domain images (Clipart1k, BDD100k daytime, or Foggy Cityscapes) for adaptation. The expert model (DINOv3 ViT-H+ Faster R-CNN) processes target images offline at 592-pixel height to pre-generate pseudo labels and class prototypes, incurring zero runtime cost during training. The teacher-student pair (VGG16 or ResNet101 backbones) operates online at 600-pixel height, producing bounding boxes and class labels for target-domain objects evaluated at IoU 0.5.

## Method

ETS implements a teach-then-consolidate strategy across two levels. At the label level, the ETCT module performs IoU-based detection matching between offline expert pseudo labels and online teacher pseudo labels, categorizing them into consistent, inconsistent, and unilateral detections; it then applies confidence-weighted box fusion, confidence-discrepancy-based class reassignment, and asymmetric thresholding (stricter for teacher unilateral detections) to produce high-quality collaborative pseudo labels. At the representation level, the ETJC module attaches a 2-layer MLP prototype network to each model's ROI features and enforces three cosine-similarity alignment losses: expert-student (using frozen prototypes extracted at iteration 20,000), teacher-student, and cross-domain (source vs. target within the student). The expert model is a frozen DINOv3 ViT-H+ Faster R-CNN trained solely on source data and used in a 'free lunch' manner, while the teacher is updated via EMA from the student, and prototype alignment begins at iteration 25,000 of an 80,000-iteration mutual-learning stage.

## Results

ETS achieves 49.8% mAP on Cityscapes→BDD100k (C→B), surpassing the previous SOTA DT [26] (47.8%) by 2.0% and the next-best DA [23] (45.8%) by 4.0%. On Pascal VOC→Clipart1k (P→Cl), ETS reaches 49.0% mAP, outperforming CDMT [2] (47.7%) and SEEN-DA [29] (47.9%). On Cityscapes→Foggy Cityscapes (C→F), ETS attains 56.2% mAP, exceeding DT [26] (55.4%) by 0.8% and DATR [7] (53.4%) by 2.8%. ETS outperforms all 22 compared baselines across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETS outperforms all 22 compared baselines on all three benchmarks. The closest competitor is DT [26] (CVPR'25), which replaces the teacher with a frozen DINOv2 labeller; ETS surpasses DT by 2.0% mAP on C→B (49.8 vs. 47.8) and by 0.8% on C→F (56.2 vs. 55.4), demonstrating that retaining the teacher model alongside the VFM expert yields complementary gains. On P→Cl, ETS beats the strongest baselines CDMT [2] (47.7%) and SEEN-DA [29] (47.9%) by 1.3% and 1.1% respectively. Among conventional teacher-student methods, ETS gains +3.3% over AT [34] and +2.0% over CMT [4] on P→Cl, and +8.0% over SOCCER [13] and +6.5% over DATR [7] on C→B, confirming the benefit of incorporating VFM-based expert knowledge. On C→F, ETS leads DATR [7] by 2.8% and CDMT [2] by 3.7%, with particularly notable improvements on rare classes such as bus (+3.4% over DT) and train (+3.9% over DT).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the teacher-student DAOD paradigm (AT, CMT, MIC) by integrating a frozen VFM-based expert model alongside the teacher, departing from DT [26] which entirely replaced the teacher with an offline DINOv2 labeller. The key conceptual departure is the argument that generalized (domain-invariant) and domain-specific knowledge are complementary rather than substitutable, motivating a dual-level collaboration: label-level fusion via ETCT and representation-level consolidation via ETJC. The prototype alignment mechanism draws from prior prototype-based DAOD work (DATR, PACF) but extends it to a three-model (expert-teacher-student) setting with cross-domain self-alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'free lunch' formulation—using VFMs purely offline for pseudo-labeling and prototype extraction without online feature alignment—offers a practical path to leverage rapidly improving foundation models without incurring their computational cost during training. The finding that the teacher model remains essential even when a strong VFM is available challenges the implicit assumption in DT that VFMs can fully subsume the teacher's role. Open questions include whether the collaborative mechanism generalizes to multi-source or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the reliance on a specific VFM backbone (DINOv3 ViT-H+) limits transferability as new foundation models emerge.
