## Problem

Existing text-to-audio models excel at synthesizing short clips (10–30 s) but cannot produce long-form narrative audio that maintains temporal coherence, emotional tone consistency, and compositional structure across extended durations. Generating coherent multi-event audio sequences (e.g., a chase scene with footsteps, thunder, and a car skid) requires decomposing complex instructions into logically ordered sub-events with precise timing—capabilities that current TTA and unified multimodal LLMs lack.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260763/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

AudioStory accepts multimodal instructions in three formats: text-only prompts for narrative generation, audio+text for continuation, and video+text for dubbing. The LLM first produces an intermediate reasoning chain (storyline summary, event count, per-event timestamps and descriptions), then sequentially emits a caption, duration, and bridging tokens for each audio segment. The final output is a concatenated long-form audio sequence (up to 150 s) composed of individually generated clips, each conditioned on its own semantic and residual bridge queries plus duration information.

## Method

AudioStory couples a Qwen-2.5-3B LLM with a TangoFlux-initialized DiT through a decoupled bridging mechanism that splits conditioning into semantic tokens (aligned to Flan-T5 text embeddings via MSE loss) and residual tokens (capturing low-level acoustic details via the DiT's flow-matching loss), merged by multi-head cross-attention before conditioning the generator. The LLM performs interleaved reasoning generation: it decomposes the instruction into temporally ordered events, then for each event autoregressively produces a caption, duration, and both token types in a single forward pass. Training follows a progressive three-stage recipe—(I) single-audio generation with LoRA on the LLM and full DiT updates, (II) unified generation and understanding on single clips, and (III) long-audio adaptation with interleaved reasoning supervision—enabling end-to-end joint optimization of instruction comprehension and audio synthesis rather than zero-shot LLM-diffusion coupling.

## Results

On the AS-10k long-form narrative audio benchmark, AudioStory achieves the best instruction-following score (4.1/5), CLAP score (0.392), reasoning score (4.2/5), and generation quality (FD 1.43, FAD 3.00) while supporting up to 150s of audio, compared to 10–30s for all baselines. On single-audio generation (AudioCaps test set), AudioStory-Base attains FD 1.52, FAD 2.29, KL 0.51, and CLAP 0.383, marginally surpassing TangoFlux (FD 1.57, FAD 2.34, CLAP 0.385). For audio understanding, AudioStory-Base leads on ClothoCaps (SPIDEr 24.1, CIDEr 37.7) and ClothoAQA (ACC 42.8, B-ACC 60.6), outperforming NExT-GPT and Spider.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AudioStory outperforms all eight baselines on the primary long-form narrative audio task. Against pure TTA models, it beats AudioLDM2 by +1.3 on instruction-following and +0.096 on CLAP, and TangoFlux by +0.9 on instruction-following and +0.075 on CLAP, while also achieving substantially lower FD (1.43 vs. 3.43 and 2.48) and FAD (3.00 vs. 4.49 and 3.49). Against LLM-based unified models, AudioStory surpasses LLM+CoDi and LLM+NExT-GPT by large margins on consistency (4.1 vs. 1.4/1.8) and generation quality (FAD 3.00 vs. 4.04/3.99), with the gap attributable to the decoupled bridging mechanism and end-to-end training. On single-audio generation (Table 3), AudioStory-Base edges out TangoFlux (FAD 2.29 vs. 2.34, FD 1.52 vs. 1.57) and clearly outperforms Make-An-Audio and Stable Audio Open. On audio understanding (Table 2), AudioStory-Base leads NExT-GPT by +10.3 SPIDEr and +17.4 CIDEr on ClothoCaps, and surpasses Spider by +1.1 SPIDEr and +1.3 CIDEr on AudioCaps. The only metric where a baseline scores higher is consistency: AudioLDM2 reaches 4.6 vs. AudioStory's 4.1, but this is on 10s clips with weak instruction-following (2.8), making it a less meaningful comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

AudioStory sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) text-to-audio generation models (AudioLDM, TangoFlux, Stable Audio) that synthesize short clips from captions but lack temporal coherence and compositional reasoning, and (2) any-to-any multimodal LLMs (CoDi, NExT-GPT, Spider) that bridge LLMs with diffusion backbones but are confined to single short segments and simple caption-to-sound tasks. The key departure is the decoupled bridging mechanism—replacing the standard text-only bridge with separate semantic tokens (supervised by Flan-T5 embeddings) and residual tokens (learned via flow-matching loss)—combined with an interleaved divide-and-conquer reasoning process that decomposes narrative instructions into temporally ordered sub-events. Unlike prior LLM-diffusion integrations that rely on zero-shot or modular pipelines, AudioStory employs end-to-end progressive training to jointly optimize instruction comprehension and audio synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first dedicated benchmark (AS-10k) for long-form narrative audio generation, filling a clear evaluation gap, and demonstrates that LLM-based reasoning can substantially improve instruction-following and temporal consistency over simple LLM+TTA pipelines. The decoupled bridging design and the finding that generation-first progressive training outperforms understanding-first ordering offer practical architectural guidance for future LLM-diffusion systems. Open questions remain: the framework does not yet handle speech or overlapping audio segments, the evaluation relies heavily on Gemini-based scoring (validated only by a small human study), and the baselines (e.g., LLM+TangoFlux with naive clip concatenation) are relatively simple, leaving room for stronger compositional baselines.
