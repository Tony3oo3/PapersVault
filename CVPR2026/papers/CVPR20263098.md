## Problem

Video LLMs generate tens of thousands of visual tokens from multi-frame inputs, causing quadratic attention cost and prohibitive inference latency. Existing training-free token pruning methods allocate budgets uniformly across frames and spatial regions, ignoring the highly uneven spatiotemporal information density inherent in videos, which wastes scarce token budgets on low-information backgrounds while under-representing complex, dynamic content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263098/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames (extracted at 1 FPS, capped at 128 frames), a text prompt, and codec metadata extracted for free from the video bitstream — specifically per-pixel residual data and per-GoP average packet sizes. It produces a compressed visual token sequence (reduced to 50% or 30% of the original budget) that is concatenated with text tokens and fed to the LLM for standard autoregressive text generation. The codec metadata requires no additional computation beyond parsing the existing bitstream, making it a zero-cost signal.

## Method

MeToM is a training-free, three-stage token merging framework that exploits codec metadata as free proxies for spatiotemporal information density. First, Residual-Guided Patch Merging (RPM) uses per-pixel codec residuals as spatial density cues to merge low-information patches into fewer tokens during tokenization, before the visual encoder runs. Second, Budget-Adaptive Token Merging (BTM) uses average GoP packet size as a temporal complexity signal to allocate per-frame token budgets proportionally to √c_t, then selects top-scoring tokens as a coreset, removes cross-frame temporal redundancy via cosine similarity, and folds remaining low-density tokens into their nearest core token. Third, Multi-Layer Attention-Guided Token Merging (MATM) operates inside the LLM, aggregating attention scores across multiple layers to identify the lowest-contribution visual tokens and merging each into its most similar surviving token, thereby reducing prefill FLOPs and KV-cache footprint.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, MeToM achieves 102.0% relative accuracy and 41.5% TTFT at the 50% token budget, and 101.2% accuracy with 27.3% TTFT at the 30% budget, both outperforming the full-token baseline (100%). The method delivers a 2.65× end-to-end inference speedup over the unpruned baseline, with the LLM backbone prefill reduced from 1329 ms to 215 ms (6.2× faster). Across five video QA benchmarks (VideoMME, LongVideoBench, MLVU, EgoSchema, NExT-QA), MeToM consistently attains the best accuracy–latency trade-off among all training-free baselines at both budget levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At the 50% budget, MeToM (102.0% avg accuracy, 41.5% TTFT) outperforms all five baselines on both accuracy and latency; the closest competitor is STTM (101.7% acc, 44.7% TTFT), while FastV and HoliTom trail by 2.5 and 1.6 percentage points in accuracy respectively. At the more aggressive 30% budget, the gap widens: FastV and HoliTom drop below the 100% baseline (98.4% and 99.6%), whereas MeToM still exceeds it (101.2%) with the lowest TTFT (27.3%). ToMe matches MeToM's accuracy on some individual datasets (e.g., VideoMME 101.4% vs. 102.7%) but incurs substantially higher latency (34.1% TTFT vs. 27.3%), and DyCoke/STTM achieve comparable accuracy (100.3%/100.7%) yet require 28.1%/31.3% TTFT. On the larger LLaVA-Video-72B backbone, MeToM is the only method to improve accuracy at 50% budget (101.4%) while also achieving the lowest TTFT (41.9%), and at 30% it retains 99.1% accuracy versus 97.1% (ToMe) and 98.3% (DyCoke). On Qwen2VL-7B, MeToM delivers the best accuracy–latency trade-off at 30% (101.3% acc, 23.7% TTFT), whereas ToMe degrades to 97.9% accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free visual token compression line (ToMe, FastV, DyCoke, STTM, HoliTom) by introducing a fundamentally different signal source: codec metadata (residual energy for spatial density, GoP packet sizes for temporal density) rather than post-encoder feature similarity or single-layer attention scores. Conceptually, it repurposes signals from traditional video compression (H.264/H.265 bitstreams) as zero-cost proxies for spatiotemporal information density, a connection not previously exploited in the VLLM efficiency literature. The three-stage pipeline (pre-encoder spatial merging, pre-LLM budget allocation, in-LLM attention-guided merging) unifies ideas from ViT token pruning and inner-LLM compression under a single metadata-driven allocation principle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MeToM offers a practical, training-free path to substantially reducing VLLM inference cost (2.65× speedup) while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy, which is directly relevant to deploying video LLMs in latency-sensitive applications. The reliance on codec metadata is both a strength (zero additional computation) and a limitation: it requires the video to be in a compressed format with accessible bitstream information, which may not hold for raw frame sequences or streaming inputs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to uncompressed or synthetic video, how it interacts with retrieval-augmented or streaming video understanding, and whether richer metadata signals (e.g., motion vectors, quantization parameters) could further improve the density estimation.
