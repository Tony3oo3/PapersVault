## Problem

Anatomical structures in MRI exhibit strong spatial priors—well-defined boundaries, low inter-subject variability, and consistent topology—that induce discrete, clustered patterns in latent space. Conventional continuous generative priors (diffusion/SDE models) assume smooth manifold distributions, which oversmooth these discrete anatomical transitions and diminish diagnostic clarity. No prior framework explicitly models and leverages the discrete structural distribution of medical images in the reconstruction pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes complex-valued undersampled k-space measurements (split into real and imaginary channels) along with coil sensitivity maps, under 1D uniform sampling at 4×, 8×, or 12× acceleration on fastMRI knee (~34,000 scans) and brain (~11,000 volumes, center-cropped to 320×320) datasets. At each of L=64 hierarchical stages, the network generates K=32 full-image candidates, refines them, and propagates the top-t selected hypotheses to the next resolution level, ultimately producing a single reconstructed MRI image. The coarse-to-fine hierarchy starts from a zero-filled input at H/2^P resolution and progressively upsamples to full resolution.

## Method

DiCoS (Discrete-Continuous Synthesis) unifies three components in a coarse-to-fine loop: (1) a Discrete Prior Network (DPN) with lightweight convolutional generators and K parallel 1×1 heads that produce diverse anatomy-aware candidates at each resolution stage; (2) Micro Diffusion Cycles (MDC) that apply T=3 predictor-corrector steps guided by a pretrained VE-SDE score function followed by a hard k-space data-consistency projection, injecting continuous texture without long-chain diffusion; and (3) a Dual-domain Balanced Scoring (DBS) module that sequentially aggregates k-space fidelity and image-domain TV regularity through a Chain-of-Experts-style expert chain with an adaptive one-layer MLP router, plus a learnable usage-balance bias to prevent hypothesis collapse. Training is supervised with a combined L1 image + L2 k-space reconstruction loss on the top-ranked candidate, plus a score-alignment loss that enforces monotonic correspondence between DBS scores and true reconstruction errors. The key insight is that medical images form compact discrete clusters (validated via VQ-VAE codebook features and clustering metrics), making a discrete hypothesis generation + lightweight continuous refinement pipeline inherently more aligned with anatomical structure than purely continuous denoising.

## Results

On the fastMRI knee dataset at 8-fold acceleration, DiCoS achieves PSNR 35.42 dB, NMSE 1.36%, and SSIM 86.64%, outperforming the strongest baseline SelfRDB (34.54 dB, 1.86%, 85.74%) by ~0.9 dB in PSNR. On the brain dataset at 8-fold, DiCoS reaches 37.06 dB PSNR and 1.23% NMSE, again leading SelfRDB (35.41 dB, 1.84%) by a clear margin. At the most challenging 12-fold setting, DiCoS maintains 35.32 dB (knee) and 37.24 dB (brain) PSNR while all continuous-diffusion baselines drop by 1–3 dB. Inference time is 3.23 s per volume (T=3 MDC steps), roughly 2.4× faster than SelfRDB (7.83 s) and 3.4× faster than AdaDiff (37.83 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiCoS outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (NMSE, PSNR, SSIM) across both the knee and brain datasets at all three acceleration factors (4×, 8×, 12×). The closest competitor is SelfRDB [2], a recursive diffusion-bridge method; DiCoS leads it by 0.88–1.65 dB in PSNR and by 0.4–0.6% in NMSE, with the gap widening at higher acceleration (e.g., 1.45 dB at 12× knee). Among continuous-diffusion baselines, HFS-SDE [4] is the strongest, yet DiCoS still exceeds it by ~1.0 dB PSNR at 8× and ~1.7 dB at 12× on the knee set. The discrete-prior baseline DDN [47] (the paper's primary discrete comparison) lags DiCoS by 4–6 dB PSNR, confirming that discrete reasoning alone without continuous refinement is insufficient. In the MedSAM-based semantic evaluation, DiCoS achieves Dice 0.921 and IoU 0.842, surpassing the next-best SelfRDB (0.892 / 0.821) and all other methods. Inference-wise, DiCoS (3.23 s at T=3) is 2.4× faster than SelfRDB (7.83 s) and 11.7× faster than AdaDiff (37.83 s), though slightly slower than DDN (2.07 s), which trades off accuracy for speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

DiCoS departs from the dominant continuous generative reconstruction paradigm (score-based SDEs, DDPM-style denoising) by arguing that anatomical structures in MRI are inherently discrete—exhibiting well-defined boundaries, low inter-subject variability, and consistent topology—that smooth manifold assumptions systematically oversmooth. It draws on discrete representation learning (VQ-VAE, DDN) for hypothesis generation and on score-based SDEs (Chung & Ye, HFS-SDE) for lightweight continuous refinement, but unifies them in a novel coarse-to-fine multi-hypothesis pipeline with a Chain-of-Experts-inspired dual-domain scoring mechanism. The closest prior work is DDN (Yang, ICLR 2025), which models discrete distributions for general inverse problems but does not incorporate the hierarchical discrete-continuous synthesis or the k-space/image-domain balanced scoring that DiCoS introduces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled reframing of MRI reconstruction as a discrete-continuous reasoning problem rather than a single-trajectory denoising task, and the MedSAM-based semantic evaluation provides a clinically meaningful validation beyond pixel metrics. Open questions include whether the discrete-continuous paradigm generalizes to 3D volumetric reconstruction, other imaging modalities (CT, ultrasound), or dynamic/functional MRI, and whether the K=32 candidate budget scales gracefully to higher-dimensional problems. The reliance on a pretrained score-SDE prior and the fixed hierarchical architecture also raise questions about adaptability to unseen acquisition protocols or pathologies not well-represented in the discrete codebook.
