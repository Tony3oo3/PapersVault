## Problem

RLVR training drives Large Reasoning Models toward over-deterministic policies, causing entropy collapse and premature convergence that limits reasoning performance. Existing remedies target token-level entropy, but the paper argues the root cause is rigidity in the latent hidden-state dynamics that govern token generation—a richer computational structure invisible to token-space interventions, especially problematic for multimodal models where cross-modal computations are misaligned with text-centric outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263755/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For LLMs, the method consumes text prompts (mathematical queries from DAPO-Math-17K and MATH levels 3–5) and produces chain-of-thought reasoning trajectories with final answers; for MLLMs, it takes image+text prompts (ViRL39K) and produces multimodal reasoning trajectories. During training, the method additionally extracts last-layer hidden state sequences (T×d tensors) from the policy model for each generated trajectory, which are fed through a frozen neural Koopman dictionary to compute per-trajectory DSD scores that modulate the GRPO loss.

## Method

ReLaX linearizes the nonlinear latent dynamics of last-layer hidden states using a ResKoopNet-based neural Koopman dictionary (a single linear layer with sigmoid activation, learned once from initial-policy trajectories and frozen thereafter). It defines Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD) as the variance of the Koopman eigenvalue magnitudes, quantifying the heterogeneity of the model's internal computational modes. DSD is integrated into the GRPO objective as a log-mean-exp regularization term, weighted by positive-truncated advantages so that exploration is encouraged only along reward-positive trajectories. An adaptive KL penalty is applied selectively to trajectories whose DSD exceeds a threshold ξ, preventing over-dispersion while preserving exploration potential in less rigid trajectories. This combination of latent-space spectral regularization, advantage shaping, and threshold-gated KL control yields a principled exploration-exploitation tradeoff that outperforms token-level entropy methods across 13 benchmarks.

## Results

On 7 multimodal reasoning benchmarks, ReLaX-VL-7B achieves a mean@1 average of 53.2, setting a new state-of-the-art among 7B-scale multimodal reasoning models (surpassing VL-Rethinker-7B at 52.5 by 0.7). On 6 text-only mathematical reasoning benchmarks, ReLaX on Qwen2.5-7B-Math reaches a 49.1 average (mean@1/mean@32), outperforming the previous best FR3E (42.8) by 6.3 points. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, ReLaX achieves 43.5 average versus FR3E's 39.2 (+4.3) and vanilla GRPO's 34.8 (+8.7). At the 3B scale, ReLaX-VL-3B (48.1) outperforms several 7B-level models including R1-VL (40.9) and OpenVLThinker (45.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLaX outperforms all 13 baselines across both multimodal and text-only settings. On multimodal benchmarks, ReLaX-VL-7B (53.2 avg) surpasses the closest competitor VL-Rethinker-7B (52.5) by 0.7, and leads MM-Eureka-7B (49.7), Vision-R1-7B (49.0), OpenVLThinker-7B (45.3), and R1-VL-7B (40.9) by 3.5–12.3 points; SRPO-7B reports partial results but trails on available benchmarks. On text-only math, ReLaX on Qwen2.5-7B-Math (49.1 avg) beats FR3E (42.8) by 6.3, DCPO (partial, ~44.5 est.), DAPO (partial), and vanilla GRPO/SimpleRL (37.6) by 11.5 points. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, ReLaX (43.5) leads FR3E (39.2) by 4.3 and SimpleRL (34.8) by 8.7, with particularly large gains on AMC23 (84.1 vs. 67.5 for FR3E) and AMC22 (65.4 vs. 49.2). The ablation in Fig. 6 further shows ReLaX outperforms KL-Cov and entropy regularization (R1-zero-Div) on multimodal tasks, with the gap widening on visually grounded benchmarks (e.g., +7.7 on EMMA-Physics over KL-Cov), confirming that latent-space exploration is more effective than token-level entropy manipulation for MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR training paradigm (GRPO/DeepSeekMath) by introducing a Koopman-operator-based diagnostic of latent dynamics, drawing on ResKoopNet and ResDMD from the dynamical systems literature. It departs from the dominant line of work on exploration in RLVR—which manipulates token-level entropy via reward reshaping, entropy regularization, or token-level heuristics—by arguing that entropy collapse is a surface symptom of deeper rigidity in hidden-state dynamics. The key conceptual move is repurposing Koopman spectral analysis (previously applied to fluid dynamics and, more recently, to LLM hidden states in [57]) as a tractable, differentiable proxy for policy exploration, yielding the DSD metric and its integration into the GRPO objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, operator-theoretic lens for understanding and regulating exploration in RLVR, shifting the intervention point from discrete token statistics to continuous latent computation. This is particularly relevant for multimodal models, where the paper shows token-level feedback poorly reflects cross-modal processing in latent space. Open questions include whether the frozen Koopman dictionary remains valid as the policy evolves substantially, how DSD scales to much larger models or longer contexts, and whether the spectral-dispersion framing generalizes beyond autoregressive LLMs to other generative architectures. The approach also raises the question of whether other Koopman-derived quantities (e.g., specific eigenvalue phases or mode interactions) could yield finer-grained control over reasoning behavior.
