## Problem

Existing event-based egocentric 3D pose estimation methods (EventEgo3D, EventEgo3D++) achieve low 3D accuracy and suffer from temporal jitter and drift because their architectures are not tailored to the asynchronous, continuous, change-driven nature of event streams. They rely on 2D heatmaps, test-time segmentation masks, and only short-term temporal context (a single previous frame), making them highly sensitive to self-occlusions and rapid motion in head-mounted settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260479/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw event streams from a head-mounted event camera with a fisheye lens, converted into Locally Normalised Event Surface (LNES) frames of size 192×256×2 (two polarity channels) over 20 ms windows, yielding N=40 frames per sequence. It outputs 16 3D joint positions per timestep at 80 Hz on a single A6000 GPU, producing temporally consistent trajectories where the latent state persists across timesteps without explicit caching. The causal inference mode uses only past and current frames, while a non-causal mode can access the full sequence for slightly higher accuracy.

## Method

E-3DPSM formulates egocentric event-based 3D pose estimation as a continuous state-evolution problem with two main modules. The Spatiotemporal Pose Encoder (SPEM) combines a four-stage CNN pyramid with per-stage deformable attention for spatial reasoning under fisheye distortion, event-specialised S5 state-space model layers at stages 2 and 4 for long-range bidirectional temporal modelling, and a joint-query transformer decoder that maps 16 learnable joint embeddings to 192-dim tokens. The Pose Regression Module (PRM) predicts both a direct 3D pose and a delta pose (relative 3D displacement aligned with the change-based nature of events), then fuses them via a learnable Kalman-style filter that maintains a latent state and covariance, adaptively weighting the delta update against the direct observation to suppress drift. Training is supervised end-to-end with 3D/2D pose MSE, delta pose loss, bone-length, and bone-orientation losses, first on EE3D-R (15 epochs) then fine-tuned on EE3D-W (10 epochs), without requiring pre-training on synthetic data.

## Results

On EE3D-R, E-3DPSM (causal) achieves 84.45 mm MPJPE and 62.64 mm PA-MPJPE, reducing MPJPE by 19% and e_smooth by 2.7× compared to EventEgo3D++ (103.28 mm MPJPE, 22.93 mm e_smooth). On EE3D-W, the causal variant reaches 158.86 mm MPJPE and 93.46 mm PA-MPJPE, an 8% MPJPE reduction and 1.7× smoother trajectories over EventEgo3D++. The non-causal variant improves further (81.32 mm MPJPE on EE3D-R, 155.82 mm on EE3D-W), while the causal mode runs at 80 Hz on a single A6000 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-3DPSM outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both datasets. Against the closest event-based competitor EventEgo3D++ [26], the causal variant reduces MPJPE by 19% on EE3D-R (84.45 vs. 103.28 mm) and 8% on EE3D-W (158.86 vs. 172.43 mm), while cutting temporal jitter (e_smooth) by 2.7× and 1.7× respectively. The RGB-based EgoPoseFormer [44] (adapted to accept LNES input) is the weakest baseline, trailing by 40–60 mm in MPJPE and 3–4× in e_smooth, confirming that frame-based architectures are poorly suited to asynchronous event streams. Even EventEgo3D [25], the original event-based method, is surpassed by 24% in MPJPE on EE3D-R. The occlusion-only evaluation (Table 2) further shows E-3DPSM achieving the lowest error on occluded joints (67.49 mm MPJPE vs. 88.43 mm for EventEgo3D++ on EE3D-R), indicating that the SSM-based temporal modelling and learned fusion specifically address the self-occlusion failures of prior approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends EventEgo3D and EventEgo3D++ for egocentric event-based 3D human pose estimation, but fundamentally rethinks the architecture: instead of predicting 2D heatmaps and lifting to 3D with a segmentation mask, E-3DPSM formulates the problem as a continuous state-space evolution where each event window updates a latent state and produces delta 3D joint displacements. It draws on event-specialised S5 state-space layers [50] for long-range temporal modelling, deformable attention from recent egocentric pose work [2, 44] for spatial reasoning under fisheye distortion, and differentiable Kalman filtering [11, 15, 20] for adaptive fusion of delta and direct pose predictions. The key conceptual departure is aligning the regression target (relative 3D joint changes) with the change-based nature of event data, rather than forcing absolute 3D position regression as in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-3DPSM demonstrates that tailoring the architecture to the asynchronous, change-driven nature of event streams—via delta pose regression, SSM-based temporal state evolution, and learned fusion—yields substantially more accurate and temporally stable 3D poses than the heatmap-and-segmentation pipeline inherited from RGB methods. The 80 Hz real-time operation on a single workstation makes it practically relevant for immersive VR/AR avatar control and hands-free interfaces. Limitations include sensitivity to strong occlusions and highly dynamic environments, and the approach is validated only on two egocentric event benchmarks; whether the state-machine formulation generalises to other event-based 3D vision tasks (e.g., object tracking, scene flow) remains an open question.
