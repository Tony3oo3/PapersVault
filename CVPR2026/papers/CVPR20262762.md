## Problem

Gas chimneys—vertical zones of fluid migration that distort seismic reflectivity—are critical indicators of hydrocarbon charge and drilling hazards, yet no public dataset provides pixel-level annotations or paired degraded/clean seismic images for training and evaluating detection and enhancement models. Existing physics-based imaging pipelines are computationally expensive and sensitive to model errors, while deep learning approaches lack the labeled seismic data needed for supervised training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262762/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For the detection task, the model receives a single 512×512-pixel 2D seismic image (covering ~4.2 km² in real-world scale) and outputs a binary pixel-level mask delineating gas chimney regions. For the enhancement task, the input is the degraded seismic image conditioned on the gas chimney mask, and the output is a restored seismic image with recovered structural continuity beneath the chimney. Each dataset sample also includes the original velocity model, gas saturation map, and gas-affected velocity model, enabling multi-modal analysis.

## Method

SIGMA is generated through a physics-based pipeline that starts from 20 real-world velocity models, injects random fracture networks grown via constrained random-walk processes, and simulates gas diffusion into surrounding shale using a closed-form diffusion equation (Eq. 5). The resulting gas saturation field modifies the bulk modulus and density of the formation (Eqs. 7–8), from which a compressional-wave velocity perturbation is derived (Eq. 9) and multiplied with the background velocity to produce the gas-affected model. Both the clean and gas-affected velocity models are then imaged via reverse time migration using the Deepwave framework, yielding 400 paired seismic images with exact pixel-level chimney masks. The paper benchmarks four segmentation architectures (FaultSeg, DualUnet, FaultFormer, FaultViT) and five enhancement models (ConditionGAN, SeisDDPM, SeisGAN, SeisResoDiff, SIST) under unified training protocols, finding that all methods struggle to fully reconstruct seismic features within and beneath chimney regions.

## Results

On the gas chimney detection task, DualUnet achieves the best performance with IoU 0.84 and Dice 0.91, followed by FaultFormer (IoU 0.80, Dice 0.87), FaultSeg (IoU 0.76, Dice 0.86), and FaultViT (IoU 0.75, Dice 0.86). On the gas chimney enhancement task, SIST leads with SSIM 0.65, PSNR 19.00, Corr 0.77, and SNR 3.51, while ConditionGAN achieves the highest PSNR (20.02) and Corr (0.81) but lower SSIM (0.52). All enhancement methods achieve relatively low SSIM and SNR scores, indicating that gas chimney enhancement remains a highly challenging open problem on this benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For detection, DualUnet [83] outperforms all other baselines, achieving IoU 0.84 and Dice 0.91, a clear margin over FaultFormer [77] (IoU 0.80) and FaultSeg [87] (IoU 0.76); FaultViT [44] trails slightly behind FaultSeg with IoU 0.75. For enhancement, SIST [22] achieves the best SSIM (0.65) and SNR (3.51), while ConditionGAN [94] leads in PSNR (20.02) and correlation (0.81), suggesting SIST better preserves structural similarity but ConditionGAN produces higher-fidelity pixel values. The diffusion-based methods SeisDDPM [23] and SeisResoDiff [95] underperform substantially, with SSIM scores of only 0.41 and 0.30 respectively, and SeisGAN [50] is the weakest overall (SSIM 0.30, Corr 0.46). The paper's central finding is that no existing method adequately reconstructs seismic features within and beneath gas chimney regions, as confirmed by qualitative inspection in Fig. 8, positioning SIGMA as a challenging benchmark that exposes significant gaps in current seismic image understanding methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the acoustic gas-diffusion model of Arntsen et al. (2007) to physically simulate how gas saturation modifies bulk modulus, density, and compressional velocity in shale formations, then couples this with reverse time migration (via Deepwave) to produce paired clean and gas-affected seismic images. It departs from prior seismic datasets (Penobscot, F3 Netherlands, TGS Salt) that lack any gas-chimney annotations, and from earlier gas-chimney detection pipelines that rely on handcrafted seismic attributes fed into classical classifiers. The key conceptual departure is treating gas chimney understanding as a supervised learning problem with ground-truth pixel masks and paired image targets, rather than as an attribute-engineering or unsupervised anomaly-detection task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIGMA fills a critical gap: no publicly available dataset provides labeled gas-chimney regions or paired clean/degraded seismic images, and no chimney has ever been physically sampled in the field, making real-world ground truth essentially unobtainable. The benchmark results reveal that even the strongest baselines achieve only moderate detection accuracy (IoU ≤ 0.84) and poor enhancement quality (SSIM ≤ 0.65, SNR ≤ 3.51), indicating that current deep learning methods do not yet handle the specific degradation patterns of gas chimneys. Key limitations include the 2D-only formulation, the high per-sample computational cost (30–45 min on a single GPU), and the modest 400-pair scale, leaving open whether synthetic-to-real transfer is viable and whether 3D volume-level benchmarks are needed for practical deployment.
