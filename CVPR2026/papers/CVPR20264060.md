## Problem

Existing driving geometry models either rely on explicit camera parameters and 2D-to-3D projection (limiting cross-configuration generalization), produce discrete occupancy grids with ~0.5 m quantization error, or require post-alignment with LiDAR to recover metric scale. There is no dense, continuous, metric-scaled 3D geometry model purpose-built for autonomous driving that is agnostic to camera configuration and operates end-to-end from unposed images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264060/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of T frames × N surround-view RGB images with no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or pose annotations. It outputs a single global dense 3D point map (H×W×3 per pixel) expressed in the ego-vehicle coordinate system of the first frame at metric scale, plus an SE(3) ego pose (3D translation + 4D quaternion) for each subsequent frame relative to that first frame. Because the output is ego-centric rather than camera-centric, the same model handles arbitrary numbers of views and focal lengths without retraining.

## Method

DVGT encodes each image with a frozen DINO backbone, appends a learnable ego token per view, and adds temporal positional embeddings to distinguish frames. The core geometry transformer replaces the expensive global attention of VGGT with a factorized three-stage attention: intra-view local attention refines per-image features, cross-view spatial attention fuses information across cameras within a frame, and cross-frame temporal attention captures static scene consistency and motion dynamics across time. A point-map head decodes image tokens into metric 3D coordinates, while a pose head aggregates ego tokens across views to regress per-frame SE(3) motion. Training uses a multi-task loss combining L1 ego-pose supervision and an uncertainty-weighted point-map loss (inherited from VGGT), with pseudo-labels generated by aligning MoGe-2 monocular depth to projected sparse LiDAR across five driving datasets (nuScenes, OpenScene, Waymo, KITTI, DDAD) after filtering out misaligned samples via validity-overlap and depth-metric thresholds.

## Results

DVGT achieves state-of-the-art ray depth accuracy across all five driving benchmarks without any post-alignment. On nuScenes, it reaches 0.069 Abs Rel and 0.953 δ<1.25, and on OpenScene, 0.049 Abs Rel and 0.971 δ<1.25, substantially outperforming the closest driving-specific baseline Driv3R (0.189/0.721 and 0.188/0.740 respectively). For 3D point reconstruction (Table 1), DVGT achieves the best accuracy on nuScenes (Acc 0.457) and OpenScene (Acc 0.402), though it trails Driv3R on Waymo (1.714 vs 0.800 Acc). Inference time is ~4.0s for 128 images (16 frames × 8 views), faster than VGGT (~13.7s) and StreamVGGT (~31.0s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVGT outperforms all general visual geometry models (CUT3R, VGGT, MapAnything, StreamVGGT) on every dataset for both ray depth and 3D reconstruction metrics, with the largest margins on nuScenes and OpenScene where it was heavily represented in training data. Against the closest driving-specific competitor Driv3R, DVGT wins on nuScenes (0.069 vs 0.189 Abs Rel), OpenScene (0.049 vs 0.188), KITTI (0.136 vs 0.164), and DDAD (0.152 vs 0.185), but loses on Waymo (0.106 vs 0.168 Abs Rel; 1.714 vs 0.800 Acc in 3D reconstruction), which the authors attribute to disproportionate sampling weights during training. For ego pose estimation (AUC@30), DVGT wins on OpenScene (74.7 vs 74.1) and DDAD (95.1 vs 92.8) but trails VGGT on KITTI (87.6 vs 96.9) and nuScenes (86.5 vs 87.8). In the driving-model depth comparison on nuScenes (Table 4), DVGT achieves 0.13 Abs Rel and 0.86 δ<1.25 without any scale recovery or pose GT, beating OmniNWM (0.23/0.81, requires Pose GT) and R3D3 (0.25/0.73, requires extrinsics). A critical practical advantage is that DVGT predicts metric-scaled geometry directly, whereas CUT3R, VGGT, StreamVGGT, and Driv3R all require post-hoc Umeyama alignment with LiDAR to recover scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

DVGT extends the general visual geometry transformer lineage (DUSt3R → VGGT → CUT3R) to the autonomous driving domain, adopting VGGT's uncertainty-weighted point map loss while departing from its global attention and camera-centric coordinate formulation. It replaces explicit 2D-to-3D geometric projection (used in BEV-based driving methods like BEVFormer and TPVFormer) with a purely data-driven, prior-free transformer that operates in ego-vehicle coordinates, decoupling the model from specific camera intrinsics and extrinsics. The factorized spatial-temporal attention (intra-view local, cross-view spatial, cross-frame temporal) is a novel architectural choice that exploits the structured multi-view, multi-frame nature of driving inputs, contrasting with the undifferentiated global attention in VGGT. For training supervision, the paper builds a pseudo-label pipeline on top of MoGe-2 monocular depth and ROE alignment, adding a multi-criteria filtering stage to handle known failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DVGT addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: most driving geometry models are locked to specific camera configurations and require post-hoc LiDAR alignment for metric scale, limiting cross-vehicle and cross-scenario generalization. By predicting metric-scaled ego-centric point maps directly from unposed images in ~4 seconds for 128 views, it offers a path toward a single universal geometry model for diverse fleets. Key limitations include weaker Waymo performance (attributed to data sampling imbalance) and reduced pose accuracy on KITTI's dual-camera setup, suggesting the ego-centric formulation may still struggle with highly overlapping or non-surround-view configurations. Open questions include whether the pseudo-label quality from MoGe-2 + ROE is sufficient for all driving conditions, and whether the factorized attention can be further optimized for real-time (sub-second) inference.
