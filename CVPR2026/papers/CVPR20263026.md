## Problem

Current fMRI-to-video reconstruction methods suffer from a semantic gap: they align fMRI embeddings only with image-text spaces, ignoring video-specific semantics such as actions and object categories, and treat each reconstruction in isolation without leveraging prior knowledge. This leads to semantically incoherent videos with incorrect objects, missing actions, and hallucinated content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263026/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw fMRI signals (sampled at 1.25 Hz) from visual cortex regions, optionally augmented with hippocampal and mPFC signals, and produces temporally consistent reconstructed videos. On cc2017 it generates 57-frame clips at 624×624, and on CineBrain 33-frame clips at 720×480, substantially longer and higher-resolution than prior methods (e.g., NeuroClips: 16 frames at 256×256). The fMRI input is tokenized into 512 spatial tokens plus one global token by a 24-layer transformer Brain Model before any semantic processing.

## Method

CINENEURON is a two-stage hierarchical framework inspired by the brain's bottom-up and top-down processing pathways. Stage 1 (bottom-up semantic enrichment) trains the Brain Model to map fMRI into a rich embedding space via three parallel tasks: CLIP-based image-text contrastive alignment, ViCLIP-based action contrastive alignment, and a multi-label category classification head (using Qwen2.5-VL-extracted labels with Focal Loss to handle class imbalance). Stage 2 (top-down memory integration) introduces a Mixture-of-Memories (MoM) module that retrieves relevant multimodal memories (text, image, action embeddings) from a pool built on training videos using a modality-aware router that computes instance-specific weighting across modalities, then fuses the top-K retrieved image/action memories via cross-attention and the top-1 text memory via a dual-stream zero-convolution residual fusion. The fused embedding conditions a Wan2.1 1.3B video diffusion decoder trained with LoRA (rank 16) under standard diffusion loss, yielding an end-to-end pipeline that requires no ControlNet, keyframe reconstruction, or auxiliary condition generation at inference.

## Results

On cc2017, CINENEURON achieves the best semantic scores (0.850 2-way, 0.240 50-way top-K accuracy) and the highest CLIP-pcc (0.972) and DTC (0.954) among all methods, while also attaining the best PSNR (9.476). On CineBrain, CINENEURON leads on 50-way accuracy (0.393 vs. 0.324 for CineSync*) and CLIP-pcc (0.988 vs. 0.977), with PSNR (16.02) essentially matching CineSync* (16.04). In human evaluation, CINENEURON is preferred over all baselines across every dimension, with 67.59% overall fidelity versus 15.80% for the next-best method (NeuroClips).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CINENEURON outperforms all six baselines on semantic-level metrics (2-way and 50-way top-K accuracy) across both datasets. On cc2017, the closest competitor NeuroClips [25] trails by 1.6 points on 2-way (0.834 vs. 0.850) and 2.0 points on 50-way (0.220 vs. 0.240), while CINENEURON's CLIP-pcc (0.972) exceeds NeuroClips' (0.738) by a large margin, indicating far superior temporal coherence. On CineBrain, CINENEURON beats the strongest baseline CineSync* [20] by 6.9 points on 50-way accuracy (0.393 vs. 0.324) and 1.1 points on CLIP-pcc (0.988 vs. 0.977), while PSNR is essentially tied (16.02 vs. 16.04). The only metric where CINENEURON does not lead is SSIM on cc2017, where NeuroClips reports 0.390 versus CINENEURON's 0.375, though the authors note this reflects a fidelity-vs.-semantics trade-off. In the human evaluation, CINENEURON is preferred in 67.59% of overall-fidelity judgments, compared to 15.80% for NeuroClips, 8.82% for Mind-Animator, and 7.79% for MindVideo.

## Relation to Prior Work

CINENEURON extends the fMRI-to-video reconstruction line initiated by MindVideo (CLIP-aligned fMRI encoder + inflated Stable Diffusion) and NeuroClips (keyframe-based perceptual flow encoding) by addressing two limitations the authors identify: (1) prior methods align fMRI only with image-text CLIP space, ignoring action and object-category semantics essential for video, and (2) they treat each reconstruction in isolation without leveraging previously seen data. The hierarchical bottom-up/top-down design is explicitly motivated by the brain's dual visual processing pathways, and the Mixture-of-Memories module draws on hippocampal memory replay as a conceptual analogy for retrieval-augmented conditioning. The video decoder backbone (Wan2.1 1.3B, a modern DiT) and the use of ViCLIP and Qwen2.5-VL for action/category supervision represent a departure from the CLIP-only conditioning used by all prior fMRI-to-video methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that enriching fMRI embeddings with multi-dimensional video semantics (action, category) and dynamically fusing retrieved multimodal memories substantially closes the semantic gap that has limited fMRI-to-video fidelity, with the integration step alone nearly doubling CLIP temporal consistency (0.802→0.973). The hippocampus ablation and voxel-weight visualization provide interpretability evidence linking the model's learned features to known visual-cortex functional areas, strengthening the neuroscience connection. Open questions remain: the memory pool is static (built from training data) rather than truly adaptive, cross-subject generalization is not deeply explored, and it is unclear whether the retrieval mechanism scales to much longer or more diverse video content. The framework is decoder-agnostic, so its semantic-enrichment and memory-integration ideas could transfer to other brain-decoding or video-generation settings.
