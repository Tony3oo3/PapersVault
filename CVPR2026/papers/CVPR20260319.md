## Problem

While Dynamic Tanh (DyT) demonstrated that a simple point-wise function can match the performance of normalization layers in Transformers, no prior work had identified a point-wise function that could surpass them. The design space of such functions—what intrinsic properties govern convergence and final performance—remained unexplored, leaving open whether normalization-free architectures could deliver stronger results than their normalization-based counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260319/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Derf is a drop-in replacement for every normalization layer (pre-attention, pre-FFN, and final norm) in a Transformer, operating element-wise on activation tensors of shape (C,) per token. It introduces only two additional learnable scalar parameters (α for input scaling and s for input shifting) plus the standard per-channel affine parameters γ and β, so the input/output interface of the host model is unchanged. This makes it applicable to any architecture that uses LayerNorm or RMSNorm, from ViT and DiT to wav2vec 2.0, HyenaDNA, Caduceus, and GPT-2.

## Method

The paper first isolates four functional properties—zero-centeredness, boundedness, center sensitivity, and monotonicity—through controlled ablations on ViT-Base, establishing design principles for effective normalization replacements. Guided by these principles, it constructs a candidate set of S-shaped functions from polynomials, rational, exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric forms (plus their clipped/scaled variants) and empirically searches across ViT and DiT. The winning design is Derf(x) = γ · erf(αx + s) + β, where erf is the rescaled Gaussian CDF, with α initialized to 0.5 and s to 0. A key finding is that Derf's gains over LayerNorm and DyT stem from stronger generalization rather than better fitting: it exhibits higher evaluation-mode training loss yet lower validation loss, because its fixed point-wise mapping acts as an implicit regularizer that constrains overfitting.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K classification, Derf achieves 82.8% top-1 accuracy with ViT-Base, surpassing LayerNorm (82.3%) and DyT (82.5%) by 0.5% and 0.3% respectively. For image generation, Derf reduces FID to 43.94 on DiT-L/4 (vs. 45.91 for LN and 45.66 for DyT) and 18.92 on DiT-XL/2 (vs. 19.94 for LN and 20.83 for DyT). In DNA sequence modeling, Derf reaches 87.3% accuracy on GenomicBenchmarks with Caduceus, outperforming both RMSNorm and DyT (86.9%). Derf also achieves lower validation loss on LibriSpeech (1.90 vs. 1.92 for LN) and matches LayerNorm on GPT-2 (2.94) while beating DyT (2.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Derf consistently outperforms LayerNorm across all evaluated domains: +0.5% top-1 on ViT-Base, +0.7% on ViT-Large, −1.97 FID on DiT-L/4, −0.02 validation loss on wav2vec 2.0 Large, and +0.4% on Caduceus DNA. Against DyT, Derf also wins uniformly: +0.3% on ViT-Base, −1.72 FID on DiT-L/4, −1.91 FID on DiT-XL/2, −0.01 on wav2vec 2.0 Large, and +0.4% on Caduceus. The one exception is GPT-2 on OpenWebText, where Derf (2.94) ties LayerNorm (2.94) but still beats DyT (2.97). Against RMSNorm (the native normalization for Caduceus), Derf gains +0.4% accuracy (87.3% vs. 86.9%). Notably, the paper shows Derf's gains stem from stronger generalization rather than better fitting: Derf's evaluation-mode training loss is consistently higher than normalization-based models (e.g., 0.2681 vs. 0.2623 for ViT-B), indicating an implicit regularization effect from the fixed point-wise transformation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the normalization-free Transformer paradigm established by Dynamic Tanh (DyT) [67], which demonstrated that a simple S-shaped point-wise function can match LayerNorm performance. Where DyT stopped at parity, this work systematically analyzes four functional properties (zero-centeredness, boundedness, center sensitivity, monotonicity) through controlled ablations and then conducts a large-scale empirical search over candidate functions to find one that surpasses normalization. The resulting Derf function (erf with learnable scale and shift) departs from DyT's tanh-based formulation by leveraging the error function's specific curvature properties, which the authors show cannot be replicated by simply rescaling tanh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a fixed point-wise function can outperform adaptive, statistics-based normalization across vision, speech, genomics, and language tasks, this work challenges the long-held assumption that normalization's per-token adaptation is essential for optimal performance. The finding that Derf's gains stem from implicit regularization (higher training loss, better validation performance) rather than stronger fitting offers a new lens on why normalization helps and what aspects are actually beneficial. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at very large scale (the largest model tested is DiT-XL/2 and GPT-2 124M), whether the GPT-2 result (comparable to LN, not clearly better) reflects a modality-specific limitation, and whether the modest but consistent gains (0.2–0.7% top-1, ~1–2 FID) compound meaningfully in downstream applications.
