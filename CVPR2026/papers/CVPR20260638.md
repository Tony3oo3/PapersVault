## Problem

High-Definition (HD) maps provide valuable static structural priors for 3D detection but are non-scalable due to their reliance on expensive manual annotation of every road feature. Meanwhile, mapping information remains under-utilized for perception tasks, and existing approaches either depend on pre-built HD maps or require complex long-context temporal fusion with up to 100 frames. This paper asks whether automatically generated scene reconstructions can serve as scalable mapping priors that improve 3D detection without human labeling or extensive temporal aggregation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes four frames of LiDAR point clouds (current + 3 history), multi-view camera images, and two pre-built mapping priors: surfel maps (0.25m voxel discretization with position, normal, and color per disk) and 3DGS representations (position, first-order SH color, 6D rotation, scale, and opacity per Gaussian). Mapping priors are generated offline from multi-traversal sensor data with dynamic objects removed. The output is 3D bounding boxes parameterized as center offsets relative to voxel centers, dimensions (l, w, h), and heading angle, evaluated within a 75m detection range.

## Method

MPA3D builds on SWFormer's sparse-window transformer backbone and introduces three key components. First, a scalable MapReduce pipeline automatically generates surfel and 3DGS maps for 600K scenes without human annotation, with 3DGS positions refined via photometric optimization to correct LiDAR sparsity. Second, a gated fusion module adaptively integrates mapping priors with LiDAR features: LiDAR features act as a gate that modulates surfel and 3DGS contributions through Swish-activated element-wise products, with residual connections preserving reliable LiDAR signals and preventing density-biased averaging. Third, a mixed-modality training strategy randomly drops surfel or 3DGS inputs during training, enabling the model to gracefully handle arbitrary modality availability at inference without architectural changes. All sparse modalities (LiDAR, surfel, 3DGS) share the same dynamic voxelization grid, while camera features are lifted into BEV via Lift-Splat-Shoot and concatenated with the fused sparse features before the transformer.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation set, MPA3D (4 frames) achieves 86.4% Overall L1 AP and 84.9% Overall L1 APH, and 81.6% Overall L2 AP and 80.1% Overall L2 APH, setting new state-of-the-art among all methods. On the WOD test set leaderboard, MPA3D reaches 87.2% L1 AP / 85.9% L1 APH and 83.0% L2 AP / 81.6% L2 APH using only 4 frames, surpassing the previous best temporal-fusion method MAD (100 frames) by 1.2% L1 AP and 1.4% L2 APH. The method also outperforms the prior best multi-frame detector SAFDNet 4f by 2.5% L1 AP and 3.0% L2 APH on validation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MPA3D outperforms all single-frame LiDAR detectors (SECOND, PointPillar, CenterPoint, DSVT-Voxel, FSDv2, HEDNet, SAFDNet) by a wide margin, with the largest gap over DSVT-Voxel (+6.1% L1 AP) and FSDv2 (+4.6% L1 AP). Among multi-frame methods, MPA3D (4f) surpasses SAFDNet 4f by +2.5% L1 AP and +3.0% L2 APH, and HEDNet 4f by +2.8% L1 AP and +3.3% L2 APH. Against temporal-fusion methods that use up to 100 frames (MAD, MSF, PTT, MPPNet), MPA3D achieves superior results with only 4 frames: on the test set, it beats MAD by +1.2% L1 AP, +1.6% L1 APH, +1.2% L2 AP, and +1.4% L2 APH. On the WOD test leaderboard, MPA3D ranks first among all non-ensemble methods, exceeding the next-best entries (MT3D, LIVOX Detection, VADet) by 1.5–2.2% in L1 AP. The consistent advantage across all object classes (Vehicle, Pedestrian, Cyclist) and both difficulty levels (L1, L2) confirms that mapping priors provide complementary information beyond what additional temporal frames can supply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SWFormer sparse-window transformer backbone and the Lift-Splat-Shoot camera-to-BEV lifting pipeline, extending them with two automatically generated scene reconstruction priors (surfel maps and 3D Gaussian Splatting) as additional inputs. Conceptually, it departs from the HD-map-based detection line (HDNet, MIM) by replacing manually annotated maps with scalable, sensor-derived reconstructions, and from the temporal-fusion line (MAD, MSF, PTT) by substituting long multi-frame aggregation with a single pre-built static scene prior. The gated fusion module is a new architectural contribution that adaptively weights mapping-prior features against LiDAR features at the voxel level, addressing the density-imbalance problem that naive averaging or concatenation cannot handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that high-quality 3D detection can be achieved with only 4 frames of sensor data plus a pre-built scene prior, outperforming methods that aggregate up to 100 frames of temporal context (MAD), which suggests static environmental knowledge can substitute for extensive temporal accumulation. The mixed-modality training strategy makes the system robust to missing or corrupted priors, addressing a real deployment concern. Open questions include: whether the approach generalizes to unseen or rapidly changing environments where pre-built maps become stale, how to handle dynamic scene elements within the reconstruction (explicitly deferred to future work), and whether the pipeline's reliance on multi-traversal data limits applicability to single-pass or novel-route scenarios.
