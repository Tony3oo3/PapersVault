## Problem

Standard patch-based video tokenization produces an excessive number of redundant spatiotemporal tokens that scale linearly with video duration, creating severe memory bottlenecks. While TrajViT demonstrated that trajectory-based tokenization can decouple token count from duration and improve accuracy, it depends on a slow, non-differentiable external segmentation-and-tracking pipeline that fixes semantic granularity independently of the downstream task. The paper addresses the gap of building an end-to-end, differentiable trajectory tokenizer whose segmentation granularity adapts to the learning objective.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer accepts either a raw video tensor (T×H×W×3) or dense patch-level feature maps from a frozen pretrained encoder, and produces a variable-length set of N trajectory tokens (N×d) where N is determined by scene complexity rather than video length. Segmentation operates at 1/4 spatial resolution, and each trajectory can emit 1, 2, or 4 sub-tokens (Matryoshka-style) to balance efficiency against expressivity for objects with complex or articulated motion. Long videos are chunked into temporal segments processed in parallel, and empty query masks are discarded to keep N dynamic.

## Method

TrajTok comprises two jointly trained differentiable modules: a universal segmenter and a trajectory encoder. The segmenter uses a ConvNeXt patch encoder to extract multi-scale features, then a 2-layer Perceiver with 128 learnable queries (with 1D RoPE applied to patch features) performs implicit spatiotemporal clustering, producing soft masks via softmax over query-feature dot products; it is trained with Dice + Focal loss (deliberately omitting cross-entropy) to prioritize region discovery over pixel-level precision. The trajectory encoder first computes initial proposals by soft-mask-weighted feature aggregation, then refines them through a second Perceiver with hard attention masks (argmax of soft masks) to enforce disentanglement between trajectories. An adaptive token mechanism duplicates each trajectory embedding into n sub-tokens initialized with Fourier positional embeddings for diversity, with n randomly sampled from {1,2,4} per batch during training. The entire tokenizer (46M parameters) is co-trained end-to-end with a ViT-Large backbone under CLIP contrastive loss plus segmentation supervision, or deployed as a frozen plug-in adapter on pretrained features.

## Results

TrajViT2 achieves the best zero-shot retrieval accuracy across all six video and image benchmarks, e.g., 42.2 R@5 vid2txt on ActivityNet and 90.4 R@5 img2txt on Flickr30K. On classification (attentive probe, top-1), it reaches 59.1% on Kinetics-400 and 48.7% on Something-Something V2, improving over the standard ViT3D by +4.9% and +2.4% respectively. As a plug-in adapter (TrajAdapter), it boosts VideoMAE-v2 probing from 80.2% to 82.5% on K400 and V-JEPA2 from 85.1% to 88.0%. As a VLM connector (TrajVLM), it gains +8.8% on LongVideoBench and +5.4% on LVBench over patch-pooling baselines on long-video QA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajViT2 outperforms all four baselines (ViT3D, TokenLearner, ViViT, RLT) on every video retrieval and video classification benchmark. Against the closest prior trajectory method TrajViT, TrajViT2 gains +4.1 R@5 on ActivityNet vid2txt, +3.9 on VATEX vid2txt, +3.8% on K400, and +3.0% on SSV2, while matching or exceeding it on image benchmarks. The sole exception is ImageNet-1K classification, where TrajViT2 (67.2%) trails ViT3D (69.4%) and TrajViT (68.1%), which the authors attribute to the segmenter producing too few tokens on single-object, simple-background images. Among the token-merging baselines, RLT is the strongest (e.g., 35.0 vid2txt on ActivityNet), yet TrajViT2 still leads by 7.2 points. In the TrajAdapter setting, TrajTok outperforms a same-size Perceiver-only module (which shows no gain over attentive probing), confirming that the trajectory prior—not just extra parameters—drives the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TrajViT [90], which first proposed representing videos as sub-object trajectory tokens but relied on a slow, non-differentiable external segmentation-and-tracking pipeline (SAM2, DirectSAM). TrajTok replaces that pipeline with a lightweight, end-to-end differentiable segmenter built on Perceiver layers [30] and ConvNeXt, trained jointly with the downstream objective. The adaptive multi-token-per-trajectory mechanism borrows the training strategy from Matryoshka representation learning [40], and the overall design departs from both fixed patch tokenization and similarity-based token merging (ToMe, RLT) by grounding token grouping in learned semantic trajectories rather than pixel similarity or fixed grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrajTok addresses a core bottleneck in video transformers—quadratic token growth with frame count—while simultaneously improving accuracy, making it a practical drop-in for efficient video understanding at scale. The finding that coarser, task-adaptive segmentation outperforms pixel-perfect masks challenges the assumption that higher segmentation fidelity is always beneficial for high-level understanding. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models and longer videos (the VLM experiments use only 128 frames), how it behaves on single-object scenes where it underperforms (ImageNet-1K), and whether the learned segmenter generalizes across domains without retraining.
