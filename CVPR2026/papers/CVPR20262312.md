## Problem

Existing command-sequence-based CAD generation methods cannot support operations that require explicit geometric entity selection (e.g., chamfer, fillet), because they encode all parameters as numerical tokens without referencing prior geometry. Additionally, discretization of continuous variables introduces quantization errors that prevent newly drawn curves from snapping to existing edges, causing topological disconnections in the generated B-rep.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262312/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language prompt describing a CAD model as input and produces a 3D solid in B-rep format through an autoregressive sequence of command tokens (Label, Value, and Pointer tokens) that translate into executable sketch-extrude, chamfer, and fillet operations. Generation is decomposed into multiple steps, each conditioned on the text prompt and the B-rep geometry accumulated from all prior steps, operating in a normalized [-0.5, 0.5]³ space. The pointer mechanism replaces continuous coordinate regression with discrete selection over a finite candidate set of faces and edges, reducing the search space and eliminating misalignment from quantization.

## Method

Pointer-CAD introduces a pointer-based command sequence where the LLM outputs a 128-d embedding that is matched via cosine similarity to the most feature-consistent candidate face or edge from the B-rep, enabling direct entity selection for chamfer/fillet and sketch-plane snapping. The architecture combines a Qwen2.5 backbone (0.5B/1.5B) fine-tuned with LoRA, a K-layer GNN encoder that processes a face-adjacency graph built from sampled geometric cues (coordinates, normals, curvature, tangents), and a multimodal fusion module that serializes node/edge embeddings into structured LLM input. Training jointly optimizes a cross-entropy loss with label smoothing for Label/Value tokens and a contrastive-style sigmoid loss over valid and invalid pointer candidates with a learnable temperature. The multi-step autoregressive strategy conditions each generation step on both the full text prompt and the evolving B-rep, ensuring global geometric consistency across the construction sequence.

## Results

On Recap-DeepCAD, Pointer-CAD-1.5B achieves the best Arc F1 (95.14%), CD mean (2.58), and SegE (0.11), while Pointer-CAD-0.5B attains the best FluxEE (2.14) and CD median (0.54), both substantially outperforming the strongest baseline CADmium-7B (Arc F1 25.68%, CD mean 10.53, SegE 1.21, FluxEE 32.22). On Recap-OmniCAD+, Pointer-CAD is the only method supporting chamfer and fillet operations, achieving Chamfer F1 of 94.32% and Fillet F1 of 89.85% (1.5B), while all baselines report no scores for these operations. The SegE improvement is particularly striking: Pointer-CAD-0.5B reduces segment error from 0.44 (Text2CAD) to 0.13 on Recap-DeepCAD, a 70% relative reduction, demonstrating that the pointer mechanism effectively eliminates topological discontinuities caused by quantization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pointer-CAD decisively outperforms all command-sequence and code-based baselines across every reported metric. Against DeepCAD (ICCV 2021), Pointer-CAD-1.5B improves Arc F1 from 31.41% to 95.14% and reduces CD mean from 37.47 to 2.58 on Recap-DeepCAD — a 14× reduction in geometric error. Against Text2CAD (NeurIPS 2024), the gap is smaller but still substantial: Arc F1 improves from 45.19% to 95.14%, SegE drops from 0.44 to 0.11, and FluxEE falls from 17.75 to 2.97. Against CADmium (arXiv 2025) at all three scales (1.5B/3B/7B), Pointer-CAD-0.5B already surpasses the 7B model on Arc F1 (85.70 vs. 25.68), CD mean (3.81 vs. 10.53), and SegE (0.13 vs. 1.21), demonstrating that the pointer mechanism provides a far more efficient representation than code tokens. On Recap-OmniCAD+, Pointer-CAD is the only method capable of generating chamfer and fillet operations (F1 89.74/82.54 at 0.5B; 94.32/89.85 at 1.5B), while DeepCAD, Text2CAD, and CADmium have no support for these operations. In the general-LLM comparison (Table 4), Pointer-CAD-1.5B achieves IR of 8.67% and CD median of 0.28 on Recap-DeepCAD-2K, compared to Claude Opus 4 (IR 29.75%, CD median 6.31), Gemini 5.2 Pro (IR 24.95%, CD median 0.58), GPT 5.2 (IR 23.90%, CD median 9.69), and Qwen3 235B (IR 35.80%, CD median 1.80), confirming that specialized CAD architectures are necessary even for frontier LLMs. The only metric where a baseline is competitive is CD median on Recap-DeepCAD, where CADmium-7B (0.44) slightly edges out Pointer-CAD-0.5B (0.54), though Pointer-CAD-1.5B (0.30) still wins.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pointer-CAD extends the command-sequence CAD generation line (DeepCAD, Text2CAD, CAD-MLLM) by borrowing the pointer mechanism from Pointer Networks [43] to explicitly reference B-rep faces and edges, a capability absent from all prior autoregressive sequence methods. It departs from code-based approaches (CADmium, Text-to-CadQuery) by using a shorter, structured token vocabulary with a dedicated pointer head rather than free-form CadQuery/Python code, and from prior B-rep encoding work (UV-Net, BrepNet) by integrating GNN-encoded face-adjacency graphs as conditioning context within an LLM pipeline rather than as a standalone geometry encoder. The multi-step generation strategy—conditioning each step on the accumulated B-rep—also distinguishes it from single-pass sequence decoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pointer mechanism resolves two long-standing practical gaps in autoregressive CAD generation: it enables chamfer and fillet operations (which require explicit entity selection) and dramatically reduces topological errors from quantization (SegE drops from ~0.44–1.21 to ~0.11–0.17). The 575K-model dataset with chamfer/fillet annotations and the finding that a 0.5B model outperforms a 7B code-based baseline make the approach practically attractive for interactive and large-scale CAD workflows. Key open questions remain: the method is limited to single-part modeling without assembly-level constraints, evaluation is text-only (no image or point-cloud conditioning), and generalization to more complex operation types (e.g., revolve, sweep, pattern) is untested.
