## Problem

Existing few-shot incremental learning methods handle data scarcity by freezing features but cannot adapt to stochastic cross-domain shifts, while hybrid incremental learning methods handle unpredictable task types through architecture expansion but severely overfit under few-shot conditions. This paper formalizes the Few-Shot Hybrid Incremental Learning (FSHIL) paradigm, where a model must simultaneously cope with stochastic class/domain increments and extreme data scarcity (K-shot per class), exposing a fundamental stability-plasticity dilemma that neither paradigm alone can resolve.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone. During pre-training, full per-class datasets are available; during each incremental step, only K samples per class (1, 5, or 10-shot) are used for training while evaluation spans all previously encountered tasks. The output is a class label among C total classes, computed by comparing the image feature against a dynamically growing prototype set that may contain multiple prototypes per class, with each class logit taken as the maximum similarity to any of its prototypes.

## Method

The architecture integrates two complementary mechanisms. CME-MoE inserts adapter-based expert branches into selected Transformer MLP layers, where a distribution detection module per expert quantifies the shift between incoming features and the expert's learned distribution; well-aligned data triggers gated reuse of existing experts for stability, while significant drift triggers instantiation of a new expert initialized from a MAML-learned template to provide task-specific plasticity without few-shot overfitting. SEPC addresses classifier-level multi-domain heterogeneity by monitoring per-class accuracy against a dynamic threshold derived from historical performance; when a class's accuracy drops below threshold, a new prototype is computed as the centroid of current training features and appended to that class's prototype set, enabling the classifier to model domain-shifted decision boundaries on demand. Training combines a distribution reconstruction loss, cross-entropy on the current task, and a prototype distillation loss to mitigate forgetting.

## Results

Under the 5-shot FSHIL setting (the paper's novel paradigm), the proposed method achieves 96.97% Avg / 95.10% Last accuracy on Office-31, 81.64% / 80.00% on Office-Home, 67.42% / 60.77% on iDigits, 78.27% / 80.84% on CORe50, and 47.20% / 40.97% on DomainNet, outperforming all baselines across all five datasets. The method also achieves state-of-the-art results in the existing FSCIL and FSDIL settings, e.g., 91.66% Avg in FSCIL on Office-31 and 92.32% Avg in FSDIL. On the most challenging DomainNet dataset, the proposed method leads the strongest FSIL baseline (SEC) by 6.75 points in FSHIL Avg accuracy (47.20 vs. 40.45) and by 17.30 points in Last accuracy (40.97 vs. 23.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every dataset and scenario. SEC [23] is the strongest FSIL baseline, achieving 95.98% Avg in FSHIL on Office-31, but the proposed method still leads by 0.99 points (96.97%) and by a wider margin on harder datasets like DomainNet (47.20 vs. 40.45 Avg, a 6.75-point gap). ASP [22] and App [35] are consistently weaker, with App particularly struggling on DomainNet FSHIL (36.39 Avg) and Office-Home FSHIL (41.85 Avg), where the proposed method achieves 47.20 and 81.64 respectively. ICON [27], the only existing HIL method, suffers catastrophic failure in FSCIL (e.g., 39.41% Avg on Office-31 vs. 91.66% for the proposed method) due to severe overfitting from unconstrained architecture expansion under few-shot conditions, and remains the weakest baseline in all scenarios. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that both CME-MoE and SEPC contribute substantially: removing both drops Office-31 FSHIL Avg from 96.97 to 64.51, while either component alone recovers most of the gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: few-shot incremental learning (FSCIL/FSDIL), which typically freezes feature extractors for stability, and hybrid incremental learning (ICON), which uses architectural expansion for plasticity but assumes ample data. The authors identify a fundamental incompatibility between these strategies—freezing prevents overfitting but blocks cross-domain adaptation, while expansion enables adaptation but overfits under few-shot conditions—and resolve it via a conditional MoE mechanism (building on adapter-based self-expansion from Wang et al. [33]) combined with MAML-based meta-initialization of new experts. At the classifier level, the self-expanding prototype classifier extends standard single-prototype few-shot classification by treating prototype count as a dynamically allocated resource, a departure from the static prototype hypothesis used in prior FSIL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FSHIL formalizes a more realistic continual learning scenario where both the type of increment (class, domain, or both) and the data budget are uncertain, bridging the gap between idealized benchmarks and deployment conditions. The stability-plasticity dilemma it identifies is a genuine and well-motivated tension that limits both existing FSIL and HIL methods. Open questions include whether the meta-expansion mechanism generalizes beyond ViT backbones, the computational overhead of maintaining growing expert pools and distribution detectors over long sequences, and whether the FSHIL protocol will be adopted as a standard benchmark by the broader community. The method is also limited to vision classification on five datasets, leaving open its applicability to language, multimodal, or higher-dimensional tasks.
