## Problem

Pruned diffusion models suffer significant quality degradation because standard finetuning with denoising score matching (DSM) does not constrain how the score function's sensitivity to perturbations evolves over the denoising trajectory. First-order Jacobian matching, a natural alternative, is mathematically redundant with DSM under noisy inputs and fails to capture long-range temporal sensitivity. No existing finetuning objective explicitly regularizes the pruned model's perturbation expansion/contraction behavior to match that of the dense teacher.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-pruned diffusion model (U-Net or Transformer backbone) paired with its frozen dense teacher, both evaluated on noise-corrupted training images at resolutions from 32×32 (CIFAR-10) to 512×512 (MS-COCO). During finetuning, random unit direction vectors v ~ N(0,I) are sampled per timestep to compute directional Jacobian quadratic forms, and the dense teacher's outputs serve as soft targets. The finetuned pruned model then generates images from Gaussian noise (optionally conditioned on text prompts for Stable Diffusion) using standard samplers (DDIM, Euler-Maruyama SDE).

## Method

2ndMatch introduces a second-order Jacobian matching loss that aligns the directional expansion rate v̂ᵀJᵀJv̂ between the pruned student and dense teacher at each timestep, inspired by Finite-Time Lyapunov Exponents which quantify how infinitesimal perturbations stretch over finite horizons. Rather than materializing full Jacobian matrices, the loss is computed via Jacobian-vector products (JVPs) supported by modern autograd frameworks, making it scalable to billion-parameter models. The total finetuning objective combines three terms: standard noise prediction (DSM), output-level knowledge distillation from the teacher, and the second-order Jacobian matching loss, with the latter providing the unique signal that constrains temporal sensitivity. A step-wise matching strategy replaces costly multi-step Jacobian propagation along the denoising trajectory with a local per-timestep sensitivity proxy, preserving the FTLE-inspired regularization while remaining computationally tractable.

## Results

On U-Net-based models at 256×256, 2ndMatch reduces FID from 13.90 to 11.25 on LSUN-Church, from 17.90 to 9.68 on LSUN-Bedroom (≈46% relative improvement), and from 10.23 to 5.68 on ImageNet, all at identical parameter counts (63.2M / 175.8M) and MACs as Diff-Pruning. On Transformer-based U-ViT, 2ndMatch achieves FID 4.05 on CIFAR-10 (vs. 4.63 for Diff-Pruning) and 3.35 on CelebA (vs. 3.57), while on Stable Diffusion v1.4 pruned to 0.76B parameters it improves FID from 15.76 to 13.84 on MS-COCO. The method consistently narrows the gap to the dense teacher (e.g., dense ImageNet FID 3.6 vs. 2ndM 5.68 vs. Diff-Pruning 10.23).

## Baselines & Comparisons

2ndMatch outperforms Diff-Pruning [10] on every dataset and metric tested: FID drops from 13.90→11.25 (LSUN-Church), 17.90→9.68 (LSUN-Bedroom), and 10.23→5.68 (ImageNet), with rFID improvements of 49%, 72%, and 56% respectively, all at identical parameter counts and MACs. Against DeepCache [32], 2ndMatch achieves comparable or better FID (9.68 vs. 9.49 on LSUN-Bedroom) while using roughly half the parameters (63.2M vs. 113.7M) and avoiding the extra memory overhead of feature caching. On Transformer-based U-ViT, 2ndMatch (FID 4.05 on CIFAR-10, 3.35 on CelebA) beats Diff-Pruning (4.63, 3.57) and SparseDM 3:4 (5.58, 5.05) at the same 14.5M parameter budget, though SparseDM 2:4 (3.81, 3.52) edges it slightly on CIFAR-10 at 22M parameters. For Stable Diffusion v1.4 pruned via BK-SDM [20], adding 2ndMatch finetuning improves FID by 1.92 (Base: 15.76→13.84), 0.81 (Small: 16.98→16.17), and 0.44 (Tiny: 17.12→16.68) on MS-COCO, with CLIP scores also improving for Base and Small variants. The ablation in Table 4 confirms that first-order Jacobian matching provides no benefit over KD alone (FID 5.14 vs. 5.05), validating the paper's argument that second-order matching is the critical component.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion model pruning (Diff-Pruning, BK-SDM, SparseDM) and derivative-based knowledge transfer (Jacobian matching in classification, sliced score matching). It departs from prior pruning finetuning work—which reuses denoising score matching or adds output-level KD—by arguing that first-order Jacobian supervision is mathematically redundant with DSM under noisy inputs, and instead introduces a second-order J^T J matching loss inspired by Finite-Time Lyapunov Exponents from dynamical systems theory. The random-projection trick for computing directional quadratic forms v^T J^T J v borrows from sliced score matching (Song et al., 2020) to avoid materializing full Jacobians, making the approach scalable to large U-Net and Transformer backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is a drop-in finetuning loss orthogonal to the pruning method and architecture, which makes it broadly applicable to the growing ecosystem of compressed diffusion models. The clean theoretical argument that first-order Jacobian matching is subsumed by DSM in the diffusion setting is a useful negative result that redirects attention to higher-order sensitivity. Open questions include whether the step-wise per-timestep approximation of J^T J captures enough of the true multi-step FTLE behavior for long-horizon generation (e.g., video diffusion), and whether the approach extends naturally to other compression paradigms like quantization or distillation to fewer steps.
