## Problem

Existing 3DGS-SLAM systems treat dynamic objects as outliers and reconstruct only static backgrounds, while the one prior attempt at dynamic 3DGS SLAM (4DGS-SLAM) relies on computationally expensive MLP-based deformation fields and category-based segmentation that fails on general dynamic scenes. The paper addresses the gap of efficient, category-agnostic dynamic 3DGS SLAM that can handle complex dynamics such as objects leaving and re-entering the view without lengthy offline training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262884/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-D video streams and internally computes RAFT optical flow between consecutive frames and YOLOv9 semantic masks as auxiliary signals. It produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene map where dynamic Gaussians have explicit 3D centers stored at discrete keyframes (interpolated linearly between them) plus GMM-parameterized temporal opacity and rotation, alongside a continuous camera pose trajectory. The category-agnostic motion mask is generated per-frame by fitting a 6-DoF camera twist to the depth-and-flow residuals, requiring no pre-defined object categories.

## Method

The core architecture combines three key mechanisms. First, a Camera-Induced Motion Decomposition module fits a 6-DoF camera twist to optical flow via IRLS with Cauchy weights over depth-anchored pixels, yielding both a category-agnostic dynamic mask (pixels with flow residuals exceeding median + k·MAD) and a coarse SE(3) pose initialization for tracking. Second, dynamic Gaussians use a hybrid representation: explicit 3D centers at keyframes propagated via optical-flow-guided scene flow (project to previous keyframe, warp by flow, unproject, then KNN-smooth for local rigidity), combined with a 3-component GMM over normalized time for smooth temporal opacity and rotation. Third, an adaptive insertion module back-tracks the current dynamic mask through backward optical flow to identify newly appearing dynamic pixels and spawns new Gaussians there, enabling the system to handle objects entering the scene without handcrafted start-time parameters. Training uses only 50 iterations per mapping step on a sliding window of 8 keyframes, achieving ~17× faster mapping than 4DGS-SLAM.

## Results

On TUM RGB-D, Flow4DGS-SLAM achieves 1.9 cm average ATE RMSE for tracking and 26.55 dB average PSNR / 0.831 SSIM / 0.177 LPIPS for rendering, outperforming the closest dynamic SLAM baseline 4DGS-SLAM (2.1 cm ATE, 22.55 dB PSNR). On the more challenging BONN dataset, the method reaches 3.5 cm ATE and 29.71 dB PSNR / 0.890 SSIM / 0.193 LPIPS, a 5.9 dB PSNR gain over 4DGS-SLAM. In terms of runtime, Flow4DGS-SLAM runs at 0.50 FPS with 6.3 s per mapping step, roughly 17× faster than 4DGS-SLAM (0.04 FPS, 110.6 s per mapping step).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow4DGS-SLAM outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest dynamic SLAM competitor 4DGS-SLAM [21], it improves tracking ATE from 2.1→1.9 cm (TUM) and 3.9→3.5 cm (BONN), boosts PSNR by 4.0 dB (TUM) and 5.9 dB (BONN), and reduces mapping time from 110.6 s to 6.3 s per step (~17× speedup). Against static 3DGS-SLAM methods MonoGS [25] and SplaTAM [18], the gains are even larger: PSNR improves by 8.8 dB and 7.2 dB respectively on TUM, while tracking ATE drops from 15.8→1.9 cm (MonoGS) and 62.2→1.9 cm (SplaTAM) on TUM. Against the offline dynamic reconstruction method SC-GS [15], Flow4DGS-SLAM achieves 5.8 dB higher PSNR on TUM and 8.1 dB on BONN, while also providing real-time tracking that SC-GS does not support. Against RoDyn-SLAM [16] (a NeRF-based dynamic SLAM), the tracking advantage is 3.2 cm on TUM and 4.4 cm on BONN, though RoDyn-SLAM is not compared on rendering quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 4DGS-SLAM line of work, which first combined 4D Gaussian Splatting with online SLAM using an MLP-based deformation field inherited from SC-GS. It departs from that formulation by replacing the costly deformation MLP with explicit keyframe positions propagated via off-the-shelf optical flow (inspired by GFlow), and by substituting category-based segmentation with a category-agnostic camera-induced motion decomposition (IRLS on the image Jacobian). The GMM-based temporal opacity and rotation is a new parametric choice not present in prior 4DGS-SLAM or SC-GS, and the adaptive Gaussian insertion via flow back-tracking addresses a failure mode (re-entering dynamic objects) that 4DGS-SLAM handles only with handcrafted start-time heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.5× mapping speedup (0.50 vs 0.04 FPS) while matching or exceeding tracking accuracy and substantially improving rendering quality (≈4 dB PSNR on TUM, ≈6 dB on BONN) makes online dynamic 4DGS-SLAM practically viable for the first time. The category-agnostic motion decomposition removes the dependency on a fixed set of dynamic object classes, broadening applicability to arbitrary moving objects. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences and more complex multi-object dynamics, robustness when depth or optical flow quality degrades, and whether the keyframe-based explicit positions can be further compressed for memory-constrained deployment. The method still requires RGB-D input and does not address loop closure, limiting its use in large-scale or GPS-denied environments.
