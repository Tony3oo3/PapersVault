## Problem

In-generation watermarking has been extensively studied for diffusion models but remains underexplored for autoregressive (AR) image generators. A direct transfer of KGW-style token-level watermarking from LLMs to AR image models works in principle but suffers from poor robustness under common image perturbations, because VQ-VAE encoding/decoding mismatches and perturbations cause reconstructed tokens to diverge from the originally generated ones, breaking the green/red token partitioning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261932/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During generation, the method takes an ImageNet class label as conditioning and produces a watermarked RGB image (256×256 or 384×384) by biasing the AR model's token sampling toward green-cluster tokens. For verification, a candidate RGB image is re-encoded through the VQ-VAE encoder to recover a token sequence, from which a binomial p-value is computed to test whether the green-token fraction significantly exceeds the expected random rate. The verification is lightweight (10–25 ms per image) and does not require access to the original generator.

## Method

The core contribution is to partition the VQ-VAE codebook vocabulary into k clusters via k-means on codebook vector similarity, then assign entire clusters (rather than individual tokens) to the green or red set at each generation step. The hash for determining the green set is computed from the cluster index of the preceding token rather than the token itself, so that perturbed images decoding to nearby tokens remain in the same cluster and preserve the watermark signal. Two variants are studied: a training-free version that only modifies the sampling procedure, and an extended version that fine-tunes a token or cluster classifier (a copy of the VQ-VAE encoder with a classification head) using adversarial perturbation augmentation for more robust token reconstruction. With k=64 clusters, δ=5 logit bias, and γ=0.25 green fraction, the cluster-based watermark achieves near-perfect detection under JPEG, Gaussian noise, color jitter, and regeneration attacks while maintaining FID close to the unwatermarked baseline.

## Results

The best variant (Clustering k=64 + Cluster Classifier) achieves TPR@FPR=1% of 0.98–1.000 across nearly all perturbations (JPEG20, Gaussian blur, salt-and-pepper, color jitter, regeneration) on all three AR models (LlamaGen GPT-B, GPT-L, RAR-XL). Image quality remains close to the unwatermarked baseline: FID of 6.12 vs. 6.01 (LlamaGen GPT-B), 4.85 vs. 4.50 (GPT-L), and 3.96 vs. 3.13 (RAR-XL). Verification runtime is 10–25 ms per image, comparable to lightweight post-hoc methods like DWT-DCT-SVD, and generation overhead is negligible (0.06 s out of 3.7 s total for GPT-B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The best ClusterMark variant (Clustering k=64 + Cluster Classifier) outperforms all six baselines on nearly every perturbation across all three models. Against the concurrent in-generation AR watermarks, it beats IndexMark by a large margin under blur (0.992 vs. 0.761 TPR on GPT-B) and salt-and-pepper (1.000 vs. 0.635), and beats WMAR on RAR-XL under blur (0.979 vs. 0.964) and regeneration (0.993 vs. 0.954) while also achieving a lower FID (3.96 vs. 4.23). Among post-hoc baselines, DWT-DCT-SVD is the weakest, collapsing to ~0.41–0.46 TPR under JPEG20 and ~0.50 under salt-and-pepper; SSL and RivaGAN handle blur well but drop to ~0.55 TPR under salt-and-pepper; TrustMark reports only AUC (no TPR) and shows poor AUC (<0.35) under most perturbations. The sole exception where a baseline edges out ClusterMark is Gaussian Blur on RAR-XL, where RivaGAN reaches 0.998 TPR vs. 0.979 for ClusterMark + Cluster Classifier, though the gap is small and RivaGAN's FID is higher (3.64 vs. 3.96 is actually lower for RivaGAN, but the paper notes RivaGAN's FID is 3.64 vs. baseline 3.13, a larger degradation than ClusterMark's 3.96 vs. 3.13).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the KGW watermarking scheme (Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023) from LLM text generation to autoregressive image generators (LlamaGen, RAR-XL), where tokens are discrete VQ-VAE visual tokens rather than text tokens. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of k-means clustering over the codebook to group similar tokens into shared red/green sets, addressing the quantization sensitivity that makes naive token-level watermarking fragile under image perturbations. Concurrent works (IndexMark, WMAR) also pursue token-level watermarking for AR image models but assign similar tokens to *different* sets or require fine-tuning the VAE decoder; this paper's training-free clustering variant and its cluster-classifier extension offer a simpler alternative that avoids decoder modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As autoregressive image generators gain traction as alternatives to diffusion models, robust in-generation watermarking is essential for provenance and content authentication, yet this space was largely unexplored. The training-free clustering step provides a drop-in robustness improvement with negligible generation overhead (~0.06s) and verification runtime comparable to lightweight post-hoc methods (10–25 ms), making it practically deployable. Key limitations remain: the watermark is vulnerable to geometric transformations (rotation, cropping) and severe blurring/salt-and-pepper noise, and the empirical hash-prefix selection is an ad-hoc workaround that the authors flag for future work. Open questions include whether clustering-based approaches generalize to continuous-token AR models (e.g., Fluid) and whether principled prefix selection or learned partitioning can replace the current heuristic.
