## Problem

Under domain-skewed federated learning, each client's data originates from a single domain, causing local representations to collapse into narrow low-dimensional subspaces and hindering cross-domain generalization. Existing elimination-based methods (e.g., FDSE) discard domain-specific features to enforce consensus, but this risks losing class-relevant information that is inherently entangled with domain biases. The paper asks whether domain-specific biases can be corrected rather than removed to recover additional discriminative signals.

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images restricted to a single domain (e.g., only Photo or only Sketch in PACS) and must predict one of C class labels. The backbone is ResNet-10 producing 512-dim embeddings, but the decoupler and corrector operate on intermediate feature maps of shape C×H×W (e.g., 512×16×16 for ResNet-10 on PACS) before the final pooling and flattening. The decoupler, corrector, and auxiliary classifier m are kept locally per client and excluded from global aggregation.

## Method

F²DC introduces two client-side modules applied after the last backbone layer. The Domain Feature Decoupler (DFD) is a two-layer CNN that scores each spatial-channel unit for cross-domain robustness, then a Gumbel-Softmax mechanism produces a differentiable pseudo-binary mask that splits the feature map into domain-robust f⁺ and domain-related f⁻; it is trained with a combined separability (cosine dissimilarity) and discriminability (correct vs. hardest-wrong-label) loss. The Domain Feature Corrector (DFC) is another two-layer CNN that learns a residual correction on f⁻, supervised by cross-entropy to inject correct semantic signals, yielding calibrated features f⋆ that complement f⁺. The final feature map f̃ = f⁺ + f⋆ feeds the classifier. On the server side, domain-aware aggregation weights each client by a sigmoid of (α·n_k/N − β·d_k), where d_k measures the client's deviation from a uniform global domain distribution, so that larger, more representative clients receive higher weight.

## Results

On PACS, F²DC achieves 76.47% average top-1 accuracy with 5.83% standard deviation across domains, outperforming the strongest baseline FedHEAL (73.34% AVG, 6.54% STD) by 3.13 points. On Office-Caltech, F²DC reaches 66.82% AVG (3.65% STD), surpassing FDSE (63.18% AVG) and FedHEAL (61.71% AVG). On Digits, F²DC attains 87.23% AVG (13.36% STD), a 3.08-point gain over FDSE (84.15% AVG). F²DC also achieves the lowest cross-domain standard deviation on all three datasets, indicating superior domain fairness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

F²DC consistently outperforms all nine baselines on average accuracy across all three datasets. On PACS, it beats the closest competitor FedHEAL by 3.13 points (76.47 vs. 73.34) and FDSE by 3.34 points (76.47 vs. 73.13), while also reducing cross-domain STD from 6.54 to 5.83. On Office-Caltech, the margin over FDSE is 3.64 points (66.82 vs. 63.18) and over FedHEAL is 5.11 points (66.82 vs. 61.71). On Digits, F²DC leads FDSE by 3.08 points (87.23 vs. 84.15) and FedSA by 4.36 points (87.23 vs. 82.87). Notably, contrast-based methods MOON and FedRCL perform significantly worse than plain FedAvg on PACS (62.64 and 65.97 vs. 66.39), suggesting that forcing alignment with compromised global representations is counterproductive under domain skew. The elimination-based FDSE, which discards domain-specific features, is consistently outperformed by F²DC's calibration approach, confirming that domain-biased features contain recoverable class-relevant information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'elimination' paradigm in domain-skewed federated learning (exemplified by FDSE, which erases domain-specific parameters) by arguing that domain-biased features are inherently entangled with class-relevant information and should be calibrated rather than discarded. It repurposes disentangled representation learning concepts—specifically, a Gumbel-Softmax-based differentiable mask to separate domain-robust from domain-related features—and introduces a correction module (DFC) that injects discriminative signals back into the domain-related branch, a mechanism absent from prior domain-skewed FL methods like FedHEAL or FedSA. The domain-aware aggregation scheme, which weights clients by their deviation from a uniform domain distribution, extends standard FedAvg aggregation with a lightweight fairness criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core conceptual shift—from eliminating to calibrating domain-specific features—offers a more principled framing for handling domain skew in FL, and the modularity (plugging DFD+DFC into FedAvg yields ~9% gain on PACS) makes the components broadly applicable to existing FL pipelines. However, all experiments use ResNet-10 on three small-scale benchmarks (≤10 classes, ≤4 domains) with only 100 communication rounds, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger architectures, more domains, or real-world deployment settings. A key open question is whether the decoupling quality degrades when domain and class information are more tightly entangled, as in natural (non-synthetic) domain shifts.
