## Problem

Existing datasets for AI-counterfeited text image detection are limited to fewer than 2,000 samples, cover only a single image type and language, rely on pre-2023 generators, and omit modern multimodal large models and commercial apps. Detection models trained on such narrow data overfit to generator-specific artifacts and fail to generalize to unseen generators, image types, or languages, leaving a critical gap for real-world robustness.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB text images resized to 1024×1536 (non-text auxiliary images at 512×512 with patch size 32) and produces two coupled outputs: a three-way image-level classification (real, fully generated, or regionally edited) via a dedicated Global Forensic Query, and instance-level bounding boxes with per-region forgery type labels (editing vs. removal) via Object queries. Image-level performance is measured by category-balanced accuracy, while localization uses F1-score under the ICDAR2015 DetEval Protocol, evaluated only on regionally counterfeited images.

## Method

DS-Net combines a Forensic Decoupling Encoder with a Synergy Denoising Decoder. The encoder pairs a Swin-Transformer backbone with a parallel Artifact Decouple Network (ConvNeXt) trained by three losses: L_dnt uses internal and external patch shuffling on non-text forgeries to force artifact-content decoupling and bridge image-level to region-level tasks; L_dt applies L2 attraction/repulsion between edited, removed, and authentic text regions to learn content-agnostic artifact features; L_da aligns text and non-text artifact features in a shared latent space. Multi-scale features from both branches are fused via channel attention. The decoder adapts DINO's denoising architecture, introducing a learnable Global Forensic Query that iteratively exchanges information with Object queries across transformer decoder layers, so the image-level verdict is informed by local evidence while global context rectifies region predictions, mimicking an expert's bidirectional reasoning.

## Results

On the DanceText benchmark, DS-Net achieves an average balanced accuracy of 77.4% and average F1-score of 53.9% across eight test subsets, outperforming the strongest baseline (CRCNN-DAF) by 2.8 points in accuracy and 4.5 points in F1. The largest gains appear in cross-generator (CG: 80.8% vs. 75.6% Acc) and cross-language (CL: 72.1% vs. 64.0% F1) settings, where decoupling artifacts from content is most critical. On the public T-IC13 dataset, DS-Net reaches 95.5 F1 (vs. 94.4 for CRCNN-DAF), and on OSTF it achieves 78.2 F1 (vs. 75.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DS-Net consistently outperforms all three baseline families (RFRM, S3R variants, and DAF variants) across every test subset and both metrics. Against the strongest baseline CRCNN-DAF [80], DS-Net gains +5.1 F1 on the in-domain Test set (83.6 vs. 78.5), +8.3 F1 on cross-generator CG (68.7 vs. 60.4), +8.1 F1 on cross-language CL (72.1 vs. 64.0), and +6.4 F1 on real-world software RW (12.3 vs. 9.8). The S3R family [117] (EAST/PSENet/ATRR/CounterNet backbones) trails DS-Net by 5–20 F1 points depending on the test subset, with the largest gap on cross-generator and cross-language settings. On the public T-IC13 dataset, DS-Net achieves 95.5 F1 versus 94.4 for CRCNN-DAF and 89.0 for the best S3R variant; on OSTF it reaches 78.2 F1 versus 75.0 and 44.5 respectively. Notably, all baselines suffer severe degradation on real-world software counterfeits (RW/RWT), where F1 drops below 20, while DS-Net maintains the highest scores (12.3/24.3), though absolute performance remains challenging due to proprietary generators and post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tampered scene text detection line (T-SROIE, T-IC13, OSTF) by reframing it as a unified problem spanning full-image generation, regional editing, and regional removal, rather than the regional-editing-only formulation of prior work. Architecturally, DS-Net builds on DINO's denoising decoder but introduces a Global Forensic Query to explicitly couple image-level classification with instance-level localization, departing from prior methods (S3R, DAF) that treat these as separate or loosely coupled tasks. The Forensic Decoupling Encoder is a novel departure: it borrows the insight from the Community Forensics dataset (2.7M non-text forgeries from 4,800+ generators) to train an auxiliary artifact-extraction network, then aligns those features with text-domain artifacts via a domain-alignment loss — a cross-domain transfer strategy not seen in prior text-forgery work. The Creative Proposer pipeline, which uses MLLMs (Gemini, GPT-4o) to generate semantically plausible editing instructions, represents a new data-construction paradigm that replaces the simplistic prompts or random edits used in earlier datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DanceText is likely to become the de facto benchmark for AI-counterfeited text detection given its 100×+ scale advantage, first-of-its-kind inclusion of MLLM and commercial-app forgeries, and the pioneering multi-axis out-of-domain evaluation protocol (cross-generator, cross-type, cross-language, real-world). However, the results reveal a sobering open problem: even the best model achieves only F1 ≈ 12–24 on real-world commercial software forgeries (RW/RWT), indicating a substantial and unresolved gap between synthetic and proprietary forgeries that current architectures cannot bridge. The finding that merely changing language degrades localization F1 by ~15 points (CL vs. Test) also suggests that artifact-content decoupling remains incomplete, pointing to future work on more robust, content-agnostic forensic features. The Creative Proposer pipeline itself may be independently useful for constructing diverse synthetic data in other detection or generation tasks.
