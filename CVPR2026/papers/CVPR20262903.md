## Problem

Diffusion-based dataset distillation methods inherit a fundamental goal misalignment: diffusion models are optimized for generative likelihood, causing synthetic samples to over-concentrate in high-density regions of the data manifold (distributional aggregation), while effective distillation requires capturing low-density boundary samples critical for classification. This results in distilled datasets that are visually plausible but lack the discriminative coverage needed for strong downstream generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262903/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real labeled dataset at 256×256 resolution, a pretrained DiT-XL/2 diffusion model, and a frozen feature encoder φ for centroid computation. It produces a compact synthetic dataset under an IPC budget (1–100 images per class), where each class's distilled subset is selected from G candidate subgroups of K images generated by the fine-tuned diffusion model. The distilled dataset is then used to train classifiers (ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, ResNet-18) from scratch for 300 epochs.

## Method

IMS³ combines two complementary strategies. First, Inversion-Matching (IM) fine-tunes the diffusion model by exploiting the inherent instability of DDIM inversion: for each real sample, the inversion noise latent z_t^inv is computed at timestep t and aligned with a directly sampled noise latent z_t via a cosine-similarity loss, combined with the standard diffusion loss to preserve fidelity. This corrective signal pushes the model toward underrepresented low-density regions. Fine-tuning uses Difffit adapters on DiT-XL/2 for parameter efficiency. Second, Selective Subgroup Sampling (S³) is a training-free inference-time strategy that generates G candidate subgroups per class, computes their centroids with a frozen feature encoder, and selects the subgroup minimizing a joint objective balancing proximity to the real class centroid (representativeness) and angular separation from other selected class centroids (discriminability), solved via greedy search.

## Results

On ImageNette (ResNetAP-10), ImS3 achieves 62.9% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10, 71.6% at IPC=20, and 84.2% at IPC=50, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline Minimax by 4.3%, 1.0%, and 0.5% respectively. On ImageWoof (ResNetAP-10, IPC=10), ImS3 reaches 41.8%, surpassing Minimax (35.7%) and DDVLCP (39.5%) by 6.1% and 2.3% absolute. On ImageIDC (ResNet-18, IPC=1), ImS3 attains 28.5%, the best among all compared methods under the most extreme compression budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ImS3 consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and IPC setting tested. Among diffusion-based methods, Minimax [8] is the strongest competitor but is surpassed by ImS3 in all configurations (e.g., +0.5% on ImageNette IPC=50, +6.1% on ImageWoof IPC=10 with ResNetAP-10). DDVLCP [53] and D⁴M [32] trail Minimax and are further behind ImS3 by 2–6% depending on the dataset and IPC. D³HR [50] is competitive on ImageWoof but does not close the gap to ImS3 at low IPC. Optimization-based baselines (SRe²L, K-Center, Herding, DM, RDED) are substantially outperformed, particularly at higher IPC settings where their limited generative diversity becomes a bottleneck. The gains are most pronounced at low IPC (1–10), where distributional coverage and discriminative structure matter most, confirming that IM and S³ address the core failure mode of diffusion-based distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based dataset distillation pipeline established by Minimax Diffusion [8], which introduced the fine-tune-then-sample paradigm using pretrained diffusion models. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it repurposes the well-documented instability of DDIM inversion trajectories [46]—typically treated as a reconstruction artifact—as a deliberate fine-tuning signal (Inversion-Matching) to push the generator toward low-density regions, and (2) it introduces a training-free, centroid-based subgroup selection mechanism (S³) that explicitly optimizes inter-class separability at inference time, a dimension largely ignored by prior diffusion-based distillation methods. The PEFT strategy via Difffit [43] is adopted for practical efficiency rather than as a core contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete failure mode—distributional aggregation—in diffusion-based dataset distillation, offering a principled explanation (generative likelihood vs. discriminative utility misalignment) and two complementary remedies. The training-free nature of S³ makes it easy to plug into existing diffusion distillation pipelines without retraining. However, the main experiments are limited to 10-class benchmarks (ImageWoof, ImageNette, ImageIDC), and the reported gains over strong baselines like Minimax and DDVLCP are modest (1–6%), leaving open whether the approach scales meaningfully to the full 1000-class ImageNet setting or to non-image modalities. A key open question is whether the inversion-instability signal remains informative as model scale and dataset diversity grow.
