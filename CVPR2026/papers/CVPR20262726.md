## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) suffer from two intertwined robustness failures: language bias (relying on textual priors over visual evidence, manifesting as object hallucination) and language sensitivity (producing inconsistent answers for the same image under superficially different prompts). Existing counterfactual decoding methods such as VCD address only bias and ignore sensitivity, while fixed robustness benchmarks fail to capture model-specific vulnerabilities since non-robust samples differ substantially across LVLMs.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text question as primary input, then internally constructs up to M visual counterfactuals (a blacked-out image and diffusion-noised images at steps 500 and 400) and N textual counterfactuals (rephrased system prompts, English↔Chinese language swaps, and identity-injection prompts). Rather than producing a single decoded token, SCI operates at the logit level: it computes and aggregates token probability distributions across all counterfactual inference rounds, yielding a reweighted next-token distribution from which the final answer is sampled (with an optional Adaptive Plausibility Constraint as a post-processing step).

## Method

SCI unifies Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) and Counterfactual VQA (CF-VQA) under a single logit-level framework by showing that VCD's probability expression is equivalent to reweighting original logits with TIE logits, where 1/α serves as a temperature parameter. The framework combines two components: a Textual Counterfactual (TC) term that takes the elementwise maximum over N+1 prompt-variant logits to enforce prompt consistency, and a Visual Counterfactual (VC) term that subtracts the average of M perturbed-image logits from the original logits to amplify visual evidence. The final next-token probability is p_SCI(y|v,q) ∝ exp(TC/τ₁)·exp(VC/τ₂), with τ₁ scaled proportionally to N to compensate for the growing magnitude of the max operation. VCD (N=0, M=1) and CF-VQA (TC constant, M=1) emerge as special cases, and increasing M+N constitutes a test-time scaling axis for robustness distinct from prior approaches that extend intermediate token length. The accompanying DRBench adaptively extracts model-specific bias and sensitivity subsets from standard LVLM datasets by filtering samples where the model's greedy prediction is invariant to visual perturbations (bias) or shifts under prompt rephrasing (sensitivity).

## Results

On the proposed DRBench BS Subset (Overall), SCI₇ achieves 34.92% top-1 accuracy for LLaVA-NeXT (vs. 18.75% base, 29.05% M3ID) and 31.72% for Qwen2-VL (vs. 14.52% base, 25.68% M3ID), demonstrating consistent gains over all counterfactual baselines. On the Bias Subset, improvements are most dramatic: LLaVA-NeXT-SCI₇ reaches 27.01% (from 0.0% base) and Qwen2-VL-SCI₇ reaches 27.65% (from 6.11% base). On real-world LVLM datasets (Table 4), SCI₅ yields more modest but consistent gains, e.g., 70.79% overall for LLaVA-NeXT (vs. 70.46% base) and 80.98% for Qwen2-VL (vs. 80.58% base), confirming that robustness improvements generalize beyond the model-specific benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCI consistently outperforms all three counterfactual baselines (TIE, VCD, M3ID) across every subset and both models. On the BS Subset (Overall), SCI₇ beats M3ID by 5.87 points for LLaVA-NeXT (34.92% vs. 29.05%) and by 6.04 points for Qwen2-VL (31.72% vs. 25.68%). The gap is largest on the Bias Subset, where the base LLaVA-NeXT scores 0.0% (by construction, since bias samples are defined as those the model always gets wrong) and SCI₇ recovers 27.01%, while M3ID only reaches 18.24%. On the Sensitivity Subset, all methods cluster more tightly (45–48% for LLaVA-NeXT), with SCI₅ achieving the best 47.95%. On real-world datasets (Table 4), SCI₅ edges out M3ID by 0.13% overall for LLaVA-NeXT (70.79% vs. 70.66%) and by 0.31% for Qwen2-VL (80.98% vs. 80.67%), with the most notable gain on the 'Others' question type (72.70% vs. 71.86% for LLaVA-NeXT), where TIE, VCD, and M3ID all degrade below the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) for object hallucination and Counterfactual VQA (TIE/TDE) for language bias—by showing that VCD's logit subtraction is mathematically equivalent to TIE-based reweighting with a temperature parameter (Eq. 4). Building on this equivalence, the proposed Self-Critical Inference (SCI) framework generalizes both by simultaneously incorporating textual counterfactuals (for sensitivity) and visual counterfactuals (for bias), and by scaling the number of counterfactual rounds as a test-time robustness lever. The companion DRBench departs from fixed robustness datasets (e.g., POPE-style exist-or-not benchmarks) by adaptively extracting model-specific vulnerable subsets from standard LVLM evaluation sets, addressing the observation that non-robust samples differ substantially across architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The model-specific vulnerability finding (only ~7% overlap in hard samples between LLaVA-NeXT and Qwen2-VL) challenges the assumption that a single fixed robustness benchmark can characterize LVLM reliability, making DRBench a practically useful diagnostic tool. The test-time scaling direction—improving robustness by increasing counterfactual inference rounds rather than intermediate token length—is a genuinely new axis, though the practical gains on full real-world datasets remain modest (~0.3–0.5% overall accuracy) and the computational overhead (up to 2.48× with batching) limits immediate deployment. Open questions include whether more sophisticated TC/VC constructions, KV-cache sharing, or learned temperature schedules can close the gap between the large DRBench-subset gains and the small full-dataset improvements.
