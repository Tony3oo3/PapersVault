## Problem

High-resolution joint depth and surface normal estimation remains difficult because existing models either operate at low resolutions (losing fine detail) or use patch-based refinement that produces inconsistent boundaries across neighboring patches. No prior method unifies global geometric reasoning with pixel-level precision for simultaneous depth-normal prediction at 4K+ resolutions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution RGB image (4K, 6K, or 8K) together with coarse depth (from Depth Anything V2) and coarse surface normal (from Metric3D V2) maps, both bilinearly upsampled to match the input resolution. Rather than predicting absolute geometry, the network outputs per-pixel offsets that are added to the coarse priors, yielding refined high-resolution depth and surface normal maps. This offset formulation means the model only needs to correct residual errors, making it robust to the quality of the upstream foundation models.

## Method

URGT reinterprets a single high-resolution image as a set of spatially distributed patches (analogous to multi-view inputs in VGGT) and processes them jointly in one forward pass. Each patch is encoded with DINOv2 alongside its aligned coarse depth and normal crops; the three token streams are summed into a unified geometry-aware representation, then concatenated across all patches. Transformer blocks alternate between intra-patch self-attention (local detail) and cross-patch self-attention (global consistency), with RoPE applied using global image coordinates so tokens retain their absolute spatial position. A GridMix strategy probabilistically samples among 1×1, 2×2, 3×3, and 4×4 grid configurations during training, acting as a data augmentation that improves inter-patch coherence. Lightweight DPT-style heads decode the transformer output into depth and normal offsets, supervised by MSE+gradient losses for depth and angle+MSE losses for normals derived from pseudo-normals computed from ground-truth depth.

## Results

On UnrealStereo4K, the joint depth-normal model (Ours Joint) achieves 0.0291 AbsRel, 0.983 δ₁, and 1.31 RMSE for depth, and 18.51° mean angular error with 9.60 RMSE for surface normals. This represents a 49% reduction in AbsRel and 35% reduction in RMSE over the strongest prior depth method (PatchRefiner p=49: 0.0582 AbsRel, 2.17 RMSE), while also reducing mean angular error from 23.36° to 18.51° over Metric3D V2. The method processes a 4K image in 0.97 s and achieves the lowest Consistency Error (0.0415) and best PDBE boundary sharpness (1.40 acc / 64.21 compl) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

URGT dominates all four baselines on the primary UnrealStereo4K benchmark. Against PatchRefiner (p=49), the strongest prior depth refiner, URGT reduces AbsRel from 0.0582 to 0.0291 (−50%) and RMSE from 2.17 to 1.31 (−40%), while also cutting inference time from 4.12 s to 0.97 s. Against PRO, URGT improves AbsRel from 0.0771 to 0.0291 and RMSE from 2.73 to 1.31, with substantially lower consistency error (0.0415 vs. 0.0549). Against the zero-shot foundation model Depth-Anything V2, URGT achieves a 64% AbsRel reduction (0.0291 vs. 0.0812) and 54% RMSE reduction (1.31 vs. 2.86). For surface normals, URGT outperforms Metric3D V2 on every metric: mean angular error drops from 23.36° to 18.51°, RMSE from 13.90 to 9.60, and the 5° threshold accuracy more than doubles from 11.74% to 29.37%. On zero-shot real-world datasets (Booster, ETH3D, Middlebury 2014), URGT Joint also achieves the best AbsRel across all three benchmarks, confirming consistent gains beyond the synthetic training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the multi-view attention architecture of VGGT for a fundamentally different setting: instead of reasoning across camera views, it treats spatially distributed patches of a single high-resolution image as 'virtual views,' enabling global geometric reasoning via cross-patch attention. It departs from the patch-based refinement lineage (PatchFusion, PatchRefiner, PRO), which processes patches largely in isolation or with limited local interaction, by introducing full cross-patch attention within a shared transformer backbone. The method also extends joint depth-normal estimation (GeoWizard, Metric3D V2) from low-resolution single-pass prediction to high-resolution multi-patch refinement, using coarse priors from both Depth Anything V2 and Metric3D V2 as conditional inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to produce globally consistent, high-resolution depth and normal maps at arbitrary resolutions (4K–8K) in a single forward pass is practically important for downstream 3D reconstruction, relighting, and scene understanding tasks that require fine geometric detail. The GridMix training strategy offers a simple yet effective data augmentation for the scarce high-resolution training data problem. Open questions include whether the method can operate without external coarse priors (making it truly end-to-end), how cross-patch attention scales computationally beyond 8K, and whether the geometric coupling between depth and normals provides benefits beyond what separate high-resolution models could achieve with sufficient data.
