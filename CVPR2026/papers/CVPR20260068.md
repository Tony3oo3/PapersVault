## Problem

Existing 4D radar–camera fusion methods for 3D object detection are either instance-based (relying on 2D proposals that lack global scene context) or BEV-based (projecting features into fixed-resolution voxel grids that waste computation on background and use rigid anchor sampling). Neither paradigm offers a flexible, adaptive representation that can dynamically concentrate resources on sparse foreground objects while still preserving holistic scene perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260068/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single monocular RGB image (resolution varies by dataset: 800×1280 for VoD, 640×800 for TJ4DRadSet, 544×960 for OmniHD-Scenes) and a 4D radar point cloud carrying per-point RCS and velocity. Beyond the primary 3D bounding-box output, RaGS produces auxiliary supervision signals—a perspective depth map, a BEV semantic segmentation map, and a fused BEV feature map—that are rendered from the Gaussian field and used to guide training. The radar point cloud is pillarized into sparse voxels with height replication to compensate for the lack of vertical resolution.

## Method

RaGS represents the scene as a continuous field of anisotropic 3D Gaussians, each carrying explicit physical attributes (position, rotation, scale, opacity) and an implicit learnable feature embedding, and refines them through a three-stage cascaded pipeline. Frustum-based Localization Initiation (FLI) unprojects top-K foreground pixels (guided by predicted depth and segmentation) into 3D to set initial Gaussian centers, augmented with radar points and furthest-sampled frustum points for spatial coverage. Iterative Multimodal Aggregation (IMA) then repeatedly enriches each Gaussian's implicit feature via 3D deformable cross-attention over a depth-aware image feature volume and sparse convolution with height-replicated radar pillars, while an MLP predicts frustum-space residual offsets that progressively shift Gaussian positions toward objects. Multi-level Gaussian Fusion (MGF) rasterizes the last L levels of refined Gaussians into BEV-aligned feature maps using a differentiable CUDA-based Gaussian splatting operator, concatenates them across scales, and fuses the result with radar pillars through a lightweight cross-modal module to produce the final BEV representation for detection. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining 3D detection, rendered depth, and BEV segmentation supervision, with auxiliary pretraining on depth and perspective segmentation.

## Results

On View-of-Delft, RaGS achieves 61.86% mAP_EAA and 81.63% mAP_DC, surpassing the previous best (SGDet3D) by 2.11% and 4.21% respectively, while running at 10.5 FPS with only 781 GFLOPs. On TJ4DRadSet, RaGS reaches 41.95% AP_3D and 51.04% AP_BEV, setting new SOTA over SGDet3D (41.82/47.16) and MSSF (37.97/43.11). On OmniHD-Scenes, RaGS obtains 35.88% mAP and 43.45% ODS, outperforming RCFusion (34.88/41.53) and BEVFusion (33.95/43.00). RaGS also uses fewer parameters (56.5M) and lower FLOPs than all compared SOTA methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RaGS achieves SOTA across all three benchmarks, winning every head-to-head comparison. On View-of-Delft, it outperforms the strongest BEV-based baseline LXL [27] by 5.55% mAP_EAA and 8.70% mAP_DC, and surpasses the prior SOTA SGDet3D [1] by 2.11% and 4.21% respectively, with a notable 7.02% gain in Car AP_DC (88.15 vs 81.13). On TJ4DRadSet, RaGS edges out SGDet3D [1] on AP_3D (41.95 vs 41.82) but leads by a wider 3.88% on AP_BEV (51.04 vs 47.16), and beats MSSF [14] (37.97/43.11) and UniBEVFusion [33] (37.76/42.92) by 4+ points. On OmniHD-Scenes, RaGS improves over RCFusion [35] by 1.00% mAP and 1.92% ODS, and over BEVFusion [16] by 1.93% mAP. Against DETR-based methods, RaGS (61.86% mAP_EAA) substantially outperforms RaCFormer [4] (54.44%) and FUTR3D [3] (49.03%). In terms of efficiency, RaGS runs at 10.5 FPS with 781 GFLOPs, faster than LXL (6.1 FPS, 1169 GFLOPs), HGSFusion (3.2 FPS, 1899 GFLOPs), and SGDet3D (9.2 FPS, 1309 GFLOPs), while using fewer parameters (56.5M vs 64–73.6M).

## Relation to Prior Work

RaGS repurposes 3D Gaussian Splatting—originally developed for neural rendering (Kerbl et al., 2023) and later adapted for occupancy prediction (GaussianFormer, Huang et al., 2024)—as a multi-modal fusion representation for 3D object detection, a use case no prior GS work has explored. It inherits the 3D deformable cross-attention mechanism from the authors' own SGDet3D and the RCS-velocity-aware sparse convolution and detection loss from RCFusion, but departs fundamentally from both instance-based fusion (which depends on 2D proposal quality) and BEV-grid fusion (which suffers from fixed-resolution voxelization and excessive background aggregation). The key conceptual shift is replacing rigid, uniform spatial sampling with an adaptive, sparse Gaussian field that dynamically concentrates representational capacity on foreground objects while retaining continuous scene coverage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a continuous Gaussian representation can outperform both dense BEV grids and instance-based pipelines while using fewer parameters (56.5M vs. 73.6M for SGDet3D) and lower compute (781 vs. 1309 GFLOPs), RaGS offers a practical efficiency path for real-time 4D radar–camera fusion. The robustness under calibration disturbance (±5°/±0.5 m) and adverse weather, where RaGS degrades substantially less than LXL, addresses a critical deployment concern. Open questions include extending to multi-view settings (acknowledged as ongoing work), exploiting radar velocity for temporal/sequential modeling, and whether the Gaussian-based fusion paradigm generalizes beyond the three tested benchmarks to larger-scale or more diverse driving scenarios.
