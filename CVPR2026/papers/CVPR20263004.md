## Problem

Most RGB-T salient object detection methods assume perfect spatial alignment between visible and thermal images, or rely on explicit geometric registration that is computationally expensive and fragile under parallax and occlusion. Under real-world misalignment, pixel-level feature distortion and semantic inconsistency cause unreliable fusion, yet no existing approach achieves robust cross-modal pixel-wise alignment without explicit registration while also adaptively suppressing noisy or conflicting thermal cues during fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of unaligned RGB and thermal infrared images (no pre-registration required), both resized to 384×384, and produces a saliency map and a boundary map in parallel at multiple decoder resolutions. The dual Swin-B backbones are initialized from ImageNet pre-trained weights, and multi-scale intermediate supervision is applied at four fusion levels within the encoder-decoder hierarchy.

## Method

UMFNet reformulates unaligned RGB-T fusion as uncertainty-aware representation learning in a probabilistic feature space. The Uncertainty Alignment Module (UAM) encodes each modality's features as pixel-wise Gaussian latent distributions (learnable mean and variance via lightweight attention), samples via the reparameterization trick, and constructs a conditionally fused cross-modal distribution to discover semantic consistency regions without explicit spatial registration; a KL divergence term regularizes the latent distributions against overconfident activations. The Confidence-Guided Global Modulation (CGM) module converts the per-pixel variances into a confidence map that gates fusion through both channel-wise scaling (γ, β from global average pooling of aligned thermal features) and spatial gating (a predicted spatial prior multiplied element-wise with the confidence map), so that unreliable thermal regions are suppressed while high-confidence regions enhance the RGB representation. UAM and CGM are inserted at the two shallowest fusion layers of the encoder, where modality heterogeneity is greatest. Training uses a composite loss combining multi-scale BCE for saliency, BCE + Dice + tolerant Dice for boundaries, and the KL regularization, optimized with Adam (lr 5e-5, batch 64) for 200 epochs on UVT20K.

## Results

On the most challenging unaligned dataset UVT20K, UMFNet achieves Sα=0.890, E_S=0.918, and F_β^w=0.836, surpassing the previous best method PCNet by 1.9%, 0.7%, and 2.8% respectively. On the weakly aligned datasets un-VT5000 and un-VT1000, UMFNet attains F_β^w scores of 0.887 and 0.927, showing improvements of 8.3% and 5.1% over SACNet. On standard aligned datasets (VT5000, VT1000, VT821), UMFNet also achieves the best performance across all three metrics, e.g., Sα=0.926 and F_β^w=0.903 on VT5000, confirming robustness under both aligned and unaligned conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UMFNet outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric on all five unaligned/weakly-aligned datasets. The closest competitor is PCNet (AAAI 2025), which uses semantic-guided progressive correlation for alignment; UMFNet surpasses it by 1.9% Sα and 2.8% F_β^w on UVT20K, and by 3.5% F_β^w on un-VT5000. Among methods specifically designed for unaligned settings, SACNet (TMM 2024) is the second-strongest baseline, but UMFNet leads it by 8.3% F_β^w on un-VT5000 and 5.1% on un-VT1000, indicating that uncertainty-aware latent-space alignment substantially outperforms asymmetric window partitioning. Traditional aligned-assumption methods (MIDD, CGFNet, SwinNet, TNet, HRTransNet, MCFNet, LSNet, LAFB) show significantly larger gaps on unaligned data, with F_β^w drops of 5–15% relative to UMFNet on UVT20K, confirming that explicit geometric registration or alignment-free attention alone is insufficient. On the three standard aligned datasets (VT5000, VT1000, VT821), UMFNet still achieves the best scores (e.g., 0.926 Sα and 0.903 F_β^w on VT5000), demonstrating that the uncertainty modeling does not sacrifice performance when inputs are already well-registered.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMFNet sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: RGB-T salient object detection (MIDD, CGFNet, SwinNet, HRTransNet) and unaligned cross-modal fusion (DCNet, SACNet, PCNet). Rather than following the dominant paradigm of explicit geometric registration (affine transforms, deformable convolutions, window matching) adopted by DCNet, SACNet, and PCNet, UMFNet repurposes variational inference machinery—Gaussian latent distributions, the reparameterization trick, and KL divergence regularization—from the VAE literature to perform implicit, pixel-wise alignment in feature space. The confidence-guided modulation (CGM) module extends standard channel-and-spatial attention fusion by conditioning it on per-pixel uncertainty estimates, a design choice that departs from the unconditional attention mechanisms used in most prior RGB-T SOD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical alternative to geometric registration for real-world RGB-T deployments where sensor misalignment is unavoidable, and its comparable FLOPs/parameter count relative to the baseline (126.10 GFLOPs, 217.60M params) while outperforming heavier specialized methods like PCNet (148.36 GFLOPs, 291.75M params, 11.31 FPS) makes it attractive for deployment. However, the approach remains confined to image-level SOD; whether the Gaussian latent alignment generalizes to video, other modality pairs (e.g., RGB-D), or larger-scale detection tasks is unexplored. An open question is whether the full probabilistic machinery (reparameterization sampling, KL regularization) is strictly necessary or whether simpler uncertainty estimates (e.g., learned per-pixel confidence scores) could achieve comparable alignment quality at lower cost.
