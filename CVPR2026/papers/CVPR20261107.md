## Problem

Existing VQualA LMMs are limited to single modalities or tasks, rely on labor-intensive human annotation that constrains data scalability, and use full-parameter fine-tuning that causes overfitting and poor transferability across model architectures. The paper asks whether machine-executed annotation can produce large-scale, task-comprehensive training data of sufficient quality, and whether centering pre-training on the vision encoder (rather than the LLM decoder) can yield a transferable foundation for diverse VQualA tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261107/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB images (resolution between 360×480 and 4K) and video clips (1–20 seconds) as visual input, with no textual prompt required at inference time—the vision token sequence alone drives quality understanding to avoid overfitting to textual prefixes. Outputs include a 5-level discrete quality score (high/good/fair/poor/low) derived from a proxy machine opinion distribution, free-text quality descriptions, distortion type-and-severity labels (e.g., 'severe-blur'), and pairwise preference probabilities computed via a Thurstone-style Gaussian difference model.

## Method

VITAL performs vision-encoder-centered generative pre-training on 4.58M machine-annotated VL pairs, freezing the LLM (Qwen2.5-7B) and projectors while training only the vision encoder (InternViT-300M + SlowFast-R50 motion extractor). For quality scoring, it introduces PMOD prediction: six no-reference IQA/VQA models produce opinion scores aggregated into a Gaussian, and the model is trained to match this distribution via KL divergence at the quality-level token, with pairwise preferences modeled as the difference of two independent Gaussians. For text generation (distortion identification and quality description), a dynamic focal loss (β=2) reweights tokens by their instantaneous prediction probability to prevent overfitting toward shorter, simpler outputs. The resulting VITAL Vision Encoder enables efficient model-zoo extension: swapping in heterogeneous decoders (InternVL-1B/2B/14B) yields VITAL-Zero models with strong zero-shot performance, and a 4000-sample decoder-only warm-up produces VITAL-Warm-up models that approach the fully trained 8B baseline at less than 1/1000 of the pre-training data.

## Results

On video quality scoring across 8 datasets, VITAL-Base-8B achieves an average SRCC of 0.820, surpassing the strongest DNN baseline KVQ (0.780) and in-domain LMM Q-Align (0.776) by 4.0 and 4.4 points respectively. On image quality scoring across 7 datasets, VITAL-Base-8B reaches 0.816 average SRCC, outperforming DeQA-Score (0.799) and Q-Align (0.785). For text generation on QBench-video-test-single, the post-trained VITAL-Assistant-8B achieves 62.94% overall accuracy, exceeding Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.33%) and the best domain-specific LMM OmniVQA-Chatter (59.94%). The VITAL-Warm-Up series (only 4,000 samples for decoder adaptation) recovers most of VITAL-Base-8B's performance, e.g., VITAL-Warm-Up-2B reaches 0.798 average SRCC on video vs. 0.820 for the full model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITAL-Base-8B outperforms all DNN-based video quality models (DOVER 0.778, KVQ 0.780, FAST-VQA 0.749) and in-domain LMMs (Q-Align 0.776, VQA²-UGC-Scorer 0.778) on average SRCC, with the largest margins on OOD datasets: KVQ (0.721 vs. 0.559 for KVQ model) and YT-Gaming (0.710 vs. 0.625–0.647). On image scoring, VITAL-Base-8B (0.816 avg SRCC) beats DeQA-Score (0.799) and Q-Align (0.785), with particularly strong gains on OOD sets like KADID-10K (0.759 vs. 0.687) and CSIQ (0.823 vs. 0.744). For text generation, VITAL-Assistant-8B (62.94%) surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (62.33%), GPT-5 (53.33%), and all open-source LMMs including Qwen3VL-32B (53.94%) and OmniVQA-Chatter (59.94%). The zero-shot VITAL-Zero models (no decoder fine-tuning) already exceed their base LLM counterparts by 20–30 SRCC points (e.g., VITAL-Zero-2B: 0.683 vs. InternVL3-Instruct-2B: 0.395 on video), demonstrating that the pre-trained vision encoder carries most of the quality perception. The VITAL-Warm-Up series, requiring only 4,000 samples (<0.1% of pre-training data), recovers 97–98% of VITAL-Base-8B's scoring performance, confirming strong structural transferability across 1B/2B/14B decoder sizes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the VQA² architecture (image encoder + SlowFast-R50 motion extractor) and the PMOD prediction strategy from DeQA-Score, extending both into a unified multi-task pre-training framework. It departs from prior VQualA LMMs (Q-Align, Compare2Score, Q-Instruct, Aes-Expert) that each target a single modality or task and rely on full-parameter SFT, by freezing the LLM decoder and training only the vision encoder on a 4.5M machine-annotated dataset. The Thurstone model informs the pairwise preference formulation, while the focal loss for text generation is adapted from object detection to address the imbalance between simple and complex quality descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The vision-encoder-centered pre-training paradigm addresses a practical bottleneck: full-parameter fine-tuning of LMMs for VQualA is expensive and overfits, limiting the ability to deploy models at different scales. The 4.5M VL-pair dataset eliminates the human-annotation bottleneck that has constrained VQualA data scaling, and the model zoo with 4000-sample warm-up offers a concrete path to deployment across hardware constraints. Open questions include whether the machine-annotation quality ceiling is sufficient for fine-grained perceptual distinctions (e.g., subtle aesthetic judgments), whether the approach transfers to other low-level perception tasks beyond quality assessment, and whether the PMOD strategy generalizes when the annotator ensemble is less diverse.
