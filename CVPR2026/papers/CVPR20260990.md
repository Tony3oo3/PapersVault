## Problem

Transformer-based text-to-video diffusion models incur quadratic sequence complexity, making high-resolution long-video generation prohibitively expensive. While Mamba offers linear-time sequence modeling, its unidirectional 1D design and lack of multimodal interaction mechanisms make it unsuitable for text-conditioned spatiotemporal video generation without substantial architectural redesign.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260990/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt as input and generates videos at 768×1280 resolution with up to 241 frames (approximately 10 seconds at 24 fps). Generation follows an autoregressive pyramid paradigm: each new frame is predicted conditioned on multi-resolution compressed latents of all preceding frames, with spatial and temporal downsampling factors varying across pyramid levels. The model also supports text-to-image generation as a sub-task during progressive training.

## Method

M4V replaces the unified Transformer blocks in PyramidFlow with a MultiModal Diffusion Mamba (MM-DiM) block that achieves O(TM + T²) complexity instead of O((TM)²). The key mechanism is MM-Token Re-Composition: text tokens are duplicated at both the start and end of the sequence (with zero-padding) so that the SSM's unidirectional scan enables bidirectional text–visual information flow through hidden states, while visual tokens are arranged via zigzag spatial scanning with learnable Per-Frame Registers inserted between frames to signal resolution changes. A lightweight parallel temporal branch applies causal attention over downsampled conditioning frames to capture long-range temporal dependencies that pure Mamba struggles with. Training uses progressive resolution/length scheduling (384p→768p, 57→241 frames) with flow-matching loss, followed by a reward-learning post-training stage using HPSv2 and CLIP scores on one-step denoised outputs to refine prompt adherence.

## Results

On VBench, M4V(Wan2.1) achieves a Total Score of 86.14 (Quality 87.56, Semantic 80.45), the highest among all models trained on public data, surpassing the original Wan2.1 (84.70) by 1.44 points while reducing inference time from 1700 s to 1210 s (28.8% faster). M4V(PyramidFlow) matches its PyramidFlow† baseline (81.55 vs. 81.61 Total Score) while cutting generation time from 812 s to 613 s for 241-frame 768p videos. The MM-DiM block reduces mixer-layer FLOPs by 45% (from 55.44 to 29.52 TFLOPs) for 241-frame generation at 768×1280 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M4V(Wan2.1) outperforms the original Wan2.1 by 1.44 Total Score points (86.14 vs. 84.70) while being 28.8% faster in inference (1210 s vs. 1700 s for 720×1280×81 videos), demonstrating that replacing all self-attention layers with MM-DiM blocks improves both quality and efficiency. M4V(PyramidFlow) essentially ties its PyramidFlow† baseline (81.55 vs. 81.61 Total Score) while reducing generation time by 24.5% (613 s vs. 812 s for 768×1280×241). Among public-data models, M4V(Wan2.1) leads all competitors by a wide margin: CogVideoX-5B (81.61), T2V-Turbo (81.01), Open-Sora 1.2 (79.76), and Open-Sora Plan v1.1 (78.00). It also surpasses proprietary models HunyuanVideo (83.24), Kling (81.85), and Gen-3 Alpha (82.32) in Total Score. In the user study, M4V shows clear advantages in semantic coherence and motion smoothness over open-source baselines (PyramidFlow, CogVideoX, T2V-Turbo), though it trails HunyuanVideo on those two criteria while outperforming it in aesthetic quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

M4V builds directly on PyramidFlow's flow-matching video generation framework with its spatial-temporal pyramid structure, replacing the unified Transformer blocks with a new MultiModal Diffusion Mamba (MM-DiM) block. It departs from prior Mamba-in-vision efforts (VideoMamba, Vision Mamba, Zigma, DiMba, Matten) that either process a single modality, rely on cross-attention for text conditioning, or operate on small class-conditioned datasets, by introducing a unified token re-composition mechanism that handles text and 3D visual tokens within a single SSM pass. The design also draws on hybrid Transformer-Mamba architectures (Jamba, TransMamba) for the lightweight temporal branch, but applies it at a finer granularity (within-block parallel branch rather than block-level alternation).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 45% FLOPs reduction in mixer layers for long video generation (241 frames at 768×1280) is a practically significant efficiency gain that could lower the barrier to deploying high-resolution, long-duration video generation. The demonstrated generalizability across two distinct base architectures (PyramidFlow and Wan2.1) strengthens the case that Mamba-based blocks are a viable drop-in replacement for attention in video diffusion models. Open questions remain: whether Mamba can fully close the quality gap with attention at even longer contexts, whether the approach scales to minute-length or higher-resolution videos without the temporal branch, and whether the modest quality improvements from reward learning and synthetic data augmentation can substitute for the massive proprietary datasets used by top commercial systems.
